# Imperial Ponds Renovation Plan – 2014

#ichthyology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #conservation_management

## Survey Objectives & Target Population

**Primary Sampling Purpose**: This renovation plan aims to completely remove nonnative fish species from six ponds at [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] to create suitable habitat for recruiting populations of [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The protocol addresses pre-stocking site preparation, chemical treatment procedures, and intensive post-renovation monitoring to ensure complete nonnative fish eradication before reintroduction of native species.

**Target Population & Spatial Boundaries**: The target population for removal includes all nonnative fish species currently inhabiting the Imperial Ponds: [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*), [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), [[Warmouth]] (*Lepomis gulosus*), and [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). The spatial boundary encompasses all six ponds at Imperial Ponds, with water elevations maintained at 185 feet. Pond volumes range from 64 to 111 acre-feet at target elevation. The ponds are now supplied by 100-percent well water, eliminating surface water as a vector for nonnative fish invasion.

## Gear Specifications & Standardization

**Equipment & Mesh/Gear Parameters**: Pre-renovation native fish removal employs trammel nets, hoop nets, and trap nets (specific mesh sizes not detailed for pre-renovation gear). Post-renovation monitoring uses: (1) ichthyoplankton nets deployed at three depths per transect (½ meter from bottom, mid-water column, surface); (2) light traps deployed systematically along transects; (3) baited and unbaited minnow traps; (4) hoop nets (baited or unbaited) deployed along shorelines and hummocks; (5) trammel nets with 0.50- to 1.5-inch mesh; (6) experimental gill nets. Chemical application uses a self-contained spray boat with venturi tube (property of USFWS Arizona Native Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office) and pesticide sprayers. Prenfish® CFT Legumine™ (5-percent rotenone) Fish Toxicant applied at 4 ppm concentration (1:10 rotenone:water mixture). Total rotenone requirement: 696 gallons for complete treatment of all six ponds (86.7 to 148.0 gallons per pond depending on volume).

**Deployment & Effort Standardization**: Pre-renovation sampling occurs spring and autumn 2014 to remove [[BONY]] and [[RASU]] from Pond 1, with population estimates generated between sampling periods. Vegetation removal (shoreline and marsh) completed October 2014 to January 2015 using hedge trimmers; Pond 5 marsh area dewatered to elevation 180 feet for 14 days, then burned or manually cleared. Chemical application follows two-part treatment protocol: first application December 2014, second followup January 2015. Application boat circles pond perimeter in decreasing radius pattern until center is reached, ensuring complete coverage. Fish survival stations (hoop nets, minnow traps, escape-proof cages) monitored every 30 minutes from treatment start until fish mortality. Post-renovation monitoring conducted monthly for 2 years or until fish captured from all ponds. Trammel/gill nets deployed maximum 2 hours, checked at 2-hour intervals. Hoop nets fished overnight, checked daily. Light traps deployed sunset to sunrise. All monitoring gear dedicated to Imperial Ponds.

**Selectivity & Bias Considerations**: Rotenone concentration (4 ppm) selected to remove bullheads and carp in rich organic ponds, following manufacturer guidelines. Water elevation managed at 185 feet to exceed river stage, creating positive head pressure to suppress groundwater intrusion that could dilute piscicide and create freshwater refugia. Vegetation removal critical to prevent concealment areas where chemical may not penetrate thick-matted vegetation. Fish survival stations placed subjectively in areas of concern (e.g., inundated vegetation) to assess treatment effectiveness and time-to-kill. Revival boat maintains minimum 15-foot distance upwind from treatment boat to avoid rotenone exposure during native fish salvage. Post-renovation monitoring targets all life stages (larvae, juveniles, adults) using multiple gear types to maximize detection probability across size classes and habitats.

## Experimental & Spatial Design Framework

**Stratification & Allocation**: The six Imperial Ponds treated as discrete units, each receiving identical two-part rotenone treatment protocol. No formal stratification within ponds, but fish survival stations deployed subjectively in areas where rotenone distribution may be compromised (vegetated areas, riprap bank lines). Post-renovation monitoring employs systematic transect-based sampling: ichthyoplankton tows at three depths per transect, light traps and minnow traps deployed systematically along transects for even distribution throughout each pond. Hoop nets target shoreline and hummock habitats. Trammel/gill nets provide additional coverage. Spatial allocation ensures comprehensive sampling across pond habitats and depth strata.

**Temporal Scheduling**: Pre-renovation activities: native fish removal spring and autumn 2014; vegetation removal October 2014 to January 2015 (outside nesting season); Pond 5 dewatering and marsh removal October 2014. Chemical applications: first treatment week of December 1–5, 2014; second followup treatment week of January 12–16, 2015 (note: document states "2014" but context indicates 2015). Application timing selected for moderate temperatures (average high 69–70°F, low 46°F) to minimize heat stress while wearing required personal protective equipment. Post-renovation monitoring monthly for 2 years following final treatment, or until fish detected in all ponds. Two-year monitoring period based on Foye (1964) recommendations for assessing renovation success.

**Detection & Bias Mitigation**: Multiple gear types deployed to maximize detection across life stages and habitats. Ichthyoplankton tows target larval fish; light traps exploit phototactic behavior of larvae and juveniles; minnow traps, hoop nets, and trammel/gill nets target juveniles and adults. Systematic transect-based deployment reduces spatial bias. Monthly sampling frequency for 2 years provides high temporal resolution to detect reinvasion or treatment failure. Equipment dedicated to Imperial Ponds; if fish captured, gear cleaned and inspected before deployment in another pond to prevent cross-contamination. Fish survival stations provide real-time assessment of treatment effectiveness. Groundwater intrusion addressed by maintaining water elevation above river stage. Vegetation removal eliminates refugia where fish could escape treatment.

## Field Data Schema & Variable Definitions

**Primary Biological Measurements**: Pre-renovation: number of [[BONY]] and [[RASU]] removed from Pond 1, PIT tag numbers, length, stocking location of recovered fish. During renovation: fish survival station data (time to mortality, treatment effectiveness). Post-renovation monitoring: all captured fish identified to species when possible, length and weight recorded. Ichthyoplankton catch preserved in 95-percent denatured ethanol for laboratory identification. Population estimates generated between spring and autumn pre-renovation sampling to assess remaining native fish abundance.

**Environmental & Spatial Covariates**: Rotenone application data: pond number, water elevation, amount of rotenone applied (gallons), application date, application start and end time, applicator names, river elevation. Water volume calculated in acre-feet for each pond at 185-foot elevation (Pond 1: 65 ac-ft; Pond 2: 111 ac-ft; Pond 3: 89 ac-ft; Pond 4: 90 ac-ft; Pond 5: 103 ac-ft; Pond 6: 64 ac-ft; total 522 ac-ft). Weather conditions monitored: application not conducted if forecast indicates high wind or rain; efforts postponed if winds exceed 15 mph. Temperature recorded for safety monitoring (heat stress assessment). Groundwater intrusion inferred from water surface elevation data in ponds and adjacent [[Colorado River]].

**QA/QC & Data Entry Protocols**: Safety briefing held each morning prior to operations; all participants review and sign Job Hazard Analysis. Data sheets created prior to renovation for standardized recording. Equipment cleaning protocols: boats and sprayers cleaned after each pond application before launching into next pond; empty rotenone containers triple-rinsed, punctured, and disposed by USFWS AZNF&WCO. Exit briefing held after each application to evaluate process and identify needed changes. Post-renovation monitoring gear dedicated to Imperial Ponds; if fish captured, equipment cleaned and inspected for remaining fish before deployment in another pond. Personal protective equipment requirements strictly enforced: chemical-resistant suits, rubber gloves, eye protection, air-purifying respirators for licensed applicators handling undiluted chemicals. Washing facilities and decontamination stations maintained onsite. All pesticide exposure incidents reported following established protocols (National Pesticide Information Center: 1-800-585-3738).
