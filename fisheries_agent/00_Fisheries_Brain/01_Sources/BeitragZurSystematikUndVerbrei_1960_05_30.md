#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cottidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 paper by Karl Müller addresses the systematics and distribution of two closely related European freshwater sculpin species, [[Cottus gobio]] and [[Cottus poecilopus]], within the family [[Cottidae]]. Building on prior morphological work (Dunker, Nybelin) and extensive field sampling (1952–1957) from the **Lule Älv** river system in northern Sweden, the study clarifies reliable taxonomic diagnostic characters distinguishing the two species, maps their European distribution, and compares their ecology, growth, and diet. The paper concludes that while the species show overlapping distributions in main river channels, they diverge ecologically in headwater/tributary habitats, likely driven by summer water temperature preferences. No significant differences were found between the species in growth rate or food composition within the study area.

## Background & Description

- **Family:** [[Cottidae]] — a species-rich family distributed across Palearctic and Nearctic marine and inland waters; believed to have originated in the northern Pacific ([[Nikolski 1957]]), with a secondary (Tertiary-era) transition to freshwater.
- **Historically unreliable diagnostic trait:** Lateral line length was long considered the primary distinguishing feature (Gasowski 1925; Dunker 1929), with *C. poecilopus* possessing a lateral line running slightly above midbody compared to *C. gobio*. However, [[Nybelin]] (1958) demonstrated this trait is highly variable, especially in *C. gobio*, and correlates more with habitat (flowing vs. still water) than species identity.
  - Confirmed by Müller: of 185 *C. gobio* from strongly flowing upper Fulda river sections, only 3 had incomplete lateral lines.
- **Ventral fin coloration** ("Buntflossenkoppe" — "colorful-finned sculpin," referring to *C. poecilopus*) is highly variable and unreliable as a sole diagnostic character; intergrading fin patterns were found in material from Swedish Lappland (Lule Älv).
- **Reliable diagnostic characters** (per Dunker correspondence with Nybelin, 1942; verified by Nybelin 1958):
  1. **Position of the lateral line relative to musculature** (assessed via cross-sections at the base of the 7th fin ray of the second dorsal fin):
     - *C. gobio*: lateral line runs near/slightly dorsal to the boundary between dorsal and ventral musculature ("Vertebrallinie").
     - *C. poecilopus*: lateral line runs consistently over the upper half of the dorsal musculature.
  2. **Inframandibular pore count** (opercular-mandibular branch of the lateral line, extending to the posterior edge of the mandibular symphysis):
     - *C. poecilopus*: **8 pores** total (4 per side), including 2 in the symphysial region.
     - *C. gobio*: **7 pores** total, with only 1 pore posterior to the symphysis.
- No intermediate/hybrid forms were observed between *C. gobio* and *C. poecilopus* even in zones of co-occurrence — contrasting with known intermediate forms between *C. gobio* and *C. koshewnikowi*.
- **Aging method:** Since Cottidae lack scales, age determination requires **otolith analysis**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution (Europe):**
- *C. gobio* shows a pronounced **western distribution** ("Westgroppe"), while *C. poecilopus* is centered in **Siberia** ("Ostgroppe"), ranging from the Pacific coast (Yalu river) across Siberian rivers, westward to Jutland, and reported east of the Elbe in Germany (e.g., East Holstein lake district, Trave river) — though not confirmed in the Großer Plöner See during October 1959 electrofishing surveys.
- A wide **zone of overlap** between the "Ostgroppe" and "Westgroppe" exists in the Pechora river region.
- Northern European range limit for both species established by [[Nybelin]] at the watershed between the Råne Älv and Vitån rivers.

**Habitat differentiation (Lule Älv system, 1952–1957 study):**
- **213 specimens** of *C. gobio* and **58 specimens** of *C. poecilopus* were collected in Lule Älv; in the nearby **Skellefte Älv**, 54 *C. gobio* and only 2 *C. poecilopus* were found.
- **Exclusive *C. gobio* habitat** — Bodträskån river system (tributary of lower Lule Älv):
  - Low elevation (~80–160 m a.s.l.), forest-fed waterway with humic, dystrophic forest lakes.
  - Catchment: 1,325 km²; lake area 41.8 km²; swamp area 199 km².
  - Water: dark brown (humus-rich), iron content 1.7–3.5 mg/l, pH 5.5–6.0, summer temps up to 25°C.
  - *C. gobio* preferred fast-flowing sections; rare in still water; absent from lakes/lake shores.
  - Co-occurring species: [[Salmo trutta]], [[Thymallus thymallus]], [[Phoxinus laevis]], [[Lota lota]].
- **Exclusive *C. poecilopus* habitat** — Parl Älv near Luvos:
  - Elevation ~400 m a.s.l., typical Scandinavian mountain stream.
  - Neutral pH, low hardness (0.5–0.7° dH), low electrolyte content.
  - Summer water temperature rarely exceeds 15°C.
- **Zone of mixed occurrence** — main channels of Lilla Lule Älv and Stora Lule Älv, and small tributary streams:
  - Water chemistry similar to Parl Älv; summer temps 17–18°C.
  - *C. gobio* consistently outnumbered *C. poecilopus* in these zones.
  - Sampling limitation: electrofishing was restricted to shore zones, so *C. poecilopus* may be underrepresented if it prefers deeper/faster mid-channel habitat.
  - Species characters remained distinct even where both species co-occurred (no intermediate/hybrid forms observed).
- **Inferred driver of habitat partitioning:** Summer water temperature appears to be a key factor separating the two species' ranges — *C. gobio* tolerating warmer conditions (up to 25°C) and *C. poecilopus* restricted to cooler waters (≤15°C).

**Diet:**
- Both species are primarily **benthic feeders**, consistent with their stone-dwelling, sheltering lifestyle.
- **Chironomid larvae** dominate the diet of both species.
- Both species act as **spawn predators**; confirmed in this study during [[Thymallus thymallus]] spawning at Spikselan.
- **Cannibalism** documented in *C. gobio* (Müller 1951).
- No marked difference in diet composition was found between *C. gobio* and *C. poecilopus* in the Lule Älv system.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic clarification:** Lateral line length, long used as a key diagnostic trait, is unreliable due to high intraspecific variability (especially in *C. gobio*) correlated with flow conditions rather than species identity.
2. **Robust diagnostic characters established:** (a) position of the lateral line relative to dorsal/ventral musculature (via cross-section analysis), and (b) inframandibular pore counts (7 in *C. gobio* vs. 8 in *C. poecilopus*), both attributed to Dunker's unpublished correspondence with Nybelin and validated on extensive material.
3. **Sympatric distribution confirmed** in the Lule Älv system, with clear zones of exclusivity for each species as well as a broad zone of co-occurrence in the main river channels — no intermediate/hybrid forms detected, unlike in *C. gobio*/*C. koshewnikowi* contact zones.
4. **Temperature as a likely ecological driver:** *C. gobio* dominates warmer, lowland, humic forest waters (up to 25°C), while *C. poecilopus* is restricted to cold, clear mountain streams (≤15°C); the overlap zone has intermediate thermal conditions (17–18°C).
5. **Growth rates:** Lule Älv populations of both species show near-identical growth trajectories (length at age I ≈ 2.7–2.8 cm, age II ≈ 5.1–5.2 cm, age III ≈ 7.0–7.3 cm), maturing at the 3rd year of life (4th summer). Spawning occurred late May–early June 1957.
   - Notably, Scandinavian *C. gobio* do **not** reach the larger maximum lengths (up to 20 cm) reported for Central European populations (Smolian 1920), while *C. poecilopus* growth matched literature values (Dunker 1937, max. 12 cm).
6. **Diet overlap:** No significant dietary differentiation between the two species was found, with chironomid larvae as the dominant prey item across sites; both exhibit spawn predation and occasional cannibalism.
7. **Biogeography:** *C. gobio* represents a "western" lineage and *C. poecilopus* an "eastern" (Siberian-centered) lineage, per Dunker's (1925) "Westgroppe"/"Ostgroppe" framework, with a broad contact/overlap zone around the Pechora river and in northern Scandinavian river systems like the Lule Älv.
