# Systematics and Variation of a New Cyprinodontid Fish, *Cyprinodon fontinalis*, from Chihuahua, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1980 taxonomic paper, by Michael Leonard Smith and Robert Rush Miller and published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (93(2): 405-416), describes *[[Cyprinodon Fontinalis]]*, a new [[Pupfish]] species from a cluster of springs in the endorheic [[Guzman Basin]] of northwestern Mexico. The species is distinguished from congeners by head form, coloration and meristic characters. The authors also examine variation among five populations, finding that morphometric differences are minor, while meristic and colour-pattern differences form a clinal and partly intermediate pattern. The authors recommend special conservation concern because the habitat is being modified for agriculture and settlement.

## Background & Description

**Regional setting.** Most of the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] lies in endorheic basins that today hold only intermittent streams or isolated springs. Pluvial lakes in the past, such as [[Lake Palomas]], left shoreline bars and lacustrine sediments, and the isolated springs preserve relict aquatic organisms. Four other new *Cyprinodon* species had recently been described from the same region (Miller, 1976).

**Type material.** The holotype (UMMZ 204189, male, 45.5 mm SL) was collected at Ojo de Carbonera, about 36 airline km west of Villa Ahumada, on 28 May 1978. Paratopotypes and paratypes were deposited in the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ), with additional material at the Field Museum of Natural History (FMNH) and the United States National Museum (USNM). The paratypes come from four additional springs (Ojo del Apache, Ojo El Medio, Ojo de las Varas and Ojo Solo).

**Diagnosis.** The species has a short, deep body typical of the genus. Key diagnostic characters include:
- Breast and abdomen fully scaled; scapular scale not enlarged.
- Mouth upturned, with a heavy, robust mandible projecting beyond the snout tip.
- Pelvic fins small, most often six-rayed, barely reaching the anus.
- Male caudal fin ending in a jet-black band wider than the pupil.
- Females with a black dorsal ocellus, and in some populations an anal ocellus.
- Six branchiostegal rays.

**Meristics (holotype marked with an asterisk in source).** Dorsal rays most often 10; anal rays most often 10; pectoral rays most often 15; pelvic rays most often 6; lateral scales most often 24; total vertebrae most often 26; gill rakers 16 to 20 on the right anterior arch. Morphometrics (Table 1) for 30 males and 30 females show variation within the population, with the holotype included among the males.

**Sexual dimorphism and colour.** Males become deeper-bodied with a high predorsal crest and develop contact organs on the head and body sides. Breeding tubercles are weaker than in many congeners. Adults are grey-green dorsally with 5 to 13 gray-blue vertical bars on the sides. Males have yellow-orange or cream dorsal fin margins. Pelvic-fin aberrations were recorded in 33% of 36 specimens from one 1979 Ojo de Carbonera collection and in 5% of specimens from other collections.

**Karyotype.** The diploid number is 48, with one pair of small metacentrics and 23 pairs of acrocentrics. This is the typical karyotype of the genus, consistent with [[Miller And Echelle 1975]]. Of 38 cells examined, 24 yielded 48 chromosomes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The species is known only from five major springs and outflows around Ejido Rancho Nuevo in the arid Bolsón de los Muertos, at about 1230 m elevation. The springs lie within 12 km of an alkali flat, El Barreal.
- **Ojo de Carbonera** is the only spring that has not been heavily modified. Water rises from spring-heads, flows about 100 m as a brook less than 10 cm deep, and enters an irrigation system. The fish are most abundant in solution holes up to about 1 m deep, and occur along undercut banks. Substrates range from quicksand to gravel. Water temperature was 27°C in 1978 and 1979.
- **Impounded springs and irrigation ditches** (Ojo Solo, Ojo de las Varas, Ojo El Medio, Ojo del Apache) hold the species in water under 0.5 m deep over sand. Water temperatures ranged from 25.5 to 28.0°C in June 1979.

**Associated biota.** Vegetation is sparse, including filamentous algae, *Chara*, *Nasturtium*, submerged grass and *Typha* around pond margins. The introduced mosquitofish *[[Gambusia Affinis]]* is common at all localities. Introduced black bass were reported locally but not seen. The introduced catfish *Ictalurus melas* was taken at Ojo Solo. The only native fish sharing the habitat is an undescribed *Notropis* known only from Ojo Solo. A new crayfish, *Cambarellus*, from the same springs was being described by [[Hobbs 1980]].

**Status and threats.** The original distribution and population size are unknown because the habitat was already modified when studies began in 1978. The pupfish is more abundant in the natural habitat at Ojo de Carbonera than in the man-made ditches and impoundments, which suggests a decline. Ongoing agricultural and settlement development is expected to modify the habitat further.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Cyprinodon fontinalis* is diagnosed by head form, coloration and meristics, and is considered a relict of the Lake Palomas drainage system.
- **Morphometric variation:** Variation in morphometric characters is primarily related to size or sex. Significant differences among populations were not detected.
- **Meristic variation:** Meristic characters are not significantly affected by size or sex, and several vary clinally among populations. Preopercular and mandibular sensory-pore frequencies show the largest between-population differences.
- **Population structure:** The three central springs (Carbonera, El Medio, Las Varas) cannot be separated meristically and form a single cluster. Apache and Solo form two distinct clusters, and the central springs are intermediate.
- **Colour differences:** Females from Ojo del Apache consistently have an anal ocellus, which is consistently absent at Ojo Solo. Males at Ojo Solo have fainter, fewer vertical bars (5 to 8) than at Apache (6 to 13).
- **Taxonomic decision:** The authors declined to erect subspecies because of high variation and intermediacy among populations.
- **Relationships:** The species is most similar to *[[Cyprinodon Macrolepis]]* in meristics, but differs in coloration, head-pore counts and mandible strength. It is also similar to *[[Cyprinodon Alvarezi]]* in jaw prominence and fin reduction.
- **Conservation:** The authors consider the species to warrant special concern and have prepared management guidelines for Mexican fisheries agencies.
- **Etymology:** The name derives from Latin *fontis* ("spring"), reflecting its spring habitat.
