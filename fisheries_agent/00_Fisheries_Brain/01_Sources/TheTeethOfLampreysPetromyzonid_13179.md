# The Teeth of Lampreys (Petromyzonidae): Terminology and Use in a Key to the Holarctic Genera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #petromyzonidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Vladykov And Follett]] (1967, *Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada*) proposes simplified terminology for the teeth of [[Petromyzonidae]] (lampreys) and applies it in a dichotomous [[Holarctic Lampreys Key]] to all known Holarctic genera. The authors argue that existing terms are "rather involved and often not uniformly applied," and they keep the exact meaning while simplifying usage. The key is based exclusively on dental characters, which the authors treat as the principal basis of lamprey taxonomy.

## Background & Description

Lamprey "teeth" differ from the true teeth of other vertebrates. They are hollow, stratified corneous cones or sheaths covering an axial papilla of the dermis. In parasitic species, these sheaths are periodically replaced, a process documented in earlier works cited by the authors.

The authors trace the English terminology to [[Günther]] (1870), who used "maxillary lamina" and "mandibulary lamina," and to later contributors including [[Regan]] (1911), [[Creaser And Hubbs]] (1922), [[Berg]] (1931), [[Holly]] (1933), [[Hubbs And Trautman]] (1937), and [[Vladykov]] (1949). The paper notes inconsistency in popular works. For example, [[Eddy]] (1957) described the funnel as "lined with cornified spines called teeth," and [[Clemens And Wilby]] (1961) listed disc plates using a different scheme.

The teeth are arranged on a sucking disc that can be schematically divided into three concentric circles:

- **Outer circle:** the [[Marginal Teeth]], anterials, posterials, and outer laterals.
- **Middle circle:** the [[Supraoral Lamina]], the [[Infraoral Lamina]], and the [[Inner Laterals]] (endolaterals).
- **Inner circle:** the tongue, bearing three lingual laminae ([[Transverse Lingual Lamina]] and two longitudinal laminae).

The [[Circumoral Row]] concept of Hubbs and Trautman is judged not useful outside the genus [[Ichthyomyzon]], and the authors use "circumorals" synonymously with inner laterals in their own earlier work.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Tooth sharpness reflects feeding strategy. In parasitic lampreys, particularly during the active feeding period, teeth are sharp, supporting [[Parasitic Feeding]]. In nonparasitic species, teeth are usually blunt. [[Caspiomyzon]] wagneri has blunt teeth unsuitable for parasitic feeding. Although its feeding habits are unknown, it is migratory and spends time in the [[Caspian Sea]] before returning to native streams to spawn.

Genera show distinct geographic distributions, as reflected in the key:

- [[Caspiomyzon]]: Caspian Sea and tributaries.
- [[Petromyzon]]: both sides of the North Atlantic Ocean and Mediterranean Sea areas.
- [[Ichthyomyzon]]: fresh waters of eastern North America.
- [[Entosphenus]]: Pacific drainage of North America.
- [[Tetrapleurodon]]: western Mexico (Rio Lerma basin).
- [[Lampetra]]: Europe and North America.
- [[Lethenteron]]: Eurasia in the Arctic Ocean drainage, from the White Sea to the North Pacific Ocean, and North America.
- [[Eudontomyzon]]: Black and Baltic sea basins, and Korea (Yalu River).

The paper also notes that [[Entosphenus]] tridentatus is occasionally taken off Hokkaido but is not definitely known to spawn in Japan. The [[Landlocked Sea Lamprey]] (*Petromyzon marinus*) is cited as a case study for infraoral lamina variation (Vladykov and Mukerji, 1961).

## Key Findings

1. **Terminology is simplified and standardized.** The authors propose clearer terms for lamprey dentition while preserving precise meaning, and they demonstrate use across all known Holarctic genera.

2. **Tooth position is organized into three circles.** Taxonomically, the [[Inner Laterals]] are the most important, and their number on one side of the disc alone is sufficient to characterize a genus.

3. **Key diagnostic characters by field:**
   - **Anterior field:** anteriors are numerous and curvilinear in [[Caspiomyzon]], [[Petromyzon]], and [[Ichthyomyzon]]. They are well spaced in [[Entosphenus]], [[Tetrapleurodon]], [[Lampetra]], and [[Lethenteron]]. In [[Eudontomyzon]] they are villiform.
   - **Posterior field:** posterials are absent only in [[Lampetra]]. They occur in 3 or 4 rows in [[Petromyzon]]-group genera, in a single row in [[Entosphenus]], [[Tetrapleurodon]], and [[Lethenteron]].
   - **Lateral field:** outer laterals are absent in [[Entosphenus]], [[Lampetra]], and [[Lethenteron]].

4. **Inner laterals separate genera.** There are 3 inner laterals per side in [[Lampetra]], [[Lethenteron]], and [[Eudontomyzon]], and 4 in [[Caspiomyzon]], [[Petromyzon]], [[Ichthyomyzon]], [[Entosphenus]], and [[Tetrapleurodon]]. Ichthyomyzon occasionally has 5.

5. **Supraoral lamina has two types.** It is either a lamina with a cusp at each end separated by a bridge (as in [[Lampetra]], [[Lethenteron]], [[Eudontomyzon]], [[Tetrapleurodon]], and [[Entosphenus]], where the median cusp is nearly as strong as the end cusps), or a single supraoral tooth with 2 to 4 cusps and no bridge (as in [[Caspiomyzon]], [[Petromyzon]], and [[Ichthyomyzon]]).

6. **Infraoral lamina has little taxonomic value.** It usually has 7 to 11 cusps, but [[Caspiomyzon]] and [[Entosphenus]] typically have only 5.

7. **Transverse lingual lamina is the most diagnostic lingual structure.** The authors recognize five main types:
   - (i) bilobed with a median groove and strong cusps ([[Petromyzon]]);
   - (ii) bilobed without a groove and with many small cusps ([[Ichthyomyzon]]);
   - (iii) not bilobed, with a greatly enlarged median cusp ([[Lampetra]], [[Lethenteron]], [[Eudontomyzon]]);
   - (iv) not bilobed, comblike, without a strong median cusp ([[Entosphenus]], [[Tetrapleurodon]]);
   - (v) not bilobed, with blunt, weakly developed cusps ([[Caspiomyzon]]).

8. **The taxonomic key (Holarctic genera) separates eight genera** using leads based on anterials, posterials, outer laterals, supraoral and lingual structure, and inner laterals. The key's splits are:
   - Numerous curvilinear teeth: [[Caspiomyzon]], [[Petromyzon]], [[Ichthyomyzon]].
   - Four inner laterals with a comblike ridge: [[Entosphenus]], [[Tetrapleurodon]].
   - Three inner laterals with an enlarged median cusp: [[Lampetra]], [[Lethenteron]], [[Eudontomyzon]].

9. **Source note on nomenclature.** The American Fisheries Society (1960) listed the Pacific lamprey as *Lampetra tridentata*, whereas the authors use *Entosphenus tridentatus*. The reference list also includes [[Vladykov And Follett]] (1958) on *Lampetra ayresii*, distinguished from the European *Lampetra fluviatilis*, and (1965) on *Lampetra richardsoni*, a new nonparasitic species from western North America.
