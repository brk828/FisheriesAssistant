# Freshwater Argulus Identification and Turtox Supply Catalog (February 1960)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #argulus

## Executive Summary

This is a 1960 issue of the [[Turtox News]] (Vol. 38, No. 2). Its main scientific content is a taxonomic article by [[David Causey]] of the [[University of Arkansas]] Department of Zoology, titled "Creepin, blastit wonner'" or On the Misidentification of Common Argulus of Freshwater Fish. The article addresses identification of the parasitic copepod genus [[Argulus]] (fish lice), reconciles the conflicting taxonomic views of [[C. B. Wilson]] and [[O. L. Meehean]], and provides a dichotomous key to the common freshwater species. The remaining pages are a commercial catalog of biological supplies, including preserved specimens, microscope slides, charts and teaching materials, with prices. No new field data or sample counts are reported.

## Background & Description

*Argulus* is a genus of parasitic Copepoda that attaches to freshwater fish. Causey describes the genus as one he finds less appealing than others, but he admits to a continuing fascination with it. He notes that the authorities disagree: some treat the freshwater species as variants of one highly variable species, while others regard each specimen as a potentially new species.

Key taxonomic sources include:

- **C. B. Wilson**, who described or redescribed most of the species in his papers. A posthumous paper (1944) restored several species.
- **O. L. Meehean (1940)**, who reviewed the genus and reduced some species to synonyms.

Causey reports that the two authorities agree more than they disagree for freshwater species. The main exception is *A. maculosus*, where he follows Wilson and notes that Meehean disagrees.

### Morphological features used for identification

Causey states there is broad agreement on the important features:

- **Size and shape** of the organism, which vary with age and preservation.
- **Shape, size and location of the respiratory areas**, which are hard to see.
- **Structure of the basal plate** and its teeth on the second maxilla.
- **Number and form of the supporting rods** of the suckers.

Other useful details include body spines and, in males, pegs and sockets on the legs. Determining most features requires dissecting out part of a sucker and clearing it, which damages the specimen. Causey also keeps a card index of key features for each species, a method he says he adapted from Peterson's bird guide.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The article provides host and distribution information compiled largely from Meehean. Causey's own additions are noted in the text.

- **Host range:** Many species are recorded from several fish families. Examples include bowfin, central mudminnow and muskellunge (*A. americanus*); brown bullhead, white sucker, gizzard shad, channel catfish, buffalofishes, largemouth bass, white bass, yellow perch and crappies (*A. appendiculosus*); carp, white sucker, northern hogsucker and large chubsucker (*A. catostomi*); goldfish (*A. japonicus*); shortnose and longnose gars (*A. lepidostei*); alligator gar and longnose gar (*A. nobilis*); chain pickerel (*A. versicolor*). Some species are described as "relatively cosmopolitan" on freshwater fish.
- **Distribution:** Records come mainly from the Mississippi and Ohio Valleys and the Atlantic States. Causey adds localities such as Lake Hamilton (Hot Springs), the Ouachita River, the White River and Reelfoot Lake in Arkansas and nearby areas. The distribution of *A. nobilis* is given as Florida, Louisiana, Mississippi and Texas.
- **Collection gaps:** Causey concludes that collecting has been inadequate. Large host species tend to be collected more often, and there is little evidence of host specificity. He contrasts this with marine biology, where collecting is more thorough, and calls for "a good monography" built on comprehensive collecting.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic disagreement:** Wilson and Meehean agree on most freshwater species. Their main conflict concerns *A. maculosus*, which Causey follows Wilson on. Meehean's hosts and distribution for some species also differ.
2. **Identification difficulty:** Diagnostic features are often hard to observe, and some are destroyed when specimens are mounted. Causey argues that species "just can't be identified by gross appearances," and that picture matching (for example, using [[Ward and Whipple Freshwater Biology]]) is unreliable.
3. **Recommended preparation:** Respiratory areas should be assessed before mounting. Many other details are best seen in balsam or polyvinyl media. For specimens in abundance, a number of hours in 10% [[Potassium Hydroxide]] (KOH) removes most non-chitinous tissue and leaves cleaner chitinous parts.
4. **Dichotomous key:** The key is adapted from Wilson and Meehean, shortened by omitting marine and foreign species and the goldfish species. It begins with respiratory area shape and position, then uses sucker rod structure, basal plate teeth and lobes, and postantennal spines. It separates about 20 couplets for the common freshwater species, including *A. stizostethi*, *A. americanus*, *A. maculosus*, *A. versicolor*, *A. mississippiensis*, *A. castostomi*, *A. appendiculosus*, *A. lepidostei* and *A. nobilis*.
5. **Known omissions:** Goldfish-associated species (described as importations) and one Texas species (recorded once) are excluded. Species in serious dispute are deferred to a later paper that Causey says he does not intend to write.
6. **Specimen policy:** Causey offers to identify and keep specimens sent to him by mail.

### Other catalog content (summarized)

The rest of the issue is a supply catalog with a small number of entries:

- **Biological specimens:** Preserved and latex-injected *Haemopis grandis* leeches (5 to 7 inches); *Loligo* squid in three size classes; *Asterias forbesi* starfish, plain and water-vascular-injected, in several sizes; a human embryo set (3 to 4 months, bisected and embedded in plastic) at $75.00 each.
- **Equipment and materials:** Botanical Riker mounts, polystyrene sorting trays, a 10X magnifier ($1.50), standard microscope slides, net-making fabrics and hardware.
- **Teaching series:** A 25-slide Human Brain Stem Set priced at $95.00, with separate slides at $1.50 each; bryophyte cultures (*Riccia*, *Marchantia*, *Anthoceros*, *Mnium*); a 14-chart genetics set at $13.60.
- **Brine shrimp eggs:** A vial of 50,000 *Artemia salina* eggs for $1.75, recommended as food for *Hydra* and aquarium fishes.
- **Feature article:** A short piece on the giant water bug *Lethocerus americanus* (45 to 60 mm long), with a photograph credited to Harold V. Green.
