# Federal Register Policy Compilation on Hybridization, Distinct Population Segments, Controlled Propagation, Recreational Fisheries and Candidate Species (1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-policy

## Executive Summary

This compilation reproduces a set of U.S. Federal Register documents issued in February through June 1996 (with a December 1996 follow-up) by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NMFS, NOAA). Together they set out administrative policy under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) rather than presenting empirical research. The core topics are: the treatment of intercrosses and hybrid progeny; the recognition of [[Distinct Population Segments]] (DPS) of vertebrates; the conditions for [[Controlled Propagation]] of listed species; a policy on conserving listed species while enhancing [[Recreational Fisheries]]; the 1996 [[Candidate Species]] Notice of Review; and listing priority guidance following the 1995–1996 funding moratorium. The documents are largely aquatic-relevant because many listed taxa are fishes, mussels, snails and aquatic insects.

## Background & Description

The compilation is a collection of federal notices, not a single study. It includes:

- **Proposed Policy on Intercrosses and Intercross Progeny (Hybridization)**, proposed rule, 61 FR 4709 (February 7, 1996), with proposed regulatory text adding definitions of "intercross" and "intercross progeny" to 50 CFR 424.02 and a new §424.03.
- **Draft Policy on Controlled Propagation** of listed species, 61 FR 4715, with comments due April 8, 1996.
- **Policy Regarding the Recognition of Distinct Vertebrate Population Segments**, 61 FR 4721, a final notice adopting a joint FWS/NMFS policy.
- **Policy for Conserving Species Listed or Proposed for Listing While Providing and Enhancing Recreational Fisheries Opportunities**, 61 FR 27977 (June 3, 1996), effective July 3, 1996, responding to Executive Order 12962.
- **Candidate Species Notice of Review**, 61 FR 7596 (February 28, 1996), listing 182 plant and animal candidate taxa and 238 species with proposed rules, with the Category 2 designation discontinued.
- **Final Listing Priority Guidance for FY 1997** and a **Notice of Final Decision on Identification of Candidates**, 61 FR 64475 and 64478 (December 5, 1996).
- Miscellaneous items: a 1996 Q&A on candidate species, a press release, an internal memorandum, a NMFS Caribbean fishery rule on [[Red Hind]] spawning aggregations, and a list of federally listed Arizona species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The documents repeatedly note that aquatic systems in North America have been significantly altered by human impacts. Key statements include:

- Degraded and altered habitats are the most frequently cited factors contributing to population extirpation and decline of federally protected aquatic species.
- As of May 1, 1996, 106 species of fish and 52 species of freshwater mussels were on the federal threatened or endangered list.
- About 36% of U.S. fishes, 64% of crayfishes and 69% of freshwater mussels are considered imperiled or extinct (data from NatureServe and Natural Heritage programs).
- Hybridization is described as occurring among plants, fishes, amphibians and reptiles, and genetic introgression is noted as often facilitated by population decline.
- Known examples cited include the Amistad gambusia (*Gambusia amistadensis*) and the Torrey pine (*Pinus torreyana*).
- Several listed or candidate taxa are of hybrid origin, including Arizona agave and Mohr's Barbara's buttons (plants).
- Controlled propagation risks include genetic introgression, loss of local adaptation, competition, disease transfer and bycatch in commercial or recreational harvest of listed species.

## Key Findings

**Intercross policy (proposed, 1996)**
- An intercross is defined as any exchange of genetic material between different species, subspecies, or distinct vertebrate population segments within a taxonomic species; intercross progeny are all offspring and descendants of one.
- Listed status extends to individuals that more closely resemble the listed species than an intermediate between listed and unlisted parents.
- Progeny of captive intercrosses are excluded unless recommended in an approved recovery plan, supported by an approved genetics management plan, implemented in a scientifically controlled manner, and undertaken to compensate for lost genetic viability in isolated listed taxa.
- "Classical hybrids" (50:50 intermediates) are not protected, but backcrosses closer to the listed parent are.
- Commercial hybrid production for aquaculture, sport fishing, pet trade and agriculture is not affected.
- Comments were solicited with a deadline of April 8, 1996 for the controlled propagation draft.

**Distinct Population Segments (final)**
- A DPS of vertebrates must be discrete (markedly separated from other populations, or delimited by international or other boundaries with regulatory differences) and significant to the taxon (e.g., persistence in an unusual setting, gap-filling range, sole surviving natural occurrence, or marked genetic difference).
- Absolute reproductive isolation is not required; genetic data are permitted but not required.
- The services received 31 letters on the draft; the policy was adopted substantially as proposed, with NMFS's salmonid ESU policy remaining in effect for Pacific salmon.

**Controlled Propagation (draft)**
- Propagation is permitted only when other recovery measures have failed or are shown likely to fail, must be linked to a recovery plan and a genetics management plan, and should occur at more than one location to reduce catastrophic loss.
- Research-only propagation is exempt from the genetics management plan requirement.

**Recreational Fisheries policy (June 1996)**
- Recognizes that about 50 million anglers spent about $24 billion on recreational fishing; expenditures exceeded $69 billion in economic output in 1991 and generated over $2.1 billion in federal tax revenue and about 1.3 million jobs.
- The policy does not extend ESA fishery goals beyond recovery criteria, does not alter ESA obligations, and seeks to resolve conflicts through cooperation, habitat-based recovery and stakeholder involvement.
- Commenters objected to perceived favoritism; the Services stated the policy is a framework only.

**Candidate Species (1996 Notice of Review)**
- 182 candidate taxa were identified, plus 238 U.S. taxa with proposed rules (159 plants and 34 animals proposed endangered; 32 plants and 9 animals proposed threatened; 84 plant and 98 animal candidates, per the summary).
- Category 2 candidates were discontinued because they were too numerous (over 4,000), of inconsistent quality and created public confusion. Of about 3,400 species reviewed by the Service, 960 were listed and about 2,400 were found not warranted.
- The Service will use the [[Natural Heritage Central Database]] (TNC) and G1–G3 / T1–T3 rankings as a reasonable subset for identifying future candidates.
- The notice reports 303 taxa removed from candidate or proposed status (Table 2 of the notice).

**Listing priority guidance (December 1996)**
- A funding moratorium from October 1995 to April 26, 1996 shut down the listing program; a backlog of 243 proposed species existed at the moratorium's end, and 57 petition findings were pending.
- FY 1997 appropriation was $5 million for listing, against $7.483 million requested.
- Four tiers were set: (1) emergency listings; (2) final decisions on proposed listings; (3) 90-day and 12-month petition findings and candidate resolution; (4) critical habitat designations and delistings.
- The Service stated that a single critical habitat designation could consume up to 10% of the listing appropriation.
- The Service classified its determinations on this guidance as not requiring NEPA review.

**Other notes**
- The Red Hind rule (NMFS, December 1996) adjusted spawning aggregation closure boundaries off southwest Puerto Rico and St. Croix to protect red hind (*Epinephelus guttatus*), a reef grouper whose population declined.
- The FWS Q&A stated that candidate species receive no statutory protection under the ESA, though section 7 conferencing applies to proposed species.
