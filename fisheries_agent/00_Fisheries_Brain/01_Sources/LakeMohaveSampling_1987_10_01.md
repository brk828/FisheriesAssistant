## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum from Jim Brooks to W.L. Minckley, dated October 1987, outlining logistical plans for a 1988 sampling effort targeting **[[Bonytail Chub]]** and **[[Razorback Sucker]]** populations in **[[Lake Mohave]]**. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the memo details a coordinated field survey strategy shifting sampling emphasis from the lower lake (Arizona Bay and below) to the understudied riverine reach known as **[[Black Canyon]]**. The rationale is grounded in recent captures of juvenile razorback suckers and historical bonytail records in this reach, suggesting Black Canyon may serve as an important, under-sampled habitat for these imperiled Colorado River native fishes.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., length, weight, meristics) are reported in this memo. The document references life-stage information relevant to population structure:
- **Juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]]** individuals were recently captured in the riverine (Black Canyon) portion of Lake Mohave, indicating potential recruitment or nursery habitat in this zone — a critical finding given the species' well-documented recruitment failure in reservoir habitats elsewhere in the Colorado River Basin.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** historical records in this reach (attributed to R.A. Wagner, reportedly from the 1930s–1950s) suggest the species previously occupied riverine habitat in this canyon, though no contemporary morphological captures are described here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat partitioning**: The memo implicitly distinguishes two habitat types within Lake Mohave — the lentic, reservoir-like lower lake (Arizona Bay and below), which has been the focus of prior sampling, versus the lotic, riverine **Black Canyon** reach upstream, which retains flowing-water characteristics more similar to historical pre-dam Colorado River conditions.
- **Significance of riverine refugia**: The presence of juvenile razorback suckers in Black Canyon supports the hypothesis that remnant riverine reaches within reservoirs may function as important spawning or rearing refugia for native Colorado River fishes, in contrast to the predominantly non-native-dominated, degraded lentic zones.
- **Site accessibility**: Proposed base camp locations at **[[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]** were selected to provide crews access throughout the canyon down to the main lake body, with spike camps deployed to increase net-running frequency.
- **Inter-agency coordination**: Collaboration with the **Arizona Game and Fish Department** (Kingman office) was planned to help identify priority sampling areas based on regional expertise.

## Key Findings
- Sampling emphasis for 1988 was proposed to shift toward the **Black Canyon** riverine reach of Lake Mohave, an area previously under-sampled relative to the Arizona Bay/lower lake zone.
- Recent juvenile **razorback sucker** captures in Black Canyon and historic **bonytail chub** records (R.A. Wagner) provided the impetus for this focus, implying this reach may hold conservation importance for two federally significant native Colorado River fish species.
- Field logistics called for a multi-agency (FWS, possibly San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge personnel) collaborative effort using 2–3 boats equipped for both electrofishing and trammel netting, with two field crews and shared camp support.
- Several unresolved programmatic questions were raised, reflecting typical pre-project planning needs:
  - Standardized methods documentation for field crews.
  - Assignment of data management responsibilities (compilation, summary, dissemination).
  - Responsibility for final report/summary memo authorship.
  - Procurement and funding of fish tags and tagging equipment.
- The sampling effort was scheduled for **6–13 March 1988**.
- The memo highlights a communication gap, noting that **Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery** and **Dexter National Fish Hatchery** may have been inadvertently omitted from the original distribution list, despite their anticipated logistical importance to the project.
