# Tule Creek Fish Barrier and Green Sunfish Occurrence

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1992 internal memorandum from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] reports on a fish barrier built on [[Tule Creek]] as mitigation for the expanded [[New Waddell Dam]] and [[Lake Pleasant]]. A post-construction inspection on June 11, 1992 found the barrier sound except for its vertical drop, and a small pool below it held seven [[Green Sunfish]]. Follow-up surveys on June 24, 1992 found no exotic fish above the barrier and concluded that the sunfish came from the [[Agua Fria River]] via Lake Pleasant, probably after high 1992 flows allowed movement. The authors judged that the barrier was already keeping exotic fish out of upper Tule Creek, where [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction sites are located.

## Background & Description

The barrier was built as a mitigative feature of New Waddell Dam to keep exotic fish from invading upper Tule Creek and its tributaries. It was completed in April–May 1992. Its design includes a 3.5-foot drop to block upstream movement of non-native fish into [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction sites. At maximum lake elevation (1712 feet), the barrier crest (1714 feet) sits about 1/4 mile upstream of the nearest lake shoreline, so the lake could supply exotic fish directly.

Key personnel and roles named in the memo:
- Eric Swanson, Aquatic Habitat Coordinator (Arizona Game and Fish Department), who led inspections and surveys
- Francisco Abarca, Special Projects Manager
- Greg (engineer), [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], who joined the June 11 inspection
- Bruce Denova (Nongame Field Crew Leader), Michael Litto, and Mark White (Wildlife Interns), who helped with the June 24 survey

The memo was addressed to all interested parties and dated July 13, 1992.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Barrier inspection (June 11, 1992).** The barrier passed inspection in all respects except the vertical standard. The Bureau of Reclamation was to modify the barrier to meet vertical standards. A pool 50 feet downstream of the barrier held seven green sunfish, 90–120 mm long, along with about 40 [[Longfin Dace]] and a number of [[Gila Topminnow]] (the exact topminnow count is illegible in the scan).

**Survey of the Agua Fria River (June 24, 1992).** The river was sampled 1/4 mile upstream of the Tule Creek confluence by [[Electrofishing]] and [[Seining]]. Catches included:
- 14 green sunfish (70–150 mm)
- 21 common carp
- Mosquitofish, red shiner, yellow bullhead, threadfin shad, and longfin dace (counts partly illegible in the scan)

**Survey of Tule Creek (June 24, 1992).** The team sampled the lower creek from the confluence to the barrier. About 3/4 mile of channel was dry, including the pool below the barrier where green sunfish had been found. No exotic species were found above the barrier, and the upper creek was sampled from its uppermost spring downstream to the barrier. Both [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were widely distributed in sporadic small pools. Topminnow were found downstream to 1/4 mile below the barrier. Pools had both adults and young, but smaller pools showed signs of rapid desiccation. Two east-side tributaries also held topminnow and longfin dace.

Other wildlife noted: Sonoran mud turtles, leopard frogs, garter snakes, great blue herons, a great horned owl, and wild burros.

**Historical and hydrological context.** Green sunfish had been recorded on the Agua Fria River in March 1988 during an earlier [[Lake Pleasant]] fisheries investigation. The memo describes central Arizona precipitation as exceptionally high in 1992, with high, continuous flows in the Agua Fria drainage above Lake Pleasant from January through April. These flows likely allowed fish to move from the lake into the river and then into lower Tule Creek.

## Key Findings

- **Barrier performance:** The fish barrier met design standards except for the vertical requirement, which the Bureau of Reclamation was to correct.
- **Origin of exotic fish:** Green sunfish found below the barrier on June 11 most likely came from the Agua Fria River, based on the June 24 survey counts and the high-flow conditions.
- **No upstream invasion:** No exotic fish were found above the barrier. The June 24 surveys showed that green sunfish were not established above the barrier.
- **Protected native fish:** [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were distributed throughout upper Tule Creek and its tributaries, with good numbers of adults and young.
- **Effective mitigation:** The authors concluded that the timely construction of the barrier had already effectively prevented upstream movement of exotic fish into Tule Creek.
- **Species recorded in the Agua Fria survey:** Green sunfish, [[Common Carp]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Threadfin Shad]], and [[Channel Catfish]] were all present, and most are non-native to the system.
- **Relevance to reintroduction:** Keeping exotic fish out is a precondition for the success of [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction in upper Tule Creek.
