## Executive Summary

These minutes document the 2-3 October 1986 meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] at Arizona State University, chaired by team leader W.L. Minckley. The meeting addressed recovery planning and status updates for multiple imperiled desert fish species across the [[Yaqui River Basin]], [[Gila River Basin]], and [[Colorado River Basin]]. Key agenda items included finalizing the **Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan** technical draft, status of the **[[Gila ditaenia]]** (Sonoran Chub) listing package, extensive discussion of **[[Cyprinodon macularius]]** (Desert Pupfish) conservation conflicts (particularly genetic contamination issues at Findley Tank and Fort Huachuca), and updates on recovery/listing status for **[[Lepidomeda vittata]]**, **[[Tiaroga cobitis]]**, **[[Meda fulgida]]**, **[[Ptychocheilus lucius]]**, **[[Gila elegans]]**, **[[Gila nigrescens]]**, **[[Xyrauchen texanus]]**, and **[[Gila cypha]]**. Recurring themes include genetic integrity of captive/refugial stocks, non-native species removal, habitat renovation, and the Team's stated philosophy of recovering entire native fish communities rather than single species.

## Background & Description

While this document is primarily administrative/policy-focused rather than descriptive, several taxon-specific notes on population biology and identification are recorded:

- **[[Cyprinodon macularius]]**: Populations in the Santa Clara Slough (Sonora/Baja California del Norte) persist in modified thermal/canal habitats; sympatric with invasive **Tilapia aurea** and **Tilapia mossambica**.
- **[[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail Chub)**: Captive stock at Dexter National Fish Hatchery reproduces via uncontrolled "volunteer" pond spawning, raising concern that a single breeding pair could produce an entire cohort, reducing effective population size and genetic diversity.
- **[[Gila nigrescens]] (Chihuahua Chub)**: Mimbres River stock maintained at Dexter; reintroduction stock for Bear Canyon planned from volunteer pond reproduction, later recommended to be replaced with manually-spawned cohorts to maximize heterozygosity.
- **[[Gila cypha]] (Humpback Chub)**: Field survey at the mouth of the Little Colorado River (LCR) in May 1986 documented spent (post-spawning) adults, plus larvae and juveniles indicating recent, successful spawning. Over 500 individuals were captured via trammel net, tagged with Floy tags, weighed, and measured. Low recapture rate (few of ~9,500 previously tagged fish recovered) suggests a large overall population size.
- **[[Cyprinodon macularius]] × Quitobaquito stock hybridization**: Populations at Fort Huachuca ponds suspected to represent mixed Gila-basin and Quitobaquito genetic stocks, complicating taxonomic/subspecies management.
- Findley Tank pupfish population suspected to show apomorphic (derived) characters evolved in isolation over ~20 years since founding from Quitobaquito stock; under electrophoretic analysis by J. Mitton (University of Colorado).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Cyprinodon macularius]]**: Occupies isolated desert spring, slough, and canal habitats in the Sonoran Desert (Santa Clara Slough, Salinity Canal, El Doctor Spring). Habitat degraded by geothermal exploration and conversion to Tilapia aquaculture; threatened by flooding-mediated invasion of non-native cichlids (**Tilapia aurea**, **T. mossambica**).
- **Findley Tank / [[Babocomari River]] / [[San Pedro River]] system**: Spring-fed tank draining into a Gila-basin tributary network; ecological connectivity raises risk of non-native pupfish stock dispersal into native habitat despite claims of hydrological isolation.
- **[[Aravaipa Creek]] Watershed**: BLM and adjacent private lands harbor non-native fish populations in tanks, threatening the native aquatic community; targeted for renovation.
- **[[Lepidomeda vittata]]**: Critical habitat proposed at Nutrioso, lower Chevelon Creek, and East Clear Creek (Little Colorado River drainage, Arizona).
- **[[Tiaroga cobitis]] and [[Meda fulgida]]**: Co-occur in Gila basin streams; potential competitive/predatory interaction with non-native **Notropis lutrensis** (Red Shiner) under joint research investigation.
- **[[Ptychocheilus lucius]]**: Occupies lower Colorado River mainstream habitats; subject of a proposed AGFD sport fishery, raising concerns about genetic "domestication" via hatchery practices.
- **[[Xyrauchen texanus]]**: Lower Colorado River backwaters; natural recruitment uncertain, prompting proposed stocking into renovated backwater habitats for growout.
- **[[Gila cypha]]**: Concentrated near the confluence of the Little Colorado River and mainstream Colorado River (Grand Canyon reach), particularly within impounded backwater zones created by high mainstream flows; limited distribution upstream of impoundment zone.

## Key Findings

1. **Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan**: Technical draft revised for Agency Review; appendices eliminated with content merged into the main text; species description sections to be added; responsibilities assigned to Propst (border-crossing taxa incl. Beautiful Shiner and Mexican Stoneroller), Galat/Robertson (San Bernardino NWR habitat management), and Minckley/Hamilton/Kennedy (federal land acquisition).

2. **[[Gila ditaenia]] listing package**: Technical Review draft in preparation for team review, targeted for Agency Review at the next meeting.

3. **[[Cyprinodon macularius]] recovery conflicts**:
   - BLM's proposed reintroduction plan reverted five "fully protected" sites to experimental status after one year; Team recommends permanent full protection instead.
   - Roper Lake State Park thermal spring (near Safford, AZ) identified as a new potential refugium after removal of **Tilapia mossambica**.
   - Non-native/Quitobaquito-origin pupfish populations persisting in Upper San Pedro/Santa Cruz drainages compromise native subspecies reintroduction; Research Ranch (Audubon Society) resisting renovation of Findley Tank due to concerns for both the resident pupfish and a **Mexican Garter Snake** population.
   - Team plans escalation via Desert Fishes Council resolution and multi-agency letters (BLM, USFS, USFWS, AGFD) if Research Ranch does not cooperate.
   - Bog Hole and three adjacent tanks (Fort Huachuca area) recommended for renovation due to known exotics; recommendation originally made seven years prior, now considered urgent.
   - Fort Huachuca ponds suspected to contain mixed Gila/Quitobaquito hybrid stocks, reinforcing urgency of exotic/hybrid removal.

4. **Aravaipa Creek Watershed**: Team recommends coordinated multi-agency meeting (BLM, USFWS, AGFD, Whittel Trust) to plan renovation removing non-native fishes from BLM and private tanks.

5. **[[Lepidomeda vittata]] / [[Tiaroga cobitis]] / [[Meda fulgida]]** status:
   - *Lepidomeda vittata* listing package finalized for publication as final rule with designated critical habitat; associated USBR water transfer project (Chevelon Creek to Lake Mary) currently on hold.
   - *Tiaroga cobitis* listing status pending in Washington; critical habitat designation deferred indefinitely.
   - Team decided to pursue a **combined Meda/Tiaroga recovery plan**, consistent with a community-level (rather than single-species) recovery philosophy; Mexican Gila basin populations to be resurveyed; Propst assigned lead responsibility.

6. **[[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado Pikeminnow)**: AGFD's proposed lower Colorado River sport fishery must not be allowed to cause genetic "domestication"; a genetic management plan is required, and AGFD is urged to retain all large squawfish currently held at Bubbling Ponds/Page Springs hatchery.

7. **[[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail)**: Concerns raised over genetic bottlenecking from uncontrolled pond spawning at Dexter; recommendation for pedigree tracking (via brands/individual ID) and manual spawning protocols. Concern also noted regarding premature classification of the species as "effectively extinct" in the upper Colorado River basin, which could undermine recovery funding/efforts.

8. **[[Gila nigrescens]] (Chihuahua Chub)**: Reintroduction into Bear Canyon planned as experimental/nonessential population; Team recommends replacing volunteer-pond-reproduced stock with manually spawned cohorts to maximize heterozygosity; letter to be sent recommending negotiations for reintroduction onto NMDGF property on the Mimbres River.

9. **Meda/Tiaroga Research Program**: Two-state (AZ/NM) cooperative agreement using Section 6 funds nearing finalization, to study these species' interactions with invasive **Notropis lutrensis** (Red Shiner).

10. **[[Xyrauchen texanus]] (Razorback Sucker)**: Identified as a potential future listing candidate; Team recommends continued recruitment monitoring past 1990 MOU expiration and interim stocking of renovated Colorado River backwater habitats.
