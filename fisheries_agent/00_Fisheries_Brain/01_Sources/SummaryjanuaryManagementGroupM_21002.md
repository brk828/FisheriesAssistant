# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program Management Group Meeting Summary - January 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #conservation-management #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a meeting summary and associated correspondence from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] Management Group meeting held January 11-12, 1989, in Lakewood, Colorado. The Recovery Program addresses four endangered/candidate fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The meeting focused on FY-89 work plan modifications, water rights acquisition strategies (particularly [[Juniper-Cross Mountain]] water rights on the [[Yampa River]]), program funding mechanisms through the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]], and coordination of biological opinions under [[Section 7 Consultation]] of the Endangered Species Act. Total program cost is estimated at $58.5 million over 15 years, with contributions from water project proponents ($10 per acre-foot of depletion) and federal appropriations.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program was established by Cooperative Agreement executed January 21-22, 1988, by the Governors of Utah, Wyoming, and Colorado, the Administrator of [[Western Area Power Administration]] (WAPA), and the Secretary of the Interior. The program targets recovery of three federally listed endangered fish species ([[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]]) and one candidate species ([[Razorback Sucker]]) inhabiting the Colorado River system. The Implementation Committee oversees program execution and includes representatives from the three states, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), [[Bureau of Reclamation]], WAPA, water development interests, and conservation groups.

The program structure includes a Management Group that reviews technical recommendations and prepares work plans, and a Technical Group that ranks projects based on consistency with recovery goals, urgency, likelihood of success, and relationship to priority work. The FY-89 approved work plan totaled $2,324,700, covering program management, habitat management, habitat development, stocking, nonnative fish management, research/monitoring, and other activities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The endangered Colorado River fishes face threats from habitat modification, flow alterations, and interactions with nonnative species. Key ecological studies approved in the FY-89 work plan include:

- **Habitat Management**: [[Flaming Gorge]] operations studies ($230,000), [[Yampa River]] flow recommendations ($140,500), Colorado River flow recommendations ($133,000), backwater mapping at Flaming Gorge ($183,000), trophic dynamics ($105,000), and sediment dynamics studies ($20,000 for Flaming Gorge; $44,600 for [[Little Snake River]]).

- **Flow Protection**: Efforts to deliver and protect [[Ruedi Reservoir]] releases through the 15-mile reach, including potential use of the [[Orchard Mesa Power Plant]] to supplement flows when discharge falls below approximately 622 cfs. The 15-mile reach gains 300-350 cfs from irrigation returns by the confluence with the [[Gunnison River]].

- **Propagation and Stocking**: Razorback sucker propagation/rearing on the [[Green River]] ($27,000), bonytail introduction ($72,000), implementation of propagation/genetic plan ($129,500), and Colorado squawfish/razorback sucker augmentation planning ($27,000).

- **Nonnative Fish Interactions**: Studies on game fish/endangered fish interactions ($75,000), [[Kenney Reservoir]] sportfishery management ($28,000), and creel surveys in Colorado and Utah ($55,000).

Water rights acquisition focuses on the [[Juniper-Cross Mountain]] project on the Yampa River. A public hearing in Craig, Colorado (January 3, 1989) drew 75-100 attendees with divided opinions: one-third supported maintaining the Yampa as free-flowing, one-third supported reservoir development. The City of Craig adopted a position supporting the Recovery Program while requesting construction of a 100,000 acre-foot reservoir on a Yampa tributary and long-term use (lease) of Juniper-Cross water rights for instream flow rather than outright purchase.

## Key Findings

**FY-89 Work Plan Modifications**: The Management Group approved Technical Group recommendations to address a $268,000 budget shortfall. Deferred projects included: Issue Paper on Habitat Development Options ($10,000), Bonytail Retrieval Program ($2,000), Issue Paper on Exotic Fish Management ($10,000), Establish Recovery Goals ($30,000), and Razorback Management Plan ($10,000). Significant budget increases occurred for FWS Program Management (from $70,000 to $157,000, including $40,000 in-kind services and $47,000 for assistant/secretarial support), Little Snake sediment dynamics (from $23,000 to $44,600), and Yampa River flow recommendations (from $72,500 to $140,500 for Phase II studies). Colorado River flow recommendations decreased from $176,500 to $133,000, with reduced emphasis below the Gunnison confluence pending completion of Yampa studies.

**Water Rights and Instream Flow**: The [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB) planned a preliminary reconnaissance survey of Yampa River basin reservoir sites by March 1989. Local interests preferred long-term lease over outright sale of water rights, though FWS expressed skepticism about leases due to lack of permanent legal protection and difficulty securing Congressional approval. The Bureau of Reclamation offered to assist evaluating/constructing alternative sites through its General Investigations program ($62,000 for appraisal-level survey of 15-mile reach instream flow options). [[The Nature Conservancy]] prepared a request-for-proposal to evaluate yield of conditional water rights on the Little Snake River (cost not to exceed $50,000). Concern emerged about how instream flows for endangered fishes would affect administration of Colorado's compact entitlement.

**Financial Mechanisms**: Three draft versions of a Cooperative Agreement between FWS and the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] were circulated for comment (due January 20, 1989). The versions differed in scope: (1) only new contributed/donated funds to Foundation; (2) new and existing contributed/donated funds to Foundation, Congressional appropriations remain with Service; (3) all funds to Foundation. The Foundation would maintain funds in interest-bearing accounts (2% administrative overhead), with interest accruing for recovery purposes. Water project proponents contribute $10 per acre-foot based on average annual depletion (10% due upon completion of federal action, balance due at construction commencement).

**Section 7 Consultations**: The [[Sandstone Project]] Biological Opinion was delayed over sediment impact issues. FWS committed to consulting with the Management Group/Implementation Committee before making unilateral decisions about sediment handling. Formal consultation on the [[Muddy Creek]] project was expected soon. The [[Uintah Mountain Club]] filed notice of intent to sue FWS; the Service indicated it might request support from other Recovery Program participants if litigation materialized.

**San Juan River Coordination**: FWS and Bureau of Reclamation planned to meet with New Mexico in early February 1989 to discuss their participation in developing an endangered fish management plan for the [[San Juan River]]. The San Juan River Management Plan completion was delayed from March 1, 1989, to September 30, 1989, with New Mexico Game and Fish Department continuing data collection through FY-89 (final report due January 1990 at FY-90 cost of $20,000).

**Hatchery Feasibility**: CWCB considered requesting $250,000 from the State legislature for an endangered fishes hatchery feasibility study. The Management Group agreed Colorado would receive credit under Section 5.3.3 of the Recovery Program only if the feasibility study was identified as a priority need through the Propagation Plan, with credit extent determined by the Implementation Committee based on report quality/usefulness and demonstrated hatchery need.

**Program Coordination**: A public hearing on the Recovery Program in Rock Springs, Wyoming (December 20, 1988) was attended by 12 people and included written testimony from Governor Sullivan and ex-Governor Herschler. The next Management Group meeting was scheduled for March 15, 1989, at the FWS Regional Office, with an Implementation Committee meeting set for February 21, 1989.
