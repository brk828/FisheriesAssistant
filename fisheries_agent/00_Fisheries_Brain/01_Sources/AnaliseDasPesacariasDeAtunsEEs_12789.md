# Tuna and Billfish Longline Fishery off Recife, Tropical Atlantic, 1963

#ichthyology #marine-biology #scombridae

## Executive Summary

This Portuguese-language fisheries analysis (Barros and Fonseca, published 1965 in the *Boletim do Estado da Pesca*) reports the 1963 catch data of tuna longliners based at [[Recife]], [[Pernambuco]], [[Brazil]]. It continues earlier work covering 1961–1962 and assesses the fishing grounds, species composition, abundance and productivity by quarter. The main conclusion is a decline of 35% in the relative abundance index and 27% in productivity compared with the 1961/62 average. The fleet operated only in Areas 1 and 2 and, for the first time, fished south of 15°S, reaching 22°S.

## Background & Description

The study extends the authors' 1961 research on the occurrence, fishing areas and abundance of tunas and related species in the [[Tropical Atlantic]], as well as the productivity of [[Japanese Tuna Longliners]] operating from Recife. The data come from the logbooks of Japanese longliners chartered to [[COPESBRA]] (Companhia de Pesca Norte do Brasil S/A), successor to INBRAPE, and from observations by technicians of the [[SUDENE]] fisheries resources division aboard the same vessels. The methods follow those ratified at the IV National Meeting of Technicians in Marine Fisheries Research (Recife, 1963).

The fleet comprised two medium-sized tuna longliners, the [[Kaiko Maru]] No. 12 and the [[Sakiyoshi Maru]] No. 8. Two-thirds of their crews were Brazilian. Fishing methods were unchanged since 1956.

Target and associated species were:

- [[Albacora Branca]] (*Thunnus alalunga*), the dominant species
- [[Yellowfin Tuna]] (*T. albacares*) and [[Bluefin Tuna]] (*T. thynnus*), recorded together as "Atum e Albacora Laje"
- [[Bigeye Tuna]] (*T. obesus*), called Albacora Bandolim
- Billfishes (Makairidae, Istiophoridae, Xiphiidae), called Agulhões
- [[Skipjack Tuna]] (*Katsuwonus pelamis*) and [[Dolphinfish]] (*Coryphaena hippurus*), grouped as "Outras espécies" or noted as correlated species

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fishing effort was concentrated in two areas. Area 1 was the main ground in the first and fourth quarters, and fishing there extended south to 22°S (the Conclusions give 23°S for the fourth quarter). In the second and third quarters, operations were split between Areas 1 and 2. Vessels moved from Area 2 back toward Area 1 late in the third quarter, where albacore abundance rose sharply.

Species composition shifted seasonally. Albacore dominated in Area 1, especially in the fourth quarter, when it made up about 2/3 of catches. Yellowfin and bluefin tuna were most common in the second and third quarters. Bigeye tuna was rare in the fourth quarter in Area 1. Billfish occurrence rose in 1963, and the authors describe it as about three times higher than in the previous biennium.

The study also notes a persistent problem with catch records: yellowfin and bluefin tuna were pooled in the COPESBRA logbooks, so their catches cannot be separated.

## Key Findings

**Overall results (all quarters and areas combined)**

- Relative abundance index (catch per 100 hooks, as given in the Conclusions): about 5 fish per 100 hooks.
- Species indices per 100 hooks: albacore 2.12; yellowfin plus bluefin tuna 1.01; billfishes 0.83; bigeye tuna 0.53; others 0.44.
- Mean productivity: 216.3 kg per 100 hooks (the Summary reports 2163 kg, a typographical error).
- Mean weight of landed fish (gutted and headless, all species combined): 43.1 kg.
- Compared with 1961/62, abundance fell 35% and productivity fell 27%. Average landed weight was slightly higher than in 1961/62.

Notes:
- The 1st quarter showed the largest decline in abundance (from 11.0 to 5.2). Abundance fell 36% in the 2nd quarter.
- The 1st and 4th quarter catches were dominated by albacore (over 50% in the 1st quarter; about 63% in the 4th).
- Fish were larger in 1963 in several quarters, which offset some of the drop in productivity.

**Other findings**

- The fleet fished in only two of the three areas under exploitation.
- Fishing trips lasted longer than in previous years.
- Fishing south of 15°S had not occurred before, and the authors describe it as unprecedented for vessels based at Recife.
- Billfish occurrence increased sharply, which helped keep average weight from falling further.
- The authors conclude that the 1961/62 finding of a decline in this fishery is confirmed.
