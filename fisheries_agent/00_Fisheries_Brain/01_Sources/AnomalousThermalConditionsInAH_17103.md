## Executive Summary

This 1968 paper by Cole and Minckley is primarily a **limnological study** documenting anomalous inverse thermal stratification in two small, artificial hypersaline pools near [[Laguna Salada]] in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, México. The pools, created for salt harvesting, exhibited an extreme temperature gradient (surface 24.0°C, bottom 47.0°C at 46 cm) driven by salinity-induced density stratification rather than typical thermal stratification. While the core focus is water chemistry and thermal dynamics, the paper is ecologically notable for documenting **three fish taxa** — two [[Gambusia]] species and an undescribed [[Cyprinodon]] species — inhabiting the upper water column of these extreme hypersaline, thermally stratified habitats, underscoring the physiological tolerance of desert-fish endemics to extreme abiotic conditions.

## Background & Description

No morphological data on the fishes themselves are presented in this paper; it is a physical limnology study. The only taxonomic information given is species identity:

- **[[Gambusia marshi]]** (Minckley and Craddock) — a poeciliid species native to the Cuatro Ciénegas basin.
- **[[Gambusia longispinis]]** (Minckley) — a second, co-occurring poeciliid species from the same basin.
- **Undescribed *[[Cyprinodon]]* sp.** — a pupfish (Cyprinodontidae) present in the pools at the time of sampling, not yet formally described.

All three taxa were observed occupying the **upper water layer** of the pool, which was cooler (~24°C surface) and less saline (14.63 g/L TDS) relative to the extreme conditions of the lower strata (47°C, 111.26 g/L TDS at bottom).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Small (~3.5 x 5.0 m), man-made, vertical-sided salt-harvesting pools, 45–50 cm deep, hydrologically connected to [[Laguna Salada]], within the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] — a globally significant desert wetland endemism hotspot.
- **Substrate**: Pool bottoms matted with blue-green algae (cyanobacteria).
- **Thermal microhabitat**: Fish were restricted to the upper water column, avoiding the extreme bottom temperatures (up to 47°C) and hypersaline bottom water (specific gravity 1.075; ~111 g/L TDS).
- **Salinity gradient**: Surface water specific gravity 1.01 (14.63 g/L TDS) vs. bottom 1.075 (111.26 g/L TDS) — a steep vertical salinity/density gradient that, combined with temperature, produced a stable, inversely stratified system (density overriding thermal buoyancy effects).
- **Co-occurrence context**: This inverse thermal stratification phenomenon links to similar anomalous profiles documented in [[Long-H Ranch Ponds]] (Arizona) and [[Hot Lake]] (Washington), and saline lakes of Saskatchewan (e.g., [[Manito Lake]]), suggesting that such extreme, layered hypersaline habitats may support similarly specialized, thermally-tolerant fish assemblages elsewhere in arid-zone endorheic systems.
- **Broader basin significance**: The Cuatro Ciénegas basin is well known for high levels of aquatic endemism, including numerous Gambusia and Cyprinodon species/populations restricted to isolated spring-fed and saline habitats — this pool represents one such microhabitat within that larger mosaic.

## Key Findings

- The pool exhibited a **simple, inverse thermal stratification** with an extreme gradient: surface 24.0°C to bottom 47.0°C over 46 cm (mean rate 0.5°C/cm; peak 0.8°C/cm between 15.5–30.5 cm depth) — among the steepest gradients reported for natural or artificial ponds, comparable to [[Hot Lake]] (0.39°C/cm) and [[Long-H Ranch Ponds]] (0.6°C/cm).
- The stratification was **stabilized by a strong vertical salinity/density gradient**, not by temperature: computed "theoretical" density (if governed by temperature alone assuming pure water) suggested instability (negative stability = −1.6 g·cm/cm²), but actual dissolved-solids concentrations reversed this, producing a physically stable system with denser, saltier, hotter water beneath lighter, cooler, less-saline water.
- Total dissolved solids: **14.63 g/L (surface)** vs. **111.26 g/L (bottom)** — nearly an 8-fold increase with depth.
- Specific gravity measurements (surface 1.01 vs. theoretical pure-water 0.997 at 24°C; bottom 1.075 vs. theoretical 0.989 at 47°C) confirm that dissolved solutes, not temperature, controlled water density and stratification stability.
- Three fish taxa — **[[Gambusia marshi]]**, **[[Gambusia longispinis]]**, and an undescribed **[[Cyprinodon]]** species — were found living exclusively in the cooler, less saline upper water layer, implying behavioral avoidance of the lethal thermal/saline bottom conditions and highlighting fine-scale habitat partitioning within an extreme, spatially compressed hypersaline microhabitat.
- The study contributes to a broader body of work (e.g., [[Cole, Whiteside & Brown 1967]]; [[Minckley & Cole 1968]]) documenting **unusual monomictic and anomalous thermal regimes** in saline Southwestern North American desert waters, relevant to understanding the physiological limits and habitat selection of endemic desert fishes.
