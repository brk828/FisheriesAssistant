#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 study by David J. Behmer investigates the spawning periodicity and reproductive biology of the [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*), a catostomid fish collected from the Des Moines River, Iowa, during summer and fall 1964. The study examined sex ratio, size/age at maturity, and detailed ovarian egg-size dynamics to test whether the species is an intermittent (multiple) spawner within a single season, a hypothesis originally proposed by Starrett (1948). Behmer found a significant female-biased sex ratio, confirmed spawning begins in early June and can extend into August, and identified three distinct size classes of ova in the ovaries. While evidence strongly suggested a single dominant spawning event per season for most fish, anomalous individuals with residual large eggs left open the possibility of repeat spawning, and the question remained unresolved.

## Background & Description

- **Ova size classes**: Three distinct size groups of eggs were identified in ripening ovaries:
  - **Small/transparent ova**: Yolkless, transparent, most numerous; diameter range ~0.07–0.8 mm (mode ~0.15 mm). Become yellow/light tan at ~0.3 mm and opaque at ~0.4 mm due to yolk formation (per Cunningham 1895).
  - **Intermediate ova**: Distinguishable from large ova only once the large ova exceeded ~0.5 mm average diameter.
  - **Large ova**: The maturing/spawning cohort; near-maturity ova reached diameters up to ~1.2 mm in some residual cases.
- **Ova measurement technique**: Diameters measured along a horizontal axis using a scale projector at 44X magnification (following Van Oosten, Deason, and Jobes methodology); formalin preservation may cause slight shrinkage (per Vladykov 1956), though Clark (1931) found no such effect in California sardine.
- **Ovary weight–egg diameter relationship**: A strong log-linear relationship was found:
  - log(ovary wt as % body wt) = −0.84 + 2.78 × log(egg diameter × 44), r² = 0.86.
- **Spatial homogeneity**: Egg size and proportions were consistent across anterior, middle, and posterior sections of the ovary (unlike [[Northern Pike]], *Esox lucius*, where Carbine 1943 found middle ova slightly larger).
- **Size at maturity**: Smallest ripening female measured 8.9 inches total length.
- **Age at maturity**: No females younger than age IV (determined via scale analysis) were found ripening or ripe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Riverine freshwater environment of the Des Moines River, Iowa; fish were collected via electric shocking (230-volt AC generator operated from a boat).
- **Collection period**: Early June through end of September, 1964.
- **Sex ratio**: Of 452 individuals, 251 were female and 201 were male — a significant deviation from 1:1 (pooled chi-square = 5.53, P < 0.025), with females predominating throughout the sampling period (heterogeneity chi-square not significant, P = 0.25), indicating a consistent female bias across dates.
- **Reproductive strategy context**: The study engages with the broader question of intermittent (batch) spawning in fishes, drawing comparisons to [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) atretic egg resorption behavior described by Vladykov (1956), and referencing sex-based differences in ripening timing noted broadly in fish (Hoar 1957) — male carpsuckers were found to ripen earlier in the season than females.
- **Seasonal spawning behavior**: Spawning activity was tracked via percentage of ripe/partially ripe females and mean ovary weight (as % body weight) across sampling dates (Table 3).

## Key Findings

1. **Sex ratio skew**: Significant female predominance (251F:201M) in the Des Moines River population, consistent across sampling dates.
2. **Spawning timing**: Spawning was underway by early June, peaked mid-June to early July, tapered through late July, and had largely ended by month's end — though two ripe females were still found in mid-August, indicating no sharply defined spawning peak and asynchronous ripening among females.
3. **Male ripening precedes female**: Ripe males (identified by milt extrusion) were common through June but became negligible by mid-July, ripening earlier than females — consistent with patterns reported in other fish species.
4. **Maturity threshold**: No carpsuckers younger than age IV were found sexually mature; minimum mature body length was 8.9 inches.
5. **Larger/older females ripen earliest**: Mean weights of ripening females declined from early June (551 g) through mid-July (247 g), suggesting the largest, oldest females spawn first in the season.
6. **Three-tier ova structure discovered**: All ripening ovaries contained transparent (yolkless), intermediate, and large ova; the intermediate ova, initially hypothesized as a reserve for a second spawning, instead showed a strong negative correlation with large egg maturity (r = −0.88, P < 0.025), supporting their identity as **atretic (degenerating) eggs** rather than a reserve batch — analogous to Vladykov's (1956) "safety valve" hypothesis in brook trout.
7. **Ambiguous evidence for repeat spawning**: Three female carpsuckers were found with only a few large residual eggs (~1.2 mm diameter) alongside a fresh batch of smaller ripening eggs, suggestive of a second spawning event within the season. However, whether these residual eggs derived from an earlier spawning that same year or were retained unresorbed from the previous year (as documented in brook trout by Vladykov) could not be conclusively determined.
8. **Post-spawning ovarian state**: By late July, nearly all examined ovaries contained only transparent eggs, marking the effective end of the spawning season; by September, some ovaries showed a new developing mode (up to ~0.5 mm), interpreted as preparation for the following year's spawning cycle.
9. **Open question**: The paper concludes that the intermittent spawning hypothesis of Starrett (1948) remains unresolved for *[[Carpiodes carpio]]*, calling for further study to determine whether river carpsuckers spawn once or multiple times per season.
