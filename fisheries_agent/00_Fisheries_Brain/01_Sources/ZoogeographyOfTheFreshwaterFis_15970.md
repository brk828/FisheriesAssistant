# Freshwater Fish Zoogeography of Iran and the Levant

#ichthyology #marine-biology #zoogeography

## Executive Summary

This compiled document contains three parts. Two are full or near-full papers on freshwater fish zoogeography: B.W. Coad's analysis of the freshwater ichthyofauna of Iran, and a partial paper by F. Krupp on the freshwater fishes of the Levant. The third is a fragment of a lepidopteran paper by C.M. Naumann (moth genus *Zygaena*), reduced here to its reference list and an Iranian distribution note. Coad's paper is the main body of the document. It is based on more than 900 collections and divides Iran into 19 drainage basins. Its central finding is that Iran's freshwater fish fauna is dominated by three families (Cyprinidae, Gobiidae and Cobitidae), which together make up 73.6% of species. Distributions are explained by Caspian Sea transgressions, drainage captures, river highways, marine penetrations and, to a lesser degree, human introduction.

## Background & Description

**Iran (Coad).** Coad argues that freshwater fishes are especially useful for zoogeography because they cannot cross marine barriers and move slowly between basins, so their distributions record geographic history. Iran is mostly Palaearctic but contains Ethiopian and Oriental elements. Earlier surveys were fragmentary: [[Bianco]] and Berg's classic 1949 work relied on collections made about 50 years earlier, and Coad's material comes mainly from his own three-year residence in Iran plus museum collections and the M.S. thesis of Saadati (1977). Coad treats the Caspian Sea (about one-third seawater salinity away from river mouths) as part of Iran's fauna for completeness. He also includes [[Qanat]] collections because they reflect basin ichthyofaunas.

**Levant (Krupp).** The Levant is a strip about 200 km wide along the eastern Mediterranean, from the Taurus Mountains to the Gulf of Aqaba. Its topography is shaped by a N-S faulting system (the Levantine graben) drained by the Orontes, Litani and Jordan river systems. A revision based on about 12,000 specimens found that of roughly 130 nominal species recorded from the region, only 58 are valid, and seven undescribed species were found. About 40% of published records were based on misidentified material.

**Zygaena (Naumann).** The Naumann fragment is about moth distribution, not fish. It notes that *Zygaena tamara* and certain mimetic insects occur together in one part of Iran, but that similar conditions are unknown in Nakhichevan (USSR) and SW Iran. *Z. seitzi* is known only from yellow/red specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Aridity and isolation:** Iran is arid or semi-arid outside the Caspian coastal strip. Many rivers do not flow continuously to their terminal basins, and springs isolate populations for long periods. Coad suggests that descriptions of species or subspecies from few specimens of one locality may be doubtful.
- **Salinity tolerance:** Some southern streams and saline inland waters hold Cyprinidae and Cobitidae, which are normally considered primary freshwater fishes. Coad notes that these fish do not regularly enter marine waters. [[Aphanius Dispar]] and [[Aphanius Sophiae]] are salt-tolerant, and Coad notes that [[Heteropneustes Fossilis]] tolerates seawater (25% mortality at 10.25 ppt).
- **Qanats:** These are artificial horizontal wells in alluvial fans that provide constant flow. Falling groundwater may leave qanats as the only fish habitat.
- **Caspian basin:** Brackish and tied to Black Sea and Mediterranean faunas, with endemic [[Gobiidae]] and [[Clupeidae]].
- **Man:** Coad judges that human movement of fish has been mostly intrabasin rather than interbasin, though it may explain the recent appearance of Anguillidae and Heteropneustidae in Iran.

## Key Findings

**Iran: diversity and composition (Coad)**
- Three families (Cyprinidae, Gobiidae, Cobitidae) make up 73.6% of species. Ostariophysi make up 63.2%.
- Cyprinidae has the greatest diversity: 43.7% of genera (about 31 genera) and 45.2% of species (70 species in Table 1).
- 24 families are reported. Fifteen (62.5%) have a single species, and 20 (83.3%) have four or fewer species. Iran has 10 primary freshwater families against 15 for the Palaearctic. The Palaearctic has 39 freshwater families in total.
- [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Cobitidae]] occur in all 19 basins. [[Cyprinodontidae]] occurs in 10 basins (52.6%).
- The Caspian basin has 15 families (62.5%), and the Tigris basin has 11 (45.8%). Endorheic basins have only 2–4 families.

**Iran: notable taxa**
- *Caspiomyzon wagneri* is the only lamprey and is endemic to the Caspian basin.
- *Acipenseridae* (four sturgeon species) are restricted to the Caspian basin.
- *Anguilla anguilla* occurs only in the Caspian basin, where its arrival is recent, linked to the Volga-Baltic waterway.
- *Tenualosa ilisha* is a regular marine migrant that spawns in southern Iranian rivers. Of the Caspian Clupeidae, eight species are in *Alosa* and *Clupeonella*.
- *Chanos chanos* is known from a single southern stream draining to the Hormuz Strait.
- [[Barilius Mesopotamicus]] is restricted to the Tigris and Gulf basins.
- *Pseudophoxinus persidis* (Coad 1981) is known only from the Kor, Gulf and Hormuz basins.
- *Cobitis linea* occurs in the Kor and Hormuz basins. Its putative relative *C. simplicispina* is in Anatolia.
- *Iranocichla hormuzensis* is a cichlid endemic to streams draining to the Hormuz Strait.
- Coad recognises 4 species of *Aphanius* in Iran. *A. dispar* lives along the Persian Gulf shore and *A. sophiae* in inland hypersaline water. The two co-occur in the Tigris basin.
- *Salmo trutta* is the only native salmonid, found in the Caspian, Reza'iye and Namak basins.

**Iran: zoogeographical events (Coad)**
1. **[[Caspian Sea Transgressions]]:** Isolation and speciation, with some northern elements arriving via the Volga.
2. **[[Drainage Capture]]:** Common in an active orogenic zone. *Capoeta aculeata* and *Aphanius sophiae* are spread across several basins this way. *Alburnoides bipunctatus* may have entered by two routes.
3. **River highways:** Schizothoracine snowtrouts in the Sistan basin likely came down the Helmand River from the Hindu Kush. The Tedzen basin's single schizothoracine may have arrived by a similar route.
4. **[[Marine Penetration]]:** Euryhaline species (*Tenualosa ilisha*, Chanidae, *Aphanius dispar*, some Gobiidae, possibly Mugilidae) reached southern Iran through the sea.
5. **Human influence:** Judged minor for natural distributions.
- Coad proposes that the Kor basin fauna mainly came from the Tigris basin, either by headwater capture or via the Gulf basin when sea levels were about 120 m lower, roughly 18,000 years ago.

**Iran: unresolved problems (Coad)**
- *Barilius mesopotamicus*, Bagridae, Sisoridae and Mastacembelidae show large disjunctions from their relatives in the Indus basin. The Gulf basin lacks Bagridae and Sisoridae, which occur in the Tigris basin, and the reason is unexplained.
- Species such as *Pseudophoxinus persidis* and *Cobitis linea* need selective population loss to explain their gaps. Pleistocene climate change may be responsible.

**Levant (Krupp, abstract only)**
- 65 primary and secondary freshwater fish species occur in the Levant, grouped into ten ichthyogeographical subprovinces.
- The Jordan and the upper, central and lower Orontes were colonised separately through branches of the Mesopotamian river system.
- Faunal elements moved from the Jordan to the Orontes via the Litani, with no evidence of the reverse.
- The Jordan basin is a centre of speciation for lineages with Palaearctic affinities.
- During the Pleistocene, fish were exchanged between the Jordan-Dead Sea basin and the Hejaz in both directions.

**Limitations noted by Coad**
- Iran's fish fauna is not completely surveyed, and systematic work at population and subspecies level is incomplete. Coad expects the broad distribution patterns to hold.
