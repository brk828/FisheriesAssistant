# Contemporary Status of Amu Darya Shovelnose Sturgeons and the Karyotype of Danube Stellate Sturgeon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae #pseudoscaphirhynchus #acipenser-stellatus

## Executive Summary

This document contains two papers from *The Sturgeon Quarterly* (Vol. 4, No. 3, July 1996).

1. **Status of two Amu Darya shovelnose sturgeons.** Salnikov, Birstein and Mayden assessed the [[Pseudoscaphirhynchus Kaufmanni]] and [[Pseudoscaphirhynchus Hermanni]] populations in the middle reaches of the [[Amu Darya River]] during expeditions in February and April 1996. *P. kaufmanni* was found to be numerous during its spawning period, with two morphs (normal and dwarf). Three *P. hermanni* were captured, showing that the species, previously regarded as extinct, is extremely rare rather than extinct.
2. **Karyotype of Danube stellate sturgeon.** Suciu and Ene described the karyotype of [[Acipenser Stellatus]] from the [[Danube River]] basin, consisting of 118±2 chromosomes and NF = 186±2, with two pairs of NOR-bearing chromosomes.

## Background & Description

### Shovelnose sturgeons of Central Asia
- The three Central Asian shovelnose species of the genus *Pseudoscaphirhynchus* have a unique morphology among sturgeons. Together with three North American *Scaphirhynchus* species, they form the subfamily Scaphirhynchinae.
- Two endemic freshwater species historically inhabited the Amu Darya: the large Amu-Dar shovelnose sturgeon (*P. kaufmanni* Bogdanov, 1874) and the small Amu-Dar shovelnose sturgeon (*P. hermanni* Kessler, 1877).
- Conservation status: both species were listed as endangered in the Uzbek SSR Red Data Book (1983), USSR Red Data Book (1984) and Turkmen SSR Red Data Book (1985). For the 1996 [[IUCN Red List]], *P. kaufmanni* was proposed as Endangered and *P. hermanni* as Critically Endangered.

### Stellate sturgeon karyology
- Sturgeon karyotypes are difficult to study because of the high number of very small microchromosomes.
- Eight European sturgeon species had been karyotyped. Of the four sturgeon species in the lower Danube (*A. gueldenstaedti*, *A. ruthenus*, *A. stellatus*, *Huso huso*), only the Danubian sterlet (*A. ruthenus*) had previously been studied.
- Earlier karyology of *A. stellatus* was limited to individuals from the [[Volga River]] population (Birstein and Vasiliev, 1987).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Anthropogenic drying of the Aral Sea.** The Amu Darya had flowed into the [[Aral Sea]] for about 6,000 years, but by 1996 it no longer reached the sea. The source attributes this to anthropogenic evaporation of the Aral Sea over the previous 30 years.
- **Habitat change.** Prior to the 1960s, *P. kaufmanni* inhabited the Amu Darya from its sources to its mouth, along with small irrigation channels and the [[Karakum Canal]] system. Between 1989 and 1991, it was found mainly between Kerki and Chardzhou. Sturgeons were concentrated near the towns of Kerki, Chardzhou and Ildzhik.
- **Study site.** Both 1996 expeditions worked near Kerki, between Tashrabad and Khalch (about 100 km of river). Sturgeon were caught in channels near small sand islands, with slower current than the main riverbed, over clean sand or sand-and-clay substrate.
- **Spawning season and temperature.** The spawning period is March to April. Water temperature rose from 13 °C to 21 °C between 8 and 23 April 1996.
- **Sampling gear.** Drift nets 65 and 55 m long and 2 m high, with 38 or 50 mm mesh, were fished non-selectively at 0.5–2.5 m depth. Nets were set across the river and allowed to drift 50–150 m.
- **Stellate sturgeon sampling.** Juveniles were taken in net traps set in the [[Black Sea]] near shore, north of the mouth of the St. George branch of the Danube, at Buival, in September 1995.

## Key Findings

### Paper 1: Amu Darya shovelnose sturgeons (Salnikov, Birstein & Mayden)

**Expedition effort and catch**
- **February 1996:** 8 sets with a 38 mm-mesh net caught 10 individuals of *P. kaufmanni* (total length 93–380 mm, as cited from Zholdasova, 1997; Table 1 lists 170–480 mm TL).
- **April 1996:** 107 sets were made (20 with 50 mm mesh, 87 with 38 mm mesh). 121 *P. kaufmanni* were caught. The catch reached 10 sturgeons per catch effort per net and 50 individuals per day.
- The April catch rate compares with 6–8 individuals per catch effort per net in the late 1960s–early 1970s (Tleuov and Sagitov, 1973). The authors describe *P. kaufmanni* as "rather numerous" at least during spawning.
- 22 of the 121 specimens were retained for study. The rest were released.

**Morphs of *P. kaufmanni***
- Of the 22 retained specimens, 16 were the normal form and 6 were the dwarf form. Dwarf individuals are dark-colored.
- Normal form: males TL 200–555 mm, 24–625 g, 4–10 years; females TL 220–550 mm, 31–220 g, 3–12 years. All normal-form specimens were mature adults (stage II females, stage III–V males).
- Previous studies reported maturity at 6–7 or 7–8 years and TL < 400 mm (Nikolskii, 1938; Tleuov and Sagitov, 1973). The authors suggest that *P. kaufmanni* may now mature earlier under new environmental conditions.
- Dwarf-form comparison (Table 3):

**Morphology and size records of *P. hermanni***
- Three *P. hermanni* were caught, the first captures since the early 1980s (Pavlov et al., 1985).
- Their sizes were 212–275 mm TL and 25.0–50.5 g. The 4–5-year-old fish described by Tleuov and Sagitov (1973) were 188–207 mm TL and 12.7–17.2 g, so these specimens are larger.
- The largest specimen (No. 1) has a standard length of 233 mm. This is the largest known for the species; Nikolskii (1938) listed 210 mm SL as the largest. It is also the largest female, with total length 275 mm and weight 50.5 g.
- Specimen No. 1 is light-colored with a longer snout (preorbital distance 26.1% of fork length) and shorter fins. Specimen No. 2 is dark, with a snout of 23.0% of fork length. The authors suggest there may be two morphs within *P. hermanni*, as in *P. kaufmanni*.
- All morphometric and meristic values for the specimens fell within the limits given by Tleuov and Sagitov (1973).

**Conclusions (as stated by the authors)**
1. *P. kaufmanni* is rather numerous in the middle Amu Darya between Tashrabad and Khalch, Turkmenistan.
2. The *P. kaufmanni* population consists of normal and dwarf morphs that differ in age, color and morphology.
3. *P. hermanni* is not extinct but should be considered extremely rare.
4. Possible light-snouted and dark-snouted morphs exist in *P. hermanni*.
5. Taxonomic clarification of these morphs is critical for conservation and management of both species. Ongoing taxonomic work by Mayden, Kuhajda and Birstein is expected to address this.

**Data discrepancies in the source**
- The text says *P. hermanni* was last recorded in 1982. The conclusions say the three specimens were the first captures "since 1983."
- Conclusion 2 gives the normal female as 550 mm TL, "855 g," and 12 years old. Table 2 gives 544.0 g for that fish, and the 855 value matches the caudal-filament length, not the weight. The source's weight of 855 g should be treated with caution.
- The text describes the dwarf male as 198 mm (215 mm with filament). Table 2 lists fork length 198 mm and total length 215 mm, consistent with that reading.

### Paper 2: Stellate sturgeon karyotype (Suciu & Ene)

- **Sample:** three young-of-the-year *A. stellatus*, collected September 1995.
- **Method:** chromosome slides were prepared from kidney tissue of colchicinized fish, hypotonized in 0.075 M KCl for 25 min. Giemsa and AgAs staining were applied.
- **Karyotype:** 118±2 chromosomes, NF = 186±2, with a high number of microchromosomes.
- **NORs:** three to four NOR-bearing chromosomes per karyotype (one pair of macrochromosomes and one pair of small chromosomes), with NORs in the telomeric region of the long arms.
- **Interpretation:** the results agree with the Volga River karyotype (Birstein and Vasiliev, 1987). Two pairs of NOR-bearing chromosomes support a tetraploid origin of sturgeons (Dingerkus and Howell, 1976; Birstein et al., 1997).
- **Source discrepancy:** the figure caption gives 2n = 120 (Zn = 120), while the text gives 118±2.
