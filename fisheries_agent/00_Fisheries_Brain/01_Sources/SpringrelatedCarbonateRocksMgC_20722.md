# Spring-Related Carbonate Rocks, Mg Clays, and Associated Minerals in Pliocene Deposits of the Amargosa Desert

#geology #sedimentology #clay-mineralogy #pliocene-paleohydrology #amargosa-desert

*Note: This document is a geological research article, not an ichthyological study. No fish species are described; the tags above reflect its actual subject matter.*

## Executive Summary

This Geological Society of America Bulletin article (1986) by Hay, Pexton, Teague and Kyser examines the [[Amargosa Desert]] of Nevada-California, a spring-fed structural basin holding large Pliocene carbonate rocks and [[Magnesium Clays]] deposited in playas, marshland, ponds and flood plains. Basin-fill deposits dated at 2.4 to 3.2 Ma are divided into three lithofacies (Tpa, Tid, Tpl). The authors combine field mapping, mineralogy (X-ray diffraction, TEM, thin sections) and oxygen and carbon isotope analyses to reconstruct depositional environments, diagenesis, water chemistry and paleohydrology. The central finding is that the largest playa, [[East Playa]], was a saline, alkaline sump fed by a mixture of volcanic-terrane runoff and [[Ash Meadows Groundwater System]] spring water, with a shift toward siliceous water near 2.5 Ma.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** An intermontane basin in the western Basin and Range province, draining south into the [[Death Valley]] drainage system. Paleozoic carbonate highlands surround it on the east, south and west; Miocene silicic ignimbrites of the [[Timber Mountain-Oasis Valley Caldera Complex]] border it on the north.
- **Stratigraphy:** Pliocene clay-rich deposits underlie the basin, with exposed thickness up to about 50 m at Fairbanks Butte and up to 295 m penetrated by drilling at Ash Meadows. Overlying unit QTlf is spring-related limestone and tufa; younger Pleistocene-Holocene colluvium and fluvial sediments cap the sequence.
- **Industrial relevance:** Mg clays and bentonite were being mined by Industrial Mineral Ventures, Inc., whose open pits supplied much of the exposure data. The study was funded by the National Science Foundation and the U.S. Geological Survey (Nevada Nuclear Waste Storage Investigations).
- **Prior interpretations:** Earlier workers proposed freshwater lakes, playas, or a saline lake for the sepiolite; this study tests these against the new data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lithofacies and paleoenvironments**

- **Tpa:** Chiefly limestone with subordinate claystone; deposited in spring-fed marshland and ponds bordering the Paleozoic hills. Fossil assemblages (bivalves, gastropods) indicated permanent spring-fed marsh, perennial flowing spring water, and seasonal ponds.
- **Tid:** Montmorillonite claystone and limestone deposited in ponds, marshland and flood plains that were alternately wetted and dried. Its uppermost limestone formed in a shallow lake of at least 15 km² that was intermittently dry and supported vegetation.
- **Tpl:** Almost entirely Mg clays, limestone and dolomite, deposited in playas and associated marshland. Its dominant unit, East Playa (10-15 m exposed, 35 m in Drill Hole B), was about 75-80% claystone and 20-25% carbonate.

**Hydrology and water chemistry**

- The Ash Meadows system discharges roughly 58,000 m³ per day and is relatively rich in Ca, Mg and HCO₃, with SiO₂ generally 20-33 ppm.
- The Oasis Valley-Fortymile Canyon system, fed from northern volcanic terrain, is rich in Na, HCO₃ and SiO₂ (57-80 ppm).
- Sepiolite formed in low-salinity water, Mg smectite in higher-salinity water, and illite plus K-feldspar in saline, alkaline water (pH about 9.0-9.5) in the south-central part of East Playa.

**Climate and sedimentary processes**

- The Pliocene was wetter than today (present mean annual temperature 18 °C; precipitation 8-10 cm/yr; pan evaporation 250 cm/yr). A gastropod, *Gyraulus circumstriatus*, found in Tpa suggests cooler conditions than today.
- Fenestral limestones formed by recrystallization in the vadose zone under a fluctuating water table.
- Caliche-breccia mounds and seepage-mound carbonates formed along fault-controlled ground-water leakage zones.

## Key Findings

**1. Stratigraphy and age**
- Pliocene basin fill dated at 2.4-3.2 Ma is divided into Tpa, Tid and Tpl. Dated tuffs include a biotitic rhyolite near 3.2 Ma and a pyroxene dacite with a zircon fission-track date of 2.1 ± 0.4 Ma.
- The study found no evidence for a large lake comparable to [[Searles Lake]] during 2.5-3.2 Ma.

**2. Clay mineralogy**
- Mg smectite is mainly [[Stevensite]] with interlayered [[Kerolite]]. Most analyses contain a few percent Al₂O₃, interpreted as illite contamination rather than structural Al.
- Relatively pure [[Sepiolite]] is mined from the Dry Lake and Moretti pits; 36 of 49 waxy claystone samples from these pits are sepiolite-dominant.
- Authigenic celadonitic illite (K/Al ratios 0.54-0.61, versus 0.25-0.35 for true illite) and authigenic K-feldspar (present in 31 of 103 East Playa claystones) are concentrated in the south-central part of East Playa, where they are interpreted as reaction products of montmorillonite in saline, alkaline water.
- Phillipsite forms from silicic vitric tuffs in saline, alkaline water. Bentonite at the Ewing and Kinney pits formed by leaching in low-salinity water and appears to be the youngest bentonite recorded in the literature.

**3. Carbonate deposition and dolomitization**
- Dolomite is common in Tpl but absent from Tid. Much of it is replacement dolomite in approximate isotopic equilibrium with the calcite it replaces.
- Dolomite is about 56 mole percent CaCO₃ by the Goldsmith and Graf (1958) method.
- [[Caliche Breccia]] masses, up to 75 m in average diameter and 3 to more than 4.5 m high, are restricted mainly to Carson Slough, with a large mass (3 km long, 600 m wide) at the Hectorite Whiting Pit.

**4. Stable isotopes**

| Tid dense fenestral limestone | +18.2 (Tid range +17.0 to +18.6) | Upper limit for groundwater δ¹⁸O of about −9.2 to −11.3 |

| Mg smectite (relatively pure) | +19.0 (9 samples) | Higher salinity |
| Silica replacing carbonate/clay | +24.1 (range +22.3 to +26.3) | Low-temperature formation (<35 °C) |

**5. Paleohydrology and climate**
- The Pliocene hydrologic system was broadly similar to the present one but with wider spring discharge. Springs were active north of East Playa, where none exist today.
- East Playa was a drainage sump supplied by alkali bicarbonate runoff from the north and Ca-Mg bicarbonate spring water from Ash Meadows.
- Silicification of carbonates along leakage zones reflects a shift toward siliceous, volcanic-sourced groundwater. The authors attribute this, and a similar change at the western caliche breccias, to a shift to drier conditions about 2.5 Ma. However, they state that no direct evidence for this climatic change was found in the Amargosa Desert.

**6. Principal conclusions**
1. The basin was a spring-fed system of playas, marshes and short-lived ponds between 2.1 and 3.2 Ma.
2. Seepage mounds and caliche breccias precipitated along linear discharge zones in playas.
3. Sepiolite precipitated from nonsaline water; Mg smectite from more saline water; illite, K-feldspar and phillipsite formed in saline, alkaline water.
4. Dolomite replacement occurred through a sequence of calcite precipitation, dilution and later dolomite crystallization in near-identical water.
