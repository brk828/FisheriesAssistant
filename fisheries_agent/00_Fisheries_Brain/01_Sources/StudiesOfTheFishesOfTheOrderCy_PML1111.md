# Studies of the Fishes of the Order Cyprinodontes VI: Miscellaneous Notes on American Genera and Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This Miscellaneous Publication of the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], issued 9 July 1926 by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], is the sixth paper in a series revising American cyprinodontoid fishes. It presents taxonomic notes, new genera and species, synonymies and distributional records for the families Cyprinodontidae, Goodeidae and Poeciliidae. Principal outputs are the erection of new genera (Cubanichthys, Leptolucania, Floridichthys, Balsadichthys, Lermichthys, Trigonophallus, Autophallus, Poeciliistes, Darienichthys-related revisions, among others), the description of five new species of Gambusia and related forms, and a proposed revised key to Gambusia subgenera. The work rests on loans from [[Carl Eigenmann]] (Indiana University) and the Field Museum, and on material collected by Elinor Behre, Frederick and Helen Gaige, and T. L. Hankinson.

## Background & Description

The paper continues earlier numbers of the Miscellaneous Publications series (Nos. 13 and an occasional paper, No. 148). The introductory note names the museum's founders and describes the two publication series: Occasional Papers (brief original papers) and Miscellaneous Publications (monographic and technical studies issued separately with their own title pages).

Taxonomic content follows a systematic order. Subfamily Fundulinae includes genera such as [[Lucania]], [[Fundulus]], [[Cubanichthys]], [[Leptolucania]], [[Chriopeops]] and [[Oxyzygonectes]]. Subfamily Cyprinodontinae covers [[Floridichthys]], [[Jordanella]] and [[Cyprinodon]]. Family Goodeidae includes [[Zoogoneticus]], [[Girardinichthys]], [[Characodon]], [[Lermichthys]], [[Balsadichthys]] and [[Skiffia]]. Family Poeciliidae is divided into Gambusiinae (tribes Gambusiini and Heterandriini) and Poeciliinae/Poeciliopsinae, with many genera revised.

Morphological criteria emphasized include the female urogenital pouch, dentition (uniserial vs. biserial vs. bands), scale counts, fin-ray counts, and the structure of the male gonopodium (anal fin modified for intromission). Coloration patterns are used diagnostically in the Heterandriini (cross-hatched scale pockets with vertical bars) versus Gambusiini (rows of spots).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Specimens were collected from a wide range of freshwater and brackish habitats. Examples noted in the text include:

- Florida: Prairie Creek, ponds near Haines City, Bivens Arm at Gainesville, Punta Rassa, Captiva Pass, Sanibel Island (Tarpon Bay), and the St. Johns River.
- Cuba: Batabanó, Cienfuegas, Santiago de las Vegas, Calabazar.
- Mexico: Río Fuerte-type streams of Tamaulipas and San Luis Potosí (Río Fuerte-region Forlón, Valles, Rascón), Río Pánuco basin, Río Balsas basin, Pecos Valley, Río Conchos.
- Central America: Costa Rica and Panama (Rio Chiriquí del Té, Coen, Almirante Bay, Quebrada Nigra, Talamanca Valley).
- Ponds, marshes, springs and lagoons, including tidal pools at Curaçao and mangrove swamps at Belize.

Hubbs notes ecological associations for some forms: the dorsal fin position and pelvic-fin posture in Lucania and related genera are linked to surface-associated habits; Fundulus heteroclitus populations occur in both marine and freshwater settings.

## Key Findings

- **New genera erected:** Cubanichthys, Leptolucania (raised from subgenus), Floridichthys, Lermichthys, Balsadichthys, Trigonophallus, Autophallus, Poeciliistes, Allogambusia-related revisions, and Arthrophallus, Schizophallus and Heterophallina as new subgenera of Gambusia.
- **New species described:** Gambusia vittata, Gambusia regani, Gambusia panuco and related forms; Trigonophallus punctifer (type from Guibari Creek, Panama; 1 male holotype, paratypes 27–51 mm females). Five new species are named in total in the introduction.
- **Synonymies proposed:** Lucania parva with L. venusta and L. affinis; Lucania ommata with Zygonectes mannii; Fundulus heteroclitus grandis, F. h. fonticola and F. h. berrnudae are treated as subspecies, with F. antillarum probably identical to fonticola; Plancterus zebra is a distinct species from P. kansae, with the Plains "zebra" referred to P. kansae.
- **Key Gambusia revision:** Eight subgenus-level groups are recognized; Gambusia is divided into Heterophallina, Gambusia, Arthrophallus and Schizophallus. G. holbrooki and G. patruelis are separated as distinct species, with G. affinis and G. patruelis shown to have been confused in the Guadalupe-San Antonio system.
- **Nomenclatural decisions:** Xiphophorus gracilis is treated as a doubtful genus and species pending new collections in east-central Mexico; Brachyrhaphis is expanded to include several Central American species formerly placed in Gambusia and Pseudoxiphophorus.
- **Distributional records:** Fundulus heteroclitus grandis recorded from Peli­can Island and Florida; Lucania ommata from Prairie Creek and Haines City, Florida; Gambusia holbrooki from more than 20 Florida and Atlantic localities.
- **Planned work:** Hubbs states intent to revise Cyprinodon and to study the relationship of Plancterus/Fundulus forms along Atlantic coastwise streams; a new expedition into east-central Mexico is called for to resolve Xiphophorus gracilis.
