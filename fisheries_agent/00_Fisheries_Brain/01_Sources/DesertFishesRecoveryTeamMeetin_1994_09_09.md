# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes — September 9, 1994

## Executive Summary
This document records the proceedings of the Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT), a multi-agency body (Arizona State University, USFWS, USFS, BLM, Bureau of Reclamation, AGFD, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, The Nature Conservancy, American Rivers) coordinating recovery of imperiled desert fish species in the southwestern United States and northern Mexico (Rio Yaqui drainage). Key topics include species survey updates (Sonora Chub, longfin dace, Yaqui chub), candidate species listings (five species elevated to Category 2 candidate status), taxonomic uncertainty in the genus *Rhinichthys*/*Tiaroga*/*Apocope*, implementation of the Central Arizona Project (CAP) Biological Opinion (jeopardy determinations for four species), design of nonnative fish exclusion barriers on [[Aravaipa Creek]], conservation agreements as alternatives to ESA listing (e.g., [[Virgin River Spinedace]], [[Wet Canyon Talussnail]]), and organizational changes to recovery team structure under new FWS "July 2" policy.

## Background & Description
The DFRT is responsible for recovery planning and coordination for a suite of desert-adapted fish species, several of which are federally listed or candidates for listing under the Endangered Species Act (ESA). Species under DFRT purview discussed in this meeting include:

- **[[Sonora Chub]]** (*Gila ditaenia*) — subject of proposed transplants into Sycamore Canyon tributaries; surveys located chub in Penasco, Atascosa, and Little Tinaja Canyons.
- **[[Beautiful Shiner]]** — clarified as a DFRT responsibility species (Rio Yaqui fishes group).
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — documented in California Gulch (only U.S. record in the Rio Altar portion) and in Sulphur Springs Valley (Turkey, Rucker drainages), where morphometric and genetic data align populations with the Yaqui form rather than the Gila form.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]**, **[[Sonora Sucker]]**, **[[Little Colorado Sucker]]**, and an undefined unit of **[[Speckled Dace]]** — newly approved as Category 2 candidate species.
- **[[Tiaroga]]** — taxonomic status in flux; R.L. Mayden's forthcoming publication proposes reclassifying *Rhinichthys osculus* as *Apocope osculus*, altering the taxonomic position of *Tiaroga*.
- **[[Spikedace]]** and **[[Loach Minnow]]** — subject of critical habitat litigation; a new loach minnow population was rediscovered in Eagle Creek.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — subject of a revised recovery plan draft, a new NSF-funded nuclear genetics study (Minckley & Hedrick, repeating Vrijenhoek's earlier work), and a documented ESA Section 9 take violation on Sonoita Creek (stream bulldozing).
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — one of four species jeopardized under the CAP Biological Opinion.
- **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*) and **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*) — populations in Turkey Creek (Rio Yaqui basin) are the focus of a proposed private-land habitat conservation arrangement with landowner Joe Austin.
- **[[Virgin River Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*) — subject of a multi-party conservation agreement intended to preclude a listing proposal.
- **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*) — anticipated to be the first DFRT species tested under the new FWS policy requiring failed conservation-agreement attempts before listing proposals proceed.
- **[[Wet Canyon Talussnail]]** — a non-fish invertebrate taxon with a single, entirely Forest Service-managed population, nearing a conservation agreement in lieu of listing.

Nonnative species discussed as threats include **mosquitofish**, **green sunfish**, **bullfrogs**, and **grass carp** (the latter tolerated under a private landowner agreement in Turkey Creek).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat systems**: Sycamore Canyon (Rio Altar/Sonora Chub range), California Gulch, Aravaipa Creek, Sonoita Creek, Turkey Creek (Rio Yaqui basin, Sulphur Springs Valley/Willcox Playa), Virgin River, Eagle Creek, and Gila River tributaries.
- **Nonnative fish invasion dynamics**: Minckley hypothesized that the descending limb of the flood hydrograph represents the most likely window for nonnative fish upstream movement, informing barrier design criteria for [[Aravaipa Creek]]. Only three isolated "tanks" containing nonnatives remained upstream of proposed barrier sites, considered a manageable containment target.
- **Sediment transport** in Aravaipa Creek was flagged as a poorly understood variable that could compromise barrier structural integrity.
- **Habitat fragmentation**: Propst raised concerns that barrier systems, while excluding nonnatives, could also fragment remaining native populations and that upstream nonnative source populations could negate barrier benefits if not concurrently managed.
- **Land tenure complexity**: Yaqui Chub populations in Turkey Creek occupy a mix of private (Joe Austin), State, and Forest Service land, complicating regulatory agreements; grazing permits on Forest Service land in the same drainage are held by the same private landowner.
- **Historic range evidence**: 1800s museum records (destroyed in the 1906 San Francisco earthquake) combined with modern morphometric (Hendrickson) and genetic (Tibbets) data support inclusion of the Willcox Playa basin/Sulphur Springs Valley within the historic range of Rio Yaqui fishes.
- **Watershed threats**: Grazing management in Sycamore Canyon (Coronado National Forest) remains an unresolved Section 7 consultation issue affecting Sonora Chub habitat.

## Key Findings
1. **Candidate species elevation**: Longfin dace, desert sucker, Sonora sucker, Little Colorado sucker, and an undefined speckled dace unit were officially approved as ESA Category 2 candidates, though public/agency notification was delayed due to staffing shortages.
2. **Taxonomic revision pending**: Publication by R.L. Mayden is expected to reclassify *Rhinichthys osculus* as *Apocope osculus*, with downstream implications for the taxonomic placement of *Tiaroga*.
3. **Critical habitat litigation outcome**: A lawsuit settlement with the Greater Gila Biodiversity Project resulted in finalization of 1985-proposed critical habitat for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] without reproposal; a new loach minnow population in Eagle Creek was identified as needing future critical habitat inclusion.
4. **CAP Biological Opinion structure**: The Central Arizona Project opinion established a Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) with two funding streams — a **recovery fund** (mitigating jeopardy for [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Gila Topminnow]]) and a **nonnative management fund** (barrier maintenance, nonnative removal, and an emergency response plan analogous to a toxic spill plan). No funds had yet been transferred due to legal/administrative mechanisms.
5. **Barrier strategy consensus**: The Team voted unanimously to support a system of nonnative fish preclusion (physical barriers plus active management) for Aravaipa Creek, while acknowledging unresolved hydrologic, sediment-transport, and fragmentation concerns.
6. **Conservation agreements as policy tool**: Multiple ongoing/proposed conservation agreements in lieu of listing (Virgin River Spinedace, Wet Canyon Talussnail) were discussed with significant Team skepticism regarding enforceability, given the absence of legal penalties beyond future listing risk.
7. **Genetics research program**: A National Science Foundation grant (Minckley & Hedrick) is funding nuclear genetics work on Gila Topminnow, maintaining captive stocks from Bylas, Sharp, Monkey Springs, and Cienega Creek populations (20 founding females each) for research and future reintroduction needs.
8. **Section 9 enforcement**: A documented take of Gila Topminnow occurred on Sonoita Creek due to unauthorized stream bulldozing, prompting civil prosecution pursued jointly by FWS law enforcement and U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Section 404 regulators.
9. **Institutional restructuring**: A new regional "riparian recovery team" (flagship species: southwestern willow flycatcher) is being organized to cover aquatic and riparian species across Arizona and New Mexico, with unclear integration plans relative to existing single-species recovery teams like the DFRT.
10. **Recovery plan timelines**: The Rio Yaqui Fishes Recovery Plan was targeted for finalization by December 31, 1994, pending distribution of review copies to Cochise County and Mexican officials (via the U.S. State Department), which had not yet occurred.
11. **Meeting frequency increase**: The Team agreed to increase meeting frequency to twice yearly, with the next meeting tentatively scheduled for March 1995 at the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]].
