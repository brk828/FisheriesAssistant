## Executive Summary
This laboratory study examined the accumulation and elimination of the organochlorine pesticide [[Dieldrin]] in the dorsal muscle tissue of [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), evaluating the roles of exposure route (water vs. food) and body size. Two size classes (150-225 mm and 350-400 mm) were exposed for 28 days to 75 parts per trillion (ppt) dieldrin in water, 2 ppm dieldrin in food, or both combined, followed by a 28-day elimination period. Larger catfish consistently accumulated significantly more dieldrin than smaller catfish across all exposure routes, likely due to greater fat content. Dietary exposure resulted in substantially higher tissue dieldrin concentrations than waterborne exposure, and combined food-plus-water exposure produced the highest accumulation, indicating additive uptake from both sources. During elimination, dieldrin acquired via water was cleared much faster than dieldrin acquired via food, and by day 28 of elimination, size-related differences in tissue concentration had largely disappeared.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Ictalurus punctatus* ([[Channel Catfish]]), family [[Ictaluridae]]
- **Tissue analyzed**: Dorsal muscle (edible portion), 10-40 g samples per fish
- **Size classes studied**:
  - Small: 150-225 mm total length
  - Large: 350-400 mm (and 350-440 mm in elimination phase) total length
- **Source populations**: Wild-caught via baited hoop nets and electrofishing from the Des Moines River below Fraser, Iowa, and hatchery-reared fish from a commercial fish culturist
- **Physiological note**: Larger catfish presumed to have greater fat content, correlating with enhanced capacity for dieldrin accumulation and retention (per [[Bulkley et al. 1974]])

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Freshwater riverine environment ([[Des Moines River]], Iowa); experimental exposure concentrations (75 ppt water, 2 ppm food) were selected to reflect maximum dieldrin levels documented from agricultural runoff in Iowa streams
- **Trophic exposure pathways**:
  - **Waterborne uptake**: Direct absorption via gills/body surface from dieldrin-contaminated water
  - **Dietary uptake**: Consumption of dieldrin-contaminated food (commercial pellets spiked with dieldrin at 2 ppm, fed at 3% body weight daily)
  - **Combined exposure**: Simultaneous water and food contamination, simulating realistic field conditions from agricultural runoff contamination of [[Aquatic Food Chains]]
- **Comparative context**: Findings align with [[Macek and Korn 1970]] (food chain as major DDT source) and [[Kleinert et al. 1968]] (dual uptake routes—diet and gill absorption), while contrasting with [[Chadwick and Brocksen 1969]] and [[Murphy 1971]], who found water to be the dominant dieldrin/DDT source in other species (e.g., guppies)
- **Environmental persistence**: No dieldrin was detected in the water of food-only exposure tanks, indicating minimal leaching from contaminated food back into the water column

## Key Findings

### Uptake Phase (28-day exposure)
| Exposure Group | Small Catfish (150-225mm) Day 28 | Large Catfish (350-400mm) Day 28 |
|---|---|---|
| Control (background) | 10 ppb | 13 ppb |
| Water (75 ppt) | 175 ppb | 274 ppb |
| Food (2 ppm) | 544 ppb | 1,243 ppb |
| Food + Water | 898 ppb | 2,418 ppb |

- Dietary exposure produced significantly higher (P=0.01) muscle dieldrin concentrations than waterborne exposure in both size classes.
- Combined food-and-water exposure produced significantly greater accumulation (P=0.01) than either route alone, demonstrating an **additive effect** of multiple exposure pathways.
- **Body size effect**: Large catfish accumulated significantly more dieldrin (P=0.01) than small catfish under all three exposure conditions (water, food, and combined).

### Elimination Phase (28 days post-exposure)
| Exposure Group | Small Catfish Day 0 → Day 28 | Large Catfish Day 0 → Day 28 |
|---|---|---|
| Water | 176 → 24 ppb | 186 → 28 ppb |
| Food | 287 → 108 ppb | 302 → 109 ppb |
| Food + Water | 365 → 172 ppb | 634 → 170 ppb |

- **Waterborne-accumulated dieldrin eliminated fastest**: >50% eliminated within 14 days; by day 28, levels matched controls.
- **Food-derived dieldrin eliminated more slowly**: residual concentrations at day 28 were >4x higher than water-exposed fish.
- **Combined-exposure fish** retained >6x the dieldrin of water-exposed fish and ~50% more than food-exposed fish at day 28.
- By the end of the elimination period, **no significant size-related differences** remained between large and small catfish across all exposure routes — indicating convergence in dieldrin burden regardless of initial body size.
- Results parallel [[Grzenda et al. 1971]], who reported ~50% dieldrin loss in goldfish within two weeks following dietary exposure.

### Analytical Methodology Notes
- Gas chromatography (Beckman GC-5) with electron capture detection was used for dieldrin quantification (5% OV-210 and 4% SE-30/QF-1 columns).
- Extraction efficiency validated using [[Aldrin]] recovery spikes (83-90%, average 85%) and radiolabeled (¹⁴C) dieldrin recovery experiments (59.4-117.0%, mean 84.2%).
- Data were **not corrected** for percent recovery, meaning reported concentrations may be conservative (slight underestimates).

### Broader Implications
- Confirms that **both water and diet are significant, non-mutually-exclusive routes** of dieldrin bioaccumulation in [[Channel Catfish]], with relative importance likely shifting based on ambient environmental concentrations of each exposure medium.
- Supports the hypothesis that fish body size and associated adiposity are key modulators of pesticide bioaccumulation, with implications for [[Bioaccumulation]] risk assessment and human consumption advisories for larger, older fish in contaminated waterways.
