# Descriptions of Nearctic Caddis Flies (Trichoptera) with Special Reference to the Illinois Species

#trichoptera #entomology #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This Illinois Natural History Survey bulletin (Volume 21, Article 4; dated March 1938 on the cover) by [[Herbert H. Ross]] formally describes a large set of new caddisfly species, mostly from [[Illinois Natural History Survey]] collections, supplemented by material from other institutions across North America. It is the first report of a planned project on the aquatic insects of Illinois. The author states that a comprehensive faunistic report, with keys to adults and larvae, will follow. The text is partly OCR-damaged and ends mid-description of *Micrasema wataga*, so the final species accounts are incomplete.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** The paper names new species so that they can be used in future Survey publications. The project began in 1931 with fieldwork led by [[Cornelius Betten]], and Ross later continued it.
- **Scope:** Specimens were assembled from many parts of the continent, because identifying the Illinois species required the whole North American fauna. A large share of the material proved to be new to science.
- **Methods:** Genitalia were cleared in caustic potash (KOH), washed, and mounted in glycerin. The author notes that many diagnostic structures cannot be seen without this treatment. Many species were reared from larvae or pupae. Drawings were made by [[C. O. Mohr]]. Types are deposited in the Survey collection, except for noted paratypes.
- **Taxonomic decisions:** Structures previously called preanal appendages or anal appendages are treated as true [[Cerci]], homologous with those in other insect orders. Family and genus order follows [[Betten (1934)]], *Caddis Flies of New York State*.
- **Species-level differences:** In some groups, notably *Hydropsyche*, adult differences are slight. The author argues they are real because they are constant across large series, correlate with larval differences, ecological habits and distribution, and often with male color pattern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Caddisflies are described as playing an important role in the "economy of our lakes and streams."
- Many species were collected in the [[Kankakee River]] near Momence, Illinois, described in the plate caption as a "clear, fast-flowing stream" that harbors many of the state's rarest caddisflies.
- Larvae of *Rhyacophila fenestra* are "common in the rocky streams of the Ozark region in southern Illinois" (see [[Ozark Region]]). The larval case of *Polytrichia xena* is described as one of the commonest "micros" in the Ozarkian uplift.
- Collection localities include Illinois sites such as [[Herod]] (Gibbons Creek), Oakwood (Salt Fork River), Muncie (Stony Creek), Wilmington (Kankakee River), Havana (Quiver Creek) and Rock Island (Mississippi River). Western sites include Mount Baker, Washington; Parco, Wyoming (North Platte River); and Turner Falls State Park, Oklahoma (Honey Creek).
- Adults were collected at light, sweeping and reared from pupae, and the collections include mating pairs, which helped associate sexes.

## Key Findings

**Overall outcome:** The paper describes about 114 new species. This count comes from the species headings and is an approximation. It also erects several genera and subgenera.

**Taxonomic actions:**
- **New genus *Cernotina*** (Polycentropidae/Psychomyiidae): It links the two groups by combining the venation and spur count of the Psychomyiidae with the habitus, mouthparts and genitalia of the Polycentropidae. Its type species is *Cernotina calcea*, and two more species are described (*C. oklahoma*, *C. spicata*). The author says the two families should probably be united.
- **New genus *Goerita*** (Goeridae): Distinguished from related subfamilies by four hind-tibial spurs, smooth eyes and head shape. Type species *Goerita semata*. *Goera stylata* is also described.
- **New subgenera:** *Anagapetus* (in *Agapetus*, type species *A. debilis*), *Aopsyche* (near *Theliopsyche*, type *T. corona*), and *Amiocentrus* (in *Brachycentrus*, type *B. aspilus*).
- **Lepidostoma:** Ross argues that genera such as *Nosopus* and *Olemira* were separated only on secondary sexual characters. He groups the complex under *Lepidostoma* until females are better studied.
- **Protoptila** is placed in the Glossosomatinae of the Rhyacophilidae, although its adults key to Hydroptilidae.
- ***Hydropsyche* group work:** Many species are separated on oedagus and clasper shape. Some earlier Betten identifications are questioned, such as the *H. phalerata* record, which Ross says does not match Hagen's type.

**Notable species and diagnostics:**
- ***Rhyacophila fenestra*** (male about 11 mm): Oedagus with digitate spurs; reared from Gibbons Creek, Herod, Illinois.
- ***Hydroptila*** has the largest group, with 15 new species (for example *H. vala*, *H. armata*, *H. dentata*, *H. grandiosa*).
- ***Hydropsyche*** has 18 new species, the largest group after *Hydroptila*. Examples include *H. cornuta*, *H. frisoni* (large eyes), and *H. aerata* (white-and-brown wing pattern).
- ***Polytrichia*** species (*shawnee*, *stylata*, *oregona*, *spinosa*, *xena*) are separated on dorsal assemblages of the genitalia.
- ***Protoptila*** species (*jeanae*, *erotica*, *cantha*, *thoracica*) lack a produced, furcate apical sternite.
- ***Limnephilus* and *Neophylax*** have several new species, separated mainly by cerci, claspers and oedagal arms.

**Data and voucher practice:** Each species account gives a holotype (usually male, with locality, date and collector), an allotype (female, from the same data), and paratype counts by sex (for example "1c, 1°"). Many paratypes come from the Illinois localities listed above.

**Limitations:**
- The author notes that female characters are often unavailable, so females are identified by association with males.
- The paper concerns adult morphology. Larval and pupal descriptions are deferred to the forthcoming Illinois report.
