## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific research paper but an internal **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS)** administrative fax — a cover sheet and survey questionnaire — circulated by the Utah State Office (UT/CO Field Office) in preparation for a meeting on the recovery and critical habitat designation of the **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The questionnaire was distributed to state/regional biologists to gather expert judgment on population status, habitat needs, and recovery goals, explicitly noting that a **lack of recruitment** in wild populations complicates the assessment. The source contains no direct morphological, physiological, or quantitative ecological data; instead, it outlines the conceptual and regulatory framework FWS used to define recovery criteria and critical habitat for this endangered catostomid.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no direct habitat data are reported, the questionnaire itself outlines the ecological parameters FWS considered essential to defining the species' niche and recovery requirements:

- **Habitat type**: Historic and current occupation within river systems of the [[Colorado River Basin]], including mainstem rivers and associated habitats.
- **Physical/biological features considered essential** (per Question 3):
  - Flow regimes
  - Presence/absence of **[[Non-Native Fishes]]** (implied competitive/predatory pressure)
  - Water quality
  - Food availability
  - Spawning substrate
- **Population structure concerns** (per Question 7): Distinguishes between viable "populations" versus **relict individuals** — isolated fish trapped by dams or diversions where full life-history requirements (e.g., spawning, recruitment, rearing) cannot be completed.
- **Recruitment failure**: Explicitly noted as a major complicating factor in status assessment, suggesting observed populations at the time were composed largely of aging adults with little to no successful reproduction/juvenile survival reaching the population.
- **Historic vs. occupied range**: Question 10 seeks identification of unoccupied historic habitat sharing key features with currently occupied habitat, for potential use in reintroduction/recovery efforts — linking to concepts of **[[Habitat Reoccupation]]** and **[[Critical Habitat Designation]]**.
