# Triplectides egleri n. sp.: A New Caddisfly from the Brazilian Amazon, with Its Immature Stages and Notes on Biology

#entomology #freshwater-biology #trichoptera #leptoceridae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 taxonomic paper by [[Werner Sattler]], published in German, describes *Triplectides egleri* n. sp., a new caddisfly (Trichoptera) of the subfamily [[Triplectidinae]] in the family [[Leptoceridae]]. The species was collected in a forest stream near Belém do Pará, Brazil. The description covers the adult, larva, pupa and the larval and pupal cases. The paper also records the habitat conditions of the stream and observations on case-building behaviour. The author notes that Triplectidinae larvae are among the most common aquatic insects of Amazonian jungle creeks. The larval cases are unusually large, reaching 150 mm in length.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic placement.** The new species belongs to the genus *Triplectides* Kolenati. Sattler lists seven South American species previously known from the genus, including [[Triplectides Gracilis]] (Burmeister), which he identifies as the closest relative. He notes that the spur formula (2, 2, 3) and the undivided discoidal cell of the forewing depart from [[Mosely 1936]]'s diagnoses of the subfamily and genus. He nonetheless places the species in *Triplectides* on the basis of genitalia and the immature stages.

**Adult.** Body length is 8 mm in males and 10 mm in females. Forewing length is 10 mm in males and 12 mm in females. Antennae measure 36 mm in males and 30 mm in females. The head lacks ocelli. Forewings and hindwings have stalked forks. Male genitalia include a pair of long, club-shaped praeanal appendages, a cleft dorsal penis scale, and a four-branched genital fork. Two branches of the genital fork are claw-shaped. The female genitalia differ in form.

**Larva.** Head capsule is dark brown and roughly egg-shaped. Larvae have five-pointed left and four-pointed right mandibles. The prothorax bears knife-like lateral projections. Abdominal segments II–VIII carry unbranched tracheal gills in three rows on each side. Anal claws each have a dorsal hook.

**Pupa.** Males and females are 12 mm and 14 mm long, including anal styles. Each pupa has a cluster of dorsal hooklets on the abdomen, and the antennae are wound around the abdomen.

**Cases.**
- *Larval case:* Built from hollow or hollowed-out twigs, 2.3–8.0 mm in diameter and 23.0–150.0 mm long. Lumen diameter is about 2 mm. Case length is about ten times the larva's length (larva about 1.5 cm).
- *Pupal case:* A cocoon of grey-yellow silk, 11.0–13.0 mm long and 2.0–2.5 mm wide, with perforated end walls. The anterior opening is slit-like with teeth. The posterior opening is cloverleaf-shaped or star-shaped. Pupal cases are attached to submerged wood by a silk disc about 2.0 mm wide.

**Etymology.** The species is named after [[Walter Egler]], former director of the [[Museu Paraense Emílio Goeldi]] in Belém, who died in 1961 during a field excursion in the interior.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality and stream conditions.** The type locality is a stream near Benfica, described in more detail in an earlier paper by Sattler (1963). Recorded data, dated 17 June 1960, are:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Riparian vegetation | Forest |
| Width | 4–6 m |
| Depth | 0.5–1.0 m |
| Substrate | Sand, clay, leaf litter, wood |
| Macrophytes | *Batrachospermum* (Rhodophyceae), *Tonia fluviatilis* (Eriocaulaceae) |
| Surface current | 25 cm/s |
| Water temperature (morning) | 24.6 °C |
| Insolation | Moderate |
| Turbidity | None |
| Water colour | Weakly yellow-brown |
| pH | 4.8 |
| Dissolved O₂ | 7.3 mg/l |
| Free CO₂ | 0.9 mg/l |

**Distribution and abundance.** Sattler states that Triplectidinae larvae are found in almost every Amazonian rainforest stream. He cautions that not all of these larvae belong to *T. egleri*.

**Feeding.** The gut contents of 10 examined larvae consisted exclusively of brown plant particles, averaging 0.15 mm in diameter. The Summary describes the larvae as feeding on wood.

**Behaviour.** Larvae attach to submerged wood with silk threads or crawl freely over it. Their cases generally lie parallel to the current, a passive alignment caused by water flow rather than an active response.

**Plastic case-building.** The case-building instinct is flexible. In one observed sequence:
1. After losing its wooden case, a larva built a soft sand tube in about 4 hours, wrapping the front of its abdomen first and extending it backwards.
2. The next day the tube had hardened into a firm, slightly curved tube.
3. Eight days later the larva left it and began gathering plant material, apparently without success.
4. Two days after that it was again in a sand tube.
5. Thirteen days later the sand case was empty and the larva was crawling free.

Larvae removed from their cases curl their abdomens ventrally and seek contact with objects, which Sattler attributes to thigmotaxis.

**Emergence.** Adults emerge at the water surface. The pupal exuviae float on the water.

**Comparison with a related species.** [[Triplectides Gracilis]] differs from *T. egleri* in having a spur formula of 2, 2, 4 and an entire (unsplit) penis sheath. The pupal cocoon of *T. egleri* has a single cloverleaf-shaped posterior opening and a slit-like anterior opening, whereas in *T. gracilis* both openings are sieve-like.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Triplectides egleri* n. sp. is described from the adult male and female, larva, and pupa. The type series includes a male holotype, a male paratype and a female paratype, all from the Benfica stream (holotype collected 30 May 1961). The types are deposited at the [[Limnologische Station Schlitz]].
- **Diagnostic characters:** Spur formula 2, 2, 3; split penis sheath and penis dorsal scale; four-branched male genital fork with two claw-shaped branches; cloverleaf-shaped posterior cocoon opening.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty:** Sattler acknowledges that the spur formula and the forewing discoidal cell depart from the generic diagnosis of [[Mosely 1936]]. He retains the species in *Triplectides* based on genitalia and immature stages.
- **Exceptional case size:** Larval cases reach 150 mm, far longer than the 1.5 cm larva. The Summary gives a maximum of 15 cm and a case-to-larva ratio of about 10:1.
- **Material examined:** Five larvae with cases (collected July 1960–June 1961) and six pupae with cases (4 males, 2 females; collected May–October 1961).
- **Plastic behaviour:** Larvae that lose their wooden cases can build temporary sand tubes, showing flexibility in the case-building instinct.
- **Habitat and food:** The species lives in clear, slightly acidic, oxygen-rich Amazonian forest streams with sandy-clay substrates and wood debris. Larvae feed on fine plant particles.
- **Abundance:** Triplectidinae larvae are among the most common aquatic insects of Amazonian jungle creeks.
- **Previous Triplectides records:** Müller (1878) described five *Triplectides* larval forms from southern Brazil. Ulmer (1955) based his diagnoses of the subfamily mainly on *T. gracilis* and *Notanatolica magna* Walker. Sattler notes that these larval diagnoses are provisional, as Ulmer himself acknowledged.
