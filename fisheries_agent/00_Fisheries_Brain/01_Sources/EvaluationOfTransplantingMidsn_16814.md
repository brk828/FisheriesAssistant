# Evaluation of Transplanting Mid-Snake River Steelhead Trout to Pahsimeroi River (March 1967 to March 1968)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Idaho Department of Fish and Game report by [[Melvin Reingold]] evaluates the transplant of juvenile [[Steelhead Trout]] from the mid-[[Snake River]] to the [[Pahsimeroi River]]. Approximately 1,292,000 juveniles were released into two one-acre holding ponds from February 8 to May 2, 1967, and an additional 72,400 were released at the river mouth on June 21 and 22, 1967. Mark-and-recovery expansion estimated that about 558,500 fish left the river between April and June 1967. Migration dropped during summer and rose sharply in fall. Fish that remained in the river all summer were in very poor condition by fall. By March 31, 1968, an estimated 931,900 hatchery steelhead had migrated out of the Pahsimeroi River past the [[Burstedt Lane Trap]]. The report recommends that transplanted juveniles exceed 170 mm total length and be planted before June.

## Background & Description

**Project purpose:** To evaluate the success of transplanting mid-Snake River steelhead to the Pahsimeroi River.

**Rearing and release system:**
- Fish were hatched and reared at [[Niagara Springs Hatchery]] and transported at "smolt size" to two one-acre ponds adjacent to the Pahsimeroi River, 14 river miles upstream from its mouth.
- Fish were fed and allowed to leave the ponds voluntarily.
- The program relocates steelhead and salmon stocks from the Snake River to the [[Salmon River]] system.
- The facilities were operated by the [[Idaho Fish And Game Department]] and financed entirely by [[Idaho Power Company]].
- Background is given in the 1966 progress report (Reingold, 1967).

**Transport:** An all-aluminum transport can hauled 5,000 pounds of steelhead per trip.

**Methods:**
- **Hauling:** 1,292,000 steelhead were hauled from Niagara Springs Hatchery in 23 truckloads between February 8 and May 2, 1967.
- **Ponds' outlet trap:** An inclined screen trap at the pond outlet monitored smolt emigration in February and March 1967. It was removed after April 4 because it could not safely hold the increasing number of emigrants.
- **Burstedt Lane Trap:** A perforated-plate metal trap, four feet square by two feet deep, was installed on an irrigation screen bypass pipe about 11 river miles downstream from the ponds.
- **Mark-and-recovery:** From April to June 1967, 1,545 migrants were marked with an opercle punch, transported upstream, and released in the pond outlet unit. Twenty-six (1.7 percent) were recaptured at Burstedt Lane over three months. Total emigrant numbers were estimated by expanding this recovery rate; see [[Mark-And-Recovery Method]].
- **Electrofishing:** An AC electric shocker sampled fish periodically through summer and fall for density and condition data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Condition factor:** Used as a measure of well-being, following Lagler (1952). The report gives these benchmarks for juvenile steelhead (see [[Condition Factor]]):
- Healthy wild juveniles generally range from 0.85 to 1.2.
- Below 0.70: thin and in poor physical condition.
- Below 0.65: physically debilitated, with little chance of survival in the natural environment.

**Comparative life-history data cited:**
- Bjornn (1966) reported an average emigrant size of 185 to 186 mm for steelhead from the [[Lemhi River]].
- Wagner and Wallace (1963) reported in the [[Alsea River]], Oregon, that hatchery steelhead liberated at 160 mm or longer showed the highest survival as returning adults.

**Environmental observations:**
- Subfreezing air temperatures prevented sampling in December 1967 and January 1968.
- Fish filled "every available hole and niche" in the river after the June 10 pond drain, according to field observations.
- SCUBA observations, boat and shoreline counts, density estimates, and condition data suggested that most fish remaining in the river died without leaving it.

## Key Findings

**Releases and emigration (spring 1967):**
- Approximately 636,000 steelhead had been delivered to the ponds by April 23, but only 18,600 had passed Burstedt Lane by that date.
- Feeding was suspended April 27 and trap catches rose on April 28. By April 30, an estimated 147,400 had passed Burstedt Lane.
- By the first week of June, about 1,292,000 had been delivered to the ponds, and an estimated 428,300 had passed Burstedt Lane.
- On June 10 the ponds were drained and fish were flushed into the river. From June 8 to June 30, about 130,200 migrants passed the trap, leaving an estimated 733,500 in the river above Burstedt Lane.

**Partition of the planted fish:**
- 428,300 (33 percent) left the ponds voluntarily before June 10.
- 863,700 were flushed from the ponds on June 10.
- By the end of July, 137,900 of the flushed fish (16 percent) had passed Burstedt Lane.
- 725,800 remained in the Pahsimeroi River until fall.

**Fall and total emigration:**
- From September 1 through November 30, about 353,200 steelhead passed Burstedt Lane.
- February 1968: about 3,000 emigrants.
- March 1968: two 1967-released mid-Snake steelhead were collected.
- Total estimated emigration from March 28, 1967 through March 31, 1968: 931,900 fish (Table 1). About 361,100 did not emigrate past Burstedt Lane and most probably died in the river. The Executive Summary rounds this to 932,000.

**Condition of emigrants:**
- Average condition factor was high at flushing and stayed fairly high through July (0.85).
- It fell to 0.64 by the end of August and to 0.60 by the end of November.
- A January 2, 1968 sample averaged 0.81. The report attributes this to survival of only the better-conditioned fish, not improvement. A wild steelhead sample at the same time averaged 1.1.

**Lengths:**
- 2,692 emigrants at Burstedt Lane averaged 171 mm total length.
- Pond samples (N = 1,663) averaged 164 mm.
- Wild steelhead at the trap (N = 206) averaged 187 mm.
- The author suggests larger fish migrate more readily.

**Losses and limitations:**
- Hauling losses were estimated at 3,000 to 6,000 fish. Losses in the holding ponds appeared minimal.
- Anglers took an estimated fewer than 2,000 fish.
- Outmigration in December and January could not be monitored.
- Estimates rest on relatively limited mark-and-recovery data, so statistical confidence limits would be wide. The author judged the estimates adequate for program purposes.
- The mouth release of 72,400 fish (June 21-22, 1967) was not evaluated.

**Recommendations:**
- Mid-Snake River steelhead juveniles delivered to the Pahsimeroi River should exceed 170 mm total length.
- All should be planted before June (OCR reads "June |," likely June 1) to promote successful migration.
