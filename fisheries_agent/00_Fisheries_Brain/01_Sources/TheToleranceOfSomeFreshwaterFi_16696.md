# The Tolerance of Some Fresh-Water Fish for Sea Water

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1958 reprinted note from *The Salmon and Trout Magazine*, by D. W. H. Herbert and H. T. Mann of the [[Water Pollution Research Laboratory]] (Stevenage, Herts), reports a small laboratory experiment on how well two freshwater fish tolerate sudden exposure to sea water. [[Roach]] (*Rutilus rutilus*) and [[European Perch]] (*Perca fluviatilis*) were transferred abruptly from fresh water into dilutions of [[Sea Water]], and survival was recorded over 100 hours. The study was motivated by fish kills in eastern English coastal rivers when [[Tidal Locks and Sluices]] failed and allowed sea water to enter. Perch tolerated roughly 1.5 times the salinity that roach withstood: about 45 per cent sea water for several days, versus about 30 per cent for roach.

## Background & Description

- **Problem:** Rivers and canals on the low-lying eastern English coastal plain discharge to the sea through locks and sluices intended to exclude sea water at high tide. When these fail, sea water enters and fish die. The authors note that little literature existed on the salinity limits of freshwater fish.
- **Study species:**
  - [[Roach]] (*Rutilus rutilus*, L.), approximately 90 mm in length.
  - [[European Perch]] (*Perca fluviatilis*, L.), approximately 80 mm in length.
- **Sea water:** Collected near the [[Nore Lightship]]. Salinity 34.56 gm per 1000 gm; chloride 19.13 gm per 1000 gm.
- **Dilution water:** [[Borehole Water]] supplied to the laboratory, total dissolved solids 413 p.p.m.
- **Sample:** Ten individuals of each species per dilution.
- **Conditions:** 17.5 °C; 40-litre solutions; aerated to keep dissolved oxygen between 8.4 and 8.75 p.p.m.
- **Duration:** 100 hours.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Osmotic stress:** Sudden salinity increase is a direct physiological challenge for freshwater teleosts. The study frames the findings as a way to understand [[Fish Mortality Events]] where an influx of sea water may have contributed.
- **Estuarine and coastal interface:** The sea-water intrusions described occur where freshwater channels meet tidal estuarine water, so the species' salinity limits matter for management of coastal drainage infrastructure.
- **Tolerance difference:** The perch's higher tolerance is consistent with the broader idea that species differ in their ability to cope with brackish conditions, though the document does not explore mechanisms.

## Key Findings

1. **Experimental design:** Fish were moved suddenly from fresh water into mixtures of fresh and sea water. Because individual survival times varied widely, the authors plotted **median survival times** (the point at which half the fish had died) rather than single values.
2. **Survival distribution:** Survival times followed a **log-normal distribution**. Vertical bars on the graph span one standard deviation of the logarithm of survival time, within which approximately two-thirds of the fish died. A logarithmic time axis was used for presentation.
3. **Relative tolerance:** Perch withstood about **1.5 times the salinity** tolerated by roach.
4. **Tolerance thresholds (from fitted curves):**
   - [[European Perch]]: about **45 per cent sea water** for several days.
   - [[Roach]]: about **30 per cent sea water**.
5. **Practical implication:** The results were intended to help explain fish mortalities where sudden sea-water influx may have played a role, and to inform responses to failures of [[Tidal Locks and Sluices]].

**Limitations noted by the design:** Only two species, one temperature (17.5 °C), a single sudden-transfer protocol, and ten fish per dilution were used. The study is a short-term survival assay, not a long-term or field study.
