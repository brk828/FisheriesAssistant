# Asian Tapeworm Control and Spread in Hatchery Fish: Internal Memorandum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cestoda

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum, dated in the header but with the year illegible in the scan, reports on efforts to control the [[Asian Tapeworm]] in hatchery fish. The author states that the parasite is panzootic and was probably spread through [[Grass Carp]] and their transport. The memo describes a treatment trial at a hatchery ([[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], spelled "Dixter" in the scan) and notes that the treatment drug is not commercially available. The author also recommends restrictions on future fish transfers from the source hatchery. The scan is heavily degraded, so specific figures, drug names and dosages cannot be reliably recovered.

## Background & Description

- **Organism:** The [[Asian Tapeworm]], a parasitic cestode that infects fish. The scan does not give a scientific name.
- **Document type:** A memorandum from a fisheries or fish health office (sender title partly illegible) to a regional recipient. It refers to an earlier correspondence on the topic.
- **Affected hosts:** The memo mentions a heavy infestation affecting [[Largemouth Bass]] at a hatchery. The author describes the infestation as severe, with large numbers of parasites in some fish.
- **Context:** The memo links the parasite's spread to moving fish, especially grass carp, and discusses the risk to wild populations in small rivers. The river names are illegible in the scan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spread pathway:** The author describes the parasite as panzootic. It was probably introduced through shipments of [[Grass Carp]] and their associated transfers between facilities. Movement of infected stock is the main route of spread named in the memo.
- **Risk to wild fish:** The author is concerned about the parasite moving into small natural rivers, where wild fish populations could be affected. The scan does not include population or survey data.
- **Management constraints:** Treatment options are limited. The memo says the drug used in the trial could not be readily obtained. It may become usable later through an [[Food And Drug Administration]] exemption process, but this is unclear in the scan.

## Key Findings

1. **Treatment trial:** Staff tested an anthelmintic treatment on infected fish at the hatchery. The scan indicates that the fish were held in a treatment bath, possibly for about 1 hour, with the drug administered at a set concentration. The exact drug name, concentration and sample size are illegible. The memo reports that the trial was successful in clearing the parasite, but no counts or statistics are legible.
2. **Drug availability:** The author states that the drug cannot currently be obtained for routine use. Its use would need [[Regulatory Approval]] or an exemption, which the memo mentions in passing.
3. **Source hatchery:** The memo says the largest infected stock was transferred from one facility to another before the infection was identified. The author recommends that any future transfers from this source be avoided or made only under strict [[Quarantine Protocols]].
4. **Recommendation:** The author recommends that all future fish transfers from the source be restricted and screened for the parasite before movement, to prevent further spread.

**Data limitations:** The scan is heavily degraded. Dates, sample sizes, drug names, dosages, river names and the recipient's name cannot be verified and are not reported here.
