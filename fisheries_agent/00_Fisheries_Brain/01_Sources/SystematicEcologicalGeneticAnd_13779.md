# Animal Protocol Review: Systematic, Ecologic, Genetic, and Behavioral Ichthyology

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona State University (ASU) Animal Care and Use Committee (ACUC) form covers a renewal and revision (previous protocol No. 86072R) of the ongoing research program **Systematic, Ecologic, Genetic, and Behavioral Ichthyology**, led by [[W. L. Minckley]] with co-investigators M. Douglas, T. Dowling and P. Marsh. The protocol is filed 12 June 1992 and supports field and laboratory work on native southwestern fishes, many of them endangered or threatened. The program combines ecology, genetic structure and phylogeny. Its animal-welfare case rests on non-lethal tissue sampling, minimal-restraint capture, anesthesia with [[MS 222]], and [[Radiotelemetry]] surgery, with lethal sampling limited to what research requires.

## Background & Description

The protocol states that native southwestern fishes are disappearing "at an alarming rate," and that little is known about why. The authors argue that experiments on selected individuals are needed to test responses to conditions that have caused natural declines. For some species, wild populations are too rare to study, so research stock must come from broodfish. Reproductive behavior studies must therefore come first. The team also works with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] on propagation and rearing of "healthy, genetically fit" fish for reintroduction.

Holding facilities are needed for genetic analyses and for developing non-lethal sampling. Fish are returned to the wild or to semi-natural habitats after sampling. Sacrifice is allowed only under state and federal scientific permits, for tissue harvest, taxonomic material, parasite/disease determination or other purposes.

The protocol covers activities on the ASU campus. Field work falls under scientific permits held by each state and, for listed species, by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The audience is a lay-inclusive committee, and the text is written accordingly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research spans several imperiled systems:

- [[Aravaipa Creek]] (community ecology of native fishes)
- [[Lake Mohave]] (AZ-NV; ecology of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]])
- [[Little Colorado River]] (ecology and conservation of [[Humpback Chub]]; spinedace genetics)
- [[Virgin River]] (NV-AZ-UT; effects of population manipulation)
- Middle Rio Grande (Texas/Chihuahua) and the Gila River drainage
- Urban lakes and Coconino National Forest streams

Target taxa include [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], Apache and Gila trouts, chubs (*Gila* spp.), speckled dace and topminnows (*Poeciliopsis* spp.). Studies are grouped into three categories: ecology and general biology (9 projects), genetic variation and population structure (6 projects), and phylogenetic relationships (5 projects). That makes about 20 current, nonfunded or funded projects, most ongoing from 1963 to the mid-1990s.

Fish are held in LSC 018. Holding periods range from a few days to indefinite for broodfish. Stock production needs multiple breeding seasons. Subjects include both sexes, aged 0 to about 50 years.

## Key Findings

**Scope and approvals**
- The protocol is a renewal/revision covering funded research (US Department of the Interior and others) and some nonfunded studies. It is not for teaching.
- All personnel doing experimental procedures are to be certified in federal training requirements. No waiver of PHS Policy is requested.
- The approval date and review dates are partly illegible in the scanned form.

**Animal numbers** (maximum individuals per year, by sacrifice / non-lethal sampling / non-lethal experiments)
- Larval razorback sucker: 200 / 0 / 200 (the largest category)
- Adult razorback sucker: 0 / 100 / 0
- Most other listed genera (chubs, spikedace/loach minnow, pupfish, topminnows, Yaqui catfish, Apache/Gila trouts): 0–30 per category
- Non-native species: 30 / 30 / 30
- Entries marked with an asterisk (*) are endangered or threatened taxa, and studying them requires both state and federal permits.
- The "entire study" total is not stated on the form.

**Procedures and categories**
- Checked use categories include tissue/blood harvest, dietary manipulation, pharmacologic use (MS 222), biohazardous materials, infectious agents, trauma/injury, environmental stress, surgery and other research. Immunization and irradiation are not checked. (Several marks are ambiguous on the scan.)
- Pain categories I, II and III appear marked; IV is not. The attending veterinarian was consulted.

**Non-lethal tissue sampling**
- Protein electrophoresis tissues now come mainly from [[Mucus, Epidermis and Fin Tissue]] and fin tips, which regenerate quickly. Internal [[Tissue Biopsy]] uses small-gauge needles under partial anesthesia (25–50 mg/L MS 222).

**Dietary manipulation experiment**
- Only larval [[Razorback Sucker]] are to be fed under three treatments: ad libitum food, minimal feeding, and starvation. They are then compared with wild-caught larvae from Lake Mohave. This work is one PhD dissertation project (M. Horn), run once.

**Radiotelemetry surgery**
- Abdominal implants use aseptic technique, polished epoxy-encapsulated transmitters, and closure at three tissue levels. Anesthesia is 50–300 mg/L MS 222, and fish are held sedated (25–50 mg/L) for a few hours. Incisions usually heal in 1–3 weeks. Post-operative mortality is described as "extremely low."

**Euthanasia**
- Chemical: MS 222 at >300 mg/L, aqueous.
- Other methods: instantaneous immersion in [[Liquid Nitrogen Euthanasia]] (about −250 °C); dry ice supercooling in the field (about −60 °C); and immersion at about −20 °C in the laboratory.
- Preservation: 10% [[Formalin Fixation]], which is preferred for museum specimens. Many species are "highly formalin sensitive."

**Justification and alternatives**
- Investigators state that replication across species is needed for statistical reliability, not for repeating prior work.
- Non-animal alternatives are judged unacceptable for these research questions.
- Practices follow published guidelines (Hubbs et al., n.d.; Lagler 1978; Nielsen and Johnson 1983; Snieszko et al. 1974). The reference list includes about 20 citations.
