# Integrated Fisheries Management Plan External Technical Review Meeting (26 May 2000)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is the agenda and objectives sheet for a one-day meeting held on 26 May 2000 to conduct an external technical review of two fisheries management approaches for an Arizona basin. The approaches are the [[Integrated Fisheries Management Plan]] (also called the [[Watershed-Based Fisheries Management Plan]]) and [[Fisheries and Watershed Management in Arizona]]. The meeting aimed to gather reviewer critiques, identify major agency and internal issues, and reach consensus on options for each approach and for combining them. The document contains no species data, sample sizes, or survey results.

## Background & Description

**Meeting objective:** Facilitate an external technical review and discussion, and reach consensus on critiques of two developed approaches:
1. [[Integrated Fisheries Management Plan]]
2. [[Fisheries and Watershed Management in Arizona]] ("Looking into the Future")

**Desired outcome:** Open and candid discussion of how well each approach meets the stated project objectives, based on evaluations and reviewer comments. Other goals were to weigh pros, cons, and costs/benefits, and to recommend ways of combining the approaches.

**Agenda structure (summarized):**
- Introductions, ground rules, and meeting objectives (1000–1030), led by Demlong.
- Project background (1030–1100), presented by Young, Allison, and Dorum.
- External reviewer assessment (1110–1230), covering pros, cons, and recommendations for each approach. Reviewers listed: Hendricks, Valdez, Schmal, and Marsh.
- Identification of major issues from agency and internal reviews (1330–1500), led by Demlong.
- Outlining and rating options, clarifying comments and issues, and wrap-up (1510–1630).

**Project objectives:**
1. Reduce current and future conflicts between [[Native Fish Management]] (game and nongame species) and [[Non-Native Sportfish]] management.
2. Provide an integrated management strategy in which all management activities within the basin work toward long-term fisheries goals for the basin.
3. Adopt a proactive stance toward native fish to improve their status within the basin, promote [[Delisting]] of currently listed species, and prevent the need for future [[Federal Listing]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document places management within a single basin and watershed in [[Arizona]], with the goal of coordinating fisheries management across the whole basin. It explicitly contrasts native fish, both game and nongame species, with non-native sportfish, and it recognises that some native fish are already federally listed. Habitat conditions, species names, population status, and water-quality data are not described.

## Key Findings

- **Two competing or complementary approaches** are being reviewed. The [[Integrated Fisheries Management Plan]] and [[Fisheries and Watershed Management in Arizona]] are each to be assessed for pros, cons, costs/benefits, and recommendations.
- **Central tension:** conflicts between native fish conservation and non-native sportfish management are a core problem to resolve.
- **Strategic goal:** a basin-wide, integrated framework in which all management actions support shared long-term fisheries goals.
- **Conservation aim:** a proactive approach to native fish, with the explicit intent of promoting [[Delisting]] of currently listed species and avoiding [[Federal Listing]] of others.
- **Process:** the meeting was structured around independent external review, agency and internal issue identification, and option rating. The purpose was to reach consensus and recommend a combined approach. No final decisions or quantitative results appear in the document.
