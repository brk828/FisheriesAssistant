# A New Crawfish of the Spiculifer Group of the Genus Procambarus (Decapoda, Astacidae) from Central Mississippi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #astacidae #decapoda #crawfish #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1971 publication by J. F. Fitzpatrick, Jr. and Horton H. Hobbs, Jr. describes *Procambarus lylei*, a new species of crawfish from the [[Spiculifer Group]] of the genus [[Procambarus]]. The species was discovered in [[Calhoun County]], [[Mississippi]], within the [[Yalobusha River]] drainage system. The new species is characterized by distinctive morphological features including asymmetrical first pleopods with five terminal elements, a long slender rostrum with marginal spines, and specific tuberculation patterns on the chelae. *P. lylei* is most closely related to *P. ablusus*, *P. elegans*, and *P. natchitochae*, sharing a simple complement of terminal elements on the male first pleopod. The species is named in honor of Dr. Clay Lyle for his work with Mississippi crawfishes.

## Background & Description

The [[Spiculifer Group]] of the crawfish genus [[Procambarus]] inhabits lotic (flowing water) habitats in the Coastal Plain and Piedmont provinces of the southeastern United States. Of the 15 described species in this group, only five have allopatric ranges. Prior to this description, only four species had been reported occurring sympatrically with another group member: *P. elegans* and *P. vioscai* in [[Louisiana]], and *P. spiculifer* and *P. versutus* in [[Alabama]] and [[Florida]]. The authors add a new sympatric association: *P. spiculifer* and *P. raneyi* in [[Big Flat Creek]], [[Walton County]], [[Georgia]].

*Procambarus lylei* exhibits the following diagnostic characteristics:
- Body pigmented with well-developed eyes
- Rostrum with convergent margins bearing a pair of marginal spines; acumen long and slender
- Areola 2.5 to 5.3 times longer than wide, constituting 25.0 to 30.2 percent of entire carapace length
- Carapace with two cervical spines on each side
- Suborbital angle obsolete; postorbital ridges terminating in spines
- Antennal scale approximately 2.3 times longer than wide, broadest proximal to midlength
- Mesial margin of palm with seven or eight tubercles
- Both fingers with moderately well-defined longitudinal ridges
- Ischia of third and fourth pereiopods with hooks, those of third markedly more acute
- Coxae of fourth and fifth pereiopods with prominences

The holotypic male Form I has a carapace length of 33.3 mm, width of 14.6 mm, and height of 14.0 mm. The areola is 4.1 times longer than wide with four or five punctations across the narrowest part. The rostrum acumen constitutes approximately 39 percent of the entire rostrum length.

The first pleopods are asymmetrical with a weakly defined shoulder on the cephalic surface, reaching cephalad to the cephalic margin of coxae of third pereiopods. The distal extremity bears five elements: (1) slender acute mesial process, (2) shorter but slender and acute cephalic process, (3) rounded caudal knob bearing strong caudal process medially, (4) short corneous central projection, and (5) stout broadly subtriangular adventitious process. All terminal elements are recurved cephalodistally.

The allotypic female differs principally in secondary sexual characters, with a proportionately wider abdomen (11.8 mm versus 12.9 mm carapace width) and shorter, stouter chelae with less pronounced ridges. The annulus ventralis is movable with a shallow submedian trough and a sinus originating in the caudal portion, winding in a reversed sigmoid curve to the caudal margin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Procambarus lylei* inhabits lotic habitats in the [[Yalobusha River]] drainage system of [[Calhoun County]], [[Mississippi]]. The type locality is [[Shutispear Creek]], 2.7 miles south of State Route 8 on State Route 9. At the time of collection, the stream exhibited:
- Sluggish current
- Bottom of sticky mud with sticks and other detritus
- Width of approximately five feet
- Depth of approximately three feet
- Wooded banks with *Salix nigra* (black willow) as the preponderant element

The species occurs sympatrically with several other crawfish species:
- *[[Procambarus acutus acutus]]*
- *[[Procambarus hayi]]*
- *[[Cambarus diogenes]]* subspecies
- An undescribed species of *[[Orconectes]]*

A second locality, [[Toposhaw Creek]], 1.9 miles west of Calhoun City on State Route 8, yielded two females collected on 4 August 1967, also with *P. a. acutus*, *P. hayi*, and an undescribed *Orconectes* species.

The range of *P. lylei* appears allopatric but is almost completely surrounded by an undescribed member of the [[Spiculifer Group]] that fills the apparent hiatus between *P. ablusus* and other species: *P. versutus* and *P. lagniappe* to the east, *P. penni* to the south, and *P. vioscai* to the west. Despite diligent searching, only these two localities have been found where the species occurs.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Description**: *Procambarus lylei* new species is described based on holotypic male Form I (USNM 131533), allotypic female (USNM 131534), and morphotypic male Form II (USNM 131535). The species is distinguished by unique morphological features including the marked cephalodistal curvature of all terminal elements of the first pleopod and a distinct shoulder on the cephalic margin of the pleopod—features unique among [[Spiculifer Group]] members.

**Type Specimens and Measurements**: The holotype male Form I measures 33.3 mm carapace length, 14.6 mm width, and 14.0 mm height. The areola is 9.1 mm long and 2.2 mm wide (ratio 4.1:1). The allotype female measures 31.5 mm carapace length, 12.9 mm width, and 13.9 mm height. The morphotype male Form II (subadult) measures 24.4 mm carapace length.

**Distribution and Sympatry**: Known only from two localities in [[Calhoun County]], [[Mississippi]], both in the [[Yalobusha River]] drainage. Type locality collection (2 April 1966) yielded 1 male Form I, 2 males Form II, 3 females, 6 juvenile males, and 3 juvenile females. Second locality ([[Toposhaw Creek]], 4 August 1967) yielded 2 females.

**Size Range**: The largest specimen is a female with 52.3 mm cephalothorax length; the next largest is also a female at 39.4 mm. Size-related variation includes: areola width (as ratio of length) appears inversely related to cephalothorax length; larger females show elevated cephalolateral margins of the annulus ventralis, with degree of elevation increasing with size; juvenile specimens have proportionately shorter annuli due to lesser development of the cephalic portion.

**Morphological Variation**: Aside from sex and maturity variations, few significant variations were observed. The holotype possesses an accessory fourth spine in the left caudolateral corner of the telson cephalic section (unique to this specimen). The row of tubercles subtending the mesialmost row on the palm is irregular and variable in number (two to nine in mature specimens). One female has an accessory tubercle cephalic to the left tubercle on the sternum. One larger female has longer, more slender chelae with more strongly tuberculate palms than other females.

**Phylogenetic Relationships**: *P. lylei* is most closely related to *P. ablusus*, *P. elegans*, and *P. natchitochae*, sharing a simple but complete complement of terminal elements on the male first pleopod and relatively unornamented caudal margin of the female thoracic sternum. Like *P. elegans*, a prominent caudal process arises distinctly from the caudal knob, and the annuli ventrales are very similar in shape. *P. elegans* shows a distinct hint of the cephalic curvature characteristic of *P. lylei*. *P. natchitochae* is probably closer to *P. lylei* than is *P. ablusus*. *P. penni* and *P. vioscai* are less closely related.

**Unique Diagnostic Features**: *P. lylei* is the only member of the [[Spiculifer Group]] exhibiting marked cephalodistal curvature of all terminal elements of the first pleopod and possessing a distinct shoulder on the cephalic margin of the pleopod. The subterminal setae arise from as far proximally as the level of this shoulder but do not occur on it. In both *P. lylei* and *P. elegans*, the subapical tuft of setae arises principally from the cephalic surface of the appendage.

**Etymology**: The species is named in honor of Dr. Clay Lyle in recognition of his work with Mississippi crawfishes.
