# Colorado Statewide Creel Census Report 1955

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #salmonidae #percidae #centrarchidae #creel-census

## Executive Summary

This annual creel census report documents Colorado's 1955 statewide fishing activity, catch statistics, and stocking programs for both trout and warm water fisheries. Department personnel checked 72,489 trout fishermen and 17,781 warm water fishermen during 1955, representing an all-time high for annual fishermen checks. Fishing license sales declined from 364,063 in 1954 to 338,806 in 1955, primarily attributed to increased license fees. The overall catch-per-man-hour (CPMH) for trout waters (streams and lakes combined) was 0.76, while warm water CPMH reached 0.99, the highest recorded in the past five years. Colorado stocked a record 940,906 pounds of fish during 1955, exceeding all previous years. The report utilizes random survey data from 1945-1946 (revised 1950) to project statewide catch from field-collected creel check data.

## Background & Description

The creel census serves primarily to monitor statewide trends in fishing quality and catch. Data collection relies on field checks conducted by law enforcement and departmental personnel who record fishermen numbers, catch by species, and hours fished. Statistical projection methods convert sample data to statewide estimates using random survey information on license holder fishing preferences and effort patterns.

**Trout Waters:** Personnel checked 31,355 fishermen on 469 trout streams who fished 86,324 hours and caught 79,134 fish. On 323 trout lakes, 41,134 fishermen fished 139,983 hours and caught 74,900 fish. [[Rainbow Trout]] dominated both stream (55% of catch) and lake (71% of catch) fisheries. In streams, [[Brook Trout]] comprised 26% of catch, [[Brown Trout]] 13%, [[Cutthroat Trout]] 5%, and [[Mountain Whitefish]] 1%. In lakes, [[Brook Trout]] made up 11%, [[Cutthroat Trout]] 5%, [[Brown Trout]] 2%, [[Mackinaw Trout]] 1%, and [[Kokanee Salmon]] 1%.

**Warm Water Fisheries:** Checks on 50 lakes and 5 streams documented 17,781 fishermen who fished 50,244 hours (average 2.8 hours per contact). [[Yellow Perch]] (28.83% of catch) and [[Bullhead Catfish]] (26.41%) remained the predominant species. [[Rainbow Trout]] comprised 22.09% of warm water catch, [[Crappie]] 12.23%, [[Sunfish]] 7.21%, [[Largemouth Bass]] 1.10%, [[White Bass]] 1.00%, [[Walleye]] 0.51%, [[Channel Catfish]] 0.40%, [[Brown Trout]] 0.12%, and [[Drum]] 0.10%.

**Stocking Program:** Total stocking reached 929,455 pounds comprising 14,746,992 fish. [[Rainbow Trout]] dominated at 872,319 pounds (9,566,183 fish), followed by [[Brown Trout]] 53,773 pounds (408,592 fish), [[Brook Trout]] 11,888 pounds (547,957 fish), [[Cutthroat Trout]] 9,219 pounds (602,428 fish), [[Kokanee Salmon]] 1,241 pounds (3,342,832 fish), and [[Grayling]] 15 pounds (275,000 fish). Catchable-size trout numbered 4,995,922 weighing 897,898 pounds. Warm water stocking totaled 7,449.5 pounds (6,410,750 fish), including 6,060,713 [[Walleye]] fry and fingerlings from [[Wray Hatchery]], [[Las Animas Hatchery]], and the Fish Control and Salvage Unit.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 1955 fishing season occurred within Colorado's diverse aquatic ecosystems spanning cold-water trout streams and lakes to warm-water reservoirs and lowland streams. The report documents fishing across elevation gradients and geographic regions, from mountain trout waters to plains reservoirs.

**Trout Waters:** Average fish lengths indicate healthy size distributions: stream [[Rainbow Trout]] averaged 9.0 inches, [[Brown Trout]] 9.6 inches, [[Brook Trout]] 7.9 inches, [[Cutthroat Trout]] 8.8 inches, and [[Mountain Whitefish]] 11.3 inches. Lake trout averaged larger: [[Rainbow Trout]] 9.8 inches, [[Brown Trout]] 10.6 inches, [[Brook Trout]] 9.8 inches, [[Cutthroat Trout]] 11.0 inches, [[Mackinaw Trout]] 12.7 inches, and [[Kokanee Salmon]] 12.6 inches.

**Warm Water Systems:** Nine representative waters provided monthly catch data: [[Two Buttes Reservoir]] (Baca County), [[Hasty Lake]] (Bent County), [[Holbrook Lake Reservoir]] (Otero County), [[Bonny Reservoir]] (Yuma County), [[Jumbo Reservoir]] (Sedgwick County), [[North Sterling Reservoir]] (Logan County), [[Sloan's Lake]] (Denver County), [[Harriman Lake]] (Arapahoe County), and [[Summit Reservoir]] (Montezuma County). Average warm water fish sizes ranged from 6.1 inches ([[Sunfish]]) to 15.8 inches ([[Walleye]]).

**Species Introductions:** Recent introductions of [[Walleye]], [[White Bass]], and [[Drum]] (freshwater sheepshead) began appearing consistently in catch records, though comprising small percentages. Colorado received 715 [[Drum]] from Kansas, stocked in [[John Martin Reservoir]], [[Two Buttes Reservoir]], and [[Sloan's Lake]]. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service provided approximately 120,000 [[Largemouth Bass]], 90,000 [[Channel Catfish]], and 130,000 red ear [[Sunfish]] fingerlings.

**Seasonal Patterns:** Monthly warm water catch data revealed peak CPMH in August (2.41) with lowest rates in March (0.29). Fishing success increased from spring through summer: March 0.29, April 0.50, May 0.69, June 1.03, July 1.48, August 2.41, September 0.94, October 1.41, averaging 1.02 for the season.

## Key Findings

**License Sales and Participation:**
- Total licenses declined 25,257 from 384,063 (1954) to 338,806 (1955)
- Resident licenses: 234,199; Non-resident season: 11,672; Non-resident 5-day: 92,935
- Total season licenses: 245,871
- License fee increase in early 1955 caused major portion of decline
- New "resident fishing only" license category created, requiring arbitrary 3% deduction for unused licenses pending new survey data

**Fishing Effort (Projected Statewide):**
- Total fishing days: 3,487,878 (down from 3,718,780 in 1954)
- Total trout days: 2,969,688 (streams: 1,850,034; lakes: 1,119,654)
- Warm water days: 478,190
- Total fishing hours: 18,116,880 (trout: 15,995,629; warm water: 2,121,251)
- Hours per day: trout streams 5.2051, trout lakes 5.6857, warm water 4.4360

**Catch Statistics:**
- Total fish caught (projected): 14,333,270 (down from 15,706,904 in 1954)
- Total trout: 12,233,232 (streams: 8,859,243; lakes: 3,373,989)
- Warm water catch: 2,100,038
- Trout stream CPMH: 0.92 (up from 0.87 in 1954)
- Trout lake CPMH: 0.53 (down from 0.65 in 1954)
- Combined trout CPMH: 0.76 (down from 0.78 in 1954)
- Warm water CPMH: 0.99 (highest in five-year period, up from 0.97 in 1954)

**Five-Year Trends (1951-1955):**
- Trout CPMH declined from 0.97 (1951) to 0.76 (1955)
- Warm water CPMH increased from 0.65 (1951) to 0.99 (1955)
- Total catch peaked in 1952 at 18,353,674 fish
- Warm water fishing approached "good fishing" criterion (1.0 fish per hour) from 1952-1955

**Stocking Success:**
- Record 940,906 pounds stocked, exceeding all previous Colorado years
- Trout: 929,455 pounds (14,746,992 fish); catchable size: 897,898 pounds (4,995,922 fish)
- Warm water: 7,449.5 pounds (6,410,750 fish) valued at $17,215.11
- [[Walleye]] fry dominated warm water stocking: 6,060,713 individuals

**Warm Water Species Composition (1951-1955 combined):**
- Group I species ([[Yellow Perch]], [[Crappie]], [[Bullhead Catfish]], [[Rainbow Trout]]) comprised 84% of total catch
- Group II species ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Walleye]], [[White Bass]]) comprised 20% of catch
- Total five-year catch: 193,176 fish weighing 39,961 pounds

**Water-Specific Performance:**
- [[North Sterling Reservoir]]: CPMH 1.35 (3,282 fishermen, 15,088 fish)
- [[Jumbo Reservoir]]: CPMH 1.78 (1,078 fishermen, 4,332 fish)
- [[Sloan's Lake]]: CPMH 1.46 (1,069 fishermen, 2,488 fish)
- [[Bonny Reservoir]]: CPMH 0.87 (2,399 fishermen, 9,599 fish)
- [[Two Buttes Reservoir]]: CPMH 0.24 (389 fishermen, 221 fish)
- Other waters (45 lakes, 5 streams): CPMH 0.77 (9,604 fishermen, 18,018 fish)

**Methodology:**
- Creel checks: 72,489 trout fishermen, 17,781 warm water fishermen (all-time high)
- Projection based on 1945-1946 random survey (revised 1950) of license holder preferences
- New random survey underway to address 1955 license structure changes
- Statistical projections account for non-fishing license holders: 15.94% (resident combination), 4.65% (non-resident 5-day), assumed 3.00% (resident season)

The report concludes that while trout lake fishing declined, this was not compensated by slight improvements in stream fishing. Warm water fisheries showed continued improvement, approaching or exceeding the "good fishing" standard of one fish per hour. The record stocking effort and emerging presence of introduced species ([[Walleye]], [[White Bass]], [[Drum]]) suggest ongoing management efforts to diversify and enhance Colorado's fisheries.
