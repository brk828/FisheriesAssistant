# Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP) for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species #river-management

## Executive Summary

This Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP) establishes a comprehensive 5-10 year strategy for recovering four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Published in January 1993 by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 6, the plan coordinates recovery actions across multiple river reaches while allowing new water development to proceed. The program addresses seven core recovery elements: protecting instream flows, restoring habitat, reducing nonnative fish impacts, conserving genetic integrity, monitoring populations, increasing public awareness, and providing program support. Actions are prioritized (Priority 1 for extinction prevention, Priority 2 for significant beneficial effects, Priority 3 for supporting research) with specific timelines for implementation across the [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[White River]], [[Colorado River]], and [[Gunnison River]] systems.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program was established through a 1988 Cooperative Agreement to recover endangered fishes while permitting Upper Basin water development. The program serves as a reasonable and prudent alternative to jeopardy findings in Section 7 consultations on water depletions. The overall recovery goal is achieving naturally self-sustaining populations and protecting essential habitat.

Species-specific recovery goals vary by conservation status:

**[[Bonytail Chub]]**: Short-term goal is preventing extinction, as this species is extremely rare in the wild with no captures in the Upper Basin for over 10 years.

**[[Razorback Sucker]]**: First priority is preventing extinction, as most captured individuals are thought to be over 20 years old with little evidence of successful recruitment.

**[[Colorado Squawfish]]**: Establish natural self-sustaining populations in: (a) [[Green River Basin]]; (b) Colorado River from [[Palisade]] to [[Lake Powell]]; (c) [[Salt River]] and/or [[Verde River]]; and (d) lower 305 km of [[San Juan River]]. Remove threats of fragmentation and legally protect essential habitats, migration routes, streamflows, and water quality.

**[[Humpback Chub]]**: Protect five viable, self-sustaining wild populations and habitat in: (a) [[Little Colorado River]] and Colorado River in [[Grand Canyon]]; (b) [[Black Rocks]]/[[Westwater Canyon]] area; (c) Yampa and Green rivers in [[Dinosaur National Monument]]; (d) [[Gray Canyon]] and [[Desolation Canyon]] in Green River; and (e) [[Cataract Canyon]] in Colorado River.

The RIPRAP builds upon previous planning documents including the 1987 "Blue Book," species recovery plans, long-range plans for biology and water acquisition, and annual work plans. Progress toward recovery is measured against accomplishment of identified actions, as species population responses may take many years to become evident.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] encompasses multiple distinct river reaches, each with unique hydrological characteristics and management challenges. The program recognizes that endangered fish recovery requires coordinated management across jurisdictional boundaries and competing water uses.

**Habitat Requirements**: Endangered fishes depend on specific flow regimes, including spring and winter flows for spawning and recruitment. [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations are central to providing appropriate seasonal flows in the Green River system. Flooded bottomland habitats are identified as important for razorback sucker and Colorado squawfish, with sites like [[Old Charlie Wash]], [[Escalante]], and [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] targeted for restoration.

**Water Quality Concerns**: Selenium contamination at [[Ashley Creek]]/[[Stewart Lake]] poses threats to endangered fishes. Heavy metals and selenium impacts near Lake Powell and petroleum derivatives in the White River require evaluation. The [[Uncompahgre River]] is identified as a potential selenium source affecting the Gunnison River.

**Nonnative Species Interactions**: Multiple nonnative species threaten endangered fish populations through predation and competition. Priority concerns include:
- [[Channel Catfish]] predation on young-of-the-year and juveniles
- [[Red Shiner]] competition with larvae
- [[Black Crappie]] predation in Green/White rivers
- [[Smallmouth Bass]] predation in Duchesne/Green rivers
- [[Northern Pike]] impacts in multiple locations

**Passage Barriers**: Agricultural diversion structures impede native fish passage at multiple locations, including [[Tusher Wash]], [[Taylor Draw Dam]], [[Redlands Diversion]], and structures at [[Price-Stubb]], [[Highline]], and [[Clifton]].

## Key Findings

**Flow Management Actions** (Priority 1-2, 1992-2000):
- Operate [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] pursuant to Biological Opinion for summer/fall flows (begin 1993, ongoing)
- Provide winter/spring test flows for research (1992-1997)
- Augment winter/spring flows permanently (begin 1998)
- Complete administrative procedures for legal protection of flows by December 1994 (summer/fall) and 2000 (winter/spring)
- Lease 3,000 acre-feet storage water from [[Steamboat Lake]] by August 1993
- Enter long-term contracts for 20,000 acre-feet from [[Ruedi Reservoir]]
- Identify year-round flow requirements for Green River below Duchesne River (1992-1998)
- Complete Biological Opinion for Colorado River between Gunnison and Green rivers (1992-1997)
- Reclamation will assist in meeting 2,000 cfs minimum flow below Gunnison confluence (1992-1997)

**Habitat Restoration Actions** (Priority 2-3, 1992-1998):
- Evaluate [[Old Charlie Wash]] for razorback sucker and Colorado squawfish (1992-1994)
- Acquire, restore, and manage bottomland habitat at two selected sites per river reach (1995-1998)
- Construct passage structures at Taylor Draw Dam (1997-1998), Redlands Diversion (1993-1998), and Price-Stubb, Highline, and Clifton diversions (1995-1998)
- Rehabilitate agricultural diversion structures in Yampa/Little Snake rivers (1995-1998)
- Correct selenium problems at Ashley Creek (1993-1998)
- Limit endangered fish access to contaminated areas (1994-1998)

**Nonnative Fish Management** (Priority 2-3, 1992-1998):
- Conduct field studies of channel catfish impacts on Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker young-of-the-year and juveniles (1992-1994)
- Conduct field studies of red shiner competition with Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker larvae in Yampa/Green rivers (1992-1994)
- Determine black crappie predation impacts in Green/White rivers (1995-1998)
- Determine smallmouth bass predation impacts in Duchesne/Green rivers (1992-1994)
- Remove smallmouth bass and black crappie in [[Duchesne River]] (1994-1997)
- Remove channel catfish and northern pike from select Green River habitats (1994-1997)
- Exclude nonnative fishes from razorback sucker habitat at Old Charlie Wash (1992-1994), Escalante (1994-1996), and Ouray National Wildlife Refuge (1994-1996)
- Exclude northern pike from potential spawning habitat in Ashley Creek, Stewart Lake, and Duchesne River (1994-1998)
- Manage flows to control nonnative fishes in Green River below Flaming Gorge Reservoir (1992-1998)
- Modify management of [[Starvation Reservoir]], [[Elkhead Reservoir]], and [[Kenney Reservoir]] fisheries to minimize impacts (1993-1997)

**Genetic Conservation and Population Augmentation** (Priority 1-2, 1991-1998):
- Refine and implement genetic management plans (begin 1992, annual)
- Secure genetic resources in at least two refugia according to populations and stocks identified (1991-1996)
- Complete genetic differentiation studies for endangered fishes (1990-1994)
- Determine species populations and stocks important to recovery goals (1992-1994)
- Augment bonytail in Green River (1994-1998) and Yampa River
- Augment razorback sucker in Yampa River above Yampa Canyon (1992-1996), Colorado River above Palisade (ongoing, 1992-1996), and Gunnison River above Redlands (ongoing, 1992-1996)
- Augment Colorado squawfish in Colorado River above Palisade (ongoing), Gunnison River above Redlands (ongoing), and Dolores River
- Augment humpback chub in Colorado River above Palisade

**Research and Monitoring** (Priority 3, 1990-2000):
- Identify additional spawning sites at [[Labyrinth Canyon]]/[[Stillwater Canyon]] (1994-1996), Desolation/Grey canyons (1994-1996), and 15- and 18-mile reaches of Gunnison River (1994-1996)
- Evaluate importance of tributaries to endangered fishes (1995-1998)
- Determine Colorado squawfish spawner population size and recruitment in [[Yampa Canyon]] (1995-1997)
- Determine relative abundance and fate of Colorado squawfish congregation below Kenney Reservoir (1992-1994)
- Conduct Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker inventory in Gunnison River above Redlands (1992-1993)
- Conduct standardized monitoring program (annual)
- Develop habitat monitoring program and evaluate results from annual channel monitoring
- Conduct interagency data management program (annual)
- Conduct chemoreception-imprinting studies (1991-2000)
- Conduct marking study of young-of-the-year Colorado squawfish (1990-1993)
- Conduct Colorado squawfish otolith study (1991-1993)
- Assess electrofishing impacts to endangered fishes (1993)
- Determine Colorado squawfish population size and recruitment in Colorado River (1991-1994, field work ends 1993)

**Program Management and Public Outreach** (1990-ongoing):
- Conduct survey to measure public awareness and attitudes (1993, again in 1995)
- Produce and distribute newsletter twice yearly
- Write and disseminate 5-10 news releases annually
- Produce and distribute brochure, information cards, signs, video, slide program, posters, and displays
- Identify endangered fishes on fishing licenses and in fishing regulations
- Organize education for river guides (1993)
- Establish research information clearinghouse (1993)
- Update, refine, and prioritize recovery actions annually using Population Viability Assessment and risk assessment modeling (begin 1994, annual)
- Update and refine Interim Management Objectives annually (begin 1994, annual)
- Monitor and assess Recovery Program accomplishments annually (begin 1994, annual)
- Update RIPRAP every 2 years
- Develop annual work plan to address priority needs
- Secure funding for annual work plan and larger projects

**Implementation Schedule**: The document provides detailed implementation schedules for each river reach with specific beginning and ending dates, priorities, and responsible actions. Actions span 1990-2000, with many designated as ongoing or annual activities. The program emphasizes adaptive management, with regular updates to recovery actions based on biological response and new scientific information.
