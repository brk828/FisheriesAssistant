# Littoral Amphipods of Príncipe and São Tomé from the Calypso Expedition (1956)

#marine-biology #amphipoda #carcinology

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by A. and E. [[Mateus]] (Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre", Porto) describes shallow-water amphipod crustaceans collected on the islands of [[Principe Island]] and [[Sao Tome Island]] during the 1956 [[Calypso Expedition]] to the [[Gulf of Guinea]]. Sixteen species belonging to 12 genera and 9 families are reported. Six are described as new to science. The study is presented as the first dedicated treatment of the littoral amphipod fauna of the [[Sao Tome and Principe]] archipelago. The authors note that the collection is small but valuable, because it was gathered deliberately to improve faunal knowledge of the region.

## Background & Description

The material was offered for study by J. [[Forest]], and the authors worked on it in Paris. The authors report several limitations: few specimens of some species, damaged or mutilated animals, poorly represented new forms that could not be prepared for microscopy, and difficulty obtaining bibliography.

**Material and methods**

- Only littoral specimens from São Tomé and Príncipe were treated. Animals taken offshore by the ship's team are reserved for a separate report.
- Samples came from **5 stations on Príncipe** and **7 on São Tomé**. Stations are listed in the "liste des stations biologiques" published by Forest (1959).
- Collecting used dredges, a seine, diving, and wading. Maximum gear depth was 12 m, and dives reached 6 m.
- Substrates were rocky, calcareous-algal, or sandy, and some animals came from pier pilings.
- Microscope slides were mounted in polyvinyl-lacto-phenol and stained with lignin pink. Drawings were made with a [[Camera Lucida Drawing]] method.
- Holotypes are held at the Instituto de Zoologia "Augusto Nobre" in Porto.

**Taxonomic scope**

The paper covers the families Leucothoidae, Stenothoidae, Colomastigidae, Atylidae, Gammaridae, Talitridae, Aoridae, Photidae, and Corophiidae. The authors also comment on the taxonomic status of the family Hyalidae, which Bulycheva (1957) separated from Talitridae. They consider that split unnatural and call for a broad revision of these genera.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Depth and substrate:** Animals were collected from 0 to 12 m. Substrates included rock, calcareous algae, sand, and pier pilings. Some specimens occurred in rock pools (cuvettes rocheuses) at Ponta Furada.
- **Biogeography:** The archipelago falls within region 6 of Reid (1951), which extends from Cameroon to Mocamedes in southern Angola.
- **Size and latitude:** The authors observe that several species are smaller in the equatorial zone than in European or other populations. Examples include [[Elasmopus Pocillimanus]], [[Ericthonius Brasiliensis]], and [[Hyale Gulbenkiani]]. They associate this with a size reduction toward the equator in the Atlantic, which does not occur in the Pacific.
- **Colour and habitat:** In [[Eurystheus Longicanthus]], one individual from Sao Tome's Santa Catarina pier pilings is pigmented brown, while one from Ponta de Sao Sebastiao on rock is depigmented. The authors could not determine whether habitat explains the difference.

## Key Findings

**Overall results**

- **16 species** were identified (the paper also notes that one determination is doubtful). They belong to **12 genera** and **9 families**.
- **6 species are new to science:** [[Leucothoe Denticulata]], [[Stenothoe Uncinifera]], [[Elasmopus Vachoni]], [[Lembos Foresti]], [[Lembos Barbatus]], and [[Eurystheus Longicanthus]].
- Only one earlier-cited species, *Lembos tetracanthus* Schellenberg (1925), was known from São Tomé. It was not part of this collection.
- **Fourteen** of the 16 species had not previously been recorded from the eastern Atlantic between Cameroon and Mocamedes. No species had earlier been recorded from both islands.
- The regional total is now **40 forms**. Of these, **14** are new records from the Calypso material.

**Species-level results**

- *Leucothoe denticulata* (Príncipe, P 10): the holotype is a male, 4.5 mm. The name refers to the teeth on the palmar margin of gnathopod II. It is close to *L. spinicarpa* but is smaller and differs in several appendages.
- *Stenothoe uncinifera* (Príncipe, P 10): known from a single ovigerous female, 4 mm, damaged and missing its telson. The name refers to the hooked tip of uropod III. It resembles *S. valida* but differs in antennal and gnathopod characters.
- *Elasmopus vachoni* (São Tomé, T 26): known from a single female, 4 mm. It is dedicated to Max Vachon, director of the Arthropod Zoology Laboratory at the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle, Paris.
- *Lembos foresti* (Príncipe, P 16): a male holotype, 7 mm, and an ovigerous female allotype bearing 50 eggs. It is dedicated to J. Forest, leader of the Calypso mission. It differs from *L. gambiense* in size and gnathopod form.
- *Lembos barbatus* (Príncipe, P 18): a single incomplete male, 5.5 mm. The name refers to the bristly maxillipeds and gnathopod II.
- *Eurystheus longicanthus* (São Tomé, T 24 and T 28): three specimens. The name refers to the long lateral head lobes.

**Records of known species**

- *Colomastix pusilla* (Príncipe, P 8): the first record for Equatorial Africa. Its distribution is Europe, the Mediterranean, and Bermuda.
- *Talorchestia tricornuta* (Príncipe, P 13): a male, 8 mm, which is smaller than the 11 mm of the original description. Its known range is Banana (Congo) and Santo Antonio (Angola).
- *Hyale gulbenkiani* (Mateus & Mateus, 1962): described from Banyuls-sur-mer and now recorded from Príncipe and São Tomé. It is the only species common to both islands. The African specimens are smaller than European ones.
- *Parhyale inyacka* (São Tomé, T 22): identified with some doubt. The specimen differs from both the original description and Chevreux's (1925) Senegalese material.
- *Elasmopus perditus* (São Tomé, T 22): the authors describe the first female of this species, which was previously known only from a male. The specimens show differences in spination, and the left gnathopod II of the male appears to be a regenerated structure.

**Conclusions and perspectives**

- The authors regard the collection as valuable despite its small size. It "considerably expands" knowledge of equatorial Atlantic amphipod fauna.
- They consider the high number of new species to indicate substantial endemism, possibly produced by isolation-driven speciation. Whether the new species occur elsewhere depends on future exploration.
- Only one species is shared between São Tomé and Príncipe. The authors do not conclude that the faunas differ, because each species is represented by few individuals. More intensive collecting may reveal closer affinities.
- The Hyalidae split and the status of the *Hyale gulbenkiani* / *H. perieri* complex need further work on larger material.
