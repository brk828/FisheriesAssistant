## Executive Summary

This excerpt from Pilsbry's monograph describes new and existing taxa within the land snail genus **[[Sonorella]]**, focusing on two taxonomic units: the newly described **[[Sonorella Grahamensis]]** (type locality: Mt. Graham, Pinaleno Range, Arizona) and the **[[Sonorella Granulatissima Group]]**, a cluster of three closely related forms endemic to the Huachuca Mountains. The work is grounded in detailed shell morphometrics and reproductive (genitalia) anatomy, which Pilsbry uses as the primary basis for species delimitation — a common approach in land snail systematics where shell variation alone is often insufficient.

## Background & Description

### *Sonorella grahamensis* (type species)
- **Shell**: Umbilicate; umbilicus width ~1/8 of shell diameter; shell very thin.
- **Color**: Tawny-olive, paler at base, with a typical band bordered indistinctly by pallid margins; not very glossy.
- **Embryonic whorls**: 1.5 whorls with radial rugosity and granulation after a smooth initial tip; followed by fine, irregular, indistinct wrinkle network; **no oblique threads** (a key diagnostic distinguishing it from the [[Hachitana Group]]).
- **Last whorl**: Weak growth-lines under lens; numerous impressed spiral lines both above and below.
- **Whorl growth**: Slow initial whorl increase, last whorl rapidly widening and descending anteriorly.
- **Aperture**: Rounded-oval, quite oblique; peristome thin, only slightly expanded.
- **Measurements**: Height 10.3 mm; diameter 19 mm; umbilicus 2.2 mm; 4.75 whorls.
- **Genitalia**: Penis with well-developed basal sheath, containing a long tapering verge; penial retractor inserted at base of epiphallus and apex of penis (as in [[Hachitana Group]]); short flagellum present.
  - Penis length: 9 mm
  - Verge: measured
  - Epiphallus: 6 mm
  - Flagellum: 0.5 mm
  - Penial retractor: 4.7 mm
  - Vagina: 7 mm
  - Spermatheca and duct: 19 mm

### *Sonorella granulatissima* Group
- **Shell**: Densely granulose surface texture.
- **Diagnostic absence**: Lacks decurrent threads on the embryonic shell (contrasting with the [[Hachitana Group]]).
- **Genital proportions**: Length of penis typically **less than** shell diameter; vagina notably long, equaling or exceeding shell diameter — this elongated vagina is the key trait separating this group from *S. clappi* and other granulose *Sonorella* species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Sonorella Grahamensis]]** inhabits **dry rock slides** on Marble Peak and its flanks in the Santa Catalina Mountains, distinguishing it in habitat preference from the related **[[Sonorella Marmoris]]** and **[[Sonorella Odorata]]**.
- The type population of *S. grahamensis* occurs at **Mt. Graham**, Pinaleno Range, Graham County, Arizona (elevation ~10,516 ft at summit), a "sky island" range composed of **crumbling granite**, described as very dry on both north and south exposures.
- Summit vegetation: **yellow pine and quaking aspen forest**.
- Co-occurring taxa noted at the same collecting sites: genus **[[Oreohelix]]** (another land snail genus), **[[Vitrina Alaskana]]** (abundant), and juvenile **[[Vallonia]]** specimens.
- The Pinaleno Range is geographically aligned with the **Chiricahua mountain system**, separated from the Dos Cabezas Mountains by a wide mesa (through which the Southern Pacific Railroad runs) — relevant to biogeographic isolation and endemism patterns in *Sonorella*.
- The **[[Sonorella Granulatissima Group]]** occupies the entirety of the **wooded portion of the Huachuca Range**, Arizona, indicating a broader elevational/habitat distribution compared to the rock-slide specialist *S. grahamensis*.

## Key Findings

1. **[[Sonorella Grahamensis]]** is described as a new species (type specimen 109075 A.N.S.P.; additional syntype/locality material 109101 A.N.S.P.), not closely related to any other known *Sonorella* species.
2. Diagnostic characters separating *S. grahamensis* from congeners: a **delicate, spirally striate shell** and **absence of oblique threads on the second (embryonic) whorl**.
3. Habitat divergence is documented within closely related taxa: *S. grahamensis* prefers dry rock slides, unlike the smooth-shelled *S. marmoris* and *S. odorata*, despite morphological/olfactory similarities (shared "peculiar odor").
4. The **[[Sonorella Granulatissima Group]]** (comprising *S. granulatissima*, *S. g. latior*, and *S. danielsi*) is defined as a distinct group based on dense shell granulation, absence of embryonic decurrent threads, and — most diagnostically — a **vagina length equal to or exceeding shell diameter**, unusually long relative to other granulose *Sonorella* species (e.g., *S. clappi*).
5. Genitalic measurements show substantial intraspecific/intrapopulation variation even within a single named taxon (e.g., *S. danielsi* vagina ranges from 13.5–18.5 mm across localities), underscoring the importance of anatomical (not just shell) characters in *Sonorella* taxonomy.
6. Biogeographic note: the Pinaleno Range's isolation from the Chiricahua/Dos Cabezas system via an intervening mesa may correlate with the endemism and distinctiveness of *S. grahamensis*.
