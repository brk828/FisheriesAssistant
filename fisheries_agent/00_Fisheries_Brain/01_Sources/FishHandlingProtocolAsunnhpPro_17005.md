# Fish Handling Protocol (ASU/NNHP) for Humpback Chub Research on the Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #FreshwaterEcology #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary

This document is a field methodology protocol, not a primary ecological study, describing standardized capture, handling, and processing procedures for [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and associated fish taxa in the [[Little Colorado River]] and its confluence with the [[Colorado River]]. The protocol was adapted from established fisheries biology techniques and specifically tailored for endangered species research to balance robust data acquisition with minimization of harm to the humpback chub population. It specifies three primary gear types — [[Hoop Nets]], [[Trammel Nets]], and [[Angling]] — each with detailed deployment, clearing intervals, and fish-processing protocols scaled to fish size class. The document also addresses handling of non-native predatory fishes (e.g., [[Channel Catfish]], trout, striped bass), tissue sampling for genetic analysis, mortality handling, and museum voucher accessioning.

## Background & Description

While this is a procedural protocol rather than a morphological study, it references size-based processing thresholds and diagnostic characters relevant to species identification and life-history staging:

- **Total Length (TL) size classes** used to determine handling procedure:
  - **<100 mm TL**: Measured, weighed, released (no tagging/marking).
  - **100–149 mm TL**: Measured, weighed, fin-clipped (one lobe of caudal fin, or right/left pelvic fin depending on capture location), examined for damage, released.
  - **>150 mm TL**: Measured, weighed, sexed, examined for external damage/tags/markings, scanned and implanted with a [[PIT Tag]] (if none present), released.
- **Diagnostic meristic characters** noted as relevant for species such as [[Razorback Sucker]]: lateral line scale counts, dorsal and anal fin ray counts, and number/arrangement of papilli on lips — standard taxonomic tools in [[Catostomidae]] and [[Cyprinidae]] identification.
- **Tissue sampling** for electrophoretic and mtDNA analysis includes gametes, muscle plugs, and fin punches, indicating a genetic/population-structure component embedded within the handling protocol.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The protocol targets humpback chub populations in the [[Little Colorado River]], a tributary of the [[Colorado River]], within flowing-water (riverine/lotic) conditions requiring specialized net deployment to avoid gear failure (rolling, spinning, collapsing) in current.
- **Community composition sampled**:
  - Native fishes (including humpback chub and other species, e.g., [[Razorback Sucker]]).
  - Predatory non-native fishes: [[Channel Catfish]], trout species, [[Striped Bass]] — treated as a threat vector to native fish via predation and thus targeted for removal and dietary analysis.
  - Non-predatory non-native fishes — documented but with lower handling intensity.
- **Predator-prey interactions**: Digestive tract contents of captured predatory non-natives (notably channel catfish) are examined specifically to detect consumed native fishes, including PIT-tagged individuals — a direct method of quantifying predation pressure on the humpback chub population.
- **Conservation management interface**: Net-clearing intervals (6–72 hours depending on gear and reach) and enhanced monitoring in reaches with known or suspected non-native predator activity reflect adaptive management responses to predation risk within the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem.

## Key Findings

- **Gear-specific protocols**:
  - *[[Hoop Nets]]*: Set by trained personnel in flowing water; upstream end anchored, downstream end floated; cleared every 12–72 hours (reach-dependent), or daily (≤24 hr) where predatory non-natives are present/anticipated.
  - *[[Trammel Nets]]*: Deployed to avoid stressful conditions (e.g., high current velocity); cleared at intervals not exceeding 6 hours — reflecting higher stress risk of this gear type relative to hoop nets.
  - *[[Angling]]*: Used specifically for predatory non-native fish (digestive tract sampling) and for humpback chub in habitats poorly sampled by nets; single-hook artificial lures mandated for chub to prevent injury from hook ingestion; baited hooks for catfish are sized to exclude adult chub bycatch.

- **Fish welfare safeguards**:
  - Fish held in water-filled containers (bucket/livernet) or left in nets during processing.
  - All fish kept moistened to protect the integument during handling.
  - PIT tag needles routinely cleansed in ethanol; Betadine available to treat tag-related wounds.
  - Removed external tags are recorded (color, legend, number) and retained.

- **Tagging and marking strategy** is size-stratified (fin clip for 100–149 mm; PIT tag for >150 mm), enabling long-term individual tracking of larger, reproductively relevant chub while minimizing invasive marking of juveniles.

- **Non-native predator management**: Channel catfish and other predatory non-natives are sacrificed upon capture; digestive tracts are examined for PIT tags and prey identity/length, generating direct evidence of predation impact on native fish populations.

- **Voucher and taxonomic documentation**: Any new species record for the Little Colorado/Colorado river system is preserved in formalin and accessioned into the [[Arizona State University Museum]], ensuring permanent taxonomic reference material.

- **Mortality protocol**: Humpback chub mortalities are preserved via ethanol (≤150 mm TL) or skeletonized/dried (>150 mm TL), with digestive tracts and gonads separately preserved in formalin or ethanol for larger specimens — enabling continued scientific value even from incidental mortality events.

- **Photographic documentation** is required for unusual or distinctive species/individuals encountered during sampling, supporting supplementary qualitative record-keeping alongside quantitative data.
