# Lake Mohave Sampling, November 1997

## Executive Summary
This report documents a three-day fisheries monitoring survey conducted by Arizona State University on [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada border) from November 26–29, 1997. The primary objective was to assess the status of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) population through trammel net sampling. Six 91-meter trammel nets were deployed at depths of 1–10 m across selected shoreline points and embayments, including the Cottonwood East area, uplake toward Carp Cove (Arizona side), and near Bill Gayes Cove (Nevada side). A total of seven fish species were collected, with particular emphasis placed on distinguishing cove-reared (hatchery-stocked) razorback suckers from wild recruits, and on collecting tissue samples for genetic analysis.

## Background & Description
Size and condition data were recorded specifically for captured razorback suckers:

- **Cove-reared individuals (n=6):**
  - 1994 cohort: 1 female, TL 625 mm
  - 1995 cohort: 1 female, TL 554 mm
  - Presumed 1997 cohort: 3 fish, TL 276 mm, 350 mm, and 302 mm
  - Unassigned/ambiguous cohort: 1 fish, TL 424 mm (larger than expected for 1997 stocking, but not matched in the tagging database at time of report)

- **Wild individuals (n=11):** Total lengths not individually itemized in the surviving OCR data, but distinguished from cove-reared fish by visible signs of "wear and tear" (i.e., physical damage/scarring typical of long-term wild survival).

**Key morphological/identification note:** The 1994 cove-reared fish measuring 625 mm TL demonstrates that body size is **no longer a reliable criterion** for distinguishing hatchery-reared [[Razorback Sucker|razorback suckers]] from wild fish, since growth in captivity/cove-rearing can produce individuals as large as older wild fish. Instead, the absence of physical wear (scarring, fin damage, etc.) remains a more dependable field indicator of cove-reared origin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use:** Razorback suckers and co-occurring species were captured across a range of depths (1–10 m) in shoreline zones and embayments, indicating use of near-shore and cove habitats within the reservoir system of [[Lake Mohave]], part of the regulated [[Colorado River]] corridor.
- **Community composition:** The fish assemblage sampled reflects a mixed native-nonnative community typical of altered Colorado River impoundments:
  - Native: [[Razorback Sucker]]
  - Nonnative/introduced: [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]]
- **Conservation context:** The presence of both cove-reared and wild razorback suckers reflects an active **reintroduction/augmentation program** using cove-rearing techniques to bolster this federally endangered species' population in Lake Mohave. Recapture of previously tagged wild individuals (4 of 11 wild fish) indicates an established, monitored resident population with some degree of long-term survivorship in the reservoir.
- **Sampling effort standardization:** Catch data were standardized using catch-per-unit-effort (CPE), with total effort reported as 30.06 units (1 unit = 100 m² of netting fished for 24 hours), enabling comparison across net sets and locations.

## Key Findings
- **Species richness:** Seven fish species were documented in the November 1997 survey: carp, razorback sucker, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, striped bass, largemouth bass, and bluegill.
- **Razorback sucker catch:** 17 total razorback suckers captured — 6 cove-reared, 11 wild.
- **Recruitment monitoring:** Of the 11 wild razorback suckers, 4 were recaptures (previously tagged/handled) and 7 were newly encountered individuals, providing data on population turnover and recruitment.
- **Size no longer diagnostic of origin:** A 625 mm TL cove-reared fish from the 1994 cohort challenges the assumption that large size indicates wild origin; visual condition (absence of wear) is a more reliable indicator.
- **Genetic sampling:** Tissue plugs were collected from 14 of the 17 razorback suckers (all except presumed 1997-stocked fish) for genetic analysis at ASU, supporting ongoing population genetics and stock-identification research.
- **Data gaps:** The 1997 stocking database was incomplete at the time of reporting, complicating cohort assignment for at least one individual (TL 424 mm) whose tag number could not be matched to existing records.
- **Geographic distribution of captures:** Fish were recovered from multiple named localities including Cottonwood, Tequila, Carp Cove, and other embayments, with at least one recorded mortality noted in the capture history table.
