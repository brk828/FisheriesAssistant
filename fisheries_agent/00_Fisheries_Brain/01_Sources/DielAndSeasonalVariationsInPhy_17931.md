# Diel and Seasonal Variations in Physicochemical Limnology, Speed River, Ontario

#limnology #water-quality #freshwater-chemistry

## Executive Summary

This Water Resources Research brief report (Vol. 5, No. 6, December 1969) by John R. M. Kelso and Hugh R. MacCrimmon, University of Guelph, examines how physicochemical properties of the [[Speed River]] vary across seasons and over 24-hour (diel) cycles. The study compares two sites, one below and one above the city of [[Guelph]]. The authors argue that routine daily, occasional or seasonal sampling cannot describe local limnological conditions. Dissolved oxygen, pH and free carbon dioxide showed clear diel pulses, while iron and fluoride remained stable. Downstream of Guelph, levels of ammonia, chloride, nitrate, nitrite, orthophosphate, sulphate and synthetic detergents were higher, and hexavalent chromium and copper were detected only there. The paper concludes that sampling at dusk and dawn is needed to capture the diel range of cyclic properties.

## Background & Description

- **Rationale:** Water-quality judgments were usually based on occasional, seasonal or at best daily samples. The authors noted that diel pulses in river chemistry had received little attention, and that only a few reports on dissolved-oxygen pulses existed (e.g., [[Tressler]] et al. 1940 on lakes; [[Reed]] 1962 and [[Minckley]] 1963 on rivers).
- **Study area:** The [[Speed River]] catchment drains 752 km² (one OCR variant reads 782 km²). Its two main branches, the Speed River and the [[Eramosa River]], rise in Garafraxa East and Erin townships and join at Guelph before reaching the [[Grand River]]. Gradients are 2.8 m/km for the Speed and 3.5 m/km for the Eramosa. About 65,000 people live in Guelph, Hespeler and Preston, in the lower 25 km of the watershed. Land elsewhere in the catchment is mainly agricultural.
- **Sampling stations:**
  - **Station 1:** 4.3 km below Guelph. Mean width 15.8 m (one reading 15.3 m), mean depth 183 cm. Bed is a composite of silt, clay and organic matter.
  - **Station 2:** 6.5 km above Guelph (control). Mean width 4.5 m (one reading 4.6 m), mean depth 35 cm. Bed is boulder and gravel.
- **Approximate coordinates:** latitude 43°30′, longitude 80°16′.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling design**
- Routine samples were collected at both stations every second Wednesday at 1000 EST, from August 1, 1967 to September 1, 1968.
- Diel (24-hour) sampling at station 1 took place on four dates: October 31–November 1, 1967 (fall); February 22–23, 1968 (winter); April 29–30, 1968 (spring); and July 23–24, 1968 (summer). Station 2 served as a control on the last two dates.
- Sampling followed meteorological criteria supplied by the University of Guelph Soil Department. Conditions had to be stable for at least one week before sampling, with cumulative rainfall no more than 1.50 cm and mean daily temperature not differing by more than 5°C.
- Station 1 samples were composites of mid-depth water at midstream plus samples taken 5 m from each bank, 15 cm below the surface. Station 2 used a single grab sample.

**Analytical methods**
- Samples were collected in 700 ml polyethylene bottles washed in HCl, and routine samples were analysed within two days.
- Field measures (pH, specific conductance and turbidity) used a portable Hach DR-EL laboratory.
- Analyses followed [[American Public Health Association]] (1960) standard methods.
- Statistics: analysis of variance for a randomised complete block design, with differences between diel means compared by the [[Least Significant Difference]] test at the 1% and 5% levels. Only the 5% level was reported.

**Diel and seasonal processes discussed**
- Diel oxygen maxima occurred near dusk (1700–2200 h) and minima near dawn (0600–0800 h).
- Free CO₂ and pH were inversely related, and high CO₂ in early morning coincided with low pH.
- Stable fall and winter temperatures narrowed the oxygen range, while reduced biological activity lessened supersaturation.
- Orthophosphate levels in winter were attributed to discharge from the [[Guelph Sewage Treatment Plant]].

## Key Findings

**Seasonal levels (Aug 1967 – Sep 1968)**
- Dissolved oxygen at station 1 ranged from 1.7 mg/l (August 1967) to 14.5 mg/l (February 1968). Highest levels (12–14.5 mg/l) occurred in January to March; lowest (5.2–7.0 mg/l) occurred in summer. Station 2 followed the same pattern.
- Lowest pH (7.48–7.60) occurred during high discharge, with CO₂ of 9.0–14.0 mg/l. Above Guelph, pH ranged from 7.69 to 8.10 and free CO₂ from 0.0 to 5.0 mg/l.
- Ammonia peaked in winter and summer. Alkalinity rose to a maximum in late fall and early winter.
- Nitrite and sulphate were consistently higher in winter and summer.

**Diel findings**
- Dissolved oxygen was the most regularly cyclic property. The maximum at station 1 was 14.5 mg/l under winter ice (0.5°C). The minimum was 2.0 mg/l on July 23–24 (22.2°C). Low oxygen coincided with high temperature.
- Station 2 oxygen varied less (range 3.7 mg/l), peaked before nightfall and was lowest between 0600 and 0800 h.
- Free CO₂ was highest just before or after sunrise and was undetectable above pH 8.10.
- Orthophosphate showed a cycle in October–February that followed dissolved oxygen, but later collections did not confirm it. Station 1 means differed significantly at the 5% level.
- Daylight samples could show oxygen levels up to five times higher than dawn or dusk samples, which could lead to an overly optimistic view of water quality.

**Noncyclic and stable properties**
- **Hexavalent chromium:** 0.57 mg/l on October 31–November 1, 1967. It rose from 0.04 mg/l at about 2000 h to 0.57 mg/l and fell back to 0.04 mg/l by 0800 h.
- **Copper and chromium:** Trace amounts only below Guelph, in about 30% of samples. Neither metal was measurable above Guelph.
- **Fluoride** (means 0.15–0.20 mg/l) and **iron** (means up to 0.25 mg/l) were stable during all samplings.

**Upstream vs downstream differences**
- Chloride averaged 36–62 mg/l below Guelph and 12–14 mg/l above.
- Ammonia averaged 1.64–2.25 mg/l, recorded only below the city.
- Nitrite above Guelph was consistently very low (0.003–0.018 mg/l). On July 23–24, 1968 the nitrite mean below the city was 0.228 mg/l, which differed from every other diel nitrite mean.
- Turbidity, sulphate, nitrate-N and nitrite were higher below Guelph. Silica was about 1.0 mg/l higher above Guelph.
- Alkalinity and hardness were similar above and below the city. Total alkalinity and hardness varied by no more than 25 mg/l CaCO₃ within a diel period.
- Sulphate changed by up to 40 mg/l within the diel period of February 22–23, 1968.
- Synthetic detergents, copper and hexavalent chromium were present below Guelph but not above.

**Table 1 (station 1, four diel sampling dates)**
- Total alkalinity: 115, 70, 68 and 123 mg/l.
- Water temperature: 8.4, 0.6, 10.9 and 23.7 °C.
- Nitrate-N, ammonia, turbidity and other values were reported in the original table. Means sharing a line were not significantly different.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Sampling at dusk and dawn (0600–0800 h and 1600–1800 h) captures the diel range of cyclic properties.
- Limits should qualify the use of dissolved oxygen, free CO₂, pH, orthophosphate, nitrogen forms, chloride, sulphate and specific conductance in temporal studies.
- Routine daily or occasional sampling can show general trends but misses much of the variability that affects human and aquatic life.
