# New species of Catostomus from the Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomus

## Executive Summary

This 1880 *Proceedings of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia* excerpt contains a formal description of a new sucker, [[Catostomus Cypho]] (sp. nov.), by [[William N. Lockington]]. The species is known from a single, damaged specimen collected from the [[Colorado River]] at its junction with the [[Gila River]]. The description focuses on a distinctive dorsal hump, an elongated caudal peduncle, inferior mouth morphology, and detailed skeletal and scale characters. The same issue also includes minutes of the [[Mineralogical and Geological Section]] (1877–1879), covering a new [[Polariscope]] design, a garnet with inverted crystallization, and the alteration of [[Serpentine]] to [[Quartz]].

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic account.** The species is named *Catostomus cypho* by Lockington. The meristic formula given is D. 3,14; A. 2,7; C. 7-1-16-1-7; P. 18; V. 10; lateral line scales 79.

**External morphology.**
- Head conical, with a long, strongly depressed snout.
- A prominent, elevated dorsal hump begins at the occiput. Its greatest height lies about one snout-length behind the occiput.
- Mouth wide and inferior. The lower lip is small, in two ovoid lobes covered with flat-topped papillae. The dentary is capped by a horny plate.
- Anterior nostril horizontally sub-elliptical. Posterior nostril large, vertical, and crescentic.
- Two rows of pores run along the top of the head, connected to a series that extends below the eye toward the snout tip.
- Pectoral fins are triangular-lanceolate, with the fourth and fifth rays longest.
- Ventral fins reach beyond the vent. The dorsal fin has its fourth and fifth rays longest.
- Caudal fin has numerous accessory rays and a triangularly emarginate posterior margin.
- Fins are scaleless, as is a small patch on the dorsal hump.

**Proportions (as stated).** Greatest depth is contained not quite 4½ times in total length. Head is a little more than 4 times in total length. Snout is a little more than 2½ times in head length. Eye is between 8 and 9 times in head length. Inter-ocular width equals snout length. Caudal peduncle is about 3½ times in greatest depth.

**Scales and lateral line.** Scales are cycloid and variable in size, with 8–16 radiating striae on the exposed portion. The largest scales lie on the caudal peduncle, and the smallest on the abdomen and behind the pectoral base. The lateral line is deflected near its origin, then runs along the median line to the caudal origin.

**Skeletal features.** The hump is supported by a large trapezoidal [[Interneural Bones]] series. The first vertebra bears a broad articulating surface and expanded transverse processes fused to bony plates that connect the air bladder to the skull. The neural spines of the first two of the next nine vertebrae are bifid.

**Coloration (preserved specimen).** The body is silvery-gray dorsally and light straw or creamy ventrally. Fins are uniform light slaty-gray. Color arises from dark dots on the scales and membranes.

**Type material.** The description is based on a single specimen. It was collected from the Colorado River, sent to the [[California Academy of Sciences]] by [[John E. Curry]], civil engineer. The specimen is badly damaged: the large interneural was extracted about two years earlier, the air bladder is destroyed (so the number of chambers cannot be determined), fin tips are broken, and the body is distorted. A dimension table accompanies the text, but OCR degradation makes many values unreliable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The author reports that the species is "not uncommon" in the collecting locality, a claim based on local information rather than on additional specimens. The lack of more material is described as a serious limitation. The collecting site, the confluence of the Colorado and [[Gila River]], is a large freshwater system. The source gives no data on diet, habitat use, reproduction, or population size.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Catostomus cypho* is described from a single damaged specimen from the Colorado River at the Gila junction.
- **Diagnostic traits:** the dorsal hump, extremely elongated caudal peduncle that widens toward the base, inferior mouth with papillate lower lip lobes, and a horny dentary plate.
- **Meristics:** dorsal rays 3,14; anal 2,7; caudal 7-1-16-1-7; pectoral 18; ventral 10; lateral line 79 scales.
- **Skeletal peculiarities:** a large trapezoidal interneural supports the hump, and the first vertebra connects to the air bladder through expanded bony plates.
- **Limitations:** the holotype is damaged, the air bladder is absent, and the species is known from one individual.
- **Mineralogical section (1877–1879):** [[H. C. Lewis]] presented a low-cost polariscope using black-glass reflection polarization and a Nicol's prism. It measured optic-axial divergence to within 30′. [[Lewis]] also exhibited a Germantown garnet showing inverted crystallization, with one octant depressed and its apex halfway to the centre. [[Theodore D. Rand]] described specimens showing the change of serpentine into quartz.
