## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative memorandum from the Colorado Division of Wildlife announcing the **1995 Annual Upper Basin Researchers Meeting**, held in conjunction with the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]]'s Biology Committee Reporting Meeting (February 8–10, 1995, Grand Junction, Colorado). While not a primary research paper, the attached agenda is a valuable index of ongoing scientific studies (as of 1994–1995) targeting the recovery of the **four endangered Colorado River fishes**: the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. The agenda enumerates specific research projects covering population demographics, habitat suitability, taxonomy, chemoreception, experimental stocking, and capital infrastructure projects (fish passage, propagation, and instream flow protection) across the Upper Colorado River Basin, including the Green, Yampa, Duchesne, and Gunnison River subbasins.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body proportions) are presented in this memorandum. However, the agenda references active taxonomic research directly relevant to morphology:

- **#26 – Gila Taxonomy: Enzyme & Morphological Analysis** (Starnes): An allozyme and morphometric study aimed at resolving taxonomic relationships within the genus *Gila*, likely addressing the contested distinctiveness of [[Bonytail]] vs. [[Humpback Chub]] vs. [[Roundtail Chub]].
- **#27 – Gila Taxonomy: Mitochondrial DNA (MtDNA)** (Starnes): A complementary molecular genetics study using mtDNA analysis to clarify phylogenetic and species-level distinctions among *Gila* taxa in the Colorado River Basin.

These paired allozyme/morphology and mtDNA studies indicate an active period of taxonomic revision for the endangered *Gila* species complex, foundational to defining recovery units and hybridization concerns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The agenda topics collectively map the ecological and habitat research priorities for the endangered fish community of the Upper Colorado River Basin:

- **Bottomland and Floodplain Habitat**: 
  - #23 – Bottomland Reconnaissance Survey (Irving)
  - Cap. Projects #6 – Flooded Bottomlands Enhancement (Nelson), highlighting the importance of seasonally inundated floodplain habitat for larval and juvenile rearing of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and other big-river fishes.
- **Mainstem and Tributary Habitat Suitability**:
  - #18 – Upper Colorado River Habitat Suitability (Anderson)
  - #49 – [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]/[[Razorback Sucker]] Green River Habitat Availability (Modde)
  - #38 – Potential [[Razorback Sucker]] & [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] Spawning Habitat (Lentsch)
- **Non-native Fish Interactions**:
  - #24 – Green/Duchesne River Non-Native Fish Control (Lentsch), indicating active management of competing/predatory non-native fish species within critical habitat.
- **Sensory Ecology**:
  - #31 – Chemoreception Studies (Modde), suggesting research into olfactory or chemosensory cues potentially relevant to spawning migration or predator avoidance behavior.
- **Population Structure**:
  - #14 – Population Size/Age Structure (Osmundson), a key demographic monitoring effort presumably for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]].
- **Infrastructure & Connectivity**:
  - Redlands Fish Passage, Price/Stubb Diversion Dam Passage, and Yampa River Fish Passage capital projects address anthropogenic barriers to migration and habitat connectivity, critical to spawning access for all four endangered species.
  - Yampa River Instream Flow Protection underscores the ecological importance of maintaining natural hydrograph and flow regimes for spawning cues and habitat maintenance.
- **Reservoir Operations**:
  - Cap. Projects #14 – Coordinated Reservoir Operations, linking upstream water management to downstream habitat conditions for endangered fish.

## Key Findings

- The meeting/agenda confirms **active, coordinated multi-agency research** (Colorado Division of Wildlife, Bureau of Reclamation, and various principal investigators) under the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] framework as of 1994–1995.
- Two parallel taxonomic studies (**allozyme/morphology** and **mtDNA**) were underway specifically to resolve species-level questions within the genus *Gila*, directly bearing on the conservation status and management units of [[Bonytail]] and [[Humpback Chub]].
- A dedicated **Bonytail Reintroduction Plan** (#25) was on the agenda, indicating this species had reached a stage of recovery planning requiring active stocking/reintroduction strategy discussion.
- An **Experimental Razorback Stocking** project (#50, Burdick) reflects early-stage augmentation efforts for [[Razorback Sucker]] populations.
- Substantial research and capital investment were directed at **fish passage infrastructure** (Redlands, Price/Stubb Diversion Dam, Yampa River) to mitigate habitat fragmentation caused by diversion structures.
- **Propagation and Genetics Management** (Cap. Projects #2, Wydoski) indicates a formal hatchery-based genetic management program was in place to support reintroduction and augmentation of the four endangered species while preserving genetic diversity.
- The breadth of topics — spanning taxonomy, habitat suitability, sensory biology, population monitoring, and engineering interventions — illustrates the integrated, multi-disciplinary approach required for recovery of endangered big-river fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].
