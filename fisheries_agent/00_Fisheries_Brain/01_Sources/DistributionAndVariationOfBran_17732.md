# Distribution and Variation of Branchiostoma caribaeum in Mississippi Sound

#ichthyology #marine-biology #branchiostomatidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 study by [[Herbert Boschung]] and [[Gordon Gunter]], published in *Tulane Studies in Zoology*, documents the distribution, abundance, habitat preference and meristic variation of the lancelet [[Branchiostoma Caribaeum]] in [[Mississippi Sound]]. Using samples from a 1960 [[Pan American Petroleum Company]] survey and student collections, the authors show that the species is present in large numbers over roughly 350 square miles, is strongly associated with coarse sand, and displays wider meristic variation than previously recorded. They conclude that statistical analysis of populations is needed before lancelet species can be unequivocally separated.

## Background & Description

Branchiostoma caribaeum is the only lancelet known from the Gulf of Mexico. Early records were sparse: [[Garman]] noted specimens from the Gulf without locality, while [[Wright]] (1890) reported the first localized collection from Tampa Bay, Florida. Subsequent records extended the known range along Florida's Gulf coast (Sanibel, Tortugas, Boca Ciega Bay, Tampa Bay and the Snapper Banks off Pensacola), and then westward to Texas (first record by Baughman, 1950), Mississippi, Louisiana and Alabama. Large numbers were recorded on West Florida beaches, but the northern Gulf distribution was described as spotty and thin, and the species appeared uncommon along the roughly thousand-mile sand coast between Sabine Pass and Tampico.

Morphological characters examined included:
- Total myotomes, preatriopore, postatriopore and postanal myotome counts
- Dorsal and preanal (ventral) fin-ray chambers
- Caudal fin shape and length, and body depth
- Oral cirri
- Gonad position and anus position

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling was conducted by coring and by modified Petersen grab (one-eighth square meter samples) across Mississippi Sound and adjacent Gulf bottoms, with stations shown in Figure 1 (closed circles marking lancelet presence). Because the geological survey crews were not searching for lancelets, the counts are considered minimal.

Key habitat findings:
- Lancelets were most abundant in coarse or medium-coarse sand, or coarse sand mixed with silt. Of hundreds of sediment samples, about nine in ten yielded lancelets, rarely in fine sand and never in clay.
- Greatest numbers in shallow water occurred where shell and plant debris was common.
- Salinity at 82 positive stations ranged from 15.4 to 33.1 per mille (mean 24.3); outside stations were up to 36.0.
- The greatest depth recorded was 15 fathoms, 20 miles south of Mobile Bay.
- Associated fauna included [[Saccoglossus]], the echinoid [[Mellita]], nemertean and annelid worms, and several pelecypods.
- The authors observed lancelets wriggling into sand, a burial behavior not previously recorded.

## Key Findings

- **Abundance:** The Pan American survey and student collections suggest the population covers about 350 square miles between Mobile Bay and Louisiana, with total numbers "in the order of several billions." One student group alone took more than a thousand specimens in one collecting effort.
- **Meristic counts (n = 100 unless noted):**
  - Total myotomes: 57–60 (mean 58.6)
  - Preatriopore myotomes: 35–37 (mean 36.1)
  - Postatriopore myotomes: 21–24 (mean 22.5)
  - Dorsal fin-ray chambers: 252–359 (mean 306.9); 84% had 281–330
  - Preanal fin-ray chambers: 35–61 (n = 84; mean 47.2); 81% had 41–55
  - Anus position: myotomes 50–53, mode 52
  - Gonads per side: 46–57 (mean 51.4, mode 52; n = 100 mature specimens, 32–52 mm)
  - Oral cirri (left side): 16–24, increasing with body length
- **Range extensions:** Combining literature data, dorsal fin-ray chamber variation across the species spans 227–359 (a range of 133), and preanal chambers span 15–61. The Mississippi variation of 108 dorsal chambers is described as unparalleled by any known population.
- **Taxonomic comparison:** Total myotome range overlaps *B. platae* (55–65) and broadly overlaps *B. bermudae* by only one count. Dorsal fin-ray chamber variation of *B. caribaeum* narrowly overlaps *B. bermudae* and encompasses *B. platae*. Overall, *B. caribaeum* is more similar to *B. platae* than to *B. bermudae*, though not for all characters.
- **Unreliable character:** Fin-ray chamber measurements, taken with an ocular micrometer, are affected by specimen opacity; the authors judged the character of limited diagnostic value. No correlation was found between dorsal chamber number and total length (e.g., specimens of 19.4 and 20.8 mm had 252 and 359 chambers).
- **Proportions:** Postatriopore length was 27.4–32.4% of total length (61% in the 29–30% class); body depth and postatriopore length scale roughly directly with total length.
- **Caudal fin and anus:** The anus is almost always behind the center of the lower caudal lobe; postanal length averages 40% of the lower caudal lobe (range 30–53%).
- **Gonad formula:** The modal formula is 9–25–1 (anterior myotomes, gonadal myotomes, post-gonadal myotomes to atriopore).
- **Conclusion:** Meristic variation in lancelets is broad, and similar characters can vary differently between species, so population-level statistical analyses are required for unequivocal species separation.
