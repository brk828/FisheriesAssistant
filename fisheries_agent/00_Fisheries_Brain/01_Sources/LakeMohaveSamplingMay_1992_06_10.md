# Lake Mohave Sampling, May 1992

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary
A cooperative survey by Arizona State University and the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation sampled [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada) over a 5-day period in May 1992 using trammel nets and nighttime electrofishing. The primary objective was to capture adult [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), two endangered native Colorado River fishes, for tagging, health assessment, and population monitoring. Trammel netting in the Cottonwood Bay vicinity yielded 1,158 fish across 9 species, dominated numerically by non-native [[Common Carp]] (81.95%). Three wild adult bonytail and 101 razorback suckers (88 netted, 13 electrofished) were captured, measured, PIT-tagged, and released. Recapture data indicated no detectable growth in length among repeat-encountered razorback suckers over the sampling intervals.

## Background & Description
### Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)
- **Sample size:** 3 adults (all considered wild stock based on size, condition, and absence of prior marks/tags)
- **Total Length (TL):** 38.4–44.5 cm (individual values: 44.5, 42.0, 38.4 cm)
- **Weight:** 770 g recorded for one individual (44.5 cm, female); weights not recorded for the other two
- **Sex ratio:** 1 female, 2 males
- **Parasitism:** All three individuals exhibited *Lernaea* (anchor worm) infestation
- **Capture depth/substrate:** <3 m depth, over cobble substrate
- **Capture dates:** One fish on 06 May 1992 (morning), two fish on 08 May 1992

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Total captured:** 101 individuals (88 via trammel net; 13 via electrofishing)
- **Males (n=41):** mean TL 56.8 ± 3.2 cm (range 48.8–66.0 cm)
- **Females (n=47):** mean TL 63.5 ± 2.9 cm (range 56.4–69.0 cm)
- Size distributions were consistent with prior routine monitoring data (November and March samples) collected over the preceding decade
- Recaptured individuals showed an average length difference of approximately -1 cm between capture and recapture events, attributed to measurement error rather than true shrinkage; **no evidence of growth in length** was detected in recaptured fish

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use:** Adult bonytail were found in relatively shallow water (<3 m) over cobble substrate in the Cottonwood Bay vicinity, suggesting a preference for structured littoral habitat during this sampling window.
- **Spatial distribution of razorback sucker:** Individuals were recaptured across multiple distinct areas of the reservoir, including [[Cottonwood Bay]]-Carp Cove, [[Arizona Bay]] (Yuma Cove), and the Six-mile Coves (Nevada), indicating movement or a broad distribution of the tagged population within Lake Mohave.
- **Community context:** The fish assemblage in Cottonwood Bay is heavily dominated by non-native [[Common Carp]] (>80% of catch), with native [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] representing a small minority (0.26% and 7.60% respectively) of the trammel net catch, reflecting the broader pattern of native fish decline in Colorado River reservoirs due to non-native species establishment.
- **Parasite ecology:** All captured bonytail were infected with *Lernaea* (copepod ectoparasite), suggesting endemic parasite pressure in the wild bonytail population of Lake Mohave.
- **Arizona Bay backwater:** Functioned as a nursery/juvenile habitat for razorback sucker, separately targeted via seining to sample juveniles, distinct from adult-targeted trammel netting elsewhere.

## Key Findings
- **Elevated razorback sucker catch:** The May 1992 trammel net catch of razorback sucker (88 fish, 7.5% of total catch) substantially exceeded catches from the same season in the previous four years (historically 3–28 fish, 0.8–3.5% of catch), suggesting either increased population abundance, improved capture efficiency, or aggregation behavior during this sampling period.
- **Confirmed wild bonytail recruitment/persistence:** Three adult bonytail were captured with no PIT tags or prior marks, and were assessed as wild-origin fish based on size and condition — a notable finding given the extreme rarity of wild bonytail in the Colorado River system.
- **Long-term recapture history:** Of 101 total razorback sucker captures, 13 were long-term recaptures (>60 days since initial marking), with initial marking dates spanning March 1990 to March 1992, demonstrating multi-year survivorship and effective long-term PIT tag retention.
- **No detectable growth:** Despite recapture intervals of up to two years, no clear growth in total length was observed among recaptured razorback sucker, consistent with the hypothesis that these are older, slow-growing adults near asymptotic length.
- **Movement patterns:** Recapture locations differing from original tagging locations (e.g., fish marked in Arizona Bay or Six-mile Coves and recaptured in Cottonwood Bay) indicate that razorback sucker in Lake Mohave are capable of substantial within-reservoir movement.
- **Parallel conservation activity:** A concurrent effort in the Arizona Bay (Yuma Cove) backwater targeted removal of adult razorback suckers and collection of juveniles, with full results to be reported separately to the [[Native Fish Work Group]].
