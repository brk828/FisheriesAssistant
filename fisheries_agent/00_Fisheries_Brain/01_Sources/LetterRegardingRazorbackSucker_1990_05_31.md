## Executive Summary
This 1990 letter from the Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit documents the discovery of a previously unknown population of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in upper [[Fossil Creek]], Arizona. The correspondence, addressed to Frank Baucom of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], outlines the urgency of the finding given the pending federal listing of the species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (proposed May 22, 1990) and announces a planned capture-depletion population study to assess the size, health, and reproductive status of this population.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Xyrauchen texanus* (Razorback Sucker), family [[Catostomidae]].
- Adult individuals captured and observed were noted to be "healthy."
- Adult razorback suckers are described as **morphologically distinctive**, characterized by a pronounced dorsal keel/hump behind the head — a trait that makes field identification straightforward even for personnel without prior species-specific experience.
- No juveniles were confirmed captured at the time of writing; their potential presence was a key unresolved question motivating the electroshocking component of the study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Upper [[Fossil Creek]], a tributary stream system in Arizona (part of the greater [[Verde River]] watershed).
- **Population origin:** Unknown — it is unclear whether this population is native/relict or the result of introduction/stocking.
- **Reproductive status:** Unknown at time of writing; whether the population is self-sustaining (actively reproducing) in Fossil Creek had not yet been determined, representing a central research question.
- **Conservation context:** The species was nearing the end of a ten-year moratorium on ESA listing, with a formal proposal for endangered status issued May 22, 1990. Should listing proceed, this Fossil Creek population would automatically be classified as endangered regardless of its origin (native vs. introduced).

## Key Findings
- A population of [[Razorback Sucker]] was newly located in upper Fossil Creek; two adults were captured and several additional individuals were visually observed.
- The individuals encountered appeared to be in good health, suggesting a potentially viable population.
- A **capture-depletion sampling study** was scheduled for **July 17–19, 1990** to estimate population size.
- **Methodology planned:**
  - [[Trammel Nets]] run in four-hour intervals, continuously around the clock, to minimize cumulative stress on captured fish while maximizing sampling coverage over multiple days.
  - Captured fish to be tagged and held temporarily in **live cars** (in-stream holding enclosures) before release.
  - Supplemental **electroshocking** to survey for juvenile razorbacks, addressing the open question of active recruitment/reproduction.
  - Logistics required 6–8 personnel per net run cycle; the Cooperative Unit supplied nets and cooking/field facilities.
- The population was hypothesized to be small enough that a multi-day depletion sampling approach would yield a statistically adequate estimate of abundance.
- The effort involved substantial interagency coordination, with copies distributed to representatives of the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Coconino National Forest]], [[Prescott National Forest]], [[Tonto National Forest]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and multiple USFWS divisions (PES, FAO, DNFH), reflecting the significance of the finding across regional resource management bodies.
- This letter serves as an early historical record potentially relevant to subsequent recovery planning efforts for the [[Razorback Sucker]] in Arizona's Verde River drainage.
