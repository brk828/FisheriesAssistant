## Executive Summary

This 1952 field survey, conducted ahead of the construction of the Kinzua High Dam, quantified the densities, species composition, sex ratios, and riffle-habitat preferences of ten [[Darter (Etheostomatinae)|darter species]] across the [[Upper Allegheny River Watershed]] and its major tributary, [[French Creek]]. Seining 75 stations (37,561 sq ft total) yielded 4,947 darters, with an overall density of one darter per 7.6 sq ft (~5,739 darters/acre). *[[Etheostoma caeruleum]]* (rainbow darter) was the most abundant species overall, followed by *[[Etheostoma flabellare]]* (fantail darter) and *[[Etheostoma zonale]]* (banded darter). Species composition and sex ratios varied significantly between French Creek, glaciated Allegheny River tributaries, and unglaciated Allegheny River tributaries. The study serves as a baseline record of darter ecology prior to anticipated anthropogenic disruption (impoundment, pollution, deforestation, water demand).

## Background & Description

- **Study area**: 7,228 of the 11,500 square miles drained by the Allegheny River, spanning from its headwaters in Potter County, PA (elevation 2,600 ft) through southwestern New York and back into northwestern Pennsylvania, terminating near Franklin, PA.
- **Major tributary**: [[French Creek]], originating near Waits Corners, NY (elevation 1,700 ft), descending to 989 ft at its mouth in Franklin, PA; averages a 10 ft/mile fall over 78 miles with riffles roughly every 1.5 miles.
- **Glacial history**: The watershed's present form results from four glacial advances/retreats (Wisconsin moraine boundary depicted in Figure 1), producing distinct **glaciated** and **unglaciated** sub-regions of the Allegheny River with differing darter assemblages.
- **Hydrology/Water Chemistry**:
  - Average annual water temperature ~8.1°C (range 0–38°C); coldest in Jan/Feb, hottest in July.
  - Average annual precipitation ~41.1 in (range 34–52 in); peak rainfall in June/July.
  - pH range: 6.0–8.5.
  - Dissolved oxygen: generally ≥6.5 ppm; dropping to 5–6.5 ppm below Bradford, Meadville, Oil City, and Olean; as low as 3 ppm below Kane and Corry, PA.
  - Alkalinity: ~50 ppm (soft water) in headwaters of Allegheny River/French Creek; 50–100 ppm (hard water) in the remainder of the system.
- **Species recorded**: *Ammocrypta pellucida*, *[[Percina caprodes|Percina c. caprodes]]*, *Percina c. semifasciata*, *Percina evides*, *Percina macrocephala*, *[[Percina maculata]]*, *[[Etheostoma blennioides|Etheostoma b. blennioides]]*, *[[Etheostoma caeruleum|Etheostoma c. caeruleum]]*, *[[Etheostoma camurum]]*, *[[Etheostoma flabellare]]*, *[[Etheostoma maculatum]]*, *[[Etheostoma nigrum|Etheostoma n. nigrum]]*, *Etheostoma tippecanoe*, *[[Etheostoma variatum]]*, *[[Etheostoma zonale]]*.
- **Geographic range**: Broadly bounded by Hudson Bay/southern Canada to Wisconsin/Minnesota, south to Alabama, east to the Allegheny Mountains, north to western PA/southern NY.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat preference**: Most darters inhabit riffles with clean, clear, moderate-flow water over rubble, sand, and gravel substrate; some tolerate filamentous algae or *Vallisneria*. Exceptions (*Percina c. caprodes*, *P. c. semifasciata*, *E. n. nigrum*, *P. maculata*, *P. macrocephala*) prefer slack/pool habitats.
- **Seasonal movement debate**: Contrary to earlier hypotheses (Trautman 1957; Winn 1958a,b) that darters migrate en masse between riffles and deep pools seasonally, Schwartz argues this is unlikely in Pennsylvania streams (which flow year-round, unlike intermittent Ohio streams) and that most Allegheny watershed darters are riffle-specific with minimal migration.
- **Species zonation within riffles**:
  - *E. maculatum* occupies the upper third (deepest/swiftest) of riffles in spring/summer, shifting to lower/shallower thirds by mid-November; remains on riffle year-round.
  - *E. camurum* occupies the lower two-thirds of the deepest riffle zone in spring but disappears from riffles entirely by November.
  - *E. flabellare* occupies the lower two-thirds of riffles, with larger adults in deeper water and juveniles near shore.
  - *E. zonale* prefers the mid-third of riffles, avoiding vegetation.
  - *E. blennioides* prefers the lower two-thirds, typically absent where vegetation/filamentous algae present.
  - *E. variatum* prefers the lower, larger regions of streams, diminishing upstream.
  - *E. caeruleum* is habitat-generalist within riffles, found at all positions and depths.
- **Anthropogenic threats identified**: The completion of the [[Kinzua Dam]] was expected to inundate and permanently alter large portions of darter habitat. Additional threats cited: domestic/industrial pollution, deforestation-driven warming and evaporation, and increasing freshwater demand from population growth.
- **Regional comparison**: Reed (1953) recorded much higher densities (1.1 darter/sq ft) in Little Sugar, Bennett Run, and Conneaut Creek via repeated seining, versus Schwartz's single-pass seining (~85% efficiency), suggesting Schwartz's figures may underestimate true densities. Wickliff (1939) cited 40,000 darters/acre for Ohio riffles versus 5,739/acre found here.

- **French Creek**: *E. caeruleum* (29.5%) and *E. zonale* (20.5%) dominant; *E. variatum* (18.6%) and *E. blennioides* (15.4%) secondary; *E. flabellare* present but subdominant except in extreme headwaters.

### Species-Specific Notes

**[[Etheostoma caeruleum]] (Rainbow Darter)**
- Comprised 0–50% of riffle community; most abundant species overall.
- Males outnumbered females ~2:1 across all sub-regions.
- Found throughout riffle zones; two nests observed at riffle crest in 6–8 inch deep water (South Branch and West Branch of French Creek). Nest in West Branch guarded by two males in fine gravel; no nests found deeper than 10 inches.
- Estimated spawning period in western PA: May 29–June 14, 1952 (differs from Reeves 1907's April 24–June 2 for likely *E. spectabile*, and from Trautman's 1930 deeper-water spawning observation).
- Most vividly colored specimens found in French Creek.

**[[Etheostoma flabellare]] (Fantail Darter)**
- Second most abundant; occurred in 60/75 collections.
- Females outnumbered males 1.6:1 on average, consistent across sub-regions; agrees with Lake (1936)'s 1:2.3 ratio.
- Occupied lower two-thirds of riffles; larger individuals in deeper water.
- Nest found June 21, 1952 in East Hickory Creek, Endeavor, PA (4–6 in depth): 349 eggs hatched within 30 minutes, larvae averaging 7.3 mm TL.
- Second nest found June 12, 1953 in French Creek at Wattsburg, PA: 176 eggs on a 12×4 in stone in a 12–14 in deep riffle, cavity opening upstream.
- Lake (1936) reports egg counts of 8–562 per nest, hatching in batches of 45; Winn (1958a,b) reports up to 300 eggs/nest.

**[[Etheostoma zonale]] (Banded Darter)**
- Comprised 0–16% of any sample; preferred mid-third of riffle, avoided vegetation.
- Females outnumbered males 1.7:1 in 21 of 33 occupied collections.
- Spawns latest of the darters studied — gravid females found as late as June 24, 1952 and June 27, 1953; some ripe as early as June 4–5.
- Notably absent from the Tionesta Creek drainage (5 collections, zero specimens); low abundance in Conewango Creek drainage; best coloration in French Creek and Brokenstraw Creek.

**[[Etheostoma blennioides]] (Greenside Darter)**
- Females slightly outnumbered males (~1.1:1) consistently across sub-regions.
- Ranges far upstream but replaced by *E. caeruleum* and *E. zonale* in smaller headwater streams.
- Preferred lower two-thirds of riffle; generally absent from vegetated riffles (contrasts with Fahy 1954 and Winn 1958a, who found spawning in vegetated/atypical slow riffles).

**[[Etheostoma variatum]] (Variegate Darter)**
- Fifth most abundant species (~13% of collections); males exceeded females 1.5:1 (dominant in 27/35 occupied stations).
- Prefers lower, larger stream reaches, diminishing upstream in favor of *E. caeruleum*; frequently associated with *E. zonale*.

**[[Etheostoma maculatum]] (Spotted Darter)**
- More abundant in French Creek than Allegheny River proper.
- Females exceeded males 1.2:1 across 8 collections (consistent with Raney & Lachner 1939).
- Headwater, swift-water specialist, commonly associated with *E. camurum*.
- Occupies upper third of riffles in spring, shifts to deepest/swiftest zones through summer, moves to lower/shallower thirds by mid-November; remains on riffle year-round (unlike *E. camurum*, absent from riffles by November).
