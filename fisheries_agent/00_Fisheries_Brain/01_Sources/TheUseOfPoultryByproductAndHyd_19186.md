# The Use of Poultry By-Product and Hydrolysed Feather Meal as a Feed for Rainbow Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This feeding trial, published by the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências, Universidade do Porto, 1992), tests whether a poultry by-product and hydrolysed feather meal (PBHFm) can replace [[Fish Meal]] in diets for [[Rainbow Trout]]. Seven diets replaced 0% to 100% of the dietary fish-meal protein with PBHFm over an 18-week trial. The study concludes that PBHFm can replace fish meal at up to 80% of dietary protein without adverse effects on growth or feed utilization, although carcass composition changed significantly, mainly through increased body lipid.

## Background & Description

- **Study organism:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), a salmonid. Initial mean weight was about 35 g.
- **Test ingredient:** PBHFm, made by grinding, cooking and drying feathers, offal, blood and necks in their natural proportions. It was sourced in Portugal.
- **Rationale:** Growing demand for animal protein and shortages of trash fish make good-quality [[Fish Meal]] increasingly hard to obtain. Animal by-products are cheaper sources of protein and lipid, and they represent a large share of salmonid feed cost.
- **Prior work:** Few studies had tested [[Poultry By-Product Meal]] as a fish-meal substitute. Earlier trials in [[Coho Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*) reported that 50% to 75% of fish-meal protein could be replaced without loss of growth. PBHFm had not previously been tested in fish feeds.
- **Ingredient quality:**
  - PBHFm crude protein was 60.16%, within the reported range of 56% to 76%.
  - [[Brown Fish Meal]] crude protein was 53.96%, below the 60% often cited as the minimum for good-quality fish meal. It was the best fish meal available in the Portuguese market at the time.
  - PBHFm lipid was high at 23.80%, which forced diet lipid to be set at 17%.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** Fish were held in 15 net cages of 0.30 m³ (1.20 m × 0.5 m × 0.5 m) at the [[Experimental Aquaculture Station of Rio Caldo]], northern Portugal.
- **Water temperature:** The mean reservoir temperature was 20.2°C (range 11°C to 23°C), with daily variation not exceeding 2°C. The author notes that this is close to the upper limit for rainbow trout culture, which makes comparison with trials run at lower temperatures difficult. Higher temperatures also raise energy requirements.
- **Feed management:** Fish were hand-fed 2% of body weight per day, split into two portions at dawn and late afternoon, in line with the natural photoperiod.
- **Essential fatty acids:** PBHFm is reported to be deficient in ω3 essential fatty acids, so the diets were supplemented with cod liver oil. No signs of essential fatty acid deficiency were seen.

## Key Findings

**Experimental design**
- 7 isonitrogenous, isocaloric diets at 40% crude protein and 17% lipid. PBHFm replaced fish-meal protein at 0%, 20%, 40%, 60%, 80%, 90% and 100%.
- 300 fish were stocked into 15 cages, with two replicate cages per diet. One cage of 20 unfed fish served as a control for natural production.
- Trial duration was 18 weeks. Fish were weighed fortnightly. Faecal collection for digestibility took place during the last month, using [[Chromic Oxide]] as the marker.

**Growth and feed efficiency**
- Every PBHFm-based diet outperformed the fish-meal control diet.
- Mean final weight rose from 62.39 g on the control diet to a maximum of 136.60 g at 90% replacement (Diet 6). Diet 7 (100% replacement) reached 127.35 g.
- [[Feed Conversion Ratio]] improved from 4.64 on the control to 1.74 on Diet 6.
- [[Protein Efficiency Ratio]] rose from 0.53 (control) to 1.40 (Diet 6).
- Specific growth rate increased from 0.47 %/day (control) to 1.09 %/day (Diet 6).

**Amino acid profile**
- Both fish meal and PBHFm were poor in the essential amino acids [[Leucine]] and [[Phenylalanine]]. Leucine was the first limiting amino acid in all seven diets, at about 48% of the requirement.
- [[Lysine]] was 1.68% in fish meal versus 2.98% in PBHFm. The control diet held about 60% of the lysine requirement, and Diet 7 about 95%.
- The author argues that the severe leucine deficiency was the main constraint, so differences in lysine and arginine probably had little effect on growth.
- The poor control performance is attributed to low-quality fish meal, which had particularly low levels of arginine, leucine, lysine and phenylalanine.

**Carcass composition**
- Carcass lipid rose from 6.18% at the start of the trial to 9.19% on the control diet and 16.75% on Diet 7.
- Moisture fell from 69.53% (control) to 63.25% (Diet 7), and protein fell from 18.41% to 17.35%. Both declines were significant.
- The author attributes the lipid gain to the high dietary lipid (17%) and notes that almost 40% of the extra weight in Diet 7 was fat.

**Digestibility**
- Protein digestibility was 83.67% on the control diet, rising to 85.83% at 80% replacement (Diet 5). Only Diets 5 and 7 differed significantly from the control.
- [[Lipid Digestibility]] was 74.73% on the control and 79.96% on Diet 7. The author notes these values were lower than the 89% to 97% typical of good fish meal.
- Apparent dry matter digestibility ranged from 57.76% (control) to 67.62% (Diet 4).

**Physiology and health**
- Haematocrit was 27.50% on the control diet, compared with 34.82% to 36.07% on PBHFm diets. The author suggests control fish were in poorer condition, possibly mildly anaemic. All treatments still fell within published normal ranges (24% to 43%).
- Plasma glucose did not differ significantly among PBHFm diets. The control value of 15.33 mg/100 cm³ in the text is inconsistent with Table 4 (75.33 mg/100 cm³), so the figure should be treated with caution.
- [[Liver Somatic Index]] was 1.01 on the control and 1.23 on Diet 7, the only significant difference from control. Histopathology showed no disorders.

**Conclusions**
- PBHFm can replace fish meal at up to 80% of dietary protein with no adverse effect on growth or feed utilization, and the author identifies 80% to 90% as the optimal range. This corresponds to about 34% to 36% of the whole diet.
- Carcass lipid changes are significant and linked to high dietary lipid.
- Growth could be improved further by correcting the [[Essential Amino Acid]] profile, for example by adding other protein sources or purified amino acids.
