# The Status of Pumpkinseed, Lepomis gibbosus (Linné), in California

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae #introduced-species #distribution-records

## Executive Summary

This 1955 report documents the distribution and status of the introduced [[Pumpkinseed]] (*Lepomis gibbosus*) in [[California]]. The species was considered rare in the state, with scattered populations in widely separated locations. The authors compile historical records from 1939-1954, correcting and expanding upon the only previous published account. Collections were made from five California counties: [[Riverside County]], [[Lassen County]], [[Modoc County]], [[Orange County]], and [[Siskiyou County]]. The fish persisted in various habitat types including artesian-fed reservoirs, mining dredge ponds, and river systems, though it occupied only a minor place in California's fishery.

## Background & Description

The [[Pumpkinseed]] is a member of the family [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes), well known as a pan fish in eastern North America. The species can be distinguished from other sunfishes in California by its definite scarlet spot on the gill cover and prominent blue and orange cheek stripes. Curtis (1949) provided the only previous published record of the species in California, stating it was "so rare [in California] that it can for all practical purposes be disregarded."

The first known California record dates to May 25, 1939, when Dr. R. R. Miller and R. G. Miller collected three half-grown to adult specimens from an artesian-fed reservoir on the Wise Ranch at the northeast corner of the [[Salton Sea]], south of Mecca, [[Riverside County]] (University of Michigan Museum of Zoology No. 133173). Mr. J. F. Wise believed these fish were planted approximately twenty-five years prior (circa 1914) by a Mr. Sheets and came "via train from Washington, D.C.," likely from a U.S. Bureau of Fisheries shipment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Pumpkinseed]] demonstrated persistence in varying water types across California, from desert artesian reservoirs to northern river systems. In the [[Susan River]], [[Lassen County]], the species maintained a population for at least 12 years between initial collection (July 4, 1942) and survey (August 16, 1954). The [[Klamath River]] system in [[Siskiyou County]] supported populations in mining dredge ponds and quieter river stretches, though the river primarily functioned as a trout and salmon stream with little angling pressure on sunfishes.

The species co-occurred with [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) in the upper [[Klamath River]] system. In the original Wise Ranch reservoir, [[Pumpkinseed]] populations apparently disappeared following introduction of [[Bullheads]] (*Ameiurus*) in 1940. Several early distribution attempts from the Wise Ranch stock failed to establish permanent populations due to various factors including cloudbursts, pond drainage, and mortality without reproduction.

## Key Findings

**Distribution Records by County:**

- **[[Riverside County]]**: May 25, 1939 - three specimens from Wise Ranch reservoir near [[Mecca]]. Multiple secondary plantings from this source to nearby locations failed to establish. Population believed extirpated by 1948.

- **[[Lassen County]]**: July 4, 1942 - seven half-grown to mature (dwarfed) adults from [[Susan River]] below U.S. Highway 395 (UMMZ 141556). July 4, 1953 - one male 5.8 inches fork length collected near same locality. August 16, 1954 - several hundred individuals ranging 0.9 to 6.1 inches fork length collected from lower Susan River near [[Honey Lake]], indicating a good population.

- **[[Modoc County]]**: September 7, 1942 - three small specimens (largest 42 mm standard length) from [[Antelope Creek]], tributary of [[Lost River]] (Stanford University Natural History Museum Cat. No. 40903). Curtis's published date of 1946 was a typographical error.

- **[[Orange County]]**: November 15, 1948 - mixed warmwater fish shipment from U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service at Dexter, New Mexico, planted in [[Irvine Lake]] (Santiago Reservoir) under permit to Stevenson Lake Corporation. Some fish identified as [[Pumpkinseed]] by field identification only; no specimens retained. No subsequent records.

- **[[Siskiyou County]]**: May 24, 1951 - 122 specimens (2.6 to 5.2 inches fork length) recovered from rotenone-treated mining dredge pond along [[Klamath River]] above [[Horse Creek]] mouth. May 29, 1951 - one specimen from fyke net in side channel opposite [[Humbug Creek]]. June 20, 1951 - 201 specimens from another treated dredge pond. Subsequent collections from Klamath River and [[Copeo Lake]]; specimens up to approximately 7 inches observed in 1953.

**Population Status**: Both [[Pumpkinseed]] and [[Green Sunfish]] appeared quite common in mining dredge holes and quieter stretches of the upper [[Klamath River]] by the early 1950s. The species occupied only a minor place in California's fishery, but its persistence in varying water types suggested potential for stronger foothold with further distribution. Origins of populations in Susan River, Lost River drainage, and Klamath systems remained unknown, though [[Lost River]] connected to Klamath via canal system.
