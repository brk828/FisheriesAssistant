# 12-Month Finding on Petition to List Roundtail Chub (Lower Colorado River DPS) and Headwater Chub

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a correspondence and briefing record from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 2 and Arizona Ecological Services Field Office), covering roughly January to May 2006. It traces the preparation, internal review, and publication of a 12-month finding on a 2003 petition from the [[Center for Biological Diversity]] to list a Distinct Population Segment (DPS) of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) in the Lower Colorado River Basin and the [[Headwater Chub]] (*Gila nigra*) throughout its range under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

The final recommendation was a **warranted but precluded** 12-month finding for the Headwater Chub and at least one DPS of the Roundtail Chub, with a **not warranted** determination for a range-wide Roundtail Chub listing. The finding was signed and sent to the Federal Register in late April 2006 for publication on May 3, 2006. Much of the correspondence concerns internal disputes over DPS boundaries, the genetic evidence, document versioning, a court-ordered deadline of April 6, 2006, and coordination with state conservation agreements.

## Background & Description

- **Roundtail Chub (*Gila robusta*):** Historically common in deep pools and eddies of large streams across the upper and lower Colorado River basins (Wyoming, Utah, Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona). Described in the press release as 9 to 14 inches, olive-gray to silver with a lighter belly.
- **Headwater Chub (*Gila nigra*):** Grows to about 8 inches, dark gray to brown with silvery sides; lives in upper and middle reaches of moderately sized streams.
- **Petition history:** The [[Center for Biological Diversity]] petitioned on April 2, 2003. A settlement agreement signed March 23, 2005 set a 90-day finding by June 30, 2005 and, if positive, a 12-month finding by April 6, 2006. A positive 90-day finding was published July 12, 2005.
- **Regulatory framework:** The review relied on the Service's DPS policy and the five-factor threats analysis under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution (lower basin):**
- Roundtail Chub in Arizona occur in two Little Colorado River tributaries, several Bill Williams River Basin tributaries, the Salt River and two tributaries, the Verde River and four tributaries, and Aravaipa Creek. In New Mexico they occur in the upper Gila River.
- Headwater Chub occur in four Verde River tributaries, Tonto Creek and eight tributaries, and in New Mexico in the upper East Fork, lower Middle Fork, and lower West Forks of the Gila River. They were recently documented in the San Carlos River drainage, status unknown.

**Range status (as stated in the finding):**
- Lower basin Roundtail Chub occupy approximately 18 to 32 percent of their former range, about 497 of 2,796 formerly occupied miles, with 14 percent of range on the San Carlos Apache Nation of unknown status. All known lower-basin populations are considered threatened by nonnative species and habitat alteration.
- Upper basin Roundtail Chub, which were not petitioned, encompass about two-thirds of the range and occupy a higher proportion of historical range, in approximately 70 streams.
- Headwater Chub occupy approximately 40 to 50 percent of former range, about 125 of 312 miles, with 10 percent of range on the San Carlos Apache Nation of unknown status. No population is considered secure.

**Threats identified (five-factor analysis, both species):** nonnative species (primary threat), livestock grazing, water withdrawal, dams and dam operation, roads and logging, recreation, mining, urban development, habitat fragmentation, and channelization. Logging is a land use in the watersheds of 13 of 16 remaining streams with known chub populations. Nonnative crayfish (*Procambarus clarkii*, *Orconectes virilis*) are thought to have contributed to the extirpation of two Headwater Chub populations.

**Conservation measures:** The Colorado River Basin states signed a conservation agreement for three fish species in 2004 covering Roundtail Chub. The Arizona Game and Fish Department developed a draft agreement and strategy for several native fishes, broadly supported by state and federal cooperators. New Mexico lists Roundtail Chub as endangered under its Wildlife Conservation Act.

## Key Findings

- **Recommendation:** A warranted but precluded 12-month finding for Headwater Chub and at least one DPS of Roundtail Chub; a not warranted determination for range-wide Roundtail Chub because upper basin status appears stable.
- **DPS dispute:** The regional draft identified a third DPS for the Little Colorado River populations, isolated for over 250,000 years by Grand Falls (about 160 ft). The Director's office (Bryan Arroyo) objected that creating DPSs beyond the petition risks expanding the scope of petition review. Later language was revised to use "discernible" rather than "discrete," and the final press release states the lower basin population "is not discernable from the upper basin population" and does not qualify as a DPS.
- **Genetics:** Roundtail Chub from Chevelon Creek share a mitochondrial DNA haplotype with upper basin fish. The haplotype was absent from limited Gila River samples. Regional staff concluded limited lower-basin sampling does not permit definitive conclusions on discreteness or significance.
- **Data gaps flagged by reviewers:** Chris Nolin (Endangered Species, Washington) asked for evidence of ongoing population declines and a threat-by-species assessment. Glen Knowles reported 18 populations with documented declines in the revision. Andrea Cherepy flagged a numerical inconsistency (18 to 32 percent occupied versus 82 percent extirpated).
- **Timeline:** Briefing paper sent to Director Benjamin Tuggle on February 27, 2006; revised March 2 to 6; briefing and approval March 16; a 3-week extension request was considered but the court deadline of April 6 was retained; finding submitted to the Federal Register April 27–28, published May 3, 2006.
- **Internal coordination concerns:** Steve Spangle and field staff objected to short-notice demands for 100 percent staff dedication on multiple packages (jaguar prudency finding due May 15, 2006; spikedace and loach minnow critical habitat due July 15, 2006; Mexican garter snake 12-month finding due August 1, 2006).
- **Supporting data cited:** AGFD 2005 survey reports for Little Colorado spinedace, Roundtail Chub (Chevelon Creek, Black River), and Gila Chub (Dix and Harden Cienega creeks). A Glen Canyon Dam Adaptive Management Work Group meeting (March 7–8, 2006) also discussed a possible lower basin big river fish recovery effort (RIP) and a Genetics Management Plan.
- **Outreach:** A news release, Q&A, and outreach plan were prepared. The release states the Headwater Chub will be added to the candidate species list and monitored annually.
