# Length-Weight Relationships of Alabama Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #actinopterygii

## Executive Summary

This document is a reference compendium of length-weight data for 95 fish species from Alabama, compiled by [[Wayne E. Swingle]] from river, river impoundment, and pond surveys conducted between 1949 and 1964. The data were gathered by the fisheries staffs of [[Auburn University Agricultural Experiment Station]] and the [[Alabama Department of Conservation]], and by surveys made in cooperation with the [[Tennessee Valley Authority]], the Georgia Game and Fish Commission, and the Florida Game and Freshwater Fish Commission. The tables (pages 1–80) give total length and weight values for each species, arranged alphabetically by common name. The closing pages (from page 81) give the parameters for the standard and polynomial [[Length-Weight Relationship]] equations, along with the [[Condition Index]] values.

## Background & Description

**Scope and sources**
- Data were collected over the period 1949 to 1964.
- Surveys were conducted by the Auburn University Agricultural Experiment Station and the Alabama Department of Conservation, and in cooperation with the Tennessee Valley Authority, the Georgia Game and Fish Commission, and the Florida Game and Freshwater Fish Commission.
- Pages 1–80 contain total length-weight values for 95 species, arranged alphabetically by common name.
- Common names follow the American Fisheries Society Special Publication No. 2 (1960), with these exceptions:
  - The carp is called the common carp, following FAO usage to separate it from Chinese and Indian carps.
  - The deep-bodied Israeli strain of mirror carp is recorded separately from the scaled common carp, because its length-weight relationships differ.
  - Four exotic tilapias are listed as the Java, Nile, Congo, and Tampa tilapias.
  - The southern variety of brown bullhead is listed as the speckled (brown) bullhead.
  - Rock bass are listed as northern and southern rock bass.
- Pond data supplement river data for some species, to extend the length range. Some tables present pond data only, for species absent or rarely collected from river waters. Pond-derived tables are flagged with an asterisk in the text.

**Field and measurement procedure**
- Fish were measured individually for total length.
- Most fish were placed in inch-groups and weighed in aggregate. The author notes that this "undoubtedly reduced the range between maximum and minimum weights for each inch-group."
- Values were calculated by a computer program described in Swingle (1964), Auburn University Agr. Exp. Sta., Zool.-Ent. Dept. Series, Fisheries No. 1, 19 pp.

**Table columns**
Each species table reports: Length (inch-group), Number of Fish, Range in Weight (minimum and maximum), Average Empirical Weight, Calculated Weights (Standard and Polynomial), and the Condition Index.

**Equations and indices**
- Condition index: C = W × 10^5 / L^3, where W is the average empirical weight in pounds and L is the inch integer for an inch-group. (The exponent appears as "10°" in the OCR scan and is read here as 10^5.)
- Standard equation: W = aL^b, or log(W) = log(a) + b·log(L). Parameters a, b, log(a), the correlation coefficient r, and the standard error of the estimate are given on the page 81 parameter table.
- Polynomial equation: W = b0 + b1·L + b2·L^2 + b3·L^3. Parameters b0–b3 are given in the same table, along with the length interval, the number of observations (n), and the number of fish included.
- Where a single standard equation could not describe the whole data range, two or more equations were fitted per species. Breakpoints were chosen where the increment of change in the condition index shifted in magnitude.
- Relative condition index (LeCren, 1951, Jour. Animal Ecol., 20(2):201–209) is used to compare individual fish and populations. It expresses condition as a multiple of the "state average" weight for fish of the same length across all populations in the state.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types represented:** river and river impoundment waters, with pond data used for some species.
- **Geographic scope:** Alabama waters, with cooperating surveys in adjoining states. Figure 1 compares populations from the Pickwick, Guntersville, Wheeler, Wilson, Warrior, Tombigbee, and Coosa systems.
- **Taxa covered:** the species list includes freshwater cyprinids, catostomids, ictalurids, centrarchids, percids, and esocids, along with exotic tilapias and some species that are primarily estuarine or marine (for example, Atlantic Needlefish, Bay Anchovy, Black Drum, Southern Flounder, Striped Mullet, Spot, and Pinfish). The document does not describe their ecological roles beyond their inclusion in the tables.
- **Exotic species:** the Java, Nile, Congo, and Tampa tilapias are included.
- **Hatchery and pond management:** the Chain Pickerel table notes data from "1 fertilized and 1 unfertilized pond." Pond data is also flagged for Common Carp (Israeli Mirror), Creek Chub, Goldfish, Golden Shiner, Java Tilapia, Nile Tilapia, Tampa Tilapia, and Blue Sucker (inch-groups 9–14).

## Key Findings

1. **Dataset scope:** length-weight data for 95 Alabama fish species, collected 1949–1964, from rivers, river impoundments, and ponds.
2. **Multi-agency collaboration:** the data come from Auburn University, the Alabama Department of Conservation, and cooperating agencies in Tennessee, Georgia, and Florida.
3. **Methodological limitation:** most fish were weighed in aggregate by inch-group. The author states this reduces the weight range within each group.
4. **Dual equation approach:** standard power equations (W = aL^b) and cubic polynomial equations (W = b0 + b1L + b2L^2 + b3L^3) are both reported. The polynomial equations describe some species over longer length intervals than the standard equations.
5. **Condition assessment:** the LeCren relative condition index (Kn) is the author's preferred tool for comparing individuals and populations. Kn is defined as the observed weight divided by the calculated weight from the standard equation, with the state-average standard weight used for population comparisons.
6. **Example of population comparison (Figure 1):** Freshwater Drum of 5, 10, and 15 inches were compared across seven populations: Pickwick, Guntersville, Wheeler, Wilson, Warrior, Tombigbee, and Coosa. The figure's y-axis spans 0.8 to 1.2 relative condition. The document does not state the individual values.
7. **Sample size example:** Freshwater Drum inch-group 6 had n = 5,812 fish, with a weight range of 0.042–0.20 lb and an average empirical weight of 0.086 lb (values as read from the scan).
8. **Naming changes:** the carp is separated into Common Carp (scaled) and Common Carp, Israeli Mirror, because the two strains have different length-weight relationships. Rock bass are split into northern and southern forms.
9. **Parameter reference:** equation parameters, r, standard errors, length intervals, and n values are compiled on the page 81 table, which the document locates after the species tables.
