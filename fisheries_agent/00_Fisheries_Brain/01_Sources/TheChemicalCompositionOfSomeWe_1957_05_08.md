# The Chemical Composition of Some Western Irish Fresh Waters

#limnology #hydrochemistry #freshwater-ecology #biogeochemistry

## Executive Summary
This 1957 paper by Eville Gorham presents one of the first complete major-ion analyses of freshwater bodies in western Ireland, addressing a gap in Irish limnological literature (previous work by [[Webb 1947]] and [[Macan And Lund 1954]] covered only partial parameters). Seven water bodies were sampled: acidic blanket bog pools at Gowlan East, four Connemara loughs draining metamorphic schist/gneiss, and two Kerry loughs draining Old Red Sandstone. The study quantifies pH, total cations/anions, and concentrations of Na, K, Ca, Mg, HCO₃, Cl, and SO₄, then partitions each ion's origin between marine aerosol deposition ("sea spray") and terrestrial weathering/organic sources. The work is foundational for understanding the **base status and buffering capacity** of oligotrophic waters in blanket-bog-dominated landscapes — directly relevant to [[Freshwater Habitat Chemistry]] and downstream aquatic biota tolerance ranges (e.g., pH-sensitive invertebrates and fish in acid-sensitive systems).

## Background & Description
*(No fish or biological taxa were sampled; this section is reframed to describe the physicochemical "morphology" of the water bodies themselves.)*

- **Blanket Bog Pools (Gowlan East):** Distinctly acidic (pH 4.4), brown-colored due to dissolved organic matter (humic substances), with **no detectable bicarbonate**, very low calcium (<1 ppm), and the highest sulphate (7.7 ppm) and magnesium (1.8 ppm) among all sites. Total ionic strength ~0.78 m.equiv/L. These pools are hydrologically isolated from mineral soil, deriving mineral input almost entirely from atmospheric precipitation.
- **Kerry Loughs (Muckross, Upper Lake):** Clear waters, weakly acidic (pH 6.4–6.6), lowest overall salt concentration (<0.5 m.equiv/L), low Na, Ca, and HCO₃ — reflecting inland position and resistant Old Red Sandstone substrate.
- **Connemara Loughs (Shindilla, Agraffard, Craiggamore, Garraunbaun):** Clear waters, circumneutral to slightly alkaline (pH 6.5–7.2), higher total ionic concentration (0.5–1.2 m.equiv/L). **Garraunbaun Lough** stands out with the highest Ca (8.7 ppm) and HCO₃ (22.0 ppm), more than double the next-highest site, attributed to adjacent limestone-banded schist.
- **General ionic hierarchy:** Na⁺ dominates cations (4.7–13.7 ppm); Cl⁻ dominates anions (7.3–23.6 ppm), consistent with maritime sea-spray influence. K⁺ is the least abundant cation (0.2–0.8 ppm).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat gradient:** The study captures a chemical gradient from ombrotrophic (rain-fed, nutrient-poor, acidic) [[Blanket Bog Pools]] to minerotrophic (groundwater/weathering-influenced) [[Oligotrophic Loughs]], illustrating classic bog-to-lake hydrochemical succession in maritime temperate climates.
- **Marine aerosol influence:** Coastal exposure to Atlantic winds means sea-spray-derived NaCl dominates dissolved salt loads — up to **69% of total ions in loughs** and **85% in bog pools** are marine-derived, per Table 2 calculations using Cl⁻ as a conservative tracer.
- **Buffering and acid neutralization:** Calcium bicarbonate derived from rock/soil weathering (not the sea) is identified as the key buffering agent that neutralizes acidic bog drainage, explaining why Connemara loughs remain circumneutral despite being surrounded by extensive acid peat — a critical ecological control for any pH-sensitive aquatic fauna in these systems, relevant to [[Acid-Sensitive Aquatic Fauna]].
- **Substrate control on productivity:** Old Red Sandstone (Kerry) yields lower ionic (especially Ca, K) concentrations than the more mineral-rich Connemara schist/gneiss (locally banded with limestone), suggesting substrate geology is a first-order determinant of water fertility and, by extension, potential biological productivity — a concept parallel to substrate-driven productivity gradients discussed in [[Substrate Geology And Water Fertility]].
- **Non-marine sulphate source:** Excess sulphate (~2/5 of total not explained by sea spray) is hypothesized to derive from anaerobic decomposition of organic matter in waterlogged peat, generating H₂S subsequently oxidized to sulphuric acid — linking peatland biogeochemistry to lake water chemistry, relevant to [[Peatland Biogeochemistry]].

## Key Findings
- **Acidity range:** pH 4.4 (bog pools) to 7.2 (Garraunbaun Lough); lough acidity attributed mainly to dissolved CO₂.
- **Total ion concentration:** Lowest in Kerry (<0.5 m.equiv/L), intermediate in bog pools (~0.78 m.equiv/L), highest in Connemara loughs (up to 1.18–1.20 m.equiv/L).
- **Marine contribution (% of total ions):** ~85% in bog pools vs. ~69% in loughs; Na, Cl, Mg, and K are overwhelmingly marine-derived (60–100%), while **Ca and HCO₃ are predominantly terrestrial** (only ~8–16% marine in loughs).
- **Calcium–bicarbonate coupling:** Strong positive relationship between pH and HCO₃⁻ concentration (pH 6.4 at 4.4 ppm HCO₃ → pH 7.2 at 22 ppm HCO₃), underscoring bicarbonate's buffering role.
- **Comparative context:** Irish lough waters are richer than the world average freshwater only in chloride (proportionally and absolutely); they are notably poor in K, Ca, Mg, and HCO₃ due to resistant underlying bedrock, contrasting sharply with ion-rich Triassic sandstone-fed waters (e.g., Shropshire meres).
- **Bog vs. stream drainage distinction:** Static bog pool water is far more acidic and colored than actual bog-derived stream runoff feeding the loughs, because rapid surface runoff limits time for organic acid diffusion and concentration — an important methodological caveat for extrapolating bog pool chemistry to catchment drainage.
- **Implication for aquatic biota:** The buffering capacity conferred by calcium bicarbonate from rock weathering, rather than marine input, is the decisive factor allowing circumneutral conditions suitable for base-sensitive aquatic organisms in an otherwise acid-peat-dominated landscape — a foundational insight for later studies of [[Fish Distribution In Acid-Sensitive Waters]] and broader [[Freshwater Water Chemistry And Biota]] relationships.
