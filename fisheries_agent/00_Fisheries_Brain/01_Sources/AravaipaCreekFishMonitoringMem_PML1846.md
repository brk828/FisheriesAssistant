# Aravaipa Creek Fish Monitoring Memoranda, April–December 1991

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a series of nine monthly monitoring memoranda (April through December 1991) issued by [[Arizona State University]] researchers (W. L. Minckley, Tony Velasco, Chris Reinus) to a group of interested agencies and organizations. They report fish sampling along [[Aravaipa Creek]] in Graham and Pinal counties, Arizona, covering three sites upstream of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]], four sites inside the canyon (in the May memo), and three sites downstream. The memoranda document standardized seine and electrofishing collections, habitat descriptions, species composition by percentage, and field observations on stream conditions, a December flood, invasive species, and wildlife. Scanned tables are partly degraded, so this note summarizes qualitative patterns and clearly legible values rather than transcribing every table cell.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** Sampling was split into three zones: an east (upstream) end with sites near the gauge station, Chimney Rock and the TNC guest house; the canyon sites (Hell Hole, Booger, Horse Camp, Painted Cave in May; Hell Hole, Horse Camp, Painted Cave in August and later); and a west (downstream) end with sites at the gauge station, White's Ranch and the sycamore tree. Each site comprised roughly 200 m of mainstream channel.

**Methods.**
- Mainstream channels were seined with a 1/8-inch mesh, 6-ft seine and/or a 1/4-inch mesh, 15-ft seine.
- Side channels and margins were sampled with a 1/16-inch mesh dipnet.
- Backpack [[Electrofishing]] (roughly 150–300 volts, 50–125 pulses per second) supplemented seining; shocker data represented the lower 100 m of each reach.
- Habitat was recorded as percent riffle, run and pool, with substrate composition (boulder to silt), mean width and mean depth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat trends.** Substrate descriptions shifted over the year. The May memo noted that the stream substrate appeared to have become coarser since April. Mean widths ranged from about 2 m (pools) to 16 m (runs and riffles), and mean depths from about 0.1 m to 0.5 m. Riffles dominated many reaches, while pools were rare and small. Side streams ("streamlets") were common at some east-end sites and persisted through much of the reach in spring.

**Flood event (December 1991).** A heavy rain event on 19–22 December produced the most significant disturbance of the period. Recorded USGS discharge was 353 cfs (19 Dec, 1730 h), 752 cfs (20 Dec, 2245 h) and 656 cfs (21 Dec, 0015 h). The creek rose rapidly, moved large rocks and boulders, and uprooted trees. At White's Ranch the water width was about 20 m, and at the sycamore tree about 45 m, with the stream reportedly rising to nearly 2 m above normal height. The west-end habitat survey could not be completed because of the flood. Water temperature was about 15 °C at the east end and about 9 °C at the west end, and pH was 8.2 at the east-end guest house.

**Other environmental observations.**
- **Turbidity:** Creek turbidity was high after storm runoff in August (August 8–9 flood from local rain) and in parts of the June sampling area, where diversions had reduced flow.
- **Diversions:** Diversion dams and ponds were reinstalled near the guest house in May and created small pools; June reports noted low water at Chimney Rock due to diversions.
- **Vegetation and fauna:** Tent caterpillars were abundant in October and defoliation was obvious along the stream. The sycamore tree near the west end fell by June, and a new reference point was needed.
- **Human activity:** Road work into the east-end wilderness parking lot in June involved tractors working in the stream; no obvious effects on fish were seen.
- **Wildlife:** Two pairs of black hawks and a zone-tailed hawk nest were recorded (May). Leopard frogs were fairly abundant between Painted Cave and Wagner Ranch (August) and were photographed and released at two locations.

## Key Findings

**Monitoring logistics.** Sampling dates were: April 14–17, May 10–12, June 7–9, July 12–14, August 9–11, September 13–15, October 12–13, November 8–10, and December 19–21 (rescheduled from December 14–15 to allow for final exams and holiday shopping). Collectors came from ASU, TNC, AGFD, USBLM and USFWS, with assistance from several graduate students.

**Species composition.** Percentage tables for each reach are in the scans. Across the period:
- Spikedace (HEFU) and longfin dace (AGCH) were numerous across most upstream and canyon reaches, and spikedace and roundtail chub showed breeding coloration in May.
- Loach minnow (TICO) and speckled dace (RHOS) appeared regularly, including breeding-colored individuals in October.
- Nonnative species included green sunfish (LECY), western mosquitofish (GAAF), red shiner (CYLU) and common carp (CYCA). Yellow bullhead, fathead minnow and green sunfish were collected and preserved in May; these three exotic species were taken to ASU.

**Largemouth bass.** Largemouth bass appear to have invaded the stream in late May or early June. Records indicated a peak in August with a significant decline in September. Stomach contents from preserved specimens were being analyzed, with results promised in later memoranda. In December, largemouth bass were more abundant than in previous months in slower stream segments.

**Tagged fish.** Tagged fish were recorded in several months, mainly from the canyon and downstream reaches. Tagged *Catostomus insignis* (sonora sucker) were returned to the creek after tag numbers were recorded and reported to ASU. Some tags were in poor condition or the fish had tag damage.

**Health and reproduction.** Some adults of several native species showed skin lesions, fungus and yellow grub infestations (April). Breeding tubercles and coloration were seen in roundtail chub, spikedace and loach minnow through the year, with the strongest breeding signs in late fall. Age determination of juveniles was difficult because many fish fell at the border between juvenile and adult size.

**Larval fish.** Larval fish were collected at all sample sites and were "abundant along all margins of the system" in June. Larval samples were sorted and identified as they came in; the exact taxon names referred to in the April summary are garbled in the scan.

**Management actions and requests.**
- Interested parties were asked to respond with requests for personnel and equipment (especially backpack electroshockers) for the August walkthrough.
- Sample reaches outside the wilderness were pre-marked and flagged; sites inside the canyon were to be marked inconspicuously.
- The guest house reach was moved upstream in anticipation of a barrier installation.

**Next steps.** The planned walkthroughs were 8–10 November 1991 and 7–9 February 1992. The December memo gives an updated date for the next walkthrough.
