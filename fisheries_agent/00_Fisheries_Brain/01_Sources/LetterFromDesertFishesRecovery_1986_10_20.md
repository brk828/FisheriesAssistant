## Executive Summary

This document is a formal letter from the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT)**, convened at Arizona State University, to Michael Spear, Regional Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Dated 20 October 1986, the letter conveys three time-sensitive management requests arising from a DFRT meeting held 2–3 October 1986. The requests concern: (1) the federal listing status of two imperiled cyprinid species, (2) the continuation of a reintroduction/monitoring program for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]], and (3) the initiation of non-native fish removal from stock tanks threatening native fish populations in the [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed. While not a primary data-generating research paper, the letter is a key primary source documenting active conservation management, interagency coordination, and species-status concerns for imperiled Southwestern desert fishes during the mid-1980s.

## Background & Description

No morphological data are presented in this correspondence. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*): Endemic cyprinid restricted to the Little Colorado River drainage in Arizona; subject of a pending federal listing package at the time of writing.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): A small benthic cyprinid native to Gila River basin streams, including [[Aravaipa Creek]]; identified as threatened by non-native fish introductions in stock tanks within its watershed.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): Large-bodied catostomid native to the Colorado River Basin; subject of an active reintroduction program coordinated between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] under a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) originally set to conclude in 1990.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*): Native cyprinid co-occurring with loach minnow in [[Aravaipa Creek]]; identified as a target species for conservation benefit from proposed non-native fish removal efforts.
- **Habitat context**: The [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed is highlighted as critical native fish habitat requiring protection from non-native species introduced via stock tanks, implicating multi-stakeholder land and water management (Bureau of Land Management Safford District, Arizona Game and Fish Department, and the Whittell Memorial Trust/Defenders of Wildlife).

## Key Findings

- The DFRT requested status updates on the **Endangered Species Act listing packages** for *Lepidomeda vittata* (Little Colorado River spinedace) and *Tiaroga cobitis* (loach minnow), citing concern over bureaucratic delay and a need for recovery team planning.
- The Team requested an **extension of the existing MOU** between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and the Arizona Game and Fish Department governing the [[Razorback Sucker]] reintroduction program, anticipating that failure to demonstrate recovery by the MOU's 1990 expiration could necessitate formal listing of the species.
- The DFRT emphasized that **long-term monitoring** is required to assess the success or failure of the razorback sucker reintroduction effort before any listing decision is made.
- The Team called for coordinated action among the **Bureau of Land Management (Safford District)**, **Arizona Game and Fish Department**, and the **Whittell Memorial Trust (Defenders of Wildlife)** to remove **non-native fishes from stock tanks** in the Aravaipa Creek watershed, a long-delayed but agreed-upon conservation measure.
- This removal effort was framed as directly benefiting two native species of concern: [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), both inhabitants of Aravaipa Creek.
- The letter illustrates the collaborative, multi-agency nature of desert fish conservation planning in Arizona during the 1980s and the proactive role of the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** in guiding regulatory and management timelines.
