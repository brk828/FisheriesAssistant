# Effects of Interim Flows from Glen Canyon Dam on Aquatic Resources (Trip 9, 1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This report details **Trip No. 9** (May 26 – June 7, 1994) of the Hualapai Aquatic Resources Study, examining fish and aquatic resource responses to interim flow regimes from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] along the Lower [[Colorado River]] between Diamond Creek (RM 226) and Pearce Ferry (RM 280) on [[Lake Mead]]. Sampling utilized trammel nets, boat and backpack electrofishing, minnow traps, hoop nets, and seines across the mainstem and key tributaries ([[Spencer Creek]], [[Surprise Creek]], [[Lost Creek]], [[Quartermaster Creek]]).

Overall fish catch was low relative to previous trips, attributed to unusually clear mainstem water (reducing electrofishing/net efficiency) and very low tributary discharge. A notable finding was a surge in [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] captures and observations, with [[Surprise Creek]] identified as a probable **nursery habitat** for juveniles. No endangered [[Humpback Chub]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] were captured or observed during this trip. Multiple-pass electrofishing in Spencer Creek continued to support the hypothesis that a high-gradient barrier segregates a healthier, non-native-free population of [[Speckled Dace]] from a stressed population coexisting with invasive [[Red Shiner]] and [[Fathead Minnow]] below the barrier.

## Background & Description

Data collected focused on individual fish condition, length, weight, and reproductive status rather than broad morphometric analysis:

- **Speckled Dace (above vs. below Spencer Creek barrier):** Dace captured upstream of the high-gradient barrier at Site 3A were consistently **more robust** (better body condition) than conspecifics at Sites 1 and 2, suggesting reduced competitive/predatory stress in the absence of non-native fish.
- **Flannelmouth Sucker (PIT-tagged individuals, n=13):** Total lengths ranged from 97 mm to 534 mm; weights ranged from 38 g to 1,439 g. A mix of juveniles (in Surprise Creek nursery habitat) and mature adults (mainstem/Spencer Creek) were documented.
  - Notable recapture: a female flannelmouth (PIT 7F7D17311C), 534 mm TL, 1,439 g, originally tagged at RM 156.93 in May 1993 by Arizona Game and Fish Department, recaptured at RM 245.3 — indicating a **~88-mile downstream movement** over approximately one year. This fish was expressing eggs at recapture, indicating reproductive readiness.
- **Striped Bass:** Reported to be in visibly better body condition during Trip 9 compared to the previous trip (Trip 8). Scale samples were collected to build an age-length relationship.
- **Common Carp:** Many individuals captured were actively expressing milt or eggs, indicating active spawning condition in the mainstem.
- **Channel Catfish (Quartermaster Creek):** All 11 individuals captured in hoop nets were juveniles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Mainstem Colorado River
- Sampled using trammel nets and boat electrofishing (220v DC).
- Extremely clear water (Secchi depths of 1.5–2.5 m, occasionally exceeding this during steady 8,000 cfs flows) likely reduced fish catchability by both nets and electrofishing gear.
- [[Striped Bass]] were routinely captured from RM 245.3 downstream, suggesting a longitudinal distribution shift potentially linked to [[Lake Mead]] proximity and water clarity/thermal conditions.
- Evidence of active carp spawning behavior was observed in the mainstem.

### Tributary Streams (Spencer, Surprise, Lost, Quartermaster Creeks)
- **[[Spencer Creek]]:** Site of ongoing multiple-pass population estimate studies (Sites 1, 2, 3A). Fish densities were the **lowest recorded since multiple-pass sampling began** (~1 year prior), potentially linked to low flow and abundant algal growth. A high-gradient section acts as an apparent **barrier to non-native fish** upstream of Site 3A, creating a refugium exclusively occupied by speckled dace for the second consecutive trip.
- **[[Surprise Creek]]:** Emerging as a critical **nursery habitat for juvenile [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** — five juveniles captured via minnow traps/hoop nets, with additional juveniles observed while snorkeling. Also harbors more fathead minnows relative to Spencer Creek.
- **[[Quartermaster Creek]]:** Hoop net catch limited to 11 juvenile channel catfish; minnow traps yielded no fish. A large crayfish was collected via electrofishing near the creek mouth.
- **Lake Mead Inflow:** Lake level was at its lowest in two years during this trip; moderately strong current persisted past Pearce Ferry, meaning **no true lake (lentic) habitat was sampled** — sampling remained in riverine conditions extending downstream of the historic reservoir boundary.

### Water Quality Conditions
- Mainstem water temperature: 16.07–20.29°C.
- Spencer Creek temperature (continuous logger, 3.5 days): 17.59–30.59°C, with a spring-fed micro-refugium ~3.5°C cooler than the main creek channel (identified via Ryan Tempmentor).
- River stage fluctuation of 52 cm recorded over 36 hours at RM 245.3, reflecting dam-operation-driven discharge variability characteristic of the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] interim flow regime.

## Key Findings

1. **Low overall catch rates** in both the mainstem and Spencer Creek were attributed to unusually clear water and very low tributary discharge, reducing gear effectiveness — 1,696 total mainstem/inflow fish captured (Table 4) dominated by [[Red Shiner]] (862) and [[Speckled Dace]] (451).
2. **No endangered species detected**: neither [[Humpback Chub]] nor [[Razorback Sucker]] were captured or observed, despite continued targeted monitoring near RM 253.2 (site of a prior October 1993 humpback chub collection).
3. **Surprise Creek as flannelmouth sucker nursery**: Repeated juvenile captures and snorkeling observations support designating this tributary as an important recruitment habitat requiring further study.
4. **Spencer Creek barrier effect reconfirmed**: For the second consecutive trip, only speckled dace were found above the high-gradient barrier (Site 3A), and these fish exhibited superior body condition versus dace below the barrier — supporting a **competitive exclusion/interference hypothesis** involving non-native [[Red Shiner]] and [[Fathead Minnow]].
5. **Long-distance flannelmouth sucker movement documented**: A tagged female moved ~88 miles downstream over one year (Havasu Creek to Spencer Creek confluence), demonstrating substantial mainstem connectivity and movement potential for this native catostomid.
6. **Striped bass condition improved** relative to the prior trip, alongside observed carp spawning activity, suggesting favorable reproductive-season conditions for these species during the interim flow period.
7. **No lake habitat sampled**: Due to historically low Lake Mead levels, riverine conditions extended beyond Pearce Ferry, limiting the study's ability to characterize the lentic [[Lake Mead]] inflow zone during this trip.
8. Ongoing recommendations emphasize continued multiple-pass sampling in [[Spencer Creek]], further investigation of the [[Surprise Creek]] nursery function, and sustained mainstem PIT-tag monitoring of native fishes.
