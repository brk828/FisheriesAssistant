# Freedom of Information Act Request for Exotic Fish Stocking Records in USFWS Region 2

#ichthyology #marine-biology #exotic-species #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) request submitted by the [[Maricopa Audubon Society]] to the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Region 2]] on October 7, 1993. The request seeks comprehensive records of exotic (non-native) fish stockings in watersheds and stock ponds within Region 2 over the preceding 10 years, including biological opinions, correspondence, and identification of responsible administrators. The organization requests fee waiver based on public interest and their established record of disseminating government information to enhance public understanding of controversial exotic species introductions.

## Background & Description

The [[Maricopa Audubon Society]] is a non-profit conservation organization focused on conserving imperiled species and threatened habitat while fulfilling educational goals for members and the general public. The organization has an established track record of obtaining and disseminating information through FOIA requests, with fee waivers consistently granted on multiple occasions between 1990-1993.

The request targets [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Region 2]], which encompasses the Southwest United States including areas of [[Arizona]], [[New Mexico]], and surrounding states. The focus is on exotic fish species—those non-native to Region 2—introduced through federal programs and federally funded state stocking programs into watersheds and stock ponds over a 10-year period (approximately 1983-1993).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The introduction of exotic fish species into native watersheds and stock ponds represents a significant conservation concern in the [[Southwest United States]]. Non-native fish can compete with, prey upon, or hybridize with native species, potentially threatening imperiled aquatic fauna. The [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]]'s involvement in exotic species introductions is characterized as "highly controversial" in the request.

Stock ponds and watershed systems in [[Region 2]] support diverse native fish assemblages that may be vulnerable to impacts from introduced species. The request reflects growing public and conservation community concern about federal agency roles in exotic species management during the early 1990s.

## Key Findings

The FOIA request specifically seeks three categories of information:

1. **Comprehensive stocking records**: A complete list of all exotic fish stockings (non-native to Region 2) within watersheds and stock ponds for the preceding 10 years, including fish species types, specific locations, introduction dates, and whether stockings were conducted by federal officials or through federally funded state programs.

2. **Documentation and correspondence**: Copies of all [[Biological Opinions]], correspondence, and memoranda addressing exotic fish introductions in Region 2 watersheds and stock ponds, covering both direct federal programs and federally funded state stocking programs.

3. **Administrative accountability**: A list of administrators responsible for authorizing exotic fish stockings within Region 2 watersheds and stock ponds over the 10-year period.

The organization demonstrates extensive prior success in disseminating FOIA-obtained information through multiple channels including: listing petitions, court documents, public interest litigation, local and national newsletters, public presentations, Congressional briefings, briefings to church and international governmental officials, and news stories. Their publications reach an estimated 6-7 million people through conservation press outlets nationally, locally, and internationally.

Publications that have featured information from previous FOIA requests include: [[Albuquerque Journal]], [[Arizona Daily Star]], [[Arizona Republic]], [[High Country News]], [[National Wildlife]], [[Sierra Magazine]], [[San Francisco Chronicle]], and numerous other regional and national outlets.

The request deadline was set for October 22, 1993, with the organization indicating intent to begin the appeal process immediately if materials were not received by that date.
