# Particle Ingestion by Tilapia galilaea Is Not Affected by Removal of Gill Rakers and Microbranchiospines

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This 1987 study in the [[Transactions of the American Fisheries Society]] examines how the filter-feeding cichlid *Tilapia galilaea* ingests plankton. The authors tested a [[Double-Filtration Hypothesis]], which proposed that [[Gill Rakers]] strain large particles such as zooplankton while [[Microbranchiospines]] strain small particles such as phytoplankton. Surgical removal of both structures did not alter particle ingestion rates or selectivity. Ingestion increased with particle size, leveling off above about 20 µm, and with particle concentration, approaching asymptotic maxima. The authors conclude that the hypothesis is rejected for fish of this size.

## Background & Description

*Tilapia galilaea* (Galilee Saint Peter's fish, also cited as *Sarotherodon galilaeum*) is a planktivorous, filter-feeding [[Cichlidae]] species from [[Lake Kinneret]], Israel. Earlier anatomical work by [[Greenwood (1953)]] and [[Gosse (1956)]] suggested a double-filtration mechanism. Microbranchiospines are small, mucus-covered structures bearing fine lateral spines. They occur in a single row posterior to the gill rakers on the second, third, and fourth gill arches. The study compared untreated fish, sham-operated fish, and fish with gill rakers, microbranchiospines, or both removed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Source of fish:** Fish were obtained from the Mevo Hamma fish ponds or by castnetting in Lake Kinneret during March and April.
- **Acclimation:** Fish were held at the [[Yigal Allon Kinneret Limnological Laboratory]] for at least one month in 60-L tubs supplied with fresh lake water containing natural plankton.
- **Test particles:** Synthetic [[Microspheres]] (4.3 to 70.3 µm) made from Sephadex G-25, Bio-Rad Bio-beads, and Ionics beads, plus heat-killed [[Zooplankton]] larger than 112 µm (including *Keratella*, *Mesocyclops*, *Bosmina*, and *Ceriodaphnia*) at 156 to 2,196 organisms per liter.
- **Conditions:** Feeding trials ran at 28.8 ± 0.1 °C under fluorescent light and natural sunlight.
- **Context from other taxa:** Related studies addressed [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) and [[Blue Tilapia]] (*Tilapia aurea*), and mucus-entrapment feeding in [[Larval Lampreys]] and [[Spined Loach]].

## Key Findings

- **Size selectivity:** Ingestion rates of small fish increased with particle size and leveled off above 20 µm. Large fish were efficient grazers of small microspheres but still selective. ANOVA showed a significant particle-size effect (P = 0.01) and no significant effect of gill rakers (P = 0.72), microbranchiospines (P = 0.65), or their interaction (P = 0.52).
- **Concentration dependence:** Ingestion rates rose with microsphere concentration (75 to 1,900 mL⁻¹) and reached asymptotic maxima of 4,785 microspheres·fish⁻¹·min⁻¹ for small fish (3.9 to 6.0 cm SL) and 84,746 microspheres·fish⁻¹·min⁻¹ for large fish (12.6 to 14.3 cm SL).
- **Michaelis-Menten parameters:** Half-saturation constants (K_m) were 122.5 for small fish and 462.7 for large fish.
- **Surgical effects:** Removal of gill rakers and microbranchiospines did not change ingestion rates. Fish without these structures showed no apparent difference from untreated or sham-operated fish, although trial numbers were too few for curve fitting.
- **Regeneration:** Partial regeneration of gill rakers and microbranchiospines occurred during the trials. Regenerated structures were abnormal in size, number, and position, so the authors argue this does not explain the null result.
- **Hypothesis rejected:** Because altered and normal large fish ingested particles at similar rates, the authors rejected the double-filtration hypothesis for fish of this size.
- **Proposed functions:** Capture may involve mucus entrapment of particles. Gill rakers may retain eggs and fry during mouth brooding. Microbranchiospines may protect gill filaments from abrasion by fine particles.
- **Experimental design:** The study used 2 × 2 factorial treatments, sham-operated fish, and small-fish groups, each replicated six times. Surgery durations averaged 22.7 ± 1.5 min. The study was supported by [[National Science Foundation]] grant BSR-8415619.
