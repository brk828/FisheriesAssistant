# Interagency Request for a Native Fish Backwater Rearing Facility on Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A letter dated October 30, 1989, from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Lower Colorado Regional Office) to [[Arizona State University]], the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], and the [[National Park Service]] requests help in developing an interagency proposal. The proposal would examine the feasibility of a native fish backwater rearing facility on [[Lake Mohave]]. The letter reports that [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] populations in Lake Mohave show little or no natural recruitment and consist solely of fish older than 30 years, which are expected to die off within a few years. Reclamation proposes rearing fish in a predator-protected backwater and releasing them at a size less vulnerable to predation. A response was requested by November 15, 1989.

## Background & Description

**Target species**
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), referred to in the letter as "Suckers" (the OCR text reads "Xyraunchen texanus," corrected here).
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), referred to as "Chubs."

**Background on the request**
- Several agencies have researched and monitored Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub in Lake Mohave for the past few years. Reclamation has funded and participated in these efforts.
- Aging of Suckers and Chubs from Lake Mohave indicates little or no natural recruitment. Populations consist only of "old (> 30 year)" fish.
- Reclamation adopted an agency Fishery Policy committed to maintaining or enhancing fishery resources on Reclamation projects. It also signed Memorandums of Understanding with the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and the [[National Park Service]] (NPS).

**Prior work**
- [[Paul Marsh]], [[W. L. Minckley]], and [[Jim Brooks]] studied spawning and rearing of Suckers in isolated backwaters of Lake Mohave. They renovated a naturally isolated backwater and stocked it with spawning Suckers.
- The Suckers spawned successfully, and the backwater "teemed with young Suckers."
- Rising lake elevations reconnected the backwater to the reservoir, allowing [[Predation]] by [[Exotic Fish]]. Small juveniles disappeared as exotic fish numbers increased.

**Proposed concept**
- A permanent or possibly temporary barrier would create a [[Native Fish Backwater Rearing Facility]].
- Reclamation considers this approach cost effective and low maintenance.
- Fish raised to a size less susceptible to predation would be released into the lake to replenish existing populations.
- The concept had been discussed informally among agency staff.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** Lake Mohave on the [[Lower Colorado River]], managed as a Reclamation project reservoir in the lower Colorado River basin. The Lake Mead National Recreation Area, administered by NPS, is also part of the setting.
- **Key threats:** Predation by exotic fish is identified as the cause of juvenile losses in the earlier backwater experiment. Reconnection with the main reservoir, driven by rising lake elevations, gave predators access to the rearing area.
- **Recruitment status:** Little or no natural recruitment has been observed. The letter states that loss of these genetic pools "would be disastrous."
- **Management implication:** Protecting early life stages through a controlled backwater, followed by release of larger fish, is the proposed strategy for population recovery.

## Key Findings

1. **Population status:** Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub in Lake Mohave show little or no natural recruitment. The populations consist solely of fish older than 30 years, which are expected to die off in the next few years.
2. **Urgency:** Reclamation states that "time is running out" for these populations and that the technology, expertise, and resources exist to save them.
3. **Proposed action:** Reclamation requests expertise from the FWS, NPS, and Arizona State University to cooperatively develop an interagency proposal assessing the feasibility of a [[Native Fish Backwater Rearing Facility]] on the lower Colorado River.
4. **Prior evidence:** Earlier backwater spawning trials by Marsh, Minckley, and Brooks produced successful spawning and abundant young Suckers. Predation by exotic fish eliminated small juveniles after the backwater reconnected with the reservoir.
5. **Proposed design:** A permanent or possibly temporary barrier would protect rearing fish. Fish reared to a larger size would be released to supplement existing populations.
6. **Next steps:** Responses were requested by November 15, 1989. If favorable, Reclamation would coordinate a meeting to discuss methods for starting the program.
