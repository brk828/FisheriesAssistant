# Investigation into Possible Occurrence of Colorado Squawfish in the Lower Green River and Little Snake River Drainages of Wyoming

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Wyoming Game and Fish Department administrative report (February 1987; authors Kevin Johnson and Melvin Oberholtzer) describes a 1986 survey for [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), a federally listed endangered species, in the [[Green River]] and [[Little Snake River]] drainages. The study was funded by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] under Section 6 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. No squawfish, and no other federally threatened or endangered fish, were found. The authors recommend that the species be considered extinct in both drainages of Wyoming.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** The Colorado squawfish was historically found in the lower Green River, but no documented occurrence exists in Wyoming since the construction of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. It has never been recorded from the Little Snake River drainage, so it was considered extinct in the state. Some potentially suitable habitat had never been sampled specifically for the species.
- **Study design:** Five stations were sampled on the Green River system in 1986 (Station A on the Green River; Stations B and C on the [[Black's Fork River]]; Stations D and E on the [[Ham's Fork River]]). Five stations on three streams were sampled in the Little Snake River drainage: Stations A, B and C on the Little Snake River, plus single lowest stations on [[Muddy Creek]] and [[Savery Creek]].
- **Study area characteristics:**
  - *Green River (Station A):* About 8 miles long, regulated by [[Fontenelle Dam]] 68 miles upstream, which has altered flow, temperature and sediment relative to the period when squawfish were known there.
  - *Black's Fork and Ham's Fork:* High sediment loads and extreme flow fluctuation. A USGS gage near Station B recorded flows from 0 to 9,980 cfs. Ham's Fork substrate is predominantly fine silt and sand.
  - *Little Snake River:* A medium-sized stream with large annual flow fluctuations. Average widths were 73.2 ft (Station A), 61.3 ft (Station B) and 71.9 ft (Station C). Pools reached 8 to 12 ft deep, while riffles were generally under 1 ft deep. The period-of-record average discharge was 523 cfs.
  - *Muddy Creek:* Much smaller, averaging about 17 ft wide and 0.87 ft deep, with mud and sand bottoms.
  - *Savery Creek:* Average discharge of 104 cfs over a 29-year record, with a 49.2 ft average width at the study site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regime:** Both drainages experience severe flow extremes, including periods of zero flow (Little Snake River in September 1968; Dixon gage in September 1977 and August 1981; Savery Creek in 1959 and 1960).
- **Habitat mismatch:** The authors concluded that typical squawfish habitat (big, deep water) is absent from the Little Snake River and its tributaries in Wyoming. Although gravel bars are present, flows often fall to the point that spawning areas are exposed or too shallow when temperatures reach the optimum 68–72 °F range for squawfish spawning.
- **Stocking effects:** Rainbow trout in the Green River were dominated by 1986 plantings (67,328 fish averaging 9.7 per lb, about 6.3 inches). Brown trout (100,000 fish averaging 53 per lb, about 3.5 inches) were also stocked in 1986. Channel catfish have been stocked into upper [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] since 1981, and the Little Snake River trout population reflects Colorado Division of Wildlife stocking upstream of Station C.
- **Sampling method:** Electrofishing was used throughout. Methods included a boom-shocking boat on the Green River, an inflatable raft with hand-held anodes on the Black's Fork and Ham's Fork, and a three-person raft crew on the Little Snake River. Population estimates used [[Mark And Recapture]] on the Little Snake River and the [[DeLury Removal Method]] on Muddy and Savery creeks.

## Key Findings

**Target species**
- No [[Colorado Squawfish]] were collected or observed at any sampling area in either drainage.
- The authors recommend that the species be considered extinct in the Green River and Little Snake River drainages of Wyoming.
- Long reaches of the Black's Fork, Ham's Fork and Little Snake rivers remain unsampled, but the authors judged a viable population very unlikely.

**Green River system (17 species, Table 1)**
- Species checklist includes trout, whitefish, catfish, suckers, minnows and sculpin. Examples are [[Rainbow Trout]], brown trout, lake trout, kokanee salmon, Snake River cutthroat trout, [[Mountain Whitefish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[White Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], carp, Utah chub, fathead minnow and mottled sculpin.
- Only two catfish were captured or observed, in swift run/riffle habitat in 1 to 2 ft of water. This was the first time channel catfish had been sampled by electrofishing in the lower Green River.
- On the Black's Fork and Ham's Fork (Table 2), twelve species were found across four stations. Suckers dominated every station, averaging 73% of the total catch. White suckers were most numerous at every station except lower Black's Fork, where flannelmouth suckers were more abundant.
- Rainbow trout occurred at 11 of the 12 sampling locations and averaged 13.0 in and 0.68 lb on the Black's Fork, and 13.7 in and 0.82 lb on the Ham's Fork.
- Two channel catfish were sampled in total: one from lower Black's Fork (22.5 in, 5 lb) and one from upper Ham's Fork (11.7 in, 0.53 lb).

**Little Snake River drainage (13 species, Table 3)**
- Thirteen species were collected. Those marked for population estimates were rainbow trout, mountain whitefish, channel catfish and roundtail chub. Relative abundance codes were used for the rest.
- Flannelmouth suckers, white suckers, speckled dace and mottled sculpins were widely distributed.
- Roundtail chubs were absent from the upper half (above roughly 6,500 ft) of Station C. Channel catfish were absent from Station C.
- Whitefish reproduction appeared fair, with 94% of measured whitefish under 9 inches. Roundtail chubs appeared to spawn in lower reaches, with virtually all fish under 8 inches collected at Station A.
- The sampled reaches represent about one-third of the 43.1 Wyoming stream miles of the Little Snake River (14.3 miles sampled on two August days).

**Combined results**
- A total of 19 species were recorded across both drainages. Eleven were common to both, one (creek chub) occurred only in the Little Snake River, and six (Snake River cutthroat trout, brown trout, kokanee salmon, lake trout, Utah chub and fathead minnow) occurred only in the Green River.
- Rainbow trout were found in limited numbers at all but one station despite poor flow and water quality for trout in the lower Black's Fork and Ham's Fork.
- The authors suggested that if channel catfish continue expanding and build in numbers, they could create local fisheries where little angling had previously existed.
