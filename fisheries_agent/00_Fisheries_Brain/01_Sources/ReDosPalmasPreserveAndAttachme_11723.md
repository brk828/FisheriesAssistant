# Dos Palmas Desert Pupfish Habitat Reconfiguration Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-pupfish

## Executive Summary

This record is a 1995 interagency package from the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Palm Springs office. It proposes reconfiguring ten abandoned fish-rearing ponds at the [[Dos Palmas Preserve]] into [[Desert Pupfish]] propagation and experimental habitat. The package includes a cover letter from the BLM Area Manager to the [[Desert Pupfish Recovery Team]], an internal memorandum from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) staff, a consultant's engineering design report (Terra Nova), and a site drawing. BLM asks the team for comments on design, project viability, and research potential, with comments requested by April 28, 1995. The design must also serve [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] habitat, which creates a design tension the documents only partly resolve.

## Background & Description

The Dos Palmas Preserve lies in the [[Salt Creek Basin]] north of the [[Salton Sea]] and is jointly administered by [[Nature Conservancy|The Nature Conservancy]] and BLM. It contains several natural oases and a series of artificial ponds. The ponds were built to rear fish for a commercial enterprise and are no longer used for that purpose. They currently support many wildlife species, including migratory and resident waterfowl, passerines, and wading birds.

The cover letter (dated March 27, 1995, following a March 8, 1995 call with Patricia Lock-Dawson) describes the site as follows:

- Ten similarly sized ponds, each about 100 m by 30 m, separated by dikes about 15 m wide.
- Each pond has its own intake and outlet structure.
- Fresh water is piped from a well in the Dos Palmas Oasis, immediately northwest of the complex.

BLM proposes to connect nine ponds in series in three groups of three by partially removing the dividing dikes, with separate intake and outlet structures for each group. The configuration is intended to create three discrete, internally connected sets of ponds.

The design rationale is:

- Proximity to naturally occurring source populations of desert pupfish.
- Because the ponds are artificial, the new population would qualify as a [[Tier 3 Population]] under the [[Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan]].
- The Recovery Plan sets an objective of establishing 27 Tier 3 populations in California, and this project would contribute toward it.

The USFWS memorandum notes that the project is in early stages and that BLM hopes to begin groundwork within one year. Team members were asked to comment as time allowed and to copy comments to Doug Duncan, who was out of the country until May 8.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Terra Nova]] engineering report frames the habitat requirements for both species of concern:

- **Yuma Clapper Rail:** Preferred habitat is shallow water with tules, relatively small areas of open water, and habitat units of 5 to 10 acres.
- **Desert pupfish:** Preferred habitat, "to the extent understood," is shallow flowing water.

The report states that the planning survey was done with ponds flooded, so pond-bottom elevations may not be accurate. A construction survey is needed when the ponds are drained to set final bid quantities.

The cover letter identifies the ten-pond complex as a chance to run [[Habitat Experimental Design]] trials. Each group of three could represent a gradient of habitat components. The variables named are:

- Water depth
- Channel roughness
- Temperature
- Vegetative cover
- Desiccation regime
- Current velocity
- Substrate variation

The letter says the Recovery Team should suggest which variables to include. BLM would coordinate with other scientists to build a statistically valid design.

## Key Findings

**1. Unit 1 (upper ten ponds, numbered 12 to 21)**

- Pond 21 stays as a regulating basin.
- Ponds 20 to 12 are connected in three groups of three. Water flows west in pond 20, east in pond 19, and west in pond 18. The same pattern repeats in the other two groups.
- Each pond keeps its existing eastern feed valve and western drain box. Ponds in each group are linked by cutting levees and installing small water-control structures.
- Operational flexibility is a stated priority. Individual ponds or groups can be drained to remove trash fish or maintain channels.
- The design specifies a channel bottom slope of 0.0005 to 0.0006 ft/ft, giving 0.225 to 0.27 ft of total fall per pond, and a channel 1.5 ft deep. The slope may be adjusted at channel layout.
- Levees around the ponds are to be lowered by at least "2+ feet" (as written in the scan) to supply fill. The west and east perimeter levees stay, because storm flows have overtopped some ponds before.
- Planned elevation is given as -110.0 (datum not stated).

**2. Earthwork quantities (Unit 1)**

- Fill needed: about 14,500 cubic yards, plus 6 small flashboard structures in levee cuts.
- Cut available: 15,563 cubic yards.
- Total fill requirement is also given as about 20,232 cubic yards, with about 5,732 cubic yards of borrow from outside the cells.
- The source figures do not reconcile internally (14,500 fill vs. 20,232 needed; 15,563 cut + 5,732 borrow ≠ 20,232). Final quantities depend on the construction survey.

**3. Water-conveyance alternatives**

- Open levee cuts with concrete flashboard structures and grouted rock riprap.
- Cut the levee, install a concrete flashboard riser with pipe to the bottom of the next pond, then refill. This is the least expensive option but looks less natural. It does allow fish to pass between ponds when the pipe is full.
- A redwood flashboard structure with downstream riprap as an alternative to concrete.
- Flashboards are recommended in all cases to control depth and allow independent draining. The existing west-side drain risers may be cut to the height needed.

**4. Construction method**

- Drain all ponds before work. Build in units of three so that work can follow available funding.
- The recommended method is to fill to below final grade, then cut the channel from the fill and spread removed material on the adjacent banks. The alternative of placing fill on both sides of a channel is described as the costliest.
- Cost estimates are to be prepared for each method and each conveyance option.

**5. Unit 2 (lower ponds, numbered 1 to 11)**

- Objective: habitat for the Yuma Clapper Rail, by removing levees and leveling pond bottoms.
- Two areas: ponds 7 to 11 (7.5 acres) and ponds 1 to 6 (7.4 acres).
- Ponds 7 to 11: about 7,700 cubic yards of cut and about 7,000 cubic yards of fill. The document notes about 30% more cut than fill may be needed and can be met by lowering the pond bottom at construction.
- Ponds 3 to 7: about 6,113 cubic yards of fill and about 9,038 cubic yards of cut.
- The unit description refers to "the lower 9 ponds" but lists ponds 1 through 11. The discrepancy should be checked against the drawings.

**6. Open questions for the Recovery Team**

- Is ten ponds' worth of shallow, flowing habitat enough to meet the Tier 3 goal and support valid experiments?
- Which habitat variables should be built into the design, and how should depth, slope, substrate, and flow be varied across the three-pond groups?
- How will the Unit 1 pond-depth design, which makes all three ponds in each group equal depth, be modified to include experimental gradients?
- How should the pupfish and clapper rail objectives be balanced, given that clapper rail habitat favors shallow, vegetated water with limited open water?
