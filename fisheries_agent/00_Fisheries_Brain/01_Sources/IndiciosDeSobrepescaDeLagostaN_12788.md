# Overfishing Indicators in the Spiny Lobster Fishery of Pina Beach, Pernambuco

#ichthyology #marine-biology #palinuridae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 SUDENE fisheries study (Boletim de Estudos de Pesca 5(2)) by Solancy J. C. de Moura examines whether the spiny lobster stocks fished off [[Pina Beach]], Recife, Pernambuco, show signs of overfishing. Landings data from January 1962 to June 1965 were analysed for the two commercial species, [[Panulirus argus]] and [[Panulirus laevicauda]]. The author concludes that the commercial fishery on [[Panulirus argus]] at Pina is depleting the stock, with the fishing effort exceeding the availability of lobsters, and that fishing at [[Pontas de Pedra]], a probable recruitment source, weakened stocks further.

## Background & Description

- **Fishery origin:** Lobster fishing off Pina intensified from 1956, when export of frozen tails to southern Brazil and the United States began.
- **Early data gap:** No statistics exist for the fishery before late 1961, so total catch and fishing effort are unknown for early years.
- **Concern:** Much of the catch exported from Pina in early years came from the area, and its contribution to exports declined in later years, raising fears of stock exhaustion in Pernambuco.
- **Monitoring:** From 1962, the [[SUDENE]] Division of Fisheries Resources (Divisão de Recursos Pesqueiros) began statistical and biological monitoring of most Pina fisheries.
- **Objective:** To test, using biological and fishery-biology data up to the first quarter of 1965, whether populations show signs of overfishing.

### Methods

- **Landing samples:** Samples were taken from the catch of one boat per sampling event, rarely the same boat. Initially 50 lobsters were sampled per sample (up to one per day); from 1964, 100 lobsters were sampled twice weekly. Statistical tests found no significant difference between the two sample sizes, so the larger scheme was adopted.
- **Sample totals (Table 1):** The table lists 97 to 113 samples per year and total lobsters sampled of 5,054 (1962), 5,414 (1963) and 4,617 (1964).
- **Production and effort:** Data came from (1) fishermen on return from trips (weight of lobsters in kg, number of traps hauled, depth and distance from shore) and (2) internal logbooks of fishing companies (number of trips and catch only).
- **Age structure:** Modal groups were separated by Petersen's principle applied to landing curves. Ages were matched to growth data from Santos, Costa and Moura (1965).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species composition:** Landings were dominated by [[Panulirus argus]] (90% in 1962, 82% in 1963, 72% in 1964), with [[Panulirus laevicauda]] rising from 10% to 18% to 28% over the same years.
- **Size trends (Table 3):** Mean carapace length of P. argus fell over 1962–1964 (males 86.2 to 81.7 mm; females 79.7 to 79.2 mm), mainly among large individuals. P. laevicauda mean size rose (males 69.2 to 72.4 mm; females 65.8 to 68.3 mm). The author attributes the P. laevicauda increase to better sampling coverage, and possibly to fishing previously not covering the whole population. A competitive shift caused by heavier fishing on P. argus is also proposed.
- **Age structure:** The dominant P. argus age group fell from 30 months (first half of 1962) to 18 months (later periods), indicating that younger lobsters increasingly support the catch.
- **Gear:** Traps (covos) were the fishing gear. Mesh size did not change during the study, so the author argues that gear selectivity followed the availability of lobsters by size.
- **Productivity (Tables 2 and 4):** Mean monthly catch per trap fell from 2.1 kg (1962) to 1.3 kg (1963) and 1.2 kg (1964). Mean annual catch per trip also declined year on year, and fishing effort appeared stable or slightly reduced, though complete statistics were lacking.

## Key Findings

1. **Species shift:** P. laevicauda's share of landings rose steadily as P. argus declined from 90% to 72% (1962–1964).
2. **Size decline:** Mean size of P. argus fell, especially among large males, while P. laevicauda mean size rose.
3. **Recruitment impact:** Fishing took place on lobsters still recruiting to the stock, and the dominant cohort shifted from 30-month to 18-month animals.
4. **Declining productivity:** Catch per trip and per trap fell from 1962 to 1964, with the 1962 semesters described as the high-yield period.
5. **Depletion conclusion:** Fishing effort at Pina exceeded stock availability, and the author concludes that commercial fishing is directly responsible for the observed depletion of P. argus.
6. **Pontas de Pedra effect:** Intensive fishing at [[Pontas de Pedra]], probably a temporary habitat for future Pina recruits, further weakened stocks available at Pina.
7. **Management implication:** Without changes, depletion was expected to continue; the author implies that protecting recruiting groups is needed.

**Note:** The source does not give statistical tests for the size or productivity trends, and the author states that complete effort data were not available.
