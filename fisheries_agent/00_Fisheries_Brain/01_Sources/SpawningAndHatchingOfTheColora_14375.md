# Spawning and hatching of the Colorado squawfish (Ptychocheilus lucius)

## Executive Summary
This 1983 hatchery study by Theophilus D. Inslee (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Dexter NFH) evaluated methods for artificially propagating the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] to support large-scale fingerling production for conservation stocking. Across two spawning seasons (1981–1982), researchers compared spawning in circular tanks, ponds, and manual stripping following hormone induction with carp pituitary (CP) and chorionic gonadotropin (CG). Hand-stripping after CP injection combined with Heath incubator hatching proved by far the most productive and controllable method, yielding **9,500 swim-up fry per pound of adult female**, versus a maximum of 2,578 fry/lb from tank spawning and as little as 0 fry/lb from unstimulated pond controls. The study also established fecundity rates, egg size parameters, optimal formalin dosages for fungal control, and comparative incubation techniques (Heath trays vs. McDonald jars). Findings emphasized that egg maturation is strongly temperature-dependent, that a narrow viability window (7–14 days) exists after natural egg maturation, and that overuse of hormone injections induces high broodstock mortality.

## Background & Description
- **Egg diameter:** Ranged from 1.70–2.19 mm (mean 1.86 mm / 0.0730 in) across 18 sampled females.
- **Egg density:** Approximately 183 eggs/mL; one liquid quart contained 173,822 eggs.
- **Fecundity:** Ranged from 18,372 to 43,137 eggs/lb of body weight (mean 31,094 eggs/lb) in 8-year-old hatchery females.
- **Sac fry density:** 254–382 sac fry/mL (mean 324/mL); swim-up fry density ranged 82–201/mL, reflecting yolk absorption and size increase during development.
- **Secondary sexual characteristics:** Developed gradually from early April onward in both sexes; males matured (ripened) before females, consistent with typical cyprinid reproductive phenology.
- **Egg adhesiveness:** A key indicator of egg quality — naturally matured eggs exhibited strong, durable adhesiveness, while prematurely hormone-induced eggs showed poor adhesiveness, duller coloration, pasty texture, and greater size variability.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Colorado Squawfish]] (also referred to historically as the Colorado Pikeminnow) is a large-bodied cyprinid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], now federally endangered. Historical spawning behavior, as documented by Vanicek and Kramer (1969) in the Green River at Dinosaur National Monument, involves natural cues tied closely to **water temperature and photoperiod**, with wild spawning occurring roughly one month later than the accelerated maturation observed in the warmer hatchery ponds at Dexter, NM.

- **Temperature dependence:** Egg maturation and ovulation are primarily temperature-driven rather than photoperiod-driven, based on comparisons with wild population data.
- **Spawning substrate:** Naturally and in hatchery ponds/tanks, squawfish deposit eggs over **gravel beds** in areas of moderate water current, mimicking riverine spawning bar conditions.
- **Brood stock origin:** Fish used in this study were originally hatched in 1974 at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and reared at Dexter NFH on a pelleted trout diet supplemented with forage fish.
- **Conservation context:** This research directly supported captive breeding programs for an endangered species, aiming to standardize hatchery techniques transferable to production-scale conservation aquaculture.

## Key Findings

### Spawning Methods Comparison (1981)
| Method | Fry Produced per lb Female |
|---|---|
| Hand stripping + CP injection | **9,500** |
| 10' circular tank | 2,578 |
| 0.1-acre pond (injected) | 333 |
| 0.1-acre pond (control) | 0 |
| 6' circular tank | 350 |
| 6' circular tank (control) | 0 |

- Stripping after hormone injection was identified as the most **cost-effective, low-labor, and controllable** method for large-scale production, largely due to superior control over fertilization and incubation.
- Tank/pond spawning suffered from uncontrolled fungal infestation, inability to monitor egg viability, and fish loss (e.g., jumping from tanks).

### Hormone Protocols
- **Optimal dose:** 2 mg carp pituitary (CP) per lb body weight induced ovulation in 30 of 35 females within 12–20 hours.
- Repeated CP injections beyond two attempts resulted in failure to ovulate and frequently caused **death from fluid retention/stress** (suspected ~50–100% mortality in early trials).
- **CG (chorionic gonadotropin)** at 100 units/lb (females) or 300 units/lb (males) successfully advanced maturation timing by ~6 weeks, but resulting eggs were of poor quality/viability, useful only for experimental (non-production) purposes such as the companion [[Temperature and Hatching Success]] study by Marsh & Pisano (1982).
- Egg viability window after natural maturation was narrow: approximately **7–14 days**, after which eggs became overripe, infertile, or resorbed.

### Incubation & Hatching
- **Heath incubator trays** with clayed (de-adhered) eggs achieved the highest hatch rates (up to 41.7%).
- Eggs adhered directly to Heath tray screens hatched poorly (12.5%) due to uneven distribution, reduced water flow, and fungal growth.
- **McDonald hatching jars** performed comparably to clayed Heath trays (41.4% vs. 40.8%), suggesting jars are a viable alternative meriting further controlled study.
- Overall 1982 hatch rate across 2,063,000 eggs from 33 females: **29.4%**.

### Fungal Control (Formalin Bioassay)
- Young eggs (8 hrs) were markedly more sensitive to formalin than older eggs (51 hrs), the inverse of the sensitivity pattern seen with malachite green in other species.
- **Recommended treatment:** 1:100 formalin for 10 minutes was safe across all egg ages tested; 1:150 for 10 minutes effectively controlled fungus in multi-day-old incubating eggs without harming viability.
- These concentrations are species-specific to Colorado squawfish and should not be extrapolated without independent bioassay for other species (cf. comparison to [[Razorback Sucker]] egg tolerance, Inslee 1982).

### Egg Enumeration Techniques
- Both the **von Bayer volumetric technique** and gravimetric measurement produced statistically comparable egg counts, validating either method for hatchery-scale quantification.

### Management Implications
- Predicting egg maturation via accumulated **temperature units** combined with observation of secondary sexual characteristics could allow hatchery managers to better time broodstock handling and hormone administration.
- Findings support adopting **CP-induced stripping + Heath tray incubation with clayed eggs** as the standard protocol for Colorado squawfish propagation, with further paired-female jar-vs-tray studies recommended to control for individual egg quality variability.
