# Bonytail Reintroduction in the Upper Colorado River Basin: FY 1999 Annual Project Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 1999 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], prepared by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] Moab Field Station, documents efforts to reestablish the functionally extirpated [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Methods were hatchery stocking of juveniles, monitoring of stocked fish by [[Electrofishing]], [[Seining]] and [[Fyke Net]] sampling, and [[Radio Telemetry]] of adult bonytail to study habitat use and overlap with [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*). In 1999, 20,000 juvenile bonytail were stocked equally into the [[Green River]] and [[Colorado River]]. Fifteen radio-tagged adults were tracked after release at [[Dewey Bridge]]. The project was on track, with 100% of FY 99 work completed and expenditures matching the budget.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** Bonytail (*Gila elegans*), a native cyprinid described in the source as functionally extirpated from the Upper Colorado River Basin. Roundtail chub (*Gila robusta*) was tagged to test habitat overlap with bonytail.
- **Project period:** Initiated 1996; final year 2000.
- **Recovery Program link:** Supports the General Recovery Program, specifically the objective to manage, augment or restore populations and the implementation of a basinwide bonytail restoration plan.
- **Principal investigators:** Steve Meismer and Melissa Trammell, [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], Moab Field Station.
- **Hatchery sources:** [[Wahweap Hatchery]] (Page, Arizona) provided stocked fish; [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] transferred larvae for spring 2000 stocking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stocked fish and size at release.** Stocked juveniles from 1999 averaged 93 mm total length (TL) and 6 g at release on 23 April 1999. All had been implanted with [[Coded Wire Tagging]] (CWT). Cumulatively, 20,381 bonytail of 80–130 mm TL had been stocked into the Colorado River and 13,000 similar-sized fish into the Green River.

**Movement and capture patterns.**
- **Green River:** Stocked bonytail were captured from RK 179.0 to RK 73.2, indicating movement of up to 120 km from the stocking point at RK 193.2. The largest single capture (18 bonytail) occurred at Three Canyon (Trin-Alcove) on 11 May 1999, after a flow drop of about 4,000 cfs; fish were in remaining deep pools (about 1 m deep).
- **Colorado River:** The largest electrofishing catch was 17 bonytail, of which 11 were from the 1999 cohort. Captures of 1999 fish extended downstream to RK 48.1, about 108 km from the Dewey Bridge stocking site.

**Radio telemetry (adult bonytail and roundtail chub).**
- Fifteen adult bonytail (6 g radio tags, 90-day battery life) were stocked at Dewey Bridge on 24 March 1999, with two roundtail chub tagged during ISMP sampling.
- All 15 bonytail moved upstream to a deep eddy/pool above the old Dewey Bridge, similar to fish from prior stockings. The first fish left the area on 12 April and the last moved on 14 May.
- Movement ranged from 0.0 to 88.2 km. Most movement was downstream, though upstream movement was not uncommon.
- Four fish moved 4.3–36.5 km downstream before returning to the bridge pool. The greatest upstream movement was 2.9 km, to the confluence with the Dolores River. Fish 641 was the only fish that remained upstream of the bridge (last contact 1.8 km above it).
- Eleven habitats were identified as frequently used by fish ranging from Dewey Bridge to Big Bend (RK 115.1).

**Habitat and gear notes.** Seining in the Green River captured 62 stocked bonytail during [[Nonnative Cyprinid Removal]]. Flooded tributary habitats immediately after stocking were identified as sites where bonytail could be seined.

## Key Findings

**Stocking and production**
- In 1999, 20,000 bonytail were stocked (10,000 per river).
- Dexter National Fish Hatchery transferred 100,000 larvae in two shipments to Wahweap Hatchery for spring 2000 stocking. All arrived in good condition.
- Table 1 projects stocking for 2000–2003 (for example, 80,000 Dexter-origin fish per cohort year), with a 70% survival rate factored into the plan. The report notes these projections reflect current facility capacity, not the needs identified in the Utah stocking plan.

**Monitoring results (summary)**

| Method | Result (as reported) |
|---|---|
| Green River electrofishing (ISMP and razorback sucker monitoring) | 5 bonytail (3 stocked 1999, 2 earlier) |
| Green River fyke nets | 14 bonytail (3 released the previous year) |
| Green River seining (nonnative cyprinid removal) | 62 stocked bonytail |
| Colorado River electrofishing (ISMP) | 17 bonytail (11 from 1999 cohort) |
| Colorado River seining (Potash to confluence) | 4 stocked 1999 fish |
| Colorado River fyke nets | 1 bonytail |
| Colorado River nonnative removal sampling | 5 fish from 1999 stocking |

**Radio telemetry**
- All 15 radio-tagged bonytail had at least three contacts. Tracking ran over 2–3-day trips, with one 24-hour intensive session.
- The Dewey Bridge pool appears to be a preferred habitat for many stocked fish.

**Objectives status**
- Objective 1 (stocking): completed.
- Objective 2 (monitoring of prior stockings and 1999 cohort): results included above; final report due May 2000.
- Objective 3 (radio telemetry): results and final movement analysis deferred to the May 2000 report.
- Objective 4: Utah State University responsibilities were listed but not detailed in this excerpt.

**Recommendations**
- Seining appears viable for capturing age-1 bonytail, especially in the period immediately after stocking. Increased seining, particularly in flooded tributaries, may be beneficial.
- Stocking under this scope of work ends in FY 2000. Projected efforts in Table 1 will not be covered under this project. Bonytail stocking needs were identified in the Utah Stocking Plan for Endangered Fish Species of the Upper Colorado River Basin (Hudson et al. 1999), which the report notes exceed what current facilities can produce.

- FY 99 work completed: 100%. Recovery Program funds for publication: $0.00.
- Project status: on track and ongoing. Data submission was pending, expected 15 January 2000.
