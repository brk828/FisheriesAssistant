# Arnett Creek/Telegraph Canyon Native Fish Reestablishment Project Environmental Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a draft Environmental Assessment (EA) issued by the [[Tonto National Forest]] (USDA Forest Service, Globe Ranger District, Pinal County, Arizona, dated April 16, 1996). It evaluates a proposal to re-establish native fish in [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]], in cooperation with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD). The project would remove non-native green sunfish and mosquitofish with fish toxicants and build a fish barrier to keep exotic species from re-entering from Queen Creek. The cover letter (April 17, 1996) invited public comment by May 20, 1996. Five alternatives (A through E) are compared. Alternative A is No Action. Alternatives B and C would stock longfin dace and desert sucker. Alternatives D and E would also stock Gila chub and Gila topminnow, a federally Endangered species.

## Background & Description

**Project origin.** In 1992 the Forest Service and AGFD identified an opportunity to re-establish a native fish community. Green sunfish and mosquitofish are aggressive exotics that prey on native fish. Success would require a barrier to block their upstream movement from Queen Creek during high flows.

**Management context.** Arnett Creek and Telegraph Canyon lie within Management Areas 2B and 2F of the Tonto National Forest Land Management Plan (LMP). The LMP directs that warmwater, non-game streams be managed to support Gila sucker (a synonym of [[Desert Sucker]]) and [[Longfin Dace]]. The adjacent Proposed [[Picketpost Mountain]] Research Natural Area (RNA) was proposed in the LMP in 1985. Its later Geological, Botanical, and Zoological Area proposal is handled in a separate EA.

**Purpose and need.** The project supports AGFD's reintroduction efforts. Native fish were historically present in the Arnett watershed but are now absent, and they cannot recolonize or survive there without intervention.

**Scoping and timeline.** Scoping began at a July 22, 1993 meeting. The Interdisciplinary (ID) Team met in January 1995 and held a field trip on February 16, 1995. The ID Team leader met with the District Ranger in September 1995. In January 1996 the Forest Service decided to advance the fisheries EA first, because its funding (largely non-appropriated grant and donation money) had to be spent or returned in 1996. Pending items included a Biological Assessment and Evaluation for TE&S species, an archaeological clearance, a 401 permit request to the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality, and a Pesticide Use Proposal.

**Alternatives summary.**
- **A (No Action):** No barrier, no toxicants, no cooperation on reintroduction.
- **B:** Barrier at Site 1 (intermittent reach); stock longfin dace and desert sucker.
- **C:** Barrier at Site 2 (ephemeral reach, access via abandoned road); stock longfin dace and desert sucker.
- **D:** As B, plus Gila chub and [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **E:** As C, plus Gila chub and Gila topminnow.

Toxicant use relies on [[Rotenone]] (still ponds) and [[Antimycin A]] (flowing water). Barrier construction is a gabion basket wall with a splash pad. Monitoring includes pre- and post-treatment macroinvertebrate sampling and timing treatments to protect [[Yavapai Leopard Frog]] breeding periods. Potassium permanganate would be on site to detoxify water if needed. Native fish would be re-evaluated within 9 years.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Setting.** The area lies in the Transition Zone between the Colorado Plateau and the Basin and Range province. Arnett Creek's watershed is about 31 square miles, with elevations from 4,600 ft in the headwaters to 2,400 ft at the Queen Creek confluence. Most of the creek is a small intermittent desert wash. A perennial reach of about 2.5 miles forms where shallow bedrock forces subflow to the surface. The analysis area covers 3.5 miles of Arnett Creek and 0.7 miles of lower Telegraph Canyon.

**Hydrology and water rights.** The Forest filed an instream flow application in October 1992. A March 1994 report set water requirements for dependent resources at 0.3 cfs (June through October) to 3.0 cfs (January through April). One measured flow reached 33.6 cfs. Indirect estimates of January 1993 flood flow exceeded 3,000 cfs. The Arizona Department of Water Resources reportedly intends to approve the permit conditioned on native fish reintroduction. A November 1992 baseflow sample was a calcium sulfate water type, and constituents were within acceptable warmwater fishery ranges or of unknown effect.

**Livestock and riparian management.** About 2 miles of Arnett Creek are inside the proposed RNA and are essentially ungrazed. The 1.2-mile Arnett Creek riparian pasture was created in 1993 under a Memorandum of Understanding. It allows 20 to 50 cattle for 3 to 30 days, with utilization limits of 50% for pole and mature woody species, 40% for seedlings, and 60% for perennial grasses. The Superior Allotment carries 315 adult cattle. Monitoring after one use year found less than 5% utilization.

**Upland vegetation.** Botanical surveys documented more than 240 vascular plant species. Community types include Semidesert Grassland on the Picketpost Mountain summit and Saguaro and Palo Verde Scrubland on south-facing slopes. Creosotebush and Bursage Scrubland occurs on conglomerates.

**Wildlife and TE&S species.** Green sunfish predation is believed to suppress leopard frog populations. Adults or tadpoles occurred only in pools with low-to-no green sunfish densities. The only Threatened or Endangered species known in the renovation zone is the Peregrine Falcon. Narrow-headed garter snake (Candidate) is within its probable range. Yavapai leopard frog (Candidate) has been recorded in the area.

**Habitat condition.** Habitat-unit surveys found that 83% of units had no instream cover from small woody debris and that 62% had no cover from terrestrial vegetation. Substrate provided no cover in 52% of units. Only 3.5% of banks were undercut. Algae provided the most aquatic cover, and an unidentified aquatic plant forming thick mats contributed to bank stability but not instream cover.

**Candidate species assessment.** The EA considered eight native fish for reintroduction. Only [[Gila Chub]] had a historical record in the Arnett drainage. Recent surveys found only green sunfish and mosquitofish.

**Recreation and special uses.** Arnett Creek is a dispersed recreation site near Superior. The Arizona Trail runs to the west along Alamo Canyon. The project is not expected to affect Roosevelt Lake or the Upper Salt River, which are a short drive away. A 1929 special use permit to the [[Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum]] covers 726 acres for livestock exclusion and revegetation studies, and the permit is outdated.

## Key Findings

**Piscicide safety.** Both toxicants are EPA-registered for fisheries use. Antimycin A is listed under the trade name Fintrol. Rotenone is a botanical compound derived from tropical plants in the Fabaceae family. Studies indicate that fish-killing concentrations are not toxic to mammals or birds, although Rotenone may harm larval forms of Yavapai leopard frogs and javelina. Skin and eye irritation can be prevented with protective gear, washing with soap and water, and public notification.

**Macroinvertebrates and plankton.** Some taxa may decline temporarily after treatment, including Cladocera, Copepoda, Amphipoda, and Ephemeroptera. Others, such as Plecoptera, Odonata, and Gastropoda, are generally not adversely affected. Recolonization is faster when untreated upstream waters act as a source. A 1985 Antimycin A treatment on Big Dry Creek in the Gila National Forest produced minor, short-term effects and no long-term effects.

**Barrier site selection.** Site 2 is preferred from a fisheries standpoint. It meets all critical selection criteria except criterion 1a. Its floodprone width (about 90 feet within a 150-foot valley) is high, which reduces flood damage risk. Anchoring is in bedrock and large boulders, and the gradient is relatively high. Access uses an abandoned road needing rehabilitation rather than new construction. Its higher barrier cost is offset by lower transportation costs compared with Site 1.

**Species suitability.**
- **[[Longfin Dace]]:** An excellent candidate. It tolerates flashy floods, repopulates quickly (as in Pinto Creek in 1993), and favors the sand, gravel, and small cobble substrates found in Arnett Creek.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]:** Suitable, and included in all action alternatives.
- **[[Gila Chub]]:** Not currently supported. Pools with undercut banks and dense cover are lacking. Monitoring is recommended.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]:** Not a preferred species at present. It is not highly resistant to flood displacement, and it is not found below 5,000 ft. Reevaluation is recommended.
- **Spikedace and Loach Minnow:** Reviewed, with loach minnow found in riffles up to about 2,200 m.

**Expected benefits.** Removing green sunfish is expected to benefit leopard frogs over the long term. The project would support the reversal of longfin dace and desert sucker declines. The Forest expects it to be compatible with the proposed Geological, Botanical, and Zoological Area designation. Alternative A would jeopardize the instream flow water right, which is conditioned on native fish reintroduction.

**Limitations and open issues.** Endangered Species Act restrictions would not apply to longfin dace or desert sucker. Concerns about mining and livestock restrictions were raised, and the EA states that these fish would not impose such restrictions. Gila topminnow (listed Endangered under the ESA of 1973) would constrain future spot treatments in Alternatives D and E. Issues about private land access, illegal off-road vehicle use, mineral claims, and watershed-wide designation were deferred to the separate Picketpost Mountain EA.

**Consultation and records.** Over 300 people on the LMP Commentor Mailing List received the initial scoping letter. Participating agencies included AGFD, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the Bureau of Reclamation, and Arizona State University. The project record lists 13 documents from 1993 to 1995, including scoping letters, meeting sign-in sheets, and public input.
