# Synopsis of the Brachypterinae (Plecoptera: Taeniopterygidae)

#entomology #plecoptera #taeniopterygidae

## Executive Summary

This synopsis revises the systematics of the [[Brachypterinae]], a subfamily of [[Taeniopterygidae]] stoneflies (order [[Plecoptera]]) found across the temperate and cool parts of the Northern Hemisphere. The authors recognize 13 genera. The most primitive is [[Kohnoperla]], from Japan. Five new genera are described: [[Bolotoperla]] from North America, and [[Kohnoperla]], [[Mesyatsia]], [[Okamotoperla]] and [[Zhiltzovia]] from Eurasia. Five new species are described by the authors, one by [[Mitsuko Kohno]], and several genus transfers are made. The work was part of a wider study of winter stoneflies in eastern North America.

## Background & Description

The subfamily [[Brachypterinae]] (type genus [[Brachyptera]]) is separated from the sister subfamily [[Taeniopteryginae]], which contains only [[Taeniopteryx]], by six male and female characters. Brachypterines have a male 9th sternite that is produced and scoop-shaped, inner male subanal lobes that are complex and partly membranous, and a [[Basicercal Process]] on the first male cercal segment. The female 9th sternite is produced well beyond the base of the subanal lobes. Brachypterines lack coxal gills, and males have at least two cercal segments.

The authors compared brachypterine external and genital structures with related stoneflies to infer ancestral (primitive) conditions. Key characters include:

- **Wings:** normal in both sexes in the ancestral form; reduced in males of some [[Brachyptera]] species and in [[Zhiltzovia]], where both sexes have very short wings.
- **Male third femur:** a callus, which is probably ancestral and survives mainly in [[Kohnoperla]] and some [[Taeniopteryx]].
- **[[Epiproct]]:** the primitive form has a basal bulb and single prong with no internal structures. Most brachypterines have a hollow epiproct containing an [[Internal Bulb]] and a [[Sleeve]] (invagination), which may contain filaments.
- **Male cerci:** primitively six to eight segments, reduced to two in [[Brachyptera]] and to four or five in [[Rhabdiopteryx]] and [[Zhiltzovia]].

The authors note that counts of veins and cercal segments are based on small samples and may be exceeded in larger series.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document gives limited ecological information, mainly collection context:

- Many records are from winter or early-spring collections, consistent with the study's focus on winter stoneflies. Examples include specimens collected in March in Virginia and West Virginia, and in February in Arkansas.
- [[Zhiltzovia brevipennis]] is known from two sites on fairly large rivers in the mountains of Armenia.
- [[Oemopteryx contorta]] is a small insect known from a very few sites in the Appalachian region.
- [[Oemopteryx vanduzeea]] is from California.
- Some [[Taenionema]] species are recorded from cool northern waters, including Great Whale River near Hudson Bay, Koksoak River in Quebec, and Hopedale in Labrador. [[Taenionema japonicum]] occurs from the Altai Mountains to Kamchatka and south to Hokkaido.
- [[Kyphopteryx dorsalis]] was collected in Tibet and Sikkim during the 1924 British Everest Expedition.

## Key Findings

**Genus-level revision**

- **13 genera** are recognized in the Brachypterinae.
- **New genera (5):** [[Bolotoperla]] (North America; one species, *B. rossi*), [[Kohnoperla]] (Japan; one species, *K. yugawae*), [[Mesyatsia]] (seven species listed, Himalaya, Japan, Central Asia), [[Okamotoperla]] (one known species, *O. zonata*, from a single specimen) and [[Zhiltzovia]] (one species, *Z. brevipennis*).
- [[Kohnoperla]] is the most primitive genus. It has a femoral callus and lacks the epiproct sleeve, and its venation is among the simplest in the subfamily.
- [[Doddsia]] is monotypic (*D. occidentalis*). The authors keep [[Mesyatsia]] separate from [[Doddsia]] despite Zwick's (1973) placement of *Mesyatsia lunata* in *Doddsia*, because the genera disagree in several characters.

**Taxonomic actions**

- **New species:** *Taenionema atlanticum* (eastern North America, Labrador and Quebec records), *Strophopteryx appalachia*, *S. arkansae*, *S. inaya* and *S. ostra*. *Kohnoperla yugawae* was described by Kohno (1964 name, formally described here).
- **Synonymy:** *Taenionema frigidum* (Navas) is a junior synonym of *T. japonicum* (Okamoto). *Rhabdiopteryx quadrata* Koponen is also treated as a synonym of *T. japonicum*.
- **New combinations:** *Obipteryx tenuis* (Needham 1905), *Strophopteryx nohirae* (Okamoto 1922) and *Taenionema japonicum* (Okamoto 1922). *Taeniopteryx tenuis* is transferred to [[Obipteryx]] because its male 9th sternite is spoon-shaped, which is diagnostic of Brachypterinae and unlike [[Taeniopteryx]].

**Species counts within genera**

- [[Brachyptera]]: Illies (1966) lists 19 species, and at least 3 have been described since. Two groups are recognized: the *trifasciata* group (six species, Europe and Balkans) and the *risi* group (16 described species).
- [[Strophopteryx]]: three groups. The *fasciata* group has five species (including the new *appalachia*, *arkansae* and *inaya*). The *limata* group has one species. The *ostra* group contains *S. ostra* and the questionable *S. cucullata*.
- [[Taenionema]]: nine species in four groups. The *pacificum* group includes the new *T. atlanticum*.
- [[Rhabdiopteryx]]: six species listed, from Europe, Pakistan and Bulgaria, among others.
- [[Oemopteryx]]: four groups (*loewii*, *glacialis*, *contorta*, *vanduzeea*).

**Diagnostic and structural findings**

- The male 10th tergite lobes are probably ancestral. They are most developed in [[Kohnoperla]], [[Obipteryx]] and [[Okamotoperla]].
- The [[Strophopteryx]] and [[Taenionema]] distinction rests on the central hind margin of the male 9th sternite, which is upturned in [[Strophopteryx]] and depressed in [[Taenionema]]. The authors consider that a good case could be made for uniting them, but note that all [[Taenionema]] except one have 10th-tergite lobes.
- The ancestor of Taeniopterygidae probably had femoral calluses, which have since been lost except in [[Kohnoperla]] and some [[Taeniopteryx]].

**Phylogenetic conclusions**

The authors could not select a single family tree from the possibilities. They tentatively treat the extra branches of Rs and Cu, and the costal crossveins, as reacquired (derived) features rather than primitive ones. They arrange the genera into five groups:

- **Group A:** [[Kohnoperla]] (primitive features).
- **Group B:** [[Obipteryx]] and [[Okamotoperla]].
- **Group C:** [[Brachyptera]] and [[Oemopteryx]].
- **Group D:** [[Doddsia]], [[Mesyatsia]] and [[Kyphopteryx]].
- **Group E:** [[Rhabdiopteryx]], [[Strophopteryx]] and [[Taenionema]].

[[Bolotoperla]] and [[Zhiltzovia]] are not placed in any group. [[Bolotoperla]] may be intermediate between [[Kohnoperla]] and [[Obipteryx]] if its missing internal bulb is a primitive trait. [[Zhiltzovia]] is tentatively treated as a specialized derivative of Group B.

**Caveats noted by the authors**

- Several species (e.g., *Okamotoperla zonata*, *Taenionema japonicum*) are known from old or unstudied types, and the status of some names remains uncertain.
- A revised grouping of the European [[Rhabdiopteryx]] species, and further work on the Japanese species, is needed.
- Future work on fine anatomy, cytology and immunological reactions is expected to clarify evolution within the subfamily.
