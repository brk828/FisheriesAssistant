# Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program Steering Committee Meeting Materials

#conservation-biology #endangered-species-act #river-ecology #multi-species-management

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting materials for the June 17, 1996 [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP) Steering Committee meeting, including memoranda, agenda, and a briefing paper on program options. The MSCP was initiated in 1993 by Arizona, California, and Nevada in response to proposed critical habitat designation for four endangered fish in the [[Colorado River]]. The program aims to conserve habitat and work toward recovery of included species within the 100-year floodplain while accommodating current water diversions and power production. The materials present two strategic options for program development: a "Wait and See" approach pending completion of section 7 consultation (scheduled for May 1997) versus a "Program in Place" approach involving immediate hiring of facilitators, administrators, and consultants to support conservation measures.

## Background & Description

The [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] represents a partnership between federal and state agencies formalized through multiple agreements. In November 1994, a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) established the partnership between Arizona, California, Nevada, and the [[Department of the Interior]]. A Feasibility Assessment completed in December 1994 recommended a comprehensive, coordinated conservation strategy as the optimal approach.

In August 1995, a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) was signed by the [[United States Department of the Interior]], [[Arizona Department of Water Resources]], [[Arizona Game & Fish Commission]], [[Colorado River Board of California]], [[California Department of Fish & Game]], [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]], and [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]]. A Memorandum of Clarification (MOC) was being circulated in 1996 to clarify the MOA and accommodate conservation organization concerns prior to full participation.

The program scope encompasses the 100-year floodplain of the [[Lower Colorado River]]. The Steering Committee is chaired by Gerald R. Zimmerman, with the Working Group chaired by Chris Harris of the [[Arizona Department of Water Resources]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The MSCP was initiated in response to proposed designation of critical habitat for four endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River]], though the specific species are not named in these administrative documents. The program addresses "included species" within the 100-year floodplain and aims to reduce the likelihood of additional species listings under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

The program operates within the context of section 7 consultation under the ESA, with [[Bureau of Reclamation]] conducting a biological assessment. The [[U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service]] is responsible for developing Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs) and Reasonable and Prudent Measures (RPMs) during consultation. The MOC provides that the Service shall consider: (1) conservation measures developed by MSCP parties and (2) the status of MSCP development when developing RPAs and RPMs.

The scope of compliance under Reclamation's section 7 consultation is five years, with provisions for reconsultation at that time or upon completion of the MSCP. The MSCP is intended to address impacts from current and future operations on included species and critical habitat beyond the five-year consultation period.

## Key Findings

**Program Goals and Objectives:**
- Conserve habitat and work toward recovery of "included species" within the 100-year floodplain of the [[Lower Colorado River]]
- Reduce the likelihood of additional species listings under the ESA
- Accommodate current water diversions and power production
- Optimize opportunities for future water and power development, consistent with law
- Achieve compliance through a "proactive, cooperative Program"

**Strategic Options Presented:**

The Working Group identified two basic options for the Steering Committee:

1. **"Wait and See" Option**: Delay further program development until Reclamation's section 7 consultation is completed (scheduled for May 1997). This option has two variations: (a) do nothing until consultation is final, or (b) proceed with administrative tasks and interim conservation measures (ICMs) but delay plan development. The briefing paper notes that failure to make substantive progress would leave stakeholders vulnerable to RPAs and RPMs developed solely by the Service.

2. **"Program in Place" Option**: Proceed immediately with program development and ICMs to accomplish partnership goals and support any potential outcome of the biological opinion. This would involve Steering Committee agreement to procure funding to hire a facilitator, program administrator, and program consultant, and fund interim conservation measures prior to the final biological opinion. This option would allow proactive support of the Service's potential determinations in the biological opinion.

**Meeting Agenda Items:**
- Status of Memorandum of Clarification
- Presentation of Working Group's recommendations
- Status of DOI/DOJ appraisal of FACA compliance
- Status of draft Upper Basin Endangered Species Legislation
- Presentation of Endangered Species Program Cost Analysis
- Status of Reclamation's Biological Assessment
- Discussion of draft RFP for Washington, D.C. representation services

**Timeline Considerations:**
- Section 7 consultation scheduled for completion: May 1997 (May 15, 1997 is the date scheduled for Reclamation's response to final biological opinion)
- Stakeholder planning horizon extends beyond the five-year consultation scope
- Reconsultation provisions exist at five years or upon MSCP completion

The Working Group requested that all Steering Committee principals and interested parties carefully review the briefing paper and be prepared for discussion at the June 17, 1996 meeting. A Risk Analysis outlining advantages and disadvantages of the two options was completed and referenced in an appendix.
