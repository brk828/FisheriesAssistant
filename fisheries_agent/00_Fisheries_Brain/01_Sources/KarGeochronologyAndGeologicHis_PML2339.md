# K-Ar Geochronology and Geologic History of Southwestern Arizona and Adjacent Areas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #geochronology

*Note: This document is a geological research paper, not an aquatic or fish study. The required ichthyology/taxon tags are not applicable; the tags above reflect the actual subject matter.*

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Geological Society Digest paper (Volume XII, 1980) by Shafiquullah, Damon, Lynch, Reynolds, Rehrig and Raymond reports 174 [[Potassium-Argon Dating|K-Ar]] ages from 154 localities in southwestern Arizona and adjacent areas, combined with a synthesis of the region's geologic history. Many ages refine or overturn earlier assignments based on lithologic similarity. The authors argue that Precambrian rocks record a long-lived convergent margin, that the Laramide and mid-Tertiary orogenies produced distinct magmatic and tectonic pulses, and that Basin and Range faulting and Plio-Pleistocene volcanism shaped the modern landscape and drainage.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** Southwestern Arizona from southern Nevada to about latitude 30°N, extending into the Sonoran Desert, with parts of northwestern Sonora. Access is limited by military ranges such as the [[Barry M. Goldwater Range]] and the Yuma Proving Ground.
- **Physiographic setting:** The area spans the [[Basin and Range Province]] and the transition to the [[Colorado Plateau]]. Ranges are generally small, separated by broad alluvial basins; Paleozoic rocks are sparse.
- **Method:** K-Ar dating assumes a closed system. Potassium is measured by atomic absorption and argon isotopes by mass spectrometry, with analytical precision of about 1.5 percent. The authors caution that reset or partially degassed minerals (for example, [[Biotite]] at temperatures near 225°C) can yield misleading ages, so each date must be interpreted against field relations and petrology.
- **Previous work:** Key early studies include [[Eldred Wilson]] (1920s–1930s reconnaissance mapping), Darton (1925), Gilluly (1937, 1946), and later work on Tertiary stratigraphy, Cretaceous–Tertiary metamorphic core complexes, and low-angle faults.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a geologic chronology rather than an ecological study, but it provides relevant environmental history:

- **Climate:** Paleoclimatic indicators (buried desert landforms, weathering patterns, evaporites) show arid conditions throughout most of the Cenozoic. Thick evaporite deposits accumulated in the [[Luke Basin]], Red Lake, and Picacho basins.
- **Drainage evolution:** The [[Colorado River]] system and the [[Gulf of California]] (which did not exist before about 17 m.y. B.P.) developed over the Cenozoic. The lower Colorado River cut down rapidly after the Pliocene uplift of the [[Colorado Plateau]] (estimated at about 130 m/m.y. of downcutting), while the upper [[Gila River]] became integrated within the past 3 m.y.
- **Subsidence:** Groundwater withdrawal has caused land fissures and subsidence in alluvial basins; in the Picacho Basin about 300 km² subsided 2 to 4 m over 25 years.

## Key Findings

**Precambrian history**
- The older Precambrian was a long-lived convergent margin with accretion to the southeast at about 2 km/m.y., ending with emplacement of transcontinental batholiths about 1,400–1,450 m.y. ago.
- The [[Yavapai Series]] (about 1,820–1,720 m.y.) and younger [[Pinal Schist]] sequences record two orogenic cycles; the [[Mazatzal Orogeny]] deformed the older sequences.
- [[Diabase Sills]] intruded at about 1,100–1,200 m.y. ago, the final Precambrian magmatic event. Precambrian K-Ar ages were grouped into four clusters (pegmatitic muscovite ≥1,400 m.y.; biotite/muscovite 1,320–1,460 m.y.; 1,080–1,180 m.y. ages tied to diabase emplacement; and unexplained ages).

**Paleozoic and Mesozoic history**
- No evidence was found for Paleozoic magmatism in Arizona. Paleozoic K-Ar ages are reset; a Rb-Sr isochron from the Sierra Estrella confirmed this.
- Mesozoic magmatism moved westward in the Late Jurassic (building the [[Peninsular Ranges Batholith]]) and eastward in the Late Cretaceous as subduction shallowed.

**Laramide orogeny (about 75–50 m.y. B.P.)**
- The largest pluton is the leucocratic two-mica [[Gunnery Range Batholith]], exposed over nearly 15,000 km² in the Yuma Gunnery Range. Biotite ages of 53.1 and 52.8 m.y. and muscovite of 53.2 m.y. are concordant.
- The [[New Cornelia Stock]] at Ajo, a copper porphyry, yields K-Ar ages of 54–55 m.y.; porphyry copper deposits in the region were emplaced between about 72 and 54 m.y. B.P.

**Mid-Tertiary orogeny and Basin and Range disturbance**
- Magmatism and volcanism peaked about 30 to 20 m.y. B.P., producing the [[Superstition-Superior Volcanic Field]] and the [[Pantano Formation]] (about 39.4 m.y. andesite and 30.1 m.y. tuff).
- Metamorphic core complexes (such as the Harcuvar, Harquahala, Buckskin–Rawhide and White Tank mountains) cooled between about 44 and 70 m.y. B.P.
- Angular unconformity: older tilted volcanic rocks were rotated between about 23.7 and 9.6 m.y. B.P., with major tilting between 17 and 12 m.y. B.P. The authors place the Basin and Range disturbance between about 14 and 8 m.y. B.P., with basin subsidence beginning about 15 m.y. B.P.
- Basin and Range basalts have 87Sr/86Sr ratios of 0.703–0.705, indicating mantle origin and transit through the crust with little contamination.

**Plio-Pleistocene volcanism and landscape**
- Four Plio-Pleistocene volcanic fields (including the [[Pinacate Volcanic Field]], [[Sentinel Plain-Arlington Volcanic Field]] near the [[Palo Verde Nuclear Generating Station]], and the [[San Bernardino Volcanic Field]]) were active in the last 5 m.y. Gillespie and Arlington cones are interpreted as monogenetic volcanoes, although their radiometric ages (4.2–1.3 m.y. and 3.2–1.6 m.y.) are spread widely.
- Tributary downcutting rates: about 160 m/m.y. (Virgin River) and 130 m/m.y. (Colorado Plateau uplift).

**Summary of K-Ar dataset**
- The appendix reports 174 K-Ar determinations from 154 localities, with sample numbers, K content, radiogenic argon percentages, and ages with 1σ errors. The full table is not reproduced here.

**Concluding conclusions**
- The authors' main conclusion is that K-Ar dates must be interpreted with field and petrologic context, and that many prior ages based on lithology alone were incorrect.
