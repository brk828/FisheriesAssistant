# Zoogeography and Ecology of the Ichthyofauna of Central Anatolia's Endorheic Lake Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 paper by [[Curt Kosswig]] (Zoologisches Institut und Museum, Hamburg) reviews the history and ecology of the freshwater fish fauna of the [[Central Anatolian Endorheic Basin]], a region of interior lakes with no surface outflow to the sea. Drawing on field visits between 1940 and 1955 and on earlier literature, the author describes the region's lakes, its geological and climatic history, and the origin of its fish fauna. The fauna is small but highly endemic: nine species and four subspecies of *Gobio gobio* are considered endemic, while about eleven widespread species are shared with neighbouring regions. The paper links this pattern to the isolation of lakes, a Pliocene-to-Pleistocene uplift and drying history, and multiple colonisation routes from the Black Sea, the Aegean and the Euphrates–Tigris system.

## Background & Description

**Geography and geological history.** Central Anatolia has been continental land since the Miocene. Its climate became progressively drier and cooler through the Pliocene and Pleistocene. The region, initially only slightly above the Miocene sea level, was uplifted to nearly 1000 m in the late Pliocene or Pleistocene. The endorheic area lies between the West Taurus (south), the West Anatolian highlands (west) and the Mysian and Paphlagonian ranges (north). Its eastern part drains to the Black Sea, the Mediterranean and the Indian Ocean via the Euphrates tributaries.

**Lakes and hydrography.** The region includes the following water bodies, described in the text:

- **Tuz Gölü (Great Salt Lake):** the only azoic lake of the steppe zone, fed by some freshwater inflows.
- **Akgöl (Eregli):** the only other large water body of the Konya plain, where the second *Aphanius* species occurs; its water disappears into a swallow hole (Düden).
- **Beyşehir and Eğirdir lakes:** freshwater lakes, both up to 13 m deep for the fished lakes described in the fauna lists. They have similar hydrography and were in contact at their northern ends.
- **Burdur Gölü:** a bitter-salt lake up to 65 m deep.
- **Acıgöl:** nearly dried out; **Salda Gölü:** unusual chemistry, MgCO3-rich.
- **Çorak Gölü:** about 30 km², sometimes dries out completely.
- **Aksehir and Eber lakes:** interconnected, with the former having dried out about 30 years before the paper.

**Historical lake system.** The authors agree that Central Anatolia was once much wetter, and a large inland lake system (the "Central Anatolian Inland Sea," after Lahn 1948) covered at least the entire endorheic area.

**Biotic interest.** Of the 18 freshwater fish families recorded in Asian Turkey, only 5 occur in the study area: four primary freshwater families and one secondary family, Cyprinodontidae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Species isolation and extreme habitats.** Cyprinodontids tolerate high salinity, extreme temperatures and isolation, and in places they are the only fish present (e.g., Burdur Gölü and Acıgöl). Isolation has produced morphological differentiation and polymorphic populations, particularly in *Aphanius anatoliae* (the Burdur population is described as a hybrid swarm). Population differences in *Pararhodeus kervillei* include the number of perforated lateral-line scales; the Eğirdir population is entirely lateral-line-less. Hybrids between isolated livebearer populations show male sterility.

**Colonisation routes.** Several hypotheses are discussed:

- **Northern (Black Sea) route:** During glacial periods the Black Sea lowered (about 40 m in the last glacial), possibly allowing freshwater species to spread along its southern coast. The Ostsee is cited as an example of a brackish sea that tolerates cyprinids.
- **Aegean (Agdopotamos) route:** A river system across the Aegean lowlands may have enabled exchange between southern Europe and Asia Minor, and later transport of Danubian refugial forms south.
- **Southeastern (Euphrates–Tigris) route:** Many species extend east or southeast; the Syrian-Persian sea was converted to a freshwater bridge during the Pliocene, which permitted only the most vagile forms to enter the inland lake system.

**Introductions.** Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) and wels (*Silurus glanis*) are considered possibly introduced by humans, since the region was long settled. Carp are wild, not domesticated, strains, and restocking from neighbouring lakes is documented. The wels occurs only in Gölhisar Gölü, near a small fortress, and may have been stocked; it is not eaten by Muslims because it lacks scales.

**Dispersal limits.** The West Anatolian mountains separate the central plateau from the Aegean drainage. Rivers such as the Büyük Menderes (Menderes) rise from springs fed partly by Eğirdir water, creating a partial connection. A few species (*Acanthorutilus maeandricus*, *Tylognathus klatti*, *Pararhodeus maeandri*) occur in Büyük Menderes headwaters.

**Ecological niches.** At Beyşehir Gölü, the predatory *Leuciscus lepidus* and the planktivorous pelagic *Alburnus akili* occupy niches that appear in Eğirdir through other species. Juvenile *Acanthorutilus handlirschi* swarm in summer near shores as planktivores; adults are rarely caught in summer, and spawning occurs in February and March. *Chondrostoma regium* (Beyşehir) and *Vimba vimba tenella* (Eğirdir) are ecological counterparts as bottom-feeders with subterminal mouths.

## Key Findings

- **Low diversity, high endemism.** The endorheic region holds only five of the 18 Turkish Asian freshwater families. The paper counts nine endemic species (excluding the doubtful *Hemigrammocapoeta kemali*), four endemic subspecies of *Gobio gobio*, two possibly introduced species (carp and wels), and roughly eleven widespread species.
- **Endemics** include *Schizothorax prophylax*, *Varicorhinus pestai*, *Acanthorutilus anatolicus*, *A. crassus*, *A. handlirschi*, *Alburnus akili* and *Gobio hettitorum*.
- **Widespread species** include *Aphanius cypris* (reaching southern Iraq), *Pararhodeus kervillei* (to Palestine), *Chondrostoma regium* (also in the Euphrates–Tigris), *Alburnus orontis* (throughout Anatolia and Hatay), and *Leuciscus lepidus* (Mosul, Orontes, Tigris).
- **Faunal relationships:** Many endemics have nearest relatives farther east: *Tylognathus* extends to Malacca, *Varicorhinus* is a West Asian genus that spread into Africa, *Schizothorax* is High Asian, and *Cobitis elongata bilseli* relates to Danubian, Chinese and Lake Hovsgol populations, which is compared with the West Mongolian fauna per Banarescu (1960).
- **Absences:** Many Anatolian and Danubian species (e.g., *Blicca bjoerkna*, *Scardinius erythrophthalmus*, *Rutilus rutilus*, *Tinca tinca*, *Aspius aspius*) are absent, as are northern Anatolian species such as *Barbus tauricus escherichi*, *Varicorhinus tinca* and *V. sieboldi*.
- **Egirdir vs. Beyşehir:** Despite similar hydrography and past connection, the two lakes have markedly different faunas. Endemics in either lake probably went extinct in the other after a postglacial split, rather than evolving during the short separation.
- **Sampling record:** The paper compiles 25 numbered localities across the basin (lakes, springs, streams and irrigation ditches), with species lists for each. Notable localities include Burdur Gölü (polymorphic *A. anatoliae*), Beyşehir Gölü (the most species-rich lake, about 13 species), and Akgöl (Eregli), which holds *A. cypris* and *A. crassus*.
- **Conservation concern:** The *Aphanius* population at Serefli Kocahisar (Büyük Tuz Gölü) is recorded as extinct, and some lakes (Acıgöl, Akşehir, Suleyman Gölü) dried out in recent years. Human-driven restocking and drainage are noted as active pressures.
- **Taxonomic caveats:** The *Cobitis taenia* group is a collective name pending revision, which may split it. *Hemigrammocapoeta kemali* was not found by the author and may be a misidentified *Pararhodeus kervillei*.
