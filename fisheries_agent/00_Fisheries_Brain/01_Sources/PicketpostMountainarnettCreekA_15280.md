# Picketpost Mountain/Arnett Creek Analysis Area Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 scoping letter and proposal from the [[Tonto National Forest]] (USDA Forest Service) describes management options for the Picketpost Mountain/Arnett Creek Analysis Area, south of Superior in Pinal County, Arizona. The central fish-related proposal is to reestablish a native fish community in [[Arnett Creek]], an intermittent desert stream whose only fish is the exotic [[Green Sunfish]], a predator of native fish and amphibians. Restoration would require a [[Fish Barrier]] to stop recolonization from [[Queen Creek]], removal of green sunfish, and reintroduction of native fish. The letter also proposes designating the adjacent [[Picketpost Mountain Research Natural Area]] (RNA) or an alternative designation, and it requests public comments by October 15, 1993.

## Background & Description

The Analysis Area covers all of [[Management Area 2E]] and part of [[Management Area 2F]]. It lies within T2N R11E sections 12 and 13 and T2N R12E sections 7, 8, 16, 17, and 18. The proposed RNA contains approximately 93 [[Mining Claims]] within or near its boundaries, which may make it unsuitable for RNA designation in its current form.

Arnett Creek is described as an intermittent desert stream. Its only fish species is green sunfish, which is characterized as exotic and predaceous. The document does not give a scientific name, sample sizes, or population data for any fish.

The [[Land And Resource Management Plan]] (LMP) sets the following direction:
- **Proposed RNA:** Keep the area natural or unmodified while allowing nondisruptive research and education.
- **Arnett Creek in Management Area 2F:** Manage for renewable resources, with primary emphasis on wildlife habitat improvement, water quality maintenance, livestock forage, and dispersed recreation. Riparian areas are to be improved to benefit riparian-dependent resources.

The letter was signed by Judith A. Miller, Acting Forest Supervisor, on September 23, 1993.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Green sunfish occur in [[Queen Creek]], which is downstream of Arnett Creek. The Forest Service predicts that they could move upstream into Arnett Creek during high flows, which is the reason a barrier is needed. A possible barrier site is in T2N R12E section 8.

Riparian conditions were noted during scoping. Riparian vegetation quality and potential are higher upstream of the proposed RNA than within it. The herbaceous component is low within the proposed RNA, and the document states that the cause is unknown.

Other resource concerns raised at scoping include:
- Impacts of [[Livestock Grazing]] on other resources.
- Illegal [[Off-Road Vehicle Use]] possibly harming resource values.
- Mining impacts in both directions, and the large number of mining claims.

## Key Findings

**Proposed management actions** (each described as consistent with the LMP):
1. Reestablish a native fish community in Arnett Creek.
2. Redraw RNA boundaries to exclude mines, making the area suitable for designation.
3. Designate all or part of the Analysis Area as a Zoological or Botanical Area.
4. Issue a [[Special Use Permit]] for all or part of the area to the [[Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum]].

**Fish restoration steps:**
- Build a fish barrier to prevent green sunfish recolonization from Queen Creek.
- Remove green sunfish from Arnett Creek.
- Reintroduce native fish. Species selection will be recommended during the [[NEPA Process]].
- Plan and implement the work with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]].

**Scoping meeting (July 22, 1993, at the Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum):** Representatives from the Arizona Game and Fish Department, the Tonto National Forest, and the Arboretum attended. Issues raised included:
- **Fish:** Use of a fish toxin in Arnett Creek, construction of a barrier within the proposed RNA, and possible limits on other forest uses if a federally threatened or endangered fish is reintroduced.
- **Biotic and physical resources:** Protection of wildlife, fish habitat, riparian vegetation, geologic, and scenic features from ground disturbance.
- **Special designations:** Permit fees, size limits, fence maintenance responsibility, and permit length.
- **Livestock:** Impacts on other resources, and the cost and difficulty of fence construction and maintenance. An accessible grazing comparison area incorporating wildlife, fish, and riparian objectives was proposed.

**Next steps:** Specific management objectives will be developed through the NEPA process. Public responses were due by October 15, 1993, to the Tonto National Forest office in Phoenix. The letter lists two different ZIP codes for that office (85006 and 85010).
