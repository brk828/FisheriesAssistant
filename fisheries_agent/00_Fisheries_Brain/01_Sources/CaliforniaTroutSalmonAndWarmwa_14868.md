# California Trout, Salmon, and Warmwater Fish Production and Costs, 1967-68

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #hatchery-production #Salmonidae #aquaculture

## Executive Summary

This administrative report documents a record-breaking year for [[California Department of Fish and Game]] hatchery operations during fiscal year 1967-68. Despite two hatcheries being out of service for the entire year, the Department planted 2,706,909 lb of fish, exceeding the previous year's record by approximately 325,000 lb. Total fish numbers decreased from 52,167,408 to 39,401,456 due to an unusually small [[King Salmon]] run in the [[American River]], resulting in approximately 15,000,000 fewer salmon fingerlings. However, increased average fish size (from 21.5 per lb to 14.5 per lb) and expanded catchable-sized trout production compensated for reduced numbers. Operating costs decreased to $2,187,843 from $2,256,489, reducing the average cost per pound from $1.05 to $0.87.

## Background & Description

The report represents the 15th consecutive annual fish hatchery cost and production summary issued by the California Department of Fish and Game. During 1967-68, [[Kern River Hatchery]] remained closed due to 1964 flood damage (scheduled to resume 1969), and [[Fillmore Hatchery]] was closed for modernization and expansion. The new [[American River Hatchery]] came online April 1, 1968, helping offset lost production. Multiple hatcheries operated under different funding mechanisms: state-financed facilities, federally-reimbursed installations (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation funding [[Nimbus Hatchery]] and [[Trinity River Hatchery]]), and privately-reimbursed operations (Pacific Power and Light Company funding 80% of [[Iron Gate Hatchery]] costs; California Department of Water Resources funding [[Feather River Hatchery]]; East Bay Municipal Utility District funding [[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]]).

The production program encompassed three major categories: resident fish (primarily trout), anadromous fish (salmon and steelhead), and warmwater fish. Fish were classified by size: fingerlings, subcatchables, catchables (4-6 per lb standard), and trophy fish. The California Fish and Game Commission authorized a trophy trout program allowing 10% of each plant to exceed standard catchable size (approximately 3 per lb).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Production facilities were distributed across five administrative regions throughout California, from [[Crystal Lake Hatchery]] and [[Darrah Springs Hatchery]] in Region 1 to [[Mt. Whitney Hatchery]] and [[Mojave River Hatchery]] in Region 5. [[San Joaquin Hatchery]] achieved the highest single-facility production at 488,262 lb, establishing a new record. The hatchery system supported both put-and-take recreational fisheries and mitigation for water development projects that blocked anadromous fish passage.

Anadromous fish production focused on mitigating impacts from hydroelectric and water supply projects. With more rearing space available in anadromous facilities (due to reduced wild salmon runs), fish size nearly doubled compared to previous years. This size increase was expected to improve adult return rates. The [[Warmwater Reservoir Study]] contributed to larger average trout sizes, with fish reared to approximately 3 per lb for research purposes.

## Key Findings

**Production Statistics:**
- Total production: 2,706,909 lb comprising 39,401,456 fish
- Resident fish: 25,384,457 fish weighing 2,390,666 lb
  - Catchable-sized trout: 7,491,469 fish (1,945,672 lb) at 3.8 per lb
  - Subcatchable trout: 2,384,585 fish (260,617 lb)
  - Fingerlings: 15,465,040 fish (117,198 lb)
- Anadromous fish: 13,756,870 fish weighing 298,496 lb
  - [[King Salmon]]: 10,299,023 fish (42,281 lb)
  - [[Silver Salmon]]: 1,581,544 fish (106,345 lb)
  - [[Steelhead Trout]]: 1,876,303 fish (149,870 lb)
- Warmwater fish: 260,129 fish weighing 17,747 lb at 14.7 per lb (increased from 60.5 per lb in 1966-67)

**Species Diversity:**
- [[Mt. Shasta Hatchery]] and [[Mt. Whitney Hatchery]] each planted 5 trout species
- [[Central Valleys Hatchery]] planted 16 warmwater species including [[Black Bullhead]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Striped Bass]], [[White Sturgeon]], and [[Tilapia]]

**Cost Analysis:**
- Total Department expenditure: $2,224,945 (after reimbursements)
- Reimbursable hatchery operations: $446,061
- Average cost per pound: $0.87 (down from $1.05)
- Catchable-sized trout: $0.73 per lb, $0.19 per fish
- Warmwater fish: $4.65 per lb, $0.32 per fish
- Anadromous fish at reimbursable hatcheries: $1.81 per lb, $0.034 per fish
- Subcatchable trout rearing costs: $1,657,358 (under Commission ceiling of $1,980,145)

**Reimbursement Programs:**
- [[Pacific Gas and Electric Company]] reimbursed $38,678 for 38,830 lb catchable trout and 125,060 [[Kokanee Salmon]] fingerlings planted in power project areas

**Operational Efficiency:**
- Record production achieved despite two facilities offline
- Cost per pound decreased $0.18 due to lower operating costs and increased production
- Average fish size increased substantially (14.5 per lb vs. 21.5 per lb previous year)
- Anadromous fish averaged 52.7 per lb at reimbursable hatcheries
