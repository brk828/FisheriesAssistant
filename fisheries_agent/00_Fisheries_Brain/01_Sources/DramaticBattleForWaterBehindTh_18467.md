# Blue Ridge Dam Water Transfer Project for Copper Mining Operations

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #habitat-creation #water-infrastructure

## Executive Summary

This 1964 newspaper article describes the construction of [[Blue Ridge Dam]] on [[East Clear Creek]] in Arizona's [[Mogollon Rim]] country, part of a three-phase water transfer project by [[Phelps Dodge Corporation]] to support expanded copper mining operations at [[Morenci]]. The $6 million dam, built by Morrison-Knudsen Company, creates a reservoir designed to hold 19,500 acre-feet of water that will be transferred across the Mogollon Rim into the [[Salt River Project]] system. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] plans to stock the resulting five-mile-long reservoir with [[Trout]] species, creating what is anticipated to become one of Arizona's premier highland fishing destinations.

## Background & Description

The [[Blue Ridge Dam]] represents an innovative engineering solution to water allocation challenges in the Southwest. [[Phelps Dodge Corporation]] required additional water for expanded copper mining operations at [[Morenci]], which drew from the [[Black River]], a tributary of the [[Salt River]]. Since all Salt River tributaries were under prior appropriation by the [[Salt River Project]] (SRP), Phelps Dodge secured water rights from [[East Clear Creek]], which flows northward into the [[Little Colorado River]] rather than into the Salt River basin.

The solution involved constructing a dam on East Clear Creek and transferring equivalent water volumes across the [[Mogollon Rim]] into the SRP system, allowing Phelps Dodge to withdraw additional water from the Black River at Morenci. The dam features an unusual double curvature arch design, measuring only 14 feet thick at the base and tapering to 6 feet at its 500-foot crest—one of the thinnest concrete arch structures built in North America at that time. The structure stands 190 feet high and contains 31,000 cubic yards of concrete.

Construction began in mid-July 1963 and the dam proper was virtually completed by mid-December 1963. The design, developed using electronic computer modeling, relies on strength rather than weight to resist water pressure. Uniquely, the dam is designed to move on its base, sliding downstream an inch or more as water accumulates, gradually absorbing stresses and anchoring the arch firmly in position.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The reservoir watershed encompasses a considerable area on the north side of the [[Mogollon Rim]], extending from the eastern slope of [[Long Valley]] to [[Bear Canyon]]. Tributary waterways draining into the reservoir include [[Cienega Canyon]], [[Kehl Canyon]], [[McCarty Draw]], [[Miller Canyon]], and [[General Springs Canyon]]. The reservoir captures the erratic flow of [[East Clear Creek]] plus runoff from this extensive watershed.

When filled, the reservoir will extend five miles in length—west up East Clear Creek canyon and southeast up General Springs and Bear canyons—reaching depths up to 175 feet with a total capacity of 19,500 acre-feet. The remote, high-altitude location in pine-clad country promises excellent conditions for cold-water fisheries.

The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] planned to stock trout in the reservoir once the dam closed and filling began. The [[U.S. Forest Service]] was expected to improve road access and open a nearby camping area for public use, transforming the site into a recreational fishing destination.

## Key Findings

- **Infrastructure Development**: The [[Blue Ridge Dam]] required construction of access roads through remote wilderness, including a steep, winding route carved into solid rock walls descending into [[East Clear Creek]] canyon. Heavy equipment and 31,000 cubic yards of concrete were transported over this challenging terrain.

- **Engineering Innovation**: The dam employs a double curvature arch design with extreme thinness (14 feet at base, 6 feet at crest) calculated by electronic computer modeling. The structure is designed to move on its base, sliding downstream to absorb water pressure stresses—an unusual engineering approach for the era.

- **Water Transfer System**: The project represents the first phase of a three-phase undertaking to transfer water from the [[Little Colorado River]] drainage across the [[Mogollon Rim]] into the [[Salt River Project]] system via an eight-mile tunnel (described in subsequent articles).

- **Fisheries Management Plan**: The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] planned trout stocking to establish a cold-water fishery in the 19,500 acre-foot reservoir, anticipated to become one of Arizona's premier highland fishing locations.

- **Watershed Characteristics**: The reservoir captures runoff from multiple canyon systems including [[Cienega Canyon]], [[Kehl Canyon]], [[McCarty Draw]], [[Miller Canyon]], [[General Springs Canyon]], and [[Bear Canyon]], creating a five-mile-long impoundment reaching 175 feet deep.

- **Access Restrictions**: As of June 1964, the [[U.S. Forest Service]] discouraged public access due to unfinished road conditions and fire hazard in surrounding forest areas, with improved access and camping facilities planned for future development.
