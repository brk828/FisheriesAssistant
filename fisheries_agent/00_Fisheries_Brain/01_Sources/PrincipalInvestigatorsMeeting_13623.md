# Humpback Chub Research Progress and 1993 Plans, Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum, dated November 28, 1992, prepares [[Bio/West]] representatives for a Principal Investigators Meeting on December 1, 1992, and summarizes progress on contract research on the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*, name not stated in the source) in the [[Grand Canyon]] of the [[Colorado River]]. It reports work on food resources, habitat use, reproduction, early-life survivorship, distribution and abundance, native and non-native fish interactions, and a preliminary life history schedule and conceptual population model. It also lists the information planned for collection in 1993 and the data requested from other researchers. The memo is a progress and planning document, not a peer-reviewed study.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** [[Humpback Chub]], the focal fish. Adults and large juveniles (>150 mm TL) and young-of-year (YOY) fish are studied separately, and some analyses use the 250 mm TL threshold for adult stomach work.
- **Study organization:** Work is coordinated by [[Bio/West]] (Principal Investigator Rich Valdez; Project Leaders Bill Masslich and Bill Leibfried) under contract with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BOR). Collaborators include [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), [[University of Arizona]] (UOA), the [[National Park Service]] (NPS), and [[Hualapai Tribe]] (Hualapai Wildlife Management Department).
- **Study area:** The mainstem [[Colorado River]] from Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56) to Diamond Creek (RM 226), plus the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) inflow, the Paria River reach (RM 1 to RM 56), and a reach from Diamond Creek to Pearce Ferry (RM 226 to RM 280).
- **Methods named in the memo:** [[Stomach Pumping]] (non-lethal), [[Drift Sampling]], [[Benthic Sampling]], [[Radiotelemetry]], [[Electrofishing]], [[Minnow Traps]], [[Seining]], [[Trammel Nets]], [[Gill Nets]], [[Hoop Nets]], [[Catch-Per-Unit-Effort]] (CPUE), [[Mark-Recapture]], [[Fin Clipping]], [[Scale Analysis]] with backcalculation, [[Macrohabitat Mapping]] using [[ARC/INFO]], and the [[GCES Geographic Information System]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Food resources:** Stomach contents from early pumping were mainly [[Gammarus]] (amphipods) and [[Simuliidae]] (black fly larvae). Drift samples were collected during increasing, decreasing, and steady flows. Drift densities of Gammarus and simulids were low. Chub may feed on local aggregations of these invertebrates at detrital traps (eddies) and on simulids on woody debris.
- **Habitat:** Adults show disproportionate use of eddies and large eddy return channels. Fish under about 150 mm TL were caught most often in sheltered rock shorelines, with many caught in 1992 from sandy beaches. Habitat availability is mapped for seven areas of the LCR Reach at different flows.
- **Spawning environment:** Mainstem water near the LCR inflow is cold, and the memo notes that mainstem temperatures of about 10°C kill eggs. Few females with expressible eggs were captured, and eggs were recovered mainly from small gravel deposits behind the LCR inflow, where they deteriorate quickly.
- **Movement and aggregation:** Adults and large juveniles form loose aggregations from July to February. Prespawning aggregations form in eddies in late February and March, followed by staging at the LCR inflow. Ascent into the LCR and spawning occur mainly in March, April, and May.
- **Native and non-native fish:** Predators of concern include large [[Trout]], [[Striped Bass]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. Other fishes sampled include [[Roundtail Chub]].
- **Flow regime:** Operations and [[Interim Flows]] from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] are discussed as affecting habitat, and the memo states that movement is related to flow level and LCR condition.

## Key Findings

### 1. Resource Use: Food
- Stomach pumping of humpback chub over 250 mm TL began in September 1992. About 40 fish were processed in 1992.
- The non-lethal stomach pump method was tested on roundtail chub (Wasowicz et al., 1992). The memo reports its efficiency as "160%" and says it did not damage pharyngeal teeth. A value above 100% is not possible for efficiency, so this is probably a scan error for 100%; the source should be checked.
- Preliminary stomach contents were mainly Gammarus and simulids, from samples in September and November.
- Drift samples were low in Gammarus and simulid densities.
- Planned 1993 work: up to 20 chub pumped on every other trip for a total of 120 fish; benthic sampling on reattachment bars; diel radiotelemetry downstream of the LCR to separate feeding from photosensitive behavior.

### 2. Resource Use: Habitat
- Radiotagged adult habitat use was monitored from October 1990 through November 1992, showing disproportionate use of eddies and large eddy return channels.
- Habitat maps are quantified with ARC/INFO and added to the GCES GIS. GCES surveyors produced depth, elevation, and substrate contour maps for the LCR inflow and areas above and below it.
- Planned 1993 work includes revisiting mapped mainstem sites to quantify changes from interim flows, and linking fish habitat maps with eddy return channel, reattachment bar, separation bar, and jetty geomorphology.

### 3. Reproductive Capacity and Success
- Very few female humpback chub with expressible eggs were captured, mostly at the LCR inflow.
- Eggs recovered from small gravel deposits behind the LCR inflow deteriorate rapidly in mainstem temperatures.
- The LCR inflow is the only mainstem location where spawning is known. BIO/WEST does not sample the LCR, so presence of protolarvae cannot be used as evidence of mainstem spawning.
- Mainstem spawning capacity is being examined through substrate analysis as part of habitat mapping.

### 4. Survivorship of Early Life Stages
- Survival of age groups (for fish <150 mm TL) is hard to determine for three reasons: (1) length-frequency analysis does not separate age groups 0, I, and II; (2) spawning/hatching timing and monthly lengths in the LCR are unknown; (3) escapement from the LCR has not been monitored.
- CPUE data exist for young chub from electrofishing and minnow traps, but this is probably not a reliable survival statistic.
- Mark-recapture estimates have not been attempted. Fin-clipping is already done by ASU in the LCR, and BIO/WEST is considering combined small fin punches to estimate longitudinal dispersion.
- Planned 1993 work: examine scales of chub <150 mm TL (the 1993 section of the OCR reads "156 mm") for annuli and checks using backcalculation; increase effort on young-chub dispersal in the mainstem.

### 5. Distribution, Movement, and Abundance
- About 95% of humpback chub captured by BIO/WEST in the mainstem came from a 16 km reach around the LCR inflow.
- Consistent aggregations were found downstream, including at the Shinumo inflow (about RM 125) and the Havasu inflow.
- Population estimates for fish larger than 175 mm TL:
  - 1,395 (95% CI 916–2,254) in March 1991
  - 2,407 (95% CI 1,102–6,564) in September (year not stated; likely 1991)
  - Estimates for 1992 were not yet available. The memo notes the large confidence intervals.
- Radiotelemetry from November 1990 showed statistically greater near-surface activity during crepuscular periods, at night, and under moderate to high turbidity. Movement toward the LCR was related to flow and LCR condition. No clear relationship was found between increasing, decreasing, or magnitude of flows and movement under moderate and interim flows.

### 6. Native and Non-Native Interactions
- The mainstem was sampled from Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56) to Diamond Creek (RM 226) every other month from November 1990 through November 1992, for a total of thirteen sample periods. All captured fish were measured, weighed, and tabulated.
- Stomach samples are being collected from large trout, striped bass, and channel catfish to measure predation on humpback chub. Stomachs from live adult chub (>250 mm TL) and trout are being compared for diet overlap as an indicator of food competition.
- Planned 1993 work includes sampling the Paria River to Kwagunt Rapid, and comparing occurrence, distribution, and abundance with earlier studies (Minckley 1985, Maddux et al. 1989, Kubly 1990) to assess trends in the [[Grand Canyon]] ichthyofauna.

### 7. Life History Schedule and Population Model
- A preliminary life history schedule and conceptual compartmental population model are to be included in the 1992 BIO/WEST Annual Report, due March 1993. The schedule is expected to be refined by March 1993.
- The model is conceptual. Mathematical relationships will be developed by individual investigators for each compartment and then integrated.

### 8. Planned Reaches and Sampling (1993)
- A third reach (labelled "Reach 9" in the OCR, probably a typo) extends from the Paria River (RM 1) to Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56).
- A fourth reach runs from Diamond Creek (RM 226) to Pearce Ferry (RM 280), established as part of Hualapai Aquatic Studies.
- Netting, electrofishing, seining, minnow traps, and hoop nets will continue in 1993, targeting unsampled areas (including overnight sets) and likely fish aggregations.
- Radiotelemetry will be extended to the [[Havasu Creek]] reach to determine movement of downstream fish. Fish will be implanted only when they are captured in large numbers.

### 9. Meeting Schedule and Data Requests
- Principal Investigators Meeting: December 1, 1992. The ACT Meeting follows on December 2. At least one representative will attend the Scientific Information Management Work Group on December 3.
- Data requested from other researchers include stomach content data for young chub from backwaters (AGF), macroinvertebrate data for the mainstem (Stevens), occurrence data from backwaters (AGF) and tributary inflows (UOA), ARC/INFO geomorphology maps (Schmidt), and reports on spawning condition and larval fish (AGF, UOA).
