## Executive Summary

This study by Colton & Alevizon (1981) investigated diurnal (time-of-day) variation in the composition of a [[Coral Reef Fish Assemblage]] at Deep Water Cay, Grand Bahama Island, using fixed-transect visual censusing. Divers conducted underwater visual counts along a 100 × 4 m transect at four time points daily (0900, 1200, 1500, 1800 h) over 20 consecutive days in July–August 1979, yielding 80 total samples. Of 42 species recorded, 25 occurred frequently enough for statistical analysis via chi-square goodness-of-fit tests. Six species (~24%) showed statistically significant diurnal variation in abundance, indicating that time-of-day effects can introduce meaningful bias into standard [[Visual Census Methodology]] of reef fish communities. The authors recommend that repeated censusing studies standardize sampling to a consistent time of day to avoid confounding temporal activity patterns with true ecological or experimental effects.

## Background & Description

This paper is a community-ecology / behavioral study rather than a morphological one; no anatomical descriptions are provided. However, taxonomic/identification notes relevant to census methodology include:

- **Squirrelfishes (Holocentridae)**: Lumped as *Holocentrus* spp. due to close resemblance among species; most individuals identified as [[Holocentrus Rufus]].
- **Groupers and hinds (Serranidae)**: Lumped as *Epinephelus*/*Mycteroperca* spp.; most individuals were [[Epinephelus Striatus]] (Nassau grouper).
- **Parrotfishes (Scaridae)**: *Scarus taeniopterus* and *Scarus croicensis* lumped together (juveniles and adults treated separately) due to difficulty distinguishing them visually; most/all individuals were [[Scarus Croicensis]].
- **Hamlets (*Hypoplectrus* spp.)**: Lumped as a single group due to unresolved taxonomic status among color morphs.
- **Excluded taxa**: The damselfish genus *Eupomacentrus* (now [[Stegastes]]) was excluded from counts because their high activity levels and small size made accurate censusing incompatible with the transect method; these territorial fish were not expected to show diurnal variation regardless.
- Transient schooling events (blue tang [[Acanthurus Coeruleus]] schools twice, juvenile parrotfish school once) passing through the transect were excluded from analysis as non-representative outliers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: High-energy fore-reef face along the northern shoreward edge of the Northwest Providence Channel, dominated by *Acropora*, *Montastraea*, and *Diploria* coral genera; depth 8–10 m with 1–4 m vertical relief above a sandy substrate; water clarity ~30 m visibility; minimal human disturbance.
- **Sampling design**: Two parallel 100 m transect lines spaced 4 m apart, established 7 days prior to sampling to allow fish habituation to transect markers. A single SCUBA diver conducted all censuses at constant swimming speed ~2 m above substrate to minimize observer bias.
- **Species-specific spatial ecology**:
  - Most reef fishes examined have restricted home ranges (per [[Smith And Tyler 1972]]), consistent with the interpretation that diurnal abundance shifts reflect changes in visibility/shelter-seeking behavior rather than large-scale migration.
  - [[French Grunt]] (*Haemulon flavolineatum*) forms small, relatively inactive daytime schools on the reef and migrates to adjacent grassbeds/sand flats at night to feed — a classic example of [[Crepuscular Migration]] in [[Haemulidae]].
  - [[Trumpetfish]] (*Aulostomus maculatus*) has a comparatively large diurnal home range and forages actively across a sizeable reef area, making it more susceptible to real movement-driven abundance shifts rather than shelter-seeking behavior.
  - [[Bluehead Wrasse]] (*Thalassoma bifasciatum*), [[Blue Chromis]] (*Chromis cyaneus*), and [[Foureye Butterflyfish]] (*Chaetodon capistratus*) are hypothesized to shift shelter-seeking/hiding behavior (retreating into reef holes and caves) at specific times, altering their visibility to observers rather than true local abundance.
  - Juvenile [[Striped Parrotfish]] and [[Princess Parrotfish]] (*Scarus croicensis*/*S. taeniopterus*) showed peak abundance at 0900 h with lower, stable abundance thereafter — consistent with predicted patterns tied to crepuscular light-level changes.
- **Community context**: This work sits within the broader literature on [[Nocturnal-Diurnal Changeover]] in reef fish behavior (e.g., Hobson 1965, 1968; Starck & Davis 1966; Collette & Talbot 1972; McFarland et al. 1979), extending the question of temporal bias into the daylight period specifically.

## Key Findings

- **42 total species** recorded across 80 samples (20 days × 4 time periods); **25 species/species-groups** had sufficient abundance (≥20 individuals) for chi-square goodness-of-fit analysis (df = 3).
- **6 of 25 species (~24%)** showed statistically significant diurnal variation in abundance:

| Species | Significant Pattern | p-value |
|---|---|---|
| [[Trumpetfish]] (*Aulostomus maculatus*) | Peaks at 0900 & 1500 h; lows at 1200 & 1800 h | <0.05 |
| [[French Grunt]] (*Haemulon flavolineatum*) | Consistent abundance except sharp drop at 1500 h | <0.05 |
| [[Foureye Butterflyfish]] (*Chaetodon capistratus*) | Sharp drop at 1200 h | <0.025 |
| [[Blue Chromis]] (*Chromis cyaneus*) | Near doubling of abundance at 1500 h | <0.005 |
| [[Bluehead Wrasse]] (*Thalassoma bifasciatum*) | Similar at 0900/1500 h; peak at 1200 h; sharp decline at 1800 h | <0.005 |
| Juvenile [[Striped Parrotfish]]/[[Princess Parrotfish]] (*S. croicensis*/*S. taeniopterus*) | Extreme peak at 0900 h, lower stable abundance thereafter | <0.005 |

- **No clear "extreme-hours" pattern**: Contrary to the a priori hypothesis that significant variation would concentrate at the extreme sampling times (0900/1800 h, correlating with greater light-level change), the data showed no consistent pattern — variation occurred idiosyncratically across all time periods depending on species.
- **19 of 25 species** showed no statistically significant diurnal variation (N.S.), including *Holocentrus* spp., *Epinephelus/Mycteroperca* spp., *Serranus tigrinus*, *Hypoplectrus* spp., [[Fairy Basslet]] (*Gramma loreto*), [[Yellowtail Snapper]] (*Ocyurus chrysurus*), *Acanthurus sciurus*, *Haemulon plumieri*, *Chaetodon striatus*, *Microspathodon chrysurus*, *Halichoeres garnoti*, *Scarus vetula*, adult *S. croicensis-taeniopterus*, *Sparisoma chrysopterum*, *S. aurofrenatum*, *S. viride*, *Acanthurus coeruleus*, *A. bahianus*, and *Canthigaster rostrata*.
- **17 additional species** (Table 2) occurred too infrequently (<20 individuals total) for statistical testing, including *Lutjanus apodus*, *Holacanthus ciliaris*, *H. bermudensis*, *Pomacanthus arcuatus*, *P. paru*, *Bodianus rufus*, *Lachnolaimus maximus*, *Balistes vetula*, and others.
- **Proposed mechanism**: Significant variation is more likely attributable to changes in shelter-seeking/visibility behavior (fish retreating into reef holes/caves at certain times, possibly to conserve energy or avoid predation) rather than large-scale movement in/out of the sampling area — with the trumpetfish being a likely exception given its larger foraging range.
- **Methodological implication**: Because no single "best" time period or universal pattern of variation was identified, the authors recommend that repeated visual census studies of coral reef fish community structure/abundance be conducted at a **consistent, standardized time of day** to avoid introducing diurnal-behavior-driven bias into comparative or longitudinal datasets.
