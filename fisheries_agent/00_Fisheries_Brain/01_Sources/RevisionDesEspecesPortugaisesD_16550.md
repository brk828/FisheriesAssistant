# Revision of the Portuguese Species of the Genus Procris Fabricius (Zygaenidae, Lepidoptera)

#lepidoptera #zygaenidae #procris

## Executive Summary

This 1971 taxonomic revision by [[Teodoro Monteiro]], published by the [[Instituto De Zoologia Dr Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto), re-examines the burnet-moth genus *Procris* in Portugal. Because external characters such as colour, wing pattern and antennae are unreliable in this genus, the author relies on [[Genitalia Morphology]] to separate species. Of the four species previously recorded in Portugal, only *Procris notata* is retained as cited. Four species are confirmed for the country: [[Procris Notata]], [[Procris Hispanica]] (new to Portugal), [[Procris Jordani]] and [[Procris Schmidti]]. Several older records were shown to be misidentifications.

The document is a terrestrial entomology paper. It contains no aquatic or marine data. It is part of the Institute's publication series, which is listed at the end of the document.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Resolve long-standing confusion in the identification of Portuguese *Procris* and establish a reliable species list. An earlier version was presented at the XXIX Congresso Luso-Espanhol para o Progresso das Ciências (Lisbon, 31 March to 4 April 1970).
- **Diagnostic problem:** Colour ranges from pure blue to brown, or from deep green to straw yellow. Antennae mainly separate subgenera. Genitalia are described as "very constant and decisive" in both sexes.
- **Material examined:** 150 specimens from Portugal and about two dozen from other countries. These came from the [[Museu De Coimbra]] and the private collections of [[Silva Cruz]] and Fernando Mendes.
- **Historical records reviewed:** [[Candido Mendes]] (1903), who listed three species near S. Fiel; Silva Cruz and Wartison (1935), who added *P. geryon*; and the author's own 1956 report of *P. jordani* and *P. schmidti*.
- **Nomenclatural dispute:** The author sides with [[Burchard Alberti]] (1954) over [[Ramon Agenjo]] (1937, 1940). Agenjo had confused *P. cognata* H.-S. with *P. soror* Rambur and redescribed the species under a new name. The author accepts the separation of *P. notata* from *P. globulariae* first proposed by Kirby (1892).
- **Series context:** The bibliography at the front of the document lists roughly 111 numbered Institute publications (1940 to 1971). Most concern arachnids, isopods, amphipods, cave fauna and Lepidoptera. A few are aquatic or fisheries-related, including studies of Portuguese sardine (*Sardina pilchardus*) age and reproduction, a *Gadus morhua* parasite, bacterial gill disease in Europe, and iron salts in fish and oyster pathology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flight periods:** *P. notata* flies April to June. *P. jordani* flies April to May and shares many sun-exposed biotopes with *P. notata*. *P. hispanica* was collected in May and June.
- **Habitats:** Sunny meadows and slopes where broom (genêt) and knapweed (centaurées) grow. The author notes that *P. jordani* and *P. notata* largely coexist in these habitats.
- **Altitude and climate:** *P. schmidti* is known from two sites with contrasting conditions. Singeverga lies at about 400 m altitude, 30 km from the sea. Serra de Rebordões in north-east Trás-os-Montes has a continental climate.
- **Geographic patterns:**
  - *P. hispanica* is known in Portugal only from the north (Ucanha, Pinhão, and a doubtful record from Soalheira). It occurs in the Iberian Peninsula and southern France.
  - *P. notata* is widespread in south-west Europe, Germany, central and Balkan Europe, and Asian Turkey.
  - *P. jordani* is common across Spain except the northern provinces, and also occurs in southern Italy and Sicily.
  - *P. schmidti* is considered exclusively Iberian, first described from Ávila.
  - *P. tenuicornis* is confined to southern Italy and Sicily according to Agenjo and Alberti, so the Portuguese record was a misidentification.

## Key Findings

1. **Genitalia are the decisive character.** Male and female genitalia reliably separate species, while external features are not reliable. The author notes that errors in published illustrations led to misidentifications.
2. **Four species confirmed for Portugal:** *P. notata*, *P. hispanica* (new to Portugal), *P. jordani* and *P. schmidti*. An unnamed species was identified from a single Coimbra specimen with a distinctive aedeagus, and the author planned to describe it later.
3. **Older records revised:**
   - Of the four species cited by Candido Mendes, Silva Cruz and Wartison, only *P. notata* is retained.
   - The S. Fiel specimen labelled *P. tenuicornis* is actually *P. jordani*.
   - The Portuguese *P. statices* records are *P. jordani*, which had been mistaken for *P. statices* because of its robust, densely pectinated antennae.
4. **Species not confirmed for Portugal:** *P. statices* and *P. geryon* were not found in any Portuguese collection. The only *P. geryon* specimen was taken to England by Wartison. The author expects both to occur in Portugal given their presence in neighbouring Spain.
5. ***P. hispanica* identification:** Four specimens were confirmed: one from the Coimbra collection, one marked number 6 (also from Coimbra), two females from Ucanha near Lamego (May), and one from Pinhão in the Alto Douro (June). Its aedeagus is short and thick, with a distal acicular cornutus, distinguishing it from *P. notata*.
6. ***P. notata* details:** The species has the longest wingspan of any Portuguese *Procris*, about 30 mm in males and 20 to 22 mm in females. Its aedeagus is a long spine with a bulbous base.
7. ***P. schmidti* details:** One male is held by Franz Daniel (Munich). Two females were located, one in the author's collection and one in the Coimbra museum. The key female feature is a densely sclerotised, S-shaped ductus bursae. Records are from Singeverga and Serra de Rebordões.
8. **Foreign material:** Additional non-Portuguese specimens in the collections included *Theresimina ampelophaga*, *Procris anatelica*, *P. statices* (English) and *P. geryon*.
9. **Scope and limits:** The revision rests on a small Portuguese sample (150 specimens) and on comparisons with published figures. The author acknowledges that the material is limited and that some earlier identifications cannot be verified.
