# Biological Opinion on Arizona Trail Effects to Gila Topminnow in Redrock Canyon

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Poeciliidae #desert-fish #ESA-consultation

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service biological opinion, issued December 23, 1992, evaluates effects of the proposed [[Canelo Pass]] to [[Patagonia]] segment of the [[Arizona Trail]] on the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis) in [[Redrock Canyon]], [[Santa Cruz County]], Arizona. The opinion concludes the trail is "not likely to jeopardize the continued existence" of the species, but identifies substantial concerns about recreational impacts to one of only nine remaining natural populations in the United States. The analysis assumes prior implementation of the June 1990 [[Redrock Action Plan]], including livestock exclosures and grazing management changes. Without these protective measures in place, consultation must be reinitiated. The opinion mandates extensive monitoring, enforcement of no-camping zones, and annual interagency reviews to minimize incidental take through habitat modification.

## Background & Description

[[Gila Topminnow]] was listed as endangered on March 11, 1967. This small (one to two inches), livebearing fish of the family [[Poeciliidae]] historically occurred in the [[Gila River]], [[Sonora River]], and [[De La Concepcion River]] drainages in Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico. Once among the most common fishes in the Gila River system, the species has been extirpated throughout most of its range due to habitat destruction (water diversion, stream downcutting, channelization, impoundment) and competition/predation by non-native species, particularly [[Mosquitofish]] (Gambusia affinis). At present, only 9 naturally occurring populations exist in the United States, plus approximately 20 reintroduced populations.

[[Redrock Canyon]] supports one of only two relict natural populations on public lands. The population was discovered in the late 1960s and occupies three perennial reaches: the Falls area (below [[Redrock Ranch]]), the [[Gate Spring]] area, and the tributary below [[Cott Tank]]. The population expands into intermittent areas during periods of plentiful surface water. Despite the presence of mosquitofish since 1979 and other non-native species ([[Largemouth Bass]] Micropterus salmoides, [[Bluegill]] Lepomis macrochirus), the Redrock Canyon population has remained relatively large and healthy—a fact of particular conservation significance given that mosquitofish typically eliminates Gila topminnow within months at other sites.

The species is short-lived (approximately one year lifespan) with extremely high reproductive capacity. Individuals reproduce from early spring to early winter in non-thermal habitats and rapidly colonize new suitable habitat. This life history strategy enables exploitation of intermittent stream areas through boom-and-crash population dynamics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The environmental baseline in [[Redrock Canyon]] reflects extensive degradation from multiple human activities. Livestock grazing has reduced ground cover, increased soil erosion, degraded riparian vegetation, and altered stream channel morphology—resulting in faster runoff, higher flood intensities, lowered base flows, wider and shallower channels, reduced shade, and higher water temperatures. Stock tanks in upper watershed reaches have facilitated introduction of non-native fishes. Road development has caused streambank destruction and channel erosion, particularly in the [[Cott Tank]] drainage above [[Silver Tank]]. Historic and inactive mining has contributed to watershed instability.

The three perennial habitat areas differ in vulnerability:

**Cott Tank Drainage**: Supports a healthy, relatively stable population (38-100% of topminnow/mosquitofish community 1987-1992) despite presence of largemouth bass and mosquitofish. Will be protected by livestock exclosure under [[Redrock Action Plan]].

**Gate Spring**: Most vulnerable concentration. Small area with closely confined channel supporting consistent Gila topminnow presence but few mosquitofish. Population undergoes higher fluctuations due to channel instability. The only surface water in central Redrock Canyon, making it highly attractive to trail users. Rock art site nearby will draw visitors.

**Falls Area (below Redrock Ranch)**: Least stable population, fluctuating from very abundant to undetectable levels. Mosquitofish generally rare. Wide channel bottom with grassy benches makes area attractive for camping and stock corralling but less vulnerable to trampling damage.

Intermittent reaches between perennial areas provide important seasonal habitat. Gila topminnow commonly found up to one-half mile below downstream ends of Cott Tank and Falls areas, and a few hundred feet below Gate Spring. These areas support large populations during wet periods, providing genetic and ecological benefits.

The [[Redrock Action Plan]] (June 1990) prescribes livestock exclosures around all three perennial reaches, modified grazing management, and road closures. Implementation prior to trail opening is mandatory for this biological opinion's validity.

## Key Findings

**Project Description**: The proposed 12-mile trail segment would begin at Forest Development Road 799 near [[Canelo Pass]] and descend through Redrock Canyon, exiting below Redrock Ranch to join [[Harshaw Canyon]] road. Only 4 miles require new construction; remainder follows existing roads/tracks. Trail width: 18 inches to 3 feet. Projected use: 300-1,728 people per year (hikers, horses/packstock, non-motorized mountain bikes). Estimated cost: $26,500 construction, $8,500 annual maintenance.

**Alternative Analysis**: Twelve alternatives considered; nine carried through planning. The FWS strongly recommends **Alternative 8** (Lampshire Canyon route) as a conservation measure. This route would avoid Cott Tank drainage and Gate Spring concentration areas entirely, crossing Redrock Canyon only at the upper Falls exclosure. Alternative 8 was rejected by Forest Service based on: (1) less aesthetic appeal than Redrock Canyon proper, (2) greater new construction costs, (3) riparian impacts in Lampshire Canyon, and (4) expectation that recreation use will increase in Redrock Canyon regardless, making a designated trail preferable for management. The FWS considers these reasons insufficient to justify impacts to an endangered species.

**Protective Measures in Preferred Alternative**:
- No-camping zones at all three perennial areas and adjacent intermittent reaches
- Closure of specific areas to motorized vehicles and mountain bikes
- Livestock exclosures (walk-through gates for hikers)
- Closure of FR 4641 (locked gate) and FR 4642 to motorized use
- Valley bottom closure from [[Red Bank Well]] to [[Redrock Well]]
- Monitoring of streambank stability, riparian vegetation, ground cover, compliance, and use levels
- Traffic counter installation below Silver Tank

**Anticipated Effects**: Trail effects difficult to predict precisely, dependent on use levels, user types, behavior, and enforcement capability. The Canelo Pass-Patagonia segment offers multiple attractive features likely to drive use toward high end of estimates: riparian corridor with wildlife, nearly level terrain, multiple access points, and possibility of one-day/overnight mountain bike loops without paved roads.

**Direct and Indirect Impacts**:

*Gate Spring*: Expected to experience most pronounced effects. As only surface water in central canyon, highly attractive for rest, watering stock, and viewing rock art. Users likely to cut through exclosure rather than detour up dry hillside. Proposed side trail to rock art would lead directly into exclosure. Small, confined area most vulnerable to damage.

*Cott Tank Drainage*: Trail enters watershed via Down Under Tank, but existing road through drainage provides usable trail into exclosure from upstream. As riparian vegetation recovers, area may become increasingly attractive. Population relatively stable and healthy.

*Falls Area*: Trail crosses stream at upstream exclosure boundary. Users likely to enter exclosure for riparian enjoyment, shade, water, birdwatching, and viewing falls. Hillside benches north of exclosure suitable for camping, potentially popular for weekend users. Population least stable but area less vulnerable to trampling due to wider channel.

*Intermittent Reaches*: Downstream ends of Cott Tank and Gate Spring exclosures and upstream end of Falls exclosure would be affected. These areas provide extensive seasonal habitat during wet periods. No-camping zones at Cott Tank and Falls downstream/upstream ends would help minimize impacts.

**Recreation Impact Mechanisms**: Sedimentation, bank destabilization, channel morphology changes, riparian vegetation destruction and reproductive loss, soil compaction, pollution. Horse and packstock create more severe effects. Small fenced exclosures provide convenient corrals—intentional or accidental livestock use inside exclosures is common problem. Stock watering in stream could create substantial adverse effects similar to livestock grazing: bank erosion, vegetation destruction, increased sedimentation, altered channel morphology, changed water chemistry/temperature, decreased habitat complexity.

**Enforcement Concerns**: Proposed action provides no enforcement mechanism and no active Forest Service presence. Prohibited activities (camping in no-camping zones, corralling/watering stock in exclosures) would be difficult to prevent. Anticipated uses (trailing through Cott Tank exclosure, using Gate Spring exclosure as through-route) not prohibited but could cause serious impacts at high levels. Federal budget constraints suggest minimal patrols and limited enforcement.

**Fire Risk**: Livestock exclusion increases ground cover and understory vegetation, raising wildfire probability and intensity. Many woody riparian species (e.g., cottonwood) have low fire tolerance. Overnight recreational use substantially increases fire risk. A fire in any perennial reach riparian zone could have serious adverse impacts to Gila topminnow, though little information exists on fire effects to desert fish.

**Cumulative Effects**: Few non-Federal actions in watershed. Two private parcels: (1) Cott Tank area—cattle grazing, water development, fishing; adverse impacts from non-native fish introductions continue; (2) Redrock Ranch—livestock grazing, house; owners visit daily; grazing impacts stream and downstream Falls population. Multiple monitoring programs (AGFD, Fall Fish Count, proposed action, Redrock Action Plan) require coordination to avoid unnecessary harassment and habitat damage.

**Non-Jeopardy Conclusion**: The opinion concludes the trail "is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of Gila topminnow" but acknowledges anticipated improvement from Redrock Action Plan would be offset by trail impacts. This may not threaten short-term survival but could impede recovery and long-term survival.

**Incidental Take Statement**: Take cannot be quantified in numbers (population estimates unobtainable; rapid population changes inherent to species) or habitat damage (improvement from exclosures makes measuring loss of improvement impossible). Take assumed **exceeded** if any of five conditions occur:

1. Recreation-attributable fire in riparian zone
2. Corralling of horses/packstock in any exclosure OR persistent evidence of livestock grazing in exclosures
3. Distinct user trail (bare soil) develops along Gate Spring exclosure stream bottom
4. Evidence of persistent no-camping violation
5. Annual interagency review finds cumulative habitat modification/degradation exceeds anticipated levels

If take exceeded, Forest Service must immediately reinitiate consultation to avoid Section 9 violation. If additional take would cause irreversible adverse impact, trail use must be suspended pending new consultation.

**Mandatory Terms and Conditions** (implementing four Reasonable and Prudent Measures):

1. Minimize direct mortalities: (1.1) minimize stream channel disturbance during construction/maintenance; (1.2) ensure no pollutants enter stream channels

2. Minimize habitat modification: (2.1) actively enforce no-camping restrictions; (2.2) inspect and maintain all three exclosures at least twice yearly; (2.3) conduct annual interagency review (FWS, AGFD, Forest Service biologists) of trail effects on riparian corridor, stream channel, and Gila topminnow; written report within 3 months

3. Maintain records: (3.1) written record of construction (plans, maps, specifications, changes); (3.2) comprehensive record of use and maintenance (traffic counter data, use information, maintenance records, violation records, livestock observations, other pertinent information); (3.3) send records and summary to FWS and AGFD annually

4. Monitor: (4.1) monitor effects on riparian/aquatic habitats and Gila topminnow; coordinate with existing monitoring programs; develop protocols with FWS/AGFD approval; (4.2) initiate monitoring at least 6 months before trail opening for baseline data; (4.3) continue monitoring as long as trail in active use; (4.4) submit annual monitoring reports to FWS and AGFD

**Critical Assumption**: Analysis validity requires completion of Redrock Action Plan implementation (grazing management, road closures, four livestock exclosures) **prior to opening** Canelo Pass-Patagonia trail segment. Failure to complete these actions or inability to maintain management/structures would alter trail effects, constitute new information, and **require reinitiation** of formal Section 7 consultation.

**Species Co-occurrence with Mosquitofish**: The coexistence of Gila topminnow and mosquitofish in Redrock Canyon is of particular conservation significance. This situation is unusual—mosquitofish typically eliminates topminnow within months. The Redrock population may hold information vital to species survival and recovery throughout its range. Federal ownership provides greater protection through Section 7 and allows more recovery action opportunities. New activities adversely affecting Gila topminnow could seriously compromise species recovery.

**Other Non-Native Species**: [[Largemouth Bass]] present in Cott Tank drainage, feeds on smaller fish including Gila topminnow; pools with bass generally devoid of other fish. [[Bluegill]] taken in canyon. Anecdotal reports of "trout" and "goldfish." Other tanks may harbor additional non-native species but have not been sampled.

**Native Fish Community**: [[Longfin Dace]] (Agosia chrysogaster) also present in Falls area, showing marked population fluctuations apparently due to stream channel instability.
