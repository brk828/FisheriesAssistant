# Revision of the Eastern Pacific Clinid Genus Labrisomus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1953 taxonomic revision by Clark Hubbs (University of Texas), reprinted from *Zoologica* (New York Zoological Society, Vol. 38, Part 3), refines the eastern Pacific species of the [[Labrisomus]] genus of [[Clinidae]] blennies. Hubbs recognizes eight species, grouped into three subgenera: *Brockius* (new), *Odontoclinus*, and *Labrisomus*. The paper provides diagnostic keys, meristic and morphometric descriptions, notes on nongenetic variation, postlarval evidence of pelagic dispersal, and a zoogeographic account of the genus, including its Atlantic and African distribution. Atlantic species are reserved for a later paper.

## Background & Description

- **Scope and prior work:** Builds on Hubbs (1952), which proposed a partial revision of the genus and its placement within the subfamily Labrisominae. Hubbs places [[Labrisomus]] and [[Malacoctenus]] in the subtribe Labrisomini, within tribe Labrisomidi.
- **Methods:** Measurements and counts follow [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1947), with counts given as extreme range followed by typical range in parentheses. Specimens were examined from several collections, including [[California Academy of Sciences]], [[Stanford Natural History Museum]], [[United States National Museum]], [[University of California at Los Angeles]], and [[Texas Natural History Collection]].
- **Nomenclature:** [[Swainson]] (1839) erected *Labrisomus* with *Clinus pectinifer* as type. Of the 12 species Swainson included, only two remain in the genus. Hubbs assigns the others to genera such as *Malacoctenus*, *Auchenionchus*, *Calliclinus*, *Clinus*, *Blennioclinus*, *Tripterygion*, and *Acanthoclinus*. Synonymies for *Lepisoma* (DeKay, 1842), *Ericteis* (Jordan, 1904), and *Odontoclinus* (Reid, 1935) are discussed, and a new name, *Brockius*, is proposed for the third subgenus because *Ericteis* was not available for it.
- **Generic description:** Dorsal fin XVII–XX spines and 10–13 soft rays; anal II spines with 16–20 rays; lateral line 42–74 scales; three pairs of head cirri (nuchal, supra-orbital, narial); six branchiostegal rays; pelvics I,3. Teeth form an outer row of enlarged conical teeth with an inner band of smaller teeth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** All species with adequate data occur on rocky bottoms. All Pacific species except *L. striatus* are intertidal.
- **Range:** In the eastern Pacific, the genus extends from the northern Gulf of California and Sebastian Vizcaino Bay to Coquimbo, Chile. On the Atlantic side, it ranges from near Rio de Janeiro to the southern United States, Bermuda, and West African coasts (Dakar to Annobón), plus the Canaries.
- **Biogeography:** Hubbs proposes that labrisomines arose in tropical American seas, with *Labrisomus* widespread before the Pliocene rise of the Isthmus of Panama. He argues against Steinitz (1950) that the African species is a Tethyan relict, favoring random dispersal on the equatorial counter current. Only *L. philippii* (northern Peruvian–North Chilean region) and *L. canariensis* occur outside the tropics.
- **Abundance patterns:** Collecting was intensive in Baja California, the Gulf of California, the Galapagos, Costa Rica–Panama, and Peru, but sparse elsewhere. Abundance is concentrated at tropical margins; Meek & Hildebrand (1928) recorded only three Pacific specimens of *Labrisomus* against 78 of *Paraclinus monophthalmus*.
- **Gulf of California partitioning:** Along the mainland coast, *L. multiporosus* and *L. xantusi* show a consistent abundance pattern north of Mazatlan, with a notable shift south of Punta Pefiasco where a temperature change occurs. *L. multiporosus* is most common near Mazatlan (54 of 65 specimens).
- **Postlarval dispersal:** Pelagic postlarvae are frequently taken under night lights in the eastern tropical Pacific. The *L. dendriticus* postlarvae from the Galapagos (92 of 99 specimens, collected April 15–June 9) indicate a long pelagic stage, which Hubbs links to labrisomine presence far from the genus's center of origin.

## Key Findings

- **Species count and arrangement:** Eight eastern Pacific species: *striatus* (new), *wigginsi* (new), *dendriticus*, *jenkinsi*, *philippii*, *soccorroensis* (new), *xantusi*, and *multiporosus* (new). They are placed in *Brockius* (*striatus*), *Odontoclinus* (*wigginsi*, *dendriticus*), and *Labrisomus* (the other five).
- **Synonymy:** *Labrisomus fortidentatus* (Cope) is probably a synonym of *philippii*. Several other former Labrisomus names are transferred to other genera.
- **Key diagnostic characters:** Lateral line scale counts, cheek and opercular scale patterns, first dorsal spine length, pectoral ray number, and cirri lengths. *L. philippii* has the most lateral line scales (68–74) and the shortest cirri; *L. dendriticus* has enlarged canines behind the jaw teeth and 13 pectoral rays.
- **Nongenetic variation:** Proportional lengths change with age in all species examined, including a marked increase in relative anterior body depth. Color pattern varies with habitat; intermediate phases are documented in *xantusi*, *multiporosus*, and *nuchipinnis*.
- **Distribution of new species:**
  - *L. striatus* (Brockius): fewer than 45 lateral line scales; striped color; known from Cape San Lucas, Las Tres Marias, and Acapulco (holotype SU 17560).
  - *L. wigginsi*: known from tidepools on the outer coast of Baja California between Arroyo Seco and Abreojos Point (holotype SU 17562).
  - *L. soccorroensis*: known only from Soccorro Island (holotype CAS 1762).
  - *L. multiporosus*: Turtle Bay, Baja California, to Lobos de Afuera, Peru; holotype from Acapulco Harbor (SU 17553); 261 paratypes examined.
- **Distribution of established species:** *L. dendriticus* is endemic to the Galapagos. *L. jenkinsi* is known from the Galapagos. *L. philippii* ranges in Peru (Lobos de Tierra Bay to Independencia Bay), with Chile reported by Delfin (1901). *L. xantusi* ranges from Sebastian Vizcaino Bay to Cape San Lucas, the Gulf of California to Mazatlan, and islands in both areas (556 other specimens examined).
- **Postlarval evidence:** *L. dendriticus* likely spawns annually in March, with pelagic postlarvae present mid-April to early June. Postlarval stages of other clinid genera (*Gibbonsia*, *Cryptotrema*, *Alloclinus*, *Malacoctenus*) are believed to be shorter.
- **Statistical note:** Panamanian, Ecuadorian, Peruvian, and Galapagos specimens of *L. multiporosus* average slightly more dorsal and anal soft rays (29.50) than Mexican specimens (29.05), giving t = 2.47 (122 df, p between 0.02 and 0.01). Hubbs judges this marginal and insufficient for a separate species, since no other character separated the groups.
- **Subspecies conclusion:** The "two undescribed species" from Bahia Honda, Panama, are now considered geographic races of *L. multiporosus*. The Panamanian race is probably not subspecifically distinct from the Mexican.
- **Name correction:** The International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature's 1953 decision means *xantusi* is retained in its revised spelling, with *xanti* as the original.
