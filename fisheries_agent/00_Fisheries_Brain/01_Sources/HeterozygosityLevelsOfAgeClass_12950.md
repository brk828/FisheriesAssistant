# Heterozygosity Levels of 1950 Age Class and Younger Cui-ui Based on Electrophoretic Analysis

## Executive Summary
This USFWS-funded study examined allozyme heterozygosity in [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered catostomid fish endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. Using starch-gel electrophoresis, researchers compared genetic variability between the pre-bottleneck 1950 age class and post-bottleneck 1969/1977 age classes to assess the genetic impact of a documented 19-year reproductive hiatus (1950–1969) in the population. While heterozygosity estimates were numerically higher in the older cohort (H = 0.0081 vs. H = 0.0045), the difference was not statistically significant when combined with a larger comparative dataset. However, the older cohort retained unique alleles absent from younger fish, supporting recommendations to prioritize spawning of pre-bottleneck individuals to preserve residual genetic diversity.

## Background & Description
This study is genetic/biochemical rather than morphological in focus; no external morphometric data were reported. Analysis relied on **tissue-level biochemical markers** rather than gross morphology:

- **Tissues sampled:** eye (E), liver (L), and muscle (M)
- **Sample size:** 13 individual fish total
  - 9 individuals from the 1950 cohort
  - 3 individuals from the 1969 cohort
  - 1 individual from the 1977 cohort
- **Storage:** tissues preserved at -80°C prior to analysis
- **Enzyme systems assayed:** 55 presumptive allozyme loci across 26 enzyme systems (see [[Table 1 - Enzyme Systems]]), including:
  - [[Aspartate Aminotransferase]] (Aat)
  - [[Isocitrate Dehydrogenase]] (Idh) — key polymorphic locus
  - [[Glucose Phosphate Isomerase]] (Gpi) — polymorphic in younger fish
  - [[Malate Dehydrogenase]] (Mdh) — polymorphic in younger fish
  - [[Lactate Dehydrogenase]] (Ldh)
  - Additional loci: Ada, Akp, Lap, Ck, Est, Fdp, Gam, Gp, Gr, Hbdh, Sdh, Me, Mdh (mannitol), Mup, Mpi, Pep (A,C,B,D), Pgd, Pgm, Sod, Xdh

- **Electrophoretic buffer systems used:**
  1. **CAM** — amine citrate buffer, pH 6.8 (Clayton & Tretiak 1972)
  2. **R** — discontinuous buffer, pH 6.8 (Ridgeway et al. 1970)
  3. **TC** — Tris-citrate buffer, pH 5.8 (Schaal & Anderson 1974)
  4. **TG** — Tris-glycine buffer, pH 8.5 (Holmes & Masters 1970)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Pyramid Lake]] is a terminal (endorheic) desert lake in the Great Basin of Nevada, remnant of the Pleistocene-era pluvial [[Lake Lahontan]] system (~8,000 years since the end of the pluvial period).
- **Life history context:** Cui-ui are long-lived lake suckers with delayed and episodic recruitment; the species experienced **no successful reproduction between 1950 and 1969**, representing a severe 19-year genetic bottleneck.
- **Population genetics driver:** Genetically effective population size (*Ne*) is critical to maintaining heterozygosity; heterozygosity is lost at a rate of 1/(2*Ne*) per generation (Hartl & Clark 1989). Factors reducing *Ne* in this population likely include:
  - The prolonged breeding hiatus (bottleneck)
  - High variance in reproductive success among breeding individuals (e.g., few successfully breeding males post-bottleneck)
  - Variance in family size
- **Conservation context:** The species is managed via the [[Koch Cui-ui Hatchery]], with implications for broodstock selection and genetic diversity preservation in captive breeding programs.
- **Comparative context:** Cui-ui exhibit notably low allozyme heterozygosity relative to other fish species (see [[Longnose Dace]] comparative data, Merritt et al. 1978), likely a historical artifact of repeated bottlenecks since the pluvial period plus anthropogenic impacts on spawning habitat.

## Key Findings
- **Polymorphic loci:** Only 2 of 55 assayed loci (**Idh-1,2**) were polymorphic in this study's sample; when pooled with Brussard et al. (1990) data, 4 loci were polymorphic in younger fish (**Idh-1,2, Gpi-4, Mdh-3**) versus 2 in older fish (**Idh-1,2**).
- **Heterozygosity estimates:**
  - 1950 cohort (older, pre-bottleneck): **H = 0.0081** (N = 9)
  - 1969/1977 cohorts (younger, post-bottleneck): **H = 0.0045** (N = 4, pooled)
- **Statistical significance:** No significant difference between age classes in number of polymorphic loci (G-test, p > 0.1, df = 1) or heterozygosity (G-test, p > 0.5, df = 1) when combined with Brussard et al. (1990) dataset (total N = 35 younger fish).
- **Unique/rare alleles in older (1950) cohort not found in younger fish:**
  - **Idh-2 "B" allele** (frequency = 0.056)
  - **Idh-1 "D" allele** — common in older fish (frequency = 0.278) but rare in younger fish (frequency = 0.014)
- **Unique/rare alleles found only in younger cohorts (absent in 1950 class):**
  - Gpi-4: alleles "87" and "74"
  - Idh-2: allele "100"
  - Mdh-3: allele "122"
  - All these rare alleles were detected in ≤2 heterozygous individuals among 35 combined younger fish.

- **Management implications:**
  - Despite lack of statistical significance, the presence of unique alleles in the 1950 cohort justifies prioritizing this age class (and older fish, if available) for hatchery spawning at the [[Koch Cui-ui Hatchery]].
  - Recommend spawning **all age classes in equal numbers** with **equal male/female genetic contribution** to maximize effective population size (*Ne*) and minimize further heterozygosity loss.
  - Low allozyme heterozygosity does not necessarily indicate low overall (quantitative/polygenic) genetic variation — quantitative trait variation is regained faster post-bottleneck than single-locus allozyme variation (Hedrick & Miller 1992).
- **Broader significance:** This work reinforces [[Brussard et al. 1990]] findings of historically low allozyme diversity in cui-ui, attributed to repeated bottlenecks since the drying of pluvial [[Lake Lahontan]] and compounded by modern anthropogenic disruption of spawning habitat.
