# Descriptions of new species of Fishes collected in Texas, New Mexico and Sonora

#ichthyology #marine-biology #taxonomy #percoidei #siluridae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1854 excerpt from the *Proceedings of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia* contains the second part of a paper by [[Spencer Fullerton Baird]] and [[Charles Girard]] describing new fish species. The fishes were collected by [[John H. Clark]] and [[Captain Stewart Van Vliet]] during the [[United States-Mexico Boundary Survey]]. The excerpt covers species of Percoidei, the Labridae heading (containing a cichlid-like genus), Siluridae, Characidae and Cyprinidae. It also includes a short, non-fish paper by [[Timothy Abbott Conrad]] on rectifying generic names of Tertiary fossil bivalves. The fish descriptions are morphological: fin-ray formulae, scale counts, body proportions and preserved colour.

## Background & Description

The paper is a taxonomic monograph in the style of the mid-nineteenth century. Each entry gives a Latin binomial with authorship, a diagnosis, a meristic formula (dorsal, anal, caudal, pectoral and ventral rays, and lateral-line scale counts), a colour description in alcohol, and a type locality with collector. The authors note in a footnote that the western Texas and Gila-drained species were collected while the Boundary Survey was under Col. J. D. Graham, and the others under Major W. H. Emory.

The percoid entries were placed in the genus [[Pomotis]], a sunfish-group name of the time. Several are re-described from earlier names. For example, *Pomotis longulus* is re-treated as *Bairdiella longulus* [OCR-uncertain] and cited with reference to earlier Philadelphia Academy proceedings. Authors used the same morphological characters across taxa, including the head-to-body length ratio, eye diameter as a fraction of head length, and the position of the maxillary tip relative to the orbit.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Localities are almost entirely inland Texas rivers and creeks, with additional material from the Gila watershed in New Mexico and Sonora. Named sites include:

- **Brownsville, Texas**: *Pomotis speciosus* and *Herichthys oxanocutatus* (freshwater).
- **Elm Creek, Texas**: *Pomotis fallax*.
- **Rio Cibolo** (tributary of Rio San Antonio), **Rio Salado** and **Rio Frio / Rio Nueces**: several *Pomotis* and *Gaystes* species.
- **Indianola, Texas**: *Ailurichthys marinus*, *Arius equestris*. This is the only record suggesting a marine or estuarine association.
- **Rio Grande del Norte**: *Pimelodus affinis*.
- **Gila drainage (Rio Santa Cruz, Rio Mimbres, Rio San Pedro)**: *Carostomus* and *Gila* species.

The Gila-system records show a fauna distinct from the Texas Gulf-slope rivers, which the authors use as a diagnostic basis for several species.

## Key Findings

**New percoid species (Pomotis and related genera):**
- *P. speciosus*: lateral-line scales 43; ground colour brownish with a narrow blackish lateral vitta; collected at Brownsville.
- *P. fallax*: lateral-line scales 39; dorsal formula D X.11 A III.9 C 3.8.7.1.2 V 6 P 14 [OCR-uncertain]; deep blackish brown above, with a very large black opercular flap.
- *P. convexifrons*: lateral-line scales 37; uniform reddish brown, Rio Cibolo.
- *P. nerastus*: lateral-line scales 45; uniform reddish brown, Rio Cibolo and Rio Salado.
- *P. enos*: lateral-line count not reported; uniform blackish brown, Rio Cibolo.
- *P. longulus* (re-described): reddish brown with greyish punctuation; Rio Cibolo.
- *Gaystes wugcensis* [OCR-uncertain]: mouth deeply cleft, head about four-thirteenths of total length; Rio Frio and Rio Nueces.

**Labridae heading, *Herichthys oxanocutatus*:** Five or six spiny anal rays; 18 lateral-line scales plus 8 on the peduncle; 19–20 scale rows across greatest depth; brownish with bluish spots; Brownsville. The authors note the genus resembles *Heros* of Heckel but differs in the simple anterior maxillary teeth.

**Siluridae:**
- *Ailurichthys marinus* (synonyms included *Galeichthys marinus*): Indianola.
- *Arius equestris*: head about 4¾ times in total length; Indianola.
- *Pimelodus affinis*: close to *P. aeneus* (printed as "ewrulescens" [OCR-uncertain]); reddish brown above, silvery beneath; Rio Grande del Norte.

**Characidae:** *Astyanax argentatus* (OCR: "agcentatus"): dorsal D II.10, anal A II.20+1 [OCR-uncertain], back deep reddish brown, black caudal-base spot; upper Rio Nueces.

**Cyprinidae (*Carostomus*, *Candiopes*, *Gila*):**
- *C. congestus*: lateral line about 46 scales; 14 scale rows; reddish brown above; Rio Salado.
- *C. cinctus* [OCR-uncertain]: 68–70 lateral-line scales; Rio Santa Cruz (Gila).
- *C. pleserus*: about 80 lateral-line scales; dark brown with orange traces on sides in some specimens; Rio Mimbres.
- *C. insignis*: about 60 lateral-line scales, black dot at each scale base; Rio San Pedro (Gila).
- *Candiopes tumidus* [OCR-uncertain]: 37–38 lateral-line scales; Fort Brown, Texas.
- *Gila arenosa* and *G. pulchella*: Gila tributaries; the latter with two spine rudiments on the dorsal margin.

In total, the excerpt describes roughly 17 new taxa. Only the specific diagnostic values are reported here. Full meristic formulae appear in the source.

**Non-fish content (Conrad):** T. A. Conrad rectifies generic names for Tertiary fossil bivalves. He applies the earliest valid authority, with the aid of more numerous reference works. Examples include *Glycimeris* Lamarck, *Mytilus* Linnaeus, *Nucula* Lamarck, *Mercenaria* Schumacher and *Venus* Linnaeus. Long synonym lists for *Leda*, *Mercenaria* and *Meretrix* are given and not reproduced here. The section is clearly part of a different publication and is included only for completeness.
