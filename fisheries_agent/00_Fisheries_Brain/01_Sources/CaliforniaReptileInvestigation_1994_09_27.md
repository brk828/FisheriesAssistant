## Key Findings
- A year-long undercover investigation by California Fish and Game targeted commercial reptile breeders in southern California.
- **25 defendants** were identified; 8 held commercial breeding permits, 2 were in the permitting process.
- **750 reptiles seized total**: 350 [[California Rosy Boa]] and 350 [[California Kingsnake]].
- Violations included: selling snakes exceeding legal length limits, laundering wild-caught native snakes as captive-bred commercial stock, and inadequate record-keeping.
- California's enforcement section recommended **eliminating the commercial breeder permit system** to its Commission due to systemic abuse.
- A companion letter (Wayne Johnston, CA Dept. of Fish and Game, to Beth Woodin, Chair of Arizona Game and Fish Commission) warns Arizona against adopting a similar captive-breeding/sale authorization, citing California's 1992 regulation as an **"enforcement nightmare"** that increased wild reptile seizures.
- A key regulatory gap identified: California's 1992 rules **did not authorize warden inspections of breeding facilities or animals**, crippling enforcement. The letter recommends Arizona explicitly include **inspection authority** for enforcement officers if it adopts a similar proposal.
