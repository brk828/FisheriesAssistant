# Migrations of Striped Bass Occurring in Tomales Bay

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Moronidae #striped-bass #migration-patterns #tagging-study

## Executive Summary

This tagging study (1963-1966) investigated the migration patterns of [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*) in [[Tomales Bay]], California, located 40 miles northwest of [[San Francisco]]. A total of 207 striped bass were tagged using disk-dangler tags, with 65 tag returns received by July 1968. Results demonstrate that [[Tomales Bay]] striped bass are part of the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] system population, participating in spawning migrations to the Delta. The study revealed seasonal absence from [[Tomales Bay]] during spring/early summer (April-June) corresponding to upstream spawning migrations, with fish returning in fall and winter. The predominance of larger, mature fish (mean 24 inches FL) and scarcity of small bass suggest no local spawning occurs in [[Tomales Bay]].

## Background & Description

[[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*) presence in [[Tomales Bay]] has fluctuated historically. The author's personal experience indicates very few striped bass were caught between 1952 and 1958, though a commercial fisherman accidentally caught 7 tons in spring 1946. [[Tomales Bay]] extends 10 miles southwest from its Pacific Coast entrance and is less than a mile wide in most places. Striped bass are caught in shallow waters near Inverness at the bay's south end.

Between December 1963 and May 1966, researchers tagged 207 striped bass: 94 caught on sport tackle and 113 caught in gill nets (December 1964 and February 1965). All fish received disk-dangler tags using methods described by Chadwick (1963). Tagged fish ranged from 10.5 to 41 inches fork length (FL), with a mean length of 24 inches, indicating most were mature individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Tomales Bay]] serves as a non-spawning habitat for mature [[Striped Bass]] originating from the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] system. The bay functions as part of a larger migratory circuit connecting the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]], [[San Francisco Bay]], and Pacific coastal waters.

Historical patterns show increased migrations into [[San Francisco Bay]] and the Pacific Ocean during the late 1950s and early 1960s compared to the early 1950s, with substantial fisheries existing in [[San Francisco Bay]] from 1938-1943 (Chadwick 1967). This broader pattern correlates with striped bass presence in [[Tomales Bay]] before 1952 and after 1958.

Seasonal patterns compiled from newspaper reports, game warden records, and personal fishing records (October 1958-December 1966) revealed a consistent 2-month absence period during spring or early summer (1960-1965), corresponding to spawning season in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. Bass were caught every fall and winter from 1961-1966.

## Key Findings

**Tag Return Summary:**
- 65 tag returns received as of July 1968 from 207 tagged fish (31.4% return rate)
- 46 fish (70.7%) recaptured in [[Tomales Bay]]
- 15 fish (23.0%) recaptured in [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] system and [[San Francisco Bay]]
- 4 fish (6.3%) caught in ocean near [[Pacifica]]

**Migration Patterns:**
- Eight bass (nearly half of outside recaptures) were recaptured in [[San Pablo Bay]] or upstream during April-May of different years, demonstrating participation in [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] spawning migration
- Four striped bass tagged in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] system (springs of 1958, 1960, 1961, 1966) were recaptured in [[Tomales Bay]], confirming bidirectional movement
- 47% of fish recaptured outside [[Tomales Bay]] were caught in April, May, and June versus only 16% of [[Tomales Bay]] recaptures during these months

**Residence Patterns in Tomales Bay:**
- Approximately 20% of [[Tomales Bay]] recaptures occurred before first spring season
- 50% recaptured between first and second spring
- 30% recaptured between second and third spring
- One fish recaptured after third spring
- Pattern suggests fish were either resident or returned after spawning migrations

**Population Characteristics:**
- Mean fork length: 24 inches (range 10.5-41 inches)
- Majority were mature fish
- Predominance of larger fish indicates population consists of size classes most likely to migrate from [[Sacramento-San Joaquin]] system to ocean
- Scarcity of small bass suggests no spawning occurs in [[Tomales Bay]]

**Management Implications:**
The study demonstrates that [[Striped Bass]] fishing success in [[Tomales Bay]] is largely controlled by migrations from other estuaries, particularly the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] system. Factors controlling migrations out of the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin]] system are presumably the most important determinant of [[Tomales Bay]] striped bass abundance.
