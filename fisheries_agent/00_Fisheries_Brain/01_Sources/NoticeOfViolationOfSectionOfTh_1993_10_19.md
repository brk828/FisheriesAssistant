## Executive Summary
This document is a legal 60-day notice letter, not a primary scientific study, sent by attorney Matthew Kenna on behalf of the [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]], the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]], Peter Galvin, and Dr. Robin Silver. It alleges that the U.S. Bureau of Land Management ([[BLM]]) violated Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) by failing to adequately assess impacts of the proposed [[Sanchez Copper Project]] on the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally listed species. While it contains minimal direct biological data, it provides important regulatory-context information regarding the species' known occurrence near the project area and documents a procedural failure in required habitat/species-impact analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species of concern**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large-bodied catostomid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Habitat association**: The Final Environmental Impact Statement (FEIS) for the Sanchez Copper Project acknowledges the species occurs "upstream" from the project and within the broader "project area" or vicinity, implying proximity to riverine habitat potentially impacted by mining operations.
- **Threats implied**: Potential habitat degradation or hydrological/water quality alteration from the proposed copper mining project, though the FEIS does not quantify or clearly analyze these effects.
- **Regulatory status**: The species is protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]], requiring federal action agencies (here, the [[Bureau of Land Management]]) to ensure any authorized action does not jeopardize its continued existence or critical habitat.

## Key Findings
- The FEIS for the [[Sanchez Copper Project]] devotes **less than one paragraph** to the [[Razorback Sucker]], with no substantive analysis of potential project effects on the species or its habitat.
- The FEIS fails to make a definitive "may affect" or "no effect" determination, which is a required first step under ESA Section 7 consultation procedures (50 C.F.R. Part 402).
- The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) explicitly disagreed with any implied "no effect" finding, indicating a substantive concern that the project could affect the species.
- Per 50 C.F.R. § 402.14(a), disagreement by FWS obligates BLM to prepare a **Biological Assessment (BA)** and initiate consultation with FWS.
- No Biological Assessment or Biological Opinion (BO) had been prepared at the time of this notice, constituting a procedural violation of ESA Section 7.
- The letter demands remedial action within 60 days: (1) preparation of a BA assessing project effects on the [[Razorback Sucker]] and its habitat, submitted to FWS for concurrence; and (2) if adverse effects are likely, initiation of formal consultation leading to a Biological Opinion.
- This case illustrates a broader pattern of *procedural* (rather than purely biological) documentation in ESA compliance disputes — the scientific substance regarding the species' population status, distribution, or specific vulnerability to copper mining impacts is notably absent from the underlying FEIS as characterized in this letter.
