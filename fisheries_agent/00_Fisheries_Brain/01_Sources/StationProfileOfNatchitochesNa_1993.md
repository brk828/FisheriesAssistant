## Executive Summary

The **Natchitoches National Fish Hatchery** is the sole [[National Fish Hatchery]] in Louisiana, established under the 1930 White Act (46 Stat. 371). Operational since 1934, the facility spans 98 acres with 45.76 acres of water across 54 ponds, drawing its water supply from [[Cane River Lake]] via pumps capable of 3,358 gallons per minute. The hatchery propagates up to 13 fish species and maintains 7 broodfish species, serving five core mandates: species **restoration** (anadromous and riverine species depleted in the [[Gulf of Mexico]] and interjurisdictional waters), **mitigation** for federal water development and TVA projects, support of **federal lands fisheries** management, and a **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)** with the State of Louisiana for interstate fry/fingerling exchange. Distribution records (1990–1992) show a steadily increasing annual output, dominated by MOU-driven striped bass fry transfers, despite fluctuating federal funding and staffing levels.

## Background & Description

This profile is administrative/operational rather than morphometric; no direct morphological descriptions of the propagated species are provided in the source text. Species under cultivation, however, represent a broad taxonomic range relevant to hatchery propagation biology:

- **[[Striped Bass]]** (*Morone saxatilis*) — anadromous [[Moronidae]] species, reared through Phase I (early juvenile) stage.
- **[[Paddlefish]]** (*Polyodon spathula*) — primitive [[Polyodontidae]] species with cartilaginous skeleton and elongate rostrum, used for restoration and TVA mitigation stocking.
- **[[Pallid Sturgeon]]** (*Scaphirhynchus albus*) — endangered [[Acipenseridae]] species, propagated for Missouri/Mississippi drainage restoration and mitigation.
- **[[Blue Catfish]]** and **[[Flathead Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus furcatus*, *Pylodictis olivaris*) — large-bodied [[Ictaluridae]] species used in federal water mitigation and fishery management.
- **[[Channel Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — widely propagated Ictaluridae species for federal lands stocking.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*), **[[Bluegill]]**, and **[[Redear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*, *Lepomis microlophus*) — [[Centrarchidae]] species reared for federal and state water management (Bluegill/Largemouth Bass at Phase I stage).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Anadromous restoration role**: Striped bass are propagated specifically to restore depleted anadromous populations historically present in the [[Gulf of Mexico]], linking freshwater hatchery production to coastal marine recruitment.
- **Interjurisdictional riverine restoration**: Paddlefish and pallid sturgeon are cultured to replenish populations in shared, interjurisdictional waters of the southeastern United States, particularly the Missouri/Mississippi drainage system — reflecting their large-river, benthic/pelagic habitat requirements.
- **Reservoir mitigation habitat**: Paddlefish stocking supports [[Tennessee Valley Authority]] impounded waters, while blue catfish stocking mitigates habitat loss from federal water development projects in Missouri and Tennessee.
- **Federal lands fisheries**: Flathead catfish, bluegill, redear sunfish, largemouth bass, and channel catfish support recreational and ecological fishery management across federal lands in Alabama, Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Tennessee — indicating warmwater lentic/lotic pond and reservoir habitats.
- **Interstate cooperative ecology management**: Under the 1990 MOU, the State of Louisiana supplies 11–14 million striped bass fry annually to Natchitoches NFH for redistribution to Service Regions 2 and 4, illustrating a coordinated multi-state anadromous species management network. In exchange, Natchitoches supplies largemouth bass and blue/flathead catfish back to Louisiana state waters.

## Key Findings

- **Historical establishment**: Land acquisition completed May 21, 1930; first broodfish collected September 14, 1931; full operation achieved by 1934 on 98 acres.
- **Current infrastructure**: 45.76 acres of water, 54 ponds, water sourced from Cane River Lake (max pumping 3,358 GPM); supporting infrastructure includes an aquarium, holding house, pump house, and residences.
- **Propagation capacity**: Up to 13 species can be propagated; 7 broodfish species actively maintained.
- **Five-part station mission**: (1) Restoration, (2) Mitigation, (3) Federal Waters management, (4) Interstate MOU exchange, (5) private/research distribution.
- **Distribution trends (FY1990–1992)**:
  - FY1990: 1,401,945 total fish distributed (no striped bass fry actually shipped that year despite coordination role).
  - FY1991: 2,576,318 total fish distributed, sharp increase driven by MOU striped bass fry (2,135,129).
  - FY1992: 3,108,728 total fish distributed, continued MOU dominance (1,472,462) alongside a rise in state waters distribution (1,449,956).
- **Program discontinuation impact**: Cessation of the Farm Pond Program eliminated bluegill production/distribution, previously accounting for approximately 3–3.5 million fish annually — a major shift in hatchery output composition.
- **Funding & staffing (1991–1994)**: Operations budget rose modestly from $237.6K (1991) to an estimated $240.1K (1994); Maintenance/Construction funding dropped drastically from $1,105.0K (1991) to $8.0K (1994 estimate); FTEs fluctuated between 4.37 and a projected 6.0.
- **Interstate distribution network**: Species distributed to Louisiana, Mississippi, Arkansas, and Tennessee include Largemouth Bass (Phase I), Striped Bass (Phase I), Bluegill, Redear Sunfish, Channel Catfish, Blue Catfish, Flathead Catfish, and Paddlefish.
- **Research linkage**: A minor but notable "Private/Research" distribution category reflects collaboration with universities (e.g., 28,620 fish in FY1992).
