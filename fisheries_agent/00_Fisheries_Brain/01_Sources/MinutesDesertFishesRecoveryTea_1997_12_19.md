## Executive Summary

These minutes document the 19 December 1997 meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), held at Arizona State University. The meeting synthesized status updates and recovery actions for several imperiled southwestern U.S. desert fish taxa, including [[Gila Chub]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]], as well as the invasive [[Red Shiner]] in [[Aravaipa Creek]]. Key themes include hatchery propagation efforts, habitat renovation and barrier construction to exclude non-native species, translocation/reintroduction planning, population genetics informing "evolutionarily significant unit" (ESU) designations for Gila topminnow, and administrative/jurisdictional questions regarding the DFRT's role in big-river fish recovery (bonytail, humpback chub, Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker) in the lower Colorado River basin.

## Background & Description

This document is procedural/administrative in nature and does not provide direct morphological descriptions of the taxa discussed. Species-specific ecological and genetic notes are limited to the following:

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]**: Genetic (not morphological) distinctions among populations were emphasized. Research on **HMC genes** (linked with immunity) and **microsatellite loci** (linked to DNA replication) revealed considerable variation among four studied populations ([[Monkey Spring]], [[Bylas Spring]] S-2, [[Sharp Spring]], [[Cienega Creek]]).
  - Monkey Spring population: most genetically unique of the four populations.
  - Bylas Spring (S-2): least genetic variation; shares one allele with Cienega Creek population.
  - Sharp Spring: shares six alleles with Monkey Spring population.
  - Bylas and Cienega Creek populations: share no alleles.
- **[[Red Shiner]]**: Noted as a **fractional spawner**, producing thousands of eggs over a reproductive season — a life-history trait contributing to its invasive potential and the difficulty of eradication once established.
- **Introduced Crayfish**: A survey by P. Marsh (ASU) cataloged **seven crayfish species** in Arizona waters, including the non-native "Australian Blue lobster" (found in aquarium trade but not yet documented in the wild).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Aravaipa Creek]] Red Shiner Invasion**: A detailed invasion history was presented:
  - 1990: First red shiner captured (September); low numbers found upstream to Turkey Creek by October.
  - 1991: Late-winter flooding apparently eliminated the population; no shiners detected in subsequent months.
  - 1992–1996: Extensive stream-wide/fixed-site monitoring caught only a single shiner (1993, below "Sycamore Tree" site).
  - 1997: Moderate shiner numbers detected at the west end and canyon reach to "Hell Hole"; none found at Turkey Creek or upstream.
  - The creek has now been invaded from downstream **three separate times**, indicating persistent high risk of re-establishment despite apparent natural (flood-driven) suppression events.

- **[[Bylas Springs]] System**: Salt Creek (S-3) was renovated via stream diversion and antimycin application at the headspring. No topminnow remained in S-3 post-treatment; stock from S-1 was moved to ASU for propagation, successfully maintained, and is available for reintroduction to S-3 and eventual S-1 renovation. Three ponds were built along S-3; tamarisk (an invasive riparian plant) was largely removed.

- **Competitive/Predatory Pressures**: In New Mexico, **yellow bullhead** and **smallmouth bass** were identified as likely drivers of loach minnow and spikedace decline in the Gila River. Flathead catfish predation is a concern for razorback sucker on the Salt/Verde rivers, prompting proposals for intensive electrofishing suppression and species-specific piscicide research.

- **Habitat Threats**: Water rights acquisition (Phelps Dodge) on the upper San Francisco River and the potential revival of the Conner Dam project on the Gila River pose risks to instream flows critical for native fish habitat.

- **Translocation/Reintroduction Sites** discussed across species:
  - Spikedace → Blue River (AZ), San Francisco River (NM)
  - Loach minnow → Granite Creek (AZ), Bonita Creek (AZ)
  - Gila chub → O'Donnell Creek, Sabino Creek (AZ), Turkey Creek (NM), San Simon drainage/Grey Ranch (NM)
  - Gila topminnow → Redrock (NM) augmentation, Frisco Hot Spring (NM) reintroduction, upper San Pedro basin (proposed, despite lack of historic evidence of presence)
  - Razorback sucker → Fossil Creek (Childs facility, AZ), Roosevelt/San Carlos reservoirs (AZ) during low-water periods to exploit predator-free space

## Key Findings

- **[[Gila Chub]]** status survey completed; species designated a **Federal Candidate**. A funded ($75K, CAP monies) status survey for **[[Roundtail Chub]]** is underway.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** propagation at the FWS Alchesay-Williams Creek hatchery derived from a single founder population (N<100); a second potential founding population died of disease. East Fork of White River is the only identified release site pending final tribal review.
- **Beaver reintroduction** planned for the San Pedro River (BLM), likely in 1998, pending a FWS Biological Opinion.
- **MOU finalized** for [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction protocol (AZGFD).
- **CAP Project funding** (~$0.5 million) discussed for topminnow, spikedace, loach minnow, and razorback sucker recovery projects; DFRT will help generate an initial project list, but final funding decisions rest with FWS, BOR, and AZGFD to avoid conflict-of-interest concerns.
- **Barrier construction** proposed/planned at multiple sites: Aravaipa Creek (upstream/downstream barriers), Blue River, Eagle Creek, White River, Bonita Creek, and Fossil Creek — primarily to exclude non-native species and secure native fish refugia.
- **Antimycin reserve** recommended to be stockpiled in anticipation of future stream renovations due to limited supplier capacity.
- **Razorback sucker retention** has been poor on the Salt/Verde rivers (AZ) versus good outcomes on the San Juan River (NM), despite both using Lake Mohave-sourced stock — suggesting stock source or rearing condition issues (Dexter-produced razorbacks specifically flagged as generally poor condition).
- **Genetic research** (HMC genes, microsatellites) supports treating the **Monkey Spring** Gila topminnow population as a distinct **Evolutionarily Significant Unit (ESU)**; team consensus favors a conservative strategy of preserving each isolated natural population absent clearer ESU delineation.
- **Jurisdictional recommendation**: DFRT voted unanimously to request that FWS Region II's Regional Director restore the team's lower Colorado River basin advisory/recovery responsibilities for the four big-river fishes ([[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]]), previously rescinded when Region VI assumed lead authority (focused on the upper basin).
- **Policy position reaffirmed**: The Team maintains that "experimental, nonessential" populations of native fishes **should not count** toward formal recovery goals.
- Next meeting tentatively scheduled for **16 June 1998** at BOR Offices, Phoenix.
