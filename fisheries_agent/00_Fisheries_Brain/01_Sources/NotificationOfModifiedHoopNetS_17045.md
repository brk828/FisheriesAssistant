## Executive Summary

This document is a formal notification letter (from Arizona State University co-investigators Paul C. Marsh and Michael E. Douglas) to regulatory and management agencies announcing a **modification of hoop net sampling protocol** for [[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Little Colorado River]]. The change extends the net-run interval from a fixed 12 hours to a flexible **12- to 72-hour** range. The letter addresses anticipated concerns from reviewing agencies (physical stress, confinement damage, and [[Channel Catfish]] predation) and argues that these concerns, while reasonable, lack quantitative or anecdotal support in the literature or from field experience. The authors propose remedial measures and a monitoring framework to empirically test the impacts of extended net-soak times, based on prior experience handling more than 2,000 humpback chubs in hoop nets. This is primarily a **fisheries management/methodology** document rather than a morphological or ecological research paper, but it contains embedded ecological observations relevant to [[Humpback Chub]] handling, stress physiology, and predator-prey interactions with [[Channel Catfish]].

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body measurements) are presented in this letter. The only physical/condition-related observations concern **potential handling artifacts** from prolonged net confinement:
- Fin fraying
- Skin abrasion
- Other physical damage from net rolling, spinning, or collapse

These are treated as *sampling-induced* variables to be monitored, not natural morphological traits of [[Humpback Chub]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: [[Humpback Chub]] are sampled via hoop nets set in the [[Little Colorado River]], a major warm-water tributary of the Colorado River near [[Grand Canyon National Park]], long recognized as critical spawning and rearing habitat for this endangered [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]].
- **Species co-occurrence**: [[Channel Catfish]] are noted as occasional (infrequent) co-captures in the same hoop nets, indicating habitat overlap between the native [[Humpback Chub]] and this non-native predatory species.
- **Predator-prey interaction**: [[Humpback Chub]] remains have been found in the stomach contents of a small number of [[Channel Catfish]], suggesting at least occasional predation. However, it is **unresolved** whether:
  1. Predation occurs before or after net entrapment, and
  2. Predation risk scales with duration of confinement in the net.
- **Feeding ecology / life history note**: Post-larval freshwater fishes, including presumably [[Humpback Chub]], are noted to tolerate extended fasting periods (well beyond 72 hours) without nutritional detriment, supporting the rationale for longer net-soak intervals.
- **Behavioral ecology**: No evidence is cited that confinement up to 72+ hours alters individual or collective (schooling) behavior of [[Humpback Chub]].

## Key Findings

1. **Protocol change**: Hoop net check intervals in the [[Little Colorado River]] will be extended from a fixed 12 hours to a flexible **12–72 hour** range, based on logistical and animal welfare reasoning.
2. **Justification gap acknowledged**: The authors concede that concerns about confinement stress and predation are reasonable but **currently unsupported by quantitative, qualitative, or anecdotal evidence** in the literature or from ASU fishery biologists' field experience.
3. **Mitigation measures proposed**:
   - Nets set only by experienced personnel; checked immediately post-deployment for stability (rolling/spinning/collapse) and reset if unstable.
   - Captured fish examined for fin fraying, skin abrasion, and other physical damage to enable **quantitative assessment of confinement impact**.
   - Daily inspection and removal of entrapped [[Channel Catfish]] to minimize potential predation on [[Humpback Chub]].
   - Comparative stomach-content analysis between hoop-net-captured and rod-and-reel/angling-captured [[Channel Catfish]] to test whether confinement increases predation rates on [[Humpback Chub]].
   - Highly productive nets to be run more frequently to reduce crowding effects during high-abundance periods/locations.
4. **Prior experience base**: Observations informing this protocol change are drawn from **prior collections of over 2,000 [[Humpback Chub]]** captured in hoop nets, though this historical dataset is referenced qualitatively rather than presented with quantitative summary statistics in this letter.
5. **Flood risk noted but deemed irrelevant**: Net loss/damage due to flood-related mooring failure is acknowledged as a risk but is explicitly stated to be **independent of net-run interval**, and thus not a valid argument against the protocol change.
6. **Regulatory implication**: The authors recognize this protocol modification may necessitate revision of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Biological Opinion, indicating this change intersects with [[Endangered Species Act]] compliance for [[Humpback Chub]], a federally listed species.
7. **Distribution list** indicates this is a coordinated multi-agency action involving the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[National Park Service]], and the [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]].
