# Commercial Production of Red Cats (Speckled Bullheads) in Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #aquaculture #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This 1956/1957 report by H. S. Swingle at Auburn's Agricultural Experiment Station summarizes seven years of research into the commercial pond culture of the **[[Red Cat]]** (speckled bullhead, *Ameiurus nebulosus marmoratus*), a catfish subspecies valued commercially for its red-colored flesh. The central management challenge addressed was uncontrolled reproduction in feeding ponds, which caused undersized fish (3-4 inch fingerlings) to dominate 59-70% of total pond biomass. The study resolved this through a **density-dependent reproductive repression** strategy (stocking 3,000-6,000 fingerlings/acre), alongside improved nutrition (the "Auburn No. 1" balanced feed formulation) and a parasite/disease control protocol using potassium permanganate, formalin, and acriflavine. These advances reduced production costs from ~48 cents/lb to as low as 11.6-15.3 cents/lb and enabled year-round commercial operation rather than seasonal cropping.

## Background & Description

- **Species**: *Ameiurus nebulosus marmoratus* — a marbled/speckled subspecies of brown bullhead.
- **Common names**: Red cat (commercial name, derived from red flesh color), marble cat, speckled cat, speckled bullhead.
- **Reproductive behavior**: Excavates holes in pond banks for egg deposition (typical [[Ictaluridae]] nesting behavior).
- **Spawning temperature threshold**: First hatching occurred at water temperatures of ~80°F at 1-foot depth.
- **Larval behavior**: Newly-hatched fry form dense surface schools, persisting for a week or more before dispersing.
- **Market size classes**:
  - Optimal restaurant size: 0.2–0.5 lb live weight (2-3 fish/lb)
  - Oversized/undesirable: 0.7–1.0 lb
  - Dressing yield: 58.4% of live weight (skinning, head/viscera removal)
- **Growth-size dressing comparison**:
  - 4 fish/lb live weight → 6.8 fish/lb dressed weight
  - 2.7 fish/lb live weight → 4.6 fish/lb dressed weight

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Constructed freshwater ponds in the Southeastern US (Alabama), including dedicated spawning ponds, holding ponds, and commercial growing ponds.
- **Trophic ecology**: Omnivorous pond fish responsive to both natural pond productivity (enhanced via inorganic fertilization, 8-8-2 formulation) and supplemental artificial feeds.
- **Reproductive ecology / density-dependent repression**: A key ecological finding is that this species exhibits a **[[Repressive Factor|reproductive repression mechanism]]** analogous to that documented in goldfish, carp, and buffalo populations — high population density (via excretory/chemical cues) suppresses spawning. This was exploited to prevent unwanted reproduction:
  - Stocking at 1,000–2,000/acre → spawning still occurred.
  - Stocking at 3,000–6,000/acre with 1-4 inch fingerlings → reproduction successfully suppressed.
  - Stocking with fish ≥4 inches → reproduction occurred before repression could establish, indicating a time-dependent buildup of the repressive effect.
- **Thermal ecology**: Feeding activity and presumably metabolic/growth activity ceases below 60°F; this defines a functional "growing season" (roughly March/April–October in Alabama).
- **Disease/parasite ecology**: Susceptible to external protozoan parasites, gill flukes, fungus, and bacterial infections, historically causing major fish kills; also susceptible to suspected [[B-Vitamin Deficiency|B-vitamin avitaminosis]] (comparable to findings in [[Common Carp]]) when fed unbalanced soybean/peanut meal diets.
- **Oxygen sensitivity**: Overfeeding (≥30 lbs/acre/day, May–September) caused fish kills from low dissolved oxygen, indicating tight water-quality/feeding-rate constraints in warm months.

## Key Findings

1. **Feed formulation matters critically**: Early single-source plant meal feeds (soybean/peanut cake alone) caused survival as low as 3.6-49.2%. A 6:1 soybean/peanut meal-to-fish meal mix improved outcomes, but the best results came from **Auburn No. 1 fish feed** (35% soybean oil meal, 35% peanut oil meal, 15% fish meal, 15% distillers dried solubles as a B-vitamin source), yielding survivals of 79.9-97.8% and feed conversion ratios below 3.0.

2. **Disinfection protocol** for disease/parasite control:
   - Transport: 1-2 ppm acriflavine
   - Brood fish: 10 ppm potassium permanganate (1 hr) → 15 ppm formalin (4-6 hrs, or overnight with 1 ppm acriflavine)
   - Fry: 15 ppm formalin (2-12 hrs)
   - Pond-wide treatment for kills: 2 ppm potassium permanganate (5.4 lbs/acre-foot), ~$1.50/acre-foot cost.

3. **Stocking density controls reproduction**: The discovery that 3,000-6,000 fingerlings/acre (sized 1-4 inches) suppresses spawning was the pivotal breakthrough enabling year-round, non-seasonal commercial culture — previously ponds had to be drained before reproduction each cycle.

4. **Production economics** (per acre, ~1 year, June-stocked, drained following July):

| Stocking Rate | Fish Produced (lbs) | Feed Used (lbs) | Cost/lb Fish (¢) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 564.2 | 3,840 | 79.0 |
| 2,000 | 745.9 | 5,964 | 48.0 |
| 3,000 | 903.4 | 1,930 | 11.6 |
| 3,500 | 853.1 | 2,505 | 14.6 |
| 6,000 | 1,238.4 | 3,620 | 15.3 |

   The 3,000/acre rate was most cost-efficient overall (11.6 ¢/lb).

5. **Optimal seasonal feeding schedule** (for 3,000/acre stocked June 11, with monthly 8-8-2 fertilization through fall):
   - Aug 22–Oct 14: 5 lbs/acre/day
   - Oct 15–Nov 15: 10 lbs/acre/day
   - Nov 15–Mar 12: **no feeding** (water <60°F)
   - Mar 12–Apr 3: 10 lbs/acre/day
   - Apr 4–30: 20 lbs/acre/day
   - May 1–Jun 1: 25 lbs/acre/day (maximum safe rate)

6. **Market preparation economics**: Dressing yield was 58.4% of live weight (41.6% loss). Labor cost ~1 man-hour per 25 fish dressed (at $0.50/hr). Labor cost per pound dressed rose sharply with smaller fish size (2 cents/fish regardless of size, but larger fish-per-pound counts increase per-pound labor cost: 13.6 ¢/lb dressed at 4 fish/lb live vs. 9.2 ¢/lb dressed at 2.7 fish/lb live).

7. **Full-cycle profitability example**: At 3,000/acre stocking, total production cost was 29.1 cents/lb dressed weight (11.6¢ live-weight production + dressing-adjustment + 9.2¢ dressing labor); fish sold at 50 cents/lb dressed, yielding a net return of 20.9 cents/lb, or **$110.25 per acre** above fertilizer, feed, and dressing labor costs.

8. **Recommended standardized management protocol** (15-step summary) integrates: brood fish selection by growth rate, disinfection at every life stage transition, seasonal spawning pond management (Jan-Jul stocking of brood fish, fry collection at 0.7-1.0 inch), holding pond rearing at controlled slow growth (to delay sexual maturity), and final growing-pond stocking at ~3,000/acre with seasonally-adjusted fertilization (100 lbs 8-8-2/acre monthly) and feeding (3-5% body weight/day, capped at 25 lbs/acre/day), targeting ~900 lbs/acre yield at ~12 cents/lb production cost.
