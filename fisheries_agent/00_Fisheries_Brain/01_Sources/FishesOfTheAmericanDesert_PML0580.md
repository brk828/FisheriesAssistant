# Desert Fishes Council Session XV: Fishes of the American Deserts

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an OCR-scanned record of Session XV, "Fishes of the American Deserts," held under the [[Desert Fishes Council]] with [[Robert R. Miller]] as chairman. It summarizes four presentations: [[Carl Hubbs]] on endemic fishes and a forthcoming Great Basin fish status paper; [[Robert Behnke]] on Great Basin trout speciation and recent transplants; a brief report by Dr. Deacon on endangered species additions; and Dr. Minckley's account of threats to desert fishes in Arizona and Mexico, including a proposed refuge at the [[Phoenix Zoo]]. No dates, sample sizes, or survey statistics are given beyond the few figures noted below.

## Background & Description

**Owens Valley endemics.** Hubbs reported that four endemic fishes of the [[Owens Valley]] system now have official recognition following a recent publication by Miller. Apart from the [[Owens Dace]], which is listed as a subspecies, these are the [[Owens Chub]] (*Gila bicolor snyderi*, OCR-corrected) and the [[Owens Sucker]] (*Catostomus fumeiventris*). The dace is given as *Rhinichthys osculus* subspecies.

**Great Basin fish status.** A paper on the fishes of the northcentral [[Great Basin]], to be authored by Hubbs, Miller, and Laura Hubbs, was described as near publication. It will document the status of all fishes in that large region.

**Trout evolution.** Behnke reviewed the [[Pluvial History]] of the Great Basin and used it to discuss trout speciation. He focused on the [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*, OCR-corrected) and contrasted the evolutionary history of the [[Lahontan Basin]] with that of the [[Bonneville Basin]]. He also reported that the Alvord form, one of two distinct subspecies from the [[Alvord Basin]], is now extinct.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Trout habitats and transplants.** Behnke's slide presentation covered the Lahontan, Bonneville, and Alvord trouts, their habitats, and recent transplants. The [[Red-Banded Trout]] of northern Nevada lives at a water temperature of 83°F. He suggested that this genotype could be expanded to desert farm ponds. He also briefly reviewed the [[Greenback Trout]] reestablishment program.

**Drought impacts in Arizona.** Minckley stated that the most recent drought alone reduced five Arizona species to critically low levels. He reported that the [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) is nearly extinct in Arizona. Arizona has attempted to spawn and reintroduce both the squawfish and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). He expressed hope that a hatchery in Arizona could rear squawfish from a Utah stock.

**Mexican deserts.** Minckley said Mexico needs the support of the Desert Fishes Council as legislative efforts try to define and regulate use of the Mexican deserts. He also called for continuing surveillance of threatened fishes across North American deserts.

**Endangered species.** Miller had earlier reviewed the endangered species list. Dr. Deacon, of the [[University of Nevada, Las Vegas]], offered additions to it. The document does not list the added species.

## Key Findings

- **Official recognition:** Four endemic Owens Valley fishes were recognized following a recent publication by Miller. The Owens Dace is treated as a subspecies.
- **Forthcoming paper:** A status review of all fishes in the northcentral Great Basin is planned by Hubbs, Miller, and Hubbs.
- **Extinction:** The Alvord trout form, one of two subspecies in the Alvord Basin, is extinct.
- **Temperature tolerance:** Red-banded trout persist at 83°F, which suggests potential for desert farm pond habitat.
- **Drought effects:** The most recent drought reduced five Arizona fish species to critically low levels.
- **Squawfish decline:** The Colorado River Squawfish is nearly extinct in Arizona. Hatchery rearing and reintroduction, including a possible Utah stock, were proposed.
- **Reintroduction efforts:** Arizona has attempted to spawn and reintroduce both the squawfish and the razorback sucker.
- **Policy need:** Mexico needs Desert Fishes Council support as legislation to regulate Mexican desert use is drafted.
- **Proposed refuge:** A cooperative effort among [[Arizona State University]], the Arizona Zoological Society, Arizona Fish and Game, and the Phoenix Zoo proposes a 27-acre zoo-type refuge for threatened desert fishes. It would allow subsurface viewing and include a lotic habitat. Water would come from a city source before chlorination and be pesticide-free.
