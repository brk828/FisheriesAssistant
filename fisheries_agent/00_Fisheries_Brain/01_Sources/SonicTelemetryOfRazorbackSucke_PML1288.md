# Razorback Sucker Movements in Lake Mohave and Desert Fishes Conservation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This entry summarizes two abstracts from the 1996 [[Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council]] (published 1997). The first, by the [[USDA Forest Service]], describes the status of desert fishes within the [[Bring Back the Natives]] program, but the scan is heavily degraded and only fragments are legible. The second, which received the [[Hubbs Student Paper Award]], reports a [[Sonic Telemetry]] study of adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]]. Ten males and ten females were implanted in November 1994, and the study tracked the four males and six females that survived 17 months. Fish showed strong site fidelity and seasonal aggregation for spawning.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large catostomid endemic to the Colorado River drainage and listed as imperiled.
- **Study system:** [[Lake Mohave]] is a regulating reservoir on the [[Colorado River]], about 100 km long. It holds the largest remaining population of the species.
- **Knowledge gap:** Some aspects of the life cycle are well documented, but movement patterns, particularly in reservoirs, were poorly known before this study.
- **Partnership:** The study was a cooperative effort between [[Arizona State University]] and the [[National Biological Service]].
- **Format:** The second abstract is bilingual, with a Spanish-language summary (*Resumen*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Reservoir boundaries:** Tracked fish ranged from within 5 km of [[Hoover Dam]] (upstream reservoir limit) to within 20 km of [[Davis Dam]] (downstream limit). Total ranges spanned about 75 km.
- **Thermal mixing zone:** Fish concentrations were linked to a highly productive mixing zone that forms each year through [[Thermal Stratification]] of the reservoir.
- **Spawning period:** Fish gathered at several [[Spawning Aggregations]] between November and March.
- **Conservation setting:** The first abstract concerns the Forest Service's efforts to assess, protect, and restore desert fishes through [[Bring Back the Natives]], with partners including the [[Bureau of Land Management]] and the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Funding was described as declining, and the legible text suggests that many local projects depend on volunteer and partner support.

## Key Findings

- **Study design:** 10 adult males and 10 adult females were surgically implanted with sonic transmitters in November 1994.
- **Survival and tracking:** 10 fish (4 males, 6 females) survived the 17-month study. They were tracked bi-weekly.
- **Site fidelity:** Individuals showed marked year-round affinity for particular areas of the reservoir and often returned to the same location at about the same time each year.
- **Localized concentrations:** Fish often aggregated in specific areas, one of which was linked to the annual thermal mixing zone.
- **Spawning behavior:** Both sexes visited several different spawning aggregations during the November–March reproductive period.
- **Management relevance:** The authors state the results add significantly to razorback sucker life-history knowledge and may guide management of the Lake Mohave population.
- **Desert fishes program (partly legible):** The Forest Service project under Bring Back the Natives is described as an ongoing, multi-partner effort to assess, protect, and restore desert fishes. Declining funding is noted as a constraint. The specific project list and outcomes are not legible in the scan.
