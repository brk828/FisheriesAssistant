# Review of the Taxonomic Status of Roundtail Chub (Gila robusta) from the Moapa River, Compared with Virgin River Chub (Gila robusta seminuda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-biology #Gila

## Executive Summary
This 1985 taxonomic review by Bruce DeMarais evaluates the systematic distinctiveness of an undescribed subspecies of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) endemic to the Moapa River, Nevada, in comparison with the well-recognized [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*). Using meristic (fin ray, gill raker counts) and morphometric (head length, caudal peduncle depth/height) data compiled from multiple unpublished and published sources, the author demonstrates that the Moapa River form is morphologically distinct from *G. r. seminuda*, differing in fin ray counts, gill raker number, and several body proportions. The Moapa chub's zoogeographic affinities appear to lie not with the Virgin River fauna but with the isolated [[Pahranagat Valley]] endemic *Gila robusta jordani*, likely reflecting a shared Pleistocene/Holocene drainage connection via the now-intermittent [[White River (Nevada)]]. The paper concludes that the Moapa River roundtail chub may warrant formal subspecific recognition, distinct from *G. r. seminuda*.

## Background & Description

### Meristic Comparisons (Table 1 & 2)
**Gila r. seminuda (Virgin River):**
- Dorsal rays: commonly 9–10 (range 9–10, X̄ = 9.2–9.5)
- Anal rays: commonly 9–10 (range 9–10, X̄ = 9.3–9.5)
- Pelvic rays: range 8–9 (sparse data)
- Gill rakers: range 24–31, X̄ = 27.7
- HL/LCPD: range 3.6–5.2, X̄ ≈ 4.1–4.3

**Gila robusta subsp. (Moapa River):**
- Dorsal rays: range 8–10, X̄ = 9.0 (most commonly 9)
- Anal rays: range 8–10, X̄ = 9.0
- Pelvic rays: range 8–9, X̄ = 8.9
- Gill rakers: range 18–25, X̄ = 21.6 (fewer than Virgin River form)

### Morphometric Comparisons (Table 3 — expressed per mille standard length)
| Variable | *G. r. seminuda* (N=6) | Moapa *G. robusta* subsp. (N=32) |
|---|---|---|
| LCPD (least caudal peduncle depth) | 64 ± 2.3 (57–70) | 72 ± 0.6 (64–79) |
| HD (head depth) | 161 ± 2.0 (154–169) | 176 ± 1.0 (167–194) |
| HL (head length) | 271 ± 4.8 (254–290) | 291 ± 2.0 (270–314) |

- The Moapa River form exhibits a **thicker caudal peduncle**, **deeper head**, and **longer head** relative to *G. r. seminuda*.
- **HL/LCPD** ratio shows measurable differentiation between the two forms.

### Original Type Description of *Gila seminuda* (Cope and Yarrow 1875)
- Dorsal rays: 10; Anal rays: 10; Pectoral rays: 15
- Teeth: 5,2–2,4 (pharyngeal formula)
- Scales: 21 (lateral series)–12
- Head length: 1/5 of total length
- Body depth at dorsal fin: 1/5.75 of total length
- Least caudal peduncle depth: 1/3.3 of depth at ventral fin
- Eye diameter: 1/4 of head length
- Coloration: purplish-brown dorsally, lower fins yellowish-pink
- Type length: 140 mm

### Jordan and Evermann (1896) Redescription
- Head 1/5, depth 1/3.75, eye 1/4 of standard measures
- D. 10; A. 10; scales 21-98-12 (lateral line series)
- Body moderately deep with slender, tapering caudal peduncle (least depth ~1/3 its length)
- Mouth small, oblique, terminal; maxillary not reaching orbit
- Coloration: brownish dorsally, pink ventral fins
- Length: ~6 inches; type locality: Rio Virgen, Utah

### Intermediacy and Hybrid Origin Hypothesis
Both original descriptions collectively indicate that *G. r. seminuda* is **morphologically intermediate** between:
- [[Gila Robusta Robusta]] (typical Roundtail Chub)
- [[Gila Elegans]] (Bonytail Chub)

Miller (1946) confirmed this intermediacy after re-examining the five syntypes of *G. seminuda*. This has led some workers (Smith et al. 1979) to propose a **hybrid origin** for *G. r. seminuda*, involving ancestral *G. elegans* × *G. r. robusta* introgression.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Virgin River Chub (*Gila r. seminuda*)
- **Type locality:** Virgin River, Utah
- **Current native range:** Restricted to mainstream Virgin River downstream of [[La Verkin Springs]]
- **Habitat:** Deep runs and pools, especially those with structural cover and sand/silt substrate
- **Limiting factor:** Hot (40–43°C), anoxic, highly saline discharge from La Verkin Springs likely precludes upstream colonization (Cross 1978)
- **Diet:** Opportunistic — macroinvertebrates and algae from perennial-flow substrates
- **Distribution driver:** Most abundant in river sections with perennial flow and productive substrates

### Moapa River Roundtail Chub (undescribed *Gila robusta* subsp.)
- **Habitat:** Middle and upper reaches of Moapa River, avoiding areas immediately adjacent to spring discharges
- **Thermal regime:** Upper river receives constant warm water input (29–32°C) from springs
- **Downstream gradient:** Progressive cooling, increasing turbidity, total dissolved solids, pH, and dissolved oxygen
- **Flow regime:** Perennial across 35 km course; lowest discharge in summer due to evaporation and irrigation withdrawal
- **Ecology:** Poorly studied; habitat preferences inferred to resemble those of *G. r. seminuda*

### Threats to Both Taxa
- Habitat destruction from damming ([[Hoover Dam]]/Lake Mead inundation, 1935)
- Pollution
- Dewatering for agricultural/domestic irrigation
- Introduction of exotic species

### Associated Endemic Ichthyofauna (Moapa River)
The Moapa River supports a distinctive native fish assemblage, none of which is shared with the Virgin River:
- *Crenichthys baileyi moapae* (Moapa form of [[White River Springfish]])
- *Rhinichthys osculus moapae* ([[Moapa Speckled Dace]])
- *Moapa coriacea* (monotypic [[Moapa Dace]], uncertain generic/specific relationships)

## Key Findings

1. **Distinct meristic profile:** The Moapa River *Gila robusta* possesses fewer dorsal, anal, and pelvic fin rays (typically 9 vs. 9–10) and notably fewer gill rakers (X̄ = 21.6 vs. 27.7) than *G. r. seminuda*.

2. **Distinct morphometrics:** Moapa fish have proportionally thicker caudal peduncles, deeper heads, and longer heads compared to Virgin River chub.

3. **Biogeographic affinity:** Despite direct hydrological connection (Moapa historically entered the Virgin River ~35 km above its Colorado River confluence, pre-Hoover Dam), the Moapa chub's closest relative appears to be **[[Gila Robusta Jordani]]** from the isolated Pahranagat Valley — not *G. r. seminuda*.

4. **Historical drainage connection:** The Pahranagat Valley formerly drained into the Moapa River via the White River during the Pleistocene or Holocene, explaining the faunal affinity despite current isolation.

5. **Endemic fauna supports distinctiveness:** Three other Moapa River endemics (*Crenichthys baileyi moapae*, *Rhinichthys osculus moapae*, *Moapa coriacea*) are absent from the Virgin River, reinforcing the biogeographic separateness of the Moapa ichthyofauna as a whole.

6. **Taxonomic conclusion:** The Moapa River roundtail chub is **distinctly different** from *G. r. seminuda* and may warrant formal subspecific description, pending more rigorous systematic analysis with larger sample sizes and standardized methodologies.

7. **Data limitations:** Direct comparisons were constrained by small, heterogeneous sample sizes across multiple unpublished/published data sources and inconsistent character selection methodologies between researchers (Holden and Stalnaker 1970; Miller 1946; Minckley unpublished; DeMarais unpublished).
