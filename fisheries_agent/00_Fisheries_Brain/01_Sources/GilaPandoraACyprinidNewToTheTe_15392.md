# Gila pandora, a Cyprinid New to the Texas Fish Fauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-fish #Gila

## Executive Summary
This 1962 taxonomic note by Miller and Hubbs establishes the first authentic record of **[[Gila Pandora]]** (a cyprinid minnow) within Texas, discovered in Nations Canyon (also known as Little Aguja Canyon) in the Davis Mountains of Jeff Davis County. The paper resolves decades of taxonomic confusion regarding prior "records" of the closely related **[[Gila Nigrescens]]** in Texas, which the authors demonstrate were erroneous, largely stemming from a mislabeled 1854 museum specimen. The newly confirmed Texas population of *G. pandora* is notable for being geographically and hydrologically isolated from the main Pecos River system, representing a disjunct, relict population.

## Background & Description
Meristic (countable trait) data were used to differentiate *Gila pandora* from *Gila nigrescens* and to characterize the Nations Canyon population specifically.

### Diagnostic Differences: *G. pandora* vs. *G. nigrescens*
| Character | *Gila pandora* | *Gila nigrescens* |
|---|---|---|
| Dorsal rays | 8 | 9 |
| Pelvic rays | 8-8 (Pecos) or 9-9 (Rio Grande) | 9-9 |
| Lateral-line scales | 51–67 | 67–78 |
| Gill rakers (total, first arch) | 6–10 | 9–14 |

### Nations Canyon Population (UMMZ 178947, n=24; TNHC 6649, n=4)
- **Standard length:** 49–81 mm (juvenile to adult)
- **Dorsal rays:** 7 (2 specimens), 8 (21 specimens), 1 injured
- **Anal rays:** 8 (all 24 specimens)
- **Pectoral rays:** 12 (1), 13 (2), 14 (39 counts), 15 (6) — *both fins counted per fish*
- **Pelvic rays:** 8 (48 counts, both fins) — *reduced compared to New Mexico populations*
- **Lateral-line scales:** 59–65 (n=15)
- **Total head sensory pores** (preoperculomandibular + infraorbital + supraorbital canals, one side): 43–50 (n=15)
- **Gill rakers:** 6–9 (n=15) — slightly fewer than New Mexico populations (7–10)
- **Pharyngeal teeth formulas:**
  - 2,5-4,2 (6 specimens) — typical formula
  - 2,5-4,1 (1 specimen)
  - 1,5-4,2 (5 specimens)
  - 1,5-4,1 (1 specimen)

### Comparison to New Mexico Pecos Drainage Populations
- **Pectoral rays:** Texas population reduced (mode 14–15) vs. New Mexico (mode 16, range 15–18)
- **Gill rakers:** Texas population slightly fewer (6–9) vs. New Mexico (7–10)
- **Pharyngeal minor row teeth:** More frequently reduced in Texas specimens (6 of 12 vs. only 2 of 40 in New Mexico)

### Historical 1854 Specimen Re-analysis (UMMZ 86940 / MCZ 1671-72)
Originally catalogued as *Tigoma pulchella*, re-examination of 5 available specimens yielded counts aligning with *G. nigrescens*, not *G. pandora* or Texas populations:
- Dorsal rays: 8 (1), 9 (4)
- Pelvic rays: 9-9 in all
- Lateral-line scales: 67–71 (1 uncountable)
- Gill rakers: 10–13
- Dental formula: 2,5-4,2 (2 examined)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Intermittent, spring-fed mountain canyon stream in a semi-arid desert mountain range.
- **Location:** Nations Canyon (Little Aguja Canyon), a tributary that joins with Madera Canyon and Big Aguja Canyon to form **[[Toyah Creek]]**, itself a flood tributary of the **[[Pecos River]]**.
- **Isolation:** Surface flow in Nations Canyon normally does not extend below the Buffalo Trail Boy Scout ranch house, and only reaches beyond this point during years of exceptional rainfall. This creates a hydrologically isolated, relict population with no regular connectivity to the broader Pecos River drainage or other canyon systems.
- **Population persistence:** Local informants indicated the species has inhabited the canyon "as long as the old timers can remember," suggesting long-term (likely historic to prehistoric) residency rather than recent introduction.
- **Broader range context:** The genus **[[Gila (Genus)]]** comprises minnows chiefly confined to western United States and northern Mexico. *G. pandora* is otherwise common in New Mexican tributaries of the Pecos River, with the type locality at Sangre de Cristo Pass, New Mexico (upper Rio Grande basin).
- **Contrast with *G. nigrescens*:** That species is now restricted to streams of interior (closed) drainage flowing into isolated basins of northern Chihuahua, Mexico, including likely a tributary of **[[Lago de Guzmán]]**, and possibly the Mimbres River near Deming, New Mexico.

## Key Findings
1. **First valid Texas record:** *Gila pandora* is confirmed, for the first time authentically, from Texas — specifically from Nations Canyon, Jeff Davis County, in the Davis Mountains (~25 miles southwest of Balmorhea, Reeves County).
2. **Correction of historical misidentification:** Prior "records" of *Gila nigrescens* in Texas (Fowler 1925; Miller 1945, 1952; Knapp 1953; Moore 1957; Eddy 1957) are invalidated. These were traced to a single mislabeled 1854 specimen (part of a lot at Harvard's MCZ, part at UMMZ) that actually likely originated from a tributary of Lago de Guzmán in northwestern Chihuahua, Mexico, or possibly the Mimbres River — not Texas.
3. **No historical presence in the Medina River:** The authors state unequivocally that *Gila* has never inhabited the Medina River, refuting Fowler's (1925) questionable citation of *Leuciscus nigrescens* from that location.
4. **Discovery credit:** Dr. Robert E. Miles of Pecos first brought the population to scientific attention, collecting 4 specimens on May 30, 1960 (deposited as TNHC 6649). Confirmatory collection of 24 specimens was made by Clark Hubbs and Jim Miles on June 29, 1961 (UMMZ 178947).
5. **Isolated, endemic population:** The Nations Canyon population represents a geographically and hydrologically isolated population with no known connectivity to the Pecos River or other canyons, and is not known to occur elsewhere in the Davis Mountains or in Texas.
6. **Minor morphological divergence:** The Texas population shows slightly reduced pectoral ray counts, marginally fewer gill rakers, and more frequent reduction in minor-row pharyngeal teeth compared to New Mexican Pecos drainage populations — consistent with a peripheral, isolated population undergoing minor divergence.
