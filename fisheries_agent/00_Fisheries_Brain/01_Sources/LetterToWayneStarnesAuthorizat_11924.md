# Subpermit 91-07 for Gila Taxonomy Program Research Activities

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This subpermit (91-07) issued July 5, 1991, authorizes Dr. Wayne Starnes and designated staff from the Division of Fishes to conduct scientific research on endangered [[Gila]] species through December 15, 1991. The permit covers capture, measurement, tissue sampling, and [[PIT Tagging]] of four chub species across multiple river systems in Utah, Colorado, Arizona, and Nevada. Activities are conducted under master permits PRT-704930 and PRT-676811, with strict protocols to minimize mortality and coordinate with federal and state agencies.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Taxonomy Program]] focuses on four endangered cyprinid species: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*), and [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta jordani*). These species inhabit the [[Colorado River Basin]] and associated waterways.

Authorized capture methods include seines, trammel nets, hoop nets, and [[Electrofishing]]. Standard procedures involve weighing, measuring, attaching [[Passive Integrated Transponder]] (PIT) tags, removing tissue samples, and releasing fish. All activities follow the "Non-lethal Sampling Protocol" to minimize stress and mortality.

Geographic scope includes the [[Yampa River]], [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], and [[Virgin River]] in Utah and Colorado; the Colorado and Virgin Rivers in Arizona; [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona; and spring outflows in [[Pahranagat Valley]], Nevada.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The permit emphasizes protection of young-of-the-year fishes, requiring special handling procedures to prevent suffocation from filamentous algae during seine hauls. Only fish that cannot be accurately identified in the field may be sacrificed; all others must be released at their point of capture.

Special provisions protect radio-tagged [[Bonytail Chub]] (Carlin tag Nos. 4,123-4,159) previously stocked in the Upper [[Green River]] by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources; these must be released immediately if captured.

Live bonytail chubs captured in the wild are transported to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] following specific handling protocols. Coordination with the [[National Park Service]] and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] is required for humpback chub sampling in Arizona.

## Key Findings

**Authorized Activities by Species and Location:**

1. **Humpback, Bonytail, and Virgin River Chubs** (Yampa, Colorado, Green, and Virgin Rivers in Utah and Colorado): Full capture, measurement, PIT tagging, tissue sampling, and release authorized.

2. **Humpback and Virgin River Chubs** (Colorado and Virgin Rivers in Arizona): Tissue samples authorized from up to 50 adult humpback chubs. Up to 10 humpback chubs ≤75 millimeters may be taken for tissue analysis. Coordination with National Park Service required.

3. **Bonytail Chubs** ([[Lake Mohave]], Arizona): Full sampling protocol authorized, with live transport to Dexter National Fish Hatchery.

4. **Pahranagat Roundtail Chubs** (Pahranagat Valley, Nevada): Tissue samples limited to maximum 25 individuals (10% of wild population). PIT tags restricted to fish ≥150 mm in length.

**Reporting and Compliance Requirements:**

- All incidentally killed fish must be preserved and provided to [[Larval Fish Laboratory]], [[Colorado State University]], Fort Collins, Colorado 80523
- Annual reporting of young-of-the-year specimens taken, collection sites, and deposition locations required
- Unexpected deaths must be reported within 24 hours to Fish and Wildlife Enhancement, Salt Lake City
- Comprehensive activity report due December 31, 1991
- Coordination required with Fish and Wildlife Service Field Supervisor in Salt Lake City for all activities
- State permits from Colorado, Utah, Arizona, and Nevada required; federal land management agency permissions necessary
