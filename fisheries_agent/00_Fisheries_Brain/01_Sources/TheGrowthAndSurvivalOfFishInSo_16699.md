# The Growth and Survival of Fish in Some Suspensions of Solids of Industrial Origin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #salmonidae #water-pollution #suspended-solids

## Executive Summary

This 1963 study by Herbert and Richards investigated the effects of suspended industrial solids on [[Rainbow Trout]] (Oncorhynchus mykiss) and [[Brown Trout]] (Salmo trutta) through laboratory experiments and field surveys. Laboratory tests examined survival and growth of yearling rainbow trout exposed to coal-washery waste and wood fiber suspensions at concentrations of 50, 100, and 200 ppm over 33-40 weeks. Field data from UK River Boards documented fish population status in rivers polluted with various industrial suspended solids. The research aimed to determine critical concentration thresholds for maintaining healthy fish populations in rivers receiving industrial waste discharges.

## Background & Description

This work formed part of a broader investigation series examining effects of chemically inert, non-poisonous suspended solids on fish populations. Previous studies by Herbert and Merkens (1961) tested [[Kaolin]] and [[Diatomaceous Earth]] suspensions, finding negligible mortality at 30 ppm but over 50% mortality at 270-810 ppm, often from disease. Field studies of [[Brown Trout]] populations in Cornish rivers polluted with [[China-Clay]] mining wastes showed trout abundance in streams with ~60 ppm suspended solids equaled that in clean control streams, while densities in stretches with 1000-6000 ppm were only one-seventh of control levels.

The current study tested two additional industrial waste types: coal-washery waste from a Nottingham installation (particles <64 μ, with 55% finer than 5 μ) and mechanically ground spruce pulp wood fiber (average diameter 16-17 μ, length 304 μ, range 130-700 μ). The wood fiber contained no fungicides or toxic preservatives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Laboratory Conditions:**
Experiments used specialized aquaria (3 ft square slate bases, 2 ft glass walls) with two-bladed paddles rotating at 40 rpm, creating circular currents averaging 22.3 cm/sec. Fish were protected by plastic-coated expanded metal false floors. Batches of 25 hatchery-reared yearling rainbow trout (initial size: 15.2 cm length, 46.9 g weight) were tested at each concentration. Fish were acclimated for one month with gradual increases in stirring rate before exposure to test suspensions.

**Water Quality Parameters:**
- Temperature: 13.6-14.5°C across all treatments
- pH: 7.98-8.14
- Dissolved oxygen: 81.0-91.8% saturation
- Actual concentrations achieved: 40.5-49.1 ppm (nominal 50), 92.7-96.0 ppm (nominal 100), 189.2-192.2 ppm (nominal 200)

Fish were transferred daily to freshly prepared suspensions and fed minced bullock's heart and liver. The same implements were used across all aquaria to allow potential disease transmission, testing whether suspended solids increased disease susceptibility.

**Field Survey Context:**
Data were collected from UK River Boards on streams polluted with suspended solids from coal washing, [[China-Clay Mining]], silica mining, gravel washing, stone sawing and polishing, and railway tunnel driving. Only stretches without other pollutants were included to isolate suspended solids effects.

## Key Findings

**Coal-Washery Waste Results:**
- Zero mortality in all concentrations (50, 100, 200 ppm) over 33-40 weeks
- All fish remained in good health throughout experiments
- No evidence of direct adverse effects on survival or general health at 200 ppm
- Growth was depressed at all concentrations compared to controls, with increasing suppression at higher concentrations

**Wood Fiber Results:**
- Zero mortality at 50 and 100 ppm over 40 weeks
- Slow, steady mortality in 200 ppm wood fiber (see Figure 2), with most deaths attributed to fin-rot
- Fish in 100 ppm showed slight fin-rot signs by experiment end, suggesting potential mortality if continued longer
- Fish in 50 ppm remained healthy
- Growth suppression occurred at all concentrations, increasing with concentration

**Growth Effects:**
Despite equal food availability, growth (measured as weight attained/weight at start) was depressed by increasing concentrations of both solids. Control fish and those in lower concentrations more than trebled their weight in 8 months. The few survivors in 200 ppm wood fiber showed greater average weight than controls, likely due to more food per individual and reduced competition in the dominance hierarchy.

**Field Survey Synthesis:**
- Fish were absent or markedly reduced in abundance when average suspended solids exceeded ~600 ppm
- Many rivers supported reasonable fish populations at concentrations up to 60-70 ppm of various industrial waste solids
- Intermediate range (90-300 ppm) showed variable effects depending on solid type and environmental conditions
- [[Brown Trout]] lengths and weights in River Camel (60 ppm suspended solids) differed little from unpolluted Cornish streams

**Critical Concentration Thresholds:**
- **>600 ppm**: Fisheries likely seriously harmed
- **200-300 ppm**: Variable effects; 200 ppm wood fiber and 270 ppm kaolin/diatomaceous earth killed substantial proportions of trout in laboratory; some rivers support fish populations at these levels
- **90-100 ppm**: Harmful to lesser degree in laboratory; some field populations unaffected
- **30-60 ppm**: No evidence of increased disease susceptibility or reduced survival in well-grown trout; slight growth reduction observed but fish grew well; field populations generally unaffected

**Comparative Toxicity:**
Wood fiber effects approximately equaled those of kaolin and diatomaceous earth from previous studies. Coal-washery waste solids appeared somewhat less harmful than other materials tested.
