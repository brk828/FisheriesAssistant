# Marble Bluff Fish Facility Station Management Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Salmonidae #FishPassage #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary

This 1994 Station Management Plan, prepared by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Northern Nevada Fishery Resource Office, addresses urgent operational deficiencies at the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] (MBFF) following significant [[Cui-ui]] mortalities during the 1993 spawning season. The document outlines the facility's role in passing the endangered cui-ui (*Chasmistes cujus*) and threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (LCT; *Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*) from [[Pyramid Lake]] to spawning habitat in the [[Lower Truckee River]]. It details historical habitat degradation caused by dam construction and water diversion, fundamental design flaws in the fishway's ladder system (modeled inappropriately on Pacific salmon infrastructure), and both completed and proposed engineering modifications intended to reduce fish mortality, particularly bird predation and fish "stacking" mortality within the terminal ladder.

## Background & Description

### Habitat Degradation History
- **1905**: Construction of [[Derby Dam]] and the Truckee-Carson Canal initiated interbasin water transfer from the Truckee River to the Carson River basin for agriculture.
- **Lake Decline**: [[Pyramid Lake]] elevation fell from a historic high of 3,885 ft to a low of 3,785 ft by 1962, causing erosion and delta sediment deposition that blocked fish migration routes.
- **Compounding Factors**: Flood control channelization (Army Corps of Engineers), grazing, and timber harvesting degraded riparian vegetation, increased bank erosion, and raised water temperatures in the lower Truckee River.
- **Species Impact**: These changes led to the **extirpation** of the native Pyramid Lake strain of LCT and a critical population decline in cui-ui. Both species are **obligatory river spawners** but reside primarily in [[Pyramid Lake]] as adults.
- The current LCT population is a reintroduced strain (stocked in the 1970s) sourced from other Nevada/California lake populations and sustained via hatcheries.

### Regulatory & Construction History
- **1967**: Cui-ui listed as Endangered.
- **1975**: LCT reclassified from Endangered to Threatened.
- **1956**: Marble Bluff Dam, Pyramid Lake Fishway, and Numana Dam Fish Ladder authorized under the Bureau of Reclamation's Washoe Project (70 Stat. 775).
- **1976**: Bureau of Reclamation completed construction of the dam, a 3.1-mile fishway, 4 ladders, and a fish handling building.
- **1992**: Second Revision of the Cui-ui Recovery Plan approved.

### Facility Infrastructure
MBFF consists of four major components:
1. **[[Marble Bluff Dam]]**: Provides grade control and diverts water to the fishway.
2. **[[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]**: An earth-lined canal (3.1 miles) with three intermediate ladders and one terminal ladder; operates at 20–50 cfs flow, with 3 ft depth in the canal and ~8 ft depth in ladders.
3. **Fish Handling Building**: Contains a vee-trap, dope-tank, sorting table, and holding pens for counting, sexing, and measuring fish before release upstream or transport to hatcheries.
4. **River Trap**: A concrete-lined channel and fish elevator at the dam base that captures fish attempting to reach the dam directly via the river.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### River Access Requirements
Two migration routes exist to reach spawning habitat, both funneling into the Fish Handling Building:
- **Delta Passage**: Preferred route; requires minimum lake elevation of 3,810–3,815 ft (mean sea level) and average annual inflow of 406,000–413,000 acre-feet. Produces earlier, larger, and less stressful runs.
- **[[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]**: Operational only between lake elevations of 3,784–3,810 ft. Produces smaller, delayed runs (up to a month later) and exposes fish to higher predation and mortality risk.

### Truckee River Flow Management
The **Truckee-Carson Hydrologic Model** (Stampede Subroutine) governs releases from Stampede Reservoir to support cui-ui reproduction:
- **Attraction volume** (Jan–Apr): 60,000 acre-feet minimum.
- **Spawning/Incubation/Rearing volume** (May–Jun): 120,000 acre-feet (60,000/month).
- **Total minimum volume** (delta access scenario): 180,000 acre-feet.
- **Delayed run scenario** (fishway-only access, as in 1993): Requires an additional 24,000 acre-feet through July, raising the total to **204,000 acre-feet**.

### Spawning Migration Behavior
- **Cui-ui**: Congregate in a "prespawning aggregate" near the Truckee River mouth in late winter/early spring. Historically spawned in the lower 45 miles of river (majority in lower 25 miles); now restricted to the lower 12 miles (mostly lower 6 miles) below [[Numana Dam]]. Cui-ui may spend up to 16 days in the river and are believed to spawn only once every 3–4 years.
- **LCT**: Do not aggregate at the delta/fishway; migrate individually or in small groups, typically preceding the cui-ui run by a few weeks.

## Key Findings

### Design Limitations of the Fishway
1. **Flawed Model**: Ladders were patterned after Ice Harbor Dam (Snake River, salmon-focused design) using a 1:10 slope with 1 ft drops between pools (27 weirs per ladder), creating a 10 ft elevation rise per ladder.
2. **Orifice Failure**: Bottom orifices intended for fish passage created excessive turbulence/upwelling; cui-ui could not use them due to water velocity.
3. **No Resting Areas**: Original design eliminated resting places between steps, exhausting migrating cui-ui.
4. **Result**: In 1976, no cui-ui were found using the fishway at all.

### Historical Modifications (1977–1980s)
- Orifices were **plugged** in all four ladders to reduce turbulence.
- **Wooden weirs** were inserted between cement weirs, reducing spillway height from 1 ft to 6–8 inches and lowering water velocity.
- This configuration remained in use through 1993. Notably, cui-ui did not need the fishway from 1984–1992 due to available delta access.

### 1993 Crisis: Ongoing Design Issues
The dramatic increase in the cui-ui population (attributed to facility success) has outgrown the fishway's capacity, leading to two major mortality events at the terminal ladder in 1993:
1. **Migration Timing**: Fishway-only access delays runs up to a month, requiring extended water releases.
2. **Curvilinear Run-Size Relationship**: Population size interacts non-linearly with migration route capacity (Buchanan & Strekal 1988).
3. **Fishway Access**: Fluctuating lake levels compromise the terminal ladder's effectiveness.
4. **Selective Pressure**: The difficult ladder route selects for younger male cui-ui; older females often fail to negotiate all four ladders.
5. **Predation**: Shallow fishway depth exposes fish to heavy bird predation (notably white pelicans).
6. **Siltation**: Continuous heavy siltation at the terminal ladder and fishway mouth requires constant dredging/maintenance.

### Proposed & Implemented 1994 Modifications
- **Weir/Orifice Repair**: Replacement of deteriorated wooden weirs and orifice covers, prioritized for Ladders 3 & 4 (closest to lake, most degraded by 1980s high water).
- **Bird Predation Mitigation**: Installation of aluminum-framed netting over ladders (removable during migration season) and deployment of noise guns for bird abatement.
- **Fishway Bypass**: A diversion canal is being constructed to allow water/fish processing in the Fish Handling Building and River Trap **without** requiring water flow through the fishway — enabling managers to keep the fishway dry when delta access is available.
- **River Trap Gate Fix**: Removal of a crossbar that previously trapped cui-ui in the gate mechanism (implemented after a June 1993 fish kill); ratchet-release mechanism redesign still pending.
- **Terminal Ladder Pool Reduction**: Proposed dredging to reshape the lakeside pool into a narrower canal (3 ft deep x 4 ft wide) with riprapped banks at ~20° angle to prevent mass aggregation of cui-ui below the terminal ladder.
- **False Bottoms**: Two proposed methods to prevent fish "stacking" mortality:
  - Filling inundated ladder steps with 1-inch gravel to maintain a uniform 3 ft depth.
  - Installing a wire basket in exposed lower steps to cap water depth at 4 feet while maintaining flow/velocity uniformity.
- **Flow Regulation Concepts** (unresolved): 
  - A **weighted chainlink fence** barrier, adjustable for siltation changes, operable by two staff.
  - An **electric fish weir**, though concerns remain regarding (a) inability to deter mass migration behavior of thousands of cui-ui without causing crush injuries, and (b) uncertain conductivity given Pyramid Lake's water salinity/alkalinity.
- **Contingency**: Two hatchery trucks retrofitted for cui-ui transport, allowing staff to physically remove/relocate fish if regulatory measures fail.

### Management Uncertainty
The report concludes with candid acknowledgment that given the current cui-ui population size, **eliminating mortality entirely within the existing confined, poorly-designed terminal ladder may be unrealistic**, underscoring the need for longer-term structural redesign beyond the proposed interim 1994 fixes.
