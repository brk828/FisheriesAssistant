# Scientific Research Experiences for High School Students - SCENE Proposal

#education #aquatic-ecology #conservation-biology #endangered-species #urban-ecology

## Executive Summary

This proposal from the [[Southwest Center for Education and the Natural Environment]] (SCENE) to the [[Arizona Community Foundation]] outlines a three-year program to involve high school students in authentic scientific research at [[Arizona State University]]. The project aims to engage 120 students annually in hands-on research experiences across biological, physical, and applied sciences, with emphasis on aquatic ecology through the [[Central Arizona Phoenix Long-Term Ecological Research Project]] (CAP LTER). Students will participate in the complete scientific inquiry cycle—questioning, action, and reflection—working directly with university researchers on ongoing projects. The program includes three components: hands-on research participation, an Internet communications network linking students and scientists, and demonstration labs where student researchers teach others. Partner organizations include ASU's Center for Environmental Studies, individual faculty researchers, high schools in the [[Tempe Union High School District]], and the Arizona State Public Information Network (ASPIN).

## Background & Description

The proposal is grounded in the philosophy that students experiencing scientific discovery firsthand develop stronger interest in science careers. The project emphasizes the complete inquiry process rather than isolated technical skills, following the framework that scientific investigation comprises three fundamental elements: questioning (hypothesis formation), action (data collection and analysis), and reflection (re-evaluation and consideration of alternative explanations). This cyclical process distinguishes authentic scientific inquiry from linear laboratory exercises.

The partnership structure brings together educational expertise from SCENE (a non-profit with five years of K-12 science education experience), research capacity from ASU (a Research I university), technological support from ASPIN, and student access through Tempe-area high schools. The [[Tempe Union High School District]] serves approximately 12,500 students in grades 9-12, with 32% from minority backgrounds (19% Hispanic, 6% African-American, 5% Asian, 3% Native American).

SCENE's track record includes Science Connections (serving over 1,000 students annually), Super Science Saturday (attracting 4,000-5,000 visitors), and Project Re-Leaf (involving 700+ K-12 students in habitat creation). Executive Administrator Kathryn Kyle brings seven years of educational program design experience and established relationships with ASU scientists.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The aquatic ecology research opportunities center on two major initiatives:

**CAP LTER Investigations**: The [[Central Arizona Phoenix Long-Term Ecological Research Project]] focuses on urban development effects on the [[Sonoran Desert]] ecosystem. Student activities include:
- Monitoring nutrients and minerals in stream water flows and groundwater at ASU's Goldwater Center
- Collecting and identifying insects from specific locations (laboratory and field work)
- Monitoring plant growth and measuring respiration across Valley locations
- Limnology of [[Rio Salado Lake]] and other urban lakes (water quality testing, microscopic organisms)
- GIS investigations using satellite imagery and remote sensing

**Endangered Species Conservation** (Dr. W.L. Minckley, Biology): The [[University Arboretum Center]] near "A" Butte features a recycling aquatic ecosystem designed for endangered fish conservation. This 50-yard stream system with pools, riparian vegetation, and marshland provides semi-natural conditions between field and laboratory settings. Student research involves:
- Water quality analysis (temperature, salts, nutrients, dissolved gases) compared to natural conditions
- Biological monitoring of organisms from introduction through ecosystem stabilization
- Life-history and ecology studies of endangered fishes using indirect methods (non-sacrificial techniques including photography for growth measurements, behavioral observation for diet analysis)
- Testing hypotheses that semi-natural conditions better resemble natural states than laboratory settings

The project collaborates with [[Phoenix Zoo]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. All required permits for endangered species work are secured.

**Planetary Geology Application** (Dr. Ronald Greeley): Though not aquatic, this demonstrates program breadth. Students model winds at the Mars Pathfinder landing site using a 1/8 scale model in an environmental boundary-layer wind tunnel, comparing results with Mars surface images.

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Timeline** (36-month implementation):
- **Months 1-4**: Project team convening, research project recruitment, establishing 10-20 diverse research opportunities
- **Months 2-7**: Student and adult liaison recruitment, training in research techniques
- **Month 7 onward**: Active student research participation (minimum 15 hours per semester per student)
- **Months 6-8**: Internet infrastructure setup (server, email accounts, threaded discussion, Web site)
- **Demonstration labs**: 1 event year one, 2 events each in years two and three
- **Evaluation**: Biannual assessments throughout project duration

**Student Learning Objectives**:
- Master complete scientific inquiry process (observing, predicting, hypothesis formulation, experimental design, data collection/analysis, reflection)
- Develop proficiency with scientific instruments and technology specific to research projects
- Acquire discipline, precision, and open-mindedness required for scientific inquiry
- Develop systematic information organization and presentation skills
- Gain awareness of scientific career pathways through direct interaction with researchers, graduate students, and undergraduates
- Master Internet navigation and communication tools
- Develop skills to convey scientific information to non-scientists

**Participation Scale**:
- **Direct involvement**: Up to 120 students annually in hands-on research
- **Indirect classroom impact**: Average 30 additional students per research participant via Internet network (3,600 students annually)
- **Public outreach**: Up to 5,000 individuals per demonstration lab event learning from student researchers
- **Duration flexibility**: Students may participate 6 months to 3 years depending on project duration and interest

**Evaluation Methods**:
- Written work plans jointly prepared by student, scientist, and liaison (reviewed every 6-9 months)
- Biannual written evaluations by scientists rating progress on 1-5 scale for each learning objective
- Monthly group meetings with liaison persons, documented in project journals
- Student-maintained research journals and portfolios
- Culminating essays on learning and application
- Optional parent/teacher questionnaires on perceived impact
- Annual quantitative synthesis by SCENE staff

**Technology and Methods Training**:
- Field and laboratory aquatic sampling equipment
- Electronic monitors and reagents for ionic concentrations, pH, dissolved gases, nutrients
- Light microscopy including videography for plankton identification
- Indirect data acquisition methods for endangered species (non-sacrificial techniques)
- Computer statistical analysis software
- GIS and remote sensing technology
- Wind tunnel operation and digital image analysis (planetary geology)
- Internet communication tools (2-3 training sessions of 2 hours each)

**Partnership Roles**:
- **SCENE**: Overall coordination, administration, partner integration, evaluation synthesis
- **ASU Center for Environmental Studies**: CAP LTER research coordination, student supervision
- **Individual Faculty Researchers**: Project-specific supervision, training, evaluation
- **High Schools**: Student/liaison recruitment, scheduling coordination, classroom integration support
- **ASPIN**: Technical infrastructure (server space, Web design, email setup, training)

**Adult Liaison Function**: Critical intermediary role filled by parents, teachers, or club sponsors who receive thorough research training from scientists, then prepare students for participation, coordinate scheduling, assist with evaluation, facilitate Internet participation, and support demonstration lab development.
