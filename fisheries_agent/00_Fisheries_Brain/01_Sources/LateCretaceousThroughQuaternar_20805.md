# Palaeogeographic Evolution of the Middle East and the Levantine Landbridge

#zoogeography #palaeogeography #biogeography

## Executive Summary

This document is the front matter of a 1985 symposium proceedings volume on the fauna and zoogeography of the Middle East, followed by two papers. The first, by [[R. Wolfart]], reconstructs the [[Palaeogeography of the Middle East]] from the Late Cretaceous to the Quaternary, focusing on the closure of the [[Tethys Ocean]], [[Alpine Orogeny]] events, and [[Quaternary Climate Variation]]. The second, by F. D. Por, sets out theses on the [[Levantine Landbridge]] as a [[Biogeographic Filter]]. The text is largely geological and biogeographic; no fish taxa are discussed. The supplied Por paper is truncated after its introduction and first two theses.

## Background & Description

**Front matter.** The Editors (Mainz, December 1986) thank the [[Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft]] for funding through the "Tubingen Atlas of the Middle East," along with students of the Zoological Institutes of Mainz and Darmstadt and staff of the Favorite Park Hotel. The symposium's closing resolution stressed that basic science and applied fields (agriculture, medicine, environmental protection) are linked. The volume aims to stimulate research on Middle Eastern systematics, zoogeography, ecology and nature conservation.

**Wolfart paper.** Wolfart draws on biostratigraphy and palaeogeographic maps (figs. 1–8) compiled from many regional sources, and notes that the continental fossil record is far more incomplete than the marine record. The region's geology spans roughly the last 70–80 million years, a period in which the Tethys linked the Mediterranean and Alpine region with the Indian and Pacific Oceans via southern Turkey, Syria, Iraq, Afghanistan and Pakistan.

**Por paper.** Por presents theses for discussion on the biogeographic significance, functioning and preservation of the Levantine Landbridge, described as "the most important crossroad of biotic exchange on the present globe." The Lessepsian migration through the [[Suez Canal]] is only briefly touched on because the symposium emphasised terrestrial and freshwater biogeography.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Tethyan closure:** Wolfart states that the faunal migration between Asia and Africa indicates the Tethys was closed, at least locally, by the Middle and Late Miocene. A first land bridge may have existed across western Arabia, Jordan and western Syria.
- **Arid-zone lakes:** Evaporation concentrates groundwater minerals in closed basins, so lacustrine deposits are often evaporitic. Marine foraminifera and molluscs can occur in these lakes without any marine ingression, reflecting the evolution of a salt pan over several thousand to ten thousand years (e.g. [[Damascus Basin]] and [[Palmyra Basin]]).
- **Glacial-era sea level:** Ice accumulation lowered sea level by about 100 m, so the [[Persian Gulf]] dried up during the Würm (the last glacial). Wolfart gives a drop of "about 89 m" for the Syrian/Lebanese coast (as printed).
- **Filter dynamics:** Por argues that biogeographic filters are periodically modified by the roughly 100,000-year glacial cycles of the Pleistocene, with at least 10, and possibly 20, such cycles.
- **Miocene fauna:** Continental Miocene localities in Saudi Arabia, Turkey and elsewhere yield vertebrates, ostracods and bovids (e.g. [[Bovidae]], [[Hipparion]]), and are used to infer biogeographic connections.

## Key Findings

**Wolfart: palaeogeographic sequence**

- **Late Cretaceous (~70 Ma):** The Tethys was up to several thousand kilometres wide and separated Gondwanan fragments (Africa, Nubo-Arabia, India) from the northern continents. This prevented continuous exchange of continental biota, producing distinct biogeographic provinces. Shelf sediments reach 1800 m (Indus trough), 2600 m (eastern Taurids) and 2800 m (Pontids), but do not exceed 1000 m in most of the Syrian–Iraqi–Persian Gulf area. Rudist reef-builders indicate a warm climate.
- **Paleocene:** Thicknesses reach 2400 m in the Indus–Suleiman–Baluchistan trough and more than 1400 m in the Zagros trough. Large Nummulites-type foraminifera were widespread from the Paleocene to the Oligocene, indicating a still-warm Tethys.
- **Eocene–Early Oligocene:** Marine sandstone and shale were deposited in basinal areas (up to 2500 m in the Pontids and more than 6000 m in the Alborz). Wolfart infers increasing tectonic activity from the thick sediments, linked to the approach and subduction of India and Afro-Arabia.
- **Miocene:** A continental regime dominated after the Late Oligocene regression. Thick evaporites accumulated in the Euphrates–Persian Gulf area, and volcanism occurred in northern Turkey.
- **Pliocene–Quaternary:** Continental conditions prevailed. Marine Pliocene sediments reach 1000–2000 m only in the Zagros trough and its eastern continuation. Pliocene/Quaternary clastics reach "?up to 12000 m" in the western foreland of the Pamir–Himalaya complex.
- **Tectonics:** Seafloor spreading from the Early Cretaceous separated the Gondwanan fragments. Collision of Afro-Arabia and India with the northern continents occurred during the Cretaceous–Paleocene transition and the Oligocene. The Tethys had effectively disappeared by the end of the Oligocene, but subduction continues, as shown by seismicity in fault zones.
- **Climate:** A colour change from red to olive green at the beginning of the Pliocene signals decreased temperature and increased humidity. Wolfart concludes that the Pleistocene climate of the Middle East was not in general more humid than today.

**Wolfart: Quaternary climate**

- The Würm glaciation was up to 4 °C cooler than today, inferred from snow-limit depression of up to 1000 m in the Hindukush and 500–800 m in Lebanon. The present snow limit in northern Afghanistan is 4600–4900 m, and warm end moraines occur at 2200–2800 m.
- The "Altithermal" (Atlanticum), a moist and relatively warm period between 9000 and 6000 years BP, is described from the Arabian Subcontinent (Anton). Precipitation during this period is given as "400–100 mm" more than at present (as printed), decreasing from south to north.
- The classical correlation of European glacial periods with Middle Eastern "pluvials" has been rejected. Cold phases can be either humid or dry.

**Por: Levantine Landbridge theses (partial)**

- **Thesis 1:** The Levantine Landbridge is one of five "Biogeographic Filters" separating the terrestrial biogeographic realms of the recent globe. Filters are permanent or temporary barriers that selectively limit biotic exchange between adjacent realms, and their width and permeability change with glacial cycles. A comparable marine filter exists in the Isthmus of Suez.
- **Thesis 2:** The Landbridge separates the Ethiopian, Palaearctic and Oriental realms. It formed when the Tethys was bridged in the Burdigalian (Early Miocene), about 15 Ma BP.
- The supplied text ends before the remaining theses, research programme and action plan.

**Relevance to aquatic systems:** The document contains no fish or other aquatic species records. Its aquatic relevance is limited to Tethyan and marine palaeoenvironments, evaporitic lakes, and Suez-related marine migration.
