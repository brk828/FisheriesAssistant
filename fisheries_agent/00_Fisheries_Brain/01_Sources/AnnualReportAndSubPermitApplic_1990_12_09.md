## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory annual report and permit renewal application submitted by Arizona State University (ASU) researchers to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) under blanket Endangered Species Permit PRT-676811. It summarizes 1990 "take" activities (collection, transport, and captive maintenance) involving numerous federally listed and candidate freshwater fishes native to the Sonoran and Chihuahuan Desert regions, and requests authorization to continue and expand work with wild and captive stocks in 1991. The report documents specific collection localities, specimen counts, and status of captive refuge populations for species such as [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and several [[Gila (Genus)|Gila chub]] taxa, alongside notes on genetic analyses (mtDNA, allozymes) performed on specimens from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery captive broodstock program.

## Background & Description

The report is authored by W.L. Minckley and colleagues (P.C. Marsh, M.E. Douglas, T.E. Dowling, B.D. DeMarais, R. Timmons, A. Velasco, D.D. Oakey) at ASU's Department of Zoology, operating as sub-permittees under a USFWS blanket permit covering endangered fish research in Region 2 (Arizona, New Mexico, and portions of Texas). The taxa involved span multiple families:

- **Salmonidae**: [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), [[Gila Trout]] (*O. gilae*)
- **Cyprinidae/Leuciscidae**: [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Virgin Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*), [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), [[Pahranagat Chub]] (*Gila robusta jordani*), [[Chihuahuan Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Cyprinella formosa*, two subspecies), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, now known as Colorado Pikeminnow)
- **Catostomidae**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — pending federal listing at time of writing
- **Cyprinodontidae**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius* and the Quitobaquito form, *C. m. eremus*)
- **Poeciliidae**: [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis* subspecies), [[Big Bend Gambusia]] (*Gambusia gaigei*)
- **Ictaluridae**: [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*)

Many of these species are endemic to isolated desert streams, springs, and river fragments in the [[Gila River Basin]], [[Colorado River Basin]], and [[Yaqui River Basin]], reflecting the extreme habitat fragmentation and endemism characteristic of southwestern U.S. desert fish faunas.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Collections targeted remnant wild populations at critical refugia including **Aravaipa Creek, Arizona** (loach minnow, spikedace), **Black Draw, San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge, Arizona** (Yaqui chub, Yaqui topminnow), **Lake Mohave, Arizona** (bonytail), **Bylas Springs, Arizona** (Gila topminnow), and **Tule Spring** (Yaqui topminnow).
- The **Quitobaquito form** of desert pupfish was managed via inter-agency transfer (U.S. National Park Service → Arizona Game and Fish Department → ASU) to augment a captive refuge population, which had grown to **>2,500 adults plus numerous young** by November 1990 — indicating a successful ex-situ conservation program for this highly restricted population.
- Ex-situ ("captive") stock maintenance was a major component of the program, functioning as insurance populations against extirpation in the wild for several critically imperiled taxa (e.g., Big Bend gambusia, beautiful shiner, Virgin chub).
- The report highlights ambiguity in permit coverage: **Virgin chub** (*Gila seminuda*) had inadvertently been omitted from the authorized species list despite being requested in the 1990 application, illustrating administrative/regulatory friction in managing multi-species endangered fish permits across agencies (ASU, USFWS, Dexter National Fish Hatchery).
- The pending listing of the **razorback sucker** as endangered (anticipated early 1991) reflects the broader ongoing decline of large-bodied native Colorado River Basin fishes due to habitat alteration, damming, and non-native species introductions.

## Key Findings

**1990 Wild Population "Takes" (by species/locality):**
- **Bonytail** — Lake Mohave, AZ: 1 wild specimen sent to Dexter NFH; 6 wild specimens sent to Willow Beach; 3 hatchery mortalities to ASU (May); 1 hatchery specimen released unharmed (November).
- **Yaqui chub** — Black Draw, San Bernardino NWR: 30 specimens (10 from each of 3 localities).
- **Loach minnow** — Upper/lower Aravaipa Creek, AZ: 43 specimens (5–10 per locality, 8 localities, 3 dates).
- **Spikedace** — Upper/lower Aravaipa Creek, AZ: 51 specimens (4–10 per locality, 8 localities, 3 dates).
- **Desert pupfish (Quitobaquito form)** — 0 collected directly; 84 fish received via interagency transfer to augment captive refuge stock (>2,500 adults + young by Nov. 1990).
- **Gila topminnow** — Bylas Springs, AZ: 75 collected in cooperation with AZGFD; captive population maintained at ASU now >75 fish.
- **Yaqui topminnow** — San Bernardino NWR: 10 preserved from Black Draw; 25 transported live from Tule Spring (captive stock >50 fish).
- **Apache trout, Gila trout, Humpback chub, Sonora chub, Beautiful shiner, Woundfin, Little Colorado spinedace, Colorado squawfish, Desert pupfish (typical subspecies)** — no wild takes reported in 1990.

**Captive Stock Receipts from Dexter National Fish Hatchery (1990):**
- **Pahranagat chub** — 20 frozen specimens; analyzed for mtDNA and allozymes.
- **Beautiful shiner** — 10 of each subspecies received alive; living stocks maintained at 9 (*C. f. formosa*) and 105 (*C. f. mearnsi*) individuals.
- **Big Bend gambusia** — 50 received alive; captive stock now >70 individuals.
- **Desert pupfish** — ~100 received for electrophoretic analysis.
- **Virgin chub** — ~300 received (preserved, frozen, and live); analyzed for mtDNA and allozymes; permit status noted as an administrative oversight requiring amendment.
- **Chihuahuan chub, Yaqui catfish** — none requested or received.
