# Gammarus Distribution and Ecology in the Flowing Waters of the Schlitzerland

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This German paper, by M. P. D. Meijering of the [[Limnologische Flußstation Schlitz]], surveys the freshwater amphipod genus *Gammarus* in the Schlitzerland region of central Hesse. It compares three widespread European species, [[Gammarus Pulex]], [[Gammarus Fossarum]] and [[Gammarus Roeseli]], along a river continuum from the rhithral to the potamal, and records a single introduction of [[Gammarus Tigrinus]]. The author links the distribution of *G. pulex* to current velocity and winter minimum temperature, examines the seasonal upstream migration of *G. pulex* in the [[Sengelbach]], and reports a 1:1 sex ratio in stable populations. The paper also documents how urban sewage in the [[Fulda River]] eliminated gammarids over a 26 km stretch. It concludes with a proposal to rename the two native species in German common usage.

## Background & Description

- **Study region:** The Schlitzerland lies in the Central German Uplands (Region 9 of the [[Limnofauna Europaea]] classification). Its valleys include the [[Fulda River]] and the [[Schlitz River]], with tributary brooks draining Buntsandstein and Vogelsberg basalt geology.
- **Species overlap:** Three *Gammarus* species overlap in the region. *G. roeseli* is a lowland and potamal species, *G. pulex* extends across north-western Europe and into the uplands, and *G. fossarum* is centred on central and eastern uplands.
- **Introduced species:** [[Gammarus Tigrinus]], native to North America, was introduced to the [[Werra River]] and spread through the [[Weser River]]. A small release occurred near Pfordt.
- **Stream setting:** Brooks rise up to about 420 m above sea level, run at mean gradients up to 8.0 ‰, and are often forested. Substrates range from sand and angular sandstone gravel to smooth, rounded basalt cobbles that trap little leaf litter.
- **Methods:** Gammarids were collected at many sites and sorted by species. Temperature was recorded with automatic loggers, with monthly means calculated from daily maxima and minima. Water chemistry data were supplied by J. Brehm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Temperature regime.** The [[Schlitz River]] showed the largest annual temperature swing. Its highest daily temperature was 20 °C, while free brooks such as the Sengelbach did not exceed 18 °C. Winter daily minima in most brooks lay close to freezing. The largest daily amplitudes occurred in mid-course brooks.

**Water chemistry (Table 3).** *G. pulex* tolerated more acidic water (pH 4.8–7.9) than *G. fossarum* (pH 5.5–8.0), but the alkaline limit did not differ. Nitrate concentrations were markedly higher in *G. pulex* brooks (1000–10 000 vs 190–440), and nitrite and ammonium were also somewhat elevated. Conductivity minima were similar, and the highest value occurred in the saline [[Salzbach]].

**Pollution.** Sewage from the City of Fulda interrupted gammarid occurrence over about 26 km of the Fulda. *G. roeseli* was the last species to recolonise downstream, which the author takes as evidence that it is the most pollution-sensitive of the three.

**Sex-determination context.** The author notes that *Gammarus* sex determination can be polyfactorial and influenced by external factors, so the observed sex ratios should be interpreted cautiously.

## Key Findings

- **Zonation of species.** In the Haune and Fulda, the dominant species shifts along the continuum: *G. fossarum* in the epirhithral, *G. pulex* in the metarhithral and hyporhithral, and *G. roeseli* in the epipotamal. *G. roeseli* reaches only the uppermost hyporhithral in the Schlitzerland.
- **Fulda sewage gap.** Urban wastewater from Fulda created a gap of about 26 km (about 29 km for *G. roeseli*) without gammarids. Recolonisation by *G. pulex* and *G. fossarum* began after the [[Schlitz River]] joined, and *G. roeseli* returned at Rimbach.
- **Full rhithral represented.** The Schwarzbach supplied the only *G. roeseli* record among the brooks, so the whole rhithral sequence is present in the brook system.
- **Species sorting by habitat.** Brooks held only *G. fossarum* (for example Rodenbach, Holzgrabenbach), only *G. pulex* (for example Trossbach, Haarwiesengrund), or mixed populations. Table 2 lists elevations and gradients: *G. fossarum* occurred up to 400 m (Eisenbach), *G. pulex* up to 340 m, and the steepest *G. fossarum* brook reached 8.0 ‰.
- **Spatial pattern in mixed brooks.** *G. pulex* peaked near springs and at the mouths of tributaries, with minima in mid-course. In the Rimbach system, *G. pulex* reached up to 100 % near springs.
- **Seasonal migration.** Sampling about every six weeks over almost three years in the lower Sengelbach (200 animals per station, at least 5 mm) showed that *G. pulex* rose sharply in late summer and autumn and declined to a minimum in March to May. This pattern recurred in three consecutive years, and the author interprets it as an annual upstream migration balanced by winter drift.
- **Rimbach comparison.** In winter and summer 1969, the Rimbach system showed a similar push-and-pull pattern, with upper and lower reaches higher in summer and the middle reach higher in winter.
- **Limiting factors.** The author proposes that current velocity and winter minimum temperature limit *G. pulex*, which persists near springs where winter temperatures stay relatively high. *G. fossarum* can persist in fast, near-freezing water, which explains its higher elevational range. *G. pulex* reaches the Arctic Circle in Finland, but has not been recorded in Norway.
- **Sex ratio.** In stable *G. pulex* populations the sexes occur in a 1:1 ratio (Haune: 1302 males and 1369 females across nine sites). The Sengelbach migrating populations were male-biased early in the migration period, which the author attributes to larger males migrating more successfully.
- **Tigrinus persistence.** About 400 *G. tigrinus* were released in the Salzbach in 1964. A mass population formed in 1965, and the species persisted six years later (last checked in 1970) alongside *G. pulex* and *G. fossarum*.
- **Proposed names.** The author recommends the common name "Bachflohkrebs" for *G. fossarum* only, and "Gemeiner Flohkrebs" for *G. pulex*.
