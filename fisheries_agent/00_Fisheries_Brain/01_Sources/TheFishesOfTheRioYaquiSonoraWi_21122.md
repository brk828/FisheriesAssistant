# The Fishes of Rio Yaqui, Sonora, with a New Silurid Genus, and Other Pacific Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Siluridae #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary
This 1896 paper (California Academy of Sciences) reports on three small collections of Pacific-slope freshwater fishes from Mexico and Arizona. Part I covers the [[Rio Yaqui]] basin, based on collections by Dr. Gustav Eisen at [[Hermosillo]] and Mr. William Wightman Price in the Yaqui headwaters. It describes the new catfish genus and species *Villarius pricei*. Part II records nine species from streams near [[Cape San Lucas]], Baja California. Part III describes a new goodeid-type species, *Characodon eiseni*, from [[Tepic]].

## Background & Description
**Collections and collectors**
- Eisen collected two species (in 1892) at Hermosillo. Price collected six species in 1894 at four stations: the [[Rio Yaqui]] east of Oposura, [[San Bernardino Creek]] near the Arizona line, and Morse and Rucker Cañons in the [[Chiricahua Mountains]].
- Earlier Sonoran fishes were known only from San Bernardino Creek (Mexican Boundary Survey).

**New genus *Villarius* Rutter**
- Allied to *Ameiurus* but with scattered hair-like cirri on the sides.
- Differs from *Ictalurus* in having the occipital process and the interspinal buckler widely separated and joined by ligament. It differs from *Ameiurus* in its narrow head and deeply forked caudal.
- *V. pricei*: B. 8; D. I,6; A. 22–23; C. 17; head 3⅓–3¾ in body. The maxillary barbel is very long, and the pectoral spine has about twelve distinct hooked serrae.
- Based on seven specimens, 7 to 17½ in. long, from San Bernardino Creek.
- Closely related to *Ameiurus dugesii* Bean, which lacks prominent serrations on the pectoral spines. Bean reported that the types have hair-like cirri, so the species is transferred as *Villarius dugesii*.

**Characodon eiseni Rutter (new)**
- Dorsal 11–13; anal 13; scales 30–32.
- Snout shorter than eye; lower jaw projecting; teeth strongly bicuspid.
- Based on four specimens, the largest 1¼ in., from a branch of the Rio Grande de Santiago at Tepic.
- Closest to *Characodon variatus* Bean, from the headwaters of the same basin. It differs in having fewer rays and scales, far fewer teeth, a larger eye, a more posterior dorsal, and different coloration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The Rio Sonora at Hermosillo is shallow and nearly dry in the dry season because water is diverted for irrigation. After summer rains it can become a torrent lasting several days. Eisen's fishes came from sandy pools fed by a tiny streamlet.
- The Yaqui region is elevated and mostly barren. Streams in Morse and Rucker Cañons sink into the sand, with no recent evidence of reaching the Yaqui.
- The Yaqui basin is more closely connected to the Gila basin. All its species except the catfish and *Campostoma* also occur in the Gila.
- The Cape San Lucas fishes include marine-derived species (*Mugil albula*, *M. curema*) that frequently ascend rivers into fresh water.

## Key Findings
- *Villarius pricei* adds the family Siluridae to Pacific-slope waters. It is the most northern freshwater catfish record on the Pacific slope.
- *Campostoma* is recorded for the first time from the west slope. *Campostoma pricei* Jordan & Thoburn is judged invalid and synonymized with *C. ornatum* (Rio Grande basin), despite the continental divide separating the basins. Five specimens (1¾–4 in.) came from Rucker Cañon, the type locality of *C. pricei*, and were compared with Rio Conchas, Chihuahua, material.
- *C. ornatum* has a distinctive gut arrangement. The alimentary canal folds anteroposteriorly below and beside the air-bladder, with only one fold on top. In *C. anomalum* from Ohio it winds around the air-bladder more than a dozen times.
- Other Yaqui and Sonora species: *Catostomus bernardini* (several poor specimens, 2½–3½ in.); *Leuciscus purpureus* (provisional and doubtful, four 1½ in. specimens); *Agosia chrysogaster* (numerous, largest about 3 in.); and *Poecilia occidentalis* (confirmed as a true *Poecilia*, not *Heterandria*, per Garman).
- *P. occidentalis* from Eisen: 33 specimens, of which 3 are adult males and 5–6 are probable immature males. The largest female was 1½ in. and the largest of Price's females was 2¼ in.
- In the Gila basin, Price also took *Leuciscus intermedius* and *P. occidentalis* from Sabino Cañon, and *Pantosteus clarki* and *A. chrysogaster* from the Rio San Pedro at Fairbank.
- Cape San Lucas collection (nine species, all well known; records of occurrence only; ten entries are listed): *Siphostoma starksi*, *Agonostomus nasutus*, *Mugil albula*, *M. curema*, *Eucinostomus californiensis*, *Philypnus lateralis*, *Dormitator maculatus*, *Eleotris pictus* (identical to *E. equidens* per Gilbert), *Awaous taiasica* and *Spheroides annulatus*.
- *Agonostomus nasutus*: a 4½ in. specimen from San José del Cabo differs slightly from the original description in eye size (3¾ in head) and fin proportions.
- *Poecilia occidentalis* was also collected at Tepic, with specimens not differing from Hermosillo material.
