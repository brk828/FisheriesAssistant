## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Marble Bluff Fish Facilities**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service installation located near Nixon, Nevada, administratively tied to the [[Lahontan National Fish Hatchery]]. Established in 1976 under the authority of the Bureau of Reclamation's Washoe Project, the facility's core mission is to **facilitate spawning migration** for two culturally and ecologically significant species endemic to the Lahontan Basin: the [[cui-ui sucker]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) and the [[Lahontan cutthroat trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*). Notably, the reporting period documented **zero fish passage** for both target species and **zero fish distribution**, indicating either a failed spawning run, a non-operational reporting year, or a facility passage failure — a critical data point for population managers.

## Background & Description

The source document is an administrative station profile and does not provide direct morphological data (e.g., length, weight, meristic counts) for either target species. However, taxonomic identity can be inferred:

- **Cui-ui sucker** (*Chasmistes cujus*): A large-bodied catostomid (sucker family), endemic solely to the [[Pyramid Lake]]/Truckee River system of Nevada. It is a lake-dwelling species that undertakes riverine spawning migrations.
- **Lahontan cutthroat trout** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*): A large inland subspecies of cutthroat trout, native to the endorheic Lahontan Basin, historically the largest cutthroat trout subspecies in North America.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The facility operates within the **Truckee River corridor**, approximately 2 miles north of Nixon, Nevada, serving as a critical fish passage node between [[Pyramid Lake]] (terminal lake) and upstream spawning habitat.
- **Ecosystem role**: Both target species are **anadromous-like lacustrine-riverine migrants** (adfluvial), residing in Pyramid Lake as adults and migrating upriver to spawn — analogous to anadromy but confined entirely to a closed (endorheic) basin rather than a marine system.
- **Conservation context**: The [[Washoe Project]] (Bureau of Reclamation, 1956) altered natural flow regimes in the Truckee-Carson river system, necessitating artificial fish passage infrastructure like Marble Bluff to sustain spawning runs disrupted by water diversion and dam construction.
- **Human interaction**: The site receives public visitation (estimated 765 visitors in the reporting year), indicating an educational/public outreach component alongside its conservation function.
