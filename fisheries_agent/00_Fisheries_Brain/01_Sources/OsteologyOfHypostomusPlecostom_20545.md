# Osteology of Hypostomus plecostomus and Phylogenetic Analysis of the Loricariid Subfamilies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #loricariidae

## Executive Summary

This monograph (Contributions in Science, No. 394, [[Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County]], 1987) provides the first comprehensive osteological description of a typical loricariid catfish, [[Hypostomus Plecostomus]], and uses it as the anatomical foundation for a cladistic analysis of the six subfamilies of the [[Loricariidae]] (Neotropical armored catfishes). The author finds a mosaic of primitive and derived traits in *H. plecostomus*, but no uniquely derived characters that diagnose either the genus [[Hypostomus]] or the subfamily [[Hypostominae]]. The family is supported as monophyletic by 16 synapomorphies, and the preferred tree is ((Hypoptopomatinae)((Loricariinae)(Ancistrinae + Hypostominae))). The analysis implies that the Hypostominae is paraphyletic, and the author retains [[Ancistrinae]] as a separate subfamily for nomenclatural stability.

## Background & Description

- **Study taxon:** *Hypostomus plecostomus* (Linnaeus), described from a specimen originally named *Acipenser plecostomus*. Boeseman (1968) designated a neotype and concluded that only coastal Surinam populations match the type description; many non-coastal Surinam specimens are probably misidentified.
- **Family context:** Loricariids include more than 600 described species in 70 genera and six subfamilies (Isbrücker, 1980), about 24% of the over 2200 described catfish species (Nelson, 1984). The loricarioid clade includes over 1100 species.
- **Genus context:** [[Hypostomus]] has over 120 described species and is the most speciose genus of the Hypostominae (16 genera).
- **Morphology:** The body is armored with bony dermal plates and odontodes (dermal teeth), and the mouth is ventral and sucker-like. Premaxillae and mandibles are highly mobile and cup-shaped, bearing replacement tooth rows. Skeletal preparations were cleared and double stained with alcian blue and alizarin red, and the osteology was compared with outgroup siluroids.
- **Materials:** Over 100 lots were examined from museum collections, including [[Field Museum of Natural History]], [[American Museum of Natural History]], [[California Academy of Sciences]], and the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (USNM). The principal illustrated specimen was RMNH 25463 (Surinam).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- *H. plecostomus* is restricted to coastal freshwater and brackish localities of Surinam and Guyana.
- Loricarioids occupy a variety of Neotropical freshwater habitats; the source gives sample localities such as the Rio Napo (Ecuador), Rio Madre de Dios (Peru), Rio Magdalena drainage (Colombia), and Orinoco drainage (Venezuela).
- The document does not report feeding ecology, reproduction, population dynamics or conservation status beyond the functional interpretation of morphology (e.g., the anterohyal is linked to feeding structures).

## Key Findings

**Osteology of *H. plecostomus***

- **Neurocranium:** Broadly triangular; the lateral ethmoid is strongly developed; the nasal capsule is fully enclosed; the parasphenoid forms a tall median ridge; the exoccipital and epioccipital are reduced or fused in characteristic ways; and the frontal does not reach the orbit.
- **Lateral-line system:** The infraorbital series has six separate canal-bearing platelets. The overall count across loricariids is 56 separate plates. The preopercular canal exits to the skin rather than entering the mandible.
- **Suspensorium:** Loricariids have lost the ecto-, endo- and mesopterygoids. The metapterygoid has a pterygoid channel. The palatine splint is interpreted as a sesamoid ossification, and the lachrimal is considered lost in callichthyids, astroblepids and loricariids.
- **Jaws and teeth:** The premaxillae bear 25–29 teeth in the emergent row. Boeseman (1968) reported an average of 25 teeth in both jaws. Teeth are Z-shaped and bifid, with a large rectangular major cusp and a small triangular minor cusp. There are about 20 rows of replacement teeth in the dentary of *Hypostomus* sp. (FMNH 95560).
- **Hyoid and branchial arches:** Four branchiostegal rays. Only the first ceratobranchial bears an accessory flange, which is unique to loricariids among siluroids. Gill rakers are replaced by comb-like epithelial structures.
- **Weberian apparatus and axial skeleton:** Pre-ural vertebral counts are 29–30 for *H. plecostomus* and 30–32 for other *Hypostomus*. The caudal skeleton shows extensive hypural fusion, with the parhypural fused to hypurals 1–2. There are 5+5 procurrent caudal rays and 8+8 principal caudal rays in hypostomines and ancistrines.
- **Fins:** The dorsal fin has one spinelet, one defensive spine and seven branched rays. The anal fin has four soft rays (first two unbranched) supported by four pterygiophores. The pectoral fin has six branched rays. The pelvic girdle has paired basipterygia and lateropterygia.

**Phylogenetic analysis**

- **Matrix and method:** A matrix of 30 characters was analysed with [[PAUP]] (Phylogenetic Analysis Using Parsimony, version 2.4.0). Over 50 equally parsimonious trees were found initially; the reduced matrix yielded one tree of 54 steps.
- **Consistency index:** 0.844, meaning about 16% of character-state changes are homoplastic.
- **Monophyly:** The Loricariidae is monophyletic on 16 synapomorphies. Among the ten derived characters listed in the Discussion, the first six include the metapterygoid contact with the lateral ethmoid, the metapterygoid channel, the canal plate, comb-shaped gill rakers, the accessory flange on the first ceratobranchial, and the expanded anterohyal.
- **Subfamily relationships:** Ancistrinae is monophyletic on four uniquely derived states. Ancistrinae plus Hypostominae is supported by eight characters. No characters diagnose the hypostomine genera apart from the Ancistrinae.
- **Paraphyly of the Hypostominae:** Because the Ancistrinae is apomorphic, the Hypostominae is paraphyletic. The author notes that traditional hypostomine diagnoses rely on plesiomorphic features (absence of cheek odontodes, non-depressed caudal peduncle, exposed pectoral girdle). He retains Ancistrinae as a subfamily for nomenclatural stability.
- **Placement of Lithogenes and Neoplecostomus:** These taxa were not included in the numerical analysis and were placed a posteriori. Eight or fewer branched pectoral-fin rays, an expanded anterohyal and a slender lateropterygium are treated as derived for loricariids. Neoplecostomus and all higher loricariids share an expanded connecting bone, elongate palatine, robust mesethmoid disk, asymmetrically bifid teeth and seven or fewer pectoral-fin rays. [[Lithogenes villosus]] is known from a single holotype (FMNH 52960) from Guyana.
- **Critique of previous classifications:** Regan (1904) and Gosline (1947) used characters without polarity assessment, and some of their diagnostic characters are shared with primitive catfishes. The author concludes that the expanded compressed neural and hemal spines and the sixth-centrum rib are not unique to loricariids.

**Conclusion**

The cladogram ((Hypoptopomatinae)((Loricariinae)(Ancistrinae + Hypostominae))) is the main result. Hypostomus remains without diagnostic derived features, so the osteological description of *H. plecostomus* is intended as a reference for future revision of the genus and for character polarity assessment in loricariid phylogenetics.
