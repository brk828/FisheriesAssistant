# Painted Rock Native Fish Survey and Interagency Coordination Letter

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence, dated November 2, 1992 (year partly garbled in the scan), is a letter from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] Native Fish Program Manager to The Nature Conservancy's Verde Valley/Eassayampa River Preserve. It reports an October 26 sampling effort at Painted Rock, Arizona, intended to determine whether [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] stocked in April 1989, along with [[Colorado River Squawfish]] stocked in February, had survived. No razorbacks or bonytails were captured. The letter concludes that survival of stocked endangered fish cannot be confirmed from this effort and proposes an interagency meeting to coordinate management under a [[Memorandum of Understanding]].

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Verify survival of endangered native fish stocked at Painted Rock.
- **Target species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Colorado River Squawfish]], a native fish whose scientific name is illegible in the scan.
- **Sampling team:** The letter's author, along with staff members Paul and Rob, identified only by first name in the text.
- **Gear:** Two 30 m x 2.? m trammel nets and one 100 m x 2.? m trammel net (dimension partly illegible), set at the site. [[Electrofishing]] was also used.
- **Agencies named:** [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), the three parties to the Memorandum of Understanding.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The author notes that the pond had low dissolved oxygen, which would make it unlikely that stocked razorbacks could have survived the summer.
- Squawfish are difficult to capture with trammel nets. They are usually taken by angling, and rarely by trammel nets or electrofishing, so the failure to collect them does not prove absence.
- The letter states that a significant proportion of endangered fish stocked into Painted Rock may not have survived the summer. The author treats this as an open question rather than a confirmed loss.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling result:** The effort collected one squawfish, reported as about 36 cm total length and roughly 4?? g (digits illegible in scan). Its exact measurements are uncertain.
- **Bycatch:** A total of 41 [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*) were collected.
- **Native fish sizes:** The letter reports a mean total length of about 35 mm for collected fish and a mean weight of 34 g (range values partly garbled in scan).
- **No stocked razorbacks or bonytails were captured.** The author notes that this does not prove they are absent, because low oxygen conditions and the trapping method may have affected capture.
- **Disposition:** All native fish were released back into the pond.
- **Recommendation:** The author proposes a meeting of representatives from the three agencies (Bureau of Reclamation, AGFD, USFWS) to review the status of the Painted Rock stocking and management of endangered fish. A tentative date was set for January, 10:00 hrs, at the Painted Rock site. The scan's year is garbled, though it appears to be 1993.
- **Distribution:** The letter was sent to participants in the Painted Rock program, including the Bureau of Reclamation, USFWS, TNC, the University of Arizona, Arizona State University, and the Center for Environmental Studies, to invite them to the meeting.
