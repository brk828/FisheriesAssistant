# Numbers of Mature Walleyes in Clear Lake, Iowa, 1952–1953, as Estimated by Tagging

#ichthyology #marine-biology #stizostedion

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Richard R Whitney]] estimated the number and harvest rate of [[Walleye]] in [[Clear Lake]], a eutrophic lake in [[Iowa]], using strap tags applied during the spring spawning runs of 1952 and 1953. A total of 2,232 walleyes was tagged. Mark-recapture estimates placed the spawning population at 12,776 fish in 1952 and 16,311 in 1953, with 12,119 of the 1953 spawners estimated to be males. The best whole-lake figures were 6,030 walleyes over 16 inches in 1952 and 30,822 walleyes over 12 inches in 1953 (about 8.5 per acre). Anglers returned 15.7% of tags in 1952 and 6.3% in 1953. Tag loss was high, at roughly 40% in a year, but had only a slight effect on the final estimates.

## Background & Description

The walleye was the most sought game fish in Clear Lake, but angling success varied from year to year. The study aimed mainly to estimate the number of walleyes in the lake and the rate at which they were harvested. The [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]] had been studying the lake's fishes since 1941.

Clear Lake covers 3,643 acres and is about 5 miles long and 2 miles wide at its east end. Its maximum depth is about 20 feet, and only 15% of the area exceeds 15 feet. Walleyes were captured during spring spawning runs on rocky shores at two field stations, [[Shady Beach]] and [[Bayside]], and at the state [[Clear Lake Hatchery]] in 1952.

Fish were marked with numbered monel strap or ear tags, mostly size 3 (7/16 inch) and some size 18 (1 inch) for larger fish. Tags were clamped around the upper premaxilla in most cases. Population estimates used [[Schnabel Estimate]] and [[Petersen Estimate]] methods, with confidence limits from Poisson and binomial distributions. Angler returns were gathered with the help of the [[Clear Lake Chamber of Commerce]], which offered prizes for reported tags, and a [[Creel Census]] by the Iowa State Conservation Commission supplied catch data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spawning behavior.** Tagged walleyes mixed well on the spawning grounds. Males stayed on the grounds longer than females, and some were still present 30 days after tagging. The author found no evidence that individual fish returned to particular spots. Recaptures of 14 walleyes released at the hatchery, 1.4 and 1.8 miles from the spawning areas, showed rapid return, with 3 caught within 1 day and 4 within 2 days. The distance between Bayside and Shady Beach is about 1.75 miles. Eight of 13 fish recaptured at Shady Beach had been tagged at Bayside.

**Sex ratio.** The spawning run was unusually prolonged in 1953 because cold weather may have delayed maturation. Only 142 of 1,431 tagged walleyes (about 9:1 male) were females, compared with 281 of 793 sexed fish (about 2:1) in 1952. Males were recaptured 59 times and females only 2 times. The total-population sex ratio appeared close to 1:1 by late summer.

**Post-spawning movement.** After spawning, angler returns shifted from the south-shore rock reefs to vegetated north-shore areas. Netting in June and July 1953 was most productive at night in these vegetated areas. Fish released at sunrise swam to about 2 feet of water and hid under algae. The authors interpreted this as cover-seeking behavior, consistent with the walleye's negative phototropism. Secchi readings averaged 7.3 feet in June and 5.3 feet in July 1951, and the vegetated zones in 7 to 10 feet of water were thought to supply enough shade.

**Size and maturation.** Walleyes under about 18 inches were underrepresented on the spawning grounds, probably because immature fish do not spawn. The percentage of fish tagged rose by about 10 percentage points for each 2-inch increase in length above 14 inches. Availability declined for fish over 22 inches, possibly because large females do not spawn every year.

**Mortality and harvest.** Annual mortality from 1952 to 1953 for the 1946–1948 year classes was estimated at about 35% overall. Mortality estimates by age were 29% for IV-year fish, 27% for V-year fish, and 56% for VI-year fish. Alternative approaches gave natural mortality estimates of 27.4% and 16.8% after subtracting harvest.

**Comparison with other Iowa lakes.** [[Rose]] estimated adult walleye density (over 13 inches) at about 7 per acre in [[Spirit Lake]] in 1954 and about 5 per acre in 1947, and about 21 per acre in [[Storm Lake]] in 1948. The present estimate of 8.5 walleyes per acre (over 12 inches) fell between these values.

## Key Findings

- **Tagging effort:** 2,232 walleyes were tagged in total (801 in 1952; 1,431 in 1953).
- **1952 spawning population:** 12,776 (95% CI 8,543–20,129), based on 23 recaptures from a catch of 1,371. The author considered this estimate unreliable but consistent with later figures.
- **1953 spawning population:** 16,311 (95% CI 12,707–20,947), based on 61 recaptures. Distortion was likely due to female emigration.
- **Male spawning population, 1953:** 12,119 (95% CI 8,790–17,242), using 983 tagged males and 37 male recaptures.
- **Petersen estimate, summer 1953:** 12,985 (95% CI 10,624–16,695) from 2-inch mesh nets. The experimental gill net Petersen estimate was 28,169 (95% CI 18,779–37,559).
- **Creel-based estimates:** 11,831 for 1952 (126 tag returns; 95% CI 8,207–14,053). The 1953 estimate of 17,179 was based on only 66 walleyes encountered, so it is weak.
- **Whole-lake estimate, 1952:** 6,030 walleyes over 16 inches (95% CI 2,984–13,456). Experimental gill net data were used for this estimate.
- **Whole-lake estimate, 1953:** 30,822 walleyes over 12 inches (about 8.5 per acre), obtained from combined samples by size group and corrected for tag loss. The abstract reports this as 30,820. The authors considered it the most accurate 1953 estimate. Over 16 inches, the estimate was 4,870.
- **Harvest rate:** 15.7% of tagged walleyes were returned by anglers in 1952 and 6.3% in 1953.
- **Tag loss:** About 40.3% (95% CI 27–55%) of 1952 tags were lost within a year. Other methods gave 50% (19–81%) and about 60%. Because sampling took place within four months of tagging, the correction changed the final estimate only slightly (from 35,173 to 30,822).
- **Age-structure estimate:** 20,647 walleyes of age III and older in the combined 1953 samples. The authors noted that this figure excludes age I and II fish and is therefore an underestimate.
- **Sex ratio:** The 1953 spawning run was about 9:1 male, while the late-summer total population was close to 1:1.
- **Methodological caution:** Size-selective sampling and unequal tagging across size groups required separate estimates for each size group. The authors recommended this approach over pooled estimates.
- **Year-to-year comparison:** The only directly comparable estimates are for walleyes over 16 inches. The 1953 population was probably similar to or somewhat smaller than the 1952 population, and the larger number of spawning males in 1953 was attributed to maturation of the abundant 1950 year class.
