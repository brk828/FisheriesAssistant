# Lake Mohave Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker Sampling: Camp Five Trip Report (March 1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from Sr. Staff Biologist [[Frank Baucom]] reports on Camp Five's part in an intensive, interagency sampling effort on [[Lake Mohave]] from March 5 to 13, 1988. The goal was to capture [[Bonytail Chub]] and tag [[Razorback Sucker]]. Camp Five worked the lower Lake Mohave reach from a base at [[Arrowhead Cove]]. The camp caught 281 fish across nine species, mostly by [[Trammel Net]] and [[Electrofishing]]. Only one bonytail chub was caught in the entire multi-camp effort. The memo also records steps taken to limit stress on razorback suckers and notes a possible angling threat to them.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** Arrowhead Cove, on the Arizona side about 2.25 river miles above [[Katherine Landing]]. The memo describes it as accessible by dirt road, well protected from wave action, rarely visited, and well stocked with razorback suckers.
- **Coordination:** The overall effort was coordinated by Drs. W. L. Minckley and P. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]], who had run this sampling for several years. They planned to provide a summary for all camps.
- **Participating agencies and groups:** [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Phoenix Field Office), University of Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit, [[Bureau of Land Management]] (Kingman Resource Area), Bureau of Reclamation (Arizona Projects Office), the U.S. Geological Survey (Tucson), the Arizona Game and Fish Department, [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], the Arizona Republic, and interested individuals. A participant list is attached to the memo (about 20 names).
- **Other camps:** Camp 1 (J. Brooks, Dexter National Fish Hatchery), Camp 2 (D. Hendrickson, Arizona Game and Fish), and Camp 4 (B. Burke, Bureau of Reclamation, Boulder City).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling methods:** Nets were run for 24 hours, and electrofishing was used in the cove. Portable radios supplied through BLM's Kingman Resource Area office improved safety and coordination.
- **Handling stress:** To avoid unnecessary handling, nine electrofished razorback suckers were held in a holding pen on the afternoon of March 12 until after the evening sample period. Two of these fish had been tagged earlier in the trip. All nine appeared to be swimming and functioning normally when released five hours later.
- **Potential threat:** A bow fisherman visited Arrowhead Cove during the week. No fish were seen being taken. The memo raises concern because the cove had very few carp, so razorback suckers could become an attractive daytime target.
- **Bonytail status:** It is still unknown whether the single bonytail chub caught was a wild or stocked individual. A memo from Buddy Jensen of Dexter National Fish Hatchery and a report on a bonytail seen underwater at Katherine Landing are attached.

## Key Findings

- **Catch totals:** The summary table gives a grand total of 281 fish, of which 212 were taken by trammel net and 69 by electrofishing. Per-species cells in the scanned table are partly illegible, so species-level counts are not reliably reproduced here.
- **Species caught:** Nine species appear in the table: bonytail chub, razorback sucker, rainbow trout, largemouth bass, channel catfish, carp, bluegill, striped bass, and green sunfish. Razorback sucker was the main target for tagging.
- **Bonytail chub:** Only one was caught across all camps. Its origin (wild or stocked) remains unresolved.
- **Razorback sucker welfare:** Holding and release of electrofished fish were managed to reduce handling stress, and the held fish recovered normally.
- **Emerging concern:** Recreational angling, specifically bow fishing, may threaten razorback suckers in low-carp coves like Arrowhead Cove. The memo recommends that this be watched.
- **Outcome and next steps:** The memo thanks participants, credits interagency cooperation for the camp's success, and hopes the coordinated Lakewide effort will continue.
