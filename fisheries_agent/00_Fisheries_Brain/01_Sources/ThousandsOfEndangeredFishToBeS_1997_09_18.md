# Thousands of Endangered Razorback Suckers Stocked in Gunnison River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #FreshwaterEcology #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary
In September 1997, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) stocked over 4,000 endangered [[Razorback Sucker|razorback suckers]] into the [[Gunnison River]] near Delta, Colorado. This effort, part of the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]], represents one of the largest single stocking events for this species in the upper [[Colorado River Basin]], scaling up significantly from previous efforts (approx. 600 fish stocked in 1993 and 1996). The initiative combines genetically-informed hatchery rearing with concurrent habitat modifications, including altered dam operations and fish passage infrastructure, to improve survival and long-term recovery prospects.

## Background & Description
- **Common Name Origin:** Named for the distinctive pointed, keel-like hump located on the dorsal surface (back) of the fish.
- **Size Class:** Stocked individuals measured **7 to 12 inches** in length.
- **Age:** Fish were approximately **1 year old** at the time of release (first-generation hatchery offspring).
- **Taxonomic Superlative:** Recognized as the **largest sucker species** native to North America.
- **Predation Avoidance Strategy:** Biologists specifically selected and reared fish to a size threshold considered "too large to be eaten" by most other predatory fish species currently present in the river system, a key strategy for reducing post-stocking mortality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]; found nowhere else on Earth. Inhabits large river channels.
- **Evolutionary History:** Believed to have evolved in the Colorado River Basin approximately **4 million years ago**.
- **Genetic Provenance:** The stocked fish were the offspring of wild adults originally captured from the [[Colorado River]] and [[Lake Powell]], ensuring the hatchery-raised population remains genetically representative of wild stock.
- **Habitat Management Interventions:**
  - **[[Blue Mesa Dam]] Operations:** Modified to provide increased water flow downstream in the Gunnison River during critical periods for endangered fish species.
  - **[[Redlands Fish Ladder]]:** Newly implemented infrastructure allowing migratory fish to bypass a dam, enabling both downstream movement and return migration upstream, restoring connectivity in the river system.

## Key Findings
- **Stocking Volume:** More than **4,000** razorback suckers were introduced into the Gunnison River by the program's target date (Sept. 30, 1997).
- **Historical Comparison:** This event represents a massive scale-up in recovery efforts—increasing from stocking "a few hundred" fish annually (600 total across 1993 and 1996) to "several thousand" in a single, concentrated effort.
- **Program Framework:** Facilitated by the availability of Grand Junction-area hatchery and grow-out facilities funded by the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]].
- **Strategic Timing:** Stocking occurred over several specific days across two weeks (beginning Sept. 12, with additional target dates of Sept. 19, 25, 29, and 30).
- **Expert Commentary:** Henry Maddux (USFWS Biologist, Program Director) noted the environment was "as healthy as we've had in years," attributing success to the combination of larger fish size, genetic diversity, and habitat improvements.
