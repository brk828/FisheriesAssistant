# A New Species of Moenkhausia from the Mato Grosso Region of Brazil (Pisces: Characidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

William L. Fink describes *Moenkhausia phaeonota*, a new characid fish from the Arinos River in the Mato Grosso region of Brazil, and uses it to question the adequacy of current characid classification. The species is named for its dusky brown color pattern. Its coloration closely resembles several [[Hyphessobrycon]] species, especially *H. metae*, yet it shares no unique characters with any recognized [[Moenkhausia]] species. Fink tentatively keeps it in *Moenkhausia* while proposing a relationship with a specialized [[Hyphessobrycon]] color-pattern group, pending a planned revision by Fink and Weitzman.

## Background & Description

- **Publication:** [[Breviora]], Museum of Comparative Zoology, Harvard University, No. 450, 21 February 1979.
- **Classification context:** The characid system dating from [[Eigenmann]] (1917) is described as obsolescent. Key diagnostic characters, particularly lateral line length, have proven inadequate to express relationships. Color pattern has been largely ignored by characid systematists.
- **Holotype:** MZUSP 13793, collected by [[Harald Schultz]] on 16 June 1962 from an island shore "below Rio dos Peixes," Rio Arinos; 32.6 mm SL.
- **Paratypes:** Ten lots in total (about 30 specimens) deposited across institutions including ANSP, BMNH, CAS, FMNH, MCZ, MZUSP, USNM and ZMA. The USNM lot alone contains 20 specimens (16.1–36.4 mm SL).
- **Morphology:**
  - Elongate, laterally compressed body.
  - Maxillary with 3–7 teeth; premaxillary inner row with 5–6 teeth. Tooth cusp counts change with growth, with adults usually showing more cusps.
  - Lateral line complete; 33–37 lateral scales (36–38 in some counts, with a mode of 36 or 37 in the table).
  - Anal fin with 22–25 branched rays (usually 22 or 23).
  - Caudal fin forked with 10/9 principal rays.
  - Vertebrae total 37–38.
- **Color in alcohol:** Light brown ground color; reticulate dark back; pale dorsolateral stripe; broad dark lateral stripe; dense humeral spot about two-thirds of eye diameter; dark middle caudal rays.
- **Etymology:** From Greek *phaios* (dusky brown) and *-nota* (having the quality of).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The type material was collected in the Jurena-Tapajós drainage, Rio Arinos, Mato Grosso. The text gives no data on habitat conditions, abundance, diet or behaviour. The comparative material spans the Amazonian and Orinoco-region basins (Brazil, Colombia, Peru, Bolivia and British Guiana), supporting a Neotropical freshwater distribution for the relatives examined.

## Key Findings

- *M. phaeonota* shares no derived characters with any examined *Moenkhausia* species. The closest phenetic match, *M. takesi*, differs in predorsal scales (11–13 vs. 9), lateral scales (36–38 vs. 33), anal-fin rays (22–25 vs. 18–19) and gill rakers (17–21 vs. 20–21).
- Preserved color pattern is nearly indistinguishable from *Hyphessobrycon metae* (Colombia). Meristic differences include anal-fin rays (22–25 vs. 17–19), vertebrae (37–38 vs. 33) and lateral scales (33–37 vs. about 33).
- Fink proposes a relationship with a group of [[Hyphessobrycon]] species (*H. metae*, *H. loretoensis*, *H. peruvianus*, and possibly *H. agulha* and *H. herbertaxelrodi*) that share a specialized color pattern: a dark lateral band with a bright dorsal lateral stripe in life.
- Final placement is deferred. The species stays in *Moenkhausia* for now, since the genus diagnosis is met, and Fink notes that the genus itself has questionable phylogenetic reality.
- A revision of this group is planned by Fink and [[Stanley H. Weitzman]], and will need redefinition of generic limits.
- The paper highlights a broader problem: lateral line length and color pattern cause conflicts in characid classification, and the [[Eigenmann System]] is no longer adequate.
- Comparative material includes about 20 holotypes and series from ANSP, CAS, FMNH, MCZ, USNM and other collections, spanning several Moenkhausia and Hyphessobrycon species.
