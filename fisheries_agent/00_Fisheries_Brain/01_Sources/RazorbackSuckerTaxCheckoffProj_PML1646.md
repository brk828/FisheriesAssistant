# Razorback Sucker Reintroduction to the Colorado River (1986 Department Memo)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memo from the California Department of Fish and Game's Endangered Species project, dated June 1986 (exact day partly illegible in the scan), announces the planned reintroduction of [[Razorback Sucker]] into the [[Colorado River]] in September of that year. The Department was raising the endangered fish at a hatchery, and the memo presents the release as a major milestone in species recovery. It asks the [[Public Affairs Office]] to run a full media campaign and to publicize the Department's [[Endangered Species Tax Check-Off]] program, which funds the work.

## Background & Description

- **Subject species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), identified from the common name in the memo; the scientific name is not stated in the document.
- **Status:** Described as endangered. The memo refers to the species' "historic range" in the Colorado River.
- **Program funding:** The memo refers to a "tax check-off" for the endangered species project, through which the public contributes to recovery efforts.
- **Source:** Scanned memo, with the first page largely illegible. The second page contains the substantive content.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The fish are to be released into the Colorado River, the species' historic range, as part of an effort to restore a native population.
- **Rearing method:** Fish were raised in captivity at a state-run warmwater hatchery before release. The hatchery name is partly illegible in the scan.
- **Release size:** The memo appears to give a size of about 8–10 inches for the released fish, but the OCR is ambiguous.
- **Timing:** Release planned for September 1986. The memo says the exact location and date would be confirmed by contacts.

## Key Findings

1. **Planned reintroduction:** The Department intended to release hatchery-raised razorback suckers into the Colorado River in September 1986, returning the species to its historic habitat.
2. **Milestone for recovery:** The memo calls the release "an extremely important milestone" in the recovery of this endangered fish.
3. **Public engagement:** The author argues that the program is "an excellent opportunity" to show the public what the tax check-off money has accomplished.
4. **Recommended action:** The author recommends that the [[Public Affairs Office]] mount a full media campaign to publicize the release.
5. **Points of contact:** Three Department staff are named for details on the release site and timing: Kim Nicol, Glenn Black, and Jim St. Amant. The memo is signed by Marlene McGriff of the Endangered Species project.
