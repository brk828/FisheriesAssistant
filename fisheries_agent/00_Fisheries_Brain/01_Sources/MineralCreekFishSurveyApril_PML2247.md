# Mineral Creek Fish Survey, April 21–22, 2008

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD) documents a backpack electrofishing and minnow-trap survey of upper [[Mineral Creek]], a tributary of the [[Gila River]] in Arizona, conducted April 21–22, 2008. The survey had three objectives: document the upstream distribution of nonnative fishes, assess the abundance and distribution of the [[Longfin Dace]] population, and attempt to capture [[Gila Chub]]. No Gila chub were captured or observed, and the authors concluded that the species is very likely extirpated from upper Mineral Creek. Nonnative fishes were confined to the lowest 1.3 km above [[Big Box Dam]] reservoir, where a series of [[Waterfalls]] acts as a barrier. Longfin dace were abundant and reproducing throughout the surveyed reaches, including areas above the falls where they had been stocked in 2006. The authors recommend a thorough survey of [[Devils Canyon]] and lower Mineral Creek, and, if Gila chub are not found, repatriation with a different lineage.

## Background & Description

Mineral Creek is impounded by Big Box Dam, constructed in 1921 just upstream of the [[ASARCO Ray Mine]]. Upper Mineral Creek and its tributaries originate in Pinal County on Tonto National Forest and drain the Pinal Mountains. Perennial flow extends roughly 7 km, from the reservoir up to the [[Government Springs Ranch]] near the confluence with [[Lyons Canyon]]. Above the mine, the watershed divides into Devils Canyon to the west and Mineral Creek to the east.

Seven fish species have been documented in Mineral Creek and its tributary Devils Canyon:

- [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), a native cyprinid, historically present
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), a native cyprinid
- [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), nonnative cyprinid
- [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), nonnative centrarchid
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), nonnative poeciliid
- [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*), nonnative ictalurid
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), native catostomid

Prior surveys (1967, 1997, 2000, 2002, 2006, and 2007) documented these fishes at varying sites. Earlier work captured Gila chub above the mine and below Big Box Dam in 1993, but no Gila chub were found in four subsequent surveys beginning in 2000.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods.** Fish were sampled with a Smith Root LR-24 backpack electrofisher (one probe, Quick-Setup parameters, increased voltage to 200 for deep pools) and Duraframe dip nets. Four collapsible minnow traps (0.85 m long, 0.3 m diameter, 9 mm mesh) baited with dog food were set in deep pools resembling potential Gila chub habitat, in canyon-bound reaches 14 and 15, for about 6–7 hours. Only longfin dace were measured for length, up to 100 individuals. Reach endpoints and waterfalls were located with a Garmin GPSmap 60csx, and distances were mapped in National Geographic TOPO! software.

**Physicochemical conditions** (measured April 22, 2008, at 11:15 am, end of reach 9):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Water temperature | 17.6 °C |
| pH | 8.59 |
| Salinity | 288 ppm |
| Total dissolved solids | 406 mg/L |
| Conductivity | 578 µS |
| Dissolved oxygen | 8.65 mg/L (91.0% saturation) |

Water was clear enough to see the bottom of the deepest pools. Filamentous algae (probably *Spirogyra*) was widespread and most abundant in the two uppermost reaches, where the canyon opens and riparian vegetation thins. Stream width was typically about 2 m, depth under 30 cm, and the deepest pools about 1.5 m.

**Habitat by reach.** Reaches 1–2 lay within the old lakebed (silt and sand, runs and riffles). Reach 3 was sand-dominated runs and riffles and was not shocked (382 m). Reach 4 had cobble riffles and pools. Reach 5 held three closely spaced waterfalls (0.7–1.5 m tall). Reaches 6–12 were riffle-dominated cobble reaches with increasing cascades. Reaches 13–16 were canyon-bound, with more cascades and plunge pools, and boulders and cobble with sand-filled pools. Reaches 17–18 had more riffles and cobble and sand substrates.

**Barriers.** Three waterfalls in reach 5 limited fathead minnow and green sunfish to downstream sections. Longfin dace occurred above and below these falls and up to, but not above, a waterfall in reach 18.

## Key Findings

**Survey effort and catch**
- Reaches spanning roughly 6.5–6.9 km from Big Box Dam reservoir were sampled. All reaches except reach 3 were shocked, and fish were captured in each reach except reach 1.
- Total catch: 1,412 longfin dace, 20 green sunfish, and 11 fathead minnow. Three species were captured.
- Mean CPUE (fish per minute shocked) in reaches 1, 2, 4, and 5: green sunfish 0.35 ± 0.34; fathead minnow 0.24 ± 0.29; longfin dace 0.53 ± 0.68.
- Mean longfin dace CPUE in the 13 reaches above reach 5 was 7.50 (SD ~7.9). Catch rates generally rose upstream, peaked in reach 13, and declined through reaches 17–18.
- Minnow traps: no fish in the furthest upstream and downstream traps. One longfin dace was caught in the downstream-middle trap (0.16 fish/hour) and two in the upstream-middle trap (0.29 fish/hour).

**Longfin dace population**
- Thousands of young-of-year longfin dace (10–20 mm TL) were seen in every reach except reach 1. The authors interpreted the size structure as indicating a well-established population.
- Longfin dace were stocked above the falls near Lyons Canyon in 2006 (149 fish in August, 140 fish in October). Numerous young-of-year observed in October 2006 indicated that the August stock had spawned, and the population has since reproduced.

**Nonnative fishes**
- Nonnative fishes (green sunfish, fathead minnow, mosquitofish, black bullhead) were found only within the lowest 1.3 km above Big Box Dam reservoir.
- A waterfall series about 1.3 km upstream limits green sunfish and fathead minnow distribution.

**Gila chub status**
- No Gila chub were captured or observed. This is the fourth consecutive survey without the species.
- The authors state, with high confidence, that Gila chub are likely extirpated from upper Mineral Creek. They may persist in Devils Canyon or lower Mineral Creek below Big Box Dam, which were not adequately surveyed.

**Drought hypothesis**
- It was hypothesized that the earlier fishless reaches (2002 and 2006) resulted from stream drying. 2002 was the driest year in the last century in Superior and Globe. However, the stream had flow during the May and September 2002 surveys, and rain fell in July–September 2002, so drying was considered only a partial explanation.

**Habitat suitability**
- Reaches 5–18, especially canyon-bound reaches 13–16, appear suitable for Gila chub, with abundant plunge pools.
- Reaches 5–13 also provide habitat for [[Loach Minnow]], with low-gradient riffles and cobble substrates.
- Habitat in reaches 5–18 looked suitable for [[Desert Pupfish]] and the desert sucker, though neither has been recorded in this section.

**Recommendations**
1. Conduct a thorough survey of Devils Canyon and Mineral Creek below Big Box Dam to confirm Gila chub extirpation.
2. If upper Mineral Creek is managed for native fishes, consider repatriating Gila chub and stocking other native species.
3. If no Gila chub are found, consider introducing another lineage (probably from [[Redfield Canyon]] or [[Bonita Creek]]). Stocking a different lineage would avoid hybridization with any remnant population in lower Mineral Creek or Devils Canyon.
4. To eliminate nonnative fishes below the waterfalls, the authors considered chemical treatment (rotenone) of the reservoir, Devils Canyon, and the lower 1.3 km of upper Mineral Creek, followed by restocking with native species. Removal was judged feasible only in limited areas, and the authors noted that native fishes would likely recolonize the fishless reaches on their own.
