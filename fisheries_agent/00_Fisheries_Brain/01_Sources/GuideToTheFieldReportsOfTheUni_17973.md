# Guide to the Field Reports of the United States Fish and Wildlife Service, circa 1860-1961

#ornithology #mammalogy #wildlife-survey

## Executive Summary

This is a finding aid (Guide Number 4) to the field reports of the [[United States Fish and Wildlife Service]] and its predecessor, the [[Bureau of Biological Survey]]. The collection is Record Unit 7176, measures 17.2 linear meters, and is held by the [[Smithsonian Archives]] at the [[Smithsonian Institution]] in Washington, D.C. The guide was compiled by William E. Cox per the title page; the introduction is signed by William W. Moss (August 1986).

The collection documents field research by staff naturalists, mainly biological surveys of U.S. states and regions, with substantial material from Canada and Mexico and smaller amounts from South and Central America, Africa, Europe, Asia, and Antarctica. The document is an inventory, not a research study, so it contains no fish species data, sample sizes, or aquatic measurements.

## Background & Description

**Historical origin.** The agency traces its roots to [[C. Hart Merriam]] and the old Bureau of Biological Survey. Key institutional steps, as given in the Historical Note:

- **1885:** Congress established an ornithology section in the Division of Entomology, Department of Agriculture, to study the relationship of birds and agriculture. Private bodies, including the [[American Ornithologists' Union]], could no longer fund the work.
- **1886:** The section became an independent Division of Ornithology.
- **1888:** Mammals were added, and the division was renamed the Division of Economic Ornithology and Mammalogy.
- **1896:** Renamed the Division of Biological Survey, based on Merriam's concept of [[Life Zones]].
- **1905:** Became a Bureau within the Department of Agriculture.
- **1939:** Transferred to the Department of the Interior.
- **1940:** Combined with the Bureau of Fisheries to form the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

**Scope of holdings.** Reports are mainly submitted to the Chief of the Bureau or the responsible division. Three report types dominate:

- **Special reports:** notes on birds and mammals, covering observation, identification, distribution, and collecting.
- **Physiography reports:** climate, topography, bodies of water, and other natural features.
- **Plant reports:** trees, vegetation, and plant life.

Other material includes project reports (predator control, faunal distribution and migration, parks and refuges, species introductions, wildlife management, environmental impact, wildlife disease, bird banding, and fur-bearer studies), photographs, maps, correspondence, newspaper clippings, and publications.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collection is oriented toward terrestrial and avian fauna and their habitats. Aquatic content is limited to general mentions of "bodies of water" within physiography reports. The document names no fish species, aquatic invertebrates, plankton, or water chemistry data.

The guide also records surveys of ecological and management questions, including:

- Mule deer and big game conditions, notably the [[Kaibab Deer]] on the [[Kaibab Plateau]] in [[Grand Canyon National Park]] and the Grand Canyon National Game Preserve
- Predatory animal control and porcupine control experiments
- Game refuge and prospective antelope refuge surveys in Arizona
- Studies of the [[Western White-Winged Dove]] in Arizona, Sonora, and Mexico
- Rodent and kangaroo rat studies, including range damage from prairie dogs
- Mountain sheep protection in Arizona

Regional examples include Arizona, New Mexico, and Mexican states such as Chihuahua, Sinaloa, Sonora, and Zacatecas.

## Key Findings

- **Size and access:** 17.2 linear meters of records, open to researchers in the Archives, Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., excluding federal holidays. Inquiries by mail or phone (202-357-1420) are accepted.
- **Organization:** The guide has four series:
  - **Series 1:** Surveys in the United States (boxes 1-112), arranged alphabetically by state from Alabama to Wyoming.
  - **Series 2:** Surveys in Canada (boxes 113-122).
  - **Series 3:** Surveys in the Caribbean, Mexico, Central America, and South America (boxes 123-132).
  - **Series 4:** Surveys in other countries and general reports (box 132).
- **Largest state holdings:** Arizona has the most detailed listing, spanning roughly boxes 21-32, with many reports by [[Edward Alphonso Goldman]], [[Walter P. Taylor]], and [[Edward William Nelson]]. Texas (boxes 92-96) and Washington (boxes 99-105) are also substantial.
- **Mexico:** Reports cover Chihuahua, Coahuila, Colima, Sinaloa, Sonora, Yucatán, and Zacatecas, with notable items such as the Sonora white-winged dove study and a list of birds collected at Hacienda Mirador, Vera Cruz, between 1860 and 1868.
- **Key personnel:** Principal contributors include [[Vernon Orlando Bailey]], [[Hartley H. T. Jackson]], [[Johnson Andrew Neff]], Ernest R. Tinkham, and Alfred Brazier Howell.
- **Photographs:** The list of illustrations includes Merriam (1919), Bailey, Merriam, Theodore S. Palmer, and Albert K. Fisher on the Death Valley Expedition (June 13, 1891), Alexander Wetmore at Boulder Basin, Wyoming (July 16, 1910), and J. Paul Miller measuring a porcupine at Pillsbury Refuge, New Hampshire (July 1937).
- **Relevance to aquatic research:** Researchers seeking fish or aquatic data will find little here. Only physiography reports touch on water bodies, and the guide itself lists no aquatic taxa.
