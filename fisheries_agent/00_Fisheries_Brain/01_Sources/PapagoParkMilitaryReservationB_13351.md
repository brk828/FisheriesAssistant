# Papago Park Military Reservation Biological Assessment

#terrestrial-ecology #biological-assessment #desert-ecosystem #herpetology #mammalogy #ornithology #botany

## Executive Summary

This biological assessment was conducted 8–18 July 1994 for the [[Papago Park Military Reservation]] (PPMR) in Phoenix, Arizona, in support of an environmental assessment for proposed construction of a consolidated maintenance facility by the Arizona Army National Guard. The survey documented 36 native bird species, 13 native mammal species, 8 reptile species, and extensive native flora characteristic of [[Sonoran Desertscrub]]. Two federally listed endangered species ([[American Peregrine Falcon]], [[Lesser Long-nosed Bat]]) and several candidate species (C2) were evaluated for potential occurrence. Evidence of [[Desert Tortoise]] (C2) was found via skeletal remains and anecdotal reports. [[Common Chuckwalla]] (C2) was confirmed present on buttes. [[Loggerhead Shrike]] (C2) was observed and considered locally common. The study ranked habitats by sensitivity: buttes and bajadas (highest diversity), terraces with creosote flats, washes, low-relief desertscrub, and previously developed areas (lowest diversity). Recommendations emphasize avoiding modification of buttes and bajadas to protect sensitive reptile and bat species.

## Background & Description

The PPMR is located in Phoenix, Arizona (T2N, R4E, Secs. 32 & 33; T1N, R4E, Secs. 4 & 5), with additional survey coverage on adjacent City of Phoenix property south of Moreland Road. Topography ranges from weathered buttes and bajadas composed of Tertiary conglomerate and sedimentary rock with occasional Precambrian granitic outcrops, to extensive terraces of Quaternary colluvium-alluvium with strongly calichified areas. The site is intersected by several desert washes. Roughly one third of the PPMR lies south of McDowell Road and two thirds north; the western half is more developed, with landscaped areas using native and non-native drought-tolerant vegetation.

The vegetative community is classified as [[Sonoran Desertscrub]] – Arizona upland subdivision: Paloverde-cacti-mixed scrub series. Predominant vegetation includes [[Foothill Paloverde]] (*Cercidium microphyllum*), [[Velvet Mesquite]] (*Prosopis velutina*), [[Ironwood]] (*Olneya tesota*), [[Creosote Bush]] (*Larrea tridentata*), [[Triangle-leaf Bursage]] (*Ambrosia deltoidea*), [[Brittle Bush]] (*Encelia farinosa*), [[California Buckwheat]] (*Eriogonum deflexum*), [[Desert Lavender]] (*Hyptis emoryi*), [[Buckhorn Cholla]] (*Opuntia acanthocarpa*), [[Barrel Cactus]] (*Ferrocactus acanthodes*), [[Engelmann Hedgehog Cactus]] (*Echinocereus engelmannii*), and [[Saguaro]] (*Carnegiea gigantea*). Several plant species exhibited heavy browsing damage by rabbits and rodents, particularly *Encelia farinosa* and *Cercidium microphyllum*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area lies within the [[Sonoran Desert]] ecosystem, characterized by high biodiversity and specialized adaptations to arid conditions. Consultations with nearby natural areas (Phoenix Parks, Phoenix Zoo, Desert Botanical Gardens) identified one additional plant species of special concern: [[Arizona Queen of the Night]] (*Peniocereus greggii*), a cryptic cactus not observed during the survey but potentially present. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service provided a list of eight potentially occurring threatened, endangered, or candidate species; the Arizona Game and Fish Department and Desert Botanical Garden added two more.

Mammal surveys included live trapping (400 trap nights over 4 days), mist netting for bats, and sign surveys. Bat sampling was unsuccessful and requires additional effort. Bird surveys were conducted during crepuscular and daylight hours; 36 native species were identified. Reptile and amphibian surveys involved active searching of appropriate habitats and examination of shed skins and skeletal remains. No amphibians were observed; eight reptile species were identified.

Relative habitat sensitivity ranking (highest to lowest):
1. **Buttes and associated bajadas**: Greatest species diversity, lowest impact north of McDowell Road.
2. **Terraces with creosote flats**: Extensively used by birds and mammals, relatively low local impact.
3. **Washes and associated vegetation**: Heavily utilized by wildlife; modification risks erosion and structural damage.
4. **Low-relief desertscrub and grasses**: Associated with previous disturbance, relatively low diversity.
5. **Areas of previous development**: Lowest native species diversity and utilization.

## Key Findings

### Flora
- Native plant species observed and expected: extensive list including families Cactaceae, Leguminosae, Compositae, and others (see Table 1 in original).
- *Peniocereus greggii* (Arizona Queen of the Night): Not observed but may occur due to cryptic nature. Chiefly occurs under trees and shrubs on flats, along washes, and occasionally on hillsides. **Recommendation**: Closely scrutinize areas under shrubs/trees before removal.

### Mammals
- 13 native mammals identified; up to 23 species may occur.
- **[[Desert Cottontail Rabbit]]** (*Sylvilagus audubonii*), **[[Black-tailed Jackrabbit]]** (*Lepus californicus*), **[[Harris' Antelope Squirrel]]** (*Ammospermophilus harrisii*), **[[Round-tailed Ground Squirrel]]** (*Spermophilus tereticaudis*), **[[Botta's Pocket Gopher]]** (*Thomomys bottae*), **[[Desert Pocket Mouse]]** (*Perognathus penicillatus*), **[[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]]** (*P. baileyi*), **[[Cactus Mouse]]** (*Peromyscus eremicus*), **[[White-throated Wood Rat]]** (*Neotoma albigula*), **[[Coyote]]** (*Canis latrans*), **[[Gray Fox]]** (*Urocyon cinereoargenteus*), **[[Western Pipistrelle]]** (*Pipistrellus hesperus*) confirmed.
- **[[Lesser Long-nosed Bat]]** (*Leptonycteris curasoae*) – Endangered: Two specimens recorded from Phoenix. Cave/mine dweller, feeds on nectar/pollen from saguaros and agaves. Suitable roost sites may be present in buttes; low forage density suggests unlikely occurrence. **Recommendation**: No modification of buttes; additional bat surveys required.
- **[[California Leaf-nosed Bat]]** (*Macrotus californicus*) – C2: Recorded near Phoenix. Cave/mine dweller, year-round desertscrub inhabitant. Potential roost sites in buttes. **Recommendation**: No modification of buttes; additional bat surveys required.
- **[[Arizona Pocket Mouse]]** (*Perognathus amplus*) – C2: Not detected during sampling but may occur. Principally a sparse desertscrub species. **Recommendation**: Restrict disturbance to immediate construction area; revegetate with native plants at similar densities.

### Birds
- 36 native birds identified.
- **[[Loggerhead Shrike]]** (*Lanius ludovicianus*) – C2: Observed and locally common. Four to six individuals identified (2 pairs south of McDowell Road, 1 pair north). Primarily utilized terraced creosote flats and occasionally bajadas/buttes. **Recommendation**: Restrict vegetation disturbance to immediate construction area; revegetate with native plants at similar densities.
- **[[American Peregrine Falcon]]** (*Falco peregrinus*) – Endangered: Sighted in adjacent urban areas; nesting pair in downtown Phoenix. No evidence of occurrence on PPMR; may occasionally move through as migrant. **Recommendation**: None proposed.
- **[[Ferruginous Hawk]]** (*Buteo regalis*) – C2: Primarily grassland/prairie inhabitant; not expected except perhaps during migration. **Recommendation**: None proposed.
- **[[Harris' Hawk]]** (*Parabuteo unicinctus*) – Sensitive (USDA Forest Service): Not observed; may occur occasionally as migrant. Not federally or state listed. **Recommendation**: None proposed.
- Other notable species: **[[Gambel's Quail]]** (*Callipepla gambelii*), **[[Mourning Dove]]** (*Zenaida macroura*), **[[White-winged Dove]]** (*Zenaida asiatica*), **[[Roadrunner]]** (*Geococcyx californianus*), **[[Great Horned Owl]]** (*Bubo virginianus*), **[[Burrowing Owl]]** (*Athene cunicularia*), **[[Gila Woodpecker]]** (*Melanerpes uropygialis*), **[[Cactus Wren]]** (*Campylorhynchus brunneicapillus*), **[[Curve-billed Thrasher]]** (*Toxostoma curvirostre*), **[[Mockingbird]]** (*Mimus polyglottos*), **[[Common Raven]]** (*Corvus corax*).

### Reptiles and Amphibians
- 8 reptile species identified; no amphibians observed.
- **[[Desert Tortoise]]** (*Xerobates agassizii*, syn. *Gopherus agassizii*) – C2: Evidence confined to skeletal remains (right abdominal, femoral, anal plastron elements) on north side of Barnes Butte and anecdotal reports of recent sightings. Barrett (1990) found average home range of 19 hectares (47.5 acres) in Arizona, restricted to mountain slopes, bajadas, and washes; no use of lower creosote/bursage areas. Small area of suitable habitat suggests viable population unlikely; observed tortoises may be remnants or escaped/released captives. **Recommendation**: No modification of buttes or adjacent bajadas to avoid negative impact.
- **[[Common Chuckwalla]]** (*Sauromalus obesus*) – C2: Present on PPMR buttes north and south of McDowell Road. Primarily occupies rocky structure with cracks/crevices for cover; occasionally moves onto nearby flats to feed. **Recommendation**: No modification of buttes.
- Other species: **[[Couch's Spadefoot Toad]]** (*Scaphiopus couchii*) – expected based on distribution/habitat; occurrence dependent on standing water after summer rains. **[[Zebra-tailed Lizard]]** (*Callisaurus draconoides*), **[[Desert Iguana]]** (*Dipsosaurus dorsalis*), **[[Side-blotched Lizard]]** (*Uta stansburiana*), **[[Western Whiptail]]** (*Cnemidophorus tigris*), **[[Coachwhip]]** (*Masticophis flagellum*), **[[Ground Snake]]** (*Sonora semiannulata*), **[[Gopher Snake]]** (*Pituophis melanoleucus*) confirmed.

### Species of Special Concern Definitions
- **E (Endangered)**: Any species in danger of extinction throughout all or significant portion of its range.
- **C2 (Candidate Category 2)**: Taxa for which USFWS has sufficient information on vulnerability/threats to potentially list; further research needed.
- **SC (State Candidate)**: Species for which threats are known/suspected but substantial population declines not documented.
- **S (Sensitive)**: Classified as sensitive by Regional Forester on USDA Forest Service lands.

### Summary of Recommendations
1. **Buttes and bajadas**: No modification to protect [[Desert Tortoise]], [[Common Chuckwalla]], and potential bat roost sites ([[Lesser Long-nosed Bat]], [[California Leaf-nosed Bat]]).
2. **Creosote flats and terraces**: Restrict disturbance to immediate construction area; revegetate with native plants at similar densities to minimize impact on [[Loggerhead Shrike]] and [[Arizona Pocket Mouse]].
3. **Washes**: Avoid modification to prevent erosion and damage to structures; maintain natural drainage.
4. **[[Arizona Queen of the Night]]**: Scrutinize areas under shrubs/trees before removal.
5. **Bat surveys**: Additional efforts required to definitively identify bat species present.
