# Biological Opinion: Effects of Kellner Jacks and Riprap on Woundfin and Virgin River Chub, Beaver Dam Wash, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) biological opinion, dated July 22, 1996, assesses the effects of a proposed [[Natural Resources Conservation Service]] (NRCS) bank stabilization project in [[Beaver Dam Wash]], Mohave County, Arizona. The project would place two lines of [[Kellner Jacks]] and a section of [[Riprap]] under the [[Emergency Watershed Protection]] (EWP) program, following March 1995 high-water damage. The listed species are the endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and the endangered [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*), the latter with proposed critical habitat in the mainstem Virgin River.

The Service concluded the action is **not likely to jeopardize** either species and that it would not destroy or adversely modify proposed critical habitat. Take is expected to arise mainly from increased sedimentation and from the loss of individuals if water must be diverted from the work site. Three Reasonable and Prudent Measures (RPMs) with terms and conditions were issued. The opinion also stresses that the project is a piecemeal action within a degraded watershed, with long-term uncertainty about its effects.

## Background & Description

**Consultation history.** Flooding in January 1993 and March 1995 damaged banks near the Draper residence, the Beaver Dam Resort golf course, and the Highway 91 bridge. The NRCS requested consultation on August 21, 1995, and additional information was received May 17, 1996.

**Proposed action.**
- Kellner jack lines on both banks downstream of the Highway 91 bridge, anchored with concrete blocks. Each jack is three angle-iron legs, spaced on eight-foot centers, with the west/south line roughly 570 feet long.
- Approximately 405 feet of rock riprap on the east/north bank downstream of the new jacks, placed in a toe trench five feet wide and five feet deep with geotextile fabric.
- Planting of cottonwood and willow. No trees would be removed.
- Work is planned on dry sand, but a diversion may be needed if the stream runs next to the bank. The BE did not describe any diversion.

**Action area.** Beaver Dam Wash flows from Nevada through Utah to the Virgin River. It is perennial but may dewater at low flows. Below the project it is public land managed by the Bureau of Land Management, and it includes a Area of Critical Environmental Concern. The wash supports riparian cottonwood-willow forests, wetlands, and a fish fauna of both native and introduced species.

**Management history.** Channel work in 1951, the late 1970s, and 1989 included unpermitted golf-course realignment and channelization without a Section 404 permit, plus three diversion dams. A 1993 remediation plan and a 1994 Kellner jack opinion (file 2-21-94-F-388) preceded the 1995 flood damage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Woundfin.** A small silver minnow, rarely over 7.5 cm, with a sharp dorsal spine. It is known only from the Virgin River drainage. Adults and juveniles use runs and quiet waters near riffles. Larvae use shallow margins, often with filamentous algae. Spawning occurs April to July, with some August spawning. Survival fell at flows below 200 cfs in one study. Mean monthly flows of 800 cfs or higher during reproduction reduced recruitment. Winter mortality averaged about 30 percent. [[Red Shiner]] competition and habitat degradation continue to reduce populations.

**Virgin River chub.** A silvery minnow of 20 to 45 cm. It occupies deep runs and pools with cover and is known only from the Virgin and Moapa river drainages. Young fish are predaceous on macroinvertebrates, and older fish eat algae and debris. Spawning occurs April to June. The Moapa population is not ESA-listed.

**Threats.** Historic water diversion, reservoir construction, channelization, and fragmentation by dams have degraded habitat. Both species' populations have declined since the 1980s. The status of each species is described as precarious.

**Other species.** The [[Peregrine Falcon]] and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] were noted as special-status species in the area. The Service expressed concern about [[Desert Tortoise]] habitat near the riprap quarry site, since vehicle access through occupied habitat could cause take.

## Key Findings

- **Jeopardy determination:** Not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of woundfin or Virgin River chub. No destruction or adverse modification of proposed critical habitat.
- **Direct effects:** Low, because individuals are unlikely to be in the dry construction area. Risk rises sharply if water must be diverted.
- **Take:** Not quantifiable. A surrogate trigger was set: one or more dead individuals observed at the site, or erosion, deposition, or damage linked to the project after high-water events.
- **Cumulative and baseline effects:** The project treats local symptoms of cumulative channel change but does not address the wash as a whole. The Service called this piecemeal management "perhaps the most important indirect effect."
- **Unknowns:** The cause of the earlier jack failure, downstream effects of armoring, effects of jacks on high-flow passage, and survival of new riparian plantings, which the 1995 flood previously removed.
- **Reasonable and Prudent Measures:**
  - RPM 1: Review the effects of the project on flows and erosion, and prefer solutions that do not maintain the degraded condition.
  - RPM 2: Implement a sediment control plan. Before any diversion, install barriers, survey the reach, and remove native fish. Keep diverted reaches short. Prevent contaminated water from returning to the wash.
  - RPM 3: Coordinate with the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] on watershed-scale needs, and submit a report within three months of completion.
- **Reinitiation triggers:** Exceeding the incidental take level, new information about effects, modified actions, or new listings. Operations causing excess take must cease pending reinitiation.
- **Conservation recommendations:** None issued.
