# Redrock Canyon Action Plan - Coronado National Forest

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-topminnow

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Forest Service letter, signed by District Ranger [[Erry A. Deiter]] of the [[Sierra Vista Ranger District]], updates interested parties on the proposed Redrock Canyon Action Plan in the [[Canelo Hills]] area of the [[Coronado National Forest]]. The plan aims to improve vegetation condition along the streamside and across the approximately 17,400-acre [[Redrock Canyon Watershed]]. Five alternatives were analyzed, including a [[No Action Alternative]]. The Forest Service's preferred option is [[Alternative 1 - Intensive Resource Management]]. Public comments were requested by June 4 (the letter's date is partly illegible in the scan; 5/90 appears on the maps).

## Background & Description

**Purpose and mission.** The project seeks to improve vegetation condition near the streamside and throughout the watershed. The Forest Service mission is to manage resources under [[Multiple Use and Sustained Yield]] principles.

**Issues and concerns identified** from an earlier solicitation of public input:
- Presence of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Sanborn's Long-Nosed Bat]]
- A road in the canyon bottom
- Impacts of dispersed recreation and [[Livestock Grazing]]
- Poor vegetation condition in the canyon bottom and upper slopes
- Rancher concerns about maintaining livelihood and accepting management changes
- Possible route for the [[Arizona Trail]]
- Continued access, water permanence, and effects of flooding
- Possible mining activities
- Prehistoric and historic sites
- [[Mearn's Quail]] habitat requirements
- Monitoring of any management implemented

**Map references.** The letter refers to a Red Rock Canyon Project Area map (scale 1:126,720) and Redrock Canyon Watershed maps showing the [[Kunde Allotment]], [[Seibold Allotment]], [[Crittenden Allotment]], [[Papago Allotment]], and [[San Rafael Allotment]], plus the Arizona Trail route.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Listed and sensitive species:** The Gila topminnow is described as endangered. Sanborn's long-nosed bat and Mearn's quail habitat needs are also flagged.
- **Riparian condition:** Streamside vegetation is a central concern. [[Riparian Exclosure]] fencing and optional riparian tree plantings are proposed to restore it.
- **Hydrology and erosion:** Concerns include water permanence and flooding. Four [[Gully Control]] projects are proposed in all alternatives to reduce sediment yield and increase water infiltration.
- **Land use pressures:** Dispersed recreation, roads in the canyon bottom, and livestock grazing are identified as impacts. Sensitive riparian areas are to be avoided by trail construction.
- **Cultural resources:** Prehistoric and historic sites are present in the area.

## Key Findings

**Alternatives considered**

1. **[[Alternative 1 - Intensive Resource Management]] (preferred):** Maintains current range allotments with new improvements and livestock numbers set by landscape goals, proper grazing levels, and periods of use.
   - Three riparian exclosures (6.75 miles of fence)
   - Additional pasture fencing (7.8 miles)
   - Three new water developments and improvement of six existing ones
   - Three miles of road closure in the bottom of Redrock Canyon
   - One mile of road improvement to maintain access
   - Optional riparian tree plantings
2. **[[Alternative 2 - Intensive Management with Ranch Combination]]:** Includes the same construction as Alternative 1. Livestock operations on the Kunde and Seibold allotments (two of five) would be combined.
3. **[[Alternative 3 - Current Management with Reduced Grazing]]:** Reduces cattle numbers on all watershed allotments until desired vegetation conditions are reached. Grazing season is unchanged. No construction, and all roads remain open.
4. **[[Alternative 4 - Riparian Management Area]]:** Closes the entire watershed to grazing, including all of the Kunde and Seibold allotments and parts of the Crittenden, Papago, and San Rafael allotments. All roads are closed except those providing access to private land.
5. **[[No Action Alternative]]:** Keeps all allotments as they are, with no new water developments or fencing. Roads remain open and user-maintained. An Arizona Trail proposal would be considered separately.

**Common elements across all alternatives**
- Four gully control projects to reduce sediment yield and increase infiltration
- Arizona Trail construction allowed within the project area, for pedestrian and equestrian use only, avoiding sensitive riparian areas
- Fuelwood gathering permitted only for on-site use

**Dropped alternatives**
- Additional watershed emphasis totaling $695,000 in structures
- Zoological-Botanical area designation

**Stated conclusions and requests**
- Alternative 1 is the preferred alternative because it best meets the objectives of the Land Management Plan.
- The Forest Service requests written or phone comments by June 4, acknowledging the short response period and its goal of implementing improvements promptly.
- Detailed descriptions and maps are available at the district office on request.
