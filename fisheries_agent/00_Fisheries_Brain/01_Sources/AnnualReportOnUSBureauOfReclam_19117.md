## Executive Summary

This progress report details a mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) restriction fragment analysis of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations across the [[Colorado River Basin]], conducted under a U.S. Bureau of Reclamation cooperative agreement by researchers at Arizona State University. The study aims to characterize genetic diversity and population structure in wild razorback sucker populations (Lake Mohave, Lake Powell, Green/Yampa Rivers, and the Grand Junction/Colorado River region) and to compare these to hatchery-produced stocks from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (DNFH). The work also incidentally screened suspected hybrids and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) samples from the Green River. Key results show exceptionally high genetic diversity in the Lake Mohave population (haplotype diversity = 0.97), declining diversity gradients moving into upstream/isolated populations (Lake Powell = 0.81, Green/Yampa = 0.66, Grand Junction = 0.00), and generally faithful preservation of source-population haplotype diversity in two of three examined DNFH hatchery year classes, with a notable exception in the 1990 year class linked to low survivorship.

## Background & Description

This report is a molecular/population-genetics study and does not describe external morphology. In lieu of morphological data, the diagnostic character set used to distinguish individuals and populations is the **mitochondrial DNA restriction haplotype**, generated using five 4-base-pair (4 bp) recognition restriction enzymes (*HinfI*, *HinPI*, *MboI*, *ScrFI*, *TaqI*) and three 6-base-pair (6 bp) enzymes (*BanI*, *BstEII*, *NheI*). Haplotypes are coded as multi-letter strings (e.g., AAAABA'AA) representing the combined restriction site pattern across all eight enzymes per individual. A key diagnostic finding was the ability to molecularly distinguish [[Razorback Sucker]] mtDNA from [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] mtDNA in putative hybrid individuals, confirming introgression/hybridization at Highline Lake and Etter Pond in the Grand Junction region.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Wild population sampling sites**: [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Powell]], the [[Green River]]/[[Yampa River]] confluence area, and the Colorado River mainstem near [[Grand Junction]] (including Highline Lake and Etter Pond), representing both lentic (reservoir) and lotic (riverine) habitats within the Colorado River Basin.
- Additional opportunistic samples were obtained from the [[Little Colorado River]] (N=1, muscle plug) and [[Lake Mead]] (N=2 successfully analyzed gamete samples).
- **Hybridization zone**: The Grand Junction region (Highline Lake, Etter Pond) is identified as a zone of sympatry and apparent hybridization between [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], evidenced by individuals carrying pure flannelmouth mtDNA despite razorback-like external identification as "suspected hybrids."
- **Hatchery context**: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] serves as the ex-situ conservation/propagation source, producing year classes (1987, 1989, 1990) derived from wild broodstock (noted as originating from "Buenos Aires" broodstock for the 1987 class) intended for reintroduction/augmentation of natural populations.
- Tissue types sampled included gametes/gonads (preferred, higher mtDNA yield/quality), muscle plugs (secondary, often insufficient for 4 bp enzyme resolution), and one sperm sample noted to be contaminated with eggs from another individual (Lake Mead: LM2/LM3).

## Key Findings

- **Lake Mohave (n=25)**: 17 distinct haplotypes identified; haplotype diversity = **0.97**, described as one of the highest diversity values recorded for any vertebrate. Indicates a large founding/breeding population with no evidence of a recent genetic bottleneck.
- **Lake Powell (n=12; 11 gamete + 1 muscle)**: 5 haplotypes identified; diversity = **0.81**. Three of five haplotypes shared with [[Lake Mohave]] population.
- **Green/Yampa Rivers (n=23)**: 4 haplotypes identified; diversity = **0.66** (reduced relative to Mohave and Powell). Two haplotypes shared with Mohave, one shared with Powell.
- **Grand Junction region (n=13 total)**: Comprised of wild razorbacks from the mainstem Colorado River (n=2), Highline Lake razorbacks (n=4), and suspected hybrids from Highline Lake (n=3) and Etter Pond (n=4). Two suspected hybrids (one from each site) carried pure [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] mtDNA; the remaining 11 individuals shared a single identical razorback haplotype (diversity = **0.00**) also found in all other sampled populations.
- **Little Colorado River / Lake Mead**: All successfully analyzed individuals (n=3) carried genotypes also found in the Lake Mohave sample, suggesting genetic continuity with that population.
- **Hatchery comparison (DNFH)**:
  - 1987 year class (n=10, from "Buenos Aires" broodstock, 55 females): 6 haplotypes, diversity = **0.89**, survivorship 49%.
  - 1989 year class (n=11, 14 females): 7 haplotypes, diversity = **0.91**, survivorship 46%.
  - 1990 year class (n=10, 17 females): only 4 haplotypes, diversity = **0.71** (significantly reduced), survivorship only **11%** (egg to swim-up) — the reduced diversity is attributed to this high mortality combined with a smaller number of founding females.
  - Overall conclusion: hatchery managers in 1987 and 1989 successfully preserved much of the source population's (Lake Mohave) haplotype diversity pattern, but the large number of haplotypes present in the wild source population makes it impossible to preserve all variants in captive propagation — management should focus on preserving the *distribution* of variation rather than every individual haplotype.
- **Population differentiation**: Comparison of 6 bp enzyme haplotype geographic distributions suggests some degree of population differentiation among regions, but small sample sizes (outside Lake Mohave and Green/Yampa) currently preclude definitive statistical conclusions.
- **Methodological finding**: Muscle plug samples yielded inferior mtDNA quantity/quality compared to gamete/gonad samples, sufficient only for 6 bp enzyme analysis (coarse resolution) and inadequate for 4 bp enzyme analysis (fine-scale haplotype resolution). Researchers plan to continue prioritizing gamete sampling, reserving muscle tissue for opportunistic cases only (e.g., Grand Canyon-area samples).
- **Data quality note**: One Lake Mead sample (LM2, sperm) was potentially contaminated by eggs from a second individual (LM3), and both yielded identical haplotypes — results for LM2 should be interpreted cautiously as potentially non-independent.
- Samples of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] were obtained from the Green River, and mtDNA has been isolated and preliminarily examined from a few individuals, though full analysis was not yet reported.
