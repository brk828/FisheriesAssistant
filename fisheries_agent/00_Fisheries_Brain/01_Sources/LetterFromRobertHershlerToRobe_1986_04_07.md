## Executive Summary

This document is a personal letter dated April 7, 1986, from Robert Hershler (Associate Curator, Department of Invertebrate Zoology/Mollusks, National Museum of Natural History, Smithsonian Institution) to Dr. Robert R. Miller (Curator of Fishes, University of Michigan Museum of Zoology). The letter is not a primary scientific data report but rather a conservation-focused communication concerning two significant desert aquatic ecosystems: [[Ash Meadows]] (Nevada) and [[Cuatro Cienegas]] (Coahuila, Mexico). It references ongoing efforts to document historical snail collections at Ash Meadows and raises urgent alarm over habitat destruction at the Mojarral spring complex in Cuatro Cienegas due to unregulated tourism development ("spa" construction, including cabins and conversion of a spring into a swimming pool). The letter proposes engaging The Nature Conservancy to purchase land for habitat protection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Ash Meadows]]**: A desert spring ecosystem in Nevada, historically significant for endemic aquatic snails (implied [[Springsnails]], family Hydrobiidae) and other endemic aquatic fauna. Hershler references "historical collections of Ash Meadows snails," indicating ongoing taxonomic/biogeographic research into this system's endemic mollusk fauna, which is closely tied to isolated, thermally stable spring habitats.
- **[[Cuatro Cienegas]]**: An endorheic desert basin in Coahuila, Mexico, renowned for its high endemism in aquatic invertebrates and fishes, supported by numerous isolated springs ("pozas"). The **Mojarral spring area** is singled out as containing what Hershler considers "the finest springs in the basin."
- **Threats identified**: Active bulldozing at the edge of the Mojarral springs for construction of a tourist "spa," including cabins and modification of a spring into a swimming pool. This represents a direct anthropogenic threat to spring-endemic aquatic biota (both invertebrates and, by extension, the basin's endemic fish fauna, e.g., [[Cyprinodon]] pupfishes and other Cuatro Cienegas endemics historically studied by Robert R. Miller).
- **Conservation response proposed**: Consultation with [[Salvador Contreras]] (Mexican ichthyologist/conservationist) and a proposal to involve **The Nature Conservancy** in land acquisition within the Cuatro Cienegas valley to prevent habitat loss.
