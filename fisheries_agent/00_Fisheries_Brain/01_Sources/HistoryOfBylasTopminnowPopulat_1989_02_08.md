# History of Bylas Topminnow Populations

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #poeciliidae #gila-topminnow #conservation-management #invasive-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989 internal correspondence from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AGFD) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, summarizing the monitoring and management history of three isolated spring/creek populations of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (referred to locally as "Bylas Topminnow") near Bylas, Arizona: **Bylas Springs**, **Middle Spring** (including Medicine and Bos Springs), and **Salt Creek**. The record spans from initial population discovery in 1968 through January 1989 and documents a recurring cycle of habitat monitoring, invasion by non-native [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), chemical renovation using [[Antimycin A]], construction of concrete fish barriers, and repeated topminnow restocking efforts sourced from Arizona State University (ASU) stock originally derived from Bylas Springs. The history illustrates the chronic vulnerability of small, isolated native fish refugia to invasive species incursion, flood-driven barrier failure, and demographic bottlenecks.

## Background & Description

The source document is administrative/historical in nature and does not provide direct morphological data on the topminnow. However, relevant biological inferences can be drawn:

- The species functions as a **small-bodied, spring-dwelling poeciliid** capable of persisting as the sole fish species in isolated spring headwaters, suggesting tolerance of low-flow, thermally stable spring environments.
- Population monitoring notes distinguish topminnow from co-occurring [[Western Mosquitofish]], implying morphological/ecological similarity sufficient to create competitive and possibly hybridization/predation pressures — a key driver of the management interventions described (e.g., see [[Gila Topminnow Decline]]).
- A 1989 entry from Salt Creek notes co-occurrence with **Hydrobiid snails**, indicating shared spring-obligate habitat with sensitive invertebrate taxa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Isolated desert spring-fed systems and spring-run creeks characteristic of the Gila River drainage in southeastern Arizona.
- **Site inventory:**
  - **[[Bylas Springs]]** — population discovered 1968; long history of alternating monospecific topminnow status and mosquitofish invasion.
  - **[[Middle Spring]]** (encompassing Medicine and Bos Springs) — population discovered 1968; comparatively more stable, remaining topminnow-only through most of its monitoring history until a severe bottleneck event in January 1989.
  - **[[Salt Creek]]** — population discovered 1968; experienced a complete extirpation event ("no fish present") in 1985–1986 followed by restocking from Middle Spring.
- **Interspecific interactions:** The central ecological threat is competitive/predatory displacement by the invasive **[[Western Mosquitofish]] (Gambusia affinis)**, which repeatedly invaded these spring systems following flood events and barrier failures.
- **Conservation infrastructure:** Concrete fish barriers were constructed at all three sites to physically exclude mosquitofish immigration from downstream reaches, though these barriers proved vulnerable to failure (notably at Bylas Springs in 1984 and Salt Creek by 1989).
- **Source population role:** ASU-maintained captive/refuge stock, originally derived from Bylas Springs, served as the genetic reservoir for repeated reintroduction efforts, and Middle Spring subsequently served as a donor population for both Roper Lake State Park and Salt Creek restocking.

## Key Findings

### Bylas Springs Timeline
- **1968:** Population discovered.
- **1976:** Topminnow monitored as the only fish species present.
- **1980 (Jul):** Mosquitofish invasion following a flood event.
- **1982 (Mar):** Site renovated with [[Antimycin A]] to remove mosquitofish.
- **1982 (Apr):** Restocked with topminnow from ASU stock (originally Bylas-derived).
- **1982 (Jul):** Monitoring found mosquitofish still present — renovation deemed **unsuccessful**.
- **1983–84:** Concrete fish barrier constructed.
- **1984 (Apr & Jul):** Renovated again for mosquitofish (twice within months); restocked with ASU topminnow.
- **1985 (Dec):** Barrier failure — mosquitofish reinvade.
- **1986 (Sep):** Both topminnow and mosquitofish present.
- **1989 (Jan):** Mosquitofish dominate the system; only a few topminnow persist in the uppermost springhead boil — indicating near-collapse of the native population at this site.

### Middle Spring Timeline (incl. Medicine & Bos Springs)
- **1968:** Population discovered.
- **1976:** Topminnow = only fish.
- **1983–84:** Concrete fish barrier constructed.
- **1985 (Dec) & 1986 (Sep):** Topminnow remained the only fish species — relatively stable.
- **1987 (Nov):** Topminnow-only status maintained; samples stocked into [[Roper Lake State Park]].
- **1988 (Sep):** Same status; additional stocking to Roper Lake State Park.
- **1989 (Jan):** **Severe population bottleneck** — large numbers of dead topminnow observed, despite remaining the only fish species present, signaling an acute stress or die-off event of unknown cause.

### Salt Creek Timeline
- **1968:** Population discovered.
- **1976:** Topminnow = only fish.
- **1980 (Jul):** Mosquitofish invasion following flood.
- **1983–84:** Concrete fish barrier constructed.
- **1984 (Feb):** Topminnow co-occurring with Hydrobiid snails.
- **1984 (Apr):** Renovated for mosquitofish (repeated in June).
- **1985 (Dec):** **No fish present** — apparent local extirpation.
- **1986 (Sep):** Still no fish present; **300 fish stocked** from Middle Spring in a reintroduction effort.
- **1989 (Jan):** Topminnow = only fish; however, the concrete barrier **no longer inhibits upstream fish movement**, posing renewed invasion risk.

### Synthesis
- All three populations trace back to a common founding event (1968 discovery) and share a recurring management pattern: **invasion → renovation (Antimycin A) → barrier construction → restocking → barrier failure → reinvasion**.
- The [[Concrete Fish Barrier]] approach, while central to management strategy, proved only temporarily effective at each site, underscoring the difficulty of maintaining invasive-species exclusion in flood-prone desert stream systems.
- By January 1989, all three sites showed signs of stress: Bylas Springs was nearly overrun by mosquitofish, Middle Spring suffered an unexplained mass mortality event, and Salt Creek's barrier had failed structurally.
- The document demonstrates the role of **ex-situ propagation (ASU stock)** and **inter-site translocation** (Middle Spring → Salt Creek, Middle Spring → Roper Lake State Park) as key tools in sustaining this endangered species complex.
- This correspondence serves as a primary historical data source for subsequent [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]] efforts under the Endangered Species Act.
