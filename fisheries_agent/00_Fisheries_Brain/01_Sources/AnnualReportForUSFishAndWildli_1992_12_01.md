## Executive Summary

This document is the 1992 annual report submitted by William J. Matter and Robert Forrest (University of Arizona) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service under Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811, covering research on the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), a federally endangered poeciliid native to the Gila River drainage of Arizona. The research combines field habitat characterization at [[Cienega Creek]] with controlled laboratory preference trials using a self-sustaining captive population. No wild topminnow were captured, killed, or removed during the reporting period. The 1993 renewal request proposes development of a novel "mini-minnow trap" methodology to enable non-lethal, quantitative sampling of topminnow in dense marshy vegetation where visual counts and standard collection methods are infeasible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types studied:** Six field sites in [[Cienega Creek]], Arizona, stratified into three microhabitat categories (2 pools, 2 backwaters, 2 glides), reflecting the heterogeneous mesohabitat structure typical of desert stream systems occupied by the [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Environmental variables measured:** Water depth, water velocity, temperature, and substrate type — standard abiotic descriptors used to characterize instream habitat use and preference.
- **Cover preference (laboratory trials):** Captive topminnow were presented choices among floating algae mats, vascular (rooted) plants, floating styrofoam sheets, and plastic strips, simulating natural and artificial cover types; time spent in each cover type served as an index of habitat preference.
- **Depth preference (laboratory trials):** Fish were also tested across varying water depths to assess depth selection behavior.
- **Marsh/backwater habitat significance:** Marshy, vegetated backwater areas (similar to those in Cienega Creek) are hypothesized to be ecologically important refugia or habitat for [[Gila Topminnow]], but their importance has been difficult to quantify due to sampling limitations (visual counts impossible in dense vegetation; conventional gear impractical).
- **Population context:** A captive, self-sustaining population (~700–1,000 individuals) is maintained at the USFWS–Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit (AZCFWRU) fisheries compound, serving as a proxy for studying wild habitat-use behavior without disturbing natural populations.

## Key Findings

- **Field surveys (May 1992):** Two site visits to Cienega Creek successfully located areas with abundant [[Gila Topminnow]] and collected detailed physical habitat measurements across pools, backwaters, and glides; no fish were captured or killed in the field.
- **Captive population status:** The research colony held at the AZCFWRU fisheries compound was stable and reproducing, numbering approximately 700–1,000 fish at time of reporting.
- **Laboratory cover/depth trials:** Topminnow were given choice trials among four cover types and multiple depths; preference was quantified via time-based indices. Mortality during trials was minimal — only 3 fish died across all laboratory testing, indicating low experimental stress/handling mortality and a methodologically sound approach.
- **Fish disposition:** All surviving trial fish were returned alive to a holding pool at the AZCFWRU compound, consistent with permit conditions for non-lethal research on an endangered species.
- **Methodological gap identified:** Existing survey methods (visual counts, standard fish collection gear) are inadequate for estimating relative abundance of topminnow in marshy, densely vegetated habitats — a critical limitation for assessing the ecological value of these sites.
- **Proposed innovation (1993):** Development and testing of small-diameter (3–4 in) "mini-minnow traps" designed to live-capture topminnow in simulated marsh vegetation within outdoor test pools, using known numbers of captive fish to calibrate trap efficiency before deployment in natural Cienega Creek marsh habitat.
- **Regulatory/permit status:** The report served as the basis for renewal of Subpermit PRT-676811, requesting continued authorization to (1) hold the captive research population and (2) conduct non-removal observational fieldwork on [[Gila Topminnow]] in [[Cienega Creek]].
