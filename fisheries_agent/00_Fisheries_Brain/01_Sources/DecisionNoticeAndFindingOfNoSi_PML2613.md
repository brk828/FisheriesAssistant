# Arnett Creek/Telegraph Canyon Native Fish Re-establishment Project Decision Notice and FONSI

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

The USDA Forest Service, [[Tonto National Forest]] (Globe Ranger District), issued a Decision Notice (May 31, 1996) selecting Alternative C for restoring native fish habitat in [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]], Pinal County, Arizona. The project removes exotic fish with piscicides ([[Rotenone]] and [[Antimycin A]]) above a new fish barrier at Barrier Site 2, then supports the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] in stocking native fish. A Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) concluded that an Environmental Impact Statement is not required.

## Background & Description

- **Lead agency:** USDA Forest Service, Tonto National Forest, Globe Ranger District. District Ranger: Larry Widner.
- **Authorities cited:** [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] (1934), Multiple-Use Sustained-Yield Act (1960), Forest and Rangeland Renewable Resources Planning Act (1974), [[National Forest Management Act]] (1976), and 36 CFR 219.19 and 36 CFR 241.2.
- **Project area:** Parts of Sections 7-11 and 13-36 of T 2 S, R 12 E; Sections 19-32 of T 2 S, R 13 E; and Sections 3, 4, 5, 8, 9 of T 3 S, R 12 E (Salt River Base and Meridian).
- **Public involvement:** Sought in 1993, 1994, and 1996. Scoping identified nine substantive issues, including piscicide use, instream flow rights, monitoring, federally listed species, barrier siting, private land fish removal, and protection of biotic and physical resources.
- **Appeal:** Appeals to the Regional Forester (Albuquerque, NM) must be filed within 45 days of legal notice in the *Mesa Tribune*, under 36 CFR 215.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Species and status**
- Native targets: longfin dace and desert sucker (not listed under the Endangered Species Act), Gila chub, Gila topminnow (listed as Endangered), and Sonora sucker (considered but not stocked).
- Exotic competitors and predators: [[Green Sunfish]] (preys on [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] larvae) and [[Mosquitofish]] (present but not abundant or thriving).

**Habitat suitability**
- Habitat surveys found habitat unsuitable for Sonora sucker, speckled dace, loach minnow, and spikedace, and for Gila chub and Gila topminnow at present. Alternatives including those species were nonetheless developed based on historical occurrence, status, and expert input.
- The Gila topminnow is not found above 5000 ft elevation (corrected typographical error noted by AGFD).

**Wildlife and biota**
- [[Javelina]] may be exposed to Rotenone; AGFD judged impacts likely insignificant.
- Macroinvertebrates may decline short-term but are expected to recover rapidly.
- Riparian and Sonoran desert scrub vegetation, geologic, and scenic features are to be protected where feasible.

**Regulatory context**
- Consistent with the Tonto National Forest Land and Resource Management Plan (LRMP), which calls for managing warmwater streams for Gila sucker (synonym of desert sucker) and longfin dace.
- Qualifies under Nationwide Permit #26 (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers letter, April 26, 1996). Arizona Department of Environmental Quality granted 401 Water Quality certification on April 22, 1996.
- Biological Assessment and Evaluation (May 29, 1996): "No Effect" determination for federally listed species, with the project possibly affecting individual lowland leopard frogs but not jeopardizing population viability.
- Cultural resource clearances obtained for the barrier (Report #95-12-100) and road obliteration (Report #95-12-100-A); SHPO concurrence received April 29, 1996.

## Key Findings

**Alternatives considered**
- **Alternative A (No Action):** No barrier, no toxins, no Forest Service cooperation. Green sunfish would continue to prey on leopard frogs, and the creeks would not help reverse native fish decline.
- **Alternative B:** Barrier at Barrier Site 1 (T 2 S, R 12 E, Section 8, NE¼SW¼), stocking of longfin dace and desert sucker, and a 9-year re-evaluation.
- **Alternative C (Selected):** Barrier at Barrier Site 2 (Section 7, SW¼NW¼), access via an abandoned road (rehabilitated, then restored or obliterated), stocking of longfin dace and desert sucker, and a 9-year re-evaluation for other native species including Gila topminnow and Gila chub.
- **Alternatives D and E:** Add Gila chub and Gila topminnow stocking. Spot treatments are barred after Gila topminnow stocking. Alternative E uses Barrier Site 2 with the same stocking as D. These alternatives carry possible mining and livestock restrictions from Biological Opinions.
- **Dropped alternatives:** Other barrier locations (engineering, habitat protected, cost, or access problems), electrofishing or seining (cannot capture all exotic fish), and stocking Sonora sucker, speckled dace, loach minnow, or spikedace (unsuitable habitat).

**Selection rationale**
- Alternative C maximizes exotic-free habitat, supports AGFD reintroduction of species likely to survive, and minimizes non-target impacts.
- The decision responded to a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service request for Alternative E. The Forest Service replied that the habitat for Sonora sucker, Gila topminnow, and Gila chub was evaluated and the 9-year re-evaluation can revisit them.
- Mosquitofish captures across multiple surveys did not establish a sustaining population.

**Mitigation and monitoring**
- Pre-treatment intensive surveys and macroinvertebrate sampling before and after treatment.
- Timing of applications to protect leopard frogs during peak breeding; collection and release of overwintering tadpoles.
- State-certified applicator on site; potassium permanganate available to detoxify; public notification; protective gear.
- Rotenone sites monitored for up to one week for javelina effects.
- Fish population surveys annually for four years, then every four years for at least eight years.
- Fish habitat monitoring every four years for 12 years; riparian photopoints replicated on the same schedule.
- Barrier structural integrity checked annually for four years, then every four years for at least eight years.

**Impact summary**
- Instream flow: the project allows the Forest to obtain a water right certificate for its Arnett Creek application, which otherwise may be jeopardized without native fish reintroduction.
- Water quality: no lasting impacts expected given rapid toxin detoxification.
- Leopard frogs: long-term benefit from removing green sunfish.
- Private lands: landowners have agreed to allow treatment.

**Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI)**
- Ten NEPA significance criteria were reviewed. Effects are mostly beneficial over the long term and short-term adverse effects are limited.
- No public health risk due to low toxin concentrations.
- No historic sites or wetlands affected. A proposed Research Natural Area was removed from consideration because of mineral claim conflicts.
- Not expected to be highly controversial or set precedent.
- Compliance with applicable laws and regulations confirmed.

**Implementation**
- Work begins no sooner than five business days after the appeal period closes. If an appeal is filed, work begins no sooner than 15 calendar days after a final appeal decision.
- The project administrator is responsible for monitoring and conformance to design.
