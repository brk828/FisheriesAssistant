## Key Findings

- **Purpose**: Establish a small Federal interagency team ([[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[National Park Service]]) to draft a proposal for native fish rearing facility construction and long-term management, later expanded to include state and public stakeholders.
- **Phased Implementation Plan**:
  - **Phase 1 (FY1990)**: Form Federal team; identify "Federal Barriers" (regulatory/administrative obstacles) and candidate backwater sites; develop preliminary designs, cost estimates, and funding identification; produce a **Draft Proposal**.
  - **Phase 2 (FY1991)**: Expand engagement to other Federal, State, and public agencies; solicit input; refine designs and management objectives; finalize proposal; execute a **formal interagency agreement** on cost-sharing and management responsibilities.
  - **Phase 3 (FY1992)**: Finalize design specifications, solicit construction bids, award contracts, construct facilities, and initiate rearing operations.
- **Key Discussion Topics for Long-Term Rearing Facilities**:
  - Barriers/constraints related to reservoir and land management.
  - Permitting needs spanning endangered species compliance, Clean Water Act Sections 404/402, and National Park Service authorizations.
  - Design, survey, and cost analysis for both permanent and temporary fish barriers.
  - Site location surveys scheduled for **March 19–23, 1990**.
  - Development of management goals and methods, funding sources, and a project schedule.
- **Arizona Bay Renovation** (ASU-affiliated site) identified as an active near-term project requiring:
  - Administrative compliance (NEPA, Section 402 permits).
  - Barrier construction (permanent vs. temporary type/design/cost trade-offs).
  - Section 404 construction permitting, manpower planning, funding allocation, and scheduling.
- **Strategic Approach**: A "Federal-first" model — a small core Federal group develops and internally vets a proposal before broader interagency and public participation is invited, intended to streamline early-stage consensus on constraints and funding before scaling up collaboration.
