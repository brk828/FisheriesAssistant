# Title 50 CFR Part 17 - Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #regulatory-framework

## Executive Summary

This document is a comprehensive regulatory compilation from the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations (Title 50, Part 17) listing all species of wildlife and plants determined by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] to be Endangered or Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The document provides the official federal list as of the early 1990s, including mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, invertebrates (crustaceans, mollusks, insects, arachnids), and plants. Each entry includes scientific and common names, historic range, population status, listing date, and references to special rules or critical habitat designations. The document establishes the regulatory framework for species protection, defining how species are listed, classified, and potentially delisted based on recovery, extinction, or data error.

## Background & Description

The regulatory framework establishes that species are listed as "E" (Endangered), "T" (Threatened), or "E/T (S/A)" (similarity of appearance). The document clarifies that common names cannot be relied upon for specimen identification due to local variation, while scientific names follow the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] for animals and [[International Code of Botanical Nomenclature]] for plants. Geographic populations of vertebrate species may be classified differently based on location, with "Entire" indicating all populations throughout the present range are listed.

The historic range column indicates known general distribution as reported in current scientific literature, though present distribution may be greatly reduced. Prohibitions under the Act apply to all individuals of listed species wherever found, regardless of historic range limitations. The document includes cross-references to special rules (§17.40-17.96) and critical habitat designations (Parts 17, 222, 226, 227), with "NA" (not applicable) indicating no special rules or critical habitat for particular species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listings reflect species threatened by multiple factors as defined in 50 CFR 424.11(c): (1) habitat destruction, modification, or curtailment; (2) overutilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes; (3) disease or predation; (4) inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms; (5) other natural or manmade factors affecting continued existence.

For aquatic species, the document lists numerous fishes including [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus albus*), [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser brevirostrum*), multiple darter species (*Etheostoma* spp.), pupfish (*Cyprinodon* spp.), and various minnows and suckers. Marine species include sea turtles ([[Loggerhead Sea Turtle]], [[Hawksbill Sea Turtle]], [[Green Sea Turtle]], [[Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle]], [[Leatherback Sea Turtle]]), marine mammals ([[West Indian Manatee]], [[Hawaiian Monk Seal]], [[Steller Sea Lion]]), and invertebrates (freshwater mussels, cave shrimp, amphipods).

The document notes experimental populations designated as "XE" (essential) or "XN" (nonessential), allowing for reintroduction programs with modified regulatory treatment. Geographic specificity varies from entire species ranges to specific river systems, states, or island populations.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Coverage**: The list encompasses 7 major vertebrate classes (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes) plus invertebrates (mollusks, crustaceans, insects, arachnids) and plants across multiple families. Fish listings include approximately 70+ species spanning families from [[Salmonidae]] (trout) to [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows) to [[Percidae]] (darters).

**Geographic Distribution**: Listed species occur across all U.S. states and territories, with concentrations in [[California]], [[Florida]], [[Hawaii]], [[Texas]], and southeastern states. International listings include species from Mexico, Central and South America, Caribbean, Pacific Islands, Africa, Asia, and other regions.

**Listing Chronology**: Federal Register citations span from 1967 (32 FR 4001) through 1991 (56 FR citations), documenting the evolution of endangered species protection. The document represents a compilation current as of the cover date, incorporating all published final rules through that time.

**Delisting Criteria**: Species may be removed from the list for three reasons: (1) extinction (with sufficient time to confirm extirpation); (2) recovery (no longer endangered or threatened based on best available data); (3) original data error (subsequent investigations show listing data or interpretation was incorrect). The document includes a separate section listing species removed from protection.

**Regulatory Structure**: Each listing includes: scientific name, common name, historic range, vertebrate population where endangered/threatened, status (E/T/XE/XN), Federal Register citation number, critical habitat designation, and special rules reference. The "When listed" column uses numerical codes corresponding to specific Federal Register publications, with codes ranging from 1 (32 FR 4001, March 11, 1967) through 428 (56 FR 28828, June 25, 1991).

**Aquatic Species Emphasis**: Fish listings include cave-adapted species ([[Alabama Cavefish]], [[Ozark Cavefish]]), desert spring endemics (pupfish, springfish species), river specialists ([[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]]), and anadromous species (sturgeons). Freshwater mussel listings are extensive, reflecting the imperiled status of North American unionid fauna. Marine species protections extend to all sea turtle species in U.S. waters and multiple marine mammal populations.

**Critical Habitat**: Many species have designated critical habitat (referenced by section numbers 17.95, 17.96, 222.41, 226.71, 227.11-12), while others show "NA" indicating no formal critical habitat designation despite listing status.
