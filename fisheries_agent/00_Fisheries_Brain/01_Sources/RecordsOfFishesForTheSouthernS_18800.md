# Records of Fishes for the Southern States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Henry W. Fowler]] (*Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington*, vol. 36, pp. 7–34, March 28, 1923) compiles state-by-state records of fishes for the southern United States. It draws on older museum collections at the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]], many of them containing [[Edward D. Cope]] types, and on recent field work. The core of the paper is a Virginia field survey from April 1922 in the Roanoke, James and Kanawha basins. The remaining sections are annotated species lists with localities, plus bibliographies of faunal works for each state. The text is a distributional and nomenclatural catalogue, not a hypothesis-driven study.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Specimens are arranged by state (Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee, Georgia, Florida, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana). Each section gives a bibliography and a locality-annotated species list.
- **Sources:** Older lots (largely Cope's collections), small contributions from many collectors, and recent collections. Fowler gives bibliographies only for states without a recent full list. For North Carolina, Florida and Tennessee he cites only later papers on distribution.
- **Field methods:** In April 1922, Fowler, J. B. Stetson Jr. and W. T. Innes sampled nine stations in Virginia, including clear spring-fed streams, tributaries of the [[Roanoke River]], [[Catawba Creek]], [[Sinking Creek]], [[Brush Creek]], [[Laurel Creek]] and the South Fork of the Roanoke. Collecting included minnow seine hauls.
- **Nomenclature:** Many names are cited as type localities (e.g., "Types of *Hybopsis longiceps* Cope, from head of James and Roanoke Rivers"). This makes the paper a reference for historical type material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Virginia stations (April 22–24, 1922):**
- Habitats ranged from clear, cold, rocky, rapid streams (Sinking Creek) to shallow sandy streams (Brush Creek) and gravel-and-sand reaches (Laurel Creek, South Fork of the Roanoke).
- Riparian vegetation along Laurel Creek included rhododendron and hemlock.
- Fish from the open brook near Cloverdale were parasitized with trematodes (the text reads "tremetodes").
- At Daleville, fish were "exceedingly abundant" in a clear rapid stream.

**Coastal and estuarine records:** North Carolina (Beaufort, Fort Macon, Cape Lookout), South Carolina (Hilton Head, Charleston), and Florida (Miami, Tortugas, Bayport) include many marine and brackish taxa, such as menhaden (*Brevoortia tyrannus*), mullets (*Mugil* spp.), and reef fishes.

**Notable taxa and observations:**
- *Chrosomus oreas*: "Great numbers" at Daleville, often over 100 per seine sweep, in rock and gravel pools. Red coloration faded quickly in captivity.
- *Notropis photogenis*: Very abundant at Brush Creek, with rosy markings on the muzzle and head.
- *Notropis cornutus cerasinus*: Spawning males at Daleville up to 83 mm, with variable vermilion fins.
- *Catostomus rhotheecus*: Three specimens seined from Catawba rapids at Kyle. Black blotches faded after death.
- Male *Campostoma anomalum* (105 mm) from Daleville showed tubercles on head and back.

## Key Findings

1. **Virginia survey:** Nine stations were sampled in April 1922. The record lists numerous cyprinids, including *Campostoma anomalum*, *Chrosomus oreas*, *Notropis* spp., *Rhinichthys* spp. and *Hybopsis* spp.
2. **Virginia historical base:** Most early Virginia records derive from Cope (1868) on the Allegheny region. Fowler recommends re-examining this material and listing Cope's remaining types in the Academy.
3. **North Carolina:** The material is largely Cope's old collection, with additional specimens from [[Fort Macon]] (Yarrow), Conrad and Leidy. Subsequent work by Smith (1907), Gudger, Nichols, Rathbun and others covers Beaufort fishes and sharks. Records include a notable *Mozistoma alleghaniensis* description and *Scorpaena colesi*.
4. **South Carolina:** The Holbrook collections (*Southern Ichthyology*, 1847–1860) and Jordan and Gilbert's Charleston collection (126 species recorded) are the main bases. Fowler describes [[Notropis Stonei]] (1921) from the Pocotaligo River.
5. **Tennessee:** The paper refers to Evermann's 1918 catalogue (171 species, largely from Tennessee) rather than reproducing it. Cope's Tennessee material from Coal Creek, the Cumberland and the Clinch River is listed.
6. **Georgia:** Cope's small collection from the Etowah River near Rome, plus Jordan's type series (*Photogenis stigmaturus*, *P. callistius*, *Notropis lirus* and others), are recorded.
7. **Florida:** The most extensive collections. Fowler cites Evermann and Kendall's 1900 check-list, and later work on the Tortugas, Miami and Florida Keys. Records include the whale shark (*Rhincodon typus*) and nurse shark (*Ginglymostoma cirratum*).
8. **Alabama:** Records are limited, with Cyprinodon variegatus and Chenobryttus gulosus. Gilbert (1891) reported on Tennessee, Alabama and Escambia River fishes.
9. **Mississippi:** Hay (1881) listed 56 species, 15 of them new. Evermann (1899) reported 74 species in total for Mississippi, Louisiana and Texas.
10. **Louisiana:** Old specimens from J. S. Walker, plus Jordan (1885) on New Orleans fishes and Wilmott (1910) on Cameron. The paper includes *Polyodon spathula* from New Orleans.
11. **Chesapeake Bay:** Kendall (1914) recorded the lumpfish (*Cyclopterus lumpus*) above Old Point Comfort, a new record for the Bay.

**Bottom line:** The paper is a reference catalogue. It consolidates localities, type material and bibliography for southern fish faunas. It also documents a recent field survey of Roanoke-basin streams. Future value lies in identifying historical type localities and checking older Cope material.
