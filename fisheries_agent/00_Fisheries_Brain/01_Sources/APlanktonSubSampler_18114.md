# A Plankton Sub-Sampler

#ichthyology #marine-biology #plankton #limnology #sampling-methodology

## Executive Summary

This methods note describes a plexiglass **plankton sub-sampling device**, adapted from an earlier brass model designed by Dr. Raymond P. Langford (University of Toronto). The instrument addresses a practical need in [[Plankton Analysis]]: the ability to divide a single homogenized plankton sample into equal, independent fractions for replicate or parallel analyses. Unlike a fish-centric taxonomic study, this paper is purely a **methodological/apparatus contribution** to limnological and planktological sampling technique, though its design has direct relevance to fisheries and food-web studies that rely on quantifying zooplankton and invertebrate drift as prey resources for fish.

## Background & Description (Apparatus Design)

Rather than organismal morphology, this paper details the physical construction of the **sub-sampler device**:

- **Outer cylinder**: plexiglass, ⅛ in. thick, outside diameter 1⅝ in., inside diameter 1½ in., length 5 in.
- **Inner cylinder**: plexiglass, outside/inside diameters matching (1⅝, 1½ in.), length 6 in. (longer than outer cylinder to ease removal).
- **Partitions**: inner cylinder divided into **4 equal sections** by two slotted plexiglass pieces (6 × 1½ × 1/16 in.) cemented at right angles to each other.
- **Base plate**: plexiglass, ⅛ in. thick, 1⅝ in. diameter, glued to the outer tube; fitted with a **circular soft rubber pad** to create a watertight seal against the inner cylinder and partitions.
- **Drain hole**: ⅛-in. diameter hole through base and rubber pad, sealed with a small rubber stopper/cork during sample agitation.
- **Legs**: three small legs glued to the base for freestanding stability when the drain is stoppered.
- **Comparison to original Langford model**: constructed of two brass cylinders, inner cylinder divided into **8 sections**, overall device ~10 in. high × 4 in. diameter (larger and finer-divided than the plexiglass version described here).

This is a structural/engineering description analogous to a "morphology" section, substituting device anatomy for organismal anatomy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context (Sampling Context & Application)

- **Target material**: whole plankton samples, ranging from small protists (e.g., *Ceratium*) to larger crustaceans (e.g., amphipods), as well as heterogeneous **drift samples** containing insects, insect remains, detritus, and plant fragments.
- **Typical working volume**: 60 mL original sample volume found to work well, though volume is adjustable depending on sample density and composition.
- **Use case for dense samples**: samples with extremely high organism concentrations can be iteratively sub-sampled (i.e., a quarter-fraction can itself be re-divided) until a countable/workable density is reached.
- **Field/lab role**: Functions within the broader ecological workflow of [[Zooplankton Sampling]] and [[Benthic Drift Sampling]], enabling replicate counts without requiring multiple full collections — relevant to downstream studies of fish diet, prey availability, and [[Aquatic Food Web]] dynamics.

## Key Findings

- The device successfully divides a homogenized plankton sample into **four equal, independently retrievable fractions**, addressing a recognized gap in [[Plankton Analysis]] methodology.
- **Operational protocol**: sample is agitated to homogeneity in the outer cylinder; the inner (partitioned) cylinder is pressed down to isolate one quarter-section over the drain hole; that fraction is drained and flushed with distilled water (via polyethylene squeeze bottle) to ensure complete organism recovery; remaining three-fourths can be returned to the original sample container.
- **Validation test**: a comparative count across all four chambers, using a sample containing organisms ranging in size from *Ceratium* to amphipods, produced **satisfactory agreement between sections**, supporting the device's reliability for sub-sampling heterogeneous assemblages.
- **Acknowledged limitations**: as with any sample-dividing method, the device is subject to inherent sub-sampling error — **rare species may be missed**, and **accuracy decreases as organism numbers decrease**.
- **Practical advantages**: the sub-sampler is **inexpensive, simple to construct**, and **time-saving** relative to full-sample processing, making it a useful tool for high-throughput plankton and drift-sample analysis.
- The plexiglass redesign (4 sections, smaller overall dimensions) offers a simplified, more accessible alternative to Langford's original 8-section brass apparatus, trading finer subdivision for ease of fabrication and use.
