# Critical Habitat Designation for Colorado River Endangered Fishes

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This memorandum and Federal Register notice document the extension of the public comment period for the proposed critical habitat designation for four endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River Basin]]: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] extended the comment deadline from March 30, 1993 to April 15, 1993, and scheduled three public hearings in California, Arizona, and Colorado for March 29-31, 1993.

## Background & Description

All four fish species are endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system and are listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.). These fishes historically occurred in main channels of the Colorado River system from [[Wyoming]] to [[Mexico]].

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) belongs to family [[Catostomidae]]. The [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) belong to family [[Cyprinidae]].

The original proposed rule was published January 29, 1993 (58 FR 6568), proposing to designate 3,370 kilometers (2,094 miles) of the Colorado River system as [[Critical Habitat]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Significant changes have occurred in habitats required for the conservation of the four endangered fishes due to:
- Diversion of water
- Introduction of nonnative fishes
- Construction and operation of dams

Further habitat changes are anticipated as these activities continue. The proposed critical habitat designation would afford greater protection for the four fishes and provide for their conservation.

The four species historically utilized main channel habitats throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]], representing a riverine ecosystem that has been substantially altered by human activities.

## Key Findings

**Administrative Actions:**
- Public comment period extended from March 30, 1993 to April 15, 1993
- Three public hearings scheduled for March 29-31, 1993:
  - March 29: San Bernardino, California (Sturges Center for Fine Arts)
  - March 30: Phoenix, Arizona (YMCA Leadership Development Center)
  - March 31: Lakewood, Colorado (Sheraton Denver West Hotel)
- All hearings scheduled 5:00-8:00 p.m.

**Specific Comments Requested:**
1. Biological, commercial trade, or other relevant data concerning any threat (or lack thereof) to the habitats of the four endangered fishes
2. Reasons why any habitat should or should not be determined to be [[Critical Habitat]] as provided by Section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]
3. Additional information concerning the range, distribution, and habitat utilization of the four endangered fishes
4. Current or planned activities in the critical habitat area and their possible impacts on the four endangered fishes

**Conservation Significance:**
The proposed designation of 3,370 kilometers (2,094 miles) of the [[Colorado River]] system as critical habitat represents a major conservation effort for these four endemic endangered species. The designation aims to provide greater protection and facilitate recovery of populations that have declined due to habitat modification and other anthropogenic factors.
