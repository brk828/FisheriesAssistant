# Bifax lacinia, a New Genus and Species of Toadfish (Batrachoididae) from the South Coast of Oman

#ichthyology #marine-biology #batrachoididae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[David W. Greenfield]], [[Jonathan K.L. Mee]] and [[John E. Randall]] formally describes *Bifax lacinia*, a new genus and species of toadfish in the subfamily [[Batrachoidinae]] (family [[Batrachoididae]]). The fish was collected from crevices in rocky walls at 6-8 m depth on the south coast of Oman, in the [[Arabian Sea]]. The genus is distinguished by a very depressed head with wide, flat frontal bones, and by a flap bearing an eye spot at the end of the maxilla on each side of the mouth. The authors also report that the fish's skin mucus is toxic to other aquarium fishes and invertebrates, a finding they link to the species' defense against predators.

## Background & Description

**Discovery and collection.** During field work in southern Oman for the [[Oman Marine Science And Fisheries Center]] in 1989, the second author noted several individuals of an unknown toadfish. The anterior portions of three individuals were seen in horizontal crevices at the base of very large limestone boulders in a small bay near Raysut. Initial collection attempts failed, and the fish retreated deeper into the crevices. In 1990 the second and third authors returned and collected the first of seven specimens using [[Quinaldine Sulfate]]. The authors concluded that the species was undescribed and belonged to no recognized genus.

**Methods.** Counts and measurements follow [[Hubbs And Lagler Counts And Measurements]] (1964). Measurements were taken with dial calipers to the nearest 0.1 mm and expressed as thousandths of standard length (SL). Counts were made from radiographs. Osteology was described from a cleared and stained specimen, BPBM 35843 (267.5 mm SL). Colour descriptions come from 35 mm photographs taken above and below water.

**Type material.**
- Holotype: BPBM 35949, 215.7 mm SL
- Cleared and stained specimen: BPBM 35843, 267.5 mm SL
- Paratype: BMNH 1994.4.5.1, 264 mm SL
- Seven specimens were collected in total.

Institutional abbreviations used: [[Natural History Museum London]] (BMNH), [[Bernice P. Bishop Museum]] (BPBM), California Academy of Sciences (CAS) and [[Smithsonian National Museum Of Natural History]] (USNM).

**Diagnosis of the genus.** *Bifax* is placed in the Batrachoidinae because it lacks the venomous, hollow dorsal-fin spines and opercular spines of the [[Thalassophryninae]], and the two opercular and two solid dorsal-fin spines of the [[Porichthyinae]]. It shares with other batrachoidines three solid dorsal-fin spines and three solid opercular spines. Key characters separating it from other batrachoidine genera are:
- Very wide, flat frontal bones that form a shelf sloping down to fuse with the vomer, rather than separating anteriorly to receive the premaxillary ascending processes.
- A flap with an eye spot at the end of the maxilla on each side of the mouth.
- A fully ossified upper pectoral-fin radial, whereas several related genera have this element wholly or partly cartilaginous.
- Two subopercular spines, whereas most related genera have one.
- Solid frontal bones lacking foramina, unlike *Batrachoemoeus* and *Halophryne*.
- A completely scaleless body, unlike genera that are fully or partly scaled.
- A dorsocranium completely covered by muscle, unlike *Amphichthys*, *Sanopus* and *Triathalassothia gloverensis*, which have exposed bone.

**Description.** The fish has three solid dorsal-fin spines and no venom glands, three opercular and two subopercular spines, and a head that is greatly depressed. Pelvic-fin bases lie about two eye diameters apart. The colour description and three plates (holotype, aquarium specimen and underwater paratype) document the species in life.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Specimens occupied narrow crevices in rocky walls and at the base of large limestone boulders, at 6-8 m depth in a small bay near Raysut, Oman.
- **Morphological adaptation:** The authors propose that the very depressed head, with its wide, flat frontal bones, and the widely spaced pelvic-fin bases are adaptations to life in narrow crevices.
- **Toxin and defense:** Extensive glandular tissue lies beneath the pectoral fin. Mucus from the skin tasted very unpleasant to the third author, suggesting a toxin. The authors suggest this is a defense against predators, particularly large [[Gymnothorax Eels]] that occupy similar crevices.
- **Aquarium observations:** When the toadfish was placed in a tank holding two other fishes (one identified as a *multitaeniatus* species) and two starfish (*Fromia* sp.), the other fishes showed loss of equilibrium within ten minutes and the water surface looked slightly foamy. The toadfish behaved normally. The two fishes were moved to clean water but died within a further ten minutes. The starfish remained mobile but were removed as a precaution and were dead the next morning.

## Key Findings

1. **New genus and species:** *Bifax lacinia* is described as the type and only species of *Bifax* n. gen., within the Batrachoidinae.
2. **Unique head structure:** The genus has very wide, flat frontal bones forming a shelf that fuses with the vomer, an arrangement not found in other batrachoidine genera.
3. **Maxillary flap:** A flap bearing an eye spot sits at the end of the maxilla on each side of the mouth.
4. **Diagnostic counts:** Three solid dorsal-fin spines, three opercular spines, two subopercular spines, no body scales, and no venom glands.
5. **Distinction from related genera:** The genus differs from *Austrobatrachus* (including *A. dussumieri*, which has pelvic bases closer together and premaxillary teeth along most of its ventral edge) and from several other genera in pectoral radial ossification, spine counts, scalation and frontal foramina.
6. **Toxicity:** Skin mucus is unpleasant and probably toxic. Aquarium tests killed two fishes within about 20 minutes and killed starfish overnight.
7. **Habitat restriction:** The species is known from crevices at 6-8 m depth on the south coast of Oman, where three individuals were first observed in 1989 and seven specimens were collected by 1990.
8. **Adaptive interpretation:** Head depression, wide frontal bones and widely spaced pelvic fins are interpreted as adaptations to crevice dwelling, and the glandular tissue is interpreted as a predator defense.
