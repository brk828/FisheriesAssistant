## Executive Summary
This 1990 letter from B. D. DeMarais and T. E. Dowling (Arizona State University) to Dr. Anthony A. Echelle addresses the genetic risks associated with stocking hatchery-produced [[Virgin Chub]] (designated DNFH-1, 1989 year-class) into the wild population. The authors agree with Echelle's recommendation against stocking but reject his characterization of the risk as "trivial." They argue that the genetic consequences of introducing hatchery fish are fundamentally unpredictable and could range from negligible to severely detrimental, depending on the actual (unknown) size of the wild population. The letter emphasizes preserving genetic diversity in the remaining wild broodstock over immediate reintroduction efforts.

## Background & Description
The source text does not describe morphological traits of the [[Virgin Chub]] directly; the letter is concerned exclusively with population genetics and hatchery management. No meristic, morphometric, or coloration data are provided in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The Virgin River system, home to a natural population of [[Virgin Chub]] undergoing an apparent natural recovery ("on the road to recovery").
- **Population Status**: Wild population size at time of writing was uncertain but presumed low ("naturally produced individuals are rare").
- **Conservation Context**: The species was managed under an active recovery program involving captive broodstock maintained at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH), reflecting its status as an imperiled or endangered freshwater cyprinid.
- **Population Genetics Risk**: The core ecological concern is the potential for [[Genetic Introgression]] or allelic dilution/loss if hatchery-reared fish with a narrow genetic base are introduced into a small wild population.

## Key Findings
- **Broodstock History**: 160 broodfish (referred to as "WFD-1") were originally transferred to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Effective Breeders**: Of these, only a small subset successfully produced the 1989 year-class (DNFH-1):
  - Hatchery data: 6 females and 18 males
  - Recovery team minutes: 6 females and 10 males
  - This represents a very narrow genetic bottleneck relative to the original broodstock.
- **Hatchery Stock Size**: The DNFH-1 year-class comprised approximately **2,500 individuals**.
- **Remaining Wild-Type Broodstock**: 140 wild fish remained at Dexter, considered a critical genetic reserve/backup population.
- **Central Genetic Risk Argument**:
  - If the wild Virgin River population is large (~100,000+ individuals), introducing 2,500 hatchery fish would likely have negligible genetic effect.
  - If the wild population is small (~2,500 individuals), the same introduction could significantly and irreversibly alter allele frequencies and reduce genetic diversity.
  - Differences in fitness, size, or reproductive readiness between hatchery and wild fish could allow hatchery genotypes to disproportionately dominate reproduction — or conversely, have no effect at all.
- **Three Reasons Against Stocking DNFH-1** (synthesizing Echelle's two reasons plus the authors' emphasis):
  1. Natural recovery is already underway, making stocking unnecessary.
  2. Reintroduction would confound scientific understanding of natural population recovery dynamics, complicating future management decisions.
  3. **(Authors' emphasis)** The genetic effects of stocking are unpredictable and could cause irreparable harm to the native population's [[Allelic Diversity]] if wild numbers are low.
- **Management Recommendation**:
  - Avoid stocking DNFH-1 hatchery fish into the wild Virgin River population.
  - If augmentation is later deemed necessary, generate a **new, more genetically representative F1 stock** from the remaining 140 wild broodfish rather than relying on the genetically narrow DNFH-1 line.
  - Maintain the 140 wild broodfish as an essential genetic safeguard against future population collapse.
  - Note of caution: even maintaining 2,500 DNFH-1 fish in the hatchery was considered by the authors to be an inefficient use of limited [[Hatchery Resources]].

> **Core Principle**: "If naturally produced individuals are rare... stocking of hatchery fish with altered allelic composition could irreparably damage the native population by reducing both diversity and frequency of individual alleles."
