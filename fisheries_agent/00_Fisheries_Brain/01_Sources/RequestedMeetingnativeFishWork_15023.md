# Native Fish Work Group Meeting Notice - Lake Mohave Rearing Facility

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #native-fish-restoration

## Executive Summary

The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Lower Colorado Regional Office scheduled a [[Native Fish Work Group]] meeting for January 10, 1991, to discuss the finalization of the Rearing Facility Management/Research Plan for [[Lake Mohave]]. Primary topics included progress on backwater renovation at [[Yuma Cove]], proposed stocking operations, preliminary designs for a temporary barrier at [[Davis Cove]], and funding requirements for fish tags and monitoring equipment. The meeting coordinated multi-agency efforts involving [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), [[National Park Service]], and [[Arizona State University]].

## Background & Description

This document represents inter-agency coordination for native fish conservation in the [[Lower Colorado River]] system. The management plan proposed using [[Yuma Cove]] for spawning and rearing operations, with [[Davis Cove]] designated for grow-out facilities. The [[National Park Service]] completed environmental compliance and obtained necessary permits for the project. Backwater renovation at [[Yuma Cove]] was conducted by AGFD with Bureau of Reclamation assistance in early November 1990, with berm modification completed during the week of November 26, 1990.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project focused on creating controlled backwater habitats for native fish propagation in [[Lake Mohave]]. [[Yuma Cove]] was prepared to receive spawning adults, with capture, stocking, and spawning success monitoring coordinated by FWS and [[Arizona State University]] personnel. The facility design incorporated temporary barriers at [[Davis Cove]], with preliminary designs examining fine-meshed netting prototypes and concepts for more permanent structures. The backwater system aimed to provide suitable spawning and rearing habitat separate from the main lake environment.

## Key Findings

**Facility Development:**
- [[Yuma Cove]] renovation completed on schedule and ready for spawner introduction
- [[Davis Cove]] barrier designs under development by Reclamation engineers
- Multi-agency reviewers supported the two-cove management concept

**Monitoring Requirements:**
- [[PIT Tags]] (Passive Integrated Transponder tags) needed by August 1991 for marking production fish
- Tag costs estimated at $5 per tag
- Annual tagging budget projected between $10,000-$30,000 depending on production levels

**Management Considerations:**
- Determination needed for realistic production levels
- Assessment required for supplemental feeding necessity to ensure desirable growth rates
- Coordination required for permits, work schedules, manpower, and equipment requirements
- Spawner capture and stocking protocols to be established

**Agency Participation:**
Comments received from [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], and [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] on draft management plan distributed September 17, 1990.
