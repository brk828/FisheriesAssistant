## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary morphological or ecological research paper**, but an interagency administrative memorandum recording a contentious September 5, 1995 meeting between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Bureau of Reclamation (BR), and Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) regarding implementation of the **Central Arizona Project (CAP) Biological Opinion**. The CAP opinion mandates recovery actions and nonnative fish management for native, imperiled desert fish species in Arizona and New Mexico river systems, including the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Gila Trout]]. The memo documents jurisdictional disputes over authority to manage native and nonnative fish species, AGFD's objection to specific field projects (nonnative species removal, native fish habitat protection), and negotiations over cooperative funding agreements, monitoring protocols, fish barrier design, and public education efforts tied to the biological opinion's Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs).

While minimal quantitative biological data are presented, the memo is a valuable primary source for understanding **applied conservation politics, interagency governance, and management history** affecting several federally listed desert fish species and the invasive species threatening them.

## Background & Description

No morphological data (e.g., meristics, body plan, coloration) are provided in this administrative source. Species referenced are identified only by common name in the context of conservation/management actions:

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — small live-bearing poeciliid native to the Gila River Basin; subject of proposed augmentation stocking at Campaign Creek and Cottonwood Artesian sites on the Tonto National Forest.
- **[[Gila Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) — native salmonid discussed regarding reintroduction under Section 10(j) "experimental nonessential population" status on the Tonto National Forest.
- **[[Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*) — nonnative poeciliid targeted for removal at Bass and Medicine Springs; ecologically significant as a competitor/predator of native topminnows.
- **Nonnative catfish** (family Ictaluridae, unspecified species) — targeted for electrofishing removal on the Salt River.

No size, coloration, or anatomical descriptors are given; the source treats species purely as management/conservation units.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memorandum implicates several desert riverine and spring habitats within the [[Gila River Basin]], forming the ecological backdrop for the CAP Biological Opinion's Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives:

- **Native fish habitats, Gila Basin** — targeted for a water rights survey to identify protection/acquisition opportunities for imperiled native fish.
- **Bass and Medicine Springs** — spring habitats slated for mosquitofish (nonnative) removal to protect native fish assemblages.
- **Down Under Tank, Redrock Canyon** — isolated tank habitat requiring nonnative fish removal.
- **Salt River** — larger lotic system where electrofishing removal of nonnative catfish was proposed by AGFD biologists themselves.
- **Kingfisher Ponds, San Pedro River** — pond habitats on land managed by the Bureau of Land Management, targeted for nonnative fish removal.
- **Campaign Creek and Cottonwood Artesian, Tonto National Forest** — proposed augmentation stocking sites for [[Gila Topminnow]], previously vetted through Section 7 consultation.
- **Little Colorado River system** — identified by AGFD as a candidate system for future fish barrier installation to exclude nonnative species, modeled on BR's CAP barrier designs.
- **San Carlos Apache Tribal lands, Coronado National Forest, BLM lands** — jurisdictional land management contexts requiring landowner/agency permissions before project implementation.

The overarching ecological threat addressed by the biological opinion is the **spread and impact of nonnative fish species** (mosquitofish, catfish, and others) on native, federally listed desert fish populations, compounded by water diversion impacts associated with the Central Arizona Project. Management approaches under discussion included fish barriers (to prevent nonnative fish encroachment), mechanical/electrofishing removal, baseline pathogen/disease studies, and population augmentation stocking — all core tools in [[Desert Fish Conservation]] and [[Nonnative Species Management]].

## Key Findings

- **Jurisdictional conflict**: AGFD asserted that native and nonnative fish species are the exclusive "property" of the State of Arizona, claiming the CAP biological opinion has "no structure in law," and that only AGFD can authorize management actions on any fish species — a position contested by FWS/BR, who cited Endangered Species Act authority over threats to listed species from federal actions.
- **Six field projects objected to by AGFD** as politically unacceptable, including:
  1. Water rights survey/acquisition assessment in Gila Basin native fish habitats
  2. Mosquitofish removal at Bass and Medicine Springs
  3. Nonnative fish removal from Down Under Tank, Redrock Canyon
  4. Electrofishing removal of nonnative catfish on the Salt River
  5. Nonnative fish removal from Kingfisher Ponds, San Pedro River
  6. Study of mechanical removal efficacy for nonnative fish (later found to be a New Mexico project, objection withdrawn)
- **AGFD conditionally accepted** the Gila Basin water rights project once "acquisition" language was removed, and dropped opposition to the mechanical-removal efficacy study upon learning it was sited in New Mexico.
- **Augmentation stocking** of [[Gila Topminnow]] at two Tonto National Forest sites (Campaign Creek, Cottonwood Artesian) had already undergone Section 7 consultation with AGFD Nongame Branch involvement, identifying augmentation as a desired future action — though AGFD later questioned its authorization status.
- **Gila trout reintroduction**: Tonto National Forest reportedly informed AGFD it would only permit reintroduction as a Section 10(j) nonessential experimental population, reflecting broader agency preference for reduced regulatory constraints on listed-species reintroductions in the current political climate.
- **Monitoring protocol disputes**: AGFD had submitted comments (attributed in part to biologist Kirk Young) on BR's draft monitoring protocol; AGFD sought to control/filter all technical communication through designated contacts (Bruce Taubert, Jim Burton, Bill Werner) rather than allow direct biologist-to-biologist contact, citing a desire to "shield" the AGFD Nongame Branch from political controversy amid legislative threats to abolish that branch.
- **Fish barrier design**: BR was actively designing CAP-mandated fish barriers to exclude nonnative species; private landowners had refused site-study access, shifting focus to allottee permissions on San Carlos Apache land. AGFD expressed interest in applying BR barrier designs to the [[Little Colorado River]] system.
- **Baseline disease/pathogen study**: AGFD raised no objection to this project, indicating some cooperative common ground beyond the contested removal projects.
- **Broader governance outcome**: Parties agreed to pursue a multi-year cooperative agreement (amended annually with project lists) between BR, FWS, AGFD, and NMGF, with project ideas to be drawn from existing agency backlogs and Desert Fishes Recovery Team input, rather than establishing an elaborate new project-generation mechanism.

> **Note for researchers**: This document is best cross-referenced with the original **CAP Biological Opinion** and associated **Gila Topminnow and Gila Trout Recovery Plans** for the underlying scientific and legal basis of the management actions discussed here. It exemplifies the sociopolitical dimension of implementing the [[Endangered Species Act]] recovery actions for [[Desert Fishes]] in the American Southwest.
