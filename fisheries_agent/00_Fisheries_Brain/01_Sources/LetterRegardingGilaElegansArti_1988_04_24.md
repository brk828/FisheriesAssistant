## Executive Summary
This document is a 1988 letter from Dr. Donald G. Buth (UCLA Department of Biology) to Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University), discussing two related manuscripts on North American cyprinid fishes of the genus [[Gila]]. The first concerns a draft article on [[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail) incorporating allozyme (protein electrophoresis) data, being prepared for submission to the *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*. The second, still in preparation, concerns systematic/genetic analysis of the "[[Gila robusta complex]]" — a taxonomically problematic group of chub species. The letter is administrative/collaborative in nature rather than a primary data report, but it documents methodology, authorship logistics, and the state of an ongoing allozyme-based taxonomic revision of *Gila* species in the Colorado River Basin.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological character data (meristics, body proportions, coloration) are presented in this correspondence. The letter references that the draft manuscript on *Gila elegans* contains substantially more content than allozyme data alone, implying inclusion of additional morphological or life-history information in the original article draft — but these specifics are not reproduced in the letter itself.

Key taxonomic entities mentioned:
- **[[Gila elegans]]** — subject of a near-complete draft manuscript combining allozyme data with other lines of evidence.
- **[[Gila robusta complex]]** — a species complex within genus *Gila* undergoing active genetic/taxonomic clarification; described as having unresolved identification issues requiring voucher-specimen verification for some samples.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Genus **[[Gila]]** comprises freshwater cyprinid fishes native to the Colorado River Basin and associated arid-land drainages of the southwestern United States.
- *Gila elegans* (Bonytail) and members of the *Gila robusta* complex (e.g., Roundtail Chub relatives) inhabit main-channel river reaches and associated backwaters, historically distributed throughout the Colorado River and its major tributaries.
- These taxa are of significant conservation concern (Bonytail is federally listed as endangered), which underlies the taxonomic urgency described in the letter — accurate species-level and population-level identification via allozyme markers has direct implications for conservation and management decisions regarding [[Colorado River Basin Fishes]].

## Key Findings
- An allozyme-based manuscript on **Gila elegans** was nearly complete as of April 1988 and was being routed for peer review; Buth recommended **Jim Shaklee** and **Tony Echelle** as potential reviewers.
- Buth emphasized that raw allozyme data cannot be usefully shared prior to full data entry and analysis, given the short (hours-to-days) pipeline from scoring to interpretable results — highlighting the workflow of allozyme electrophoretic studies in fish systematics.
- A second manuscript, addressing the **Gila robusta complex**, was in active analytical development; Buth was nearing completion of the dataset and planned to circulate computer output containing **allele frequency databases** to co-authors Minckley and Mayden.
- An unresolved issue was flagged regarding the taxonomic identity of certain samples in the robusta complex analysis, requiring cross-checking against **voucher specimens** before final figures could be produced.
- A collaborative work session was planned for **Rick Mayden's** visit to UCLA in mid-May 1988 to finalize the robusta complex analysis and to discuss the broader remaining database for genus **[[Gila]]**.
- This letter provides historical documentation of the collaborative process (Buth, Minckley, Mayden) underlying what became foundational allozyme systematic work on *Gila* species of the American Southwest.
