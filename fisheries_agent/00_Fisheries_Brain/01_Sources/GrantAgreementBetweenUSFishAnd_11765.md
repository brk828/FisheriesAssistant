# Grant Agreement: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service & Arizona State University

## Executive Summary
This document is **not a primary scientific research paper**, but a formal **grant agreement** (FWS Agreement No. 1448-20181-99-G951) between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) and Arizona State University's Department of Biology. The $5,500 award, active for a 6-month period of performance, funded an **information exchange initiative** between Mexican and U.S. researchers/managers focused on aquatic resources in arid lands. The core deliverable was compiling and reproducing scientific literature referenced in a 1993 bibliography (and subsequent publications) concerning the natural history, hydrology, and biology of the **[[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]]**, Coahuila, Mexico. Though administrative in nature, the document is scientifically significant because it references [[W.L. Minckley]]'s foundational bibliographic work on this globally important desert wetland ecosystem and draws an explicit ecological parallel to **[[Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge]]** in New Mexico.

## Background & Description
No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic descriptions of specific fish or aquatic species are provided in this document. The agreement is administrative/financial in scope and does not contain primary biological data. However, it implicitly references the existence of numerous **endemic aquatic organisms** (fish, invertebrates, and other taxa) inhabiting the springs and sinkholes of Cuatro Ciénegas, without detailing specific species identities, morphology, or diagnostic characters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Cuatro Ciénegas Basin (Coahuila, Mexico):**
  - Located in central Coahuila, characterized by **[[Karst Terrain]]** and associated **groundwater hydrology**.
  - Contains numerous springs and sinkholes ("pozas") that have driven a **high rate of endemism** among aquatic organisms.
  - Long-standing importance to local agricultural interests (>200 years).
  - Formally designated a **protected area of flora and fauna** in 1994 (Área de Protección de Flora y Fauna, SEDESOL).
  - Functions as a refuge for rare and unique aquatic species dependent on isolated spring-fed habitats.

- **Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge (southeastern New Mexico):**
  - Analogous desert wetland ecosystem featuring springs and sinkholes.
  - Also harbors a variety of **rare aquatic animals**.
  - Surrounding basin is heavily utilized for agricultural development, paralleling the land-use pressures at Cuatro Ciénegas.

- **Shared Ecological Theme:** Both sites exemplify the tension between **groundwater-dependent endemic aquatic biodiversity** and **agricultural water demand** in arid-land basins, underscoring the rationale for cross-border conservation collaboration.

## Key Findings
- The agreement's central purpose was to **fund bilateral scientific information exchange** rather than conduct new field research or biological surveys.
- Two copies of all attainable papers referenced in **Minckley (1994)** — *"A bibliography for natural history of the Cuatro Cienegas Basin and Environs, Coahuila, Mexico"* (Proceedings of the 1993 Annual Symposium, [[Desert Fishes Council]] 25:47–64) — were to be compiled, along with relevant papers published since that bibliography.
- One copy of each reproduced paper was designated for the **Área de Protección de Flora y Fauna** (Cuatro Ciénegas, Coahuila, Mexico); the second copy was retained for official U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service use.
- The project formalizes recognition of **Cuatro Ciénegas** as a critical site of aquatic endemism, comparable in ecological importance and threat profile to **Bitter Lake NWR**.
- **Dr. W.L. Minckley**, a leading authority on desert fishes and author of the referenced bibliography, served as the University's Project Officer, reinforcing the scientific credibility underpinning the agreement's biological rationale.
- The agreement highlights the role of **institutional and archival science communication** (literature compilation/exchange) as a conservation tool for supporting resource management decisions in shared, water-stressed arid ecosystems.
- No new empirical data on fish populations, water chemistry, or species inventories were generated under this specific agreement; its output was bibliographic and archival in nature.
