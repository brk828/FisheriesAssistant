# Benthic Macroinvertebrate Secondary Production Estimates

#benthos #secondary-production #freshwater-ecology #trichoptera #chironomidae

## Executive Summary

This scanned document is a set of tabulated field calculations, not a narrative report. It contains monthly averages and variances of abundance and biomass, size-class breakdowns, and secondary production estimates for several benthic macroinvertebrate taxa, most apparently [[Trichoptera]] (caddisflies) and [[Chironomidae]] (midges). No fish are recorded. The OCR is heavily degraded, so taxon names, dates, and some units are uncertain. The most reliable content is the summary tables of standing stock, production, and P/B ratios, which follow the [[Cohort Production Interval]] (CPI) approach with [[Hynes Method]] variance estimates.

## Background & Description

The tables are organised by taxon and by size class. Each taxon block gives:

- **Monthly means and variances** of number per sample (N) and biomass (dry weight, typically reported in grams per square foot and grams per square metre).
- **Size-class distributions**, with sample counts and mean weights, listed for roughly 15 to 20 collection dates per taxon.
- **Annual summaries**: [[Standing Stock]], variance of standing stock, standard error, annual [[P/B Ratio]] (production divided by mean biomass), and a CPI-modified P/B ratio.
- **Production estimates** with upper and lower bounds, expressed as dry g/m²/yr and kg/ha/yr.

Taxon labels that could be read include **Helicopsyche**, **Corynoneura**, and **Psychomyia**, with other partly legible names. Each taxon has its own block, so the document reads as a multi-taxon secondary production dataset.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not describe the study site, habitat, water chemistry, or sampling protocol. The units and the CPI method point to a benthic sampling programme in which secondary production is calculated from repeated size-frequency samples through the year. Secondary production estimates of this kind relate standing crop to the rate at which biomass is generated, which is central to energy flow through stream or lake food webs. Caddisfly and midge larvae are important grazers, detritivores, and prey for fish, so these values are relevant to fisheries productivity even though no fish appear in the record.

Because the location, habitat, and dates are not legible, the ecological context cannot be confirmed from the text alone.

## Key Findings

**Annual standing stock and production (values as legible; verify against scan):**

- One taxon block gives an annual standing stock of about **0.680 g/m²**. Its variance and standard error are reported but are illegible in places.
- Another block gives an annual standing stock of about **0.873** (units garbled, likely g/m²), with an annual P/B ratio near the 3 range and a CPI-modified P/B ratio close to **1.36**.
- A further taxon block lists annual standing stock values of roughly **0.155** and **0.136** g/m², with CPI-modified P/B ratios that are partly legible.
- The first taxon block gives CPI production estimates around **90 to 104 dry g/m²/yr**, with upper and lower bounds. Standing stock and production figures are not always consistent across blocks because the OCR has corrupted some digits.

**Variance and estimator details:**

- Variance of standing stock is estimated with the [[Hynes Method]]. Several blocks report it in g/m² and g/ft² units, with values ranging from about 0.00000 to 4.5 in the most extreme cases. These values should be treated with caution.
- Sample-level statistics (N, mean biomass, standard error) are given for every collection date. Sample sizes per date are typically around 10 to 15 in legible entries.

**Size structure:**

- Each taxon has a [[Size Class Distribution]] table. Size classes are marked by the mean body length of the sample, and the tables show how biomass shifts across size classes over the year.
- Smaller size classes dominate abundance counts, while biomass is weighted toward larger classes. This pattern is typical of cohort-based production calculations.

**Overall:**

- The document delivers the raw and derived quantities needed for a [[Secondary Production]] analysis: abundance, biomass, cohort structure, standing stock, production, and P/B ratios.
- Its value depends on accurate digitisation. Several numeric fields, unit labels, dates, and taxon names are corrupted in the scan, so any use of these values should start from the original source.
