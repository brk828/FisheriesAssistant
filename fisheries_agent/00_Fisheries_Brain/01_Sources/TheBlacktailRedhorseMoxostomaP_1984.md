# The blacktail redhorse, Moxostoma poecilurum (Catostomidae), in Kentucky, with other additions to the state ichthyofauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #malacology #unionidae #catostomidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1984 Transactions of the Kentucky Academy of Science note reports a 1982 survey of freshwater unionacean mussels from six sites along [[Elkhorn Creek]] (North Fork, South Fork, and main stem), a tributary of the [[Kentucky River]] in the Bluegrass region near Lexington, Georgetown, and Frankfort, Kentucky. Nineteen native mussel species plus the invasive Asiatic clam [[Corbicula fluminea]] were documented via hand-picking of fresh-dead shells. The same journal issue also includes a companion faunal note documenting new Kentucky fish records, most notably the first state record of the [[Blacktail Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma poecilurum*) from [[Terrapin Creek]] (Graves County), alongside other range extensions and introductions affecting the Kentucky ichthyofauna. Collectively, the notes underscore Elkhorn Creek and Terrapin Creek as biologically significant freshwater systems within the [[Kentucky River]] and [[Obion River]] drainages.

## Background & Description
**Mussels (Unionidae):**
- Voucher material limited to fresh-dead shells; live specimens not systematically retained.
- Notable size-class/abundance species: [[Elliptio dilatata]] (34 shells, most abundant), [[Lampsilis radiata luteola]] (39 shells), [[Fusconaia flava]] (27 shells), [[Lasmigona costata]] (24 shells).
- Rare/singleton taxa: [[Anodonta imbecillis]], [[Lasmigona complanata]], and [[Quadrula quadrula]] each represented by a single shell.
- [[Corbicula fluminea]] (Asiatic clam) occurred in large numbers at all six stations, indicating high-density invasive colonization.

**Fish (Catostomidae — Moxostoma poecilurum):**
- Two Kentucky specimens (juveniles) collected from Terrapin Creek: SIUC 7720 (41 mm SL, 6 March) and SIUC 7852 (62 mm SL, 18 June 1983).
- Comparative Tennessee (Obion River drainage) specimens: a large juvenile (SIUC 7828, 186 mm SL) and a tuberculate adult male with running milt (SIUC 5053, 250 mm SL), the latter indicating breeding condition/reproductive readiness.
- Tuberculate nuptial features and running milt in the adult male are classic catostomid breeding morphology markers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**Habitat — Elkhorn Creek mussel survey:**
- Elkhorn Creek is a shallow (rarely >1 m deep), low-gradient stream (~170 km total length including both forks) draining rich agricultural (bluegrass) farmland before discharging into the [[Kentucky River]] ~16 km north of Frankfort.
- Mussel sampling used hand-picking from water and shoreline substrate across six defined stations spanning North Fork, South Fork, and main-stem reaches.
- Species richness varied by site (range: 6–14 species per station), with main-stem downstream sites (Site 6, Frankfort Federal Fish Hatchery) showing highest richness (14 species).
- [[Corbicula fluminea]] presence at every station reflects the broader pattern of Asian clam invasion into eastern U.S. drainages during this period, with potential competitive impacts on native unionids.

**Habitat — Fish faunal notes:**
- *Moxostoma poecilurum* is primarily a Gulf Slope species (Galveston Bay tributaries, Texas, to Choctawhatchee drainage, Florida); its occurrence in [[Terrapin Creek]], Kentucky represents the northernmost known record for the species, well outside its core range.
- Closest reproducing population occurs in the [[Obion River]], Tennessee, itself at low density (2 of 25 targeted collections over 5 years yielded specimens).
- [[Terrapin Creek]] hosts an unusually diverse assemblage found nowhere else in Kentucky, including [[Noturus hildebrandi]] (least madtom), [[Noturus phaeus]] (brown madtom), [[Etheostoma swaini]] (gulf darter), [[Etheostoma zonale lynceum]] (banded darter), and an undescribed lowland snubnose darter (*Etheostoma* [*Nanostoma*] sp.).
- Additional unusual reproducing populations in Terrapin Creek: a dwarfed, partially neotenic [[Lampetra aepyptera]] (least brook lamprey), [[Umbra limi]] (central mudminnow), [[Notropis camurus]] (bluntface shiner), [[Elassoma zonatum]] (banded pygmy sunfish), [[Lepomis marginatus]] (dollar sunfish), and [[Etheostoma parvipinne]] (goldstripe darter).
- Terrapin Creek was designated an Outstanding Resource Water by the Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission in recognition of this diversity.

## Key Findings
- **Mussel diversity:** 19 native unionacean species plus *Corbicula fluminea* documented in Elkhorn Creek (1982); dominant natives were [[Elliptio dilatata]], [[Fusconaia flava]], [[Lampsilis radiata luteola]], and [[Lasmigona costata]].
- **Invasive species baseline:** Widespread, abundant establishment of [[Corbicula fluminea]] across all six survey stations, an early record of Asian clam saturation in a Kentucky River tributary.
- **New Kentucky fish state total:** Recent additions (including *M. poecilurum*) bring Kentucky's reported native+established fish fauna to 234 species, with only 13–14 attributable to human introduction.
- **First Kentucky record of Blacktail Redhorse:** *Moxostoma poecilurum* confirmed via two juvenile specimens from [[Terrapin Creek]] — the most northern record for the species and a significant range extension also documented in the North Fork Obion River, Tennessee.
- **Other faunal additions noted:** Introduced [[Alosa pseudoharengus]] (alewife, Ohio River), introduced [[Salvelinus namaycush]] (lake trout, Dale Hollow Lake), introduced [[Culaea inconstans]] (brook stickleback, Levisa Fork/Big Sandy and Owsley County), and native [[Moxostoma valenciennesi]] (greater redhorse), now extirpated from the Ohio River.
- **Non-established exotics recorded but excluded from state fauna list:** [[Hypophthalmichthys molitrix]] (silver carp, Ohio River below Uniontown Dam) and an unidentified *Serrasalmus* piranha species from Lighthouse Lake near Louisville.
- **Conservation significance:** Terrapin Creek is flagged as a unique, high-priority conservation resource in Kentucky due to its unparalleled concentration of rare and range-restricted fish taxa.
