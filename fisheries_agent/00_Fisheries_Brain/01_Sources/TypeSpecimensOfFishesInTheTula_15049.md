# Type Specimens of Fishes in the Tulane University Collection

#ichthyology #marine-biology #type-specimens

## Executive Summary

This paper, by [[Royal D. Suttkus]] of the Department of Biology at [[Tulane University]], gives a brief history of the university's fish collection and a catalogue of its type specimens. Publication of the list follows the recommendation of the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] that institutions housing types publish them. The modern collection was begun in 1950, and by 31 December 1969 it held 60,030 catalogue entries totalling 1,849,315 specimens. The paper lists holotypes and paratypes for dozens of species, mainly freshwater fishes of the southeastern United States, with marine and Mexican material also represented.

## Background & Description

The collection is not a typological or purely local reference collection. Its strengths are southeastern, southern and southwestern United States freshwater fishes, with good coverage of central United States, Mexican, and Costa Rican freshwater fishes, and of marine fishes from the [[Gulf of Mexico]] and [[Caribbean Sea]].

Types are kept in a separate room. Holotype jars are ringed with red paint and paratype jars with blue paint. Specimens are arranged by family in phylogenetic order following [[Berg Classification of Fishes]] (1947), with genera and species alphabetical within families. Most material is stored in 40% isopropyl alcohol, diluted from 99% stock with tap water. Earlier series (about 1951 to 1955) were kept in 70% ethanol.

Other holdings include:
- A fish skeletal collection of 549 specimens, mostly complete skeletons, catalogued separately.
- 953 x-ray negatives, representing 12,042 specimens, catalogued on file cards.

The collection's history runs from an earlier exhibit museum at Tulane, which was disbanded in the early 1950s. Only two dry-mounted specimens survived from it: a stuffed [[Lake Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*) labelled "Niagara Falls, July 1881" and a partial skin of a [[Cobia]] (*Rachycentron canadum*) labelled "New Orleans, June 3, 1894". The modern collection was started by Suttkus in the fall of 1950, and the first permanent catalogue entry was made on 11 July 1951. The collection has been curated by volunteer students and later supported by staff appointments: an assistant from 1965 and, after 1966, Frank Thomas. The collection is being moved to the [[Environmental Science Center]], 15 miles away on the west bank of the [[Mississippi River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The type localities document a wide range of habitats:
- **Freshwater rivers and creeks** in Alabama, Mississippi, Tennessee, Arkansas, Georgia and Florida. Examples include the [[Alabama River]], [[Pearl River]], [[Cahaba River]], [[Stones River]], [[Chattooga River]] and [[Bayou Pierre]].
- **Springs and spring-fed streams**, including the spring-fed darter habitats in Georgia and Alabama and the blind catfish locality in Coahuila, Mexico.
- **Mexican inland waters**, including the Rio Juchipila (Zacatecas), Laguna de Santiago Tilapa and Laguna de Almoloya (Puebla).
- **Marine and estuarine waters** of the Gulf of Mexico, Caribbean Sea and western Atlantic, typically sampled by research vessels *Oregon* and *Silver Bay* at depths from a few feet to 200 fathoms.

Several Alabama River paratype series are sampled at numbered river miles (for example, Yellow Jacket Bar, River Mile 129.8), which documents a long-term sampling programme on that river.

## Key Findings

- **Holotypes (7 in total):** *Algansea monticola*, *Notropis edwardraneyi*, *Notropis uranoscopus*, *Noturus munitus*, *Etheostoma ditrema*, *Percina aurolineata* and *Microdesmus suttkusi*.
- **Paratypes:** 30,190 paratypes of 47 species are reported.
- **Largest paratype series:** *Notropis edwardraneyi* is the most extensively represented, with many lots from Alabama River bars, including a series of 7,324 specimens taken with the holotype (TU 44028). Several lots exceed 1,000 specimens, such as TU 42737 (5,630 specimens) from Yellow Jacket Bar.
- **Correction notes:** The authors record several errors in earlier publications, including counts given incorrectly by Suttkus and Clemmer (1968) for lots TU 44045 and TU 42759, and a catalogue number given incorrectly for *Draconetta oregona* (TU 18831 not 188831).
- **Fish listed by family:** The list covers Clupeidae (*Brevoortia gunteri*, paratype from Aransas Bay, Texas); Alepisauridae (*Alepisaurus brevirostris*, Atlantic); Cyprinidae (about a dozen *Notropis*, *Nocomis* and *Algansea* species, mostly from Alabama, Tennessee and Mexico); Ictaluridae (*Ictalurus serracanthus*, several *Noturus* species, including *Noturus munitus* from the Pearl River); Percidae (several *Etheostoma* and *Percina* darters from springs and rivers in the southeast); and marine families from the Gulf, Caribbean and Atlantic, such as Serranidae, Triglidae, Batrachoididae and Tetraodontidae.
- **Complete citations:** The paper ends with full bibliographic citations for each described species, and a list of references including [[Berg]] (1947).
