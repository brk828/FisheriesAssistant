# California Trout, Salmon, and Warmwater Fish Production and Costs, 1965-66

## Executive Summary
The California Department of Fish and Game's 1965-66 hatchery report documents record-breaking fish production across the state's hatchery system. During this fiscal year, hatcheries produced **63,192,719 fish** weighing **2,276,895 pounds**, surpassing all previous records since reporting began in 1953-54. This represented an increase of 3,062,746 fish over 1964-65 and 189,836 pounds over the prior weight record set in 1963-64. Despite higher total operational costs ($2,159,800, up from $1,966,403), the average cost per pound of fish produced *decreased* slightly to $1.09/lb (from $1.10/lb), driven by increased production efficiency. A key driver of change was the newly implemented "[[Trophy Fish Program]]," which allowed larger trout to be included in catchable stocking loads, increasing average trout size despite lower numbers of some species like [[Rainbow Trout]].

## Background & Description
This is the 13th annual cost/production report compiled by the Inland Fisheries Branch, authored by David L. Ward, submitted March 1967 (Inland Fisheries Administrative Report No. 67-1). The report catalogs production from California's state and federally/municipally reimbursed hatcheries, covering:

- **Resident (freshwater) fish**: primarily trout species reared for stocking lakes, rivers, and reservoirs.
- **Anadromous fish**: [[King Salmon]] (Chinook), [[Silver Salmon]] (Coho), and [[Steelhead Trout]], reared for release into rivers to support runs blocked by dam construction.
- **Warmwater fish**: [[Channel Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Red-Ear Sunfish]], and Red-ear Bluegill Hybrids, a minor component of overall production.

Major hatcheries referenced include [[Darrah Springs]], [[San Joaquin Hatchery]], [[Fish Springs Hatchery]], [[Mojave River Hatchery]], [[Moccasin Creek Hatchery]], [[Trinity River Hatchery]], [[Nimbus Hatchery]], and [[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Anadromous fish production** is tied directly to compensatory hatchery programs mandated by water development projects (dams) that blocked natural salmon and steelhead runs. [[Trinity River Hatchery]] and [[Nimbus Hatchery]] operations are funded by the **Federal Bureau of Reclamation**, while [[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]] is funded by the **East Bay Municipal Utility District**.
- **Disease and diet management**: The report attributes record production partly to minimal disease/diet losses and improved food quality via comparative vendor testing programs.
- **Infrastructure failure and mitigation**: At Trinity River Hatchery, algal buildup clogged fine screens in silver salmon rearing ponds, causing screen failures and premature fingerling releases (average size dropped to 1,075 fish/lb). This was mitigated by installing **electrically driven rotary screens**.
- **Habitat-linked stocking policy**: The [[Lake Tahoe]] large fish program and the Fish and Game Commission's Trout Policy (effective 1965-66) permitted up to 10% of catchable truckloads by weight to be "trophy" trout (0.5–1 lb), replacing equivalent poundage of standard catchables — directly shaping size-class distributions released into the wild.
- **Warmwater fish sourcing**: Beyond hatchery rearing, warmwater fish (notably at [[Central Valleys Hatchery]]) were supplemented through procurement from nursery reservoirs/farm ponds and rescue operations in the **San Joaquin and Sacramento River delta** during spring and summer, reflecting reliance on wild/naturalized stock recovery alongside artificial propagation.

## Key Findings

### Overall Production (1965-66 vs. 1964-65)
- Total: **63,192,719 fish** / **2,276,895 lbs** (record high), vs. 60,129,973 fish / 1,879,769 lbs prior year.
- Resident fish: **27,325,996 fish** / **2,077,646 lbs** (+630,983 fish, +361,521 lbs YoY).
- Anadromous fish: **35,810,632 fish** / **195,573 lbs** (+3,250,238 fish, +34,780 lbs YoY).

### Trout (Resident Fish)
- **Catchable trout**: 7,745,090 fish / 1,770,815 lbs; average size **4.37 fish/lb** (vs. 4.7/lb in 1964-65) — larger fish due to Trophy Fish Program.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]**: Largest weight increase of any species (+272,058 lbs) despite ~1 million fewer fish planted, attributed to the [[Lake Tahoe]] trophy program.
- Decrease in rainbow trout numbers offset by increased **[[Kokanee Salmon]]** production (1,818,618 lbs / 5,706,756 fish), driven by improved egg availability and the Region 2 kokanee program.

### Anadromous Fish
- **[[King Salmon]] (Chinook)**: 26,784,287 fish / 59,045 lbs (vs. 29,129,011 fish / 42,692 lbs in 1964-65); poundage increase from yearling releases at Trinity and Nimbus hatcheries.
- **[[Silver Salmon]] (Coho)**: 3,149,320 fish / 79,477 lbs (vs. 1,310,281 fish / 87,585 lbs) — numbers up ~1.75 million but weight down due to smaller fingerling size (318/lb vs. 22/lb prior year); Trinity River Hatchery fingerlings averaged 1,075/lb due to early release from screen failures.
- **[[Steelhead Trout]]**: 5,877,025 fish / 57,051 lbs (vs. 2,121,102 fish / 30,516 lbs) — driven by a strong adult return to Trinity Hatchery in spring 1965 and larger yearlings from Mokelumne River and Nimbus hatcheries.
  - **Trinity River**: 5,163,557 fish / 19,148 lbs (up from 590,100 fish / 8,710 lbs).
  - **Mokelumne River**: 155,350 fish / 14,162 lbs; average size improved to 11.0/lb (from 21.6/lb).
  - **Nimbus**: 550,118 fish / 23,741 lbs; average size improved to 24/lb (from 60/lb).

### Warmwater Fish
- Total: 56,091 fish / 3,676 lbs (vs. 874,566 fish / 2,851 lbs in 1964-65) — fewer but larger fish.
- Decline in numbers attributed to poor channel catfish spawning year at [[Central Valleys Hatchery]].
- Average size: 15.26 fish/lb (vs. 30.7/lb previous year) — all warmwater species (catfish, bass, sunfish, hybrids) were larger than prior year.

### Cost Analysis
- **Total hatchery cost**: $2,159,800 (+$193,397 YoY), driven by salary increases, new equipment (planting trucks), and higher overhead — partially offset by lower per-unit food costs.
- **Overall average cost**: $1.09/lb (down slightly from $1.10/lb), underscoring efficiency gains from full-capacity production.
- **Trout costs**:
  - Fingerlings: $2.44/lb, $0.014/fish (down from $3.76/lb, $0.016/fish).
  - Subcatchable/catchable/broodstock combined: $0.874/lb (down $0.03 from $0.904/lb).
- **Anadromous fish costs** (state-financed only, salmon — no state-funded steelhead in 1965-66): Total $110,529; $1.51/lb (down from $1.77/lb) despite per-fish cost rising to $0.130 (from $0.011) due to smaller average fingerling size (37/lb vs. 356/lb).
- **Warmwater fish costs**: $75,466 total (vs. $51,227 in 1964-65); cost per pound rose to $20.53 (from $17.97), partly due to $11,203 in non-productive costs at the [[Imperial Warmwater Test Hatchery]].
- **Federally/municipally reimbursed hatcheries** (Table 5): Trinity River, Nimbus, and Mokelumne River costs reimbursed by the Bureau of Reclamation and East Bay Municipal Utility District, respectively — includes per-pound costs for compensatory salmon/steelhead production tied to dam-blocked runs.
- **Three-year average trout cost trend**: Declined from $1.01/lb (1964-65 three-year average) to **$0.97/lb** (1965-66), with decreases in food and operating expense categories cited as key drivers.
