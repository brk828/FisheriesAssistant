# Culturing fruit flies (Drosophila), and other live fish food: Parts one and two

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Drosophilidae #Daphniidae #aquaculture #live-fish-food

## Executive Summary

This 1954 two-part paper from *The Aquarium Journal*, authored by [[Clark Hubbs]] and [[Kirk Strawn]] at the University of Texas, details laboratory-tested protocols for culturing live foods for aquarium fish, focusing primarily on [[Drosophila]] (fruit flies) and secondarily on [[Daphnia magna]] and microworms. The work synthesizes genetics-laboratory culturing techniques (originally developed for *Drosophila* stock maintenance) into accessible methods for aquarists, emphasizing banana-mash media, half-pint milk bottle culture vessels, and species-specific handling for four *Drosophila* species: *D. melanogaster*, *D. simulans*, *D. virilis*, and *D. hydei*. The paper also addresses [[Daphnia]] culture in barrels/pans and provides comparative production data across fly species, establishing best practices still cited in classic aquarium literature.

## Background & Description

### Drosophila Life Stages
- **Egg**: Elongate, nearly white; comparable in size to an elongated black pepper grain. *D. virilis* is unique among the four species tested in laying eggs on the bottle wall (others lay on/near the medium surface).
- **Larva**: Small maggot-form; grows progressively larger, "working" (softening/darkening) the upper medium layer as it feeds. Larval size varies significantly by species — *D. hydei* and *D. virilis* larvae are markedly larger than those of *D. melanogaster* and *D. simulans*.
- **Pupa**: Motionless, darker-colored than larvae; morphologically distinct across species.
- **Adult**: Sexually dimorphic — females possess a larger abdomen than males; males emerge from pupation before females. Adult body size scales with species (*D. hydei* and *D. virilis* > *D. melanogaster* ≈ *D. simulans*).
- **Vestigial-winged mutant** (a *D. melanogaster* variant): Flightless due to reduced wings; behaviorally and reproductively inferior in culture compared to wild-type flies.

### Daphnia magna
- Freshwater [[Cladocera|cladoceran]] crustacean; reproduction and population health are visually indicated by the appearance of dark "winter eggs" (ephippia) on the body under adverse culture conditions (e.g., overcrowding, poor water quality).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Drosophila** occupy a detritivore/saprophage niche in culture, feeding on yeast growing atop a fermenting banana-mash medium — functionally analogous to their wild role as fruit-fermentation feeders. Wild-caught flies are most abundant near fruit markets and are collected preferentially in the 60–80°F range.
- **Daphnia magna** function as freshwater filter-feeding zooplankton, consuming solid banana-mash waste and wash water from Drosophila cultures; sensitive to dissolved oxygen, CO2, and residual chlorine, making them a bioindicator species for water safety (e.g., detecting unsafe tap water chlorine levels before use in fish tanks).
- **Trophic role as fish food**: Drosophila larvae are consumed by native Texas darters and minnows, largemouth bass fry, angelfish, and bettas — with bettas reportedly preferring larvae over daphnia. Scavengers such as [[Mystery Snails]], *[[Corydoras]]*, and *[[Hoplosternum]]* consume excess larvae in aquaria, though they also opportunistically prey on live larvae.
- Feeding suitability is size- and life-stage dependent: larvae are viable fish food only briefly underwater (~1 hour), limiting their use for fish requiring continuous live-food access; floating vegetation (e.g., *[[Riccia]]*) or nylon "rafts" can extend larval availability.

## Key Findings

- **Culture medium**: A standardized banana-mash medium (agar, malt extract, corn syrup, brewer's yeast, banana, live yeast) developed from University of Texas genetics lab protocols is presented as superior to alternatives because it avoids leaving medium particles in larvae after washing.
- **Optimal vessel**: Half-pint milk bottles balance surface-area efficiency, cotton-plug economy, and ease of fly transfer; larger containers lose efficiency due to surface-area-limited yeast growth.
- **Species suitability for home culture**: *D. melanogaster*, *D. simulans*, *D. virilis*, and *D. hydei* tolerate variable home conditions and grow well on banana mash; the vestigial-winged *D. melanogaster* mutant is inferior for culturing due to lower larval yield and higher adult mortality (sticking to medium).
- **Production comparisons** (half-pint bottle, per cycle):
  - *D. melanogaster* / *D. simulans*: 2–4 cc larvae (upright, no yeast) up to 5–9 cc with yeast supplementation.
  - *D. virilis*: ~5–9.5 cc depending on orientation and yeast use.
  - *D. hydei*: highest yield, 7–18+ cc (up to 24 cc in one sample), attributed to larger larvae and higher female fecundity (~150 eggs/day vs. ~50 for *D. virilis*).
- **Life cycle durations** (egg-to-egg, 76–82°F): *D. melanogaster* ~9 days; *D. simulans* ~11 days; *D. virilis* ~16 days; *D. hydei* ~18 days — meaning one generation of the larger species spans roughly two generations of the smaller species.
- **Temperature sensitivity**: Sustained temperatures above the high 80s°F sterilize or kill Drosophila; refrigeration halts development and can extend usable culture life to ~1 week post-first-pupation but eventually causes death if prolonged.
- **Harvesting**: Maximum larval yield occurs just before pupation, when the medium surface becomes semi-liquid; larvae are separated from mash via water stream and fine-mesh nylon netting.
- **Daphnia culturing**: Contrary to pessimistic assessments in prior literature ([[Innes 1952]]), the authors achieved high indoor *Daphnia magna* production using strong aeration, frequent heavy feeding with Drosophila-culture waste, and elongated (rather than tall) containers to allow particulate settling. Optimal temperature ~70°F; production declines sharply above 85°F.
- **Water safety indicator**: *Daphnia magna* mortality serves as a practical proxy for assessing whether tap water chlorine levels are safe for use in fish aquaria.
- **Microworm culturing**: Effective on banana mash or a tortilla-mix/brewer's-yeast medium in shallow enamel pans with wooden strips for harvesting; the tortilla-mix medium was found more durable than traditional oatmeal-based recipes.
- **Practical recommendation**: Two *D. virilis* culture bottles/day (with associated rotation of ~4 dozen half-pint bottles) is sufficient for most home aquarists' feeding needs.
