# USFWS Review of Spikedace and Red Shiner Interactions Study

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #threatened-species #habitat-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal review letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] [[Ecological Services]] office in Phoenix, Arizona, dated July 27, 1989, addressed to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The letter provides detailed comments on a draft final report examining interactions between the threatened [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and the introduced [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). The USFWS review identifies significant deficiencies in the report's organization, data presentation, and analytical rigor, recommending major revisions before the contract can be considered adequately fulfilled. The original study included both field surveys across multiple stream reaches and artificial stream experiments to assess microhabitat use and interspecific interactions.

## Background & Description

The reviewed study was contracted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to investigate interactions between [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), a federally threatened species, and the non-native [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). The original contract also included examination of [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) habitat use and interactions with [[Red Shiner]], but this component was suspended during the project. The study experienced numerous difficulties and substantial changes to project specifications during execution.

The study comprised two main components:

1. **Field surveys**: Spring-summer sampling conducted across seven reaches in five streams, including the [[Gila River]] near Redrock, New Mexico, and the [[Verde River]]. Data collection focused on microhabitat characteristics including substrate types and sizes, water depths and velocities, and cover types used by both species.

2. **Artificial stream experiments**: Laboratory studies using an artificial stream system designed to represent microhabitats differentially used by [[Spikedace]] and [[Red Shiner]] in nature. The system included multiple subunits (designated A1, A2, A3, B1, B2, C1, C2) representing different habitat types, though it lacked riffles and had lower velocities than natural systems.

Additional data collection included:
- Food habits analysis (noted as adequately presented)
- Length, weight, and sex data from preserved specimens
- Biomass estimates
- Triplicate kick samples for benthic invertebrates
- Drift samples for aquatic invertebrates

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addresses a critical conservation concern: the potential displacement of threatened native cyprinids by introduced species. [[Spikedace]] is federally listed as threatened, and understanding its interactions with the invasive [[Red Shiner]] is essential for recovery planning and management.

**Habitat characteristics examined:**
- Microhabitat preferences including substrate composition, water depth, water velocity, and cover availability
- Pool versus riffle utilization
- Seasonal habitat shifts (September-October observations noted terrestrial grass cover influencing habitat use)
- Aggregation versus non-aggregation behavior patterns

**Geographic context:**
The [[Gila River]] at Redrock, New Mexico was noted as problematic for study inclusion, as it does not support [[Loach Minnow]] and supports [[Spikedace]] only during wet periods. This raises concerns about the representativeness of data collected from this site for understanding typical habitat use in the presence of [[Red Shiner]].

**Preliminary findings on [[Loach Minnow]]:**
Field data indicated that [[Loach Minnow]] microhabitat was "so different from that of [[Red Shiner]] that no microhabitat relationships could be inferred that would reasonably explain spatial displacement of this species by [[Red Shiner]]." This led to suspension of further [[Loach Minnow]] work, though the USFWS review emphasizes this conclusion should be supported with actual presentation of the habitat data collected.

## Key Findings

**Major deficiencies identified by USFWS review:**

1. **Inadequate organization and writing**: With exception of the food habits section, the draft report was deemed inadequately organized and written, lacking adequate discussion of study results and containing confusing tables.

2. **Insufficient data presentation**: The report failed to include adequate summary data despite being a contract report (not a journal publication). Critical data on substrate sizes and types, water depths and velocities, and cover types used by both species were not presented, despite being collected.

3. **Incomplete integration of first-year work**: The 1988 annual report was included only by reference and summary rather than being fully incorporated. The final report should have included methods, results, discussion, and data from the total project in detail.

4. **Missing [[Loach Minnow]] data**: Despite field data collection on [[Loach Minnow]] habitat, no data or analysis was presented to support the conclusion that its microhabitat differed substantially from [[Red Shiner]].

5. **Incomplete specimen analysis**: Length, weight, and sex data from preserved specimens were not analyzed or presented. Length frequency analyses and sex ratios should have been included and compared with previous studies.

6. **Overreaching conclusions**: The conclusions regarding [[Spikedace]]/[[Red Shiner]] interactions were deemed "more far-reaching than can be supported by the results of the study." Confounding factors were not adequately considered, and most study results were equivocal.

**Specific technical issues:**

- **Artificial stream experiments**: Contradictory statements about [[Red Shiner]] habitat preferences (subunits B1/B2 versus A1/A2) required clarification. The influence of terrestrial grass cover (present September-October) versus interspecific interactions on habitat use was not adequately separated.

- **Statistical presentation**: Statistical data were not adequately presented. Confusion existed regarding significant differences in [[Spikedace]] distribution "on a monthly basis" when differences were found for only one unspecified month.

- **Sample size concerns**: [[Spikedace]] sample size from the [[Verde River]] was noted as very small, though specific numbers were not provided in the review letter.

- **Methodological details lacking**: Exact sampling dates, precise location descriptors (legal descriptions, latitude/longitude), reach selection criteria (representative versus "good" habitat), and various procedural details were not adequately documented.

**Tables and data presentation issues:**

- Tables Al and A2 were missing from the reviewed copy
- Table designations were inconsistent and confusing (using same designations as artificial stream subunits)
- Terminology was unclear ("Total pers." versus "Total Obs.")
- Arithmetical errors were identified in Table 1C
- Column labels were misleading regarding percentages versus counts

**USFWS recommendation**: Major rewriting of the report incorporating all comments. The report as submitted did not adequately fulfill the contract requirements. The review emphasized that incorporation of recommended changes was "well within the scope of the contract and consistent with the amount of funding involved."
