## Executive Summary
This study by Hubbs, Peden & Stevenson (1969) investigates the effect of constant incubation temperature on the developmental rate of the [[Greenthroat Darter]] (*Etheostoma lepidum*), a small percid fish native to the [[North Llano River]] in Kimble County, Texas. Using a temperature-gradient block system spanning temperatures from 7°C to 28°C in increments of ≤0.5°C, the researchers tracked over 1000 fertilized eggs through four developmental stages: early embryo, eye pigmentation, hatching, and starvation death. Hatching time ranged from 3 days at 27°C to 25 days at 12°C. The data indicate that developmental rate does not follow a smooth exponential curve but instead is better modeled as a series of connected straight-line segments, each presumably governed by a distinct enzyme system, with consistent inflection points near 12°C, 17°C, and 25°C across all developmental stages. The study also examined the interactive effects of egg agitation (aeration) on hatching time, finding that agitation accelerates hatching at cold temperatures but has little effect at warm temperatures — a pattern interpreted as an adaptive strategy balancing larval dispersion versus thermal escape.

## Background & Description
- **Developmental staging system** (adapted from [[Oppenheimer 1937]] stages, originally defined for *Fundulus heteroclitus*):
  1. **Early embryo** = Oppenheimer Stage 19
  2. **Eye pigmentation** = Oppenheimer Stage 28
  3. **Hatching** = Oppenheimer Stage 34
  4. **Starvation death** (post-hatch larval mortality from yolk depletion)
- Cross-species staging correlation is only approximate, as darters (Etheostomatinae) and cyprinodonts (*Fundulus*) belong to separate superorders per [[Greenwood et al. 1966]] classification.
- Eggs were incubated individually (or in small groups of 2–3 for control comparisons) in 20 × 125 mm screw-capped culture tubes (~26 cc volume).
- No morphological reversals in structural/organ appearance sequence were observed across temperatures, contrasting with findings in [[Salmo salar]] (Atlantic salmon) by Hayes et al. (1953).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Collected from a 100-meter segment of the North Llano River immediately adjacent to the confluence of the North and South Forks, Junction, Texas — a spring-influenced freshwater stream system.
- **Thermal ecology**: Eggs and larvae show strong sensitivity to ambient water temperature, with developmental rate strongly temperature-dependent, especially at early ontogenetic stages.
- **Reproductive/life-history strategy**: The [[Greenthroat Darter]] is inferred to hide egg clutches (non-schooling behavior of larvae), and hatching timing appears to be an adaptive trait:
  - At **cool temperatures**, delayed hatching may maximize larval dispersion once hatched, since agitation/water movement seems necessary to cue synchronized hatching, reducing predation risk from clustered larvae.
  - At **warm (sublethal) temperatures**, early hatching is favored, allowing larvae to swim away from thermally stressful conditions toward cooler microhabitats.
- **Thermal tolerance**: Heavy mortality and abnormality rates occur above 25–25.4°C, consistent with prior findings ([[Hubbs 1961a]]) on developmental temperature tolerances of Texan etheostomatine fishes.
- Survival curves show two significant thermal breakpoints: one near 12°C (coinciding with a developmental rate inflection) and another at 21–22°C (not aligned with rate inflections).

## Key Findings
- **Hatching time**: Ranged from 3 days (27°C) to 25 days (12°C); single eggs at the warmest stations survived 24–25 days despite extreme heat.
- **Developmental rate model**: Best fits a **square root of time** relationship rather than the reciprocal-of-time model proposed by [[Hayes et al. 1953]] for salmon.
- **Piecewise linear rate structure**: Developmental curves for all four stages show consistent inflection points at approximately **12°C, 17°C, and 25°C**, suggesting distinct temperature-dependent enzymatic control regimes for each thermal range.
- **Stage-specific temperature sensitivity**:
  - *Early embryo*: Time doubles with a 5°C decrease between 12–17°C (steepest response).
  - *Eye pigmentation*: Time is roughly half of prehatching time; doubles with 5–6°C increase between 12–17°C; less pronounced above 17°C.
  - *Hatching*: Doubles with a 6°C decrease (12–17°C) and 8°C decrease (17–25°C); steeper below 12°C and above 25°C.
  - *Starvation death* (yolk depletion proxy): Doubles every 8°C (12–17°C) and every 10°C (17–25°C); slope flattens further above 25°C.
- **Temperature sensitivity decreases with ontogeny** — earliest developmental stages are most strongly affected by temperature; effect diminishes progressively with maturity. Theoretical slope convergence estimated at ~1100 days at −23°C (extrapolated, not biologically realistic).
- **No reversal of organ formation sequence** was detected across temperatures, contradicting the reversal pattern reported by [[Hayes et al. 1953]] for trout — attributed to methodological differences (unaerated crowded containers, inconsistent egg introduction timing) rather than true biological reversal.
- **Effect of agitation/aeration**: 
  - Aerated eggs and culture-tube eggs reached eye pigmentation at equivalent times, but aerated eggs hatched earlier than culture-tube eggs at cold/intermediate temperatures — with convergence at warm temperatures (~5 days at 26–27°C in both conditions).
  - This suggests agitation's critical influence window lies specifically **between eye pigmentation and hatching**.
  - Comparable agitation effects were previously noted in [[Grunion]] ([[Walker 1952]]) and [[Cottidae|cottid fishes]] ([[Hubbs 1966]]).
- **Egg density/crowding control**: Comparing 1-egg vs. 2–3-egg per tube setups showed no distinguishable developmental differences below 20–24°C, indicating crowding did not substantially distort results.
- **Methodological critique of prior work**: Reanalysis of [[Hayes et al. 1953]] salmon data suggests their claimed thermal breakpoints (e.g., 9°C) are artifacts of coarse daily-interval sampling rather than true biological inflection points; all breaks were more consistent with ~12°C.
- **Conclusion**: Developmental rate control in *E. lepidum* likely reflects a series of distinct enzyme systems operating over specific temperature ranges, with shared inflection points across all measured life stages (12°C, 17°C, 25°C).
