# Biology and Ecology of Two Endemic Cuatro Ciénegas Pupfishes (Cyprinodon bifasciatus and C. atrorus)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This report, a multi-part ecological study from the Bolsón de Cuatro Ciénegas in central Coahuila, México, examines two endemic pupfishes, [[Cyprinodon Bifasciatus]] and [[Cyprinodon Atrorus]]. It describes their habitats, diets, behavior, population structure, reproduction and hybridization, with intensive fieldwork centred on [[Laguna West Mojarral]]. The basin's thermally stable springs lack plankton and rely on benthic algae, diatoms and snails for productivity. *C. bifasciatus* is an unusual pupfish: it lives sympatrically with many other fishes, shifts from a mixed diet to a detritus-and-plant diet as it grows, and forms permanent lek-like breeding aggregations. *C. atrorus* occupies harsh, variable playa and marsh waters, behaves more typically for pupfishes, and is limited by competition and high salinity. The two species hybridize only in disturbed or peripheral habitats.

## Background & Description

The basin lies in the Chihuahuan Desert, roughly 40 × 25 km and ringed by mountains above 2,000 m. Its isolated, varied aquatic habitats, including thermal headsprings, streams, lagunas, canals, saline lakes and marshes, have produced many endemics, including snails, crustaceans, insects, plants and vertebrates. Minckley (1969) reported that nine of the 18 fish species present in the basin are endemic.

*C. bifasciatus* is elongate and laterally compressed, with a more anteriorly inserted dorsal fin. Breeding males are silvery-blue with golden eyes and yellow caudal peduncles. Females and juveniles bear two or three horizontal stripes, and ocelli are absent from the fins. The species is relatively stenohaline and stenothermal and uses water to depths of about seven m.

*C. atrorus* is more typical of the genus. It generally lives in water under 20 cm deep, tolerates wide ranges of temperature and salinity, and avoids currents. Its principal habitats are marshes and saline lakes on the basin floor, with some isolated populations in headsprings of the southeast lobe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry and physical conditions.** Waters of the central springs are nearly constant in temperature, around 33.5–34 °C in Laguna West Mojarral, with pH 7.2–7.6, hard, alkaline water, sulfate as the dominant anion and calcium as the dominant cation. Water is spectacularly clear. Dissolved oxygen is consistently low, about 1.4–3.3 mg/l, and the pool shows little diel variation. Mean inflow velocity was about 0.4 cm/s. Table 1 lists the inorganic constituents of the occupied waters.

**Primary production.** No plankton was found in open water, and light-dark bottle experiments in 1968 showed no photosynthesis in the water column. Production is benthic: diatoms, filamentous cyanobacteria (*Lyngbya*, *Oscillatoria*, *Amphithrix*) and algal mats on travertine, sediments and macrophytes. Estimated summer gas production was 21.9 cc/m²/hour and winter about 10 cc/m²/hour, and copropel (snail fecal sediment) accounted for more than 70% of total gas evolved. Floating benthic material exported from the laguna was estimated at about 350 g dry weight/hour in daylight.

**Benthic fauna.** Snails (notably *Mexipyrgus*) are abundant, though under-sampled. Oligochaetes, chironomid larvae and the amphipod *Hyalella azteca* are the main invertebrates. Invertebrate distribution is patchy and non-random, with worms favouring coarse shelter-providing substrates and chironomids absent from strong currents.

**Vertebrate community.** *C. bifasciatus* co-occurs with almost all 18 known local fishes, including three undescribed *Cichlasoma* species, *Gambusia marshi*, *Astyanax*, *Notropis*, *Dionda*, *Ictalurus lupus* and a small population of largemouth bass. Turtles (*Terrapene coahuila*, *Pseudemys scripta taylori*, softshells), water snakes and wading birds also use the habitats. The proposed food web (Fig. 8) places [[Snail Feces And Detritus]] and algal mats at the base, with *C. bifasciatus* as a principal herbivore-detritivore.

**Riparian and marsh vegetation.** Shrubs and trees are largely absent from spring margins, which are dominated by sedges, grasses, bulrush, *Nymphaea ampla* (waterlily) and *Chara*. The adjoining marsh was dominated by *Chara* and had water chemistry and temperature (about 30–33 °C) suited to the species.

## Key Findings

**Diet and feeding ecology (*C. bifasciatus*)**
- Juveniles fed on a mix of plant and animal material, including chironomids, ostracods, mites, nematodes, oligochaetes and conspecific eggs. Adults ate mainly bottom debris, algae and snail-fecal sediment.
- Gut length increased linearly with body size: Y = 205.104 + 84.3019X (r = 0.86), where Y is the gut/standard length index and X is standard length. The diet switch occurred at standard lengths of about 24–29 mm.
- Thin-layer chromatography of depot fat matched diatom oils in several bands (three of four diatom bands congruent with depot fat), suggesting diatom oils are a major energy source.
- Diatoms, the stalked "overstory" of the aufwuchs, were the only plant material that appeared visibly digested.

**Trophic status**
- Based on gut contents, the species is principally a herbivore and detritivore, and probably the greatest-biomass fish in Laguna West Mojarral.

**Behaviour and breeding (*C. bifasciatus*)**
- Mature males hold congruent, permanent breeding and feeding territories. About 200 males held 30–60 cm territories in summer 1968, compared with about 12 in January 1969.
- Females school in currents at inflow pits, and young are randomly dispersed in shallows.
- Territorial pits function as markers, not feeding stations, a difference from most pupfishes. The authors suggest this may show one route by which territoriality could evolve.
- Territory placement was linked to protection from wind and to encrusting diatoms, especially on algal mats, sedges and vertical travertine.
- Reproduction occurs year-round, with a surge in spring and summer. Females had no distinct spawning season, and spawning appeared reduced in winter.

**Population and reproductive parameters (*C. bifasciatus*)**
- Sex ratio in fish under 26 mm: 39 males to 101 females (2:5) out of 140 reliably sexed fish, with 64 juveniles questionable.
- Females mature at 26–30 mm and males at 25–29 mm standard length. Maximum sizes were 45 mm (females) and 57 mm (males, with senescent males in one riachuelo collection).
- Total egg counts averaged 150.3 in summer females and 181.7 in winter females, with about 14.9 mature ova per fertile female in summer and 14.6 in winter.

**Behaviour and diet (*C. atrorus*)**
- Males hold isolated or semi-isolated territories without breeding aggregations. Both sexes dig and defend feeding pits.
- Diet is least diverse in extreme habitats, where it consists only of bottom material. In springs, it includes a wider range of plants and invertebrates, and pupfish eggs appeared in only one stomach.
- Lethal-stress concentrations were near 95 g/l total dissolved solids in summer 1968. In Laguna Salada at 240 g/l, dead fish were found.
- Fish in the saltier north side of a divided lake had pale, fragile fat and ovaries, and length-weight curves showed stress, supporting an effect of high salinity on fat metabolism.

**Distribution and hybridization**
- Limiting factors for *C. atrorus* are thought to be competition with *C. bifasciatus*, high mineralization and physiology. *C. bifasciatus* is an obligate stenotherm, and dispersal appears to be one-way into *C. atrorus* habitats.
- Hybrids occur in disturbed or peripheral habitats, such as the drained Posos de la Becerra marsh and a developing intermediate laguna in the Laguna Churince–Laguna Grande system.
- The authors conclude that the two species remain distinct because hybrids are selected against in the harsh barrial environment.

**Evolutionary interpretation**
- The authors argue that lek-like territoriality in *C. bifasciatus* is a response to predation pressure and that the species' unique behavioural features, along with its morphology, justify its distinctiveness, despite hybridization.
