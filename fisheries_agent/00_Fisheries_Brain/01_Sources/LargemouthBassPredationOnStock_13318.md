## Executive Summary
This 1986 Research Information Bulletin presents interim findings from the Utah Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit examining predation by non-indigenous [[Largemouth Bass]] on hatchery-reared, stocked [[Colorado Squawfish]], an endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River drainage. The study combined field monitoring of stocked ponds near Grand Junction, Colorado, with laboratory preference trials to assess bass diet selectivity among squawfish and other Colorado River forage fishes. Results indicate that juvenile squawfish are highly vulnerable to bass predation, particularly at smaller size classes, though survival was not solely a function of bass density, implicating additional mortality factors. The findings support a proposed moratorium on further largemouth bass introductions and recommend revised squawfish stocking protocols, including stocking at larger sizes (230–250 mm) and scatter-stocking in bass-free or bass-scarce habitats.

## Background & Description
- **Colorado Squawfish (stocked specimens):** 45–145 mm total length at time of stocking; laboratory trials tested three size classes: 50–65 mm, 75–90 mm, and 100–120 mm.
- **Predation vulnerability threshold:** Based on predator body length/mouth width-to-prey size relationships, squawfish reaching **230–250 mm** in length are estimated to be largely invulnerable to predation by most largemouth bass size classes present in the study ponds.
- **Largemouth Bass:** No specific size data reported for bass populations in the ponds, though bass and forage fish densities varied across the five study ponds by design.
- Size-selective predation was consistently observed: smallest squawfish size class was consumed with the greatest frequency in both field and laboratory settings, consistent with general predator-prey morphological constraints (relative mouth gape vs. prey body depth/length).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Five riverside ponds near Grand Junction, Colorado, selected to represent a gradient of [[Largemouth Bass]] densities and forage fish densities, simulating conditions in Colorado River drainage backwaters where squawfish recovery efforts occur.
- **Prey community assessed in lab trials:** [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], and [[Green Sunfish]] — representing the broader forage fish assemblage of the Colorado River drainage.
- **Predator selectivity:**
  - Bass preferred squawfish over both green sunfish and red shiners.
  - Bass showed equal selection between fathead minnows and squawfish in lab trials.
  - However, under pond (field) conditions, fathead minnows did **not** buffer predation pressure on stocked squawfish — squawfish were disproportionately more vulnerable in situ than lab equal-preference results predicted.
- **Trophic interaction:** For several days immediately following stocking events, bass stomach contents contained **exclusively** squawfish, indicating an acute predation pulse immediately post-release, likely tied to naivety/disorientation of hatchery-reared fish and/or a preference cue.
- This study is directly relevant to [[Endangered Species Recovery]] efforts and highlights conflict between non-native [[Sportfish Introduction]] and native fish conservation in the [[Colorado River Drainage]].

## Key Findings
- **Stocking survival:** Squawfish survival, checked 2–6 months post-stocking (depending on season), was **less than 1% in 9 of 13 stocking attempts** across October 1983, October 1984, and July 1984 cohorts.
- **Bass density not the sole driver:** In one pond containing **no bass**, survival across three separate stocking events was highly variable — 8%, 46%, and 0.2%, respectively — demonstrating that factors beyond bass predation (e.g., other predators, environmental stress, disease, or handling mortality) significantly influenced squawfish survival.
- **Size-selective predation confirmed:** Both field (bass stomach contents) and laboratory (preference trials) data concur that the smallest squawfish size class is preferentially and most heavily preyed upon.
- **Preference hierarchy (lab trials):** Squawfish ≈ Fathead Minnow > Green Sunfish and Red Shiner (in terms of bass preference), but this hierarchy did not hold under natural pond conditions, where squawfish suffered disproportionate predation despite the presence of fathead minnows.
- **Management threshold established:** A squawfish length of **230–250 mm** was estimated as the approximate size at which fish become largely invulnerable to bass predation, based on predator mouth-width/body-length allometry.
- **Management recommendations:**
  1. Stock squawfish at larger sizes (approaching 230–250 mm) when feasible.
  2. If smaller fish must be stocked, use **scatter stocking** in areas where bass are absent or scarce to reduce localized predation pressure.
  3. Support for a **moratorium on further largemouth bass introductions** into the Colorado River drainage.
  4. Broader implication: a moratorium on stocking additional non-indigenous fishes in the drainage may be warranted to support [[Colorado Squawfish]] recovery.
- **Study caveat:** Data are explicitly labeled provisional/interim, pending completion of research, further analysis, and final interpretation.
