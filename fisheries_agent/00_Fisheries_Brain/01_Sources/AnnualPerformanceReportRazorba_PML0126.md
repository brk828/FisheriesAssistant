# Razorback Sucker and Colorado Squawfish Reintroduction and Monitoring in the Verde and Salt Rivers, Arizona (FY 1996-97)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department performance report (Segment 3 of 5, July 1, 1996 to June 30, 1997) covers the [[Endangered Species Act]]-linked reintroduction and monitoring of two federally listed fishes, [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), in the [[Verde River]] and [[Salt River]]. The job objective was to evaluate survival of large sub-adults stocked into these rivers, track their movements, and determine whether stocking can aid recovery in Lower Basin waters.

Hatchery-reared fish from [[Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery]] (BPSH) were stocked into the Verde and Salt rivers. Surveys across both systems were dominated by [[Nonnative Species]], and few native fish were found. Stocked fish were recaptured in the Verde River, but the 1997 Colorado Squawfish movement pattern differed from 1996, and the report recommends further monitoring.

## Background & Description

**Target species**

- **Razorback Sucker** (Catostomidae): a large-bodied catostomid native to the Colorado River system, listed as endangered.
- **Colorado Squawfish** (Cyprinidae): a large native cyprinid of the Colorado River Basin, also listed as endangered. It is a piscivorous fish, and the report describes it as a large-river species.

**Propagation and stocking**

Propagation at BPSH focused on growing large individuals for release. Table 1 of the report lists stocking events for fiscal year 1996-97:

| Stocking site | Species | Number stocked | Mean total length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verde River, Beasley Flat | Colorado Squawfish | 999 | 362 mm |
| Verde River, Childs | Colorado Squawfish | 1,045 | 362 mm |
| Verde River, Childs | Razorback Sucker | 1,000 + 1,000 + 1,437 | 325 mm |
| Salt River, Horseshoe Bend | Razorback Sucker | 2,046 | 285 mm |

Totals were 3,437 razorbacks and 2,044 squawfish stocked into the Verde River, and 2,046 razorbacks stocked into the Salt River. Razorbacks for the Salt River came from [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] (Utah) and were held at BPSH.

**Marking**

- All stocked fish received [[Coded Wire Tag]]s for stock identification. Squawfish were tagged in the left opercle or cheek. Verde River razorbacks were tagged under the dorsal fin on the right side. Salt River razorbacks were tagged in the nose.
- Ten of the largest Verde River razorbacks received [[PIT Tag]]s.
- 340 Salt River razorbacks received PIT tags: 303 already tagged in Utah and 37 tagged by department staff.

**Survey methods**

Sampling used [[Electrofishing]] (canoe-mounted Smith-Root units), [[Gillnet Survey]] effort, seining, and trammel nets. Effort was reported as catch per unit effort (CPUE).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flows and water quality**

- Flows in both rivers were normal to above normal, driven by spring snowpack runoff. Wet late winter storms raised Verde flows earlier than usual.
- Salt River flow was 825 cfs in May 1997. Surveys recorded 880 cfs (April), 881 cfs (May), and 154 cfs (June).
- Verde flows were 722 cfs in January, 175 cfs into [[Horseshoe Reservoir]] in May, and 166 cfs in June.
- Conductivity averaged about 750 µS on the Verde River and about 1,050 µS on the Salt River in April and May. June Salt River samples exceeded 3,000 µS.

**Nonnative dominance**

- The upper Salt River survey (April 22-25, 1997) caught 5,796 fish. Nine nonnative species made up 99.8% of abundance. Native [[Sonora Sucker]] and [[Desert Sucker]] together made up 0.2%.
- Nonnative species dominated Horseshoe Reservoir, the lower Verde River, and most Verde River reaches.
- Native [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were recorded only in the Beasley Flat to Childs reach.
- [[Flathead Catfish]] was the dominant predator in the upper Salt River, and reports note its extreme piscivory.

**Parasites and health**

- Verde River Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker carried heavy parasite loads. Every fish had at least ten *Lernaea* (a parasitic copepod) attached.
- About 80% of Verde razorbacks had ulcerated sores, mostly on the ventral side.
- Roosevelt Lake's single razorback had no parasites.
- One decomposed razorback mortality was found at river mile 39.5. The cause was unknown, and the report states sampling did not cause it. No sampling-stress mortalities were recorded.

**Habitat use**

Squawfish were taken in fast water next to sand and cobble bars and in riffle and rapid habitats. None were collected from slow, slack, or backwater habitats, even though those habitats were sampled extensively.

## Key Findings

**Survey results by reach**

1. **Salt River, Roosevelt Lake arm (February 26-27, 1997):** Ten gillnets (22 net night units) caught 459 fish, a CPUE of 20.4 fish/NNU as reported. One Razorback Sucker (340 mm, 480 g) was captured off Pit Island. It carried a coded wire tag showing November 1996 stocking from BPSH, and it was PIT tagged and released. Electrofishing of the lower Salt River produced 80 fish (0.86 fish/minute).
2. **Upper Salt River (April-June 1997):** Three surveys recorded only nonnative fish, with the exception of two native suckers in April. No razorbacks or squawfish were collected in the linear surveys. Flathead catfish tagging continued, with 12 tagged in June. One recapture from 1996 was recorded.
3. **Horseshoe Reservoir (March 12-13, 1997):** Surveys caught 561 fish, all nonnative. Gillnets took 437 fish (CPUE 27.8/net night unit), and electrofishing took 124 fish (1.0 fish/minute). Eight nonnative species were recorded, including [[Walleye]]. No squawfish were captured, unlike in 1996.
4. **Lower Verde River (July 1996):** Three sites between Horseshoe Dam and Bartlett Lake yielded only 12 nonnative fish. Overall gillnet CPUE was 5.7 fish per net unit.
5. **Childs Powerplant campground (January 31, 1997):** 14 razorbacks and 22 squawfish were collected under high flow (722 cfs). No PIT tags were detected, and no wire tag reader was available. The report notes that squawfish stocked in 1995 moved 42 miles in 36 days to Horseshoe Reservoir, but similar movements were not documented for 1996 stockings.
6. **Verde River, Beasley Flat to Childs (May 28-30, 1997):** Six Colorado Squawfish and three Razorback Suckers were captured by electrofishing (Table 2). Six native species were recorded in total. Electrofishing CPUE was 1.96 fish/minute, seining 5.10 fish/m², and trammel netting 14.8 fish/net unit.
7. **Verde River, Childs to Sheep Bridge (June 9-12, 1997):** 2,608 fish were caught, with natives making up 2.5% and nonnatives 97.5%. Three Colorado Squawfish and 12 Razorback Suckers were captured. Overall CPUE was 1.7 fish/minute, 0.761 fish/m², and 10.8 fish/net unit.

**Stocking survival and movement**

- Thirty Colorado Squawfish were sampled between Beasley Flat and Sheep Bridge in this period. None had PIT tags, and all were tagged by field crews at capture.
- Of 29 razorbacks sampled from the Verde River and one from Roosevelt Lake, several came from the December 1996 stocking. Eight of 16 Verde razorbacks sampled carried coded wire tags from December 1996.
- One razorback at river mile 41.7 carried a coded wire tag from December 1995 and had survived about 1.5 years. It measured 426 mm and weighed 780 g, and appeared healthy.
- The Roosevelt Lake razorback was stocked in December 1996 at Horseshoe Bend.
- The squawfish stock in 1997 did not appear to move like the 1996 stock. Fewer squawfish were found in Horseshoe Reservoir.

**Recommendations**

- **Salt River:** Pursue an agreement with the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] to resume stocking at Gleason Flat, which tribal objections halted. Continue the target of 2,000 fish per species (>300 mm) from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center. Study nonnative control, especially of large catfish. Augment native suckers and [[Roundtail Chub]], and study [[Red Shiner]] control.
- **Survey standards:** Use at least 3,000 seconds of electrofishing, 10 seine hauls, and 3 net sets per day. Limit upper Salt River surveys to one per year above Horseshoe Bend until stocking resumes.
- **Verde River:** Establish stratified minimum effort per reach, including 10 seine hauls, 2 gillnet or trammel sets per night, and 3,000 seconds of electrofishing per day. Continue stocking at least 2,000 razorbacks and 2,000 squawfish (>300 mm) at Childs each year. Quantify parasite loads and their effects on reintroduced fish.

**Protocol deviations**

- Stocking at Gleason Flat and the U.S. Highway 60 bridge was not carried out because of tribal objections.
- Habitat measurements (depth, width, substrate, temperature, pH) were not taken at capture sites because of a misreading of procedures. Only qualitative habitat notes were recorded. Future recaptures will include habitat measurements.

**Other notes**

- No laboratory or museum specimens were collected.
- Sensitive-species locality data were submitted to the Heritage Data Management System for [[Section 7 Consultation]] with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
