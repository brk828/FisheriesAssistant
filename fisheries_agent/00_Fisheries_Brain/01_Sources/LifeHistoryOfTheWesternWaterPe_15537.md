## Executive Summary

This 1974 paper by Murvosh and Miller reconstructs the life history of *Psephenus falli*, the "western water penny beetle" (Coleoptera: Psephenidae), based on ~8 years of field and laboratory observation in southern California (Deep Creek, Big Rock Creek) and supplementary data from Idaho, Oregon, and northern California streams. The species is a riffle-dwelling aquatic beetle whose larvae ("water pennies") graze algae on submerged rocks. Combining laboratory rearing of sorted larval size-classes with field frequency-distribution sampling, the authors estimate a life cycle of **15–18 months**, notably shorter than the ~2-year cycle proposed for the eastern congener [[Psephenus herricki]]. Although life-cycle duration differs, microhabitat, behavior, and morphology of *P. falli* closely parallel those of *P. herricki*, differing mainly in adult body size. The study highlights extreme intraspecific growth-rate variability, even among larvae from the same egg mass, and flags taxonomic uncertainty surrounding *P. falli* vs. the Baja California *P. haldemani*.

## Background & Description

- **Adults**: Sexually dimorphic in size — males 3.3–4.3 mm, females 3.8–5.1 mm (females larger, consistent across the genus).
- **Eggs**: Lemon-yellow, deposited in tightly packed single-layer masses of ~500 eggs; combined ovipositions by multiple females can form masses of up to 2,000 eggs. Eggs are morphologically indistinguishable (superficially) from those of [[Psephenus herricki]]; no microscopic comparison has been done.
- **First-instar larva**: ~0.75 mm, whitish/colorless, essentially invisible to the naked eye on natural substrate.
- **Larval growth**: Body length used as primary size-class metric (measured live with dial calipers, 2–9 mm range observed). Minimum of 4 instars inferred from lab data; authors speculate up to 6.
- **Last instar**: Exoskeleton (pupal case) measurements range from 6.0–8.5 mm; females generally emerge larger, though a 4.3 mm female emerged from a 6.3 mm last-instar case.
- **Taxonomic note**: *P. falli* is now understood to be synonymous with previously described *P. veluticollis*, *P. calaveras* (California), and *P. lanei* (Idaho) (per Brown & Murvosh, unpublished). Distinguishable from [[Psephenus herricki]] primarily by smaller overall body size; otherwise morphologically very similar.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Lotic riffle zones of interior-draining mountain streams; adults and prepupae/pupae found on the lee and sides of wave-splashed rocks, particularly rocks projecting above the water line at riffle margins.
- **Key study sites**: Deep Creek (San Bernardino Mountains, draining to Mojave River) and Big Rock Creek (San Gabriel Mountains, crossing the San Andreas Fault at Valyermo) — both southern California, both with interior (closed) drainage.
- **Larval feeding**: Algae-grazers on rock surfaces; laboratory maintenance required algae-coated rocks and adequate light.
- **Oviposition site selection**: Females typically oviposit on undersides of splash-zone rocks in turbulent riffles; one atypical case on Idaho's Clearwater River involved oviposition in calm, non-turbulent shoreline water.
- **Microdistribution**: Prepupae and pupae are highly contagiously (clumped) distributed under shoreline rocks, mirroring patterns described for [[Psephenus herricki]].
- **Population patchiness**: Evidence of spatially segregated sub-populations within the same stream reach (e.g., adjacent riffles 300 m apart in Deep Creek showing opposite size-class dominance on the same date), suggesting limited larval dispersal/mixing.
- **Adult phenology**: Adults present roughly early May–mid August, varying by locality and year; emergence timing appears more genetically than environmentally controlled given consistency across diverse geographic sites. Adult field lifespan estimated at only 1–7 days; ovipositing females die shortly after entering the water ("dirty" appearance, never leave streambed).
- **Comparative niche**: Habitat, microdistribution, and behavior are described as essentially identical to [[Psephenus herricki]] in eastern North America, positioning the genus [[Psephenidae]] as ecologically conservative across a wide geographic range despite life-history divergence.

## Key Findings

- **Life cycle duration**: Estimated at 15–18 months for California populations, based on an average field growth rate of ~1 mm/summer month; contrasts with the ~2-year cycle hypothesized for [[Psephenus herricki]] (West 1929; Schafer 1950).
- **Growth variability**: Substantial variation in larval growth rate even within a single egg mass — some lab-reared larvae grew ~1 mm/month while others grew <0.5 mm/month, implying some individuals could require up to 2 full years to complete development ("opportunistic" extended cycles for late-season eggs).
- **Egg incubation**: Hatching took 16–17 days in the laboratory at 23°C, slightly longer than the 12–15 days reported for *P. herricki* (Schafer 1950; Davis 1965). Hatch success estimated at 75–90%.
- **No evidence of parthenogenesis**: Eggs laid by two unmated, lab-reared females failed to hatch.
- **Pupation duration**: Estimated ~2 weeks under lab conditions (vs. 10–12 days reported for *P. herricki*), though rearing conditions were considered non-representative of natural conditions.
- **Overwintering strategy**: Larvae hatching late in the season (August) generally fail to complete development before winter and overwinter as larvae; some individuals may overwinter twice, extending total development to 20+ months.
- **Adult emergence timing**: Consistently occurs May–August across California, Idaho, and Oregon streams sampled, with July generally the most reliable collection period; at least 2 years of sampling recommended per site due to inter-annual variability.
- **Species complex resolution**: *P. falli*, *P. veluticollis*, *P. calaveras*, and *P. lanei* are synonymized as a single species; unresolved taxonomic question remains whether *P. haldemani* (Baja California) is distinct from *P. falli*, complicated by the type series being based only on females.
- **Broader implication**: Despite the shorter life cycle, near-identical habitat, morphology, and behavior between *P. falli* and [[Psephenus herricki]] suggest life-history timing (rather than niche or morphology) is the primary axis of divergence between western and eastern *Psephenus* species — a pattern relevant to understanding [[Psephenidae]] biogeography across North America.
