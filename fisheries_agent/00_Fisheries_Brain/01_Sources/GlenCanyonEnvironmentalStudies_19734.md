# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Phase II Document Catalog and Review Correspondence

#administrative #environmental-studies #glen-canyon-dam #document-management

## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative catalog and correspondence from the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Phase II program, dated September 5, 1995. It consists of a cover memo from Dave Wegner requesting principal investigators to incorporate outside peer review comments into their final reports, followed by an extensive database listing of GCES research documents. The catalog contains hundreds of technical reports, trip reports, quarterly updates, and final documents covering aquatic ecology, physical processes, cultural resources, recreation, and water quality studies related to [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and their effects on the [[Colorado River]] corridor through [[Grand Canyon National Park]].

## Background & Description

The Glen Canyon Environmental Studies represented a comprehensive, multi-year research program examining the environmental impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on downstream resources. The document catalog organizes reports by study identification numbers (SIM_ID_NO), with categories including:

- **Aquatic studies (AQU series)**: Fish ecology, [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Rainbow Trout]], aquatic food base, invertebrates
- **Physical studies (PHY series)**: Sediment transport, sand bar dynamics, geomorphology, erosion processes
- **Terrestrial studies (TER series)**: Riparian vegetation, avifauna, waterfowl, marshes
- **Cultural resources (CUL series)**: Archaeological sites, ethnographic studies, tribal consultations
- **Water quality (WAQ series)**: Chemistry, temperature, nutrients, Lake Powell forebay
- **Recreation (REC series)**: Campsite availability, economic impacts, visitor use
- **Hydrological studies (HYD series)**: [[Little Colorado River]] hydrology, flow regimes

The cover memo emphasizes the importance of robust peer review to ensure credible information and professional quality documents for resource managers and decision makers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The GCES program addressed the ecological consequences of dam operations on a highly regulated river system. Key environmental contexts include:

- **Flow regime alterations**: Daily fluctuations from hydropower operations affecting aquatic and riparian habitats
- **Sediment dynamics**: Reduced sediment supply below the dam, erosion of beaches and sandbars
- **Endangered species**: [[Humpback Chub]] populations in the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence and mainstem habitats
- **Tributary influences**: [[Little Colorado River]] and [[Paria River]] as sediment and warm-water sources
- **Native vs. non-native species**: Competition and predation effects on native fish assemblages
- **Riparian communities**: Vegetation changes under regulated flows, including marsh development
- **Cultural landscapes**: Effects on archaeological sites and tribal cultural resources

Studies examined multiple spatial scales from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to [[Lake Mead]], with intensive focus on reaches between Lees Ferry and Separation Canyon.

## Key Findings

The document catalog reveals the comprehensive scope of GCES Phase II research:

**Aquatic Ecology Studies**: Extensive work on [[Humpback Chub]] life history and ecology (Valdez et al., multiple reports C1 series), habitat use in tributaries (Gorman, Leon et al., D series), effects of interim flows on aquatic food base (Blinn et al., P01, P16), and [[Rainbow Trout]] spawning and stranding (Persons et al., B3 series). Studies documented native fish distributions, thermal tolerances, age structure, and movement patterns.

**Physical Processes**: Comprehensive sediment studies including sand bar monitoring (Beus et al., P03, P18 series), daily photogrammetric monitoring (Cluer et al., P11, P12), sediment budgets (Smillie et al., P03(6)), and seepage erosion mechanisms (Budhu et al., T5, P03(1)). Research tracked sand storage changes, bar stability, and campsite dynamics under varying flow regimes.

**Cultural Resources**: Multiple tribal consultation programs including [[Hopi Tribe]] ethnographic studies (Dongoske et al., H series), [[Southern Paiute]] ethnographic inventory (Stoffle et al., Q4), [[Navajo Nation]] cultural resources (Roberts et al., N series), [[Hualapai Tribe]] studies (Jackson et al., J, K series), and [[Zuni Pueblo]] ethnohistorical evaluation (Anyon et al., U series). Archaeological site monitoring documented erosion impacts.

**Biological Monitoring**: Riparian vegetation monitoring (Stevens et al., P02, P08), avian community surveys including [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (Sogge et al., P15, P17), [[Bald Eagle]] winter use (Montgomery et al., P05), waterfowl populations (Pinnock et al., Q2), and breeding bird surveys.

**Water Quality**: Studies of [[Lake Powell]] forebay conditions (Bennett et al., E05), synoptic experiments (Averett et al., E10), nutrient spiraling in eddies (Parnell et al., P06), and temperature regimes affecting fish.

**Recreation and Economics**: Campsite inventory and availability (Kearsley et al., P10), non-use value studies (Welsh et al., G2), power system impacts (Stone & Webster, G3), and tribal recreation use ([[Hualapai Tribe]], K series).

**Integration and Monitoring**: Development of fisheries databases (Brown et al., C2 series), population models for [[Humpback Chub]] (Ryel et al., C3 series), long-term monitoring plans (INT0423), and integrated technical reports (INT0900 series).

The peer review process emphasized ensuring technical quality and credibility of all research products before incorporation into the Environmental Impact Statement and management decisions. The catalog documents draft reports, final reports, quarterly updates, trip reports, and data supplements spanning 1990-1995, representing one of the most comprehensive river ecosystem studies conducted in North America.
