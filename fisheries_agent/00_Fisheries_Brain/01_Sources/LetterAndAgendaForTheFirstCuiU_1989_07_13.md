## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific paper but administrative correspondence and an agenda for the **first meeting of the Cui-ui Recovery Team**, convened under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) in 1989. The letter, authored by Team Leader Tom Strekal, organizes the inaugural session of a federally mandated recovery team tasked with developing a conservation and management plan for the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered lake sucker endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. While it contains no direct morphological or ecological data itself, it identifies the key institutional stakeholders, active research/management programs, and the intended structure of the forthcoming recovery plan — all of which frame the scientific and conservation context for the species.

## Background & Description
No morphological data are presented in this document. The cui-ui is referenced only taxonomically as the focal species of the recovery effort. (For morphological detail, refer to primary cui-ui biology reports referenced but not reproduced here, described as a "packet of cui-ui reports" compiled by Chet Buchanan.)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The cui-ui is endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], a terminal desert lake in Nevada fed by the [[Lower Truckee River]], which serves as its primary spawning migration corridor.
- **Associated infrastructure**: Mentions of the **Dave Koch Cui-ui Hatchery** and the **Marble Bluff Fish Facility** indicate active hatchery supplementation and fish-passage/fishway management to support upstream spawning migration, implying that natural recruitment is impaired (likely due to water diversion, low flows, or barriers—consistent with known threats to this species).
- **Field operations**: A "fish-sampling crew" operating on Pyramid Lake indicates ongoing population monitoring efforts by [[Pyramid Lake Fisheries]] staff.
- **Ecosystem context**: The species occupies a lacustrine niche within a closed-basin (endorheic) desert lake ecosystem, with an obligate riverine spawning migration component — a life-history pattern typical of many *Chasmistes* and *Catostomus* suckers in the Great Basin.

## Key Findings
- The **Cui-ui Recovery Team** was formally organized in 1989, with its first meeting scheduled for August 2, 1989, at the FWS Reno office.
- Key institutional programs referenced as contributing to recovery planning:
  - **FWS Research Program** (updates presented by Scoppettone)
  - **Pyramid Lake Fisheries Program** (updates presented by Wagner)
  - **Management Operations** (updates presented by Buchanan)
- The recovery team's mandated agenda included: defining the "Goal" of recovery, formally defining "Recovery" for the species, outlining a stepdown planning procedure, assigning consultant roles, and delegating work assignments among team members — standard elements of an [[Endangered Species Recovery Plan]] framework under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.
- A coordinated **field trip** (August 3, 1989) to Pyramid Lake, the Dave Koch Cui-ui Hatchery, Marble Bluff Fish Facility, and the lower Truckee River was planned to give recovery team members direct exposure to on-the-ground conservation infrastructure and active fish-sampling operations.
- The document underscores an early, foundational institutional step in cui-ui conservation, situating the species within a coordinated multi-agency (FWS, Pyramid Lake Fisheries, academic consultants) recovery framework.
