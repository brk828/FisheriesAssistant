## Executive Summary
This letter, written by Norman Reimers of the Sierra Nevada Aquatic Research Laboratory to Dr. Leo Shapovalov, documents an unusual population of extremely aged [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) surviving in [[Bunny Lake]], a high-elevation Sierra Nevada lake. The correspondence reports on a final 1968 field visit (October) during which an estimated 36–48 aged survivors (roughly 16–18 years old) were observed in ripe spawning condition. Critically, the author made the first-ever positive observations of juvenile trout (year classes I and II) at the lake, suggesting rare and previously undocumented successful reproduction by geriatric brook trout. The letter also notes evidence of resumed somatic growth in surviving fish following a reduction in population density, consistent with density-dependent growth suppression ("stunting") being relieved.

## Background & Description
- **Age structure:** Surviving cohort estimated at 16–18 years old — an exceptional lifespan for [[Brook Trout]], a species typically associated with much shorter lifespans (5–6 years) in most wild populations.
- **Body size (aged survivors, current cohort):** Average length ~25 cm across both sexes at ~18 years of age.
- **Body size (select large males):** Two males approaching ~30 cm in length, indicating above-average growth for the age cohort.
- **Growth plasticity example:** A transferred 13-year-old fish held in captivity and moderately fed for 18 months **doubled in length** and **quintupled in weight**, recovering from a previously **stunted condition** — direct evidence of latent growth capacity suppressed under wild density conditions.
- **Reproductive condition:** Aged individuals collected were "ripe," actively engaged in spawning or pre-spawning behavior at time of observation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat substrate:** Spawning activity observed on shallow lake-bottom areas composed of **decomposed-granite granules**, indicating substrate suitability for redd construction in this alpine lentic environment.
- **Population dynamics:** The aged cohort appears to represent a long-standing, self-sustaining relict population within [[Bunny Lake]], with population size reduction hypothesized as a driver of renewed individual growth (density-dependent growth release).
- **Life-history anomaly:** Historically (in "past years"), the observer had **negative findings** for juvenile recruitment, suggesting either failed spawning or undetectable low-level reproduction in the "middle years" of the population's history. The 1968 observations of year-class I and II juveniles mark a notable ecological shift.
- **Sampling limitations:** Attempts to confirm juvenile identity via spot-rotenone application or pistol collection were unsuccessful, leaving species/year-class confirmation based on visual observation only.
- **Thermal management (captive holding):** Collected specimens were maintained in filtered stream water at low temperature (~4°C) specifically to reduce metabolic activity and infection risk during transport and captivity — relevant to husbandry of aged, presumably physiologically fragile fish for laboratory study.

## Key Findings
- **Extreme longevity with active reproduction:** Documented spawning behavior in brook trout aged 16–18 years is described by the author as a potentially unprecedented record for the species, even accounting for a possibly low hatching success rate.
- **First evidence of recruitment at Bunny Lake:** This visit marked the first-ever positive sighting of small trout (year classes I–II) at the lake in the observer's monitoring history, suggesting recent successful spawning and hatching events not previously detected.
- **Growth resumption tied to population reduction:** Renewed growth in surviving aged fish (e.g., males nearing 30 cm) is interpreted as a density-dependent release effect following reduction in overall population size — supported by a prior captive case where a stunted 13-year-old fish rapidly doubled in length and quintupled in weight under improved feeding conditions.
- **Ongoing research application:** Four live specimens were collected and transferred to the [[Sierra Nevada Aquatic Research Laboratory]] for use in **histochemical aging studies**, aimed at refining age-determination techniques in long-lived salmonids.
- **Research status:** The author notes that fuller findings are pending publication ("the next paper"), with this letter serving as an informal but data-rich preliminary field report on the [[Bunny Lake]] brook trout population.
