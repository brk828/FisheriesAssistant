# Rio Grande Silvery Minnow Critical Habitat Redesignation and Related Federal Notices (April 2001)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a compilation of Federal Register notices and a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) letter dated April 4, 2001. Its main subject is the proposed redesignation of [[Critical Habitat]] for the endangered [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]]. The redesignation follows a November 2000 New Mexico District Court decision that required USFWS to issue a new proposed rule and prepare an [[Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS). The notices also cover a Florida [[Scrub-Jay]] habitat permit, an [[Alaska Migratory Bird Co-Management Council]] meeting, an antidumping investigation of Chilean and Mexican table grapes, a Bureau of Land Management mineral interests conveyance, and a correction notice. Only the silvery minnow material concerns fish.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The Rio Grande silvery minnow is an endangered freshwater cyprinid. Its historic and current range includes the [[Rio Grande]] and the [[Pecos River]] in New Mexico and Texas. The source gives no scientific name, morphology or life-history data.
- **Legal framework:** Critical habitat is governed by section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). Environmental review falls under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) and its implementing regulations.
- **Lead agency:** The USFWS is the lead federal agency. Its Albuquerque Ecological Services Field Office issued the notice and the letter, signed by Field Supervisor Joy E. Nicholopoulos.
- **Recovery reference:** The USFWS *Rio Grande Silvery Minnow Recovery Plan* (1999) defines the species' current and historic range.
- **Florida scrub-jay:** A notice describes a permit for habitat impacts on about 5 acres of scrub-jay habitat in Florida, linked to initial road construction and home site development. The permittee was to purchase and conserve habitat, with management ensured in perpetuity. The source does not give the scrub-jay's scientific name.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat loss and fragmentation:** The letter notes that habitat loss and fragmentation are the main threats cited for the silvery minnow. The notice also links scrub-jay habitat impacts to permitted development.
- **Scope of study:** The USFWS reports that it has not yet fully evaluated the species' distribution and habitat needs. It states that the designation must address the essential features of the critical habitat and any areas that may need special management or protection.
- **Economic and social factors:** The USFWS is considering economic and other impacts of designation, including impacts on small entities. It also seeks information on the benefits of excluding specific areas from critical habitat.
- **Rio Grande geography:** The redesignation would cover the entire historic range of the silvery minnow, including the Pecos River in New Mexico and the Rio Grande in New Mexico and Texas.

## Key Findings

**Silvery minnow critical habitat (USFWS letter, April 4, 2001, and Federal Register notice):**

1. **Court-mandated action:** A November 2000 New Mexico District Court decision requires USFWS to issue a new proposed rule for the silvery minnow's critical habitat and to prepare an EIS. The agency must decide whether to re-issue the designation.
2. **Proposed scope:** The agency will consider designating the entire historic range of the silvery minnow as critical habitat. It has not yet determined which areas require special management.
3. **Alternatives:** Because the NEPA analysis is incomplete, the preferred alternative and other alternatives are not yet known. The draft EIS (DEIS) will be circulated for a minimum 45-day public review period.
4. **Information requests:** The USFWS seeks comments on:
   - Reasons why any habitat should or should not be designated.
   - The amount and distribution of silvery minnow habitat, and which habitat is essential.
   - Current or planned activities in the subject areas.
   - Economic, social and other impacts, including impacts on small entities.
   - The location and status of silvery minnow populations.
5. **Public meetings (scoping):** Meetings were scheduled for April 2001 at the Indian Pueblo Cultural Center in Albuquerque, New Mexico State University in Las Cruces, and the Pecos County Commission in Fort Stockton, Texas. The source gives April 23 and 24 as meeting dates, but the OCR is unclear on the Albuquerque date.
6. **Comment deadline:** The letter calls for written comments by early May 2001. The OCR does not clearly show the exact date.
7. **Contacts:** Eric Hein, Jude Smith and Theresa Davidson at the Albuquerque office.

**Other notices in the same Federal Register issue:**

- **Alaska Migratory Bird Co-Management Council:** Meeting on April 26–27, 2001, to develop recommendations for spring/summer subsistence harvest regulations. The Council was formed under the amended Migratory Bird Treaty with Canada and includes Native, State and federal representatives.
- **Florida scrub-jay permit:** A habitat impact permit covering about 5 acres, as summarized above.
- **Spring table grapes from Chile and Mexico (International Trade Commission, investigations 731-TA-926 and 927):** A preliminary antidumping investigation. The Commission's preliminary determination is due by May 14, 2001, and its views are due by May 21, 2001.
- **Bureau of Land Management:** A notice on the conveyance of mineral interests in California (CACA 426461).
- **Correction notice:** A correction to a legal land description in an earlier notice of March 26, 2001.
