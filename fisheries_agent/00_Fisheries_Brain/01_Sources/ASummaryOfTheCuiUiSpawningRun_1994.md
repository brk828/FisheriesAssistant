# A Summary of the 1994 Cui-ui Spawning Run

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes monitoring data from the 1994 spawning migration of the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered lake sucker endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, as it migrated up the [[Truckee River]] through the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]. Fish were enumerated and partially sexed at three collection points: the [[Fishway]], the [[Ladders]] (used for trap-and-transport operations), and the [[River Trap]]. A total of **66,321 cui-ui** were enumerated across all facilities in 1994, representing one of the largest recorded runs in the dataset spanning 1976–1994. The overall sex ratio was heavily skewed toward males (2.88:1), with an estimated 49,228 males and 17,093 females. Total estimated mortality across all structures was 1,650 fish. The run occurred in three major migratory pulses and was tracked alongside Truckee River discharge relative to established flow targets for cui-ui spawning.

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] is a large, long-lived lake sucker (family [[Catostomidae]]) endemic to Pyramid Lake, Nevada, and listed as endangered under the U.S. Endangered Species Act. Each spring, adult cui-ui migrate out of Pyramid Lake into the lower [[Truckee River]] to spawn, a migration historically constrained by river discharge, water temperature, and fish passage barriers such as the Marble Bluff Dam.

The [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] was constructed to provide fish passage around Marble Bluff Dam and includes multiple structures for monitoring and managing the spawning run:
- **Fishway**: A ladder-based passage structure allowing fish to swim upstream under their own power.
- **Ladders (Transport Operations)**: Used to collect cui-ui during migration surges for truck transport downstream to a release site near the Nixon bridge, bypassing sections of river.
- **River Trap**: A trapping structure used to enumerate and sample fish separately from the fishway.

These structures allow biologists to enumerate the run, estimate sex ratios, and track mortality, which are critical demographic indicators for managing this endangered species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Cui-ui require adequate river discharge in the Truckee River to successfully migrate from Pyramid Lake to spawning grounds. The report references a **Target Flow Regime** for cui-ui spawning migration, compared against actual daily discharge (measured in cubic feet per second) from January through June 1994.
- **Migration timing**: The fishway was operated from April 11 to May 18, with the first cui-ui arriving at the processing building on April 18. The river trap operated 21 of 33 days between April 17 and May 19.
- **Migration pulses**: Three distinct migration surges were identified for transport operations: April 20–21, April 29–30, and May 6–8. These surges corresponded to spikes in river discharge and fish abundance, and necessitated installation of a downstream gate on at least 6 occasions to prevent fish kills from crowding.
- **Interannual context**: Historical data (1976–1994) show extreme variability in run size, from 0 fish in 1976–1977 to peaks of 35,080 (1986, river trap only) and 47,101+18,911 in 1994 (fishway + river trap). Sex ratios have also shifted over time, from near parity or female-skewed ratios in the 1980s (e.g., 1.0:2.5 in 1984) to strongly male-skewed ratios by the early-to-mid 1990s (2.4:1.0 in 1993; 2.9:1.0 in 1994).

## Key Findings

### Fishway Operations
- **Period operated**: April 11 – May 18, 1994
- **First arrival**: April 18
- **Number enumerated**: 37,101
- **Percent sexed**: 23.8%
- **Sex ratio**: 3.25 Males : 1 Female
- **Estimated mortalities**: 450 (~100 under false bottoms on 5/5; ~350 on 5/8)
- Downstream gate installed on 6+ occasions to mitigate fish kill risk during surges; installed permanently from May 9 onward to prioritize river trap operation.

### Ladder / Transport Operations
- **Migration surges**: April 20–21, April 29–30, May 6–8
- **Release site**: Downstream from Nixon bridge
- **Number transported**: 10,309
- **Percent sexed**: 3.5%
- **Sex ratio**: 1 Male : 3.45 Females (notably female-skewed, opposite of fishway/river trap ratios)

### River Trap Operations
- **Period operated**: 21 of 33 days, April 17 – May 19
- **Number enumerated**: 18,911
- **Percent sexed**: 10.6%
- **Sex ratio**: 2.59 Males : 1 Female
- **Estimated mortalities**: 1,200 (~200 on 5/6; ~1,000 on 5/10)

### Total Run Summary (1994)
- **Total enumerated**: 66,321
- **Overall sex ratio**: 2.88 Males : 1 Female
- **Estimated males**: 49,228
- **Estimated females**: 17,093
- **Total estimated mortalities**: 1,650
