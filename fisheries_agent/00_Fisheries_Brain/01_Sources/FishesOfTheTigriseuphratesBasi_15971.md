# Fishes of the Tigris-Euphrates Basin: A Critical Check-List

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Syllogeus monograph (No. 68, 1991) by [[Brian Coad]] of the Canadian Museum of Nature presents the first critical check-list covering the entire [[Tigris-Euphrates Basin]], the major drainage of southwest Asia. The author reports about 66 freshwater species in 12 native families. Earlier partial lists are described as containing numerous errors, so the list is compiled from literature review plus examination of collected and museum specimens. The document has three parts: the main freshwater list, a list of [[Rejected Records]] considered erroneous, and an annotated list of [[Marine Fishes Entering Freshwater]] from the [[Persian Gulf]]. A short bibliography of systematic works closes the document.

## Background & Description

The basin lies between the African Nile and the Indian Indus systems. Its two principal rivers, the [[Tigris River]] and the [[Euphrates River]], join to form the [[Shatt-al-Arab]], which flows about 200 km before entering the head of the Persian Gulf. The Euphrates (Firat Nehri in Turkey, Nahr al Furat in Iraq and Syria) rises from two Turkish headstreams, the Karasu and the Murat Nehri. The Euphrates is over 2700 km long; the Tigris is over 1900 km long and is the larger and swifter river, fed by Zagros tributaries such as the Great and Little Zab and the Diyala. Lake Van is explicitly excluded from the list.

The author explains the list's construction:

- **Main list:** all species recorded from the basin, with principal synonyms. Some synonyms refer to localities outside the basin, included because of widespread misuse of names in the Middle East.
- **Marine species:** included subjectively where they penetrate far inland, breed in fresh water, or are commonly caught.
- **Status symbols:** "?" marks doubtful validity, "*" uncertain presence, and "#" introduced species (not all of which are confirmed still present).

Taxonomy follows [[Nelson Classification]] (1984) for higher fish groupings. The Cypriniformes section is the most detailed, reflecting the large number of Heckel-described species and the contested generic placement of many *Barbus*-like fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The basin's hydrology has been heavily modified. The Turkish headwaters include the [[Keban Dam]] (where the Karasu and Murat Nehri meet), the Devegecidi Dam on the Tigris near Diyarbakir, and the Hazar Gölü near Elazig. Syria has the Assad Dam on the Euphrates; Iraq has the Dokan Reservoir (Little Zab) and the Darband-i Khan Reservoir (Diyala); Iran has a dam on the Dez River near Dezful. Lowland Iraq and Iran are crossed by irrigation canals. The largest marsh, the [[Hawr al Hammar]] near Basrah, may reach 5200 sq km but fluctuates greatly in area.

Several other features shape the fauna:

- **Marine penetration:** Al-Hassan and Hussain (1985) discuss the hydrological parameters that control how far Arabian Gulf fishes penetrate the Shatt al Arab.
- **Biogeographic position:** The basin sits between Levantine, Caspian, Black Sea, and Iranian faunas, so many species are endemic to neighbouring basins and were wrongly attributed to the Tigris-Euphrates.
- **Cave and subterranean fishes:** The blind [[Typhlogarra Widdowsoni]] from Iraq and the cave fish [[Iranocypris Typhlops]] (Iran) are notable relict or specialized forms.
- **Introduced species:** At least 14 species are marked as introduced, including carps and Chinese carps, North American catfishes and centrarchids, and *Gambusia* used for malaria control.

## Key Findings

**Overall richness and status**

- The basin's freshwater fauna comprises 12 native families and about 66 species.
- No previous list covered the whole basin, and partial lists were described as "replete with errors."
- Freshwater shark records from the basin were resolved by Garrick (1982) and Compagno (1984): only [[Carcharhinus Leucas]] is believed to occur in the rivers as far as Baghdad.

**Freshwater main list**

- Cyprinidae dominates, with the largest number of entries, including the *Barbus* complex (e.g., [[Barbus Luteus]], *B. xanthopterus*, *B. grypus*, *B. pectoralis*), *Capoeta* (e.g., *C. damascina*, *C. aculeata*), *Garra* (e.g., [[Garra Rufa]]), and *Alburnus*/*Chalcalburnus*.
- Other represented groups include Cobitidae (e.g., *Cobitis elazigensis*, *Turcinemacheilus kosswigi*), Sisoridae (*Glyptothorax*, including [[Glyptothorax Silviae]]), Siluridae (*Silurus glanis*), Bagridae (*Mystus pelusius*), Cyprinodontidae (*Aphanius*, including [[Aphanius Vladykovi]]), Mastacembelidae ([[Mastacembelus Mastacembelus]]), Gobiidae, and Mugilidae.
- Several species are flagged as doubtful (?), including *Alburnus akili*, *Barbus lacerta*, and *Mystus* records. Five *Glyptothorax* species were questioned by Coad and Delmastro (1985).
- Genus-level taxonomy is unsettled. The *Barbus* group has been split into multiple proposed genera that have not gained general acceptance, and *Chalcalburnus* may be a synonym of *Alburnus*.
- The author notes that *Barbus belayewi* is probably a synonym of *Capoeta damascina*, and that *Capoeta capoeta* records are probably *C. damascina* with low scale counts.

**Introduced species (#)**

Fourteen species are marked as introduced, among them [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), grass carp (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), silver carp (*Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*), [[Gambusia Holbrooki]], largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*), pike-perch (*Stizostedion lucioperca*), and Nile tilapia (*Oreochromis niloticus*). Some introductions remain unconfirmed.

**Rejected records**

- A second list gives 19 cyprinid and 4 cobitid records considered erroneous, through misidentification or misreading of the literature.
- Many rejections rest on endemism: for example, *Acanthobrama centisquama* is endemic to the Amik Gölü basin, *Acanthobrama tricolor* to the Damascus basin, and *Chalcalburnus tarichi* to Lake Van.
- Some Iraqi records derived from Beckman's (1962) Syrian list were included on the assumption they would be found in Iraq; Beckman's list itself included species endemic to other drainages.

**Marine fishes entering freshwater**

- An appended annotated list covers marine taxa recorded in the Shatt-al-Arab and, in some cases, the lower rivers. Records are mostly from Iraqi waters. The list spans about 43 entries across Clupeidae, Engraulidae, Mugilidae, Sciaenidae, Sparidae, Gobiidae, and Soleidae, among others.
- Asterisked marine species are recorded from the Persian Gulf or Sea of Oman but may be misidentifications.
- Many goby identities from freshwater are doubtful, and taxonomic allocations vary between authors.

**Documentation and reference value**

- The author directs readers to Coad (1990a) for an extensive Persian Gulf marine bibliography, and to Coad and Al-Hassan (1988) for further Tigris-Euphrates literature.
- The bibliography is described as a selection of principal systematic and distributional works, including revisionary works relevant to the basin's species, and covers Turkish, Iraqi, and Iranian studies.
