# The Resistance of Rainbow Trout and Roach to Alkaline Solutions

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #water-quality #toxicology

## Executive Summary

This 1964 laboratory study by Jordan and Lloyd investigated the tolerance of [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*) and [[Roach]] (*Rutilus rutilus*) to alkaline solutions. Experiments measured survival times at various pH levels following acclimatization to different pH values. [[Rainbow Trout]] acclimatized to pH 8.3 showed 50% mortality within 15 days at pH 9.5, while [[Roach]] survived 10 days at pH 10.15. The research suggests rainbow trout might be harmed by prolonged exposure (several months) to pH values of 9.0 or above. The study relates laboratory findings to a 1956 fish mortality event in the [[River Tweed]] caused by high pH values from intense algal photosynthesis.

## Background & Description

The study addresses the limited experimental data on fish survival in alkaline waters, despite field observations of mortalities at high pH levels. Previous literature (Doudoroff and Katz, 1950) indicated pH values greater than 10.0 are rapidly lethal to many species, and Bandt (1936) suggested continuous exposure above pH 9.2 might be lethal to trout.

**Experimental Design:**
- Tests conducted in 40-liter aquaria aerated with carbon dioxide-free air
- Alkaline solutions prepared using hard borehole water (total hardness 320 ppm as CaCO₃)
- Sodium hydroxide added to raise pH; caused calcium carbonate precipitation
- pH control: ±0.05 units above pH 10.0; ±0.15 units in range 9.5-10.0; ±0.3 units below pH 9.5
- Tests at room temperature with air-saturation dissolved oxygen
- Sample sizes: 10 [[Rainbow Trout]] (4.5-8.0 cm) or 7 [[Roach]] (8.0-13.0 cm) per pH level
- Fish transferred to fresh solutions daily; fed on alternate days
- Survival measured from immersion to cessation of respiratory movements

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research was motivated by fish mortalities in natural waters where pH fluctuates due to photosynthetic activity. In 1956, high pH values from algal photosynthesis caused considerable mortalities among salmon and trout in the [[River Tweed]]. The [[Tweed River Purification Board]] documented diurnal pH fluctuations on 26 June 1956, showing pH values reaching approximately 10.0 during daylight hours when photosynthesis was most intense.

The study notes that survival depends not only on whether lethal pH is reached but also on duration of exposure. Field observations by Eicher (1946) found trout killed when pH rose above 10.2 but survived at pH 9.4-9.5, consistent with these experimental findings.

## Key Findings

**Rainbow Trout pH Tolerance:**
- Fish acclimatized to pH 6.55, 7.50, and 8.40 for 5 days at 16-17°C
- 1-day LC50 values: pH 9.86 (acclimatized to 6.55), pH 9.91 (acclimatized to 7.50), pH 10.13 (acclimatized to 8.40)
- Acclimatization pH had little effect on short-term resistance (hours)
- Fish acclimatized to pH 8.40 significantly more resistant than those at lower pH values
- 15-day experiment (14.5°C ± 1°C): pH 9.5 killed 50% of trout acclimatized to pH 8.3
- Trend suggests rainbow trout might be harmed by exposure to pH ≥9.0 for several months

**Roach pH Tolerance:**
- Acclimatized 1 day to pH 8.3 at 13.5°C ± 1.5°C
- pH 10.15 killed 50% within 10 days
- [[Roach]] considerably more resistant to alkali than [[Rainbow Trout]]
- Might be killed at pH values ≥9.8 with prolonged exposure

**pH-Survival Relationship:**
- Relationship between pH and log median survival time is a shallow curve
- No threshold concentration identifiable from 10-15 day experiments
- Differs from acids (linear relationship) and some poisons (threshold exists)

**Field Application:**
- Laboratory curves compared with [[River Tweed]] diurnal pH fluctuation (26 June 1956)
- Conditions on that overcast day were marginal for trout survival; no mortalities observed
- Earlier mortalities occurred in bright sunny weather when peak pH may have been higher or persisted longer
- Laboratory-derived minimum lethal pH values: 9.0 for trout, 9.8 for roach
- Slightly lower than Bandt's (1936) values: 9.2 for trout, 10.4 for roach

**Methodological Notes:**
- Control fish showed zero mortality in all experiments
- Comparison between constant laboratory pH and fluctuating field pH not strictly valid but provides useful predictions
- Method allows prediction of conditions marginal for fish survival based on exposure duration and pH level
