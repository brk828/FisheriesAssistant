# Virgin River Chub (*Gila robusta seminuda*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This document is a **regulatory public notice**, not a primary scientific study, issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) in September 1986. It announces a public hearing regarding the proposed listing of the **Virgin River Chub** (*Gila robusta seminuda*) as an **endangered species** under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, along with a proposal to designate **critical habitat**. The species is endemic to the [[Virgin River]] system spanning Arizona, Nevada, and Utah, and is imperiled primarily due to anthropogenic habitat alteration and biotic threats from introduced species. No morphological or population-level scientific data are presented in this notice; its content is administrative/procedural in nature.

## Background & Description

The source text contains **no morphological, meristic, or physiological data** on *Gila robusta seminuda*. As a subspecies of the [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), it is taxonomically placed within the genus *Gila*, family [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows), but no distinguishing physical traits, size ranges, coloration, or diagnostic characters are described in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Restricted entirely to the [[Virgin River]], a tributary within the Colorado River drainage basin, flowing through Arizona, Nevada, and Utah.
- **Endemism**: The species/subspecies is found *only* in this river system, indicating a highly restricted range and narrow ecological niche typical of desert-adapted cyprinids.
- **Threats identified**:
  - Water diversion
  - Desalination activities
  - Urban growth and associated development pressure
  - Impoundment (damming) of river flow
  - Pollution
  - Sedimentation
  - Competition and predation by **exotic (introduced) fish species**

These stressors collectively reflect the broader pattern of decline seen in many endemic [[Southwestern Desert Fishes]], where habitat fragmentation and non-native species introductions compound the effects of water resource development.

## Key Findings

- **Proposed Action**: USFWS proposed on June 24, 1986 (51 FR 22949) to list the Virgin River Chub as **Endangered** and to designate accompanying **critical habitat** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended.
- **Public Hearing**: Scheduled for October 15, 1986, in St. George, Utah (Dixie College), to solicit public and scientific comment on the proposal.
- **Comment Period**: Originally closed August 25, 1986; reopened to accommodate the hearing and extended through December 15, 1986.
- **Regulatory Consequence if Finalized**:
  - Full protections under the Endangered Species Act would apply.
  - Federal agencies would be prohibited from funding, authorizing, or carrying out actions that jeopardize the species or destroy/adversely modify its critical habitat.
  - Direct "take" (harm, harassment, killing) of the species by any person would be prohibited.
- **Correspondence Context**: The notice was sent to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University), a noted ichthyologist specializing in [[Desert Fishes]], suggesting solicitation of expert scientific input into the listing process.
- **Contact for Technical Information**: Gerald L. Burton, Endangered Species Biologist, USFWS Albuquerque office.
