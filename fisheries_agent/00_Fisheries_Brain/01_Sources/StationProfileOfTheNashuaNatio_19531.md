## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Nashua National Fish Hatchery**, established in 1894 under USFWS Region 5, whose core mission is the restoration of [[Atlantic Salmon]] populations in the **Merrimack River basin**. The facility operates as an egg-production and broodstock-holding station, targeting an annual output of **7.4 million eggs** — 6 million destined for fry stocking in the upper Merrimack River and 1.4 million allocated to research needs and the State of Rhode Island. Eggs are sourced from both wild **sea-run adult Atlantic salmon** captured in the Merrimack River and from **domestic broodstock** maintained on-station. The profile also details physical infrastructure, multi-year funding/staffing trends (FY1991–1995), and fish distribution records (FY1991–1993), reflecting the hatchery's role in a broader anadromous fish restoration program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Life history strategy:** [[Anadromous Fish]] — Atlantic salmon migrate between marine and freshwater environments; the station specifically manages the freshwater/riverine phase of this life cycle.
- **Wild population component:** Sea-run adult salmon are captured directly from the **Merrimack River** for use as wild broodstock, supplementing the domestic (hatchery-origin) broodstock line.
- **Stocking strategy:** Fry are released into the **upper reaches of the Merrimack River**, with a smolt release facility (10 earthen raceways) located in **Litchfield, NH**, supporting a distinct life-stage release strategy (fry vs. smolt).
- **Range of program impact:** Distribution extends across **New Hampshire, Massachusetts, and Rhode Island**, indicating a regional, multi-state restoration effort tied to the [[Merrimack River]] watershed.
- **Fishery significance:** The release of 600 surplus broodstock (8–15 lbs) into the Merrimack was noted as providing a salmon fishery in the river for the first time in **100 years**, highlighting the historical extirpation and ongoing reintroduction effort for this population.

## Key Findings

1. **Mission-driven egg production target:** 7.4 million Atlantic salmon eggs annually — 6 million for Merrimack fry stocking, 1.4 million for research and Rhode Island program needs.
2. **Egg source breakdown (reporting year):**
   - Wild sea-run captured salmon: **538,000 eggs**
   - Domestic broodstock: **1.3 million eggs**
3. **Fry stocking output:** 10,000 fry stocked into the Merrimack River during the reporting period (below the 6-million target, suggesting either a partial reporting period or a production shortfall).
4. **Broodstock release event:** 600 surplus broodstock (8–15 lbs) released into the Merrimack River — described as restoring a river salmon fishery absent for a century.
5. **Infrastructure rehabilitation:** Station raceway rehabilitation was completed after a one-year delay, enabling the station to rear a **full complement of broodstock** and increasing rearing capacity toward meeting the 6-million-egg program need.
6. **Physical facilities:**
   - 20 rearing raceways
   - 29 hatchery tanks
   - Hatchery/office complex, feed storage building, workshop/garage
   - Wild salmon broodstock isolation building
   - Off-site smolt release facility: 10 earthen raceways in [[Litchfield, NH]]
7. **Visitation:** Approximately 3,000 visitors toured the station during the fiscal year.
