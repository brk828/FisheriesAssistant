# A New Cave Catfish, Rhamdia zongolicensis sp. nov. (Pimelodidae), from the Sierra de Zongolica, Veracruz, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pimelodidae

## Executive Summary

Horst Wilkens describes a new cave catfish, [[Rhamdia Zongolicensis]], from the [[Sierra de Zongolica]] in Veracruz, Mexico, based on four specimens (one holotype and three paratypes). The species is derived from the epigean [[Rhamdia Laticauda]] and shows reduced eyes, reduced dark pigmentation, prolonged head and barbels, and a fat-containing dorsal ridge beneath the adipose fin. Its morphology is very close to the cave species [[Rhamdia Reddelli]] from Oaxaca, differing only minutely, which the author attributes to convergent evolution. Laboratory crosses indicate species distinctness, but the F1 progeny were exclusively female. All type specimens show signs of malnutrition.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Rhamdia]] in Mexico and adjacent regions was recently reviewed by [[Miller (1984)]], who recognised three epigean species: *R. guatemalensis*, *R. laticauda* and *R. parryi*. Cave forms previously described include *R. laticauda typhla* from Belize ([[Greenfield et al. (1982)]]) and *R. reddelli* from Oaxaca ([[Miller (1984)]]). Additional cave populations were noted by Robertson (1983a,b) and Mosier (1984), one of which is the population in the Sierra de Zongolica described here.

**Type material:**
- Holotype: ZMH 8249, 92 mm SL, collected 17 December 1992 by Ulrike Strecker and Horst Wilkens.
- Paratypes: 3 specimens, 65-85 mm SL, ZMH 8250.
- Type locality: [[Cueva del Ostoc]] (also Cueva de El Tunel), road from Comalapa to Tezonapa.

**Diagnosis and key characters:**
- Broad, moderately depressed skull; long head (28% of SL in the new species versus 23% in *R. laticauda*).
- Almost complete absence of dark pigmentation; eyes reduced to varying size.
- Strong retrorse serrae on the posterior edge of the pectoral spine (9-10 serrae, triangular; none on the anterior edge), shared with *R. laticauda*.
- Shallowly notched caudal fin.
- Longer and more numerous gill rakers than *R. laticauda*.
- Maxillary barbel about 50% of SL, extending as far back as in *R. reddelli*.
- Adipose fin 29-36% of SL, sitting on a fat-containing ridge between dorsal and caudal fins (a feature also found in *R. reddelli*).

**Meristics:** Dorsal fin I/6; anal rays 13-16; pectoral rays I/8 or I/10; pelvic rays 6; articulated caudal rays 20-21; gill rakers 11; vertebrae 39.

**Proportional measurements (‰ of SL):** Holotype and paratype mean/range values are given in Table 1 of the source (e.g. body depth 141 in the holotype; head length 272 in the holotype versus 262-271 in paratypes; maxillary barbel 489 versus 424-538).

**Coloration:** In its natural habitat the species is yellowish rather than dark. Slight dark pigmentation along the sides appears after preservation through physiological colour change, and specimens darken considerably when held in laboratory light.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** [[Cueva del Ostoc]] lies in the Sierra de Zongolica, about two kilometres north of the source of the Rio Tonto, at roughly 250 m above sea level, and has been mapped by Robertson (1983).
- **Associated fauna:** No other aquatic fauna was found in the three pools inhabited by these fish.
- **Nutritional state:** All type specimens show malnutrition. The dorsal fat-containing ridge is almost completely reduced. The holotype condition factor is K = 0.87 and the paratype mean is K = 0.92, matching 13 specimens caught in 1988 (K = 0.93). Well-nourished *R. reddelli* reach K = 1.41. Laboratory experiments suggest the type specimens must have been starving for at least one year.
- **Growth potential:** Under good food conditions, specimens from Cueva del Ostoc grow to about 150 mm SL and can breed.
- **Convergent evolution:** Minute morphological differences from *R. reddelli* are attributed to convergent evolution in cave conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described:** *Rhamdia zongolicensis* sp. nov. is distinguished from *R. laticauda* by a larger head, a fat-containing dorsal ridge, and longer, more numerous gill rakers. Its eyes average larger than those of *R. reddelli*, although the ranges overlap.
2. **Close relationship to cave congeners:** Differences from [[Rhamdia Reddelli]] are minute, consistent with convergent evolution.
3. **Reproductive isolation evidence:** Laboratory crosses with *R. laticauda* and *R. reddelli* produced an F1 generation that was exclusively female, probably due to genetic causes (Haldane's rule), supporting species status.
4. **Condition and starvation:** Type specimens are malnourished, implying prolonged food scarcity in the cave; condition factors are far below those of well-fed *R. reddelli*.
5. **Restricted distribution:** Known only from one cave system with three pools and no other associated aquatic fauna.
6. **Future work:** The author plans a study on the adaptation and phylogenetic age of Mexican cave and epigean *Rhamdia* (in preparation).
