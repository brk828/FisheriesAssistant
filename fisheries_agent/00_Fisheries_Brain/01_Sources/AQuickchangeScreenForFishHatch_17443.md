# Quick-Change Screen for Fish Hatchery Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-hatchery

## Executive Summary

This short technical note from *The Progressive Fish-Culturist* (October 1949, pp. 265–266) describes a "quick-change" screen for [[Hatchery Pond Screens]]. The design lets a single worker swap pond screens in under one minute, without lifting out the full frame. The authors, [[Fred V. Carr]] and [[Leonard N. Allison]] of the [[Michigan Department of Conservation]], developed it at the [[State Fish Hatchery]] in [[Grayling Michigan]]. The document is an equipment design report, not a fish biology study, and it contains no species data.

## Background & Description

Pond screens at fish hatcheries must be removed periodically to clean ponds, carry out repairs, or change mesh size. Conventional screens are lifted out as a whole frame. This takes considerable time and labour when many screens are involved, and resetting them so they are fish-tight requires an experienced worker. A clean [[Bulkhead Keyway]] seat is also needed to prevent fish from escaping.

Problems at Grayling were specific to the site:
- Screens ice up heavily in winter, and the frames freeze to the bulkheads.
- Pond bottoms accumulate sand, gravel, and other debris from the supply water, which is drawn directly from the [[East Branch Au Sable River]], so the floor seat must be cleaned each time.

The design is analogous to a combination storm window and screen in a house: a fixed main frame stays in the keyway, and smaller interchangeable screen frames slide into it.

**Construction (as described):**
- **Main frame:** 2 by 4 inch lumber, fastened at each corner with four brass screws. Strips (1-1/4 by 2-3/4 inches) on the downstream side form a 1-1/2 inch flange that slides into the keyway.
- **Upstream plate:** A 4-inch square of 1/8-inch strap iron, screwed to the centre of the bottom, projects to form a 1/2-inch flange.
- **Top clips:** 1/8-inch strap iron, 7 inches long and 1 inch wide, fixed with lag screws so they can be loosened or tightened.
- **Screen frames:** 2-1/2 by 1-1/4 inch lumber, sized to fit inside the main frame with 1/4-inch clearance on all sides.
- **Finish:** All wooden parts heavily coated with creosote.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not study fish, species, or aquatic communities. Its environmental content is limited to:
- **Setting:** Hatchery ponds fed by river water from the [[East Branch Au Sable River]], which brings sand and gravel into the ponds.
- **Winter conditions:** Ice build-up that freezes large frames to the bulkheads.
- **Fish containment:** The screens exist to stop fish escaping, so a fish-tight seat matters for both stock retention and operations.

## Key Findings

- **Time saving:** By actual timing, one worker can remove a screen and replace it with another in less than 1 minute.
- **Ease of use:** An inexperienced worker can change the quick-change screens with ease, whereas resetting the large frames so they are fish-tight needs an experienced hand.
- **Winter performance:** The smaller screens do not freeze in as tightly, so they can be removed in winter with little difficulty.
- **Material savings:** The smaller screens need less mesh material, a further advantage.
- **Maintenance:** The main frame stays in the keyway and needs removal only for repair or replacement.
- **Status:** The authors describe the design as proven at the Grayling station but say experimentation is not complete. They plan refinements, including aluminum angle bars as the frame material.
- **Transferability:** The authors suggest the design may help other hatcheries with similar problems, though no multi-site trials are reported.
