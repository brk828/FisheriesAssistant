# Stenasellus brignolii n. sp.

#Stenasellidae #Isopoda #Crustacea #Stygofauna

> **Note:** This source describes a **subterranean freshwater isopod** (Crustacea: Isopoda: Asellota), not a fish. The note is structured using the requested template but reflects the taxonomic focus of the original paper — [[Stenasellidae]], an ancient family of phreatic, depigmented, blind crustaceans.

## Executive Summary

This paper (Pesce & Argano, 1981) describes **Stenasellus brignolii n. sp.**, a new species of stygobiotic (subterranean groundwater-dwelling) isopod in the family [[Stenasellidae]], collected from an artificial freshwater well on **Phuket Island, Thailand**. It represents only the **third stenasellid species known from Southeast Asia**, following *St. chapmani* Magniez (Sarawak, Malaysia/Borneo) and an undescribed *Stenasellus* sp. reported by Boutin (1971) from Cambodia. The discovery reinforces hypotheses about the ancient Tethyan origin of the [[Stenasellidae]] and their present-day relictual distribution across circum-Mediterranean and pantropical groundwater systems. All known material consists exclusively of female specimens, limiting full comparative diagnosis with congeners.

## Background & Description

*Stenasellus brignolii* is described as a **very small-sized stenasellid**, **depigmented and blind (anophthalmic)**, exhibiting the typical *Stenasellus* body plan (habitus).

**Type material measurements:**
- Holotype (♀): 3.1 mm
- Paratype ♀: 2.7 mm
- Paratype ♀: 2.6 mm

**Diagnostic features:**
- **Pleonites 1–2:** free, well-developed in width; pleural points not strongly marked, each armed with 3 distal spines + 1 subdistal spine; 6–7 spines along posterior margin of each pleonite.
- **Pleotelson:** subrectangular, longer than wide (L/W ratio = 1.15–1.16); regular marginal spinulation plus three characteristic centro-distal spines and one central subdistal spine; additional spines distributed over the tergal surface.
- **Antennula:** short; peduncle of 4 articles; flagellum of 5 articles, the two distal articles each bearing a long aesthetasc.
- **Antenna:** peduncle of 6 articles; third article with a relatively reduced squama bearing two thin setae; flagellum very short, composed of only 12 articles.
- **Mandible:** triarticulate, well-developed palp; incisor process with four robust teeth.
  - *Left mandible:* lacinia mobilis with four large teeth followed by a series of 5 dentate spines (dentation on one side only); molar lobe strongly raised, bearing 6–7 long setae.
  - *Right mandible:* bifid lamina with strongly dentate lobes below the incisor process, followed by 3–4 spines (first squamiform), dentate on one side — possibly a derived specialization of the typical dentate spines found in this mandibular region.
- **Maxillule:** outer lobe armed with 9 coarsely dentate spines; inner lobe bearing three large spines (largest strongly dentate, two others more finely dentate) plus one smaller internal spine.
- **Maxillae and maxillipeds:** no particular distinguishing features versus other known species.
- **Gnathopod:** propodite sternal margin with 4 large spines (two with a subdistal denticle, two clearly dentate) plus two long setae; dactylopodite with two large bifid spines on inner margin; the unguis occupies about half the segment length.
- **Pereiopods 2–7:** dactylopodite with a single sternal spine.
- **Pleopod 2:** subtriangular, very sinuous outer border; one distal seta and one submedian spine on both outer and inner margins.
- **Pleopod 3:** well-developed, subrectangular, distal margin distinctly rounded; distal segment bears 3 well-spaced simple setae along distal/outer margin; one subdistal seta on outer margin of proximal segment; endopodite strongly bifid, lobes slightly unequal in size.
- **Pleopods 4 & 5:** similar to each other; exopodite formed of a single lamina (no suture trace), margin finely undulate, uniformly covered with very fine setules; two large plumose spiniform setae on distal margin; endopodite bifid from the base, lobes of differing size.
- **Uropods:** unremarkable; endopodite slightly longer than exopodite.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Phreatic (groundwater) freshwater systems — specifically an **artificial freshwater well** on Phuket Island, Thailand.
- **Ecological adaptations:** Depigmentation and anophthalmy (blindness) are classic stygobiotic adaptations to subterranean, aphotic aquatic environments, consistent with the broader [[Stenasellidae]] body plan.
- **Biogeographic context:** [[Stenasellidae]] are distributed across subterranean aquatic systems of emerged lands in the circum-Mediterranean region and throughout the entire tropical belt. Their presence in Southeast Asia is considered a biogeographically significant and non-accidental pattern, echoing similar recent discoveries in Central America.
- **Evolutionary hypothesis:** The paper situates the origin of [[Stenasellidae]] within ancestral **Tethyan interstitial eulittoral Asellota** — depigmented, anophthalmic marine ancestors that gave rise to freshwater phreatic lineages. This hypothesis (supported by Kussakin 1973; Magniez 1974) links marine interstitial fauna to the deep continental groundwater fauna seen today.
- **Phylogenetic relationships discussed:** The authors align with Fresi et al. (1980) in doubting Magniez's (1974, 1976) proposed phylogenetic link between [[Stenasellidae]] and [[Microcerberidea]], instead supporting the view that [[Gnathostenetroidea]] derived from the Stenetroidea–Stenasellidae complex.

## Key Findings

- **New species description:** *Stenasellus brignolii* n. sp. is formally described from Phuket, Thailand, based on one holotype and two paratypes (all female), collected 21 January 1981 by P. Brignoli & M. El Helou.
- **Third Southeast Asian stenasellid:** Confirms Magniez's (1981) prediction of additional stenasellid species in the Malay Peninsula and greater Southeast Asia region, alongside *St. chapmani* (Sarawak/Borneo, Malaysia) and an undescribed *Stenasellus* sp. (Cambodia; Boutin, 1971).
- **Comparative affinities:** Based on the structure of the female pleopod 2 and endopodite conformation of the pleopods, *S. brignolii* appears **more closely related to *St. chapmani* Magniez** (Malaysia) **than to the Cambodian *Stenasellus* sp.** (Boutin, 1971).
- **Distinguishing characters:** *S. brignolii* is readily distinguished from its regional congeners by the **shortness of the antennae and antennulae**, **reduced armature of the gnathopods and other pereiopods**, and especially the **distinct configuration of all pleopods**.
- **Broader phyletic hypothesis:** The three currently known Southeast Asian stenasellid species may represent a single, phyletically homogeneous complex — though the authors caution this may be only the "tip of the iceberg" of a much larger, still poorly documented regional groundwater fauna.
- **Taxonomic limitation:** All known Southeast Asian stenasellid specimens (across all three species) are female-only, constraining full comparative and diagnostic certainty pending discovery of male specimens.
- **Etymology:** The species is named in honor of Prof. P. Brignoli, who promoted the Southeast Asian research expeditions and collected the type material.
