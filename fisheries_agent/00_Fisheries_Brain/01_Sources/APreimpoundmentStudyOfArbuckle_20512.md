# Pre-Impoundment Study of Arbuckle Reservoir, Rock Creek, Murray County, Oklahoma

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This 1950s technical report from the [[Oklahoma Fishery Research Laboratory]] documents a pre-impoundment survey of the streams that would be flooded by the proposed [[Arbuckle Reservoir]] on [[Rock Creek]] in [[Murray County]], Oklahoma. Field collections in 1957 and 1958 recorded 39 fish species and estimated standing crops in pounds per acre. Using that baseline and data from neighboring lakes, the authors predicted the post-impoundment fishery, forecast a lake of about 3,000 acres at flood pool, and recommended rough fish removal, stocking, access facilities and a creel census.

## Background & Description

The study was undertaken at the request of the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], whose "Reconnaissance Report on Arbuckle Project Oklahoma" proposed an earthfill dam and multipurpose reservoir for flood control, municipal and industrial water supply, and recreation. The Fishery Research Laboratory was directed to describe the existing fishery, predict the post-impoundment fishery and its problems, and design a management program. The report was supported by the [[Oklahoma Wildlife Conservation Department]] and the [[University of Oklahoma Biological Survey]].

**Drainage basin**
- Rock Creek is a major tributary of the [[Washita River]], entering at river mile 84, and contributes about 42,840 acre-feet per year on average (1926–1957 record, total 1,370,900 acre-feet).
- Total basin area is 142.03 square miles, of which 131.71 square miles lie above the dam site.
- Main tributaries are [[Big Sandy Creek]], [[Buckhorn Creek]] and [[Travertine Creek]].
- The dam-site stream bed is at 785 ft msl; headwaters reach about 1,200 ft.
- The drainage has a dendritic pattern of old age with low gradients.

**Land use and climate**
- Land use is predominantly livestock agriculture, with asphalt mines and sand and gravel pits in the project area.
- The City of Sulphur, adjacent to [[Platt National Park]], is nationally known for its mineral waters.
- The average annual precipitation is 38.27 inches (41-year record at Sulphur), with a frost-free growing season of 218 days.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water quality**
- Total hardness was 300 ppm in Rock Creek, 205 ppm in Big Sandy Creek and 228 ppm in Buckhorn Creek. The authors attributed hardness to carbonates and described high calcium and magnesium levels as beneficial to fish growth.
- Bicarbonate averaged 336 ppm (1926–1957). Total dissolved solids averaged 449 ppm and specific conductance averaged 762 micromhos.
- Methyl orange alkalinity at sampling sites ranged from about 150 to 195 ppm, and pH was about 7.5 to 8.1.

**Hydrology and sediment**
- Flows at the dam site are mostly runoff. Big Sandy Creek is intermittent and has lost much of its channel storage to siltation. Rock Creek and Buckhorn Creek are permanent, fed by springs.
- Soils are alluvial, dark upland and parent material types. The reservoir would inundate about 3,173 acres below the flood pool.
- Sedimentation would be dominated by deltaic deposits at the mouths of the tributaries, with Big Sandy contributing mostly silt, sand and fine gravel and Buckhorn contributing a small volume of erodible material.
- Wind-driven waves would sort shoreline materials and form gravel bars, producing periodic turbidity mainly during floods.

**Reservoir physical forecasts**
- Thermal behavior was expected to resemble neighboring lakes, with midwinter homothermy and midsummer stratification. A thermocline of about 14–18 ft was expected in July–October.
- Shorelines would be highly indented, which the authors considered favorable for angling. Rough broken land would form about 65 percent of the shoreline at average annual maximum pool.

## Key Findings

**1. Pre-impoundment fish community (1957–1958)**
- Thirty-nine species were collected by minnow seine and rotenone at five stations (RC-1 to RC-5) in Rock Creek drainage.
- Relative abundance: 4 very abundant, 3 abundant, 5 common, 15 uncommon and 12 rare.
- Very abundant species included [[Red Shiner]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Gizzard Shad]].
- Sport and panfish were relatively numerous and made up about 44 percent of the total collected weight.

**2. Standing crop**
- The 1957 average was 392.8 lb/acre (range about 129 at RC-2/RC-6 to about 1,100 at RC-4).
- The 1958 average was 655.6 lb/acre (range about 55 to about 1,600).
- Combined average for both years was 524.2 lb/acre.

**3. Growth rates**
- Growth of collected fishes was slightly below State averages. Calculated growth was tabulated for 14 species, for example [[Largemouth Bass]] at 4.4, 7.8 and 12.3 inches at years 1 to 3.

**4. Predicted post-impoundment fishery**
- Table 14 lists about 59 species expected to occur in the reservoir, drawn from neighboring lakes and the local collections.
- Predicted standing crop totals 463.0 lb/acre. Key predictions include:
  - [[White Crappie]] about 50 lb/acre
  - Bluegill about 40 lb/acre
  - Gizzard shad about 130 lb/acre
  - Carp about 60 lb/acre
  - Freshwater drum about 25 lb/acre
  - Largemouth and spotted bass about 20 and 15 lb/acre
  - Channel catfish about 20 lb/acre
- Channel catfish were expected to reach about 12 inches and 0.5 lb.
- Rough fish could become abundant and dominate the standing crop by weight.

**5. Conclusions**
- The reservoir should be highly productive and favor largemouth bass, spotted bass, white bass, channel catfish and crappies.
- Restricted spawning and feeding habitat for rough fish should keep carp, carpsuckers and buffalo less numerous than game fish.
- Gizzard shad would be the primary early forage fish. Threadfin shad was expected but its significance was uncertain.
- Turbidity should be low except during floods, and sand and gravel shorelines should favor sunfish spawning.

**6. Recommendations**
- Reduce or eliminate rough fish before closure of the dam using emulsifiable [[Rotenone]], and ban the use of carp and rough fish as bait.
- Protect [[Ardmore City Lake]] from rough fish contamination through the connecting waterline, and manage the water system that supplies [[Veterans Lake]] and the City of Sulphur.
- Provide fish concentration points, fishing piers, launch ramps and cleared areas to aid removal.
- Stock largemouth bass, spotted bass, black crappie, channel catfish, redear sunfish and warmouth after rotenoning.
- Maintain a creel census to guide the management plan of the Oklahoma Wildlife Conservation Department.
