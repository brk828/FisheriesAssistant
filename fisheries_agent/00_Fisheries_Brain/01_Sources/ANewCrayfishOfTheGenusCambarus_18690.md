## Executive Summary
This paper serves as the formal species description for **[[Cambarus reburrus]]**, a novel crayfish species discovered in a single tributary stream in Jackson County, North Carolina. The species is morphologically distinct due to its long rostrum, broad areola, and notably long, subcylindrical, setiferous (bristly) chelae. Phylogenetically, the paper argues that *C. reburrus* represents a potential evolutionary intermediate between **[[Cambarus pristinus]]** and **[[Cambarus obeyensis]]**, retaining "primitive" body characteristics while displaying an "advanced" pleopod (reproductive appendage) structure.

## Background & Description
*Cambarus reburrus* is described based on a Holotype (Form I male), an Allotype (female), and a Morphotype (Form II male), collected from a single locality.

### Diagnostic Morphology
*   **Body:** Pigmented body with normal eyes.
*   **Carapace:** Subovate, punctate dorsally, granulate laterally. Features one prominent cervical spine on each side, posterior to the cervical groove.
*   **Rostrum:** Long (23–27% of carapace length), slightly excavate, with subparallel margins that lack marginal spines.
*   **Areola:** Notably broad (2.07 to 3.15 times longer than wide), comprising 30.2–33.8% of the total carapace length, containing 6–9 small punctations at its narrowest point.
*   **Chelae (Claws):** The defining feature of the species. They are subcylindrical, non-gaping, and bear conspicuous setae (bristles), with "bearded" bases on the fingers. The palm is very long, making up 33.3–37.3% of the total outer margin length of the chela. The inner margin of the palm has two rows of tubercles.
*   **First Pleopods (Males):** In Form I males, these reach the coxa of the third pereiopods when the abdomen is flexed. The terminal elements are recurved at more than a right angle to the main shaft—a critical diagnostic trait distinguishing it from relatives.
*   **Annulus Ventralis (Female):** Slightly movable, roughly 1.5x wider than long, with a distinct sinus pattern directed sinistrally then dextrally (see Fig. 10).

### Coloration
The species exhibits an olive-brown coloration with dark brown pigmented crescents in the branchiocardial region (lateral to the areola). This pattern continues posteriorly as dorsolateral stripes on the abdominal segments. The undersurface is tan to cream, with a slightly orange tint at the tips of the chelae fingers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*   **Type Locality:** A small tributary (~10 feet wide, pools up to 3 feet deep) flowing from Sapphire Lake (an impoundment, also known as Fairfield Lake) into the **[[Horsepasture River]]**, part of the upper **[[Savannah River]]** drainage system.
*   **Habitat Substrate:** The stream near the collection site (off U.S. 64) featured muddy to sandy substrates with overhanging banks. The water was noted as "somewhat malodorous when disturbed."
*   **Water Chemistry:** The water was tinted brown, attributed to drainage from an "iron" seepage located upstream of the highway crossing.
*   **Co-occurring Species:** Notably, no associate species of crayfish were found alongside *C. reburrus* at the type locality, suggesting a potentially isolated or specialized niche. This contrasts with the surrounding region, where **[[Cambarus asperimanus]]** and *C. b. bartonii* are the dominant crayfish species.
*   **Life History/Phenology:**
    *   Early April: Peak collection period; mating (copulation) was observed in collecting containers. Notably, no Form II males were found during this time.
    *   Early May: Females were found "in berry" (carrying eggs), though adult males of both reproductive forms (I and II) became scarce.

## Key Findings
1.  **New Species Confirmation:** *Cambarus reburrus* is validated as a distinct species based on a unique combination of traits: an elongate, slender, setiferous chela; a first pleopod bent at more than a right angle; a broad, short areola; and a rostrum lacking marginal spines.
2.  **Evolutionary Intermediate Hypothesis:** The primary scientific significance of this paper lies in its phylogenetic placement. *C. reburrus* shares strong morphological affinities with **[[Cambarus pristinus]]**, particularly in the "Procambarus-like" shape of the chelae.
3.  **Pleopod Evolution:** Despite retaining primitive non-secondary sexual characteristics (as defined by Hobbs, 1965) similar to *C. pristinus*, *C. reburrus* possesses a more "advanced" first pleopod structure. The author notes that if its pleopod more closely resembled that of **[[Cambarus obeyensis]]**, it would perfectly represent a missing evolutionary link between *C. pristinus* and *C. obeyensis*.
4.  **Endemism:** As of publication, *C. reburrus* was known only from its single type locality, indicating a highly restricted range within the **[[Savannah River Drainage]]**.
5.  **Type Repository:** Physical type specimens were deposited in the United States National Museum (USNM) and the Museum of Comparative Zoology (MCZ) to ensure taxonomic verifiability.
