# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Technical Team Meeting Notes March 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #environmental-policy #dam-operations #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting notes and correspondence from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Technical Team meetings held March 20-21, 1989, in Las Vegas, Nevada. The meetings addressed the Fiscal Year 1989 technical study plan, organizational restructuring of study teams, integration of environmental and economic research components, and specific research protocols for studying impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on aquatic resources. Primary focus areas included [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery dynamics, [[Humpback Chub]] conservation measures, sediment transport, recreational economics, and hydropower operations. The Bureau of Reclamation appointed study team leaders (Reed Harris for Environmental Team, Michael Roluti for Economic Team) and negotiated with Dr. Duncan Patten as Senior Scientist to coordinate technical work groups and ensure scientific credibility.

## Background & Description

The Glen Canyon Environmental Studies program was mandated by December 6, 1982 authorization from the Commissioner of Reclamation, Director of [[National Park Service]], and Assistant Secretaries to investigate environmental impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on downstream resources. Phase II studies commenced in 1989 following completion of Phase I investigations. The program operates under oversight of an Executive Review Committee (ERC) comprising representatives from Bureau of Reclamation, National Park Service, Western Area Power Administration, and other stakeholders.

The tailwater fishery below Glen Canyon Dam supports a [[Rainbow Trout]] population sustained by both natural reproduction and artificial stocking by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The river also provides critical habitat for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), requiring conservation measures under Section 7 consultation with [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Dam operations create fluctuating flows (ranging from 5,000 to over 30,000 cfs) driven by hydropower demand, with potential impacts on fish spawning, egg survival, juvenile stranding, sediment transport, and recreational access.

The organizational structure includes Environmental and Economic Study Teams meeting quarterly, with smaller technical work groups meeting more frequently to develop detailed study plans, analyze data, and prepare reports. The Senior Scientist position (40% time commitment via Intergovernmental Personnel Act) was created to ensure scientific credibility, coordinate integration across disciplines, and address National Academy of Sciences criticisms of Phase I regarding Reclamation leadership bias.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Colorado River below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] represents a highly modified aquatic ecosystem where dam operations control flow regime, water temperature (via thermal stratification in [[Lake Powell]]), and sediment supply. The tailwater supports a blue-ribbon trout fishery extending approximately 15 miles from the dam to [[Lees Ferry]], with productivity influenced by flow fluctuations, spawning habitat availability, food resources, water quality, and fishing pressure.

Key ecological concerns include:

**Trout Population Dynamics**: Flow fluctuations affect egg survival through desiccation when water levels drop, stranding of juvenile fish, access to spawning habitat, and food availability. Thermal stratification in Lake Powell influences downstream water temperature and nutrient/food base. Recent changes in fishery productivity (post-1986) require quantification and attribution to specific limiting factors.

**Endangered Species**: [[Humpback Chub]] populations in [[Grand Canyon]] require specific water quality conditions and habitat characteristics. The Fish and Wildlife Service issued a Non-Jeopardy Biological Opinion with conservation measures requiring implementation and monitoring. PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags were discussed as alternative to Carlin tags for fish tracking, though concerns existed about equipment reliability and cost.

**Sediment Transport**: Gaging stations monitor sediment, biological, chemical, and flow parameters. Statistical analysis of rating curves and sampling frequency requirements must be determined. Event-alert protocols needed for flood conditions. Computing sediment budgets involves calculating differences between large numbers with inherent uncertainty.

**Hydrological Constraints**: Annual Operating Plan (AOP) developed cooperatively with Seven Basin States determines monthly release volumes based on hydrological forecasts (probable maximum, most probable, probable minimum scenarios). Standard Operating Procedures define operational limits for each dam in the Colorado River Storage Project (CRSP) system. Firm load commitments drive fluctuating flow patterns with high flows December-January and July-August, low flows October-November and April-May.

## Key Findings

**Organizational Decisions**:
- Reed Harris appointed Environmental Team Leader; Michael Roluti appointed Economic Team Leader
- Dr. Duncan Patten selected as Senior Scientist (40% time via IPA, anticipated start April 1, 1989)
- Each agency/bureau/group requested to designate one primary representative and one alternate
- Technical work groups to meet frequently (weekly if needed) to develop detailed study plans; full teams to meet quarterly minimum
- Next meetings scheduled June 15-16, 1989, relocated from Grand Canyon to Flagstaff, Arizona due to limited summer lodging

**Study Plan Modifications**:
- **Sediment Transport (B-2)**: Add "flow" to event alert protocols; modify language from "are/is" to "may be"; specify sampling requirements for rating curve analysis at each gage station
- **Integration River Trip (A-2)**: CREDA offered to provide Glen Canyon Dam/Navajo Powerplant tour, one boat, and provisions; half-day raft trip from dam to Lees Ferry suggested
- **Flood Risk Analysis (A-4)**: Revised to analyze "flooding ratio (risk), hydrologic requirements, and relationship to marketing of hydropower" under "normal (post-filling) operational conditions"
- **Aerial Photography (A-6)**: Low flow removed from title; 5,000 cfs added to 1984 photography specifications
- **Recreational Benefits (C-4)**: Day-use rafting and angling benefits related to flows needed; carrying capacity analysis for Glen Canyon National Recreation Area stretch deferred to 1990-91 studies
- **Endangered Fish (D-1)**: Biological Opinion (Non-Jeopardy with conservation measures) transmitted to Albuquerque; $25,000 budget for study plan development and two river trips; PIT tags recommended over Carlin tags to avoid carbonate encrustation abrasion

**Trout Fishery Research Questions** (from Bill Davis, Salt River Project):

*Survival factors*: fishing pressure role, artificial stocking contribution, thermal stratification effects on food resources, rate and absolute flow impacts

*Spawning questions*: management alternatives to enhance reproduction, habitat availability at low/fluctuating flows, area of existing habitat, comparative worth of hatchery versus naturally spawned fish

*Management considerations*: Arizona Game and Fish Department practices, biomass production capacity, blue-ribbon fishery requirements, measurement methods sensitive enough to separate flow effects from other factors

**Economic Study Approach**:
- Evaluate three methods: EGEAS model, ELFIN model, and Western Area Power Administration (Peach report) method
- Analyze impacts on three generic entities representative of CRSP customers with generation resources
- Separate methodology may be needed for numerous small CRSP customers without generation
- Evaluation criteria include ability to handle institutional constraints (economic vs. non-economic dispatch, transmission availability, hydrology impacts, resource apportionment mechanisms)
- Trout scenario analysis revealed potential conflicts with normal operations requiring water movement between months
- Ramp rate restrictions pose significant operational challenges
- Non-firm power impacts to be examined for inclusion in analysis

**Hydrological and Operational Constraints**:
- 1989 Annual Operating Plan: dry year with 8.23 million acre-feet minimum release
- Scenarios modeled for probable maximum, most probable, and probable minimum conditions across seven CRSP dams
- Standard Operating Procedures define low limits for each dam (not CRSP operating criteria)
- Monthly water allocations difficult to modify ("wall" at monthly boundary)
- October 5,000 cfs controlled flow accommodated relatively easily; Sundays are off-peak days facilitating low flows
- Flood risk analysis completion uncertain for FY89 due to pending litigation constraints

**Integration and Protocol Issues**:
- Senior Scientist to lead development of 1990-91 studies and Integrated Resource Plan
- Work groups to coordinate information flow to study teams
- ERC Report format to be outlined up-front to ensure appropriate data collection
- Policy recommendations remain ERC responsibility, though scientific recommendations may be requested
- All research requests (except legally required permits) to be routed through GCES Flagstaff Office to avoid overwhelming park superintendents
- Protocol to be developed with both Grand Canyon National Park and Glen Canyon National Recreation Area

**Budget**: FY1989 total $533,000 (corrected from draft errors): Integration Studies $118,000, Trout Studies $110,000, other components as specified. Costs reflect basic scientific research but exclude Western Area Power Administration, power groups, Basin States, Senior Scientist, and Denver Office expenses.

**Fiscal Year 1990 Planning**: Study plan development to begin immediately for October 1989 approval, providing full year for implementation (addressing chronic problem of being six months behind). Senior Scientist's first task to coordinate this effort. Conservation measures for humpback chub may extend beyond ERC decision timeline, potentially requiring Section 7 reconsultation if major operational changes recommended.
