# Richard Cronin National Salmon Station

## Executive Summary
This document is a **station profile** (Exhibit 1) submitted by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Fisheries program for the **Richard Cronin National Salmon Station**, a National Fish Hatchery located in Sunderland, Massachusetts. Established in 1982 via a Congressional add-on under P.L. 96-126, the station's core mission is to annually transport, hold, and spawn returning [[Atlantic Salmon]] captured at the [[Holyoke Dam]] and/or [[Turners Falls Dam]] trapping facilities on the Connecticut River, and to distribute the resulting eggs to designated hatcheries such as [[White River National Fish Hatchery]]. The FY93 report highlights a record egg take (1,727,000 eggs) and the initiation of a pilot **kelt rejuvenation program**, reflecting active efforts to support the broader [[Connecticut River Atlantic Salmon Restoration Program]].

## Background & Description
While the source is a facility/administrative profile rather than a morphological study, it references distinct life-history stages of [[Atlantic Salmon]] relevant to hatchery operations:
- **Adults**: Broodstock captured as sexually mature returning fish (114 females, 50 males held in FY93).
- **Kelts**: Post-spawning adult salmon (spent fish) — 50 individuals from the '92 year class were retained for a rejuvenation program; 120 kelts were later shipped to [[Berkshire National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Captive Broodfish**: 600 individuals transferred from [[White River National Fish Hatchery]], housed in repaired outside raceways, representing a captive (non-anadromous) rearing lineage distinct from wild-origin returning adults.
- **Eggs/Fry**: Reproductive output measured in raw egg numbers (1,727,000 total), not characterized morphologically in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The station functions as a critical node in the anadromous life cycle management of Atlantic salmon within the **Connecticut River watershed**:
- **Anadromy management**: Fish are intercepted at dam trapping facilities ([[Holyoke Dam]], [[Turners Falls Dam]]) during their upstream spawning migration, artificially completing the reproductive cycle that dam infrastructure otherwise disrupts.
- **Hatchery infrastructure**: 20 concrete/gravel raceways (100' × 15') and multiple hatchery buildings support holding, spawning, and rearing operations, simulating riverine flow conditions for controlled propagation.
- **Distribution network**: Eggs and kelts are moved across a regional hatchery network (White River NFH, Berkshire NFH, State of Connecticut) to support both large-scale restoration stocking and genetic/egg banking safeguards.
- **Human interaction**: Approximately 2,500 visitors annually indicates a public education/outreach role tied to salmon restoration awareness in the region.

## Key Findings
- **FY93 broodstock**: 164 adult Atlantic salmon held (114 females, 50 males).
- **Record egg production**: 1,727,000 eggs taken in fall FY93 — described as a station record.
  - 68,000 eggs shipped to the State of Connecticut for **egg banking** purposes.
  - Remainder sent to [[White River National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Kelt rejuvenation pilot program**: 50 kelts from the 1992 year class retained on-station to test post-spawning survival/re-spawning potential; 120 kelts later distributed to [[Berkshire National Fish Hatchery]] in FY93.
- **Captive broodstock integration**: 600 captive broodfish from White River NFH added to four repaired outdoor raceways, suggesting infrastructure investment to expand captive breeding capacity.
- **Funding**: FY93 operations budget $110,800; maintenance $33,994.42 (up sharply from FY92's $18,800, indicating significant facility repairs). Staffing remained stable at 2 FTE across FY92–FY93.
- **Physical infrastructure**: Multiple steel/wood-frame buildings plus 20 raceways define the station's hatchery capacity.
- **Legislative/administrative context**: Station created under a Congressional add-on (P.L. 96-126, 96th Congress), overseen by USFWS Region 5, with mailing address in Sunderland, MA, under project leadership of Bernard M. Novak Jr.
