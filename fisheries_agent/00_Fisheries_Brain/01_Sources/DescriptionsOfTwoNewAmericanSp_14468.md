# Two New American Rockfish Species of the Genus Sebastodes, with Notes on Related Species (Hubbs & Schultz, 1933)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #scorpaenidae

## Executive Summary

This 1933 taxonomic paper by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Leonard P. Schultz]] describes two new rockfish species of the genus [[Sebastodes]] from the west coast of North America: [[Sebastodes Columbianus]] and [[Sebastodes Littoris]]. It also resolves long-standing synonymies and misidentifications among several related species. The authors revise three subgenera (Sebastosomus, Auctospina and Pteropodus) as defined by [[Jordan And Evermann]], while retaining the broad concept of Sebastodes. The work is based on collecting from 1922 to 1926 and on examination of museum and market specimens.

## Background & Description

The authors state that the species treated make up a considerable share of those assigned by Jordan and Evermann (1898) to the subgenera Sebastosomus, Auctospina and Pteropodus. These groups had recently been raised to generic rank by Jordan, Evermann and Clark (1930). Hubbs and Schultz did not assess the naturalness of these subgroups. They noted that relationships within them were "very imperfectly known," and so they revised each group as constituted by Jordan and Evermann.

Diagnostic characters used in the revision include:
- Body depth relative to standard length
- Snout and symphyseal knob shape
- Nasal spine exposure and occipital ridge development
- Fin-ray counts (see [[Fin-Ray Counts]])
- Coloration, including dark specks, light blotches and mid-side bands
- Maximum adult size

Each subgenus is keyed to species, with descriptions of the new taxa and synonymies for the others.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitat and distribution data appear for several species:

- **Columbia River mouth:** The holotype of *S. columbianus* was taken in a salmon trap at the Columbia River mouth. Surface water was silty, fairly warm, and had a density of 1.005 at high tide. Depth was 25 feet, the bottom sandy, and the shore moderately wide sand flats at low tide. The authors suggest the species is a local representative of *S. melanops*, confined to this brackish estuary.
- **Monterey, California:** *S. littoris* was caught at about 2 fathoms on sand bottom just off rocks covered with kelp and other algae. The authors describe it as an inshore species.
- **Inshore versus offshore pairs:** Several species appear to form inshore/offshore pairs. *S. chrysomelas* lives close to shore on rocky reefs and often enters the [[Tide-Pools]], whereas *S. carnatus* is caught in waters of moderate depth. The authors suspect these may be depth-related forms, and that the depths in current accounts may be reversed.
- **Range of Sebastosomus species:** *S. flavidus* ranges along the whole coast of Washington, Oregon and California, while *S. serranoides* ranges from San Francisco to Lower California and is common in southern California. *S. melanops* ranges from Southern Alaska to Point Arguello.
- **Market context:** At San Francisco, *S. flavidus* is taken commonly, sometimes covering the whole floor of a fish house. It is sold as "yellow-tail."

## Key Findings

**1. Synonymies resolved**
- *Sebastodes alexandri* is a synonym of *S. auriculatus*. The authors saw no character separating the two, apart from injury and absent coronal spines in one specimen.
- *Sebastodes gilberti* is a synonym of *S. dallii*. The authors transfer *dallii* out of the *auriculatus* group, since the original *dallii* description fits the species called *gilberti*.

**2. Confusion between S. serranoides and S. flavidus clarified**
- The error began with [[Frank Cramer]], who apparently had no true *flavidus* specimens and separated the two on "fictitious differences." [[Jordan And Evermann]] copied this error.
- The authors cite the Eigenmanns (1890), who first separated the forms correctly, and Hubbs (1928) as the only later author who avoided the error.
- *S. flavidus* extends north to Cape Flattery, while *S. serranoides* is not known north of San Francisco.
- The authors could not confirm *S. flavidus* at San Diego. They doubt earlier records of it there.

**3. Sebastodes columbianus, new species**
- The holotype is an adult 443 mm long, taken on July 20, 1926. Four other known specimens measure 400 to 458 mm.
- Counts: dorsal XIII, 14 (paratypes 13 or 14); anal III, 8 (7 or 8); pectoral 19 (18 or 19), with 9 unbranched rays; lateral-line pores 47 (48 to 53); scale rows 53 (53 to 57).
- Color is pale yellowish dusky, lacking the pale median streak of *S. melanops*. Dark spots are conspicuous but small.
- Named for the Columbia River, the type locality. The authors note it is distinct from *S. ovalis* in lacking flaring preocular spines and in having a larger mouth and less distinct symphyseal knob.

**4. Sebastodes littoris, new species**
- The holotype is a 274 mm adult from Monterey, California (1922). A 170 mm paratype was collected at Mussel Point, Pacific Grove, in 1923.
- Counts: dorsal XIII (XIV), 13; anal III, 6; pectoral 17, with 8 (9 or 10) unbranched rays; lateral-line pores 42 (41).
- It is closest to *S. vexillaris* and interpreted as its inshore counterpart. It also resembles *S. caurinus* and *S. dallii*, and differs from them in multiple characters.
- Color is deep brown and yellowish, with much more intense markings than in *S. vexillaris*. Named "of the shore" for its predicted habitat.

**5. Type-locality and identity of S. melanops**
- Girard's type of *S. melanops* came from Astoria, Oregon, at the mouth of the Columbia River. This raised doubts about whether *S. columbianus* might be the same species.
- The authors resolve these doubts by pointing to Girard's diagnostic features, including blackish color, pale median streak and ten unbranched pectoral rays.

**6. Variation in S. maliger and an aberrant specimen**
- *S. maliger* shows excessive variation in ground color, orange spotting, cheek stripes and fin-ray counts. The authors could not divide their material into two types.
- A 20-inch specimen from near Port Orford, Oregon, may represent an undescribed species or an extreme of individual variation.

**7. Range extension of S. dallii**
- A specimen from the San Pedro market extends the range of *S. dallii* into southern California. It measures 6.7 inches overall.

**8. Pteropodus key and status of S. rastrelliger**
- The authors accept the eight species recognized by Jordan and Evermann plus the new *S. littoris*, while noting that *S. rastrelliger* is probably not closely related to the others.

**9. Suggested future work**
- A detailed study of variable species pairs, including *S. chrysomelas* and *S. carnatus*, is recommended as "very promising."
- Walford was preparing a statement on *S. serranoides* and *S. flavidus* for *California Fish and Game*.
