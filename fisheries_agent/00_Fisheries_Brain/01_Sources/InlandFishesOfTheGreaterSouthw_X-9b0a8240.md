# Inland Fishes of the Greater Southwest: Centrarchid Species Accounts

#ichthyology #centrarchidae #non-native_species

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** Three centrarchid species are described: [[Lepomis microlophus]] (redear sunfish), [[Micropterus dolomieui]] (smallmouth bass), and [[Micropterus punctulatus]] (spotted bass). [[Lepomis microlophus]] exhibits geographic variation with two forms: one with 40-47 lateral-line scales and another with 34-39 lateral-line scales; Arizona specimens examined are the second form. [[Micropterus dolomieui]] reaches maximum TL of 69 cm, has 69-77 lateral-line scales, 13-15 soft dorsal rays, 11 (12) anal soft rays, and unbranched pyloric caecae; tongue is typically toothless. [[Micropterus punctulatus]] reaches maximum TL of ~61 cm, has 55-77 (usually 60-68) lateral-line scales, 11-13 soft dorsal rays, 9-11 anal soft rays, 15-16 pectoral rays, and unbranched pyloric caecae; distinct patch of teeth on tongue. Age, maturity, fecundity, and growth parameters: Not reported.

**Habitat Associations:** [[Micropterus dolomieui]] native to St. Lawrence-Great Lakes, Hudson Bay (Red River), and Mississippi basins from Quebec to North Dakota, south to Alabama and Oklahoma. [[Micropterus punctulatus]] naturally distributed in Mississippi basin from Ohio and West Virginia. Both species now established in southwestern river systems including [[Verde River]], [[Colorado River]], [[Salt River]], [[Gila River]], and [[San Juan Basin]].

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** [[Micropterus dolomieui]] "exploded" to invade and populate most of the [[Salt River]] system in only two to three years following early 1970s stocking in [[Black River]], eastern Arizona, demonstrating rapid dispersal capability.

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** [[Micropterus dolomieui]] is a carnivore feeding on crayfish, insects, and other fishes; feeds exclusively on fishes if sufficient numbers are available. When abundant, consistently has negative impact on native southwestern fishes, presumably through predation on early life-stages and adults of smaller taxa. Removal of the species from [[Fossil Creek]], Arizona, was accompanied by recruitment of native fishes unprecedented in recent history.

**Environmental Drivers:** Not reported.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** [[Micropterus dolomieui]] populations expanded rapidly following introductions; stocked in central Arizona (Verde River basin) in ~1942, lower Colorado River in 1950, and Black River in early 1970s. In New Mexico, widespread in Gila River and known from San Juan basin. No records yet from México.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** [[Micropterus dolomieui]] poses significant threat to native southwestern fish fauna through predation. Documented negative impacts on native species when abundant, particularly affecting early life-stages and smaller taxa.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations:** Removal of [[Micropterus dolomieui]] from systems supporting native fishes can result in unprecedented native fish recruitment, as demonstrated at [[Fossil Creek]], Arizona. This suggests active removal may be effective conservation strategy for native fish recovery.

**Biological Reference Points:** Not reported.
