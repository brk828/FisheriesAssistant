# Records of Freshwater Fishes in Florida

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #percidae

## Executive Summary

This faunal note by [[Suttkus and Yerger]] reports nine freshwater fish species added to the [[Florida Freshwater Fish Checklist]] from recent collections in western Florida. Six were collected for the first time in the state, two were resurrected from synonymy as valid species, and one had been mentioned only parenthetically in a earlier description. The authors also note several undescribed minnows and darters. The state total is raised to 103 primary freshwater species. Nearly all additions are confined to the Panhandle, a region where many [[Mississippi Valley Fauna]] species reach their eastern range limits.

## Background & Description

The paper extends the statewide inventories begun with [[Evermann and Kendall Checklist]] (1900), followed by [[Carr Key to Florida Freshwater Fishes]] (1937), [[Carr and Goin Guide]] (1955), [[Briggs Florida Fish Checklist]] (1958), and [[Kilby et al. Florida Additions]] (1959). Collections were made by the Florida State University and Tulane University teams, with substantial assistance from [[James M. Barkuloo]] of the Florida Game and Fresh Water Fish Commission. Specimens are catalogued in the FSU, USNM, TU, UF and CU collections, and records give specimen counts, standard length ranges, locality, county, date and collectors.

The most detailed account concerns the [[Black Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma duquesnei*). Western Florida specimens from the [[Apalachicola River]] were tentatively identified as this species. Meristic and proportional comparisons were made with northern populations (Pennsylvania, West Virginia, New York) and with [[Moxostoma erythrurum]] from Ohio and Arkansas. Specimens were collected during spawning on March 28-29, 1960.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Panhandle faunal break:** The Choctawhatchee and Escambia rivers share similar fish faunas and together mark a distinct faunal break in western Florida (Bailey, Winn and Smith, 1954).
- **Range extensions:** [[Moxostoma carinatum]] and [[Hybopsis aestivalis]] were recorded near the Alabama-Florida line in the Escambia and Choctawhatchee systems; both were spawning when collected.
- **Disjunct distributions:** [[Notropis welaka]] was found from the St. Johns River to the Chipola drainage, leaving a wide gap in central Florida that may reflect poor collecting or disjunct populations.
- **Sampling conditions:** Ohoopee shiner collections below [[Lake Talquin Dam]] were possible because of a severe drought and dam repair work that exposed normally inaccessible channels. The species occurs in sandy, unvegetated areas with moderate current.
- **Endemic darter:** [[Etheostoma okaloosae]] (Okaloosa darter) is restricted to a few small streams near Niceville, Okaloosa and Walton counties, around Choctawhatchee Bay.

## Key Findings

- **Nine new species** were recorded for Florida: *Moxostoma duquesnei*, *M. carinatum*, *Hybopsis aestivalis*, *Notropis welaka*, *N. zonistius*, *N. leedsi*, *Etheostoma histrio*, *E. parvipinne* and *E. okaloosae*. All but *N. welaka* are restricted to the Panhandle.
- **Taxonomic revisions:** *Notropis welaka* (formerly placed in synonymy with *N. maculatus*) and *Etheostoma okaloosae* are now recognized as valid species.
- **Apalachicola suckers:** Specimens from the Apalachicola River (66 examined) are tentatively identified as *M. duquesnei*. Tubercle patterns, meristics and proportions agree better with northern *M. duquesnei* than with *M. erythrurum*. The authors note that a clinal explanation is uncertain given limited northern samples.
- **Size-related change:** Within *M. duquesnei*, the caudal peduncle deepens and the snout lengthens relative to eye and postorbital measures as fish grow.
- **Range records:** *Moxostoma carinatum* extends the species' known range east from the Pearl River to the Escambia system. *Hybopsis aestivalis* extends east along the Gulf Coast to the Choctawhatchee River, and its tetranemus (four-barbel) condition appears in Escambia and Choctawhatchee populations but not in Pearl River or Cahaba specimens.
- **Checklist totals:** Evermann and Kendall (1900) recorded 61 species, of which 45 are freshwater. Carr (1937) listed 59 freshwater forms, Carr and Goin (1955) 77, and Briggs (1958) 88. The present paper brings the total to 103.
- **Future work:** Additional surveys of the larger rivers, particularly the Apalachicola and Conecuh, are expected to reveal further unrecorded species, though the total is unlikely to rise greatly.
