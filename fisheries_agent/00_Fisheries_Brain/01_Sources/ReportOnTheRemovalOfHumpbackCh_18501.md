# Report on the Removal of Humpback Chubs from the Little Colorado River (October 1975)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This report documents a brood-stock collection survey conducted by C.O. Minckley (Museum of Northern Arizona) on the [[Little Colorado River]] near its confluence with the mainstem [[Colorado River]] in October 1975. The objective was to capture adult [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) for transport to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] as part of an ongoing propagation program for this species, which is now recognized as endangered. Despite multi-method sampling efforts (trammel nets, Swedish gill nets, seining), only six adult humpback chubs were captured, of which three survived transport to the hatchery. The unexpectedly low adult catch rates across all species were attributed to abnormally low mainstem Colorado River discharge (1026 cfs) from [[Glen Canyon Dam]], which dried out habitat areas historically used for fishing this species. Notably, smaller/juvenile humpback chubs and young-of-year of other species were abundant in an upstream backwater area, suggesting differential habitat use and vulnerability to flow conditions by life stage.

## Background & Description
- Focus of this report is population/collection data rather than detailed morphology; however, size-class distinctions are noted:
  - **Adult Humpback Chub**: 6 individuals captured via Swedish gill nets in the mainstream/confluence area.
  - **Juvenile/Young-of-Year Humpback Chub**: 23 smaller individuals captured via seining ~0.4 km upstream in the Little Colorado River, primarily from a backwater habitat.
- No morphometric measurements (length, weight, hump depth) are reported in this document.
- Species co-occurring with humpback chub in samples for comparative context: [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Mountain-Sucker]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Cutthroat Trout]] (mainstem only).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Segregation by Life Stage**:
  - Adult humpback chubs were associated with the confluence area and required specific mainstream flow conditions; their scarcity coincided with dried-out habitat due to low dam releases.
  - Juvenile/YOY humpback chubs occupied a low-velocity **backwater habitat** ~0.4 km upstream, alongside [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Mountain-Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], and [[Common Carp]].
- **Water Temperature**: 21°C recorded in both the mainstream Little Colorado River and the upstream backwater; deemed *not* a limiting factor for adult chub absence.
- **Flow Dependency**: Adult chub presence/capture success appears strongly tied to Colorado River discharge levels from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] — low releases (1026 cfs) desiccated historically productive fishing areas.
- **Downstream Seining Station**: No humpback chubs found; habitat was either dry or dominated by colder mainstem Colorado River water intrusion. This zone was dominated by [[Fathead Minnow]] (n=40) and larval catostomids.
- **General System Use**: Overall fish abundance (including typically ubiquitous [[Common Carp]]) was low throughout the system during the survey, indicating reduced habitat availability/use under low-flow conditions.

## Key Findings
- **Total Adult Humpback Chub Catch**: 6 individuals (via Swedish gill nets); 2 escaped, 1 died, **3 successfully transported** to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery.
- **Transport Method**: Styrofoam coolers lined with triple garbage bags (3-mil), 5 gallons water, pure oxygen atmosphere; fish were in hatchery raceways within 5 hours of capture.
- **Trammel Net Yield**: 16 [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], 17 [[Channel Catfish]], 8 [[Bluehead Mountain-Sucker]], 2 [[Common Carp]].
- **Swedish Gill Net Yield**: 6 [[Humpback Chub]], 13 [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], 1 [[Cutthroat Trout]] (mainstem Colorado River).
- **Seining Yield (Upstream Station, 0.4 km)**: 23 Humpback Chub, 7 Flannelmouth Sucker, 5 Channel Catfish, 1 Speckled Dace, 1 Bluehead Mountain-Sucker, 40 Fathead Minnow, 1 larval catostomid.
- **Seining Yield (Downstream Station)**: 0 Humpback Chub, 40 Fathead Minnow, 2 Common Carp, 3 larval catostomids — no adult or juvenile chubs recorded.
- **Low Adult Abundance Hypothesis**: Attributed to reduced mainstream Colorado River flows (1026 cfs from Glen Canyon Dam) drying out preferred adult fishing/habitat areas — **not** attributable to water temperature (21°C, within normal tolerance).
- **Life-Stage Independence**: Presence of abundant juvenile/YOY humpback chubs despite adult scarcity suggests that low-flow conditions affecting adults do not similarly impact smaller/younger fish, possibly due to differing habitat requirements (backwater vs. mainstem use).
- **Management Implication**: Findings underscore the need for additional ecological studies of the Little Colorado River system, particularly regarding flow-habitat relationships and their differential impacts across humpback chub life stages.
- **Permitting/Funding**: Conducted under permit PRT 8-225-C (Office of Endangered Species Region II funding), with field support from Arizona State University and Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery personnel, and access facilitated by Arizona Game and Fish Department, National Park Service (Grand Canyon), and the Navajo Nation.
