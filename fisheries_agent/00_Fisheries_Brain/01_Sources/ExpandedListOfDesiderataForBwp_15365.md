# Museum of Zoology Correspondence on Catostomus Taxonomy and Distribution (1981)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a June 1, 1981 letter from [[Robert R. Miller]], Curator of Fishes at the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, to [[Wendell L. Minckley]] at Arizona State University. It is attached to a May 23, 1981 list of Mexican fish photographs requested for the planned *Fishes of México*. The letter covers three topics: dorsal fin ray variation in the flannelmouth sucker ([[Catostomus Latipinnis]]), the native status of the [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*) in the upper [[Rio Grande]], and a historical record of *Catostomus insignis* from the [[Virgin River]] drainage. The letter also comments on how North American freshwater fish zoogeography may be shaped by a forthcoming atlas.

## Background & Description

- **Sender:** [[Robert R. Miller]], Curator of Fishes, Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan.
- **Recipient:** [[Wendell L. Minckley]], Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe.
- **Enclosures:** (1) an expanded list of black-and-white photograph requests for *Fishes of México*, dated May 23, 1981; (2) a table of dorsal fin ray counts for *C. latipinnis*.
- **Photo request list:** About 40 Mexican taxa are listed, grouped by family: Catostomidae (e.g., *C. wigginsi*, *C. bernardini*, *C. insignis*, *C. plebeius*, *Cycleptus elongatus*, *Ictiobus labiosus*, *Ictiobus niger*, *Moxostoma* spp.), Cyprinidae (e.g., *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Dionda* episcopa complex, *Hybopsis aestivalis*, *Notropis* spp., *Pimephales* spp., *Rhinichthys osculus*, *Tiaroga cobitis*), Ictaluridae (*Ictalurus lupus*, *I. mexicanus*, *I. pricei*, *Pylodictis olivaris*), Percidae (*Etheostoma australe*, *E. grahami*), Centrarchidae (*Lepomis megalotis*), and Poeciliidae (*Gambusia longispinis*, *G. marshi*). The list is a request, not a data set.
- **Context:** Minckley had submitted an NPS report on Middle Colorado River suckers, which Miller refers to as "your Table 4."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Middle Colorado River suckers:** Miller reports that his own data on *C. latipinnis* (collected 1951–1965) are comparable with Minckley's Table 4. He notes "a sharp increase" in the [[Gila River]] basin.
- **Introduced white sucker:** Miller cites [[Koster (1957)]] (p. 44) as documenting the introduction of the white sucker in the upper Rio Grande. He reports that Jerry Smith has Pleistocene fossils from the upper Rio Grande (in Colorado) containing two *Catostomus* (sensu lato) species, neither of which is *C. commersoni*. Miller therefore disputes the native status implied by the Lee and Cucas (1980, map p. 376) record.
- **Virgin River system:** Miller points to a *Catostomus insignis* record from the Virgin River drainage (Woodbury, 1933, *Ecol. Monogr.* 3:186), which Minckley's 1978 paper with Jerry Smith had overlooked. The two specimens (1 adult, 1 young) were collected by Tanner in 1927 and 1928, one from Zion National Park and one from the Santa Clara River. Both are coarse-scaled (ca. 70 lateral-line scales). Miller links this to the "Virgin River problems" Minckley had been studying.
- **Zoogeographic outlook:** Miller expresses concern that the zoogeography of North American freshwater fishes will rest largely on the forthcoming Atlas.

## Key Findings

1. **Dorsal fin ray variation in *Catostomus latipinnis*** (counts by drainage; the table's column alignment is partly garbled in the scan, so values are reported as given):

| Drainage | Samples (N) | Mean dorsal rays |
|---|---|---|
| Upper Colorado (Lee's Ferry and above) | 217 | 12.92 |
| Little Colorado | 64 | 12.00 |
| Little Colorado to Virgin River | 35 | 12.06 |
| Virgin River system | 101 | 12.22 |
| Gila River system | 27 | 13.85 |

   The total is 444 specimens. The Gila River sample has the highest mean (13.85), while the other drainages fall between 12.00 and 12.92.

2. **White sucker status:** Miller disputes a native origin for *C. commersoni* in the upper Rio Grande, citing a historical introduction (Koster, 1957) and fossil evidence of two other *Catostomus* species.

3. **Virgin River record:** Specimens of *C. insignis* from Zion National Park and the Santa Clara River (Tanner, 1927–1928) are held by the museum. Their identification requires careful checking, and Miller planned to ask Brigham Young University (BYU) about additional specimens, which were originally "duplicates" given to Hubbs.

4. **Photographic needs:** The museum is seeking photographs for *Fishes of México*, with Minckley asked to supply images of the taxa listed in the enclosure.

5. **Next steps:** Miller plans to meet Minckley at the Corvallis meeting, and he will bring a copy of the photo request list.
