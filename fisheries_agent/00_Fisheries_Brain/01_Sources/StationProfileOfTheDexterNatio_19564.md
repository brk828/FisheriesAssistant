# Station Profile of the Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative and operational **station profile** for the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) facility located near Dexter, New Mexico. Established under the White Act of 1931 (station founded 1932), the facility serves as the primary national hub for the captive propagation, genetic conservation, and applied research of **imperiled native fishes of the western United States**, particularly species endemic to southwestern desert river systems (Colorado River, Rio Grande, Gila River basins). Unlike a typical production hatchery, its mission is oriented toward genetic gene-bank maintenance, culture technique development, and interagency/international coordination (including Mexican states of Chihuahua, Sonora, and Nuevo León) rather than sport or commercial fish production.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Facility habitat structure**: Station lands total 640 acres (Section 16, Township 13 South, Range 26 East). Culture infrastructure includes:
  - A holding house with sixteen 360-gallon concrete tanks, a hatching battery, and laboratory space.
  - Four 6' × 40' raceways.
  - Sixteen 3' × 10' fiberglass circular tanks.
  - 48 earthen ponds ranging from 0.1 to 1.8 surface acres, totaling **22 surface acres** of pond habitat.
- These varied culture environments (raceways, circular tanks, earthen ponds) allow the station to replicate different life-stage and species-specific habitat requirements for desert fishes threatened with extinction in the wild, functioning as artificial refugia analogous to natural [[Southwestern Desert Fish Habitat]].
- The program's ecological role extends beyond the physical station: it maintains **genetically fit and diverse gene pools** for wild populations facing habitat loss, hybridization, and other extinction pressures, effectively serving as an ex-situ conservation reservoir linked to recovery efforts in the wild across multiple states (AR, AZ, CA, CO, NM, NV, SD, TX, UT).

## Key Findings
- **Station mission** is sevenfold: (1) maintain genetically diverse gene pools of extinction-threatened fishes, (2) develop culture techniques for imperiled species, (3) conduct biological studies addressing knowledge gaps, (4) publish and disseminate findings, (5) supply live/preserved specimens to permitted agencies/institutions, (6) provide tissue material for electrophoretic and mtDNA studies, and (7) run public outreach on the plight of southwestern imperiled fishes.
- **Interagency coordination** is extensive, spanning Service divisions (Endangered Species, Fishery Assistance Offices), other FWS Regions, federal agencies, state fish and game agencies, universities, Native American Tribes, and Mexican state entities (Chihuahua, Sonora, Nuevo León) — reflecting the transboundary nature of southwestern desert fish conservation, relevant to [[Rio Grande Basin Fish Conservation]] and [[Colorado River Basin Fish Conservation]].
- **Egg and fry distribution** (FY1987–FY1991) shows substantial output to federal and state waters, peaking at over 1.6 million eggs/fry distributed in FY1987, with FWS/other federal waters receiving up to 448,406 (FY1990) and state waters receiving as much as 1,346,791 (FY1987).
- **Fish (non-egg/fry) distribution** data (FY1987–FY1991) shows a general decline over time, from 343,093 in FY1987 to under 45,000 in FY1991, though this may reflect a shift in program emphasis toward eggs/fry rather than reduced overall output.
- **Funding and staffing** trends (1988–1992): Operations budget ranged from $349,000–$400,000 (estimated FY1992 high), while Maintenance funding rose sharply from $41,000 (1989) to an estimated $335,000 (1992), possibly reflecting infrastructure investment. Full-time equivalent (FTE) staffing fluctuated between 7.69 and 9.0 over the period, indicating relatively stable but modest staffing levels for a program of this national scope.
- The facility's physical plant (640 acres, 22 acres of ponds, multiple raceway/tank systems, visitor's center, and multiple support/maintenance buildings) underscores its role as a **national-scale specialized facility**, distinct from standard production hatcheries, dedicated to the [[Species Recovery Program|recovery]] of the region's most imperiled native fish fauna.
