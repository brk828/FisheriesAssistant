## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 administrative letter and concurrent-session program from the Madrean Archipelago Conference, organized by Dr. John Rinne (USDA Rocky Mountain Forest and Range Experiment Station). Rather than a single research paper, it compiles abstracts from **Concurrent Session IV: Aquatic Resources**, addressing native fish conservation in the "sky islands" of southern Arizona and northern Mexico. Central themes include the ecological role of montane [[Sky Island Ecosystems]] as refugia for endangered and threatened native fishes, the ongoing displacement of native ichthyofauna by nonnative invasive species, binational (U.S.–Mexico) recovery efforts for [[Yaqui River Basin]] fishes, and species-specific microhabitat studies of the [[Sonora Chub]]. A related non-fish abstract on the [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] and a biogeographic classification/mapping paper are also included, reflecting the conference's broader ecosystem-level focus on Madrean biodiversity.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological description is minimal, but size-class data are reported for the **Sonora Chub (Gila ditaenia)**, used to structure a microhabitat-use study:

- **Juveniles:** < 2.5 cm (total length)
- **Subadults:** 2.5–5.0 cm
- **Adults:** > 5.0 cm

Body-size class strongly influenced microhabitat selection (depth, substrate, cover proximity), suggesting ontogenetic shifts in swimming capability and predator-avoidance strategy typical of small-bodied [[Cyprinidae]].

Other species are noted only taxonomically:
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) — federally endangered live-bearing poeciliid.
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — native cyprinid co-occurring with Gila topminnow.
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*) — a single individual collected in Redrock Canyon in 1987, indicating rarity/marginal presence.
- [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Cyprinella formosa*) and [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) — targets of binational reintroduction and broodstock programs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sky Island Hydrology and Refugia**
Isolated mountain ranges in the Basin and Range Province receive orographically enhanced precipitation, sustaining perennial headwater streams unavailable in surrounding desert basins. These streams:
- Serve as **refugia** for rare, often federally/state-listed native fishes.
- Recharge aquifers that emerge downstream as desert springs and reocrenes, extending habitat availability.
- Are hydrologically and biologically **isolated**, limiting fish dispersal between drainages and even within the same mountain range ([[W.L. Minckley]] abstract).

**Redrock Canyon Case Study (Gila Topminnow)**
- Intermittent tributary to Sonoita Creek (Santa Cruz River drainage), one of only eight Arizona sites with a natural Gila topminnow population.
- Monitored 1979–1993: native assemblage (Gila topminnow + longfin dace) present throughout in 1979.
- Nonnative **western mosquitofish** and **largemouth bass** established over time; **green sunfish** and **bluegill** later detected.
- Western mosquitofish now dominate headwaters, tributaries, and stream mouth — pattern interpreted as either coexistence or slow competitive replacement, consistent with documented mosquitofish-driven extirpations of Gila topminnow elsewhere.
- Conservation actions: livestock/vehicle exclusion fencing, grazing management changes, watershed stabilization structures, recreational trail siting restrictions.

**Sonora Chub Microhabitat Use (Sycamore Creek)**
- Endemic to the **Rio de la Concepción Basin**; listed as Federal Threatened and Arizona State Endangered.
- Quantified during 1991 summer drought across eight stream sites (pools).
- **Depth:** juveniles in shallow water (5–20 cm); subadults intermediate (10–40 cm); adults used full depth range but favored deepest water.
- **Substrate:** bedrock preferred by adults/subadults when available; in its absence, subadults chose coarse substrate (gravel/rubble/boulder); juveniles used fine substrate.
- **Velocity:** juveniles and adults selected near-zero-velocity microhabitats; subadults used velocity in proportion to availability (generalist).
- **Cover/Shade:** all size classes favored unshaded areas near rock crevices; cover-proximity decreased with increasing body size (juveniles closest, adults farthest).
- Habitat selection patterns varied among pools — interpreted as **ecological plasticity** in response to local resource availability.

**Yaqui River Basin Ichthyofauna**
- 36 native species recorded across Arizona, Sonora, and Chihuahua; 4 species remain undescribed.
- ~50% of the basin lies within the **Madrean Biogeographic Province**, hosting 13 native species, 8 of which historically ranged across both the U.S. and Mexico.
- Four historically present species have been **extirpated from southeastern Arizona** due to land/water use practices and nonnative species introductions.
- Binational recovery actions:
  - Reintroduction of **beautiful shiner** into the San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge.
  - Acquisition of **Yaqui catfish** broodstock for future Arizona reintroductions.
  - Development of a U.S. recovery plan and a Sonora/Chihuahua population monitoring program.

**Chiricahua Leopard Frog (associated amphibian system)**
- Metapopulation structure tied to Madrean mountain ranges and lower-elevation permanent water systems.
- Metapopulations typically comprise <10 subpopulations within a few km of each other along interconnected drainages.
- Historical persistence higher at Madrean (montane) sites (7 of 24) than lowland sites (1 of 9).
- Subpopulation extinction now outpaces recolonization, likely due to degraded dispersal corridors — a pattern ecologically parallel to isolated sky-island fish populations.

## Key Findings

- **Sky islands function as critical refugia** for rare native desert fishes via localized precipitation enhancement, perennial headwater streams, and aquifer recharge feeding downstream springs.
- **Nonnative species introduction is the dominant threat** to native fish persistence across the region — documented at Redrock Canyon (mosquitofish, bass, sunfish, bluegill displacing/coexisting with Gila topminnow) and across the broader Yaqui Basin (extirpation of 4 native species).
- **Habitat isolation** (both among sky-island drainages and within single mountains) shapes fish community vulnerability and limits natural recolonization after local extirpation.
- **Ontogenetic microhabitat partitioning** in [[Sonora Chub]] (by depth, substrate, velocity, and cover) reflects adaptive plasticity to variable pool conditions during drought stress.
- **Binational U.S.-Mexico collaboration** is actively driving native fish recovery in the Yaqui River system, including reintroduction of beautiful shiner and broodstock development for Yaqui catfish.
- Species-level taxonomic knowledge remains incomplete — 4 undescribed fish species persist in the Yaqui River Basin, underscoring urgent need for further taxonomic and biological study before habitat loss or nonnative introductions eliminate them.
- Cross-taxon parallels (fish and [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] metapopulations) support a **regional metapopulation conservation framework** for Madrean Archipelago aquatic biodiversity, emphasizing corridor restoration and rate-of-extinction/recolonization dynamics.
- A hierarchical **biotic community classification and mapping system** (Brown & Reichenbacher, adopted by EPA's Arid Ecosystems Resource Group) provides a standardized tool for delineating Madrean habitats relevant to aquatic and terrestrial biodiversity assessment.
