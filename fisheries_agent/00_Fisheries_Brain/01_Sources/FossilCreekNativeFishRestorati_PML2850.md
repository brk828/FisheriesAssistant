# Fossil Creek Native Fish Restoration Project Report (2004 Renovation)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This draft final report by David A. Weedman of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) documents the [[Fossil Creek Native Fish Restoration]] project, conducted in autumn 2004 in the Mazatzal Mountains of central Arizona. The project removed invasive [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]] (and suspected [[Yellow Bullhead]]) from about 8.7 miles of stream by salvaging native fish, applying the piscicide [[Antimycin]] (as Fintrol and Fintrol-15 sand), and neutralizing the toxicant with [[Potassium Permanganate]]. Stream renovation was motivated by the planned decommissioning of the [[Childs-Irving Hydroelectric Project]], which would return full flows to the creek. Post-treatment monitoring found no live target nonnative fish.

## Background & Description

- **Project context:** Arizona Public Service (APS) operated the Childs and Irving hydroelectric plants under a Special Use Permit from the [[U.S. Forest Service]]. Diversion of Fossil Springs flow (about 43 cfs base flow) supplied the plants. APS signed an Agreement in Principle in 1999 to decommission the facilities; FERC was analyzing the effects of decommissioning. Full base flow was to return by 31 December 2004, so native fish restoration had to be completed first.
- **NEPA:** A draft Environmental Assessment was prepared in December 2003; the USFS issued a Decision Notice and Finding of No Significant Impact on 9 June 2004. The selected action was a concrete fish barrier in the Mazatzal Wilderness and renovation of the stream with Fintrol. A 45-day appeal period passed without appeals, followed by a five-day wait; implementation began 2 August 2004.
- **Cooperators:** Coconino and Tonto National Forests, USFS Southwestern Region, [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR), AGFD, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), [[Northern Arizona University]] (NAU) and APS.
- **Study area:** Fossil Springs produces a constant flow of about 43 cfs at about 21 °C (approximately 70 °F). Most flow is captured at the Fossil Springs Diversion Dam, leaving base flows of 0.4 to 5 cfs below it. The creek forms the boundary between Yavapai and Gila counties and flows about 14.3 miles before joining the [[Verde River]].
- **Native fishes present:** Six native species were reported: Sonoran sucker, desert sucker, roundtail chub, headwater chub, speckled dace and longfin dace. Gila topminnow and razorback sucker had been reintroduced previously.
- **Nonnative fishes:** Green sunfish were found from the Verde confluence upstream to a sunfish barrier about 0.5 km below the diversion dam. Smallmouth bass were found from the confluence upstream to a waterfall near Irving.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Travertine system:** Fossil Creek is unusual for its high dissolved CO2 and calcium carbonate. Outgassing deposits travertine, which forms small dams and pools that provide habitat for fish and invertebrates. Diversion removed travertine formation from the upper creek and shifted it downstream to the Irving and Childs outflow areas.
- **Water quality:** Water chemistry changed sharply along the project reach, most notably in iron, turbidity and total suspended solids. Dissolved iron was 519 µg/L just above Irving, compared with less than 100 µg/L at the upper diversion dam and other sites. Turbidity was 45.6 NTU at the same location, and it is attributed to spring inflow, runoff and road and parking-lot sediment.
- **Concern for invasives:** Smallmouth bass and green sunfish were limiting native recruitment; few age 0–1 natives were found in invaded areas.

## Key Findings

**Rationale for piscicide selection**
- [[Fintrol]] works at 10–25 ppb, compared with about 1 ppm for [[Rotenone]].
- Antimycin is toxic to all life stages, including eggs, and its action is irreversible (Berger et al. 1969). Rotenone is ineffective on eggs and has been shown to cause avoidance.

**Bioassays (four in total)**
1. **First bioassay (3–5 Aug 2004):** Not positive. Drip stations at 25 ppb (6 h) and 100 ppb (3 h) did not produce complete sunfish mortality. Mortality was about 50% at 100 ppb after 20 hours. Low water-quality compatibility and possible pool-related delays were suspected.
2. **Second bioassay, controlled tanks (10–12 Aug 2004):** Results were positive. Green sunfish reached 100% mortality at 25 ppb in 20 h and at 50 ppb in 4 h. Smallmouth bass reached 100% at 25 ppb in 6 h and at 50 ppb in 3 h. Yellow bullhead were unaffected at 25–400 ppb; 20% mortality occurred at 800 ppb.
3. **Third bioassay (24–26 Aug 2004):** Not fully successful. Sunfish were 100% dead within 100 m at 100 ppb but not at 50 ppb. The results were linked to high iron concentrations.
4. **Fourth bioassay (7–8 Sep 2004):** Positive. About 100% green sunfish mortality at 50 ppb over four hours, aided by Antimycin-coated sand and backpack sprayers.
- **Conclusion:** Successful renovation should be possible at 50 ppb, with 100 ppb needed in the high-iron reach.

**Iron effect**
- Manufacturer communication indicated that iron is taken up by fish hemoglobin and can partly block antimycin transfer to mitochondria, so higher doses may be needed in high-iron water.

**Renovation phases**
- **Fish salvage (Oct 11–15 and Nov 3–8, 2004):** Captured with backpack electrofishers, hoop nets, seines and minnow traps. A flume collapse on Oct 16 caused the captive roundtail chub to be returned to the creek. Native fish were held in twelve circular raceways.
- **Reaches 1–2 (Oct 18–22, 2004):** Treated at 0.5 cfs with 44 units of Fintrol (21.1 L) and 112 lb of Fintrol-15 sand. About 30 people covered about 2.9 miles.
- **Reaches 3–4 (Nov 8–12, 2004):** Treated at 11–17 cfs with 750 units of Fintrol (360 L) and 301 lb of Fintrol-15 sand. About 40 people covered 5.8 miles. Actual application was 35–54 ppb because of discharge changes.
- **Neutralization:** About 160 gallons of 20% liquid sodium permanganate were used (Nov 10–13). A 2.5% potassium permanganate solution was used in Reaches 1–2.
- **Repatriation:** Native fish were returned on Nov 17, 2004, and at least 90% of the reported numbers were estimated to be repatriated. Mortality in captivity was low, except for speckled dace, where about 80 died.

**Results**
- Post-treatment observations found no live fish in the treated reaches. Electrofishing in Reaches 1–2 and hoop netting in Reaches 3–4 found no green sunfish or smallmouth bass (Reid 2004).
- No mortality was observed below the neutralization station. A rainfall event of likely 20 cfs or more diluted residual toxicant.

**Recommendations and lessons**
- Drip stations should be spaced about 150 m apart because of antimycin degradation.
- Drip-bucket clogging was caused by undissolved soy lipids. Solutions included decanting, adding acetone and surfactant, and reducing charge volumes.
- A modified five-gallon bucket dispenser (Stefferud and Propst 1996 design, with brass fittings and O-ring seals) is described in Appendix 6.
