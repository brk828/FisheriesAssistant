# A Basic Survey of Waters in District 1 (Luna Lake, Arizona)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #Salmonidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1958–1959 Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report presents a basic physical, chemical, and biological survey of **[[Luna Lake]]**, a high-elevation reservoir (7,880 ft) in the Alpine Valley of Apache County, Arizona, used as a representative case study for District 1 waters (which also included Big Crescent, Woods Canyon, Show Low, Fool Hollow, Lyman, Concho, and Lee Valley Lakes). The study followed standardized limnological methods (Lagler, *Studies in Freshwater Fishery Biology*) with bi-weekly sampling during spring–fall and monthly sampling in winter. The lake supports a self-sustaining and heavily stocked **[[Rainbow Trout]]** fishery, with historic but now-absent **[[Brown Trout]]**, and a native/naturalized population of **[[Northern Fathead Minnow]]** (*Pimephales promelas*). Overall water quality was found to be productive, with high plankton standing crops, generally adequate dissolved oxygen (except localized summer hypolimnetic depletion), and excellent trout growth and condition factors.

## Background & Description

### Lake Morphometry (Physical Habitat Structure)
- **Maximum surface area:** 154.5 acres (yearly average ~125 acres)
- **Maximum length:** 6,980 ft; **Maximum width:** 1,560 ft
- **Mean width:** 985 ft
- **Mean depth:** 8.99 ft; **Maximum depth:** 24 ft
- **Shoreline length:** 19,500 ft
- **Shore development index:** 1.77 (moderate)
- **Mean basin slope:** 30.9% (shallow, simple basin form)
- **Shoal area:** 99.8% of lake — highly productive littoral zone
- **Basin type:** Simple basin, sandy silt bottom with organic detritus; localized broken rock/rubble on steeper shoals

### Fish Growth & Condition (Rainbow Trout)
Length-weight relationships were sampled across a size range of 5.0–18.9 inches (Table II), showing:
- **Condition factor (C)** ranged from ~37.6 (small fish, 6–6.9 in) to ~43.0 (10–10.9 in class), generally stabilizing in the high 30s to low 40s across size classes — indicative of **excellent** body condition relative to regional norms.
- Weight increased proportionally from 0.082 lb (5.0–5.9 in class) to 2.54 lb (18.0–18.9 in class).
- No data gaps existed for 8.0–9.9 in and 16.0–16.9 in size classes (no fish sampled in those ranges).

### Minnow Population
- **[[Northern Fathead Minnow]]** (*Pimephales promelas*) present in small numbers; no morphometric data reported beyond species identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Physical & Climatic Setting
- Located in the upper **[[San Francisco River]]** drainage, eastern slope of the White Mountains, bounded by Noble Mountain, the Blue Range, and Escudilla Mountain.
- Surrounded by **ponderosa pine (Transition Life Zone)** forest; aspen above 8,200 ft; short-grass valley with light agriculture.
- Summer temps: 80–85°F day, 40s°F night; winter: moderate-heavy snow, frequent sub-freezing temps, ~2 months ice cover.
- Hydrology dominated by snowmelt and summer rains; one small intermittent inlet stream (1.2 second-feet in dry weather) plus minor perimeter drainage ditches. Subject to summer irrigation drawdown.

### Vegetation & Primary Producers
- Rooted aquatic macrophytes: *Eleocharis*, *Potamogeton*, *Juncus* (upper shallow end); sparse *Myriophyllum* on sandy shoals.
- Filamentous green algae *Rhizoclonium* sp. associated with rooted vegetation.
- Dense **pelagic phytoplankton blooms** (*Closterium* sp.) in mid-summer, driving turbidity and pH swings.

### Water Chemistry (Habitat Quality Drivers)
- **Temperature:** Surface max 70°F (Aug 21), min 33°F (Jan 10); no thermal stratification/thermocline detected — a shallow, well-mixed system.
- **Transparency (Secchi disc):** ranged 3.1 ft (April snowmelt turbidity) to typical 7.5 ft; max 9 ft.
- **Dissolved Oxygen:** Surface 7.1–12.0 ppm; mid-depth 5.0–10.4 ppm; bottom 2.1–9.8 ppm. Bottom-strata O₂ dropped below levels sustaining fish life only in **June and July** (hypolimnetic stress period).
- **Free CO₂:** 0–4.2 ppm (derived via pH/alkalinity stability diagram, per Moore 1939, due to technique error avoidance for direct titration).
- **pH:** Ranged 7.8 (Feb, winter minimum) to 10.0 (July, associated with dense phytoplankton bloom) — strong seasonal alkaline drift.
- **Total dissolved solids:** 280 ppm (relatively high, "desirable" productivity indicator)
- **Total hardness:** 100 ppm as CaCO₃
- **Total alkalinity:** ~119 ppm (stable), dipping to 90 ppm during heavy spring runoff (dilution effect)
- **Ion composition:** Ca 29 ppm, Mg 6.5 ppm, Na 15 ppm, Cl 4.5 ppm, F 0.35 ppm, SO₄ 15 ppm, NO₃ 1 ppm, NO₂ 0.06 ppm

### Trophic Structure
- **Bottom fauna:** Highly productive; dominated by **damselfly larvae**, followed by snails, chironomid larvae, and caddisfly larvae.
- **Plankton community:** Dominant zooplankton — *Daphnia* sp. (Cladocera) and *Diaptomus* sp./*Cyclops* sp. (Copepoda). Phytoplankton blooms (*Closterium*) drove seasonal water chemistry shifts.
- **Plankton seasonality:** Standing crop peaked in **late August** (delayed by late spring runoff), with a secondary fall plateau and winter low by late December — see [[Plankton Phenology]].

## Key Findings

- **[[Luna Lake]]** functions as a productive, shallow, well-mixed (non-stratifying) montane reservoir supporting a healthy **rainbow trout** fishery with excellent growth and condition factors compared to regional standards.
- **Brown trout**, present in sparse numbers in 1957, were **completely absent** from 1958 sampling — suggesting a failed or transient stocking/naturalization event, possibly linked to competitive exclusion or environmental factors.
- Dissolved oxygen depletion in the bottom strata during **June–July** represents the primary seasonal environmental stressor, though it did not appear to compromise overall trout population health, likely because trout could avoid the hypoxic bottom layer in this shallow, non-stratified lake.
- Water chemistry (high alkalinity, TDS, and hardness) combined with dense zooplankton/phytoplankton standing crops indicates a **eutrophic to mesotrophic, highly productive system**, generally favorable for supporting a stocked trout fishery.
- The lake has been subject to an intensive, near-continuous **stocking program (1955–1958)** — see Table III — including rainbow trout fry, fingerlings, and catchables, plus an experimental introduction of **Kokanee salmon** (82,000 stocked at 2" in April 1958), indicating active management supplementing natural reproduction.
- The extremely high percentage of shoal area (99.8%) and abundant rooted vegetation suggest strong potential spawning and nursery habitat, though the report notes stream survey work elsewhere in the District focused on assessing **planting suitability** rather than natural spawning success.
- Recommendation: continued **periodic resurveys** to build a longitudinal dataset beyond this single-year baseline.
