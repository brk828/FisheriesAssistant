# Reconnaissance Inventory and Prioritization of Existing and Potential Bottomlands in the Upper Colorado River Basin, 1993-1994

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared by David B. Irving and Bob D. Burdick for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for Endangered Fishes, inventories and prioritizes flooded bottomland habitats believed important for recovery of endangered fishes, especially [[Razorback Sucker]]. All potential bottomland habitats adjacent to 871 river miles of the Upper Colorado River Basin were inventoried in 1993, identifying 293 sites (135 in the [[Green River]] drainage, 158 in the [[Colorado River]] drainage, including the [[Gunnison River]]). Sites were scored against four selection criteria and ranked; staff gauges and cross sections were then installed in 1994 to estimate the flows needed to inundate the highest-ranked sites. Results are explicitly provisional because 1994 runoff was too low to verify stage-discharge relationships.

## Background & Description

- **Ecological rationale:** Upper basin floodplains are no longer regularly connected to rivers because of levees, dikes, rip-rap, and tamarisk (*Tamarisk* spp.) encroachment. Flooded bottomlands are thought to be nursery areas for razorback sucker, feeding areas for adults, and velocity shelters during spring runoff. Oxbows and flooded pastures near Grand Junction were historically primary razorback sucker spawning habitat, and off-channel areas were used by adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] at high flows.
- **Zooplankton link:** Turbid upper-basin rivers are poor zooplankton producers, whereas inundated bottomlands yield higher densities, suggesting lost floodplain habitat limits razorback sucker recruitment.
- **Recovery framework:** Habitat development is one of five principal elements of the [[Recovery Program]]. Floodplain restoration is described as necessary before razorback sucker reintroduction into the Upper Colorado and Gunnison rivers.
- **Objectives:** (1) identify bottomlands representing potential endangered fish habitat; (2) categorize and classify them by value for recovery; (3) rank them by perceived value. A secondary goal was preliminary hydrology screening at up to 20 top-ranked sites.
- **Methods:** Aerial 35-mm slide and color infrared photography from a Cessna 206 (about 5,000-6,000 ft above ground) at high, medium, and low flows in 1993; sites digitized or grid-overlaid (estimates within ±10%). Ten classification parameters were recorded per site. Ranking used a five-step procedure with four weighted criteria, sub-criteria point values, and a total score per site. Hydrology used temporary staff gauges, automated cameras, channel cross sections, and stage-discharge regressions tied to [[USGS Stream Gauges]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study reaches (river miles inventoried):** Green River 398 rm; Yampa River 50 rm; White River 104.5 rm; Colorado River 241 rm; Gunnison River 75 rm.

**Habitat types:** Of the Colorado River sites, 41 were floodplain terraces, 23 gravel-pit ponds, 22 side channels, 16 canyon mouths, 13 floodplain depressions, and one oxbow. Of the Gunnison River sites, 35 were terraces, six gravel-pit ponds, three oxbows, and two side channels. Green River habitat was dominated by terraces (99) with 30 depressions.

**Hydrologic alteration:** Runoff magnitude in May and June has declined about 45% in the Colorado and Gunnison rivers and 22% in the Green River, with reduced flood duration. Causes include transmountain diversions, reservoir storage (including [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]), and levees and dikes. Sites that flood more often now will likely continue to do so under future compact development, making them priority candidates.

**Demonstration sites (2 Green, 2 Colorado, 1 Gunnison in the original five):** [[Moab Slough]], 29-5/8 Road gravel-pit pond, [[Escalante State Wildlife Area]], [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] (Old Charlie Wash/Woods Bottom), and [[Escalante Ranch]]. Cooper and Severn (1994a-e) documented hydrology, water chemistry, vegetation and invertebrates at each. Selenium was noted as a concern in the Gunnison and at Escalante SWA, and Moab Slough may need seasonal flushing to reduce salinity.

**Land ownership:** Green River bottomlands near [[Browns Park National Wildlife Refuge]] are about 75% federal; Ouray NWR sites are fully federal; 71% of sites between Ouray and Pariette Draw are tribal. Colorado River sites are 51% private (about 6,355 acres); Gunnison River sites are 70% private (about 2,221 acres). Canyonlands National Park holds 83% of federal Colorado River bottomland.

## Key Findings

**Inventory totals**
- Green River drainage: 132 sites with a historical potential of 18,430 acres (7,459 ha); 7,720 acres (3,124 ha; 74 sites) inundated in May and 2,438 acres (987 ha; 28 sites) in September. Browns Park NWR reach: 1,371 acres (555 ha). Pariette Draw to Escalante Ranch: 6,093 acres (2,466 ha).
- Colorado River: 110 sites with 12,315 acres (4,984 ha) potential; 2,472 acres (1,000 ha; 101 sites) inundated in May-June and 1,135 acres (459 ha) in September. Largest single area: [[Moab Slough]] (~875 acres, 354 ha).
- Gunnison River: 48 sites, 3,227 acres (1,306 ha) potential; 828 acres (335 ha; 41 sites) inundated in May-June. Austin to Roubideau Creek (17 rm) held 1,739 acres.
- Yampa River: one site (22 acres). White River: two sites (634 acres potential).

**Priority ranking**
- Green River: the top 11 sites are in Pariette Draw to Escalante Ranch, led by Johnson Bottom and Leota Pond Complex (Ouray NWR), scoring 101. Browns Park sites ranked 12th-14th; the lowest-ranked sites are near Desert Spring to the confluence.
- Colorado and Gunnison: the top-ranked site is the [[Walter Walker State Wildlife Area]] (18-mile reach; score 87). Johnson Boy's Slough (Gunnison) ranked 2nd, Clifton Pond 3rd, and Debeque I-70 Slough 4th. Other highly ranked sites include Battlement Mesa, EXXON, Griffith's, and Humphrey's.

**Hydrology results (1994, provisional)**
- Green River, seven sites: Woods Bottom floods 95-100% of years (2,000-4,000 cfs); Sheppard Bottom 65-70% (7,000-8,000 cfs); Johnson Bottom Pond 2 40-50% (12,000-13,000 cfs); Baeser Bend 5-10% (19,000-20,000 cfs), reducible to 10,000-11,000 cfs by removing about five feet of bank.
- Colorado and Gunnison, eight estimated sites: Griffith's floods about 50% of years (9,000-10,000 cfs); Confluence Park 15-20% (8,000-9,000 cfs); Adobe Creek side channel 20-25% (19,000-20,000 cfs); Battlement Mesa 44-54% (10,500-12,000 cfs); 30-29 Road about 10% (16,000-19,000 cfs).
- Clifton Sanitation No. 1 would be reconnected more frequently (about once every two years) by lowering the dike about four feet.
- Stage-discharge correlation coefficients were high at most Green River sites (r² 0.83-0.97 at six of seven) but low at Meril Snow Ranch (r² 0.64) and Pike's/Adobe Creek (r² 0.29 at the lowest), so estimates are provisional.

**Recommendations**
1. Validate overbank flows by redeploying staff gauges at seven Green River sites, Adobe Creek, Pike's, Johnson Boy's Slough, and Confluence Park during 1995 runoff (snowpack forecasts were above normal: Green River 201%, Colorado 132%, Gunnison 161%).
2. Complete contaminant and geomorphological screening for the remaining 19 sites.
3. Field-check three canyon-bound depressions downstream of Moab (each under 25 acres), which are wet only through groundwater and may need levee excavation.
4. Obtain a complete set of current color aerial photographs (1:6,000-1:12,000) timed to May-June runoff.
5. Re-rank sites if new criteria emerge from site visits.

**Landowner engagement**
- Of 12 private Green River owners, only five were contacted in 1993; Little Stewart Lake is partly under a [[Wildlife Easement]] administered by Ouray NWR. Johnson Boy's Slough owners were cooperative, and the 30-29 Road site is largely state-owned and willing. Griffith's acquisition was also considered as mitigation under the [[Grand Valley Salinity Control Project]]. Several owners declined to sell or develop easements, so voluntary strategies and creative agreements will be critical.

**Appendices (summary)** cover the restoration program FAQ and right-of-entry form (compensation for damages, no owner liability for injured federal staff), inventory photo plans (complete infrared plan est. $28,700 for Green River), site data tables D.1-D.2, parameter tables E.1-E.16 (158 sites), scoring tables F.1-F.2, land-ownership data (G), and mean daily flow exceedances (I).
