# Rediscovery of Etheostoma histrio and Percina ouachitae in Green River, Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #freshwater-ecology #Etheostoma #Percina

## Executive Summary

This 1982 paper documents the rediscovery of two rare darter species — the **Harlequin Darter** (*Etheostoma histrio*) and the **Yellow Darter** (*Percina ouachitae*) — in the Green River drainage of Kentucky, 91 years after their only prior record (Woolman 1892) from the Rough River. Both species are characteristic of the Gulf Coastal Plain and are extremely rare north of the **Fall Line** in the Ohio River Valley, with the Wabash River system representing their previously known northernmost limit (where *P. ouachitae* is now considered extirpated). Surveys conducted by the Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission (Aug–Oct 1980) found *E. histrio* persisting at one Rough River locality, and both species co-occurring in the lower **Mud River** and a 32 km segment of the **Green River** main channel between U.S. Lock and Dams No. 4 and 5. This finding extends the known range of both darters approximately 42 km upstream from Woolman's original Rough River site. The populations are considered threatened by ongoing mining pollution in the drainage, and no change in conservation status was recommended despite the rediscovery.

## Background & Description

- **Etheostoma histrio** (Harlequin Darter):
  - Standard length (SL) range across all stations: **28–51 mm**
  - Rough River specimens: 29–48 mm SL
  - Mud River Station 1: 28–48 mm SL
  - Mud River Station 2: 36–51 mm SL
  - Green River Station A (Lock & Dam No. 4): 40–47 mm SL

- **Percina ouachitae** (Yellow Darter):
  - Standard length (SL) range across all stations: **31–54 mm**
  - Mud River Station 1: 31–53 mm SL
  - Mud River Station 2: 43–54 mm SL
  - Green River Station B (KY 185 crossing): 49 mm SL (single specimen)
  - Green River Station C (Lock & Dam No. 5): 53–54 mm SL

- Taxonomy follows [[Robins Et Al 1980]] nomenclature; *P. ouachitae* belongs to the darter subgenus **Imostoma**, described by [[Williams And Etnier 1977]] as a generalized species capable of adapting to varied stream conditions — an interpretation supported by this study's Green River observations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Preferences:**
- Both species were obtained in greatest numbers in **shallow, gravel riffles with slow-to-moderate current**, particularly in the [[Mud River]].
- *E. histrio* showed broad ecological plasticity, occurring across substrates ranging from soft mud/detritus in sluggish streams to boulder-strewn swift riffles, consistent with prior reports ([[Hubbs And Pigg 1972]], [[Tsai 1968]], [[Sisk And Webb 1976]]).
- *P. ouachitae* preferred **gravel substrates in flowing water**, was more abundant in the smaller Mud River than the Green River main channel, and was described as a habitat generalist within its subgenus.
- Neither species was collected in pools or areas lacking discernible flow.

**Species Associations:**
- Frequent direct associate of both target species: **[[Percina Phoxocephala]]**
- Other co-occurring percids: *P. caprodes*, *P. maculata*, *P. sciera*, *P. copelandi*, *P. evides*, *Etheostoma asprigene*, *E. blennioides*, *E. nigrum*, *E. squamiceps*, *E. bellum*, *E. zonale*, and *Noturus eleutherus*.
- Woolman's original 1892 Rough River collection also reported all three species (*E. histrio*, *P. ouachitae*, *P. phoxocephala*) together, and this tri-species association has been repeatedly observed in western Kentucky and Texas ([[Hubbs And Pigg 1972]]), reflecting shared habitat preference and adaptive plasticity.

**Biogeography:**
- Both species belong primarily to the **Gulf Coastal Plain** ichthyofauna; their restriction in the Green River drainage to areas downstream of a geological floodplain constriction (coinciding with the Mississippian–Pennsylvanian strata contact zone) suggests gradient and/or lowland habitat requirements limit further upstream dispersal.
- This same floodplain-morphology transition marks the downstream limit of upland darter species (*P. copelandi*, *P. evides*, *E. bellum*, *E. maculatum*), reinforcing a habitat-driven biogeographic boundary within the drainage.

## Key Findings

- **First record in 91 years**: Neither species had been documented in the Green River drainage since [[Woolman 1892]]'s collection at Hartford, Rough River — despite subsequent targeted surveys ([[Laflin 1980]], [[Retzer 1980]]) that failed to detect them, leading [[Thompson And Cashner 1980]] to presume *P. ouachitae* extirpated from the system.
- **Range extension**: New records extend the known distribution ~42 km upstream from Woolman's original site.
- **Regional significance**: The Green River drainage appears to hold the **only extant population of P. ouachitae** and **one of only two extant populations of E. histrio** in the Ohio River Valley above the Fall Line (the other *E. histrio* population being in the Embarras River, Illinois, itself rare/possibly extirpated).
- **Distribution within drainage**: *E. histrio* persists at one Rough River site; both species occur together in the lower Mud River and in a 32 km segment of the Green River mainstem between [[U.S. Lock And Dam No. 4]] and [[U.S. Lock And Dam No. 5]].
- **Anthropogenic influence**: The 1965 breach of Lock and Dam No. 4 lowered the pool by ~3 m, restoring riverine (lotic) shoal habitat that likely benefits these riffle-dependent darters.
- **Conservation threats**: Both species face significant threats from **mining pollution** in the lower Green River and tributaries (notably the severely polluted Pond River, which is locally devoid of fish). *E. histrio* is listed as **threatened** in Kentucky ([[Branson Et Al 1981]]); *P. ouachitae* status remains undetermined, though few additional populations are expected to persist in the drainage.
- **Biogeographic hypothesis**: Absence of both darters upstream of a floodplain/geological transition zone suggests habitat (gradient, substrate, lowland conditions) rather than dispersal barriers alone limits their range within the Green River system.
