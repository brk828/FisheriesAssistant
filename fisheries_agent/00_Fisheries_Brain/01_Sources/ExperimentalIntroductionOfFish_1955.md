# Experimental Introduction of Fish Species (Texas Segment Completion Report, 1955–1956)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Moronidae #Sciaenidae #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This Texas Game and Fish Commission segment completion report (Project F7R3, Job F-1) documents an experimental fisheries management program aimed at introducing non-native, primarily marine/estuarine, fish species into inland Texas reservoirs — particularly saline lakes of North Central Texas and fertile but species-poor lakes of the Texas Panhandle. The rationale was twofold: (1) establish salt-tolerant sport and predatory fishes in waters too saline for native [[Game Fish]] species, and (2) introduce predacious species to help control overabundant [[Rough Fish]] and forage fish populations. Three species were the focus of active stocking during this period: [[White Bass]] (*Morone chrysops*) in Buffalo Lake, and [[Red Drum]] (locally called "redfish") and [[Spotted Seatrout]] (called "speckled weakfish" or "speckled trout") in Lake Kemp and the saline Salt Creek watershed. Early results were promising, particularly for White Bass, though longer-term reproductive success remained uncertain by the report's end. The program generated substantial public and angler interest.

## Background & Description

- **White Bass**: Stocked individuals from Lake Diversion transplants showed rapid growth and were reported in "excellent condition" after establishment in Buffalo Lake; specific size data not detailed beyond noting "small" young-of-year fish captured by anglers in 1955 and larger individuals (presumed original stock) recovered in later gill-net surveys.
- **Red Drum ("Redfish")**: Initial broodstock releases in June 1955 weighed 1–3 lbs each; a subsequent release in August 1955 consisted of ~0.5 lb yearlings. Gulf-caught fingerlings transported in March 1956 (n=1,603) suffered substantial transit and post-release mortality (~150 died in transit, ~150 estimated dead shortly after release), leaving an estimated ~1,300 survivors. A probable authentic recapture recorded two fish at 20 inches in length, indicating a minimum growth of 12 inches over 18 months.
- **Spotted Seatrout ("Speckled Weakfish")**: First stocked in August 1955 at 7–12 inches in length; some individuals were noted to be in poor condition upon release. Subsequent stockings included yearlings and fingerlings of unspecified condition (March 1956, n=89 fingerlings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target habitats**: Highly saline inland lakes and impoundments of North Central Texas unsuitable for native freshwater game fish, and fertile but low-diversity Panhandle lakes with excessive rough fish populations.
- **Species salinity tolerance**: The core management premise relied on the tolerance of marine/estuarine [[Sciaenidae]] and [[Moronidae]] species to reduced salinity, enabling their establishment in brackish inland reservoirs such as [[Lake Kemp]] and the saline [[Salt Creek]] watershed.
- **Trophic role**: Introduced species were selected specifically for their predacious, game-fish qualities, intended to exert top-down control on undesirable rough and forage fish populations while simultaneously providing new recreational angling opportunities.
- **Source populations**: Redfish fingerlings were collected directly from Gulf of Mexico waters and transported inland; White Bass founder stock originated from Lake Diversion, itself a downstream/connected water body relative to Buffalo Lake.
- **Companion reservoir list** indicates a broader multi-species experimental stocking program across Texas reservoirs, including [[Red Drum]], [[Black Drum]], Black x Red Drum hybrids ("Brown Drum"), [[Orangemouth Corvina]], [[Spotted Seatrout]], Corvina x Seatrout hybrids, [[Atlantic Croaker]], [[Southern Flounder]], [[Pinfish]], [[Tarpon]], [[Peacock Bass]] (Cichlidae), and [[Nile Perch]] — situating this report within a much larger exotic/estuarine species introduction initiative.

## Key Findings

- **White Bass in Buffalo Lake**: A single small 1954 release of 28 White Bass resulted in confirmed natural reproduction, with fishermen catching small juvenile White Bass in fair numbers by August 1955. An additional 78 White Bass transplanted from Lake Diversion in July 1955 supplemented the population, rendering further introductions unnecessary. However, subsequent gill-net surveys over the following year captured only 16 White Bass (some clearly large survivors from initial stocking), suggesting reproduction had not met initial expectations despite seemingly ideal lake conditions. The authors considered it too early for a definitive conclusion on establishment success.
- **Redfish (Red Drum) in Lake Kemp**: Multiple stocking events (June 1955, August 1955, October 1955, March 1956) totaled 16 large non-breeding-age adults and approximately 1,300 surviving fingerlings. No confirmed direct observations of stocked redfish were made by project staff, though numerous (mostly unverified) angler reports circulated. One probable authentic recapture record documented substantial growth (12 inches in 18 months, reaching 20 inches total length), suggesting viable growth potential in the reservoir environment.
- **Speckled Weakfish (Spotted Seatrout) in Lake Kemp**: Stocked in three events (August 1955, October 1955, March 1956) totaling 34 large non-breeding adults and 98 fingerlings, some in questionable condition upon release. No specimens were directly recovered or verified by project staff during the report period.
- **Public/community impact**: The redfish and speckled weakfish introductions generated more angler and public interest and goodwill than any other job undertaken by the department to that point, despite the lack of confirmed catches.
- **Program constraints**: The report explicitly notes that success should not be measured by numbers of fish introduced, given the experimental and research-intensive nature of understanding distribution and natural history of these species in novel environments. Insufficient travel funding was cited as a significant impediment to the job's progress.
- **Related publication**: Findings were also disseminated publicly via "Saltwater Fish in Freshwater Lakes," *Texas Game and Fish Magazine*, April 1954.
- **Broader program context**: This job represents an early phase of a larger, multi-species [[Exotic Species Introduction]] strategy for Texas reservoirs targeting saline and brackish systems, foreshadowing later work with additional marine and even African/South American species (e.g., [[Nile Perch]], [[Peacock Bass]]) for sport and biological control purposes.
