# Ein neuer semiarmatus-Typ von Gasterosteus aculeatus L. aus dem Izniksee

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gasterosteidae #freshwater-relict #biogeography

## Executive Summary

This 1962 study by Joachim Münzing describes a previously unknown, morphologically homogeneous **semiarmatus** lateral-plate phenotype of the three-spined stickleback (**[[Gasterosteus aculeatus]]**) in the landlocked **[[Lake Iznik]]** (Izniksee), Northwestern Turkey. Based on a sample of 1,000 formalin-preserved adults, the population was found to consist almost exclusively (99%) of a low-plated, remarkably uniform semiarmatus type, in contrast to the highly variable semiarmatus phenotypes known from Northwest European coastal hybrid populations. The author argues that the Lake Iznik semiarmatus form is **genetically distinct** from — and not homologous with — the Northwest European semiarmatus type: whereas the latter arises from **trachurus × leiurus hybridization**, the Iznik form likely represents a **stabilized, homozygous genotype** produced by mutation-driven plate reduction under long-term freshwater isolation from an originally higher-plated (trachurus-like) marine ancestor. The paper further discusses the zoogeographic origin and colonization age of this relict marine element in the lake, proposing a most probable Black Sea-derived origin via the Bosporus, with immigration into Lake Iznik occurring within the last ~10,000–30,000 years.

## Background & Description

- **Geographic setting**: Lake Iznik is one of four large freshwater lakes (with Manyas, Abulyont, and Sapanca) on the Asian side of the Sea of Marmara, all originating from Pleistocene block-faulting (Pfannenstiel, 1944).
- **Current hydrology**: The lake sits **85 m above sea level** with **no surface outflow** to the Marmara; only a small stream, the **Karsak dere**, drains seepage water near Gemlik into the Marmara.
- **Ichthyofauna context**: The lake harbors a mix of:
  - Mediterranean species (*Blennius vulgaris*)
  - Central European species (*Silurus glanis*, *Cyprinus carpio*, *Vimba vimba*)
  - Sarmatic/Pontic relict species (*Proterorhinus marmoratus*, *Leuciscus borysthenicus*, *Pararutilus frisii*, *Chalcalburnus chalcoides*)
- **Focal question** (raised earlier by Kosswig, 1954): how and when did the clearly marine-derived **[[Gasterosteus aculeatus]]** colonize this landlocked lake?
- **Lateral plate morphs**: In *G. aculeatus*, three classical lateral bony plate morphs are recognized:
  - **trachurus** — fully plated
  - **semiarmatus** — intermediate plating
  - **leiurus** — plate-reduced (naked sides)
- Populations are normally either pure trachurus, pure leiurus, or **mixed populations** containing variable proportions of all three morphs. A **pure semiarmatus population**, as found in Lake Iznik, was previously unknown and considered rare.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water chemistry**: Lake Iznik was noted (Traut, 1960) to have **extreme calcium deficiency**, which the author tentatively links to the observed **unusually small otolith size** in the stickleback population compared to Northwest European freshwater populations.
- **Growth rate**: Lake Iznik sticklebacks show comparatively **fast growth** — reaching ~52 mm (males) / ~54 mm (females) average length after just one year, a size North German freshwater populations typically take two years to reach.
- **Sexual size dimorphism**: Present but appears **less pronounced** than in northern populations.
- **Isolation history**: The population is considered to have been isolated from marine gene flow for a very long period (potentially tens of thousands of years), a key factor invoked to explain both the loss of winter growth rings in otoliths and the stabilized plate phenotype.
- **Comparative model system**: The **[[Techirghiol Lake]]** (Romania, near the Black Sea), isolated from the Black Sea for only ~100 years, shows an analogous but earlier-stage process: a majority of individuals are already semiarmatus (though still high-plated, 14–22 plates) evolving from an originally pure trachurus (Black Sea) stock, without trachurus × leiurus hybridization — supporting a non-hybrid, mutation/selection-driven pathway to semiarmatus phenotypes in isolated waters.

## Key Findings

### 1. Standard Length, Age & Growth
- Sex ratio in sample: ♂♂ : ♀♀ = 409 : 591
- Two age groups identified via otolith analysis and length-frequency distributions:
  - Males: Age I = 45–55 mm; Age II = 56–61 mm (division at 55/56 mm)
  - Females: Age I = 46–57 mm; Age II = 58–65 mm (division at 57/58 mm)
- Otolith winter rings were largely **absent**, complicating age determination by otoliths alone.
- Otoliths noted as unusually **small**, possibly linked to low-calcium lake water.

### 2. Vertebral Counts
- Mean vertebral number: **males 31.02 ± 0.059**; **females 30.96 ± 0.058**
- No statistically significant sex difference.
- Values are **lower** than typical isolated northern (colder) freshwater populations.

### 3. Lateral Plate (Bony Armor) Counts
- **Pure semiarmatus population** — no leiurus individuals found among 1,000 fish examined.
- Total plate counts: mean **12.52 (♂)** / **12.26 (♀)** — much lower than Northwest European semiarmatus forms (~20–22 plates).
- Pectoral region: consistently ~7 plates (mean 6.95 ♂ / 6.82 ♀).
- Caudal region: consistently 5–6 plates (mean 5.56 ♂ / 5.38 ♀).
- No significant sex difference in plate number (total, pectoral, or caudal).
- **Remarkable morphological uniformity**: only **10 individuals (1%)** showed higher, irregular plate counts, often with left–right asymmetry — interpreted as residual traces of an ancestral higher-plated condition.
- No case of caudal plate reduction was observed even in the standard semiarmatus phenotype, suggesting the reduction process has reached an **end-stage/stable equilibrium**.

### 4. Genetic Interpretation
- Northwest European coastal **semiarmatus** = **hybrid product of trachurus × leiurus crossing** (per Münzing 1959, 1962), governed by a Mendelian allele pair (T/t) plus modifier genes, producing high phenotypic variability and asymmetry.
- Lake Iznik **semiarmatus** is proposed to represent a **distinct, non-homologous phenomenon**: a **stabilized, likely homozygous genotype** arising via **mutational plate reduction** from an originally higher-plated (trachurus-like) founder population under freshwater isolation — **not** from trachurus × leiurus hybridization.
- The two "semiarmatus" phenotypes (Northwest Europe vs. Lake Iznik) are argued to be **genetically incomparable** despite superficial phenotypic similarity.

### 5. Zoogeographic Origin & Colonization Age
Three competing hypotheses for how *G. aculeatus* reached Lake Iznik were evaluated:

1. **Mediterranean glacial marine lineage** — branching from Würm-glacial Mediterranean marine populations, entering via a former river connection to the Marmara shortly after the Dardanelles Strait reopened (~50,000 years ago). Considered **less likely**, since such antiquity would predict a more advanced (leiurus-like) plate reduction than observed.
2. **Black Sea lineage via Bosporus/ancient Sakarya-Bosporus** (~20,000–30,000 years ago, late Würm II) — considered the **most probable** scenario, consistent with the modern Black Sea being populated exclusively by the trachurus form.
3. **Postglacial Baltic-derived Black Sea lineage**, entering the Black Sea in early postglacial times and subsequently colonizing Lake Iznik — implying a **maximum population age of ~10,000 years**.

- The author concludes the Lake Iznik population likely does **not** exceed ~10,000–30,000 years in age, far younger than the theoretical minimum of 125,000 years implied by Pfannenstiel's (1944) geological model of Lake Iznik as a secondarily isolated former marine embayment — a discrepancy used as evidence against very deep antiquity of the colonization event.
- No **leiurus** individuals were found, arguing against an origin from perimediterranean freshwater leiurus stocks (which are morphologically distinct and considered products of much older, fully completed plate-reduction processes).
