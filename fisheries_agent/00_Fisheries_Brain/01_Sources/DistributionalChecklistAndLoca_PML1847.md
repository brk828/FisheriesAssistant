# Checklist of the Freshwater and Euryhaline Fishes of Mexico, with Conservation Status

#ichthyology #marine-biology #mexican-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a regional checklist of the fishes of Mexico's freshwater, brackish and coastal waters. It was abstracted and paraphrased by [[William L. Minckley]] from the manuscript *Guide to the Freshwater Fishes of Mexico* by [[Robert Rush Miller]] (September 1993). The checklist gives each species' scientific and Spanish common names, its geographic distribution by river basin and slope, and, for some taxa, its introduction or extirpation status. It is paired with an official Mexican list of rare, threatened, endangered and specially protected species (SEDUE, *Gaceta Ecologica* 3(15): 227, 1991). The OCR-derived text has been cleaned, and the entries are summarised here rather than reproduced in full.

## Background & Description

The checklist covers the fauna of Mexico and adjacent Central American drainages. Its taxa range from lampreys and sharks to killifishes, pupfishes and goodeids. Entries are organised by order and family and follow a consistent pattern:

- Scientific name, author and year
- Spanish common name (e.g., *charalito*, *matalote*, *sardinita*, *mojarra*)
- English common name
- Distribution, given by drainage basin, slope and country

Representative groups include:

- **Marine and estuarine taxa:** [[Carcharhinus Leucas]] (Bull Shark), two [[Pristis]] sawfish species, tarpon ([[Megalops Atlanticus]]), ladyfish, anchovies, menhadens and herrings, which enter lagoons and occasionally rivers.
- **Diadromous and transitional taxa:** the American eel (*Anguilla rostrata*), which enters rivers to mature and returns to sea to spawn.
- **Freshwater Cyprinidae:** the largest group, including the chubs (*Algansea*, *Gila*), shiners (*Notropis*), minnows (*Dionda*), stonerollers (*Campostoma*) and suckers (*Catostomus*).
- **Killifishes and pupfishes:** Cyprinodontidae (*Cyprinodon*, *Cualac*, *Floridichthys*) and Fundulidae (*Fundulus*, *Lucania*), with many endemics in the Yucatán Peninsula and Cuatro Ciénegas.
- **Goodeidae:** the livebearing splitfins, skiffias and related genera of the central Mexican highlands.
- **Catfishes:** Ictaluridae (e.g., [[Ictalurus Furcatus]], [[Ictalurus Punctatus]]) and Pimelodidae, including several blind and cave forms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fauna follows the distribution of Mexico's major drainages and the boundaries between the Atlantic (Gulf) and Pacific slopes. Several interior basins are endorheic or closed, including the Valley of Mexico, the Nazas-Aguanaval system and Cuatro Ciénegas. Many species are restricted to a single spring, lake or river, which makes them vulnerable to habitat loss.

Several entries describe specialised habitats:

- **Caves and subterranean waters:** the blind brotula (*Ogilbia pearsei*) in Yucatán cenotes, the blind Muzquiz catfish (*Prietella phreatophila*) in groundwater, and blind catfishes of the Papaloapan basin.
- **Cenotes and coastal lagoons:** the Yucatán pupfishes and several halfbeaks and needlefishes.
- **Desert springs:** the Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and the Cuatro Ciénegas endemics.

The document records many taxa as extinct, extirpated or threatened. Examples include:

- *Gila elegans* (Bonytail), extirpated from the Colorado River.
- *Ptychocheilus lucius* (Colorado Squawfish), extirpated from Mexico.
- *Xyrauchen texanus* (Razorback Sucker), extirpated from Mexico.
- *Stypodon signifer*, described as extinct in the Parras Valley.
- *Cyprinodon laciniatus*, the Parras pupfish, described as extinct.
- Several species of *Evarra* in the Valley of Mexico, described as evidently extinct.

## Key Findings

1. **Scale of the checklist:** The Miller manuscript covers the freshwater and euryhaline fishes of Mexico across roughly 35 families. Each family has multiple species, and the distributions often span international borders.

2. **High endemism:** Many taxa have ranges limited to a single basin, spring or lake. Examples include the Lerma chub, the Patzcuaro chub, the Chapala chub and the Chihuahua chub.

3. **Introductions:** Some species are recorded as introduced outside their native range. Examples include the Rainbow Trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), which is widely introduced, and several catfishes and cichlids.

4. **Taxonomic uncertainty:** The author notes that several groups may need revision. The *Dionda episcopa* complex is likely a species complex. The *Catostomus plebeius* populations in the Fuerte, Mezquital and Presidio basins are likely an undescribed taxon. The taxonomic status of trouts from the Yaqui, Mayo and Casas Grandes rivers had not been established.

5. **Official conservation listings (1991):** The companion list from SEDUE (Gaceta Ecológica, 1991) sorts species into four categories:
   - **Rara** (Rare): includes several chubs, shiners, pupfishes and a catfish.
   - **Amenazada** (Threatened): a long list of Mexican shiners, minnows, killifishes, goodeids and catfishes.
   - **En Peligro de Extinción** (Endangered): includes pupfishes and killifishes from Cuatro Ciénegas, Yucatán and Potosí, as well as the Mexican Golden Trout (*Oncorhynchus chrysogaster*).
   - **Sujeta a Protección Especial** (Subject to Special Protection): includes the Totoaba (*Totoaba macdonaldi*) and several other species.

6. **Transboundary species:** Many species are shared with the United States (Texas, New Mexico, Arizona), Guatemala, Belize, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama. Conservation of these taxa requires binational coordination.

7. **Fundamental role of the checklist:** The document provides a baseline for the systematic study and management of Mexican freshwater fishes. Its distribution data and conservation categories are the reference points for future surveys, taxonomic revisions and protection decisions.
