# Egg Components and Hatchling Lipid Reserves in Kinosternid Turtles

#herpetology #kinosternidae #chelonian-biology #reproductive-biology

## Executive Summary

This study compares egg and hatchling lipid provisioning in four [[Kinosternidae|kinosternid]] turtles from the southeastern United States: [[Kinosternon Baurii]], [[Kinosternon Subrubrum]], [[Sternotherus Minor]], and [[Sternotherus Odoratus]]. The authors tested whether species with [[Delayed Nest Emergence]] allocate more energy-storage lipid to eggs and hatchlings than species with [[Immediate Emergence]]. The hypothesis was not supported. Egg non-polar lipid (NPL) proportions differed among species, but hatchling NPL proportions did not. Lipid class composition was similar across species and dominated by [[Triacylglycerol]]. Incubation length appeared to drive differences in energy use during [[Embryogenesis]]. Hatchlings carry enough stored lipid to cover basal maintenance for long periods, which the authors suggest is important for survival under negative energy balance regardless of emergence strategy.

## Background & Description

Small-bodied turtles provision offspring through [[Parental Investment]] in eggs rather than post-ovulatory care. Non-polar lipids allocated to eggs in excess of what is needed to build an embryo become hatchling reserves. Because an egg is treated as a closed energy system, faster embryonic development or a larger embryo leaves less lipid for the hatchling. The paper addresses this trade-off across four closely related species, which reduces the chance that differences reflect phylogeny rather than ecology.

Post-hatching behaviour differs among the study species:

- **K. subrubrum** shows delayed emergence and overwinters in the nest.
- **S. odoratus** generally emerges immediately.
- **K. baurii** emerges immediately, but eggs laid in autumn can enter [[Diapause]] for several months. Its energetics were judged most similar to immediate-emergence species.
- **S. minor** had no documented post-hatching strategy.

Females were collected from South Carolina (K. subrubrum, n = 5; S. odoratus, n = 6), Florida (S. minor, n = 10; K. baurii clutches from natural nests), Alabama (S. minor, n = 1), and Tennessee (S. minor, n = 1). Most turtles were X-rayed to confirm shelled eggs and induced to oviposit with oxytocin. Eggs were incubated at 29 °C on moist vermiculite. Hatchlings were frozen within 24 h of pipping.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occupy shallow, vegetated freshwater habitats. Hatchling energy reserves are presented as a buffer against periods of negative energy balance. The authors argue that immediate-emerging hatchlings may face unpredictable, dwindling resources, so they may need large reserves as well. Long incubation periods are linked to the production of precocial hatchlings. Developmental time and emergence timing, including exposure to fall versus spring conditions, are identified as important factors for interpreting [[Parental Investment]] strategies. The paper also notes that the choice of nest-emergence strategy alone does not explain these patterns.

## Key Findings

**Egg characteristics**
- Egg wet mass means ranged from 3.46 g (K. subrubrum) to 5.51 g (S. minor). K. baurii eggs were significantly larger in length, width, and mass than K. subrubrum and S. odoratus.
- S. minor eggs were significantly wider and heavier than those of K. subrubrum and S. odoratus.
- K. baurii and S. minor eggs were similar to Florida populations reported elsewhere. K. subrubrum and S. odoratus eggs were consistent with South Carolina reports.

**Egg NPL allocation**
- Egg NPL proportions by dry mass were K. baurii 34.1%, K. subrubrum 32.9%, S. minor 30.9%, and S. odoratus 28.2% (P = 0.003).
- Egg NPL was significantly higher in K. baurii and K. subrubrum than in S. odoratus.

**Hatchling traits**
- Hatchling wet mass ranged from 3.33 g (K. subrubrum) to 5.89 g (S. minor). S. minor hatchlings were significantly heavier than S. odoratus and K. subrubrum hatchlings.
- Incubation time differed significantly among species (P < 0.0001): K. subrubrum 110 days, S. minor 81 days, S. odoratus 68 days.

**Hatchling NPL and parental investment in care**
- Hatchling NPL proportions were S. minor 18.6%, S. odoratus 17.5%, and K. subrubrum 15.8%. These differences were not significant (F = 1.38, P = 0.30).
- Within each species, egg and hatchling NPL differed significantly (P < 0.0001).
- Parental investment in care (hatchling NPL divided by egg NPL) was 40% for K. subrubrum, 50% for S. minor, and 55% for S. odoratus.
- The authors note that most turtle studies report PIC of 50–60%, and above 70% in alligators.

**Lipid class composition**
- Lipid class proportions were similar among species for both eggs and hatchlings.
- Triacylglycerol was the dominant class in eggs (83.5–86.4%) and was reduced in hatchlings (reported as 58–65% in the discussion).
- During development, triacylglycerol was depleted, [[Cholesteryl Esters]] rose by about one order of magnitude, and [[Phospholipids]] shifted from mainly phosphatidylcholine in eggs to mainly phosphatidylethanolamine in hatchlings.
- Phospholipid amounts did not decline in absolute terms within species, which contrasts with a previous alligator study.
- The authors attribute the rise in cholesteryl esters to active transport and assimilation of [[Yolk Sac]] lipids, since the yolk sac is often internalized at hatching in kinosternids.

**Interpretation and conclusions**
- The results contradict the expectation that delayed-emergence hatchlings carry more energy-storage lipid.
- Because K. subrubrum hatches about 40 days after S. odoratus, its hatchlings may hold more NPL than S. odoratus hatchlings in similar winter conditions.
- Long development in K. subrubrum may explain why much of its egg NPL goes to embryogenesis.
- Hatchling K. subrubrum and S. odoratus carry enough lipid to sustain a [[Standard Metabolic Rate]] of 0.06 ml O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹ at 20 °C for more than 180 days (unpublished data).
- Future work should include experimental manipulation of hatchling lipid reserves, the study of embryonic growth dynamics, and field data on resource availability and predation.

**Methods note**
- Lipids were quantified with a modified Folch extraction, [[Thin-Layer Chromatography]] and flame-ionization detection on a [[Iatroscan TLC-FID]] system.
- Analyses used 31 egg clutches and 24 hatchlings from 12 clutches (three species).
- Group comparisons used Tukey's studentized range test, and paired egg–hatchling comparisons used t-tests, with significance set at α < 0.05.
