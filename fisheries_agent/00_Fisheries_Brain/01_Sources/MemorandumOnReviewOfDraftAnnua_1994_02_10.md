## Executive Summary

This document is a critical **peer-review memorandum**, not a primary data report. Rich Valdez (BIO/WEST, Inc.) evaluates the Arizona Game and Fish Department's 1993 Draft Annual Report for the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES), Phase II. The review addresses five chapters: Executive Summary, Ecosystem Processes/Lower Trophic Levels, [[Rainbow Trout]] studies, [[Mainstem Fish Studies]], and Native Fishes of the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR). The reviewer identifies major deficiencies in data analysis, statistical reporting, methodological clarity, and timeliness, citing a persistent backlog in laboratory processing (nutrients, water quality, *Gammarus*) and data entry that undermines the report's scientific utility. Chapter 5 (LCR native fishes) is deemed best executed; Chapter 4 (Mainstem Studies) is judged weakest, lacking usable results on fish abundance, movement, habitat use, growth, and food habits needed for BIO/WEST's Final Report integration.

## Background & Description

Little direct morphological data is presented in this memorandum (it is a report critique), but the following organism-level parameters are referenced as data points under review:

- **[[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)**: Population estimates reported for individuals **>150 mm total length (TL)**; length/age/growth relationships (Figure 5.5) noted as valuable; small juvenile size classes ("very small juvenile chubs") flagged for standardized life-stage terminology (larvae vs. young-of-year vs. age-0).
- **[[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)**: Length-frequency analysis via **MULTIFAN** (maximum likelihood cohort/growth model) used to distinguish cohorts and estimate age/growth; growth rate noted to slow with cold-water dam releases (Appendix 2.1).
- **[[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*)**: Composed **9.2%–48.5%** of catch in certain samples — reviewer disputes characterization as "ubiquitous," recommending "common" instead.
- **[[Gammarus]] (amphipod)**: Density data collected relative to flow, location, and season, though smallest size-class recruitment could not be inferred due to incomplete data (Figure 1.3).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types sampled**: mainstem Colorado River backwaters ("backwater proper" vs. "eddy return channel"), Lee's Ferry reach, six unidentified springs in Glen Canyon, and the Little Colorado River (LCR) tributary.
- **Flow relationships**: Spawning habitat of rainbow trout examined against fluctuating dam discharge via regression; stranding of trout (life stage unspecified — eggs/fry/juveniles/adults) linked to flow fluctuations since at least 1984.
- **Backwater dynamics**: Discussed as critical nursery/rearing habitat for native fishes; datasonde temperature/water-quality monitoring in backwaters flagged as vital to evaluating the proposed **steady summer flow regime** under the Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement (GCDEIS).
- **Species interactions**: Potential relationship between [[Humpback Chub]] CPUE and turbidity/temperature gradients in mainstem and LCR habitats; possible displacement of juvenile native fishes downstream from rearing areas.
- **Trophic considerations**: Periphyton biomass and *Gammarus* (secondary production) sampled as prey/food-web base; grazing invertebrates in periphyton samples suspected of confounding biomass measurements.
- **Thermal tolerance**: A companion manuscript on **temperature tolerance of humpback chub** is noted as high-quality and directly relevant to understanding survival of young humpback chub in the thermally-altered mainstem below [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

## Key Findings

- **Systemic reporting deficiencies**: Nearly every chapter references unanalyzed data due to lack of a contract laboratory (nutrient analyses — TKN, Total P, TOC; water quality; *Gammarus* identification) and unresolved data-entry backlog, echoing similar problems in the 1992 AGF Annual Report.
- **Mainstem Fish Studies (Ch. 4)** judged weakest: insufficient data on fish abundance, movement, habitat use, growth, length-frequency, survival, and food habits; ambiguous methodology regarding electrofishing equipment (Coffelt VVP vs. CPS Mark XX pulsators), CPUE computation (arithmetic/geometric/pooled mean unspecified), and habitat classification (backwater vs. eddy return channel).
- **Native Fishes – LCR (Ch. 5)** judged most complete: contains a population estimate for humpback chub >150 mm TL; documents PIT-tag exchange between LCR and mainstem; valuable length/age/growth data (Fig. 5.5) for mainstem-captured fish.
- **Statistical concerns**: Ambiguity over log(x+1) transformations (used only for ANOVA vs. applied to reported means); an R² of 0.15 reported alongside a significant p-value (0.0123) needing explanation; unclear whether turbidity/temperature "relationships" were tested via regression or comparative statistics.
- **Terminology/precision issues**: Recommends replacing "behavioral responses" with quantifiable field metrics (habitat use, diet, movement, growth, survival); "habitat preference" should be reframed as "selection," "utilization," or "suitability" given limited habitat availability in Grand Canyon; life-stage terms (larvae, young-of-year, age-0) require standardization.
- **Methodological modernization urged**: Datasonde data should be routinely downloaded to ASCII/SASS files for analysis; MULTIFAN inputs (empirical vs. assumed parameters) need transparent documentation; length-frequency data should be disaggregated by month and habitat rather than pooled, and reported as percentages with sample sizes (n).
- **Programmatic critique**: The reviewer questions the value of publishing a "series of manuscripts" for journal review in lieu of a comprehensive annual report, arguing this delays timely dissemination of findings to collaborators such as [[BIO/WEST]] for integration into the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Final Report.
- **Positive notes**: Chapter 5 (LCR native fishes) and the temperature-tolerance manuscript on humpback chub are highlighted as scientifically sound and useful contributions.
