# Summary of Manual Spawning of Razorback Suckers at Lake Mohave, Arizona, 1993

## Executive Summary
This 1993 field report, submitted by Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, documents a captive-assisted spawning program for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) at [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada. Conducted under an interagency [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG), the project aimed to preserve genetic diversity from an estimated 60,000 adult razorback suckers in the lake by capturing wild adults, manually spawning them (via natural ripening or hormonal induction), and incubating fertilized eggs in isolated backwater habitats. The program targeted 200,000 eggs (50 mL/~10,000 eggs from 20+ females) and achieved this goal via three netting events in February–March 1993, using a combination of spontaneous and hormone-induced spawning.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Xyrauchen texanus*, an endangered catostomid (sucker) endemic to the Colorado River Basin.
- **Adults measured**: Total Length (TL, nearest mm) and weight (nearest 10 g) recorded for all captured fish.
- **Sexual sampling**: Both sexes captured via trammel nets; males consistently outnumbered females in two of three netting events (e.g., 107 males vs. 56 females on 13–15 March).
- **Egg characteristics**: Fertilized ova became adhesive within 30–60 seconds post-fertilization, necessitating a bentonite slurry treatment to prevent clumping during incubation transfer.
- **Tagging**: All untagged adults received PIT tags prior to release, enabling future identification and tracking within the [[Lake Mohave]] population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Adult razorback suckers were captured primarily along shorelines of Arizona Bay (Yuma to Jeff Davis coves) and a shallow rocky reef at river mile (RM) 30; also inhabits deeper lake environments outside spawning season.
- **Spawning substrate**: Incubation sites were selected in shallow (0.5–1 m depth) shoreline zones with substrates ranging from coarse sand and sand-gravel mixtures to coarse gravel/cobble — consistent with natural [[Catostomid Spawning Habitat]] preferences requiring interstitial spaces for egg incubation.
- **Backwater incubation sites**: 
  - **Yuma Cove (Arizona Bay)** — primary incubation site for eggs meeting the 50 mL target.
  - **"NV-E"** — a naturally isolated backwater at a Finger Cove on the Nevada side (~RM 13), used for "partial" and "excess" egg samples.
- **Larval monitoring**: Follow-up included periodic visual inspections and crepuscular/nighttime spotlight searches for larvae, captured via fine-mesh dip nets, indicating nocturnal or low-light larval activity patterns post-hatch.
- **Population context**: Endemic to the regulated Colorado River system; this program reflects broader conservation concern for the species' decline linked to habitat alteration, reservoir dynamics, and predation pressure — a common threat profile for [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]].

## Key Findings
- **Total adults captured across three netting events**: 282 individuals (61 on 16 Feb; 58 on 3–5 March; 163 on 13–15 March).
- **16 February 1993**: 61 adults (31 males, 30 females) captured; no female produced a full 50 mL egg sample. Only small "partial" samples obtained from 5 fish (mostly one individual); no lake stocking occurred — eggs transported to ASU instead.
- **3–5 March 1993**: 58 adults (33 males, 25 females) captured. Thirteen females yielded ≥50 mL egg samples: 5 spontaneously, 9 via [[Hormonal Induction]] (HCG injection), with one female contributing eggs via both methods. Embryos stocked into Yuma Cove; excess sent to NV-E.
- **13–15 March 1993**: 163 adults (107 males, 56 females) captured. Seven females produced ≥50 mL spontaneously (no induction needed). Fertilized eggs stocked into Yuma Cove; partials/excess to NV-E.
- **Hormonal induction protocol**: Females injected intramuscularly with 300 IU HCG/kg body weight; held in live cages 12–24 hr; re-injected once if unproductive after 24 hr (max 2 injections, max 48 hr holding). No induction was required for males to obtain viable sperm.
- **Fertilization protocol**: Eggs manually stripped (50 mL ≈ 10,000 eggs per female target), fertilized with sperm from 2–3 males per batch, treated with bentonite slurry to prevent adhesion/clumping, and water-hardened ≥45 minutes before stocking.
- **Genetic sampling**: A 5 mL egg subsample from each female was reserved for mitochondrial DNA analysis (Dr. T.E. Dowling, ASU) to assess maternal genetic contributions to the resulting cohort — supporting downstream [[Genetic Diversity Monitoring]] via progeny sampling.
- **Overall 1993 season total**: 50 mL (~10,000 egg) samples successfully obtained from **20 females**; ~200,000 developing embryos stocked into Yuma Cove backwater. Of these, 12 females spawned spontaneously and 9 following hormone induction (one female contributed via both routes) — meeting the program's original numeric goal despite the failed first attempt in February.
- **Data availability**: Raw individual fish data (lengths, weights, PIT tag numbers) noted as available upon request, supporting future longitudinal or genetic studies on this *Xyrauchen texanus* population.
