# Cenozoic Geology in the Mammoth Area, Pinal County, Arizona

#geology #stratigraphy #cenozoic-geology

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Geological Survey bulletin by L. A. Heindl revises the Cenozoic history of the lower [[San Pedro River]] valley near Mammoth, Arizona. Mapping of discrete alluvial units led the author to replace the earlier single-formation view of the [[Gila Group]] with a sequence of named units. The document does not address fish, invertebrates, plankton, or water chemistry. Its aquatic relevance is indirect: it describes the valley-fill deposits and the flood plain of the San Pedro River, which were part of a reconnaissance of ground-water resources in the lower valley.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** About 35 miles northeast of Tucson, including the town of Mammoth and the San Manuel copper mine near Red Hill. It spans the full width of the lower San Pedro Valley between the [[Galiuro Mountains]] and the north slopes of the [[Santa Catalina Mountains]].
- **Setting:** The area lies in the [[Basin and Range Province]]. Consolidated rocks form small inliers within a basin largely covered by terrestrial deposits, the largest being the [[Black Hills]].
- **Purpose:** To show how mapping of separate valley-fill units can broaden understanding of Cenozoic history in Arizona intermontane basins.
- **Context:** The mapping was part of a U.S. Geological Survey reconnaissance of ground-water resources in the lower San Pedro Valley, prepared in cooperation with the Arizona State Land Department. The San Manuel Copper Co. allowed access to its properties.
- **Bedrock:** Early Precambrian [[Oracle Granite]] (quartz monzonite) is the principal pre-Cretaceous rock. It is intruded by aplite, diabase, andesite porphyry, and fine-grained quartz monzonite porphyry, which is linked to the [[San Manuel Copper Deposit]].

### Stratigraphic Units

The area contains seven alluvial and two volcanic units, all predominantly Cenozoic and possibly Late Cretaceous at the base west of Mammoth.

| Unit | Summary |
|---|---|
| [[Galiuro Volcanic Rocks]] (middle(?) Tertiary) | About 1,800 ft of flows, agglomerates, tuffs, and welded tuffs, basalt to rhyolite; form the Galiuro Mountains |
| [[Cloudburst Formation]] (Late Cretaceous(?) to early–middle(?) Tertiary) | Volcanic unit and fanglomerate unit; fanglomerate estimated at about 5,000 ft; volcanic unit may exceed 3,000 ft |
| [[San Manuel Formation]] (middle(?) Tertiary) | Estimated about 5,000 ft thick; local conglomerate, Kannally, and Tucson Wash members |
| [[Quiburis Formation]] (Pliocene to early Pleistocene) | Lake beds in the valley center; about 1,000–1,700 ft total in the central valley |
| [[Sacaton Formation]] (Pleistocene) | Up to about 250 ft thick; fills a valley cut into the Quiburis |
| Pleistocene and Recent alluvium | Flood-plain, channel-fill, and terrace deposits of the San Pedro River |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes no biological communities, species, or water-quality data. Its environmental content concerns the physical setting:

- **Flood plain:** The present San Pedro River flood plain stands less than 5 to about 10 ft above the channel floor. It is composed of sand and gravel, sandy silt, and sandy mudstone. Well logs show sand and gravel to depths of 60 to 80 ft.
- **Terraces:** Poorly developed terraces lie 10 to 30 ft above adjoining channels.
- **Gypsum and lake beds:** Fine-grained [[Quiburis Formation]] deposits in the valley center contain gypsum and were laid down in [[Lake Quiburis]], an ancient Pliocene lake.
- **Ground water:** The report notes the deposits' relevance to ground-water resources, but gives no hydrologic measurements.
- **Erosional benches:** The [[Tombstone Pediment]] (higher) and [[Whetstone Pediment]] (lower) mark Pleistocene erosion stages.
- **Fossils:** Middle Pliocene fossils occur south of Mammoth at about 2,500 ft and near Redington at about 3,200 ft. These were used for age control.

## Key Findings

1. **Revised Gila Group:** The Gila conglomerate is replaced locally by the [[Gila Group]], which includes the San Manuel, Quiburis, and Sacaton formations. The [[Cloudburst Formation]] is excluded from it.
2. **Age range:** The Gila group is assigned a Tertiary and Quaternary age, from middle(?) Tertiary to early Pleistocene. This is broader than the Pliocene and Pleistocene span previously assumed.
3. **Cloudburst age:** Dated only indirectly. The [[Cloudburst Formation]] is younger than a quartz monzonite porphyry dated at about 120 million years (preliminary zircon alpha-recoil dating). It is judged early(?) to middle(?) Tertiary west of Mammoth.
4. **Three erosion intervals:** Cretaceous(?) to Late Cretaceous(?) or early Tertiary; middle(?) Tertiary; and the late Quaternary degradation.
5. **Middle(?) Tertiary tableland:** Volcanic rocks once formed a tableland extending from about the Black Hills eastward. The Galiuro Mountains are its erosional remnant. The San Pedro Valley did not yet exist as a major feature.
6. **San Manuel fault as thrust:** Sedimentary relationships are judged incompatible with a tilted normal fault. They are compatible with thrusting, a view supported by E. D. Wilson (1957) using secondary fault-plane attitudes. The [[San Manuel Fault]] displacement would otherwise exceed 10,000 ft.
7. **Basin-and-range faulting:** Block faulting of the classic type did not occur extensively until after the last major movement on the [[San Manuel Fault]]. It likely began around the start of the Pliocene, not earlier.
8. **Two aggradation cycles:** The central trough deposits are not continuous. The [[Quiburis Formation]] and [[Sacaton Formation]] represent two aggradation phases separated by erosion and entrenchment, and the [[Kannally Member]] and [[Tucson Wash Member]] show a similar break.
9. **Revised degradation model:** Degradation was interrupted by periods of considerable aggradation. Unconformities lie about 30 to 50 ft above and 60 to 80 ft below the San Pedro River.
10. **Late history:** The San Pedro River cut a channel about 60 to 80 ft below its present level. The present channel began cutting into the flood-plain deposits in the late 1880s.
11. **Fault systems:** The [[Cholla Fault]] and related faults are normal faults that partly bound a horst formed by the Black Hills. The [[Copper Creek Fault]] has a maximum exposed throw of about 700 ft.
12. **Summary of deformation:** Post-Cloudburst deformation, uplift of the Galiuro and Santa Catalina ranges, and downdropping of the San Pedro trough occurred in the early part of late Tertiary time.
