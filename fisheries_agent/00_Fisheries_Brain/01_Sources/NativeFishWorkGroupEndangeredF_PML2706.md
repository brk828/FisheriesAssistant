# Native Fish Work Group Correspondence: Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub Recovery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of administrative correspondence about the recovery of endangered fishes in the [[Lower Colorado River]]. It has three parts: a September 1996 National Park Service letter announcing a [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) meeting, a mailing list of about 33 recipients, and a 27 November 1995 Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memo from [[Owen Gorman]] to George Devine. The memo is the substantive core. It argues that the NFWG operates without mandate or accountability, that the FWS has largely ceded its recovery role to the group, and that the [[Razorback Sucker]] population in [[Lake Mohave]] faces imminent collapse. It also argues that the [[Bonytail Chub]] is effectively extinct in the wild and that cryopreserved sperm should be used to protect genetic diversity.

## Background & Description

**Meeting notice (NPS letter, September 1996).** The [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]] office announced an NFWG meeting for Tuesday and Wednesday, 3 to 4 December 1996, from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. each day. The letter also noted that the Glen Canyon Fishery Management Plan (FMP) and a Memorandum of Agreement on [[Captive-Reared Endangered Fish]] stocking into [[Lake Powell]] were to be completed in 1996. It listed the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] office as the contact for agenda items and reservations. The position of Resource Management Secretary was vacant after the resignation of Susan Dodson in May, and recruitment awaited budget approval.

**Mailing list.** The list contains about 33 individuals from the FWS, [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BOR), National Park Service, state wildlife agencies (Arizona, Nevada, Utah, New Mexico, Colorado), universities including [[Arizona State University]], and the National Biological Service. Recipients include field biologists and resource managers. Among them are [[Paul Marsh]], W.L. Minckley, and [[Owen Gorman]].

**Memo (27 November 1995).** The memo follows up on the 22 November 1995 NFWG meeting at Cottonwood Cove, Nevada. Its stated subject is the Service's role in recovering razorback sucker and bonytail chub. The author notes that the NFWG is an ad hoc committee with no officers, no meeting minutes, no mandate, and no funds. Its work agreements and recommendations are non-binding.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Razorback sucker** is adapted to spawn, hatch, and grow in flowing river environments. In this case, it is being propagated in a lentic setting (Lake Mohave).
- **Population decline.** The memo states that the population of adult razorback suckers in Lake Mohave was estimated at about 60,000 adults in the 1980s. The best NFWG estimate for 1993 was fewer than 25,000 adults. The 1940s cohort disappeared in the late 1980s, and the 1950s cohort was expected to disappear by the start of the new century.
- **Bonytail chub** in Lake Mohave is so reduced that spawning is sporadic and population estimates cannot be made. The memo describes the species as "essentially extinct in the wild."
- **Gene pool concerns.** The memo states that the bonytail brood stock at Dexter National Fish Hatchery descends from a very small founding population and needs augmentation with other genotypes.
- **Hatchery environment.** Artificial culture, rearing conditions, crowding, stress, and disease are all identified as sources of selection that alter the genetic composition of propagated fish.

## Key Findings

**1. Institutional role of the Service.** The memo concludes that the FWS, which holds the legal mandate for endangered fish recovery, has been a participant but not a leader in the NFWG. It states that guidance and integration of recovery work has been done by the NFWG, and that the Service does not review the group's work plans or processes. The author calls for a Service biologist to coordinate recovery.

**2. Accountability of the Bureau of Reclamation.** The memo says BOR, which the author identifies as the principal bureaucracy that brought these species into endangerment, distributes monitoring and recovery funds at its own discretion. It reports no formal recovery program from BOR, no regular reporting to the Service, and no meaningful accountability for expenditures.

**3. Razorback sucker stocking strategy.** The NFWG strategy relies on propagating wild-caught larvae in hatcheries and grow-out or off-site ponds. The memo judges that this strategy is unlikely to succeed in protecting the gene pool. Its stated concerns are:
- Assortive mating, in which adults may not contribute equally to reproduction.
- Lack of knowledge about genotype-local adaptation links.
- Post-spawning selection on embryos, given the change from lotic to lentic conditions.
- Propagation selection in hatcheries.
- Comparisons of larval and adult genetics using mtDNA are ambiguous. The memo recommends nuclear DNA methods.

**4. Stocking numbers.** To date, fewer than 1,500 razorback suckers have been stocked into Lake Mohave, and there is not enough recapture data to estimate survival. The memo says that stocking at least 10,000 fish per year would be needed to maintain population levels, but even this would not preserve genetic diversity while selection sources remain unaddressed.

**5. Risk assessment.** The memo concludes there is a good chance the NFWG strategy will not succeed. It warns that failure would be permanent and recommends that other approaches be implemented.

**6. Gene banking.** The memo recommends establishing a [[Gene Banking]] program using [[Cryopreservation]] of sperm, collected during routine sampling. Each year without collection means a lost opportunity to bank the genomes of more than 1,000 wild adult razorback suckers from the pre-crash era. It calls on the Service to take the lead.

**7. Bonytail chub options.** The memo proposes collecting and cryopreserving sperm from males to improve the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] brood stock. This is preferred over transporting large wild bonytail chub, which the memo describes as risky, since recent transfers ended in fish deaths. The NFWG reportedly opposes cryopreservation, arguing that sperm is useless without eggs. The memo disputes this and states there is no evidence that high maternal mtDNA diversity is critical or related to local adaptation. The FWS has stopped issuing new collection permits for chubs, and the NFWG has refused to cooperate on sperm collection.

**8. Contingency planning.** The memo asks several unresolved questions: what happens if larval collection targets are not met at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] or the grow-out facilities; which other fish could be stocked into Lake Mohave; and how cryopreservation can be used to maintain genetic diversity.
