## Executive Summary
This 1954 *Nature* correspondence by H.B.N. Hynes resolves a taxonomic misidentification concerning an amphipod species introduced to British waters. *Gammarus tigrinus* Sexton, originally described from saline waters in the Avon area and subsequently recorded from fresh and saline waters across England and Ireland, is identified as a junior synonym of the North American species *Gammarus fasciatus* Say. This determination was made through direct specimen comparison with the assistance of Dr. E.L. Bousfield of the National Museum of Canada, correcting an earlier hypothesis by Spooner that the species was related to *Gammarus annulatus* Smith. The note also highlights the ecological significance of this introduced species as a potential food source for fish populations in Great Britain.

## Background & Description
- **Distinguishing feature**: Distinctly striped ("tigrinus"-like) appearance when alive.
- This striping character is present in **[[Gammarus Fasciatus]]** but notably **absent** in the morphologically similar brackish-water species **[[Gammarus Annulatus]]**.
- Direct comparative examination of British *G. tigrinus* specimens against authentic North American *G. fasciatus* specimens (supplied by Dr. Bousfield) confirmed morphological identity between the two nominal taxa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native distribution**: Widespread in eastern North America; described as common in "weedy places" (per Clemens, 1950).
- **Introduced distribution**: Recorded in both fresh and saline waters at multiple localities in England and Ireland (map to be published in *Proceedings of the International Association of Theoretical and Applied Limnology*, Vol. 12).
- **Habitat preference in Britain**: Weedy areas on muddy substrata — a niche **not utilized by native British [[Gammarus]] species**.
- **Co-occurring introduced species**: This muddy-weedy habitat niche is also often colonized by **[[Crangonyx Gracilis]]** Smith, another North American amphipod introduced to Britain.
- **Case study site**: At Wyken Slough, near Coventry (a muddy, weedy lake), *G. fasciatus* appears to be the principal food organism supporting a large and diverse population of coarse fishes.
- **Trophic significance**: Amphipods of this type are noted as important food items for fish generally, suggesting potential value for deliberate introduction into fish-ponds as a forage species.

## Key Findings
1. **Taxonomic resolution**: *Gammarus tigrinus* Sexton, 1939 is a junior synonym of *Gammarus fasciatus* Say; the name *G. tigrinus* should be superseded by *G. fasciatus*.
2. **Origin confirmed as introduced**: The species is unknown elsewhere in continental Europe, supporting Spooner's original hypothesis that it represents a **New World introduction** to Britain, though his proposed sister-species link (*G. annulatus*) was incorrect — the true source population is *G. fasciatus*.
3. **Ecological parallel**: *G. fasciatus* and *Crangonyx gracilis* — both North American in origin — share a habitat niche (weedy, muddy substrata) in Britain that is left unoccupied by native amphipod fauna, suggesting an open ecological niche facilitated their establishment.
4. **Applied significance**: Given its abundance and apparent role as a key prey item for coarse fish populations (as observed at Wyken Slough), *G. fasciatus* is proposed as a candidate species for deliberate introduction into fish-ponds to enhance food availability for fish.
5. **Methodology**: Resolution of this taxonomic question relied on international collaboration and direct specimen exchange between the University of Liverpool (H.B.N. Hynes) and the National Museum of Canada (E.L. Bousfield), prompted by a suggestion from S.G. Segerstråle of Helsinki.
