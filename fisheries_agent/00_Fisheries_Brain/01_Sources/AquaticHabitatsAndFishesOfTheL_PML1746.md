# Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Lower Colorado River – Cibola Division

#ichthyology #marine-biology #colorado-river-fishes

## Executive Summary

This appendix describes the aquatic habitats, fish fauna, benthic invertebrates, aquatic vegetation, plankton-related pigments, and physical-chemical conditions of the [[Cibola Division]] of the [[Colorado River]], a channelized reach of roughly 37 km (the scanned length digit is uncertain) near Blythe, California. Sampling dates from 1974–76 for fishes and March 1975 for pigments and water chemistry. The study is a comparative baseline. It reports a fish community dominated by [[Red Shiner]], sparse-to-moderate benthos, dense emergent vegetation in backwaters, and dissolved oxygen and nutrient patterns that are linked to inflowing drainage and wastewater.

## Background & Description

The Cibola Division is heavily modified. About 86% of its banks have been altered by levees, jetties, or [[Riprap]] armoring. At maximum discharge about 45% of the surface area is backwater. Remnant [[Oxbow Lake]] habitats (for example [[Cibola Lake]]) and artificially diked lentic habitats persist.

Channel bottoms are 50–90% sand, are unstable, and shift with the current in most segments. Backwaters have sand-silt bottoms with varying organic content. Maximum depths rarely exceed 2.5 m, and channel currents tend toward uniformity.

The appendix is organized into sections on fishes, benthos, higher vascular plants and algae, pigment analyses, and physical-chemical analyses. Appendix tables A–E hold the underlying data on sediments, fishes, benthos, pigments, and physical-chemical measurements. The fish species list (Table C-I) includes 14 kinds recorded or observed. Common names follow Bailey et al. (1970), with some modifications from Minckley (1973).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish community and sampling gear.** Four of the 14 kinds were captured in seines. Red shiner made up almost 62% of total fish numbers, followed by [[Mosquitofish]], [[Common Carp]], and [[Largemouth Bass]]. Estimated overall density was approximately 3,181 fishes/ha (the scanned figure is partly illegible), with an average weight of 3.6 kg/ha.

Near the upper end of the division, only red shiner was taken (weight 0.4 kg/ha). In the succeeding 16-km segment, the species collectively numbered about 4,105 individuals and weighed 5.3 kg/ha.

**Benthos.** Benthic invertebrates in the mainstream were sparse to moderately abundant, varying widely between samples. The upstream segment supported 121 animals per m² (from [[Ekman Dredge]] samples). The succeeding reach averaged 284 individuals/m² (5 samples). Biomass differed sharply between the two areas (0.01 and 20.3 kg/ha), largely because of greater average weight of individual [[Chironomidae]] in the lower reach.

Cibola Lake, the only backwater sampled for benthos, had more animals per unit area than the river, averaging 759/m². Mean biomass was 89.7 kg/ha, but after the bivalve [[Corbicula]] was excluded, about 8.9 kg/ha of chironomids and oligochaetes remained.

**Vegetation and algae.** Vegetation resembled that of the [[Palo Verde Division]]. Unstable channel bottoms had no submerged aquatic plants during the study. Algae occurred only on solid substrates such as riprap and logs. Backwaters and drains are edged by dense [[Cattail]] and sedge stands. [[Spiny Naiad]] (*Najas marina*) forms rank stands, probably favored by elevated total dissolved solids from field drainage. Bacillariophycean (diatom) films and bluegreen algae occurred in shallow silty backwaters, and mats floated at midday at several sites.

**Hydrology and drainage.** Many backwaters and oxbows receive drainage from the [[Blythe Area]] and related agricultural lands. This supplies additional dissolved solids and nutrients and complicates the separation of lentic from return-flow effects.

## Key Findings

**Pigment analyses**
- Long-term pigment data showed only a slight tendency toward diurnal cycling. Concentrations tended to be higher from late evening to early morning, relating to periods of high, then declining, discharge.
- No definite upstream-to-downstream increase in pigments was found.
- Most pigments varied erratically, except [[Chlorophyll A]]. Values ranged from not detected to at least two orders of magnitude.
- 23 grab samples from the mainstream were comparable to values from 24-hour sampling periods.
- Computed "diatom" values averaged higher in backwaters than in the mainstream. Orders of magnitude were similar in both habitats, with only a tendency toward higher levels in backwaters. Surface and bottom values in backwaters differed only slightly, suggesting random distribution of pigment-yielding organisms.

**Temperature**
- Daytime-to-nighttime fluctuation in the river was about 2.0 °C in March. Averages ranged from 13.0 to 14.6 °C.
- Oxbow lakes and drains were 2–4 °C warmer than the river. No vertical stratification was evident at sampling.

**Dissolved oxygen**
- Levels were near 100% saturation in most habitats, mostly 9.6 to above 12.0 mg/L.
- A 25-hour study at RM 600.0 found under-saturation throughout, with values of 7.4–9.6 mg/L (average 9.0 mg/L at mean temperature 13.3 °C).
- A similarly depressed oxygen curve was recorded a day earlier in the Palo Verde Division. This was tentatively attributed to inflowing wastewater.
- Only one of six Palo Verde Oxbow sites had levels below 9.5 mg/L.
- Drains and backwaters receiving [[Blythe Area]] water had relatively high dissolved oxygen. Little loading of these systems seems indicated. The cause of depression at the two mainstream sites remains unknown.

**Turbidity and pH**
- Mainstream [[Turbidity]] ranged from not detected to 55 JTU. Other habitats showed similar ranges.
- Mainstream pH ranged from 7.2 to 8.6 and fluctuated erratically with daylight and other parameters.
- Backwaters and drains were less variable, with a range of 7.9 to 8.4 across 23 determinations.

**Phosphate (PO₄-P)**
- Mainstream values ranged from not detected to 0.98 mg/L.
- The highest average (0.15 mg/L, 25 samples) and greatest variation occurred at RM 600.0. This coincided with depressed [[Dissolved Oxygen]], suggesting allochthonous input affected mainstream chemistry.
- Other mainstream averages were about 0.07 mg/L.
- The oxbow entering at RM 619.0, which drains much of the Blythe area, consistently had higher PO₄-P (0.07–0.26 mg/L).
- Palo Verde Oxbow was more similar to the mainstream (not detected to 0.12 mg/L). [[Cibola Lake]] ranged 0.04–0.05 mg/L.
- Directly sampled drains in March ranged from not detected to 0.12 mg/L (4 samples).

**Nitrogen**
- NO₃-N was not analyzed in the Cibola Division.

**Overall pattern.** The division's fish, benthos, and water chemistry reflect a channelized, sandy, low-velocity river. Its backwaters and drains are shaped by agricultural return flows and possible wastewater inputs. The appendix's own caveat is that lentic and return-flow effects are confounded and were not separated in the analysis.
