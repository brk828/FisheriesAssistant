## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although fish are not directly discussed, several publications authored or co-authored by Brown pertain to the **aquatic and riparian habitat matrix** that supports fish and other aquatic fauna in arid environments:

- **[[Riparian Habitat Classification]]**: Brown's 1977 paper "Inventory of riparian habitats" and his 1978 "Southwestern wetlands -- their classification and characteristics" establish foundational classification schemes for riparian and wetland ecosystems in the Southwest, environments that serve as critical habitat corridors for native desert fishes.
- **[[Biotic Communities of the American Southwest]]**: Brown's edited volume (1982, *Desert Plants* 4:1-342) and related digitized classification systems (with C. H. Lowe, 1974a/b; with C. P. Pase, 1979/1980) categorize natural ecosystems, including aquatic and semi-aquatic biotic communities, across Arizona and the broader Southwest/Mexico region.
- **[[Perennial Stream and Wetland Mapping]]**: Co-authored drainage maps (Brown, Carmony, Lowe & Turner, 1977, 1981) at 1:1,000,000 scale document perennial streams and important wetlands in Arizona — key baseline data for identifying native fish habitat and aquatic connectivity.
- **[[Arizona's Wetlands and Waterfowl]]** (1985 book, Univ. of Arizona Press): while focused on waterfowl, this work implicitly documents wetland/aquatic habitat conditions relevant to co-occurring fish communities.
- Popular articles such as "Return Of the Webfeet" (*Fins and Feathers*, 1984) and "Border Webfoot" (*Shooting Sportsman*, 1988/89) address waterfowl and wetland conservation themes adjacent to aquatic ecosystem health.
- A single newspaper article, "Fishing license sales fall" (*Arizona Republic*, 12/29/90), references fisheries management/licensing trends in Arizona but provides no biological data.
