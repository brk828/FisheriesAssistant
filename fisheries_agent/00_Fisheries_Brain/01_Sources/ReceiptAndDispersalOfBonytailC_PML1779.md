# Bonytail Chub Receipt and Dispersal for Pen Culture Experiment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1996 interagency memorandum from the Parker Fish and Wildlife Service office records the receipt, sorting, and dispersal of [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) delivered by truck from a hatchery for a [[Net Pen Culture]] experiment. Fish were counted into three size categories (large, medium, small), acclimated to lake water in coolers, transported by boat, and placed into labeled net pens. A subset was preserved and sent to [[Arizona State University]]. The memo is largely operational and contains no formal research conclusions.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** Receipt and dispersal of bonytail chub for a pen culture experiment.
- **Date:** 23 July 1996.
- **Origin:** Fish arrived from a hatchery truck at approximately 0900 (scan reads "0500"/"0900"; verify).
- **Size classes:** Large, medium, and small groups, handled separately so categories would not be mixed if fish escaped the dip net.
- **Participants named:** Randy VanHaverbeke and staff of the [[Bureau of Land Management]]; Mitch Thoreson (Parker FRO); ASU personnel; boat crews for Edna, Denny, Cameron, James, and Curt of Dexter.
- **Sender/recipients:** Project coordination staff at the Parker FRO to the project leader and regional technology center.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Acclimation method:** Hatchery truck water and lake water were mixed in coolers to bring temperatures closer together before release. Recorded temperatures are partly illegible in the scan (truck water and lake water values are unreadable), so exact figures cannot be reported.
- **Oxygenation:** Supplemental oxygen was added to the coolers during holding.
- **Handling:** Fish were netted in small batches, placed in 5-gallon buckets labeled with their destination net pen, and transported by boat. Boats typically carried about 15 buckets per trip. Large and medium fish were dispersed first, then small fish.
- **Enumeration:** Counts were made by hand per bucket, with staff counting out required numbers for each pen.

## Key Findings

- **Dispersal logistics:** Fish were distributed to net pens in three size-based batches. Large and medium groups were completed first, followed by small fish, with one boat loading while another delivered.
- **Totals:** The scan gives the three group totals as roughly "219, 432, and 270" for large, medium, and small fish respectively. The first figure is OCR-garbled ("Z'IQ") and should be verified against the original.
- **Preserved specimens:** Twenty-five to 30 fish from each group were preserved and sent to [[Arizona State University]]; the rest were segregated into groups at the hatchery and released to their pens.
- **Mortality:** The memo states that mortality was low. The scan of this sentence is degraded, so the exact wording and counts cannot be confirmed.
- **Quality control:** Separate coolers for each size class prevented cross-contamination of categories during escapes at the dip net.
