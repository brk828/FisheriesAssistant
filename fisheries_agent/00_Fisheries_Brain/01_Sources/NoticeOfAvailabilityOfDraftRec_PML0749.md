# Notice of availability of draft recovery plan for Lahontan cutthroat trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation of February 1993 *Federal Register* notices is mostly administrative. Its one fish-related item is a notice from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Service) announcing a draft recovery plan for the threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*[[Oncorhynchus Clarkii Henshawi]]*) and soliciting public comment before April 26, 1993. The plan targets the [[Lahontan Basin]], where the subspecies is endemic. The other notices cover National Institutes of Health review panels, a National Park Service advisory group for the Missouri National Recreational River, Mississippi River Coordinating Commission meetings, National Register of Historic Places listings, a Drug Enforcement Administration manufacturing application, and Department of Labor job-training allotments. Only the trout plan has aquatic-ecology content.

## Background & Description

*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi* is a salmonid fish endemic to the [[Lahontan Basin]] in northern Nevada, eastern California, and southern Oregon. The notice describes it as threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended.

- **Historic range:** 13 Nevada counties (Carson City, Churchill, Douglas, Elko, Eureka, Humboldt, Lander, Lyon, Mineral, Nye, Pershing, Storey, Washoe), 7 California counties (Alpine, El Dorado, Lassen, Mono, Nevada, Placer, Sierra), and 2 Oregon counties (Harney, Malheur).
- **Principal threats:** habitat modification and nonnative fish introductions.
- **Land status:** Historic habitat is largely public land managed by the USDA Forest Service and the Bureau of Land Management, plus Indian tribal and private lands.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Remaining native populations occur in six basins: Carson, Truckee, Walker, Humboldt, Quinn/Black Rock, and Whitehorse. Populations also exist outside the historic range because of introductions. The plan's recovery strategy has three parts:

1. Maintain and enhance **135 stream populations and two lake populations**.
2. Increase the extent of suitable habitat and eliminate threats from nonnative fish species.
3. Reestablish some populations within historic habitat where few populations now persist.

Under [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 4(f), the Service must provide public notice and a comment period during recovery plan development, and must consider all comments before approving a new or revised [[Recovery Plan]]. Federal agencies also use approved plans in implementation.

## Key Findings

- **Draft plan released for review:** The Service seeks written public comments by **April 26, 1993**. The plan sets recovery criteria for downlisting or delisting and estimates time and cost for recovery actions.
- **Recovery targets:** 135 stream and two lake populations are to be maintained and enhanced.
- **Contacts for the plan:** Field Supervisor David L. Harlow and Patrick Coffin, Nevada Ecological Services Field Office, Reno, Nevada; Assistant Regional Director, Ecological Services, Portland, Oregon.
- **Authority:** Section 4(f) of the Endangered Species Act (16 U.S.C. 1533(f)). The notice is dated February 12, 1993, and signed by Regional Director Marvin L. Pienert.

**Other notices in the compilation (non-aquatic):**

- **NIH review panels:** Scheduled March 1993 meetings on hepatitis, angiographic trials in women, and related grant or contract reviews.
- **Missouri National Recreational River Advisory Group:** Established May 24, 1991, with a 39-mile free-flowing segment of the Missouri River. The first advisory group meeting was scheduled.
- **Mississippi River Coordinating Commission:** Meetings were set for April 5 and April 29, 1993, to review the draft comprehensive management plan and environmental impact statement for the Mississippi National River and Recreation Area.
- **National Register of Historic Places:** Listings across Iowa, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee, and Wisconsin, including multiple Lincoln Highway segments in Greene County, Iowa.
- **Drug Enforcement Administration:** Orpharm, Inc. applied to become a bulk manufacturer of controlled substances, including methadone. Comments were due within 30 days of publication.
- **Department of Labor:** Announced Job Training Partnership Act allotments and Wagner-Peyser preliminary planning estimates for Program Year 1993 (July 1, 1993 to June 30, 1994).
