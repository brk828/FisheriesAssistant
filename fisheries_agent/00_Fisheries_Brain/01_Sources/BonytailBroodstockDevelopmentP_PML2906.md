# Bonytail Broodstock Development Plan (1997 Correspondence)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #bonytail

## Executive Summary

This January 27, 1997 letter from J. Holt Williamson, Research Leader at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (USFWS, Dexter, NM), is addressed to [[Phil Hedrick]] of Arizona State University. It sets out the early plan to build a new [[Bonytail]] (BTC) [[Broodstock Development]] program. The program would use the only known captive adult population and possibly wild adults from [[Lake Mohave]]. The letter outlines four conditions for the effort and reports that funding and a contract were in progress. It is correspondence rather than research, so it reports no experimental data.

## Background & Description

- **Captive population:** Dexter NFH&TC holds the only adult captive bonytail population in existence, approximately 350 individuals, most of them PIT-tagged.
- **Origin:** The fish are first-generation descendants of wild fish. They were produced in 1981 from fewer than 10 wild Lake Mohave adults. The letter does not say who captured and identified the founders, though it suggests [[W.L. Minckley]] may have.
- **Spawning site:** The adults were believed to have been spawned at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], AZ, at the upper end of Lake Mohave.
- **Goal:** Develop a new BTC broodstock from these fish and perhaps wild Lake Mohave adults. The letter states that maximizing species diversity within the new broodstock is desirable, and it asks how best to incorporate recently identified wild bonytail material.
- **Wild capture:** Capturing wild adults from anywhere, including Mohave, is described as a rare event. Some fish taken from Mohave may be hatchery fish derived from Dexter's broodstock and stocked there years earlier.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter's ecological content is limited to the origin and status of the bonytail stock. Key points:

- Wild adult bonytail are rarely captured, so the captive population is the main available genetic resource.
- Some apparent wild fish in Lake Mohave may be stocked hatchery fish, so their origin must be verified.
- The author calls for comparative analysis, including appropriate [[Genetic Analysis]], to establish the nature of these animals before broodstock decisions are made.

## Key Findings

1. **Captive stock size:** About 350 adult bonytail at Dexter NFH&TC, mostly PIT-tagged, are the only captive adults known to exist.
2. **Limited founder base:** The captive stock descends from fewer than 10 wild Lake Mohave adults, which were spawned in 1981.
3. **Genetic verification needed:** Wild-caught Mohave fish may be stocked hatchery progeny, so comparative and genetic analysis is required before they are used.
4. **Broodstock aim:** The new BTC broodstock should maximize species diversity and may include wild Lake Mohave material.
5. **Four conditions set by the Service:**
   - The plan must be jointly developed by the Service and the contractor.
   - The plan must undergo broad critical review within a reasonable time frame.
   - The plan must be published in a conservation journal.
   - The plan must be funded.
6. **Project status (as of January 1997):**
   - Dr. C.O. Minckley had received funds from the Albuquerque Ecological Services office to contract work.
   - Hedrick had been awarded the contract, but Williamson had not yet seen it.
   - Williamson asked Hedrick to review the enclosed outline and related documents.
7. **Proposed next step:** A conference call among the core cooperators (Hedrick, C.O. Minckley, and USFWS staff including Jim Hanson) to develop a fuller strategy. A site visit was also offered.

**Copied recipients:** Stuart Leon, C.O. Minckley, James Hanson, Jim Brooks, Jim Young, Sam Spiller, W.L. Minckley, and Paul Marsh.
