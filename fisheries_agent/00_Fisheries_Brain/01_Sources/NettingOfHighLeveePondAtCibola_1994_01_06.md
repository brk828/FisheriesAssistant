# Netting of High Levee Pond at Cibola NWR and Growout Site Assessment at Imperial NWR

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary
This memorandum documents a fisheries monitoring effort conducted by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service on 28–29 December 1993 at the High Levee Pond, [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]], targeting two endangered big-river fishes native to the [[Lower Colorado River]]: the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail Chub]]. Trammel net sampling recaptured 13 razorback suckers and 1 bonytail chub, all of which had been stocked into the pond roughly two months earlier as part of a repatriation/growout program. Growth comparisons between stocking and recapture indicate substantial size increases over this short interval, supporting the viability of refuge ponds as growout habitat. No non-native (introduced) fish were captured, suggesting favorable rearing conditions free of predatory or competitive interference at time of sampling. The report also documents a site visit to [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] to evaluate additional embayments and ponds — including the previously-stocked "Cormorant Pond" — for future development as growout facilities in 1994.

## Background & Description
**Razorback Sucker (n=13 recaptured):**
- Total length range: 190–214 mm
- Mean total length: 201 mm (n=13)
- Weight range: 75–110 g (n=12)
- At initial stocking (18 October): mean total length was 98 mm (n=100)
- Net growth: approximately +103 mm mean TL over ~2.3 months in pond conditions

**Bonytail Chub (n=1 recaptured):**
- Total length: 300 mm
- Weight: 220 g
- PIT tag ID: #7F7A19207A
- At initial stocking (21 October): mean total length was 67 mm
- Net growth: approximately +233 mm TL over ~2 months — noted by the author as an unusually rapid increase, attributable to the recruitment stock containing larger individuals at time of stocking rather than uniform growth of the entire cohort

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Both species are Colorado River Basin big-river fishes historically associated with mainstem riverine habitats, now heavily reliant on refuge pond and backwater "growout" programs as part of conservation efforts due to population declines driven by non-native species introductions, habitat alteration, and river regulation.

- **Habitat sampled:** High Levee Pond, an isolated refuge impoundment used for rearing/repatriation of hatchery-origin razorback suckers and bonytail chubs prior to potential release into the mainstem [[Colorado River]].
- **Sampling method:** 400 m of trammel net set for 15 hours (1700–0800 hrs), a passive entanglement method effective for capturing benthic/pelagic catostomids and cyprinids in still-water ponds.
- **Community composition:** No introduced/non-native fish species were captured during this sampling event, suggesting the pond may currently function as a refuge from competitive/predatory pressure common in open river systems.
- **Growout candidate sites:** Additional ponds and embayments at [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] were evaluated for suitability as expanded growout facilities, including "Cormorant Pond," which had previously been stocked with razorback suckers.

## Key Findings
- **Recapture success:** 13 of the originally stocked razorback suckers and 1 bonytail chub were recaptured, confirming survival and retention within the pond environment over the two-month period.
- **Growth performance:** Both species exhibited substantial growth in total length within the pond setting, though the bonytail growth figure is confounded by initial size variability in the stocked recruitment cohort rather than reflecting uniform individual growth rates.
- **Tagging:** The recaptured bonytail chub carried a PIT tag (#7F7A19207A), enabling individual-level tracking consistent with standard endangered fish monitoring protocols.
- **Absence of non-native fish:** No introduced species were captured, a positive indicator for pond suitability as a low-competition growout environment.
- **Management implications:** Based on observed growth and survival, plus favorable site conditions at [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]], the author committed to pursuing development of one or more additional growout facilities there in 1994 to expand rearing capacity for [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] populations.
- **Collaborative effort:** Fieldwork and planning involved coordination among the [[Parker Fish and Wildlife Resource Office]], [[Arizona Fish and Wildlife Resource Office|AZFRO]], and refuge staff from both Cibola and Imperial NWRs.
