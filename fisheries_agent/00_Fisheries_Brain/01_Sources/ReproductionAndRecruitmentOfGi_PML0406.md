# Reproduction and Recruitment of Gila spp. and Associated Fishes in Desolation and Gray Canyons of the Green River, Utah, 1992–1996

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report (2000) from the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, funded by the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin, presents fish community monitoring from five annual trips (1992–1996) in Desolation and Gray Canyons of the [[Green River]], with pre-project data from 1985 incorporated. Low-velocity habitats were seined in backwaters, while main-channel habitats were sampled with trammel nets, hoop nets and [[Electrofishing]] at four trend sites.

Non-native cyprinids, mainly [[Red Shiner]], dominated low-velocity catches. Young-of-year (YOY) [[Gila spp.]] (native chubs) were rare, with the strongest cohort in 1993. Adult chub catch rates remained below 0.2 fish per 23 m net-hour and fell below 0.1 in 1996. [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] catch rates stayed low but concentrations of ripe males were documented at [[Rabbit Valley]] and [[Joe Hutch]], refining the [[Three Fords Spawning Area]]. Native catostomid netting catches declined overall but recruitment improved after 1993. [[Channel Catfish]] were the most abundant main-channel species. The authors recommend integrating the monitoring into the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] and targeting sampling flows of 7,000–8,000 cfs.

## Background & Description

Desolation and Gray Canyons hold the largest concentration of native [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*G. robusta*) and their intergrades in the Green River sub-basin. One [[Bonytail]] (*G. elegans*) was collected in Gray Canyon during 1982–1985 surveys. Native Colorado River chubs are collectively termed *Gila* spp. in the report.

Identification to species is difficult. [[McElroy and Douglas]] (1995) found sympatric humpback and roundtail chub from Desolation Canyon more similar to each other than to allopatric conspecifics, though they argued the species are distinct. Most chubs collected here could not be assigned to species; many were recorded as "chub" or field-identified as *Gila* spp. Morphological traits of "Desolation-type" chubs mix features of both species.

The study had three secondary objectives under the broader Recovery Program: (1) PIT tagging and age/growth analysis of *Gila* spp. to determine recruitment as affected by Green River flows; (2) testing whether [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] spawn outside the documented Three Fords site; and (3) monitoring non-native fish trends, including declining native catostomids and increasing channel catfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Desolation Canyon (RK 345.6–251.2) is deep, incised into Wasatch and Green River formations, with slow flat water above Cedar Ridge (RK 296) and steeper gradients below. Gray Canyon (RK 251.2–211.2) begins at Three Fords Rapid, cuts the Mesa Verde formation, and has gradients near 1.1 m/km. Vegetation is cold desert and pinyon-juniper; annual precipitation averages about 40 cm.

**Trend sites** (each 0.5–1.0 km long, established 1989):
- Cedar Ridge (RK 295.7), low-gradient, bracketed by debris-fan rapids
- Surprise Canyon / Rock Creek (RK 280.5 / 275.4), narrower valley, deepest site (maximum depth 9.7 m)
- Joe Hutch (RK 256), below a debris fan and above flat water at Florence Creek
- Coal Creek (RK 232.8), Gray Canyon, with eddies from shoreline debris

**Flow regime.** Green River flows (USGS gage 09315000) ranged widely. Peak flow was highest in 1995 (approx. 29,800 cfs per the report's discussion), and the 1993 peak was 25,400 cfs. Overwinter flows averaged 3,250 cfs (Oct 1993–Feb 1994) versus 2,250 cfs the following winter.

**Sampling methods.** Backwaters were seined (two-person seine, 4 m × 1.2 m, 3.2 mm mesh). Main-channel sampling occurred mainly at dusk and night using six trammel nets (23 m × 1.8 m), three hoop nets and electrofishing. Target fish over 175 mm were PIT tagged. Catch-per-effort was standardized to fish per 23 m net-hour, fish per actual hour, and fish per square metre of low-velocity habitat.

## Key Findings

**Small-bodied fish and YOY *Gila* spp.**
- Non-native cyprinids (red shiner, fathead minnow, sand shiner) made up over 80% of low-velocity catches each year and reached 98.5% in 1996.
- The largest YOY chub catch was in August 1993 (n=162; approx. 0.09–0.1 fish/m²). YOY chub CPE was weakly correlated with peak flow (R² = 0.1).
- YOY chub spawning occurred across flows from 1,650 to 25,800 cfs.
- Back-calculated 1993 hatch was June 21 and spawn June 16, at flows of 14,000–16,700 cfs. The August 1993 sample grew at 0.36 mm/day at mean flows of 2,924 cfs and 24.5 °C.

**YOY Colorado Pikeminnow**
- Collected only in 1993 (n=203) and 1994 (n=1). Trip CPE was 0.11 in 1993 and 0.0006 in 1994.
- Back-calculated spawning was around 17 July 1993, at 4,360 cfs.

**Adult and juvenile *Gila* spp.**
- 182 juvenile and adult chubs were collected in 1992–1996; 132 were PIT tagged (24 humpback, 30 roundtail, 78 unidentified).
- Nine chubs were recaptured, all at their original capture site with negligible growth or movement.
- Trammel catch fell from 0.72 fish/hour at Cedar Ridge and 0.54 at Joe Hutch in 1989 (significantly higher, p<0.1) to below 0.1 in 1993 and 1996.
- The 1993 cohort was the strongest recruitment since pre-project monitoring; it was seen as Age 1+ fish in 1994 and juveniles in 1995 (35.3% of catch, n=51).
- Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests showed significant shifts in population structure over time (e.g., 1985 vs. 1989, K-S = 0.59, p=0.0).

**Colorado Pikeminnow**
- Present at all four trend sites; greatest trammel CPE was 0.065–0.08 at RK 280.5 in 1994 and 1995.
- Rabbit Valley (RK 238), 12.8 km downstream of Three Fords, held a concentration of ripe males in 1994 and 1995 (89.7% of 39 fish). Several fish were tagged farther upstream in the Uinta Basin, suggesting they moved down to spawn.
- At Joe Hutch on 18 July 1997, 44 adults were counted, the largest concentration ever observed in Desolation/Gray.
- The Three Fords spawning area was redefined as an 18 km reach between RK 256 and 238, with spawning focus shifting through time.

**Native and non-native catostomids and catfish**
- Netting catch of native catostomids declined from 1989, but native species rose to about half of net catch after 1993.
- Electrofishing produced a relatively strong sample of flannelmouth and bluehead suckers after 1994; juvenile flannelmouth suckers were more abundant after 1993.
- Bluehead sucker CPE was lowest in 1990 (0.009) and highest in 1994 (0.08).
- Channel catfish were the most abundant main-channel species. The only apparent recruitment lapse was in 1994, following the high flows of 1993.

**Management recommendations**
- Incorporate Desolation/Gray fish community monitoring into the Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program, avoiding sampling flows above 10,000 cfs and targeting 7,000–8,000 cfs.
- Add a specific fall effort to monitor YOY Colorado Pikeminnow abundance.
- Estimate chub population size at the four trend sites, and consider a catfish removal component.
- Gather morphological measurements (per Douglas et al. 1998) to aid chub identification.
- Further investigate the discrepancy in channel catfish densities between Desolation/Gray and Westwater Canyon.

**Conclusions on flow.** High flows in the mid-1980s and 1993 appear to benefit the native fish community. The authors note that the monitoring data alone do not support flow recommendations, and that the companion nursery-habitat study ([[Day et al.]], Report B) is needed to establish causal links.
