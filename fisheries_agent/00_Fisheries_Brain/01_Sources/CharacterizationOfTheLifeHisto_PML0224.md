# Humpback Chub Life History and Ecology in the Grand Canyon: Data Collection Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a Data Collection Plan (BIO/WEST, Inc. Report TR-250-01, dated January 1991) prepared for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under contract No. 0-CS-40-09110, "Characterization of the Life History and Ecology of the Humpback Chub in the Grand Canyon." It sets out the sampling design, methods, schedule, logistics and data management for a multi-year study of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the mainstem [[Colorado River]] below [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The plan is supported by three appendices: a Fish Sampling Protocol (Appendix A), a Fish Handling Protocol (Appendix B) and a Data Management Protocol (Appendix C). The research is designed to test hypotheses on how dam operations affect habitat, water quality, food, reproduction, survivorship, distribution and biotic interactions of the species. The Study runs about 39 field trips between October 1990 and December 1993.

The memoranda at the front of the document (January 10 and 18, 1991) transmit the plan to the Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT) and include a corrected signature sheet; they contain no additional scientific data.

## Background & Description

The study is led by [[Richard Valdez]] as Principal Investigator for [[BIO/WEST, Inc.]] and is one component of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program. It was commissioned to meet portions of two of seven conservation measures from a 1988 [[Biological Opinion]] on Glen Canyon Dam: Measure 5 (research on impacts of dam operations on humpback chub in the mainstem and tributaries) and Measure 7 (establishing a second spawning population in the Grand Canyon). Results are intended to feed the Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement.

The work is coordinated through the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] (ACT), which includes the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), the [[National Park Service]], Reclamation, GCES, and the Navajo and Hopi Nations. Tributary studies are carried out by USFWS, AGFD and ASU, while BIO/WEST conducts the mainstem investigation. The plan lists about 35 recipients of the transmittal memoranda, which is a sign of the broad interagency involvement.

The overall objective is to determine the relationship between Glen Canyon Dam operations and the ecology and life history requirements of the endangered humpback chub population in the Grand Canyon, with the ultimate aim of supporting recovery of the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** The investigation covers a 170-mile reach from Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56) to Diamond Creek (RM 226), divided into three reaches:

- **Upper Reach (LCR Reach):** Kwagunt Rapid (RM 56) to below Red Canyon (RM 77.4), 21.5 to 22 miles (about 34.4 km). It includes the confluence with the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) at RM 61, and is the primary intensive sampling area with radiotelemetry.
- **Middle Reach (Granite Gorge Reach):** Below Red Canyon (RM 77.4 to RM 159.9), an 82.5-mile reach (about 132.7 km) of steep, rocky shoreline. Sampled by quantitative gill and trammel netting, electrofishing, seining and minnow traps, without radiotelemetry.
- **Lower Reach (Havasu Creek Reach):** Below Havasu Creek (RM 160) to Diamond Creek (RM 226).

Sampling is concentrated at tributary confluences where humpback chub have been captured previously. Five tributary inflow areas in the Granite Gorge Reach are treated as separate substrata: Bright Angel, Shinumo, Tapeats, Kanab and Havasu Creeks. Geomorphic strata follow [[Howard and Dolan]] (1991) and [[Schmidt and Graf]] (1990), who recognised 11 morphologically distinct areas.

**Prior knowledge.** Earlier work (Kaeding and Zimmerman 1983; Maddux et al. 1987) showed that humpback chub enter the LCR in spring to spawn, and that many may remain in the mainstem thereafter. The Granite Gorge Reach was judged an important nursery and rearing area for native fishes; young-of-year and juveniles were found, but no spawning sites or larvae were confirmed there. Cold water releases (about 40 °F) from the dam are thought to have reduced the pre-dam distribution and abundance of the species, and the species has been absent from the dam base where it was once captured.

**Operational context.** GCES research flows (controlled releases) were scheduled for the first 10 months of the study (October 1, 1990 to July 29, 1991) to test habitat and movement responses to stable and fluctuating flows.

**Sampling schedule.** Trips alternate between 20-day trips (two teams, each with about 5 to 6 BIO/WEST and 1 ACT biologist) and 10-day trips (one team). A total of 39 trips is planned (19 twenty-day and 20 ten-day). Research boats are carried on [[Oars]] support rafts. Radiotagged fish are the main means of tracking habitat use, and about 60 implants per year are planned.

## Key Findings

The plan makes its central points through a set of testable hypotheses, grouped by task.

**Task 1: Resource availability and use**
- **Ho 1A-1:** Habitat is limiting under certain flow conditions. Tested with macrohabitat mapping on mylar overlays, analysed by AutoCAD, and with radiotelemetry of adult fish.
- **Ho 1A-2:** Water quality is limiting under certain flow conditions. Tested with temperature, turbidity, salinity, TDS and dissolved oxygen measurements, including 24-hour Datasonde and Hydrolab monitoring.
- **Ho 1A-3:** Food is limiting under certain flow conditions. Tested by nonlethal stomach pumping, linked to drift and benthic sampling. The rationale draws on [[Leibfried]] (1988), who found rainbow trout below the dam eating large quantities of [[Cladophora]].

**Task 1B: Reproduction**
- **Ho 1B-1:** Humpback chub do not actively spawn in the mainstem. The plan describes main-channel reproduction as "at best extremely limited, or more likely nonexistent" because of cold water. It will check for spawning through nuptial condition and radiotag movements, and will track fish that may ascend tributaries such as the LCR, Shinumo, Havasu, Kanab and Bright Angel Creeks.

**Task 1C: Early survival**
- **Ho 1C-1:** Survival of early life stages is low in the mainstem. Age-0 fish from the LCR will be marked by fin clipping at the LCR mouth in late May and early June. Permanent marks are urgently needed. Age-3 and older fish (greater than 125 mm TL) can be PIT tagged. Survival will be estimated separately for age-0 to age-4 fish, with adults treated as one group.

**Task 1D: Distribution, abundance and movement**
- **Ho 1D-1:** Distribution and abundance are affected by dam operations. Predam data are almost non-existent. Tributary access will be assessed by measuring depth and velocity at the mouths of six tributaries (LCR, Bright Angel, Tapeats, Shinumo, Kanab, Havasu).
- **Ho 1D-2:** Cold water releases affect distribution and abundance independently of fluctuating flows.
- **Ho 1D-3:** Movement is greater during fluctuating flows than during stable flows. Long-term movement is measured from recaptures of PIT-tagged or fin-clipped fish, and short-term movement from radiotagged fish over 2 to 24 hours.

**Task 1E: Biotic interactions**
- **Ho 1E-1:** Introduced fish species have a negative effect on humpback chub. Channel catfish, carp, rainbow trout, brown trout and striped bass are to be examined for predation on humpback chub life stages.

**Task 2 and Task 2A: Life history and population model**
- The life history schedule will be described from literature and field data, and a population model will be developed or modified under GCES guidance to analyse reproductive success, recruitment and survivorship.

**Methods highlights (from appendices)**
- Electrofishing uses a Coffelt Mark XX Complex Pulse System, with output of 15 to 20 amperes and 300 to 350 volts, and catch-per-unit effort is expressed as fish per 10 hours.
- Gill nets are 100 ft long and 6 ft deep; experimental gill nets have four mesh sizes. Gill nets are checked at least every 2 hours.
- Trammel nets have a 12-inch outer mesh and a 1 1/2-inch or 1 1/4-inch inner mesh, found most effective for humpback chub.
- Radiotags must not exceed 2% of body weight. 11 g tags are implanted only in fish over 550 g, and 16 g tags only in fish over 800 g.
- Surgical incision follows a modified technique, with the antenna guided along the inner abdominal wall. A 3 cm incision is made along the abdominal midline.
- All humpback chub over 175 mm TL receive PIT tags, and fin clips are used for temporary marks.
- Stomach samples are collected by a nonlethal pump method that flushes material into a funnel, based on [[Seaburg and Moyle]] (1964).
- Data are recorded on seven standard forms and entered into dBASE III Plus, with quality control performed at four levels: training, in-field supervision, standard-format entry and final accuracy evaluation.

**Limitations.** The plan acknowledges that one year of data will be preliminary, and that the age-determination methods for older fish are uncertain because of variable growth and maturation effects.
