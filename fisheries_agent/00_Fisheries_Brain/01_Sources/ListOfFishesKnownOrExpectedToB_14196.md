# List of Fishes Known or Expected to Belong to the Fauna of Big Bend National Park

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae #species-inventory

## Executive Summary

This document by Clark Hubbs from [[The University of Texas]] provides a comprehensive inventory of fish species in [[Big Bend National Park]]. The report documents 25 species confirmed within the park and 13 additional species expected but not yet collected (marked with asterisks). Species descriptions include habitat preferences, distribution within the park (primarily [[Rio Grande]], [[Tornillo Creek]], [[Terlingua Creek]], and various springs), maximum sizes, and diagnostic morphological characters. The document includes a dichotomous identification key for all 38 species and emphasizes the dominance of silty water habitats in the [[Rio Grande]] versus clear spring-fed tributaries.

## Background & Description

The fish fauna of [[Big Bend National Park]] reflects the transition between temperate and subtropical ichthyofaunas along the [[Rio Grande]] drainage. The report follows nomenclature from G.A. Moore's "Fishes" in Blair et al.'s "Vertebrates of the United States" and provides alternative references including Eddy's "How to Know the Freshwater Fishes" and Koster's "Guide to the Fishes of New Mexico."

Key taxonomic groups represented include:
- **Sturgeons** (Acipenseridae): [[Scaphirhynchus platorynchus]] (Shovelnose Sturgeon)
- **Gars** (Lepisosteidae): [[Lepisosteus osseus]] (Longnose Gar) - dominant predator
- **Herrings** (Clupeidae): [[Dorosoma cepedianum]] (Gizzard Shad) - major forage species
- **Characins** (Characidae): [[Astyanax fasciatus]] (Banded Tetra)
- **Suckers** (Catostomidae): [[Cycleptus elongatus]], [[Ictiobus bubalus]], [[Carpiodes carpio]], [[Moxostoma congestum]]
- **Minnows** (Cyprinidae): 15 species including [[Notropis]] spp., [[Hybopsis aestivalis]], [[Dionda episcopa]], [[Campostoma ornatum]]
- **Catfishes** (Ictaluridae): [[Ictalurus punctatus]], [[Ictalurus furcatus]], [[Pylodictis olivaris]]
- **Killifishes** (Fundulidae): [[Fundulus zebrinus]]
- **Livebearers** (Poeciliidae): [[Gambusia gaigei]] (endemic), [[Gambusia senilis]], [[Gambusia affinis]]
- **Sunfishes** (Centrarchidae): [[Micropterus salmoides]], [[Lepomis]] spp., [[Chaenobryttus gulosus]]
- **Drums** (Sciaenidae): [[Aplodinotus grunniens]]
- **Cichlids** (Cichlidae): [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]]

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Differentiation:**
The park's aquatic habitats divide into two primary types:
1. **Silty Rio Grande waters**: Support species adapted to turbid conditions including [[Lepisosteus osseus]], [[Dorosoma cepedianum]], [[Cyprinus carpio]], [[Hybognathus placita]], and [[Ictalurus furcatus]]
2. **Clear spring-fed streams**: Support species requiring clear water including [[Astyanax fasciatus]], [[Notropis lutrensis]], [[Dionda episcopa]], and [[Campostoma ornatum]]

**Endemic and Rare Species:**
[[Gambusia gaigei]] (Big Bend Mosquitofish) is restricted to the park and represents a conservation concern. Originally found in a spring near Boquillas (now extinct), the sole remaining native population inhabits [[Graham Ranch Warm Springs]]. The species faces endangerment from introduced [[Gambusia affinis]] (Common Mosquitofish) at the same location. Translocation efforts have established populations at Glenn Springs and another spring near Boquillas.

[[Notropis chihuahua]] (Chihuahua Shiner) records from lower Tornillo and Terlingua creeks and one individual from the Rio Grande at Boquillas represent the only United States records for this species.

**Introduced Species:**
Several species show evidence of introduction: [[Cyprinus carpio]] (native to Asia, widely introduced), [[Fundulus zebrinus]] (likely released as bait), [[Chaenobryttus gulosus]], [[Lepomis microlophus]], and possibly [[Gambusia affinis]] at Graham Ranch.

**Historical Changes:**
The report notes that prior to extensive soil erosion from overgrazing in the watershed, species like [[Notropis amabilis]] (Texas Shiner) probably inhabited the river but are now questionable in silty conditions. [[Scaphirhynchus platorynchus]] reports are mostly pre-1940, suggesting population decline.

**Barriers to Migration:**
[[Anguilla rostrata]] (American Eel) was collected at Castalon in 1954, but Falcon Dam now blocks upstream migration from the Gulf of Mexico, making additional specimens unlikely.

## Key Findings

**Species Richness:** 25 confirmed species, 13 expected species (38 total documented or anticipated)

**Dominant Species by Habitat:**
- **Rio Grande predator**: [[Lepisosteus osseus]] (maximum size near 4 feet)
- **Major forage fish**: [[Dorosoma cepedianum]] (feeds on microscopic organisms)
- **Clear water abundance**: [[Notropis lutrensis]] (abundant in most fish-inhabited waters except Rio Grande)

**Size Ranges:**
- Largest species: [[Ictalurus furcatus]] (over 3 feet), [[Pylodictis olivaris]] (over 3 feet), [[Anguilla rostrata]] (near 5 feet), [[Aplodinotus grunniens]] (near 3 feet)
- Smallest species: [[Gambusia gaigei]] (near 1 inch), various minnows (2-4 inches)

**Conservation Priority:**
[[Gambusia gaigei]] requires immediate conservation attention due to:
- Restriction to single native population at [[Graham Ranch Warm Springs]]
- Threat from introduced [[Gambusia affinis]]
- Previous extinction of Boquillas spring population
- Translocation efforts underway but native stock endangered

**Game Fish Present:**
Confirmed or expected game species include [[Micropterus salmoides]] (Largemouth Bass), [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (Channel Catfish), [[Ictalurus furcatus]] (Blue Catfish), [[Pylodictis olivaris]] (Flathead Catfish), and various sunfishes ([[Lepomis]] spp.)

**Bait Fish:**
[[Astyanax fasciatus]], [[Notropis lutrensis]], [[Dionda episcopa]], and [[Pimephales promelas]] are noted as excellent bait minnows

**Identification Key:**
A comprehensive dichotomous key with 37 numbered couplets enables field identification of all species using morphological characters including fin ray counts, scale counts, body proportions, mouth position, and coloration patterns

**Morphological Terminology:**
Document provides definitions for technical terms (barbel, truncate, midline, adipose fin, spinous ray, soft ray) and includes anatomical diagram of [[Notropis chihuahua]] showing key identification features
