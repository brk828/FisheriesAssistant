# Two Desert Fishes: *Cyprinodon macularius* and *C. nevadensis*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodon

## Executive Summary

This document is a page excerpt from a taxonomic study of two desert pupfishes, [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] and [[Cyprinodon Nevadensis]], published as part of the Occasional Papers series of the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (No. 478, by [[Robert R. Miller]]). The readable text compares meristic characters (lateral-line pore counts, scales, and fin rays) between the two species, and reviews the historical literature and range of *C. macularius*. Much of the scan is garbled, and the tables are largely unreadable, so only the clearly legible content is summarized here.

## Background & Description

*Cyprinodon macularius* was originally described by [[Baird and Girard]] in 1853 from the Rio Gila, Arizona. The synonymy presented in the text spans more than a century of literature, from Girard (1859) through [[Hubbs and Miller]] (1941) and Shapovalov (1941). Records place the species in the Colorado Desert, the Salton Sink, Fish Springs, Figtree John Spring (about 7 miles southwest of Mecca), an artesian well at Mecca, the Gila River at Gila Bend and Adonde, the Sonoyta River, and a spring-fed pond at Lerdo, Sonora. Earlier authors described its tolerance of high water temperature and salinity, its breeding habitat, and its predators (Lanier 1939; Coleman 1929).

The paper compares the two species using counts of:
- **Lateral-line pores** on the head, counted on both sides. These are the most useful character in the text, though the two species overlap widely.
- **Scales and fin rays**, which are very similar in both species. Most counts average slightly lower in *C. nevadensis*.
- **Pelvic fin rays**, summarized in Table III for six separate populations in and around the Salton Sea.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Populations in well-isolated springs and creeks show a distinct set of reduced traits. These relic populations have:
- fewer lateral-line pores (preopercular and lachrymal),
- reduced pelvic fins, both in fin size and ray number,
- a more posterior dorsal fin, and
- scale irregularities.

Trends like these appear in isolated stocks of *C. macularius*. None of the *C. macularius* races examined, however, shows these traits to the degree characteristic of [[Cyprinodon Nevadensis]]. The text links the pattern to the isolation of desert spring habitats, which are commonly referred to as [[Isolated Desert Springs]].

## Key Findings

- **Preopercular pores:** The average is 14.22 in 656 specimens of *C. macularius* (all populations; usually 7 per side). The average is 12.71 in 651 specimens of *C. nevadensis*. The range is 9–19 in *C. macularius* and 10–16 in *C. nevadensis*, so the variability is much greater in *C. macularius*.
- **Low pore counts:** The lowest counts in *C. macularius* were recorded only from populations in well-isolated springs and creeks.
- **Lachrymal pores:** A similar but weaker reduction trend appears in these counts.
- **Scales and fin rays:** Counts are very similar between species, with *C. nevadensis* averaging slightly lower on many counts.
- **Table III (pelvic fin rays):** Summarizes pelvic fin ray variation across six separate populations in and about the Salton Sea. The scan is too degraded to transcribe the values reliably, so specific counts are not reported here.
- **Table II:** Appears to present measurement or distance data for the two species, but the OCR is largely illegible and no values are cited.
- **Taxonomic history:** *C. macularius* has a long, complex synonymy and range history, with many literature records from the Colorado River basin and the Salton Sea region.
