# Distribution and Taxonomic Status of the Stoneroller, *Campostoma anomalum*, in Illinois

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Campostoma

## Executive Summary

This study by Brooks M. Burr (1976), based on examination of more than 1,000 specimens housed at the Illinois Natural History Survey, resolves the taxonomic status and geographic distribution of the [[Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma anomalum*) in [[Illinois]] and adjacent regions. Two subspecies are confirmed to occur: the nominate **[[Campostoma anomalum anomalum]]** ("Ohio Stoneroller") and **[[Campostoma anomalum pullum]]** ("Central Stoneroller"). Contrary to prior assumptions (Gerking 1945) that restricted *C. a. anomalum* to the Whitewater drainage of southeastern Indiana, Burr demonstrates that this subspecies extends westward through Kentucky into direct Ohio River tributaries of southeastern Illinois. The two subspecies intergrade in a narrow contact zone, while *C. a. pullum* occupies the remainder of the upper Mississippi Valley with notable meristic uniformity and no evidence of clinal variation.

## Background & Description

Diagnostic separation between subspecies relies primarily on **meristic (scale and gill raker) counts** and **gape width**, as the two taxa lack meaningful differences in tuberculation, fin pigmentation, breeding/nonbreeding coloration, pharyngeal tooth counts, fin ray counts, or cephalic lateral line features.

### *Campostoma anomalum anomalum* (Ohio Stoneroller)
- Lateral line scales: 45–51 (usually 46–50)
- Body circumferential scales: 34–41 (usually 36–39)
- Scales above lateral line: usually 15–17
- Predorsal scales: 18–23 (usually 19–20)
- Sum of body circumferential + lateral line scales: 83–91 (usually 83–88)
- Gape width (thousandths of SL, non-breeding): range 44–60, mean = 55
- Gill rakers on first arch: range 21–29, mean = 24.9 (n=14)

### *Campostoma anomalum pullum* (Central Stoneroller)
- Lateral line scales: 46–63 (usually 48–55)
- Body circumferential scales: 36–50 (usually 38–47)
- Scales above lateral line: usually 18–20
- Predorsal scales: 20–27 (usually 20–26)
- Sum of body circumferential + lateral line scales: 85–108 (usually 87–100)
- Gape width (thousandths of SL, non-breeding): range 44–58, mean = 50
- Gill rakers on first arch: range 26–35, mean = 30.0 (n=31)

### Intergrades (*pullum* × *anomalum*)
- Gape width: range 40–55, mean = 47
- Gill rakers on first arch: range 27–32, mean = 29.3 (n=18)

### Diagnostic Index
A threshold of 38–39 body circumferential scales separates 97.9% of *pullum* from 78.4% of *anomalum*, yielding an average index of divergence ([[Ginsburg Index]], 1954) of **88.1%** — indicating that scale counts, particularly body circumferential scales, are the most reliable non-geographic diagnostic character. Gape width differences diminish in breeding individuals, reducing its diagnostic reliability during spawning season.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat overlap**: Both subspecies occupy broadly similar habitat types, but subtle ecological divergence is noted.
- ***C. a. anomalum***: Interpreted as a more **montane form**, primarily restricted to the Ohio Valley and Appalachian Mountain streams (per [[Ross 1952]]). In Illinois, it is confined to direct Ohio River tributaries in Hardin, Pope, Johnson, and Massac counties — a disjunct distribution pattern shared with other upland-associated fishes such as [[Hypentelium nigricans]], [[Ambloplites rupestris]], and [[Micropterus dolomieui]].
- ***C. a. pullum***: A more **western, generalist form** tolerant of varied bottom types and labile ecological conditions (per [[Cross 1967]]; [[Burr and Smith 1976]]). It occupies nearly all major Illinois drainages except a hiatus in south-central Illinois (north of the Shawnee Hills), attributed to a lack of suitable habitat. Habitat availability for *pullum* may have paradoxically increased in Illinois due to stream degradation converting formerly large, stable streams into smaller, shallower habitats resembling headwater conditions.
- **Migration**: Stonerollers are known to migrate extensively ([[Metcalf 1959]]), facilitating gene flow and intergradation in contact-zone streams.
- **Zoogeographic note**: Some downstream intergrade populations may result from either active gene exchange via migration, or from historical **Mississippi River invasion** into the ancient (pre-Wisconsin) Ohio River course.

## Key Findings

1. **Two valid subspecies** of *Campostoma anomalum* occur in Illinois: *C. a. anomalum* and *C. a. pullum*, distinguishable chiefly by scale counts and gape width.
2. *C. a. anomalum* extends **farther west than previously recognized**, reaching Ohio River tributaries in southeastern Illinois and western Kentucky, refuting Gerking's (1945) claim that it was restricted to the Whitewater drainage of Indiana.
3. A **narrow intergradation zone** exists between the two subspecies, documented in the Cache River, Mississippi River tributaries in Alexander County, and Ohio River tributaries in Pulaski, Massac, and Pope counties (IL) and McCracken and Ballard counties (KY).
4. *C. a. pullum* populations show **remarkable morphological uniformity** across the upper Mississippi River valley, with **no evidence of clinal variation**, consistent with findings from Missouri ([[Pflieger 1971]]) and the upper Mississippi region ([[Burr and Smith 1976]]).
5. A distributional **hiatus** for *C. a. pullum* exists in south-central Illinois north of the Shawnee Hills, likely due to unsuitable habitat.
6. Gill raker counts on the first arch provide an additional reliable diagnostic character, with *anomalum* showing notably fewer rakers (mean 24.9) than *pullum* (mean 30.0).
7. The disjunct southern Illinois distribution of *C. a. anomalum* parallels patterns seen in other Ohio River tributary specialist fishes, supporting a shared biogeographic history tied to relict Ohio Valley drainage connections.
