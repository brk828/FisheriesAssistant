# Information Letter No. 6: Lower Colorado River Drainage System Endangered Fishes Recovery Team

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative "Information Letter" (No. 6) issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, dated 1973, addressed to the [[Lower Colorado River Drainage System Endangered Fishes Recovery Team]]. The letter announces a recovery team meeting in Needles, California, and details two primary administrative action items: the review of the first draft of a recovery plan for the [[Razorback Sucker]], and the urgent allocation of federal funds ($1,500 and an additional $1,000) toward acquiring brood stock for the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically referred to as "squawfish"). While not a data-driven scientific paper, the letter provides critical historical context regarding the earliest federal recovery planning efforts for these two endangered Colorado River endemic fishes.

## Background & Description
The letter originates from the Division of Fishery Services in Parker, Arizona, and pertains to the operational logistics of the Recovery Team tasked with the conservation of endangered fish species native to the Lower Colorado River Basin.

Two species are explicitly named:
1. **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): A large-bodied catostomid fish endemic to the Colorado River Basin, distinguished by a sharp-edged keel behind the head. A draft recovery plan was authored in two parts—the initial section by Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]] and the remainder by the letter's author (likely the Recovery Team Coordinator).
2. **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, referred to in the historical text by the now-retired common name "squawfish"): The largest native cyprinid (minnow family) in North America. The letter focuses on securing "brood stock" (breeding-age adults) for what appears to be an early captive propagation or hatchery-support program.

The document references key figures in Southwestern ichthyology, including Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]] of Arizona State University and Dr. [[Clair Stalnaker]] of Utah State University, both of whom were consulted regarding fish acquisition and fund allocation strategies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The document underscores the administrative and funding mechanisms underlying species recovery efforts in the Lower Colorado River Drainage during the early 1970s, a period concurrent with the passage of the Endangered Species Act (1973). The mention of a "step-down planning" approach reflects an early, evolving methodology for structuring formal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery plans—a hierarchical framework (Objective > Sub-objective > Task) that would later become standardized practice for federal recovery plans.

The urgency to "obligate" federal funds by a fiscal deadline (June 15th/16th) highlights the logistical constraints on conservation science tied to government fiscal years, directly influencing field actions such as the timing and sourcing of live brood stock for the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]. The reliance on academic partners (Arizona State University, Utah State University) for both fish acquisition and scientific expertise illustrates the collaborative government-university model foundational to early [[Endangered Species Recovery Planning]] for Colorado River fishes.

## Key Findings
- A **Recovery Team meeting** was scheduled for **June 6, 1973**, in Needles, California, to discuss the draft [[Razorback Sucker]] Recovery Plan and fund allocation for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] brood stock acquisition.
- The first draft of the **[[Razorback Sucker]] Recovery Plan** was co-authored by Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]] (first part) and the Recovery Team coordinator (remainder); the Albuquerque regional office flagged the "step-down planning" section as needing further refinement.
- **$1,500** in federal funds was earmarked specifically for the acquisition of **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** ("squawfish") brood stock, to be obtained with assistance from Dr. [[Clair Stalnaker]] at Utah State University.
- An **additional $1,000** in funding was made available, with Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]] of Arizona State University tasked with proposing how to allocate it, to be presented at the June 6 meeting.
- All funds were required to be formally **obligated by June 15–16, 1973**, reflecting the fiscal-year-driven constraints on early endangered species conservation funding.
- The letter provides documentary evidence of active, coordinated **captive brood stock programs** for the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] as early as 1973, predating many formal hatchery propagation efforts publicized in later decades.
