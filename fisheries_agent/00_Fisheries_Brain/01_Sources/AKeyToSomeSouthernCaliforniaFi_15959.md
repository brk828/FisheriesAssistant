# A Key to Some Southern California Fishes Based on Vertebral Characters

#ichthyology #marine-biology #vertebral-morphology

## Executive Summary

Fish Bulletin No. 79 by [[Charles R. Clothier]] (1950), published by the [[California Division of Fish and Game]] through its [[Bureau of Marine Fisheries]], presents a dichotomous identification key for 163 teleost fish species (excluding Elasmobranchii) found between Point Conception and San Diego. The key is based on vertebral characters, chiefly total vertebra count including the [[Urostyle]], the vertebra bearing the [[First Haemal Spine]], and the vertebra bearing the [[First Haemal Arch]]. It is intended to aid identification of larval fish, stomach-content fragments, and fossil fish, where external features are unavailable. The document also includes a glossary of skeletal terms, species descriptions with specimen counts and meristic ranges, a literature list, a clearing-and-staining protocol, and a catalogue of earlier Fish Bulletins.

## Background & Description

The study began in 1938 and was conducted with material from the [[California State Fisheries Laboratory]], [[Hopkins Marine Station]], [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]], and San Pedro wholesale fish markets. Specimens were mainly fresh adults lightly boiled to free flesh from bone; preserved material was cleared and stained using a modified [[Hollister Clearing and Staining Technique]] (alizarin in a potassium hydroxide and glycerine series).

Methodological conventions include:
- Skeletal nomenclature follows [[Starks 1901]], with the terminal fan-like tail plate termed the [[Urostyle]] rather than "hypural."
- Abdominal versus caudal vertebrae are separated following [[Hubbs and Lagler 1947]].
- Fin counts use Roman numerals for spines and Arabic numerals for soft rays; the last two rays are counted separately unless they share a base.
- Vertebral length is measured from the atlas-skull joint to the farthest extension of the urostyle.

Key structural terms defined in the glossary include [[Haemal Arch]], [[Haemal Spine]], [[Haemapophyses]], [[Neural Arch]], [[Parapophyses]], [[Zygapophyses]], [[Supraoccipital]], and [[Atlas]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is primarily taxonomic and morphological. It notes that specimens came from a range of coastal habitats, including [[Salton Sea]], [[San Pedro Bay]], [[Newport Bay]], [[Monterey Bay]], [[Santa Catalina Island]], [[Santa Cruz Island]], [[Gulf of California]], and Lower California localities such as [[Magdalena Bay]] and [[Guaymas]]. Some species are noted as non-native or occasional visitors, for example [[Threadfin]] (*Polydactylus approximans*), which was probably released from tuna clipper live-bait tanks. Ecological notes are limited to a few behavioural observations, such as the tide-controlled spawning of [[Grunion]] (*Leuresthes tenuis*), which is referenced as a related publication.

The author cautions that vertebral form may change between juvenile and adult stages (e.g., [[Clevelandia ios]]), which may affect diagnostic reliability for small or young fish.

## Key Findings

- **Purpose and scope:** A vertebral key covering 163 species from Point Conception to San Diego; further units for areas north and south were planned.
- **Core diagnostic characters:** Total vertebrae (including urostyle), the vertebra of the first haemal spine (HS), and the vertebra of the first haemal arch (HA). Fin counts were included only where necessary.
- **Special structures recognised:**
  - [[Haemal Funnel]] in *Atherinops*, *Atherinopsis* and *Leuresthes* (begins 6 to 10 vertebrae before the first haemal spine).
  - Indeterminate first haemal spine in *Thunnus*, *Neothunnus*, *Sarda*, *Oncorhynchus* and *Albula vulpes*, where a "sudden increase in length" is used instead.
  - Two superimposed arches in *Pimelometopon pulchrum*.
  - Jointed upper urostyle lobe in *Porichthys* (counted as one vertebra).
  - Very small atlas in *Cetengraulis mysticetus*.
- **Vertebral range examples (specimen counts as reported):**
  - *Trachurus symmetricus*: 560 specimens from Southern California and 256 from Monterey Bay; total vertebrae 23 (1), 24 (813), 25 (2).
  - *Pneumatophorus diego*: over 1000 specimens; total vertebrae 31 (typical), very rarely 30 or 32.
  - *Sebastodes* group: 39 specimens of multiple species; total vertebrae 26 (38) and 27 (1).
  - *Sardinops caerulea*: 10 specimens from Oceanside to San Pedro; known vertebral range 49–54.
  - *Clupea pallasii*: known vertebral range for Southern California 49–53.
- **Notable extremes:** *Anarrhichthys ocellatus* (Wolf-eel) with about 242 vertebrae (one specimen, HS 39); *Gymnothorax mordax* (Moray) with 146–150 vertebrae; *Trachypterus rex-salmonorum* with 92 vertebrae.
- **Method notes:** The author acknowledges the key is weakest where characters are described in general terms (e.g., "a large bulge in the prootic region") and where young-adult vertebral changes may alter diagnoses.
- **Bulletin catalogue:** Appended list of California Fish Bulletins Nos. 1–86 (1913–1950), several marked out of print, including Bulletin No. 60, [[Godsil and Byers 1944]], on Pacific tunas, cited within this key.
