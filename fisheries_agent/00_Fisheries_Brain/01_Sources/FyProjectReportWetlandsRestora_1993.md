# Wetlands Restoration/Demonstration Project at Old Charley Wash, Ouray NWR, Utah (FY93)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #wetland-restoration

## Executive Summary
This FY93 progress report describes an ongoing habitat restoration and demonstration project at **Old Charley Wash (OCW)**, a flooded bottomland complex adjacent to the Green River within Ouray National Wildlife Refuge, Utah. The project is part of the **Colorado River Recovery Program** (Recovery Program #18-12) and targets the recovery of the endangered **[[Razorback Sucker]]** by restoring critical bottomland habitat used by both juvenile and adult life stages. A secondary conservation benefit involves documented use of the site by the endangered **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (historically termed "Colorado squawfish"). The core engineering objective — constructing a water-control and fish-access weir/structure at the wash's outflow ("Woods' Bottom") — experienced significant delays due to underfunding, failed bid processes, and administrative errors, with construction costs (~$87,160) far exceeding original estimates ($35,000). Preliminary biological monitoring in spring 1993 documented adult Colorado pikeminnow in an adjacent flooded bottomland (Lake Wyasket) and putative larval razorback sucker at the wash's inflow/outflow, supporting the hypothesis that such managed bottomlands provide valuable nursery and adult habitat. However, incomplete drainage infrastructure resulted in fish stranding and avian predation losses during fall drawdown, limiting the ability to fully evaluate underyearling habitat use this cycle.

## Background & Description
This report is a wetland habitat-restoration and management document rather than a morphological study; it does not provide direct morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data for the species discussed. The only life-stage-specific references pertain to:
- **[[Razorback Sucker]] larvae**: Suspected larval razorback sucker were collected at both inflow and outflow sites of Old Charley Wash, indicating in-situ reproduction or larval drift/retention within the wetland.
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]] adults**: Adult individuals were collected in Lake Wyasket, a large flooded bottomland adjacent to OCW, indicating adult habitat use of connected floodplain waters.
- **Underyearling fish** (unspecified species, presumably including razorback sucker and/or Colorado pikeminnow) were noted as present in shallow residual pool water during fall drawdown, prior to being stranded and depredated.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Seasonally flooded bottomland/backwater wetland connected to the Green River mainstem, functioning as off-channel nursery and rearing habitat for endangered Upper Colorado River Basin fishes.
- **Life-stage habitat use**:
  - *Adult razorback sucker* and *adult Colorado pikeminnow* utilize flooded bottomlands (e.g., OCW, Lake Wyasket) seasonally, likely for foraging and/or spawning-adjacent staging.
  - *Larval/underyearling razorback sucker* appear to use the wash inflow/outflow as rearing or transit habitat, consistent with known reliance of this species on floodplain habitats for early life-stage development ([[Razorback Sucker Recovery]]).
- **Water management regime**: The wetland is manipulated via water control structures (dikes, weirs, pumps) to control water elevation, mimicking natural flood-drought bottomland cycles historically provided by the unregulated Green River hydrograph.
- **Food resources**: 1991 data on food resources and razorback sucker growth were collected (though not detailed numerically in this report) to inform diking/habitat design decisions — implying trophic productivity of diked vs. undiked areas was a design consideration.
- **Predation pressure**: Fish stranded in residual shallow pools during incomplete fall drainage were subject to significant mortality from **avian predation**, highlighting an ecological risk associated with drawdown management in incompletely graded wetlands.
- **Connectivity**: The site's function depends on controlled connectivity to the Green River mainstem via a fish access/water control weir, enabling monitored ingress/egress of adult fish and evaluation of underyearling use through seasonal draining.

## Key Findings
- **Species detections (1993)**: 
  - Adult **Colorado pikeminnow** collected in Lake Wyasket (adjacent flooded bottomland) by Utah State University (USU) personnel, spring 1993.
  - Suspected larval **razorback sucker** collected at both inflow and outflow of Old Charley Wash, indicating potential reproductive/nursery use of the wetland.
- **Infrastructure delays**: Construction of the fish access/water control structure was delayed across three bid cycles (fall 1992, March 1993 — voided due to administrative error, and July 1993) before being awarded to Tri West Construction for $87,160 (more than double the original $35,000 estimate).
- **Cost overruns**: Total actual costs ($32,500 pump + $87,160 construction) vastly exceeded the original budget allocation ($24,000 pump + $35,000 structure), attributed to unrealistic initial cost estimates.
- **Design compromise**: To reduce contract costs, the winch apparatus for the fish cage and the dredging/grading of the drainage canal were excluded from the construction contract — a decision with direct negative ecological consequences.
- **Stranding/mortality event**: Fall 1993 drawdown of Old Charley Wash left a large residual pool in the deepest area (due to the ungraded drainage canal), stranding large numbers of fish, which were subsequently consumed by avian predators over several weeks — compromising evaluation of underyearling habitat benefit.
- **Monitoring strategy proposed**: Future evaluation should use the completed fish access trap to monitor adult use, combined with fall drainage events to assess underyearling use, contingent on completing drainage canal grading.
- **Budget summary (FY93)**: Funds provided: $20,000; Funds expended: $87,160; Deficit ($67,160) covered by ~$25,600 carryover from prior year and ~$41,560 from the Bureau of Reclamation Capital Improvements Project.
- **Recommendation**: Complete drainage canal grading over winter using Capital Improvement Program funds to enable proper evaluation of the site's value to underyearling endangered fishes, followed by systematic monitoring of both adult (spring) and underyearling (spring–fall) use of [[Old Charley Wash]].
- **Broader relevance**: Findings are intended to inform criteria for **future bottomland acquisition and restoration** decisions across the Upper Colorado River Basin under the **[[Colorado River Recovery Program]]**, with specific coordination noted with concurrent **[[Flaming Gorge]]** studies.
