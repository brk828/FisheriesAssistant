# Semipermeable Species Boundaries Permit Gene Flow but Prevent a Hybrid Swarm among Sympatric Catostomids of a Desert Ecosystem

#ichthyology #catostomidae

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** Three native catostomids form a hybridizing assemblage in the San Juan River: bluehead sucker (*Catostomus discobolus*), flannelmouth sucker (*C. latipinnis*), and razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Razorback sucker is endangered and was extirpated from the river in the 1990s; hatchery-reared fish were reintroduced and reproduce annually. Fish were assigned to four life stages using total length (TL) and development: larvae (developing young-of-year), juveniles (<100 mm TL post-metamorphosis), age 1+ (>100 mm TL but <250 mm bluehead, <350 mm flannelmouth, <400 mm razorback), and adults (above these thresholds; hybrids >400 mm TL only). Age, maturity size, fecundity, and growth parameters: Not reported (the authors state hybrid growth rates remain uncertain).

**Habitat Associations:** Samples came from the San Juan River between river km −22.5 and 238, including the Piute Farms Waterfall (km 0) and the San Juan Arm of Lake Powell downstream. Spawning beds shared by razorback and flannelmouth suckers occur in the Colorado River Basin. Habitat-specific data beyond sampling locations: Not reported.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** Movement and migration data: Not reported. Spawning behavior is described from the literature: male catostomids typically hold territories above spawning beds, while females enter to spawn with multiple males. Flannelmouth sucker spawning has been recorded at 9–18 °C.

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** Trophic data: Not reported. Community interactions are framed as hybridization: among 1128 genotyped individuals, 346 were hybrids, and 98.8% of hybrids were flannelmouth × razorback crosses (N = 342). Bluehead × flannelmouth and bluehead × razorback hybrids were rare (4 bluehead hybrids in total). The authors did not examine competition for resources or wasted reproductive effort.

**Environmental Drivers:** Hybridization outcomes are linked to community composition and habitat type. Flow and temperature regimes were altered by large dams and reservoirs. Cold-water dam releases (16 °C) may delay razorback larval development, and the authors suggest late-fall or winter conditions may affect juvenile survival.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** Razorback sucker reproduces annually, yet recruitment is exceptionally rare. Non-admixed razorback sucker are abundant at TL ≤90 mm and ≥480 mm but rarely found from 90 to 360 mm. Adult razorback sucker are described as mostly hatchery-origin. Overall population size and trend estimates: Not reported.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** Principal concerns are a recruitment bottleneck and potential genetic swamping. The authors conclude that displacement of razorback sucker by hybridization is unlikely under current conditions and stocking regimes, so the lack of recruitment remains the larger concern. Flannelmouth × razorback hybrids were regularly encountered as adults, with F1 hybrids most common (68.4%). Genetic swamping risk is low (see below), but ecological impacts of hybrids were not evaluated.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations:** The authors' management insights are:
- Target the recruitment bottleneck, which likely affects juveniles ≤90 mm TL during late summer through winter.
- Continue hatchery stocking at ≥300 mm TL, the size at which razorback are currently stocked.
- Monitor thermal regimes at critical growth periods, since modified temperatures may reduce young-of-year survival.
- Investigate larval thermal tolerance of flannelmouth sucker and hybrids, which remains unexamined.
- Assess other impacts of hybrids (competition, wasted reproductive effort), which were not studied.

**Biological Reference Points:**
- Simulated hybrid classification accuracy: >98% for each species pair (bluehead × flannelmouth 98.3%; bluehead × razorback 99.1%; flannelmouth × razorback 98.6%).
- Hybrid generation composition (N = 346): F1 68.4% (N = 234), third generation 28.4% (N = 97), second generation 3.2% (N = 11).
- Razorback mtDNA in 92.7% of hybrids; 95.7% (224/234) of F1 hybrids; 54.5% of second-generation hybrids.
- Genomic cline outliers: 72 of 2500 SNPs (2.9%); 9 α-outliers favoring flannelmouth, 2 favoring razorback, and 61 β-outliers on chromosome 2 indicating heterozygote advantage.
- Recombination sensitivity: α-outliers fell from 69 (0.558 cM/Mb) to 3 (2.404 cM/Mb); chromosome 2 β-outliers were robust.
