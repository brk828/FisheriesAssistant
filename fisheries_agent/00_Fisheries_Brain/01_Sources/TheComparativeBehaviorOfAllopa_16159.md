# The Comparative Behavior of Allopatric Pupfish Species (*Cyprinodon*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 UCLA doctoral dissertation by Robert Kung-shan Liu compares the courtship, agonistic and reproductive behavior of geographically isolated pupfishes of the genus [[Cyprinodon]]. The study tests whether morphological divergence among allopatric forms implies sufficient genetic divergence to produce reproductive isolation. Forty-five forms were observed in the laboratory and field, yielding 1,274 observations on roughly 2,000 specimens. Behavior is uniform within species-complexes but differs among them, especially in courtship, while hybridization occurs readily. The author concludes there is no complete genetic or reproductive isolation within or between adjacent superspecies, and that the many nominal allopatric species likely represent allopatric semispecies within several superspecies.

## Background & Description

The genus contains 26 nominal and unnamed species, most allopatric, with hybridization or introgression reported at two of four sympatric localities. The author recognized 13 species, 6 subspecies, 13 populations, 19 hybrid forms and one form of uncertain parentage in the laboratory work. Specimens were collected by seine, trap and dip-net, and maintained in aquaria under controlled photoperiod (14 to 16 hour) and temperature (ambient about 22 to 31°C).

Pupfish phenotype varies with environment: Sweet and Kinne (1964) showed phenotypic plasticity in [[Salton Sea]] macularius embryos under different temperatures and salinities. Males display a banded, striped, pale or barred pigment system that darkens during aggression and courtship, while females and juveniles lack the banded pattern.

Behavior was classified using the units of Nelson (1964), with motor patterns grouped into feeding, resting, agonistic, and reproductive categories. Each form was tested in up to seven situations, including previously isolated conspecific and heterospecific pairs, non-isolated stock tanks, isolated males, and the field. Recording used high-speed 16 mm cine film and a written code.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitats range from fresh to hypersaline water and from constant-temperature desert springs to shallow waters with wide seasonal swings. The [[Death Valley]] and [[Colorado River Basin]] systems, [[Cuatro Cienegas]] in Coahuila, and the Gulf and Atlantic coastal plain all host forms. Key environmental observations include:

- Temperature ranges up to 8 to 42°C within a single biotope (Taylor and Minckley, 1966); pupfish are active from 7 to 42°C (Deacon, 1968).
- Reproduction is thought not to occur below about 18°C or above about 25°C, though embryos develop as low as 13°C.
- Waters containing pupfish typically have high calcium concentrations (>90 ppm) and soft sand, silt or mud substrates.
- Species associates range from 0 to 12 native and introduced fishes, depending on region; the [[Variegatus Complex]] shares coastal habitats with up to seven cyprinodontoid species.
- Introduced poeciliids and exotic fishes are considered a threat to native cyprinodontoids, and several forms are extinct or threatened through habitat alteration.

The genus was present in the Death Valley basin by the early Pleistocene, based on a single late Pliocene or early Pleistocene fossil. Pluvial lakes during the Quaternary likely connected drainages that are now disjunct.

## Key Findings

**Behavioral repertoire**
- 49 behavioral actions were observed with enough regularity for comparison across 43 forms, grouped into four categories: feeding (3), resting (5), agonistic (15) and reproductive (courtship 17, spawning 7, other reproductive 2).
- Resting, agonistic, spawning and feeding behavior is largely uniform across forms; plowing is absent in some forms, possibly reflecting limited observations.

**Courtship differences among species-complexes**
- The [[Variegatus Complex]] relies mainly on visual displays such as looping.
- The [[Eximius Complex]] combines visual displays with tactile stimulation.
- The [[Macularius Complex]] shows decreasing visual displays and increasing tactile contact.
- The [[Nevadensis Complex]] has little or no courtship; when present, it is tactile.
- Forms of undetermined affinity (e.g., bif, dia, ele) rely predominantly on visual display.

**Observations and matings**
- 1,274 observations were made: isolated conspecific pairs 470, isolated heterospecific pairs 367, stock conspecific 299, stock heterospecific 56, isolated conspecific males 23, isolated heterospecific males 32, and field 27.
- Of roughly 1,558 possible crosses, 167 mating combinations were attempted (33 unsuccessful, 59 successful, 75 mixed).
- Forty-two crosses produced one to three filial generations, yielding one intraspecific and 18 interspecific hybrid types.
- Courtship occurred in about 70% of isolated trials; it was largely absent from stock tanks and the field.
- Most hybrids showed intermediate behavior. Some male hybrids were sterile, but most were fertile, especially between adjacent superspecies.

**Conclusions**
- The large number of nominal allopatric species likely represent allopatric semispecies belonging to several superspecies.
- No complete genetic or reproductive isolation exists within or between adjacent superspecies.
- Female pupfish largely determine mating; males respond sexually to many stimuli, and female recognition of males depends more on behavior than on color.
- Pupfish show a pod-spawning-like mating system in which multiple males occasionally spawn with one female.

**Methodological caveats**
- Isolation and staged encounters may exaggerate aggression and courtship compared with the field, but the author found that field behaviors were largely reproduced in the laboratory.
- Sample sizes were uneven, so many apparent omissions are considered artifacts of too few observations.
