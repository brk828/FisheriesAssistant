# FY-1990 Work Plan: Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is the fiscal year (FY) 1990 work plan and budget for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin (Recovery Program). It was issued by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 6, Denver) on August 30, 1989, and approved the same day by the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]]. FY-90 began October 1, 1989. The plan covers management, habitat, propagation, nonnative fish control, research, and monitoring for four rare fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]].

The Implementation Committee's recommended budget is $2.39M, a 4.1 percent increase over the FY-89 budget of $2.3M. The detailed work plan totals $2,889.7 thousand across 44 projects, with 3 contingent projects ($31.1 thousand) listed separately.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program was signed in January 1988. Its purpose is to recommend priorities for spending under six recovery elements:

- Program management
- [[Habitat Management]] (defining and acquiring flows)
- Habitat development (jetties, fish passage, etc.)
- Stocking native fishes
- [[Nonnative Fish Control]] and sportfishing control
- Research, monitoring, and data management

The Committee's guide allocation (1988 dollars) totals $2,300,000. It gives $1,000,000 to habitat management, $450,000 to research, monitoring, and data management, $400,000 to habitat development, $245,000 to stocking, $105,000 to nonnative and sportfishing control, and $100,000 to program management.

**Work plan process:** The [[Technical Group]] ranked about 75 submitted proposals. Criteria included consistency with the Recovery Program, urgency (to avoid jeopardy), whether the work is essential for recovery, timeliness, likelihood of success, relationship to other priority work, opportunity to act now, and proposal quality. The FY-90 program guidance was a new step in this process. The [[Management Group]] reviewed the Technical Group's draft, and the Committee gave final approval. The goal was to have all activities contracted and funded by the end of the first quarter of FY-90.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan is built around flow management, because the four rare fishes depend on river flows, backwater nursery habitat, and instream conditions. Key issues it identifies:

- **Flow regulation:** [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations affect Green River fishes. The biological opinion on Flaming Gorge is a major FY-90 effort. Water acquisition for instream flows is a priority in the [[Yampa River]] and the [[15-Mile Reach]] of the Colorado River. Flows in the [[Aspinall Units]] area (Gunnison River) also require Section 7 consultation.
- **Biotic interactions:** Predation and competition with warmwater game fish and nonnative species are considered possible limiting factors. The plan notes that suitable physical and chemical habitat alone may not lead to recovery.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty:** Questions about the taxonomy of the [[Gila Taxonomy]] complex (humpback, bonytail, and roundtail chub) and possible hybridization complicate recovery planning.
- **Disease and genetics:** The plan lists Asian tapeworm occurrence in hatchery and wild stocks, and genetic variability in razorback sucker populations, as issues to study.
- **Habitat restoration:** Options include backwater nursery habitat, spawning habitat, grow-out ponds, and access to historic migration routes. The [[Dolores River]] is being assessed for the return of native fishes as flows and water quality improve.

- The Committee's recommended total ($2.39M) and the Section 3 total ($2,889.7K) differ. The document does not reconcile them.

**Major emphases**

1. **[[Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion]]:** An interagency Section 7 team is preparing an "Integrated Technical Report." The draft biological opinion is due March 15, 1990.
2. **[[Water Rights Acquisition]]:** Priorities are the Yampa River and the 15-mile reach. Actions include interim year-round flow recommendations, an evaluation of 15-mile reach supply options (early 1990), a reservoir feasibility study in the Yampa Basin (50,000 to 150,000 acre-feet of storage), and an evaluation of Little Snake River water rights. [[The Nature Conservancy]] was authorized to pursue senior rights on the lower Yampa.
3. **Propagation and rearing:** This implements the 3-year plan approved in FY-89. Tasks include genetics management, facility criteria, refugia recommendations, and razorback and squawfish broodstock development. A [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] hatchery feasibility study ($312.4K) is funded outside the Recovery Program.
4. **Gila taxonomy:** The [[Smithsonian Institution]] reviewed Gila information in FY-89. FY-90 work implements its high-priority study needs and begins field surveys of intraspecific variation.

**New initiatives**

- Flow effects on backwater nursery habitat and young-of-the-year Colorado squawfish survival
- Dolores River habitat suitability for stocking or nonnative control
- Discussion paper on nonnative fish management options
- Aspinall Units Section 7 work plan
- A process for quantitative recovery goals

**Ongoing activities**

- Standardized monitoring and data management
- [[Game Fish and Endangered Fish Interactions]] studies
- Colorado squawfish sportfishery at [[Kenney Reservoir]]
- Information and education program
- Creel census for incidental capture by anglers

**Section 7 funds**

- About 60 biological opinions (1981 to January 1988) created a conservation fund. Under the Recovery Program, water project sponsors pay a one-time $10/acre-foot depletion charge (FY-90: $10.41/acre-foot), paid to the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] under a June 14, 1989 Cooperative Agreement.
- Expended from FY-81 to date: $792,000 ($718,000 before July 1985). Currently available: $492,914. Outstanding: about $2,980,000, though much may never be received.
- Earmarked: $319,000 for the Redlands fish passageway, and $899,000 for Williams Fork and Two Forks water rights acquisition.
- The Committee recommended reserving Section 7 funds for water rights acquisition and related work. Other uses require case-by-case approval by the Management Group.

**Recommendations from the August 30, 1989 meeting**

- Prepare 5-year "road maps" for each of the five recovery elements (habitat management, habitat development, stocking, nonnative and sportfishing control, and research/monitoring/data), to be updated annually.
- Prepare summary reports on each FY-89 project, covering accomplishments, next-year recommendations, and expenditures.
- Several members wanted more FY-90 emphasis on water acquisition, so the Management Group was directed to identify additional acquisition-expediting activities.

**Project list (Table 3.1 and Appendix 6.2)**

- 44 projects in Table 3.1, mostly categorized as program management, habitat management, habitat development, stocking, nonnative control, research/monitoring, and other. Three "other" projects are the Flaming Gorge tailwater trout study, the San Juan River Management Plan (outside the Recovery Program's geographic scope), and a Razorback Sucker Management Plan.
- Three contingent projects in Table 3.2 total $31.1K: Squawfish Age and Growth Study ($8.9K), Environmental Baseline for Upper Colorado River Fishes ($11.5K), and Trophic Dynamics of Colorado River Fishes ($10.7K).
- Appendix 6.2 lists cost, lead agency, and dates for each project, with overhead of 17.65 percent on Reclamation funds transferred to the Service.
