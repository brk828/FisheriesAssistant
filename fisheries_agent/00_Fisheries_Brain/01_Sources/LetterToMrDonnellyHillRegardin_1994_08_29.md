## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 technical letter from ichthyologist W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) to Donnelly Hill, presenting a preliminary hierarchical biogeographic classification of freshwater fish faunas across the western coterminous United States, developed for the [[ECOMAP Project]]. The analysis treats river/drainage systems as "taxa" and fish species presence/absence as "characters" in a cladistic framework, supplemented by phenetic analysis and traditional faunal comparison ("gestalt"). The central finding is that the **[[Colorado River Basin]]** ichthyofauna is the most distinctive drainage system in western North America and warrants status as a separate top-level "Division," while all other western drainages are grouped into a second Division comprising six major Provinces (Bonneville, Lahontan, Death Valley, Sacramento/San Joaquin, Oregon Lakes, and Columbia). The classification is explicitly labeled an **untested hypothesis** pending full quantitative cladistic/phenetic analysis, based on compiled fish-occurrence matrices (compiled with Liza Bizios, Tonto National Forest).

## Background & Description

This letter does not describe individual species morphology in detail but references key morphologically/taxonomically diagnostic taxa used as biogeographic markers:

- **[[Lepidomeda]]** (genus, spiny-rayed cyprinid/Plagopterini) — shared between the White/Virgin/Las Vegas Wash Section and Little Colorado Section of the Middle Colorado Province, used as evidence of their sister-group relationship.
- **[[Novumbra hubbsi]]** (family [[Umbridae]]) — mudminnow species diagnostic of the Coastal/Chehalis Section of the Columbia Province; family otherwise rare/absent in western North America.
- **[[Columbia transmontana]]** (listed under family [[Percopsidae]], likely referring to *Novumbra*-associated trout-perch relatives) — second diagnostic species of the Coastal/Chehalis Section, similarly rare regionally.
- **Primary freshwater fishes** — defined as taxa whose evolutionary lineages have been restricted to freshwater throughout their history (e.g., dominant in Sacramento/San Joaquin Central Valley).
- **Non-primary (secondary/marine-derived) freshwater fishes** — taxa derived from marine ancestors, capable of some seawater dispersal, spawning primarily in freshwater; dominant along Pacific coastal drainages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter frames ichthyofaunal distribution as a product of drainage history, hydrographic connectivity, and tectonic/geologic events rather than purely local ecology:

- **[[Colorado River Basin]]**: Bisected biogeographically by the Grand Canyon (~5–6 million years ago), separating "Upper" and "Middle/Lower" faunas. Subdivided into:
  - **Mainstem/Tributaries Province** (Upper Section above Black Canyon; Lower Section below Black Canyon)
  - **Middle Province** (White/Virgin/Las Vegas Wash Section; Little Colorado Section) — linked via shared genus *Lepidomeda*
  - **Gila River Province**

- **[[Bonneville Province]]**: Novel proposal placing the upper Snake River (above Shoshone Falls) fauna within the Bonneville system rather than treating it as part of the Columbia/Snake system, based on Pliocene–Pleistocene lake-spillover connections during high stands of [[Lake Bonneville]].

- **[[Death Valley Province]]**: Composite fauna shaped heavily by extreme aridity and repeated extinction events; surviving species reflect "extinction resistance" and chance persistence near remnant water rather than clean phylogenetic derivation — described as strongly influenced by stochastic ("lucky") survival and subsequent in situ differentiation.

- **[[Sacramento/San Joaquin Province]]**: Ecological gradient observed — primary freshwater fishes dominate larger, more interconnected watersheds (Central Valley/tributaries), while non-primary (marine-derived) fishes dominate smaller, more isolated coastal drainages. Faunal breaks occur first between Central Valley and coastal drainages, then between north and south coastal drainages.

- **[[Oregon Lakes Province]]**: Complex of closed (endorheic) basins fed by drainages descending from a rotating tectonic subplate; fauna is a mixture derived from all adjacent provinces (with weakest affinity to Lahontan) plus autochthonous endemics. Includes Pit/McCloud (headwaters shared with Sacramento system) and Upper Klamath subunits (distinct from lower Klamath fauna).

- **[[Columbia Province]]**: Ecologically distinguished into a glaciated section (cold-adapted, northern fish fauna with limited penetration into rest of basin) versus an unglaciated section (diverse endemics of primary and non-primary origin, plus widespread coastal taxa).

## Key Findings

- The **Colorado River drainage** is proposed as a standalone top-level biogeographic **Division (Division I)**, distinct from all other western drainage/geologic/biogeographic units, consistent with longstanding recognition as the most distinctive ichthyofauna in North America.
- All remaining western drainages are grouped into **Division II**, subdivided into six Provinces: [[Bonneville Province]], [[Lahontan Province]], [[Death Valley Province]], [[Sacramento/San Joaquin Province]], [[Oregon Lakes Province]], and [[Columbia Province]].
- Cladistic results support reclassifying the **upper Snake River fauna** as part of the Bonneville biotic system rather than a separate/Columbia-linked unit — a departure from traditional classification.
- The historical "Middle Province" division of the Colorado Basin into two equal-rank Sections (White/Virgin/Las Vegas Wash vs. Little Colorado) is flagged as potentially over-split; author suggests it could be merged, contingent on accepting Grand Canyon bisection (~5–6 Mya) as the driving vicariance event.
- The "**Death Valley System**," once treated as a single historical unit, is now understood as a **composite** fauna derived from multiple sources, heavily shaped by extinction and chance survival in an arid landscape.
- A general macroecological pattern is identified: **primary freshwater fish dominance increases with watershed/stream size**, while smaller, more isolated coastal watersheds are dominated by non-primary (marine-derived) fishes — most evident in direct-to-sea coastal drainages.
- The **[[Oregon Lakes Province]]** is flagged as the most taxonomically/biogeographically controversial unit due to its complex mixed ancestry and association with a rotating Cascade/Klamath tectonic subplate.
- The **Columbia Province** is subdivided using both faunal endemism (Coastal/Chehalis Section marked by *Novumbra hubbsi* and *Columbia transmontana*) and Pleistocene glaciation history (glaciated vs. unglaciated mainstem/tributary sections).
- The entire classification is explicitly stated to be a **preliminary, untested hypothesis**, based on qualitative literature synthesis rather than completed quantitative cladistic/phenetic analysis; fish-occurrence matrices for further quantitative work had been compiled (with Liza Bizios, Tonto National Forest) and were available for follow-up study.
- Reflects methodological framing of rivers as biogeographic "taxa" and fish species presence as cladistic "characters," an approach explicitly noted as incomplete due to time constraints.
