# Station Profile: Genoa National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
This profile summarizes the operational and biological output of the [[Genoa National Fish Hatchery]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1932 under the Upper Mississippi Wildlife and Fish Refuge Act of 1931. Located on the Upper Mississippi River near Genoa, Wisconsin, the station's mission is to produce fish of appropriate species, size, and quantity to support federal and state research programs and to aid restoration of threatened and endangered species. In FY 1993, the hatchery produced and shipped over 26 million eggs and fry, distributed live fish to eleven states, and reared a distinct research-purposed cohort of 22,154 fish across multiple species. The facility operates warmwater ponds and coldwater raceways supplied by a combination of artesian and pumped wells, reflecting a dual-purpose production system supporting both [[Warmwater Fish Culture]] and [[Coldwater Fish Culture]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The hatchery supports a mixed-assemblage aquatic production system tied to the [[Upper Mississippi River]] ecosystem, encompassing:
- **Warmwater habitat**: 15 warmwater ponds supporting centrarchids (bass, sunfish, crappie), ictalurids (bullhead), and cyprinids (carp, bonytail chub).
- **Coldwater habitat**: 6 trout rearing raceways supporting salmonids (brown trout, lake trout, rainbow trout).
- **Water sourcing**: 3 artesian wells and 3 pumped wells provide the hydrologic basis for controlled-environment aquaculture, decoupling production from ambient river conditions and enabling year-round rearing independent of Mississippi River water quality fluctuations.
- **Endangered species restoration role**: The presence of [[Bonytail Chub]] (a federally endangered Colorado River Basin cyprinid) among the distributed species suggests the hatchery contributes fish to interstate and interbasin endangered species recovery efforts, extending its ecological role beyond the Upper Mississippi system itself (fish were shipped to Arizona, among other states).
- **Distribution network**: Fish were shipped to Federal waters, other (state/tribal) waters, and negligible private waters, indicating the hatchery's niche as a supplier to public trust resources and interjurisdictional [[Fisheries Management]] programs rather than commercial aquaculture.

## Key Findings
- **Founding & Authority**: Established in 1932 under the Upper Mississippi Wildlife and Fish Refuge Act (1931); administered under USFWS Region 3.
- **Production scale (FY1993)**: 26,063,552 eggs and fry produced/shipped to eleven states (Arizona, Iowa, Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota, Ohio, Oklahoma, Kansas, Texas, Tennessee, Wisconsin).
- **Live fish distribution trend (FY91–FY93)**: Total distributed fish declined sharply from 520,545 (FY91) to 298,601 (FY92) to 194,651 (FY93), with Federal Waters allocations decreasing markedly (178,343 → 94,608 → 24,373) while "Other Waters" (state waters) remained the dominant recipient category.
- **Research production**: 22,154 fish specifically produced for research purposes in FY1993, dominated by [[Black Bullhead]] (14,195) and [[Largemouth Bass]] (6,570), with smaller contributions from Bluegill, Common Carp, Green Sunfish, Bonytail Chub, Rainbow Trout, and Yellow Perch.
- **Infrastructure**: Facility includes a residence, office/shop storage, hatchery/holding house, oil house, fish food storage, and wet laboratory, alongside 15 warmwater ponds and 6 trout raceways — indicating dual warmwater/coldwater culture capacity.
- **Funding trajectory**: Operations budget rose steadily from $173,047 (1991) to a projected $321,328 (1994–1995); Maintenance budget showed volatility, spiking to $955,500 in the 1995 estimate from $19,661 in 1991, suggesting planned capital investment or infrastructure overhaul.
- **Staffing**: Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staffing increased gradually from 3.66 (1991) to 5.0 (1994–1995), reflecting modest but steady growth in station capacity.
- **Public engagement**: Approximately 3,000 annual visitors, with noted potential for increased ecotourism/education value via a proposed on-site aquarium, given the hatchery's roadside visibility on Wisconsin State Highway #35 (approximately 3,030 monthly passenger vehicles per 1986 data).
- **Conservation linkage**: Inclusion of the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] in distribution and research production data ties this warmwater/coldwater hatchery directly to national [[Endangered Species Recovery]] efforts, despite its primary regional focus on the Upper Mississippi River system.
