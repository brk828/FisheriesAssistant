# Bonytail Chub Recovery Program: Issue Paper and Unfunded Needs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (April 1998, date partly illegible in the scan) from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (Dexter NFHTC) to Region 2 management. It presents an issue paper on the [[Bonytail Chub]] recovery program, following a January teleconference of Service scientists and a March 13, 1998 decision meeting at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH). The memo asks managers to approve immediate actions to prevent extinction of bonytail, and to fund a set of unfunded needs totaling **$86,500**.

The core concern is that the wild population is near extinction, and that regional budget shortfalls in the fisheries program, together with imminent staff retirements at WBNFH and the Dexter hatchery, threaten the bonytail recovery effort and the loss of scarce genetic material.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The bonytail chub (BTC) is one of four endangered fishes of the Colorado River system discussed in the document, along with [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The memo gives bonytail special attention because of its severe decline.
- **Status:** The Service lists these species as endangered. Few wild bonytail remain, and studies indicate most are old fish that apparently are no longer reproducing.
- **Historical context:** The [[Bonytail Chub Recovery Plan]] sets the framework for the actions described here. Bonytail broodstock were drawn from [[Lake Mohave]], which had supported an important adult population.
- **Participating facilities:** WBNFH and Dexter NFHTC hold captive and wild-collected fish, and both face staffing gaps.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range:** The species is found primarily in the lower [[Colorado River]] system, with Lake Mohave a key location for wild adults.
- **Threats:** The recovery plan identifies threats including loss of broodstock, limited wild reproduction, nonnative fish introductions and predation/competition with nonnative species. The document also notes the risk of [[Hybridization]] with related species.
- **Regulatory context:** Actions reference compliance with the [[Endangered Species Act]], including [[Section 7 Consultation]] by federal agencies, and the need for cooperative interagency programs.
- **Recovery framework:** Upper Basin recovery is coordinated through the [[Recovery Implementation Program]]. A similar program is planned for the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]].

## Key Findings

### 1. Immediate actions (1998)

**A. Safety-net collection from Lake Mohave (March–May 1998)**
- Several lots of captive BTC at Dexter NFHTC were maintained. Free-swimming BTC collected from Lake Mohave in 1998 were held at WBNFH as a safety net against loss of captive stock.
- Proposed disposition: fish not used for the purpose of the collection would be placed at WBNFH. WBNFH would receive [[Hormone Induction]] injections to induce ovulation in 1998.
- **Unfunded needs:** None listed for this action.

**B. Additional adult collection and transport (May 1998)**
- The Service and cooperating agencies planned to collect additional adult BTC from Lake Mohave using permits and procedures already in force.
- Wild fish were to be transported to WBNFH and then to Dexter NFHTC for [[Captive Broodstock]] development.

**C. Staffing gap at WBNFH**
- The hatchery manager and assistant manager were both scheduled to retire before July 1, 1998. The memo asks that these positions be filled as soon as possible, or that a replacement be in place by late April 1998, to maintain oversight of the recovery effort.
- A Fish Health Biologist was needed on site at WBNFH to implement and oversee treatment and stabilization of wild-caught fish.

### 2. Broodstock development (1998–1999)

- **Captive development:** Develop a new BTC broodstock at Dexter NFHTC. Make no more than 10 paired matings of captive-maintained fish, with free-swimming wild fish as source material. The effort needs additional family-lot tanks, because current units are occupied by razorback sucker and channel catfish family lots.
- **Genetic data:** Secure and assemble genetic data on BTC by September 30, 1998, to inform broodstock decisions for spring 1999 spawning. This includes determining the genetic status of the Dexter broodstock and of Lake Mohave fish held at WBNFH.
- **Transport:** Move BTC from WBNFH to Dexter NFHTC, including fish health inspections, per diem and overtime.
- **Long-term plan:** Build on Dexter NFHTC's BTC Hatchery Broodstock Development Plan (May 1, 1996 draft), and on genetic analyses by Hedrick et al. (Arizona State University, 1992), to complete a broodstock development and management guide for 1999 and beyond.

### 4. Long-term recovery plan (Part II of the document)

- **Immediate goal:** Prevent extinction of bonytail chub. Once the extinction threat is removed and essential life-history information is obtained, quantitative downlisting and delisting goals will be set.
- **Recovery date:** The goal for the Upper Colorado River Basin, excluding the San Juan River, is 2003. A date for the Lower Basin will be set during development of a Lower Basin program.
- **Stepdown outline (summary):**
  1. *Prevent extinction in the wild:* establish at least two [[Refugia]] (including Dexter NFHTC and WBNFH); evaluate captive genetics; obtain wild fish; verify taxonomic status of fish received at refugia.
  2. *Protect populations and habitat:* enforce regulations; ensure Section 7 compliance; develop interagency programs; prohibit further introduction of [[Nonnative Fishes]].
  3. *Reintroduce hatchery-reared fish:* assess propagation techniques, maximize [[Genetic Diversity]] (retain both Upper and Lower Basin genetic lines), and stock fish in priority recovery areas.
  4. *Research:* describe spawning requirements, predation/competition, movement patterns, essential habitat, food habits, aging techniques, age distribution and growth, and hybridization.
  5. *Public outreach:* develop education materials and workshops to inform the public and coordinate research.

### 5. Research and reintroduction priorities

- Adult BTC at [[Lake Mohave]] would be radio-tagged ([[Radiotelemetry]]) to determine habitat use and interactions with nonnative fishes. The lake is a priority site for a wild refugium and study area.
- Hatchery-reared fish should be monitored for survival after stocking, with stocking sizes and conditions refined to maximize survival.
- Captive propagation for at least 10 years may be required, so genetic integrity must be maintained.

### 6. Overall conclusion

The memo concludes that immediate, funded action is needed to avoid further loss of genetic material, to fill key hatchery positions, and to secure the 1998–1999 broodstock program. It also asks managers to support continuation of the effort to address regional fisheries budget shortfalls.
