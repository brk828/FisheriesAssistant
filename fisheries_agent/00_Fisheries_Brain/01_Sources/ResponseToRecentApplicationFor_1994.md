## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990s news article (not a primary scientific paper) reporting on a lawsuit filed by 13 environmental groups against the U.S. Army over the proposed expansion of Fort Huachuca in Sierra Vista, Arizona. The plaintiffs argue that base expansion and associated off-post civilian development would increase groundwater pumping, thereby reducing surface flows in the [[San Pedro River]] — described as "the only healthy river left in Arizona." The reduction in flow is alleged to threaten five federally endangered fish species and one candidate bird species, the southwestern willow flycatcher. The suit does not seek to halt expansion but to compel consultation with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service under environmental law (implicitly the Endangered Species Act Section 7 consultation process).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** All five species inhabit the [[San Pedro River]], a free-flowing (undammed) desert river in southeastern Arizona, noted in the article as unusually intact — free of dams and largely free of cattle grazing pressure.
- **Threat mechanism:** Groundwater pumping associated with military base expansion and related off-post civilian development is alleged to reduce surface and subsurface flows, degrading the river ecosystem that supports these fish.
- **Co-occurring indicator species:** The southwestern willow flycatcher (a riparian-obligate bird proposed for endangered listing) is cited as an additional indicator of riparian/river ecosystem health, suggesting the fish declines are linked to broader riparian habitat degradation, not just aquatic flow reduction.
- **Conservation status context:** All five fish species — [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — are identified as already listed as endangered at the time of the article, indicating pre-existing population vulnerability compounded by the proposed hydrological alteration.

## Key Findings
- A 1994-era lawsuit (Southwest Center for Biological Diversity et al. v. Perry et al.) was filed in U.S. District Court, Tucson, against the Secretary of Defense, Secretary of the Army, and Fort Huachuca's post commander.
- The core scientific/ecological claim: expansion-driven groundwater pumping will reduce river flows in the [[San Pedro River]], threatening five endangered fish species and one candidate bird species.
- The suit sought a court order compelling formal consultation between the Army and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regarding project impacts — not a halt to construction.
- A separate, related lawsuit filed earlier the same year sought to compel an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) for the base's master development plan.
