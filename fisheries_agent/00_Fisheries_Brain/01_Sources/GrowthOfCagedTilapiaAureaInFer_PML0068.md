# Growth of caged Tilapia aurea in fertile farm ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by William Armbrester, Jr. (Agricultural Experiment Station, [[Auburn University]], Alabama) reports a 10-week experiment on caged [[Blue Tilapia]] (*Tilapia aurea*) stocked in four fertile farm ponds. The study tested how efficiently the fish convert [[Plankton]] into growth and whether supplemental feeds ([[Purina Trout Chow]] and [[Auburn No. 2 Feed]]) improve yield. Plankton alone supported considerable growth, Purina Trout Chow gave the largest crops, and Auburn No. 2 was judged unsatisfactory in all cases. Catfish-culture ponds outproduced [[Bluegill-Bass Ponds]]. The document also reviews earlier work on the species' value and risks as a potential exotic in Florida and temperate waters.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Tilapia aurea* (Steindachner), previously identified in the literature as *T. nilotica*.
- **Prior evaluations** cited:
  - [[Swingle]] (1960): efficient pondfish, yielding a maximum of 4,008.7 lb/acre in 208 days; ponds tolerated feeding up to 100 lb feed/acre/day without oxygen depletion.
  - [[McBay]] (1961): sustained temperatures below 65°F were lethal to fish of 11 inches and below; primarily phytoplankton consumers.
  - [[Shell]] (1966): optimum conversion occurred at a feeding rate lower than that giving maximum growth.
  - [[Kilgen]] (1969): stocking with catfish increased catfish crops.
  - [[Pagan]] (1970): cage culture is feasible and the fish do not reproduce in cages.
  - [[Buntz and Manooch]] (1969): in Florida lakes, the species added no sport fishery but supplied food when harvested by snagging or cast netting.
- **Concern:** the lower lethal temperature limits spread in temperate waters, but the author argues that possible permanent introduction must be considered before culture.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pond types:** two [[Catfish Culture Ponds]] (intensively stocked, about 4,000 catfish per acre) and two [[Bluegill-Bass Ponds]].
- **Ponds:** S-8 (10.7 acres) and S-14 (12.4 acres) were catfish ponds; S-3 (10 acres) and S-6 (25.5 acres) were bluegill-bass ponds.
- **Plankton management:** catfish ponds received supplemental catfish feed plus 17.4 lb/acre triple superphosphate (dense blooms); bluegill-bass ponds received only inorganic fertilizer (moderate blooms).
- **Water clarity:** mean Secchi visibility over nine weekly readings was 2.29 ft (S-8), 1.98 ft (S-6), 1.18 ft (S-3), and 1.22 ft (S-14).
- **Feed impact:** the author judged that up to 5.8 lb of feed per acre over 10 weeks would have a negligible effect on pond plankton.
- **Stocking:** fish were reared in plastic pools, treated for external parasites (100 ppm formalin, 1.0 hour), size-sorted (fish under 1.5 in discarded), and stocked at 150 per cage.

## Key Findings

**Methods and design**
- 16 cages (0.25 m³ each), 2,400 *T. aurea* total, four cages per pond, 150 fish per cage.
- Four feeding treatments: A, Purina Trout Chow at 3.0% body weight/day; B, Auburn No. 2 at 3.0% (text also reports 8.0%); C, Auburn No. 2 at 1.5%; D, no supplemental feed.
- Statistical comparison used Student's t-distribution on cage production values.
- Mean stocking weight: 0.956 lb per cage. Coefficient of variation of stocked weight (sample of 226 fish) reported as 114.

**Production results**
- Mean production by treatment (lb/cage): A = 25.92, B = 16.62, C = 15.57, D = 16.20. B, C, and D differed significantly from A at the 0.05 level.
- Unfed cages produced 8.90 lb/cage in bluegill-bass ponds and 24.39 lb/cage in catfish ponds.
- Mean production was 24.11 lb/cage in catfish ponds versus 12.99 lb/cage in bluegill-bass ponds (significant at 0.05).
- Individual cage values (Table 4) ranged from 8.78 lb (S-3, treatment D) to 34.28 lb (S-8, treatment A).

**Feed conversion (C values, Swingle 1958)**
- Auburn No. 2 was unsatisfactory in all cases.
- Purina Trout Chow in bluegill-bass ponds: C = 1.51 and 1.05, judged satisfactory.
- Purina Trout Chow in catfish ponds: C = 6.27 and 3.29, judged unsatisfactory.

**Variation in size**
- Harvested fish were less variable in weight than stocked fingerlings.
- Fish in catfish ponds were less variable than those in bluegill-bass ponds (Table 6 coefficients of variation roughly 13–20 in catfish ponds versus 17–33 in bluegill-bass ponds, excluding the treatment-B value of 83 in S-6).

**Conclusions**
- Plankton alone sustains considerable *T. aurea* growth, so cage culture without feed is viable.
- Purina Trout Chow gives larger crops than Auburn No. 2.
- Feeding Auburn No. 2 at 1.5–8.0% yields only slightly larger crops than no feeding.
- Caveat: dish pans used in treatments B and C occupied cage space near the surface, confounding the B versus C comparison.
