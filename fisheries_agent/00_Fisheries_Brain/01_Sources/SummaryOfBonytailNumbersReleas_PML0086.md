# Bonytail Stocking Records and Gila Topminnow Barrier Survey, Lake Havasu Region

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This short document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum from the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] in Parker, Arizona, dated 7 April 2004. It reports the cumulative number of [[Bonytail]] chubs released into [[Lake Havasu]] from fiscal year 1994 (FY94) through FY04, based on [[Mohave Fisheries Resources Office]] records. The memo was sent to partners in the [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]]. A second, older page (dated 5 May 1992) is a field report on a survey of the [[Palm Lake]] spring-fed pools. It documents electrofishing results for [[Gila Topminnow]] and the condition of a barrier intended to exclude [[Mosquitofish]].

## Background & Description

- **Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)**: A native cyprinid of the Colorado River system. The source does not give a scientific name or describe the species. It is the focus of a stocking program in Lake Havasu.
- **Gila topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*)**: A small native poeciliid fish. The field report refers to it by its scientific name, which is given once in the source.
- **Mosquitofish (*Gambusia* sp.)**: A non-native competitor and predator of native fish. The 1992 report treats it as the threat that the barrier is meant to exclude.
- **Program context**: The stocking summary is prepared by Mitch Thorson, biological technician, MFRO-Parker, and coordinated by Chuck Minckley, Project Coordinator. The memo is distributed to partners including USGS, ASU, and AESO representatives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake Havasu**: The release site for bonytail. The source gives no habitat measurements.
- **Palm Lake spring-fed pools**: Two spring-fed pools were surveyed. The upper pool was shocked for 210 seconds without capturing any fish. The lower pool produced 83 fish, all of them Gila topminnow.
- **Fish barrier**: A physical barrier separates the Palm Lake pools from the lake. Visual inspection in 1992 found it "in good shape" and still functioning as a barrier to mosquitofish invasion.
- **Field conditions**: The 1992 report notes that personnel worked to reduce stress on the fish during sampling. The sampling window was roughly 1300 to 1400 hours. The OCR text is partly garbled, so exact sampling durations should be checked against the original scan.

## Key Findings

1. **Cumulative bonytail releases**: The memo reports a total of **23,549** bonytail chubs released into Lake Havasu from FY94 through FY04, as of the date recorded in the memo header (OCR-garbled, interpreted as 2004).
2. **Data source**: All release figures come from MFRO-Parker fiscal-year records. The source does not provide year-by-year breakdowns in the text.
3. **Palm Lake upper pool**: No fish were captured after 210 seconds of electrofishing.
4. **Palm Lake lower pool**: 83 Gila topminnow were collected. The source states that all were of this species.
5. **Barrier status (1992)**: A visual inspection found the barrier intact and effective against mosquitofish invasion.
6. **Program significance**: The stocking totals and the topminnow survey both serve native fish conservation in the Lake Havasu region. The memo is a tracking and coordination document rather than a research study, so it includes no statistical analyses.
