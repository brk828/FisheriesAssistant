## Executive Summary
This memorandum, authored by W. L. Minckley of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] at Arizona State University, addresses logistical arrangements for an April 1986 team meeting and introduces a critical conservation agenda item. The added agenda item concerns the escalating threat posed by non-native fish introductions into habitats occupied by listed and candidate desert fish species. Two specific case studies are highlighted: the encroachment of invasive [[Green Sunfish]] into isolated native fish watersheds via stock pond overflow, and the problematic co-mingling of distinct [[Desert Pupfish]] subspecies/forms across their native ranges due to unauthorized or historical translocations.

## Background & Description
This document is administrative in nature and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions, meristic data, or morphometric measurements for any species. However, it explicitly distinguishes between two genetically/geographically distinct forms of the same species:

- **Quitobaquito Form** of [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — a genetically distinct population associated with the Quitobaquito Springs lineage.
- **Gila River Form** of [[Desert Pupfish]] — a distinct population/form native to the Gila River drainage.

The memo underscores the taxonomic/conservation importance of maintaining separation between these forms to preserve distinct evolutionary lineages, even though no morphological keys are given.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range Integrity**: The Gila River form of Desert Pupfish is native to tanks and waters within the Gila River watershed, including the Research Ranch near Elgin, Arizona.
- **Non-native Introduction Pathway**: [[Green Sunfish]] populations exist in two restricted and isolated stock ponds in the [[Aravaipa Creek]] and [[Sycamore Creek]] watersheds. These non-native predators are known to disperse into adjacent native creek habitats during stock pond overflow events, posing a direct predation/competition threat to native and listed desert fish species inhabiting these watersheds.
- **Cross-Contamination of Pupfish Forms**: The Quitobaquito form of Desert Pupfish has been found outside its native range in **Bog Hole Tank** and in a tank on the **Research Ranch near Elgin** — both located within the native range of the distinct Gila River form. This represents a genetic/ecological contamination risk between two evolutionarily significant units of the same species.
- **Habitat Type**: The relevant habitats are artificial and semi-natural water bodies (stock ponds, tanks) interconnected with natural desert creek systems, illustrating how anthropogenic water management structures serve as vectors for non-native species dispersal and interspecific/intersubspecific mixing.

## Key Findings
- The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] identified an urgent need to address the **spread of non-native Green Sunfish** into isolated watersheds critical to listed/candidate desert fish conservation (Aravaipa Creek and Sycamore Creek).
- Overflow from stock ponds is confirmed as a **dispersal mechanism** enabling non-native fish to invade sensitive native fish habitats.
- The presence of the **Quitobaquito form of Desert Pupfish** in Bog Hole Tank and a Research Ranch tank near Elgin — within the range of the **Gila River form** — was flagged as a conservation concern requiring active management.
- The author (Minckley) advocated strongly for **removal of the non-native pupfish form and replacement with the native Gila River form**, but noted institutional resistance to this action, indicating an unresolved management conflict within the recovery program.
- A broader discussion was proposed regarding strategies to **expedite removal of non-native fish populations** from sensitive watersheds as a standing conservation priority.
- A separate **status report on Region II fishes** was in preparation, indicating ongoing regional assessment efforts tied to the recovery team's mandate.
