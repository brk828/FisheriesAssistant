# Radioactivity in Columbia River Aquatic Organisms, Hanford Area (1952 Field Program)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #plankton-radioecology

## Executive Summary

This scanned Hanford technical report (document HW-28636) describes a revised field program that monitored radioactivity in aquatic organisms of the [[Columbia River]] near the [[Hanford Works]] and downstream toward the [[McNary Dam]] region. The program sampled a control station, several stations around [[Richland]] and Hanford, and sites in the McNary Dam region, collecting samples about once a month. Radioactivity was highest near Hanford and fell with distance downstream. The report also records seasonal patterns in plankton and algae, observations on salmon spawning and juvenile salmon migration, and preliminary data on planned dam-pool investigations. The text is partly garbled by OCR, so some figures are uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Program design:** Monitoring stations included a control site upstream of the new production area, one station each at Hanford and Richland, and two in the McNary Dam region. Samples were collected from each station about once a month.
- **Routine sample types:** Radioanalyses covered one species of small fish, two macroinvertebrate species, plankton, and the bottom-dwelling alga dominant at the time of sampling. Quantitative samples of aquatic biota were taken within the Hanford Operations area.
- **Special studies:** A cross-sectional study examined how the plankton population and its radioactivity density were distributed across the river. A preliminary survey described the characteristics of [[Nannoplankton]], the very small plankton that standard net mesh does not retain.
- **Dam-pool investigations:** Work in the future McNary Dam pool included deep-water dredging and monthly cross-sectional plankton sampling.
- **Biological observations:** Records were kept on spawning adult salmon, their spawning areas, and migrating juvenile salmon.
- **Methods continuity:** Sampling followed procedures from the prior annual program (the "OSE Annual Report").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spatial gradient:** Maximum activity density in river organisms occurred between Hanford and Richland. It fell rapidly below Richland, more slowly below the next station, and became very gradual below McNary Dam. This pattern persisted through the collecting season.
- **Seasonal patterns in fauna:** Caddis fly larvae and shiners showed high values in spring, according to the figure data. High water levels in spring prevented littoral-zone collecting and created hydrographic conditions the report describes as unfavorable for the organisms' growth.
- **Plants and plankton:** The bottom-dwelling alga and the plankton, which is composed almost entirely of diatoms, both showed seasonal fluctuations in radioactivity, and these were tied to water levels. The report notes that the plankton and the alga followed a somewhat different trend from the animals.
- **Figure 1 (1952):** The chart plots activity densities of river organisms at Hanford on a logarithmic scale, along with water temperature and water activity. It covers shiners, caddis fly larvae, plankton, algae, and water.

## Key Findings

1. **Radioactivity declined with distance downstream.** Activity density peaked between Hanford and Richland and dropped sharply below Richland, then more gradually toward McNary Dam.
2. **Sampling scope:** Four station groups (control, Hanford, Richland, McNary region) were sampled monthly, with routine radioanalysis of one small fish species, two macroinvertebrates, plankton, and dominant algae.
3. **Plankton characterization:** A preliminary survey described nannoplankton, and a special study mapped cross-sectional plankton radioactivity density across the river.
4. **Seasonal effects:** Spring high water limited sampling of littoral organisms and affected growth conditions. Plankton and algae showed seasonal changes in radioactivity tied to water conditions.
5. **Fish life-history data:** Spawning of adult salmon, spawning-area locations, and juvenile salmon migration were recorded alongside the radioactivity work.
6. **Future dam pool:** Planned investigations for the McNary Dam pool included deep-water dredging and monthly plankton sampling.
7. **Data limitations:** The OCR text is incomplete. Several numerical comparisons, including one on relative activity density and one on winter organism numbers, are not legible, so no figures are reported here.
