# Arizona State University 1993 Annual Progress Report: Humpback Chub Ecology in the Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-cypha

## Executive Summary

This document is a March 9, 1994 memorandum from [[David L. Wegner]] of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], transmitting the [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) 1993 annual progress report on the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Little Colorado River]]. The study runs under Cooperative Agreement No. 1-FC-40-10490 and is jointly administered by ASU and the [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]]. The enclosed cover letter from co-investigators [[Michael E. Douglas]] and [[Paul C. Marsh]] reports on 1993 field trips, fishing effort, the numbers of native and non-native fish captured, tag and recapture results for humpback chub, flannelmouth sucker, and bluehead sucker, and humpback chub mortalities. Twelve field trips were completed in 1993, with 93,177.9 hours of fishing effort, 9,314 fish captured, and 5,551 adult humpback chub handled. Four adult and four juvenile humpback chub died from high spring and autumn flows.

## Background & Description

- **Study:** "Ecology and Conservation Biology of Humpback Chub, *Gila cypha*, in the Little Colorado River (LCR)," jointly administered by [[Arizona State University]] and the [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]].
- **Co-investigators:** Paul Marsh and Michael Douglas, Department of Zoology, ASU, Tempe, Arizona.
- **Distribution list:** The memo was sent to the [[Aquatic Coordination Team]] and the GCES Endangered Species Program. Recipients included staff from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (FWS), the [[National Park Service]] (NPS), the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGF), the [[Hualapai Tribe]], and the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]].
- **Sampling camps:** Fieldwork was organized by camp: Confluence, Powell, and Salt.
- **Size classes used:** Fish were grouped by total length (TL). Native fish were split at 150 mm TL. Humpback chub were further sorted into six size classes: 1 = 150–200 mm, 2 = 201–250 mm, 3 = 251–300 mm, 4 = 301–350 mm, 5 = 351–400 mm, 6 = 401+ mm.
- **Tag types:** New PIT tags (issued in 1993), old PIT tags, and old Carlin/Floy tags, which are recorded separately.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study river:** The [[Little Colorado River]] is the focus of the humpback chub work. Fish were also sampled at reaches associated with the Colorado River (Glen Canyon) camps.
- **Native fish community:** Humpback chub, [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Speckled Dace]] were captured.
- **Non-native fish community:** [[Brown Trout]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], and [[Rainbow Trout]] were captured.
- **Flow-related mortality:** Humpback chub deaths in 1993 were "a result of high spring and autumn flows." This points to flow regime as a direct threat to the species during those seasons.
- **Regulatory context:** Mortalities were reported to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service "as per our permit obligations." The species is part of the GCES Endangered Species Program.
