## Executive Summary

This document is a set of meeting minutes and administrative correspondence from the **Virgin River Recovery Team (VRRT)**, dated October 3, 1997 (fall meeting) and forwarded April 21–23, 1998, ahead of a spring 1998 meeting. It is not a primary research paper but a management/coordination record concerning the recovery of federally listed and native fishes of the **Virgin River Basin** (Utah, Arizona, Nevada). Key taxa of concern are the endangered **[[Woundfin]]** and **[[Virgin River Chub]]**, along with the sensitive **[[Virgin Spinedace]]**, and native **[[Desert Sucker]]** and **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** populations. The minutes document fall 1997 monitoring results across three states, ongoing genetic/propagation management at Dexter National Fish Hatchery, nonnative species control efforts (particularly **[[Red Shiner]]** eradication), habitat and instream-flow studies, and prioritization of 1998–1999 recovery actions under the Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program (VRBIRMRP).

## Background & Description

Limited direct morphological data are reported, but the following field measurements and life-stage classifications are noted:

- **Desert Sucker** (Lytle Ranch/BDW site): population estimate of ~1,300–1,500 individuals, with **average length ~150 mm**.
- **Woundfin**: fish transferred from Dexter Fish Hatchery and Mormon Well (Arizona) source populations; approximately **280 fish** stocked into Beaver Creek (Water Fall Canyon) tributary in Nevada.
- Age-class differentiation is repeatedly used in monitoring: **YOY (young-of-year)** vs. adult classes tracked separately for woundfin, Virgin River chub, and suckers, indicating recruitment-based population assessment rather than detailed morphometrics.
- No meristic, coloration, or diagnostic morphological descriptions are provided in this administrative record.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat segregation observed in Beaver Creek (Water Fall Canyon)**: upper 1/3 of the tributary was historically **fishless**, fed by a **~70°F warm spring**, while the lower 2/3 supported nonnative **[[Rainbow Trout]]** and native **[[Speckled Dace]]**. Rainbow trout were selectively removed via electrofishing prior to spinedace stocking, indicating **competitive/predatory displacement dynamics** between introduced salmonids and native cyprinids.
- **Beaver-modified habitat at BDW (confluence area)**: Colonization by beaver created dense vegetation and impounded beaver ponds, converting formerly productive **spinedace and speckled dace habitat** into red shiner-dominated habitat, illustrating how physical habitat change can favor nonnative cyprinids over natives.
- **Turbidity and flow regime**: Standard monitoring occurred under turbid water conditions considered "optimum" for sampling; storm events were noted to **wash YOY woundfin downstream**, suggesting flow-mediated recruitment and dispersal patterns.
- **Spatial population patterns (Bio/West, lower river/Nevada)**: A recurring seasonal cycle was documented at Riverside and Mesquite Bridge sites — YOY abundance builds through spring, declines by late summer (September), with cohorts detectable the following spring, suggesting a **strong seasonal recruitment-mortality cycle** possibly linked to high-flow scouring events combined with local recruitment.
- **Nonnative interactions**: **Red Shiner** numbers were reported "in the thousands" at some sites and increasing at others (Arizona), while native shiner-adjacent habitats declined; **bullheads** and rainbow trout were also present as competing/predatory nonnatives.
- **Irrigation and diversion-altered habitats**: Reaches between Washington Fields Diversion and Johnson Diversion (including Ft. Pierce Wash, canals, and ponds) were targeted for **nonnative fish eradication treatments**, reflecting the anthropogenically modified niche structure of the basin.

## Key Findings

- **Fall 1997 Monitoring Results by State**:
  - **Utah**: 117 YOY woundfin recorded at Washington Bridge; low YOY numbers attributed to storm-driven downstream displacement; 2 red shiners detected near Johnson Diversion (early detection of invasion front).
  - **Nevada**: No woundfin captured; very few native fish at Halfway Wash; only a few Virgin River chub captured; catch included 76 flannelmouth suckers, 11 speckled dace, and 11 desert suckers.
  - **Arizona**: No woundfin captured; 5 YOY Virgin River chub found; abundant YOY desert and flannelmouth suckers; red shiner numbers increasing; a few bullheads present.
  - **Bio/West (5-year lower river dataset, Nevada)**: Documented consistent seasonal woundfin decline pattern; marked/stocked woundfin detected briefly post-release then declined sharply, raising questions about **tag retention vs. mortality** and the **efficacy of stocking as a recovery tool**.

- **Stocking Efficacy Debate**: Disagreement among team members (Paul Holden vs. Leo Lentsch/Jon Sjoberg) on whether Nevada stocking of woundfin was effective — noted that stocking was not conducted as a rigorous experimental study, and habitat suitability in stocked reaches may be marginal for the species.

- **Virgin Spinedace Translocation**: Approximately 280 spinedace translocated from a Mormon Well, Arizona source population into Beaver Creek's Water Fall Canyon, following selective removal of nonnative rainbow trout; both adults and progeny were later detected upon return survey, indicating **short-term establishment success**.

- **Genetic/Propagation Management (Dexter National Fish Hatchery)**: Woundfin broodstock initiated in 1979, augmented in 1989; Virgin River chub broodstock has **never been augmented**, raising concerns about genetic diversity. 5,000–10,000 woundfin were slated for availability by mid-October 1997 for distribution to state agencies.

- **Nonnative Eradication Program**: Plans to treat two river reaches (Washington Fields Diversion to Johnson Diversion; Johnson Diversion to the fish barrier, including Ft. Pierce Wash and associated ponds) tentatively scheduled for November/December, with salvaged native fish either released upstream or transferred to Wahweep Fish Hatchery to establish a secondary local broodstock.

- **Fish Barrier Construction Priorities**: Two barriers under engineering/biological feasibility review — (1) at the mouth of the canyon (BLM/AZGF lead) to prevent nonnative recolonization, and (2) at Bloomington to support red shiner eradication (WCWCD lead).

- **Database and Habitat Monitoring Infrastructure**: A centralized fish/habitat database (Utah State University, led by Thom Hardy) was nearing completion with GIS linkage planned; annual maintenance cost estimated at **$2,000**. Aerial multispectral imagery was used to cross-reference seine haul sites and monitor habitat/river geomorphic change over time.

- **1998 Recovery Priorities Identified**:
  1. Develop dedicated Virgin River chub sampling protocols (currently "overlooked" in general monitoring).
  2. Establish genetic sampling protocols related to migration barrier impacts.
  3. Formalize database stewardship (Utah to take lead).
  4. Produce annual summary reports (3-year summary in progress).
  5. Continue red shiner and other nonnative eradication treatments.
  6. Develop instream flow and water quality monitoring protocols (USU and BioWest leads).
  7. Pursue habitat acquisition (e.g., duck pond, Sciglmiller Marsh) to support red shiner control and corridor protection.

- **Instream Flow Studies**: BLM-led instream flow study (Ron Hooper, Arizona) faced delays; Recovery Team agreed to send inter-agency letters urging completion for use in the [[Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program]].
