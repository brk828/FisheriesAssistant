# Proposed Action Plan for Reintroduction of Razorback Sucker in the Lower Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #conservation-biology #reintroduction

## Executive Summary

This 1989 action plan proposes an accelerated reintroduction strategy for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]. The species is disappearing throughout its native range despite cooperative efforts begun in 1973-75. The plan addresses recruitment failure in wild populations by proposing to rear hatchery-produced larvae in isolated off-channel habitats until they reach 35-40 cm total length, at which size they gain relative immunity from predation by non-native fishes. The strategy involves selecting suitable off-channel habitats adjacent to rivers, removing predators through chemical reclamation, stocking with larvae from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], monitoring growth, and passively releasing subadults into adjacent river channels. This approach aims to circumvent logistical obstacles of pond space and transport while exploiting natural growth rates in predator-free environments.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Abbott) is a native species of the [[Colorado River]] drainage in western North America. Cooperative research, propagation, and reintroduction efforts were initiated by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) in 1973-75, reinitiated in 1981, and pursued under Memoranda of Understanding with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF, since 1981) and [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (CFG, since 1985). [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) has carried out work with FWS-[[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR) support since 1974.

Despite successful annual reproduction and larval production by wild fish in [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada), no recruitment has been detected. Larval disappearance has been attributed to three factors: (1) predation by non-native fishes (most likely), (2) nutritional constraints, and/or (3) transport from the system by subsurface currents. Similar unexplained recruitment failure has been documented in the upper Colorado River basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] presents significant challenges for [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery. Off-channel habitats experience seasonal water-level fluctuations of several meters due to changes in reservoir release volumes, which dewaters many potential rearing sites. The presence of non-native predatory fishes poses the primary threat to newly-stocked and larval suckers.

Suitable off-channel habitats for the proposed rearing program should be 0.5 to 5 acres (0.2 to 2 ha) in surface area, perennial with minimum depths of at least 1 meter (3 ft), and immediately adjacent or directly connected to river channels. Sites already identified include locations along the [[Gila River]] near Safford, Arizona, and the [[Verde River]] near Cottonwood, Arizona. Additional habitats along the lower Colorado River required identification at the time of this proposal.

The plan recommends selecting waters in already-protected areas controlled by FWS, [[National Park Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), State agencies, or cooperative private lands.

## Key Findings

**Research Conclusions Supporting the Action Plan:**

1. **Predation Impact**: Predation by non-native fishes on newly-stocked suckers is extensive and represents a major cause of post-stocking mortality. Field results have been corroborated by controlled experiments.

2. **Size-Dependent Survival**: Predation losses are directly related to size of reintroduced fish. [[Razorback Sucker]]s reared to 35-40 cm total length (TL) should enjoy relative immunity from existing predators.

3. **Growth Rates**: Growth of hatchery-produced razorback suckers is rapid. Newly-hatched larvae (ca. 10 mm TL) grow to 35-40 cm TL in 12 to 24 months without supplemental feeding in a variety of predator-free, natural or artificial habitats.

4. **Migration Behavior**: Razorback suckers of all sizes migrate immediately from isolation when afforded the opportunity and move downstream when stocked into a riverine environment.

5. **Logistical Constraints**: Pond space for rearing razorback suckers to large size and transport of large fish from regional hatcheries to stocking sites constitute formidable logistic obstacles and may be impractical.

**Proposed Protocol:**

- **Habitat Reclamation**: Use rotenone to remove undesired fishes where permits allow; alternative methods (explosives, pumping dry) where conditions dictate. Treatment should ensure complete kills of all non-native fishes. Sampling during reclamation recommended to document precedent conditions.

- **Stocking**: Larval razorback suckers from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to be stocked directly into prepared habitats 2 to 3 weeks after reclamation. Representative subsample of stocked fish should be preserved to document initial size distribution.

- **Stock Assessment**: Size assessment semi-annually or annually using appropriate capture methods (passive or active netting, electrofishing). Sample of at least 20 individuals should be captured and measured.

- **Release**: Once fish attain 35-40 cm TL, passive release into adjacent main channel by breaching separation barrier (ca. 2 m wide, 0.3-0.5 m deep). Sample of 20 suckers should be preserved for documentation, or 20-50 measured at release.

- **Marking**: All fish to be marked with oxytetracycline to allow recognition of reintroduced individuals and future program evaluation.

**Implementation Timeline**: Initial implementation proposed for spring 1989 to accommodate stocking of larvae in March 1989. The plan anticipates cooperation from AGF, CFG, FWS, BR, BLM, ASU, and other interested agencies through provision of funding, equipment, manpower, logistical support, and expertise.

**Anticipated Benefits**: Cost and effort savings through use of cost-free habitats and readily-available larvae; production of more predator-immune fish to accelerate establishment of adult populations; potential reduction in downstream movement tendency of stocked fish.
