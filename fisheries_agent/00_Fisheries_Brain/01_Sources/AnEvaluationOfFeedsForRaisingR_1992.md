# An Evaluation of Feeds for Raising Razorback Sucker (Xyrauchen texanus)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #aquaculture

## Executive Summary
This study evaluated dietary regimens for rearing larval and juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], under intensive hatchery conditions. Fry were reared for 126 days across three sequential feeding phases using five commercial formulated diets: BioKyowa B-250, BioKyowa A-250/A-400, Bio-Dry Starter #1, AP-100, and Silver Cup Salmon Starter (SCSS). Results demonstrated that a sequential feeding protocol—BioKyowa B-250 (days 5–25), BioKyowa A-250 (days 26–71), and BioKyowa A-400 (days 72–126)—produced the best combination of growth and survival. BioKyowa-A diets consistently outperformed SCSS, AP-100, and Bio-Dry in both growth rate and survival. However, spinal and muscular deformities were observed across all diet treatments, most severely in fish fed only SCSS (83%), likely linked to vitamin C degradation during prolonged feed storage (>90 days).

## Background & Description
- **Initial size:** Sac-fry averaged 0.005 g at start of exogenous feeding (day 5); swim-up occurred 5 days post-hatch.
- **Growth trajectory (BioKyowa regimen):**
  - Day 25: ~0.01 g (doubled from initial weight)
  - Day 52 (Phase II, A-250): mean weight gain 6.0 (×10⁻²) g units; final total length 22.8 mm
  - Day 126 (Phase III, A-250→A-250 continuous): final length 41.4–42.1 mm
- **Deformities:** Spinal and muscular abnormalities recorded across all treatment groups, including:
  - Displaced vertebral columns
  - Compressed spinal cords
  - Displaced musculature
  - Highest incidence (83%) in fish fed SCSS only throughout Phases II–III; lowest (0–2%) in fish fed BioKyowa-A continuously or fish switched from SCSS to BioKyowa-A.
- Deformities are consistent with known signs of [[Vitamin C Deficiency]] in cultured fishes (per Steffens 1989).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range/status:** Wild broodstock (eggs/milt) collected from the [[Green River]] near Jensen, Utah; species is protected under state statutes and was proposed for federal listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (USFWS 1990).
- **Conservation driver:** Population declines and lack of recruitment in wild stocks (Lanigan & Tyus 1989; Marsh & Minckley 1989) have driven development of hatchery propagation techniques to maintain genetic stocks and produce taggable fingerlings.
- **Rearing environment:** Reared in a controlled hatchery setting (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service hatchery, Ouray, Utah) using single-pass, degassed, heated well water; water quality parameters maintained near pH 7.7, dissolved oxygen 5.4–6.0 mg/l, hardness 440 mg/l, temperatures 19.3–20.0°C.
- **Life stage focus:** Study spans early larval development (sac-fry) through fingerling stage (126 days post-hatch), a critical bottleneck period for [[Endangered Fish Propagation]] programs.

## Key Findings
- **Phase I (Days 5–25, BioKyowa B-250):** 96% survival; body weight doubled (0.005 g → 0.01 g); daily growth rate G = 0.038, higher survival than a previous 45-day trial (Tyus & Severson 1990) attributed to increased feeding rate.
- **Phase II (Days 26–52):**
  - BioKyowa A-250 produced significantly higher growth, length, and survival (P<0.05) than Bio-Dry, AP-100, and SCSS.
  - Average survival on A-250 was 101% (some replicates >100% attributed to counting error of small larvae); SCSS survival averaged only 71%.
  - Growth/survival among Bio-Dry, AP-100, and SCSS were statistically similar.
- **Phase III (Days 53–126):**
  - Fish continuously fed BioKyowa-A had the best growth and survival (96–97%).
  - Fish maintained solely on SCSS had poor survival (12%) and minimal growth.
  - Diet-reversal experiments showed:
    - Fish switched from A-250 to SCSS: small size (30.5 mm) but high survival (97%).
    - Fish switched from SCSS to BioKyowa-A: high survival (88%) and improved growth relative to SCSS-only fish, though less than continuous BioKyowa-A group.
  - Establishes early dietary superiority of [[BioKyowa Diet]] regardless of prior conditioning.
- **Proximate analysis (Table 1):** Protein ranged 48.2% (Bio-Dry) to 57.2% (B-250); fat ranged 14.7% (B-250/AP-100) to 22.4% (A-400). BioKyowa-A had the highest fat content among Phase II/III diets but not the highest protein — suggesting fat content or unmeasured micronutrient/formulation factors (not just crude protein/fat) drove differential performance.
- **Deformity risk:** Attributed to probable [[Vitamin C]] degradation from extended cold storage (>90 days) exceeding the recommended 90-day shelf life for open-formula dry diets (USFWS specifications); dry catfish diets have been shown to lose 50% of ascorbic acid after 3 months at 21°C (Piper et al. 1982).
- **Practical recommendation:** For successful 126-day rearing of razorback sucker fry to fingerling stage, use sequential diet: **BioKyowa B-250** (days 5–25) → **BioKyowa A-250** (days 26–71) → **BioKyowa A-400** (days 72–126), with attention to fresh feed supply or supplemental vitamin fortification to mitigate deformities.
- **Broader implication:** Findings support ongoing efforts in [[Razorback Sucker]] conservation aquaculture and contribute species-specific diet protocols lacking in prior literature (Hamman 1987 established pond-rearing feasibility but not formulated diet requirements).
