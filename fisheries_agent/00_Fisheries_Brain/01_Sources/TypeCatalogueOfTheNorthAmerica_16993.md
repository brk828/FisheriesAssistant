# Type Catalogue of the North American Cyprinid Fish Genus Notropis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This bulletin of the [[Florida State Museum]] presents a type catalogue for the North American cyprinid genus [[Notropis]], written by Carter R. Gilbert and published in 1978 (Bulletin of the Florida State Museum, Biological Sciences, Vol. 23, No. 1). The genus, as then constituted, is the largest group of North American fishes, comprising 131 valid described species and subspecies (119 species and 12 subspecies, one of the species being fossil), at least a dozen undescribed forms, and seven species presently in [[Hybopsis]] that may later be transferred to Notropis. A total of 304 names are available for the 138 valid described forms.

The catalogue lists all nominal forms alphabetically, giving for each the original combination, type locality, catalogue numbers of types, collectors, paratype data, nomenclatural remarks and present status. Supplementary lists cover generic synonymy, valid species with synonyms, hybrid-based names and nomina nuda. The work is driven by the confusion caused by morphological similarity, numerous junior synonyms and hybridization within the group.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Notropis Rafinesque 1818]] was established with [[Notropis atherinoides]] as type species. Until recently it was diagnosed by: (a) one or two rows of pharyngeal teeth, never more than four in the major row; (b) normally eight dorsal rays; (c) a short gut; (d) no maxillary barbel; (e) relatively large scales, never more than 55 in the lateral series and usually fewer than 40; and (f) small body size, usually under 100 mm standard length and never over 175 mm SL.

Several of these characters have been undermined. [[Notropis Emiliae]] has nine dorsal rays and, in one subspecies, four pharyngeal teeth in the right arch. Barbels vary within species and have evolved in parallel across cyprinid lines. [[Dionda nubila]] and [[Notropis mekistocholas]] have long, coiled guts. Only the scale and body-size characters remain unrefuted. Gilbert notes that the genus may be unified or dismembered by future work on nuptial tuberculation, gut coiling, serum proteins, karyology and osteology.

The taxonomic history follows four periods adapted from [[Collette]] (1967) for darters: Beginnings (1818–1840), Golden Era (1841–1892), Doldrums (1893–1931) and Reawakening (1932–present). Of 295 specific and subspecific names proposed (valid and invalid), 131 are valid. The 1850s (54 names, 16 valid) and 1860s–1880s were most productive, driven largely by [[Charles Girard]] (118 new cyprinids in his 1857 paper alone, 33 of 44 new Notropis forms invalid) and [[Edward D. Cope]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is primarily nomenclatural and taxonomic. It provides type localities that frequently cover drainages across the eastern United States, the Great Plains, Texas, and northeastern Mexico, reflecting the broad distribution of the genus. Several type localities were erroneous and were corrected using the day-by-day accounts of railroad and boundary surveys (for example, the [[Pacific Railroad Surveys]] and the [[Mexican Boundary Survey]]).

Hybridization is a recurrent theme. Hybrid-based nominal forms include [[Notropis germanus]] (Hybognathus hankinsoni x N. heterolepis), [[Notropis kanawha]] (N. rubellus x N. volucellus) and [[Notropis macdonaldi]] (N. cornutus x N. rubellus). The subspecies status of several forms (such as [[Notropis lutrensis]] and [[Notropis stramineus]]) was clarified regionally, while others remained unresolved.

Gilbert also addresses the museum history of the types. Many [[Girard]] syntypes were lost after being distributed by the United States National Museum curator Barton A. Bean between 1892 and 1932, and a fire at Indiana University in 1883 destroyed other material. Of the types, Gilbert personally examined over 90 percent.

## Key Findings

- **Scale of the genus:** 131 valid described species and subspecies (119 species, 12 subspecies; one species fossil), at least a dozen undescribed forms, and seven Hybopsis species that may move to Notropis. The 138 valid described forms carry 304 available names.
- **Synonymy burden:** Over 625 specific and subspecific names exist for the roughly 300 valid described North American Cyprinidae. Notropis cornutus has 14 junior synonyms and Notropis lutrensis 25.
- **Decadal productivity:** The largest number of valid names appeared in the 1850s (16), 1860s (23) and 1870s (23). The 1930s had the fewest (one valid name, Notropis volucellus wickliffi, Trautman).
- **Type loss:** Most of Girard's species are represented by fewer type specimens than originally present, and some by none. Many of his descriptions are too vague to identify those species with certainty.
- **Nomenclatural corrections:** Gilbert resolved several disputed lectotype designations (notably Fowler's 1910 designations for Hypsilepis cornutus cerasinus and Alburnops plumbeolus), which he petitioned the [[International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature]] to suspend or suppress.
- **Synonymy changes:** Notropis phenacobius Forbes 1885 is probably a senior synonym of Notropis amnis Hubbs and Greene 1951. Gilbert deferred action because no types are known. Cyprinella ludibunda Girard 1857 is a senior synonym of both N. stramineus and N. volucellus, pending lectotype designation.
- **Genus definition:** The traditional diagnostic characters of Notropis have been weakened, and no single character now clearly unifies the genus. Gilbert retained the subgenera Codoma and Opsopoeodus within Notropis for convenience.
- **Publication dates:** Gilbert reviewed publication dates under the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature, correcting several dates (for example, Bulletin 16 of the U.S. National Museum, mailed 1 April 1883).
- **Taxonomic lists:** The valid species list runs to 119 numbered entries, with synonyms and subspecies. The lists also cover Hybopsis species, hybrid-based names, nomina nuda and names now referred to other genera (such as Dionda and Yuriria).
