## Executive Summary
This paper (Spangler, 1972) describes **Bunites**, a new genus, and **Bunites phyllisae**, a new species of predaceous diving beetle (family [[Dytiscidae]], subfamily Colymbetinae, tribe [[Colymbetini]]) collected in Bolivia in 1969. Though not a fish, this beetle represents an important **aquatic macroinvertebrate** taxon relevant to freshwater ecosystem studies, particularly high-altitude puna grassland pools in the Bolivian Andes. The paper situates the new genus taxonomically relative to the Old World genus **Meladema** and provides a dichotomous key to all genera of Colymbetini in the Western Hemisphere (Hoperius, Colymbetes, Rhantus, Neoscutopterus, and Bunites).

## Background & Description
**Diagnostic features of genus Bunites:**
- Head large, broadest across the eyes; eyes emarginate above antennal bases.
- Labrum broadly emarginate medially with a dense fringe of golden setae.
- Maxillary palpus 4-segmented; labial palpus 3-segmented, with distinct sensory/setose regions on apical segments.
- Pronotum >2x wider than long; margined laterally; anterolateral angles acute and prolonged; posterolateral angles obtuse; base narrower than elytral base.
- Prosternal process distinctly convex, margined, with an acute apex fitting into a deep metasternal notch.
- Elytra convex, widest at apical third, finely margined laterally.
- Metacoxal plates broad, slightly incised posteriorly; metacoxal lines diverging anteriorly.
- **Sexual dimorphism**: Males possess broadened, sucker-setae-bearing pro- and mesotarsi (adhesive structures for mating); basal protarsal segment longer than the next three segments combined.
- Metatibia bears several discontinuous rows of large aciculate seta-bearing punctures (diagnostic vs. Meladema, which has a single continuous stria).
- Metatarsal segments only feebly lobed on outer lower part (basal two segments only) — a character atypical for Colymbetini per Brinck's (1948) classification, but reconciled by comparison with **Rhantus vianus** and **R. validus**, which show similarly weak lobing.
- Outer metatarsal claw curved and shorter than the straighter inner claw.
- Male genitalia: parameres broad and curved near base, tapering to slender, densely pubescent apices; median lobe curved with a recurved tip, slightly sinuous and cleft distally.

**Holotype (male) specifics:**
- Length: 14.00 mm; greatest width: 6.50 mm (at apical third).
- Coloration: piceous (blackish) head, pronotum, elytra, with reddish-yellow maculae on the head (between eyes) and elytra (near lateral margin, mid-length); epipleuron dark reddish brown; ventral surface piceous except for reddish-brown coxae and abdominal sternal margins.
- Antennae 11-segmented.
- Size range across type series: 13.00–14.00 mm length, 6.00–7.00 mm width.
- Elytral maculae variably reduced or absent among paratypes.

**Sexual dimorphism (Female):**
- Pro- and mesotarsi not broadened; lack sucker-bearing setae.
- Setose sulcus of profemur reduced to a single row of golden setae (vs. dense sulcus in male).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Collected from a small, shallow freshwater pool (~6 ft × 3 ft × 1 ft) within a spring-fed gutter draining into the Corani Dam, at an elevation of approximately 9,000 feet (2,743 m) in the Bolivian Andes.
- **Vegetation/biome**: Typical high-altitude puna grassland ecosystem.
- **Life history note**: No larvae were found during collection, suggesting either seasonal absence, cryptic larval habitat, or limited sampling.
- **Biogeography**: Genus appears restricted to South America, distinguishing it from related genera with North American ([[Hoperius]], [[Colymbetes]], [[Neoscutopterus]]) or broader Pan-American ([[Rhantus]]) distributions. The Old World genus [[Meladema]] is its closest morphological analog, suggesting a historical trans-Atlantic or Gondwanan biogeographic relationship within Colymbetinae.

## Key Findings
1. **New genus and species established**: *Bunites* gen. nov. and *Bunites phyllisae* sp. nov., type species by monotypy, described from male holotype, allotype female, and numerous paratypes (124 males, 22 females from Cochabamba; additional paratypes from Pongo de Quime, collected 1921–22 by the Mulford Biological Expedition).
2. **Taxonomic placement debated but resolved**: The genus keys clearly to Colymbetini under Zimmerman's (1919) system but conflicts with Brinck's (1948) tribal diagnosis due to only feebly lobed (rather than distinctly lobed) metatarsal segments. Spangler resolves this by noting analogous weak lobing in *Rhantus vianus* and *R. validus*, supporting inclusion in [[Colymbetini]].
3. **Differentiation from Meladema**: *Bunites* differs from the Old World genus [[Meladema]] by having only slightly unequal (vs. distinctly unequal) metatarsal claws, and by discontinuous (vs. continuous) rows of aciculate punctures on the metatibia.
4. **New identification key provided**: A dichotomous key to all five Western Hemisphere genera of Colymbetini ([[Hoperius]], [[Colymbetes]], [[Rhantus]], [[Neoscutopterus]], and [[Bunites]]) is presented, using characters such as prosternal process shape, elytral sculpture, pronotal margination, and metatarsal lobing.
5. **Etymology**: The genus name *Bunites* derives from Greek *bounites* ("dweller in the hills"), reflecting the high-altitude puna habitat; the species epithet *phyllisae* honors the author's wife, Phyllis Spangler, who co-collected the type series.
