# The Acute Toxicity of Phenol to Rainbow Trout in Saline Waters

## Executive Summary
This 1967 study by Brown, Shurben, and Fawell at the Water Pollution Research Laboratory investigated the acute toxicity of [[Phenol]] to [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*) acclimatized to a gradient of saline waters (0–60% sea water), at 15°C. Unlike previously studied toxicants—[[Zinc Sulphate]] and [[Ammonium Chloride]]—which show minimum toxicity at intermediate salinities (30–40% sea water), phenol toxicity increased **linearly** with increasing salinity. The 48-hr median lethal concentration (LC50) fell from 9.3 mg/l in fresh water to 5.2 mg/l in 60% sea water. The findings suggest that the greatest risk to migrating [[Salmonidae|salmonid]] fish from phenol discharges occurs at the seaward end of their estuarine migration, contrasting with the mid-estuary risk zone identified for zinc and ammonia.

## Background & Description
- **Test subjects:** Five 2(+)-year-old hatchery-reared [[Rainbow Trout]] per concentration.
- **Average fork-length:** 20 cm (range: 18–23 cm).
- **Comparative species context:** Findings were related to [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*) smolts, which in prior studies were shown to be slightly more sensitive than trout to zinc and ammonia under similar estuarine conditions; a similar sensitivity pattern was inferred, though not directly tested, for phenol.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Estuarine environments experiencing salinity gradients from freshwater to full sea water (33 g/kg salinity, North Sea water collected at Dovercourt, Harwich).
- **Water Chemistry:**
  - Diluent: raw, hard bore-hole water (total hardness 290 mg/l as CaCO₃; total alkalinity 230 mg/l as CaCO₃).
  - pH maintained naturally between 8.1–8.4 (uncontrolled).
  - Dissolved oxygen maintained near saturation via gentle aeration.
  - Temperature controlled at 15.0–15.8°C.
- **Acclimatization protocol:** Stepwise salinity increases (0→15→30→40→60% sea water) over 14 days; fish fed during acclimatization, then starved 48 hr prior to and during testing.
- **Migratory relevance:** The study frames [[Rainbow Trout]] as a practical proxy species for migratory [[Salmonidae]] (e.g., [[Atlantic Salmon]] smolts) navigating estuaries with fluctuating salinity, addressing pollution risk during real-world migratory passage.
- **Comparative toxicant behavior:**
  - [[Zinc Sulphate]] and [[Ammonium Chloride]] toxicity is minimized at 30–40% sea water (isotonic/slightly hypertonic to smolt blood), creating a "safe zone" for migrating fish mid-estuary.
  - [[Phenol]] toxicity, in contrast, increases monotonically with salinity, shifting the zone of greatest risk toward the seaward (high-salinity) end of migration.

## Key Findings
- **48-hr LC50 of phenol decreased linearly with increasing salinity:**
  - Fresh water (0%): 9.3 mg phenol/l
  - 60% sea water: 5.2 mg phenol/l
- **24-hr and 48-hr LC50 values were identical at each salinity**, indicating that lethality was determined within the first 24 hr of exposure.
- **Time-to-death patterns:**
  - In 15% and 20% sea water, all phenol-related deaths occurred within the first 12 hr of exposure.
  - In 40% and 60% sea water, deaths occurred more slowly but still concluded within 24 hr.
- **6-hr LC50 values** were only ~1.5 mg phenol/l higher than the corresponding 48-hr LC50 at each salinity tested, meaning phenol could be lethal to fish within the timeframe of a single tidal cycle.
- **Greatest response variability** occurred at 30% sea water, though this salinity did not correspond to a toxicity minimum (unlike zinc/ammonia).
- **All control fish survived** throughout testing, validating experimental conditions.
- **Statistical methods:** Survival time distributions were lognormal at concentrations killing ≥80% of fish; LC50 values and 95% confidence intervals were calculated via maximum likelihood estimation (Finney, 1952) using log-probability plots.
- **Ecological implication:** The seaward end of estuarine passage represents the highest-risk zone for phenol exposure to migrating salmonids, the opposite pattern from [[Zinc Sulphate]] and [[Ammonium Chloride]] toxicity, which peak in risk at lower-to-mid estuarine salinities.
