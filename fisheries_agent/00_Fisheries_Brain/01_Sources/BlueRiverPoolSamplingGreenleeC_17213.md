# Blue River Pool Sampling Survey - Greenlee County, Arizona (June 1997)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #salmonidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

A comprehensive fisheries survey was conducted along the [[Blue River]] in [[Greenlee County]], Arizona from June 16-19, 1997. The survey team from [[Arizona State University]] and [[United States Forest Service]] sampled 90 pools between [[Smith Ranch]] and F.S. Road 475 ([[Juan Miller Crossing]]). Nine fish species were documented using multiple sampling methods including backpack electrofishing, trammel nets, seines, and visual observation. The survey documented both native species including the [[Loach Minnow]] (Tiaroga cobitis) and invasive species such as [[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio) extending upstream to 3/4 mile downstream of [[Horse Creek]].

## Background & Description

The survey was conducted by Brian Bagley and Terry Inman from Arizona State University, with Bob Csargo from the United States Forest Service. The study area encompassed approximately 90 pools along a reach of the Blue River between Smith Ranch (upper limit) and Juan Miller Crossing at F.S. Road 475 (lower limit).

Sampling methods included:
- Gas-powered Smith-Root DC backpack electrofisher
- Dip nets
- Trammel nets
- Seines
- Visual observation with mask and snorkel

The survey documented species composition and distribution patterns across multiple sampling locations identified by proximity to tributary confluences and landmarks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Blue River]] system in Greenlee County represents important habitat for both native and non-native fish species in Arizona. The survey area includes multiple tributary confluences including [[Strayhorse Creek]], [[Beeler Creek]], [[Oak Creek]], [[Thomas Creek]], [[Little Blue Creek]], [[Maple Canyon]], [[Horse Canyon]], [[Jump Up Canyon]], [[Manning Canyon]], and [[Fritz Canyon]].

Notable ecological observations included:
- [[Leopard Frogs]] were observed near Manning Canyon but were very rare to absent throughout most of the survey reach
- The upstream extent of [[Carp]] distribution reached 3/4 mile downstream of Horse Creek, indicating invasive species penetration into the upper watershed
- [[Loach Minnow]] populations were present in moderate numbers at both the upper limit (Smith Ranch) and lower limit (Juan Miller Crossing) of the survey area

## Key Findings

**Species Inventory:**
Nine fish species were collected during the survey:
1. [[Rainbow Trout]] (Oncorhynchus mykiss) - Family [[Salmonidae]]
2. [[Carp]] (Cyprinus carpio) - Family [[Cyprinidae]]
3. [[Longfin Dace]] (Agosia chrysogaster) - Family Cyprinidae
4. [[Speckled Dace]] (Rhinichthys osculus) - Family Cyprinidae
5. [[Loach Minnow]] (Tiaroga cobitis) - Family Cyprinidae
6. [[Red Shiner]] (Cyprinella lutrensis) - Family Cyprinidae
7. [[Fathead Minnow]] (Pimephales promelas) - Family Cyprinidae
8. [[Sonora Sucker]] (Catostomus insignis) - Family [[Catostomidae]]
9. [[Desert Sucker]] (Pantosteus clarki) - Family Catostomidae

**Distribution Patterns:**
- Loach minnow were documented in moderate numbers at both survey boundaries (Smith Ranch and Juan Miller Crossing)
- Carp distribution extended upstream to 3/4 mile below Horse Creek
- Multiple native cyprinid species (longfin dace, speckled dace) were widely distributed throughout the survey reach
- Both native sucker species (Sonora sucker and desert sucker) were present in the system

**Amphibian Observations:**
- Leopard frogs were rare to absent throughout most of the survey reach
- Leopard frogs were observed only near Manning Canyon

The survey provides baseline data on fish community composition and distribution in the Blue River system, documenting the presence of native species of conservation concern alongside invasive species.
