# Recommendations for Marble Bluff Fish Facilities Operation to Avoid Cui-ui Hybridization

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cui-ui #Salmonidae #ConservationManagement #FreshwaterEcology

## Executive Summary

This 1989 letter from the [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) outlines urgent management recommendations for the 1990 spawning season at [[Marble Bluff Dam]] and its associated fish facilities on the lower [[Truckee River]] (Nevada). The core concern is preventing further genetic introgression ("backcrossing") between endangered pure-strain [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) and putative hybrid individuals (likely cui-ui × another catostomid, potentially Tahoe sucker) during the spring spawning run. Three primary interventions are proposed: (1) an electric fish barrier on the lower river delta, (2) a weir-and-trap system at the terminus of the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]] to physically sort and cull hybrids, and (3) full-time, on-site fishery biologist staffing during the spawning period to make real-time identification and operational decisions.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:**
  - **[[Cui-ui]]** (*Chasmistes cujus*) — an endangered, endemic lake sucker (Catostomidae) found only in [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. Anadromous-like life history within the closed Lahontan Basin: adults migrate from the lake up the [[Truckee River]] to spawn.
  - **[[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*) — a threatened salmonid subspecies co-occurring in the same river/lake system and passage infrastructure.
  - **Putative Cui-ui Hybrids** — suspected F1 (and potential F2 backcross) hybrids between cui-ui and another sucker species, representing a genetic contamination risk to the pure cui-ui gene pool.

- **Key infrastructure discussed:**
  - **[[Marble Bluff Dam]]** — a low-head dam on the lower Truckee River constructed to aid passage of cui-ui and cutthroat trout to Pyramid Lake, equipped with fish-handling facilities.
  - **[[Pyramid Lake Fishway]]** — a constructed fish passage structure allowing upstream migration around Marble Bluff Dam, terminating in a point where trapping is proposed.
  - **Truckee River Delta** — the point where the river enters Pyramid Lake; identified as one of two possible upstream entry avenues for spawning cui-ui and hybrids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat setting:** [[Pyramid Lake]] is a terminal, closed-basin desert lake in the Lahontan Basin (western Nevada), fed primarily by the Truckee River. This endorheic system supports one of the few remaining habitats for cui-ui, a species found nowhere else in the world.
- **Hydrological considerations:** Recommendations explicitly account for variable river discharge — the proposed electric fence must function as a barrier at flows up to **2,000 cfs**, and fishway operation timing depends on runoff patterns and the seasonal formation/movement of the cui-ui prespawning aggregate at the river mouth.
- **Hybridization risk:** The central ecological threat is introgressive hybridization — inadvertent spawning between pure-strain cui-ui and putative hybrids over "marginal substrate" downstream of Marble Bluff Dam, which could produce backcrossed **F2 generation** individuals, further diluting the pure cui-ui gene pool. This represents a critical genetic conservation concern for a federally protected, single-lake endemic species.
- **Sympatric species management:** The trapping protocol must discriminate among multiple co-migrating taxa — pure cui-ui, Lahontan cutthroat trout, putative hybrids, and other incidental species — each requiring different handling (pass upstream, cull, or return to lake).

## Key Findings

- **Recommendation 1 — Electric Barrier:** USFWS should evaluate installing an electric fence across the lower Truckee River between the delta and Marble Bluff Dam to block all upstream fish passage during the 1990 run, sized to operate effectively up to 2,000 cfs flow without causing fish injury or mortality. Purpose: prevent inadvertent spawning/hybridization on marginal substrate below the dam.
- **Recommendation 2 — Weir and Trap System:** Install a weir and trap at the terminus of the Pyramid Lake Fishway.
  - Fishway to run at **maximum discharge** but for a **truncated window** (several weeks to two months), timed to the runoff pattern and cui-ui prespawning aggregation dynamics.
  - Daily staffing required to net and sort **all fish** captured in the trap:
    - Pure-strain cui-ui & Lahontan cutthroat trout → released above trap for upstream migration/hatchery transport.
    - Putative hybrids → **culled** (removed from spawning population).
    - Other species → returned to the lake.
  - Rationale: removes hybridization risk while preserving natural reproduction of confirmed pure cui-ui and enabling hatchery propagation.
- **Recommendation 3 — Dedicated On-Site Biologist:** A fishery biologist should be stationed full-time at the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facilities]] before and during the spawning run to make time-critical decisions on hybrid identification, facility operations, and run timing — emphasizing that spawning-run management is a full-time responsibility requiring specialized expertise.
- **Institutional follow-up:** The Cui-ui Recovery Team committed to providing a detailed weir/trap design to USFWS (Duane Wainwright, Lahontan National Fish Hatchery) and offered ongoing technical consultation, with [[Chet Buchanan]] designated as the primary USFWS liaison during the team leader's temporary absence.
