# Astyanax armandoi n. sp. from Chiapas, Mexico (Pisces, Ostariophysi: Characidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This paper describes **[[Astyanax Armandoi]]**, a new characid fish from an unnamed arroyo at Penjamo, near Palenque, in northeastern [[Chiapas]], Mexico. The species is diagnosed by a combination of characters, most notably expanded, internally papillose lower-lip flaps, a flat snout meeting the upper lip at an acute angle, and a club-shaped orbitosphenoid process. The authors also summarize the confused nomenclatural history of *Astyanax* in Mexico and compare the new form with its presumed close relative, **[[Astyanax Aeneus]]**, and with the widespread northern form, **[[Astyanax Mexicanus]]**. The authors argue that the new species is probably derived from *A. aeneus* and merits recognition despite the lack of a recent revision of the genus.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The Mexican *Astyanax* are described as taxonomically complex and poorly diagnosed, with no revision since early in the twentieth century. Names proposed between 1853 and 1935 (e.g., *Tetragonopterus mexicanus* by Filippi, *T. aeneus* by Günther) were variously combined, split or synonymized, and later authors used different names for the same forms. The authors note that the bases for this inconsistent nomenclature were never discussed. Cave forms formerly placed in *[[Anoptichthys]]* were excluded from the summary.

**Type material.**
- Holotype: UANL 6380, mature male, 61.2 mm SL, from the unnamed creek at Penjamo.
- Paratypes: UANL 6202 (19 specimens, 46.8–80.1 mm SL), UANL 6378 (6 specimens, 57.2–82.1 mm SL, cleared and stained), UANL 6346 (13 specimens, 41.3–76.9 mm SL), UANL 6381 (1 specimen, 75.0 mm SL, cleared and stained). Collected by M.L. Lozano et al., 6 April 1985.
- Non-type material: UANL 6225 (45 specimens, 15.4–45.4 mm SL), collected 3 August 1981.
- Paratypes were distributed to several museums, including USNM, UMMZ, AMNH, TNHC, UNAM and others.

**Diagnosis and morphology.** The species is a large *Astyanax* (maximum known SL 82.1 mm) with a relatively high, strongly compressed body (greatest depth 2.5–2.8 in SL). Key diagnostic traits include:
- Lower lip expanded into irregular, variable flaps with internal rows of papillae.
- Snout flat in profile, meeting the upper lip at an angle of about 65°.
- Orbitosphenoid ventral process club-shaped, with two steps at its base, a flat distal portion and a concave anterior profile.
- Premaxillary ascending process acute, with its apex above or behind the posterior third of the second tooth.
- Anal fin base ending under a vertical through the posterior border of the adipose fin.
- Lateral line complete.

The holotype has 10 dorsal fin rays, and paratypes have a mean of 10.0 dorsal rays. A full comparative table (Table 1) lists proportional measurements in thousandths of SL for the holotype, paratypes, *A. aeneus* and *A. mexicanus*, and Table 3 compares about 40 qualitative osteological and external characters across the three species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The type locality is an arroyo 2.5–4 m wide and 0.3–0.4 m deep. Flow was nearly even over coarse gravel with small areas of sand and mud, and the upstream reaches were muddier. Water temperature was 27 °C in both August 1981 and April 1985, while air temperatures ranged from 25 to 37 °C. The authors infer that the site is fed by a stenothermal spring discharge, but the spring could not be located.

**Associated fauna.** Fishes collected with the new species were the pimelodid *Rhamdia guatemalensis*; the poeciliids *Poecilia mexicana*, *Xiphophorus maculatus*, *X. helleri* and *Heterandria bimaculata*; and the cichlids *Cichlasoma octofasciatum* and *Petenia splendida*.

**Hypoxia and lip expansion.** Winemiller (1989) reported that dermal lip protuberances in several characids are a response to aquatic surface respiration under hypoxia. The authors reject a simple hypoxia explanation for *A. armandoi*, because all collections, from all ages, show expanded lips. However, some specimens of other populations (including *A. aeneus* and *A. mexicanus* from low-oxygen localities) show expanded lips, possibly caused by oxygen depletion. The authors suggest that the trait may begin as an acclimation to hypoxia and become genetically fixed under selection, a process they describe as a possible preadaptation.

**Distribution.** *Astyanax* occurs throughout most of Mexico (except the northwest) and south to Argentina. *A. armandoi* is known only from its type locality, a restriction the authors cite as one reason for recognizing it. Its presumed relative, *A. aeneus*, occupies most of Chiapas and extends into Guatemala, as does an undescribed related form in the southeastern Pacific drainages.

## Key Findings

- **New species recognized.** *Astyanax armandoi* is described from Penjamo, Palenque, Chiapas, and distinguished from all known Mexican *Astyanax* by a unique combination of characters (papillose expanded lower lip, acute snout–lip angle, club-shaped orbitosphenoid process, anal fin origin position).
- **Close relationship.** The new species is considered most similar to *A. aeneus* and probably derived from a single invasion of the ancestral *Astyanax* stock into northern Central America and Mexico. The authors reject alternatives such as a relict southern invasion on the grounds of parsimony.
- **Diagnostic osteology.** Shape differences in the orbitosphenoid, premaxilla, maxilla, lower jaw (articular, retroarticular, dentary) and mouth gape separate the three species. The authors describe these as fairly constant in cleared and stained specimens.
- **Lip expansion as a character.** Expanded lips may be a hypoxia-related plastic trait that became fixed. The authors argue that if the population is isolated and all individuals share the trait, it is probably genetically controlled.
- **Taxonomic position.** The authors consider the new form to be at least at a semispecies level, based on the examination of nearby *A. aeneus* populations.
- **Nomenclatural conclusions.** The authors provisionally recognize several Mexican forms as probably valid (*A. mexicanus*, *A. aeneus*, an *Astyanax* from the Nazas-Aguanaval complex, an *Astyanax* from a spring in the Upper Rio Balsas, a form related to *A. aeneus* in coastal Chiapas–Guatemala, and *A. armandoi*), while deferring judgment on *A. f. altior* of the Yucatán Peninsula and on the cave forms.
- **Justification for description.** The authors argue that taxonomists should describe new forms, especially endemics, despite the absence of a revision, because rapid development may cause extinctions before forms are studied.
- **Etymology.** The name honors Biol. Armando J. Contreras-Balderas, a collector who supported the fieldwork and the characterization of the species.
- **Comparative material.** Additional *A. aeneus* material from the Rio Usumacinta (Boca del Cerro, Chacamax at La Libertad, Chancala) and *A. mexicanus* from Rio San Juan, Nuevo León, was used for comparative statistics.
