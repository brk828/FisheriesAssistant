# Freshwater Ecosystems, Rabbit Virus Trial, and Reverse Weathering: Science Issue Excerpt (22 October 1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #geochemistry

## Executive Summary

This excerpt from *Science* (Vol. 270, 22 October 1995) combines three items. The main feature is a policy essay by [[Naiman]], [[Magnuson]], [[McKnight]], [[Stanford]] and [[Karr]] on the state of U.S. [[Freshwater Ecosystems]]. It argues that current laws and programs are fragmented and under-funded, and it calls for a science-based, prioritized national freshwater research program. The issue also includes a news item on a failed [[Rabbit Calicivirus Disease]] (RCD) biocontrol release in Australia, and a short news piece on the new [[Global Oscillation Network Group]] (GONG) solar network and related programs. A research article by Mackenzie and Kump examines [[Reverse Weathering]] and authigenic clay formation in Amazon delta sediments.

## Background & Description

**Freshwater policy essay (pp. 584–585, continued on p. 585 in the scanned text).** The authors describe a patchwork of federal and state legislation, including the [[Clean Water Act]] and the [[Endangered Species Act]], together with regulations and judicial decisions that have developed over time. Sewage and point-source pollution controls have been broadly successful, but the authors note that the nation's freshwater systems remain under pressure. Pollution has shifted toward diffuse [[Nonpoint-Source Pollution]], and many aquatic species are threatened with extinction. Freshwater systems are described as among the most ecologically endangered in the United States.

**News items (p. 2).** These include:
- A [[Rabbit Calicivirus Disease]] release on Wardang Island, South Australia, intended as a limited, quick-kill control for introduced [[European Rabbit]] populations. Earlier, a [[Myxoma Virus]] introduced in 1950 had only partly controlled rabbits.
- The [[Ocean Drilling Program]] (ODP), which has moved toward higher-risk drilling and has an annual budget of $45 million.
- The Tech Corps volunteer program, which helps schools adopt computer and communications technology.
- The GONG network, which uses six observation stations (California, Hawaii, Australia, India, the Canary Islands and Chile) to measure solar oscillations every 60 seconds.

**Reverse weathering article (pp. 586–587).** [[Mackenzie]] and [[Kump]] review the reverse-weathering hypothesis, first proposed about 30 years earlier. Under this hypothesis, marine clay formation removes soluble cations and releases carbon dioxide (acidity). They discuss an experimental study by [[Michalopoulos And Aller]] that demonstrates rapid clay formation in Amazon delta sediments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water quantity and quality.** Water consumption in U.S. states has more than doubled and is likely to double again in 20 years. Water quality still fails to meet current standards in one-third of the nation's freshwater ecosystems.
- **Hydrological change.** Altered flow patterns have reduced habitat for organisms adapted to natural discharge and water-level cycles. Rivers are less able to act as migratory corridors, and [[Riparian Zones]] no longer filter runoff between uplands and aquatic systems.
- **Extinction risk.** The essay cites 65% of crayfishes and 75% of [[Unionid Mussels]] as imperiled. For [[Pacific Salmon]], it reports that 74% of 214 stocks face high or moderate risk of extinction. (The scan garbles the opening percentage for fish, so the fish figure is not reproduced here.)
- **Invasive species.** Exotic invasions such as the [[Zebra Mussel]] are cited as a significant threat to ecosystem integrity.
- **Amazon delta geochemistry.** Muddy tropical deltas receive about 60% of the continental river particulate flux to the oceans. Clay formation in Amazon shelf sediments could consume about 10% of the present global riverine K⁺ flux.

## Key Findings

### Freshwater policy essay

1. **Research priorities.** The essay identifies four priority areas: ecosystem goods and services, predictive management, solving future problems, and achieving coordination. Predictive management requires data on disturbance regimes and their legacies so that cumulative and synergistic impacts can be forecast.
2. **Cost of the proposed program.** The authors estimate that a comprehensive national approach would cost about $200 million per year, which is less than 1% of what the United States spends annually on freshwater procurement, regulation and remedial protection.
3. **Inefficient spending.** More than $150 million per year goes to recovering degraded [[Columbia River]] salmon and steelhead runs. The authors note that no monitoring program exists to measure key sources of mortality, and this spending is well over twice the annual budget of the [[National Science Foundation]] (NSF) Environmental Biology Program. This program is the main competitive source of funding for basic freshwater ecology research.
4. **Implementation, institutions.** Recommendations include strengthening existing agencies, enhancing agency programs for innovative research, creating regional institutions that link human and natural sciences, and launching an integrated NSF interdisciplinary freshwater program.
5. **Implementation, knowledge and literacy.** Recommendations include a national freshwater biodiversity center with sensitive biotic indices, an array of [[Long-Term Ecological Research]] sites focused on fresh waters, and improved education and continuing training for scientists and managers.
6. **Conclusion.** The authors call for science-based policy, prioritized research, and cost-effective, monitored management. They argue that without these steps the nation cannot protect its long-term cultural, economic and environmental interests in freshwater resources.

### Rabbit virus trial (news)

- The RCD virus was released in a quarantined enclosure on Wardang Island, where it killed 80% of the rabbits within a few days.
- Virus-killed rabbits appeared outside the enclosure, and infected rabbits were found on the mainland near Point Pearce. Scientists suspect [[Bushflies]] carried the virus across about 5 km of sea.
- Scientists believe the outbreak has been contained, but officials want further public discussion before any wider release.

### Reverse weathering (research article)

- Michalopoulos and Aller formed K–Fe–Mg clay minerals (a mica-type clay with a 9.9 Å spacing) in unaltered, anoxic Amazon delta sediments incubated for 12 to 18 months at 28 °C.
- Field evidence suggests that similar clays form naturally in Amazon delta sediments. Clay formation or reconstitution is said to account for about 7% of the global riverine fluorine supply to the ocean and about 10% of the potassium input.
- Aluminum appears to be mobile in these sediments, which challenges the view that Al is immobile. Reactive silica, from diatom dissolution or degraded aluminosilicates, appears to limit how much new clay forms.
- If silica controls clay formation, authigenic clays become a sink for biogenic silica in the ocean. Mackenzie and Kump state that this supports the reverse-weathering scheme originally suggested by [[Mackenzie And Garrels]], although hydrothermal cycling at mid-ocean ridges has since been recognized as a major sink for dissolved elements.
