## Executive Summary
This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Section 7 consultation letter (superseding an earlier erroneous draft) addressing a habitat restoration project for the federally listed **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** (*Lepidomeda vittata*) at Dines Tank, Coconino County, Arizona. The Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests proposed augmenting **[[Large Woody Debris]] (LWD)** in the tank to benefit spinedace habitat. USFWS provided informal concurrence with a "not likely to adversely affect" determination for the completed action and related fencing/anchoring activities, but declined to grant a blanket concurrence for unspecified future LWD additions pending a more detailed long-term management and monitoring plan.

## Background & Description
The source text is a regulatory/administrative consultation letter and does not contain morphological, meristic, or anatomical data on *Lepidomeda vittata*. No descriptions of body form, fin structure, coloration, or size are provided in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Dines Tank, an impounded freshwater habitat within the Little Colorado River drainage in Coconino County, Arizona, historically deficient in **[[Large Woody Debris]]**, a habitat feature noted as generally scarce across many Arizona streams.
- **Habitat enhancement strategy:** Introduction of felled trees/LWD into the tank intended to create structural complexity (cover, flow refugia, substrate diversity) hypothesized to produce a "beneficial response" for the spinedace population.
- **Associated habitat management actions:**
  - Anchoring a previously felled tree within the tank to prevent displacement during flood events.
  - Repair of existing livestock exclosure fencing to limit grazing-related habitat degradation.
  - Removal of superfluous fencing within the exclosure.
- **Threats/vulnerabilities noted:** Risk of LWD being washed out during flood/high-flow events, indicating the tank is subject to periodic hydrological disturbance.
- **Conservation status context:** *Lepidomeda vittata* is treated as a species of concern under the Endangered Species Act, subject to Section 7 consultation requirements for federal actions with potential effects on the species. See also **[[Endangered Species Act Section 7 Consultation]]**.

## Key Findings
- USFWS concurred that the completed LWD augmentation (implemented August 12, 1997) and associated actions (tree anchoring, fencing repair, removal of superfluous fencing) are **"not likely to adversely affect"** the **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]**.
- Informal consultation occurred via phone on August 11, 1997, prior to project implementation, followed by written documentation submitted August 15, 1997, fulfilling conditions for concurrence.
- USFWS **declined blanket concurrence** for future, unspecified LWD augmentations, citing insufficient information on:
  - Spatial criteria for tree/LWD placement.
  - An upper limit on the quantity of LWD to be added.
  - Contingency plans for LWD loss during flood events.
  - A clear, measurable definition of the anticipated "beneficial response" for the spinedace.
- USFWS recommended that future proposals include:
  - A cap on the amount of LWD or duration (in years) for which concurrence is sought.
  - Commitment to timely written reporting after each augmentation action.
  - Provision of all pre- and post-project sampling and monitoring data.
- The agency expressed support for the broader habitat goal, acknowledging that **scarcity of LWD is a widespread problem in Arizona streams**, and indicated that a sufficiently detailed long-term plan would likely receive future concurrence.
- Designated USFWS contacts for **[[Little Colorado Spinedace]]** recovery: Lesley Fitzpatrick (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) and Chuck Minckley (Parker Office, Arizona Fishery Resources Office).
