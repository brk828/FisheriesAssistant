# Virgin River Chub (*Gila seminuda*) — Taxonomic Reclassification & Muddy River Status Review

## Executive Summary
This 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) proposal addresses a taxonomic reclassification of the [[Virgin River Chub]] based on new systematic work by DeMarais et al. (1992). Historically treated as a subspecies of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*), the Virgin River population is elevated to full species status as *Gila seminuda*. Critically, the study also determined that the previously separate "[[Moapa Roundtail Chub]]" (Muddy/Moapa River population, formerly classified as an undescribed subspecies of *G. robusta*) is **conspecific** with the Virgin River Chub. This finding triggers two regulatory actions: (1) reclassifying the Virgin River listing from "subspecies" to a "vertebrate population segment," and (2) initiating a formal status review of the Muddy River population to assess whether it independently warrants listing as threatened or endangered under the Endangered Species Act (ESA).

## Background & Description
- **Common name:** Virgin River Chub
- **Family:** Cyprinidae (minnows)
- **Body form:** Silvery minnow
- **Average total length:** ~20 cm (7.9 in)
- **Maximum total length:** ~45 cm (17.7 in)
- **Scalation:** Small, deeply embedded scales present on back, breast, and belly (distinguishing feature among most individuals)
- **Diagnostic fin ray counts:** 9–10 dorsal, anal, and pelvic fin rays (distinguishes it from other [[Gila (Genus)]] species)
- **Gill rakers:** 24–31
- **Taxonomic history:**
  - First described as a full species, *Gila seminuda*, by Cope and Yarrow (1875)
  - Reduced to subspecies status (*G. robusta seminuda*) by Max M. Ellis (1914), who considered it morphologically intermediate between [[Roundtail Chub]] (*G. robusta*) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*G. elegans*)
  - Elevated back to full species rank, *Gila seminuda*, by DeMarais et al. (1992); accepted by the American Fisheries Society/American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists (ASIH) Fish Names Committee (Nelson, in litt., 1993)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Range:** Endemic to the [[Virgin River]] (southwestern Utah, northwestern Arizona, southeastern Nevada) and the [[Muddy River]] (Moapa River, southeastern Nevada), a Virgin River tributary
- **Historical Virgin River distribution:** Restricted between Hurricane, Utah, and the confluence with the [[Colorado River]]
- **Historical Muddy River distribution:** Extended from Muddy River headwaters to its confluence with the Virgin River in Clark County, Nevada
- **Current Virgin River status:** Present in low numbers across an ~80 km reach of mainstem river
- **Current Muddy River status:** Abundance/distribution unknown as of 1994; documented range contraction and decline since the 1960s; eliminated from lower Muddy River by 1974–1975 (Cross 1976)
- **Reproductive isolation:** Virgin River population is genetically distinct and reproductively isolated from the Muddy River population due to the creation of [[Lake Mead]], which severed historical connectivity
- **Sympatric native fish (Muddy River):** Only four native species occur, including Virgin River Chub, [[Moapa Dace]] (*Moapa coriacea*, endangered), [[Moapa White River Springfish]] (*Crenichthys baileyi*, category 2 candidate), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*, category 2 candidate)
- **Nonnative fish competitors/threats (~12 species documented in Muddy River):** shortfin molly (*Poecilia mexicana*), mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*), common carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), red shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), fathead minnow (*Pimephales promelas*), black bullhead (*Ameiurus melas*), channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*), largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*), green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*), golden shiner (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*), rainbow trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), and blue tilapia (*Tilapia aurea*)
- **Parasitism:** Heavily infected with anchor worm (*Lernaea* spp.), causing lesions, blood loss, and secondary bacterial/fungal/viral infections; also documented with Asian fish tapeworm (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*), which can cause weight loss, sterility, and intestinal blockage in severe cases
- **Habitat threats:** Dewatering, impoundments, sedimentation, pollution, channel alteration, disease, competition/predation from nonnative species, irrigation return flows, dairy farm organic waste effluent, reduced instream flow/dilution capacity, and Lake Mead inundation effects (slowed flows, increased sediment deposition)
- **Anthropogenic water use pressures (Muddy River/Moapa Valley):** Supports agricultural, municipal, and energy interests; four municipalities (Moapa, Logandale, Glendale, Overton, NV) plus a 657-megawatt coal-fired power plant draw on the system; irrigation diversions can cause the lower river to run completely dry seasonally

## Key Findings
- **Taxonomic reclassification:** DeMarais et al. (1992) determined *Gila seminuda* warrants full species rank, and that the Muddy River population (previously "[[Moapa Roundtail Chub]]," *G. robusta* ssp.) is conspecific with the Virgin River Chub rather than a separate subspecies of [[Roundtail Chub]].
- **Regulatory action:** USFWS proposed changing the Endangered Species Act listing for the Virgin River population from subspecies (*Gila robusta seminuda*) to a "vertebrate population segment" designation of *Gila seminuda*, effective under 50 CFR § 17.11(h).
- **Status review initiated:** A formal status review was opened for the Muddy River population to determine whether it independently warrants listing as threatened or endangered, given it is now recognized as the same species as the already-endangered Virgin River population.
- **Population decline documentation (Muddy River):**
  - Abundance decreased >83% at a 1938 collection site by 1964
  - Abundance decreased ~92% at a 1942 collection site by 1964 (Wilson et al. 1966)
  - Upstream distributional shift documented 1964–1968 (Deacon & Bradley 1972)
  - Complete elimination from lower Muddy River by 1974–1975 (Cross 1976)
- **Cumulative decline drivers:** Parasitism, altered flow/water quality/substrate, channelization, and nonnative species establishment (synthesizing Wilson et al. 1966; Deacon & Bradley 1972; Cross 1976; Deacon et al. 1964; Hubbs & Deacon 1964).
- **Legal/administrative context:** The Service faced litigation (Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance) with a court-ordered 20-month deadline (set March 15, 1994) to complete a critical habitat proposal for Virgin River Chub, adding urgency to reclassification and range-wide status reevaluation.
- **Stakeholder concerns:** Moapa Valley landowners expressed concern over potential listing of a second fish species in the Muddy River and impacts to agricultural, domestic, and industrial water supplies.
- **Data gaps identified for future research:** Nonnative species interaction studies (predation, dietary/spatial overlap, disease transference) specific to Virgin River Chub in the Muddy River (contrasted with existing studies on [[Moapa Dace]] and [[Moapa White River Springfish]]); chub movement/connectivity data between Virgin and Muddy Rivers; current range, distribution, and population size data for the Muddy River segment.
