# Economic Effects of Rainbow Trout Stocking by U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Hatcheries, FY 2004

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This report, summarized from a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) study by Dr. Jim Caudill (Division of Economics, December 2005), estimates the recreational and economic value of [[Rainbow Trout]] produced and stocked by [[National Fish Hatchery System]] (NFHS) facilities in fiscal year 2004. Eleven hatcheries in six regions stocked about 9.4 million rainbow trout weighing 4.1 million pounds across 16 states. The stocked fish generated 3,378,616 angler days, $172.8 million in retail sales, $325.1 million in total economic output, 3,502 jobs, and $197.9 million in net economic value (consumer surplus). Each dollar of rainbow trout hatchery budget expenditure was associated with $32.20 in retail sales and $36.88 in net economic value.

## Background & Description

The USFWS manages a national network of hatcheries, fish technology centers, fish health centers and fishery resource offices. Its mandates include recovery and restoration of depleted fish stocks, conservation of imperiled species under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and mitigation for fishery losses caused by federal water development projects, including dams. Hatchery fish stocked in mitigation partly compensate anglers for habitat lost to such projects.

The study is a reconnaissance-level estimate. Because data quality varied by region, different data sets and methods were used for different regions. Economic effects were calculated as:

- **Economic impacts**: total output, jobs, job income, and federal and state tax revenue generated by consumer spending on angling-related goods and services.
- **Economic value**: the net benefit to anglers, measured as consumer surplus, i.e., willingness to pay minus actual cost.

The methodology links trout stocking numbers to angler days, angler days to retail expenditures, and retail expenditures to economic impacts using [[Regional Input-Output Modeling System]] (RIMS II) multipliers. Impacts are state-level and gross rather than net.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Federal hatcheries also provide wider environmental goods and services, including:

- **Recreation**: replacing lost fishing opportunities, creating new ones, and visitor tours.
- **Information**: education programs, [[Fisheries Research]], and fish health diagnostics.
- **Ecological use**: mitigation of environmental damage.
- **Federal spending**: hatchery budgets affecting local and regional economies.

Several hatcheries also conserve imperiled non-game fishes and endangered species. The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] culture program, for example, supports endangered fishes in the lower Colorado River. Hatcheries also supply disease-free eggs and broodstock to state agencies, and some serve tribal trust responsibilities on Native American lands.

## Key Findings

**Hatchery Network and Stocking**
- The study covers 11 [[National Fish Hatchery]] facilities across six regions, including [[Alchesay-Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]], [[Neosho National Fish Hatchery]], [[Dale Hollow National Fish Hatchery]], [[Norfork National Fish Hatchery]], [[Wolf Creek National Fish Hatchery]], [[Garrison Dam National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Jones Hole National Fish Hatchery]].
- Arkansas received the most stocked trout by number (2.9 million), followed by Colorado (1.2 million) and Tennessee (1.0 million). Stocked fish totaled 4.1 million pounds.

**Angler Use and Economic Impacts (FY 2004)**
- Angler days: 3,378,616 total (approximately 3.4 million), of which about 89 percent were resident and 11 percent non-resident by the report's breakdown.
- Retail sales: $172.8 million.
- Total economic output: $325.1 million.
- Jobs: 3,502, with job income of $80.3 million.
- Sales and motor fuel tax: $9.9 million. State income tax: $2.9 million. Federal income tax: $10.6 million.
- Total tax revenue: $24.4 million.

**Economic Value**
- Net economic value (consumer surplus): $197.9 million. Region 4 contributed $139.3 million, Region 6 $45.1 million, Region 3 $8.2 million and Region 2 $5.3 million.
- Net economic value per angler day was estimated at $45 to $69 for trout fishing, based on [[Aiken and La Rouche]] (2005) and related work.

**Regional Highlights**
- [[Dale Hollow National Fish Hatchery]] (Tennessee and Kentucky) and [[Norfork National Fish Hatchery]] (Arkansas) were among the largest contributors, with Dale Hollow generating about 640,341 angler days and $50.3 million in total output.
- [[Hotchkiss National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Garrison Dam National Fish Hatchery]] contributed substantial output in Region 6, with Garrison Dam's rainbow trout production ranking among the largest.
- [[Chattahoochee Forest National Fish Hatchery]] (Georgia) reported 2004 production about 30 percent lower than in other years, due to fewer fingerlings received from another source. Its economic output is therefore not representative of normal production.
- [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] had 2004 output about 80 percent lower than normal because of a raceway construction project.

**Cost-Effectiveness Comparison**
- Rainbow trout budget expenditures in FY 2004 were $5.4 million.
- Each $1 of budget expenditure was associated with $32.20 in retail sales and $36.88 in net economic value. The authors caution that these ratios are for broad comparison only and are not benefit-cost ratios.

**Limitations**
- Impacts are gross state-level estimates. Local and county-level data were unavailable, and resident spending is a transfer rather than new money, so net impacts could not be computed.
- Regional data sets and methods varied, and the study was a reconnaissance-level estimate.
