# Legal proceedings against USFWS to protect loach minnow/spikedace

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #ConservationLaw #FreshwaterEcology

## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific study but a legal notice of intent to sue, filed by attorney Matthew Kenna on behalf of the [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]] and the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] against the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS). The core issue is the FWS's failure to designate legally mandated "critical habitat" under the Endangered Species Act (ESA) for two southwestern freshwater fish species: the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). Both species were listed as Threatened in the mid-1980s, with a critical habitat package proposed in 1985 but never finalized due to political pressure from the cattle and mining industries. The document underscores a broader ecological crisis, noting that 90% of native fish species in the Southwest are biologically endangered due to habitat degradation and invasive species introduction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While lacking morphological detail, the text provides significant ecological and habitat-threat context:

- **Habitat**: Both species are native to the [[Greater Gila Ecosystem]], a freshwater riverine system in the Southwestern United States (Arizona/New Mexico region).
- **Threats identified**:
  - Overgrazing (linked to the cattle industry)
  - Dewatering of streams and rivers
  - Logging
  - Human-exacerbated flooding
  - Introduction of exotic (non-native) fish and amphibian species
- **Regional Context**: The document states that **90% of the Southwest's native fish species** are biologically endangered, primarily due to habitat loss and competition/predation from introduced exotic species. This positions the [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] as indicator species within a broader regional biodiversity crisis affecting [[Native Southwestern Fishes]].
- **Regulatory Habitat Status**: Despite being listed as Threatened, no *legally designated* critical habitat exists for either species, leaving their ecological ranges without formal federal protection mechanisms (e.g., restrictions on grazing permits, water diversion, or mining near occupied habitat).

## Key Findings

- **Listing Dates**: The [[Loach Minnow]] was listed as Threatened in 1985; the [[Spikedace]] was listed as Threatened on July 8, 1986 (51 F.R. 23769).
- **Proposed Critical Habitat**: A critical habitat designation package for both species was proposed on June 18, 1985 (50 F.R. 25390) — notably *before* the Spikedace's formal listing — demonstrating that critical habitat was "determinable" at the time of listing per ESA Section 4(a)(3).
- **Regulatory Failure**: Despite the 1985 proposal, FWS never finalized critical habitat designation for either species, a delay attributed to "intense political pressure" from cattle ranching, mining interests, and county governments.
- **Legal Violation**: This inaction constitutes a violation of ESA Section 4(a)(3)(A), which requires critical habitat designation to occur *concurrently* with a threatened/endangered species listing when such habitat is prudent and determinable.
- **Requested Remedy**: The plaintiffs requested immediate implementation of the 1985 proposed critical habitat as an interim measure, to be followed by a scientific review reflecting habitat changes since 1985.
- **Broader Pattern**: This case is framed as the opening action in a larger campaign by the [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]] and [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] to compel protection for the region's imperiled native fish fauna as a whole.
- **Timeline Pressure**: The 60-day notice (dated May 13, 1993) set a mid-July 1993 deadline for FWS compliance before formal litigation would commence.
