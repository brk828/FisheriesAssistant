# Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge Draft Master Plan - Edition 3

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Odontophoridae #endangered-species #refuge-planning #grassland-restoration

## Executive Summary

This document is the third edition Planning Bulletin for [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] (NWR) in [[Pima County]], [[Arizona]], presenting the draft master plan for public review in 1986. The refuge's primary mission is recovery of the endangered [[Masked Bobwhite Quail]] (*Colinus virginianus ridgwayi*) through restoration of [[Sonoran Savanna Grassland]] habitat in the [[Altar Valley]]. The plan proposes a phased, experimental approach (1987-1991 and 1992-1996) balancing masked bobwhite recovery with wildlife diversity, public recreation, and limited economic uses. Key strategies include cessation of grazing impacts, brush management, native grass seeding, water retention through check dams, and potential reintroduction of extirpated species including [[Pronghorn Antelope]] (*Antilocapra americana mexicanus*). The plan acknowledges significant knowledge gaps regarding masked bobwhite ecology and historical ecosystem composition, necessitating adaptive management and ongoing research. Public use programs include interpretive trails, auto tours, hunting (excluding quail), and camping, with approximately 10% of the refuge closed to hunting for safety and intensive public use around headquarters and [[Aguirre Lake]].

## Background & Description

[[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] was established primarily for recovery of the [[Masked Bobwhite Quail]], an endangered subspecies. The refuge encompasses lands in the [[Altar Valley]] of southeastern Arizona, including areas previously managed as Buenos Aires Ranch under Victorio Company. The planning process involved collection of resource data, analysis of compatible activities, review by specialists from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[University of Arizona]], [[Soil Conservation Service]], and other agencies.

The refuge supports diverse habitats including semidesert grasslands, desert shrub, wetlands, riparian deciduous areas, and ephemeral washes. The region historically supported [[Sonoran Savanna Grassland]] characterized by scattered brush cover and native grasses. Current knowledge gaps include: precise habitat requirements of masked bobwhite, ecosystem response to grazing cessation, original species complement, and carrying capacity for native and extirpated species.

The refuge contains 36 "species of special emphasis" recognized as significant on local, state, or national scales, including: 2 mammals ([[Pronghorn Antelope]], [[Antelope Jackrabbit]]); 5 reptiles ([[Gila Monster]], vine snake, [[Desert Tortoise]], western box turtle, yellow mud turtle); 3 plant species; and 30 bird species including [[Mourning Dove]], [[White-winged Dove]], and various waterbirds, Mexican vagrants, and peripherals. [[Bald Eagle]] and [[Peregrine Falcon]] are present. The refuge lies in a drainage that historically supported native fishes, though none currently exist on refuge waters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Altar Valley]] ecosystem has been significantly altered from its historical state. The draft master plan acknowledges this is "by necessity experimental" due to limited understanding of masked bobwhite ecology and historical ecosystem structure. The plan aims to restore [[Sonoran Savanna Grassland]] through multiple interventions:

**Habitat Management**: Limiting brush encroachment in some areas while thinning or planting in others to create scattered cover characteristic of savannas. Native grass seeding in selected areas. Surface water management through vegetation recovery and check dam installation to slow runoff, promote aggradation of washes, and potentially create perennial flow where currently ephemeral.

**Grazing Research**: Phase 1 includes experimental grazing program designed by Refuge Manager with range and wildlife experts to determine if grazing serves as useful management tool. Research plan subject to Section 7 consultation for masked bobwhite impacts. If beneficial, commercial grazing as management tool would be implemented in Phase 2.

**Species Reintroductions**: Extirpated species may be reintroduced if compatible with masked bobwhite objectives. [[Aplomado Falcon]] reintroduction considered possible without extensive habitat alterations; wild quail populations adapted to raptor predation. [[Pronghorn Antelope]] (*A. a. mexicanus*) reintroduction is high priority; interagency release team to be formed in first year to conduct studies and develop release plan if appropriate. [[Mexican Wolf]] reintroduction unlikely as refuge marginally suited. [[Ocelot]], [[Jaguar]], and jaguarundi not considered native to refuge habitat.

**Water Features**: Development of marsh at [[Aguirre Lake]] and additional ponds and riparian areas to support aquatic and riparian-dependent birds and reptiles. Small food plot in old farm area for waterfowl, doves, and masked bobwhite.

**Fire Management**: Controlled burns proposed for deer habitat management.

The refuge may support [[Endangered Species Program]] for rare desert fishes through rearing ponds or refugium, if compatible with refuge objectives. No sport fishery program planned; quality fishery would require put-and-take program contrary to FWS policy.

## Key Findings

**Primary Management Objective**: Emphasize masked bobwhite production until self-sustaining population established. All developments for other programs carefully scrutinized for effects on masked bobwhite. Small percentage of prime masked bobwhite habitat may overlap with other wildlife programs and public uses. Once secure population achieved, refuge progresses to more balanced program supporting maximum possible masked bobwhite numbers within common sense limits.

**Habitat Classification**: Refuge mapped for masked bobwhite habitat potential: (1) Suitable/High Potential, (2) Medium Potential, (3) Low Potential, (4) Not Suitable. Areas with very low potential devoted to other species.

**Public Use Program**: 
- **Visitor Center**: Major ranch house remodeled; handicapped accessible; staffed during high use periods
- **Auto Tour Routes**: Phase 1 = ~7 miles on improved existing roads; Phase 2 extension to ~15 miles if impacts acceptable
- **Hiking Trails**: Interpretive loop around [[Aguirre Lake]] complex, partially handicapped accessible
- **Conducted Tours**: 100 programs per year by end of Phase 2
- **Environmental Education**: No provision initially; may be added if demand develops

**Hunting Regulations**: 
- Entire refuge closed to quail hunting effective fall 1987 to protect endangered bobwhites
- Approximately 10% of refuge (headquarters/Aguirre Lake area) closed to all hunting for safety
- Continued hunting of mule deer, whitetailed deer, javelina, waterfowl, doves, rabbits, feral hogs, predators, furbearers, mountain lions
- Refuge open September 1 through end of javelina season; closed to hunting other times (except mountain lions, predators, furbearers during other game seasons)
- Recommendation: designate Buenos Aires NWR as special hunting unit for better management
- [[Mormon Lake]] remains open to waterfowl and dove hunting; most stock ponds remain open

**Other Public Uses**:
- Trapping allowed with special use permits (Phase 1)
- No hobby collecting; scientific collecting with permits
- Gold panning with written Victorio Company permission (pans only, no machinery)
- Horseback riding allowed north of Arivaca Road and on designated trail south
- Camping: designated sites for large RVs; dispersed primitive camping along designated roads (not in essential masked bobwhite habitat, high public use areas, riparian areas, or high fire danger areas)
- No off-road vehicle use; vehicles prohibited in washes

**Economic Uses**: All require special use permits and must be compatible with wildlife objectives:
- Beekeeping allowed outside main public use area
- Mineral/energy exploration subject to standard FWS policy
- Woodcutting allowed only as management tool after [[Masked Bobwhite Ecology Study]] provides guidance
- Experimental grazing in Phase 1 to determine utility as management tool

**Cultural Resources**: No formal archaeological surveys conducted due to budget constraints. Localized surveys where construction planned. Significant sites protected if discovered.

**Land Ownership**: Plan assumes FWS control of all refuge lands (including State of Arizona lands) necessary for quality wildlife and public use programs while protecting natural resources.

**Public Review Process**: Two public meetings scheduled (September 3 at refuge headquarters, September 4 in Tucson). Comment deadline: September 30, 1986. Final plan to be distributed winter 1986.

**Phased Implementation**: Two 5-year phases (1987-1991, 1992-1996) allow evaluation of initial construction impacts on wildlife and habitat before extensive developments. Management experiments stipulated to determine successful approaches. Full-time outdoor recreation planner coordinates public use program including volunteer coordination.
