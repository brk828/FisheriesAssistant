# A New Species of Lachlania from New Mexico, with Notes on the Genus

#entomology #ephemeroptera #oligoneuriidae

## Executive Summary

This 1970 taxonomic paper in the *Proceedings of the Entomological Society of Washington* (Vol. 72, No. 1, pp. 55-65) describes [[Lachlania Dencyanna]], a new mayfly species from the [[Gila River]] system in New Mexico. It is the third *Lachlania* species described from North America. The authors separate it from its congeners using forewing crossvein counts in the adult and mid-dorsal abdominal tubercles in the nymph. They also show that crossvein number is highly variable within species, so it should be used cautiously in taxonomy. The paper includes a key to North American *Lachlania* and notes on wing venation, nymph morphology and adult mating behaviour.

## Background & Description

**Study area and collections.** Specimens came from the [[Gila National Forest]] north of [[Silver City]], near the [[Gila Cliff Dwelling National Monument]]. The authors describe the local mayfly fauna as poorly known. Other species collected there included *Heptagenia petersi*, *Leptohyphes apache*, *Thraulodes brunneus*, several *Baetis* species, *Isonychia intermedia* and *Tricorythodes* sp. The paper names only the *Lachlania* species as new.

**Adult male (imago).** Body 12-15 mm; forewing 11-13 mm; caudal filaments 38-43 mm. Forewings are iridescent blue when dry and green in alcohol. Tergum 10 is red-brown with a dark sclerotized "V". The male genitalia have a sclerotized "Y" on the subgenital plate and a dorsomesal apical corner on the penes that is the longest.

**Adult female (imago).** Body 11-17 mm; forewing 13-15 mm; caudal filaments 8-11 mm. The sublateral sclerotized bars are more conspicuous than in males, and the subanal plate is broadly and deeply excavated.

**Mature nymph.** Body 15-17 mm; caudal filaments 10-12 mm. The defining character is a single median tubercle on the posterior margin of abdominal terga 2-9, each densely covered in short spines. Other traits include well-developed mesl and metepimeral lateral projections, abdominal claws with 3-6 denticles, and a pronounced posterolateral projection on segment 9.

**Holotype and types.** The holotype male was collected at the junction of the East Fork and main Gila River, 40 miles north of Silver City, on 10 September 1967 (R. W. Koss and D. W. Argyle). The authors deposited paratypes across university and museum collections in the United States, Canada, Belgium and the United Kingdom.

**Etymology.** The species is named after Dency Anne Koss, the first author's wife.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The East Fork of the Gila River at its junction with the Gila River is described as warm, turbid and rapid. It is mostly 6 inches to 2 feet deep and 6-10 feet wide, and unshaded for most of the day.

**Nymphal habitat.** Nymphs were not found clinging to rocks, unlike *[[Lachlania Powelli]]* as described by Edmunds (1951). Instead, they were abundant on sticks and other vegetation caught in rock crevices. Nymphs died when kept overnight in standing water in a bucket.

**Emergence timing.** In July most nymphs were 1-2 weeks from emergence, and some had darkened wing pads. No adults were captured in July or from rearing cages in the river. All adults were collected on the September 10 trip.

**Adult flight and mating behaviour.** Activity began around 11:30 A.M. and ceased at about 1:30 P.M., at an air temperature of 82°F.
- Males flew 1-2 inches above the water, 3-5 feet back and forth across the stream at roughly 45° to the current, with tails spread.
- Males sometimes landed on the water for 1-2 seconds, possibly as a resting behaviour. One male landed and took off 14 times in 2 minutes.
- Males also rested in grass along the bank, which the authors say differs from Edmunds' (1951) observation that *L. powelli* never alighted.
- Females flew parallel to the current and often darted downstream 20 feet or more, possibly as an escape response to the observer.
- Only two matings were observed, both with pairs floating on the water in tandem.

## Key Findings

1. **Species description.** *Lachlania dencyanna* n. sp. is distinguished from its North American congeners by forewing crossvein counts and by nymphal mid-dorsal abdominal tubercles. The tubercles are unique to this species among known *Lachlania* nymphs, apart from one unlocalized "Peru" specimen seen by the junior author.

2. **Forewing crossveins.** The new species has 3 or more forewing crossveins, usually 5 or 6 in the R₁-Rs interspace and 2-3 in the IR₃-R₄₊₅ and MA interspaces. This contrasts with *L. powelli* and *L. saskatchewanensis*, which usually have 3 total crossveins.
   - Males: 6-14 total, usually 9-14 (32 specimens examined).
   - Females: 5-14 total, usually 8-13 (22 specimens examined).

3. **Variability in crossveins.** In males, 12 of 32 specimens had a crossvein within the MP₁-IMP₂ fork or a spurious vein external to it. In 15 of 32 males, branched or partial crossveins occurred in the R₁-Rs and/or MA interspaces. The left and right wings matched in only 7 of 54 specimens. The authors conclude that crossvein characters should be used cautiously for species definitions, and that earlier species may prove to be synonyms.

4. **Differences from congeners.**
   - *L. dencyanna* lacks crossveins between IMP₂ and MP₂, which separates it from *L. cacautana*, *L. garciai* and *L. pallipes*.
   - Male femur-tibia joints of the meso- and metathoracic legs are pale in *L. dencyanna* and brown in *L. powelli*.
   - Male sublateral abdominal bars are poorly developed in *L. dencyanna* and well developed in *L. powelli*.
   - The wing iridescence differs from *L. radai*, which is copper.

5. **Wing venation.** The authors found that the Re vein arises at the wing base in several oligoneuriid genera, not at the midpoint as earlier authors illustrated. In *Lachlania* the hindwing Sc is present only for the basal half, and the CuP vein runs close to CuA.

6. **Taxonomic key.** The paper provides a key to the North American species of *Lachlania*. Adults with five or more crossveins anterior to MA₂ are *L. dencyanna*. Nymphs with mid-dorsal tubercles on terga 2-9 are also *L. dencyanna*, as opposed to *L. powelli*, which lacks them.

7. **Generic limits.** The authors note that the largest frons among American oligoneuriid nymphs occurs in the nymph of *L. cacautana*. They question whether the large-frons nymphs described from South America belong to *Spaniophlebia*. *Alloydia* and *Noya*, previously regarded as separate genera on crossvein counts, may be distinct, but the material is insufficient to decide. Association of more nymphs and adults is needed.

8. **Status of *L. powelli*.** The authors suggest that *L. powelli* may be a synonym of *L. saskatchewanensis*. The latter is known from a single female collected in September from saline Stoney Lake near Humboldt, Saskatchewan. The authors recommend that Canadian entomologists collect *Lachlania* in warm Saskatchewan rivers or tributaries.

9. **Behaviour.** The males' landing and resting behaviour and the female escape flights differ from earlier descriptions of *L. powelli*. Observed matings were rare (two pairs).
