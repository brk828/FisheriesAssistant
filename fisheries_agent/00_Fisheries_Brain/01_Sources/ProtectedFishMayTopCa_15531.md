## Executive Summary

This news article reports on a leaked draft U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) biological opinion concluding that the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) canal — a Colorado River water delivery system — jeopardizes the continued existence of four protected native fish species in Arizona: the [[Arizona Loach Minnow]], the threatened [[Spikedace]], the [[Razorback Sucker]], and the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]]. The central ecological concern is that the CAP Canal functions as an artificial aquatic corridor ("superhighway") allowing aggressive nonnative fish, notably [[Striped Bass]], and other invasive organisms to invade central Arizona's rivers and streams, threatening native fish populations over the project's projected 100-year operational lifespan. The report fueled environmental legal challenges alleging violations of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: All four species are native to Arizona's riverine ecosystems, historically part of the Gila River/Colorado River drainage system, now impacted by the CAP Canal infrastructure.
- **Threat vector — Invasion corridor**: The CAP Canal is characterized as an artificial conduit connecting previously isolated native fish habitats to nonnative species populations elsewhere in the state, facilitating range expansion of invasive organisms.
- **Primary invasive threat**: [[Striped Bass]], identified as a "highly aggressive" nonnative fish capable of using the canal to colonize central Arizona rivers and streams.
- **Secondary invasive threats**: The USFWS report also flags risk from nonnative mollusks, insects, plants, and parasitic/disease organisms moving through the canal system, with potential "adverse effects on the ecosystem as a whole" beyond the four listed fish species.
- **Timescale of risk**: Over the CAP's ~100-year projected operational life, USFWS biologists concluded it is "highly likely" that one or more nonnative fish species will breach containment measures and cause "major adverse consequences" to native, protected fish populations.
- **Regulatory context**: The situation is framed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], with the biological opinion process potentially compelling changes to CAP operations to avoid jeopardizing listed species.
