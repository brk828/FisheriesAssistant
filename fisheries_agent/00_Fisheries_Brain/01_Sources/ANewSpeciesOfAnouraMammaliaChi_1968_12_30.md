# Anoura brevirostrum (New Species)

#mammalogy #chiroptera #phyllostomidae

> **Note on scope:** This source paper describes a new species of leaf-nosed bat (Mammalia: Chiroptera: Phyllostomidae) and contains **no fish, ichthyological, or aquatic ecosystem data**. The structured note below faithfully represents the actual taxonomic and morphological content of the source rather than forcing an inapplicable fish-centric framing.

## Executive Summary
This 1968 taxonomic paper by Dilford C. Carter describes **Anoura brevirostrum**, a new species of nectar-feeding phyllostomid bat, based on five specimens collected in Perú (1964, 1967) and one in Colombia (1945). The species is distinguished primarily by its unusually short rostrum (the shortest among congeners), a distinctive first lower premolar cusp position, absence of a tail, and reduced dentition features (small first upper premolar, weak/absent hypocones on upper molars). It is most closely related to **[[Anoura Cultrata]]**, from which it differs in several cranial and dental characters, and is more distantly related to **[[Anoura Geoffroyi]]** and **[[Anoura Caudifer]]**.

## Background & Description
- **Size:** Medium-sized *Anoura*; holotype weight 12.5 g; head & body length 61–64 mm (mean 62.5 mm, n=4).
- **Rostrum:** Relatively short — the shortest of the four recognized *Anoura* species at the time — with a more crowded toothrow than *A. cultrata*.
- **Pelage/Coloration:**
  - Dorsal: gray; individual hairs with basal two-thirds white, distal third gray with paler tips.
  - Ventral (thoracic): basal half very pale gray, distal half pale gray with very pale tips.
  - Ventral (abdominal): basal two-thirds dark gray, distal third very pale gray/near white.
  - Membranes blackish; ears and noseleaf gray.
- **Tail:** Absent (key diagnostic trait vs. *A. cultrata*).
- **Interfemoral membrane:** Narrow, with dense fringe of hairs extending 2–3 mm beyond the edge.
- **Cranial features:**
  - Zygomatic arch complete, moderately developed.
  - Basisphenoid pits well developed, separated by a narrow ridge.
  - First upper premolar greatly reduced; its dorso-ventral dimension exceeds its antero-posterior dimension (opposite condition in *A. cultrata*).
  - Upper molars with well-developed protocone; hypocone weak on M¹, nearly absent on M², absent on M³.
  - First lower premolar is the largest of the lower premolars, with the main cusp positioned posterior to the tooth's midpoint (diagnostic vs. *A. geoffroyi* and *A. caudifer*, where the cusp is anterior to the midpoint and the tooth is not enlarged).
- **Selected holotype measurements (mm):** forearm 40.3; third metacarpal 41.5; tibia 14.2; calcar 3.6; greatest skull length 23.1; zygomatic breadth 9.5; braincase breadth 9.3; maxillary toothrow 8.2; width across upper molars 5.6; width across canines 4.7.
- **Comparative data (Table 1):** *A. brevirostrum* is consistently smaller than *A. cultrata* across nearly all cranial and postcranial variates (e.g., mean skull length 23.4 mm vs. 26.3 mm; mean forearm 39.9 mm vs. 43.7 mm).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Eastern Andean foothills; type locality on a steep hillside with second-growth brush 2–3 m tall, situated within second-growth and cut-over rain forest undergoing (diminishing) active logging.
- **Elevational range:** ~600–850 m in Perú; Colombian record elevation unknown.
- **Additional locality:** Pass through the Cordillera Azul (~45 km NE of Tingo María) along the Tingo María–Pucallpa road, at ~600 m, with less-disturbed vegetation than the type locality.
- **Colombian record:** West slope of the Cordillera Oriental, east of the Magdalena River (Gramales, Santander).
- **Reproductive notes:**
  - Two females from the type locality (25–26 Aug 1964): neither pregnant nor lactating.
  - Peruvian female from Cordillera Azul (21 Aug 1967): lactating.
  - Male from type locality (26 Aug 1964): testes 4 mm in length.
  - Colombian female (Dec 1945): reproductive status not recorded.
- **Trophic guild (inferred from genus):** *Anoura* species are nectarivorous/frugivorous phyllostomid bats, though this is not explicitly detailed in the text beyond taxonomic context.

## Key Findings
- **New species erected:** *Anoura brevirostrum* Carter, 1968, based on 5 specimens (holotype + 4 paratypes) from Perú and Colombia.
- **Closest relative:** [[Anoura Cultrata]] — distinguished by absence of tail, shorter/more crowded rostrum, smaller and differently proportioned first upper premolar, and weaker hypocone development on M¹ and M².
- **Diagnostic separation from [[Anoura Geoffroyi]] and [[Anoura Caudifer]]:** based on relative size and cusp position of the first lower premolar.
- **Etymology:** From Latin *brevis* ("short") + *rostrum* ("snout"), reflecting the diagnostic shortened rostrum.
- **Type material:** Holotype (adult female, TCWC no. 11882) deposited at Texas Cooperative Wildlife Collection; additional paratypes at USNM-related institutions, LSU, and FMNH.
- **Taxonomic history note:** The author revisits uncertainty around the holotype status of *Glossophaga caudifer* Geoffroy (basionym of *A. caudifer*) at the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle, concluding the specimen (MNHN no. 937) is likely the true holotype despite catalog date discrepancies.
- **Broader taxonomic context:** Comparative type specimens examined include those of *Anoura geoffroyi* (BMNH), *Glossonycteris lasiopyga* (ZMB, a synonym of *A. geoffroyi lasiopyga*), and *Lonchoglossa wiedi aequatoris* (NR, synonym of *A. caudifer*).
