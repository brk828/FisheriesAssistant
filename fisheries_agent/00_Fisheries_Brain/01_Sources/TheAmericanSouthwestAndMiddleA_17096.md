# The American Southwest and Middle America: A Limnological Review

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecosystems

## Executive Summary

This regional review, a chapter by Gerald A. Cole, surveys the limnology of the American Southwest and Middle America, an area of about 3.8 million km² spanning roughly 25° of latitude and 42° of longitude. The region's aquatic habitats are extremely varied, including [[Thermal Springs]], extremely saline waters, lava-collapse ponds, [[Cenotes of Yucatan]], water-filled caves, and [[Ephemeral Playa Lakes]]. The author stresses that knowledge is very uneven: some areas are almost unstudied, and most data come from reservoirs, the [[Lake Patzcuaro]] and [[Lake Chapala]] systems, and Guatemalan and Salvadoran lakes. The chapter also documents habitat loss and fish extinctions in the arid Southwest, and proposes systematic, district-based research as the way forward.

## Background & Description

**Scope and regional framing.** The chapter treats lakes by political unit or by physiographic district, since many areas lack published studies. Shared physiographic features link the region: the [[Colorado Plateau]] extends across northern Arizona and New Mexico, the [[Basin and Range Province]] reaches from Trans-Pecos Texas to about 18° N in Mexico, and Middle American republics share Caribbean and Pacific lowlands.

**Key prior works.** Deevey (1957) is described as the most valuable synthesis of southwestern and Middle American limnology, although it rests on hurried visits and earlier literature. Miller (1961) addressed fish-fauna changes in the Southwest. Koster (1957) summarized New Mexican ichthyology, and Alvarez et al. (1961) compiled a bibliography of 1,831 titles on Mexican aquatic biology.

**Physiographic and climatic setting by state.**
- **Texas:** Altitudes range from sea level to 2,612 m. Precipitation falls from 109 cm in the east to less than 25 cm in parts of the [[Trans-Pecos Region]]. Reservoirs cover more than 283,000 ha.
- **New Mexico:** Elevations span 1,000 to 4,600 m. More than half the state receives under 37.5 cm of precipitation a year, and only Connecticut has less total lake, pond, and stream area in the United States.
- **Arizona:** Altitudes run from about 30 m to 3,862 m, and temperatures have been recorded from −36° C to +53° C.
- **Mexico:** Elevations span sea level to 5,500 m, with a great interior plateau flanked by the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] and [[Sierra Madre Oriental]].
- **Central America:** Highland volcanic lakes, coastal lagoons, and large lakes such as [[Lake Nicaragua]] make up the region.

**Historical disturbance.** Since 1900, six or seven fish species have become extinct in the Southwest, and at least 13 more are seriously threatened (Miller, 1961). Many once-permanent streams now flow intermittently, and cienegas (freshwater marshes) have nearly disappeared. Causes include overgrazing, lumbering, pollution, river impoundment, dredging, ditching, groundwater pumping, and introduced exotic species. Middle America has also been heavily altered by plantations, lumbering, charcoal manufacture, and erosion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat types.**
- **Reservoirs and impoundments:** These are the best-known aquatic habitats in Texas and much of the Southwest. Flash floods and irrigation drawdown often disrupt thermal and oxygen regimes.
- **Natural lakes:** Glacial lakes in the [[Sangre de Cristo Mountains]] and volcanic lakes on the [[Mogollon Rim]] and [[Colorado Plateau]] are typical. [[Mormon Lake]] is the largest natural lake in Arizona (2,590 ha).
- **Solution basins:** [[Montezuma Well]] (Arizona), the [[Bottomless Lakes]] (New Mexico), and the cenotes of Yucatan are sinkholes in limestone or gypsum.
- **Playa and deflation lakes:** The [[Llano Estacado]] has thousands of basins, some up to 115 m deep. Many are ephemeral and saline.
- **Springs and springbrooks:** These are mineralized, often isolated oases, such as [[Quitobaquito Springs]] and [[Lander Springbrook]].
- **Caves and lava-tube ponds:** [[Grants Lava Bed]] contains more than 100 ponds in collapse depressions and caves within 570 km².
- **Bogs:** Acid peat bogs occur in east-central Texas and high-altitude Costa Rica, including the [[Talamanca Bogs]].
- **Artificial habitats:** Irrigation canals, stock tanks, and tinajas (rock potholes) support simple, often understudied communities.

**Chemistry patterns.**
- Many New Mexican waters have high sulfate, linked to widespread gypsum deposits.
- Texas water quality follows an east-to-west gradient, with low-alkalinity, stained waters in the east and phenolphthalein alkalinity farther west.
- Silica is high across much of the region. Mean values are about 43 mg/L in Mexican, Guatemalan, and Salvadoran lakes.
- Trans-Pecos playas have low magnesium and a high share of sestonic phosphorus (about 85%).

**Biological notes.**
- **Crustaceans and plankton:** Calanoid copepods are poorly known in Arizona. Only about seven species had been confirmed by 1961. Stock tanks typically hold one calanoid, one or two *Daphnia* species, and *Cyclops vernalis*.
- **Fishes and endemism:** Cyprinodon populations persist in several [[Bottomless Lakes]], while [[Anoptichthys]] is a cave-adapted characin in San Luis Potosi caves. Goodeids and *Chirostoma* atherinids are characteristic of the [[Lerma River System]].
- **Marine relicts:** [[Lake Nicaragua]] contains landlocked elasmobranchs and Atlantic tarpon. Cenote fauna includes cirolanid isopods and blind shrimps with probable marine ancestry.

## Key Findings

- **Extent of knowledge is uneven.** Texas and Mexico are relatively well documented, while Arizona, New Mexico (for invertebrates), and parts of Central America are poorly known.
- **Lake Nicaragua and Lake Managua** are very productive. About 375 professional fishermen harvest about 810,000 kg of fish per year, while total fish production is estimated at about 91,370,000 kg. Lake Managua is more saline than Lake Nicaragua (TDS 747 vs. 151 mg/L).
- **Montezuma Well** is a unique limnocrene and solution basin. Its outflow is about 5,600 m³/day, and its water is about 23.7° C. It has no fish, which probably explains the extreme abundance of *Hyalella azteca*. Net phytoplankton are almost absent, and calanoid copepods, rotifers, and cladocerans are missing.
- **Cenotes of Yucatan** show a successional sequence from cave to aguada. Young cenotes have more fauna than older ones. Some cenotes are stratified: below 6 m in Xtolok and Xanaba II there is no detectable oxygen, and H₂S is present.
- **Lake Patzcuaro and Lake Chapala** are both carbonate lakes with high sodium. Total phosphorus in Chapala is four times that of Patzcuaro. The N/P ratio is 61.7 in Patzcuaro and 5.9 in Chapala. Lake Patzcuaro is 2,035 m above sea level with an area of 111 km² and a maximum depth of 15 m.
- **Lake Atitlan and Lake Amatitlan** (Guatemala) have high primary productivity. Amatitlan's dark-light bottle experiments gave 0.514 mg O₂/cm² per day. Atitlan has a remarkably high summer heat income for a tropical lake.
- **Elephant Butte Reservoir** (New Mexico) is the largest New Mexican water body (up to 15,783 ha). Flash floods and irrigation draw-off alter its oxygen and thermal structure.
- **Pena Blanca Lake** (Arizona) receives flash-flood organic inputs estimated above 300,000 kg per year, about 15,000 kg/ha per year.
- **Lake Izabal** (Guatemala) is large (over 63,700 ha) and shallow, with mean depth around 8–9 m. It is strongly wind-mixed and isothermal at times.
- **Willow Lake and the Bottomless Lakes** (New Mexico) are very saline. Mean total solids in Willow Lake are 3,465 mg/L. Cottonwood Lake and similar sinks have steep vertical banks.
- **Lake Nejapa** (Nicaragua) had pH above 10, carbonate alkalinity of 10,440 mg/L, and no zooplankton. Blue-green algae dominated.
- **Saline Arizona waters** show high chloride in the Salt River (330 to over 1,000 mg/L), while Lake Carl Pleasant (total soluble salts 374 mg/L) lacks the brackish-water flora found in canals.
- **Inland shrimp fisheries** exist in the Honduran mountains and the Mexican northwest (Mercado Sanchez, 1961).
- **Future research priorities** include systematic regional work on individual lake districts, plus studies of stock tanks, tinajas, springbrooks, and endemic cave and spring faunas in northern Mexico (Coahuila and Chihuahua).
