# Fish Remains from Stanton's Cave, Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Robert Rush Miller]] and [[Gerald R. Smith]] (Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan; identifications dated 1969 and 1971) reports fish bones recovered from [[Stanton's Cave]], a Native American site on the Red Wall cliff of the [[Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon]], Arizona. The remains represent nearly all fish species known to be native to this reach of the river. The authors use the material to document the historical occurrence of the [[Humpback Chub]] and to note that its cleithrum, a potentially useful systematic structure, had not been studied for taxonomic characters. The paper also includes a length-frequency figure for humpback chub from the [[Little Colorado River]].

## Background & Description

- **Site:** Stanton's Cave is named after [[Robert Brewster Stanton]], leader of the 1889–90 railroad survey through Grand Canyon, which was financed by railroad president Frank M. Brown. The cave lies on the west side of the Colorado River in Marble Gorge, about 32 river miles below Lees Ferry, and about 30 river miles north of the Little Colorado River mouth. The entrance is roughly 120 feet above the river and measures about 20 feet wide by 30 feet high. The passage extends almost 200 feet into the limestone bluff. The site had earlier bird remains studied by Parmley (1969) and is designated ARIZ C:5:3. The elevation figure in the OCR text is partly illegible.
- **Nomenclature:** The humpback chub, *Gila cypha* Miller, was described from Grand Canyon, near the mouth of Bright Angel Creek (Miller, 1946). Taxonomists have questioned whether it is a full species (Holden and Stalnaker, 1970), but no definitive study had resolved this.
- **Material:** The Stanton's Cave collection is entirely historical (non-fossilized), except for one set of fossil vertebrae. Its age is uncertain, with some specimens possibly as old as about 4,000 years B.P. (Robert C. Euler, pers. comm.).
- **Scope:** The cleithrum (pectoral girdle bone) had not been examined for systematic characters. The authors therefore compared it across specimens from the middle and upper Colorado River basin (Minckley and Deacon, 1958).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Historical range of the humpback chub:** The species has been recorded from archaeological material as far downstream as Soldier Canyon, about 15 miles below Hoover Dam, and upstream to the Green River and Colorado River in Utah, including Dinosaur National Monument. The authors suggest it may have occurred in the San Juan River, but this is speculative. They note that its range is not limited to three areas: (1) the canyon regions of the upper Green and Colorado Rivers, (2) the reach between Lees Ferry and Glen Canyon Dam, and (3) the Grand Canyon region.

**Co-occurring species:** The Stanton's Cave assemblage represents nearly all native fish species of this part of the Colorado River. The [[Roundtail Chub]] is probably not represented, although it occurs in the Little Colorado River.

## Key Findings

**Species list (summary):** The identified remains belong to five species in two families. Family Cyprinidae includes:
- [[Gila Cypha]] (humpback chub), confirmed
- [[Gila Elegans]] (bonytail), probably present
- [[Ptychocheilus Lucius]] ([[Colorado Squawfish]]), confirmed

Family Catostomidae includes:
- [[Catostomus Latipinnis]] ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]]), confirmed
- [[Catostomus Discobolus]] ([[Bluehead Sucker]]), confirmed

**Specimen inventory (six lots):**

1. **Surface, antechamber 1:** An incomplete neurocranium with [[Weberian Apparatus]] attached, plus nine attached vertebrae and three to five ribs (counts differ between text and table). Identified as *Gila cypha*.
2. **Catalog SC62:** Right branchiostegal ray of Colorado squawfish, from a fish perhaps 4–5 feet long.
3. **Rat midden adjacent to FS #14 (June 1969, L. Powers):** Left pectoral girdle, probably *Gila cypha*. The specimen shows a ventral ridge on the cleithrum, a characteristic absent in *G. elegans*.
4. **Main room (June 27, 1969, BH):** Incomplete left dentary of *Catostomus discobolus* (about 315 mm SL) and incomplete neurocranium of *C. latipinnis* (about 400 mm SL).
5. **Main room (June 24, 1969, BH and JW):** Incomplete right dentary of *C. discobolus* (about 264 mm SL).
6. **Pack rat midden near FS #13 and FS #15 (June 17, 1969, R. Miller):** Parts of three vertebral columns, representing *G. elegans* and/or *G. cypha*.

**Fossil material:** Five articulated cyprinid vertebrae from the cave passageway (June 18, 1969) represent the caudal column of a large minnow, tentatively referred to *Gila*, roughly 400 mm SL. These are the only fossilized remains in the collection.

**Taxonomic significance:** The authors conclude that the material is sufficient to show the historical presence of the humpback chub in this reach, but they stress that the cleithrum is a largely unexplored character set that could help resolve the species' status.

**Length-frequency data (Figure 1):** The figure shows the length distribution (mm) of humpback chub from the Little Colorado River in Grand Canyon National Park, with sample sizes by collection period:
- Nov.–Dec. 1977: n = 10
- June 1978: n = 74
- July–Aug. 1978: n = 368
- Sept.–Oct. 1978: n = 71
