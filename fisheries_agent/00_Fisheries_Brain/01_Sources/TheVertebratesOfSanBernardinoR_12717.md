# Vertebrates of San Bernardino Ranch, Cochise County, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #vertebrate-survey

## Executive Summary

This 1981 report by Dirk V. Lanning, prepared for [[The Nature Conservancy]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] through the [[Arizona Natural Heritage Program]], presents a baseline vertebrate survey of the 984 ha [[San Bernardino Ranch]], a spring-fed riparian oasis at the northern headwaters of the [[Rio Yaqui]]. The survey recorded 234 vertebrate species on the Ranch in 1981 and documented 78 additional species from earlier records within 10 km. The report also reviews the Ranch's habitat history, which was degraded by livestock and agriculture from the 1800s until The Nature Conservancy purchased it in 1979, and closes with management recommendations centered on removing livestock and introduced species and on restoring native grasslands and riparian habitat.

## Background & Description

The Ranch is a remnant of the 29,644 ha [[San Bernardino Land Grant]], titled in 1822. In 1884 John Slaughter purchased the northern half, later known as the [[Slaughter Ranch]], which was reduced to roughly the present ~1000 ha after 1937. Its water comes from springs and artesian wells (wells 105–205 m deep, roughly 28–32 °C, per Meinzer and Kelton 1913). Dominant habitats in 1981 were [[Chihuahuan Desertscrub]] (39%), [[Desert Grassland]] (25%) and [[Mesquite Bosque]] (22%), with Old Field at 11% and riparian habitats at 2.4%. Vegetation records follow Lehr (1978) and Lehr and Pinkava (1980). The author lived on the Ranch and surveyed it from January to October 1981.

Historical accounts from 1846–1854 (Cooke, Powell, Aldrich, Bartlett, Froebel, Emory) and Mearns' 1892–1893 collections at the [[U.S. National Museum]] provide the baseline against which change is measured. Mearns collected 852 specimens of 125 vertebrate species on the Slaughter Ranch.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Climate:** 1981 precipitation totalled 458 mm in the first 10 months, 64% above the 1941–1970 mean of 334 mm (range 124–550 mm), mostly from summer thunderstorms. Winter nights fell below 0 °C.
- **Hydrology and geology:** The 1887 earthquake lowered the alluvial valley 0.15–0.6 m, altered existing springs and created new ones. [[Black Draw]] was a cottonwood-lined creek in the 1890s and later became a 3–5 m deep ditch that is usually dry.
- **Land use history:** Livestock and agriculture modified the habitats from the early 1800s. Marshlands became fields, desert grassland declined, and riparian and aquatic habitats were reduced to remnants. Cattle and horses were removed after the 1979 purchase, and vegetative cover has since increased "dramatically" according to the author.
- **Habitat areas (Fig. 2):** Desert Grassland 255 ha, Chihuahuan Desertscrub 385 ha, Riparian Forest/Woodland 7 ha, Mesquite Bosque 220 ha, Riparian Scrub 10 ha, Marshland 6 ha, Aquatic 1 ha, Old Field 110 ha.

## Key Findings

**Survey totals**
- 234 vertebrate species recorded on the Ranch in 1981; 78 more recorded earlier on the Ranch or within 10 km.
- Fishes: 15 species discussed; 6 recorded in 1981 (3 native, 3 introduced). Of the 7 native Rio Yaqui headwater fishes, 5 have been found on the Ranch.
- Amphibians and reptiles: 9 amphibian and 21 reptile species recorded on or within 5 km of the Ranch in 1981; 28 species total on the Ranch in 1981 including those within 5 km.
- Birds: 158 species recorded in 1981, 121 of them along transects; 215 species in the annotated list.
- Mammals: 42 species recorded in 1981, including 9 bat species (198 individuals in mist nets over 22 nights) and 15 rodent species (4,864 trap-nights).

**Fishes**
- Yaqui Chub was common in House Pond and North Pond and uncommon in Pipe Springs.
- Longfin Dace was common in the perennial creek in Black Draw.
- Beautiful Shiner and Yaqui Sucker are listed as extirpated (last caught 1965 and 1968, respectively).
- Introduced species include Carp, Goldfish, Black Bullhead, Mosquitofish (abundant in House Pond and House Creek), Largemouth Bass, Green Sunfish, Bluegill and Black Crappie (House Pond, February 1980).
- Sonoran Topminnow was abundant in the Three-Springs Area and North Pond; the author notes introduced Mosquitofish were more abundant where they occurred.

**Amphibians and reptiles**
- Couch's Spadefoot was abundant near ephemeral pools during summer rains; Leopard Frog was abundant throughout the Ranch; Bullfrog (introduced) was abundant in larger permanent ponds.
- Sonoran Mud Turtle was uncommon (five caught in turtle traps in House Pond, 1–2 October).
- Desert Grassland Whiptail was the most commonly observed lizard, common in Chihuahuan Desertscrub, Desert Grassland and Old Field.
- Western Diamondback Rattlesnake (18 observed) was the most-observed snake; Coachwhip (10) was fairly common.
- Gila Monster was uncommon (one found near House Creek, 3 July).

**Birds**
- Mourning Dove was the most numerous and widespread bird.
- Gambel's Quail, Killdeer, Bewick's Wren and Red-winged Blackbird were among the abundant species (Table 5 density categories).
- Breeding (B) status was recorded for many species, including Barn Owl, Gila Woodpecker and Lucy's Warbler.

**Mammals**
- Cave Myotis (27 caught) and Red Bat (21) were among the commoner bats; Brazilian Free-tailed Bat was abundant (117 caught).
- Desert Cottontail (5–8/100 ha) and Black-tailed Jackrabbit (5–7/100 ha in Desert Grassland and Old Field) were common.
- Merriam's Kangaroo Rat was abundant, and Arizona Cotton Rat was abundant in several habitats.
- The author reports Black-tailed Prairie Dog, Jaguar, Gray Wolf, Grizzly Bear and Pronghorn as formerly occurring or possibly present, with no recent records.
- Lowest (or absent) mammal counts include Striped Skunk, which the author notes may reflect below-normal population sizes.

**Recommendations (author's)**
1. Active management should begin immediately, including erosion control in the draws and planting of native grasses in fallow fields.
2. All livestock should be removed and fences vigorously maintained.
3. All introduced species should be eliminated, prioritizing Mosquitofish and Goldfish in aquatic areas before they invade remaining systems.
4. The Ranch environment should continue to be studied to document habitat change and to measure management effectiveness.

**Limitations noted by the author**
- Fish sampling was irregular, and dense summer aquatic vegetation prevented systematic sampling of Aquatic habitats. A 10 m seine was used once, in North Pond.
- Rabbit and rodent densities are "best considered as relative densities."
