## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 internal U.S. Forest Service memorandum (Tonto National Forest, Phoenix, AZ) transmitting **summary data sheets from fishery surveys conducted on the upper [[Verde River]] in May 1995** by the Rocky Mountain Research Station (RMRS), under the direction of fishery biologist Jerome A. Stefferud. The survey sampled fish assemblages at **seven sites** along the upper Verde River and also conducted a **Floy T-bar anchor tag mark-recapture study** on four native/non-native species at two of those sites (Railroad Bridge near [[Perkinsville]] and [[Sycamore]]). A total of **1,274 fish** were collected and identified across 14 species codes, and dozens of individuals of [[Sonora Sucker]] (Cain), [[Common Carp]] (Cyca), [[Roundtail Chub]] (Giro), and [[Desert Sucker]] (Pacl) were tagged with red Floy tags and released with length/weight measurements recorded.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** The **upper [[Verde River]]**, a perennial warmwater stream in central Arizona and a major tributary of the [[Gila River]] (itself part of the Colorado River drainage). The river supports a mix of native Sonoran Desert fishes and introduced non-native sportfish/baitfish.
- **Study sites (7 total):**
  - [[Burnt Ranch]]
  - FR638 (Forest Road 638 access site)
  - [[Duff Spring]]
  - [[Bear Siding]]
  - [[Perkinsville]]
  - [[Railroad Bridge]] (east of Perkinsville)
  - [[Sycamore]]
- **Methodology:**
  - Fish were sampled at each of the seven sites and identified to species, with raw counts (Table 1) and relative percent abundance (Table 2) calculated per site.
  - At two sites (Railroad Bridge and Sycamore), individual fish of four species were tagged with **red Floy FD-94 T-bar anchor tags**, and total length (mm) and weight (g) were recorded for each tagged individual, dated 5–10 May 1995.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The upper Verde River assemblage documented here reflects a **mixed native–nonnative Sonoran Desert fish community**, typical of southwestern warmwater streams undergoing invasion pressure from introduced cyprinids, ictalurids, and centrarchids.
- **Native species** present include [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Longfin Dace]], and the federally listed [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), a small native cyprinid of conservation concern in Gila River Basin drainages.
- **Non-native species** recorded include [[Common Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Smallmouth Bass]] — all recognized threats to native Southwestern fish assemblages through predation and competition.
- The presence of [[Spikedace]] and [[Roundtail Chub]] in these collections is ecologically significant, as both taxa were subjects of intense conservation and status-review attention in Arizona during the 1990s (recipients on the mailing list include USFWS, AGFD, and Arizona State University herpetology/ichthyology researcher W.L. Minckley, indicating this dataset likely fed into broader status assessments).
- Site-level differences in assemblage composition (e.g., very high [[Roundtail Chub]] dominance at Sycamore ~52.8% vs. more balanced native sucker/chub/dace communities at other sites) suggest **spatial heterogeneity in habitat conditions or non-native fish pressure** along the river's longitudinal gradient from Burnt Ranch downstream to Sycamore.
- The tagging effort at Railroad Bridge and Sycamore focused on larger-bodied, easily tagged fishes — [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Common Carp]], [[Roundtail Chub]], and [[Desert Sucker]] — likely to support a **mark-recapture growth and movement study**, a common technique for estimating population size, growth rates, and site fidelity/movement in riverine fish populations.

## Key Findings

- **Total catch:** 1,274 fish collected across all seven sites in May 1995.
- **Overall community composition (approximate, based on Table 2 total row):**
  - [[Roundtail Chub]] (GIRO): ~26.8%
  - [[Desert Sucker]] (CACL): ~25.7%
  - [[Sonora Sucker]] (CAIN): ~25.3%
  - [[Red Shiner]] (CYLU): ~7.8%
  - [[Spikedace]] (MEFU): ~5.7%
  - [[Yellow Bullhead]] (AMNA) and [[Green Sunfish]] (LECY): ~2.3% each
  - [[Speckled Dace]] (RHOS): ~2.0%
  - [[Smallmouth Bass]] (MIDO): ~0.8%
  - [[Longfin Dace]] (AGCH): ~0.8%
  - [[Common Carp]] (CYCA): ~0.5%
  - [[Channel Catfish]] (ICPU) and [[Flathead Catfish]] (PYOL): <0.5% each (rare)
- **Site-level highlights:**
  - **Sycamore** was dominated overwhelmingly by [[Roundtail Chub]] (~52.8% of catch), suggesting either a strong local population or favorable habitat for this species.
  - **Perkinsville** had the highest proportion of [[Sonora Sucker]] (~46.2%).
  - **Bear Siding** and **Burnt Ranch** showed relatively balanced [[Desert Sucker]]–[[Sonora Sucker]] co-dominance.
  - **Duff Spring** had the largest total catch among individual sites (275 fish) with strong native sucker and chub presence plus a notable [[Common Carp]] component (~20.0%).
- **Tagging data (Table 3):** Dozens of [[Sonora Sucker]] (Cain), several [[Common Carp]] (Cyca), [[Roundtail Chub]] (Giro), and [[Desert Sucker]] (Pacl) individuals were tagged with red Floy FD-94 T-bar anchor tags between 5–10 May 1995 at the Railroad Bridge (east of Perkinsville) and Sycamore sites.
  - Tagged [[Sonora Sucker]] ranged roughly from **219–470+ mm total length**, with weights spanning from tens of grams up to over 1,200 g for the largest individuals, indicating a broad size/age range was sampled — consistent with a population growth or demographic study.
  - Tagged [[Desert Sucker]] (Pacl) ranged approximately **230–475 mm TL**, with weights up to ~875 g.
  - The tagging distribution across two dates (5-May and 10-May) suggests either a multi-day sampling event or a short-term recapture check within the same survey period.
- **Data limitations:** Several numeric values in the original OCR source (particularly in Tables 1–2 and portions of Table 3) are degraded or ambiguous; percentages and counts reported here reflect best-effort reconstruction from the available scanned data and should be verified against original RMRS/Forest Service records where precision is critical.
- **Institutional context:** This memo circulated to a coalition of agencies and researchers — [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), [[Rocky Mountain Research Station]] (RMRS), [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), and [[Arizona State University]] — reflecting the multi-agency, conservation-driven nature of native fish monitoring on the Verde River during the mid-1990s.
