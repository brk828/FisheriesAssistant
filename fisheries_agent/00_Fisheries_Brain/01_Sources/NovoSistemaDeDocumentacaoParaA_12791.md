# Termos Coordenados: A New Documentation System for Fisheries Information

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-documentation

## Executive Summary

This 1965 bulletin article, published in the *Boletim de Estudos da Pesca* (vol. 5, no. 2), presents **Termos Coordenados**, a manual-punched-card indexing system developed by [[Silvia Augusta Marques]] and [[J. M. Brandão]] for the documentation service of the [[SUDENE]] Fisheries Development Group (GCDP) in Recife, Brazil. The system combines a systematic alphabetical index with "secondary indices" and coordinated terms, retrieved by superimposing punched cards. The authors present it as adaptable to any specialized documentation service and as a candidate for IBM mechanization. The text contains no biological data on fish or aquatic ecosystems; its substance concerns library and information science applied to fisheries literature.

## Background & Description

The article is a structured restatement of a preliminary report (Documentos de Pesca, no. 2, 1965). Its central premise is that the availability of information underpins the success of research and economic planning, and that documents must be collected, classified and disseminated using systems suited to diverse subject demands. The authors state that adopting Termos Coordenados at the GCDP gives Brazil a pioneering position among Latin American countries in documentation.

The article is organized into sections on structure, methodology, equipment, conclusions, the GCDP documentation scheme, a summary in Portuguese, English and French, and a bibliography.

Core structural features:

- **Indexing by breakdown (*indexação por desdobramento*):** each term may expand into a [[Secondary Index]] when publication volume or subject complexity requires it.
- **Terms:** words or phrases describing document content, chosen by the documentalist with specialist advice. Meaning depends on the secondary index; the example given is "Lagosta" (lobster), which differs between the *Crustáceos* and *Métodos de Pesca* indices.
- **Physical cards:** printed cards measuring 38 × 27 cm, bearing 18,000 squares of 2 mm² arranged in 100-square groups forming 10 × 10 blocks, with numeric guides from 0 to 9 on the margins.
- **Access numbers:** documents are numbered progressively within each secondary index, with letter prefixes identifying each index.
- **Retrieval:** documents sharing several terms are found by stacking the relevant cards; holes that align let light pass through, marking matching documents.
- **Author catalogue:** a single card per author, listing access numbers of that author's works.
- **Abstracts (RAPI):** analytic summaries written for each indexed publication and transcribed on the reverse of the topographic card.

## Key Findings

- **25 secondary indices** make up the GCDP general index, with a stated ability to add more. The list runs from ADP (Administração Pesqueira) to TEP (Tecnologia do Pescado), with separate indices for PEM (Peixes Marinhos) and PAD (Peixes de Água Doce).
- **Capacity:** each card holds 18,000 squares, and the authors claim storage is effectively unlimited because overfull terms can be split into new secondary indices.
- **Advantages claimed (section 5.1):** exhaustive subject analysis, rapid subject identification, simple service organization with few cards and catalogues, immediate document retrieval, low cost, and suitability for electronic mechanization.
- **Disadvantages (section 5.2):** need to build special indices for each subject, need for translation to share indices with similar services, and difficulty from linguistic synonymy.
- **Difficulties (section 5.3):** indexers need triple competence in documentation, general science (especially the subject matter), and languages.
- **Proposal to replace manual punching:** the authors conclude that the manual cards can be replaced by IBM punched cards without changing the system's basic concept.
- **Bibliography:** about 20 references, including FAO work on aquatic science literature retrieval (Saila et al., 1962) and works on punched-card methods (Casey, 1958; Sheele, 1961).
