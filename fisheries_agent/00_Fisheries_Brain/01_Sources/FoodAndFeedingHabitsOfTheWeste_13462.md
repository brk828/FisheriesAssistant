# Food and Feeding Habits of the Western Blacknose Dace in Doe Run, Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Donald C. Tarter]] analyzes the stomach contents of 934 western blacknose dace (*Rhinichthys atratulus meleagris*) collected semimonthly from October 1965 through September 1966 at three stations in the upper 3.1 miles of [[Doe Run]], Meade County, Kentucky. Stomach contents were compared with the benthic fauna sampled with a [[Surber Sampler]] to evaluate food selectivity using the [[Forage Ratio]] framework. Dipteran larvae (chiefly [[Chironomidae]]) dominated the diet of young-of-year fish, while amphipods (chiefly [[Gammarus bousfieldi]]) and mayfly nymphs became more important in older age groups. The dace were found to be almost entirely carnivorous and to function as secondary consumers feeding on benthic invertebrates.

## Background & Description

The western blacknose dace is a small cyprinid of spring-fed Kentucky streams. Doe Run is a torrent spring rising near Ekron that flows about 3.5 miles before entering [[Doe Valley Lake]], a recent impoundment of more than 500 acres (200 ha) and about 4 miles long. Below the lake, the stream crosses the [[Ohio River]] floodplain for about 2 miles before reaching the Ohio River about 36 miles downstream from Louisville.

The study was a dissertation-based contribution supported in part by the [[U.S. Atomic Energy Commission]], and was motivated by the limitation of stomach-only food habit studies. The author paired fish diet data with simultaneous benthic sampling so that prey availability could be compared with prey consumption.

Methods included:
- Stomach contents of 934 dace sorted under a dissecting microscope, weighed to the nearest milligram, and counted by taxon.
- Of 934 stomachs, 734 (78.6%) contained food and 200 (21.4%) were empty. Station I had 220 stomachs with food and 63 empty; Station II had 386 with food and 103 empty; Station III had 128 with food and 34 empty.
- Calculated metrics included percentage frequency of occurrence (%FO), average volume percentages, and percentage of total volume (%TV).
- Benthic samples were preserved in 10% formalin and separated by handpicking and salt flotation.
- Data were grouped by age group (0, I, II, III) and season (winter, spring, summer, autumn).
- Diel feeding was assessed on three summer occasions by comparing empty-stomach proportions across collection times.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Stations differed in habitat character. The younger dace fed in quiet, shallow water over soft, silty bottoms, where [[Dipteran Larvae]] were abundant. Older dace fed in progressively rapid riffles with some vegetation and in deep, eddying vegetated pools. Bottom fauna included amphipods, mayfly nymphs (notably [[Baetis vagans]]), caddisfly larvae (including [[Agraylea multiplicata]], [[Hydropsyche]], and [[Cheumatopsyche]]), isopods (*Asellus bivittatus*), oligochaetes, and gastropods (*Goniobasis semicarinata* group). Gastropods were abundant in the benthos yet almost absent from the stomachs, indicating avoidance. Amphipods were the most numerous benthic organisms at Station I and were scarce at Station III.

The author noted that feeding activity was linked to water temperature and prey scarcity: empty-stomach proportions peaked in winter at Station II (38.5%) and in summer at Station III (55.0%, based on only 20 stomachs). Daily feeding was concentrated in the morning, with digestion status indicating feeding largely ceased by midnight.

The banded sculpin (*Cottus carolinae*) was considered the probable top carnivore in Doe Run, feeding on decapods, other fishes, and most of the organisms eaten by the dace. The dace therefore occupied a secondary-consumer position in the [[Doe Run Trophic Structure]].

## Key Findings

- **Diet composition:** Dipteran larvae ranked first or second in %FO in age group 0 dace at all stations. Amphipods replaced dipterans as the most frequent item in older age groups at Stations I and II.
- **Young-of-year feeding:** At Station I, chironomid larvae comprised 67.9% of total volume in age group 0 fish (1,208 individuals ranked first in numbers).
- **Amphipod importance:** Amphipods (mainly *Gammarus bousfieldi*) occurred in 71.4%, 79.4%, and 46.7% of stomachs in age groups I, II, and III at Station I. Amphipods made up 37.8 to 70.2% of total volume in most seasons at Stations I and II.
- **Ontogenetic shift:** Trichopterans rose in frequency with age (12.6% in age group 0 to 100% in age group III at Station II, though based on only two fish). Diptera declined with age at Stations I and II.
- **Selectivity:** Dace showed some selectivity. Gastropods were avoided despite abundance; Gammarus was selected on occasion; mayfly nymphs appeared selected in autumn and winter at Station II (stomach numerical percentages at least twice those in benthos).
- **Seasonal patterns:** Empty stomachs were lowest in spring at Station I (7.9%), coinciding with 94.3% amphipod frequency. Lepidopteran larvae made up 76.8% of total volume in age group III at Station I.
- **Diet classification:** The dace was described as almost entirely carnivorous, with plant material and algae making up trace amounts.
- **Comparison with other studies:** Amphipods were more important in Doe Run than in most cited studies; immature aquatic insects were more important for the eastern blacknose dace (*R. a. atratulus*) than for the western subspecies in Doe Run.
- **Trophic role:** The western blacknose dace functioned as a secondary consumer feeding on benthic invertebrates.
