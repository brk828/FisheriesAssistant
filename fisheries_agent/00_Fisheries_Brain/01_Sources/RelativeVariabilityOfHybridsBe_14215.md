# Relative Variability of Hybrids between Notropis lepidus and N. proserpinus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Notropis #fish-hybridization

## Executive Summary
This study examines a single sample of F1 hybrids produced from a laboratory cross between two closely related, allopatric minnow species, [[Notropis lepidus]] and [[Notropis proserpinus]] (family [[Cyprinidae]]). Contrary to the classical expectation that interspecific fish hybrids display intermediate ("blending") characteristics between parental species, these hybrids exhibited **markedly increased variability** and, in the case of anal ray counts, phenotypes **far exceeding the range of either parental species**. The author (Clark Hubbs) discusses three possible genetic/developmental mechanisms for this phenomenon — previous introgression, modifying genes, and differential developmental rates — favoring the latter as the most likely explanation, while also weighing potential confounding effects of laboratory rearing conditions.

## Background & Description

### Parental Species Overview
- **[[Notropis lepidus]]**: Inhabits clear, spring-fed headwaters of the Edwards Plateau, Texas. Closely related and allopatric to [[Notropis lutrensis]].
- **[[Notropis proserpinus]]**: Inhabits clear, spring-fed streams tributary to the Rio Grande across Texas, New Mexico, Nuevo León, Coahuila, and Chihuahua. May represent a complex of several allopatric populations/cryptic species.
- Both species belong to the subgenus **Moniana**, along with *N. lutrensis*.

### Meristic Comparisons

**Anal Rays (Table I)**

| Sample | Mean | Min | Max | SD | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N. proserpinus | 8.10 | 7 | 9 | 0.43 | 0.053 |
| **Hybrids** | **14.65** | **9** | **20** | **3.03** | **0.207** |
| N. lepidus (control) | 9.52 | 8 | 11 | 0.71 | 0.075 |
| N. lepidus (wild) | 8.47 | 8 | 9 | 0.50 | 0.059 |

- Hybrid anal ray variance is **2–3x greater** than any parental sample.
- Hybrids average **5–6 more anal rays** than any parental stock.
- Anterior anal rays normally spaced; posterior rays crowded.
- Maximum hybrid count (20) far exceeds the highest previously reported count in the genus *Notropis* (13, in *N. signipinnis*).
- No anal ray/fertility correlation found — a ripe female had 17 anal rays; males with strongest secondary sexual characters had counts similar to less-developed males.

**Dorsal Rays (Table II)**

| Sample | Mean | Min | Max | SD | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N. proserpinus | 8.01 | 7 | 9 | 0.19 | 0.024 |
| **Hybrids** | **8.62** | **8** | **10** | **0.57** | **0.066** |
| N. lepidus (control) | 7.96 | 7 | 9 | 0.35 | 0.044 |
| N. lepidus (wild) | 7.86 | 7 | 8 | 0.35 | 0.045 |

- Genus *Notropis* normally has a modal dorsal ray count of 8; hybrids uniquely show a mode of **9**.
- No correlation found between dorsal and anal ray counts, or between dorsal rays and hybrid fertility.

**Pelvic Fins**
- 23 of 26 hybrids (88%) had **no pelvic fins**; one had a degenerate left pelvic and no right pelvic; only 2 had normal pelvic fins.
- The two hybrids with normal pelvics had low anal ray counts (9, 10), close to parental values.
- Absence of pelvic fins appears **correlated with increased anal ray counts**.
- Only one parental specimen (N. lepidus control) lacked pelvic fins, and it had an extreme anal ray count (11) for its sample.

**Lateral Line Scales (Table III)**

| Sample | Mean | Min | Max | SD | Variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| N. proserpinus | 35.39 | 34 | 38 | 0.82 | 0.023 |
| Hybrids | 35.81 | 34 | 39 | 1.10 | 0.031 |
| N. lepidus (control) | 35.28 | 34 | 37 | 0.68 | 0.019 |
| N. lepidus (wild) | 34.04 | 34 | 36 | 0.88 | 0.025 |

- Hybrid variance is highest, but plausibly within range of natural variation seen in other N. lepidus samples.
- Elevated hybrid scale counts likely attributable to laboratory rearing conditions (parallels the wild vs. control N. lepidus difference).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both parental species occupy **clear, spring-fed freshwater streams**, a specialized and environmentally stable habitat type in the Edwards Plateau/Rio Grande drainage system.
- Current geographic ranges of *N. lepidus* and *N. proserpinus* are **entirely allopatric**, precluding natural hybridization; the hybrid sample analyzed was produced only via laboratory conditions.
- *N. proserpinus* is sympatric with *[[Notropis lutrensis]]*, yet no natural hybrids between these two are known, despite lab-demonstrated interfertility potential within subgenus Moniana.
- Members of genus *Notropis* are noted as **voracious egg predators**, complicating rearing of viable young in aquarium settings — a likely explanation for the lack of surviving F2 offspring despite observed courtship behavior.
- Environmental/laboratory temperature conditions may influence meristic trait expression (see [[Taning 1952]], [[Weisel 1955]] on *Richardsonius balteatus*), though the paper argues temperature alone cannot fully explain the extreme anal ray counts observed.

## Key Findings
1. **Extreme (non-intermediate) hybrid phenotypes**: Unlike typical interspecific fish hybrids that show intermediate character states, *N. lepidus* × *N. proserpinus* F1 hybrids exceed both parental species' ranges in anal ray count and pelvic fin reduction — a pattern also seen in [[Etheostoma spectabile]] × [[Percina caprodes]] hybrids studied by Hubbs and Strawn.
2. **Anal ray hyper-variability**: Hybrid anal ray counts (mean 14.65, range 9–20) dramatically exceed even the highest previously recorded count for the genus *Notropis* (13 rays, *N. signipinnis*).
3. **Pelvic fin loss**: 88% of hybrids exhibited partial or total pelvic fin absence, correlated with higher anal ray counts — a novel developmental link.
4. **Reduced fertility, not sterility**: Most hybrid females had reduced/undeveloped ovaries; only 1 of 14 examined had developing eggs. Some males showed reduced nuptial tuberculation. This suggests hybrids are subfertile rather than fully sterile.
5. **Three candidate explanatory mechanisms** for excess variability (per Hubbs & Strawn framework):
   - (1) **Previous introgression** — releasing suppressed chromosome segments; author considers this plausible since these hybrids show signs of partial fertility (unlike the darter hybrids where introgression was discounted).
   - (2) **Modifying genes** — rejected, since this model predicts intermediate (not extreme) phenotypes, which is contrary to observations.
   - (3) **Differential developmental rate/timing** (favored hypothesis) — hybrids may initiate/terminate trait development at mismatched "points of inflection" ([[Martin 1949]]) inherited from each parent species, permitting extreme trait expression beyond parental ranges.
6. **Laboratory environment as a confound**: Some increase in anal ray count and scale count in the *N. lepidus* control sample versus the wild sample suggests laboratory-rearing conditions contribute partially (~1 additional ray/scale) to elevated hybrid counts, but cannot account for the full magnitude of variability observed.
7. **Comparative precedent**: Increased hybrid variability has also been documented in F1 *Fundulus* hybrids ([[Newman 1908]]) and *Xiphophorus* hybrids ([[Gordon and Rosen 1951]]), suggesting this may be a broader phenomenon in fish hybridization rather than isolated to Cyprinidae.
8. **Taxonomic status caveat**: The specific distinction between *N. lepidus* and *N. proserpinus* has not been confirmed via selective mating trials; *N. proserpinus* itself may comprise multiple allopatric cryptic species.
