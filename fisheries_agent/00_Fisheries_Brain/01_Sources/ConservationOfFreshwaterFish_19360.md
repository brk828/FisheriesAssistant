# Conservation of Freshwater Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This MedWet publication (number 7, Tour du Valat, 1996) by [[Peter Maitland]] and [[Alain Crivelli]] reviews the biology, diversity, threats and conservation options for freshwater fish of the north Mediterranean region. It argues that freshwater fish are among the most threatened vertebrates, that Mediterranean fish are especially at risk because of water scarcity and high endemism, and that fish have been largely overlooked in wetland policy. Of 230 endemic taxa (132 species and 98 subspecies) in 13 families, over 70% are threatened in some way and four are extinct. Only 35 (15.2%) are legally protected in at least one country. The authors call for habitat restoration, catchment-scale water management, statistically based fishery regulation, controlled introductions, captive breeding and wider use of the [[Ramsar Convention]] for fish habitat.

## Background & Description

The document is a conservation handbook in the MedWet series, prepared with support from the European Community and partners including the [[Ramsar Convention]], WWF and Wetlands International. Its preface, by the Council of Europe, notes that Great Britain holds 34 fish species, Denmark 44, Spain 68 and Greece 106, and that the Mediterranean has the greatest proportion of endangered species.

Key background points:

- Over 20,000 fish species are described; about one-third of fish are primary freshwater species, mainly carps, characins and catfishes.
- Fish are limited to their water body, so a single toxic spill or acidification event can affect a whole population.
- The [[Mediterranean Basin]] supports rich but threatened fish faunas, with freshwater fish of the south more at risk than those of the north.
- Taxonomy is incompletely resolved. The genus [[Barbus]] (nearly 800 species) illustrates how hybridisation and cryptic lineages complicate management.
- Fish age can be estimated from scales, otoliths, opercular bones and fin rays, and growth varies widely with food, temperature and latitude.

Regional diversity is summarised in the table of native and introduced species by country (for example Greece 106 indigenous and 11 introduced; Iceland 5 indigenous and none introduced). The Mediterranean islands are naturally impoverished: Malta and Cyprus have no native freshwater fish, and Crete has none either, while the Balearics, Corsica, Sardinia, Sicily and the Ionian islands together have 16 native and some 14 introduced species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Biodiversity and endemism.** The northern Mediterranean fauna is more diverse than the rest of Europe, which was impoverished after the last ice age. Endemic taxa are concentrated in lowland rivers (67%) and natural lakes (54%), with mountain torrents (34%) and springs (17%) also important. Marshes (11%), coastal lagoons (6%) and reservoirs (7%) hold few endemics. [[Cyprinodontidae]] are characteristic Mediterranean fish, occurring only in the genera [[Valencia]] and [[Aphanius]] (nine Aphanius species recorded).

**Ecosystem role.** Fish are linked to food webs through primary producers, consumers and decomposers. The document gives examples such as [[Lake Kinneret]], where a predatory barbel and a cichlid depend on endemic prey and alga. Piscivorous fish can drive trophic cascades, and fish stocking in Israeli reservoirs with [[Silver Carp]] and [[Bighead Carp]] controlled algal blooms and zooplankton.

**Habitat types.** Rivers, lakes, reservoirs and lagoons are discussed separately. Lagoons (salinity 5 to 80 ‰) support at least 136 fish species in Mediterranean France alone, against 179 recorded from adjacent coastal waters. Saline lakes such as Lake Tuz, Turkey, may hold no fish at all.

**Pressures.** Pollution, land use change, [[Eutrophication]], engineering, overfishing, introductions, recreation and angling are all examined. The River Ter (Catalonia) and the [[River Rhone]] are presented as case studies of long-term degradation from industry, urbanisation, dams and thermal discharges.

## Key Findings

- **Endemism and threat:** 230 north Mediterranean endemic taxa are recognised; over 70% are threatened, four are extinct, and only 27% are regarded as not threatened. Dozens of taxa could disappear within a decade without action.
- **Legal protection gap:** 35 taxa (15.2%) are protected in at least one country; 24 of these were first protected between 1981 and 1990, and 11 between 1991 and 1992.
- **Introductions:** At [[Lake Egirdir]], Turkey, 10,000 [[Pikeperch]] released in 1955 led to the loss of three Phoxinellus species and the probable disappearance of two more endemics. The authors conclude a single piscivorous species can transform an ecosystem. On Mediterranean islands, introduced species now outnumber native ones.
- **Marbled Trout:** Hybridisation with introduced [[Brown Trout]] has eliminated the species from many rivers; pure stock survives mainly in the River Zadlascica. Recovery includes four new pure populations, a captive breeding stock and a stocking-prohibition campaign.
- **Atlantic Sturgeon:** The construction of a dam in 1930 on the Guadalquivir cut spawning access; no fish have been recorded since 1975. The species is believed extinct in Spain, and by the mid-1990s only six captive fish remained in France.
- **Karst Sculpin:** Discovered in 1964, this 30 mm species occupies only a few kilometres of stream below a single spring. A species conservation plan and a captive breeding programme were in preparation.
- **Lake Kerkini and Lake Koronia:** Koronia's Carp catch fell from 205 kg/ha/year (1948–60) to 42 kg/ha/year (1972–83), with four species lost entirely.
- **Lake Mikri Prespa:** Catches recovered after a 1990s rise in biomass, attributed to reduced fishing effort, a ban on nets with mesh under 45 mm, longer closed seasons and favourable spawning water levels.
- **Fisheries management:** Mesh-size regulation, catch-per-unit-effort monitoring and closed seasons are recommended to maintain sustainable yields.
- **Ramsar criteria:** In 1996 the Scientific and Technical Review Panel proposed criteria allowing wetlands to be designated as international fish habitat, even without waterfowl value.
- **Legislation:** Fish were absent from EU protection until the 1992 [[Habitats Directive]]. Its Annex lists about 80 fish taxa but omits many endemic Mediterranean species and needs updating.
- **Management principles:** The authors recommend catchment-scale integrated management, minimum flow setting, restoration of eutrophic wetlands, habitat restoration as the primary conservation objective, and genetic monitoring of stocking programmes.
