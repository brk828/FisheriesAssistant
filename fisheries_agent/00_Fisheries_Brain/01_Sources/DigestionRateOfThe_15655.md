# Digestion Rate of the Clear Lake Black Bullhead

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This study (Baur, 1970) quantified digestion rate and daily food consumption of yearling [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*) in Clear Lake, Iowa, using paired laboratory force-feeding experiments and field sampling (via the [[Bajkov Method]]). Fish were force-fed [[Chironomid Larvae]] (*Chironomus riparius*) after a 3-day starvation period, and stomach evacuation was tracked over 2–12 hours. Digestion rate was found to depend jointly on meal size and digestion time, with stomachs mostly empty by 12 hours post-feeding. Tripling the force-fed meal size increased digestion rate 2.4-fold, a magnitude consistent with findings in other fishes (bluegill, Florida gar). Fish digesting natural lake diets processed food more slowly than laboratory-fed fish, likely due to differing food composition (detritus, larvae, fish fry vs. pure chironomid larvae). Daily food consumption scaled with food abundance, reaching ~5.6% body weight/day in both turbid lake water (lab) and free-ranging lake fish, with two daily feeding peaks (1:00 AM and 5:00 PM) reflecting a nocturnal-crepuscular feeding chronology. The study underscores the ecological significance of black bullheads as major consumers of benthic chironomid biomass, potentially competing with more desirable sport fish species in [[Clear Lake, Iowa]].

## Background & Description

- **Study specimens:** Age-group I yearling black bullheads
  - Total length range: 7.6–14.5 cm
  - Live weight range: 4.4–28.5 g
  - Force-feeding subset: TL 8.3–10.7 cm (mean 9.2 cm)
  - Bajkov method subset: TL 8.0–12.2 cm
  - Lake food-consumption subset: mean weight 13.7 g, mean TL 10.1 cm
- **Feeding apparatus/physiology relevant to diet:** Stomach capable of retaining/evacuating chironomid larvae meals up to 300 mg; regurgitation observed variably after force-feeding (retained weights ranged 0.02–0.30 g from 0.20–0.30 g force-fed).
- **Prey size reference:** Laboratory chironomid larvae (*C. riparius*) averaged 228 larvae/gram; lake-collected chironomid larvae (*Tendipes tentans*) were noted to be substantially larger than laboratory specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** [[Clear Lake, Iowa]] — a shallow, turbid, eutrophic lake; fish sampled via seining in littoral zones.
- **Trophic role:** Primary benthic invertivore; main prey is [[Chironomid Larvae]], supplemented by detritus, vegetation, adult insects, and fish fry.
- **Co-occurring species utilizing same chironomid resource:** [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Yellow Bass]] (*Morone mississippiensis*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*), and [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) — implying potential interspecific food competition.
- **Benthic prey standing crop:** Mrachek and Bachmann (1967) estimated summer benthic organism standing crop (dominated by chironomids) at ~79 kg/hectare in Clear Lake, contextualizing the substantial dietary pressure exerted by abundant bullhead populations.
- **Environmental conditions during study:**
  - Lab digestion trials: water temp 21–24°C (force-feeding), 22–25°C (Bajkov method)
  - Lab food consumption trials: turbid lake water 25–28°C; non-turbid tap water 20–23°C
  - Field lake sampling (Aug 20–22): water temp 25–27°C
- **Feeding chronology:** Nocturnal/crepuscular feeding pattern; peaks at 1:00 AM and 5:00 PM; minimal feeding activity 6:00 AM–1:00 PM and 7:00–10:00 PM. Consistent with prior findings (Carlander & Cleary 1949; Darnell & Meierotto 1965) that bullheads are more active at night than day.

## Key Findings

### Digestion Rate (Force-Feeding Method)
- 74 fish force-fed 0.20 g or 0.30 g of chironomid larvae; actual retained weights ranged 0.02–0.30 g due to regurgitation.
- Digestion rates ranged 2–26 mg larvae evacuated/hour, increasing rapidly with meal size up to ~160 mg fed, then leveling toward a maximum near 300 mg fed (approaching **26 mg/hr** asymptote).
- Percentage stomach evacuation increased with time: **31.4%/hr at 2 hrs** → **96.8%/hr at 12 hrs** post-feeding; absolute digestion rate (%/hr) *decreased* from 15.7% (2 hr) to 8.1% (12 hr).
- Stomachs were mostly empty of food by **12 hours** after force-feeding.

### Statistical Modeling
- Covariance analysis (untransformed): 80% (R²) of variation in food recovered explained by weight fed (X₁) and digestion time (X₂):
  **Y = 0.051 + 0.544 X₁ − 0.014 X₂**
- Log-transformed digestion rate model (77% R²):
  **log Y = −0.978 − 0.022 X₁ + 0.803 log X₂**
- Tripling force-fed meal size increased digestion rate **2.4×** — comparable to:
  - Bluegill sunfish (Windell 1966): 2.2× rate increase with 2.7× meal size increase (mealworm larvae, not force-fed)
  - Florida gar (Hunt 1960): 2.2× rate increase with ~3× meal size increase (force-fed *Gambusia*)

### Digestion Rate Under Natural Conditions (Bajkov Method)
- 110 lake-caught bullheads (2 samples of 55) sampled at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 hrs post-capture.
- Mean stomach food content decreased from **49 mg (time 0)** to **6 mg (8 hrs)**.
- Regression: **Y = 0.042 − 0.005X**; r² = 0.88 (88% of variance explained by time).
- Digestion was **slower in naturally feeding lake fish** than in lab force-fed fish, attributed to differing food composition (detritus/mixed diet vs. pure chironomid larvae).

### Daily Food Consumption — Laboratory
- Fish fed 3% or 6% body-weight rations of chironomid larvae daily.
- **Non-turbid tap water (20–23°C):** 3% ration → 1.3% BW/day consumed; 6% ration → 3.6% BW/day consumed.
- **Turbid lake water (25–28°C):** 3% ration → fully consumed; 6% ration → **5.6% BW/day** consumed.
- Consumption nearly doubled in turbid/warmer water vs. non-turbid/cooler water, though temperature and turbidity were confounded (cites Darnell & Meierotto 1962: 10°C rise doubles gut passage rate).

### Daily Food Consumption — Field (Clear Lake)
- 204 fish sampled over 24 non-consecutive hourly periods (Aug 20–22, 1968).
- Mean stomach content: **0.24 g** food = **1.78% body weight** present at any time.
- Estimated digestion rate via lab-derived equation → theoretical daily consumption: **0.77 g/day = 5.6% body weight/day**.
- This lake-derived consumption estimate **exactly matched** the 5.6% BW/day laboratory result for turbid water at equivalent temperatures — a key validation of the lab-to-field extrapolation.
- Estimated ~175 chironomid larvae consumed/fish/day (based on lab-sized larvae; actual lake larvae were larger, so true larvae count likely lower).

### Ecological Implication
- Given high bullhead abundance and substantial chironomid consumption rates, the species likely exerts significant competitive pressure on shared benthic food resources also used by more economically desirable species like [[Bluegill]] and [[Yellow Perch]].
