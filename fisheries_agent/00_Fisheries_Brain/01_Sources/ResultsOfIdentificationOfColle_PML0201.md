# Identification of Larval Fish Collections from the Upper Salt and Gila Rivers, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by [[Kevin R. Beetgen]] of Colorado State University for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Office of Endangered Species (dated 10 September 1985), summarizes the identification of 16 collections of larval fishes made 19–24 May 1985 in the upper [[Salt River]] and [[Gila River]] drainages of Arizona. A total of 7 species and 7,952 specimens were identified. No non-native species were found in these collections. The report notes the taxonomic uncertainty surrounding the genus [[Gila]], particularly for larval specimens from [[Eagle Creek]].

## Background & Description

The study analyzed larval fish collections gathered by a team of collectors, listed as DLP ([[D.L. Propst]]), PCM ([[P.C. Marsh]]), WLM ([[W.L. Minckley]]), GLB (G.L. Burton), CLC (C.L. Couret), JNH (J.N. Hanson), and RDM (R.D. Mayor). All larval collections were to be housed at the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]]. Table I (locality data, counts and size ranges) is summarized in the source but is not reproduced here.

Identification relied on morphological characters, and the report highlights the difficulty of separating larval [[Gila]] species in the lower and upper [[Colorado River]] basin. Identifications of larval Gila were therefore treated as tentative and based mainly on whether adults of the species had been collected at the same time and place as the larvae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Dominant species:** Sonora sucker (*Catostomus insignis*) and desert sucker (*Pantosteus clarki*) were numerically dominant, comprising 45.62% and 42.57% of all specimens, respectively.
- **Other common species:** Longfin dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*) made up 7.37%, followed by speckled dace (*Rhinichthys osculus*) at 1.42%.
- **Rarer taxa:** Gila chub (*Gila intermedia*, 0.25%, n=21), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*, 0.15%, n=12) and roundtail chub (*Gila robusta*, 0.14%, n=11).
- **Non-native fishes:** None were identified in the 16 collections, although introduced fishes have been documented from some collection localities in earlier studies.
- **Spikedace records:** Spikedace were found in two [[Eagle Creek]] collections, a stream not previously known to support the species.

Distinguishing Gila species relied on myomere counts and pigmentation. Larvae from the [[Blue River]], a locality known to support Gila intermedia, were heavily pigmented with 24–25 preanal myomeres. Larvae from Eagle Creek were lightly pigmented with 28–29 preanal myomeres, characteristics typical of Gila robusta from the upper Colorado River system and the Gila River of New Mexico; Eagle Creek specimens were therefore referred to Gila robusta.

## Key Findings

- 7 species and 7,952 larval specimens were identified from 16 collections (19–24 May 1985).
- Catostomids dominated the sample: Sonora sucker (45.62%) and desert sucker (42.57%).
- No non-native species were recorded.
- Spikedace were documented in Eagle Creek, extending the known range of the species in this drainage.
- Identifications of larval Gila are tentative due to taxonomic confusion; Eagle Creek specimens were assigned to Gila robusta on the basis of myomere counts and pigmentation.
- All larval collections were designated for deposit at the Arizona State University Collection of Fishes.
