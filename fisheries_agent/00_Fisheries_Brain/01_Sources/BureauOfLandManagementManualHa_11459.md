# BLM Manual Section 6780 - Habitat Management Plans

#wildlife-management #fisheries-management #conservation-policy #habitat-management #ecosystem-management

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Manual Section 6780 establishes comprehensive policy, procedures, and guidelines for the preparation and implementation of Habitat Management Plans (HMPs) on public lands. Released December 23, 1981 (Rel. 6-85), it provides the administrative framework for managing fish, wildlife, and plant habitats consistent with multiple-use management principles. The manual emphasizes coordination with State wildlife agencies under the [[Sikes Act]], ecosystem-based management approaches, and integration with Resource Management Plans (RMPs) and Management Framework Plans (MFPs). HMPs serve as "comprehensive plans" under Title II of the Sikes Act and represent the primary vehicle for wildlife habitat development funding and implementation on BLM lands.

## Background & Description

The manual defines HMPs as written and approved activity plans for geographical areas of public lands identifying wildlife habitat management actions to achieve specific objectives related to RMP/MFP decisions. Several HMP categories are distinguished: [[Sikes Act]] HMPs (prepared jointly with State agencies), Other HMPs (not covered under Sikes Act), Implemented HMPs (approved for funding), Completed HMPs (at least 90% funded and in maintenance phase), New HMPs (ready for upcoming implementation), and Revised HMPs (updated to meet current standards).

The document establishes clear organizational responsibilities from the Director through State Directors, District Managers, and Area Managers. Staff Wildlife and Fisheries Biologists and Botanists assist in HMP preparation and coordination with State agency personnel. The manual emphasizes that HMPs must be prepared in consultation and coordination with State agencies or other appropriate agencies for plants.

**Priority Species and Habitats** are defined as those identified in MFP/RMP or special studies as having special significance, including endangered, threatened, or sensitive species; species of high economic or recreational value; and aquatic or riparian habitats. The manual also recognizes individuals or populations of significance based on density, diversity, size, disjunct occurrences, or age.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The manual adopts an **[[Ecosystem Management]]** approach defined as "the integration of different land management goals to ensure that the integrity of the ecosystem will be maintained." This philosophy directs management toward habitat rather than individual species, operating on the principle that species will be maintained naturally if a proper mosaic of habitats exists. The overall goal is retention or management of all natural habitats in sufficient quantities to support viable and self-sustaining populations of all native wildlife, including riparian areas, cliffs, wetlands, and old-growth forests.

**Crucial Habitat** is defined as habitat absolutely basic to maintaining viable populations during certain seasons or specific reproduction periods. The manual emphasizes protection and enhancement of fragile wetland and riparian habitats, anadromous fish habitats, and crucial biological use areas such as strutting grounds, rookeries, calving areas, and nesting sites.

Habitat management areas are established to encompass the biological requirements of priority species regardless of land status and are managed on an ecosystem basis. The manual requires consideration of all habitat elements including nutrition, cover, water, and living space in amounts, qualities, and locations required for organisms to complete their life cycles normally and naturally.

## Key Findings

**Policy Framework:**
- All HMPs must be prepared in consultation and coordination with State agencies; as many as possible should be covered under [[Sikes Act]] authority
- An RMP or MFP must normally be completed prior to HMP development, with exceptions for special circumstances including conservation of threatened/endangered species, protection of fragile wetland/riparian habitats, and protection of crucial biological use areas
- Funding priority for habitat development is given to Sikes Act HMPs unless otherwise justified
- HMPs concentrate on priority species and their habitats in geographically limited areas to focus special management attention

**Plan Components and Format:**
The manual prescribes a standardized 18-component format including: Cover, Title Page, Abstract, Table of Contents, Location Map, Checklist for Preparation and Review (Form 6780-1), Introduction, Land Status/Administration, Management Objectives, Planned Actions, Evaluation and Monitoring, Habitat Management Plan Progress Report (Form 6780-2), Coordination sections, Wildlife Economics, Public Affairs, Costs and Funding Needs, Concurrence and Approval, and Appendix.

**Management Objectives** must be realistic and quantified in terms of time, location, habitat site, and priority species. Examples provided include: "Reestablish pronghorn antelope in Puddle Valley by 1984 and provide forage and cover annually to support an average annual population of 500 mature animals" and "Protect 30 miles of riparian vegetation on Indian Creek to reduce turbidity from 400 mg per liter to 50 mg per liter, thus increasing the population of [[Colorado Cutthroat Trout]] from 25 fish per mile to 200 fish per mile by 1983."

**Planned Actions** must address:
- Direct actions (chaining and seedings, gabions, fencing) with cost and manpower needs
- Supporting actions (roads, fire protection, cadastral survey, land exchange)
- Mitigation and protection actions
- Feasible alternatives requiring minimum funds and conflicts
- Visitor management rules and regulations
- Enforcement programs

**Economic Analysis:**
The manual requires comprehensive economic analysis using either least-cost analysis (when benefits cannot be monetized) or benefit/cost analysis (when benefits can be estimated in monetary terms). BLM subscribes to the willingness-to-pay theory of wildlife valuation. Economic analysis must be conducted during HMP preparation and included in plan documents.

**Coordination Requirements:**
Extensive coordination is mandated with other BLM programs including: Rangeland Management (livestock grazing systems, fence design, streambank protection, water development), Forest Management (harvest methods, silvicultural procedures), Recreation, Wilderness, Areas of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC), Soil/Water/Air Resources, Wild Horse and Burro Management, Energy/Minerals Development, and Fire Management.

**Monitoring and Evaluation:**
Plans must include descriptions of studies and analyses to evaluate and monitor progress, with estimates of personnel, workmonths, season, and costs needed annually. Form 6780-2 (Habitat Management Plan Progress Report) provides standardized tracking of objectives, planned actions, and evaluation/monitoring efforts.

**Statistical Reporting:**
Annual HMP statistics must be submitted by District Managers to State Directors, who consolidate reports and submit to the Director by November 15 for the preceding fiscal year using Form 6780-3. This report serves as the principal data backup source for Bureau budget justifications and public information.

**Examples of Planned Action Techniques** provided include:
- Big Game: pinon-juniper control, timber clearcuts for edge habitat, livestock class conversion, water developments, fence modification for antelope passage, public use closures, protection of calving/kidding/lambing areas
- Small Game/Waterfowl/Nongame: use restrictions on critical habitats, bird watering devices, installation of "guzzlers" for sage grouse/chukar/quail, construction of dikes and nesting islands
- Fish: acquisition of water rights, reservoir construction, sedimentation reduction through streambank cover improvement, stream pool-riffle ratio improvement using gabions
- Threatened/Endangered Plants: control of competing exotic plants, livestock grazing fences, water rights acquisition, ORV closures

The manual emphasizes that State Directors should establish a balanced mix of "new," "completed," and "implemented" HMPs to ensure varied geographic locations receive habitat development funding during different fiscal years, with long-term schedules for project survey and design preceding actual on-the-ground work.
