# Protocol for Handling and Transport of Wild Bonytail

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #conservation-protocol

## Executive Summary

This 1987 protocol document from the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] establishes standardized procedures for field personnel to identify, capture, handle, and transport wild [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) to appropriate refugia facilities. The protocol addresses field identification criteria, transport logistics, equipment requirements, and coordination among [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] facilities across the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. All wild bonytail encountered are to be retained alive for placement in conservation refugia at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) or [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Arizona).

## Background & Description

The [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) is an endangered cyprinid native to the [[Colorado River]] system. Field identification criteria specified in this protocol include:

- Minimum total length of 300 mm
- Dorsal/anal fin-ray count of 10/10 or greater
- Caudal peduncle length divided by head length >1.0 (after Minckley 1973), OR
- Head length divided by depth of caudal peduncle >5.0 (after Minckley 1973)

Priority sampling areas where bonytail capture is anticipated include specific river reaches with recent [[Humpback Chub]] and bonytail records:

**Upper Colorado River:**
- [[Westwater Canyon]] (river miles 111-125)
- [[Cataract Canyon]] (mile 11)
- [[Moab Canyon]] (mile 72)
- [[Black Rocks]] (miles 135-136)

**Green River:**
- [[Gray Canyon]] (miles 146-171)
- [[Whirlpool Canyon]] (mile 342)

**Yampa River:**
- [[Yampa Canyon]] to [[Cross Mountain]] (miles 18-56)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The protocol recognizes that bonytail populations in the upper basin are critically depleted, necessitating immediate conservation action for any wild-caught individuals. Field teams conducting intensive sampling with electrofishing, entanglement nets, seines, and angling in reaches with known [[Humpback Chub]] populations must be prepared for potential bonytail encounters.

The document emphasizes minimal handling stress and proper water quality maintenance during transport. Temperature management is critical—aeration should only be applied if water temperature does not exceed upper thermal tolerance. Cooling may be accomplished with crystal ice or sealed frozen containers. For holds exceeding 12 hours, partial submersion of holding containers in ambient river water is recommended.

## Key Findings

**Field Handling Requirements:**
- Fish should be placed immediately in coolers (100-150 quart capacity) filled with fresh water from capture site
- Individual specimen data collection (length, weight) should be deferred to minimize handling stress
- Aeration using portable aerators with diffusers applied only under appropriate temperature conditions
- Water should be partially exchanged at 1- to several-hour intervals to provide agitation and aeration
- Container should remain closed except for water addition and fish inspection
- Transport downstream to designated takeout should occur as quickly as reasonably possible

**Required Equipment (provided by FWS):**
1. One 100-150 quart cooler
2. One to two 23 cubic foot "E" oxygen cylinders (~3 lbs) with regulator, tubing, and carbon diffuser
3. One live net
4. One Vernier caliper
5. One identification key for morphometric measurements of *Gila*

**Coordination Protocol:**
- Field teams must notify appropriate [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] (CRFP) stations one week prior to sampling trips in priority reaches
- [[Green River]] subbasin teams contact Vernal, UT station (Harold Tyus, 801-789-0354)
- [[Colorado River]] subbasin teams contact Grand Junction, CO station (Lynn Kaeding, 303-245-9319)
- Upon capture, immediate contact required to arrange pickup and transport

**Transport Chain:**
1. **River to Takeout:** Field team transports fish downstream via boat/raft
2. **Takeout to Temporary Facility:** CRFP personnel use 250-gallon fish distribution tank mounted in pickup truck
3. **Temporary Holding:** [[Green River]] captures held at [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] (earthen ponds); [[Colorado River]] captures held at [[Hotchkiss National Fish Hatchery]] (8-foot circular fiberglass tank with continuous groundwater supply)
4. **Permanent Facility:** Transfer within two weeks via airplane or distribution tank to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) or [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Arizona)

**Capture Documentation:**
- Date, time, location, and anecdotal notes must be recorded
- Information should accompany fish or be made available to final recipient as soon as practicable

**Contingency Provisions:**
- In remote areas without transport equipment, fish well-being takes priority
- Uninjured fish should be returned alive to capture site if recovery not feasible
- Leaving fish unattended for later retrieval is not recommended
- If primary contacts unavailable, Regional Endangered Species Office should be contacted (John Hamill, Denver: 303-236-7398; Jerry Burton, Albuquerque: 505-766-3979)
