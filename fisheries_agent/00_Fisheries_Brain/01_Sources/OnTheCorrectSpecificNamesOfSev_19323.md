# On the Correct Specific Names of Several North American Species of the Phyllopod Genus *Branchinecta* Verrill

#branchiopoda #anostraca #branchinecta

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[J. G. Mackin]] (Texas A&M College) corrects a long-standing error in the names of North American fairy shrimps in the genus [[Branchinecta]]. The error began when [[Alfred S. Packard]] described [[Branchinecta Coloradensis]] in 1874. A few years later the name was transferred to a different species, and a third species, [[Branchinecta Lindahli]], was drawn into the confusion. For roughly half a century, three species were carried in the literature under incorrect names.

Mackin shows that *B. coloradensis* is a species of intermontane valleys and high plains that inhabits temporary waters, not an alpine permanent-pool form. He reassigns the name *B. lindahli* to a plains species (known as *B. packardi*), names the alpine species *B. shantzi* sp. nov., and provides a synonymy and a key to the U.S. species.

## Background & Description

The genus *Branchinecta* belongs to the phyllopods (Branchiopoda, order [[Anostraca]]), commonly called fairy shrimps. The paper's central problem is nomenclatural: the species name attached to a taxon did not match the original description.

**Original description (Packard, 1874).** The description of *B. coloradensis* was based on two collections:
- one female from a pond near Twin Lake Creek, Colorado, at 12,500 feet elevation (collected by Lieut. W. L. Carpenter during Hayden's Survey of Colorado, 1873);
- "about a hundred males, and females, with eggs," from Colorado (Dr. Viele, Museum of Comparative Zoology, no date).

The text was short and, in Mackin's view, based on a single male. It compared the species with *B. groenlandica*, noting less curved claspers, unarmed basal and second joints, and broad, flattened, subspatulate second joints. The stated length was 0.57 inch. Three drawings were grouped as figure 12, one of which was mislabeled as a female head.

**Redescription (Packard, 1883).** Packard's monograph on North American phyllopods was treated as the authoritative taxonomic work for more than half a century. His redescription used several hundred specimens from a pool at about 12,000 feet on Gray's Peak, Colorado, and described "stout prominent tubercles" on the male claspers. However, it reused the 1874 figures, which do not show those tubercles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat contrast:** Mackin's specimens show that *B. coloradensis* lives in temporary waters of intermontane valleys and high plains. The literature after 1874, by contrast, described it as an alpine, permanent-water form. Mackin attributes this shift mainly to the 1883 redescription.
- **Alpine species:** The high-elevation form (Gray's Peak, about 12,000 ft) is the species Mackin renames *B. shantzi*. It is described as alpine in habitat.
- **Plains species:** *B. lindahli* was described from specimens collected at Wallace, Kansas, and is therefore a plains form. Mackin reports that the plains species ranges from a line through central Oklahoma and Kansas west to the foothills of the Rockies.
- **Reported ranges and collection sites:** Colorado (Twin Lake Creek area, Gray's Peak, an intermontane valley), the foothills of the Rockies in New Mexico, Wallace (Kansas), and central Oklahoma and Kansas.

## Key Findings

1. **Error origin:** Packard (1874) claimed a basis in two collections, but the figures and text came from the approximately 100-specimen Colorado collection of unknown locality. The single female from Twin Lake Creek had no detectable influence on the description.
2. **Redescription problems:** The 1883 text is not an original description, and its specimens are at best homoeotypes. There is no evidence they were compared with the 1874 material or deposited in a museum. Its figures, however, were based on primary type material.
3. **Identity of *B. coloradensis*:** It is a species of intermontane valleys and high plains that lives in temporary waters, not an alpine form.
4. **Linder's position rejected:** [[Linder 1941]] argued that *B. lindahli* is an immature *B. coloradensis*, and that no North American species has unarmed male claspers when mature. Mackin rejects this. Linder had not seen *B. lindahli* specimens, had apparently not known of the 1874 description, and had misidentified the alpine species. Packard (1874) and Shantz (1905) both described mature unarmed-clasper males.
5. **New name for the alpine species:** The alpine species is named *Branchinecta shantzi* sp. nov., in honor of [[Herbert L. Shantz]], whose 1905 description Mackin considered the best available.
6. **Identity of *B. lindahli*:** Packard's (1883) description of the clasper spur, "much larger and more pointed than in *B. coloradensis*," identifies the species. It corresponds to the plains species known as *B. packardi* [[Pearse 1913]], which Mackin reassigns to the name *B. lindahli*.
7. **Synonymies:** Mackin provides full synonymies for *B. coloradensis*, *B. shantzi* sp. nov., and *B. lindahli*, listing the many misidentifications across works by [[Shantz 1905]], [[Daday 1910]], [[Pearse 1918]], [[Creaser 1935]], [[Mackin 1939]], and [[Linder 1941]].
8. **Key to U.S. species:** The paper gives a dichotomous key to the known U.S. *Branchinecta*, separated first by male clasper armature:
   - Unarmed male claspers: *B. gigas* Lynch (very large, 50–90 mm, with small widely spaced cercopod spines) versus *B. coloradensis* Packard (cercopods with fine pectinate setae, bases nearly contiguous).
   - Armed male claspers: *B. paludosa* O. F. Mueller (basal clasper joint with an internal median row of spines and no basal process) versus species with a basal process. Among these, *B. shantzi* has a low, thickly spined internal eminence and is alpine, while *B. lindahli* has a spur-like process directed proximally and is a plains species.
