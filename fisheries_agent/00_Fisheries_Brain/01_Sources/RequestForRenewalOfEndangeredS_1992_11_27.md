# Request for Renewal of Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #woundfin #virgin-roundtail-chub #endangered-species #virgin-river

## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative permit renewal request submitted by Dr. Thomas B. Hardy (Utah State University, Institute for Natural Systems Engineering) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque). The request seeks renewal of **Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811**, authorizing continued population monitoring and life-history studies of two federally protected [[Virgin River]] fishes: the [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and the [[Virgin Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*). The work is conducted under a cooperative agreement between [[Utah State University]] and the [[Bureau Of Land Management]], with participation from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] and state fish and game agencies of Utah, Arizona, and Nevada. The stated purpose is to determine **instream flow requirements** for these species, a designated priority action under the **Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan**. Attached collection data (92 mainstem sampling events plus North Creek tributary stations) were compiled into the Virgin River Fishes Data Base and distributed to USFWS and state agencies. All captured specimens were enumerated, measured, and released at the site of capture (catch-and-release protocol).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat**: The [[Virgin River]] mainstem and associated tributary/spring systems in southwestern Utah, northwestern Arizona, and southeastern Nevada.
- **Sampling stations** span a longitudinal gradient of the river system, including:
  - Above/below [[Pah Tempe Springs]]
  - [[LaVerkin Creek]] (above and upstream)
  - [[Ash Creek]] (multiple stations upstream, near, and below)
  - [[Quail Creek]], [[Hurricane Bridge]] (above/below)
  - [[Washington Diversion]] (above/below)
  - [[Twin Bridges]], [[I-15 Bridge]], [[Atkinville Wash]]
  - [[Beaver Dam Wash]] and [[Beaver Dam Creek]] (up and near)
  - [[Big Bend]], [[Mesquite Diversion]] (above/below)
  - [[Riverside]] and stations up to 6 miles below
  - [[Virgin River Narrows]] (Cedar Pocket)
  - [[Johnson Diversion]] (above/below)
  - [[Lower Utah Fish Barrier]] (above/below) — a critical management structure separating native fish habitat from non-native fish invasion routes.
  - [[North Creek]] tributary stations (7 distinct stations, 00–06 numbering)
- **Co-occurring species** documented alongside the two target endangered taxa include both native cyprinids/catostomids (Speckled Dace, Flannelmouth Sucker, Desert Sucker, Spine Dace) and several **non-native competitors/predators** (Red Shiner, Gambusia, Carp, Channel Catfish, Bullhead species) — a community structure indicative of the invasion pressure facing native Virgin River fish assemblages.
- **Conservation framework**: Research is explicitly tied to determining **instream flow requirements**, implying that flow regime alteration (e.g., diversions noted at Washington, Mesquite, and Johnson Diversions) is a key ecological stressor under investigation for these federally listed species.

## Key Findings

- The permit renewal covers continued **population monitoring and life-history research** on Woundfin and Virgin Roundtail Chub as mandated by the **Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan** and reviewed by the **Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team**.
- Research is a **multi-agency cooperative effort**: Utah State University, Bureau of Land Management, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and state fish/game agencies of Utah, Arizona, and Nevada.
- A **catch-and-release protocol** was strictly followed — all specimens were enumerated, measured, and returned to their capture location, minimizing study-induced mortality to these endangered species.
- Collection data were compiled into a centralized **Virgin River Fishes Data Base**, shared across federal and state resource agencies for coordinated recovery tracking.
- The dataset comprises **92 mainstem collection events** plus dedicated **North Creek tributary sampling** (stations 00–06), spanning multiple months and years, indicating a robust, spatially distributed long-term monitoring program.
- Presence of multiple **non-native fish species** (Red Shiner, Carp, Channel Catfish, Gambusia, Bullhead taxa) at numerous stations underscores ongoing invasive species pressure as a management concern for native Virgin River fish conservation.
- The explicit focus on **instream flow requirements** links this fisheries monitoring effort directly to water management and diversion operations (e.g., Washington, Mesquite, Johnson Diversions) affecting the Virgin River system.
