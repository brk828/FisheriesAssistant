# Cyphocharax pantostictos: A New Curimatid from the Western Amazon Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #curimatidae

## Executive Summary

This 1990 taxonomic paper by [[Richard P. Vari]] and [[Ramiro Barriga S.]] describes *Cyphocharax pantostictos* as a new species of curimatid characiform from the western Amazon Basin. Its defining feature is seven or eight longitudinal series of prominent dark spots on the body. The species was initially suspected to be a range extension of [[Steindachnerina fasciata]], a species endemic to the [[Rio Madeira Basin]] in Brazil. Detailed comparison showed it is not a conspecific and is not closely related to that species. It belongs to the genus [[Cyphocharax]], and the authors note that this genus may not be monophyletic. The species is known from Ecuador and northern Peru, and it is another member of the [[Western Amazonian Endemic Area]].

## Background & Description

The western Amazon Basin has a diverse drainage network, varied stream gradients and complex aquatic habitats, which support a rich fish fauna. The family [[Curimatidae]] is well represented in this region. Some curimatids are widespread across the Amazon, while others have restricted ranges. The authors first collected the specimens during fish surveys of eastern Ecuador and northern Peru.

**Morphology**
- Body moderately elongate and somewhat compressed, with a pointed head and a forked caudal fin.
- Holotype: USNM 306594, 72.4 mm standard length (SL).
- Meristics: 31 total vertebrae (counted in 8 specimens); dorsal-fin rays ii,9; anal-fin rays ii,7 or iii,7; pectoral-fin rays 13 to 15; pelvic-fin rays i,8 or i,7,i.
- Pored lateral-line scales from supracleithrum to hypural joint: 29 to 31.
- Pelvic fin length 0.20–0.23 SL; pectoral fin 0.18–0.21 SL; head length 0.29–0.33 SL.
- Colour in life: silvery with an olive-grey dorsal cast, black spots in horizontal series, a distinct black mid-lateral stripe on the caudal peduncle, and hyaline fins.
- Colour in alcohol: yellowish-tan ground colour, with a dark patch on each scale that is largest mid-laterally.

**Type Series and Material**
- 10 specimens examined, 34.4–98.2 mm SL.
- The holotype was collected from Laguna de Jatuncocha, Napo, Ecuador, on 29 September 1988 by R. Barriga.
- Paratypes come from Ecuador (Napo, Estero Culebrero, Rio Napo near Coca) and Peru (Loreto: Rio Nanay, Rio Putumayo, Rio Ucayali).

**Etymology**
- The name *pantostictos* comes from Greek, meaning "spotted all over," which refers to the spots on the lateral and dorsal body surfaces.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occurs in blackwater habitats:
- Holotype locality (Laguna de Jatuncocha): submerged vegetation in blackwater at pH 5.5, at a depth of 1.5 m.
- Peruvian specimens (Rio Nanay and Rio Putumayo area): acidic black waters among grass and submerged vegetation.
- Rio Yasuni specimens: a slow-flowing, turbid stream at pH 6.0 with no submerged vegetation.

The authors used these habitat data to characterise the environments where the species was collected, but they did not present broader ecological studies.

**Biogeographic context**
- The range of *C. pantostictos* does not overlap with that of *Steindachnerina fasciata*. The two ranges are separated by more than 2,000 river kilometres.
- Because of this separation, the authors concluded that mimicry is unlikely to explain the strong external resemblance between the two species.
- The second congener with a similar pattern, [[Cyphocharax multilineatus]], occurs in the Rio Negro system of Venezuela and Brazil.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Cyphocharax pantostictos* is described as new, and its diagnosis separates it from congeners on the basis of body pigmentation.
- **Distinctive pigmentation:** It has seven or eight horizontal series of prominent dark spots aligned along the scale centres. The authors consider this pattern unique within the genus. Its mid-lateral caudal peduncle patch is absent in *C. multilineatus*.
- **Distinction from *C. multilineatus*:** The latter has solid wavy horizontal lines rather than discrete spots, lies along scale overlap zones rather than scale centres, and has a discrete band across the head that is absent in the new species.
- **Misidentification risk:** Its external appearance is nearly identical to that of *Steindachnerina fasciata*, which has led to past misidentifications.
- **Key morphological differences from *S. fasciata*:**
  - Vertebrae: 31 versus 32–34 (typically 33)
  - Lateral-line scales to hypural joint: 29–31 versus 32–37
  - Scale rows above the lateral line to the dorsal-fin origin: 4 versus 5 or 6
  - Pelvic fin length: 0.20–0.23 versus 0.23–0.25 SL
  - Gape width: 0.24–0.28 versus 0.28–0.32 head length
- **Buccopharyngeal complex:** *C. pantostictos* has three simple longitudinal fleshy folds on the roof of the oral cavity. *S. fasciata* has a mass of lobulate fleshy bodies, which the authors consider a derived condition.
- **Phylogenetic status:** Based on polarised characters, the two species are not closely related. *C. pantostictos* lacks the derived features that define *Steindachnerina* and its relevant clades.
- **Genus status:** The authors place the species in *Cyphocharax* as a provisional decision. They note that the genus lacks identified synapomorphies and may not be monophyletic. Ongoing revisionary work by the senior author is expected to address this.
- **Range:** The species is recorded from the Rio Napo, Rio Putumayo, Rio Ucayali and Rio Nanay systems, in Ecuador and northern Peru. It is endemic to the Western Amazonian Endemic Area.
- **Mimicry ruled out:** Because the two species' ranges do not overlap, the authors consider intergeneric mimicry an unlikely explanation for their resemblance.
- **Identification to be refined:** The authors expect further meristic and morphometric characters to distinguish the species within *Cyphocharax*, but these await revisionary studies of that speciose genus.
