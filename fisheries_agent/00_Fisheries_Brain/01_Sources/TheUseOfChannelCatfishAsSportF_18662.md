# The Use of Channel Catfish as Sport Fish

#ichthyology #aquaculture #Ictaluridae #pond-management #sport-fishing

## Executive Summary

This 1959 study by E. E. Prather at [[Auburn University]] evaluated [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) as a sport fish in managed ponds. A 12.4-acre pond stocked at 2,000 fingerlings per acre with supplemental feeding produced exceptional results: fishermen harvested 1,292.5 pounds of catfish per acre over 14 months, with 579 fishing trips per acre averaging 2.3 pounds per trip. The operation generated $734.20 income per acre against $481.46 in production costs (fertilizer, feed, fingerlings), yielding $252.74 per acre return for labor and capital. Mortality was 29 percent (approximately 20 percent higher than unfished ponds), with 62 percent of stocked fish harvested by anglers. The study demonstrated that channel catfish, when properly managed with supplemental feeding, can produce eight times the catch of traditional [[Largemouth Bass]]-[[Bluegill]] ponds.

## Background & Description

[[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) has long been valued by anglers for fighting characteristics and flavor, though typically classified as non-game or commercial species. Several states including [[Arkansas]], [[Oklahoma]], [[Kansas]], [[Missouri]], [[Texas]], and [[California]] had established hatchery programs for stocking channel catfish in streams, rivers, and lakes. However, limited data existed on their value as sport fish in pond environments.

Previous work by Swingle showed poor results when channel catfish were stocked alone in ponds, with production in fertilized [[Alabama]] ponds averaging less than 100 pounds per acre annually due to contamination with [[Green Sunfish]] and other native species, slow growth, and high mortality. Later experiments with supplemental feeding achieved maximum production of 2,363 pounds harvestable channel catfish per acre annually with annual mortality less than 10 percent.

The fingerlings used in these experiments were provided by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Initial 2.2-Acre Pond Trial (1957):**
- Stocked March 18, 1957 at 1,000 fingerlings (2-3 inches) per acre
- Fertilized seven times with triple superphosphate and uran (equivalent to 100 pounds 8-8-0 per acre per application)
- Fed [[Auburn No. 1 Fish Feed]] daily 6 days per week, totaling 2,236 pounds per acre
- Feeding rates increased from 10 pounds per acre per day (April-May) to 50 pounds per acre per day (September-October)
- Opened to public fishing September 17 when catfish averaged 1.0 pound each
- Poor fishing success: only 64 fishermen per acre fished between September 17 and October 14
- Average catch 1.25 catfish per fisherman (1.0 pound each), 0.3 pound per hour
- 65 percent of total catch harvested first day; fishing success declined rapidly thereafter
- Only 38 percent of fishermen caught fish
- Drained December 15: recovered 868 channel catfish weighing 882.3 pounds per acre
- Survival 95 percent, but fishermen removed only 8 percent of stocked number

**Main 12.4-Acre Pond Study (1958-1959):**

*Stocking:*
- February 27, 1958: 2,000 channel catfish fingerlings (3 inches) per acre
- April 28, 1958: 1,000 adult [[Fathead Minnows]] (*Pimephales promelas*) per acre
- June 7-26, 1958: 66 [[Largemouth Bass]] fingerlings (*Micropterus salmoides*) per acre (added when green sunfish contamination discovered)
- May 14, 1959: Restocked with 100 bass per acre

*Pond Management:*
- Four full applications and two half applications of fertilizer (100 pounds 8-8-0 equivalent per acre per application)
- [[Auburn No. 2 Fish Feed]] (pelleted form) fed daily except Sundays
- Feed composition: 35% soybean oil meal, 35% ground peanut cake, 15% fish meal, 15% distillers dried solubles
- Total feed: 5,423.9 pounds per acre costing $311.46 (April 1, 1958 to October 3, 1959)
- Feeding rates varied from 5.0 to 25.0 pounds per acre per day

*Fishing Regulations:*
- Opened September 24, 1958 (catfish averaged 0.7 pound)
- $1 per permit; initial limit 3 catfish and 3 bass per permit
- Open daily except Sundays
- Closed December 8, 1958 due to cold weather; reopened March 14, 1959
- May 14, 1959 onward: limit increased to 5 catfish plus 3 bass per permit
- Over-limit fish: 20 cents each additional
- Boats available for $1 extra
- Closed October 6, 1959

## Key Findings

**Harvest and Catch Statistics:**
- Total fishing pressure: 579 fishermen per acre over 14-month period
- Total harvest per acre: 1,241 channel catfish weighing 1,292.5 pounds, plus 36.9 pounds largemouth bass and 27.0 pounds miscellaneous sunfish
- Total catch: 1,356.4 pounds per acre (approximately 8 times the average 150 pounds per acre from fertilized bass-bluegill ponds)
- Fishermen harvested 62 percent of stocked catfish
- Average catch: 2.3 pounds per fishing trip

**Seasonal Patterns:**
- Best fishing: May 1959 with 240 fishermen per acre catching 668 catfish (695.6 pounds)
- 54 percent of entire catch occurred in May 1959
- Poorest fishing: September-October 1959 (0.2-0.3 fish per trip)
- Catch per trip ranged from 0.42 pound (September 1959) to 2.90 pounds (May 1959)
- Catch per hour ranged from 0.08 pound (September 1959) to 0.58 pound (May 1959)

**Population Dynamics at Draining (November 17, 1959):**
- Channel catfish: 180 individuals, 391.2 pounds per acre (9% of stocked number remaining)
- Largemouth bass: 51 individuals, 34.5 pounds per acre
- Fathead minnows: 907 individuals, 2.4 pounds per acre
- Bluegills: 2,918 individuals, 58.1 pounds per acre
- Green sunfish: 1,862 individuals, 18.4 pounds per acre
- Miscellaneous (Gambusia, Golden Shiners, Goldfish, Bullheads): 79 individuals, 3.7 pounds per acre
- Total recovery: 508.3 pounds per acre

**Mortality and Reproduction:**
- Total mortality: 29 percent (71% of stocked fish accounted for: 62% harvested + 9% recovered)
- Mortality approximately 20 percent higher than unfished ponds (which average 5% annual mortality)
- Suspected cause of elevated mortality: fish dying after being caught and returned by fishermen catching larger specimens
- Channel catfish reproduction minimal: less than one young catfish per acre at draining

**Economic Analysis:**
- Income from fishing permits: $593.37 per acre
- Income from fish recovered at draining: $140.83 per acre (234.7 pounds dressed weight at $0.60/pound)
- Total income: $734.20 per acre
- Production costs (fertilizer, feed, fingerlings at $0.08 each): $481.46 per acre
- Net return for labor and capital: $252.74 per acre
- Price paid by fishermen: $0.35 to $2.41 per pound (average $0.46/pound)
- Production cost: $0.37 per pound

**Management Implications:**
The study concluded that channel catfish should be ranked as a most promising species for sport fish in ponds due to very high production with supplemental feeding, popularity among fishermen, and favorable catch per unit effort. The author noted ongoing research at [[Alabama Polytechnic Institute]] to determine optimal stocking and feeding rates, understocking/restocking strategies, and species combinations.
