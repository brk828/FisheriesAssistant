# Tailwater Trout Investigation: Creel Census and Limnology of the Colorado River Below Glen Canyon Dam, 1967–68

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary
This is the fifth annual segment (1 July 1967 – 30 June 1968) of a multi-year [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] study of the fishery created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. A [[Creel Census]] at [[Lee's Ferry]] was combined with limnological, plankton, bottom-fauna and fish-collection work, plus a 10-day float trip down to [[Lake Mead]].

- Estimated harvest was 9,433 fish, mostly [[Rainbow Trout]] (about 6,597) and [[Channel Catfish]] (about 2,638).
- Angler success was .39 fish per hour. This continues a decline since the study began (1.05 in 1963–64) and is attributed partly to fewer catchable-size trout being stocked.
- Water chemistry showed nothing detrimental to trout.
- Bottom fauna was sparse, apart from areas that had received transplants.

The report recommends continuing the creel census, more [[Bottom Fauna Transplants]] (especially snails), continued stocking of large sub-catchable trout, and evaluating a second game species.

## Background & Description
- **Objectives:** measure fishing pressure and harvest, and determine the physical and chemical character of the tailwater, to support a management plan for the fishery.
- **Setting:** Lee's Ferry lies about 15 miles below Glen Canyon Dam. It is the only public vehicle access to the [[Colorado River]] between the dam and Lake Mead. About 16 miles of water are fishable from the access point, with 3 miles reachable from shore and 14 from boats.
- **Funding:** mainly Section 8 of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] (Public Law 485).
- **Creel methods:**
  - Census days were set at three weekdays and three weekend days.
  - Anglers were checked by road block (the only access road) plus river-bank checks.
  - Pressure was expanded from known daily totals, with allowance for weekends and holidays.
  - Return of stocked trout was gauged with plastic streamer-tagged fish.
- **Sampling:**
  - Monthly [[Plankton]] samples.
  - Monthly bottom-fauna samples at three stations, using an [[Ekman Dredge]] and a [[Surber Sampler]].
  - Overnight [[Gill Netting]], plus seining, angling and, on the river trip, a back-pack electroshocker.
  - [[Stomach Content Analysis]] of rainbow trout.
  - A [[Condition Factor]] (C) was calculated from length and weight.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Flow and clarity:**
  - Average daily flows were higher than in the previous period, though not the extreme flows of earlier years.
  - Water stayed deep green, with the bottom visible at the Lee's Ferry stations.
  - The [[Paria River]] enters near Lee's Ferry.
  - High flows kept the riffles from being sampled, so benthic sampling was limited to 3 stations.
- **Chemistry:** all parameters, including DO, alkalinity, hardness, pH and temperature, were within acceptable ranges at the dam (closed area) and ramp stations. Temperature near Lee's Ferry was about 48 °F, and the river warmed downstream.
- **Benthos in the tailwater:**
  - Counts ranged 0–151.2 bloodworms ([[Chironomidae]] midge larvae), 0–30 snails and 0–5.5 other organisms per square foot.
  - Station III, just below the west diversion tunnel, had good numbers of *Physa* snails because of 1966–67 transplants.
  - Elsewhere midge larvae dominated.
- **Algae:** nets set below the immediate tailwater clogged quickly with algae.
- **Lee's Ferry to Lake Mead:** about 220 miles with almost no road or trail access.
  - Water was relatively clear down to the [[Little Colorado River]], with Secchi visibility of 1–3 ft.
  - Below the confluence the river turned chocolate brown, with Secchi readings of only inches.
  - Bottom fauna was almost non-existent: 102 Ekman samples yielded only 43 midge larvae (reported as .95 per sq ft).
  - Side streams had good bottom fauna, including Odonata, Plecoptera and Coleoptera larvae and *Physa* snails.

## Key Findings
**Pressure and harvest**
- Estimated harvest was 9,433 fish. Checked fish totalled 1,353: 970 rainbow trout, 354 channel catfish and 29 others.
- About 71% of anglers were male.
- Boat and shore anglers were nearly equal in number (51% and 49%). Boat catch rate was .46 fish per hour and shore catch rate was .31, probably because of concentrated fish just below the dam, reachable only by boat.
- Average success was .39 fish/hour, 2.06 fish per trip and 5.22 hours per trip.
- Earlier success was .46 (1966–67), .64 (1964–65 to 1965–66 series), .99 and 1.05 (1963–64). The desirable target is about .5 fish/hour, which the authors expect with large plants of sub-catchable fish.
- Anglers were mostly Arizona residents. Out-of-state anglers came largely from California and Utah.

**Stocking and returns**
- 33,900 trout were planted in four lots (10,000 at 3.5–4.5 in; 2,800 at 10.5–11 in; 1,000 at 9 in; 20,000 at 4–6 in), partly tagged.
- Return of the catchable-size marked plants was estimated by direct expansion of marked-fish recoveries, at roughly 28%.
- One marked fish was recovered about 12 miles downstream nine days after planting.

**Fish condition**
- 188 rainbow trout from the creel averaged .68 lb and 12.2 in, with C ≈ 38 (37 in the text). This was .02 lb lighter and .2 in longer than the previous segment.
- 17 channel catfish averaged .52 lb and 10.4 in, with C = 46.

**Collections**
- Gill nets took 734 fish. These included 278 rainbow trout (10.4 in, .39 lb, C = 35), 244 flannelmouth suckers, 98 green sunfish, 48 channel catfish, 18 carp, 17 humpback chub, 16 bluehead suckers and 10 largemouth bass, plus single brown trout and black bullhead and 2 kokanee.
- Catch fell with distance from the dam.
- All methods together produced 528 rainbow trout (11.3 in, .51 lb, C = 36).
- In October 1967 ripe trout (48 seined, about 13 in, .6 lb, C = 28) were found massed below the diversion tunnels, apparently attempting to spawn. One kokanee was also seined.
- Angling by project staff took 14 rainbow trout (10.9 in, .43 lb, C = 33).

**Diet**
- Zooplankton and algae were the most frequent items in trout stomachs (each roughly 36% of occurrences). Insects, snails and fish were minor.

**Species composition**
- 15 species were collected in the tailwater. Flannelmouth sucker, rainbow trout and channel catfish were most common. The list included native [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] (rare), [[Bonytail]], [[Bluehead Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]. Introduced species included [[Brown Trout]], [[Kokanee]], [[Largemouth Bass]], black crappie, walleye, carp and green sunfish.

**Transplants**
- Snail (*Physa*) transplants are the only clearly visible success to date.
- Crayfish (*Orconectes* sp.) transplants of about 500 (July) and 2,500 (August) from Springerville, Arizona, have shown little result: one crayfish in a gill net and a few diver sightings.
- 20,000 snails from Nelson Reservoir were also planted in the tailwater.

**River trip (April 1968)**
- Gill nets (1,400 ft overnight) caught only two flannelmouth suckers, one channel catfish and one carp. Cove renovation at [[House Rock Wash]] yielded no fish.
- Angling at Lee's Ferry took rainbow trout.
- Side streams were sampled with the shocker.
  - [[Bright Angel Creek]]: only bluehead suckers and speckled dace, with no trout, perhaps because of earlier flood damage.
  - [[Shinumo Creek]]: one 18.7-in rainbow, bluehead suckers and dace.
  - [[Tapeats Creek]] and [[Deer Creek]]: rainbow trout fingerlings and speckled dace.
  - [[Havasu Creek]]: no fish.
  - Spencer Creek: red shiners and speckled dace.

**Recommendations**
1. Continue the creel census.
2. Make more bottom-fauna transplants, with the greatest emphasis on snails.
3. Continue large (5–6 in) trout plants, with catchable-size plants during peak pressure.
4. Evaluate planting a second game fish such as cutthroat or brown trout.
5. Begin an intensified age-and-growth study of rainbow trout.
