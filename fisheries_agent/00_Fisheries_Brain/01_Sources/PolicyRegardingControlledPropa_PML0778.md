# Final Policy on Controlled Propagation of Species Listed Under the Endangered Species Act

#ichthyology #marine-biology #listed-species

## Executive Summary

This is a Federal Register notice (Vol. 65, No. 183, September 20, 2000, pp. 56917–56921) announcing the final joint policy of the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NMFS) on [[Controlled Propagation]] of species listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) of 1973. The policy gives guidance on using captive breeding and rearing as one component of a listed species' recovery strategy. It takes effect October 20, 2000. The notice also contains a Paperwork Reduction Act request for the HUD 2020 Partners survey, and a Bureau of Land Management direct-sale notice for public land in New Mexico. The document does not study any fish population, habitat, or water chemistry. Its aquatic relevance is limited to the joint FWS/NMFS mandate and a single passing mention of [[Pacific Salmon]].

## Background & Description

- **Legal basis:** The ESA charges the Services with identifying, protecting, managing, and recovering endangered and threatened plants and animals. Its definition of "species" (section 3(15)) includes subspecies, and any distinct population segment of vertebrate fish or wildlife that interbreeds when mature.
- **Purpose:** The policy promotes consistency across propagation, reintroduction, and monitoring, and aims to use funds efficiently.
- **Permitted uses:** Propagation is supported when recommended in an approved [[Recovery Plan]] or when needed to prevent extinction. Appropriate uses include recovery-related research, maintaining [[Refugia Population]]s, supplying animals or plants for [[Reintroduction]] or [[Population Augmentation]], and conserving species at imminent risk of extinction or extirpation.
- **Development history:** A draft policy was published February 7, 1996 (61 FR 4776). Comments were received from 47 [[American Zoo and Aquarium Association]] (AZA) members, 24 zoos/aquariums/botanical facilities, 6 private individuals, 3 academic institutions, and 12 State resource agencies. The scanned text is partly illegible, so these counts should be checked against the original. Most comments supported the policy.
- **Signatories:** Jamie Rappaport Clark, Director, FWS (signed August 4, 2000), and Penelope D. Dalton, Assistant Administrator for Fisheries, NMFS (dated August 18, 2000).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Genetic integrity:** The policy stresses maintaining genetic adaptability within each species. Its guidance covers the risks of [[Genetic Swamping]], intercrossing between distinct lineages, and the use of surrogates. It also addresses [[Genetic Management]] documents, which are recommended where information allows.
- **Release criteria:** Propagated individuals must be behaviorally and physiologically suited to the release site. Suitability considers habitat similarity, genetic similarity, phenotypic characteristics, life histories, and habitat use.
- **Release locations:** Releases typically occur on Federal or State lands. On private land, the private party works with the Services as a partner.
- **Ecological risks:** The notice lists risks from predation, competition, disease, and the loss of behaviors such as migration. It also lists environmental hazards such as storms, fires, and flooding, and the effect of removing individuals from wild populations.
- **Pacific salmon:** A fragment of the text refers to the recovery of listed [[Pacific Salmon]] using propagation, with a note that the Services will consider these activities case by case.

## Key Findings

1. **Scope and exemptions:**
   - The policy applies to listed U.S. species with recovery plans. Foreign listed species held for conservation in the United States are exempt.
   - Research described in recovery plans is not covered unless its offspring are intended for release.
   - Existing permit holders are exempt for the duration of their permit, unless the Regional Director or Assistant Administrator decides otherwise.
   - Research on species with short lifespans that requires a captive population not meant for release is also exempt. The scanned text gives this range as "7 to 2 years," which is probably a garbled "2 to 7 years."

2. **Changes after public comment:**
   - Genetic and reintroduction guidance documents are now recommended, not required, where biological knowledge is limited.
   - Reporting requirements were reduced, but the Services still need to track species in propagation programs and efforts to secure habitat.
   - The requirement that propagation appear in an approved final recovery plan was kept. Where propagation is later found necessary, the recovery plan is to be revised.

3. **Management requirements:**
   - Within 6 months of the policy's effective date, FWS Regional Directors must identify all listed species held in captivity, in pre-propagation research, or in propagation programs.
   - Annual reports based on fiscal years are due to the Director by the 31st of each year (the month is unclear in the scanned text). Reports cover priority numbers, status, cooperators, expenditures, and obstacles.
   - Surplus or non-releasable individuals may be used for research, as broodstock, or for other purposes.

4. **Permitting and paperwork:**
   - FWS permit forms are #3-200-55 (interstate commerce and recovery) and #3-200-56 (incidental take). FWS estimates 2 hours per application. NMFS estimates 80 hours.
   - Information collection is approved under OMB numbers 1018-0094 (FWS) and 0648-0230 (NMFS). The NMFS 0648-0402 number also appears in the text.
   - Confidential business information must be marked "Business Confidential."

5. **Compliance and environmental review:**
   - The Department of the Interior found the policy categorically excluded under NEPA, and NMFS concurred. No further NEPA analysis is required.
   - The policy is not a significant regulatory action under Executive Order 12866, and its economic effect is below $100 million per year.
   - The policy has no takings, unfunded mandate, or federalism implications.

6. **Tribal relations:** No federally propagated listed species have yet been reintroduced. The Services recognize the potential for such actions and will relate to federally recognized tribes on a government-to-government basis.

7. **Unrelated notices in the same issue:**
   - **HUD 2020 Partners:** This is a new information collection (no form numbers) to survey perceptions of HUD partner groups about HUD performance and 2020 management reforms. It is collected biannually, with an estimated burden of 605 hours. Comments were due October 20, 2000. The contact is Wayne Eddins.
   - **BLM direct sale:** The Bureau of Land Management found public land near Santa Fe, New Mexico, suitable for direct sale to Richard Montoya under FLPMA Section 203, at no less than estimated fair market value. The land was not to be offered until at least 60 days after the notice.
