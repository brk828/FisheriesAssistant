# Letter from Don Buth to W. L. Minckley Regarding Gila robusta Specimens

## Executive Summary
This document is a piece of scientific correspondence dated 3 August 1988 from Don Buth (UCLA) to W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) concerning voucher specimens labeled "Upper Basin [[Gila robusta]]." The letter addresses a taxonomic ambiguity between [[Gila robusta]] (Roundtail Chub) and [[Gila cypha]] (Humpback Chub), two closely related cyprinid species native to the Colorado River Basin. Buth processed previously dissected specimens (tissue-sampled for what appears to be allozyme or biochemical analysis) from two field-collection subsamples, fixed them in formalin, and raised the question of whether the specimens in hand truly represent *G. robusta* or are misidentified *G. cypha*. The letter also references a separate, ongoing manuscript review concerning [[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail Chub).

## Background & Description
Direct morphological description is minimal in this correspondence, as the specimens had already been dissected by a prior researcher prior to Buth's handling. Notable specimen-condition details include:

- **Dissection state**: Eyeballs removed and a muscle plug excised from each specimen — consistent with tissue sampling protocols for biochemical/genetic analysis (e.g., allozyme electrophoresis) rather than external morphological voucher study.
- **Specimen count**: Five specimens recovered from bag XXII; three specimens recovered from bag XXIII (total of eight specimens).
- **Preservation condition**: Specimens fixed in 10% formalin for over two weeks; one specimen in bag XXII remained a "floater," indicating incomplete fixation/buoyancy due to retained gas or incomplete formalin penetration.
- **Labeling**: Most specimens were tagged, and collection records indicated that lots XXII and XXIII represent subdivided samples from a single original field collection (i.e., XXII = XXIII, not independent collections).

The letter explicitly raises unresolved taxonomic uncertainty: whether the "Upper Basin Gila robusta" specimens are correctly identified as [[Gila robusta]] or are in fact [[Gila cypha]] — two morphologically similar and historically confused taxa within the genus [[Gila]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Specimens originate from the "Upper Basin," referring to the Upper Colorado River Basin, a freshwater riverine system historically inhabited by multiple endemic *Gila* species.
- **Sympatric taxa**: [[Gila robusta]] and [[Gila cypha]] are known to co-occur in Colorado River Basin habitats, contributing to field identification challenges — a central concern of this letter.
- **Conservation context**: Both [[Gila cypha]] (Humpback Chub) and [[Gila elegans]] (Bonytail Chub) are federally listed as endangered species native to the Colorado River system, underscoring the importance of accurate taxonomic identification for conservation-related voucher and genetic studies.

## Key Findings
- Eight dissected *Gila* specimens (five from lot XXII, three from lot XXIII) were recovered, fixed in 10% formalin, and prepared as vouchers to be retained at Arizona State University (ASU) for the "Upper Basin" locality.
- Collection records confirm lots XXII and XXIII are subsamples of a single original field collection, not independent samples.
- A central unresolved scientific question is raised: whether these voucher specimens are genuinely [[Gila robusta]] or are misidentified [[Gila cypha]], reflecting known taxonomic and field-identification difficulties between these two Colorado River Basin cyprinids.
- Buth proposes a contingency plan: depending on the resolved identification, he intends to use a sample of "the other" species (i.e., whichever species the specimens are not) for comparative purposes — implying ongoing biochemical/genetic comparative work between *G. robusta* and *G. cypha*.
- The correspondence also references a concurrent, separate manuscript review process regarding [[Gila elegans]], indicating that Buth and Minckley were collaborating across multiple *Gila* taxonomic and systematic projects during this period.
- The letter provides logistical/methodological context typical of museum voucher-specimen exchange and tissue-sampling workflows in ichthyological systematics research of the late 1980s, including formalin fixation protocols and coordination of frozen tissue shipments.
