# Faunal Resource Use in the Prehistoric Tonto Basin — Fish Remains

#ichthyology #archaeozoology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This chapter, drawn from zooarchaeological analysis of over 47,000 faunal bones recovered from more than 100 prehistoric habitation, ceremonial, and midden sites in the [[Tonto Basin]] (Arizona), documents a small but ecologically significant assemblage of fish remains. The fish component (Table 5.1) is dominated by native Southwestern cyprinids (minnows) and catostomids (suckers), reflecting exploitation of the Salt River/Tonto Creek drainage system by prehistoric inhabitants. While [[Lagomorpha|rabbits]] dominate the overall identifiable faunal assemblage, the fish taxa recovered provide a valuable proxy record of the historic ichthyofauna of the Tonto Basin prior to modern hydrological modification (e.g., construction of [[Roosevelt Dam]]) and the subsequent decline of native fish populations in the region.

## Background & Description

The assemblage was identified primarily via skeletal (osteological) elements, following a taxonomic hierarchy from class (Osteichthyes) down to genus and species level where diagnostic elements permitted. Identified taxa include:

- **Fish, general (Osteichthyes)** — unidentifiable to lower taxonomic level, likely representing fragmentary or diagnostic-poor skeletal elements.
- **Minnows ([[Cyprinidae]])** — family-level identification.
  - **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila* cf. *G. elegans*) — a large-bodied, streamlined cyprinid adapted to swift, turbid mainstem river conditions; now critically endangered/extirpated from much of its historic range.
  - **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robustus*) — a moderately sized chub adapted to a range of stream and pool habitats within the Gila River basin.
  - **Chubs** (*Gila* sp.) — genus-level identifications where species-diagnostic elements were absent.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Catostomidae) — a large sucker distinguished by a pronounced dorsal keel ("razorback"); a large-river specialist.
- **[[Gila Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus* cf. *P. clarki*) — a mountain-sucker relative adapted to swifter tributary stream reaches.
- **Gila Coarse-Scaled Sucker** (*Catostomus insignis*) — a sucker species with distinctly larger body scales relative to congeners.

Faunal elements were tabulated as identifiable when non-mammal remains could be assigned to at least class level (fish = Osteichthyes) or lower; partial/complete individuals were each counted as a single occurrence (NISP-style minimum counting).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All identified fish taxa are native components of the historic aquatic fauna of the **Gila River drainage** (specifically the Salt River and Tonto Creek systems feeding into what is now [[Roosevelt Lake]]), an inland freshwater system tributary to the Colorado River basin. Taxonomically, the assemblage spans two major native Southwestern freshwater fish families:

- **[[Cyprinidae]] (minnows/chubs)** — *Gila* spp. occupy a range of habitats from swift mainstem currents (*G. elegans*) to pools and eddies (*G. robustus*) within perennial desert streams and rivers.
- **[[Catostomidae]] (suckers)** — *Xyrauchen texanus*, *Pantosteus clarki*, and *Catostomus insignis* are benthic-foraging fish adapted to different flow regimes, from large-river habitats (*Xyrauchen*) to smaller tributary streams (*Pantosteus*).

These species collectively indicate that prehistoric inhabitants of the Tonto Basin had access to and exploited perennial riverine habitats characterized by a mix of swift-water and pool/tributary conditions, consistent with the pre-dam hydrology of the Salt River and Tonto Creek. The presence of these taxa in archaeological midden and habitation contexts suggests fish were a component—if a minor one relative to [[Lagomorpha|rabbits]] and other terrestrial taxa—of prehistoric subsistence strategies in this desert riverine environment.

## Key Findings

- Fish remains represent one of several nonmammalian faunal classes recovered (alongside amphibians, reptiles, and birds), but are numerically minor compared to dominant terrestrial taxa such as rabbits.
- The fish assemblage is taxonomically restricted to native Gila River basin fauna: minnows (Cyprinidae) and suckers (Catostomidae), with no evidence of nonnative or introduced species — consistent with a pre-Columbian/prehistoric chronological context.
- Several identified taxa, notably the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], are now federally listed as endangered and are extinct or nearly extirpated from the Salt River/Tonto Basin system, making this assemblage a valuable historical ecological baseline documenting former native fish distributions.
- Identification resolution varied, with some remains identifiable only to family (Cyprinidae) or genus (*Gila* sp.) level, likely due to fragmentary preservation typical of small vertebrate skeletal elements in archaeological contexts.
- The data derive from multiple archaeological projects spanning two decades of fieldwork (Ash Creek project, FLEX Tonto Basin project, Roosevelt Rural Sites Study, Roosevelt Platform Mound Study, Roosevelt Community Development Study, and the Rye Creek project), providing a broad spatial sampling across the upper and lower Tonto Basin, including areas now inundated by [[Roosevelt Lake]].
- The mixed presence of large-river specialists (*Xyrauchen texanus*) alongside tributary-adapted species (*Pantosteus clarki*) suggests prehistoric fishing exploited a range of aquatic microhabitats across the basin's drainage network.
