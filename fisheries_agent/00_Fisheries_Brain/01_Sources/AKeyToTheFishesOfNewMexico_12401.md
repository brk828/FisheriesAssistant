# Dichotomous Key to Freshwater Fishes of North America

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #dichotomous-key

## Executive Summary

This document is a multi-level identification key to freshwater fishes, most likely of the southwestern and central United States, judging by taxon names such as [[Gila Trout]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Pecos Shiner]]. It begins with a family-level key, then gives a series of species-level keys for the most diverse families, and closes with a set of short diagnostic accounts for each family. The text is OCR-scanned and includes some layout debris, which has been cleaned up here. The document does not present original research, sampling data or site information.

## Background & Description

The key uses paired, numbered couplets (for example 1a/1b, 2a/2b). Each couplet offers two contrasting character states, and the reader follows the one that matches the specimen until a name is reached. The characters are mostly morphological and meristic:

- **External form:** body shape, presence or absence of scales, and whether the head is scaled.
- **Fin structure:** pelvic fin position (thoracic or abdominal), spine presence, number of dorsal, anal and pelvic rays, and whether the dorsal spine is serrated.
- **Mouth and jaw:** upper jaw length, barbels, whether the premaxillary is [[Protractile Premaxillary]], and lip shape (plicate or papillose).
- **Pharyngeal teeth:** number of rows and teeth per row, a standard feature of [[Cyprinidae]] identification.
- **Meristic counts:** [[Lateral Line Scale Count]], gill raker counts, scale rows, and branchiostegal rays.
- **Internal features:** air bladder chambers, intestinal length and coiling, and peritoneum colour.

Footnotes define how fin rays are counted. The first ray counted is the first full-length unbranched ray, and the last ray, which is often split, is counted as one.

## Key Findings

**1. Family-level key (couplets 1–18).** The top-level key separates families using pelvic fin position and spine presence, scale cover, premaxillary protractility, and fin shape. It leads to 18 family groupings. Among them, [[Anguillidae]] is identified by its eel-shaped body with no pelvic fins, and [[Poeciliidae]] by a modified anal fin (gonopodium) in males together with live-bearing.

**2. Families with detailed keys.** Species-level keys are provided for:
- [[Salmonidae]]: trout and char, including [[Salvelinus fontinalis]] (Eastern brook trout), [[Salvelinus malma]] (Dolly Varden), [[Salmo trutta]] (brown trout), [[Salmo gairdneri]] (rainbow trout), [[Salmo gilae]] (Gila trout), [[Salmo clarki]] (cutthroat trout) and [[Salmo aguabonita]] (golden trout).
- [[Cyprinidae]]: the largest key, covering about 50 species across roughly 13 genera, including [[Cyprinus carpio]] (carp), [[Carassius auratus]] (goldfish), [[Campostoma anomalum]] (stoneroller) and several [[Gila]] species.
- [[Catostomidae]]: suckers and buffalo, including [[Ictiobus bubalus]] (smallmouth buffalo) and [[Ictiobus niger]] (black buffalo).
- [[Ictaluridae]]: catfish, including [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish) and [[Pylodictis olivaris]] (flathead catfish).
- [[Cyprinodontidae]] and [[Poeciliidae]]: killifish, gambusia and topminnows.
- [[Centrarchidae]]: sunfish and black bass, including [[Micropterus salmoides]] (largemouth bass) and [[Micropterus dolomieu]] (smallmouth bass).
- [[Percidae]]: perch, walleye and darters.

**3. Diagnostic counts that separate close relatives.** The document gives explicit thresholds, for example:
- Gizzard shad (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) has 52–83 transverse scale series and 25–36 anal rays. Threadfin shad (*Dorosoma petenense*) has 40–43 scale series and 20–23 anal rays.
- Gila trout (*Salmo gilae*) has 126–151 (average about 140) transverse scale series, while most other trout have more than 150 or fewer than 150, depending on the group.
- Rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdneri*) can be recognised by fewer than 150 scale series and no teeth on the basibranchial.
- Channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*) has 25–29 anal fin rays and a more deeply forked caudal fin than [[Headwater Channel Catfish]] (22–25 rays).
