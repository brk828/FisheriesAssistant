# What Becomes of the PhDs in Fisheries?

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-personnel

## Executive Summary

Kenneth D. Carlander of the [[Department of Zoology and Entomology]] at Iowa State College surveyed the careers of 343 people who filed doctoral dissertations in fisheries and allied subjects between 1934 and 1949, using the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] report of those dissertations as the starting list. Of the 343, 114 were in fisheries research or teaching when the survey was made. Slightly over half (54 percent) were teaching in colleges, and 37 were training future fisheries biologists. The study was motivated by the need to find qualified fisheries personnel and to guide the training of future doctoral candidates.

## Background & Description

The source is a reprint from the *Transactions of the [[American Fisheries Society]]* (Volume 81, 1951). It is a career-tracking survey rather than a field or laboratory study.

- **Population:** 343 PhD recipients in fisheries and allied subjects, 1934–1949.
- **Method:** Membership lists of scientific societies and several personnel directories were searched to find present positions. The dissertations were grouped by subject (Management, Limnology, Taxonomy, Ecology, Crustacea, Physiology, Pollution, Oceanography, Miscellaneous). Classes with similar position distributions and closely related subjects were combined to shorten Table 1.
- **Caveat:** Many dissertations were not directly fisheries-oriented, and many candidates probably never planned to enter the field.
- **Non-locatable candidates:** The author could not find current addresses for 48 candidates (14 percent). Nine were women, who may have changed names or fields, and three were Oriental students who had probably returned home. The author judged that few of the 48 were likely in fisheries.
- **Society membership:** 85 of the 343 belonged to the [[American Fisheries Society]], 122 to the [[American Society of Limnology and Oceanography]], and 64 to the [[American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists]]. Among the 114 in fisheries work, the counts were 75 (AFS), 83 (ASLO) and 55 (ASIH).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address any particular fish species, habitat, water body or ocean basin. Its context is institutional and professional: where fisheries-trained doctorates work and how their training relates to the jobs they hold.

Fisheries research jobs were classified by employer:

- **Academic agencies:** organizations not directly concerned with management, such as the [[Marine Biological Laboratory]], [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]], the American Museum, the Illinois Natural History Survey, and some aquaria. These held 25 positions.
- **Canadian agencies:** the [[Fisheries Research Board of Canada]] and the Canadian Wildlife Service. These held 13 positions.
- **U.S. federal agencies:** the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], Public Health Service laboratories working on fisheries problems, and one executive of the [[Sport Fishing Institute]]. These held 24 positions.
- **State agencies:** 15 positions.
- **Fisheries teaching:** courses in fishery biology and closely allied fields (limnology, oceanography, aquatic entomology) at colleges that train fisheries biologists. These held 37 positions.

## Key Findings

1. **Overall outcome:** 114 of 343 (about 33 percent) were in fisheries research or teaching at the time of the survey.
2. **Teaching dominates:** 54 percent of all PhD recipients were teaching in colleges. About half of these were in zoology or biology departments with little link to fisheries research. Others were in medical colleges (24), botany, geology, engineering, and psychology.
3. **Other biological fields:** 19 candidates worked in areas such as insecticide work, public health, biological supply and soil conservation.
4. **Diverse exits:** some had moved far from their thesis topics into housewifery, testing bureaus, airlines and the armed services.
5. **Subject-area retention varied:**
   - Management: 78 percent in fisheries positions
   - Taxonomy: 64 percent
   - Ecology: 44 percent
   - Limnology: 31 percent
   
   Together, these four classes make up 52 percent of the theses but supply 76 percent of the people who entered fisheries employment.
6. **Professional affiliation:** AFS membership was the lowest of the three societies in the full group (85), but among fisheries workers, ASLO (83) and AFS (75) were close, with ASIH at 55.
7. **Stated implication:** the author argues that the answer to "what becomes of these PhDs" bears on recruiting qualified fisheries personnel and on designing doctoral training, since most graduates with fisheries dissertations were not working in fisheries.
