# Gavins Point National Fish Hatchery: 1994 Station Profile

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This is a partly illegible 1994 station profile for the [[Gavins Point National Fish Hatchery]], a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) fisheries facility near [[Yankton]], [[South Dakota]]. The hatchery was established in 1956. Its stated mission is to produce and distribute fish for stocking, with a focus on the [[Missouri River]] system. The profile lists facilities, staffing, fiscal year 1994 distribution by state and species, and fish numbers by water category. Many figures, including most of the funding table and the species codes, are unreadable in the scan, so the quantitative content is limited.

## Background & Description

- **Operator:** USFWS Fisheries. The profile is dated 1994 (revised 12/08/93), with a 10/08/88 header date.
- **Project leader:** Herb Bollig, listed with a Yankton, South Dakota, rural route address and phone number.
- **Established:** 1956.
- **Congressional context:** Two U.S. senators and one U.S. representative are named, along with the congressional district (12). The names are partly illegible.
- **Authority:** Enabling legislation cited as 52 Stat. 262 and 70 Stat. (the volume and page numbers are partly corrupted in the scan).
- **Mission (as legible):** To produce and distribute fish for stocking. The hatchery has developed techniques for "species of concern" in the Missouri River system. The rest of the mission statement is illegible.
- **Physical facilities (as legible):**
  - Rearing ponds, including one with a stated area of about 3 acres (the figure is garbled).
  - A hatchery building with a tank production building.
  - Brick and steel-sided storage structures.
  - A pump house.
  - Aquaria and related facilities.
- **Staffing:** Full-time equivalent (FTE) positions are listed, with a value of about 2.0 for one year. Most year-by-year columns for 1991 to 1995 (actual and estimated) are illegible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery operates within the [[Missouri River]] basin and supports stocking of native and sport fish species. The profile reports distribution to a wide range of states and waters, which is consistent with a regional restoration and stocking role. The profile does not address habitat conditions, water chemistry, plankton, invertebrates, or population status. Its environmental content is limited to the hatchery's operating setting.

**Distribution (fiscal year 1994):**
- **States:** Approximately 14 U.S. states are listed (the scanned abbreviations are partly corrupted). Legible entries include MT, ND, NE, SD, UT, WY, TX, WA, WI, WV, and PA.
- **Species:** About 15 species are coded with abbreviations (for example, WHC, SLC, CCF, SN, CLS, PH, GMB, SHB, WAS, SEH, MUH, PAR). The codes are not expanded in the document, and several are unreadable. Scientific and common names therefore cannot be confirmed from this source. The codes may correspond to [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], and similar taxa, but this is not stated in the document.

## Key Findings

1. **Hatchery role:** The [[Gavins Point National Fish Hatchery]] is a federal rearing and distribution facility that has operated since 1956 and produces fish for stocking in the Missouri River system and beyond.
2. **Species of concern:** The hatchery has developed rearing techniques for species of concern, although the specific species are not legible.
3. **Wide distribution:** Fiscal year 1994 stocking reached roughly 14 states and about 15 coded species.
4. **Distribution by water type:** The table separates federal, state, private, and other waters. Legible values include 131,803; 131,122; 495,625; and 91,016 fish. The row labels and total column are corrupted, so these values cannot be reliably assigned to specific categories or totals.
5. **Limited legibility:** Funding (operations, maintenance, cost recoverables) for 1991 to 1995 and most species codes are unreadable. Any budget or species-level conclusions require the original document.
