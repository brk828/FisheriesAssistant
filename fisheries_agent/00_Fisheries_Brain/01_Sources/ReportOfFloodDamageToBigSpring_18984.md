# Report of Flood Damage to Big Spring Gila Topminnow Population

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents severe flood damage to the [[Big Spring]] [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction site in [[Graham County]], Arizona, following an intense localized storm on August 21, 1990. The flood destroyed critical pool habitat, reduced the observable topminnow population from abundant to only 8 individuals, and damaged infrastructure including the dam, exclosure fence, and cattle watering system. The site, established in 1985 with [[Monkey Springs]] stock, was one of 5 reintroduction sites within the [[Safford District]]. The report evaluates restoration options versus site abandonment given the drainage's unstable nature.

## Background & Description

[[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from [[Monkey Springs]] stock were introduced to [[Big Spring]] in 1985 as part of recovery efforts. The site was engineered with a dam built in 1984 to create pool habitat using spring flow. Maximum pool dimensions were approximately 30 feet long, 8 feet wide, and 6 feet deep. 

The site provided three main pool areas: a large plunge pool at the spring source, a second pool approximately 150 feet downstream created by the dam, and a third pool in the plunge area below the dam. A 5.3-acre exclosure was constructed in 1980 to protect the developing riparian community. Native trees and plants were established, resulting in an extensive riparian community. The engineered dam replaced a previous masonry dam that had failed from flooding in 1982.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Big Spring]] is located in [[Graham County]], Arizona (T 6 S., R 25 E., NE1/4 of the SE1/4 Sec. 5) and represents one of 5 reintroduction sites within the [[Safford District]] managed by the [[Bureau of Land Management]]. The site had successfully withstood floods for 5 years prior to the August 1990 event, though gradual filling of the pool behind the dam had been occurring.

The August 21, 1990 storm was intense and localized, hitting the [[Gila Mountains]] and causing severe flooding throughout the region. The storm trapped 2 people in [[Bonita Creek]], damaged crops, flooded homes, and caused one fatality near [[Safford]]. No damage was observed at [[Big Spring]] during a visit on July 24, 1990, but inspection on August 24, 1990 revealed extensive flood damage.

## Key Findings

**Flood Damage Assessment:**
- Large boulders used for the dam apron were moved downstream
- Eastern abutment of the dam was eroded
- Trees were knocked over or ripped out
- Most emergent vegetation was removed
- Exclosure fence was blown open
- Cattle watering system was damaged
- Two of three pools that held abundant topminnow were filled with coarse sediment

**Population Impact:**
- Only 8 [[Gila Topminnow]] could be located following the flood
- Surviving fish remained in a small pool with tea-colored water adjacent to the downstream side of the dam
- The headwater pool remained intact due to an overhanging ledge at its upstream end that caused water to scour the pool with plunge action

**Restoration Options:**
- Water still flows from the spring
- The large pool at the head of the spring could support topminnow again
- The dam remains stable but requires repair to one abutment
- Sediment behind the dam could be dredged with a backhoe
- Fish could be replaced with [[Bylas Spring]] strain topminnows if desired
- Alternative option is to abandon the site for topminnow recovery given the unstable nature of the drainage
- The site will be restored for other wildlife species regardless of the decision on topminnow reintroduction
