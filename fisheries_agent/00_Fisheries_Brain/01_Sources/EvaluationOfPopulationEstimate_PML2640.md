# Evaluation of Population Estimates for Colorado Pikeminnow and Humpback Chub in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report from the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] (UCRRP) synthesizes two workshops (2001 and 2004) and an ad hoc committee review of population estimates for the federally endangered [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service needs reliable, precise estimates to judge downlisting and delisting under 2002 recovery goals. Estimates use [[Mark-Recapture]] models across eight upper-basin regions. Precision targets (capture probability P-hat > 0.10; coefficient of variation CV < 0.15) are not consistently met. Flow variation during ongoing drought reduces capture efficiency and precision. The report states that its estimates are preliminary and not final until evaluated by the Service.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Colorado pikeminnow (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and humpback chub (*Gila cypha*), both endangered big-river fishes. Razorback sucker and bonytail were not numerous enough in the wild for estimates.
- **Regulatory context:** [[Recovery Goals]] approved August 1, 2002, set demographic criteria: number of populations, trends in annual point estimates, mean annual recruitment, and core populations meeting minimum viable population size.
- **Coordination:** Region 6 of the Service leads recovery. The UCRRP coordinates; state and federal agencies collect and analyze data, often with university help.
- **Workshops:** Workshop I (Fort Collins, December 6-7, 2001) and Workshop II (Grand Junction, August 24-25, 2004) drew 57 attendees from five basin states, four federal agencies, two national environmental organizations, two universities and four private entities. A nine-member [[Population Estimates Ad Hoc Committee]] answered 11 evaluation questions.
- **Sampling history:** Long-term work began with the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP) in 1986. It produced relative abundance indices but not absolute population size. Basin-wide mark-recapture estimates followed, starting with Colorado pikeminnow in the Upper Colorado River in 1991.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Environmental drivers:** Flow strongly affects capture efficiency and precision. Drought in the Colorado River Basin lowered summer base flows. Low flows reduced backwater trammel-netting success. Nonnative fish, including [[Smallmouth Bass]], are a recognized predator and may increase during drought.
- **Habitat and boundaries:** Whitewater rapids restrict sampling boundaries. Fish movement across those boundaries between passes causes marked-fish losses and emigration, which may bias humpback chub estimates.
- **Fire and sediment:** The Rattle Complex fire (July 2002) delivered heavy ash into the river. A reported fish kill followed in September 2002 in Desolation/Gray Canyons.
- **Recruitment linkage:** Recruit-size Colorado pikeminnow (400-449 mm TL) may be 5-7 years old. Their abundance appears linked to strong or weak age-0 year-classes in the middle and lower Green River, which relate to nursery backwater conditions.

## Key Findings

### Methods and Precision
- **Estimator types:** Closed models (Lincoln-Petersen, Otis et al. Mo/Mt/Chao Mh) estimate N-hat and P-hat. Open models (Jolly-Seber, Pradel's lambda) estimate survival and recruitment. The [[Huggins Robust Design]] was used in the Green River.
- **Recommended assumptions:** Closed geographic boundaries, equal sampling effort across years, zero tag loss, and no differential mortality of marked fish.
- **Precision guidelines:** P-hat > 0.10 and CV < 0.15 (acceptable range 0.10-0.20) are targets, not requirements. Large populations and higher P-hat give more precise estimates.
- **Sampling schedule:** Colorado pikeminnow estimates use 3 consecutive years on and 2 off. Initial humpback chub estimates use 3 years on and 2 off, followed by 2 and 2.

### Colorado Pikeminnow
- **Green River Subbasin (2000-2003):** Five reaches covering 817 river km (508 miles). Total sampling was 3,723 electrofishing hours, with 3,108 juvenile and adult fish captured. Basin-wide abundance fell from 3,303 (2001) to 2,142 (2003), about a 35% decline. Adult survival was 0.65 (95% CI 0.58-0.70), lower than 0.82 (0.709-0.891) from ISMP data for 1991-1999. Mean recruit percentages (3.3% in 2000-2003, versus 24.7% for 1991-1999) were consistently below adult mortality, so recruitment failed to offset losses.
- **Population trend:** Pradel's lambda was greater than 1 from 1992 to 2000 (except 1997). It was below 1 from 2001 to 2003, indicating decline.
- **Upper Colorado River Subbasin (1991-2004):** Adult estimates averaged 372 (1991-1994) and 534 (1998-2000). The difference was not statistically significant. Precision was low in 2003 because of low water. For 2003, the report estimated 754 adults and 203 recruit-size fish. For 2004, it estimated 491 adults and 110 recruits.
- **San Juan River:** Only 15 adults were captured in 1991-1995 (Schnabel estimate 19, 95% CI 10-42). Fewer than 40 adults were estimated in October 1995. Hatchery augmentation totaled about 1.22 million fish across 1996-2003. Formal estimates should begin when stocked fish reach age 5 (around 2006).

### Humpback Chub
- **Black Rocks:** Estimates were 948 (1998), 921 (1999), 539 (2000) and 478 (2003). Catch fell sharply in 2000 and 2003. Adding a fourth pass in 1999 raised P-hat from 0.07 to 0.09 but was judged stressful to fish.
- **Westwater Canyon:** Estimates were 5,171 (1998), 2,261 (1999), 1,704 (2000) and 2,413 (2003). Confidence intervals are wide, and sampling covered about 15% of occupied habitat in 1998-2000 (about 20% in 2003). Recaptures were 54 in 1998 and 76 in 2000.
- **Desolation/Gray Canyons:** Estimates were 1,998 (2001), 2,193 (2002) and 945 (2003), a 53% decline from 2001 to 2003. Sampling covered about 7% of occupied habitat (6 of 84 miles) in 2001, rising to about 10%.
- **Yampa Canyon:** About 400 adults (N-hat 391, 95% CI 180-2,750) in 2000, using a likelihood model. Total of 609 individuals of all sizes captured in 1998-2004. Estimated density was less than 5 fish per river mile.
- **Cataract Canyon:** Sampling began in fall 2003 after record low flows delayed it from 2002. 32 unique adults and 3 recaptures gave an estimate of 150 (95% CI 71-407; P-hat 0.084; CV 0.50). Bonytail estimated at 66 hatchery-origin individuals.

### Ad Hoc Committee Consensus
- Study designs and field methods are generally appropriate, but precision targets may not be consistently achievable.
- Insufficient recruit-size fish are captured to estimate recruitment reliably, especially for humpback chub.
- Humpback chub samples do not adequately cover most populations in Westwater Canyon and Desolation/Gray Canyons.
- Committee members stress limiting over-handling. They recommend aerators, salt baths and shorter holding times.
- Members were uncertain whether a three-year decline reflects normal fluctuation or a long-term trend.

### Recommendations (Section 6.0, unprioritized)
1. Keep refining study designs and sampling techniques in all eight regions.
2. Use CAPTURE or MARK for analysis and justify model choice.
3. Quantify and correlate effects of flow, temperature and nonnative fish.
4. Use ISMP, larval drift and overwinter survival data to aid interpretation.
5. Apply robust-design analyses (as in [[Bestgen et al. 2005]]) where data allow.
6. Capture more recruit-size fish.
7. Minimize handling stress.
8. Continue ISMP monitoring and larval sampling.
9. Implement San Juan River estimates once hatchery fish reach about 5 years.
10. Improve precision through adaptive management.
