# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Desolation/Gray Canyon, Green River, Utah (FY 2001)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This FY 2001 annual project report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] describes the first of three planned years of sampling for an [[Endangered Species]] [[Humpback Chub]] population in Desolation/Gray Canyon on the [[Green River]], Utah. The work was run by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (Moab Field Station). Its two objectives were to estimate the population of juvenile and adult humpback chub and to test whether [[Long-Term Monitoring]] catch rates relate to population size. Three sampling trips were made in June and July 2001 across 12 sites. A total of 348 chub were captured, and the overall catch rate was 0.1241 fish/net hour. Catch rates fell significantly across the three trips. The report describes the data as raw and preliminary, so no population estimate was produced this year.

## Background & Description

The [[Recovery Program]] is setting recovery goals for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]]. Those goals depend partly on maintaining several populations, including the one in Desolation/Gray Canyon. Managing and monitoring that population requires accurate [[Population Estimate]]s.

The report refers to the fish as *Gila* spp. and treats all specimens as "chub." Field crews found that identifying [[Gila Species]] (humpback chub, [[Roundtail Chub]], and bonytail-type fish) was inconsistent across observers, so no species-level separation was attempted this year.

Study schedule: initial year 2001, final year 2003. The work is linked to the Recovery Program's General Support Action Plan item V.A.1, "Conduct standardized monitoring program."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling sites and river conditions**

- Twelve sites were sampled. Four are long-term trend sites at river miles (RM) 185, 174.4, 160.4 and 145.7. The other eight were RM 202, 182, 174.2, 166.8, 158.7, 155, 153.5 and 147.5. Some of the non-trend sites had been sampled earlier as "wild card" sites.
- Trips were timed to target flows below 8,000 cfs, when catch rates are higher. Discharge fell on each trip because the [[Hydrograph]] was on its descending limb:
  - Trip 1: 3,560–5,699 cfs (report also lists 5699–3560 cfs)
  - Trip 2: 2,220–2,624 cfs
  - Trip 3: 1,624–1,750 cfs
- Low flows on Trip 3 made boat handling in the channel difficult, and fewer nets were set.
- Main-channel water temperatures were 19–21 °C on Trips 1 and 3, and 25–28 °C on Trip 2.

**Sampling methods**

- [[Trammel Net]]s were the main gear, targeting adults. Past work found them the most productive method for adults. About seven nets were set per site, from late afternoon to midnight and again in the pre-dawn and morning hours. Nets were checked every two hours, and captured fish were held in a pen to avoid recapturing the same fish at a site. One night was spent at each of the 12 sites.
- [[Electrofishing]] was tried on Trip 2 to increase recaptures and to collect juveniles. It did not significantly increase recaptures, and few juveniles were caught.
- Captured fish were tagged with [[Passive Integrated Transponder]] (PIT) tags, and [[Mark-Recapture]] was used to track recaptures across years.

## Key Findings

**Catch and size results**

- Total catch: **348 chub** from net sets across the three trips.
- Overall catch rate: **0.1241 fish/net hour**.
- Trip-level results (from Table 1):

| Trip | Fish caught | Net hours | Catch rate (fish/net hour) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 208 | 961.94 | 0.2162 |
| 2 | 95 | 962.83 | 0.0986 |
| 3 | 45 | 877.77 | 0.0512 |

- The report lists the all-trip net hours as 1,462. That figure conflicts with the sum of the trip values (2,802.54 hours), and the reported overall catch rate of 0.1241 matches the sum, so 1,462 appears to be an error in the source.
- Average total length: **262.5 mm**, range **165–473 mm**.

**Recaptures**

- Twelve chub tagged on Trip 1 were recaptured on Trips 2 and 3.
- Long-term recaptures (fish PIT-tagged in earlier years): 17 on Trip 1, 4 on Trip 3. The Trip 2 figure is garbled in the scan.
- Some untagged fish were noticed during sampling. Tag loss reduced the number of long-term and annual recaptures.

**Trends**

- Catch rates **decreased significantly** over the three sampling trips.
- The report states that the results are preliminary. A population estimate will be attempted only after further verification and future sampling.

**Recommendations for FY 2002**

1. Start sampling earlier in the year to cover more of the hydrograph before base flows are reached.
2. Discontinue electrofishing. The added juvenile data did not justify the logistical problems and the potential effects on the fish. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Vernal) is running electrofishing surveys for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] in the same canyons, and that work may cover humpback chub monitoring in the project years.
3. Use [[Hoop Net]]s in FY 2002 to increase overall captures and target smaller chub.

**Project status and administration**

- Status: ongoing. The first of three project years is complete.
- FY 2001 budget: $67,600 budgeted, $67,600 spent or obligated, $0 difference. Work completed: 100%. Recovery Program funds spent on publication charges: $0.
- Data entry and transfer to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service were scheduled for January 15, 2002.
- The report is signed and dated 12/2001.
