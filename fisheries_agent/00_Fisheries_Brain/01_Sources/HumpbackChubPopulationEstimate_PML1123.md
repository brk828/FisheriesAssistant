# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Desolation/Gray Canyons, Green River, Utah (FY2002 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY2002 annual progress report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers the second year of a three-year project to estimate the population of [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Desolation Canyon]] and [[Gray Canyon]] on the [[Green River]] in Utah. The work was led by J. Michael Hudson and Julie A. Jackson of the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]], Moab Field Station. Recovery goals for the federally endangered humpback chub depend partly on maintaining populations in several locations, and this reach is one of them. The project has two objectives: to obtain a population estimate for late juvenile and adult humpback chub, and to determine whether a relationship exists between catch per unit effort (CPUE) and population size.

Two of three planned sampling trips were completed, and 259 humpback chub were collected. The third trip was cancelled because of low flows. The population estimate itself was not yet produced; the report presents preliminary raw data only.

## Background & Description

The humpback chub is an endangered freshwater fish of the Colorado River basin. The project's Recovery Program framework is the [[Green River Action Plan]] (Mainstem), under task V.B.1, which calls for population estimates in Desolation/Gray Canyons.

The study area is a canyon reach of the Green River. The field program used five capture methods:

- [[Trammel Netting]], the primary method for capturing adult-sized chub
- [[Electrofishing]], used to capture juveniles and to supplement netting
- [[Hoop Net]] sets and [[Minnow Trap]] sets, used to target smaller fish

The project is funded at $99,000 for FY2002.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow and timing.** Trips are normally scheduled to target flows below 8,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) to maximize catch rates. Flows during 2002 sampling ranged from 5,600 to 1,800 cfs. Flows dropped more rapidly than in 2001, so the 2002 trips were moved forward two weeks in an attempt to sample at higher water. Flows were monitored using [[USGS Gage 09315000]] (Green River at Green River gage).

- Trip 1 (June 1–8): 5,600–4,700 cfs
- Trip 2 (June 15–22): 3,170–1,810 cfs
- Cancelled Trip 3 (June 29–July 6): expected flows of about 1,250–1,040 cfs

The third trip was cancelled to avoid damage to equipment (boats and motors) and to reduce safety risks from low water in the canyons.

**Sampling sites.** Twelve sites were sampled. Four were long-term monitoring sites at river miles (RM) 185, 174.4, 160.4, and 145.7. The other eight were at RM 202, 192, 174.2, 166.9, 158.7, 155, 153.5, and 147.5. Four "wildcard" sites from previous monitoring were included.

**Seasonal timing.** The report recommends sampling in the fall of FY2003. This would avoid high summer water temperatures that tend to increase fish stress. It would also lower the likelihood of incidentally capturing [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], a potentially co-occurring endangered species.

## Key Findings

**Catch totals and effort**

- Trammel nets: 259 humpback chub collected in 1,598 net sets (2,007.88 net-hours). Overall CPUE was 0.127 fish/net-hour (0.106 in Trip 1; 0.147 in Trip 2). Trammel nets yielded the highest catch.
- Electrofishing: 42 humpback chub collected in 22.52 shock-hours. Overall CPUE was 2.44 fish/shock-hour (3.07 in Trip 1; 1.44 in Trip 2). Electrofishing captured about 13% of all humpbacks. The report notes that electrofishing CPUE remained similar between trips, though significance was not tested.
- Hoop nets and minnow traps: 9 humpback chub collected, 6 of them juveniles. Overall CPUE was about 0.006 fish/hour.

The report gives two different totals for the year. The summary cites 293 individual humpback chub, while the methods and table cite 259 collected during the two trips. The report does not reconcile these figures.

**Size structure**

- Overall mean total length of all humpback chub collected: 240.5 mm.
- Trammel-caught fish: mean 252 mm (range 109–409 mm).
- Electrofished fish (n = 42): mean 190 mm (range 82–359 mm).
- Hoop-net fish (n = 9): mean 151 mm (range 71–246 mm).
- Subadults (< 200 mm TL) made up 15% of the total catch. The subadult fraction was split roughly evenly between fish large enough to tag (> 150 mm TL) and smaller fish (< 150 mm TL), at about 7–8% each.

**Movement and recapture (mark-recapture)**

- Two fish were recaptured within the same trip, five days apart. One moved 10 miles upstream and the other moved 13 miles downstream.
- Six fish tagged during Trip 1 were recaptured on Trip 2. One moved 8 miles downstream, and five stayed within one mile of their original capture location.
- Eighteen long-term recaptures, fish tagged with [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging]] (PIT tags) in previous years, were captured in 2002.

**Methods used**

- Trammel nets: six to eight nets per site, fished from late afternoon to midnight and again before dawn to morning. Nets were checked every 1.5–2 hours, and captured fish were held in a pen to avoid recapturing them within a site.
- Electrofishing: one pass across the study area, with more intensive sampling at each of the twelve sites.
- Hoop nets and minnow traps: one or two hoop nets and one to four minnow traps per site, baited with cat food and set parallel to flow where possible.

**Status and budget**

- Project status: ongoing; second of three planned years.
- FY2002 budget: $99,000 budgeted, $79,256 expended/obligated, leaving $19,744 unspent (80% of work completed).
- Data will be entered and transferred to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service by January 15, 2003.

**Recommendations for FY2003**

- Sample in the fall to avoid high summer water temperatures and reduce the chance of capturing Colorado pikeminnow.
- Continue hoop net and minnow trap sampling to increase overall captures and target smaller chub.

**Interpretation note**

The report states that these results are preliminary raw data. Verification and analysis are still needed before a population estimate can be generated. The objective linking [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] to population size has therefore not yet been tested.
