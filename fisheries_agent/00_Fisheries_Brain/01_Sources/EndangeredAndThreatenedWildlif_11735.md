# Federal Register Notices: Alaska Title V Interim Approval and FY 1997 Endangered Species Listing Priorities

#conservation-policy #endangered-species #environmental-regulation

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register compilation contains three regulatory items. The first is the [[Environmental Protection Agency]]'s interim approval of the [[Alaska Department Of Environmental Conservation]]'s [[Title V Operating Permits]] program, effective December 5, 1996. The second and largest is the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]]'s final [[Listing Priority Guidance]] for fiscal year (FY) 1997 under section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The third is the Service's final decision to discontinue its "[[Category-2 Candidates]]" list. The document contains no field data, survey results or ecological measurements. Its content is administrative and policy-based. Fish species appear only in passing, in a list of candidate species.

## Background & Description

**Alaska Title V (40 CFR Part 70).** The [[Part 71 Federal Permitting Program]] took effect in Alaska on 1996 under 61 FR 34202. Sources were required to file federal applications and fees by January 31, 1997. Many sources had not filed, expecting the State program to be approved first. The EPA amended Appendix A to Part 70 to record the approval. The approval was submitted May 31, 1995, supplemented through October 17, 1996, and expires December 7, 1998.

**Endangered species listing.** The Service's listing priorities are set under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and the 1983 listing priority guidelines. These guidelines rank species by the immediacy and magnitude of threats, and also give monotypic genera, full species and subspecies descending priority. The 1983 guidelines do not rank different types of listing action against each other, so this notice fills that gap.

**Budget history.** Public Law 104-6 (April 1995) rescinded $1.5 million from listing activities and imposed a moratorium on final listings. Continuing resolutions kept that moratorium in place until April 26, 1996. The Service's listing program was effectively shut down during that period. The FY 1997 appropriation provides $5 million for listing, against a President's request of $7.483 million.

**Litigation.** The notice cites the [[Fund For Animals Settlement Agreement]] (D.D.C., Civ. No. 92-800). Full compliance would require the Service to address all 184 candidate species in FY 1997. The Service also cites *Sierra Club v. Babbitt* (E.D. Cal., July 23, 1996), in which the court deferred to the Service's biologically based priorities.

**Candidate lists.** The February 28, 1996 candidate notice (61 FR 7596) changed how candidates are identified. The notice also ends the [[Category-2 Candidates]] list. The Service's September 17, 1996 notice (61 FR 48875) opened public comment on that decision, with comments accepted through October 17, 1996.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses conservation status and threats only in general terms. It contains no population counts, habitat descriptions or water-quality measurements. The most relevant ecological items are:

- **Alabama sturgeon** ([[Scaphirhynchus Suttkusi]]) and the **short-tailed albatross** ([[Diomedea Albatrus]]) were added to the candidate list after the last candidate notice (footnote 2).
- The **Burton Springs salamander** proposed listing was withdrawn, and two plants (*Dudleya blochmaniae* ssp. *brevifolia* and *Corethrogyne filaginifolia* var. *incana*) had proposed listings dropped (footnote 1).
- The Service states that [[Critical Habitat]] designation "generally provides limited conservation benefits" beyond section 7 consultation and section 9 prohibitions. It notes that a single critical habitat designation could consume up to ten percent of the listing appropriation.
- The [[National Environmental Policy Act]] does not apply to this guidance. The Department of the Interior's categorical exclusion covers administrative policy.

## Key Findings

**Alaska Title V**
- Interim approval took effect December 5, 1996 and expires December 7, 1998.
- The 30-day delay under Alaska law means the program takes effect under both state and federal law on the same date. This addresses [[Administrative Procedure Act]] concerns.
- Sources that delayed renewal filings risked violating state law. Sources also risked dual federal and state filing requirements if approval was delayed.

**FY 1997 listing priority guidance**
- The Service adopts a four-tier system, applicable from December 5, 1996 to September 30, 1997:
  - **Tier 1:** Emergency listings for species facing significant risk to well-being.
  - **Tier 2:** Final decisions on pending proposed listings. Most of these face high-magnitude threats.
  - **Tier 3:** Resolving candidate status and processing 90-day or 12-month petition findings.
  - **Tier 4:** Critical habitat designations and delistings or reclassifications.
- Effective April 1, 1997, Tiers 1 to 3 become equal in priority under a "balanced listing program." Tier 4 remains lowest until other backlogs are cleared.
- The guidance is described as temporary, with a return to normal section 4 administration by the start of FY 1998.

**Backlog figures**
- Backlog of proposed listings at April 1996: 243 species, later reduced. Final rules were issued for 89 species after April 26, 1996, leaving 151 pending.
- Petition backlog at April 1996: 57 species requiring 90-day or 12-month findings.
- Candidate species: 184 total (85 under the settlement agreement, 99 not). Of the settlement species, 41 have listing priority 1 to 3. Of the non-settlement species, 64 have priority 1 to 3.
- Full settlement compliance would have pushed the proposed backlog to about 330 species.
- The Service expects pending proposed species to fall to 90–110 taxa by April 1, 1997.

**Regional allocation**
- The Service has seven Regional Offices. Funds are allocated mainly by the number of proposed and candidate species each Region leads.
- Region 1 (Pacific) has 111 proposed species and will focus mainly on Tier 2.
- Region 3 (Great Lakes) has two proposed species and may begin Tier 3 work sooner.

**Public comment**
- Four comment letters were received. Three opposed the proposed guidance.
- The Service responded to seven issues. Among them, it agreed to place delistings and reclassifications in the lowest tier, and it kept the withdrawal of proposed listings within Tier 2.
- The Service rejected the claim that critical habitat provides significant added protection, and it rejected the claim that litigation status should determine priority.

**Category-2 candidates**
- The Service discontinues the category-2 list. Future candidate lists will include only species with enough information to support a proposed rule.
- Other lists of "species of concern" will be used for land management and conservation planning, and the Service will continue working with state, tribal and federal partners.
