# Miller to Minckley Letter on Fish Classification and Genus Recognition (1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1993 letter from [[Robert R Miller]], Emeritus Curator of Fishes at the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, to Dr. W. C. Minckley of Arizona State University. It responds to Minckley's review of Volume 1 of the *Annotated List of Mexican Fishes*. Miller agrees with Minckley on the philosophy of classification, favoring smaller groupings ("splitters") over broad ones ("lumpers"). He outlines specific genus-level changes, mainly in the cichlids, and encloses a record for *Ictalurus pricei* from the [[Rio Sonora Basin]]. The letter is a preliminary reply, and Miller says he has not yet addressed all of Minckley's comments.

## Background & Description

The letter is correspondence, not a research report. It contains no sample sizes, field data or statistics. Its content is taxonomic opinion and planned nomenclatural changes:

- **Context:** A review of a regional checklist of Mexican fishes, which Miller describes as "a thick chunk to read."
- **Philosophy:** Miller and Minckley agree that smaller taxonomic groupings may better reflect biological diversity.
- **Enclosure:** One record for *Ictalurus pricei* in the Rio Sonora basin.
- **Author's status:** Miller signs as Emeritus Curator of Fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter does not describe habitat conditions, water chemistry, community composition, or threats. Its only geographic information concerns distribution:

- Several cichlid species are restricted to the Central American isthmus, with *Petenia* species other than *P. splendida* not occurring farther north than Panama.
- Three *Petenia*-related species (currently called *Caquetaia*) live in South America.
- The West Indian cichlid species are grouped together, with at least four living species plus one Miocene fossil.
- The Rio Sonora basin in Mexico is the locality for the enclosed *Ictalurus pricei* record.

Miller links these distributions to conservation. He argues that splitting taxa helps conservation biologists locate areas of higher species diversity.

## Key Findings

**Classification philosophy**
- Miller and Minckley agree that smaller groupings (splitters) may better reflect biological diversity than larger ones (lumpers).
- Miller notes a strong trend over the past ~25 years to elevate subspecies to full species rank. He argues this helps conservation planning.

**Planned genus-level changes**
- **[[Tiaroga]]:** Miller has returned to recognizing this genus, following Minckley's review.
- **[[Heterophallus]]:** To be recognized as a valid genus, not a synonym of [[Gambusia]].
- **[[Pseudoxiphophores]]:** To be recognized as a distinct assemblage, not part of the monotypic *[[Heterandria Formosa]]*.
- **Cichlid reorganization:** Miller is working toward monophyletic groupings, assisted by molecular data.
  - All West Indian species will be assigned to *[[Nandopsis]]*.
  - The [[Firemouth Cichlid]] group is a valid genus, *[[Thorichthys]]*.
  - The [[Convict Cichlid]] is placed in *[[Archocentrus]]*.
  - *[[Petenia]]* includes more than *P. splendida*. Other species are restricted to Panama and South America, where they are currently called *[[Caquetaia]]*.
  - *[[Herichthys]]* (excluding *pearsei* and *bocourti*) may be recognized. It contains more species than first thought, originally all identified as *Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum*, including *Herichthys geddesi* Regan.
  - Miller is considering recognizing *[[Pantosteus]]* again.

**Status and next steps**
- The letter is a preliminary response. Miller states he has not yet reviewed all of Minckley's comments and will reply again.
- Enclosed: one record for *[[Ictalurus Pricei]]* from the Rio Sonora basin.
