# Gila Topminnow Recovery Team Appointment Decision

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-topminnow

## Executive Summary

This April 2006 letter from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Albuquerque, New Mexico) responds to a request from [[Paul C. Marsh]], School of Life Sciences, Arizona State University, that a [[Recovery Team]] be appointed for the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]]. The Service declines to appoint a new team at this stage. The draft revised [[Recovery Plan]], begun under the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], is nearly complete for public review, and the Service judges that a new team would be a poor use of limited staff time and resources. Marsh is invited to comment during public review and to engage with the [[Native Fish Conservation Team]], which is chaired by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and coordinates implementation of recovery actions.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Official correspondence (reply letter), not a research study.
- **Correspondent:** Marsh wrote on February 15, 2006, expressing concern about the topminnow's status and asking that the draft revised recovery plan be completed using the best available science. He had also written on August 1, 2005, to the previous Regional Director, Dale Hall, requesting appointment to the team.
- **Species:** The [[Gila Topminnow]] is described only as an endangered species. The letter gives no biological description, population data, or distribution details.
- **Recovery planning status:** A draft revised recovery plan exists and was initiated under the oversight of the Desert Fishes Recovery Team.
- **Copies to:** The Recovery Coordinator (Ecological Services), Albuquerque, and the Supervisor, Ecological Services Field Office, Phoenix, Arizona (attention Sherry Barett).
- **Contact:** Elizabeth Oms, Deputy Assistant Regional Director for Ecological Services, at 505-248-6646.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter does not address habitat, water chemistry, population trends, threats, life history, or ecosystem relationships for the Gila topminnow. Its environmental relevance is limited to the policy framework for recovery: the species is listed as endangered, the recovery plan is in draft, and implementation is coordinated through the Native Fish Conservation Team, which is chaired by the Arizona Game and Fish Department. Any ecological detail would need to come from the draft recovery plan itself, which the letter does not reproduce.

## Key Findings

- **Decision:** The Service will not appoint a new Recovery Team at this stage.
- **Rationale:**
  - The draft revised recovery plan is "very close to completion" for public review.
  - Appointing a new team now is not considered "the best use of our limited staff time and resources."
- **Public comment:** Marsh is encouraged to submit comments once the draft plan is released. The Service states it will consider these comments in the final revision.
- **Next phase:** After finalization, the Service plans to focus on implementing recovery actions.
- **Implementation mechanism:** The [[Native Fish Conservation Team]], chaired by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], is identified as the vehicle for carrying out conservation and recovery actions for the Gila topminnow.
- **Expert engagement:** The Service invites Marsh to contribute his expertise through the Native Fish Conservation Team.
- **Acknowledgment:** The Service recognizes Marsh's expertise and his continued efforts on behalf of endangered species.
