# Fisheries Inventory of Gap Creek, Yavapai County, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Region VI fisheries inventory, conducted on August 25, 1987, assessed the extent and viability of a [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*) population in [[Gap Creek]], Yavapai County, Arizona. The survey was funded under [[Federal Aid Project F7R-30]] and carried out by [[Jim Warnecke]] and colleagues from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD). Gila trout were confined to a 2.325 km (1.5 mile) reach. Fish were in excellent condition, and the team judged the population reproductively stable. The report recommends further survey upstream, a three-year baseline monitoring schedule, and continued angling closure.

## Background & Description

**Stocking history.** Gila trout were first stocked in Gap Creek by G. Edwards (AGFD) in 1974 around the [[Government Springs]] area. In 1981, J. Brooks (AGFD) found a viable population in a 0.5 km section below the planting site, estimated at ca. 150 individuals. Efforts from 1982 to 1985 to move fish upstream by bucket failed, and seasonal flooding was suggested as a possible cause. A spot survey in August 1986 found a viable trout population in the mid-upper 0.8 km of the creek.

**Objectives.** The 1987 survey aimed to document the current distribution of Gila trout and record the general viability of the population.

**Methods.**
- Sampling used a [[Coffelt BP-2 Backpack Shocker]] operated on DC current, chosen to minimize mortality. Fish were sampled randomly through [[Electrofishing]].
- Survey distance was measured with a hip chain in kilometers.
- Cameras documented the survey photographically.
- The inventory ran from 9:30 am at [[Chalk Tank]] to 5:30 pm at [[Salt Mine Road]], about 5 miles downstream of Government Springs.
- A time-and-location event log was kept (Table 1).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey began below Government Springs in a lush [[Riparian Habitat]] canopy of sycamore, walnut and elm, which shaded the stream extensively. Stream temperature was approximately 20 °C. A wide variety of insects was observed.

The creek's structure strongly shaped fish distribution:
- **Plunge pools.** Two geologic barriers in the form of [[Plunge Pool]] features, including the 20 ft pools at "[[Virgin Falls]]", lie within the viable reaches. The report describes the population as stable in spite of them.
- **Dry reaches.** The streambed was dry for 5.0 km (3.1 miles) before a spring entered from the south bank at marker 7.3 km. Flow ceased at about 2.6 km, and the channel went underground at several points.
- **Habitat change.** The streambed "opened up" near 2.3 km, with sparser riparian vegetation and chalk stair-step substrate.

Below the spring, only [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) were found, and no other species, to the Salt Mine Road crossing.

## Key Findings

**Distribution**
- The first Gila trout was collected at about 0.2 km (214 m, 24 cm fish) below the upper pools. The last trout was at marker 2.5 km (1.6 miles).
- Gila trout were confined to a reach of 2.325 km (1.5 miles).
- Total inventory distance was 8.2 km (5.1 miles).
- Catch effort on the backpack shocker totaled 1.57 electrofishing units (E.F.U.).

**Abundance and condition**
- A random sample of 38 trout was collected. Length frequencies (Table 2) were judged to indicate a viable population.
- An additional 32 trout were observed without capture, bringing the total number of fish seen to 70.
- Fish were in excellent condition. Logged sizes ranged from about 8 cm to 30.6 cm, and Table 2 records several larger specimens weighing 40 g to 290 g.

**Population status**
- The team concluded the population appears reproductively stable.
- The survey documented that the 1986 spot-survey population persisted and extended from the mid-upper creek downstream.

**Recommendations**
1. Conduct an additional inventory above Government Springs to the drainage spring head to check for upstream trout extensions.
2. Establish a three-year [[Baseline Monitoring]] schedule to determine the integrity of the Gila trout population.
3. Continue the [[Angling Closure]] of Gap Creek, as specified in Arizona statutes, to protect this isolated population.

**Report details.** The report was prepared by J. Warnecke, Region VI Fish Specialist, Mesa, Arizona, in September 1987.
