# A New Troglobitic Crayfish from Oklahoma (Decapoda: Astacidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Astacidae #Decapoda #crayfish #troglobitic #cave-fauna

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *[[Cambarus (Jugicambarus) tartarus]]*, a new troglobitic (cave-dwelling) crayfish species from northeastern [[Oklahoma]]. Discovered by Jeffrey H. Black in the [[Stansberry-January Cave System]], this species represents the fourth member of the subgenus *Jugicambarus* known to have adapted to subterranean existence. The species exhibits typical cave adaptations including complete lack of pigmentation, reduced eyes, and elongated appendages with conspicuous setae. The description is based on three specimens: a holotypic male (form I), an allotypic female, and a morphotypic male (form I). The species is most closely related to *C. (J.) setosus* and *C. (J.) zophonastes* from the [[Ozark Region]].

## Background & Description

*[[Cambarus (Jugicambarus) tartarus]]* is characterized by a completely depigmented body with reduced, subcylindrical eyes. The body and chelipeds bear conspicuous stiff setae. The areola is extremely narrow, measuring 15.5 to 17.5 times longer than wide and comprising 44.3 to 46.4 percent of entire carapace length (51.5 to 52.2 percent of postorbital length), with only two punctations across the narrowest part. Cervical spines are absent and postorbital ridges are degenerate but retain small apical tubercles.

The holotypic male (form I) has a carapace length of 30.4 mm, width of 14.6 mm, and height of 10.0 mm. The rostrum lacks marginal spines or tubercles in the holotype but the allotypic female shows small marginal tubercles at the base of the acumen. The antennal scale is approximately 1.6 times longer than broad, broadest distal to midlength.

The chela features a rectangular palm with two sublinear arrangements of 8 and 10 tubercles along the mesial surface. Longitudinal ridges on the fingers are poorly developed. Hooks are present only on the ischia of the third pereiopods and are distinctly compressed.

The first pleopod of the first form male is diagnostic: the central projection is short, broad, bladelike, and strongly recurved with the distal portion directed toward the base of the appendage, bearing a distinct terminal notch. The mesial process has a broad base and its distal third tapers and projects approximately 120 degrees to the shaft. The proximolateral lobe is distinctly delimited from the major portion of the shaft by a conspicuous groove.

The annulus ventralis is symmetrical with the caudal portion somewhat movable; the cephalic third is membranous and bears a deep, broad median trough. First pleopods are lacking in the female.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*[[Cambarus (Jugicambarus) tartarus]]* inhabits the subterranean waters of the [[Stansberry-January Cave System]] in [[Delaware County]], [[Oklahoma]], within the [[Spavinaw Creek]] drainage of the [[Arkansas River Basin]]. The holotype was collected approximately 1 mile inside the cave from the upper end where the stream becomes quite small and narrow. The allotype and morphotype were collected from a very small stream over 2,000 feet from the Stansberry entrance.

The species co-occurs with *[[Orconectes neglectus neglectus]]*, which was found in the large stream as far as 2,000 feet inside the entrance. This represents a typical pattern where epigean (surface-dwelling) species penetrate the twilight zone while troglobitic species occupy deeper cave reaches.

Four members of the subgenus *Jugicambarus* are now known to have adapted to spelean existence: *C. (J.) cryptodytes* from the panhandle of [[Florida]] and southwestern [[Georgia]]; and three from the [[Ozark Region]]—*C. (J.) setosus* from southwestern [[Missouri]], *C. (J.) zophonastes* from north-central [[Arkansas]], and *C. (J.) tartarus* from northeastern [[Oklahoma]].

The epigean relatives of this troglobitic lineage include stream-dwelling members occurring on the [[Cumberland Plateau]], [[Highland Rim]], and southern [[Appalachians]].

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Description**: *[[Cambarus (Jugicambarus) tartarus]]* new species is described from three specimens collected from the [[Stansberry-January Cave System]], 4 miles north of Colcord, [[Delaware County]], [[Oklahoma]]. Type specimens are deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]], [[Smithsonian Institution]] (holotype no. 131951, allotype no. 131411, morphotype no. 132754).

**Diagnostic Characters**: The species can be distinguished from other troglobitic members of the subgenus by: (1) from *C. (J.) cryptodytes* by its much narrower areola, more than 10 times longer than broad; (2) from *C. (J.) setosus* and *C. (J.) zophonastes* by the short, strongly recurved central projection of the first pleopod that bears a well-defined subapical notch in first form males.

**Morphological Variation**: The three available specimens show some variation. The allotypic female differs from the holotype in having small marginal tubercles on the rostrum, a more spiniform distal portion of the antennal scale, and differences in chela tuberculation. The morphotypic male shows an abnormal cephalic region due to past injury and exhibits a heavier, less strongly recurved central projection on the first pleopod with only a trace of the subapical notch.

**Phylogenetic Relationships**: *C. (J.) tartarus* shares more characters with *C. (J.) setosus* and *C. (J.) zophonastes* than with other crayfishes. Fundamental similarities exist in the annulus ventralis, epistome, and markedly well-developed setae on chelipeds and carapace. Only *C. (J.) conasaugaensis*, *C. (J.) distans*, *C. (J.) parvoculus*, *C. (J.) tartarus*, and *C. (J.) cryptodytes* have retained the primitive subapical notch of the central projection of the first pleopod.

**Distribution Puzzle**: Surprisingly, specimens previously cited from other localities in the same [[Spavinaw Creek]] drainage in [[Delaware County]] and [[Mayes County]] (Twin Cave between Spavinaw and Jay; Spring Creek 5 miles south of Locust Grove) are not conspecific with *C. (J.) tartarus*. However, first form males from these localities are not available for definitive determination.

**Etymology**: The species name *tartarus* derives from Latin meaning "the infernal regions," referring to its subterranean mode of life.
