# Review of Biological Assessment: Hoover Dam Powerplant Modification Project

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1991 memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) to the Bureau of Reclamation (BR), reviewing a Biological Assessment (BA) for a proposed modification to the [[Hoover Dam]] Powerplant. The project sought to increase maximum discharge capacity from 40,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) to 62,000 cfs. The FWS rejected the BR's "no effect" determination, concluding that the project **may affect** two imperiled fish species native to the Colorado River: the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and the proposed-endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The FWS recommended formal Section 7 consultation (for the bonytail chub) and a Section 7 conference (for the razorback sucker) under the Endangered Species Act, citing potential alterations to the physical, chemical, thermal, and biological conditions of the river below the dam and in [[Lake Mohave]].

## Background & Description
This document is regulatory/administrative in nature and does not provide direct morphological data on the species. However, it references the following taxa relevant to the assessment:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
  - Family: [[Catostomidae]]
  - Status at time of writing: Proposed for federal listing as endangered (not yet formally listed).
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*)
  - Family: [[Cyprinidae]]
  - Status at time of writing: Federally listed endangered.
- Note: The BA references numerous unspecified **exotic (non-native) fish species** present in the project area, implicated as predators of the two native species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Context:** The assessment covers the Colorado River reach immediately below [[Hoover Dam]] and the downstream reservoir, [[Lake Mohave]] — a critical remnant habitat corridor for native big-river fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Environmental Baseline:** The memo explicitly notes that recruitment (i.e., survival of young fish to reproductive maturity) in wild populations of both the razorback sucker and bonytail chub is "nearly non-existent" in this system — indicating populations were likely being sustained by adult survivorship alone, with little to no natural recruitment success.
- **Predation Pressure:** The BA identifies predation by exotic fish species as a major ecological stressor on both native species, implying a food-web/community structure heavily altered by non-native introductions.
- **Flow-Ecosystem Linkage:** The proposed increase in discharge capacity (40,000 → 62,000 cfs) is flagged as a potential driver of change in:
  - Physical conditions (e.g., flow velocity, channel geomorphology, habitat availability)
  - Chemical conditions (water quality)
  - Thermal regime (water temperature, likely affecting spawning cues and larval survival)
  - Broader biological conditions (community composition, food web dynamics)

## Key Findings
- **FWS Non-Concurrence:** The FWS explicitly disagreed with the Bureau of Reclamation's "no effect" finding for threatened/endangered species.
- **"May Affect" Determination:** The proposed powerplant capacity increase (to 62,000 cfs) was determined to **may affect** both the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Regulatory Consequence — Bonytail Chub:** Because this species is federally listed as endangered, a "may affect" finding triggers a mandatory recommendation for **formal Section 7 consultation** under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Regulatory Consequence — Razorback Sucker:** As a *proposed* (not yet listed) species, the relevant standard shifts to whether the project is likely to **jeopardize** the species' continued existence; if so, a **Section 7 conference** is recommended.
- **Environmental Baseline Doctrine:** The FWS emphasized that the current lack of documented recruitment constitutes the environmental baseline against which the project's incremental impacts must be judged — additional stressors are considered particularly consequential given the species' already precarious status.
- **Procedural Recommendation:** The FWS recommended an informal, pre-consultation meeting between BR, FWS, and other stakeholders (state wildlife agencies, National Recreation Area management) to close data gaps *before* initiating formal consultation, since the formal process imposes a strict 90-day decision timeline.
- **Precautionary Principle Invoked:** The FWS noted that if formal consultation proceeds without adequate data, any resulting Biological Opinion would need to "err on the side of species conservation" due to data insufficiency — an implicit warning that data gaps could lead to a more restrictive outcome for the BR's project.
- **Multi-Agency Interest:** Distribution of the memo to Arizona Game and Fish Department, Nevada Department of Wildlife, and Lake Mead National Recreation Area indicates broad multi-jurisdictional concern over impacts to the [[Colorado River Basin]] native fish community.
