## Executive Summary
This note documents a local news report on the capture of a second [[Razorback Sucker]] (also known as the Humpback Sucker) within a two-week span from an irrigation canal on the Colorado River Indian Reservation. Once abundant in the Lower Colorado River, the species has become rare, primarily due to habitat alteration following dam construction. The article highlights ongoing conservation and telemetry research efforts by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] and federal partners to better understand the species' movement and life history.

## Background & Description
- **Diagnostic Feature:** Characterized by a distinctive high, narrow, keel-like hump (dorsal ridge) located behind the head, giving rise to the common name "razorback" or "humpback" sucker.
- **Specimen 1 (Caught early January):**
  - Length: 12.9 in.
  - Weight: 0.75 lbs.
  - Captors: Chance Esquerra and David Prieta.
- **Specimen 2 (Caught January 26):**
  - Length: 14.6 in.
  - Weight: 1.25 lbs.
  - Captors: Eloy DeLeon, Levi Esquerra, Robert Esquerra, Jeff Esquerra, and Greg Mojado.
- **Location of Capture:** Both specimens were caught at the same location, Lateral 27R on Burns Rd., within the irrigation canal system of the [[Colorado River Indian Reservation]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range:** Historically common throughout the [[Lower Colorado River]] system.
- **Current Distribution:** Now rare, with only a few known local populations remaining, including:
  - [[Senator Wash]] (near Imperial Dam)
  - [[12-Mile Lake]]
  - [[Lake Havasu]]
- **Habitat Impact:** Population numbers decreased drastically following the construction of dams along the Colorado River, which altered the natural flow regimes, temperature, and spawning habitats required by the species.
- **Anthropogenic Interaction:** The species is now occasionally found in artificial irrigation canal systems adjacent to its historic riverine range.

## Key Findings
- **Conservation Status:** The [[Razorback Sucker]] is officially labeled "endangered" in both California and Nevada.
- **Research Initiative:** The State of California is conducting a sonic beeper tag program in the Senator Wash/Imperial Dam area to monitor the movements of the fish within the reservoir.
- **Research Goals:** The primary objectives of this tagging study are to gain further understanding of:
  - Feeding patterns
  - Migration patterns
  - Spawning habits
- **Interagency Cooperation:** The monitoring effort is a collaborative undertaking involving multiple agencies:
  - [[California Fish and Game Department]]
  - [[Water and Power Resources Service]]
  - [[Bureau of Land Management]]
  - [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]
- **Key Personnel:** Mike Donahoo, fishery manager biologist for the Parker office of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], served as the primary source for population and conservation program details in this report.
