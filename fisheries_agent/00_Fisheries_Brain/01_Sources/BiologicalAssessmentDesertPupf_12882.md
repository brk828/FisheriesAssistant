# Desert Pupfish Introduction Proposal - Cold Spring Seep and Big Spring, BLM Safford District

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1989 Bureau of Land Management biological assessment documents a proposed introduction of [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) into three spring-fed ponds at two locations in the Safford District, Arizona. The sites—Cold Spring Seep (Ponds 1 and 2) and Big Spring—already support reproducing populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) stocked in 1985. The proposal requires formal Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] due to the presence of the endangered topminnow. Fish for introduction were raised by students at [[Flowing Wells High School]] in Tucson from captive-bred stock originally from [[Santa Clara Slough]], New Mexico. The BLM requests U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service concurrence that the co-introduction will have beneficial or no impacts on either species.

## Background & Description

### Species Accounts

**[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*): An endangered cyprinodontid native to the [[Gila River]] basin and surrounding desert aquatic systems. The proposed introduction sites fall within the species' historical range. Source fish originated from Santa Clara Slough and were transferred through [[Arizona State University]], [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]], and [[Deer Valley High School]] before reaching Flowing Wells High School for captive breeding.

**[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*): An endangered poeciliid already established at both proposed introduction sites. Approximately 300 individuals were stocked at each location in 1985 and are successfully reproducing. Source population came from [[Monkey Springs]].

### Site Descriptions

**Cold Spring Seep** (T5S, R24E, Section 17): Two constructed ponds excavated by BLM in 1983 and 1985 below existing seeps. Pond 1 is circular, approximately 20 feet wide, 1-4 feet deep, dominated by *Elodia*. Pond 2 measures 12' x 12', 2-3 feet deep, with mixed *Elodia* and other aquatic vegetation. Water quality suitable for pupfish despite high salt concentration. Located over one mile from the Gila River with limited flood connectivity. Surrounded by salt-tolerant riparian vegetation (salt cedar, mesquite, four-wing saltbush, hackberry) grading into saltgrass/reed cienega. BLM holds Certificate of Water Right No. 33-87783 for 0.40 acre-feet/year.

**Big Spring** (T6S, R25E, Section 5): Located in sharply cut drainage over two miles from the Gila River. Small reservoir (8' x 15') behind concrete dam constructed in 1984 after 1982 flash flood destroyed previous structure. Upper plunge pool (15' x 10') fed by spring flowing approximately 2 gpm. Fish present in plunge pool, behind dam, and in pools/runs extending 200 yards downstream. A 2.5-acre exclosure protects the spring and drainage from livestock. Riparian restoration includes planted [[Goodding Willow]], [[Fremont Cottonwood]], phragmites, and alkali sacaton. Natural reestablishment of seep willow and cattails. Clay substrate prevents water percolation for 200-yard stretch. BLM filed Application No. 33-87611 for 0.862 acre-feet/year (protested, pending Gila River adjudication).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Species Interactions

According to Jim Brooks (Dexter National Hatchery, personal communication 1989), no competition has been observed between [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]]. The two species co-existed at Monkey Springs (source of topminnows) and Boyce Thompson Arboretum (indirect source of pupfish). Co-introductions are encouraged by USFWS and Arizona Game and Fish Department.

### Habitat Conditions

Both sites classified as Sensitivity Category 3 under BLM Arizona Manual Supplement 6820 (limited multiple use issues with low public interest and easily resolvable conflicts). Sites located in ephemeral pastures with infrequent livestock grazing. Cold Spring Seep shows moderate grazing use; Big Spring exclosure has excluded livestock since 1984, allowing riparian recovery.

### Threats and Risk Factors

- **Flash flooding**: Potential threat at Big Spring where floods could breach dam, though no structural damage has occurred since 1984 construction. Risk considered acceptable given conservation benefits.
- **Sedimentation**: Erosion has partly filled pond behind Big Spring dam, reducing available habitat.
- **Exotic vegetation**: Salt cedar dominance in drainages.
- **Bullfrogs**: Present at Cold Spring Seep; may require control.
- **Aquatic vegetation overgrowth**: Dense *Elodia* growth in Cold Spring Pond 1 may require periodic selective removal.

## Key Findings

### Proposed Management Actions

**Cold Spring Seep:**
1. Periodic selective removal of aquatic vegetation as needed
2. Temporary fish removal to off-site holding facility if extensive excavation required
3. Bullfrog control using methods legal under AGFD regulations
4. Potential construction of third pond, naturally stocked by overflow from Ponds 1 and 2
5. Periodic maintenance of roads and barbed wire fences
6. Grazing system changes permitted if vegetation utilization remains same or decreases

**Big Spring:**
1. Pond restoration to original size through trenching above existing pond with temporary gravel barrier to minimize impacts to existing topminnows
2. Gabion construction in drainage above spring to reduce sediment deposition
3. Removal of exotic salt cedars after planted willows mature
4. Hand control of cattails if habitat area significantly reduced
5. Planting of native riparian species (giant sacaton, cottonwood, Goodding willow) for soil stabilization
6. Fish relocation to holding facility during major construction activities (dam repair/replacement)

### Monitoring and Responsibility

- Introduction success determination: Arizona Game and Fish Department
- Habitat monitoring: Bureau of Land Management
- Both agencies will record fish population health and habitat condition during site visits
- Records maintained by AGFD, BLM, and FWS

### Incidental Take Assessment

Minimal fish losses anticipated during:
- Collection at school
- Transport and handling at sites
- Stress-induced shock after introduction
- Re-excavation activities

Losses considered acceptable and offset by opportunity to establish self-sustaining populations within historic range. No negative impact to wild desert pupfish populations as source fish are captive-bred.

### Conservation Commitment

BLM and AGFD committed to full recovery of both [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]]. Proposed action intended to enhance numbers and distribution of desert pupfish, contributing to eventual downlisting and delisting. All planned management actions assessed as having beneficial impacts or no impacts on both species.

### Special Status Species

[[Marsh Rosemary]] (*Limonium limbatum*), a Federal category 3c and Arizona Natural Heritage Program listed plant species, occurs within 1/2 mile of Cold Spring Seep. No adverse impacts anticipated to this or other special status species.
