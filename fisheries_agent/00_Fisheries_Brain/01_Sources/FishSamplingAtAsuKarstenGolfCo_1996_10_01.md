# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, September 1996

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary

This report documents a fish community monitoring survey conducted by Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies at the ASU Karsten Golf Course (ASUGC) lakes on 26–27 September 1996. The primary objective was to assess the persistence status of three imperiled native Colorado River Basin fishes — [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — which had been experimentally stocked into the North and South lakes in 1988 (all three species) and again in 1991 (bonytail only). All stocked individuals originated from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Dexter, New Mexico), a key refugium/propagation facility for endangered Southwestern fishes.

Sampling using entanglement gear (trammel and experimental gill nets) in both North and South lakes yielded only 17 individuals across six species, all of which were non-native, introduced sportfish or baitfish species. **No native target species (bonytail, Colorado squawfish, or razorback sucker) were captured** during this survey. This continues a trend of low catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) relative to historical surveys, with an unexplained decline in overall fish abundance/catchability. Notably, one razorback sucker had been documented in the preceding Spring 1996 survey, indicating that at least a remnant presence of stocked natives may persist, despite non-detection in this survey.

## Background & Description

The source document is a management/monitoring memo and does not provide morphometric, meristic, or anatomical descriptions of individual specimens. Species were identified only to the taxonomic level required for a catch census. The six species documented in the catch are morphologically and ecologically distinct non-native taxa:

- **[[Threadfin Shad]]** (*Dorosoma petenense*) — small-bodied pelagic clupeid, forage fish.
- **[[Carp]]** (*Cyprinus carpio*) — large-bodied benthivorous cyprinid.
- **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** (*Ameiurus natalis*) — benthic ictalurid catfish.
- **[[Yellow Bass]]** (*Morone mississippiensis*) — moronid, schooling piscivore/planktivore.
- **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*) — small, aggressive centrarchid.
- **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*) — deep-bodied centrarchid, common panfish.

No morphological data were reported for the three target native species (bonytail, Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker), as none were captured in this sampling event.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Artificial lentic (lake) habitats situated within a golf course (ASUGC), representing a novel, human-modified refuge environment for stocked native fish rather than their historic riverine habitat in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]].
- **Stocking history/context**: The ASUGC lakes have served as an experimental grow-out/refuge site for endangered native Colorado River fishes since 1988, reflecting broader recovery efforts for the [[Colorado River Fish Recovery Program]] using hatchery-reared stock from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]].
- **Community composition**: The extant fish community sampled in September 1996 was composed entirely of non-native, widely established warmwater sportfish and forage fish typical of reservoir/pond ecosystems in the southwestern U.S. This suggests the lake community is now dominated by introduced competitors and potential predators of the stocked natives.
- **Species interactions**: Non-native species such as [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Bass]] are known predators/competitors of larval and juvenile native cyprinids and catostomids, and their dominance in this system may partly explain the failure to detect native target species.
- **Sampling method influence**: Use of large-mesh entanglement gear (trammel nets, experimental gill nets) targets larger-bodied fish and may bias detection away from smaller or evasive individuals, potentially underrepresenting persisting natives.

## Key Findings

1. **Zero native fish detected**: No bonytail, Colorado squawfish, or razorback sucker were captured in North or South lakes during the 26–27 September 1996 sampling, despite historical stocking efforts in 1988 and 1991.
2. **Prior detection context**: One razorback sucker was recorded during the Spring 1996 survey, indicating a native individual persisted in the system as recently as several months prior to this survey.
3. **Total catch was low**: Only 17 fish total were captured across four nets (two lakes × two net types) fished overnight (~10 hour soak time), continuing a trend of low CPUE compared to historical ASUGC surveys — the cause of this decline remains undetermined.
4. **Community entirely non-native**: All 17 captured individuals belonged to six non-native species: [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Carp]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Bluegill]].
5. **Ongoing monitoring planned**: A follow-up survey was scheduled for Spring 1997 to continue tracking status of native stockings at ASUGC.
6. **Conservation implication**: The absence of native target species raises concern regarding survivorship, predation pressure from non-native fish, or emigration/mortality of the 1988/1991 stocked cohorts, warranting continued monitoring and potential re-evaluation of the ASUGC as a viable refuge site for [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]].
