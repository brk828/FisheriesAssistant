# Biochemical Analysis of Gene Products in the Yaqui Catfish Ictalurus pricei

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #conservation-genetics #electrophoresis

## Executive Summary

This 1991 report documents a biochemical genetic analysis of [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) held at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] using horizontal starch gel electrophoresis. The study aimed to develop non-lethal tissue sampling methods, characterize genetic relationships between Yaqui and [[Channel Catfish]] (*I. punctatus*), identify marker loci for species discrimination, and assess genetic purity of 227 captive Yaqui catfish from two wild collections (1987 and 1990). The analysis revealed significant hybridization between Yaqui and channel catfish in the 1987 collection, identified malate dehydrogenase (MDH) as a diagnostic marker locus, and provided recommendations for maintaining genetically pure broodstock for conservation purposes.

## Background & Description

The [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) was originally described by Rutter in 1896. Its native distribution is limited to the [[Rio Yaqui]] River basin of northwestern Mexico, encompassing a small section of southeastern Arizona. The species is of conservation concern due to recent range reductions and apparent extirpation from the United States (McNatt, 1974). The liberal introduction of [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) into its native range poses threats through competition and potential hybridization. Channel catfish has occasionally hybridized with [[Headwater Catfish]] (*I. lupus*), a species thought to be closely related to Yaqui catfish.

Morphologically, Yaqui catfish are highly similar to and difficult to distinguish from channel catfish. Traditional dichotomous keys use anal fin ray counts (23-25 in *I. pricei* vs. 24-29 in *I. punctatus*) and anal fin base length, but these characters overlap substantially, leaving many specimens' identities uncertain.

The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] obtained live specimens from two wild collections: 56 fish in October 1987 and 171 fish in April 1990, totaling 227 individuals. Each fish was electronically tagged with an individual PIT tag identification number.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Rio Yaqui]] basin represents a limited geographic range for this endemic catfish species. The introduction of non-native [[Channel Catfish]] into this drainage has created conditions for competitive exclusion and genetic introgression through hybridization. The species' apparent extirpation from United States waters indicates severe population decline and range contraction.

The study examined genetic diversity metrics within the captive populations. Average heterozygosity values for the 1990 (0.11) and 1987 (0.12) populations were in the range typically reported for individuals (0.05-0.15) rather than population averages, suggesting relatively high inbreeding within each population. Polymorphism levels were 18% (1990 population) and 24% (1987 population), slightly below the typical range of 25-50% reported for various organisms but higher than levels reported for [[Headwater Catfish]].

## Key Findings

**Methodology Development:**
- Successfully developed a low-mortality biopsy technique using caudal fin tissue for biochemical analysis
- Caudal fin tissue provided sufficient enzyme activity for electrophoretic resolution of many enzyme systems
- Fish were anesthetized in dilute MS-222 solution; 1/4 inch of posterior caudal fin edge was trimmed and discarded, then an additional 1/4 inch was collected
- Tissues were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen (-195.8°C) for preservation
- Reasonable care during tissue removal followed by antibiotic treatment resulted in minimal mortality

**Electrophoretic Survey Results:**
- Attempted to assay 30 enzyme systems on 9 buffer systems (pH 5.7-8.7)
- Successfully resolved 17 enzyme loci from caudal fin tissue
- Only 5 of 17 resolved enzyme loci exhibited mobility differences among specimens examined
- Channel and Yaqui catfish appear very closely related, with genetic divergence comparable to that between channel and headwater catfish

**Genetic Distance Analysis:**
- Nei's genetic distance (D) calculations revealed:
  - 1990 Yaqui population appears relatively pure
  - 1987 Yaqui population shows evidence of introgression with channel catfish
  - 1987 Yaqui population is more closely related to channel catfish than to 1990 Yaqui population

**Marker Locus Identification:**
- Malate dehydrogenase (MDH) enzyme system identified as the primary genetic marker capable of distinguishing Yaqui and channel catfish
- MDH is a complex dimeric enzyme system with two gene loci producing peptide subunits
- Four distinct banding patterns identified:
  - Pattern A: unique to channel catfish and backcrossed individuals (1 of 10 from 1987 collection)
  - Pattern B: common "good" Yaqui catfish pattern
  - Pattern C: hybrid pattern exhibiting characteristics of both A and B (possibly F₁ hybrids; 6 of 10 from 1987 collection)
  - Pattern D: rare Yaqui catfish pattern with different allele

**Individual Fish Identification:**
- All 227 Yaqui catfish were electrophoretically identified using MDH marker loci
- Results categorized individuals as:
  - Pure Yaqui catfish (pattern B)
  - Yaqui catfish with different allele (pattern D)
  - Hybrids, possibly F₁ (pattern C)
  - Channel catfish or backcross individuals (pattern A)
- Detailed identification table provided for each specimen by PIT tag number

**Genetic Diversity Assessment:**
- Average heterozygosity: 0.11 (1990 population), 0.12 (1987 population)
- These values suggest relatively high inbreeding compared to typical population averages
- Polymorphism: 18% (1990 population), 24% (1987 population)
- Genetic diversity slightly below average but higher than related [[Headwater Catfish]]

**Recommendations:**
1. Use only specimens identified as "Yaqui" or "Yaqui (different allele)" as broodstock for maintaining pure populations
2. Morphometrically verify specimens by comparison with type specimens prior to propagation
3. Collect additional Yaqui and channel catfish from other portions of the range to capture broader genetic diversity
4. Electrophoretically characterize future specimens considered for broodstock to eliminate hybrids
5. Compare channel catfish from [[Rio Yaqui]] drainage with Yaqui catfish, as these are the individuals with which hybridization would occur
6. Conduct multivariate morphometric analysis (discriminant function analysis) on current stock after eliminating hybrids to identify useful morphometric characters for field identification

**Conservation Implications:**
- The 1990 collection appears to represent a relatively pure population suitable for conservation breeding
- Significant hybridization detected in 1987 collection indicates ongoing genetic threats in wild populations
- Electrophoretic screening is essential for maintaining genetic integrity of captive breeding programs
- The species may not possess majority of genetic diversity in current captive stock, necessitating additional wild collections from throughout the range
