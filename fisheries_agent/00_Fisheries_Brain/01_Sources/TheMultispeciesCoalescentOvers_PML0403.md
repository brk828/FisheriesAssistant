# The Multispecies Coalescent Over-Splits Species in the Case of Geographically Widespread Taxa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #colubridae

*Note: This is a methodological and taxonomic commentary on snakes (reptiles), not fish. The tag line above follows the required format but the taxon is non-fish; the tag #colubridae reflects the family studied.*

## Executive Summary

Chambers and Hillis argue that coalescent-based species delimitation, particularly using [[Bayesian Phylogenetics And Phylogeography]] (BPP), can over-split species when applied to geographically widespread, clinally varying, or parapatrically distributed taxa. Their case study reanalyzes the nuclear and mitochondrial data of [[Ruane Et Al 2014]] on the [[American Milksnake]] complex (*Lampropeltis triangulum*), which had been split into seven species. The authors conclude that the best available evidence supports three species: *L. triangulum*, *L. elapsoides*, and *L. polyzona*. They recommend that coalescent-based studies include thorough geographic sampling and examine contact zones before taxonomic change.

## Background & Description

- **Species concept framework:** Species are treated as separately evolving metapopulation lineages (see [[General Lineage Species Concept]]; de Queiroz 1998, 2007). Species boundaries are fuzzy in origin, and different operational criteria can define a splitting event.
- **Problem taxa:** Widespread, geographically variable, continuously distributed species and complexes are difficult to delimit, because variation may be clinal (gene flow across the range) or may reflect parapatric taxa with little gene flow at contact.
- **Multispecies coalescent (MSC):** The MSC models species trees from gene trees while accounting for incomplete lineage sorting. MSC-based delimitation methods (e.g., [[Bayesian Phylogenetics And Phylogeography]], [[Structurama]]) are often used in isolation, despite known assumptions and limitations.
- **Study organism:** The American milksnake complex was historically treated as a single species (Williams 1988). Ruane et al. (2014) elevated several lineages to full species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Contact zones:** Contact zone analyses assess genetic isolation between putative taxa. Three outcomes are distinguished: (a) no hybridization, (b) a narrow "genetic sink" hybrid zone restricting gene flow, and (c) broad gene flow and integration. Outcome (c) refutes independent lineage evolution.
- **Sympatry in the milksnakes:** *L. triangulum* and *L. elapsoides* are sympatric with little or no hybridization across a broad region of Kentucky, Tennessee, Alabama, Georgia, North Carolina, and Virginia. *L. triangulum* and *L. polyzona* co-occur only at a single locality in northern Mexico (as reported by Williams 1988).
- **Mitochondrial introgression:** Western *L. triangulum* populations (formerly *gentilis* and *annulata*) carry mitochondrial haplotypes deeply embedded within *L. alterna*, with introgression events that appear to have occurred several times and remain ongoing. Identical haplotypes are shared where *L. alterna* and *L. triangulum* coexist in Val Verde County, Texas.
- **Sampling extent:** Structurama-tested samples spanned thousands of kilometers, from New York to Montana to Arizona. The closest samples of *annulata* and *gentilis-triangulum* are about 485 km apart.

## Key Findings

- **Structurama did not separate taxa:** Structurama failed to distinguish *gentilis* from *triangulum*, and grouped *pyromelana*, *abnorma*, and *micropholis* into a single cluster. Results varied among runs, indicating the data were not informative enough for consistent clustering.
- **Nuclear data show no divergence:** Across 11 nuclear genes, nuclear divergence was very low (0.0174–0.076 substitutions per site among the most divergent *L. triangulum* complex samples). No nucleotide differs diagnostically between *gentilis* and *triangulum*. Geographically closest *gentilis* and *triangulum* individuals are genetically indistinguishable at every nuclear locus.
- **Only mitochondrial introgression separates taxa:** The only consistent difference between *gentilis* and *triangulum* is that *gentilis* individuals carry introgressed *L. alterna* mtDNA.
- **BPP over-splits:** BPP, using Structurama-derived terminal lineages, gave high support (posterior probability 99.2%) for six lineages. Testing five east-west splits, BPP supported all of them (posterior probability of 100% for Splits 1–4 and at least 96% for Split 5). BPP therefore supports virtually any geographic partition of samples along the cline.
- **Simulation consistency:** These empirical results match Barley et al. (2018) simulations, in which BPP supports separated geographic clusters from one isolation-by-distance species as distinct species.
- **Revised taxonomy:** The best-supported hypothesis is three species: *L. triangulum*, *L. elapsoides*, and *L. polyzona*. The *elapsoides* and *triangulum* contact zone has extensive sympatry without hybridization, and the *triangulum-polyzona* contact has sharp genetic breaks. The remaining Ruane et al. (2014) splits show no sympatry and no sharp genetic breaks at contacts, and are not supported.
- **Recommendations:** Coalescent-based studies should integrate morphological, behavioral, and ecological data and analyze contact zones. Taxonomic changes should not rest on limited geographic samples. A conservative approach may underestimate diversity, but it is preferable to poorly supported changes with each new dataset.
