# Observations on the Fishes of the Genus Notropis

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Notropis #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1909 taxonomic study by Cockerell and Callaway examines scale morphology—specifically the number of "apical radii" (fine ridges radiating from the scale focus)—across numerous species of the minnow genus **[[Notropis]]** (family [[Cyprinidae]]). The authors argue that Notropis evolved in North America from a **[[Pimephalinae]]** ancestor (via **[[Pimephales]]** → **[[Cliola]]** → subgenus **[[Luxilus]]**, exemplified by **[[Notropis Cornutus]]**), rather than descending from Old World **[[Leuciscinae]]**. They propose a new subfamily, **Notropinae**, encompassing Notropis and Cliola, with **[[Hybopsis]]** and possibly **[[Phenacobius]]** as an aberrant branch. A new subgenus, **[[Coccogenia]]**, is erected for *N. coccogenis*. The paper is fundamentally a comparative morphometric and phylogenetic exercise using scale sculpture (apical radii counts, nuclear area position, sculpture strength) as the primary diagnostic and evolutionary character, supplemented by external features (body depth, eye size, snout shape, pigmentation bands).

## Background & Description

### Primary Diagnostic Character: Scale Apical Radii

The number of apical radii (including partly developed ones) counted near the lateral line at the level of the dorsal fin origin was used as the key comparative trait across subgenera:

### Other Scale Features

- Scale shape (broader than long, subcircular, or longer than broad)
- Position/breadth of the **nuclear area** (fraction of distance from base)
- **Sculpture strength** (distinct/strong vs. weak/feeble)
- **Circuli** regularity and spacing (regular vs. irregular, close vs. far apart)

### External/Body Morphology

- **Cliola smithii**: deep-bodied, large scales, peritoneum red-brown (unspotted) on silver substratum (paralleling **[[Orthodon]]** and **[[Acrocheilus]]**); gill-lamellae strongly fimbriate.
- **[[Coccogenia]]** (new subgenus), type *N. coccogenis*: mouth large and oblique, lower jaw projecting beyond upper; teeth 2,4-4,2; silvery sides, no dark band; large black patch on dorsal fin as recognition mark.
- **Chriope** species distinguished by snout length relative to eye diameter, caudal spot distinctness, lateral band color/intensity, iris color, and scale apex shape. Measurement table (max body depth vs. caudal peduncle depth) given for *N. kendalli, N. muskoka, N. heterodon, N. anogenus, N. cayuga, N. bifrenatus*.
- **Moniana**: *N. formosus* is small, deep-bodied, with conspicuous cross-hatched pigment pattern and narrow dark dorsal band, resembling *N. cornutus* in pattern despite smaller size.
- **Cyprinella**: *N. niveus* and *N. galacturus* both warm reddish dorsally with silvery sides; *N. galacturus* has a notably larger eye than *N. niveus* (eye diameter 3¾ mm in niveus vs. 5 mm in [[Notropis Albeolus]]).
- **Notropis s. str.**: six similar small species (jejunus, leuciodus, stilbius, telescopus, atherinoides, swaini) with large eyes and broad silvery lateral bands; distinguished by eye diameter, presence/absence of a black caudal spot, presence of dark spots on lateral-line scales, and snout shape.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Notropis species inhabit a broad range of North American freshwater habitats: small streams, large rivers, and lakes, spanning from Maine and Ontario to Texas and Colorado.
- The authors suggest that **small body size and slender form** in some species represent an adaptation enabling colonization of smaller streams unsuitable for the ancestral (stem) form, *N. cornutus* — an example of niche diversification via body-size reduction.
- Some minor forms are hypothesized to be evolutionarily recent, potentially **post-glacial**, reflecting rapid diversification into newly available freshwater habitats following glacial retreat.
- Geographic ranges cited include: Tellico River (Tennessee), Lake Maxinkuckee (Indiana), Sebago Lake (Maine), Guadalupe River (Texas), Chautauqua Lake (New York), Neosho Spring Brook (Missouri), Colonia Juárez (Mexico), Holston River (Virginia), White River (Arkansas), and Medicine Hat (Canada) — indicating wide continental distribution across the genus as a whole, with individual species often narrowly endemic.
- *N. cornutus* itself shows an "enormous range," collected from Cross Lake Thoroughfare, Maine, to Boulder County, Colorado, consistent with its designation as the abundant, widespread stem-form.

## Key Findings

1. **Phylogenetic origin**: Notropis is proposed to have arisen in North America from **[[Pimephalinae]]** stock (Pimephales → Cliola → Luxilus), independent of Old World Leuciscinae — supporting recognition of a new subfamily, **Notropinae** (Notropis + Cliola), with Hybopsis (and possibly Phenacobius) as an aberrant branch.
2. **Stem-form identification**: *[[Notropis Cornutus]]* (subgenus Luxilus) is identified as the stem-form of the genus based on high, broad apical radii counts and wide morphological/geographic range; its abundance suggests the genus's diversification was not due to failure of the ancestral type but rather evolutionary "exuberance."
3. **Notropis s. str. is not ancestral**: Despite lending its name to the genus, the nominate subgenus (Notropis s. str.) shows the *smallest* range of apical radii variation (5–11) and is interpreted as a **terminal/end-form**, not a stem-form.
4. **New taxa proposed**:
   - Subgenus **[[Coccogenia]]** (type: *N. coccogenis*), split off from Hydrophlox due to divergent scale characters.
   - Species-level elevation of ***[[Notropis Atrocaudalis]]*** (formerly *N. cayuga atrocaudalis*), supported by Evermann's assent.
5. **Independent subgenus origins**: **[[Chriope]]**, **[[Hudsonius]]**, and **[[Moniana]]** each appear to represent separate branches diverging from the Luxiline stem rather than being derived from one another — Hudsonius specifically cannot derive from Chriope or Alburnops based on dentition.
6. **Cyprinella lineage**: Likely derived from Luxilus-type ancestors via forms resembling *[[Notropis Albeolus]]*, based on scale and coloration similarity (though this may not generalize to all Cyprinella species).
7. **Proposed within-subgenus evolutionary schemes**: A branching diagram is offered for **Chriope**, positioning *N. muskoka* as ancestral-like and radiating to *maculatus*, *heterodon*, *atrocaudalis*, *cayuga*, *kendalli*, and *bifrenatus*. A similar (unresolved, non-linear) radiating pattern is proposed for **Hudsonius** (gilberti, piptolepis, hudsonius, illecebrosus).
8. **Taxonomic caution**: The authors explicitly decline to formally split Notropis into multiple genera at this time, citing insufficient material/experience, despite acknowledging that Luxilus could be separated — since doing so would logically require elevating nearly all other subgenera to generic rank as well.
9. **Methodological caveat**: Apical radii ranges reported are likely underestimates of true intraspecific variation, since scales were sampled from a single body location and from limited series of specimens per species.
