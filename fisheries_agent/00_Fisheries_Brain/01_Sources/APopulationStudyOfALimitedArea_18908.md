# Population Study of a Limited Area in a Michigan Trout Stream, September 1940

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1940 study by Shetter and Leonard of the Michigan Department of Conservation enumerated the entire fish population of a 580.5-foot (0.131-acre) section of [[Hunt Creek]], a tributary of the [[Thunder Bay River]] in Montmorency County, Michigan. Using stream diversion, blocking seines, and [[Rotenone]] poisoning, the investigators recovered 605 [[Eastern Brook Trout]] and 188 [[Muddler]] sculpins. Results were used to estimate standing crop, age structure, survival, growth and condition, and to evaluate the efficiency of seining as a sole capture method.

## Background & Description

The authors note the scarcity of published whole-population studies of trout streams. The study area, designated Section C of Hunt Creek, was divided into two contiguous sections, II-A (282.5 ft, 0.068 acre) and III-A (298 ft, 0.063 acre). A series of by-pass channels was excavated so the entire stream flow could be diverted, allowing concrete bulkheads to isolate the reach. The method follows [[Embody (1929)]] and was intended to support an intensive [[Creel Census]] of the experimental area.

Field work was conducted on September 25–26, 1940. Blocking seines (¾-inch bar mesh) were set at points X and Y, and flow was diverted by a sandbag dam at point Z. Seining on day one was followed by an overnight hold and rotenone treatment on day two (200 g of 5% powdered derris root in 5 quarts of water, applied at 38°F air and 44°F water temperature).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology and water quality:** Flow was about 6,500 gallons per minute, with surface velocities of 1 to 2.5 ft/s. The water is almost wholly spring-fed. Dissolved oxygen was typically about 10.5 ppm, free CO₂ rarely above 2 ppm and usually absent, methyl-orange alkalinity about 175 ppm, and pH 7.8 to 8.0.
- **Substrate:** Smoothly rounded glacial-drift gravel, averaging about the size of a hen's egg, with little exposed sand.
- **Benthic fauna:** Averaged about 0.750 cc of bottom organisms per square foot in September. Caddisfly larvae and pupae (notably *Mystrophora americana*) dominated in number and volume, followed by mayflies (*Baetis*, *Paraleptophlebia*, *Ephemerella*) and Diptera. Stoneflies were numerous, including *Capnia*, *Allocapnia*, *Leuctra* and *Nemoura*.
- **Riparian and aquatic vegetation:** Banks were generally under 1 to 2 feet high, with alder, tamarack, white cedar and aspen. Aquatic vegetation was negligible, with minor patches of speedwell and algal mats (*Vaucheria*).

## Key Findings

**Population and standing crop**
- Total count: 605 brook trout (12.36 lb) and 188 muddlers (1.27 lb).
- Calculated density for Section C: 4,619 trout/acre (94.40 lb/acre) and 1,435 muddlers/acre (9.68 lb/acre).
- Size structure of trout: 2.3% legal (≥7 in; n = 14), 22.0% sublegal 4–6.9 in (n = 133), and 75.7% fingerlings under 4 in (n = 458).
- Legal-sized trout were 2.5 times more numerous in the deeper section II-A than in the shallower III-A. Fingerlings were more abundant in III-A.
- Hatchery origin: 40 marked fish (6.7% of trout count, 2.7% of trout weight), almost all released in August 1940 and all below legal size.

**Capture efficiency**
- Seining alone recovered 81.4% of all fish in III-A (by number) and 72.6% in II-A. Seining accounted for 91.9% of trout in III-A and 86.2% in II-A.
- Seining alone recovered 84.3% of total weight in II-A, versus nearly 93% in III-A.
- The authors conclude seining cannot be expected to exceed about 80% accuracy as the sole method, even under favorable conditions. Rotenone was especially important for small muddlers, which burrow into the gravel.
- By the authors' Trippensee (1937) comparison, II-A was "moderately difficult to seine" and III-A "easy to seine."

**Age, growth and survival**
- Legal size (7 in) is generally reached during the third or fourth summer. About 4.4% of age group II (third summer) exceeded 7 in, and about 63% of age group III (fourth summer) were legal.
- Mean calculated total lengths: about 8 in at 1 year, 5 in at 2 years, just under 6.5 in at 3 years, and 8 in at 3.33 years.
- Growth was the slowest of any brook trout water with available data, including [[New York Brook Trout Streams]] (Hazzard, 1932) and Ontario trout (Ricker, 1932).
- Age composition: age 0 46.7%, I 30.8%, II 19.8%, III 2.7%.
- Survival per 1,000 young-of-the-year: 659 at year 1, 424 at year 2, 58 at year 3. Only about 5.4 of every 1,000 fingerlings reached legal size by the fourth summer.
- Estimated legal-sized standing crop was about 114 fish. The 1940 creel catch of 113 legal trout was roughly 50% of this standing crop.

**Condition**
- Mean coefficient of condition (K) for 144 trout (98–240 mm) was 1.469, with a range of 0.970 to 2.029. The authors attribute the higher values partly to live-weighing (versus dead weights used elsewhere) and partly to pre-spawning condition in larger fish.

**Summary conclusions**
- The study provides a near-complete census of a small trout reach, and the authors regard the figures as representing 100% capture.
- The authors state the station's most important future task is determining the fate of the 97.3% of fingerlings that do not reach legal size, and whether angler return can be improved practicably.
