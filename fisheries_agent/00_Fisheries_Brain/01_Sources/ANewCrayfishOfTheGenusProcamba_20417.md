# A New Crayfish of the Genus Procambarus from Mississippi (*Procambarus fitzpatricki*)

#carcinology #freshwater-biology #procambarus

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper formally describes *Procambarus fitzpatricki* as a new species of [[Crayfish]] from southern [[Mississippi]]. The species was first listed as *Cambarus (Paracambarus) harnedi* by [[Lyle 1938]], a name the source treats as a nomen nudum. The author, [[Horton H. Hobbs Jr.]] of the [[Smithsonian Institution]], based the description on material from the [[Lyle Collection]] and later collections, and named the species in honor of [[Joseph F. Fitzpatrick Jr.]] The species is distinguished by male hooks on the fourth pereiopods only, a distinctive first pleopod, and two uropod spines projecting beyond the inner ramus margin. It is placed in the [[Gracilis Section]] of the genus, but the paper leaves open how one terminal pleopod element should be interpreted.

## Background & Description

**Discovery and naming:** The existence of the species was first reported by Lyle (1938) in an abstract of a doctoral dissertation. The collection behind that work was held at [[Mississippi State University]] and was unavailable for study for some years. Fitzpatrick refurbished it after joining the university staff, and the material, together with much new collection from across the state, was deposited at the Smithsonian Institution.

**Diagnostic characters (from the source):**
- Body pigmented; eyes small but well developed.
- Rostrum with subparallel to gently convergent margins, lacking marginal spines; acumen short and indistinctly delimited basally.
- Areola 16 to 27.4 times longer than wide, constituting 31.1 to 35.8 percent of carapace length.
- Carapace without cervical spines; cervical tubercles very small or absent.
- Suborbital angle prominent and subacute.
- Mesial margin of chela palm with a row of nine to 11 tubercles.
- Hooks present only on the ischia of the fourth pereiopods.
- Inner ramus of uropod with two conspicuous spines projecting distally beyond the margin.
- First pleopods slightly asymmetrical, with a distinctive terminal complex including a broad, bladelike centrocaudal process.

**Sexual forms and types:**
- Holotypic male (form I), allotypic female, and morphotypic male (form II) are deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]] (Smithsonian Institution) under nos. 131205, 131206, and 131207, respectively.
- Paratypes: three males (form I), three males (second form), nine females, one juvenile male, and two juvenile females, all in the Smithsonian collection.

**Size:** The largest specimen examined is a female with a carapace length of 27.4 mm. The holotype (25.0 mm) is the largest first form male, and the smallest first form male measures 21.5 mm.

**Color:** Ground color is greenish brown, with the carapace and chelipeds darker than the abdomen and remaining pereiopods. A second color phase of yellowish tan and dark brown occurs in some individuals, though the pattern varies little.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Range:** [[Procambarus Fitzpatricki]] is known only from southern Mississippi between the Wolf and Pascagoula rivers. Documented localities include:
- [[Harrison County]]: the [[Type Locality]] (roadside ditch at the junction of State Route 67 and Woolmarket Road, just north of D'Iberville); a strawberry field near Bayou Bernard; and other collections from 1933 and 1968.
- [[George County]]: 3.2 mi N of Black Creek on State Route 57 (11 June 1968).
- [[Stone County]]: 8.9 mi W of Wiggins on State Route 26 at its junction with U.S. Highway 49 (12 June and 30 July 1968).

**Habitat and burrowing:** All specimens with detailed habitat data came from comparatively simple burrows that are subject to flooding after rains. The paper notes that it is unknown whether the species is largely confined to burrows, as is its neighbor [[Procambarus Hagenianus]].

**Life history:** First form males have been collected in February, June, and July. No females carrying eggs or young have been found.

**Relationships:** The species has closest affinities with the [[Gracilis Section]], including:
- [[Procambarus Gracilis]]
- [[Procambarus Hagenianus]]
- [[Procambarus Simulans]] (*P. simulans simulans*)
- [[Procambarus Simulans Regiomontanus]]
- [[Procambarus Tulanei]]

Relationship is most evident in the first pleopods and the [[Annulus Ventralis]]. The species shares with *P. hagenianus* alone the two uropod spines projecting beyond the inner ramus margin, and its suborbital angle aligns it with all Section members except *P. hagenianus*. It lacks the dense, matted setation on the third maxilliped ventral surface seen in *P. gracilis*.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described:** *Procambarus fitzpatricki* is a new crayfish from southern Mississippi, named for Dr. Joseph F. Fitzpatrick Jr., and it is the species previously listed as *Cambarus (Paracambarus) harnedi* (nomen nudum) by Lyle (1938).
2. **Unique male character:** Hooks occur only on the ischia of the fourth pereiopods, a trait previously known in the subgenus *Paracambarus* only from a limited group of Mexican *Procambarus* species.
3. **Uropod spines:** The inner ramus of the uropod carries two conspicuous distal spines. Only *P. hagenianus* shares this character.
4. **Taxonomic placement:** The species belongs to the [[Gracilis Section]] of *Procambarus*, not the two crayfishes currently assigned to *Paracambarus*, which is a change from Lyle's earlier assumption.
5. **Unresolved pleopod homology:** One terminal element of the first pleopod is interpreted as the centrocaudal process, though it resembles the caudal process of Gracilis Section relatives. Its sperm groove passes between it and the centrocephalic process. The author argues that the caudal element is represented by a narrow ridge at the base of the centrocaudal process, since no species in the subfamily is known to lack the centrocaudal element.
6. **Type specimens:** Holotype, allotype, and morphotype are deposited at the [[National Museum of Natural History]] under nos. 131205 to 131207, with paratypes in the same collection.
7. **Known distribution:** The species is confined to southern Mississippi between the Wolf and Pascagoula rivers, with records from Harrison, George, and Stone counties.
8. **Reproductive data gap:** Form I males are known from February, June, and July, but no ovigerous females have been recorded.
9. **Habitat:** Known specimens come from simple, flood-prone burrows. Whether the species is restricted to burrows remains unknown.
