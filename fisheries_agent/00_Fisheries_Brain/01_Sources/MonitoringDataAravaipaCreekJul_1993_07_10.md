## Executive Summary

This monitoring report from Arizona State University (W.L. Minckley, R. Timmons et al.) documents a fish community survey of [[Aravaipa Creek]], a perennial desert stream in the Gila River drainage of Arizona, conducted 6–8 July 1993. Six sites spanning the western ("sycamore tree", White's Ranch, Wood's Ranch) and eastern (Turkey Creek, the Chimney, TNC Guesthouse) reaches of the creek were sampled using seines (1/8" mesh 4-ft and 1/4" mesh 15-ft) over standardized 200 m reaches. The survey confirms a fish assemblage overwhelmingly dominated by native Sonoran Desert fishes — [[Longfin Dace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Spikedace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Sonora Sucker]] — with near-complete absence of invasive nonnative species ([[Common Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Western Mosquitofish]]) except for two [[Yellow Bullhead]] specimens at one site. The data reflect a rare, largely intact native fish community, a notable conservation benchmark for southwestern desert streams. Incidental observations of leopard frogs (*Rana pipiens* complex) along streamside margins were also recorded.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological data were limited in this monitoring note, but the following size/age-class observations were recorded during identification:

- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*): Young-of-year individuals noted at White's Ranch (2 individuals); juveniles and adults documented at Wood's Ranch (4 adults, 1 juvenile) and the Chimney (6 adults).
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Adults with breeding "red" coloration noted at the sycamore tree site (2 adults, 1 with red) and the Chimney (1 adult with red); a single adult recorded at the Chimney apart from the reddened individual.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Catostomus clarki*): One adult identified at the sycamore tree site.
- **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** (*Ameiurus natalis*): Both individuals collected at the sycamore tree site were small (<6 cm), suggesting recent recruitment or a marginal/incidental population.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): Presence of "red" breeding coloration noted in an adult at the Chimney site, consistent with reproductive condition during summer sampling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure**: Gravel substrate predominated across nearly all sampled reaches; large cobble and sand were secondary substrate types. The TNC Guesthouse reach was atypical due to an artificial earthen berm creating an impounded pool with silt substrate and dense *Cladophora* algal growth — a distinct mesohabitat compared to the free-flowing riffle/run/pool mosaic elsewhere.
- **Sampling effort by mesohabitat**: Runs were the dominant habitat type sampled at every site (16–41 hauls per site), followed by riffles (3–17 hauls) and pools (2–7 hauls), reflecting the natural channel morphology of Aravaipa Creek as a run-dominated desert stream.
- **Native fish dominance**: The near-total absence of nonnative predatory/competitive species (*Micropterus salmoides*, *Lepomis cyanellus*, *Cyprinus carpio*, *Cyprinella lutrensis*, *Pimephales promelas*, *Ameiurus melas*, *Gambusia affinis*) across all six sites indicates Aravaipa Creek functions as a refugium for the native Gila River fish fauna, likely due to its protected status (The Nature Conservancy preserve) and relative habitat integrity.
- **Co-occurring herpetofauna**: Ranid frogs of the *Rana pipiens* complex were observed along streamside margins at all sites except the sycamore tree, typically associated with bankside grass or watercress — indicating riparian vegetation plays a role in supporting associated amphibian populations alongside the fish community.
- **Spatial distribution gradient**: [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Roundtail Chub]] abundances were notably higher at the eastern sites (Turkey Creek, the Chimney) compared to the western sites, while [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) was abundant at nearly every site, indicating it is the most widespread and dominant species in the system.

## Key Findings

- **Six sites sampled**: Sycamore Tree (West End), White's Ranch, Wood's Ranch, Turkey Creek (East End), the Chimney, and TNC Guesthouse — spanning Pinal and Graham Counties, AZ.
- **Total seine hauls** ranged from 32–57 per site (232 total across all sites), split among run, riffle, and pool mesohabitats.
- **Native species recorded at every site**: *Agosia chrysogaster* (Longfin Dace), *Tiaroga cobitis* (Loach Minnow), *Catostomus insignis* (Sonora Sucker), and *Catostomus clarki* (Desert Sucker) were present at all six locations.
- **Meda fulgida (Spikedace)** showed strong abundance at Turkey Creek (375 individuals) and the Chimney (155) and TNC Guesthouse (208, "numerous adults"), suggesting these eastern reaches support a robust population of this federally listed species.
- **Gila robusta (Roundtail Chub)** was absent at the sycamore tree site (0 individuals) but present at all other sites, peaking at Turkey Creek (29 individuals).
- **Rhinichthys osculus (Speckled Dace)** was rare, recorded only at the Chimney (34 individuals, including one breeding adult); absent from all other sites.
- **Nonnative species were virtually absent**: *Cyprinus carpio*, *Cyprinella lutrensis*, *Pimephales promelas*, *Ameiurus melas*, *Lepomis cyanellus*, *Micropterus salmoides*, and *Gambusia affinis* each recorded zero individuals at every single site sampled.
- **Only nonnative catch**: Two *Ameiurus natalis* (Yellow Bullhead), both juveniles (<6 cm), captured solely at the sycamore tree site — indicating minimal establishment of this species in the system at the time of sampling.
- **Incidental amphibian observations**: *Rana pipiens* complex frogs were observed at 5/6 sites; at least 15 individuals seen at the three uppermost (eastern) sites, with 2 positively identified in-hand at the East End and 5 unconfirmed individuals at the West End.
- **Habitat anomaly**: The TNC Guesthouse reach's impounded, silt-bottomed pool (created by an earthen berm) supported a dense *Cladophora* mat, differing markedly from the gravel/cobble-dominated substrate found elsewhere in the creek.
