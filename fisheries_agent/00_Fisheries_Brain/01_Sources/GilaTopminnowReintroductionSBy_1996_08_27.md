## Executive Summary
This document is a 1996 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Pinetop, AZ) to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University), reporting on a reintroduction effort of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) into the renovated spring system designated "S2" at [[Bylas Springs]], Arizona. The project transferred captive-reared fish of "S2 lineage" from an ASU-maintained captive population back into their historic habitat following stream course renovation. Early post-release monitoring indicated a successful reintroduction with no observed mortality and good fish health at follow-up.

## Background & Description
- No detailed morphological measurements are provided in this source; the document is administrative/monitoring correspondence rather than a morphological study.
- Sex ratio of translocated individuals: 39 adults total — 8 males and 31 females (~1:3.9 male-to-female ratio).
- One individual noted as a "presumably older pregnant female" showing signs of stress post-transport, consistent with the species' [[Livebearing Reproduction]] strategy typical of Poeciliidae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Desert spring/stream system (Bylas Springs, part of the [[Gila River Basin]] drainage), previously subjected to habitat renovation, with new cement troughs installed along the stream course to support reintroduction.
- **Population source:** Captive lineage maintained at [[Arizona State University]], originally derived from S2 stock, indicating an established captive brood-stock/refuge population strategy for conservation of this imperiled species.
- **Conservation context:** The reintroduction is part of broader recovery efforts for the [[Gila Topminnow]], a federally listed endangered fish native to the Gila River drainage of Arizona and Sonora, Mexico, whose wild populations have been decimated by habitat loss and introduction of non-native competitors/predators (e.g., mosquitofish, *Gambusia affinis*).
- **Companion site:** A second site, "S1," is referenced as a candidate for a future translocation/reintroduction and potential establishment of an additional captive population at ASU, suggesting a multi-site metapopulation management strategy typical of [[Endangered Fish Species Recovery]] programs in the American Southwest.

## Key Findings
- **Translocation event:** 39 adult Gila topminnow (8 males, 31 females) of S2 lineage were collected from the ASU captive population on 2 August 1996 and transported directly to the S2 renovation site.
- **Release protocol:** Fish were tempered for one hour prior to release into the first and second cement troughs installed during stream renovation — a standard acclimation practice to reduce transport/temperature stress.
- **Immediate outcome:** No mortalities were recorded during transport and release; only one older pregnant female displayed stress symptoms.
- **Follow-up monitoring:** A health check on 6 August 1996 (4 days post-release) confirmed the topminnow population was in good health.
- **Ongoing monitoring commitment:** Arizona Fishery Resources Office (FRO) committed to active monitoring of the S2 system over the following year, with periodic reporting to Dr. Minckley on reintroduction success/failure.
- **Future plans:** Potential future collection of individuals from site "S1" was proposed, along with possible establishment of a new captive population at ASU, contingent on stakeholder interest and completion of [[NEPA Documentation]] requirements.
- **Programmatic goal:** The report being developed was intended to address and diffuse uneasy speculation regarding the S2 renovation, indicating some public or scientific community concern about the renovation/reintroduction approach.
