# Implementing Agreement — El Coronado Ranch HCP (West Turkey Creek, Arizona)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This document is a **draft Implementing Agreement (1997)** supporting a Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP) for the **El Coronado Ranch** in West Turkey Creek, Cochise County, Arizona. It establishes a legal and cooperative framework among the ranch owners (Joe and Valer Austin), the **USDA Forest Service**, the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service)**, and the **Arizona Game and Fish Department/Commission** to conserve three fish taxa associated with the Rio Yaqui drainage: the endangered **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*), the threatened **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*), and the sensitive, unlisted **[[Yaqui Form of Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*). The agreement authorizes an incidental take permit under **Section 10(a)(1)(B)** of the Endangered Species Act (ESA), in exchange for ranch operational commitments to habitat maintenance, water management, monitoring, and non-native species control across a **6,154 ha (15,204 ac)** permit area spanning private and Coronado National Forest lands.

## Background & Description

- **Listing status (Federal Register, August 31, 1984)**:
  - **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*) — listed **Endangered**, with designated critical habitat.
  - **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*) — listed **Threatened**, with designated critical habitat.
  - **[[Yaqui Form of Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — **not federally listed**, but treated as a Forest Service–sensitive species and included as a "Plan Species."
- **Taxonomic affiliations**:
  - Yaqui Chub and Longfin Dace: family **Cyprinidae** (minnows).
  - Yaqui Catfish: family **Ictaluridae** (North American catfishes), notable as one of the few catfish species native to the desert Southwest/Rio Yaqui basin outside the Mississippi-associated ictalurid radiation.
- **Site context**: El Coronado Ranch (ECR) occupies portions of the **West Turkey Creek watershed**, a tributary system within the upper Rio Yaqui basin (an interior drainage flowing into Sonora, Mexico). The ranch contains extant populations of Yaqui Chub and Yaqui longfin dace, and potential (but not confirmed extant) habitat for Yaqui Catfish.
- **Permit Area**: 778 ha (1,920 ac) private land + 5,376 ha (13,284 ac) of Coronado National Forest grazing allotment (T17S–T18S, R29E–R30E, Cochise County, AZ).
- **Legal mechanism**: HCP developed under **ESA Section 10(a)(2)(A)**, enabling issuance of an incidental take permit (Section 10(a)(1)(B)) covering ranching/grazing/water-management operations that could otherwise harm listed species or their habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Small desert perennial/intermittent stream (West Turkey Creek) plus artificial/managed **ponds** on private ranch land that serve as refugia and habitat for Plan Species — a common conservation strategy for imperiled Rio Yaqui basin fishes threatened by habitat desiccation and non-native species.
- **Primary environmental stressors addressed**:
  - **Water diversion and supply variability** — balancing water allocation between the creek and ranch ponds to sustain fish populations.
  - **Livestock grazing impacts** on watershed and riparian/native fish habitat.
  - **Non-native/non-indigenous predators and competitors** — explicit management goal to eliminate/avoid reintroduction of non-native aquatic species (with limited exception for non-reproducing individuals).
  - **Drought and invasion by non-native species** — flagged as key "emergency" scenarios requiring specific monitoring-plan protocols.
- **Watershed-scale cooperation**: Because West Turkey Creek crosses both private (ECR) and Coronado National Forest lands, conservation requires coordinated management under the Forest Service's **Coordinated Land Management Plan for the West Turkey Creek Allotment**.
- **Ecological role of ponds**: Ranch-maintained ponds function as translocation/refuge sites for Plan Species, with provisions for agency-assisted translocation, restocking, relocation, and reintroduction as management tools.

## Key Findings

- **Species-specific status**:
  - Yaqui Chub — confirmed extant population on ECR; Endangered with critical habitat.
  - Yaqui Catfish — no confirmed population, but ECR contains potential habitat; Threatened with critical habitat; a priority for future restocking/reintroduction efforts.
  - Yaqui longfin dace — confirmed extant population; unlisted but conservation-managed under the same plan.
- **Conservation program elements** (via HCP & Agreement):
  1. Maintenance of water-delivery infrastructure and pond systems to buffer variable creek flow.
  2. Active exclusion of non-indigenous aquatic predators/competitors.
  3. Grazing management to minimize impacts on native fish habitat.
  4. Cooperative, multi-agency **monitoring plan** (co-led by USFWS and Arizona Game & Fish Department, reviewed by Forest Service and Permittee) covering population and habitat monitoring, with explicit drought/invasive-species contingency procedures.
  5. Structured reporting schedule: **annual reports for first 5 years**, **triennial for next 15 years**, then **every 5 years** for the remainder of the 25-year permit term, plus **5-year cumulative summary reports** following full linear surveys of the West Turkey Creek system.
  6. Provisions for agency-assisted **restocking, translocation, relocation, and reintroduction** of Plan Species, requiring unanimous approval among Parties.
  7. Removal projects for non-indigenous aquatic species, co-led by USFWS and Arizona Game & Fish Department.
- **Regulatory assurances**: Incorporates the **"No Surprises" policy** (50 CFR; Federal Register 62(103):29091–29098, May 29, 1997) — once the Permittee complies with HCP terms, no additional land-use restrictions or financial mitigation can be imposed absent "unforeseen circumstances" (defined as significant, unanticipated adverse changes in species status, habitat, or project impacts).
- **Permit term**: 25 years from Service issuance of the Section 10(a)(1)(B) Permit, though habitat protections for Plan Species are intended to extend beyond the formal term, given the permanence of habitat modification and species conservation goals.
- **Institutional roles**:
  - **Permittee (ECR/Austins)**: funds and implements on-the-ground water/pond management, grazing controls, monitoring cost-share (up to $500/year cap when agencies cannot perform monitoring), and reporting.
  - **USFWS**: technical assistance, co-lead on monitoring plan and species reintroduction/removal projects, provides San Bernardino NWR and Arizona Ecological Services Field Office personnel.
  - **Arizona Game and Fish Department**: co-lead with USFWS on monitoring and species management actions.
  - **USDA Forest Service**: implements the Coordinated Land Management Plan for the allotment, assists with Forest System lands monitoring/reintroduction.
- **Broader significance**: This HCP represents an early (1997) example of a **multi-species, cross-jurisdictional private-public conservation agreement** for imperiled Rio Yaqui basin ichthyofauna, integrating ranching operations with endangered species recovery — relevant to broader conservation strategies for desert fishes such as [[Gila Chub]] and other Sonoran Desert cyprinids and ictalurids facing habitat fragmentation and non-native species invasion.
