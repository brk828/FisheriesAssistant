# Humpback Chub Collection Localities in the Colorado River System

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive summary of collecting localities for the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) from the [[Colorado River]] and selected tributaries in [[Coconino County]] and [[Mohave County]], Arizona, spanning from archaeological remains (4000 years B.P.) through 1979. Collection records document the species' distribution from [[Lake Powell]] and [[Glen Canyon Dam]] downstream through the [[Grand Canyon]] to [[Havasu Creek]], with particular concentration at the confluence of the [[Little Colorado River]]. Data sources include the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[National Park Service]], academic researchers, and commercial river runners.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is a cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] basin. This compilation documents historical and contemporary collection localities across a 276.3 river mile stretch of the Colorado River system. The earliest record comes from archaeological remains at [[Stanton's Cave]] dating to approximately 4000 years before present, indicating long-term presence of the species in the region.

Key collecting areas include the tailwaters of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], [[Lees Ferry]], multiple sites throughout the [[Grand Canyon]] (River Miles 1-276.3), [[Bright Angel Creek]], the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence and upstream reaches, and [[Havasu Creek]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Collection localities span diverse habitat types within the [[Colorado River]] system:

- **Impounded waters**: [[Lake Powell]] (1961-1963)
- **Dam tailwaters**: Immediate downstream areas of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], including sites 100 yards below the dam (winter 1966, August 1969, July 1967, August 1970)
- **Mainstem river**: Multiple sites from River Mile 1 through River Mile 276.3
- **Tributary confluences**: Particularly the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence (multiple collections 1977-1979), [[Bright Angel Creek]] (June 1944, June 1968, 1970-1976), [[Shinumo Creek]] (1973), and [[Havasu Creek]] (Summer 1979)
- **Upstream tributary reaches**: Eight miles and six miles upstream in the [[Little Colorado River]]

The temporal span of collections (1944-1979, plus archaeological material) provides baseline distribution data across periods of major hydrological modification, particularly following construction of [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

## Key Findings

**Distribution Pattern**: [[Humpback Chub]] collections documented throughout the [[Colorado River]] corridor from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] through the [[Grand Canyon]], with concentrated sampling at tributary confluences, particularly the [[Little Colorado River]].

**Temporal Coverage**: Collections span from June 1944 through October 1979, with intensive sampling periods during 1970-1976 (multiple river miles) and 1977-1979 (focused on [[Little Colorado River]] confluence).

**Key Localities**:
- [[Stanton's Cave]]: Archaeological remains (4000 years B.P.)
- [[Glen Canyon Dam]] area: 1961-1970
- [[Lees Ferry]]: 1962-1970
- [[Grand Canyon]] mainstem: River Miles 1-276.3 (1968-1978)
- [[Little Colorado River]] confluence: Intensive sampling 1977-1979 (September 1977, November 1977, January 1978, May 1978, June 1978, July 1978, August 1978, October 1978, April 1979, July 1979, October 1979)
- [[Bright Angel Creek]]: June 1944, June 1968, 1970-1976

**Data Sources**: Multiple agencies and researchers contributed records including [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], Holden and Stalnaker, Suttkus and colleagues, Miller and Smith (1967), [[National Park Service]] Grand Canyon, Carothers and Minckley, river monitoring programs, and commercial boatmen reports.

**Specific River Mile Documentation**: Collections recorded at River Miles 19.5, 27.5, 31, 32, 33, 44, 55, 61.5, 63, 69, 70, 71, 72, 108, 108.7, 132, 179.1, 194, and 276.3.
