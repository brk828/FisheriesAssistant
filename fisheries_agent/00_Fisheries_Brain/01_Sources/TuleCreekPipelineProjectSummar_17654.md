# Tule Creek Pipeline Project Summary Report

#ichthyology #conservation #Poeciliidae #riparian-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Land Management (BLM) report documents the construction of a pipeline system at [[Tule Creek]] in Yavapai County, Arizona, to provide livestock water outside a riparian exclosure while protecting habitat for the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis) and [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (Rana yavapaiensis). The project was conducted October 1-3, 1991, in compliance with Biological Opinion 2-21-91-F-469. The pipeline successfully delivered water to troughs beyond the exclosure boundary, allowing the fence to remain closed and protecting the riparian habitat and topminnow population.

## Background & Description

[[Tule Creek]] supports a population of [[Gila Topminnow]], a federally protected species. In spring 1991, BLM and the Arizona Game and Fish Department constructed a fence exclosure to eliminate livestock and burro use and limit vehicle access to the creek, thereby improving riparian conditions. However, following exclosure completion on May 2, 1991, water no longer flowed beyond the fence-line. The fence was opened June 19, 1991, to provide livestock access to water as guaranteed to the grazing permittee. To resolve this conflict between conservation and grazing needs, a permanent water source outside the exclosure was required.

The pipeline system consisted of a 250 ft. trench (1.5 ft. deep, 1 ft. wide) extending from approximately 220 ft. upstream of the first road crossing to 20 ft. downstream. A polyethylene pipe (1.25 in. inside diameter) with an approximate 6 ft. length of well screen at the upstream end was placed in the trench adjacent to the east side of the creek. The well screen was covered with gravel and cobble to facilitate percolation. An additional 385 ft. of unburied pipeline extended to water troughs, including a 30 ft. section suspended above the creek with steel "T" posts. Two identical metal water troughs (50 gallons each) were installed on a bench on the west side of the creek, approximately 20 ft. beyond the fence-line, connected end-to-end with float valve regulation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Tule Creek]] represents critical riparian habitat in the arid Arizona landscape. The site supports [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis), a federally endangered species, and [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (Rana yavapaiensis). The riparian vegetation includes bulrush communities. The project area is located in Yavapai County, T. 8N., R. 1 E., sec. 28, SW¼.

The exclosure was designed to protect riparian habitat from degradation by livestock and feral burros while maintaining water availability for permitted grazing operations downstream. The project demonstrates an approach to balancing conservation objectives with traditional land uses in the western United States.

## Key Findings

**Construction Activities (October 1-3, 1991):**
- Pipeline construction was completed using trenching methods with minimal disturbance to the stream channel
- No mortality of [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis) was observed during construction activities
- No mortality of [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (Rana yavapaiensis) was observed during construction
- No garter snakes (Thamnophis sp.) were observed during construction

**Post-Construction Monitoring (November 6, 1991):**
- Flow of water to troughs was confirmed on October 3, 1991
- The downstream boundary fence was successfully closed following pipeline completion
- Site inspection on November 6, 1991, showed the pipeline trench recovering well
- Young bulrush shoots were emerging along the margin of the trench
- Five reference locations along the stream course were photographed for monitoring

**Project Outcomes:**
- The pipeline successfully provides livestock water outside the exclosure
- The riparian exclosure can now function as intended to protect [[Gila Topminnow]] habitat
- The project complies with Terms and Conditions for Biological Opinion 2-21-91-F-469
- The agreement between BLM and the grazing permittee was fulfilled
