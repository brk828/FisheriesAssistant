## Ecological & Environmental Context

The chapter frames desert waters as a distinct category of aquatic habitat. Its physical and chemical sections address how lakes are supplied with water, how their basins form, how salts accumulate and are lost, and how temperature and density stratification and light penetration shape these systems. The biological sections then move from primary production through plankton and benthos to fish, and compare saline waters with ephemeral pools and desert streams. The Isaiah epigraph about rivers in the desert reflects the central theme: life depends on episodic and marginal water.

The reference list points to the regional scope of the work. It cites studies from the western United States (e.g., closed-basin lakes and their tributaries), Israel (temporary rain pools and Lake Tiberias), Australia (lake typology and inland fauna), East Africa, and the Soviet Union. Recurring topics include plant productivity comparisons, plankton copepod communities, irrigation water chemistry, and biochemical heating in lake mud.
