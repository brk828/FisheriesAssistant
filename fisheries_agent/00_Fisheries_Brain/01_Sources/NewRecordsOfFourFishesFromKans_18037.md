# New Records of Four Fishes from Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #percidae

## Executive Summary

This short faunal note reports new Kansas occurrences of four fish species whose state records were scarce or poorly documented: [[Notropis Illecebrosus]] (silverstripe shiner), [[Notropis Dorsalis]] (bigmouth shiner), [[Noturus Gyrinus]] (tadpole madtom), and [[Etheostoma Stigmaeum]] (speckled darter). Specimens are deposited in the [[University Of Kansas Museum Of Natural History]] (KU), the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (UMMZ), and formerly the Museum of Comparative Zoology (MCZ). The authors conclude that the silverstripe and bigmouth shiners are probably confined to the [[Missouri River]] system in the state, the tadpole madtom appears confined to the [[Osage River System]], and the scarcity of earlier records for the silverstripe shiner likely reflects inadequate collecting rather than rarity.

## Background & Description

The paper is a taxonomic and distributional record compiled by Frank B. Cross and W. L. Minckley of the University of Kansas, published in the *Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science* (Vol. 61, No. 1, 1958). Diagnostic characters are drawn from [[Bailey Winn And Smith 1954]], [[Bailey 1956]], and [[Moore 1957]]. Dr. Reeve M. Bailey of the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology reviewed the manuscript and supplied data for some specimens.

**Silverstripe shiner (*Notropis illecebrosus*).** Diagnosed by pharyngeal teeth usually 1 or 2, 4-4, 2 or 1; anal rays usually 9; a high dorsal fin; and a silvery lateral stripe. Its range is given by Moore as the Mississippi River and large silty tributaries.

**Bigmouth shiner (*Notropis dorsalis*).** Diagnosed by anal rays usually 8, a sub-inferior nearly horizontal mouth, and crowded predorsal scale-rows. It resembles [[Notropis Deliciosus]], which usually has 7 anal rays and different pharyngeal teeth.

**Tadpole madtom (*Noturus gyrinus*).** Diagnosed by a short, stout body, a large fleshy head, anal rays usually 14, and a conspicuous dark mid-lateral line. Its pectoral spine has a posterior groove.

**Speckled darter (*Etheostoma stigmaeum*).** Diagnosed by protractile premaxillae, 2 anal spines, dorsal spines X to XII, and an incomplete lateral line. The name *Etheostoma saxatile* was placed in synonymy with this species by [[Bailey Winn And Smith 1954]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Missouri River and lower Kansas River:** The silverstripe shiner was seined from strong current over sand bottom, alongside [[Notropis Percobromus]], which has 11 anal rays. Collections were made at small sandbars downstream of bank-protection log structures. The authors note that navigational improvements are removing sandbars and confining the river to a narrow, deep, swift channel, which makes collecting harder.
- **Little Walnut Creek and the Kansas River:** The bigmouth shiner was taken in slowly flowing, shallow water over sand in the Kansas River during unusually low flow. In Little Walnut Creek, it occurred in mud-bottomed pools about 400 yards above the Missouri River confluence. It was absent upstream, where pools had rubble or gravel bottoms and steep gradient.
- **Oxbows and strip-mine water (Osage system):** The tadpole madtom was taken from shallow, muddy oxbows within the [[Marais Des Cygnes Waterfowl Refuge]] and from a water-filled [[Strip-Coal Mine]] in Bourbon County that is sometimes connected to the Little Osage River. One specimen came from a small, deep, muddy pool farther upstream in the Marais des Cygnes basin.
- **Spring River riffle:** Speckled darters were collected in a shallow mainstream riffle (mostly under one foot deep) with clean, rounded gravel of uniform size.

## Key Findings

**1. Silverstripe shiner (*Notropis illecebrosus*)**
- Two specimens (KU 3846), standard lengths 33.0 and 32.5 mm, were collected from the [[Missouri River]] in Atchison County on 24 August 1957 by W. L. Minckley and A. L. Metcalf.
- Both specimens had 9 anal rays and 15 predorsal scale rows.
- The northernmost previously known record is a single specimen from Sioux City, Iowa (S. E. Meek, 1890), which Bailey questioned. A further specimen (UMMZ 87099, formerly MCZ 18432) is labeled from the Missouri River at Leavenworth, received in 1871 from an unknown collector.
- The authors conclude that the species probably occurs in Kansas only in the Missouri River and possibly the lower Kansas River, and that its scarcity reflects limited collecting.

**2. Bigmouth shiner (*Notropis dorsalis*)**
- One specimen (KU 3479) came from the [[Kansas River]] in Douglas County (F. B. Cross, 20 April 1955).
- Eleven specimens (KU 3852 and UMMZ 173141) came from [[Little Walnut Creek]] in Atchison County (24 August 1957).
- For ten Kansas specimens (SL 30.6 to 35.2 mm), the averages were: head length into SL 3.4 (3.3 to 3.5); anal rays 7.8 (7 to 8); lateral-line scales 34.7 (33 to 36); predorsal scale rows 18.2 (17 to 20). Two specimens (22.5 and 51.0 mm SL) were excluded from these averages.
- The authors expect the species to be restricted to the Missouri River, lower Kansas River, and their Kansas tributaries.

**3. Tadpole madtom (*Noturus gyrinus*)**
- Records consist of three specimens (KU 3735 and UMMZ 171770) from a Marais des Cygnes oxbow in Linn County (25 May 1956); three specimens (KU 3864) from another Linn County oxbow (12 June 1956); two specimens from the Bourbon County strip-coal mine (2 November 1956, held in C. Burner's private collection); and one additional specimen (KU 3890) from Franklin County (13 September 1957, J. E. Deacon).
- Graham (1885) listed the species for Kansas without locality data. These specimens are therefore considered the first definite state record.
- In Kansas the species appears confined to the Osage River System.

**4. Speckled darter (*Etheostoma stigmaeum*)**
- Collections include one specimen (KU 3587) from [[Shoal Creek]] in Cherokee County and 20 specimens (KU 3597 and UMMZ 171769) from the [[Spring River]] in Cherokee County (6 and 7 April 1956, F. B. Cross).
- For Spring River specimens (SL 36.0 to 39.0 mm): lateral-line scales 47.8 (45 to 53), of which 27.3 (23 to 31) were pored; scale rows around the caudal peduncle 16.4 (15 to 18); dorsal soft rays 11.3 (10 to 12); anal soft rays 8.5 (8 to 9). Dorsal spines were XI in 12 specimens and XII in 8.
- The description of nuptial male coloration (from specimens preserved in formalin) notes six dark dorsal saddles, 9 to 11 light blue mid-lateral spots, and a dark spinous dorsal fin with orange and black bands.

**Overall conclusion:** The paper adds definitive or expanded Kansas records for four species, notes the scarcity of prior documentation for three of them, and identifies probable drainage restrictions that future collecting should test. Specimens are catalogued and cited by museum number for verification.
