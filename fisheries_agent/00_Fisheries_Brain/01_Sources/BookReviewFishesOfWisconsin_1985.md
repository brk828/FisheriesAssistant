## Executive Summary

This document is a 1985 book review published in *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*, evaluating George C. Becker's *Fishes of Wisconsin* (1983, University of Wisconsin Press, 1,052 pages). The reviewer, E. J. Crossman of the [[Royal Ontario Museum]], situates the work as filling a significant gap in regional ichthyological literature, since prior resources on [[Wisconsin Fish Fauna]] were outdated (Greene's 1935 *The Distribution of Wisconsin Fishes*) or mistitled/misfocused (Eddy and Surber's 1947 and Eddy and Underhill's 1974 *Northern Fishes*, which primarily covered Minnesota). The review assesses the book's structure, scientific rigor, illustrative quality, and utility to diverse audiences including anglers, naturalists, resource managers, and research scientists.

## Background & Description

While the source is a book review rather than a primary morphological study, it describes the *treatment* of morphological and taxonomic data within the reviewed work:

- **Taxonomic keys**: The book includes keys to families and to individual species within families, extensively illustrated with line drawings depicting representative specimens and diagnostic features.
- **Key complexity**: Keys incorporate numerous characters, including some drawn from specialist keys published elsewhere, enhancing identification accuracy.
- **Hybrid documentation**: Notably, the key to [[Sunfishes]] (Centrarchidae) includes descriptions of hybrids commonly observed in Wisconsin waters — a detail praised as unusual and valuable for field identification.
- **Illustrations**: 162 color illustrations are grouped before the species accounts, though reviewer notes uneven quality due to a mix of source types (paintings, aquarium photographs, and preserved specimen photos). Black-and-white illustrations within individual species accounts are noted as more visually consistent, though small in size with detail loss against black backgrounds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The reviewed work addresses ecological and biogeographic dimensions of Wisconsin's fish fauna comprehensively:

- **Distribution mapping**: Each species account includes a spot-distribution map specific to Wisconsin (with historical occurrence data) plus a North American range inset map, allowing readers to contextualize state-level distribution within continental range.
- **Supplementary cartography**: Additional maps detail collecting effort/coverage, major streams, county boundaries, drainage basins, and glacial geomorphological features relevant to fish distribution — linking [[Glacial Geomorphology]] to aquatic habitat structuring.
- **Habitat and status accounts**: Individual species accounts cover distribution status and habitat characteristics specific to Wisconsin waters.
- **Biological and ecological depth**: Biological notes extend well beyond Wisconsin's borders, incorporating chromosome numbers, physiological parameters, and community structure data — an unusually thorough scope for a state faunal text.
- **Conservation status**: Introductory sections address exotic, endangered, and extirpated species within the state, alongside parasite information and management-relevant angling pressure data by region.

## Key Findings

- The book represents the first comprehensive, standalone treatment of [[Wisconsin Fish Fauna]] since Greene's 1935 publication, correcting the prior reliance on Minnesota-focused literature mislabeled as regional ("northern") references.
- Approximately **1,500 references** are cited, reflecting extensive literature synthesis that the reviewer found to be of high quality upon checking accounts for personally familiar species.
- The book's glossary is positioned at the beginning (atypical for the genre), improving accessibility for readers needing foundational terminology before diving into species accounts.
- The index is highlighted as **excellent**, cross-referencing both scientific and common names (including subspecies-level entries) and largely avoiding frustrating "see"-type redirections — though page-specific subject searches still require cross-referencing common name entries.
- Species accounts follow a consistent, detailed template: spot map, North American inset, etymology, description, distribution/status/habitat, biology, importance/management, and systematic notes.
- The reviewer's principal criticisms are: (1) absence of a simple taxonomically ordered species checklist near the book's start, (2) uneven quality of color plates due to mixed-media sourcing, (3) small size and detail loss in black-and-white photographic illustrations, and (4) the inherent lag between literature cutoff and publication date common to large reference works.
- Overall assessment: the book is deemed a **remarkable synthesis** of regional ichthyological data with utility extending well beyond Wisconsin's borders, limited primarily by its size (1,052 pages) and cost ($75.00, 1983 USD).
- The dust jacket features an original painting by [[Virgil Beck]], noted by the reviewer as unlikely to physically endure alongside the book's lifespan.
