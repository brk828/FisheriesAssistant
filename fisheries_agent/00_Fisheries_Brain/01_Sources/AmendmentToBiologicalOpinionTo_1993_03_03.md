# Amendment to Biological Opinion: Arizona Game and Fish Collecting Permit (Humpback Chub & Razorback Sucker)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ConservationPolicy #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary
This document is an inter-agency memorandum from the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), dated March 3, 1993. It addresses a procedural and scientific dispute regarding an amendment to a Biological Opinion governing the Arizona Game and Fish Department's (AGFD) permit to collect two federally endangered fish species — [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] — in [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The core issue concerns an unreviewed modification allowing the "take" (collection) of individuals up to 150 mm in total length, purportedly justified by AGFD age-and-growth data suggesting young-of-year (YOY) fish reach this size. GCES disputes this finding, noting it contradicts other research conducted in the [[Colorado River]] mainstem, and raises concerns about the lack of scientific coordination through the GCES Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT).

## Background & Description
While this document is administrative rather than a direct morphological study, it centers on a critical size-based classification dispute relevant to fish life-history staging:

- **Disputed Trait:** Total length (TL) as a proxy for age-class designation (young-of-year vs. juvenile/subadult).
- **Contested Threshold:** AGFD study findings reportedly indicate that young-of-year [[Humpback Chub]] and/or [[Razorback Sucker]] can attain a total length of up to **150 mm**.
- **Conflicting Data:** GCES states this finding is inconsistent with age-and-growth relationships established by other research entities studying the same [[Colorado River]] mainstem population, implying a much smaller expected YOY maximum size in comparable studies.
- **Scientific Implication:** The size threshold used to define "young-of-year" directly determines which size classes of fish are eligible for collection (take) under the permit, making this a consequential taxonomic/demographic boundary rather than a purely administrative detail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Both species inhabit the [[Colorado River]] mainstem and [[Little Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon National Park]], a system heavily influenced by regulated flows from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] (the subject of the broader GCES research program).
- **Population Status:** Both the [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are referenced as species with "a limited population," consistent with their federal endangered status, underscoring the sensitivity of any collection/take activity.
- **Research Context:** The document references ongoing endangered species research coordinated by GCES-ACT, involving multiple entities (AGFD, USFWS, Arizona State University, Bio-West, Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit) studying population dynamics, age, and growth in the same study area.
- **Study Area Sensitivity:** GCES specifically flags concern that removing larger-bodied individuals (up to 150 mm, if actually juveniles rather than YOY) from the population could have downstream effects on concurrent research examining population structure in the same reach of river.

## Key Findings
- A second amendment to the Biological Opinion permitted AGFD to collect (take) [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] individuals up to 150 mm in total length in Grand Canyon National Park.
- This modification was **not reviewed or discussed** at GCES Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT) meetings, breaking established protocol for interagency scientific coordination.
- GCES states it is **unaware of any AGFD study documentation** substantiating the claim that young-of-year fish reach 150 mm TL, and formally requests access to this data if it exists.
- The claimed 150 mm YOY threshold **conflicts with findings from other research programs** active in the Colorado River mainstem, raising concern about inconsistent age/growth interpretations across concurrent studies.
- GCES warns that permitting collection of this larger size class could **negatively impact concurrent endangered species research** occurring in the same study area, due to unintended removal of individuals that may not actually be young-of-year.
- The memorandum underscores the importance of transparent, coordinated review processes for permit modifications affecting [[Endangered Species Act]]-listed aquatic species, particularly when methodological changes (e.g., size-based take criteria) have cascading effects on multi-agency research programs.
- Distribution list confirms broad interagency involvement, including [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona State University]], and [[Bio-West]] — reflecting the collaborative but contested nature of endangered species management in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the Colorado River.
