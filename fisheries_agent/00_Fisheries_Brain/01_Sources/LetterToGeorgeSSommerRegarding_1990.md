## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) to Mr. George S. Sommer of Utah B.A.S.S., addressing a proposal by the state of Utah to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Powell]] to support the sport fishery. Rather than presenting original morphological or ecological research, the letter serves as a regulatory risk assessment, flagging concerns that smelt are anticipated to migrate beyond the reservoir into the broader [[Colorado River Basin]]. The Service raises the potential for conflict with the [[Endangered Species Act]], given the presence of threatened and endangered native fish species in the basin, and notes that Section 7 consultation may be legally required due to federal funding involvement in Lake Powell's fishery management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target habitat**: [[Lake Powell]], a large reservoir within the [[Colorado River Basin]], managed partly with federal funding.
- **Introduction vector**: Proposed intentional stocking by the [[Utah Department of Wildlife Resources]] to enhance or support the sport fishery (notably as a forage species, implied by typical rainbow smelt stocking rationale, though not explicitly stated in text).
- **Dispersal risk**: Utah's own proposal acknowledges that smelt are expected to migrate out of Lake Powell, raising the possibility of establishment "throughout much of the Colorado River Basin."
- **Community interaction concern**: Potential negative impacts on native fish populations remaining in the basin, particularly species already reduced in range/abundance.
- **Conservation conflict**: Basin native fish species currently listed as threatened or endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] could face compounded recovery obstacles if smelt become widely established.

## Key Findings
- Utah proposed introducing [[Rainbow Smelt]] into Lake Powell to bolster the recreational/sport fishery.
- The proposal itself anticipates that smelt will emigrate from Lake Powell into connected river systems.
- The U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] identifies a credible risk of basin-wide smelt establishment following escape from the reservoir.
- Basin-wide establishment is flagged as a potential threat to native fish populations and to ongoing recovery efforts for species listed as threatened or endangered.
- Because federal funds support Lake Powell fishery management, the Service indicates that **Section 7 consultation** under the Endangered Species Act may be legally required before the introduction proceeds.
- The Service states it is coordinating with the [[Utah Department of Wildlife Resources]] and other agencies, expressing support for maintaining sport fisheries while balancing native species protection obligations.
- No final decision on the introduction's approval or denial is communicated in this letter; it functions as an interim regulatory concern notice.
