# Annotated Bibliography of the Federally Endangered Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*) and Related Colorado River Cyprinids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a compiled bibliography prepared by C.O. Minckley (Northern Arizona University) for the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies program of the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Contract 1-FC-40-10500), dated March 1992 with an update to June 1995. It lists 479 entries on the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), described as the rarest of the endangered Colorado River fishes, and cross-references related material on the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and other Colorado River fishes. Sources span 1853 to 1995, with coverage most complete through 1994. Entries include peer-reviewed articles, theses and dissertations, government publications, contract reports, popular accounts, newspapers, and unpublished field notes.

The surrounding material also includes a 1991 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service letter on recovery goals for the [[Razorback Sucker]], Arizona State University interlibrary loan and photocopy forms, and a guide for authors on scientific citation, which are administrative rather than scientific content.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). The bibliography states it has been extirpated from most of its native range and survives in only a small population in Lake Mohave on the lower Colorado River.
- **Related species:** [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a separate bibliography of about 1,250 articles compiled by the same author, and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), which is cited in many entries as a companion endangered fish. Colorado squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) also appears in the listed literature.
- **Scope:** Entries were included when they were directly about the bonytail, background to its biology, or only mentioned it in passing.
- **Compilation context:** The work was prepared to serve as a foundation for future studies. Copies were deposited at the Canyon Environmental Studies Office (Flagstaff), Hayden Library Special Collections (Arizona State University), and the Northern Arizona University library.
- **Federal listing:** The bonytail was federally listed as endangered (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, 1980 determination, Federal Register 45(80)). A 1984 [[Bonytail Chub Recovery Plan]] was issued by the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team, and a revised plan followed in 1990.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The literature covers the Colorado River system from the upper basin (Green, Yampa, White, Gunnison, Dolores rivers) through Grand Canyon, [[Lake Mead]], and [[Lake Mohave]] in the lower basin. Major recurring themes include:

- **Impoundment effects:** Dam construction (for example [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Glen Canyon Dam]], and [[Hoover Dam]]) changed flow regimes, water temperature, and backwater habitats. The 1991 letter identifies dams, impoundments, flow changes, and channel alteration as the main causes of decline for the native fishes.
- **Non-native species:** Stocking records and reservoir fish communities are documented, including introductions into Lake Mohave and ponds at Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge.
- **Hatchery and culture work:** Propagation at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) and studies of hybridization among chubs.
- **Taxonomy:** Work on the *Gila* complex, including chromosomal and electrophoretic studies (Rosenfeld, 1983 and 1991) and larval identification (Muth et al., 1985).
- **Habitat preferences:** Studies of temperature, dissolved solids, and swimming performance (for example Bulkley et al., 1983; Berry and Pimentel, 1985).
- **Other organisms:** Some entries cover macroinvertebrates, aquatic plants, and fish disease (Flagg, 1982), and drift of larval fishes (Valdez et al., 1985).

## Key Findings

- **Size and composition:** The bibliography contains 479 entries dated from 1853 to 1995 (most complete through 1994). It is organized as a numbered, volume-by-volume list in the appendix.
- **Entry types:** The sources include peer-reviewed articles, dissertations and theses, government publications, contract reports, popular accounts, newspaper articles, and unpublished memoranda and field notes.
- **Range status:** The bonytail is now largely restricted to Lake Mohave, after extirpation from most of its native range.
- **Recovery planning:** Recovery plans (1984, 1990) and recovery implementation programs for the upper basin are cited throughout the bibliography.
- **Data-quality notes:** The bibliography flags items that were "not obtained," "not found," or "already copied," so several entries were not verified against original sources.
- **Early references:** Foundational descriptions of the species are included, such as Baird and Girard (1853, 1854), Cope and Yarrow (1875), and Jordan and Gilbert (1883). The bonytail is described in the sources as *Gila elegans* (Baird and Girard).
- **Key recent monographs:** Minckley (1991) "Native fishes of the Grand Canyon Region: an obituary?"; Wydoski and Hamill (1991) on cooperative recovery; and Minckley and Deacon (1968) "Southwestern fishes and the enigma of 'endangered species.'"
