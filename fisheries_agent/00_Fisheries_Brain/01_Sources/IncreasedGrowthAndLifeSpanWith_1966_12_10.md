# Increased Growth and Life-span with Lowered Ambient Temperature in *Cynolebias adloffi*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontiformes #annual-fish #comparative-gerontology

## Executive Summary
This 1966 *Nature* study by Liu and Walford investigated the effects of ambient water temperature on growth rate and life-span in the South American [[Annual Fish]] *[[Cynolebias adloffi]]*. Using a controlled laboratory design, fish were reared at either 16°C or 22°C following hatching at 22°C. The results demonstrated that **lower ambient temperature (16°C) significantly increased both growth (length and weight) and life-span**, a finding that contrasts with typical patterns in [[Homeothermic Animals]], where lower temperatures usually *decrease* growth and longevity due to increased thermoregulatory energy expenditure. The authors relate their findings to broader theories of [[Aging|senescence]] and the [[Immune Theory of Ageing]], proposing that suppressed autoimmune activity at lower temperatures may underlie the extended lifespan.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Cynolebias adloffi*, a short-lived [[Annual Fish]] species related to *[[Cynolebias bellottii]]*.
- **Measurements taken:** Standard length (snout tip to base of caudal peduncle) and body weight.
- **Growth differences (16°C vs. 22°C):**
  - Males at 16°C were **19.0% longer** and **76.3% heavier** than males at 22°C.
  - Females at 16°C were **17.0% longer** and **84.8% heavier** than females at 22°C.
  - Divergence in length and weight between temperature groups was detectable within **one month** of separation.
  - Differences in size/weight between and within sexes were statistically significant (**p > 0.9995**, ANOVA).
- Growth curves did not converge over time between the two temperature groups, unlike patterns seen in *[[Cyprinodon macularius]]* (Kinne, 1960), where temperature-based size differences diminished after ~35 weeks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- *C. adloffi* belongs to a group of **annual killifishes** native to South America, adapted to ephemeral aquatic habitats that dry seasonally, driving a compressed, senescence-exhibiting life cycle.
- Related species, *[[Cynolebias bellottii]]*, reportedly has an optimal maintenance temperature range of **14–20°C** (Boschi, cited).
- Comparisons drawn to other [[Cyprinodontoid Fishes]] (e.g., *Cyprinodon macularius*) show that growth-temperature relationships vary by species: in *C. macularius*, growth rate increases with temperature up to an optimum (~25–30°C) before declining at higher extremes (35°C).
- Broader ecological context: [[Bourlière]] previously documented that natural fish, amphibian, and reptile populations in **cooler climates** often show slower growth but **longer lifespans** than warmer-water conspecifics — consistent with the lab findings here.

## Key Findings
1. **Experimental Design:** ~450 eggs hatched at 22°C yielded ~200 fry; at 5 months (sexual maturity), 140 fish (70 males, 70 females) were split into four tanks — two held at **16°C (±1°C)** and two at **22°C (±1°C)**.
2. **Survival differences:**
   - At 22°C, mean survival time was **11–12 months**, with near-total extinction by ~19 months (only one female survived beyond that point).
   - At 16°C, the population had **not yet reached 60% mortality by 19 months**, indicating a substantially extended lifespan.
   - Findings align with survival curve models by [[Leslie et al.]] and [[Comfort]], showing an intermediate pattern between idealized senescent and non-senescent survival curves.
3. **Growth-Temperature Relationship:** Fish at the **lower temperature (16°C) grew larger** in both length and weight compared to those at 22°C — an inverse relationship to that seen in homeothermic animals, where reduced temperature typically decreases growth due to thermoregulatory energy costs.
4. **Physiological Interpretation:**
   - Reduced activity at lower temperatures may improve **food conversion efficiency**, contributing to greater growth despite consistent feeding.
   - The authors propose that at lower temperatures, **suppression of autoimmune or immunological degradation processes** could explain extended lifespan, referencing enhanced graft acceptance in *[[Carassius]]* at 10°C (Hildemann, 1957) as supporting evidence for the [[Immune Theory of Ageing]].
5. **Contrast with Homeothermic Physiology:** In warm-blooded animals, decreased ambient temperature (9°C vs. 28°C) reduces both growth and lifespan due to increased catabolic energy demand for thermoregulation — the opposite of the pattern observed in this poikilothermic annual fish.
6. **Broader Significance:** This study represents one of the first **controlled laboratory demonstrations** (rather than purely field/natural population observations) that ambient temperature causally affects both growth rate and lifespan in a poikilothermic vertebrate, supporting *Bourlière's* earlier field-based hypotheses via experimental means.
