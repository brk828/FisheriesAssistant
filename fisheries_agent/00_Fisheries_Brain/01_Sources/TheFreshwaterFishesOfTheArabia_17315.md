# The Freshwater Fishes of the Arabian Peninsula

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic monograph, published in the Journal of Oman Studies special report on the 1975 Oman Flora and Fauna Survey, revises the freshwater fishes of the Arabian Peninsula by Keith Edward Banister and Margaret Anne Clarke. The authors recognise nine species (eight [[Cyprinidae]] and one [[Cyprinodontidae]]), four of which are described as new to science: [[Barbus apoensis]], [[Barbus exulatus]], [[Cyprinion acinaces]] and [[Garra longipinnis]]. A new subspecies, [[Garra barreimiae Shawkahensis]], is also described. The fauna is divided into African, Levantine and Iranian zoogeographical groups, and the authors discuss why certain African and Asian genera, notably [[Clarias]], are absent from Arabia.

## Background & Description

The paper's foreword, written by His Majesty Sultan Qaboos bin Said, frames the survey as a basis for rational resource conservation and development in Oman. The Arabian fauna is depauperate, reflecting arid climate: Aden averages 47 mm of rain per year, and permanent water is scarce, confined largely to upper wadi tributaries, cisterns and wells.

Only seven nominal species had been described from Arabia before this work. Of these, four were recognised as valid (B. arabicus, G. barreimiae, G. tibanica, A. dispar); Scaphiodon muscatensis was treated as a synonym of Cyprinion microphthalmum. Only Barbus arabicus and Garra barreimiae are endemic.

The study drew on seven significant collections deposited at the British Museum (Natural History), including material gathered by W. A. King-Webster in the Wadi Hadramut, G. Popov, F. Tippler, and I. G. Dunn in Yemen.

Morphometric and meristic characters were recorded as percentages of standard length, with counts such as lateral line scales, branched dorsal and anal fin rays, gill rakers and pharyngeal teeth. Vertebral counts included the Weberian apparatus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Climate and water:** Rainfall is very low. Jeddah receives just over 60 mm per year and Khormaskar about 20 mm. Permanent wadis are maintained mainly by mountain rainfall on the western escarpment. The Wadi Hadramut is the largest permanent watercourse and holds about half the peninsula's freshwater fish species.
- **Habitat:** Some cisterns are spring-fed, and Garra species occur in partly artificial cisterns. Tectonic uplift has disconnected some wadi beds from the water table.
- **Palaeoclimate:** Wet conditions prevailed several times between 100,000 and 10,000 B.P., after which progressive desiccation set in. Pleistocene gravels and lake beds indicate former surface water across the region, which the authors suggest permitted fish movement.
- **Persian Gulf:** Sea level fall of about 120 m during the Pleistocene left the Gulf as a freshwater system for roughly 90,000 years, potentially enabling dispersal of freshwater species.
- **Zoogeographical groups:**
  - African: Barbus arabicus, Garra tibanica
  - Levantine: Barbus apoensis, Barbus exulatus, Cyprinion acinaces, Garra barreimiae, Garra longipinnis
  - Iranian: Cyprinion microphthalmum
  - Aphanius dispar is unplaced because its salt tolerance allows coastal dispersal.
- **Absent taxa:** Small Barbus and [[Clarias]] catfishes, both common in Africa, are absent from Arabia. Clarias is also absent from Iran, Iraq and Afghanistan.

## Key Findings

**New taxa**
- [[Barbus apoensis]] (Levantine group): known from three localities, including a permanent stream near Khamis Mushyat (holotype 212 mm SL). Differs from B. exulatus in having one pair of barbels rather than two. Resembles [[Barbus canis]] in having six branched anal rays.
- [[Barbus exulatus]] (Levantine group): 35 specimens, 49-179 mm SL, mostly from the Wadi Hadramut, plus Wadi Maran (East Yemen). Has two pairs of barbels, an inferior mouth and six branched anal rays.
- [[Cyprinion acinaces]]: two disjunct populations, the Wadi Hadramut and a stream near Ta'if about 1000 km apart. Named for the scimitar-like curve of the mouth, with a strong serrated dorsal spine.
- [[Garra longipinnis]]: known only from a wadi near Saiq, Jabal al Akhdar (eight specimens, 36-52 mm SL). Characterised by a thin body and very long paired fins.
- [[Garra barreimiae Shawkahensis]]: described as a subspecies from the Wadi Shawkah, separated from the nominate subspecies by gill raker count (15-18 vs 11-14) and pelvic fin position.

**Revised and reported taxa**
- [[Barbus arabicus]]: 64 non-typical specimens (26-203 mm SL) plus a large holotype (about 345 mm SL). Placed in the African [[Barbus Intermedius Complex]] rather than with Asian species, based on scale striations, body shape and distribution across the Red Sea.
- [[Garra tibanica]]: 93 specimens (46-110 mm SL) examined. Gill raker counts range from 6 to 17, a variation the authors consider too broad to justify subspecies.
- [[Cyprinion microphthalmum]]: 13 specimens (66-112 mm SL), with Scaphiodon muscatensis treated as a synonym. Recorded from the Wadi Bani-Awf, where it reportedly reaches 150 mm SL.
- [[Aphanius dispar]]: euryhaline, tolerates brackish and saline water, and occurs in coastal drainages of the Red Sea, Persian Gulf and Arabian Sea, plus Iraq, the Dead Sea and Asraq Oasis.

**Biogeographical conclusions**
- The authors could not decide between two hypotheses for the African group: a dispersal from Africa via Arabia, or an eastern radiation centred on the Ethiopian highlands. Both would require a saline barrier to have developed at the Red Sea.
- The absence of [[Clarias]] from Arabia is attributed to two apparently impassable barriers, topography (the Baluchistan and Hindu Kush mountains) and temperature (the 10°C isotherm). The authors conclude that Clarias was probably Gondwanic in origin.
- The Levantine discontinuity is attributed most probably to desiccation of the Rub' al Khali, which restricted the fauna to high-altitude biotopes.
- The authors state that insufficient factual evidence exists to support any formal hypothesis on the origin of the Arabian fauna.

**Key references and cross-links**
- Novacek and Marshall (1976) proposed a South American origin for the Ostariophysi. The authors find this irreconcilable with Owen's (1976) continental drift maps.
- The paper concludes with a field key to the nine species, separating them by teeth, dorsal spine type, ventral head disc, colour and anal ray count.
