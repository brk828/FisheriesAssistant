#ichthyology #marine-biology #Eleotridae

## Executive Summary
This ichthyological note by Rezneat M. Darnell (1955) documents an unusual behavioral observation: the nocturnal terrestrial excursions of the tropical gobioid fish [[Gobiomorus dormitor]] (locally known as "metapil") in headwater streams of the Rio Tamesi drainage, east-central Mexico. Individuals were repeatedly found on rocky stream-side "playas" at night, generally within 4–5 feet of the water's edge, often near foraging ant columns. The paper also details the species' burying/concealment behavior, diet (via stomach content analysis), solitary and possibly territorial spacing, and broad [[Euryhaline Fish|euryhaline]] salinity tolerance, drawing a comparison to [[Anguilla rostrata]] as the only other North American euryhaline fish previously known to exhibit terrestrial habits.

## Background & Description
- **Common name:** Metapil (local Mexican name)
- **Eye position:** Dorsally-placed eyes, allowing the fish to remain buried in bottom detritus while still able to see, with only the eyes exposed above the substrate.
- **Body form:** Capable of rapid darting movement and clearing the water surface in leaps when startled; specimen captured by seine was caught mid-air while attempting to leap over a raised cork line, indicating strong burst-swimming/jumping capability.
- No detailed meristic or morphometric data (fin ray counts, standard length ranges, etc.) are reported in this note; observations are primarily behavioral and ecological.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat range:** Recorded from brackish coastal lagoons and freshwater streams entering the [[Gulf of Mexico]] and [[Caribbean Sea]], from Texas south to Brazil, and throughout the [[West Indies]] — indicating tolerance of marine salinity as well.
- **Classification:** [[Euryhaline Fish|Euryhaline]] species, per criteria used by Gunter (1942) in his listing of North American euryhaline fishes.
- **Microhabitat:** Rocky stream beaches ("playas") of headwater streams; also shallow streams and arroyos with detritus-covered bottoms.
- **Diel activity pattern:** Nocturnal terrestrial forays onto rocky beaches; diurnal/nocturnal resting on stream bottoms; occasional slow creeping among bottom rocks.
- **Concealment behavior:** Buries itself by fanning bottom detritus over its body with its fins, leaving only the dorsally-placed eyes exposed — a response frequently triggered by disturbance (light or gentle prodding) at night.
- **Escape behavior:** Under stronger stimulation, darts rapidly from concealment, often leaping clear of the water.
- **Social structure:** Predominantly solitary; only once was a pair observed swimming together. Estimated density of ~1 individual per 20–30 meters of stream bank, with roughly even spacing — suggestive of [[Territorial behavior in fish|territorial behavior]].
- **Local ecological knowledge:** Local residents were reportedly well aware of the species' nocturnal terrestrial foraging habits.
- **Comparative context:** Among North American euryhaline fishes catalogued by Gunter (1942), only [[Anguilla rostrata]] (American eel) had previously been documented exhibiting terrestrial habits — making this the first terrestrial record for G. dormitor.

## Key Findings
- **First terrestrial record:** This is the first documented instance of terrestrial habitat use by *Gobiomorus dormitor*, occurring exclusively at night on rocky stream-side playas, always within 4–5 feet of water.
- **Feeding ecology (diet analysis, n=7 stomachs):**
  - 3 stomachs: empty (no food)
  - 2 stomachs: remains of ≥6 fishes
  - 1 stomach: remains of ≥6 aquatic insects
  - 1 stomach: one large tarantula plus several unidentified arthropods
  - No evidence supporting the initial hypothesis that the fish were feeding on the ant columns observed nearby on the playas.
- **Prior dietary literature:** Corroborates Meek & Hildebrand (1916) and Hildebrand (1938), who described the species as a sluggish, ambush-style carnivore feeding on crustaceans, fishes, water beetles, and other suitably-sized aquatic animals from concealment among vegetation.
- **Capture methods:** Species readily takes a hook; the banded characin *[[Astyanax fasciatus]]* was effective as bait; spearing in shallow water at night was the most efficient capture technique.
- **Salinity tolerance:** Confirmed as euryhaline, occupying habitats ranging from freshwater streams to brackish coastal lagoons, with a distribution suggesting possible tolerance of full marine salinity across the West Indies.
- **Research implications:** The author calls for detailed physiological studies of osmoregulation, respiration, and locomotion in *G. dormitor*, given its unusual combination of wide salinity tolerance and terrestrial excursion behavior — a rare combination among gobioid fishes.
- **Taxonomic/ecological context:** Species is classified within the [[Gobioidei]] (gobioid fishes), specifically as a member of the sleeper family, informally related to but ecologically distinct from other Central American headwater stream fauna.
