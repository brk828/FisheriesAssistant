## Executive Summary

This document is an internal **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum** (dated February 17, 1995) rather than a primary scientific research paper. It concerns the administrative rollout of a **"Big River Fishes" Management Plan** for the **[[Lower Colorado River]]**, drafted to initiate a cooperative State, Federal, and private fisheries management framework. The memo does not contain morphological, physiological, or ecological data on specific fish species; instead, it outlines the bureaucratic process, stakeholders, and timeline for developing conservation policy for endangered big-river fish populations in the lower basin of the Colorado River.

## Background & Description

No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic data are provided in this source. The text does not specify which species comprise the "big river fishes" group, though this designation historically refers to large-bodied, endemic Colorado River fishes such as the **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]**, **[[Razorback Sucker]]**, **[[Bonytail]]**, and **[[Humpback Chub]]** — all federally listed under the Endangered Species Act. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The management plan targets the **[[Lower Colorado River]]**, distinguishing it ecologically and managerially from the **Upper Colorado River Basin**, which has separate endangered fish management needs.
- **Geographic Scope**: Proposed stakeholder workshops were planned for **Yuma** and **Lake Havasu City, Arizona**, indicating the plan's geographic focus on the lower mainstem river reaches and reservoir-adjacent habitats.
- **Basin Differentiation**: The memo explicitly notes a need to establish "the difference in management needs between the upper and lower basins," implying distinct ecological or hydrological conditions affecting native fish populations in each basin segment (e.g., differing flow regimes, temperature, sediment transport, and non-native species pressures).
- **Human Interactions**: The plan acknowledges "many conflicts and interests on the river," suggesting competing water use demands (agricultural, municipal, recreational) that intersect with endangered fish habitat needs — a key ecological stressor for [[Big River Fishes]] in this system.

## Key Findings

- A **management plan** for Lower Colorado River big-river fishes was drafted and distributed to **104 individuals and organizations** for public/stakeholder comment.
- The plan's stated purposes include:
  - Serving as a foundation for a **cooperative State-Federal-private fisheries management framework**.
  - **Recovering endangered fish species** in the lower basin.
  - Clarifying **differential management needs** between upper and lower Colorado River basins.
  - Establishing **Service leadership** in endangered fish management on the river.
  - Justifying **Service funding needs** for lower basin conservation work.
- Endangered fish management in the lower basin is identified as a **high agency priority**, with funding opportunities considered more favorable there than elsewhere in Arizona due to the high level of competing water-use interests.
- The plan's timeline was tied to several external administrative processes:
  - The **Bureau of Reclamation's biological assessment**.
  - **FY 1996 Congressional appropriations** process.
  - **FY 1997 Service budget deliberations**.
- A **firm completion deadline of May 1, 1995**, was set for the finalized management document.
- Regional leadership assigned the **Arizona Fishery Resources Office (AZFRO) Project Leader** as lead on the initiative, with contingency plans to reassign responsibility if station priorities (e.g., commitments in the **White Mountains**) conflicted with this workload.
- Planned **stakeholder workshops** (tentatively in Yuma and Lake Havasu City) were intended to consolidate public comments and refine the plan following regional office review.
