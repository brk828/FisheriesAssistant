# NPS Coordination Group Appointment for NPCA Commission on Research and Resource Management Policy

#administrative #resource-management #policy #marine-biology

## Executive Summary

This April 13, 1988 memorandum from the National Park Service Western Regional Director announces the appointment of an NPS coordination group to work with the newly-appointed [[National Parks and Conservation Association]] (NPCA) Commission on Research and Resource Management Policy in the National Park System. The coordination group comprises experts across natural resources, cultural resources, and social sciences who will ensure Commission members are fully informed on resource management issues. The initiative aims to produce recommendations of equal or greater importance than the original [[Leopold Report]], which profoundly impacted natural resource management in the National Park System.

## Background & Description

The National Park Service established a coordination group to interface with the NPCA Commission examining research and resource management policy across the National Park System. Coordination group members were selected based on their expertise, respect within and outside the Service, broad perspective, servicewide view, collaborative skills, and professional networks enabling them to gather specialized information and consultation as needed.

The group is chaired by Ms. Carol F., Chief, Office of Natural Resources, National Park Service, Washington, D.C. Members represent expertise categories including:

**Natural Resources:**
- Forest Ecosystems: Dr. Susan P. Bratton ([[University of Georgia]])
- Air Quality: Ms. Christine L. Shaver (Denver)
- Coastal/Marine Environments: Mr. Gary E. Davis, Research Marine Biologist, [[Channel Islands National Park]]
- Earth Sciences: Mr. Daniel B. Kimball (Water Resources Division, Denver)

**Cultural Resources:**
- History: Mr. Barry Mackintosh (Bureau Historian)
- Architectural/Landscape: Mr. Hugh C. Miller
- Archeology/Anthropology: Mr. Douglas H. Scovill

**Social Science:**
- Socio-economics: Dr. Donald R. Field ([[Oregon State University]])
- Recreation/Leisure: Mr. Robert W. McIntosh, Jr. ([[Gateway National Recreation Area]])
- Education: Mr. William E. Brown ([[Denali National Park]])

Additional members include Mr. G. Bryan Harry (Pacific Area Director), Mr. Ernest W. Ortega ([[Wind Cave National Park]]), and Mr. J. T. Reynolds (North Atlantic Regional Office).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The coordination group addresses multiple ecological domains relevant to National Park System management. The Coastal/Marine Environments representative, Gary E. Davis at [[Channel Islands National Park]], brings research marine biology expertise to inform Commission deliberations on marine and coastal resource management policy. The broader group encompasses terrestrial ecosystems (forest ecosystems), atmospheric resources (air quality), aquatic systems (water resources/earth sciences), and the human dimensions of resource management (socio-economics, recreation, education).

This comprehensive approach recognizes that effective resource management policy must integrate natural sciences, cultural resource protection, and social science understanding of visitor use and park values. The marine biology component specifically addresses coastal and marine environments within the National Park System, which include significant marine protected areas.

## Key Findings

**Primary Objectives and Structure:**
- The NPS coordination group functions as individual coordinators, technical experts, and information gatherers rather than meeting as a separate body
- Members serve as representatives of the National Park Service and their respective peer groups
- The Commission's first meeting was scheduled for April 19, 1988 in Washington, D.C.

**Scope and Significance:**
- The Commission undertakes a comprehensive effort exceeding the scope of the original [[Leopold Report]]
- The Regional Director characterizes this as "an even more comprehensive effort" with potential for "equal or greater importance" than the Leopold Report's impact on natural resource management
- The initiative addresses research and resource management policy across the entire National Park System

**Operational Approach:**
- Coordination group members are expected to ensure Commission members receive complete information on issues under consideration
- Members can draw upon their professional networks within the Service for specialized information and consultation
- All Western Region areas, offices, directorate and division chiefs are urged to cooperate with coordination group members requesting information

**Expertise Coverage:**
- Natural resources expertise spans forest ecosystems, air quality, coastal/marine environments, and earth sciences including water resources
- Cultural resources representation includes history, architecture/landscape, and archeology/anthropology
- Social science perspectives encompass socio-economics, recreation/leisure, and education
- Geographic representation includes Western Region, Pacific Area, North Atlantic Region, and specific parks including [[Channel Islands National Park]], [[Denali National Park]], [[Wind Cave National Park]], and [[Gateway National Recreation Area]]
