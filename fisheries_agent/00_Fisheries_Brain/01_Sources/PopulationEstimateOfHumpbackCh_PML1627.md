# Population Estimate of Humpback Chub in Black Rocks (Project 131, 2005 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers Project 131, a second population estimate for [[Humpback Chub]] in the Black Rocks reach of the [[Colorado River]]. The project is led by [[Chuck McAda]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Grand Junction, Colorado). Recovery goals require population estimates at regular intervals to measure the response of the species to management actions. Sampling in fall 2003, fall 2004 and fall 2005 captured 70, 74 and a further number of humpback chub, respectively. Recapture rates were low, so the [[Population Estimation]] models gave very wide confidence intervals and unreliable estimates. The project is behind schedule, with a draft final report due by March 31, 2006.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Humpback Chub]], a native Colorado River fish. The report also records captures of [[Roundtail Chub]].
- **Purpose:** Robust population estimates are needed to monitor recovery under the [[Recovery Program]], as called for in the [[Colorado River Action Plan]] (RIPRAP, Mainstem, task V.C.1).
- **Study area:** The entire Black Rocks reach of the Colorado River.
- **Schedule:** FY 2003 to FY 2005. Sampling occurs in September and October, so it spans two fiscal years and is reported by calendar year (2003 and 2004).
- **Continuity:** The work follows an earlier estimate for the 1998–2000 period ([[McAda]] 2000 is cited in the report).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling methods:**
  - In fall 2003, [[Electrofishing]] was used more heavily than before, covering the whole reach during one early morning session per trip. This prevented setting [[Trammel Net]]s at those times.
  - Trammel nets with 0.5-in inner mesh caught smaller fish but at a much lower catch rate than 1-in inner mesh, giving mixed results.
  - Fewer nets were set, and net sets were kept to a short length to reduce the time between net checks and minimize stress to fish.
  - In fall 2004, trammel nets were set for set periods, with electrofishing used at least once per trip to capture smaller chubs and sample areas that nets could not cover well. Most humpback chub were caught with nets, while electrofishing was most effective for roundtail chub.
- **Recapture and tagging:** Several fish were previously tagged. Recaptures were sparse in 2004, which the authors link to poor [[Sampling Efficiency]] at Black Rocks. [[Pit Tagging]] data for stocked fish were submitted to the database.
- **Size structure:** Size distributions were similar across years, with a slight shift toward larger fish in 2005 (see Figure 1 in the appendix, covering roughly 23 to 35 cm in the scanned axis).

## Key Findings

**Fall 2003 sampling**
- 70 individual humpback chub were captured. Five were recaptured: one within the same sampling rotation and four in different rotations.
- Twelve roundtail chub captured had been tagged in previous years.
- The population estimate was presented at the September workshop. Five models ([[Mo Model]], [[Darroch Model]], [[Chao Estimator]] Mt and Mh, and Mth) produced the following estimates:

| Model | Estimate | 95% CI | CV | P-hat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mo | 478 | 221–1,176 | 0.46 | 0.04 |
| Darroch | 454 | 214–983 (approx.) | 0.46 | 0.04 |
| Chao Mt | 365 | 184–834 | 0.42 | 0.05 |
| Chao Mh | 597 | 259–1,539 | 0.50 | 0.03 |
| Chao Mth | 475 | 210–1,244 | 0.50 | 0.04 |

- Confidence intervals were wide, and the CV and P-hat values did not meet the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] recommendations for population estimation.

**Fall 2004 sampling**
- 74 humpback chub were captured, with only one recaptured within the study period. Few recaptures came from previous years.
- The low within-year recapture rate produced unreliable estimates with very wide confidence intervals and very low P-hat values. Reported estimates (per the scanned table) ranged from about 932 (Chao) to 2,056 (Mo), with CVs between 0.69 and 0.98.
- Adding one more recapture to the matrix roughly halved the estimates, showing how sensitive they are to a single recapture.

**Comparisons and conclusions**
- Total fish collected and mean catch per effort were about the same in fall 2004 and fall 2005.
- The estimates for the two years differed, but the very wide confidence limits meant no significant difference between years could be shown.
- The high recapture rates of previous years and the poor P-hat values both point to low sampling efficiency at Black Rocks.
- The authors recommend completing the report write-up as described in the scope of work.

**Project status and budget**
- Status: ongoing and behind schedule. The final report is due in calendar year 2005, and a draft will go to the propagation coordinator by March 31, 2006.
- Budget: $45,100 provided and $45,100 spent (no difference). About 71% of the FY 2005 work was completed. No [[Recovery Program]] funds were spent on publication charges.
- Data: PIT tag numbers and stocked-fish data were submitted to the database, and catch data were due in March 2005.
