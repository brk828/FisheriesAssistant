# Potential Fish Habitat Benefits in Independence Creek and Little Truckee River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #freshwater-ecology #flow-management

## Executive Summary

This 1994 California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG) report evaluates proposed operational changes to [[Independence Dam]] and [[Independence Lake]] releases, aimed at mitigating fish habitat degradation in [[Independence Creek]] and the [[Little Truckee River]]. The existing management regime prioritizes high-volume, short-duration late-summer releases (August–October) to meet municipal and industrial (M&I) water demands, which conflicts with flow requirements needed to sustain resident and lake-run trout populations. The report also references major hydrological disruption caused by [[Stampede Reservoir]] releases managed for endangered [[Cui-ui]] spawning in the lower [[Truckee River]], which imposes extreme high flows for approximately 4 months, stranding and killing resident fish in secondary channels.

The proposed modification would extend releases from [[Independence Lake]] (increasing total volume from 5,000 to 7,500 acre-feet) over a longer period (June–December, rather than August–October only). Using [[Physical Habitat Simulation Model (PHABSIM)]] outputs, the report demonstrates that this operational shift would substantially increase weighted usable area (WUA) habitat for spawning, fry, juvenile, and adult life stages of [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], and [[Kokanee]] in both streams, with the greatest gains occurring in the Little Truckee River between October and December.

## Background & Description

This report does not describe morphological traits of the fish species directly; it is a hydrological/habitat management study. However, it implicitly distinguishes between:
- **Resident populations** (fish completing full life cycle within stream reaches)
- **Lake-run (adfluvial) populations** (fish migrating from [[Stampede Reservoir]] into the Little Truckee River to spawn, notably lake-run [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], and [[Kokanee]])

Species affected but not modeled via PHABSIM include:
- [[Tahoe Sucker]]
- [[Paiute Sculpin]]
- [[Redside Shiner]] (Lahontan redside)
- [[Mountain Whitefish]]
- Mountain sucker and dace (unspecified species)

These non-salmonid species are noted as likely beneficiaries due to later (post-June) spawning phenology and/or preference for faster-water habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**System context:** The Little Truckee River drainage is part of the [[Truckee River]] basin, historically supporting one of California's premier wild trout fisheries. Flow regimes are currently dictated by downstream needs (Cui-ui spawning in the lower Truckee River via [[Pyramid Lake]] system), creating conflict with upstream (Little Truckee/Independence Creek) trout and kokanee habitat needs.

**Independence Creek** (downstream of Independence Dam):
- Resident [[Rainbow Trout]]: spawn March–June (prior to flow modification window); benefit stages = egg incubation, fry, juvenile, adult rearing.
- Resident [[Brown Trout]]: spawn September–December, directly overlapping with the proposed high-flow period; greatest single beneficiary of the modification.

**Upper Little Truckee River** (between [[Stampede Reservoir]] and Independence Creek):
- Resident and lake-run [[Rainbow Trout]]: spawn March–June (unaffected by flow change); fry present July–October, juveniles October–December (affected positively).
- Resident and lake-run [[Brown Trout]]: spawn September–December (directly benefited); fry present January–June (outside benefit window); juvenile/adult present during modified flow period.
- [[Kokanee]]: migrate from Stampede Reservoir to spawn in the Little Truckee River September–December; fry emerge and migrate to the lake January–March.

**Stampede Reservoir fishery:** At risk from reduced storage causing increased temperature and turbidity, threatening resident trout and kokanee fisheries — a secondary rationale for the proposed Independence Lake release changes as partial mitigation.

## Key Findings

**Proposal:** Increase Independence Lake release from 5,000 to 7,500 acre-feet, extending the release window from June through December (versus the original August–October schedule), leaving 10,000 acre-feet in storage by December 31, 1994. Requires concurrence from Westpac, Bureau of Reclamation, Fish and Wildlife Service, and the Paiute Tribe, with free storage allowance in Stampede Reservoir and no net loss of M&I water usage.

### Independence Creek — PHABSIM Habitat Results (October–December increases)
- **Rainbow Trout:**
  - Adult rearing habitat: >3-fold increase
  - Juvenile rearing habitat: ~2.5-fold increase
  - Fry rearing habitat: ~10% increase (most flow-sensitive life stage)
  - Lower August flows also projected to modestly improve habitat for all three life stages.
- **Brown Trout:**
  - Spawning habitat: ~5-fold increase
  - Adult rearing habitat: ~4-fold increase
  - Juvenile rearing habitat: ~20% increase

### Upper Little Truckee River — PHABSIM Habitat Results (October–December increases)
- **Rainbow Trout:**
  - Fry habitat: ~2-fold increase
  - Juvenile habitat: ~3-fold increase
  - Adult habitat: ~4-fold increase
- **Brown Trout:**
  - Spawning habitat: ~5-fold increase
  - Juvenile habitat: ~140% increase
  - Resident adult habitat: ~2.5-fold increase
- **Kokanee:**
  - Spawning habitat: 3-fold increase

### Broader Ecosystem Impacts Noted
- Extended high-flow Cui-ui management releases from Stampede Reservoir cause approximately 4 months of unnaturally high flows in the Little Truckee River downstream of Stampede, displacing fish into [[Boca Reservoir]] or stranding them in secondary channels that later dry, resulting in significant resident fish mortality.
- Reduced Stampede Reservoir storage volume linked to potential temperature and turbidity increases, threatening the reservoir's trout and kokanee fishery.
- Species not modeled (Tahoe Sucker, Paiute Sculpin, Redside Shiner, Mountain Whitefish) are expected to benefit indirectly, especially later-spawning and fast-water-adapted species.

### Data Limitation
- PHABSIM modeling was applied only to trout (rainbow, brown) and kokanee; benefits to sucker, sculpin, shiner, and whitefish populations remain qualitative/inferred, not quantitatively modeled.
