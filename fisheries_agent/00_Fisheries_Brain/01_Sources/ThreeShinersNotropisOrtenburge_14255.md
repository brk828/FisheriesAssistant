#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This 1956 brief communication reports the extension of the known ranges of three shiner species (family Cyprinidae) into East Texas. Two species, the [[Kiamichi Shiner]] (*Notropis ortenburgeri*) and the [[Taillight Shiner]] (*Notropis maculatus*), are documented in Texas for the first time. The third, the [[River Shiner]] (*Notropis blennius*), previously known in Texas only from the Red River, is newly recorded from the Sabine River drainage. The findings are based on collections made between 1951 and 1955 primarily in the Caddo Lake, Sabine River, and associated tributary systems of northeast Texas.

## Background & Description
- **Notropis ortenburgeri (Kiamichi Shiner):** Distinct enough taxonomically to be distinguished from more common co-occurring shiner species, though the note suggests historical confusion may have led to under-reporting (specimens possibly misidentified as a "more common species").
- **Notropis blennius (River Shiner):** Exhibits subspecific variation identified by [[Hubbs and Bonham (1951)]]:
  - *N. b. blennius* — southwestern form.
  - *N. b. jejunus* (Forbes) — eastern and northern form.
  - Comparison of the two East Texas specimens against diagnostic criteria showed:
    - Specimen from Sabine River (Panola County, Dec 12, 1954) more closely resembled *N. b. jejunus*.
    - Specimen from Shelby County (Feb 8, 1955) more closely resembled *N. b. blennius*.
  - This intermediate/mixed character expression suggests a zone of intergradation or morphological overlap in this drainage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Notropis ortenburgeri:**
  - Native habitat: headwater streams of the [[Ouachita Mountains]] (Poteau River, Kiamichi River system, Mountain Fork River, Little River system) in Arkansas and Oklahoma.
  - East Texas habitat: Found in slow-moving, cypress-filled sloughs (e.g., Haggerty Creek) and in [[Caddo Lake]] itself, occurring in small numbers at multiple seining stations located on cypress-free sandy bottoms (seining limited to these zones due to dense cypress cover elsewhere in the lake).

- **Notropis maculatus:**
  - Broad native range: southern North Carolina to peninsular Florida, along the Gulf Coastal Plain, and north through the Mississippi Valley to southeastern Oklahoma and Missouri (per [[Bailey, Winn, and Smith (1954)]]).
  - East Texas habitat: Collected from Caddo Lake, the Sabine River, and White Oak Bayou — indicating adaptability to lake, river, and bayou environments.

- **Notropis blennius:**
  - Broad native range: plains streams of Alberta and Manitoba (Canada) through the Mississippi River Valley to the Gulf of Mexico, east to the Tombigbee River, Alabama.
  - Previously known in Texas only from the [[Red River]] drainage.
  - Newly documented habitat: [[Sabine River]] at two localities (Panola County and Shelby County), indicating expansion of known distribution to a second major Texas drainage.

## Key Findings
- **First Texas records** established for *Notropis ortenburgeri* and *Notropis maculatus*, expanding their known ranges southward/westward into the Sabine and Caddo Lake drainages of northeast Texas.
- **New drainage record** for *Notropis blennius* in Texas — previously restricted to the Red River, now confirmed in the Sabine River.
- Authors explicitly **discount bait-bucket introduction** as the source of the *N. ortenburgeri* Caddo Lake population, citing that the Haggerty Creek collection predates significant bait-fish activity in the area and represents one of the earliest surveys of the region.
- The apparent "sudden" appearance of *N. ortenburgeri* in Caddo Lake by 1954 is attributed to either (a) prior misidentification/confusion with a common congener, or (b) a genuine increase in local abundance — the authors cannot distinguish between these hypotheses.
- Specimens of *N. blennius* from two nearby Sabine River sites show conflicting subspecific characteristics (one aligning with *N. b. jejunus*, the other with *N. b. blennius*), and the authors note insufficient prior survey data in the Sabine River to assess whether this reflects natural intergradation or a bait-introduction artifact.
- Collection efforts spanned 1951–1955 and involved multiple contributing collectors: [[Wilmot A. Thornton]], [[William M. Milstead]], [[David L. Jameson]], [[Charles E. Gray]], [[Robert J. Kemp Jr.]], [[E. W. Bonn]], and [[C. R. Inman]].
- This note also contains an unrelated **errata correction** for a separate paper by [[George K. Reid Jr.]] on the biology and ecology of East Bay, Texas, correcting typographical errors in species names (e.g., *Harengula pensacolae pensacolae*, *Dorosoma cepedianum*, *Cyprinodon variegatus variegatus*, *Leiostomus xanthurus*) and taxonomic family formatting (Sparidae).
