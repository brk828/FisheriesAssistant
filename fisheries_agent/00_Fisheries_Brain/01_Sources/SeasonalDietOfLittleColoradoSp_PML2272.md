# Diet and Life History of the Little Colorado Spinedace

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines the diet, feeding behavior, size structure and reproductive condition of the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), a small cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]] in eastern Arizona. Field collections from [[Nutrioso Creek]] were combined with [[Laboratory Feeding Trials]] and a [[Length-Weight Relationship]] analysis. The species proved to be an opportunistic, mid-water feeder whose diet tracked the seasonal availability of [[Aquatic Insects]] and terrestrial prey. The work was motivated by the species' rarity and its 1987 listing as a threatened species with critical habitat.

## Background & Description

The [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] is a cyprinid fish known from the Little Colorado River system in Arizona. Earlier work described its distribution, variation and ecology (Miller 1963), and a broader account of the species in Arizona was published by Minckley and Carufel (1967). The [[Threatened Species Listing]] (U.S. Department of the Interior, 1987) designated the species as threatened and identified critical habitat. Prior to this study, little was known about its feeding biology, which the authors identify as a gap relevant to management.

The study area included [[Nutrioso Creek]] and [[Chevelon Creek]], with the most intensive sampling in Nutrioso Creek, a perennial stream in northeastern Arizona. Fish were collected with seines and stream sampling gear, then preserved and examined in the laboratory. Measurements recorded for each fish included total length, wet weight, sex, maturity stage and digestive tract contents. Stomach contents were sorted into food categories, and gut fullness was estimated as the percentage of the digestive tract containing food. Benthic invertebrates were collected with [[Surber Sampler]] samples to compare prey availability with fish diets.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species is associated with:

- **Habitat:** Pools and areas of current (>20 cm/s). Larvae were associated with soft substrate. Adult spinedace were found mainly in mid-water.
- **Prey base:** Benthic invertebrates were abundant, with [[Chironomidae]] larvae the most common aquatic insects. Densities of aquatic insects were highest in May.
- **Seasonal pattern:** Diet composition and fish condition varied across the sampled months, reflecting changes in prey availability.

The authors note that the species' diet shifted between aquatic and terrestrial prey over the year, consistent with [[Opportunistic Feeding]] in a variable stream environment.

## Key Findings

**Sampling and sample sizes**
- Specimens were collected in May 1990, July 1990, October 1990 and January 1992.
- Table 1 lists sample sizes of 57, 27, 32 and 18 fish for these four periods, respectively.
- Total length ranges were approximately 22–95 mm across collections, and the species showed a wide size range within each sample.
- Empty stomachs and gut fullness were recorded for each sample, with the percentage of empty stomachs varying among months.

**Diet**
- Diet included a wide range of food items: [[Chironomidae]], [[Corixidae]], [[Elmidae]], [[Ephemeroptera]], [[Odonata]], [[Plecoptera]], [[Trichoptera]], terrestrial insects, filamentous green algae, higher plant material and woody debris.
- Chironomidae were among the most frequent aquatic prey.
- Diet composition shifted between aquatic and terrestrial insects across seasons.
- No apparent difference was found between the diets of males and females.

**Laboratory feeding trials**
- Spinedace of 45–75 mm total length (n = 60) consumed all food items offered. These included immature mayflies, damselflies, dragonflies, belostomatids, amphipods, leeches, cladocerans, snails, corixids, notonectids, earthworms, mealworms, water mites, crayfish (20 mm), filamentous algae and commercial trout food.
- Nearly all food was captured at the surface or within the water column, supporting the description of the species as a [[Mid-Water Feeding]] fish.
- Fish of 40–75 mm total length (n = 60) were maintained for eight weeks in laboratory aquaria on commercial fish pellets. The authors state it is unknown whether any single food item, or combination, would sustain the species over long periods, or whether the food items were assimilated.

**Length-weight relationship**
- Total length and wet weight were significantly related (r = 0.984, n = 141) for fish from Nutrioso Creek.
- Males showed a near-linear relationship between total length and wet weight.
- Females showed a curvilinear relationship, likely because of egg production in individuals over 70 mm total length.

**Reproduction**
- Mature eggs were recorded in female fish, and the percentage of fish with mature eggs increased with fish size.
- The percentage of mature eggs was highest in May, suggesting that [[Spawning Season]] in Nutrioso Creek occurs in spring.
- May was also the month of highest aquatic insect density, which may be linked to the spawning period.

**Management relevance**
- The findings describe an opportunistic, mid-water feeder that depends on benthic invertebrates, with seasonal shifts in diet. These results bear on the [[Threatened Species Listing]] and on conservation of the species' habitat in [[Nutrioso Creek]] and related streams.
