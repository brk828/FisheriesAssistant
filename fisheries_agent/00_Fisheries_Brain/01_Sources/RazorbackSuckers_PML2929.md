# Razorback Sucker Capture Records and Release Memorandum, Colorado River Area, January 1986

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen

## Executive Summary

This one-to-two-page U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service interoffice memorandum reports four [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) captured in January 1986 near [[Parker Arizona]] and released in the main canal of the [[Colorado River]]. It was sent from the Fishery Management Biology project office in Parker to the Endangered Species office in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and was received by [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 2 on February 3, 1986. The memorandum is a capture-and-release notification for an endangered species. It contains no population estimates, sample design, or analysis.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interoffice memorandum, with a distribution list of roughly a dozen Fish and Wildlife staff names (largely illegible in the scan).
- **Sender:** Scott Yess, Project Leader, Fishery Management Biology (FMB), Parker, Arizona.
- **Addressee:** Jerry Burton, Endangered Species, Albuquerque, New Mexico.
- **Subject:** Razorback Suckers, with the memo marked "End. Sp. R.2" (Endangered Species, Region 2).
- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]], a large catostomid fish listed as endangered and the focus of the memo.
- **Scan quality:** Heavy OCR noise. Much of the page 1 layout and banner text is unreadable, and several numeric values are partly garbled (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Capture setting:** Fish were taken from irrigation and sand canals in the Parker area, not from open river channels. The memo describes one capture at the intersection of two irrigation canals and another from under a car bridge over a sand canal.
- **Habitat link:** The fish were released into the main canal of the [[Colorado River]], which is the mainstem channel that connects the canal system to the river.
- **Page 2 map:** The second page is a map of the study area, with labels such as "Lost Lake," "Wilson Road," and a site-marking legend. It is too degraded to read reliably, so no site coordinates or distances can be confirmed.
- **Regulatory context:** The memo is addressed to the [[Endangered Species Act]] program office, which indicates that the captures were reported under endangered-species oversight.

**Other key points:**

- **Release:** All four fish were released in the main canal of the Colorado River.
- **Sites:** Three sites were recorded (A, B, C), with two captures at one location.
- **Capture methods:** The memo names the capturing individuals but does not describe gear, effort, or catch-per-unit data.
- **Purpose:** The memo is a notification of endangered-species captures for the regional program. It states no management recommendation, conclusion, or population trend.
- **Data limits:** The document contains no sample sizes beyond four individuals, no population estimates, and no information on sex, age, tagging, or subsequent recapture.
