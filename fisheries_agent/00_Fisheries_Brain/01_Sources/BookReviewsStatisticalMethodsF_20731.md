## Executive Summary
This 1959 book review, authored by **Don W. Hayne** of the [[Institute for Fisheries Research]] (Ann Arbor, Michigan), evaluates three statistics texts for their utility to field biologists, including fisheries scientists:

1. **Wallis & Roberts (1956)** — *Statistics: A New Approach*: emphasizes conceptual understanding of statistical reasoning over rote technique, using illustrative examples rather than exhaustive method coverage.
2. **Snedecor & Cochran (1956)** — *Statistical Methods Applied to Experiments in Agriculture and Biology* (5th ed.): the long-standing standard reference for applied [[Analysis of Variance]] in biological and agricultural research, now updated with a new sampling chapter by Cochran.
3. **Federer (1955)** — *Experimental Design: Theory and Application*: a specialized, advanced treatment of analysis-of-variance experimental designs rooted in agricultural research traditions following [[R. A. Fisher]]'s *The Design of Experiments* (1935).

The review assesses each book's suitability as a practical reference for field biologists, particularly those in [[Wildlife Management]] and [[Fisheries Research]], who must apply statistical methods without extensive mathematical training.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Not applicable in the traditional sense. However, the review situates statistical methodology within the "ecological niche" of **field biology practice**, noting that:

- Field biologists (including fisheries workers) increasingly use simple forms of [[Analysis of Variance]], but rarely require the complex experimental designs common in agricultural research (e.g., incomplete block designs), because field studies seldom involve testing many controllable variables simultaneously (unlike crop variety trials).
- Field data often fail to meet the stringent statistical assumptions required by advanced agricultural experimental designs.
- Many useful designs remain "largely unknown" to biologists, limiting their practical adoption in wildlife and fisheries contexts.

## Key Findings
- **Wallis & Roberts**: Excellent for teaching statistical reasoning and conceptual generalization, but explicitly omits *t*, *F*, and chi-square tests (substituting normal approximations), which the reviewer considers a serious deficiency for biologists needing to interpret published field studies using these tests.
- **Snedecor & Cochran**: Remains the closest available single reference for biologists; the new sampling chapter by [[William G. Cochran]] is highlighted as "almost directly applicable today in field biology." The book now includes multiple-range tests, nonparametric methods, and discussion of Type II error and statistical power. The reviewer notes uneven revision quality despite overall excellence.
- **Federer**: A specialized, advanced text on analysis-of-variance experimental designs; roughly half is judged directly or potentially applicable to field biology. Recommended for those with more advanced statistical background and serious interest in experimental design, not for beginners.
- **Overall verdict (Hayne)**: No single existing text fully satisfies field biologists' need for a comprehensive statistical methods handbook. Snedecor's text comes closest; Wallis & Roberts is best for conceptual teaching; Federer is valuable but narrow and advanced, pointing toward future development needs in [[Experimental Design]] methodology for biological research.
