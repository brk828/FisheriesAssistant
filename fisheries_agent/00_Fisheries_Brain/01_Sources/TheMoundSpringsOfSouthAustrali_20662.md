## Executive Summary

This document is a postscript/reference appendix to the 1978 Nature Conservation Society of South Australia (NCSSA) survey of the [[Mound Springs Of South Australia]], rather than a primary data paper. It summarizes follow-up research activity (1978–1985) on the mound spring ecosystems of the [[Great Artesian Basin]], including South Australian Museum studies on **fish populations** (Glover & Sim 1978a, 1978b; Glover 1979), hydrological work, and taxonomic description of an endemic ostracod, *Ngarawa dirga* (De Deckker 1979). The core conservation concern discussed is the potential hydrological impact of a proposed mining operation (Olympic Dam, Roxby Management Services) on spring discharge, which threatens the endemic biota — including fish, hydrobiid snails, and the aquatic plant *Eriocaulon carsoni* — that depend on continuous artesian outflow. No direct morphological or taxonomic fish data is presented in this excerpt; the text is administrative/bibliographic in nature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Isolated, spring-fed wetlands ("mound springs") formed by artesian groundwater discharge from the [[Great Artesian Basin]], located in arid northern South Australia near Lake Eyre South.
- **Hydrological dependency**: These springs form discrete, often highly isolated aquatic habitats supporting endemic biota (fish, invertebrates, aquatic plants) adapted to permanent, stable-temperature groundwater discharge in an otherwise arid landscape.
- **Threatened endemics**:
  - *[[Eriocaulon Carsoni]]* — an aquatic plant rediscovered at Hermit Hill during the NCSSA survey, of particular conservation concern.
  - Undescribed, endemic **hydrobiid snails** (family [[Hydrobiidae]]), subject of ongoing taxonomic work by Dr. Winston Ponder (Australian Museum).
  - *[[Ngarawa Dirga]]* — an endemic ostracod described from material collected during the NCSSA survey.
  - Endemic **mound spring fish populations**, studied by Glover & Sim and Glover (South Australian Museum).
- **Anthropogenic threat**: A mining consortium (Roxby Management Services Pty. Ltd.) proposed a 33-megalitre/day water extraction from borefields near Lake Eyre South to supply the Olympic Dam mining prospect. Predictive modelling in the Draft Environmental Impact Statement projected drawdown effects on nearby mound springs ranging from a **100% reduction in discharge** at Priscilla Spring to **10% reduction** at Davenport, Wangianna, and Welcome springs — directly threatening spring-dependent endemic fauna and flora.
- **Conservation response**: The South Australian Department of Environment and Planning supported taxonomic studies (Ponder's hydrobiid work), commissioned a broader heritage survey (natural, Aboriginal, European) of northern mound springs, and pursued (with the Pastoral Board) a fencing programme to exclude livestock from ecologically significant springs.

## Key Findings

- Fish population studies of the mound springs were conducted by the South Australian Museum and published as Glover & Sim (1978a, 1978b) and Glover (1979), following the 1978 NCSSA survey — these represent key primary sources on [[Mound Spring Fish]] ecology and taxonomy, though their specific findings are not detailed in this postscript.
- Hydrological studies (Williams & Holmes 1978; Holmes et al. 1981) provide supporting context for spring discharge dynamics relevant to fish and invertebrate habitat stability.
- The mining-driven bore field development at Lake Eyre South represents the primary anthropogenic threat identified for mound spring ecosystems as of the mid-1980s, with quantified discharge-reduction predictions for named springs (Priscilla: 100%; Davenport, Wangianna, Welcome: 10%).
- Endemic biodiversity of conservation concern extends beyond fish to include an aquatic macrophyte (*Eriocaulon carsoni*), undescribed hydrobiid gastropods, and an endemic ostracod (*Ngarawa dirga*), indicating that mound springs function as biodiversity hotspots of high endemism requiring integrated, multi-taxon conservation planning.
- Institutional response included active government-funded taxonomic research (Ponder's hydrobiid work), a planned heritage survey program, and habitat protection measures (livestock exclusion fencing) — precursors to a proposed integrated conservation programme for South Australia's mound springs.
- This document functions primarily as a bibliographic and administrative record, underscoring the importance of cross-referencing the cited primary literature (particularly Glover & Sim 1978a/b and Glover 1979) for substantive fish science data on [[Mound Spring Fish]] populations.
