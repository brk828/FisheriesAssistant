# Extent of Atresia in Maturing Ovaries of the Eastern Brook Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 paper by [[Nancy E. Henderson]] (University of Toronto) tests whether [[Atresia]], the degeneration of developing ovarian follicles, substantially reduces the number of maturing ova in the [[Eastern Brook Trout]] (*[[Salvelinus Fontinalis]]*) during the [[Secondary Growth Phase]] of [[Oogenesis]]. The study compares [[Relative Fecundity]] with [[Total Fecundity]] in hatchery-reared stocks and challenges the earlier estimate by [[Vladykov]] (1956) that only 56–61% of maturing eggs ripen. Henderson concludes that there is no major reduction in developing ova during this phase. The apparent decline in the original data is explained mainly by the fish growing in length while their eggs develop.

## Background & Description

*Salvelinus fontinalis* is a salmonid whose fecundity scales with body size. Atresia can begin at any follicle stage, so the number of ova that start development may exceed the number that complete [[Vitellogenesis]] and are shed. The paper distinguishes two measures:

- **Relative fecundity:** the number of maturing ova present in the ovary at a given stage of the secondary growth phase.
- **Total fecundity:** the number of mature, fully developed ova produced.

During the secondary growth phase, ova increase in diameter from about 1 mm to more than 3.5 mm. In brook trout this phase normally takes about 3.5 months, running from early July to mid October at the latitude of Toronto, which is also the usual spawning time. Females may grow several centimetres in length during this interval. Because of this growth, the mean number of ova in females of a given size falls as the cycle advances. The author removes this effect statistically by adjusting each fish's length to its computed size at the midpoint of the phase, using growth data from [[Henderson 1963]].

Material came from three stocks of hatchery-reared brook trout, used in three experimental series on the effects of [[Day Length]] and [[Temperature]] on [[Gonadal Development]]:

- **Series 1:** Experiments 1–6 (includes controls)
- **Series 2:** Experiments 7–12
- **Series 3:** Experiments 14–16 (includes controls)

Each series came from a different stock, so fecundity comparisons were limited to fish from the same population because of possible genetic differences.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study is experimental rather than field-based. Fish were held under controlled conditions in the context of an [[Ontario Fisheries Research Laboratory]] project, jointly operated by the [[University of Toronto]] and the Ontario Department of Lands and Forests. The paper cites earlier fecundity-length work on brook trout in Ontario ([[Ricker]] 1932), Quebec and Legendre (Vladykov and Legendre, 1940), and Michigan ([[Cooper]] 1953). It also cites [[Rounsefell]] (1957), who showed that fecundity-length data from these studies are approximately linear on a log-log scale. The same log transformation is used here.

## Key Findings

- **Growth-adjusted fecundity is stable.** After correcting for female growth, the number of developing ova changes little across the secondary growth phase. From the 2–3 mm to the 3–4+ mm diameter class there was no significant change in any of the three series. In Series 3 the mean fell by about 10%.
- **Early counts are slightly low.** A small rise from the 1–2 mm to the 2–3 mm class is attributed to some counts being made before the full set of maturing ova had entered this phase.
- **Atresia is present but small.** Atretic ova (larger than 1 mm) made up 3.1–4.7% of maturing plus atretic ova across the three series, excluding Experiments 8 and 10.
- **Vladykov's estimate is overstated.** Henderson argues that [[Vladykov]]'s (1956) conclusion that only 56–61% of maturing eggs ripen was a misinterpretation. Vladykov did not account for the increase in female length during the secondary growth phase.
- **Fecundity-length relationship.** Across the 15 experimental groups, adjusted lengths ranged from 21.1 to 29.8 cm and adjusted ova counts from 667 to 2,410. Series 1 and 2 appear to share a similar fecundity-length relation, while Series 3 may differ. Experiment 8 deviated and was not explained.
- **Series means.** Mean adjusted ova counts by diameter class were:
  - Series 1 (Experiments 1–6): 721, 730 and 714 (1–2 mm, 2–3 mm, 3–4+ mm); mean adjusted length 21.4 cm
  - Series 2 (Experiments 7–12): 927, 1,082 and 1,136; mean adjusted length 24.8 cm
  - Series 3 (Experiments 14–16): 2,127, 2,639 and 2,311; mean adjusted length 29.2 cm
- **Methods.** Ova diameters were averaged from a random sample of 30 ova per fish. Ovaries were teased apart and maturing ova were counted directly. Ova retained in the ovarian lamellae were added to the count for ripe females.
