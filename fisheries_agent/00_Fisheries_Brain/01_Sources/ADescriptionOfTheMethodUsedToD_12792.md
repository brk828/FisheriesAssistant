## Executive Summary

This paper, authored by Robert P. Dempster (Steinhart Aquarium) and Bryce P. Anderson (Consulting Chemist), does not describe a fish species but instead presents a **colorimetric analytical method** for detecting and quantifying chlorite concentration in aquarium and large-scale aquatic system water following treatment with **sodium chlorite**. Sodium chlorite was used in aquarium management as a water treatment/disinfection agent beneficial to aquatic life at low concentrations, but only effective and safe when water pH exceeds 6.0. The described protocol is a modification of the **Black and Whittle method** for colorimetric determination of free and residual chlorine, adapted specifically for chlorite detection in both freshwater and saltwater systems. This methodology is foundational to [[Aquarium Water Quality Management]] and complements broader practices in [[Fish Tank Disinfection Protocols]].

## Key Findings

### Purpose and Rationale
- **Sodium chlorite** is beneficial at low concentrations for controlling water quality in systems supporting aquatic life, but must not be used if water pH is below 6.0.
- Calculated theoretical chlorite dosing does not always match the actual resulting concentration, necessitating empirical verification via direct testing.

### Dosing Formula
To calculate the amount of sodium chlorite (80%) needed to reach a desired concentration:

```
(V × 6.34 × ppm) / 1000 = grams of sodium chlorite (80%) required
```
- V = volume in gallons
- 6.34 = constant
- ppm = desired chlorite concentration

### Reagents for Chlorite Testing
1. **Leuco crystal violet solution** — indicator compound dissolved in perchloric acid (prepared in darkness; target pH 1.4–1.6).
2. **Saturated mercuric chloride solution** — stabilizing/oxidizing reagent.
3. **Mixed indicator solution** — combination of leuco crystal violet and mercuric chloride solutions.
4. **Acetate buffer solution (pH 4)** — containing potassium iodide, used to stabilize reaction pH.
5. **EDTA·2Na solution** — chelating agent added to halt/stabilize color development.
6. **Sodium chloride solution** — used to standardize ionic strength between saltwater and freshwater samples.

### Test Procedure Summary
1. Dilute/prepare sample depending on source (freshwater vs. saltwater) to a standardized 100 ml volume using de-ionized water and/or NaCl solution.
2. Transfer 25 ml aliquot to a flask.
3. Add acetate buffer (target pH 3.8–4.1), then mercuric chloride solution, then mixed indicator solution.
4. **Color development**: Presence of chlorite produces a blue-to-purple color, with intensity proportional to chlorite concentration.
5. Add EDTA·2Na solution and allow the sample to age 15–30 minutes for full color development.
6. Measure **optical density (absorbance)** at **575 nm** using a Bausch and Lomb Spectrometer 20.
7. Determine ppm chlorite from a calibration curve (Figure 1), with the most accurate detectable range being **0.5–1.5 ppm**.

### Calculation of Original Sample Concentration
For diluted samples, the actual chlorite concentration of the original water is calculated as:

```
P² = (100 × P') / V
```
- P' = ppm from calibration graph (diluted sample)
- V = volume (ml) of original sample used in dilution
- 100 = total diluted volume (ml)

**Worked Example**: For V = 20 ml and P' = 1.0 ppm, P² = 5 ppm (actual chlorite concentration of the undiluted original sample).

### Dilution Protocol for High-Concentration Samples
Because the test is highly sensitive and only accurate in the 0.5–1.5 ppm range, samples with estimated chlorite concentrations ≥2.0 ppm must be pre-diluted using:

```
Vs = 100 / P
```
- Vs = volume of sample required (ml)
- P = estimated concentration (ppm)

**Worked Example**: For an estimated 5 ppm sample, Vs = 100/5 = 20 ml, which is then diluted per the standard preparation protocol.

### Methodological Lineage
This test procedure is explicitly derived from and modifies the method described in:
> A. P. Black and G. P. Whittle, "New Methods for the Colorimetric Determination of Halogen Residuals, Part 2: Free and Total Chlorine," *Journal AWWA*, 59:607–619 (1967).
