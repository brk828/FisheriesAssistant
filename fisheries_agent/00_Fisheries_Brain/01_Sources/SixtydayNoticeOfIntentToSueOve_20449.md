# Sixty-Day Notice of Intent to Sue Over Inadequacy of Loach Minnow and Spikedace Critical Habitat Designation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #critical-habitat #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This legal notice, dated September 12, 1994, from the Gila Watershed Partnership to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service challenges the adequacy of critical habitat designated for [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) on March 8, 1994. The notice argues that the designation fails to meet Endangered Species Act (ESA) requirements by excluding habitat necessary for long-term survival and recovery, failing to protect all genetically distinctive populations, and not representing the historic geographical and ecological distribution of these species. The letter provides sixty-day notice of intent to sue under the ESA.

## Background & Description

Critical habitat for [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] was designated on March 8, 1994 (Federal Register 59(45):10906). Both species are members of the family [[Cyprinidae]]. According to genetic studies by Tibbets (1992), all remaining populations of both species are genetically distinctive, with populations found in the [[Gila River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Tularosa River]], [[White River]], [[Blue River]], and [[Aravaipa Creek]].

Historically, both [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] were found predominantly below 4000 feet elevation. The currently designated critical habitat is found predominantly above 4000 feet in elevation, representing a significant departure from the species' historic geographical and ecological distribution.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Federal Register critical habitat rules acknowledged that designated areas possess necessary factors for survival, growth, and reproduction of the species. However, the Service explicitly stated that several areas currently occupied by [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] were not included in the designation, despite being "considered important for the long-term survival and recovery" of both species.

The Service noted that "maintenance of the widely separated populations found in the [[Gila River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Tularosa River]], [[White River]] and [[Blue River]] and in [[Aravaipa Creek]] as independent entities is critical to buffer against threats to each individual population." Each remnant population proposed for critical habitat designation has unique characteristics contributing to species' future viability.

Genetic studies in progress (Tibbets 1992) indicate that the populations are genetically distinctive, suggesting that protection of all remaining populations is critical to species conservation.

## Key Findings

**Legal Deficiencies Identified:**

1. **Failure to Include Recovery Habitat**: The Service acknowledged considering "adding certain unoccupied areas...considered vital for recovery of the species," indicating the current designation fails to include areas essential for conservation as defined by ESA Section 3.(5)(A)(i).

2. **Incomplete Population Protection**: While genetic studies (Tibbets 1992) indicate all remaining [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] populations are genetically distinctive, the critical habitat designation does not protect all remaining populations despite their unique genetic characteristics.

3. **Failure to Represent Historic Distribution**: ESA implementing procedures at 50 C.F.R. 424.12 require critical habitat designations to include "habitats that are representative of the historic geographical and ecological distributions of a species." Currently designated critical habitat is found predominantly above 4000 feet elevation, while both species historically occurred predominantly below 4000 feet.

4. **Conservation Standard Not Met**: Under ESA Section 3.(3), conservation includes "methods and procedures which are necessary to bring any endangered species or threatened species to the point at which the measures provided pursuant to this Act are no longer necessary." The Service's own Colorado River Endangered Fishes Critical Habitat Proposal states that "conservation represents the areas required to recover a species to the point of delisting." Currently designated critical habitat does not provide for both long-term survival and recovery habitat.

**Legal Action**: This letter serves as sixty-day notice of intent to sue under the Endangered Species Act for inadequate critical habitat designation for [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]].
