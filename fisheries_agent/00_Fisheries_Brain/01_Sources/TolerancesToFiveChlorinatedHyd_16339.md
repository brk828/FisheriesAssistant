# Tolerances to Five Chlorinated Hydrocarbon Insecticides in Two Species of Fish from a Transect of the Lower Mississippi River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Ictaluridae #Poeciliidae #ecotoxicology

## Executive Summary
This study measured 36-hour median tolerated limits (TL50) to five chlorinated hydrocarbon insecticides (DDT, toxaphene, aldrin, dieldrin, endrin) in wild populations of **[[Black Bullhead]]** (*Ictalurus melas*) and **[[Mosquito Fish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*) collected along a longitudinal transect of the Lower Mississippi River, from Walls, Mississippi to St. Francisville, Louisiana. The work was motivated by major fish kills (1960–1964) attributed to endrin/dieldrin contamination traced to an industrial source near Memphis, Tennessee. The researchers hypothesized that if a source of chronic sublethal contamination existed upstream, fish downstream would show a **gradient of increasing insecticide resistance**, analogous to documented cases of genetically based pesticide resistance in Mississippi Delta fish populations. No such resistance gradient was detected — particularly for endrin — indicating that populations along this transect had not experienced prolonged or repeated lethal-level exposure to these insecticides, despite the historical fish kills attributed to the region.

## Background & Description
- **Black Bullhead (*Ictalurus melas*)**: Test specimens were fingerlings/juveniles; mean weights ranged from 0.43 g (Noxubee, n=50) to 2.27 g (Wayside, n=75); Greenville population mean weight 1.35 g (n=100). Ferriday sample weights not determined (n.d.).
- **Mosquito Fish (*Gambusia affinis*)**: Small-bodied poeciliid, tested in groups of 3 individuals per 0.5 L test solution; exact weights not reported, but fish were uniformly small enough for standardized jar bioassays.
- **Bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*)**, referenced only for comparison: small individuals from Walls used to benchmark endrin sensitivity (36-hr TL50 = 0.5 ppb).
- Fish from schools of fingerling catfish were noted to likely represent progeny of only one or a few parents, limiting the ability of school-based sampling to capture true population-level variation in tolerance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Borrow pits adjacent to river levees — isolated pools for most of the year but periodically inundated during high water, allowing genetic interchange with the main river channel population. This creates a hydrologically connected mosaic of habitats functioning as a single metapopulation.
- **Geographic transect**: Sampling spanned ~250+ river miles, from **[[Walls, Mississippi]]** and **[[Greenville, Mississippi]]** (Washington Co.) down to **[[Rodney Lake]]** (Jefferson Co., Miss.), **[[St. Francisville, Louisiana]]** (West Feliciana Parish), and **[[Ferriday, Louisiana]]** (Concordia Parish).
- **Reference/control populations**: 
  - **[[Noxubee National Wildlife Refuge]]** (Black Bullhead) — minimal/no pesticide exposure, used as a susceptible baseline.
  - **[[State College, Mississippi]]** (Mosquito Fish) — nonagricultural area, susceptible baseline.
  - **[[Yazoo River]]** near Yazoo City (Mosquito Fish) — drains the cotton-producing Mississippi Delta, representing agricultural runoff exposure.
  - **[[Sidon, Mississippi]]** (Mosquito Fish) — known pesticide-resistant population, used as a positive control for resistance detection.
- **Land use context**: Wayside sampling site is downstream of a large, heavily cotton-cultivated peninsula inside the levee, a plausible localized contamination source.
- **Field validation experiment**: Caged Wayside and Noxubee bullheads placed in **[[Twin Bayou]]** (Sunflower Co., Miss.) during a period of DDT/toxaphene field runoff following heavy rain; sediment analysis showed 520 ppb DDT in bayou mud. All 150 caged fish (both populations) died within 4 days, demonstrating that even the more resistant Wayside fish could not survive real-world agricultural runoff concentrations.

### Principal Conclusions
1. **No resistance gradient detected**: TL50 values for mosquito fish *decreased* moving downstream from Walls to St. Francisville, the opposite of what would be expected if upstream (e.g., Memphis-area) contamination had selected for resistant populations moving downstream.
2. **Walls population resembles unexposed baseline**: The most tolerant Mississippi River mosquito fish population (Walls) showed TL50 values comparable to the pesticide-naïve State College population — indicating an absence of true resistance, not just low exposure.
3. **Downstream populations show heightened susceptibility**: St. Francisville and Rodney Lake mosquito fish were *more* sensitive than typical nonagricultural baseline populations, possibly reflecting sublethal body-burden accumulation of insecticides below the Yazoo River confluence, which could lower the additional dose needed to reach lethality.
4. **Yazoo River population shows weak selection**: Elevated dieldrin/endrin TL50 values in the Yazoo City population suggest mild selective pressure from Delta agricultural runoff, but far weaker than the strongly resistant Sidon population from a directly treated tributary.
5. **Black Bullhead results are inconsistent and inconclusive**: Greenville fish showed no resistance; Wayside fish (only 11 miles downstream) were moderately resistant to four of five insecticides (but *not* endrin) — with no identified contamination source explaining this local anomaly. Fish size/school-based sampling limitations complicate interpretation.
6. **Endrin sensitivity was uniformly high across all populations and species**: All bullhead and mosquito fish populations had TL50 values between 0.5–2.5 ppb endrin, essentially indistinguishable from the susceptible bluegill reference (0.5 ppb). This uniformity across the entire transect argues against any localized chronic endrin contamination source (e.g., near Memphis) having produced selective mortality/resistance in these species.
7. **Field cage experiment confirms real-world lethality**: Even the "resistant" Wayside bullhead population suffered 100% mortality (n=150, combined with Noxubee controls) when exposed to actual agricultural runoff in Twin Bayou containing high sediment DDT (520 ppb), underscoring that laboratory-defined "resistance" does not confer field-level survivability under acute runoff exposure.
8. **Overall interpretation**: The absence of a coherent resistance gradient — especially for endrin — supports the conclusion that Lower Mississippi River fish populations in this transect had not been subject to prolonged, repeated, lethal-level pesticide exposure, contrasting with resistant populations documented in Delta tributaries directly receiving field runoff (e.g., **[[Sidon Population]]**).
