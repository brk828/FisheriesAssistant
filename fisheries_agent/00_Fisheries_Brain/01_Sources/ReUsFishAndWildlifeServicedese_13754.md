# Desert Fishes Council Dispute with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Over Upper Colorado River Basin Recovery Program

#ichthyology #conservation-policy #endangered-species #institutional-conflict

## Executive Summary

This document comprises correspondence from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC) regarding a dispute with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) over the "Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin." The DFC was excluded from initial review processes in 1985-1986, submitted extensive critical comments in December 1986 after requesting access, and had those comments arbitrarily "discounted" by the Service as "emotional and not based on complete fact." The Service proceeded with a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) and finalized the Implementation Program without addressing DFC concerns. The correspondence documents DFC's response strategy and formal protest to Regional Director Galen Buterbaugh in February 1988.

## Background & Description

The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] developed a recovery program for endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] during 1985-1986. A first draft circulated for external review in March 1986, primarily to upper basin states, explicitly excluding the [[Desert Fishes Council]] from the reviewer list. A second public draft circulated in September 1986, again excluding DFC. Service personnel presented the program at the 18th Annual DFC Meeting in [[St. George, Utah]] (November 1986), where DFC requested review copies despite the review period officially closing November 15, 1986.

The Service granted DFC's request and DFC appointed a committee (the "Colorado River Review Committee") to produce comments. DFC transmitted extensive, substantive, and critical review comments to the Service on December 8, 1986, simultaneously providing summary assessments to agencies, private individuals, and legislators from Lower Colorado River basin states who had not received the document.

The Service produced an Environmental Assessment ([[USFWS]] 1987a) that did not incorporate any DFC comments, recommending a FONSI for Service participation. The final "Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin" ([[USFWS]] 1987b) proceeded to completion. Secretary of Interior Hodel, governors of Upper Basin States, and Director of the Federal Water and Power Commission signed the Implementation Program shortly after the 19th Annual DFC meeting in [[Hermosillo, Sonora, Mexico]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery program addresses endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], representing a major federal conservation initiative for big-river fishes. The DFC review committee expressed "great reservations about the Implementation Program" based on concerns that portions were "biased, questionable, or otherwise flawed" and did not "place welfare of the resource first in priority."

The document emphasizes the need to keep "the resource at the forefront of all considerations" and questions whether the Implementation Program in its final form would "really enhance recovery of big-river fishes." The committee stood by their original assessment that the program contained substantive flaws requiring correction.

## Key Findings

**Service Response to DFC Review (Statement by Steve Lanigan/John Hamill):**
1. The Service welcomed DFC input and participation (even if negative) as valuable for negotiating with the Coordinating Committee
2. Many DFC comments were "viewed as emotional and not based on complete fact" and were therefore "discounted in the review process"
3. The Service criticized DFC for releasing comments to Congressional delegations without providing FWS prior opportunity to review them

**DFC Position and Actions:**
- The DFC review committee thoroughly re-examined their original comments and decided to "stand by them" with "little alteration" needed
- DFC found the arbitrary discounting of solicited comments without documentation to be "a dangerous precedent" that "cannot be allowed to stand"
- DFC disagreed that their review was emotional and "seriously question[ed] that such is a valid criticism"
- The organization requested placement on permanent mailing lists for all proposals and projects under the Implementation Program

**Proposed DFC Response Strategy (February 1988):**
1. Notify full DFC membership of events through historical documentation
2. Send formal response to Regional Director requesting: (a) firm commitment to continue surveillance of FWS activities in upper basin; (b) notification of all proposals/projects under the Implementation Program; (c) acknowledgment that ethical/legal procedure violations are under scrutiny
3. Explore legal action regarding arbitrary discounting of review comments
4. Consider public disclosure through orchestrated news releases to outdoor writers
5. Appoint new committee to explore legal and political avenues

**Institutional Concerns:**
- DFC questioned whether the Service's action set "a policy precedent for future Service decisions"
- The organization emphasized that rejection of substantive review "does not speak well for decision making, for the Implementation Program itself, or for the review process"
- DFC committed to "participate maximally in the process of endangered species recovery in the future, as in the past"
