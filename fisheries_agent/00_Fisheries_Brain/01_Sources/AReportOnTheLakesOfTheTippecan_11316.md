## Executive Summary

This report presents preliminary findings from a hydrographic and limnological survey of lakes within the [[Tippecanoe Basin]], Indiana, an area covering 1,890 square miles. The study represents the first section of a broader survey of Indiana lakes, focused on constructing hydrographic maps and characterizing water chemistry (temperature, dissolved oxygen, free carbon dioxide, carbonates) and plankton composition at critical depths. Eleven lakes were mapped in detail (including [[Manitou Lake]], [[Yellow Creek Lake]], and [[Sawmill Lake]]), while gas determinations and plankton collections were conducted across eight lakes. The lakes originated from irregularities in the [[Erie-Saginaw Interlobate Moraine]], formed by the Erie and Huron-Saginaw lobes of the Wisconsin ice sheet, resulting in kettle holes, ground moraine irregularities, and channel lake basins. Note: this paper is limnological/geological in focus and does not report specific fish taxa; data pertains to lake morphometry, thermal stratification, dissolved gas dynamics, and plankton abundance as they relate to aquatic habitat quality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal stratification**: Bottom temperatures ranged from 5.3°C ([[Tippecanoe Lake]]) to 15°C ([[Little Eagle Lake (Chapman)]]), indicating variable degrees of thermal stratification across the basin's lakes.
- **Wind-distributed heat**: Calculated in gram calories per square centimeter of surface (heat in excess of 4°C), ranging from 5,361 gram calories ([[Manitou Lake]]) to 10,563 calories ([[Yellow Creek Lake]]) — reflecting differences in lake mixing dynamics and exposure.
- **Dissolved Oxygen (DO) dynamics**:
  - Epilimnion: Oxygen consistently abundant; supersaturation observed in six instances at one or more depth levels — likely tied to photosynthetic activity.
  - Hypolimnion: Oxygen consistently reduced with depth.
  - **Anoxic/hypoxic lakes** (no free oxygen in lower levels): [[Hammon Lake]], [[Lingle Lake]], [[Little Eagle Lake]], [[Pike Lake]], [[Center Lake]], [[Webster Lake]] — critical for understanding habitat limitations for oxygen-dependent aquatic fauna.
- **Water chemistry — hardness and carbon dioxide**:
  - All surveyed lakes classified as **hard water lakes**.
  - Carbon dioxide (as carbonates) maximum values ranged from 27 cc./liter to 60 cc./liter across lakes.
  - Vertical acidity gradient: increasingly acidic in lower (hypolimnetic) levels; epilimnion occasionally alkaline due to photosynthetic CO₂ drawdown.
- **Seasonal scope**: All chemical and thermal data reflect **summer conditions only**; seasonal variability not addressed in this report.
- **Plankton–gas correlation**: No strong general correlation found between plankton abundance and dissolved gas concentrations across lakes.
- **Littoral vegetation link**: Preliminary evidence suggests plankton richness may correlate with, and possibly be driven by, the abundance of **phanerogams** (flowering aquatic plants) in the littoral zone — a potential indirect ecological driver of primary/secondary productivity.

## Key Findings

1. The [[Tippecanoe Basin]] lakes are geologically uniform in origin, all resulting from moraine irregularities left by Wisconsin-age glaciation (Erie and Huron-Saginaw lobes).
2. Significant morphometric diversity exists among lakes — surface area, volume, and depth vary by more than an order of magnitude between the smallest ([[Sawmill Lake]]) and largest ([[Manitou Lake]]) basins.
3. Hypolimnetic oxygen depletion is common, with six of the studied lakes showing complete anoxia at depth during summer stratification — a critical constraint for cold-water or oxygen-sensitive aquatic species.
4. All lakes are hard-water systems with measurable carbonate buffering, showing a consistent pattern of increasing acidity with depth, mediated by photosynthetic activity in surface waters.
5. Plankton abundance does not correlate straightforwardly with dissolved gas levels, suggesting that other factors — potentially littoral zone vegetation (phanerogams) — may be more influential drivers of plankton productivity.
6. This work represents a foundational hydrographic baseline for the [[Tippecanoe Basin]], intended to be expanded with full maps and tabulated data in a forthcoming Indiana University Studies publication (July 1916).
