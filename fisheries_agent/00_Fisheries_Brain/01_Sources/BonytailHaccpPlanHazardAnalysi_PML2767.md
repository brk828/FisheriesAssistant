# Bonytail Subadult Production HACCP Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point]] (HACCP) plan for producing subadult [[Bonytail]] in hatchery facilities. It covers the product description, the production flow diagram, a list of potential [[Aquatic Nuisance Species]] (ANS) hazards, a step-by-step hazard analysis worksheet, and a plan form. The main concern is preventing the unintended introduction of ANS, especially the [[Asian Tapeworm]] and [[Local Snails]], into receiving waters such as the [[Colorado River]]. Two steps are identified as critical control points: treatment of juveniles and adults for tapeworm, and crowding and netting onto trucks.

The source is a scanned, partly garbled plan. The HACCP plan form (section 5) appears only as a heading, so no completed form is available in the text.

## Background & Description

**Product:** Subadult bonytail, produced for stocking. Fish are held in [[Fiberglass Tank]]s or glass aquaria and hauled by truck to either [[Dexter Fish Technology Center]] (Dexter FTC) or [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Willow Beach NFH). Depending on the stage, fish may be released into the [[Colorado River]], or held at Dexter FTC as [[Broodstock]] (listed as "broodfish").

**Source and handling:** Eggs are received from certified disease-free sources. Juveniles and adults may also be received from the same sources. Eggs are stocked into incubation units or glass aquaria supplied with well water. After hatching, sac fry move to grow-out containers and are raised to appropriate stocking sizes.

**Production flow (from the flow diagram):**
1. Eggs or juveniles/adults received
2. Eggs stocked into incubators or glass aquaria (well water)
3. Sac fry placed into grow-out containers
4. Juveniles or adults treated for Asian tapeworm
5. Fish grown to size for stocking
6. Fish crowded and dip-netted onto trucks, or loaded by fish pump
7. Fish transported by truck to rivers, lakes, or alternate holding facilities
8. Fish dip-netted from trucks, or drained from trucks, into lakes, rivers, or alternate facilities

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Potential hazards (ANS screen):** The plan lists aquatic species in the hatchery water supply or local waters that could hitchhike to receiving waters and cause ecological harm.

- **ANS fish:** None identified.
- **ANS invertebrates:** Asian tapeworm and local snails. The plan lists examples such as zebra mussels, Asian clams, spiny water fleas, and rusty crayfish, but these are generic examples and are not identified as present.
- **ANS plants:** None identified.

**Hazard analysis worksheet (summary):** The worksheet assesses eight steps. Only invertebrate hazards are considered significant. Fish and plant hazards are marked "ANS not present" throughout. The scanned worksheet is partly ambiguous, so the following summary follows the clearest entries:

| Step | Significant hazard? | Key reasoning | Preventive measure / CCP |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Eggs, juveniles, or adults received | Yes | Possible tapeworm transfer with juveniles or adults | Hazard controlled at a later step |
| 2. Eggs stocked into incubators or aquaria | No | Transfer with eggs possible but unlikely | Not applicable |

## Key Findings

- **Main ANS risk:** Tapeworm and snails are the only hazards identified. No fish or plant ANS are flagged.
- **Critical control points:** Step 4 (tapeworm treatment with praziquantel) and Step 6 (snail removal and equipment sanitation during crowding and netting).
- **Treatment protocol:** Juveniles and adults are to be treated for [[Asian Tapeworm]] with praziquantel at an appropriate dose.
- **Snail control:** Local snails could be present when fish are crowded onto trucks. Control measures are to remove snails, sweep raceways, and dry nets.
- **Source control:** Eggs and juveniles come from certified disease-free sources, which limits introduction risk at the earliest steps.
- **Facility network:** Fish move among the hatchery sites Dexter FTC and Willow Beach NFH, and to release sites in the Colorado River and other lakes or rivers.
- **Document status:** The HACCP plan form (section 5) is listed but not included in the text. The hazard worksheet is partly illegible from OCR.
