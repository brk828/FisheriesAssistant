# Cui-ui Recovery Team's Recommendation Against Reclassification (1993)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This letter documents the formal recommendation of the [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) against reclassifying the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) from Endangered to Threatened status, despite a reported dramatic increase in adult population size (from ~150,000 in the early 1980s to ~1.1 million in 1993, with total population possibly reaching 3–4 million individuals). The Team determined that raw population size alone is an insufficient criterion for reclassification under the 1992 [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]], and that none of the three required biological criteria for downlisting had been conclusively satisfied. Key concerns include a lack of genetic diversity, persistent single-year-class dominance, insufficient secured water flows, and unusually high mortality from fish passage failure and pelican predation.

## Background & Description
The source document is a management/policy letter and does not provide direct morphological descriptions of *Chasmistes cujus*. However, several population-structural characteristics relevant to the species' biology are noted:
- **Longevity/Year-class structure**: The population includes at least 16 distinct year classes, indicating a long-lived species capable of persisting many years without successful recruitment.
- **Dominant cohort**: A single year class (spawned in 1981) has remained the predominant cohort in the population since at least 1978, suggesting infrequent successful recruitment events relative to lifespan.
- **Genetic variability**: Documented low allozyme (genetic) variability at the individual level, potentially linked to a historically reduced effective (spawning) population size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[Cui-ui]] are endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]] (Nevada) and rely on the lower [[Truckee River]] for spawning migration and passage to upstream spawning habitat.
- **Anthropogenic habitat alteration**: Water diversion (Truckee River to Carson River basin), channel straightening, and construction of the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]] have altered natural delta and river-mouth conditions, creating unnaturally persistent shallow-water zones near the fishway entrance.
- **Predation pressure**: Resident American white pelicans exploit these artificially shallow-water conditions near the river mouth/fishway entrance, resulting in exceptional predation rates — an estimated **30% of the prespawning aggregate** may have been consumed by pelicans in a single season.
- **Passage mortality**: An estimated **20% mortality** occurs among cui-ui that enter the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]] and Marble Bluff river trap system, indicating the fish facility is only marginally effective at delivering spawners upstream.
- **Effective spawning population**: Of the prespawning aggregate, only approximately **17,000 spawners** were successfully passed upstream of Marble Bluff Dam, underscoring a large gap between total population size and effective (reproductively contributing) population.
- **Hydrologic dependency**: Population dynamics are modeled via the [[Cui-ui Model]] in conjunction with the [[Truckee-Carson Hydrologic (Negotiation) Model]], which links river inflow, fish passage, and survival rates across cui-ui life stages.

## Key Findings
1. **Reclassification criteria not met**: The 1992 Recovery Plan specifies three criteria for downlisting from Endangered to Threatened:
   - (a) ≥0.85 probability of persistence over 200 years (per the Cui-ui Model);
   - (b) securing an additional 45,000 acre-feet/year of Truckee River inflow to Pyramid Lake (at a minimum rate of 5,000 acre-feet/year);
   - (c) stable or increasing adult/juvenile year-class numbers over the previous 15 years.
   **None of these three criteria were confirmed as satisfied** at the time of the letter.

2. **Population size ≠ recovery**: Despite a population increase widely publicized by USFWS (150,000 → 1.1 million adults), the Recovery Team emphasized that sheer numbers do not reflect genetic health, recruitment stability, or reduced extinction risk.

3. **Model limitations**: The Cui-ui Model was not rerun with updated data because survival-rate assumptions could not be justifiably revised without new independent variable data (beyond river flow/passage) known to affect survival — rerunning without such justification would yield invalid results.

4. **No secured water gains**: As of the letter's writing, **no additional water supply** had been secured for cui-ui in the lower Truckee River/Pyramid Lake system, despite ongoing negotiations over reservoir and diversion operations.

5. **Recruitment stagnation**: A single dominant year class (1981) has persisted since 1978 with no appreciable change, indicating a lack of consistent successful recruitment — a red flag despite population size gains.

6. **Genetic bottleneck concerns**: Low effective spawning population (~17,000 of a much larger prespawning aggregate) may be driving reduced allozyme variability. The Team recommended further research, particularly **mitochondrial DNA analysis** of older year classes, to clarify genetic risk.

7. **Predation as a human-mediated threat**: While some natural pelican predation likely occurred historically near the river mouth, current elevated predation (up to 30% of the prespawning aggregate) is attributed largely to anthropogenic habitat changes (diversion, channelization, fishway design) rather than natural conditions — and must be mitigated before reclassification can be reconsidered.

8. **Final recommendation**: The [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] concluded that the species' status has **not improved appreciably** and recommended continued Endangered classification, along with diligent pursuit of Recovery Plan actions (management, research, habitat protection/rehabilitation) by USFWS and other action agencies.

9. **Ongoing advisory role**: The Recovery Team agreed to continue serving as an advisory body to USFWS Regional Director Marvin Plenert, with its first task being review of the Service's 1994 operating plan for the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]].
