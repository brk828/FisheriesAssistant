# A Simple Method of Mounting Scales

#ichthyology #marine-biology #methodology #age-determination #Centrarchidae #Percidae #Serranidae

## Executive Summary

This technical note describes a practical method for mounting fish scales for age and growth studies, developed at the [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]]. The technique uses two microscope slides bound with waterproof adhesive tape to create permanent, easily stored mounts that preserve scales for repeated examination. The method has been successfully applied to several species in the families [[Centrarchidae]], [[Percidae]], and [[Serranidae]].

## Background & Description

Fish scale mounting has historically employed various media, but dry scales or scales mounted in water typically show essential features more clearly than other mounting methods. The primary disadvantages of temporary dry or wet mounts include the time required for preparation and difficulty relocating specific scales for future examination.

The described method involves mounting two or three scales from a fish between two microscope slides, with ends bound together using 1-inch squares of waterproof adhesive tape. Serial numbers or other pertinent data can be written directly on the tape binding. Some scales may require cleaning by rubbing between fingers or scrubbing with a small brush after soaking in water, though most scales display essential features without extensive cleaning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The method was developed for age and growth studies on freshwater fish species in Iowa, specifically targeting members of three families: [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes), [[Percidae]] (perches), and [[Serranidae]] (sea basses). For body-scale relationship determinations, scales should be selected from a precisely prescribed body location, typically just above or below the lateral line and even with the insertion of the dorsal fin.

## Key Findings

**Mounting Technique:**
- Small thin scales are most easily examined with a microprojector while dry, requiring no further treatment
- Thicker scales project more clearly when thoroughly wet
- The two-slide binding holds scales flat for examination

**Wetting Protocol:**
- Series of slide mounts can be placed in shallow pan with quarter-inch of hot distilled water
- Capillary action immediately draws water around scales
- Hot water soaks scales more rapidly than cold water
- Distilled water prevents residual salt deposits as water dries

**Examination Timing:**
- Thinner scales can be examined immediately after wetting
- Thicker scales often show air bubbles initially, requiring soaking
- Within 2 hours, even thickest scales reach optimum condition for examination
- Dried scales immediately regain optimum condition when re-moistened

**Important Considerations:**
- Scales expand somewhat when thoroughly moistened
- For body-scale relationship determinations, all scales must be read either dry or wet consistently
- Standardized body location for scale collection is essential for comparative studies
