## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific research paper but an **Arizona Game and Fish Department regulatory review memorandum** (Commission Order 42, effective 1/1/1995) covering harvest regulations for crustaceans, mollusks, and associated proposed changes to Commission Order 40 (fishing regulations). While largely administrative, the text contains substantive **conservation and fisheries management data**, including protections for the endangered [[Kanab Ambersnail]], management strategies for native fish recovery in the [[Grand Canyon]] (notably [[Humpback Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and [[Bluehead Sucker]]), and evidence-based angler survey data informing special regulation waters such as [[Bear Canyon Lake]] and [[Lee Valley Reservoir]]. The proposals reflect a broader shift in Arizona sportfish management to accommodate the recovery of 20 native fish species listed as threatened or endangered.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Kanab Ambersnail]]**: Occupies specialized wetland/spring-associated habitat; complete harvest closure (no open season) reflects extreme habitat specificity and vulnerability, consistent with endangered mollusk status.
- **[[White Amur]]**: Introduced as a biological control agent for aquatic macrophytes in irrigation infrastructure (Salt River Project and Yuma Water District canals). Its ecological role is anthropogenically engineered (weed suppression) rather than natural community integration; unauthorized harvest by anglers is disrupting management costs.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]**: Endangered native cyprinid in the [[Colorado River]]/[[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem; occupies swift, turbid mainstem river habitat historically shaped by pre-dam hydrology.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** and **[[Bluehead Sucker]]**: Native benthic catostomids in the Colorado River system, ecologically vulnerable to **predation and competition from introduced trout**, particularly at early life stages.
- **Introduced Trout (Salmonidae)** in the Grand Canyon: Function as a **predatory threat to juvenile native fish**, directly implicated in suppressing native recruitment — driving the proposed unlimited bag limit as an active removal/control strategy.
- **[[Sunfish]] (Centrarchidae)**: Serve a trophic role as forage/bait organisms in central Arizona reservoir food webs (e.g., [[Lake Pleasant]]), illustrating an angler-mediated bait-fish subsidy pathway.
- **[[Crappie]]**: Managed across a transboundary aquatic ecosystem (Colorado River bordering California/Nevada), highlighting the need for cross-jurisdictional harvest consistency in a shared riverine habitat.
- **Trout fisheries at [[Becker Lake]] and [[Bear Canyon Lake]]**: Represent put-and-take stocked salmonid fisheries where regulatory structure (bag limits, gear restrictions, seasonal closures) is used to balance angler pressure, catch-and-release survivorship, and recruitment into larger size classes.

## Key Findings

- **No regulatory change** proposed for Commission Order 42 (Crustaceans and Mollusks) for 1995 — status quo maintained, including the harvest ban on [[Kanab Ambersnail]].
- Arizona has **20 native fish species listed as threatened or endangered**, driving a fundamental shift in sportfish management philosophy (Executive-level framing).
- Proposed **unlimited trout bag limit in the Grand Canyon** is an explicit predator-control measure intended to reduce trout predation pressure on juvenile [[Humpback Chub]] and protect [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and [[Bluehead Sucker]] populations — a direct case of sportfish regulation being repurposed as a native species recovery tool.
- **Angler survey data (Clarkson and Dreyer, 1992)** at [[Bear Canyon Lake]] showed only 54–59% support for "artificial lure only" restrictions versus 82–90% support at the comparably managed [[Lee Valley Reservoir]], correlating with lower angler satisfaction and poor regulatory compliance at Bear Canyon — used as evidence to justify liberalizing gear restrictions (6 trout bag, bait allowed) while shifting toward stocked cutthroat/brook trout as a "featured species" approach.
- **[[White Amur]]** possession ban proposed to curb unauthorized harvest of a vegetation-control species stocked in irrigation canals, reflecting economic/ecological cost trade-offs of restocking non-native biocontrol fish.
- **[[Becker Lake]]** regulation redesign (year-round season, 2-trout limit, artificial lures/flies only Oct 1–May 25) intended to create a stable winter fishery amid uncertain continued stocking at nearby Nelson and Lyman lakes, balancing catch-and-release conservation with bait-fishing tradition.
- **Crappie bag limit standardization (25 fish)** proposed for the Colorado River south of the CA-NV border, [[Mittry Lake]], and [[Topock Marsh]] to harmonize with California's statewide limit — illustrating interstate management coordination for a shared river fishery.
- **[[Lake Pleasant]]** proposed to legalize live sunfish as bait, aligning it with regulatory precedent at other central Arizona reservoirs.
- Numerous **spatial/temporal closures** (e.g., areas below major dams — Davis, Hoover, Glen Canyon, Roosevelt; seasonal closures at Luna Lake, Becker Lake, Tonto Arm of Roosevelt Lake, Mittry Lake) function as protective refugia, likely tied to spawning periods or sensitive habitat zones for aquatic wildlife broadly (fish, crustaceans, and mollusks).
