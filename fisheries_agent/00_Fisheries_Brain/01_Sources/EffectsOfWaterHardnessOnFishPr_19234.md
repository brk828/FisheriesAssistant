# Effects of Water Hardness on Fish Production in Plastic Pools

#ichthyology #aquaculture #cyprinidae #water-chemistry #experimental-ecology

## Executive Summary

This experimental study investigated the effects of water hardness on fish production using [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) in circular plastic-lined pools at [[Auburn University]]. The research examined how different sources of hardness (calcium carbonate, calcium sulfate, and magnesium carbonate) and the presence of bottom soil affected fish production over a growing season. Fish production ranged from 33.4 to 998 lb/acre, with calcium sulfate additions producing significantly higher yields than calcium carbonate or magnesium carbonate. The study demonstrated that the anion accompanying calcium (sulfate vs. carbonate) had a greater effect on production than hardness alone, suggesting that water chemistry factors beyond simple hardness measurements influence fish productivity.

## Background & Description

Previous research by Hayes and Anthony (1964) and Turner (1960) found correlations between alkalinity and fish production in natural lakes, while Ryder (1965) identified relationships between total dissolved solids and fish production. However, in natural systems, fertility is correlated with multiple factors (alkalinity, total dissolved solids, hardness), making it difficult to isolate individual effects. Additionally, morphometric and climatic factors obscure these relationships in lakes.

This controlled experiment used 75 circular plastic-lined pools (10 ft diameter, 18-25 inches deep) filled with well water having approximately 30 ppm total hardness (two-thirds calcium hardness). Each pool was stocked with 50 [[Fathead Minnow]]s weighing approximately 50 gm total. The experiment ran from April through November (harvest November 14-27). Pools were fertilized monthly with 8 pounds nitrogen and 8 pounds P₂O₅ per acre equivalent. Drain tiles provided spawning sites.

Fifteen treatment combinations were tested with 5 replicates each:
- 45 pools contained 4 inches of [[Cecil Soil]]
- 30 pools had no soil
- Hardness was increased using CaCO₃, CaSO₄, or MgCO₃
- Target hardness increases: 0, 50, or 100 ppm as CaCO₃ equivalent

Water chemistry was analyzed four times at monthly intervals using [[EDTA Titration]] for calcium and total hardness. Fish were harvested by rotenone poisoning, with production measured as total weight harvested (most stocked adults did not survive to harvest).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The experiment addressed fundamental questions about water quality and aquatic productivity relevant to pond management and aquaculture. Natural water bodies show strong correlations between hardness-related parameters and fish production, but confounding variables make causation difficult to establish.

Water hardness affects multiple ecological processes:
- Nutrient availability and cycling
- Primary productivity (phytoplankton and algae)
- pH buffering capacity
- Osmoregulation in fish

The use of plastic pools eliminated confounding factors present in natural systems:
- Uniform morphometry
- Controlled stocking and fertilization
- Elimination of predation and competition variables
- Standardized climatic exposure

[[Cecil Soil]], a common southeastern U.S. soil type, was tested to determine whether bottom sediments contribute significantly to productivity in fertilized systems. The soil's lime requirement was satisfied in one treatment (100 ppm from combined CaCO₃ and MgCO₃).

## Key Findings

**Effect of Bottom Soil:**
- Cecil soil presence showed no significant effect on fish production (p > 0.25)
- Mean production with soil: 234.1 gm/pool
- Mean production without soil: 190.7 gm/pool
- Large variation between replicate pools obscured any potential soil effect
- In fertilized pools, nutrients from soil appear to play a negligible role

**Effect of Calcium Hardness:**
- Fish production increased significantly with calcium hardness across all pools (p < 0.005)
- Relationship: F = 118.1 + 2.32C (r = 0.51), where F = grams fish/pool, C = ppm Ca as CaCO₃
- Calcium hardness ranged from 16.7 to 157 ppm as CaCO₃

**Effect of Total Hardness:**
- Total hardness significantly correlated with fish production (p < 0.005)
- Relationship: F = 100.4 + 1.708H (r = 0.43), where H = ppm total hardness as CaCO₃
- Magnesium hardness ranged from 3.7 to 118 ppm as CaCO₃

**Source of Hardness Effects:**

*Calcium Sulfate (CaSO₄):*
- Produced significantly higher fish production than all other treatments (p < 0.005)
- Linear relationship: F = 20.5 + 1.68C (23 pools analyzed)
- Production range: up to 815.8 gm/pool (998 lb/acre) at 100 ppm addition
- Mean production at 100 ppm CaSO₄: 392.9-482.9 gm/pool

*Calcium Carbonate (CaCO₃):*
- Showed significant but weaker relationship (p < 0.05)
- Linear: F = 287.1 - 3.11C (r = 0.39)
- Quadratic: F = 540.2 - 17.26C + 0.182C² (r = 0.48)
- Both regressions suggest decreased production with increased CaCO₃
- Production at 100 ppm CaCO₃: 86.6-116.6 gm/pool (lower than controls)

*Magnesium Carbonate (MgCO₃):*
- No significant relationship between magnesium hardness and fish production
- Pools with higher magnesium showed lower production, but not statistically significant
- Lowest production recorded: 27.8 gm/pool (33.4 lb/acre) at 100 ppm MgCO₃

**Anion Effects:**
- At comparable calcium levels, CaSO₄ pools produced more fish than CaCO₃ pools
- Greater production with CaSO₄ not simply due to higher solubility
- Sulfate may enhance fish production
- Carbonate (or associated factors like increased pH) may inhibit production
- The author suggests increased pH associated with carbonate as a possible inhibiting mechanism

**Statistical Notes:**
- Three pools suffered complete mortality from unknown causes (excluded from analysis)
- One pool leaked and went dry (excluded from analysis)
- Large variation between replicate pools reduced statistical power
- Analysis used computer programs for ANOVA and linear, quadratic, and cubic regression
