# The Gymnotidae of Trans-Andean Colombia and Ecuador

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gymnotidae #neotropical-ichthyology #taxonomy #species-distribution

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic report by [[Carl H. Eigenmann]] and Homer G. Fisher documents the distribution and collection records of the family [[Gymnotidae]] (electric knifefishes) from Trans-Andean Colombia and Ecuador. The study reports on extensive collections made between 1912-1913 in the [[Magdalena River]], [[Cauca River]], [[Dagua River]], [[San Juan River]], [[Atrato River]], and western Ecuador. Seven species are documented, including one new species ([[Sternarchus mariae]]) and range extensions for [[Sternarchus leptorhynchus]]. The work provides detailed morphometric data, collection localities, and specimen catalog numbers for museum collections at Indiana University and Carnegie Museum.

## Background & Description

The [[Gymnotidae]] family comprises weakly electric fishes distributed across South and Central America. Prior to this study, four species were known from the Trans-Andean region: [[Gymnotus carapo]], [[Sternopygus macrurus]], [[Eigenmannia virescens]], and [[Hypopomus brevirostris]]. These four species represent the most widely distributed gymnotids, occurring from Central America through the [[Amazon Basin]], [[Orinoco River]], Guiana, Uruguay, southeastern Brazil, and the [[La Plata Basin]].

**Sternarchus mariae** (new species): Type specimen 201 mm from [[Girardot]], paratype 273 mm from [[Apulo]]. Head 6.3-7.1 in length to end of anal; depth 0.92-0.94 in head length; anal rays 173-176; snout 2.5-2.7 in head length; eye 5.5-5.7 in interorbital, 5-6 in head. Head decurved; gape reaching vertical from eye; light buff ground color with minute dark chromatophores more abundant dorsally; uninterrupted whitish streak along median dorsal line from snout to caudal base. Named for Brother Apolinar Maria, director of the Natural History Museum of Bogota.

**Sternarchus rostratus**: Head 5.3-6.5 in length to anal end; depth 1.1-1.3 in head length; anal rays 151-175; snout 2.3-2.7 in head; eye 3-6.1 in interorbital. Evenly dotted or marbled, darker dorsally with narrow indistinct light stripe along median dorsal line.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Collections were made across multiple river basins representing both Pacific and Atlantic drainages of Colombia and Ecuador. The [[Pacific Slope]] localities included the [[Dagua River]], [[San Juan Basin]], [[Calima River]], [[Patia Basin]], and western Ecuador. Atlantic drainage sites included the [[Magdalena Basin]], [[Cauca River]], and [[Atrato Basin]].

[[Sternopygus macrurus]] proved to be the only gymnotid species found in western Ecuador, collected extensively at [[Guayaquil]] market (31 specimens, 282-700 mm). [[Gymnotus carapo]] reaches the farthest north of any gymnotid, occurring in rivers and lakes on both slopes of Guatemala and recently recorded from the [[Rio San Juan]] of Trans-Andean Colombia.

The collections document habitat use in small creeks, tributaries, and main river channels across elevational gradients from lowland coastal areas to upland tributaries. Species were collected from localities including [[Soplaviento]], [[Manigru]], [[Truando]], [[Istmina]], [[Cartago]], [[Cali]], [[Cordova]], [[Barbacoas]], and [[Villavicencio]].

## Key Findings

**Range Extensions and New Records:**
- [[Sternarchus leptorhynchus]], previously known only from the [[Essequibo Basin]] of British Guiana, documented from Pacific slope Colombia: one specimen (130 mm) from creek near mouth of [[Calima River]], one specimen (209 mm) from [[Cordova]] in [[Dagua River]]
- [[Sternarchus rostratus]] found in [[Magdalena Basin]] (Atlantic drainage) and [[Tuyra Basin]] of Pacific slope Panama
- Five gymnotid species now confirmed from Trans-Andean region (previously four)

**New Species Description:**
- [[Sternarchus mariae]] sp. nov. described from [[Girardot]] (type) and [[Apulo]] (paratype) in [[Magdalena Basin]]

**Collection Summary by Species:**

1. **Sternarchus leptorhynchus**: 2 specimens from Pacific slope
2. **Sternarchus rostratus**: 24 specimens from [[Girardot]], [[Apulo]], [[Cartago]], [[Cali]] (94-304 mm)
3. **Sternarchus mariae**: 2 specimens (type series)
4. **Hypopomus brevirostris**: 29 specimens from [[Soplaviento]], [[Manigru]], [[Truando]], [[Calima River]], [[Raspadura]], [[Quibdo]] (50-168 mm)
5. **Gymnotus carapo**: 8 specimens from [[Calima River]] and [[Truando]] (75-306 mm)
6. **Sternopygus macrurus**: 89+ specimens from 17 localities (29-700 mm); only species in western Ecuador
7. **Eigenmannia virescens**: 245+ specimens from 11 localities including [[Barbacoas]], [[Rio Magui]], [[Calamar]], [[Puerto del Rio]] (46-414 mm)

**Taxonomic Notes:**
- [[Sternarchus rostratus]] synonymized with Sternarchus spurrellii Regan, 1914
- [[Hypopomus brevirostris]] synonymized with Hypopomus occidentalis Regan, 1914

**Collectors:** Collections made by [[Carl H. Eigenmann]] (January-April 1912), Chas. Wilson (January-March 1913), Arthur Henn (1913), Manuel Gonzales, with specimens deposited at Indiana University Museum (I.U.M.) and Carnegie Museum (C.M.).
