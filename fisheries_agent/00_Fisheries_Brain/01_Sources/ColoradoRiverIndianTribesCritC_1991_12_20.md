# Colorado River Indian Tribes (CRIT) Canal Drawdown — Razorback Sucker Salvage Operation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #razorback-sucker #freshwater-ecology #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (dated December 20, 1991) coordinating a field salvage operation tied to the annual drawdown of irrigation canals on the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]] (CRIT) Reservation near Parker, Arizona. The canal drawdown, conducted annually to facilitate maintenance, strands fish and creates an opportunistic sampling window for capturing the federally endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The memo references a prior successful salvage event in 1989 (21 razorback suckers captured) and announces a new sampling effort scheduled for January 5–7, 1992. The note is administrative/logistical in nature rather than a data-rich scientific report, but it documents an important recurring survey methodology used in [[Razorback Sucker Recovery]] efforts within the lower [[Colorado River]] basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** The captures occur not in natural river habitat but in **irrigation canals** on the CRIT Reservation, which are hydrologically connected to the lower [[Colorado River]] mainstem. This suggests razorback suckers are entrained from the river into agricultural diversion infrastructure.
- **Stranding dynamic:** Annual canal drawdowns for maintenance purposes cause water levels to drop rapidly, stranding fish (including razorback suckers and presumably other species) in isolated pools or dry canal beds, allowing for efficient, concentrated capture.
- **Conservation significance:** This stranding event serves as a *de facto* sampling/recovery mechanism for a species listed as endangered, functioning within the broader [[Razorback Sucker Recovery]] Program as a low-cost, high-yield opportunity to monitor and rescue individuals otherwise vulnerable to entrainment mortality in canal systems.
- **Interagency network:** The cooperator list (Bureau of Reclamation, Arizona State University Center for Environmental Studies, Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES)-Flagstaff, Pinetop Fishery Assistance Office, San Carlos Fishery Management, CRIT Fish and Game) indicates this drawdown event is embedded in a multi-agency [[Colorado River Basin]] native fish monitoring network.

## Key Findings
- **Recurring opportunistic sampling event:** The CRIT irrigation system's annual canal drawdown is used as a standardized recovery/capture opportunity for [[Razorback Sucker]] population monitoring.
- **Historical capture data point:** In 1989, **21 razorback suckers** were captured during this drawdown event — providing a baseline reference for expected catch effort/success in subsequent years.
- **Planned 1992 sampling:** A follow-up sampling effort was scheduled for **January 5–7, 1992**, coordinated from the CRIT Fish and Game Office in Parker, AZ.
- **Equipment logistics:** Handwritten annotations reference field gear needs, including **PIT tags (PIT #23)** and **injectors (1–2)**, indicating that captured razorback suckers were likely marked with [[Passive Integrated Transponder (PIT) Tags]] for individual identification and long-term recovery tracking — a standard tool in endangered fish monitoring programs.
- **Multi-agency collaboration:** The event drew participation from federal (USFWS, Bureau of Reclamation), tribal (CRIT Fish and Game), and academic (ASU-Center for Environmental Studies) cooperators, reflecting the coordinated nature of [[Endangered Fish Recovery Programs]] in the lower Colorado River.
- **Data limitation:** This source is a logistical planning memo, not a peer-reviewed study; it lacks quantitative catch data, size/age structure, or habitat measurements beyond the anecdotal 1989 capture count.
