# Rocky Intertidal Zonation at Puerto Peñasco

#marine-biology #intertidal-ecology #invertebrate-zoology

## Executive Summary

This preliminary study proposes a four-zone scheme of rocky intertidal zonation at [[Puerto Peñasco]], based on surveys of four locations at [[Norse Beach]] and the beach in front of the [[Desalting Plant]]. The zones (I to IV, from the top of the beach down) follow [[Ricketts Zonation Scheme]] categories by organism type, but their elevation boundaries are set by local tides rather than the California values Ricketts proposed. The intertidal zone is about 22 feet high. Each zone is dominated by a characteristic organism, and the boundaries are often blurred. The scheme is explicitly provisional and is expected to be revised as knowledge of the area grows. The survey was carried out by Peter E. Pickens with students in Marine Invertebrate Zoology (Zoology 223a) during the first summer session of 1965. Tidal data were analyzed by Dr. Brian Matthews of the Institute of Atmospheric Physics, University of Arizona.

## Background & Description

The study sets out an arbitrary but workable framework for classifying the shore by tidal height and dominant biota. The zone boundaries are:

- **Zone I (splash zone):** +19 ft to +11 ft. Contains [[Littorina Aspera]] snails, like the California Zone I.
- **Zone II:** +11 ft to roughly +7 ft (the source value is OCR-ambiguous), reaching about [[Mean Sea Level]].
- **Zone III:** [[Mean Sea Level]] to 0 ft, the "Head of the Lower Low Tides."
- **Zone IV:** Zero Datum to −4 ft, the "Lowest of the Low Tides."

The [[Zero Datum]] equals +3.9 ft on the tide gauge at Puerto Peñasco. Zone I is much broader here than in California. Its lower limit lies about halfway between mean high water and mean sea level, not at mean high water.

The width of the intertidal zone varies with the shore type. It is less than 50 ft wide at the steep granite outcrops near [[Pelican Point]] and several hundred feet wide on the gently sloping flats of Norse Beach and the Desalting Plant.

Dominant organisms by zone:

| Zone | Dominant organism |
|---|---|
| I | Blue-green algae ([[Blue-Green Algae]]) |
| II | Large barnacles ([[Tetraclita Squamosa]]) |
| III | Small barnacles ([[Balanus]]) |
| IV | Soft coral ([[Palythoa Ignota]]) and brown sponges |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Zone I subdivision at Norse Beach.** The lower subzone runs from +11 ft to about +15 ft (the mean of the higher high tides). Its upper edge is a sharp line between bare granite and rock encrusted with near-black blue-green algae. The upper subzone has bare white rock and very few marine organisms. The only conspicuous animal is the isopod [[Ligia Occidentalis]], which ranges across the whole intertidal zone at low tide. Where rock is absent, sandy-beach isopods (mainly [[Tylos Latreilli]]) and amphipods take over as the main scavengers. At Norse Beach, sand reaches from the highest intertidal level to the edge of the sandstone-coquina reef, so most rocky intertidal forms of Zones I and II are missing there. At the Desalting Plant, the upper reef edge has large barnacles, but the area above it is sandy beach, which limits rock-dwelling habitat in Zone II.

**Basaltic boulders.** At the Desalting Plant, small round basaltic boulders lie in tide pools and on the reef surface. Their upper limit is about Mean Sea Level, the top of Zone III. They are rarely found more than about 3½ ft below Mean Sea Level (the source value is partly garbled). The undersides of the boulders shelter many animals. Some species, such as chitons, extend further into the lower intertidal at Norse Beach than at the Desalting Plant, where suitable shelter is scarce.

**Upward displacement in tide pools.** Some Zone IV species occur higher up in permanent tide pools. The most frequent of these are the brown algae [[Padina]] and [[Sargassum]], the corals [[Palythoa Ignota]] and [[Porites Californica]], and a brown sponge. The author recommends that any zonation scheme note this upward displacement, and that tide-pool organisms be placed in a separate category or ignored when the general scheme is applied.

**Boundary blurring.** Boundaries between dominant organisms are not always sharp. Blue-green algae may extend into Zone II, and large barnacles may extend into Zone I. The zones are only clearly visible when a wide area of shore is viewed.

## Key Findings

- The Puerto Peñasco shore is divided into four preliminary zones. Their elevation limits (+19 to −4 ft, about 22 ft total) differ from the California standard because local tide levels set the boundaries.
- Each zone has a dominant organism: blue-green algae (I), *Tetraclita* (II), *Balanus* (III), and *Palythoa* with brown sponges (IV).
- Sandy beach replaces rocky habitat at Norse Beach and the Desalting Plant. Zone I and II rock fauna are therefore sparse or absent there, and sandy-beach isopods and amphipods replace *Ligia* as the main scavengers in places.
- Basaltic boulders at the Desalting Plant provide shelter, with an upper limit near Mean Sea Level. Their undersides support many animals and extend some species, such as chitons, lower on the shore at Norse Beach.
- Zone IV species occur upward in permanent tide pools, so a zonation scheme must account for this displacement or treat tide-pool organisms separately.
- The associated species checklist contains about 110 numbered taxa. It includes gastropods (for example *Littorina*, *Nerita*, *Tegula*, *Cypraea*), bivalves (for example *Lithophaga*, *Ostrea*, *Modiolus*), barnacles, crabs (for example *Eriphia*, *Petrolisthes*), echinoderms (for example *Heliaster*, *Arbacia*, *Echinometra*), chitons, sponges, tunicates, corals, and algae. No fish are listed in the text.
- The study is preliminary and is expected to be revised as more data become available.
