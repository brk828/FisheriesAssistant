# A New Species of *Serradigitus* from Central California (Scorpiones: Vaejovidae)

#arachnology #scorpiones #vaejovidae

> **Scope note:** This source is a terrestrial arachnid taxonomy paper. It contains no fish, aquatic invertebrate, plankton, or water chemistry data, so the aquatic-ecosystem sections below record what is available and mark the rest as N/A.

## Executive Summary

Williams and Berke (1986) in the *[[Pan-Pacific Entomologist]]* revise and resurrect the scorpion genus *[[Serradigitus]]* Stahnke, 1974, which had been placed in synonymy with *[[Vaejovis]]* in 1980. They redefine the genus with diagnostic characters, transfer a set of *Vaejovis* species into it, and describe a new species, *[[Serradigitus torridus]]*, from the Mojave Desert of Kern County, California. The paper is taxonomic and has no aquatic or ecological monitoring component.

## Background & Description

**Historical context.** Stahnke (1974) proposed *Serradigitus* for taxa associated with the *wupatkiensis* group of *Vaejovis*, because *Vaejovis* had become very large and diverse. Later work found Baja California species intermediate between the two genera (Williams, 1980). This led to the genus being sunk into synonymy and the *wupatkiensis* group being returned to *Vaejovis* (see [[Williams 1980 Scorpions of Baja California]]).

**Revised diagnosis.** The authors argue that several characters Stahnke used do not hold up:
- Stahnke gave 27 chela trichobothria for *Serradigitus*, but neither his table nor illustration supports this. All specimens examined had 26, which is also typical of *Vaejovis*.
- Trichobothrial position on the pedipalp brachium varied too much to be diagnostic.

The revised genus is characterized by traits including an emarginate carapace frontal margin, three lateral ocelli per group, elongate hook-like terminal denticles on the chela fingers, metasomal keels ending in elongate denticles, and pectines with three anterior marginal lamellae. Females have proximal pectine teeth 1–3 often more elongate or swollen.

**Transfers.** Fifteen species are listed as belonging to *Serradigitus* (for example *S. wupatkiensis*, *S. gertschi*, *S. armadentis*, *S. joshuaensis*), along with a further group of *Vaejovis* species that the authors consider to have secondarily evolved the elongate terminal denticle (for example *V. peninsularis*, *V. janssi*, *V. baueri*, *V. pacificus*, *V. bechteli*, *V. littoralis*). The abstract states that sixteen species are placed in *Serradigitus*, which differs from the fifteen names in the species list.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The type locality is a hot, dry desert setting in Red Rock Canyon State Recreation Area, Kern County. Additional records come from Jaw Bone Canyon and an area 20 miles north of Mojave. The species name *torridus* refers to this habitat.
- **Water/aquatic data:** None reported. No water chemistry, aquatic invertebrate, plankton, or freshwater habitat data are provided.
- **Specimen depository:** Holotype and allotype are held at the [[California Academy of Sciences]] (Entomology Type No. 15750).

## Key Findings

**1. Genus resurrected and redefined.** *[[Serradigitus]]* is restored as a valid genus distinct from *[[Vaejovis]]*, with revised diagnostic characters. The authors reject the 27-trichobothria claim and the brachial trichobothrium position as diagnostic.

**2. New species described.** *[[Serradigitus torridus]]* (Williams & Berke) is described from Red Rock Canyon, Kern County, California.
- Total length up to 31 mm in males and 34 mm in females (diagnosis).
- Holotype male: total length 31.0 mm. Allotype: 33.1 mm.
- Adult paratypes measured 24–32 mm; juveniles 16–20 mm.
- Pectine teeth: 16–18 in males, 14 in females.
- Chela length to palm width ratio: 3.9 in males, 4.1 in females.
- Seven macrosetae formula on the vesicle ventrally (7 pairs).

**3. Distinguishing features.** The new species is compared with three related congeners:
- *[[Serradigitus gertschi]]*: *S. torridus* has 2 pairs of macrosetae on the dorsal keels of metasomal segment IV (versus 1 pair), and a more swollen palm (chela/palm ratio 4.3 or less).
- *[[Serradigitus wupatkiensis]]*: *S. torridus* has a more swollen palm (palm length/width ratio 1.8 or less).
- *[[Serradigitus armadentis]]*: *S. torridus* has a less hirsute telson with about 7 ventral macrosetae pairs (not more than 9), and trichobothrium *ip* lies between supernumerary denticles 5–6 rather than proximal to 6.

**4. Type series.** The holotype (male, collected 8 Oct 1980) and allotype (collected 16 Aug 1981) were both collected by S. C. Williams in Red Rock Canyon. The paratype lots listed in the text total 18 specimens when the per-lot counts are summed (lots from 1968, 1972, 1980, 1981, and 1984, across Kern County localities).

**5. Phylogenetic conclusion.** The *wupatkiensis* group is heterogeneous. Some *Vaejovis* species have evolved the *Serradigitus*-type terminal denticle independently, so the characteristic is not a reliable indicator of shared ancestry.
