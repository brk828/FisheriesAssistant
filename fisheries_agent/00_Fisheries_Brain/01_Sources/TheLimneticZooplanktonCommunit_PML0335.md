# Limnetic Zooplankton Community of Boulder Basin, Lake Mead, and Its Role in the Metalimnetic Oxygen Minimum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #zooplankton

## Executive Summary

This master's thesis by Thomas A. Burke (University of Nevada, Las Vegas, April 1977) examines whether the limnetic zooplankton community of [[Boulder Basin]], [[Lake Mead]], contributes to the [[Metalimnetic Oxygen Minimum]] that regularly forms during summer stratification. Zooplankton were sampled from May 1975 to April 1976, and oxygen budgets were estimated for 17 June to 28 August 1975. The study concludes that zooplankton respiration accounts for a substantial share of the oxygen lost from the metalimnion, although it is not the sole consumer. The thesis is a limnological study of invertebrates and water chemistry; no fish data are reported.

## Background & Description

Earlier work (Hoffman et al., 1967) proposed three causes for the oxygen minimum: oxidation of organic matter, sediment oxygen uptake on a midwater shelf, and zooplankton respiration. Bacterial studies (Egdorf and Tew, 1975) and reviews of lake elevation and outlet depth made several of these explanations unlikely. The thesis therefore focuses on zooplankton respiration as the primary candidate.

[[Lake Mead]] was formed in 1935 by impoundment of the [[Colorado River]] behind [[Hoover Dam]]. It is a deep, subtropical, monomictic lake with surface temperatures of 10.5 to 27 °C. Boulder Basin is the lowermost of four major basins, with a maximum depth of 135 m and a surface area of about 30,000 acres. Sampling occurred at a permanent station off Saddle Island (maximum depth 65 m).

The zooplankton community comprised roughly ten genera with major and minor components listed in the source tables. Major taxa included the rotifers *Asplanchna priodonta*, *Synchaeta*, *Polyarthra*, *Keratella cochlearis*, and *Collotheca pelagica*; the cladocerans *Daphnia* and *Bosmina longirostris*; and the copepods *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi*, *Mesocyclops edax*, *Diaptomus clavipes*, and *Diaptomus siciloides*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stratification and oxygen.** Thermal stratification was present for much of the summer. The metalimnion was about 30 m thick and was split into two 15 m layers (10 to 25 m and 25 to 40 m) for analysis. Wind-driven currents and internal waves moved oxygen downward and upward across the metalimnion.

**Sampling methods.** Zooplankton were filtered through a #20 Clarke-Bumpus net (about 76 µm mesh) at 5 m intervals. Summer samples used a Sears Model 5553.2592 pump (8 L/min) and winter samples a gasoline pump (12.5 L/min). Counts were made in Sedgwick-Rafter cells.

**Diapause and resting stages.** Several taxa have resting stages. *Synchaeta* produces resting eggs (found October and November), *Polyarthra* hatches from resting eggs, *Daphnia* produces ephippia, and copepods diapause as copepodids or cysts. These stages help keep a full age-class assemblage present throughout the water column.

**Vertical migration.** Rotifers and cladocerans migrated vertically, but in summer their movement was restricted. *Polyarthra* was confined to the upper 20 m in July when the oxygen minimum was present. Copepods showed active migration, and *Mesocyclops edax* moved over a greater range than *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi*.

**Energy transport.** Phytoplankton (*Fragilaria* chains) sank through the metalimnion and were largely unused by herbivores. Burke and Baker (manuscript) indicated that phytoplankton contributed 33 to 58 percent of upper-metalimnion oxygen loss.

## Key Findings

- **Zooplankton respiration is a major oxygen sink.** From 17 June to 28 August 1975, zooplankton consumed 33 to 69 percent of the daily oxygen lost from the upper metalimnion (10 to 25 m) and 12 to 42 percent from the lower metalimnion (25 to 40 m). The seasonal averages were 44 percent for the upper zone and 25 percent for the lower zone.
- **Zooplankton are not the only consumers.** Phytoplankton and bacteria also contribute. Phytoplankton accounted for 33 to 58 percent of oxygen loss in the upper metalimnion, so zooplankton and phytoplankton together explain most of the upper-zone loss.
- **Zooplankton concentrate in the upper metalimnion.** Most organisms occurred at 10 to 25 m during summer. Seventy-five percent of 54 depth-of-maximum-density observations fell between 10 and 25 m.
- **Oxygen transport was calculated with a heat-transport method.** Eddy diffusivity was estimated from heat gain. Oxygen transported across the 12.5 m plane was 2 to 15 times greater than across the 37.5 m plane. A worked example gave 126 µg O₂ L⁻¹ d⁻¹ transported into the upper metalimnion between 17 June and 2 July 1975.
- **Community structure is typical of large, deep North American lakes.** The species composition matches the typical large, deep freshwater communities cited by Pennak and Patalas.
- **Seasonal succession followed a long spring period.** Abundance peaked in spring through July, with a short autumn peak in November. *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi* dominated in spring and early summer, and *Mesocyclops edax* replaced it in July.
- **Diurnal migration was restricted.** Vertical movement of zooplankton was limited by oxygen and other conditions in the metalimnion, and migration amplitudes were modest for most taxa.
- **Respiration rates were estimated from literature values.** Rates were taken from Kibby (1971), Scherbakoff (1935), Comita (1968), Vollenweider and Ravera (1958), and others, and were adjusted to 15 and 20 °C with Q10 values. Duval and Geen (1976) suggested daily respiration may be underestimated by about 25 percent.
- **Zooplankton and phytoplankton jointly account for most upper-metalimnion oxygen loss.** The thesis concludes that the metalimnetic oxygen minimum results from the combined respiration of the whole aquatic community, with zooplankton as a major share.
