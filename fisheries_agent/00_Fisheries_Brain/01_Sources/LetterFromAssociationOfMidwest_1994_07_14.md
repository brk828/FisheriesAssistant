## Executive Summary

This 1994 letter from [[Association of Midwest Fish and Wildlife Agencies]] (AMFWA) President Bruce L. McCloskey to [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Director Mollie Beattie articulates a formal grievance from 14 member states regarding perceived federal disregard for state input in natural resource decision-making. Three primary issues are raised: (1) unilateral development of [[Ecosystem Management]] policy without adequate state consultation; (2) new [[Refuge Management Priorities]] that de-emphasize hunting, fishing, and recreational access in favor of ecological diversity and endangered species protection, including an abrupt nontoxic shot mandate; and (3) procedural failures in the [[Endangered Species Act]] listing process for the [[Swift Fox]], including lack of timely state notification. The letter requests a direct meeting between AMFWA leadership and the Service Director to resolve deteriorating state-federal relations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no specific fish ecology is detailed, the letter touches on habitat and management-relevant ecological themes:

- **Refuge and WPA habitat management**: Federal [[National Wildlife Refuge]] lands and [[Waterfowl Production Area]]s are managed under competing priorities of ecological diversity, endangered species conservation, and consumptive recreational use (hunting, fishing, trapping).
- **Nontoxic shot policy**: A Service-mandated transition to nontoxic (non-lead) shot across all refuges and WPAs by 1995, intended to reduce toxic lead exposure to wildlife (particularly waterfowl) in wetland ecosystems, is noted as a management action lacking adequate phase-in and public education.
- **Swift Fox range**: The [[Swift Fox]] historically ranges across agricultural and grassland ecosystems in the central U.S., with [[Kansas]] and [[Colorado]] specifically cited as states permitting regulated harvest, indicating an established population capable of supporting limited take.
- **Ecosystem Management framework**: The letter references the Clinton Administration-era push for landscape-scale [[Ecosystem Management]] approaches across federal agencies, intended to integrate shared habitat and species responsibilities across jurisdictional boundaries.

## Key Findings

- AMFWA represents **14 Midwestern member states** collectively raising concerns about USFWS governance practices.
- The Service adopted an **ecosystem management approach** without early state involvement; implementation varies inconsistently across USFWS regions, generating field-level confusion.
- New **refuge management priorities** shift emphasis away from hunting, fishing, and public recreation toward ecological diversity and endangered species management, raising concern among stakeholder hunters/anglers who fund refuge acquisition and management through license/excise revenues.
- A **nontoxic shot requirement** was mandated for all refuges and WPAs starting 1995 without sufficient state consultation or public education phase-in, risking noncompliance and enforcement challenges.
- The **Swift Fox listing proposal** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] was published in the Federal Register without adequate advance notice to affected states; the associated "90-Day Finding on Pending Petition for the Swift Fox" was not shared with states for six months despite citing state biologists' data.
- [[Kansas]] and [[Colorado]] currently permit regulated swift fox harvest and are directly impacted by the potential federal listing.
- AMFWA formally requests direct, pre-decisional consultation with states on future policy actions and requests an in-person meeting with USFWS Director Mollie Beattie prior to the September [[International Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies]] (IAFWA) meeting.
- The letter characterizes state-federal relations as being at their **lowest point in many years**, citing repeated unaddressed concerns raised through IAFWA staff and regional associations.
