# Protocol for Quantifying Adult Humpback Chub Microhabitat in the Colorado River of Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This methods protocol, authored by R.A. Valdez and W.J. Masslich, sets out a standard procedure for measuring the [[Microhabitat]] used by adult [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Colorado River]] of [[Grand Canyon]]. The work is framed around two objectives: characterizing habitat complexes used by adult fish compared with unused sites, and quantifying how those complexes change with flow under [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. The protocol is a plan rather than a results report. It relies on one year of [[Radiotelemetry]] data (October 1990 through September 1991) to select target sites, and it proposes a field method built on [[Cross-Sectional Transects]], point measurements of depth, velocity, substrate and cover, and GIS-style integration of those layers.

## Background & Description

The protocol addresses a measurement problem. Adult Humpback Chub live in the Colorado River of Grand Canyon, where the authors describe great depths, high velocities, shearing currents and wide channels. These conditions make conventional microhabitat measurement difficult. Daily flows also vary, from about 8,000 to 26,000 cfs, which complicates depth and velocity measurement for any given flow.

Radiotracking gave the authors a good understanding of the [[Macrohabitat]] used by these fish and identified specific sites occupied by more than one radiotagged individual. Macrohabitat dynamics relative to flow were being mapped at the surface to assess the effects of dam operations. Few microhabitat measurements had been taken, however, so the protocol was developed to fill that gap.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area lies in the Colorado River corridor, with the target sites located relative to river mile (RM) markers and the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) inflow:

- **[[Awatubi Site]]** (RM 58.7–58.8): well above the LCR inflow.
- **[[60-Mile Site]]** (RM 60.0–60.1): immediately above the LCR inflow.
- **[[ESPN Site]]** (RM 60.7–60.8): immediately above the LCR inflow.
- **[[Carbon Creek Site]]** (RM 64.6–64.7): downstream of the LCR inflow.

The protocol also calls for comparison sites that fish did not use, selected for the same detailed analysis. The authors note that the method was designed around these high-use sites and that additional sites may be added as more information is gathered.

Velocity sampling is constrained by telemetry. Radiosignal extinction occurs at about 4 m depth, so velocity is measured 2 m below the surface. Transects are placed in winter, when boat traffic is lowest.

## Key Findings

The document is a protocol and contains no species-level results, population counts or statistical analyses. Its main methodological points are:

1. **Objectives.** Characterize the habitat complexes used by adult Humpback Chub by measuring depth, velocity, substrate and cover in and around used and unused sites. Quantify how these complexes change with flow from Glen Canyon Dam operations.

2. **Site selection.** Target sites are areas occupied by two or more radiotagged adults during the first year of study. Four are identified so far. Additional sites may be added as more information becomes available.

3. **Transect design.** About 200 linear meters of river are covered per site, with a cross-section every 20 m. Each transect is anchored by a [[Kevlar Cable]] stretched between the shorelines, and the boat holds position at midstream. An observer is posted on each shore to watch for oncoming traffic so the cable can be quickly released. Each point is located on acetate overlays on 1:1200 aerial photographs.

4. **Habitat parameters.**
   - *Depth* is recorded with an extendible [[Philadelphia Rod]] in shallow water and a [[Fathometer]] in deeper water.
   - *Substrate* is assessed by probing with the depth rod and by reading fathometer signal characteristics.
   - *Velocity* is measured 2 m below the surface using a [[Marsh-McBirney Current Meter]] and grouped into velocity regions (e.g., 0–0.5, 0.5–1.0, 1.0–1.5 m/s). The exact class ranges are to be set through field trials. The aim is to compare velocity regions used by fish with those not used.
   - *Cover* is identified and mapped on 1:1200 scale maps. Features include overhanging cover such as tapeats ledges, instream boulders, sand shoals and deep channels.
   - *Habitat type* is delineated on the cross-sectional transects to refine macrohabitat mapping.

5. **Integration.** Each parameter is mapped on 1:1200 acetate overlays, with isopleths connecting common depths, velocity regions and substrate types. The parameter maps are entered into the [[MIFPS System]] to compare parameters across flows and across used and unused sites, and to overlay all four parameters (depth, velocity, substrate, cover) to characterize habitat complexes and their change with flow.

6. **Staffing and time.** Five people are required: two shore observers and three boat crew (operating the boat, holding the cable and recording data). The work is expected to take about 4 hours per site. It must be done within a short period of relatively stable flow so that depth and velocity represent a single flow condition. The authors state a preference for accurate data from a few sites over less reliable data from many.

7. **Equipment and cost.** The method needs a research boat (a BIO/WEST electrofishing SU-16 is named), an extendible Philadelphia rod, a Marsh-McBirney current meter with a 3 m rod, a fathometer, and 1,000 feet of Kevlar cable. Cable costs about $1,000 and the current meter about $2,600. Total additional equipment cost is estimated at about $4,000, not currently budgeted under the contract. No additional labor costs are expected, because cross-section time is drawn from existing radiotelemetry work. Existing substrate characterizations and channel profiles by other investigators are to be consulted at the GCES office.
