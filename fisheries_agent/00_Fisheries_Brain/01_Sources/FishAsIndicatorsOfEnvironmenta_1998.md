## Executive Summary

This study by Soto-Galera et al. (1998) uses fish species distribution as a bioindicator to assess environmental degradation across the **[[Rio Lerma Basin]]**, one of Mexico's most significant hydrographic regions and a hotspot of freshwater fish endemism (72% basin endemism among 42 native species). By comparing historical fish occurrence records (1885–1975, reconstructed from museum collections at ENCB-IPN) against contemporary resampling (1985–1993) across 116 sites, the authors document catastrophic ecosystem decline. Over 50% of sites can no longer support fish life, driven by groundwater extraction, water diversion, urbanization, and untreated industrial/municipal discharge. Only 15% of sites retain environmentally sensitive species, concentrated in protected highland streams and lakes. Three endemic species — *Algansea barbata*, *Chirostoma charari*, and *Chirostoma compressum* — are potentially extinct. The **[[Rio Turbio]]** stands out as the only lowland river maintaining relatively good ecological quality, making it a priority conservation target.

## Background & Description

This paper does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of individual species; its focus is distributional and ecological. However, it catalogs the taxonomic composition of the Rio Lerma fish fauna across nine families, establishing a reference framework for identification and tolerance classification:

- **[[Petromyzontidae]]** (lampreys): *Lampetra geminis*, *Lampetra spadicea* — both classified as sensitive (S).
- **[[Cyprinidae]]** (minnows/carps): includes native genera *Algansea*, *Notropis (Hybopsis)*, *Yuriria*, plus introduced exotics *Carassius auratus* (goldfish) and *Cyprinus carpio* (common carp).
- **[[Catostomidae]]** (suckers): *Moxostoma austrinum* (= *Scartomyzon austrinus*).
- **[[Ictaluridae]]** (catfishes): *Ictalurus dugesi* — sensitive.
- **[[Atherinidae]]** (silversides, "charales"): the genus *Chirostoma* is the most speciose group in the basin, exhibiting a wide range of tolerance classifications from tolerant (*C. jordani*, *C. aculeatum*) to sensitive (*C. labarcae*).
- **[[Poeciliidae]]** (livebearers): native *Poeciliopsis infans* (tolerant) alongside exotics *Poecilia reticulata* (guppy) and *Xiphophorus helleri* (swordtail).
- **[[Goodeidae]]** (goodeids, viviparous endemics): the most taxonomically diverse endemic family, including *Goodea atripinnis*, *Xenotoca variata*, *Skiffia* spp., *Allotoca* spp., and *Zoogonecticus quitzeoensis*.
- **[[Centrarchidae]]**: introduced sport fishes *Lepomis cyanellus* and *Micropterus salmoides* (largemouth bass), both classified as sensitive in this basin context (likely indicating restriction to particular habitat types rather than true pollution sensitivity).
- **[[Cichlidae]]**: introduced *Oreochromis mossambicus* (Mozambique tilapia), classified as tolerant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Basin context**: The Rio Lerma Basin spans 52,500 km² across the states of México, Querétaro, Guanajuato, Michoacán, and Jalisco, originating at 3000 m elevation and terminating in **[[Lago de Chapala]]** at 1500 m over a 700 km course.
- **Subprovinces**: The basin is divided into three biogeographic subprovinces — **[[Alto Lerma]]**, **[[Medio Lerma]]**, and **[[Bajo Lerma]]** — consolidated from five original hydrological subbasins (including the endorheic Patzcuaro, Zirahuén, and Cuitzeo lake drainages).
- **Habitat associations by tolerance class**:
  - *Poor-quality/no-fish sites*: Located downstream of major cities (Toluca, Morelia, Celaya, Querétaro, Guanajuato, Irapuato, Salamanca, León) with untreated industrial/municipal discharge and heavy groundwater extraction.
  - *Fair-quality sites*: Tributaries and reservoirs distant from urban centers but within zones of intensive irrigated or seasonal agriculture; house tolerant or intermediate species.
  - *Good-quality sites*: Small headwater streams, springs, reservoirs, and highland lakes with natural vegetation and minimal agriculture, often under park or municipal water-supply protection; harbor sensitive species.
- **Rio Turbio exception**: A lowland river in the Medio Lerma subprovince that, despite headwater pollution from León, shows substantial downstream ecosystem recovery — the only lowland river retaining good water quality and sensitive species.
- **Endemism**: The basin's isolation and Central Plateau location have produced strong endemism, particularly within **[[Goodeidae]]** and **[[Atherinidae]]** (Chirostoma), making it biogeographically distinct enough to be classified as its own subregion/province in Mexican fish zoogeography.

## Key Findings

- **Site loss**: Of 116 historically documented sites, over 50% no longer support fish life by 1985–93; in the Alto Lerma subprovince alone, 56% of 36 historical sites (20 sites) had disappeared entirely due to groundwater extraction, diversions, or urbanization.
- **Species decline**: 40% of the basin's 42 native fish species experienced major declines in frequency of occurrence between the pre-1985 and 1985–93 periods.
- **Possible extinctions**: Three endemic species — ***[[Algansea barbata]]***, ***[[Chirostoma charari]]***, and ***[[Chirostoma compressum]]*** — were not recorded in 1985–93 sampling and may be extinct.
- **Tolerance classification** (via PCA ordination of presence/absence data correlated with water quality):
  - Sensitive species were generally restricted to high-quality, low-impact habitats and showed the steepest declines in distribution.
  - Even the broadly tolerant goodeid *Goodea atripinnis* showed major distributional reduction within the heavily degraded Alto Lerma subprovince — signaling basin-wide severity of impact.
- **Site quality classification (1985–93, including historically destroyed sites as "poor")**:
  - **Alto Lerma**: 67% poor, 17% fair, 17% good.
  - **Medio Lerma**: 49% poor, 39% fair, 14% good.
  - **Bajo Lerma**: 35% poor, 52% fair, 12% good (of 21 sites without historical-loss adjustment noted).
  - **Entire basin**: 51% poor, 34% fair, 15% good.
- **Conservation priority**: Only 15% of sites basin-wide currently support sensitive species, mostly protected headwater streams/highland lakes. The **[[Rio Turbio]]** is singled out as the single most important lowland conservation target due to its unique retention of ecological integrity.
- **Comparative severity**: The authors note that the extent of degradation in the Rio Lerma Basin "matches or exceeds the worst cases reported for comparably sized basins elsewhere in the world" (citing Karr et al. 1985; Moyle and Leidy 1992).
- **Methodological note**: The presence/absence retrospective approach (rather than abundance-based indices like **[[Index of Biotic Integrity]]**) is effective for detecting large-scale, long-term qualitative shifts but is comparatively imprecise for subtler environmental impacts.
