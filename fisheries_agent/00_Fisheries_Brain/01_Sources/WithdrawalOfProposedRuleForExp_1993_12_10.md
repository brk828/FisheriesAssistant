## Executive Summary

This is a 1993 letter from the Arizona Game & Fish Department to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regarding the withdrawal of a proposed rule for "experimental nonessential" designation of the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the Lower Colorado River. While the Department agrees to the withdrawal, it disputes the Service's rationale, arguing instead that the primary cause of reintroduction failure was the stocking of predominantly small, predation-vulnerable fish rather than larger, more resilient individuals. The letter presents stocking and recapture data for both Colorado Squawfish and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the Salt River and Verde River drainages between 1981–1989, and advocates for continued use of experimental nonessential designations as a tool for endangered species conservation, including development of a trophy sport fishery for squawfish.

## Background & Description

The document does not provide detailed morphological descriptions but references size classes critical to survival and management thresholds:

- **Colorado Squawfish**: Stocking size threshold of concern set at **>150 mm** (juvenile/subadult) and **>300 mm** (large, presumed predator-resistant size class).
- **Razorback Sucker**: Stocking size threshold of concern set at **>200 mm** for predator avoidance.
- Fish stocked at fry and fingerling sizes were noted to be highly vulnerable to predation in main channel habitats, implying that smaller body size correlates directly with predation susceptibility in these riverine systems.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Both species were stocked into **main channel habitats** of the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]], as well as into **upstream tributary and headwater reaches** intended to have lower predator density.
- **Predation Dynamics**: Main channel habitats were associated with heavy predation pressure on stocked fish. Headwater/tributary stocking was attempted as a refuge strategy, but monitoring revealed that many stocked fish migrated or were passively transported downstream back into higher-predation zones.
- **Management Response**: This led to an evolving stocking protocol, culminating in a recommendation to stock only larger fish (>300 mm for squawfish), ideally **reared in situ**, to bypass the vulnerable early life-history predation bottleneck.
- **Reintroduction Context**: Both species are part of ongoing efforts under the [[Lower Colorado River Basin Recovery Implementation Plan]], a planning framework in development for over 20 years at the time of writing.
- **Regulatory Tool**: The "[[Experimental Nonessential Population]]" designation under the Endangered Species Act was used/proposed as a mechanism enabling regulated take (e.g., a trophy sport fishery) of the Colorado Squawfish while still pursuing recovery goals.

## Key Findings

- **Razorback Sucker stocking (1981–1989)**:
  - Upper Salt River drainage: **7,800,000+** stocked; **7,829,847** total reported, of which only **11,287 (0.14%)** were >200 mm.
  - Upper Verde River drainage: **783,000+** stocked; **783,299** total reported, of which **6,382 (0.81%)** were >200 mm.
  - Recaptures: **519 total razorback suckers** recaptured across both drainages, mostly within weeks of stocking.

- **Colorado Squawfish stocking (1985–1989)**:
  - Salt River drainage: **543,426** total stocked; **0 individuals** were >150 mm.
  - Verde River drainage: **276,540** total stocked; **301 individuals (0.11%)** were >150 mm.
  - Recaptures: **444 total squawfish** recaptured across both drainages, mostly within weeks of stocking.

- **Interpretation**: Given the overwhelming dominance of small-bodied stocked fish (>99% under threshold size in nearly all cohorts), the Department concludes that **current data are inconclusive** regarding whether large (>300 mm) Colorado Squawfish populations can or cannot be established via reintroduction in the Salt and Verde Rivers — because the appropriate size class was essentially never tested at scale.

- **Policy Conclusions**:
  - The Department supports withdrawal of the 1987 proposed rule for the Lower Colorado River but maintains that [[Experimental Nonessential Population]] designations remain a valid and useful conservation and research tool.
  - The Department intends to focus squawfish sport fish management efforts on experimental nonessential designated portions of the Salt and/or Verde River System due to fiscal and hatchery constraints.
  - Clarifies that intended management is a **trophy sport fishery** (e.g., large-fish-only harvest or catch-and-release), not a "put-and-take fishery" as previously and erroneously referenced in 1987 correspondence.
  - The Department expresses intent to revisit lower Colorado River sport fish management options with the Service if Salt/Verde management proves successful.
