## Executive Summary

This paper is a museum catalog inventory rather than a field ecology study, documenting the [[Ephemeroptera]] (mayfly) holdings of the Michigan State University Entomology Museum. The collection comprises **35 genera and 112 determined species/subspecies**, preserved primarily in alcohol, with a smaller pinned-material collection. Although the bulk of specimens originate from Michigan, New England, and adjacent Canadian provinces, the collection also includes disjunct records from Alaska, Oregon, Colorado, Oklahoma, Kansas, Utah, and several southeastern states. The work is taxonomically significant for aquatic entomology and indirectly for [[Ichthyology]], as mayfly nymphs constitute a major prey base for freshwater fish and are widely used as [[Bioindicator Taxa]] for stream water quality. Numerous **new state records** (marked with asterisks) are reported, several of which extend known species distributions substantially beyond previously documented ranges.

## Background & Description

The paper does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of individual taxa (this being a specimen list rather than a taxonomic revision), but it does document **life-stage differentiation** critical to mayfly identification and ecology:

- **Nymph (n)** — the aquatic larval stage, often the most collected life stage (e.g., *Arthroplea bipunctata*, 20n; *Baetisca obesa*, 13n)
- **Subimago (male: ♂s; female: ♀s)** — the short-lived winged pre-adult stage unique to [[Ephemeroptera]] among insects
- **Imago (male: ♂; female: ♀)** — the fully mature, reproductive winged adult

Several taxa are recorded predominantly or exclusively as nymphs (e.g., *Cinygmula subaequalis*, *Heptagenia aphrodite*), reflecting the aquatic collecting bias, while others (e.g., *Hexagenia limbata* subspecies complex) are represented across all life stages, useful for correlating nymphal and adult diagnostic characters — a persistent challenge in mayfly systematics referenced implicitly by the author's need to consult external specialists (Dr. Jay R. Traver for [[Heptageniidae]]; Dr. Fred P. Ide for *Stenonema* and *Potamanthus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Mayfly nymphs are obligate freshwater organisms occupying benthic habitats in streams, rivers, and lakes, and serve several key ecological roles noted implicitly by the taxonomic and geographic breadth of this collection:

- **Habitat diversity**: Genera such as [[Isonychia]] and [[Stenonema]] (Heptageniidae) are typically associated with fast-flowing, well-oxygenated rocky streams, while [[Hexagenia]] and [[Ephemera]] (Ephemeridae) are characteristic of burrowing lifestyles in soft-sediment lake and river bottoms — a distinction reflected in the differing collection localities (e.g., *Hexagenia limbata* recorded extensively from Michigan lakes).
- **Trophic importance**: As a dominant component of freshwater macroinvertebrate assemblages, mayfly nymphs form a critical prey resource in [[Freshwater Food Webs]], directly supporting salmonid and other sport fish populations in the Great Lakes and Northeastern drainages.
- **Water quality indication**: The presence of sensitive families like [[Heptageniidae]] and [[Ephemerellidae]] is often used as a proxy for high water quality, underpinning their continued relevance to modern [[Bioindicator Taxa]] studies in stream ecology.
- **Geographic distribution**: The collection's core focus on Michigan, the Great Lakes basin, and New England, with outlying records from Alaska, Oregon, Colorado, and the southeastern US, illustrates the broad continental distribution of many genera (e.g., *Baetis*, *Callibaetis*, *Paraleptophlebia*) and the regional endemism of others (e.g., *Ephemera triplex* limited to West Virginia).

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic scope**: 12 families represented — [[Siphlonuridae]], [[Baetidae]], [[Heptageniidae]], [[Ametropodidae]], [[Leptophlebiidae]], [[Ephemerellidae]], [[Tricorythidae]], [[Potamanthidae]], [[Ephemeridae]], [[Polymitarcidae]], [[Caenidae]], and [[Baetiscidae]].
- **New state records**: Numerous asterisked records extend known ranges, including *Parameletus columbiae* in Alaska, *Siphlonurus mirus* in Maine, *Stenonema lepton* and *S. minnetonka* in Michigan, and *Hexagenia munda munda* in Michigan — several of which the author notes "extend the known range significantly."
- **Determination caveats**: Question marks flag uncertain identifications at species level (e.g., *Habrophlebia vibrans*?, *Hexagenia bilineata*?), underscoring ongoing taxonomic ambiguity within genera like [[Stenonema]] and [[Ephemerella]].
- **Historical taxonomic revision**: Pinned material determined earlier by Nathan Banks contains acknowledged inaccuracies (e.g., use of outdated genus names like *Siphlurus*, *Blasturus*, and *Ecdyonurus*), illustrating shifting mayfly nomenclature over the early-to-mid 20th century.
- **Collection utility**: The author explicitly frames the catalog as a resource for regional faunal studies of the Northeast and Great Lakes, of continued relevance for researchers studying [[Freshwater Macroinvertebrates]] and their role in aquatic and fisheries ecology.
- **Subgenus-level complexity**: The genus [[Ephemerella]] is shown split into multiple subgenera (*Attenuatella*, *Dannella*, *Drunella*, *Eurylophella*, *Serratella*, *Timpanoga*), reflecting significant taxonomic granularity within Ephemerellidae important for bioassessment applications.
