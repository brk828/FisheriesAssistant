# Invasion Potential of Nonnative Fishes Through Glen Canyon Dam into Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae #Percidae #reservoir_ecology #invasion_biology

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: This study examined 14 fish species in Lake Powell, with nonnative fishes comprising >99% of total catch (n=3,586 individuals across gillnets and minnow traps). Eight nonnative species were prevalent enough for modeling: [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*), [[Walleye]] (*Sander vitreus*), and [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*). Native fishes were rare (n=5 individuals): [[Bonytail]] (1), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (1), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (3). Larval fish (n=217) captured via ichthyoplankton tows and quatrefoil light traps consisted primarily of Green Sunfish (60%), Smallmouth Bass (28%), Gizzard Shad (9%), and [[Largemouth Bass]] (1%), collected mainly during June (13%) and August (87%) sampling. Juvenile fishes (30–100 mm TL) were captured in minnow traps, dominated by *Lepomis* spp. with juvenile Smallmouth Bass present during August and October. Adult fish captured in gillnets showed species-specific size distributions: Channel Catfish mean TL 490±72 mm, Common Carp 534±55 mm, Gizzard Shad 440±60 mm, Smallmouth Bass 298±69 mm, Striped Bass 372±107 mm, Walleye 375±73 mm.

**Habitat Associations**: [[Lake Powell]] is a monomictic reservoir (stratified April–December, mixed in winter) with surface elevation fluctuating based on inflows and [[Glen Canyon Dam]] (GCD) releases. During the study (2022–2023), reservoir storage ranged 22–39% of capacity, with water elevation reaching historic lows of 1074 m (3523 ft) in early 2022, only 10 m above minimum power pool. Three study subunits exhibited distinct bathymetry: (1) **Forebay** (nearest dam): steep canyon walls, mean depth 84–93 m, max 103–118 m; (2) **Confluence**: junction of Forebay, Wahweap Bay, and Antelope arm, mean depth 78–84 m, max 103–118 m, with broad sloping southeastern shore; (3) **Wahweap**: shallow complex shoreline, mean depth 21–24 m, max 90–105 m. Epilimnion depth varied dramatically between years: ~24 m in 2022 vs. ~39 m in 2023, influenced by runoff volume. Low dissolved oxygen zones (2–3 mg/L) developed at the bottom of the epilimnion during late summer both years. Littoral slope influenced habitat selection, with steeper slopes in Forebay contrasting with gentler slopes in Wahweap offering thermal refugia and complex microhabitats.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Acoustic telemetry of 20 Smallmouth Bass and 10 Channel Catfish (August–October 2022 implantation, tracked November 2022–June 2023) revealed species-specific depth use patterns. Smallmouth Bass exhibited shallow depth preference regardless of stratification: 3.9 m (95% CI 2.2–6.9 m) during mixed conditions, 4.4 m (95% CI 2.5–7.6 m) during stratification, with monthly average depths ranging 3.9–10.4 m. Channel Catfish used deeper water but shifted shallower during stratification: 10.4 m (95% CI 5.0–21.3 m) mixed, 7.2 m (95% CI 3.5–14.6 m) stratified, with monthly averages 9.7–31.7 m. Individual variability was substantial, with both species occasionally venturing ≥50 m depth. Detection rates varied monthly: 6–18 unique Smallmouth Bass and 3–8 unique Channel Catfish detected per month. Six of 30 implanted fish were recaptured by anglers (all Smallmouth Bass), three released alive. Spatial distribution showed gillnet catch increased with distance from dam during all sampling events. During mixed conditions (March), fish occupied full water column depth but were more abundant in warmer, shallower Wahweap subunit further from dam, suggesting winter thermal refugia use.

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: Not reported in detail. Study focused on distribution patterns rather than feeding ecology or interspecific interactions.

**Environmental Drivers**: Temperature was the strongest driver of fish distribution, appearing in all top occupancy models. All species showed increased occupancy probability with increasing temperature, with strongest effects for Channel Catfish and Common Carp, lesser effects for Smallmouth Bass, Gizzard Shad, and Striped Bass. During stratification (May–December), fishes largely occupied the epilimnion where temperatures were warmest (approaching 30°C at surface). Relative abundance increased significantly with temperature for all species tested (p<0.003). Dissolved oxygen significantly influenced community-wide relative abundance (p<0.003), with positive relationships for all species showing significant effects. Channel Catfish and Common Carp occupancy increased with DO (beta coefficients 0.81±0.23 and 0.81±0.25, respectively), and Channel Catfish avoided low-DO zones (<3 mg/L) at epilimnion base despite deep-water preference. Depth effects varied: Channel Catfish occupancy increased with depth (beta 0.18±0.08), while Gizzard Shad and Smallmouth Bass showed negative relationships (beta -0.22±0.05 and -0.15±0.04, respectively). Littoral slope negatively affected Gizzard Shad, Smallmouth Bass, and Striped Bass occupancy and relative abundance, indicating preference for shallower slopes (Wahweap) over steep canyon walls (Forebay). Year effect was significant, with occupancy decreasing community-wide from 2022 to 2023 (negative beta coefficients for four species: Channel Catfish -1.54±0.79, Common Carp -2.07±0.84, Gizzard Shad -1.80±0.59, Striped Bass -2.73±1.08). *Lepomis* spp. showed high constant occupancy (0.92) with minimal covariate effects.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: Hydroacoustic surveys estimated fish abundance varied dramatically by season and location. In Forebay, total abundance across eight sampling events was ~24,000 fish (all sizes), with individual sampling events ranging from lows of 32 fish >200 mm (October 2022) and 220 all fish (March 2023) to highs of 1,786 fish >200 mm and 9,840 all fish (both August 2023). Confluence abundance was ~3× higher (~73,000 total), while Wahweap contained >470,000 fish total. Fish <200 mm comprised 58–99% of hydroacoustic targets across sampling dates, likely dominated by [[Threadfin Shad]], *Lepomis* spp., and young-of-year of other species. Gillnet catch during mixed conditions (March) was dramatically reduced compared to stratified conditions: only 12% of average stratified catch in Forebay, 20% in Confluence, 16% in Wahweap. Minnow trap catch peaked in August both years with more homogenous spatial distribution. Native fish populations remain imperiled: only 5 individuals captured (Bonytail n=1, Flannelmouth Sucker n=1, Razorback Sucker n=3) despite robust sampling effort (426 gillnet captures, 852 minnow trap captures, 82 ichthyoplankton, 135 quatrefoil trap).

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: Entrainment risk of nonnative fishes through GCD into [[Grand Canyon]] varies seasonally with reservoir stratification and water level. When stratified (May–December), fishes concentrate in epilimnion, placing them at high entrainment risk when penstock intakes draw from epilimnetic water. Historic low water levels (1074 m in 2022, 26% capacity) positioned penstock intakes within or near epilimnion, dramatically increasing entrainment probability. Epilimnion depth variability (24 m in 2022 vs. 39 m in 2023) means minimum safe reservoir elevation varies annually. Based on historical thermocline depth data (5th–95th percentiles), avoiding epilimnetic entrainment requires reservoir elevations between 1080 m (10.1 km³ storage) and 1104 m (17.9 km³ storage)—a 75% difference in water volume. During mixed conditions (winter), fish distribute throughout water column but abundance near dam decreases, reducing but not eliminating entrainment risk. *Lepomis* spp. showed constant year-round Forebay occupancy, posing particular winter entrainment concern as these species are already present below GCD and considered among greatest threats to native fishes. Successful entrainment and establishment downstream could devastate recovering native fish populations in Grand Canyon tributaries and mainstem, where native species have recently expanded due to conservation efforts. Climate change projections indicate continued reservoir drying, suggesting entrainment risk will persist or worsen.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: (1) **Maintain reservoir elevations to draw water from hypolimnion**: Most direct means of limiting nonnative fish entrainment is maintaining Lake Powell elevation such that epilimnion and thermocline remain above GCD penstock intakes during stratified conditions. Minimum elevations of 1083 m (2022) and 1096 m (2023) would have prevented epilimnetic entrainment in those years; historical data suggest 1080–1104 m range needed to account for thermocline variability. (2) **Implement adaptive management framework**: Variable epilimnion thickness requires flexible elevation targets adjusted annually based on predicted stratification patterns. (3) **Consider operational alternatives**: Release water through lower river outlets (30 m below penstocks) during high-risk periods, though this may impact hydropower generation. (4) **Evaluate engineering solutions**: Large fish barrier nets or thermal curtains across Forebay could block fish approach to dam; thermal curtains could also provide tailwater temperature control to limit Smallmouth Bass spawning. Interdiction nets below draft tubes could intercept entrained fish. (5) **Monitor entrainment directly**: Deploy PIT tag antennas at penstocks and below dam to quantify actual entrainment rates and passage survival under different conditions. (6) **Account for winter risk**: *Lepomis* spp. year-round Forebay presence indicates entrainment threat persists during mixed conditions despite reduced overall abundance. (7) **Regional application**: Findings applicable to other western U.S. and global arid-region reservoirs experiencing climate-driven water level declines and potential nonnative fish escapement.

**Biological Reference Points**: Minimum reservoir elevations to prevent epilimnetic entrainment: 1083 m (3552 ft) in 2022, 1096 m (3594 ft) in 2023. Historical thermocline depth range (5th–95th percentiles) indicates 1080–1104 m elevation range needed across years, corresponding to 10.1–17.9 km³ (8.2–14.5 M acre-ft) storage. Minimum power pool elevation: 1064 m (3490 ft). Epilimnion depth: ~24 m (2022), ~39 m (2023). Penstock intake elevation range: 4.6 m vertical span. Temperature thresholds: mixed conditions defined as average daily temperature at 5 m depth <10°C (25 December 2022–10 April 2023). Low dissolved oxygen threshold: <3 mg/L at epilimnion base during late summer. Species-specific depth preferences: Smallmouth Bass 3.9–4.4 m average, Channel Catfish 7.2–10.4 m average (stratified vs. mixed).
