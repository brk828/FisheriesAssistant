# Stoneflies (Plecoptera) of the Far North of North America

#plecoptera #entomology #freshwater-biology

## Executive Summary

This 1944 paper by [[William E. Ricker]] reviews the stonefly (Plecoptera) fauna of the far north of North America, based on specimens in the [[Canadian National Collection]], the [[United States National Museum]] and the [[Royal Ontario Museum of Zoology]]. It documents new regional records, clarifies nomenclature for several species described in the nineteenth century, and proposes four new synonymies. The study area is defined mainly by vegetation (treeless [[Arctic Tundra]] and most of the [[Boreal Forest]]) rather than by latitude alone. The paper also sorts the northern fauna into six distribution groups.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** The region includes the arctic islands (District of Franklin), Alaska (excluding its southern coastal region), the Yukon, the Districts of Mackenzie and Keewatin, northern Alberta, Saskatchewan, Manitoba, Ontario, and Quebec, and Labrador. Settlements are sparse, so collections come from scattered sites.
- **Historical collections:** The best single-locality collection comes from [[St. Martin's Falls]] on the [[Albany River]], Ontario. It was assembled in the 1830s and early 1840s by naturalist [[George Barnston]], a factor of the [[Hudson's Bay Company]], and is now in the British Museum. Barnston wrote an unpublished account of the species and their habits. Six of his manuscript names were credited as "Barnston MSS" in publications by [[Newport]] and [[Walker]].
- **Early descriptions:** Three English entomologists, Newman, Newport and Walker, described 15 species from the far north. None is now considered a synonym of a species familiar to them. Of these, 14 have been recognized in later collections, leaving only *Isoperla citronella* still to be rediscovered at the time of writing.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** The name *Perla sonans* Barnston is a [[Nomen Nudum]], since it was never published with a description. The specimen Ricker had designated as a type is actually *Acroneuria lycorias*, not *A. abnormis*.
- **Specimen notation:** Records are tagged by source: USNM, ROMZ, WER (Ricker's own collection), and CNC for all other new records.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution groups.** Ricker grouped northern stoneflies by their known ranges:

1. **Tundra-only or near-tundra species (4):** *Capnia hantzschi*, *C. nearctica*, *Dictyopterygella postica*, *Isoperla decolorata*.
2. **Boreal-forest-only species (3):** *Taeniopteryx glacialis*, *Nemoura* sp. "E", *Isoperla citronella*.
3. **Tundra species also found south into the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence region or western cordillera (9):** including *Pteronarcys dorsata*, *Taeniopteryx maura*, *T. pacifica*, *Nemoura rotunda*, *N. trispinosa*, *Isogenoides frontalis*, *Arcynopteryx americana*, *Isoperla transmarina*, *Hastaperla brevis*.
4. **Boreal species extending south through the Great Lakes forest (12):** including *Nemoura venosa*, *Leuctra decepta*, *Allocapnia minima*, *Hydroperla olivacea*, *Diploperla decisa*, *Isoperla bilineata*, *Togoperla media*, *Acroneuria abnormis*, *A. lycorias*.
5. **Northern Great Plains into boreal forest (1):** *Capnia vernalis*.
6. **Western cordillera species reaching the boreal forest (3, plus 2 related):** *Diploperla fraseri*, *Isoperla fontium*, *Alloperla elevata* (tentative), with *Claassenia arctica* and *Capnia columbiana* noted as similar.

**Holarctic affinities.** No stonefly was confirmed as holarctic, though *Arcynopteryx americana* closely resembles the European *A. compacta*, and *Dictyopterygella postica* resembles the European *D. bicaudata*. Whether these pairs are conspecific requires comparing specimens from both hemispheres. Ricker found no other closely allied European species in the faunal accounts he consulted.

**Habitat notes.** Nymphs and adults were collected from rivers such as the [[Mackenzie River]], [[Athabaska River]], [[Albany River]] and [[Mattagami River]], from [[Great Slave Lake]] and [[Great Bear Lake]], and from a snowbank at 4,000 feet in [[Mount McKinley National Park]]. Collection sites span Hudson Bay and James Bay drainages, including [[Churchill]], [[Fort Churchill]] and [[Eastmain River]].

## Key Findings

- **Species treated:** The paper covers 34 species across Pteronarcyidae, Taeniopterygidae, Nemouridae, Leuctridae, Capniidae, Perlodidae and related families, with records from more than 30 localities.
- **Range extensions:** *Pteronarcys dorsata* is probably the only northern member of its genus. It occurs from Alberta and Saskatchewan to Labrador, with records as far north as Ungava Bay.
- **Eastern-western gap:** *Taeniopteryx pacifica*, a western species, is recorded from Labrador. Its eastern and western populations differ only slightly, and the gap in range remains to be filled.
- **Variation in key species:** *Nemoura trispinosa* males show marked variation in the posterolateral cercal process across its range. Arctic specimens have a broadened, shelf-like process with several blunt cusps.
- **Redescribed species:** *Isoperla decolorata* was redescribed from its type and a new male neallotype, since it had not been correctly identified since Walker's 1852 description.
- **Capnia key revision:** Ricker amended his male key to *Capnia* to separate *C. nearctica* from *C. hantzschi* using tergite tubercle extent and the form of the supra-anal process.
- **New synonymies proposed:**
  - *Dictyopterygella hudsonica* Hanson (1940) = *Dictyopterygella postica* (Walker)
  - *Perla verticalis* Banks (1920) = *Diploperla decisa* (Walker)
  - *Arcynopteryx minor* Klapálek (1912) = *Arcynopteryx americana* Klapálek
  - *Isogenoides hudsonicus* Hanson (1943) = *Isogenoides frontalis* (Newman)
- **Dictyopterygella postica:** Its type is a female, since all known males are brachypterous. Five females in the CNC agree with the type in structure and colour, and the species is common on the Mackenzie River.
- **Arcynopteryx americana:** Ricker found no non-intergrading characters separating its northern specimens. Its Baffin Land and Nettilling Lake specimens are unusually large, and one Great Slave Lake female has wings shortened to about half length.
- **Isogenoides frontalis:** Genital hook shape varies geographically across Mackenzie, Ungava and cordilleran populations. Ricker considered *hudsonicus* to be one of many minor geographic variants in a widespread species.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty:** Several determinations remain provisional, including *Pteronarcys* records based on P. regalis or P. biloba from Barnston's site, and the *Alloperla* sp. cf. *elevata* from Fort Norman. Ricker stressed that these need restudy or more material before inclusion in a definitive northern list.
