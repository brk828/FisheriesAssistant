# Supplemental Environmental Assessment: Introduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish into Selected Waters in Phoenix District

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #endangered-species #species-reintroduction

## Executive Summary

This Supplemental Environmental Assessment, prepared by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] Phoenix District Office in May 1984, authorizes the introduction of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*) into 11 selected public water sites within the Phoenix District. The action aims to reestablish self-sustaining populations of both federally listed species within their historic range and facilitate their eventual recovery. Three sites ([[Peoples Canyon]], [[Tres Alamos Falls]], and [[Grapevine Spring]]) will receive full [[Endangered Species Act]] protection, while remaining sites will be designated as experimental populations under Section 10j of the ESA. The proposal serves as mitigation for the loss of the [[Cocio Wash]] topminnow population and is consistent with the USFWS Gila and Yaqui Topminnow Recovery Plan (1984).

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, family [[Poeciliidae]]) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*, family [[Cyprinodontidae]]) are federally listed species requiring active recovery efforts. The desert pupfish is additionally listed as a Group 1 state-listed species in Arizona. Both species historically occupied springs, small streams, and isolated aquatic systems throughout their range in Arizona.

This supplemental assessment builds upon two previous environmental assessments: EA AZ-020-1-246 (July 1981) covering [[South Peoples Spring]] and [[Grapevine Spring]], and Supplemental EA AZ-025-EA-018 (December 1983) expanding introduction sites and incorporating experimental population designations authorized under the 1982 ESA amendments.

The introduction program is authorized under Sections 4 and 10 of the [[Endangered Species Act]], BLM Manual Section 6820, Arizona 6820 Supplement, and a separate Memorandum of Understanding (dated August 10, 1982) with an accompanying Reintroduction Plan (dated October 20, 1982). Implementation involves cooperation among [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The introduction sites represent enclosed or extremely isolated aquatic systems within the historic range of both species. All 11 sites are classified as Sensitivity Category 3 under Arizona Supplement 6820, indicating limited multiple-use issues with low public interest and easily resolvable conflicts.

One site, [[Yerba Mansa]] (also known as [[Grapevine Spring]]), requires removal of non-native [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), a known predator of topminnow, prior to native fish introduction. Removal will be accomplished using [[rotenone]], a plant-derived ichthyotoxin from Derris root. Rotenone disrupts oxygen utilization in fish and gilled invertebrates but poses no harm to humans, terrestrial animals, or plants. The treated area at Yerba Mansa comprises 0.18 acres. Vector control will continue through the topminnow/pupfish introduction process.

Site locations will be coordinated with the [[Arizona Department of Health Services]] Vector Control Program and appropriate county health departments to prevent reintroduction of mosquitofish. No physical barriers are required at introduction sites due to their enclosed or isolated nature. No fencing or additional protective structures have been identified as necessary.

The proposal addresses mitigation for the loss of the [[Cocio Wash]] topminnow population on public lands and advances recovery objectives that may ultimately result in delisting of both species.

## Key Findings

**Introduction Sites and Protection Status:**
- Total of 11 introduction sites identified across Phoenix District
- Three sites receive full ESA protection: [[Peoples Canyon]] (T.12N. R.10W. SW Sec. 14), [[Tres Alamos Falls]] (T.10N. R.9W. NW Sec. 13), and [[Grapevine Spring]]/[[Yerba Mansa]] (T.11N. R.11W. NW Sec. 21)
- Eight sites designated as experimental populations under ESA Section 10j: [[Mesquite Spring]], unnamed tributary to [[Cottonwood Gulch]], [[Dandrea Spring]], [[Sooz Spring]], [[Doe Peak Spring]], [[Hidden Spring]], [[Chalky Spring]], and [[Hackberry Spring]]
- Experimental populations have no declared critical habitat and no protected status designation

**Environmental Impact Assessment:**
- No adverse impacts identified to threatened and endangered species, floodplains and wetlands, wilderness areas, Areas of Critical Environmental Concern (ACECs), visual resources, water quality and quantity, cultural and historical resources, paleontological artifacts, air quality, or prime and unique farmland
- Rotenone application at Yerba Mansa (0.18 acre closed system) poses no risk to human health or terrestrial wildlife
- All sites are enclosed systems or extremely isolated, preventing invasion by non-native predaceous fish

**Public Participation and Support:**
- Public meeting held November 21, 1983 at Phoenix District Office
- Meetings conducted with Kingman and Phoenix/Lower Gila Grazing Advisory Boards and affected allottees
- No adverse public comment received
- Arizona congressional delegation and related management agencies consulted
- General support from all contacted individuals and groups
- No significant public concern identified

**Regulatory Compliance:**
- Action consistent with USFWS Gila and Yaqui Topminnow Recovery Plan (1984)
- Authorized under ESA Sections 4 and 10, BLM Manual Section 6820, Arizona 6820 Supplement
- Memorandum of Understanding for desert pupfish dated August 10, 1982
- Reintroduction Plan dated October 20, 1982
- Mineral/energy potential reviewed for three protected sites as required

**Decision and Rationale:**
- Finding of No Significant Impact issued under NEPA Section 102(2)c
- No Environmental Impact Statement required
- Successful reestablishment expected to benefit perpetuation and recovery of both species
- No action alternative rejected as it would prevent species recovery and fail to mitigate [[Cocio Wash]] population loss
- Authorization granted to AGFD and USFWS for proposed introductions
