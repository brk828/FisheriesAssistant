# Gila cypha Population Survey Data - Little Colorado River, Summer 1991

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #mark-recapture

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from Arizona State University researchers Michael E. Douglas and Paul C. Marsh reports data collected on [[Gila cypha]] (humpback chub) populations in the [[Little Colorado River]] during July-August 1991. The study was conducted as part of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program. Over three field trips spanning 1 July to 23 August 1991, researchers captured 4,793 individual chub across three study reaches (Confluence, Powell, and Salt), employing multiple gear types with hoop nets being most effective. The study utilized [[PIT Tags]] (Passive Integrated Transponder tags) for mark-recapture analysis, achieving a 34.6% overall recapture rate. Four mortalities occurred early in the study due to 1-inch trammel net use; switching to 1.5-inch trammel nets eliminated further mortality.

## Background & Description

[[Gila cypha]] is an endangered cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] basin. This study focused on populations in the [[Little Colorado River]], a critical tributary habitat. The research team from Arizona State University's Department of Zoology, in collaboration with the Arizona Natural Heritage Program (NNHP), conducted systematic sampling to assess population status and potential stress factors in [[Little Colorado Canyon]].

The study employed standardized size classification: juvenile chub (<150 mm total length) and adult chub (≥150 mm total length). Three distinct study reaches were established: Confluence (C), Powell (P), and Salt (S), representing different habitat conditions along the river system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Little Colorado River]] serves as important habitat for [[Gila cypha]], and this study was conducted within the broader context of [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] to evaluate potential environmental stressors on the population. The confluence area with the [[Colorado River]] represents a critical ecotone where chub populations interface with mainstem river conditions.

Multiple capture methods were employed to sample different microhabitats: hoop nets (primary method), trammel nets, angling, and seining. The dominance of hoop net captures (4,606 of 4,793 total) indicates this gear type's effectiveness for this species in riverine habitat.

## Key Findings

**Population Capture Data (1 July - 23 August 1991):**
- Total chub captured: 4,793 individuals
- Juveniles (<150 mm): 2,195 individuals (45.8%)
- Adults (≥150 mm): 2,598 individuals (54.2%)

**Captures by Reach and Trip:**
- Confluence Trip 2: 602 chub (397 juvenile, 205 adult)
- Confluence Trip 3: 173 chub (53 juvenile, 120 adult)
- Powell Trip 1: 470 chub (284 juvenile, 186 adult)
- Powell Trip 2: 807 chub (480 juvenile, 327 adult)
- Powell Trip 3: 734 chub (444 juvenile, 290 adult)
- Salt Trip 1: 412 chub (27 juvenile, 385 adult)
- Salt Trip 2: 940 chub (379 juvenile, 561 adult)
- Salt Trip 3: 655 chub (131 juvenile, 524 adult)

**Gear Type Effectiveness:**
- Hoop net: 4,606 chub (96.1% of total)
- Trammel net: 175 chub (3.7%)
- Angling: 6 chub (0.1%)
- Seine: 6 chub (0.1%)

**Mark-Recapture Results:**
- New [[PIT Tags]] deployed: 1,669
- Total recaptures: 899 individuals
- New PIT tag recaptures: 523 (20.1% recapture rate)
- Old PIT tag recaptures: 335 (14.1% recapture rate)
- [[Carlin Floy Tags]] recaptured: 42
- Overall recapture rate: 34.6%

**Mortality:**
- Four total mortalities recorded
- All mortalities attributed to 1-inch trammel net use at study initiation
- Zero additional mortalities after switching to 1.5-inch trammel nets
- Mortality rate: 0.08% of total captures

**Temporal and Spatial Patterns:**
The Salt reach showed highest adult abundance (Trip 2: 561 adults), while Powell reach showed highest juvenile abundance (Trip 2: 480 juveniles). The high recapture rate (34.6%) indicates strong site fidelity and/or effective sampling coverage within the study reaches.
