# Aravaipa Creek Fish Monitoring Memoranda, 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A series of monthly field memoranda from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) and partner agencies documents fish and habitat sampling along [[Aravaipa Creek]] in Graham and Pinal counties, Arizona, from February through December 1992. Sampling covered three reaches upstream of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] (WA), three within the WA, and three downstream. Surveys used [[Seine Netting]], [[Backpack Electrofishing]] and dipnets, with habitat typed and substrate estimated at each site. The memos track native and non-native species, breeding condition in native cyprinids, and wildlife observations, and they record data losses. The principal native taxa are [[Longfin Dace]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Speckled Dace]], with non-native species such as [[Red Shiner]], [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] present at lower densities.

## Background & Description

The memos were addressed mainly to Dave Gori of [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), with copies to [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service staff. Collectors included AGFD biologists, BLM and TNC staff, ASU students, and volunteers.

Standard methods, as stated in the memos:
- **Mainstream seining:** 200 m reaches with a 1/8-inch mesh, 6-foot seine, and a 1/4-inch mesh, 15-foot seine when necessary.
- **Side channels and margins:** 1/8-inch mesh dipnets.
- **Backpack electrofishing:** reported at roughly 125–250 V, with the lower 100 m of each reach used as the shocker sample. Settings varied by month.
- **Habitat:** reach length, width, depth and substrate composition (estimated percent of boulder, rubble, cobble, pebble, gravel, sand and silt) for each habitat unit, typed as run, riffle or pool.

Reported units were numbers of specimens with percentages, seine hauls (including empty hauls), and shocking seconds. Catch per unit effort (CPUE) was given as fish per 10 m².

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature and weather:** August was hot and humid with afternoon monsoon thunderstorms. Air temperatures ranged from 23 °C to 35 °C and water from 24 °C to 29 °C. November air temperatures ran from about 5 °C to 22 °C, with water at 10–18 °C. In December, water ranged from 9.5 °C to 17 °C after two days of rain, and the creek was moderately turbid.
- **Flow and turbidity:** In December, calculated flows ranged from 1.75 cms (Chimney Rock) to 7.88 cms (White's Ranch). Floods in early 1992 scoured substrates and uprooted trees. Turbidity prevented sampling at several sites in March and April.
- **Habitat composition:** Upstream and within-canyon reaches were riffle- and run-dominated, with cobble, pebble and gravel. Downstream reaches had more sand. The east-end reaches carried heavy aquatic vegetation, including [[Cladophora]], Nasturtium and sedges, while cocklebur and rabbitsfoot dominated the banks.
- **Wildlife:** A herd of about 17 [[Bighorn Sheep]] was seen upstream of [[Hell Hole]] in August, along with white-tailed deer, black-tailed rattlesnake, [[Gila Monster]] and canyon tree frogs. Coati-mundi, javalina and deer were reported in November and December.

## Key Findings

**Species composition (August 1992, seining)**
- **Upper Aravaipa Creek:** 794 specimens from 122 seine hauls (30 empty). [[Spikedace]] made up 52.8% (419), [[Longfin Dace]] 33.8% (268), and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*) 6.4% (51).
- **Lower Aravaipa Creek:** 345 specimens from 197 seine hauls (78 empty). [[Longfin Dace]] was 75.9% (262). Non-native species were present at low levels, including [[Western Mosquitofish]] and [[Green Sunfish]].

**Shocking (August 1992)**
- Upper Aravaipa Creek was shocked over 859 seconds, and Lower Aravaipa Creek over 1,303 seconds. Native cyprinids and catostomids dominated the shocker catches.

**Breeding and life stages**
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) showed breeding coloration in nearly every sampled reach in February, April and November. In December, approximately 11 individuals showed breeding color, and 17 post-larval specimens under 30 mm were collected as vouchers.
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and [[Spikedace]] showed tubercles or breeding color in several memos. In May, [[Spikedace]] appeared to be at peak breeding condition, especially in canyon reaches.
- Several [[Desert Sucker]] adults had tubercles on the anal fins in May. In April, one female expelled eggs when handled.
- Larval and juvenile fishes were common along the margins in spring. Young-of-year *Gila robusta* and *Catostomus insignis* were recorded in later months.
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*) with yellow grub were noted in August.

**Non-native species**
- In the May memo, [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) were abundant in interconnected pools of the Horse Camp tributary. No native fish were found there.

**Data losses**
- A tape-recorder malfunction at the Turkey Creek site in April resulted in lost habitat data (about 45 m) and 10–12 lost seine hauls. Several reaches in March and April were unsampled because of turbidity.

**Program outcomes**
- The memos give a consistent monitoring record across the year: native cyprinids and catostomids dominate the reaches sampled, and breeding conditions appear across most of the year. The monitoring supported management questions about the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] and its native fish community.
