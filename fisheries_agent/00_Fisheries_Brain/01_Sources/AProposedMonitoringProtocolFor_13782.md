# A Proposed Monitoring Protocol for Fishes of Aravaipa Creek, Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology #invasive-species #threatened-species

## Executive Summary

This proposal outlines a comprehensive 12-month monitoring protocol for fish populations in [[Aravaipa Creek]], one of the last [[Sonoran Desert]] streams supporting a diverse native fish fauna. The program focuses on tracking the invasion of non-native [[Red Shiner]] (*[[Cyprinella lutrensis]]*) and its potential impacts on three federally listed or candidate species: [[Loach Minnow]] (*[[Tiaroga cobitis]]*), [[Spikedace]] (*[[Meda fulgida]]*), and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*[[Gila robusta]]*). The monitoring will employ standardized seining and electrofishing at 6-9 stations monthly, with data integrated into a 25-27 year long-term dataset. The program is designed to provide baseline data for management decisions and evaluate impacts of both biological invasions and catastrophic events such as floods or droughts.

## Background & Description

[[Aravaipa Creek]] in Graham and Pinal counties, Arizona, represents a critical refuge for native southwestern fish fauna. Seven native species persist in this desert stream system, which has remained relatively free of non-native fishes due to isolation and violent flash flooding that eliminates lentic-adapted invaders. Native fishes demonstrate flood tolerance, while non-native generalist species are poorly suited to withstand desert flash floods.

During the 1980s, non-native fish populations began increasing in [[Aravaipa Creek]] for reasons that remain partially unknown. In 1990, [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), a minnow native to the Mississippi-Rio Grande basins, appeared in the system. This species has been present in Arizona since 1953 and has spread slowly but inexorably through the [[Gila River]] basin. Red shiner establishment has been associated with declines in native species, particularly [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], though whether this results from direct biological interactions or habitat degradation remains under debate.

The native fish assemblage includes: *[[Agosia chrysogaster]]* (longfin dace, maturity >45 mm), *[[Catostomus insignis]]* (Sonora sucker, >150 mm), *[[Gila robusta]]* (roundtail chub, >150 mm), *[[Meda fulgida]]* (spikedace, >45 mm), *[[Pantosteus clarki]]* (desert sucker, >125 mm), *[[Rhinichthys osculus]]* (speckled dace, >45 mm), and *[[Tiaroga cobitis]]* (loach minnow, >38 mm).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Aravaipa Creek]] flows through the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] (ACWA) managed by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) and [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC). The stream exhibits typical desert creek habitat characteristics including riffles, runs, and pools with variable depths, water velocities, and substrate composition.

The monitoring protocol recognizes the importance of flash flooding as a natural disturbance regime that historically excluded non-native species. Native fishes possess adaptations to withstand these floods, while invading non-native species typically favor lentic (still-water) habitats or lack flood tolerance. Water developments including damming, de-watering, and channelization represent the primary threats to southwestern native fish fauna through habitat alteration and destruction.

The appearance of [[Red Shiner]] signals potential ecosystem disruption. This species matures at small size (>38 mm) and may outcompete or otherwise negatively interact with native minnows of similar size classes, particularly the federally listed [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]].

## Key Findings

**Monitoring Goals and Design:**
- Two principal goals: (1) obtain data on fish populations over a one-year period with emphasis on [[Red Shiner]] invasion and establishment; (2) acquire close-order data to enhance interpretation of the existing 25-27 year long-term dataset
- Minimum of 6 stations sampled monthly (3 above and 3 below ACWA), with 3 additional stations within ACWA sampled quarterly (9 total stations)
- Vigorous and thorough seining of at least 200 meters per station covering all available habitats
- Electrofishing (battery-powered in ACWA) to minimum of 100 meters per station quarterly to adequately sample larger species and individuals

**Standardized Sampling Gear:**
- Two seine types: 6 feet long × 4 feet deep with 1/8-inch mesh; 15 feet long × 4 feet deep with 1/4-inch mesh
- Both seine types fitted with double-weighting on bottom line
- Standardization ensures compatibility with existing long-term ASU dataset

**Data Collection Protocol:**
- All native fishes separated into sub-adults and adults based on size at sexual maturity (Table 1), enumerated, and released alive
- Non-native fishes consistently removed for food habits analysis and interaction studies
- Habitat conditions recorded: cover amount, riffle-run-pool distribution, depths, water velocities, substrate composition
- Each reach photographed multiple times from fixed points with permanent landmarks
- Seine haul numbers, area sampled per haul, and electrofishing duration (seconds) recorded for abundance estimation

**Sampling Schedule (1991-1992):**
- Nine-station sampling: May, August, November 1991; February 1992
- Six-station sampling: June, July, September, October 1991; December 1991, January, March, April 1992
- Four-week intervals maintained when possible
- Baseline sampling completed 9-10 February (9 stations), 8-10 March and 12-14 April (6 stations each)

**Data Management:**
- Field notes and raw data copied monthly to BLM, FWS, and TNC; originals filed at ASU
- [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) designated permanent data depository, receiving raw and computer-formatted data
- Monthly letter memoranda provided to all agencies with station-specific results, observations, and photograph catalog
- Annual report by April 1992 integrating 1991-92 data with >25-year historical record

**Cooperating Institutions:**
- Arizona State University (lead: W.L. Minckley, Professor; Anthony Velasco, M.Sc. student)
- Arizona Game and Fish Department
- U.S. Bureau of Land Management
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service
- The Nature Conservancy

**Budget Estimates:**
- Budget A (ASU-paid student, rental vehicles): $6,092.31 total (personnel $3,427.20; travel/subsistence $2,375.00; ASU handling 5%)
- Budget B (TNC-paid student, BLM vehicle provided): $1,076.25 total (contingency travel $225.00; subsistence $800.00; ASU handling 5%)
- Contingency funding included for two additional sampling trips following floods or unusual events
- Minimum staffing: 2-3 persons for 6-station periods; 3+ persons for 9-station quarterly sampling

**Conservation Significance:**
- Program provides early detection system for [[Red Shiner]] establishment and spread
- Baseline data enables evaluation of catastrophic events (floods, droughts) on event-by-event basis
- Long-term monitoring framework established for tracking impacts on federally listed species
- Data will guide future management decisions and potential recovery efforts
