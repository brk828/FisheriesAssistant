# Leadville National Fish Hatchery - 1993 Station Profile

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #fish-hatchery #FisheriesManagement

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service station profile for the [[Leadville National Fish Hatchery]], the oldest trout hatchery in the Rocky Mountain Region (established 1889). Rather than a species-specific biological study, the source describes hatchery operations, infrastructure, funding, and fish distribution data for multiple [[Salmonidae|salmonid]] species reared to mitigate fishery losses associated with the [[Fryingpan-Arkansas Reclamation Project]] and to support fishery management on federal lands, including Department of Defense waters managed under the [[Sikes Act]]. In FY1993, the hatchery distributed over 522,000 fish across Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming.

## Background & Description
The source does not provide morphological data on individual species. It identifies the cultured taxa only by common name, all within family **Salmonidae**:
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*)
- [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarkii*)
- [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*)
- [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*)
- [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*)

No length, weight, coloration, or meristic data are reported in this station profile.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Facility function**: Leadville NFH operates as a **mitigation hatchery**, artificially propagating trout to offset fishery losses caused by water reclamation infrastructure (the [[Fryingpan-Arkansas Reclamation Project]]).
- **Distribution range**: Fish reared at the hatchery are stocked into a mix of federal (FWS and other federal agency), state, and (in some years) private waters across **Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming**.
- **Habitat provided on-site**: 2 production fish ponds, 6 lakes (2 used for production), and 16 raceways support multiple life stages of salmonids prior to release.
- **Anthropogenic linkage**: Stocking supports fishery management on Department of Defense lands under the [[Sikes Act]], linking hatchery ecology directly to federal land-use and military installation management.
- **Human interaction/education**: The station also functions as a public engagement site, hosting nearly 20,000 visitors in FY1993 and providing volunteer/training opportunities (Job Training Partnership Act, college volunteers, Outward Bound School), reflecting an applied conservation-education niche alongside its biological production role.

## Key Findings
- **Historical significance**: Founded in 1889, Leadville NFH is the first trout hatchery established in the Rocky Mountain Region.
- **Species portfolio**: Actively rears five salmonid taxa — rainbow, cutthroat, brook, lake, and brown trout — for mitigation and management purposes.
- **FY1993 distribution total**: 522,008 fish distributed (excluding eggs/fry), compared to 397,227 in FY1992 and 0 reported in FY1991 (data gap or non-reporting year).
- **Distribution breakdown (FY93)**: 3,785 to FWS federal waters; 476,318 to other federal waters; 41,905 to state waters; 0 to private waters.
- **Infrastructure investment**: FY1993 maintenance work included removal of 7 underground fuel tanks, installation of 2 above-ground tanks, kitchen cabinet replacement, asbestos removal, and furnace ductwork replacement in staff quarters — indicating significant environmental compliance and facility modernization efforts.
- **Funding trends**: Operations budget increased from $205.0K (1992) to an estimated $230.0K (1994/1995); maintenance funding fluctuated sharply (from $264.0K in 1992 down to $5.8K in 1993), suggesting a major one-time maintenance investment in 1992 followed by reduced spending.
- **Staffing**: Full-time equivalent (FTE) staffing held steady around 4.5–5.0 positions from 1991–1995.
- **New revenue stream**: "Cost Recoverables" funding appears starting in 1993 ($20.8K), continuing into 1994–1995 estimates ($20.0K), indicating a shift toward partial cost recovery for hatchery services.
- **Community engagement**: Nearly 20,019 visitors recorded in FY1993, supported by volunteer host couples and youth/college training programs, underscoring the hatchery's dual role in fish production and public outreach.
