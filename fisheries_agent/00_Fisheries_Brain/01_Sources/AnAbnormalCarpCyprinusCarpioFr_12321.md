# An Abnormal Carp from California Waters

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #skeletal-morphology #vertebral-anomalies

## Executive Summary

This report describes a severely deformed [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) caught in October 1949 from an irrigation canal in [[Imperial County]], California. The specimen exhibited extreme vertebral fusion with at least 10 centra fused into a solid segment at the midlength of the vertebral column, resulting in a drastically shortened body (159 mm total length). Despite the severe skeletal deformity, all external meristic features (fin rays and scale counts) corresponded to normal [[Carp]] characteristics. X-ray analysis revealed that neural and haemal processes remained distinct and present in normal numbers, preserving the identity of individual centra even where fusion occurred. The fish was approximately six years old but much smaller than normal for its age.

## Background & Description

The specimen was caught on hook and line by Mr. Williams of Brawley, California, and brought to [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]] by Captain Willard Greenwald of the California Division of Fish and Game Bureau of Patrol and Law Enforcement. The fish was tentatively identified as [[Carp]] and confirmed as *Cyprinus carpio* Linnaeus despite obvious deformity.

[[Carp]] and close relatives (family [[Cyprinidae]]) are notably variable in body form and structure. The domestic [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*) demonstrates this variability through numerous bred varieties. Body form and growth rate in [[Carp]] are known to be extremely sensitive to environmental effects. The specimen's outward appearance resembled the "mast-form" described by Wunder (1939).

Meristic counts were: dorsal III, 20; anal III, 5; pectoral 16; pelvic 9; scales 6-37-6. These counts correspond with normal [[Carp]] specifications. The fish measured 159 mm in length and was approximately in its sixth year of life based on scale analysis, making it much smaller than a normal [[Carp]] of this age.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimen was collected from an irrigation canal environment east of [[El Centro]] in [[Imperial County]], California. The extreme sensitivity of [[Carp]] body form and growth rate to environmental conditions is well documented. However, the specific environmental factors that may have contributed to this particular deformity were not determined.

The foreshortening of the body had its most marked external effect in the region overlying the section of maximum vertebral coalescence, located approximately midway between the pelvic and anal fin insertions. In the lateral line region posterior to this point, scales were crowded together with less exposed area. Under microscopic examination, these scales appeared regenerated, with granular-textured centers lacking regular circuli patterns. Scales in the anterior body portion appeared normal.

## Key Findings

**Vertebral Deformities:**
- Multiple malformations occurred in at least nine regions of the backbone
- At least 10 centra were fused in a solid segment at approximately the midlength of the vertebral column
- Fused groups of vertebrae (except the major 10-centra segment) were each approximately as long as a single vertebra
- Adjacent centra were fused in several regions, reducing overall vertebral column length
- Neural and haemal processes remained present in numbers normal for the species, preserving individual centra identity
- Fused vertebral groups were separated by distinct articular spaces

**External Morphology:**
- Total length: 159 mm (abnormally small for age)
- Age: approximately 6 years (determined from scale analysis)
- Caudal peduncle extremely short
- Median fins overlapped caudal fin when depressed
- All fin-ray and scale counts within normal range for species

**Comparative Context:**
- Vertebral fusions and deformities involving limited numbers of vertebrae are not uncommon in natural fish populations
- In [[Atlantic Herring]] (*Clupea harengus*) and [[Pacific Herring]] (*Clupea pallasi*), such anomalies occur in up to 14% of individuals
- Most common herring anomalies occur at the posterior end of the vertebral column with duplication of neural or haemal spines
- The [[Carp]] specimen described differs from typical herring-type fusions in location and extent
- A similar abnormality in a [[Carp]] from the upper Illinois River (O'Donnell, 1945) involved complete fusion of the last 8 precaudal and 17 caudal centra, resembling arthritis deformans

**Taxonomic Implications:**
- True vertebral number indicated by vertebral processes, not by fused centra count
- Numerical adjustment methods (such as Schmidt's 1.5 value for double vertebrae) would produce ridiculously low vertebral counts for this specimen
- Despite severe deformity, specimen agreed in all essential respects with *Cyprinus carpio* Linnaeus
