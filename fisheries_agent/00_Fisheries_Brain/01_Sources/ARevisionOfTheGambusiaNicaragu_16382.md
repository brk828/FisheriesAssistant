# Revision of the Gambusia nicaraguensis Species Group (Pisces: Poeciliidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 taxonomic revision by William L. Fink, published in the [[Publications of the Gulf Coast Research Laboratory Museum]], reviews the [[Gambusia Nicaraguensis Species Group]] within the livebearer genus [[Gambusia]]. The group comprises four recognized species: [[Gambusia Nicaraguensis]], [[Gambusia Wrayi]], [[Gambusia Melapleura]] and the newly described [[Gambusia Hispaniolae]]. Fink places [[Gambusia Gracilior]] in synonymy with *G. wrayi*, finds [[Gambusia Dominicensis]] to belong to a different species group, and provides a dichotomous key and zoogeographic notes. The revision builds on the gonopodial-based subgeneric system of [[Rivas 1963]] and largely accepts it.

## Background & Description

The study follows the methods of [[Fink 1971]] and uses specimens from institutions including the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]], the [[British Museum (Natural History)]], the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] and the [[United States National Museum]]. Lengths are standard length (SL), and coloration is described from alcoholic specimens.

**Diagnostic features of the group:**
- Gonopodium about one-third of SL, with ray 3 spines tapering to a point and reaching beyond the terminal hook of ray 4p.
- Gonopodial suspensorium with three or four (usually three) gonapophyses; uncini usually on the second and often on the third.
- Anal rays 11; scales around the caudal peduncle 16.
- Males have a sigmoid dorsal pectoral-fin margin.
- Dentition of outer and inner rows of enlarged canines with a middle band of smaller canines.

**Species key (summary):**
- *G. nicaraguensis*: dorsal rays 7-9; gonopodium tip tapered to a sharp point.
- *G. hispaniolae*: dorsal rays 8-10 (usually 9); distinct arch in ray 4a; no abrupt constriction proximal to elbow.
- *G. wrayi*: dorsal rays 8-10; branched caudal rays 12-16; fins usually spotted.
- *G. melapleura*: dorsal rays 10-12; branched caudal rays 16-20; fins unspotted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution of *G. nicaraguensis***: fresh and brackish waters from Lago de Izabal and Puerto Barrios, Guatemala, south to Gatun Lake, Panama, plus San Andres Island. Its range does not overlap with [[Gambusia Sexradiata]] or [[Gambusia Puncticulata Yucatana]], although the latter two are sympatric in southern Mexico and northern Guatemala. The species shows clinal variation only in ray 3 spine number, which is higher in northern populations.
- **Distribution of *G. hispaniolae***: freshwaters of Haiti and western Dominican Republic. It is described as the dominant Gambusia in Haiti.
- **Distribution of *G. wrayi***: freshwaters of Jamaica, densest in southern drainages. It is replaced in brackish and salt water by [[Gambusia Puncticulata]], though the two occasionally co-occur.
- **Distribution of *G. melapleura***: known only from the type locality at Bluefields, Jamaica, and a headwater spring of the Shrewsberry River.
- **Zoogeography**: the authors infer at least three West Indian invasions by Gambusia. The [[Gambusia Punctata Group]] (represented in Haiti by relict populations of *G. beebei* and *G. pseudopunctata*) was probably the first. The [[Gambusia Nicaraguensis Group]] probably descended from a Central American ancestor that dispersed from the Honduras-Nicaragua area to Jamaica and then to Haiti. Fink suggests the punctata-group relicts persist only where *G. hispaniolae* is excluded.
- **Remarks on *G. melapleura***: a large, restricted-range species that may have been more widespread during Pleistocene low sea levels, becoming isolated as water levels rose.

## Key Findings

- **Nomenclature**: *G. gracilior* is a junior synonym of *G. wrayi*, since proportional differences fall within the range of *G. wrayi* and that name has page priority. *G. dominicensis* belongs to the [[Gambusia Nobilis Group]]; the form called *dominicensis* by Rivas (1963) is undescribed and is named *G. hispaniolae*.
- **New species**: *Gambusia hispaniolae* is described from a holotype (USNM 204865, adult male, 24.4 mm) from Haiti. The type series includes 366 specimens (199 adult females, 57 young, 56 immature males, 54 adult males).
- **Synonymies within *G. nicaraguensis***: *G. dovii*, *G. menieli* and *G. aestiputeus* are all treated as synonyms. The holotype of *G. menieli* (ANSP 6818) has 28 lateral scales, not the roughly 20 originally reported. The paratypes of *G. aestiputeus* have 29 lateral scales, contrary to Fowler's count of 25+2.
- **Type status**: the syntypes of *G. nicaraguensis* are thought to derive from an Atlantic drainage stream rather than Lake Nicaragua, since no *Gambusia* has been collected in that lake since.
- **Meristics (Table 1-4 summary)**:
  - Dorsal rays: mean 9.0 in *hispaniolae* and *wrayi*; 11.4 in *melapleura*.
  - Lateral scales: mean 29.7 (*nicaraguensis*), 30.9 (*hispaniolae*), 31.6 (*wrayi*), 31.5 (*melapleura*).
  - Ray 3 spines: mean 8.7 (*nicaraguensis*), 10.1 (*hispaniolae*), 8.2 (*wrayi*), 8.3 (*melapleura*).
- **Size ranges (males / females, SL)**: *nicaraguensis* 16.1-29.3 / 18.1-35.0 mm; *hispaniolae* 18.1-29.8 / 19.7-51.8 mm; *wrayi* 16.2-32.2 / 21.0-56.4 mm; *melapleura* 20.6-34.2 / 25.4-59.1 mm.
- **Cuba record**: a *G. hispaniolae* collection (UMMZ 136377) from Cienfuegos, Cuba, is considered to have a labeling error.
- **Salinity tolerance**: *G. nicaraguensis* is the only member of the group that tolerates salt water, whereas the insular members are now virtually confined to fresh water.
- **Specimen basis**: the study uses material from Panama, Nicaragua, Honduras, Guatemala, Colombia (San Andres), Haiti, the Dominican Republic, and Jamaica. Gonopodial suspensorium counts are based on 27 adult male *G. nicaraguensis*, 42 *G. hispaniolae*, 47 *G. wrayi*, and 47 *G. melapleura*.
