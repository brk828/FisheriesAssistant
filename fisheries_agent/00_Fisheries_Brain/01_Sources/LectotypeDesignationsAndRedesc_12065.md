# Lectotype Designations and Redescription of *Serradigitus wupatkiensis* (Scorpiones: Vaejovidae) and a Mermithid Parasite of *Erebia occulta* (Lepidoptera: Satyridae)

#arachnology #scorpiones #vaejovidae #parasitology #lepidoptera #taxonomy

*Note: This document does not address fish or aquatic ecosystems. Its content is terrestrial arthropod taxonomy and invertebrate parasitology, so the standard aquatic fields are recorded as N/A or as the terrestrial localities given.*

## Executive Summary

This item is a two-part journal publication from the *Pan-Pacific Entomologist* (vol. 63, no. 4, 1987). Its main part is a taxonomic paper by Stanley C. Williams (San Francisco State University) that designates a [[Lectotype]], an [[Allolectotype]] and six [[Paralectotype]]s for *Vejovis wupatkiensis* [[Stahnke]], redescribes the species from the lectotype, and confirms its placement in the genus [[Serradigitus]] as *Serradigitus wupatkiensis*. The types are held at the [[California Academy Of Sciences]]. The second part is a Scientific Note reporting a mermithid nematode extracted from a male [[Erebia occulta]] butterfly collected in Alaska in 1986.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** In 1940, [[H. L. Stahnke]] published an abstract of his 1939 doctoral dissertation on Arizona scorpions, validating nine new species-group names and one genus-group name. He named one species *Vejovis wupatkiensis* after its type locality at [[Wupatki National Monument]]. The species was distinguished by elongate pedipalp fingers, an elongate terminal pedipalp denticle, and swollen basal teeth on the female pectines. Because the types had not been available for study, the phylogenetic relationships of the species remained unclear.

**Diagnosis (as given).** A small, slender *Serradigitus*, adults up to 35 mm long, with a uniform whitish-yellow to amber-brown body. Key characters:
- Chela length to palm length ratio of 5.6 in males and 5.0 in females.
- Movable finger longer than the carapace.
- Chela supernumerary denticles of 6/7 on the fixed and movable fingers.
- Stigma 2–3 times longer than wide; carapace length to stigma length of 14–15.
- Small subaculear tubercle on the telson.

The species is similar to *Serradigitus gertschi* and *Serradigitus torridus*. It differs from *S. gertschi* in having smooth to obsolete inferior median keels on metasomal segment I, and from *S. torridus* in having four (not five) prolateral macrosetae on the pedipalp humerus.

**Redescription from the lectotype (female).** Body coloration is uniform golden brown, with pectines and genital operculum slightly paler. The carapace has three pairs of macrosetae and a finely granular dorsal surface. Pectines have 15 teeth per comb, and the two proximal teeth are swollen and elongate. The pedipalp chela has 47/43 primary-row denticles on the fixed and movable fingers, respectively, and each finger has an elongate terminal denticle more than five times the length of the first supernumerary.

**Measurements (mm, from Table 1).** The lectotype (female) and the allolectotype (male, #75.3) are compared below:

| Character | Lectotype (female) | Allolectotype (male) |

| Total length | 33 | 25 |
| Carapace (l/w at median eyes) | 4.3/3.2 | 3.1/2.4 |

**Variation.** The allolectotype and six paralectotypes are similar to the lectotype in structure and coloration. Six females measure 29–32 mm total length, and the single male measures 25 mm. Female pectine teeth number 15–16 (mode 16), and the male has 17. The fixed finger is usually slightly shorter than the carapace, whereas it is slightly longer in the lectotype.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** The species is known from northern Arizona, southern Utah, southern Nevada, and adjacent eastern California. Williams reports little geographic variation, but recognizes a distinctive race from the Panamint Mountains of eastern California. This race has darker pedipalp fingers and keels, and slightly more slender palms.

**Type material.** Of the nine specimens Stahnke used in his 1939 description, only eight reached the [[California Academy Of Sciences]], received from Stahnke in 1983. The missing specimen, numbered 75.2 in the dissertation, has an unknown location.

**Parasitism in Lepidoptera.** According to [[Poinar (1975)]], nematode parasites have been recorded from 232 lepidopteran species, of which only 20 are butterflies. North American records cited include *Pieris rapae* (Pieridae) and three Nymphalidae species (*Polygonia comma*, *P. interrogationis*, *Vanessa atalanta*). As of November 1986, no nematode parasite records were known for Satyridae. Mermithidae are obligate parasites with no free-living or feeding stages outside the host. Most moth nematode parasites are also Mermithidae, with six records of Steinernematidae (facultative and obligate) and one of Rhabditidae (facultative).

**Collection site (butterfly).** The *Erebia occulta* specimen was collected at mile 41.5 on the Nome-Council Road, Alaska (64°39'N, 164°20'W, 30–120 m elevation), flying over a low scree slope. This is the only habitat description in the note, and it is terrestrial.

## Key Findings

1. **Type designations.** The following were designated:
   - **Lectotype:** female, Arizona, Coconino Co., Wupatki National Monument, 10 November 1938, D. J. Jones (#67). Catalogue number: California Academy of Sciences Entomology Type Number 15173. The specimen is damaged: left movable pedipalp finger missing, right chela detached, right tarsi of leg 4 missing, and many setae missing.
   - **Allolectotype:** male (#75.3), Wupatki National Monument, collected before August 1938 by D. J. Jones. Same depository and type number.
   - **Six paralectotypes:** five females and one small subadult female, from Flagstaff, Arizona, dated 1938–1939. Each is labeled #72, #73.4, #73.3, #75.11, #326 and #325.

2. **Taxonomic confirmation.** The nearest relatives of *V. wupatkiensis* are *Serradigitus gertschi* (Williams) and *S. torridus* Williams & Berke. The placement of the species in *Serradigitus* as *S. wupatkiensis* (Stahnke) is confirmed.

3. **Possible geographic race.** Williams recognizes a distinct Panamint Mountains race, separated by darker pigmentation and slightly more slender palms. It is not formally named.

4. **Nematode parasitism record.** On 3 July 1986, the author collected a male *Erebia occulta*. A mermithid protruded about 5 mm from the seventh abdominal segment, and the worm was extracted with forceps. The worm measured 10.1 cm, and the butterfly's abdomen measured 9 mm. The specimen was preserved in 70% isopropanol and sent to [[George O. Poinar Jr.]], who identified it as a "postparasitic juvenile" that could only be identified to family (Mermithidae).

5. **Significance.** The record adds a Satyridae host to the known Lepidoptera nematode parasite records, a group for which Poinar had no prior records as of November 1986.
