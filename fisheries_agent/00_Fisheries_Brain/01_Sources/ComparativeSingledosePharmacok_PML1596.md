# Comparative Single-Dose Pharmacokinetics of Four Quinolones in Atlantic Salmon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Martinsen and Horsberg]] (Norwegian College of Veterinary Medicine, Oslo) compared the single-dose pharmacokinetics of four [[Quinolone Antimicrobials]] in [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*) postsmolts held in seawater at 10.2 ± 0.2 °C. The agents were [[Oxolinic Acid]], [[Flumequine]], [[Sarafloxacin]], and [[Enrofloxacin]], given intravenously (i.v.) and orally as medicated feed (and flumequine also as Aqualets). Bioavailability varied considerably among the four drugs. Enrofloxacin had the highest mean plasma concentration when dose-normalized and the longest elimination half-life, and the authors concluded that flumequine and enrofloxacin had better pharmacokinetic properties than oxolinic acid, while sarafloxacin performed poorer.

## Background & Description

Quinolones were the most commonly used antimicrobial group in [[Norwegian Aquaculture]] at the time, with oxolinic acid and flumequine the most frequently used agents. Newer fluoroquinolones (sarafloxacin, enrofloxacin) showed enhanced in vitro activity against fish pathogenic bacteria such as [[Aeromonas salmonicida]], and were bactericidal. Pharmacokinetic data for quinolones in fish were sparse, and differences between compounds were suspected but unquantified. Prior work on oxolinic acid and flumequine in Atlantic salmon existed, and one study had examined sarafloxacin at 8.5 °C, but no data existed for enrofloxacin in Atlantic salmon in seawater.

The study aimed to compare the four compounds under identical experimental designs and to estimate bioavailability from plasma kinetics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** Fish were held in 1 m³ fiberglass tanks with running seawater (salinity ~33‰) at 10.2 ± 0.2 °C, at NIVA Marine Research Station, Drøbak.
- **Test animals:** 300 Atlantic salmon postsmolts, mean weight 240 ± 50 g, divided into nine groups of 40 after a 14-day acclimation on a commercial diet.
- **Relevance:** Quinolone resistance and minimum inhibitory concentrations (MICs) against key pathogens such as *Aeromonas salmonicida*, *Vibrio salmonicida*, *Vibrio anguillarum* and *Yersinia ruckeri* were reviewed to interpret the plasma data.
- **Context of prior work:** Bioavailability and elimination half-life in freshwater salmonids (e.g. [[Rainbow Trout]]) and in other temperatures differed from the present seawater results, which the authors attributed partly to species and water type.

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- Plasma quinolones were measured by [[High-Performance Liquid Chromatography]] with fluorescence detection; limits of quantification were 5 ng/ml (enrofloxacin, oxolinic acid) and 10 ng/ml (flumequine, sarafloxacin).
- Pharmacokinetic analysis used PCNONLIN with a two-compartment model for i.v. data, and model selection by [[Akaike Information Criterion]].
- Dosing: oxolinic acid and flumequine at 25 mg/kg; sarafloxacin and enrofloxacin at 10 mg/kg. Medicated feed contained 4.2 mg/g oxolinic acid, 4.7 mg/g flumequine, 1.0 mg/g sarafloxacin and 2.0 mg/g enrofloxacin, per the check analyses.

**Bioavailability (F) after oral dosing**
| Agent | Oral formulation | F (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Oxolinic acid | Medicated feed | 30.1 |
| Flumequine | Medicated feed | 44.7 |
| Flumequine | Aqualets | 44.6 |
| Sarafloxacin | Medicated feed | 2.2 |
| Enrofloxacin | Medicated feed | 55.5 |

- Flumequine bioavailability was nearly identical between the two oral formulations.

**Distribution and elimination (i.v.)**
- Apparent volume of distribution at steady state (Vss): oxolinic acid 5.4, flumequine 3.5, sarafloxacin 2.3, enrofloxacin 6.1 liters/kg. All were well distributed (Vss above 2.3 liters/kg).
- Total body clearance was highest for oxolinic acid (0.28 liter/h/kg), with enrofloxacin at 0.14 liter/h/kg.
- Elimination half-lives: oxolinic acid 18.2 h (shortest), flumequine 22.8 h, sarafloxacin 24.0 h, and enrofloxacin 34.2 h (about twice that of oxolinic acid). Enrofloxacin's long half-life was attributed mainly to its large Vss.

**Absorption and peak concentrations**
- Absorption was fairly rapid but incomplete. Time to peak (Tmax) was 6 h for flumequine and enrofloxacin, and 12 h for oxolinic acid and sarafloxacin (feed); flumequine Aqualets peaked at 12 h.
- Normalized to dose, enrofloxacin showed the highest mean Cmax (about 1.54 µg/ml), followed by flumequine, oxolinic acid and sarafloxacin.

**Comparison with previous work**
- Oxolinic acid bioavailability (30.1%) was higher than values earlier reported at other temperatures and dose-dependent in prior studies.
- The earlier sarafloxacin study (F ≈ 3–7%) likely underestimated the terminal elimination phase, since sampling ended at 48 h.
- The enrofloxacin F of 55% was the highest reported for any quinolone in Atlantic salmon in seawater, though much lower than in mammals.

**Conclusions and implications**
- Flumequine and, more so, enrofloxacin had better pharmacokinetic properties than oxolinic acid; sarafloxacin had poorer properties.
- Peak enrofloxacin concentrations exceeded the highest MIC reported for quinolone-resistant *A. salmonicida* (1.28 µg/ml), and flumequine and enrofloxacin exceeded the peak/MIC ratio of 8:1 associated with bactericidal effect and reduced resistance emergence (per [[Blaser et al.]]).
- The authors judged enrofloxacin the most promising candidate for clinical trials.
