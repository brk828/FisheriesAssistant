#ichthyology #marine-biology #Acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

This paper documents the historical and contemporary status of two [[Acipenseridae|sturgeon]] species in North Carolina waters: the [[Atlantic sturgeon]] (*Acipenser oxyrhynchus*) and the [[Shortnose sturgeon]] (*Acipenser brevirostrum*). While *A. brevirostrum* was historically rare and poorly recognized by commercial fishermen, it is now considered **extinct/extirpated in North Carolina**. *A. oxyrhynchus*, once a substantial contributor to the regional commercial fishery (pre-1900), has undergone a dramatic population decline in both abundance and average body size. The study links this decline to shifts in commercial fishing gear and degradation of estuarine habitats, and notes that both species have since been placed on national and [[IUCN]] rare and endangered species lists.

## Background & Description

- **Historical size (pre-1900, *A. oxyrhynchus*):**
  - Common weight: **~500 lb (225 kg)**
  - Common length: **~12 ft (3.7 m)**
- **Contemporary size (post-decline, *A. oxyrhynchus*):**
  - Maximum weight rarely exceeds **380 lb (172 kg)**
  - Maximum length rarely exceeds **8.5 ft (2.6 m)**
- Represents a marked reduction in maximum attainable size/age structure within the population, suggestive of truncated age classes due to fishing pressure and/or habitat loss.
- No detailed morphometric data provided for *A. brevirostrum* in this excerpt, consistent with its rarity and lack of recognition by fishermen (possible confusion with juvenile *A. oxyrhynchus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Both species historically inhabited [[Estuarine Ecosystems|North Carolina estuaries]] and adjoining coastal/marine waters — anadromous life history typical of [[Acipenseridae]].
- **Movement ecology:** *A. oxyrhynchus* exhibits **extensive movements**, indicating significant migratory behavior between estuarine and marine habitats (consistent with anadromous spawning migrations).
- **Habitat degradation:** Changes in estuarine habitat quality/structure are implicated as a primary driver in:
  - The presumed extinction of *A. brevirostrum* in NC.
  - The marked reduction of *A. oxyrhynchus* populations and body sizes.
- **Fishery interaction:**
  - Primary fishing grounds/gear shifted over time (see Key Findings).
  - Currently, most landings originate from **otter trawl fisheries** and a **single gill net fishery at Bogue Inlet, Carteret County**.

## Key Findings

- **Commercial fishery history (*A. oxyrhynchus*):**
  - Pre-1900 landings: at least **436,900 lbs (198,174 kg)**, valued at **$18,894 in 1880**.
  - **Gear shift timeline:**
    - **1880–1902:** Predominantly gill nets and seines.
    - **Since 1967:** Shift to otter trawls and gill nets.
  - Present-day landings concentrated in **otter trawl fisheries** and the **Bogue Inlet gill net fishery** (Carteret County, NC).
- **Population decline indicators:**
  - Significant reduction in maximum body size/length compared to historical records (500 lb/12 ft → 380 lb/8.5 ft).
  - Presumed **local extinction of *A. brevirostrum*** in North Carolina waters.
- **Conservation status:**
  - Both *A. oxyrhynchus* and *A. brevirostrum* listed as **rare/endangered** on national and **[[IUCN]] Red List** classifications (as of the 1976 publication).
- **Causal factors proposed:**
  - [[Estuarine Habitat Degradation]] linked to demise of shortnose sturgeon and decline of Atlantic sturgeon.
  - Historical overexploitation via commercial fisheries (gear changes reflecting fishing pressure patterns).
