# Razorback Sucker and Colorado River Squawfish Reintroduction Research Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1991 research proposal outlines a three-year study of radio-telemetered and otherwise marked subadult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Verde River]] drainage of Arizona. The work aims to generate the basic ecological data (movements, habitat selection, food habits, age and growth) that current reintroduction efforts lack. The two "big river" fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]], the razorback and the [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), had been extirpated or reduced to senescent reservoir populations across much of their former lower-basin range. Stocking has been extensive, but post-stocking survival has been poor and recapture data scarce. The proposal argues that reintroduction failure is largely due to [[Predation by Exotic Fishes]], and that larger, established fish may be needed. Reproductive studies are excluded, since spawning is not expected for two to five years after stocking.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker and Colorado River squawfish are described as biologically imperiled native fishes of the Colorado River basin. Both were formerly widespread in lower-basin drainages of Arizona. At the time of writing, the squawfish is listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and the razorback has been proposed for listing. Razorbacks are reliably propagated and grown to large sizes in hatcheries, whereas squawfish are not.

Reintroduction history:
- **Razorback sucker:** Sustained reintroduction in Arizona began in 1981 under a 10-year Memorandum of Understanding between the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). (The OCR reads "1@ year"; this is interpreted as 10 year.)
- **Squawfish:** Reintroduction began in 1985 under an "experimental, non-essential" designation from the 1984 amendment to the Endangered Species Act.
- **Stocking totals:** By the end of 1992, about 11,800,000 razorbacks and 605,000 squawfish had been stocked into fluvial habitats of the Salt, Verde, and [[Gila River Basin]] drainages.
- **Size of stocked fish:** About 90% of releases averaged 165 mm or less in total length, and more than 99% of numbers stocked fell in that size range (AGFD records).

Survivorship experiments and monitoring indicated that post-stocking mortality regularly approached unity (near 100%) within several weeks (Minckley 1983; Marsh and Brooks 1989; Marsh and Langhorst 1989; Minckley et al. 1991). Because recapture data are rare, the proposal states that little biological or ecological insight has been gained to guide recovery.

The study focuses on razorbacks because they can be reliably hatchery-reared to large sizes. The [[Verde River]] is described as the most logistically feasible Gila Basin mainstream for detailed ecological work.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Predation and stocking size.** The reintroduction failure is attributed largely to predation by exotic fishes. The proposal suggests that stocking larger specimens could avoid size-selective predation. Ecological information must first be gathered from fish that have become established and resident.

**Geomorphic and habitat framework.** Habitat distribution is described as ultimately controlled by watershed geomorphology, following the river continuum concept (Vannote et al. 1980). The proposal uses a channel classification similar to Rosgen (1985) to divide the Verde mainstem into homogeneous reaches. Habitat types are then characterized using the scheme of Bisson et al. (1981), such as low-gradient riffle, glide, and plunge pool. This system allows comparison with other potential reintroduction sites.

**Study area.** The Verde River is studied from the upper, middle, and lower reaches, and the scope includes the area above and including [[Horseshoe Reservoir]]. A remote-sensing radio receiver is to be placed above the head of Horseshoe Reservoir to detect emigration from the Verde. Similar units may be placed at major irrigation diversions.

**Reproduction.** Spawning is not expected for a minimum of two to five years after stocking of 1+ year old fish. Reproductive studies are therefore deferred, with a new plan to be written if spawning is observed.

## Key Findings

The proposal's principal content is its study design, which comprises five objectives. Statistical testing uses a significance level of α = 0.25, and parametric tests are applied where normality allows.

**Objective 1: Geomorphic reach patterns.** Delineate Verde River reaches using geologic, topographic, aerial, and ground survey information. Reach patterns considered include gradient, confinement, entrenchment, sinuosity, substrate, valley bottom width, and riparian vegetation.

**Objective 2: Habitat types and morphology.** Each reach is systematically subsampled along three 520–1900 m longitudinal sections during modal flow. Measurements include dimensions, substrate, cover, and current velocity. Hypotheses compare habitat descriptors, frequency, surface area, volume, and substrate among reaches, using ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis, and chi-square tests.

**Objective 3: Movements and habitat use of radio-tagged razorbacks.** This is the core objective.
- *Tagging:* About five razorbacks near 400 mm total length, weighing roughly 600 g, will be surgically implanted with ~12 g [[Radiotelemetry]] tags at the [[Page Springs Hatchery]]. The study compares external, internal, and subcutaneous antenna placement.
- *Release:* Tagged fish are held in cages in the river for aerial calibration, then released at one of three sites (lower, middle, upper Verde). About 2,000 similarly sized internally [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging]] (PIT) fish are planted at the same time. Overflights are planned every other day for the first week or two after stocking.
- *Tracking:* Localized fish are tracked by canoe and foot. Each fish is sampled over a 72-hour period at least once; if observations are not independent (Swihart and Slade 1985), three nonadjacent 24-hour periods are sampled.
- *Analysis:* Habitat use is compared with availability using [[Manly's Selectivity Index]] and [[Discriminant Analysis]]. Hypotheses address movement relative to random, and frequency of use by reach, season, and diel period.
- *Release schedule:* Battery life is not expected to exceed three months, so tagged groups are released quarterly, with sites randomized so each receives plants from every season. Up to 10 radio-tagged fish per plant are planned. Late in battery life, fish are to be recaptured to assess long-term effects of surgery.

**Objective 4: Feeding habits.** After the first year, if PIT-tagged razorbacks can be readily recaptured, about 10 specimens per habitat type will be sacrificed quarterly for [[Food Habit Analysis]]. Measures include taxonomic composition, relative wet and dry weight of food, and percent stomach fullness. Diel feeding periodicity is examined if specimens permit.

**Objective 5: Age, growth, and mortality.** Length, weight, and condition are monitored in PIT-tagged recaptures from hoop net, trammel net, or electrofishing surveys, supporting [[Age and Growth]] analysis and otolith verification. Length-frequency comparisons use Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, and condition factors are compared by ANOVA.

**Schedule and budget.** The work is scheduled from April 1991 through September 1994, including field tagging, habitat typing, netting and electroshocking, data analysis, final report writing, and journal manuscripts. A summary table gives effort by personnel category and fiscal year, although the OCR-scanned values are partly garbled. The budget request lists personnel (one Wildlife Specialist II for six months; one Wildlife Specialist I, one Wildlife Assistant II, and two Wildlife Assistant I, each for 12 months), with categories for employee-related expenses, overtime under the Fair Labor Standards Act, travel (including 50 department aircraft flights per year), and equipment (vehicles, radio tags, receivers, canoes, PIT tags, and a PIT tag reader). No dollar totals are given in the scanned text.

**Overall conclusion.** The proposal presents its work as necessary to evaluate the long-term prospects for reestablishing razorbacks in the Verde drainage and elsewhere. Its main recommendation is a shift toward larger hatchery-reared fish to reduce predation mortality, supported by the ecological data this study would produce.
