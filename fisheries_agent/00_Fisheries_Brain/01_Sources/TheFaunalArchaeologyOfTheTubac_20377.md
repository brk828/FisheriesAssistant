# Faunal Archaeology of the Tubac Presidio: Fish and Amphibian Remains

#ichthyology #marine-biology #zooarchaeology #archaeological-fish #historical-ecology

## Executive Summary

This appendix documents fish and amphibian remains recovered from archaeological excavations at the [[Tubac Presidio]], a Spanish colonial site in Arizona. Of 60 fish bones recovered, 32 specimens were identified by Dr. W. L. Minckley. The assemblage includes exotic species not native to the local [[Santa Cruz River]], indicating long-distance trade or transport networks during the Spanish colonial period. Notable finds include [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*), marine fish families ([[Belonidae]] and [[Sciaenidae]]), and local freshwater [[Catostomidae|suckers]]. The presence of these non-local species provides evidence of extensive trade connections and resource procurement patterns at the presidio.

## Background & Description

The fish remains were analyzed as part of broader faunal archaeological investigations at the [[Tubac Presidio]]. Specimens were recovered from stratified contexts representing Early, Middle, and Late periods of occupation. Dr. W. L. Minckley of Arizona State University identified 32 of the 60 total fish bones recovered.

**Identified Taxa:**

- **[[Colorado River Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): A large predatory minnow (family [[Cyprinidae]]) historically reaching weights of 100 pounds or more, now nearly extinct in Arizona. Described as a "big-river fish" inhabiting depths greater than one meter.

- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*, family [[Ictaluridae]]): Native to the Río Sonora in Mexico, not introduced into the [[Santa Cruz Basin]] until 1899.

- **[[Catostomidae]]** (freshwater suckers): Moderately large specimens, not identifiable to genus or species. These represent the only local fish in the assemblage.

- **Marine fish**: Possibly members of [[Belonidae]] (needlefishes) and [[Sciaenidae]] (croakers or drums).

Three amphibian specimens (frogs or toads, [[Ranidae]] or [[Bufonidae]]) were recovered, represented by long bones lacking epiphyses. These may be intrusive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish assemblage reveals significant ecological and geographical information about resource procurement at the presidio:

**Local Habitat Limitations:** The [[Santa Cruz River]] was never a deep river, at least historically, making it unsuitable habitat for [[Colorado River Squawfish]], which require depths greater than one meter. The closest suitable habitat would have been the [[San Pedro River]], located at considerable distance from Tubac.

**Exotic Species Presence:** The occurrence of *Ptychocheilus lucius* indicates procurement from distant river systems. The presence of [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) is particularly anomalous, as this species is native to the Río Sonora in Mexico and was not introduced to the [[Santa Cruz Basin]] until 1899—well after the Spanish colonial period represented by these deposits.

**Marine Connections:** The presence of marine fish families ([[Belonidae]] and [[Sciaenidae]]) indicates trade networks extending to coastal areas, likely the Gulf of California.

## Key Findings

1. **Total Fish Remains:** 60 specimens recovered; 32 identified to family or species level.

2. **Temporal Distribution:**
   - Early period: 11 specimens
   - Middle period: 13 specimens  
   - Late period: 5 specimens
   - (Material from mixed contexts not included in these totals)

3. **Species-Specific Counts:**
   - *Ptychocheilus lucius*: 4 specimens (Early period only)
   - *Catostomidae*: 6 specimens (2 Early, 1 Middle, 3 Late)
   - *Ictalurus pricei*: 3 specimens (all Early period)
   - Marine fish: 6 specimens (2 Early, 3 Middle, 1 Late)
   - Unidentified: 12 specimens (3 Early, 8 Middle, 1 Late)

4. **Evidence of Long-Distance Trade:** The presence of [[Colorado River Squawfish]] from distant river systems and marine fish from coastal waters demonstrates extensive trade or transport networks serving the presidio.

5. **Chronological Anomaly:** The presence of [[Yaqui Catfish]] in Early-period contexts predates its documented introduction to the region by decades, suggesting either misidentification, contextual mixing, or previously undocumented early introduction.

6. **Local Resources:** Only [[Catostomidae|freshwater suckers]] represent locally available fish resources from the [[Santa Cruz River]].

7. **Amphibian Remains:** Three specimens (possibly [[Ranidae]] or [[Bufonidae]]) distributed across Middle and Late periods; potentially intrusive rather than cultural deposits.
