# Gila Topminnow Sites in Arizona: Provisional Extirpation Report 1996–97 Field Season

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) technical report, issued June 1997 as Nongame and Endangered Wildlife Program Technical Report 116, reviews [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction sites in Arizona. Many sites stocked since 1975–1983 no longer hold fish. The authors apply the provisional extirpation criteria proposed by Abarca et al. (1994) to reduce a Section 7 consultation backlog. The report evaluates 27 sites in total:

- **8 sites** are recommended as provisionally extirpated and not restocked.
- **13 sites** are recommended for restocking or other management action.
- **6 sites** require additional surveys before they can meet the criteria.

All recommendations are provisional and require review and final approval by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS).

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) is a small livebearing fish native to historical springs and streams in Arizona. Since 1981, AGFD, the Bureau of Land Management (BLM), USFWS, and the U.S. Forest Service (FS) have cooperated on its recovery. Fish were reintroduced repeatedly, mostly from stock held at [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]] that originated from Monkey, Cocio, and Bylas springs. Many early failures were attributed to poor site selection. Early criteria required only "any available, possibly permanent water" within the historical range.

The report also covers the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), which was stocked at some sites, including Howard Well.

**Extirpation criteria (Abarca et al. 1994).** A population is provisionally extirpated when:
1. Qualified biologists fail to collect topminnows in at least five consecutive surveys for natural populations, or three for reintroduced populations;
2. Surveys cover most available habitat;
3. Surveys occur when detection is most likely (usually June–November);
4. Mosquitofish, if present, are preserved as voucher specimens; and
5. If a site is completely dry, surface waters above and below it are surveyed by the same methods, and the population may be declared extirpated only if no other waters are present.

After provisional extirpation, a report is prepared, reviewed by AGFD and other biologists, and then finalized by AGFD and USFWS with the Desert Fishes Recovery Team.

**Data limitations.** Survey thoroughness and percent of habitat surveyed were inconsistently recorded in historical monitoring. Estimates were based on personal knowledge and communication with previous collectors. Surveyor qualifications were not verified; only organization and job title are listed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Across the sites, the main threats and habitat problems were:

- **Drying and low water.** Many sites were dry or nearly dry in one or more survey years. Examples include Blue Mountain Spring (dry in 1983, 1994, and 1996), Mesquite Tank #2 (dry during most visits), and Fig Spring (shallow or no water).
- **Flooding and scouring.** Flood events eliminated or reduced populations at Bain Spring (April 1991), Cave Creek (January 1993, "the largest on record for this stream"), Cow Creek (January 1993), and TT Spring (probably three consecutive floods in 1987).
- **Water diversion.** Springs were piped to other uses at White Rock Spring and Unnamed Spring #2. A pipe also removed water from TT Spring.
- **Cattle grazing and recreation.** Heavy grazing was recorded at many sites. Heavy public use affected Sabino Canyon, and fence and exclosure damage was noted at Thicket Spring.
- **Vegetation overgrowth.** Cattails (*Typha*) and aquatic plants were a concern at Howard Well, Thicket Spring, and The Lake.
- **Nonnative species.** [[Green Sunfish]] are a barrier to recovery in Sabino Canyon and Lime Creek. Mosquitofish, fathead minnows, and [[Bullfrog]] populations also occur at several sites. Mosquitofish at Martin Well may have been mistaken for topminnows at Howard Well.
- **Water quality and habitat quality.** At Mansfield, mining activity was evident and pH measured 5.8. Cold temperatures and steep gradients made Canada del Oro unsuitable.

Native fishes frequently co-occurring with topminnows include [[Longfin Dace]], desert sucker, [[Gila Chub]], and speckled dace. Lowland leopard frogs, tiger salamanders, and aquatic invertebrates were also recorded.

## Key Findings

### Provisionally Extirpated Sites (8)
These sites are recommended as provisionally extirpated and not for restocking:

| Site (Site #) | Key reason |
|---|---|
| Blue Mountain Spring (#244) | Dry conditions; no fish in 1996 |
| Josephine Canyon (#197) | Physical barriers, flood-scoured canyon, dry downstream; extirpated in 1977 per Minckley and Brooks (1985) |
| Mansfield (#93) | No fish ever found; poor water quality (pH 5.8), mining impacts, steep narrow canyon |
| Pasture Well (#31) | Dried 1987–1989 after supporting fish for 4–6 years |
| Unnamed Spring #2 (#29) | Completely dry before 1987; no fish since |
| Unnamed Spring #6 (#66) | Insufficient water; poor fish habitat |
| Unnamed (TT) Spring (#14B) | No fish since 1983; intermittent flows |
| White Rock Spring (#249) | Water piped away; never established |

### Sites Recommended for Management or Restocking (13)
- **Restock:** Bain Spring (#26; flood-damaged, Category 1; create pools and protect from floods), Camp Creek (#160; suitable habitat but flood-prone; attempt several reintroductions), Cave Creek (#49B; Category 4; reintroduce repeatedly), Cow Creek (#72; Category 1; restock near the old dam), Lime Cabin Spring (#248; restock from Lime Creek; control green sunfish), Sabino Canyon (#250; restock after sunfish-free renovation), The Lake (#121; no known cause for loss), Thicket Spring (#15; restock only after deepening pond, rebuilding fence, and managing *Typha*), and T.T. Spring (#14A; restock only if flows are adequate).
- **Restock with conditions:** Bronco Canyon Spring Tank (#54; re-stock only if the tank holds water year-round), Howard Well (#83; modify habitat and restock; limit public access), Mesquite Tank #2 (#68A; repair dam and valve, then restock), and Tule Creek Seep (2E) (#73; improve habitat, then restock from Tule Creek).

Notable population history: Cave Creek had Gila topminnows from at least 1989 to 1992. Cow Creek had them from 1981 to 1992, and they dispersed to Humbug Creek. Bain Spring had a large population in 1989, but flood damage in 1991 apparently eliminated it. Lime Creek still held topminnows in 1996, about 2.5 miles of occupied habitat.

### Sites Requiring Additional Surveys (6)
- **Apache Canyon, East Fork (#176):** Stocked 1976; extirpation listed by Minckley and Brooks (1985), but the location may be a different Apache Canyon. Resurvey needed.
- **Canada del Oro (#79):** Probably extirpated; unsuitable due to cold water, high flows, and steep gradient. Further surveys needed.
- **Fig Spring (#246):** Gone by June 1983; spring head completely sedimented. One final survey needed.
- **Rock Tank Spring (#64):** Fish absent since 1984–1985; two more surveys needed. Restocking not recommended.
- **Romero Canyon (#252):** Not monitored until 1997; only green sunfish collected. Two more surveys over two years needed.
- **Unnamed Spring #5 (Paradise Spring, #65):** Extirpated by 1985; one more survey needed. Restocking not recommended.

### Additional Findings
- Gila topminnow reintroduction and monitoring have occurred for over 20 years. The report lists about 50 surveyors and affiliations (AGFD, BLM, FS, USFWS, and Arizona State University).
- The 1990 USFWS Biological Opinion identified Mesquite Tank #2 for additional reintroduction.
- The 1996 survey at Cave Creek recorded speckled dace, possibly rare or recently reintroduced from Camp Creek.
- Funding came from the AGFD Heritage Fund, hunting and fishing license revenues, and Project ES, Job 09, under Title VI of the Endangered Species Act.
