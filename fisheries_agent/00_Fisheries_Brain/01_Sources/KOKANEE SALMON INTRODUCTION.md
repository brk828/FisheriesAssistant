# Kokanee Salmon Introduction — Lake Mohave (1962)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary
This report documents a fish stocking event conducted by the [[Nevada Fish and Game Department]] on April 19, 1962, in which 100,000 [[Kokanee Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*) fry were introduced into [[Lake Mohave]], within a three-mile stretch of the [[Colorado River]] immediately below [[Hoover Dam]]. The fry, sourced from the [[Verdi Hatchery]], were transported via tank truck and boat and released over submerged gravel bars at approximately 10 feet of depth. The stocking strategy was explicitly designed with future spawning behavior in mind, targeting substrate expected to remain inundated through the fall and winter spawning season. The report was compiled by Norman Wood on January 21, 1963, likely as a post-introduction monitoring or planning record.

## Background & Description
- **Life stage at release:** Fry (early juvenile stage, post-yolk-sac absorption)
- **Average length:** ~1 inch (2.54 cm)
- **Species identity:** Kokanee Salmon is the landlocked, non-anadromous form of [[Sockeye Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*), a member of the family [[Salmonidae]]

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Landlocked reservoir/riverine system — [[Lake Mohave]] functions as a substitute for the natural lacustrine environment Kokanee typically require, since this population lacks ocean access (unlike anadromous [[Sockeye Salmon]])
- **Release microhabitat:** Gravel bars submerged under ~10 feet of water within a high-flow river stretch below [[Hoover Dam]]
- **Spawning ecology considerations:** Kokanee are known to return to natal gravel substrates to spawn in fall/winter; release site selection was deliberately constrained to gravel bars predicted to remain water-covered during this critical return period, reflecting hatchery managers' anticipation of the species' spawning migration behavior even in a closed freshwater system
- **Hydrological context:** River was at "high stage" (elevated flow/water level) at time of release, directly influencing site selection logic
- **Trophic role:** Not discussed in source; Kokanee are typically planktivorous filter feeders in lentic systems, a niche presumably intended for Lake Mohave's pelagic zone

## Key Findings
- 100,000 Kokanee Salmon fry (~1 in. average length) were released on **April 19, 1962**
- Origin: **Verdi Hatchery**, transported by tank truck to the Hoover Dam powerhouse ramp, then by boat downriver
- Release zone: a **3-mile stretch** of the Colorado River below Hoover Dam, within the Lake Mohave system
- Fry were planted over **gravel bars** in water ~**10 feet deep**
- Site selection criterion: gravel bars needed to remain submerged through **fall/winter** to coincide with expected **spawning return** period
- This represents an early fisheries management effort to establish a self-sustaining Kokanee population in a landlocked western reservoir system
