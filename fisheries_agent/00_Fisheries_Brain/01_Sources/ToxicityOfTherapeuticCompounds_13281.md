# Toxicity of 22 Therapeutic Compounds to Six Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife report (Resource Publication 35, December 1966) by [[Wayne A. Willford]] of the [[Fish Control Laboratory]] measures the acute toxicity of 22 therapeutic chemicals to six freshwater fish taxa. The compounds were 18 parasiticides and 4 oral bacteriostats. Using 24- and 48-hour [[Static Bioassay]] trials, the author found that 16 compounds were toxic and 6 were not within the test window. The study was designed to supply toxicity data before efficacy and residue research, which the [[Food And Drug Administration]] requires before clearing drugs. The authors explicitly state that the data are not recommendations for use.

## Background & Description

Prevention and control of fish diseases is a core objective of the [[Fish Control Laboratory]]. Many drugs had known effective concentrations, but their toxicity had not been systematically reported. The 18 parasiticides were chosen from recommendations by other investigators as possible external treatments. The 4 oral bacteriostats were tested to see whether leaching or excretion into water could harm fish.

**Test organisms** (obtained from hatcheries, quarantined 10 days, and acclimated):
- [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], and [[Lake Trout]], all tested at 12°C
- [[Bluegill]], tested at 12°C
- [[Channel Catfish]], tested at 17°C at the Southeastern Fish Control Laboratory, Warm Springs, Georgia

**Method:** Tests used 5-gallon glass jars with 15 L of reconstituted deionized water (total hardness 42 ppm) and no more than 1 g of fish per liter. Each test had 10 concentrations, 10 fish per concentration, and 20 control fish. Survival was recorded at 24 and 48 hours. The LC50 (median lethal concentration) was estimated by probit analysis following [[Litchfield and Wilcoxon]] (1949), with 95-percent confidence intervals. Results are reported as ppm of total formulated or technical material, not active ingredient.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper focuses on controlled laboratory toxicity, not field ecology. Its relevance to the aquatic environment is in the disease-control context:

- Several compounds were studied as prolonged or indefinite pond and tank treatments against [[Ichthyophthirius]] ("Ich"), a protozoan parasite. Others, such as [[Malachite Green]] and [[Formalin]], were considered for other parasites and fungi.
- Temperature affected toxicity. Formalin's toxicity was higher at 17°C than at 12°C, and the author suggests that toxicity rises with temperature generally.
- Water solubility limited some tests. [[TV-1096]] saturated at about 30 ppm, and the sulfonamides and [[Flagyl]] were not soluble enough to reach lethal levels.
- The fish were healthy. The author notes that sick or stressed fish could respond differently.

## Key Findings

**Overall results:** Of 22 compounds, 16 were toxic and 6 were not. The toxic compounds, ranked by toxicity, were:
[[Malachite Green]], [[Trolene]], [[CoRal]], [[Tiguvon]], [[Roccal]], P.M.A. ([[Pyridylmercuric Acetate]]), [[Acriflavine]], amopyroquin dihydrochloride, [[Merthiolate]], [[Methylene Blue]], [[Neguvon]], [[Ruelene]], [[TV-1096]], [[Nickel Sulfate]], [[Formalin]], and [[Quinacrine Hydrochloride]].

The non-toxic compounds were [[Erythromycin Thiocyanate]], quinine hydrochloride, [[Flagyl]], sulfamerazine, sulfamethazine, and sulfisoxazole. These were not toxic at 100 ppm or at saturation within 48 hours.

**Approximate 48-hour LC50 ranges by toxicity class:**

| Toxicity class | Compounds | 48-h LC50 range |
|---|---|---|
| Most toxic | [[Malachite Green]] | 0.11–0.40 ppm, uniform across species |
| High | Trolene, CoRal, Tiguvon, Roccal, P.M.A. | Roughly 0.5–10 ppm (Roccal 1.12–3.40; P.M.A. 2.9–16.0) |
| Intermediate | Acriflavine, amopyroquin, merthiolate, methylene blue, Neguvon, Ruelene, TV-1096 | About 10–100 ppm |
| Low | Formalin, nickel sulfate, quinacrine | Usually above 100 ppm (formalin 96–185; nickel sulfate 75–495) |

**Compound-specific findings:**
- **Malachite green:** The author reports 0.21 and 0.20 ppm LC50 at 24 and 48 hours on channel catfish at 17°C. [[Clemens and Sneed]] (1958a) reported 0.14 ppm at 25°C. Concentrations above 0.11 ppm were toxic within 48 hours, so [[Amlacher]]'s recommended 0.15 ppm long-term treatment carries risk.
- **Roccal:** Showed relatively uniform toxicity. A 10-fold lower treatment level was possible when converted from 10% to 50% active formulation.
- **P.M.A.:** Showed wide variation among species. Rainbow trout were up to three times as sensitive as brook trout. Channel catfish LC50 values (3.22 and 2.89 ppm at 24 and 48 hours) matched earlier reports.
- **Merthiolate:** Showed extreme variation. At 24 hours, LC50 ranged from 7.5 ppm (channel catfish) to 110 ppm (brown trout). At 48 hours, lake trout were the most sensitive, with an LC50 of 2.1 ppm. The author cautioned that this variation makes the compound difficult to use in routine multi-species treatments.
- **Acriflavine:** Bluegill were most sensitive (18.0 and 13.5 ppm at 24 and 48 hours). Channel catfish were most resistant (43.5 and 33.2 ppm). The result was about one-fourth the toxicity [[Clemens and Sneed]] reported (6.8 ppm at 48 hours at 20°C).
- **Amopyroquin:** Trout (except lake trout) had 48-hour LC50 values of 35–40 ppm. A 0.1 ppm treatment level gives a safety margin of more than a hundredfold.
- **Methylene blue:** Rainbow trout were most sensitive (LC50 16 ppm at 48 hours). Channel catfish were most resistant (104 ppm). The author considers the safety margin good at use levels.
- **Neguvon vs. Ruelene:** Neguvon's 24- and 48-hour LC50 values often differed by at least a factor of two (lake trout: 41 ppm at 24 hours, 9 ppm at 48 hours). Ruelene's were nearly identical (lake trout: 27 ppm at both times), suggesting it may degrade rapidly in the test vessel.
- **TV-1096:** Brown trout did not die at concentrations below its 30 ppm solubility limit. For the other species, 48-hour LC50 values were 16.1–28.2 ppm.
- **Nickel sulfate:** Lake trout 75 ppm to bluegill 495 ppm at 48 hours. Fifty to 275 ppm for 24 hours did not kill brook trout.
- **Formalin:** Relatively uniform 48-hour LC50 range of 96–185 ppm. Toxicity rose with temperature. The author estimates at least a sixfold safety margin at recommended use levels.
- **Quinacrine hydrochloride:** Lake trout were roughly 10 times as sensitive as other trout and 3–4 times as sensitive as bluegill and channel catfish. Lake trout 48-hour LC50 was 21 ppm. Mortality was erratic over a wide concentration range, suggesting individual variation in resistance.
- **Erythromycin thiocyanate and quinine hydrochloride:** Not toxic at 100 ppm within 48 hours.
- **Flagyl and sulfonamides:** Not toxic at saturation (about 100 ppm) within 48 hours.

**Limitations and conclusions:**
- The author notes that discrepancies with earlier studies may come from differences in water quality, temperature, fish condition, and compound purity.
- None of the compounds were cleared by the Food and Drug Administration or the Department of Agriculture for fish destined for human consumption. The data are not recommendations for use.
- The author recommends further study of efficacy and residues, especially for compounds under consideration for prolonged treatment of Ichthyophthirius, such as [[Tiguvon]] (the least toxic of the organophosphates tested).
