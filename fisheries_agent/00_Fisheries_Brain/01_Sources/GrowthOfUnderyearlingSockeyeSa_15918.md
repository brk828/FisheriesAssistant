# Growth of Underyearling Sockeye Salmon (Oncorhynchus nerka) on Diel Temperature Cycles

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #aquaculture #thermal-biology

## Executive Summary

This 1978 technical report by W.C. Clarke examines whether cyclical (diel) water temperature regimes can promote faster growth in underyearling [[Sockeye Salmon]] compared to constant-temperature rearing, a question directly relevant to hatchery efficiency in Pacific salmon [[Aquaculture]]. Fish were reared for 42 days at five constant temperatures (7.5–17.5°C) and two diel thermocycles (7–13°C and 5–15°C, both averaging 10°C). Growth rate increased linearly with constant temperature, but critically, both thermocycles produced growth rates *exceeding* that predicted from the 10°C mean — with the 5–15°C cycle producing the strongest effect. This indicates that sockeye growth physiologically acclimates to the **maximum**, not the mean, temperature of a diel cycle, suggesting cycling regimes could be a more cost-effective hatchery heating strategy than constant elevated temperature.

## Background & Description

- **Study stock**: Sockeye salmon eggs from the [[Fulton River]], fertilized October 1975, reared at [[Rosewall Creek]] hatchery until September 1976.
- **Initial body size**: Mean weight at start of experiment = 19.3 g.
- **Growth metrics measured**: Individual specific growth rate for length and weight (freeze-branded for individual ID), coefficient of condition, percent dry weight, and plasma sodium concentration (post-seawater challenge).
- **Percent dry weight**: Baseline stock = 27.8%; ranged from 27.4% (7.5°C) to 30.6% (17.5°C) by week 8 — a linear relationship (r = 0.66) with constant temperature.
- **Coefficient of condition**: Increased linearly with constant temperature (r = 0.54); diel thermocycles did not significantly alter condition, though it trended slightly lower due to length outpacing weight gain.
- **Smolting status**: All groups (constant and thermocycle) were judged smolted based on [[Plasma Sodium]] response to a 24-hr seawater challenge test (Clarke and Blackburn 1977 protocol).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Sockeye salmon fry are known to perform a **diel vertical migration through the thermocline** of their nursery lakes (e.g., [[Babine Lake]]), as documented by McDonald (1973) and Narver (1970) — this natural behavior motivated testing thermocycling regimes rather than constant temperature in this species.
- The experimental design mimicked a natural diel thermal environment: fish experienced 12 hr of elevated (feeding) temperature and 12 hr at a cooler baseline, with gradual 1.5-hr transitions, approximating lake thermocline dynamics experienced by wild underyearlings.
- Feeding was concentrated during the warm phase of each cycle (3 feedings between 0530–1630), aligning food availability with peak metabolic/appetite windows — consistent with findings that sockeye appetite increases most rapidly 7–11 hr into food deprivation ([[Brett 1971]]) and that a meal is 90% digested within 11 hr at 15°C ([[Brett and Higgs 1970]]).
- This connects to the broader concept of **thermal acclimation of specific physiological functions** rather than whole-organism acclimation — paralleling other ectotherms such as desert pupfish ([[Cyprinodon nevadensis]]), goldfish ([[Carassius auratus]]), cutthroat trout ([[Salmo clarki clarki]]), and lizards, where different traits (growth, reproduction, critical thermal maximum) acclimate differently to cycle mean vs. maximum.

## Key Findings

- **Constant temperature growth**: Specific growth rate for weight increased linearly with temperature over 7.5–17.5°C (r = 0.75; Y = 0.06596X − 0.311). Specific growth rate for length was similarly linear (r = 0.74; Y = 0.01827X − 0.064).
- **Thermocycle growth in weight**:
  - 7–13°C cycle → 0.46%/day
  - 5–15°C cycle → 0.63%/day
  - Both exceeded the predicted 0.35%/day at a constant 10°C (the cycles' mean temperature).
- **Thermocycle growth in length**:
  - 7–13°C cycle (avg of 2 replicates) → 0.15%/day
  - 5–15°C cycle → 0.21%/day
  - Compared to 0.12%/day at constant 10°C.
- **Apparent acclimation temperature** (equivalent constant temperature, via bioassay regression):
  - Growth in length: 7–13°C cycle ≈ 10.8–12.3°C (replicate-dependent); 5–15°C cycle ≈ 15.3°C.
  - Growth in weight: 7–13°C cycle ≈ 11.4°C; 5–15°C cycle ≈ 13.9°C.
  - Shift per 1°C increase in cycle **maximum** temperature: **+0.8°C** for length growth, **+0.6°C** for weight growth — demonstrating acclimation to the cycle's peak rather than its mean.
- **Non-growth parameters** (condition, % dry weight, plasma sodium) did **not** show a significant shift in apparent acclimation temperature from the 10°C cycle mean — indicating the acclimation shift is specific to growth rate rather than a generalized metabolic/physiological compensation.
- **Disproportionate effect of cycle amplitude**: The wider-amplitude 5–15°C cycle stimulated growth disproportionately more than the narrower 7–13°C cycle, suggesting a non-linear relationship between thermocycle amplitude and acclimation shift — possibly reflecting synchronization strength of an underlying [[Circadian Rhythm]] (cf. Hoffman 1969 on lizard thermocycle synchronization).
- **Comparative context**: The 0.6°C/°C shift for weight gain in sockeye is comparable to a 0.4°C/°C shift reported for rainbow trout fry ([[Hokanson et al. 1977]]).
- **Practical implication**: Diel thermocycling — particularly with feeding timed to the warm phase — may offer a more **cost-effective method to accelerate hatchery salmon growth** than continuously heating water to a constant elevated temperature, since less total thermal energy input yields comparable or superior growth acceleration.
- **Future directions proposed**: Testing effects of thermocycle phase, amplitude, and duration; assessing whether growth stimulation persists beyond 8 weeks; testing generalizability across other salmonid species.
