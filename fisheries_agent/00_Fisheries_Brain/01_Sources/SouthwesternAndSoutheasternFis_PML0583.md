# Southwestern and Southeastern Fishes Council Proceedings: Recovery Planning, Habitat Protection and Council Formation (1975)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This compiled record from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] and the [[Southwestern Fishes Council]] covers several 1975 activities in fish conservation. It includes an update on recovery team functioning, a presentation on the [[Endangered Species Act]] and [[Critical Habitat]], and a proposed list of endangered and threatened fishes of the southwestern United States. It also reports on a planned refugium for [[Colorado River Fishes]] and describes the formation of the [[Southeastern Fishes Council]]. The main formal output is two resolutions from the Seventh Annual Symposium of the Desert Fishes Council (Death Valley, California, 13–14 November 1975): one commending Utah and Wyoming for their work on a native fish, and one asking Colorado to designate the Yampa River as a wild river.

## Background & Description

The document is a set of council minutes, reports and resolutions rather than a research paper, so it contains no sampling data, sample sizes or population statistics.

Recovery teams were described as entities that prepare a [[Recovery Plan]] and coordinate its objectives. Teams appointed by the Director of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] take on an official role and are eligible for administrative funding. At the time, that funding did not cover research, studies or team member salaries.

The Southeastern Fishes Council had its origins in a draft constitution circulated in the fall of 1974. Its formation followed these steps:

- **Fall 1974:** Draft constitution sent to selected southeastern individuals for review.
- **March 1975:** Group met at [[Herbert Boschung]]'s residence in Tuscaloosa, Alabama, during an Alabama endangered species meeting.
- **June 1975:** Council formally established at the annual meeting of the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists (ASIH) in Williamsburg, Virginia.

Officers were [[Royal D. Suttkus]] (Tulane University, Chairman), [[David Etnier]] (University of Tennessee, Chairman-elect) and Herbert Boschung (University of Alabama, Secretary-Treasurer).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Southeastern Fishes Council constitution puts more emphasis on environmental preservation than the Desert Fishes Council constitution does. Its stated objectives include:

- Preserving habitats with rich natural fish assemblages.
- Protecting populations of single, unique species.
- Ensuring retention and curatorial care of collected specimens as documentary evidence.

The resolutions identify habitat destruction and change across the native range as a major cause of endangerment for [[Big River Fishes]]. The [[Colorado River System]] species named are:

- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)

The [[Yampa River]] in Colorado still provides habitat that supports breeding populations of these fishes. Its loss "would likely render these fishes extinct," according to the resolution.

## Key Findings

- **Recovery planning:** Recovery teams function as official entities for preparing recovery plans and coordinating their objectives. Administrative funding is available, but research, studies and salaries are not covered at this stage.
- **Endangered Species Act update:** [[Jim Williams]] of the Office of Endangered Species reported on progress under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. He also introduced the concept of critical habitat and how it fits into recovery planning.
- **Proposed endangered and threatened fishes list:** Williams presented a proposed list of endangered and threatened fishes of the southwestern United States for Council discussion. The list was discussed at length. The full list is not reproduced in this record.
- **Colorado River refugium:** [[W. L. Minckley]] of Arizona State University reviewed funding problems for a proposed refugium at the [[Phoenix Zoo]]. The project is considered promising for Colorado River fishes, and efforts to secure finances will continue. Minckley is the Council's liaison to the zoo.
- **Steinhart Aquarium report:** Castro of the [[Steinhart Aquarium]] in San Francisco could not report because he had not yet received his fish.
- **Southeastern Fishes Council formation:** The council was formally established in June 1975, with [[Royal D. Suttkus]] as Chairman, [[David Etnier]] as Chairman-elect and [[Herbert Boschung]] as Secretary-Treasurer. Its objectives focus on habitat preservation, protection of unique species and curation of specimens.
- **Resolution commending Utah and Wyoming:** Adopted unanimously at the Seventh Annual Symposium of the Desert Fishes Council (Death Valley, California, 13–14 November 1975). It commends the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources and the Wyoming Game and Fish Department for their efforts on behalf of a fish species. Copies go to the state agency directors, both governors and the Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The record is signed by [[Robert Rush Miller]], Chairman.
- **Resolution 75-2 (Yampa River wild river designation):** Adopted unanimously at the same symposium. It asks the State of Colorado to designate the Yampa River as a [[Wild River Designation]]. The resolution argues that this would preserve essential habitat for the razorback sucker, Colorado River squawfish and humpback chub, as well as the state's and nation's scenic and fishery resources. Copies go to the Colorado Division of Wildlife, the Governor of Colorado and the Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. It is also signed by Robert Rush Miller.
