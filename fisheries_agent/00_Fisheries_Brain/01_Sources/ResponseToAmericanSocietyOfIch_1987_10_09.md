# Response to ASIH Regarding Strawberry Reservoir Rotenone Eradication Project

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1987 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service letter responds to concerns raised by the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists (ASIH) regarding a proposed rotenone-based fish eradication project at [[Strawberry Reservoir]], Utah. The document defends the use of [[Rotenone]] as a fishery management tool, citing precedent from the 1962 [[Green River]] treatment below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], and outlines the rationale for eradicating undesirable competitor species ([[Utah Chub]] and [[Utah Sucker]]) to support reintroduction of the piscivorous [[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]] in place of the extirpated [[Yellowstone Cutthroat Trout]]. The letter emphasizes interagency coordination, environmental review processes, and projected economic/recreational benefits of the restoration project.

## Background & Description
The source is a regulatory correspondence document rather than a morphological study; however, it references species-level ecological traits relevant to management decisions:
- **[[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]]**: Described as growing to a "rather large size" and being **piscivorous**, traits that make it suitable for controlling undesirable prey/competitor fish populations such as the [[Utah Chub]].
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]**: Noted to aggregate ("concentrate") below dam structures under altered flow conditions, indicating behavioral/physiological sensitivity to environmental disruption rather than direct toxicant morphology effects.
- No detailed anatomical, meristic, or coloration data are provided in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Strawberry Reservoir]]**: A managed reservoir ecosystem within Utah's [[Central Utah Project]], where trout populations are managed against competition from non-game fishes.
- **Competitive dynamics**: [[Utah Chub]] and [[Utah Sucker]] are identified as competitors with trout for resources, prompting eradication efforts to favor salmonid restoration.
- **Trophic replacement strategy**: The [[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]], as a native piscivorous subspecies, is proposed to fill the ecological role vacated by the [[Yellowstone Cutthroat Trout]] while simultaneously suppressing chub populations through predation.
- **Tributary stream rehabilitation**: Management includes habitat improvement of tributary streams (in cooperation with the [[U.S. Forest Service]]) to establish a **self-sustaining fishery**, reducing reliance on hatchery stocking.
- **River-wide precedent**: The 1962 treatment of 225 miles of the [[Green River]] is cited as a historical case study of large-scale rotenone application; post-treatment changes in invertebrate fauna were attributed to **recolonization dynamics** driven by altered environmental conditions from dam operation, not solely toxicant effects.
- **Regulatory/environmental oversight**: The [[Environmental Protection Agency]] reviewed rotenone's environmental effects and concluded it does not pose a broader environmental problem when properly applied.

## Key Findings
- The USFWS asserts that the decline of [[Colorado Squawfish]] below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] resulted from **altered environmental conditions** following impoundment, not from the 1962 rotenone treatment itself.
- Invertebrate community shifts observed after the Green River rotenone treatment were **temporary**, with recolonization by taxa tolerant of new dam-regulated conditions.
- [[Rotenone]] is affirmed by both ASIH and USFWS as a **valuable fishery management tool** when applied under proper planning and safety precautions.
- The Utah Division of Wildlife Resources projects that establishing a self-sustaining [[Bear Lake Cutthroat Trout]] fishery would:
  - Save resources equivalent to **60,000 additional pounds of trout** stocked elsewhere in Utah.
  - Generate **37,500 additional angler-days** of recreation.
  - Contribute approximately **$1 million** to Utah's economy.
- Project planning involved a 10-member **Strawberry Interagency Citizen Advisory Team**, with multi-year coordination (over 3 years) and a formal **Environmental Assessment** process open to public and organizational review.
- The letter frames the Strawberry Reservoir project as procedurally sound, contrasting it with ASIH's characterization of the Green River treatment as an "environmental disaster."
