# New Entocytherid Ostracods from Tennessee and Virginia

#ostracoda #freshwater-biology #entocytheridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes four new entocytherid ostracods: [[Ascetocythere holti]], [[Dactylocythere enoploholca]], [[Dactylocythere myura]], and [[Dactylocythere spinata]]. The specimens come from the upper Tennessee and [[Cumberland River]] drainages in Tennessee and the [[Holston River]] drainage in Virginia. Entocytherids are small crustaceans that live as epibionts on [[Cambarus]] crayfish. The paper's main novel observation is an unpaired, heavily sclerotized structure in males, named the [[Sternal Spine]]. The authors also document the associated internal skeletal apodemes and trabeculae, and they provide host, locality, and type-depository data for each new species.

## Background & Description

Entocytherids are epizoic ostracods that occur on crayfish hosts. The paper focuses on two genera, [[Dactylocythere]] and [[Ascetocythere]], and on the male copulatory complex and the internal endoskeleton as diagnostic characters.

**The sternal spine.** The authors first observed this structure in *Dactylocythere spinata*. It is an unpaired, posteriorly extending spine on the ventromedian line between the first pair of legs. It articulates anteriorly with paired paramedian ventral prongs, which extend between the bases of the maxillae and mandibles. The prongs are supported dorsolaterally by the horns of paired A-shaped apodemes. Trabeculae connect these elements to the mandible and maxilla. The illustrated specimen is a paratypic male of *Dt. xystroides* from Hurricane Creek, Humphreys County, Tennessee.

**Distribution of the structure within the genus.** The sternal spine occurs, in various forms, in males of at least 10 of the 27 described species of *Dactylocythere*. Three further species (*Dt. jeanae*, *Dt. striophylax*, *Dt. suteri*) have the A-shaped apodemes and ventral prongs but no sternal spine. The remaining species appear to lack the apodemes. The authors note that the structure may be present but weakly sclerotized, and therefore hard to see, in *Ankylocythere heterodonta* and some unlisted *Dactylocythere*. Earlier work by [[Enrique Rioja]] (1940, 1941) on the endoskeleton of *Entocythere heterodonta* did not recognize these elements.

**Species descriptions.** Each new species is described from males and females. The entries cover the shell outline, the male copulatory complex (peniferum, clasping apparatus, finger guard), female genital complex, measurements, type-locality, type depository, hosts, and associated entocytherids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hosts:** Each species is tied to particular crayfish. *Ascetocythere holti* was found on [[Cambarus sphenoides]] and on crayfish related to *Cambarus distans* and *Cambarus obeyensis*. *Dactylocythere enoploholca* was collected from [[Cambarus longirostris]] and a *Cambarus* sp. *Dactylocythere myura* occurred on an undescribed crayfish allied to [[Cambarus carolinus]]. *Dactylocythere spinata* was on a crayfish tentatively identified as *Cambarus distans*.
- **Habitat:** Collection sites were small streams, spring-fed streams, creek systems, and burrows along a stream bank. Localities span the Cumberland River drainage (Anderson, Campbell, Fentress, Morgan, and Scott counties, Tennessee) and the Holston River drainage (Washington and Smyth counties, Virginia).
- **Co-occurring entocytherids:** The paper lists associations with *Donnaldsoncythere tuberosa*, *Dn. scalis*, *Ascetocythere hyperoche*, *Dactylocythere falcata*, and several unidentified *Dactylocythere* and *Entocythere* species. Together these show that multiple entocytherid species can share a crayfish host and locality.
- **Range notes:** *Dt. enoploholca* is known only from its type-locality, the South Fork of the Holston River, Washington County, Virginia. *Dt. myura* is known from two nearby sites in the Chilhowie area, about 2.5 miles apart.

## Key Findings

1. **New structure described.** The paper reports the sternal spine, an unpaired ventral sclerotized spine in males, with its associated ventral prongs, A-shaped apodemes, and trabeculae. It is present in males of at least 10 of 27 *Dactylocythere* species.

2. ***Ascetocythere holti* (new species).** The male has a copulatory complex with three peniferal processes. Its closest relative is *A. sclera*, from which it differs in having a much longer anterior process and an acute, undulating posterior process. Males measure 0.37–0.41 mm long and 0.21–0.24 mm high (10 males, 10 females measured). The holotype male and allotype are NMNH no. 126974. The type-locality is a stream in Scott County, Tennessee. The species is associated with *Donnaldsoncythere tuberosa* at several Tennessee sites. The species is named for Perry C. Holt, who supplied many specimens.

3. ***Dactylocythere enoploholca* (new species).** This species lacks a sternal spine and has a heel-like prominence on the posteroventral margin of the peniferum. Its nearest relatives are *Dt. chalaza*, *Dt. pachysphyrata*, and *Dt. spinata*. Males measure 0.44–0.49 mm long and 0.25–0.28 mm high. The holotype male and allotype are NMNH no. 126973. The only known locality is the South Fork of the Holston River, Washington County, Virginia. The name derives from Greek words meaning "armed furrow", referring to the folded dorsal extremity of the accessory groove.

4. ***Dactylocythere myura* (new species).** This species also lacks a sternal spine. Its clasping apparatus is L-shaped and clearly divided into vertical and horizontal rami. The finger guard has three lobes. Males measure 0.46–0.48 mm long and 0.25–0.27 mm high (9 males and 5 females in the type series). The holotype male and allotype are NMNH no. 126975. The type-locality is a spring-fed stream near Chilhowie, Washington County, Virginia. A second locality is in Smyth County, Virginia. It is most similar in finger-guard shape to *Dt. suteri*.

5. ***Dactylocythere spinata* (new species).** This species has a prominent, long sternal spine directed posteriorly, the defining feature of the paper. Its finger guard has a bituberculate posteroventral prominence. Males measure 0.44–0.48 mm long and 0.27–0.29 mm high (8 males and 9 females in the type series). The holotype male and allotype are NMNH no. 126972. The type-locality is a small stream near Caryville, Campbell County, Tennessee (Cumberland River drainage). The species is most closely related to *Dt. chalaza*, and it is distinguished from *Dt. pachysphyrata* and *Dt. chalaza* by differences in the sternal spine and finger guard.

6. **Type deposition.** All four holotypic males and allotypes are deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]] (Smithsonian Institution). Paratypes are split among the Smithsonian, [[C. Willard Hart Jr.]], and [[Horton H. Hobbs III]].
