# Cactáceas y Suculentas Mexicanas: Vol. XIII, No. 3 (July–September 1968)

#botany #cactaceae #crassulaceae

## Executive Summary

This issue of the journal of the [[Sociedad Mexicana De Cactologia]] (Mexican Cactology Society) is a botanical bulletin on cacti and succulents. It describes a new species, [[Echeveria Minima]], collected by [[Felipe Otero]] near Tasquillo, Hidalgo. It also reports a field expedition by [[Norman H. Boke]] and University of Oklahoma students to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in Coahuila, and an account of cactus diversity in Colombia. The issue includes society news, membership information and an index. The aquatic content is limited to incidental observations of the basin's wetlands and fauna.

**Echeveria minima sp. nov.** Collected in March 1967 by Felipe Otero in a small ravine on the Río Tula near the Tasquillo bridge, Hidalgo. The type specimen was cultivated in the author's collection (no. 3060) and deposited in the Herbario Nacional, Instituto de Biología, UNAM (no. 110023). Key diagnostic traits:
- Solitary rosette, 20–40 mm in diameter, with 30–50 leaves (Latin diagnosis: up to 35 mm).
- Leaves 8–20 mm long, 5–9 mm wide, glaucous green with reddish mucro and margins.
- Inflorescence a solitary cyme of 3–7 (–9) flowers, May–July.
- Corolla 8–11 mm long, yellow with a pink to reddish base.

The author places it in series *Secundae* and considers it distinct from [[Echeveria Secunda]], which it resembles but is smaller and has thicker, narrower leaves. Dr. Reid Moran reviewed the description.

**Expedition to Cuatro Ciénegas (Boke, 1967).** A University of Oklahoma group left on 24 March 1967 for a 10-day trip, with five students. The basin is roughly 50 km east–west and 30 km north–south, at about 650 m elevation, bounded by desert ranges and split by the [[Sierra De San Marcos]]. Its fauna is unusual, including fish, turtles and snails found nowhere else; the author notes three snail families endemic to the basin, according to Dr. L. E. Cullum.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cuatro Ciénegas Basin:** a desert basin with marshes, dry lake beds, springs, lagoons and streams. Mineral content is generally high, with saline and salt-accumulating areas similar to those of the American West. Pools are often calcareous, and the author mentions [[Laguna Mezquite]] (hard calcareous bottom, water lilies and bladderworts) and a muddy lagoon that was less suitable for swimming.
- **Flora and fauna:** dominant cacti included [[Grusonia Bradtiana]] (abundant, prostrate habit, locally called "alicoche"), [[Echinocereus]], [[Thelocactus]] and [[Epithelantha]]. Associated plants were creosote bush (gobernadora), mesquite, ocotillo and a spiny Cassia. Peyote grew under shrubs at the foot of hills. The author suggests the area was largely unexplored botanically.
- **Notable plants observed:** [[Astrophytum Capricorne]] var. *nivea* in a canyon of the Sierra del Anteojo; [[Hamatocactus Hamatacanthus]] in the same area; [[Selaginella Lepidophylla]] fully expanded after recent rain on the Sierra de San Marcos; and [[Thelocactus Bicolor]] in flower near Saltillo.
- **Seed predation:** many dry fruits of Grusonia were found with no seeds, suggesting an unidentified animal consumes them.
- **Colombia:** cacti were found at about 4,200 ft (about 50 miles from Bogotá), including *Acanthocereus colombianus*, *Nyctocereus*, *Hylocereus*, *Selenicereus* and *Melocactus obtusipetalus*. A *Melocactus* type-locality dispute is raised with Dr. Martín Cárdenas's *M. loboguerreroi*.

## Key Findings

1. **New species:** *Echeveria minima* is described as distinct from *E. secunda*. Its rosettes are much smaller, and its leaves are thicker and narrower relative to length. The author notes that in *E. secunda*, rosettes measure 3–16 cm and have 30–55 leaves, while *E. minima* rosettes are at most about 3 cm in the wild.
2. **Taxonomic note:** the author argues that *E. minima* is not merely a dwarf form of *E. secunda*, and that *E. secunda* var. *elatior* is also treated by some authors as a variety.
3. **Under-explored region:** the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin is considered botanically under-collected, with probable new species of cacti and other families awaiting description.
4. **Conservation concern:** Grusonia bradtiana was previously thought rare and near extinction, but was found abundant in the basin. The author notes seeds were scarce during the visit.
5. **Colombian diversity:** the brief survey suggests high cactus and succulent richness in Colombia, and calls for further exploration.
6. **Society outlook:** the society promotes scientific study of cacti and reports active regional meetings and ongoing publications.
