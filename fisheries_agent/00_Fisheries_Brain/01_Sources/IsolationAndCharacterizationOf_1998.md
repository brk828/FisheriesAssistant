## Executive Summary

This primer note describes the isolation and characterization of microsatellite DNA loci from the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), developed to assess neutral genetic variation across four isolated Arizona watershed populations. Ten microsatellite primer sets were successfully developed and screened in topminnows, five of which proved polymorphic. Cross-species utility testing showed five of these primer sets also amplified in the [[Guppy]] (*Poecilia reticulata*), a related poeciliid, with three loci polymorphic in that species. The study found generally lower heterozygosity in Gila topminnows compared to guppies, consistent with prior findings of low genetic diversity in this species based on mitochondrial DNA and allozyme studies. These markers provide tools for population genetic monitoring of an endangered fish and for paternity analysis in guppies.

## Background & Description

- The Gila topminnow is described as a **small, live-bearing fish** (family [[Poeciliidae]]), consistent with its viviparous reproductive mode typical of the genus *Poeciliopsis*.
- No detailed external morphometric or meristic data are provided in this molecular primer note; the focus is entirely on genetic (microsatellite DNA) characterization rather than gross morphology.
- Microsatellite loci characterized include repeat motifs such as (GT)n, (AG)n, (AC)n, and complex compound repeats (e.g., Pooc-G49: (GT)11GC2(GT)41GC2(GT)2; Pooc-JJ42: complex (TG)/(CA)/(GT) compound repeat).
- Ten functional primer pairs were derived from an initial screen of ~3100 clones, of which 18 were positive for (GA)n/(CA)n repeat probes; 14 were sequenced successfully, with four failing to amplify.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The [[Gila Topminnow]] inhabits **four isolated, fragmented watersheds** in Arizona: **Bylas Spring, Monkey Spring, Sharp Spring, and Cienega Creek**, all within the historic [[Gila River]] drainage.
- Historically, it was **the most abundant fish** in the Gila River drainage, but populations have severely declined due to:
  - **Habitat destruction and/or fragmentation**
  - **Introduction of the non-native [[Western Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*), a congener that competitively displaces topminnows (Meffe et al. 1983).
- The species is currently classified as **endangered**, with genetic isolation among the four remaining populations being a central conservation concern.
- The comparative species, the [[Guppy]] (*Poecilia reticulata*), is a related poeciliid used here as a cross-species test case for marker transferability and is not native to the same desert ecosystem but shares family-level genetic architecture allowing primer cross-amplification.

## Key Findings

- **Marker development**: From ~3100 screened clones, 16 microsatellite-positive clones were sequenced; 14 yielded usable primers, and 10 primer sets successfully amplified topminnow genomic DNA (Table 1).
- **Polymorphism in topminnows**: Of 10 loci tested across 160 individuals (20 initial + 140 additional, 35/population), **5 loci were polymorphic** (Pooc-G49, Pooc-C15, Pooc-O56, Pooc-LL53, Pooc-6-10), with total allele counts per locus ranging from **2 to 15**.
- **Low genetic diversity confirmed**: 
  - Bylas Spring population was **monomorphic at 4 of 5 polymorphic loci**.
  - **Pooc-6-10** showed no within-population heterozygosity — allelic variation existed only *between* populations (Bylas, Sharp, and Cienega fixed for one allele; Monkey Spring fixed for the alternate allele), suggesting strong population-level genetic structuring/isolation.
  - Observed heterozygosity values for topminnow loci ranged from 0.00 to 0.50 (Table 1), generally low, consistent with earlier reports of minimal variation via mtDNA restriction analysis ([[Quattro et al. 1996]]) and allozymes ([[Vrijenhoek et al. 1985]]).
- **Cross-species amplification in guppies**: Of the 10 primer sets, **5 failed to amplify** in *Poecilia reticulata*; of the remaining 5, **3 were polymorphic** (Pooc-G49, Pooc-C15, Pooc-G10), with allele numbers ranging from **2 to 5** and observed heterozygosity from 0.00 to 0.60 (Table 2).
- **Notable reversal in polymorphism**: The locus **Pooc-G10**, monomorphic in topminnows (1 allele), was the **most polymorphic locus in guppies** (5 alleles), highlighting species-specific differences in microsatellite evolution despite locus conservation.
- **Overall comparative diversity**: Heterozygosity was generally **lower in Gila topminnows than in guppies** for shared loci, even though topminnow sampling was more extensive both in sample size and geographic spread — reinforcing conclusions of historically reduced genetic diversity in this endangered species, potentially linked to population bottlenecks, fragmentation, and small effective population sizes.
- **Applied utility**: These microsatellite markers have proven useful for **paternity determination** in guppy family studies, demonstrating practical applications beyond the original target species.
- Complements prior work on **[[MHC Class II Gene]]** variation in Gila topminnow, which identified nine alleles across the four populations (Hedrick & Parker, unpublished), providing a broader genetic context alongside these neutral microsatellite markers.
