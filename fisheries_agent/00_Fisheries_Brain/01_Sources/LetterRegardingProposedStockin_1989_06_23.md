# Letter Regarding Proposed Stocking of Rainbow Smelt in Lake Powell

#ichthyology #marine-biology #osmeridae #freshwater-ecology #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary
This 1989 interagency letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) addresses a proposal by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources to stock nonnative **[[Rainbow Smelt]]** into **[[Lake Powell]]**, a reservoir within the **[[Upper Colorado River Basin]]**. The letter is a procedural and regulatory intervention, invoking the **[[Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin]]** (Recovery Program) to require formal consultation before the Colorado River Fish and Wildlife Council votes on the stocking proposal. The core concern is the potential ecological conflict between introducing a nonnative forage fish and the conservation of endangered native fish species in the basin.

## Background & Description
- **Regulatory Framework**: In January 1988, a Cooperative Agreement was signed by the Governors of Colorado, Wyoming, and Utah, the Secretary of the Interior, and the Administrator of the Western Area Power Administration, formally establishing the Recovery Program and an oversight **[[Implementation Committee]]**.
- **Key Provision (Section 4.4.3(a))**: The Recovery Program explicitly restricts stocking of nonnative fish species to areas where there is demonstrable absence of conflict with rare or endangered species. This applies to:
  - Augmentation of existing nonsalmonid fish populations.
  - Introduction of new populations of any nonnative fish species that could compete with or prey upon rare/endangered species.
- **Species in Question**: **[[Rainbow Smelt]]** (*Osmerus mordax*), a small pelagic forage fish in the family **[[Osmeridae]]**, proposed for introduction into Lake Powell by the **[[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]]**.
- **Procedural Timeline**: The stocking proposal was scheduled for a vote by the Colorado River Fish and Wildlife Council at its July 2–3, 1989 meeting, prompting this letter as a request for prior consultation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Location**: Lake Powell, a major reservoir on the Colorado River, situated within the Upper Colorado River Basin — a critical habitat region for multiple federally endangered fish species (e.g., **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]**, **[[Humpback Chub]]**, **[[Razorback Sucker]]**, **[[Bonytail Chub]]**, though not explicitly named in this letter).
- **Ecological Concern**: Rainbow Smelt, as a nonnative species, poses potential risks through:
  - **Competition** with native/endangered fish species for food resources or habitat.
  - **Predation** risk, particularly on larval or juvenile life stages of endangered native fishes.
- **Institutional Context**: The concern was raised at meetings of the **Technical Group** and **Management Group** of the Implementation Committee, indicating that this issue was flagged by fisheries management professionals as a potential threat to basin-wide endangered species recovery efforts prior to any formal ecological consultation.

## Key Findings
- **No formal consultation had occurred** between the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources and either the Fish and Wildlife Service or the Implementation Committee regarding the ecological effects of stocking Rainbow Smelt on endangered fishes, despite this being a requirement under the Recovery Program agreement.
- The **Management Group formally requested** that the Colorado River Fish and Wildlife Council consult with the Service and hear from other interested/affected parties **before** adopting a formal position or approving the stocking proposal.
- This letter functions as a **regulatory checkpoint**, demonstrating the enforcement mechanism of Section 4.4.3(a) of the Recovery Program: nonnative species introductions in the Upper Basin require documented review to resolve potential conflicts with endangered species conservation goals.
- Distribution of the letter to state wildlife directors across **Colorado**, **Wyoming**, **Utah**, **California**, **New Mexico**, and **Nevada** indicates the multi-state jurisdictional stakes of nonnative species introductions into shared basin waters, even though the immediate stocking site (Lake Powell) is a single-state managed reservoir.
- No direct biological or experimental data (e.g., population studies, predation rates, competition indices) on Rainbow Smelt impacts are presented in this letter — it is purely a procedural/policy document flagging the need for such an ecological review.
