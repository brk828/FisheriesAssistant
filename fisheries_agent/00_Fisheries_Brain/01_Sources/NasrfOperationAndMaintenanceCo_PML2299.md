# Colorado River Recovery Program FY 2005 Annual Project Report: NASRF Operation and Maintenance

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This FY 2005 annual project report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers operation and maintenance of the J. W. Mumma [[Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility]] (NASRF) in Alamosa, Colorado. The facility rears [[Bonytail]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] for stocking in the upper Colorado River Basin, including the San Juan, Gunnison, Green, and Colorado Rivers. The project is ongoing and is filed under the [[Recovery Goals and Action Plan]] for managing genetic integrity and augmenting or restoring populations. The report was signed by Principal Investigator [[K. David Schnoor]] on 30 October 2005. It describes fry intake, inventory, stocking, pond losses, a budget shortfall, and expenditures totaling $113,224.51.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** NASRF operation and maintenance, project number 29d, implemented by the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]].
- **Principal investigators:** K. David Schnoor and [[Jenn Logan]], both at the Colorado Division of Wildlife office in Alamosa, CO.
- **Recovery program linkage:** The project supports the RIPRAP action plan, specifically item IV.C (operate and maintain facilities) and IV.C.4 (Mumma facility). It also supports genetic management and population augmentation.
- **Study schedule:** Ongoing.
- **Species managed:**
  - [[Bonytail]] (BYT), a native cyprinid of the upper Colorado River Basin.
  - [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (CPM), a large native cyprinid of the same basin.
  - [[Razorback Sucker]], present only as a small remnant holding group (2001 year class).
- **Facilities:**
  - NASRF tanks and ponds (Ponds 3 and 4).
  - [[Trinidad State Junior College]] (TSJC) ponds 1, 2, and 3, leased by the program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fry source:** Bonytail and Colorado Pikeminnow fry came from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery in New Mexico ([[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]).
- **Disease:** No apparent disease problems were observed at the TSJC ponds in 2004 or 2005. Tilapia were thought to be the main vector of pathogens in 2003, so efforts were made to exclude them from the ponds.
- **Predation:** Bird predation is a recurring threat at the ponds. Netting purchased last year was to be reinstalled over the TSJC ponds to reduce it. Predation was also a cause of fish loss.
- **Stocking loss factors:** Losses during the year came from fish movement, lethal sampling for health inspection, and predation.
- **Stocking destinations:**
  - [[San Juan River]]: Colorado Pikeminnow.
  - [[Green River]] and the Colorado River from [[Palisade, Colorado]] to Loma: Bonytail.

## Key Findings

**Fry intake and inventory**

- **Bonytail:**
  - About 10,000 BYT fry were received from Dexter, NM.
  - No BYT fry were received from Dexter in 2005, so the 2003 and 2004 year classes were used to cover the 2005 stocking schedule.
  - Current inventory is about 1,000 BYT from the 2003 year class and about 5,000 BYT from the 2004 year class at NASRF.
  - Approximately 6,000 BYT are held in TSJC ponds, where they are planned to overwinter.
- **Colorado Pikeminnow:**
  - About 10,000 CPM fry were received from Dexter, NM, in 2004.
  - No CPM fry were received in 2005. Survival from the 2003 and 2004 year classes was adequate for the 2005 schedule.
  - Current inventory is about 1,100 CPM from the 2003 year class and about 2,900 CPM from the 2004 year class.

**Stocking in FY 2004–2005**

- 1,493 CPM from the 2003 year class were stocked into the San Juan River.
- 2,680 BYT were stocked into the Green River and the Colorado River from Palisade to Loma.
- The report's stocking summary table lists totals of 5,147 and 1,493. Column alignment in the scanned table is unclear, so these figures should be checked against the source.

**Remaining fish and tank inventory (as of 30 September 2005)**

- Razorback Sucker (2001 year class): 30 and 5 fish listed in the remaining-fish table.
- Pond and tank holdings are summarized in the source table. Entries include NASRF tanks (4,000 and 2,900 fish), NASRF Ponds 3 and 4 (4,200 and 5,000), and TSJC Ponds 1–3 (6,000). Sizes are given in fish per pound.

**Recommendations**

- Continue culturing Bonytail and Colorado Pikeminnow at NASRF to meet the stocking needs of the Colorado River Recovery Program.
- PIT-tag Bonytail before stocking, for monitoring during grow-out and after release.

**Budget status (FY 2005)**

- The project expected about $23,000 from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BoR). Only $17,000 was to be transferred. The remainder was to come from Colorado's contribution to the recovery program.
- As of the report date, the $17,000 had not been received and the Colorado contribution had not been reimbursed.
- Total expenditures were $113,224.51. The largest categories were:
  - Labor: $52,800.00
  - TSJC lease: $19,100.00
  - Utilities: $13,875.42
  - Stocking vehicle use and maintenance: $7,232.42
  - Equipment maintenance and repair: $6,552.26
- Other categories included feed ($3,753.56), travel and training ($965.07), building and grounds ($3,042.23), discharge chlorine ($3,960.00), TSJC pond improvements ($422.48), and miscellaneous equipment and rental ($1,521.07).

**Project status**

- Fish culture is ongoing. Data submission status was not reported.
