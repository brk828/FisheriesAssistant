## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary field/laboratory data paper but a formal **critique letter** from the Environmental Quality Committee of the [[American Society Of Ichthyologists And Herpetologists]] (ASIH), addressed to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] regarding the draft *Procedures for Stocking Nonnative Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin*. The letter challenges the scientific and procedural basis for continuing to stock nonnative predatory fishes (salmonids and warm-water sport fishes) in a river system harboring threatened and endangered native species. Key objections include: undefined terminology ("stocking" vs. "restocking" vs. "introduction"), reliance on containment barriers considered unreliable against flood events, misapplication of the [[AFS Position Statement On The Introduction Of Aquatic Species]], and an apparent institutional bias favoring recreational sport-fishing interests over native species protection in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or taxonomic descriptive data are presented in this source. The document is a policy commentary rather than a species-level scientific study; it references broad functional/ecological categories of fish (**salmonids**, **warm-water sport fishes**, unspecified **native threatened/endangered fishes**) without providing anatomical, coloration, or identification detail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System**: The [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] is described as an interstate river system subject to cooperative but jurisdictionally complex management between the USFWS and state wildlife agencies.
- **Trophic role of nonnatives**: Both stocked [[Salmonidae|salmonids]] and warm-water sport fishes are explicitly characterized as **predators**, occupying a niche that places them in direct predatory or competitive contact with native threatened/endangered fish populations.
- **Barrier ecology**: Nonnative populations are physically separated from native/endangered fish habitats by "generalized artificial barriers," but the author asserts these barriers **will be breached** via floods, natural disasters, or human-mediated movement — undermining claims of containment safety.
- **Flood-event containment standards**: A handwritten annotation on the original draft indicated that a **100-year flood event** was deemed insufficient containment, while a **20–40 year flood event** was considered acceptable — a standard the author calls into question given recent major flooding in the western United States.
- **Synergistic habitat degradation**: The USFWS draft document attributes native fish decline to a combination of factors — nonnative fish introductions **plus** habitat modification/alteration — implying synergistic negative effects rather than attributing decline to introductions alone. The letter requests documentation/citations substantiating this multi-causal claim.

## Key Findings

- The ASIH Environmental Quality Committee was **not properly consulted** during the draft review period, receiving notice with less than 24 hours before the public comment deadline (fax received 25 Jan, deadline 26 Jan 1994 — noted as an inconsistency in the review timeline).
- **Table 1** of the original PROCEDURES document lists nonnative species eligible for introduction/restocking; the letter criticizes this as circular logic — i.e., species desired by managers are simply designated "protected" under the Procedures, without ecological justification tied to native species recovery.
- The document's stated goal (protecting/restoring threatened and endangered native fishes) is seen as **inconsistent** with continued authorization of nonnative predator stocking (salmonids, warm-water sport fish), since no data are cited showing these introductions have *not* harmed protected native species.
- The closing clause of the original Procedures — allowing any signatory party to request review/update "at any time" — is criticized as creating a loophole that could allow the entire framework to be abandoned or weakened without a defined evaluation period.
- The letter argues that the [[AFS Position Statement On The Introduction Of Aquatic Species]] was **misapplied**: reliance on "existing research and literature" was deemed invalid because such research originated **outside** the Upper Colorado Basin, in different waters/regions, and did not involve the specific threatened/endangered species of concern.
- Objection is raised to a provision allowing expedited/non-public "timely review" of proposed introductions, bypassing standard peer publication/review channels (e.g., in *Fisheries* journal), which the author frames as an ethical breach of professional introduction-review norms.
- The author recommends that management objectives should aim to **reduce**, not maintain or increase, the number of nonnative predatory fish species in the basin if native species protection is genuinely the priority.
- Overall conclusion: the Procedures document, as drafted, appears to favor state-level recreational fisheries management interests over rigorous, basin-specific scientific support for continued nonnative fish stocking near [[Threatened And Endangered Fish Species|threatened and endangered native fish]] habitats.
