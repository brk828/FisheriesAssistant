> **Note on scope:** This source paper does **not** contain fish science data. It is a botanical/ecological survey of the persistent perennial vegetation (trees, shrubs, cacti) on the rim of Montezuma Well, a limestone sinkhole in Arizona that also hosts a well-studied aquatic ecosystem (referenced via [[Cole 1963 Limnology|Cole (1963)]]). The note below faithfully reframes the extracted data around the actual taxon studied — desert perennial vegetation — rather than forcing a fish-centric structure. Where the source touches on the aquatic feature itself ([[Montezuma Well]]), that context is preserved for cross-linking to related limnological/ichthyological literature.

## Executive Summary
Clark & Burgess (1966) quantitatively surveyed the persistent perennial vegetation (trees, shrubs, and cacti — explicitly excluding ephemeral annuals and bulbous herbaceous perennials) on the rim of [[Montezuma Well]], a ~470-foot-diameter natural limestone sink in [[Montezuma Castle National Monument]], Yavapai County, Arizona. Using the line-intercept method (Canfield 1941) along 6,000 feet of transects across north, west, and south exposures (east excluded due to a narrow rim), the authors identified 23 vascular plant species, calculated Relative Frequency, Relative Cover, and a derived "Importance Index" (Frequency × Cover) for each species and exposure. The study is presented as a companion terrestrial-ecology complement to the extensive limnological work on the Well's aquatic pool summarized by [[Gerald Cole]] (1963), which is the primary source of any fish/aquatic fauna data for this locality — not the present paper.

## Background & Description
Not applicable to fish; the paper addresses plant growth form categories rather than fish morphology. Vegetation was classified by growth habit:
- **Trees (3 species):** *Prosopis juliflora* (mesquite), *Juniperus monosperma*, *J. osteosperma*
- **Shrubs (15 species):** e.g., *[[Gutierrezia sarothrae]]* (snakeweed), *[[Parthenium incanum]]*, *Acacia greggii*, *Dalea formosa*, *Larrea tridentata* (creosote), *Sphaeralcea* spp., *Krameria parvifolia*, *Atriplex canescens*, *Aloysia wrightii*, *Yucca elata*, *Encelia frutescens*, *Ephedra viridis*, *Berberis haematocarpa*, *Cowania mexicana*, *Sapindus saponaria*
- **Cacti (4 species):** *Opuntia engelmannii*, *O. leptocaulis*, *O. macrocentra*, *Echinocereus boyce-thompsoni*
- **Additional record (plot-only, not transect):** *Mammillaria arizonica* — new record for the Monument.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat structure:** The rim of [[Montezuma Well]] is a limestone bedrock high; slopes gently away on north, west, and south sides, with an abrupt escarpment on the narrow east side (excluded from sampling). The Well itself is an artesian-fed sinkhole (~80 ft deep walls, ~55 ft deep pool, ~1,000 gal/min inflow) draining via a solution cavern toward Beaver Creek — a physically distinct aquatic habitat referenced but not sampled biologically in this study.
- **Community composition:** Desert shrub community overwhelmingly dominant across all three exposures — shrubs contribute ~87% of relative frequency, ~90% of cover, and ~99% of the combined Importance Index (Table 4).
- **Dominant species (whole rim):** *Gutierrezia sarothrae* (Importance Index 50.17) and *Parthenium incanum* (41.24), followed by *Acacia greggii*, *Dalea formosa*, and *Larrea tridentata* — the five dominant species.
- **Exposure-driven gradient:** A north-to-south inversion in relative dominance between *Gutierrezia sarothrae* and *Parthenium incanum* suggests *Parthenium* is more warm-adapted (consistent with its southern biogeographic range into Chihuahua, Coahuila, Sonora, and trans-Pecos Texas), while *Gutierrezia* ranges more broadly through the Great Basin and northern Great Plains.
- **Tree distribution niche partitioning:** *Juniperus* spp. (more mesic) restricted to north/west rim; *Prosopis juliflora* (more xerophytic mesquite) occurs only as scattered individuals on the south-facing slope — interpreted as species replacement along a microclimatic moisture/temperature gradient.
- **Floristic richness gradient:** West rim shows greatest richness (15 species) due to unique occurrences of *Encelia*, *Ephedra*, *Aloysia*, *Sapindus*, and *Larrea tridentata*, the latter forming a distinct patch extending westward from the rim (Fig. 5).
- **Protection history:** Site protected from grazing since 1947, permitting vegetation recovery — relevant to interpreting current community structure as a successional/recovery state rather than a pristine baseline.

## Key Findings
- 23 total vascular plant species recorded; 22 quantified via line-intercept (3 gymnosperms, 1 monocot, 18 dicots [5 legumes, 4 cacti, 3 composites]).
- Two new records for Montezuma Castle National Monument: *Mammillaria arizonica* and *Opuntia leptocaulis* ("Christmas Cactus").
- North rim: 13 species (2 trees, 8 shrubs, 3 cacti); >99% of Importance Index from shrubs; ~97% from just four species (*Gutierrezia sarothrae*, *Parthenium incanum*, *Acacia greggii*, *Dalea formosa*).
- West rim: 15 species (1 tree, 10 shrubs, 4 cacti); *Gutierrezia* and *Parthenium* nearly co-dominant.
- South rim: 12 species (1 tree, 7 shrubs, 4 cacti); *Parthenium incanum* Importance Index (53.59) exceeds *Gutierrezia sarothrae* (38.24) by ~1.4×.
- Combined rim totals (Table 4): Grand total Importance Index ≈ 100%, with shrubs = 98.76%, cacti = 0.987%, trees = 0.244%.
- Taxonomic ambiguities noted: difficulty distinguishing *Opuntia engelmannii* from *O. macrocentra* in the field; potential conflation of *Gutierrezia lucida* (few-flowered, Fig. 4) with *G. sarothrae*; four *Sphaeralcea* species lumped generically due to vegetative-stage indistinguishability.
- Methodological note: "Importance Index" defined as the multiplicative product (not sum) of Relative Frequency and Relative Cover, on the rationale that a species with double the frequency and double the cover of another should be more than twice as ecologically important — though the authors flag this as a debatable assumption.
- Cross-reference: Aquatic/limnological characterization of the Well's pool itself (potentially including fish and aquatic invertebrate fauna) is attributed to [[Gerald Cole]] (1963) and is **not** covered in this vegetation-focused paper — researchers seeking [[Montezuma Well]] fish or aquatic invertebrate data should consult that separate limnological source.
