# Review Correspondence on Rosenfeld's Draft Reports on Gila

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #gila #taxonomy #morphometrics

## Executive Summary

This document is a critical peer-review letter authored by W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology), addressed to Randy D. Radant of the Utah Department of Wildlife Resources, along with attached technical comments from Dr. Michael Douglas. The correspondence evaluates two draft reports by Mark Rosenfeld concerning the taxonomy and identification of [[Gila]] fishes (Cyprinidae) using morphometric and electrophoretic methodologies. Both reviewers (Minckley and Douglas) conclude that the reports are **premature for formal review or distribution**, citing significant unresolved analytical deficiencies — most critically, a failure to correct Principal Components Analysis (PCA) data for body size, incomplete electrophoretic enzyme assays, and missing samples (e.g., [[Little Colorado River]] fish not yet incorporated). The letter serves as a methodological critique rather than a presentation of finalized scientific findings on Gila systematics.

## Background & Description

While the letter does not present finalized morphological data (this is explicitly the central criticism), it references several morphological variables under investigation in the Rosenfeld report:

- **Gillrakers**: The draft report includes an "ad hoc" argument for natural selection acting on gillraker morphology in *Gila*, which Douglas criticizes as premature, poorly constructed, and internally contradictory (arguing both "pro" and "against" selection within the same section).
- **Sexual dimorphism**: Identified as a confounding variable, alongside body size, that has not yet been adequately addressed in the morphometric analysis.
- **Body size (isometric scaling)**: A major unresolved issue — PCA data presented in the draft were **not corrected for overall body size**, rendering results "entirely inadequate" and "uninterpretable" per Douglas. Techniques to correct for allometric/isometric scaling effects are noted as long-established in the literature but were not applied.
- **Comparative literature omission**: The draft's argument for "narrow morphological criteria" in these fishes is criticized for omitting Suttkus and Clemmer's extensive morphological dataset on **[[Gila Cypha]]** (humpback chub) from the [[Grand Canyon]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Taxa involved**: The genus *[[Gila]]* (Cyprinidae), including at least [[Gila Cypha]] (humpback chub), within the context of broader Colorado River basin chub taxonomy.
- **Habitat/Geographic scope**: Specimens and data referenced include populations from the **[[Little Colorado River]]** and the **[[Grand Canyon]]** region of the [[Colorado River]] basin — indicating the taxonomic study is centered on differentiating morphologically similar chub species/populations in this inland freshwater drainage.
- **Conservation/taxonomic context**: The overarching goal of the reports under review is to resolve questions of **taxonomy and species identification** within *Gila*, a genus of conservation concern in the Colorado River system, using combined morphometric and electrophoretic (allozyme) approaches.

## Key Findings

- **Analytical incompleteness**: Both Minckley and Douglas judged the draft reports unsuitable for formal review/distribution because major data analyses were incomplete:
  - PCA had not been factored for body size effects.
  - Electrophoretic data set incomplete — not all enzymes assayed across all samples.
  - [[Little Colorado River]] fish samples had not yet been incorporated into the analysis.
- **Methodological validity affirmed in principle**: Minckley states that the general methodologies (morphometrics, electrophoresis, multivariate statistics such as PCA/UPGMA) are valid approaches for resolving *Gila* taxonomy questions — the criticism is of execution, not of method choice.
- **Statistical/technical critiques (Douglas)**:
  - Ambiguous or incorrect terminology; unclear meaning of "isometric size factoring"; unclear whether Discriminant Function Analysis (DFA) was actually performed — no results or discriminant function tables were shown despite claims.
  - Misuse of the term "significant" without statistical support.
  - Conclusions (p. 14) deemed unwarranted given inadequate analysis.
  - Confusing use of the term "teleological" applied to an analysis (p. 15).
  - Repeated spelling errors cited as evidence of a broader lack of attention to detail.
- **Concerns about premature conclusions**: Minckley explicitly states alarm that conclusions about relationships among *Gila* taxa were presented despite the investigator's own admission that a large portion of the evidence remained unanalyzed — warning that such conclusions "may (or may not) prove true" once all data are fully processed.
- **Procedural recommendation**: External review and substantive commentary (including detailed feedback promised by Minckley) should be reserved until final, complete drafts are available. Donald Buth was tasked with sending separate comments specifically on the electrophoretic study directly to Radant.
- **Overall assessment**: Douglas summarizes the morphometric report as reflecting "poor planning," an "inadequate grasp of methodologies," and constituting "analysis by innuendo" — a strong critique given the taxonomic and conservation importance of correctly resolving *Gila* species identities.
