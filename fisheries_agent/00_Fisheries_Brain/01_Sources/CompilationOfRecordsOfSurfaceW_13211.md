# Gila River Basin Surface Water Records (USGS Water-Supply Paper 1313, Part 9)

#hydrology #surface-water #streamflow-records #gila-river-basin

## Executive Summary

This document is a compilation of surface-water records for the [[Gila River Basin]], part of the [[Colorado River Basin]] in southwestern New Mexico and Arizona. It was prepared under the direction of J. V. B. Wells, Chief, Surface Water Branch, for the [[United States Geological Survey]] as [[Water-Supply Paper 1313]], covering records through September 1950. The text covers gaging stations, streamflow statistics, irrigation diversions, and reservoir contents. It contains no fish, invertebrate, or other aquatic biological data. The source is a scanned, OCR-processed reference table set, so many numeric values are partly garbled and should be verified against the original publication before use.

## Background & Description

- **Publisher and series:** U.S. Geological Survey, Water-Supply Paper 1313, Part 9, Colorado River Basin. Printed by the United States Government Printing Office, Washington, 1954.
- **Scope:** Station descriptions (location, drainage area, gage datum, period of record) and tabulated monthly, yearly, and calendar-year discharge, runoff, and diversion data.
- **Principal stations and reaches described:**
  - [[Gila River]] near Silver City, N. Mex. (drainage area not clearly legible in scan)
  - Gila River near Gila, N. Mex.
  - Gila River near Cliff, N. Mex. (average discharge 128 cfs, 1942–50)
  - Gila River near Red Rock, N. Mex. (average discharge 205 cfs; 41 years of record)
  - Gila River near Virden and Duncan, Ariz. (see [[Duncan Valley]])
  - [[San Francisco River]] near Glenwood, N. Mex. (drainage area 1,660 sq mi; average 67.5 cfs, 23 years, 1927–50)
  - San Francisco River at Clifton, Ariz. (drainage area 2,790 sq mi; average 195 cfs, 26 years)
  - [[San Pedro River]] at Charleston, Palominas, Fairbank, and Redington, Ariz.
  - [[San Carlos Reservoir]] and [[Coolidge Dam]], Ariz.
  - [[Eagle Creek]] and [[Willow Creek]] near Morenci, Ariz.
  - [[Safford Valley]] canals (Brown, Tidwell, Fourness, San Jose, Montezuma, Graham, and others)
- **Diversion infrastructure:** Canals listed include Sunset Canal, Cosper-Windhan Canal, Moddle Canal, Shriver Canal, Valley Canal, Duncan Canal, Black & McClesky Canal, Colvin-Jones Canal, Oregon Canal, Union Canal, Curtis Canal, Fort Thomas Canal, and Dodge-Nevada Canal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The records describe the hydrologic setting of the Gila River system rather than its biota. Relevant environmental conditions reported in the text include:

- **Flow regime:** Many reaches have periods of zero flow. Examples include Sunset Canal ("no flow" for periods in each year) and Gila River near Solomon ("no flow for several periods"). Several streams show extreme floods, such as the Gila River near Red Rock maximum of 40,000 cfs (Sept. 29, 1941) and the San Francisco River at Clifton maximum of 24,100 cfs.
- **Irrigation and withdrawals:** Diversions for irrigation dominate the basin. Examples include about 19,000 acres above the Gila River near Solomon station and about 12,500 acres above the Gila River near Virden. Groundwater pumping for municipal, mining, and metallurgical use occurs at Clifton and Morenci.
- **Water-rights regulation:** The [[Gila River Decree]] took effect Jan. 1, 1936. Under it, canal water is regulated so that waste returned to the river is greatly reduced. Before 1936, considerable excess water was returned to the river below many stations.
- **Water transfers:** Water is pumped from Black River into the Eagle Creek basin, and that flow makes up most of the flow of Willow Creek and Eagle Creek near Morenci.
- **Reservoir storage:** [[San Carlos Reservoir]] fully regulates flow at Coolidge Dam, where storage began Nov. 15, 1928.

## Key Findings

- **No biological data:** The document contains no fish species, invertebrates, plankton, water chemistry values, or habitat surveys. Chemical analyses are noted only as published in other Geological Survey reports.
- **San Carlos Reservoir:**
  - Total usable capacity: 1,205,000 acre-ft, between elevations 2,382.63 ft and 2,823.0 ft.
  - Maximum contents: 819,200 acre-ft on Mar. 18, 1942 (elevation 2,501.62 ft).
  - Dead storage below 2,382.63 ft: 26,250 acre-ft (1928–36 capacity table).
- **Gila River near Coolidge Dam:** Maximum discharge since dam completion was 1,240 cfs on July 31, 1937. Flow is completely regulated after Nov. 15, 1928.
- **Gila River near Red Rock:** Average discharge 205 cfs over 41 years. The largest recorded peak was 40,000 cfs on Sept. 29, 1941.
- **San Francisco River near Glenwood:** Average 67.5 cfs (1927–50). The maximum was 7,800 cfs on Jan. 18, 1949, and the minimum daily flow was 3.0 cfs on June 18–19, 1950.
- **San Francisco River at Clifton:** Average 195 cfs over 26 years. Estimated peak of 90,000 cfs in a historic flood, and a maximum of 24,100 cfs (gage height 16.4 ft).
- **Eagle Creek near Double Circle Ranch:** Average 18.6 cfs (1944–50). Maximum 2,400 cfs on Sept. 11, 1944 and Jan. 13, 1949. Minimum 1.7 cfs on June 21, 1946.
- **Willow Creek near Point of Pines:** Average 11.2 cfs (revised). Maximum 1,390 cfs (revised) on July 10, 1946.
- **Eagle Creek above pumping plant:** Average 28.2 cfs (1944–52). Approximately 97 percent of pumpage to Clifton and Morenci comes from the Eagle Creek basin.
- **Irrigation diversions:** Stations are regularly affected by upstream diversions. Examples include about 2,450 acres (Moddle Canal), about 4,000 acres (Montezuma Canal), and about 2,000 acres (San Francisco River near Glenwood).
- **Data quality:** Many entries are marked as revised, estimated, or not previously published. Several values are unreadable after OCR and should be checked against the printed volume.
