# Bonytail Reintroduction in the Upper Colorado River Basin: FY2001 Annual Project Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-elegans

## Executive Summary

This FY2001 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers Project 25, the reintroduction of [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) into the Upper [[Colorado River Basin]]. The species is thought to be functionally extirpated from the basin. The project stocks hatchery-reared juveniles and monitors radio-tagged adults to define the habitat they use and whether it overlaps with that of [[Roundtail Chub]]. Principal investigators are J. Michael Hudson and Julie A. Jackson of the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR) Moab Field Station. Study years run from 1996 to a final year listed in the source as "20Al" (OCR-garbled, probably 2011). The project status is ongoing, and FY2001 spending met its budget in full.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), described in the source as functionally extirpated from the Upper Colorado River Basin.
- **Project goal:** Re-establish bonytail in the Upper Colorado River Basin by stocking juveniles and monitoring adults.
- **Program linkage:** Supports the RIFRAP general recovery framework, specifically genetic integrity management and implementation of the basinwide bonytail restoration plan.
- **Hatchery origin:** Fish were hatched at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and reared at the UDWR [[Wahweap Hatchery]] in Big Water, Utah.
- **Marking:** All stocked fish carried [[Coded Wire Tag]]s, unique to each lot. Codes can only be read from dead fish.
- **Sampling methods:** [[Electrofishing]] and seining, with [[Radio Telemetry]] for adults.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stocking sites:** Green River at Green River State Park (approximately RM 120.0) and the Colorado River at the Cisco boat ramp (RM 110.0).
- **Habitat use:** Bonytail were captured across varied habitats, including low-velocity and swift water, backwaters, areas around tributary mouths, flooded tributaries, and low-velocity shoreline areas.
- **Dispersal:** Most fish were caught within a few river miles of the stocking site soon after release. On the Green River, fish dispersed downstream to about one mile above the confluence with the Colorado River. One bonytail was collected on the Colorado River at approximately RM 21, roughly 90 to 100 miles below the stocking sites.
- **Size pattern:** Average total length was slightly higher for fish found farther downstream. The source suggests this reflects greater dispersal by larger stocked fish and/or recaptures from earlier years.
- **Species interaction:** A key question is habitat overlap with [[Roundtail Chub]], which the monitoring design is intended to address.
- **Co-sampling opportunity:** The [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] population estimate on the Green River allowed sampling of the full reach from the stocking site to the confluence, which had not been possible in earlier years.

## Key Findings

**Stocking (Objective 1: Reintroduction)**
- Two stockings were completed in April 2001:
  - Green River at Green River State Park: approximately 46,500 bonytail.
  - Colorado River at the Cisco boat ramp: approximately 27,968 bonytail.
- Cumulative totals to date: over 103,000 stocked in the Colorado River, over 109,000 in the Green River (table total 109,175), and 30,000 in the Yampa River.
- FY2001 stocking was slightly lower than FY2000 on the Colorado and Green rivers and doubled on the Yampa River.
- No fall stockings were made in any of the three rivers.
- Most fish were held in ponds for one year before stocking; some were held for two years.

**Monitoring of Previous Stockings (Objective 2, Task 1)**
- Most bonytail captured in 2001 appeared to come from the most recent stockings.
- Stocking date, location, and size at stocking cannot be determined without killing fish to read tags. The proportion of fish from earlier years is therefore unknown.
- Growth rates and movement patterns also require lethal sampling.

**Radio Telemetry and Habitat Overlap (Objective 3)**
- No radio telemetry was conducted in 2001.
- Results will be reported in the stocking evaluation report, with a draft due January 2002.

**Flow Training (Objective 4)**
- Work was conducted by Dr. Todd A. Crowl of [[Utah State University]], who is responsible for the annual and final reports.

**Recommendations**
- Continue producing bonytail under the approved annual stocking plans.
- The optimal stocking size and timing remain undetermined. Stocking "more than a few thousand fish per year" appears necessary to produce recaptures in later years.
- Current [[Coded Wire Tag]]s provide too little information for sound biological decisions. Approval for [[Lethal Sampling]] should be obtained, or [[PIT Tag]]s should be adopted, to evaluate stocking methods.

**Budget and Status**
- FY2001 budget: $57,000 budgeted and $57,000 expended or obligated (difference $0; 100% of FY2001 work completed).
- No Recovery Program funds were spent on publication charges.
- Data will be submitted with the final report, with a draft due January 2002.
- Project status: Ongoing.
