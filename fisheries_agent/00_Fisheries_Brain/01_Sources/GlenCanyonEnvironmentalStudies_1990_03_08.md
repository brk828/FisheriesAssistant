# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Technical Study Team Meeting - March 8, 1990

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae #Colorado-River-Basin

## Executive Summary

This document summarizes a technical planning meeting held to coordinate research supporting the Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement (EIS), under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program. While primarily an administrative/programmatic record, the notes contain substantive information on planned fisheries and aquatic ecosystem research, including studies on [[Rainbow Trout]] population dynamics below Glen Canyon Dam and conservation research on the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the Little Colorado River (LCR) and mainstem backwaters. The meeting also addressed proposed "research flows" (controlled dam releases, including constant 3,000 cfs and up to 31,500 cfs peak releases) designed to support sediment, water quality, and biological data collection, with explicit discussion of the potential effects of flow regimes on native and non-native fish populations at various life stages.

## Background & Description

The GCES program was established to generate scientific information needed for the Glen Canyon Dam EIS, with a target completion date of December 1991. The research plan was organized around four themes:
1. Effects of dam operations
2. Effects of recreation
3. Economic values
4. Mitigation efforts

Research issues for the first three themes were formulated as specific null hypotheses; mitigation research hypotheses were deferred pending the outcome of the public scoping process.

Multiple agencies contributed to the fisheries and aquatic research agenda:
- **[[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF)**: Lead agency for trout research and endangered fish monitoring in the LCR.
- **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS)**: Focused on food availability and habitat resource use for fish in tributaries and the main channel.
- **[[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BOR)**: Coordinating endangered fish population modeling, flood probability reduction, and long-term monitoring contracts.
- **[[U.S. Geological Survey]] (USGS)**: Leading sediment transport, geomorphology, and water quality research relevant to fish habitat (e.g., backwater and beach formation).
- **[[National Park Service]] (NPS)**: Conducting interdisciplinary research on sediment, recreation, and biota, including primary productivity studies relevant to the food base for fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Glen Canyon Dam's regulated flow regime affects the Colorado River ecosystem through the Grand Canyon, influencing:

- **Sediment transport and beach/backwater habitat formation** — critical for native fish rearing habitat. USGS proposed long-term (10-year) research on bed evolution in eddies linked to sediment transport, given the complexity of these processes.
- **Water quality** — A two-pronged synoptic study (October 1990 and May/June 1991) was proposed by USGS, AGF, and NPS to collect nutrient, water chemistry, sediment chemistry, biological, and benthic samples at 17 sites (including one in Lake Powell), aimed at developing a water quality model relevant to aquatic productivity and fish habitat.
- **Flow regime and native fish life stages** — Discussion emphasized that a steady flow period in late spring/early summer could benefit native fishes, with several participants noting that the *duration* of steady flow was a more critical factor than magnitude for supporting native fish life stages.
- **Research (experimental) flows** — Proposed controlled releases (including sustained 3,000 cfs flows and peak releases up to 31,500 cfs, later revised) were designed to enable physical and biological measurements of the effects of dam operations, including impacts to fish, recreation, and wildlife. The Park Service noted these flows fell within normal operating limits of the dam.
- **Endangered species conservation** — BOR sought to reduce flood probability to a 1-in-20-year recurrence level and pursued establishment of a second population of [[Humpback Chub]] as a conservation measure, alongside long-term population monitoring to be run partly by an outside contractor.

## Key Findings

- **[[Rainbow Trout]] Research (AGF)**: Because of AGF's management mandate, nearly all AGF-led research would focus on trout, including:
  - Stranding of trout due to flow fluctuations
  - Reproductive success
  - Age-growth studies
  - Behavioral studies
  - Long-term effects of flow regimes on trout population dynamics
  - Overall goal: determine the **carrying capacity for trout** in the regulated reach below Glen Canyon Dam.

- **Endangered Fish Research (AGF & BOR)**: AGF's endangered fish work would focus on the **[[Little Colorado River]] (LCR)** and mainstem **backwater habitats**, with the primary goal of establishing a long-term monitoring program for the [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fish.

- **FWS Research Priorities**: The Fish and Wildlife Service was evaluating which conservation measures were research-oriented and could be conducted in-house, with likely focus areas including:
  - Food availability for native and non-native fish
  - Habitat resource use in both tributaries and the main channel
  - Estimated 3–4 years required to complete these studies satisfactorily

- **Flow-Ecology Linkage**: A proposal to request a steady, late spring/early summer research flow generated substantial discussion regarding the needs of native fishes at various life stages, with consensus that the **length (duration) of steady flow** — rather than just the discharge magnitude — was a critical determinant of benefit to native fish populations.

- **BOR Endangered Species Program**: 
  - Reduction of flood probability to a **1-in-20-year** level (modeling led by Randy Peterson), to be calibrated by year's end.
  - Long-term monitoring of endangered fish populations, partly contracted to an outside entity to be selected by summer 1990.
  - Goal of establishing a **second population of Humpback Chub** as a redundancy/conservation measure.
  - A biological opinion was expected to be ready for inclusion in the EIS despite some studies extending beyond the EIS's 14-month timeframe.
  - Interagency tension arose regarding a BOR-contemplated contract for endangered fish research, with AGF and FWS raising concerns about research capabilities and agency mandates; a follow-up meeting among interested agencies was proposed to resolve these concerns.

- **GCES Coordination**: GCES was noted to be helping coordinate a **management plan for the [[Little Colorado River]]**, reflecting its importance as critical habitat for the Humpback Chub.

- **Research Flow Design Considerations**: Controlled experimental flows (e.g., Releases D, F, and G) were engineered with specific ramping rates, minimum/maximum discharge, and constant-flow periods (e.g., sustained 3,000 cfs) explicitly to allow researchers — including fisheries biologists — to measure physical and ecological changes (e.g., stranding, backwater habitat changes) resulting from dam operations.
