# Tubular and Glandular Organs in Chydorid Cladocerans

#ichthyology #marine-biology #chydoridae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 paper by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] of the Freshwater Biological Association describes internal excretory-like organs in the freshwater crustacean family Chydoridae (Cladocera). Using serial sections, Fryer documents two mutually exclusive organs: a long [[Tubular Organ]] in *Chydorus*, *Peracantha* and *Pleuroxus*, and a shorter [[Glandular Organ]] present in many other chydorid genera. Some genera, including *Eurycercus* and *Leydigia*, lack both. The function of these organs is unknown. Fryer proposes that they are homologous, with the tubular organ a specialized form of the glandular organ, and suggests the tubular organ may diagnose a close phyletic group.

## Background & Description

The study forms part of a wider investigation of the functional morphology and adaptive radiation of anomopod cladocerans in the Chydoridae, building on earlier work by Fryer (1963, 1968). Sectioning of many species revealed the organs in the thorax, associated with the alimentary canal. Earlier, [[Weigold]] (1910) had noticed the tubular organ only by staining living *Chydorus sphaericus* with neutral red. Weigold misidentified it as part of the rectal diverticulum and underestimated its length.

**Tubular organ:** A long, narrow, thin-walled, blind-ending tube lying freely in the haemocoele. It opens into the rectal region (directly in *Peracantha*, via the diverticulum in *Chydorus* and *Pleuroxus*). It runs forward, crosses the body beneath the gut, and ends blindly on the left side. Its posterior part has a thickened wall that stains red-purple.

**Glandular organ:** A shorter organ, continuous with the dorsal wall of the rectal diverticulum. Its posterior part has a thick wall made of about 20 contiguous vesicles, with a thinner wall lined by villiform cells. It contains a non-staining median strand whose nature is unknown.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper is a morphological and taxonomic study with no field ecology or water chemistry data. Its ecological relevance lies in the function of the organs, which remains unknown. Fryer notes that both organs lie in the haemocoele and open into the rectum, which suggests a possible role in [[Excretion]] or [[Salt Balance]]. The comparison with [[Malpighian Tubules]] of insects is considered but rejected, since insects have multiple tubules and do not unite with the gut in this posterior position. Staining with neutral red shows the tubular organ takes up dye, but the paper states this "tells us little" about function. The same applies to the antennal gland rudiments of daphnids and other cladocerans.

Taxonomically, the organs were used to examine relationships among genera. The taxonomic methods referred to include [[Head Pore Arrangement]], [[Trunk Limb Setation]] and [[Mandibular Suspension]], which together define four chydorid subfamilies (Frey, 1967).

## Key Findings

- **Tubular organ restricted to three genera:** It is present in all examined species of *Peracantha*, *Pleuroxus* and *Chydorus* (six species in total: *P. truncata*, *P. trigonellus*, *P. uncinatus*, *C. sphaericus*, *C. ovalis*, *C. piger*). It is absent from *Pseudochydorus globosus*, which was recently removed from *Chydorus*.
- **Glandular organ widespread:** Present in several species across at least seven, and probably more, chydorid genera. Confirmed species include *Acroperus harpae*, *Graptoleberis testudinaria*, *Alona guttata*, *Alonella excisa*, *Pseudochydorus globosus* and *Anchistropus emarginatus*. It is almost certainly present in *Monospilus dispar* and probably in *Rhynchotalona falcata*.
- **Incomplete cases:** *Disparalona rostrata* has a similar but shorter organ with less clearly vesicular walls. *Alonella exigua* (maximum length c. 0.32 mm) has a structure that gives rise to branched tubes, possibly ending in glands. This is only partly described.
- **Rudimentary structure in *Alona affinis*:** The rectal diverticulum has a thick, apparently glandular dorsal wall with slight segmentation. This may be a precursor or regressive rudiment of a glandular organ. Its congener *A. guttata* has a typical glandular organ.
- **Absence in two species:** Neither organ occurs in *Eurycercus lamellatus* or *Leydigia leydigii*. Both have a simple rectal diverticulum with no glandular tissue. Lilljeborg (1900) stated that *Leydigia* lacks a diverticulum, but Fryer found one in *L. leydigii*.
- **Homology proposed:** Fryer argues that the tubular and glandular organs are probably homologous, with the tubular organ the more specialized form. He cautions that specialization does not reliably indicate primitiveness.
- **Possible phylogenetic marker:** The tubular organ may prove diagnostic of the *Chydorus*, *Pleuroxus* and *Peracantha* group and an indicator of close affinity. It also helps separate *Alonella* from that complex, since *Alonella* lacks a tubular organ.
- **Function unresolved:** The function of both organs is unknown. Fryer suggests a possible link to excretion or salt balance but presents no evidence.
