# A New Abundance Estimate for Vaquitas: First Step for Recovery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #phocoenidae

## Executive Summary

This paper reports the first survey specifically designed to estimate [[Vaquita]] (*Phocoena sinus*) abundance across its full range. A [[Line-Transect Survey]] was run by three vessels in summer 1997 as a joint cruise of the [[Instituto Nacional de la Pesca]] and the [[NOAA National Marine Fisheries Service]]. The survey recorded 125 sighting groups and estimated a total population of 567 animals (95% CI 177 to 1,073). The authors confirm that the species is restricted to the northwestern [[Gulf of California]], that the [[Upper Gulf of California and Colorado River Delta Biosphere Reserve]] boundaries poorly match vaquita distribution, and that the shallow water north of San Felipe holds more animals than previously thought. The 1997 estimate is more than twice the 1993 estimate (224), but the two are not directly comparable, and the authors state the data do not show the population is increasing.

## Background & Description

The vaquita was described scientifically in 1958 from skeletal material (Norris and McFarland 1958). Its inconspicuous behavior, small size and turbid habitat made it hard to study. Pre-survey "guesstimates" ranged from 50 to 1,000 animals. The 1993 survey, run as part of a multi-species cruise, produced 224 animals (95% CI 106 to 470) but did not cover all potential habitat and relied on a [[Detection Probability Function]] parameter g(0) borrowed from [[Harbor Porpoise]] studies.

The species is rated "critically endangered" by the [[IUCN]] and listed under [[CITES]], and is listed as endangered by Mexico. Gillnet bycatch in artisanal fisheries is the main recognized threat. In 1993 Mexico created the Upper Gulf reserve, and in 1996 it announced a recovery strategy and formed the [[International Committee for the Recovery of the Vaquita]], which requested a new abundance estimate as its first step.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The range is confined to the northwestern Gulf of California near the Colorado River mouth. The study area was stratified by depth and density into four strata:

- **Core and southeast** (20-100 m), surveyed from the 52 m [[R/V David Starr Jordan]] (DSJ).
- **Shallow** (5-20 m), surveyed from the 21 m [[R/V BIP XI]] (BIP), a shrimp trawler.
- **Delta** (<5 m), surveyed by a 10 m skiff (panga) near the Colorado River.

Vaquitas travel in small groups and surface inconspicuously, and they move away from approaching vessels. Groups within about 913 m of the ship showed avoidance, so surveys relying on low viewing platforms or hand-held binoculars detect few animals. Aggregation behavior was strong: on 17 September 41 groups (37% of the cruise total) were seen in a few hours. Aggregations appeared after the shrimp fleet (trawling and gillnet) began operating in early September. Shrimp fisheries and artisanal gillnets are therefore spatially clumped, which the authors say makes the population vulnerable to a single large mortality event.

Other reported vaquita research includes genetics, habitat, diet, behavior, morphology, life history, bycatch and conservation status, cited in the reference list.

## Key Findings

- **Abundance:** 567 vaquitas (bootstrap 95% BC CI 177-1,073; analytical lognormal CI 237-1,358; bootstrap CV 0.51). Pooled density was 0.142 vaquitas/km².
- **Core area:** 409 vaquitas (CV 0.61; density 0.154/km²) from 70 sightings on 32 transects totalling 439.8 km. The total core stratum area was 2,659.7 km².
- **Shallow sub-area (north of San Felipe):** 158 vaquitas (CV 0.94; density 0.119/km²) from 6 sightings in 342 km of effort. Density here was 77% of the core density.
- **Delta:** no sightings in 111 km of search effort.
- **Detection:** the 25X binoculars on the DSJ were about seven times as effective as 7X binoculars on the BIP. The estimated g(0) was 0.571 (CV 0.33) for the DSJ flying bridge and 0.148 for the 7X bridge team. The BIP value was implied at about 0.28 by assumption, not measured.
- **Sightings:** 125 groups in total, more than all previous studies combined.
- **Comparison with 1993:** the 1997 estimate was not directly comparable with 224 (1993). Using the 1997 g(0) would have given 311 for 1993, close to 409 given the variances. The authors state the data do not indicate population growth.
- **Mortality:** [[D'Agrosa]] et al. estimated 83 vaquitas/year killed in one fishing town in 1993-94, equal to 14% of the 1997 estimate. Total gillnet mortality is expected to be large enough to cause decline.
- **Reserve:** 40% of 1997 sightings fell outside the reserve boundary, and none fell within the nuclear zone, where fishing is prohibited. The authors recommend new boundaries and regulations.
- **Recommendations:** future visual surveys should use large vessels with 25X binoculars. Acoustic surveys ([[Passive Acoustic Monitoring]]) are promising for shallow water and less weather-dependent.
