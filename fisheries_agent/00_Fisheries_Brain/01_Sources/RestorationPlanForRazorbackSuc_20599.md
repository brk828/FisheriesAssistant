# Restoration Plan for Razorback Sucker in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #restoration-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document presents a restoration plan for the federally endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Once abundant throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]], the species has been virtually extirpated from riverine habitats in the lower basin due to water development and nonnative fish introductions. The largest remaining riverine population consists of approximately 500 declining mature individuals occupying 160 km of the middle [[Green River]] system. The restoration plan employs captive propagation strategies, genetic management guidelines, and adaptive management approaches to prevent extinction and restore naturally sustaining populations while minimizing risks to other endemic fish populations.

## Background & Description

[[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) was federally listed as endangered in 1991. The species was historically abundant throughout major river systems of the [[Colorado River Basin]] but has experienced severe population declines. Water development and introduction of nonnative fishes have affected habitat quality and are associated with the virtual extirpation of the species from riverine habitats in the lower [[Colorado River Basin]].

The species exhibits spawning site fidelity, with individuals traveling as far as 70 km to reach spawning locations. Fish can be collected predictably at spawning sites in the [[Green River]] and its major tributary, the [[Yampa River]]. In the upper mainstem [[Colorado River]], [[Razorback Sucker]] are infrequently collected, spawning aggregations and recruitment are no longer observed, and hybridization with native [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and introduced [[White Sucker]] is a growing concern. The status of populations in the [[San Juan River]] is being defined.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The largest remaining riverine [[Razorback Sucker]] population consists of approximately 500 mature individuals occupying 160 km of the middle [[Green River]] system. These fish demonstrate spawning site fidelity and undertake spawning migrations. The population is declining, and recruitment appears limited.

Habitat degradation from water development has significantly impacted the species throughout its range. The introduction of nonnative fish species has created additional pressures through competition, predation, and hybridization. In the upper mainstem [[Colorado River]], hybridization with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersonii*) represents a growing threat to genetic integrity.

The species still persists in riverine habitat in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] above [[Lake Powell]] in both the [[Green River]] and [[Upper Colorado River]] mainstem systems, distinguishing these populations from those in the lower basin.

## Key Findings

**Recovery Goals and Management Framework:**
- Major recovery goals are to prevent immediate population extinction and protect, maintain, and restore naturally sustaining populations in historic habitat
- Recovery and management depend on protection, maintenance, and improvement in population status and trends defined in demographic and genetic terms
- Management opportunities are defined and priorities assigned on a population-by-population basis
- Appropriate production strategies are selected with genetic risks assessed and primary and secondary objectives determined

**Population Status:**
- Approximately 500 mature individuals occupy 160 km of the middle [[Green River]] system, representing the largest riverine population
- Population is declining
- Fish show spawning site fidelity and travel up to 70 km to spawning sites
- Predictable spawning aggregations occur at two sites: one in the [[Green River]] and one in the [[Yampa River]]
- Upper mainstem [[Colorado River]] populations are infrequently collected with no observed spawning aggregations or recruitment
- Hybridization with native [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and introduced [[White Sucker]] is a growing concern in the upper mainstem [[Colorado River]]
- [[San Juan River]] population status is being defined

**Captive Propagation Strategy:**
- Current status and trends indicate captive propagation strategies may be useful in recovery
- First step is accurate identification and characterization of all wild and captive [[Razorback Sucker]] populations throughout the basin
- Recovery actions include broad, basin-wide genetic surveys
- Development of artificial genetic refuge (AGR) populations of high priority natural populations
- Breeding and rearing strategies designed to maintain natural population characteristics
- Genetically appropriate broodstocks can be developed from AGR populations for augmentation or restoration stocking if required
- Stocking decisions depend on population status and trends, availability of suitable fish and habitat, and risks to other native fish populations

**Genetic Management:**
- Genetic management guidelines provide rationale for the restoration plan as an integral component of comprehensive, long-term recovery
- Basin-wide genetic surveys conducted to characterize populations
- AGR populations established to preserve genetic diversity of high priority natural populations

**Adaptive Management and Research:**
- Adaptive management strategies employed to guide management decisions and actions in light of biological uncertainty
- Research on spawning migration, natal imprinting, and chemoreception is described
- Research results being used to reduce risks associated with biological uncertainty and enhance stocking results
- All decisions and actions focused on preventing extinction and maintaining or restoring naturally sustaining populations while reducing risks to other endemic fish populations

**Management Recommendations:**
- Habitat protection and restoration included in work proposals and action plans
- Recovery activities developed in sufficient detail to implement and evaluate in terms of population status, trends, and recovery goals
- Management decisions provided regarding captive propagation and stocking in the [[Green River]], [[Colorado River]], and [[San Juan River]] systems
- Criteria established for defining population status and trends and habitat requirements to identify management recommendations
