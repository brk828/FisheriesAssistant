# Artificial Destratification of Lakes and Its Significance in Fisheries Management

#limnology #reservoir-management #fisheries-management #destratification

## Executive Summary

This 1966 administrative report by the California Department of Fish and Game examines artificial destratification of lakes using compressed air as a fisheries management technique. The study focuses on two California reservoirs: [[Lake Wohlford]] (Escondido) and [[El Capitan Reservoir]] (San Diego County). Thermal stratification in eutrophic lakes creates oxygen deficits below the thermocline during summer, deteriorating water quality and excluding organisms from the hypolimnion. Compressed air systems successfully eliminate stratification, improve dissolved oxygen throughout the water column, enhance water quality, and expand habitable space for fish and other aquatic organisms. Preliminary results suggest additional benefits including reduced evaporation rates and dampened phytoplankton bloom magnitude.

## Background & Description

**Thermal Stratification Process:**
Lakes undergo annual stratification cycles with maximum stratification during summer. Physical stratification results from vertical distribution of thermal energy affecting water density. Chemical properties (oxygen, pH) and biological properties (fish, plankton, benthos) stratify in response to the physical density barrier. In eutrophic systems, oxygen deficits commonly develop in the hypolimnion, creating anaerobic conditions, hydrogen sulfide production, and deteriorated drinking water quality.

**Destratification Mechanism:**
Compressed air released near the reservoir bottom creates vertical currents. Cold hypolimnetic water upwells to the surface, flows horizontally, then sinks below adjacent warm surface water. During descent, cold water mixes with warmer epilimnion and thermocline water, forming intermediate-density water that flows out at corresponding depth levels. The mixing rate depends primarily on density differences between upwelled and epilimnetic water. Oxygen is gained primarily through atmospheric contact and photosynthesis rather than direct air injection.

**Study Sites:**

*[[Lake Wohlford]]:*
- Location: 7 miles northeast of Escondido, San Diego County
- Owned by Escondido Mutual Water Company
- Maximum volume: 7,000 acre-feet; maximum surface area: 222 acres
- Average volume: ~2,500 acre-feet covering 130 acres
- Temporary aeration system installed April 1961; permanent system operational since April 1962
- System: 210 cfm at 125 psi; 2 hours required to eliminate stratification
- Operated on "need" basis based on plankton samples, turbidity, oxygen, pH, and temperature profiles

*[[El Capitan Reservoir]]:*
- Location: ~25 miles east of San Diego on San Diego River
- Dam completed 1935; filled to capacity by 1939
- Eutrophic warmwater reservoir; classified as alkali lake
- Steep-sided with reduced littoral area
- High turbidity discourages rooted aquatic plant growth
- Destratification commenced June 10, 1965
- System: 100-foot perforated 1.25-inch plastic pipe suspended in 80 feet of water; ninety 9/64-inch holes; 210 cfm at 125 psi

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pre-Destratification Conditions:**
Eutrophic reservoirs in San Diego County exhibit strong summer stratification with oxygen depletion below the thermocline. At [[El Capitan Reservoir]], oxygen deficits existed at 9-10 meters depth during July 1964. Temperature and oxygen profiles showed typical stratified patterns with sharp thermoclines and anoxic hypolimnia. The hypolimnion remained uninhabitable for most organisms during stratified periods.

**Water Chemistry Changes:**
Complete chemical analyses from [[El Capitan Reservoir]] (August 1964 vs. August 1965) revealed profound changes following destratification. Most affected constituents included:
- Iron: reduced from 0.39 ppm (17m depth, 1964) to 0.02 ppm (1965)
- Manganese: reduced from 1.07 ppm (7m, 1964) to 0.10 ppm (1965)
- Carbonate: eliminated (6.0 ppm at surface in 1964, 0 in 1965)
- Bicarbonate: more uniform vertically (166-209 ppm range in 1964 vs. 182-183 ppm in 1965)
- Total phosphate: reduced from 0.360-0.420 ppm to 0.074-0.178 ppm

**Phytoplankton Dynamics:**
[[Lake Wohlford]] phytoplankton data (December 1957-September 1963) showed trends following aeration initiation:
- Average number of organisms per month decreased
- Number of different genera per month increased
- Amplitude of monthly variation dampened
- Fall overturn blooms eliminated after aeration began

However, these trends may reflect improved counting technique rather than biological changes, as counters received formal training in 1962 when permanent aeration commenced.

**Zoobenthos Distribution:**
[[El Capitan Reservoir]] benthic fauna showed dramatic vertical distribution changes. During July 1964 (stratified), benthos were abruptly limited by oxygen depletion at 9-10 meters depth. During July 1965 and 1966 (destratified), oxygen remained plentiful at all depths, allowing benthic organisms to inhabit previously anoxic profundal zones. Preliminary results indicate increased benthic food organisms in the profundal zone and increased total benthos within the lake.

**Evaporation Reduction:**
[[Lake Wohlford]] experienced approximately 4% yearly evaporation reduction attributed to lower surface water temperatures during summer months. Greater reductions might be expected in large, deep reservoirs due to higher hypolimnion:epilimnion water ratios.

## Key Findings

**Lake Wohlford Operational Results (1962-1965):**

1. **Water Quality Improvements:**
   - Chlorine demand reduced from 14 ppm (pre-aeration) to 7 ppm (post-aeration)
   - Net savings: $6,000 annually to water company
   - Taste and odor complaints decreased
   - Hydrogen sulfide eliminated
   - Complete destratification achieved in approximately 2 hours of operation

2. **Operational Costs:**
   - Annual cost including amortization, power, operation, and maintenance: ~$2,300
   - Cost substantially lower than water treatment plant construction and maintenance

3. **Copper Sulfate Application:**
   - Average yearly treatments: 6.0 (1958-1961) vs. 3.7 (1962-1964)
   - No significant relationship between destratification and application rates per acre or per acre-foot
   - Reduction in treatment frequency suggests improved phytoplankton control

4. **Fishery Impacts:**
   - 1959 rapid drawdown caused fish kill due to mixing oxygen-depleted hypolimnion water
   - Subsequent rotenone treatment and restocking prevented full evaluation of destratification effects on fishery
   - Impossible to distinguish fish kill effects from destratification effects in short-term study

**El Capitan Reservoir Destratification Study (1964-1966):**

1. **Temperature and Oxygen Profiles:**
   - June 10, 1965 (pre-destratification): Strong stratification similar to June 1964
   - June 21, 1965 (after 11 days continuous operation): Striking elimination of stratification
   - July 1, 1965 (after 10-day shutdown): Progression toward restratification
   - September 1965: Progressively more vertical profiles with continued operation
   - October 1965: Lake maintained destratified condition after compressor shutdown due to seasonal heat loss

2. **Restratification Rate:**
   - Experimental shutdown (June 21-July 1) demonstrated measurable restratification within 10 days
   - Rate dependent on heat absorption and density differences

3. **Seasonal Maintenance:**
   - Lake remained destratified through winter after October shutdown
   - Heat loss at air-water interface caused cooled water to sink and mix
   - Natural destratification maintained until following spring when net heat inflow resumes

4. **Benthic Fauna Response:**
   - Vertical distribution expanded into previously uninhabitable depths
   - Increased living space for benthic organisms
   - Preliminary evidence of increased total benthos population

**Fisheries Management Applications:**

1. **Chemical Treatment Distribution:**
   - Compressed air can distribute rotenone uniformly throughout reservoir
   - Eliminates pockets of low concentration
   - Application possible at air discharge location or multiple sites

2. **Post-Treatment Rehabilitation:**
   - Continued destratification reduces detoxification time
   - Rotenone detoxifies rapidly with increased temperature, light, and oxygen
   - Entire lake volume detoxifies simultaneously rather than in zones

3. **Increased Living Space:**
   - Eliminates anaerobic conditions in summer hypolimnion
   - Allows fish populations to inhabit previously uninhabitable areas
   - Increased benthic food organisms benefit most fish species

4. **Fish Kill Prevention:**
   - Eliminates oxygen depletion during fall overturns
   - Dampens phytoplankton population fluctuations
   - Maintains more uniform production rate
   - Prevents suffocation events like [[Lake Wohlford]] October 1959 overturn

5. **Winter Applications:**
   - Potential to eliminate ice cover and reduce winter fish kills in northern lakes
   - Variable success reported by other researchers

**Productivity Considerations:**

Forced circulation in large, deep reservoirs may affect primary productivity through altered photic zone residence time. If phytoplankton cannot maintain position in the photic zone, productivity may decrease due to increased time in aphotic zone (respiration only). However, if phytoplankton can maintain photic zone position, productivity may increase due to enhanced nutrient circulation. Selection may favor forms capable of maintaining photic zone position.

**Technical Methods Summary:**

Multiple compressed air destratification techniques described:
- Floating aerators (Indian Brook Reservoir, NY: 160 cfm, 7.5 ft depth, 30 hours to destratify)
- Aero-hydraulic cannons (commercial, polyethylene tubes 12-18 inch diameter)
- Plastic hose grid systems (sewage treatment ponds, 4x capacity increase)
- Blower-type systems (Babson Reservoir, MA: 477 cfm at 7 psi, 4 ft depth)
- Single-line air release (Lake Wohlford and El Capitan method)

**Project Status:**

Study initiated 1964 with one year pre-destratification baseline data collection. Destratification commenced June 1965. Project expected to continue through 1967. Comprehensive final reports forthcoming from California Department of Fish and Game and collaborating agencies (City of San Diego, Helix Irrigation District, U.S. Geological Survey, County of San Diego).
