# San Pedro River Interim Management Guidelines Newsletter (Issue No. 1)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #riparian-ecosystem

## Executive Summary

This first issue of the [[San Pedro River]] newsletter, issued by the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) [[Safford District Office]], reports on the agency's management of recently acquired riparian lands. It announces draft [[Interim Management Guidelines]] released on April 3, 1986, and the start of a formal long-term management plan with an [[Environmental Impact Statement]]. It also reports on fencing of historic ruins, a public-use closure, interim facilities and fire protection, staffing, and the formation of two technical advisory committees. The document is an agency update, not a research study, and it contains no fish survey data. Its only fish-related content is the naming of [[W.L. Minckley]] as a fish expert on a committee.

## Background & Description

The BLM acquired lands along the San Pedro River in southeastern Arizona. The newsletter describes the following:

- **Interim guidelines:** Developed from public comments at meetings in Sierra Vista, Tucson, and Phoenix, with input from the San Pedro Steering Committee. They were to remain in use for about two years, until a formal management plan was adopted.
- **Existing leases:** By agreement with the previous owner, all current leases and permits would be honored to expiration. The [[Livestock Grazing]] lease expires at the end of 1987. Two [[Sand And Gravel Extraction]] leases expire at the end of 1986 and at the end of 1988.
- **Key sites:** The [[Presidio Of Santa Cruz De Terrenate]] ruins and the BLM temporary headquarters at [[Fairbank]], at the junction of State Highway 82 and the San Pedro River.
- **Project leadership:** [[Erick Campbell]], formerly the Safford District Wildlife Biologist, was named Project Manager.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The primary interim objective is to protect the [[Riparian Ecosystem]], cultural values, and other important resources until a comprehensive plan is completed. The draft guidelines listed the following resource concerns:

- **Water:** Establish monitoring sites to build baseline data on surface and groundwater quality and quantity. Evaluate the impacts of current sand and gravel operations.
- **Air:** Establish monitoring equipment to build an air quality database.
- **Soils:** Determine the extent of soil erosion on the San Pedro and its tributaries, and inventory area soils.
- **Vegetation:** Do not permit harvest of firewood, cactus, or ornamental plants until long-term planning is complete.
- **Grazing:** Evaluate livestock impacts during the interim period to determine where, and whether, grazing can continue long term.
- **Wildlife and habitat:** Inventory resources to provide baseline data. Wildlife inventories are led by the [[Biological Resources Technical Committee]].
- **Wildfire:** Control wildfire to protect structures and the riparian ecosystem, pending a plan that addresses prescribed fire and limited suppression.
- **Scenic and recreational values:** Determine how scenic values interact with other uses, and inventory recreational potential for the long term.

## Key Findings

**1. Interim guidelines (draft released April 3, 1986)**
The guidelines include the following items:
- Close the area to the general public until the formal plan is complete. BLM will ask the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] to close it to hunting, trapping, and off-road vehicle use during the closure.
- Protect cultural and paleontological resources, seek funds to protect the Presidio ruins, and explore a cooperative agreement with [[Arizona State Parks]].
- Honor existing sand and gravel leases until expiration. Keep the area closed to mineral entry until the final plan is developed.
- Acquire contiguous lands, identified and prioritized for purchase.
- Educate the public and involve them in planning.

**2. Management plan initiated**
A notice of intent to prepare a management plan and EIS was published in the *Federal Register* on May 13, 1986. The process is expected to finish by June 1988. Four preliminary alternatives were identified:
1. **Resource Preservation:** maximum protection of existing resources.
2. **Resource Utilization:** appropriate grazing, full recreational development, and sand and gravel extraction.
3. **Balanced Use:** may end sand and gravel operations and livestock use, and develop some recreational sites.
4. **No Action:** continue the levels of use present at acquisition.

A Record of Decision may adopt any alternative in full or combine elements of several.

**3. Protection of the Presidio ruins**
The maintenance crew spent about one week building a four-strand barbed wire fence around the ruins of the [[Presidio Of Santa Cruz De Terrenate]]. The fence keeps livestock from rubbing against the few remaining standing walls and pushing them down.

**4. Closure and patrols**
BLM published a *Federal Register* notice closing the lands to the public after taking possession. The closure prevents conflicts with lessees and damage to resources. District personnel were detailed to the area to advise the public, monitor use, and begin inventory work.

**5. Interim facilities and fire protection**
- Services were installed for three house trailers at Fairbank, which will serve as temporary headquarters for administrative offices, fire crew headquarters, and living quarters. The site is expected to be used for at least two years.
- A four-person fire crew with one engine is stationed at Fairbank for 60 days, or until summer rains reduce the wildfire threat. Its duties include trash cleanup, patrol, and inventory work, and it is on call 24 hours a day.

**6. Staffing and committees**
- Erick Campbell was named Project Manager. He holds a B.S. and an M.S. in Wildlife Management from Humboldt State University and has worked for BLM in California, Nevada, Washington, and Arizona. His interests are riparian ecosystem management and game and nongame wildlife.
- The **Biological Resources Technical Committee** has 10 members, including [[W.L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University, recognized as the leading fish expert in the southwestern United States), [[Robert Ohmart]] (riparian management), and experts in birds, small mammals, herpetofauna, and wildlife habitat. The committee reviews proposed inventories to avoid duplication and has helped BLM recruit volunteers.
- The **Cultural Resource Technical Committee** has six members, including [[Bruce Huckell]], [[Bernard Fontana]], [[W. Bruce Masse]], [[Jack S. Williams]], [[Shereen Lerner]], and [[Anne Woosley]]. Its orientation meeting was held on February 18, 1986. Its discussions covered interim management issues, protection needs, data needs for land use planning, and the planning schedule.
