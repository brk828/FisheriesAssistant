# Map Index of the Chihuahuan Desert Region

#cartography #chihuahuan-desert #map-index

## Executive Summary

This document is a map index and atlas reference compiled by [[José García]] of Ciudad Chihuahua, covering the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] region of northern Mexico and the southwestern United States. It contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, or water-chemistry data. Its content is cartographic: a locator map, a sheet index, and reduced sheets of topographic maps drawn from the [[United States Geological Survey]] (USGS) and the [[Army Map Service]] (AMS). The document is therefore relevant to aquatic ecology only as background for locating desert study sites.

## Background & Description

The index lists 49 entries on its first page, grouped by type:

- **Locator map:** an outline of Mexico showing the approximate extent of the Chihuahuan Desert.
- **Sheets 2–36:** reductions of USGS (USA) and AMS (Mexico) 1:250,000 topographic sheets. Named sheets include [[Socorro]], [[Fort Sumner]], [[Tularosa]], [[Roswell]], [[Silver City]], [[Las Cruces]], [[Carlsbad]], [[Hobbs]], [[Douglas]], [[El Paso]], [[Van Horn]], [[Marfa]], [[Fort Stockton]], [[Presidio]], [[Chihuahua]], [[Torreón]], [[Monterrey]], and [[Durango]].
- **Sheet 37:** [[Sierra del Carmen]], Coahuila, Texas, with a gazetteer of 36 numbered place names.
- **Sheets 38–40:** 1:500,000 Mexican maps covering portions of Zacatecas, San Luis Potosí, and Nuevo León.
- **Sheets 41–43:** the 1906 [[Abbott Map]] of Coahuila, issued in three parts.
- **Sheets 44–49:** the *Atlas Geográfica por Estados de la República Mexicana* (1946–56), covering Chihuahua (in part), Coahuila, Nuevo León, Durango, Zacatecas, and San Luis Potosí.

Page 3 is a 1:3,500,000 overview map showing the region's principal towns and highways, including [[Alpine]], [[Del Rio]], and [[Eagle Pass]]. Most sheets carry their own scale bars, such as 1:250,000 with statute-mile and kilometer equivalents.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sheets record features relevant to desert hydrology and habitat, though they do not quantify them. Named features include:

- Dry lake and playa names, such as "Laguna de Mayrán," and numerous stock tanks and dams (labelled "tanque" or "presa"), which indicate where surface water is or was collected.
- Canyons and springs in the Sierra del Carmen gazetteer, including Cañón del Diablo, Cañón de Guadalupe, Cañón del Oso, Agua Chile, and Ojo de Cuchillo.
- A note on the [[Sierra del Carmen]] sheet that several former fluorite mines are marked (e.g., "fluorita abandonada").
- Marginal notes on the Van Horn and Hobbs sheets marking numerous sinkholes in Andrews and Martin counties, Texas.

The document does not report species occurrences, water quality, or biological survey data.

## Key Findings

- The document is a **cartographic index**, not a biological study. It catalogues 49 index entries and reduced topographic sheets at scales of 1:250,000, 1:500,000, and 1:3,500,000.
- Coverage spans USGS and AMS sheets (US and Mexican), the 1906 Abbott Map of Coahuila, and the 1946–56 Mexican state atlas.
- The Sierra del Carmen gazetteer lists 36 numbered localities, including canyons, springs, mines, and campsites.
- Mapped water features (tanks, springs, playas, dams) could support desert aquatic site selection, but the document provides no species, abundance, or water-chemistry data.
- Source-map notes state that some [[CETENAL Maps]] data were not included in the gazetteer.
- No conclusions, plans, or aquatic findings are stated. The document's value to aquatic ecology lies in its use as a locator for [[Chihuahuan Desert]] field sites.
