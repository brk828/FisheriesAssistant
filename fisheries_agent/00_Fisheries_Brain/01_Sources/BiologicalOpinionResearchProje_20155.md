# Status and Conservation Impacts on the Desert Pupfish at Quitobaquito Springs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #desert-fishes #endangered-species #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Biological Opinion evaluating a proposed research project on the [[Rio Sonoyta Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense longifemorale*) at Quitobaquito Springs, Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument, Arizona. Although the primary research subject is a turtle, the consultation is legally centered on potential impacts to the co-occurring, federally endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*), the Quitobaquito subspecies, whose critical habitat overlaps the turtle trapping area. The FWS concluded the turtle research project is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the desert pupfish nor destroy/adversely modify its critical habitat, while authorizing a small, capped level of incidental take.

## Background & Description
- Described in the source as **"a small fish"** (*Cyprinodon macularius*), consistent with the general body plan of the Cyprinodontidae (pupfishes): small-bodied, robust, deep-bodied desert fish adapted to extreme aquatic environments.
- No detailed morphometric, meristic, or coloration data are provided in this document; morphological description is limited to taxonomic subspecies designation (*C. m. eremus*), distinguished from the nominate form per [[Miller and Fuiman 1987]].
- Full taxonomic description and conservation status of the subspecies is referenced to [[Miller and Fuiman 1987]], which formally described *Cyprinodon macularius eremus* as new from Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historical range**: Historically common throughout the lower [[Gila River]] system, the [[Lower Colorado River]] system, and the [[Rio Sonoyta]] system across Arizona, California, and Mexico (Minckley 1973).
- **Current distribution**: Drastically reduced — now known from only four natural localities in California and Arizona, plus the Rio Sonoyta, [[Laguna Salada]], and lower Colorado River delta in Sonora and Baja California, Mexico.
- **Habitat**: Desert spring/pond/channel ecosystem at Quitobaquito Springs; designated critical habitat also includes three locations in Imperial County, California.
- **Threats to habitat/population**: Water impoundment, water diversion, stream downcutting, backwater draining, vegetation clearing, channelization, groundwater pumping, pesticide exposure, and introduction of predatory/competitive nonnative fishes.
- **Co-occurring species of conservation concern**: Shares habitat with the endangered [[Rio Sonoyta Mud Turtle]], creating overlapping conservation management needs and creating incidental interaction risk during turtle-focused field research (e.g., minnow trap bycatch).
- **Population monitoring**: Subject to annual census by the National Park Service; the most recent survey (1991) recorded a population estimate via trapping of **2,617 individuals** with only **11 mortalities**, indicating trapping-related mortality is typically very low.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory status**: Desert pupfish listed as Endangered on March 31, 1986; critical habitat designated at Quitobaquito Spring (AZ) and three sites in Imperial County (CA).
- **Research interaction**: The proposed Rio Sonoyta mud turtle study (Philip Rosen, University of Arizona, with NPS collaborators Michael Kunzmann and Peter Bennett) would deploy ~20 minnow traps around Quitobaquito Pond to trap juvenile turtles; these traps would incidentally catch desert pupfish, which would be released after each inspection (checked ≥2x/day).
- **Adult turtle traps** (full-sized) are too large to capture pupfish, limiting bycatch risk to juvenile-turtle trap sets only.
- **Anticipated incidental take**: FWS authorized incidental take of **fewer than 25 desert pupfish** via direct mortality from trapping and handling during the study period (October 1992 and 2–3 sessions in 1993).
- **Biological Opinion conclusion**: The project is **not likely to jeopardize** the desert pupfish or adversely modify critical habitat; no habitat alteration or destruction is expected, and any fish mortality would not substantially harm the subspecies.
- **Reasonable and Prudent Measures** imposed:
  1. Conduct study to minimize trapping and incidental take of desert pupfish.
  2. Maintain complete records of actions potentially resulting in pupfish take.
- **Terms and Conditions**: Study must follow the July 22, 1992 Biological Assessment protocol; a final report must document incidental capture, release, and mortality of desert pupfish.
- **Reinitiation triggers**: Formal consultation must be reinitiated if incidental take limits are exceeded, new information reveals unconsidered effects, the action is modified in ways affecting the species, or new critical habitat/species listings occur affecting the action.
- **Key literature underpinning pupfish status**: [[Minckley 1973]] (historical distribution), [[Miller and Fuiman 1987]] (subspecies description), [[Hendrickson and Varela-Romero 1989]] (Mexico/Arizona conservation status), [[Black 1980]] (California status), [[Schoenherr 1988]] (life history review), and NPS (1991) annual monitoring report.
