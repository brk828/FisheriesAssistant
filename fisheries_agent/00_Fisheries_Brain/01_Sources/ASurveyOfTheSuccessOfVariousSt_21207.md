# Farm Pond Stocking Ratios and Bass–Bluegill Population Balance in Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources bulletin (Federal Aid Project F-3-R, Farm Pond Investigations) evaluates the success of past [[Stocking Ratios]] and rates of [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]] in state farm ponds. Between 1945 and 1953 more than 24,000 ponds were reported stocked. The authors surveyed past stocking records, field-checked 272 randomly sampled ponds in summer 1952, and analysed population balance, pond classification, carrying capacity and age and growth. The study concludes that pond physical condition and the presence of wild fishes were more important limits on success than stocking ratios alone.

## Background & Description

- **Program history:** The state farm pond stocking program began in 1945 with 150 ponds. Over 24,000 ponds were reported stocked from 1945 to 1953, peaking at 6,223 in 1953.
- **Stock sources:** Largemouth and Kentucky bass were supplied by state hatcheries from the program's outset. Until 1947 bluegill were seined locally; thereafter they were supplied largely by [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] hatcheries, with some adults taken from overcrowded ponds.
- **Stocking methods:** Two methods were used intermittently: (1) adult bluegill with fingerling or fry bass, and (2) fingerling bluegill with fingerling or fry bass. Ratios were based on initial 1945 trials and on Alabama and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recommendations.
- **Objectives:** Immediate aims were testing stocking ratios, rates and methods; ultimate aims were ratios suited to Kentucky's climate and soils.
- **Study regions:** Kentucky was divided into six soil regions (per [[Kentucky Soil Regions]]): Jackson Purchase, Western Coalfields, Pennyroyal, Outer Bluegrass, Inner Bluegrass and Eastern Coalfields.
- **Pond classification:** Ponds were rated as [[Manageable Ponds]], [[Marginal Ponds]] or [[Unmanageable Ponds]], based on depth, water-level stability, overflow, shallow areas and clarity.
- **Methods:** Seines of up to 75 ft were used (longer seines were dropped after two weeks); population balance followed [[Swingle Balance Concept]] criteria. Scale samples were used for age and growth, and back-calculation followed [[Kirkwood Scale-Body Relationship]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Soils and fertility:** Most regions were low in phosphate and acidic, except the Inner Bluegrass, whose soils derive from phosphatic limestone.
- **Pond size:** Ponds under 1/2 acre were unbalanced more often. Unfertilized ponds of 1/2 acre or larger were balanced twice as often as smaller unfertilized ponds (Table 5 data: 18% vs 36%).
- **Fertilization:** Only 6% of ponds (14 of 238 sampled) were fertilized adequately to maintain light plankton growth, per [[Swingle and Smith Fertilization Guidelines]]. Fertilization was generally irregular and ineffective.
- **Wild fishes:** Green sunfish, bullheads and crappies were the main non-stocked species. Wild fishes reduced balanced populations by 14% in manageable ponds, 46% in marginal ponds and 50% in unmanageable ponds.
- **Siltation and livestock:** Unrestricted livestock use caused the greatest siltation and turbidity, the primary limiting factor in unmanageable and marginal ponds.
- **Other losses:** Insecticide kills, rotenone, oxygen depletion and acid mine water each eliminated or prevented fish populations in a few ponds.

## Key Findings

**Stocking success**
- Data came from 63 ponds (fingerling bass to fingerling bluegill) and 50 ponds (fry/fingerling bass to adult bluegill), a total of 113 usable ponds from 872 selected.
- Among adult-bluegill ratios, 3 bass : 1 bluegill gave the highest significant balanced share (46%).
- Among fingerling ratios, 1 bass : 8 bluegill gave the highest balanced share (87%).
- Best fingerling range was 1:10 to 1:6. Best adult range was about 2:1 to 6:1.
- Sixty percent of fingerling-stocked ponds and 12% of adult-stocked ponds were balanced.

**Pond condition**
- Primary limiting factors were determined for 147 unmanageable and 50 marginal ponds. Top causes were siltation/turbidity from livestock (37% and 26%) and inadequate depth (31% of unmanageable).
- Field sample of 272 ponds: 27% bass and bluegill only, 41% containing other species, 5% fishless, 14% dry, 2% not seinable, 11% not worth seining.
- Of 238 sampled ponds, 53% were unmanageable, 22% marginal and 25% manageable.
- Pond owners' reports of "fair to good" fishing: 53% of manageable, 16% of marginal and 10% of unmanageable ponds.

**Carrying capacity**
- Recoverable bluegill standing crop in seven ponds ranged from 100 lb/acre (Pennyroyal) to 509 lb/acre (Outer Bluegrass). The authors caution these values cannot be treated as regional averages.

**Age and growth**
- 1,608 bluegill (91 ponds), 274 largemouth bass (58 ponds) and 68 Kentucky bass (27 ponds) were aged.
- Bluegill did not average 6 inches until their fourth summer; longevity rarely exceeded five years.
- Largemouth bass averaged 2 years to reach 10 inches.
- Kentucky bass grew more slowly and died earlier, so the authors judged them less desirable than largemouth bass for stocking.

**Conclusions**
- Physically unsuited ponds are the main cause of failure and deserve far more attention than ratios alone.
- Judicious fertilization may benefit ponds, especially in the Eastern Coalfields, but its proper role remains undetermined.
