# Hybopsis lineapunctata, A New Cyprinid Fish from the Upper Alabama River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 taxonomic paper, by [[Glenn H. Clemmer]] and [[Royal D. Suttkus]], describes *Hybopsis lineapunctata*, the Lined Chub, as a new cyprinid species from freshwater streams above the [[Fall Line]] in the [[Coosa River]] and [[Tallapoosa River]] of the [[Mobile Basin]]. The species is diagnosed by coarse scales (usually 20–22 circumferential body scales) and a well-developed dark lateral band ending in a large caudal spot. It is compared with [[Hybopsis amblops]] of the [[Tennessee River]], its closest relative, and the authors propose that it derived from an *H. amblops* stock that reached the Coosa River by stream capture and later spread into the Tallapoosa.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic history:** The species was previously confused with several related forms, including *H. amblops*, *H. rubrifrons* and *Hybopsis* sp., under names such as *Nocomis winchelli* and *Ceratichthys winchelli* (Jordan, 1877; Jordan and Brayton, 1878; Gilbert, 1891). Its nomenclatural history is discussed in Clemmer (1971).
- **Holotype:** TU 53405, a nuptial male, 61 mm standard length, collected 13 April 1966 from Enitachope Creek, a tributary of Hillabee Creek, Clay County, Alabama. Forty-six paratopotypes (TU 40643) were collected with it.
- **Diagnosis:** A large member of the *H. amblops* group (maximum length examined 66 mm SL) with coarse scales; circumferential scales usually 20–22, sometimes 24 in the Coosa population. The dark lateral band narrows over the caudal peduncle and is confluent with a large, darkly pigmented caudal spot. A light band lies above the lateral band, and dorsal scales are darkly outlined posteriorly.
- **Morphology:** Subterete, somewhat compressed body; head averages about 27% of SL; single pair of maxillary barbels (some specimens lacked them); 8 dorsal fin rays; 12 scales around the caudal peduncle; pharyngeal teeth 1,4–4,1.
- **Sexual characters:** Males develop nuptial tubercles on the head and first seven or eight pectoral rays during breeding. No breeding colours or sexual dimorphism in pigmentation were noted beyond this; the Tennessee-related *H. rubrifrons* has red breeding colour, which is not shared by this species.
- **Materials:** Paratypes and other material were examined from numerous institutional collections (UAIC, CU, UMMZ, ANSP, USNM, UT, TU and others), with the bulk of paratype material from the Tallapoosa system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Found in small to moderate streams in slightly flowing to quiet water, usually near riffles. Collected over sand and sand-silt bottoms in deeper pools of smaller streams and along banks in slow to quiet water of larger tributaries with moderate current.
- **Water conditions during collection (April 1966):** Water temperatures 17–21 °C; air temperatures 21–26 °C; stream widths 2–45 ft; water clear to moderately turbid.
- **Reproduction:** Ripe males and females were taken from mid-March to early June, with ripe fish collected 2–13 April 1966 in four Tallapoosa tributaries. Individuals as small as 34 mm had enlarged gonads. Post-spawning adults were collected as early as 23 May, when males had lost nuptial tubercles and both sexes were emaciated.
- **Impoundment effects:** In recent collections from streams affected by impoundments, the species was absent to rare.
- **Fall Line:** The Fall Line marks a break in geology and biotic distribution, and its rapids and falls act as barriers that isolate upstream fauna such as this chub.
- **Variation:** Circumferential body scale counts averaged 20.65 in the Tallapoosa population (range 19–24) and 22.24 in the Coosa population (range 20–25). Higher Coosa counts came mainly from headwater specimens in Georgia, which the authors attribute possibly to altitude and environmental effects during development.
- **Range:** Endemic to the Tallapoosa and Coosa rivers above the Fall Line in Alabama and Georgia, with one record from Mill Creek, Tennessee. Two Uphapee Creek records (Williams, 1965; Tucker, 1967) below the Fall Line were found to be misidentified.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *H. lineapunctata* is described as new and placed in a close species group with *H. amblops* and *H. rubrifrons*, distinct from other *Hybopsis* in neighbouring streams.
- **Diagnostic characters:** Coarse scales (usually 20–22 around the body) and a dark lateral band with a prominent caudal spot, which the epithet *lineapunctata* refers to.
- **Morphometrics and meristics:** Based on Tallapoosa (n = 50), Coosa (n = 23) and Tennessee (n = 63) material, the species is similar to *H. amblops* in most measurements. Differences are seen in scale counts. Lateral line scales average 35.27 (Tallapoosa), 36.10 (Coosa) and 36.22 (Tennessee). Predorsal scale rows average 12.76, 12.89 and 13.59 respectively.
- **Vertebrae:** Mean vertebral counts were similar among populations: 36.47 (Tallapoosa), 36.48 (Coosa) and 36.61 (Tennessee).
- **Origin and biogeography:** The authors infer that *H. lineapunctata* derived from Tennessee River *H. amblops* stock that entered the Coosa River by stream capture, supported by geological evidence (Campbell, 1896; Hayes, 1899) and faunal studies (Ramsey, 1966; Smith-Vaniz, 1968). It later dispersed into the Tallapoosa, probably also by stream piracy, and is now isolated above the Fall Line.
- **Distinctive traits:** The coarse scales and lateral pigmentation are regarded as derived, modified conditions after isolation.
- **Specimen base:** Over 60 collection localities are listed across Georgia and Alabama, with the Gold Branch (Elmore Co.) and Crooked Creek (Clay Co.) series among the largest. The list is summarised here rather than reproduced in full.
