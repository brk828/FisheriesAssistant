# Cyprinid Fishes of the Subgenus Cyprinella of Notropis from Southeast Texas and Northeast Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1972 Master of Science thesis by [[Glady Lou Lytle]], Arizona State University, examines the four nominal forms of the [[Notropis proserpinus Group]] (subgenus Cyprinella of the genus Notropis) from southern Texas and northeastern Mexico. The study combined computer-based [[Taximetrics]] clustering with classical morphological taxonomy, analysing 971 specimens from 60 localities. The author retains all four forms as valid species, redescribes them, and argues they are an older lineage unrelated to the [[Red Shiner]] (*N. lutrensis*) group. A geological and faunal reconstruction of the [[Rio Grande]] drainage history is used to explain their origins and present allopatric distributions.

## Background & Description

The research was motivated by earlier work on the [[Gila]] species complex (Holden and Stalnaker, 1970) and the taximetric method developed by Estabrook (1966) and refined by Wirth et al. (1966), which allows non-quantitative colour and pattern characters to be scored into states. The four forms were previously treated as synonyms or variants of the red shiner, but are separated on the basis of:

- Nuptial male colouration (yellowing fins; green or aqua-marine body).
- Male nuptial tuberculation.
- Ecological similarity among themselves and relative to *N. lutrensis*.
- Largely allopatric distributions.

Each species is redescribed:

- ***Notropis xanthicara*** (Cuatro Ciénegas shiner): endemic to the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila. It has a blue-black lateral band, a black peritoneum and a heart-shaped pigment patch on the head, and it is the most distinctive form.
- ***Notropis lepidus*** (Edwards Plateau shiner): restricted to Nueces River system headwaters and the Rio Frio, Texas, with no gular bar and typically unpigmented lips.
- ***Notropis proserpinus*** (proserpine shiner): found in clear, spring-fed tributaries of the Pecos and Devil's rivers, Texas, and at one site in northern Coahuila. Much of its habitat is now inundated by Amistad Reservoir.
- ***Notropis rutilus*** (Salado-San Juan shiner): found in the Rio Salado and Rio San Juan systems, Coahuila and Nuevo León, and possibly Tamaulipas.

Morphometric means for the 14 characters are tabulated for all four species. Meristic data (scale counts, fin rays) are summarised as percentage frequencies. Scale counts are typically 33 or 34 in the lateral line, and anal fin rays are typically 8 (9 in *N. lepidus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study scope and methods.** A total of 971 specimens was examined from 60 collections. Specimens of 30 to 60 mm standard length were used. Fifty-four characters were scored: 14 morphometric, 5 meristic and 35 qualitative. Each character was split into 2 to 5 states, and the data were analysed per collection using modes for qualitative traits. Material was sourced from the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]], the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], [[Texas Natural History Collection]], [[Tulane University Collections]] and [[Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León]].

**Habitat and ecology.** The species occupy spring-fed headwaters, clear spring outflows and their tributaries. Water clarity and temperature appear to influence melanophore expression. *N. lutrensis* is typically found further downstream in turbid, warmer water, while the proserpinus-group species tend to occur just downstream of headsprings. Salinity from Pecos River sediments acts as a barrier to some aquatic animals.

**Geological and biogeographic context.** The thesis reconstructs the history of the [[Rio Grande]] system. Key points include:

- The lower Rio Grande may have existed since the Eocene.
- An ancestral upper Rio Grande may have flowed south into the Guzmán basin during Miocene–Pliocene times.
- The river was reintegrated in mid-Pleistocene times, with downcutting following.
- Western faunal elements (e.g., *Rhinichthys cataractae*, *Gila pandora*, hydrobiid snails) survive as relicts in the upper basin and spring refugia.

**Hybridisation and disturbance.** Hybridisation between *N. proserpinus* or *N. rutilus* and *N. lutrensis* is suspected but not confirmed in nature. Laboratory hybrids of *N. lepidus* and *N. proserpinus* showed anal fin-ray counts well above either parent. Gene flow from *N. xanthicara* into the Rio Salado *rutilus* stocks is also suggested.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic status:** The four nominal forms are retained as valid species. *N. lepidus* and *N. xanthicara* are treated as well-differentiated species, while *N. proserpinus* and *N. rutilus* are kept as "semi-species" in the sense of Mayr (1970).
- **Lineage separation:** The proserpinus group is separated from the *N. lutrensis–ornatus* group as an older, relatively unrelated lineage. The *lutrensis* group is redefined to include only *N. lutrensis* and its Mexican derivatives. *N. ornatus* is treated as a distinct lineage.
- **Clustering results:** The taximetric analysis formed four cores, which correspond to *xanthicara* (Cuatro Ciénegas), *lepidus* (Nueces), *rutilus* (Salado) and *proserpinus* (Pecos/Devil's). All 60 collections were merged into one cluster at similarity c = 0.57407. Of 110 total interconnections at the final level, *N. xanthicara* made only three extra-cluster connections.
- **Relationships:** Similarity analysis indicates *N. rutilus* is most similar to *N. proserpinus*, followed by *N. lepidus*, with *N. xanthicara* the most distinctive. At level 14, 77% of *rutilus* connections were internal (versus 53% for *proserpinus*, 64% for *lepidus* and 97% for *xanthicara*). Connections between *rutilus* and *proserpinus* totalled 25 at level 14, rising to 32 if problem sample 36 is included.
- **Problem samples:** Collections 36, 48 and 54 did not match their geographic expectations. Sample 36 (Río San Juan) clustered with *proserpinus* despite being geographically *rutilus*. These were treated as geographically assigned.
- **Taxonomic caveats:** The author notes that about 17 of 35 qualitative characters seem questionably variable, that population means may hide individual variants, and that hybridisation may affect morphology.
- **Biogeographic hypothesis:** The Rio Grande was likely a highly integrated drainage with strong western affinities before Mississippi River fishes invaded following mid-Pleistocene reintegration. The four species are interpreted as relicts of this older fauna, persisting in isolated springs.
- **Ancestry:** *N. spilopterus* is considered the most likely basal stock for the proserpinus group, with *N. venustus* as a related coastal derivative. *N. lutrensis* is derived from western *N. spilopterus* populations in the Plains.
- **Synonymy:** Synonymies are considered tentative because of inconsistent historical lumping and splitting by Girard, Jordan and colleagues, and the loss or mixing of type series.
