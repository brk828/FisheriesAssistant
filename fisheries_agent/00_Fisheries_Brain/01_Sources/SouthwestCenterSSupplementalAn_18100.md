## Executive Summary

This document is a legal filing — supplemental interrogatory answers in *Southwest Center for Biological Diversity v. McGee* — rather than a primary scientific study. It contains no direct morphological, physiological, or population data on the [[Yaqui Chub]]. However, it provides indirect ecological documentation confirming the **presence of the Yaqui Chub in Turkey Creek** on the West Turkey Creek Allotment, within the [[Coronado National Forest]], and situates this species within a riparian habitat shared with the [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]]. The filing is useful primarily as a legal/occurrence record establishing standing for litigants under the [[Endangered Species Act]], with photographic documentation (1995) serving as the only cited "data point" of species occurrence.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data for the Yaqui Chub are provided in this source. The document references only that Dr. Robin Silver **photographed** the species in the field (Turkey Creek, April 17, 1995), which is cited as documentary evidence of occurrence rather than a morphological description. For morphological characterization, see primary ichthyological literature on [[Gila purpurea]] and related [[Gila (genus)|Gila chub species complex]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Riparian stream corridor within Turkey Creek, part of the West Turkey Creek Allotment in the Chiricahua Mountains portion of the Coronado National Forest.
- **Co-occurring species of conservation concern:** [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]] (*Leptonycteris curasoae yerbabuenae*), which shares the same general riparian habitat corridor monitored by declarants, despite differing life-history requirements (bat vs. fish).
- **Habitat pressures referenced:** The underlying litigation concerns impacts of livestock grazing (cattle allotment activities) on habitat integrity — implying that grazing-related degradation of riparian/stream habitat is the central ecological threat to the Yaqui Chub in this locality, consistent with broader literature on livestock impacts on desert stream fishes.
- **Site-specific occurrence record:** Confirmed sighting/photographic documentation of the Yaqui Chub specifically in **Turkey Creek** on the allotment, establishing a documented recent (as of 1995) occurrence locality.
- **Human use context:** The stream and surrounding forest are used for recreation (camping, hiking), bird-watching, scientific monitoring, and wildlife photography — activities cited by plaintiffs' declarants as the basis for legal standing tied to the species' habitat.

## Key Findings

- The Yaqui Chub was **directly observed and photographed** in Turkey Creek on the West Turkey Creek Allotment by Dr. Robin Silver on **April 17, 1995**, as part of an ongoing project to photograph all surviving native fish of Arizona.
- Southwest Center declarants (Peter Galvin, David Hodges) describe ongoing ecological monitoring of the **habitat status** of the Yaqui Chub and the Lesser Long-Nosed Bat on the allotment, citing repeated site visits since 1998.
- The document establishes that the Yaqui Chub's habitat is considered impacted by activities on the grazing allotment, forming the ecological basis of the underlying ESA-related litigation, though no quantitative habitat assessment or population data are included in this filing.
- No new (supplemental) biological findings are added beyond referencing prior declarations; this document mainly reiterates standing-related facts tied to species presence rather than presenting novel field or laboratory data.
- The [[West Turkey Creek Allotment]] and [[Turkey Creek]] are identified as a **documented locality of occurrence** for the endangered Yaqui Chub, relevant for cross-referencing in species distribution records for [[Native Fish of Arizona]].
