# Addendum to Hydrology and Interactive Computer Modeling of Ground and Surface Water in the Lower Virgin River Valley

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hydrology (no fish taxa addressed; document concerns the aquatic and riparian water system)

## Executive Summary

This 1993 addendum, prepared by the [[Las Vegas Valley Water District]] (District), updates an earlier hydrologic study of the lower [[Virgin River]] valley. It supports a proposed water diversion at [[Halfway Wash]] with a maximum rate of 700 cfs and a storage facility of about 113,000 acre-feet. Two independent flow estimates (a regression and a [[Hydrologic Basin Model]]) were used, and the project diversion model was run for three minimum bypass flows (10, 25, and 45 cfs). The report concludes that even under the largest modeled diversion (99,000 acre-feet/year), sufficient baseline flow remains to sustain [[Phreatophytes]] below Halfway Wash, although dry summers may require floodplain groundwater. Final bypass flows are to be set later by regulatory agencies.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Addendum to Brothers et al. (1992) hydrology report and Woodward-Clyde et al. (1993) environmental addendum; prepared by Kay Brothers, Terry Katzer, Reza (Duke) M. Mojib, Garith Grinnell, Alan Bernholtz, and Michael Johnson.
- **Project history:** The original 1989 application was amended in May 1992 to move the point of diversion to Halfway Wash. In February 1993 the District became the operating entity for the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]] (SNWA). In September 1993 the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR) stated that the salinity phase of the project was not needed for Colorado River salinity goals, so the District proceeds without USBR assistance.
- **Project changes (Table 1):**
  - Diversion rate raised from 500 to 700 cfs.
  - Diversion reservoir surface reduced from 355 to 100 acres.
  - Diversion structure reduced from 50 to 30 feet high.
  - Storage increased from 70,000 to 113,000 acre-feet.
  - Treatment and treated-water storage facilities to be relocated outside the basin.
- **Estimated annual diversion:** 83,000 to 99,000 acre-feet, versus the 113,000 acre-feet stated in the most recent application (used for planning flexibility).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Gage and data basis**
- Principal record: [[Littlefield Gage]], Arizona, daily means for water years 1930–1992 (63 years).
- Halfway Wash gage (Nevada): seven water years of record (1978–1983 and 1985), rated "Poor" accuracy.
- Littlefield record accuracy rated "Good" to "Poor"; overall reach accuracy "Fair."

**Hydrologic complexity:** Agricultural diversions and return flows, a large acreage of phreatophytes, bypassing irrigation and spring flow, occasional surface inflow, and groundwater inflow all complicate flow interpretation between gages.

**Phreatophyte water needs**
- Phreatophytes between Halfway Wash and Lake Mead consume about 17,000 acre-feet/year.
- About 70–80% of their yearly use occurs June–September; assuming 80%, about 14,000 acre-feet is needed during summer. Two of the seven gaged years at Halfway Wash fell below 14,000 acre-feet/year.

**Surface water chemistry**
- Base flow about 65 cfs, dominated by [[Littlefield Springs]], plus about 5 cfs from Beaver Dam Wash and Petrified Springs.
- Virgin River TDS rose from 1970 mg/L above Littlefield Spring to 2510 mg/L above the Lake Mead confluence (late October 1992 sampling).
- [[Beaver Dam Wash]] TDS about 370 mg/L; the Virgin River has about ten times the Na and SO4 and eighteen times the Cl of Beaver Dam Wash.
- The Virgin River is sulfate-calcium dominated; Beaver Dam Wash is calcium-bicarbonate dominated.

**Groundwater chemistry**
- Nine Mesquite (Virgin Valley Water District/Mesquite Farmstead) wells, two Bunkerville wells, and the two springs were sampled.
- Well chloride ranged 13.9–125 mg/L versus an average river concentration of 440 mg/L; the river is therefore not the recharge source for the alluvial system.
- Mesquite well TDS 712–1500 mg/L (MF-04 at 3135 mg/L); Bunkerville wells 420–510 mg/L.
- [[Carbon-14 Dating]]: Littlefield and Petrified Springs 19,000–20,000 years; Beaver Dam Wash about 1080 years.

**Groundwater levels and flow**
- Water levels show no appreciable decline; pumping appears within the safe yield of the local system.
- Regional flow runs from recharge areas in the Clover and Beaver Dam mountains toward the Virgin River; elevations range from 5000 ft above MSL in the northeast to 1200 ft at the Lake Mead confluence.
- Faults within the [[Virgin River Depression]] control flow direction and may convey deeper groundwater into the basin.

## Key Findings

**1. Flow estimation at Halfway Wash**
- Linear regression (monthly means, $Y = 21.14 + 0.8109X$, R = 0.98) against Littlefield yields an annual average of 154,000 acre-feet.
- The hydrologic basin model yields 141,000 acre-feet, within about 8% of the regression.
- The regression overpredicts flows below about 100 cfs and underpredicts high flows; it is considered conservative for October–May diversions.

**2. Project diversion model results (700 cfs maximum, October–May operation)**

| Minimum bypass | Average annual diversion |
|---|---|
| 10 cfs | 99,000 acre-feet |
| 25 cfs | 93,000 acre-feet |
| 45 cfs | 83,000 acre-feet |

- A 10 cfs bypass is the lowest considered; it corresponds to USGS flow duration data showing ≥10 cfs about 91% of the time over the October-start year.
- The three driest consecutive years (1989–1991) were modeled. 1991 is the driest year of record, with about 74,000 acre-feet at Littlefield versus 72,600 acre-feet measured.

**3. Hydrologic basin model (Table 10 and Figure 6)**
- Under the 99,000 acre-feet diversion with all Nevada permitted diversions, about 18,000 acre-feet/year still reaches Lake Mead, and phreatophyte needs (about 17,000 acre-feet) are met.
- Basin-wide, the model predicts about 30,000 acre-feet/year lost between Littlefield and Halfway Wash.
- Long-term water budget (Table 9): inflow about 179,000 acre-feet/year (1968 and 1992 comparison), outflow about 54,000 acre-feet, flow to Lake Mead about 125,000 acre-feet.

**4. Key limitations**
- Halfway Wash records are of "Poor" accuracy and were partly synthesized.
- The regression is not reliable for summer, which is a non-diversion period.
- Bypass flows and drought operating rules remain undefined; they depend on floodplain groundwater behavior.

**5. Conclusions**
- The two independent methods agree "in good agreement" given data limits.
- Groundwater contributes roughly 7% of the total basin water budget.
- The Virgin River does not recharge the Bunkerville–Mesquite groundwater system, which is supplied by other sources.
- For low-flow years, phreatophytes may need floodplain groundwater in summer; the 10 cfs bypass is probably sufficient for the downstream ecosystem.
