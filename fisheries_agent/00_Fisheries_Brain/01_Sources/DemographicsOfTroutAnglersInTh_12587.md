## Executive Summary

This paper is **not a morphological or physiological study of fish**, but a **human-dimensions review** synthesizing angler survey data across the [[Rocky Mountain Region]] (Arizona, Colorado, Idaho, Montana, New Mexico, Nevada, Utah, Wyoming). It compiles state-by-state survey results (1982–1993) on angler demographics, preferences, and attitudes toward [[Wild Trout]] versus [[Hatchery-Stocked Trout]] management. The central management tension addressed is balancing put-and-take (planted/hatchery) fisheries against wild trout conservation, framed as background for a "Wild Trout–Planted Trout Symposium." Data sources include the 1991 National Survey of Fishing, Hunting and Wildlife-Associated Recreation and the AFTMA-sponsored Fishing Motivation Study (1990–1993), supplemented by individual state angler opinion surveys.

## Background & Description

Not applicable — this source addresses angler demographics and fisheries management preferences rather than the anatomy, morphology, or physiology of [[Salmonidae]] species. The taxon of focus (generalized "trout," including [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Cutthroat Trout]], and stocked hatchery strains) is treated purely as a managed fisheries resource.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not a biological ecology study, the paper documents **habitat-use patterns by anglers targeting trout**, which indirectly reflects perceived trout habitat associations:

- **Colorado (1991):** Angler use by habitat type — coldwater lakes/reservoirs (60%), coldwater streams (21%), warmwaters (19%).
- **Arizona (1986):** Trout user-days by water type — Lakes (36.5%), Streams and Rivers (13.8%), Large Inland Reservoirs (10.2%), Colorado River Reservoirs (10.2%), Indian Reservation waters (9.4%), Colorado River (6.9%), Large Inland Rivers (4.1%), City Waters (3.7%), Canals (3%), Ponds/Tanks (2.7%), Other (2.0%), Military Base waters (1.1%).
- **Montana (1993):** Current anglers preferred streams over lakes (39.5% vs 24.5%); males favored streams (43.6% vs 19.3%), females favored lakes (38% vs 28.5%).
- **Montana stream anglers (1988):** 73% of fishing time spent on rivers/creeks/spring creeks vs. 24% on lakes.
- **Colorado residents** fish for trout alongside [[Walleye]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] (84.1% trout incidence), while non-residents fish almost exclusively for trout (92.2%).

These patterns suggest coldwater lentic and lotic systems (lakes, reservoirs, streams) are the dominant managed habitats for regional trout fisheries, with strong angler concentration in coldwater habitat types across all states surveyed.

## Key Findings

### Regional Participation & Effort
- Total Mountain Region anglers (1991 National Survey): **3,035,000**; regional participation rate **21%** (vs. national 19%).
- Fishing Motivation Study (1992) estimated **4,023,428** freshwater anglers regionally; household incidence **35%** (vs. national 37%).
- Colorado had the most anglers (778,000); [[Nevada]] the fewest (171,000).
- Days fished per angler ranged from ~6.85 (Nevada) to 7.2 (Colorado) per National Survey; Fishing Motivation Study estimated a much higher 21 days/angler.

### Species Preference
- Trout was the top species sought regionally: **60%** (Table 1), vs. Bass 20%, Catfish 4%, Panfish 3%, Perch 1%.
- Nationally, trout ranked 6th among species by total days fished.
- 12% of anglers nationwide participated in fly-fishing (1991).

### State-Level Highlights
- **Arizona:** Trout preferred by 34.5–39.8% of anglers (1986–1992), consistently outranking [[Largemouth Bass]] (25–32%). Preference for "four 12-inch trout" over fewer larger or more smaller fish (45%).
- **Colorado:** 80–90% of anglers target trout; tackle preference split among bait (31.7%), flies+lures combined (16.5%), bait/flies/lures mixed (28.6%). Only ~1/3 of anglers expressed strong management-concept preference; among those, 51.4% favored [[Wild Fish]] management over put-and-take (30.1%).
- **Idaho:** Median angler age 40.4 years, 91% male; 55% fly fish, 85% bait fish. Strong support (55.8%) for restricting harvest to protect wild trout vs. replacing with hatchery stock (27.4%). Majority favored increased emphasis on wild trout protection (67.3%) alongside continued hatchery production for lakes (54.8%) and streams (60.0%).
- **Montana:** Highest resident license-buying rate in the region; 73% male/27% female anglers. 66.8% preferred trout/salmon fisheries over bass/walleye (10.5%). Top management priority: protecting trout habitat (72%), followed by special regulations (46%) and stocking streams (31%). Angler "cluster" analysis (Generalist 1/2, Occasionist, Specialist) showed differing emphasis on stocking vs. habitat protection (Table 9).
- **New Mexico:** "Stocking more trout" rated very important by 69% of anglers; "stocking bigger trout" 49%; "stocking more warmwater species" 47%.
- **Wyoming:** Public meeting attendees favored trophy/wild fish management and reduced creel limits (fly preference 49.4%); mail-in resident anglers favored sustained-yield management and catchable-sized stocking (bait preference 44.3%). Five angler typologies identified (Wild/Wilderness, Family, Yield, Trophy, Warmwater) with divergent preferences for stocking vs. restrictions.

### Demographic Profile
- Western trout anglers are predominantly **white, male, and over 25 years old**.
- Montana shows the highest reported female participation rate (27%) among surveyed states.
- Urban and minority angler recruitment is identified as dependent on **non-wild, stocked fisheries** rather than wild trout programs.

### Management Implication
- Persistent tension exists between demand for [[Hatchery-Stocked Trout]] (put-and-take) and growing interest in [[Wild Trout]] conservation.
- No state survey adequately quantified anglers' willingness to trade reduced catchable rainbow trout stocking for expanded wild trout fisheries — identified as a key data gap for the Wild Trout–Planted Trout Symposium.
