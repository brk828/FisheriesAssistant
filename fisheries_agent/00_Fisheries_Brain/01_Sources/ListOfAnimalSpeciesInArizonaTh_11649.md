# Federally Listed and Candidate Species of Arizona - Fish and Wildlife Service Status Review (1994)

#ichthyology #herpetology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Ranidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service document from July-September 1994 provides comprehensive listings of federally threatened, endangered, proposed, and candidate species in Arizona. The document includes 54 listed species total (7 proposed, 4 petitioned), with 18 listed fish species and 1 proposed fish. Detailed Listing Priority Number Assignment Forms are provided for multiple fish species and amphibians being added to or reclassified within the candidate system, documenting population declines, habitat threats, and conservation status across Arizona's aquatic ecosystems.

## Background & Description

The document catalogs Arizona's imperiled biodiversity across multiple taxa: 15 listed plants (2 proposed, 2 petitioned), 18 listed fish (1 proposed), 10 listed birds (1 proposed, 1 petitioned), 8 listed mammals (1 proposed), and 2 listed reptiles/amphibians (1 proposed, 1 petitioned). Federal Candidate Category 1 and 2 species are also enumerated, representing taxa warranting listing consideration.

### Fish Species Documented

**Category 2 Additions (1994):**
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) - [[Gila River]] and [[Bill Williams River]] basin populations
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) - [[Cyprinidae]]
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) - lower [[Colorado River]] basin population only
- [[Little Colorado Sucker]] (*Catostomus* sp.) - undescribed species endemic to [[Little Colorado River]] basin
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*) - multiple subspecies across range
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*) - [[Gila River]] and [[Bill Williams River]] basins

**Amphibians:**
- [[Ramsey Canyon Leopard Frog]] (*Rana subaquavocalis*) - newly described 1993, Category 2
- [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] (*Rana chiricahuensis*) - recommended for Category 1

### Morphology and Habitat

[[Speckled Dace]] inhabit fast-flowing, shallow waters with hard substrates. [[Longfin Dace]] occupy shallow runs and pools over sand and gravel. [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and other catostomids utilize pools and riffles in rocky-bottomed streams. [[Ramsey Canyon Leopard Frog]] exists only in two artificial impoundments with total adult population under 100 individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Geographic Distribution

Fish species documented span the [[Colorado River]] basin (upper and lower), [[Gila River]] system, [[Bill Williams River]], [[Virgin River]], and [[Little Colorado River]]. Historic ranges extended into Mexico (Sonora, Chihuahua, Sinaloa). Many species show dramatic range contractions from historic distributions.

### Habitat Requirements

Native fish occupy diverse flowing-water habitats: rapids, runs, pools, riffles in streams with gravel, sand, and rocky substrates. [[Ramsey Canyon Leopard Frog]] inhabits montane streams and pools in southeastern Arizona mountains (Huachuca, Swisshelm ranges). [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] occupies montane streams in southeastern and east-central Arizona, southwestern New Mexico, and Sierra Madre Occidental slopes.

### Threats to Aquatic Ecosystems

**Habitat Destruction (Factor A):**
- Dewatering and diversion of streams
- Groundwater pumping
- Dam construction fragmenting populations
- Channel and watershed erosion
- Ongoing water development projects

**Nonnative Species (Factor C):**
- Introduction and spread of exotic fish causing predation, competition, habitat alteration
- [[Bullfrog]] predation on native amphibians
- Exotic diseases and parasites

**Regulatory Inadequacy (Factor D):**
Existing mechanisms allow continuing decline across all documented species.

**Population Fragmentation (Factor E):**
Isolated populations lack metapopulation dynamics, preventing genetic exchange and recolonization. [[Ramsey Canyon Leopard Frog]] exists at only 2 localities with populations under 100 adults total.

### Land Ownership

Habitats occur on U.S. Forest Service lands ([[Coronado National Forest]], [[Gila National Forest]]), Bureau of Land Management, National Park Service, tribal reservations (San Carlos Apache, White Mountain Apache), state lands, and private/corporate properties.

## Key Findings

### Population Status and Trends

1. **[[Speckled Dace]]** (Gila/Bill Williams populations): Historic and ongoing declines due to habitat loss, nonnative fish, water development. Taxonomy in flux across Columbia River to Gila River basin range.

2. **[[Longfin Dace]]**: "Least deteriorated of all Gila basin native fishes" but large habitat areas destroyed. Form in Gila/Bill Williams/Willcox Playa may be distinctive. Established in [[Mimbres River]] from introductions.

3. **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]**: Lower basin populations severely declined; only [[Virgin River]] and [[Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon]]/lower [[Little Colorado River]] populations remain. Upper basin populations thriving (Region 6 does not support Category 2 for upper basin). Correction needed: 1991 Notice of Review erroneously listed entire species rather than lower basin population only.

4. **[[Little Colorado Sucker]]** (undescribed *Catostomus* sp.): Endemic to [[Little Colorado River]] basin. Reduced in numbers and distribution. Narrow endemic range increases vulnerability. Inhabits flowing pools, feeds in riffles.

5. **[[Desert Sucker]]**: Extirpated or seriously reduced across substantial portions of historic range (White River NV, Meadow Valley Wash NV, Virgin River system, Bill Williams system, Gila River system). Two Nevada subspecies already Category 2 (*C. c. intermedius*, *C. c.* sp.). May comprise several undescribed subspecies.

6. **[[Sonora Sucker]]**: Endemic to Gila and Bill Williams basins. Extirpated or seriously reduced across substantial range portions. Inhabits pools in gravel/rocky-bottomed streams.

7. **[[Ramsey Canyon Leopard Frog]]**: Described 1993. Extant only at 2 artificial impoundments in Ramsey and Brown canyons, [[Huachuca Mountains]]. Total adults <100. Recently extirpated from third Brown Canyon locality. Related undescribed species may occur in Leslie Canyon, [[Swisshelm Mountains]]. Calls underwater (unique behavior). Critical extinction danger due to small population, only 2 sites, dynamic streamside habitats. Likely originated in upper [[San Pedro River]] drainage (no longer supports leopard frogs). Protected on [[Nature Conservancy]]'s Ramsey Canyon Preserve.

8. **[[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]**: Extant at only 2 of 36 historic Arizona localities surveyed in 1980s. Dramatic declines in southeastern Arizona, declining in New Mexico, nearly disappeared from central Arizona. Morphologically distinct forms in southeastern Arizona/Mexico versus central Arizona/New Mexico may represent distinct subspecies or species. Region 2 Herpetological Team recommended Category 1. Disappeared from many apparently pristine localities—mechanisms not fully understood. Small, fluctuating populations in dynamic riparian habitats.

### Conservation Recommendations

- **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** recommended Category 2 status for multiple fish species based on historic/ongoing declines and future threats
- **Region 2 Herpetological Team** recommended [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] move to Category 1
- Ongoing prelisting activities include efforts by USFWS, state agencies (Arizona Game and Fish Department, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish), federal land managers, and private organizations to protect river flows and aquatic habitats
- [[Glen Canyon Dam]] Environmental Studies addressing Colorado River fishes
- Virgin River fish protection and recovery efforts ongoing

### Data Sources

Priority assignment forms cite Desert Fishes Recovery Team expertise, published/unpublished data, status reports. Amphibian data from Platz (1993) species descriptions, Clarkson and Rorabaugh (1989) status surveys, Wood (1991) monitoring, Region 2 Herpetological Team recommendations.

### Sample Sizes and Statistics

- [[Ramsey Canyon Leopard Frog]]: <100 total adults across 2 sites
- [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]: 2 of 36 (5.6%) historic Arizona localities still occupied in 1980s surveys
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]: 2 remaining lower basin populations (Virgin River, Grand Canyon/lower Little Colorado)
