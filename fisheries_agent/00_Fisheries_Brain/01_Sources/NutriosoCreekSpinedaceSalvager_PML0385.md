# Nutrioso Creek Spinedace Salvage-Repatriation Report, May–June 2006

#ichthyology #marine-biology #spinedace

## Executive Summary

In May and June 2006, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) salvaged fish from a reach of [[Nutrioso Creek]] where drought had dried the stream. The reach was home to the [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], a federally [[Threatened Species]] with [[Critical Habitat]] in the creek. Crews captured 767 spinedace, 99 [[Bluehead Sucker]] and 175 [[Speckled Dace]], and moved them to perennial water on the [[EC Bar Ranch]]. Eleven spinedace died during the operation. The report argues that active management will be needed for the population to persist in the long term.

## Background & Description

The [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*) was listed as threatened by the USFWS in 1987, and about 5 miles of [[Nutrioso Creek]] are designated critical habitat (USFWS 1987; USFWS 1998). The study area was the stream reach above [[Nelson Reservoir]], which contained spinedace.

An April 2006 fishery survey of [[Nutrioso Creek]] and [[Rudd Creek]] assessed the native fish assemblage, with special emphasis on spinedace (Carter 2006). Because of drought, the reach above Nelson Reservoir was monitored for water persistence. A site visit on May 30, 2006 found significant drying. The lower end of the reach next to Nelson Reservoir was completely dry. The salvage began May 31, 2006, and [[U.S. Forest Service]] and USFWS personnel were contacted before work started.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods.** Fish were collected by [[Electrofishing]] (Smith-Root LR-24 backpack electrofisher) and by [[Seining]] on U.S. Forest Service land and small private parcels. Single passes were made with both gear types in the lower reach (BEGIN to P-1). Seining was more effective and was the sole capture method from P-2 to END. Deeper pools between P-1 and P-2, some containing many bluehead sucker, were salvaged with single-pass electrofishing only. Some of these pools may be [[Perennial Pools]] able to hold fish through summer, so only part of the spinedace were removed. The reach will continue to be monitored, and further salvage will occur if needed.

Native fish captured were [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Bluehead Sucker]] and [[Speckled Dace]]. [[Fathead Minnow]], a non-native species, was removed from the stream and left on the bank. Fish were handled under the AGFD small-bodied warm-water native fish collection, transport and stocking protocol. All salvaged native fishes were released at three sites on the [[EC Bar Ranch]] (REL-1, REL-2, REL-3), which have perennial habitat. Spinedace mortalities were preserved in ethanol and held at the AGFD [[Pinetop Regional Office]].

**Hydrology and population history.** The report attributes the decline to drying. Spinedace were common in 1994–2000 surveys (Lopez et al. 2001), but 2005 surveys found only 7 spinedace in the reach above Nelson Reservoir (McKell 2005). The report suggests much of [[Nutrioso Creek]] dried at some point between 2000 and 2004, with 2002 a particularly dry year. A wet winter–spring in 2004–2005 probably triggered a successful [[Spawning]] event, producing many more spinedace in 2006. [[Perennial Pools]] and flows that persisted through 2005 and 2006 probably let spinedace spread through the salvaged reach.

## Key Findings

- **Salvage totals (Table 2):** 767 [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], 99 [[Bluehead Sucker]] and 175 [[Speckled Dace]] were salvaged and transported. Eleven spinedace mortalities occurred.
- **Capture by reach and method:**
  - BEGIN–P-2, electrofishing (May 31): 85 spinedace, 2 bluehead sucker, 23 speckled dace, 752 fathead minnow.
  - BEGIN–P-1, seining: 333 spinedace, 32 bluehead sucker, 48 speckled dace, fathead minnow numerous.
  - P-2–END, seining: 349 spinedace, 65 bluehead sucker, 104 speckled dace, fathead minnow numerous.
- **Method:** Seining was more effective than electrofishing in the reach it was used in.
- **Population recovery:** The spinedace population above Nelson Reservoir showed significant [[Recruitment]] between the 2005 and 2006 surveys.
- **Management need:** Because water availability in [[Nutrioso Creek]] is insecure, ongoing active management will probably be needed for spinedace to persist over the long term.
- **Refugia:** A refugia pond for the Little Colorado River / Nutrioso Creek / Rudd Creek spinedace population was being built at the [[Grasslands Wildlife Area]]. It could receive fish as early as fall 2006 and could play an important role in keeping spinedace in the drainage (see [[Refugia]]).
- **Next steps:** Monitor the P-1 to P-2 pools and run further salvages if needed.
