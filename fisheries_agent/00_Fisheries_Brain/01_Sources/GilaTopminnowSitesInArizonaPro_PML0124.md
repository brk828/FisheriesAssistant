# Gila Topminnow Extirpation Report: Arizona Reintroduction Sites, 1996–1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliopsis-occidentalis

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover memorandum and draft technical report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), dated February 1997. It provides a provisional extirpation review of 26 [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction sites in Arizona, based on the 1996–97 field season and historical records from 1975 to 1996. Of the 26 sites, 11 are recommended for restocking or modification and restocking, 10 are judged extirpated with restocking not recommended, and 5 require further surveys before a status decision. The authors ask the [[Recovery Team]] to review the site recommendations and return comments by March 31, 1997, so the results can be submitted to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) for a final status declaration.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) is a small livebearing fish of the Sonoran Desert that was listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Most reintroduction stock came from the [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]] captive population, which originated from [[Monkey Spring]], [[Cocio Spring]] and [[Bylas Springs]]. Sites were stocked between 1975 and 1983, with individual stockings ranging from about 100 to 2,000 fish. Stocking records are incomplete for some sites, such as [[Cow Creek]] (unknown number, 1981) and [[Tule Creek Seep]] (unknown number, 1982).

The memo explains the review criteria. Natural sites with minimal water development were prioritized for recovery. Man-made sites such as tanks and troughs were written off. The team agreed on these concepts at an earlier meeting in August 1996. The report is a draft, funded by the AGFD Heritage Fund, [[Arizona Fishing License]] revenues, and Project E5, Job 09, under Title VI of the Endangered Species Act. A roster of roughly 50 surveyors and collaborators from AGFD, the [[U.S. Forest Service]], the [[Bureau Of Land Management]], USFWS and [[Arizona State University]] is included.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most site failures were linked to a small set of habitat threats:

- **Drying and low water:** Many springs and wells went dry, including [[Unnamed Spring #2]], [[Pasture Well]], [[Fig Spring]], [[Blue Mountain Spring]] and [[Mansfield]]. Water diversion through pipes at [[T.T. Spring]], [[Unnamed (T.T.) Spring]] and [[White Rock Spring]] was a major cause.
- **Flooding:** The January 1993 flood, described as the largest on record for [[Cave Creek]], is believed to have eliminated topminnow at Cave Creek. Flood damage was also reported at [[Bain Spring]], and flash flooding is a concern at [[Camp Creek]] and [[Sheep Creek]].
- **Cattle grazing:** Heavy cattle use, including bank erosion, was noted at many sites, such as [[Bronco Canyon Spring Tank]] and [[Cow Creek]].
- **Vegetation overgrowth:** Dense [[Cattail]] (*Typha*) and tules were a habitat concern at [[Thicket Spring]] and [[Howard Well]].
- **Nonnative species:** [[Green Sunfish]] were found in [[Lime Creek]] and [[Sabino Canyon]]. [[Bullfrog]] and [[Fathead Minnow]] are noted at other sites, and [[Mosquitofish]] were suspected at Howard Well.
- **Physical barriers and temperature:** Steep gradients, waterfalls and cold water limited suitability at [[Josephine Canyon]] and [[Canada Del Oro]].

Co-occurring native fishes were recorded at several sites, including [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Chub]].

## Key Findings

**Site status and recommendations (26 sites):**

- **Restock or modify and restock (11 sites):** T.T. Spring #14A (after water flows are evaluated), [[Thicket Spring]] #15 (after habitat improvements), [[Bain Spring]] #26, [[Cave Creek Site]] #49B, [[Cow Creek]] #72 (Category 1 stream, the population persisted from 1981 to 1991), [[Tule Creek Seep]] #73, [[Howard Well]] #83, [[The Lake]] #121, [[Camp Creek]] #160, [[Lime Cabin Spring]] #248 (restock from Lime Creek), and [[Sabino Canyon]] #250 (after renovation).
- **Extirpated, restocking not recommended (10 sites):** T.T. Spring #14B, [[Unnamed Spring #2]] #29, [[Pasture Well]] #3, [[Paradise Spring]] #65, [[Unnamed Spring #6]] #66, [[Mansfield]] #93, [[Josephine Canyon]] #197, [[Blue Mountain Spring]] #244, [[Fig Spring]] #246 and [[White Rock Spring]] #249.
- **Resurvey required (5 sites):** [[Rock Tank Spring]] #64 and [[Bronco Canyon Spring Tank]] #54 (restocking not recommended in the text despite the resurvey heading), [[Canada Del Oro]] #79, [[Apache Canyon]] #176, and [[Romero Canyon]] #252 (insufficient information).

**Notable population histories:**

- [[Thicket Spring]] (stocked June 3, 1983 with 1,000 fish): about 50 adults and 50–75 young were seen in 1986, but the population was extirpated by 1989.
- [[Pasture Well]]: supported topminnow for at least 4 and possibly 6 years, and was extirpated between 1987 and 1989. The well was completely dry during the last three visits.
- [[Cave Creek Site]] #49B: fish were collected from 1989 through 1992 and likely present since about 1980 after stocking into [[Seven Springs Wash]]. None were collected after December 1992.
- [[Cow Creek]] #72: present from 1981 through 1991, with the last collection in August 1991. The population may also have dispersed downstream into [[Humbug Creek]].
- [[Lime Creek]]: topminnow were abundant in the upstream 2.5 miles of watered channel below the [[Lime Cabin Spring]] confluence, even though the spring itself is extirpated. Green sunfish were found in the lower reaches in 1996.
- [[Sabino Canyon]] #250: 1 male and 1 female were seen in December 1982, six months after stocking with 2,000 fish. No fish have been collected in numerous surveys since 1983.

**Recommended next steps:**

- Recovery Team members should review each site recommendation and respond in writing by March 31, 1997.
- Any additional survey data should be supplied to the author.
- The author will compile responses into final recovery-team recommendations for USFWS.
- Controlling green sunfish is recommended to allow Lime Creek topminnow to expand their range.
- Flood-prone streams such as Cave Creek should be reintroduced repeatedly.
- Signs and restricted public access are recommended at Howard Well.
