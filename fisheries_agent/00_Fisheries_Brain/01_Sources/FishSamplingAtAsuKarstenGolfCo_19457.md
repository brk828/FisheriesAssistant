# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, Autumn 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Center for Environmental Studies]] at [[Arizona State University]] reports autumn 1992 fish sampling at the ASU Karsten Golf Course (ASUGC) lakes in Tempe, Arizona. The survey assessed the status of three native fishes stocked in 1988 after propagation at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]: [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Only five native fish were captured across two sampling dates. Colorado Squawfish was not found, and no juvenile natives were collected, so there was no evidence of recruitment. The catch was dominated by non-native species. Planned follow-up work includes light-trap sampling for larval fish and spring-early summer netting and seining.

## Background & Description

- **Stocking history:** Native bonytail, Colorado squawfish, and razorback sucker were stocked at ASUGC in September-October 1988. Fish from these initial stockings were approximately four years old at sampling.
- **Additional transfer:** On 23 April 1991, 549 PIT-tagged bonytail averaging 381 mm total length (TL) were moved from Dexter to ASUGC.
- **Later transfer:** On 10 November 1992, seven bonytail measuring 230 to 370 mm TL were moved from ASU Animal Care Facilities to ASUGC. These fish came from the original autumn 1988 group and were not PIT-tagged before stocking.
- **Study sites:** [[North Lake]] and [[South Lake]] at ASUGC were sampled on each date.
- **Sampling dates:** 05-06 October and 10 November 1992.

### Methods

- **Entanglement and experimental gill nets** targeted larger fish. They were set at sunset and retrieved after 3 to 6 hours to limit capture stress and mortality.
- **Seining** targeted smaller fish. A 3 x 1.2 m straight seine with 0.3 cm mesh was pulled along the shoreline after dark on 05 October.
- **Handling:** Native fish were measured (TL, mm), weighed (to the nearest 2 g), checked for health, condition, and external parasites, scanned, PIT-tagged if untagged, and released. Non-native fish were removed, except white amur, which ASUGC had stocked. No native fish died during sampling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature:** The authors attribute part of the low native catch to inactivity at cooler autumn water temperatures.
- **Mortality:** Cumulative mortality since stocking may have reduced the number of fish available for capture.
- **Habitat use:** Fish may have concentrated in local areas that the sampling did not cover well.
- **Unknown factors:** Other, unidentified factors may have affected catch rates on the sampling dates.
- **Urban context:** The authors note that Colorado Squawfish has been difficult to collect in other urban lakes.

## Key Findings

- **Native catch was very low.** On 05 October, one razorback sucker was captured. On 10 November, one more razorback sucker and three bonytail were captured. Bonytail accounted for 3 of 131 fish in [[Entanglement Netting]] (Table 1). Colorado Squawfish was not captured.
- **Native fish condition (Table 2):** Five native fish were measured. Three were bonytail, with TL of 277, 363, and 330 mm and weights of 148, 362, and 266 g. Two were razorback suckers, at 335 mm/444 g (ripe) and 328 mm/300 g (emaciated). The text states that none of the native fish was PIT-tagged. Table 2 nevertheless lists PIT tag codes for the bonytail and razorback suckers, so this discrepancy is unresolved.
- **No recruitment evidence:** No juvenile native fish were collected by seining. The authors conclude that, if reproduction occurred, the young did not reach the juvenile stage.
- **Entanglement netting (Table 1):** 131 fish were captured in total, 46 at South Lake and 85 at North Lake. The most common species were [[Common Carp]] (55, 42%), [[Threadfin Shad]] (46, 35%), and [[Green Sunfish]] (16, 12%). Other species included one [[Grass Carp]], five [[Yellow Bullhead]], two [[Yellow Bass]], and one Lepomis hybrid.
- **Seining (Table 3):** Nearly 1,500 specimens of three non-native species were captured on 05 October. The table totals 1,486 fish: 596 from South Lake and 890 from North Lake. [[Red Shiner]] was the most abundant species, followed by threadfin shad and juvenile green sunfish.
  - *Table checks:* The red shiner total is printed as 1,056, but the site counts (290 + 775) sum to 1,065. The percentages in the printed table are also misaligned with their rows. Recalculated from the site counts, red shiner is 71.7% of the seine catch, threadfin shad 10.8%, and green sunfish 17.6%.
- **Explanations for low native catch:** The authors list four possible causes: (1) inactivity at cool autumn temperatures, (2) cumulative mortality since stocking, (3) fish occupying areas that were not sampled effectively, and (4) unknown factors affecting catch rates. They propose that recapturing PIT-tagged fish will help identify the operative cause.
- **Planned work:** [[Light Trap Sampling]] for larval fish is planned for winter-spring. Netting and seining are planned for spring-early summer.
