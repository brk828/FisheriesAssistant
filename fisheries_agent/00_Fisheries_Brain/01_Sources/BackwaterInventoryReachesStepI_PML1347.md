# Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program: Backwater Inventory, Reaches 5 & 6 (Step 1)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #backwater-habitat

## Executive Summary

This September 2007 report from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Lower Colorado Region) documents Step 1 of the five-step backwater screening process under the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (LCR MSCP). Reclamation inventoried 768 backwaters in Reaches 5 and 6, screened them with [[Geographic Information System]] (GIS) analysis and land-use review, and verified open water through a two-day [[Aerial Helicopter Survey]] in December 2006. The process produced 25 candidate backwaters and 6 alternate sites for brief site visits in Step 2. The report makes no habitat suitability conclusions. Its purpose is to generate the candidate list.

## Background & Description

The LCR MSCP planning area includes Lakes Mead, Mohave, and Havasu up to full-pool elevation, plus the historical floodplain of the [[Colorado River]] from Lake Mead to the Southerly International Boundary. The planning area is divided into seven reaches. Reaches 5 and 6 form the first phase of backwater site selection, and Reaches 3 and 4 form the second.

- **Reach 5:** from the Reclamation Cibola Gage (RM 87.3) to [[Imperial Dam]] (RM 49.2).
- **Reach 6:** from Imperial Dam (RM 49.2) to the Northerly International Boundary (RM 23.1).

Backwater creation under the LCR MSCP requires a total of 360 acres in Reaches 3–6. Backwaters may be "connected" (open surface connection to the river) or "disconnected" (no open surface connection). Disconnected backwaters, which are isolated and relatively free of non-native fishes, are preferred for the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. Backwaters for the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] can be ephemeral but must be connected to the river. Up to 85 acres are targeted for flannelmouth sucker in Reach 3, so this report excludes flannelmouth-related sites.

Target species for the study area are razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*), bonytail (*Gila elegans*), and flannelmouth sucker (*Catostomus latipinnis*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Reach 5** holds most of the large backwaters (>1 acre), which the report attributes to water impoundment at Imperial Dam that keeps surface and groundwater levels elevated.
- **Reach 6** has relatively few large backwaters because the channel is narrower than upstream.
- Backwater classification follows [[Prieto Wetland Classification]] (Prieto 1998), which groups wetlands by degree of river connection and resulting water chemistry:
  - **[[Connected Lake]]:** visible surface connection to the river; specific conductivity similar to the river (31,275 µS/cm).
  - **[[True Seep]]:** no surface connection; very high conductivity and salinity relative to the river (23,000 µS/cm); may lie several meters to several hundred meters from the river.
  - **[[Pseudo-Seep]]:** no apparent surface connection; conductivity and salinity similar to the river (generally 31,275 µS/cm, possibly up to 3,000 µS/cm).
- Land ownership is mostly federal: the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service ([[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]]), Reclamation, and the Bureau of Land Management. Some sites belong to the State of California or are privately held. Many candidate sites lie within a designated wilderness area, which would likely prevent new road construction outside the floodplain, so heavy equipment access would be by river only.
- Emergent vegetation coverage in candidate sites most frequently ranged between 10% and 60%. Sites below 10% often showed visible salt rings. Sites with heavier vegetation often lacked open water or were very shallow.

## Key Findings

**Backwater inventory and GIS screening**

- 768 backwaters were identified in the GIS: 555 in Reach 5 and 213 in Reach 6.
- Backwaters were sorted into six size classes (<1, 1–4.99, 5–9.99, 10–19.99, 20–39.99, and ≥40 acres). The 1-acre minimum threshold set by LCR MSCP (2006) removed 672 backwaters (88%).
- Only three sites (<1%) exceeded 40 acres. The remaining sites (96 at or above 1 acre, including those three) were carried forward.

**Aerial survey (December 13–14, 2006)**

- The two-day helicopter survey covered the full extent of Reaches 5 and 6 on both sides of the river during typical winter low flows.
- Day 1 visually inspected selected backwaters for open water, public use, access points, emergent vegetation, and water visibility. Each visit lasted up to about one minute.
- Backwaters with no visible open water during the flights were removed from consideration. All candidate sites selected for evaluation had visible open water.
- Most surveyed backwaters were remote, with no road or trail access. Public use was seen only at backwaters with boat access.

**Water quality and depth**

- Water quality and depth were measured at nine backwaters by helicopter (single reading just below the surface) and at two by boat (profiles at 0.5-meter increments). A Hydrolab minisonde was used, with a grounding wire attached to the helicopter frame to discharge static electricity.
- The mean estimated depth of the 11 backwaters with profiles was 0.9 m (min 0.5 m, max 1.5 m, n = 11).

**Candidate and alternate sites**

- A total of 25 candidate sites and 6 alternate sites were selected for Step 2 site visits in summer 2007.
- The report gives the following acreage figures: Reach 5 has 28 sites (299 acres) and Reach 6 has 3 sites (62 acres). Cumulative acreage is 303 acres for candidate sites and 58 acres for alternates. Sites are split between 17 Arizona sites (160 acres) and 14 California sites (201 acres).
- Table 4 totals (361 acres across Arizona and California, by reach) do not fully match these in-text counts. Readers should check Appendix B and the original tables.

**Selected site highlights (Appendix B)**

- **[[Norton Lake]]** (A69.7a & b, Reach 5, 7.2 ac): two indirectly connected backwaters, classified as pseudo-seep; water clarity reached the bottom.
- **[[Hidden Lake]]** (A67.9, Reach 5, 28.8 ac): two directly connected backwaters (21.9 and 6.9 ac), classified as connected lake.
- **[[Ferguson Lake]]** (C57.6a Northern Ferguson Lake, Reach 5, 22.1 ac): directly connected connected lake; closed to the public November–April for waterfowl habitat; summer water quality declines.
- **[[Headquarters Lakes]]** (A59.7a & b, Reach 5, 12 ac): two directly connected lakes (7 ac and 5 ac) reached by a dredged inlet canal; public access is restricted.
- **C57.6** (Reach 5, 15 ac total): several isolated backwaters classified as [[True Seep]]s with moderately high salinity; site access very limited.
- **[[West Pond]]** (C48.5, Reach 6, 52.1 ac): a true seep with no direct river connection, fed by seepage from the All American Canal and Senator Wash. Razorback suckers were stocked from 1980 to 1983. A 1988 survey found none, but sport fish (largemouth bass, flathead catfish, sunfish) were present.
- **Horseshoe Pond** (C48.2, Reach 6, 5.4 ac): a true seep located between the All American Canal and the road to Yuma Proving Grounds. No documentation was found of razorback sucker stocking.
- **[[Arizona Channel]] backwaters** (e.g., A55.4, A54.3, A53.4, A51.4, A49.2): dredged-channel connected lakes. Water quality appears excellent, with numerous fish visible during the survey. Recreational use is expected to be high where boat access exists.

**Next steps**

- Step 2 brief site visits (1–2 days each) in summer 2007 will collect the data needed to apply the Site Selection Guidelines and identify high-potential sites for habitat creation.
- Classifications are preliminary, based on limited field observations and sampling, and may need refinement after site visits.
