# Lake Mohave Sampling, May 1995

## Executive Summary
A multi-agency cooperative survey (Arizona State University, Bureau of Reclamation, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, National Biological Service, National Park Service) sampled [[Lake Mohave]] over a 4-day period (08-11 May 1995) using trammel nets to target two endangered native Colorado River fishes: [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Sampling occurred in the Cottonwood East area and uplake to Carp Cove. The effort yielded one adult bonytail and five adult razorback suckers, alongside a mix of non-native sportfish and forage species. Three of the five razorback suckers were recaptures with known stocking/tagging histories, providing valuable growth and movement data. The single bonytail was PIT-tagged and transferred live to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]].

## Background & Description
**Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)**
- Sex: Male
- Total Length (TL): 45.9 cm
- Weight: 0.905 kg
- Condition: Good; no distinguishing natural or man-made marks
- Origin: Unknown (wild vs. hatchery-produced could not be determined)
- Marking: PIT tagged (ID: 1F662C2D22)

**Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)** — n=5
- 4 females: average TL 64.7 cm
- 1 male: TL 56.9 cm
- Recapture individual (male) growth record:
  - Juvenile release (Nov 1992, Lake Mohave): 36.4 cm TL, 0.608 kg
  - Recaptured May 1995: 56.9 cm TL (growth of ~20.5 cm TL over ~2.5 years)
- Two female recaptures had prior PIT tag histories from Nine Mile, NV (March 1993) and Arizona Bay, AZ (March 1994)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Sampling targeted depths of 0.5–6 m using six trammel nets, set along points, shoreline, and embayments.
- **Bonytail capture microhabitat**: Captured ~200 m offshore, mid-depth in a 1.8-m-deep net set over a cobble point in ~5 m of water at the northwestern extremity of Cottonwood East. Surface water temperature at time of capture: 20.0°C. Weather was calm during the net set period (1200–0600 hrs MST).
- **Movement/origin data**: Recaptured razorback suckers demonstrate connectivity across the reservoir, including individuals originally tagged/released at Nine Mile (NV), Arizona Bay (AZ), and Yuma Cove backwater, indicating a wide-ranging adult population utilizing multiple embayments within [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Co-occurring species**: Trammel net catch included non-native species such as [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Striped Bass]], and [[Bluegill]], reflecting a mixed native-nonnative fish assemblage typical of lower Colorado River reservoirs.

## Key Findings
- **Rarity of native catch**: Only 1 bonytail and 5 razorback suckers were captured across 30.06 units of trammel net effort (1 unit = 100 m² netting fished for 24 hrs), underscoring the scarcity of these endangered species relative to the broader reservoir fish community.
- **Recapture value**: 3 of 5 (60%) razorback suckers captured were previously tagged individuals, providing direct evidence of long-term survival, growth, and movement within the reservoir system — critical for population monitoring of this endangered [[Catostomidae]] species.
- **Uncertain provenance of bonytail**: The captured bonytail showed no distinguishing marks, leaving its wild vs. hatchery origin undetermined — a recurring challenge in assessing recruitment of naturally-produced individuals in refuge populations.
- **Conservation handling protocol**: The bonytail was transported live in >100 L of fresh lake water via watercraft to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] following PIT tagging, while all razorback suckers were released back into the lake after standard workup — reflecting differing management priorities (ex-situ conservation vs. in-situ population monitoring) for the two species.
- **Sampling design**: Nets were cleared at intervals not exceeding 6 hours (early morning, early evening, near midnight), minimizing stress/mortality risk to captured native fish.
