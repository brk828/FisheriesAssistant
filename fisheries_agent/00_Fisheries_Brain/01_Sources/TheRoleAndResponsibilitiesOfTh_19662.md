## Executive Summary

This document is a policy and administrative overview—not a primary biological study—presented by Joe Webster (Assistant Regional Director, Fisheries and Federal Aid, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) at a workshop on Rocky Mountain trout management. It outlines the legislative authorities, jurisdictional roles, and evolving policy priorities of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] regarding [[Inland Resident Salmonids]] (primarily trout species) in the [[Rocky Mountains]]. The paper emphasizes that the Service functions largely in a *supportive* rather than regulatory capacity for resident fish, deferring management authority to individual States except in cases involving [[Endangered Species Act]] listings or exclusive federal jurisdiction (e.g., [[Yellowstone National Park]]). It traces the historical evolution of Service fisheries policy from ad hoc, congressionally-directed hatchery construction toward a more structured, ecosystem-based conservation approach emphasizing native species protection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not a biological/ecological research paper, the document addresses the *institutional ecology* of salmonid management:

- **Geographic scope**: Resident (non-anadromous) salmonid populations across the eight states of USFWS Region 6, concentrated in the [[Rocky Mountains]] (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming, Montana, and surrounding states).
- **Habitat types referenced**: Rivers, streams, reservoirs, and impounded waters subject to federal water development projects (dams, diversions), as regulated under the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]].
- **Federal hatchery facilities** functioning as artificial propagation habitats supporting wild population mitigation:
  - [[Jones Hole National Fish Hatchery]] (Utah) — mitigation facility tied to the [[Colorado River Storage Project Act]]
  - [[Hotchkiss National Fish Hatchery]] (Colorado) — mitigation facility, Colorado River Storage Project Act
  - [[Jackson National Fish Hatchery]] (Wyoming) — established via the [[Palisades Project Act]]
  - [[Leadville National Fish Hatchery]] (Colorado) — historic facility, operational since the 1890s
  - Original [[Bozeman National Fish Hatchery]] (Montana) — historic facility, 1890s origin
  - Several broodstock facilities producing trout eggs for federal/state hatchery distribution nationwide
- **Associated technical support**: [[Bozeman Fish Technology Center]] (aquaculture efficiency research) and cooperative fish health programs with State agencies.
- **Jurisdictional ecological note**: [[Yellowstone National Park]] represents a unique niche of *exclusive federal jurisdiction*, where the National Park Service—not USFWS or the States—holds full regulatory authority over fish and wildlife populations.

## Key Findings

1. **Legislative Foundations**: The Service's fisheries authority derives from multiple acts, including the [[Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956]], [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] (1934/1946), [[Sikes Act]], [[Refuge Recreation Act of 1962]], [[Endangered Species Act]], the [[Lacey Act]], and the [[Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration Act]] (including the 1984 [[Wallop-Breaux Amendment]]).

2. **Division of Authority**: The USFWS is generally *not* a management/regulatory agency for resident fish; this authority rests with individual States. Exceptions occur for:
   - Species formally listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]
   - Areas of exclusive federal jurisdiction (e.g., Yellowstone National Park)
   - Habitat management on National Wildlife Refuges (Service manages habitat; States regulate harvest and populations)

3. **Current Service Activities in the Rocky Mountains (Region 6)**:
   - Operation of **7 national fish hatcheries** across CO, UT, WY, and MT for mitigation, broodstock, and cooperative state management programs.
   - Operation of **6 Fishery Assistance stations** providing technical support to Tribal governments and other agencies.
   - Maintenance of **Ecological Services offices** in each Rocky Mountain state, handling NEPA compliance, Clean Water Act issues, and ESA-related consultation.
   - Historical involvement in **fisheries research**, recently consolidated into the newly formed [[National Biological Survey]].
   - Administration of the **Federal Aid (Dingell-Johnson/Wallop-Breaux) program**, distributing approximately $23 million in 1994 to the 8 states of Region 6, substantially benefiting trout programs.

4. **Policy Evolution**:
   - Pre-1980s: Fisheries programs largely driven by congressional appropriations rather than strategic planning.
   - Early-mid 1980s: Adoption of the "Statement of Responsibilities and Role," focusing on restoration of depleted resources, mitigation for federal water development, fishery management on federal/Indian lands, and scientific leadership. Coincided with transfer of **16 federal hatcheries** to State operation.
   - 1988: Service became a signatory to the **National Recreational Fisheries Policy**, reaffirming commitment to recreational fisheries conservation.
   - 1991: Development of the Service's "**Vision for the Future**," emphasizing partnerships, habitat protection, hatchery/wild stock compatibility, and program evaluation.
   - Ongoing (as of writing): Draft **National Fisheries Action Plan**, prioritizing native species conservation, partnership building, public education, judicious hatchery use, and long-term ecosystem health.

5. **Future Directions Identified**:
   - **Ecosystem Approach**: Under Director [[Mollie Beattie]], the Service is shifting toward ecosystem-level management rather than single-species focus, potentially decentralizing authority from Regional Offices to field-level operations.
   - **Conservation of Native Species**: Proactive measures aimed at preventing future ESA listings ("trainwrecks"), referencing precedents like the [[Spotted Owl]] and [[Pacific Salmon]] controversies, per direction from Secretary [[Bruce Babbitt]].
   - **Hatchery System Review**: A 1994 congressionally mandated comprehensive policy review of the National Fish Hatchery System, conducted by the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] with input from States, academia, and the aquaculture industry — explicitly not intended as a "hatchery bashing" or divestiture exercise.

6. **Author's Outlook**: The presenter anticipates that despite institutional shifts toward ecosystem management and native species prioritization, core traditional hatchery programs supporting State mitigation and management needs will likely be preserved.
