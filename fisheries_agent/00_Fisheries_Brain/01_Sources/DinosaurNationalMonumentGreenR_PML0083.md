# Dinosaur National Monument Green River Fish Collections (1962)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (Fishery Management Biologist [[Robert L. Azevedo]], [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]], Albuquerque, dated November 9, 1962) reports a fish sampling survey of the [[Green River]] within [[Dinosaur National Monument]] conducted September 24 to October 5, 1962. The survey followed reports that a passage of toxic materials had caused moderate fish mortality. Crews from the federal office, the [[Utah Fish and Game Department]], and the [[Wyoming Fish and Game Department]] used [[Gill Net]], [[Fyke Net]] and [[Electrofishing]] gear at several sites. The memo is a continuation of an earlier Fishery Management Report (September 27, 1962). It also summarizes a July 1962 checklist and compares fish catches before and after the [[Rough Fish Control]] (rotenone) program.

## Background & Description

- **Trigger:** Preliminary investigations on September 14, 1962 indicated toxic material had moderately affected certain fishes.
- **Coordination:** Surveys were arranged after discussions on September 15, 1962 among the Bureau, the [[National Park Service]] and the Utah Fish and Game Department at Split Mountain Campground.
- **Participants:** A Wyoming fishery biologist joined the Utah State crew. Park Service staff at Park Headquarters (Vernal, Utah) were notified, and special airboat and collector's permits were obtained.
- **Sampling sites:** [[Split Mountain Canyon]], the [[Yampa River]] at [[Echo Park]] (confluence with the Green River), the Green River at Echo Park, and [[Brown's Park]] (below the detoxification station) with [[Lodore Canyon]] downstream. Brown's Park and Island Park lie above or within the monument.
- **Equipment problems:** On September 25 the airboat struck a submerged rock and was punctured. Temporary repairs were made. A heavy night rain on October 4–5 raised the river level and collapsed a net.

The document also includes a July 16–23, 1962 species checklist and three appendices (collection log, catch-per-effort table and the before/after comparison).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Species composition (July 16–23, 1962 checklist, Utah Cooperative Fishery Unit, Utah State University):** The Green River between Flaming Gorge Dam and the lower monument boundary was sampled at about eight locations (for example Vernilion Creek, Pot Creek, Jones Hole and Split Mountain). Listed natives include [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Mottled Sculpin]]. Listed exotics include [[Common Carp]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Walleye]] and [[Redside Shiner]]. The memo gives the list as stated and does not give a total count.

**Environmental conditions:**
- Low water volume in late September made gear more effective and increased sampling success. The memo notes it is unknown whether more individual fish were present at that time or were concentrated at sampling sites.
- Navigation through rapids was dangerous. Gill nets could not be safely rechecked far upstream.
- Turbid water from heavy rain prevented fish capture in the Brown's Park area downstream to Lodore Canyon.
- Sampling in July 1962 was difficult due to the sediment and debris load carried by the Green River.

**Rough fish control context:** Appendix B lists "BEFORE ERADICATION" and "AFTER ERADICATION" collections, and the memo compares species captured before and after the program. Appendix C lists sampling gear and effort before and after the program.

## Key Findings

**1. Post-toxicant survey, September 24 – October 5, 1962**

- **September 25, Split Mountain Canyon:** A 125-foot gill net (14-hour set) yielded channel catfish, bluehead sucker and bonytail.
- **September 26, Split Mountain Canyon:** A gill net and fyke net set below the campground (15-hour set) yielded channel catfish, flannelmouth sucker, carp and squawfish.
- **October 2, Echo Park:** Two gill nets (15-hour set) in the Yampa River captured razorback sucker and carp. An electrofisher in the Yampa River near the confluence collected only fingerlings: redside shiner, speckled dace, sucker, humpback chub and carp.
- **October 3, Echo Park:** A Yampa River gill net (21.5 hours) yielded bluehead sucker. A Green River gill net at the confluence (21.5 hours) yielded squawfish. A 4x4 fyke trap was reset below Split Mountain Canyon.
- **October 4, below campground:** An 11-hour trap-fyke set yielded channel catfish and bonytail.
- **October 5:** No fish were collected after heavy rain.

**2. Utah and Wyoming survey, September 21–27, 1962 (below the Brown's Park detoxification station):** Seines and gill nets were used. Species taken included channel catfish, bonytail, carp, redside shiner, green sunfish and roundtail chub. No fish were taken in the Brown's Park area downstream to Lodore Canyon because of turbid river conditions from heavy rain.

**4. Before-and-after rough fish control comparison:** The memo compares species captured in the Green River before and after the program at Brown's Park, Split Mountain and Echo Park. Before the program, the Green River at Echo Park and Split Mountain yielded bluehead sucker, bonytail (roundtail and humpback forms), channel catfish, squawfish, carp and walleye. The memo states that the post-program catches were "not taken" for several species. Exact counts for each comparison are not given in the text.

**5. Data limitations:** The memo says it represents an effort to determine species composition rather than population numbers. Sampling effort varied, and the catch figures reflect unusually high or low water conditions.

**6. Conservation significance:** Bonytail, Colorado squawfish, humpback chub and razorback sucker are native Green River species recorded in the survey. Their presence alongside exotic species is a key concern for the [[Rough Fish Control]] program.
