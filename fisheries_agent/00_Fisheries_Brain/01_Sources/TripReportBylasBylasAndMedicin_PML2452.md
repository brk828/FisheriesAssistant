# Bylas Springs Barrier Trip Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Ecological Services office, written by [[Sally Stefferud]], reports a field inspection on August 19, 1990 of a fish barrier at [[Bylas Springs]]. The barrier was recently repaired by the office. The inspection followed heavy rains the previous week. The author found that the barrier was leaking at its front and through or under its west-side [[Gabion Barrier]] wing. Concern is that high flows could breach the structure, allowing [[Gambusia Affinis|mosquitofish]] to move upstream past it. The memo recommends further repair work and notes that a cost figure in the text is partly illegible.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal trip report (memorandum), not a research study.
- **Author:** Sally Stefferud, Fish and Wildlife Biologist.
- **Addressee:** Field Supervisor.
- **Subject:** Status of the Bylas (Bylas and Medicine) springs barrier.
- **Route:** The author visited Bylas and Medicine Springs and then [[Salt Creek]]. Time was limited, so only the barrier was inspected at Bylas.
- **Copies to:** S.L. Minckley, Paul Marsh, Francisco Abarca, Stewart Jacks, and one further recipient whose name is illegible in the scan.
- **Structure:** The barrier is built largely from gabions (wire-mesh baskets of rock). Cloth was used on the upstream face of the gabions during the repair.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Purpose of the barrier:** The structure is intended to stop nonnative [[Gambusia Affinis|mosquitofish]] from moving upstream into the springs. The document implies this purpose but does not describe the native fauna being protected.
- **Hydrologic conditions:** Heavy rains and flooding occurred in the area during the week before the visit. The author notes that flows could go over or around the barrier.
- **Observed leakage:** Water leaks at the front of the barrier and through or under the west gabion wing. The leak under the wing is not visible at the surface, but running water can be heard. Water surfaces about 5 feet from the barrier are described as lower than the water level behind it.
- **Fish behavior:** Mosquitofish were seen gathering in the apron area below the barrier, which the author describes as typical behavior at this site. The author suggests they may be attempting to pass upstream through the leaks.
- **Management setting:** The site is near the [[San Carlos Apache Reservation]] area, and the text refers to a recreation and wildlife context, though the scan is partly garbled.

## Key Findings

1. **The barrier is leaking.** Leakage occurs at the front of the structure and through or under the west gabion wing. The author recommends further action.
2. **Repair status:** The recent repair, which included cloth on the gabion face, held up during the inspection. The author did not see damage from the previous week's rains. The text does not specify how much the repair held up under flood flows.
3. **Breach risk:** The author judges that high flows will probably breach the barrier. This would let mosquitofish move upstream, which is the central management concern.
4. **Fish passage:** Mosquitofish congregating below the barrier suggest the fish are actively testing the structure. The author links this to the leakage.
5. **Recommendations (partly illegible):** The author states that the barrier either needs repair or replacement. The text references a cost figure of roughly $10,000 that cannot be read reliably in the scan. The memo may also discuss an alternative structure, but the relevant passage is too degraded to confirm.
6. **Scope limits:** This is an observational trip report. It contains no quantitative fish counts, water-quality data, or sample sizes.
