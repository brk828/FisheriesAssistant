# Fishes of the Miocene-Pliocene Deer Butte Formation, Southeast Oregon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #catostomidae #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae #cottidae

## Executive Summary

This paleontological study describes the fossil fish fauna of the [[Deer Butte Formation]] in Malheur County, Oregon. The fauna comprises about 24 species in 15 genera, found as disarticulated fossils in loosely consolidated siltstones, sandstones and conglomerates. The author interprets the sediments as a flood basin or lake-edge deposit rather than a deep lacustrine one. The fauna is most closely related to the younger [[Glenns Ferry Formation]] fauna of [[Lake Idaho]] in adjacent Idaho. The paper names several new taxa, including the salmonine genus *Paleolox* and the species [[Paleolox Larsoni]], [[Rhabdofario Carinatum]], [[Orthodon Onkognathus]] and [[Cottus Calcatus]]. It also argues that the Blackjack Basalt should be treated as a member of the Deer Butte Formation rather than a synonym of the [[Owyhee Basalt]].

## Background & Description

The fossils were collected from five localities between 1962 and 1975, especially in summer 1974: Tunnel Road, Tunnel Mountain, Desert Pavement, Shenk Ranch and Blackjack. Elevations range from about 2,540 to 3,200 ft. Four localities form a stratigraphically similar group, bracketed by white, gray and yellow ash beds. The Blackjack locality lies above a basalt flow on Blackjack Butte and is stratigraphically higher. The formation's name has shifted over time: Bryan (1929) used Payette Formation, Corcoran et al. (1962) used Chalk Butte Formation, and Kittleman et al. (1967) used Deer Butte Formation, which the author adopts as the most comprehensive current usage.

No radiometric or mammalian age determinations exist for these beds. The upper Deer Butte Formation may correlate with the [[Poison Creek Formation]], dated at about 11 million years by K-Ar analysis of an overlying basalt (Armstrong et al., 1975). The fauna is described as Miocene to Pliocene, with mammal faunas in other areas of Barstovian (upper Miocene) age.

The paper is organized taxonomically. Each new species is given a holotype, description, diagnosis and remarks, with comparisons to modern and Glenns Ferry taxa. Diagnostic characters are mainly skeletal, especially maxillae, premaxillae, dentaries, pharyngeal arches, preopercles and pectoral spines.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Depositional setting.** Sediments range from siltstone to pebble conglomerate, with petrified wood and rare terrestrial fossils. Articulated fish in siltstones suggest rapid deposition, while conglomerates indicate strong currents at times. The author concludes the environment was a flood basin or lake edge, not a large deep lake. Conglomerates thicken northward toward Brown Butte near Adrian, Oregon, which suggests a large fluvial system and perhaps proximity to the outlet of Lake Idaho.

**Ecological tension.** The fish fauna itself suggests a lacustrine habitat. Its diversity is higher than expected for a fluvial system at about 3,000 ft and 43° N, and three predators approach 1 m in length. Geological evidence, however, points to fluvial conditions. The author reconciles this by comparing the fauna with Glenns Ferry localities. The Hagerman floodplain section lacks typical lacustrine genera, while the Sand Point locality, also fluvial, contains them along with a high proportion of catfish.

**Trophic structure.** The large predators include [[Paleolox Larsoni]], [[Rhabdofario Carinatum]] and [[Ptychocheilus Arciferus]], the largest cyprinid, which reached about 4 ft. Herbivores include [[Acrocheilus Latus]] and *Orthodon* species. Molluscivores include [[Mylocheilus Inflexus]] and [[Mylocheilus Robustus]]. Catostomids were benthic small-particle omnivores. [[Ictalurus Vespertinus]] is thought to have been a medium-sized nocturnal omnivore, scavenger and carnivore. [[Archoplites Taylori]] was a medium-sized predator. Cottids were small benthic crustacean predators. Glenns Ferry species absent from the lower Deer Butte are mostly planktivores or midwater carnivores, which may reflect either the depositional environment or the evolutionary stage of the fauna.

**Paleodrainage.** The affinities of *Cottus* and *Ptychocheilus* point to connections with the Pit, Klamath, Sacramento and Columbia drainages. These connections are consistent with the paleodrainage theory of [[Wheeler and Cook]] (1954), in which Lake Idaho drained before Hells Canyon was cut.

## Key Findings

- **Fauna composition:** About 24 species in 15 genera across six families: Salmonidae, Catostomidae, Cyprinidae, Ictaluridae, Centrarchidae and Cottidae. Cyprinids are the most diverse, with about eight species in five genera.
- **Salmonids:** Three genera and at least three species. *Paleolox larsoni* is a new genus and species, estimated at 1,200–1,300 mm standard length from maxillary length (150 mm), making it among the largest known salmonids. *Rhabdofario carinatum* was about 900–1,000 mm. The fauna also includes a large extinct whitefish, *Prosopium prolixus*, which occurs only in the higher Blackjack local fauna.
- **Suckers:** About five *Catostomus* species, including *C. cristatus*, *C. owyhee* and *C. shoshonensis*, which are abundant. *Chasmistes spatulifer* occurs only in the Blackjack fauna, represented by two dentaries, possibly one individual.
- **Catfish:** *Ictalurus vespertinus* is the only catfish. Deer Butte specimens are more abundant, larger and more variable than Glenns Ferry ones. The author notes that *I. peregrinus* may be a local variant of *I. vespertinus*. Examination of 54 pectoral spines from Tunnel Mountain showed continuous, not bimodal, variation with age-related characters.
- **Cyprinids:** *Orthodon onkognathus* is new, distinguished by four sensory pores and robust, short dentaries, and known from 14 specimens at the Tunnel Road and Tunnel Mountain localities. *Ptychocheilus arciferus* is the most abundant large predator. Its presence suggests a connection between Lake Idaho and the Klamath Basin before the early Pliocene and possibly before the late Miocene.
- **Sculpins:** Less common than in the Glenns Ferry Formation, possibly because of taphonomy, sediment coarseness or fragility. Species include the new *Cottus calcatus*, *Kerocottus divaricatus* (earliest record, from Blackjack), and an intermediate *Myoxocephalus* sp. that may be ancestral to two Glenns Ferry species.
- **Stratigraphy:** The Blackjack local fauna differs from the others by several species, and its similarity to the Glenns Ferry fauna supports the idea of a later Lake Idaho stage. *Prosopium prolixus* may serve as a stratigraphic marker.
- **Blackjack Basalt:** Should be regarded as a member of the Deer Butte Formation, not a synonym of the Owyhee Basalt, because its stratigraphic relations differ.
- **Evolution:** Several Deer Butte species may be ancestral to Glenns Ferry forms, including *Rhabdofario carinatum* (to *R. lacustris*) and *Myoxocephalus* sp. The faunal change between the Deer Butte and Glenns Ferry is smaller than between the Glenns Ferry and Recent faunas.
- **Naming:** *Paleolox* means "ancient salmon." *Paleolox larsoni* honors Gerald P. Larson, a stratigrapher who died in a field accident in 1974.
- **Dating gap:** No absolute dates exist for the fish beds. Fission-track dating of the associated ash beds is proposed to test the correlation with the Poison Creek Formation.
