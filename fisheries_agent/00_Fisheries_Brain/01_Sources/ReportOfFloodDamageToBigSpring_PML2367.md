# Flood Damage to the Big Spring Gila Topminnow Population (1990 Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliopsis-occidentalis

## Executive Summary

This memorandum, issued by [[Jeffrey R. Simms]], Fishery Biologist at the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] [[Safford District]] Office, reports flood damage to the reintroduced [[Gila Topminnow]] population at [[Big Spring]], Graham County, Arizona. A storm on August 21, 1990 caused flooding that damaged the dam, pools, riparian vegetation, and fish habitat. Inspection on August 24 found only one Gila topminnow. The memo evaluates restoration options: dredging sediment and possibly restocking with [[Bylas Springs Topminnow]], or abandoning the site for topminnow recovery. The cover letter, dated September 26, 1990, sends the memo to [[Paul Marsh]] at the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies. The memo was distributed to several university, federal, and state contacts.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*). The stock used at Big Spring came from [[Monkey Springs]].
- **Site:** Big Spring is one of 5 reintroduction sites in the [[Safford District]]. The population was introduced in 1985.
- **Infrastructure:**
  - A dam built in 1984 created pool habitat using spring flow. Its maximum pool size when filled was approximately 30 ft long and 8 ft wide. The depth figure is not legible in the source scan.
  - A 5.3-acre [[Exclosure Fence]] was built to protect the developing [[Riparian Habitat]]. The source gives the year as 1980 and also describes native trees and plants planted inside it.
  - A masonry dam that preceded the current one failed in a 1982 flood. The present dam was engineered to be substantially stronger.
- **Habitat layout:** Three main pools held topminnows: a large [[Plunge Pool]] at the spring source, a second pool about 150 feet downstream created by the dam, and a third pool in the plunge area below the dam.
- **Prior performance:** The system withstood floods for 5 years, though the pool behind the dam gradually filled with sediment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flood event:** The August 21, 1990 storm was intense and localized in the [[Gila Mountains]]. It trapped two people in Bonita Creek, damaged crops and homes, and caused one fatality near Safford. Big Spring showed no damage during a July 24 visit, but the August 24 inspection found damage.
- **Physical damage:**
  - Large boulders were moved onto the dam apron downstream.
  - The eastern abutment was eroded.
  - Trees were knocked over or uprooted.
  - Most emergent vegetation was removed.
  - The exclosure fence was blown open.
  - The livestock watering system was damaged.
- **Fish population impact:** Only one Gila topminnow was found, in a small pool with tea-colored water just downstream of the dam. Two of the three pools that once held many topminnows were filled with coarse sediment. The headwater pool survived, at least partly, because an overhanging ledge at its upstream end scours it with plunge action.
- **Drainage stability:** The memo describes the site's drainage as unstable, which is a central factor in the decision about future topminnow recovery.

## Key Findings

1. **Damage was extensive but not total.** The dam remains stable, though one abutment needs repair. Water still flows from the spring, and the headwater pool could again support topminnows.
2. **Population loss was severe.** Only one Gila topminnow was documented after the flood, and two of three pools were buried in sediment.
3. **Restoration options were presented:**
   - Dredge the sediment that filled the pool behind the dam using a backhoe.
   - If desired, restock with Bylas Springs strain topminnows.
   - Alternatively, abandon Big Spring for topminnow recovery because of the unstable drainage.
4. **Wildlife restoration is planned regardless.** The site will be restored for other wildlife species whatever the topminnow decision.
5. **Distribution:** The memo was sent to [[Arizona State University]] zoology staff, the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] Phoenix Ecological Services office, the Phoenix District Office, and [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] personnel.
