# The Age and Growth of Bluegill (Lepomis macrochirus) at Cachuma and Lower Otay Reservoirs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 California Department of Fish and Game administrative report presents comparative age and growth data for [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) populations in two contrasting Southern California warmwater reservoirs: the large, deep [[Cachuma Reservoir]] and the small, shallow [[Lower Otay Reservoir]]. The study, part of a broader initiative to document game fish age and growth across Southern California reservoirs for management purposes, used scale analysis (annuli counts and back-calculation) to determine age and growth rates. Bluegill from Lower Otay consistently grew faster and larger at each age than those from Cachuma. Growth at both reservoirs fell within the range reported for other California and U.S. waters, with Cachuma below average and Lower Otay above average nationally. Length-weight relationships and condition factors were similar between the two reservoirs and comparable to other studied waters in the Midwest and California.

## Background & Description

- **Species**: [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), family [[Centrarchidae]]
- **Age determination**: Based on scale annuli counts (winter rings); no other environmental marks were observed on scales from either reservoir.
- **Scale sampling location**: Collected just above the lateral line, below the origin of the dorsal fin, left side of fish; key scales not selected, sexes not separated (no fish observed to be sexually mature).
- **Body length–scale length relationships** (linear regression, Whitney & Carlander 1956 method):
  - **Cachuma** (fish 2.1–8.5 in): y = 1.12 + (−0.046x); correlation r = 0.93
  - **Lower Otay** (fish 1.8–7.9 in): y = 0.60 + 0.053x; correlation r = 0.95
- **Length-weight relationships** (log W = log a + b log L), computed via IBM 7090 program:

| Cachuma | 1963 | log W = −3.36219 + 3.26888 log L |
| Cachuma | 1964 | log W = −2.97811 + 2.79077 log L |
| Cachuma | 1963–64 combined | log W = −3.09945 + 2.93030 log L |
| Lower Otay | 1963 | log W = −3.34848 + 3.29985 log L |
| Lower Otay | 1964 | log W = −3.17655 + 3.14486 log L |
| Lower Otay | 1963–64 combined | log W = −3.28817 + 3.23984 log L |

- **Condition factor (Coefficient of Condition, Cc)** — Lagler (1956) method:
  - Cachuma: 1963 avg. = 0.68; 1964 avg. = 0.73; combined 1963–64 avg. = 0.70
  - Lower Otay: 1963 avg. = 0.72; 1964 avg. = 0.85; combined 1963–64 avg. = 0.76
  - Condition rated as "average" relative to Illinois, Iowa, and Minnesota bluegill populations (Carlander).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Cachuma Reservoir** (large, deep):
- Located on the Santa Ynez River, 25 mi NE of Santa Barbara; watershed = 421 sq mi
- Max depth: 190 ft; max storage: 205,000 acre-feet (3,250 acres); study period max volume: 170,000 acre-feet
- Steep-sided, rocky shoreline; grassy terrain with oak as climax vegetation
- Water temp range: ~53°F (Jan/Feb) to 80°F (Aug); thermally/chemically stratified in summer with hypolimnetic oxygen depletion
- Managed by U.S. Bureau of Reclamation for irrigation, municipal supply, recreation, flood control
- **Co-occurring species**: [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), [[Arroyo Chub]] (*Gila orcuttii*), [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Brown Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus nebulosus*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui*), [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), [[Riffle Sculpin]] (*Cottus gulosus*)

**Lower Otay Reservoir** (small, shallow):
- Located on the Otay River, 20 mi SE of San Diego; watershed = 98 sq mi
- Max depth: 120 ft; max capacity: 56,519 acre-feet (1,266 acres); 60-month average volume: 3,480 acre-feet
- Gradually sloping sides lined with tules (*Scirpus* spp.); chaparral (*Ceanothus cuneatus*) as climax vegetation
- Water temp range: ~51°F (Jan) to low 80s°F (Aug); thermally/chemically stratified with anoxia below thermocline
- Managed by City of San Diego for recreation and municipal supply; not open to fishing during sampling
- **Co-occurring species**: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[White Crappie]], [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*), [[Brown Bullhead]], [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*), [[Channel Catfish]], [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*), [[Green Sunfish]]

## Key Findings

- **Growth comparison between reservoirs**: Bluegill at Lower Otay grew consistently faster than at Cachuma at every age class.
  - Mean total lengths (Cachuma vs. Lower Otay), Ages I–IV: 1.9 vs 2.2 in; 3.6 vs 4.5 in; 5.3 vs 6.0 in; 5.9 vs 7.6 in
  - Both populations exhibited slow first-year growth, a common pattern in bluegill.
  - Growth trends were "somewhat erratic" but generally declined by year class since 1959 (Figure 1).

- **Regional comparisons** (Table 5):
  - Cachuma growth was slower than [[Clear Lake]], [[Lake Havasu]], and [[Sutherland Reservoir]] (California), but faster than [[Folsom Lake]], California and [[Lake Ahquabi]], Iowa.
  - Lower Otay growth exceeded Folsom Lake but was slower than three other California waters (Clear Lake, Lake Havasu, Sutherland Reservoir).
  - Cachuma growth was slightly below the average reported for most U.S. bluegill populations; Lower Otay was slightly above average.

- **Length-weight relationships**: Nearly identical between the two reservoirs and comparable to those reported for [[Sutherland Reservoir]] bluegill, Clear Lake (Iowa) bluegill ("Ike Lake"), and Mississippi River bluegill populations (Carlander 1950, 1953).

- **Condition factor**: Both reservoirs' bluegill showed "average" condition relative to Illinois, Iowa, and Minnesota populations; Lower Otay fish showed generally higher condition values than Cachuma fish across sampled years.

- **Sampling methodology**: Fish were collected via seine (80-ft bag or 300-ft beach seine), midwater trawl (18×10×5 ft), boat-mounted electrofishing, rotenone, and hook-and-line (Cachuma only, via angler creel surveys); approximately 12 fish per inch-class were targeted per year per reservoir.

- **Data quality**: Approximately 5% of scale samples were rejected due to inter-reader disagreement on age; ages were corroborated by a second staff biologist to ensure reliability.

- **No sexual maturity observed**: All bluegill sampled during the study were immature, suggesting sampling may have been biased toward younger/smaller size classes or that maturity onset in this system occurs at larger sizes/older ages than sampled.

- **Environmental scale marks**: No environmental checks (false annuli) were noted on scales from either reservoir, simplifying age determination via consistent identification of winter annuli.
