# Eye Degeneration in Two Cave Forms of Astyanax mexicanus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This German-language study by Nikolaus and Gabriele Peters (Zoologisches Staatsinstitut Hamburg, 1966) compares the eye rudiments of blind, unpigmented cave forms of [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] from two isolated limestone caves in central Mexico, the [[Pachon Cave]] and the [[Sabinos Cave]]. Both are reference points for the population in [[Cueva Chica]], which is a hybrid population. The authors ask whether eye degeneration stops once rapid growth ends, how variable the rudiments are within and between populations, and what genetic processes explain the differences. They conclude that degeneration has stopped in adults, that the rudiments are largely homozygous at the genetic level, and that the three cave populations reached similar but genetically distinct end states.

## Background & Description

The surface form of [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] is a sighted, pigmented characin that feeds on plankton, schools, and hunts by sight. Blind cave forms occupy separate limestone caves in central Mexico, with a maximum separation of about 90 km between caves. Earlier taxonomy had split these cave fish into several species, but crossing experiments showed unrestricted interbreeding among all blind populations and with the surface form, so the authors treat them as one species.

Eye size in the surface fish was used as the reference standard. The surface eye bulb measures 4300 µm proximo-distally and 5100 µm dorso-ventrally. The retina averages 173 µm in cross-section. Over an 80 µm stretch the authors counted 12 cones and 124 rods. The lens is about 2400 µm in diameter, with a strong lens muscle. The authors describe the surface fish's eye as highly acute, with a high rod density suited to dim light.

**Material and methods.** Sabinos fish were wild-caught in 1964. Pachon fish came from laboratory stocks maintained for several years. The sample comprised:

- 10 adult Sabinos fish
- 10 adult one-year-old Pachon fish
- 4 two-year-old Pachon fish
- 2 adult surface fish
- 10 adult F2 hybrids (surface × Pachon)

Heads were fixed in Bouin's solution, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned transversely at 10 µm. Sections were stained with hematoxylin/picric acid/thiazine red or with Azan. Size measurements were taken from median sections through the largest diameter of each eye or rudiment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Earlier work on cave characins (Breder and Gresser, 1941; Kuhn and Kanneng, 1954; Lilling, 1953, 1955) focused mainly on the Cueva Chica population, which shows wide variation in both eye development and pigmentation. Across the caves, the authors note that all transitions from sighted to blind individuals occur, and that eye development and pigmentation vary independently. The authors argue that the Cueva Chica population is a hybrid, probably formed when surface fish from the nearby [[Rio Tampaon]] re-entered the cave and interbred with a formerly uniformly blind population. Its variability therefore does not reflect ongoing degeneration.

Developmental studies (Lilling, 1955; Cahn, 1958) showed that cave embryos have smaller initial eye primordia. Growth of the eye lags as they age, some structures begin to form and then break down, and others never form. The rudiment is eventually overgrown by surrounding tissue and sunk deep below the skin, so it is no longer externally visible. A sinking canal, the remnant of this process, may stay open or become an epidermal strand.

Sensory compensation was also examined. Breder and Rasquin (1943) reported more compensatory skin sense organs in the northern, presumably older, cave populations. Unpublished work by Schemmel (1965) distinguished chemical from mechanical receptors and found that Pachon fish have denser taste buds on the head than Sabinos fish, with no difference in neuromast structure.

## Key Findings

**1. Eye rudiments in Pachon fish are variable, but a common degeneration stage predominates.**
- The sinking canal contained conspicuous pigment in 17 of 20 eyes.
- Eleven of 20 eyes reached the intermediate degeneration stage (Pa1-type). This stage retains a sclera, optic nerve, choroid, two pigment layers (argentea and pigment epithelium), a layered retina, and a vitreous body.
- The most advanced rudiments (Pa3-type, left eye) show pigment and retinal remnants fused into a single mass.
- Maximum sclera diameters ranged from 550 µm to 910 µm across the examined animals (for example, Pa1 right 860 µm, Pa1 left 910 µm, Pa3 right 880 µm, Pa3 left 550 µm).
- Only one rudiment kept lens muscle, lens capsule, and a regenerated lens. The authors describe this as unique among the 20 eyes.

**2. Left-right asymmetry is as large as individual variation.**
- Of 10 Pachon animals, four had the same degeneration stage in both eyes, and three were clearly asymmetric.
- In one animal, the two eyes were at the two most extreme degeneration stages.
- The authors conclude that the same genotype produces both eyes, so the variation reflects amplified developmental variability rather than a heterozygous genotype.

**3. Degeneration does not progress after growth ends.**
- Pachon fish reach sexual maturity at about 8 months and are fully grown after about 1 year in the laboratory.
- Two-year-old Pachon fish showed the same degeneration stages as one-year-old fish. Three of four had layered retinas and bilateral rudiments, while one had a retinal remnant in a simple pigment cyst on one side.

**4. Sabinos rudiments are more uniform and lack an optic nerve.**
- Ten adults (56–66 mm total length) were examined. The authors' 64 mm specimen represents the usual condition.
- The sclera is partly cartilaginous and partly fibrous, so the eye bulb is flattened proximo-distally.
- The pigment cyst often contains a vitreous remnant or retinal elements, and sometimes collapses into a pigment ball.
- The optic nerve is absent.
- Variability is markedly lower than in Pachon fish, so the authors infer a largely homozygous genotype that differs in part from the Pachon genotype.

**5. Cueva Chica rudiments are similar but distinct.**
- Extreme Chica rudiments reach a degeneration level comparable to the best-differentiated Pachon eyes, with cornea, iris, vitreous, layered retina, and optic nerve present.
- Two differences were noted: Pachon eyes have a complete cartilaginous to bony sclera, while Chica sclera is fraying; and Chica irises are concave rather than convex or flat.
- The authors describe these differences as genetically determined and non-overlapping within each population.

**6. Crossing results raise questions about gene action.**
- Sadowski's 1957 crosses (cited in the paper) gave F2 hybrids with eye diameters that were smallest with a Pachon parent, intermediate with a Sabinos parent, and largest with a Chica parent. The authors note the data were not tested for significance.
- Because Pachon eyes can be much better differentiated than Sabinos eyes, the authors argue that genes act non-additively and that gene combination matters as much as gene number.
- They predict that some F2 hybrids may have eyes either more or less reduced than either parent. These predictions have not been tested histologically.

**7. Proposed genetic mechanism.**
- The authors invoke the Darwinian degeneration principle. Eye development is polygenic, so isolated cave colonists quickly accumulate a heterozygous, variable population.
- As degeneration proceeds, the genotype becomes more homozygous for degenerate alleles. Rudiments persist when their controlling genes are pleiotropic and cannot change without harming other traits.
- Because all three populations began from the same surface ancestor, their end-state genotypes should be similar, but they still differ in small details. The authors suggest that the greater the time since separation, the smaller the differences.

**8. Biogeography of cave colonization.**
- All known blind characin caves drain into the same valley, which discharges near Cueva Chica into the Rio Tampaon, where surface fish are common.
- The caves are thought to have been colonized from north to south during a gradual lowering of the water table, so northern populations may be older.
