## Executive Summary
This memorandum documents a fisheries monitoring event conducted on the evening of 4 August 1993 at the Pittsburgh Point backwater growout facility, part of a [[Razorback Sucker]] repatriation program managed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker FRO) in coordination with the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]]. A single 100 m trammel net was fished hourly from 7PM to 11PM, capturing 16 razorback suckers that had been stocked into the backwater approximately six weeks prior (18 June 1993). Growth data indicate healthy condition and good post-stocking survival, though one mortality was recorded, potentially linked to thermal/oxygen stress. Concurrent water quality profiling revealed a pronounced vertical stratification of dissolved oxygen, with hypoxic conditions in deeper water layers, raising habitat quality concerns for the growout site.

## Background & Description
- **Species monitored:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Initial stocking (18 June 1993):**
  - N = 199 fish
  - Average total length (TL): 177.5 mm
  - Average weight: 68 g
- **Recapture sample (4 August 1993):**
  - N = 16 fish
  - Average total length: 245.6 mm
  - Average weight: 190.3 g
- **Growth increment (over ~47 days):**
  - Mean TL increase: 68.7 mm (range 57–80 mm)
  - Mean weight increase: 124.3 g (range 90–150 g)
- **General condition:** Good; no external parasites (*Lernaea*, anchor worm) observed
- **Mortality:** 1 individual (TL 235 mm; weight 155 g)
  - PIT tag recovered: `7F7A191F5C` (tag slated for reuse)
  - Suspected cause: heat stress

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Isolated backwater used as a controlled growout facility for juvenile razorback suckers prior to release into the mainstem [[Colorado River]] system.
- **Thermal regime:** Surface water temperatures ranged from ~24.0–31.0°C across sampling hours; deeper strata (2–2.5 m) generally cooler but still warm (21.0–27.5°C), indicating minimal thermal buffering at depth.
- **Dissolved oxygen stratification:**
  - Surface DO: highest recorded values (up to 6.7 ppm)
  - Bottom DO (2.5 m): critically low, down to 0.7–0.9 ppm
  - Upper 2 meters generally retained sufficient oxygen to support fish; deeper water frequently hypoxic.
- **Sampling gear:** One 100 m [[Trammel Net]], fished hourly (7PM–11PM)
- **Capture timing:** Majority of fish captured early in the sampling window (7–8 PM, N=13), suggesting behavioral association with cooler, better-oxygenated surface waters during dusk before further stratification set in.
- **Collaborators:** Field assistance provided by the [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge|BWRNWR]] Youth Conservation Corps (YCC) group.

## Key Findings
1. Recaptured razorback suckers exhibited substantial growth over ~47 days in the growout backwater, with average length increasing by 68.7 mm and weight nearly tripling (68 g → 190.3 g).
2. Overall fish condition was good, with no observed ectoparasite (*Lernaea*) infestation.
3. One mortality was documented, potentially attributable to heat/oxygen stress, underscoring risks associated with strong thermal and DO stratification in the backwater.
4. Water quality data revealed a strong dissolved oxygen gradient with depth — surface waters were adequately oxygenated (up to 6.7 ppm) while bottom waters (2.5 m) were frequently hypoxic (as low as 0.7 ppm), limiting usable habitat to the upper ~2 meters of the water column.
5. Oxygen measurements were spatially inconsistent due to lack of a fixed sampling location; a **buoy marker is planned** to standardize future dissolved oxygen monitoring at a fixed point.
6. Findings support continued monitoring of growout facility conditions as part of the broader [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery and repatriation effort in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]].
