# Cuatro Ciénegas Water Use and Ecological Ordering Project (Primer Informe, 1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endemic-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document set comprises a 1993 cover letter and the Primer Informe (First Report: Description and Analysis) of the *Proyecto de Ordenamiento Ecológico, del Uso y Aprovechamiento del Agua* for the [[Cuatro Ciénegas]] region of [[Coahuila]], Mexico. The project was commissioned by [[SEDESOL]] (Secretaría de Desarrollo Social) and carried out by a consulting firm, ASI (Advanced Sciences Integrated Mexico S.A. de C.V.). The cover letter, addressed to [[W.L. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]], describes the work as a "quick look" at project objectives and invites discussion with Dr. James Kunkel.

The report stresses three themes: groundwater resources, endemism, and sustainable development. It notes that the basin has been studied by the international scientific community since 1936, that its endemic flora and fauna are exceptional, and that intensive agriculture and ranching have increased pressure on its aquifers over the past decade. The report is a planning document, not a fish survey; its fish content is limited to species lists and endemism counts.

## Background & Description

The Área de Ordenamiento Ecológico (AOE) lies in Mexico's desert zone and has one of the highest levels of marginalization in Coahuila. The area is known for its springs and its endemic biota. In November 1983 the 14th Annual Meeting of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], held at Arizona State University, brought together American and Mexican specialists and highlighted the need to protect the ecosystem. In 1992 SEDESOL proposed the area for protection and included it in the Programa Ambiental de México.

The report's stated objectives are to:
- Characterize natural resources and identify endangered, threatened, migratory, endemic, and rare species and their habitats.
- Identify regional suitability for productive activities that do not damage vegetation cover.
- Assess environmental deterioration and resource degradation trends, and prepare ecological restoration programs.
- Identify trends in water use and set criteria for regulation to protect soil, water, and natural resource quality.
- Describe natural, social, and economic components.
- Promote programs and legal instruments consistent with federal, state, and municipal authority.
- Promote monitoring of the ordering program, supported by an information database.

Methods were largely bibliographic. Reviews covered US university information systems (California, Colorado, New Mexico, Texas) and Mexican institutions (UNAM, Instituto Nacional de Ecología, Comisión Nacional del Agua). Climate series from the Cuatro Ciénegas climate station (since 1941) were used. Planned fieldwork on soil, water, and vegetation density was to be coordinated with the Universidad Autónoma Agraria Antonio Narro and the CNA state office in Saltillo.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Groundwater.** The AOE has 210 water-extraction points: 121 wells, 56 *norias* (shallow wells), and 36 springs. Note that these components sum to 213, not 210, so the source figures conflict. Estimated annual recharge is "10 000 a 15 000 millones cúbicos," mainly from the mountain slopes that border the valley. Water has been exported out of the basin since 1906 to supply the municipalities of Sacramento, Nadadores, San Buenaventura, and Frontera. Salinity ranges from 1,000 to 2,500 ppm, classed as C2-S1 and C3-S1, which the report says implies a risk of soil salinization.

**Endemism.**
- **Snails:** The highest endemism is in gastropods, with 9 endemic species, including *Coahuilix hubbsi*, *Paludiscala caramba*, *Mexithauma quadripaludium*, *Mexipyrgus churinceanus*, and *Nymphophilus minckleyi*.
- **Fishes:** 8 of the 16 native fish species are cited as endemic or of concern. The list includes *Notropis xanticara*, *Cyprinodon atrorus*, *C. bifasciatus*, *Lucania interioris*, *Gambusia longispinis*, *Xiphophorus gordoni*, and *Cichlasoma minckleyi*. The eighth entry is truncated in the source ("Etheostoma").
- **Reptiles (turtles):** 3 endemic species are listed: *Trionyx ater*, *Pseudemys scripta*, and *Terrapene coahuila*. Note that *P. scripta* is widely distributed, so its endemic status in the source should be checked.
- **Plants:** 49 taxa are recorded, of which 23 are endemic and 4 are no longer recognized. Examples include *Agave scabra*, *Mimosa unipinnata*, and *Euphorbia pinkavana*.

**Human setting.** The 1990 census recorded 12,302 inhabitants, 8,454 of them in the town of Cuatro Ciénegas. The rural population is spread across 40 communities of 9 to 295 people. Most are *ejidos* (25) granted land in 1936, averaging 5 hectares per ejidatario. Land use is mainly grazing, with 15,335 goats and only 5,760 cattle in the ejidos. Gypsum production (95% purity) employs about 100 families, and an ICI subsidiary explosives factory employs 80 workers. The economically active population of 3,378 is 26% primary sector, 36% mining, construction, and manufacturing, and 38% services. The main employment problem is the layoff of about 200 workers from Altos Hornos de México in Monclova, and candelilla wax production has been displaced by synthetics.

## Key Findings

- **Aquifer pressure is rising.** Agricultural and livestock expansion over the past decade has intensified demand on groundwater, and the report links sustainable water use directly to conservation of endemic fauna.
- **Endemism is exceptional.** The basin supports 9 endemic snails, 8 listed fishes (of 16 native), 3 turtle species, and 23 endemic plant taxa.
- **Soil and salinity risks.** Water salinity of 1,000 to 2,500 ppm poses a salinization risk to irrigated soils.
- **Economic transition is underway.** The local economy is shifting from primary production toward mining and services, with weak employment prospects after industrial layoffs.
- **Planning is multi-level.** The project involves federal, state, and municipal agencies, and the report proposes a monitoring program backed by a data bank.
- **Timeline and deliverables.** A Pre-Diagnosis document was due on 2 March 1993, and variable-selection and study-boundary definitions were to be agreed with SEDESOL in the third week after project start.
- **Data gaps and quality issues.** The report contains internal inconsistencies (the 210 versus 213 water points) and an incomplete fish list. Fieldwork results on soils, water, and vegetation were still preliminary.
