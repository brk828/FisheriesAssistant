# Comparative Morphology of Three Cyprinid Fishes: *Notropis cornutus*, *Notropis rubellus*, and Their Hybrid

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 study by [[Rudolph J. Miller]] compares the external morphology, meristic and morphometric characters, and selected internal anatomy of two closely related minnows, [[Common Shiner]] (*Notropis cornutus*) and [[Rosyface Shiner]] (*Notropis rubellus*), and their natural hybrid. Specimens came from [[Catatonk Creek]] at Candor, New York, where a dam blocks upstream migration and forces both species to spawn in the same reaches. The hybrid was usually intermediate between parents, though many characters approached one parent or the other. The author could not identify any backcross or F2 individuals on morphological grounds.

## Background & Description

- **Hybridization context:** Cyprinidae is the most speciose North American freshwater family and has the highest rate of natural hybridization. [[Carl L. Hubbs]] reported that 11 per cent (68) of 608 genus and subgenus pairs that co-occur are known to hybridize naturally. The [[Hybrid Index Method]], introduced by Hubbs and Kuronuma (1942) and formalized by Hubbs, Hubbs, and Johnson (1943), assigns parents values of 0 and 100, with exact intermediacy near 50.
- **Study population:** Catatonk Creek is a long-studied site where Cornell University ichthyologists have collected hybrids for over 20 years. Both parent species spawn there from late May to June, usually over the gravel nests of the [[River Chub]] (*Hybopsis micropogon*).
- **Scope:** The paper is "Part two" of a doctoral dissertation; Part one addressed the reproductive behavior of [[Central Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma anomalum*).
- **Methods:** Fixed in 10% formalin and preserved in 40% isopropyl alcohol. At least 30 specimens per form were used for characters that differed appreciably, and at least 45 were dissected for internal anatomy. Measurements were taken with a dial caliper to 0.1 mm, and linear regressions were fitted against standard length (SL).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Feeding:** All three forms are primarily visual feeders that sample objects at the surface or in midwater. The parents are basically carnivorous, although *N. cornutus* is more omnivorous, with a longer gut. Reed (1957) found plant material in only 18.1% of 1650 *N. rubellus* stomachs.
- **Hybrid distribution:** Trautman (1957) found hybrids in Ohio only in high-gradient streams faster than the normal spawning reaches. Hubbs and Brown (1929) found them most often below obstructions, where the species are forced into shared spawning areas.
- **Sample years:** Hybrids were more common than *N. rubellus* during much of the year at Catatonk Creek in 1959, 1960, and 1961.
- **Brain-behavior correlation:** Following Evans (1952) and others, the sight-feeding *N. rubellus* has large optic lobes, while the lobe patterns of the hybrid fall between those of the parents.

## Key Findings

**External morphology and pigmentation**
- *N. rubellus* is small, terete, and slim-bodied, seldom exceeding 75 mm SL. *N. cornutus* is coarser and deeper-bodied, commonly exceeding 100 mm SL. The hybrid is intermediate in size, seldom exceeding 100 mm SL (one specimen of 111.0 mm was collected).
- Melanophores are smallest in *N. cornutus* and largest in *N. rubellus*. Hybrid melanophores are intermediate in size but lean toward *rubellus*.
- Most hybrid pigment traits are intermediate, though several resemble *rubellus*. Lip color, fin color, and the scale pigmentation below the lateral line also lean toward *rubellus*.

**Sexual dimorphism**
- Breeding males of *N. rubellus* are bright orange-red and tuberculate. Those of *N. cornutus* show a red-banded fin pattern and slate-gray head, with large hooked tubercles.
- Hybrid males are variably colored, with tiny breeding tubercles over most of the body. None of the fifteen females examined in the breeding season had tubercles.

**Meristic characters (5 of 8 differed among forms; 6 characters indexed)**
- Hybrid indices: anal rays 42 (trend toward *cornutus*), pectoral rays 52 (near intermediate), body-circumference scales 80 (toward *rubellus*), caudal peduncle scales 63 (toward *rubellus*).
- Predorsal scales are similar in the parents but differ markedly in the hybrid.
- The mean of the four counts indexed was 62, leaning toward *rubellus*.

**Morphometric characters (16 measurements on 30 specimens per form)**
- Five measurements (pectoral fin length, anal fin length, eye length, snout length, and head length) did not separate the forms.
- The ratio of predorsal to postdorsal length is the only character that reliably separates all three forms, with virtually no overlap.
- Caudal peduncle depth, predorsal length, and postdorsal length fall near intermediate values. Dorsal fin height, pelvic fin length, interorbital width, and several distance measurements approach *cornutus*. Head depth, body width, and, to a lesser degree, body depth approach *rubellus*.
- Hybrid body width, especially in adults, is smaller than in either parent.

**Internal anatomy**
- Internal differences among the parents are mostly in the degree of development of shared structures. The digestive tract is similar across forms. The intestinal bulb is treated as intestine, since no gastric glands are present.
- Gut length is greatest in *N. cornutus*, is shortest in *N. rubellus*, and is intermediate in the hybrid. Smaller hybrids tend toward *rubellus* and larger ones toward *cornutus*.
- *N. cornutus* has a ventro-lateral kidney swelling that is absent or weak in the other two forms. The spleen is fully embedded in the liver in *N. rubellus* and lies behind the last intestinal loop in *N. cornutus*. The hybrid is intermediate.
- The tripus shape is consistent across forms, with one difference: the medial projection is more expanded in *N. cornutus*.

**Brain pattern**
- *N. rubellus* has large optic lobes and flattened cerebellum and optic surfaces, which fits sight-feeding. *N. cornutus* has more elongate cerebral hemispheres and divergent olfactory stalks.
- Hybrid brain features are intermediate, except that its olfactory stalks are the longest of the three forms.

**Backcross question**
- The author could not identify any single hybrid specimen as a backcross, and no specimen approached one parent in all characters.
- Sex ratio is 1:1 and most hybrids appear to have functional gonads, although many individuals have poorly developed gonads (Miller, 1962). Raney (1940b) observed hybrids of both sexes spawning with *N. cornutus*.
- Three interpretations are offered: a fully mixed population of several generations (considered least likely), a pure F1 population, or a population with occasional backcrosses surviving each year (considered most likely). The author states that experimental verification is required.

**Conclusion**
- Overall, hybrids average close to exact intermediacy, but individual characters vary in parental influence. The author suggests a complex, non-simple genetic control in which some traits are dominated by one parent.
