## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific study but a **legal notice letter** sent by the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund (on behalf of the Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance) to the U.S. Secretary of the Interior, Manuel Lujan, Jr., on August 5, 1991. The letter formally notifies the Department of the Interior of an alleged violation of Section 4 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] for failing to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an endangered species within the statutorily mandated timeframe. While the letter contains no direct morphological, physiological, or ecological data on the species itself, it references the regulatory and administrative timeline surrounding the species' conservation status determination, which is relevant to understanding its historical population decline and conservation urgency.

## Key Findings
- **Species in question:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]].
- **Petition history:** A listing petition was filed with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) in **March 1989** by the Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance.
- **Proposed rule:** On **May 22, 1990**, FWS published a proposed regulation to list the razorback sucker as endangered (55 Fed. Reg. 21154).
- **Statutory deadline:** Under 16 U.S.C. § 1533(b)(6)(A–B), the ESA required a final listing determination within **one year** of the proposed rule (i.e., by May 22, 1991).
- **Violation alleged:** No final listing action had been taken by the one-year deadline, constituting an alleged violation of ESA Section 4.
- **Absence of scientific controversy:** The letter asserts there was **no "substantial disagreement regarding the sufficiency or accuracy of the available data"** underlying the proposed listing determination — i.e., the scientific case for endangerment was considered settled and uncontested at the time.
- **Legal action threatened:** Pursuant to the ESA's citizen-suit provision (16 U.S.C. § 1540(g)(2)), the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund provided the required 60-day pre-suit notice, threatening federal litigation if the Department of the Interior did not act.
- **Outcome context:** This notice reflects a pivotal moment in the razorback sucker's regulatory history; the species was later formally listed as **endangered** under the ESA in **October 1991**, shortly after this notice, underscoring the role of citizen-suit pressure in enforcing statutory listing deadlines under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
