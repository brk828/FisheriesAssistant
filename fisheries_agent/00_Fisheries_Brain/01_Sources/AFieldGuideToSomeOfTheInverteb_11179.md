# A Field Guide to Some of the Invertebrates Found at Puerto Peñasco, Sonora, Mexico

#marine-biology #invertebrate-zoology #intertidal-ecology #Gulf-of-California

## Executive Summary

This 1966 field guide by P. E. Pickens from the [[University of Arizona]] Department of Zoology documents the invertebrate fauna of [[Puerto Peñasco]], [[Sonora]], [[Mexico]] in the [[Gulf of California]]. The guide presents identification keys, illustrations, and distributional data for major invertebrate groups including sponges, coelenterates (anemones, corals), echinoderms (starfish, sea urchins, sea cucumbers, brittle stars), brachyuran crabs, anomuran crabs (hermit crabs, porcelain crabs), molluscs (gastropods, bivalves), and opisthobranchs (sea slugs, nudibranchs). The document establishes a preliminary intertidal zonation scheme for the region, dividing the approximately 22-foot vertical intertidal zone into four zones (I-IV) based on dominant organisms and tidal levels. This represents a progress report on invertebrate diversity at the newly established [[University of Sonora]] Marine Research Station.

## Background & Description

The guide was produced as a working document to support research and education at the [[University of Sonora]] Marine Research Station at [[Puerto Peñasco]]. The author acknowledges that identifications are preliminary and distributions may change with additional research. Taxonomic identifications were provided by multiple experts:

- **Sponges**: Dr. Gerald J. Bakus ([[Allan Hancock Foundation]], [[University of Southern California]])
- **Anemones**: Mr. Charles E. Cutress (Institute of Marine Biology, [[University of Puerto Rico]])
- **Echinoderms**: Mr. Joseph A. Beatty (Department of Zoology, [[Southern Illinois University]])
- **Brachyurans**: Dr. John Garth ([[Allan Hancock Foundation]])
- **Anomurans**: Mr. Clinton Westervelt (Department of Biology, Chapman College), consulting with Dr. Janet Haig
- **Opisthobranchs**: Dr. and Mrs. Ernest Marcus ([[University of São Paulo]], Brazil)
- **Molluscs**: Identified using Keen's "Sea Shells of Tropical West America" and other references

The guide focuses primarily on intertidal forms, with distribution indicated by zone. The document includes detailed illustrations and diagnostic characteristics for species identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Intertidal Zonation System

The guide establishes a four-zone system for [[Puerto Peñasco]] intertidal areas:

- **Zone I** (+19 to +11 feet): Blue-green algae zone, extends from splash zone to halfway between mean high tide and mean sea level. Upper subzone (+15 to +19 feet) characterized by bare granite rocks with few organisms except [[Ligia]] isopods. Lower subzone (+11 to +15 feet) marked by dark blue-green to black algal encrustation.

- **Zone II** (+11 to +7.5 feet): Large barnacle zone, dominated by [[Tetraclita squamosa]]. Extends to approximately mean sea level.

- **Zone III** (Mean sea level to 0 feet): Small barnacle zone, dominated by [[Balanus]] species. Extends to mean of lower low tides.

- **Zone IV** (0 to -3 feet): Soft coral ([[Palythoa ignota]]) and brown sponge ([[Geodia mesotriaena]]) zone. Extends from zero datum to lowest low tides.

The total intertidal zone spans approximately 22 feet vertically but varies in width from less than 50 feet at precipitous granite outcroppings near [[Pelican Point]] to several hundred feet along gently sloping reefs at [[Norse Beach]] and the Marine Research Station. The zero datum corresponds to +3.9 feet on the Puerto Peñasco tide gauge.

### Habitat Variation

**Rocky Intertidal**: Characterized by granite outcroppings and sandstone-coquina reefs. At the Marine Research Station, a distinctive zone of small, round basaltic boulders occurs in tide pools and on the sandstone reef, with upper distribution limit at mean sea level and extending approximately 3.5 feet below. These boulders provide substrate for numerous species including chitons.

**Sandy Beach**: In several areas at [[Norse Beach]], sand extends from highest intertidal to reef edge, eliminating rocky substrate and associated Zones I and II fauna. Beach isopods ([[Tylos]]) and amphipods replace [[Ligia]] as primary scavengers.

**Cholla Bay**: Approximately four miles wide at zero tide, exposing extensive areas at lower tides including sandstone reefs two miles from settlement. These reefs support tunicates, sponges, and lower intertidal organisms. Main channel drains large areas and sloughs radiating into desert marshland. Water persists in larger sloughs and main channel even during extreme low tides. Tides of +12 to +13 feet push water into sloughs, though bay doesn't fill until approximately +15 feet. Prolonged exposure limits organisms to hardy mud and sand dwellers. Grain size and tidal movement produce zonation patterns not marked by surface bands.

### Upward Displacement

Lower intertidal organisms extend into higher tide pools with permanent water bodies. Characteristic Zone IV species found in higher pools include brown algae ([[Padina]], [[Sargassum]]), soft coral ([[Palythoa]]), hard coral ([[Porites californica]]), and brown sponge ([[Geodia mesotriaena]]).

## Key Findings

### Taxonomic Diversity by Major Group

**PORIFERA (Sponges)**
- Class Calcarea: [[Leucetta losangelensis]] (white calcareous sponge, mid-intertidal under rocks)
- Class Demospongia: [[Hymeniacidon sinapium]] (dull orange, encrusting with volcano-like projections), [[Haliclona]] sp. (violet colonies), [[Pseudosuberites pseudos]] (large orange colonies, lowest intertidal [[Cholla Bay]]), [[Geodia mesotriaena]] (brown to black, large colonies in tide pools, spicules readily felt)

**CNIDARIA (Coelenterates)**

*Anthozoa - Anemones*:
- [[Phyllactis concinnata]] and [[Phyllactis bradleyi]]: Distinguished by collar/ruff; P. concinnata with unmarked tentacles and pink/white column among rocks; P. bradleyi with orange column and brown-striped tentacles buried in sand
- [[Anthopleura dowii]], [[Bunodosoma californica]], [[Bunodactis mexicana]]: Xanthid-like forms; Bunodosoma most abundant with non-adhesive vesicles in longitudinal rows, red/brown/olive green coloration; Bunodactis with adhesive verrucae, olive green long tentacles, white elliptical marks at inner tentacle bases; Anthopleura smallest, dark green/brown with transparent tentacles with white patches
- [[Actinoporus]] sp.: Large anemone with bright pink column, many rows of short brown and white tentacles forming ruff, vesicles on upper column, buried in [[Cholla Bay]] sand
- [[Telmatactis panamensis]]: Orange with leathery column, short club-shaped tentacles (knobs in older specimens), oral disc and tentacles mottled orange/red/brown, possesses acontia
- [[Aiptasia californica]]: Small white to dark greenish-brown, often translucent with iridescent patches, covers tidepool edges, possesses acontia
- [[Metapeachia]] sp. and [[Calamactis praelongus]]: Elongate white anemones without pedal disc, having rounded physa; Metapeachia with 16 short banded tentacles and tubular siphonoglyph; Calamactis commonest in [[Cholla Bay]] with more and longer tentacles
- [[Pachycerianthus insignis]]: Cerianthid in mucus tube in sandstone reef, dark gray column, many very long thin tentacles with longitudinal brown stripes, rapid retraction
- [[Anthothoe carcinophila]]: Rides on snail shells in [[Cholla Bay]], many fine tentacles, longitudinal gray and white stripes

*Zoanthids (Soft Corals)*:
- [[Palythoa ignota]]: Light to dark brown, very short tentacles folded when exposed, forms colonies in tide pools, covers large areas of lower intertidal sandstone reef
- [[Zoanthus danae]]: Similar but smaller, bright green oral disc, forms colonies on reef
- [[Epizoanthus gabrieli]]: Very small with many fine banded tentacles, column encrusted with sand grains, under rocks and ledges in lowest intertidal

*Other Anthozoans*:
- [[Astrangia pedersenii]]: Solitary coral
- [[Porites californica]]: Colonial coral
- Gorgonians and sea pens present
- [[Aglaophenia diegensi]]: Conspicuous sedentary hydrozoan forming feather-like colony

**ECHINODERMATA**

*Asteroidea (Starfish)*:
- [[Luidia phragma]]: Sand star in [[Cholla Bay]], aboral surface with orange/light tan/black mosaic, black spots border arms, orange patches cover center
- [[Luidia columbia]]: Similar shape, 2-3 times larger, dark gray to black aboral surface
- [[Astropecten armatus]]: Pale gray-violet sand star in [[Cholla Bay]], conspicuous rectangular marginal plates
- [[Othilia tenuispina]]: Common orange starfish of lowest rocky intertidal, red "eye spots" on arm tips
- [[Heliaster kubiniji]]: Common multi-rayed starfish of rocky intertidal

*Echinoidea (Sea Urchins)*:
- [[Eucidaris thouarsii]]: Few thick club-shaped spines often covered with encrusting organisms
- [[Echinometra vanbrunti]]: Purple with medium-sized spines covering test
- [[Arbacia incisa]]: Darker purple than Echinometra, 5-rayed petaloid pattern on aboral surface where spines absent in interambulacral areas

*Sand Dollars*:
- [[Mellita longifissa]]: Light grayish-purple to light green, notches and slit-like lunule present
- [[Encope micropora]]: Dark reddish-purple, no notches, oval lunules, abundant in lowest intertidal [[Cholla Bay]]
- [[Encope grandis]]: Dark reddish-purple, open notches in test, straight edge behind single lunule, abundant in [[Cholla Bay]]

*Holothuroidea (Sea Cucumbers)*:
- [[Brandtothuria impatiens]]: Large brown often banded cucumber with prominent papillae, readily discharges cuvierian tubules (very sticky)
- [[Brandtothuria arenicola]]: Hard-walled with brown and white patches
- [[Holothuria arenicola]]: Hard-walled with patches of various shades of brown on light brown base
- [[Neothyone gibbosa]]: Hard-walled white to purple, often V-shaped in habitat, relatively long tube feet
- [[Pentamera chierchia]]: Smooth reddish-purple, rocky intertidal, often L-shaped
- [[Selenkothuria lubrica]]: Commonest cucumber in Gulf, dark green to black, under rocks or buried in sand in tide pools
- [[Theelothuria parviceps]]: Long large black cucumber occasionally buried in [[Cholla Bay]] sand, lighter papillae surrounded by white ring
- [[Chiridota aponocrita]]: Pinkish-red footless cucumber with many small white papillae, under rocks in lower intertidal
- [[Epitomapta tabogae]]: Small white footless cucumber, lower intertidal under rocks or buried in sand

*Ophiuroidea (Brittle Stars)*:
- [[Ophiocoma aethiops]]: Medium-length arm spines, dark gray to black arms and disc, common large brittle star under rocks
- [[Ophiocoma alexandri]]: Medium-length arm spines, gray disc, arms banded gray and white, thinner and longer arms than O. aethiops
- [[Ophionereis annulata]]: Smaller, aboral disc surface covered with small scales rather than granules
- [[Ophiothrix spiculata]]: Very long arm spines, disc tends pentagonal, various colors (usually orange or brown), arms banded orange and white
- [[Ophioderma teres]]: Short arm spines, dark brown to black disc and arms
- [[Ophioderma panamensis]]: Similar but white bands at arm tips; both species common in rocky intertidal
- [[Ophiolepsis variegata]]: Very short arm spines, arms banded dark blue to black, aboral disc with large white blotch extending to base of one arm, rare in [[Cholla Bay]]
- [[Ophiactis savignyi]]: Small six-rayed brittle star, sometimes quite abundant in mid-intertidal under rocks

**CRUSTACEA**

*Brachyura (True Crabs)*:
- [[Ala cornuta]]: Majid crab with projecting rostrum, groups of hairs on legs and posterior carapace, often covered with algae and sponge bits ("Decorator Crab"), olive brown
- [[Epialtus minimus]]: Common kelp/sargassum crab of lowest rocky intertidal, olive brown (not pictured)
- [[Grapsus grapsus]]: "Sally Lightfoot" absent at Puerto Peñasco (prominent elsewhere in southern two-thirds of Gulf)
- [[Geotice americana]]: Grayish-violet carapace and legs, almost rectangular carapace
- [[Portunus xantusii minimus]]: One long spine each side of carapace, fourth pair of legs with paddles
- [[Callinectes bellicosus]]: Blue swimming crab, commonest in [[Cholla Bay]]
- Xanthid crabs (transversely oval carapace): [[Pilumnus limosus]] (covered with fine light brown hairs, "furry" appearance), [[Pilumnus townsendi]] (numerous short spines on chelipeds, dark gray scattered over legs and chelipeds, violet brown, "Spiny Crabs"), [[Leptodius occidentalis]] (gray/pale violet/white carapace with two darker patches in center, legs banded dark gray)
- [[Eriphia squamata]]: Commonest large crab in rocky intertidal, dark gray or gray, wide intertidal distribution (not illustrated)

*Anomura*:
- Hermit crabs: [[Paguristes anahuacus]] (blue mouthparts/antennae/antennules/eyestalks in young, equal chelipeds, "furry" appearance, [[Cholla Bay]] and rocky intertidal), [[Clibanarius digueti]] (red legs and chelipeds with white spots, red antennae/antennules, brown eyes, equal chelipeds, commonest rocky intertidal hermit), [[Paguristes sanguinimanus]] (bright blue eyes, red legs/chelipeds/mouthparts/eyestalks, equal chelipeds, [[Cholla Bay]]), [[Petrochirus californiensis]] (largest hermit at Rocky Point, in murex shells in [[Cholla Bay]], unequal chelipeds), [[Paguristes]] sp. (small dark blue spot on inside of cheliped merus, equal chelipeds, white eyes and eyestalks, [[Cholla Bay]]), [[Pagurus albus]] (white legs/chelipeds/eyes, unequal chelipeds, [[Cholla Bay]])
- Porcelain crabs: [[Pachycheles setimanus]] (unequal chelipeds, coarse hairs on chelipeds and legs, pale brown), [[Petrolisthes armatus]] (smooth black chelipeds/legs/carapace, equal chelipeds), [[Petrolisthes hirtipes]] (red-orange cheliped tips, blue maxillipeds, fine hairs giving "furry" appearance, equal chelipeds, brown)
- Ghost shrimps found when digging in [[Cholla Bay]]
