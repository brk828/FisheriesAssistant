# Concentration and Transport of ³²P and ⁶⁵Zn by Columbia River Plankton

#ichthyology #marine-biology #phytoplankton

## Executive Summary

This short note by C. E. Cushing (Battelle Memorial Institute, Pacific Northwest Laboratory) reports the seasonal concentration and downstream transport of radioactive phosphorus (³²P) and zinc (⁶⁵Zn) by net plankton in the [[Columbia River]], sampled about 16 km below reactor areas on the [[Hanford Site]] in southeastern Washington. Improved radioanalytical methods allowed a more precise estimate of the plankton radionuclide burden than earlier surveys. Radionuclide concentrations were lowest during the spring-summer high-water period, while the amount transported by plankton peaked in summer because plankton biomass rises with river flow. The plankton-associated activity was only about 1–2% of total river transport of each isotope. The paper was presented at an IAEA symposium on radioactive waste disposal (Vienna, May 1966) and was performed under U.S. Atomic Energy Commission Contract AT(45-1)-1830.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Net plankton (living and dead organic matter retained by a No. 20 mesh net, 68 meshes/cm) from the Columbia River. Inorganic silt and sand were largely removed by decanting.
- **Sampling design:** Samples were collected twice monthly at several stations along a cross-river transect. Water was pumped from 1.5 m below the surface using an Oceanic Instruments submersible plankton pump. Filtered volumes ranged from 500 to 3,000 liters per sample, depending on plankton abundance.
- **Chemical analyses:** Dissolved phosphate (PO₄³⁻) was measured by the phosphomolybdate blue method, and Zn²⁺ by dithizone extraction. Dissolved ³²P and ⁶⁵Zn were measured after concentration and organic chemical extraction.
- **Context:** Earlier radionuclide studies of the river were cited ([[Coopey 1953]], [[Robeck et al. 1954]], [[Davis et al. 1956]], [[Davis 1960]]). Coopey and Davis et al. reported total beta activity, which inflated values because it included radionuclides not concentrated by plankton.
- **Key terms:** Concentration factor was defined as the ratio of nCi/g wet weight of plankton to nCi/ml of water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regime:** River flow rises in May, peaks in June, and declines to normal levels by September. Reactor effluent inputs remain relatively constant, so radionuclide dilution is greatest during peak river volume.
- **Plankton community:** The spring peak is dominated by *Asterionella*; the later increase is dominated by *Fragilaria* sp. Recent work linked *Fragilaria* increases, particularly in spring, to nutrient cycling studies.
- **Anthropogenic influence:** The author suggests that species change and biomass increase since earlier studies may relate to the completion of two power dams immediately upstream of the Hanford reservation.
- **Seasonal pattern:** Plankton biomass was lowest in fall and winter, with peaks in spring and late summer. Biomass was roughly twice that reported by Coopey (1953).

## Key Findings

- **Concentration factors:** For ³²P they ranged from 5,000 to 118,000; for ⁶⁵Zn from 300 to 19,000. Highest values for both isotopes occurred in fall and winter, and lowest in spring and summer.
- **Seasonal concentration vs. transport:** Lower spring-summer concentrations per unit plankton weight were offset by greater plankton biomass in higher flows, so more ³²P and ⁶⁵Zn were transported at that time.
- **Daily plankton transport:** Ranged from 0.3 × 10⁴ kg dry wt/day (2 January) to 38.5 × 10⁴ kg dry wt/day (22 July).
- **Daily ³²P transport:** From 0.002 Ci/day (29 January) to 1.43 Ci/day (19 August).
- **Daily ⁶⁵Zn transport:** From 0.14 Ci/day (26 February) to 3.8 Ci/day (22 July).
- **Flow-related pattern:** Radionuclide transport was low and stable during low river flow and rose with random fluctuations during high flow.
- **Relative importance:** Plankton-associated radioactivity at the study site was about 1–2% of total river transport of each radionuclide, estimated against data from 35 km downstream (Wilson 1965). Despite high concentration factors, the community's relative transport was small.
- **Concordance:** Seasonal patterns agreed with [[Robeck et al. 1954]], [[Davis et al. 1956]], and [[Davis 1960]]; the seasonal curve matched Coopey (1953), though absolute values differed due to methodology.
