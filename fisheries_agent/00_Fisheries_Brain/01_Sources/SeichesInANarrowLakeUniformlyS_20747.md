# Seiches in a Narrow Lake, Uniformly Stratified in Three Layers

#limnology #physical-limnology #internal-seiches

## Executive Summary

This 1961 paper by N. S. Heaps presents an analytical theory of longitudinal [[Seiche]] (standing wave) oscillations in a narrow, thermally stratified lake. The lake is modelled as three horizontal layers, with density varying linearly with depth inside each layer and continuous across the interfaces. The model is applied to the northern basin of [[Lake Windermere]] to compute the periods and modes of [[Ordinary Seiche]] and [[Internal Seiche]] motions. The main result is that, beyond the thermocline-centred seiches predicted by a [[Two-Layer Model]], the theory also yields modes whose vertical displacement is greatest below the [[Thermocline]]. The author suggests these may explain large, complex temperature oscillations observed in the deep water, but states that further observations are needed to test this.

## Background & Description

**Problem.** Field studies of Lake Windermere by [[C. H. Mortimer]] (1952a, 1952b) showed that two-layer and three-layer homogeneous models did not adequately describe water movements, particularly below the thermocline. Mortimer concluded that a full interpretation required a theory of internal seiches in a lake where density varies continuously with depth.

**Prior work.** Earlier theory on internal progressive waves in continuously stratified water came from Fjeldstad (1933, 1936) and Groen (1948). The only known theory of internal seiches in a continuously stratified lake was that of [[Wedderburn Theory]] (1912), which is limited to oscillations at thermocline level because it assumes a boundary of zero horizontal motion there.

**Approach.** The author uses a rectangular longitudinal section of length *l* and total depth *h*, with the density profile in three layers (equation 1). Key assumptions include:
- Motion is two-dimensional, in the longitudinal plane.
- Displacements and velocities are small, so the equations are linearised.
- Vertical acceleration is negligible (later tested).
- Viscosity, friction, turbulent mixing, geostrophic effects and compressibility are ignored.

Solutions are sought analytically, rather than by numerical integration, to show the mathematical structure of the motions. The work is presented as a necessary first step: free-oscillation periods and modes must be known before water displacements and velocities can be found from their superposition under wind forcing.

**Governing equations.** Within each layer, the linearised equations of motion, volume continuity and density continuity reduce to a single ordinary differential equation for the horizontal velocity profile, with the boundary conditions at the surface, bottom and layer interfaces. The characteristic equation for the modes (equation 41) is transcendental in a dimensionless parameter ε, with positive roots ε_k. Each root sets a seiche family, and each integer *n* sets a horizontal wavelength of 2*l*/*n*, giving modes indexed by the pair (k, n).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper is physical limnology rather than biology, but its ecological relevance is clear:

- **Thermal stratification.** Summer density structure, with a thermocline separating warm surface water from cold deep water, controls how the lake responds to wind.
- **Wind forcing.** A gale blowing along the lake displaces water both horizontally and vertically. After the wind drops, free seiches develop from the displaced state and decay through friction until the next wind event.
- **Mixing and transport.** Internal waves in the lower depths are expected to produce considerable horizontal water movement there. Mortimer (1951, 1952a) suggested that such motion could account for turbulent diffusion of heat and dissolved salts in the bottom waters of stratified lakes.
- **Stability.** Steep vertical velocity gradients in the thermocline region may lead to flow instability, which the author notes as a possible consequence of the higher-order modes.

The paper does not address biological organisms, fish, or invertebrates.

## Key Findings

### Study site and model parameters (Lake Windermere, northern basin)
- Mean density profile from temperature observations made 10–13 June 1947 (hourly vertical temperature profiles at one station, plus longitudinal temperature sections on 10 and 13 June).
- Fitted layer depths: *h*₁ ≈ 5.4 m (top), *h*₂ ≈ 5.0 m (middle), *h*₃ ≈ 31.6 m (bottom), giving a total depth of about 42 m. (The OCR text gives these as "54 m" and "50 m"; the decimal point is restored here from the consistent total depth.)
- Fitted surface density ≈ 0.9991 g/cm³.
- Dimensionless parameters: q = 2.3241 and r = 1.6377.
- Lake length *l* = 6.6 km; gravity g = 981 cm/s².

### Seiche periods (Table 1, hours)
- **Ordinary seiches (k = 1):** T = 0.181/*n* h. The fundamental (n = 1) period is 0.181 h.
- **Internal seiches (k = 2):** uninodal (n = 1) period 18.1 h.
- **k = 3:** uninodal period 62.2 h.
- **k = 4–8:** uninodal periods of 88.5, 116, 149, 175 and 194 h, respectively (the table covers n = 1–7).

Internal seiche periods are much longer than the surface seiche of the same wavelength. The surface elevation is zero in internal seiches, to the order of approximation of the theory.

### Comparison with earlier models
- **k = 2 uninodal mode (18.1 h):** matches the uninodal internal seiche from Mortimer's two-layer model, where periods ranged from 18 to 19.3 h. The modelled thermocline-centred maximum of vertical displacement is at about 9.5 m.
- **k = 3 uninodal mode (62.2 h):** corresponds to the lower-interface internal seiche in Mortimer's three-layer model, where periods ranged from 34 to 57 h.

### Vertical displacement structure
- **Ordinary seiche (k = 1):** vertical displacement is greatest at the surface, and horizontal velocity does not vary with depth.
- **k = 2:** vertical displacement peaks at about 9.5 m, in the lower thermocline.
- **k = 3:** displacement maxima at about 5.5 m and 20 m, with the greater maximum at 20 m.
- **k = 4 and k = 7:** large vertical displacement below the thermocline (deeper than about 10 m) compared with low values at and above it.
- **Conclusion:** several seiche modes have their largest vertical movements in the lower depths, which the author proposes may explain the observed large, complex deep-water temperature oscillations.

### Validity of the neglect of vertical acceleration
- For the Windermere parameters, the conditions n²π²h²/(l²ε²) and related terms do not exceed 0.03 for the modes considered.
- The author concludes that neglecting vertical acceleration is justified, with errors of 3% or less.

### Conclusions stated by the author
1. Internal seiches with maximum vertical displacement at the thermocline exist and are consistent with the two-layer model. Observed thermocline temperature oscillations appear to be mainly controlled by them.
2. Longer-period internal seiches have their greatest vertical displacement below the thermocline. These may explain deep-water temperature oscillations and drive substantial horizontal movement in the bottom waters.
3. Horizontal velocity reverses direction with depth. The number of reversals rises with mode order, and higher-order modes tend to concentrate reversals in the thermocline. Model-tank experiments (Mortimer 1952b) have shown similar reversals.
4. Testing the role of these modes requires more detailed field data on deep-water motion, such as temperature oscillations, velocity measurements, or drift-body tracks. The modal constants *C*ₙₖ must also be determined before actual water displacements can be calculated.
5. Vertical velocity gradients in the thermocline may be steep enough to cause flow instability there. Counterbalancing this, density gradients are greatest in the thermocline and velocities are small in higher-order, longer-period seiches.

### Limitations
- The 1947 Windermere data set was incomplete in the lower depths, so the modal amplitudes *C*ₙₖ could not be determined. Vertical displacements therefore could not be compared directly with observations.
- The paper states that the deep-water explanation is a hypothesis requiring further investigation.
