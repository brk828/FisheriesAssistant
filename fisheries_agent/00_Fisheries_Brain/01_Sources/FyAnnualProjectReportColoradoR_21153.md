# Green River Channel Monitoring and Razorback Bar High Flow Work (FY-93 Annual Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This FY-93 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] documents the first year of a limited long-term [[Channel Monitoring]] effort on the [[Green River]] between Split Mountain and Jensen, Utah. The work, led by principal investigator George R. Smith of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Water Resources Division), had three components: cataloging old [[PHABSIM]] study sites, establishing a baseline survey of 23 cross sections on the [[Razorback Bar]] spawning reach, and conducting [[High Flow Monitoring]] during the 1993 runoff. The 1993 peak discharge of 20,000 cfs deposited large amounts of sand over the cobble spawning substrate, burying part of a historical [[Razorback Sucker]] spawning area under about 2 feet of sand. The report recommends targeted rather than annual cross-section surveys and details the survey work needed to design a wetland inlet at the [[Escalanti Wetland]].

## Background & Description

**Project framing.** The project is catalogued as Recovery Program Project Number 20, with an initial year of 1990 and an ongoing final year. It relates to the [[RIPRAP]] items I.A.1.b and II.A.1.a.(4). The stated goals were to document past PHABSIM site work and to develop a channel monitoring baseline for Razorback Bar, specifically a limited baseline data set on channel morphology and backwater characteristics for three sites on the Green River.

**Rationale.** The Recovery Program is shifting toward active habitat restoration, development, and management. The report argues that baseline conditions must be known before management begins so that treatment effects can be evaluated. It also notes that the river is dynamic, responding to climate, vegetation, sediment supply, and flow regimes, and that channel monitoring supports flow recommendations.

**Planned tasks.** The FY-93 tasks were to:
- Research [[Service Records]] for old PHABSIM sites that could support future monitoring, and plot cross sections where data were usable.
- Contact current and former researchers and the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife Resources]] and [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] for historical photographs.
- Recommend a library and archive for long-term data such as transect data, aerial video, and field notes.
- Establish transects across Razorback Bar (RM 311), map and digitize the bar, and add transects every half mile down to the Escalanti Ranch wetland property.
- Use the [[HEC-2 Model]] to determine the flows at which water enters the wetland, and develop flow recommendations to maintain spawning bars and downstream nursery habitat.
- Coordinate with the Earth Resources Department at [[Colorado State University]] and ongoing [[Flaming Gorge]] research, scheduling field work to match research flows.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sediment and channel setting.** Most of the study reach has a cobble bed beneath sand bars and sand substrate. Sand depth varies: it is deep in some areas and cobble is exposed in others. Cross sections around the spawning bar are mostly cobble with small pockets or strands of sand. Bedrock outcrops on both sides of the river confine the channels around the bar.

**Channel incision.** Incision has been observed throughout the reach. The report suggests it may reflect the reduced sediment supply after the construction and operation of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. Some bank erosion, on the bar banks and outside channel banks, may be linked to this incision. Because most of the reach has cobble substrate, further incision is expected to be limited, and the report expects the channels confined by bedrock to remain stable over the long term.

**Sand source.** Sand deposition in the Escalanti reach depends on sand removed from the river upstream of Split Mountain. Sand may be stored in the flat, meandering [[Island and Rainbow Parks]] reach over several low to moderate runoff years. A large peak, like the 1993 event, can move that stored sand downstream.

**Spawning habitat concern.** The report describes the right channel cobble as a historical razorback sucker spawning area. The effect of total sand inundation on spawning habitat is unknown, but repeated severe inundation would likely reduce reproductive success. The left channel cobble bar, which receives more flow and stays relatively free of sand, is a candidate for future spawning or larval use, although no fish have yet been observed there, possibly due to sampling gear bias from water depth.

## Key Findings

### Site Documentation (PHABSIM Archive)
- Review of Service files identified **12 PHABSIM investigations**.
- A channel monitoring filing and archiving system was created for transect data, photographs, and video tape.
- The data were generally useful, but some sites had incomplete documentation, missing data, or inappropriate site selection.
- **13 sites** were listed as Service-controlled data, including the IFIM Clifton, Redlands, Ouray (1985 and 1986), Government Bridge, Palisade, Willow Creek & Ouray, Box Elder/Cross Mountain, Black Rocks, 5th Street Bridge (Colorado River), Three Fords, Mineral Bottom, and Gunnison River at Bridgeport sites.
- Other cross sections held by [[Bureau of Reclamation]], universities, and consultants have been identified but not yet added to the database.

### Razorback Bar Baseline and Survey Schedule
- Channel monitoring cross sections were established and monumented in early April 1993 along a **7-mile section (RM 304 to 311)** above Jensen, Utah, roughly every half mile from above Razorback Bar to the mouth of Brush Creek below the Escalanti Wetland.
- **April 1-5:** 23 cross sections were measured, photographed, and sampled for bed material.
- **June 2-7 (high flow):** transects were remeasured, suspended and bedload sediment samples were collected, and maximum high-water marks were surveyed for HEC-2 calibration.
- **August 18-21 (base flow):** the 23 cross sections were remeasured. Additional cross sections were measured across the upper end of the Escalanti Wetland to identify possible inlet locations.
- A stage recorder was installed on the pump dike near cross section 17.5 and operated from **May 13 to June 30**, when water dropped below the inlet. The stand pipe and enclosure were left in place for reinstallation in future high-flow years.

### 1993 Channel Response
- The 1993 peak discharge was **20,000 cfs**.
- Of the 23 cross sections, **10 showed net deposition, 6 showed scour, 5 showed no change, and 2 had no pre-peak survey**.
- Predominant changes were redistribution of sand deposits and thalweg shifts.
- Contrary to the expectation that sand would scour during the peak and redeposit later, large amounts of sand were deposited during the peak.
- At cross section **E-4**, the cobble substrate in the right channel was buried to about **2 feet** of sand during the peak. Sand had not been removed by mid-August, during the recessional limb.
- Between cross sections **E-4 and E-5**, the right-channel cobble was totally inundated with sand at peak discharge.
- The bar itself appears relatively stable in shape after several high runoff seasons on the Yampa and Green rivers since 1983.
- The report concludes that the 1993 peak was insufficient to mobilize substantial portions of the cobble substrate.

### Recommendations
1. **General:** Sand buildup in the right channel may mean the full spawning bar will not be available for years. Start drift net larval sampling at the lower end of the left channel to test whether razorback use the left-channel cobble.
2. **Future cross-section surveys:** Annual surveys are not needed to predict water surface elevation unless discharge is extreme. Surveys should instead target specific objectives:
   - Whether the spawning bar cobble has been scoured of sand before spring runoff.
   - Inundation of the bar cobble during peak flows of future low, moderate, or high years.
   - Channel changes after very high flow years.
   - Detail around the wetland inlet and return flow channels for design.
   - Long-term channel incision.
3. **Wetland topography:** Survey the inlet area, the potential channel from inlet to wetland (following the old meander thalweg near the bluff line), the return drainage, and wetland topography. Survey the inlet near cross section E-14.5 or E-15 to minimize excavation.
4. **Hydraulic design of inflow and outflow:** Decisions are needed on inundation frequency, inflow discharge, maximum ponded volume, sediment dead storage, and [[Water Rights]].
