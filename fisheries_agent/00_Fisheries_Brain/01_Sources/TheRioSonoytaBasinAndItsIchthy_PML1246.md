# Río Sonoyta Basin and Its Ichthyofauna in the Pinacate Reserve: Study and Conservation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This paper, presented at the First International Pinacate Symposium (Hermosillo, Sonora, October 1988), assesses the fish fauna of the [[Río Sonoyta Basin]], an arid desert drainage shared by Sonora, Mexico, and Arizona, USA. Four species were collected in 1982: two native fishes, the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and the [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), and two exotics, [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) and [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus melas*). The authors conclude that the native fauna is threatened by habitat loss from groundwater pumping, agriculture, stream diversion, pollution and exotic species. They propose including the permanent-water portion of the river within the [[Pinacate Biosphere Reserve]] as a refuge, along with monitoring and environmental education.

## Background & Description

The Pinacate region lies within the Gran Desierto, part of the [[Sonoran Desert]], and is shaped by recent volcanic activity. The area was first protected as a forest reserve under the Secretaría de Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos (SARH) and later made a national park under the Secretaría de Desarrollo Urbano y Ecología (SEDUE). The reserve boundary includes a small section of the Río Sonoyta. The river is a former distributary of the Colorado River whose outlet shifted southward, probably during the Pleistocene, because of sediment from volcanic fields (Ives 1936, 1964; Hubbs and Miller 1948).

The Desert Pupfish is listed as endangered and has been the most studied species in the basin because its decline indicates deteriorating habitat conditions. Earlier reports of the pupfish from Laguna Prieta were judged erroneous by later workers (Miller and Fuiman 1987; Hendrickson and Varela-Romero, in press).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and climate.** The Sonoyta Basin covers 3,360 km² in an arid region. Mean annual surface runoff is given as 25 million m³. Weighted mean annual precipitation is 180 mm, while evaporation reaches 2,400 mm. Only short reaches of the channel hold permanent surface water, mainly near the town of Sonoyta. Most of the channel is intermittent.

**Threats.**
- Groundwater extraction: about 195 wells near the channel pump an average of 80,000,000 m³ per year. Combined with recent droughts, this has overdrafted the aquifer and reduced surface flow, a process that has continued since 1976 (Ing. Urrutia, SARH, pers. comm.).
- Agriculture and livestock: riparian vegetation was cleared to the river's edge at locality 5 (Ejido Josefa Ortiz de Domínguez) for farming and grazing.
- Pollution: domestic discharges carrying oils, greases and sewage, along with herbicides used in the region, degrade water quality.
- Exotic fishes: Mosquitofish and Black Bullhead are established, and bullheads have moved upstream through intermittent reaches during flood pulses, forming isolated populations in pools.

**Riparian habitat.** Gallery vegetation is described as very fragile in this arid setting, and its loss directly reduces habitat quality for associated fauna.

## Key Findings

**Survey methods.** Fish were sampled from late September to 9 October 1982 at eight localities. Gear included trammel nets (15 m and 8 m long, 6.4 × 3.2 mm mesh), a 3 mm mesh seine, and backpack electrofishing (Coffelt BP-4) at one site. Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin, transferred to 50% isopropanol, and deposited in the Centro Ecológico de Sonora reference collection. Field observations on channel condition were also recorded.

**Species composition.**
- Four species were collected: two native and two exotic.
- *Poeciliopsis* was reported for the basin by May (1976) and was not among the four species collected in 1982. McMahon and Miller (1982) treated it as not native to the basin, probably derived from the Gila River.
- The most abundant native fish was *Agosia chrysogaster*, and the pupfish was also found at several sites. Mosquitofish occurred at every site visited.
- Table 1 reports 1,027 specimens in total. The per-species percentages are too degraded in the scanned copy to reproduce reliably.

**Status trends.** Compared with earlier surveys (May 1926; McMahon and Miller 1985; Miller and Fuiman 1987), the authors judge conditions in the reserve section to be somewhat better in recent years. They attribute this mainly to wetter climatic conditions that temporarily increased habitat for native fishes rather than to management changes.

**Management recommendations.**
1. Adopt a long-term study and conservation program for the basin and its fish fauna, including periodic habitat and population assessment.
2. Extend the [[Biosphere Reserve Proposal]] to cover the permanent-water stretch of the Río Sonoyta, from the Sonoyta area to the Organ Pipe reserve, to reduce habitat loss and contamination and to provide a refuge for native fishes.
3. Evaluate the feasibility of reducing and eventually eliminating exotic fish populations.
4. Develop an environmental education program for residents of Sonoyta and neighboring ejidos to support conservation and cleanup efforts.
