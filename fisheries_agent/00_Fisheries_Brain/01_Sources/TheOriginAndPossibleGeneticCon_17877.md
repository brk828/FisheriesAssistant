# Origin and Possible Genetic Control of New, Stable Pigment Patterns in *Xiphophorus maculatus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 study by [[Kallman]] and [[Schreibman]] examines the origin of new, stable macromelanophore pigment patterns in the platyfish *Xiphophorus maculatus*. Pigment patterns in this species are controlled by sex-linked [[Macromelanophore]] genes that interact with autosomal and sex-linked modifiers, so their expression depends on the genetic background. Two exceptional males arose in laboratory stocks, one with a spotted-dorsal pattern linked to stripe-sided expression (*SrSd*) and one with a novel stripe-sided variant (*Sr'*). Both changes proved heritable and stable, which contrasts with the reversible pigment shifts previously seen after hybridization. The authors propose that the changes result from crossovers that separate a closely linked regulator gene from its structural pigment gene.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Xiphophorus]] has been studied for over 40 years, largely because interspecific hybrids of *X. maculatus* × *X. hellerii* produce pigment cell tumors (melanomas). Fish melanomas resemble human melanomas, and the fish melanoma shows the highest tyrosinase activity among the normal and abnormal vertebrate tissues tested, making these fish a valuable [[Biomedical Model Organisms]] tool.

Gordon named the large, densely pigmented sex-linked melanophores **macromelanophores**. Five natural macromelanophore patterns were recognized early on (Sd, Sp, Sr, N, and others), but the authors infer that many more exist. Key patterns include:

- **Sd** (spotted-dorsal): few black spots in the dorsal fin
- **Sp** (spot-sided) and **Sr** (stripe-sided)
- **N** (black-sided or nigra)
- Red patterns: **Dr** (dorsal-red), **Ar** (anal-red), and others

Two river populations, the Rio Jamapa (Mexico) and the Belize River (British Honduras), are both polymorphic for Sd, Dr, and Ar, but their patterns have different genetic bases. Pigment factors from one population give population-specific patterns only in their own gene pool.

Materials included three inbred Jamapa stocks (Jp 30, Jp 163 A, Jp 163 B), Hondo stocks (Hp-1, Hp-2) derived from fish collected in 1954, a Belize stock (Bp), a Lake Peten male, and a *X. couchianus* stock from the Rio Santa Catarina maintained for 25 generations. Methods included histology (Holly's fixative, Masson's trichrome, Van Gieson's stain) and standard pedigree scoring.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study is based on laboratory-maintained stocks rather than field ecology. Source populations are geographically distinct river systems in Mexico and British Honduras, and the authors note that wild fish with strong pigmentation are at one end of a spectrum of expression. Pigment patterns are described as "coadapted to their own gene pool," which suggests that gene interactions shaped by local populations determine phenotype. Heavily pigmented fish are generally not sustained in wild populations, and the authors discuss the possibility that natural selection eliminates deleterious macromelanophore mutations unless modifiers evolve to neutralize their effects.

## Key Findings

- **Jamapa crosses:** Of 3,305 progeny from crosses involving Jp males, all but one were either Sd or Sr. The exceptional male (ped. 1850-11) carried Sd linked to Sr (*SrSd*) on the Y chromosome.
- **Stable inheritance of SrSd:** The new pattern was maintained for five generations with no change. The dorsal fin was almost solid black, and caudal fin spotting ranged from 12 to 49 spots (mean 23) in eight-month-old males, compared with virtually none in standard Sd fish.
- **Sr' origin:** A second spontaneous change (ped. 1981-11), from a Sibun River female and a Jp 163 B male, produced a stable, heritable pattern (*Sr'*). It was distinct from Sr, showing wider pigmentation, dorsal fin involvement, and melanotic gonopodia. A fin graft from Jp 163 B survived on this male, supporting a complete set of Jamapa chromosomes.
- **Altered expression in foreign genotypes:**
  - In the Hondo (Hp) background, SrSd expression was reduced compared with Jamapa, and Sd was also decreased.
  - In *X. couchianus*, Sd was completely suppressed, while SrSd was strongly enhanced to melanotic and melanomatous dorsal and caudal fins. Sr expression was variable, ranging from enhanced stripes to zero expression.
- **Interpretation:** The authors favor a crossover model. Two macromelanophore genes became linked, and a regulator gene (R) adjacent to each structural gene was separated from its pigment locus. The phenotype in a foreign genotype is therefore primarily set by this regulator, which the authors call a position effect.
- **Crossover frequency:** Among 430 fish related to ped. 1850-11, six crossovers (1.4%) occurred, compared with a general sex-locus to macromelanophore-locus crossover rate of 0.2–0.3%.
- **Rarity:** Mutations to a new pattern were not detected among 18,228 fish with 20,370 Jamapa factors. The only deviant fish were 1850-11 and 1981-11.
- **Comparison with prior work:** [[Macintyre]] (1961) reported a similar Sd–Sr linkage in Np stock, but the phenotype there was normal until introduced into Jamapa, where Sd expression was enhanced. Atz, Kallman and Nigrelli (1963) had attributed similar abnormalities to position effect.
- **Conclusion:** The authors demonstrate that stable pigment changes in *Xiphophorus* can arise from crossing over within the macromelanophore locus, and that the foreign genetic background strongly influences the outcome.
