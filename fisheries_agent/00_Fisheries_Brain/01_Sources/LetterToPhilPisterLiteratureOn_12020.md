# Lake Victoria Cichlid Species Flock Conservation Correspondence

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This packet is a 1985–1986 conservation appeal concerning the [[Haplochromine Cichlids]] species flock of [[Lake Victoria]]. It contains a cover letter from [[Jack E Williams]] of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, a Leiden solicitation letter, the resolutions of the [[Cichlid Workshop]], an informational pamphlet by [[Cornelis D N Barel]] for the [[Haplochromis Ecology Survey Team]] (HEST), and an endorsement form signed by Williams. The core claim is that the introduced [[Nile Perch]] (*Lates* sp./*Lates niloticus*) is driving the native haplochromine cichlids toward extinction, possibly within a few years. The proposed response has five sub-resolutions: gene banking, reducing Nile perch numbers, reintroduction, expanded research, and permanent museum collections.

## Background & Description

**Documents in the packet**

- **Letter to E. Philip Pister (Desert Fishes Council), 7 February 1986:** Williams forwards literature on the threat to the Lake Victoria cichlids and suggests an immediate letter of support from the Desert Fishes Council. He offers to draft it.
- **Letter from Leiden, 20 September 1985:** M.J.P. van Oijen and A.J. Ribbink ask colleagues to sign the resolutions and comment. They also ask whether institutes can help by providing aquaria, gene bank facilities, or cryopreservation expertise.
- **Resolution of the Fifth Congress of European Ichthyologists:** Held at Stockholm, 12–16 August 1985. The Cichlid Workshop discussed the Lake Victoria problem in detail on 15–16 August. The resolution was proposed by Ribbink, seconded by Barel, and carried.
- **Pamphlet "Cichlid Species Flock of Lake Victoria on the Verge of Extinction":** Written by Barel on behalf of HEST, with 14 cited references.
- **Endorsement form:** Signed by Williams, Ph.D., U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Endangered Species Office, dated 7 February 1986.

**Taxa and species counts**

- The haplochromine cichlids of Lake Victoria comprise approximately 300 species. Most were discovered only recently, and new species are still being collected.
- Lake Victoria is the youngest of the three Great African Lakes with a similarly diverse cichlid flock. It originated less than a million years ago.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Nile perch introduction:** Nile perch entered the northern part of Lake Victoria in 1960. It apparently came from ponds where it had been stocked, while a dispute over the profitability of introducing it into the lake was pending. It then spread across the lake.
- **Timeline of decline:** An alarming increase in Nile perch, with a concurrent decline in haplochromines and other fishes, was noted in Kenyan waters at the end of the 1970s. In Mwanza (south-eastern corner), Nile perch was occasionally present in HEST trawl catches from 1977. A sudden exponential increase in catch occurred there in early 1984. Local people in the south-western corner reported a similar sudden appearance.
- **Habitat specialization:** Each haplochromine species has a restricted distribution tied to its ecological specializations, including diet and habitat (rocky shores, sand bottom, or papyrus fringes).
- **Deep-water penetration:** A 1985 HEST trawl survey in the south-western part of the lake found that in deeper water hardly any fish other than Nile perch were caught. HEST also observed Nile perch in deeper waters, so the predator's effects are not expected to be limited to shallow areas.
- **Social and environmental consequences:** Haplochromines formerly supported small-scale fisheries. In the Kenyan area, fishermen who depended on them were driven out, because Nile perch fishing requires more expensive nets and is controlled by wealthier individuals. Nile perch is an oily fish that cannot be sun-dried, so it must be smoked, which increases firewood demand and causes deforestation.
- **Knowledge gaps:** Only the Mwanza and Jinja areas have been investigated to any extent. Destruction could occur before the species' real diversity is documented.

## Key Findings

**Conservation resolution (Cichlid Workshop)**

The resolution states that, in a single ill-advised step, humans placed many vertebrate species at serious risk of extinction. It also threatened a food resource and traditional way of life of riparian dwellers. The five sub-resolutions are:

1. **Gene banks:** Place as many threatened species as possible in gene banks, using [[Cryopreservation]] and holding species in aquaria, to buy time.
2. **Nile perch reduction:** Carry out intensive selective fishing to drastically reduce Nile perch, and seek a practical biological control method through research.
3. **Reintroduction:** Return gene-banked species to the lake once Nile perch stocks are reduced enough for native populations to be substantially maintained.
4. **Research:** Greatly increase research to identify exactly which species are currently threatened, with funding and personnel to be found as soon as possible.
5. **Museum collections:** Make comprehensive [[Museum Collections]] as permanent records of Lake Victoria's diversity, since gene banks are not entirely safe refuges.

**Key claims from the pamphlet**

- Haplochromines and other endemic fishes have declined drastically over the past seven years due to the Nile perch introduction.
- In Kenyan waters, several hundred thousand haplochromines were caught per hour of trawling before the Nile perch. In 1984, only two specimens were obtained with similar effort, and the catch was mainly Nile perch.
- The Nile perch is an "alien predator" that grows to a size several orders of magnitude larger than the lake's native fauna.
- The lake's haplochromine flock is expected to largely disappear within a few years without precautions.

**Stated support and requests**

- Van Oijen and Ribbink requested endorsements, comments, and institutional help (aquaria, gene bank facilities, cryopreservation expertise) to publish the resolutions in ichthyological and zoological journals.
- Williams endorsed the resolution and sub-resolutions in full. He noted that Ash Meadows (Nevada) and [[Cuatro Cienegas]] (Coahuila, Mexico) are major centers of endemism in North America, but neither matches the species richness of the Lake Victoria cichlids. He wrote that the introduction of the Nile perch is "a disaster without parallel" to Lake Victoria as it has been known. He urged that native cichlids be preserved and the exotic Nile perch reduced and, ideally, eliminated.
- Further information is available from Dr. C.D.N. Barel at the Zoologisch Laboratorium, Leiden.
