# Zur tagesperiodik von; Niphargus aquilex schellenbergi Karaman (Gammaridae, Amphipoda)

#chronobiology #Gammaridae #Amphipoda #groundwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1963 study investigates the diel (day-night) activity rhythm of **[[Niphargus aquilex schellenbergi]]**, an eyeless, subterranean amphipod inhabiting groundwater springs. Contrary to earlier findings on related cave-dwelling crustaceans (e.g., **[[Niphargus puteanus puteanus]]** and *Orconectes*), which showed weak or non-24-hour (~10.8–12h) periodicity under constant environmental conditions, the authors demonstrate a clear **24-hour activity rhythm** in *N. a. schellenbergi*, measured via nocturnal drift (out-migration) from spring sources. The study further examines whether this rhythm is driven by light-dark cycling or by physicochemical factors such as temperature, CO₂, dissolved O₂, and KMnO₄ consumption (a proxy for organic content), concluding that **light (photoperiod)** is the dominant "Zeitgeber" (time-cue), while chemical/physical fluctuations are too minor within a single spring to serve this function.

## Background & Description

- **Species**: *Niphargus aquilex schellenbergi* KARAMAN
- **Taxonomic placement**: Family Gammaridae, Order Amphipoda
- Described as **eyeless (augenlos)** — a typical adaptation to permanent darkness in groundwater/cave habitats
- **Negatively phototactic** — actively avoids light, consistent with hypogean (subterranean) lifestyle
- No significant morphological differences noted between day- and night-collected individuals:
  - No significant **sex-ratio** differences between day and night drift samples
  - No significant **size differences** between day- and night-active individuals
- Comparative context: related species **[[Gammarus pulex]]** (a stream-dwelling, non-subterranean amphipod) was used in prior work by the same research group as a model for drift-based activity measurement

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Lives in groundwater springs (Quellen) under near-constant temperature and chemical conditions
- **Study site**: Multiple springs near Schlitz, Hesse, Germany (Hydrobiologische Anstalt der Max-Planck-Gesellschaft, Limnologische Flußstation)
- **Environmental stability**:
  - Temperature fluctuation within a single spring: ≤0.5°C over a 14-day recording period
  - Temperature differs by several degrees **between** different springs
- **Chemical parameters** (see Key Findings table) show much greater variability **between** springs than **within** a single spring over a day (diel) cycle
- **Feeding ecology**: Inhabits a food-poor (nahrungsarm) subterranean microhabitat; nocturnal drift is hypothesized to represent **foraging behavior**, allowing individuals to exploit food resources outside the spring itself under cover of darkness
- **Predator avoidance / phototaxis**: Negative phototaxis likely reduces predation risk and UV/light stress, reinforcing nocturnal emergence patterns

## Key Findings

### 1. Clear 24-hour (circadian) periodicity established
- Unlike *Niphargus puteanus puteanus* (BÜNNING et al.), which showed a ~10.8–12h periodicity with **no** 24h signal under 12:12 light-dark cycles, *N. a. schellenbergi* exhibits a **distinct 24-hour rhythm**, tied closely to sunrise/sunset.

### 4. Fine-scale (2h interval) drift patterns
- **25.–26.3.1963**: Night phase showed **two activity maxima** (bimodal nocturnal activity)
- **22.–23.4.1963** (repeated at same spring 4 weeks later, finer mesh 0.1mm): Confirmed the same bimodal nocturnal activity tendency despite a shorter night (10:14 light:dark ratio)
- Activity **onset** closely coupled to **sunset**
- Activity **offset** less tightly coupled to sunrise — attributed to the spring's westward-facing location in a deep gorge, delaying the light intensity threshold needed to trigger the phase shift, thus prolonging morning activity

### 5. Physicochemical factors — ruled out as primary "Zeitgeber"

| Parameter | Max daily variation (single spring) | Range across different springs |
|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | 5 mg/l | 14.4–30.2 mg/l |
| KMnO₄ consumption | 3.2 mg/l | 3.8–15.0 mg/l |
| Temperature | ≤0.5°C (14-day) | differs by several °C between springs |
| pH | 0.15 | 0.05–2.0 |

- Dissolved **O₂** showed an inverse relationship with CO₂ over the daily cycle (O₂ saturation deficit increases with light/temperature), but this pattern was **not synchronized** across different springs — it varied depending on each spring's sun exposure
- **Conclusion**: Because within-spring (diel) chemical variation is minor relative to between-spring variation, and O₂/CO₂ cycles are not synchronized with the observed behavioral rhythm across sites, chemical-physical factors are unlikely to serve as the primary time-cue (Zeitgeber)

### 6. Proposed driver of nocturnal drift
- **Light-dark cycle (photoperiod)** is identified as the most probable Zeitgeber, given the tight coupling of activity onset to sunset
- **Food acquisition** is proposed as the ultimate ecological/functional explanation: nocturnal emergence allows access to food resources beyond the nutrient-poor spring interior
