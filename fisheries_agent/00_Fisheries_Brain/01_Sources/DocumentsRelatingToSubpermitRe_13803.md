# Documents relating to subpermit renewal, collecting activities, information on species and supporting document for request for renewal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1992 USFWS endangered species subpermit renewal report and application filed by W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) on behalf of a research team including Michael E. Douglas, Thomas E. Dowling, and Paul C. Marsh. It details "take," monitoring, and captive transfer activities for multiple federally listed and sensitive fish species native to the Gila and Colorado River basins, conducted under Subpermit PRT-676811. The report emphasizes non-lethal research (PIT-tagging, scanning, catch-and-release) on wild populations, alongside a detailed research proposal justifying limited "take" of eggs and larvae from the newly listed [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in Lake Mohave to investigate a suspected recruitment failure linked to larval starvation. The report exemplifies interagency cooperation among ASU, USFWS, USBR, and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] via the [[Colorado River Fishes Working Group]].

## Background & Description

While primarily an administrative/regulatory document, it references key biological parameters used to define sampling and life-history classes:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: Adults captured were large-bodied (captured with net/trap methods sufficient for tagging); larvae targeted were **<25 mm** in length. Otolith aging indicates a maximum estimated longevity of **40–50 years**.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]**: Inadvertent mortalities were restricted to **adults >150 mm**, indicating this size class is more vulnerable to handling stress or capture methods (e.g., seining/trapping) than juveniles.
- **Larval staging** is a central morphological/developmental concern for razorback sucker: research specifically targets the transition point between successful hatching and larval mortality, occurring within roughly **three weeks post-hatch** in the wild.
- Fish handled across taxa were subject to **PIT-tagging** and scanning, implying body sizes sufficient for tag retention (i.e., subadult to adult classes for most cyprinids and catostomids).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada)**: Primary habitat for the remnant wild [[Razorback Sucker]] population and also supports [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*). This reservoir on the lower [[Colorado River]] is characterized by **low productivity**, which is hypothesized to starve razorback sucker larvae before they can recruit to older life stages.
- **[[Little Colorado River]] (Navajo County, AZ)**: Core habitat for the [[Humpback Chub]], with a large sample of 5,613 individuals captured across all post-larval age classes, indicating a robust, actively reproducing population relative to other listed species in this report.
- **[[Virgin River]] (Mohave County, AZ, at Beaver Dam Wash)**: Habitat for [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), a highly specialized sand/gravel-substrate cyprinid.
- **[[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] (Black Draw & Leslie Creek, Cochise County, AZ)**: Spring/stream refugium habitat supporting both [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis o. sonoriensis*) — isolated desert spring ecosystems near the US-Mexico border.
- **Captive/Refugium Systems**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) maintained in a backyard refugium pond (Tempe, AZ) and transferred to the Phoenix Zoo "Cheetah Moat" — an example of ex-situ conservation using non-traditional urban habitats.
- **[[Hassayampa River Preserve]] (Palm Lake, TNC property)**: Captive holding site for [[Bonytail]] broodstock, illustrating multi-agency refugium networks distinct from the primary Lake Mohave wild population.
- **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico)**: Serves as an ex-situ genetic reservoir and research comparison site for razorback sucker early life history, with broodstock originally sourced from Lake Mohave (preserving genetic continuity).

## Key Findings

- **Recruitment Failure Hypothesis (Razorback Sucker)**: Long-term biannual monitoring (since ~1985) in Lake Mohave has detected **no apparent recruitment** to the adult population. Combined with an aging adult cohort nearing maximum longevity (40–50 yrs), the population is projected to **decline and disappear within a decade** absent intervention.
- **Larval Bottleneck**: Research (ASU, Dexter NFH) indicates recruitment failure occurs at the **larval stage**, not from spawning failure — adults spawn successfully, but no larvae survive beyond ~3 weeks in the lake.
- **Nutritional Starvation Hypothesis**: Laboratory work suggests low reservoir productivity may cause larvae to die of starvation; a secondary, unaddressed **predation hypothesis** is noted as a possible interacting factor (poor nutrition may increase vulnerability to non-native piscivores).
- **Proposed 1993 Research Design**: A three-pronged sampling strategy was proposed to minimize wild population impact while enabling nutritional/physiological comparison:
  1. ~320 wild larvae collected (20/locality/date, Jan–Apr) from Lake Mohave.
  2. Non-hormone-induced stripping of eggs/sperm from 10–20 wild ripe adults (target ~10,000 eggs for ~5,000 larvae), with immediate release of adults unharmed.
  3. Backup larvae/ova sourced from Dexter NFH (same genetic stock) if field collection is inadequate.
- **Scale of Reproductive Output**: A single female razorback sucker spawns **>100,000 eggs/year**, making the proposed research egg-take (~10,000) ecologically insignificant at the population level.
- **Species-Specific Take Summary (1991–92)**:
  - [[Humpback Chub]]: 5,613 captured/tagged/released; 15 incidental mortalities (all >150 mm adults).
  - [[Razorback Sucker]]: ~500 adults captured, PIT-tagged, released unharmed at Lake Mohave.
  - [[Bonytail]]: 3 wild specimens tagged/released; ~50 hatchery-origin young-of-year received for allozyme/mtDNA analysis; 13 adults transferred from Hassayampa refugium to USFWS Region 6.
  - [[Yaqui Chub]] & [[Yaqui Topminnow]]: ~50 and ~100 individuals respectively, captured and immediately released (no lethal take).
  - [[Woundfin]]: 3 adults captured/released.
  - No take reported for [[Virgin Chub]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Desert Pupfish]] (wild), [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]], or [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **Regulatory Mechanism**: Illustrates use of **Section 6 (Endangered Species Act) cooperative agreements** between AGFD and USFWS as a legal vehicle enabling continued field research immediately after a species' emergency federal listing (paralleling prior precedent with the Mohave desert tortoise listing in 1989).
- **Multi-Agency Governance Structure**: Establishes the [[Colorado River Fishes Working Group]] (USBR, USFWS, NPS, AGFD, NDOW, ASU) as the central coordinating body for razorback sucker and Colorado squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) conservation across the Lower Colorado Basin.
