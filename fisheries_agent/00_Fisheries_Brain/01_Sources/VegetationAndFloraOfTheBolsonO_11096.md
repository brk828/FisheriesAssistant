# Vegetation and Flora of the Bolsón of Cuatro Ciénegas Region, Coahuila, Mexico: III. Cactaceae to Compositae

#botany #floristics #cactaceae #compositae

## Executive Summary

This is the third installment of a floristic survey of the vascular plants of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] and surrounding ranges in [[Coahuila]], Mexico, by [[Donald J. Pinkava]] of the Department of Botany and Microbiology, Arizona State University. It annotates the families from Cactaceae through Compositae, covering 36 families and several hundred taxa. Each entry gives the habitat, relative abundance (common to very uncommon), and collection localities, coded to the [[Minckley Grid System]] and to collector numbers. The survey documents a flora dominated by desert shrubs and cacti on bajadas, a strong gypsum-soil element, and an upland flora of oak-pine forest in the adjacent mountains. Parts I and II were published in the *Boletín de la Sociedad Botánica de México* in 1979 and 1980.

## Background & Description

- **Publication:** *Boletín de la Sociedad Botánica de México*, No. 41, 1981. Parts I and II appeared in volumes 38 (1979) and 39 (1980).
- **Scope:** Systematic checklist of vascular plants from Cactaceae to Compositae, with abundance codes and locality data.
- **Method:** Each taxon is listed with an abundance category, habitat, and localities. Localities cite field numbers (P, MCJ, H, W, IMJ and others), herbarium-type specimens, and collections by [[Meyer]], [[Minckley]], [[Turner]], [[Richardson]] and others. Several records are marked "not seen," meaning they are cited from literature only.
- **Locality key:** The appended gazetteer lists lagunas (for example [[Laguna Churince]], [[Laguna Grande]], [[Laguna Salada]], [[El Mojarral]]), pozos (for example [[Pozo Anteojo]], [[Los Fresnos]], [[Juan Santos]]), and rivers (for example [[Río Cañón]] and [[Río Mesquites]]). Distances are straight-line kilometers from the town of Cuatro Ciénegas.
- **Named sites:** Local terms and place abbreviations (for example "SSM," "SM," "CC," "P.") are used throughout the text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The flora is organized by habitat and substrate:

- **Bajadas and flats:** Lower alluvial slopes and desert flats carry the most common taxa, including [[Echinocactus horizonthalonius]], [[Opuntia]] species, [[Lophophora williamsii]], and shrubs such as [[Randia pringlei]], [[Lippia graveolens]] and [[Leucophyllum frutescens]].
- **Gypsum dunes and gypseous soils:** A distinctive assemblage of gypsophiles occurs on dunes and flats around the lagunas. Examples are [[Tiquilia turneri]], [[Tiquilia gossypina]], [[Sartwellia mexicana]], [[Varilla mexicana]], [[Gaillardia gypsophila]], [[Dyssodia gypsophia]] and [[Machaeranthera gypsophila]].
- **Wet habitats:** Lagunas, pozos, springs, streams and marshes host aquatic and riparian taxa such as [[Samolus ebracteatus]], [[Bacopa monnieri]], [[Eustoma exaltatum]], [[Utricularia obtusa]], [[Mentha rotundifolia]], [[Phyla nodiflora]] and [[Eupatorium betonicifolium]]. Several are described as abundant or very common near water.
- **Saline flats:** [[Haploesthes robusta]] is an endemic of saline flats near lagunas.
- **Mountain slopes and forests:** Chaparral and oak-pine forest on the mountain ranges (for example [[Sierra San Marcos]] and the [[Mt. Anteojo]] complex) support taxa absent from the basin floor, including [[Cornus stolonifera]], [[Arbutus texana]], [[Garrya ovata]], [[Salvia regla]] and [[Penstemon henricksonii]].

## Key Findings

1. **Inventory:** The survey covers 36 families, from Cactaceae through Compositae. Compositae contains by far the largest number of entries, reflecting the diversity of desert shrubs and herbs.
2. **Cacti:** Common desert cacti include [[Coryphantha strobiliformis]], [[Epithelantha micromeris]], [[Ferocactus hamatocanthus]] and [[Lophophora williamsii]]. Several species are restricted to specific microhabitats, such as [[Ariocarpus fissuratus]] on lower pajadas. Pinkava also describes [[Opuntia anteojoensis]], a locally common species from gentle bajada slopes. The type of this species is cited as LL-TEX, from about 21 km west of Cuatro Ciénegas.
3. **Gypsum endemism:** Several taxa are endemic to or strongly associated with gypsum soils. Among them are [[Gaillardia gypsophila]] (endemic on gypsum dunes), [[Erigeron pinkavii]] (uncommon gypsophile on bajadas), [[Tiquilia turneri]] (common in gypsum dunes) and [[Limonium limbatum]] (uncommon in gypsum soils near water).
4. **Endemic and new taxa noted:** [[Haploesthes robusta]] is described as an endemic of saline flats. The text also proposes a new variety, *Viguiera stenoloba* var. *chihuahuensis* (manuscript name, Butterwick), found on bajadas and in arroyos, with records cited from several sites.
5. **Range observations:** [[Ipomoea sagittata]] is described as apparently disjunct from coastal populations. [[Forestiera angustifolia]] is intermediate with [[Forestiera pubescens]]. [[Gilia stewartii]] is intermediate with *G. platyloba*. Several other taxa are noted as intermediate, atypical or tentatively identified, usually from sterile or immature material.
6. **Upland disjuncts:** Taxa of conifer and oak forests, such as [[Cornus stolonifera]] (noted as fairly common, with leaves pilose beneath), [[Monotropa hypopithys]] (rare) and [[Arbutus texana]] (common), are recorded from the mountain ranges rather than the basin floor.
7. **Abundance and distribution:** Most entries are qualified as common, uncommon or infrequent. Records cluster around the lagunas and pozos, the Río Cañón and Puerto Salado, and the mountain localities of Sierra San Marcos and Mt. Anteojo.
8. **Historical and reference value:** The survey gives a baseline of collection localities and field numbers for the basin's flora, including localities that were later affected by water extraction. Laguna de Ferriño is noted as dried in 1966–1967.
