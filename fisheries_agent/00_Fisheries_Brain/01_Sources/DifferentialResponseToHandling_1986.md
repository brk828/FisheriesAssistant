# Differential Response to Handling Stress by Florida, Northern, and Hybrid Largemouth Bass

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This 1986 study by Williamson and Carmichael examined physiological and survival responses to handling/net-confinement stress in electrophoretically verified strains of [[Largemouth Bass]]: the [[Northern Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides salmoides*), the [[Florida Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides floridanus*), and their reciprocal F1 hybrids (N x F and F x N). While resting plasma glucose and chloride concentrations did not differ among strains, 12–24 h of net confinement produced markedly divergent stress responses. Florida bass and the N x F hybrid showed the greatest physiological disturbance and began dying before confinement even ended, whereas Northern bass showed minimal stress response and superior survival. After a 7-day recovery period, only 12% of Florida bass survived compared to 96% of Northern bass, with hybrids showing intermediate survival (31–81%). The findings demonstrate genetically based differences in [[Handling Stress]] tolerance between subspecies, with important implications for hatchery culture and stocking practices, and no evidence of hybrid vigor (heterosis) in stress tolerance.

## Background & Description
- Florida and Northern largemouth bass subspecies are **morphologically very similar** and cannot be reliably distinguished by external morphology alone; accurate subspecific/hybrid identification requires **protein electrophoresis** ([[Electrophoretic Identification]]).
- Body size differed substantially among strains at time of testing (not attributable to morphology but to differential growth):
  - Florida (F x F): 78 ± 2 g (SE) (separately reared); 82 ± 2 g (communally reared)
  - Northern (N x N): 120 ± 5 g (separately reared); 152 ± 3 g (communally reared)
  - Hybrids: intermediate weights in both rearing conditions
- Prior work (Carmichael et al. 1984b) established that body size (71–144 g range) does not significantly affect plasma stress indices or mortality rates, supporting the interpretation that observed differences reflect genetic/physiological strain differences rather than size effects.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both subspecies are native to distinct but overlapping geographic ranges in the southeastern USA, separated by a natural **intergrade belt** north and east of the Gulf of Mexico ([[Bailey and Hubbs 1949]]); this zone has become blurred due to widespread indiscriminate stocking.
- Latitudinal distribution patterns imply differing **thermal tolerances** and inherent physiological adaptations between subspecies (e.g., [[Cold Shock Tolerance]] differences documented by Cichra et al. 1982).
- Both subspecies are intensively cultured and stocked in temperate and subtropical freshwater systems as prized [[Game Fish]], making hatchery-related physiological stress responses of direct management relevance.
- Experimental fish were reared in 0.04-hectare earthen ponds (both single-strain and communal stocking designs) then transferred to raceways/tanks supplied with constant-flow well water (23°C; dissolved oxygen 5–6 mg/L; pH 7.3; hardness 320 mg/L; CO₂ 35 mg/L) — representative of standard hatchery aquaculture conditions rather than natural habitat.

## Key Findings

### Resting (Quiescent) Plasma Chemistry
- No significant differences among the four strains in baseline plasma glucose (68–74 mg/dL) or plasma chloride (101–115 meq/L) at pond harvest (ANOVA, P < 0.05).
- Weighing/hauling procedures increased plasma glucose similarly across all strains (98–156 mg/dL) with no detectable effect on plasma chloride.

### Response to Net Confinement (Handling Stress)
- By 3 h of confinement, plasma glucose and chloride values shifted and **significant strain differences emerged**.
- By 6 h, glucose remained elevated and chloride declined, with strain differences intensifying.
- By 24 h, [[Northern Largemouth Bass]] showed **significantly less stress response** (smaller glucose increase, smaller chloride decrease) than all other strains.
- Florida bass and N x F hybrids showed the **greatest deviation** in plasma glucose/chloride and began dying before the 24-h confinement period ended; all were dead by 1 day into recovery.
- A complete physiological sampling sequence (through 24 h confinement + 14 d recovery) could only be obtained for the Northern strain, as other strains suffered high mortality.

### Mortality Outcomes
- **7-day post-confinement survival** (Treatment S-1):
  - Northern (N x N): 96% survival
  - Florida (F x F): 12% survival
  - Hybrids: 31–81% survival (intermediate, strain- and cross-direction dependent)
- **Pooled mortality across S-2, S-3, C-1, C-2, C-3 treatments**:
  - F x F: 88% mortality
  - N x N: 4% mortality
  - N x F: 69% mortality
  - F x N: 19% mortality
- Florida bass were so intolerant of confinement that experimental protocols had to be modified (shortened confinement time, or reduced net density) to prevent fish from losing the ability to remain upright before the standard 24-h period ended.
- Acclimatization periods of 14–26 days in well water prior to confinement, and reduced-severity confinement for F x F fish, **did not change the relative stress tolerance ranking** among strains — Florida bass remained the least tolerant under all conditions tested.

### Interpretation
- Differential responses reflect **real genetically based physiological differences** between subspecies, not artifacts of fish size, rearing history, or misidentification (confirmed via electrophoresis).
- **No hybrid vigor (heterosis)** was observed for stress tolerance — neither hybrid cross outperformed the best parental (Northern) strain, consistent with patterns of heterosis being uncommon in fish for most traits (cf. [[Common Carp]] studies, Moav et al. 1975).
- Directionality of the cross (maternal effects) appeared to matter: F x N hybrids notably outperformed N x F hybrids in survival, suggesting a possible **maternal or cytoplasmic influence** on stress response that warrants further study.
- Findings support practical hatchery/management recommendation: **exercise particular care during harvest, hauling, and stocking of Florida largemouth bass and F1 hybrids with Florida maternal lineage**, as they are substantially more vulnerable to handling-induced mortality than Northern largemouth bass.
- Plasma glucose (indicator of generalized stress) and plasma chloride (indicator of osmoregulatory dysfunction) proved to be reliable predictive correlates of subsequent mortality risk under confinement stress.
