# Revised Checklist of the Coastal Marine Fishes of Nayarit, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ichthyofauna

## Executive Summary

This paper presents an updated checklist of the coastal marine fishes of [[Nayarit]], Mexico, on the Mexican Pacific coast. Published in *Publicaciones Biológicas* (F.C.B./U.A.N.L., Vol. 6, No. 2, 1992, pp. 124–132) by María Elena García-Ramírez and María de Lourdes Lozano-Vilano of the Laboratorio de Ictiología, U.A.N.L., it addresses a major gap: published work on the marine ichthyofauna of Nayarit is scarce. The authors combined literature records, including the [[Islas Marías]], with nine collections made in 1983–1990. The combined list comprises 55 families, 117 genera and 149 species (28 unidentified). The 1990 collections yielded 828 specimens from 37 families, 94 species (16 unidentified), of which 67 are new records for the state.

## Background & Description

Earlier work on the region was limited. The only prior record for the Nayarit mainland cited was Dawson (1968) for [[San Blas]], which reported *Microdesmus dorsipunctatus*. Most other records came from the Islas Marías, based on studies by Brock (1943), Briggs (1951, 1955, 1960), Rosenblatt and Parr (1969) and Lozano-Vilano et al. (1988), who listed 37 species from Isla María Madre. Many broader regional works, such as Thomson and McKibbin (1978), Burgess and Axelrod (1984) and Castro-Aguirre (1976), covered the [[Gulf of California]] and adjacent states but did not give locality-level data for Nayarit.

The study area lies between 21°00' and 21°50' N and 105°10' and 106°53' W. Collections were made at:

- **Three trawl stations (Arrastre 86-1, 86-2, 86-3)** off Nayarit, 15 October 1986, using the research vessel B/O *El Puma* of the [[Instituto De Ciencias Del Mar Y Limnologia]], UNAM.
- **Two shrimp-boat collections** off Chacala (29 November 1984 and 1985).
- **Four shoreline localities:** Aticama/San Blas (August 1983), Piedra de Elefante near Aticama (August 1983), Santa Cruz 12 km W of Aticama (October 1989), and 2 km W of Santa Cruz (October 1990).

Gear consisted of a 3 m beach seine (*chinchorro*) and a trawl. Specimens were fixed following Hall et al. (1962), and taxonomic arrangement followed Greenwood et al. (1966). Voucher material is housed in the U.A.N.L. ichthyology collection. For each taxon the list gives its status (IM = Islas Marías, CN = Nayarit coast, RL = literature record) and, for new records, the voucher number, specimen count, size range and general distribution.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors describe the fauna as mostly widely distributed species typical of the region. Zoogeographically, the species belong mainly to the [[Panamic Province]]:

- **Panamic Province:** 84% of species
- **Pantropical:** 8%
- **Californian Province:** 4%
- **Gulf of California:** 2%
- **Local (endemic to the area):** 2%

Several species are relevant to fisheries. The authors note that the coastal fish fauna of Nayarit is poorly known and that more detailed studies are needed to define the use of commercially important species and to support appropriate regulation. The collections also documented estuarine and freshwater-entering species, such as *Dormitator latifrons*, whose range extends into rivers along the tropical American west coast.

## Key Findings

**Checklist totals**
- Combined list (literature plus this study): **55 families, 117 genera, 149 species (28 unidentified)**, including the Islas Marías.
- 1990 collections: **828 specimens**, **37 families**, **75 genera** (abstract), **94 species (16 unidentified)**.
- **67 species are new records** for the coastal fish fauna of Nayarit.

*Data note:* The Discussion section gives 78 genera for the collected material, while the Abstract and Methods give 75. Both figures are reported as published.

**Representative new records (CN, NR)**
- *Rhinobatos glaucostigma* (Pacific guitarfish): 6 specimens from trawls and the shoreline, 307–316 mm LP.
- *Aetobatus narinari* (spotted eagle ray): 1 specimen, 222 mm disc length, 2 km W of Santa Cruz.
- *Elops affinis*: 3 specimens, 288–290 mm LP.
- *Albula vulpes* (bonefish), from a shrimp-boat collection.
- *Opisthonema medirastre*: 8 specimens, 115–136 mm LP.
- *Centropomus nigriscens* and *Centropomus robalito*: snooks recorded from both shoreline and trawl samples.
- *Diplectrum pacificum*: 16 specimens across two trawl stations.
- *Corvula macrops*: 1 specimen, 150.9 mm LP. Jordan and Evermann (1886) previously recorded it only from Mazatlán, Sinaloa, based on a single specimen, so this is the **second record for the Mexican Pacific coast**.
- *Dormitator latifrons* (estuarine/river-entering): 12 specimens, 95.7–224 mm LP.

**Most abundant collected taxa:** *Pomadasys panamensis* (31 specimens from a single trawl), *Diapterus peruvianus* (35 specimens from one trawl), *Pseudupeneus grandisquamis* (23 specimens), *Xenichthys* sp. (20 specimens) and *Chloroscombrus orqueta* (13–14 specimens per sample).

**Taxonomic notes**
- Several species were recorded only to genus, such as *Ophichthus*, *Arius*, *Cynoscion*, *Syacium* and *Sphoeroides*, reflecting the 28 unidentified taxa in the combined list.
- Some species, such as *Ginglymostoma cirratum*, *Alopias superciliosus* and *Paralabrax nebulifer*, have distributional ranges spanning from California to Panama or Ecuador.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- The authors state that this checklist will serve as a baseline for future work on Nayarit's coastal ichthyofauna.
- More detailed studies are needed to support sustainable fishery management and appropriate regulation of commercially important species.
