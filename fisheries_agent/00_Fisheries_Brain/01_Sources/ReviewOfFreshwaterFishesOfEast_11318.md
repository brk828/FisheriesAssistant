## Executive Summary

This is a 1968 book review published in *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society*, evaluating the second edition (1967) of F. B. Scott's *Freshwater Fishes of Eastern Canada* (University of Toronto Press, 137 p.). The reviewer, Frank J. Schwartz, highlights that the fish fauna of eastern Canada — particularly the [[Great Lakes]] and [[St. Lawrence River]] regions — is undergoing rapid, human-driven transformation due to pollution, hydroelectric dam construction, and watershed diversions. The review notes taxonomic and range updates between the 1954/1955 and 1967 editions, an expanded species list (142 → 154 species), added family accounts, newly recognized species, revised trout taxonomy, and a list of successful/unsuccessful salmonid introductions. The reviewer praises the book's structure but criticizes its organization and incomplete taxonomic keys, and calls for stronger conservation action to prevent further degradation of Canada's freshwater fish faunas.

## Background & Description

The source is a review/bibliographic commentary and does not provide primary morphological data (e.g., meristics, body proportions, coloration) for any species. It does, however, note taxonomic reclassifications relevant to morphological identity:

- **[[Kamloops Trout]]** — previously listed separately in 1955, now reclassified/subsumed under **[[Rainbow Trout]]**.
- **Aurora Trout** — previously listed separately, now considered synonymous with **[[Brook Trout]]**.
- **Marston's Trout** — previously listed separately, now considered synonymous with **[[Arctic Char]]**.
- **[[Splake]]** — a hybrid trout (cross between [[Lake Trout]] and [[Brook Trout]]), newly discussed in the 1967 edition.
- The 1967 edition introduces a **family-level identification key** covering 27 families, though only the trout ([[Salmonidae]]) and minnow ([[Cyprinidae]]) families are keyed down to species level, and only sparingly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The review describes substantial ecological and biogeographic disruption within the eastern Canadian freshwater fish fauna, concentrated in the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence system:

- **Invasive/introduced species establishing populations**: [[Sea Lamprey]], [[Alewife]], and [[White Perch]] have invaded the region, contributing to ecosystem-level disruption.
- **Declining or extirpated native populations**: [[Blue Pike]], [[Lake Whitefish]], [[Cisco]], and [[Lake Trout]] populations have been restricted in range, reduced in abundance, or extirpated in parts of the Great Lakes basin.
- **Drivers of decline**: pollution, hydroelectric dam construction, and watershed diversions are identified as the primary anthropogenic stressors reshaping the regional fish fauna.
- **Regional variation in impact**: The freshwater fish faunas of **Quebec** and the **Maritime Provinces** are reported as comparatively unaffected ("spared, for the moment") relative to the Great Lakes and St. Lawrence River systems, suggesting these regions retain more intact native fish communities.
- **Salmonid introductions**: [[Arctic Grayling]], [[Pink Salmon]], [[Chum Salmon]], [[Kokanee Salmon]], [[Coho Salmon]], and [[Chinook Salmon]] have all been introduced into eastern Canadian waters. Of these, all transplants except **Coho** and **Chinook Salmon** are reported as successfully established, implying differential habitat suitability or competitive/environmental barriers to Pacific salmon establishment in eastern Canadian systems.

## Key Findings

- The eastern Canadian freshwater fish fauna, especially in the **Great Lakes and St. Lawrence River region**, is undergoing rapid decline driven by pollution, dam construction, and watershed diversion — sufficient to require major revisions to species accounts between the 1955 and 1967 editions.
- The documented species list expanded from **142 species (1955) to 154 species (1967)**, reflecting both improved taxonomic resolution and the addition of newly recognized or introduced species.
- A new family account for **[[Bowfin]] (Amiidae)** was added in the 1967 edition.
- Five new species accounts were added: **[[Pigmy Whitefish]], [[Pink Salmon]], [[Kokanee Salmon]], [[Spotted Sucker]], and [[Black-spotted Stickleback]]**, along with new commentary on the **[[Splake]]** hybrid.
- Three previously distinct trout taxa (**Kamloops, Aurora, and Marston's trout**) were taxonomically absorbed into **Rainbow Trout, Brook Trout, and Arctic Char**, respectively, reducing the 1967 species list.
- Of six salmonid species introduced to eastern Canada (**Arctic Grayling, Pink, Chum, Kokanee, Coho, Chinook**), only the **Coho and Chinook Salmon** introductions failed to establish successfully.
- The reviewer identifies a structural weakness in the book: species accounts are frequently interrupted by general family-level discussions, disrupting narrative flow despite the appealing use of species photographs to open each entry.
- The review closes with a conservation appeal, urging Canada to act proactively — unlike other nations, including the reviewer's own — to protect freshwater fish faunas before further irreversible losses occur.
