# Little Colorado River (LCR) Bibliography: Draft Annotated Reference Database (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #hydrology #bibliography

## Executive Summary

This draft annotated bibliography, prepared in September 1992 by the [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]] for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, catalogs published reports, peer-reviewed literature, maps, unpublished reports, memoranda, historical photos and slides relevant to the Little Colorado River (LCR) basin. The compilers state that the list is incomplete and may contain errors in authorship, categorization, years, titles or publishers. At the time of writing it contained 1,362 numbered citations (plus 42 in a temporary database and about 10 not yet entered), organized alphabetically by author and year and tagged by subject. A final bibliography was scheduled for August 30, 1993.

The document is a reference index rather than a research study. Its value lies in its subject coverage: fish and aquatic biology, hydrology and water supply, geology and uranium mining, riparian ecology, and water law and management.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** To support GCES and related planning by consolidating LCR literature, with copies held where indicated by an "LCR#" designation.
- **Compilers:** Tom Garrison, Annette Polt and Claudia Willeto.
- **Citation sources:** Published reports, peer-reviewed journals, maps; unpublished reports, internal memoranda and notes; historical photographs; recent slides.
- **Organization:** Citations are numbered and categorized under subject headings (e.g., BIOLOGY:FISH, HYDROLOGY:GROUNDWATER, GEOLOGY:MINING, ENVIRONMENTAL:REGULATORY).
- **Scale of subject categories (approximate citation counts from the index):**
  - BIOLOGY:FISH: the largest biology category, with well over 200 numbers.
  - HYDROLOGY:GROUNDWATER: over 200 numbers.
  - ENVIRONMENTAL:MANAGEMENT: roughly 200 numbers.
  - HYDROLOGY:WATER SUPPLY: roughly 200 numbers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography reflects the basin's main environmental concerns as of 1992:

- **Native and endangered fishes:** Heavy coverage of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Woundfin]], [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Sonoran Topminnow]]. Many entries are unpublished field notes, capture records and status reviews.
- **Water supply and groundwater:** Numerous studies of aquifers including the [[N Aquifer]], [[Coconino Aquifer]] and [[Navajo Sandstone]], with pumping effects from [[Peabody Coal Company]] mining on [[Black Mesa]].
- **Water quality and contamination:** Uranium mill tailings effects on the [[Puerco River]], [[Church Rock Uranium Mill Spill]], and [[Grants Mineral Belt]] groundwater contamination.
- **Riparian ecology:** Vegetation changes along the lower Colorado and Little Colorado rivers, [[Tamarix]] (salt cedar) invasion, and riparian management guidelines.
- **Hydrology and geomorphology:** Flood histories, arroyo cutting, sediment loads, and climate-driven channel change, including [[Tree-Ring Reconstruction]] of streamflow.
- **Regulation and management:** The [[Arizona Groundwater Management Act]] of 1980, instream flow proposals, and riparian habitat task force recommendations.

## Key Findings

The document states no research findings of its own. Its main points are these:

1. **Scope and status.** The bibliography is a draft, with known gaps, and a final version was planned for August 1993.
2. **Fish emphasis.** Fish-related citations form one of the largest categories, supported by many references on the [[Humpback Chub]] in the Little Colorado River and Grand Canyon, including a 1990 humpback chub monitoring report and a [[Hendrickson]] et al. (1989) native fish monitoring summary.
3. **Groundwater dependence.** Several sources note that [[Groundwater]] is the principal water supply for many communities. For example, Brown (1994 citation in the index) reports average Black Mesa withdrawals of 5,480 acre-feet per year (1972–1986), of which 3,820 acre-feet supported a coal mine, and projects water levels 60 feet lower by 2031 under the proposed mining plan.
4. **Water chemistry in diatremes.** Hopi Buttes volcanic-field wells showed dissolved solids from 262 to 8,140 ppm, and springs from 246 to 1,450 ppm. Of 14 test wells, eight produced adequate potable water, three were insufficient, and three were dry.
5. **Uranium contamination.** The Grants Mineral Belt study found that only seven of 72 sampling locations had radium-226 concentrations above 3 pCi/L, attributable to mining and milling activity.
6. **Sediment and climate.** Hereford's studies describe the Little Colorado as an ephemeral stream draining about 44,000 square km. Its channel was wide and vegetation-free until at least 1987, and discharge fell to about 57% of earlier levels during the 1940s–1950s drought.
7. **Fish physiology.** A 1965 swimming study found that large Colorado squawfish (432 mm) had fatigue-time ratios of 2.0–2.3 versus 4.0–4.5 for small individuals (104 mm), with swimming ability increasing at higher water temperatures.
8. **Management needs.** Multiple references call for integrated water supply and quality management, instream flow protection, and riparian rehabilitation, and the Arizona Governor's Riparian Habitat Task Force (1990) was charged with classifying and inventorying riparian areas.
