# Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Lower Colorado River: Limitrophe Division

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-ecology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This appendix to a study report characterises the aquatic habitats, fish fauna, benthos, higher aquatic plants, pigments and physico-chemical conditions of the [[Limitrophe Division]] of the lower [[Colorado River]] and the surrounding [[Yuma Valley]], sampled in 1974–75. The river segment below [[Morelos Dam]] forms the international boundary between the United States and Mexico, and nearly all surface flow is diverted into Mexico. Most aquatic habitat in the area consists of canals, agricultural drains and small backwaters. Fish assemblages were dominated by introduced and warm-water tolerant species, benthos by chironomids and oligochaetes, and standing crops of plants and algae were high relative to the mainstream. No critical conditions for biota were found, although dissolved oxygen fell to lethal levels for some fishes in certain drains.

## Background & Description

The Limitrophe Division downstream of Morelos Dam covers roughly 377 ha of total surface water in the Yuma Valley. Channel water is supplied largely by the [[Wellton-Mohawk Drain]] and by seepage from irrigated lands. Study sampling used seines, hoop nets, gill nets, a [[Ekman Dredge]] for benthos, quadrats for vegetation, and water chemistry profiles.

Habitat composition (Table LI-I) was summarised as follows:

- **Mainstream Colorado River** below Morelos Dam: about 34.6 linear km with average width near 3.7 m and surface area about 25.3 ha.
- **Major canals**: about 77 km, roughly 47 ha.
- **Major drains**: about 154 km, roughly 26 ha.
- **Lateral canals and minor drains**: about 125 km, roughly 33 ha.
- **Backwaters** (including [[Gadsen Lake]], [[Hunters Hole]] and off-river connectives): about 17 km, roughly 12 ha.
- **Totals**: about 440 km of linear habitat and about 140 ha; canals and drains together represent about 55% of surface water.

The river is channelised and deeply incised (2–5 m) through alluvial soils. Depths in the mainstream rarely exceeded 2 m. Hunters Hole reached just over 5 m. Currents are relatively uniform and rarely exceed about 0.7 m/s.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fishes.** Mainstream seining produced 11 species, averaging about 7,300 individuals and nearly 6.0 kg/ha. [[Red Shiner]] was most numerous (67.5% of catch). Poeciliids ([[Sailfin Molly]] and [[Mosquitofish]]) made up almost 20%, and juvenile [[Mozambique Mouthbrooder]] about 2.2%. By weight, the top five species (sailfin molly, mouthbrooder, red shiner, bluegill and mosquitofish) accounted for 86.1%. Gadsen Lake, a cut-off, long-isolated pool choked with spiny naiad, yielded about 127.9 kg/ha and 31,200 fish/ha, with an unusually high carnivore proportion (35%) including [[Black Crappie]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]]. Drain habitat had moderate abundance but very low diversity (six species), dominated by poeciliids and [[Tilapia]] of tropical origin. Hoop-net catches at night were dominated by catfishes ([[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]]). Striped mullet ([[Striped Mullet]]) and mouthbrooder were absent from Gadsen Lakes, which the report attributes to long isolation and lack of introduction there. Striped mullet young were, however, collected at Morelos Dam, suggesting local spawning, with most reproduction likely occurring downstream near the Gulf of California.

**Benthos.** Benthos was sampled with an Ekman dredge in fine sediments. Chironomid dipterans and oligochaete worms, together with the [[Asiatic Clam]], dominated. Chironomids and oligochaetes made up 83–98% of organisms in mainstream samples and about 99% in backwaters. Clams contributed 89–97% of biomass in river segments. Biomass estimates ranged from 1.3 to 13.5 g/m² and 1.3 to 3.5 mg/m² when large dragonfly larvae were excluded. Crayfish ([[Procambarus clarkii]]) and shrimp were abundant but not sampled quantitatively.

**Higher aquatic plants and algae.** Emergent species dominated, with submergent plants such as [[Potamogeton pectinatus]] in flowing water and [[Najas]] in backwaters. Standing crops in canals averaged more than twice those of the most vegetated mainstream reach, and drift was almost five times higher. Standing crop estimates in drains were up to two orders of magnitude above the mainstream. Canal estimates were derived from heaps of plants removed during mechanical clearing, and the report notes that canal clearing had last been done in June, so crops represent about 2.5 months of growth. Bluegreen algal mats were common and drifted daily.

**Pigments.** Chlorophyll and related pigments were higher than elsewhere in the lower Colorado basin. Of the samples, 280 fell in magnitude class III (10.0–99.99 mg/m³) and 143 in class II (1.0–9.99 mg/m³). The highest values occurred in backwaters, where 50 of 71 class III samples came from. Nutrient loading from Yuma municipality and irrigated agriculture, plus sub-tropical climate and flow-through conditions, were cited as the main drivers.

**Physico-chemistry.** Wellton-Mohawk Drain showed strong diel cycling of dissolved oxygen, pH and PO4-P, indicating substantial productivity. Mainstream waters carried high inorganic PO4-P and showed diel oxygen fluctuations, but no evidence of critical loading was found. Canals had lower conductance (by almost 3,000 µmhos in many cases) and more stable pH than the river, and higher dissolved oxygen at all times, probably because of management manipulation and high flow speed. In drains, dissolved oxygen fell below 2.0 ppm in the Southwest and South Central drains, lethal to some fishes, and temperatures exceeded 32 °C. A few localised fish kills occurred in drains, some possibly from low oxygen or heat. Gadsen Lake and Hunters Hole displayed contrasting vertical profiles reflecting depth, wind exposure and fish-driven mixing.

## Key Findings

- The Limitrophe Division is a highly modified system of canals, drains and small backwaters; the mainstream channel is channelised and receives little natural inflow.
- Mainstream fish communities were numerically dominated by red shiner, poeciliids and juvenile mouthbrooder; biomass was concentrated in a few species.
- Gadsen Lake supported the highest fish density and biomass recorded (about 127.9 kg/ha) and the highest carnivore share, but its fauna reflects transitory, periodically drained conditions.
- Drain fish fauna was of low diversity (six species) and dominated by warm-water, tropical-origin species.
- Benthos was overwhelmingly chironomids, oligochaetes and Asiatic clams; biomass varied little among habitats (1.3–13.5 g/m²).
- Standing crops of aquatic plants in canals and drains greatly exceeded mainstream values, and canal estimates depend on clearing schedules.
- Pigment levels were high across the division, linked to nutrient inputs and warm, flow-through conditions.
- No critical physico-chemical conditions threatening the biota were identified overall, but low dissolved oxygen and high temperatures in some drains caused localised stress and fish kills.
- Data were collected before a major pesticide fish kill in canals; the authors note that results do not reflect its effects.
