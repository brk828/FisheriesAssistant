# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program FY-89 Work Plan and Meeting Materials

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #recovery-program #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting materials for the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]] meeting on August 30, 1988, including the final draft FY-89 work plan, a proposed plan for [[San Juan River Basin]] endangered species activities, and administrative correspondence. The Recovery Program addresses four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The annual budget is estimated at $2.3 million, with funding from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] ($600,000), [[Bureau of Reclamation]] ($1,500,000), and the states of Colorado ($104,000), Utah ($73,000), and Wyoming ($23,000). The work plan emphasizes habitat management, flow acquisition, propagation and stocking, nonnative fish control, and standardized monitoring.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program was established through a Cooperative Agreement signed January 21-22, 1988, to recover endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The program addresses populations in the [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], and potentially the [[San Juan River]]. The four target species are all native cyprinids and catostomids endemic to the Colorado River system.

The Implementation Committee's responsibilities include developing annual budget priorities and work plans. The FY-89 work plan follows a structured development process: the Technical Group ranks projects in July based on consistency with recovery goals, urgency, likelihood of success, and relationship to other priority work; the Management Group prepares a draft work plan by August 15; and the Implementation Committee reviews and approves the plan by August 30.

The program allocates funds across six categories: Program Management ($100,000), Habitat Management including flow acquisition ($1,000,000), Habitat Development ($400,000), Stocking Native Fishes ($245,000), Nonnative Fish Control and Sportfishing Control ($105,000), and Research, Monitoring, and Data Management ($450,000). This allocation serves as a general guide to ensure balanced recovery efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The endangered Colorado River fishes face multiple threats including habitat loss, flow alterations from dam operations, competition and predation from nonnative species, and water depletions. The [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operation significantly affects downstream habitat conditions in the [[Green River]], particularly temperature regimes, flow patterns, and sediment dynamics that influence backwater nursery habitats critical for young-of-year (YOY) fish survival.

Flow requirements are being quantified using [[IFIM]] (Instream Flow Incremental Methodology) and [[PHABSIM]] (Physical Habitat Simulation) models. Studies focus on identifying physical habitat variables important to rare fishes, including backwater availability, temperature, and sediment conditions. The [[Yampa River]] and [[Colorado River]] flow recommendation studies aim to develop biologically defensible flow regimes for spawning, recruitment, and adult maintenance.

Nonnative warmwater game fish (including [[Green Sunfish]], [[Red Shiner]], and other species) potentially compete with or prey upon endangered fishes, particularly during larval and YOY stages. The [[Little Snake River]] plays an important role in maintaining stable channel and sediment conditions in the [[Yampa River]] and [[Green River]] systems.

The [[San Juan River]] represents one of three remaining natural [[Colorado Squawfish]] populations. Field studies in 1987 captured 6 adult fish and 20 YOY; 1988 studies captured three adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] and one ripe [[Razorback Sucker]]. The river provides 200 miles of unobstructed riverine habitat, though historical abuses (dewatering, poisoning) may have depressed populations. Recovery potential remains uncertain due to questions about flow adequacy and operational flexibility at [[Navajo Reservoir]].

## Key Findings

**FY-89 Work Plan Priorities:**

The approved work plan emphasizes completion or continuation of:
- Standardized monitoring and data management programs across the basin
- Development of instream flow recommendations for [[Yampa River]] ($72,500) and [[Colorado River]] ($176,500)
- Completion of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operation studies ($205,900) and development of a draft biological opinion in 1989
- Studies of competition between warmwater game fish and rare native fishes ($85,000)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] stocking and monitoring on the [[Green River]] ($65,000)

**New Initiatives for FY-89:**

1. Implementation of endangered fish propagation and rearing plan, including assessment of new/expanded hatchery facilities ($143,700)
2. Development of quantitative recovery goals for the rare fishes ($30,000)
3. Information and education program for anglers to reduce incidental take, plus newsletter development ($35,000)
4. Studies to clarify taxonomy of *Gila* spp. (humpback, bonytail, and roundtail chub) ($90,000)
5. Literature review on habitat development techniques and exotic fish management ($10,000)
6. Creel census to determine extent of incidental captures by anglers ($55,000)

**Major Study Components:**

*Flaming Gorge Studies* ($525,900 total):
- Backwater mapping to quantify nursery habitats at various flows ($180,000)
- Trophic dynamics study assessing ecological importance of backwaters ($120,000)
- Sediment dynamics and river geomorphology ($20,000)
- Tailwater trout fishery impacts ($323,000)
- Hydrological support and modeling ($30,600)

*Propagation and Stocking*:
- [[Razorback Sucker]] propagation/rearing in [[Green River]] near Ouray, Utah ($23,500)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] retrieval program to prevent extinction ($2,000)
- Expansion of genetic diversity in broodstock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]
- Development of augmentation plans for [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] ($35,000)

*Monitoring and Research*:
- Standardized monitoring program continuation ($92,000)
- Database management for recovery program ($25,000)
- Overwinter survival studies of YOY [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- Age-growth studies of adult [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- Game fish/endangered fish interaction studies ($85,000)

**San Juan River Strategy:**

The Implementation Committee recommends completing initial evaluation of [[San Juan River]] recovery potential in FY-89 (not to exceed $50,000):
- Complete field studies determining abundance and distribution of rare fishes
- Assess present and future hydrology and operational flexibility at [[Navajo Reservoir]]
- Develop draft recovery/management plan by March 1, 1989
- Future use of Recovery Program funds to be evaluated case-by-case

For Section 7 consultations, implement depletion charge on small projects using San Juan-specific formula; evaluate large projects (e.g., [[Animas-LaPlata Project]] proposing 198,000 AF depletion) case-by-case.

**Administrative and Financial Issues:**

Total Recovery Program cost estimated at $58.5 million over 15 years. Funding sources include:
- Congressional appropriations: $10 million minimum for water rights acquisition, $5 million for recovery construction (hatcheries, fish passage, habitat development)
- Water project proponent contributions: $9-10 million expected at $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion
- Private donations from conservation groups

The Implementation Committee requests assistance from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Director to establish mechanism for transferring Recovery Program funds to the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] for administration. Proposed agreement would establish separate accounts for water developer contributions and congressional appropriations, with disbursements requiring written direction from FWS Director accompanied by certified Implementation Committee resolution.

**Section 7 Consultation Framework:**

Biological opinions will specify one-time contributions from water project proponents: 10% payable upon completion of Federal action initiating consultation, balance due when construction commences or prior to depletion. Funds applied equally to flow acquisition and other priority recovery activities unless otherwise directed by Implementation Committee.

**Recommendations:**

1. Each agency must provide adequate staffing and funding early in fiscal year to ensure successful implementation
2. Technical Group should develop and distribute requests for proposals for priority activities (particularly habitat development) in FY-90
3. Each funded project must provide annual report by January 31 summarizing work accomplished, preliminary data analysis, and recommendations for coming year
