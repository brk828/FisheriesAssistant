# Arizona Game & Fish Department Razorback Sucker Specimen Collection and Analysis

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #conservation-biology #hatchery-management

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the Arizona Game and Fish Department to Dr. W.L. Minckley documents the collection and preservation of 192 [[Razorback Sucker]] specimens from the 1994 Year Class for genetic and morphometric analysis. The collection occurred on January 31, 1995, when an earthen pond at [[Bubbling Ponds]] was drained, revealing significant deformities in a portion of the population. The department seeks to determine whether observed deformities are genetic or environmentally induced to inform management decisions at both Bubbling Ponds and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) specimens originated from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center (DNFHTC) as part of the 1994 Year Class. The fish were held in an earthen pond at [[Bubbling Ponds]] that also contained high numbers of [[Mosquitofish]], [[Fathead Minnows]], and bullfrog tadpoles.

Observed deformities included:
- Misshapened heads and mouths
- Scoliosis and lordosis
- Kinked caudal peduncles
- Anomalies in caudal fin rays
- Severe malnourishment or undernourishment in smaller individuals

The letter also references activities with the 1993 Year Class razorbacks scheduled for stocking into the [[Verde River]], including coded wire tagging of approximately 2,938 individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimens were collected from an earthen pond environment at [[Bubbling Ponds]], Arizona. The presence of multiple non-native species ([[Mosquitofish]], [[Fathead Minnows]]) and amphibian larvae (bullfrog tadpoles) in the same pond suggests potential competition or environmental stressors that may have contributed to the observed conditions.

The [[Verde River]] is identified as the intended stocking location for the 1993 Year Class, indicating ongoing native fish restoration efforts in Arizona river systems.

## Key Findings

**Population Assessment:**
- Total razorbacks collected: 1,530 individuals
- Normal specimens: 1,391 (90.9%)
- Deformed specimens: 139 (9.1%)

**Specimen Preservation:**
- 96 specimens randomly selected for analysis (48 normal, 48 deformed)
- 48 specimens preserved in 10% formalin
- 48 specimens preserved alive on dry ice

**Planned Analysis:**
- Genetic (allozyme) analysis by Dr. Dowling
- Morphometric analysis
- Goal: Determine if deformities are genetic or environmentally induced
- Results will inform management actions at [[Bubbling Ponds]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]

**1993 Year Class Activities (January 31 - February 1, 1995):**
- Approximately 2,938 razorbacks marked with coded wire tags
- Subsample of 100 fish examined for:
  - *Lernaea* infestations: 20% prevalence
  - Body anomalies: 75% prevalence
- Fish scheduled for stocking into [[Verde River]]

**Management Implications:**
The high prevalence of deformities and parasitic infestations indicates potential problems in hatchery rearing protocols or environmental conditions requiring corrective action pending analysis results.
