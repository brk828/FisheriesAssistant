# Upper Colorado River Endangered Fishes Recovery Program: Management Committee Records, November 1990 to January 1991

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This administrative packet from the [[Recovery Program]] for the Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin includes the final summary of the November 26, 1990 [[Management Committee]] meeting, its attendee and assignment lists, a calendar of events for January to August 1991, and a January 28, 1991 transmittal of the "Status Review of the Razorback Sucker." It is a management and coordination record, not a research study. It covers funding, water acquisition, flow studies, species recovery planning, consultation schedules, and agency roles, and it reports no new field data on fish populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Recovery Program]] is a multi-agency effort led by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] with the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Western Area Power Administration]], state wildlife and water agencies in Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, environmental groups, and Upper Basin water users. Its governance includes a Management Committee, an Implementation Committee, and technical committees: Biology, Information and Education, and Water Acquisition. Each technical committee has subcommittees, such as Habitat/Life History, Nonnative/Sportfish, and Instream Flow. The meeting reviewed mission statements, subcommittee structures, and the organizational chart. A final program charter and membership package was to be issued.

The meeting attendance list contained about 20 named individuals from federal agencies, state agencies, the Colorado Water Conservation Board, the Colorado River Water Conservation District, The Nature Conservancy, the National Park Service, and the Colorado River Energy Distributors Association.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish of concern are the endangered Colorado River fishes, with the [[Razorback Sucker]] named explicitly. The document notes that [[Population Viability Analysis]] (PVA) has rarely been applied to species whose population sizes are poorly known, and that the Colorado River fishes fall into this category. A basin-wide PVA workshop was planned for February 1991 and will include the razorback sucker.

Habitat and flow work covers several reaches:

- **[[15-Mile Reach]]:** Service draft October–June flow recommendations went out for biological review, and water availability and acquisition were discussed. A broader ecosystem-level focus was proposed.
- **[[Yampa River]]:** A Phase III work plan and feasibility study are underway, with final flow recommendations needed by August 1992.
- **[[San Juan River]]:** A recovery program outline was prepared, and the Service committed to a draft biological opinion.
- **Gunnison Basin, [[Flaming Gorge]], and Price-San Rafael:** These are covered under [[Section 7 Consultation]] activities.
- **Redlands passage:** A potential opportunity was identified for opening fish passage.

Water and habitat management is closely tied to the [[Animas-La Plata]] project, [[Cross Mountain]] land acquisition, and the [[Ouray Refugia Facility]], which needs operation and maintenance funding.

## Key Findings

**Funding and budget**
- The FY 91 Service budget was approved with Colorado River resource management funds of $424,000 intact.
- Region 6 took a further $100,000 cut in [[Section 6 Funding]]. The Colorado River allocation of $200,000 still comes from the top of the Region's share. Region 6 expects a further 10 percent annual cut until its Section 6 total falls to about $400,000.
- The Committee proposed seeking a larger increase in Colorado River resource management funds to free Section 6 money for other priorities. The issue goes to the Implementation Committee in February.
- A request to waive the 17.65 percent overhead charge on funds transferred from Reclamation was sent to Washington. A reduction is possible, but no response had been received.
- A [[Washington Briefing Trip]] was proposed for late January or February 1991. Topics include program progress, resource management funding, O&M funding for the Ouray refugia facility, and the water acquisition report.

**Rate dispute**
- [[Western Area Power Administration]] imposed a 47 percent rate increase effective October 1990.
- The [[Colorado River Energy Distributors Association]] objects to using electric revenues for Recovery Program funding and for Navajo Dam repair. Litigation is expected to take about two years, with a [[Federal Energy Regulatory Commission]] ruling to follow.

**Water acquisition**
- The Senate appropriated $2 million for Cross Mountain, but the amount was cut in conference. The Bureau of Land Management may still acquire the ranch through a land exchange.
- Priority options for the 15-Mile Reach are low-cost, high-impact measures: junior appropriations, reservoir water purchases, and salvage water from the Grand Valley Salinity Project.

**Species recovery and consultation**
- A draft [[Recovery Goals]] procedure was developed for endangered fish. Its final product came from a FY 90 project, and a PVA workshop was planned for February 1991.
- The San Juan draft biological opinion was due January 1, 1991, with finalization targeted for April 1, 1991.
- The Flaming Gorge consolidated biology and hydrology report was to be reviewed by January 17, 1991. Sandstone conservation measures were agreed in concept with Wyoming. The Aspinall impacts report was completed.

**Committees and reporting**
- Annual project reports were due December 7, 1990, and principal investigator presentations were scheduled for January 8–10, 1991.
- Long Range Plan comments were due December 15, 1990, with Management Committee review on January 14–15, 1991.
- The [[National Park Service]] is considering formal participation and may submit a proposal.
- Bob Weaver resigned from the Management Committee due to a conflict of interest. Environmental representation was proposed for Dan Luecke (Implementation Committee) and Robert Wigington (Water Acquisition Committee).

**Calendar highlights (January–August 1991)**
- January 28: Yampa River Phase III meeting, Denver.
- February 12–13: Annual Upper Colorado River Basin Researchers' meeting, Grand Junction.
- February 20 meeting: canceled and rescheduled for August 21, 1991.
- April 29: Management Committee meeting, Dutch John, Utah.
- January 28, 1991: Transmittal of the "Status Review of the Razorback Sucker."
