## Executive Summary
This 1960 field trip road log documents the aquatic biology of [[Doe Run]], a 9.7-mile spring-fed stream in the karst topography of Meade County, Kentucky, along with associated [[Sinkhole Ponds]]. The transect spans a gradient from cold headwater springs (56°F) to warmer downstream reaches (62°F) before the stream empties into the [[Ohio River]]. Fish community composition shifts predictably along this thermal and hydrological gradient — from spring-adapted species like [[Banded Sculpin]] and [[Blacknose Dace]] in upper reaches, to sunfish, bass, and introduced [[Common Carp]] and [[Channel Catfish]] in downstream sinkhole ponds. The data illustrate strong habitat partitioning driven by water chemistry (pH, alkalinity, dissolved oxygen), substrate (marl vs. peat/mud), and current velocity.

## Background & Description
While the source is primarily an ecological/habitat survey rather than a morphological study, it notes functional trait relevance for stream-dwelling taxa:
- **[[Banded Sculpin]]** (*Cottus* sp.): Benthic, cryptic sculpin restricted to cold spring-influenced upper Doe Run — implies a flattened, current-resistant body plan typical of *Cottus*, though not explicitly described.
- **[[Blacknose Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys* sp.): Small cyprinid associated with swift, spring-fed riffle habitat, consistent with a streamlined body adapted to turbulent flow.
- Aquatic macroinvertebrates in the same riffle habitats show explicit current-adapted morphology (e.g., flattened Plecoptera nymphs, net-spinning Hydropsychid caddisflies), used by the authors as a comparative baseline for the physical demands of the swift-water niche fish also occupy.
- No direct morphometric fish data (length, weight, meristics) are reported in this log.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The log delineates a longitudinal (headwater-to-mouth) zonation of aquatic habitats and their biota along [[Doe Run]]:

**Headwater Springs (Minckley's Station 1, mile 10.0):**
- Constant cold temperature, low turbidity (multiple subterranean sources).
- Fauna: small Crustacea, snail *[[Goniobasis]]*, blind cave crayfish *[[Orconectes Pellucidus]]* (troglobitic).
- Flora zonation downstream: mosses → *Potamogeton* → *Nitella flexilis* → *Myriophyllum* → *Lemanea* (red alga) on marl riffles.
- No fish reported at this station — habitat too extreme/cold or inaccessible.

**Doe Run Hotel Reach (Minckley's Station 2, mile 8.2):**
- Spring-like character; marl deposition minimal but increasing downstream.
- Densely shaded (beech, sycamore); abundant ferns, watercress, mint.
- Fauna: amphipods, isopods (spring-water indicators), snail *Goniobasis* (dominant by biomass).
- Fish: **[[Banded Sculpin]]**, **[[Blacknose Dace]]** — both spring/riffle specialists; a few **[[Bluegill]]** and **[[White Sucker]]** in impounded pools above dams; **[[Rainbow Trout]]**/*Salmo* sporadically stocked.
- Explicitly noted: **no river fish** present at this station — indicates a barrier or unsuitable habitat for lowland/river fish fauna upstream of this point.

**Downstream Doe Run (Minckley's Station 3, mile 5.0):**
- Clear water except after flooding; temp constant 56°F at springs, up to 62°F at stream mouth.
- High dissolved oxygen; pH 7.0–8.4; high alkalinity (300 ppm), hard water.
- Heavy marl deposition on plants, animals, and riffle substrates; significant human disturbance (dams).
- Fauna: current-adapted insect larvae (flattened Plecoptera nymphs; net-spinning *Hydropsyche*), marl-encrusted snails and crayfish burrows.
- A bridge/dam structure noted as a **major barrier to upstream fish migration** from the river.

**Sinkhole Pond — Beauty Bog (Stop 1, mile 2.1):**
- Shallow (~3 ft) acidic, humic-stained water over fluid peat; surface pH 7.0, bottom pH 5.8; low/no bottom oxygen; residual CuSO4 (copper sulfate) treatment effects.
- Vegetation: floating diatoms, blue-green algal mats, bladderwort, naiad, water primrose, sedge tussocks, buttonbush, cattail, blue flag iris.
- Fish restricted to upper foot of water near shore: **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*), **[[Longear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis megalotis*), **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*), recently stocked **[[Common Carp]]** (*Cyprinus carpio*), **[[Channel Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus punctatus*).
- Fish habitat use appears constrained to oxygenated surface layer due to anoxic bottom conditions.

**Twin Sinkholes, Fontaine's Meadow (Stop 4, mile 14.0):**
- Shallow, warm, soft-mud-bottomed pools; used for recreational fishing (species unspecified).
- Flora: diatoms, desmids, *Nitella*, mud plantain, *Potamogeton diversifolius*, spike rush.

## Key Findings
- **Longitudinal fish zonation**: Cold-water spring specialists (*[[Banded Sculpin|Cottus]]*, *[[Blacknose Dace|Rhinichthys]]*) dominate upper Doe Run, while warm-water pond/lowland species (sunfish, bass, catfish, carp) dominate downstream sinkhole ponds — a classic upstream-downstream faunal replacement driven by temperature and habitat structure.
- **Barrier effects**: A bridge/dam on Doe Run is identified as a major physical barrier preventing upstream migration of river fish, effectively isolating the upper spring-fed reach's fauna from Ohio River fish assemblages.
- **Water chemistry gradient**: Marked shift from acidic, oxygen-poor sinkhole pond water (pH 5.8 at bottom, humic-stained) to alkaline, oxygen-rich, hard spring water in Doe Run (pH 7.0–8.4, 300 ppm alkalinity) — creating sharply distinct habitats within a small geographic area (~18-mile loop).
- **Anthropogenic influence**: Evidence of human management/disturbance includes CuSO4 (algicide/molluscicide) application residue in Beauty Bog, recent carp stocking, sporadic trout stocking in Doe Run, and dam construction altering flow and marl deposition patterns.
- **Marl deposition as habitat modifier**: Precipitation of marl onto plants, animals, snails, and crayfish burrows in downstream Doe Run represents a distinctive geochemical process shaping benthic microhabitat structure in this karst spring-stream system.
- **Non-fish faunal indicators**: The presence of the blind cave crayfish *[[Orconectes Pellucidus]]* at the spring cave entrance and spring-obligate snail *[[Goniobasis]]* serve as bioindicators of stable, cold, subterranean-influenced groundwater inputs feeding Doe Run.
- **Trophic note**: Net-spinning caddisfly larvae (*Hydropsyche*) filter-feed on diatom-dominated plankton in swift riffle currents, representing a key invertebrate food resource likely supporting riffle-dwelling fish such as sculpin and dace.
