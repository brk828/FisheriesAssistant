# Females of Enallagma laterale Morse and Enallagma recurvatum Davis (Odonata: Coenagrionidae)

#odonata #entomology #coenagrionidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 taxonomic paper by Robert H. Gibbs Jr. (Cornell University) describes the previously unknown or poorly known females of two northeastern damselflies, [[Enallagma Laterale]] and [[Enallagma Recurvatum]]. Both were based on specimens collected in tandem with males at Cape Cod, Massachusetts, and on a few specimens from Riverhead, Long Island, New York. The paper also provides a dichotomous key to the females of ten *Enallagma* species, based mainly on the shape of the [[Mesostigmal Laminae]], a thoracic structure the author identifies as the most reliable character for separating females.

## Background & Description

- **Problem addressed:** Of the 23 northeastern members of the genus *Enallagma*, the females of *E. piscinarium*, *E. laterale*, and *E. recurvatum* had not been adequately described. *E. piscinarium* was known only from the type male. The female of *E. laterale* was unrecorded, and the female attributed to *E. recurvatum* by Davis (1913) was considered inadequate and not diagnostic.
- **Prior work:** Byers (1927) could not distinguish *recurvatum* from *E. hageni* except by its rarity. Garman (1927) noted that all supposed females examined had belonged to other species.
- **Material:** Systematic collecting on Cape Cod yielded tandem pairs of both species. Specimens from Riverhead, Long Island, were sent to Thomas W. Donnelly by Roy Latham.

### Descriptions

**Enallagma laterale (female)**
- Olivaceous with light blue wash and black markings.
- Head dorsum black; postocular spots comma-shaped and connected by a narrow pale occipital bar.
- Prothorax with prominent dorsolateral pits near the caudal margin.
- Mesostigmal laminae twisted and upturned at 60° or more in the lateral half.
- Abdomen: segment 1 with a squared middorsal basal spot; segment 8 widened gradually from base to apex.
- Measurements (total length, abdomen, front wing, hind wing): 24.8–28.0 mm, 20.0–22.7 mm, 14.5–17.8 mm, 15.4–16.3 mm.
- Material: two Cape Cod specimens (Jabinette's Pond, West Yarmouth, Massachusetts, June 18, 1953) and one Riverhead specimen (dated May 29, 1958 in the text).

**Enallagma recurvatum (female)**
- Similar overall coloration to *laterale*, but postocular spots are isolated from the occipital carina.
- Dorsolateral prothoracic pale spot either present or absent.
- Mesostigmal laminae have a mesal edge at about 30° to the middorsal carina, a much thickened postero-mesal corner, and a deep depression near the mesal margin.
- Abdomen: segments 3–7 abruptly constricted basally; segment 8 has a narrow middorsal line abruptly widened over the apical dorsum.
- Measurements: 26.8–28.2 mm, 21.0–22.2 mm, 16.1–18.0 mm, 14.7–16.9 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Collections were made at ponds, namely Jabinette's Pond (West Yarmouth) and [[Flax Pond]] (North Falmouth), Massachusetts, and at Riverhead on Long Island. Ponds are the only habitat type the document describes.
- **Phenology:** Records fall in late May through mid-June, indicating the flight period of these species in the region.
- **Relationships:** Based on male anal appendages, *E. laterale* appears close to *E. piscinarium*, *E. davisi*, and *E. boreale*, and possibly to *E. minusculum*. The male of *E. recurvatum* resembles *E. vernale* superficially, although *vernale* is considered closer to *E. cyathigerum*. The recurved superior appendages of *recurvatum* are similar to those of *E. hageni*, which are straight in lateral view.
- **Larval evidence:** The caudal lamellae of the nymphs of *E. ebrium* and *E. geminatum* resemble those of *laterale*, suggesting possible relationship. The nymph of *recurvatum* is unknown, and that of *laterale* has not been adequately described.

## Key Findings

1. **Females described:** The paper provides the first adequate female descriptions for *E. laterale* and *E. recurvatum*, based on Cape Cod and Long Island material.
2. **Diagnostic character:** The shape of the mesostigmal laminae is the most useful character for identifying females. Gibbs argues that Byers (1927) could have separated *hageni* and *recurvatum* at a glance had he had both sexes.
3. **Ten-species key:** The key covers *E. ebrium*, *E. hageni*, *E. vernale*, *E. cyathigerum*, *E. minusculum*, *E. laterale*, *E. recurvatum*, *E. geminatum*, *E. davisi*, and *E. boreale*. It separates them using lamina width and tilt, the presence or absence of a mesal concavity, prothoracic pits, and abdominal color patterns.
4. **Figure 1:** Left mesostigmal laminae are illustrated for *laterale*, *recurvatum*, *davisi*, and [[Enallagma Minusculum]], with the minusculum figure included because Walker (1953) had an illustration the author judged misleading. The author found the lateral ends of *minusculum* bluntly pointed and a diagonal ridge diagnostic.
5. **Male colour mark:** Both *laterale* and *davisi* males have a lateral black mark on abdominal segment 8. Several Cape Cod *recurvatum* also show it, though it is not common.
6. **Data inconsistencies to note:**
   - The *recurvatum* description lists 11 specimens, but the enumerated localities total 16 (Flax Pond 8 + 5; Jabinette's Pond 1; Riverhead 1 + 1).
   - The Riverhead *laterale* specimen is dated May 29, 1958, while a *recurvatum* Riverhead specimen is dated May 29, 1953. The 1958 date may be an error.
   - The text says the *recurvatum* measurements were taken from four Cape Cod specimens and one from Long Island, which does not match the 11-specimen total.
