## Executive Summary
This 1987 letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) Director to Fort Huachuca's Garrison Engineering and Housing Director outlines an interagency plan to reintroduce the threatened [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) into Garden Canyon Creek. The document is primarily a regulatory and conservation-management communication rather than a primary research paper, but it contains valuable data regarding the species' historic range, source population genetics, drainage basin constraints, and interagency stocking protocols. The action represents an attempt to establish an additional refuge population within the species' presumed historic range, contingent on multi-agency consultation (USFWS, BLM, US Forest Service).

## Background & Description

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat suitability**: Garden Canyon Creek on Fort Huachuca is identified as providing "adequate habitat to support Gila chub populations," implying suitable perennial flow, pool structure, and water quality for the species within its historic range in the region.
- **Historic range**: The species is presumed to have historically occupied drainages within the Fort Huachuca area, making Garden Canyon Creek an appropriate reintroduction site.
- **Source population / genetic provenance**: The proposed donor population originates from **Turkey Creek**, near Canelo, Arizona, which is genetically and geographically tied to the **[[Babocomari River]]/[[San Pedro River]] drainage** — the same basin historically associated with Fort Huachuca's east-side waters.
- **Basin separation constraint**: The letter explicitly prohibits transferring introduced chub to the Fort's west-side drainages/impoundments, as these belong to the **Santa Cruz River Basin**, a distinct hydrological and evolutionary lineage incompatible with Turkey Creek ancestral stock. This underscores the importance of basin-specific genetic management in native fish conservation.
- **Threatened species designation**: *Gila intermedia* is explicitly referred to as a "threatened fish," reflecting its conservation status at the time and the rationale for establishing additional refuge populations.

## Key Findings
- **Reintroduction rationale**: Stocking Gila chub into Garden Canyon Creek is expected to benefit the species by creating an additional refuge population within its presumed historic range, though success is not guaranteed ("no introduction efforts are assured success").
- **Interagency coordination requirement**: Before approval, AGFD must consult the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[US Forest Service]], and AGFD's Tucson regional office — reflecting standard practice for native/endangered species reintroductions.
- **Genetic matching protocol**: AGFD policy requires that introduced fish be "as similar as possible genetically" to populations presumed to have historically existed at the release site. **Turkey Creek** stock was selected as the best genetic match for Fort Huachuca's Garden Canyon Creek population.
- **Basin integrity protocol**: Post-introduction, Gila chub in Garden Canyon Creek must not be transferred elsewhere without AGFD consultation, particularly not into west-side (Santa Cruz Basin) drainages, to prevent inappropriate inter-basin genetic mixing.
- **Monitoring and reporting**: Fort Huachuca personnel are tasked with informing AGFD of the status of the Gila chub population in Garden Canyon Creek, as well as reporting on other native fish species present on the installation.
- **Institutional framework**: The action is to proceed under AGFD's "Procedures for Nongame Wildlife and Endangered Species Re-Establishment Projects," indicating a formalized reintroduction protocol applicable to future native fauna projects.
- **Key personnel/roles**: Coordination responsibilities are assigned to AGFD's Native Fish Specialist (Dean Hendrickson), assistant (Lee Simons), Region V Fish Specialist (Will Hayes), and Fort Huachuca's Wildlife Management Section (Dennis Coleman).
- **Timeline**: AGFD aimed to complete interagency coordination and review by **15 August 1987**.
- **Broader context**: The letter references AGFD's "statewide native fish program," indicating this stocking action was part of a larger, coordinated effort involving multiple cooperating agencies (e.g., [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], [[Coronado National Forest]]) to conserve native desert fish species such as the [[Gila Chub]] across Arizona.
