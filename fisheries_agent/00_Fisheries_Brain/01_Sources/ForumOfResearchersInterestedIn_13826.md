# Forum on the Paleoecology, Ecology and Physical Geography of Northwestern Chihuahua (1989 Invitation)

#aquatic-ecology #limnology #northwestern-chihuahua

## Executive Summary

This document is a first-circular invitation letter, dated January 25 (1989), from R. B. Brown for an informal research forum on the paleoecology, ecology and physical geography of northwestern Chihuahua. The forum was to be held April 28–30, 1989, in [[Nuevo Casas Grandes]], Chihuahua, Mexico. The remaining pages are tourist and regional information about the [[Casas Grandes Archaeological Zone]] and surrounding areas. The document contains no fish, invertebrate, or other aquatic species data. Its only water-related content concerns the forum theme of water and climate and the prehispanic hydrology of [[Paquimé]].

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Conference invitation and regional travel guide (OCR-degraded scan).
- **Host venue:** Classrooms of the Federal Preparatoria "Francisco Villa," made available through Julian Hernandez; field visits were also planned.
- **Forum purpose:** Assess the present state of knowledge and enable personal interaction among researchers with parallel topical and geographic interests.
- **Suggested themes:**
  - Water and climate
  - Avian ecology
  - Small mammal ecology
  - Plant ecology
  - Prehispanic agricultural systems
- **Invitees:** Scholars from the US, Canada, France, Britain and Mexico.
- **Funding:** None. Each participant paid their own expenses, with group costs shared by all.
- **Accommodation options:** Camping at nearby sites, the [[Pinon Motel]] (preferred by most foreigners, the Hacienda being more expensive), Hotel Paquime (Juarez 385), San Luis (Av. Constitucion 188 as scanned) and Hotel California (Av. Constitucion 207).
- **Scan note:** Page 2 is upside-down and mirrored OCR text. It is not reliably recoverable and is not summarized here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water and hydrology (prehispanic Paquimé):**
- Builders were described as "experts in hydrology," with irrigation canals and lengthy aqueducts.
- Water flowed from tanks into patios and plazas that were drained underground.
- Water sources: nearby arroyos, the [[Casas Grandes River]], and a warm spring northwest of the pueblo.

**Setting and climate:**
- [[Nuevo Casas Grandes]] altitude given as 4450 (sea level reference as scanned; units not stated).
- Population given as 45,000.
- [[Deming New Mexico]] and Nuevo Casas Grandes described as having year-round mild weather "without summer or winter extremes," about 360 days a year.

**Regional landscape and land use:**
- [[Colonia Juarez]]: Mormon community known for apples and peaches.
- Former [[Terrazas Hacienda]] of General Terrazas, now an ejido (agrarian land reform).
- Janos: historic presidio and refuge of Apaches during the time of Cochise and Geronimo.

## Key Findings

1. **Forum details:** Informal meeting, April 28–30, 1989, Nuevo Casas Grandes, with self-funded participation and classroom sessions at the Federal Preparatoria "Francisco Villa."
2. **Research themes:** Water and climate, avian ecology, small mammal ecology, plant ecology and prehispanic agricultural systems. Aquatic ecology is only implied through the "water and climate" theme.
3. **Paquimé chronology:** Three occupation periods (early, middle, late); peak civilization reported between 1060 and 1200 A.D.
4. **Paquimé scale:**
   - City area of 8 hectares, originally about twice that size.
   - Buildings with hundreds of rooms; some five stories high.
   - Ceremonial area with truncated mounds, temple structures, a ball court, ceremonial pyramids and a "mound of the cross" with astronomical orientation.
5. **Paquimé hydrology:** Irrigation canals, aqueducts, underground drainage of patios and plazas; water drawn from arroyos, the Casas Grandes River and a warm spring.
6. **Accommodation costs (as scanned):** Pinon Motel approximately $40.00 single and $55.00 double. These OCR-garbled figures should be verified against the original.
7. **Travel access:**
   - Paved highway from Ciudad Juarez and Chihuahua City.
   - About 2.5 hours' drive from Deming, New Mexico.
   - Gila Cliff Dwellings National Monument and the Santa Rita open-pit copper mine are within 2 hours of Deming.
   - Deming hosts an annual Wild West event starting the second Thursday in October.
8. **Data limitations:** The document contains no species lists, sample sizes, aquatic survey results or quantitative ecological measurements.
