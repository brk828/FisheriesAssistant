# Native Fish Work Group: Lake Mohave Native Fish Backwater Rearing Facility

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum (April 1990) summarizes two March 1990 meetings of the [[Native Fish Work Group]] on a proposed on-site backwater rearing facility at [[Lake Mohave]]. The group aims to protect the [[Razorback Sucker]] population, which is judged to be at risk of extirpation within about 10 years without intervention. Participants agreed on two parallel efforts: a long-term proposal examining onsite rearing facilities, and short-term renovation of the natural berm at [[Yuma Cove]] to support a 1991 sucker spawn. Work is planned for September or October 1990, with stocking in December 1990 or January 1991, under a timeline that depends on a [[Section 404 Permit]] and [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) compliance.

## Background & Description

**Meeting history**
- **March 2, 1990:** Held at [[Arizona State University]] (Krause Hall, Tempe, AZ). Attended by ASU, [[National Park Service]] (NPS), [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), and [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Reclamation). [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW) could not attend because of out-of-state travel restrictions. Future meetings were to be held closer to the Arizona-Nevada border.
- **March 21, 1990:** Held at Yuma Cove, Lake Mohave, with Reclamation engineers. The source text also refers to a "Razorback Sucker Roundup" on March 19, 1990, which is a date discrepancy in the document.

**Origin of the effort**
- A January 16, 1990, meeting of Reclamation, FWS, and NPS executive staffs recommended a long-term proposal on onsite rearing facilities and an attempt to duplicate or improve the 1984 renovation work.
- Reclamation was also working with the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] on a backwater enhancement effort on the lower Colorado River, which could incorporate dredging to enlarge existing backwaters.

**1984 renovation at Yuma Cove (presented by [[Paul Marsh]])**
- The backwater was poisoned twice.
- Spawning razorback suckers were seined from Lake Mohave and placed in the natural backwater, where they successfully spawned.
- Young suckers were abundant and grew rapidly.
- Higher reservoir elevations and storm-induced wave action breached the natural gravel barrier. Other fish invaded, and the young suckers disappeared, which was attributed to predation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Population status**
- Genetic testing of the existing Lake Mohave population showed 97 percent genetic diversity, which the group described as indicating a unique population.
- Dr. Marsh stated that existing data suggest the population may die out within 10 years if nothing is done.
- Population estimates by [[Dr. Minckley]] place the current population at approximately 50,000 to 65,000 aging fish.

**Limiting factors**
- Predation is the primary factor limiting recruitment in Lake Mohave.
- Dr. Marsh suggested fish be grown to a minimum of 25 cm before release into the lake.

**Lake elevation and timing**
- Lake elevations are projected to reach their lowest level (elevation 633) by the end of September 1990 and to remain there until late October 1990.
- Construction is ideally scheduled during this low-water window. From November 1990, rising elevations make it harder to obtain materials to build up the berm from the backwater.

**Berm condition**
- The existing natural berm appears to have formed from storm-induced movement of material from land points north of the cove.
- It appeared the berm lacked only 2 to 3 feet of height to be high enough.
- Reclamation engineer Mr. David Jennings estimated that 5 feet of material would need to be added. The area would need to be surveyed for an accurate estimate.

**Other species**
- The group agreed that [[Bonytail Chub]] and other endangered large-river fishes should be considered, but initial efforts focus on the razorback sucker.

## Key Findings

**Goals and production targets**
- Initial goal: maintain the existing population level of about 65,000 fish.
- Dr. Minckley suggested producing 10,000 to 30,000 suckers of 25-30 cm per year as a starting point.
- Monitoring through fish marking and subsequent testing is required to determine actual recruitment, and rearing and stocking would be adjusted accordingly.
- The number of backwaters needed depends on input from the [[Dexter Complex]] (FWS) and future experimentation at Yuma Cove. If resident spawning proves insufficient, fry may be brought in from the Dexter facility.

**Berm improvement options**
- Options discussed included building up the existing berm, riprapping it, and placing temporary netting across it.
- Dr. Minckley recommended keeping the project simple: build up the berm and repair it if it erodes. He warned that riprap could affect spawning habitat on the lake side of the berm.
- Other ideas included breaching the berm or installing a culvert so grown suckers could escape into the lake.
- NPS may retain an oceanographer to study the storm-driven sediment mechanics.

**Access and logistics**
- NPS preferred no new road because of past illegal road problems in the area, and favored barge access. Reclamation did not own a barge; [[Bureau of Land Management]] (Kingman) had used one on Lake Mohave, and Mr. Mueller agreed to check.
- NPS indicated equipment might "walk" in, with tracks dragged on exit to remove evidence.
- Reclamation noted it has the heavy equipment and manpower if funded, and that two pieces of equipment would be ideal.
- Mr. Gene Hertzog offered helicopter photography of the lower river during a week the following month.
- Aerial photos from 1977 were identified as a resource for locating other potential backwater sites.

**Permits and compliance**
- Modifying the berm may require a Section 404 permit. Reclamation noted that permitting typically takes 3 to 4 months.
- To permit work by October 1990, NEPA compliance must be finalized and the permit applied for by the end of May 1990.
- A categorical exclusion or an [[Environmental Assessment]] with a finding of no significant impact was considered likely.
- Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] is required. FWS offered to help write the biological assessment.
- Mr. Frank Baucom suggested [[Section 6 Funding]] might be appropriate. Long-term involvement should be budgeted early if the pilot succeeds.

**Commitments and assignments**

| Agency | Commitment |
|---|---|
| AGFD | Lead fish removal efforts (two efforts planned for fall 1990) in coordination with NPS |
| NPS | Pursue NEPA compliance and evaluate applying for the 404 permit; coordinate agency input |
| ASU | Write the biological portion of the EA/proposal by April 30, 1990 |
| FWS | Assist NPS with Section 7 compliance and check for an existing EA |
| Reclamation | Survey the site; identify berm alternatives, access options, preliminary designs, and cost estimates by April 30, 1990; take aerial photos/video in April 1990 |

**Schedule**
- Berm work: September or October 1990.
- Backwater renovation and stocking of spawning razorback suckers: December 1990 or January 1991.
- Refinement of designs and costs may take longer.
- The group consensus was that the effort represents an emergency action for preserving the razorback sucker.
- Next meeting: at the writing of a draft proposal, to refine the course of action, confirm funding and permits, and validate equipment access routes.
