# Arizona 319(h) Non-Point Source Project Call for Information (1997)

#water-quality #nonpoint-source-pollution #grant-administration

## Executive Summary

This document is a January 1997 Request for Information (RFI) from the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] (ADEQ), Non-Point Source Unit, inviting public, private, and tribal entities to submit outlines of qualifying projects for funding under the [[Section 319(h) Grants]] program. The program is funded by the US Environmental Protection Agency and administered by ADEQ. The RFI is an informal, preliminary step: it gathers input to help ADEQ set priorities and plan, and it precedes a formal [[Request for Proposals]]. Responses were due by close of business on February 28, 1997. The document contains no fish species, habitat survey data, or ecological measurements. Its aquatic relevance lies in its focus on [[Non-Point Source Pollution]] control and on protecting riparian and aquatic habitats.

## Background & Description

- **Issuing agency:** ADEQ, Non-Point Source Unit, Phoenix, Arizona (contact: Daniel Smolnik).
- **Program restructuring:** The 319(h) projects program was restructured to increase participation by public, private, and tribal organizations in helping ADEQ prioritize projects. The letter apologizes for a delay in mailing the RFI caused by agency restructuring.
- **Funding basis:** The EPA provides annual monies to ADEQ for establishing or continuing 319(h) projects. The ADEQ Director issues grant monies and monitors project progress.
- **Purpose:** ADEQ wants input to complete short- and long-range planning and to identify geographic areas and concerns that should be addressed.

### Eligible Project Categories

The RFI lists general categories that could qualify, including (non-exhaustive):

- Recreational activities, [[Best Management Practices]] (BMPs), and educational outreach programs
- Non-point source zone management activities
- Urban runoff management practices (BMPs)
- Ongoing activities for [[Wetlands Restoration]]
- Grazing activities BMPs and educational outreach
- Sand and gravel activities BMPs and educational outreach
- [[Interstate Watershed Projects]]
- Other innovative projects that further non-point source water quality improvements

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The RFI's ecological focus is on maintaining and restoring the natural processes that sustain perennial and intermittent streams and their riparian and aquatic habitats. Proposals are evaluated on whether they:

- protect, enhance, or restore these natural processes, including those that sustain stream conditions **without an outside water supply**;
- address a specific habitat need or threat;
- contribute to long-term benefits, rated as lasting more than 20 years, 5 to 20 years, or less than 5 years;
- identify the water body involved and the media and geographic area.

Non-point source pollution, such as urban runoff, grazing, and sand and gravel operations, is the main pollution concern named in the document. Each proposal must include a public information and education outreach element.

## Key Findings

**Proposal requirements (Development of Detailed Project Scope of Work):**

1. **Need for the project:** Benefits to riparian or aquatic habitats, duration of benefit, and whether the project depends on an outside water supply.
2. **Project specifics:** Target habitat need or threat; media, geographic area, and public and private organizations involved; tasks, sub-tasks, and activities with funding sources; methodologies; timeline to measurable outcomes; a case that the project can become self-sustaining after the funding period; and the water body involved.
3. **Public outreach:** Every project must include public information and education.

**Funding and matching requirements:**

- Funding for some projects may be available immediately. ADEQ determines eligibility at its discretion.
- Grantees must provide matching funds (cash or in-kind) equal to **at least 40 percent of the total project cost**. The document calls this requirement "not negotiable under any circumstances."
- The worked example gives a $100,000 total project cost. Its wording on the grantee's maximum contribution ("not to exceed $60,000") appears inconsistent with the 40 percent rule as stated, so the 40 percent rule should be treated as the operative requirement.

**Submission process:**

- Responses to this informal call do not need the detail of a formal RFP. Responses should be concise, with a maximum of about **ten pages**.
- Awards will **not** be made based solely on this call for information.
- Responses were due by **close of business, February 28, 1997**.
- A single copy of each project outline was to be mailed to Daniel Smolnik, ADEQ Non-Point Source Unit, 3033 North Central Avenue, 5th Floor, Phoenix, Arizona 85012-2809 (telephone 602-207-4474).
- ADEQ intended to issue the formal [[Request for Proposals]] shortly after reviewing the input.
