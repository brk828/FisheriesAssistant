## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice (Vol. 59, No. 131; July 11, 1994) documents U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) findings on a petition to list **83 mollusc species** as threatened or endangered under the Endangered Species Act. While the majority of the notice addresses terrestrial land snails and slugs, a significant aquatic component includes **37 freshwater snails and 3 freshwater clams**, among them the **shortface lanx (Fisherola nuttalli)** — also known as the giant Columbia River limpet — a freshwater gastropod inhabiting swift, cold streams of the Columbia River basin. The petition originated from the Oregon Natural Resources Council (ONRC), supported by the Frest and Johannes (1993) report on molluscs of concern within the range of the Northern Spotted Owl. The Service issued a **negative 90-day finding for 75 species** (including Fisherola nuttalli) and a **positive 90-day but "not warranted" 12-month finding for 8 category 2 candidate species**, citing insufficient rangewide survey data and lack of demonstrated population declines tied to ecological threats.

## Background & Description

- No detailed morphological descriptions of the aquatic taxa are provided in this regulatory notice; it is a legal/administrative finding rather than a taxonomic treatment.
- Taxonomic status of many petitioned species (including several undescribed "n. sp." freshwater pebblesnails, duskysnails, and lanx species) remains **unresolved**, per the Service's assessment: *"The taxonomic distinctiveness or validity of many of the species has not been determined."*
- Freshwater taxa affected span multiple genera:
  - **Lanx** (limpet-like freshwater gastropods): highcap lanx, scale lanx, kneecap lanx, rotund lanx, shortface lanx (Fisherola nuttalli)
  - **Fluminicola** (pebblesnails): at least 9 species/undescribed taxa, including Columbia pebblesnail (category 2 candidate)
  - **Lyogyrus** (duskysnails): 5 species/undescribed taxa
  - **Vorticifex** (rams-horn snails): Klamath, Sinitsin, knobby, nerite rams-horns
  - **Juga** (freshwater snails): scalloped juga, topaz juga, Dalles juga, brown juga, Chace juga, redwood juga, and 3 undescribed species
  - **Anodonta** (freshwater mussels/floaters): California floater (category 2 candidate), Willamette floater
  - **Pisidium** (peaclams): montane peaclam (category 2 candidate)
  - **Physella/Pyrgulopsis**: rotund physa, Archimedes pyrg, Crooked Creek springsnail

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Freshwater streams, springs, and river systems within the Columbia River basin and other Pacific Northwest drainages (Washington, Oregon, California, Idaho); many co-occurring terrestrial taxa are tied to **late-successional (old-growth) forest** and **riparian corridor** ecosystems associated with the range of the [[Northern Spotted Owl]].
- Freshwater molluscs such as **Fisherola nuttalli** occupy cold, well-oxygenated, flowing water habitats — the "shortface lanx" clings to stable substrates in swift currents, a niche shared with several congeneric lanx species petitioned in this action.
- **Ecological sensitivity**: The Service acknowledges that "many of the species covered by this petition may be sensitive to ecological perturbations resulting from the impacts of human activities" but notes that **no data were presented correlating this sensitivity with actual population declines**.
- **Threats** are implied but not substantiated for most species: hydrological alteration, sedimentation, forestry practices, and riparian habitat loss are the presumed pressures on freshwater and riparian mollusc populations, paralleling threats to spotted owl old-growth habitat, though the Service found **no direct evidence of specific rangewide threats** for any petitioned taxon.
- Only one species — the **Trinity bristlesnail (Monadenia setosa)**, a terrestrial land snail — had adequate rangewide survey data; all aquatic taxa lacked comparable survey coverage.

## Key Findings

- **Petition scope**: 83 mollusc species total — 36 land snails, 7 slugs, **37 freshwater snails**, and **3 freshwater clams**, submitted by the Oregon Natural Resources Council (August 18, 1993), based on Frest & Johannes (1993), *"Mollusc Species of Special Concern within the Range of the Northern Spotted Owl."*
- **Fisherola nuttalli (shortface lanx / giant Columbia River limpet)**: Previously petitioned separately in 1987 by Dr. Peter Bowler; evaluated jointly with the ONRC petition. Received a **negative 90-day finding** — insufficient substantial information to warrant listing.
- **8 category 2 candidate species** (already under Service status review) received a **positive 90-day finding but "not warranted" 12-month finding**, remaining in category 2:
  1. Dalles sideband (Monadenia fidelis minor) — terrestrial
  2. Rocky coast sideband (Monadenia fidelis pronotis) — terrestrial
  3. Trinity bristlesnail (Monadenia setosa) — terrestrial
  4. Shasta sideband (Monadenia troglodytes) — terrestrial
  5. Karok hesperian (Vespericola karokorum) — terrestrial
  6. **Columbia pebblesnail (Fluminicola columbiana)** — freshwater
  7. **California floater (Anodonta californiensis)** — freshwater clam
  8. **Montane peaclam (Pisidium ultramontanum)** — freshwater clam
- **75 remaining species** (including all other freshwater snails/clams and the shortface lanx) received a **negative 90-day finding** — petition did not present substantial information that listing "may be warranted."
- **Rationale for negative findings**: (1) No adequate rangewide surveys existed for nearly all petitioned taxa; (2) no documented correlation between habitat sensitivity and actual population decline; (3) unresolved taxonomic validity for numerous undescribed ("n. sp.") freshwater taxa.
- **Regulatory outcome**: None of the 83 species gained new ESA protections at this time; the Service committed to revisiting the finding if new substantial information emerges.
- **Cross-reference**: This mollusc finding was published in the same Federal Register notice as the [[Lathyrus grimesii]] (Grimes' Vetchling) 90-day finding and the proposed listing extension for the [[Northern Copperbelly Water Snake]], reflecting a broader 1994 batch of ESA petition determinations.
- **Broader ecological linkage**: The freshwater mollusc taxa are geographically and ecologically tied to the same Pacific Northwest riparian/old-growth forest systems central to conservation debates over the [[Northern Spotted Owl]] and associated aquatic habitat quality.
