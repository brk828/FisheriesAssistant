# Aphanius ginaonis (Holly, 1929)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #aphanius #hot-spring-endemic #iran

## Executive Summary

This paper provides a taxonomic re-description of **Aphanius ginaonis**, a cyprinodontid fish endemic to a single hot spring (Ginao/Ab-e-Garm) near Bandar Abbas in southern Iran. Originally described by Holly (1929) as *Cyprinodon ginaonis* from only three specimens, and later tentatively synonymized with **[[Aphanius dispar]]** by Berg (1949), the species' distinct status had remained unresolved. Based on a much larger sample (77 specimens of *A. ginaonis* vs. comparative material of *A. dispar* from nearby drainages), Coad (1980) demonstrates statistically significant differences in nearly all meristic and morphometric characters between the two taxa, re-affirming **A. ginaonis** as a valid, distinct species restricted to a single isolated thermal spring habitat — a classic example of extreme habitat-driven endemism in **[[Cyprinodontiformes]]**.

## Background & Description

### Meristic Comparison (A. ginaonis vs. A. dispar), all p<0.001 unless noted

| Total anal fin rays (♂/♀) | 8.33 / 8.87 | 10.13 / 10.00 |
| Total ventral fin rays | 6.43 | 6.90 |
| Total pectoral fin rays | 15.40 | 16.10 |
| Branched caudal fin rays | 12.23 | 14.07 |
| Total vertebrae (♂/♀) | 26.40 / 27.40 | 28.20 / 28.00 |
| Lateral line scales | 25.87 | 27.73 |
| Scales around body | 24.17 | 25.83 |

- **Non-significant differences (p>0.05)** between species: scales around caudal peduncle, total scale radii, total flank bars.
- Sexual dimorphism noted within *A. ginaonis*: females have significantly higher anal fin ray counts, vertebral counts, and flank bar counts than males.

### Morphometric Comparison (ratios relative to total length or head length)
- Body proportions (total length/head length): *A. ginaonis* mean 3.58 vs. *A. dispar* 4.00 (p<0.001) — **A. ginaonis has a relatively longer head**.
- Head length/eye width: *A. ginaonis* 4.31 vs. *A. dispar* 3.39 (p<0.001) — smaller relative eye in *A. ginaonis*.
- Head length/dorsal & anal fin heights significantly greater in *A. dispar* (i.e., taller fins relative to head).
- Head length/pectoral fin length: *A. ginaonis* 2.07 vs. *A. dispar* 1.66 (p<0.001) — shorter pectoral fins in *A. ginaonis*.
- **No significant difference** in total length/body depth (p>0.05), head length/snout length (p>0.05), or head length/interorbital distance (p>0.05).
- Sexual dimorphism in ventral fin length/head length ratio: reversed pattern between species — in *A. ginaonis* males > females; in *A. dispar* females > males.

### Pigmentation
- **Males (both species):** 2 black vertical bars on caudal fin (occasionally 1, 3, or Y-shaped forked bar), hyaline posterior margin; 4–5 horizontal rows of dark spots on dorsal fin; pectoral fin pigmentation concentrated on rays in *A. ginaonis*.
- **Females (both species):** Caudal fin lightly speckled (no bars); weak dorsal fin pigmentation; flank bars less defined than males.
- **Live coloration in A. ginaonis:** Fins light orange; flank bars alternately black-orange and grey-white/light blue/light orange; head iridescent light blue-green anteriorly; opercle orange dorsally, blue postero-ventrally; iris iridescent green.
- **Live coloration in A. dispar:** Fins typically lemon yellow (sometimes orange-tinged); body bars black and light orange; some individuals with orange spot on opercle.
- **Distinguishing trait:** Milky-white coloration around anal fin base, caudal peduncle, and ventrally from pectoral fins to isthmus observed in *A. dispar* (both sexes) but **absent in A. ginaonis**.

### Gut Morphology
- *A. ginaonis*: gut coiling pattern **variable** among individuals (three distinct patterns illustrated).
- *A. dispar*: gut pattern **constant spiral** shape.

### Scalation
- *A. dispar*: scales variably absent from belly (between pectoral and ventral fins) and on isthmus in some specimens.
- *A. ginaonis*: these areas consistently scaled.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**[[Aphanius ginaonis]]** is a hot-spring endemic restricted to a single locality — the Ginao spring (Ab-e-Garm), ~38 km north of Bandar Abbas, at ~400 m altitude.

### Habitat Characteristics
- Spring partially enclosed by historic brick hammam (bath-house) structure.
- Main stream 10–15 m wide near source; stony/pebble substrate covered in lime-green algae.
- Fish notably **absent from the main spring flow and source area**; instead concentrated along stream margins and in shallow (as little as 1 cm deep), mud-bottomed subsidiary springs subject to soap and food debris pollution.
- **Water temperature:** Spring water constant at 41°C; subsidiary springs slightly cooler (37–40°C); shade air temp recorded at 20°C.
- **Water chemistry (Joneidi et al. 1971):**
  - pH: 6.2
  - Conductivity: 14,000 µmhos
  - Dry residue (180°C): 9,933 mg/L
  - Flow rate: 30 L/s
  - H₂S: 34 (units unspecified, likely ppb)
  - Major ions (reacting values): Ca 22.4, Mg 9.9, Na+K 6.1, Cl 147, SO₄ 15.4, HCO₃ 46
  - SiO₂: 10 mg/L; trace CO₂; no measurable Fe, NO₂, or CO₃
  - Strong sulphurous odor noted despite clear, colorless water.

### Comparative Species Habitat — A. dispar
- Found in nearby non-thermal drainages: marshy areas (Shirin River drainage), streams of the Sarzeh River drainage, and Kul River drainage — all within the broader Bandar Abbas region but ecologically distinct (ambient temperature freshwater/marsh systems) from the geothermal Ginao spring.

### Ecological Significance
This represents a case of **allopatric speciation/isolation driven by extreme thermal habitat specialization**, comparable to other hot-spring pupfish systems (e.g., [[Cyprinodon]] species of Death Valley). The single-species, single-locality endemism combined with morphological divergence supports classification as a distinct evolutionary lineage adapted to a physiologically extreme, isolated niche.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic resolution:** Statistically significant differences (p<0.001 for most characters) between *A. ginaonis* and *A. dispar* across nearly all meristic and morphometric traits **re-affirm species-level distinction**, refuting Berg's (1949) tentative synonymy.
- **Non-diagnostic characters:** Scales around caudal peduncle, total scale radii, and total flank bar counts did not differ significantly between species, indicating some morphological conservatism in these traits.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Present in both species, particularly in anal fin ray counts, vertebral counts, flank bar counts, and (in reversed direction between species) ventral fin length ratios.
- **Unique diagnostic pigmentation:** Absence of milky-white coloration (present in *A. dispar*) in *A. ginaonis* serves as a useful live-specimen field character.
- **Gut morphology divergence:** Constant spiral gut pattern in *A. dispar* vs. variable gut coiling in *A. ginaonis* may reflect dietary or developmental differences worth further study.
- **Conservation relevance (implicit):** As a single-locality hot-spring endemic subject to anthropogenic pollution (soap, food debris) in its marginal habitats, *A. ginaonis* represents a range-restricted species of conservation concern, though this is not explicitly discussed by the original 1980 paper.
- **Type locality microhabitat specificity:** Fish avoid the main spring source/flow, instead relying on cooler, shallower marginal subsidiary springs — an important detail for habitat management and future surveys.
