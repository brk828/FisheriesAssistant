# Endangered and Threatened Species Recovery Planning Guidelines

#policy #endangered-species #conservation-management #administrative

## Executive Summary

This document comprises U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memoranda and guidelines from 1985 establishing standardized procedures for developing and implementing recovery plans for federally listed endangered and threatened species under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended. The guidelines originated from a Recovery Planning Workshop held in October 1982 and incorporate regional office comments. The document addresses the relationship between recovery plans and other planning processes, defines the role of recovery teams, establishes a priority system for recovery tasks, and delegates approval authority to Regional Directors for certain species recovery plans. The guidelines mandate that recovery plans include quantifiable objectives, annual reviews, and standardized implementation schedules with three-tiered task priorities.

## Background & Description

The [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 (Section 4(f)) directs the Secretary of the Interior and Secretary of Commerce to develop and implement recovery plans for conservation and survival of listed species. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] share administrative responsibility, with FWS responsible for terrestrial species and most birds, while NMFS handles marine species except birds.

Recovery plans serve as guidance documents that combine Federal, State, local, and private organization programs into coordinated efforts aimed at improving species status and ultimately achieving delisting. Plans are structured in three parts: Part I (Introduction) defines problems confronting the species including habitat requirements, limiting factors, distribution, and threats; Part II (Recovery) states primary objectives in quantifiable terms and provides a Stepdown Outline with narrative describing recovery tasks; Part III (Implementation Schedule) identifies specific tasks, organizational assignments, schedules, priorities, and funding requirements for a 3-year planning horizon.

The guidelines establish that recovery planning will be the only process used to plan for management and recovery of listed species, eliminating duplicative planning under the Regional Resource Planning (RRP) system. Plans must be reviewed at least annually and updated as appropriate based on task completion and new information.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recovery plans address species across diverse ecosystems and taxonomic groups. The guidelines recognize that some species may require ecosystem-level approaches, allowing single plans for groups of species occupying common habitats and facing similar problems (e.g., [[Antioch Dunes]] Recovery Plan), while widespread species may require separate population-specific plans (e.g., [[Bald Eagle]] plans).

The priority system for species recovery incorporates degree of threat (high, moderate, low), recovery potential, and taxonomic distinctness (monotypic genus, species, subspecies), creating 18 priority categories. A conflict designation (C) elevates priority for species in conflict with construction or development projects, as mandated by 1982 Act amendments. This results in priorities ranging from 1C (highest: monotypic genus, high threat, high recovery potential, in conflict) to 18 (lowest: subspecies, low threat, low recovery potential).

Essential habitat identification is addressed in recovery plans, though specific protection strategies (fee title acquisition, easements, cooperative agreements) are determined through separate Land Protection Planning (LPP) processes. The term "essential habitat" is distinguished from "Critical Habitat," which requires formal determination by the Secretary and Federal Register publication.

## Key Findings

**Major Policy Resolutions:**
- Recovery planning process replaces duplicative RRP planning for listed species
- Recovery team role defined with regional flexibility and accountability
- Regional Directors granted approval authority for plans covering species entirely within their region or not involving national policy issues
- Plans for Regional Director approval require only one combined technical/agency draft reviewed by Washington Office, streamlining the process

**Recovery Plan Requirements:**
- Primary recovery objectives must be stated in quantifiable and verifiable terms (biological, administrative, or measurable parameters)
- Objectives should correspond to factors causing listing and define parameters for delisting or reclassification
- If delisting appears unlikely, plans must clearly state this and identify quantified objectives for maintaining or preventing extinction
- Plans require annual review at minimum, with updates and revisions as necessary

**Task Priority System (Three Tiers):**
- Priority 1: Actions required to prevent extinction or irreversible decline in foreseeable future (e.g., "Provide [[Socorro Isopod]] permanent water flow")
- Priority 2: Actions to prevent significant population/habitat decline short of extinction (e.g., "[[Plymouth Red-bellied Turtle]] law enforcement to prevent taking")
- Priority 3: All other actions necessary for full recovery (e.g., "Establishing new [[Peregrine Falcon]] nest sites")

**Species Recovery Priority System (18 Categories):**
- Based on degree of threat (high/moderate/low), recovery potential (high/low), and taxonomic status (monotypic genus/species/subspecies)
- Conflict designation (C) added for species in conflict with development projects, identified through Section 7 consultations generating jeopardy biological opinions
- Combined species-task priority creates two-tiered system (e.g., 1C-1 represents highest priority activity)

**Processing and Approval:**
- Plans for Director approval: technical draft (60 days review) → agency draft (60 days) → final (45 days)
- Plans for Regional Director approval: combined technical/agency draft (60 days) → final
- All plans require 25 copies sent to Washington Office for distribution to cooperating agencies
- Printed plans must include specific disclaimer language depending on draft status

**Implementation Schedule Format:**
- Must identify tasks as research, management, or administrative
- Specify organizational assignments, schedules, priorities, and funding for 3-year period
- Include columns for: general category, plan task, task number, priority (1-3), duration, responsible agencies (FWS and non-FWS), fiscal year costs, and comments
- Tasks completed should be indicated in comments column; ongoing tasks tracked continuously

**Recovery Team Guidelines:**
- Teams appointed by Regional Directors based on expertise, current involvement, willingness, and availability
- Team expenses (travel, clerical services, supplies, printing) funded by Service; salaries borne by members' employing agencies
- Teams draft recovery plans and may provide technical assistance on biological matters when requested
- Teams do NOT: distribute drafts beyond technical review, implement recovery actions, determine Critical Habitat, act as pressure groups, or represent agency positions
- Team meeting minutes must be forwarded to all cooperators and Washington Office

**Contracting Procedures:**
- Requirements under $10,000: DI-1 Requisition, 21-day processing
- Requirements $10,000-$25,000: DI-1 Requisition, 60-day processing including Commerce Business Daily publication
- Requirements over $25,000: formal advertising (60 days), competitive negotiation (120 days), or non-competitive negotiation (120 days) with sole-source justification
- All procurements over $10,000 require CBD announcement minimum 30 days prior to award, even for sole-source awards

**Update vs. Revision Procedures:**
- Updates: minor changes to Implementation Schedule, completed tasks noted; no administrative action required beyond informing Office of Endangered Species
- Revisions: rewriting portions of Parts I or II, major editorial changes; must be scheduled in Program Advice and processed through appropriate draft reviews
