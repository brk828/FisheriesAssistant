# Standing Crops of Fish in Iowa Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This 1956 paper, by Kenneth D. Carlander and Robert B. Moorman of the [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]], summarizes fish population estimates from 42 Iowa ponds made over roughly the previous ten years. Populations were estimated mainly by [[Mark and Recapture Method]] (seining) and, in some ponds, by [[Rotenone Sampling]]. Standing crops (pounds per acre) ranged from 28 to 1,235 lb/acre. High totals were driven by [[Bullhead]] and [[Buffalo]] populations, while [[Bluegill]] standing crops were generally above 100 lb/acre unless competition or turbidity limited them. [[Largemouth Bass]] estimates were the least reliable.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Consolidate previously scattered pond survey data (earlier published in Fessler 1950, Fredin 1950, Carlander 1951, 1952, Ruhr 1952, Lawrence 1952) into a single standing-crop summary.
- **Pond set:** 42 ponds across multiple Iowa counties (including Boone, Davis, Jasper, Jefferson, Lucas, Marion, Marshall, Story and Wayne), ranging from 0.1 to 4.8 acres (Table 1).
- **Study programme:** Sponsored by the Iowa State Conservation Commission and the Industrial Science Research Institute of Iowa State Colleges, with Fish and Wildlife Service cooperation.
- **Estimation methods:**
  - Marking by fin clipping, with recapture by repeated seining (Petersen-type formula P = cn/m; the [[Schumacher and Eschmeyer Formula]] was also used, giving similar results). Traps were used in a few ponds.
  - Rotenone poisoning with collection of dead fish; where possible, fish were pre-marked so the recovery fraction could be estimated.
- **Standing crop calculation:** Estimated numbers per acre multiplied by mean weight, using length-weight relationships derived from measured fish.
- **Balance criterion:** Pond balance was determined following [[Swingle Balance Criterion]] (Swingle, 1945).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Balanced populations:** Ponds in which both bass and bluegill reproduced. Bluegill standing crops in balanced bluegill-bass ponds averaged 173 lb/acre across 13 estimates (Table 3); total mean standing crop for these ponds was 187 lb/acre.
- **Turbidity:** Ponds with persistent fine suspended clay had low productivity. Pond M-18 held 41 lb/acre of bluegill and F-18 held 55 lb/acre; both were excessively turbid. Ponds F-16, F-36 and M-43 (34, 13 and 9 lb/acre of bluegill) were also very turbid.
- **Competition and crowding:** Ponds overcrowded with bluegill or with bullheads and other species usually had 30 to 75 lb/acre of bluegill (M-29 at 90 lb/acre was the highest). Crowding and slow growth were thought to limit biomass.
- **Short food chains:** Buffalo and bullheads are thought to dominate biomass because their food chains are short (Thompson, 1941; Carlander, 1955).
- **Winterkill case study:** On McFarland's Lake (4.4 acres, northeast of Ames), a March 31, 1956 winterkill yielded minimum standing crops of 75 lb/acre largemouth bass and 66.6 lb/acre bluegill. The bass figure was judged to be a complete kill, while the bluegill figure was judged incomplete.
- **Species mix in unbalanced ponds:** Standing crops of bullheads reached 653 lb/acre, and six ponds exceeded 200 lb/acre of bullheads.

## Key Findings

- **Range:** Standing crops in the 42 ponds spanned 28 to 1,235 lb/acre. The highest total (1,235 lb/acre, pond P-3) was largely buffalo.
- **Bullheads and buffalo:** Standing crops over 300 lb/acre were usually due to abundant bullheads or buffalo. Bullheads reached 653 lb/acre in one pond; six ponds had more than 200 lb/acre.
- **Bluegill:** Typically above 100 lb/acre unless limited by heavy competition from other species or high clay turbidity.
- **Largemouth bass:** Hard to estimate because seines capture bass inefficiently. In pond M-46 (0.1 acre), only one of two marked bass was recaptured in three hauls covering 80–90% of the pond. The largest bass standing crop, 80 lb/acre (pond F-17, 1950), consisted mostly of yearling bass 3–6 inches long.
- **Method bias:** Seine-based estimates for bullheads were consistently lower than rotenone estimates in five bullhead populations, suggesting part of the bullhead population avoids seines. Rotenone estimates were used in Tables 3 and 4 when both methods were available.
- **Method agreement:** Seine estimates closely matched rotenone estimates for bluegill, white crappie, golden shiner, bass, orangespot sunfish and bullhead populations in other ponds.
- **Management value:** Standing crop data can be used to assess the severity of fish kills, as in the McFarland's Lake winterkill. Comparisons with other ponds suggested the bass kill was complete and the bluegill kill was not.
