## Executive Summary
This short scientific note by R. R. Rawstron documents field observations of [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) spawning in two California reservoirs, [[Pine Flat Lake]] and [[Millerton Lake]], during 1963. The primary significance of this note is the empirical documentation of spawning behavior at water temperatures significantly lower than previously recorded. Prior literature ([[Kimsey 1958]]) suggested spawning occurred at approximately 70°F, but did not define a specific range. These observations extend the known lower limit of the spawning temperature range down to 58°F.

## Background & Description
While this note does not provide detailed morphometric data (e.g., length, weight, fin ray counts), it describes specific behavioral and physical interactions with the environment relevant to reproductive biology:
*   **Schooling Behavior:** Spawning fish collected in "small, dense schools."
*   **Locomotion:** Fish swam rapidly near the surface of the water during spawning bouts.
*   **Aerial Behavior:** Frequent jumping out of the water was observed during the spawning act.
*   **Vulnerability:** Fish were observed occasionally impaling or entangling themselves in floating debris (brush, logs) during the frenetic spawning activity, and some became stranded on shore.
*   **Egg Characteristics:** Eggs were observed adhering loosely to submerged debris (floating brush/logs) and to gillnet material, suggesting an adhesive egg chorion typical of clupeids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Threadfin Shad]] utilizes specific microhabitats and environmental triggers for reproduction within large, warmwater reservoir ecosystems.
*   **Habitat Preference:** Spawning aggregations were specifically associated with structural cover, namely floating brush and small logs that had been loosened and mobilized by rising water levels.
*   **Environmental Triggers:** The first spawning event at Pine Flat Lake occurred immediately following a heavy rainstorm, suggesting that rising water levels or associated turbidity/inflow may act as a spawning cue, in addition to temperature.
*   **Water Column Position:** Spawning activity occurs at the surface layer of the water column.
*   **Substrate Interaction:** In the absence of natural debris (as observed at Millerton Lake), shad utilized artificial substrates such as gillnets for egg deposition, indicating a general preference for available adhesive surfaces near the surface rather than a specific reliance on natural woody debris alone.

## Key Findings
*   **Lower Temperature Limit Established:** Threadfin shad were confirmed to spawn at a surface water temperature as low as **58°F** (Pine Flat Lake, April 25, 1963, 7:00 AM).
*   **Continued Activity Range:** Spawning continued to be observed at **62°F** (same day, 3:00 PM) and **64°F** (Millerton Lake, May 13, 1963).
*   **Revision of Previous Data:** This significantly lowers the previously assumed minimum spawning threshold of ~70°F cited by [[Kimsey 1958]].
*   **Duration of Spawning Bouts:** Individual spawning periods/acts were observed to last approximately 3 minutes.
*   **Frequency:** A single school was observed to engage in spawning activity twice within a 20-minute observation window, indicating rapid, repeated bouts of reproductive behavior.
*   **Environmental Correlation:** The onset of spawning was linked to a heavy rainstorm event, implying that flood pulses or rising water levels in reservoirs may serve as an environmental trigger complementing temperature thresholds.
