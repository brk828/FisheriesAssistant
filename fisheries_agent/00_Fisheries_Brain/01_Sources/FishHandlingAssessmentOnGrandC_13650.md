# Fish Handling Assessment and Habitat Protocol for Grand Canyon Humpback Chub

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from BIO/WEST, Inc. to the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program documents fish handling impacts and protocols for endangered [[Humpback Chub]] research in the [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]]. From October 1990 through August 1991, researchers handled 569 humpback chub with minimal mortality (3 fish, 0.5%) and low stress indicators. The document includes a detailed protocol for characterizing juvenile humpback chub habitat, focusing on microhabitat measurements in shallow shoreline areas where juveniles concentrate, particularly in a 4-mile reach below the [[Little Colorado River]] inflow (RM 61.5-65.5).

## Background & Description

[[BIO/WEST]] conducted monthly field investigations of [[Humpback Chub]] populations in the mainstem [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]] as part of [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]]. The research employed multiple capture methods including netting (gill and trammel nets), trapping (hoop and minnow traps), electrofishing, and seining. A total of 468 fish were [[PIT Tag|PIT tagged]] during the study period, with 459 fish greater than 200 mm TL and 110 fish less than 200 mm TL. The study documented 125 total recaptures (21.9% recapture rate) including fish marked by multiple agencies.

Juvenile humpback chub were captured in significant numbers from May through September 1991, with the majority found in shallow shoreline areas with emergent boulders and sand pockets. Few juveniles occupied open sand beaches or vertical rock cliffs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Grand Canyon]] occupy specific microhabitat characterized by shallow shoreline areas with structural complexity. The mainstem [[Colorado River]] maintains cold temperatures (45-50°F) which are more conducive to fish handling than warmer tributary streams. Juvenile fish concentrate in a 4-mile region below the [[Little Colorado River]] inflow (RM 61.5-65.5), with fewer individuals found downstream to [[Shinumo Creek]] (RM 109).

Shoreline types in the study area include talus scree, sand beach, vertical cliff, and vegetated earthen bank. The distribution of juvenile humpback chub correlates strongly with shoreline type, with preference for structurally complex habitats over homogeneous substrates.

## Key Findings

**Fish Handling Results (October 1990 - August 1991):**
- Total humpback chub handled: 569
- Total recaptures: 125 (21.9%)
- PIT tagged by BIO/WEST: 468
- Recapture rate on BIO/WEST PIT tags: 38 (8.1%)
- Mortality: 3 fish (0.5% mortality rate)

**Capture Methods:**
- Netting (gill and trammel): 484 fish
- Trapping (hoop and minnow): 32 fish
- [[Electrofishing]]: 81 fish
- Seine: 2 fish

**Stress and Impact Observations:**
- Little evidence of stress (lethargy, slimming, inability to upright, color changes) observed
- Net scars observed on humpback chub, [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and trout; marks superficial and not significantly affecting fish health
- Most net scars from 2-inch mesh gill nets; this gear type phased down in use
- [[Trammel Net|Trammel nets]] less stressful as they quickly entangle fish before gilling occurs
- No burn marks and only one deformity observed from electrofishing (Coffelt CPS system with spherical electrodes)
- No adverse effects noted from [[PIT Tag|PIT tagging]]
- Open lesions and inflammation reported from [[Carlin Tag|Carlin tags]] and [[Floy Tag|Floy tags]]; these tags being removed and fish re-tagged with PIT tags

**Mortality Details:**
1. One radiotagged recapture (PIT tag #7F7F3F3626, 432 mm TL)
2. One captured in minnow trap (51 mm TL)
3. One found unentangled in trammel net (PIT tag #7F7D08186A, 340 mm TL) at [[Little Colorado River]] mouth

**Physiological Observations:**
- Humpback chub frequently have difficulty uprighting due to air trapped in intestinal system
- Air entrapment likely from neutralizing air bladder pressure or swallowing air during handling
- Gentle abdominal massaging helps fish expel excess air and swim normally

**Stomach Pumping Pilot Study:**
- Eight adult humpback chub subjected to stomach pumping in January 1991
- No evidence of stress observed
- Procedure appeared to alleviate air-trapping problem
- One fish recaptured later with no detrimental effects

**Stress Reduction Measures Implemented:**
1. Adherence to Fish Handling Protocol with senior biologist supervision on every team
2. Eliminated video recording of each fish to reduce handling time
3. Process fish on-site for immediate release (except radiotagging candidates)
4. Constant recirculating system in live wells
5. Gill and trammel nets checked at maximum 2-hour intervals
6. Shortened electrofishing runs to prevent live well crowding
7. Limited radiotag implants to 48 fish (vs. 60 anticipated) to meet study objectives
8. Removal of old tags ([[Carlin Tag|Carlin]] and [[Floy Tag|Floy]]) and replacement with PIT tags
9. Stomach pump study to eliminate need for fish sacrifice in diet analysis

**Juvenile Habitat Characterization Protocol:**

*Objectives:*
1. Quantitatively describe microhabitat (depth, velocity, substrate, cover) used by juvenile humpback chub
2. Compare microhabitat used by juveniles with unoccupied areas
3. Compare microhabitat longitudinally across various Grand Canyon reaches

*Methodology:*
- Shoreline types segregated during electrofishing: talus scree, sand beach, vertical cliff, vegetated earthen bank
- 100-m linear shoreline designated as habitat measurement area
- Transects taken every 10 m along shoreline (9 total transects)
- Each transect extends 3 m from water's edge
- Microhabitat measurements (depth, velocity, substrate) taken at 0.5, 1.5, and 2.5 m from shoreline
- Data averaged for each distance interval and compared statistically between occupied and unoccupied areas

*Personnel and Equipment:*
- 2-3 people required (boat operator, measurer, recorder)
- Equipment: BIO/WEST SH-170 research boat, extendible Philadelphia rod, Swoffer current meter, 10-m tape/rope
- Staff gage established to monitor flow changes
- Starting points randomly selected and mapped at 1:1200 scale for replication
