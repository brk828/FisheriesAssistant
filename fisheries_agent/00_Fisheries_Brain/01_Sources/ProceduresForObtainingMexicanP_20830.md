## Executive Summary
This document is **not a primary scientific or taxonomic source**; rather, it is an administrative guideline issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] outlining the bureaucratic procedure for U.S. citizens seeking to obtain Mexican government permits for **non-marine biological investigations** (with notes on marine species/vessel considerations) within the Republic of Mexico. It details the diplomatic channels, required documentation, fees, and post-permit obligations governing biological fieldwork, including the collection of animal specimens (which would encompass [[Ichthyology|fish]] and other taxa) in Mexican territory. ## Key Findings
- **Processing Time**: Applications require approximately **6 months** for review and approval by the Mexican government.
- **Submission Protocol**: Three copies (English acceptable) must be routed through the **U.S. Embassy Science & Technology Section** in Laredo, TX, then forwarded via diplomatic pouch to the **Secretariat of Exterior Relations (SRE)** in Mexico City — direct submission to Mexican agencies is prohibited.
- **Required Application Contents**:
  - Full study plan (objectives, methodology, funding sources).
  - Investigator vitae, including birth date, education, professional history, and passport photos.
  - Letters of institutional support (U.S. and Mexican).
  - Defined field schedule and locations, with buffer time for delays.
- **Collection Fees**: A specimen collection fee of **931,000 pesos (~$345 USD, as of Jan. 1990)** payable to "Tesorería de la Federación," submitted with the application.
- **Species/Quantity Reporting**: Applicants must specify species and maximum numbers intended for collection; special notice is required if **undescribed species** may be encountered.
- **Restricted Taxa**: Permits are nearly impossible to obtain for **threatened/endangered species** or for collection within **Protected Natural Areas**.
- **Mexican Collaboration Requirement**: Applications must specify **Mexican co-investigators** who will participate in and benefit from the fieldwork — a policy shift from historical patterns where data/specimens left Mexico entirely with foreign researchers.
- **Equipment Disclosure**: All field equipment (nets, traps, vehicles, aircraft, boats, radios, generators, cameras, etc.) must be itemized to facilitate customs entry and avoid interior inspection issues.
- **Post-Permit Obligations**:
  - Permits are **non-transferable**, issued only to the principal investigator.
  - **40% of collected specimens** and associated data must be deposited with a **Mexican scientific institution**.
  - Permit receipt must be confirmed with Mexico within **15 days**.
  - Unused permits must be returned to the U.S. Embassy for cancellation.
  - Copies of the permit/proposal must go to the **State Delegate for Ecology (SEDUE)**.
  - All progress reports, final reports, and publications resulting from the permitted work **must be submitted to SRE**; failure to comply jeopardizes future permit approval.
- **Legal Scope**: Only activities, locations, and time periods explicitly specified in the permit are legally authorized; the accompanying **Diplomatic Note from SRE** serves as the definitive authorization document.
- **Overlapping U.S. Jurisdiction**: Even while conducting fieldwork abroad, U.S. researchers remain subject to U.S. laws including the [[Endangered Species Act]], [[CITES]], the **Migratory Bird Treaty Act**, and the **Lacey Act**.
- **Marine-Specific Considerations**: Marine environment or marine species (e.g., sea turtle) studies, and any use of a research vessel entering Mexican waters, require additional ministerial review and detailed disclosure of vessel documentation, crew, and onboard communication/detection equipment (radio, radar, sonar).
