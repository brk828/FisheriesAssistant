# Comparative Cutaneous Sense Organs of Epigean and Hypogean Astyanax Forms

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Characidae

## Executive Summary

This 1967 German-language dissertation by [[Christian Schemmel]] (Zoologisches Staatsinstitut und Museum Hamburg) compares the cutaneous sense organs of the surface-dwelling [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] river fish with three cave populations: [[Chica Cave Fish]], [[Sabinos Cave Fish]] and [[Pachon Cave Fish]]. The study covers the [[Lateral Line System]], the [[Labyrinth]], the [[Taste Buds]] and the [[Olfactory Organ]]. It finds no degenerative or constructive change in neuromasts, labyrinth or nasal organs, but a graded increase in taste bud number and extent. The author argues that the lost sense of vision was compensated by enhanced non-visual senses, and that the differences are polygenic.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** [[Hubbs]] and Innes described the first cave form in 1936 as *Anoptichthys jordani*. Alvarez later named *A. hubbsi* and *A. antrobius*. Schemmel treats all cave characins as ecological races of *Astyanax mexicanus*, since [[Sadowski]] crossbreeding showed fertile F1 hybrids. The author retains the name *mexicanus* instead of *fasciatus* for literature continuity.
- **Cave system:** Five limestone caves lie in one valley draining underground into the Rio Tampaon. Distances from the river range from 1.2 km (Cueva Chica) to 87.6 km (Cueva del Pachon). The cave populations are blind and largely depigmented, but differ slightly in fin rays, orbital bones and body proportions.
- **Sample material:** Surface fish were from laboratory stocks. Sabinos fish were wild-caught in 1964 and imported directly. Chica fish were not examined because only genetically heterogeneous material was available.
- **Core questions:** (1) Did the surface fish possess predispositions that allowed the transition to lightless habitats? (2) Did sense organs degenerate, stay unchanged, or improve to compensate for the loss of sight?

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The cave forms have reduced eyes, usually lacking a lens, with atrophied retina and optic nerve. A light sense nevertheless persists, probably in the pineal complex, eye remnants and trunk region. The surface fish relies on vision for food capture, schooling and orientation. The author proposes that its dense neuromast system acts as a distance-touch sense that could substitute for vision. Blinded surface fish keep swimming restlessly, as cave fish do, and reproduce in darkness. The author therefore classes the river fish as a dim-light or crepuscular animal. Other characids also have dense neuromast systems, which may indicate a broader pre-adaptation to subterranean life.

## Key Findings

1. **Lateral line canal system:** The canal system is typical of teleosts and shows no morphological or histological difference between forms. The only notable difference is more frequent fragmentation of the infraorbital canal in cave fish. This is linked to the loss of the large eye, which reshapes the orbital bones.
2. **Free neuromasts:** All forms are richly supplied with free neuromasts on the trunk, roughly 4–8 per scale. Cave forms have more free neuromasts in the suborbital and opercular regions, where the surface fish has bare skin. Other characids were examined (Hemigrammus, Gymnocorymbus, Nematobrycon, Hyphessobrycon) and are hardly inferior. Canal counts per side are 25–35 on the trunk and about 10 in the preopercular–mandibular canal.
3. **Labyrinth and Weberian apparatus:** No differences were found in semicircular canals, utricle, sacculus, lagena, sagitta, or Weberian ossicles. The author concludes there is no sign of degeneration or functional change in these organs.
4. **Taste buds:** Taste buds are restricted to the head. Lip densities are about 30 (upper) and 65 (lower) per 1/4 mm² in surface fish, and 35 and 70 in Sabinos and Pachon fish. Distribution extends further across the head in cave forms. Pachon fish have the widest distribution, with ventral head density 3–4 times that of the mouth cavity. The order is surface fish (minimum) < Sabinos < Pachon (maximum).
5. **Heredity of taste bud pattern:** F1 hybrids (surface × Pachon) were intermediate. F2 hybrids (392 fish from 6 broods) showed a continuous range with an intermediate peak. The distribution was judged leptokurtic. The author concludes the trait is polygenic and largely polymeric, and that it segregates independently of eye and pigment reduction.
6. **Feeding behaviour:** Blinded surface fish adopt a near-vertical posture while feeding, which cave fish do not. This posture is not explained by taste bud extent. The author suggests the expanded ventral gustatory field helps cave fish locate food lying on the bottom.
7. **Olfactory organ:** The nasal lamellae are the same in form and histology across forms. Lamella counts in surface fish range from 0 to 29 per nasal pit, depending on size. Previous reports of fixed counts (16 surface, 14 Chica, 5 Sabinos pairs) are judged incorrect. Surface fish have slightly more lamellae than cave forms at equal length (about 1 at 2 cm, 4 at 6 cm). Males have more lamellae than females from about 3 cm standard length. Cave nasal pits are flatter and more open.
8. **Overall conclusion:** The eye is the only sense organ that has degenerated in the cave forms. The lateral line and taste systems were maintained or enhanced, which the author interprets as compensation for lost vision. The author considers taste bud expansion a probable adaptive gain, with the river fish's dense neuromast system serving as its pre-adaptation.
