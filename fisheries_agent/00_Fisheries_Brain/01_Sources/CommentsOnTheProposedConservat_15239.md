## Executive Summary
This document is not a standard morphological/ecological field study but a **taxonomic nomenclature debate** published in the *Bulletin of Zoological Nomenclature* (1994), concerning [[ICZN Case 2808]]. The dispute centers on whether the specific name *[[Notropis topeka]]* (originally described as *Cliola (Hybopsis) topeka* by Gilbert, 1884) should be formally conserved by the International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature (ICZN), overriding the principle of nomenclatural priority that would otherwise mandate replacement with the older name *[[Notropis tristis]]* (originally *Moniana tristis* Girard, 1856). Two independent commentaries — from Richard L. Mayden (opposing conservation) and Reeve M. Bailey (supporting conservation) — present competing arguments rooted in taxonomic history, type specimen identification, and dating of Girard's original publication.

## Background & Description
No new morphological data on *Notropis topeka* itself is presented. However, the paper discusses **type specimen identification** issues relevant to taxonomic diagnosis:
- The original description of *Moniana tristis* by [[Girard]] (1856) is characterized by both commentators as vague/inadequate — insufficient to unambiguously assign the name to a single known taxon without direct reference to type material.
- Two **syntypes** of *Moniana tristis* Girard, 1856 have been located and identified as belonging to **two distinct, well-marked species**:
  - *[[Lythrurus umbratilis]]* (or *Notropis umbratilis*), Girard, 1856
  - *[[Notropis topeka]]* (Gilbert, 1884)
- This dual identification of syntypes with two morphologically distinguishable species is cited by Bailey as direct evidence of the inadequacy of Girard's original species description, undermining Mayden & Gilbert's (1989) claim that the description was "good according to the standards at the time."
- C.R. Gilbert (1978, p. 84) previously ranked Girard's description of *tristis* as "not definitely identifiable" without type material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- *Notropis topeka* is described as a **familiar cyprinid fish** (minnow, family Cyprinidae) native to **north-central United States**, with strong regional association with **Kansas**.
- No detailed habitat, diet, or population ecology data are provided in this nomenclatural correspondence; the taxon's ecological context is referenced only insofar as it pertains to conservation status.
- Notably, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] candidate species list for federal protection (used as supporting evidence for taxonomic urgency) lists the species under the name **N. tristis**, not *N. topeka* — indicating the federal agency had already adopted the priority name change proposed by Mayden & Gilbert (1989).

## Key Findings
- **Nomenclatural dispute:** [[Richard L. Mayden]] (with Carter R. Gilbert, 1989) proposed renaming *Notropis topeka* to *Notropis tristis* based on strict application of the **Principle of Priority**, following lectotype selection for *Moniana tristis*.
- **Opposing view (Cross & Collins):** Original application authors argued Girard's (1856) description of *tristis* was too poor/inaccurate to confidently apply to a known taxon, and that *N. tristis* was a candidate species for federal protection — both arguments rejected by Mayden as lacking scientific merit and reflecting "personal bias towards a local name."
- **Dating controversy:** Bailey provides detailed bibliographic analysis showing Girard's paper (containing the description of *Moniana tristis*) was likely issued in **1856**, not 1857 as commonly cited in the application — based on:
  - Acceptance date of 30 September 1856 (Proceedings vol. 8, p. 163).
  - A separate offprint issued in September 1856 with different pagination (pp. 1–54) and a slightly altered title (omitting "of America").
  - Confirmed receipt of the bound Proceedings volume by the New York State Library trustees by 27 December 1856.
  - The volume's title page (dated 1857) reflects assembly date, not actual issue date — a known Academy publication practice.
- **Stability argument:** Bailey invokes **[[ICZN Article 23b]]** (importance of nomenclatural stability) and **Recommendation 24A** (first revisers should choose the name/act that best serves stability), arguing Mayden & Gilbert's 1989 lectotype designation was an avoidable, destabilizing act that could have preserved *N. topeka* had priority-of-stability principles been followed.
- **Consensus building:** Bailey reports endorsement of the conservation proposal from four additional ichthyologists — [[William L. Fink]], [[William A. Gosline]], [[Robert Rush Miller]], and [[Gerald R. Smith]] — countering the suggestion that support for conserving *N. topeka* is merely regional/Kansas-based bias.
- **Outcome context:** Despite the priority-based rename to *N. tristis* being adopted by some federal listings, over a century of consistent scientific usage of *Notropis topeka* is cited as strong justification for formal conservation of the junior but stable name under the ICZN Code.
