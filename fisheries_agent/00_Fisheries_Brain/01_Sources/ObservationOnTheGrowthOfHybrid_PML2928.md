# Growth of Hybrid Tilapias in a Pond Polyculture Trial

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This short report by A. Yashouv (late) and A. Halevy, of the [[Dor Fish And Aquaculture Research Station]], describes an unplanned observation of a single 0.1 ha pond stocked with [[Hybrid Tilapia]] fry and several other species. Over a 230-day culture period that included winter, the pond produced a general weight increment of 701 kg, a result the authors call surprising. The authors state that the observations are not a basis for firm conclusions, but they suggest that tilapias have considerable capacity to exploit suitable pond conditions and that tilapia culture deserves more attention within [[Pond Polyculture]] systems.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Fish culturists need to know the maximum growth rate each species can reach at each size under different conditions. The report presents observations from the nursery and culture of hybrid tilapias.
- **Study pond:** 0.1 ha, stocked on 27 August 1969 and emptied on 14 April 1970. This gave a growing season of 230 days, including winter.
- **Stock:** Hybrid fry from a cross of male [[Tilapia Aurea]] with female [[Tilapia Vulcani]], with an average stocking weight of 1 g.
- **Polyculture species:** [[Flathead Mullet]] (*Mugil capito*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) and [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) fry were added. The stated aims were to use the pond's productivity more fully without competition for natural food, and to keep the pond free of submerged vegetation and filamentous algae.
- **Date discrepancy:** The introduction gives the year of the observations as 1959, while the stocking and harvest dates point to 1969–1970. The 1969–1970 dates are consistent with the 230-day period and appear to be correct.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pond management**
- **Fertilization:** Applied about once a week with 6 kg superphosphate, 6 kg ammonium sulphate and 0.04 m³ poultry manure. Two months (one in the autumn and one in January) had less frequent applications. Season totals were 144 kg superphosphate, 120 kg ammonium sulphate and 0.8 m³ poultry manure. See [[Pond Fertilization]].
- **Feeding:** A total of 775 kg of pelleted feed containing 25% protein was given. About 54% was offered from stocking until 14 November. The rest was offered from 1 February until harvest on 13–14 April. No supplementary feed was given during the 78-day period from mid-November to early February. See [[Supplementary Feeding]].

**Temperature and growth limits**
- Tilapias do not grow at water temperatures below 15 °C.
- The authors calculate that tilapias grew until the last week of November (90 days) and again from the first week of March (39 days), giving only 130 days of effective growth.
- The authors assume the other species also grew little below 15 °C. The water temperature curve is shown in the original Figure 1, which is not reproduced here. See [[Water Temperature]].

## Key Findings

**Harvest results (after 230 days)**

| Group | Fish count | Approx. weight (kg) | Weight increment (kg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tilapia (three size classes) | 3,830 | ~669–673 | ~669 |
| Mullet | 184 | 12.8 | 12.4 |
| Common carp | 4 | 4.0 | 3.6 |
| Grass carp | 4 | 6.0 | 6.0 |

- Tilapia size classes were 1,150 (size 1), 1,280 (size 2) and 1,400 (size 3) fish. The class weights are about 370, 166 and 137 kg, which sum to 673 kg. The total-weight figure of 669 kg appears in another part of the table. The OCR text is inconsistent, so the exact tilapia total should be checked against the source.
- The overall weight increment for the pond was 701 kg, giving a food conversion ratio of 1.1.

**Growth rates**
- Over the full 230-day season, the daily increment was 30 kg/ha.
- Counting only the 130 days of effective growth, the daily increment rises to 54 kg/ha. The authors argue that the 701 kg yield would be "considerably higher" if only effective growing days were counted.

**Conclusions and recommendations (as stated by the authors)**
- The results were unplanned observations from a single pond, so "no firm conclusion" can be drawn from them.
- The results indicate that tilapias have considerable capacity to exploit suitable culture conditions in ponds.
- More attention should be given to planning tilapia culture within a range of pond polyculture conditions.
- The authors note that the results have "great significance for breeding plans at the fish farms."
