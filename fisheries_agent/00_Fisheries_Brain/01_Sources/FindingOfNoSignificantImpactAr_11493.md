# Arnett Creek/Telegraph Canyon Native Fish Re-establishment Project

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #Catostomidae #conservation-management #Gila-River-Basin

## Executive Summary

This USDA Forest Service Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) documents a native fish re-establishment project on [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]] within the [[Tonto National Forest]], Arizona. The project's core management action involves the use of [[Piscicides]] (Rotenone and Antimycin A) to eradicate non-native [[Green Sunfish]] and other exotic fish, followed by reintroduction of native fish species including [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Gila Chub]], and the federally Endangered [[Gila Topminnow]]. Five alternatives (A–E) were evaluated, ranging from no action to full-scale native fish restoration with endangered species reintroduction. The agency's selected action is **Alternative C**, which combines piscicide treatment, barrier construction at a downstream site, and phased reintroduction of non-listed native fish, with future re-evaluation for reintroducing [[Gila Chub]] and [[Gila Topminnow]]. The project is also linked to securing an instream flow water right for Arnett Creek, contingent upon successful native fish reintroduction.

## Background & Description

The source document is a regulatory/NEPA impact summary and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of the fish species. Species mentioned by common ecological role rather than anatomy:

- **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*) — Native cyprinid; federally listed status referenced (candidate for reintroduction under later alternatives); benthic/pool-dwelling desert stream fish.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — Small-bodied live-bearing poeciliid; listed as **Endangered** under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended.
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — Small native cyprinid; not ESA-listed; tolerant of warmwater intermittent desert streams.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Catostomus clarkii*) — Native catostomid; not ESA-listed; benthic grazer typical of Sonoran Desert streams.
- **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*) — Non-native centrarchid predator; identified as a key threat to native amphibian ([[Lowland Leopard Frog]]) populations via predation.

No meristic, coloration, or size data are reported in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Warmwater desert stream system (Arnett Creek/Telegraph Canyon) within Sonoran desert scrub, part of the [[Gila River]] drainage basin.
- **Community structure**: Native fish assemblage (cyprinids, catostomids, poeciliids) historically displaced or suppressed by non-native predatory sunfish.
- **Predator-prey dynamics**: [[Green Sunfish]] prey heavily on larval and adult [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (*Lithobates yavapaiensis*), a species of conservation concern; removal of sunfish is expected to yield long-term population benefits for leopard frogs.
- **Macroinvertebrate community**: Piscicide treatment is expected to cause short-term negative impacts on macroinvertebrates, with rapid recovery anticipated due to fast detoxification rates of Antimycin A and Rotenone in warmwater systems.
- **Barrier ecology**: Fish barriers (Site 1: T2S, R12E, Sec. 8; Site 2: T2S, R12E, Sec. 7) are engineered to prevent upstream recolonization by exotic fish, creating an isolated refuge for reintroduced native species.
- **Watershed scope of treatment**: Piscicide-treatable waters span portions of Sections 7–11, 13–36 (T2S, R12E); Sections 19–32 (T2S, R13E); and Sections 3, 4, 5, 8, 9 (T3S, R12E), Salt River Base and Meridian.
- **Instream flow linkage**: Native fish presence is tied to a pending Forest Service instream flow water right application — an example of the interdependence between species conservation status and water law/hydrological management in arid ecosystems.

## Key Findings

- **Alternatives evaluated**: One No Action alternative (A) and four action alternatives (B–E), differing primarily in barrier site location (Site 1 vs. Site 2), scope of piscicide restrictions, and which native species are reintroduced (non-listed only vs. inclusion of the Endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Gila Chub]]).
- **Selected alternative**: **Alternative C** — barrier at Site 2, full watershed piscicide treatment authorization, reintroduction of [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Desert Sucker]] initially, with a 9-year re-evaluation window for adding [[Gila Chub]] and [[Gila Topminnow]].
- **No Action consequences**: Failure to act risks loss of the instream flow water right and continued unchecked predation by [[Green Sunfish]] on leopard frogs, with no contribution to native fish recovery.
- **Piscicide safety**: Rotenone and Antimycin A were assessed as having rapid detoxification rates in warmwater systems, resulting in no lasting water quality impacts; temporary discoloration possible if potassium permanganate neutralizer is used.
- **Non-target species impacts**: Potential short-term impacts to [[Javelina]] and larval [[Lowland Leopard Frog]]s from piscicide exposure, mitigated through minimal Rotenone use and relocation of native fish during spot treatments.
- **Endangered species safeguard**: Under Alternatives D and E, once [[Gila Topminnow]] is stocked, further piscicide spot/multiple treatments are prohibited to avoid harming this ESA-listed species.
- **No jeopardy finding**: The Biological Assessment and Evaluation (dated May 29, 1996) concluded **No Effect** on federally listed Threatened or Endangered species/habitat; project may negatively affect individual [[Lowland Leopard Frog]]s but benefits the population overall.
- **Private lands**: Some exotic fish habitat exists on private land within the watershed; landowners consented to piscicide treatment access.
- **Regulatory context**: A Research Natural Area (RNA) proposal for part of the project area was withdrawn due to conflicting mineral claims, unrelated to fish conservation outcomes.
- **NEPA conclusion**: No significant environmental effects were identified under any of the Ten Significance Criteria (40 CFR 1508.27); therefore, an Environmental Impact Statement was determined unnecessary, and the FONSI was issued by Forest Supervisor Charles R. Bazan.
