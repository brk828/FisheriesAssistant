# Distribution and Variation of *Etheostoma spectabile* (Orangethroat Darter)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 monograph by Donald A. Distler (University of Kansas Science Bulletin, vol. 48, no. 5, pp. 143–208) revises the [[Orangethroat Darter]], *Etheostoma spectabile* (Agassiz), a small bottom-dwelling percid. It combines range mapping, morphometric and meristic variation, laboratory breeding experiments, and a zoogeographic reconstruction. The author recognizes five subspecies, three of them described as new: [[Etheostoma Spectabile Uniporum]], [[Etheostoma Spectabile Fragi]] and [[Etheostoma Spectabile Squamosum]]. The nominate [[Etheostoma Spectabile Spectabile]] and [[Plains Orangethroat]] (*E. s. pulchellum*) are retained. Laboratory crosses produced progeny intermediate in most characters, and the species is interpreted as originating in the Ozark Plateaus during the Pleistocene.

## Background & Description

The orangethroat belongs to the tribe Etheostomatini (subfamily Percinae), a group of roughly 100 darter species. Its air bladder is reduced or absent, an adaptation to a bottom-dwelling life. The species has a long taxonomic history. Agassiz described it in 1854 as *Poecilichthys spectabilis*, from the Osage River, Missouri. Early workers often confused it with the [[Rainbow Darter]] (*Etheostoma caeruleum*) until Trautman (1930) established the two as distinct species.

**Diagnosis:** Interrupted infraorbital canal, body depth greatest near the spinous dorsal fin origin, 11–12 pectoral rays (usually 11–12), and, in nuptial males, bright orange branchiostegal membranes.

**Key counts (species description):**
- Dorsal spines 8–12 (usually 9–10); dorsal rays 10–15 (usually 12–13)
- Anal soft rays 4–8 (usually 6–7); pectoral rays 10–14
- Vertebrae 35–37 (usually 36)
- Standard length to head length 3.0–3.8 (head length)

**Coloration:** Breeding males show blue or blue-green transverse bars on orange or red interspaces, and orange or red on the branchiostegal membranes. Females are duller and lack bright ventral color.

**Methods:** Thirty-two morphometric and 25 meristic characters were recorded from small series (20–30 fish) across the range. Only specimens over 30 mm standard length were used to limit allometric effects. Measurements were judged to have little diagnostic value and were omitted from the descriptions. Anterior squamation was scored on a 0–3 index and summed and averaged into an anterior scale index.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species occupies hardwater, gravelly streams. Its range covers the Edwards, Ozark and Interior Low plateaus and adjacent Central Lowlands, extending into the Great Plains. In nature, aquatic insect larvae make up most of its diet. Copepods predominated in the plankton fed to laboratory fish, giving way to cladocerans in spring and fall.

Subspecies boundaries are often clinal. Several areas of intergradation exist, most notably between [[Etheostoma Spectabile Spectabile]] and [[Plains Orangethroat]] in the lower Kansas and Missouri basins. Hybridization with related darters is uncommon but documented:
- [[Etheostoma Whipplei]] and [[Etheostoma Radiosum]] (Kansas, Oklahoma)
- [[Rainbow Darter]] (Kentucky, Arkansas)
- [[Percina Sciera]] (Hubbs and Laritz 1961)

Laboratory temperatures of 15–21 °C were used. Spawning began at 15–16 °C and peaked between 16 and 20 °C. Darters held at 12 °C did not spawn. Under lab conditions, breeding peaked in late March through May, close to the natural peak at this latitude.

## Key Findings

**1. Five subspecies are recognized.**

| Subspecies | Authority | Range (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| *E. s. spectabile* | Agassiz 1854 | Northern Ozarks, Interior Low Plateaus, Ohio/Mississippi tributaries; four racial groups |
| *E. s. pulchellum* | (Girard 1859) | Great Plains: Platte, Republican, Kansas, Arkansas, Red and Texas rivers |
| *E. s. squamosum* | Distler, new subspecies | Western slope of Springfield Plateau (Grand/Neosho and Illinois rivers) |
| *E. s. uniporum* | Distler, new subspecies | Black River drainage (Cane Creek, Current, Eleven Point, Spring rivers) |
| *E. s. fragi* | Distler, new subspecies | Strawberry River, northeastern Arkansas |

**2. Subspecies diagnostics (selected).**
- *uniporum* usually has a single posterior infraorbital pore, an interrupted supratemporal canal, and modal 11 pectoral rays. It is the most neotenic member of the complex, with the reduced sensory canals and lowest mean pectoral and pored scale counts.
- *fragi* is the most scaly-headed form. It has the highest mean lateral line scales (50, range 43–55), pored scales (35, range 26–42) and anterior scale index (2.5), and lacks breeding tubercles. Its males have a red gular region.
- *squamosum* has moderately scaled anterior regions (anterior index 2) and a tri-saddle dorsal pattern shared only with [[Etheostoma Spectabile Squamosum]] relatives and the rainbow darter.
- *pulchellum* has the most sparsely scaled anterior region (mean anterior index 0.6) and usually 9 or more blue-green vertical bars in breeding males.

**3. Sample sizes.** Type series include 230 paratypes for *uniporum* (holotype KU 7478), 145 for *fragi* (KU 7481) and 33 for *squamosum* (KU 7476). The *uniporum* holotype was collected from Pigeon Creek, Dent County, Missouri, on April 7, 1963.

**4. Range of *E. spectabile* sensu lato.** Map 1 plots more than 100 localities. Records in Indiana and Ohio were partly taken from earlier published surveys. A questionable Virginia record (UMMZ 96877, North Fork Holston River) was judged likely an introduced fish or a labeling error.

**5. Laboratory breeding.**
- Intrasubspecific crosses of *pulchellum* and *spectabile* produced F1 and F2 progeny of stable color and squamation.
- Intersubspecific crosses (e.g., *spectabile* × *pulchellum*) gave progeny intermediate in color, squamation and fin banding. Backcross progeny were uniformly intermediate, with no evidence of Mendelian segregation.
- Lateral line canal lengths decreased in almost all laboratory crosses, possibly due to the lentic laboratory environment or to an effect of productivity.
- Malformations appeared in some lines. In one progeny group (BABA), 63% had one or both pelvic fins malformed.

**6. Hybridization.** Natural hybrids were examined from several localities. A *uniporum* population in Blue River, Oklahoma, was interpreted as a hybrid swarm between *Etheostoma radiosum cyanorum* and *E. s. pulchellum*. The rainbow darter hybridized with *spectabile* in Kentucky, where about 10% of one mixed sample was judged hybrid. Hybrid swarms are rare.

**7. Zoogeographic history.**
- The species likely originated in the Ozark Plateaus, which hold the greatest racial diversity. Dispersal then extended east, north and west into the Teays-Mississippi system.
- Pleistocene glaciation (Kansan and Illinoian ice) isolated populations in refugia. Later drainage changes, including Mississippi River shifts and Arkansas River captures, reconnected or separated them.
- *E. s. pulchellum* probably entered the Missouri-Platte system early and was later isolated by Nebraskan or Kansan ice.

**8. Conclusions.** The author concludes that the species is a set of weakly differentiated races and subspecies whose boundaries track past drainage changes. Some populations show evidence of unidirectional hybridization. Gametic differences among geographic variants may explain some anomalous progeny in the lab.
