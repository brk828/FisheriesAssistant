# Hemigrammus mimus: A New Characin from the Rio Negro, Brazil

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, the eighth in James Bohlke's series on the family Characidae, describes [[Hemigrammus Mimus]] as a new species from rapids at Camandos on the [[Rio Negro]], Brazil. The fish was found while Bohlke searched for more material of two [[Microschemobrycon]] species described in an earlier paper. Its generic placement is treated as provisional, because the boundaries between [[Hemigrammus]] and [[Hyphessobrycon]] are described as artificial. The author places the species in Hemigrammus because it most resembles [[Hemigrammus Cylindricus]], while noting that it is an intermediate form in caudal scalation.

## Background & Description

The two type specimens were initially mistaken for Rio Negro species of [[Microschemobrycon]], which they resemble in body shape and coloration. Examination of the premaxillary dentition, which is biserial, showed that they belong to the tetragonopterine characins ([[Tetragonopterine Characins]]). The specific name *mimus* refers to this resemblance, from the Greek word for an imitator or mimic actor.

**Type material:**
- Holotype: SU 47759, 28.0 mm standard length, collected by [[Carl Ternetz]] on January 22, 1925.
- Paratype: SU 47760, 28.3 mm standard length, same data as the holotype.
- Specimens are held in the [[Stanford University]] collection (SU).

**Morphological features:**
- **Body:** Nearly subcylindrical and only slightly compressed. Greatest depth is at the dorsal fin origin.
- **Dentition:** Premaxillae carry 10 or 11 inner-series tricuspid teeth and a single outer-series tooth on each side. The mandible has about 16 to 18 teeth on each side.
- **Gill rakers:** 6 + 1 + 10 or 11 on the first arch.
- **Fins:** Dorsal fin count is 11,9. Anal fin is iii,14. Accessory caudal rays number 10 to 13 above and below. The caudal fin is deeply forked.
- **Scales:** Cycloid, with 32 scales in the lateral series. The anal fin sheath extends to about the sixth branched ray.
- **Pseudotympanum:** Present but very faint.

**Coloration in alcohol:** Light tan ground color with no dorsal, anal, or humeral spots. The main marking is a small, deep-lying dark spot at the base of each caudal lobe. Melanophores line the anal fin rays, and scattered pigment appears on the dorsal scales and snout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The type locality is a rapids zone on the Rio Negro, downstream of São Gabriel. Bohlke quotes [[Rice]] (1918), who describes Camandos as a section of "long and dangerous rapids" with violent whirlpools and cross-currents over broad granite reefs. The document contains no data on water chemistry, population abundance, diet, reproduction, or associated fauna. The habitat description is limited to the historical account of the locality.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** [[Hemigrammus Mimus]] is distinguished from its closest relative, [[Hemigrammus Cylindricus]] (Durbin 1909), by several characters:
  - Premaxillary teeth: a single outer tricuspid tooth and 10 or 11 inner teeth in *mimus*, versus 3 or 4 outer and 6 inner in *cylindricus*.
  - Anal rays: iii,14 in *mimus*, versus 17 to 20 in *cylindricus*.
  - Humeral spot: present in *cylindricus* and absent in *mimus*.
  - Body compression: *mimus* is less compressed.
  - Mouth and maxillary: *mimus* has a smaller mouth and a shorter, broader maxillary.
- **Shared traits:** Both species have tricuspid teeth, similar body shape, large eyes, and a dark spot at the base of each caudal lobe.
- **Caudal scalation debate:** [[Caudal Fin Scalation]] has been used to separate the two genera. Ellis, working for Durbin, assigned intermediate species to [[Hyphessobrycon]] when enlarged basal caudal scales were present. By that rule, *mimus* would fall in Hyphessobrycon. Bohlke instead places it in Hemigrammus on grounds of closest apparent relationship, while acknowledging that the classification is artificial.
- **Systematic conclusion:** Hemigrammus and Hyphessobrycon are described as unnatural, polyphyletic assemblages. Hyphessobrycon stictus from British Guiana shares the same caudal condition. The author calls for a broader revision covering all tetragonopterine and cheirodontine genera.
- **Future work:** Bohlke plans a separate paper on caudal scalation in characins, arguing that scaled and unscaled caudal fins evolved several times independently.
