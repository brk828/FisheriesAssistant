# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Executive Review Committee Meeting Notes - June 15, 1989

#river-ecology #dam-impacts #environmental-policy #Colorado-River #flow-management

## Executive Summary

This document records the meeting notes from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) Executive Review Committee (ERC) meeting held June 15, 1989, in Flagstaff, Arizona. The ERC, composed of representatives from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], [[National Park Service]], and Department of Interior, convened to discuss Phase II study design, operational constraints at [[Glen Canyon Dam]], experimental flow requirements, and the timing of [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) compliance. The meeting revealed significant tension between scientific needs for controlled experimental flows to understand dam impacts on the downstream riverine environment and operational/economic constraints imposed by power generation commitments. Scientists requested specific flow regimes (including steady flows at 1,000-5,000 cfs) to establish baseline data and test hypotheses, while power administrators emphasized the cost ($100,000+ per day for replacement power) and legal constraints of deviating from "normal operations." The ERC committed to reviewing scientifically justified flow requests but declined to treat the dam as an experimental tool without compelling evidence of necessity.

## Background & Description

The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program was initiated to assess environmental impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on the downstream [[Colorado River]] ecosystem through [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. Phase I studies had been completed, and the ERC was established to provide oversight representing federal agency interests, review scientific findings, and make recommendations to the Secretary of the Interior. The committee's stated goal was achieving 75% confidence level in understanding dam operation effects on the riverine environment, though members acknowledged certain aspects might not reach this threshold.

The meeting occurred during a transition period following the Bush administration's inauguration, with key Department of Interior positions unfilled. Legal complications included ongoing litigation by [[Utah Power and Light]] and [[National Wildlife Federation]] against [[Western Area Power Administration]] regarding NEPA compliance in post-1989 power marketing plans. The Secretary's directive instructed data collection under "normal operational conditions" rather than using the dam as an experimental device, though one concession—5,000 cfs flows in October—had been granted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] represents a highly managed dynamic ecosystem where dam operations control flow regimes, sediment transport, water temperature, and habitat availability. Scientists emphasized that natural stream systems are inherently dynamic, and the regulated system created novel challenges for understanding cause-effect relationships between flow patterns and ecological responses.

Key ecological concerns raised included:

- **Flow regime impacts**: Daily fluctuations (peaking power operations) versus steady flows affect aquatic habitat, riparian vegetation, sediment dynamics, and recreational resources
- **Sediment deficit**: The dam traps sediment essential for beach formation, backwater habitats, and ecosystem function downstream
- **Minimum flow effects**: Understanding impacts of 5,000 cfs releases versus higher flows on fish populations, invertebrates, and riparian communities
- **Trout fishery**: A non-native coldwater fishery established below the dam, absent before dam construction
- **Temporal dynamics**: Scientists noted that 3-4 years might be insufficient to capture natural variability and long-term trends in ecosystem responses

The study area extended through [[Grand Canyon National Park]], though scientists indicated geographical constraints might need expansion to understand system-wide dynamics. Resources under consideration included fish populations (managed by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), riparian vegetation, recreational values, sediment transport, and water quality.

## Key Findings

**Operational Constraints and Scientific Needs:**

- Normal dam operations are defined by multiple factors: monthly release commitments to Lower Basin (500,000 to 1.7 million acre-feet), load-following on daily/weekly/seasonal basis, minimum flow criteria (winter/summer), and hydrological conditions
- Scientists requested controlled steady flows at specific levels (1,000 cfs, 4,000 cfs, 5,000 cfs) during off-peak periods (weekends, holidays, 8-hour off-peak daily periods) to establish baseline data
- [[Western Area Power Administration]] indicated flexibility during off-peak periods depending on season and monthly volume release requirements, but peak-period experimental flows would require replacement power at $100,000+ per day
- Scientists argued that 4 days of controlled flows could provide data equivalent to 3 years of observations under fluctuating regimes

**Institutional and Legal Framework:**

- Disagreement exists within the ERC regarding timing and process for NEPA compliance; [[Bureau of Reclamation]] position is that no action is being proposed yet, so EIS scoping is premature
- The ERC committed to presenting flow requirements, study processes, and NEPA questions to new DOI leadership once appointed
- Legal constraints from the "Law of the River" ([[Colorado River Compact]] and related legislation) must be considered, but the ERC acknowledged need for solicitor clarification on boundaries
- Power customers fund studies through [[Western Area Power Administration]]; discussion of user fees from recreational or other beneficiaries was raised but not resolved

**Study Design and Timeline:**

- An integration trip scheduled for July 1989 would produce framework for integrated research plan and establish scientific effort needed to reach 75% confidence level
- FY90 program deliberately left undefined to avoid constraining the senior scientist's approach
- Scientists emphasized need for advance notice of flow releases (improved over Phase I communication problems) and flexibility to extend geographical study boundaries
- The ERC requested identification of: (1) what can be determined in 2 years versus 4 years, (2) data gaps that cannot be filled without experimental flows, (3) flow-related relationships for all parameters to predict impacts of operational changes

**Stakeholder Concerns:**

- Public frustration expressed over study duration and delays in developing management plan; calls for immediate EIS initiation
- Power customers (represented by [[Colorado River Energy Distributors Association]]) opposed extending studies and continually paying costs, but preferred thorough process over "quick fix"
- Environmental groups ([[Environmental Defense Fund]], [[Grand Canyon Trust]], [[Arizona Wildlife Federation]]) advocated for immediate NEPA process and questioned legitimacy of studies without EIS framework
- Recreational interests emphasized [[Grand Canyon]] as nationally significant resource requiring protection

**Decision Framework:**

- The ERC's mandate is to identify effects of dam operations on downstream environment, quantify adverse effects if present, and suggest remedies
- Operational scenarios developed by technical teams will either become EIS alternatives or issues addressed through the study process
- If changes to operations are recommended and cannot be accommodated through normal Congressional consultation process for [[Colorado River]] operations, NEPA public involvement process would be initiated
- Scientists expected to identify unacceptable releases that put resources at risk, though timeframe acknowledged as too short to answer all questions
