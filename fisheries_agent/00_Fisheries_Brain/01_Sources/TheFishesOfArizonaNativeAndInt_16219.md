# Identification Key to the Fishes of Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Actinopterygii

## Executive Summary

This document is a dichotomous identification key and glossary for the fishes of [[Arizona Fish Fauna|Arizona]], written by Charles H. Lowe Jr. of the Department of Zoology, University of Arizona. It is a revision and extension of the earlier mimeographed keys by Miller (1949) and Lowe (1955). The key moves from family-level separation, through genus and species keys, to subspecies, and it includes a summary table of representative fishes from each family. Most of the taxa covered are freshwater, though a few marine-associated families (such as [[Mugilidae]] and [[Serranidae]]) are included. The document contains no population, ecological, or survey data; its content is taxonomic and diagnostic.

## Background & Description

The key is organized as a series of paired couplets. Each couplet presents two contrasting character states (for example, presence or absence of a barbel, number of anal fin spines, scale counts along the [[Lateral Line]], or pharyngeal tooth formulae), and the user follows the couplet that matches the specimen until a name is reached. Characters used include:

- **Fin structure:** presence of an adipose fin, spines versus soft rays in the dorsal, anal, and pelvic fins, and fin ray counts.
- **Body covering:** scale presence or absence, scale counts in the lateral line, and the form of predorsal scales.
- **Head and mouth:** mouth position (terminal, subterminal, or inferior), lip texture, barbels, the subopercle shape, and the presence of a horny sheath inside the lower lip.
- **Internal structures:** pharyngeal teeth (number of rows and tooth formula), vomer shape, and intestinal length.
- **Coloration:** spot patterns, belly color in life, and fin borders.

The key begins by separating families (for example, adipose fin present or absent, pelvic fin position, and presence of a sharp pelvic spine). It then proceeds through genera and species. For selected groups, the document adds a glossary of morphological terms, which the title refers to. Because the key depends on counts and proportions (such as the ratio of caudal peduncle depth to eye diameter), it is best applied to adult specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document contains no habitat, population, or water-chemistry data, but it gives some distribution notes for selected taxa:

- The Yellowstone cutthroat subspecies (*Salmo clarkii lewisi*) is noted as occurring in the upper tributaries of the Little Colorado River on the north slope of the White Mountains. The key states that this subspecies is now believed to be extinct in Arizona.
- The Arizona native trout (*Salmo gilae*) and the [[Narrow-headed Gila Trout]] are noted as occurring together in the headwaters of the Gila River in eastern Arizona.
- The [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*) is restricted to one tributary, Sycamore Canyon Creek in Santa Cruz County, a tributary of the Rio Altar.
- The [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) is restricted to tributaries of the Rio San Bernardino in Cochise County.
- The [[Virgin River Chub]] and the [[Virgin River Mountain Sucker]] are associated with the Virgin River system.

Several species in the key are native to the Colorado River basin and are now uncommon or of conservation concern, including the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Spikedace]]. Others are introduced, such as the [[Common Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] (both from Eurasia), and [[Western Mosquitofish]] and [[Largemouth Bass]]. The document identifies the introduced status for the carp and goldfish but does not discuss their ecological effects.

## Key Findings

- **Family and genus separations:** The key separates Salmonidae into trouts (*Salvelinus*, *Salmo*) and places the brook trout (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) apart from *Salmo* by its vomer and the presence of red or yellow spots.
- **Native trout identification:** Cutthroat trout are identified by red or orange marks on the throat, and the brown trout (*S. trutta*) is separated from rainbow trout (*S. gairdneri*) by spot size, the presence or absence of a light margin on the dorsal fin, and the number of dorsal soft rays (12 in rainbow trout).
- **Sucker subspecies:** The genus *Pantosteus* (mountain suckers) includes at least two Arizona taxa separated by predorsal scale size and lateral line scale counts. The genus *Catostomus* is split into four or more species by caudal peduncle shape, scale counts, and dorsal fin ray numbers.
- **Chub diversity:** The genus *Gila* (chubs) is the most detailed group in the key. Species and subspecies are distinguished by fin ray counts, lateral line scale counts (ranging from 51 to 96 among taxa), and the ratio of peduncle depth to head length. The key also includes *Gila robusta* subspecies (*intermedia*, *elegans*, *seminuda*) with different scale patterns.
- **Catfish families:** The Ameiuridae are separated into *Ictalurus* (deeply to slightly forked caudal fin) and *Ameiurus* (weakly emarginate to rounded caudal fin). Species are distinguished by anal fin ray counts, anal fin shape, and the presence of spots.
- **Topminnows and mosquitofish:** The Poeciliidae are separated by dorsal fin ray count, the presence of a dark lateral streak, and tooth shape. *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* (Gila topminnow) and *Gambusia affinis* (western mosquitofish) are distinguished by those features.
- **Sunfish identification:** The Centrarchidae are separated by anal fin spine number, opercle shape, and pectoral fin length. The bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*) is separated from its southwestern subspecies by anal fin soft ray number (10 to 12 versus 9 to 11). The crappies (*Pomoxis annularis* and *P. nigromaculatus*) are separated by dorsal fin spine number and spine length relative to fin base.
- **Cyprinid diversity:** The key includes a large group of minnows (genera including *Notropis*, *Rhinichthys*, *Agosia*, *Meda*, *Lepidomeda*, *Plagopterus*, *Tiaroga*, *Campostoma*, and *Ptychocheilus*). Among these, the [[Plains Red Shiner]] and [[Vaquí Shiner]] are separated by scale counts and the presence of red coloration in breeding males.
- **Scale of the document:** The key covers dozens of taxa across 12 families. The most conservation-relevant taxa, including Colorado River natives and endemic Gila chubs, are keyed with the most detailed characters.
