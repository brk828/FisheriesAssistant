# Native Fish Handling Protocol Memorandum (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1991 internal memorandum from the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office]] (Pinetop, AZ) to the Regional Director in Albuquerque, NM, sets out the author's views on how [[Native Fish Handling Protocol|native fishes]] should be managed during surveys, collections, and hatchery work. The author argues that the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) should be involved in all fish surveys because it holds legal responsibility for the species. The memo calls for early notice to all parties, study-based collection permits, defined sampling limits, approved chemical protocols, and hatchery changes that favor [[Endangered Species|endangered]] [[Colorado River Fishes]]. It ends with a sharp criticism of a hatchery staff comment, which the author considers unacceptable.

## Background & Description

The memo is a policy and operational document, not a research paper. It contains no population data, sample sizes, or species-level measurements. Its subject is the handling of native fishes, with particular attention to [[Colorado River Fishes]] and to suckers collected during roundups.

The author writes from a field-office perspective and lists five numbered concerns, followed by a closing note on hatchery staff attitudes. The memo was copied to the Parker FRO (AZ) and the Denver FRO (NM).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses the handling of wild fish, not their ecology. Its main environmental points are:

- **Sampling stress and survival:** Seines should be checked frequently and should not be left in place for more than 4 or 5 hours. Traps may be set for up to 12 hours. Fish caught during roundups, especially suckers, should be held for observation to check for latent mortality, either in live cars or at a hatchery or station.
- **Disease risk:** The author asks whether every collected fish should receive prophylactic treatment, whether or not it is released immediately. The concern is the spread of disease from handled fish.
- **Chemical use:** Field and hatchery personnel need approved chemicals and clear instructions on how to use them.
- **Hatchery design:** Hatchery facilities should be modified to be more compatible with endangered fishes. Suggested changes include settling basins and a set of raceways that can hold and rear Colorado River fishes.

The memo does not address electrofishing, although the author notes that no one has covered it.

## Key Findings

1. **FWS involvement in all surveys.** FWS holds responsibility for the species but often does not know what is happening with the fish. The author argues that FWS should take part in planning and follow-up for all types of surveys, even when states and universities have qualified staff.

2. **Early notice to all parties.** States, universities, hatcheries, FWS, other federal agencies, and Indian tribes that work with native fishes must be notified of sampling and research well in advance. The protocol may need to set a communication format that is acceptable to all parties.

3. **Study-based collection permits.** The author has asked, and will continue to ask, that collection permits be issued for studies for which the Fishery Resources Office holds lead responsibility. The protocol being drafted should include this requirement. States and the Regional Office will be informed of upcoming surveys and studies.

4. **Sampling standards.**
   - Seines should be checked frequently and not left in place for more than 4 or 5 hours.
   - Traps may be set for up to 12 hours.
   - Suckers collected in roundups should be held to check for latent mortality.

5. **Chemical approval and disease control.** The memo asks which chemicals are approved for field and hatchery use, and whether every collected fish should receive prophylactic treatment. Field and hatchery staff need approved chemicals and training in their use.

6. **Hatchery modifications.** Hatchery facilities should be adapted for endangered species, including settling basins and a set of raceways for holding and rearing [[Colorado River Fishes]].

7. **Staff attitudes.** The author calls a comment from hatchery staff (attributed to Lyle) unacceptable. The author also states that this staff member has shown more intolerance for native fish than the rest of the crew.
