# Endangered and Threatened Species: Interagency Cooperation (Section 7 Consultation) Regulations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory package rather than a fish study. It contains the August 4, 1995 *Federal Register* proposal (60 FR 39923) by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NMFS) to adopt joint counterpart regulations under [[Section 7 Consultation]] of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). The rules would apply to the [[Forest Service]] (FS) and the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM). It also includes a 24 August 1995 memo from the FWS Arizona Ecological Services office to the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], with a plain-language summary of the proposal. The memo's critical assessment is that the proposal contains major changes and significant loopholes that could weaken meaningful consultation. Comments were due October 3, 1995.

## Background & Description

The proposal would supplement the general consultation rules in [[50 CFR Part 402]] (subparts A and B). It would create a new subpart C that uses an alternative, earlier, and more flexible framework for FS and BLM actions. Its main elements are:

- **Two consultation levels:** [[Non-Site-Specific Consultation]] at the program or plan level, and [[Site-Specific Consultation]] at the project level.
- **Consultation/Conference Agreements:** Agencies would sign these to set the process, species list, geographic scope, schedules, and applicant roles for each case.
- **Triggering events:** Consultation agreements would be required when (1) a new species is proposed or listed, (2) critical habitat is proposed or designated, (3) a land management plan revision or amendment is formally announced, or (4) FS, BLM, or a Service requests consultation.
- **Parameters:** Non-site-specific consultation would identify standards and guidelines. Site-specific actions that conform to them would not require further project-level analysis, and the agency would only notify the Service.
- **Ongoing actions:** The rule would define how existing actions continue during consultation. Actions with an [[Imminent Threat]] to listed species would be reviewed and possibly halted, and actions "likely to adversely affect" species would be reviewed under the same framework.

The authors were Jay Slack (FWS), Jim Hoff (BLM), Hazy Joregron (FS), and Rob Ziobro (NMFS). The rule was framed as a response to a gap in existing regulations on how ongoing actions may continue while consultation proceeds on newly listed species or critical habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address fish biology, population data, or water quality. Its ecological relevance comes from its link to federally listed and proposed species managed on public lands, and from the desert fishes context of the recovery team memo. The document makes several points about environmental uncertainty:

- [[Land and Resource Management Plan]]s (FS) and [[Resource Management Plan]]s (BLM) set general land use allocations, desired future conditions, goals, and standards. They do not normally prescribe specific activities, which creates "a significant level of uncertainty" about environmental consequences.
- Plans operate for several years, so new species listings or new scientific information could require reevaluation of plan effects.
- Consultation on plans would rely on parameters that might not reflect site-specific impacts. The memo states that analysis of [[Incidental Take]], indirect effects, [[Cumulative Effects]], and the [[Environmental Baseline]] would apparently be left to agency discretion.

## Key Findings

- **Scope of change:** The proposal would shift some authority to make findings of "not likely to adversely affect," [[Jeopardy Determination]], and "adverse modification" from FWS/NMFS to FS and BLM. It would also replace the standard [[Biological Opinion]] with a "written statement" for non-site-specific consultation. The memo notes that no criteria are given for that statement's contents.
- **Flexibility and discretion:** Non-site-specific consultation is discretionary. The memo states that the rules' use of terms such as "consultation" and "conferencing" differs from their use in the existing regulations.
- **Human resource considerations:** Consultation timing may account for "human resource considerations," which the memo flags as a concern.
- **Ongoing actions:** Actions identified as ongoing could continue during consultation without interruption unless suspended. The memo notes that the requirement for reinitiation of consultation under the 1986 regulations was not included, and that FWS may request reinitiation only if it disagrees with an FS or BLM notification. Recommended changes included suspending actions that would foreclose [[Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives]], but the proposal leaves this to agency judgment. "Imminence" of threats is not defined.
- **Timeframes:** The proposal sets a 45-day window to complete consultation/conference agreements and a 135-day window for Service written statements after receipt of agency information. Timeframes may be modified by mutual agreement.
- **Economic and regulatory findings:** FWS certified no significant economic effect on small entities under the Regulatory Flexibility Act. It stated no new recordkeeping or information collection requirements beyond existing rules. It also concluded that no federalism or takings assessment was required.
- **Memo conclusion:** The FWS memo describes the proposal as "difficult to read and not particularly coherent," with inconsistencies between the Background section and the regulation text. It recommends that recovery team members comment individually or through the team, with comments due to the Regional Director before the October 3, 1995 deadline.
