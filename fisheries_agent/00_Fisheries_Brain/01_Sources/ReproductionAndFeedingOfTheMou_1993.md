## Executive Summary

This study investigated the reproductive biology and feeding ecology of the [[Mountain Mullet]] (*Agonostomus monticola*), a freshwater member of the family [[Mugilidae]], in streams of northeastern Trinidad. Based on macroscopic and microscopic examination of 250 fish collected from five rivers, the study identified a single, distinct reproductive season occurring from June to October during the wet season, with evidence supporting a single spawning event per individual per season. A strongly skewed sex ratio favoring females (20.4:1) was observed, and mean size at maturity for females was 135 mm fork length (FL). Fecundity scaled with body size, ranging up to over 1.4 million eggs. Dietary analysis revealed *A. monticola* to be omnivorous, with insects, prawns, fruits, and algae dominating stomach contents, and clear seasonal shifts in diet composition linked to food availability. The findings support the hypothesis of [[Catadromy]] in this species, with spawning likely occurring in the sea followed by return of spent adults to freshwater streams.

## Background & Description

- **Size range collected:** 46–320 mm fork length (FL); modal lengths of 75 mm and 195 mm FL.
- **Juveniles:** Defined as <120 mm FL (N = 55); caught from January to May; first appearance in rivers at a minimum size of 46 mm FL, approximately three months after peak spawning.
- **Gonad developmental staging** (following Abraham et al. 1966), five stages defined:
  - **Stage I – Virgin:** Small, slender, wedge-shaped, laterally flattened, colourless gonads; Stage A and B oocytes only.
  - **Stage II – Resting:** Gonad 0.2–0.6× body cavity length, soft, translucent, peach-coloured; oocytes up to Stage C; includes recovering spents.
  - **Stage III – Maturing/Developing:** Gonads larger, oocytes yellow and visible through ovary wall; wall may appear reddish (vascularization); oocytes up to Stage E.
  - **Stage IV – Ripe:** Gonads firm, reaching maximum size (up to 0.98× body cavity length), often displacing the stomach; abdomen rounded.
  - **Stage V – Spent:** Ovaries wrinkled, shrunken, empty, flaccid; abdomen flattens; genital papilla area swollen and bloodshot.
- **Gut morphology:** Relatively unspecialized, consistent with a euryphagous (broad, omnivorous) feeding strategy capable of processing a diverse diet.
- **Oocyte development:** Group-synchronous, with two distinct size cohorts (pre-vitellogenic and vitellogenic) present simultaneously in the ovary during the spawning season (measured via oocyte diameter frequency distributions, N = 250 per stage).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Clear mountain streams with strong currents in the Caribbean and Central America; in Trinidad restricted to streams along the north and northeastern coasts.
- **Study sites:**
  - **Tompire River** (main sampling site): 16 km long, mean width 15 m, max depth 4 m; grassy banks, stone-to-mud substrate, seasonal filamentous algal blooms in dry season; current 0.04–0.55 m/s (wet season), 0.08–0.88 m/s (dry season); surface temperature 26–28.5°C.
  - **Shark River** (feeding observation site): 6.9 km long, mean width 20 m, max depth 9 m; rocky bed with pools/riffles, heavy sedimentation, encrusting algae on rocks.
- **Life history strategy:** Likely [[Catadromy|catadromous]] — indirect evidence (absence of ripe-running females in rivers, presence of spent individuals, larvae found offshore) suggests spawning occurs in the sea, with adults returning to freshwater post-spawning. This pattern aligns with prior work by Anderson (1957) documenting larval *A. monticola* in offshore Bahamian waters.
- **Trophic role:** Omnivorous generalist feeder (euryphagous), consuming insects, prawns, fruits, algae, and lesser amounts of sediment, molluscs, terrestrial plant fragments, crabs, and fish. This dietary breadth may reduce intraspecific competition and support the species across its range.
- **Reproductive synchrony with rainfall:** Spawning season (June–October) coincides with the wet season; peak spawning (October) coincides with peak rainfall, paralleling patterns seen in other tropical fishes and in Honduran *A. monticola* populations (Cruz 1987).
- **Comparative fishery context:** Supports a small commercial and recreational fishery in Trinidad; considered to have aquaculture potential (Price 1955); taken mainly via subsistence fisheries elsewhere in its range (Thomson 1978).

## Key Findings

- **Sex ratio:** Females significantly outnumbered males, at a ratio of **20.4:1** (based on 193 histologically examined gonads) — a far greater skew than reported by Cruz (1987) in Honduras.
- **Size at maturity:** Mean size at maturity for females was **135 mm FL**; percentage mature rose from 0% below 130 mm to 100% above 179 mm FL.
- **Reproductive season:** Single, distinct spawning season from **June to October** during the wet season; no evidence of multiple spawning events per individual within a season (group-synchronous oocyte development).
  - June: 64% of gonads developing.
  - August: ripe gonads begin appearing.
  - September: ripe gonads peak at 65%; GSI peaks at ~17%.
  - October: spent ovaries peak at 37%; GSI drops to ~11%; peak spawning coincides with highest rainfall.
  - November–May: all ovaries in resting state; GSI low (0.50–0.63%).
- **Fecundity:** Ranged from **273,199 eggs** (6.54 g ovary, 156 mm FL fish) to **1,414,800 eggs** (69.75 g ovary, 252 mm FL fish).
  - Fecundity–length relationship: **F = 167.3 FL^2.7** (r = 0.85, N = 17)
  - Fecundity–weight relationship: **F = 16,430.7·G + 235,079** (r = 0.89, N = 17)
  - These fecundity estimates are substantially higher than Cruz's (1987) Honduran estimate of ~300,000 eggs in a 75 g ovary, but comparable to other [[Mugilidae]] such as [[Mugil Cephalus]] and [[Liza Ramada]].
- **Diet composition** (from 155 stomachs; 17 empty, 11%):
  - Dominant food groups (decreasing importance): **insects > prawns > fruits > algae**.
  - Animal material significantly more prevalent than plant material (χ² = 7.2, 0.001 < p < 0.01).
  - Total occurrences: Insects (70), Fruits (44), Prawns (46), Algae (42), Insect larvae (10), Sediment (8), Molluscs (4), Land plants (4), Crabs (2), Fish (1).
  - **Seasonal dietary shifts:**
    - Algae significantly more frequent in dry season (χ² = 35, p < 0.001).
    - Insects and prawns significantly more frequent in wet season (χ² = 8.3, 0.001 < p < 0.01; χ² = 5.6, 0.01 < p < 0.05, respectively).
    - Sediment, molluscs, terrestrial plant fragments, crabs, and fish only appeared in wet season diets.
- **Feeding classification:** Unlike Cruz's (1987) classification of Honduran *A. monticola* as insectivorous, this Trinidad population is best classified as **omnivorous/euryphagous**, as no single food category dominated sufficiently to justify a narrower classification (contrasts with herbivorous classifications given for other mugilids like [[Mugil Cephalus]] and [[Joturus Pichardi]]).
- **Catadromy inference:** Absence of ripe-running females, presence of spent fish in rivers, and prior documentation of offshore larvae collectively support marine spawning followed by return of adults to freshwater — consistent with findings from Costa Rica, Puerto Rico, and Honduras populations.
- **Juvenile recruitment:** Juveniles first appear in rivers at ~46 mm FL, about three months post-peak-spawning, coinciding with the dry season when low river discharge facilitates upstream movement — consistent with Anderson (1957) and Cruz (1987).
