# Biology: A Review of the 1966 Literature on Wastewater and Water Pollution Control

#ichthyology #marine-biology #water-pollution #ecotoxicology #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary
This is the annual "Biology" review for the Water Pollution Control Federation, covering the 1966 literature. It summarizes about 324 cited papers plus 50 additional references. Authors are Katz, Sparks and Oglesby, with contributors from the University of Washington College of Fisheries. The review spans surveys and indicator organisms, domestic, industrial and agricultural wastes, detergents, metals, pesticides, temperature, fish physiology, algae, methodology, and estuarine and marine pollution. It also carries an errata sheet of minor corrections. The main theme is that responses to pollutants differ by species, life stage and water chemistry, so standardized bioassays, community surveys and physiological endpoints are all needed.

## Background & Description
The review is a compilation, not a single study. Its sections are:
- Surveys and investigations
- Water quality and biological indicators
- Domestic wastes
- Detergents
- Industrial wastes (oil, phenols, cyanide, mining, pulping)
- Metals
- Pesticides
- Herbicides and miscellaneous toxicants
- Temperature
- Physiology and pathology
- Algae
- Methodology
- Estuarine and marine pollution

Taxa covered include fishes (salmonids, cyprinids, centrarchids, poeciliids, sturgeon), benthic insects, oligochaetes, protozoa, rotifers, plankton, molluscs, crustaceans, algae and macrophytes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Biological surveys compare polluted and unpolluted stream reaches. [[Saprobic System]] classification, [[Biotic Index]] methods and [[Macroinvertebrate Indicators]] are widely used.
- Estuaries are naturally variable, so the central problem is separating man-made effects from natural but abnormal variation.
- Pollutant sources include sewage, [[Oil Pollution]], pulp and paper mills, mining, agriculture ([[Pesticides]] and herbicides), [[Thermal Pollution]] and sedimentation.
- Study regions include the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Estuary]], [[Lake Erie]], [[Miramichi River]], [[Matsushima Bay]], the [[Gulf of Mexico]], Californian coastal waters and British rivers.

## Key Findings
**Surveys and indicators**
- [[Lake Erie]] benthos at 40 stations in 1961 versus 1930 showed a ninefold increase in Oligochaeta, a fourfold increase in Tendipedidae, a twofold increase in Sphaeriidae and a sixfold increase in Gastropoda. Hexagenia fell to under 1 percent of former abundance. The polluted area grew from 263 km² to 1,020 km².
- Sladecek's guide covers 830 limnosaprobic organisms (64 bacteria, 434 plants, 332 animals).
- Cairns analyzed protozoan communities from 202 areas.
- Oligochaetes (especially tubificids), caddisflies and midges were evaluated as indicators. Hynes advocated macroinvertebrate surveys for detecting mild or past pollution.
- The FAO report on suspended solids concluded that below 25 mg/l there are no harmful effects, 25–80 mg/l allows good to moderate fisheries, and 80–400 mg/l usually gives poor fisheries.

**Oxygen, pH and tolerance**
- Sturgeon eggs need flowing, oxygen-saturated water, with a minimum usually 5.5 mg/l.
- Juvenile carp growth declined below 3 cc/l oxygen.
- Crenichthys baileyi persisted at 31.3°C with 1.6 mg/l DO.
- Oyster embryos developed normally at pH 6.75–8.75 and clam embryos at pH 7.00–8.75.
- Cairns et al. found that relative sensitivity to chemicals differed between zebra danios and [[Bluegill]] and between life stages.

**Detergents**
- Thatcher tested ABS on 11 fish species. The 96-hr TL50 was 7.4–11.3 mg/l for 8 species. Common shiner (17.0), carp (18.0) and black bullhead (22.0 mg/l) were more tolerant.
- LAS was more than twice as toxic as ABS to fathead minnow eggs in 9-day tests.
- 1 mg/l tetrapropylenebenzenesulphonate impaired guppy growth after six weeks.

**Industrial wastes**
- Oil: a refinery study found species reduction once organic carbon exceeded 4.5 percent in benthos and 8 percent in sediment bottles. Microbial oxidation of sea oil may reach 100–960 mg/m³/day.
- Phenol: 17 mg/l killed 37 percent of fish in 12 hr, and 50 mg/l killed all in 15 min. The lethal level for Baltic salmon larvae was 1 mg/l.
- Cyanide caused a fish kill along 55 km of the Jihlava River at concentrations over 0.14 mg/l. Molecular HCN drives acute toxicity.
- Pulp fibre: trout and walleye eggs survived 250 mg/l of fibre, but trout alevins could not. Kraft bleach waste inhibited sockeye alevin growth.

**Metals**
- For young [[Atlantic Salmon]], incipient lethal levels were 48 µg/l Cu or 600 µg/l Zn. Fish avoided concentrations below one-tenth of those levels, and the metals were at least additive.
- Copper was most toxic in soft water, and hexavalent chromium was least toxic of the metals tested on four fish species.
- Taiwanese oysters accumulated copper to 1180 mg/kg, against 5.6 mg/kg in normal oysters.

**Pesticides**
- DDT in Sebago Lake landlocked salmon was 1.2 mg/kg (age III), 8.0 (age IV) and 8.8 (age V).
- Fish populations from contaminated bayous and ditches developed resistance. Yellow bullhead were 60 times as resistant to endrin, and other species were 3–5 times as tolerant of dursban.
- Toxaphene at 0.001 mg/l gave a complete lamprey kill in 14 days. Mountain lakes treated with toxaphene could not be restocked for up to 6 years, whereas shallow lakes could be restocked within one year.
- DDT at 0.001 mg/l reduced oyster growth. Blue crab survival time dropped at 0.5 ppb DDT.
- Rotenone was toxic to polychaetes at 0.025 mg/l in Lake Merced.

**Temperature**
- A heated estuarine effluent (maximum +8°C) altered primary productivity and benthos over 300–400 m.
- Alabaster and Downing concluded that fish kills from thermal pollution in Britain were unlikely because fish avoid warm surface layers.
- The upper lethal temperature for Windermere char eggs is 8–12°C, which may limit the species' distribution.
- Winter mass mortalities of Tilapia in Transvaal dams were due to low temperature rather than pollution.

**Estuarine and marine**
- Matsushima Bay oyster mortality was 62.5, 41.6, 42.3 and 51.9 percent in 1961–1964. It was linked to gonadal over-maturation and organic enrichment, with little evidence of bacterial infection.
- Kelp-bed decline off California was not due to toxicity. Sea urchin grazing was detrimental, and quicklime controlled the urchins.
- Sewage-affected coastal productivity was 9.9 metric tons C/day in spring–summer versus 4.6 in fall–winter over 60.2 km².
- Fall-chinook egg mortality reached 85 percent when 15–30 percent of gravel voids were filled with sediment.

**Methods**
- Notable developments were the serial-dilution apparatus (Mount and Warner), a fish respirometer, continuous-flow and static [[Bioassay]] systems, a floating estuarine laboratory, enzymic dechorionation of medaka eggs, and an oyster shell-deposition assay for toxicants.

**Overall conclusion:** pollutant effects vary with species, life stage, hardness, pH, temperature and exposure time. Sublethal and physiological endpoints, together with community-level indicators, are emphasized.
