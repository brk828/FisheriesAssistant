# USFWS Consultation on Fort Apache Road Construction Project FAIR 42(3)

#ichthyology #conservation #threatened-species #Salmonidae #Cyprinidae #riparian-ecology

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (July 22, 1994) provides formal consultation findings for road construction project FAIR 42(3) on the Fort Apache Indian Reservation, Arizona. The project involves paving 0.67 miles of road, constructing a bridge over the [[East Fork White River]], and closing an existing vehicle wet-crossing. USFWS concurs with Bureau of Indian Affairs findings that the project will not affect threatened [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), or proposed endangered [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), contingent upon implementation of fifteen protective conditions.

## Background & Description

The project encompasses three areas on the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]]: routes 1, 7, and 42. Routes 1 and 7 involve constructing short access roads in upland areas. Route 42 work includes:
- Paving 0.67 miles of existing road
- Constructing a bridge crossing over the [[East Fork White River]]
- Closing and revegetating the existing unimproved vehicle wet-crossing

The consultation addresses protection of three federally listed species: threatened [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and proposed endangered [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*). Additional information was provided by Bureau of Indian Affairs staff in a meeting on July 15, 1994, following an initial information request dated June 16, 1994.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[East Fork White River]] provides habitat for two threatened fish species and potential habitat for a proposed endangered riparian bird species. The project area includes the 100-year floodplain (below elevation 4,997 feet) and riparian vegetation critical to these species' survival.

The existing unimproved wet-crossing represents ongoing disturbance to the stream channel and aquatic habitat. Bridge construction offers opportunity to eliminate this direct stream impact while maintaining access. Protection of riparian vegetation and streambank integrity is essential for maintaining habitat quality for both aquatic and riparian-dependent species.

## Key Findings

USFWS concurrence with "no effect" determination is contingent upon fifteen mandatory conditions:

**Stream Channel Protection:**
1. No structures of any kind shall be placed in the wetted portion of the river channel
2. No machinery or vehicles shall be operated within the wetted river channel; no construction or support activity in wetted channel; no temporary stream crossing constructed
3. No water shall be removed from the [[East Fork White River]] for project use; no water impoundment

**Floodplain and Riparian Protection:**
4. Construction activities and staging shall disturb no more than 300 linear feet (200 feet left, 100 feet right of roadway centerline) of 100-year floodplain
5. All reasonable efforts to minimize soil and vegetation disturbance within 100-year floodplain
6. No riparian vegetation removal outside the 300 linear feet floodplain area
7. All machinery, vehicle, equipment, and supply storage/staging outside 100-year floodplain
8. No structure placement, machinery use, or construction within 50 feet of wetted stream edge (except immediate vicinity of north and middle bridge abutments and existing wet-crossing)

**Bridge Construction Specifications:**
9. During drilling and placement of middle and north bridge abutments and rail-bank protection construction, minimize streambank disturbance; no soil or vegetation disturbance between sediment catchment fences and wetted stream edge

**Wet-Crossing Closure:**
10. No heavy equipment or construction vehicles shall use existing wet-crossing
11. Construction at existing wet-crossing limited to minimum necessary for closure and revegetation
12. Closure of existing wet-crossing shall be enforced to prevent continued vehicle use

**Materials Management:**
13. Revegetation shall use native plants
14. Excavated material used for bridge approach construction or removed from floodplain; no materials disposed within 100-year floodplain except for approach fill and rail-bank protection

**Contractor Compliance:**
15. Copy of conditions furnished to contractor; written confirmation that contractor has read, understood, and agreed to comply with conditions provided to USFWS prior to initiating construction

The consultation demonstrates protective measures necessary for infrastructure development in threatened species habitat, emphasizing avoidance of direct stream impacts, minimization of floodplain disturbance, and restoration through wet-crossing elimination.
