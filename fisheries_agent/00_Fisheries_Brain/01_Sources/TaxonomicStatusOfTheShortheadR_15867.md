# Taxonomic Status of the Shorthead Redhorse in the Kansas River Basin, with Amphibian and Reptile Distributional Records from Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #moxostoma

## Executive Summary

This document combines two items from the *Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science* (Vol. 63, No. 1, 1960). The first, by [[W L Minckley]] and [[Frank B Cross]], re-examines the taxonomic status of the [[Shorthead Redhorse]] ([[Moxostoma Aureolum]]) in the [[Kansas River Basin]]. The authors find that most Kansas specimens are intermediate between the two recognized subspecies, [[Moxostoma Aureolum Aureolum]] and [[Moxostoma Aureolum Pisolabrum]], and extend the known zone of intergradation about 220 miles west. The second, by [[John M Legler]], reports noteworthy amphibian and reptile records from [[University Of Kansas Museum Of Natural History]] (KU) collections made from 1955 to 1959, including range extensions for several species.

## Background & Description

*Moxostoma aureolum* is a sucker whose subspecies were separated mainly on proportional measurements of the mouth. In *M. a. pisolabrum*, the tip of the upper lip is enlarged into a bulbous knob, conspicuous in ventral or lateral view. In *M. a. aureolum* the upper lip is nearly uniform in width (Trautman and Martin, 1951:4). The authors measured specimens by the methods of [[Trautman And Martin 1951]] and expressed results as arithmetic ratios: thickness of the upper lip in head length, and thickness of the upper lip in width of the lower lip.

Range summary given in the paper:
- *M. a. pisolabrum*: Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Arkansas, west of the [[Mississippi River]] and south of the [[Missouri River]]. Mainly Ozarkian, extending west onto the Plains in the Arkansas and Osage river systems.
- *M. a. aureolum*: Montana and eastern Wyoming east through northern Nebraska, Iowa, and Illinois to New York north of the Ohio River, and in Canada from the Northwest Territories to Quebec.
- Intergrades: mapped from streams in Missouri north of the Missouri River. The westernmost mapped intergrade locality is in the [[Chariton River]].

The authors retained the name *aureolum* despite [[Hubbs And Lagler]] (1958) rejecting it, pending a fuller treatment of the nomenclatural problem.

Specimens examined came from KU and the Kansas State University Museum (KSU). The Kansas River Basin material included collections from [[Mill Creek]], the [[Wakarusa River]], [[Rock Creek]], the [[Little Blue River]], and the [[Big Blue River]]. Paratypes of *M. a. pisolabrum* from 1911 to 1912 were also examined, along with KU material from the Neosho, South Fork of Cottonwood, Spring, and Marmaton rivers. Additional comparative material included 30 specimens from the [[Niobrara River]] (Holt County, Nebraska, 5 May 1958) representing *M. a. aureolum*.

Legler's paper covers amphibians and reptiles from northeastern and southeastern Kansas, collected largely from the [[Big Blue River]] drainage. Minckley and Dale Hoyt contributed a large collection from that drainage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Kansas River Basin specimens lie in a transition zone between the Ozarkian range of *M. a. pisolabrum* and the northern range of *M. a. aureolum*. The authors describe the area as a zone of intergradation. Most of the Kansas collections come from small to mid-sized prairie streams and tributaries, including Mill Creek, the Big Blue and Little Blue rivers, and the Neosho and Marais des Cygnes rivers.

Legler's amphibian and reptile records also come from tributary streams of the Big Blue and Little Blue rivers in northeastern Kansas and from southeastern Kansas prairie habitats, with several species at the edges of their known Kansas ranges.

## Key Findings

**Shorthead Redhorse taxonomy (Minckley and Cross)**

- Specimen-based comparison: most Kansas River Basin specimens are intermediate between *M. a. aureolum* and *M. a. pisolabrum*. The paper states that 85 per cent of the 48 Kansas River Basin specimens have ratios between the mean values for the two subspecies.
- Variability: Kansas specimens vary widely in upper lip thickness. Two fish taken together from the Big Blue River had ratios of 13.9 and 3.6 versus 18.7 and 5.2, so one could fall under either subspecies.
- Missouri River: the single specimen from the Missouri River has ratios of 19.2 and 5.5, similar to *M. a. aureolum*.
- Range extension: the zone of intergradation extends about 220 miles west of the previously known area.

Reference ratios from Trautman and Martin (1951), given as mean (range):

| Group | Upper lip in head length | Upper lip in lower lip width |
|---|---|---|
| *M. a. aureolum* | 20.0 (15.5–27.0) | 5.2 (4.2–6.9) |
| Intergrades | 17.5 (13.9–24.0) | 4.5 (3.6–6.0) |
| *M. a. pisolabrum* | 13.7 (10.5–16.3) | 3.5 (2.8–4.7) |

The authors conclude that the Kansas material is intermediate and that the subspecies boundary is a gradient rather than a sharp break. The name *aureolum* is kept pending further nomenclatural work.

**Amphibian and reptile distributional records (Legler)**

- [[Scaphiopus Bombifrons]] (plains spadefoot): a collection of 48 adult and 101 larval specimens from northeastern Kansas extends the known range northeastward. Records are given for four counties: Washington, Marshall, Riley, and Pottawatomie. Legler expects similar distribution along Kansas River tributaries, where the species has not yet been recorded.
- [[Bufo Cognatus]]: specimens from Cowley County mark the southeastern limit of the species' range in Kansas.
- [[Hyla Versicolor]] (*H. v. versicolor*): five specimens from Marshall County are the first recorded there and lie farther north and west than any other known Kansas specimens.
- [[Macroclemys Temminckii]]: a single specimen is recorded in the paper; the excerpt ends before full details.
