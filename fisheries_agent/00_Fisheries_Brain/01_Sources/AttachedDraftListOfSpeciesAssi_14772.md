## Executive Summary

This source is **not a primary scientific study** on fish morphology or ecology, but rather a **1988 administrative tracking table** produced by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Phoenix Field Office, cataloguing endangered, threatened, and candidate ("Status 2," "3C") species within its jurisdiction. It spans nearly all taxonomic groups — invertebrates, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals — with a **substantial subset of imperiled freshwater fishes** native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], [[Gila River Basin]], and [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] in Arizona and adjoining states. The document assigns each species a listing status, regional lead office (R1/R2/R6), support offices, and responsible staff biologist, rather than providing morphological or ecological descriptions. Because of this format, the "data" extractable is primarily **taxonomic identity, conservation status, and administrative/geographic assignment** rather than biometric or life-history detail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While ecological detail is minimal, the document implicitly situates each fish species within specific Southwestern desert freshwater drainages by virtue of jurisdictional assignment (e.g., Albuquerque [Alb], Dexter National Fish Hatchery [Dex], San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge [SBNWR]):

- **Colorado River Basin mainstem/tributary specialists**: [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*, implied by title context) — large-river, turbid, warm-water habitats.
- **Small-stream/spring endemics**: [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) — headwater and spring-fed perennial streams of the Gila and Little Colorado drainages.
- **Isolated desert spring/pupfish habitats**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon* sp.) — highly restricted, thermally stable spring systems prone to desiccation and invasive species pressure.
- **Rio Yaqui basin endemics** (Mexico/Arizona border drainages): [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*), [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*) — supported administratively by SBNWR (San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge).
- **Woundfin** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and **Virgin River Roundtail Chub** (*Gila robusta seminuda*) — Virgin River system, a Colorado River tributary, subject to R1/R6 (regional) co-leadership given interstate range.

These species collectively occupy niches characterized by **low ecological redundancy, high endemism, and extreme habitat fragmentation**, driving their conservation-priority listing. Co-occurring imperiled non-fish aquatic/semi-aquatic taxa in the same document (e.g., [[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]], [[Tarahumara Frog]], [[Arizona Mud Turtle]], numerous springsnails such as *Pyrgulopsis* spp.) indicate these fish inhabit spring/stream ecosystems that are simultaneously refugia for many other rare aquatic organisms, underscoring the broader desert-spring biodiversity crisis in the Southwest.

## Key Findings

- The document lists at least **20 fish species/subspecies** under USFWS Phoenix Field Office purview as of 1988, nearly all assigned **Status "2"** (then-current terminology for species under review for possible listing, i.e., candidate species), with a few coded differently.
- Listed fish taxa include members of **Cyprinidae** (chubs, shiners, spinedace, squawfish, stoneroller), **Catostomidae** (razorback sucker), **Poeciliidae** (topminnows), and **Ictaluridae** (Yaqui catfish) — reflecting the taxonomic diversity of Southwestern desert fish faunas.
- Regional lead (R2) and support offices (R1, R6, Alb, Dex, SBNWR, Phx) indicate **interstate/interagency management structures**, particularly for wide-ranging Colorado River Basin fish (Humpback Chub, Razorback Sucker, Colorado Squawfish) versus locally endemic spring fish (Monkey Springs Pupfish, Gila Chub).

- The species-lead structure (e.g., "Stefferud," "Baucom") documents **individual staff biologist accountability**, a notable administrative feature suggesting this was an internal USFWS workload/responsibility-tracking tool rather than a scientific publication.
- **Caveat**: Given OCR degradation and the tabular/administrative nature of the source, several scientific names are fragmentary or have been standardized according to current nomenclature (e.g., *Ptychocheilus lucius* for Colorado Squawfish, *Gila robusta grahami* for the Gila Roundtail Chub); these should be verified against primary sources such as the [[Endangered Species Act]] listing rules for authoritative taxonomy.
