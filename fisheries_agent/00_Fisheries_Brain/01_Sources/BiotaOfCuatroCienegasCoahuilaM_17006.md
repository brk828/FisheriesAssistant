## Executive Summary

This document is the **Preface/Prólogo** to the proceedings of a special symposium on the biota of **[[Cuatro Ciénegas]]**, Coahuila, México, held during the 14th Annual Meeting of the **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** (18–20 November 1983, Arizona State University). Edited by Paul C. Marsh, the volume compiles research on plants and animals — including aquatic species — of this small (<1,500 km²), highly endemic intermontane desert basin. The text itself contains no species-specific morphological or ecological data; rather, it frames the basin's unique aquatic ecosystems, high endemism, and urgent conservation needs, setting context for the papers that follow in the proceedings. No specific fish taxa, measurements, or diagnostic characters are presented in this preface.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The preface describes the **physical and hydrological setting** that supports the endemic aquatic biota of Cuatro Ciénegas, relevant background for any associated fish taxa discussed elsewhere in the proceedings:

- **Location**: Intermontane valley on the eastern edge of the **[[Sierra Madre Oriental]]**, Coahuila, northern México.
- **Basin size**: Less than 1,500 km².
- **Elevation**: Basin floor ~740 m MSL; surrounding mountains exceeding 3,000 m.
- **Climate**: Extremely arid desert climate; mean annual precipitation <200 mm; air temperatures range from below 0°C (winter) to above 44°C (summer).
- **Landscape features**: Bajadas, canyons, alluvial fans, dune fields interdigitating with the basin floor.
- **Aquatic/semi-aquatic habitats**: Springs (manantiales), marshes (ciénegas), rivers, lakes, playas, and canals — a mosaic supporting a biologically rich, highly endemic fauna and flora.
- **Biotic character**: High levels of **[[Endemism]]** across the basin's terrestrial and mesic habitats, underpinning its status as a globally significant biodiversity hotspot for desert aquatic organisms, likely including pupfish, cichlids, and other **[[Desert Fish]]** lineages typically associated with Cuatro Ciénegas (not itemized in this preface).

## Key Findings

- Cuatro Ciénegas is characterized by biologists as **one of the most important natural areas in North America**, notable for its combination of small geographic size and disproportionately high endemism.
- The basin's aquatic and semi-aquatic habitats (springs, marshes, rivers, lakes, playas, canals) are described as **singular and fragile**, forming the central theme of the symposium proceedings.
- At the time of writing (1983/1984), the region's unique resources were considered to be **actively threatened or imminently threatened by development**, motivating the symposium's convening.
- The stated purpose of the volume is to **update and consolidate scientific knowledge** on Cuatro Ciénegas flora and fauna to inform conservation policy and planning decisions.
- Organizers explicitly intended the scientific content to be **translated into popularized accounts** to broaden public awareness and influence decision-makers and planners responsible for the region's future.
- The proceedings advocate for **controlled, preservation-oriented resource use** as an alternative to unlimited exploitation leading to irretrievable environmental damage — framing the conservation choice as fundamentally a political decision that should be grounded in sound biological data.
- Funding and institutional support came from the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Office of Endangered Species** (Albuquerque, NM) and the **Center for Environmental Studies, Arizona State University**, underscoring binational (U.S.–México) interest in the basin's conservation.
