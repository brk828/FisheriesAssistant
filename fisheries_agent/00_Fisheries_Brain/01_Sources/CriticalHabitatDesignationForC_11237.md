## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific research paper but a piece of **administrative correspondence** dated early 1994, sent from the Colorado River Board of California (on behalf of the Lower Division states: California, Nevada, Arizona) to the U.S. Department of the Interior. The letter addresses the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's (Service) pending action to designate **critical habitat** for four endangered fish species inhabiting the [[Colorado River System]], under the authority of the **Endangered Species Act (ESA)**. While lacking morphological or physiological data, the letter provides valuable context regarding the **conservation status**, **geographic distribution**, and **regulatory framework** surrounding these species, specifically advocating for a multi-species **Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP)** under Section 10 of the ESA as an alternative to piecemeal Section 7 consultations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System:** The three primary species of concern reside in the **Lower Colorado River Basin**, a large-scale riverine ecosystem subject to extensive human water and power management.
- **Habitat Type:** Riverine (large river) habitat, specifically noted as a "system" requiring an "eco-system approach" rather than isolated management.
- **Co-occurring Species:** The letter notes the existence of "other endangered and at-risk riverine species" occupying the Colorado River and its associated riverine habitats, though these are not specifically named in this correspondence.
- **Anthropogenic Pressures:** The primary ecological stressor identified is **water and power development/operations**, which directly impacts the habitat integrity of these species. The Lower Division states explicitly link the fate of these species to hydrological management decisions.

## Key Findings

- **Species Identified:** Four endangered fish species inhabit the Colorado River System; three specifically reside in the Lower Colorado River Basin:
    1. [[Razorback Sucker]]
    2. [[Bonytail Chub]]
    3. [[Humpback Chub]]
    *(A fourth species is referenced as inhabiting the broader Colorado River System but is not named in the Lower Basin context within this excerpt).*

- **Regulatory Action:** The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service was in the process of finalizing a rule to designate **critical habitat** for these species, with a final rule expected by **March 15, 1994**.

- **Legal Framework Tension:** The designation of critical habitat was anticipated to increase the frequency of **Section 7 consultations** under the [[Endangered Species Act]], which the Lower Division states characterized as fostering a "disjointed, piecemeal approach" to species management.

- **Proposed Alternative — Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP):** In response, the Lower Division states (California, Nevada, and implicitly Arizona) proposed development of a **multi-species Habitat Conservation Plan** under **Section 10** of the ESA, taking a broader "eco-system approach" to address the needs of the endangered species alongside human water/power operational needs.

- **Governance Structure:** A **Steering Committee** was established, comprising representatives from:
    - State governments
    - Indian communities
    - Water and power user groups
    - (Environmental community representation was "being identified" at the time of writing)

- **Funding:** Initial planning was funded via a **$100,000 budget**, equally contributed by the participating states, earmarked partly for hiring a professional facilitator.

- **Stakeholder Objective:** The letter's core request was to secure a meeting with Department of the Interior officials (Assistant Secretaries George T. Frampton Jr. and Elizabeth Ann Rieke) to negotiate the Department's role and commitment to the proposed [[Section 10 Habitat Conservation Plan]] process, as a previous request to meet directly with Secretary Babbitt had been declined due to scheduling constraints.

- **Significance:** This correspondence illustrates the historical policy tension between **species-by-species regulatory compliance** (Section 7) and **holistic, multi-species ecosystem planning** (Section 10) in the management of endangered freshwater fish in heavily engineered river systems like the [[Colorado River]].
