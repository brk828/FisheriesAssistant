# Evidence for Multiple Intrusion and Deformation within the Santa Catalina–Rincon-Tortolita Crystalline Complex, Southeastern Arizona

This note is a geological synthesis rather than an ichthyological or aquatic study; no fish, invertebrate, plankton or water-chemistry data are present in the source.

#geology #batholith #mylonitization #geochronology #metamorphic-core-complex

## Executive Summary

Keith and co-authors integrate field mapping, Rb-Sr whole-rock and mineral isochrons, K-Ar, fission-track and U-Th-Pb dating to produce a new synthesis of the [[Santa Catalina–Rincon-Tortolita Crystalline Complex]]. The crystalline core is interpreted as a composite batholith deformed by variable cataclasis. It was built by three episodes of plutonism: the [[Leatherwood Suite]] (75–64 m.y. B.P.), the [[Wilderness Suite]] (44–50 m.y. B.P.) and the [[Catalina Suite]] (27–25 m.y. B.P.). Mylonitization is tied to these intrusive episodes, with at least three events constrained to about 70–20 m.y. ago. The authors conclude that plutonism was clearly episodic, while the episodicity of deformation is supported but not proven.

## Background & Description

The complex lies at the southeast end of a northwest-trending zone of [[Metamorphic Core Complexes]] in southern Arizona, within the [[Basin and Range Province]]. Its oldest rocks are middle Proterozoic metasedimentary and metaigneous units, including the [[Pinal Schist]] (about 1.7 b.y.) and [[Oracle Granite]] (about 1.44 b.y.). These were overlain by Apache Group sediments and Paleozoic carbonates and clastics. Earlier workers, beginning with [[C. F. Tolman]], recognized the central granite mass but misassigned several plutons, including the [[Catalina Quartz Monzonite]], to Precambrian age.

The paper uses the term "mylonitic" for rocks with a foliation in which feldspars are comminuted (about 60–80% of the rock) and quartz is recrystallized (up to about 40%). The authors argue for a "mylonitic model" in which deformation was largely isochemical. Nomenclature is revised: the term "gneiss" is largely dropped, and terms with plutonic names are retained as intrusive in origin. The complex is about 75% Phanerozoic plutonic rock, about 20% middle Proterozoic plutonic rock, and subordinate metasedimentary rock.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The source has no ecological content. Its environmental relevance is geological: the complex is described as a region of crustal extension with low-angle dislocation surfaces (such as the [[Catalina Fault]]) overlying mylonitic gneisses. Post-Paleozoic cooling is documented: the structurally lowest part cooled from about 400 °C at 28 m.y. ago to about 100 °C at about 21 m.y. ago under a steep geothermal gradient. Most K-Ar and fission-track dates fall between 20 and 30 m.y., which the authors interpret as the termination of a thermal event rather than emplacement ages. Post-batholith dikes and [[Basin and Range Faulting]] record later extension.

## Key Findings

- **Leatherwood Suite (Laramide, 75–64 m.y.):** calc-alkalic, epidote-bearing biotite quartz diorite to granodiorite, including the [[Leatherwood Quartz Diorite]], [[Rice Peak Granodiorite]] and [[Chirreon Wash Granodiorite]]. Emplacement is supported by a 73 m.y. Rb-Sr reference isochron (assumed comagmatic), a 64.4 m.y. hornblende K-Ar age, and a 37.7 m.y. sericite age from a cross-cutting quartz vein giving a minimum age.
- **Wilderness Suite (Eocene, 44–50 m.y.):** leucocratic, muscovite-garnet peraluminous granites, including the [[Wilderness Granite]], [[Lemmon Rock Leucogranite]], [[Derrio Canyon Granite]], [[Wrong Mountain Granite]] and [[Espiritu Canyon Granite]]. A 47 m.y. Rb-Sr reference isochron with initial ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr of 0.7110 is the principal age constraint. Supporting data include a 47.9 m.y. K-Ar coarse muscovite age, 44–47 m.y. monazite U-Th-Pb ages, and a 50 m.y. zircon lower intercept. The suite occupies about 65% of the complex area.
- **Catalina Suite (middle Tertiary, 27–25 m.y.):** biotite quartz monzonite to granite, including the [[Catalina Quartz Monzonite]], [[Tortolita Quartz Monzonite]], [[Reef of Rock Granite]] and [[Happy Valley Quartz Monzonite]] (provisional). A 26 m.y. Rb-Sr reference isochron is supported by a 27 m.y. U-Pb zircon age, concordant K-Ar biotite–hornblende pairs and fission-track ages. These plutons form about 15% of the batholithic rocks.
- **Mylonitic deformation:** at least three episodes are recognized. The oldest is post-75 m.y. and pre-50 m.y., shown by mylonitic Leatherwood cut by undeformed Wilderness pegmatites. The second affected the structurally lower Wilderness granite and Wrong Mountain granite, post-44–50 m.y. and pre-31–25 m.y. The youngest is bracketed close to 25 m.y. by a 24 m.y. K-Ar biotite age from a cross-cutting dike, and is pre-cooling at 17–20 m.y.
- **Lineation contrast:** Leatherwood lineations trend about N85°E (16 measurements), whereas Wilderness lineations trend about N35°E to N65°E, supporting distinct deformation episodes. Mylonitization is constrained to a 50 m.y. interval, 70 to 20 m.y. ago.
- **Reduced ages:** K-Ar biotite ages of about 26–32 m.y. are interpreted as thermally reset. Coarse muscovite ages of 33–36 m.y. are discordant and are interpreted as partial resetting of retentive micas, not excess argon; garnet analysis constrains excess argon to at most about 0.1 m.y.
- **Porphyry copper link:** the [[Leatherwood Quartz Diorite]] hosts skarn mineralization at Marble Peak, and porphyry copper mineralization in southeast Arizona is generally 70–50 m.y. old.
- **Conclusions:** plutonism was episodic in three distinct suites. Mylonitization is spatially and temporally associated with plutonism, which suggests a genetic link and supports episodic deformation, though a prolonged continuum cannot be excluded.
