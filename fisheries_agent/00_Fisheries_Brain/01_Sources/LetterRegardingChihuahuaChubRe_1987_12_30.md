# Chihuahua Chub Recovery Proposal — Mimbres River

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #ConservationBiology #Gila-nigrescens

## Executive Summary
This 1987 letter from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), Arizona State University, addresses a proposal by Jeff Whitney (Gila National Forest, US Forest Service) to enhance recovery of the [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*) via establishment of a new secure population on National Forest lands. The plan involves physically rehabilitating an abandoned side-channel of the [[Mimbres River]] and rewatering it using existing Forest Service water rights. The DFRT reviewed the site, endorsed the concept in principle, and recommended a feasibility study be developed collaboratively with named technical experts before implementation. The letter requests that the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director convey support to the Forest Service and formally thank Mr. Whitney.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species status:** *Gila nigrescens* is federally listed as **Threatened** in the United States (listed 1979), with **no designated critical habitat**.
- **Current distribution/population structure:**
  - Persists in **small numbers** within **extremely limited habitat** on **private lands** along the [[Mimbres River]], New Mexico.
  - Additional wild populations of **unknown size and status** exist in the **Rio Casas Grandes**, **Rio Santa Maria**, and **Rio del Carmen** drainages of Chihuahua, Mexico — indicating a transboundary distribution tied to endorheic/desert river systems.
  - An **ex-situ refuge/broodstock population** of Mimbres River origin is maintained at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], serving as a genetic reservoir and spawning source.
- **Habitat context:** The species inhabits a **xeric (desert) river system**, where water and land are subject to heavy competing human uses, severely constraining opportunities for habitat restoration or new population establishment.
- **Threats implied:** Habitat limitation on private lands, water scarcity/competition, and the general rarity of secure habitat within the arid Mimbres basin are cited as primary constraints to recovery.

## Key Findings
- The [[Chihuahua Chub Recovery Plan]] explicitly identifies establishing populations on **public lands within the Mimbres River basin** as a recovery goal — this proposal directly targets that objective.
- Since federal listing in **1979**, **few recovery actions** had been implemented prior to this 1987 proposal, underscoring the significance of this initiative.
- The proposed action: rehabilitate an **abandoned side-channel** of the Mimbres River and **rewater it using Forest Service–held water rights**, creating new secure habitat for translocation/establishment of a chub population.
- DFRT members conducted an on-site evaluation of the proposed habitat development location and concluded the site has **potential merit** and aligns with the Recovery Plan's implementation guidelines.
- The DFRT recommended that Jeff Whitney collaborate with recovery team members **David Propst** and **Jerry Stefferud**, along with consultant **Jim Brooks**, to refine the project design—balancing species recovery needs with the management policies of involved agencies and stakeholders.
- Outstanding **technical and logistical questions** remain unresolved and must be answered before any population establishment can proceed; the immediate ask is DFRT/USFWS concurrence to begin **feasibility studies**, not full implementation.
- This document illustrates a broader model of **multi-agency collaborative recovery planning** for threatened desert fishes, involving the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Gila National Forest]] (USFS), and academic/consultant technical review via the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]].
