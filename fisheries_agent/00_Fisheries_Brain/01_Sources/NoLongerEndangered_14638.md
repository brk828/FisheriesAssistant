# Sport Fishing Institute Bulletin No. 188 (September 1967)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sport-fisheries

## Executive Summary

SFI Bulletin No. 188 (September 1967) is a multi-topic newsletter from the [[Sport Fishing Institute]]. It covers endangered southwestern fishes, a conservation writing award, the effects of noise and leisure on health, reservoir speed zoning, camping and recreation-use statistics, wilderness litter at the [[Boundary Waters Area]], research value of the [[San Marcos River]], Iowa creel data, farm pond management, graduate research grants, and Wisconsin sturgeon spearing. The unifying theme is that aquatic habitat diversity and sound resource-use policy are needed to protect both sport fishing and rare species.

## Background & Description

The Institute's directors passed a 1964 resolution urging governments to weigh the effects of water development on threatened aquatic resources and to maintain habitat variety. Following this, the Institute funded [[William L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University) to study five small, geographically restricted southwestern fishes: [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], and [[Gila Topminnow]]. Phase one (1965–1967) established distribution and status. Secondary laboratory goals were only partly met because of pesticide losses and partial water-supply failure. Funding in 1967 extended fieldwork and attempted culture of [[Colorado Squawfish]].

The bulletin also reprints Institute material on recreation, including articles on leisure and health, camping behavior, and a farm pond magazine. It closes with a list of research grants, a foreign symposium, and fishery harvest reports.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Little Colorado spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*):** Originally listed as "endangered" by the U.S. Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife's Committee on Endangered Species. Field work showed it was more abundant than thought. Minckley recommended keeping it as a "rare" form to watch, because of its restricted range and sensitivity to introduced fishes. Laboratory spawning had not been achieved, and no spawning individuals had been found in nature.
- **Exotic fishes:** The report states that exotic fishes are a major cause of decline in most indigenous southwestern forms.
- **Kinzua Reservoir (Pennsylvania):** A 12,000-acre lake. The Pennsylvania Fish Commission set speed zones: no horsepower limit; 8 mph on the main arms; a high-speed buoyed lane about 200 feet wide in the Kinzua arm; and 3 mph in small bays where good fishing was expected.
- **San Marcos River (Texas):** Dr. [[Clark Hubbs]] opposed channelization of the upper river. He described it as home to locally unique forms, including four co-occurring [[Black Basses]] species (*Micropterus*) between Interstate 35 and the Blanco River mouth. He warned that silt increases from dredging could be "catastrophic" for the biota.
- **Boundary Waters Area (Minnesota):** Heavy use and litter at canoe campsites were documented, with fresh trash found at sites immediately after parties left.
- **Okoboji Lakes (Iowa):** Creel census data are reported (see Key Findings).
- **Farm ponds:** Over two million exist in the U.S., and a little less than half are designed or managed for fish production.
- **Global context:** An FAO symposium noted that wild inland fisheries have declined due to water diversion, drainage of swamps, barriers to migration, and pollution. Pond culture was presented as a replacement for lost supply.

## Key Findings

**Endangered species and policy**
- The 1964 resolution called for protecting rare aquatic habitats and species from water developments.
- Minckley's work reclassified *L. vittata* from "endangered" to "rare," pending further monitoring. A paper with Louis H. Carufel was submitted to the *Southwestern Naturalist*.

**Conservation writing award (1967)**
- James M. Thomas received the Sport Fishing Institute–Du Pont Conservation Award, a plaque and $500, for work on Texas Panhandle water pollution.
- Special citations went to Jack Van Coevering (Lake Erie article, *Sports Afield*, October 1966) and Curt Carpenter (*Outdoor Oklahoma*, February 1967).

**Leisure and health**
- Dr. Lee E. Farr (University of Texas) linked long-term loud noise to nervous fatigue, digestive problems, allergies, and migraine.
- Professor Lawrence L. Suhm (University of Wisconsin) argued that poor leisure resources, not leisure time itself, harm health.
- Nearly 75 percent of leisure time is spent at home: 18–20 hours per week watching television and six hours reading.
- Adult men average 31 leisure hours per week and women over 35.

**Recreation-use statistics**
- Camping families in auto campgrounds on the Huron-Manistee National Forests (1962) spent an average of 12.4 hours per day: relaxation 8.3 h (67%), swimming 1.1 h, fishing 1.0 h (8%), auto sightseeing 0.9 h.
- 32 percent of campers fished, while 93 percent relaxed and 40 percent swam.
- The bulletin argues that the Recreation Advisory Council's 12-hour "visitor-day" (Policy No. 6) is artificial. Typical fishing trips involve 3–4 hours on-site, plus travel time. Forest Service average hours per calendar day were: freshwater fisherman 4, hunter 5, saltwater fisherman 6, bird watcher/photographer 4.

**Boundary Waters wilderness litter**
- 632,000 visitor-days in 1966 (65 major entrances), with 47 percent in the Basswood Lake zones.
- This is nearly five times the 1960 use.
- Proposed remedies: manned check points, a $5 per person per day refundable deposit tied to refuse return, and designated campsites.

**Research value of the San Marcos River**
- Hubbs estimated the annual loss of research value from dredging at $50,000, or $16,666 per mile of affected stream.
- He stated that the endemic list is "greater than that of any comparable area in the United States."
- At least 11 papers on the upper San Marcos appeared in 1957–1966; more than 70 ichthyological papers were estimated.

**Iowa creel census (Okoboji Lakes, 1966)**
- Total catch: 875,705 fish weighing 387,166 pounds, from 157,258 trips totaling 441,003 hours.
- Average: 5.57 fish per trip, 1.99 fish per hour.
- Species shares: bullheads 38%, perch 37%, bluegill 16%, crappie 4%, walleye 3% (12 species total).
- West Okoboji produced the most fish.

**Farm ponds**
- About 20 percent of U.S. anglers fished farm ponds, per a 1959 survey.
- Well-managed ponds averaging about one acre might yield 100 pounds of fish per year.
- The new quarterly *Farm Pond Harvest* was launched in Spring 1967.

**Research grants**
- The Institute has granted more than $250,000 since 1950 to about 60 institutions, supporting roughly 100 fishery scientists.
- Nine awards in 1967 total $13,038 (calculated from listed amounts). The largest were [[Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution]] ($2,000, marlin and tuna migration) and the University of Illinois ($2,473, weather and Atlantic salmon production).

**Wisconsin sturgeon**
- Spear fishermen took 1,423 sturgeon through the ice on Lake Winnebago and Little Lake Butte des Morts during a 26-day 1967 season.
- This set a record for a one-fish limit; the previous high was 1,001 in 1963.
- Licenses sold: about 5,000, a 15 percent increase over the 4,300 tags sold in 1966.
