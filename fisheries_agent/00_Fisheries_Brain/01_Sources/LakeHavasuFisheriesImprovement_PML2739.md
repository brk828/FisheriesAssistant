# Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program Technical Committee Meeting

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum transmits the minutes of the [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]] Technical Committee meeting held on July 29, 1998, at the [[Bill Williams Refuge]] office. The committee is a partnership among the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], the [[California Department Of Fish And Game]], the [[Bureau Of Land Management]], and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]]. Discussion covered habitat installation, native fish production of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], a fish-kill event at a rearing pond, partner funding and grants, facility construction, and planning for a 1999 fishing event. The next meeting was set for October 7, 1998, in Blythe, California.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency memorandum (dated August 26, 1998) with attached meeting minutes. The memo is from the Lake Havasu Fisheries Program Manager to the Fisheries Program Coordinator, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Parker, Arizona.
- **Meeting attendees (July 29, 1998):** Brad Jacobson (Arizona Game and Fish), Chris Hayes (California Department of Fish and Game), Al Doelker (BLM), Chuck Minckley, Dale Webster, and Kirk Koch (the chair, who led the agenda). Affiliations for Minckley, Webster, and Koch are unclear in the scanned text.
- **Program focus:** Restoring two native fishes, the razorback sucker and bonytail, which are listed as threatened or endangered (the minutes refer to them as "T&E" fish). Activities include hatchery rearing, pond growout, stocking into [[Lake Havasu]], and installing fish habitat structures.
- **Key sites named in the minutes:**
  - [[Site Six]], where a habitat facility was dedicated and where the third large boat was installing habitat.
  - [[Achii Hanyo]], a rearing facility with ponds and ongoing upkeep needs.
  - [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]], the source of razorback stocking this year.
  - [[Bulkhead Cove]] and [[Office Cove]], where native fish were held for fall release.
  - [[Golf Course Ponds]], including Pond 1.
  - [[Mesquite Phase Ii]] and the [[Bluebird Project]], which were under construction or planned.
  - [[Havasu Cultural Center]], where a water feature was proposed.
- **Partner agreements:** An agreement for the Niland facility was being advanced by California, pending a lease modification with the [[Imperial Irrigation District]] and state approval from Sacramento.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structures:** Dale Webster reported clouds of fry and young fish around the installed structures and more large catfish than ever before. The group agreed that spawning on the structures was unlikely, since bass and sunfish lay adhesive eggs in nests and water depth was not considered a major factor. Webster still felt the structures were improving recruitment because of the number of young fish using them. Chuck Minckley said this was why the catfish were on the structures. Video of diver surveys was shared to show habitat performance.
- **Acreage:** The project was near 100 acres of habitat installed for the fiscal year.
- **Dissolved oxygen mortality:** At the golf course ponds, roughly 600 bonytail died over about a week, at a rate of several dozen per day. The fish were large enough for lake release. Minckley attributed the losses to marginal dissolved oxygen caused by weed growth. Four aerators had been installed and water flow was continuous. Weed control and deeper ponds were the remaining steps.
- **Native fish production status:**
  - Several hundred native fish at Bulkhead Cove were ready for release once the weather cooled.
  - Pond 1 showed abundant razorbacks in early July seining.
  - Office Cove held many fish to be captured and planted that fall.
- **Public reporting:** The committee agreed that anglers should be encouraged to report native or dead T&E fish. Brad Jacobson and Chuck Minckley said officers should not harass citizens for reporting catches.

## Key Findings

1. **Stocking and supply:**
   - 5,954 razorbacks were stocked into the lake from Bubbling Ponds this year, with at least 1,000 more to be delivered once conditions allowed.
   - Young razorbacks were in short supply after a die-off at [[Willow Beach]].
   - Buenos Aires refuge had a pond with 2,000 to 3,000 razorbacks to be moved to the project.
   - About 4,000 razorbacks at Achii Hanyo were ready for release when weather permitted.
   - Over 10,000 bonytail at Achii Hanyo were nearing releasable size.
2. **Research:** The planned tagging of razorbacks for tracking in Havasu was delayed. The first fish were to be marked for release on the Parker Strip, with similar Havasu efforts to follow later.
3. **Infrastructure and construction:**
   - Mesquite Phase II construction was scheduled to begin in mid-September 1998.
   - Bluebird construction was hoped to start in January or February 1999.
   - Information panels at Mesquite had been delayed by the contractor process. Kirk Koch planned to contact the regional contracting office in Albuquerque to speed them up.
   - A concept water feature for the Havasu Cultural Center, stocked with game fish for public display, was proposed. Permit needs and possible heritage grant funds were noted.
4. **Funding and partnerships:**
   - Al Doelker asked about matching funds for the "bring back the natives" program. The group felt volunteer contributions could be a substantial match.
   - Chris Hayes said California grants were available, and he would help partners apply but could not administer them alone.
   - BLM was drafting a mid-point promotional report for the project.
5. **Public engagement:**
   - A native fish poster was updated to guide anglers on what to do if they catch a native fish.
   - The committee planned a [[National Fishing Week]] event on the new docks in June 1999, with indoor classes and night youth fishing.
   - Volunteer incentives (hats, calendars, key chains) were proposed for reinforcement.
6. **Management actions and decisions:**
   - Kirk Koch noted the loss of Larry Forbis, who retired on May 3, 1998, and asked for help filling the gap.
   - Brad Jacobson offered intern help the following year.
   - Chuck Minckley requested dive assistance to install a commercially available screening device on the river inlet at the golf course ponds.
   - The committee agreed to reconvene on October 7, 1998, in Blythe.
