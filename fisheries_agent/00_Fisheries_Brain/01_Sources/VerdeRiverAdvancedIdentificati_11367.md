# Verde River Advanced Identification Project (ADID) - Public Workshop Notice and Interim Final Site Identifications

#environmental-regulation #wetlands-protection #riparian-ecology #Section404 #ADID

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Region IX and U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (Corps) conducted an Advanced Identification (ADID) project for the [[Verde River]] in Arizona under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act. This document announces public workshops (April 25-26, 1994) to discuss interim final site identifications along the Verde River from [[Sullivan Lake]] to [[Horseshoe Reservoir]], including one mile up major tributaries ([[Hell Canyon]], [[Sycamore Creek]], [[Oak Creek]], [[Beaver Creek]], [[West Clear Creek]], [[Fossil Creek]], and [[East Verde River]]). The ADID process identifies areas as "potentially suitable" or "generally unsuitable" for disposal of dredged or fill material, based on functional assessment of the riparian ecosystem. The entire ADID area is designated as generally unsuitable for fill activities, with specific exceptions for minor activities such as bridge repair, restoration projects, and utility crossings that meet strict criteria.

## Background & Description

The Verde River ADID project represents a proactive planning process under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act to identify suitable and unsuitable sites for dredged or fill material disposal before permit applications are submitted. The project area encompasses the main stem of the Verde River from Sullivan Lake to Horseshoe Reservoir and extends one mile up each perennial tributary, with lateral boundaries defined by the upland edge of riparian habitat as mapped by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service National Wetland Inventory (NWI).

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service conducted a functional assessment using a modified Wetland Evaluation Technique (WET) to evaluate ten functions across thirteen river reaches: groundwater recharge, groundwater discharge, floodflow alteration, sediment stabilization, nutrient removal/transformation, production export, aquatic diversity, wildlife diversity, recreation, and uniqueness/heritage. Each reach was assessed for the presence or absence of physical and biological characteristics that predict functional performance.

The project area contains outstanding natural resources of local, regional, and national importance. The Verde River is listed on the Department of Interior's National Rivers Inventory as one of the nation's most significant free-flowing rivers. In 1984, 39.5 miles downstream of Beasley Flat were designated Wild and Scenic. The entire riparian corridor is designated Resource Category 1 by USFWS, indicating high value for wildlife species and irreplaceable status on a national basis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Verde River riparian ecosystem supports exceptional biodiversity and performs critical ecological functions. All reaches provide habitat for one or more of 31 special status species (Federal listed or candidate, State listed or candidate). The entire ADID area is designated as critical habitat for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally and state endangered species. Upper reaches from Sullivan Lake to Sycamore Canyon are unique in Arizona due to the predominance of native fish species.

The Cottonwood-Willow riparian gallery forest between [[Sycamore Creek]] and [[West Clear Creek]] (Reaches 2A-4) represents one of only five extensive stands of this forest type remaining in Arizona. [[Peck's Lake]] is the only naturally formed oxbow lake in Arizona, with recorded bird species observations higher than any other reach. The area supports 60% of vertebrate species inhabiting the three National Forests represented within the ADID area.

The reaches between Perkinsville and Childs (Reaches 1B-5) serve as important reintroduction sites for [[Colorado Squawfish]] (federally and state endangered) and razorback sucker. The Verde River from Sob Canyon to Horseshoe Reservoir, including Peck's Lake and Tavasci Marsh, Beaver Creek, Wet Beaver Creek, and West Clear Creek, are proposed as critical habitat for the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (Reaches 2A-7).

The functional assessment matrix indicates that all functions are likely effectively occurring in portions of all reaches except groundwater recharge at Peck's Lake and Tavasci Marsh (Reach 2C). All reaches effectively perform three or more functions for greater than 50% of the reach length. Impacts from Section 404 regulated activities are particularly noticeable in portions of five stream reaches, primarily associated with sand and gravel mining operations.

## Key Findings

**Interim Final Site Identifications:**

1. **Generally Unsuitable Sites:** The entire Verde River ADID area is designated as generally unsuitable for disposal of dredged or fill material. This determination is based on the likelihood that most fill activities would not comply with Section 404(b)(1) Guidelines, specifically Section 230.10(c), which prohibits discharges causing significant degradation of waters of the United States.

2. **Potentially Suitable Sites:** No stream reaches are identified as potentially suitable within the Verde River ADID area.

3. **Site Specific Exceptions:** Limited exceptions exist for activities with minimal adverse effects:
   - Repair, rehabilitation, or replacement of previously authorized bridges, river crossings, and utility crossings
   - Projects whose purpose is restoration and enhancement of the riparian ecosystem
   - New utility crossings
   - New bridges and other river crossings (limited to 1/3 acre fill, maximum 200 linear feet in special aquatic sites)
   - Minor bank stabilization (not exceeding 200 linear feet cumulative impact)

**Functional Assessment Results:**

- All reaches perform multiple critical functions including floodflow alteration, sediment stabilization, nutrient retention/transformation, production export, aquatic diversity support, and wildlife diversity support
- The Verde River functions overall as a high-value riverine system
- Resource values are significant on local, state, regional, and national scales
- High probability exists that concentrating fill activities in limited reaches would cause adverse cumulative effects upstream and downstream

**Regulatory Framework:**

- Activities must comply with Section 404(b)(1) Guidelines and 401 Certification conditions
- Proposed activities must not adversely affect special status species or designated critical habitat
- Construction and maintenance of agricultural diversion structures are generally exempt from Section 404 regulation but may be "recaptured" if effects bring waters into new use or reduce flow/circulation
- The Corps proposes to modify applicable Nationwide Permits (NWP 3, 12, 13, 14, 27) to incorporate ADID guidance and revoke other Nationwide Permits for the ADID area

**Project Timeline:**

- Project initiated December 1989
- Functional assessment conducted January-August 1991
- Public workshops on interim final site identifications: April 25-26, 1994
- Comment period closes: May 20, 1994
- Final revisions and joint public notice: May-June 1994

The ADID results are strictly informational and advisory, not constituting permit approval or denial. Individuals may still apply for Section 404 permits for designated unsuitable sites, but the ADID indicates the relative difficulty of obtaining permits and encourages alternative solutions avoiding impacts to important aquatic sites.
