# Walleye and Northern Pike Populations in Three Alberta Lakes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[J. F. Craig]] and K. Smiley (Alberta Environmental Centre, Vegreville) reports a single-season (May–September 1983) survey of [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*) and [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) in Ethel, Marie and Wolf Lakes in the Cold Lake region of [[Alberta]]. The work provided pre-impact baseline data ahead of [[Bitumen Extraction]] development using [[Cyclic Steam Stimulation]]. Northern pike were abundant in all three lakes, while walleye populations were dominated by a few strong year classes that were not synchronised between lakes. Growth of both species fitted the [[Von Bertalanffy Growth Model]], and northern pike appeared under-exploited, suggesting that higher harvests could be sustained and might benefit walleye. The authors state that, because permission was granted for only one year, conclusions are tentative.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** Cold Lake area, Alberta. Wolf Lake is a highly valued walleye angling lake, and several lakes support commercial winter fisheries.
- **Lake morphometry (Table I):**

| Marie | 3621 | 26.7 | 14.1 | 384.04 | 133 | 9.4 |

- **Methods:** Three experimental [[Gill Net]] types were fished mainly inshore. Nets A and B each had four 8 m panels (32 m total, 2.15 m deep) with stretched mesh sizes of 19–64 mm and 76–127 mm respectively. Net C had four 17 m panels (68 m total, 1.2 m deep) with mesh sizes of 25–102 mm. Ageing used the [[Operculum]] bone; back-calculation and [[Walford Plot]] analysis were applied. Fecundity was assessed from ripe females.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Community:** Walleye, northern pike, [[Lake Whitefish]], [[White Sucker]], [[Longnose Sucker]], [[Yellow Perch]] and [[Spottail Shiner]] were caught in all three lakes. [[Burbot]] occurred only in Marie Lake. [[Cisco]] are present in all three lakes but were not caught in 1983 nets.
- **Dominance:** Ethel and Marie catches were dominated by northern pike, white sucker and yellow perch. In Wolf Lake, northern pike dominated, while walleye, white sucker, yellow perch and spottail shiner were similar in numbers.
- **Diet:** Walleye were often empty-stomached in May–June. In Ethel and Wolf Lakes both predators ate a range of fish and invertebrates (including [[Dragonfly Nymphs]] for pike). In Marie Lake both species ate almost exclusively fish, notably whitefish. The authors attribute this to lake morphology, with Wolf Lake being shallow and containing extensive littoral habitat. Similar diets suggest possible competition for prey.
- **Environmental drivers:** The strong 1975 walleye year class in Ethel and Wolf Lakes may be linked to high spring (April–June) precipitation and run-off, which could have enhanced spawning success.

## Key Findings

**Age, growth and population structure**
- Operculum checks were validated as annual using tetracycline. Two readers agreed on 96% of walleye ages (n = 274).
- Walleye populations were dominated by a few strong year classes, with no synchronism among lakes. Walleye in Wolf Lake showed significant differences in length-at-age, with the 1978 and 1979 year classes growing faster than earlier cohorts.
- Northern pike showed dominant year classes (notably 1979), but variability was less marked than in walleye. Marie Lake had the widest age range and the oldest fish. No significant year-class differences in length-at-age were detected.
- Females of both species reached greater asymptotic length (L∞) and lived longer than males. Walleye females matured at about age 5 (males about age 4). Northern pike were mostly mature at age 4.

**Growth models and length–weight**
- Both species were well described by the von Bertalanffy model. Male walleye grew more quickly (K) than females, particularly in Marie Lake.
- Walleye length–weight relationships were significant across groups. Northern pike b was 0.952 (±0.017), significantly different from 1 but not from the pooled value used in analysis.

**Fecundity**
- Walleye (Wolf Lake): log₁₀ F = 0.856 + 2.441 log₁₀ L. A 5-year-old (37 cm) produced about 48,000 eggs; a 7-year-old (47 cm) about 87,000.
- Northern pike (Marie Lake): log₁₀ F = −2.671 + 4.052 log₁₀ L. A 3-year-old (57 cm) produced about 13,000 eggs; an 8-year-old (69 cm) about 60,000.

**Mortality**
- Northern pike mortality appeared linked to growth: faster-growing fish had higher instantaneous total mortality (Z). Marie Lake pike had higher L∞ and lower K and Z than the other lakes.

**Yields and exploitation**
- Wolf Lake walleye commercial yield was 0.69 kg ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ (8% of total catch), falling to about 0.30 kg ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ after mesh size was increased in 1970–71. Angler removal was 0.48 kg ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹.
- Wolf Lake northern pike commercial yield averaged about 2.00 kg ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹, stable since about 1960–61.
- Ethel and Marie lakes yielded poor walleye and pike catches. Marie Lake pike were barely exploited.
- All three lakes were fished above their MEI-based theoretical yields, although Ethel and Marie means fell within the confidence limits of total catch.

**Yield modelling and recommendations**
- A modified Beverton & Holt model indicated that faster-growing northern pike have much higher potential yields than walleye.
- Instantaneous fishing mortality (F) for northern pike was <0.01 in Marie Lake and 0.09 in Wolf Lake, so both lakes are relatively unexploited for pike.
- The authors suggest northern pike could be harvested at higher rates in Marie and Wolf Lakes, possibly improving walleye stocks. Walleye are vulnerable to over-exploitation, and angling removes many immature fish, so careful monitoring is recommended.
