# Dichotomous Key to the Amphipod Species of Oklahoma

#carcinology #freshwater-biology #amphipoda

## Executive Summary

This document is a dichotomous identification key to amphipod crustaceans (order [[Amphipoda]]) of [[Oklahoma]], not a fish study. It separates the state's keyed species by antennal structure, accessory flagellum, uropod proportions, eye presence, and gnathopod (second pereiopod) armature. The key covers roughly seven named or partially legible taxa and ties each to its distribution in springs, streams, lakes, and subterranean waters across physiographic regions such as the [[Arbuckle Uplift]], [[Ouachita Uplift]], and [[Ozarkian Uplift]].

## Background & Description

The key is written in standard couplet format. Each step offers two contrasting character states, and each state either leads to another couplet or to a species name with author, year, and distribution notes. Characters used include:

- **Antennae:** relative length of the first and second antennae, and presence or absence of an accessory flagellum and its segment count.
- **Abdomen and telson:** whether abdominal terga project backward into a median ridge, and the depth of the telson cleft.
- **Uropods:** the relative length of the inner and outer rami on the third uropod, and whether the exopodite and endopodite are multi-segmented.
- **Eyes:** well developed versus absent (subterranean forms).
- **Gnathopods:** shape of the palmar margin of the propodus, notching of the spines, and dentition of the dactyl.

Specimen sizes are given where they serve as diagnostic aids. For example, one [[Gammarus Lacustris]]-group entry is described as robust, with average specimens 16 mm or longer, while [[Gammarus Minus]] is a small species with a maximum size of about 10 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The key itself provides only limited ecological information, mostly distributional:

- [[Hyalella Azteca]] is reported from the entire state, in clear permanent waters.
- A Gammarus species is locally abundant in springs of the Arbuckle Uplift.
- [[Gammarus Minus]] is found only in springs of the Ozarkian Uplift in the northeast part of the state.
- [[Eucrangonyx Shoemakeri]] occurs in small temporary streams in the eastern half of the state.
- [[Eucrangonyx Gracilis]] has been recorded only from a lake at Antlers in the southeast part of the state.
- One uropod-group species occurs in springs and subterranean waters of the Arbuckle Uplift.
- One keyed species occurs in the Arbuckle, Ouachita, and Ozarkian uplifts, in springs and subterranean waters.
- One species was collected by [[Hubricht]] and the author in Spring Creek at a Girl Scout camp south of Locust Grove, [[Mayes County]], and was described by Hubricht.

The subterranean taxa are keyed as eyeless, which is consistent with cave or groundwater habitat, though the document does not elaborate on this.

## Key Findings

- The document keys amphipods of Oklahoma. It is taxonomic reference material and does not address fish.
- Diagnostic characters are mainly antennal flagellum structure, uropod ramus proportions, eye presence, and gnathopod spine and dactyl morphology.
- [[Hyalella Azteca]] is the widest-ranging species keyed, reported statewide in clear permanent waters.
- [[Gammarus Minus]] is restricted to springs in the northeast, in the [[Ozarkian Uplift]].
- [[Eucrangonyx Shoemakeri]] is confined to small temporary streams in the eastern half of Oklahoma.
- [[Eucrangonyx Gracilis]] has a single documented locality, a lake at Antlers.
- Several entries are subterranean and eyeless, linking taxonomy to groundwater habitats.
- Some epithets and dates are illegible or uncertain after OCR, including the Gammarus name in the first couplets and two later species names. These should be verified against the original publication before use in [[Taxonomic Identification]] work.
