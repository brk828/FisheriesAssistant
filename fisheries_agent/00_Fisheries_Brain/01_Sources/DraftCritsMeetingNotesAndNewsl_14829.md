# CRiTS Meeting Notes - Colorado River Trophic Studies GCES-II

#freshwater-ecology #river-ecosystem #trophic-studies #water-quality #macroinvertebrates

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting notes and correspondence from the third Colorado River Trophic Studies (CRiTS) meeting held on 6 August 1991 in Flagstaff, Arizona. The CRiTS group is an informal discussion forum involving members from USGS, NPS, Bureau of Reclamation, and Arizona Game and Fish, focused on water quality and lower trophic level studies in the [[Colorado River]] fluvial ecosystem within [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The meeting addressed ongoing research projects under the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies-II (GCES-II) program and established a timeline for synthesis of trophic studies for the December 1991 Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) report deadline.

## Background & Description

The CRiTS group convened at the GCES Conference Room in Flagstaff from 8:30 a.m. to 3:45 p.m., joined by Dr. Bill Lewis and Dr. Clark Hubbs of the National Academy of Sciences GCES-II Review panel. The meeting involved coordination among multiple agencies conducting parallel studies on the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem below [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

The group decided to use the CRiTS forum to begin synthesis of trophic studies for the December 1991 EIS report deadline. Each project leader was required to prepare detailed outlines of their projects for discussion at the next meeting scheduled for 11 October 1991. The standardized report format was limited to 12 pages of text and included sections for Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Boundaries of Use, and evaluation of EIS Alternatives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The studies encompass the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] through [[Grand Canyon]], including:

**Habitat Types Studied:**
- Dam tailwaters pool area
- [[Lees Ferry]] reach (CR Mile -14)
- Mainstream river sites (10 locations: 5 wide reaches, 5 narrow reaches)
- Tributary mouths and backwater sites
- Eddy and beach environments
- Hyporheic zones

**Water Quality Parameters Monitored:**
- Dissolved organic carbon (DOC)
- Various ions
- Chlorophyll-a
- Dissolved oxygen (DO)
- Temperature
- pH
- Specific conductance
- Light extinction
- Fine particulate organic matter (FPOM)
- Coarse particulate organic matter (CPOM)

**Flow Conditions:**
The studies examined multiple flow regimes including 5,000 cfs, 15,000 cfs constant flow, and fluctuating conditions. Interim Test Flows were imposed by the Secretary from 1 August to 1 November 1991, featuring considerably reduced fluctuations, range, and slower ramping rates compared to GCES-II Test Flows.

## Key Findings

**USGS Synoptic Studies (Wes Campbell):**
- Second synoptic study (June 1991) analyses were being completed
- Water quality components completed for first synoptic effort (November 1990)
- Invertebrate samples sent to Ken Cummings laboratory for identification
- First synoptic samples nearly completed; second synoptic sorting nearly completed
- Identifications expected by 1 November 1991
- Plankton and zooplankton samples preserved but not yet identified
- Monitoring proposal in preparation by Bob Averett

**USGS Water Quality Studies (Bob Hart):**
- Monthly monitoring of dam pool area underway
- Parameters analyzed: DOC, various ions, chlorophyll-a, DO, temperature, pH, specific conductance, light
- Next sampling trip planned for 26 August

**Arizona Game and Fish Nutrient Studies (Angradi, Kubly):**
- Three desiccation experiments completed (2 in dam tailwaters, 1 at CR Mile -14)
- Colonization study initiated at two sites in upper [[Lees Ferry]] reach
- Colonization rates evaluated on "Kubly bricks" at three levels: permanently submerged, lower littoral zone, upper littoral zone
- Benthic surveys using mini-Ponar dredge showed high system variability
- Semi-monthly water quality sampling at tailwaters pool and [[Lees Ferry]] to establish input/output relationships
- Light extinction studies conducted under moderate to high water conditions
- Drift sampling using high-speed Miller tube at 5,000 cfs, 15,000 cfs, and fluctuating conditions
- Limited replication for drift and water quality sampling
- Correlation between [[Gammarus]] and fish diet being assessed
- No meteorological data available for [[Glen Canyon]] reach

**AGF Mainstream Fisheries (Alan Kingsolving):**
- Two study approaches planned for native fish species documentation
- Tributary mouths to be sampled seasonally using manual netting and shocking techniques
- Habitat use model development planned
- Intensive multi-day censuses of important backwater sites with detailed physical and chemical mapping

**NAU Aquatic Studies Program (Joe Shannon):**
- Macroinvertebrate surveys at 10 sites (5 wide reaches, 5 narrow reaches) fully underway
- Taxonomic and standing crop samples collected for primary producers, detritus, and primary consumers (7 taxonomic categories)
- Peterson dredge used in low velocity sites; Hess samples in high velocity sites and tributaries
- [[Cladophora]] and macroinvertebrate desiccation experiments completed at [[Lees Ferry]] using floating laboratory and in situ river cobbles
- Hyporheic zone sampling throughout [[Grand Canyon]] collected minute amphipods
- Tributary mouths appeared sealed off from river

**Eddies as Nutrient Pumps (Jeff Bennett):**
- NAU Master's Degree project investigating whether eddy/backwater/beach environments contribute to nutrient exchange with river
- Questions addressed: rate of contribution, extent of hyporheic contributions, temperature effects
- Survey approach with detailed studies at selected sites
- Need identified for clear definition of terms and study questions

**Colorado River Light Studies (Mike Yard):**
- Bureau of Reclamation light crew collected scalar and cosine-corrected light extinction data every 15 kilometers from dam to Diamond Creek
- Data collected at 5,000 cfs and 15,000 cfs constant flow
- Water quality data and depth-integrated sediment samples collected
- Recommendations: assess total sediment concentration first, take surface grab samples to complement depth-integrated samples
- Effects of fluctuating flows not yet evaluated but planned using dilution tests

**BioWest Fisheries Studies (Bill Leibfried):**
- Movement of [[Humpback Chub]] in mainstream under study
- Drift examined in mainstream versus eddies

**Interim Test Flows Monitoring Needs:**
Priority monitoring criteria identified for short-term and long-term assessment:
1. Sediment deposit stability
2. Sediment transport
3. Lower trophic levels, especially [[Cladophora]] recolonization
4. Physical habitat changes (video recommended)
5. Marshes
6. Trout stranding

Water chemistry, most fisheries aspects, and terrestrial biological components considered more related to seasonal aspects or unable to respond rapidly to short-term (three-month) flow changes. The 5,000 cfs flow identified as potentially having consequences for the Nankoweap reach. Long-term monitoring meeting scheduled for November 1991. No Environmental Assessment monitoring plan or funding developed at time of meeting.
