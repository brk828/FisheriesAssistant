# Arizona Endangered Fish Species: Lead Responsibilities and Status (1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This January 20, 1989 internal list assigns lead responsibility for 22 Arizona fish taxa that were federally listed, proposed, or under review as candidates. For each taxon it gives the conservation status, the lead Region (R2 or R6), and the responsible contact office or person. The document is an administrative roster, not a research report, so it contains no population, habitat, or survey data. Most listings fall under Region 2, and most are assigned to the [[Phoenix Field Office]] with [[Stefferud]] as the most frequent contact.

## Background & Description

The list covers a mix of native Arizona and adjacent Southwestern freshwater fishes. They include minnows and chubs (*Gila* spp., *Meda*, *Tiaroga*, *Plagopterus*, *Notropis*, *Campostoma*, *Lepidomeda*), pupfishes (*Cyprinodon*), topminnows (*Poeciliopsis*), a trout (*Salmo apache*), a catfish (*Ictalurus pricei*), a squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and a sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Several are subspecies or unnamed forms, such as the Yaqui topminnow and the Monkey Springs pupfish (*Cyprinodon* sp.).

The document defines the status codes used in the table:

- **E**: Endangered
- **T**: Threatened
- **P**: Proposed
- **CH**: Critical habitat
- **2**: Candidate, category 2
- **3A**: Category 3A

The table also gives office abbreviations: PHX (Phoenix), SBNWR (San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge), PINFAO (Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office), ALB (Albuquerque), and FW (Fort Worth).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not describe habitat, distribution, life history, threats, or population trends for any species. Its only location information is the regional office assigned to each taxon. Some lead offices are at refuges or fisheries assistance facilities, which suggests some taxa are tied to specific sites, but the text does not confirm this. Where habitat or threat details are needed, they would have to come from other sources.

## Key Findings

- **Total scope:** 22 fish taxa are listed, comprising 16 entries on page 1 and 6 on page 2.
- **Status mix:** Several taxa are listed as Endangered (E), including [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], and [[Woundfin]]. Others are Threatened (T), such as [[Apache Trout]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], and [[Yaqui Catfish]]. [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]] is listed as Proposed Endangered with critical habitat (PE/CH). Several *Gila* taxa, including [[Gila Chub]] and [[Gila Roundtail Chub]], are listed as Category 2 candidates. [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]] is listed as Category 3A.
- **Critical habitat:** Several entries carry a CH or PCH designation, meaning critical habitat has been designated or proposed.
- **Lead regions:** Most taxa are assigned to Region 2. The [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Woundfin]] entries are assigned to Region 6. Woundfin's Region 6 entry is marked with a question mark.
- **Responsible contacts:** [[Stefferud]] is named for the largest share of taxa, with Robertson, Hanson, Baucom, and Burton handling the rest. [[Robertson]] is named for the Yaqui topminnow, Yaqui catfish, and some other entries.
- **Scan caveats:** The source is OCR text. Some status codes and column alignments on page 2 are ambiguous. Entries were matched to contacts by their row position. The status codes above should be checked against the original document before use.
