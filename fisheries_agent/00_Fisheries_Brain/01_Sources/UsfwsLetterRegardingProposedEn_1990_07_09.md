## Executive Summary
This document is a formal letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Portland, Oregon office, dated July 9, 1990, addressed to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University), a noted expert on Southwestern desert fishes. The letter solicits scientific and public comment on a proposal to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an endangered species under the U.S. Endangered Species Act. The action is driven primarily by a documented failure of recruitment into the adult population for approximately 30 years, coupled with a measurable decline in adult population numbers over the preceding decade. This is not a primary research paper but a regulatory action notice; it lacks direct morphological or ecological data but provides critical population-status information relevant to the species' conservation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Historically distributed throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]], including large, turbid, warm-water river reaches.
- **Population Trend**: No recruitment (successful reproduction leading to juvenile survival into the adult population) documented for ~30 years prior to 1990.
- **Adult Population Status**: Declining over the 10 years preceding the letter (i.e., throughout the 1980s), suggesting an aging population with no replacement cohort — a classic indicator of imminent extinction risk absent intervention.
- **Threats Solicited for Comment**: The Service explicitly requests data on threats (or lack thereof), current/planned human activities in the range, and habitat impacts, implying concern over water development, habitat alteration, and possibly hybridization or predation by non-native species (common threats cited in Colorado River Basin fish literature).

## Key Findings
- The USFWS formally proposed listing the [[Razorback Sucker]] as **Endangered** under the Endangered Species Act, with lead responsibility assigned to the Denver, Colorado regional office and the Salt Lake City Field Office.
- **Critical trigger for listing**: Zero observed recruitment into the adult population for approximately three decades (~1960s–1990).
- **Secondary trigger**: A documented decline in the standing adult population over the most recent 10-year period (~1980–1990).
- The Service issued a public call for data on:
  1. Threats to the species (biological or commercial trade-related).
  2. Locations of additional populations and justification for [[Critical Habitat]] designation under Section 4 of the ESA.
  3. Range, distribution, and population size data.
  4. Current or planned anthropogenic activities that could impact the species.
- Public comment period deadline: **July 23, 1990**.
- Contact for further information: USFWS biologist Patricia Schrader, Grand Junction, Colorado office.
- This letter reflects broader conservation concerns for other endemic Colorado River Basin fishes facing similar recruitment failure, including [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]], often attributed to dam regulation, altered flow regimes, and introduced predatory fishes.
