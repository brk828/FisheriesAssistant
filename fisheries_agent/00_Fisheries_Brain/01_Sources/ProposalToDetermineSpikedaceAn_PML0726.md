# Spikedace (Meda fulgida) Proposed Threatened Listing and Critical Habitat Designation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #meda-fulgida

## Executive Summary

This 1985 *Federal Register* proposed rule, issued by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Albuquerque Regional Office), proposes listing the [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda Fulgida]]) as a **threatened** species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. It also proposes [[Critical Habitat]] in Arizona and New Mexico, a [[Special Rule]] allowing limited take, and corresponding amendments to 50 CFR Part 17. The species is endemic to the [[Gila River]] system and now survives in only a small fraction of its historic range, mainly because of habitat alteration, water diversion, dam proposals, and exotic fish species. The Service concluded that the species is not in immediate danger of extinction, so threatened rather than endangered status was judged appropriate.

## Background & Description

- **Discovery and taxonomy:** Collected in 1851 from the Rio San Pedro in Arizona and described from those specimens by Girard (1856, as cited in the reference list).
- **Morphology:** Small fish, less than 75 mm in length, with very silvery sides and spines in the dorsal and pelvic fins. Adults develop a brassy-golden color.
- **Habitat preference:** Inhabits moderate to large perennial streams, specifically shallow riffles with gravel and rubble substrates, and swift pools over sand or gravel. It lives only in flowing water.
- **Life history:** Recurrent flooding is described as very important to its life history and helps maintain its competitive edge over invading exotic fishes.
- **Historic range:** Once common throughout much of the Verde, Agua Fria, San Pedro, San Francisco, and Gila river systems (from Phoenix upstream), up to about 1,800 m elevation. The historic range included roughly 260 km of river.
- **Current range:** Restricted to approximately:
  - 24 km of [[Aravaipa Creek]] (Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona)
  - 73 km of the upper [[Gila River]] (Cliff-Gila Valley, Middle Box, and the lower ends of the West and Middle Forks; Grant and Catron Counties, New Mexico)
  - 57 km of the [[Verde River]] (Sullivan Lake downstream to just below the mouth of Sycamore Canyon; Yavapai County, Arizona)
- **Land ownership:** Mixed. Key holders include the Bureau of Land Management (including the Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness and the Middle Box Area of Critical Environmental Concern), the U.S. Forest Service (Gila National Forest, Prescott National Forest), The Nature Conservancy, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, and many private parcels.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat loss and alteration.** Damming, channelization, riparian destruction, channel downcutting, water diversion, and groundwater pumping have sharply reduced its distribution and abundance. The document estimates that only about 1 percent of the historic range currently supports populations (one passage of the scanned text gives a figure that is partly illegible).

**Status of the native fish fauna.** Of the native Gila River fishes, about 35 percent are federally listed as endangered and another 35 percent are considered threatened or endangered by Arizona, New Mexico, or the American Fisheries Society. Spikedace has been extirpated from much of its former range, including parts of the Salt River, the San Pedro River (except Aravaipa Creek), and the Agua Fria drainage.

**Dam and water projects:**
- A proposed high dam at the Conner site on the Gila mainstem (part of the [[Central Arizona Project]] Upper Gila Water Supply Study by the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]]) could inundate up to 29 km of river, about 10 percent of the Gila's existing range, and affect habitat downstream by changing temperature, bedload, and flow regimes.
- A small dam at the same site would inundate an estimated 14 km of river.
- The floodplain storage alternative would eliminate about 18 km of aquatic habitat.
- Past dam construction on the Salt, Verde, and Gila rivers caused complete extirpation of spikedace downstream of those dams.
- On the Verde, the city of Prescott and the Yavapai-Prescott Indian Reservation jointly proposed a gallery diversion about 4 km below [[Sullivan Lake]], with a maximum rate of 13 cubic feet per second. Average natural monthly discharge near the point is about 6 to 10 cfs or less for 10 months of the year, so the diversion would be significant.

**Exotic predators and competitors.** Predation was historically not a significant factor, but the introduction of exotic predatory fishes has increased its importance. Probable predators in the Gila and Verde include native [[Roundtail Chub]] and exotic black and yellow bullheads, channel catfish, green sunfish, [[Flathead Catfish]], smallmouth and largemouth bass, and rainbow and brown trout. Reservoirs increase predation by favoring exotic predators and supplying them from stocked fisheries.

**Competition with [[Red Shiner]].** The red shiner (*Notropis lutrensis*) is spreading through the Gila system and appears to be displacing spikedace under certain conditions, particularly where flood regimes have been disrupted by impoundment. Red shiner is absent from Aravaipa Creek, present in the Verde with spikedace, and occurs in the upper Gila as far upstream as Cliff, New Mexico.

## Key Findings

### Proposed Actions
1. **List as threatened** under the Endangered Species Act (based on the Service's review of past, present, and future threats).
2. **Designate critical habitat** in four areas (see below).
3. **Adopt a special rule** under Section 4(d), which would allow take for educational, scientific, enhancement, zoological exhibition, and related conservation purposes, and incidental to state-permitted recreational angling if the fish is immediately returned to its habitat.

### Critical Habitat Proposed
- **Aravaipa Creek, Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona:** perennial stream, approximately 24 km (BLM and private lands).
- **Verde River, Yavapai County, Arizona:** approximately 67 km, from about 0.8 km below the Sycamore Creek confluence to Sullivan Lake (Forest Service, private, and state lands).
- **Sycamore Creek, Yavapai County, Arizona:** approximately 1.5 km near the Verde confluence.
- **Gila River, Grant and Catron Counties, New Mexico:** three sections totaling about 73 km (Forest Service, BLM, New Mexico Game and Fish, and private lands).

### Status Under State Law
- **New Mexico:** listed as an endangered species, Group 2, which prohibits taking except under a scientific collecting permit. New Mexico bait rules allow the use of red shiner and other minnows as bait in spikedace areas, which the document identifies as encouraging the spread of exotic species.
- **Arizona:** listed as a threatened species, Group 3, with no special protection beyond angling restrictions. Neither state protects the habitat on which the species depends.

### Threat Assessment
- **Habitat destruction and modification** (dams, diversions, grazing, mining, and watershed disturbance) is the principal threat.
- **Overutilization** for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes is not known to be a threat.
- **Disease and predation** are significant under altered habitat conditions, though not in good habitat.
- **Other factors** include exotic species introduction and inadequate regulatory mechanisms.

### Federal Obligations
- **Section 7 consultation** may be needed for Forest Service lands along the Gila and Verde rivers if grazing, timbering, or recreational activities change. Little federal activity is expected in the BLM Area of Critical Environmental Concern on the upper Gila.
- Section 7 consultation would also apply to any federally funded projects in the Cliff-Gila Valley, and to Bureau of Reclamation water development on the upper Gila and Verde.
- Actions that reduce minimum flows, significantly alter the natural flow regime, change water chemistry, or introduce exotic predators could adversely modify critical habitat.

### Regulatory Notes
- No environmental assessment was required under the National Environmental Policy Act. A notice was published in the *Federal Register* on October 25, 1984 (49 FR 49244).
- Economic impacts of critical habitat designation will be considered, with the final rule incorporating public comments.

### Public Comment and Dates
- **Comments due:** August 19, 1985
- **Public hearing requests due:** August 2, 1985, to the Regional Director, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, P.O. Box 1306, Albuquerque, New Mexico 87103
- Comments are solicited on threats, additional populations, critical habitat, planned activities, and foreseeable economic impacts.
