# Fishes of Nevada

#ichthyology #conservation #endangered-species #native-fish #desert-ecosystems #species-inventory #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1984 reference document from the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] provides a comprehensive inventory of Nevada's native and introduced fish species, documenting the state's unique desert aquatic fauna and conservation challenges. Nevada harbors 61 native fish species and subspecies across relict river systems and closed basins, with 11 classified as federally endangered, one as threatened, 20 as state sensitive, seven as native game fish, and 16 as unprotected. An additional 27 introduced game fish and 20 nongame fish species occur statewide. Eleven fish species representing five genera are documented as extinct. The sensitive desert ecosystem and water scarcity have placed many aquatic species in jeopardy, with isolated springs and disjunct stream sections serving as critical habitat for endemic populations.

## Background & Description

The [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] operates under statutory regulation and Commission policy to preserve, protect, manage, and restore wildlife contributing to aesthetic, recreational, and economic values. Nevada's distinctive aquatic life evolved in relict river systems and closed basins, creating unique endemic populations adapted to desert conditions. The document follows taxonomic nomenclature from the [[American Fisheries Society]] Special Publication No. 12, Fourth Edition (1980), with historic baseline information from La Rivers' "Fishes and Fisheries in Nevada" (1962).

Protected endemic fish species face detriment from habitat changes, restricted native ranges, population declines from competition or predation, and decreases in available water habitat. Introduced species, placed in waters with little regard to potential impacts on native aquatic life, generally prove better competitors than native species. The state maintains obligations to preserve these species for scientific study and public enjoyment.

Nevada also harbors a broad amphibian, crustacean, and mollusk community that remains inadequately understood, with many taxa indiscriminately introduced without concern for native species protection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Nevada's aquatic ecosystems exist within a desert environment characterized by water scarcity and isolated habitats. The [[Lahontan Drainage]], [[Colorado River Drainage]], [[Columbia River Drainage]], and [[Bonneville Drainage]] represent the major hydrological systems supporting fish populations. Critical habitats include:

- [[Pyramid Lake]] - historic home to extinct [[Pyramid Lake Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri smaragdus*)
- [[Devil's Hole]] - sole habitat for [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*)
- [[Ash Meadows]] - supports multiple endemic pupfish and springfish populations
- [[White River]] system - harbors endemic spinedace, springfish, and desert sucker subspecies
- Isolated thermal springs throughout southern Nevada

The sensitive nature of desert ecosystems makes these aquatic habitats particularly vulnerable to disturbance. Closed basin systems create evolutionary isolation, resulting in distinct subspecies and populations with limited dispersal capability. Water availability directly determines habitat extent and population viability.

## Key Findings

**Extinct Species (11 taxa):**
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) - extinct in Nevada
- [[Ash Meadows Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys merriami*)
- [[Pahrump Ranch Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos pahrump*)
- [[Raycraft Ranch Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos concavus*)
- [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] Pyramid Lake race (*Salmo clarki henshawi*)
- [[Pyramid Lake Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri smaragdus*)
- [[Tahoe Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri regalis*)
- [[King Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) - historically present, now only in other portions of historic range
- [[Pahranagat Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki intermedius*)
- [[Pahranagat Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda altivelis*)
- [[Independence Valley Chub]] (*Gila bicolor isolata*)
- [[Grass Valley Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus reliquus*)

**Federal and State Endangered Species (11 taxa):**
- [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*)
- [[Moapa Dace]] (*Moapa coriacea*)
- [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta jordani*)
- [[Colorado Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
- [[Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos latos*)
- [[Ash Meadows Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis mionectes*)
- [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*)
- [[Warm Spring Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis pectoralis*)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*)
- [[Amargosa Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus nevadensis*)

**State Protected Species (8 taxa):**
- [[Desert Dace]] (Soldier Meadows) (*Eremichthys acros*)
- [[Big Spring Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis pratensis*)
- [[Virgin River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*)
- [[White River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda albivallis*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[White River Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki intermedius*)
- [[White River Springfish]] (*Crenichthys baileyi* ssp.)
- [[Railroad Valley Springfish]] (*Crenichthys nevadae*)

**Federal Threatened Species (1 taxon):**
- [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*)

**State Sensitive Species (20 taxa):**
Multiple subspecies of [[Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), [[Relict Dace]] (*Relictus solitarius*), [[Redband Trout]] (*Salmo* sp.), and [[Bonneville Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki utah*)

**Native Game Fish (7 species):**
- [[Bull Trout]] (*Salvelinus confluentus*)
- [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*)
- [[Yellowstone Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki lewisi*)
- [[Bonneville Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki utah*)
- [[Redband Trout]] (*Salmo* sp.)
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri* ssp.)
- [[Mountain Whitefish]] (*Prosopium williamsoni*)

**Introduced Game Fish (27 species):**
Including [[Kokanee Salmon]], [[Lake Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Black Crappie]], and various other salmonids, catfish, bass, and sunfish species

**Native Unprotected Species (16 species):**
Various suckers (*Catostomus* spp.), squawfish, chubs, shiners, dace, and sculpins distributed across Lahontan, Columbia River, Bonneville, and Colorado River drainages

**Introduced Unprotected Species (20 species):**
Including [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), various minnows, livebearers (guppies, mollies, swordtails), [[Grass Carp]], and multiple cichlid and tilapia species established in southern warm springs and urban waters

The document emphasizes that present life history and status of many species remain unknown, with little information on habitat conditions necessary for survival. Management of native fish species is required to perpetuate their existence for scientific study and public enjoyment.
