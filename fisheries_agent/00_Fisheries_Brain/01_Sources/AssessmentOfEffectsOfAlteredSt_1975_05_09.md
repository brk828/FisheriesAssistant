# Assessment of Effects of Altered Stream Flow Characteristics on Fish and Wildlife

## Executive Summary
This 1975 proposal by Enviro Control, Inc., submitted to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in response to RFP No. FWS-8-175, outlines a research plan to evaluate how [[Altered Stream Flow]] regimes below dams and diversions affect fish and wildlife populations. Rather than presenting original species-level findings, the document is a **methodological framework** proposing to select 30+ major water development projects across two western U.S. regions, conduct in-depth case studies (via record review, interviews, and site visits), and generate recommendations to improve [[In-Stream Flow Requirements]] methodologies and project evaluation processes for future and existing water projects.

## Background & Description
- **Objective**: Determine actual downstream effects of altered flow characteristics on fish and wildlife, evaluate the success of methodologies used to predict these effects, and identify biological/nonbiological constraints on their success.
- **Study regions** (Figure 1):
  - **Region 2 – Pacific Northwest**: Washington, Oregon, Idaho
  - **Region 3 – Rocky Mountain**: Montana, Wyoming, Utah, Colorado, Arizona, New Mexico
- **Scale**: At least 30 major projects and 50–100 associated in-stream flows per region, selected for adequate data availability and representativeness of physical settings, project purposes, and sponsoring organizations.
- **Structure of work** (4 tasks):
  1. **Task 1 – Select Candidate Projects** (700 person-hours, ~2 months): Identify potential projects using FWS/BSFW records, USGS hydrologic data, and interviews with field biologists and state fish and game departments; develop weighted selection criteria; finalize and document project list.
  2. **Task 2 – Conduct Case Studies** (4,200 person-hours, ~6 months): Develop interview guide, review existing data, conduct telephone interviews, perform on-site visits to regional agency offices, and synthesize findings per project (description, flow recommendations, implementation status, conclusions).
  3. **Task 3 – Develop Recommendations** (1,980 person-hours, ~2 months): Analyze case study findings by region, project purpose, sponsoring organization, physical setting, flow/release type, methodology used, constraint type, and degree of implementation/success; identify problems and constraints; recommend improved procedures for [[In-Stream Flow Methodology]] and institutional arrangements.
  4. **Final Report** (due 30 June 1976): Executive summary, methods, data presentation, analysis, and conclusions/recommendations, including case study appendices.

- **Key personnel**: Dr. Donald M. MacArthur (overall management), Dr. Louis C. Peltier (technical direction/Analysis Team lead), Dr. Alex Hershaft (Director of Environmental Studies), Gerald C. Horak (Project Coordinator/Field Team lead). Senior Review Panel included Howard L. Cook, Dr. Robert K. Davis, Dr. John F. Orsborn, and Dr. Lawrence B. Slobodkin (fish and wildlife ecology authority).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Core ecological issue**: Dams and diversions alter natural [[Streamflow Regime]] characteristics (timing, magnitude, duration of flows), with downstream consequences for fish and wildlife habitat quality and availability.
- **Pre- vs. post-project comparison**: The study design emphasizes comparing biological and hydrologic conditions before and after project implementation to assess real-world (not just predicted) ecological effects — a form of [[Ex Post Evaluation]] of mitigation effectiveness.
- **Institutional and multi-agency context**: Case studies span federal agencies (FWS, Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, Army Corps of Engineers, Soil Conservation Service), state fish and game departments, and project sponsors, reflecting the many stakeholders influencing [[Fish and Wildlife Mitigation]] outcomes.
- **Geographic/habitat diversity**: Physical settings across the Pacific Northwest and Rocky Mountain regions vary in climate, hydrology, and stream morphology, requiring representative sampling of project types (e.g., irrigation diversions, hydropower dams) and purposes.
- **Data sources considered ecologically relevant**:
  - Hydrologic records of flows pre- and post-project (USGS)
  - In-stream flow recommendations and the methods used to derive them
  - Field notes of biologists who generated flow recommendations
  - Agency and independent consultant reports on project impacts
  - Records of stakeholder (state agency, sponsor) reactions and follow-up investigations

## Key Findings
*Note: As a research proposal, this document does not contain empirical ecological results; the "findings" below represent the proposed analytical framework and anticipated deliverables rather than completed data.*

- The study proposes a **systematic evaluation framework** to assess the real-world effectiveness of in-stream flow methodologies used historically to protect fish and wildlife below dams/diversions — addressing a recognized gap between predicted and actual ecological outcomes of [[Water Resources Development]] projects.
- Selection criteria for case studies require four minimum data elements per project: (1) pre/post hydrologic records, (2) in-stream flow recommendation records, (3) biologist field notes or contacts, (4) agency/consultant reports — establishing a **data-sufficiency standard** for future stream-flow impact assessments.
- Analytical classification variables proposed for cross-project comparison include: region/state, project purpose, sponsoring organization, physical setting type, flow/release type, methodology type, constraint type, and degree of implementation/success — a template for systematic [[Comparative Case Study Analysis]] in fish and wildlife flow research.
- The proposal references prior work by the team recommending revisions to the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]], indicating this project's intended real-world policy relevance (amendments were under consideration by the U.S. House Subcommittee on Fisheries and Wildlife Conservation and the Environment at time of writing).
- Anticipated final products include graphic representations correlating **altered flow effects** with physical setting, project purpose, sponsoring organization, and methodology — intended to generalize lessons for improving [[Fish and Wildlife Compatibility]] in future water development projects across the western United States.
