# Effects of Watershed Perturbation on Stream Potassium and Calcium Dynamics

#ichthyology #marine-biology #stream-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study, published in *Ecological Monographs* (1979), examines how changes to the terrestrial vegetation of three small headwater watersheds at [[Coweeta Hydrologic Laboratory]] alter the potassium (K) and calcium (Ca) dynamics of the streams draining them. The three watersheds compared are an [[Old Field Watershed]] (grass cover, later succession), a [[Pine Plantation Watershed]] (white pine planted in 1956), and an unperturbed [[Hardwood Forest Watershed]]. The authors combined litterfall, benthic standing crop, drift, emergence, and radiotracer (⁴⁷Ca/⁴⁵Ca-type and ¹³⁴Cs/⁸⁵Sr-type isotope) experiments on detritivores to estimate ingestion and elimination rates. The central conclusion is that perturbed streams process allochthonous inputs less efficiently physically but use them more biologically, and that stream ecosystems show high resilience, with recovery limited by the rate of leaf-litter inputs.

## Background & Description

Headwater streams are considered processors of organic matter from the surrounding riparian ecosystem rather than self-sustaining producers. Their energy base is [[Allochthonous Leaf Litter]], and their biological function is controlled externally by the watershed, a view summarized by Hynes (1975): "in every respect the valley rules the stream." Material cycling at a single point is negligible because of unidirectional flow, but within a reach, detritus is repeatedly ingested, egested, and passed downstream in a "rolling motion."

The study focused on four detritivore taxa:
- Nymphs of *Peltoperla maria* (Plecoptera)
- Nymphs of *Stenonema* sp. (Ephemeroptera)
- Larvae of *Tipula* sp. (Diptera)
- Crayfish (*Cambarus bartoni*)

Site characteristics (Coweeta, Nantahala Mountains, Southern Appalachians) included average annual precipitation of 170 cm at lower elevations to 250 cm on upper slopes, with less than 2% of precipitation falling as snow. Watershed areas were 6.86 ha (old field), 13.48 ha (white pine), and 12.46 ha (hardwood), all on northwest-facing slopes within 2.59 km².

The hardwood watershed was dominated by chestnut oak, red maple, scarlet oak, northern red oak, tulip poplar, pignut hickory, and black oak, with rhododendron and mountain laurel in the understory. The old field had a long manipulation history (riparian cutting in 1942, grass conversion in 1959, dolomitic limestone fertilization 1959–1965, herbicide kill in 1966–1968), followed by successional vegetation dominated by horseweed, fireweed, and black locust.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Nutrient roles.** Potassium is essential for enzyme activation and protein conformation and is rapidly assimilated from the gut; it is rarely limiting in streams. Calcium is structurally important (endoskeleton, exoskeleton, and physiology), has low gut permeability, and is excreted by insects as calcium carbonate granules. Stream Ca concentrations depend mainly on substrate rock type.

**Stability.** Following Webster et al. (1975), resistance (withstanding perturbation) and resilience (recovery) were treated as distinct, and the authors note that streams are typically highly resilient but poorly resistant. Rapid turnover from continual flushing and the annual input of allochthonous detritus contribute to resilience.

**Hydrology.** Flow frequency diagrams showed that the three streams had very similar hydrologic properties, even though the pine watershed reduced flow relative to the hardwood watershed. The old field stream showed greater flow stability at low flows, attributed to a larger groundwater reservoir.

**Temperature.** Mean annual temperature in 1972 was 12.1 °C in the old field stream and 10.8 °C in the hardwood forest stream. The old field stream had the greater fluctuations, including a maximum daily range of 6.7 °C versus 3.3 °C in the hardwood stream.

**Radiotracer methods.** ⁴⁵Ca-analogue (⁴⁷Ca) and ⁸⁵Sr tracers were used for Ca, and ¹³⁴Cs for K, because radioisotopes of K are poor tracers. Animals fed on tagged [[Birch Leaf Litter]] (*Betula lenta*) in aerated stream water, and elimination was followed by gamma counting.

## Key Findings

- **Watershed export.** All three watersheds showed net losses of both K and Ca. The old field had the greatest Ca loss, likely due to limestone fertilization in 1959.
- **Sediment losses were small.** Calcium sediment loss was about 1% of dissolved loss and K sediment loss about 0.1% of dissolved loss. Sediment losses were greatest from the old field.
- **Litterfall.** Autumn accounted for the largest share of annual litterfall: 77% in the hardwood forest, 62% in the pine plantation, and 56% in the old field. Total litterfall for hardwood (362 g m⁻²) and pine (321 g m⁻²) closely matched Cromack's (1972) measurements.
- **Litter nutrient content.** Mean annual K and Ca concentrations in litter were highest in the old field and lowest in the pine plantation. Pine litter (2.10 and 9.28 mg g⁻¹) was enriched relative to expectation, probably due to invading hardwoods.
- **Benthic standing crop.** Large particulate organic matter (LPOM) standing crop was highest in the hardwood forest and lowest in the old field stream. The hardwood and old field streams differed significantly (paired t test, α = 0.05).
- **Dominant insects.** *Peltoperla maria*, *Stenonema*, and tipulids together made up 73%, 56%, and 67% of total insect biomass in the old field, pine plantation, and hardwood streams, respectively. Tipulids were 56% of benthic insect biomass in the old field stream.
- **Crayfish.** Crayfish biomass was greatest in the old field and least in the hardwood stream, and exceeded insect detritivore biomass in all three streams, although rapid turnover meant their biomass overstated their impact.
- **Emergence.** Emergence was greatest from the hardwood stream and least from the pine stream. Annual emergence represented about 29% of average standing crop in the hardwood stream.
- **Elimination rates.** Crayfish eliminated ⁴⁵Ca and ⁸⁵Sr more slowly than any insect, consistent with slower turnover. Among insects, *Tipula* eliminated ⁴⁵Ca more rapidly, and *Peltoperla* eliminated ⁸⁵Sr more slowly than *Tipula* or *Stenonema*.
- **Consumptive indices.** Consumptive indices (d⁻¹) were 3.37 for *Peltoperla*, 2.12 for *Stenonema*, and 1.28 for *Tipula*, with crayfish about 0.027 d⁻¹ (values as reported in Table 10).
- **Ecosystem-level conclusion.** Perturbed streams had less efficient physical processing of inputs but greater biological utilization, and recovery was limited mainly by the recovery rate of allochthonous inputs. Watershed perturbation did not substantially alter the hydrologic character of the streams, and the authors argue that the observed changes reflect biology, timing, and litterfall composition more than hydrology.
