## Executive Summary
This document is an interagency coordination memorandum (not a primary data paper) from BIO/WEST, Inc. to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), summarizing a February 1993 meeting on the joint monitoring strategy for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. The memo outlines plans for coordinated field sampling of nearshore and backwater habitats to inform flow management recommendations for [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. While it contains no raw morphometric or population data, it defines the sampling protocols, life-stage targets, and habitat classifications that would generate future humpback chub datasets.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological measurements are reported in this memo. However, the sampling protocol establishes size/age-class targets relevant to future morphological and growth studies:
- **Target size class**: Young [[Humpback Chub]] of age groups 0, I, and II, up to approximately **150 mm total length (TL)**.
- **Marking method**: Fin-clip identification scheme used to track cohorts and movement:
  - **Dorsal fin punch** — fish captured **above** the Lava-Chuar reach.
  - **Caudal fin punch** — fish captured **below** the Lava-Chuar reach.
  - A "refined punch combination" was planned to allow finer-scale hypothesis testing (e.g., seasonal/diel movement patterns).
- Length-frequency data collection was planned as a shared metric between agencies to assess population size structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The memo emphasizes habitat use patterns central to humpback chub ecology in the Grand Canyon:
- **Primary habitats of interest**:
  - [[Eddy Complexes]] and associated return channels
  - [[Backwaters]] (return channel habitats)
  - Nearshore [[Shoreline Habitat|shoreline habitats]]
- **Life stage habitat use**: Young chub (age 0–II, ≤150 mm TL) were the focus of shoreline habitat sampling to assess abundance, distribution, habitat use, and survival — suggesting these nearshore/backwater zones serve as critical nursery habitat.
- **Environmental gradients tested**: Sampling design explicitly incorporated:
  - **Diel variation** (day vs. night use of backwaters)
  - **Turbidity conditions** (turbid vs. clear water use)
- **Geomorphic context**: Fish distribution data were planned for integration via GIS with geomorphic mapping of eddy complexes (led by J. Schmidt), linking fish habitat use to physical channel morphology.
- **Management driver**: Understanding of habitat use was intended to directly inform flow recommendations for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations to protect the species and its ecosystem.

## Key Findings
- Coordination between [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[BIO/WEST, Inc.]] was formalized to unify field methods and reduce redundant/conflicting sampling efforts in Grand Canyon humpback chub studies.
- A joint field trip (AGF and BIO/WEST) was planned for July–August to sample eddy complexes and backwaters simultaneously.
- Sampling design would explicitly test **diel use** and **turbidity-dependent use** of backwater habitats by native fish.
- Data sharing via a computerized shared database after each field trip was proposed to enable cross-agency analysis of fish distribution by area/habitat, size (length-frequency), and recapture rates.
- AGF planned to modify their backwater sampling protocol from standard "A and B level" sampling to a "modified A" approach combined with opportunistic sampling.
- A standardized fin-clip marking scheme (dorsal above Lava-Chuar, caudal below Lava-Chuar) was adopted by AGF to match BIO/WEST's existing method, enabling combined-agency movement and mark-recapture analyses.
- Personnel exchanges between agencies were suggested as a future mechanism to improve methodological consistency, though not yet implemented at time of writing.
- Overall objective: improve understanding of humpback chub life history to support **rational flow management recommendations** for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to protect this endangered species and the broader Grand Canyon aquatic ecosystem.
