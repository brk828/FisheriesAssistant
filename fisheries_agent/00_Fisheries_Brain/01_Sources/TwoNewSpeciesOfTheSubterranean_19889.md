# Two New Species of the Subterranean Amphipod Genus Bahadzia (Hadziidae) from the Yucatan Peninsula, with Phylogeny and Biogeography

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hadziidae

## Executive Summary

This 1992 taxonomic paper by [[John R. Holsinger]] describes two new stygobiont amphipod species of the genus [[Bahadzia]] from [[Anchialine Caves]] on the northeastern Yucatan Peninsula and the adjacent island of [[Isla de Cozumel]], Quintana Roo, Mexico. It raises the genus total to seven species, provides a dichotomous key, and presents a cladistic analysis of phylogeny and biogeography. The author concludes that Bahadzia is most closely related to the Yucatan cave genera [[Mayaweckelia]] and [[Tuluweckelia]], that its species track geographically separate areas of endemism, and that Yucatan populations colonized present caves during the Pleistocene. Although the group is an amphipod crustacean rather than a fish, the paper addresses the subterranean aquatic ecosystem, and no fish taxa are described.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Bahadzia]] was erected by Holsinger & Yager (1985) for two species from anchialine caves in the Bahamas and Turks and Caicos Islands. Stock later added species from Haiti and the Bahamas. The new Yucatan records came from cave diving surveys in 1984–1987 of water-filled chambers along the northeastern coast of the peninsula and on Cozumel. Such caves have subterranean connections to the sea and are classed as anchialine habitats. Locally they are known as [[Cenotes]].

**Diagnostic features (generic):** Emended diagnosis includes 8–10 apical serrate or pectinate spines on the outer plate of maxilla 1, small inner lobes on the lower lip in some species, and small unstalked coxal gills in *B. latipalpus*.

**Bahadzia bozanici n. sp.**
- Holotype female, ca. 4.0 mm, deposited at USNM (239475); collected at Cueva Quebrada, Parque Chankanaab, Cozumel, September 1985.
- Known from one mainland cave (Carwash Cenote) and two Cozumel caves (Cueva Quebrada, Aereolita Cenote).
- Small to medium-sized; largest males 5.0 mm, largest females 6.0 mm.
- Distinguished by numerous facial setae on the inner plate of maxilla 2 (approximately 64) and numerous long setae on segment 6 of pereopod 6.

**Bahadzia setodactylus n. sp.**
- Holotype female, 10 mm, deposited at USNM (239476); from Xcan-ha Cenote (Cenote Roja), Cozumel.
- Known only from this single cave.
- Relatively large; largest male 9.0 mm, largest female 10.0 mm.
- Distinguished by a reduced right lacinia mobilis (single tooth), an elongate, heavily setose dactyl of pereopod 6 (35–40 fine setae in 10 clusters), and fewer telson spines.

**Dichotomous key:** The paper provides a seven-species key separating taxa by gnathopod 2 palm spines, maxilla 2 facial setae (approximately 64 in *B. bozanici* versus fewer than 50 in others), uropod 2 peduncle spines, and telson spine counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and water chemistry**
- *B. bozanici* at Cueva Quebrada occurs below a halocline in mesohaline water (19 ppt) at 2–4 m depth. The cave has approximately 23.5 km of surveyed passage, five surface openings, and a maximum depth of about 12 m.
- At Carwash Cenote (mainland) the few specimens came from euhaline water (35 ppt) at 21–23 m.
- At Aereolita Cenote, specimens came from ceiling pockets in brackish/marine water above a halocline.
- *B. setodactylus* at Xcan-ha Cenote lies at about 52 m maximum depth, with salinity about 34 ppt. The type series was collected below a halocline at about 12 m.

**Reproduction:** Only two of approximately 60 sampled females of *B. bozanici* were ovigerous.

**Associated fauna:** Co-occurring stygobionts include cirolanid isopods (*Bahalana mayana*), thermosbaenaceans (*Tulumella unidens*), remipedes (*Speleonectes tulumensis*), mysids (*Stygiomysis* sp.), shrimps (*Typhlatya* sp.), and the fish *Ogilbia pearsei* at Carwash Cenote. Cueva Quebrada also holds many epigean immigrants, including marine amphipods of the families Phoxocephalidae and Synopiidae.

**Named etymology:** *B. bozanici* honors Jeffery Bozanic; *B. setodactylus* refers to the setose dactyl.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species described:** *B. bozanici* (three localities) and *B. setodactylus* (one locality), raising the genus to seven species.
2. **Three areas of endemism:** Bahamian archipelago (4 species), Hispaniola (1 species, *B. latipalpus*), and northeastern Yucatan plus Cozumel (2 species). The Bahamas and Yucatan areas are 800–1000 km apart.
3. **Phylogeny:** Nested species subsets correspond closely to geographic areas. The *bozanici–setodactylus* pair forms a sister clade restricted to the Yucatan region.
4. **Closest relatives:** Bahadzia is more closely allied with [[Mayaweckelia]] and [[Tuluweckelia]] than with any other hadziid genus.
5. **Ecological divergence:** *B. latipalpus* inhabits shallow limnic groundwater rather than anchialine caves.
6. **Vicariance favored:** The distributions of Bahadzia and four other small crustacean genera (*Speleonectes*, *Tulumella*, *Bahalana*, *Agostocaris*), plus the ostracode *Danielopolina*, are nearly congruent, forming a generalized track from the Bahamas across Cuba to Yucatan. The author argues that these taxa lack dispersing larval stages, so vicariance is more persuasive than dispersal.
7. **Shallow-water origin:** Biogeographic data support a shallow-water origin of anchialine stygobionts, in line with the hypothesis of [[Stock]] and [[Danielopol]].
8. **Colonization timing:** The author hypothesizes that Yucatan Bahadzia colonized present caves during or after Pleistocene development, driven by sea-level changes. Karst on both Cozumel and the mainland has a similar history of at least 125,000 years.
9. **Cozumel–mainland channel:** The deep channel (about 400 m deep, about 55 km wide) separating Cozumel from the mainland was likely formed by movement along the Rio Hondo Fault, possibly as old as Late Cretaceous or early Tertiary. The author favors recent simultaneous invasion of caves on both sides over dispersal or long-term isolation to explain the shared *B. bozanici* populations.
10. **Predictions:** Further anchialine cave exploration on Cuba, Hispaniola, and Jamaica is expected to yield new species of these crustacean groups.
