# Studien an Crustacea Subterranea (Isopoda, Syncarida, Copepoda) aus dem Norden Portugals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #crustacea #subterranean-fauna

## Executive Summary

This 1969 monograph, in German and published by the [[Instituto De Zoologia Dr Augusto Nobre]] in Porto, describes new subterranean crustaceans from interstitial groundwater along rivers in northern Portugal. The authors, Wolfram Noodt (Kiel) and M. Helena Galhano (Porto), report a species-rich groundwater fauna in a small area, comparable in density to some tropical regions. They describe one isopod, three bathynellid taxa, three parabathynellid species and five harpacticoid copepod species, all new to science or new to Portugal, and discuss their biogeographic significance for a region unaffected by Pleistocene glaciation. The document also contains the publication's reference list and a catalogue of the series' earlier issues, which are summarized below.

## Background & Description

The paper is one of the Publicações of the Instituto de Zoologia «Dr. Augusto Nobre» (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto, No. 107). The volume's opening pages list about 80 earlier publications from 1940 to 1969, covering arachnids, Lepidoptera, isopods, amphipods, cave biology and fisheries (including sardine studies by Macedo-Cruz and a paper on [[Bacterial Gill Disease]] in Europe). Several earlier works by J. M. Braga described Portuguese [[Synasellus]] and related isopods.

The research sought to document the limnic mesopsammon (groundwater fauna of sandy and gravel sediments). Sampling began in spring 1969 under funding from NATO. Animals were collected by digging in sand and gravel on riverbanks, washed out, sorted live or fixed, and mounted in glycerin. Specimens and type material were deposited partly in the authors' collections and partly in the Porto institute.

Seven stations were sampled between 5 April 1967 and 29 March 1969 at riverbank groundwater on the Rio Lima, Rio Cavado, Rio Douro (four sites, including Zebreiros, Entre-os-Rios and Régua) and Rio Mondego, at water temperatures of roughly 8 to 17 °C. The authors note that March 1969 was unusually rainy, which likely disturbed the fauna and explains why many species were represented by only single specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Yellow gravels and sands along river banks in northern Portugal, considered an ideal substrate for limnic mesopsammon.
- **Biogeography:** The authors argue that western Iberia, being geologically stable and little affected by Pleistocene climate change, preserves a relict, possibly Tertiary, groundwater fauna. Bathynellacea may be even older.
- **Comparison:** The density of species in a small area was described as unusually high, approaching conditions in tropical areas such as Amazonia (cited work Noodt 1963, 1970).
- **Disturbance:** Exceptional flooding in March 1969 may have affected the fauna sampled.
- **Groups:** Asellids (Synasellus group, endemic to Portugal), bathynellids (stammeri group, widespread across the Palaearctic), parabathynellids (many plesiomorphic traits) and parastenocaridids (first record for Portugal) make up the community. Associated taxa included Canthocamptidae, Cyclopoidea and Ostracoda.

## Key Findings

**Isopoda, Asellota, Asellidae**
- [[Synasellus Barcelensis]] n. sp. was described from 25 specimens (6 males, 2 females and juveniles) collected 27 March 1969 from the Rio Cavado at Barcelos. The type is a female of 1.84 mm body length.
- Its morphology lies between [[Synasellus]] and [[Psammasellus]] (Psammasellus capitatus, a monotypic species), so the authors suspect the two genera may be one, with the distinguishing traits having only specific value. A decision was deferred pending more mesopsammal material.
- This is the first direct find of a Synasellus species in the mesopsammon; the genus was previously known only from wells, springs and caves.

**Bathynellacea, Bathynellidae**
- [[Bathynella Stammeri]] tameguensis n. ssp. was described from numerous specimens at Entre-os-Rios (5 April 1967), named after the Rio Tâmega. Type: female, 0.74 mm.
- Provisional taxa B. s. occidentalis (Areinho de Valbom) and B. s. reguensis (Régua) are named but not fully described. Their differences lie chiefly in the 8th pereiopod and the uropod spines.
- The authors conclude that western Europe holds only the stammeri group, and that B. natans is apparently absent from western Europe.

**Parabathynellidae**
- [[Parabathynella Lusitanica]] barcelensis n. ssp. (Rio Cavado, Barcelos, 7 March 1969; type female 3.44 mm) differs from the nominate species in uropod, furca and antennule characters, and is considered more plesiomorphic.
- [[Parabathynella Cavadoensis]] n. sp. (Rio Cavado; type female 1.4 mm; very numerous specimens) is related to P. fagei and shows mixed plesiomorphic and apomorphic traits. Maturity was estimated at about 1.0 mm for females and 0.98 mm for males. The smallest specimens (0.51 mm) already had complete segmentation.
- [[Parabathynella Minuta]] n. sp. (Rio Douro at Zebreiros, 6 March 1969; 7 females and 1 male) lacks pereiopod VII, a trait that may have arisen more than once. A key (Table 3) separates the species with a missing P. VII.
- Unidentified Parabathynella specimens were also found at Régua (1 female, 0.8 mm) and on the Rios Cavado and Mondego.

**Harpacticoida, Parastenocarididae** (first record of the family from Portugal)
- [[Parastenocaris Mateusi]] n. sp. (Rio Cavado, 7 March 1969; also found in 3 females at Coimbra). Dedicated to Amílcar Mateus. About 10% of females have leaf-shaped furcal setae. Closest relative: P. dianae (western Pyrenees).
- [[Parastenocaris Xyrophora]] n. sp. (Rio Cavado; also Coimbra). Named for its razor-like apophysis on the male P. III exopod. Belongs to the nana group.
- [[Parastenocaris Cruzi]] n. sp. (Rio Cavado, male only known). Dedicated to J. Amorim Macedo-Cruz. Belongs to the proserpina group; closest to P. cantabrica.
- [[Parastenocaris Lusitanica]] n. sp. (Rio Douro at Zebreiros, 6 March 1969; male only). Belongs to the minuta group.
- [[Parastenocaris Conimbrigensis]] n. sp. (Rio Mondego at Coimbra, 9 March 1969; 2 males and 2 females). Belongs to the fontinalis group and is named after Roman Conimbriga.
- Several additional species, represented by few specimens, were not described.

**Summary and conclusions**
- The authors state that nearly all European parastenocaridid species groups occur in Portugal, and that the Portuguese groundwater fauna probably changed little during the Pleistocene, making the region biogeographically important.
- Taxonomic work is to continue on material that has been collected but not yet analysed.
