# Anomalies Encountered in Freshwater and Marine Fishes from the Eastern United States

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-biology #teratology #Sciaenidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 survey by Burgess and Schwartz documents **skeletal and pigmentation anomalies** across 23 fish species collected from freshwater and marine habitats of the Eastern United States, with the majority of specimens originating from North Carolina waters. The study spans **4 freshwater families** ([[Cyprinidae]], [[Ictaluridae]], [[Centrarchidae]], [[Percidae]]) and **11 marine families**, with [[Sciaenidae]] (drums/croakers) showing the highest representation (6 species affected). The paper serves as a catalog of naturally occurring developmental and traumatic abnormalities — including jaw deformities, pugheadedness, spinal curvatures (lordosis/kyphosis), vertebral fusion (ankylosis), fin deformities, and partial ambicoloration — observed opportunistically during ichthyological surveys, rather than an experimental or causal study.

## Background & Description

The anomalies reported fall into several distinct morphological categories:

- **Jaw anomalies** — observed in *Leiostomus xanthurus* (#21) and *Micropogon undulatus* (#22).
- **Snout anomalies** — observed in *Micropogon undulatus* (#22).
- **Pugheadedness** (foreshortening/blunting of the cranium and snout) — observed in *Notropis chrysocephalus* (#2), *Etheostoma olmstedi atromaculatum* (#5), *Brevoortia tyrannus* (#7), *Bairdiella chrysura* (#17), *Larimus fasciatus* (#20), and *Leiostomus xanthurus* (#21).
- **Lordosis** (ventral spinal curvature) — observed in *Notropis telescopus* (#1), *Alosa mediocris* (#6), *Anchoa mitchilli* (#8), *Morone americana* (#15), and *Cynoscion nebulosus* (#18).
- **Kyphosis** (dorsal spinal curvature) — observed in *Membras martinica* (#10), *Menidia menidia* (#11), *Morone americana* (#15), and *Cynoscion regalis* (#19).
- **Ankylosis** (vertebral fusion) — observed in *Regalecus glesne* (#13), *Leiostomus xanthurus* (#21), and *Micropogon undulatus* (#22).
- **Deformed or missing fins** — observed in *Ictalurus catus* (#3), *Lepomis gibbosus* (#4), *Brevoortia tyrannus* (#7), *Urophycis regius* (#9), *Zenopsis ocellatus* (#12), *Macrorhamphosus scolopax* (#14), *Lagodon rhomboides* (#16), *Leiostomus xanthurus* (#21), and *Micropogon undulatus* (#22).
- **Partial ambicoloration** (bilateral pigmentation abnormality typical of flatfish, where the "blind" side develops pigment normally restricted to the "eyed" side) — observed uniquely in the bothid flatfish *Scophthalmus aquosus* (#23), consistent with known [[flatfish pigmentation anomalies]].

Several species exhibited **multiple co-occurring anomalies** (e.g., *Leiostomus xanthurus* and *Micropogon undulatus* each display jaw, ankylosis, and fin anomalies simultaneously), suggesting either compounding developmental disruption or shared traumatic/environmental etiology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The affected species span a broad range of habitats and trophic contexts, reflecting the eastern U.S. coastal-freshwater continuum:

- **Freshwater stream/river fishes**: *Notropis telescopus*, *Notropis chrysocephalus* ([[Cyprinidae]] — minnows/shiners); *Ictalurus catus* ([[Ictaluridae]] — catfish); *Lepomis gibbosus* ([[Centrarchidae]] — sunfish); *Etheostoma olmstedi atromaculatum* ([[Percidae]] — darter).
- **Estuarine and inshore marine fishes**: *Alosa mediocris*, *Brevoortia tyrannus* ([[Clupeidae]] — herrings/menhaden), *Anchoa mitchilli* ([[Engraulidae]] — anchovy), *Menidia menidia*, *Membras martinica* ([[Atherinidae]] — silversides), *Morone americana* ([[Percichthyidae]] — white perch), *Lagodon rhomboides* ([[Sparidae]] — pinfish), and multiple [[Sciaenidae]] (drums, croakers, spot, seatrout).
- **Continental shelf/deep-water and pelagic fishes**: *Urophycis regius* ([[Gadidae]] — hake), *Zenopsis ocellatus* ([[Zeidae]] — dory), *Regalecus glesne* ([[Regalecidae]] — oarfish, a deep-pelagic species notable for its rarity in anomaly records), and *Macrorhamphosus scolopax* ([[Centriscidae]] — snipefish).
- **Demersal flatfish**: *Scophthalmus aquosus* ([[Bothidae]] — windowpane flounder), notable for exhibiting the sole case of ambicoloration.

The predominance of North Carolina specimens situates this work within the broader context of [[estuarine fish health monitoring]] along the Atlantic coast, with only 4 of the 23 species originating from outside North Carolina waters.

## Key Findings

- **23 fish species** across **15 families** (4 freshwater, 11 marine) exhibited detectable anomalies in this survey.
- [[Sciaenidae]] was the most heavily affected family, with **6 species** showing anomalies (spot, croaker, seatrout, weakfish, banded drum, silver perch).
- **Fin deformities/absence** was the most frequently observed anomaly category, affecting 9 of the 23 species.
- **Pugheadedness** and **lordosis** were the next most common anomaly types, each affecting 5–6 species.
- **Ambicoloration** was uniquely restricted to the flatfish *Scophthalmus aquosus*, consistent with the known developmental basis of pigment-side determination in [[Pleuronectiformes]].
- **Ankylosis** (vertebral fusion) was notably documented in *Regalecus glesne*, an unusual and rarely studied deep-sea species, highlighting the broad taxonomic and ecological reach of the survey.
- The overwhelming majority (19 of 23 species) of anomalous specimens were collected from **North Carolina waters**, underscoring the regional focus of the Institute of Marine Sciences' long-term ichthyofaunal surveys.
- This work functions as a **baseline teratological catalog**, useful for comparison in later studies of pollution-linked or environmentally induced skeletal anomalies in [[fish population health assessment]].
