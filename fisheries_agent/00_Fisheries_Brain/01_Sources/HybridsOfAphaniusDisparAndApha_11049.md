# Hybrids of Aphanius dispar and Aphanius mento (Cyprinodontidae: Pisces)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This 1978 study by Goren & Rychwalski documents successful reciprocal hybridization experiments between two Israeli cyprinodont (killifish) species, [[Aphanius mento mento]] and [[Aphanius dispar richardsoni]], which are sympatric at Ein Fashkha near the Dead Sea. Controlled aquarium crosses produced viable F1 hybrids, though at drastically reduced fecundity compared to pure crosses (1–8 offspring vs. 40–100 for parental species), and attempts to produce F2 or backcross generations failed. The hybrids exhibited pronounced sexual dimorphism in phenotypic affinity: male hybrids resembled [[Aphanius mento]] in morphology, behavior, and breeding coloration, while female hybrids more closely resembled [[Aphanius dispar]]. The paper raises the open question of whether such hybrids occur and persist in wild mixed populations, and proposes breeding-season coloration as the most reliable field identification method.

## Background & Description
- **Study taxa**: *Aphanius mento mento* (Heckel), *A. mento striptus* (Goren), *A. dispar richardsoni* (Boulenger), and their experimental hybrids.
- **Fin ray counts**: Hybrids show a **lower number of rays in the dorsal and anal fins** compared to *A. mento mento*, trending toward *A. dispar richardsoni* values.
- **Body proportions measured**: PH/TL (peduncle length/total length), SA/TL (snout–anal distance/total length), E/HL (eye diameter/head length), SD/TL, TH/TL, and others (see original Table 1).
  - Hybrids differ from *A. mento mento* in PH/TL, SA/TL, and E/HL.
  - Hybrids differ from *A. dispar richardsoni* only in PH/TL and SD/TL — indicating overall closer morphometric affinity to *A. dispar richardsoni*.
- **Dentition**: Male hybrid teeth show an **intermediate morphology** between the two parental species (illustrated in Fig. 2). Female hybrid teeth closely resemble those of *A. mento mento*.
- **Fish size in figures**: 48–51 mm (standard reference specimens).
- **Colour pattern (winter, non-breeding)**:
  - *A. mento mento* males: silvery sides, dark brown back.
  - *A. dispar richardsoni* males: silvery sides, grey/black-brown dorsum, irregular vertical dark bands.
  - Hybrid males: resemble *A. mento mento*.
- **Colour pattern (breeding season)** — key diagnostic differences:
  - *A. mento mento* males: blue-black body, silvery spots in lines on fins, irregular body spotting.
  - *A. dispar richardsoni* males: mottled white/brown narrow stripes, ventral blue tint, 2–3 vertical black stripes on caudal fin.
  - **Hybrid males**: blue-black body with bright narrow stripes on caudal/dorsal fins and bright large body spots — a distinct intermediate/mosaic pattern.
  - Females of both parental species and hybrids differ subtly; hybrid females show silvery sides with irregular dark areas and dark brown back, with no seasonal colour change (as in *A. dispar*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution context**: Three cyprinodont species occur in Israel — *A. mento* (widespread in freshwater systems), *A. fasciatus* (Mediterranean coastal, occasional freshwater penetration), and *A. dispar* (shallow waters around the Sinai Peninsula, having colonized the Mediterranean via the Suez Canal).
- **Sympatry zone**: [[Ein Fashkha]], an area of ponds and springs at the northwestern Dead Sea, hosts *A. mento mento*, *A. mento striptus*, and *A. dispar richardsoni* together — the only known site of natural sympatry among these taxa discussed here.
- **Hybridization drivers**: Seasonal water-level fluctuations create small, temporarily isolated water bodies at Ein Fashkha, restricting mate choice and enabling either direct interspecific mating or indirect fertilization (eggs of one species fertilized by sperm of another in shared flowing water) — a mechanism previously documented in [[Barbus longiceps]] and [[Capoeta damascina]] (Steinitz & Ben Tuvia, 1959).
- **Broader hybrid zones**: Natural *A. dispar × A. fasciatus* hybrid populations are common along the eastern Mediterranean (e.g., Ashdod Harbour, Bardawil Lagoon), sometimes comprising mostly hybrids.
- **Predicted additional contact zones**: Lower reaches of rivers draining into the Persian Gulf and eastern Mediterranean, where *A. dispar* and *A. mento* ranges overlap (per Kosswig 1967 biogeographic mapping).
- **Salinity tolerance**: Experimental hybridization tanks used 18–25% seawater concentration, reflecting the euryhaline tolerance typical of the genus [[Aphanius]].

## Key Findings
1. **Interspecific hybridization is biologically possible**: Reciprocal crosses (♀ *A. mento mento* × ♂ *A. dispar richardsoni* and ♂ *A. mento mento* × ♀ *A. dispar richardsoni*) both produced viable offspring.
2. **Reduced fecundity**: Hybrid crosses yielded only 1–8 offspring versus 40–100 in normal conspecific reproduction — indicating a partial reproductive barrier.
3. **F1 sterility/failure to backcross**: All attempts to breed F2 or backcross generations from hybrids failed, despite apparently normal hybrid behavior.
4. **Sex-biased phenotypic inheritance**: Male hybrids behaviorally and morphologically resemble *A. mento* (territorial behavior), while female hybrids resemble *A. dispar* — suggesting sex-linked or sex-influenced expression of parental traits.
5. **Skewed sex ratios in crosses**: The cross ♂ *A. mento mento* × ♀ *A. dispar richardsoni* produced only female hybrids; the reciprocal cross produced a 1:2 male:female ratio.
6. **Diagnostic tool for wild populations**: Breeding-season colour pattern is proposed as the most reliable (but ephemeral) trait for identifying hybrids in the field, since it fades rapidly upon capture/preservation — complicating detection of natural hybrids in museum collections.
7. **Open ecological question**: Whether natural hybrids exist and persist in wild sympatric populations (e.g., Ein Fashkha, Persian Gulf/Mediterranean river mouths) remains unresolved and would require large-scale breeding-season sampling.
