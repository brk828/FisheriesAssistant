# Endangered Fish Principal Investigators Integration Meeting, Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (December 1, 1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] Upper Colorado Region, [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) office, circulates notes from the December 1, 1992 meeting of Endangered Fish Principal Investigators (PIs) held in Flagstaff, Arizona. The meeting aimed to identify available data from researchers, facilitate data transfer, and integrate research on endangered fishes of the Colorado River in Grand Canyon. Participating agencies were the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[BioWest]] (B/W), the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), and the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD). Field summaries, research status, and agreed commitments are recorded, along with action items on reporting, data exchange, and a possible population model.

## Background & Description

The document is an administrative record rather than a research paper. It contains a cover memorandum from Allen Haden (GCES), a list of meeting attendees and copy recipients (about 12 names across the agencies), and meeting notes organized by agency. The study focuses on [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), and native fishes such as [[Speckled Dace]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and [[Bluehead Sucker]] in the Colorado River mainstem and tributaries. The memo notes that the 1992 season was the start of a program with only two years of remaining field work, which created time pressure on permits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Mainstem Colorado River:** Catch was concentrated within a 16 km reach near the mouth of the [[Little Colorado River]]. Smaller aggregations occurred downstream at approximately river mile (RM) 127 (~50 fish), RM 119 (~20 fish), and RM 108 at Shinumo Creek (~30 fish).
- **Flow effects:** Extended high flows in 1992 reduced catch rates in the Little Colorado River and likely reduced larval drift catches. Young-of-year (YOY) catfish and fatheads were found above Atomizer after large flows.
- **Habitat use:** Adult fish were using eddies and return channels. Driftnet catch rates were higher in nearshore habitats, and spawning areas were thought to need clear water for identification.
- **Tributaries:** Paria, Bright Angel, Shinumo, Deer, Kanab, and Havasu creeks were sampled, with natives generally dominant and dace most common. Two humpback chub were caught at Shinumo Creek and one cutthroat trout at Bright Angel Creek.
- **Food web:** Stomach samples from about 40 fish were dominated by [[Gammarus]] and simuliids (black flies), with no strong correlation to drift samples.

## Key Findings

**Research status by agency**

- **USFWS (Owen Gorman):** 69 field days in 1992; 615 ASU hoopnets and 306 transects measured; 693 chubs captured, with speckled dace and bluehead suckers predominant. Catch rates were low in the Little Colorado River, presumably due to high flows. Permits were a priority.
- **BioWest (Rich Valdez):** Food habit study showed no strong link to drift. Benthic sampling in eddies and return channels was planned. Length histograms showed a bottleneck at ages 2–4, and the downstream distribution of young fish was to be described.
- **AGFD (Rob Clarkson, Glen Doster):** Driftnets were run 24 hours once monthly, with visual and dipnet sweeps for larvae. Photographic study of nearshore habitat was planned. Mainstem growth was slower, and 1992 YOY growth was poor. Minnow traps proved ineffective; seining caught far more fish. Estimated population was about 6,000 chubs over 150 mm in the Little Colorado River. Of 169 PIT tag records shared by ASU and B/W, about 30 gave useful movement data.
- **ASU (Paul Marsh, Michael Douglas, W.L. Minckley):** Masters theses covered Paria and Bright Angel creeks; Havasu Creek effort was increased. Habitat comparisons with upper basin work were recommended.

**Reproduction and propagation**

- Some spawning evidence occurred in the Little Colorado River inflow area.
- Propagation efforts had been unsuccessful; a bad batch of sperm diluent was suspected. No adult chubs were to be brought out in 1993. A propagation specialist was to be hired in January 1993, with facilities at [[Bubbling Springs Hatchery]].

**Commitments and action items**

1. AGFD to notify PIs and GCES of mainstem methodology changes.
2. Quarterly notification of data availability, with transmittal letters copied to GCES ACT members.
3. USFWS to supply macro habitat maps of the Little Colorado River to ASU, and monthly fish data.
4. ASU to supply quarterly hoopnet data updates to USFWS.
5. B/W tag information due by January 1, 1993; B/W 1992 data by December 15, 1992.
6. AGFD benthic data expected by spring 1993; humpback chub otolith study by December 31, 1992.
7. Base map of the Little Colorado River inflow area due by end of February 1993.
8. Next PI meeting planned for July 1993.
9. Valdez and others to evaluate a population model to identify data gaps.
