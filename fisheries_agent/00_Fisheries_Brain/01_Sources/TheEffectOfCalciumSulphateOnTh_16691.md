# The Effect of Calcium Sulphate on the Survival of Rainbow Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #ecotoxicology #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1962 study by Herbert and Wakeford, conducted at the Water Pollution Research Laboratory, investigated the tolerance of yearling [[Rainbow Trout]] to calcium sulphate (gypsum), a common constituent of industrial effluents (e.g., from sulphuric acid neutralization and phosphate/superphosphate manufacturing). Since gypsum has limited solubility (~2,000 p.p.m. at 18°C), the researchers tested both dissolved concentrations and suspensions of undissolved solids to simulate real-world discharge conditions. The study found that low-to-moderate concentrations (up to 3,163 p.p.m.) were entirely non-lethal over a 28-day exposure period, while higher concentrations (6,820–10,000 p.p.m.) caused significant mortality, establishing critical safety thresholds for effluent management.

## Background & Description
- **Test Subjects:** Yearling [[Rainbow Trout]], approximately 1 year old, 3–4 inches (7.6–10.2 cm) in length.
- **Physical Effects of Toxicant Exposure:**
  - No physical signs of disease or injury were observed in fish held in safe concentrations (≤3,163 p.p.m.) or in the highest lethal concentrations (6,820 and 10,000 p.p.m.).
  - In the 4,649 p.p.m. tank, intraspecific aggression was noted during the third week; 6 of the 10 fish that died exhibited **severe wounds on their tails and flanks**, suggesting fighting-related mortality rather than direct chemical toxicity confounded these specific results.
- **Behavioral Adaptation:** Fish required a 2-week training period involving graduated exposure to paddle-induced water turbulence to acclimate hatchery-reared trout to the circular current systems used in the aquaria, preventing stress-related health decline unrelated to the toxicant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Environmental Context:** [[Calcium Sulphate]] (gypsum) enters aquatic ecosystems primarily via industrial effluent, specifically:
  - Neutralization of waste sulphuric acid with lime.
  - Manufacturing processes for phosphoric acid and superphosphate (treatment of apatite/tricalcium phosphate with sulphuric acid).
- **Water Chemistry Parameters (Test Conditions):**
  - Temperature: 14.5–18°C
  - pH: 8.1
  - Dissolved Oxygen: 8.0–9.5 p.p.m.
- **Comparative Toxicology:** The paper references prior work by [[Doudoroff]] and Katz on **Calcium Chloride (CaCl2)** toxicity in other species, providing an ecological benchmark:
  - [[Bluegill Sunfish]] (Centrarchidae): 24 h median tolerance limit (TLm) of 8,400 p.p.m. CaCl2.
  - [[Golden Shiner]] (Cyprinidae): Killed within 14.5 h at 5,000 p.p.m. CaCl2.
  - [[European Eel]]/Eels: Survived at least 6 days at 27,748 p.p.m. CaCl2.
- **Suspended Solids Interaction:** The study notes that prolonged contact with suspended solids (hundreds of p.p.m.) has independently been shown to harm trout, necessitating the dual-phase (solution + suspension) testing design used in this study (citing [[Herbert]] and Merkens, 1961).

## Key Findings
1. **No-Effect Threshold:** Rainbow trout survived 100% (10/10) in concentrations up to and including 3,163 p.p.m. gypsum (with 553 p.p.m. in suspension) for the full 28-day trial period, with zero mortality also observed in controls and lower concentrations (0–2,166 p.p.m.).
2. **Lethal Threshold:** Significant mortality occurred at higher concentrations:
   - **6,820 p.p.m.** (4,250 p.p.m. suspended): 5 of 10 fish died within 28 days.
   - **10,000 p.p.m.** (7,433 p.p.m. suspended): 6 of 10 fish died within 28 days.
3. **Time-Mortality Relationship:** Time-mortality curves (Fig. 2) indicated that trout could withstand the lower lethal concentration (6,820 p.p.m.) for a longer duration before death compared to the higher concentration (10,000 p.p.m.), despite the raw mortality count at 28 days being similar.
4. **Confounding Variable:** Data from the 4,649 p.p.m. treatment group were deemed unreliable ("suspect") due to observed intraspecific aggression and physical trauma (wounds) contributing to the 100% mortality rate (10/10), rather than the chemical toxicity of the gypsum alone.
5. **Management Recommendation:** The authors concluded that a trout population of similar sensitivity should experience negligible mortality from:
   - 10,000 p.p.m. gypsum for exposure durations of **no more than 1 day**.
   - 6,820 p.p.m. for exposure durations of up to **three weeks**.
   - Concentrations of **3,163 p.p.m. or less** are considered safe for at least four weeks, and likely for much longer periods.
