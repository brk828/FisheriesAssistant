# Proposed Fish Barrier in Hot Springs Canyon, Cochise County, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a public scoping notice issued jointly by the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Phoenix Area Office) and the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (Safford Field Office). It announces a proposal to build a steel-reinforced concrete fish barrier in a remote reach of [[Hot Springs Canyon]], Arizona, to block the upstream invasion of nonnative fishes from the [[San Pedro River]]. The agencies will prepare an [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA) under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) and are soliciting public comment on key issues and alternatives. The comment deadline was July 10, 2008.

## Background & Description

The notice is a scoping document, not a research report. It describes the project, its rationale, the species involved, and the procedure for public comment.

**Proposed Action.** The barrier would consist of four primary features:
- A 5-foot-high, 24-foot-long vertical drop structure.
- A concrete splash apron spanning the drop structure to prevent streambed scour and plunge pool development.
- Upstream and downstream subsurface keys (scour walls) anchoring the barrier.
- Buried gabion armoring across the streambed along the downstream key.

The site lies approximately 5.1 linear miles (5.6 stream miles) upstream of the San Pedro River on BLM land. Because no roads serve the site, materials would be moved by helicopter. Reclamation is also considering walking a backhoe up the stream channel to avoid the high cost of aerial transport, with impacts to be evaluated in the NEPA process.

**Study Area.** The barrier lies within the BLM-administered [[Hot Springs Area of Critical Environmental Concern]], the southern portion of the [[Muleshoe Ranch Cooperative Management Area]]. The ACEC covers 16,763 acres.

**Background and Mandate.** The project is part of a wider [[Reclamation]] program to build barriers across the [[Gila River Basin]] protecting high-priority streams. The program is required by three [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (FWS) biological opinions on [[Central Arizona Project]] water transfers. Habitat loss was the chief cause of native fish declines in the American Southwest before the mid-1900s. Since then, nonnative competitive and predatory fishes have been recognized as the most serious obstacle to recovering imperiled native fishes.

**Species Present and Potential.** Hot Springs Canyon currently supports five native fish species:
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*)
- [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), endangered

Suitable habitat also exists for:
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), threatened
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), threatened
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), endangered
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), endangered

In October 2007, the BLM, with partners including FWS, the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], [[Arizona State University]], the [[Arizona State Land Department]], Reclamation, [[The Nature Conservancy]], and the [[U.S. Forest Service]], stocked loach minnow, spikedace, desert pupfish, and Gila topminnow into perennial waters. The goal was to aid recovery of each species and restore historical species diversity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Purpose and Need.** The barrier is intended to remove the threat that nonnative fishes living in the San Pedro River pose to existing and reintroduced native populations. Nonnative fishes can move upstream into perennial Hot Springs Canyon waters during high seasonal flows or floods that connect the two systems. No nonnative fishes are currently present in the stream.

**Preliminary Issues for the EA.** The agencies expect the EA to address:
- Effects on biological resources, including special status species
- Effects on cultural resources
- Effects on water resources
- Effects on soils and sediment transport
- Effects on land use
- Effects on the ACEC

**Management Consistency.** The Safford Field Office manages the ACEC under the [[Safford District Resource Management Plan]] (RMP, Part I 1992 and Part II 1994) and the [[Muleshoe Ecosystem Management Plan]] (EMP, 1998), alongside national policies such as the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The RMP designated the ACEC to protect riparian, cultural, fish and wildlife (including federally listed species), and scenic values. The project would conform to two objectives:
- Protect populations of sensitive, threatened, and endangered fish and their habitats (RMP).
- Maintain native fish diversity by removing threats to them (EMP).

**Alternatives.** Under [[Council On Environmental Quality]] regulations (40 CFR 1502.14(d)), a no-action alternative must be analyzed. It also serves as the baseline for comparison. Under no action, the barrier would not be built. Other alternatives will be considered during scoping.

**Decision Authority.** Reclamation and BLM must choose which alternative to implement. Approval for construction on BLM land within the ACEC rests with the Field Manager of the Safford Field Office.

## Key Findings

- **Threat:** Nonnative fishes from the San Pedro River are identified as the primary threat to native fish recovery in Hot Springs Canyon, and no nonnative fishes are currently present in the stream.
- **Native fauna:** Five native species are present, and four additional threatened or endangered species have suitable habitat, with stocking completed in October 2007.
- **Proposed solution:** A 5-foot-high, 24-foot-long concrete drop barrier with scour protection, sited 5.1 linear miles upstream of the San Pedro confluence.
- **Logistics:** Lack of road access means construction would rely on helicopter transport, or a backhoe walked up the channel, a question still under review.
- **Process:** An EA will be prepared; public comments were accepted until July 10, 2008, to a designated contact at the Reclamation Phoenix Area Office.
- **Legal basis:** The project is mandated by FWS biological opinions and aligns with RMP and EMP objectives for the Hot Springs ACEC.
