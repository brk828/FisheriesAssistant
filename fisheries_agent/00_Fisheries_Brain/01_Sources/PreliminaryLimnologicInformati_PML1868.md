# Preliminary Limnologic Information on Waters of the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, Coahuila, México

#limnology #aquatic-ecology #water-chemistry #cuatro-cienegas

## Executive Summary

This paper by W. L. Minckley and G. A. Cole (Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe) reports preliminary limnological data on the aquatic habitats of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], a desert basin in central [[Coahuila]], México. Habitats range from thermal springs, seeps, canals and shallow lakes to ephemeral playas. Most waters are dominated by Ca++ and SO4-- and become progressively more concentrated and sulfato-chloride in type as they evaporate. The authors state that a more detailed study by Minckley was forthcoming, and the paper is framed as a summary of water chemistry for the basin's fauna-rich aquatic systems. The data come from 23 water samples, mostly collected in April 1961 and December 1961 (with some later collections).

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** The basin lies in the Coahuilan Folded Belt of the Sierra Madre Oriental at roughly 740 m above sea level, with surrounding mountains rising above 3,000 m. Average annual rainfall is given as roughly 200 mm (the OCR is partly illegible).
- **Water sources:** Water originates mainly from thermal springs at 25–35 °C, which feed seeps, ponds, lakes and marshes. Many are now canalized and interconnected, complicating drainage interpretation.
- **Lost lakes:** Historically, closed shallow basins held permanent saline lakes. The only remaining large lake noted is Laguna Salada, which no longer receives its original spring source but is occasionally supplied by surface runoff.
- **Regional context:** Limnological studies in northern México were described as scarce. Laguna de Mayrán, which once occupied part of a larger Pleistocene lake basin, was dry when visited in later years, possibly due to water diversions and recession.
- **Biodiversity:** The basin is noted for exceptional vertebrate endemism, with related fauna including [[Cyprinodont Fishes]] (such as *Gambusia* and *Xiphophorus* species), turtles and box turtles. Previous work described an endemic aquatic mollusc fauna and a new cirolanid isopod, [[Speocirolana thermydron]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.** Water samples were collected in one-liter polyethylene bottles for principal inorganic constituents, with dissolved oxygen collected by siphon in 250 ml glass-stoppered bottles. Analytical methods (summarized in Table 1) included pocket thermometers for temperature, colorimetric or titration methods for pH and alkalinity, Winkler titration for dissolved oxygen, EDTA titration for hardness, flame photometry for Na+ and K+, and gravimetric determination for total dissolved solids.

**Water chemistry:**
- **pH:** No residual acidity was present; aerated samples rapidly attained the phenolphthalein endpoint (~pH 8.3). Spring inflows were near neutral (pH 7.0–7.2, six samples); open lagunas ranged pH 7.3–7.8 (eight samples); effluent streams and lateral lagunas ranged pH 7.7–9.0 (15 samples); drying habitats reached pH 8.0–9.7 (eight samples).
- **Hardness:** Most spring waters had total hardness between roughly 490 and 660 mg/l, with most spring-origin waters averaging near 1,000 mg/l. Laguna Salada exceeded 6,000 mg/l EDTA hardness.
- **Ionic balance:** Mean gross cation–anion balance error was 6.2%, reduced to 4.0% when Escobeda and La Angostura Canal samples were excluded.
- **Anions:** None of the 23 waters had less than 50% (by weight) of total anions as SO4--; Cl- was relatively minor, and CO3-- was usually second-most abundant.
- **Cations:** Ca++ dominates most samples; Mg++ generally exceeds Na+ + K+.
- **Concentration trend:** Waters shift from a sulfato-carbonate type to a sulfato-chloride type with concentration, and the Ca:Mg ratio decreases markedly as salinity increases.

**Case study: Churince–Laguna Grande system.** This is the least disturbed system, representing a spring-fed series from the Churince source through a marshy stream to Laguna Grande. Anion percentages (SO4--, CO3--, Cl-) were:
- Churince Laguna: 81.4 / 11.6 / 7.0
- Inlet of Río Churince to Laguna Grande: 85.2 / 8.0 / 6.8
- Laguna Grande: 86.7 / 4.9 / 9.4

**Terminal stage: Laguna Salada.** This shallow, roughly circular pond (~100 m across) had a salt rim 2–3 m wide, clumps of *Ruppia* sp., and abundant harpacticoid copepod *Cletocamptus albuquerquensis*, a species common in brackish and saline waters. Its anion composition was 86.4 / 4.3 / 13.3, with total dissolved solids of 309.4 g/l (reported as given) and a reddish color possibly due to sulfur bacteria.

## Key Findings

- The basin contains a wide range of aquatic chemical habitats within a few hundred square kilometers, described as unexcelled in any other known desert bolsón.
- Most waters are dominated by Ca++ and SO4--, with hardness and salinity increasing sharply toward evaporative end-members.
- Sequential changes in ionic composition occur along the concentration gradient: sulfato-carbonate waters progress to sulfato-chloride waters, and the Ca:Mg ratio falls.
- Spring sources maintain circumneutral pH, while evaporating and drying habitats reach pH 9.7.
- The Churince–Laguna Grande system is the most natural series and best illustrates evaporative concentration.
- Many original lakes have dried or been altered by canalization and water diversion, and several remnant aquatic habitats have not been studied before.
- The authors call for further sampling, including additional inflow data, to resolve uncertainties in the Escobeda and La Angostura Canal analyses and in drainage connections.
- The paper is preliminary; full results were to be published by Minckley.
