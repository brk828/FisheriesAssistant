## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative letter from the Arizona Game & Fish Department (dated July 31, 1987) addressed to representatives of The Arizona Nature Conservancy (TANC) regarding the establishment of a native, endangered fish display at **Palm Lake**, located within the **[[Hassayampa River Preserve]]**. The letter is not a primary scientific research paper but a regulatory/logistical communication outlining the permitting process for holding and displaying endangered native fish species for educational purposes. It provides insight into inter-agency conservation coordination between state (Arizona Game & Fish Department), federal (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, or FWS), and non-governmental (TANC) entities involved in native fish conservation and habitat renovation in Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Context**: Palm Lake is described as a "pond" undergoing renovation, situated within the broader [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] ecosystem — a riparian desert habitat in Arizona.
- **Habitat Management Actions Discussed**:
  - **Spring blocking/diverting**: Proposed experimental manipulation of a natural spring feeding into the pond system, likely to control water flow, temperature, or connectivity for habitat renovation purposes.
  - **Fish toxin application**: Mention of applying piscicide (fish toxin) to the pond/spring system, a common technique used in native fish conservation to eradicate non-native or invasive fish populations prior to reintroducing native/endangered species. This suggests the presence of undesirable competitor or predator species requiring removal before establishing the display population.
  - **Tank/filter conditioning**: Prior to introduction of the true native fish specimens, non-specific "fish of any kind" were to be used to biologically cycle and condition the holding tank's filtration system — a standard aquarium/aquaculture practice to establish nitrifying bacteria colonies before introducing sensitive or endangered specimens.
- **Legal/Regulatory Niche**: The endangered species involved fall under both state jurisdiction (Arizona Game & Fish Commission permitting) and federal jurisdiction (FWS documentation requirements confirming specimens were "legally taken"), indicating the species are federally and/or state-listed as endangered or threatened.

## Key Findings

- The Arizona Game & Fish Department was in the process of requesting Commission approval for an **educational display holding permit** for endangered native fish at Palm Lake, with a decision expected at the **September 1987** Commission meeting.
- The **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS)** had changed its regulatory practice by 1987, no longer issuing "loan" holding permits; instead, only requiring documentation of legal collection/taking of specimens.
- A target **public "open house" date of November 1, 1987** was set for unveiling the display, contingent on completion of physical infrastructure (floor reinforcement, tank setup, filter conditioning) at the preserve.
- Habitat renovation of the pond was planned to follow the open house event, potentially involving **spring diversion** and **piscicide (fish toxin) treatment** — standard native fish conservation techniques to remove competing/non-native fish before reintroduction of endangered natives.
- The project involved multi-agency cooperation, including the **[[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]**, **The Arizona Nature Conservancy (TANC)**, and the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service**, with additional correspondence copied to fish specialists and staff at the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, suggesting the endangered fish specimens may have originated from or been coordinated with this federal facility, historically known for propagating rare southwestern native fishes.
- The letter is signed by **Dean Hendrickson, Native Fish Biologist**, on behalf of Director Temple A. Reynolds, situating this correspondence within Arizona's broader native fish recovery and conservation program of the late 1980s.
