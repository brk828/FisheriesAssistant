# Consultation Requirements for Species in Artificial Environments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #endangered-species-act #national-fish-hatchery

## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum, not a primary biological or morphological study. It addresses a regulatory/legal question regarding whether the [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 interagency consultation process applies to actions by other federal agencies (specifically the Water and Power Resources Service) that could affect listed species held at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. The memo concludes that because Willow Beach is a "totally artificial environment" — where listed species survive only through active hatchery management — external agencies proposing actions affecting that facility are **not required** to enter Section 7 consultation. No morphological, ecological, or population data on the species themselves are provided in the source text.

## Background & Description

No morphological data are present in this source. The memorandum does not identify the specific listed fish species being maintained at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], nor does it describe body form, coloration, size, or anatomical traits. (Note: Willow Beach NFH, located on the Colorado River in Arizona, has historically been associated with captive propagation of Colorado River native fishes such as [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], but this association is contextual and not stated explicitly in the provided text.)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Explicitly characterized as a **wholly artificial environment** — i.e., not a natural riverine or lacustrine habitat, but a hatchery facility under direct human management.
- **Dependency on management**: The memo states that individuals of the listed species "remain alive only through the good graces of hatchery personnel," underscoring complete dependence on artificial husbandry (water delivery, feeding, containment) rather than self-sustaining wild ecological processes.
- **Administrative context**: [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] falls under [[U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service]] administration, distinguishing it from natural habitat under multi-agency jurisdiction.
- **Interacting agency**: The [[Water and Power Resources Service]] (predecessor to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation) proposed actions related to water/power operations that could indirectly affect hatchery operations or water supply to the facility.

## Key Findings

- No formal, nationally written USFWS policy existed (as of 1980) explicitly addressing whether Section 7 consultation applies to artificial environments housing listed species.
- The memo's authors interpret the **intent of Congress** and the legislative record behind the [[Endangered Species Act]] as **not requiring** other agencies to enter Section 7 consultation for actions affecting species confined to artificial environments.
- [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] is designated as a clear-cut case of an "artificial environment," even though the authors acknowledge "gray areas" may exist in defining this term for other, less obvious cases.
- The [[Water and Power Resources Service]]'s position — that consultation is not required for actions affecting the hatchery — was explicitly endorsed by USFWS in this memorandum.
- This finding sets an **internal precedent/interpretive guidance** for future determinations of Section 7 applicability regarding captive or hatchery-maintained populations of listed species, though it is not a formal rulemaking or nationally codified policy.
