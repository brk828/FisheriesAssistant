# Colorado River Studies (FW-16-R-6, WP3, J4)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #riverine-habitat #fisheries-management #[[Colorado River]]

## Executive Summary

This completion report, prepared by Bud Bristow for the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] under Project FW-16-R-6, documents fish and wildlife management activities on the [[Lower Colorado River]] from August 1966 to March 1967. Rather than focusing on individual species biology, the study addresses the ecological consequences of large-scale river engineering — specifically the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s **Front Work and Levee System Project** — on fish and wildlife habitat. Key activities included joint aerial surveys with the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] to quantify angler and recreational use, review of navigation permit applications for habitat impacts, initiation of a public-use river zoning plan, and mitigation engineering (culvert installation and planned dredging) in channelized backwater habitats near Blythe, California (Palo Verde Division). The report highlights an emerging tension between river channelization/bank stabilization for flood control and navigation versus the maintenance of productive fish and waterfowl habitat.

## Background & Description

Not applicable — this report is a river/habitat management and aerial survey study and does not describe individual fish species, taxonomy, or morphology. Habitat structural characteristics are described in lieu of organismal morphology:

- **Backwater pools**: 8–12 feet deep, formed by old river meanders and braided channel sections cut off during channelization.
- **Shallow water areas**: Interspersed with the deep pools; subject to dewatering during low river flow periods.
- **Hydrology**: Water elevation in backwaters fluctuates in direct response to river flow; prior to channelization, natural currents periodically scoured pools, removed invasive vegetation, and formed new sandbars.
- **Post-channelization changes**: Levee construction eliminated natural scouring action, leading to rapid encroachment of marsh vegetation into shallow zones.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report centers on the **[[Palo Verde Division]]** backwaters as critical fish and wildlife habitat within the broader Lower Colorado River ecosystem:

- **Habitat type**: Cutoff meanders and braided river sections converted to isolated backwater pools by channelization and bank stabilization.
- **Faunal use**: Backwaters are used extensively by anglers (fish habitat) and waterfowl hunters, indicating a dual role as fish production/angling waters and avian habitat.
- **Accessibility**: Deep, narrow, confined pools are easily fished from shore via levee roads, making them high-value public recreation zones.
- **Threats to niche integrity**:
  - Levees prevent natural river scouring, allowing marsh vegetation to overtake shallow habitat.
  - Lack of water rights and absence of a water manipulation structure prevent artificial flooding to control vegetation encroachment.
  - Anticipated **severe decline in waterfowl use over the next decade** due to unchecked vegetative succession in shallow backwater areas.
- **Mitigation intervention**: Culverts installed by the Bureau of Reclamation (November 1966) at upstream and downstream ends of backwaters reconnect them hydrologically to the main river channel, restoring a "freshening effect" and preventing summer water temperature extremes — an important thermal habitat consideration for fish.
- **Broader river zoning concept**: The report describes a concerted effort to identify and legally protect river stretches with high fish and wildlife value from private development (docks, cabins), which was found to degrade shoreline accessibility and habitat quality, referencing the [[Lower Colorado River Land Use Plan]] (1964).

## Key Findings

- **Aerial Survey Program**: 48 total flights (24 by each agency) conducted March 1966–January 1967 between Davis Dam and Morelos Dam to quantify angler and recreational/wildlife use; data being processed by California Department of Fish and Game to support evaluation of Bureau of Reclamation channelization impacts.
- **Channelization Project Status**:
  - Complete: Mohave and Palo Verde Divisions.
  - Under construction: Cibola and Laguna Divisions.
  - Under study (preliminary construction started): Topock, Parker, and Yuma Divisions.
- **Interagency Coordination**: Disagreements persisted between the Arizona Game and Fish Department and the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife regarding evaluation methodology for fish/wildlife features in the Topock, Parker, and Yuma Division reports (see [[Kufeld 1966]]); agencies agreed in principle to consolidate future reports for congressional appropriations.
- **Navigation Permits**: 14 applications reviewed; 1 rejected at the Department's request (proposed boat dock threatening to set a precedent for further dock development in a high-value fish/wildlife area) — a successful case of habitat protection via regulatory review.
- **Private Development Encroachment**: Rows of boat docks and cabins were identified as degrading public shoreline access and fish/wildlife value to the point of practical abandonment by anglers in some river sections — driving the recommendation for a formal public-use zoning plan and consolidated community marinas instead of dispersed private structures.
- **Backwater Habitat Enhancement**:
  - Culverts installed (Nov. 1966) in Palo Verde Division backwaters restored partial hydrologic connectivity to the river.
  - A dredging plan (joint effort of FW-16-R, F-7-R, and W-53-R project personnel, with Bureau of Reclamation engineering support) was in development to create 20–30 surface acres of open, vegetation-free water suitable for fish production and angling, to be funded by the Front Work and Levee System Project as a fish and wildlife enhancement feature.
  - No feasible method identified to prevent marsh vegetation encroachment in shallow areas without water rights/periodic flooding capability, forecasting a significant long-term decline in waterfowl habitat value.
- **Recommendations**:
  1. Draft a cooperative interagency zoning plan to reserve select high-value river stretches for public fishing/recreation use.
  2. Hire an additional summer aide to collect ground-truth visitor-use data along the river (Davis Dam to Arizona-Mexico border) to calibrate and validate aerial survey methodology.
