# Occurence of the sturgeon chub, Hybopsis gelida (Girard) in Kansas

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This 1953 note by Frank B. Cross documents the first confirmed records of the **Sturgeon Chub** (*Hybopsis gelida*) in the [[Kansas River]] system, clarifying decades of taxonomic confusion stemming from early misidentifications by [[Jordan and Meek (1886)]] and [[Graham (1885)]]. Earlier "sturgeon chub" records from Missouri and Kansas were subsequently reassigned to a distinct species, *Hybopsis meeki* ([[Silver Chub]]), described by [[Jordan and Evermann]] in 1896. Cross's collections (1951–1952) establish that true *H. gelida* is "rather common" in the Kansas River near Lawrence and also occurs in a tributary of the [[Wakarusa River]], extending the known distribution of the species southward within the [[Missouri River Basin]].

## Background & Description
- **Diagnostic feature**: Presence of **sensory keels** on most of the lateral-line scales — unique among fishes of the Kansas River system.
- **Standard length range** of Kansas specimens: 18–70 mm.
- **Eye size**: Diameter enters head length 5–6 times (larger eye than *H. meeki*, which is ~7 times; but smaller relative eye than *H. aestivalis*, ~4 times).
- **Lateral-line scale counts**:
  - *H. gelida*: 39–43 (avg. 41)
  - *H. meeki*: 45–50 (avg. 47)
  - *H. aestivalis*: 35–39 (avg. 36)
- **Head length in standard length**:
  - *H. gelida*: 3.6–3.9×
  - *H. aestivalis*: 3.8–4.0×
  - *H. meeki*: 4.3–5.0×
- **Maxillary barbel**: In *H. gelida* and *H. meeki*, barbel length is slightly less than or about equal to the narial pit length; in *H. aestivalis*, the barbel greatly exceeds the narial pit length.
- **Snout shape**: *H. gelida* and *H. aestivalis* have compressed, fleshy snouts protruding far forward of the ventral mouth; *H. meeki* has a bluntly rounded snout extending little beyond the mouth.
- **Fin shape**: *H. meeki* has strongly falcate dorsal and pectoral fins with rays exceeding head length; in *H. gelida* and *H. aestivalis* the longest rays are shorter than the head.
- **Pigmentation**:
  - *H. aestivalis* ("speckled chub"): sparse, large, irregularly placed melanophores; caudal fin bears two symmetrical diffuse spots, one near the base of each lobe.
  - *H. gelida* and *H. meeki*: more uniformly pigmented; lower caudal lobe distinctly darker than upper, especially near fin base.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Inhabits **large, turbid river systems** with sand/gravel substrates typical of Great Plains streams (e.g., [[Kansas River]], [[Missouri River]]).
- Also documented in smaller tributary habitat: **Rock Creek**, a tributary of the [[Wakarusa River]] in Douglas County, Kansas — suggesting some tolerance for smaller stream environments.
- Co-occurs with closely related congeners *H. meeki* and *H. aestivalis*, with which it shares habitat but differs in fine-scale morphology, implying niche partitioning or close ecological association within the [[Cyprinidae]] guild of plains minnows.
- Broader range (per [[Johnson 1942]]) spans from the Milk, Powder, and Yellowstone rivers of Montana, southward through the Missouri basin to the Republican River of Nebraska, and to the Mississippi River between Illinois and Missouri.

## Key Findings
- **Taxonomic correction**: Historical records of "*Hybopsis gelida*" from Missouri (Jordan and Meek, 1886) and Kansas (Graham, 1885) were misidentifications; true specimens belonged to the newly described *Hybopsis meeki* ([[Jordan and Evermann 1896]]).
- Jordan and Evermann's 1896 taxonomic revision **restricted the known range of true *H. gelida*** to regions north of Kansas.
- **Evermann and Cox (1896)** correctly reassigned Graham's original Kansas record to *H. meeki*.
- Cross's fieldwork (1951–1952) provides the **first verified Kansas records** of true *H. gelida*: 42 specimens (K.U. 1852, 1888, 2171, 2456) from five collections in the Kansas River near Lawrence (Sec. 30, T.12S., R.20E.), plus one specimen (K.U. 1963) from Rock Creek (Sec. 18, T.13S., R.20E.).
- Establishes *H. gelida* as **"rather common"** in the Kansas River near Lawrence, extending its documented southern range within the [[Missouri River Basin]].
- Provides a clear **morphological key** distinguishing *H. gelida*, *H. meeki*, and *H. aestivalis* based on scale keels, eye size, lateral-line scale counts, barbel length, snout shape, fin length, and pigmentation pattern — useful for field identification of plains cyprinids.
