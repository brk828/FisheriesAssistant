# Bibliography of Historical Biogeography, Plate Tectonics and Dispersal Theory

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bibliography

## Executive Summary

This document is not a research article but the reference list (pages 539–584) of a larger work on historical biogeography. It contains roughly 1,000 entries, spanning the 1800s to about 1981, and covers plate tectonics, continental drift, dispersal and vicariance theory, paleogeography, island biogeography, and phylogenetic systematics. Its taxonomic scope is broad, including plants, insects (especially [[Carabid Beetles]]), birds, mammals, land snails, and some fish, but the list itself reports no new data. Fish-related entries are few and peripheral. The list is most useful as a map of the literature that underpins the biogeographic framework of the parent work.

## Background & Description

The list is alphabetized by first author, running from AAPG Geological Highway Maps and Abbott (1977) through Zwick (1979) on stoneflies. Entry types include:

- Journal articles (e.g., *Systematic Zoology*, *Nature*, *Journal of Geophysical Research*, *Ecological Monographs*)
- Monographs and books (e.g., [[Croizat]]'s *Panbiogeography*, [[Darwin]]'s *Origin of Species*, [[Wegener]]'s *Origin of Continents and Ocean Basins*, [[MacArthur]] and Wilson's *Theory of Island Biogeography*)
- Symposium volumes, notably *Aspects of Tethyan Biogeography* (Adams and Ager, eds., 1967) and *Organisms and Continents Through Time* (Hughes, ed., 1973)
- Geological atlases and reports, including Deep Sea Drilling Project reports and the Atlas of Palaeobiogeography (Hallam, ed., 1973)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The references describe a field organized around how continents, oceans and climate have shaped the distribution of organisms. Recurring themes include:

- **Continental drift and plate tectonics:** Wegener's hypothesis and later plate reconstructions, such as Pangaea breakup, the opening of the Atlantic and South Atlantic, the Drake Passage, and the Tethys Sea.
- **Dispersal versus vicariance:** Long-distance dispersal of plants and insects (Carlquist's 1965–1974 series, Croizat's panbiogeography) contrasted with vicariance explanations (Nelson, Rosen, Platnick).
- **Refugia and Pleistocene change:** Forest refugia in the Neotropics (Haffer, Brown and Ab'Saber, Prance) and glacial-period migrations (Simpson, Colinvaux).
- **Island biogeography:** Equilibrium theory and colonization on oceanic islands, including Hawaii, the Galápagos and the West Indies.
- **Aquatic and marine elements:** Fossil and extant freshwater fishes (Patterson 1975; Rosen and Bailey 1963 on poeciliids; Rosen 1974 on salmoniforms; Nelson 1973a on Cretaceous and Eocene teleosts), Mesozoic freshwater fishes, Pacific marine invertebrates, and the bathymetry and sediments of ocean basins.

## Key Findings

The document itself reports no empirical results. Its significance lies in the scope of the literature it cites:

- **Size:** About 1,000 entries from roughly 1800 to 1981, with a heavy concentration in the 1970s.
- **Fish-relevant entries:** A small number, mainly [[Patterson]] (1975, Mesozoic freshwater fishes), [[Rosen]] (1974a, salmoniform phylogeny and biogeography), [[Rosen]] and [[Bailey]] (1963, poeciliid zoogeography), [[Nelson]] (1973a, Cretaceous and Eocene teleosts), Taverne (1977–1978, osteoglossomorphs), Wiley (1976, gars, Lepisosteidae), Collette (1974, central Pacific halfbeak), and Schultz (1969, hybridization in *Poeciliopsis*).
- **Core theoretical works:** [[Croizat]] (1958, 1964) on panbiogeography; [[Darwin]] (1859) on natural selection; [[Wegener]] (1915, 1924) on continental drift; [[Nelson]] (1976) and [[Rosen]] (1976a) on vicariance; [[MacArthur]] and Wilson (1967) on island biogeography.
- **Plate tectonics and paleogeography:** Extensive literature on Atlantic, Pacific, Indian Ocean and Caribbean plate histories (e.g., Larson and Pitman 1972; Hallam 1973; Smith and Briden 1977).
- **Methodology debates:** Cladistics and phylogenetic systematics (Hennig 1966; Farris 1970; Platnick and Nelson 1978), and the philosophy of science (Popper, Kuhn-era debates, Feyerabend).
