# Mise en évidence d'un caractère sexuel secondaire chez les larves de Caenis (Ephemeroptera, Caenidae)

## Executive Summary
This 1970 study by Max Thibault (Bulletin de la Société entomologique de France) identifies a previously unknown **secondary sexual character** in the larvae of mayflies of the genus [[Caenis]] (family [[Caenidae]]), based on the shape of the caudal filaments (cerci and paracercus). Prior to this work, no external morphological criterion existed to distinguish male from female larvae in Caenidae, even at the final instar — unlike other Ephemeroptera families where gonopods or divided compound eyes allow sex determination in larvae. The discovery was made incidentally during an ecological study of mayflies in a trout stream ([[Lissuraga]] stream, Pyrénées-Atlantiques, France) as part of rearing experiments to obtain corresponding adults from larvae. The paper also presents three years (1965–1967) of quantitative field data on the sex ratio of *Caenis rivulorum* larval populations sampled monthly via Surber sampler.

## Background & Description
- **Diagnostic character**: Shape and size of the **cerci and paracercus** (caudal filaments) at the abdominal tip of final-instar larvae differ between sexes.
  - **Male larvae**: Cerci and paracercus are **thick and contiguous at their base**, forming a **lyre-shaped** structure together (see Fig. 1a).
  - **Female larvae**: Cerci and paracercus are **thinner at the base** and **rather straight along their entire length** (see Fig. 1b).
- This sexual dimorphism **persists into the subimaginal stage**, but disappears in adults — in both sexes, adult cerci and the terminal filament become long and thin, erasing the distinction.
- **Size dimorphism**: In the final instar of *C. rivulorum*, male larvae are smaller (2.5–3 mm) than female larvae (3–3.5 mm).
- The character was verified across multiple congeneric species: *C. rivulorum*, *C. horaria*, *C. moesta*, *C. macrura*, *C. robusta*, and *C. pseudorivulorum* — suggesting this may be a **genus-wide diagnostic trait** for [[Caenis]], at least in late-instar larvae.
- Broader context: adult/subimago Ephemeroptera generally show marked sexual dimorphism (presence of gonopods in males, longer forelegs, more vivid coloration, and often divided compound eyes — maximal in [[Baetidae]], where males appear to have two pairs of eyes). Larval sexual dimorphism in other families is typically based on presence/absence of gonopods ([[Ephemeridae]], [[Heptageniidae]]) or a second pair of compound eyes ([[Baetidae]], [[Leptophlebiidae]], [[Ephemerellidae]]). Caenidae previously lacked any such known larval criterion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Benthic larvae of *Caenis rivulorum* inhabit a trout stream (the [[Lissuraga]], Pyrénées-Atlantiques, France), sampled as part of a broader ecological study of stream Ephemeroptera (Thibault, in press).
- **Species distribution**: *C. rivulorum* was first recorded in France in the Alps (Degrange, 1957), later found in Brittany (Abgrall, 1966), and subsequently in southwestern France (this study).
- **Phenology**: Final larval instars occur from **April to July** in the Pyrénées-Atlantiques population.
- **Adult behavior**: Subimagos and adults are small-bodied and fly primarily at **dusk (crepuscular activity)**, making aerial capture difficult and biasing sex-ratio estimates from adult/subimago collections.
- **Sampling methodology**: Larvae were collected monthly over three years (1965–1967) using a **Surber sampler** (Surber, 1937) at four sub-sampling localities along the stream, totaling 0.8 m² of streambed sampled per month.

## Key Findings
- **Sex ratio in larvae**: Total of 3,772 *C. rivulorum* larvae sexed (446 males, 478 females) across 1965–1967, yielding a male:female ratio very close to 1, with a slight predominance of females (ratios of 0.96, 0.95, and 0.89 for 1965, 1966, and 1967 respectively).
- **Sampling artifact**: The markedly lower catch in 1967 (580 individuals vs. 1,245 and 1,947 in prior years) is attributed to a change in net mesh size from 0.25 mm (1965–1966) to 0.8 mm (1967); the coarser mesh likely favored capture of larger female larvae over smaller males, potentially skewing the observed sex ratio.
- **Adult/subimago sex ratio discrepancy**: Only 33 winged individuals were captured (4 males, 29 females) — a ~7:1 female-to-male ratio — attributed to the small size and dusk-flight behavior of the species making male capture disproportionately difficult, rather than reflecting a true sex ratio.
- **Taxonomic/methodological significance**: This is presented as the first known external, non-dissective character for sexing Caenidae larvae, with potential applicability across the genus *Caenis*, enabling more accurate demographic and life-history studies (e.g., sex-specific growth, phenology, production estimates) in future ecological work such as [[Turpault (in press) - Ecologie d'un Ruisseau à Truites des Pyrénées]].
