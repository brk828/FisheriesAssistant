# Dispersal of Stocked Northern Pike in Clear Lake, Iowa

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This short 1955 study tested whether [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) stocked in [[Clear Lake]], Iowa, were recaptured mainly near their stocking sites. In fall 1953, 2,630 individually tagged young-of-the-year pike were released at eight locations. Anglers returned 780 tagged fish in the summer of 1954, and capture locations were recorded for 651 of them. Tagged pike were recaptured in all seven lake areas, indicating that stocked fish dispersed widely within six months. Catch distribution still differed among plantings, but the differences were small. The authors conclude that pike stocked at one end of the lake reach the other end, though not in quite the same abundance.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Northern pike, *Esox lucius*, stocked as young-of-the-year fish. Stocked fish were mostly 10.5 to 16.0 inches total length.
- **Study site:** Clear Lake, north-central Iowa. Area is 3,643 acres, maximum depth is about 20 feet, and the lake measures roughly 5 by 2 miles. It is shallow and has no important inlets or outlets that pike use for migration. The exception is [[Ventura Marsh]], which has a trap where migrating pike can be counted.
- **Stocking:** In October 1953, 15,192 young pike were stocked. Of these, 2,630 (17%) were tagged with monel-metal strap tags around the premaxilla and individually numbered. Eight stocking locations were used, including [[Clausen's Cove]], [[South Bay]], [[State Dock]], [[The Rushes]], [[Witke's]], [[Bayside]], the [[State Park]], and the [[Fish Hatchery]].
- **Pre-existing population:** Before 1953 a small resident pike population was present. Gill-net surveys from 1947 to 1952 caught few pike (0 to 0.3 per day per 125 feet of experimental gill net), compared with 4.4 to 18.0 [[Walleye]] per day. In 1954, the catch averaged about 1.3 pike and 6.3 walleye per gill-net day.
- **Affiliation:** The study was Project 39 of the [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]]. It was sponsored by the Iowa State Conservation Commission and the Industrial Science Research Institute of Iowa State College, with cooperation from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
- **Authors:** Kenneth D. Carlander and Richard Ridenhour, Iowa State College, Ames, Iowa.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake zonation:** The lake was divided into seven areas (Areas I to VII). Boundaries were drawn to make each area as environmentally uniform as possible. The authors noted that some areas probably offered better pike habitat than others, so pike were not expected to be spread evenly.
- **Movement window:** More than six months passed between stocking and the summer 1954 angler returns. The authors judged this enough time for fish to settle into suitable habitat.
- **Catch context:** Anglers caught more pike in summer 1954 than in any earlier year. Recapture data therefore reflect a relatively strong angler effort, as well as fish distribution.
- **Limits:** The lake has no significant migration routes apart from Ventura Marsh. Movement inside the lake was therefore assessed only through recaptures of tagged fish.

## Key Findings

1. **Wide dispersal:** Tagged pike were recaptured from all seven lake areas, regardless of where they were stocked. The authors concluded that stocked pike move to all parts of Clear Lake within six months, even when released at a single end of the lake.
2. **Non-random distribution:** A chi-square test of the full recapture distribution against the expected pattern gave χ² = 65.6 with 36 degrees of freedom. The authors reported that such a value would arise by chance less than once in 100 trials if all plantings had mixed equally. Some plantings therefore showed a tendency to be recaptured more in certain areas than others.
3. **Small departures from stocking-area fidelity:** Fish did not stay in unusual proportions in their stocking areas. Most stocking groups had slightly fewer recaptures than expected in their own area. Witke's and Hatchery fish were slightly above expectation in Area VI. A second chi-square test on the area-level differences gave χ² = 3.0 with 6 degrees of freedom. The authors judged these differences to be within sampling variation.
4. **Notable deviations:**
   - Fish stocked in Area IV were recaptured more often than expected in Area I, and possibly less often than expected in Areas VI and VII at the opposite end of the lake.
   - Fish stocked in Area VI and at the State Park (Area VII) were recaptured less often than expected in Area I, and perhaps more often in Areas VI and VII.
5. **Recommendation implied by the results:** The authors suggested that a longer interval between stocking and recapture might remove even the slight differences seen in distribution.
