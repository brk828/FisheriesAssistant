# Virgin River Chub Endangered Species Listing (Federal Register, August 24, 1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document comprises a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service cover letter (September 7, 1989) and the Federal Register final rule, published August 24, 1989 (effective September 28, 1989), that lists the [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*) as an endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. Final designation of [[Critical Habitat]] was postponed pending a full economic analysis. The rule summarizes the species' taxonomy, distribution, decline, threats, comments received during the comment period, the Service's responses, and the protections and recovery measures that follow from listing.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** First collected from the Virgin River near Washington, Utah, by the [[Wheeler Survey]] and described as intermediate between *Gila robusta* and *Gila elegans* (Cope and Yarrow 1875). Later authors treated it as a subspecies of *robusta*. Holden and Stalnaker (1970) restricted the name *seminuda* to Virgin River fish, and Smith et al. (1977) supported its validity with taxonomic analysis. Taxonomic experts unanimously agreed it is a valid subspecies.
- **Morphology:** A silvery, medium-sized minnow averaging about 20 cm (8 in) total length, growing to 45 cm (18 in). Distinguished by 9 to 10 rays in the dorsal, anal and pelvic fins and 12 to 14 gill rakers. Small, deeply embedded scales on the back, breast and belly, sometimes absent, give the subspecies its name.
- **Relatives:** The [[Moapa Chub Form]] (*Gila robusta* subspecies in the Moapa River, Nevada) was originally a tributary of the Virgin River; both now drain to [[Lake Mead]]. It was not affected by this listing.
- **Listing history:** A proposal was published August 23, 1978 (43 FR 37005) and withdrawn September 30, 1980 after lapsing under the 1978 Amendments. The species was placed in Category 1 of the Vertebrate Notice of Review (December 1982). The [[Woundfin Recovery Team]] recommended listing in April 1983, and a June 24, 1984 proposal (49 FR 22949) to list and designate critical habitat led to this final rule.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range:** Endemic to about 100 miles of the Virgin River. Historically it occupied most of the river from the Colorado confluence upstream to La Verkin Creek near Hurricane, Utah. Currently it occupies only about 50 miles of mainstem between Mesquite, Nevada, and La Verkin Creek; it has been recorded in a tributary only twice.
- **Habitat preferences:** Deeper areas with swift but not turbulent water, usually associated with boulders or other cover. Occurs over sand and gravel in water below 30°C and tolerates high salinity and turbidity.
- **Diet:** Omnivorous, eating algae, aquatic and terrestrial insects, organic detritus and crustaceans.
- **Population dynamics:** [[Cross 1975]] captured only 153 individuals out of 10,822 native fish collected (about 1.5 percent). Reproduction was good in 1978, 1983 and 1986 (Woundfin Recovery Team); Hickman (1985) reported good reproduction in 1983 and 1984 but very little in 1984–1985, 1987 or 1988. The chub spawned successfully in only 3 of the previous 12 years.
- **Historical decline:** Water diversions between roughly 1880 and 1900 dewatered about 35 miles of habitat. Construction of [[Hoover Dam]] and inundation of [[Lake Mead]] destroyed about 10 additional miles, eliminating roughly 50 percent or more of original habitat.
- **Recent events:** In 1988 a [[Red Shiner]] eradication project removed all fish from a 21-mile reach (Washington Fields Diversion to the head of Virgin River Gorge). Later, the collapse of a dike at [[Quail Creek Reservoir]] released about 25,000 acre-feet of water, with a devastating effect on fish populations in 85 miles of river.
- **Land ownership:** In Arizona about 80–90 percent of riparian lands are administered by the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), with private land concentrated near Littlefield. In Utah about 11 miles are managed by BLM, the State owns 4 small parcels, and the rest is private. Land above Mesquite, Nevada, is privately owned.

## Key Findings

**Threats identified**
- Further water removal, additional impoundments, sedimentation, pollution, channel alteration, disease, and competition with or predation by introduced species.
- Habitat alteration (impoundments, irrigation diversions, riparian destruction, channel downcutting, flood control, road construction) was judged the major factor (Factor A).
- Planned projects, including a Washington County Water Conservancy District proposal for four potential reservoirs, could reduce habitat further.

**Listing decision**
- The Service determined the chub should be listed as endangered under Section 4(a)(1) based on the best available scientific and commercial data, without regard to economic impacts.
- Critical habitat designation was postponed for up to one additional year under Section 4(b)(6)(C) because of the complexity of the economic analysis.

**Public comment (32 parties in writing; 16 people representing 17 parties at the October 15, 1980 hearing in St. George, Utah)**
- Written comments: 12 supported the proposal; 7 questioned or opposed it; 13 offered other comments. At the hearing, 7 parties opposed and 6 supported listing; 4 gave other comments.
- Supporters included the Arizona Game and Fish Department, Nevada Department of Wildlife, American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists, and the American Fisheries Society.
- Opponents included Governor Norman Bangerter, Washington County Commission, and several Utah local governments.

**Key responses to issues raised**
- Listing would not curtail economic development; the Act requires only that federal agencies conserve listed species. Listing the [[Woundfin]] did not stop irrigation projects or Quail Creek Reservoir.
- Hickman's 1985 and 1988 sampling was not directly comparable to Cross (1975) because of different gear and sites.
- Three years of successful spawning in twelve years does not establish a trend or prove the species is not endangered.
- The Virgin River chub meets the listing criteria independently; protection for the woundfin would not adequately cover its habitat needs because the two species' requirements differ.
- Recovery will be coordinated by the Woundfin Recovery Team, to be renamed the Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team.

**Protections and recovery measures**
- Section 7 requires federal agencies to consult with the Service on actions that may affect the species or its critical habitat. Relevant agencies include BLM, the Bureau of Reclamation, the Soil Conservation Service, and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (Clean Water Act Section 404 permits).
- Section 9 prohibits take, import, export, interstate sale and possession of the species. Permits (50 CFR 17.22) may be issued for scientific purposes, propagation, or incidental take.
- Section 5 cooperative agreements would allow Arizona, Nevada and Utah to receive funds.
- Potential recovery actions include studies of land drift and parasite and red shiner impacts, chemical removal of fish below Washington Fields Diversion followed by restocking with native species, construction of a fish passage barrier below Riverside, Nevada, recommended water management policies, and legally protected in-stream flows.

**Environmental review**
- The Service determined that an Environmental Assessment under the National Environmental Policy Act was not required for this rule (notice published October 25, 1983).
