# The Rapid Spread of *Potamopyrgus antipodarum* and Its Impacts on Native Snail Fauna in the Middle Snake River, Southern Idaho

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Hydrobiidae #malacology #invasive-species #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1989 Desert Fishes Council paper documents the rapid invasion of the exotic New Zealand mudsnail, [[Potamopyrgus antipodarum]], into the Middle Snake River between Shoshone Falls and C.J. Strike Reservoir, Southern Idaho. This reach supports a relict molluscan fauna (~25 species) dating back to the Pliocene [[Blancan Lake Idaho]] and Pleistocene lake/river systems, including six candidate endangered species. First recorded in 1987 by D.W. Taylor, *P. antipodarum* has since established enormous, patchily distributed populations throughout the mainstem river and its "Thousand Springs" tributary system, reaching up to 89% of total snail abundance at some unimpounded sites. The species is outcompeting native molluscs for substrate space, particularly on rocks in riffle zones and on filamentous algae/macrophytes, raising serious conservation concerns for the last refuge population of native Snake River molluscs.

## Background & Description
- **Potamopyrgus antipodarum**: Small hydrobiid snail; parthenogenetic reproductive strategy (asexual, clonal population growth facilitating rapid range expansion).
- **Photophobic behavior distinction**: Unlike the native [[Bliss Rapids Snail]] and several other native hydrobiids, *P. antipodarum* is **not photophobic**, allowing it to occupy exposed rock surfaces and habitats avoided by native species.
- **Native comparanda** (candidate endangered species treated as morphologically/taxonomically distinct relict lineages):
  - [[Bliss Rapids Snail]] — undescribed, monotypic hydrobiid genus, photophobic.
  - [[Fontelicella idahoensis]] — relict hydrobiid.
  - **"Frest's Lanx"** — undescribed limpet-like gastropod (Lanx sp.), petitioned for Emergency Endangered Species Listing.
  - [[Physa natricina]] — Pleistocene lake/river relict physid.
  - [[Valvata utahensis]] — relict valvatid.
  - [[Fluminicola columbiana]] — relict pleurocerid/hydrobiid.
  - [[Fisherola nuttalli]] — native limpet.
  - [[Fluminicola hindsi]] and [[Vorticifex effusus]] — additional native molluscs affected by competition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat range of *P. antipodarum***: Broadly but "spottily" distributed across the mainstem Middle Snake River and associated spring-fed tributaries ("Thousand Springs" complex); population densities highly variable, especially in spring habitats.
- **Substrate preference**: Dominant colonizer of filamentous green algae and attached macrophytes; also dominant on rocky substrate in riffle sections of unimpounded river reaches.
- **Tolerance to hydrological disturbance**: Thrives in impounded (reservoir) habitats and tolerates hydroelectric peakloading — daily water-level fluctuations of a meter or more that are otherwise devastating to native fauna. Forms thick mats on the undersides of rocks, the very refuge zones used by native snails during dewatering events, potentially displacing them through simple spatial occupation.
- **Niche overlap with native fauna**: Competing directly for habitat/space with native mollusc species including [[Fluminicola hindsi]] and [[Vorticifex effusus]], as well as the six candidate endangered relict species.
- **Regional context**: The Shoshone Falls–C.J. Strike Reservoir reach functions as the last significant refuge for native Snake River mollusc fauna, since river reaches upstream and downstream are comparatively "sterile" due to impoundments, water diversion, and water quality degradation — making this stretch ecologically critical and highly vulnerable to invasive disruption.

## Key Findings
- *Potamopyrgus antipodarum* was first documented in the Middle Snake River in **1987** (D.W. Taylor); by 1989 it had built substantial populations across the mainstem and tributary spring systems.
- Its **parthenogenetic reproduction** likely underlies its capacity for rapid population growth and range expansion without need for mate encounters.
- At some non-impounded sites, *P. antipodarum* comprises up to **89% of total snail abundance**, with likely even higher dominance in impounded and other unsurveyed localities.
- The snail has become the **dominant epifaunal species** on both algae/macrophyte substrates and rock substrates in riffle habitats.
- Unlike photophobic natives (e.g., [[Bliss Rapids Snail]]), *P. antipodarum*'s non-photophobic behavior lets it occupy habitat niches — including exposed rock surfaces — not fully utilized by natives, potentially intensifying competitive exclusion.
- The species' tolerance of daily water-level fluctuations from **hydroelectric peakloading** gives it a competitive advantage over native species that rely on rock-associated refugia during these disturbance events.
- The Middle Snake River reach harbors **~25 native mollusc species**, including **six candidate endangered species** tied to Pliocene ([[Blancan Lake Idaho]]) and Pleistocene relict lineages, underscoring the high conservation stakes of this invasion.
- Mechanisms of competition beyond spatial/substrate occupation remain largely **unknown**, flagged by the author as a priority for further research.
- The paper frames *P. antipodarum* as being in an active **phase of range expansion and population growth** in the Middle Snake River as of the time of writing (1989).
