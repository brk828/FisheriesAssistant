# Interim Report: Bonytail Broodstock Collections from Lake Mojave, Arizona-Nevada, April-June 2001

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This interim report by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Arizona Fishery Resources Office (Parker) documents an effort to collect wild [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) from [[Lake Mojave]] for the [[Broodstock Management|broodstock]] program at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH). The work was funded through the [[Multi-Species Conservation Plan]] Interim Conservation Plan. Six collection trips were made between 2 April and 22 June 2001 in the [[Arrowhead Cove]] area, using [[Trammel Net Sampling]]. Only three bonytail were captured, none large enough for broodstock use. The authors conclude that recovery of wild fish is a high priority and recommend further collections in March-May 2002.

## Background & Description

- **Conservation status:** Bonytail were listed as Endangered in 1981. A recovery plan was completed in 1991, and [[Critical Habitat]] was designated in 1994.
- **Historical decline:** Minckley (1973) reported the species as extinct in moderate-sized Colorado River tributaries such as the [[Gila River]] and [[Little Colorado River]], persisting only in unknown numbers in mainstream reservoirs. The authors state that wild populations are now closer to extinction, if not extinct.
- **Historical records:** Table 1 of the report summarizes bonytail records from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] downstream to the international border, dating from about 4000 BP (a flood deposit at Stanton's Cave, Grand Canyon) to 1979. Records span Grand Canyon, [[Lake Mead]], the reach between [[Hoover Dam]] and [[Davis Dam]], and the reach between [[Parker Dam]] and the border.
- **Hatchery broodstock:** The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] produced F1 progeny in 1981 from 10 or fewer wild broodfish held at WBNFH. These progeny are the basis for stocking throughout the Colorado River Basin. As of 18 December 2001, 230 individuals remained at WBNFH. The current broodstock is aging, which increases the need for new wild genetic material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Lake Mojave is a mainstream reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] between [[Davis Dam]] and [[Hoover Dam]] (Cottonwood Basin lies roughly halfway between them). The primary collection site was Arrowhead Cove, about 3 miles north of Katherine's Landing. Water fished was generally shallower than 5 m, with occasional deeper sets.

**Historical bonytail localities at Lake Mojave (1980-2001, Table 2).** A total of 111 bonytail were recorded:
- Cottonwood Basin (Carp Cove, 9 Mile, Bill Gayes Cove, Cottonwood East, Dandy, North Arm): N = 92
- Arrowhead Cove complex: N = 19
- Katherine Landing / Powering Cove and other sites: small numbers

Of the 111 fish, 19 were too small for broodstock use or died on site, leaving 92 potential broodfish. The report notes that nearly all of these died before integration into the broodstock. Telemetry work (Marsh 1997) indicated that larger radioed bonytail showed an affinity for the Arrowhead area.

**Fish community (2001).** Ten species were captured. Eight are introduced and two are native. The [[Threadfin Shad]], the major forage fish of Lake Mojave, was absent from the catch.

**Genetic concerns.** The authors cite [[Genetic Diversity]] limitations in the hatchery stock. Hedrick et al. (2000) is cited as indicating that even one wild fish would increase broodstock genetic diversity by about 20%.

## Key Findings

### Methods

- **Collection trips:** 2-5 April, 16-19 April, 30 April-3 May, 14-17 May, 29-31 May, and 19-22 June 2001.
- **Gear:** Six to eight trammel nets, each 100 m x 2 m x 2 m, with 25 cm outer and 2.5 cm inner mesh, fished for three to four nights per trip. Nets were set in the early evening, checked late at night and early morning, and cleaned on return to camp.
- **Handling:** Native fish were measured for total length (mm) and weight (g), scanned for wire and [[PIT Tagging|PIT tags]], and PIT-tagged if untagged. Introduced fish were counted and returned to the lake.
- **Transport protocol (planned for any hatchery transfer):** 30 gallons of oxygen-aerated water containing MS-222, stress coat, 1% rock salt solution, and Chlorotetracycline (Chlorotamine T). Responsibility transferred to WBNFH on handoff.

### Effort and Catch

- **Total effort:** 135 net sets of about 12 hours each, totaling 13,500 m of net and 1,620 hours of netting.
- **Total catch:** 987 fish across all trips.
- **Per-trip totals (Table 4):** 74 (2-5 April), 95 (16-19 April), 169 (30 April-3 May), 270 (14-17 May), 169 (29-31 May), and 210 (19-22 June).
- **Catch per net:** Ranged from 3.2 to 9.6 fish per net. Catch per 30 m of net ranged from 1.1 to 3.2.
- **Composition:** [[Common Carp]] dominated the catch (about 64.5% in Table 3 totals), followed by sunfishes ([[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]]) and [[Striped Bass]]. Catfish ([[Channel Catfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]]) made up a small share. [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] were also captured.

### Native Species Results

- **Bonytail:** Three were captured. Lengths were 264, 276, and 277 mm total length, with weights of 121, 126, and 125 g. One was a gravid female with a dorsal wire tag. It died during processing. The other two were not sexed or tagged, were PIT-tagged, and were released.
- **Razorback sucker:** Five were captured, with lengths of 560 to 659 mm (recorded values: 609, 657, 560, 659 mm). Two were recaptures. One fish, apparently a repatriated individual, escaped before processing. The untagged fish were PIT-tagged and released.

### Conclusions and Recommendations

- The failure to collect bonytail large enough for broodstock was described as disappointing. The authors raise the concern that larger wild fish may no longer be present in the system.
- The presence of smaller, stocked individuals is encouraging, and the authors argue that larger fish should not be ruled out. Wild fish were still thought to be present in 1997 (Marsh 1997b).
- Given the genetic concerns and the aging broodstock, the authors consider recovery of additional wild fish the highest priority.
- **Planned work:** Further collections are scheduled for March-May 2002, alternating between Arrowhead Cove and Cottonwood East, using the same methods. A supplement is to be produced by 31 August 2002.
