## Executive Summary

This document is a **U.S. District Court ruling** (D.N.M., Judge Howard C. Bratton, 1994) in *Board of County Commissioners of the County of Catron v. United States Fish and Wildlife Service*, concerning the regulatory process behind designating **critical habitat** for two federally threatened freshwater fish species: the **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) and the **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*). While the case is primarily a legal/administrative dispute over compliance with the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA), it contains substantive scientific-regulatory history on the listing timeline, habitat designation process, and specific environmental/hydrological threats identified for these two imperiled southwestern cyprinid fishes. The court found that critical habitat designation is subject to NEPA review, set aside the FWS's 1994 critical habitat rule for both species, and enjoined its enforcement pending NEPA compliance.

## Background & Description

- **Species involved:**
  - **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) — a small benthic cyprinid native to the Gila River basin.
  - **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*, syn. *Rhinichthys cobitis*) — a small benthic cyprinid co-occurring with spikedace in the same river systems.
- **Listing history:**
  - Both species were **proposed for listing as Threatened** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] on **June 18, 1985** (50 Fed. Reg. 25380 for loach minnow; 50 Fed. Reg. 28390 for spikedace).
  - **Final listing as Threatened** occurred in 1986:
    - Spikedace: 51 Fed. Reg. 23769 (July 1, 1986)
    - Loach Minnow: 51 Fed. Reg. 39468 (Oct. 28, 1986)
  - At the time of final listing, FWS determined **critical habitat was "not then determinable"** for either species and extended the designation deadline to **June 18, 1987**, per 16 U.S.C. § 1533(b)(6)(C)(ii).
  - Despite the statutory extension, FWS did not finalize critical habitat designation for nearly **seven years**. Instead, on **September 30, 1991**, FWS adopted separate **species recovery plans** without first designating critical habitat.
  - Litigation pressure (a related Arizona case, *Greater Gila Biodiversity Project v. FWS*, No. 93-1913, D. Ariz.) prompted FWS to finally issue **final critical habitat designation** rules:
    - Loach minnow: 59 Fed. Reg. 10898 (March 8, 1994)
    - Spikedace: 59 Fed. Reg. 10906 (March 8, 1994)
  - These rules became **effective April 7, 1994**.
- **Procedural/regulatory posture:** FWS asserted (per an October 25, 1983 Federal Register notice, 48 Fed. Reg. 49244, and reliance on *Pacific Legal Foundation v. Andrus*, 657 F.2d 829 (6th Cir. 1981)) that ESA § 4(a) critical habitat actions were categorically exempt from NEPA environmental assessment/EIS requirements. The court rejected this position, following *Douglas County v. Babbitt*, 810 F. Supp. 1470 (D. Or. 1992), holding that **critical habitat designation is a "major federal action" subject to NEPA review**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range/Habitat:** Both species inhabit streams and rivers within the **Gila River drainage** in Catron County, New Mexico, and adjacent Arizona watersheds — part of the arid Southwestern U.S. riverine ecosystem.
- **Identified environmental and anthropogenic pressures/interactions cited by the litigating county** (as context for socioeconomic/habitat conflict, not direct fish biology, but relevant to habitat management):
  - Flooding and stream erosion dynamics affecting channel/habitat structure.
  - Land use changes, subdivision of ranches, and water rights allocation affecting flow regimes.
  - Human infrastructure interactions: roads, bridges, flood control structures, and water diversion structures within or adjacent to designated critical habitat.
  - Co-occurrence of critical habitat designation with habitat for the **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]**, indicating overlapping multi-species conservation zones in the same riparian/riverine corridor.
  - Reference to potential effects on a local **fish hatchery**, suggesting managed fish populations or stocking activity in the region intersecting with wild critical habitat.
- **Conservation management framework:** FWS's 1991 recovery plans for spikedace and loach minnow were adopted *prior to* finalizing critical habitat, reflecting a fragmented regulatory sequence (recovery planning preceding habitat protection instead of following it).

## Key Findings

1. **Threatened Status Confirmed:** Both the spikedace and loach minnow are legally classified as **Threatened** species under the ESA, with final listing rules issued in 1986.
2. **Critical Habitat Designation Delay:** FWS took approximately **seven years** (from the 1987 extended statutory deadline to March 1994) to finalize critical habitat for these species, despite ESA's one-year (extendable) statutory mandate.
3. **NEPA Applies to Critical Habitat Designation:** The court held, following *Douglas County v. Babbitt*, that **NEPA procedural requirements (environmental assessment/EIS) apply to ESA critical habitat designations** — rejecting FWS's categorical exemption argument based on *Pacific Legal Foundation v. Andrus*.
4. **Critical Habitat Rule Vacated:** The FWS's March 1994 final rules designating critical habitat for spikedace and loach minnow (59 Fed. Reg. 10898 and 10906) were **set aside** by the court due to NEPA non-compliance, with the vacatur stayed pending appeal.
5. **Preliminary Injunction Granted:** Defendants (FWS) were enjoined from implementing/enforcing the critical habitat designation for both species until NEPA compliance was achieved, based on findings of irreparable environmental injury absent injunctive relief and a substantial likelihood of the plaintiff prevailing on the merits.
6. **Standing Established via Environmental & Procedural Injury:** Catron County was found to have standing under NEPA based on **geographic nexus** to the critical habitat area and procedural injury from FWS's failure to prepare an environmental assessment/EIS — reinforcing the precedent that procedural NEPA injury alone can support standing in critical habitat litigation.
