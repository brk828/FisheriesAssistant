# Cui-ui Recovery Team Meeting Minutes - October 23, 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This document contains the official minutes from the [[Cui-ui]] Recovery Team meeting held October 23, 1989 in Reno, Nevada. The meeting addressed critical issues in developing a recovery plan for [[Chasmistes cujus]], an endangered sucker species endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]]. Key concerns included hybridization with [[Lake Tahoe Sucker]]s, recovery plan timeline extensions, and urgent management recommendations for the upcoming spawning season at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]].

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] ([[Chasmistes cujus]]) is an endangered member of the family [[Catostomidae]] endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. The species spawns in the [[Truckee River]] and its tributaries. The Recovery Team consisted of five members: Thomas Strekal (team leader), Wendell Minckley, Gary Scoppettone, Gary Vineyard, and Paul Wagner, with Chester Buchanan serving as the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] liaison.

The team was originally instructed to use 1984 guidelines and an abbreviated format for the recovery plan, but was redirected to use draft 1989 guidelines and the classical recovery plan format. The meeting addressed administrative procedures, leadership continuity, and critical biological concerns regarding the species' survival.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recovery of [[Cui-ui]] is complicated by multiple factors including the species' biological and genetic characteristics, habitat requirements, and water availability. The primary ecological concern is securing the genome through establishment of a minimum viable population. 

Hybridization between [[Cui-ui]] and [[Lake Tahoe Sucker]]s represents a critical threat. Physical characteristics of many individuals suggest hybrid presence, though electrophoretic analysis by Dr. Peter Brussard ([[University of Nevada Reno]]) was incomplete at the time of the meeting. Introgression of the [[Cui-ui]] genome through hybridization may jeopardize the continued existence of the species.

The population structure shows dominance by the 1963 and 1969 year classes, which are considered genetically pure and critical for recovery efforts.

## Key Findings

**Recovery Plan Timeline:**
- Original deadline of January 31, 1990 deemed impossible due to hybrid issue complexity, modeling requirements, and need for additional information
- Revised target date: January 31, 1991
- Information gathering to continue for 6-8 months, including interviews with knowledgeable individuals

**Hybridization Crisis:**
- Electrophoretic analysis delayed; Dr. Brussard to use facilities at [[University of California Davis]] or [[Montana State University]]
- Hybrid presence assumed based on physical characteristics pending genetic confirmation
- Team recommended immediate establishment of task force by Regional Director to oversee spawning run management at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]

**Management Recommendations:**
- Prevent purported hybrids from spawning until extent of hybridization determined and mechanisms understood
- Proposed interdiction methods: electric barrier across delta to prevent upstream passage; culling hybrids at fishway entrance
- Allow only individuals from 1963 and 1969 year classes to proceed up fishway

**Hatchery Program:**
- Hatchery rearing designated as "fail safe" system for maintaining genetic purity and variation
- Recommended collection: approximately 500 females and numerous males from 1969 year class annually
- Protocol: collect only a few hundred eggs per female to produce small, genetically pure year class nearly every year

**Leadership Transition:**
- Team leader Strekal assigned 30-day temporary detail to [[Bureau of Reclamation]] in Salt Lake City for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] EIS coordination
- Permanent assignment likely by February 1990
- Team consensus: premature for leadership change despite reduced availability
