# Investigations of Commercial Fishery Potentials in Reservoirs - Quarterly Report

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #reservoir-management #commercial-fishery

## Executive Summary

This quarterly report (1 April - 30 June 1966) documents the initial phase of Project 6-1-R-1, investigating commercial fishery potentials in Arizona reservoirs. The project established operational infrastructure including equipment procurement, personnel hiring, and preliminary fish collections across six reservoirs on the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] systems. [[Threadfin Shad]] were found abundant in all reservoirs studied, with spawning observations documented. [[Bigmouth Buffalo]] populations showed disease symptoms in [[Apache Lake]], and potential hybridization was noted in [[Roosevelt Lake]]. The project successfully deployed multiple sampling methods including gill nets, electrofishing, and plankton sampling equipment.

## Background & Description

This project, conducted by [[Arizona State University]] in cooperation with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], aims to assess commercial fishery potentials in Arizona reservoir systems. The study focuses on six reservoirs: four on the Salt River ([[Apache Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[Canyon Lake]], [[Saguaro Lake]]) and two on the Verde River ([[Bartlett Lake]], [[Horseshoe Lake]]).

Field personnel included Mr. James E. Johnson (M.A. Purdue University, Ph.D. student) as project leader on a one-year contract, and Mr. Willard E. Barber (B.S. Arizona State University, Master's student) as temporary field assistant, scheduled for replacement in July.

Equipment procurement included:
- Four experimental gill nets for smaller fishes (meshes 0.5 to 1.5 inches bar measure, 150 feet long)
- One 300-foot gill net (meshes 1 through 6 inches)
- Bulk netting (meshes 2 through 5 inches) for constructing four additional experimental nets
- One trammel net (300 feet long)
- Two hoop nets with bulk netting for leads and wings
- Electrofishing equipment (2600-watt A.C. generator and accessories)
- One fiberglass boat with eighteen-horsepower engine and trailer

Additional equipment provided by the Arizona Game and Fish Department or University included: recording bathythermograph, [[Clarke-Bumpus Plankton Sampler]], haul-nets for plankton sampling, aluminum boat (14 feet long), 900-watt A.C. generator for electrofishing, seines (10 to 60 feet long), two additional hoop nets, and laboratory equipment including microscopes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study encompasses reservoir ecosystems in central Arizona, representing impounded sections of the Salt River and Verde River drainages. These reservoirs support diverse fish assemblages including native and introduced species.

Sampling effort to date (including pre-contract collections from February-March 1966):
- [[Apache Lake]]: 157 hours
- [[Bartlett Lake]]: 24 hours
- [[Canyon Lake]]: 48 hours
- [[Horseshoe Lake]]: 8 hours
- [[Roosevelt Lake]]: 264 hours
- [[Saguaro Lake]]: 54 hours

During sampling periods, four to nine nets were fished simultaneously with electrofishing operations. The report notes that available data do not yet allow reliable estimates of relative abundance or complete species composition.

## Key Findings

**Species Distribution Patterns:**

[[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) were found abundant in all reservoirs studied, appearing most abundant in Roosevelt, Saguaro, and Bartlett lakes. Spawning commenced in late April and continued through the reporting period. Detailed observations documented spawning behavior, embryonic development, and post-embryonic changes for the first 7 days after hatching.

**Buffalo Species Distribution and Health:**

[[Bigmouth Buffalo]] (*Ictiobus cyprinellus*) occurred in all four Salt River lakes but were absent from the Verde River drainage. The Roosevelt Lake population appeared aberrant and may represent a hybrid swarm involving bigmouth buffalo and one or both of the other buffalo species. 

A disease outbreak affected bigmouth buffalo in Apache Lake from February through April, characterized by:
- Emaciation
- Fluid accumulation in the visceral cavity
- Fin disintegration (possibly secondary condition)

This disease was not observed in other lakes. A similar epidemic has been reported in this species from [[Tennessee Valley Authority]] lakes in Tennessee and Kentucky.

[[Smallmouth Buffalo]] (*Ictiobus bubalus*) were taken in small numbers in Apache and Canyon lakes. [[Black Buffalo]] (*Ictiobus niger*) were found only in Apache Lake and were rare.

**Other Species:**

[[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) were present throughout all lakes. Largest individuals occurred in Canyon and Saguaro lakes, but the species appeared most abundant in Roosevelt Lake.

Commercial fishermen (Messrs. Sexton and Marley operating on Apache and Roosevelt lakes, respectively) provided advice and numerous specimens of carp and buffalo species for the study.

**Complete Species Checklist by Reservoir:**

Present in all six reservoirs: [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Largemouth Bass]]

Apache Lake (most diverse): All buffalo species, [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Gila Mountain-sucker]], carp, multiple catfish species, centrarchids, [[Yellow Bass]], [[Walleye]]

Bartlett Lake: Threadfin shad, channel catfish, bluegill, green sunfish, largemouth bass, yellow bass

Canyon Lake: Threadfin shad, bigmouth buffalo, smallmouth buffalo, carp, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, black bullhead, bluegill, largemouth bass

Horseshoe Lake: Threadfin shad, channel catfish, bluegill, largemouth bass

Roosevelt Lake: Threadfin shad, razorback sucker, bigmouth buffalo, carp, channel catfish, bluegill, green sunfish, largemouth bass, yellow bass, walleye, sailfin molly

Saguaro Lake: Threadfin shad, carp, channel catfish, bluegill, green sunfish, largemouth bass, [[Gambusia]], [[Longjaw Mudsucker]]

**Project Infrastructure:**

All sampling gear became operational and field-tested during the quarter. The University provided temporary space including storage and research areas, with permanent space allotments scheduled for the next quarter. Curatorial space, offices, and specimen storage areas were in use by the end of the reporting period.
