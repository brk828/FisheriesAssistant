## Executive Summary

This station profile documents operations at the **[[Tishomingo National Fish Hatchery]]**, a federal aquaculture facility established in 1928 under Region II of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Located near Tishomingo, Oklahoma, the hatchery's mission centers on propagating fish for stocking federal waters, federally-owned/state-managed waters, mitigation waters, and for restoration of depleted, nationally significant fishery resources. The facility specializes in **[[Striped Bass]]** (and hybrid striped bass), **[[Paddlefish]]**, **[[Smallmouth Bass]]**, and **[[Channel Catfish]]** production. In FY1991, the hatchery achieved notable production milestones including tank-spawning of adult striped bass with jar-hatched eggs yielding 162,000 fingerlings, contributing to a total striped bass fingerling production of 324,950. The facility also produced 2,590 ten-inch paddlefish. Distribution data spans multiple states (OK, TX, NM, WY, CO), with total fish distributed increasing substantially from FY1989 (1,175,592) to FY1991 (2,137,391).

- **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** and **[[Channel Catfish]]**: Distributed as finished fingerling/stocking-size fish; no morphometric data provided.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Facility Role**: The hatchery functions as an artificial propagation and stocking node within a broader freshwater fisheries management network, supplying federal reservoirs, state-managed waters, and mitigation waters (waters compensating for habitat loss, e.g., from dam construction).
- **Target Habitats for Stocking**: Fish produced at this station are distributed into federal waters, other federal agency waters, state university research waters, and other state-managed waters across a multi-state region (Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico, Wyoming, Colorado).
- **Species-Habitat Associations**:
  - **[[Striped Bass]]** and hybrid striped bass are typically stocked into large reservoirs as a recreational and predatory sport fish, filling a piscivorous niche in reservoir ecosystems.
  - **[[Paddlefish]]**, a filter-feeding planktivore native to large river systems (notably the Mississippi River basin), represents a restoration-focused species given its status as a nationally significant, often depleted resource.
  - **[[Smallmouth Bass]]** are typically associated with clearer, rockier reservoir and riverine habitats.
  - **[[Channel Catfish]]** occupy a generalist benthic niche in reservoirs and rivers.
- **Institutional Partnerships**: A Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) with the **Agricultural Research Service** ($30,000 in FY1991) indicates interagency collaboration, likely relevant to fish health, nutrition, or production research. Historical partnerships also included the U.S. Army and Navy (per cost recoverables table), suggesting mitigation stocking tied to military installation water bodies.

## Key Findings

- **Station Establishment**: Founded in 1928 under the White Act; part of USFWS Region II.
- **FY1991 Production Highlights**:
  - Tank-spawned 3 adult **[[Striped Bass]]**; jar-hatched eggs produced 162,000 fingerlings.
  - Total striped bass fingerling production: **324,950**.
  - Produced **2,590** ten-inch **[[Paddlefish]]** using rectangular and circular tanks.
  - Recorded **7,000 visitors** in FY1991.
- **Funding Trends (1987–1991)**: Operations budget fluctuated between $237,000–$300,800; maintenance funding varied widely ($20,000–$195,000), with a notable spike in 1989 ($195,000). Staffing (FTE) declined slightly from 8.0 (1987–1989) to 6.98 (1991).
- **Cost Recoverables**: Consistent ~$25,500/year from Agricultural Research Service (ARS) partnership across 1987–1991, plus smaller variable amounts (~$1.5–5.6K) from Army/Navy sources.
- **Distribution Trends**:
  - FY1989: 1,175,592 total fish distributed.
  - FY1990: 2,306,732 total fish distributed (major increase).
  - FY1991: 2,137,391 total fish distributed across federal, state, university, and other waters.
  - FY1990 species distributed: **[[Smallmouth Bass]]**, **[[Striped Bass]]**, hybrid striped bass, **[[Channel Catfish]]**, sent to Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico, Wyoming, and Colorado.
- **Infrastructure**: Facility includes 4 residences, a fish holding house, a hatching-rearing building, 5 pump houses, office/garage complexes, storage buildings, a chemical storage building, and an oil/paint storage house — indicating a moderately sized, multi-function hatchery campus.
- **Data Limitation Note**: Distribution figures exclude egg and fry data, which are instead reported under "Other Information" (e.g., the 162,000 STB fingerlings derived from jar-hatched eggs).
