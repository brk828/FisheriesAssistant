# Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program (ISMP) on the Colorado, White, and Yampa Rivers in Colorado, FY2000

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ptychocheilus

## Executive Summary

This FY2000 annual report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] closes the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP), a standardized fish monitoring effort run by the [[Colorado Division Of Wildlife]] and led by principal investigator [[William R Elmblad]]. The program tracked population trends of the endangered [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and sympatric fishes, including the [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], in the [[Colorado River]], [[White River]], and [[Yampa River]]. The project ran from 1986 to 2000. Its final task was data submission by January 15, 2001. The program was discontinued in 2001 so that effort could go to population estimates for Colorado pikeminnow. Funds were 93% spent, and all FY2000 work was reported as 100% complete.

## Background & Description

The ISMP was designed to standardize data collection so that trends in populations of the Colorado pikeminnow and co-occurring native and nonnative fishes could be assessed. Its work was linked to the Recovery Program's support action plan, which calls for a standardized monitoring program (RIPRAP item V.A.1).

Sampling covered three components:

- **Late-juvenile/adult Colorado pikeminnow monitoring.** Fish were captured by [[Electrofishing]] on the Colorado River (RM 110–122 region, sampled in multiple segments), the White River (RM 104.3–95), and the Yampa River (RM 105–95, 80–77, and 54–49). All Colorado pikeminnow were recorded, and [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging]] (PIT tagging) was applied to new fish. Humpback chub and razorback sucker captures were also recorded.
- **Sympatric fish monitoring.** Fish were sampled by electrofishing in five-mile ISMP reaches. Each mile was sampled along one bank, with two dipnetters collecting as many fish as possible. Captured fish were identified, measured for total length, and at least 20 fish per species per reach were weighed.
- **Post-larval/Age 0 Colorado pikeminnow monitoring.** Larval fish were collected by [[Seining]] in backwaters on the Colorado River (RM 195–140). Habitat data were recorded, and samples were sent to the [[Colorado State University Larval Fish Lab]] for identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three rivers in the study support a mixed assemblage of endangered natives and abundant nonnative fishes. The [[Kenney Reservoir]] system influences the White River below it. Stocked Colorado pikeminnow from Kenney Reservoir (stocked 1988–1990) were a possible source of fish appearing in the White River, which the report examined through marks such as [[Fin Clip Marking]] and internal tetracycline. The Yampa River above Cross Mountain (Juniper Springs and Maybell areas) is dominated by nonnative fishes, while the Yampa below Cross Mountain (Lily Park area) and the Colorado River are predominantly native.

## Key Findings

### Late-Juvenile/Adult Colorado Pikeminnow

- **White River:** The [[Catch Per Effort]] (C/E) for Colorado pikeminnow continued a strong increasing trend that began around 1994. In 2000, 33 Colorado pikeminnow were captured and 6 observed on the sampling dates in late May and early June (total miles sampled: 9.3).
- **Colorado River:** C/E was stable and possibly slightly increasing. In 2000, the captured count for Colorado pikeminnow was partly illegible in the scan. Captures also included 3 humpback chub and 4 razorback sucker, with 5 Colorado pikeminnow and 3 humpback chub observed. A total of 879 fish were collected in sympatric sampling on the Colorado River.
- **Yampa River:** C/E was stable and possibly slightly increasing. In 2000, 16 Colorado pikeminnow were captured and 1 observed. A total of 224 fish were collected in sympatric sampling on the Yampa River.

### Hatchery-Origin Fish and Fin Mark Evidence

- Stocked fish from Kenney Reservoir were not seen with marks until 1997, when five fish with asymmetric pelvic fin ray counts (possibly regenerated fin clips) appeared. These made up 18% of the 1997 catch.
- In 1998, asymmetric fish and one fish with a complete fin clip made up 22% of the catch. In 1999, seven fish with asymmetric pelvic fin rays made up 18% of the catch. In 2000, eight such fish made up 24%.
- No Colorado pikeminnow with asymmetric pelvic fin rays were found in the Colorado or Yampa rivers.

### Size Structure in the White River

- Small Colorado pikeminnow (<500 mm total length) declined in 2000. They were absent in 1991–1993, averaged 22% of the catch in 1994–1997, 44% in 1998, and 50% in 1999, then fell to 18% in 2000.
- Of the small fish, 25% had asymmetric pelvic fins in 1997–1999. None had them in 2000. The report suggests that Kenney Reservoir fish may have grown past 500 mm, so future small fish entering the upper White River may be wild-origin.

### Sympatric Species

- Results from 2000 were similar to the three-year average (1995–1997).
- Fish density, expressed as fish per hour, was highest in the White River below Kenney Reservoir (581 in 2000; 393 three-year average) and lowest in the Yampa River above Cross Mountain (31 in 2000; 50 three-year average). The Colorado River reach had 200 fish per hour in 2000, and the Yampa below Cross Mountain had 155.
- Native species made up about 94% of the Colorado River catch and 92% of the White River catch below Kenney Reservoir in 2000.
- In the Yampa River above Cross Mountain, [[White Sucker]] and white/flannelmouth hybrid suckers were the majority of the catch.
- Table 4 lists about 25 species codes, including [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Green Sunfish]].

### Post-Larval/Age 0 Colorado Pikeminnow

- No post-larval/Age 0 Colorado pikeminnow were identified in the field in 1999. The last identification of this species in field or collections was in 1992.
- In 1999 larval collections (35 collections from 18 backwaters, sampled September 20–22, 1999), 8% of fish were native, mostly Gila species. Nonnative fishes included [[Red Shiner]] (42%), [[Fathead Minnow]] (29%), and [[Sand Shiner]] (15%).

### Recommendations

- The ISMP was discontinued in 2001 so that effort could be directed to population estimates for Colorado pikeminnow across the three rivers.
- Once estimates are complete, the ISMP may be restarted as a tool to follow future population trends.

### Project Status and Budget

- **Status:** On track and nearing completion. FY2000 was the final year.
- **Funds provided:** $34,200. **Funds expended:** $24,254. **Difference:** $9,950.
- **Reasons for the difference:** Indirect costs of $2,230 that could not be spent; an outboard motor not purchased ($4,100); and a second seasonal employee not hired.
- **Publication charges from Recovery Program funds:** $0.
- **Data submission:** Field data were to be in database format by December 31, 2000, and were to be submitted to the ISMP database manager at the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Grand Junction, by January 15, 2001.
