# Fundulus lariversi: A New Miocene Fossil Cyprinodont from Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

Thomas Lugaski (1977, *The Wasmann Journal of Biology* 35(2): 203–211) describes *Fundulus lariversi*, a new fossil cyprinodont fish from the Miocene Siebert Tuff near Tonopah, Nye County, central Nevada. The species is based on a holotype and two complete paratypes, and it is compared morphologically and meristically with living and fossil cyprinodontids from [[Nevada]] and adjacent regions. The author places the species in [[Fundulus]] because it has pelvic fins and conical teeth, and proposes that it may be ancestral to the Pliocene *Fundulus* fishes and to the living [[Empetrichthys]]. Its K-Ar-dated age (about 16–18 million years) is presented as making it the oldest described cyprinodontid fish.

## Background & Description

The front matter of the source is a reference list (Rogick, Sebestyén, Weibach, Weise, Williams and others) on the freshwater entoproct *Urnatella gracilis*. It belongs to an unrelated publication and is not analyzed here. The main article begins on page 203.

The late Tertiary fish fauna of the western [[Great Basin]] was poorly known when the paper was written. Previously reported Miocene fish from northwestern Nevada included only *Salmo cyniclope*. Several Pliocene fishes had been recorded from western Nevada, and five cyprinodontids from the [[Mojave Desert]] region ranged from Pliocene to Pleistocene. The new material comes from Miocene lakes and streams near Tonopah and falls between the Pliocene *F. nevadensis* fauna about 161 km north and the Pliocene *F. curryi* fauna about 242 km south.

**Type material**

- **Holotype:** BSN 60-1 (Biological Society of Nevada, Lugaski collection). Standard length (SL) 48 mm, total length (TL) 57 mm, in nearly perfect condition. Collected 10 June 1975 by Thomas Lugaski and [[Ira La Rivers]] at the Tonopah sand dunes road-fill pit.
- **Paratypes:** BSN 60-2 (32 mm SL, 40 mm TL, nearly perfect) and BSN 60-3 (26.5 mm SL, 33 mm TL, nearly perfect). Specimens BSN 60-4, 7 and 11 are fragmentary, and 60-7 and 60-11 were too poorly preserved for counts.
- All types are deposited in the Biology Museum of the Biological Society of Nevada.
- **Etymology:** Named after the late Dr. Ira La Rivers, a student of Great Basin fishes.

**Diagnostic morphology**

- Body elongate and compressed behind, robust anteriorly, with a moderately large head.
- Snout short and depressed, with a strongly developed lower jaw projecting beyond the upper jaw.
- Pelvic fins extremely reduced.
- Caudal fin large and slightly convex; the holotype's caudal fin depth is 18% greater than maximum body depth.
- Dorsal fin origin lies over vertebrae 16–17 and directly over the anal fin origin (holotype).
- Scales of moderate size; 11–14 in a diagonal series (holotype), with 5–7 radii (average 6).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geology and age**

- The Siebert Tuff is fluvio-lacustrine, with ash-fall and lapillistone. It consists of water-reworked volcaniclastic rocks deposited in streams and shallow lakes (Bonham and Garside, 1974).
- Henshaw (1942) described a Barstovian mammal fauna from the Siebert Tuff 16 km north of Tonopah.
- The Siebert Tuff unconformably overlies the Fraction Tuff (17.8 m.y. K-Ar) and is overlain by the Oddie Rhyolite and the Brougher Dacite (16.2 m.y. K-Ar). These dates place *F. lariversi* in the late Hemingfordian to early Barstovian Miocene.

**Type locality**

- Located 17.6 km northwest of Tonopah (sec. 13, T4N, R41E), east of Nevada State Highway 89 and west of the San Antonio Mountains, in a road-fill pit.
- Fish occurred in a single layer about one-third of the way up a 7.8 m exposure. Few intact fish were found, but numerous small fragments were scattered through the layer.
- No other fossils were recovered from the deposit.

**Paleoenvironment**

The author infers a shallow to moderately deep lake or marsh rather than a stream with rapid deposition. Evidence includes the fine volcanic dust, the deposit's size (7.8 m high and several hundred meters in extent), and the good preservation of several fossils.

## Key Findings

**1. Taxonomic placement in *Fundulus***

- Cyprinodontidae is represented in Nevada by four living and fossil genera: [[Cyprinodon]], [[Crenichthys]], [[Empetrichthys]] and [[Fundulus]].
- The new fossil is not a *Cyprinodon* (fewer anal rays, 9–11 versus 13; more pectoral rays, 14–18 versus 12; tricuspid jaw teeth in *Cyprinodon*).
- It is not a *Crenichthys* (no pelvic fin in *Crenichthys*, 28 caudal rays versus 19; bicuspid teeth).
- It shows close affinity with *Empetrichthys*, which shares conical jaw teeth and similar dorsal, anal and caudal ray counts. However, all *Empetrichthys* lack pelvic fins, which separates them from *Fundulus*.
- Because it has pelvic fins and conical teeth on the dentary and premaxillary bones, it is assigned to *Fundulus*.

**2. Differences from other Nevada and southern California *Fundulus***

- Versus *F. nevadensis* (Pliocene, western Nevada): the dorsal fin lies well in advance of the anal fin in *F. nevadensis* but is level with it in *F. lariversi*. *F. nevadensis* has 23 caudal rays versus 19, and *F. lariversi* is larger and more robust.
- Versus *F. curryi*, *F. eulepis* and *F. davidae* (Pliocene, Mojave region): *F. lariversi* has more pelvic rays (8 versus 6), fewer pectoral rays (12 versus 14–16), and a deeper head (4.5–5.5 versus 3.5 times into SL). Dorsal fin placement also differs from each of the three.

**3. Age and phylogenetic significance**

- The K-Ar dates support a Miocene age. The author concludes that *F. lariversi* is the oldest described cyprinodontid fish.
- Uyeno and Miller (1962) proposed that *Empetrichthys* derives from *Fundulus* stock before the Middle Pliocene. Because *F. lariversi* is Miocene and its morphology closely matches *Empetrichthys*, Lugaski suggests it may represent the ancestral stock of *Empetrichthys* and of other Nevada *Fundulus*.

**4. Limitations noted in the text**

- The holotype and paratypes are the only specimens described, and they come from one layer at a single locality.
- Several paratypes are incomplete, so some counts are missing for them.
- Table 2 lists *E. erdisi* pectoral counts as lacking, so some comparisons rest on incomplete data.
- The phylogenetic hypothesis is framed as a possibility ("may represent") rather than a tested result.

**Summary**

The paper establishes *Fundulus lariversi* as a new Miocene cyprinodontid from the Tonopah area, based on three well-preserved specimens. It supports a *Fundulus*–*Empetrichthys* relationship and offers an early-Miocene record for an ancestral lineage in the Great Basin.
