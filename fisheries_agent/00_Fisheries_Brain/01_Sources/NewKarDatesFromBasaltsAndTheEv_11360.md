# Late Tertiary and Quaternary Evolution of the Southern Rio Grande Rift: New K-Ar Dates on Basalts

#geology #tectonics #rio-grande-rift

## Executive Summary

This paper reports 18 new [[K-Ar Dating|K-Ar dates]] on basalts from the southern [[Rio Grande Rift]] in south-central New Mexico. The authors use these dates, together with published data, to reconstruct two extensional regimes separated by a [[Middle Miocene Magma Gap]], and to test whether the rift's development fits a regional model of an eastward-expanding [[Slab-Free Triangle]] in the [[Basin and Range Province]]. The main conclusions are that early back-arc extension began by about 28 m.y. B.P., that modern block faulting was largely established by about 5 m.y. B.P., that the [[Ancestral Rio Grande]] reached the southern rift by 3 to 4 m.y. B.P. and entrenched its modern valley between 0.7 and 0.5 m.y. B.P., and that Basin and Range tectonism and mafic volcanism have migrated progressively eastward over the past 20 m.y.

## Background & Description

The study was carried out by R. Seager, Shafiqullah, and W. Hawley, working at New Mexico State University, the University of Arizona, and the New Mexico Bureau of Mines and Mineral Resources. The authors describe the work as a study of the tectonic and geomorphic history of the southern rift, set within the wider southern Basin and Range.

Two volcanic suites are distinguished:

- **Older suite (late Oligocene to early Miocene):** Calc-alkaline rocks ranging from latite to [[Basaltic Andesite]] and rare basalt. They have relatively high [[Strontium Isotope Ratios]] (0.704 to 0.709) and are chemically similar to the Oligocene volcanics of the [[Mogollon-Datil Plateau]].
- **Younger suite (latest Miocene to Quaternary):** True basalts, mostly [[Alkali-Olivine Basalt]] with some tholeiitic basalt. Initial strontium ratios are about 0.703 to 0.704.

The authors relate the older suite to back-arc or interarc extension and the younger suite to a late Miocene to Quaternary thermotectonic system beneath the rift.

The paper's sample set spans about 28 m.y. to 180,000 yr B.P. The authors note that 13 of the 18 new dates are about 5 m.y. or younger. Dated samples come from localities including the [[Palomas Basin]], the [[Potrillo Volcanic Field]], the [[Robledo Mountains]], the [[Caballo Mountains]], and the [[La Mesa Surface]] near [[Las Cruces]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document covers geology, not biology, so it contains no information on fish, invertebrates, plankton, or aquatic habitats. The environmental context it provides is physiographic and hydrological:

- **Drainage evolution:** Near-modern drainage ways were cut across fault blocks into [[Bolson]] basins by about 5.0 m.y. B.P. The [[Pliocene Drainage System]] was later deeply incised as the Rio Grande cut its present valley.
- **Sedimentary basins:** Basins such as the [[Palomas Basin]] and [[Hueco Bolson]] were closed or semi-closed for much of the Pliocene. Lake deposits, including those of "Lake Cabeza de Vaca," record the ancestral river's distributaries.
- **Mantle and heat-flow setting:** The authors argue that mantle upwelling through the slab-free window promotes lithospheric thinning and increased heat flow. New heat-flow and seismic-refraction data across southern New Mexico, however, did not confirm large-scale geophysical differences between the southern rift and adjacent Basin and Range areas (Daggett, 1981, as cited).

## Key Findings

### Two extensional regimes

- **Early regime (about 28 to 29 m.y. B.P.):** Marked by [[Basaltic Andesite]] flows with relatively high strontium ratios, broad and relatively deep northwest-trending basins, and incipient uplift of some fault-block mountains. The authors interpret it as back-arc extension behind a steepening slab.
- **Renewed or accelerated regime (latest Miocene to Pliocene, 10 to 3 m.y. B.P.):** Produced the modern north-trending basins and uplifts, regional uplift of the rift 1 to 2 km above sea level, and renewed alkali-olivine basalt volcanism.
- **Magma gap:** A gap of 8 to 15 m.y. separates the two volcanic episodes. The authors also describe a quiet period of as much as 16 m.y. in parts of the rift.

### Timing of latest rifting

- The main faulting episode that outlined the modern basins and ranges is bracketed between 9 and 3 m.y. B.P. in the southern rift.
- In the [[Robledo Mountains]], about 0.6 to 0.7 km of uplift and a similar amount of erosion occurred between 9.6 and 7.1 m.y. B.P.
- Uplift along the range-boundary fault since late Pliocene time has amounted to about another 100 m.
- At San Diego Mountain, a horst of Precambrian granite rose 2,400 m.

### Ancestral Rio Grande

- The ancestral river reached the Socorro area by 4.5 m.y. B.P. and the Elephant Butte area before 2.9 m.y. B.P.
- Basalt in basal [[Camp Rice Formation]] fanglomerate at the Caballo Mountains is dated 3.1 ± 0.07 m.y. B.P., indicating establishment of the river there by about 3.1 m.y. B.P.
- Volcanic ash about 2.0 m.y. old occurs in lower Camp Rice beds of the [[Hueco Bolson]].
- The river reached the southernmost rift basins before 2 m.y. B.P.
- The river entrenched its modern valley between 0.7 and 0.5 m.y. B.P. Entrenchment near [[Las Cruces]] was under way by 0.5 m.y. B.P.

The authors note a tension in the timing: basalt dates of about 0.5 m.y. imply that Camp Rice aggradation, La Mesa soil formation, and headward entrenchment from the Presidio basin to the Mesilla basin all took place within about 100,000 to 200,000 years. Earlier dates of 0.15 to 0.23 m.y. for the same flows were judged less consistent with the data.

### Eastward migration of Basin and Range tectonism

- The Basin and Range province shows an east-younging pattern. Culmination of block faulting is dated at 21 to 17 m.y. B.P. in southeastern California, 15 to 8 m.y. B.P. in central and southwestern Arizona, and 9.5 to 3 m.y. B.P. in the Rio Grande rift.
- Late Quaternary fault scarp density increases eastward, pointing to the most recent tectonic activity along the eastern margin, from southeastern Arizona into west Texas.
- A broad zone of mafic volcanism moved from southeastern California to the Rio Grande rift over the past 20 m.y., but has not yet reached the eastern edge of the province in far west Texas.

### Slab-Free Triangle model

- The authors support the model of Dickinson and Snyder (1979) of an [[Eastward-Expanding Slab-Free Triangle]]. Termination of subduction and the start of the [[San Andreas Transform]] about 29 m.y. B.P. created a slab-free region east of the transform.
- Upwelling mantle through this region may drive extension, volcanism, and uplift, and promote lithospheric thinning and increased heat flow.
- Modern extent of the slab-free area fits the present extent of the Basin and Range, Rio Grande rift, and Colorado Plateau provinces.

### Conclusions

- Early rift extension, in progress by 28 m.y. B.P., was closer to northeast-southwest than to east-west. It was related to back-arc spreading that was transitional with, but independent of, the main latest Miocene rift episode.
- Broad, deep northwest-trending early basins formed shortly after 26 m.y. B.P.
- The Robledo uplift was wholly or nearly wholly uplifted between 7.1 and 9.3 m.y. B.P., during renewed east-west extension.
- Alkali-olivine basalt volcanism accelerated during the culmination of rifting, peaking within the past 5 m.y.
- Basalts in the Potrillo field range from 1.2 to 0.18 m.y. old. Basalts in the Palomas field range from 5.2 to 3.0 m.y. old.
- The Rio Grande rift and west Texas fault blocks are the youngest parts of the southern Basin and Range.
