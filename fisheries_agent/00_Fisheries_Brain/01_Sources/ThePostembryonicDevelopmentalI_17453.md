# Postembryonic Developmental Instars of Diaptomus siciloides

#ichthyology #marine-biology #diaptomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 morphological study by G. W. Comita and D. M. Tommerdahl (Department of Zoology, North Dakota State University) describes every postembryonic instar of the freshwater calanoid copepod *Diaptomus siciloides* [[Lilljeborg]] 1889. Specimens came from a single population sampled from 15 April to 2 November 1955 in [[Severson Lake]], a small alkaline lake in Becker County, Minnesota. The authors illustrate six [[Nauplius Stage|nauplius]] and six [[Copepodid Stage|copepodid]] instars, with appendages drawn in 141 figures, and provide tables of armature, segmentation and body length. The work was supported by [[National Science Foundation]] grant 1257.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Provide morphology of larval and adult stages so that all life-cycle stages from one population could be identified and counted in a seasonal study. Prior literature (notably [[Grandori 1912]], [[Gurney 1931]], [[Wuthrich 1948]] and [[Ravera 1953]]) was found to lack detail, especially for immature copepodids.
- **Nauplius figures:** Earlier drawings by [[Ewers 1930]] were useful, but the authors noted differences from their own nauplius specimens.
- **Methods:** Drawings were made with a [[Camera Lucida]] under high dry (430x) and oil immersion, and later with phase contrast. Specimens were unstained and preserved in about 10% formaldehyde; molted skins were not used. Appendages were dissected, sometimes requiring 10 to 12 specimens because setae were broken.
- **Developmental scheme:** Diaptomids have 6 nauplius stages and 6 copepodid stages, the sixth copepodid being the adult. No evidence supported a seventh (oversize) instar, although oversize adults occur in two overlapping size classes that yield a bimodal size curve.
- **Egg:** Spherical, translucent, granular, with a slight yellowish tinge. Mean maximum diameter 0.126 mm and mean minimum 0.096 mm, largest on 29 June. A single [[Spermatophore]] measured 0.43 mm and was one of two attached to an adult female.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study lake:** Severson Lake, maximum depth 4.7 m, alkaline year-round (pH 7.5 to 8). Maximum summer temperature was 27°C. Strong stratification in June broke down in early July, after which the lake was nearly homothermal. The lake froze to about 1 m in winter 1955-1956.
- **Population context:** The copepod's cyclical activity was studied in this population; a broader limnological study of the lake was reported as in preparation.
- **Feeding:** Gut enlargement, presumed to be food, was absent in nauplius I, suggesting that stage is non-feeding. It was present in later nauplius stages.
- **Morphological variation:** The authors suggest that spines on the caudal end, shown by Ewers, may be absent or well developed depending on population.

## Key Findings

- **Length data:** Measurements were based on at least 400 specimens per instar. Median length ranged from 0.138 mm (nauplius I) to 0.311 mm (nauplius VI). In copepodids, median cephalothorax length ranged from 0.324 mm (copepodid I) to 0.780 mm (adult male) and 0.885 mm (adult female).
- **Body segments:** Total body segments rise from 6 in copepodid I to 7 (II), 8 (III), 9 (IV), and 10 (male V) and 11 (adult male). The cephalosome is one segment; the thorax has 5 free somites except in copepodid I, which has 4.
- **Antennule:** Uniramous and unchanged in segment count through the six nauplius stages. It has 11 discrete segments in copepodid I and develops into 25 segments by the adult stage, tracked by an armature-based segment accounting (Table 3).
- **Antenna:** Biramous throughout. The exopod has about 5 to 6 segments in nauplii and 9 in copepodids and adults. The endopod grows from 1 to 2 segments, and its setae increase from 10 to 15.
- **Mandible:** The masticatory process first appears in nauplius IV with 4 teeth, reaching 9 teeth in copepodids II onward (11 teeth noted in copepodid I). The exopod is unsegmented in nauplii and has 4 segments in copepodids.
- **Maxillule:** First appears in nauplius III as a single stout seta, becomes a 4-lobed appendage with 10 setae in nauplius IV, and forms a compact appendage with precoxa, coxa, basis, exopod and endopod by copepodid stages.
- **Maxilla:** Appears in nauplius V as a small two-setae lobe. Total setae increase from 19 or 20 (copepodid I) to 24 (copepodid II onward).
- **Maxilliped:** Appears in nauplius VI as two lobes; in copepodid I the sympod has three segments, and the endopod becomes segmented in copepodid IV.
- **Swimming legs:** The first four pairs begin as bilobed rudiments and become biramous. The first endopod stays two-segmented in adults; other rami reach three segments. The first two pairs are present by nauplius VI.
- **Fifth legs:** Appear in copepodid III as a bilobed rudiment. Sexual dimorphism appears first in copepodid IV (asymmetry in males) and becomes more pronounced in copepodid V and adults.
- **Abdomen:** Segments increase from 1 (copepodids I and II) to 2 (III), 3 (IV and females), 4 (male V) and 5 (adult male).
- **Body shape and ratio:** Body changes from an ellipse (nauplius I) to pear-shaped (nauplius VI) to club-shaped (copepodid I, cephalothorax to abdomen ratio about 3.23). The ratio falls to about 2.7 in adult males and 2.9 in adult females.
- **Figures and tables:** The paper provides 141 figures, 14 tables and 18 plates. Table 14 indexes figure numbers by instar and appendage.
