# Mid-Tertiary Conglomerates and Their Bearing on Transverse Range Tectonics, Southern California

#geology #tectonics #transverse-ranges

## Executive Summary

This is a geology paper, not an aquatic or fish study. Robert G. Bohannon (U.S. Geological Survey, Denver, Colo.) examines coarse redbed conglomerates of mid-Tertiary age in southern California. The units are the [[Simmler Formation]], [[Sespe Formation]], [[Plush Ranch Formation]], [[Vasquez Formation]], and the [[Formation of Diligencia]]. Their clast types, provenance, and paleocurrent indicators are used to reconstruct east-west trending, fault-bounded basins and uplifts. The author argues these structures crossed the present traces of the [[San Andreas Fault]] and [[San Gabriel Fault]] and were later offset by them. The document also includes a reference list and a [[Palinspastic Reconstruction]] of slip on major faults. It contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, or water-chemistry data.

## Background & Description

- **Study units:** Simmler, Sespe, Plush Ranch, and Vasquez formations, and the formation of Diligencia, studied at 10 localities (Figure 1).
- **Setting:** Coarse redbeds mark an interruption in the extensive marine deposition that prevailed in much of southern California during the Tertiary.
- **Timing:** Deposition occurred about the same time as, or just before, the onset of Tertiary volcanism and triple junction migration along the coast (Atwater, 1970), and just before the inferred initiation of strike-slip faulting on the [[San Andreas Fault]] system.
- **Methods:** Conglomerate facies analysis (clast types, provenance, paleocurrents), stratigraphic comparison among basins, and [[Potassium-Argon Dating]] of basalt flows in three areas.
- **Localities:** La Panza Range, Caliente Range, Cuyama Badlands, Lockwood Valley, Upper Sespe Creek, Canton Canyon, Charlie Canyon, Texas Canyon, Vasquez Rocks, and Orocopia Mountains.
- **Support:** N.S.F. grant DES 71-00498 (GA 30901) to John C. Crowell.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

No ecological or aquatic biota data are reported. The environmental context is geologic:

- **Depositional settings:** Alluvial fans, fault-scarp-fed conglomerates, and landslide breccias in fault-bounded basins.
- **Lacustrine elements:** The [[Formation of Diligencia]] includes a lakebed sequence of arkose, siltstone, limestone, and borates in the Orocopia Mountains.
- **Marine context:** Mid-Tertiary redbeds interrupted the extensive marine deposition of the Tertiary in southern California.
- **Source terrains:** Basement and igneous terrains included the [[Pelona Schist]], [[Orocopia Schist]], [[Lowe Granodiorite]], anorthosite and gabbro bodies, and the Mount Pinos and Frazier Mountain granitic and augen-gneiss terrains.

## Key Findings

**Age control**
- Basalt in the Plush Ranch Formation: 17.4±3.7 and 19.6±1.1 m.y. (OCR-corrected).
- Basalt in the Vasquez Formation: 23.9±0.8 and 24.9±2.1 m.y.
- Basalt in the formation of Diligencia: 22.4±2.9 m.y. (Crowell, 1973).
- Dated sections possibly overlap in age and may represent one tectonic and sedimentary phase that developed in different places over time.

**Basin-by-basin results**
- **[[Simmler Formation]]** (La Panza Range, northern Cuyama Badlands, eastern Caliente Range): Pebble imbrication indicates transport from southwest to northeast. Source was likely an active scarp near the [[Nacimiento Fault]]. Conglomerate clasts conflict with the sandstone and shale found across that fault, which the author attributes to erosion of a former conglomerate source or to strike slip.
- **[[Sespe Formation]]** (Upper Sespe Creek, Canton Canyon): Clasts include the 220-m.y.-old Lowe Granodiorite and anorthosite (plagioclase AN 45-50). Clasts reach up to 7 m, indicating a high-relief nearby source. Right slip of about 60 km on the [[San Gabriel Fault]] is required to link the Sespe to its source.
- **[[Plush Ranch Formation]]** (Lockwood Valley, members 1 to 5 of Carman, 1964): Member 1 paleocurrents are ambiguous. Breccia lenses derive from Mount Pinos, and the breccia along the [[Big Pine Fault]] derives from Frazier Mountain, about 12-14 km southeast. Left slip of 14 km is supported on the Big Pine fault.
- **[[Vasquez Formation]]**:
  - *Charlie Canyon:* Clasts 2-10 cm, with a monolithologic quartz diorite breccia containing clasts up to 5 m. Transport shifts from east-to-west to south-to-north. Source is east to south, across the [[San Francisquito Fault]].
  - *Texas Canyon:* Basalt clasts (plagioclase An60), anorthosite, and gneiss. Joined with the Vasquez Rocks basin at that time, per the author's inference.
  - *Vasquez Rocks:* Two clast suites. Transport is from the south across the [[Soledad Fault]], indicating dip-slip activity during Vasquez deposition.
- **[[Formation of Diligencia]]** (Orocopia Mountains): Basal conglomerates derive from local quartz monzonite and gneiss. Anorthosite and gabbro sources lie across the Clemens Well fault. Basalt clasts are coarsest in the west and suggest a westerly source, possibly buried in the Mecca Hills.

**Palinspastic reconstruction and tectonics**
- Slip of 220-280 km on the San Andreas fault is compatible with the Eocene shoreline data, and 260 km establishes the reconstruction in Figure 2.
- Summarized values since the Miocene: 210 km right slip on the San Andreas, 60 km right slip on the San Gabriel, and 14 km left slip on the Big Pine fault.
- The mid-Tertiary structure is dominated by east-west trends: normal-fault-bounded basins, uplifts, and an actively rising anticline (Sierra Pelona-Orocopia).
- The San Andreas and San Gabriel faults do not appear to have been active during the mid-Tertiary. Later right-slip displaced these features to their present positions.
- The Simmler and Charlie Canyon sections are very similar, suggesting deposition in an interconnected basin.
