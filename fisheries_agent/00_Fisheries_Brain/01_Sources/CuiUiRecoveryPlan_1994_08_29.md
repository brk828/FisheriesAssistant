## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 administrative letter from the Cui-ui Recovery Team Leader (Thomas A. Strekal) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director, summarizing conclusions from an August 1994 Recovery Team meeting. The letter does not present new morphological or population data directly, but discusses the status of the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) relative to recovery criteria in the **Cui-ui Recovery Plan, Second Revision (1992)**. The team concluded that reclassification (change in listing status) is **not warranted** at this time because recovery objectives have not been met, and recommended an immediate reevaluation of the recovery plan's modeling assumptions, water management strategy, and habitat restoration approach. The letter is significant for documenting the interplay between hydrology, water management (Stampede Reservoir, Truckee River Operating Agreement), and the biological recovery strategy for this endangered, lake-dwelling sucker species.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological data (body measurements, meristics, coloration) are provided in this source.
- The species is referenced taxonomically only by common name "Cui-ui," a member of the family [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), historically classified within the genus *Chasmistes* (lake suckers).
- Indirect biological life-history parameters referenced include:
  - **Spawning run** timing and magnitude (adult upstream migration).
  - **Larvae production** as a monitored life-stage metric.
  - **Year-class strength** used as an indicator of recruitment success.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The [[Cui-ui]] is endemic to the [[Pyramid Lake]] and lower [[Truckee River]] system within the Lahontan Basin of Nevada — a closed (terminal) lake ecosystem historically connected to riverine spawning habitat.
- **Migration**: The species exhibits an **anadromous-like lacustrine-riverine migration pattern**, requiring upstream (spawning) and downstream (post-spawning/larval) migration between Pyramid Lake and the lower Truckee River.
- **Flow dependency**: Successful spawning migration, egg/larval development, and downstream larval transport are contingent upon sufficient **discharge (unregulated and stored water)** in the lower Truckee River — a threshold-flow model currently underpins water management for the species.
- **Water management interactions**:
  - **[[Stampede Reservoir]]** water is managed under Public Law 101-618 to support cui-ui spawning runs, currently using a threshold-discharge approach.
  - An alternative approach discussed is managing reservoir releases to **mimic the natural unregulated flow pattern** (timing/shape, not necessarily magnitude) of the lower Truckee River, contingent on observed cui-ui spawning response — reflecting a shift toward **ecosystem-based flow management** rather than pure water-delivery management.
  - **Fish passage** over or around the **Truckee River delta** is identified as a critical habitat-connectivity issue affecting migration success.
- **Riparian ecosystem linkage**: The recovery team highlighted the need to evaluate benefits of **reestablishing/maintaining a functional riparian ecosystem** along the lower Truckee River as an "equivalent benefit" to direct water acquisition for recovery.

## Key Findings
- **Recovery status**: Based on the existing Recovery Plan criteria, a change in the cui-ui's endangered listing status is **not warranted**, as recovery objectives have not yet been achieved.
- **Model deficiencies identified**:
  - The **Negotiated Settlement Model** (hydrologic computer model used in Truckee River Operating Agreement negotiations) contains **programming errors** requiring correction by the Bureau of Reclamation.
  - The **cui-ui biological model** embeds assumptions about population dynamics, life history, fish passage facility operation, lower river hydraulics, and hydrologic response that may need revision given new data.
- **Data accrual**: Additional years of **hydrologic (discharge)** and **biological (spawning run, larvae production, year-class strength)** data have accumulated since the 1992 plan and warrant incorporation into model reanalysis.
- **Recommended actions**:
  1. Reevaluate the hydrologic and cui-ui biological models with updated data (estimated ≥6 months of work).
  2. Reassess Stampede Reservoir water management strategy, potentially shifting toward mimicking natural flow patterns.
  3. Secure "equivalent benefits" (e.g., riparian ecosystem restoration, improved fish passage) as alternatives/complements to direct water rights acquisition.
- **Policy context**: The review was prompted partly by public interest in the species' listing status and by federal implementation of **Public Law 101-618** (the Truckee-Carson-Pyramid Lake Water Rights Settlement Act).
- **Institutional collaboration**: Stakeholders present at the recovery team meeting included the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Town of Truckee, and the [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]], reflecting the multi-agency, multi-stakeholder nature of cui-ui recovery efforts.
- **Next steps**: Following model reevaluation, the Recovery Team intends to formally review recovery criteria and determine whether a **Third Revision** of the Recovery Plan — potentially incorporating broader ecosystem-based concepts — is warranted.
