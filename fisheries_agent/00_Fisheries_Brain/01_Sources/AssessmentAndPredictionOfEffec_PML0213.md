# Assessment and Prediction of Effects of Selenium Exposure to Larval Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report from the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (Project 9SLCAP-6 SE), prepared by Daniel W. Beyers and Cris Sodergren (Colorado State University, 24 November 1999), assesses whether [[Selenium]] in the [[Colorado River Basin]] threatens larval [[Razorback Sucker]], an endangered species. Selenium concentrations in several proposed nursery habitats are elevated, largely because of human activity.

The study compared the relative importance of inorganic (dissolved) and organic (dietary) selenium. A three-trophic-level food chain (algae, rotifer, larval fish) was cultured across five dissolved selenium concentrations (5.35 to 27.2 µg/L) for a 28-day exposure.

- No changes in survival or growth of larval razorback sucker were detected.
- Selenium bioaccumulation in food organisms was lower than expected. Rotifers reached at most 1.40 µg/g dry weight, about half the 3 µg/g dietary threshold cited by other investigators.
- Because adverse dietary effects were never reached, a dose-response relationship for diet could not be estimated.
- A separate 96-hour acute test found the two species to be similar in sensitivity to dissolved selenium. Fathead minnow were somewhat more sensitive.

## Background & Description

Selenium is a metalloid found in geologic formations and surface waters of the Colorado River basin. It is an essential micronutrient, but at elevated environmental concentrations it becomes toxic. In fish, selenium substitutes for sulfur in amino acids, altering protein structure and function. Organic forms such as selenoamino acids are more bioavailable and bioaccumulate rapidly, making diet the most important exposure pathway for fish.

Human activities have increased selenium mobilization from geologic formations and concentrated dissolved selenium in evaporation basins. Several objectives of the [[Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin]] call for restoring nursery habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]]. Many of these habitats were recently found to have elevated selenium, raising concern for larvae that reside there.

The study had two components:

1. **Dietary study:** a 28-day [[Early Life-Stage Toxicity Test]] with a [[Dietary Selenium Exposure]] design, using [[Chlorella Vulgaris]] algae, [[Brachionus Calyciflorus]] rotifers, and larval razorback sucker.
2. **Renewal-acute tests:** 96-hour [[Acute Toxicity Test]] runs comparing razorback sucker with [[Fathead Minnow]] under identical conditions.

Funding came from the Recovery Implementation Program and the [[National Irrigation Water Quality Program]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry.** Dilution water came from a well on the Colorado State University campus, heated and aerated to 20 ± 1°C. Dissolved oxygen was 6.8–7.3 mg/L, alkalinity 233–251 mg/L as CaCO₃, hardness 358–378 mg/L as CaCO₃, and pH 7.7–8.2 in the dietary study. Acute-test water had alkalinity of 242–250 mg/L, hardness of 364–367 mg/L, and pH of 7.8–8.1. Lighting was cool-white fluorescent (530 lx) on a 12:12 h light:dark cycle.

**Background selenium.** The well water contained selenium at an average of 5.35 µg/L (SE 0.153). Earlier multi-year analyses had not detected it, and the authors concluded those earlier analyses were in error or had inadequate detection limits. All data were therefore expressed as measured concentrations.

**Bioaccumulation.** Earlier work by [[Dobbs et al. (1996)]] reported [[Bioconcentration Factor]] values of about 400–500 in algae and rotifers. In this study, factors ranged from 51 to 77. Zooplankton in Colorado River habitats near Grand Junction reportedly show factors of 267–5700 (C. Osmundson, pers. comm.). The authors attribute the gap to a simplified food chain that omitted sediment and other natural cycling processes, and to a slow culture exchange rate that may have let selenium settle out with dead algae.

**Growth and energetics.** Fish grew slowly, from 10.6 to 11.5 mm TL over 28 days. The authors suggest rations were below maximum consumption. That limited energy reserves for compensating stress, so the study may give a conservative (worst-case) estimate of effects.

## Key Findings

**Dietary study (28 days)**

- Design: a 5 × 2 randomized factorial, with five measured dissolved concentrations (5.35, 8.23, 11.1, 16.5, 27.2 µg/L) and either control or selenium-enriched rotifer diet. Twenty larvae were assigned to each beaker, with four replicates per treatment.
- Survival averaged about 93% across all treatments. No dose-related change in survival was detected.
- Growth in mass and in TL showed no significant water or diet effect for mass. For TL, the trend test gave p = 0.586, and fish on the dietary treatment were suggested to be smaller (p = 0.0621). The authors describe this as a possible subtle effect.
- Rotifer selenium ranged from 0.0349 µg/g (control) to 1.40 µg/g dry weight (27.2 µg/L culture). Algae selenium at the highest exposure was 1.46 µg/g.
- Pasteurization of culture medium (about 70°C for 1 h) did not reduce selenium. Measured values were 8.60 vs 7.6 µg/L (lowest treatment) and 26.7 vs 27.7 µg/L (highest), a shift the authors attribute to analytical error.
- Conclusion: dietary selenium below 1.4 µg/g dry weight does not adversely affect survival or growth of larval razorback sucker. A dose-response model for diet could not be fitted because toxic dietary levels were never reached.

**Acute toxicity (96 hours, renewal design)**

| Species | Age at test | 96-h LC50 (mg/L) | 95% CI (mg/L) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Razorback sucker | ~27 days post-hatch | 40.8 | 37.0–44.9 |
| Fathead minnow | ~3 days post-hatch | 33.3 | 29.8–37.1 |

- The ratio of LC50 values (about 1.2) falls within the 0.5–1.5 range the authors treat as normal experimental variation (Mayer and Ellersieck 1986).
- Slopes of the two concentration-response lines were parallel (p = 0.555). Intercepts differed (p = 0.0002), which the authors read as fathead minnow being more sensitive.
- Acute exposure concentrations (6.38–204 mg/L) were over 1000 times higher than dietary-study concentrations.
- Survival was monitored at 6, 12, 24, 48, 72, and 96 h. Logistic regression yielded models for each species. For razorback sucker, logit = −13.7 + 8.53·log₁₀(concentration). For fathead minnow, logit = −13.9 + 9.14·log₁₀(concentration).
- The authors cite [[Finger et al. (1995)]] and [[Dwyer et al. (1995)]] as independent studies that also found the two species generally similar in selenium sensitivity.
- Age differed between species. The authors note that older fish are generally less sensitive, so the razorback sucker LC50 may be slightly higher than it would be at 10 days, the age when larvae probably first enter flooded bottomland habitat.

**Interpretation and limitations**

- Bioaccumulation was lower than expected, so the study did not test dietary toxicity at the levels that cause effects. Adverse effects "were not observed," but this does not show safety at higher levels.
- Reference thresholds cited in the report are 2 µg/L in water and 3 µg/g in diet ([[Lemly 1993a]]; [[National Irrigation Water Quality Program Guidelines]], 1998).
- The authors recommend interpreting future results from tissue concentrations rather than water concentrations alone, since cycling in natural systems controls bioaccumulation.

**Ongoing investigations and recommendations**

- The report makes no recommendations pending completion of ongoing work.
- A study comparing [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] sensitivity to dietary selenium is designed to extend the exposure range to levels expected to reduce survival and growth.
- A [[Field Verification]] study will compare predicted and observed toxicity of water from potential razorback sucker nursery habitats along the Colorado River.
- Planned changes include increasing culture exchange to about once every 3 days (from about 10 days) and broadening exposure concentrations so dietary levels exceed toxic thresholds.
- The acute test will be repeated to assess the effect of age on razorback sucker sensitivity.
