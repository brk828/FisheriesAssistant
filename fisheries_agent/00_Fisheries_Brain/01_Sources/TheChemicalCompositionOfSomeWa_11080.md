## Executive Summary

This 1960 paper by Eville Gorham presents a chemical analysis of surface waters collected from dune slacks and a permanent pond at [[Sandscale Dunes]], North Lancashire, in October 1957. The study characterizes the ionic composition (Na, K, Ca, Mg, HCO₃, Cl, SO₄) and pH of four water samples associated with distinct dune slack plant communities. The waters are shown to be highly calcareous — comparable to waters from true limestone terrain — despite occurring in a siliceous sand dune system, attributable to abundant calcareous shell fragments in the dune sand. The findings are contextualized against [[Lake District Tarns]], [[Cheshire Meres]], [[Blakeney Point]] dune waters, and world average freshwater composition, situating Sandscale among the most ion-rich water bodies in the broader Lake District region.

## Background & Description
*(Physicochemical characteristics of the water bodies, in place of organismal morphology — no fish or animal taxa are described in this source)*

- **Sample types:** Four water bodies sampled at ~15–50 cm depth:
  1. Surface water from a flooded *Salix repens* slack near *Juncus gerardii*, with *Equisetum variegatum*, *Hydrocotyle vulgaris*, and mats of *Campylium stellatum*.
  2. Surface water (~25 cm) from another *Salix* slack near abundant *Eleocharis palustris*.
  3. Surface water (~50 cm) from a flooded slack with a permanently submerged patch, black anaerobic ooze rich in ferrous sulphide, *Sparganium* and *Chara* present.
  4. A large permanent pond with abundant *Potamogeton natans* and *Chara*, growing in blackened sandy ooze.

- **pH:** Ranged from 8.0–8.1 before aeration, rising to 8.3–8.4 after aeration (expulsion of excess CO₂) — consistently alkaline across all sites.

- **Ionic composition (average of four surface waters, milli-equivalents/litre):**
  - Calcium bicarbonate (CaHCO₃): ~4.0 m.equiv./l (dominant ion pair)
  - Sodium chloride (NaCl): ~0.8 m.equiv./l
  - Magnesium sulphate (MgSO₄): ~0.6 m.equiv./l
  - Potassium (K): ~0.05 m.equiv./l — the least abundant major ion

- **Key comparative anomaly:** Magnesium-to-chloride ratio in Sandscale waters (~0.7) is far higher than in seawater (~0.2), suggesting sea spray is not the principal magnesium source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Calcareous sand dune slack system with seasonal flooding and one permanent pond, underlain by clay/silt causing persistent dampness — a substrate condition favoring dominance of *Salix repens* per [[Pearsall 1934]].

- **Vegetation succession sequence:**
  - Early colonizers: *Carex arenaria*, *Centaurium umbellatum*, *Juncus articulatus* var. *littoralis*, *Carex oederi*, *Potentilla anserina*, *Orchis purpurella*, *O. fuchsii*, *Samolus valerandi*, *Epipactis palustris*, *Listera ovata*.
  - Mid-stage: *Salix repens* establishment with *Equisetum variegatum*, *Parnassia palustris*, *Galium verum*, mosses (*Funaria hygrometrica*, *Bryum* sp.), *Gentiana amarella* agg.
  - Later dune-forming stage: *Festuca rubra* var. *arenaria*, *Carlina vulgaris*, *Carex arenaria*, with *Tortula* sp., *Pyrola minor*, *Rhacomitrium lanuginosum*.

- **Chemical drivers of habitat:** Calcareous shell fragments within the dune sand supply high concentrations of calcium bicarbonate; the permanent pond shows lower calcium concentration than the shallow flooded slacks, attributed either to (a) reduced leaching of calcareous soils by water reaching the pond, or (b) marl precipitation by abundant *Chara* in the pond.

- **Regional comparison:** Sandscale waters are chemically similar in lime content to inland limestone [[Sunbiggin Tarn]] and [[Malham Tarn]] systems, but are markedly enriched in sodium and chloride due to coastal sea spray influence — unlike the inland tarns. Compared to the siliceous dune well-water at [[Blakeney Point]], Sandscale shows a very different ion-exchange signature: high calcium relative to sodium/magnesium chloride likely suppresses the calcium-for-sodium/magnesium exchange anomalies documented at Blakeney (Gorham 1958).

- **Relative enrichment:** Compared to world average freshwater, Sandscale waters are considerably richer in all major ions except potassium, which is only slightly deficient. Overall ionic strength is comparable to the [[Cheshire Meres]], though with a different ionic balance (Sandscale being more calcium bicarbonate–dominated; Cheshire meres richer in magnesium sulphate).

## Key Findings

- Dune slack waters at Sandscale are unusually calcareous for a siliceous dune sand system, rivaling true limestone-derived waters in calcium bicarbonate concentration — driven by shell fragment content in the sand rather than bedrock geology.
- The permanent pond is chemically more dilute than the ephemeral flooded slacks, primarily due to lower calcium bicarbonate, possibly reflecting differential runoff leaching or biogenic marl precipitation by *Chara*.
- Magnesium enrichment relative to chloride cannot be fully explained by marine sea-spray input alone, pointing to an additional terrestrial or dune-sand source.
- Unlike the siliceous dunes of [[Blakeney Point]], Sandscale's high calcium carbonate abundance appears to inhibit the ion-exchange anomalies (calcium exchanging for sodium/magnesium) observed in less calcareous dune systems.
- Sandscale dune waters rank among the most ion-rich waters in the Lake District region, with a chemical fingerprint (pH 8.0–8.4, calcium bicarbonate dominant) distinct from both inland limestone tarns (lower Na/Cl) and other lowland lake systems like the Cheshire meres (different Ca:Mg:SO₄ balance).
- No fish, invertebrate, or plankton community data are reported in this study; the ecological content is restricted to vascular plant/bryophyte associations and water geochemistry.
