# Draft Environmental Assessment: Renovation of Stillman Lake to Remove Non-Native Species and Repatriate Native Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This draft Environmental Assessment (EA), prepared by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) in March 2007 with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), evaluates the renovation of [[Stillman Lake]] in the headwaters of the [[Verde River]], Arizona. The purpose is to restore and enhance a native fish community by removing non-native fishes and amphibians with a [[Piscicide]] treatment and then restocking native species over a three-year period. Three options are compared: a No-Action Alternative (Alternative A, the no-action baseline), the Preferred Alternative (chemical renovation, repatriation, and monitoring), and Alternative B (removal of the sediment berm, draining, and mechanical removal). The Service concludes that the Preferred Alternative is the best option, and that its effects are short-term and largely beneficial, with the main concerns being the costs and the risks of sediment and piscicide exposure.

## Background & Description

The document is a draft EA with a 45-day public review period. It covers the purpose and need, alternatives, the affected environment, environmental consequences, and agency consultation, and it includes a [[Native Fish Sanctuary Plan]] (Appendix A) and hydrology data (Appendix B).

Stillman Lake is described as a long, narrow body of water of about 20 surface acres, formed by a sediment berm (wedge) at the mouth of [[Granite Creek]] and fed by springs. The lake lies within the [[Upper Verde River Wildlife Area]] (UVRWA), purchased in 1996. Water levels are stable, ranging from about 0.5 m near the inflow to about 3.0 m near the berm. Baseflow to the Verde River is almost entirely subsurface, with about 2 cfs of spring flow.

Species of concern include the [[Razorback Sucker]] (federally endangered), the [[Spikedace]] (federally threatened), the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (experimental, non-essential), and the [[Yellow-billed Cuckoo]] (candidate). The [[Bald Eagle]] was downlisted to threatened in 1995.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Non-native species dominance.** The enclosed, lentic-like nature of Stillman Lake gives prime spawning and recruitment habitat for non-native species, including common carp and flathead catfish. During Verde River floods, the lake acts as a source population for these fish downstream. Sonora suckers were absent from 2000 and 2004 surveys but appeared after high flows in 2005, probably washed in from Granite Creek.

**Historical stocking.** AGFD stocked razorback suckers near Stillman Lake from 1986 to 1994. Hendrickson (1993) documented captures in a pool 100 m upstream of Granite Creek, which the authors infer came from Stillman Lake.

**Mechanical removal history.** Electrofishing and intensive netting were used in 2001, 2004, 2005, and 2006 (Table 1). Mean carp length did not decline over time, and the efforts were judged unsuccessful at reducing biomass. Small sample sizes precluded statistical analysis. The document notes that mechanical removal alone would not meet the project goals, though it may be useful as a supporting tool.

**Hydrology.** Water in Stillman Lake is thought to be regulated by springs. High flows in fall 2004 and spring 2005 overtopped the berm and created continuous flow to the Verde River. Upper Verde River flows exceeded 12,000 cfs in spring 2005 without eroding the berm. The bankfull discharge was estimated at about 947 cfs.

**Water quality.** Water quality parameters were summarized in Table 3 (temperature 17.9–19.9 °C, DO 5.9–7.5 mg/L, pH 7.7–7.9, conductivity 487–519 µS/cm). Granite Creek has been listed as impaired, and the USGS detected organochlorine pesticides in some stream reaches of the region.

**Threatened and endangered species.** Spikedace have never been recorded from Stillman Lake, but the downstream habitat is suitable. Yellow-billed cuckoo habitat is absent from the action area. The nearest bald eagle nest is 21 miles downstream near Perkinsville.

**Recreation.** Stillman Lake receives little recreational use because of limited access through private land. Horseback riding and a local bed-and-breakfast use the area.

## Key Findings

**Preferred Alternative (chemical renovation and repatriation):**
- Non-native fishes and amphibians would be removed from Stillman Lake using [[Rotenone]] (Nusyn-Noxfish or CFT Legumine) and/or [[Antimycin A]] (Fintrol-Concentrate or Fintrol-15), followed by [[Potassium Permanganate]] neutralization at downstream detoxification stations.
- Crayfish and bullfrogs are not controlled by these piscicides and would be addressed by mechanical trapping.
- Native fishes would be salvaged, held, and restocked over three years. Target species include the razorback sucker, desert sucker, Sonora sucker, roundtail chub, and speckled dace.
- Up to three piscicide treatments could be needed within a three-month period, and the document expects that up to two complete re-treatments might be required within the ten-year period covered by the EA.
- Monitoring covers physical habitat, zooplankton, macroinvertebrates, and fish populations.

**Alternative B (berm removal):** Would breach the sediment berm, drain the lake, build an overflow channel, and use mechanical removal. It carries greater risk of sediment input, a probable effect on Granite Creek, and channel instability. The document also notes that the channel would need to be over 3 feet (1 m) deep at its start.

**Pumping is not feasible:** Pumping to reduce water levels would need about 54,000 gallons per hour to offset the 2 cfs inflow, and a 10-foot (3 m) elevation head would need to be overcome.

**Environmental consequences (summary, Table 5):**
- Piscicide effects are described as short-term, and the byproducts are expected to be non-harmful.
- Benefits include a reduction in non-native fishes in the Upper Verde watershed and the reintroduction of native species, including the endangered razorback sucker.
- Berm removal presents potential temporary, high sediment loads that cannot be quantified.
- Recreational sportfishing would shift from non-native to native species, with a short-term impact from fish kills.
- No effect on cultural resources is expected.

**Cumulative effects:** Groundwater pumping and population growth in Yavapai County may reduce spring flows. The document concludes that current spring flows of about 2 cfs are stable and that no significant cumulative impacts are expected.

**Public comments and agency input:** Public concerns included piscicide use, the cost of the preferred alternative, the benefits of renovation, beaver dams, effects on birds and mammals, Clean Water Act compliance, and sedimentation. AGFD recommended re-sampling after the September 2004 flood, maximizing mechanical removal, salvaging large fish before treatment, and defining re-treatment triggers.

**Decision:** The Service will decide after the 45-day review. If significant impacts are identified, an Environmental Impact Statement would be required; otherwise a Finding of No Significant Impact would be issued.
