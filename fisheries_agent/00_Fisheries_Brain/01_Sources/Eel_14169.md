# Eels (Order Anguilliformes)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #anguillidae #muraenidae #ophichthidae

## Executive Summary

This encyclopedic entry provides a comprehensive overview of eels (order [[Anguilliformes]]), covering their distinctive morphology, life history, and ecological diversity. The document emphasizes the commercially important fresh-water eels (family [[Anguillidae]]) and their remarkable catadromous migrations, alongside descriptions of diverse marine eel families inhabiting coral reefs and deep-sea environments. All eels share an elongate body form, absence of pelvic fins, and passage through a transparent [[Leptocephalus]] larval stage in marine waters.

## Background & Description

True eels belong to the order [[Anguilliformes]] (Apodes), characterized by elongate form and complete absence of pelvic fins. The body is smooth, muscular, and snakelike, usually scaleless (embedded scales present in few species), covered with copious mucus making them difficult to grasp. The long dorsal fin and anal fin typically join around the tail with the greatly reduced caudal fin to form a single soft-rayed vertical fin. Gill openings are small; gas bladder possesses a duct to the esophagus. Vertebrae are greatly increased in number compared to other fishes; face bones including jawbones are reduced in number.

All eels pass through a protracted [[Leptocephalus]] larval stage with much compressed, generally elongate, glassy-clear body. These larvae commonly reach lengths exceeding six inches; one five-foot-long larva has been thought to represent young of a gigantic eel possibly underlying sea serpent reports.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fresh-water eels (family [[Anguillidae]]) utilize streams, ponds, and estuaries for growth and development but breed and pass through larval life in the sea. Species include [[Anguilla anguilla]] (Europe), [[Anguilla rostrata]] (eastern North America), [[Anguilla japonica]] (Japan), and other [[Anguilla]] species in western Pacific, [[New Zealand]], and [[Indian Ocean]] regions.

The [[European Eel]] migrates from streams to an oceanic area southeast of [[Bermuda]] where spawning and death occur. [[Johannes Schmidt]] determined spawning areas through prolonged study of larval distribution patterns. The [[American Eel]] breeds in an overlapping area. The [[European Eel]] leptocephalus drifts slowly to Europe in the [[Gulf Stream]], requiring three years to reach the elver transformation stage, at which young eels enter streams for freshwater growth. The [[American Eel]], with shorter open-sea journey, completes larval life in a single year.

Marine eels inhabit diverse ocean habitats, particularly warm seas. [[Morays]] (family [[Muraenidae]]) are conspicuous on coral reefs—sharp-toothed, large-mouthed, often vicious fishes feared by skin divers. Morays lack pectoral and pelvic fins with gill openings reduced to pores. [[Snake Eels]] ([[Ophichthidae]]) have finless tail tips projecting beyond dorsal and anal fins, widely distributed in warm coastal waters. Worm eels (generally less than one foot long) represent additional families.

Deep-water bottom eels include [[Synaphobranchus]] (gill slits united below) and [[Simenchelys]] (blunt-headed eel that burrows into other fishes to consume muscle, similar to hagfishes). Cold black mid-depths harbor bizarre forms including [[Snipe Eels]] ([[Nemichthyidae]]) with threadlike jaws rough with tiny teeth for entangling prey and extremely short body cavity. The excessively elongate tail region extends into a long filament except in the bobtail snipe eel [[Cyema atrum]].

## Key Findings

- All eels (order [[Anguilliformes]]) lack pelvic fins and pass through transparent [[Leptocephalus]] larval stage in marine waters
- Fresh-water eels are catadromous: grow in freshwater but breed in ocean
- [[European Eel]] leptocephalus requires three years drifting in [[Gulf Stream]] to reach elver stage; [[American Eel]] completes larval development in one year
- [[Johannes Schmidt]] discovered eel spawning areas southeast of [[Bermuda]] through larval distribution studies
- Fresh-water eels are highly esteemed for food, especially in Europe and Japan; main European fishery targets "silver eels" rich in fats beginning downstream migration
- Eels are reared for market in ponds in Japan and to lesser extent in Europe
- Several hundred known marine eel species represent diverse types and habitats in oceans, particularly warm seas
- [[Morays]] are sharp-toothed, vicious coral reef predators lacking pectoral and pelvic fins
- Deep-sea eels include specialized forms: [[Synaphobranchus]] (united gill slits), [[Simenchelys]] (muscle-consuming burrower), and [[Snipe Eels]] with threadlike jaws and extremely short body cavity
- The name "eel" is often misapplied to other anguilliform fishes including lampreys, hagfishes, spiny eels, and electric eels (which are not true eels)
