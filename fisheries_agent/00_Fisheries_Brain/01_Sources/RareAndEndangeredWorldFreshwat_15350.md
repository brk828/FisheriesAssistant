# Rare and Endangered World Freshwater Fishes (IUCN, 1968)

## Executive Summary
This document is a **provisional IUCN checklist**, compiled by [[Robert R. Miller]] in January 1968 for the Freshwater Fish Group of the Survival Service Commission, cataloguing **rare and endangered freshwater fish taxa worldwide**. It spans 18 families and over 40 species/subspecies, most exhibiting extreme range restriction — many confined to single springs, caves, wells, or short river reaches. The dominant threats identified are **overfishing, habitat destruction/modification, damming, introduced competitors/predators, and desiccation of arid-zone habitats**. The report is heavily weighted toward the **desert Southwest of the United States (Nevada, California, Texas, Arizona)**, reflecting a concentration of relict, spring-endemic taxa in the Death Valley/Great Basin region, alongside notable cases from Japan, Thailand, the USSR (Amur/Volga/Caspian basins), Mexico, and Turkey.

## Background & Description
The list functions as an early global taxonomic survey of freshwater fish conservation status, predating formal IUCN Red List categories. Entries are organized taxonomically by family, each with common name, scientific name (with authority), and a brief note on distribution and threat status. Many species described are **monotypic relict genera** — evolutionary remnants isolated in refugial habitats such as isolated desert springs (e.g., [[Devils Hole Pupfish]], [[Pahrump Killifish]]), artesian well systems (e.g., [[Widemouth Blindcat]], [[Toothless Blindcat]]), or single lakes (e.g., [[Waccamaw Killifish]], [[Waccamaw Darter]], [[Waccamaw Silverside]] — all endemic to Lake Waccamaw, North Carolina).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Recurring ecological themes across entries include:
- **Relictualism and endemism**: Many taxa are the sole survivors of once-wider distributions, now confined to single springs or short stream segments (e.g., [[Moapa Dace]], [[Desert Dace]], [[Checkered Killifish]]).
- **Desert spring ecosystems**: The Death Valley/Ash Meadows/Pahrump Valley region (Nevada/California, USA) hosts a cluster of pupfish (*Cyprinodon* spp.) and killifish taxa highly vulnerable to spring desiccation, water table lowering, and introduced species.
- **Damming and flow modification**: Threats to migratory or riverine species from dam construction, e.g., [[Beloribitsa]] (Volga River dams), [[Cui-Ui]] (Truckee River access blocked), [[Woundfin]] (proposed Virgin River dam).
- **Overfishing pressure**: Particularly acute for large-bodied Asian and Eurasian species — [[Kaluga]], [[Amur Sturgeon]], [[Giant Catfish]] (Mekong), [[Pla Thepa]] (Menam Chao Phya basin), and [[Ikan Temoleh]].
- **Introduced species competition/predation**: [[Balkhash Perch]] threatened by introduced *Stizostedion lucioperca*; [[Ash Meadows Pupfish]] threatened by introduced food/aquarium fishes.
- **Urbanization/agricultural habitat loss**: [[Miyako-Tanago]] (Tokyo expansion); [[Ayumodoki]] (rice culture and river damming in Japan).

## Key Findings (By Family)

### ACIPENSERIDAE — Sturgeons
- **[[Shortnose Sturgeon]]** (*Acipenser brevirostrum*) — Virtually restricted to the Hudson River, USA; formerly ranged New Brunswick to Florida.
- **[[Lake Sturgeon]]** (*Acipenser fulvescens*) — Rare in northeastern USA; historic Great Lakes commercial catch reached 8.5 million lbs in 1885.
- **[[Amur Sturgeon]]** (*Acipenser schrencki*) — Amur River basin, USSR; rare due to overfishing.
- **[[Kaluga]]** (*Huso dauricus*) — Amur River/Amur Gulf basin, USSR; rare due to overfishing.

### OSTEOGLOSSIDAE — Bony Tongues
- **[[Asian Bonytongue]]** (*Scleropages formosus*) — Malaysian relict; threatened by overfishing as a prized food fish among aboriginal groups.

### SALMONIDAE — Trouts, Whitefishes, Graylings
- **[[Longjaw Cisco]]** (*Coregonus alpenae*) — Great Lakes, USA; decline from sea lamprey predation, commercial fishing, and competition from *C. hoyi* and exotic alewives.
- **[[Beloribitsa]]** (*Stenodus leucichthys leucichthys*) — Monotypic genus; Caspian subspecies threatened by Volga River dams (USSR).
- **[[Arctic Grayling]]** (*Thymallus arcticus*) — Extinct in Michigan and most of upper Missouri basin (Montana); partially protected in Montana.
- **[[Gila Trout]]** (*Salmo gilae*) — Confined to Gila River basin, Arizona/New Mexico; scarce but protected, alongside undescribed Apache trout relative.
- **[[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Salmo clarki henshawi*) — Extremely rare; a few isolated creeks/lakes in California and Nevada.
- **[[Atlantic Salmon]]** (*Salmo salar*) — Restricted in North America to 8 coastal streams in Maine; endangered only in the USA.

### UMBRIDAE — Mudminnows
- **[[Olympic Mudminnow]]** (*Novumbra hubbsi*) — Relict genus restricted to Olympic Peninsula waters, Washington, USA.

### CYPRINIDAE — Minnows
- **[[Miyako-Tanago]]** (*Tanakia tanago*) — Kanto District, Tokyo, Japan; habitat destroyed by urban expansion.
- **[[Ikan Temoleh]]** (*Probarbus jullieni*) — Monotypic genus; threatened by overfishing and habitat modification.
- **[[Desert Dace]]** (*Eremichthys acros*) — Endangered relict; known only from Soldier Meadows, Nevada, USA.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Scarce in upper Colorado River basin, Utah/Colorado, USA.
- **[[Mohave Chub]]** (*Gila (Siphateles) mohavensis*) — Formerly the only native fish of the Mohave River, California; now survives only in a spring-fed pool near Baker.
- **[[Moapa Dace]]** (*Moapa coriacea*) — Fewer than 1,000 individuals; warm springs, southern Nevada, USA.
- **[[Woundfin]]** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) — Virgin River below St. George, Utah; endangered by proposed dam.

### CATOSTOMIDAE — Suckers
- **[[Modoc Sucker]]** (*Catostomus microps*) — Single creek in northeastern California; previously thought extinct.
- **[[Cui-Ui]]** (*Chasmistes cujus*) — Restricted to Pyramid Lake, Nevada; can no longer spawn up the Truckee River.
- **[[Klamath Sucker]]** (*Chasmistes brevirostris*) — Largely gone from Klamath River basin, California/Oregon; genus-wide protection need noted.

### COBITIDAE — Loaches
- **[[Ayumodoki]]** (*Hymenophysa curta*) — Lake Biwa and Okayama Prefecture rivers, Japan; habitat loss from rice culture and damming.

### BAGRIDAE — Asian Catfishes
- **[[Nekogigi]]** (*Coreobagrus ichikawai*) — Known only from rivers of Mie, Gifu, and Aichi Prefectures, Honshu, Japan.

### ICTALURIDAE — North American Catfishes
- **[[Widemouth Blindcat]]** (*Satan eurystomus*) — Deep artesian wells, Edwards Plateau, San Antonio, Texas.
- **[[Toothless Blindcat]]** (*Trogloglanis pattersoni*) — Artesian waters near San Antonio, Texas.
- **[[Mexican Blindcat]]** (*Prietella phreatophila*) — Single well near Musquiz, Coahuila, Mexico; status uncertain (reported extinct since 1964, but seen in 1967).

### SCHILBEIDAE — Catfishes
- **[[Pla Thepa]]** (*Pangasius sanitwongsei*) — Menam Chao Phya basin, Thailand; overfishing has reduced population to fewer than 2,000 individuals.
- **[[Giant Catfish]]** (*Pangasianodon gigas*) — Monotypic relict genus, Mekong River basin, SE Asia; threatened by overfishing.

### CYPRINODONTIDAE — Killifishes
- **[[Checkered Killifish]]** (*Cualac tessellatus*) — Monotypic relict; single spring-fed ditch, San Luis Potosí, Mexico.
- **[[Devils Hole Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon diabolis*) — Single spring hole, Ash Meadows, Nevada; population <800; protected by National Park Service.
- **[[Comanche Springs Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon elegans*) — Irrigation ditches near Toyahvale, Texas.
- **[[Ash Meadows Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis mionectes*) — Ash Meadows, Nye County, Nevada; threatened by introduced fishes; refuge under consideration.
- **[[Warm Spring Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis pectoralis*) — 1–2 springs in Ash Meadows, Nevada.
- **[[Tecopa Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis calidae*) — Hot spring outlet near Tecopa, California; rare, possibly extinct.
- **[[Shoshone Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis shoshone*) — Spring outflow at Shoshone, California; last collected 1965, possibly extinct.
- **[[Owens Valley Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon radiosus*) — Nearly disappeared after 1942; ~200 individuals found 1964; sanctuary planned for 1968.
- **[[Waccamaw Killifish]]** (*Fundulus waccamensis*) — Restricted to Lake Waccamaw, North Carolina.
- **[[Pahrump Killifish]]** (*Empetrichthys latos*) — Single spring-fed pool, Pahrump Valley, Nevada; ~1,300 individuals counted 1967; type species (*Empetrichthys*) evidently extinct.
- **[[Scaleless Killifish]]** (*Kosswigichthys asquamatus*) — Monotypic genus, Lake Hazer, eastern Anatolia, Turkey; threatened by lake-level lowering.

### AMBLYOPSIDAE — Cavefishes
- **[[Ozark Cavefish]]** (*Amblyopsis rosae*) — Restricted to a few caves and wells in southwestern Missouri, USA.

### POECILIIDAE — Livebearers
- **[[Clear Creek Gambusia]]** (*Gambusia heterochir*) — Headwaters of Clear Creek, Texas.
- **[[Big Bend Gambusia]]** (*Gambusia gagei*) — Big Bend National Park, Texas; nearly extinct in 1957, threatened with extirpation multiple times since.
- **[[Pecos Gambusia]]** (*Gambusia nobilis*) — Spring-fed ditches around Toyahvale, Texas.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — Formerly abundant across Arizona, western New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico; reduced to virtually a single population in southern Arizona over 50 years.

### CENTRARCHIDAE — Sunfishes
- **[[Suwannee Bass]]** (*Micropterus notius*) — Restricted to Ichetucknee Springs and adjacent springs, northern Florida.

### PERCIDAE — Perches and Darters
- **[[Balkhash Perch]]** (*Perca schrencki*) — Endemic to desiccating Lakes Balkhash and Alakul, Central Asia; threatened by introduced predatory *Stizostedion lucioperca*.
- **[[Maryland Darter]]** (*Etheostoma sellare*) — Few tributaries of Chesapeake Bay, Maryland; one of the rarest darters known.
- **[[Niangua Darter]]** (*Etheostoma nianguae*) — Fewer than 1,000 individuals estimated; Niangua River tributary and Maries River, Osage drainage, Missouri.
- **[[Fountain Darter]]** (*Etheostoma fonticola*) — Known only from two springs in central Texas.
- **[[Waccamaw Darter]]** (*Etheostoma perlongum*) — Restricted to Lake Waccamaw, North Carolina.

### GASTEROSTEIDAE — Sticklebacks
- **[[Unarmored Threespine Stickleback]]** (*Gasterosteus aculeatus williamsoni*) — Extirpated from native range except headwaters of Santa Clara River, southern California; also planted in the Mohave River.

### COTTIDAE — Freshwater Sculpins
- **[[Rough Sculpin]]** (*Cottus asperrimus*) — Known only from cold tributaries of the Pit River, Shasta and Modoc counties, California.

### ATHERINIDAE — Silversides
- **[[Waccamaw Silverside]]** (*Menidia extensa*) — Restricted to Lake Waccamaw, North Carolina.

---
**Notable Ecological Hotspot — Lake Waccamaw, North Carolina**: Uniquely hosts three endemic taxa on this list — [[Waccamaw Killifish]], [[Waccamaw Darter]], and [[Waccamaw Silverside]] — indicating a significant center of freshwater endemism warranting focused conservation attention.

**Notable Ecological Hotspot — Ash Meadows/Death Valley Region, Nevada-California**: Hosts numerous spring-endemic *Cyprinodon* and *Empetrichthys* taxa ([[Devils Hole Pupfish]], [[Ash Meadows Pupfish]], [[Warm Spring Pupfish]], [[Tecopa Pupfish]], [[Shoshone Pupfish]], [[Owens Valley Pupfish]], [[Pahrump Killifish]]), representing one of the most concentrated arrays of critically restricted freshwater fish taxa in the world.
