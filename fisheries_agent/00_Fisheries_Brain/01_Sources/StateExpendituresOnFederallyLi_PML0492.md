# State Expenditures on Federally Listed Threatened and Endangered Species

#birds #endangered-species #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This article summarizes the 1993 *State Expenditure Report* by the [[International Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies]], the third annual report on state spending for federally listed threatened and endangered (T/E) species. The survey was mandated by a 1988 amendment to the [[Endangered Species Act]], which requires reporting of reasonably identifiable expenditures made primarily for the conservation of listed species under Section 6 grants. In the most recent fiscal year (FY 1991 or FY 1992, depending on the state), states spent about $131 million of non-federal money on 633 of the 775 listed species in the U.S. Birds received the largest share, and nearly all of it went to a single species, the [[Red-Cockaded Woodpecker]].

## Background & Description

- **Source:** Report compiled by Kristen Conway. The article presents key information from the full report with additional figures and tables.
- **Scope:** 775 federally listed T/E species occur within the U.S. Of these, 633 received state expenditures in the reporting year.
- **Reporting method:** Most states reported spending per species. Where states gave gross estimates, the total was divided evenly among all listed species in that state. Hawaii, Louisiana, Texas, and Wisconsin reported portions or entire budgets divided evenly.
- **Year-over-year change:** Reported spending was more than double the prior year's figure of $66.6 million (the source reads "566.6"; this is read as a scan error). The authors attribute the increase partly to anticipated budget requests and more thorough reporting, including new contacts with agencies such as state departments of transportation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Spending was concentrated on a few taxa. The red-cockaded woodpecker (*Picoides borealis*) accounted for 89% of all money spent on birds, mainly through [[Land Acquisition]] purchases in [[Florida]] intended to benefit the species. The report does not describe the woodpecker's habitat or ecology in detail, so this context is limited to the funding decisions.

Spending across other groups was much smaller:

- **Mammals:** about $40 million, the second-highest funded group.
- **Fish:** 0.9% of total non-federal funds.
- **Reptiles:** about 10%.
- **Amphibians and invertebrates:** each received 10% or less. The exact percentages are garbled in the source and are not reported here.

## Key Findings

- **Total spending:** About $131 million in non-federal state funds on 633 of 775 federally listed T/E species in the most recent fiscal year.
- **Birds dominate:** Birds received about $70 million, far more than mammals ($40 million), the next-highest group.
- **Single-species concentration:** 89% of bird spending was attributed to the red-cockaded woodpecker, largely from Florida land acquisitions.
- **Limited funding for fish:** Fish received 0.9% of total non-federal funds. This is a key point for [[Fish Conservation]] and for the aquatic taxa the report covers only briefly.
- **Reporting caveats:** Some states divided gross budgets evenly across all their listed species, so per-species figures for those states are estimates. The jump from the prior year reflects better reporting as well as real spending changes.
