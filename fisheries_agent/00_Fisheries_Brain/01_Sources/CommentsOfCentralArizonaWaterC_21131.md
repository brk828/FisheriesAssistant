# Comments of the Central Arizona Water Conservation District on the Lower Colorado River Biological and Conference Opinion

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 25 July 1997 comment letter from a law firm representing the Central Arizona Water Conservation District (CAWCD) to the Regional Director of the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], Lower Colorado Regional Office. It responds to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Biological and Conference Opinion (BO) of 30 April 1997 on Lower Colorado River operations and maintenance from Lake Mead to the Southerly International Boundary. CAWCD argues that the BO has fundamental legal and factual errors. It says the BO blurs the environmental baseline with the effects of the action, so that the analysis in effect judges the existence of the dams rather than Reclamation's discretionary operations. It disputes the jeopardy and adverse-modification findings for the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], the scope of the reasonable and prudent alternatives (RPAs), and several statements about Reclamation's discretion. CAWCD asks Reclamation to forward the comments to the Service and request corrections.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** advocacy and legal correspondence, not a research paper. It contains no original field data.
- **Subject action:** Reclamation's discretionary operation and maintenance programs on the lower Colorado River (LCR), consulted on under [[Endangered Species Act Section 7]].
- **Species at issue:** the [[Bonytail Chub]], the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]. The [[Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard]] is also discussed.
- **Related documents cited:**
  - the Service's July 1, 1994 biological opinion no. 2-21-94-F-244 on non-native fish stocking
  - Reclamation's Final Biological Assessment
  - a January 22, 1997 Service briefing statement
  - Service memoranda of December 28, 1994 and January 3, 1995
  - the Solicitor's Opinion, 88 I.D. 903 (1981)
- **Other contributors:** CAWCD also adopts comments submitted separately by the Arizona Power Authority. The letter is copied to the Service's Regional Director.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The BO's environmental baseline includes many introduced non-native fish in the LCR. CAWCD notes the Service itself stocked these fish for decades.
- CAWCD argues that the stressors named in the BO result from the existence of the dams and cannot be changed by Reclamation's discretionary operations. They include cold, clear water, water storage, flood prevention, barriers to fish movement, altered sediment load and temperature, and the lack of a natural hydrograph.
- [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]] are sites of native fish restoration. Reclamation plans to release thousands of razorback suckers and bonytail chub over 10 years.
- Reclamation voluntarily manages Lake Mohave water levels to support the backwater rearing program. The Service had said this "may enhance the probability of successful spawning and recruitment."
- Critical habitat for the razorback sucker and bonytail chub was designated in 1995. CAWCD says it remains in essentially the same condition.
- Non-federal participants in the [[Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP) have obtained approvals and committed funds for interim conservation measures.

## Key Findings

1. **Baseline errors:**
   - The BO omits the Service's own long-running non-native fish stocking, which was not reviewed under Section 7 until 1992. CAWCD says this violates the "first-in-time, first-in-right" principle (Solicitor's Opinion, 88 I.D. 903, 905).
   - The BO gives little credit to Reclamation's conservation efforts, which it describes at pages 28-34 of the BO.
   - The BO counts Reclamation's native fish releases toward the Service's own obligations under the 1994 opinion, and CAWCD calls this improper.
   - CAWCD recommends that Reclamation find new conservation actions and leave the Service to meet its own RPMs.
2. **Baseline versus effects of the action:**
   - The BO (p. 127) treats baseline effects as additive to the effects of the action. CAWCD says this violates 50 C.F.R. § 402.02 and § 402.14(g)(4) and turns the consultation into a review of the dams' existence.
3. **Cumulative effects:**
   - The BO included bait bucket releases, petroleum and contaminant spills, and accidental or unauthorized fish introductions. CAWCD says none of these is "reasonably certain to occur."
   - The BO omitted beneficial non-federal MSCP interim measures that are reasonably certain to occur.
4. **Jeopardy finding:** the Service admitted that Reclamation's operations would only "maintain the unfavorable baseline effects" during the five-year period. CAWCD concludes that no significant effect of the action supports a jeopardy finding.
5. **Critical habitat:** the adverse modification finding rests on the action not improving the baseline. CAWCD says the only change Reclamation made, Lake Mohave level management, is more likely to help than harm.
6. **RPAs:**
   - The Service said the baseline for the razorback sucker, bonytail chub and flycatcher is "past the jeopardy threshold."
   - The RPAs are meant to improve the baseline to a point below that threshold.
   - CAWCD argues that Section 7(a)(2) does not require compensation for baseline effects and that the BO is not a recovery plan.
7. **Scope of discretion:**
   - Table 1 of the BO says Reclamation may renegotiate downstream water contracts. CAWCD says this is wrong because under 43 U.S.C. § 617d the Lake Mead contracts are for permanent service, and only Congress can incur monetary obligations.
   - RPA 13 asks for an account of Reclamation's discretionary flexibility. CAWCD suggests a public forum with water and power users.
8. **Flat-tailed horned lizard:** the Service withdrew its proposed listing on 15 July 1997 (62 Fed. Reg. 37,852). CAWCD asks that all references and measures about the species be deleted.
9. **Clarifications requested:**
   - **Santa Cruz River:** the BO says the biological assessment on water delivery to the Santa Cruz River Basin is still being prepared. Reclamation actually submitted it in September 1996, and the Service responded in June 1997 after being sued, so formal consultation should now be underway.
   - **Incidental take:** the take is exceeded if 2 or more bonytail chub and razorback suckers, in any combination, are found dead in the first 2 years of the 5-year period. Service staff said at a 9 May 1997 meeting that this does not apply to fish found dead in the [[Central Arizona Project]] aqueduct or other diversions already consulted on.
   - **Flycatcher RPM 1:** Service staff said at the same meeting that protecting occupied habitat does not extend to the Lake Mead delta, and CAWCD asks that this be written into the BO.
