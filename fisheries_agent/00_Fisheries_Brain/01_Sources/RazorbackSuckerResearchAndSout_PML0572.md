# Razorback Sucker Research and Southwestern Native Fish Conservation Activities (1980)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This compiled 1980 document gathers several conservation and research items on native fishes of the southwestern United States, centered on the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Colorado River]] system. Its main components are a spawning-ecology study of razorback suckers in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service summary of 1980 listings and contracts, a [[Desert Fishes Council]] resolution opposing the Juniper-Cross Mountain Project on the [[Yampa River]], a [[Death Valley System]] status report, and two Water and Power Resources Service reports on the Lower Colorado Region. Together they show a region where dams, power projects and habitat loss are driving conservation actions for multiple endangered species.

## Background & Description

The document is a set of short reports and correspondence rather than a single study. Its components are:

- **Razorback sucker spawning study (abstract):** Telemetry tracking of five adult razorback suckers through two spawning periods, with substrate analysis, underwater observation and egg and larval rearing.
- **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Albuquerque, New Mexico):** a summary of 1980 species listings, a long list of aquatic contracts, and notes on the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Desert Fishes Council Resolution 80-4:** a resolution passed at the Council's Twelfth Annual Symposium (November 5–7, 1980, Monterrey, Mexico).
- **Death Valley System Committee report:** status notes on several pupfish and sucker populations, signed by chairman E. P. Pister.
- **Lower Colorado Region reports (William E. Rinne, [[Water And Power Resources Service]]):** a coordinator report on native fish research and an agency report on power, flood-control and habitat projects.

Several passages are garbled by OCR. Where the source was unclear, dates and counts are noted as uncertain below.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Razorback sucker spawning habitat.** Radio-tracked fish congregated and spawned over two gravel areas in the littoral zone of Senator Wash Reservoir, at depths of 2–18 feet. Substrate samples from the two spawning sites and a third location on the Colorado River had similar geological and petrological composition. Observations of spawning behavior and predation on eggs by other species were made from shore and underwater using SCUBA.

**Lower Colorado River recruitment.** The authors discuss why razorback suckers are not recruiting in the lower Colorado River and what is required for reproduction to succeed through the larval stage.

**Lake Mohave and Lake Mead.** Bonytail chub are now known from only one site, [[Lake Mohave]]. Largemouth bass populations in [[Lake Mead]] had declined, as shown by reduced harvest.

**Habitat threats.** Several projects threaten habitat. The Juniper-Cross Mountain Project would dam the free-flowing Yampa River, which the resolution calls essential habitat. [[Hoover Dam]] uprating and modification would change discharge patterns. Proposed pumped-storage projects would move water between reservoirs. Flood-control and vegetation clearing along the Colorado River would alter riparian and levee habitat.

**Desert species.** The [[Desert Pupfish]] is described as highly endangered from habitat loss and introduced fishes. Its possible recovery is linked to water demand near the [[Salton Sea]].

## Key Findings

### 1. Razorback sucker spawning and early life history (Senator Wash Reservoir)
- Five adult razorback suckers were monitored by radio telemetry over two spawning periods.
- Fish congregated and spawned over two gravel areas in shallow littoral water (2–18 ft).
- Substrate from both spawning sites and a Colorado River site was geologically and petrologically similar.
- Eggs and larvae were photographed at various developmental stages. One hundred eggs were artificially reared to the larval stage, and feeding and behavior were recorded.
- Study dates in the source are partly illegible.

### 2. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, 1980
- **Listings:** The Devils River minnow (*Dionda diabolis*) was proposed for listing but not listed, because the Service Director declined to sign the package. Reasons cited include local residents' misunderstanding of critical habitat and insufficient data on minnow numbers. Critical habitat was also noted for the fountain darter (*Etheostoma fonticola*), with the Leon Springs pupfish (*Cyprinodon bovinus*) named in the same listing text.
- **Contracts:** About 25 aquatic studies were initiated or completed in 1980. They covered rare mollusc surveys in Texas, Oklahoma, Arizona and New Mexico, and many species of *Gila*, *Gambusia*, pupfish, shiners, suckers, the woundfin, the Colorado squawfish, and the fountain darter.
- **Hatchery:** [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] was rearing several species. Biologists wishing to use them should contact the Albuquerque Regional Office.

### 3. Desert Fishes Council Resolution 80-4 (Yampa River)
- The Colorado River Water Conservation District and Colorado-Ute Electric Association applied to build two dams on the Yampa River (Juniper-Cross Mountain Project).
- The resolution states that the free-flowing Yampa River is essential habitat for four native fishes: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- The Council, with more than 300 members, asked the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission to protect the listed species fully and preferably not build the dams. The resolution passed without dissent.

### 4. Death Valley System status (Pister report)
- **Saratoga Springs pupfish** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis nevadensis*): stable. Potential impacts from Fort Irwin's expansion are being watched.
- **Amargosa pupfish** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis amargosae*): stable near Tecopa. A second population exists at River Springs, Adobe Valley, Mono County, from an introduction made in 1940 (date partly garbled).
- **Salt Creek pupfish** (*Cyprinodon salinus*): stable. Its interpretive facility is described as highly successful.
- **Owens sucker** (*Catostomus fumeiventris*): abundant and stable, especially in the northern part of the Long Valley Reservoir (Crowley Lake) area.
- **Desert pupfish** (*Cyprinodon macularius*): highly endangered. It is in current state and federal listing plans and should be taken soon to the California Fish and Game Commission.
- **Other items:** Little Lake in Inyo County was proposed as a native fish refugium. The California Desert Plan draft EIS was open for review. The next committee meeting was planned for late February 1981.

### 5. Lower Colorado Region: coordinator report (William E. Rinne)
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] is the major concern. Its threatened status on the federal list expired April 24, 1980. An ad hoc committee began updating the 1975 razorback management plan and considering re-proposing the species for listing.
- The [[Senator Wash Reservoir]] razorback study, run by the California Department of Fish and Game, had gathered significant data on movement, spawning behavior, spawning requirements and population dynamics. Water and Power is a participating agency.
- Water and Power was negotiating a multi-year study of razorback suckers and bonytails in [[Lake Mohave]]. The source's study length is garbled (probably 2 years).
- The bonytail was listed as endangered on May 23, 1980. Surveys found it only in Lake Mohave. Intensive sampling captured **5 bonytails, all females**, which were sent to [[Willow Beach Fish Hatchery]] for broodstock.
- Spring and fall woundfin surveys were completed by Recovery Team members.

### 6. Water and Power Resources Service: agency projects (Lower Colorado)
- **Hoover Powerplant uprating:** Adds generators to raise capacity to over 1,800 MW. Maximum discharge rises from 40,000 to 49,000 ft³/s (1,133 to 1,388 m³/s). Target completion is 1987.
- **Hoover modification (planning):** Adds generator units to increase peaking power. Peak discharge from Hoover Dam would rise by 13,000 ft³/s (368 m³/s). The average weekly discharge would stay the same, but daily release patterns would change.
- **Pumped storage (planning):** Two projects would pump water from [[Lake Mead]] or [[Lake Mohave]] into side-storage reservoirs during low demand, then release it back during peak demand.
- **Yuma Division flood control (early planning):** Measures to protect the Yuma levee in high Colorado River flows. Habitat was evaluated using Habitat Evaluation Procedures.
- **Cibola Division vegetation clearing:** Removes vegetation along a 6-mile reach inside the levees, with a 1-year biological follow-up.
- **Lake Mead largemouth bass study:** A 5-year study by Water and Power and the Nevada Department of Wildlife, in its third year. It focuses on nesting success, cover and food of [[Largemouth Bass]], aiming to restore harvest to 1959 levels.
- **Coachella Canal fish inventory:** A complete inventory of two sample reaches was planned for November 10, 1980 (source wording partly garbled). It aims to gather quantitative fishery data in the unlined canal.
- **Lake Mohave razorback and bonytail study (proposed):** Would study both species in the river reach between Hoover Dam and Lake Mohave. It may start by February 1981.
