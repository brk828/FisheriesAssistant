# Anguilla Recorded from California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Anguillidae #species-occurrence #introduced-species

## Executive Summary

This paper documents the first confirmed records of true eels (Family [[Anguillidae]]) captured alive in California waters. Two specimens representing different species were taken from the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]: [[Anguilla rostrata]] (American Eel) in 1964 and [[Anguilla anguilla]] (European Eel) in 1969. Both occurrences are considered isolated cases of accidental introduction rather than evidence of established populations. The most probable source is transport in ship ballast from foreign ports, similar to the introduction pathway of [[Acanthogobius flavimanus]] (Japanese Goby).

## Background & Description

The Family [[Anguillidae]] comprises the true eels, catadromous fishes that spawn in the ocean but spend most of their lives in freshwater or estuarine habitats. Prior to these records, the only documented eel occurrences in California were intentional introductions of [[Anguilla rostrata]] in 1874, 1879, and 1882, none of which resulted in authenticated survival.

**Anguilla anguilla specimen (1969):**
- Adult female, 925 mm total length initially (916 mm in preservative)
- Captured January 24, 1969 at Delta Fish Protective Facility near Byron, [[Contra Costa County]]
- Water temperature approximately 11°C
- Captured at 1900 hours during routine fish salvage operations
- Location: 65 airline miles east of [[San Francisco]]

**Anguilla rostrata specimen (1964):**
- Approximately 30 inches (762 mm) in length
- Captured June 1-5, 1964 near [[Honker Cut]] and Disappointment Slough, 15 miles northwest of [[Stockton]]
- Captured at 1700 hours on hook baited with freshwater clam
- Coloration: white belly, olive drab sides and back
- Specimen skinned; skin retained as SIO 64-219

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both specimens were captured in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] estuarine system, a complex network of tidal sloughs and channels. The 1969 specimen was collected at a louver fish screen on Italian Slough, which connects to Old River and the [[San Joaquin River]], flowing westward to the [[Pacific Ocean]]. The 1964 specimen was taken in tidal waters near [[King Island Resort]].

The cities of [[Sacramento]] and [[Stockton]] had been opened to foreign commercial shipping within the two decades preceding these captures, creating potential introduction pathways. The [[Japanese Goby]] ([[Acanthogobius flavimanus]]) had previously become established in the [[San Francisco Bay]] and estuarine system through similar ballast water introduction.

## Key Findings

**Species Identification - Anguilla anguilla:**
- Total vertebrae count: 114 (range for A. anguilla: 110-119; eliminates A. rostrata with 108-111)
- Precaudal vertebrae: 46 (range for A. anguilla: 44-47; eliminates A. japonica with 42-45)
- Preanal length without head: 30.2% of total length (modal for A. anguilla)
- Distance between vertical through anus and dorsal fin origin: 12.2% of total length
- Distance from eye-center perpendicular to angle of gape: 12.5% of gape length
- Identified by W. I. Follett, California Academy of Sciences
- Catalogued as California Academy of Sciences specimen

**Species Identification - Anguilla rostrata:**
- Length to vertical below dorsal origin minus head length: 22-24% (agrees with A. rostrata)
- Length to vertical above anus minus head: 30% (eliminates A. japonica)
- Interval between verticals through anus and dorsal fin origin: approximately 10% (fits A. japonica or A. rostrata better than A. anguilla)
- Identified by Dr. Carl Hubbs, [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]]
- Skin catalogued as SIO 64-219

**Introduction Pathways Evaluated:**
1. Aquarium trade: extremely unlikely (denied by major public aquariums)
2. Epicurean trade: extremely unlikely (eels rarely imported alive)
3. Straying: extremely remote possibility
4. Ship ballast: most logical explanation (both [[Sacramento]] and [[Stockton]] opened to foreign commerce within prior two decades)

**Ecological Implications:**
- Both occurrences considered isolated cases of accidental origin
- No evidence of established populations
- No authentic records of survival from previous intentional introductions (1874, 1879, 1882)
- Ecological consequences of eels in [[San Francisco Bay]]-Delta estuary warrant careful documentation

**Historical Context:**
- First confirmed live captures of [[Anguillidae]] in California
- Previous introductions of [[Anguilla rostrata]] in 1874, 1879, and 1882 showed no authenticated survival
- No other eel introductions documented or reported in California
