# Clave para los Peces de las Aguas Continentales de Costa Rica

## Executive Summary
This 1968 University of Costa Rica thesis (Departamento de Biología) by **Myrna I. López Sánchez** presents a comprehensive **dichotomous taxonomic key** for identifying the continental (freshwater and estuarine-associated) fishes of Costa Rica. The work is structured hierarchically: it begins with definitions of standard morphometric measurements and meristic counts (rays, spines, scales, teeth, gill arches), then proceeds through a **Family-level key**, followed by nested **genus- and species-level keys** for the most diverse and taxonomically important groups, notably [[Cichlidae]], [[Characidae]], [[Pimelodidae]], [[Poeciliidae]], [[Cyprinodontidae]], [[Mugilidae]], [[Gerridae]], [[Syngnathidae]], [[Pomadasyidae]], [[Eleotridae]], [[Centropomidae]], and [[Gobiidae]]. The document is a foundational reference for Costa Rican ichthyofaunal identification, combining classical morphometric/meristic taxonomy with illustrative diagrams of fish anatomy (fin rays, spines, teeth types, caudal fin shapes, gill arch structures).

## Background & Description
The paper is explicitly a **taxonomic identification tool**, not an ecological field study. It is organized into the following major sections:

- **Fish Morphology ("Fisonomía de un Pez")**: Defines external landmarks used throughout the keys, including standard length (LE), total length (LT), body depth (AM), caudal peduncle depth/length, head length, snout, preorbital/postorbital/interorbital distances, and fin abbreviations (AP=anal, AC=caudal, AD=dorsal, Pp=pectoral, Pv=pelvic).
- **Measurements ("Medidas")**: Precise operational definitions for total length, standard length, body depth, peduncle depth/length, predorsal distance, fin-base lengths, head length, snout length, and orbital distances — all measured as straight-line distances, not along body contours.
- **Rays and Spines ("Radios" / "Espinas")**: Distinguishes segmented, often-branched **rays** (Arabic numerals) from rigid, unsegmented **spines** (Roman numerals), with formula conventions (e.g., XV,18 = 15 spines + 18 rays in one fin; IV-II,10 = two fins with separate counts).
- **Scales ("Escamas")**: Defines lateral-line scale counts, predorsal scales, scales above/below the lateral line, and the scale-formula notation (e.g., 6-32-7).
- **Gill Arches ("Arcos Branquiales")**: Differentiates **gill rakers** (branquiespinas, anterior, spine-like, used taxonomically) from **gill filaments** (posterior, fine, respiratory).
- **Spines**: Further subdivided into **antrorse** (denticulations pointing same direction as spine) and **retrorse** (denticulations pointing opposite direction) spines.
- **Barbels**: Maxillary barbels (e.g., single pair in [[Pimelodidae]]).
- **Teeth**: Classified as conical/pointed (non-compressed), pointed-compressed-base, truncate-compressed-base, incisor-like truncate (compressed throughout), tricuspid, multicuspid, and filiform; associated dentigerous bones (mandible, premaxilla, maxilla, palatine, vomer) illustrated.
- **Caudal Fin Shapes**: Four basic forms — rounded, truncate, emarginate ("recortada"), and forked (bifurcate).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While primarily taxonomic, the key implicitly reflects Costa Rica's continental aquatic habitat diversity by spanning:
- **Strictly freshwater lineages**: [[Cichlidae]], [[Characidae]], [[Poeciliidae]], [[Cyprinodontidae]], [[Loricariidae]], [[Trichomycteridae]], [[Pimelodidae]], [[Gymnotidae]], [[Synbranchidae]].
- **Euryhaline/estuarine-tolerant groups**: [[Centropomidae]] (snook), [[Mugilidae]] (mullets), [[Gerridae]], [[Pomadasyidae]], [[Eleotridae]], [[Gobiidae]], [[Syngnathidae]] (pipefishes), [[Ariidae]] (sea catfishes) — indicating sampling across the freshwater-to-brackish continuum typical of Costa Rica's short Pacific and Caribbean-draining rivers.
- **Marine strays/euryhaline elasmobranchs and flatfishes**: [[Pristidae]] (sawfish), [[Carcharhinidae]] (requiem sharks), [[Bothidae]] and [[Soleidae]] (flatfishes) — reflecting incursions of marine taxa into lower river reaches and estuaries, consistent with Costa Rica's dual Caribbean/Pacific drainage systems referenced in the [[Ocean Basin]] context.
- The presence of both fully freshwater and euryhaline/marine-derived families in a single continental key underscores the **connectivity between freshwater drainages and coastal marine systems** in Neotropical ichthyofaunal assemblages.

## Key Findings

### Family-Level Key Highlights (selected diagnostic characters)
- **Caudal fin type + scale type** is the primary top-level divider: heterocercal/abbreviated-heterocercal with ganoid/placoid scales (cartilaginous/primitive fishes) vs. homocercal with cycloid/ctenoid scales or naked body (teleosts).
- [[Lepisosteidae]]: abbreviated heterocercal tail, ganoid scales, single gill opening.
- [[Pristidae]] vs [[Carcharhinidae]]: distinguished by presence/absence of a saw-like elongated rostrum with lateral teeth.
- [[Bothidae]] vs [[Soleidae]]: distinguished by eye/pigment side (left vs. right side of body) in these asymmetric flatfishes.
- Absence of pelvic fins separates [[Syngnathidae]] (bony plates, no anal fin), [[Gymnotidae]] (no dorsal/caudal fins, elongated anal fin base), [[Synbranchidae]] (no pectoral fins, scaleless), and [[Anguillidae]] (rudimentary scales, pectoral fins present).
- Pelvic fins fused into an adhesive disc distinguish [[Gobiesocidae]] (disc formed partly by pectorals) and [[Gobiidae]] (pectorals free from disc).
- Adipose fin presence/absence and dorsal/anal spine counts separate [[Characidae]] (8–17/28 anal rays), [[Lebiasinidae]] (26–27 lateral scales), and [[Curimatidae]] (36–37 lateral scales).
- [[Poeciliidae]] vs [[Cyprinodontidae]]: males with modified anal-fin rays forming a **gonopodium** (Poeciliidae) vs. unmodified anal fin (Cyprinodontidae).
- [[Pimelodidae]] vs [[Ariidae]]: adipose fin base length relative to head length (~equal in Pimelodidae vs. 3.1–4.4× longer in Ariidae), and anal ray counts (11–13 vs. 20–23).
- [[Cichlidae]] vs [[Gerridae]] vs [[Lutjanidae]]/[[Pomadasyidae]]: nasal openings (simple vs. double), lateral line interruption, premaxillary protractility, and vomerine dentition.

### Cichlidae (Costa Rican Cichlids) — Genus/Species Key
Detailed morphometric separation of genera/species including:
- **Parachromis (Cichlasoma) dovii, friedrichsthalii, managuense**: differentiated by anal spine counts (4–7 vs. 7–9), dorsal formula, preopercle margin shape, body depth ratios, and subopercle proportions.
- **C. tuba, C. centrarchus, C. lyonsi, C. citrinellum, C. altifrons, C. nigrofasciatum, C. spilurum, C. alfari, C. maculicauda, C. spinosum, C. spilotum, C. longimanus, C. rostratum, C. terrabae, C. sieboldii**: distinguished via labial frenulum presence, caudal fin shape, anal spine counts, body-depth ratios, peduncle spot presence/size, and live coloration (e.g., golden vs. blue eyes).
- **Aequidens coeruleopunctatus**: distinguished by having only 3 anal spines.
- **Neetroplus nematopus** vs. **Herotilapia multispinosa**: distinguished by truncate incisor-like teeth vs. tricuspid anterior teeth, and dorsal/anal formulae.

### Characidae — Genus/Species Key
- **Roeboides**: near-transparent body, 46–50 anal rays, 73–74 lateral-line scales, two upturned external premaxillary teeth.
  - *R. guatemalensis* (no humeral spot) vs. *R. salvadoris* (humeral spot smaller than eye diameter) vs. *Roeboides sp.* (large humeral spot ≥ eye size, 84–91 lateral scales).
- **Gephyrocharax intermedius**: dorsal origin behind anal origin, 39–43 lateral scales.
- **Brycon** (*B. striatulus* vs. *B. guatemalensis*): separated by lateral scale counts (64–75 vs. 43–58).
- **Bryconamericus** (*B. terrabensis* vs. *B. picae*): 45–50 vs. 36–37 lateral-line scales.
- **Hyphessobrycon** (*H. savagei* vs. *H. tortuguerae*): differ in anal ray counts (20–23 vs. 24–26), maxillary teeth (2–4 vs. 9–10), and gill raker counts (16–18 vs. 26–28).
- **Rhoadsia eigenmanni** (multicuspid teeth) vs. **Bramocharax bransfordi** (conical teeth).
- **Compsura gorgonae** vs. **Pseudocheirodon terrabae**: anal ray counts and maxilla-to-orbital-diameter ratios; males of *Compsura* bear small hooks on anal rays.
- **Astyanax fasciatus** vs. **Bryconamericus**: differentiated by suborbital bone contact with preopercle.

### Pimelodidae
- **Rhamdia** (humeral spine present, free orbital margin) vs. **Cetopsorhamdia** (no humeral spine, orbital margin continuous).
- **Rhamdia species key** (*R. rogersi, nasuta, wagneri, godmani, underwoodi, heteracantha, Rhamdia sp.*): differentiated using caudal fin bifurcation degree, occipital process length ratios, adipose fin length, maxillary barbel length, gill raker counts (6–12), and mouth width ratios.

### Poeciliidae — Genus/Species Keys
- Genera separated by: caudal peduncle margin shape (*Alfaro cultratus* — sharp-edged), snout elongation (*Belonesox belizanus* — pike-like), gonopodium length/coloration, dorsal fin spotting patterns (*Poeciliopsis*, *Xiphophorus*, *Brachyrhaphis*, *Neoheterandria umbratilis*, *Gambusia nicaraguensis*, *Poecilia sphenops*, *Phallichthys*, *Priapichthys*).
- **Xiphophorus helleri** vs. **X. variatus**: distinguished by dorsal ray counts and presence of a caudal "sword" extension in males (X. variatus).
- **Brachyrhaphis** (*parismina, holdridgei, rhabdophora, terrabensis*): separated by anal fin length relative to head length, dorsal ray counts, and dorsal fin pigmentation.
- **Phallichthys** (*tico* vs. *amates*): presence/absence of black spot on dorsal fin margin.
- **Poeciliopsis** (*paucimaculata, turrubarensis, retropinna*): presence and size of ovoid black spots, anal ray counts, transverse banding.
- **Priapichthys** (*annectens* vs. *panamensis*): predorsal distance ratios and vertical body barring.

### Cyprinodontidae
- **Oxyzygonectes dovii**: elongated jaws, dorsal origin posterior to anal fin base.
- **Rivulus hildebrandi** vs. **R. isthmensis**: presence of small white body spots and orbital diameter ratios.

### Mugilidae
- **Agonostomus monticola** (2 anal spines, pointed teeth) vs. **Joturus pichardi** (3 anal spines, incisor-like serrated laminar teeth, no symphyseal teeth).

### Gerridae
- **Diapterus brevimanus** (elevated dorsal profile, 39–40 lateral scales) vs. **Eucinostomus californiensis** (non-elevated profile, 45–47 lateral scales).

### Syngnathidae
- **Pseudophallus starksi** (no caudal fin; females with penis-like anal papilla) vs. **Syngnathus elcapitanense** (small caudal fin present; no anal papilla in females).

### Pomadasyidae
- **Pomadasys bayanus** vs. **P. crocro**: differentiated by lateral scale counts (57–70 vs. 54–62) and scale-row counts between lateral line and dorsal fin base.

### Eleotridae
- Genera (*Gobiomorus, Dormitator, Eleotris, Guavina, Hemieleotris*) separated by vomerine dentition, gill-slit extension, preopercular spine presence, and scale-row counts.
- **Gobiomorus dormitor** vs. **G. maculatus**: caudal peduncle depth ratios, lateral scale counts, predorsal scale counts.
- **Eleotris picta, E. amblyopsis, E. pisonis**: tooth uniformity, eye-size-to-head-length ratios, lateral scale counts.
- **Dormitator maculatus** vs. **D. latifrons**: mouth obliquity, lateral scale counts, pelvic fin length relative to anal opening.

### Centropomidae
- **Centropomus robalito** (50–53 scales above lateral line) vs. **C. pectinatus** (7 branched anal rays, 2nd/3rd spines equally elongate) vs. **C. undecimalis** (6 branched anal rays, 2nd spine markedly stronger than 3rd).

### Gobiidae
- Genera (*Sicydium, Awaous, Evorthodus, Gobionellus*) separated by tooth arrangement (single row vs. bands) and presence of dermal fleshy projections on the pectoral arch.
