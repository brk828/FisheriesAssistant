# Dichotomous Key to the Gila robusta Complex: Roundtail, Humpback, and Bonytail Chubs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-chub

## Executive Summary

This is a draft field identification guide prepared by [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) field crews, attributed to C. R. Smith and dated March 1983. It provides an alternate [[Dichotomous Key]] for distinguishing three chub species of the *Gila robusta* complex in the [[Colorado River]] basin: [[Gila Robusta|Roundtail Chub]], [[Gila Cypha|Humpback Chub]], and [[Gila Elegans|Bonytail Chub]]. The key relies on fin-ray counts, body proportions, head and snout shape, gill raker and vertebral counts, and total length. It also addresses [[Hybridization]] between species, which the author treats as a major identification problem. The author emphasizes that no single character is diagnostic, and that finray counts alone are not proof of identity.

## Background & Description

The document is a working draft, not a formal publication. Its layout is partly garbled from scanning, and several passages are incomplete or ambiguous. Its purpose is practical: to give field crews a consistent set of criteria for separating the three species and their hybrids.

The key begins with a broad split. Specimens with 9 dorsal and 9 anal fin rays, a large head, small fins, and no hump are assigned to *G. robusta*. Most other specimens are then separated by fin-ray counts (dorsal and anal), caudal peduncle depth relative to fin base length, the shape of the hump behind the head, total length (20 cm threshold), and eye size. Specimens that fit no clear category are referred to hybrid or "problem specimen" categories.

Three species accounts follow the key. Each lists typical counts and proportions:

- **[[Gila Robusta]] (Roundtail Chub):** Dorsal rays almost always 9; anal rays usually 9 (about 90%), occasionally 10. Gill rakers 12 to 15, usually 12 to 14. Vertebrae 40 to 43. Head large, with a slight hump in large individuals. Caudal peduncle always thick, with a ratio of 1.5 to 2.3 (higher in some areas, possibly through hybridization). Upper and lower jaws nearly equal. Fins small.
- **[[Gila Cypha]] (Humpback Chub):** Dorsal rays usually 9, not infrequently 10. Anal rays usually 10, sometimes 9. Gill rakers 13 to 17, usually 14 to 16. Vertebrae 41 to 44, not counting the [[Weberian Apparatus]]. Head small and concave on top. Abrupt hump behind the head, except in juveniles. Caudal peduncle ratio 2.2 to 3.2, usually 2.4 to 2.8. Long, sometimes fleshy snout that overhangs a short lower jaw. Fins large, especially pectorals and caudal.
- **[[Gila Elegans]] (Bonytail Chub):** Dorsal rays usually 10; anal rays usually 10 or 11. Gill rakers 15 to 21 (document partly illegible), usually 17 to 20. Vertebrae 44 to 46. Slender, elongated caudal peduncle, with a gradual hump and round eye. Snout not overhanging.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses only fish identification. It contains no information on habitat preferences, population status, water chemistry, invertebrate prey, or ecosystem interactions. The "main river" and "upper basin" are mentioned in passing (for example, in the context of caudal peduncle ratios and bonytail occurrence), but the text does not describe these locations in detail.

Hybridization is the main ecological concern in the text. The author notes that hybrids of *G. robusta* and *G. cypha* have smaller heads and larger fins than typical roundtails, and that hybrids of *G. cypha* and *G. elegans* show intermediate features. Peduncle ratios in some *G. robusta* populations are higher than in the main river, which the author attributes possibly to hybridization or to selection in the absence of other species.

## Key Findings

- **Diagnostic structure:** The key is designed as a stepwise dichotomy. Its first split separates *G. robusta* (9 dorsal and 9 anal rays, large head, small fins, no hump). Later steps separate *G. cypha* and *G. elegans* using hump shape, snout form, and peduncle proportions.
- **Length threshold:** Specimens under or over 20 cm total length follow different branches of the key, so length is an initial screening criterion.
- **Peduncle ratio:** Caudal peduncle depth relative to the dorsal or anal fin base is a key character. Values for *G. robusta* (1.5 to 2.3) and *G. cypha* (2.2 to 3.2, usually 2.4 to 2.8) overlap, so the ratio should not be used alone.
- **Fin-ray counts:** Counts overlap among species, especially for *G. robusta* and *G. cypha*. The author notes that a 9/9 or 10/10 count can still belong to a different species, so counts must be combined with other characters.
- **Meristic ranges:** Gill raker and vertebral ranges differ by species but overlap at the edges. For example, *G. cypha* (13 to 17 gill rakers, 41 to 44 vertebrae) and *G. elegans* (usually 17 to 20 gill rakers, 44 to 46 vertebrae) share some values.
- **Hybrid categories:** The key provides separate categories for *G. robusta* × *G. cypha* and *G. cypha* × *G. elegans* hybrids, plus "problem specimens" that match no clear category. The author states that hybrids may resemble either parent, so identification requires a judgment across multiple characters.
- **Eye and hump features:** A round eye and gradual hump point to *G. elegans*. A depressed eye and abrupt hump point to *G. cypha*. An eye diameter greater than two-thirds of peduncle depth is one criterion used to separate some groups.
- **Overall conclusion:** The author's central message is that no single trait is reliable. Identification depends on combining fin counts, body proportions, head and snout morphology, gill rakers, vertebrae, and total length, with explicit allowance for hybrids and borderline specimens.
