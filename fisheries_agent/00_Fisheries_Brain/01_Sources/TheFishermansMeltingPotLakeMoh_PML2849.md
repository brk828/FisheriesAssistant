# The Fisherman's Melting Pot - Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sportfish

## Executive Summary

This magazine feature by Bob Wedge profiles [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] that the author calls a "fisherman's melting pot." The article reports a seasonal run of [[Trout]] moving down the lake from the [[Willow Beach]] area, large catches of [[Bass]] and [[Catfish]], and a brief history of the area and its dam. It is an angling report and travel piece, not a research study, and it gives no formal sample design or ecological measurements.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Lake Mohave is described as primarily a bass fishery that also yields mixed strings of bass, catfish and trout. Fishing is launched from [[Cottonwood Cove]], a public campground area on the Arizona shore. [[Davis Mountain]] sits directly across from the cove.
- **Fishing methods:** Anglers commonly troll [[Flatfish Lure]] over sandy bars for trout. One angler reportedly keeps at least two rods set up so he can target different species without changing bait or lure. Catfish were taken on [[Doughball Bait]].
- **Historical context:** A stern-wheeler carrying [[Lt. J. C. Ives]] reached the present site of Bullhead City and Lake Mohave. Ives named a prominent dark mountain Jefferson Davis Mountain. The dam was originally called Bullhead Dam and was later renamed [[Davis Dam]] after Arthur Powell Davis, described in the text as engineer and chief of the Reclamation Service in 1942. The name change was unrelated to Davis Mountain.
- **Water quality:** The article states that silt is filtered out by [[Lake Mead]] upstream, so Lake Mohave and the river below the dam are described as the clearest, cleanest and bluest on the river, earning the title "Cream of the Colorado."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Trout are described as following [[Shad]] (forage fish). The author reports an angler's theory that birds feeding over the water indicate shad, and that trout follow the shad.
- Trout movement is seasonal: the text says they begin moving downlake in October or November and are caught all the way to Davis Dam.
- Lt. Ives' original 1800s-era voyage encountered muck, sand bars and rapids at Ring Bolt Rapids south of [[Boulder Dam]]. The article contrasts that with today's clear reservoir water.
- The article gives no population estimates, sampling protocols, or species-level scientific names. Trout are called "Mohave trout" and are not identified taxonomically in the text.

## Key Findings

- **Trout run:** Trout move down Lake Mohave from the Willow Beach area starting around October or November and are caught as far as Davis Dam.
- **Notable catches:**
  - A morning catch of 11 trout was taken by Bill Williams and Harold Daynes of San Bernardino.
  - Bill Williams landed a trout of about 5½ pounds (printed as "5% pound") on his first cast.
  - Cliff Merchon caught a 10 lb. 5 oz. bass.
  - A 23½-pound catfish (printed as "23% pound") was taken on a doughball bait the week before the article.
  - A Kingman party of four (Jerry Staht, Pete Jasper, Joe Chambers and John Gollenstein) posed with a one-day catch that the article describes as among the best in the West.
- **Mixed fishery:** Lake Mohave remains primarily a bass lake, and strings often combine bass, catfish and trout.
- **Water clarity:** Lake Mohave's water is described as exceptionally clear, attributed to silt filtering through Lake Mead.
- **Historical note:** Davis Dam's name honors Arthur Powell Davis and is unrelated to Davis Mountain, which Lt. Ives named after Jefferson Davis.
