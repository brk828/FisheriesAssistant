## Executive Summary

This 1987 correspondence and appendix from the [[Upper Colorado River Basin Biological Subcommittee]] (BSC) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's [[Colorado River Coordinating Committee]] documents a review and re-prioritization of research and recovery tasks for four rare/endangered [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] fishes: the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically called "Colorado squawfish"), [[Humpback Chub]] (HBC), [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]], along with unresolved taxonomic questions surrounding the [[Gila Complex]] (which includes *Gila cypha*, *G. elegans*, and related roundtail-type chubs). Rather than presenting new morphological or field data, the document is a management/administrative record establishing a tiered research prioritization matrix (Appendix 6.2) covering monitoring, life history/habitat, management-applicable, and administrative task categories. It also flags specific programmatic concerns: suspension of the [[Redlands Diversion Fish Ladder]] project, skepticism about a proposed [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] sport fishery at [[Kenney Reservoir]], the need for genetic/taxonomic clarification among endangered fish stocks, and threats to Section 6 federal funding for state endangered species programs.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source. The document instead references unresolved **taxonomic ambiguity** within the [[Gila Complex]], explicitly noting the need to:
- "Evaluate taxonomic status of Gila complex; recommend future course of action"
- "Gain understanding of Gila hybridization (including *G. elegans*)"
- "Develop field methods to identify *Gila* spp."
- "Develop taxonomic key/technique for razorback sucker larvae"
- "Develop marking techniques for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] larvae"
- "Develop aging techniques for adult endangered fishes"

These items collectively indicate that, as of 1987, reliable field-level species/hybrid identification techniques for larval and juvenile life stages of these fishes were still lacking, particularly for distinguishing [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and roundtail chub within the broader Gila species complex.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species addressed inhabit the mainstem channels, tributaries, and associated riparian/reservoir habitats of the **Upper Colorado River Basin**, including the Green and Colorado sub-basins. Key ecological and habitat-management concerns identified include:

- **Flow dependency**: Need to "define" and "validate" habitat/flow models, and assess "the effects of flow fluctuations on the survival of endangered fishes."
- **Spawning habitat**: Priority to "locate, describe physical spawning micro-habitat for rare fishes" and investigate effects of "temperatures, salinity, sediments, turbidity on endangered fishes' eggs/larvae."
- **Larval ecology**: Field and data studies on "downstream transport, habitat use by larval endangered fishes."
- **Geomorphology**: Development of "flow criteria to protect [[Bonytail Chub]] geomorphology, habitats."
- **Biotic interactions**: Concern over competition with "nongame/exotic fishes" affecting "endangered fishes' abundance, distribution," and evaluation of "flow/temperature effects on exotic fishes" as a potential management lever.
- **Contaminants and parasites**: Calls for an "environmental contaminant survey" and bioassays, plus estimating "the importance of parasitic infestation on endangered fishes."
- **Riparian condition**: Assessment of "riparian condition relative to endangered fishes."
- **Migration barriers**: Evaluation of "migration routes" and identification of "potential barrier to migration for rare fishes," directly tied to the [[Redlands Diversion Fish Ladder]] debate.
- **Population status**: Determining "the range, distribution, abundance, etc., for [[Razorback Sucker]]" and monitoring "known or suspected" [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] populations.
- **Reintroduction**: Stocking and monitoring to "re-establish Green River HBC population," and stocking [[Bonytail Chub]] into the Green River with habitat-use monitoring.

## Key Findings

- **Prioritization Framework (Appendix 6.2)**: Research and management tasks were sorted into a 4-category × 5-priority matrix — Monitoring (I), Life History/Habitat (II), Management-Applicable (III), and Administrative (IV) — spanning priority levels 1 (highest) through 5.
- **Top priority (1) actions** included: standardized adult/juvenile monitoring programs, larval CSF (Colorado squawfish) surveys, habitat/flow model development and validation, razorback sucker range/distribution assessment, Gila complex taxonomic evaluation, fish passage design and feasibility, hatchery program efficacy evaluation, an [[Information and Education (I&E)]] program (including a potential CSF sport fishery), a basin-wide oversight/coordination panel, cessation of non-native fish stocking that harms rare fishes, and Green River [[Bonytail Chub]] stocking with habitat monitoring.
- **Redlands Diversion Fish Ladder**: The BSC recommended **suspending** construction activities at Redlands due to unresolved feasibility questions and insufficient funding, proposing instead a cheaper alternative — a passage device on a low-head hydro dam — potentially reallocating the $500,000 earmarked for Redlands.
- **Kenney Reservoir Sport Fishery**: The BSC expressed skepticism about the readiness and appropriateness of establishing a [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] sport fishery at Kenney Reservoir, questioning angler interest and noting insufficient resources and possibly unsuitable site selection despite political pressure.
- **Genetics/Taxonomy Gap**: The BSC identified an urgent need to resolve genetic differences between squawfish populations in the Green vs. Colorado sub-basins, and between wild upper-basin fish and hatchery brood stock, recommending an expert taxonomic/genetic panel — directly impacting decisions about grow-out ponds and hatchery programs.
- **Peer Review Process**: Proposed annual (starting August 1988) BSC review of Section 6/7 (Endangered Species Act) projects and agency publications to reduce duplication and improve inter-agency coordination.
- **Funding Risk**: The BSC flagged jeopardy to federal **Section 6** funding — described as "the staple of endangered species programs at the state level" — and urged FWS action to reinstate it.
- **Programmatic Transition**: Acknowledgment that the Implementation-Recovery Program was in transition, with a recommendation to synchronize the oversight panel's review cycle with planning/budgeting processes.
- **Guiding Principle**: The Subcommittee explicitly cautioned against funding allocation based solely on "biggest bang for the buck," advocating that monitoring and less immediately tangible activities remain integral to a comprehensive recovery strategy for [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
