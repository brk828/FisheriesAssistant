# Lower Basin Subcommittee Minutes (1981) and Bonytail Chub Stocking Summary (1983–1993), Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines two items on native and endangered fishes of the [[Colorado River]] system. The first is the minutes of a [[Lower Basin Subcommittee]] meeting held March 13–14, 1981, covering backwater renovation at [[Lake Mohave]], a [[Bonytail Chub]] rearing and stocking plan, a [[Lower Basin Conservation Plan]], a [[Virgin River]] [[Woundfin]] broodstock collection, and fish production needs. The second is a summary by C.O. Minckley of [[Bonytail Chub]] stocked from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and the [[Technology Center]] into the lower Colorado River between 1983 and 1993. That summary documents that most fish were stocked into ponds and backwaters, and that none were confirmed to have entered the mainstem.

## Background & Description

**Meeting (March 13–14, 1981).** Participants included representatives of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), the [[National Park Service]] (NPS), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (CFG), the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGF), and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]. The main aim was to assess and select backwaters on Lake Mohave for stocking native fishes. The minutes describe the bonytail as one of the target species of the stocking effort.

**Bonytail Chub stocking summary (1983–1993).** This is a record of transfers from the [[Parker Fish Hatchery]] (Parker NFH) and Dexter NFH. It was compiled from hatchery records and phone interviews with former staff, including refuge managers. The summary is supplementary to the original stocking records and has not been independently verified.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake Mohave backwaters.** Three candidate backwaters were examined:
- The Arizona Bay backwater, about 10 hectares, with a maximum depth of about 4 m. It is separated from the lake by a sand bar and its water level fluctuates with lake level and wave action. Seining there captured no fish, but one jumping fish and SCUBA observations suggested use by carp.
- A second backwater about 1 km north of Arizona Bay, which was seined without captures.
- A third backwater, still unsurveyed, was to be examined by [[Jim Brooks]] and [[Jim Johnson]] (both AGF/USFWS).

Two backwaters were selected for the study. The Arizona Bay site was to be renovated to remove all fish, and the other site was to be consulted on with the National Park Service. [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] would apply if a [[Bonytail Chub]] were captured.

**Gill-netting and collections.** One bonytail was captured by six trammel nets, in good condition, and transported to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH), then on to Dexter NFH. Other fishes collected were:
- About 187 [[Razorback Sucker]]s. The OCR text is partly garbled; the figure is most likely 187. Spawning was observed over gravel bars in Arizona Bay and on the Arizona shore opposite Cottonwood Landing.
- 16 [[Striped Bass]], of two age classes, averaging 433.8 mm total length (range given as 31x–577 mm; the lower bound is OCR-garbled). Rainbow trout was the predominant prey item in their stomachs.

**Virgin River.** [[Paul Marsh]], [[Paul Barrett]], [[Bob Foster]], and Brooks traveled to the Virgin River on March 15 for [[Woundfin]] broodstock collection. A total of 456 juvenile and adult woundfin was collected and sent to WBNFH and then Dexter NFH for shipping with the bonytail. Five other native species occurred in the Virgin River, along with two specimens each of [[Red Shiner]] and [[Rainbow Trout]]. Water temperature at the Beaver Dam confluence was 11 °C.

## Key Findings

**Management decisions and plans (1981 minutes):**
- **Renovation and monitoring.** The Arizona Bay site was to be renovated to remove all fish. Pre-renovation monitoring of water chemistry, phytoplankton, zooplankton, and fish diversity was planned, with post-renovation monitoring to include limnology, bonytail growth, and survival.
- **Stocking plan.** A total of 75,000 bonytail fry (OCR-garbled figure, read as 75,000) was to be stocked into each backwater. After one year, survivors were to be seined, weighed, measured, nose-tagged, and released into Lake Mohave. Expansion to other backwaters, and use of [[Razorback Sucker]] fry, were possible if resources allowed.
- **Lower Basin Conservation Plan.** This plan was to focus first on the [[Colorado Squawfish]], razorback sucker, humpback chub, and bonytail, then move toward an ecosystem approach. Brooks was to draft an outline, solicit comments, and produce a formal inter-agency plan. It was to specify stocking numbers, locations, timing, monitoring and funding responsibilities, and grow-out pond needs.
- **Fish production needs.** Requested totals included 10,000 bonytail fry and 100,000 squawfish, among other species.

**Stocking history (1983–1993):**
| Date | Number | Recipient |
|---|---|---|
| 6-8-83 | 10,000 | California Game and Fish |
| 5-7-84 | 68,211 fry | California (Linda Turner) |
| 7-16-87 | About 1,000 (OCR-garbled) | California Game and Fish, Imperial Valley |
| 10-21-93 | 21,573 (3–4 cm) | Cibola NWR, High Levee Pond |

- The 1983 and 1984 transfers went to California agencies, which held fish in small ponds and then in Blythe golf-course ponds. Around 300–320 fish were placed in Cormorant Pond on [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] in April 1988. They were initially healthy but were not found the following spring and were presumed dead.
- Fish stocked into three backwater lakes on Imperial NWR (Island Lake, Cable Lake, and the end of Yuma Wash) in 1985 were small (under 1 cm), and no numbers are recorded.
- The 1987 fish were placed in Blythe golf-course ponds and died.
- The 1993 fish were placed in High Levee Pond, an isolated pond on Cibola NWR. Three larger individuals have been recaptured, all returned to the pond.

**Summary and discrepancies.** The summary reports 68,531 bonytail stocked before 1990, with 300–320 in Cormorant Pond and the remainder (68,211) placed in the Colorado mainstem. It also states that none were put into the mainstem. These statements conflict, and the 1983 transfer of 10,000 fish is not clearly accounted for. No bonytail from the 1993 stocking had been placed into the mainstem at the time of the summary.

**Policy history.** In the 1980s the USFWS proposed a [[Nonessential Experimental Population]] of bonytail in the Salt and Gila watersheds and the lower Colorado River. Opposition from stakeholders led to withdrawal of the proposal in 1989.
