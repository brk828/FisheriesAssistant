## Executive Summary
This Water Pollution Research Laboratory paper by R. Lloyd presents a graphical method for predicting the toxicity of ammonia to [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*) in freshwater systems. The study establishes that ammonia toxicity is governed primarily by the **un-ionized ammonia molecule** concentration, which is itself controlled by pH, bicarbonate alkalinity, temperature, dissolved oxygen, and free carbon dioxide concentration. A **threshold concentration** — the concentration lethal to 50% of a fish population even after prolonged exposure — is defined and can be calculated from empirical curves (Figs. 2–4) with predicted values matching observed laboratory results within ~12% error. The paper concludes with a proposed "maximum safe concentration" (~70% of threshold) to prevent fish mortality in polluted rivers, and notes that co-occurring pollutants (e.g., phenol) can act synergistically to increase ammonia's toxicity.

## Background & Description
This paper is a toxicological/water-chemistry study and does not describe morphological features of *Salmo gairdnerii*. No anatomical, meristic, or coloration data are presented. The species is referenced solely as the test organism for [[Ammonia Toxicity]] bioassays conducted at the [[Water Pollution Research Laboratory]], Stevenage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Rainbow Trout]] inhabit freshwater rivers subject to pollutant discharge, including ammonia from industrial/agricultural sources.
- **Environmental sensitivity factors:**
  - **pH value** — the dominant factor controlling the proportion of un-ionized (toxic) ammonia relative to ionized ammonium.
  - **Temperature** — decreases in temperature increase survival time; the threshold concentration of un-ionized ammonia is believed to be independent of temperature itself, but temperature affects the ionization equilibrium of ammonia.
  - **Dissolved oxygen (DO) concentration** — reduced DO increases ammonia toxicity, with the effect modulated by free CO₂ concentration; below ~20% air-saturation DO, oxygen depletion itself becomes lethal to trout independent of ammonia.
  - **Bicarbonate alkalinity** and **free carbon dioxide** — both influence the calculated threshold concentration via their effect on water chemistry equilibria.
  - **Calcium concentration** — does NOT influence ammonia toxicity (unlike some other poisons).
  - **Fish age/size** — no detectable variation in susceptibility to ammonia across different age/size classes of rainbow trout was found.
- **Co-pollutant interactions:** Ammonia and [[Phenol]] toxicities act in a combined (synergistic) manner, meaning safe ammonia concentrations must be reduced in rivers where phenol is also present.
- **Comparative note:** A similar curvilinear log-concentration/log-survival relationship (and temperature-independence of the threshold) was observed for [[Zinc Toxicity]] in rainbow trout, suggesting a shared toxicological pattern among certain poisons.

## Key Findings
- **Threshold concentration definition:** The concentration of ammonia that kills only 50% of a trout population even after prolonged exposure; log(concentration) vs. log(median survival time) is curvilinear, becoming vertical beyond ~250 minutes median survival (Fig. 1).
- **pH & alkalinity relationship (Fig. 2):** Threshold total ammonia concentration varies with pH and bicarbonate alkalinity at a fixed 20°C and air-saturated dissolved oxygen; curves allow interpolation for intermediate alkalinities.
- **Temperature correction (Fig. 3):** Threshold values (from Fig. 2, valid at 20°C) must be multiplied by a temperature correction factor for temperatures below 20°C; factors calculated at pH 8.0 are valid (±5%) across pH 6.5–8.5.
- **Dissolved oxygen correction (Fig. 4):** Threshold values must be further multiplied by a DO correction factor dependent on free CO₂ concentration; correction factors for free CO₂ >20 p.p.m. do not differ substantially from the 20 p.p.m. curve. Below ~35% air saturation, the factor drops rapidly toward zero due to independent oxygen lethality below ~20% saturation.
- **Worked example:** For a river with bicarbonate alkalinity 200 p.p.m. CaCO₃, pH 7.6, temperature 18°C, DO 60% air saturation, free CO₂ 11.0 p.p.m.:
  - Base threshold (Fig. 2) = 36 p.p.m. as N
  - × Temperature factor (1.15, Fig. 3)
  - × DO factor (0.73, Fig. 4)
  - **Final predicted threshold = 30.2 p.p.m. as N**
- **Validation (Table 1):** Across 10 independent laboratory experiments (bicarbonate alkalinity 50–250 p.p.m., pH 7.0–8.2, temperature 10.7–20.7°C, DO 37.5–100% saturation), predicted vs. observed threshold concentrations differed by no more than ~12%, confirming the method's reliability.
- **Maximum safe concentration:** Threshold values (50% mortality) should be multiplied by a factor of **0.7** to estimate the concentration causing ~1% mortality — proposed as the maximum safe concentration to avoid fish kills.
- **Practical application:** The method allows prediction of ammonia toxicity in real river systems using standard water chemistry measurements (pH, alkalinity, temperature, DO, free CO₂), enabling regulatory assessment of pollution discharge safety — but only valid in the absence of other toxicants; combined toxicity (e.g., with phenol) requires more conservative safe limits.
