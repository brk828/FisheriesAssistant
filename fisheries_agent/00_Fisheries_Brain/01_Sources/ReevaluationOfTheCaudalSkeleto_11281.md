# Reevaluation of the Caudal Skeleton of Actinopterygian Fishes: I. Lepisosteus and Amia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepisosteidae #amiidae

## Executive Summary

Schultze and Arratia (1986), publishing in the [[Journal of Morphology]], reexamine the caudal (tail) skeleton of two living "lower" actinopterygians, the [[Longnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus*) and the [[Bowfin]] (*Amia*). Using growth series, cleared-and-stained specimens, serial sections and fossil material, they test the assumption that ural (tail) vertebrae form the same way as anterior preural vertebrae. They conclude that the centra of the two genera develop by fundamentally different routes: gar centra are perichondral ossifications of arcualia (arcocentra), whereas bowfin centra are direct perichordal ossifications (autocentra). The authors argue that bowfin is closer to the teleost condition in ural centrum formation and hypural fusion, and that the two genera should not be grouped together as Halecostomi, as proposed by [[Nybelin]] (1977).

## Background & Description

The caudal skeleton has long been a key character in the systematics of teleostean actinopterygians. Teleosts have a diural caudal skeleton with two ural centra, while lower actinopterygians have a polyural skeleton with a one-to-one relationship between neural arches, centra and hypurals (equivalents of haemal spines). Both gar and bowfin are considered closest extant relatives of teleosts by different authors, with bowfin associated with [[Patterson]] (1973) and gar with [[Olsen]] (1984).

Key terminology defined in the paper:
- **Autocentrum:** a centrum or part of one formed outside the elastica externa by direct ossification.
- **Arcocentrum:** formed by ossification of cartilage extending from the arcualia around the notochord.
- **Chordacentrum:** calcification in the middle fibrous part of the chordal sheath.
- **Arcuale:** a cartilaginous preformed element lying dorsal or ventral to the notochord. Four arcualia (interdorsal, basidorsal, interventral, basiventral) typically contribute to each centrum.
- **Preural centrum 1:** the vertebra bearing the last haemal arch passed by the caudal artery, where the artery bifurcates into the arteriae pinnales. This marks the boundary with the ural region.
- **True epurals** are modified neural spines (gar), whereas "epurals" that lie between neural spines are radials or pterygiophores (bowfin).

Materials comprised 13 recent *Lepisosteus* and 15 recent plus 12 fossil amiids, with serial sections of one *L. osseus* and one *Amia calva*. Specimens were cleared and double-stained with Alcian blue and alizarin. Measurements of centrum length and area were expressed as percentages of preural centrum 5.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a morphological and developmental study and does not address habitat, ecology, water chemistry or distribution beyond specimen provenance. Specimen localities include Pine Hill, Union County, Illinois, and Rivière des Prairies, Quebec, for recent bowfin, and fossil localities at [[Messel]] (Eocene, Germany), Armissan (Late Oligocene, France) and the [[Hessisches Landesmuseum]], Darmstadt.

## Key Findings

**Centrum formation differs between the genera**
- *Lepisosteus* centra are arcocentra, formed from dorsal and ventral arcualia.
- *Amia* centra are autocentra that enclose the arcualia.
- Chordacentra appear early in both genera but do not contribute substantially to the final centra.

**Preural and ural organization**
- In *Lepisosteus*, preural centra 1–10 to 2 are monospondylous. Preural centrum 1 is fused with ural centrum 1 in adults, based on several lines of evidence.
- In *Amia*, preural centra 4 to 2 are diplospondylous in large specimens, while preural centrum 1 and ural centra are monospondylous. Transition from diplospondyly to monospondyly occurs within the preural series in larger animals.
- Ural centra in *Lepisosteus* are expansions of dorsal arcualia (interdorsal and basidorsal). In *Amia* they are mainly expansions of the basiventral autocentrum.

**Hypurals, neural arches and epurals**
- In *Lepisosteus*, hypurals are autogenous and neural arches are fused to ural centra. Ural centra are not fused to hypurals.
- In *Amia*, hypurals are fused to ural centra (basiventral autocentra) and neural arches are autogenous. The compound ural neural arch is free.
- *Lepisosteus* has true epurals derived from neural spines. In *Amia* the "epurals" are radial-like elements between neural spines.
- Juvenile *Lepisosteus* show more epurals (5–8) than larger specimens (5; 7 in a 730 mm *L. osseus*).

**Developmental and count data**
- *Lepisosteus* juveniles (60–70 mm) show arcocentra forming by perichondral ossification of arcualia, with ossification proceeding caudally along the notochord.
- Juvenile *Lepisosteus* have 7–10 hypurals; a 189 mm specimen has 12, and a 645 mm *L. platostomus* has nine.
- *Amia* 37–63 mm specimens have 24–32 caudal fin-rays, supported by nine or ten hypurals and haemal spines of ten preural vertebrae.
- A 139 mm *Lepisosteus* shows preural centrum 1 at 75% of the length of preural centrum 5. In *Amia*, preural centrum 1 is about half the length of preural centrum 5 in the adult study specimens.

**Vascular patterns**
- Both genera show the caudal artery bifurcating in the posterior part of the haemal arch of preural centrum 1. In *Lepisosteus* the vascular pattern is asymmetric, with arterial networks mainly on the right and venous networks on the left. In *Amia* arrangement varies so much among specimens that no single pattern could be described.

**Conclusions**
1. Centra of *Lepisosteus* and *Amia* form differently, and the authors argue they should not be placed together within Halecostomi.
2. Both genera have a polyural caudal skeleton with a one-to-one relationship between ural centra and hypurals, which is most obvious in juveniles.
3. Ural centra and hypural/neural arch relationships differ between the genera. The authors conclude that *Amia* is closer to the teleost condition in ural centrum formation and in fusion of hypurals to ural centra.
4. *Lepisosteus* has rudimentary neural arches in the tail, while in *Amia* a compound ural neural arch covers the neural cord from ural centrum 2 caudally.
5. Epurals have different origins in the two genera, which the authors suggest explains discrepancies in the literature.

Fossil *Amia* from Armissan and *A. kehreri* from Messel show no remarkable differences from extant *A. calva* in caudal structure.
