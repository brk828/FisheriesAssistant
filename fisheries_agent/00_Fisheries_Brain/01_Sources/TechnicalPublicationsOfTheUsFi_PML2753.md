# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Fish Technology Centers Publication Listing, 1966–2001

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-technology

## Executive Summary

This document is a bibliographic listing of publications produced by the [[Fish Technology Centers]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), covering roughly 1966 to 2000 (title dates 1966–2001). It lists journal articles, station leaflets, proceedings papers, and technical reports, grouped by Center. Its stated purpose is [[Technology Transfer]]: making research on fishery management and culture available to the public so that current science can be applied to fisheries work.

## Background & Description

In 1965 the USFWS established seven Fish Technology Centers nationwide to provide leadership and technical guidance to the [[National Fish Hatchery System]] and the fish culture community. Early work focused on reducing costs, improving fish quality, and streamlining hatchery operations. The mandate later expanded to include:

- Captive propagation of imperiled aquatic species
- Interjurisdictional restoration programs
- Control of invasive aquatic species
- Genetic management of aquatic populations

Center work ranges from developing diets for species never before held in captivity to marking techniques, genetic stock identification, and study design support. Centers draw on expertise in physiology, genetics, and biometrics, and act as a cohesive system that uses geographic differences to make results applicable across users. They also partner with other Service programs, federal agencies, states, tribes, and the private sector.

The listing is arranged by facility:

- [[Bozeman Fish Technology Center]] (Montana): the largest section, with journal articles, [[Bozeman Information Leaflets]], and many entries on salmonid and walleye culture, fish health, and oxygenation
- [[Dexter Fish Technology Center]] (New Mexico): entries on [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], and cryopreservation of sperm from endangered fishes
- [[Lamar Fish Technology Center]] (Pennsylvania): entries on [[Atlantic Salmon]], [[American Shad]], [[Atlantic Sturgeon]], and marking methods such as calcein and coded wire tags
- [[San Marcos Fish Technology Center]] (Texas): entries on [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], fish transport, and [[Texas Wildrice]]
- [[Warm Springs Regional Fisheries Center]] (Georgia): peer-reviewed and proceedings papers on fish anesthesia, catheterization, cryopreservation, and sturgeon and redhorse culture
- Other Centers, including Abernathy (Washington), Leadville (Colorado), and Cortland (New York), are cited in collaborative or cross-listed entries

The compilation includes peer-reviewed journal articles, Progressive Fish-Culturist papers, proceedings, Technical Information Leaflets, and Miscellaneous Publications. Titles prior to 1995 are available by request from the relevant facility, and the list is updated online.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listing addresses fish culture and management rather than field ecology, but many entries involve environmental conditions and stressors:

- **Water quality and reuse:** effects of water reuse on [[Lake Trout]], [[Coho Salmon]], and Atlantic salmon; acute toxicity of ammonia and nitrite to cutthroat trout fry; [[Nitrite-Induced Methemoglobinemia]]; formalin and sulfamerazine toxicity
- **Oxygen and temperature:** [[Oxygen Supersaturation]] effects on cutthroat and rainbow trout; low-temperature and low-oxygen tolerance of largemouth bass; high-temperature tolerance of Arctic grayling in the Big Hole River, Montana
- **Pathogens and disease:** [[Whirling Disease]] in wild trout; [[Proliferative Kidney Disease]] (first North American outbreak documented in 1981); [[Gas Bubble Disease]]; the myxosporean parasite [[Ceratomyxa Shasta]]; the protozoan *Amphilepti­s branchiarum* in pond fish; *Saprolegnia* treatment on salmon eggs
- **Contaminants:** absorption of dietary dieldrin by striped bass; dietary and environmental effects on liver disease and tumors in Atlantic tomcod from the Hudson River estuary
- **Invasive species:** feasibility of preventing [[Zebra Mussel]] spread to the western United States (1993); zebra mussel summaries for Region 2 field stations
- **Habitat restoration:** reintroduction of Texas wildrice in Spring Lake, Texas, and environmental factors affecting its growth

## Key Findings

The listing shows the main research and technology themes of the Centers across their history:

1. **Nutrition and diets.** A large share of entries concern practical diets, starter diets, and feed evaluations for species including [[Walleye]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Atlantic Salmon]], [[Lake Trout]], [[Striped Bass]], and Arctic grayling. Examples include soybean meal as a herring meal substitute (Reinitz, 1980), herring versus menhaden oil effects on fin condition in Atlantic salmon (Mohler et al., 1996; Farrell and Gaston, 1998), and dietary effects on dorsal fin erosion in steelhead (Lellis and Barrows, 1997).

2. **Fish health and disease.** Many papers address diagnosis, pathology, and treatment, including [[Whirling Disease]] re-emergence in wild trout (Hedrick et al., 1998), gas bubble disease histopathology (Smith, 1987 and 1988), and treatments for *Saprolegnia* on fall chinook eggs (Waterstrat and Marking, 1995).

3. **Oxygenation and water management.** Studies on supplemental oxygen, oxygen supersaturation, the Aquatector oxygenation system, and pure-oxygen injection into sealed columns (Kindschi, Edsall, Dwyer, and colleagues, 1987–1990) report performance and oxygen consumption.

4. **Imperiled species and genetic management.** Dexter Center work covers captive propagation, genetic guidelines for salmon and steelhead supplementation, and genetic management for the upper Colorado River endangered fishes. Cryopreservation of sperm from [[Razorback Sucker]] (Tiersch et al., 1997, 1998) and from the bonytail chub is a notable line of work.

5. **Marking and evaluation.** Methods include calcein marking of larval American shad and glass eels (Mohler, 1999), coded wire tag implantation in Atlantic salmon sac-fry (Fletcher and Mohler, 1994), and an electronic walleye fry counter (Kindschi and Barrows, 1990).

6. **Sublethal effects of handling and electroshock.** Electroshock studies on juvenile bull trout, Arctic grayling, and cutthroat trout (Barton and Dwyer, 1997; Dwyer and White, 1995, 1997) examine physiological stress and growth after treatment.

7. **Transport and anesthesia.** The San Marcos Center assessed aeration in fish-hauling tanks (Fries et al., 1992, 1993), and the Warm Springs Center published on anesthesia and surgical technique in fish (Harms and Bakal, 1994).

8. **Atlantic salmon and sturgeon culture.** Lamar Center produced a series of Atlantic Salmon Workshop proceedings (1984–1993) and sturgeon culture guidance, such as observations on oocyte development in captive Atlantic sturgeon (Mohler et al., 1995).

Overall, the listing documents a sustained technology-transfer effort. Its value lies in linking practitioners to peer-reviewed and leaflet-level information on fish culture, disease management, and conservation of imperiled species.
