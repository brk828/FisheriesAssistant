# Grant Awarded for Endangered Species Habitat to Be Protected in Coal Mine Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #topminnow

## Executive Summary

This October 20, 2004 press release announces a $750,000 grant to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD) for the purchase and protection of habitat for the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] in Coal Mine Canyon, [[Santa Cruz County]], Arizona. The grant comes from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) through its [[Recovery Land Acquisition Grant Program]] and is administered with the [[Trust for Public Land]] (TPL), which holds the property until transfer. The land will be managed by AZGFD as a conservation area. The release describes the purchase as a second acquisition for the species in the watershed, following an earlier grant.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** The [[Gila Topminnow]] is an endangered small freshwater fish. The release describes it as once the most common fish in the watershed, which indicates a severe decline.
- **Funding body:** USFWS grants under the Recovery Land Acquisition program buy land for threatened and endangered species in support of recovery plans.
- **Lead agency:** AZGFD receives the grant and will own and manage the property.
- **Partners:** TPL describes the project as part of a multi-partner collaborative effort. The release cites the Arizona State Director of TPL as a source of critical funding support.
- **Contacts:** Mary Meneses (TPL) and Michael Patrick (USFWS, Arizona) are listed for media inquiries.
- **Acreage:** The size of the parcel is not legible in the OCR text. The release refers to a figure for total acres protected, but the number could not be reliably recovered.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat value:** The property adjoins existing protected land near [[Sonoita Creek State Natural Area]], and is intended to protect topminnow habitat and the watershed that supports it.
- **Threats:** The release identifies the introduction and spread of [[Exotic Species]] (non-native fish and other organisms) as a key threat to native topminnow populations. Protecting the habitat is intended to limit such invasions.
- **Co-occurring species:** The property also provides habitat for the [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]] and the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], both listed species, which adds to its conservation value.
- **Water connectivity:** The protected waters are described as interconnecting waterways within the watershed, so management of the canyon supports the wider aquatic system.

## Key Findings

1. A $750,000 USFWS Recovery Land Acquisition grant was awarded to AZGFD to protect Gila topminnow habitat in Coal Mine Canyon.
2. The purchase is the second acquisition for the topminnow in this watershed; a previous grant protected a separate tract of topminnow habitat.
3. The property will be held by TPL until it is transferred to AZGFD, which will manage it for the species.
4. Exotic species are the main threat the project is meant to counter, and the site's protection is expected to help maintain the watershed's native fish populations.
5. The parcel also supports two federally listed species, the lesser long-nosed bat and the Mexican spotted owl, which strengthens its conservation value.
6. The project is a multi-partner collaboration among federal, state, and nonprofit organizations.
