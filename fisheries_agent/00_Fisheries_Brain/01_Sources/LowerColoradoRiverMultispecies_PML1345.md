# Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program: Final Implementation Report, FY2008 Work Plan and Budget, FY2006 Accomplishment Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This June 2007 report from the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (LCR MSCP) documents the program's FY2006 accomplishments, FY2008 work plan and budget, and associated compliance reporting. The LCR MSCP is a 50-year partnership of federal agencies, states, water and power users, tribes and conservation groups, implementing a [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] (HCP) to balance water diversion and power production on the Lower Colorado River with conservation of native species. It covers 26 federal- or state-listed candidate and sensitive species, of which 9 are listed under the federal [[Endangered Species Act]].

The estimated program cost is $626 million (2003 dollars), split 50% to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and 50% to the states of California, Nevada and Arizona. The report covers financial contributions, habitat creation goals, fish augmentation, species research, system monitoring, conservation area development and adaptive management, and lists work tasks grouped by section.

## Background & Description

- **Program scope:** Long-term plan to conserve at least 26 species and their habitats from Lake Mead to the Mexican border. It accommodates current water diversions and power production while allowing future development consistent with law.
- **Covered taxa:** Mammals, birds, fish, amphibians, reptiles, invertebrates and plants. Key covered fishes include [[Razorback Sucker]] (RASU), [[Bonytail]] (BONY), [[Humpback Chub]] (HUCH) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (FLSU).
- **Implementation documents (signed April 2005):** Environmental Impact Statement/Report, Biological Assessment, a [[Biological and Conference Opinion]] (2005 BO), HCP, Record of Decision, [[Funding and Management Agreement]] (FMA), [[Implementation Agreement]] (IA), and a Secretarial Implementation Agreement (SIA).
- **Related actions:** Compliance with a December 2001 [[USFWS]] Biological Opinion on [[Interim Surplus Criteria]] (ISC) and the [[Secretarial Implementation Agreement]]; a [[California Endangered Species Act]] (CESA) permit with the [[California Department of Fish and Game]].
- **Governance:** A Steering Committee with Federal, Arizona, California, Nevada, Native American, Conservation and Other Interested Parties participant groups (over 50 member entities listed in the report) provides input and oversight.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat creation goals under the HCP:**
- 5,940 acres cottonwood-willow
- 1,320 acres honey mesquite
- 512 acres marsh
- 360 acres backwater

The report notes these targets total roughly 8,132 acres of new habitat. Habitat is to be created in integrated mosaics approximating natural floodplain relationships. [[Conservation Areas]] include sites such as [[Beal Lake]], [[Palo Verde Ecological Reserve]], [[Cibola Valley Conservation Area]], [[Ahakhav Tribal Preserve]] and [[Imperial Ponds]].

**Augmentation targets:** 660,000 razorback suckers and 620,000 bonytail to supplement existing populations in the lower river, with stocking ratios and production goals set by facility.

**Threats and stressors noted:** Quagga mussel infestation of Lakes Mead and Mohave (discovered January 2007), which restricted fish transport; brown-headed cowbird parasitism of [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] nests; reduced flows from water transfers; invasive plants such as morning glory and saltcedar; and predation by nonnative fish and birds.

## Key Findings

**Financial status**
- Federal and non-federal funding requirements are set in Table 1-1a, indexed annually for inflation (composite index from two years prior).
- Program account FY2008 contribution schedule totals roughly $13.3 million, with the 2001 BO account holding $6 million plus interest.
- Reclamation sought a FY06 credit of $506,149; San Diego County Water Authority sought $500,000.
- A Habitat Maintenance Fund totals $541,500 deposited in FY06, with $593,500 expected for FY07.

**FY2006 accomplishments (selected)**
- Razorback sucker stocking below Parker Dam: 20,012 fish stocked between 2003 and January 2007 (meeting the 20,000 requirement under the 2001 BO).
- Lake Mohave larval collection: 63,975 RASU larvae collected in FY06 for brood stock and augmentation.
- Achii Hanyo rearing facility: 5,714 BONY tagged and released in December 2006 (1,708 to Reach 3, 4,006 to Reaches 4 and 5).
- Uvalde NFH trial: 3,000 BONY reared to 300 mm TL with 92% survival (2,744 fish) over 180 days.
- Lower river survey: 14,782 fish of at least 24 species contacted, including 489 RASU capture events.
- Brown-headed cowbird control study: trapping reduced local abundance and parasitism; populations took 5–6 years post-trapping to return to pre-trap levels. Trapping intervals of 3–6 years were suggested depending on landscape factors.
- Beal Lake: non-native fish eradicated by rotenone treatments in April 2006 and a cylindrical wedge-wire screen system tested for exclusion.

**Work plan priorities (FY2008)**
- Continue fish augmentation at Willow Beach NFH, Achii Hanyo, Dexter NFH, Bubbling Ponds, Lake Mead and Uvalde NFH.
- Initiate Imperial Ponds native fish research and a Lake Mead RASU raceway rearing evaluation.
- Develop a multi-year science strategy and an adaptive management program with a science advisory panel.
- Continue marsh bird, southwestern willow flycatcher, bat, small mammal and yellow-billed cuckoo surveys.
- Complete the LCR MSCP database framework; annual fishery database cost estimated at $110,000 per year.

**Compliance and reporting**
- The report documents the running tabulation of habitat created, conservation measures completed, and incidental take (surrogate measures include flow-related loss of cottonwood-willow habitat and non-flow-related acreage affected).
- The USFWS commented in its letter (Appendix C) that two new work tasks for FY06 were consistent with the 2001 BO, and the state of California and Arizona gave recommendations on the Overton Wildlife Management Area as a replacement for Boulder City Wetland Ponds.
- No minor modifications to the LCR MSCP were made during this reporting period.

**Closed work tasks:** Sixteen work tasks were closed in FY05–FY06 (for example, Brown-headed Cowbird Trap Assessment, closed in FY06).
