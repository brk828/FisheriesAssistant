# Fish Remains from Two Submerged Deposits in Tomales Bay, Marin County, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #archaeoichthyology

## Executive Summary

This Occasional Paper by W. I. Follett (California Academy of Sciences, 1968) reports 26 fragmentary fish remains recovered in 1964 by [[Calvin Crowell Daetwyler Jr]] from two submerged deposits in [[Tomales Bay]], Marin County, California. The remains represent six species in six genera and five families. Most specimens came from a shell midden sampled by [[Test Boring No. 1]], while a natural deposit sampled by [[Test Boring No. 3]] yielded only a shark tooth and one sardine scale. Radiocarbon dating and the species composition together point to aboriginal fishing in shallow bay waters and to recent tectonic subsidence of the bay floor.

## Background & Description

**Study area and sampling.** [[Test Boring No. 1]] was located in [[White Gulch]] on the southwest shore of Tomales Bay, opposite Hog Island, in water 7 feet deep at mean lower low water. Specimens came from 10.7 to about 12.1 feet below mean lower low water (3.7 to about 5.1 feet below the bay bottom). The top 3 feet were black silty clay, probably from historic agricultural erosion. Below this lay sandier sediment with midden-like shell and charcoal, plus a small shell bead at 12.1 feet. [[Test Boring No. 3]] lay about 1300 feet off [[Manila Marina]] on the northeast shore, in 16 feet of water. Specimens came from 21.0 to 24.5 feet below mean lower low water (5.0 to 8.4 feet below the bay bottom), and no midden was observed there.

**Dating.** Material from Test Boring No. 1 was judged archaeological by James R. Moriarty and gave a [[Radiocarbon Dating]] result of 340 ± 130 years BP (LJ-GAP-43). Material from Test Boring No. 3 gave 1700 ± 190 years BP (LJ-GAP-46). Daetwyler linked the shelly pebble horizon at the second site to a horizon widespread in the bay, possibly a lower sea stand related to submergence along an active fault zone.

**Specimens.** The remains are fragmentary and are deposited in the marine-geological collections at the [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]], La Jolla.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species list spans shallow estuarine fishes and offshore fishes:

- **Shallow-water and estuarine species** such as [[Pacific Herring]], [[Pacific Sardine]], [[Bat Stingray]] and [[Southern Starry Flounder]] are known from or enter Tomales Bay.
- **Oceanic species** such as [[Pacific Hake]] normally live at depths greater than 50 fathoms. Its occurrence is attributed to shallow-water pursuit of prey or stranding rather than to a resident population.
- **Large predators** such as the [[Sevengill Shark]] enter the bay at times in considerable numbers.

Follett also noted that small numbers of [[Pacific Sardine]] persist in Tomales Bay despite the species' large decline in California waters. Frank Spenger Sr. seined large sardines there each early spring, and the most recent verified record was a large adult taken on 26 April 1964 at Young Landing.

**Aboriginal fishing methods.** Follett suggests that the herring and sardine were taken by seine or gill net, and the stingray and flounder by hand line from the shore. The hake may have been caught by net or hook in the bay, or it may have stranded on nearby beaches. Its presence is not evidence of watercraft use.

## Key Findings

- **Assemblage:** 26 fragmentary specimens, representing six species, six genera and five families.
- **Sevengill shark (*Notorynchus maculatus*):** One incomplete lower tooth (base 14 mm), representing a shark about 190 cm long, from Test Boring No. 3. The species is said to reach about 300 cm.
- **Bat stingray (*Myliobatis californica*):** One incomplete centrum from Test Boring No. 1, possibly a half-grown individual. The species reaches about 95 kg and is exceedingly abundant in Tomales Bay.
- **Pacific herring (*Clupea pallasii*):** Eight incomplete vertebrae, representing one or more fish about 21 cm long, from Test Boring No. 1. The species spawns in the bay in January to March.
- **Pacific sardine (*Sardinops sagax*):** One right dentary, 7 scales and 5 vertebrae from Test Boring No. 1, representing fish about 25 cm long, plus one scale from Test Boring No. 3. This is the most sampled species after herring.
- **Pacific hake (*Merluccius productus*):** One precaudal vertebra from Test Boring No. 1, representing a fish about 50 cm long. It is not previously reported from Tomales Bay.
- **Southern starry flounder (*?Platichthys stellatus rugosus*):** One questionably referred vertebra fragment from Test Boring No. 1, possibly a small adult. The species averages about 41 cm and 1 kg.
- **Site distribution:** Nearly all material, including all species except the shark and one sardine scale, came from the midden at Test Boring No. 1.
- **Regional comparisons:** Follett compared the species with aboriginal use at Burton Mound, Conejo Rock Shelter, Newport Bay, Strawberry Point and Estero de Limantour. He also listed Pleistocene and Pliocene fossil records from Playa del Rey, San Pedro, Capistrano Beach and Long Beach. These include sevengill shark teeth, bat stingray teeth, herring otoliths and hake otoliths.
- **Conclusion:** The assemblage documents shallow-water fishing by aboriginal people in Tomales Bay and supports evidence of recent tectonic subsidence. The hake record is ambiguous as evidence of watercraft use.
