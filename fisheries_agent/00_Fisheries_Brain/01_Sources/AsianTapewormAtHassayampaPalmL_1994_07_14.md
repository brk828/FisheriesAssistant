# Asian Tapeworm Outbreak in Bonytail Chubs — Hassayampa Preserve (Palm Lake)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #parasitology #Cyprinidae #FishHealth

## Executive Summary
This internal U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service correspondence (June–July 1994) documents the detection of the invasive **Asian Tapeworm** (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*) in [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Longfin Dace]] populations at Palm Lake, part of The Nature Conservancy's Hassayampa Preserve in Arizona. The parasite was discovered during a routine health screening prior to a planned translocation of bonytail chubs to [[Lake Havasu]] for conservation stocking. Post-hoc necropsy of frozen specimens confirmed infection, raising serious concern that already-infected fish had been transferred to Lake Havasu before diagnosis was complete. The report also details a successful **Praziquantel** treatment protocol previously validated at Dexter National Fish Hatchery and a California hatchery, and discusses the broader panzootic spread of the parasite via [[Grass Carp]] and bait minnow trade.

## Background & Description
This document is a fish health/parasitology report rather than a taxonomic description; recorded biometric data pertains to host specimens examined for infection intensity, not comparative morphology of the species itself.

- **Host specimens examined (Bonytail Chub):**
  - Specimen 1: 390 mm TL / 330 mm SL — harbored **1 adult tapeworm**
  - Specimen 2: 350 mm TL / 300 mm SL — harbored **3 adult tapeworms**
  - Both fish were PIT-tagged, indicating they were part of a monitored conservation stocking program.
- **Parasite (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*):** Adult cestode recovered directly from host stomach/intestinal tract; pathogenic mechanism involves intestinal rupture in small-bodied fish, occasionally causing mortality.
- **Co-occurring ectoparasites/pathogens noted during screening:**
  - *Chilodonella* sp. — light infestation on skin (Brown Bullhead, Bonytail Chub)
  - *Trichodina* sp. — light infestation on gills (Brown Bullhead)
  - *Aeromonas* sp. — bacterial isolate (both species)
  - *Pseudomonas* sp. — bacterial isolate (Brown Bullhead)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Palm Lake is a closed, spring-fed preserve water body at the [[Hassayampa Preserve]], functioning as a refuge/rearing site for the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] prior to translocation into larger reservoirs of the Lower Colorado River system ([[Lake Havasu]], [[Lake Mohave]]).
- **Host range within this report:** [[Bonytail Chub]] (large-bodied cyprinid, less susceptible to lethal effects), [[Longfin Dace]] (small-bodied cyprinid, experiencing losses attributed to tapeworm burden), [[Brown Bullhead]] (co-habiting ictalurid, screened but tapeworm not confirmed in this species here), and previously affected [[Beautiful Shiner]] at Dexter NFH.
- **Vector/spread pathway:** The Asian Tapeworm is described as **panzootic**, disseminated primarily through movement of [[Grass Carp]] (its original introduction vector) and contaminated bait minnows, with probable origin traced to Arkansas.
- **Differential risk by system size:** The author notes that dilution effects in large reservoirs (Lake Mohave, Lake Havasu) likely reduce transmission risk relative to small, confined riverine systems such as the [[Virgin River]], where infection intensity could be proportionally more damaging to small-fish populations.
- **Conservation stocking risk:** The case highlights a critical management vulnerability — endangered species (Bonytail Chub) held in isolated refuge populations can become parasite reservoirs, complicating recovery efforts when specimens are moved to reintroduction sites.

## Key Findings
- **Diagnosis confirmed post-transfer:** Longfin dace from the Hassayampa aquarium (submitted May 9, 1994) tested positive for *Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*; subsequent necropsy of two frozen bonytail chubs (received June 4, thawed June 10) confirmed the same infection, but **only after** the live cohort had already been shipped to Lake Havasu on the night of April 28, 1994.
- **Management directive:** Fish Health Biologist Jerry Landye recommended that **no fish from Hassayampa Preserve be used for stock transfer without prior deworming**, and that any bonytail chubs later recovered from Lake Havasu be treated as well.
- **Validated treatment protocol:** A single **Praziquantel (Prazi)** water bath at **0.75 ppm for 24 hours** achieved 100% elimination of Asian Tapeworm in bonytail chubs (California State Fish Hatchery, prior to Lake Mohave stocking) and in beautiful shiners at Dexter NFH, confirmed via necropsy.
- **Reinfection risk:** Dexter National Fish Hatchery experienced reinfections despite successful initial treatment, underscoring the need for source-water control to prevent reintroduction of the parasite.
- **Regulatory constraint:** Praziquantel lacked a USFWS-approved label for use in this context; officials considered extra-label veterinary administration or pursuing an FDA exemption for Threatened/Endangered species as pathways to legal treatment.
- **Pathogenicity assessment:** Large-bodied fish like adult bonytail chubs appear to tolerate tapeworm infection without severe effects, while small fish are at risk of intestinal rupture and mortality under heavy parasite loads.
- **Acknowledged management failure:** The author explicitly states, "It is unfortunate that the infected bonytails were transferred from Palm Lake to Lake Havasu," while noting the tapeworm was likely already present at Havasu independently, tempering the direct causal impact of this specific transfer.
