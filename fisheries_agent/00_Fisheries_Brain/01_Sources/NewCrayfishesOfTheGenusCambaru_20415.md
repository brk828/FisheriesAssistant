# New Crayfishes of the Genus Cambarus from Tennessee and Georgia

#carcinology #decapoda #cambarus

## Executive Summary

This 1970 taxonomic paper by [[Horton H. Hobbs Jr.]] describes two new crayfish species of the genus [[Cambarus]] (Decapoda, Astacidae): [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] from Tennessee and [[Cambarus Cymatilis]] from Georgia. The author argues that [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] is a primitive species linking the most primitive known member of the genus, [[Cambarus Pristinus]], with more advanced subgenera. Based on this, the author emends the subgenus [[Subgenus Veticambarus]] to include three species and supports the hypothesis that the [[Cumberland Plateau]] is the ancestral home of the genus. The document is a formal species description with diagnoses, measurements, types, distribution, and relationships.

## Background & Description

The paper opens with a biogeographic hypothesis from earlier work (Hobbs, 1965, 1969): the genus *Cambarus* originated on the [[Cumberland Plateau]], which also hosts [[Cambarus Pristinus]] (restricted to the [[Caney Fork]] of the [[Cumberland River]]), two relict troglobitic subspecies of [[Orconectes]], and [[Cambarus Obeyensis]] in the [[Obey River]] drainage. The new species was found in headwater streams of the [[Big South Fork]] of the Cumberland River.

Key morphological characters used to place the species:

- **First pleopod (male):** the terminal elements, especially the central projection and mesial process, are the main diagnostic structures. [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] resembles [[Cambarus Pristinus]] most closely, but its terminal elements are less reflected caudally. The caudal knob is less developed than in [[Cambarus Pristinus]]. See [[First Pleopod]].
- **Chela:** [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] has a subquadrate chela like the [[Subgenus Jugicambarus]], whereas [[Cambarus Pristinus]] has an elongate, tuberculate chela resembling generalized [[Procambarus]]. Tubercle arrangement on the mesial palm varies within [[Cambarus Bouchardi]], sometimes resembling [[Subgenus Depressicambarus]] or [[Subgenus Puncticambarus]].
- **Annulus ventralis (female):** a sternal structure used for species separation. See [[Annulus Ventralis]].

The author notes that this variability does not weaken subgeneric concepts. Instead, it suggests the subgenera share a common ancestral stock.

**Subgenus emendation:** Because [[Cambarus Pristinus]] was previously isolated in a monotypic subgenus, and because [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] and [[Cambarus Obeyensis]] also have primitive first pleopods, the author redefines [[Subgenus Veticambarus]] to contain all three species. The type species is [[Cambarus Pristinus]]. The diagnosis includes eye size, areola proportions (1.8 to 4.4 times longer than wide, 30.0 to 38.6 percent of carapace length), and the male first pleopod structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**[[Cambarus Bouchardi]]** (type locality: [[Perkins Creek]], 6.9 miles north of [[Oneida Tennessee]], Scott County, Tennessee, on U.S. Highway 27):
- A tributary of the Big South Fork of the Cumberland River.
- Collected 6 April 1969 when the stream was flooded, with cloudy water and swift current over a rocky bed.
- Creek width 25 to 50 feet, maximum depth about two feet in seined areas.
- No aquatic plants. Hemlock (*Tsuga*), alder (*Alnus*), and rhododendron thickets lined the water line, with pine (*Pinus*) and oak (*Quercus*) on the banks.
- Known from three localities, including Roaring Paunch Creek (about 8.5 miles north of Oneida) and the same creek at County Route 2449.
- Color in life: mottled greenish tan with a banded abdomen. Specimens collected in April were encrusted with a black coating.

**[[Cambarus Cymatilis]]** (type locality: burrows near the western city limits of [[Chatsworth Georgia]], Murray County, Georgia, in a lawn and rose garden):
- The water table was only six inches below the surface, and specimens were easily extracted.
- Males dug simple burrows with one opening. Females built more complex burrows with at least two crude chimneys.
- Known from only three localities near Chatsworth, including a field adjacent to Holly Creek.
- Color: dark cobalt blue dorsally, which gives the species its name (from Latin *cymatilis*, "sea-colored, blue").

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic results**

- **[[Cambarus Bouchardi]] new species** is a primitive member of [[Subgenus Veticambarus]] that links [[Cambarus Pristinus]] with more advanced subgenera. It is named after discoverer [[Raymond W. Bouchard]].
- **[[Cambarus Cymatilis]] new species** belongs to [[Subgenus Depressicambarus]] and is distinguished by blue coloration, a narrow or nearly absent areola (41.7 to 44.8 percent of carapace length), a subterminal notch on the central projection, and a conical mesial process. Its closest relatives are *Cambarus striatus* and *Cambarus sphenoides*.
- The subgenus [[Subgenus Veticambarus]] was emended to include [[Cambarus Pristinus]], [[Cambarus Bouchardi]], and [[Cambarus Obeyensis]].

**Measurements and sizes**

| Measure | [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] | [[Cambarus Cymatilis]] |
|---|---|---|
| Holotype carapace length | 27.4 mm | 31.0 mm |
| Largest form I male | 37.5 mm | 33.3 mm |
| Largest female | 38.4 mm | 39.7 mm |
| Smallest form I male | 25.7 mm | 30.7 mm |
| Areola (% of carapace length) | 31.1 to 38.6 | 41.7 to 44.8 |

**Type specimens**

- [[Cambarus Bouchardi]]: holotype male (form I), allotype female, and morphotype male (form II) are at the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (nos. 130295, 130296, 130297). The paratypes include 48 form I males, 204 form II males, 319 females, and juveniles. Additional paratypes are held by [[Raymond W. Bouchard]].
- [[Cambarus Cymatilis]]: holotype male, allotype, and morphotype are at the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (nos. 129860, 129861, 129862). Paratypes total 26 form I males, 14 form II males, 42 females, and one juvenile female.

**Distribution**

- [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] is known from three localities in the Big South Fork drainage, all in Scott County, Tennessee.
- [[Cambarus Cymatilis]] is known from three localities near Chatsworth, Georgia.

**Life history observations**

- [[Cambarus Bouchardi]]: collected 26 May 1968, 17 and 22 September 1968, and 6 April 1969. First form males were present in September and April. No females carried eggs or young.
- [[Cambarus Cymatilis]]: all specimens collected in April. The sample included three first form males, two second form males, six females, and one juvenile female.

**Diagnostic and identification notes**

- Hobbs' key (1969) couplet 5 is difficult to apply to [[Cambarus Cymatilis]], because some specimens have eight tubercles in the mesial palm row. The author proposes a modified couplet to help identification.
- The annulus ventralis of [[Cambarus Cymatilis]] is deeply embedded and asymmetrical. In [[Cambarus Bouchardi]] it is shallowly embedded and nearly symmetrical.
