# Squawfish Spawning Habits in the Payette River System and a 1958 Carp Spawning Study

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1958 fisheries report documents the spawning habits, timing and movements of [[Squawfish]] (a predatory cyprinid described as a "rough fish") in [[Cascade Reservoir]], its tributaries and the nearby [[Payette Lake]] system in Idaho. It also summarizes a parallel study of [[Common Carp]] spawning at an unnamed Idaho lake. Field observations, temperature logs, gonad staging, size measurements and stomach checks were combined to test whether water temperature controlled where and when squawfish spawned. The report concludes that tributary water temperature was probably the decisive factor, that spawning was short-lived, and that control measures were feasible. The source is a 1958 field report, probably by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (inferred from the report's references to the Service's electrical trap).

## Background & Description

- **Objectives:** Determine the spawning habits of squawfish, particularly in Cascade Reservoir and the North Fork of the Payette River; locate spawning areas; and assess control options.
- **Methods:** Squawfish were collected by seine, gill net and hook and line, and some were taken by blasting. Gonads were staged as green (ripe), spawning or spawned-out. Surface and tributary water temperatures were logged daily, and sucker gonads were examined periodically. Fish were measured to the nearest half inch and weighed to the nearest quarter pound. Scale samples were taken for age determination.
- **Study area:** The report includes a map of the study area at a scale of roughly 6 miles to 1 inch. Key sites were the North Fork above Cascade Reservoir, Lake Fork, Gold Fork Creek, Payette Lake, Upper Payette Lake and Little Payette Lake.
- **Species present:** Besides squawfish, the streams and lakes held large numbers of [[Sucker Spawning|suckers]] (described as coarse-scale suckers), other "rough fish" and shiner fry.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature as the trigger:** Reservoir and tributary temperatures were the central variable. Squawfish spawned when tributary water was about 60 °F or above. Streams entering Upper and Little Payette lakes ran roughly 10 °F colder than those used by squawfish, and squawfish did not enter them to spawn.
- **Reservoir temperatures:** Cascade Reservoir reached 70 °F on June 5, fell to 59 °F on June 9 and rose to 69 °F on June 16 and 17. North Fork water ranged from 42 to 64 °F between June 13 and 24. Before that period it did not exceed 46 °F.
- **Carp at Lake Ipsall:** Carp spawned at water temperatures of 58 to 66 °F and moved offshore during cold, stormy weather. Their activity was tied to temperature and weather.
- **Sucker spawning:** Suckers spawned in the North Fork above Upper Payette Lake and in Lake Fork above Little Payette Lake at 46 to 56 °F. Their gravel nests were large. Ten to fifty suckers were seen on individual redds, and sucker fry stayed in the streams through summer and early fall.

## Key Findings

**Spawning timing and location**

- Spawning in the North Fork and Gold Fork was short. Most activity was over in about two to three weeks, after which adults returned to the lake.
- Large concentrations of squawfish were seen in boulder areas of the North Fork on June 17 and 18. The first spawned-out fish were captured on June 17.
- Squawfish spawned on the upstream side of cobble-bar and rock areas where a slight current was present. Spawning was most conspicuous at the Lake Fork dam, where fish spawned only on the left bank in the current.
- Squawfish spawning was also observed at the Ranger Falls bridge on June 21, where fish wedged their bodies between rocks and quivered while eggs adhered loosely to rocks in less than four feet of water.
- Spawning in Gold Fork Creek was observed up to July 9, when eggs were found in the gravel. Spawning there had ended by July 16.
- Little Payette Lake spawning occurred from before June 26 until July 11. Payette Lake showed no visible spawning, though aerial surveys were made on June 27 and 28.

**Management actions**

- Water flow in the North Fork was cut on June 19 when gates closed at the Cardo Dam. Squawfish concentrations were seen just below the dam.
- The North Fork was treated with [[Rotenone Treatment|rotenone]] on June 20 to eliminate spawning squawfish. Approximately a few thousand fish were killed, though the exact count is garbled in the scan.
- An [[Electrical Fish Trap]] operated by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] was placed across the North Fork arm on June 18. The trap appeared to block migration, possibly diverting fish to spawn elsewhere or too late.
- Control was deemed feasible. The report recommended a 1959 study with marked fish released above and below the trap.

**Biology**

- Squawfish fry appeared in the North Fork in July and were abundant by late September. The Payette Lake squawfish fry were found near the lake outlet.
- Mature squawfish ranged in size as follows. Females from Cascade Reservoir averaged 14.0 inches total length, with a maximum of about 21 inches. Males were slightly smaller, ranging from 10.1 to 15.6 inches and averaging 12.3 inches.
- Scale analysis indicated that most squawfish spawned at ages IV to VI (OCR-garbled).
- Stomach checks were qualitative only. Contents were noted as present or absent, and no quantitative analysis was attempted. Some fish regurgitated food when brought to the boat.
- Eggs taken from gravel and placed in live boxes hatched in less than 12 days at 50 to 65 °F. Artificial fertilization and hatching were unsuccessful.

**Carp study conclusions**

- Carp spawned from April 15 to May 19 at Lake Ipsall, with the peak of egg production around May 9 to 11. Dead and fungus-covered eggs were common in cold periods.
- Gonad development in carp was linked to temperature and weather. Most carp eggs were lost during cold spells.

**Overall conclusion**

- The report concluded that the squawfish spawning observed in 1958 was probably not an annual event. A reservoir spawning event had been noted in 1957 as well.
- Further studies were recommended to find lake-spawning areas for Upper and Little Payette lakes before controls could be finalized.
