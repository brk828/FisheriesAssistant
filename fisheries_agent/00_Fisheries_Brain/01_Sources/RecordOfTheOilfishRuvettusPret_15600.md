# Record of the Oilfish (Ruvettus pretiosus) in California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Gempylidae #deep-sea-fish #first-record

## Executive Summary

This paper documents the first known occurrence of the [[Oilfish]] (*Ruvettus pretiosus*) in the eastern Pacific Ocean. A single specimen measuring 475 mm standard length was caught off [[Encinitas]], [[San Diego County]], California on August 13, 1942. This represents only the second gempylid species recorded from California waters, following a 1932 record of *Xenogramma carinatum*. The specimen was likely captured on a set-line at depths between 50 and 400 fathoms and was brought to market with [[Rockfish]] (*Sebastodes*).

## Background & Description

The family [[Gempylidae]] (the "escolars") was previously known in California only from Myers' (1932) record of *Xenogramma carinatum* found cast up on the strand at [[Long Beach]]. The [[Oilfish]] (*Ruvettus pretiosus* Cocco) is best known from both sides of the North Atlantic but has also been recorded from various South Sea islands, the East Indies, Japan, Hawaii, and South Africa.

The specimen measures 475 mm in standard length and is preserved in the museum of the [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]]. The Pacific population has been referred to a distinct species (*Ruvettus tydemani* Weber) by some authors based primarily on lower numbers of dorsal and anal rays, though this distinction is not universally accepted. The authors provisionally recognize only one species and refer the California specimen to *R. pretiosus* pending revisionary study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Oilfish]] usually inhabits depths between 50 and 400 fathoms. The specimen was caught off [[Encinitas]] and brought to market with a catch of [[Rockfish]] (*Sebastodes*), suggesting it was taken on a set-line at considerable depth.

The flesh of the [[Oilfish]] is charged with an oil having strong purgative properties. *Xenogramma carinatum* is also reported to be an extremely oily fish. The authors note that these species may potentially become the object of commercial fishery if the oil proves valuable for medicinal or industrial uses and if the species are found to be common in the little-exploited deep-water basins off the coast of southern California.

## Key Findings

- First record of *Ruvettus pretiosus* from the eastern Pacific Ocean
- Specimen caught off [[Encinitas]], California on August 13, 1942
- Standard length: 475 mm
- Likely captured on set-line at depths of 50-400 fathoms
- Second gempylid species recorded from California waters
- Taxonomic uncertainty exists regarding Pacific vs. Atlantic populations
- Flesh contains oil with strong purgative properties
- Potential commercial value for oil extraction if species proves abundant in deep-water basins off southern California
