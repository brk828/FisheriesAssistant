# Effects of Supplemental Feeding and Controlled Fishing in Largemouth Bass-Bluegill Populations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This study by H. R. Schmittou, [[Auburn University]] Agricultural Experiment Station, examined how [[Supplemental Feeding]] and [[Controlled Fishing]] affect [[Largemouth Bass]]-[[Bluegill]] pond populations. It ran in two phases. Phase 1 compared one control pond (C) with two fed treatment ponds (T-1 and T-2) over 19 months. Phase 2 followed the fished treatment pond T-2 through 1964-1967.

Supplemental feeding increased bluegill weight and total fish production in the fed ponds, but did not change bluegill survival. Total production in the fed, fished pond T-2 over four years reached 2307 pounds per acre, including 2194 pounds of forage fish. Fishing success in T-2 averaged about 60 percent better than in [[Alabama State-Owned Lakes]] fertilized impoundments. An unexplained loss of bass caused an out-of-balance population during the fishing phase.

## Background & Description

**Study context.** The [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus* Rafinesque) was widely stocked in Southeastern ponds with [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides* Lacepede), following recommendations by [[Swingle]] and colleagues. Prior work cited in the document put the annual catch of this combination at under 50 pounds per acre in unfertilized ponds and 175 to 200 pounds per acre in fertilized ponds. Carrying capacity for fertilized Southeastern ponds was given as 300 to 500 pounds per acre, averaging about 400. The author's stated aim was to test whether supplemental feed could raise carrying capacity above fertilized-pond levels. Feeding had already been shown to raise channel catfish production in ponds.

**Study design.**
- **Ponds:** One 3.5-acre pond (S-9, designated T-2), one 1-acre pond (E-8, designated T-1), and one 1-acre pond (E-7, designated C, the control). Each pond averaged about 4 feet deep.
- **Stocking (per acre, March 1963):** Ponds C and T-1 received 1000 [[Fathead Minnow]] (approximately 3.0 pounds total) and 2000 bluegill averaging 1.7 inches and 0.005 pound each. Pond T-2 received 1143 fathead minnows and 2286 bluegill. All ponds received 150 one-inch largemouth bass fingerlings in April 1963.
- **Feeding:** [[Auburn No. 2 Pelleted Fish Feed]] was fed from March to October-November, once daily, six days per week. The rate rose monthly to 3% of estimated bluegill weight, capped at 10 pounds per acre per day in September.
- **Fertilizer:** [[Triple Superphosphate]] was applied to all ponds.
- **Sampling:** Bi-weekly seining of about 20 bluegill per pond, plus a 50-fish sample from the stocking lot. Fish were preserved in 10 percent formalin, measured, weighed, and sexed by dissection. Length-weight data were processed on an IBM 1620 using a program by [[Swingle]] (1964).

**Phase 2 (controlled fishing).** Carried out only in T-2 from 1964 to October 1967. The goal was to remove 10 pounds of bluegill and 3 pounds of bass per acre per week, but manpower limits meant fishing took place as staffing allowed. Public fishing was restricted to March through October each year.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Feeding limits.** Earlier Auburn studies cited in the document found that feed above 10 pounds per acre per day was not used by bluegill. Uneaten feed decomposed, depleted dissolved oxygen, and caused fish kills. Bluegill also did not use 10 pounds per acre per day well at water temperatures below 55°F, a period from November to March at Auburn.

**Population balance.** Balance was judged by seining with [[Population Balance]] analysis (Swingle, 1950). Control pond C became static (no reproduction) by the end of the first phase. In T-2, bass reproduction stopped after 1966, and no bass or bluegill reproduction was found in 1967. The author attributed the bass deficit to an unknown cause that was not linked to feeding or fishing pressure.

**Competing and incidental species.** Redear sunfish (*Lepomis microlophus*), green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*), brown bullhead (*Ictalurus nebulosus*), and golden shiner (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*) entered T-2 in early summer 1963. A mild [[Trichodina]] parasite epizootic occurred in T-2 in fall 1963, with insignificant mortality. The author suggested it may have lowered condition in that pond.

**Fish-kill risk.** Excess feed that accumulated and decomposed was identified as a risk to dissolved oxygen, which links feeding rate directly to pond water quality.

## Key Findings

**Growth and condition**
- Bluegill in all ponds grew at comparable rates for the first six months after stocking.
- Growth from supplemental feeding became evident in September of the first year. By the end of October, male and female bluegill in the control pond weighed significantly less (P=.05) than their counterparts in the treatment ponds.
- Condition indices began to differ in the fifth month (July, 1963), when bluegill reached the 4-inch group. The difference became visible in the samples in the seventh month (September).
- Control pond growth practically ceased 7 to 8 months after stocking, and originally stocked bluegill remained in the 6- and 7-inch groups. At draining in October 1964 (the second year), originally stocked bluegill averaged 0.33 pound in T-1 and 0.14 pound in C.
- The slightly lower condition of T-2 compared with T-1 was attributed to 286 more bluegill per acre stocked in T-2, plus the parasite epizootic.

**Survival and balance**
- At draining, 1694 bluegill (84.7%) were recovered from T-1 and 1744 (87.2%) from C, out of 2000 stocked per pond. Survival did not differ significantly (P=.05). The author concluded feeding had no effect on bluegill survival through 19 months.
- Spawning occurred in all ponds by May 18, with fry present from mid-May through September. The control pond was judged out of balance and static, with 63% of its bluegill harvestable (0.1 pound or larger).

- Fish production in T-1 exceeded the control by 320 pounds (74.6%). Bluegill production exceeded it by 308 pounds (82.4%). Overall, supplemental feeding raised total fish and bluegill production by about 75 to 80%.

**Feed conversion**
- Corrected conversion factors (C = F/W, with F = 2896 pounds of feed) were 9.2 for harvestable bluegill and 9.4 for total bluegill.
- The author estimated that withholding feed during the first-year expansion (until September) would have saved 550 pounds of feed and lowered those values to 7.4 and 7.6, without significantly affecting growth or total production.

**Carrying capacity**
- Standing crop was approximately equal to maximum productivity in the control pond and T-2, but below maximum in T-1.
- T-2 standing crop was 1129 pounds after 55 months. The 768-pound difference from the control pond's standing crop was taken as the approximate difference in carrying capacity between the fed and unfed populations.

**Controlled fishing phase (T-2, 1964-1967)**
- Bass stocking: 150 one-inch fingerlings per acre in April 1963, plus 100 two- to three-inch fingerlings on 25 May 1967 and 15 adult bass averaging 0.3 pound on 9 June 1967. A total of 265 bass per acre were stocked.
- Bass reproduced every year except 1967. Only 153 bass per acre were recovered during and at the end of the experiment. At draining, 33 bass weighing 34 pounds per acre were recovered (3% of total population weight, including the 1967 stockings).
- The largest annual bass removal was 59 bass weighing 32 pounds per acre in 1966. The annual average bass catch was 19 pounds per acre, compared with 29 pounds per acre for Alabama state lakes (Byrd and Crance, 1965).
- Population analysis for T-2 at draining (Table 2): largemouth bass 3.03% of total weight; bluegill 81.90%; brown bullhead 4.06%; golden shiner 11.01%. The F/C and Y/C ratios confirmed the out-of-balance condition, indicating bass deficiency rather than over-fishing.

- Bluegill made up 91.6% of the total catch over the four years. Bass made up 7.0%.
- Compared with the 14-year average for 20 Alabama state-owned fertilized lakes, catch per trip and total per-acre catch were about 60 percent greater in T-2. The reported comparison figures were: annual catch of 173 pounds (state lakes) versus 274 pounds (T-2), a difference of 63%. Forage fish: 144 versus 255 pounds (56%). Bass: 29 versus 19 pounds (66%).

**Total production in T-2 (4 years)**
- Total: 2307 pounds per acre, comprising 113 pounds of bass, 2007 pounds of bluegill, and 187 pounds of other forage species.
- Forage fish total: 2194 pounds, produced from 8766 pounds of feed.
- Conversion (S = 8766/2194): 4.0.
- Cost of forage fish production, including feed and fertilizer: $0.22 per pound (feed at 5.5 cents/lb, fertilizer at 3.3 cents/lb).
- Standing crop at draining: 1129 pounds.

**Author's summary points**
1. Growth rate was not effectively increased by supplemental feeding during the first six months.
2. Note on timing: the abstract and Summary item 2 place the significant weight difference at seven and eight months after stocking, respectively. The abstract says condition differences began in the fifth month; the Summary says the control-pond growth difference was significant at eight months.
3. The control population was static by draining at 19 months, while the two treatment populations were in balance.
4. Supplemental feeding had no effect on bluegill survival through 19 months.
5. Feeding is recommended to be adjusted seasonally: increase rates in March through May and September through November, decrease in summer, and skip feeding during initial expansion to save feed.
6. Unexplained bass loss in T-2 created the out-of-balance condition during the fishing phase.
7. Predation was replaced by supplemental feeding in the fished population, which continued to expand despite a high F/C ratio.
