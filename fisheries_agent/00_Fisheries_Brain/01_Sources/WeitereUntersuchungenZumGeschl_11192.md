# Weitere Untersuchungen zum Geschlechtsdimorphismus bei *Cynolebias bellotti*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #Rivulidae #annual-fish #sexual-dimorphism

## Executive Summary

This study investigates the pronounced sexual dimorphism in meristic characters (dorsal and anal fin ray counts) in the annual killifish [[Cynolebias bellotti]], a species historically split into two nominal taxa (*C. bellotti* and *C. maculatus*) by [[Steindachner]] due to differences between males and females. Building on prior work ([[Peters 1964]]), the author establishes robust ray-count ranges from a large, environmentally-controlled brood stock and then performs hormone administration experiments (androgen: methyltestosterone; estrogen: estradiol) to test whether this meristic dimorphism is under direct gonadal hormonal control, as is typical for many other secondary sexual characteristics. The key finding is that fin-ray count dimorphism emerges far earlier in development than gonadal hormone-dependent traits (such as adult coloration) and is **not altered by exogenous sex hormone treatment**, strongly suggesting that this dimorphism is controlled by **sex-linked genetic factors** rather than by gonadal hormone secretion. Additionally, dorsal and anal fin ray counts are positively correlated within individuals of both sexes, implying a shared developmental/genetic basis for both fins.

## Background & Description

- **Species in question:** *Cynolebias bellotti* Steindachner (Cyprinodontidae), an oviparous annual killifish from periodically desiccating South American waters.
- **Sexually dimorphic traits:**
  - Number of rays in the **dorsal fin (D)** and **anal fin (A)**
  - Body coloration (males develop distinct nuptial coloration; females retain juvenile pattern)
  - Body size and body shape (as a consequence of unequal dorsal/anal fin size)
- **Historical/comparative ray counts:**

- **Vertebral column:** Vertebral number is **identical between sexes** and shows no dependency relationship with fin ray count; dorsal and anal fins begin at the same vertebral level in both sexes, but extend over a greater number of vertebrae in males.
- **Growth:** Under crowded rearing conditions, mean total length was reduced (~28 mm) compared to more favorable conditions (40–50 mm), though sexual maturation timing (6–8 weeks) was unaffected.
- **Correlation between fins:** Dorsal and anal ray counts are positively correlated within individuals of both sexes (established quantitatively in [[Peters 1964]]), suggesting a shared developmental mechanism and common genetic control for both fins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *C. bellotti* is an **annual fish** inhabiting temporary, periodically desiccating freshwater pools in South America (Argentina), a lifestyle requiring rapid embryonic development and diapause adaptations (see [[Peters 1963]] on embryonic adaptations of oviparous killifish in drying waters).
- **Life history:** Rapid growth and early sexual maturation (6–8 weeks) is an adaptation to short-lived aquatic habitats. Fin ray differentiation is complete by ~7 weeks, coincident with gonadal maturation and onset of breeding behavior/pairing.
- **Developmental timeline:**
  - Day 10: gonads already histologically distinguishable as testes or ovaries.
  - Day 14 and younger: fin ray sexual dimorphism already detectable.
  - Week 5–8: males acquire nuptial coloration (accelerated to ~5 weeks under methyltestosterone treatment).
  - Week 6–8: sexual maturity, pairing behavior begins.
  - Week 7–10: full/definitive fin ray counts established in both sexes.
- **Environmental sensitivity:** Meristic characters are known to be influenced by embryonic/larval rearing temperature and other environmental factors, necessitating uniform rearing conditions (same tank, same brood) for valid comparative analysis — a key methodological control emphasized from [[Peters 1964]].

## Key Findings

1. **Robust sexual dimorphism confirmed:** Males consistently show statistically significantly higher dorsal and anal fin ray counts than females (Control: D — ♂ 22.43 vs ♀ 18.27, P<0.001; A — ♂ 28.36 vs ♀ 26.07, P<0.001; Tab. 4).

2. **Early developmental onset:** The ray-count dimorphism is detectable by 14 days of age or earlier — far in advance of gonadal maturation (6–8 weeks), onset of male nuptial coloration, and reproductive activity. Males simply add fin rays at a faster rate per unit time, not over a longer developmental period (both sexes reach final ray counts by ~7 weeks).

3. **Hormone experiments (Methyltestosterone & Estradiol treatment):**
   - Weekly 24-hour immersion in hormone solution (1 mg/L methyltestosterone or 5 mg/L estradiol) from age 7 days through 8 applications.
   - Hormone treatment was physiologically effective: methyltestosterone induced full male coloration in all treated fish (including genetic females) by ~5 weeks; estradiol suppressed male coloration entirely, keeping all fish in juvenile/female color pattern.
   - **Despite effective hormonal action on coloration, fin ray counts were unaffected:**

| Treatment | D x̄ | A x̄ | P vs. control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control (♂+♀) | 19.92 | 26.98 | — |
| Methyltestosterone | 19.51 | 26.38 | D: ~0.25; A: ~0.04 (not significant/borderline) |
| Estradiol | 19.22 | 26.95 | D: 0.18; A: >0.90 (not significant) |

   - Frequency distributions for hormone-treated groups remained bimodal, matching control distribution shape rather than shifting unimodally toward higher (androgen) or lower (estrogen) values as would be expected if hormones directly controlled ray number.

4. **Conclusion — genetic rather than hormonal control:** Because (a) the dimorphism precedes hormone-dependent secondary sexual characters developmentally, and (b) direct exogenous hormone administration fails to shift ray counts, the author concludes that fin ray count dimorphism in *C. bellotti* is **not gonadal-hormone-dependent** but is instead governed by **sex-linked genetic factors** (genes linked to the sex-determining chromosomes/gonosomes), analogous to known sex-linked color pattern inheritance in other killifish (XY/ZW-type systems).

5. **Shared genetic/developmental basis for D and A fins:** Positive correlation between dorsal and anal ray counts within individuals across both sexes indicates a single common developmental mechanism and shared genetic control underlying ray number in both fins, rather than independent control systems.

6. **Taxonomic implication:** These results reinforce that *C. bellotti* and *C. maculatus* (Steindachner) represent male and female morphs of a single species, not distinct taxa — the meristic "species-level" differences originally described are in fact sex-linked intraspecific variation.
