# Stocking of Endangered Bonytail Chub into Selected Ponds on the Buenos Aires NWR

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) correspondence, a memorandum with a related reply, concerning an interim plan to stock endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] into selected ponds on the [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]]. The proposal stocks about 1,000 fish per surface acre into six ponds. Three ponds are currently fish-free, and three contain exotic fish that would be removed with [[Fintrol]], an FWS-approved fish toxicant. The chubs would come from the spawning ponds at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], where an estimated 10,000 to 12,000 chubs are expected to be produced. The document is brief and addresses a specific, time-sensitive problem rather than a full research study.

## Background & Description

- **Species of concern:** The bonytail chub is described as perhaps the most endangered of all the fish in the region, and it is listed as endangered.
- **Origin of the fish:** The hatchery's pond production for the year is the offspring of uncontrolled natural spawning in ponds where broodfish were being held. The author states that the fish are valuable and should not be destroyed.
- **Hatchery situation:** The Dexter ponds were to be drained within a few weeks, which created urgency for placement of the fish.
- **Exotic fish removal:** Removal of exotic fish from three ponds would be carried out by Dr. W. L. [[Minckley]] and his graduate students from [[Arizona State University]].
- **Reply context:** The second memorandum is addressed from a zone supervisor in Arizona and New Mexico to an assistant chief of the Endangered Species program, and it is dated around September 1986 based on the receipt stamp.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use:** The proposal uses refuge impoundments as interim rearing and holding habitat for the endangered species.
- **Exotic species management:** Three ponds contain exotic fish, which would be eradicated with a toxicant before stocking. This indicates that non-native fish were a competing or threatening presence in the refuge ponds.
- **Uncontrolled reproduction:** Chubs reproduced in ponds used for broodfish holding, which the document describes as an unplanned source of the year's production.
- **Long-term potential:** The memo notes that the refuge could be used for rearing and holding endangered fish species over the long term, to be considered in the master planning process.

## Key Findings

- **Interim stocking plan:** Stock roughly 1,000 bonytail chub per surface acre into six ponds on the Buenos Aires NWR.
- **Pond selection:** Three ponds currently have no fish, and three contain exotic fish that would be removed first.
- **Exotic removal method:** Use Fintrol, an FWS-approved fish toxicant, applied by Dr. Minckley and his students.
- **Source population:** Expected output of 10,000 to 12,000 chubs from the Dexter NFH ponds, which are being drained.
- **Urgency:** The plan is an interim action with a short timeline. The author requests early approval because the fish cannot wait.
- **Future planning:** The refuge may serve as a long-term rearing and holding site for endangered fish, to be evaluated during master planning.
- **Official response:** The second document is a reply from FWS Arizona and New Mexico officials to the endangered species program. Its specific decision is not clearly legible in the scanned text.
