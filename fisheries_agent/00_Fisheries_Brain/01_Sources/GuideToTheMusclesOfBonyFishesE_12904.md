## Executive Summary

This paper is a practical dissection guide describing the gross musculature of generalized bony fishes ([[Teleostei]]), intended for use in vertebrate anatomy instruction. Rather than focusing on a single species, the author (Branley Allan Branson) synthesizes comparative myology across common freshwater fishes (emphasizing [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]]) to provide a systematic guide to the muscles of the [[Pectoral Girdle]], median fins, trunk, [[Caudal Fin]], [[Pelvic Fin]]s, eye, and head (branchial, hyoidal, and mandibular musculature). The work is explicitly framed as a companion to osteological studies (Branson 1962; Nelson 1963) and serves as a functional atlas for understanding fish locomotion and feeding mechanics.

## Background & Description

### Pectoral Girdle Musculature
- **Dorsal Mass** (common abductors/extensors): Originates from the upright part of the [[Cleithrum]]; inserts on basal pterygiophores or bifurcated proximal fin ray ends.
- **Ventral Mass** (common adductors/flexors): Has a double origin—outer and inner halves of the cleithrum. Some fibers pass through an opening between the cleithrum, [[Coracoid]], and scapula to insert on basals and rays.
- **Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Fibers**: Short intrinsic fibers originate from basals and extend to ray bases, enabling independent ray movement; the bulk of the muscle mass is extrinsic, anchoring the fin to the body.

### Median Fin Musculature (Anal & Dorsal Fins)
- Each spine/ray has three paired muscles:
  - **Depressor**: Originates from the anterolateral face of the next posterior basal pterygiophore; inserts on the posterior ray base projection.
  - **Erector**: Originates from connective tissue between basals or the anterior end of the next posterior basal; inserts on the anterior ray base projection.
  - **Inclinator**: Inserts near the middle of the ray base; origin varies (connective tissue or erector/depressor surface in [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]]).
- **Infracarinales**: Paired muscles running from pelvic basals to the anal fin basals.
- **Supracarinales**: Paired, darker-pigmented muscles arising from the supraoccipital, inserting on dorsal fin basals.

### Trunk Musculature
- Body organized into zig-zag **myotomes** (parietal muscles) separated by **myosepta**.
- Divided into **epaxial** (dorsal) and **hypaxial** (ventral) masses by the **horizontal skeletogenous septum** (housing the *lateralis vagii* nerve branch).
- Epaxial muscles: two dorsal longitudinal bundles.
- Hypaxial muscles: three bundles — a darker **lateral longitudinal bundle** just below the septum, and two **ventral longitudinal bundles**.
- **Linea Alba**: Midventral connective tissue septum preventing myotome crossover between body sides.

### Caudal Fin Musculature
- Epaxial/hypaxial fibers converge caudally, inserting on connective tissue sheets (wide anteriorly, narrow posteriorly) attaching to caudal basals.
- Smaller muscles may extend from hypural elements to fin rays, enhancing mobility.

### Pelvic Fin Musculature
- Roughly triangular, apex directed anteriorly; four major bundles (two dorsal extensors, two ventral flexors), paired and separated by the linea alba.
- Fibers originate from linea alba or pelvic girdle; insert on basals. Small accessory muscles run from basals to ray bases (myotomic origin).

### Ocular Musculature
- **Six extraocular muscles**: superior obliquus, inferior obliquus, superior rectus, inferior rectus, anterior rectus, posterior rectus.
- **Innervation**:
  - Cranial Nerve III (oculomotor): internal, superior, inferior recti + inferior obliquus.
  - Cranial Nerve IV (trochlear): superior oblique.
  - Cranial Nerve VI (abducens): external (posterior) rectus.

### Head Musculature

**A. Dorsal Branchial Muscles**
- Five *levatores arcuum branchialia* (2 internal, 3 external) originate near the pterotic/hyomandibular articulation, inserting variably on pharyngobranchials, epibranchials, and the fifth ceratobranchial.
- *Retractores dorsales arcuum branchialia*: Paired, ribbon-like, arise from first two vertebrae, insert on 4th/5th epibranchial.
- *Epiarcualia recti* (interarcuales dorsales): Connect 3rd/4th pharyngobranchial to 4th ceratobranchial.
- *Subarcualia obliqui* present ventrally; oblique epiarcualia and ventral recti absent.
- *Epiarcualium transversum* (anterior and posterior): Thin sheets connecting infrabranchial bones via a median aponeurosis.
- *Abductoria arcualia branchialia*: Five short, stout muscles connecting ceratobranchials to epibranchials.

**B. Ventral Branchial Muscles**
- *Coracobranchialis internus*: From 5th ceratobranchial to dorsal coracoid edge.
- *Coracobranchialis externus*: Opposing muscle, dorsolateral coracoid to 5th ceratobranchial.
- *Pharyngohyoideus*: Paired, from urohyal to 5th ceratobranchial.
- *Genio-hyoideus*: Notably restricted to **elasmobranchs**, *[[Amia]]*, *[[Lepisosteus]]*, *[[Polypterus]]*, and *[[Calamichthys]]* — absent in typical teleosts.
- *Coracohyoideus*: Massive paired muscle from coracoid to hypohyals, surrounding the urohyal.

**Hyoidal & Mandibular Constrictors**
- *Submentalis*: Connects dentaries across the jaw symphysis.
- *Protractor hyoidei*: Ceratohyal to dentary/branchiostegal rays.
- *Pars abductores/adductores branchiostegalium*: Control opening/closing of the branchiostegal membrane.
- *Pars dorsalis*: Connects last branchiostegal ray to opercle fascia.

**Hyoidal Muscles (Non-constrictor)**
1. Adductor arcus palatini
2. Adductor hyomandibulae (often fused with #1 in higher fishes)
3. Adductor operculi — large muscle from pterotic/opisthotic to inner operculum.
4. Levator operculi — dorsal continuation of pars dorsalis of hyo-hyoideus; opercle elevation.

**Mandibular Muscles (Non-constrictor)**
- *Levator arcus palatini*: Orbit margin/prootic to metapterygoid.
- *Dilator operculi*: From the dilator fossa (frontal, pterotic, sphenotic) to preopercle tip.
- **Mandibular Adductors** (complex, two sets):
  - *Temporo-masseteric muscle* (pars anterior + pars posterior): Involves the **maxillo-labial fascia** and **ligamentum mandibulo-maxillare posterius/anterius**, critical for jaw protrusion/retraction mechanics.
  - *Pterygoideus*: Quadrate/pterotic to the articular bone via a bipartite tendon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The guide is explicitly oriented toward "common stream and pond fishes" — freshwater taxa accessible for classroom dissection (e.g., [[Cyprinidae]], [[Catostomidae]]).
- **Functional Ecology**: The muscular arrangements described underpin critical ecological functions:
  - Fine control of median and paired fin rays supports maneuverability in flowing or still freshwater habitats.
  - The branchiostegal and opercular musculature (abductors/adductors) governs respiration and buccal pumping.
  - The complex mandibular adductor and maxillo-labial fascia system suggests specialized **protrusible jaw mechanics**, relevant to benthic feeding strategies seen in suckers ([[Catostomidae]]) and minnows.
  - The taxon-restricted *genio-hyoideus* muscle (present in [[Elasmobranchii]], [[Amia]], [[Lepisosteus]], [[Polypterus]], [[Calamichthys]]) highlights a phylogenetically conserved trait lost in derived teleosts, relevant to evolutionary functional morphology.

## Key Findings

- Fish musculature is organized into a highly modular system of paired extrinsic (body-anchoring) and intrinsic (fine ray-control) muscles across all fins, allowing independent ray movement — critical for fine locomotory control.
- The trunk's epaxial/hypaxial division via the horizontal skeletogenous septum, and its further subdivision into longitudinal bundles, forms the fundamental swimming musculature (myotomal system) common to all bony fishes.
- Branchial (gill arch) musculature is extremely complex, involving at least five levator muscles, transverse and rectus epiarcualia, and multiple abductor muscles — reflecting the mechanical demands of ventilation and food processing.
- The jaw adductor system (temporo-masseteric complex + pterygoideus) is tightly linked to specialized connective tissue structures (maxillo-labial fascia, mandibulo-maxillare ligaments) that enable **upper jaw protrusion**, a key innovation in teleost feeding ecology.
- The presence/absence of the *genio-hyoideus* muscle serves as a distinguishing anatomical marker between basal (non-teleost) actinopterygians/elasmobranchs and higher teleosts.
- This guide complements prior osteological work (Branson 1962; Nelson 1963) and cites foundational comparative anatomy literature (Edgeworth 1928; Kesteven 1943; Tchernavin 1948), situating it within a broader tradition of cranial and postcranial musculoskeletal research in [[Actinopterygii]].
