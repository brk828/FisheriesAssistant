# Training Local Citizens in Wetland Management: Cuatro Cienegas Reserve, Coahuila, Mexico

#conservation #wetland-management #desert-aquatic-ecosystems #endemic-species #biodiversity #international-cooperation

## Executive Summary

This grant proposal, submitted jointly by the municipal government of [[Cuatro Cienegas]], [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), and the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC), requests $48,031 USD for a one-year training program in wetland management. The [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] in Coahuila, Mexico contains approximately 150,000 hectares of recently designated biological reserve featuring exceptional aquatic biodiversity, including 15 fish species (12 endemic), four endemic reptiles, six crustaceans, at least 10 molluscs, and over 20 endemic plants. The project aims to train four local citizens selected by the municipality through site visits to established conservation facilities in the western United States, attendance at scientific meetings, and a culminating workshop in [[Cuatro Cienegas]]. The program will provide hands-on experience in wetland management, establish computer and internet communication capabilities, and develop educational materials for local conservation efforts. This initiative implements an Agreement of Mutual Assistance (AMA) between the municipal government and DFC, representing a "bottom-up" approach to conservation through local capacity building.

## Background & Description

The [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] is an intermontane desert basin in Coahuila, Mexico, characterized by remarkable aquatic habitat diversity despite its arid setting. The basin contains at least 80 discrete springs flowing >4.0 L·sec⁻¹, with some discharges exceeding 1.0 m³·sec⁻¹. These springs feed through convoluted channels to form marshlands, saline lakes, and salt deposits on the valley floor. The surrounding limestone mountains rise >2500 m above sea level, supporting vegetation zones from bunchgrass/saltgrass associations through Chihuahuan desertscrub to coniferous forests.

The basin's biological significance was first recognized during 1930s surveys for a possible Sierra del Carmen-Chisos Mountains International Park. More than 50 taxa are endemic to the basin, including cacti, vascular plants, invertebrates (snails, crustaceans, scorpions), fishes, and reptiles. The area supports 64 amphibian and reptile species, representing >37% of the 170 species comprising the entire [[Chihuahuan Desert]] herpetofauna, within <1000 m elevation.

Comparative analysis demonstrates [[Cuatro Cienegas]]' exceptional biodiversity concentration. [[Ash Meadows]], USA had five endemic fishes (one extinct), nine molluscs, nine insects, and many vascular plants. [[Dalhousie Springs]], Australia has six fishes (four endemic), six molluscs, numerous crustaceans and a frog. [[Cuatro Cienegas]] supports 15 fishes (12 endemic), four endemic reptiles, six crustaceans, at least 10 molluscs, plus >20 endemic plants and numerous insects and scorpions.

A group of concerned citizens and municipal public servants successfully petitioned the Mexican Federal Government to preserve the basin's biodiversity, resulting in designation of ~150,000 hectares as a biological reserve including substantial wetland areas. The citizen group negotiated an Agreement of Mutual Assistance with the [[Desert Fishes Council]] to continue as active partners in reserve management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]] represents a critical concentration of desert aquatic biodiversity with global significance. The basin's ecological stability and presence of reliable water through the Holocene allowed endemic taxa to evolve and relict taxa to persist. Local habitat diversity promoted concentration of common and ubiquitous taxa, yielding remarkable biodiversity in a relatively small geographic area (only ~30 by 40 km).

The basin's aquatic ecosystems face documented degradation. Multiple Mexican scientists have expressed alarm at negative changes detected in the area. The [[Chihuahuan Desert]] and [[Sonoran Desert]], spanning the USA-Mexico border, support Earth's largest indigenous aquatic biota persisting under arid-land conditions, making the region globally important for understanding desert aquatic ecosystem patterns and processes.

Environmental context includes three conservation priority levels:

**Local**: Mexican conservationists require information to base arguments, proposals, and actions. Foreign expertise should support both "top-down" program development through agencies and "bottom-up" capacity building through education and technical support.

**Regional**: The [[North American Free Trade Agreement]] (NAFTA) provides mechanisms for promoting sustainable development with environmental safeguards. An informed citizenry is mandatory for protecting environments from unsustainable industrialization, pollution, and resource destruction that could spread from borderlands into Mexico's interior.

**Global**: A quarter of Earth's land surface is desert or semi-desert, supporting increasing human populations. Desertification trends are accelerating. Projected global warming will increase water limitation and subject now-mesic environments to desert influences. Understanding isolation, survival, and extinction patterns in existing deserts has future applicability to other regions.

## Key Findings

**Project Structure and Objectives:**

The proposal outlines four specific objectives for the one-year program:

1. **Site Visits and Professional Contacts**: Mexican participants will conduct two 8-12 day visits (months 4 and 7) to operating preserves, refuges, and conservation units in western USA, including:
   - [[Death Valley National Park]], [[Owens Valley Refuge]], [[Ash Meadows]], [[Moapa]], [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]
   - [[The Nature Conservancy]] sites: Canelo Cienega, Aravaipa, Ramsey Canyons
   - [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center
   - [[Bitter Lakes National Wildlife Refuge]], [[Bottomless Lakes State Park]], [[Balmorhea State Park]], [[Big Bend National Park]]
   - Research facilities at ASU and University of Nevada, Las Vegas

2. **Training and Experience**: Four participants selected by the municipal government (one from municipal government, one from local educational institution, two laypersons) will receive hands-on management training and decision-making expertise enhancement.

3. **Regional Conservation Focus**: Emphasis on aquatic/semi-aquatic biota conservation as part of larger USA-Mexican borderlands program, including attendance at [[Society for Conservation Biology]] annual meeting (Ft. Collins, CO) and [[Desert Fishes Council]] meeting (Reno, NV) in 1995.

4. **Information and Education Capabilities**: Establishment of computer hardware/software for electronic communication, compilation of pertinent literature, and development of conservation education materials.

**Workshop and Synthesis:**

Month 9 includes a 12-day USA team visit to [[Cuatro Cienegas]] featuring a three-day workshop addressing:
- Comparisons between USA conservation units and [[Cuatro Cienegas]] conditions
- Existing and projected biological and non-biological problems (economic, political)
- Strategies for problem remediation through comparative information, monitoring techniques, data acquisition/analysis, and risk assessment
- Discussion of aquatic/semi-aquatic habitats and biodiversity status in USA-Mexico borderlands

**Historical Support and Documentation:**

The proposal documents extensive scientific and institutional support for [[Cuatro Cienegas]] conservation spanning decades. Early Mexican reports emphasized uniqueness and diversity, with later publications expressing alarm at habitat degradation. The US National Park Service sponsored a major Chihuahuan Desert conservation symposium in 1974, and USFWS/DFC sponsored a [[Cuatro Cienegas]]-specific symposium in 1983. DFC published assessments of imperiled aquatic ecosystems and major works on desert fish management featuring [[Cuatro Cienegas]] prominently.

Some basin sections were set aside as early as 1983, with governmental hearings and reports recognizing biodiversity. [[Cuatro Cienegas]] organisms began appearing on official lists of imperiled species. The municipal government and concerned citizens organized a well-documented proposal leading to reserve designation and the Agreement of Mutual Assistance with DFC.

Monthly telephone consultations among project officers and contracting officer will ensure continuity. Written evaluations will be requested from all participants, administrators, managers, and staff of visited facilities. Interim reports are scheduled at month 6, with a comprehensive final report synthesizing workshop results. Contingent on future funding, results will be presented at the 1996 DFC annual meeting in La Paz, Baja California Sur, Mexico.
