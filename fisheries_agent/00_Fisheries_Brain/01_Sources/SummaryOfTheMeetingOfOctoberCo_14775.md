# Virgin River Rotenone Fish Kill: Meeting Summary (3 November 1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This paraphrased meeting summary documents a post-incident review of a [[Rotenone]] renovation (piscicide) operation on the [[Virgin River]] in which a fish kill occurred well beyond the planned treatment reach. Rotenone was not neutralized in time by the [[Detox Station]] because it was set up roughly 24 hours late and the rotenone travelled farther than calculated. Participants concluded that a miscalculation of detoxification, rather than the initial rotenone slug alone, likely caused the long-term die-off of native fishes. The meeting also set out the plan for biological recovery, including sampling before a second treatment, backup populations of [[Woundfin]] and Gila, and a chronology of events. The source notes that tape quality was poor and that the summary is not an exact transcript.

## Background & Description

- **Operation:** A rotenone renovation of the Virgin River, performed in part to remove non-native fishes ahead of native fish recovery. The project was on a limited budget, and [[Potassium Permanganate]] was used as the detoxicant.
- **Alternative considered:** [[Antimycin]] was considered but rejected as too expensive. Don Archer also reportedly judged it not useful in hard water such as the Virgin River.
- **Staffing:** No fisheries staff from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service were present at the operation. Participating agencies included the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Utah Wildlife Resources, the University of Nevada, Las Vegas, Arizona State University (ASU), and the U.S. Forest Service. Radio contact was maintained among participants throughout.
- **Pre-treatment salvage (August):** Approximately 5600 woundfin were salvaged: 1600 to [[Dexter Hatchery]], 4000 returned to the Virgin River at Littlefield, and about 1200 Gila were held at a facility above the [[Washington Fields Diversion]].
- **Timeline notes:** Initial dead fish were reported at [[Little Round Valley]] on Wednesday, 5 October, at 6:00 am and at and below the barrier around 10:30 am. A Nevada Conservation Officer found no dead fish upriver from Lake Mead on Sunday, 9 October.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow and carriage:** The river carried rotenone farther downstream than anticipated. Additional sprayers and marsh spraying may have increased concentrations. Spraying in the marsh was reportedly accounted for in calculations.
- **Detoxification design:** Detox calculations were based on 3 ppm rotenone. Participants noted that the actual amount reaching the detox station was likely greater than 3 ppm.
- **Downstream extent:** Estimates ranged from [[Mesquite]] to [[Riverside]] diversions, with one participant suggesting the first operation may have killed fish as far as about 20 miles downstream, versus the six miles assumed by supervisors. No dead fish were reported at Riverside.
- **Survivors:** Red shiners (a non-native species) survived downstream and were most abundant in collections where SS participated. Native fishes were few below the I-15 bridge during the second operation.
- **Conflicting data:** Hickman's data suggested a good Gila population in the lower reach, while pre-poisoning electroshocking during salvage found very low Gila numbers.
- **Potassium permanganate:** Considered far less toxic than rotenone and short-lived. Overdosing could kill fish, but over a short distance only.
- **Marsh hydrology:** Manipulating marsh water levels to hold rotenone concentrations was judged very difficult because of the complex channel network.

## Key Findings

1. **Detox timing failure:** The detox station was scheduled for Tuesday, 4:00 pm, but was not operating until Wednesday, about 1:00 pm (recorded by Sally Stefferud as 3:00 pm). This was roughly 24 hours later than planned.
2. **Miscalculated carriage:** The rotenone carried farther than expected. Participants believed the long-term fish kill resulted from detoxification miscalculation rather than the initial slug alone. Denise Knight and William L. Minckley, however, indicated the undetoxified rotenone likely caused most of the damage.
3. **Unverified success:** Denise Knight stated the treatment was "very successful," but no attempts had been made to verify this.
4. **Undetermined damage:** No quantitative assessment of the kill or remaining fishes had been made. Damage could not be determined without sampling.
5. **Planning deficiencies:** Participants (Denver office and [[Dave Hales]]) cited poor communication, last-minute equipment and manpower requests, and insufficient planning. The major treatment was delayed by barrier construction, where bedrock was deeper than expected.
6. **Pre-treatment sampling:** Riverside, Mesquite, Big Bend, and Beaverdam Wash are standard [[Woundfin Recovery Team]] monitoring sites. Sampling above and below the treatment area was agreed to occur a couple of days before the second treatment, and Atkinville Wash and sites above the Washington Fields Diversion were also recommended.
7. **Genetic and community data:** Minckley recommended immediate collection of detailed community and genetic data, before reproduction in the main channel. Main-channel genetic sampling must precede the second poisoning, with tributary sampling possibly later. Woundfin populations below the Narrows may have differed genetically from those above.
8. **Backup populations:** Woundfin should be held at a facility such as Dexter (not stocked now) to allow recolonization from upstream, and a sample of Gila should be placed in a secure facility. Bob Ruesink committed AGFD office resources to backup population and restoration planning.
9. **Detoxification rule:** Participants recommended detoxifying for the maximum possible rotenone dosage applied, assuming no breakdown, because potassium permanganate is inexpensive and its effects are limited to a few miles.
10. **Chronology and documentation:** Denise Knight and Don Archer were to assemble a chronology of events within about two hours, and Bob Ruesink's and Don Archer's notes were to be made available. The Desert Fishes Council will require an accounting, possibly presented at the November meeting and published in its proceedings.
11. **Mechanism for information sharing:** The [[Woundfin Recovery Team]] was designated to circulate information.
12. **Unresolved issues:** Whether the chub listing had delayed dam/barrier work was to be checked. The question of what caused the detox team's absence on Tuesday afternoon was raised but was discussed off the record.
