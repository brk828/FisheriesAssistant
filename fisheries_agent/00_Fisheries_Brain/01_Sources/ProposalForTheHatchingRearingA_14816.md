## Executive Summary
This document is a management/recovery proposal—not a primary morphological or ecological study—concerning the threatened [[Arizona Trout]] (*Salmo apache*, now recognized as *Oncorhynchus apache*, the [[Apache Trout]]). Submitted by the Arizona Trout Recovery Team and hatchery personnel, the proposal outlines a plan to collect fertile eggs from the type locality on the East Fork of the White River (Fort Apache Indian Reservation) and transport them to [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]] for hatching, rearing, and controlled hybridization experiments with [[Rainbow Trout]] (RBT). The stated goals are species recovery, taxonomic classification support, brood stock development, and stream/impoundment restocking for conservation ("renovation") efforts on National Forest lands.

## Background & Description
The source text contains **no direct morphological data** (e.g., meristics, coloration, body proportions) for *Salmo apache*. It references only:
- **Hybrid classification**: Offspring from AZT × RBT crosses were intended for use by Dr. John Rinne (U.S. Forest Service, Rocky Mountain Forest and Range Experiment Station) to aid in **classifying present Arizona trout populations**, implying morphological/genetic differentiation between pure [[Arizona Trout]] populations and hybridized or introgressed populations was an active taxonomic concern at the time.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Type locality / native range**: East Fork of the [[White River (Arizona)]], within the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] — the original description locality for *Salmo apache*.
- **Habitat type**: Cold, clear headwater/mountain stream habitat typical of high-elevation salmonid ranges in the White Mountains of Arizona.
- **Spawning timing**: Arizona trout in the East Fork were expected to spawn between **mid-May and mid-June (1983)**, indicating a spring spawning phenology consistent with other inland trout species.
- **Sympatric/interacting species**: [[Rainbow Trout]] — used both as a disease-free sperm source for hybridization experiments and as a known hybridization threat/factor relevant to the species' threatened status (hybridization with introduced rainbow trout is a recognized conservation concern for native western trout).
- **Conservation status driver**: The species was formally listed as **threatened**, prompting active recovery intervention including egg collection, captive propagation, and stocking of restored streams and impoundments on National Forest land.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory/Conservation Framework**: The proposal was a coordinated, multi-agency effort involving the [[Arizona Trout Recovery Team]], the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] (via the White Mountain Recreation Enterprise), the [[Alchesay-Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]], and the U.S. Forest Service's [[Rocky Mountain Forest and Range Experiment Station]].
- **Egg Sourcing**: Fertile eggs were to be collected directly from the East Fork type locality population — a genetically significant, wild source population.
- **Hybridization Design**: A portion of eggs would be fertilized with disease-free [[Rainbow Trout]] sperm (creating AZT × RBT hybrids) explicitly for taxonomic/classification research, while the remainder would be fertilized with milt from East Fork males to preserve pure lineage.
- **Brood Stock Strategy**: Both pure and hybrid individuals were to be retained at the hatchery as brood stock to support long-term hybridization research and domestication efforts.
- **Restoration Application**: Fry and fingerlings produced at Williams Creek were earmarked for stocking ("renovation") of National Forest streams and impoundments, directly supporting wild population recovery.
- **Disease Management**: Rigorous biosecurity protocols were followed — three consecutive years of disease inspection at the source population, an additional pre-spawning inspection, and disinfection of all collected eggs — reflecting hatchery best practices for protecting sensitive/threatened stocks.
- **Project Scope & Duration**: The initiative was time-limited, scheduled to terminate on **September 30, 1986** absent formal extension, and was designed to have no negative impact on existing hatchery production operations.
- **Taxonomic Research Linkage**: A direct institutional link was established between hatchery propagation work and academic/agency taxonomic research (Dr. John Rinne), using hybrid offspring as a tool to better understand and classify wild Arizona trout populations, some of which may have already experienced natural introgression with introduced rainbow trout.
