# Population Structure and Reproductive Success of Woundfin in the Virgin River, 1977–1980

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1980 report by [[Deacon]] and [[Hardy]] (Environmental Consultants, Inc., Las Vegas, Nevada) analyzes length-frequency data for [[Woundfin]] (*Gila elegans*) in the [[Virgin River]] from 1977 to 1980. The authors link poor reproduction and recruitment in 1977 to low mean spring flows of about 90 cfs, and show that the poor 1977 year class depressed spawning in 1979 and adult numbers through winter 1979–80. Good reproduction and survival were associated with mean quarterly spring flows of 450–1000 cfs. The report argues that the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] flow requirement of 110 cfs for fall, winter and spring is highly conservative and recommends continuous monitoring to assess its effectiveness.

## Background & Description

[[Woundfin]] is a small cyprinid fish whose range in this study is the [[Virgin River]] between La Verkin Spring and the Washington Diversion, through the St. George area, the canyon reaches near [[Berry Springs]], and [[Beaver Dam Wash]]. The study uses length-frequency analysis (total length in mm, TL) of collected samples to infer year classes, hatch timing and spawning success. Age classes are inferred from size: young-of-year and one-year-old fish, and two- and three-year-old spawners.

Earlier work referenced includes [[Deacon]] (1979), a [[Vaughn Hansen Study]] of 1977 adult survival, and [[Roundtail Chub]] recovery team data. The brief report updates the 1977 conclusions with data from 1978–1980.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning season:** Primary spawning by two- and three-year-old fish occurs in spring (April–May); a smaller secondary spawn by one-year-old fish may occur in July or August.
- **Flow regime:** Mean quarterly flows in July–September 1978 and 1979 were 60–90 cfs, coincident with relatively good spawning and survival. Mean spring (April–June) flows of 90–94 cfs in 1977 coincided with below-average spawning and near-complete reproductive failure.
- **Flow variability:** Spring 1977 flows ranged from 57 to 827 cfs; spring 1978–1980 flows ranged from 63 to 2660 cfs. Good reproduction years had both higher mean flows and higher variability.
- **Recruitment dynamics:** Young fish grow to near 60 mm by early summer after their first winter. Older fish are largely absent from the population by June, following spawning.
- **Regional impacts of 1977:** Reproduction and survival of adults were very poor near St. George, where recovery did not occur until summer 1980. Downstream of Berry Springs, young fish were more severely affected than adults. Near Beaver Dam Wash, effects were less severe, but still evident through 1980.
- **Population density:** At Twin Bridges near St. George, only 9 woundfin were collected in 12 sampling runs (about 360 m²) on 28 September 1978 (0.025 per m²).

## Key Findings

1. **1977 failure:** Spawning, recruitment and adult survival were very poor across the Virgin River in 1977. The poor year class depressed reproduction in 1979 and the low adult population through winter 1979–80.
2. **Recovery timing:** Population recovery near St. George did not occur until 1980.
3. **Flow thresholds:** Mean spring flows of 90–94 cfs (1977) produced near-failure of spawning. Good reproduction and survival coincided with mean spring flows of 450–1000 cfs. No data exist for flows between about 100 and 450 cfs, and none show good reproduction below 450 cfs.
4. **Summer flows:** Mean July–October flows of 70 cfs appear compatible with good woundfin habitat, consistent with conditions from 1977–1980.
5. **Canyon refugia:** Some reproduction occurred in confined canyon reaches in 1977, suggesting slightly higher flows might be sufficient for adequate reproduction.
6. **Policy assessment:** The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service flow requirement of 110 cfs (fall, winter, spring) and 70 cfs (summer) is judged "extremely conservative," as it is much closer to flows coincident with reproductive failure than success.
7. **Management recommendation:** Continuous monitoring must accompany application of the flow requirement. Two consecutive years of poor reproduction could be extremely detrimental to the woundfin population.
