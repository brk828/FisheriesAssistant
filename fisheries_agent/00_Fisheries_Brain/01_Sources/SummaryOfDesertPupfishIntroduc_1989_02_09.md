# Summary of Desert Pupfish Introduction Records for Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #desert-pupfish #conservation-biology #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This document is an internal correspondence and data summary from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Phoenix, AZ) to the Arizona Game and Fish Department, compiling **introduction and reintroduction records** for the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) in Arizona between 1982 and 1988. The data are organized by subspecific form — the nominate [[Gila Desert Pupfish]] (*C. m. macularius*) and the endangered [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*C. m. eremus*) — and were intended to support the Desert Fishes Recovery Team's reintroduction efforts, referenced in the broader work *Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West*. Rather than reporting morphological or physiological data, the source provides a chronological ledger of stocking events, including numbers of individuals, dates, precise land-survey locations, elevations, and responsible agencies (BLM, AGFD, FWS, University of Arizona, W. L. Minckley, Arizona State University, and the Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum).

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide morphological descriptions, meristic counts, or physical characteristics of *Cyprinodon macularius* or its subspecies. It is a records/data-tracking document focused exclusively on stocking and translocation events. For morphological context, refer to primary taxonomic literature on [[Cyprinodontidae]] and species accounts of the [[Desert Pupfish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although not explicitly described in narrative form, the stocking locations reveal key aspects of the species' ecological requirements and conservation strategy:

- **Habitat types utilized for reintroduction:**
  - Natural desert springs and canyon pools (e.g., Peoples Canyon, Peoples Canyon Spring, South Peoples Canyon, Mesquite Spring, Grapevine Spring/Yerba Mansa, Howard Well, Bog Hole, Finley Tank)
  - Artificial and institutional refugia (Deer Valley High School Refugium, Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum, Desert Botanical Garden, Arizona State University, Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum)
- **Elevation range of introduction sites:** approximately **1,360 ft to 3,420 ft**, indicating tolerance for a range of desert spring/pool elevations within the Sonoran Desert ecoregion.
- **Watershed/geographic distribution:** Sites span multiple township-range survey sections across Arizona, consistent with the historically fragmented, relict distribution of pupfish populations in desert spring systems following loss of primary riverine habitat.
- The reliance on **refugia populations** (zoos, arboreta, university tanks) reflects the species' [[Endangered Species]] status and the standard conservation practice of maintaining ex-situ backup populations alongside in-situ reintroductions.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic scope:** Records are split by subspecies — *C. m. macularius* ([[Gila Desert Pupfish]]) and *C. m. eremus* ([[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]) — reflecting distinct management units under the Desert Fishes Recovery Team.
- **Timeframe of records:** August 1982 – September 1988.
- **Stocking effort magnitude:** Introduction numbers (n) ranged widely, from 50 to 1,000 individuals per event, with multiple large-scale releases (e.g., 1,000 individuals to Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum in 1985; 1,000 individuals to Arizona State University in 1988).
- **Key reintroduction sites for *C. m. macularius*:**
  - Tres Alamos Falls (Aug 1982, n=75)
  - Peoples Canyon and Peoples Canyon Spring (multiple events, 1982–1985)
  - Mesquite Spring (Dec 1983, n=200)
  - Howard Well (Dec 1983, n=150)
  - Deer Valley High School Refugium, Phoenix (Dec 1983, n=50)
  - Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum (three separate events, 1983–1985, up to n=1,000)
  - Desert Botanical Garden, Phoenix (Nov 1987)
- **Key reintroduction sites for *C. m. eremus*:**
  - Grapevine Spring / Yerba Mansa (Aug 1988)
  - Peoples Canyon (Sept 1988)
  - Bog Hole, Coronado National Forest (University of Arizona)
  - Finley Tank, Audubon Research Ranch, Elgin (University of Arizona)
  - Arizona State University, Tempe (introduced by W. L. Minckley, n=1,000)
  - Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum, Tucson (introduced by ASDM)
- **Institutional collaboration:** Introductions were conducted by a broad coalition including the [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[University of Arizona]], [[Arizona State University]], and prominent ichthyologist [[W. L. Minckley]], illustrating a multi-agency recovery strategy typical of endangered desert fish conservation.
- **Document purpose:** Compiled explicitly to support the "Reintroduction Section" of a native fish management publication, underscoring its role as a conservation-tracking and historical reference document rather than a primary research study.
