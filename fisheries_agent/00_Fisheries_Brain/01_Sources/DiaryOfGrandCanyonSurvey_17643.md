# Grand Canyon Survey Expedition Diary 1923

#survey #expedition #Colorado-River #Grand-Canyon #hydrography #topography

## Executive Summary

This document is the personal diary of C.H. Birdseye, Chief of Party for the 1923 [[United States Geological Survey]] expedition through [[Marble Canyon]] and [[Grand Canyon]]. The survey party departed [[Flagstaff]] on July 18, 1923, and navigated the [[Colorado River]] from [[Lees Ferry]] through the Grand Canyon system using four cataract-type boats and one canvas canoe. The expedition conducted detailed topographic and hydrographic surveys, including dam site evaluations, stream flow measurements, and geological observations. The party encountered 52 numbered rapids, experienced a catastrophic 20-foot flood at [[Lava Falls]], and completed the survey at [[Diamond Creek]] on October 7, 1923, having traveled approximately 255 miles from Lees Ferry.

## Background & Description

**Party Composition:**
- C.H. Birdseye (Chief of Party)
- E.C. LaRue (Hydraulic Engineer)
- R.C. Moore (Geologist)
- R.W. Burchard (Topographic Engineer)
- Emery Kolb (Head Boatman)
- Leigh Lint, Blake, Freeman, Dodge (Boatmen)
- Frank Word, later Felix Koms (Cooks)
- H. Stabler (joined August 27)

**Equipment:**
Four wooden cataract-type boats (Marble, Grand, Glen, Boulder) and one canvas canoe. The expedition carried radio equipment that successfully received broadcasts from [[Los Angeles]] (KHJ), [[Salt Lake City]] (KZN), and other stations despite the deep canyon environment. The party used vertical angle stadia surveying methods with frequent instrument stations along the river banks.

**Route and Timeline:**
- July 18: Departed Flagstaff
- July 19: Reached Lees Ferry (140 miles north of Flagstaff)
- August 2: Launched from Lees Ferry
- August 13: Reached mouth of [[Little Colorado River]] (60.5 miles)
- August 16-20: Resupply at [[Hance Trail]]
- August 27: Reached [[Bright Angel Creek]]
- September 2: Reached [[Bass Trail]]
- September 14: Reached [[Havasu Creek]]
- September 18-22: Stranded by 20-foot flood at [[Lava Falls]]
- October 2: Reached [[Diamond Creek]]
- October 12: Connected with Burchard's 1920 survey at Last Chance Rapids

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrological Observations:**

The expedition documented numerous side streams and their flow rates:
- [[Tapeats Creek]]: 92 second-feet (largest tributary measured)
- [[Havasu Creek]]: 75 second-feet
- [[Kanab Creek]]: 34 second-feet
- [[Shinumo Creek]]: 16 second-feet
- [[Deer Creek]]: 8.21 second-feet
- [[Clear Creek]]: Clear, cold water entering at mile 22.6

Water temperatures varied significantly. [[Tapeats Creek]] springs measured 52°F, described as "almost ice cold," while warm springs at [[Lava Falls]] measured approximately 90°F and were heavily mineralized with lime or soda, creating travertine terraces.

**Flood Event:**

On September 18-19, the party experienced a catastrophic flood at [[Lava Falls]] that rose 20 feet over 24 hours, reaching a peak estimated at 60,000 second-feet additional flow. The flood was attributed to heavy rains September 13-16, though the exact source (Little Colorado or upper Colorado) remained uncertain. The river remained elevated 7 feet above normal for several days following the peak.

**Geological Features:**

The expedition documented the transition from [[Marble Canyon]]'s limestone formations to the [[Granite Gorge]] of Grand Canyon. Notable features included:
- Granite narrows at mile 73.1 (walls only 75 feet apart, rising 700+ feet)
- [[Lava Rock Island]] at mile 116.0 (cylindrical lava pinnacle, 50 feet diameter, 60 feet high)
- [[Toroweap Fault]] displacement of approximately 580 feet at mile 117.6
- Extensive lava flows and volcanic features in lower canyon sections

**Springs and Water Sources:**

Numerous springs were documented, many surrounded by "green verdure" in contrast to the arid canyon walls. [[Vasey's Paradise]] at mile 31.1 featured a large stream gushing from cliffs 75 feet above river level. Warm springs at [[Lava Falls]] created unique riparian habitat with tule grass and thistles covering a large area between river and cliffs.

**Wildlife:**

Limited wildlife observations included:
- One bighorn sheep startled at mile 71.1
- One large rattlesnake killed near boat mooring at [[Lava Falls]]
- Flies noted as bothersome for several days around mile 68

**Archaeological Sites:**

The expedition documented several [[Indian ruins]] and archaeological features:
- Cave with steel traps and tools at mile 27.2 (occupied after 1911 Kolb expedition)
- Ruins at mile 75.3 on left bank above high water
- [[Tapeats Creek]] ruins with human jaw bone, teeth, and arrowheads
- Rock mounds with initials "R.P." chiseled near Devil's Slide Canyon

## Key Findings

**Survey Accomplishments:**

The expedition completed a continuous topographic and hydrographic survey from [[Lees Ferry]] to connection with Burchard's 1920 work below [[Diamond Creek]], a distance of approximately 255 miles. Vertical angle stadia methods achieved remarkable accuracy: the survey checked at [[Hance Trail]] with +8.2 feet error over 76 miles, at [[Havasu Creek]] with +4.3 feet error, and at [[Diamond Creek]] with 8.9 feet error over 69 miles (0.13 feet per mile).

**Dam Site Surveys:**

The expedition conducted detailed surveys of 15 potential dam sites, with [[Dam Site No. 7]] at mile 24.7 noted as having the best narrow section and abutments encountered, though lacking natural spillway. [[Dam Site No. 5]] at mile 22.9 below [[Clear Creek]] showed good prospects with potential tunnel spillway. [[Dam Site No. 14]] at [[Havasu Creek]] was evaluated as one of the best for low-power development (165 feet wide at water, 360 feet across top at 235 feet above water).

**Rapids Classification:**

The expedition numbered and measured 52 major rapids with falls ranging from 3 to 28 feet. The most significant rapids were:
- [[Hance Rapids]] (#6): 28-foot fall, "one of the worst on the river"
- [[Sockdolager Rapids]] (#6): 19-foot fall (not 80 feet as Dellenbaugh reported)
- [[Lava Falls]] (#44): 10-foot fall at low water, deemed worst rapid; never successfully run before
- [[Separation Rapids]] (#51): 19.5-foot fall (15 feet upper, 4.5 feet lower)
- [[Mattowitteki Rapids]] (#62): 17-foot fall, "one of the worst rapids on the river"

**Navigation Incidents:**

- August 4: Lost canvas canoe at Spring Cave Rapids
- August 11: Blake thrown from Glen at Boulder Rapids but recovered
- October 10: Grand capsized at [[Separation Rapids]]; Freeman, LaRue, and Moore thrown out but recovered with no serious injuries
- October 10: Marble upset at [[Mattowitteki Rapids]]; Kolb trapped under boat but emerged safely

**Radio Communications:**

The expedition successfully demonstrated radio reception in the deep canyon environment, regularly receiving broadcasts from [[Los Angeles]] (KHJ), [[Salt Lake City]] (KZN), San Francisco, Phoenix, and Chicago. The party heard news of President Harding's death on August 2, only 5.75 hours after it occurred. Radio equipment required battery replacement at [[Havasu Creek]] after B-battery failure from extreme heat.

**Mapping Accuracy:**

The expedition validated the exceptional accuracy of the [[Vishnu Quadrangle]], [[Bright Angel Sheet]], and [[Shinumo Sheet]] topographic maps by Evans and Matthes, finding only slight discrepancies in minor details. Water elevation at [[Little Colorado River]] mouth was found to be 2718 feet (adjusted), approximately 20 feet lower than interpolated from existing maps.

**Environmental Conditions:**

Temperature extremes ranged from 110°F in shade at [[Lees Ferry]] (July 19) to 52°F minimum at Granite Park (September 28). The expedition experienced numerous thunderstorms and flash floods, with particularly severe storms on August 30 (cloud burst at [[Crystal Creek]]) and September 18-22 (major flood event).

**Historical Corrections:**

The expedition corrected several errors in previous expedition accounts:
- [[Sockdolager Rapids]] fall measured at 19 feet, not 80 feet with 30-foot waves as Dellenbaugh reported
- Many rapids previously portaged or lined were successfully run
- Distances and falls of rapids found to be less than reported in earlier accounts
- [[Cataract Creek]] location clarified as distinct from [[Havasu Creek]]
