# Hoover Powerplant Modification Feasibility Report: Appendix C Hydrology

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hydrology

## Executive Summary

This 1987 Bureau of Reclamation appendix (Lower Colorado Region) documents the hydrologic studies supporting the feasibility investigation of increasing peaking capacity at [[Hoover Dam]] and Powerplant. The study compares a 500 MW surface or underground powerhouse with replacement of units A8 and A9, a pumped-back storage option, and uprating of existing units. It covers water supply and reservoir operation, [[Tailwater Studies]], cofferdam effects, channel degradation, penstock head loss, and water temperature and interface conditions relevant to the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. The appendix concludes that the 500 MW alternative is hydrologically feasible within stated operating limits, while pumped storage and a regulatory dam at mile 9.8 were dropped.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Provide a detailed presentation of the hydrologic studies for the feasibility investigation of the Hoover Powerplant Modification Project.
- **Existing capacity:** Peak nameplate capacity of 1,340 MW, being uprated to about 1,800 MW as operation and maintenance work.
- **Additions studied:** Investigation concentrated on additions of 260 MW and 500 MW beyond the uprated capacity.
- **Location:** Black Canyon of the Colorado River on the Arizona-Nevada border, about 25 miles southeast of Las Vegas and 7 miles northeast of Boulder City, Nevada. The dam lies about halfway between [[Lee Ferry]] and the Gulf of California.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  1. Surface powerhouse: two 250 MW units (500 MW total).
  2. Underground powerhouse: same capacity, located behind the existing Arizona powerhouse.
  3. Replacement of units A8 and A9 with a single 350 MW unit (net gain of 260 MW), the smallest units in the plant.
  4. [[Pumped-Back Storage]] using reversible pump-turbines to pump water from [[Lake Mohave]] to [[Lake Mead]] during low-demand periods.

Key structures: Hoover Dam has a maximum allowable reservoir elevation of 1229 ft and a power outlet capacity of 40,600 ft³/s; [[Davis Dam]] has a maximum allowable reservoir elevation of 647 ft.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water supply and apportionment.** The Lower Colorado Basin apportionment totals 9.0 MAF: California 4.4, Arizona 2.8, Mexico 1.5 (treaty guarantee), and Nevada 0.3. Main-stem consumptive use was 7.80 MAF in 1976, 8.18 MAF in 1977, and 7.53 MAF in 1978. Governance follows the [[Law of the River]], including the [[Colorado River Compact]], [[Boulder Canyon Project Act]], the 1944 Mexican Treaty, and the 1964 [[Arizona v. California]] Supreme Court Decree.

**Historic inflow.** Average annual inflow to Lake Mead was 11.83 MAF/yr (1935-62), 2.74 MAF/yr for two years after Glen Canyon Dam filled from 1963, and 9.17 MAF/yr for 1965-78. Average discharge below Hoover Dam for 1935-78 was 9.57 MAF/yr.

**Ecological flow concerns.**
- Releases maintain about 2,000 ft³/s minimum below Davis and Parker Dams as a live stream for fish and wildlife.
- Historic low discharges at Hoover were 784 ft³/s (December 1978, 6 hours), 1,479 ft³/s (February 1979, 203.5 hours), and 3,587 ft³/s (January 1974, 462 hours).
- Lake Mohave is scheduled no lower than 630.5 ft for navigation and recreation, and normally no higher than 645 ft to avoid exceeding 647 ft.

**Water temperature and fisheries.** Discharge temperature is about 54°F year-round under current operations. Lake Mohave's warm/cold water interface affects the [[Willow Beach Cold Water Fishery]]. Ground-water use at the hatchery was judged infeasible because the single temperature reading was 30°C and well water quality was at least twice as poor as river water.

## Key Findings

**Hydrology and operations**
- Future average annual inflow to Lake Mead (1980-2040, 61 years) was projected at 9.4 MAF/yr; average Lake Mead elevation 1157 ft; average Hoover generation 4,124 MkWh.
- Across 15 CRSP hydrologic sequences, the driest sequence (No. 5) gave 8.7 MAF discharge and 3,634 MkWh; the wettest (No. 13) gave 9.7 MAF and 4,371 MkWh. The most critical 5-year drought (2010-2014, sequence No. 4) yielded about 3,104 MkWh/yr at 7.89 MAF/yr.
- Central Arizona Project (CAP) diversions begin around 1985 and raise Arizona's actual consumptive use toward its full 2.8 MAF allocation.
- Hoover operates as a peaking and regulating facility, with the highest demand in April-May and July-August. The plant runs on a roughly 5-day peaking cycle.

**Peak discharges (Table 14)**
- 500 MW powerhouse: 62,000 ft³/s
- Replacement of A8/A9: 56,000 ft³/s
- Pumped-back storage: 76,000 ft³/s
- Uprated conditions: 49,000 ft³/s
- Present condition: 40,000 ft³/s

**Tailwater and channel effects**
- A calibrated HEC-2 steady-flow model (Manning's n averaging 0.042) matched recorded water levels; the unsteady model reproduced levels only to ±1.5 ft, which was deemed too coarse.
- With Lake Mohave at 647 ft and 62,000 ft³/s, tailbay elevation reaches 662.8 ft; at 630 ft Lake Mohave, the tailbay reaches 661.3 ft.
- Maximum daily fluctuation is about 15.7 ft at the tailbay and 4.3 ft at Willow Beach (647 ft case), and about 29 ft at the tailbay and 5.8 ft at Willow Beach (630 ft case).
- Degradation analysis: required armoring size rises from about 3.5 in to 5.0 in. Existing armor at the three controls (near miles 1, 2, and 3.6) is larger than required, so the channel should remain stable.

**Cofferdam effects**
- Cofferdam No. 3 (about 140 ft wide) raises the tailbay elevation 1.9 ft at 40,000 ft³/s and 2.6 ft at 49,000 ft³/s; the latter reduces rated output by 9.2 MW.
- Maximum velocity through the cofferdam area is about 12 ft/s at 49,000 ft³/s.

**Proposed powerhouse discharge**
- A worst-case HEC-2 jet-obstruction simulation gave a maximum tailbay elevation of about 664 ft, within the 666 ft allowable limit; no further study was judged necessary.

**Head loss and capacity**
- Head loss from penstock friction and tailbay rise reduces capacity by about 24 MW (4.8 percent) for the 500 MW alternative, and about 13.8 MW for the A8/A9 replacement.

**Water temperature and interface**
- Modeled discharge temperature at 62,000 ft³/s is 13.2°C, with about 1°C daily fluctuation.
- The river-lake interface, located 14-22 miles below Hoover Dam, is predicted at about 14.8 miles under modified conditions, so it should not reach the Willow Beach hatchery, about 12 miles downstream.

**Alternatives dropped**
- Pumped-back storage was dropped because of severe environmental and recreational impacts, lack of baseload power for pumping, and loss of head. A regulatory control dam at mile 9.8 was judged not feasible.

**Conclusion**
- The modified powerplant is hydrologically feasible under the stated operating criteria, with Lake Mohave levels, drawdown limits (1 ft/day averaged over 10 days, 15 ft in 30 days), and downstream demands satisfied.
