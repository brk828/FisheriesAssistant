# Biological assessment for developing a habitat for razorback suckers and bonytail chubs at the Phoenix Zoo, Phoenix, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #ConservationAquaculture #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary

This 1996 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service biological assessment outlines a conservation project to convert an existing 5.0-acre ornamental lake at the [[Phoenix Zoo]] into a long-term refugial habitat for two federally endangered Colorado River Basin fishes: the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail Chub]]. The project, coordinated by the [[Parker Fishery Resource Office]], is intended to serve dual purposes: (1) prevent extinction of these species by establishing an ex-situ population, and (2) potentially serve as a broodstock/source population for future reintroduction efforts into historic native habitats. The document details lake renovation procedures, containment/escapement safeguards, and interagency monitoring responsibilities, alongside a standard endangered species screening for the terrestrial project footprint (identifying only incidental potential occurrence of [[American Peregrine Falcon]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Constructed Habitat**: A 5.0-acre lake with a maximum depth of 15 ft (average 5–6 ft) and solid rock substrate, situated between the Tropical Flight and African Veldt exhibits at the Phoenix Zoo. This represents an artificial refugium rather than natural riverine habitat.
- **Water Source**: Supplied via the [[Salt River Project]] canal system, filtered through pea-sized gravel to exclude fish ingress.
- **Containment Design**: Outflow passes through a fine screen and a significant elevation drop to prevent upstream fish invasion and to deter escapement.
- **Escape Risk Pathway**: In the unlikely event of reproduction and juvenile escapement, fish could move downstream into two connected lakes containing established populations of introduced predatory fish, which would likely consume any escapees. Surviving individuals would theoretically continue through a pipe/canal system into the Salt River Project's pretreatment ponds, where survival is presumed unlikely.
- **Zooplankton Establishment**: Following draining, substrate removal, and refilling, the lake is allowed to sit undisturbed (~1 month) to permit zooplankton community reestablishment prior to fish introduction — indicating dependence on a naturalized planktonic food base for early fish nutrition/support.
- **Co-occurring Terrestrial Species**: The lake and surrounding landscaped area may provide incidental feeding/resting habitat for the [[American Peregrine Falcon]], a species associated primarily with mountainous breeding habitat elsewhere in Arizona but potentially transient in this urban setting.
- **Anthropogenic Context**: The site is embedded within a zoo landscape designed to reflect regional southwestern habitat aesthetics, combining conservation function with public education/signage initiatives.

## Key Findings

- The project constitutes an **ex-situ conservation strategy** for two federally endangered Colorado River Basin fishes, leveraging a non-native (zoo) setting to reduce extinction risk.
- Physical design incorporates **multiple redundant containment features** (gravel filtration on intake, fine screening plus a drop structure on outflow) to prevent both unauthorized entry and species escapement.
- Long-term **monitoring will be a multi-agency collaborative effort**, involving the [[Arizona Fishery Resource Office]] (Parker substation), [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Arizona State University]], and the Phoenix Zoo, focused on fish health and reproductive activity.
- Fish stock for the project will be supplied directly by the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service** via the Parker Fishery Resource Office, rather than sourced from wild populations at the project site.
- The **potential for future reintroduction** of zoo-reared/held individuals into historic native range habitats is explicitly identified as a secondary long-term goal.
- Standard **Section 7 consultation** procedures were followed, with the Arizona Ecological Services Office concurring on the project; terrestrial endangered species screening found negligible risk, with the [[American Peregrine Falcon]] identified as the only species of potential (beneficial, incidental) relevance.
- The initiative reflects a broader pattern of **zoo-based conservation partnerships** supporting federal recovery efforts for imperiled freshwater fishes of the American Southwest, connecting institutional (zoo) infrastructure with federal recovery mandates under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
