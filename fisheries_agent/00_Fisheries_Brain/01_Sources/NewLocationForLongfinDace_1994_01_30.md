## Executive Summary

This field memorandum, authored by Jerry Stefferud and addressed to W. L. Minckley, Kirk Young, and Sally Stefferud, documents a **new locality record** for the [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) in Conner Wash, a tributary drainage in the Sierra Ancha region of Arizona. Discovered incidentally during a visit to inspect habitat improvement work at the Cottonwood Artesian [[Gila Topminnow]] site, the observation extends the known distribution of longfin dace into a previously undocumented stream reach. The population occupied roughly 200 feet of flowing water within a larger ~400-foot wetted reach fed by a small spring/artesian source. Eight voucher specimens were collected and slated for accession into the Arizona State University (ASU) fish museum collection.

## Background & Description

The source document is a brief field survey memo and does not provide detailed morphological data (e.g., meristic counts, coloration, fin ray formulas) for the collected *Agosia chrysogaster* specimens. It confirms only:

- **Specimens collected:** 8 individuals
- **Disposition:** Preserved specimens designated for accession into the [[ASU Museum of Fishes]] collection for future taxonomic/morphological reference.

No further anatomical description is included in this record.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology:** Discharge at the site measured approximately **25 gallons/minute**, sourced from an artesian/spring input in Conner Wash.
- **Channel dimensions:** Stream approximately **3 feet wide × 0.3 feet deep**, with continuous flowing water extending for about **400 feet**; longfin dace specifically occupied ~200 feet of this reach.
- **Substrate:** Predominantly **cobble**.
- **Riparian vegetation:** Sparse woody riparian cover, including [[Seepwillow]] (*Baccharis* sp.), noted as "extremely hedged" due to **intense grazing pressure** in the area — indicating anthropogenic habitat degradation via livestock use.
- **Watershed context:** Conner Wash originates from the [[Sierra Ancha]] mountains and flows into Cottonwood Wash, which subsequently enters the Salt River arm of [[Roosevelt Lake]], roughly 5 miles downstream of the survey site.
- **Co-occurring / nearby habitat features:** The site lies about 0.5 mile upstream of the Cottonwood Artesian [[Gila Topminnow]] site, suggesting the broader wash system supports multiple native desert fish species. [[Cellar Creek]], an intermittent tributary, enters Conner Wash a short distance upstream of the discovery site and may represent an additional area of interest for surveys.
- **Unmapped water source:** The Cottonwood Artesian well feeding this system is not represented on any available maps, complicating hydrological documentation of the site.

## Key Findings

1. **New distributional record:** This appears to be the first documented occurrence of [[Longfin Dace]] in Conner Wash, expanding known range within the Salt River/Gila River drainage system.
2. **Habitat degradation observed:** Intense grazing has heavily hedged the sparse riparian woody vegetation, raising concern for long-term habitat quality and stream stability.
3. **Incomplete population assessment:** The survey did not determine the full upstream or downstream extent of the longfin dace population; the authors explicitly recommend **further surveys of Conner Wash** to clarify distribution and status of the species.
4. **Voucher specimens secured:** Eight specimens were preserved for accession into the ASU fish museum, providing a permanent scientific record of this range extension.
5. **Broader conservation relevance:** The find is significant in the context of ongoing habitat improvement and monitoring efforts for native Arizona fishes, particularly given the proximity to the imperiled [[Gila Topminnow]] population at the Cottonwood Artesian site.
