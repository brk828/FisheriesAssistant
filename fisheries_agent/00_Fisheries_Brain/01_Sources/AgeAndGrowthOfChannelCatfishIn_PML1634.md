# Age and Growth of Channel Catfish in the Coachella Canal, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictalurus-punctatus

## Executive Summary

This study by [[McCarthy and Marsh]] (1982, Arizona State University) reports the age, growth, length-weight relationship, and condition factor of [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) from the [[Coachella Canal]], a large earthen canal in southeastern California. Fish aged 0 to 5 years, with average annual growth of 73–123 mm. The authors conclude that the canal supports a healthy, reproducing population of fast-growing catfish, and that arid-region canals should be considered for recreational fishery management.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Channel catfish were introduced to the [[Lower Colorado River]] in 1892–1898 and have since spread to most suitable habitats, including canals. They are an important sport fish in the Southwest, but local life-history data were scarce.
- **Context:** Aquatic fauna of arid-region canals had only recently begun to be studied.
- **Sampling:** Fish were collected by [[Rotenone Sampling]] in November 1980. Collection was supported by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], and the California Department of Fish and Game provided permits and assistance.
- **Measurements:** Total length (TL, mm), weight (g), and left pectoral spines were recorded.
- **Ageing method:** Pectoral spines were sectioned using the [[Spine Sectioning Method]] and annuli were measured under 10X magnification. Lengths at earlier ages were estimated by [[Back-Calculation]] from the spine-length relationship.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Abundance:** Channel catfish are abundant in the Coachella Canal, with standing stocks of 24–253 g/m² (Minckley 1981). Similar populations occur in other Southwestern canals.
- **Reproduction:** The canal was stocked by the California Department of Fish and Game until 1979. The presence of 0-age fish in the 1980 sample indicates successful natural reproduction.
- **Food resources:** Benthic macroinvertebrates in the canal are generally depauperate (Marsh and Stinemetz, in press). Catfish nonetheless exploit locally abundant prey, especially [[Caddisflies]] (Marsh 1981).
- **Habitat implication:** The authors argue that the environmental constraints of canal habitats do not preclude substantial fish production.

## Key Findings

**Sample and age structure**
- 147 channel catfish were examined.
- Total length ranged from 75 to 575 mm.
- Age ranged from 0 to 5 years (Table 1, Fig. 1).

**Growth**
- Average annual growth increments were 73–123 mm.
- Growth was similar to other southeastern U.S. populations and relatively rapid (Table 2).
- Growth was slightly greater than in the [[Lower Colorado River]] (Kimsey et al. 1957) but lower than in the [[Salt River]], Arizona (Busdosh et al. 1978).

**Length-weight relationship**
- Log(WT) = 2.96 × log(TL) − 5.06.
- The relationship was similar to previously published values (Table 2).

**Condition factor**
- Mean condition factor (K) was 0.7196.

**Length–spine diameter relationship**
- A linear regression was used to back-calculate lengths, with a slope of 0.0126 (spine diameter in mm to TL in mm).

**Conclusions**
- The canal supports a healthy, reproducing, fast-growing catfish population.
- Canal habitats, despite their limited invertebrate fauna, can support substantial fish production and should be evaluated for recreational fishery potential.
