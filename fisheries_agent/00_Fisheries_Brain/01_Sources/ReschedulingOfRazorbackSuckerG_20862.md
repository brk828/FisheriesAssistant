# Recovery Program Genetics Meeting for Razorback Sucker - July 1994

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] Recovery Implementation Program documents the rescheduling of a genetics meeting focused on [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) conservation. The meeting, originally planned for August 1994, was rescheduled to accommodate field schedules and vacations. The document includes a detailed agenda covering genetics studies, presumptive stocks, hybridization concerns, broodstock management, and stocking protocols for this endangered fish species.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is an endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The Recovery Implementation Program involves multiple federal agencies (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Western Area Power Administration) and state agencies from Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, along with environmental organizations and water user associations.

The meeting coordinator, Dick Wydoski (Propagation and Nonnative Fish Stocking Coordinator), distributed this memorandum to 15 genetics experts and program participants. The meeting was planned for 1.5 days in Denver, Colorado, at the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility in Lakewood.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses conservation genetics within the context of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] ecosystem. Key ecological considerations include:

- Geographic distribution of presumptive stocks
- Spawning behavior and movement patterns
- Stock interactions within the basin
- Hybridization risks, with specific concern about Etter Pond razorback suckers
- Natural reproduction and wild larvae collection
- Protected off-channel habitats for stocking
- Riverine environment release protocols

## Key Findings

**Meeting Logistics:**
- Original August 1994 meeting cancelled due to scheduling conflicts
- Rescheduled for September, October, or November 1994
- Two-day meeting format (1.5 days of content)
- Location: Denver/Lakewood, Colorado for optimal airline connections and reduced travel costs

**Agenda Topics:**

1. **Genetics Studies Review**: Assessment of completed analyses, publication plans
2. **Presumptive Stocks Management**: Review of Genetics Management Plan, stock identification, ecological and genetic information gaps
3. **Hybridization Concerns**: General risks and specific concerns regarding Etter Pond population
4. **Broodstock Characterization**: Nonlethal sampling protocols using muscle and fin tissues
5. **Stocking Protocols**: Breeding strategies, genetic risk assessment, experimental design for captive-reared fish releases
6. **Wild Larvae Protocols**: Strategies for obtaining naturally-produced larvae, genetic characterization of survivors, determination of appropriate release numbers

**Scheduling Constraints:**
- Conflicts with Labor Day (September 5, 1994)
- Veterans Day (November 11, 1994)
- Thanksgiving (November 24, 1994)
- Desert Fishes Council Annual Conference (November 17-20, 1994, Death Valley, California)

The meeting represents a critical planning session for genetic management of endangered fish recovery efforts in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]].
