# Humboldtiana taylori: A New Land Snail from Northern Coahuila

#malacology #terrestrial-mollusca #helicidae #humboldtiana

## Executive Summary

This 1951 taxonomic paper by [[Robert J Drake]] describes *Humboldtiana taylori*, a new species of helicid land snail from northern [[Coahuila]], Mexico. The shell was collected dead in October 1947 by [[Walter W Taylor]] during archaeological work funded by the Northern Mexico Archaeological Fund of the [[Smithsonian Institution]]. The species is separated from its northern Mexican congeners mainly by its dense shell granulation. The paper also adds sparse molluscan records for the state, drawn from earlier archaeological collections.

## Background & Description

**Archaeological context.** Taylor's 1941 archaeological survey of Coahuila yielded an additional land snail record for the state, *[[Humboldtiana Montezuma]]*, found in situ at [[Frightful Cave]] (survey site C68). Its identity was confirmed by [[Harald A Rehder]] of the U.S. National Museum. Other shells from Frightful Cave (U.S.N.M. accession 162071), also determined by Rehder, were:
- *Bulimulus dealbatus schiedeanus* (Pfeiffer)
- *Nephronaias iridella* Pilsbry, as listed in the source
- *Holospira* sp.

Museum records for the *H. montezuma* material are U.S.N.M. 533948 (5 shells), 533949 (3 shells) and 533950 (1 shell). The author notes these records considerably add to the sparse malacological literature for Coahuila.

**Holotype (U.S.N.M. 596939).** The shell is depressed-subglobose, of medium size for the genus, thin but not fragile, with three bands. The lower band equals the combined width of the other two. Key features:
- Four convex whorls, the last descending slightly in side view
- Smooth embryonic shell producing a low spire
- Narrow, oblique umbilicus, partly covered by a prominent, white, transparent parietal callus
- Deeply impressed sutures and heavy growth wrinkles
- Dense oblong granulation over all but the embryonic shell and umbilical region, with about three granules per millimetre from top to bottom and two per millimetre around the shell on the body whorl
- Roundly oval aperture, wider than high, with a suggested rim formed by growth-wrinkle concentration

Holotype measurements: height 35 mm; width 39 mm; aperture height 15 mm; aperture width 20 mm. It was collected dead from the surface of cultural deposits at site C96.

**Paratypes.** Taylor collected eleven dead shells in total. Two are illustrated (U.S.N.M. 596940 and 596941). The remaining nine are deposited in the [[Drake Molluscan Collection]] (no. 1545) and the Laboratory of Conchology of the Biblioteca y Museo de Sonora.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality.** Site C96 is the same as Spring Cave in Raymond Emerson's survey. It lies in [[Media Luna Canyon]], in the Fronteriza Range (Serranías del Burro). The canyon is deep, cut into volcanic (probably rhyolitic) rock, and supports large oak, madroño, pine and cedar in its upper reaches, where the cave site is located.

**Regional comparison.** Most northern Mexican *Humboldtiana* lack sculptural granulation or have very limited granulation. The paper cites these comparisons:
- *H. torreri* (Chihuahua) and *H. fortis* (Nuevo León): no granulation
- *H. hegeana* (Chihuahua) and *H. ferrissiana* (Texas): limited granulation
- *H. chrysogona* (Zacatecas): smaller form, but dense granulation
- *H. buffoniana* and *H. humboldtiana* (near Mexico City): dense granulation
- *H. nuevoleonis*: the northern Mexican species with the most granulation, closest to *H. taylori*

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Humboldtiana taylori* is described from a holotype and eleven paratypes collected dead in October 1947.
- **Diagnostic traits:** Dense granulation, lack of granulation on the spire, and a parietal callus that covers the umbilicus without sealing it. Granulation is the most important trait for separating it from other humboldtianas.
- **Relationships uncertain:** The author states that its relationships "cannot be accurately determined" until the anatomy of the reproductive system is known, if that proves definitive.
- **New state records:** The paper adds *H. montezuma* and other shells from Frightful Cave to the sparse Coahuila molluscan record.
- **Literature cited:** Works by Pilsbry (1927, 1935, 1948) on Humboldtiana and related helicid genera, and Taylor (1948) on archaeology.
