# Bonytail Chub Tissue Handling, Sampling, and Aging Protocol (1993 Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 internal memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 2 Fisheries office sets out a protocol for using Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*) specimens that became available after events in the spring of 1993. The specimens comprise frozen fish held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] (8 fish) and at [[Arizona State University]] (2 fish). The Gila Taxonomy Group wants tissue-based information from these fish, plus known-age Bonytail from Dexter for comparison. The memo lays out tissue sampling, freezing, shipping, and a blind aging study. It asks the recipients to report freezing history and to submit a work schedule by September 1, 1993, before a notice to proceed is issued.

## Background & Description

The memo is an administrative communication, not a research report. It is addressed to the Gila Taxonomy Group and a list of collaborators, including [[Wayne Starnes]], [[Buddy Jensen]], [[Chuck Minckley]], [[Don Morizot]], [[Jack L. Millard]], [[Tom Dowling]], [[Wendell Minckley]], [[Mike Douglas]], [[Paul Marsh]], and [[George Divine]]. It was issued by the Associate Manager of Fisheries, Region 2.

The subject is the handling and disposition of harvested Bonytail Chub tissues. The memo's opening line is partly garbled in the scan, so the exact wording of the subject is uncertain.

The memo uses four procedural tracks:
- **Step 1:** Assess the condition of frozen fish, including time of death, time of freezing, and condition at freezing.
- **Methods A–C:** Full sampling for fish in good condition.
- **Method D:** An alternate, reduced procedure for fish dead too long before freezing, where tissues are of little value.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo does not describe habitat, distribution, population status, or water chemistry. Its ecological relevance lies in the use of hatchery-reared, known-age fish to calibrate age estimates for field-collected specimens. This matters for managing a species of conservation concern in the [[Gila River Basin]] system, though the memo itself does not state that management context.

## Key Findings

**1. Specimen inventory and condition assessment**
- 10 Bonytail Chub are in question: 8 at Dexter and 2 at ASU.
- Each facility must document date and time of death, date and time of freezing, and condition at freezing.
- Only fish in good condition go through full sampling (A–C). Fish left unfrozen too long go through Method D.

**2. Tissue sampling protocol (Method A, Dexter and ASU)**
- Each specimen gets five labeled [[Nunc Tubes]], and the specimen is jaw-tagged with the same unique number. The tubes are labeled "DNA/ASU," "GAl" (allozymes), "skeletal muscle," "GAl-pelvic fin," and "GAl-caudal fin."
- A 1–2 cm cube of skeletal muscle is excised from the right dorsolateral area, below the middorsal line anterior to the dorsal fin, using a sterilized scalpel or muscle punch.
- The muscle cube is split into three equal parts for the DNA/ASU, allozyme (GAl), and skeletal muscle storage tubes.
- The outer third of the right pelvic fin and the base of the caudal fin are also sampled into the fin tubes.
- All tubes are frozen immediately in an [[Ultracold Freezer]]. The whole carcass is then refrozen.

**3. Shipping and coordination**
- Whole specimens go to ASU in large coolers of dry ice, coordinated with Mike Douglas and Tom Dowling.
- DNA/ASU tubes go separately to Mike Douglas at ASU, in their own dry-ice container, apart from carcasses.
- GAl tubes go to Rene Schmidt at [[Genetic Analyses Incorporated]] in Smithville, Texas, after coordination with Rene.

**4. Laboratory handling at ASU (Method B)**
- Mike Douglas thaws specimens enough to handle them, sets poses for video-camera data, and videotapes the fish.
- Otoliths, vertebrae, and scales are removed and stored in tubes that match the jaw-tag numbers. Storage follows methods acceptable to ASU and the Fairbanks Fisheries Resources Office, with guidance from Wendell Minckley and Monty Millard.
- Carcasses are preserved in [[Formalin]] and retained with other Gila specimens.

**5. Aging protocol (Method C)**
- Otoliths, vertebrae, and scales from the unknown-age fish are compared against two known-age fish from Dexter. Buddy Jensen is to supply the known-age structures.
- A **blind study** is planned: known- and unknown-age fish are unmarked as to age. Wendell Minckley and Monty Millard are to coordinate this.
- ASU and Fairbanks each read one otolith from the same fish. If their results agree, the age is corroborated.
- If results disagree, a third group (suggested: Jeff Isley, Clemson, South Carolina) repeats the analysis.
- If no consensus is reached, the fish is classed as "un-age-able."

**6. Alternate procedure (Method D)**
- For fish that were dead too long before freezing, Method D is the same as Method A except that steps A1–A6 (the tube labeling, excision, and freezing steps) are omitted.

**7. Action items and timeline**
- Dexter and ASU must report freezing history and fish condition.
- The study team must be named and a work schedule submitted by **September 1, 1993**.
- The Associate Manager will then issue a notice to proceed.
- Copies go to the Regional Director, Deputy Regional Director, and Assistant Regional Directors for Fisheries and Ecological Services, Region 2.
