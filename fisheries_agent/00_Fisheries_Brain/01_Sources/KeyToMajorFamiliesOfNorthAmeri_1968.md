## Executive Summary

This document is a **dichotomous taxonomic key** for identifying major families of North American freshwater snails based on **external, living-animal characters** rather than shell morphology alone. Although the requested framework is fish-oriented, the source material concerns [[Gastropoda]] (Phylum Mollusca), specifically freshwater snails split into two major subclasses: **Prosobranchia** (operculate, gilled snails) and **Euthyneura, Order Basommatophora** (pulmonate, lung-breathing snails). The key uses features such as operculum structure, ctenidium (gill) architecture, eye placement, tentacle shape, fecal pellet morphology, mantle/pseudobranch structures, and reproductive anatomy to discriminate among 11 families across several superfamilies. This represents a classic applied systematics tool for field and laboratory identification of freshwater malacofauna, with direct relevance to aquatic ecosystem surveys where snails serve as intermediate hosts, grazers, and bioindicators.

## Background & Description

### Subclass Prosobranchia (operculate snails)
- **Operculum**: horny (corneous) or calcareous plate closing the shell aperture; may be multispiral, concentric, or paucispiral (3–4 turns)
- **Head-foot**: separated by a deep cleft; bears an elongate, transversely wrinkled, mobile **rostrum (snout)**
- **Eyes**: positioned laterally on or near the tentacle bases (not between tentacle bases)
- **Tentacles**: tapering, slender
- **Ctenidium (gill)**: either **bipectinate** (lamellae on both sides of central blade; [[Valvatidae]]) or **monopectinate** (triangular lamellae hanging from mantle roof; most other families)
- **Feces**: extruded as discrete pellets (ovoid/spindle-shaped) or dorsally grooved, ragged-ended ropes
- **Shell**: low- to high-spired, almost always **dextral**
- Diagnostic reproductive traits used at lower key levels: presence/location of male **verge (penis)** (right tentacle, mantle roof, or mantle floor), presence of **oviducal groove**, presence of **brood pouch** on head, and **epitaenia** (vertical lamella dividing mantle cavity floor)

### Subclass Euthyneura, Order Basommatophora (pulmonate snails)
- **Head-foot**: closely united, **no rostrum**
- **Eyes**: located **between** tentacle bases (not lateral)
- **Tentacles**: flat/triangular ([[Lymnaeacea]] group) or long, narrow, rod-like with a **postvelar flap** ([[Planorbacea]] group)
- **Feces**: smooth, ragged-ended ropes (no discrete pellets)
- **Shell**: limpet-like, discoidal, or high-spired; may be **sinistral or dextral**
- **No true operculum** closing the aperture
- **Blood**: may be red (hemoglobin-bearing, e.g. [[Planorbidae]]) or colorless — a key diagnostic trait
- **Pseudobranch**: a secondary gill-like structure formed from modification of the opening between hyposeptal and pulmonary cavities; morphology (flap vs. T-shaped siphon) separates families
- **Posterior foot shape**: rounded vs. acutely pointed distinguishes genera/families (e.g., [[Ancylidae]] vs. [[Planorbidae]])

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range**: primarily submerged in fresh or brackish water; some taxa (e.g., [[Assimineidae]]) occupy amphibious niches at spring-bogs or saline pool margins, often found just out of water
- **Substrate/attachment strategies**: limpet-like shell forms ([[Ancylidae]], [[Lancidae]]) indicate adaptation to clinging on hard substrates in flowing water
- **Respiratory strategies** reflect microhabitat: true ctenidium-bearing prosobranchs are obligate aquatic gill-breathers, while pulmonates (Basommatophora) use an air-breathing pulmonary cavity or secondary pseudobranch gill, permitting occupation of stagnant, hypoxic, or ephemeral waters
- **Reproductive strategies** vary ecologically: oviparous egg-laying in capsules ([[Bithyniidae]]), calcareous egg spherules deposited out of water ([[Ampullariidae]] — indicating amphibious reproduction), and ovoviviparous live-bearing with brood pouches (some [[Pleuroceridae]]-adjacent taxa), each suited to different hydroperiod stability
- **Size and coloration**: shell size ranges from ~1 mm ([[Hydrobiidae]]) to ~100 mm ([[Viviparidae]]/[[Ampullariidae]]), correlating with habitat permanence and predation pressure; pigmentation patterns (mottled/banded vs. plain/colorless) may relate to substrate camouflage and light exposure

## Key Findings

- The key establishes a hierarchical bifurcation at the subclass level using **operculum presence** and **rostrum/eye position** as primary characters separating [[Prosobranchia]] from [[Basommatophora]]
- **Ctenidium structure** (bipectinate vs. monopectinate) cleanly separates Superorder **Ectobranchia** ([[Valvatidae]]) from Superorder **Pectinibranchia** (Order Taenioglossa)
- Within Pectinibranchia, **external color pattern** (splotched/marbled vs. plain/simple) and **presence of nuchal lobes** (functioning as siphons) divide the **Viviparacea**, **Cerithiacea**, and **Rissoacea** superfamilies
- **Superfamily Rissoacea** contains small (<10 mm), simply-colored taxa with a floor-mounted verge, including [[Hydrobiidae]], [[Bithyniidae]], and [[Assimineidae]] — distinguished from each other by operculum composition (corneous vs. calcareous) and eye/tentacle configuration
- **Superfamily Viviparacea** (e.g. [[Ampullariidae]]) is marked by an epitaenia, pulmonary sac with siphon, and out-of-water calcareous egg deposition — an unusual amphibious reproductive adaptation among gilled snails
- **Superfamily Cerithiacea** ([[Pleuroceridae]]) lacks a penis in males; females instead bear either an oviducal groove or a head brood pouch, and shells can exceed 15–25 mm with banded/splotched ornamentation
- Within **Basommatophora**, the presence/absence of a **postvelar flap** and tentacle shape separate **Lymnaeacea** (flattened triangular tentacles, dextral genital pore) from a second sinistral lineage encompassing **Planorbacea** and related limpet-like taxa
- **Blood color** (red vs. colorless) and **pseudobranch morphology** (simple triangular flap vs. T-shaped protruding siphon) are decisive final characters separating [[Ancylidae]] (small, limpet-like, <5 mm) from [[Planorbidae]] (discoidal/spired, 2–30 mm, dextral- or sinistral-appearing)
- This key exemplifies how **soft-body anatomical traits** (rarely preserved in shell-only collections) — rostrum shape, foot lobation, fecal pellet morphology, and genital tract architecture — provide more robust and phylogenetically informative diagnostic characters than shell shape alone for freshwater gastropod systematics
