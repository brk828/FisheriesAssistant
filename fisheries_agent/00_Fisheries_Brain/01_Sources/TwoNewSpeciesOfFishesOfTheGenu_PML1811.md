# Two New Species of Gambusia (Poeciliidae) from Northeastern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes two new livebearing fishes of the genus [[Gambusia]] from northeastern Mexico. [[Gambusia Longispinis]] is restricted to the semi-isolated, intermontane [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in Coahuila. [[Gambusia Marshi]] is widespread throughout the [[Río Salado Basin]]. The author states that the descriptions were published now to support later, fuller analyses by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], [[Robert R. Miller]] and the author. Descriptions are based on specimens collected from 1939 to 1961, mostly held at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ).

## Background & Description

**Collection history.** In the late 1930s, [[E. G. Marsh Jr.]] made extensive collections of animals and plants in northeastern Mexico. Hubbs identified the fishes, but the descriptions were delayed. Later collections were made by L. V. Guerra in 1951 and by the author and others in 1958, 1960 and 1961. Fieldwork was supported by a [[Sigma Xi]] research grant.

**Taxonomic setting.** The two species belong to different groups within the genus:
- *G. longispinis* belongs to the [[Gambusia Nobilis Group]] of the [[Gambusia Tabirtis Group]]. Its subgenus is not stated in the text. The author notes that the group is found in Texas and northern Mexico.
- *G. marshi* belongs to the subgenus [[Heterophallina]], which is found further south.

The author notes that *G. marshi* may represent a species complex. Its population in certain isolated waters of the Cuatro Ciénegas area appears differentiated. For this reason, the description is based on material from a single locality.

**Methods.** Counts and measurements follow [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1958). Measurements were taken over the body contour with dividers. Terminology for the [[Gonopodium]] follows Hubbs, and the [[Gonopodial Suspensorium]] follows [[Hubbs and Springer]] (1957).

### Gambusia longispinis sp. nov.

- **Type material:** The holotype is an adult male, 20.4 mm standard length (UMMZ 179620). It was seined on 8 April 1961 from a small marsh adjacent to La Angostura Canal, about 4 miles S and 6 miles W of Cuatro Ciénegas.
- **Paratypes:** The text lists five lots, totaling the 97 extant specimens from five localities in the basin. The main lots are UMMZ 130382 (50 specimens, collected by Marsh, 15 August 1939), UMMZ 179621 (15 specimens, collected with the holotype), and UMMZ 130393–130394 (10 specimens, collected by Marsh, 17 August 1939).
- **Diagnosis:** A small, delicate but thick-bodied gambusia. It is differentiated from other members of the nobilis group by:
  - a gonopodium that projects beyond the terminal hook of ray 4p;
  - a broad third-ray segment wider than the combined base lengths of its spine-bearing segments;
  - 7 to 8 dorsal fin rays (typically 8);
  - a broad dark lateral stripe in life.
- **Meristics:** Dorsal typically 8 (7–9); anal 9 (8 or 10); pectorals 11 (12–14); pelvics 6. Lateral scales 30–31 (29 or 32); 15–17 scales before the dorsal fin.
- **Etymology:** *longispinis* refers to the long distal spines of the gonopodium.

### Gambusia marshi sp. nov.

- **Type material:** The description is based on material from a single locality. The text also mentions a series from Río Cañón del Ágo, 3.3 miles N of Cuatro Ciénegas, which is illustrated.
- **Diagnosis:** A large, robust species of subgenus *Heterophallina*. It differs from other members of the subgenus by:
  - the most distal serrae on ray 4p lying distal to the middle of the elbow;
  - the distal extension of ray 4a past the tip of ray 4p;
  - highly developed, generally erect distal spines on ray 3.
- **Meristics:** Dorsal 8 (8–11); anal 10 (9–11); pectorals 13 (12–15); pelvics 6. Lateral scales 30 or 31 (rarely 32); 16–17 scales anterior to the dorsal fin.
- **Morphology:** The body is narrow but relatively deep. The head is large, about 3.6 times in standard length. The male pectoral fins are elongate and upcurved at the tips. The male pelvic fins reach slightly past the base of the gonopodium.
- **Gonopodium:** The male has 12–15 spine-bearing segments on ray 3 distal to the elbow tip, and about two-thirds are erect. Ray 4p has 6–8 large, strongly recurved serrae.
- **Etymology:** The name honors E. G. Marsh Jr., who made the early collections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *G. longispinis* occurs in small marshes, canal margins and spring-fed localities (e.g., Puente Colorado and Puente Chiquito). *G. marshi* occurs in the wider Río Salado system.
- **Behavior:** The deep, rounded anterior body profile suggests a midwater habit rather than a surface habit. Aquarium observations supported this.
- **Coloration:** Live *G. longispinis* are yellowish-brown with a broad dark lateral stripe and sky-blue scale pockets. Live *G. marshi* vary between males with light bars and an overall brassy-yellow phase. Dark and light phases appear to grade into each other, though some lots show distinct color types.
- **Zoogeography:** The author highlights the semi-isolated Cuatro Ciénegas basin as a site of major zoogeographic and evolutionary interest. The text also notes that *G. marshi* in the basin shows variation that may reflect isolation.
- **Reproduction:** Modified pectoral fin leading edges in males are thought to aid copulation. The author also notes that dark and light phases in *G. marshi* have been linked to sexual selection and to courtship behavior. Interspecific isolation mechanisms are discussed in the closing section, but the excerpt is incomplete.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species are described.** *Gambusia longispinis* is endemic to the Cuatro Ciénegas basin (97 extant specimens from five localities). *Gambusia marshi* is widespread in the Río Salado system.
2. **Diagnostic gonopodial characters separate both species.** *G. longispinis* is distinguished by the extension of its gonopodium beyond ray 4p and by a dark lateral stripe in life. *G. marshi* is distinguished by its robust body and by distal serrae and spines on rays 3 and 4.
3. **Meristic ranges are given for both species.** Dorsal, anal and pectoral ray counts, and lateral scale counts, overlap between species but differ in their typical values.
4. **The *G. marshi* complex may need revision.** Its populations in isolated waters of the Cuatro Ciénegas area appear differentiated. The author plans to publish a fuller analysis of variation later.
5. **Future work is planned.** Co-authors Hubbs and Miller are preparing a broader analysis of the Cuatro Ciénegas fishes, which the author describes as important for zoogeography and evolution.
