# San Pedro River Wildlife Update - Vegetation Inventory and Transect Establishment

#riparian-ecology #habitat-assessment #vegetation-survey

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the Bureau of Land Management Safford District Office (dated June 5, 1986) provides an update on vegetation inventory and transect establishment along the [[San Pedro River]] in Arizona. The biologist reports completion of 12 transects across multiple habitat types and a comprehensive vegetation inventory covering approximately 30 miles of river from [[Hereford]] to near [[St. David]]. The document describes modifications to original survey methods necessitated by the river's unique "ribboniferous" growth pattern, where broadleaf riparian trees grow in narrow ribbon-like bands rather than wide corridors. The inventory documented size class distribution of broadleaf riparian species and livestock browsing impacts, with 1826 sample points recorded for computer analysis.

## Background & Description

The [[San Pedro River Management Area]] study was designed to inventory wildlife and vegetation across defined habitat types. Original plans called for three distinct study areas with 12 transects sampling broadleaf riparian, mesquite, mesquite/grassland, and grassland habitats. [[Emlin Transects]] for broadleaf riparian areas were specified to be 400 feet wide and one-half mile long.

The vegetation inventory was conducted from May 5 to May 20, 1986, covering approximately 30 miles of river from Hereford to the end of the San Juan de las Boquillas y Nogales Land Grant near St. David. The biologist worked alone, necessitating methodological adaptations from the original Proposed San Pedro Inventory Methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[San Pedro River]] exhibits a distinctive "ribboniferous" growth pattern where broadleaf riparian trees grow in narrow, ribbon-like bands along the river rather than in wide corridors. This pattern results from specific topographic positioning: saplings and pole-class trees generally occur along lower banks, while mature trees concentrate along the border between middle and upper banks. The distance between saplings and mature trees commonly ranges from 500-1000 feet.

Only three broadleaf riparian areas meeting the 400-foot width criterion were found along the surveyed reach. These wider areas exist due to historical flood control work protecting the railroad grade. Aerial photographs confirmed the ribbon-like distribution pattern observed in field work.

Upper banks consist of either dense mesquite, open [[Sacaton Grassland]], or combinations thereof. The area around the [[Hereford Bridge]] is predominantly grassland with small mesquite trees; no area is 100% grassland.

## Key Findings

**Transect Establishment:**
- 12 transects established throughout the San Pedro River Management Area
- Three broadleaf riparian transects meeting standard size criteria (400 feet wide, 0.5 mile long)
- Two ribboniferous transects required extended length (longer than 0.5 mile) to compensate for narrowness
- Transects will serve dual purpose for herp and mammal inventories

**Vegetation Inventory Method:**
- 1826 sample points recorded along approximately 30 miles of river
- Sampling conducted at 60-pace intervals (360 feet) along river, with perpendicular transects at 20-pace intervals (120 feet) from lower to upper bank
- At each sample point: location recorded (lower/middle/upper bank), closest broadleaf riparian species identified, closest woody species identified
- Tenth-acre density plots established for broadleaf riparian trees

**Size Class Categories:**
- Seedlings: under 1 inch at base, susceptible to cattle browsing
- Sapling: 1-4 inches diameter
- Pole: 4-12 inches diameter
- Tree: 12-24 inches diameter
- Veteran: 24+ inches diameter

**Species Documentation:**
- For [[Mesquite]], [[Salt Cedar]], and [[Hackberry]]: basal diameter noted rather than complete counts
- Salt cedar occurrence recorded as single trees or dense thickets
- Other species recorded as present for frequency data
- Livestock browsing impacts on young trees documented

**Data Analysis Plans:**
- Computer analysis planned for entire river dataset
- Capability to analyze lower and middle bank communities separately
- Upper bank community excluded from detailed analysis (recorded only to define middle bank extent)

**Timeline:**
- Mammal and herp inventories scheduled to begin approximately two weeks from correspondence date
- Field trip scheduled for June 20, 1986 at BLM Administrative Site at [[Fairbank]] (0800 hours) for Technical Committee members
