# Annual Report: Gila Topminnow Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-57681-1

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-topminnow

## Executive Summary

This annual report, submitted by [[William J. Matter]] of the [[University Of Arizona]] to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] under endangered species subpermit PRT-57681-1, covers work on the [[Gila Topminnow]]. Planned field trapping in [[Cienega Creek]] did not take place because no graduate student was available. A captive population was lost in a pool failure, and the author reports that he no longer holds any Gila topminnow. A letter dated November 30, 1993 accompanies the report.

## Background & Description

- **Subpermit:** PRT-57681-1, issued under the [[Endangered Species Act]] framework administered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Principal investigator:** William J. Matter, Associate Professor, Wildlife and Fisheries Science, School of Renewable Natural Resources, University of Arizona, Tucson, AZ.
- **Target species:** Gila topminnow, a federally protected freshwater fish. The document does not give a scientific name, morphology or life-history details.
- **Reporting period:** Work described as "last year" in the report, with Gila topminnow collections made in 1993.
- **Addressee:** Acting Regional Director Kenneth R. Russell, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Albuquerque, New Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area was the marshy margins of [[Cienega Creek]]. The planned trapping method was the [[Mini-Minnow Trap]], which the author proposed to test for capturing Gila topminnow in these habitats.

Laboratory work used a captive population kept at the [[Arizona Cooperative Fish And Wildlife Research Unit]]. The population was used for laboratory [[Habitat Preference Testing]], in which topminnow preference was assessed under selected environmental conditions. The document does not report the results of these tests.

Source population: 25 fish collected from Cienega Creek in 1991. These produced a population of several hundred fish.

## Key Findings

1. **Field trapping was not completed.** The proposed test of mini-minnow traps in Cienega Creek marshy areas was not carried out because no graduate student could be assigned. Gila topminnow collections were made in 1993.

2. **Captive population was lost.** An outdoor above-ground pool holding the fish was damaged during a summer event, and its vinyl liner failed. Water drained from the pool and the author could not recover any fish. Rapid decomposition and predation by birds and other animals prevented preservation of dead fish for the university collection.

3. **No replacement planned.** The author is not requesting permission to replace the captive population. Mr. Forrest completed his M.S. thesis on Gila topminnow, which removed the need for the captive stock. The author is notifying the Service that he no longer holds Gila topminnow.

4. **Cooperation acknowledged.** The November 30, 1993 letter to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service thanks the agency for its cooperation in the research effort. The letter's text is partly illegible in the scan, so its full content could not be verified.
