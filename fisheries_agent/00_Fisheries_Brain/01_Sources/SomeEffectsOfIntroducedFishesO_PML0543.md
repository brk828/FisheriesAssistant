# Some Effects of Introduced Fishes on the Native Fish Fauna of Southern Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 *Copeia* paper by Deacon, Hubbs and Zerunian documents the establishment of several introduced fishes in the depauperate fish fauna of southern Nevada and assesses their effects on localized endemic species. The authors confirm successful introductions of two poeciliids and one [[Cichlid]], and report evidence that these fishes have reduced or threatened native populations. They recommend eradication efforts and stronger controls on the release of live bait and ornamental fishes.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Nevada has a sparse native fish fauna with many small, localized endemics, which makes it especially vulnerable to introductions (see [[Biological Invasion]]).
- **Prior records:** [[Miller and Alcorn 1946]] listed 28 attempted introductions to Nevada, 18 of them successful. [[La Rivers 1962]] listed 27 of 63 known Nevada fish species as introduced. The present authors add three more successful introductions, bringing the introduced fraction to nearly 45%.
- **Introduction pathways:** Most introductions were released from aquarium and bait sources. Earlier introductions involved large sport or food fishes, while recent ones were mainly small bait or ornamental species, which the authors consider more dangerous to endemics.
- **Species involved:** The paper focuses on [[Guppy]] (*Lebistes reticulatus*), [[Sailfin Molly]] (*Mollienesia latipinna*), [[Shortfin Molly]] (*M. mexicana*), [[Platy]] (*Xiphophorus maculatus*), [[Convict Cichlid]] (*Cichlasoma nigrofasciatum*), and [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), among others.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Native endemics at risk:**
- [[Poolfish]] (*Empetrichthys latos*), restricted to Manse Ranch. The authors describe it as the only remaining stock, with the other two subspecies' springs filled in.
- [[Ash Meadows Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), a local Rogers Spring race that the authors consider extinct.
- [[Preston Springfish]] (*Crenichthys baileyi*), which declined after guppies established at Preston Town Spring.
- [[Crenichthys Nevadae]], found at Duckwater and Lockes.

**Habitat interactions:** Introduced [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) occupied open, quiet water that *C. baileyi* previously used. At Moapa, the authors observed the two species occupying largely separate habitats. Vegetation removal at Manse Ranch (10 May to 24 July 1962) further reduced cover and primary productivity for young poolfish.

**Water and climate:** Preston Town Spring pools stayed fish-viable through winter, when nearby roadside pools were ice-covered. The authors judged conditions there to be well within the guppy's tolerance limits.

## Key Findings

1. **Confirmed establishment:**
   - Guppies at Preston Town Spring persisted for more than a year, with pregnant females and young present in February 1963.
   - Sailfin mollies were established at Rogers Spring and Blue Point Spring. The Blue Point Spring population is melanistic in all collected individuals.
   - Shortfin mollies, guppies and platies were taken at Blue Point Spring. Those stocks likely descend from an aquarium-fish rearing establishment that ceased operation in 1956 or 1957, so they maintained themselves for more than six years.
2. **Failed claim:** [[La Rivers 1962]] reported platies and swordtails established at Rogers Spring. Later collections (March 1961, February and March 1963) found no *Xiphophorus*, so only the sailfin molly was confirmed there. The authors suggest the report may refer to Blue Point Spring.
3. **Reduced native populations:**
   - At Preston, *C. baileyi* was far more abundant in Big Spring (225 fish, about 90 per trap-hour, in a 15-minute trap set) than in Town Spring with guppies, where fewer were caught.
   - Comparing springs with and without *Gambusia*, native fish catches were 10 and 32 per trap-hour in the spring with the introduced relative, against 62 and 90 per trap-hour in springs without it.
   - The authors conclude the data support the hypothesis that native fishes decline after exotic introductions.
4. **Manse Ranch decline:** Goldfish introduced in late fall 1961 were established by July 1962. Trapping success for the poolfish fell markedly after February 1963, and few adults were observed. The authors identify three possible causes: removal of 274 adults for studies over a two-year span, vegetation removal, and competition and predation by goldfish. They consider goldfish the most significant factor.
5. **Recommendations:**
   - Attempt to eradicate the fish fauna in Rogers Spring to prevent release of the [[Convict Cichlid]], which could damage the Lake Mead sport fishery.
   - Avoid introductions of live bait and exotic ornamental fishes whenever possible.
6. **Unassessed status:** The authors state that guppies, shortfin mollies and convict cichlids had not been recorded as established in the United States. They suspect established populations near Florida aquarium operations, and note a 1950 report of a related molly near Houston, Texas, with no later collections.
