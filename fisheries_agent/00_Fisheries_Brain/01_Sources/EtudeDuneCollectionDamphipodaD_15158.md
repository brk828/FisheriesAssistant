# Study of an Amphipoda Collection from the Azores with the Description of a New Species

#marine-biology #amphipoda #amphithoidae

## Executive Summary

This 1974 taxonomic paper by [[Emília de Oliveira Mateus]] and [[Odette Afonso]], published by the [[Institute of Zoology Dr. Augusto Nobre]] at the Faculty of Sciences of Porto, Portugal, reports on a small collection of littoral amphipod crustaceans from the [[Azores]]. The authors identified seven species previously recorded from the islands and described one new species, *Amphithoe (Pleonexes) pomboi* sp. n., named after collector [[Dalberto Pombo]]. The work also comments on the subgeneric status of *Pleonexes* and on the degree of endemism in the Azorean amphipod fauna.

## Background & Description

The paper was supported by Project PB4 of the Instituto de Alta Cultura and was presented at the VI Congresso Latinoamericano de Zoologia (Mexico, September–October 1974). The material was collected and sent by Dalberto Pombo, and comes from a beach and a grotto with an inland lake located close to the sea. The authors note that a second collection, taken at depths of 17–32 m, would be studied in a later paper.

Specimens were dissected and mounted in polyvinyl lactophenol stained with lignin pink, and drawings were made with a camera lucida. The description of the new species follows the standard gammaridean character set: head lobes, coxal plates, telson, antennae, mouthparts (mandibles, maxillae, maxillipeds), gnathopods, pereiopods, pleopods and uropods.

The front matter of the document is an exchange list of the Institute's publications, containing about 126 numbered titles from 1940 to 1974. These cover arachnids, isopods, cave fauna, Lepidoptera, birds, foraminifera, and fishery studies, including work on Portuguese sardine (*Sardina pilchardus*) and the Lernaeocera copepod parasite of hake.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Littoral and cave habitats:** The collection came from beach and cave habitats near the sea.
- **Association with sea turtles:** *Podocerus chelonophilus* was collected from the sternal shield (plastron) of a sea turtle. The species is known to live on marine turtles.
- **Floating substrates:** *Hyale grimaldii* is reported in the literature from algae on sea turtles and floating wreckage. In this study it was found on a coconut stranded on a beach at Santa Maria.
- **Dispersal:** The authors note that amphipods are easily transported, which may explain why many Azorean species are also known from other regions.
- **Insularity:** Minor morphological variations found in the material may reflect insularity and the early stages of evolutionary differentiation, possibly leading to speciation, as the authors suggest for *A. (P.) pomboi*.
- **Biogeography of the subgenus:** The distribution of *Amphithoe (Pleonexes)* is discontinuous. The authors attribute this partly to collecting gaps, and note that the subgenus has few species. The first described species, *A. (P.) gammaroides*, has the widest range, from Norway to Algeria and Tunisia.

## Key Findings

1. **Seven known species identified** from the Azores collection:
   - *Pseudoniphargus africanus* (Gammaridae): two young specimens from Grotte des Figueiras, Santa Maria, plus one very young specimen. Its Azorean record (Pico and Faial) was previously given by [[Dahl]] (1958). The authors note a difference in the uropod III peduncle-to-outer-ramus ratio (1/6, versus 1/1 in the original description). Portuguese mainland specimens show the same 1/6 ratio. The authors did not consider this grounds for a subspecies or new species yet, and planned further study.
   - *Orchestia gammarellus* (Talitridae): collected at several Santa Maria localities, including S. Lourenço, Almagreira, and a site at 4 m altitude. Specimens were typical, with no special notes needed.
   - *Hyale nilssoni stebbingi* (Hyalidae): treated as a subspecies of *H. nilssoni* rather than a full species. It is smaller, with a longer and more setose antenna II, a longer and narrower gnathopod I, and different pereiopod setation.
   - *Hyale pontica*: males showed two spines on the palmar margin of gnathopod II, where the descriptions consulted report one. A further strong, striated spine was found on the inner face of the gnathopod I propodite.
   - *Hyale perieri*: no differences from the literature descriptions were found.
   - *Hyale grimaldii*: 10 specimens (6 males, 4 females) from Santa Maria, 12 November 1967. Males measured 5 mm.
   - *Podocerus chelonophilus* (Podoceridae): 3 males and 6 females from a sea turtle plastron, Santa Maria, 26 June 1966. The male gnathopod II palmar margin differs slightly from published descriptions.
2. **One new species described:** *Amphithoe (Pleonexes) pomboi* sp. n. The holotype is a male, 6.9 mm long, collected at Santa Maria on 20 April 1969 (Institute catalogue no. 286a). Paratypes are held in the Institute's collection. Material included 3 males and 7 ovigerous females from April 1964 and 2 ovigerous females, 1 non-ovigerous female and 5 males from April 1969.
3. **Diagnostic features of *A. (P.) pomboi*:**
   - Telson triangular, with two hook-shaped spines turned dorsally.
   - Pereiopods V–VII markedly dilated.
   - Two striated spines on the palmar margin of gnathopods I and II, a character shared with *A. (P.) aptos*, *A. (P.) poipu*, and *A. (P.) bicuspis*.
   - Antenna I flagellum of 17–22 articles.
4. **Subgeneric status:** Following [[Barnard]] (1970), the authors treat *Pleonexes* as a subgenus of *Amphithoe*. They recognise five other valid species in *A. (Pleonexes)* and exclude *Pleonexes ferox*, which they place in the nominotypical subgenus as *A. (Amphithoe) ferox*.
5. **Endemism:** The authors report some endemism in the Azorean amphipod fauna. They note, however, that it is less pronounced than the islands' isolation would suggest, likely because amphipods are easily transported.
6. **Taxonomic decisions:** The authors keep *Amphithoidae* and *Amphithoe* (not *Ampithoidae* and *Ampithoe*), following reasons given by [[Ruffo]] (1969). They treat *Hyale nilssoni stebbingi* as a subspecies because the term "variety" has no nomenclatural status.
