## Executive Summary

This 1993 field memorandum documents a multi-agency "razorback roundup" conservation effort on Lake Mohave, targeting the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Over a six-day period (14-19 March 1993), 20 personnel from five agencies and organizations collected a combined total of **750 razorback suckers** from two camps operating in different reaches of the reservoir/river system. The operation included tagging (PIT, with legacy Floy and Carlin tags noted), morphometric measurement, disease screening, and recapture analysis to monitor population status, movement, and health of this imperiled catostomid.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large-bodied, long-lived catostomid fish native to the Colorado River Basin, listed as endangered due to habitat alteration (impoundment, flow regulation) and non-native species introductions. [[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir on the Colorado River formed by Davis Dam, straddling the Arizona-Nevada border, and represents one of the last strongholds of wild and augmented razorback sucker populations.

This report describes an annual monitoring and stock-augmentation verification effort ("roundup") conducted by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker FRO) in coordination with:
- Pinetop, San Carlos, and Flagstaff Fish Resource Offices (FRO)
- Pinetop Fish Health Center
- Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge
- Arizona Game and Fish Department
- Bureau of Land Management
- Colorado River Indian Tribes
- Hualapai Nation
- Region 2 (Albuquerque) and Region 6 (Denver) USFWS offices
- Ecoplan Associates Inc. (private contractor)

Two field camps were deployed:
1. **Upriver camp ("El Dorado camp")** — supervised by Brent Bristow, six personnel, based at river mile 37, working river miles 35–45. This effort also supported the Arizona Game and Fish [[Willow Beach Trout Study]].
2. **Lower camp** — location not explicitly named, focused on the reservoir proper, using trammel nets and electrofishing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use**: Razorback suckers were found in both riverine (river mile 35-45) and lacustrine (reservoir) habitats, indicating the species utilizes a range of habitat types within the regulated Colorado River/[[Lake Mohave]] system.
- **Movement/dispersal**: Three hatchery-raised razorback suckers released at [[Yuma Cove]] in November 1992 were recaptured in the upriver reach, having moved **15–24 miles** from their release site within approximately 4 months, demonstrating post-stocking dispersal capability.
- **Sympatric non-native species**: Disease and health assessments incidentally sampled several non-native gamefish species co-occurring in the system, including [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill Sunfish]], and [[Striped Bass]] — highlighting the mixed native/non-native fish community pressuring razorback sucker recovery.
- **Mortality observations**: Four razorback sucker mortalities were recovered, three of which were found floating (not netted), suggesting natural or handling-related die-off outside of active capture gear. One mortality exhibited a tumor associated with the air bladder, collected for pathological examination.
- **Sampling methodology**: Combination of trammel netting (multiple nets fished on a 6-hour rotation: first light, 1200, 1800, 2400 hrs) and electrofishing (both nocturnal 2000-2400 hrs and daytime, condition-dependent) was used to maximize capture across diel activity patterns and habitat.

## Key Findings

### Catch and Recapture Summary
| Camp | Total Razorbacks Captured | Recaptures | Recapture Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upriver (El Dorado) | 225 | 13 | 5.7% |
| Lower camp | 525 | 98 | 18.5% |
| **Combined Total** | **750** | **111** | **14.8%** |

- Of the 111 total recaptures, the proportion attributable to prior years vs. current-year recapture was not determined at time of reporting.
- Three recaptured fish bore legacy tags from previous studies: two [[Floy Tag|Floy tags]] and one [[Carlin Tag|Carlin tag]], indicating some individuals have been tracked across multiple years/studies.

### Stocked Fish Recapture (Yuma Cove Cohort)
- 3 fish from the Yuma Cove release (November 1992) recaptured in upriver reach.
- Size range: 361–380 mm total length.
- One individual was a ripe, tuberculate male, indicating reproductive maturity/readiness within ~4 months of release.
- Movement of 15-24 miles from release site confirms measurable post-stocking dispersal.

### Standard Data Collected per Fish
- PIT tag scan (existing tag check)
- New PIT tag application if untagged
- Weight
- Total length (TL)

### Mortality & Pathology
- 4 total mortalities recovered (1 untagged, 3 PIT-tagged).
- All 3 tagged mortalities recovered as floaters, not in active gear — PIT tags retrieved, carcasses skeletonized (likely for voucher/reference collection).
- One mortality had an air-bladder-associated tumor, excised and submitted for further examination by [[Carol Shanks]] (Pinetop Fish Health Center).

### Fish Health Assessment
- Conducted by Carol Shanks (Pinetop Fish Health Center).
- ~60 fish examined total.
- Necropsies performed on several gamefish.
- Bacteriological smears taken specifically from razorback suckers.
- Non-native species also examined for disease: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill Sunfish]], [[Striped Bass]].

### Operational Notes
- Boats provided by Pinetop, San Carlos, and Parker AZFRO offices, and Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge.
- Effort logistics required late evening/early morning shifts to align with fish activity and netting schedules.
- Trip organized as a collaborative, multi-agency conservation monitoring exercise, reflecting broader Colorado River endangered fish recovery program efforts (cf. [[Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]]).
