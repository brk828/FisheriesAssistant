# Station Profile of McKinney Lake National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Moronidae #fisheries-management #aquaculture

## Executive Summary

The [[McKinney Lake National Fish Hatchery]] is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Hoffman, North Carolina, established in 1937 under Executive Order No. 7775 pursuant to Title II of the National Industrial Recovery Act of 1933. Originally constructed to acquire 500 acres of land, the hatchery began operations with 22 earthen ponds (0.8 acre each) and has since evolved into a 412-acre facility with 23 earthen ponds, a 70-acre lake, and associated water control infrastructure. The station's mission has shifted over time from rearing warmwater sportfish species ([[Largemouth Bass]], catfish, sunfish) to a primary focus on [[Striped Bass]] production for restoration efforts along the Mid-Atlantic Coast and within the [[Chesapeake Bay]] watershed. The hatchery also supports mitigation of fishery resources impacted by Federal water development projects in North Carolina.

## Background & Description

This document is a station/administrative profile rather than a biological or morphological study; no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data on the reared species are provided. Species referenced (Striped Bass, Largemouth Bass, Bluegill) are managed as production stocks rather than characterized biologically within this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water Source:** The hatchery's primary water supply is [[McKinney Lake]], which drains into [[Hitchcock Creek]], a tributary of the [[Pee Dee River]] — situating the facility within the greater Atlantic slope drainage system.
- **Habitat Infrastructure:** Consists of 23 earthen ponds, 16 tanks, a display pool, a 70-acre reservoir lake, a dam, water control structures, and a concrete flumeline system, providing controlled freshwater rearing habitat analogous to natural pond/lake ecosystems.
- **Target Restoration Waters:** Reared fish are distributed for stocking into:
  - Federal waters (managed directly by USFWS)
  - [[Chesapeake Bay]] waters (primary striped bass restoration target)
  - State waters of North Carolina, South Carolina, Maryland, and Virginia
- **Ecological Role:** The hatchery functions as a **stock enhancement and mitigation facility**, supplementing depleted or impaired fish populations resulting from Federal water development projects (e.g., dam construction, habitat alteration) and supporting recreational/ecological restoration on Service lands and military installations.

## Key Findings

- **Historical Shift in Mission:** The hatchery transitioned from general warmwater sportfish culture (largemouth bass, catfish, sunfish) to specialized [[Striped Bass]] restoration production, reflecting a strategic realignment toward anadromous species recovery in the [[Chesapeake Bay]] system.
- **Facility Growth:** Expanded from 22 ponds (1937) to 23 ponds plus a 70-acre lake and expanded infrastructure (412 total acres) by the time of this profile.
- **Distribution Trends (Fiscal Years 1990–1992):**

  | Fiscal Year | Federal (FWS) | Others | Chesapeake Bay | State Waters | Total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1990 | 55,000 | 94,500 | 146,880 | 507,250 | 803,630 |
  | 1991 | 51,000 | 41,000 | 209,466 | 189,500 | 490,966 |
  | 1992 | 45,000 | 21,900 | 106,066 | 255,402 | 428,368 |

  - Total distribution numbers **declined significantly** from 1990 to 1992 (803,630 → 428,368), a decrease of roughly 47%.
  - Chesapeake Bay allocations fluctuated notably, peaking in 1991 (209,466) before dropping in 1992.
  - State waters distribution showed the largest overall decline, dropping from 507,250 (1990) to 255,402 (1992).

- **Funding & Staffing (1991–1994):**

  | Category | 1991 (Actual) | 1992 (Actual) | 1993 (Allocation) | 1994 (Estimated) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Operations ($K) | 175.0 | 180.4 | 178.1 | 185.2 |
  | Maintenance ($K) | — | 24.0 | 53.0 | 87.0 |
  | FTEs | 4.0 | 4.0 | 4.0 | 4.0 |

  - Staffing remained constant at 4.0 FTEs across all years despite increasing maintenance funding, suggesting rising infrastructure/upkeep costs without corresponding personnel growth.
  - Maintenance budget increased substantially (24.0 → 87.0), potentially reflecting aging infrastructure (dam, water control structures) requiring greater capital investment.

- **Geographic Distribution Scope:** Stocking efforts span four states (NC, SC, MD, VA), emphasizing the hatchery's regional role in the [[Mid-Atlantic Coast]] fisheries restoration network.

- **Legislative/Administrative Origin:** Notable as one of the New Deal-era conservation infrastructure projects, tracing authority to the National Industrial Recovery Act of 1933 — linking the hatchery's establishment to broader Depression-era natural resource development programs.
