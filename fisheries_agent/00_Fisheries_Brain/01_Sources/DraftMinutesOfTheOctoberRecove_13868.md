# Cui-ui Recovery Team Meeting Minutes - October 23, 1989

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #conservation #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

Minutes from the October 23, 1989 [[Cui-ui]] Recovery Team meeting in Reno, Nevada, documenting critical discussions about recovery plan development, hybridization concerns, and management strategies for this endangered sucker species. The team addressed timeline extensions, leadership transitions, and urgent recommendations for preventing hybridization with [[Lake Tahoe Sucker]]s at the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]].

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) is an endangered member of the [[Catostomidae]] family endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. The Recovery Team consists of five members: Thomas Strekal (team leader), Wendell Minckley, Gary Scoppettone, Gary Vinyard, and Paul Wagner, with Chester Buchanan serving as the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] liaison.

The team was originally instructed to use revised guidelines from 1984 with an abbreviated format, but the Assistant Regional Director for Fish and Wildlife Enhancement redirected them to use draft 1989 guidelines with the classical recovery plan format. All correspondence was requested to route through the Assistant Regional Director's office, though direct communication with the Regional Director remains available for urgent issues.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recovery of [[Cui-ui]] is complicated by multiple interacting factors including the species' biological and genetic characteristics, habitat requirements, and water availability. The over-riding issue identified is securing the genome of the species. The establishment and maintenance of a minimum viable population is considered essential for recovery, though defining such a population remains complex.

Hybridization between [[Cui-ui]] and [[Lake Tahoe Sucker]]s represents a critical threat. Physical characteristics of many individuals suggest hybrid presence, though electrophoretic analysis had not yet confirmed this at the time of the meeting. Introgression of the [[Cui-ui]] genome through hybridization may jeopardize the continued existence of the species.

The [[Truckee River]] spawning runs and [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] serve as critical management points for the species. Two year classes from 1963 and 1969 were specifically identified as important cohorts for management purposes.

## Key Findings

**Recovery Plan Timeline**: The team determined that completing the first draft recovery plan by January 31, 1990 was not possible due to the hybrid issue, complexity of modeling, and related details. Additional information about hybrids is essential to developing the plan. The team agreed on a more realistic completion date of January 31, 1991, and the team leader would request this extension from the Regional Director.

**Leadership Transition**: Team leader Thomas Strekal was scheduled for temporary assignment (30 days starting mid-November) to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] in Salt Lake City, Utah to coordinate development of an EIS for [[Lake Powell]], likely leading to permanent assignment by February. The team consensus was that it was premature for Strekal to step down from leadership or resign from the team.

**Electrophoretic Analysis**: Dr. Peter Brussard from [[University of Nevada - Reno]] would not have his laboratory re-assembled in time to complete electrophoretic analysis by December 1. He planned to use facilities at [[University of California at Davis]] or [[Montana State University]] instead.

**Urgent Management Recommendations**: The team recommended immediate establishment of a task force by the Regional Director to oversee spawning run management at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] for the upcoming spring. Since electrophoretic analysis had not confirmed or denied the presence of [[Cui-ui]] × [[Lake Tahoe Sucker]] hybrids, their existence must be assumed based on physical characteristics.

**Hybrid Prevention Measures**: The team recommended preventing purported hybrids from spawning until the extent of hybridization is determined, mechanisms promoting hybridization are understood, and a long-term management program can be established. Proposed interdiction measures included: (1) an electric barrier across the delta to prevent possible upstream passage, and (2) culling hybrids at the fishway entrance, allowing only individuals from the 1963 and 1969 year classes to proceed up the fishway.

**Hatchery Fail-Safe System**: Hatchery rearing was identified as a "fail safe" system for maintaining the population's genetic purity and variation. The team recommended collecting approximately 500 females and numerous males from the 1969 year class from the fishway each year for hatchery spawning and rearing. Only a few hundred eggs should be collected from each female to produce at least a small, genetically pure year class nearly every year.

**Information Gathering**: The team agreed to continue gathering essential information over the next 6-8 months for developing the recovery plan. One approach discussed was interviewing individuals who can provide useful information for developing recovery strategies and defining recovery.
