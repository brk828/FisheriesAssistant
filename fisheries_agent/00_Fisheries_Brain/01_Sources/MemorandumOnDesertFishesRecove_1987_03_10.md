## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) announcing a **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** meeting held April 1–2, 1987, at Kofa National Wildlife Refuge headquarters in Yuma, Arizona. The meeting's primary purpose was to begin drafting a **federal recovery plan** for the endangered **Desert Pupfish ([[Cyprinodon macularius]])**. The memo assigns specific research and reporting tasks to team members covering historic and present distribution, causes of decline, limiting factors, threats, captive propagation efforts, and species biology/taxonomy across Arizona, California, and Mexico. No primary morphological, ecological, or population data are presented in the text itself; rather, the document outlines the administrative framework and task assignments for compiling such data into a formal recovery plan (Parts I and II).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While explicit ecological data are not detailed in the memo, the assignments imply the following ecological framework for [[Desert Pupfish]]:
- **Historic and present distribution** in Arizona (assigned to Dean Hendrickson and Bill Kepner, respectively).
- **Distribution in California**, including historic and present ranges (assigned to Ed Lorentzen).
- **Distribution in Mexico**, past and present (assigned to Lourdes Juarez Romero).
- **Limiting factors and future threats** were to be addressed for each region, suggesting the species faces region-specific pressures across its range in the [[Sonoran Desert]] aquatic systems.
- **Captive propagation/conservation efforts**: Jim Brooks was tasked with reporting on culture efforts at the **Dexter National Fish Hatchery (Dexter NFH)**, including considerations for future broodfish stock management — indicating active ex-situ conservation programs for the species.

## Key Findings
- The **Desert Pupfish ([[Cyprinodon macularius]])** was the central focus species for the 1987 [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting, indicating its status as a federally recognized species of conservation concern.
- The recovery planning process was structured in two parts:
  - **Part I**: Species background — historic/present distribution, reasons for decline, limiting factors, and future threats (by region: Arizona, California, Mexico).
  - **Part II**: Recovery strategy — objectives, a stepdown outline, and narrative recovery actions, including potential **downlisting criteria**.
- Conservation efforts documented include an active **captive breeding/culture program** at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], with explicit attention to future broodstock management — a key tool for preventing extinction of isolated desert fish populations.
- The species' range spans multiple political jurisdictions (**Arizona, California, and Mexico**), reflecting the fragmented, isolated nature of desert spring/marsh habitats typical of pupfish distribution in the [[Sonoran Desert]] region.
- Literature citations were explicitly required for all assignments, indicating the recovery plan was intended to be grounded in existing scientific literature on the species' biology, ecology, and taxonomy (per [[W.L. Minckley]]'s assignment).
- This memorandum reflects broader 1980s-era U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service efforts to formalize recovery planning for imperiled desert fishes under the **Endangered Species Act**, situating the Desert Pupfish within the wider context of [[Desert Fishes Conservation]] initiatives in the American Southwest.
