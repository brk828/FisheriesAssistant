# Movements of Inland Anglers Within California in 1960

#ichthyology #inland-fisheries #fisheries-management #Salmonidae #Moronidae #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This administrative report from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] describes the spatial movements of successful inland anglers throughout California during 1960, based on postal card survey data. The study analyzed angler travel patterns and regional catch contributions for four major fish groups: [[Trout]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Black Bass]], and [[Catfish]]. Trout anglers exhibited the most extensive travel patterns, while striped bass fishing remained highly localized to the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] system. The report documents significant changes in fishing patterns since 1948, particularly increased catches in [[Southern California]] due to extensive stocking programs with catchable-sized trout. Regional analysis divided the state into five administrative regions and six residence areas to track angler interchange and catch distribution.

## Background & Description

The 1960 postal card angling survey provided data on angler movements, catch locations, and success rates across California's inland waters. Respondents' answers were hand-tabulated and categorized by: (1) kind of fish reported, (2) county of residence, (3) county of catch, and (4) number of fish reported in each county. Counties of catch were assigned to five administrative regions (Regions 1-5, with Region 5 subdivided into 5a and 5b), while counties of residence were grouped into six residence areas based on population centers: [[Northern California]], [[Sacramento]], [[San Francisco]], [[San Joaquin]], [[South Central California]], and [[Southern California]].

The study acknowledges that catch estimates include an unknown degree of exaggeration, but assumes this bias is consistent across all angler categories. The report differs slightly from the concurrent 1960 inland angling survey in that anglers were counted once for every region fished (rather than once per fish species), resulting in minor variations in total estimates.

**Trout**: California's most important inland sport fish, taken from waters ranging from naturally reproducing populations to entirely stocked fisheries. The 1960 survey sampled 2,086 successful trout anglers.

**Striped Bass**: A highly valuable fishery largely confined to the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] system and [[San Francisco Bay]] area, absorbing approximately 18% of total statewide inland angling effort despite its localized character.

**Black Bass**: Increasing in importance with construction of low-elevation reservoirs. The fishery showed considerable shifts in county rankings between 1948 and 1960, reflecting popularity of newer waters like [[Lake Berryessa]], [[Folsom Lake]], and [[Lake Isabella]].

**Catfish**: Ranked second only to trout in total catch among California's inland game fish, but did not attract large numbers of anglers from distant areas.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report documents a transitional period in California inland fisheries management, reflecting both natural distribution patterns and anthropogenic modifications through reservoir construction and stocking programs. The extensive stocking of catchable-sized trout by the Department and private agencies fundamentally altered fishing patterns, particularly in [[Southern California]] (Region 5a), where nearly all trout taken were planted fish.

Regional fisheries characteristics varied substantially:

- **Region 1** (Northern California): Contributed 14.3% of statewide trout catch (4,070,000 fish estimated)
- **Region 2** (Sacramento area): Contributed 21.8% of trout catch (6,200,000 fish) and dominated catfish production with nearly 50% of state total (4,490,000 fish)
- **Region 3** (San Francisco Bay area): Major striped bass region alongside Region 2; together these regions contributed 98.6% of state striped bass catch (2,780,000 fish)
- **Region 4** (San Joaquin): Contributed 20.9% of trout catch (5,930,000 fish)
- **Region 5a** (Southern California counties): Contributed 14.7% of trout catch (4,180,000 fish), virtually all caught by local anglers
- **Region 5b** ([[Mono County]] and [[Inyo County]]): Contributed 19.1% of trout catch (5,420,000 fish), with 75% taken by southern California anglers who comprised 81% of anglers fishing there

The [[Colorado River]] received its first striped bass plants in April 1959, representing expansion of this fishery into Region 5.

## Key Findings

**Trout Angler Movement Patterns:**
- Trout anglers traveled far more extensively than anglers targeting other species
- Considerable interchange occurred between all six residence areas and five administrative regions, with one major exception: angler movement into Region 5a (southern California) was virtually absent from outside areas
- Southern California residence area anglers (42% of all trout anglers) took 36% of statewide trout catch, with almost 15% of state total caught within southern California itself
- San Francisco residence area anglers (23% of all trout anglers) took slightly more than 20% of statewide catch
- The basic pattern of trout angler movement in 1960 remained virtually unchanged from 1948, with one notable exception: in 1960, southern California counties (Region 5a) contributed more trout to southern California anglers than any other single region, whereas in 1948 [[Inyo County]] and [[Mono County]] (Region 5b) were the top contributors
- The increased catch in southern California counties is attributed to extensive planting of catchable-sized trout by the Department and private agencies since 1948
- The percentage of southern California anglers fishing in southern California counties remained nearly identical: 40.4% in 1948 vs. 41.1% in 1960

**Top Trout Counties (1960 ranking):**
1. [[Mono County]]: 8,137 fish reported (251 reports)
2. [[Inyo County]]: 4,773 fish reported (165 reports)
3. [[San Bernardino County]]: 3,829 fish reported (133 reports)
4. [[Fresno County]]: 3,332 fish reported (106 reports)
5. [[Tuolumne County]]: 3,273 fish reported (84 reports)

**Striped Bass Fishery Characteristics:**
- Highly localized: over 97% of successful striped bass anglers fished in Regions 2 and 3, which together contributed 98.6% of total catch (estimated 2,780,000 fish)
- Despite localized character, absorbed substantial statewide effort (~18% of inland fishing days)
- Limited fisheries also found in Region 4 (slightly more than 1% of state catch) and newly established in Region 5 (Colorado River, first planted April 1959)
- Attracted few anglers from outside the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] and [[San Francisco Bay]] area

**Top Striped Bass Counties:**
1. [[Contra Costa County]]: 1,163 fish reported (83 reports)
2. [[Sacramento County]]: 993 fish reported (93 reports)
3. [[Marin County]]: 668 fish reported (65 reports)

**Black Bass Fishery Dynamics:**
- Increasing importance correlated with construction of low-elevation reservoirs
- Considerable shift in county rankings between 1948 and 1960, reflecting popularity of newer fishing waters
- Most black bass anglers fished in regions within or adjacent to their residence areas
- Notable exception: nearly 22% of bass anglers in Region 4 were from southern California
- Fairly good black bass fisheries found within each of the five regions

**Top Black Bass Counties (1960):**
1. [[Lake County]]: 2,523 fish reported (82 reports) - ranked 4th in 1948
2. [[Napa County]]: 1,457 fish reported (56 reports)
3. [[San Diego County]]: 1,258 fish reported (46 reports) - ranked 1st in 1948
4. [[Sacramento County]]: 872 fish reported (35 reports)
5. [[Kern County]]: 702 fish reported (30 reports)

**Catfish Fishery Patterns:**
- Ranked second only to trout in total catch among California inland game fish
- Did not attract large numbers of anglers from distant areas, unlike trout
- Nearly 50% of catfish caught in 1960 were taken in Region 2 (estimated 4,490,000 fish, 48.7% of state total)
- Southern California anglers reported catches in each of the five regions

**Top Catfish Counties:**
1. [[San Joaquin County]]: 6,333 fish reported (82 reports)
2. [[Lake County]]: 2,798 fish reported (60 reports)
3. [[Contra Costa County]]: 2,421 fish reported (57 reports)

**Methodological Notes:**
- Estimated total catch obtained by multiplying reported catch by 419.83
- Boat anglers fishing waters bordering multiple counties likely listed launch county as county of catch
- Some respondents designated areas in general terms ("Delta", "San Joaquin River", "Sacramento River") rather than specific counties
- Catch estimates include unknown degree of exaggeration, assumed consistent across angler categories
