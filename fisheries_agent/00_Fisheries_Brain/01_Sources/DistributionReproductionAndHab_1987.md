# Distribution, Reproduction, and Habitat Use of the Razorback Sucker in the Green River, Utah, 1979-1986

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Xyrauchen-texanus #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This study by Harold M. Tyus (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) represents the largest body of field-collected data on the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in its native riverine habitat, drawn from electrofishing, seining, trammel netting, and radiotelemetry conducted in the [[Green River]] basin (Utah/Colorado) from 1979–1986. A total of 323 adult razorback suckers were captured, with distribution concentrated in flat-water sections of the upper Green River; none were found in white-water canyons (Split Mountain, Desolation, Gray canyons). Three spawning reaches were identified based on 101 fish in breeding condition, and larval razorback suckers were collected downstream of these reaches in 1984, confirming successful spawning. However, **no juveniles were collected across the study period**, indicating negligible recruitment. The population is characterized as small, slow-growing, and rarer than the co-occurring endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). The authors conclude that without intervention, the species faces functional extirpation from the wild as aging adults die without replacement.

## Background & Description

- **Total Length (TL) range:** 426–608 mm (N = 323 captured adults)
- **Sexual dimorphism:** Females significantly longer and heavier than males (Student's *t*, P < 0.01)
  - Ripe males: mean TL = 503 mm (N = 37)
  - Ripe females: mean TL = 544 mm (N = 23)
- **Length-frequency distribution:** Unimodal — not useful for distinguishing age classes; all captured fish assumed to be adults.
- **Comparative size (Lower Colorado River Basin populations):** Larger, older individuals reported — males to 640 mm TL, females to 740 mm TL ([[Lake Mohave]] population, Minckley 1983).
- **Age estimates (Green River specimens):**
  - 505-mm specimen: 12 annular rings via vertebral examination (>12 years old)
  - 560-mm specimen: 19 years old via otolith reading (University of Michigan)
  - Contrasts with reported ages of 24–44 years for [[Lake Mohave]] fish (McCarthy 1986)
- **Growth rate:** Extremely slow — 2.2 mm/year based on 39 recaptured fish over 1–8 years (Student's *t*, P < 0.01), consistent with growth rates observed in lower Colorado River reservoir populations.
- **Larvae:** Identified at 10.6–13.6 mm length via comparison with cultured protolarvae/mesolarvae at the Larval Fish Laboratory, Colorado State University.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat & Distribution
- Preferentially occupies **flat-water mainstem sections** of the Green and lower Yampa rivers; center of distribution between the mouth of the [[Duchesne River]] and the mouth of the [[Yampa River]].
- **Absent from white-water canyon habitats** (Split Mountain Canyon, Desolation Canyon, Gray Canyon) within [[Dinosaur National Monument]].
- Six fish collected in lower Green River sections; overall low relative abundance compared to sympatric species (see Key Findings).

### Spawning Habitat
- Ripe fish captured over **coarse sand substrate**, with some near gravel/cobble bars.
- Water temperature at capture: 10–18°C (mean ~15°C; 15.5°C in 1986; 16°C for ripe females in 1984).
- Additional ripe females captured in warm (17–19°C) **flooded bottomlands** with variable substrate (vegetation, sand, silt).
- Three identified spawning reaches:
  1. Island and Echo Parks (Green River, DNM) including lower [[Yampa River]] (1 km)
  2. Jensen area (Ashley Creek to Split Mountain) — produced 73% of ripe fish (N = 60)
  3. Ouray area (Green River, including lower [[Duchesne River]])
  - Two additional tuberculate fish captured in [[Labyrinth Canyon]].

### Non-breeding Habitat (Radiotelemetry, N = 6 fish)
- Main channel habitats in the Green and Duchesne rivers.
- Depths ranged 0.6–3.4 m; mean depth 1.3–1.5 m; mean velocity 0.3–0.4 m/s.
- Seasonal shift: shoreline runs in spring → **midchannel sand bars** in summer (unconsolidated coarse sand with dips/ridges), depths <2 m, velocities ~0.5 m/s.
- Staging areas (e.g., Ashley Creek/Green River eddy junction) distinct from spawning sites; substrate too silty for spawning at staging sites.

### Movement Patterns
- 61 recaptures (21% of captured fish); 39 recaptured 1–8 years post-tagging.
- 24 of 43 non-local recaptures were sedentary (<10 km movement).
- 28 fish were more mobile, averaging 59.3 km movement (range 13–206 km); extreme cases: 192 km in 4 years and 206 km in 5 years.
- Documented long-distance round-trip: 136 km downstream (Island Park DNM → Duchesne River, 1978–1982) followed by 130 km return migration (1982–1986).
- 21 tagged fish recaptured at two or more suspected spawning areas, suggesting inter-annual and intra-seasonal movement between spawning reaches.
- Radio-tagged fish (1980) showed localized movement (~6 km) into the lower Duchesne River, consistent with staging/spawning behavior.

### Community Context / Threats
- Co-occurs with the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] and abundant [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*).
- Introduced predators, notably [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), suspected to prey on larvae and suppress recruitment.
- Post-1962 [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] impoundment implicated as a potential driver of reduced recruitment via habitat alteration and thermal regime changes.

## Key Findings

1. **Extreme rarity:** Razorback sucker catch rates were far lower than co-occurring species — ratio to flannelmouth sucker was 1:355 (standardized sampling) and 1:32 (spring spawning-season sampling). Total and spring catches equaled only 1.8% and 33%, respectively, of Colorado Squawfish catches, indicating the razorback sucker is even rarer than this officially endangered species.

2. **No evidence of recruitment:** Despite confirmed spawning (101 ripe fish; larvae in 31% of 42 shoreline larval samples, N = 33 larvae of 1,085 total specimens), **no juveniles were captured** at any point during the 1979–1986 study, strongly suggesting a recruitment bottleneck.

3. **Interannual spawning variability:** 1984 and 1986 showed heavy spawning activity (55% and 58.7% of captured fish were ripe, N = 163); 1983 and 1985 showed minimal spawning activity with no ripe fish detected.

4. **Sex ratio:** Male:female ratio among ripe fish (all years combined) was 1.61:1.

5. **Thermal sensitivity hypothesis:** Recorded spawning temperatures (15–15.5°C average) fall below the 20°C optimum for embryo hatching success identified by Marsh (1985), who documented complete embryo mortality at 5–10°C and significantly reduced hatch at 15°C — suggesting river thermal regimes (potentially altered by [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]) may impair reproductive success.

6. **Population trajectory:** Combination of low abundance, extremely slow growth (2.2 mm/yr), advanced adult age (12–19+ years), and apparent zero recruitment led the author to conclude the Green River population — likely the largest surviving riverine stock of the species — faces eventual extirpation absent intervention.

7. **Staging vs. spawning habitat differentiation:** Fish congregate in specific non-spawning "staging" habitats (e.g., Ashley Creek confluence eddy) distinct from actual spawning substrates, suggesting a two-phase reproductive habitat use strategy.
