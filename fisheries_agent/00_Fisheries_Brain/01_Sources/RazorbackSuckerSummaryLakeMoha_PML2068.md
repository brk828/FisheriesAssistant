# Razorback Sucker Summary: Lake Mohave, AZ-NV (March 2011)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This March 2011 summary by [[Mansfield & Associates LLC]] (scanned, partly illegible) reports the adult [[Razorback Sucker]] population estimate for [[Lake Mohave]] and the results of the March 2011 [[Roundup]], in which 194 fish were collected and analyzed. The population estimate uses a [[Mark-Recapture Analysis]] approach, and the document gives the 2011 estimate alongside a 2009/2010 estimate. Most fish captured in 2011 were [[Hatchery Repatriate]]s, not wild adults. Numeric values below are reproduced as legible in the scan; values flagged as uncertain could not be reliably read.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a fish whose Lake Mohave population is monitored through mark-recapture sampling. The document covers two population estimates:

- **Adult estimate (Chapman-modified Petersen method**, Ricker 1975): a field-data-based estimate that covers the whole Lake Mohave population.
- **Single-census estimate (modified Petersen method**, Seber 1973): a 2011 estimate adjusted to exclude fish that were tagged and released in March 2010 and recaptured, which would otherwise bias the count.

Fish were marked with [[PIT Tags]] (passive integrated transponder tags), and the recovery of these tags determined which fish could be tracked. Collecting agencies included the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), Mansfield & Associates (M&A), the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW) and the [[National Park Service]] (NPS).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fish in the analysis were classified by capture history, based on information in the NFWG database:

- **Wild adult**: fish captured in the field and not known to be hatchery-origin.
- **Repatriate**: fish originally reared in hatcheries or rearing ponds and released back into the lake.
- **Unknown**: fish captured in the field with no release record in the database.

Repatriates dominated the sample. Rearing and release locations were spread across Arizona and Nevada sites, including Lake Mohave coves (for example [[Yuma Cove]], [[Bandy Cove]], [[Davis Cove]], [[North Chemehuevi Cove]] and [[Willow Cove]]), the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and ponds and wetlands near Boulder City.

## Key Findings

**Population estimates**

- The Chapman-modified Petersen estimate for 2009/2010 was reported as **1,439 fish (95% CI 753–2,805)**.
- The 2011 estimate was adjusted to exclude fish captured in March 2010 that were released in March 2010 (n=0) and fish released in March 2010 and recaptured in March 2011 (N reads as 41 in the scan). The scan of the final 2011 figure is not reliable.
- A separate table gives a 2009/2010 estimate of **2,707 (95% CI 1,376–5,529)** with **147,398 repatriates released** as of March 1, 2010. The estimated survival value is illegible in the scan.
- The population estimate figures for 2010/2011 are partly illegible; the legible entries are 13, 4 and 250, with the meaning of each column unclear.

**March 2011 roundup**

- **194 fish** were collected. BR contributed 25, FWS 117, M&A 13, and NDOW/NPS 39.
- 20 fish had no PIT tag and 174 had one. Five tagged fish were recaptures and were removed from further analysis, leaving **169 fish**.
- Gender: 128 females and 2 unknown in the full sample; the male count is illegible in the scan.
- Capture history of the 169 analyzed fish: **4 wild adults** (1 female, 3 male, 0 unknown), **165 repatriates** (104 female, 59 male, 2 unknown), and 0 fish of unknown origin.

**Rearing location and size (Table 5)**

- Twelve fish with unknown rearing location were omitted, leaving **153 fish**.
- At release, average total length (TL) was **433 mm** (SD 67; range 253–597 mm). At collection, average TL was **556 mm** (SD 58; range 340–710 mm).
- Of the 153 fish, 97 were female, 54 male and 2 unknown.
- The largest single rearing sources were [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (39 fish) and [[Yuma Cove]] (30 fish).

**Time at large (Table 6)**

- Fish were grouped by release year, from 1995 to 2011.
- The largest cohorts were released in **2010 (55 fish)** and **2009 (50 fish)**.
- Average time at large across all fish was **1,198 days** (about 40 months, or 3.3 years).
- Time at large increased with the age of the release cohort, ranging from roughly 69 days for fish released in 2011 to about 5,614 days for fish released in 1995.
