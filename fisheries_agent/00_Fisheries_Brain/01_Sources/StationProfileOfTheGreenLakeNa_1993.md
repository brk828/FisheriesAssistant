## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the [[Green Lake National Fish Hatchery]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 5) facility located in Ellsworth, Maine, authorized in 1967 under Public Law 90-28. The hatchery's core mission is to facilitate restoration of the [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*), described as "a depleted nationally significant fishery resource." Fiscal Year 1993 was reported as highly successful, with approximately 641,000 large smolts stocked, 415,000 fall parr released in September, and 1,519,000 domestic Atlantic salmon eggs produced to support the [[Southern New England Rivers Salmon Restoration Program]]. The station also engaged in public outreach (Maine Sportsman Show, Common Ground Fair; ~80,000 attendees) and hosted student trainees during summer 1993.

## Background & Description

The source document is an administrative/operational station profile and does not provide morphological, meristic, or anatomical data for the species reared. Life-stage terminology used includes:

- **Smolts** — juvenile Atlantic salmon undergoing physiological transformation (smoltification) for seaward migration; ~641,000 stocked in FY93.
- **Parr** — pre-smolt juvenile salmon; 415,000 fall parr stocked in September FY93.
- **Eggs** — 1,519,000 domestic Atlantic salmon eggs produced for restoration stocking.

No length, weight, coloration, or fin-ray count data are reported in this profile.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Green Lake National Fish Hatchery]] functions within the broader ecological and management context of anadromous [[Atlantic Salmon]] life history and restoration ecology in the Gulf of Maine region:

- **Anadromy**: Atlantic salmon reared at the facility are released as parr and smolts to migrate downstream to the [[North Atlantic]], later returning as adults to spawn in freshwater rivers — supporting the species' natural anadromous life cycle.
- **Restoration role**: Eggs produced at the station supply the [[Southern Rivers Restoration Program]], targeting depleted salmon runs in southern New England river systems outside Maine's core range.
- **Landlocked populations**: The hatchery also maintains non-anadromous [[Landlocked Salmon]] stocks (*Salmo salar sebago*), an ecologically distinct freshwater-resident form.
- **Multi-species stocking**: [[Brook Trout]] and [[Lake Trout]] are also propagated in smaller numbers, indicating a broader coldwater fisheries management role supporting recreational and federal/state waters beyond salmon restoration alone.
- **Water quality infrastructure**: The facility uses filtration and U.V. treatment (13,500 gpm capacity) and six effluent treatment ponds, reflecting an engineered coldwater ecosystem designed to mimic natural rearing conditions while managing hatchery effluent impact on receiving waters.

## Key Findings

- **Station identity**: Green Lake National Fish Hatchery, Region 5, Ellsworth, Maine; authorized 1967 (PL 90-28); mission centered on [[Atlantic Salmon]] restoration.
- **FY93 production highlights**:
  - ~641,000 large smolts stocked (93 yearclass off to a strong start)
  - 415,000 fall parr stocked (September)
  - 1,519,000 domestic Atlantic salmon eggs produced for southern rivers restoration
- **Total fish distributed (excluding eggs/fry), FY93**: 1,073,312 individuals, comprising:
  - Atlantic Salmon: 1,068,870
  - Lake Trout: 2,212
  - Brook Trout: 2,300
  - Landlocked Salmon: 2,230
- **Distribution trend across years** (Totals): FY90 = 794,430; FY91 = 1,102,914; FY92 = 1,168,300; FY93 = 1,073,312 — indicating a generally increasing then slightly declining distribution trend, with the majority consistently allocated to "International" waters/programs.
- **Physical infrastructure**: Combination hatchery/shop/administration/broodstock building; 102 circular concrete rearing ponds (20' and 30' diameter) under open-sided steel-frame roofing; dual 30" intake/delivery lines; filtration and U.V. treatment plant (13,500 gpm capacity); six effluent treatment ponds.
- **Funding & staffing (1990–1993)**:
  - Operations budget rose from $421,400 (1990) to $477,426 (1993)
  - Maintenance budget varied significantly, spiking to $267,500 in 1993 from $46,998 (1990)
  - Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staffing remained stable (~8–8.8), plus 1 FTE funded via Cost Recoverables (NMFA)
- **Outreach & training**: Station personnel staffed exhibits at the Maine Sportsman Show and Common Ground Fair (combined ~80,000 attendees); employed one COOP student (Dulcie Lishness) and one student trainee (Jason Colon) in summer 1993.
- **Congressional district**: Maine's 2nd Federal District, represented by Congresswoman [[Olympia Snowe]] and Senators [[George Mitchell]] and [[William Cohen]].
