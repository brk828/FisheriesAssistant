# Reproductive Tactics of Four Sympatric Sticklebacks

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gasterosteidae

## Executive Summary

This study compares life history traits of four sympatric [[Gasterosteidae|sticklebacks]] in a tidal creek at Isle Verte, Quebec: [[Threespine Stickleback]], [[Blackspotted Stickleback]], [[Ninespine Stickleback]] and [[Fourspine Stickleback]]. The authors examined growth, longevity, age at maturity, fecundity, egg size and breeding season, and compared them with published populations across the Atlantic coast of North America. Significant differences were found for growth, longevity, age at maturity, fecundity and breeding season, but not for egg diameter. Fecundity was lowest in *Apeltes quadracus* and highest in *Gasterosteus aculeatus*. The authors interpret these differences as mechanisms that may allow coexistence of closely related territorial fish, all of which show male [[Parental Care]]. Inter-population patterns did not follow the reduced northern fecundity predicted by [[Life History Theory]] for non-parental fish.

## Background & Description

**Study context.** The paper (Env. Biol. Fish. 7(4): 369-375, 1982) addresses how [[Reproductive Tactics]] vary within and among closely related species. The authors note that theory developed for non-parental fish is poorly tested for fish with advanced parental care. Sticklebacks are among the few fish families with parental care, and most fish instead produce many small young.

**Species.** The four species migrate from the [[St. Lawrence Estuary]] and breed in May and June in tidal rivers and creeks on its southern shore. They differ in their degree of male parental care, which is thought to buffer young against environmental variability. *G. wheatlandi* is restricted to salt or brackish water, whereas *G. aculeatus*, *P. pungitius* and *A. quadracus* also have freshwater and anadromous forms.

**Methods.**
- Fish were collected with seines and minnow traps in May and June 1979 and 1980 at [[Isle Verte]], preserved in about 4% formaldehyde, and measured to the nearest mm (standard length) and 0.001 g (weights).
- Eggs were counted at maturity stage 4 or 5 ([[Nikolsky Maturity Scale]]). Diameters were measured on 20 stage-5 eggs per specimen.
- Ageing relied on [[Length-Frequency Analysis]], because sticklebacks lack scales. Otoliths were not used in this study.
- Isle Verte was chosen as the central site where all four species co-occur. Comparisons with other populations used published data.

**Sample sizes.** Fecundity sample sizes were 35 (*G. aculeatus*), 53 (*G. wheatlandi*), 33 (*P. pungitius*) and 36 (*A. quadracus*). Egg diameter sample sizes were 22, 10, 15 and 23, respectively.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat partitioning.** Adult sticklebacks segregate spatially in the salt marsh. *G. aculeatus* usually breeds in open areas, while *G. wheatlandi* and especially *P. pungitius* nest in algae. *A. quadracus* does not breed in the tidal pools at Isle Verte, where densities of the other three species are high, but does breed in nearby tidal rivers with lower densities.
- **Predation.** The Isle Verte area is relatively free of fish predators on breeding sticklebacks, although bird predation on adults may be important.
- **Seasonal cycle.** Males guard young in tidal pools until mid-July, when adults leave. Young remain in the pools until late autumn and then leave on tidal flooding, presumably to escape ice cover. No mass mortality after spawning was observed.
- **Environmental confounding.** Proximate factors such as water temperature and food supply can mask adaptive differences in reproductive tactics. Shorter growing seasons in Quebec may favour reduced fecundity to allocate energy to maintenance and growth.

## Key Findings

### Growth and age structure
- *G. aculeatus* and *A. quadracus* showed bimodal length-frequency distributions; *P. pungitius* and *G. wheatlandi* were unimodal.
- *G. aculeatus* was judged to have three age classes (0+, 1+, 2+). Fecund females (5.1-7.0 cm, mean 6.4 cm, N = 35) were 2+.
- *G. wheatlandi* lived about 1-2 years. Females (2.7-4.1 cm, mean 3.3 cm, N = 53) apparently bred at 1+.
- *P. pungitius* females (3.6-5.3 cm, mean 4.4 cm, N = 33) showed a single size class on the breeding grounds. The earliest maturity is probably 1+, with some 2+ fish spawning in a second year.
- *A. quadracus* females (3.1-5.2 cm, mean 4.1 cm, N = 36) formed two distinct size classes and apparently lived 2-3 years.

### Fecundity
Fecundity differed significantly among species (Table 1; values are mean ± SD, N, and range):

| Species | Egg number | Range |
|---|---|---|
| *G. aculeatus* | 366.3 ± 175.4 (35) | 116-838 |
| *G. wheatlandi* | 80.1 ± 20.3 (53) | 45-142 |
| *P. pungitius* | 76.1 ± 22.4 (33) | 37-136 |
| *A. quadracus* | 36.1 ± 11.9 (36) | 16-61 |

- *G. aculeatus* was significantly more fecund than the other three species. *G. wheatlandi* and *P. pungitius* were similar and intermediate.
- Fecundity-somatic weight regressions were significant for all four species (p < 0.01):
  - *G. aculeatus*: F = 145.1 SW − 72.2, n = 35, r = 0.57
  - *G. wheatlandi*: F = 67.0 SW − 47.5, n = 53, r = 0.54
  - *P. pungitius*: F = 62.6 SW − 25.9, n = 33, r = 0.64
  - *A. quadracus*: F = 27.0 SW − 12.6, n = 36, r = 0.91
- Covariance analysis showed that slopes for *G. wheatlandi* and *P. pungitius* did not differ, while those for *G. aculeatus* and *A. quadracus* differed from each other and from the first pair.

### Egg size
- Egg diameters were not significantly related to length or somatic weight in *G. aculeatus*, *G. wheatlandi* or *P. pungitius*.
- In *A. quadracus*, egg diameter was significantly related to somatic weight (y = 0.56 SW + 1.170, n = 20, r = 0.66, p < 0.01) and to standard length (y = 0.092 SL + 1.317, n = 20, r = 0.65, p < 0.01).
- Mean egg diameters at Isle Verte were 1.39 mm (*G. aculeatus*), 1.25 mm (*G. wheatlandi*), 1.14 mm (*P. pungitius*) and 1.40 mm (*A. quadracus*). Egg diameters overlapped and did not differ significantly among species.

### Breeding season
- *G. aculeatus*, *G. wheatlandi* and *P. pungitius* breed from early May to the end of June.
- *A. quadracus* is in breeding condition from early May to the end of July, the longest season of the four.

### Inter-population comparisons
- **Fecundity:** Isle Verte *G. aculeatus* (mean 366) exceeded all populations compared. Freshwater populations had lower fecundity (e.g. 88 eggs at the Matamek River System). Isle Verte *G. wheatlandi* (mean 80) was lower than Amory Cove (126) and Newfoundland (186). Isle Verte *P. pungitius* (mean 76) far exceeded the freshwater Matamek form (mean 31). Isle Verte *A. quadracus* (mean 36) exceeded Matamek freshwater (mean 14) and Woods Hole (mean 27).
- **Size:** Isle Verte *G. aculeatus* resembled Matamek fish but were much larger than Woods Hole fish. *G. wheatlandi* was similar to Matamek but smaller than Long Island and Newfoundland fish.

### Interpretation and coexistence
- The authors suggest that the low fecundity of *A. quadracus* is offset by several factors: breeding in less dense tidal rivers, repeat spawning in different years, a longer breeding season, and poorer predation pressure on its offspring. Its poorly developed parental care (no care of free-swimming young) is a disadvantage.
- Selective predation on the more fecund, open-nesting *G. aculeatus* might let the less fecund species compete better for nest sites.
- Fecundity was measured per spawning. Sticklebacks are partial spawners, so a species with lower fecundity per spawning could achieve more spawnings per season.
- Inter-population patterns did not match the reduced northern fecundity predicted by [[Life History Theory]] for non-parental fish. Apparent differences may reflect growth differences before breeding rather than allocation of energy to eggs.

### Conclusions and future work
The authors call for a detailed study of parental care across environmental gradients, to test links between female reproductive investment, male care and reproductive effort in these sympatric [[Gasterosteidae]].
