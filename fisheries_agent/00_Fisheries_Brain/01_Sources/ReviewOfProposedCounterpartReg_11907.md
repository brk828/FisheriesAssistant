# Review of Proposed Counterpart Regulations with Forest Service and Bureau of Land Management

#endangered-species-act #federal-policy #regulatory-review #land-management

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Arizona Ecological Services State Office]] to the Assistant Regional Director provides detailed comments on proposed joint counterpart section 7 regulations for the [[Forest Service]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]]. Dated August 25, 1995, the document expresses support for streamlining consultation processes under the [[Endangered Species Act]] but raises substantial concerns about clarity, implementation feasibility, and potential legal complications. The office identifies fundamental problems with vague terminology, unclear agency responsibilities, and inconsistent treatment of consultation requirements across federal agencies.

## Background & Description

The proposed counterpart regulations aim to address consultation requirements for [[Forest Service]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]] land use planning activities under section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The regulations attempt to streamline review processes, particularly for:

- Ongoing actions when new species are listed
- Tiering of plans (forest plan to landscape/ecosystem plan to site-specific action)
- Non-site-specific (planning or program-level) consultations
- Site-specific project consultations

The Arizona office notes extensive section 7 experience but finds the proposed regulations confusing due to unclear writing, vague grammatical structure, and poorly defined goals, terms, processes, and responsibilities. The document identifies that existing regulations have been used successfully for many years and questions whether new regulations are necessary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memorandum addresses consultation requirements for listed species and critical habitats across [[Forest Service]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]] lands. Key ecological considerations include:

- Direct and indirect effects on listed species and critical habitats
- Cumulative effects from multiple actions over time
- Environmental baseline conditions
- Interdependent and interrelated actions
- Complex interactions between biotic and abiotic factors (vegetation, soils, water, geology, climate, weather, habitat conditions, population demographics, genetics)

The document emphasizes that effects to species or habitats are highly dependent on project specifics and that establishing adequate parameters requires considering numerous interrelated variables. Actions benign under some circumstances may be disastrous under others, and moving a fence location by a few feet may completely change effects on listed species.

## Key Findings

**Major Concerns with Proposed Regulations:**

1. **Clarity Issues**: The proposed regulations lack clearly defined goals, terms, processes, and responsibilities, creating abundant opportunity for widely differing interpretations and potential legal challenges.

2. **Scope Limitations**: The regulations address only [[Forest Service]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]] activities, creating inconsistent consultation requirements across federal agencies. The problem of consultation on ongoing actions extends to all federal agencies and should be addressed comprehensively.

3. **Non-Site-Specific Consultation Problems**: The proposed approach adds two levels of consultation prior to site-specific consultation, potentially accomplishing the opposite of streamlining while diminishing species protection. Making non-site-specific consultation discretionary eliminates the advantage of addressing "big picture" land management issues early.

4. **Existing Regulations Adequate**: The concept of substituting non-site-specific consultation using standards, guidelines, and parameters for site-specific consultation has already been used successfully under existing regulations. Tiering site-specific from non-site-specific consultations is also well-established.

5. **Standards and Parameters Concerns**: Using broad standards, guidelines, and parameters to assess effects requires large buffering provisions to avoid jeopardy. This approach is valuable for actions with direct, well-defined effects (e.g., road maintenance) or limited human control (e.g., fire) but may be inappropriate for actions with many indirect effects (e.g., livestock grazing) or significant cumulative effects (e.g., recreation).

**Specific Technical Issues:**

- **Consultation/Conference Agreements**: The 45-day time period for completion is too short to adequately conform to section 7(a)(2) requirements for using best available scientific and commercial information. This timeframe conflicts with the time required to assemble and analyze adequate information.

- **Definition Problems**: Terms including "imminent threat," "parameter," "consultation/conference agreement," "relevant parameters," "event," and "human resource considerations" lack clear definitions. The definition of "non-site-specific action" is vague and confusing.

- **Mandatory Agreement Requirement**: Section 402.22(b)(3) makes agreement between agencies mandatory, potentially ensuring dominance of politics over science. The agency with more political clout can dictate terms by refusing to agree, forcing the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] to concede to potentially detrimental actions.

- **Information Requirements**: The proposed regulations drop three of five information requirements from existing regulations for initiating formal consultation, potentially weakening the consultation process.

- **Incidental Take**: Unclear how incidental take will be handled for site-specific concurrences and non-site-specific consultations under the proposed regulations.

- **Reinitiation Criteria**: No criteria provided for reinitiation of non-site-specific consultations when new information becomes available revealing previously unconsidered effects.

- **Multiple Agency Consultations**: Unclear which regulations apply when multiple action agencies are involved, including [[Corps of Engineers]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Federal Highway Administration]], [[Federal Emergency Management Agency]], or [[Natural Resources Conservation Service]] along with [[Forest Service]] and [[Bureau of Land Management]].

**Recommendations:**

1. Amend existing regulations to deal with consultation on ongoing actions for all agencies and all species and critical habitats, rather than creating separate regulations for two agencies.

2. Clarify the logical sequence of steps, roles and responsibilities of each agency, and what actions are discretionary versus non-discretionary.

3. Require [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] concurrence with [[Bureau of Land Management]] or [[Forest Service]] notification of conformance, rather than notification alone.

4. Define all key terms clearly and consistently throughout the regulations.

5. Maintain existing successful approaches to non-site-specific and tiered consultations rather than creating new, potentially more cumbersome processes.

6. Ensure that site-specific consultation continues to provide adequate protection to species while maintaining project flexibility.

The memorandum concludes that while the need to improve section 7 handling with the two land management agencies is recognized and the effort is applauded, the proposed regulations raise many additional questions and would create confusion if implemented as written. Many proposed changes can already be accommodated within existing regulations.
