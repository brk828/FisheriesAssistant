# Reproductive Cycles of Three Viviparous Goodeid Teleosts from Lake Pátzcuaro

#ichthyology #marine-biology #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 paper by Guillermo Mendoza (Grinnell College) reports the first intensive field-based study of the female reproductive cycles of three viviparous [[Goodeidae]] from [[Lake Pátzcuaro]], Mexico: [[Alloophorus Robustus]], [[Goodea Luitpoldii]] and [[Neoophorus Diazi]]. Based on 3,261 females collected in 1957, the study finds that the first two species have a single, short breeding cycle per year, while [[Neoophorus Diazi]] breeds over an extended 8 to 9 month period, probably with multiple broods. Gestation is estimated at roughly 60 to 75 days. The author attributes the differences mainly to genetic factors.

## Background & Description

- **Prior knowledge:** Goodeid reproduction was poorly known. The main sources were a three-year laboratory study of [[Neotoca Bilineata]] (Mendoza, 1939) and brief statements in [[Meek]] (1904) taxonomic descriptions, plus scattered notes by [[C. L. Turner]].
- **Study species:** Mature Alloophorus and Goodea females typically measure 90–110 mm (occasionally over 130 mm). Mature Neoophorus females measure 70–90 mm.
- **Collection methods:** Local fishermen supplied specimens using "butterfly" nets from canoes in shoreline vegetation and large seines (300 feet or more) in open water. Market fish were also purchased. Roughly 50 females per species were collected twice monthly through 1957. Collections from 1956 and 1958 (over 6,000 females) served for comparison.
- **Ovary stages:** Ovaries were classed as immature, resting, growing eggs, free eggs, embryos (sorted into twelve size classes from 3.5 to 32.5 mm), and post-partum. The ovary doubles as a uterus, so all development from fertilization to birth occurs in the ovarian lumen.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** [[Lake Pátzcuaro]] lies at high altitude (over 7,000 feet) in a tropical latitude, with cool to chilly weather even in summer. The lake is shallow and fairly homogeneous, with some evidence of ecological segregation among species.
- **Shared conditions:** All three species live in the same lake and face common physical factors, which makes the divergent cycles notable.
- **Comparative context:** The multi-brood, year-round cycle of Neoophorus resembles that of [[Brachyrhaphis Episcopi]] in the tropical Panama Canal Zone. Single annual broods in Alloophorus and Goodea are more typical of marine viviparous fishes such as [[Zoarces Viviparus]] and [[Cymatogaster Aggregatus]].

## Key Findings

1. **Single brood per year in Alloophorus and Goodea:** Free eggs appeared April to June, and young were born from June through August. Free eggs disappeared before the first births, so a second brood was impossible.
2. **Multiple, prolonged breeding in Neoophorus:** Embryos appeared from late March and were present until January or February of the following year. The author judges it likely that it has multiple broods, but this is not conclusively shown.
3. **Gestation length:** Estimated at approximately 60–75 days. The author calls this a suggestive approximation only.
4. **Brood size:**
   - Alloophorus: mean 23.7 young per ovary (50 ovaries, 1,186 young).
   - Goodea: mean 19.1 young per ovary (44 ovaries, 860 young).
   - Neoophorus: mean 39.9 young per ovary (193 ovaries, 7,677 young).
5. **Brood uniformity:** Embryos within one ovary seldom differed by more than 2 to 3 mm.
6. **Runts (abnormal young) were rare:** 0.49% in Neoophorus, 0.42% in Alloophorus, 0.11% in Goodea.
7. **Size–fecundity relationship:** Larger females have larger ovaries and larger broods. In Neoophorus this progressive relationship is absent because breeding is asynchronous.
8. **Maturation time:** Alloophorus and Goodea are thought to take two years to mature, which is unusual among viviparous teleosts. Neoophorus is believed to mature within one year. Small one-year-old females (Alloophorus 60–90 mm; Goodea 75–85 mm; Neoophorus 50 mm or less) form a separate size mode, which is why the length distribution is bimodal in the first two species.
9. **Fertilization timing:** Eggs are fertilized at about the time they leave the follicle, and all development occurs in the ovarian lumen. This contrasts with [[Anableps Anableps]] and most poeciliids.
10. **No sperm storage or superfetation:** Each brood needs a separate insemination, and superfetation was not observed. The author attributes its absence to eggs not growing to full size before expulsion and to the lack of sperm storage.
11. **Low embryonic mortality:** The author suggests that most fertilized eggs survive to birth. This disagrees with Turner's generalization about Goodeidae and Jenynsiidae.
12. **Genetic basis:** The author concludes that differences in reproductive cycle among the three species are "primarily genetic in character." He notes that tagging or laboratory studies are still needed to confirm gestation length and whether Neoophorus breeds in single or multiple broods.
