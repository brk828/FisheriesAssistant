# Title 50—Wildlife and Fisheries, Part 17—Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#endangered-species #conservation-law #regulatory-framework

## Executive Summary

This document is a comprehensive regulatory compilation from the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations (50 CFR Part 17) listing all species of wildlife and plants determined by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to be Endangered or Threatened under the Endangered Species Act. Published as of January 1, 1986, it provides the official federal registry of protected species, including mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, snails, clams, crustaceans, insects, and plants. The document establishes the legal framework for species protection, defines listing criteria, and provides cross-references to special rules and critical habitat designations. This is an administrative reference document rather than a research study.

## Background & Description

The regulatory framework establishes that species are listed based on scientific and common names, with geographic populations defined by their boundaries. The "Status" column uses standardized symbols: "E" for Endangered, "T" for Threatened, "E (S/A)" or "T (S/A)" for similarity of appearance species, "XE" for essential experimental populations, and "XN" for nonessential experimental populations. The document clarifies that common names cannot be relied upon for specimen identification due to local variation, and the Services use the most recently accepted scientific nomenclature following the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] for animals and [[International Code of Botanical Nomenclature]] for plants.

Historic range indicates known general distribution as reported in scientific literature, though present distribution may be greatly reduced. The listing applies to all individuals of a species wherever found, not limited by historic range boundaries. Footnotes reference Federal Register publications that include the basis for listing and effective dates. The compilation includes 1,985 Code of Federal Regulations updates and all published final rules through the cover date.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses species across diverse ecosystems and taxonomic groups. For aquatic species specifically mentioned:

**Marine and Coastal Species**: Multiple sea turtles including [[Green Sea Turtle]] (*Chelonia mydas*), [[Hawksbill Sea Turtle]] (*Eretmochelys imbricata*), [[Kemp's Ridley Sea Turtle]] (*Lepidochelys kempii*), [[Leatherback Sea Turtle]] (*Dermochelys coriacea*), [[Loggerhead Sea Turtle]] (*Caretta caretta*), and [[Olive Ridley Sea Turtle]] (*Lepidochelys olivacea*) are listed with varying status across their circumglobal tropical and temperate ranges.

**Marine Mammals**: [[Hawaiian Monk Seal]] (*Monachus schauinslandi*), [[Mediterranean Monk Seal]] (*Monachus monachus*), [[Caribbean Monk Seal]] (*Monachus tropicalis*), [[Southern Sea Otter]] (*Enhydra lutris nereis*), and multiple whale species including [[Blue Whale]] (*Balaenoptera musculus*), [[Bowhead Whale]] (*Balaena mysticetus*), [[Finback Whale]] (*Balaenoptera physalus*), [[Gray Whale]] (*Eschrichtius robustus*), [[Humpback Whale]] (*Megaptera novaeangliae*), [[Right Whale]] (*Balaena glacialis*), [[Sei Whale]] (*Balaenoptera borealis*), and [[Sperm Whale]] (*Physeter catodon*).

**Freshwater Fishes**: Extensive listings include endangered pupfishes (genus *Cyprinodon*), darters (genera *Etheostoma*, *Percina*), gambusias (*Gambusia* spp.), chubs (*Gila* spp.), and threatened trout subspecies including [[Apache Trout]] (*Salmo apache*), [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*), [[Greenback Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki stomias*), and [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*).

**Aquatic Invertebrates**: Freshwater pearly mussels (family Unionidae) comprise a significant portion of listed mollusks, with species like [[Alabama Lamp Pearly Mussel]] (*Lampsilis virescens*), [[Birdwing Pearly Mussel]] (*Conradilla caelata*), and [[Pink Mucket Pearly Mussel]] (*Lampsilis orbiculata*). Crustaceans include cave-adapted species like [[Hay's Spring Amphipod]] (*Stygobromus hayi*) and [[Socorro Isopod]] (*Thermosphaeroma thermophilus*).

Threats to aquatic species include habitat destruction and modification (particularly for freshwater fishes in western U.S. river systems), overutilization (marine turtles, commercial fish species), inadequate regulatory mechanisms, and other anthropogenic factors including water diversion, pollution, and introduced species.

## Key Findings

**Listing Criteria and Process**: Species are listed or reclassified when the Secretary determines, based on best scientific and commercial data, that the species is endangered or threatened due to: (1) habitat destruction, modification, or curtailment; (2) overutilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes; (3) disease or predation; (4) inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms; or (5) other natural or manmade factors affecting continued existence.

**Delisting Criteria**: Species may be removed from the list for three reasons: (1) Extinction—when all previously identified individuals are extirpated after a sufficient time period; (2) Recovery—when best available data indicate the species is no longer endangered or threatened; (3) Original data in error—when subsequent investigations show listing data or interpretation were incorrect.

**Geographic Scope**: The lists encompass species from the United States (including all states, territories, and trust territories), Canada, Mexico, Central and South America, Africa, Europe, Asia, Australia, Pacific Islands, Indian Ocean islands, and Antarctic/subpolar regions. U.S. listings include both resident species and migratory species that occur within U.S. jurisdiction during part of their life cycle.

**Taxonomic Distribution**: Among vertebrates, mammals comprise the largest group with extensive listings of primates, carnivores, ungulates, rodents, bats, and marine mammals. Birds include numerous Hawaiian honeycreepers, raptors, waterfowl, and seabirds. Reptiles feature extensive turtle and crocodilian listings. Fishes represent a major component with particular concentration on southwestern U.S. desert fishes and southeastern freshwater mussels.

**Special Designations**: The document cross-references special rules (Part 17.40-17.46) and critical habitat designations (Part 17.95-17.96) for specific species. Experimental populations are designated as either essential (XE) or nonessential (XN), with different regulatory treatment. For example, [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) populations in the Gila River drainage of Arizona and New Mexico are designated as nonessential experimental (XN).

**Recent Delistings** (as of January 1, 1986): Seven species were removed from the endangered list: [[Mexican Duck]] (*Anas diazi*)—original data in error; [[Tecopa Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis calidae*)—extinct; [[Longjaw Cisco]] (*Coregonus alpenae*)—extinct; [[Blue Pike]] (*Stizostedion vitreum glaucum*)—extinct; [[Santa Barbara Song Sparrow]] (*Melospiza melodia graminea*)—extinct; [[Pine Barrens Treefrog]] (*Hyla andersonii*)—original data in error; [[Sampsons Pearly Mussel]] (*Epioblasma sampsoni*)—extinct; [[Indian Flap-shelled Turtle]] (*Lissemys punctata punctata*)—original data in error; [[Bahama Swallowtail Butterfly]]—original data in error; [[Palau Dove]] (*Gallicolumba canifrons*)—recovered.

**Administrative Framework**: The compilation is maintained by the Office of Endangered Species, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Washington, D.C. Minor changes including spelling corrections, historical range updates, footnotes, synonyms, and current names may be amended without public notice in annual revisions. However, changes to species definitions or status require following procedures in Part 424 of Title 50 CFR.
