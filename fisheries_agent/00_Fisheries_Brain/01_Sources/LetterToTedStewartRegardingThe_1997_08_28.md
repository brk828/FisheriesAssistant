# Letter to Ted Stewart Regarding the Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program

## Executive Summary
This document is **not a primary scientific paper** but a 1997 advocacy/policy letter sent by a coalition of environmental organizations (Grand Canyon Trust, American Rivers, Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance, and Southwest Resource Council) to Ted Stewart, Director of the Utah Department of Natural Resources. The letter addresses the governance and stakeholder inclusion process for the **Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program**, an initiative intended to "recover, conserve, enhance and protect native species and their habitat" in the [[Virgin River Basin]] while balancing human water and recreational demands. No direct morphological, physiological, or population-level fish data are presented; however, the letter provides important contextual and institutional information relevant to conservation efforts for endangered native fish species in this basin, and it names key scientific personnel associated with the **Virgin River Fish Recovery Team**.

## Background & Description
No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic data are provided in this letter. The source does not describe any specific fish species' physical traits, size, coloration, or diagnostic features. The document is administrative/policy-focused rather than descriptive of organismal biology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The [[Virgin River Basin]], a tributary system of the [[Colorado River]] drainage spanning Utah, Arizona, and Nevada, is identified as the ecosystem of concern.
- **Ecosystem Pressures**: The letter explicitly references the need to balance:
  - Native species and habitat recovery/conservation
  - Recreational water use
  - Consumptive water needs of a "growing human population" in Washington County and the broader basin
- **Institutional Stakeholders** managing this ecological niche include:
  - [[Utah Department of Natural Resources]]
  - [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]]
  - [[Bureau of Land Management]]
  - [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]]
  - [[National Park Service]] (including [[Zion National Park]])
  - [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]]
  - [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]]
- **Recovery Team**: The **Virgin River Fish Recovery Team** is cited as a scientific body involved in this ecosystem's management, with named affiliated scientists:
  - Jon Sjoberg, Jim Heinrich, Jerry Stein (Nevada Division of Wildlife)
  - Jim Deacon ([[University of Nevada Las Vegas]])
  - Thom Hardy ([[Utah State University]])
  - Paul Marsh
  - W.L. Minckley ([[Arizona State University]])

This roster indicates an interdisciplinary, multi-agency approach to endangered native fish conservation in the basin, consistent with recovery efforts for federally listed Virgin River fishes.

## Key Findings
- The **Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program** was developed primarily through agency-level negotiation (Utah DNR and partner agencies) **without direct input from the environmental community**, prompting this formal letter of concern.
- Environmental organizations sought inclusion via two nominated representatives — **Rick Johnson** (Grand Canyon Trust, ecology background, prior [[The Nature Conservancy]] experience in Minnesota and New Mexico) and **Pam Hyde** (American Rivers, background in law and natural resource economics/policy; also served on the Steering Committee for the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]]).
- The coalition requested that these representatives be given equal standing with other stakeholders, the opportunity to review and suggest changes to Program documents, and that no final agreements be signed until environmental community concerns were substantively addressed.
- The letter underscores the collaborative, multi-agency, consensus-based governance model intended for endangered species recovery in the [[Virgin River Basin]], involving federal, state, and local water management entities alongside conservation NGOs.
- Distribution of the letter reveals the **broad network of scientific and regulatory stakeholders** engaged in Virgin River fish conservation circa 1997, including representatives from [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] regional and field offices, [[Bureau of Land Management]] field offices (Dixie Resource Area, Arizona Strip), [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (Native Aquatics program), and the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]].
- No quantitative ecological, hydrological, or population data (e.g., flow rates, population counts, habitat area) are included in this letter; its scientific relevance is primarily as an **institutional/historical record** of the stakeholder process surrounding native fish recovery efforts in the Virgin River system.
