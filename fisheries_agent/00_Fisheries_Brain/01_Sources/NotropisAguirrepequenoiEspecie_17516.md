# Notropis aguirrepequenoi: A New Endemic Cyprinid from the Río Soto la Marina, Tamaulipas, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 taxonomic paper, published in the [[Publicaciones Biologicas]] series of the [[Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon]], describes *[[Notropis Aguirrepequenoi]]* sp. nov., a cyprinid minnow endemic to the [[Rio Soto La Marina]] in [[Tamaulipas]], northeastern Mexico. The species was described from 705 specimens collected at four localities. It belongs to the [[Braytoni Species Group]] of the genus [[Notropis]] and is distinguished by its pharyngeal tooth formula of 4-4 and the absence of barbels. Its closest relatives are [[Notropis Braytoni]] from the [[Rio Bravo]] and an undescribed *Notropis* sp. from the [[Rio Panuco]], both of which have 1,4-4,1 pharyngeal teeth. The species is named in honor of [[Eduardo Aguirre Pequeno]].

## Background & Description

**Context of the work.** The principal author had studied the freshwater fish fauna of northeastern Mexico since 1958. During that work he recognized this form, which had previously been identified by [[Carl L. Hubbs]], who allowed the authors to describe it. The co-author began a taxonomic revision of the fishes of the Rio San Fernando and Rio Soto la Marina in 1970. The paper was received on May 31, 1973, and transferred from a planned homage volume to this journal.

**Diagnosis.** Pharyngeal teeth 4-4; anal and dorsal rays usually 8; lateral line scales 33 to 35; 7-2-5 scales around the caudal peduncle; blunt snout; a longitudinal black lateral band with a triangular basicaudal spot (with its anterior apex) about as wide as the band and the pupil.

**Morphology.**
- Slightly compressed body with a convex dorsal profile; males have a slightly convex ventral profile, which is more pronounced in females.
- Maximum body height 3.5 to 4 times in standard length (SL).
- Small head, 3.4 to 3.8 times in SL.
- Small, subterminal, oblique mouth with the upper lip larger than the lower.
- Long pectoral fins that nearly reach the pelvic fin origin, especially in males.
- Forked caudal fin with a narrow, pointed dorsal lobe and a broad, rounded ventral lobe.

**Meristic ranges (mode in parentheses).** Dorsal 7 (8) 9; anal 7 (8) 8; pectorals 12 (14) 16; pelvics 7 (8) 9; lateral line 32 (34) 38; predorsal scales 13 (15) 17; total circumpeduncular scales 12 (14) 17.

**Breeding tubercles.** Present in both sexes: moderate to strong in males, fine in females. They cover the head, are less conspicuous on the throat, and disappear near the isthmus. They are absent from the anal fin.

**Coloration (preserved specimens).** Strong yellow body with two parallel, adjacent stripes: a black ventral band and a silvery dorsal band, extending from the posterior eye margin to the hypural. A line of melanophores runs behind the head and continues postdorsally to the caudal base. The operculum is silvery and the fins are yellowish.

**Type material.** The holotype is a 39.7 mm SL male (UNAM IB/CML-P434) collected on May 21, 1966, from the Río Pilón where it crosses Highway 85, Mainero, Tamaulipas, by Salvador Contreras, Daniel Molina, Alejandro Aseff, and Alfonso Martinez. Paratypes are deposited in the [[Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon]] (UANL) collection and in several other institutions, including the [[Instituto Politecnico Nacional]], [[Universidad Nacional Autonoma de Mexico]], and US institutions such as the [[United States National Museum]]. Morphometric data for the holotype and 60 paratypes are given in a comparison table with *N. braytoni* (Table I).

**Etymology.** The name honors Dr. Eduardo Aguirre Pequeno, a Nuevo Leon naturalist and parasitologist who founded the School of Biological Sciences at UANL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The species occurs mainly in low, wide arroyos less than 1 m deep and 20 to 30 m across. Banks are fringed with [[Eleocharis]] and common [[Potamogeton]], and the substrate is sand or gravel covered by a thin layer of mud in generally moderate current. The authors found it most abundant at 24 °C and tolerant of temperatures up to 32 °C. It occupied all available habitats within these general conditions.

**Distribution.** The species is endemic to the Río Soto la Marina, known from the middle reaches and headwaters of a cooler tributary. A distribution map (Fig. 2) shows the type locality and the Río Bravo, Río San Fernando, and Río Soto la Marina, with reference to the cities of Monterrey, Matamoros, and Ciudad Victoria.

**Biogeographic relationships.** Mexican *Notropis* of the braytoni group are mostly allopatric and morphologically similar. Some species have small maxillary barbels:
- *N. braytoni* (Río Bravo): no barbels.
- *N. aguirrepequenoi* (Soto la Marina): no barbels.
- *Notropis* sp. (Río Pánuco): no barbels; pharyngeal teeth 1,4-4,1.
- *[[Notropis Boucardi]]* (Río Balsas): barbels in 50% of specimens.
- *[[Notropis Moralesi]]* (Río Papaloapan): barbels in 90% of specimens.
- *[[Notropis Imeldae]]* (Río Verde, Oaxaca): barbels in 100% of specimens.

Regan (1908) had synonymized *N. braytoni* and *N. boucardi*, which the authors take as evidence of the group's close relationships. Earlier records of *N. braytoni* from the Soto la Marina and Pánuco are attributed to misidentifications of *N. aguirrepequenoi* and *Notropis* sp., respectively.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *Notropis aguirrepequenoi* is described from 705 specimens at four localities in the Río Soto la Marina, Tamaulipas.
- **Diagnostic combination:** Pharyngeal teeth 4-4 with no barbels separates it from its nearest relatives, which have 1,4-4,1 teeth. Its other diagnostic traits are an oblique subterminal mouth, a convex snout, a black lateral stripe, and a triangular basicaudal spot.
- **Meristic counts:** The summary gives a modal lateral line of 36, predorsals 14 to 16, anal rays 8, and pectorals 14 to 15. The descriptive text gives a modal lateral line of 34 (range 32 to 38), predorsals mode 15 (range 13 to 17), and pectorals mode 14 (range 12 to 16). These modal values differ between sections and should be checked against the source tables.
- **Taxonomic revision needed:** The authors consider the species-level differences within the braytoni group real but note that a complete revision of the group is still required.
- **Habitat preference:** The species is most abundant at 24 °C and tolerates up to 32 °C in shallow, sandy-gravel, moderately flowing arroyos with emergent and submerged vegetation.
- **Conservation relevance:** The species is endemic to one river system, and its records have been confused with other taxa, so accurate identification matters for documenting its range.
- **Publication context:** The paper is one of several titles in the series, which at the time also included a checklist of Nuevo Leon fishes, a catalog of Mexican Notropis reviews, and a Tamaulipas inland fish checklist in press.
