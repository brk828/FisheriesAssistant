# Razorback Sucker Proposed Endangered Listing: Federal Request for Comments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service), Portland, Oregon, to [[W.L. Minckley]] of the Department of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]], Tempe. It transmits a proposal to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and requests public comment. The date is given as "July 9, 1" in the scan; the comment deadline of July 23, 1990 indicates the letter was written in 1990. The Service cites two grounds for the action: no [[Recruitment]] into the adult population has been observed for 30 years, and the adult population has declined over the last 10 years.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Official request for comments accompanying a proposed rule (enclosure not included).
- **Lead offices:** The Denver, Colorado Region and the Salt Lake City Field Office.
- **Comment submission:** To the Field Supervisor, Salt Lake City, Utah, received by July 23, 1990.
- **Contact:** Service biologist [[Patricia Schrader]], Grand Junction, Colorado, telephone 303-243-2778.
- **Signatory:** William E. Martin.

The letter does not describe the species' morphology, life history, or taxonomy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter gives only limited ecological information:

- **Population status:** Adult numbers have fallen over the past decade, and no new adults have been recruited for about 30 years. This pattern is the basis for the listing proposal.
- **Range and distribution:** The Service explicitly asks for data on range, distribution, and population size. The letter does not give current figures, so the scale of the decline is not quantified here.
- **Threats:** Current or planned activities and their possible impacts are named as a comment topic, but no specific threats are identified in the text.
- **Habitat:** The Service requests information on whether certain habitats should be designated as [[Critical Habitat]] under Section 4 of the Endangered Species Act. No habitat types or sites are described.

## Key Findings

1. **Listing proposal:** The Service proposes to list the razorback sucker as endangered.
2. **Recruitment failure:** No recruitment into the adult population has been observed for 30 years.
3. **Adult decline:** The adult population has declined during the last 10 years.
4. **Requested information:** The Service seeks four categories of input:
   - Biological, commercial trade, or other data on threats (or their absence) to the species.
   - Locations of additional populations and reasons for or against designating critical habitat.
   - Further information on range, distribution, and population size.
   - Details of current or planned activities in the study area and their potential impacts.
5. **Deadline:** Comments must reach the Field Supervisor by July 23, 1990.
6. **Process:** The proposal is led by the Denver, Colorado Region and Salt Lake City Field Office, with a [[Species Listing]] decision pending public comment.
