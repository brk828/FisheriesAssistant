# Colorado Squawfish and Bonytail Chub Stocking Request for Arizona State University Research Park

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum, dated October 8 (year not legible), from the Albuquerque, New Mexico office to [[George Divine]], Fisheries Chief for Endangered Species Resources (Region 2). It concerns a request from [[Paul Marsh]] of [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) for federally managed native fishes to be stocked into three ponds at the [[Arizona State University Research Park]] in Tempe, Arizona. The Service concurs with the proposal and agrees to supply the fish. The stated purpose is research on interactions between native and exotic fishes, which the memo describes as needed for species recovery efforts.

## Background & Description

The memo identifies three target species:

- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

The ponds total approximately 20 surface acres. The memo states that permission to use the ponds was obtained by Drs. [[Paul Marsh]] and [[W.L. Minckley]]. The signature block lists Dr. Marsh, Dr. Minckley, and the hatchery manager at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], who would likely handle supply and delivery.

The memo is brief and does not describe the research design, pond conditions, or the origin or genetic background of the fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The stated research aim is to study how native fishes interact with exotic (non-native) fishes in a pond setting. The memo says such work is needed to better understand species interactions and to support [[Species Recovery]] for listed native species. Studies of this kind relate to the broader [[Native-Exotic Fish Interactions]] problem in the southwestern United States.

The document contains no water chemistry, temperature, habitat, invertebrate, or population data. It also gives no information on the exotic species present or planned for the ponds.

## Key Findings

- **Request:** Dr. Marsh asked the Service to supply Colorado squawfish, bonytail chub, and razorback sucker for stocking into three ponds at ASU Research Park.
- **Scale:** The three ponds total approximately 20 surface acres.
- **Quantities requested:** About 2,000 Colorado squawfish and about 2,000 bonytail chub fingerlings, to be stocked "this fall." The memo gives no quantity for razorback sucker, although the species is named in the request.
- **Permission:** Use of the ponds was approved by Drs. Minckley and Marsh.
- **Decision:** The Service concurs with the proposal and will make "every effort" to supply the fish. The memo asks the Fisheries Chief to arrange delivery if the fish are available.
- **Rationale:** The work is intended to clarify native–exotic species interactions and support recovery of the listed fishes.

**Data limitations:** The memo contains no sample sizes beyond the requested stocking numbers, no survey results, and no monitoring or outcome criteria. The year is not legible in the scan.
