# The Fishes of the Lahontan System of Nevada and Northeastern California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #Salmonidae #Cottidae

## Executive Summary

John Otterbein Snyder's Bureau of Fisheries bulletin (1915–16) is a systematic and ecological survey of the freshwater fishes of the [[Lahontan System]], an endorheic basin of about 40,700 square miles in Nevada and northeastern California. The paper recognizes 15 native species, describes four new species (*Catostomus arenarius*, *Richardsonius microdon*, *Leucidius pectinifer*, and three new *Salmo* forms), reviews their biogeographic affinities, links fish distribution to the [[Quaternary Lake Lahontan]] history reconstructed by [[Israel Cook Russell]], and assesses fisheries, introductions and threats. It includes detailed morphometric and meristic tables, notes on spawning and migration, and angling methods.

## Background & Description

The Great Basin fishes occupy closed basins "in much the same position as animals on oceanic islands," isolated from marine dispersal. The Lahontan system comprises six isolated basins: the [[Truckee River]], Honey Lake, Eagle Lake, Quinn River, Walker River and the Carson-Humboldt. The Truckee, the largest stream, drops 2,460 feet in about 100 miles from [[Lake Tahoe]] to [[Pyramid Lake]] and [[Winnemucca Lake]]. Pyramid Lake is brackish, lined with [[Tufa Deposits]], and hosts breeding colonies of water birds. Walker Lake becomes saline toward its lower end, where only carp are said to survive.

Native species are grouped into four families:

- **Catostomidae:** *Catostomus tahoensis*, *C. arenarius*, *Pantosteus lahontan*, *Chasmistes cujus*
- **Cyprinidae:** *Richardsonius egregius*, *R. microdon*, *Siphateles obesus*, *Leucidius pectinifer*, *Agosia robusta*
- **Salmonidae:** *Coregonus williamsoni*, *Salmo henshawi*, *S. aquilarum*, *S. regalis*, *S. smaragdus*
- **Cottidae:** *Cottus beldingi*

Morphometrics follow a standard convention: measurements are expressed in hundredths of body length (snout to end of last vertebra), with meristic counts including lateral-line scales, pharyngeal teeth, gillrakers and fin rays.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geological framework.** Russell's work shows that Lake Lahontan had no outlet, reached a maximum depth of 880 feet, and went through at least two high stands separated by desiccation. Lacustrine marls exceed 150 feet in thickness, and tufa deposits record changes in lake chemistry. A post-Lahontan desiccation ended within roughly the last 300 years. Snyder argues that the absence of a relationship to any single neighboring fauna is consistent with long isolation and with the absence of a large outlet.

**Biogeographic affinities.** Lahontan fishes are most closely allied to the Oregon Lake system, the Columbia, the Bonneville and, to a lesser extent, the Sacramento and Klamath systems. The Colorado fauna is not directly represented. The Lahontan species are classed as channel-connection or stream-capture arrivals. Some species (*Chasmistes cujus*, *Siphateles obesus*, *Leucidius pectinifer*) are lacustrine or lake-associated; *Chasmistes* is also known from Klamath and Utah lakes.

**Hydrology and disturbance.** Streams are fed by snowmelt, warm and silt up on reaching the valleys, and suffer large seasonal volume swings. Irrigation diverts entire flows for periods. Thunderstorm "cloud bursts" can wash out fish populations entirely in upper desert streams. The 1911–12 low water and dam closures at Tahoe City and Derby caused mass trout mortality in the lower Truckee, and reverse flow from Pyramid Lake made the Truckee–Winnemucca channel nearly as brackish as the lakes.

## Key Findings

**Species inventory and status**
- Fifteen native species are recognized; six are of commercial value, including the trouts, the whitefish and the cui-ui.
- *Chasmistes cujus* (cui-ui) is confined to Pyramid and Winnemucca Lakes. Adults ascend the lower Truckee to spawn, typically from about April 15 to mid-May. Snyder notes it "will no doubt disappear" if the lakes become too saline.
- *Salmo smaragdus* (emerald trout) is endemic to Pyramid and Winnemucca Lakes and is apparently rare, with no record in the river.
- *Salmo regalis* (royal silver trout), described from Lake Tahoe, is blue-backed and unspotted and was previously confused with silver forms of *S. henshawi*.
- *Salmo aquilarum* (Eagle Lake trout) is a Sierra-derived rainbow-trout type replacing *S. henshawi* at Eagle Lake; it is severely threatened by spawning-run harvest in Pine Creek.

**Diagnostic results**
- *Catostomus arenarius* is a coarse-scaled sucker with 75 lateral-series scales in the type and resembles *C. warnerensis* of the Oregon Lake system.
- *Leucidius* is a new genus restricted to the system; *L. pectinifer* has 5–5 pharyngeal teeth and 29–36 gillrakers and was earlier confused with *Siphateles obesus*.
- Snyder notes that a careful series of specimens shows no differentiation characteristic of any single basin, implying introduction before separation.

**Trout migrations and angling**
- Two trout runs enter the lower Truckee: the large "redfish" (*S. henshawi*, locally "Tomoo-agaih") from about October–March, and the smaller "tommy" run (April, well over by May 1).
- Trolling with a 4-inch attraction spoon and light bait-casting tackle are recommended; fly fishing is limited to the rivers and high Sierra.

**Introduced species and threats**
- Introduced fishes include carp (regarded as a nuisance), Mackinaw trout, brook trout, brown trout, bluegill, catfish, Sacramento perch, and rainbow trout (*Salmo irideus shasta*), which appears to be displacing the native cutthroat.
- Management priorities: maintain flows and fishways, screen irrigation ditches, prevent pollution, and study the native species' habits before they disappear.

**Management conclusions**
- Snyder argues that conservation depends on "the intelligent application" of knowledge of native habits, with fry planting and poacher arrests as secondary measures.
- Jurisdiction over the system by two states hampers coordinated protection.
