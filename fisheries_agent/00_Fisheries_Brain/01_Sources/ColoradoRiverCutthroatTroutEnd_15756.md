# Colorado River Cutthroat Trout - Endangered Species Petition (1999)

## Executive Summary
This document is a news article (Denver Post, Dec. 10, 1999) reporting on a formal petition filed by a coalition of seven environmental groups requesting that the [[Colorado River Cutthroat Trout]] be listed under the U.S. Endangered Species Act. The petition, submitted to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, cites habitat destruction and the introduction of nonnative species as primary drivers of population decline. The state of Colorado, via the Colorado Division of Wildlife, disputes the necessity of federal listing, arguing that state-led conservation programs are successfully increasing both population numbers and distribution. This is not a primary scientific paper but a policy/conservation-status record documenting the regulatory and ecological debate surrounding the subspecies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historic Range:** West of the Continental Divide in Colorado, Wyoming, eastern Utah, and northwestern New Mexico.
- **Current Range:** Reduced to approximately 5% of historical habitat, occupying 87 streams and 9 lakes in Colorado as of the petition date.
- **Habitat Type:** Coldwater montane streams and lakes; sensitive to water quality, minimum stream flow requirements, and isolation from disease.
- **Threats Identified:**
  - Introduction and competition with nonnative species (notably [[Brook Trout]] and [[Rainbow Trout]])
  - Livestock grazing
  - Logging
  - Mining
  - Water-diversion projects
  - Habitat fragmentation on federal lands
- **Land Management Context:** Most remaining habitat and populations occur on federal lands, a key point of contention regarding whether state vs. federal protections are sufficient.
- **Restoration Method:** Removal of nonnative competitors from smaller streams combined with construction of barriers (dams/waterfalls) to prevent reinvasion by nonnative trout, enabling reintroduction of native cutthroat populations.

## Key Findings
- **Habitat Loss:** The Colorado River cutthroat trout has been extirpated from ~95% of its historical range, primarily due to anthropogenic habitat alteration and species introductions.
- **Petition for Listing:** A coalition including the [[Center for Biological Diversity]], [[Biodiversity Legal Foundation]], and [[Biodiversity Associates]] filed a petition (~Dec. 9, 1999) to list the subspecies under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Regulatory Timeline:** The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service had one year from the petition date to make a listing determination.
- **Conflicting Conservation Assessments:**
  - Environmental groups argue federal protection is necessary given the severity of habitat loss and the fact that most populations occur on federal lands.
  - The Colorado Division of Wildlife (represented by spokesman Todd Malmsbury) contends that state management programs are effective, citing increasing population numbers and distribution, and opposes federal listing as unwarranted.
- **Current Conservation Targets (Colorado):** Existing populations in 87 streams/9 lakes, with a stated goal of expanding to 111 streams and 15 lakes.
- **Barrier to Full Restoration:** Permanent establishment of nonnative [[Brook Trout]] and [[Rainbow Trout]] populations over the past century makes full historical range restoration unfeasible; restoration is instead targeted at isolated headwater stream segments protected by physical barriers.
- **Broader Context:** This case exemplifies a recurring conservation conflict pattern among native North American trout subspecies — see also related cases such as [[Gila Trout]] and [[Apache Trout]] — involving habitat degradation, nonnative species introduction, and disputes between state and federal regulatory jurisdiction.
