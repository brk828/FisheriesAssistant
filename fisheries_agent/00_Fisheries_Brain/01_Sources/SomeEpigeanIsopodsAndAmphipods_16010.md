# Epigean Isopods and Amphipods of Kentucky

#malacostraca #isopoda #amphipoda #asellidae #freshwater-biology

## Executive Summary

This 1957 study by [[Gerald A. Cole]] (Department of Biology, University of Louisville) reports surface-dwelling (epigean) aquatic isopods and amphipods collected from 85 stations in Kentucky, mostly in [[Jefferson County]]. The study covers more than 140 collections gathered over roughly three years, starting in late 1953. It provides a key to the four Kentucky *Asellus* species and the four amphipod genera, notes habitat preferences and breeding seasons, and discusses how often species coexist. Subterranean forms are excluded, and no collections were made in July or August.

## Background & Description

Earlier work on Kentucky malacostracans by [[Packard]] (1871) and [[Hay]] (1902) was often based on brief collecting trips, many to the [[Mammoth Cave]] region, and is now of limited use for identification. Taxonomic clarification came from [[Hubricht]] (1943) and [[Hubricht and Mackin]] (1949). The study was motivated by abundant isopods and amphipods found in freshwater invertebrate collections near Louisville.

Limitations acknowledged by the author:
- Most stations (72) were in Jefferson County.
- Subterranean species were not discussed.
- No collections were made in July or August.

The collections are believed to include most epigean species in the state, with one notable exception, *Crangonyx anomalus*, known from Fayette, Mason and Bourbon counties and possibly restricted to the [[Cincinnati Anticline]].

**Isopod identification.** [[Asellus]] and [[Lirceus]] are separated by the oblique or non-oblique suture on the two-segmented exopodites of the third pleopods. In *Asellus* the suture is nearly transverse, while in *Lirceus* it is markedly oblique. Females have only the first pair of pleopods anterior to the third. Males have three pairs, and in *Asellus* the first two pairs are important for species determination.

**Pleopod key for male *Asellus*.** The author provides a differential table:
- *A. brevicaudus*: first pleopod broadly truncate with long plumose setae; second pleopod protopodite about three times as long as either ramus.
- *A. intermedius*: first pleopod broadly truncate with bristles; protopodite subequal to exopodite; endopodite tip bifid.
- *A. militaris*: first pleopod tip narrow and rounded with short bristles; endopodite tip curved outward and bluntly rounded.
- Undescribed species: endopodite tapered and narrow, with basal extensions.

**Amphipod identification.** Six species from four genera were collected: *Crangonyx*, *Gammarus*, *Hyalella* and *Synurella*. Key characters include:
- Antennae: the first pair is shorter than the second in *Gammarus*, but longer in the others. In *Hyalella* it is markedly shorter.
- Accessory flagellum: absent only in *Hyalella*. It is large in *Gammarus* and small or inconspicuous in *Synurella* and *Crangonyx*.
- Telson: uncleft in *Gammarus*, cleft in the others.
- Third uropod: longer than the telson in *Gammarus*. Its relation to the telson differs among genera.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area is around Louisville, with collection sites in streams, springs, ditches, and temporary and permanent ponds. Several sites are linked to the [[Ohio River]] floodplain. The author notes that [[Morgan Cave]] in Otter Creek Park (Meade County) has a stream that supported dense *Gammarus minus* populations, though none were found inside the cave.

Habitat patterns noted in the text:
- *Lirceus fontinalis* was present at 85% of collecting sites. It occurred in intermittent streams and ditches, and was the only isopod in shallow, rocky upper creek reaches.
- *Asellus militaris* is nearly restricted to [[Temporary Ponds]]. The one exception was specimens from Mill Creek in November, possibly carried in during high water from nearby ponds.
- *Asellus intermedius* was common in springs and streams but absent from temporary ponds and shallow headwaters.
- *Crangonyx gracilis* was taken from permanent ponds, where other species were rare.
- *Synurella dentata* was absent from both pond types and was found in dense watercress in a spring brook.
- *Hyalella azteca* was found at a single station, a permanent flood-plain pond with few weedy ponds nearby.

Interspecific relationships discussed by the author:
- Isopods: [[Lirceus fontinalis]] coexisted with all other isopod species. Two *Asellus* species were rarely found together, though *A. intermedius* and *A. brevicaudus* were both taken in one stream collection.
- Amphipods: where several species co-occurred, one was usually predominant. *Synurella* dominated most streams, while *Crangonyx* (usually *C. shoemakeri*) outnumbered it in springs, grassy ditches and most spring brooks.
- The author suggests that [[Competition]] may restrict *Hyalella* and *Gammarus*, though their rarity limits conclusions.

Reproduction timing (from the text):
- *A. brevicaudus*: pairing in February, gravid females in May.
- *A. intermedius*: pairing in late February, gravid females March to May.
- *A. militaris*: males carrying females under ice on December 26, 1954; gravid females from early January to late April.
- *Lirceus fontinalis*: mainly spring breeding, with some gravid females in November and December.
- *Crangonyx shoemakeri*: peak in March and April in Jefferson County; gravid females from late December to mid-May.
- *Gammarus minus*: males carrying females on December 31, 1956.

## Key Findings

- **Survey scope:** Epigean isopods and amphipods were collected from 85 stations and more than 140 collections, mostly in Jefferson County (72 stations).
- **Isopod species** (by frequency): *Lirceus fontinalis*, *Asellus intermedius*, *A. militaris*, *A. brevicaudus*, and an undescribed *Asellus* species.
- **Amphipod species** (by frequency): *Synurella dentata*, *Crangonyx shoemakeri*, *C. gracilis*, *C. obliquus*, *Gammarus minus*, and *Hyalella azteca*. Six species in total.
- **Most abundant taxa:** *Lirceus fontinalis* was the most common aquatic malacostracan in Jefferson County. *Synurella dentata* was the most frequently encountered amphipod.
- **Restricted taxa:** *Asellus militaris* is largely confined to temporary ponds. *Hyalella azteca* was found at only one station. *Crangonyx obliquus* was recorded at seven stations (Jefferson 6, Oldham 1).
- **Coexistence:** *Lirceus* co-occurred with all other isopod species and with all amphipods except *Hyalella*. Two *Asellus* species were rarely found together.
- **Taxonomic caution:** Van Name (1936) described *Asellus communis* as the most abundant freshwater isopod in the eastern United States. The author argues that this name covers several distinct species, and that non-specialists often misapply "Asellus" and "Gammarus" to isopods and amphipods, respectively. Identifications around Louisville are therefore unlikely to be correct.
- **Identification aids:** Pleopod morphology (for *Asellus*) and antennal, telson and uropod characters (for amphipods) are presented as practical keys, with the caveat that the *Asellus* table may fail when more species are considered.
- **Acknowledgments:** Species determinations were aided by J. G. Mackin and Leslie Hubricht.
