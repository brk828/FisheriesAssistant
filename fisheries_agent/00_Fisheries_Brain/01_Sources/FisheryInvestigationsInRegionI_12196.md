# Arizona Game and Fish Department Fishery Investigations - Creel Census (Lakes) 1965

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #recreational-fishing #creel-census

## Executive Summary

This completion report documents creel census activities conducted across seven regions of Arizona from January to December 1965. The Arizona Game and Fish Department gathered data on fishing success, species composition, angler origin, and fishing methods across multiple lakes including [[Big Lake]], [[Lyman Lake]], [[Rainbow Lake]], [[Show Low Lake]], [[Woods Canyon Lake]], [[Apache Lake]], [[Bartlett Lake]], [[Canyon Lake]], [[Pleasant Lake]], [[Saguaro Lake]], [[Roosevelt Lake]], [[San Carlos Lake]], [[Parker Canyon Lake]], [[Pena Blanca Lake]], [[Rose Canyon Lake]], [[Riggs Flat Lake]], and [[Frye Mesa Lake]], plus [[Lake Mead]] and the [[Martinez Lake]] area. The census employed scheduled spot-check methodology with anglers interviewed at checking stations or while fishing. Overall catch rates varied by water body from 0.11 to 2.0 fish per man-hour, with [[Rainbow Trout]] being the primary species in high-elevation lakes and [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Crappie]] dominating warm-water fisheries.

## Background & Description

The creel census program was implemented as Project FS-1, Work Plans 1-7, Job C-1, covering all major fishery regions in Arizona. Census methodology consisted of scheduled spot checks with a minimum of two weekdays and two weekend days per month on most waters, conducted during ice-free periods or year-round depending on the water body. Data collectors interviewed anglers either at checking stations or while fishing, recording species and length of fish harvested, angler origin (county of residence), hours fished, total fish taken, fishing method (prepared baits, artificial lures, flies, worms), and percentage of unlicensed juvenile anglers. Census periods varied by region: high-elevation trout lakes were typically censused May through September or October, while warm-water reservoirs received year-round or February-through-October coverage.

The program covered diverse aquatic habitats from high-elevation mountain lakes (e.g., [[Big Lake]] at approximately 9,000 feet elevation) to large desert reservoirs ([[Roosevelt Lake]], [[San Carlos Lake]]) and Colorado River impoundments ([[Lake Mead]], [[Martinez Lake]] area). Waters censused ranged from small mountain lakes under 100 surface acres to large reservoirs exceeding 17,000 acres at full pool.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 1965 census captured fisheries during varied environmental conditions. Several waters experienced significant water level fluctuations: [[Lake Pleasant]] suffered from two years of low water and poor fishing success until excellent 1965 run-off filled the lake to capacity by year's end. [[Pena Blanca Lake]] remained low through summer until a Labor Day weekend storm raised water above the spillway. Spring and fall run-off in some Region I waters contributed to fish loss over spillways.

Temperature and elevation strongly influenced species distribution and fishing patterns. High-elevation trout lakes showed seasonal patterns with best fishing typically in spring and early summer, declining in late summer as water temperatures rose. [[Rainbow Trout]] dominated cold-water fisheries, with [[Brook Trout]] present in some waters. Warm-water fisheries were characterized by [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Crappie]], and [[Carp]]. Some waters like [[Pena Blanca Lake]] supported both trout (winter-spring) and warm-water species (summer), with trout fishing ending by July.

Stocking programs heavily influenced catch composition. Fingerling [[Rainbow Trout]] plants (ranging from 10,000 to 60,000 per lake) were supplemented by catchable-size trout in many waters. The timing of fingerling plants proved critical: June plants in Region I arrived two months later than the preferred April timing, resulting in fish too small for harvest during peak summer fishing and contributing to mortality from handling by anglers who released undersized fish. Growth rates varied considerably by water body, with some lakes showing poor growth attributed to high population densities and competition.

Spawning success varied by species and location. [[Largemouth Bass]] spawning and survival was traditionally better in [[Bartlett Lake]] and [[Lake Pleasant]] than in [[Apache Lake]], [[Canyon Lake]], and [[Saguaro Lake]], resulting in higher percentages of young bass in populations. [[Pena Blanca Lake]] witnessed good bass spawns with fish on beds until mid-June, and excellent [[Threadfin Shad]] spawns with numerous fingerling schools observed in August-September.

## Key Findings

**Region I (Northern Arizona Mountain Lakes):**
- [[Big Lake]]: 1.04 fish per man-hour (FPMH), average [[Rainbow Trout]] length 9.64 inches, [[Brook Trout]] 9.71 inches
- [[Lyman Lake]]: 0.37 FPMH, average [[Rainbow Trout]] 10.65 inches (largest average among Region I lakes)
- [[Rainbow Lake]]: 0.44 FPMH, average [[Rainbow Trout]] 10.31 inches
- [[Show Low Lake]]: 0.42 FPMH, average [[Rainbow Trout]] 8.70 inches
- [[Woods Canyon Lake]]: 0.36 FPMH, average [[Rainbow Trout]] 9.51 inches
- Maricopa County residents comprised the largest angler group at [[Big Lake]], [[Show Low Lake]], and [[Woods Canyon Lake]]
- Unlicensed juveniles comprised 10-20% of total fishermen
- Prepared baits were the most popular fishing method; fly fishermen represented ≤3% of anglers

**Region II (Williams Area Lakes):**
- Overall success: 0.61 FPMH across all lakes
- [[Santa Fe Lake]]: 0.73 FPMH (highest); [[Kaibab Lake]]: 0.50 FPMH (lowest)
- [[Cataract Lake]]: 0.67 FPMH; [[White Horse Lake]]: 0.62 FPMH; [[Lynx Lake]]: 0.47 FPMH
- Late June fingerling plants (vs. preferred April timing) resulted in fish too small for summer harvest and increased mortality
- [[Lynx Lake]] could not sustain satisfactory fishing on fingerling plants alone; catchables planted in late summer
- Spring fingerling growth was poorer than 1964, attributed to late planting
- Fishing pressure increased on [[Kaibab Lake]] due to larger fish availability
- 90-97% of anglers used bait; only 2-10% used artificial lures/flies
- Unlicensed juveniles: 14.8-36.4% of anglers (highest at [[Santa Fe Lake]] due to proximity to Williams)

**Region III (Lake Mead and Topock Areas):**
- [[Lake Mead]]: 38 man-days, 857 anglers, 1,003 fish, 3,120.5 hours, 0.32 FPMH
  - Species composition: 67.5% [[Largemouth Bass]] (avg 13.1"), 19.7% [[Crappie]] (11.7"), 11.7% [[Channel Catfish]] (13.2")
- [[Topock Marsh]]-[[Topock Gorge]]: 29 man-days, 197 anglers, 141 fish, 466.5 hours, 0.30 FPMH
  - Species: 53.9% [[Largemouth Bass]] (13.5"), 27.7% [[Crappie]] (11.9"), 9.2% [[Channel Catfish]] (14.0"), 5.7% [[Rainbow Trout]] (15.6")
- Fishing quality rated "fair" based on catch rates

**Region IV (Martinez Lake Area, Lower Colorado River):**
- 998 anglers, 2,821 hours, 1,020 fish, 0.36 FPMH (declined from 0.44 in 1964)
- Angler origin: 61.4% California, 38.1% Arizona, 0.4% other states
- Species composition: 25.9% [[Largemouth Bass]] (avg 12.0"), 23.7% [[Bluegill]] (6.7"), 30.1% [[Crappie]], 19.0% [[Channel Catfish]] (14.3")
- [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Channel Catfish]] showed marked increases in percent of total catch vs. 1964

**Region V (Central Arizona Reservoirs):**
- Fishing pressure increased on [[Apache Lake]], [[Bartlett Lake]], and [[Saguaro Lake]]; declined on [[Lake Pleasant]]
- Total 9-month estimates: 499,644 angling hours, 166,286 anglers, 193,415 fish harvested
- [[Apache Lake]]: 0.33 FPMH, 46,595 anglers, 144,444 hours
  - 30.9% [[Largemouth Bass]] (14.4"), 19.8% [[Channel Catfish]] (13.6"), 32.4% [[Bluegill]], 16.2% [[Green Sunfish]]
- [[Bartlett Lake]]: 0.43 FPMH, 41,701 anglers, 133,443 hours
  - 48.5% [[Largemouth Bass]] (10.9"), 31.8% [[Bluegill]], 11.1% [[Channel Catfish]] (11.0")
- [[Canyon Lake]]: 0.46 FPMH, 31,896 anglers, 73,360 hours
  - 62.4% [[Bluegill]], 17.8% [[Green Sunfish]], 10.3% [[Largemouth Bass]] (13.7")
- [[Pleasant Lake]]: 0.32 FPMH, 18,356 anglers, 62,410 hours
  - 27.2% [[Bluegill]], 35.4% [[Green Sunfish]], 22.1% [[Largemouth Bass]] (10.8")
- [[Saguaro Lake]]: 0.40 FPMH, 27,738 anglers, 85,987 hours
  - 16.7% [[Largemouth Bass]] (13.9"), 10.0% [[Yellow Bass]] (9.5"), 3.2% [[Bluegill]]
- Unlicensed juveniles: 8-17% of anglers (0% data missing for [[Bartlett Lake]])
- Maricopa County was primary angler source for most lakes; Pima County dominated at [[Lake Pleasant]]

**Region VI (Southern Arizona Lakes):**
- [[Parker Canyon Lake]]: 2,094 anglers, 4,264 [[Rainbow Trout]], 0.81 FPMH, 62% success rate
  - Average trout size: 6.9-8.0 inches (small, with complaints from anglers)
  - Only 216 of 4,264 trout exceeded 8.0 inches; largest checked was 14.0 inches
  - Poor growth attributed to high population density; stocking reduced from 91,000 (1964) to 60,000 fingerlings (1965)
  - One 13.0-inch [[Largemouth Bass]] caught (unauthorized introduction concern)
  - 3,000 [[Channel Catfish]] fingerlings planted experimentally for summer fishery
  - Angler origin: 35% Tucson, 34% Ft. Huachuca-Sierra Vista, 16% Bisbee-Douglas
- [[Pena Blanca Lake]]: 1,837 anglers, 7,925.5 hours, 2,339 fish, 0.30 FPMH
  - Species: 40.5% [[Rainbow Trout]], 28.3% [[Largemouth Bass]], 22.6% [[Green Sunfish]], 6.5% [[Crappie]], 1.8% [[Channel Catfish]]
  - Trout fishing November-March (84% of catch); only one trout caught in July
  - Bass harvest nearly doubled vs. 1964 but size remained small (only 14 bass >15 inches)
  - 7,250 catchable [[Rainbow Trout]] stocked in four monthly plants
  - Good bass and [[Threadfin Shad]] spawns observed
- [[Rose Canyon Lake]]: 665 anglers, 827 [[Rainbow Trout]], 23 [[Bluegill]], 0.9 FPMH
  - 22,000 catchable [[Rainbow Trout]] stocked
  - Most trout caught within 72 hours of each plant

**Region VII (Eastern Arizona):**
- [[Roosevelt Lake]]: 37 days census, 1,323 anglers, 1,630 fish, 0.37 FPMH (down from 0.41 in 1964)
  - Species: 43.3% [[Largemouth Bass]] (avg varied by month), 32.4% [[Bluegill]], 16.2% [[Channel Catfish]]
  - Bass per hour: 0.16 (down from 0.19 in 1964)
  - July had highest success: 0.75 FPMH
  - 56.7% of anglers from Maricopa County, 20.1% from Pima County
- [[San Carlos Lake]]: 40 days census, 1,362 anglers, 1,948 fish, 0.37 FPMH (down from 0.46 in 1964)
  - 87.1% of catch was [[Largemouth Bass]] (0.31 FPMH), 4.8% [[Channel Catfish]]
  - February most successful: 0.71 FPMH
  - 44.4% of anglers from Pima County, 16.8% from Maricopa County
- [[Riggs Flat Lake]]: 11 days census, 352 anglers, 798 trout, 0.68 FPMH (down from 0.90 in 1964)
  - Decline attributed to increased fishing pressure
  - Large [[Rainbow Trout]] taken in spring; significant [[Brook Trout]] numbers in late spring-summer
  - 14.8% unlicensed juveniles

**Management Recommendations:**
- Continue present creel census methods and schedules
- [[Parker Canyon Lake]]: Stock no more than 10,000 fingerling trout in spring 1966; fall stocking contingent on growth; no additional warm-water fish introductions
- Region II: Make spring fingerling plants in April if possible (not June); adjust catchable plants by lake
- [[Lynx Lake]]: Weekly catchable plants (minimum 1,000) from late May through early September to sustain fishery
- [[Santa Fe Lake]]: Discontinue catchable plants; increase fingerling plants (fall and spring)
- [[Riggs Flat Lake]]: Stock fingerling [[Brook Trout]] if available, given good returns from 1964 planting
- [[White Horse Lake]]: Continue catchable plants; fingerling plants of 10,000 fall + 20,000 spring recommended
- If fingerling trout stocked in Salt River below Stewart Mountain Dam, conduct creel census October-December
