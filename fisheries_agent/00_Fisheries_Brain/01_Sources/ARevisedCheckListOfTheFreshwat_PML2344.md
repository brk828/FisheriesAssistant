# A Revised Check List of the Freshwater and Anadromous Fishes of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a revised official checklist of the freshwater and anadromous fishes of California, prepared by [[Shapovalov and Dill]] for the [[California Department of Fish and Game]]. It follows a 1950 original edition and aims to (1) provide an accurate inventory as the first step toward a detailed handbook, and (2) promote stability and uniformity in common and scientific names. The main list covers 110 full species, including native forms and established exotic species. A supplementary list covers exotic species that were unsuccessfully introduced or of uncertain occurrence, and a separate section covers marine and brackish species established in the [[Salton Sea]].

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** The authors state that the state's fish fauna is not stable. Nomenclature has changed through taxonomic revision, and the list is intended to replace the obsolete [[Evermann and Clark]] inventory (1931) as the reference for professionals and laypeople.
- **Scientific names:** Stability depends on careful taxonomy. The authors quote [[Schultz]] (1957) that generic recognition requires comprehensive family-level study, and they adopt a conservative approach: new genera or species should not be proposed without adequate supporting evidence.
- **Subspecies and clines:** Geographic variation is often clinal, a gradual gradient rather than discrete units. The authors cite [[Bailey]] (1956) and [[Bailey, Winn, and Smith]] (1954) in favoring binomials over trinomials wherever published evidence shows a cline. Trinomials are retained where justified.
- **Common names:** Names follow five criteria, including agreement with names in actual common use, consistency with other authoritative lists, indication of relationships, and descriptiveness. Common names are assigned to every species and subspecies, and related species share a base name (for example, all [[Chub]] species of the genus *Gila*-related group carry "chub"; subspecies receive additional prefixes such as Sacramento for *Siphateles bicolor formosa*).
- **Scope:** The main list includes native forms and successfully established exotics. The authors favor inclusion of native forms whose taxonomic identity has not been disproven, and they exclude fishes recorded only from outside the state unless they inhabit streams crossing the boundary (for example, the [[Colorado River]] and [[Truckee River]] systems).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fauna at risk:** Several native taxa are extinct or extremely rare, including [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]], [[Gila Bonytail]], and the [[Thicktail Chub]]. Records of *Plagopterus argentissimus* rest on a single 1890 collection.
- **Introduction pathways:** Introductions came from state stocking programs, bait-minnow farms along the lower [[Colorado River]], and accidental transfers with other shipments (the log perch was introduced inadvertently with largemouth bass and bluegill).
- **Salton Sea:** This inland body of water is saline, approaching ocean salinity. Several marine species established breeding populations there, although some (such as [[Mullet]]) are declining because of lack of recruitment after new dams and headgates cut off access.
- **Colorado River:** Threadfin shad, red shiner, and other introduced forage fishes became established in this system, with some evidence of population eruption.

## Key Findings

**Checklist structure**
- The revised main list contains 110 full species in 26 families and 63 genera, according to the text. Species introduced into California are marked with an asterisk, and marine or brackish species that occasionally enter fresh water are marked separately.
- Since the 1950 edition, 11 species and seven subspecies have been added. Several are forage or game species imported by the California Department of Fish and Game.
- Some taxa were removed after taxonomic revision. Examples include [[Salmo gairdneri rosei]] (identity disproved) and some subspecies reduced to variants of other forms.

**Salton Sea established species**
- Three species from the main list are firmly established: [[Sacramento Perch]]-related *Cyprinodon macularius*, *Gambusia affinis*, and *Gillichthys mirabilis*.
- Marine species with established breeding populations include [[Sargo]] (*Anisotremus davidsonii*, introduced 1951), [[Gulf Croaker]] (*Bairdiella icistia*, introduced October 1950), [[Shortfin Corvina]] (*Cynoscion parvipinnis*, reproducing but may be displaced), and [[Orangemouth Corvina]] (*Cynoscion xanthulus*, present in large and increasing numbers).
- *Dorosoma petenense* ([[Threadfin Shad]]) is abundant in the sea, but no successful spawning was documented there.

**Recent additions and introductions**
- [[Threadfin Shad]]: about 520 fish were planted in [[Lake Havasu]] in November 1954 and 500 more in March 1955. By the end of 1955 the species occupied the Colorado River from Davis Dam to the Mexican border and the Salton Sea.
- [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*): probably escaped from Arizona fish farms; a broodstock of 368 adults was taken to Central Valleys Hatchery in April 1954, and 600 fish were stocked in two private ponds in 1957.
- [[Fathead Minnow]]: 40,000 were imported under permit in 1953, and the department purchased 1,500 for hatchery propagation as forage.
- [[Kamloops Rainbow Trout]]: the first known introduction was 1,000 fish in June 1950 into tributaries of Shasta Lake.
- [[Log Perch]]: first recorded in March 1958 from three lakes in Yuba County; breeding populations were confirmed.
- [[Red-Ear Sunfish]]: 3,960 fingerlings were imported in 1954, and breeding populations are now widespread.
- [[Splake]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis* × *S. namaycush*): stocked experimentally in high lakes from 1955 as part of the trout management study; its success had not been evaluated.
- [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Mollie]] (*Mollienesia latipinna*) are established in canals near the Salton Sea.

**Taxonomic revisions noted**
- *Cottus bairdii* complex: [[Robins and Miller]] (1957) treated western forms as distinct, but the authors synonymized *C. bairdii* with *C. bairdii shasti* and *C. b. semiscaber* as recommended.
- *Cottus micropos* was synonymized with *C. hypselurus* after a comparison of type material and collections.
- *Catostomus occidentalis* subspecies *lactarius* and *Ptychocheilus* forms were reviewed, with some subspecies recognized and others reduced.

**Editorial policy**
- The authors aim to revise the list every one to ten years.
- The main goal is to balance stability and taxonomic accuracy. Where a common name conflicts with relationships, the authors prefer changing the name to reflect the correct relationship, and they note that most common names remain recognizable despite such changes.
