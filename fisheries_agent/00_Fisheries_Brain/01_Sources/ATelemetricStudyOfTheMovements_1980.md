# A Telemetric Study of the Movements of the Razorback Sucker During Spawning Season in Senator Wash Reservoir, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #freshwater-ecology #biotelemetry

## Executive Summary
This document is a research proposal (with associated CVs) describing a planned four-month (February–June 1980) ultrasonic telemetry study of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) movements during the spawning season in Senator Wash Reservoir, California. The study is designed to supplement work already conducted by the California Department of Fish and Game and aims to identify preferred spawning habitat, characterize spawning behavior via direct visual/SCUBA observation, and correlate fish location data with physical and chemical environmental parameters. The proposal is motivated by the species' historic decline in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] due to dam construction and flow alteration, and by the puzzling absence of juvenile suckers in a reservoir that otherwise appears to meet known spawning habitat requirements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historic Range:** Once abundantly distributed throughout the mainstream Colorado River system and its tributaries.
- **Habitat Alteration:** Populations declined sharply over ~20 years preceding the study due to altered flow rates/regimes from construction of hydroelectric [[Dam Construction (Colorado River)|dams]] during the mid-20th century.
- **Current Range:** By the time of writing, only one population existed below [[Davis Dam]] — the population residing in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]].
- **Spawning Ecology:**
  - Spawning season: typically March–May, triggered when water temperature reaches ~12°C.
  - Spawning substrate: shallow, submerged areas with **gravel bottoms**.
  - Senator Wash Reservoir reportedly meets the basic physical requirements for spawning, yet **no juvenile razorback suckers had ever been recorded there** — a central mystery motivating the study.
- **Habitat Parameters of Interest:** water temperature, depth, light intensity, bottom particle size distribution, pH, dissolved oxygen, conductivity, and oxidation-reduction potential were all slated for measurement to build a predictive model of preferred spawning habitat.
- **Interspecific Interactions:** The study also aimed to investigate possible social aggregations during spawning and any interspecific interactions occurring on spawning grounds.

## Key Findings
*(Note: This document is a research proposal, not a results paper; the "findings" below represent stated objectives, rationale, and proposed methodology rather than completed results.)*

1. **Population Status:** Razorback sucker numbers have declined and range has contracted sharply in the lower Colorado River basin over the prior 20 years; only one known population remained (Senator Wash Reservoir) below Davis Dam.
2. **Central Research Puzzle:** Despite reservoir conditions seemingly meeting basic spawning requirements (gravel substrate, appropriate shallow areas), no juvenile suckers had ever been documented in Senator Wash Reservoir — suggesting either spawning failure, egg/fry mortality, or unmonitored recruitment issues.
3. **Proposed Methodology:**
   - Ultrasonic telemetry using individually-tagged fish (tagged internally in January 1980 by CA Dept. of Fish and Game).
   - Tracking via tunable narrow-band receiver + directional hydrophone from a small boat, using compass/triangulation from land reference points.
   - Monitoring schedule: 2-hour interval tracking for 24 hours, 10 days/month; dusk/dawn position plotting 8 times/month.
   - Direct SCUBA observation to supplement telemetry once movement patterns were established, to document spawning behavior, feeding activity, and social aggregations.
   - Statistical analysis via SPSS, correlating fish location with depth, temperature, reservoir zone, month, day, time of day, water level fluctuation, and water chemistry.
4. **Objectives:**
   - Determine preferred spawning areas via movement data.
   - Qualitatively define spawning grounds and associated behavior through visual observation.
   - Quantitatively define spawning habitat via environmental/chemical parameter monitoring.
   - Assess specific habitat requirements necessary for successful spawning in the reservoir.
   - (Secondary) Investigate diel movement patterns and social/interspecific interactions.
5. **Comparative Literature Context:** The proposal references prior telemetric fish studies as methodological precedent, including [[Gaiduk et al. 1971]] (swimming depth of pike, salmon, sturgeon in Russia) and the review by [[Stasko and Pincock 1977]] on underwater biotelemetry emphasizing ultrasonic techniques in freshwater fishes.
6. **Budget/Logistics:** Total proposed project cost was $3,000, covering diving equipment, four months of graduate assistant manpower, travel, and trailer rental; major equipment supplied by the California Department of Fish and Game.
