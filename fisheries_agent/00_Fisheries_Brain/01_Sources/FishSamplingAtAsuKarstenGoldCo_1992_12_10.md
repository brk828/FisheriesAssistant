# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, Autumn 1992

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This memorandum (Paul C. Marsh, Arizona State University, 10 December 1992) reports results of fish sampling conducted at the **ASU Karsten Golf Course (ASUGC)** water features in Tempe, Arizona, to assess the status of three federally significant native Colorado River fishes — [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — stocked as a refuge/grow-out population. These species originated from the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Research Center** (Dexter, New Mexico) and were introduced to ASUGC in September–October 1988, making surviving individuals ~4 years old at time of sampling. An additional cohort of 549 PIT-tagged bonytail (mean 381 mm TL) was transferred from Dexter in April 1991.

Sampling combined **entanglement netting** (trammel and experimental gill nets) for larger fish and **seining** for smaller fish across both North and South Lakes. Native fish catch was low (5 individuals total: 3 bonytail, 2 razorback sucker); Colorado squawfish was not captured. Seining recovered nearly 1,500 non-native fish across three species, with no juvenile native fish detected, suggesting either absent recruitment or a sampling/survival limitation affecting the native population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Constructed, urban lentic (lake) habitat within a golf course — an atypical, ex-situ refuge setting for species native to large, historically riverine/floodplain habitats of the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Native species assessed**: [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — all federally listed, imperiled big-river Cyprinidae/Catostomidae taxa maintained here as a conservation/refuge population outside their natural range.
- **Co-occurring non-native community**: The lakes host an established assemblage of non-native fishes, including [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Common Carp]], [[Grass Carp]] (intentionally stocked by ASUGC and exempted from removal), [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], and Lepomis hybrids — indicating a mixed, human-modified fish community with strong competitive/predatory potential toward native fishes.
- **Trophic/size structure**: Seine catches were dominated by small-bodied, likely nest/opportunistic spawners — [[Red Shiner]] (most abundant, 48.6–87.1% of catch by lake), [[Threadfin Shad]], and juvenile [[Green Sunfish]] — species known to compete with or prey upon early life stages of native fishes, a documented threat to Colorado River native fish recruitment elsewhere.
- **Management practice**: Non-native fish were actively removed upon capture (except stocked grass carp), reflecting an active management strategy to reduce competitive/predatory pressure on the native refuge population.
- **Sampling design**: Entanglement nets targeted larger native/non-native fish (short-duration sets, 3–6 hrs, at sunset, to minimize stress/mortality); seining (3 × 1.2 m, 0.3 cm mesh) targeted small-bodied fish along lake perimeters after dark.

## Key Findings

1. **Native catch was extremely low**: Only 5 native fish were captured across all effort — 3 [[Bonytail]] and 2 [[Razorback Sucker]] — despite a known stocked population of hundreds of individuals (549 PIT-tagged bonytail alone from 1991).
2. **Colorado squawfish was not detected**, consistent with prior difficulty collecting this species in other urban lake settings.
3. **No juvenile native fish were captured**, despite one razorback sucker being in "ripe" (reproductive) condition — suggesting that even if spawning occurs, there is **no evidence of recruitment to the juvenile stage**, a critical concern for reintroduction success.
4. **Non-native catch was overwhelmingly larger** in the seine samples (~1,486 individuals) than native catch, dominated by [[Red Shiner]] (1,056 individuals, ~71% of total seine catch), followed by [[Threadfin Shad]] (160) and [[Green Sunfish]] (261).
5. Entanglement net totals (131 individuals) were dominated by non-natives — [[Common Carp]] (42%) and [[Threadfin Shad]] (35%) — with natives (bonytail + razorback sucker) comprising only ~4% of this catch.
6. Four possible explanations were proposed for low native catch: (1) reduced activity at cooler autumn temperatures, (2) cumulative post-stocking mortality, (3) fish occupying under-sampled microhabitats, or (4) unknown factors affecting catchability at sampling times.
7. One captured razorback sucker showed **emaciation**, raising concern about condition/survivorship of the refuge population.
8. Follow-up plans included **light-trap sampling for larval fish** (winter–spring) and continued **entanglement/seine sampling** (spring–early summer) to better resolve recruitment status and population trends via PIT-tag recapture data.
