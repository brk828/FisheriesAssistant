# Seasonal Variations in the Growth Hormone Content of Pituitary Glands of the Perch (*Perca fluviatilis*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #endocrinology #growth-hormone

## Executive Summary
This 1962 conference abstract reports on a bioassay-based investigation into the seasonal fluctuation of growth-hormone (GH) potency in the pituitary glands of the [[European Perch]] (*Perca fluviatilis*), collected from Windermere lake in the UK. Pituitary extracts from perch sampled across different months were injected into hypophysectomized [[Fundulus Heteroclitus]] (mummichog) as a bioassay host to quantify growth-promoting activity via increase in body length. The study found that pituitary GH potency rises sharply in early spring (coinciding with the onset of the perch's natural growth season) and then declines during the summer months of peak somatic growth, suggesting an inverse relationship between hormone storage/potency and active growth utilization.

## Background & Description
- **Study organism (hormone source):** *Perca fluviatilis* (Perch) — pituitary glands were excised, lyophilized, and homogenized in 0.6% NaCl at a concentration of 10 mg/ml to create an injectable "brei" (slurry).
- **Bioassay host:** Male *[[Fundulus Heteroclitus]]* — hypophysectomized (pituitary surgically removed) 8–9 weeks prior to testing, rendering them dependent on exogenous GH for growth.
- **Morphological endpoint measured:** Percentage increase in body length of the bioassay host fish, used as a proxy for pituitary GH potency.
- No direct morphological description of the perch itself is given beyond the collection and processing of pituitary tissue.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Natural growth cycle of Perch:** Perch in [[Windermere]] exhibit a pronounced annual growth cycle, initiating rapid growth in **May** and ceasing growth by **October**.
- **Thermal ecology:** Maximum somatic growth occurs during **July and August**, correlating with water temperatures exceeding **12°C**. This contrasts with [[Trout]], which show two annual growth peaks tied to an optimum temperature of exactly 12°C.
- **Comparative teleost growth strategy:** The perch's single-peak summer growth strategy is presented as distinct from the bimodal growth pattern observed in salmonids, highlighting species-specific interactions between [[Water Temperature]] and endocrine-driven growth in [[Teleost Fishes]].
- **Bioassay host ecology:** *Fundulus heteroclitus* was maintained under controlled conditions (20°C, salt water, 8-hour daily illumination, ad libitum feeding) to standardize the physiological environment for GH sensitivity testing.

## Key Findings
- **Experimental design:** Eight hypophysectomized *F. heteroclitus* per treatment group received intraperitoneal injections of perch pituitary extract three times weekly for 6 weeks (dose: 100 µg in 0.01 ml/injection/gram body weight).
- **Controls:**
  - Saline-only controls (0.6% NaCl): body length **decreased by 0.16%**.
  - Beef growth hormone (NIH-1) positive controls (20 µg/gram): body length **increased by 48% ± 1.1 SE**.
- **Seasonal Perch Pituitary GH Potency (% length increase in bioassay fish):**

| Sample Month | Increase in Length (%) | Standard Error |
|---|---|---|
| February | 48 | — |
| April | 6.1 | 0.8 |
| June | 8.9 | 1.0 |
| July | 5.7 | 1.2 |
| August | 2.0 | 0.6 |
| September | 5.6 | 1.0 |
| November | 48 | 0.9 |

- **Core interpretation:** Pituitary GH potency is **highest in winter (February, November)** — comparable to the potent beef GH positive control — and **drops sharply through the summer**, reaching a minimum in **August** despite August being within the perch's peak natural somatic growth period.
- **Proposed physiological mechanism:** The onset of the perch's natural rapid growth phase (spring) is preceded/accompanied by a sharp **rise** in pituitary GH potency, but during the sustained rapid growth phase itself (summer), pituitary potency becomes **greatly reduced** — likely reflecting active hormone secretion/depletion from the gland into circulation rather than reduced synthesis, i.e., the gland is releasing GH faster than it is stored during peak growth demand.
- **Broader significance:** This work provides early quantitative evidence linking seasonal environmental cues (temperature, day length) to [[Pituitary Gland]] endocrine activity and whole-organism growth regulation in freshwater [[Percidae]], using a cross-species hypophysectomized bioassay model.
