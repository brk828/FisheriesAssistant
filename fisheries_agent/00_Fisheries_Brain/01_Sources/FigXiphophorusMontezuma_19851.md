## Executive Summary
This document presents the original taxonomic description of **Xiphophorus montezumae** Jordan & Snyder, a newly described poeciliid fish notable for the pronounced sexual dimorphism in males, including an elongated "sword"-like extension of the lower caudal fin rays and a modified anal fin (gonopodium) used for internal fertilization. The text details meristic counts, scalation, coloration, and considerable intraspecific variation observed among type specimens, particularly in fin morphology and pigmentation patterns.

## Background & Description
The species is described from type material with the following key morphological features:

**Body & Head:**
- Body strongly compressed; dorsal contour arched, highest at the insertion of the dorsal fin
- Caudal peduncle narrow and very deep
- Head small and pointed; interorbital space wide, slightly convex
- Eye large, positioned slightly nearer the snout tip than the posterior edge of the opercle
- Mouth oriented vertically

**Dentition:**
- Two tooth series in the jaws
- Outer row: minute, flat, pointed teeth, arranged singly
- Inner row: villiform band of much smaller teeth, strongly recurved

**Gill Apparatus:**
- Gill opening extends above the pectoral base a distance equal to the pupil diameter
- Gill rakers: 19 on the first arch, slender, longest raker equal to half the eye diameter

**Internal Anatomy:**
- Intestinal canal slender and elongate
- Peritoneum black (a taxonomically useful pigmentation trait in [[Poeciliidae]])

**Scalation:**
- Large scales on head and body
- One enlarged round scale on the interorbital space, followed by a row of 11 scales extending to the first dorsal ray
- 3 rows of scales at the base of the caudal fin

**Fin Morphology:**
- **Dorsal fin:** Short-based (4.16 in body length); first ray shortest, rays graduated in length to the 8th (longest); 9th–11th rays progressively shorter, creating a distinct notch in the fin outline; 12th and 13th rays elongate again
- **Anal fin:** Advanced in position, originating beneath the 3rd dorsal ray; first three rays greatly enlarged/lengthened (1st most so) and fused via membranes into a pointed, half-tube structure — consistent with a **gonopodium**, the intromittent organ characteristic of male [[Poeciliidae]]
- **Caudal fin:** Upper lobe rounded; lower 5 rays extended into a long, blunt appendage (the diagnostic "sword" for which [[Xiphophorus]] swordtails are named)
- **Ventral fins:** Pointed, extending nearly to the tip of the anal fin
- **Pectoral fins:** Sharply rounded

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The species belongs to [[Xiphophorus]], a genus of live-bearing freshwater fishes (family [[Poeciliidae]]) native to Mesoamerica, with the type locality associated with the Río Montezuma system in Mexico.
- The species name references its type locality/habitat association with the Montezuma river system.
- As a poeciliid, this species is viviparous/ovoviviparous, with male reproductive anatomy (gonopodium) adapted for internal fertilization — a key ecological and reproductive strategy distinguishing this group from egg-laying fishes.
- No specific water chemistry, depth, or co-occurring species data were preserved in the extracted text.

## Key Findings

**Coloration (live specimens):**
- Base color yellowish-olive marked with black
- 4 narrow longitudinal orange bands present on the body during life
- Dusky median dorsal band on top of head
- Dusky band along the edge of the lower jaw
- 2 short vertical bands present
- Irregularly scattered black spots
- Caudal fin coloration extends along the upper and lower edges of the caudal peduncle to the end of the lower caudal rays
- Distal portion of caudal bordered in black, with a wider lower border
- Underside of head and belly lack dark pigmentation
- Dorsal fin marked with black dots and lines
- Pectoral, ventral, and anal fins plain/unmarked

**Intraspecific Variation:**
- Considerable variation observed in fin shape and color among male specimens
- Caudal "sword" ranges from nearly undeveloped (slight lengthening of lower rays only) to very long extensions, even among apparently fully grown individuals
- Scales consistently and conspicuously dark-edged across variants
- Some individuals show black spots aggregated into a definite dark line from eye to caudal fin; others instead show large, irregular black blotches below this line, with the dark caudal patch nearly absent

**Sexual Dimorphism:**
- Females possess low or short fins lacking the specialized modifications (elongated sword, gonopodium) seen in males
- This marked dimorphism supports classification within [[Xiphophorus]] and is consistent with sexually selected traits typical of swordtail fishes

**Taxonomic Note:**
- This description formally establishes *Xiphophorus montezumae* as distinct from congeners based on the combination of fin ray counts, scale arrangement, gill raker counts (19), peritoneal pigmentation (black), and the specific caudal/anal fin modifications described above.
