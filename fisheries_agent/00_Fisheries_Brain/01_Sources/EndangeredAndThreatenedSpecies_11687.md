# Candidate and Proposed Taxa Notice of Review (1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service notice, published in the *Federal Register* on September 19, 1997 (62 FR 49397 et seq.), is an annual update under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. It lists native U.S. plant and animal taxa that are candidates for listing, or proposed for listing, as endangered or threatened. The notice also reports findings on recycled petitions and the Service's progress in revising the official lists. Its stated purpose is early notice: flagging potential listings so that [[Environmental Planning]] can reduce threats and possibly avoid the need to list taxa at all.

As of September 3, 1997, the notice counts 207 candidate taxa (107 plants and 100 animals) and 99 proposed taxa (51 plants and 20 animals proposed endangered; 20 plants and 8 animals proposed threatened). Fish make up a small share of the tables, but they include several well-known species such as [[Bull Trout]], [[Atlantic Salmon]] and [[Sacramento Splittail]].

## Background & Description

The notice is a combined animal and plant review, organized by Table 1 (candidate and proposed taxa) and Table 2 (taxa removed from candidate or proposed status since the 1996 notice). Its key features are:

- **Candidate taxa (C):** The Service has enough information on biological vulnerability and threats to propose listing, but a proposed rule is precluded by other listing work. Candidates are not proposed for listing by this notice.
- **Proposed taxa (PE/PT):** Taxa with a published proposed rule to list as endangered (PE) or threatened (PT).
- **Listing priority:** Each candidate receives a priority number (1 to 12) based on immediacy and magnitude of threats and taxonomic status, following the 1983 guidelines (48 FR 43098) and Fiscal Year 1997 guidance (61 FR 64475).
- **Recycled petitions (C(w)):** Petitions found "warranted but precluded" must be re-reviewed within a year. Eighteen recycled petitions came due between February 28, 1996, and September 3, 1997; all received continued "warranted but precluded" findings and remain candidates.

The notice also describes the Service's listing-priority guidance of May, September and December 1996. This guidance followed a listing moratorium (Public Law 104-6, April 10, 1995) that was lifted on April 26, 1996, and a funding restoration on the same day, after a period of continuing resolutions.

Most taxa were reviewed by regional offices (Regions 1 to 7). Common names are often missing or nonstandard, so the tables are arranged by scientific name.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice covers a wide range of aquatic and semi-aquatic taxa, many of which depend on specific freshwater and spring habitats:

- **Fishes:** Desert and Southwestern species such as the [[Pecos Pupfish]], [[Devils River Minnow]] and [[Gila Chub]]; Great Plains and Midwest species such as the [[Sicklefin Chub]] and Topeka shiner; and Pacific and Atlantic salmonids including [[Bull Trout]] and [[Atlantic Salmon]].
- **Freshwater mussels and snails:** Many Southeastern unionid mussels (for example, *Amblema neislerii* and *Medionidus penicillatus*), numerous springsnails (*Pyrgulopsis* and *Tryonia* spp.) in New Mexico and Texas, and Pacific island tree snails (*Partula* and *Samoana*).
- **Aquatic insects and crustaceans:** Hawaiian damselflies (*Megalagrion* spp.), Comal Springs beetles and cave amphipods such as *Gammarus acherondytes* (Illinois cave amphipod).
- **Other taxa:** Mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, flowering plants, ferns and conifers are also included, including the [[Bog Turtle]] (southern population), which was proposed as threatened for similarity of appearance and is not counted in the totals.

Many taxa are island or spring endemics with very small historic ranges, such as Hawaiian and Mariana Islands species and the Pecos and Comal Springs fauna. Threats are summarized in the tables only briefly, so the notice is best read as a status and priority register rather than a threat assessment.

## Key Findings

**Overall status (as of September 3, 1997):**
- Proposed endangered: 51 plants and 20 animals.
- Proposed threatened: 20 plants and 8 animals.
- Candidates awaiting proposed rules: 107 plants and 100 animals (207 total).
- Table 2: 181 taxa that were candidates or proposed in 1996 are no longer in those categories.

**Progress in revising the lists (February 28, 1996 to September 3, 1997):**
- Final listing actions for 152 taxa.
- Proposed listing actions for 23 taxa.
- One proposed delisting.
- Withdrawals of proposed rules for 13 taxa.
- The small number of new proposals reflects a backlog of outstanding proposed listings, which took priority under the 1996 guidance.

**Fish-relevant findings:**
- **Proposed threatened:** [[Sacramento Splittail]] (California), Atlantic salmon distinct population in Maine rivers, and the Columbia River and Klamath River bull trout populations (the Klamath population is proposed endangered).
- **Candidate fishes (Table 1):** Santa Ana sucker, Pecos pupfish, Devils River minnow, Arkansas darter, Gila chub, least chub, sicklefin chub, Topeka shiner, McCloud River redband trout, Arctic grayling (Upper Missouri River fluvial population) and Alabama sturgeon.
- **Removed fishes (Table 2):** The High Rock Spring tui chub is marked extinct (code X). The coastal/Puget Sound bull trout was removed because it proved more abundant than believed (code A), as were the Jarbidge and Saskatchewan River bull trout populations.
- **Copperbelly water snake and salamanders** are listed among the Table 2 amphibians and reptiles, not fishes, and are noted here only for context.

**Recycled petitions:** Eighteen petitions were re-reviewed, all with continued "warranted but precluded" findings. The Service committed to publishing these findings and progress reports annually.

**Requests for information:** The Service asked for data on (1) taxa to be removed; (2) taxa to be added; (3) critical habitat recommendations; (4) documented threats; (5) the immediacy and magnitude of threats; (6) taxonomic changes; (7) common names; and (8) errors in historic ranges. Comments on taxa go to the lead regional director, and general comments go to the Chief, Division of Endangered Species, in Washington, DC.

**Taxonomic note:** *Sclerocactus brevispinus* (Pariette cactus) was mistakenly included as a candidate in 1996. Because it was part of *S. glaucus*, which is already listed as threatened, a separate proposed rule is planned.

**Authorship:** The notice was compiled from staff biologist evaluations and edited by Dr. Joan Canfield, Martin Miller, Dr. Joy Nicholopoulos and Dr. George Drewry. It was signed by Director Jamie Rappaport Clark on September 3, 1997.
