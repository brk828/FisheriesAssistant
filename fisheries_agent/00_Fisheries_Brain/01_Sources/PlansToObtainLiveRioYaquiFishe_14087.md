# Proposal for Collection and Translocation of Rio Yaqui Basin Fishes

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This December 1986 letter from [[Dean Hendrickson]] (Arizona Game & Fish Department) to [[Gerald Burton]] (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) proposes a collaborative field expedition to collect live specimens of endangered Rio Yaqui basin fishes from Mexico for translocation to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]. The proposal outlines a multi-day collecting trip in late May 1987 to obtain up to 300 specimens each of [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*), [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Notropis formosus*), [[Yaqui Sucker]] (*Catostomus bernardini*), and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) from specific localities in Sonora and Chihuahua, Mexico. The effort requires coordination with Mexican biologist [[Lourdes Juarez]] from Centro Ecologico, importation permits, and funding for Mexican collaborators.

## Background & Description

The proposal targets four primary species from the [[Rio Yaqui Basin]]:

- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus pricei*, [[Ictaluridae]]): Target of 300 specimens from [[Rio Sirupa]] (site 69 of Hendrickson et al. 1981), to be collected using trammel nets, gill nets, electrofishing, and seining over 3-5 days
- **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Notropis formosus*, [[Cyprinidae]]): Target of 300 specimens from [[Arroyo Moctezuma]] (sites 76-77), [[Rio de Bavispe]] drainage
- **[[Yaqui Sucker]]** (*Catostomus bernardini*, [[Catostomidae]]): Target of 300 specimens from [[Cajon Bonito]], Sonora (sites 21-22)
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*, [[Cyprinidae]]): Target of 300 specimens from [[Cajon Bonito]], with backup sites in Chihuahua if unavailable

Additional species mentioned as potential additions:
- **[[Mexican Stoneroller]]** (*Campostoma ornatum*): Could be easily added at [[Cajon Bonito]]
- **[[Mimbres Shiner]]**: Could be collected from Piedras Verdes at Colonia Juarez

The proposal references the 1978 survey by Hendrickson et al. (1981, J. AZ Nev. Acad. Sci. 15(3):100) as baseline data for site selection and species availability.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic Rationale**: Collection sites were selected to obtain fish from populations geographically and phylogenetically close to historic habitats on [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]. [[Cajon Bonito]] provides the closest source populations to San Bernardino. [[Arroyo Moctezuma]], while farther, is within the [[Rio de Bavispe]] drainage, the same drainage as San Bernardino.

**Population Selection**: [[Rio Sirupa]] was chosen for [[Yaqui Catfish]] because it had a large population free from [[Channel Catfish]], avoiding the danger of collecting hybrid individuals present at other 1978 survey sites. [[Arroyo Moctezuma]] had the largest *Notropis* population in the 1978 survey.

**Timing**: The late May timeframe was designed to coincide with low flows prior to summer monsoon rains, optimizing collection conditions.

**Habitat Concerns**: The 1978 survey data indicated questionable availability of [[Roundtail Chub]] at [[Cajon Bonito]], necessitating backup collection sites in Chihuahua where large numbers were encountered.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Itinerary** (8-12 days total):

1. **Days 1-2**: Travel from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to Douglas, Arizona; proceed to [[Cajon Bonito]], Sonora to collect up to 300 [[Yaqui Sucker]] and 300 [[Roundtail Chub]] by seining and/or electrofishing

2. **Day 3**: Deliver all fish to ponds at [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]]

3. **Days 4-6**: Travel via Columbus, New Mexico to Madera, Chihuahua, then to [[Arroyo Moctezuma]] to collect approximately 300 [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Notropis formosus*) by seining or electrofishing (2-3 days)

4. **Days 7-11**: Travel to [[Rio Sirupa]] to capture up to 300 [[Yaqui Catfish]] using trammel nets, gill nets, electrofishing, and seining (3-5 days estimated)

5. **Day 12**: Return to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (with fish), Phoenix, and Hermosillo

**Logistical Requirements**:
- Importation permits from U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service
- Mexican collecting permits through SEDUE (Sonora office) coordinated by [[Lourdes Juarez]]
- Funding for Mexican collaborators: mileage for one vehicle and per diem for two persons (Lourdes and assistant Alejandro)
- Tank truck from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] with participation of [[Jim Brooks]]
- Vehicle from Centro Ecologico
- Arizona Game and Fish vehicle (funded through Section 6, Seven Fish Project)

**Critical Timeline**: Dates, itinerary, species list, and specimen numbers must be finalized and submitted to [[Lourdes Juarez]] by end of January 1987 due to permit processing requirements.

**Flexibility**: Proposal explicitly noted as "starting point only" with openness to modifications based on input from Dexter and San Bernardino regarding capacity to accommodate fish at proposed time.
