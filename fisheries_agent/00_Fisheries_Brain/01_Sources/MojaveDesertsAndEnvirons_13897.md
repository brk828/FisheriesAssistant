# Mojave Desert and Environs: Late Precambrian to Cenozoic Geologic Synthesis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #non-fish-document

## Executive Summary

This document is a geological synthesis of the [[Mojave Desert]] region of southeastern California, not an ichthyological or aquatic ecology study. It contains no fish species, fish habitat data, or aquatic survey results, so the species and habitat fields are recorded as N/A. The authors, Burchfiel and Davis, reconstruct the region's paleogeography, magmatic history, and structural evolution from Precambrian basement through Cenozoic faulting. Their central claims are:

- Western Mojave Paleozoic rocks are "out of place" and were probably displaced by left-slip faulting during a [[Permo-Triassic Truncation]] event.
- Mesozoic magmatism formed a northwest-trending [[Andean-Type Magmatic Arc]].
- [[Franciscan Rocks]] were underthrust beneath North America at a shallow angle in latest Cretaceous to earliest Tertiary time, producing the [[Pelona-Orocopia Schist]] and related schists.
- The western Mojave and adjacent terranes were bent clockwise by [[Oroclinal Bending]].

## Background & Description

The Mojave Desert is a wedge-shaped physiographic province that narrows westward. It is bounded by the Tehachapi Mountains and southern [[Sierra Nevada]] to the northwest, the Basin and Range province to the north, and the San Gabriel and San Bernardino Mountains to the southwest and south. The eastern and southeastern limits are drawn along the Colorado River and the northeastern edge of the Salton trough.

Approximately 50% of the desert is covered by recent alluvium. Of the remaining 50%, two-thirds is underlain by Mesozoic plutonic rocks and Cenozoic igneous and sedimentary rocks. Pre-Cenozoic stratigraphic exposure is sparse, which makes reconstructing the older geology difficult. The paper relies on scattered outcrops at localities such as [[Victorville]], the [[Clark Mountains]], the Marble Mountains, the Chocolate Mountains, the Orocopia Mountains, the Panamint Range, and the El Paso Mountains.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address living organisms, fish, invertebrates, plankton, water chemistry, or aquatic habitats. Its only environmental content is geological and paleoenvironmental:

- **Marine and nonmarine settings:** The Lower Triassic [[Moenkopi Formation]] has marine sandy limestone at its base and nonmarine red beds above. Cambrian and Devonian carbonates reflect shallow marine shelf conditions.
- **Continental margin environments:** Early Paleozoic cherts, slates, and limestones of the [[Continental Slope and Rise Sequence]] were deposited in deeper-water slope and rise settings.
- **Arc and forearc settings:** The Mesozoic back-arc area was largely nonmarine, while forearc basin deposits and accreted oceanic rocks record marine conditions to the west.
- **Desert setting:** The modern environment is described only as a desert, with alluvium covering about half the surface.

Any ecological interpretation of these settings lies outside the scope of this document.

## Key Findings

**Precambrian basement**
- Precambrian crystalline rocks were deformed and metamorphosed about 1.7 b.y. ago, then intruded by granitic rocks at about 1.4 b.y.
- Syenites and carbonatites in the Clark Mountains were intruded 1.35 to 1.4 b.y. ago.
- Syenites and anorthosites in the San Gabriel Mountains and formerly adjacent terranes were intruded at about 1.2 b.y.

**Pahrump Group and Late Precambrian strata**
- The [[Pahrump Group]] rests unconformably on Precambrian basement and is locally more than 2 km thick. It was deposited in a fault-controlled trough, the [[Death Valley Aulacogen]].
- The sequence comprises the Crystal Spring Formation, the Beck Spring Dolomite, and the Kingston Peak Formation.

**Paleozoic paleogeography**
- The eastern three-quarters of the Mojave was cratonal and miogeoclinal in the late Precambrian, but cratonal during the Paleozoic.
- The [[Cratonal Sequence]] is 2000 to 2500 m thick.
- The [[Miogeocline]] in the western Spring Mountains includes more than 3000 m of late Precambrian to early Middle Cambrian terrigenous rocks and 4000 to 5000 m of Middle Cambrian to Permian shallow marine carbonates.
- Rocks in the western quarter of the Mojave, including the [[Garlock Series]] (more than 10,000 m) and the [[Coyote Group]] (more than 7000 m), are regarded as eugeosynclinal and out of place.

**Permo-Triassic truncation and displacement**
- The authors propose that the out-of-place Paleozoic slivers were moved southward along a major left-slip fault during [[Permo-Triassic Truncation]] (Hamilton, 1969; Burchfiel and Davis, 1972, 1975).
- They reject the view that these rocks are a simple southward continuation of the [[Antler Orogeny]] and [[Sonoma Orogeny]] belts of west-central Nevada.

**Mesozoic magmatism**
- The Mesozoic magmatic arc is Andean-type and superposed on all earlier paleogeographic and structural trends.
- The eastern limit of arc magmatism shifted irregularly. The arc was at least partly quiescent in the Early Jurassic, when the nonmarine Aztec Sandstone was deposited across the eastern arc.
- Dated plutonic episodes include about 230 ± 10 m.y. (Granite Mountains), 220 ± 10 m.y. (Mount Lowe granodiorite), 190 to 160 m.y., 160 to 135 m.y., and 135 to 80 m.y.
- Latest Cretaceous to earliest Tertiary plutons (70 to 60 m.y.) are rare north of the [[Garlock Fault]].

**Mesozoic deformation**
- The earliest arc-related deformation is latest Permian to earliest Triassic at Victorville, based on a monzonite pluton dated at 230 ± 10 m.y. and preliminary hornblende data.
- Middle Triassic to Early Jurassic east-directed thrusting occurred in the Panamint, Inyo, Cowhole, and Clark Mountains.
- Middle to early Late Jurassic thrusting is recorded by the [[Swansea-Coso Thrust System]], cut by a pluton dated at 156 m.y.
- Latest Jurassic to Early Cretaceous deformation is recorded in the Ivanpah Mountains (a syntectonic pluton at 135 ± 5 m.y.) and at Goodsprings.
- Keystone-Muddy Mountains thrusting ended before about 84 to 94 m.y., based on post-Keystone plutons in the Ivanpah Mountains.
- Mesozoic high-angle faults include Triassic dip-slip faults in the Clark Mountains and left-slip faults in the Argus Range.

**Underthrusting and the Pelona-Orocopia Schist problem**
- At least twice in latest Cretaceous to earliest Tertiary time, Franciscan-equivalent rocks were underthrust eastward at a shallow angle beneath North America. This is the authors' preferred interpretation.
- The schist of the northern schists (Rand and Sierra de Salinas) was underthrust before about 80 m.y. The Pelona-Orocopia schist was underthrust between about 50 and 60 m.y.
- Underthrusting extended at least 200 to 250 km, and the shallow underthrusting stripped away lower arc terrane and continental crust.
- The authors reject the alternative of an intracontinental ensimatic back-arc basin because the basin, its rift deposits, and its suture are not found in the Mojave geology.
- They acknowledge that field evidence for northeast-directed thrust motion (Ehlig, 1958; Haxel and Dillon, 1978) is a difficulty for their model.

**Cenozoic oroclinal bending**
- Trends in the western Mojave, southern Sierra Nevada, and northern Salinian block were bent clockwise, which the authors describe as a right-lateral bend.
- The bend deflects the Permo-Triassic truncation boundary, the western limit of the Sierran batholith, and the Great Valley and Franciscan terranes. It is constrained to the east by the Late Jurassic Independence dike swarm.
- The timing of the bend is not clear. The abstract gives latest Cretaceous to mid-Tertiary, while the conclusions give mid-Cretaceous to mid-Tertiary. The document does not resolve this difference.

**Overall conclusion**
- The authors summarize the western Mojave-Salinian block as having been left-laterally displaced in Permo-Triassic time, tectonically accreted, underthrust by Franciscan-affinity rocks, oroclinally folded, and disrupted by late Cenozoic right-lateral faulting. The initial paleogeographic location of the Salinian basement is still unknown.
