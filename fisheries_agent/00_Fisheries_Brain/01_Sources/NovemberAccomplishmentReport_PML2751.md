# Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center: Monthly Activity Report, November 2006

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This monthly staff report from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center (New Mexico) covers November 2006. The center's main work in this period was rearing and stocking endangered southwestern fishes. Staff stocked 7,149 marked [[Woundfin]] into the upper and lower [[Virgin River]] under the Virgin River Resource Management and Recovery Program. Other activities included distributing [[Bonytail]] and sending specimens to research and museum collections, tagging and harvesting ponds with partner agencies, genetic work on several listed species, and facility maintenance. The report also covers staffing changes, a fish health investigation at Isleta Lake, and a fish health meeting on the Devils Hole pupfish.

## Background & Description

The report is an internal, staff-level update that lists activities by category: partnerships and accountability, aquatic species conservation management, leadership in science and technology, cooperation with Native Americans, workforce management, additional center activities, and facility maintenance. It does not present a research design, so no new experimental results or statistical analyses are reported.

The center holds a range of threatened and endangered fishes from the American Southwest. Species named in the report include:

- **Fishes of the Colorado River basin:** [[Woundfin]], [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]
- **Desert and spring-dwelling fishes:** [[Devils Hole Pupfish]], [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Big Bend Gambusia]], [[Leon Springs Pupfish]], [[Guzman Beautiful Shiner]], and [[Devils River Minnow]]
- **Other listed or research species:** [[Gila Trout]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Virgin River Chub]], and [[Channel Catfish]] (a cause of a die-off in Isleta Lake)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Virgin River recovery.** The center's stocking work supports the Virgin River recovery program. Native minnows are returned to historical habitat in Arizona, Utah, and Nevada. A local newspaper article described the marking, hauling, and stocking process used to release fish safely into historical habitat.

**Water quality and pond conditions.** The report links one mortality event to environmental stress. At [[Isleta Lake]] in New Mexico, adult channel catfish died after several cold fronts caused extreme temperature fluctuations. The lake's shallow depth is believed to have made this worse. Opportunistic external pathogens and bacteria were found in the fish sent for diagnosis.

**Captive-population management.** Captive stocks of several species are managed to maintain genetic diversity and health. The report describes exchanges of [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] between [[Uvalde National Fish Hatchery]] and Dexter, consistent with a genetic reserve population and stock management process. Dexter also overwintered several species from ponds in the fish culture building, including Gila topminnow, Big Bend gambusia, Guzman beautiful shiner, Leon Springs pupfish, Comanche Springs pupfish, and desert pupfish.

**Disease and culture of Devils Hole pupfish.** Fish health services for the [[Devils Hole Pupfish]] recovery program remain a priority. A partner meeting in Las Vegas reviewed fish health and culture issues at the Devils Hole refuge fish culture program. The detection of [[Lymphosarcoma]] in the fish was a key concern. Histology at the [[Fish Health Center]] was used for detection, and further testing is planned to determine whether a virus or contaminants cause it.

## Key Findings

**1. Virgin River stocking and marked fish release**
- On November 29, Dexter staff and Virgin River program personnel stocked 7,149 marked woundfin into the upper and lower Virgin River drainage.
- An additional 510 unmarked woundfin were transferred to Wahweap State Fish Hatchery near Big Water, Utah, for broodstock development.

**2. Research and specimen support**
- Two hundred age-1 bonytail were provided to Dr. David Walker at the University of Arizona for phase II of a project titled "Quantifying Potential Endocrine Disruption in Effluent-Dominated and Effluent Dependent Waters Using Fish as Indicators."
- Dexter provided preserved specimens of 12 different listed species to the National Museum collection (Alan Temple, NCTC) and the Museum of Southwestern Biology at the University of New Mexico. All transferred fish were mortalities from the annual fish health assessment and were transferred under Region 2 Director's permit TE676811-8.

**3. Monthly stocking table**
- The November 2006 stocking table lists fish by species, number, size, agency, and destination. Destinations include the University of Arizona, the Bureau of Land Management (Rio Grande, San Marcial), Uvalde NFH, the Bureau of Reclamation (Colorado River, Lake Mohave, and Lake Havasu), Page Springs and Bubbling Ponds SFHs, San Juan River sites, Wahweap SFH, and the upper and lower Virgin River.
- The table includes several thousand fish. The largest entries are 105,480 and 69,7xx fish at 2.0 inches (ST), though the OCR of these figures is incomplete.
- The Regional Distribution Unit hauled bonytail to Lake Havasu and Lake Mohave, Arizona.

**4. Partner tagging and pond harvest**
- Staff from the Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City, Nevada) and the Arizona Game and Fish Department assisted Dexter with wire and [[Pit Tagging]] of bonytail and razorback sucker.
- Arizona Game and Fish PIT-tagged razorback sucker and moved 20 young-of-the-year Colorado pikeminnow to Bubbling Ponds SFH. Staff also helped harvest and sort razorback sucker and bonytail from pond R.10.

**5. Comanche Springs pupfish exchange**
- Five hundred Comanche Springs pupfish were exchanged between Uvalde NFH and Dexter as part of guidelines for maintaining captive stocks.

**6. Devils Hole pupfish fish health and culture**
- A partner meeting in Las Vegas reassessed the Devils Hole refuge fish culture program.
- Main recommendations were to build and use a water reuse system and to use prepared flake diet as the main food source for the pupfish.
- Further testing is planned to determine whether the lymphosarcoma detected in the fish is caused by a virus or by contaminants.

**7. Molecular and genetic work**
- On November 30, staff from the [[Molecular Ecology Lab]] and the [[Fish Health Unit]] received online training on the Applied Biosystems 7300 Real-Time PCR System. The training covered allelic discrimination for analyzing [[Single Nucleotide Polymorphism]]s (SNPs). Staff plan to use this technology for an upcoming endangered [[Gila Trout]] project.
- Dr. Carole Conway submitted a proposal to study the genetics of the Virgin River chub. The objectives are to: (1) investigate population genetic structure in the Virgin River; (2) compare wild and captive stocks for possible genetic divergence; (3) assess genetic variation in wild and captive stocks; and (4) estimate the contribution of captive-raised fish to recruitment in the wild. The results are intended to form a genetic baseline for the recovery program.
- Dr. Conway also screened additional primer pairs for the Devils River minnow project. Two new primer pairs were identified to supplement the earlier survey of genetic variation and to clarify possible inbreeding within wild stocks.
- Krista Heideman finished data collection for the [[Humpback Chub]] project and began analysis. She is also genotyping 45 Devils Hole pupfish fin clips, DNA from hybrid pupfish from Devils Hole and Ash Meadows, and 81 fin clip samples from Devils Hole and Ash Meadows pupfish. These analyses will produce a more comprehensive genetic baseline for assigning genetic identity to unknown pupfish samples.
- The Molecular Ecology Lab developed and began using a standard system for storing tissue and DNA samples and keeping records. Staff expansion made standard operating procedures essential for continuity.

**8. Cooperation with Native Americans and fish health**
- At Isleta Pueblo, New Mexico, an adult channel catfish die-off occurred in Isleta Lake. Opportunistic external pathogens and bacteria were detected, and extreme temperature fluctuations from multiple cold fronts, combined with the lake's shallow depth, were judged the most likely cause.

**9. Training and outreach**
- Dr. Connie Keeler-Foster began developing a workshop to train hatchery personnel in captive propagation techniques and disease issues for non-fish aquatic species. The workshop is planned for spring 2007 at Dexter, with the Fish Health Unit involved in its development.
- Barbara Giesecke, working with Region 2 Division of Visitor Services, developed the fact sheet "Imperiled Fish of Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center" for visitors.

**10. Workforce and facilities**
- Linda Vannest of the Bozeman Fish Health Center (Montana) completed a two-week cross-training program at Dexter.
- Career ladder promotions were approved for Krista Storey, Dr. Carole Conway, William Knight, and Karin Eldridge.
- Renee Martin joined the staff through the Student Conservation Association program, working with the Molecular Ecology Lab and Fish Health Unit.
- Facility maintenance included sewer repairs to the Fish Health Unit, wiring and fume hood repairs in the fish health laboratory, cleaning of mobile homes and trailers for students and volunteers, replacement of actuators on the cooling and heating system in the administration/lab building, and repairs to the ice machine in the fish culture building.
