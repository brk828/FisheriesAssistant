# Ichthyological Survey of the Rio Salado, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #cyprinidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1952 Master of Arts thesis by Luciano Val Guerra, submitted to The University of Texas, is the first published ichthyological and ecological survey of the [[Rio Salado]] system in Tamaulipas, Nuevo Leon and Coahuila, Mexico. The study recorded **30 fish species** in **11 families** and **20 genera** from **22 collections**. It was undertaken before the planned [[Falcon Dam]] on the [[Rio Grande]] impounds the river, so that the pre-impoundment fauna could be documented and compared with the post-impoundment community. The author concludes that the fauna is ecologically very similar to that of central Texas, with differences likely due to geographic isolation.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** No prior ichthyological work existed on the Rio Salado. The author chose the system because the Falcon Dam impoundment would introduce exotic fishes and alter the tributaries through backwater effects. The survey aimed to describe the native fauna, species distributions and ecological niches before these changes occurred.
- **Acknowledgements:** Support came from Texas Game and Fish Commission biologists (Marion Toole, Kenneth C. Jurgens, William H. Brown) and from Clark Hubbs of The University of Texas, who verified identifications. Permission to collect was granted by Mexican military authorities.
- **Study organisms:** Fishes collected by seining. Nomenclature follows [[Hubbs and Bonham]] (1940), with later revisions.
- **Thesis details:** Submitted to the committee on December 20, 1951; dated January 31, 1952.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and course.** The river is formed at [[Don Martin Lake]] by two northern forks: the [[Rio Sabinas de Coahuila]] (from a spring at Nacimiento, Coahuila) and the [[Rio Salado de los Nadadores]] (from a spring near Sacramento, Coahuila, at about 1750 feet). Two tributaries join lower down: the intermittent [[Rio de Candela]] and the [[Rio Sabinas de Nuevo Leon]], which rises at Ojo de Aqua. The river flows south-southeast, then east, to the Rio Grande near Guerrero, Tamaulipas. The source spring is sweet, but the water becomes brackish after passing a salt basin near Abasolo and Hermanas, which gives the river its name.

**Distances and area.** The straight-line distance from Don Martin Dam to the mouth is 105 miles. The author estimates the meandering course exceeds 300 miles. Reported total flow lengths differ between sections: "more than 520 miles" from spring source to mouth in the Ecology section, and "about 670 miles" in the Summary. The watershed covers **17,383 square miles** across Tamaulipas, Nuevo Leon and part of Coahuila.

**Geology.** Headwaters arise in Lower Cretaceous massive limestone of the Sierra del Carmen. Basaltic igneous outcrops occur near Nueva Rosita-Musquiz. Upper Cretaceous soft limestone and shale underlie the central reaches, and Eocene sands, clays and sandstones (Midway, Wilcox, Claiborne formations) occur near the mouth.

**Four ecologic segments.** The author divides the system into four segments, each containing many localized niches:
1. **Rio Sabinas de Coahuila:** fast, clear water over bedrock. Vegetation is present upstream. Coal mine waste at station 17 eliminated plants and reduced the fish collection.
2. **Rio Salado de los Nadadores:** mud and silt bottoms, turbid and murky water, and nearly absent aquatic vegetation, probably because of salinity.
3. **Rio Salado proper:** cypress-lined banks, slow to moderate current, and elevations from 900 feet at Don Martin to 450 feet near La Gloria. Aquatic vegetation is largely absent. Cut-off pools were warm and poorly aerated, and soft-shelled turtles (*Amyda* sp.) were abundant there.
4. **Rio Sabinas de Nuevo Leon:** intermittent. Only one positive station was found out of eight dry stations, and the headwater site at 1025 feet was fresh, swift and vegetated.

**Biogeography.** The system lies mostly in the Tamaulipan and partly in the Chihuahuan biotic provinces of Blair (1950).

## Key Findings

**Overall fauna**
- 30 species across 11 families and 20 genera. The most widespread species were common across most of the basin.
- The fauna is ecologically very similar to central Texas. Any differences are attributed to geographic isolation.

**Species accounts (selected, with reported counts)**
- **[[Spotted Gar]]** (*Lepisosteus productus*): 14 specimens from 5 stations. Restricted to deep, non-vegetated, mud-and-silt bottoms.
- **Southern Long-nose Gar** (*L. osseus leptorhynchus*): 1 specimen, taken at night. Large gars were collected only at night, suggesting feeding is concentrated in early night.
- **[[Gizzard Shad]]** (*Dorosoma cepedianum*): 5 collections. Central reaches in quiet water. Stocked in Don Martin Lake, where it became a common forage species. Appears to replace the [[Rio Grande Tetra]] in that vicinity, suggesting possible competition (unconfirmed).
- **[[Rio Grande Tetra]]** (*Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus*): one of the most common species, absent from only five stations. Indifferent to vegetation. Schools as a pelagic form.
- **Slender Carpsucker** (*Carpiodes carpio elongatus*): 16 specimens from 5 stations. Restricted to the Rio Salado proper, in mud-and-silt areas rich in invertebrates.
- **Mexican Gray Redhorse** (*Moxostoma congestum albidum*): one specimen, from the high-salinity Rio Salado de los Nadadores.
- **Mexican Speckled Dace** (*Extrarius aestivalis aestivalis*): 12 localities, from San Felipe to La Gloria. Absent from the Rio Salado de los Nadadores and from the Rio Sabinas de Nuevo Leon.
- **[[Proserpine Shiner]]** (*Notropis proserpinis*): the most common species in the Rio Salado and upper-fork tributaries. Recorded in nearly all 22 collections, absent from the Rio Sabinas de Nuevo Leon and one Rio Salado de los Nadadores locality.
- **[[Tamaulipas Shiner]]** (*N. braytoni*): 16 of 22 stations. Absent from the saline Rio Salado de los Nadadores.
- **Texas Blunt-nose Shiner** (*N. simus orca*): 14 localities. Vegetation was of no ecological significance.
- **Plains Red Shiner** (*N. lutrensis lutrensis*): 12 localities. Shares habitat preferences with *N. simus orca*.
- **[[Texas Shiner]]** (*N. amabilis*): 4 of 22 collections, widely scattered.
- **Chihuahua Shiner** (*N. chihuahua*): half of the collections (11), 68 specimens. Absent from headwaters and saline waters.
- **Robust Shiner** (*N. robustus*): 10 localities. Avoids headwaters.
- **Sand Shiner** (*N. deliciosus deliciosus*): one specimen, from Presita, Coahuila.
- **Undescribed *Notropis* sp.:** 6 specimens from 2 headwater stations of the Rio Sabinas de Coahuila. Water temperature was 86 °F.
- **[[Rio Grande Round-nose Minnow]]** (*Dionda episcopa couchi*): 223 specimens from 4 locations, centered on the headwaters. Water temperature was relatively constant at about 89 °F. Schools, and shelters in bank-line vegetation when disturbed.
- **Parrot Minnow** (*Pimephales vigilax vigilax*): 9 localities, restricted to the lower river. Most abundant near Ejido de Nuevo Camaron.
- **Channel Catfish** (*Ictalurus punctatus punctatus x lupus*): intergrades, from 7 localities. Not abundant in vegetated habitat.
- **Rio Grande Blue Catfish** (*I. furcatus affinis*): one specimen, from Ejido de Nuevo Camaron.
- **Flat-head Catfish** (*Pilodictis olivaris*): one specimen, 10 miles west of Anahuac.
- ***Gambusia affinis*:** 8 localities, restricted to the headwaters of the Rio Sabinas de Coahuila. Absent from the Rio Sabinas de Nuevo Leon and the saline waters.
- ***Gambusia* sp.:** 12 locations. An apparently undescribed species in the more saline headwaters of the Rio Salado de los Nadadores. Alvarez (1950) had identified it as *G. nobilis* or *G. senilis*. Miller (1951) corrected this, because *G. nobilis* is restricted to the Pecos drainage.
- **[[Rio Grande Darter]]** (*Poecilichthys lepidus grahami*): the only darter, with 27 specimens from one locality at Musquiz, Coahuila. Restricted to fast, clear, vegetated water.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*): found in very clear to very muddy water, at depths of 2 to 6 feet (average 4 feet). Present in 4 of 11 sites with vegetation. The author suggests the local race may be superior to hatchery stock, and that the population is among the least contaminated by hatchery stocking.
- **[[Rio Grande Longear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis megalotis occidentalis*): 18 specimens from 8 locations, confined to the headwater forks. Occurs in quiet vegetated pools.
- **[[Bluegill]]** (*L. macrochirus speciosus*): restricted to the lower Rio Salado. Never occurred in the same collections as the longear sunfish, except at one site.
- **[[Fresh Water Drum]]** (*Aplodinotus grunniens*): 5 locations, in the Rio Salado and one lower Rio Sabinas de Coahuila site.
- **[[Rio Grande Perch]]** (*Herichthys cyanoguttatus*): the only cichlid, found in 17 of 22 collections. Prefers shaded, quieter water. Stream gradient had no effect on its distribution.

**Management implications**
- The author argues that documenting the pre-impoundment niches is necessary for fisheries planning at [[Falcon Dam]]. Impoundment is expected to introduce alien species and cause backwater effects in the lower Rio Salado and its tributaries.
- Sparse vegetation across the basin is linked to bedrock bottoms and high salinity. Pollution from coal mining at station 17 was also noted.
