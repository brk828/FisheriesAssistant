# Sampling at ASU Research Park, May 1991

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Centrarchidae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary
This report documents a fisheries status survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel at three artificial lakes (Lake A, Lake B, Lake C) within the ASU Research Park, Tempe, Arizona, on 22–23 May 1991. Sampling combined overnight entanglement gear (trammel and experimental gill nets) with daytime shoreline seining. The survey aimed to monitor persistence of endangered native Colorado River Basin fishes — [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] — alongside a diverse assemblage of non-native sport and forage fishes. A total of 210 fish (10 taxa) were captured via entanglement gear, dominated by [[Threadfin Shad]] (77%), [[Largemouth Bass]] (12%), and [[Blue Tilapia]] (5%). Seining yielded 967 individuals (6 taxa), dominated by juvenile Largemouth Bass (49%) and [[Bluegill]] (39%). Notably, [[Red Shiner]] and mosquitofish, previously common, were entirely absent from seine catches, continuing a decline first observed in autumn 1990. Colorado Pikeminnow was not captured by research gear (last netted March 1988) despite continued angler reports from Lake B. Follow-up larval fish surveys in June 1991, prompted by reports of a possible bonytail "spawning aggregation," yielded only centrarchid larvae.

## Background & Description
- **Bonytail** (n = 3, Lake B only): Total lengths of 35.9, 36.0, and 37.3 cm; approximate mass of 400 g each. Sex indeterminate for all; the two larger individuals were tuberculate with rosy-orange coloration at fin bases and jaw, suggestive of male breeding condition. All fish in good overall condition; released alive.
- **Razorback Sucker** (n = 2, Lake A only): Total lengths of 41.5 and 43.0 cm; masses of 900 g and 1000 g, respectively. Sex undetermined; released in good condition.
- **Colorado Pikeminnow**: No specimens captured or measured during this survey; species status inferred solely from angler hook-and-line reports in Lake B.
- Juvenile Largemouth Bass dominating seine catches were predominantly young-of-year, measuring less than 2.4 cm TL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Three interconnected artificial lakes (A, B, C) at ASU Research Park serve as refugial habitat for native Colorado River Basin fishes alongside a broader assemblage of introduced sport and forage species — an unusual urban conservation context for endangered [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]].
- **Species segregation by lake**:
  - Lake A: Dominant Threadfin Shad and Largemouth Bass; sole capture site for Razorback Sucker.
  - Lake B: Dominant Largemouth Bass and Blue Tilapia; sole capture site for Bonytail via entanglement gear; anecdotal reports of Colorado Pikeminnow and a possible Bonytail schooling/spawning aggregation along a gravel shoreline section.
  - Lake C: Sampling compromised by unauthorized gear disturbance (an experimental gill net was tampered with overnight), rendering catch data conservative; low overall catch (n = 4 via entanglement gear).
- **Trophic/community structure**: Forage base dominated by Threadfin Shad; sport fish community includes Largemouth Bass, sunfishes (Bluegill, Green Sunfish, Redear Sunfish), Black Crappie, and Channel/Yellow Bullhead catfishes. [[Blue Tilapia]], a non-native cichlid, is well established across all lakes.
- **Notable ecological shift**: [[Red Shiner]] (Cyprinella lutrensis) and mosquitofish (Gambusia sp.), previously common-to-abundant in shoreline seine collections, were completely absent in May 1991 — continuing a decline first documented in autumn 1990. This suggests a significant community-level disturbance or competitive/predatory shift, possibly linked to expanding Blue Tilapia and/or Largemouth Bass populations.
- **Reproductive ecology**: Targeted nighttime larval fish sampling in June 1991, conducted near a reported adult bonytail aggregation site, produced only larval and early-juvenile centrarchids (primarily Largemouth Bass), with no confirmed native fish spawning detected.

## Key Findings
- **Entanglement net catch (Table 1)**: 210 individuals, 10 taxa, across Lakes A–C. Composition: Threadfin Shad 77% (161), Largemouth Bass 12% (25), Blue Tilapia 5% (11), Bonytail 1% (3), Channel Catfish 1% (3), Razorback Sucker 1% (2), Bluegill 1% (2), and single individuals each of Yellow Bullhead, Green Sunfish, and Black Crappie.
- **Seine catch (Table 2)**: 967 individuals, 6 taxa. Composition: Largemouth Bass 49% (473, mostly young-of-year), Bluegill 39% (379), Threadfin Shad 11% (105), with Green Sunfish, Redear Sunfish, and Blue Tilapia each <1%.
- **Catch per effort**: Overall seine CPE = 9.9 fish/haul (Lake A: 9.5; Lake B: 14.5; Lake C: 7.4).
- **Gear compromise**: An experimental gill net in Lake C was disturbed by unauthorized persons overnight, with an unknown number of fish potentially removed — Lake C catch data flagged as conservative/minimum estimates.
- **[[Bonytail]] status**: Captured consistently in Lake B but absent from recent collections in Lake A (since April 1989) and Lake C (since October 1988). Low sampling effort (2–6 net sets per lake) may be insufficient to detect small residual populations. Unconfirmed reports of a schooling aggregation along Lake B's northwest gravel shoreline hint at possible localized spawning behavior.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]] status**: Low catch (n=2) attributed to limited sampling effort rather than confirmed scarcity; captured only in Lake A.
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]] status**: Not captured by research gear since March 1988, but persistent, reliable angler reports of hook-and-line catches in Lake B suggest the species remains present at low density.
- **Community change**: Disappearance of Red Shiner and mosquitofish from seine collections since autumn 1990 represents a significant, unexplained faunal shift warranting further investigation.
- **Management implications**: Report recommends continuation of weekly-to-biweekly winter-spring larval fish surveys (beginning February) to confirm native species spawning, with routine autumn adult surveys suspended pending detection of spawning activity. All native species handled were assessed as in good condition and released alive, consistent with ongoing conservation monitoring of these Colorado River Basin endangered fishes at this urban refuge site.
