## Executive Summary
This 1960 study by J.M. Lawrence (Auburn University) presents a predictive morphometric model estimating the maximum size of forage fish that [[Chain Pickerel]] (*Esox niger*) can swallow, based on body depth relationships. Building on prior methodology developed for [[Largemouth Bass]] predation studies, the paper establishes total length–body depth equations for both the predator ([[Chain Pickerel]]) and four common forage species: [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Golden Shiner]]. The core finding is that swallowing capacity is governed by a simple depth-matching rule: a chain pickerel can ingest prey with a body depth equal to or less than its own undistended body depth. This allows fisheries managers to estimate prey-size limits (Iₚ, S₈, Y/C ratio) for pickerel of any given length, an important tool for evaluating predator-prey population dynamics in managed fisheries.

## Background & Description
- **Esophageal structure**: Unlike [[Centrarchidae|Centrarchid]] basses, chain pickerel lack the semirigid paired cleithrum bones surrounding the anterior esophagus. Instead, they possess a series of **slim, pliable bones** in this region, allowing the esophageal opening to expand to a **greater degree** than in bass species — a key anatomical adaptation permitting ingestion of relatively deep-bodied prey.
- **Body depth measurement protocol**: Predator body depth was measured by placing the fish on its right side and measuring from the dorsal body surface to the ventral stomach margin, taking care to avoid compression artifacts that could artificially inflate the measurement.
- **Length–depth relationship of Chain Pickerel** (D = body depth, L = total length, mm):

| 100–199 | D = 1.95 + 0.110L |
| 200–299 | D = -3.19 + 0.134L |
| 300–399 | D = -18.59 + 0.182L |
| 400–499 | D = -22.08 + 0.193L |
| 500–599 | D = -29.47 + 0.203L |
| 600–699 | D = -77.53 + 0.264L |

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Predator-prey dynamics**: [[Chain Pickerel]] is characterized as a **voracious piscivore**, consuming a range of common forage fishes co-occurring in Southeastern U.S. freshwater systems.
- **Habitat overlap**: The four forage species analyzed — [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Golden Shiner]] — represent typical pond and lake forage assemblages in the region, indicating pickerel functions as a generalist mid-to-upper trophic level predator in these systems.
- **Functional constraint on diet breadth**: Prey selection is fundamentally constrained by a **morphological gape/esophagus limitation** rather than behavioral preference alone — deeper-bodied prey (e.g., bluegill, green sunfish) are limited to smaller total lengths compared to more fusiform/laterally compressed prey (e.g., golden shiners), which can be swallowed at substantially greater lengths for a given pickerel size.
- **Fisheries management relevance**: Understanding these size constraints is essential for calculating population metrics such as **predation index (Iₚ)**, **survival (S₈)**, and the **yield/consumption (Y/C) ratio**, which inform stocking, harvest, and pond management decisions in mixed predator-prey fisheries.

## Key Findings
1. **Depth-matching rule**: A chain pickerel can swallow forage fish whose body depth is ≤ its own undistended body depth: *Body depth (forage) ≤ Body depth (chain pickerel)*.
2. **Prey size increases with predator size**: As chain pickerel total length increases from 4.5 to 27.5 inches, the maximum swallowable prey length increases proportionally across all four forage species.
3. **Golden Shiners are swallowed at the largest sizes** relative to other forage fish for any given pickerel length (e.g., a 12.5-inch pickerel can swallow a golden shiner up to 6.70 inches, versus 4.43 inches for bluegill), reflecting the shiner's more slender body shape.
4. **Bluegill and Green Sunfish impose the most restrictive prey-size limits**, due to their comparatively deep-bodied morphology, consistent with known limitations on [[Centrarchidae|centrarchid]] prey vulnerability to piscivores.
5. **Gizzard Shad occupy an intermediate position**, swallowable at moderately larger sizes than bluegill/green sunfish but smaller than golden shiners, for equivalent pickerel lengths.
6. **Anatomical basis for the difference from bass predation limits**: The pliable bone structure around the pickerel esophagus (versus the rigid cleithrum bones in [[Largemouth Bass]] and other centrarchid basses) likely permits chain pickerel to swallow proportionally larger/deeper prey than similarly sized bass, per Lawrence's companion 1957 study.
7. **Table I** provides a complete lookup reference (pickerel total length 4.5–27.5 in.) for maximum swallowable forage fish length across the four species, useful for direct field/management application.
