# Systematics of Arizona Hydrobiidae (Gastropoda: Prosobranchia)

#malacology #freshwater-biology #hydrobiidae

## Executive Summary

This systematic revision by [[Robert Hershler]] and [[J. Jerry Landye]] describes the hydrobiid snail (springsnail) fauna of Arizona. The authors report 14 species: 12 [[Pyrgulopsis]] and 2 [[Tryonia]]. Thirteen are described as new, and one, *Pyrgulopsis deserta* (Pilsbry), is previously known. The work is motivated by the threatened status of spring-dependent fauna and by the near absence of earlier taxonomic study of this group. Species were separated using shell and anatomical characters, and [[Discriminant Analysis]] was used to test whether the species are distinct. Male penial anatomy gave the strongest separation.

## Background & Description

- **Motivation:** Many southwestern aquatic taxa are threatened by loss or degradation of springs. The text states that 12 southwestern species were then under consideration for federal listing. Prior to this work, the only published record of live-collected hydrobiids from the state was a single spring in the Huachuca Mountains. Subfossil shells had also been reported.
- **Material:** 45 lots collected with fine-meshed hand sieves from 38 numbered sites (Fig. 1; Appendix 1). Specimens were relaxed with menthol, fixed in formalin, and preserved in 70% ethanol. Vouchers are deposited mainly in the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM), with comparative material from the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]] (ANSP).
- **Shell measurements:** Thirteen shell characters were measured from camera lucida outlines, including whorl number, shell height and width, aperture dimensions, and body whorl dimensions.
- **Anatomical measurements:** Counts and measurements were taken from five males and five females per lot. The 22 raw characters and 14 ratios cover the penis, penial filament, glandular ridges, [[Radula]], female reproductive tract, and the prostate.
- **Morphological traits:** Pyrgulopsis shells are globose to elongate-conic. The penis has a small lobe, a narrow filament, and one to fifteen glandular ridges. The genus's closest relative is considered to be *Cincinnatia*, which is restricted to eastern North America.
- **Tryonia traits:** *Tryonia* has elongate-conic to turreted shells and a penis with several glandular lobes. Its brood pouch contains shelled embryos, consistent with ovoviviparity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The snails occupy springs, spring outflows, and seeps. Several species are endemic to single springs. Examples are *P. solus* at Brown Spring and *P. montezumensis* at Montezuma Well and its upper outflow.
- **Geographic scope:** Sites lie in Mohave, Yavapai, Gila, Apache, Graham, Pima, Santa Cruz, and Cochise counties in Arizona. Additional sites are in Washington County, Utah, and Sonora, Mexico.
- **Drainages:** Named localities include the [[Virgin River]] (southwestern Utah and northwestern Arizona), the [[Gila River]] (*T. gilae*), and the Black River headwaters (*P. confluentis* at Three Forks).
- **Conservation relevance:** Narrow endemism and dependence on spring flow make these species vulnerable. The authors frame the systematic work as a prerequisite for conservation decisions.

## Key Findings

- **Species count:** 14 species were recognized: 12 *Pyrgulopsis* (11 of them new, with *P. deserta* previously described) and 2 *Tryonia* (both new). The text describes the 12 *Pyrgulopsis* as allopatric. Each new species is given a diagnosis, description, material examined, type locality, distribution, and etymology.
- **Dimorphism:** Shell sexual dimorphism was significant in at least one population for 10 of 12 species, and in 11 of 33 total populations studied (Appendix 2).
- **Discriminant analysis, correct classification of topotypes (Table 6):**
  - Male shell: 89% (n = 113)
  - Female shell: 93% (n = 108)
  - Male anatomy: 100% (n = 60)
  - Female anatomy: 100% (n = 60)
- **Diagnostic characters:** Male penial anatomy, especially the filament, lobe, and number of glandular ridges, gave the best species separation. Female reproductive morphology was more conserved and showed little variation beyond size, and absence of a seminal receptacle in *P. sancarlosensis*.
- **Ungrouped specimens:** Specimens from additional populations fell close to their topotype clusters but did not always overlap them. Classification of these specimens varied widely, from 0% for several *P. thompsoni* populations to 70% for one additional population of that species.
- **Morphological examples:**
  - *P. glandulosus* has an enlarged penial filament with an elongate ridge, and its holotype is from Nelson Place Spring.
  - *P. conicus* has a short filament with a single ridge near the lobe tip.
  - *P. simplex* is characterized by a minute, squat penis, which the authors describe as unique among southwestern *Pyrgulopsis*.
  - *P. thompsoni*, named for Dr. F.G. Thompson, is found at several springs in southern Santa Cruz County and in Sonora.
  - *T. gilae* is distributed along the Gila River between Bylas and Fort Thomas.
- **Overall conclusion:** The discriminant results support recognizing 12 distinct *Pyrgulopsis* species in Arizona. The authors judge that multivariate morphology can inform the decision when genetic or experimental data are absent.
