## Executive Summary

This document is a **federal fisheries administrative report**, not a species-level scientific study. It was produced by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Division of Fish Hatcheries, and summarizes **fish and fish egg distribution data for Fiscal Year 1992** across the National Fish Hatchery System. The report was compiled on 03/30/1994 and organizes distribution outputs by **hatchery station, program code, and agency**, expressing results as raw numbers (eggs, fish, and weight in presumably pounds) and as percentages of station totals.

The data cover dozens of hatchery stations distributing fish and eggs under distinct **program codes** — most commonly Restoration/Mitigation (RM), Management Assistance (MA), Research (RE), Endangered/Threatened Species (ES), Other (OT), Broodstock (BS), and Farm Pond (FP). No taxonomic, morphological, or ecological species data are included; the utility of this document lies in quantifying **hatchery production allocation** across federal fisheries management priorities.

Aggregate FY1992 totals across all reporting stations were:
- **82,000,949 eggs** distributed
- **208,896,734 fish** distributed
- **6,510,962 lbs (weight)** of fish distributed

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While the report does not describe ecological niches directly, the **program code structure** implies ecological and management context for each distribution activity:

- **RM (Restoration/Mitigation):** Stocking to restore native fish populations or mitigate habitat loss (e.g., dam impacts) — dominant program at hydro-adjacent stations such as [[Dworshak]], [[Hagerman]], [[Craig Brook]], and [[Willard]].
- **ES (Endangered/Threatened Species):** Propagation of federally listed species — notably 100% of output at [[Dexter]] Station and significant fractions at [[Alchesay/Williams Ck]] and [[Saratoga]].
- **MA (Management Assistance):** Support to federal/tribal/state land fisheries management, prevalent at stations like [[Chattahoochee Forest]] and [[Harrison Lake]].
- **RE (Research):** Egg and fish allocations for scientific research and health/technology programs, present in smaller percentages across many stations (e.g., [[Bozeman]], [[Carbon Hill]]).
- **BS (Broodstock):** Egg-only allocations reflecting captive broodstock maintenance, seen at stations like [[Ennis]], [[Creston]], and [[Quinault]].
- **OT/FP (Other/Farm Pond):** Minor distribution categories, including community fishing pond stocking (e.g., [[Private John Allen]]).

Habitat types referenced in agency water-type codes—**Hatchery Federal, Hatchery Other, Lake/Pond, Reservoir, Stream/River/Canal, Estuarine, Tailwater, Aquarium, and Research**—indicate that hatchery-reared fish were released or maintained across a wide range of **lotic, lentic, and estuarine systems**, consistent with a broad national mitigation and restoration mandate spanning [[Anadromous Fish Restoration]] and [[Native Species Recovery]] initiatives.

## Key Findings

- **Total FY1992 national distribution**: 82,000,949 eggs; 208,896,734 fish; 6,510,962 lbs of fish.
- **Restoration/Mitigation (RM)** was the dominant program by fish numbers and weight at the majority of reporting stations (e.g., 100% at [[Abernathy]], [[Craig Brook]], [[Dworshak]], [[Hagerman]], [[Iron River]], [[Quilcene]], [[Willard]], [[White Sulphur Springs]]).
- **Endangered/Threatened Species (ES)** programs achieved 100% station output at [[Dexter]] (215,782 eggs; 145,172 fish; 1,395 lbs) — indicating this station's exclusive dedication to imperiled species propagation.
- **Broodstock (BS)** egg production was substantial but non-distributive at several stations (e.g., [[Ennis]]: 11,509,166 eggs; [[Carson]]: 2,258,400 eggs; [[Creston]]: 2,717,684 eggs), reflecting captive population maintenance rather than field stocking.
- **Gavins Point** and **Garrison Dam** stations handled some of the largest reported fish numbers nationally (21.9 million and 29.5 million+ fish respectively), with Gavins Point showing a mixed RM/MA/OT/RE program allocation.
- **Coleman Station** distributed the highest single-station fish count observed in the dataset (26,552,957 fish), almost entirely under the RM program (100.00%).
- Several small stations (e.g., [[Bears Bluff]], [[Berkshire]]) reported minimal, single-program outputs (100% MA or RM), suggesting narrow operational scope.
- The dataset provides a valuable **quantitative baseline for tracking shifts in USFWS hatchery program priorities**, useful for comparative analysis against later fiscal years to assess trends in [[Endangered Species Propagation]] versus [[Sport Fish Stocking]] versus [[Habitat Mitigation]] efforts.

**Data Limitation Note:** This report is structurally a **program-allocation ledger**, not a biological or ecological dataset. For meaningful ichthyological synthesis, it should be cross-referenced with species-specific USFWS hatchery production records (e.g., [[National Fish Hatchery System]] annual species reports) to link program codes and station names to actual taxa distributed.
