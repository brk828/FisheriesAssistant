# DDT Metabolism in Tolerant and Susceptible Populations of Mosquito Fish (*Gambusia affinis*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #ecotoxicology #DDT #pesticide-resistance

## Executive Summary
This study compares DDT uptake and metabolic conversion (to DDD and DDE) between a **DDT-tolerant population** of [[Gambusia affinis]] from Sidon, Mississippi, and a **DDT-susceptible population** from State College, Mississippi. Fish were experimentally exposed to DDT solutions (10–85 ppb) for 16–120 hours, and tissues were analyzed via gas chromatography. Field-collected Sidon fish already carried substantial DDT and metabolite residues, while State College fish were initially clean. Both populations demonstrated the capacity to metabolize DDT into [[DDD]] and [[DDE]] given sufficient exposure time, with the tolerant population showing a slightly faster conversion rate, suggesting an enzymatic or physiological basis for [[Pesticide Resistance]] in [[Gambusia affinis]].

## Background & Description
- No distinct morphological differences between populations are described; the study is biochemical/physiological rather than anatomical.
- Tissue compartments analyzed for DDT and metabolite distribution:
  - **Whole fish** (n = 107 individuals analyzed)
  - **Heads, bodies, and viscera** analyzed separately (n = 34 individuals)
- Pooled sample groups of 5–7 fish were used for gas chromatographic analysis (method adapted from Mills, 1959).
- **Tissue distribution pattern**: DDT and metabolite concentrations in **heads and viscera were 2–4 times higher** than in the **bodies**, indicating differential lipid distribution and/or metabolic activity across body regions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species**: *Gambusia affinis* (Mosquito Fish), family [[Poeciliidae]].
- **Habitat**: Freshwater systems in Mississippi, USA — both a naturally DDT-contaminated site (Sidon) and a reference site with no prior DDT exposure (State College).
- **Ecological role**: Widely used as a biological mosquito control agent, placing this species directly in contact with agricultural/public health DDT applications historically used for vector control.
- **Environmental context**: Sidon population represents a field population under long-term agricultural DDT exposure pressure, providing a natural experiment in pesticide-driven [[Evolutionary Tolerance]].
- Relevant to broader research on [[Bioaccumulation]] and [[Biomagnification]] of organochlorine pesticides in aquatic food webs.

## Key Findings
- **Field baseline (Sidon, tolerant population)**: Contained 5.49 ppm DDT, 4.48 ppm DDD, and 2.34 ppm DDE at time of collection — confirming chronic environmental exposure and metabolic processing in the wild.
- **Experimental exposure (Sidon fish)**: In 40–85 ppb DDT solutions, DDT tissue levels increased with exposure duration up to 48 hr; DDE and DDD levels remained stable (no significant further conversion detected in this timeframe).
- **State College (susceptible) population — baseline**: No detectable DDT or metabolites prior to experimental exposure.
- **Short-term exposure (State College)**: After 40 hr in 10–15 ppb DDT, whole fish accumulated 1.29–3.37 ppm DDT with **no metabolite conversion**.
- **Depuration trial 1**: 33 hr exposure (40 ppb DDT) + 19 hr in tapwater → **no conversion to DDD/DDE** detected.
- **Depuration trial 2**: 120 hr exposure (10 ppb DDT) + 96 hr in tapwater → **DDT, DDD, and DDE all recovered**, indicating that longer exposure and depuration time is required for susceptible fish to metabolize DDT.
- **Metabolite abundance hierarchy**: When both metabolites were present, the order of abundance was consistently **DDT > DDD > DDE**.
- **Tissue-specific accumulation**: Heads and viscera consistently showed 2–4x higher total DDT residues and individual compound levels compared to body tissue, likely reflecting lipid-rich organ concentration.
- **Comparative conclusion**: Both tolerant and susceptible populations of *Gambusia affinis* are capable of metabolizing DDT into DDD and DDE given adequate time; however, the **tolerant (Sidon) population may exhibit a somewhat faster metabolic conversion rate**, supporting the hypothesis that enhanced detoxification capacity underlies field-observed [[DDT Resistance]] in this species.
- This work is a **preliminary study**, indicating need for further research to confirm mechanistic and kinetic differences between populations.
