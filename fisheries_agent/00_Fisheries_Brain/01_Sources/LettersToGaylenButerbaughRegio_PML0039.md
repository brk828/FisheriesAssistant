# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Correspondence, 1982–1983

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of letters and a cover note from the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Regional Director, Region 6, in Denver. The letters span July 1982 to August 1983. They concern the recovery of endangered upper basin Colorado River fishes, chiefly [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The team comments on draft Recovery Plans and on a proposed [[Conservation Plan]]. It also raises concerns about unanswered correspondence and about the urgent need to collect [[Bonytail Chub]] brood stock. The team asks for a response and for an extension of its review deadline to October 1, 1983.

## Background & Description

The correspondence is from the team leader, J. A. St. Amant, and is copied to team members including Don Archer (Recovery Team Coordinator), Fred Bolwahn, and Jane Roybal. Its main addressees are Galen Buterbaugh, Regional Director, and Robert E. Shields, Acting Regional Director, both at the Denver Federal Center.

The document has four parts:

- **Letter of July 13, 1982** (to Regional Director Ron Winchell): the team's comments on the Conservation Plan presented by Don Archer at the May 12–13, 1982 meeting.
- **Letter of July 26, 1983** (to Galen Buterbaugh): the team's response to a June 16, 1983 letter on draft revisions of the Colorado squawfish and bonytail chub Recovery Plans.
- **Letter of July 29, 1983** (to Robert E. Shields): a request for a review extension to October 1, 1983.
- **Cover letter of August 20, 1983** (to Galen Buterbaugh): a complaint that earlier letters had received no response or acknowledgment, and a request for a reply.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letters describe the following threats and conditions for the upper basin fishes:

- **Habitat loss:** The draft Recovery Plan states that [[Colorado Squawfish]] has been extirpated from much of its historic range. The remaining range is being fragmented by water projects. The Taylor Draw Project was under construction at the time, and the team expected it to cause a loss of Colorado squawfish habitat in the White River.
- **Flow alteration:** Proposed projects on the Yampa River, including Juniper Canyon, and water depletion from major tributaries such as the Little Snake, Duchesne, and Blue Rivers, could reduce flows to levels that cannot sustain the species.
- **Migration:** Colorado squawfish make long spawning migrations and occupy seasonal home ranges. The team noted that Green River and Colorado River populations are linked, and that spawning migrations from the Green River into the Yampa and White Rivers have been documented by several agencies.
- **Population status:** Catch-per-unit-effort data show that Colorado squawfish catch rates in the upper Colorado River are considerably lower than in the Green River. The team regarded this as evidence of a possible rapid decline. It warned that assertions of population stability would be "capricious."
- **Bonytail rarity:** Field reports described bonytail as extremely rare in riverine habitats. The team considered it likely that no viable wild population currently exists in these habitats.
- **Genetic limits:** Existing captive brood stocks were judged insufficient to maintain genetic variability.

## Key Findings

**1. Conservation Plan and Recovery Plan structure (July 13, 1982)**
- The team considers the Conservation Plan useful for directing specific USFWS programs, but only if it does not discourage recovery efforts.
- The Conservation Plan should be a separate document from the Recovery Plans. References to it should be deleted from the Recovery Plan drafts.
- If a Conservation Plan Council is needed, the team recommends that its functions be handled through the Colorado River Wildlife Council, or that each team member be made an ad hoc member.
- A Conservation Plan should also be developed for the lower basin.

**2. Colorado Squawfish status and habitat (July 26, 1983)**
- The USFWS had reported that squawfish occupy about 900 miles of riverine habitat. The team disputes that this shows a stable population. It notes that the species has been extirpated from much of its historic range, and that the draft Conservation Plan estimates a reduction of 75 percent (OCR reading).
- The team recommends that the species receive legal protection under the Endangered Species Act before any further habitat alteration occurs.
- The team notes that no sufficient or viable population of bonytail chub exists in natural habitats. Studies of habitat requirements and population dynamics are therefore possible only with reintroduced populations.

**3. Bonytail Chub emergency recommendations (July 26, 1983)**
- The June 1983 Recovery Team meeting concluded that drastic measures must be taken immediately if bonytail chub is to be prevented from disappearing from riverine habitats.
- The main objective of the revised draft Bonytail Chub Recovery Plan is to collect enough brood stock to protect against extinction and to ensure genetic heterozygosity. The team recommends that brood stock be collected from both the Green River and Lake Mohave and transported to the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- The team recommends that the USFWS Colorado River Squawfish Management Program (CRSMP) field crews be directed to sample as many suitable habitats as possible while capturing bonytail chub. The field crews should also be given equipment and facilities to temporarily hold and transport any phenotypically pure bonytail specimens.
- The team recommends that an interagency group be asked to collect bonytail from Lake Mohave. This could be done alongside other research or management work, and the team believes the cost would be relatively low. Helicopter transport to Dexter or another suitable facility was suggested as a possible arrangement.
- The team asks that these measures be carried out immediately to secure adequate captive brood stock in 1983.

**4. Bonytail Chub recovery objectives (July 13, 1982 and July 26, 1983)**
- The USFWS had questioned whether the stated recovery objectives were practical. The team responded that the recovery objectives are reasonable and necessary to maintain species security.
- Stocking numbers, maintaining populations, the need for an upper basin hatchery for endangered species, and related issues were raised in the USFWS letter. The team judges most of these to be inadequately addressed in the latest draft Recovery Plans.
- The team asks the Regional Director to identify any issues in the latest draft Recovery Plans that the team has not addressed or has not addressed adequately.

**5. Communication and process (August 20, 1983)**
- The team complains of a one-sided correspondence, noting that its letters of July 13, 1982, July 26, 1983, and July 29, 1983 went unanswered.
- The team leader notes that he failed to copy the 1983 letter to Don Archer, and that Archer had not seen the team's request for an extension of the Conservation Plan review deadline.
- The team asks for a written response to this letter and to the enclosed letters.

**6. Review deadline (July 29, 1983)**
- The team requests an extension of the review deadline to October 1, 1983. This is needed so that it can provide complete and accurate recommendations on the Preliminary Conservation Plan proposal.
