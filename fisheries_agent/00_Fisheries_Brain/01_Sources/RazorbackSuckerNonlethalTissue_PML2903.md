# Razorback Sucker Non-Lethal Tissue Sampling Protocol for Genetic Analysis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This protocol, issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Colorado River Fishes Project (CRFP), sets out field procedures for collecting muscle and fin tissue from [[Razorback Sucker]] and related catostomids (bluehead, flannelmouth, white sucker and hybrids) for [[Allozyme Analysis]] and [[Mitochondrial DNA Analysis]]. Five field teams would sample wild and hatchery fish across the Colorado River Basin during spring sampling windows. Sampling is non-lethal, using biopsy punches on anesthetized fish, and the tissues are preserved on dry ice and shipped to the laboratory. The fiscal year is not legible in the scan. The protocol also covers gamete collection and tissue collection from dead fish.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endangered [[Catostomidae]] fish of the Colorado River system. The protocol treats it as the focal species and samples three closely related catostomids and hybrids for comparison. The purpose is genetic characterization of populations, including stocks held for recovery at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and wild fish captured for transfer to refuge facilities.

The document specifies a target of 30 fish per species (n=30) at each site. The scanned text reads "99 fish," which appears to be an OCR error. Teams are asked to meet this target only where it does not endanger razorback suckers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling occurs across several reservoir and river reaches:

- **Lake Powell:** San Juan and Colorado River arms, including Hite Marina. Sampling window April 1–28.
- **Upper Colorado River mainstem:** Grand Valley area. Sampling window March 18–20 and April.
- **Green River:** Echo Park, Razorback Bar and Escalante Ranch. Sampling window May/June.
- **Lake Mojave:** Sampling window March 17–19. Samples go directly to UCLA/ASU.
- **Dexter NFH broodstock:** Sampling window February 25–March 1.

Team 2 would capture 13 to 18 razorback suckers from the Grand Valley reach and transport them to Dexter NFH for tissue collection. Team 5 would sample either broodfish (preferred) or offspring. Broodfish sampling would require work in the following year. Fish are handled with care for their survival, and the protocol states that teams should collect only as many samples as they can without jeopardizing razorback sucker lives. The protocol also requires state and federal collecting permits, including National Park Service permission.

## Key Findings

**Sampling procedure (non-lethal)**
- Anesthetize each fish with an appropriate concentration of anesthetic (the agent name is garbled in the scan). Cover fish with wet towels to protect from sun and wind.
- Take two tissues per fish for allozyme analysis: **dorsal muscle** and **caudal fin**.
- Use biopsy punches to remove tissue plugs of about 5 mm × 5 mm (about 1/4 inch). Swab the site with Betadine. Return the fish to water once data collection is complete.
- Clean and dry instruments between samples to avoid cross-contamination.
- Assign roles: one person takes tissues, another labels vials and records data.

**Sample coding and labeling**
- Each team is assigned a unique block of sample numbers. Teams listed include 001–149 (Team 1), 150–299 (Team 2) and 300–999 (Vernal CRFP).
- Each vial is labeled with a unique three-digit number plus a species code (RB, BH, FM, WS, H) and a tissue code (M = muscle, F = fin).
- Example: the first razorback sucker captured by team 001–149 has muscle in vial **001RBM** and fin in vial **001RBF**. Labels are written twice, on front and back.
- For razorback suckers, the PIT-tag number is recorded with the vial number in the field notebook.

**Field data recorded**
- Fish number and PIT-tag number, date, tissues collected, collector initials, river mile location, length and weight, and ecological data (depth, velocity, substrate, water temperature).

**Storage and shipment**
- Place vials in racks on ice or dry ice as soon as possible. Muscle and fin samples can be held on wet ice for 6–7 hours.
- Tissues can be kept in a standard freezer at −20 °C for a couple of weeks. The priority is to move them to an ultracold freezer (−80 °C) quickly, and avoid thawing.
- Ship on dry ice by overnight mail, with enough dry ice (about 5–10 lb) to keep tissues frozen on arrival. Send to the CRFP lab in Vernal, Utah. Lake Mojave samples go directly to UCLA.

**Gamete collection**
- Express gametes into sterile 15 ml conical tubes, using gentle pressure on the fish's flanks. Duplicate tubes are recommended. Avoid contaminating samples with water or dirt.
- Tissues may be kept on wet ice for up to about 4–6 days before flash-freezing. Frozen tubes become brittle at −80 °C and are prone to cracking.

**Tissues from deceased fish**
- Preferred tissue order for DNA work: gonads, heart, liver, spleen, kidney, muscle. Dissect 1–5 g per tissue, place in tubes, duplicate if possible, freeze immediately on dry ice or at −20 °C, then ship on dry ice.

**Equipment**
- Core kit supplied by Vernal CRFP includes ice chests, anesthetic, Betadine, biopsy punches, forceps, tissue scissors, 1.5 ml snap-cap vials, 15 ml tubes, waterproof field notebooks, permanent markers (not felt-tip "markers" that can be lost), masking tape, aluminum foil, dry ice and a measuring board.
