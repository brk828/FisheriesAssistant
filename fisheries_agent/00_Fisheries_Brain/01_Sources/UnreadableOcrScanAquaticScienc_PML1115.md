# Studies of the Fishes of the Order Cyprinodontes XVII: Genera and Species of the Colorado River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This Occasional Paper from the [[Museum of Zoology]], University of Michigan (No. 433, June 23, 1941), by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Robert R. Miller]], is a taxonomic and nomenclatural study of cyprinodont fishes (order Cyprinodontes) of the [[Colorado River System]]. It covers four genera and species from the families Cyprinodontidae and Poeciliidae, and it revises the status of the genus [[Arizonichthys]]. The authors conclude that [[Arizonichthys Psammophilus]] is a synonym of [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]], and that [[Crenichthys Baileyi]] belongs in [[Crenichthys]] rather than [[Cyprinodon]]. The paper also places the relict fish populations of Crenichthys baileyi in the context of an extinct Quaternary river.

## Background & Description

The paper responds to a description of *Arizonichthys psammophilus* by [[J. T. Nichols]] (1940). The authors treat four Colorado River taxa in more detail in later publications. Their approach combines synonymy, original descriptions, comparisons, and literature records, with extensive references to earlier workers such as [[David Starr Jordan]], [[Charles H. Gilbert]], and [[Carl Hubbs]] himself.

Key taxonomic points for each taxon:

- **[[Crenichthys]]**: A derivative of [[Empetrichthys]] with a wide distribution in springs and creeks of southern Nevada, including one present tributary of the Colorado River. The type species is *C. nevadae*. The authors state that field studies in 1934 and 1938 demonstrated this distribution.
- **[[Cyprinodon Macularius]]**: The Colorado River representatives are not revised here because a revision of the genus was in preparation. The authors note that several forms from other river systems have been confused with this species, and that a record from "Colorado (Eigenmann)" by [[C. Tate Regan]] (1907) was probably based on specimens from the [[Colorado Desert]].
- **[[Gambusia Affinis Affinis]]**: Widely introduced and established in the Colorado River system and elsewhere in the West.
- **[[Poeciliopsis]]**: A genus whose status as a unit "remains somewhat uncertain." Large series from the Pacific drainage of Mexico are available for a prospective revision.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Colorado River system in this paper includes desert springs, creeks, and river courses. The authors describe several habitat and historical features:

- **Warm springs and isolated waters**: *Crenichthys baileyi* is now confined to a chain of isolated warm-spring waters. These were formerly connected to one another and to the Colorado River through the Quaternary [[White River]], an extinct river. Its former course can be traced by relict fish populations, continuous terraces, and dry canyons.
- **Moapa River**: The warm-spring headwaters of the [[Moapa River]] (Muddy River) still flow to the Colorado River. Its lower course is now flooded by [[Lake Mead]]. This population is therefore included in the present Colorado River fauna.
- **Distribution limits**: *C. baileyi* does not occur in Railroad Valley, Nevada, where *C. nevadae* abounds in warm springs. Instead, it replaces that species in the White River and [[Pahranagat Valley]].
- **Santa Cruz River system**: *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* is described as one of the commonest fishes in the southern part of the Colorado River drainage basin, particularly in the [[Santa Cruz River]] system.
- **Desert forms**: The authors state that the occurrence of peculiar cyprinodont fishes in isolated desert environments is not without precedent. However, *Empetrichthys* and *Crenichthys* have a wider distribution than first indicated, and *Arizonichthys* is "definitely not to be included among the isolated types."

## Key Findings

1. **Crenichthys baileyi is a member of Crenichthys.** Although it was long confused with *Cyprinodon*, it agrees with *C. nevadae* in all essential respects. Both lack pelvic fins and have bifid rather than trifid teeth. The main difference is coloration: the lateral dark spots of *C. baileyi* are in two rows rather than a single series. This conclusion had received preliminary notice in a paper by [[Sumner and Sargent]] (1940).

2. **Populations of C. baileyi are partially differentiated.** The isolated populations show indications of partial differentiation and will probably be treated as subspecies. Several thousand specimens have been preserved for the analysis of this speciation.

3. **Arizonichthys psammophilus is a synonym of Poeciliopsis occidentalis.** The holotype, a 48-mm type, matched a large *P. occidentalis* in every respect the authors compared. No series topotypic with *psammophilus* (from Tanque Verde Creek) had been seen, but the authors see no reason to doubt the identity. The type locality is "34 miles east of Tanque Verde, Pima County, Arizona."

4. **The validity of Arizonichthys is doubtful.** The authors note that it is not certain that the name was validly proposed. Nichols gave no formal generic diagnosis, no comparison, no allocation to family, and no formal designation of a type species. The authors do not wish to stress this point, given uncertain rules about generic names proposed after 1930.

5. **Poeciliopsis occidentalis may be generically separable, but the name Arizonichthys will likely remain a synonym.** *P. occidentalis* seems separable from *Poeciliopsis infans* (type species of *Leptorhaphis*) only on very trivial characters. The two are geographically connected by a chain of related forms, to be described in a later publication.

6. **Ovarian structure can identify family.** The ovarian structure differs between viviparous and oviparous cyprinodonts. This provides a means of family identification for specimen series that do not include males.

7. **Regan's record of Cyprinodon macularius from "Colorado" likely refers to the Colorado Desert** in southeastern California, not to the Colorado River itself.

8. **Isolated desert fishes are not all unique.** The authors conclude that *Empetrichthys* and *Crenichthys* have wider distributions than first indicated, and that the Colorado River fauna includes relict populations documenting a former river course.
