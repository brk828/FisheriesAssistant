# Fishery Investigations of the Central Arizona Project Canal System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish-communities

## Executive Summary

This abstract describes fishery monitoring of the [[Central Arizona Project Canal System]], a roughly 335-mile conveyance that carries [[Colorado River]] water from [[Lake Havasu]] across Arizona to [[Tucson]]. The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] has surveyed fish communities since 1986 under a mitigation agreement with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. Before 1990, surveys extended only as far as the [[Brady Pumping Plant]] near [[Eloy]]. In 1992 the canal was fully operational to Tucson, so the survey covered its entire length. The authors report that fish communities change markedly along the canal and that the 1991 and 1992 results are compared with the 1986–1989 findings.

## Background & Description

- **System:** The canal is a series of concrete-lined channels, inverted siphons, tunnels, pumping plants, and pipelines.
- **Authors:** Thomas Burke, Joe Kahr, and Marty Jakler, Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City, NV, and Phoenix, AZ).
- **Monitoring basis:** The survey is a [[Mitigation Agreement]] obligation, and the sampling program has run since 1986.
- **Survey extent:** Pre-1990 surveys covered only the upper reaches. The 1992 survey covered the full canal, which is the main change in scope.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The abstract describes a strong downflow gradient:

- **Physical structure:** Canals and pumping plants get smaller in the downflow direction.
- **Hydraulics:** Water volume and velocity decrease downflow.
- **Water quality:** Conductance and temperature increase downflow.
- **Fish communities:** Communities show exaggerated [[Boom and Bust Dynamics]], meaning sharp population swings in response to conditions.

Observed conditions in the 1991 and 1992 surveys include emaciated and poor-condition [[Common Carp]], the return of [[Threadfin Shad]], a relative absence of [[Striped Bass]], and the introduction of new (unnamed) species. These observations relate to [[Invasive Species]] establishment in a managed, engineered water conveyance.

## Key Findings

1. **Full-length survey:** In 1992 the survey covered the entire canal to Tucson, extending well beyond the Brady Pumping Plant limit used before 1990.
2. **Downflow gradient:** Physical size, flow volume, velocity, conductance, and temperature all change predictably along the system.
3. **Boom-and-bust fish dynamics:** Fish communities fluctuate strongly, with effects that are described as exaggerated.
4. **Carp condition:** Carp were observed to be emaciated and in poor condition in 1991 and 1992.
5. **Species changes:** Threadfin shad returned, striped bass were relatively absent, and new species were introduced. The abstract does not name the new species or give counts.
6. **Temporal comparison:** The 1991–1992 results are to be contrasted with the 1986–1989 survey findings. The abstract does not report the comparison's outcome or any statistics.
