> **Note:** This report concerns a **freshwater** riverine system (Little Colorado River / Colorado River confluence, Grand Canyon), not a marine ecosystem. Metadata fields above reflect this despite template naming conventions.

## Executive Summary

This semi-annual report documents fish population monitoring conducted by Arizona State University (ASU) and the Nevada Natural Heritage Program (NNHP) along the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) near its confluence with the [[Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon]], as part of Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (Cooperative Agreement No. 1-FC-40-10490). Seven ten-day research trips were conducted between January and June 1992 across three sampling reaches: **Confluence**, **Powell**, and **Salt**. A total of **6,044 fish** were captured, of which **6,006 (99.4%)** were native species and only **38 (0.6%)** were non-native, underscoring the LCR's importance as a native fish refuge. The dominant species captured was the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (n=4,671), followed by [[Bluehead Sucker]] (n=1,039) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (n=295). A single [[Razorback Sucker]] was also recorded. Tag/recapture data indicate active, ongoing PIT-tag monitoring programs for the three primary native species, with recapture rates ranging from 9.7% (bluehead sucker) to 53.4% (humpback chub). No chub mortalities occurred during the reporting period, attributed to a switch from 2.5 cm to 3.8 cm trammel net mesh.

## Background & Description

The report is a population-monitoring dataset rather than a morphological study; detailed morphometric data are not presented. However, size-class data are reported using a threshold of **150 mm total length (TL)**, used operationally to distinguish juvenile/sub-adult from adult native fishes:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]**: 1,082 individuals <150 mm TL; 3,589 individuals ≥150 mm TL (adults) — adults dominate captures across all three reaches.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]**: 45 individuals <150 mm TL; 250 individuals ≥150 mm TL.
- **[[Bluehead Sucker]]**: 42 individuals <150 mm TL; 997 individuals ≥150 mm TL.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: 1 individual captured (all size classes combined), at the Confluence reach only — indicating extreme rarity of this species in the LCR system by 1992.

Fish were marked using **PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags**, with some individuals still carrying legacy **carlin/floy tags** ("V-OLD") from earlier tagging efforts, indicating a multi-year, longitudinal mark-recapture study design.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The study reaches — **Confluence**, **Powell**, and **Salt** — represent discrete sampling zones along the lower [[Little Colorado River]], a critical tributary spawning and rearing habitat for native Colorado River basin fishes, particularly the endangered [[Humpback Chub]].
- **Hydrology**: Two of the seven trips (Winter2 and Spring4) occurred during **LCR flood conditions**, which measurably reduced fishing effort (net-hours) and catch per unit effort (CPUE) in several instances, indicating flow regime as an important ecological driver of fish accessibility/catchability.
- **Native fish dominance**: Native species constituted >99% of the catch, suggesting the LCR functions as a refuge from non-native fish introgression/competition present elsewhere in the [[Colorado River]] mainstem — likely due to the LCR's naturally variable, sediment-laden, warm-water conditions that are less favorable to introduced cold-adapted salmonids and other non-natives.
- **Non-native assemblage**: Non-natives captured included [[Channel Catfish]] (n=23, most abundant non-native), [[Rainbow Trout]] (n=7), [[Common Carp]] (n=3), [[Black Bullhead]] (n=2), [[Brown Trout]] (n=2), and [[Yellow Bullhead]] (n=1). No [[Fathead Minnow]] or [[Green Sunfish]] were captured despite being monitored for.
- **Spatial distribution**: The **Salt** reach yielded the highest native fish catch (2,434) and generally the highest CPUE for humpback chub in peak season (Spring3: 12.52/100 hrs; Summer1: 17.27/100 hrs), while the **Confluence** reach showed the highest overall HBC CPUE trends in early season trips.
- **Seasonal patterns**: CPUE for [[Humpback Chub]] increased substantially from winter into summer across all reaches (e.g., Powell: 1.73 in Winter2 to 8.64–11.09 in Spring3/Summer1), suggesting increased LCR occupancy/activity of adult chub in warmer months, possibly linked to spawning migration and warmer water temperatures.

## Key Findings

1. **Total catch (Jan–Jun 1992)**: 6,044 fish captured; 6,006 native (99.4%), 38 non-native (0.6%).
2. **Species composition (native)**: [[Humpback Chub]] 4,671 (77.8%); [[Bluehead Sucker]] 1,039 (17.3%); [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] 295 (4.9%); [[Razorback Sucker]] 1 (<0.1%).
3. **Non-native composition**: [[Channel Catfish]] most abundant (23 of 38, 60.5%), followed by [[Rainbow Trout]] (7) and [[Common Carp]] (3).
4. **Fishing effort**: 49,404 total net-hours across 154 trap-days; grand mean CPUE across all reaches = 9.79 native fish/100 net-hrs vs. 0.08 non-native fish/100 net-hrs — a ~120:1 native-to-non-native catch ratio.
5. **Humpback Chub tag/recapture (n=3,589 adults)**:
   - 1,673 new PIT tags issued; 1,916 recaptures (1,456 old tags + 460 new-in-1992 tags recaptured).
   - Overall recapture rate: **53.4%**; recapture rate of old tags specifically: **40.6%**.
   - Zero mortalities recorded — attributed to switch from 2.5 cm to **3.8 cm trammel net mesh**.
6. **Flannelmouth Sucker tag/recapture (n=276 adults)**:
   - 176 new PIT tags issued; 74 recaptures; 26 fish escaped/released untagged.
   - Overall recapture rate: **26.8%** (all recaptures were of old tags; 0.0% same-year recapture).
7. **Bluehead Sucker tag/recapture (n=1,017 adults)**:
   - 898 new PIT tags issued; 99 recaptures; 20 fish escaped/released untagged.
   - Overall recapture rate: **9.7%** — lowest of the three native species monitored, possibly reflecting higher population size, lower catchability, or higher tag loss.
8. **Reach-level totals (native fish)**: Salt (2,434) > Powell (1,998) > Confluence (1,574).
9. **Flood effects**: Trips conducted during LCR flood conditions (Winter2, Spring4) consistently showed reduced fishing effort and, in several cases, depressed CPUE, highlighting flow as a confound in monitoring data interpretation.
10. **Program continuity**: Presence of legacy carlin/floy tags alongside PIT tags confirms this is a long-term, multi-year mark-recapture effort supporting demographic and movement studies of the [[Humpback Chub]] population within the [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem.
