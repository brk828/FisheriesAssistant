# Multiple Sex Chromosomes in a Mexican Cyprinodontid Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 *Nature* report by Teruya Uyeno and [[Robert Rush Miller]] (Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan) describes an undescribed killifish from Mexico with a multiple sex chromosome system, the first such mechanism reported in fishes. Females have 2n = 48 and males 2n = 47. The authors propose that a Y chromosome fused with an autosome, producing an X1X1X2X2 female and an X1X2Y male. The study places the fish in an unnamed genus most closely related to [[Cyprinodon]].

## Background & Description

- **Family context:** The chromosomes of [[Cyprinodontidae]] (killifishes) were under active study. Karyotypes of 48 of roughly 85 North American species had been determined, 27 of them by the authors. Nearly all of the 15–16 North American genera had been examined.
- **Typical karyotype:** The diploid number in American killifishes is usually 48. Exceptions include some [[Fundulus]] species and *Lucania parva* (2n = 46), *L. interioris* (2n = 36), and *Adinia xenica* (2n = 32). The last three have large metacentric chromosomes that indicate centric fusions. For example, *Adinia* has 16 large metacentrics (48 − 32).
- **Study material:** Chromosomes were prepared from gill epithelium using the [[Mcphail And Jones Method]]. Fifty cells from three females and 32 cells from four males were examined. A testis squash provided meiotic figures.
- **Somatic karyotype (both sexes):** One metacentric pair, 18 pairs of submetacentric–subtelocentric chromosomes, and four pairs of acrocentric chromosomes. The female has one additional acrocentric pair. The male has a single large metacentric chromosome, which is about three to five times longer than any other chromosome.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Heterogamety in fishes:** Summarizing earlier work, [[Ebeling And Chen]] reported XX–XY and XX–XO systems in about 20 of the roughly 200 fish species karyotyped. Twelve of these came from the deep-sea habitat. The authors' fish is the first fish with a multiple sex chromosome mechanism.
- **Comparative cases:** Multiple sex chromosomes (X1X1X2X2/X1X2Y) were previously known in *Anolis* lizards and in the rodent *Mus minutoides*. The origin proposed for the fish parallels the explanation offered for *Mus minutoides*.
- **Relationship:** The authors suggest the new genus is most closely related to *Cyprinodon*, based on shared morphology such as tricuspid teeth, body shape, size, fin-ray counts, and squamation. The ancestral diploid number of 48 matches the 13 *Cyprinodon* species karyotyped to that point.

## Key Findings

1. **Sex chromosome system:** Female karyotype is 2n = 48, interpreted as 44 autosomes plus X1X1X2X2. Male karyotype is 2n = 47, interpreted as 44 autosomes plus X1X2Y. This is the first multiple sex chromosome mechanism reported in fishes.
2. **Origin hypothesis:** A Y chromosome fused with an acrocentric autosome (a Robertsonian fusion), which reduced the male's acrocentric count by one relative to the female. The authors also suggest some addition of chromatin, since the Y is longer than the combined length of any two acrocentric chromosomes.
3. **Meiosis:** In the testis squash, the long Y is visible at spermatogonial prophase. At diakinesis, the cell shows 22 bivalents and one trivalent, in which X1 and X2 appear attached to both ends of the Y.
4. **Evolutionary sequence:** The authors propose that both sexes once had identical karyotypes with 46 autosomes plus indistinguishable X and Y chromosomes. The Y then fused with an acrocentric autosome, and the homologous pair in the female became the additional X chromosome (X2).
5. **Broader implication:** The authors argue that the cytological sex-determining mechanism in fishes is more complex than previously thought. They expect further fish karyotype studies may reveal diversity comparable to that seen in higher vertebrates.
