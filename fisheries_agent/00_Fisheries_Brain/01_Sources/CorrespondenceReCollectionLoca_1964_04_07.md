## Executive Summary
This document is not a formal scientific paper but a personal letter from Arthur O. Flechsig (Scripps Institution of Oceanography) to Dr. Robert R. Miller (University of Michigan), dated 7 April 1964. The letter provides reconstructed locality and habitat data for a fish collection site designated **FM-5**, located in the middle fork of the [[Rio Papigochic]] drainage, Chihuahua, Mexico. The correspondence is valuable as a primary-source record of field methodology and site characterization from a 1953 collecting expedition, illustrating how historical ichthyological locality data were documented, cross-referenced, and later reconstructed from memory and period cartography (American Geographical Society 1:1,000,000 series map).

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or taxonomic data on specific fish specimens are provided in this letter. The correspondence is strictly locality- and habitat-descriptive in nature. No species identifications, counts, or physical descriptions of collected fish are given.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The habitat described at site **FM-5** includes the following features, relevant to reconstructing the ecological context of the original collection:

- **Site type**: A pool within an intermittent stream reach (surface flow absent on the date of collection, 28 June 1953), fed by an **underground inlet**.
- **Physical structure**: 
  - One side (~4 ft deep) bordered by **rock walls**.
  - Opposite side characterized by a **stony shingle** substrate.
- **Vegetation**: **Sparse aquatic vegetation**; minimal grass cover at the pool edge.
- **Surrounding land use**: **Plowed agricultural fields** adjacent to the site, indicating anthropogenic disturbance/proximity to cultivation.
- **Hydrology**: Classified as part of the middle fork of the [[Rio Papigochic]], a drainage system in the Sierra Madre Occidental region of Chihuahua, Mexico — a system of biogeographic interest for endemic freshwater fish faunas (e.g., [[Cyprinidae]], [[Poeciliidae]] lineages native to northern Mexican highland streams).
- Nearby comparative site **FM-4** was located ~2 miles farther upstream, suggesting a longitudinal sampling transect along the same stream reach.

The letter also references a secondary locality of ecological interest: a stream near **Bacoyna** described as "axolotl laden," suggesting the presence of neotenic salamanders ([[Ambystoma]] sp.), indicating the broader region supports diverse aquatic vertebrate fauna beyond fishes, including amphibians associated with montane freshwater habitats.

## Key Findings
- **Precise georeferencing** of historical collection site FM-5: Lat. 28° 11′ N, Long. 107° 18′ W, derived from a 1:1,000,000-scale American Geographical Society map — useful for modern georeferencing/verification efforts in ichthyological collection databases.
- Distances from FM-5 to nearby towns are provided for cross-validation of locality accuracy:
  - 1.5 miles from **Temeichic**
  - 5.2 miles from **San Miguel**
  - 9.1 miles from **Pachera**
- The intermittent nature of the stream (dry surface flow with underground inlet) on 28 June 1953 highlights seasonal hydrological variability in this Sierra Madre Occidental drainage, an important ecological consideration for interpreting historical fish collection records from the region.
- The letter documents the informal, memory-based reconstruction of field data common in mid-20th-century ichthyological fieldwork, underscoring both the value and limitations of historical museum locality records tied to specimens from the [[Rio Papigochic]] and greater [[Rio Conchos]] systems (the latter referenced near Panalachic).
- Additional unstructured notes mention mineral occurrences (calcite crystals in red, yellow, green forms) near **Sisoguichic**, indicating the correspondence blends geological and biological field observations typical of exploratory expedition notes from this era.
