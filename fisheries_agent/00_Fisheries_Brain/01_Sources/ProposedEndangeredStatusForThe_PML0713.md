# Proposed Endangered and Threatened Status for Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1978 Federal Register proposed rule, issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (dated April 13, 1978, signed by Director Lynn A. Greenwalt), proposes to list the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) as Endangered and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Both fishes are endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]] and have declined sharply due to [[Dam Construction]], impoundment, water diversion, habitat alteration and predation or competition by [[Introduced Fish Species]]. The proposal also includes a [[Special Rule]] allowing razorback sucker take under state law, and it requests public comment. The issue also contains unrelated notices from the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare on research involving institutionalized persons.

## Background & Description

- **Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*):** Historically found throughout the large river habitat of the Colorado River basin. It alternates between swift canyon reaches with torrential rapids and slow, sand-bottomed meanders. Adults are now found mainly in reservoirs and in the lower basin, such as [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]]. The most recent collection of adults cited is from [[Desolation Canyon]] on the Green River, Utah.
- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*):** Named for a pronounced, bony keel behind the head. It prefers slow backwater areas, where it feeds on bottom detritus and possibly plankton. It was historically abundant and was caught and sold as food by early settlers. Apparent maximum age is about 30 years. It grows to large sizes in reservoirs and is relatively abundant there, but recruitment appears insufficient to maintain populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat alteration:** Dams for power generation and irrigation in the 20th century changed much of the original river ecosystem. Much of the Colorado River no longer has a natural flood regime. Impoundment and diversion are the main drivers of decline.
- **Green River and Dinosaur National Monument:** The most dramatic bonytail decline occurred here during the 1960s, after the 1962 closure of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. The decline apparently began after 1966, when the reservoir stabilized near its planned capacity. No reproduction had been noted in recent years, despite targeted searches for young fish.
- **Upper basin conditions:** Microhabitat alteration by tributary dams, competition with exotic species, and predation are the most likely causes of decline. A synergistic effect of these factors is also possible. Razorback sucker reasons for decline in the upper basin are less clear, but minor habitat alteration by upstream impoundments and water withdrawals, possibly combined with competition and predation, are suspected.
- **Reproduction status:** Razorback sucker spawning was observed in reservoirs such as Lake Mead, Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu, but no juveniles had been found there. Adults are old, but some younger individuals suggest limited recruitment since dam closure. Whether this is enough to maintain populations is unknown. Razorback sucker young were reported from the Yampa River area in 1975 and 1976, and from a gravel pit near Grand Junction, Colorado, in 1975, but no recent juvenile records exist.
- **Predation and competition by introduced fishes:** Introduced predators such as bass, sunfish and catfish are likely contributors, although these species are not abundant in the upper basin. [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) and [[Redside Shiner]] (*Richardsonius balteatus*) are considered serious potential predators on larval chubs and young razorback suckers. In the lower Black River, Arizona, introduced bass virtually eliminated native fishes, including two sucker species, within two years.
- **Grand Canyon:** Bonytail chub are believed to have been extirpated from the Grand Canyon portion of the Colorado River, probably because of the cold tailwaters below high dams.

## Key Findings

1. **Proposed listing:** Bonytail chub should be listed as Endangered and razorback sucker as Threatened. Both are considered in serious decline throughout their range.
2. **Special Rule for razorback sucker:** Under 50 CFR 17.31(c), a special rule would allow razorback suckers to be taken in accordance with state law, under state supervision. Any violation of state law would also be a violation of the Act.
3. **Prohibitions and permits:** Standard endangered-species prohibitions (50 CFR 17.21) would apply to bonytail chub. Razorback sucker would be covered by 50 CFR 17.31, which generally mirrors endangered-species protections. Scientific or enhancement permits (50 CFR 17.22) and economic hardship permits (50 CFR 17.23) would be available.
4. **Critical habitat:** No critical habitat is proposed. The Service must still apply other Section 7 protections, and federal agencies must consult with the Secretary of the Interior on actions that may affect these species.
5. **Public comment solicited:** Comments are requested on biological or other data about threats to these fishes, the location of habitat that should or should not be designated as critical, and other relevant information. Comments were due by a date noted in the notice, and a public hearing was not scheduled. Materials were available for inspection at the Office of Endangered Species in Washington, D.C.
6. **Environmental and economic review:** An environmental assessment was prepared. The Service will determine at final rulemaking whether the action is a major federal action under Section 102(2)(C) of [[National Environmental Policy Act]]. The rule is not considered a major proposal requiring an Economic Impact Statement under Executive Order 11949 and OMB Circular A-107.
7. **State notification:** Under Section 4(b) of the Act, the Director will notify the governors of California, Nevada, Arizona, New Mexico and Utah and request their comments before final determinations.
8. **Unrelated items in the same issue:** The Federal Register also contains a HEW notice of a decision to develop regulations on research involving institutionalized mentally retarded and infirm individuals, a notice of public hearing procedures, and an unrelated Department of the Interior endangered-species notice. These items are not part of the fish proposal.
