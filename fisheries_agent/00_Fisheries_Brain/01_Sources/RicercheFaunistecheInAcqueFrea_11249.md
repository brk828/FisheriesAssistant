# Faunistic Research on Phreatic Waters of the Marche (Central Italy) and the State of Knowledge of the Italian Interstitial Fauna

#ichthyology #marine-biology #copepoda #stygofauna

*Note: the source concerns groundwater invertebrates (copepods, amphipods, isopods, gastropods, oligochaetes, mites, ostracods); no fish are reported. The fish-specific tags above are retained only as the mandated header line and should be read in this context.*

## Executive Summary

This paper by Giuseppe Lucio Pesce (Istituto di Zoologia, Università dell'Aquila) reviews the history and current state of Italian interstitial and phreatic (groundwater) fauna research and reports results of freatobiological surveys in the Marche region carried out in 1978–1979. Seventy-three stations (wells of fresh or oligohaline water) were prospected over six field missions, using Cvetkov phreatobiological nets. The fauna is dominated by crustaceans (cyclopid and harpacticoid copepods, asellid isopods, gammarid amphipods), with oligochaetes, gastropods and mites. Several taxa are new to Italy or to science, and three species (*Proasellus adriaticus*, *Peloscolex pescei*, *Acanthocyclops venustus italicus*) are provisionally regarded as endemic to the Marche. The author argues that the sampling gear is selective and that the recorded fauna is only a fraction of the true community, and proposes long-term monitoring of groundwater biocenoses as biological indicators of surface pollution.

## Background & Description

The study situates itself within the historical development of Biospeleology, beginning with Vejdovský (1882) and expanding over the last thirty years thanks to specialised collecting techniques (e.g., [[Cvetkov Phreatobiological Net]], [[Karaman-Chappuis Method]]). Italian knowledge of interstitial fauna is described as fragmentary: regions such as Piedmont, Umbria, Basilicata, Calabria and Sicily had no data at the time, and many groups lacked species-level identification, partly because Italian material was sent to foreign specialists. Earlier Italian work included [[Ruffo]] (1952–1958) in the Po Plain and Apulia, and more recent work by the L'Aquila group in the Conca Aquilana (Abruzzo) from 1972 onward.

The study area comprises two aquifer types:
- **Coastal oligohaline systems**: shallow (2.5–13.5 m), temperatures 15.5–18.0 °C, pH 6.6–7.5, salinity 0.9–2.5‰, sandy organogenous sediments rich in foraminifera.
- **Inland freshwater systems**: deeper (7.0–30.5 m), temperatures 13.2–14.9 °C, pH about 7, fine sands and silts.

Coastal systems host richer, denser faunas typical of ecotonal habitats with high external trophic input; inland systems support less diverse communities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling limitations:** Cvetkov nets penetrate poorly and select against deep-interstitial forms (e.g., arpacticoid copepods, hydracarinids, microparasellids), so the recorded fauna is considered an incomplete sample.
- **Azoic stations:** Several stations were nearly devoid of life. One station (Camerano, via Dante Alighieri) was pH 5.9 with traces of inorganic pollutants and was recorded as azoic. The author plans to test whether this reflects surface pollution.
- **Ecological groups identified:** (a) epigean eurioecious accidental visitors (stygoxenes); (b) regularly present surface and groundwater forms (stygophiles); (c) stably present interstitial forms (eustygophiles); (d) highly specialised stygobionts; (e) "thalassoid" forms confined to coastal groundwater.
- **Biogeographic elements:** cosmopolitan and widespread species; Palaearctic northern forms; Mediterranean forms; and Balkan-Dalmatian (trans-Adriatic) species, reflecting links with the Balkan coast.
- **Groundwater and surface pollution:** Earlier work by Ferrarese & Sambugar (1976) on the Adige suggested a correlation between surface and hyporheic pollution. Crustaceans and certain oligochaetes are expected to be sensitive indicators.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling effort:** six field missions, 73 stations, about 35–40 L of water filtered per station (roughly 150 cc of filtrate).
- **Overall character:** the fauna is mainly crustacean, with copepods (cyclopids and arpacticoids), asellid isopods, amphipods, ostracods, plus oligochaetes, gastropods, mites and dipteran larvae.
- **Taxonomic novelties and first records:**
  - *Speocyclops italicus* Kiefer: previously known only from Castelcivita Cave (Campania); its discovery in central Italy is biogeographically significant (11 females, 2 males examined).
  - *Eucyclops subterraneus*: a new element for the Italian fauna; the author recognises *E. s. intermedius* and *E. s. damianae*.
  - *Acanthocyclops venustus italicus* Pesce & Maggi: described as a Marche-endemic subspecies known only from phreatic waters.
  - *Parapseudoleptomesochra italica* Pesce & Petkovski: a coastal "thalassoid" harpacticoid, recorded from Marche, Abruzzo and Apulia.
  - *Peloscolex pescei* Dumnicka: new to science.
  - *Arganiella pescei* Giusti & Pezzoli: a gastropod recorded across several central Italian stations.
- **Endemism:** *Proasellus adriaticus* (a Danubian-group stygobiont whose closest Italian relative, *P. vignai*, is endemic to Lazio), *Peloscolex pescei* and *Acanthocyclops venustus italicus* are provisionally endemic to the Marche.
- **Most widespread stations:** Porto Recanati, Numana, Macerata (via Acquaviva), Tolentino and Trodica yielded the most diverse faunas; *Tropocyclops prasinus*, *Diacyclops bicuspidatus lubbocki* and *Niphargus* species were among the commonest taxa.
- **Taxonomic comments:** the author discusses the *Acanthocyclops robustus*/*vernalis*/*americanus* complex (following Kiefer 1976, 1978), the *Elaphoidella elaphoides* species group (questioning the status of *E. minos*, *E. varians*, *E. juxtaputealis*), and the *Salentinella angelieri* synonymy proposed by Karaman (1980).
- **Ecological conclusion:** groundwater communities of the Marche are not strongly isolated from those of the Central Apennines (Abruzzo, Lazio) and share affinities with Balkan-Dalmatian faunas, implying common origin.
- **Future programme:** repeated monitoring of selected wells to detect fluctuations linked to pollution, followed by testing of organisms as biological indicators for groundwater protection.
- **Conservation/monitoring statement:** the author stresses that the present study is a starting point rather than an endpoint, and that fuller data require improved collection techniques.
