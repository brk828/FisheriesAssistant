# Phase I Baseline Limnological and Fisheries Investigation of Lake Pleasant

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #moronidae #ictaluridae #cyprinidae #limnology

## Executive Summary

This final report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) documents a two-year (September 1987 to August 1989) pre-construction baseline survey of [[Lake Pleasant]], a warm monomictic reservoir northwest of Phoenix, Arizona. The study was funded under a cooperative agreement with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], which planned to enlarge the reservoir as part of the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) and committed to mitigate adverse impacts on the sportfishery based on catch per unit effort (CPUE) measured by this study. The work covered fish species composition, fish standing crop, the [[Agua Fria River]] fishery, angler creel surveys, and water quality. Seventeen fish species and two or more hybrids were recorded. Largemouth bass and white bass were the most abundant game fish, the lake was judged eutrophic by nutrient and secchi data but mesotrophic by chlorophyll a, and the report recommends annual monitoring to time a later Phase II study.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Describe existing aquatic conditions so that post-CAP changes in the sport fishery and water quality can be detected and mitigated. The study objectives were (A) fish species composition, (B) fish standing crop, (C) Agua Fria River fishery resources, (D) angler preference, pressure and CPUE, and (E) reservoir water quality.
- **Setting:** The lake was created in 1927 when Waddell Dam was built on the Agua Fria River. It became a Maricopa County park in 1960 and has received nearly one million visitors in recent years. The [[New Waddell Dam]] will increase maximum surface area from 1,497 to 4,169 ha and raise storage roughly 3.5 times. CAP water from [[Lake Havasu]] will add about 6.3 × 10^7 m³ annually.
- **Stocking history:** The AGFD stocked largemouth bass, bluegill, channel catfish, crappie, white bass and threadfin shad. These introductions created a self-sustaining warmwater fishery and the only white bass fishery in Arizona.
- **Potential impacts identified in the 1984 EIS:** increased salt loading, changes in water quality, reservoir eutrophication, effects of water-level fluctuation on spawning, and introduction of new fish species such as striped bass and blue tilapia.
- **Methods overview:** Gill netting, trap netting and electrofishing for fish populations; rotenone cove sampling for standing crop; backpack electrofishing and habitat mapping on the Agua Fria River; creel surveys; and monthly to bimonthly water quality profiling and laboratory analysis at eight stations.
- **Report context:** A transmittal memorandum dated June 1990 and the report's Key Words (fish occurrence, CPUE, bald eagles, nutrients, metals, productivity) frame the work.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Basins and habitat.** The lake was divided into an upper basin (Agua Fria River arm, Humbug Creek, Cole's Bay, Castle Creek; turbid, gradual slopes, silt substrate) and a lower basin (deep main body, steep eastern shore, gravel substrate, islands varying with level). Seventy-eight fixed shoreline sampling sites (40 lower, 38 upper) covered 50 to 85% of total shoreline depending on elevation. Habitat codes combined exposure (cove or open), slope and substrate.

**Water levels.** Surface elevation during the study ranged from 476.1 m msl (October 1987) to 482.6 m msl (March 1988). Mean storage was 56,050 ac-ft in July and August and 73,200 ac-ft in February. In 1988 summer releases were bypassed by supplying downstream needs from the CAP canal, producing an unusual summer rise.

**Thermal regime.** Surface temperature ranged from 9 °C in January to 30 °C in August. The lake stratified in May with a thermocline near 8 m and stayed stratified through October, then mixed from November to early April. During stratification the hypolimnion became anoxic (DO < 1.0 mg/L), and in July to October it was slightly acidic (pH < 7.0). Iron and manganese were much higher in the hypolimnion (Mn averaged 454 ppb versus 55 ppb in the epilimnion).

**Water chemistry.** Total alkalinity averaged 174.8 mg/L CaCO₃, classing the water as "hard," with carbonates as the major anions. Potassium and calcium were the predominant cations, with calcium and sodium nearly equal. Total dissolved solids were about 334 mg/L in year one and 326 mg/L in year two, roughly half the values reported for Lake Havasu. Copper, zinc and manganese stayed within EPA aquatic-life limits; iron exceeded a 1.0 mg/L guideline at some upper-basin stations in summer.

**Productivity.** Total Kjeldahl nitrogen averaged 0.385 mg/L and total phosphorus 0.069 mg/L (indicative of eutrophy). Secchi depth averaged 1.1 m and turbidity 15.0 NTU. Chlorophyll a ranged from 1.7 to 19.5 mg/m³ (mean 6.1), highest in the upper basin. Chlorophyll a alone classifies the lake as mesotrophic; phosphorus and secchi depth support eutrophic status.

**Agua Fria River.** The river is intermittent and was sampled over 12 km from the lake to the Ford at SE¼ Sec. 6, T7N, R2E. Discharge in March 1988 ranged from 14.7 to 18.1 L/s, below the 19-year March mean of 287.2 L/s, and was likely near the minimum needed to sustain upstream carp movement. A pair of [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*) nesting on a cliff upstream had been unsuccessful; carp and catfish from littoral areas of the lake are the eagles' main accessible prey.

## Key Findings

**Fish species composition.** Seventeen species and two or more hybrids were collected (about 22,823 fish across all gears). Rare species included one [[Gila Sucker]] and one goldfish (rotenone), and blue tilapia were also uncommon. White bass (11.6%) and largemouth bass (10.3%) were the most abundant game fish; threadfin shad (36.7%) and sunfish (33.8%) were the dominant forage fish.

**Gear performance.** Gill netting captured 4,056 fish in 237.8 net-night units (NNU), mean CPUE 19.2 fish/NNU. Trap netting took 1,191 fish in 157.6 NNU (8.8 fish/NNU), much of it from one entangled shad event. Electrofishing took 17,576 fish in 84.3 electrofishing units (EFU), mean CPUE 208.5 fish/EFU. Results matched known gear biases: gill nets over-represented white bass, trap nets favored crappie and sunfish, and electrofishing under-sampled pelagic fish.

**Temporal trends.** White bass and threadfin shad declined over the study, while largemouth bass, channel catfish and sunfish increased. Largemouth bass gill-net CPUE rose from 1.3 (year one) to 3.9 fish/NNU (year two). Crappie were significantly more abundant in the upper basin.

**Condition.** Channel catfish, crappie and white bass were in good condition. Largemouth bass were in fair but improving condition (mean K 1.26 in year one, 1.32 later, with the proportion of large fish declining). Bluegill were abundant and stunted, with poor but improving condition (mean K rose to 1.68 in year two). Carp were abundant and in poor condition (K fell from 1.32 to 1.27).

**Standing crop (rotenone).** Three coves were sampled in 1988 (2.31 ha) and three in 1989 (1.66 ha). Standing crop fell from 537.6 kg/ha in 1988 to 241.1 kg/ha in 1989 (mean 387.9 kg/ha). Threadfin shad made up 85.0% of 1988 fish numbers but only 15.1% in 1989, when sunfish rose to 79.3% (about 20,220 fish). Standing crop of largemouth bass was 62.2 kg/ha in 1988 and 24.5 kg/ha in 1989.

**Quality indices.** Largemouth bass PSD dropped from 29 to 20, indicating too few large fish. Bluegill PSD of 1 was taken as strong evidence of stunting. Shad and sunfish competition was proposed as a factor.

**Agua Fria River fishery.** In March 1988, 753 fish were collected in 19.5 EFU across eight species. Red shiners (39%) and longfin dace (32%) dominated. Runs were the predominant habitat (83.9%).

**Angler survey.** 18,969 anglers were interviewed. Mean CPUE was 0.44 fish/hr (total ratio estimate 0.39 fish/hr). Largemouth bass was the most sought species (about 32.5% of anglers targeted it alone), followed by white bass and channel catfish. An estimated 1.47 million fish were caught, with harvest of about 545,370 fish. Angling pressure was estimated at 434,615 anglers in year one and 398,738 in year two (down 8.3%), likely a response to lower water levels. Anglers rating their trip excellent caught about six times as many fish as those rating it poor; 60.6% rated their trip poor.

**Creel size trends.** Mean largemouth bass length in the creel fell from 330 mm TL to 318 mm TL, while white bass (327 to 357 mm), crappie and sunfish increased.

**Management recommendations.** Because new reservoirs typically show a period of elevated production before equilibrium, the authors recommend annual monitoring (about 75 creel days per year, plus fall fish and water quality surveys) to determine when Phase II should begin. Phase II should follow the methods documented here so that results are comparable.
