# Letter Regarding Meeting of Endangered Fish Principal Investigators for Glen Canyon Dam EIS

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ConservationPolicy

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) administrative letter coordinating a meeting of principal investigators studying endangered and native fish species in the [[Grand Canyon]] segment of the [[Colorado River]]. The correspondence was issued in the context of formal Section 7 consultation between the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and FWS regarding the **Low Fluctuating Flow Alternative** under consideration in the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] Environmental Impact Statement (EIS). The letter does not itself present primary morphological or ecological data, but it defines the research framework and data categories required to support a Biological Opinion on dam operations affecting the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and other native fish species. It represents a key procedural node in the Endangered Species Act (ESA) consultation process governing Glen Canyon Dam flow management.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The letter outlines the **ecological and habitat data categories** deemed essential by FWS for evaluating dam-flow impacts on native fish in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. These categories effectively define the ecological niche parameters under investigation:

- **Habitat use by life stage** — ontogenetic shifts in habitat occupancy (e.g., larval, juvenile, adult).
- **Availability and description of suitable habitats** — quantifying and characterizing usable habitat types.
- **Food habits by life stage** — trophic ecology across life history stages.
- **Distribution and movement** — seasonal, short-term, and daily movement patterns within the river corridor.
- **Use of tributaries** — role of tributary streams (e.g., [[Little Colorado River]]) as spawning, rearing, or refuge habitat.
- **Predation and competition** — evidence for or against biotic interactions affecting native fish, likely referencing interactions with non-native [[Rainbow Trout]] and other introduced species.
- **Response of species and habitats to flow regimes** — direct linkage to the EIS's central management question regarding dam discharge patterns.
- **Population size estimates** — spatially and seasonally resolved abundance data.
- **Limiting factors** — identification of constraints on native fish populations in the Grand Canyon ecosystem.

The overarching ecological question posed is whether **Grand Canyon conditions can be improved for the Humpback Chub, Razorback Sucker, and other native fishes**, and whether such improvement is achievable through **dam operational changes** (e.g., flow regime modification) or through **non-operational management actions** (e.g., non-native species control, habitat restoration).

## Key Findings
- The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] entered **formal Section 7 consultation** with FWS on the Low Fluctuating Flow Alternative for the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] EIS, triggering a legal requirement for "best scientific and commercial data available" under the Endangered Species Act.
- FWS convened a meeting (March 31, 1993, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Phoenix) of **endangered fish principal investigators** to compile existing research into a summary usable for the forthcoming **Biological Opinion**.
- Invited investigators and their institutions/affiliations included:
  - Dr. Paul Marsh — [[Arizona State University]], Center for Environmental Studies
  - Dr. Owen Gorman — [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], Flagstaff
  - Dr. O.B. Maughan — Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit, [[University of Arizona]]
  - Mr. Bill Persons — [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]
  - Mr. Pat Ryan — Navajo Natural Heritage Program
  - Dr. Richard Valdez — [[BIO/WEST, Inc.]]
  - Mr. Don Bay — Hualapai Wildlife Management
- The letter explicitly identifies **data gaps** as a priority: "Questions not answered by current research but that remain essential to the understanding of the humpback chub and other native fish species ecology may be included as part of the biological opinion" — indicating that as of 1993, substantial ecological uncertainty remained regarding native fish response to dam operations.
- This correspondence illustrates the institutional and interagency coordination structure (FWS, Bureau of Reclamation, Arizona Game and Fish, National Park Service, academic researchers) underlying long-term [[Glen Canyon Dam Adaptive Management Program]] research on the Colorado River ecosystem.
- No quantitative population, flow-response, or habitat data are contained in this specific document; it functions solely as a **procedural/coordinating instrument** in the ESA consultation process.
