# A Checklist of the Fishes of West Virginia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #biogeography #faunal-checklist #Cyprinidae #Percidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 paper by [[Robert F. Denoncourt]], [[Edward C. Raney]], [[Charles H. Hocutt]], and [[Jay R. Stauffer]] presents a comprehensive checklist of the fish fauna of **West Virginia**, documenting **151 known species** across **21 families**, with an additional **18 expected species** marked with an asterisk. The study synthesizes prior regional surveys (Goldsborough and Clark 1908; Addair 1944; Raney 1947; Raney and Seaman 1950; Jenkins et al. 1971) into an updated, unified checklist. The paper emphasizes the state's unique biogeographic position, straddling **seven major drainages** split between the Atlantic Slope and Ohio River systems, which has driven historic patterns of dispersal, stream piracy, and speciation since the [[Pleistocene]].

## Background & Description

As a faunal checklist rather than a morphological study, the paper does not provide detailed anatomical descriptions of individual species. However, it notes key taxonomic distinctions relevant to identification and classification:

- **[[Percina Crassa Roanoka]]** (Piedmont darter) — taxonomic treatment follows Hocutt and Hambrick (1973) and Page (1974), diverging from Bailey et al. (1970) nomenclature used for the rest of the checklist.
- Species-level differentiation is provided primarily via **binomial nomenclature and common names**, following Bailey et al. (1970) as the standard taxonomic authority.
- The genus **[[Notropis]]** (shiners) is the most speciose group documented, with **25 species** listed, reflecting high diversity within [[Cyprinidae]] in the region.
- The genus **[[Moxostoma]]** (redhorses) and **[[Percina]]** (darters) also show substantial regional diversification, including several endemic or range-restricted forms (e.g., *Moxostoma rhothoecum*, "Torrent sucker"; *Percina oxyrhyncha*, "Sharpnose darter").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

West Virginia's fish fauna occupies a biogeographically significant transition zone:

- **Seven major drainages** are documented:
  - Atlantic Slope: **[[Potomac River]]** and **[[James River]]**
  - Ohio River system: **[[Monongahela River]]**, **[[Little Kanawha River]]**, **upper and lower [[Kanawha River]]** (separated by **Kanawha Falls**), **[[Guyandotte River]]**, and **[[Big Sandy River]]**
- The **upper Kanawha system** is ichthyologically treated as synonymous with the **[[New River]]** system, a globally recognized center of endemism.
- Historic **stream piracy** and faunal transfers between drainages since the Pleistocene have promoted **isolation and speciation**, contributing to distinct regional forms (e.g., *Notropis cerasinus*, "New River shiner"; *Cottus cognatus* as an expected disjunct species).
- Habitat types span cold headwater trout streams (e.g., **[[Salvelinus Fontinalis]]**, brook trout), large lowland rivers (e.g., **[[Aplodinotus Grunniens]]**, freshwater drum; **[[Scaphirhynchus Platorynchus]]**, shovelnose sturgeon), and montane rocky streams supporting darter and sculpin diversity (**[[Etheostoma]]**, **[[Cottus]]**).
- Several species are anadromous or migratory relicts of Atlantic drainage connections (e.g., **[[Alosa Pseudoharengus]]**, alewife; **[[Alosa Chrysochloris]]**, skipjack herring).

## Key Findings

- **151 species** in **21 families** are confirmed present in West Virginia; **18 additional species** (marked with *) are expected but unconfirmed, including *Umbra limi*, *Lepisosteus platostomus*, *Nocomis biguttatus*, *Erimyzon sucetta*, *Cycleptus elongatus*, and *Cottus cognatus*.
- The family **[[Cyprinidae]]** (minnows and shiners) is by far the most diverse, dominated by the genus *Notropis*.
- The family **[[Percidae]]** (darters, perches) shows high regional endemism, with several taxa of biogeographic interest tied to the New River/upper Kanawha drainage (e.g., *Etheostoma kanawhae*, Kanawha darter; *Percina oxyrhyncha*, Sharpnose darter).
- Taxonomic treatment of ***Percina crassa roanoka*** required special citation outside the standard Bailey et al. (1970) reference, reflecting active taxonomic revision at the time (Hocutt and Hambrick 1973; Page 1974).
- The paper explicitly distinguishes **upper vs. lower Kanawha faunal associations**, separated by **Kanawha Falls**, a critical biogeographic barrier following Jenkins, Lachner, and Schwartz (1971).
- This checklist supersedes earlier partial surveys (Goldsborough and Clark 1908; Addair 1944; Raney 1947; Raney and Seaman 1950) and is positioned as a precursor to a forthcoming **diagnostic key and subspecies-level distributional study**.
- Acknowledged data contributions came from the **West Virginia State Game and Fish Commission**, **West Virginia Department of Natural Resources**, and unpublished collection data from **Dr. Frank Schwartz**.
