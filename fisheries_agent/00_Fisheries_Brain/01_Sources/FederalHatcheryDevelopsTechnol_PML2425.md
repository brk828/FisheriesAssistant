# Bonytail Chub Genetic Management at Dexter National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This feature article describes how the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] & Technology Center in [[Dexter, New Mexico]] uses genetic tools to manage the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), a critically imperiled fish with very few adults remaining in the wild. Geneticist [[Connie Keeler-Foster]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] uses DNA sequencing to identify individual fish and to pair broodstock so that offspring are more genetically diverse and better suited to survival. The hatchery's goal is a self-sustaining wild population. The article is promotional in tone and gives no population counts or quantitative results.

## Background & Description

The bonytail chub is a freshwater member of the family [[Cyprinidae]]. The article describes it as possibly one of the most endangered vertebrates in North America, though the source text is partly garbled on this point. No morphological or life-history details are given.

The [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] & Technology Center was founded in 1931, when good water supply supported its establishment. Its early work focused on game fish. Over time its mission shifted toward technology development for managing critically imperiled fishes such as the bonytail chub. It is one of 70 national fish hatcheries operated by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, and the only one dedicated to endangered fish conservation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery sits in the grasslands of southeast New Mexico, a region known for cattle ranching and crops. Dexter is a small town that serves local ranchers and farmers. The source does not describe wild habitat conditions, water chemistry, or the threats facing bonytail chub in the wild, beyond noting that very few adults remain.

## Key Findings

- **Genetic broodstock management:** The hatchery's genetics laboratory was recently equipped with a DNA sequencer that lets staff identify individual fish by their genes. Keeler-Foster compares this to fingerprinting.
- **Genetic pedigree approach:** Knowing the genetic makeup of the entire [[Broodstock]] allows staff to selectively pair males and females. Pairing genetically divergent individuals is expected to produce offspring that are more fit for survival in the wild.
- **Expected benefits:** According to Keeler-Foster, well-matched mates may produce young that are less prone to disease and more likely to reach adulthood and reproduce in the wild.
- **Conservation goal:** The intended end product is a reproducing population in the wild. The article presents hatchery work as the first step in preventing extinction, and gives no data on release outcomes.
- **Facility profile:** The Dexter hatchery is one of 70 national fish hatcheries and the only one devoted to endangered fish conservation.
- **Public access:** The hatchery's visitor center is open daily from 7:00 am to 4:00 pm. Special group tours are available by appointment at (505) 734-5910.
