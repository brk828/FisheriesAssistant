# Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Lower Colorado River – Parker Division

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This appendix describes the aquatic habitats, fishes, benthic invertebrates, higher aquatic plants and algae, pigments, and physical-chemical conditions of the [[Lower Colorado River]] in the Parker Division, the longest free-flowing segment on the lower river. The study extends from [[Headgate Rock Dam]] to the [[Palo Verde Irrigation Diversion]] and was carried out largely in 1974–1976. It supersedes an earlier interim report. The fish assemblage is dominated numerically by small planktivorous and forage species, while [[Channel Catfish]] supports the principal sport fishery. Benthic communities are dominated by the introduced clam [[Corbicula]] near the upstream dam, with a shift toward chironomid and oligochaete dominance downstream of the [[Poston Wasteway]], where agricultural inputs appear to enrich the system. Aquatic plant drift and dissolved oxygen patterns indicate high productivity and organic loading.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** The Parker Division is divided into two reaches. [[Parker Division Upper Reach]] (Parker-I) runs from Headgate Rock Dam to about river mile 540.6. About half of its shoreline is armored, leveed, or modified by jetties, and its bottoms are dominated by gravel and rubble. The downstream reach (Parker-II) runs to the Palo Verde Irrigation Diversion, has more gravel–sand bottoms, and is about 97% open river at maximum discharge.
- **Hydrology:** Upstream of the reach, water-level fluctuations exceed 2.0 m in constrained channels, particularly in early summer when irrigation demand is high. Downstream, channels broaden and fluctuations decline. The river is braided with numerous logs, sand bars, and islands, which create heterogeneous depths, velocities, and turbulence.
- **Riparian zone:** Unmodified banks support diverse woody vegetation with understories of [[Arrowweed]], seep willow, sedges, cattail, and giant reed. Some marshland has developed adjacent to islands.
- **Scope:** Sections cover fishes, benthos, higher aquatic plants and algae, pigment analyses, and physical-chemical analyses, with appendix tables for sediments, fishes, benthos, pigments, and physical-chemical data. Detailed tables are summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish assemblage and fisheries**
- Of 21 fish kinds recorded for the division (OCR-read count), 15 were taken in the 1974–1976 surveys; the species list is marked with asterisks in the source table.
- Anglers' creels contained channel catfish (principal fishery), largemouth bass, bluegill, redear sunfish, black crappie, and yellow bullhead. Striped bass were reported occasionally, along with an occasional flathead catfish.
- Carp, Corbicula, crayfish, and [[Bullfrog]] were harvested by local residents as food.

**Benthic invertebrates**
- Benthic samples contained 1 to 14 taxa each, with high abundance and biomass near Headgate Rock Dam.
- Corbicula is the dominant group by weight throughout the upper segments, reaching very high biomass in dense beds.
- Filter feeders (simuliid dipterans, hydropsychid caddisflies) peak near the dam, where particulate input is highest.
- Chironomids and oligochaetes increase in relative abundance downstream of the Poston Wasteway, associated with particulate matter and dissolved nutrients from irrigated lands.

**Higher aquatic plants and algae**
- [[Potamogeton Pectinatus]] was the most abundant submergent plant, comprising 73.4% of total wet weight in quadrats. [[Myriophyllum Exalbescens]] comprised 19.6%, [[Chara]] 5.5%, and [[Ceratophyllum Demersum]] 1.8%.
- Plants grow in largely monospecific stands. Accumulated beds trap sand and silt, forming mounds that provide substrate for further rooting.

**Drift of plant material**
- Drift at the dam increased from June to early July as plant populations grew. Possible drivers include watercraft disturbance and decomposition of upstream materials.
- Of 227 drift samples, 93 were sorted by species composition. Potamogeton dominated, and terrestrial plus emergent material made up only about 12%, much lower than in forested streams, where particulate terrestrial matter often approaches 100% of drift.

**Physical-chemical conditions**
- Dissolved oxygen (DO) below Headgate Rock Dam averaged about 1.5 mg/L lower than upstream, reflecting turbulence and structure passage, and was near saturation.
- Upstream DO peaked at 9.6 mg/L in late afternoon and declined to about 7.5 mg/L near dawn, indicating strong daytime photosynthesis and nighttime respiration.
- Water temperatures rose slightly downstream, with day–night swings up to 4.7 °C. Declining mean DO and diel cycling are attributed to increasing overall respiration.
- The Poston Wasteway was warm, low in DO, and had considerably higher conductance and PO4-P concentrations (consistently above 0.10 mg/L). Its water was traced along the east bank for at least 5.0 km before mixing.

## Key Findings

1. **Fish abundance is high but dominated by forage species.** Fine-mesh seines captured 12 fish kinds, with an estimated 11,714 individuals per ha (OCR-read value). Red shiner and threadfin shad each made up about 30% of numbers, mosquitofish about 21%, and juvenile bluegill about 6.4%.
2. **Biomass and diversity peak near the Poston Wasteway.** The reach between river miles 530.0 and 539.9 supported the greatest numbers of fishes, while the inflow of the Poston Wasteway had the highest weights and diversity (9 species, 19,130 individuals, 24.3 kg/ha). The two downstream segments had the smallest catches despite greater sampling effort.
3. **Gear catches vary by time and habitat.** Hoop nets caught seven fish species plus softshell turtles, with night sets (3.10 animals, 1.63 kg per set) much more productive than day sets (0.55 animals, 0.34 kg). Softshell turtles were also more common at night. In backwaters and drains, turtles damaged many catches.
4. **Fish catches decline downstream of the dam.** Entrapment and entanglement catches were highest in the first 7 km below Headgate Rock Dam and declined in each succeeding 16-km segment. In the downstream area, 44 gill, trammel, and hoop net sets caught only one bluegill.
5. **Benthic diversity and biomass are strongly structured.** Corbicula supplied most biomass; in one Surber sample its wet weight nearly reached 20 g, extrapolated to nearly one tonne per ha. Diversity and biomass fell sharply in the segment below the Poston Wasteway, where only four taxa were recorded in the lowermost reach.
6. **Corbicula has an invasion history.** It was first recorded as common in the Southwest in the Coachella Valley, California, in 1953, and it has since clogged irrigation works near Parker, Arizona.
7. **Aquatic plant production is high and monospecific.** Potamogeton pectinatus dominated the submergent community and its drift, and plant-driven sediment accumulation supports silt-loving invertebrates.
8. **Allochthonous inputs are small relative to forested streams.** Terrestrial and emergent material made up only about 12% of drift, which the authors contrast with values near 100% reported for forest streams.
9. **Productivity and organic loading are evident.** Strong daily DO cycling, pH patterns, and the nutrient-rich Poston Wasteway plume all indicate high primary production and respiration, and the authors attribute part of the upstream organic loading to plant agitation and decomposition.
10. **Pigment concentrations were consistently low.** Across nearly 300 pigment samples, concentrations of all components were low, with no clear day–night or upstream–downstream trends. Chlorophyll a was slightly higher on the Arizona side below the Poston Wasteway.
