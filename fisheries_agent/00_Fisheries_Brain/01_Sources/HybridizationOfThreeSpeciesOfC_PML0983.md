# Hybridization of Three Chub Species in a Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This hatchery study tested whether [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) can hybridize with [[Roundtail Chub]] (*G. robusta*) and [[Humpback Chub]] (*G. cypha*), three chub species of the [[Gila Complex]] in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Using induced spawning with hormones and heated water at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], the authors produced viable hybrid embryos and fry from both crosses. Embryos failed to develop at 12–13°C, which the authors suggest may help explain the decline of these endangered species. Natural spawning of the hybrid pairs did not occur, although the authors note that hybrids have been observed in the wild.

## Background & Description

The three chubs coexist in the Green and Colorado rivers. Their taxonomy has long been difficult. [[Deacon et al. (1979)]] listed the bonytail chub and humpback chub as endangered, and the U.S. Department of the Interior (1980) gave both federal endangered status. [[Holden and Stalnaker (1970)]] reported intergrades between roundtail and bonytail chubs, and between bonytail and humpback chubs, with bonytail–humpback intergrades the more numerous. [[Smith et al. (1979)]] argued that many specimens called intergrades were pure species. The study's stated purpose was to determine whether bonytail chub could hybridize with the other two species.

**Broodstock:**
- Five bonytail chub females, eight roundtail chub males, and five humpback chub males.
- Bonytail were collected from [[Lake Mohave]] near Cottonwood Cove (30 April–1 May 1979 and 29 April–3 May 1980). Roundtail and humpback were collected from the Colorado River near Black Rocks (5 November 1979).
- Size ranges: bonytail 457–559 mm and 850–1,300 g; roundtail 291–352 mm (weight range illegible in the scan); humpback 304–330 mm and 275–385 g.

**Facilities and Methods:**
- Incoming hatchery water (12–13°C) was heated to 20–21°C by a propane heater rated at 45,000 BTU/h, which could heat 23 L/min.
- Two recirculating concrete tanks (6 × 0.75 × 0.75 m) provided settling and aeration. Adults were held in the first tank over cobblestone, which the fish did not use.
- Eggs were incubated in three aluminum troughs: two with heated water and one with river water (12–13°C). Hatching trays were slanted at 30°.
- Maturation was induced with [[Carp Pituitary]] (bonytail × roundtail cross) or [[Oxytetracycline]] (bonytail × humpback cross). Injections were 4 mg/kg body weight, given intraperitoneally at 24-hour intervals under [[Tricaine Methanesulfonate]] anesthesia.
- Fry were moved to an outdoor recirculating raceway, fed a trout diet and natural zooplankton (mainly cladocerans).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors link the hybridization work to conservation. Spawning temperatures for these species are 18–21°C (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 1979). In the upper Colorado River, water is much colder, and the embryo mortality observed at 12–13°C suggests that low water temperature may limit egg survival and contribute to the decline of the endangered chubs. The authors also note that alterations in the upper basin may increase the potential for hybridization, which could erode genetically pure stocks.

Hybrid and intergrade individuals have been observed in the upper Colorado River basin (Holden and Stalnaker 1970), so natural hybridization appears to occur. The hatchery pairs did not spawn naturally, despite simultaneous sexual maturity and apparently suitable substrate in the brood tank.

## Key Findings

**Maturation and fecundity**
- *Bonytail × roundtail:* All roundtail males produced low-viscosity milt after two injections. One bonytail female was ripe at capture, and two more ripened about 24 hours after the first injection. Egg diameter was 1.5–2.0 mm, with 103 eggs/mL. Fecundity was 15,852 and 20,806 in the two injected females and 7,216 in one uninjected female. Eggs from the uninjected female were infertile.
- *Bonytail × humpback:* Humpback males produced low-viscosity milt after two injections. The single female stripped ripened after one injection and produced 22,660 eggs (1.5–2.0 mm, 103 eggs/mL).

**Embryonic development by temperature**
- *Bonytail × roundtail at 13°C:* Embryos formed by 96 h but died after about 110 h. Development was very slow.
- *Bonytail × roundtail at 20°C:* First cleavage at 3.6 h, vigorous movement by 80 h (OCR-uncertain), hatching from 86 to 95 h. Newly hatched fry measured 5.5–6.5 mm. The yolk sac was absorbed by 72 h, and all fry were swimming by 96 h. Hybrids reached 12–13 mm total length in 16 days.
- *Bonytail × humpback at 12°C:* Eggs failed to hatch. Embryos formed by 96 h but all died after about 110 h.
- *Bonytail × humpback at 21°C:* First cleavage at 4.8 h, vigorous movement at 72 h, and hatching from about 101 h to about 172 h. Newly hatched fry measured 5.5–6.5 mm. The yolk sac was absorbed by 96 h, and fry were swimming by 120 h at 6.5–7.5 mm. Hybrids reached 13–14 mm in 15 days.

**Hatchery transfer and survival**
- A total of 12,000 bonytail × roundtail and 8,000 bonytail × humpback hybrids were transferred to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in Dexter, New Mexico.
- Seven months after hatching, 500 to 1,000 hybrids from each cross had survived.

**Conclusions and implications**
- Bonytail chub can hybridize with both roundtail and humpback chubs under hatchery conditions, and viable hybrids can be reared.
- Embryos at 12–13°C died, supporting the hypothesis that cold upper-basin water limits reproduction of these endangered species.
- Natural spawning of the hybrid pairs did not occur, though hybrids have been observed in the wild.
- Progeny will be used to study taxonomy. Parental fish are held at Willow Beach for species validation, and a preserved developmental series is planned.
- The spawning techniques may also be applied to culture genetically pure bonytail, humpback, and roundtail chubs.
