## Executive Summary
This historical report documents the first known introduction of **Kamloops Rainbow Trout** (*[[Salmo Gairdneri Kamloops]]*), a subspecies of [[Rainbow Trout]], into California waters. On June 17, 1950, 1,000 juvenile fish were released into six tributary streams feeding [[Shasta Lake]], Shasta County. The initiative was a collaborative effort between local sportsmen from Redding, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and the [[California Division of Fish and Game]]. The primary motivation was the belief that this subspecies exhibits superior growth rates compared to native rainbow trout populations, potentially offering a better sport fishing resource.

## Background & Description
- **Subspecies Classification:** *Salmo gairdneri kamloops*, distinguished taxonomically from the standard native rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdneri*).
- **Age at Release:** 11 months old.
- **Size at Release:** Averaged 12 fish per pound (small juvenile/fingerling stage).
- **Identifying Marks:** All stocked fish were artificially marked via fin clipping—specifically, removal of the **left ventral fin** and the **adipose fin**. This served as the sole conclusive method to distinguish introduced Kamloops from native rainbow trout populations in the wild.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Origin of Stock:** 2,500 eggs were imported from Idaho.
- **Rearing Facility:** Eggs were hatched and reared at the **Coleman Hatchery** (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service).
- **Release Strategy:** Fish were distributed across six tributary streams directly above their confluence with [[Shasta Lake]], based on the hypothesis that they would migrate downstream into the lake environment post-acclimation.
- **Stocked Locations & Distribution:**
  | Location | Number of Fish |
  |---|---|
  | Squaw Creek | 100 |
  | McCloud River | 200 |
  | Sacramento River | 200 |
  | Sugarloaf Creek | 50 |
  | Middle Salt Creek | 50 |
  | Big Backbone Creek | 100 |
  | Shasta Lake at Bridge Bay | 100 |
  | Shasta Lake at Dam | 100 |
  | **Total** | **1,000** |
- **Habitat Rationale:** Since optimal tributary conditions for the subspecies were unknown, fish were deliberately scattered across multiple stream types to test suitability and maximize potential establishment success.
- **Interaction with Native Species:** Introduced to coexist with native rainbow trout populations within the same watershed, setting up an implicit comparative growth study.

## Key Findings
1. **First Introduction:** This event marks the inaugural documented stocking of Kamloops Rainbow Trout in California state waters.
2. **Growth Hypothesis:** The core scientific/management premise was that Kamloops trout grow faster than native rainbow trout, making them potentially superior for sport fisheries.
3. **Spawning Uncertainty:** Researchers acknowledged that due to the small size and young age of the fish at release, establishing a self-sustaining spawning run was uncertain, even under favorable survival conditions.
4. **Citizen Science Component:** The success of the program relied heavily on public engagement—local anglers were enlisted to identify and report marked fish (missing left ventral/adipose fins) to Division of Fish and Game offices.
5. **Contingent Future Stocking:** Larger-scale future introductions were explicitly contingent on demonstrable evidence that Kamloops trout exhibited superior growth rates compared to native counterparts, based on data gathered from recaptured marked fish.
6. **Collaborative Model:** This introduction exemplifies early-mid 20th century collaborative fisheries management between local sportsmen's groups, federal agencies ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]), and state agencies ([[California Division of Fish and Game]]).
