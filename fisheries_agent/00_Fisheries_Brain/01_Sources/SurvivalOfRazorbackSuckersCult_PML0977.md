# Survival of Razorback Suckers Cultured in Earthen Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #salmonidae

## Background & Description

### Razorback Sucker

The razorback sucker is one of the larger catostomids in western North America. It is reported to reach total lengths near 3.3 ft and weights of 11–13 lb (Minckley 1973). It is considered endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], where it has declined over the past century. Deacon et al. (1979) listed it as threatened in Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, Utah, and Wyoming, but it was not listed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Johnson and Rinne 1982). See [[Endangered Species Act]].

A propagation program began in 1974 at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], Arizona, to develop spawning and rearing techniques (Toney 1974). It moved to Dexter National Fish Hatchery in 1981. Reproductive biology ([[Inslee 1982]]; Hamman 1985; Marsh 1985) and early life history (Minckley and Gustafson 1983) had been studied, but the author notes little documented information on survival under culture from fry to fingerling size. This study addresses that gap.

### Atlantic Salmon Alevins

For teleost larvae, vision is the main sense used in prey detection (Rosenthal 1969; Protasov 1970; Blaxter 1980). Salmonid alevins are visual planktivores, so their feeding should respond to the color of prey and of the background against which prey are seen. High early mortality in hatchery-reared larvae, including Atlantic salmon, has been linked to inadequate or inefficient feeding at the onset of exogenous feeding (May 1974; Hunter 1980; Li and Mathias 1982; Lemm and Hendrix 1981; Lemm 1983). The authors hypothesize that improving feeding efficiency through prey and background color manipulation could reduce this mortality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Razorback Sucker Pond Conditions

- **Ponds:** Earthen ponds of 0.34–1.08 acres, 3–4 ft average depth.
- **Water temperature:** 47–71°F (fry, 69–78 d); 61–75°F (subfingerlings, 51–70 d); 67–76°F (fingerlings, 50–55 d).
- **Fertilization:** Initial [[Pond Fertilization]] with alfalfa pellets (300 lb/acre) and superphosphate (50 lb/acre) about 7 d before fry stocking, followed by weekly alfalfa pellet applications (100 lb/acre) in most ponds. Fry were also fed a commercial trout starter diet as a supplement to natural [[Plankton]]. Feeding rate was not calculated.
- **Handling:** No significant handling mortality was observed after transfers.

### Atlantic Salmon Alevin Holding System

- **Fish:** Alevins 8 weeks posthatch, 25 ± 2 mm total length, obtained in May 1983 from the North American Salmon Research Center (St. Andrews) and the Florenceville Federal Fish Hatchery.
- **Tanks:** Identical 91-L holding tanks under natural lighting at the [[Huntsman Marine Laboratory]].
- **Water:** Running fresh water from the [[Hypolimnion]] of a nearby lake, filtered through 64-µm mesh, held at about 12°C.
- **Diet:** Live [[Zooplankton]] twice daily at about 200 prey/L. Feeding stopped 18 h before experiments, when fish were moved to tanks with filtered water only.
- **Background manipulation:** Aquaria were enclosed on all sides with red, blue, or aqua-green cloth, which also blocked external movement that could startle the fish.

## Key Findings

### Razorback Sucker Culture (Hamman, 1985 season)

- **Fry:** 276,200 swim-up fry were stocked in February–March 1985 into four ponds at 101,000–109,000/acre. Stocked size was 71,000–94,000/lb. Survival was 87.8–98.6%. Rearing lasted 69–78 d.
- **Subfingerlings:** 150,051 fish were stocked into three ponds at 51,000–58,000/acre, sized 549–724/lb. Survival was 92.7–95.8%. Rearing lasted 51–70 d. The pond that received one alfalfa pellet treatment before stocking had the highest survival (95.8%) but also the highest food conversion (2.67) of the three ponds.
- **Fingerlings:** 52,930 fish were stocked into two ponds at 26,000–27,000/acre, sized 158/lb. Total survival was 96.2%. Food conversion was 2.13 and 2.64. Rearing lasted 50–55 d.
- **Food conversion:** Calculated as weight of food fed divided by weight of fish gained.
- **Historical trend:** Fry-to-fingerling survival at Dexter was 10.8% (1981), 14.1% (1982), 35.7% (1983), and 23.6% (1984). Total 1985 survival was 91.8%. The author attributes the improvement to better fertilization and stocking rates.
- **Conservation implication:** Hatchery propagation may be important for restocking former habitats and supplementing native populations. Knowledge gained on reproduction and early development may help ensure the species' survival.

### Atlantic Salmon Alevin Feeding (Browman and Marcotte, 1987)

- **Hypothesis:** Prey color and background color affect feeding by Atlantic salmon alevins.
- **Abstract finding:** Feeding preference was affected by combinations of background and prey color. The authors suggest that carefully chosen color combinations could increase feeding efficiency, growth, and survival of cultured Atlantic salmon.
