# Aravaipa Creek Fish Sampling and Fish Barrier Apron Hydraulics (2001)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of 2001 email correspondence about fish sampling along [[Aravaipa Creek]] and the hydraulic performance of the [[Fish Barrier]] aprons built there. It contains three items: a May 2001 report from [[Robert Clarkson]] on current velocity and depth measurements on the barrier aprons, an April 2001 sampling report from [[Peter Unmack]] and [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) covering April 14–15, 2001, and a November 2001 results summary from the same sampling program. The sampling counted native and non-native fishes at about 15 sites, including large numbers of juvenile suckers. Juvenile sucker identifications were not verified at the time, so the species breakdown is provisional.

## Background & Description

The correspondence concerns a fish barrier project on Aravaipa Creek, Arizona. The barriers were built with [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] involvement, and the lower site and barriers sit on [[San Carlos Apache Tribe]] land. Access requires tribal permission, and the area was fenced and keyed after completion. The barrier system includes an [[Irrigation Diversion]] pipe on the lower barrier, which the contractor was sealing to prevent seepage.

The genera reported are:

- Cyprinidae (minnows and dace): [[Agosia]], [[Gila]], [[Meda]], [[Tiaroga]], [[Rhinichthys]], [[Cyprinella]]
- Catostomidae (suckers): [[Catostomus]], [[Pantosteus]], plus unidentified juvenile suckers and one hybrid sucker
- Other fishes: [[Ameiurus]] (catfish) and [[Lepomis]] (sunfish)

Sites were listed from upstream to downstream, including the TNC house (upper end), Chimney Rock, Turkey Creek confluence, Parsons/Deer Canyon confluence, Horse Camp Canyon confluence, Painted Cave Canyon confluence, House Canyon confluence, Virgus Canyon region, Woods Ranch, Withers/Barrossirs Ranch, and just above the fish barriers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hydraulic measurements describe the flow conditions that the fish barrier aprons create. The aprons are designed to let fish pass the barriers. The measurements were taken on May 3, 2001 near base flow with a [[Marsh-McBirney Model 201O Electromagnetic Flow Meter]].

**Lower barrier apron:**
- Depth was uniformly 6 cm or less, except behind the waterfall, where depths were greater but were not measured.
- Current velocities were 0.63–0.71 m/s across the lower third, 0.45–0.61 m/s across the middle third, and 0.64–0.93 m/s across the upper third.
- Maximum velocity just below the waterfall averaged 1.2 m/s.
- The [[Whitewater Zone]] extended about 3 m below the waterfall. The apron is about 10 m long.

**Upper barrier apron:**
- Backwater from the lower barrier affected depths. The spacing between barriers was constrained by property and ownership issues.
- A V-shaped eddy covered about one quarter of the apron on the left bank (looking downstream). Depths there were 15–18 cm, with negative velocities of −0.05 to −0.1 m/s.
- Outside the eddy, the lower third had depths of 24–27 cm and velocities of about 0.26 m/s. The middle third had a depth of 21 cm and velocities of 0.38–0.42 m/s.
- The whitewater zone on the upper apron covered roughly the upper third. Depths were 9–12 cm at its lower edge and about 6 cm nearer the waterfall. Velocities were 0.14–0.53 m/s near the downstream edge and 0.30–0.95 m/s near the upper edge.
- Maximum velocity below the waterfall was 1.7–2.0 m/s. Velocity on the barrier crest was 0.75–0.95 m/s.

The author noted that steeper aprons, with shallower water and faster currents, would have been possible without the inter-barrier spacing constraint. Future barrier sites are planned as single barriers, so the spacing issue should not recur.

## Key Findings

**Sampling results (April 14–15, 2001):**
- Juvenile suckers were abundant, with counts of 25 to 700 per site. Examples include 500 at Parsons Canyon, 500 at Woods Ranch, 450 just above the barriers, 700 at Virgus Canyon, and 313 at Chimney Rock.
- Four observers independently estimated that about 95.8% or more of the juvenile suckers were [[Pantosteus]]. Juvenile suckers were not critically examined for identification at the time, which the author acknowledged as an error. These identifications are therefore unverified.
- [[Tiaroga]] was the most numerous adult cyprinid at the upper sites. The TNC house site recorded 146 and Turkey Creek recorded 104.
- Most [[Agosia]], [[Meda]], and [[Tiaroga]] were either very large or very small, and one year class appeared to be missing.
- Only juvenile suckers were caught between and just below the barriers.

**November 2001 results:**
- Withers Ranch recorded 225 [[Agosia]], the highest single-species count in the summary.
- Woods Ranch recorded 93 [[Catostomus]] and 71 [[Pantosteus]].
- Just above the barriers, 75 [[Cyprinella]] were recorded, along with 41.7 (as printed, possibly garbled) [[Agosia]].
- Horse Camp Canyon recorded 196 [[Agosia]] and 56 [[Gila]] (as printed).
- Non-native or other fishes, including [[Lepomis]] and [[Ameiurus]], were recorded in small numbers at a few sites.

**Barrier hydraulics (May 2001):**
- The lower barrier apron appears to be functioning as designed. The upper portion of the upper apron also functions well.
- Velocities were high below both waterfalls, reaching up to 2.0 m/s.
- The eddy on the upper apron reduced effective flow area and produced backwater-related depth variation.

**Administrative and access notes:**
- Access to the lower site and barriers requires permission from the San Carlos tribe.
- Reclamation cannot give keys to other parties under its lease, so ASU needed separate permission from the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) or a key obtained for each monitoring visit.
- Irrigation diversion pipes on the lower barrier were being sealed to stop seepage.
