# Description of Two New Small African Cichlids (*Nanochromis robertsi* and *Nanochromis cavalliensis*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

Thys van den Audenaerde and Loiselle describe two new small, elongate, fluvial cichlids of the genus [[Nanochromis]] from West Africa. [[Nanochromis Robertsi]] is known from headwater streams of the Birim River (Prah Basin) in west-central Ghana, and [[Nanochromis Cavalliensis]] is known from the Cavally River in Liberia/Ivory Coast. Both species share an elongate body, two symmetric caudal streamers in adult males, and a reduced number of caudal peduncle scale rows, but are separated by body proportions, interorbital width, dorsal spine count and dentition. The paper also documents the population structure, reproduction, habitat and diet of *N. robertsi*.

## Background & Description

The first species was first noticed from a photograph of an unidentified Ghanaian cichlid sent by collector Dr. Tyson Roberts. It had been provisionally labelled *Pelmatochromis* sp. nov. in an earlier systematic paper, and renewed field work and possible aquarium trade interest prompted a formal description. The second species was recognised during a survey of Liberian and western Ivory Coast fishes, where specimens first identified as juvenile [[Chromidotilapia Guntheri]] were found, on closer examination, to differ from that species.

**Nanochromis robertsi sp. nov.**
- Holotype: male, 107 mm total length (TL), 76.5 mm standard length (SL), collected 19 January 1963 near Asiakwa, Ghana (Stanford University, SU-63143).
- Paratypes: one female (79.5 mm TL) and two males (56.0 and 62.5 mm TL).
- Diagnostic features: all buccal teeth conical; hanging pad on roof of pharynx present; only 13 scale rows around the caudal peduncle; pelvic outer soft ray longest; two symmetric caudal streamers in adult males.
- Colour: males show golden-beige flanks, a conspicuous dark, gold-edged opercular spot and a dark-red and orange caudal fin with a black lower margin; females are brown with a magenta ventral blotch.
- Sexual dimorphism: males develop bilobed caudal fin lobes from about 60 mm TL, growing into long streamers.

**Nanochromis cavalliensis sp. nov.**
- Holotype: female, 86.5 mm TL, 66.5 mm SL, collected 20 November 1969 at Sahibly, Ivory Coast (Tervuren Museum, reg. no. 168574).
- Paratypes: two females and two males from the Cavally River near Toulepleu (Paris Museum, reg. no. 1963-471).
- Diagnostic features relative to *N. robertsi*: more elongate body (29.3–31.6% SL versus 34.5–35.2%), narrower interorbital width (17.6–19.0% head length versus 24.4–27.8%), and XVI dorsal spines versus XV.
- Colour (preserved): greyish to beige body, black opercular spot, midlateral line and five or six dark vertical bars.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Nanochromis robertsi* was collected from a small lateral tributary of the Birim River about 5 km west of Kibi, Ghana. The stream ran down a hillside with a slope of about 30°, consisting of shallow pools linked by riffles whose flow depended on rainfall. The bed was bedrock with organic detritus and loose rocks; neither algae nor vascular aquatic plants were recorded, and banks were shaded by broadleaved herbs up to one metre tall in a degraded cocoa grove.

Water temperature ranged from 22 °C (August, rainy season) to 25 °C (April, dry season), with a constant pH of 6.0. The fauna was depauperate: [[Barbus Walkeri]], [[Epiplatys Chaperi]], [[Amphilius Atesuensis]] and occasional [[Barbus Ablabes]] were present, along with insect larvae (chironomids most conspicuous), a freshwater prawn of the genus [[Macrobrachium]] and a small freshwater crab. *A. atesuensis* and an unidentified anuran larva occurred only in riffles, whereas the other fishes preferred the quiet pools.

Diet was assessed from twelve guts containing mud, quartzite granules, diatom shell cases, insect larva fragments and organic matter. The long intestine and short, broadly tuberculate gill rakers indicate a detritivorous feeding pattern.

## Key Findings

- **Two new species are described**: *N. robertsi* (Ghana, Birim River headwaters) and *N. cavalliensis* (Cavally River, Liberia/Ivory Coast). Both are placed in [[Nanochromis]] because of their reduced caudal scale-row count, although the authors note the genus is heterogeneous (type species *N. nudiceps* differs strongly).
- **Population structure**: in a sample of twenty specimens from April 1970, the male-to-female ratio was 1:2.3, and one short stretch of stream held a single large male, many adult females, several young males and numerous juveniles of 15–30 mm SL. The authors suggest a polygynous pattern with female-dominated brood care, consistent with captive-bred members of the genus.
- **Early sexual maturity**: colour dimorphism was recognisable at small sizes. Males of 14.0–24.0 mm SL and females of 19.0–24.0 mm SL could be distinguished. The smallest female with ovarian development was 32.0 mm SL and the smallest male with testicular development 39.5 mm SL.
- **Fecundity**: the single ripe female (57.5 mm SL) had 26 and 22 ova in her two ovaries, each ovum pale yellow and measuring 2.0 × 1.5 mm. Egg deposition is presumed to occur in enclosed spaces, given the species' preference for caves and rocky overhangs.
- **Restricted range**: *N. robertsi* is probably confined to the headwater streams of the Offin-Prah-Birim drainage in west-central Ghana. *N. cavalliensis* is known only from its type locality and is probably endemic to the middle and lower Cavally River.
- **Behaviour**: aquarium individuals defended fixed shelters aggressively, mainly against conspecifics but also against young tilapiines and small prawns.
- **Morphometric and meristic comparison** (Table 1): the two species differ in dorsal spine count, interorbital width, body depth ratios, scale rows around the caudal peduncle (13 versus 12 to 14 in the comparison) and pharyngeal tooth morphology (*N. cavalliensis* has stronger, often bicuspid median pharyngeal teeth).
- **Taxonomic status**: the authors consider the differences sufficient for specific separation, while noting that intermediate populations could alter this view.
