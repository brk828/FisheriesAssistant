# Species stocking history in Texas, data from various years between 1954-1987

## Executive Summary

This document is a compiled administrative record from the **Texas Parks & Wildlife Department (TPWD)**, dated January 26, 1987, cataloging historical fish stocking events across Texas reservoirs, rivers, and coastal bays from **1954 to 1986**. Unlike a typical single-species research paper, this source is a **multi-species stocking ledger**, detailing water bodies, stocking years, and numbers of fish released for dozens of species — spanning freshwater game fish (e.g., [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]]) to marine/estuarine transplants stocked experimentally into power-plant reservoirs and coastal bay systems (e.g., [[Red Drum]], [[Spotted Seatrout]], [[Black Drum]], [[Orangemouth Corvina]], [[Nile Perch]], [[Peacock Bass]], [[Snook]]). The document also notes several **incidental, non-deliberate species introductions** (Tarpon, Naked Goby, Atlantic Needlefish, Atlantic Croaker) that resulted from stocking operations targeting other species, offering insight into unintentional faunal transfer via hatchery and stocking logistics.

## Background & Description

The stocking history spans a wide taxonomic range, reflecting TPWD's efforts to:
1. **Establish sport fisheries** in freshwater reservoirs (e.g., [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Walleye]], [[Muskellunge]]).
2. **Enhance coastal/estuarine fisheries** through large-scale releases of marine species such as [[Red Drum]] and [[Spotted Seatrout]] into bays (Matagorda Bay, San Antonio Bay, Nueces Bay, Espiritu Santo Bay, Christmas Bay, Lower/Upper Laguna Madre).
3. **Experiment with novel or exotic species** for biocontrol, forage, or sport purposes, including tropical/exotic taxa like [[Nile Perch]] and [[Peacock Bass]] (*Cichla ocellaris*), and hybrid crosses such as [[Black X Red Drum Hybrid]] and [[Spotted Seatrout X Orangemouth Corvina Hybrid]].
4. **Utilize heated power-plant reservoirs** (e.g., Braunig, Calaveras, Coleto Creek) as thermal refugia for warm-water and marine species trials, including a notable **Tarpon** cold-tolerance release.

Species entries in the table of contents include over 90 numbered codes, ranging from established sport fish (Largemouth Bass, Bluegill, Crappie) to little-known experimental stockings (e.g., Redbreast Sunfish, Golden Shiners, Mississippi Silversides, Chain Pickerel, Fathead Minnow).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range**: Records span freshwater reservoirs (Lake Braunig, Calaveras, Coleto Creek, Fairfield, Trinity/Trading House Creek), estuarine bays (Matagorda Bay, San Antonio Bay, Nueces Bay, Aransas Bay, Christmas Bay), and river systems (Pecos River, Red Bluff, White River, Brazos River).
- **Salinity gradient stocking**: Many marine species (Red Drum, Spotted Seatrout, Orangemouth Corvina, Snook) were stocked directly into **saltwater bay systems**, while others (e.g., Nile Perch, Peacock Bass) were placed into **freshwater or heated inland reservoirs**, some clearly as aquaculture/tolerance trials rather than sustained wild population establishment.
- **Power-plant thermal reservoirs** (Braunig, Calaveras, Coleto Creek) served as artificial warm-water habitats permitting survival of tropical and subtropical species (Peacock Bass, Tarpon, Nile Perch) outside their natural thermal range.
- **Incidental species transfer**: Several non-target species became established or were incidentally observed due to shared stocking infrastructure:
  - **Naked Goby** (*Gobiosoma bosc*) likely entered Calaveras Reservoir via saltwater rearing ponds used for Red Drum/Corvina stocking at the Perry Bass Marine Station.
  - **Atlantic Needlefish** became established in Trading House Creek Reservoir without deliberate stocking.
  - **Atlantic Croaker** specimen recovered from Alcoa Reservoir, likely introduced during Southern Flounder stocking operations.

## Key Findings

### [[Red Drum]] (*Sciaenops ocellatus*)
- Longest and most extensive stocking record in the dataset, spanning **1954–1986**.
- Early stockings (1954–1966) were modest (tens to low thousands) in freshwater reservoirs like Kemp (Baylor Co.) and Pecos River.
- Massive escalation by the 1970s–1980s: **1975 total ≈ 12.3 million** fish stocked (dominated by White River releases of approximately 1,500,000 and 1,000,000+).
- **1983 total ≈ 8.6 million**; **1984 total ≈ 8.98 million**; **1985 total ≈ 9.99 million**; **1986 total ≈ 9.25 million** — reflecting a shift toward large-scale bay stocking (Espiritu Santo Bay, San Antonio Bay, Christmas Bay, West Bay, Lower Laguna Madre).
- Primary release sites in later years were **saltwater bays**, indicating a strategic shift toward direct estuarine stock enhancement.

### [[Black Drum]] (*Pogonias cromis*)
- Stocked in 1976 across multiple freshwater sites (Long/Walter E, Lake Creek, Red Bluff, Experimental) totaling **119,674** fish.
- 1977: Braunig (Victor) reservoir received **80,245**.
- 1984 saltwater stockings into Matagorda Bay (4,249) and San Antonio Bay (31,000) bring the overall species total to **235,164**.

### [[Black X Red Drum Hybrid]] ("Bream Drum")
- Stocked in 1983 (Braunig: 5,995; Lake Creek: 80,809 → year total 92,795) and 1984 (Braunig: 50,351).
- **Species total: 143,146**.

### [[Orangemouth Corvina]] (*Cynoscion xanthulus*)
- Stocked at Braunig (1985: 3,150) and Calaveras (1986: 166,825).
- **Species total: 169,975**.

### [[Spotted Seatrout X Orangemouth Corvina Hybrid]]
- Experimental hybrid stocked at Calaveras Reservoir across 1984–1986 (2,518 + 24,589 + 1,550).
- **Species total: 28,657**.

### [[Spotted Seatrout]] (*Cynoscion nebulosus*)
- Early trial releases (1974–1975) into Long (Walter E) totaled small numbers (20, then 6,072).
- Major bay stockings began 1983 (Matagorda Bay: 50,000) and expanded through 1986 (Turtle Bay: 232,000; Matagorda Bay: 9,000).
- **Species total: 1,373,554** across all years.

### [[Nile Perch]] (*Lates niloticus*)
- Experimental stockings at Braunig (1978: 88; 1979: 14), Coleto Creek Lake (1981: 6,819), and Fairfield (1983: 1,310).
- Note in source: *L. angustifrons* and *L. niloticus* were used interchangeably/confused in some records — indicates taxonomic uncertainty in original stocking documentation.
- **Species total: ~65,331**.

### [[Peacock Bass]] (*Cichla ocellaris*)
- Stocked in Bastrop Reservoir (1973: 519; 1979: 3,234), Coleto Creek Lake (1980: 414), Johnson Creek/Wilke (1981: 4,646), Trading House Creek (1982: 1,600), and Alcoa Reservoir (1983: 2,978; 1984: 878).
- Represents an experimental tropical cichlid introduction into Texas power-plant thermal reservoirs.

### [[Snook]] (*Centropomus undecimalis*)
- Single recorded stocking: Port Bay (Aransas), saltwater, 1985 — **333** individuals.

### [[Southern Flounder]] (*Paralichthys lethostigma*)
- Small-scale stockings across Long (Walter E) 1974 (7), Town Lake 1975 (2,500 + 319), Alcoa 1977 (4,500), and Eugene City Park Lake 1976 (170).
- **Species total: 7,490** — noted as likely also source of incidental Atlantic Croaker introduction (see below).

### [[White Perch]] (*Morone americana*)
- Single record: Town Lake, 1975 — **250** individuals stocked.

### [[Golden Croaker]] ("Bay Croaker")
- Single stocking: Town Lake, 1975 — **500** individuals.

### Incidentally Introduced / Non-Target Species

- **[[Tarpon]]** (*Megalops atlanticus*): 34 individuals released into Victor Braunig Reservoir (Dec 1984–Mar 1985) as survivors/controls from freshwater cold-tolerance laboratory trials. Released into a heated power-plant reservoir rather than returned to the coast due to cool seasonal ocean temperatures. One angler-caught specimen (Apr 1986) measured 37 inches (10 lb 12 oz), confirming post-release growth and survival. This was explicitly a **one-time experimental release**, not a stocking program.
- **[[Naked Goby]]** (*Gobiosoma bosc*): Collected during 1985 field sampling in Calaveras Reservoir; presumed incidental transfer via saltwater rearing ponds used for Red Drum/Corvina culture at the Perry Bass Marine Station.
- **[[Atlantic Needlefish]]** (*Strongylura marina*): Became established in Trading House Creek Reservoir without deliberate TPWD stocking.
- **[[Atlantic Croaker]]** (*Micropogonias undulatus*): Single specimen caught by an angler in Alcoa Reservoir, likely introduced during Southern Flounder stocking operations.

## Data Limitations
- The source is a heavily OCR-degraded scan; numeric stocking totals for some entries show internal inconsistencies likely attributable to OCR misreads rather than original reporting errors.
- Water body codes and names are inconsistently rendered in the source but have been standardized here based on repeated cross-references (Braunig/Victor Braunig Reservoir, Coleto Creek Lake).
- Some species codes in the Table of Contents were not detailed further in the extracted text and are omitted from Key Findings.
