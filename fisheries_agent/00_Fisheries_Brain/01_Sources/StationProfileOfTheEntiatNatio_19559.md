# Station Profile: Entiat National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #FisheriesManagement #Aquaculture

## Executive Summary

The Entiat National Fish Hatchery (Region One, USFWS) is a mitigation and conservation hatchery established in 1938 under the [[Mitchell Act]] to compensate for spawning habitat lost due to the construction of [[Grand Coulee Dam]]. Its core operational mandate is the annual release of 800,000 [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] smolts, supporting statutory obligations under the Pacific Northwest Power Planning and Conservation Act, the Salmon and Steelhead Conservation Act, the Pacific Salmon Treaty Act of 1985, and associated Indian Treaty rights. During FY1993, the hatchery recorded its highest adult return in recent years (730 fish), achieved strong egg-to-eye survival (97.8%), and implemented disease-mitigation protocols including [[Erythromycin]] injections and formalin baths. However, chronic groundwater shortages and river-borne pathogens (notably [[Myxobolus]] spp. and [[Bacterial Kidney Disease]]) have forced a 50% reduction in smolt production capacity, highlighting a persistent tension between water quality/quantity constraints and production targets.

## Background & Description

While this station profile is administrative/operational rather than morphometric, it contains several biologically relevant reproductive and developmental metrics for the hatchery's [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] broodstock:

- **Adult run composition (FY1993):** 407 females, 323 males entered the adult holding pond (total return = 730 adults) — the highest return in recent years.
- **Spawning sex ratio:** 299 males : 299 females spawned (near 1:1 ratio); 90 excess females and 5 excess males were culled.
- **Fecundity:** 4,191 green eggs per female (FY1993), up slightly from 4,133 eggs/female the prior fiscal year.
- **Total egg take:** 1,119,000 eggs; 375,000 were destroyed post-eye-up to align inventory with production targets and manage disease-positive lots.
- **Egg eye-up survival:** 97.8% — described as consistently high.
- **Prespawn mortality:** Low — 2.0% dead-in-pond, 1.3% jump-out mortality, despite handling stress from repeated antibiotic injections.

These metrics reflect the physiological condition and reproductive performance of the captive broodstock population rather than external morphology, consistent with the hatchery-management focus of the source document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Entiat NFH occupies a critical node in the anadromous life cycle of [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] within the Columbia River Basin, specifically the [[Entiat River]] tributary system near its confluence-adjacent reach to the Columbia mainstem, downstream of [[Grand Coulee Dam]].

Key ecological interactions and constraints identified:

- **Anadromy and Dam Mitigation:** The hatchery exists specifically to offset lost natural spawning habitat caused by the impassable Grand Coulee Dam, functioning as an artificial substitute for historical upstream spawning grounds.
- **Water Source Duality:** The hatchery relies on both groundwater (wells) and surface water from the Entiat River. Groundwater is strongly preferred for fish health, but is insufficient in supply.
- **Parasite Seasonality:** Entiat River surface water is compromised seasonally by *[[Myxobolus]]* spp., an internal parasite colonizing fish brain stems, with peak infectivity from early spring through summer — directly limiting the usable water window for rearing.
- **Thermal Stress:** Elevated summer water temperatures in the Entiat River exacerbate susceptibility to bacterial and parasitic infection, compounding the parasite-driven water-use restrictions.
- **Wild-Hatchery Interaction / Disease Vector Risk:** Free-ranging wild spring/summer Chinook, sockeye, and steelhead migrating past the hatchery en route to upstream spawning grounds pose a horizontal disease transmission risk, particularly for [[Bacterial Kidney Disease]] (BKD), threatening hatchery stock health.
- **Broodstock Disease Management:** BKD-positive females (32 individuals, high/moderate/low detection) had their egg lots culled entirely, reflecting active genetic/disease-load management within the captive population.
- **Community/Recreational Interface:** Brock Pond serves as a public engagement site (National Fishing Week event), linking the hatchery's ecological role to local recreational fisheries education, drawing over 200 children (majority ages 4–7).

## Key Findings

- **Record Return:** FY1993 saw 730 adult Chinook return to the hatchery rack (407 females, 323 males) — the highest return in recent years, opening opportunities for expanded genetic diversity in egg-take management.
- **Disease Prevention Protocols:** Dual [[Erythromycin]] injections (June 8 and July 22) were administered to most females, with 50 females retained as an uninjected experimental control group to evaluate single- vs. double- vs. no-injection efficacy.
- **Formalin Treatment Efficacy:** Thrice-weekly flow-through formalin treatments (1:6,000 dilution), initiated three days after first adult arrival and continued through spawning, were associated with very low prespawn mortality (2.0% dead-in-pond, 1.3% jump-out).
- **Egg Surplus Management:** Of 1,119,000 total eggs taken, 375,000 were deliberately destroyed post-eye-up (after BKD-positive lot removal and proportional sub-lot reduction) to match production goals while minimizing genetic bottlenecking — a notable applied population-genetics management technique.
- **Production Capacity Crisis:** Groundwater shortages have forced a 50% cut in smolt output — from the mandated 800,000 to 400,000 smolts (plus 400,000 zero-age fingerlings) — despite groundwater-reared fish showing markedly better health outcomes than river-water-reared fish.
- **Environmental Water Quality Constraints:** *[[Myxobolus]]* spp. contamination and elevated summer temperatures render Entiat River water seasonally unusable, structurally limiting the hatchery's operational flexibility independent of funding.
- **Distribution Trends:** Numbers of Spring Chinook distributed to federal waters fluctuated across recent years: FY91 = 1,137,700; FY92 = 694,800; FY93 = 708,000 — indicating a downward trend likely tied to the groundwater production constraint.
- **Infrastructure Needs:** Identified capital priorities include doubling groundwater supply (top priority), a multi-stall storage/shop building, emergency generator upgrades, well rehabilitation, and new outdoor food storage.
- **Community Outreach:** The Brock Pond fishing day exemplifies hatchery integration with local recreational and educational fisheries programming, supported by partnerships with the Mid-Columbia Fishery Resource Office, U.S. Forest Service, and local civic organizations (Kiwanis Club).
