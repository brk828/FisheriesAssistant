# Sexual Dimorphism in the Adult Razorback Sucker (Xyrauchen texanus)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1974 technical report, prepared by [[Eric S. Gustafson]] and submitted by [[W. L. Minckley]] at the [[Lower Colorado River Basin Research Laboratory]], [[Arizona State University]], to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Regional Office, evaluates external secondary sex characteristics for sexing adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*[[Xyrauchen texanus]]*). The authors aimed to provide non-lethal sexing criteria for a threatened or endangered species, so that males and females could be segregated without sacrificing individuals. The study examined preserved museum specimens and drew on literature and personal communications. Four criteria were assessed: [[Breeding Coloration]], [[Nuptial Tubercles]] (tuberculation), [[Fin Proportions]], and [[Urogenital Papillae]] size. The report is explicitly preliminary and covers adults only.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Reliable external sexing criteria were considered essential for future research on population structure, including changes in sex ratios with increasing population age, in the ongoing [[Life History]] study of the species.
- **Sources:** Information came from the literature, personal communications with [[Carl L. Hubbs]] (Scripps Institution of Oceanography), [[W. L. Minckley]] (Arizona State University), and [[Gail C. Kobetich]] (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service), and from examination of preserved specimens in the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]].
- **Specimens:** All individuals examined were adults, ranging from 30.5 to 48.1 cm standard length. Each fish was sexed by direct examination of its gonads (Table 1).
- **Context:** The species is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]], and the report compares its traits with those of related genera such as [[Catostomus]] and [[Moxostoma]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution of specimens:** Museum material came from Lake Mohave (Mohave Co., Arizona, 2.5 mi N. of [[Davis Dam]]), the Yampa River (Utah), Lake Mead (Mohave Co., Arizona, S. Cove), and the Colorado River near [[Imperial Dam]] (Yuma Co., Arizona). Collection dates ranged from 1966 to 1973.
- **Reproductive timing:** The spawning season runs from late February to early June, during which breeding colour and tubercles are expressed.
- **Conservation relevance:** The species is threatened or endangered, so non-lethal sexing methods are directly relevant to management and to monitoring population structure within the [[Colorado River]] system.

## Key Findings

1. **Breeding coloration:** During spawning, breeding males become blackish dorsally and along the upper flanks (to about one inch below the lateral line) and brilliant orange laterally and ventrally. Females keep the usual coloration. Outside the spawning season, both sexes look alike.
2. **Nuptial tubercles:** Tubercles are conical, associated with fin rays rather than interradial membranes, and range from 0.5 to 3.0 mm in diameter.
   - The study examined 3 tuberculate males and 8 tuberculate females.
   - Tuberculation was more widespread in males. Males had tubercles on the dorsal surfaces of the pelvic fins, anal fin, caudal peduncle (except the dorsum), and lower caudal lobe. Female tuberculation was limited to the caudal peduncle below the lateral line and the lower caudal lobe.
   - Where homologous structures carried tubercles, males had more (Table 2). Male counts were 148, 150, and 196 (caudal peduncle) and 255, 206, and 260 (caudal fin). Female counts ranged from 0 to 40 (caudal peduncle) and from 13 to 132 (caudal fin). Spent females' resorbed or worn tubercles, recorded as scars, were included.
   - Figure caption counts: 15 tubercles on a ripe male's pelvic fin, 98 on the anal fin, 98 on the caudal peduncle, and 114 on the lower caudal lobe.
3. **Fin proportions:** Pectoral fins showed no sexual dimorphism. Male pelvic and anal fins averaged 1.8% and 2.4% longer, respectively, than those of females. Standard length was used to standardise fin lengths (Table 3), with each male paired to a female of similar length (seven males in the museum inventory).
4. **Urogenital papillae:** In all but one case, female papillae were larger than male papillae, by an average of 39.9%. The single exception was a male whose papilla was 11.1% larger than the female's. Qualitatively, male papillae appeared more pointed at the apex and were more oriented along the body's long axis (Table 4, Fig. 5).
5. **Comparison with other catostomids:** Branson (1966) had suggested that Xyrauchen tubercle arrangement resembled that of *Catostomus*. The present authors found it more similar to *Moxostoma*, because tubercles also occur on the pelvic fins and caudal peduncle, as in *Moxostoma carinatum*.
6. **Limitations and future work:** The authors state that the study is far from complete. The report is preliminary, covers adults only, and relies on a small sample. Larval, postlarval, and juvenile data are needed, and more information on tuberculation, fin lengths, and papillae size is required to improve sexing reliability. Live or freshly preserved individuals are to be examined where possible.
