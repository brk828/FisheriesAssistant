# Electrophoretic and Cytogenetic Variation in the Tui Chub (*Gila bicolor*) from the Lahontan System, Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by Mark J. Rosenfeld examines genetic variation in the [[Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor*) across the [[Lahontan System]] of north-central Nevada using horizontal starch electrophoresis of 21 protein loci and chromosome analysis with Giemsa and silver staining. Samples came from the Humboldt River, the Lahontan Basin proper, Newark Valley and Railroad Valley, with comparisons to the [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]] (*G. robusta seminuda*). The work also introduces improved [[Fish Cytogenetics]] methods built on non-lethal fin biopsy and tissue culture. Key conclusions are that Railroad Valley tui chubs are of hybrid origin, that the two Newark Valley subspecies cannot be separated genetically, and that chromosome structure is largely uniform, apart from a unique fourth Ag-NOR in Bull Creek fish.

## Background & Description

The tui chub is a cyprinid native to insular Great Basin drainages that were once connected to [[Pleistocene Lake Lahontan]], as well as to the Columbia, Klamath and Sacramento Rivers. It may be one of the oldest extant North American fishes. Its geographic variation and disjunct distribution have led to many described subspecies, including:

- *G. b. obesa* (Humboldt River and some Lahontan lakes)
- *G. b. pectinifer* (Lahontan Basin proper lakes)
- *G. b. newarkensis* and *G. b. euchila* (Newark Valley)

Distinguishing features include pharyngeal tooth formula (mostly 5-5 in *G. b. pectinifer* versus 5-4 in *G. b. obesa*) and higher gill raker, scale and pelvic ray counts in *G. b. pectinifer*. Prior work relied only on character-by-character morphology and formal naming. This study adds molecular and chromosomal data.

The [[Lahontan Basin]] region was shaped by volcanism and crustal extension, and it went through several [[Pleistocene Pluvial Cycles]]. Holocene desiccation left patchy permanent waters that now hold isolated tui chub populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat isolation:** Most populations occupy springs or small streams. Bishop Creek and Bull Creek are larger stream habitats, and Blue Eagle Spring is a small geothermal water body (about 30 °C).
- **Hydrological history:** Railroad Valley lacks evidence of connection to the Lahontan System during the last mesic period, which suggests earlier isolation. Earlier connections with the Pluvial White River (Colorado River system) are documented.
- **Conservation status:** *G. b. euchila* is protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (1973) because of habitat destruction and predation by introduced salmonids. *G. r. jordani* is protected, and some Railroad Valley isolates have gone extinct. *G. r. seminuda* is nearly extinct.
- **Introductions:** Little Soda Lake *G. b. obesa* was introduced by humans from elsewhere, which may limit its allelic representation.
- **Natural and anthropogenic change:** The natural channel between the two Fish Creek Springs populations was eliminated by humans about 30 years ago.

## Key Findings

**Sampling**
- 201 *G. bicolor* were collected in 1986 from 10 sites. 29 *G. r. seminuda* were collected from the Virgin River at Bloomington, Utah.
- Analyses covered 21 presumptive loci for electrophoresis and 3 males and 3 females per sample for cytogenetics.

**Electrophoresis**
- Average heterozygosity (H) was 0.028 and polymorphism (P) was 0.136 for *G. bicolor*. Horse Trap Spring *G. b. newarkensis* was monomorphic (H = 0).
- Bull Creek (H = 0.052, P = 0.238) and Bishop Creek (H = 0.045, P = 0.191) were the most variable samples. Their larger habitats may explain this.
- Average polymorphism in *G. bicolor* (14%) was lower than in the Colorado River Gila complex (20–36%).
- Intersample differences accounted for about 23% of total variation (F_ST = 0.231).
- Icdh-3 was present in all *G. bicolor* but absent from *G. r. seminuda*. Fixed differences at Icdh-1 and Icdh-2 separated the two taxa.
- Icdh-3 likely arose from a gene duplication after the *G. bicolor* lineage became isolated.
- Cbp-2(b) and GP-1(b) were found only in Railroad Valley *G. bicolor* and in *G. r. seminuda*. This points to past hybridization.
- Genetic distances ranged from near zero to 0.035 (Nei) and from 0.006 to 0.082 (Rogers). Bull Creek was the most divergent population, separated by 0.066 units in the UPGMA phenogram (cophenetic correlation = 0.95).
- The UPGMA phenogram and PCA did not group samples consistently by subspecies or geography.
- Newark Valley subspecies had no exclusive alleles. Allelic differences did not verify the morphological taxonomy. Stochastic losses of variation in small demes are the likely cause.

**Cytogenetics**
- Diploid karyotypes contained 50 chromosomes, and 88% or more of spreads in each OTU had 50 chromosomes.
- All 25 chromosome pairs are bi-armed, so the fundamental arm number was revised from 94 to 100.
- Most populations had 3 Ag-NOR pairs, on the distal short arms of chromosomes 18, 24 and 25.
- Bull Creek fish had a fourth Ag-NOR, an interstitial long-arm site on chromosome 10. This feature is unprecedented among North American cyprinids.

**Conclusions**
1. Icdh-3 is a gene duplication that arose after the *G. bicolor* lineage became isolated.
2. Stochastic processes in small demes account for most allelic differences. Whether GP-1(b) in Bishop Creek or Sod-1(c) in Stillwater Marsh are diagnostic remains unresolved.
3. Newark Valley subspecies do not differ genetically in a way that verifies morphological taxonomy.
4. Railroad Valley fish are of hybrid origin, with introgression from a [[Colorado River Gila]] (probably a *G. robusta* derivative) perhaps as early as the Late Pliocene or Early Pleistocene.
5. Karyotypes are similar across *G. bicolor* at the Giemsa level. The fourth Ag-NOR in Railroad Valley is likely a local origin.

**Methods Advances**
- A non-lethal fin biopsy and storage medium (L-15 with antibiotics and nystatin) keeps tissue viable for up to 180 days of refrigeration.
- Cells can be cryopreserved in 11.5% propanediol. Over 96% of cultures (143 of 146 flasks) remained uncontaminated.
- Samples remained viable after long-distance shipment, including from Spain and the former Soviet Union.
