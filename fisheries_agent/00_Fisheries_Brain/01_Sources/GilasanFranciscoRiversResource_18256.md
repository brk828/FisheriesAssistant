# Gila-San Francisco Rivers Wildlife Habitat Resource Report

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae #riparian-ecosystems

## Executive Summary

This June 1971 resource report assesses wildlife habitat conditions in the proposed [[Gila River]]-[[San Francisco River]] study area in Arizona. The perennial river flow creates critical riparian and aquatic communities in an otherwise arid landscape, supporting diverse wildlife including 68 bird species, multiple mammals, and four known fish species. The report identifies the area as a small, relatively isolated remnant of desert riparian habitat type that is rapidly disappearing throughout the arid southwest. Current conditions show degraded riparian vegetation due to intensive livestock grazing and periodic flooding, though the area maintains significant wildlife value. The [[San Francisco River]] provides crucial year-round flow essential for fish survival, particularly during hot summer months.

## Background & Description

The study area represents a desert riparian ecosystem centered on perennial river flow through canyon terrain. Dominant riparian vegetation includes [[Mesquite]] (*Prosopis juliflora*) and seep willow (*Baccharis glutinosa*), with moderately abundant [[Cottonwood]] (*Populus fremonti*) occurring primarily on remaining flood plain areas in small scattered stands. More important deciduous tree species for wildlife—poplar, sycamore, willow, and ash—vary in occurrence. [[Willow]] (*Salix gooddingii*) and ash (*Fraxinus vetulina*) are very rare, represented only by mature trees well outside the main channel. [[Sycamore]] (*Platanus wrighti*) trees are restricted to the downstream portion near [[Bonita Creek]].

Abundant cottonwood reproduction occurs immediately adjacent to the river channel, but young saplings are heavily grazed and kept diminutive by domestic livestock, with few intermediate-sized trees evident. No large-scale invasion of [[Tamarisk]] (*Tamarix pentandra*) has occurred; scattered seedlings are found with cottonwood seedlings and are also heavily grazed. Canyon walls show little vegetation on vertical surfaces, while areas inaccessible to livestock support good stands of perennial grasses. Accessible slopes are dominated by [[Creosote Bush]] (*Larrea tridentata*), snake weed, leguminous shrubs, and cacti.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Desert riparian ecosystems function as critical wildlife concentration areas but are rapidly succumbing as biological entities due to human activities throughout the arid southwest. The perennial flow provides year-long water for wildlife in an otherwise arid landscape. The [[San Francisco River]] appears to provide stable year-round flow crucial for fish survival, especially during hot summer months in both the Gila and San Francisco Rivers.

Two major factors limit riparian plant community development: (1) periodic flooding and riverbed scouring that destroys plants volunteering along the river channel, and (2) intensive grazing by domestic livestock that concentrate along canyon bottoms for permanent water, shade, and easy walking. Heavy grazing has suppressed growth of larger, more important deciduous tree species. Pollution of the San Francisco River by Phelps-Dodge mining company of Morenci has occurred historically and represents a future threat (referenced in Arizona Game and Fish Department report, Investigation of Fish Killed, July 10, 1969).

The aquatic habitat supports four known fish species: [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*). Amphibians include [[Bullfrog]] (*Rana catesbeiana*), [[Leopard Frog]] (*Rana pipiens*), and toad (*Bufo* sp.). A softshell turtle was also documented.

Birds are especially well represented with 68 species recorded. Certain species utilize caves and crevices in rocky canyon walls for roosting and nesting, while far more species depend on cottonwood trees and mesquite "bosques." [[Mexican Black Hawk]] (*Buteogallus anthracinus*), included in the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife list of peripheral species, was present in good numbers during Spring 1970 and 1971. The report notes possible presence of rare or endangered species such as [[Peregrine Falcon]] and [[Prairie Falcon]] pending further investigation.

Observed mammals include bat (*Pipistrellis*), [[Raccoon]], skunk, [[Coyote]], [[Gray Fox]], [[Mountain Lion]], chipmunk, rock squirrel, cottontail rabbit, [[Javelina]], and [[Mule Deer]]. Other mammals likely present include several bat species, ringtail cat, bobcat, various rats and mice, and porcupine.

## Key Findings

1. **Habitat Significance**: The proposed Gila-San Francisco Rivers study area represents a very important and scarce habitat type in its present condition—a small, relatively isolated remnant of desert riparian ecosystem.

2. **Current Degradation**: The riparian plant community is not as lush or well-developed as it could be, primarily due to intensive livestock grazing that prevents regeneration of larger, more desirable deciduous trees, especially cottonwood.

3. **Management Potential**: With protection from livestock grazing and/or adjustments in grazing patterns, the riparian plant community could attain fuller development, thus increasing wildlife and aesthetic value.

4. **Wildlife Diversity**: The area supports 68 bird species, multiple mammal species, four known fish species, and various reptiles and amphibians, demonstrating high biodiversity for a desert riparian system.

5. **Threatened Species**: [[Mexican Black Hawk]] present in good numbers; potential habitat for rare or endangered species including peregrine and prairie falcons requires further investigation.

6. **Recommended Actions**:
   - Stream sampling to obtain more information on fish species present and their relative abundance
   - Observation trips during different seasons to better determine wintering, nesting, and migratory bird use; additional information needed on mammals, reptiles, and amphibians
   - Immediate Bureau action to resolve heavy livestock use along river bottoms
   - Protection from indiscriminate and malicious shooting, especially during spring nesting season

7. **Water Quality Concerns**: Historical pollution from Phelps-Dodge mining company represents ongoing threat to aquatic resources.
