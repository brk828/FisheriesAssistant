# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes, May 12-13, 1982

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

These minutes, with a cover memorandum dated July 13, 1982, record the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting held May 12-13, 1982, in St. George, Utah. The team reviewed revisions to three recovery plans for [[Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]], and discussed a proposed [[Conservation Plan]] for the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The team's main position was that the conservation plan should be kept separate from the recovery plans, that the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] must be addressed, and that the team should continue to meet and advise. The memorandum lists nine action items with assigned members and due dates.

## Background & Description

The meeting was attended by about two dozen participants from federal and state agencies and universities, including the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BuRec), the National Park Service, and the state wildlife agencies of California, Colorado, Utah, Arizona, and Nevada. Colorado State University was also represented.

Key personnel changes:
- Jim St. Amant was introduced as the new team leader, contingent on FWS approval.
- Bob Ruesink replaced Tom Lytle as BLM's representative. Jim Bennett replaced Lytle as Colorado's representative.
- The team unanimously agreed that Lytle should be retained as a team consultant, if possible.

Don Archer of the Colorado River Fisheries Project (Salt Lake City) presented the concept of a conservation plan in a slide show. The plan covers only the upper basin. Its information sources include the recovery plans, Colorado fishes project data, Section 7 consultations, ongoing development, and culture techniques for the endangered fishes.

Species goals presented in the conservation plan:
- **Squawfish:** rejuvenate populations and reestablish them in uninhabited areas; promote the species as an economic and social asset and as a sport fish.
- **Humpback chub:** maintain Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon populations; reestablish the species in the Green/Yampa system.
- **Bonytail chub:** reestablish the species in the upper basin, with strategies that include determining current status, hatchery propagation, and taxonomic evaluation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Several threats and management issues recur throughout the discussion:
- **Development and Section 7 consultation:** Section 7 work in the upper basin was ongoing. Terry Hickman reported that several biological opinions had been issued recently, with 20 more scheduled for the Bureau of Reclamation in the next year. These involve proposed projects on the Yampa, White, Green, Colorado, and Gunnison rivers.
- **Habitat protection:** Participants said the conservation plan should "draw the line" for development and protect upper-basin habitat. Habitat protection was added to the humpback chub step-down narrative.
- **Upper and lower basin differences:** The upper basin is managed through the conservation plan, while the lower basin relies on a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) that follows the recovery plan guidelines. Regional efforts did not agree on Section 7 consultation, so FWS headquarters directed Region 6 to write the upper-basin plan. Funding was enough for only one region to prepare a plan.
- **Hatchery role:** Reed Harris worried that the hatchery program in the conservation plan could turn hatcheries into refugia only. Bonytail chub recovery included a captive gene pool, separate from reintroduction.
- **Funding and visibility:** FWS had discussed cutting team funding or disbanding the team. The team's visibility and the number of ongoing projects may affect future funding.

## Key Findings

**Position on the conservation plan**
- The team voted unanimously to support separating the conservation plan from the recovery plans.
- The team does not recognize the conservation plan as part of the recovery plan, but will review and comment on it.
- All references to the conservation plan in the recovery plans should be deleted.
- The lower basin must be included in the conservation plan or covered by a separate plan.
- A conservation plan council was discussed but considered too great a commitment, given the existing [[Upper Colorado River Basin Endangered Fishes Council]] proposal and the existing Colorado River Wildlife Council. The team unanimously agreed that each team member should be an ad hoc member of the council once it is formed.

**Meetings and communication**
- The team recommended two meetings per year to improve information exchange between the basins.
- Linda Ulmer proposed an informal newsletter, which the team accepted. Ulmer will request information from state and federal agencies.
- The next meeting should be held in Vernal, Utah, with only the lower-basin members attending to reduce costs.
- Team support was set at $4,000.

**Squawfish plan**
- Personal communication citations will be eliminated.
- The revision is due July 1, 1982, with a draft to FWS by mid-July (tentative).
- Tyus and Bennett will revise the introduction, and St. Amant will revise the step-down outline and narrative.

**Bonytail chub plan**
- The team considered the draft to be effectively a pre-recovery plan.
- Jerry Burton will check on federal protection for look-alikes.
- A table of contents and a hatchery propagation section will be added.
- The goal was revised to: "To restore and maintain bonytail chubs in a nonendangered status." Its objectives are to determine status in the riverine environment; consider downlisting once two self-sustaining populations are identified; and reintroduce the species into suitable habitat if it has been extirpated from the river.
- Randy Radant and Bob Ruesink will draft the goal and step-down outline.

**Humpback chub plan**
- Steve Petersburg and Reed Harris will write a habitat protection section for the step-down narrative.
- Tyus and Bennett will revise the introduction.

**Other action items**
- Jim Brooks will provide information on the bonytail chub to Tyus.
- St. Amant will review all completed plans and send them to FWS, with copies to team members.
- Final revision dates were listed in a table from FWS, which is partly illegible in the source.
