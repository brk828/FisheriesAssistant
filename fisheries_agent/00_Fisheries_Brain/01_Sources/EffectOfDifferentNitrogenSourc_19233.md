# Effect of Different Nitrogen Sources and Soil on Fish Production and Water Chemistry in Fertilized Plastic Pools

#ichthyology #aquaculture #cyprinidae #pond-management #fertilization

## Executive Summary

This experimental study by [[George N. Greene]] at [[Auburn University]] (1967-1968) investigated the effects of three nitrogen sources (sodium nitrate, ammonium carbonate, and cottonseed meal) and the presence of [[Cecil Soil]] on fish production and water chemistry in fertilized plastic pools. Fifty pools (1/555 acre each) were stocked with [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) and subjected to ten treatment combinations with five replicates each. Phosphate fertilization alone increased production five-fold over unfertilized controls (187.7 vs 37.6 g/pool). Organic nitrogen from cottonseed meal produced the highest yields (349.7 g/pool, 9.3 times unfertilized), significantly exceeding inorganic nitrogen sources. Soil presence reduced organic carbon levels but did not significantly affect fish production or nitrogen concentrations.

## Background & Description

Pond fertilization to increase fish production is a widespread management practice, with nitrogen typically supplied as nitrate, ammonium, or organic nitrogen. While comparative studies of organic versus inorganic nitrogen existed (Hickling 1962; Swingle 1947), the effects of different inorganic nitrogen sources had not been systematically measured. Additionally, bottom soil represents an important factor because much fertilizer settles to the bottom and becomes tied up in mud, reducing immediate availability.

The experiment utilized 50 plastic-lined pools, each 10 feet in diameter (1/555 acre) and 20-25 inches deep, with a 4-inch drain tile as spawning site. Each pool was stocked with 50 adult [[Fathead Minnow]] between March 1-7, 1967. Fish were harvested October 2-4, 1967 using rotenone. Reproduction occurred in all pools, with collected fish primarily consisting of offspring rather than original stock.

Ten treatment combinations were established with five replicates each:
- Unfertilized (with and without soil)
- Phosphate only (with and without soil)
- Ammonium + phosphate (with and without soil)
- Nitrate + phosphate (with and without soil)
- Cottonseed meal + phosphate (with and without soil)

Fertilizers were applied monthly by surface broadcasting at rates of 8 lbs P₂O₅ and 8 lbs nitrogen per acre. [[Cecil Soil]], a local reddish-brown soil of schistic origin, was applied as a 4-inch layer where used.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Water samples were collected April 19, May 29, July 6, and August 14, 1967. Analyses measured:
- [[Ammonia Nitrogen]]
- [[Organic Nitrogen]]
- Total organic carbon
- Dissolved organic carbon (filtered through 0.45 micron millipore filter)

Methods followed [[American Public Health Association]] (1965) protocols with modifications. Organic carbon was determined using a Beckman carbonaceous analyzer after acidification and nitrogen bubble washing to remove inorganic carbon.

The presence of [[Cecil Soil]] significantly affected water chemistry but not fish production. Pools with soil contained approximately 55% of the organic carbon found in pools without soil, suggesting soil particles adsorbed organic matter or tied up nutrients, reducing plankton growth and organic matter production.

## Key Findings

**Fish Production Results:**
- Unfertilized pools: 37.6 g/pool mean production
- Phosphate only: 187.7 g/pool (5-fold increase)
- Inorganic nitrogen (NH₃ or NO₃) + phosphate: 225.3 g/pool average (slight, non-significant increase)
- Cottonseed meal + phosphate: 349.7 g/pool (9.3-fold increase, statistically significant)
- No significant difference between ammonium and nitrate fertilization
- Soil presence showed no significant effect on fish production across treatments

**Water Chemistry Results:**

Total Organic Carbon (ppm, ranked by treatment):
- NO₃: 59.96
- NH₃: 50.09
- Organic N: 47.74
- PO₄: 39.97
- None: 23.47

Ammonia Nitrogen (ppm, ranked by treatment):
- NO₃: 0.202
- Organic N: 0.181
- NH₃: 0.166
- PO₄: 0.130
- None: 0.068

Organic Nitrogen showed similar patterns, with unfertilized treatments significantly lower than fertilized treatments. Nitrate and organic nitrogen fertilizer treatments were highest.

Dissolved organic carbon showed no significant differences between fertilizer treatments or between fertilized and unfertilized pools.

**Soil Effects on Water Chemistry:**
- Pools with [[Cecil Soil]] had significantly less total and dissolved organic carbon than pools without soil
- No measurable effect on ammonia nitrogen or organic nitrogen concentrations

**Key Interpretation:**
The superior performance of cottonseed meal was attributed to its direct utilization as food by fish and lower organisms in the food chain, beyond its role as a nutrient source. By the second sampling date (after April 19), phosphate-only fertilized pools had accumulated sufficient nitrogen through [[Nitrogen Fixation]] to become equivalent to nitrogen-fertilized pools in ammonia content.

Water quality measures were not directly correlated with fish production, despite unfertilized pools being lowest in both parameters.
