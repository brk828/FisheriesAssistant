# Immunohistochemical Localization of Neuropeptide Y-like Substance in the Abdominal Ganglion of the Sea Hare *Aplysia kurodai*: Relationship with FMRFamide-like Substance

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aplysiidae

*Note: This document concerns a marine gastropod mollusc, not a fish. The taxonomic tag above reflects the source's subject and is used as specified for non-fish taxa.*

## Executive Summary

This 1994 study by Honma, Kawahara and Chiba (Fisheries Science 60(1), 53-58) used a [[Streptavidin-Biotin Technique]] to map [[Neuropeptide Y]] (NPY)-like immunoreactivity in the abdominal ganglion of the common sea hare *Aplysia kurodai*. The authors compared NPY distribution with that of the cardioexcitatory tetrapeptide [[FMRFamide]] on serial sections. Both substances were found in the same neurons and fibres, and cross-blocking (preabsorption) tests showed that each antiserum recognized only its homologous antigen. The authors concluded that NPY-like and FMRFamide-like substances coexist in the same cells of the ganglion.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Aplysia kurodai* Baba, a very common littoral gastropod mollusc. It is eaten only in limited localities, including the Tsushima Islands, and is considered a pest for cultivation of the seaweed *Undaria pinnatifida* in Japan.
- **Experimental value:** The species has a pair of giant axons and distinct ganglia made of a small number of large neurons. The nervous system is easy to separate and remove from surrounding tissue.
- **Neuropeptide Y:** A 36-amino-acid, tyrosine-rich member of the pancreatic polypeptide family. In porcine brain it has a molecular weight of 4,254 daltons. It acts as a neurotransmitter or neuromodulator, with roles including presynaptic inhibition of transmitter release and vasoconstriction together with catecholamines.
- **FMRFamide:** The cardioexcitatory tetrapeptide (Phe-Met-Arg-Phe-NH2), first discovered in the bivalve *Macrocallista nimbosa*.
- **Prior work:** Earlier immunohistochemical studies on *Aplysia* had mapped serotonin, FMRFamide, vasotocin, gastrin/cholecystokinin, pedal peptide and enkephalin. Yui et al. reported no reaction to mammalian NPY antiserum in the cephalic ganglion of *A. kurodai*.
- **Study focus:** Special attention went to the paired [[Bag Cells]] on the anterior margin of the ganglion, which produce the hormone controlling egg-laying behaviour.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Collection:** Ten sea hares (young and adult) were collected from a rocky beach at the Sado Marine Biological Station, Niigata University, on the northwestern coast of Sado Island, Sea of Japan, between May and December 1992.
- **Distribution of NPY in invertebrates:** The source notes that reports on NPY in invertebrates are scarce. Prior records include the freshwater snail *Planorbarius corneus*, marine gastropods *Bulla* and *Fusitriton*, the locust *Locusta*, and the sabellid annelid *Sabellastarte*.
- **Comparative vertebrate context:** NPY has been found in higher vertebrates, including man, and in lower vertebrates such as elasmobranchs (cloudy dogfish, *Scyliorhinus torazame*) and hagfish (*Paramyxine atami*). Colocalization of FMRFamide and NPY was previously reported in the hagfish brain.
- **Functional roles in other taxa:** In vertebrates NPY inhibits secretion of LH and growth hormone (not FSH) in rat anterior pituitary cells, and it potentiates vasoconstriction in rabbit blood vessels. FMRFamide regulates heart pulsation in molluscs and radular muscle activity in *Aplysia*.

## Key Findings

1. **Colocalization:** NPY-like and FMRFamide-like immunoreactivities were found together in the same cells of the abdominal ganglion, in the same fibres of the peripheral nerves, and at presumed synaptic junctions.
2. **Distribution:**
   - Dorsal part: large numbers of immunoreactive cells in the left and right quarters of the rostral hemisphere, plus immunoreactive beaded (varicose) fibres in the caudal hemisphere.
   - Ventral part: a small number of immunoreactive cells in the rostral hemisphere and the left quarter of the caudal hemisphere.
3. **Cell characteristics:** All neurons were spherical or ovoid. Large cells lay in the outer zone and small cells in the inner zone near the neuropil. Strong immunoreactivity was typical of small and medium cells, while large cells were generally weakly reactive.
4. **Peripheral nerves:** Both substances occurred in the left and right pleuro-abdominal connectives, vaginal nerve, branchial nerve, siphon nerve and genital-pericardial nerve.
5. **Bag cells:** The bag cell masses themselves showed no immunoreactivity, but the bag cell nucleus reacted specifically with NPY antiserum.
6. **Antibody specificity (cross-blocking):**
   - NPY antiserum signal was abolished by preabsorption with NPY but not by FMRFamide.
   - FMRFamide antiserum signal was abolished by preabsorption with FMRFamide but not by NPY.
   - Each antiserum therefore recognized only its own antigen.
7. **Conclusion:** The authors state that "it is evident that NPY-like and FMRFamide-like substances coexist in the same cells" of the *A. kurodai* abdominal ganglion. They suggest NPY may be antagonistic to the neuromodulatory action of FMRFamide, but note that identification of individual cells is needed to confirm function.
8. **Limitations:** The authors could not identify individual cells, unlike earlier work on *Aplysia californica* by Frazier et al.
