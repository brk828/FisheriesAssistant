# Subterranean Amphipods of the Genus Stygonectes (Gammaridae) from Texas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

John R. Holsinger (Department of Zoology and Institute of Speleology, University of Kentucky) revises the troglobitic amphipod genus [[Stygonectes]] from central Texas caves. He redefines the genus, sinks [[Synpleonia]] into it as a synonym, treats five species (three newly described), and proposes that speciation in the [[Edwards Plateau]] resulted from geological barriers to dispersal and Pleistocene climate change. The paper was submitted 14 April 1965 and accepted 2 June 1965.

Note: the source is a crustacean systematics paper. It contains no fish data, so the fish-oriented fields above are populated with the closest applicable taxonomic information.

## Background & Description

- **Historical record:** [[Benedict]] (1896) described *Crangonyx flagellatus* from an artesian well at San Marcos, Hays County, dug by the U.S. Fish Commission. [[Hay]] (1903) erected *Stygonectes* for this species. [[Creaser]] (1934) described *Synpleonia*, and [[Hubricht]] (1943) described *S. balconis* from caves in Hays and Kendall counties.
- **Generic redefinition:** Earlier confusion over whether urosome segments were fused (Creaser's criterion) was resolved by [[Shoemaker]] (1938), who showed that all three urosome segments are coalesced in both genera. Holsinger found no reliable difference between the genera; the presence or absence of bifurcate lateral sternal gills varies even within a species. He united them under the older name *Stygonectes*.
- **Diagnostic traits:** Eyeless, unpigmented troglobites. Antenna 1 is longer than antenna 2. The urosome segments are fused. Paired lateral sternal gills appear on peraeon segments 6 and 7. The telson is as long as or longer than broad.
- **Key to Texas species:** Separation rests on antenna length, the telson ratio, the shape of the gnathopod palmar margins, and the length of peraeopod 3 relative to peraeopod 4.
- **Methods:** Material came mainly from [[Texas Speleological Survey]] collecting (notably James R. Reddell), plus type material from the [[United States National Museum]] and the Hubricht collection. Mouthparts were described in detail only for *S. flagellatus*; other species were described only where differences had possible specific value.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types:** Deep phreatic streams and artesian outlets (*S. flagellatus*, *S. longipes*), vadose and drip pools (*S. balconis*, *S. longipes* in Cave Without-A-Name), and shallow pools of a deep vertical sinkhole (*S. hadenoecus*).
- **Devils Sinkhole:** A vertical cave in the Edwards limestone (Cretaceous) with a near-circular entrance about 18 m in diameter and a vertical drop of 43 m. The bottom room is roughly 73 x 110 m. Amphipods were found in shallow pool margins alongside troglobitic isopods (*Cirolanides texensis*). Bat guano covering the pool bottoms was suggested as a nutrient source.
- **Water and nutrients:** *S. balconis* prefers quiet pools with organic debris and silt. Bacterial content of silt was presumed to be an important nutrient source.
- **Reproduction:** Ovigerous *S. balconis* females were recorded in January, May and August. Mean brood size was 5 eggs or embryos (range 4 to 6). First instars averaged about 2 mm.
- **Climate and geology:** The Edwards Plateau has cavernous limestones and extensive subsurface drainage. The subhumid modern climate has average annual precipitation of 28 to 32 inches.

## Key Findings

1. **Genus revision:** [[Stygonectes]] is redefined and [[Synpleonia]] is treated as a junior synonym. Holsinger considered the sternal-gill criterion too artificial to separate the genera.
2. **Five Texas species** are recognized:
   - *S. flagellatus* (Benedict), redescribed. Known only from the Purgatory Creek System, Hays County. Largest male 12 mm, largest female 14 mm. Very rare: only two specimens recorded from the San Marcos artesian well since 1896, and one female from Ezells Cave. Holsinger found no amphipods in the 1964 search of the well outlets.
   - *S. longipes* sp. nov. Known only from Cave Without-A-Name (Century Caverns), Kendall County. Holotype male 9.0 mm; largest female 10.5 mm. Closely similar to *S. flagellatus*, with narrower peraeopod 3 bases and longer dactyls.
   - *S. hadenoecus* sp. nov. Known only from Devils Sinkhole, Edwards County (type locality about 7 mi NE of Rocksprings). Largest male 9.75 mm, largest female 11.25 mm. Material: holotype and allotype, plus paratypes (count given in the source as 8 and 1 paratypes collected in June 1964, and 6 allotype and 5 female plus 1 male paratypes collected in October 1963).
   - *S. balconis* Hubricht, partially redescribed. Range runs from southern Kendall County east to Hays County, north along the western side of the Balcones fault zone to southern Coryell County, and west to eastern San Saba County. Largest males 16.0 mm, largest females 14.0 mm. Bifurcate sternal gills occur at random in larger males and females (over 10.5 to 11.0 mm).
   - *S. reddelli* sp. nov. Known from Whiteface Cave (type locality, about 20 mi SW of San Saba) and Neel Cave, Menard County. Largest female 13.5 mm, largest male 9.75 mm. Distinguished from *S. balconis* by straight gnathopod palmar margins and calceoli on male antenna 1.
3. **Unassigned material:** Specimens from Balcones Sink Cave, Spanish Wells Cave, Landa Park and Cascade Caverns could not be assigned to a described species. The Cascade Caverns females were judged not conspecific with *S. flagellatus*.
4. **Biogeography and origin:** A marine-to-freshwater invasion during the Eocene Mississippi embayment is proposed. Fully subterranean habits probably evolved later, in the Pleistocene.
5. **Relationship to *S. americanus*:** *S. balconis* is interpreted as an isolated remnant of a former range extension of [[Stygonectes americanus]] (formerly *Synpleonia americana*). Drying of central Texas in the Pleistocene forced a facultatively subterranean population into full troglobitism. A gap in cavernous terrain in the Arbuckles region is proposed to have isolated the two.
6. **Speciation mechanisms:** Geological barriers, including faulting, stratigraphic breaks and the Balcones fault zone, are proposed to limit gene flow. *S. balconis* spans three surface drainage basins, and subterranean gene transfer under divides is suggested but unverified.
7. **Clinal variation:** Palmar-margin concavity of the gnathopods decreases northward along the western side of the Balcones fault zone, which Holsinger regards as strongly indicative of clinal variation needing more material to test.
8. **Conservation-relevant rarity:** *S. flagellatus* is known from very few specimens, and its type locality is a source of concern given the paucity of recent records.
