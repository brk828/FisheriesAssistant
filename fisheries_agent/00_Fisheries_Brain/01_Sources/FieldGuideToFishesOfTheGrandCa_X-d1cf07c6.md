# Field Guide to Fishes of the Grand Canyon State: Redeye Bass and Smallmouth Bass

#ichthyology #Centrarchidae #non-native #invasive_species

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: Two non-native centrarchid species are documented. [[Redeye Bass]] (*Micropterus coosae*) reaches maximum size about 19 in (47 cm). [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*) is thick and chunky with large mouth, upper jaw extending almost to rear margin of eye; maximum size about 27 in (69 cm). Age, maturity, fecundity, and growth parameters: Not reported.

**Smallmouth Bass Morphology**: Lateral line has 68 to 80 scales with 12 or 13 scales above lateral line. Dorsal fin has 9 to 11 spines and 13 to 15 soft rays, clearly separated. Anal fin has 3 spines and 10 or 11 soft rays. Body color bronze to olive with numerous irregularly spaced dark spots; belly dusky in adults, whitish in young. Juveniles have several dark vertical blotches on sides and caudal fin with yellow or orange base, black center, and white tip. Eyes usually reddish, though eye may have reddish tint in adults.

**Habitat Associations**: [[Redeye Bass]] found in middle and upper reaches of [[Verde River]] and tributaries including [[Beaver Creek]], [[Fossil Creek]], [[Oak Creek]], lower [[West Clear Creek]], and [[Wet Beaver Creek]] in Coconino, Gila, and Yavapai counties. [[Smallmouth Bass]] widespread in [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], and [[Verde River]] and their reservoirs and tributaries including some higher elevation streams mostly below 7220 ft (2200 m); [[Colorado River]] downstream from [[Grand Canyon]]; [[Central Arizona Project]] aqueduct; canals and artificial waters in Phoenix metropolitan area.

**Native Range**: [[Smallmouth Bass]] native to St. Lawrence-Great Lakes, Red River, and Mississippi River basins from Quebec to North Dakota and south to Alabama and Oklahoma.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Not reported.

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: [[Redeye Bass]] hybridizes with [[Smallmouth Bass]]. [[Smallmouth Bass]], wherever it has established in Arizona in medium-to-large sized rivers, is strongly implicated in decline of native fishes and as a detriment to their conservation.

**Environmental Drivers**: Not reported.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: Both species are non-native introductions. Date and mechanism of introduction to Arizona are unknown for [[Redeye Bass]], but may have first been introduced to [[Wet Beaver Creek]]. [[Smallmouth Bass]] widely introduced globally as a sport fish.

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: [[Smallmouth Bass]] represents significant conservation threat to native fishes in Arizona. Strongly implicated in decline of native fishes wherever established in medium-to-large sized rivers. [[Redeye Bass]] has had limited introductions outside of native range.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: [[Redeye Bass]] is not yet listed as a sport fish by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGED), but anglers should check current state regulations for [[Smallmouth Bass]] for details. [[Smallmouth Bass]] is an important sport fish in Arizona; check current state regulations for details.
