# Response to Technical Reviews: Gila Taxonomy Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a correspondence item, not a research report. It is the principal investigators' (PIs') formal response to technical reviews of a proposal for a [[Gila Taxonomy Project]] submitted in response to a [[Request For Proposals]] (RFP). The project covers three schedules: morphological investigations, protein electrophoresis (allozymes), and DNA investigations. The response is organized point by point against reviewer weaknesses and comments. It defends the proposed methods, clarifies the sampling strategy, and describes revisions to personnel, equipment, budget, and Mexican study scope. Many reviewer criticisms are answered by stating that they are unsubstantiated or subjective and are therefore not argued against, and the PIs say they "stand on their record." The response is produced by [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) investigators.

## Background & Description

The proposal concerns the taxonomy and phylogeography of the freshwater cyprinid genus *Gila*, with [[Gila Cypha]] as the main study focus. The proposal relies on three lines of evidence:

- **Morphology:** Non-lethal measurement of live and preserved fish by [[Video Image Analysis]] and meristic counts, led by Douglas.
- **Protein electrophoresis:** Allozyme screening, led by Dowling.
- **DNA:** [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) analysis using [[Transfer-Hybridization]] and [[Polymerase Chain Reaction]] (PCR), led by Dowling.

Project coordination and sample management are handled by Starnes. The response cites three key pieces of supporting work by Douglas:

- Ongoing monitoring of Gila cypha in the [[Little Colorado River]] for three years, with about 80 chubs captured, filmed, and released. A 1989 analysis of sexual dimorphism was presented at the [[Desert Fishes Council]] meeting in Albuquerque, NM, and a manuscript is in draft.
- A July 1989 paper at the International Congress of Systematic and Evolutionary Biology (University of Maryland) on morphological and biochemical variation in [[Fundulus Heteroclitus]], an estuarine killifish from the US East Coast. The data were collected by video image analysis of preserved specimens. These results are not yet in manuscript form.
- A morphological analysis by Douglas, Minckley, and Miller (in prep.), presented at the 1990 Desert Fishes Council meeting in Ensenada, Baja California, Mexico (14–17 November 1990). It is the basis for the proposed meristic counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The review and response reveal several ecological and methodological constraints:

- **Specimen condition:** Bent or twisted preserved specimens complicate morphometric measurement. Moderately bent specimens can be straightened for analysis, but badly twisted specimens cannot be measured. The response concedes that some historical specimens must be excluded.
- **Non-lethal sampling:** The project emphasizes non-lethal methods to minimize stress on captive and wild fish. Scale and ray counts are hard to obtain from live fish, so only a minimum set of meristic characters is proposed.
- **Genetic structure of mtDNA:** The response argues that mtDNA in cyprinids shows more variation between populations than within them, because of maternal and clonal inheritance. Sampling should therefore include few individuals from many populations, not many individuals from few populations.
- **Diploid loci limits:** Cyprinids are diploid, so they resolve fewer allozyme loci than tetraploid fishes such as trout. Proposed loci number 30–50 because more loci (e.g., adenosine deaminase, triosephosphate isomerase, mannose phosphate isomerase) carry much higher staining costs.
- **Basin sampling:** Sampling focuses on upper-basin populations. ASU has already sampled numerous lower-basin Gila populations, which will be included in the final evaluation without resampling.

## Key Findings

**Morphology (Schedule I)**
- Non-lethal video filming of live fish is retained. Other morphological data collection is limited by the non-lethal design.
- Transverse measurements are not needed. Lateral measurements discriminate species adequately, according to unpublished analyses by Douglas, Minckley, and Miller.
- A study correlating caliper-based and video-based measurements is under way at ASU to test whether the data sets can be combined.
- Reviewers questioned whether 30 localities are enough. ASU clarified that sampling populations are distinct from populations already evaluated, and added pages identifying required sample sites.

**DNA (Schedule III)**
- DNA extraction from historical preserved material was not in the original proposal due to cost-benefit concerns. The revised proposal adds PCR and sequence analysis, applied only where they yield reliable information.
- The response argues that transfer-hybridization needs very small tissue amounts (e.g., from blood drops and fin clips), so it is compatible with non-lethal sampling.

**Personnel and Qualifications**
- Post-doctoral associates and technicians were not named because affirmative action rules require advertising and evaluation after contract negotiation. The response says no contract has been awarded, so candidates cannot yet be solicited.
- Roles were clarified: Douglas oversees morphology, Dowling oversees protein electrophoresis and DNA research, and Starnes coordinates samples. The technicians for morphology, allozymes, and DNA were eliminated, and an administrative assistant is no longer needed.
- Other researchers withdrew from the project.

**Budget and Equipment**
- Some equipment is reallocated from other projects. Higher-resolution equipment is still purchased and cost-shared with the university. Expensive items were deleted because other research projects were curtailed.
- Reviewers called the cost of Mexican research excessive. Mexican sampling was scaled down, and those costs were folded into the general study plan rather than shown as a separate budget component.

**Reviewer Disputes**
- Reviewers questioned the timeliness of past reports, the reporting history of ASU, and possible overcommitment of PIs. The PIs answered that they have no record of agency complaints and invited documentation, but none was given, so they declined to argue.
- The PIs argue that prior research experience with Gila is an evaluation criterion under the RFP. They reject the implication that it biases results, but offer no formal defense against that criticism.
