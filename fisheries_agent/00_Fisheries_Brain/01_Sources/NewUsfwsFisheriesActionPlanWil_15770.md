## Executive Summary

This article summarizes the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's (USFWS) **Action Plan for Fishery Resources and Aquatic Ecosystems**, signed by Director Mollie Beattie on 12 May 1994. Unlike prior single-species or single-function management approaches, the plan adopts a **holistic, ecosystem-level conservation strategy** for aquatic resources across the United States. It was developed cooperatively with public and private stakeholders and is structured around **12 Action Components** organized into three functional areas: (1) ecosystem/watershed conservation, restoration, and enhancement; (2) fisheries management support; and (3) public support, outreach, and education. The plan prioritizes federal-interest waters — including habitats of threatened, endangered, and candidate species, interjurisdictional and transboundary waters, national wildlife refuges, tribal lands, and military installations — and emphasizes **partnership-building** as the foundational strategy for achieving long-term sustainability of [[Aquatic Ecosystems]] and [[Fishery Resources]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan frames aquatic species within a broader **ecosystem and watershed context** rather than isolated population units:

- **Federal-interest ecosystems** targeted include areas with threatened, endangered, or candidate species; interjurisdictional and transboundary waters (e.g., the [[Great Lakes]]); federal mitigation project sites; [[National Wildlife Refuge System|National Wildlife Refuges]] (91+ million acres, 250+ of ~500 refuges open to recreational fishing); tribal lands and waters; and military installations.
- **Water quantity and quality** are identified as essential prerequisites for ecosystem function, with explicit attention to fish passage and instream flow requirements (Component 3).
- **Hatchery-wild interactions**: The [[National Fish Hatchery System]] is tasked with maintaining native fish diversity and supporting recovery of threatened/endangered/candidate species while safeguarding wild population genetic integrity (Component 4), including detection and mitigation of pathogen transfer between hatchery and wild fish.
- **Nonindigenous species risk**: Technology centers (Component 5) are directed to develop methods preventing introduction of nonindigenous aquatic nuisance species and controlling those already established — an explicit invasive species management niche.
- **Marine mammal stewardship**: USFWS holds federal responsibility for [[Polar Bear|polar bears]], [[Walrus|walruses]], sea and marine otters, [[Manatee|manatees]], and [[Dugong|dugongs]] (Component 12), requiring partnerships with Alaskan Native communities, other federal agencies, and foreign governments — situating these species within Arctic/North Pacific and coastal marine ecosystems.
- **Private aquaculture interface** (Component 8): The plan addresses fish health, nutrition, genetic risk, and bird depredation issues at the boundary between wild and cultured fish populations.

## Key Findings

- The plan represents a shift from **single-species/single-function management** toward **holistic ecosystem and watershed-level conservation** for [[Aquatic Ecosystems]].
- **Partnerships** (Component 1) are identified as the most critical element for success, given diminishing budgets and rising public demand; this includes cross-program integration within USFWS (e.g., hatchery/habitat staff working with endangered species staff on [[Apache Trout]] and [[Greenback Cutthroat Trout]] recovery).
- **Proactive protection** of intact natural systems (Component 2) is emphasized as more efficient than crisis-driven restoration after degradation occurs.
- **Water quantity/quality assurance** (Component 3) is framed as a contentious but essential precondition for functioning aquatic ecosystems, particularly regarding fish passage and instream flows.
- The **National Fish Hatchery System** (Component 4) will pursue an "innovative propagation program" grounded in conservation genetics and ecosystem management principles rather than simple production augmentation.
- **Technology centers** (Component 5) support hatchery evaluation, pathogen control, and nonindigenous species management — linking genetics, fish health, and invasive species science.
- The **National Wildlife Refuge System** (Component 6) manages 91+ million acres, with over half of its 500 refuges open to recreational fishing under developed fishery management plans.
- **Federal Aid Program partnerships** (Component 7) encourage state agencies to integrate habitat and population restoration into recreational fisheries management goals.
- **Public education and outreach** are heavily weighted in the plan — half of the 12 components (Components 9–12) address public support, outreach, and education, reflecting the agency's view that public involvement is essential to long-term success.
- **Enforcement** of federal laws protecting aquatic species and habitats (Component 11) is paired with public awareness efforts to reduce illegal take and habitat destruction.
- **Marine mammal management** (Component 12) is explicitly incorporated into the aquatic ecosystem plan, covering [[Polar Bear]], [[Walrus]], sea/marine otters, [[Manatee]], and [[Dugong]] populations via international and cross-jurisdictional partnerships.
- The stated long-term goal is a transition from **restoration** to **maintenance** as the primary management objective for U.S. fishery resources and aquatic ecosystems by the 21st century.
