# Over a Ton of Channel Catfish per Acre

#ichthyology #aquaculture #Ictaluridae #pond-management

## Executive Summary

Research at [[Auburn University]] Agricultural Experiment Station demonstrated that intensive pond management can yield up to 1.5 tons of [[Channel Catfish]] (Ictalurus punctatus) per acre annually. The optimal stocking combination consists of 3,000 catfish, 1,000 [[Fathead Minnow]]s (Pimephales promelas), and 50 [[Largemouth Bass]] (Micropterus salmoides) per acre stocked during winter. Success requires daily feeding from March through October, fertilizer applications, and vigilant monitoring for parasites and oxygen depletion. Field trials at a 10.7-acre pond yielded 2,655 lb. of catfish per acre over 10 months of fishing, with 1,096 anglers catching 85% of stocked fish.

## Background & Description

The [[Channel Catfish]] has transitioned from a valued stream species to an important pond sport fish. Previously believed to spawn only in running water, research revealed its value for pond aquaculture. Key attributes include excellent feed conversion, good flavor, desirable size, lack of hard bones, and sporting qualities when caught.

Pond requirements mirror those for [[Bass-Bluegill]] combinations: satisfactory water supply, adequate clay subsoil to minimize seepage, and ample watershed. Essential features include a drain pipe and water diversion systems, as catfish will escape through excessive spillway overflow. Optimal depth ranges from 1.5 ft. minimum to 6-7 ft. maximum. Ponds as small as 0.5-0.75 acres can successfully provide family fishing for catfish, though typically too small for bass-bluegill production.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Success of the polyculture system depends on absence of competing or predatory species that would consume small stocked catfish or compete for food resources. The [[Fathead Minnow]]s serve as forage, while [[Largemouth Bass]] control reproduction but at stocking densities (50 per acre) low enough to avoid excessive predation on catfish.

Water quality management is critical. Heavy feeding causes pollution with lowered oxygen concentrations that may cause fish distress or mortality. Daily feeding exceeding 30 lb. per acre is dangerous in ponds with little water exchange or no mechanical aeration. Excessive algal blooms decrease light penetration and lower oxygen in deeper water; these can be controlled with periodic treatments of 0.25 lb. copper sulfate per surface acre.

Fertilization regime consists of two applications in March and April; additional fertilizer is not needed. Pond owners must maintain constant vigilance for parasites, diseases, and oxygen depletion. Treatment of fingerlings before stocking provides important but not lifetime immunity.

## Key Findings

**Stocking Rates and Timing:**
- Optimal density: 3,000 [[Channel Catfish]], 1,000 [[Fathead Minnow]]s, 50 [[Largemouth Bass]] per acre
- Stocking period: winter (by February for 4-in. fingerlings)

**Feeding Protocol:**
- Daily feeding from March 1 through October or until water temperature drops below 60°F
- Pelleted fish feed: 3 lb. per acre at start, increasing to maximum 30 lb. per acre
- Feed amounts reduced in subsequent years as fish are harvested

**Growth Performance:**
- 4-in. fingerlings stocked by February reached harvestable size (0.7 lb.) by October
- 8-in. fingerlings averaged 0.7 lb. by August when fishing began

**Production Results (10.7-acre pond, Auburn, Alabama, 1966-67):**
- Total harvest: 2,655 lb. catfish and 30 lb. bass per acre over 10 months
- Fishing pressure: 1,096 persons per acre
- Harvest efficiency: 85% of stocked catfish caught
- Peak monthly catches: 522 lb. in April, 364 lb. in September

**Reproductive Dynamics:**
- Catfish spawned in second summer but insufficient young escaped bass predation to maintain satisfactory fishing
- After heavy fishing pressure, few fish remained; pond was drained and restocked
- Lightly fished ponds should provide good fishing for several years

**Management Considerations:**
- High yields require considerably more expense than [[Bass-Bluegill]] ponds
- Only pond owners willing to meet necessary costs should pursue maximum catfish production
- Frequent pond inspection for sick or dead fish is mandatory
- Occasional pond treatment may be necessary for parasite and disease control
