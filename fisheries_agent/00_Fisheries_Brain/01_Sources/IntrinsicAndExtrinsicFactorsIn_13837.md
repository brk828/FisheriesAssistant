# Turtox News, March 1957: Speciation Debate and Laboratory Supply Catalog

#evolutionary-biology #speciation #subspecies

## Executive Summary

This March 1957 issue of [[Turtox News]] contains three items. The main article is a reply by [[W. L. Brown Jr.]] of [[Harvard University]] to [[J. G. Edwards]]' earlier writing on subspecies. Brown argues that Edwards misuses the terms "intrinsic" and "extrinsic" and that his temporal and ecological "subspecies" are better understood as [[Sibling Species]] separated by intrinsic, genetically controlled barriers. The remaining two pages are laboratory supply advertisements for [[Plant Hormones]] and [[Colchicine]] and for the [[Hausman Collecting Sack]]. The document contains no fish data, and no fish species, habitats or aquatic field observations are reported.

## Background & Description

The issue is a periodical from the General Biological Supply House, Inc., Chicago, copyright 1957, with the cover theme "Termites of the Sea" (cited as page 93, not included in this text). The featured article runs from page 82 to page 83 and is titled "Intrinsic and Extrinsic Factors in Animal Speciation: A Reply."

Brown defines the core terms. "Intrinsic" refers to factors under genetic control, and "extrinsic" refers to other factors. He acknowledges that the two are inseparable in producing a phenotype. He notes that genetics relies on a working assumption: when genotypes are held constant and environments are changed, differences are attributed to extrinsic causes.

Edwards, writing in Turtox News (1956a, pp. 200-202), defines subspecies as "recognizably different demes" that would interbreed if they met, but that are kept apart during mating. Edwards calls this extrinsic isolation. He recognizes temporal (allochronic) and spatial isolation, the latter including macrogeographic and microgeographic (ecological) situations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The article is about speciation theory, not field ecology, but it touches on several biological settings:

- **Sympatric populations** with different breeding seasons (temporal isolation). Brown argues that the triggers for breeding are internal to the organism, so such differences are intrinsic.
- **Ecological or microgeographic isolation**, where populations are restricted to different niches. Brown argues that organisms' own constitutions drive niche restriction, and that no natural force sorts interbreeding populations by habitat while ignoring those intrinsic responses.
- **Food-plant strains** of insects. Brown notes that many such strains in [[Chrysomelid Beetles]] are distinct sibling species.
- **Macrogeographic isolation** by physiographic or biotic barriers, or by distance. Brown accepts that these create extrinsic isolation but disputes that such populations should be called subspecies.

## Key Findings

**1. Brown's critique of Edwards's definitions**
- Edwards's definition of subspecies is "basically rhetorical" and lacks supporting examples from nature.
- Edwards misunderstands and misapplies "intrinsic" and "extrinsic." Brown argues that Edwards's temporal and ecological subspecies are probably sibling species separated by intrinsic factors.
- Brown says Edwards has offered no well-chosen, carefully interpreted examples for temporal or ecological subspecies. He states that Edwards's case would need such examples to be convincing.

**2. Points of agreement and disagreement**
- Brown agrees that macrogeographic populations can be isolated by purely extrinsic factors such as barriers or distance.
- Brown disagrees that these populations should be called "subspecies." He considers the subspecies concept to have "scant value" in studying geographical variation and says adherence to it has "blocked advance" in understanding speciation as a process.
- Brown says Edwards's macrogeographic subspecies matches the conventional race described by [[Ernst Mayr]] and most recent authors.

**3. Literature cited**
- The reply cites six works: Brown and Wilson (1954, "The case against the trinomen"); Edwards (1954, "A new approach to infraspecific categories"); Edwards (1956a, Turtox News); Edwards (1956b, Systematic Zoology); Mayr (1954, "Points of view"); and Wilson and Brown (1953, "The subspecies concept and its taxonomic application").
- In the text, Brown cites "Brown and Wilson, 1955," but the reference list gives 1954.
