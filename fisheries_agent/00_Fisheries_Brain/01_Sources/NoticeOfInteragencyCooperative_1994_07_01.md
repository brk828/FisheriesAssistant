# Notice of Interagency Cooperative Policy for Peer Review in Endangered Species Act Activities

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #endangered-species-act #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific research paper** on fish morphology or ecology; it is a **1994 Federal Register regulatory notice** jointly issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] (NOAA). It establishes **interagency policy governing peer review standards** for scientific and commercial data used in decisions made under the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]] (ESA). The policy applies Servicewide to all species of fish, wildlife, and plants as defined under Section 3 of the Act (16 U.S.C. 1532), and governs listing determinations, recovery planning, critical habitat designation, and Section 7 consultations. While it contains no direct morphological or ecological species data, it is foundational to how scientific data on threatened and endangered fish species (e.g., [[Gila Chub]], [[Pacific Salmon]], and other listed taxa) are vetted, validated, and incorporated into regulatory decision-making.

## Background & Description

Not applicable. This document is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not describe morphological traits, taxonomy, or physical characteristics of any specific fish species. It references "taxonomy" only in the context of requiring independent peer review of **taxonomic assumptions** used in listing decisions (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Not applicable in the traditional sense of habitat or niche description for a single species. However, the policy explicitly emphasizes an **ecosystem-based conservation approach**, directing the Services to:

- Restore, reconstruct, or rehabilitate the **structure, distribution, connectivity, and function** of ecosystems upon which listed species depend.
- Develop recovery plans for **entire communities of species** occupying shared ecosystems, rather than a strict single-species approach.
- Integrate [[Ecosystem-Based Conservation]] mandates with other environmental statutes, including the [[National Environmental Policy Act]], [[Clean Water Act]], [[Clean Air Act]], the [[Magnuson-Stevens Fishery Conservation and Management Act]], and the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]].
- Prioritize genetic resources, species populations, biological communities, and ecological processes via adaptive management strategies.

This ecosystem-level framing is directly relevant to aquatic and marine species whose recovery depends on watershed, riverine, or marine habitat integrity rather than isolated population management.

## Key Findings

- **Peer Review Requirement (Listing):** For any species under consideration for listing, the Services must solicit expert opinions from **three independent, qualified specialists** on taxonomy, population models, and supporting biological/ecological data.
- **Peer Review Requirement (Recovery Plans):** Independent peer review must be actively solicited during the development of draft [[Recovery Plans]], and all opinions/reports must be summarized and archived in the administrative record.
- **Special Independent Review:** A supplementary peer review process may be triggered when scientific disagreement is significant enough to warrant a 6-month extension of the statutory rulemaking period; findings must be documented and made available for public comment.
- **Best Available Data Standard:** Companion policy notices (found within the same Federal Register issue) require Service biologists to rely on the **"best scientific and commercial data available"** (16 U.S.C. 1533), drawing from status surveys, biological assessments, gray literature, and peer-reviewed journals — with explicit evaluation of data reliability and credibility.
- **State Agency Collaboration:** A related interagency policy (Section 6 of the ESA) formalizes cooperation with **State agencies**, recognizing their expertise in population status inventories, geographic distribution surveys, and habitat management, particularly valuable for regionally endemic or range-limited species such as many freshwater and anadromous fishes.
- **Section 9 Prohibitions Policy:** A companion notice clarifies that, at the time of listing, the Services will identify activities likely (or unlikely) to constitute a "take" violation under [[Section 9 of the Endangered Species Act]], directly affecting management of commercial, recreational, and habitat-modifying activities affecting fish species.
- **Ecosystem Approach Policy:** Introduces a shift from species-by-species management toward multi-species and ecosystem-level recovery planning, with direct relevance to multi-species fish assemblages sharing degraded aquatic habitats.
- **Scope:** All policies apply Servicewide to fish, wildlife, and plant species as defined under the [[Endangered Species Act]], effective July 1, 1994.
