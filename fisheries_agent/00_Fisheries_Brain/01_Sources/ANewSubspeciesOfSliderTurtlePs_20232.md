# A New Subspecies of Slider Turtle (*Pseudemys scripta taylori*) from Coahuila, Mexico

#herpetology #chelonian #pseudemys

## Executive Summary

John M. Legler describes a previously unrecognized subspecies of slider turtle, [[Pseudemys Scripta Taylori]] new subspecies, from the basin of [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], [[Coahuila]], Mexico. The turtles were collected in September 1958 from small ponds and the [[Rio Chiquito]]. The subspecies is named for [[Edward Harrison Taylor]], who contributed more than anyone else to knowledge of Mexican herpetofauna. The author diagnoses it against its closest relative, [[Pseudemys Scripta Elegans]], and against the wider [[Pseudemys Scripta]] complex in Mexico. He concludes that the Mexican subspecies are members of a single polytypic species.

## Background & Description

The study is published in the University of Kansas Publications, Museum of Natural History (Vol. 13, No. 3, pp. 73–84, 1960). The author is [[John M Legler]], and the material is housed in the [[University Of Kansas Museum Of Natural History]].

- **Type series:** The [[Holotype]] is KU 46952, an adult female collected 6 September 1958, 16 km S of Cuatro Ciénegas. The author lists 52 paratypes from several localities, including the Rio Chiquito and sites 6 mi W and 8.5 mi SW of Cuatro Ciénegas.
- **Diagnosis:** The subspecies most closely resembles *P. s. elegans*. It differs in five characters:
  1. An extensive black [[Plastron]] pattern, with all parts interconnected and covering about half of the plastron.
  2. A tendency toward [[Melanism]] in large adults of both sexes, especially on the posterior plastron.
  3. A coarsely serrate lower jaw cutting edge.
  4. Femoral plastral edges reflected ventrally, especially in males.
  5. A pectoral scute longer than the gular scute.
- **Shell and head:** The carapace is oval, deep and highly arched. Shell height is 53% of width in the holotype. Head markings include a red postorbital mark about 4–5 mm wide and about 26 mm long.
- **Size:** The largest female measures 218 mm and the largest male 179 mm. Taylor's slider is probably smaller on average than *elegans* or northern *ornata*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic range.** *P. s. taylori* is known only from ponds and the Rio Chiquito in the Cuatro Ciénegas basin. With this discovery, Mexico has six valid *scripta* subspecies (*elegans*, *gaigeae*, *hiltoni*, *nebulosa*, *ornata*, *taylori*). Coahuila has three (*elegans*, *gaigeae*, *taylori*).

**Habitat and natural history.** Specimens were caught in hoop nets in clear, deep pools and in the Rio Chiquito. None were collected or observed in marshy habitats. The Rio Chiquito is also the river through which the basin drains; specimens from a point 8 mi W of Nadadores (2100 ft) show mixed characters.

**Intergradation zone.** Three specimens from the Rio Chiquito near Nadadores resemble *taylori* in some features but resemble *elegans* in others, including weak serration and non-reflected femoral edges. The author suggests that parts of the [[Rio Salado]] drainage north and east of Cuatro Ciénegas form an [[Intergradation]] zone between *taylori* and *elegans*.

**Taxonomic context.**
- The author rejects *cataspila* as an invalid name, following Williams (1956).
- Specimens previously reported as *P. s. gaigeae* from central Coahuila were re-identified as *Pseudemys floridana texana*.
- Populations in central eastern Coahuila likely consist of only two subspecies, *elegans* and *taylori*.
- The [[Gaigeae Group]] (*gaigeae*, *hiltoni*, *nebulosa*) is distinguished from *taylori* by its yellow or orange postorbital mark, narrower or brownish plastral pattern, and nearly smooth lower jaw.

## Key Findings

- **Diagnostic characters:** *P. s. taylori* differs from *P. s. elegans* in the plastral pattern, carapace markings, mandible serration, male foreclaw form, gular-to-pectoral length ratio, shell height and posterior plastral lobe width, and reflection of the posterior plastral edges.
- **Gular vs. pectoral length:** The gular scute is shorter than the pectoral in 91% of *taylori* specimens. In *elegans*, the gular is longer than the pectoral in 90% of specimens and longer than the femoral in all specimens.
- **Variation within the type series:** The postorbital mark contacts the eye on one or both sides in 46% of specimens. It contacts a neck stripe in 35%.
- **Morphometric comparison (Fig. 3):** Data for 62 *taylori* specimens and 37 *elegans* specimens show that *taylori* has a relatively higher shell and a narrower posterior plastral lobe. The author links the higher, deeper bridge of *taylori* to a slightly higher center of gravity.
- **Comparison with *ornata*:** Four *P. s. ornata* from the Soto la Marina drainage in Tamaulipas differ from *taylori* in having a diffuse, brownish plastral pattern, a gular longer than the pectoral, and a weakly serrate jaw. Their postorbital stripe is also connected to the neck stripe.
- **Colour in life:** The postorbital mark is red in living specimens. Other head markings are cream to buffy yellow. Previous colour records for *ornata* were based on preserved specimens and are unreliable.
- **Conclusions:** The author concludes that the six Mexican subspecies are, beyond reasonable doubt, members of a single polytypic species, *Pseudemys scripta*. He states that further collecting in the Rio Salado and its tributaries is needed to determine the exact range of *P. s. taylori*.
