# Disease Resistance in Atlantic Salmon Selected for High or Low Stress Response

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This study tested whether [[Atlantic Salmon]] lines selected for high or low post-confinement [[Cortisol]] stress response differ in resistance to three bacterial pathogens of farmed salmon. Fish from the second generation of the selection lines, reared at [[AKVAFORSK]] in Sunndalsøra, were challenged at Vikan AkvaVet with [[Aeromonas salmonicida]] (furunculosis), [[Vibrio salmonicida]] (cold-water vibriosis) and [[Renibacterium salmoninarum]] (bacterial kidney disease, BKD). The high-stress line had significantly higher mortality in the furunculosis and cold-water vibriosis challenges, no significant difference in the BKD challenge, and lower serum haemolytic activity. Because all fish had been previously exposed to furunculosis bacteria, the results are confounded and are reported as preliminary.

## Background & Description

- **Selection programme:** Stress-response selection lines of Atlantic salmon and [[Rainbow Trout]] were initiated in 1985. Selection is based on serum cortisol levels following short-term confinement stress, which is considered a measurable trait in live, healthy fish.
- **Rationale:** Stress is linked to disease susceptibility in farmed fish, and genetic variation in stress response has been found in salmonids. Selecting on stress response rather than specific immune traits was hypothesised to affect resistance to several diseases at once.
- **Study fish:** About 1,500 fish per line were fin-clipped in late November 1990. At 11 months old, mean weight was 37.7 ± 12.8 g (mean ± s.d.). Fish were pooled, divided into three groups, and held in freshwater as presmolts.
- **Pathogens:** All three agents have caused serious economic losses in the Norwegian salmon farming industry.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature management:** Challenge tanks were held at 12 °C for the furunculosis test. For the vibriosis and BKD tests, temperature was lowered to 5.5 °C to limit a furunculosis outbreak in tanks holding previously exposed fish.
- **Transmission routes:** Furunculosis was spread by cohabitant challenge, with infected conspecifics injected intraperitoneally (i.p.) with 10⁴ CFU per fish. *V. salmonicida* does not infect through fresh water, so i.p. injection at 10⁵ CFU per fish was used instead of immersion. BKD was induced by i.p. injection of 10⁵ cells per fish.
- **Background infection:** Fish arriving at the facility had already been exposed to *A. salmonicida*. Isolates were recovered from fish that died before challenge began. Fish not used in the trials also developed furunculosis through natural water infection.
- **Incubation timing:** The second mortality peak in the furunculosis test, 4–5 days after the first, matched the typical furunculosis incubation period at 12 °C.

## Key Findings

- **Furunculosis (*A. salmonicida*):** Mortality was significantly higher in the high-stress line. Cumulative mortality test: χ² = 52.51, P < 0.001. Log-rank χ² = 71.61 and Wilcoxon χ² = 53.30, both P < 0.001. The difference appeared early in the experiment. The challenge tank started with 1,005 fish.
- **Cold-water vibriosis (*V. salmonicida*):** High-stress fish had poorer performance under double infection. Cumulative mortality χ² = 14.21 (P < 0.001); log-rank χ² = 10.17 (P = 0.001); Wilcoxon χ² = 6.63 (P = 0.001). Tank size at start of registration was 946 fish. Pre-challenge mortality (5.8% vs. 3.8%) was not significantly different (P = 0.148). A rapid rise in mortality occurred 11–12 days after injection, and high mortality persisted for 8–10 days. Mortality curves were complicated by recurrent furunculosis, so data were censored in the analysis.
- **BKD (*R. salmoninarum*):** No significant line difference was found. Cumulative mortality χ² = 0.003 (P = 0.957); log-rank χ² = 0.78 (P = 0.375); Wilcoxon χ² = 3.43 (P = 0.064). The tank had 814 fish at the start of registration. Before injection, 20% of fish (162 in total) had died, and pre-injection mortality was 27.9% in the high-stress line vs. 13.8% in the low-stress line (P < 0.001). Of 100 dead fish tested, 85% were positive for *A. salmonicida*, so the ultimate cause of death (BKD or furunculosis) could not be determined.
- **Serum haemolytic activity (parental generation):** Values were generally lower in the high-stress line. Differences were significant for spontaneous haemolytic activity (SH; F = 9.66, P = 0.002) and for SH after absorption of natural antibodies (SH_abs; F = 6.11, P = 0.014). The difference in complete haemolytic activity (CH) was not significant (F = 1.06, P = 0.304). Sample sizes were 138 (high-stress) and 252 (low-stress).
- **Interpretation:** The authors link SH_abs to the alternative complement pathway, which may be especially important in early furunculosis infection. This is speculative. The pattern matches earlier rainbow trout work.
- **Limitations:** All test fish had prior furunculosis exposure, which could have weakened or altered their immunity before the other challenges. The authors note that the study was not restarted with uninfected fish because all fish in the selection lines were already infected. Results are therefore described as "sufficiently promising to report progress."
- **Comparison with rainbow trout:** In earlier rainbow trout work, the high-stress line was more susceptible to furunculosis, but the low-stress line was more susceptible to vibriosis. Atlantic salmon results suggest the low-stress line is more resistant to furunculosis and, with reservations, to cold-water vibriosis. Overall, selection for stress response affects specific disease resistance in a pathogen-dependent way.
