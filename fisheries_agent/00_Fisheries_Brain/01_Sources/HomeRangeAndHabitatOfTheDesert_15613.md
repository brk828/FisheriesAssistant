> **Note:** This source is a herpetological field study, not an ichthyological one. No fish species, fish habitat, or aquatic ecosystem data are present in this text. The note below faithfully represents the actual taxon and ecosystem covered: the desert tortoise (*Xerobates agassizi*) and its terrestrial desert habitat in the Picacho Mountains, Arizona.

## Executive Summary

This 1990 *Herpetologica* study by Sheryl L. Barrett examined home range size and seasonal habitat use of 14 adult desert tortoises (*[[Xerobates agassizi]]*, syn. *Gopherus agassizii*) via radio telemetry in the Picacho Mountains, Pinal County, Arizona, from April 1982 to September 1983. Tortoises restricted movements to palo verde-cacti-mixed scrub vegetation on rocky slopes, bajadas, and associated washes, with an average corrected home range of 19.07 ha (range 3–53 ha). No significant difference in home range size was found between sexes or in correlation with carapace length or number of observations. Seasonal habitat shifts included movement to steeper slopes in winter and use of larger dens in summer. Findings corroborated prior Sonoran Desert observations but contrasted sharply with habitat-use patterns documented in the Mohave Desert, supporting broader phylogeographic and morphometric distinctions between these regional tortoise populations.

## Background & Description

- **Carapace length:** Measured to nearest 1 mm via calipers; sampled individuals ranged approximately 213–274 mm (Table 1).
- **Sexual identification:** Based on external morphology allowing differentiation of 5 males and 9 females among the 14 monitored tortoises.
- **Hatchlings:** Five hatchling tortoises documented, measuring 40–60 mm.
- **Eggs:** Three eggs found during the study — one exposed in a wash (18 July 1983) and two within den openings in washes (25 August 1983).
- **Radio transmitter attachment:** 164 MHz Telonics transmitters (depth 0.6 cm, width 1.9 cm, length 2.3 cm, mass 27 g) epoxied to carapace below the highest point; placement varied by sex (anterior on females to avoid mating interference; posterior on males) to reduce interference with natural behaviors and den access.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Exclusively observed in palo verde-cacti-mixed scrub vegetation of the [[Sonoran Desertscrub]] biotic community, occupying rocky slopes, bajadas, and associated washes (dry stream beds).
- **Elevation range:** 538–719 m; no significant seasonal difference in elevation use (P > 0.05).
- **Slope use (seasonal):**
  - Winter: significant avoidance of gentle slopes (≤20%; P < 0.05); significant preference for steep slopes of 41–60% (P < 0.01) and 61–80% (P < 0.005).
  - Spring: significant preference for slopes of 41–60% (P < 0.025).
- **Den ecology:**
  - Tortoises used an average of 7.6 dens (± 0.63) per individual, with reuse of previously occupied dens.
  - Den types: wash cavities (caliche erosion or excavation), rock-pile crevices, depressions beneath rocks/shrubs, and soil excavations.
  - Den size varied significantly by season (P < 0.05): largest in summer (162.18 ± 6.43 cm) vs. autumn (105.67 ± 6.43 cm), winter (108.59 ± 8.70 cm), and spring (126.11 ± 9.72 cm).
  - Larger summer dens likely provide thermal protection and reduce evaporative water loss.
- **Reproductive habitat:** All eggs, hatchlings, and eggshell fragments were found specifically in washes, often at den entrances — highlighting washes as critical nesting substrate (friable caliche and sandy soils).
- **Comparative biogeography:** Contrasts with the [[Mohave Desert]] population, where tortoises winter in deep permanent wash dens and summer in creosote bush (*Larrea tridentata*) hummocks — a reversal of the Sonoran Desert pattern, attributed to colder Mohave winters requiring deeper dens to avoid freezing.
- **Excluded habitat:** Radio-tracked tortoises were never observed in the lower-elevation creosote bush-bursage series, despite its adjacency to palo verde-cacti-mixed scrub.

## Key Findings

- **Home range size:** Mean corrected home range = 19.07 ± 4.63 ha (range 3–53 ha); comparable to Mohave Desert populations in Ivanpah Valley, California (22 ha and 19 ha reported in prior studies).
- **No sex-based difference:** Males (25.80 ± 8.24 ha) vs. females (15.33 ± 5.52 ha) did not differ significantly (P > 0.05, t = 1.090, df = 12).
- **No size correlation:** Home range size uncorrelated with carapace length (r = –0.032) or number of observations (r = –0.097).
- **Seasonal den-size variation:** Statistically significant (P < 0.05, F = 4.16, df = 3,51), with summer dens largest, aligning with thermoregulatory and water-conservation needs.
- **Seasonal slope-use variation:** Statistically significant shift to steeper terrain in winter, interpreted as a strategy to avoid cold-air "thermal sinks" in milder Sonoran Desert winters (as opposed to deep dens used in the colder Mohave Desert).
- **Regional habitat divergence:** Habitat use patterns in the Sonoran Desert Picacho population diverge substantially from Mohave Desert populations, reinforcing genetic (mitochondrial DNA; [[Lamb Et Al. 1989]]) and morphometric ([[Weinstein And Berry 1988]]) evidence of phylogeographic structuring within *Xerobates agassizi*.
- **Methodological note:** Home range was estimated using minimum convex polygon method with a sample-size bias correction (Jennrich and Turner 1969 formula), important for standardizing telemetry-based home range comparisons across studies.
