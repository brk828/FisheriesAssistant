# Percy Viosca, Jr. (1892–1961)

## Executive Summary
This document is an obituary/tribute published in *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* (Vol. 91, No. 1, January 1962, p. 132), written by Gordon Gunter, honoring naturalist **Percy Viosca, Jr.** Though not a primary research paper presenting new species data, it is a valuable historical record documenting Viosca's broad contributions to Southern U.S. natural history, ichthyology, herpetology, and warm-water aquaculture in Louisiana between the early 1900s and 1961. The note catalogs his major fields of study and legacy rather than specific quantitative findings.

## Background & Description
- **Subject:** Percy Viosca, Jr., born 24 June 1892 in New Orleans, Louisiana; died 27 August 1961 in the same city.
- **Education:** Earned both Bachelor's and Master's degrees from [[Tulane University]], regarded as its most outstanding biologist.
- **Career path:** Briefly taught at Tulane but preferred fieldwork over academic routine. Became biologist for the [[Louisiana Wild Life and Fisheries Commission]] and its predecessor agencies, cycling in and out of the position four times (1916–1961) due to state politics.
- **Entrepreneurial ventures:**
  - Founded the [[Southern Biological Supply House]], prioritizing rare live specimen collection over profitable operations.
  - Assisted in establishing Japanese [[Bullfrog Culture]] techniques, which eventually became commercially competitive with his own efforts.
  - Co-founded (with Mrs. Viosca) the **garfish scale jewelry** industry in the United States, an early example of applied use of fish byproducts.
- **Society membership:** Active member of the [[American Fisheries Society]] for forty-two years.
- **Legacy comparison:** Described as following in the tradition of Creole naturalists, in the lineage of [[John James Audubon]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Viosca's work spanned a remarkably broad cross-section of Louisiana's aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems, reflecting an integrative, ecosystem-level approach uncommon for his era:

- **Aquatic/Fisheries Topics:**
  - [[Warm-Water Pond Culture]] of fishes and crawfish (early foundational work later adopted by American aquaculturists).
  - Studies contributing to [[Fish Culture]] broadly — his ideas were cited even in later French aquaculture literature.
  - Estuarine mollusk biology.
  - Penaeid shrimp biology (relevant to Gulf of Mexico [[Penaeid Shrimp]] fisheries).
- **Herpetological Topics:**
  - Alligators and other reptiles.
  - Frogs and salamanders (amphibian diversity of Louisiana wetlands).
- **Botanical/Entomological Topics:**
  - Wild irises (notable enough that he claimed he could identify his Louisiana parish location by iris and garter snake species alone).
  - Monarch butterflies.
  - Mosquito biology (of public health and ecological relevance in Gulf Coast wetlands).
- **Habitats studied:** Louisiana marshes, swamps, and estuarine systems — core [[Gulf of Mexico]] coastal wetland ecosystems.

## Key Findings
Because this is a tribute/obituary rather than a data-driven research article, there are no quantitative findings, species counts, or measurements to extract. However, several qualitative "findings" about Viosca's scientific impact are notable:

- **Breadth of expertise:** Viosca possessed exceptional integrated ecological knowledge of Louisiana's flora and fauna, spanning [[Ichthyology]], [[Herpetology]], entomology, and botany — an early example of a broad, ecosystem-based naturalist rather than a narrow specialist.
- **Foundational aquaculture influence:** His early papers in *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* set forth ideas that became central to modern **warm-water fish culture** practices, with citation influence extending internationally (French aquaculture literature).
- **Applied/commercial innovation:** Pioneered novel economic uses of fish byproducts (garfish scale jewelry) and contributed indirectly to the global spread of bullfrog aquaculture.
- **Science communication and education:** Played a significant informal teaching role — engaging children, the public, and journalists — helping popularize fisheries and wildlife science in the American South. This educational influence reportedly helped launch the careers of several biologists who went on to achieve international recognition.
- **Institutional contributions:** His long, though politically disrupted, tenure with the [[Louisiana Wild Life and Fisheries Commission]] underscores the instability historically faced by state conservation biologists, yet also his enduring commitment to Louisiana's natural resources.
