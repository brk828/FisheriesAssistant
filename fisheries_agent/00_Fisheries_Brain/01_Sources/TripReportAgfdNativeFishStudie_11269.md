# Trip 20 Report: AGFD Native Fish Studies (Grand Canyon)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #GrandCanyon #ColoradoRiver

## Executive Summary

This report documents the findings of **Trip 20** of the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) Native Fish Studies program, conducted **September 4–17, 1993**, along the Colorado River through Grand Canyon (Lee's Ferry to below Havasu Creek). The study focused on monitoring populations of the endangered **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) and other native and non-native fish species using seining, hoop nets, and opportunistic sampling across multiple river reaches and tributaries. A major finding was strong evidence of **active humpback chub spawning and recruitment in the Marble Canyon reach**, marked by the capture of over 2,000 chub (predominantly young-of-year, YOY) following a monsoon-driven flood pulse from the **[[Little Colorado River]] (LCR)**. Tag-recapture data spanning multiple years (1991–1993) provided growth and movement information for several individual fish.

## Background & Description

- **Young-of-Year (YOY) Humpback Chub**: Captured at sizes ranging from **24–46 mm**, with additional YOY individuals recorded at 25–41 mm in downstream reaches (mile 193.85R–203.80R).
- **Juvenile/subadult Humpback Chub**: Lengths in the tagged sample ranged from ~144–276 mm, weights 29–241 g.
- **Recaptured Humpback Chub** (multi-year growth records):
  - Fish tagged in 1991 (270 mm, 130 g) grew to 310 mm, 218 g by Sept. 1993 (~2 years).
  - Fish tagged in 1991 (193 mm, 66 g) showed incremental growth to 200 mm (1992) and 210 mm (1993).
  - One fish grew from 251 mm (Jul 1992) to 275 mm (Sept 1993) over four recorded recapture events, indicating relatively slow but steady growth.
- **Flannelmouth Sucker (FMS)**: Adult sizes recorded up to 550 mm and 1584 g (largest individual, mile 60.10R). Recaptured juveniles/adults ranged 169–452 mm.
- **Bluehead Sucker (BHS)**: Adult individuals in the 238–277 mm range, 144–211 g, captured via hoop net at Havasu Creek mouth.
- One nearly moribund **humpback chub of 96 mm** was recovered from the mouth of Shinumo Creek.
- Several chub exhibited **upper caudal fin clips** without corresponding pelvic fin clips (ambiguous recapture marks), and two recaptured chub had **dorsal fin punches**.
- Some fish had attached **lernaeid copepod parasites (lerneae)** encrusted with travertine deposits, and dying YOY flannelmouth suckers were also observed with lerneae infestations at Kanab Creek.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Mainstem Habitat Conditions**: Colorado River flows during the trip ranged from **8,000–14,000 cfs**. Turbidity varied dramatically — 13–40 NTUs upstream of the LCR confluence, spiking to over **6,000 NTUs** at the LCR due to monsoon flooding, then declining to below 50 NTUs downstream as the sediment pulse dissipated.
- **Temperature Gradients**: Mainstem water temperature increased from 10.5°C to 14.5°C over the two-week trip; backwaters ranged 12–20°C. Tributary temperatures were comparatively warm: LCR 18.7°C, [[Shinumo Creek]] 18.4°C, [[Kanab Creek]] 14.8°C, and [[Havasu Creek]] 16.5°C.
- **Spawning and Recruitment Zone**: The reach between the **LCR and Bright Angel Creek** functioned as the primary nursery habitat for **[[Humpback Chub]]**, with YOY densities dropping sharply below Bright Angel Creek — supporting the hypothesis that the [[Little Colorado River]] is a core spawning aggregation site and that a monsoon flood pulse displaced large numbers of YOY into the mainstem.
- **Backwaters as Nursery Habitat**: Isolated and connected backwaters (e.g., mile 44.27L, 63.08L, Awatubi mile 58.23R) served as important rearing habitats for YOY of multiple native and non-native species, including chub, [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[Speckled Dace]].
- **Tributary Mouths as Refugia**: Hoop net sets at the mouths of [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]] captured adult suckers and occasional trout, suggesting these clearwater tributary confluences serve as thermal/turbidity refugia and staging areas for spawning-condition suckers (ripe/tuberculate bluehead suckers found at Havasu Creek).
- **Co-occurring Non-native Species**: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Fathead Minnow]], and [[Green Sunfish]] were all documented alongside native species, reflecting the mixed native/non-native fish community characteristic of the regulated Colorado River ecosystem below Glen Canyon Dam.

## Key Findings

1. **Strong evidence of humpback chub spawning in Marble Canyon**: YOY chub (27, 36, 41 mm) captured at backwater mile 44.27L confirm local reproduction beyond the LCR itself.
2. **Monsoon-driven dispersal event**: A large LCR flood pushed thousands of YOY chub into the mainstem Colorado River; over 2,000 chubs were caught and measured on this trip alone, mostly between the LCR and Bright Angel Creek.
3. **Longitudinal decline in chub abundance downstream**: Very few chub were captured below Bright Angel Creek, indicating limited downstream dispersal at the time of sampling (to be reassessed on Trip 21, Oct 23–Nov 12).
4. **Multi-year tag-recapture growth data**: Original tagging data show consistent, trackable growth trajectories for individual humpback chub and flannelmouth sucker over 1–2+ year intervals, useful for long-term growth rate modeling.
5. **Parasite loads observed**: Lernaeid copepods (with travertine encrustation) on chub, and heavy internal parasite loads (tapeworms, roundworms, encysted parasites) in a gravid female channel catfish with an empty stomach.
6. **Reproductive activity in native suckers**: Ripe female and tuberculate male [[Bluehead Sucker]] captured at the mouth of Havasu Creek indicate an active spawning population using this tributary confluence.
7. **Ambiguous mark data**: Fin-clip inconsistencies (caudal clips without matching pelvic clips) complicated recapture identification, highlighting a methodological challenge in the marking protocol.
8. **Preserved specimens for otolith/stomach analysis**: Nine humpback chub (24–98 mm) and several YOY flannelmouth/bluehead suckers were preserved for further laboratory diet and age-growth analysis.
