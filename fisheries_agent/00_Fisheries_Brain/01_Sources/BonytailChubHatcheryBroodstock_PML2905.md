# Bonytail Chub Hatchery Broodstock Development Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This undated-in-part planning document (dated May 1, 1996) from the Region 2 Southwest U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service office sets out a genetic conservation plan for the captive [[Bonytail Chub]] broodstock held at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (NFH) and Technology Center, New Mexico. The stated goal is to maintain and/or increase the genetic diversity of the hatchery stock. The plan covers verifying the origin of the founders, developing a mating strategy, establishing a genetic baseline, rearing and maintenance protocols, sperm cryopreservation, and alternate broodstock development. It also flags a central risk: the only wild population is in [[Lake Mohave]], where few individuals can be captured, and unmarked hatchery and wild fish may have been mixed.

The document is a working planning draft, not a research report. Several sections contain open questions, and parts of the OCR text are garbled, so some figures are reconstructed with caution (noted below).

## Background & Description

The bonytail is a large-bodied cyprinid of the Colorado River system. The document treats it as a federally managed species under a recovery program, with links to [[Recovery Goals]] and [[Section 7 Consultation]] requirements.

Captive broodstock history, as stated in the text:

- **Founders:** The original captive stock traces to wild fish (the "BTC F1 1981 yC" group). The document asks for verification of the collecting agency, date, location, method, number of founders contributing offspring, estimated age, and whether voucher fish or tissues were preserved (museum collection status is unresolved).
- **1991 Lake Mohave spawning:** Wild fish from Lake Mohave, 5 females and 5 males, were spawned to produce F1 offspring. These were transported to Dexter NFH.
- **Stocking:** Several thousand fish were stocked from Dexter back into Lake Mohave. These fish were not marked.
- **Current stock:** Several thousand remained at Dexter to form the current broodstock, of which **374** fish remain. All recent stockings are believed to derive from this captive Lake Mohave stock and to be F1 offspring of the 1981 founders.
- **Return of stock:** The plan calls for returning the 1991 BTC F1 broodstock from Arizona to Dexter.
- **Additional fish:** Dexter also holds four individuals captured from Lake Mohave (year garbled in OCR). Their origin (wild, hatchery, or hatchery-derived) is unknown.

Key data gaps identified include the number of generations in captivity, the effective population size of the source stock, and the mating and rearing history of each derivative broodstock.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Wild status:** [[Lake Mohave]] is the only location where bonytail can still be captured with any regularity. The wild population size and distribution are unknown. Searches in spring (April/May) usually yield only a few specimens.
- **Identification problem:** Fish captured from Lake Mohave that are unmarked may be wild, hatchery-origin, or hatchery-derived. The document asks whether individuals and full-sibling groups can be distinguished genetically, and whether wild fish can be told apart from hatchery fish.
- **Hatchery environment:** Rearing is managed at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Arizona). Water quality monitoring, diet, and growth and behavioral monitoring are listed as maintenance requirements. Fish health monitoring includes tracking physical anomalies.
- **Reintroduction:** The Upper Colorado River Basin is identified as a potential reintroduction area. The text notes competing demands from upper basin research and reintroduction requirements.

## Key Findings

**1. Genetic conservation objectives**
- Maintain and/or increase genetic diversity of the hatchery broodstock.
- Obtain F2 offspring from every individual broodfish to develop a new broodstock.
- Verify the identity and origin of the 1981 founders and the number of generations in captivity.

**2. Genetic baseline and monitoring**
- Develop a genetic profile of each broodfish at Dexter using allozymes, nuclear DNA, and mitochondrial DNA (the latter "if necessary").
- PIT-tag every individual for identification.
- Collect tissues for genetic analysis and gene banking.
- Track genetic monitoring throughout the program.

**3. Mating strategy**
- Use pedigreed or factorial paired matings, with family lots reared and held separately to maintain their identity.
- Proposed paired-mating schedule: 30 paired matings in each of five annual cohorts. The OCR text is partly garbled; the total is reconstructed as 150 pairs (about 300 adults) from these five 30-pair sets.
- Retain 10 adults per family lot (five males and five females) at first maturity, PIT-tagged before spawning.
- Spawn where possible and evaluate alternatives. Crosses considered include Mohave x Mohave and Mohave x Dexter.

**4. Capture and collection protocol for wild fish**
- Develop a collection and transport protocol for fish from Lake Mohave to Willow Beach NFH.
- At initial handling: recover fish, PIT-tag and photograph them, collect tissues for genetic analysis and tissue culture, and collect sperm for short- and long-term storage.
- Whether wild fish should be introduced into the captive program (and at what frequency or percentage of the adult population) is an open decision.

**5. Sperm cryopreservation and tissue banking**
- Develop short- and long-term cryopreservation methods.
- Bank sperm and somatic tissue, and maintain a database.

**6. Planning and decisions still required**
- Whether wild Lake Mohave fish are currently held in captivity elsewhere.
- Scheduling of future Lake Mohave searches.
- Current capture, transport, maintenance, and breeding protocols.
- Whether to add wild fish to the broodstock program, and how often.
- Funding, budget, cost, roles, and responsibilities. Partner agencies include Willow Beach NFH, the Arizona Fish and Wildlife Office, Arizona State University, and others. The document also lists the need for a strategic and tactical plan, work plans, and solicitation of proposals.

**7. Core constraint**
The document states that genetic conservation goals must be balanced against the current knowledge base, the production schedule the fish impose, facilities, technology, personnel, and competing requests. These include upper basin research and reintroduction needs.
