# A Systematic Study of Cyprinid Fishes of the Genus Gila in North America

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #systematic-revision #North-American-fish

## Executive Summary

This document represents a comprehensive systematic revision of the cyprinid fish genus [[Gila]] in [[North America]], focusing on populations in the western [[United States]] and [[Mexico]]. Authors [[Robert Rush Miller]] and [[Teruya Uyeno]] undertake a taxonomic clarification of a genus that has experienced considerable nomenclatural confusion, having been assigned no fewer than 14 generic names by different investigators over the past century. The study examines species and subspecies across major drainage systems including the [[Colorado River]], [[Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]], [[Gila River]], and numerous Mexican river systems on both sides of the [[Continental Divide]]. The work addresses marked variations in meristic characters, body form, size, and coloration that have complicated classification, and proposes a systematic arrangement based on detailed morphological comparisons.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Gila]] (family [[Cyprinidae]]) exhibits extreme morphological diversity that has led to taxonomic instability since [[Baird and Girard]] first described *Gila robusta* from [[New Mexico]] in 1853. Early classification attempts were hampered by gross errors in locality data (e.g., [[Cope and Yarrow]], 1875), mixing of species from distinct drainage systems, and inadequate material that provided little appreciation of intraspecific variation or zoogeography. The phylogenetic position of [[Gila]] among American minnows and its relationship to Old World genera ([[Squalius]], [[Leuciscus]], [[Phoxinus]]) remains under investigation.

The nominal species chronologically described or recorded from the study area include: *G. robusta* [[Baird and Girard]] 1853a (Zuni River, New Mexico); *G. elegans* [[Baird and Girard]] 1853a (same locality); *G. gracilis* [[Baird and Girard]] 1853 (same locality, equals *G. robusta robusta*); *G. emoryi* [[Baird and Girard]] 1853b ([[Gila River]] near mouth, Arizona); *G. grahami* [[Baird and Girard]] 1853b ([[San Pedro River]], Arizona); *G. gibbosa* [[Baird and Girard]] 1854 ([[Santa Cruz River]], Sonora, Mexico, equals *G. robusta intermedia* as secondary homonym); *G. pulchella* [[Baird and Girard]] 1854 ([[Mimbres River]], New Mexico, equals *G. nigrescens* as secondary homonym); *Tigoma pulchra* [[Girard]] 1856 ([[Chihuahua River]], Mexico, valid species); *Tigoma intermedia* [[Girard]] 1856 ([[San Pedro River]], Arizona); *Tigoma purpurea* [[Girard]] 1856 ([[San Bernardino Creek]], Arizona, valid species).

Additional species described include *Clinostomus pandora* [[Cope]] 1871 ([[Rio Grande]], New Mexico); *Gila conspersa* [[Garman]] 1881 ([[Rio Nazas]], Mexico); *Cheonda modesta* [[Garman]] 1881 (Saltillo, Mexico); *Gila minacae* [[Meek]] 1904 ([[Rio Yaqui]], Mexico); and *Gila ditaenia* [[Miller]] 1945 ([[Rio Magdalena]], Mexico).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The distribution of [[Gila]] species reflects two primary faunal origins: populations that migrated from the north (originating from [[Colorado River]] and [[Rio Grande]] systems) and those with origins within Mexico. The [[Colorado River]] fauna includes *G. robusta*, *G. ditaenia*, *G. purpurea*, and *G. minacae*. The [[Rio Grande]] fauna includes *G. pandora*, *G. nigrescens*, *G. conspersa*, *G. modesta*, and *G. pulchra*.

*[[Gila robusta]]* is well distributed throughout the [[Colorado River]] system, characterized by very fine scales, slender and strong caudal peduncle, almost no basal radii on scales, and many head sensory pores. In Mexico, this species inhabits most streams flowing into the [[Gulf of California]] and [[Pacific Ocean]] as far south as [[Rio Culiacan]] and [[Rio Sinaloa]]. The fish apparently dispersed through the [[Gulf of California]] from the [[Colorado River]] when freshwater flow was much greater than present.

*[[Gila ditaenia]]* inhabits the [[Rio Magdalena]] system and is characterized by many clear basal radii and fine scales. *[[Gila purpurea]]* is distributed through [[Rio Sonora]] and northern headwaters of [[Rio Yaqui]], related to *G. ditaenia* but with coarser scales along the lateral line.

The [[Rio Grande]] fauna shows distinct characteristics. [[Rio Grande]] populations typically have 9 pelvic rays and fewer sensory pores on the head, while [[Pecos River]] populations have 8 pelvic rays and more head pores. *[[Gila pandora]]* inhabits the [[Rio Grande]] and [[Rio Conchos]] systems.

Stream capture events have significantly influenced distribution patterns. [[Meek]] stated that portions of western rivers east of the [[Sierra Madre Mountains]] were formerly upper tributaries of eastward-flowing streams. Western streams cut back, captured headwaters of eastern streams, and acquired their fish fauna. [[Rio Casas Grande]], [[Rio Santa Maria]], and [[Rio Carmen]] are former tributaries to [[Rio Grande]] that now reach the sea independently. *[[Gila nigrescens]]* from these rivers shows morphological characters intermediate between *G. robusta* of [[Colorado River]] and *G. pandora* of [[Rio Grande]].

[[Laguna Bustillos]], south of [[Rio Carmen]], contains [[Gila]] very similar to [[Rio Casas Grande]] populations except for fewer lateral line scales, fewer head pores, and fewer vertebrae. [[Rio Nazas]], also a former [[Rio Grande]] tributary, contains *[[Gila conspersa]]* with variable tooth formula: approximately 60% have 2,4-4,2 teeth, others have 2,5-4,2; 1,4-4,1; or 2,4-4,1. This species has 8 pelvic rays and basal radii on scales.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Arrangement**: The study recognizes multiple species and subspecies of [[Gila]], including: *G. atraria* (with subspecies *atraria* and *ursilacus* ssp. nov.); *G. bicolor*; *G. crassicauda*; *G. robusta* (with subspecies *intermedia*, *robusta*, and *elegans*); *G. nigrescens*; *G. pandora* (with subspecies *pandora* and *pecosensis* ssp. nov.); *G. orcutti*; *G. ditaenia*; *G. conspersa*; *G. modesta*; and *G. purpurea*.

**Pharyngeal Teeth**: Three groups exist with different tooth formulae: (1) 2,5-5,2 teeth (primary formula for genus [[Gila]]), inhabiting [[Colorado River]], [[Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]], and numerous Mexican systems; (2) 1,4-4,1 teeth, living in [[Rio Conchos]] system and headwaters of [[Rio Yaqui]] and [[Rio Fuerte]]; (3) variable formula with 2,4-4,2 and 1,4-4,1 teeth (occasionally 1,4-4,2 or 2,4-4,1), inhabiting [[Rio Nazas]] system and [[El Salto Creek]]. In the [[Nazas]] group, tooth formula ratio is: 2,4-4,2 (40%); 1,4-4,1 (40%); others (20%).

**Fin Ray Counts**: Four combinations of dorsal-anal-pelvic fin rays are recognized: 10,10-10,10 (*G. robusta elegans* from [[Colorado River]]); 9,9-9,9 (*G. robusta robusta* from [[Colorado River]], [[Yaqui]], [[Fuerte]], [[Culiacan]], [[Sinaloa]]); 9,9-9,8 ([[Casas Grande]], [[Santa Maria]], [[Carmen]], [[Laguna Bustillos]]); 8,9-9,8 ([[Conchos]], [[Papigochic]], [[Verde]], [[Rio Grande]], [[Metzquital]], [[Laguna de Santiaguillo]]); 8,8-8,8 ([[Nazas]], [[Parras]], [[Saltillos]], [[Pecos River]]). Pelvic ray number effectively separates [[Rio Grande]] populations (always 9-9) from [[Pecos River]] populations (always 8-8 or occasionally 8-9).

**Lateral Line Scales and Sensory Pores**: Strong correlation exists between number of lateral line scales and sensory pores on head, useful for distinguishing forms. Three scale size groups: larger scale (55-67 scales: [[Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]], [[Saltillos]]); intermediate scale (60-70 scales: [[Laguna Bustillos]], [[Metzquital]], [[Laguna de Santiaguillo]], [[Nazas]], [[Conchos]], [[Papigochic]], [[Verde]]); finer scale (68-80 scales: [[Casas Grande]], [[Santa Maria]], [[Carmen]], [[Parras]], [[El Salto]]).

**Basal Radii on Scales**: Three categories identified: (1) many distinctive basal radii (*G. purpurea*, *G. ditaenia*); (2) no basal radii, very rarely faint ones ([[Laguna de Santiaguillo]], [[Casas Grande]], [[Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]], [[Laguna Bustillos]], [[Metzquital]], [[Nazas]], [[El Salto Creek]], *G. robusta* except *intermedia*); (3) faint or weak basal radii ([[Conchos]], [[Papigochic]], [[Verde]], [[Parras]] with some strong radii, [[Saltillos]], *G. robusta intermedia*). Regenerated scales show radii even in populations normally lacking them.

**Vertebrae**: Number fairly constant compared to lateral line scale variation. Range 39-44 in [[Rio Grande]] fauna, 38-45 in [[Colorado River]] fauna (*G. robusta elegans* has 49-51). Modal counts: 41 ([[Parras]], [[Pecos River]], [[Laguna Bustillos]], some [[Papigochic]], [[Nazas]], some [[Conchos]]); 42 ([[El Salto Creek]], some [[Conchos]], some [[Papigochic]], [[Rio Grande]], [[Carmen]], some [[Metzquital]], [[Laguna de Santiaguillo]]); 43 ([[Casas Grande]], [[Santa Maria]], some [[Metzquital]]). In *G. robusta* group: 41 (*intermedia*); 45 (*robusta* from [[Gila River]] system, *jordani*, [[Rio Choix]]); 46 (*robusta* from [[Green River]] system); 49 (*elegans*).

**Caudal Peduncle Depth**: [[Gila]] from [[Metzquital]] and [[Parras]] have very slender caudal peduncles (head length into least depth 2.7-3.4); populations from [[Rio Grande]], [[Pecos River]], [[Laguna Bustillos]], [[Conchos]], [[Papigochic]], and [[Verde]] have deep caudal peduncles (2.1-2.8); others intermediate.

**Hybridization and Intergradation**: The document notes that hybrids and intergrades occur among species of [[Gila]], though specific details are not fully elaborated in the provided text.

**Zoogeographic Implications**: Stream capture events have profoundly influenced current distribution patterns. Former [[Rio Grande]] tributaries ([[Casas Grande]], [[Santa Maria]], [[Carmen]]) now flow independently to the sea, carrying intermediate forms between [[Colorado River]] and [[Rio Grande]] faunas. One tributary of [[Rio Nazas]] originates near [[Rio Metzquital]], and populations show morphological similarities suggesting historical connections. The southern limit of [[Gila]] distribution is [[Rio Metzquital]] and [[Rio Panuco]] systems.

**Methodological Notes**: The study emphasizes that five or more specimens from a population are necessary for effective use of identification keys due to wide variation in characters. Scale characters, especially basal radii, prove effective for separating groups. The relationship between lateral line scale number and vertebrae number is not always correlated; for example, [[Parras]] populations have very fine scales (68-80) but vertebrae mode of 41.
