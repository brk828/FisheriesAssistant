# Warmouth (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*) in the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #freshwater-fish #range-extension

## Executive Summary
This short faunal note documents the first confirmed occurrence of the **Warmouth** (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*), a centrarchid native to eastern and central North America, in the **lower Colorado River** system along the California–Arizona border. The initial specimen was collected on July 31, 1961, near the **Palo Verde Diversion Dam** north of Blythe, California, with identification verified by ichthyologist **Carl L. Hubbs** of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography. Subsequent captures via angling and gill netting between 1963 and 1964 at multiple nearby localities (including sites on the Arizona side of the river) indicate that the species had become **established** in this reach of the river, though the vector or source of introduction remains **unknown**. This represents an early record of a non-native centrarchid colonizing the [[Lower Colorado River]] ecosystem, a system already known for hosting numerous introduced warmwater sportfish species.

- Size range across specimens: **11.2–19.0 cm total length**, **28–170 g** in weight.
- Age classes represented: primarily **Age Group I–II**, based on scale annuli, suggesting a young, recently-established population rather than long-term residency.
- Growth pattern (11.2 cm at Age I to ~15–19 cm at Age II) is broadly consistent with typical [[Centrarchidae]] growth rates in warm, productive lentic/lotic habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Slow-moving river channel margins, diversion lagoon outflows, and areas immediately below a diversion dam — habitats characteristic of warmwater, low-gradient river systems.
- **Specific localities**:
  - Directly below the **Palo Verde Diversion Dam** (10 mi north of Blythe, CA) — original capture site.
  - Near **26th Avenue County Park**, Blythe, CA — first angler-caught specimen.
  - **Intake Boulevard**, between 24th and 26th Avenues (5 mi south of Blythe) — two additional specimens.
  - Arizona side of the river, ~500 yards below the confluence of the **Palo Verde Lagoon** — two specimens taken in gill nets, indicating cross-boundary establishment.
- **Trophic/ecological role**: The [[Warmouth]] is a benthic, ambush-predator centrarchid typically associated with vegetated, sluggish backwaters; its presence suggests suitable structural habitat (cover, low flow, warm temperatures) exists within diversion-affected reaches of the Colorado River.
- **Community context**: Represents an addition to the non-native warmwater fish assemblage of the [[Lower Colorado River]], which historically has been heavily modified by damming and diversion structures (e.g., Palo Verde Diversion Dam), creating habitat conducive to eastern U.S. centrarchid species.

## Key Findings
- **First documented record** of Warmouth (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*) in the lower Colorado River, dated July 31, 1961 (collected by Shoken Sasaki, CDFG), with taxonomic verification by **Carl L. Hubbs**.
- **First angler-caught specimen** recorded March 20, 1963 by Warden Larry Redfern, also verified by Hubbs.
- Multiple subsequent captures (1963–1964) across several sites spanning both the California and Arizona banks of the river indicate a **reproducing, established population** rather than an isolated stray occurrence.
- **Source of introduction is unknown** — no stocking record or documented vector is cited, consistent with unauthorized or incidental introduction (e.g., bait-bucket transfer, aquarium release, or connection to irrigation/diversion infrastructure).
- Age and growth data (scale analysis) show individuals in **Age Groups I and II**, suggesting the population was relatively recently founded at the time of the report (early 1960s).
- This note exemplifies the broader pattern of **non-native centrarchid establishment** in the highly engineered [[Colorado River]] system, relevant to ongoing concerns about introduced species altering native fish community structure in the American Southwest.
