# Speocirolana Thermydronis (Crustacea: Isopoda) from Northeast Mexico: Re-discovery, Habitat and Redescription

#carcinology #isopoda #cirolanidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 taxonomic paper by [[Paul C. Marsh]] (as printed in the reprint header), [[W. L. Minckley]] and [[Gerald A. Cole]] of [[Arizona State University]] reports the re-discovery of the cave isopod [[Speocirolana Thermydronis]] in northeast Mexico. The authors describe its habitat and provide a supplemental description, with emphasis on the setation and mouthpart structures that separate it from related members of the genus [[Speocirolana]]. The paper is a revision of the original description rather than a fish study. Some scanned text is badly garbled, so specific locality names and several measurements cannot be reliably recovered.

## Background & Description

- **Publication:** Reprinted from *[[Tulane Studies In Zoology And Botany]]*, Vol. 15, No. 1, October 16, 1968. The editorial committee included [[Thomas E. Bowman]] (Smithsonian Institution, Division of Crustacea) and an associate professor of biology at Tulane University.
- **Taxonomic group:** An isopod crustacean in the family [[Cirolanidae]], order [[Isopoda]]. It is a troglobitic (cave-adapted) species.
- **Type material:** The type specimen was one of three individuals of similar size. The paper describes the type as having broad, acutely rounded body proportions and a small pleotelson-related area. Exact body measurements are not legible in the scan.
- **Supplemental description:** Features include sparsely plumose spines and two setae. The text notes a strong distal spine armament (described as 12 strong distal spines in one passage, though this is partly garbled). The [[Mandible]]s are asymmetrical. The exoskeleton is ivory-white in preserved material and turns yellow in alcohol.
- **Pleopod setation:** Both sexes lack distal setae on the endopods of the [[Pleopod]]s. Setae are present on the first pleopod in females and at least on the second in males. The authors note that the earlier tentative separation of this species from *[[Speocirolana Pelaezi]]* on this basis is not justified.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The species lives in a cave pool with a flocculent, sediment-covered bottom. Individuals were observed moving over and beneath sediment surfaces in the water.
- **Population variation:** The paper states that the length and number of certain setae in the genus *Speocirolana* may vary considerably, which complicates species-level separation.
- **Threats:** The authors report that diversion of water is a concern for this and other cave populations, and that remaining habitats are limited. They emphasize the importance of protecting the remaining cave-pool environments.
- **Regional context:** The species is compared with other subterranean cirolanids from Mexico, including congeners in the genus *Speocirolana*, and the paper cites earlier work on troglobitic fauna in Mexican caves (for example, work by [[Bolívar]] and [[Mexican Cave Fauna]] surveys).

## Key Findings

1. **Re-discovery:** *[[Speocirolana Thermydronis]]* is confirmed as a valid, re-found troglobitic isopod in northeast Mexico.
2. **Setation diagnosis:** The absence of distal endopod setae on the pleopods in both sexes is the key character that separates this species from *S. pelaezi*, overturning an earlier tentative distinction.
3. **Intraspecific variability:** Setal length and number can vary within *Speocirolana*, so these characters must be used cautiously in identification.
4. **Habitat specialization:** The species occupies a sediment-rich cave pool, a habitat that is vulnerable to water diversion.
5. **Conservation concern:** The authors highlight limited remaining habitat and recommend protecting it.
6. **Data limitations:** Many specimen counts, dimensions and locality details are illegible in the OCR text, so they are not reported here.
