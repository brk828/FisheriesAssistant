# Northern Pike Control at Lake Davis, California

#ichthyology #esocidae #invasive_species #eradication #fisheries_management

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary**: [[Northern Pike]] (Esox lucius) are listed as a "detrimental animal" under California Code of Regulations Title 14 Section 671. The species has a holarctic distribution, ranging from northwestern Europe across northern Asia to northern North America. In North America, they range from Alaska to Labrador, south through New England, New York, the Ohio Valley, Great Lakes region, and southward to Missouri and Nebraska. Northern pike were not historically present in California waters. First confirmed in California at [[Frenchman Reservoir]] in 1989, then at [[Lake Davis]] in 1994. The species is described as large, voracious, and fast-growing predatory fish. Young-of-year fish were collected in 1999, indicating successful reproduction in spring 1999. Multiple year classes were documented, suggesting establishment of a breeding population. Age, maturity, fecundity, and specific growth parameters not reported in this document.

**Habitat Associations**: [[Lake Davis]] is located in the [[Feather River]] drainage at elevation 5,775 feet m.s.l. within [[Plumas National Forest]]. The reservoir has surface area of 4,026 acres (1,619 ha), storage capacity 84,371 acre-feet, mean depth 20.5 feet (6.3 m), maximum depth 108 feet (33 m), and shoreline length 32 miles (52 km). Basin morphology is a flooded river valley with deep narrow channel and broad littoral zone. Extensive aquatic macrophyte growth occurs in nearshore areas, comprising up to 40% of lake area. Lake classified as meso-eutrophic based on growing season inorganic nitrogen concentration. Coldwater habitat restricted in hypolimnion and metalimnion in summer due to oxygen depletion. Stratification occurs mid-April to mid-May; destratification late September to early October. Water chemistry: pH 6.8–9, total dissolved solids 49–68 mg/L, total hardness 24–44 mg/L (soft). Annual ice cover March–April; water temperatures range from 5–7°C minimum to 17–18°C maximum surface temperatures (July–August), with maximum 23.5°C in August. Three main tributaries feed the reservoir: [[Big Grizzly Creek]], [[Freeman Creek]], and [[Cow Creek]]. Total drainage area approximately 44 square miles. Northern pike reported to be "very adaptable and can live in a whole host of conditions" according to Minnesota Department of Natural Resources expert.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration**: Northern pike utilize tributary streams for spawning. Physical barriers were recommended for tributaries during spring thaw to prevent spawning and establishment of stream populations. Spawning occurs following spring "ice out" in shallow areas and tributaries. Adult pike move into spawning areas at spring thaw, where block nets could trap spawning individuals. No detailed migration patterns or movement data reported.

**Trophic & Community Interactions**: Northern pike demonstrated severe predatory impact on [[Lake Davis]] fish community. By summer 1997, northern pike were common at the expense of the trout population. Available trout population was dramatically reduced by northern pike predation. Few remaining trout were larger fish, seventeen inches and longer. Northern pike "lived up to their reputation of being a large, voracious and fast-growing predatory fish that can readily destroy other fisheries." Alaska Department of Fish and Game expert stated that in systems with shallow lakes and ponds, "some of them (salmonid fisheries) have been completely devastated to the point where there are none left." Prey species include [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Brown Bullhead]]. Management plan recommended discontinuing stocking of fingerling trout (prey source for pike) and planting only larger catchable-sized trout. [[Brown Trout]] recommended for stocking to increase predation on smaller pike.

**Environmental Drivers**: Spring thaw and ice-out timing critical for spawning behavior and management interventions. Water temperature influences activity patterns. Ice cover (18-inch cap) during winter 1997–1998 eliminated sunlight, decreased water temperature, and restricted water movement, affecting chemical degradation rates. Shallow lake morphology and extensive macrophyte beds provide favorable habitat for pike establishment and reproduction.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory**: Northern pike first confirmed in California at [[Frenchman Reservoir]] in 1988 (7-pound individual captured). Chemical treatment of Frenchman Reservoir in 1991 with 15,000 gallons of Nusyn-noxfish (20,000 acre-feet treated) considered successful; subsequent sampling failed to capture any northern pike. First northern pike confirmed at [[Lake Davis]] in August 1994. By 1995, northern pike commonly caught by anglers and DFG personnel. Population expanded rapidly through 1997. Chemical treatment of Lake Davis conducted October 1997 using 64,000 pounds powdered rotenone and 16,000 gallons Nusyn-noxfish (50,000 acre-feet treated, target concentration 50 µg/L rotenone per acre-foot). Approximately 20 tons of dead fish removed following treatment; majority were northern pike. Treatment initially considered successful. However, in May 1999 (seventeen months post-treatment), northern pike rediscovered. Subsequent monitoring through summer 1999 collected two year classes including several hundred young-of-year fish, confirming reproduction in spring 1999. At least three fish species survived 1997 chemical treatment based on numbers and year classes collected 1998–1999: [[Golden Shiner]], [[Brown Bullhead]], and [[Pumpkinseed]]. Unknown whether northern pike survived treatment or were reintroduced afterward.

**Threat & Risk Assessment**: Northern pike pose severe threat to native and established fisheries in California. Species listed as "detrimental animal"; unlawful to import, transport, or possess alive except under permit. Risk of spread to other California waters documented: confirmed occurrences at [[Frenchman Reservoir]] (extirpated), [[Feather River]] (extirpated), [[Lake Davis]] (established), with unsubstantiated reports from [[Lake Oroville]] and [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. Minnesota expert stated "Minnesota experienced absolutely no success in angler control of northern pike populations." Illegal introductions by fishermen suspected as source. Threat to trout fishery and associated economic benefits to local communities. Risk of establishment in shallow lakes and ponds with potential for complete devastation of salmonid fisheries. Adaptability of pike fry to various conditions increases establishment risk.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations**: The February 2000 plan "Managing Northern Pike at Lake Davis: A Plan for Y2000" described 12 recommended containment and control actions (chemical treatment not included): (1) **Experimental control measures**: net barriers, electrofishing, detonation cord, and encircling nets in combination during spring/early summer to contain and remove adult, juvenile, and larval pike; (2) **Tributary barriers**: physical barriers during spring thaw to prevent spawning and stream population establishment; (3) **Spawning area blocking**: block nets at spring thaw to trap spawning pike for elimination; (4) **Food supply reduction**: discontinue fingerling trout stocking (prey source), plant only larger catchable-sized trout, include [[Brown Trout]] as predator on smaller pike; (5) **Encourage pike fishing**: organized fishing events and derbies to remove pike without promoting angler interest; (6) **Drag nets and purse seines**: following spring thaw, various nets fished for adult and juvenile pike to additively reduce population; (7) **Increased electrofishing**: following spring ice-out continuing to fall, in lake and tributaries; (8) **Electrofishing to herd pike**: monthly use to drive pike into nets for removal; (9) **Dam-related actions**: upstream containment barrier, electric barrier, modified discharge orifice, maintain lake level to avoid spill, retain fish grate at outflow wall, deploy aquatic exclusion systems; (10) **Fyke nets and trap nets**: following ice-out, set at various locations for additive removal; (11) **Increased enforcement**: contain pike in Lake Davis, improve DNA analysis as enforcement tool; (12) **Public education**: substantially increase public information efforts to motivate citizens to help contain and control pike. Plan included monitoring program to measure success and provide opportunities for informed course corrections. Director Hight assured community (October 7, 1999) that no Proposition 65 chemicals (carcinogenic or reproductive effects) or persistent harmful chemicals would be used, removing formulated rotenone as control option.

**Biological Reference Points**: Target rotenone concentration: 50 µg/L per acre-foot. Treatment volumes: Frenchman Reservoir 20,000 acre-feet (15,000 gallons Nusyn-noxfish); Lake Davis 50,000 acre-feet (64,000 pounds powdered rotenone + 16,000 gallons Nusyn-noxfish). Surface water temperature at October 1997 treatment: low 50s°F. Chemical degradation timeline: by late-November 1997, all chemicals degraded except piperonyl butoxide (PBO); by June 1998, almost all PBO traces gone; by late-July 1998, PBO and all chemicals undetectable; September 1998, California Department of Health Services deemed water safe to drink. Fish stocking: more than 2 million trout planted by end of 1998 season; approximately 1,200,000 trout stocked in alternative eastern Plumas County waters during treatment period. Minimum trout size remaining after pike predation: seventeen inches and longer.
