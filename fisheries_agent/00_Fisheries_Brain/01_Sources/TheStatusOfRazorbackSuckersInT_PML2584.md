# Status of Razorback Suckers in the Colorado River between Davis and Parker Dams (Lake Havasu), 2003 to 2005

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Bureau of Reclamation and U.S. Geological Survey technical memorandum (July 2006) reports the status of stocked [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in the [[Colorado River]] between [[Davis Dam]] and [[Parker Dam]], including [[Lake Havasu]]. Sampling from 2003 to 2005 used [[Trammel Net]] surveys, radio and sonic telemetry, [[Larval Light Trap]] sampling and visual boat counts. The authors describe the population as the most successful riverine reintroduction of razorback suckers in the Colorado River basin, and they report discovery of a large spawning aggregate near Needles, California. Their data indicate a declining population with no detectable natural recruitment.

## Background & Description

The study builds on earlier work by the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] and Bureau of Reclamation from 1999 to 2002, which captured 257 razorback suckers and one [[Bonytail]]. Since 1993, 31,516 razorback suckers and 31,480 bonytail chubs have been stocked into Lake Havasu and the river below Davis Dam as part of the [[Lake Havasu Fish Improvement Project]], a multiagency effort to augment or reestablish endangered fish. Stated goals were to maximize contacts with razorback suckers, improve population estimates, and identify spawning sites. River miles (RM) are measured upstream of Parker Dam (RM 0).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling sites:** Park Moabi, Blankenship Bend (California side), Clear Bay, Castle Rock, Catfish Cove, Laughlin Lagoon, Topock Marsh, Windsor Beach, Mesquite Bay and the Needles spawning reach (about RM 53–58).
- **Community composition:** Of 12,119 fish captured by trammel net (2003–2005), 96.2% (11,661) were non-native, from fifteen non-native species. The dominant species included [[Common Carp]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]].
- **Native species:** 458 native fish were captured, comprising 129 razorback suckers, 313 [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] and 16 bonytail.
- **Spawning habitat:** The Needles site had water about 1 m deep over large clean gravel and cobble. Some sections were deeper (about 2 m) with small cobble to sand.
- **Hydrology:** Water levels were very low through February 2005 because of rain and low irrigation demand.
- **Mortality and dispersal:** Reported adult mortality on Lake Mohave is nearly 25%. Recapture data suggest mostly downstream movement, with fish tending to stay near stocking locations.

## Key Findings

**Population estimates (Chapman modified single-census Petersen method):**
- 2003: 3,570 razorback suckers (95% CL 1,306–8,925)
- 2004: 1,768 (95% CL 878–3,867); 1,554 from fish stocked into Lake Havasu alone
- 2005: 1,652 (95% CL 706–5,164) including wild adults, stocked fish and escapees from Lake Mohave; 1,532 for Lake Havasu-stocked fish
- Estimated survival of stocked fish is roughly 5% overall (from 31,516 stocked). Return-based survival varied from 18–76% at Laughlin Lagoon, 12–19% at Topock Marsh and 5% at Windsor Beach. No fish released in lower Lake Havasu were recaptured.

**Three trends (1999–2005):**
1. CPUE declined steadily from 9.2 fish/1000 m² to 1.0 fish/1000 m² in 2005.
2. The sex ratio reversed: females outnumbered males (about 2:1 in 2000–2002 samples was reversed) and in 2003 the ratio shifted toward females, with males becoming difficult to find.
3. Most captured fish came from recent stockings, even though they were fewer in number.

**Spawning discovery:**
- In 2005, visual counts over a four-mile reach near Needles found 126 razorback suckers; 101 were in a one-mile stretch. Extrapolation gave an estimate of about 625 fish using the spawning area.
- A radio-tagged male led researchers to the site, where a spawning group of eight was seen with the transmitter fish.
- Larval light traps caught razorback sucker larvae only from the Needles site downstream to Clear Bay, suggesting significant spawning occurs at Needles or farther downstream.

**Origin of recaptured fish:**
- Of 254 razorback suckers with traceable origin (1999–2005), 163 came from Arizona Game and Fish Department releases in Lake Havasu; about 18% could not be traced.
- Seventy-eight fish from Bureau of Reclamation releases in Laughlin Lagoon (2001) were recaptured, and seven FWS/BLM fish from Topock Marsh.

**Bonytail:** Only one bonytail was caught in 2003 and one in 2004, and fourteen in 2005, mostly at Park Moabi.

**Recommendations:**
- Continue monitoring the Needles spawning aggregate, using visual float counts (non-invasive) and electrofishing (a large sample was collected there in 2006).
- Conduct a multi-year telemetry study to track fish spawning near Needles.
- Survey lower Lake Havasu with trammel nets and larval light traps to determine whether stocked fish there have established another spawning site.

**Conclusion:** The authors conclude the population is declining without continued stocking, although the rate of decline may be less severe than previously feared. Additional sampling and improved methods are needed to measure the decline accurately.
