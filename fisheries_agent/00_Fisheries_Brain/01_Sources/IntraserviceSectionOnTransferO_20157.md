# Desert Pupfish and Gila Topminnow Reintroduction to AD Wash, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) correspondence, dated February 16, 1993, transmits an intra-Service [[Section 7 Consultation]] biological evaluation for the transfer of endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD). The fish are to be stocked in [[AD Wash]], a small tributary of the [[Agua Fria River]] in Yavapai County, Arizona. The Service concurred with the finding that the action is "not likely to adversely affect" the listed species, with a beneficial effect expected. No effect was determined for three Category 2 candidate species.

## Background & Description

- **Species involved:** Desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius* per the evaluation form) and Gila topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*). Both are listed as endangered.
- **Historical status:** Both species formerly occurred throughout southern Arizona and were considered among the most abundant fishes there.
- **Listing status:** The Gila topminnow was listed in 1967 without critical habitat. No critical habitat exists within the action area for either species.
- **Genetic lineage:** The pupfish are of documented [[Santa Clara Slough]] lineage. The topminnow are of documented [[Sharp Spring]] stock (Dexter).
- **Captive source:** Both species come from captive populations held at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center, Dexter, New Mexico.
- **Document type:** Memorandum from the Field Supervisor, Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, to the Director of Dexter National Fish Hatchery, copied to the Regional Director (Albuquerque) and the AGFD Director. The attached Section 7 form was originated by Buddy L. Jensen on January 8, 1993 (Region 2).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area: AD Wash**
- Narrow canyon immediately downstream of [[Layton Tank]].
- Surface flow appears to be groundwater forced to the surface by underlying bedrock.
- Located on [[Arizona State Trust Land]], about 16 miles west of Black Canyon City, Arizona.
- Mapped on the Garfias Mountain Quadrangle, 7.5 Minute Series (Topographic).
- Legal description: SW 1/4 SW 1/4, Section 36, T8N, R2W.

**Physical habitat**
- Substrate: predominantly bedrock overlain by silt and detritus.
- Gravel, cobble, and boulders occur in some pools and in shallow sloped riffles between large pools.
- Several large pools with an estimated maximum depth of over 8 feet.
- The combination of shallow runs and deep pools is described as meeting the habitat requirements for self-sustaining populations.

**Biota**
- Aquatic vegetation: filamentous algae, cattails, and emergent grasses.
- Riparian vegetation: dominated by willows, with mesquite and salt cedar (saltcedar) also present.
- Other fauna: leopard frogs, canyon tree frogs, and a black-necked garter snake.

**Candidate species present (not affected)**
- Arizona Gilbert's Skink (*Eumeces gilberti arizonensis*), Category 2.
- Lowland leopard frog (*Rana yavapaiensis*), Category 2.
- Sonoran desert tortoise (*Gopherus agassizii*), Category 2.
- Source of occurrence data: Arizona Game and Fish Department Heritage Data Base System.

**Land use**
- The land is leased to D.S. Layton (lease 05-979) for livestock grazing.
- The State Land Department has no record of other proposed uses.

## Key Findings

1. **Proposed action:** AGFD proposes stocking approximately 500 desert pupfish and approximately 500 Gila topminnow into AD Wash, to be carried out in winter.
2. **Transport:** Fish will be shipped from Dexter to Phoenix and held temporarily in several large aquariums before release.
3. **Determination for listed species:** "Is not likely to adversely affect" (beneficial effect) for desert pupfish and Gila topminnow. Concurrence was requested and signed by the Service.
4. **Determination for candidate species:** "Will not affect" for Arizona Gilbert's Skink, Lowland leopard frog, and Sonoran desert tortoise.
5. **Donor population impact:** Removal of fish from Dexter is expected to minimally affect the donor population, with enough remaining for future propagation and reintroduction.
6. **Mortality:** Limited incidental mortality is expected during transport, holding, and stocking. Mortality is covered by Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811, issued to AGFD.
7. **Seasonal risk:** Winter stocking may result in slightly higher incidental mortality than optimal spring-summer stocking. Winter timing was chosen because the state permit is revocable at any time.
8. **Permit:** Arizona State Land Department Right of Entry No. 29-52820, issued December 21, 1992, for a one-year term expiring December 21, 1993. The permit is revocable at the State Land Department's discretion.
9. **Conservation significance:** The project is described as a beneficial step toward recovery of both species. Replicating natural populations of the Santa Clara Slough pupfish and Sharp Spring (Dexter) topminnow is identified as important to the overall recovery programs.
10. **Restoration approach:** Stocking is intended to mimic historic conditions in southern Arizona, when both species were abundant.
