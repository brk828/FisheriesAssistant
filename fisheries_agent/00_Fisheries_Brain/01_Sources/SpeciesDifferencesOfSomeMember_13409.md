# Species Differences of Some Members of Salmonidae Based on Their Muscle Myogen Patterns

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 study by [[Tsuyuki and Roberts]] (Fisheries Research Board of Canada, Technological Station, Vancouver) compares the muscle myogen protein patterns of ten salmonid taxa using [[Starch Gel Electrophoresis]]. Species from three genera were examined: [[Salvelinus]] (Dolly Varden and eastern brook trout), [[Salmo]] (rainbow and steelhead trouts, Atlantic salmon), and [[Oncorhynchus]] (coho, spring, sockeye, chum and pink salmon). Myogen patterns were distinct for every species except rainbow and steelhead trouts, which were identical. The authors propose the patterns as a stable, fingerprint-like criterion for species identification and classification, and their results are broadly consistent with an evolutionary sequence from Salvelinus to Salmo to Oncorhynchus, though some groupings conflict with morphological and serological evidence.

## Background & Description

Earlier myogen studies used moving boundary electrophoresis ([[Connell]], 1953; [[Lillevik and Schloemer]], 1961, 10 sunfish species), paper electrophoresis ([[Nikkila and Linko]], 1955) and column chromatography ([[Tsuyuki and Roberts]], 1961). The authors consider the starch gel method, developed by [[Smithies]] (1955), the most definitive because of its high sensitivity, ease of handling, and capacity to run many samples at once.

Methods followed earlier protocols (Tsuyuki et al., 1962a, 1962b). Myogen extracts were separated on starch gel prepared in 0.023 M sodium borate buffer at pH 8.5. Sample sources were:
- Sockeye, spring and coho salmon: collected as in earlier studies.
- Atlantic salmon: frozen in dry ice, supplied by Dr D. R. Idler (Halifax, N.S.).
- Eastern brook trout: live, from the Cache Creek area, B.C.
- Dolly Varden: frozen in dry ice, from Cultus Lake, B.C.

Sockeye samples were also taken from Port Renfrew and Cultus Lake, B.C.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper addresses taxonomy rather than habitat or ecology. Its framework is the accepted classification of the Salmonidae, in which Oncorhynchus is thought to have evolved from Salmo, which in turn may have arisen from Salvelinus (Milne, 1948; Neave, 1958; Regan, 1920; Clemens, 1953; personal communication from Dr C. C. Lindsey). The authors note that rainbow and steelhead trouts are thought to be one species (Clemens and Wilby, 1961; Carl, Clemens and Lindsey, 1959). Habitat, ocean basin and population ecology are not discussed.

## Key Findings

- **Species discrimination:** Myogen patterns were clearly distinct for each species studied except rainbow and steelhead trouts, which gave identical patterns. The patterns were consistent among adults of one species regardless of sex or physiological condition, and the authors propose them as "fingerprints" for identifying unknown frozen fish flesh.
- **Atlantic salmon:** Lacked a strong anionic zone at 2.5 cm and showed weak anionic and cationic zones at 2.7 cm and 1.5 cm from the origin. Its protein distribution on either side of the origin resembled that of coho and spring salmon.
- **Salvelinus comparison:** Eastern brook trout lacked the most cationic component of rainbow trout. Dolly Varden matched rainbow trout on the cathode side but had two extra anionic zones (0.8 cm and 1.5 cm). The 0.8 cm zone also occurs in coho, spring and chum salmon, and the 1.5 cm zone in sockeye and pink salmon.
- **Oncorhynchus pairs:** Coho and spring salmon, and sockeye and pink salmon, each differ by one zone position. Chum lies between the two pairs, closer to the sockeye–pink pair.
- **Similarity to Salmo:** The three Salmo species resemble coho salmon most closely, followed by spring salmon, then chum, sockeye and pink salmon. Coho is closer to rainbow trout than spring salmon is.
- **Cationic zone at 3 cm:** This zone increases in intensity from extremely faint in Atlantic salmon, through coho and spring, to strong in sockeye salmon.
- **Shared zone:** One of the two Dolly Varden zones (j or k) absent from eastern brook, rainbow and steelhead trouts is present in every Oncorhynchus species examined.
- **Relation to other evidence:** Myogen results partly agree with morphological and life-history work (Clemens, 1953), which places pink and chum as closely related and sockeye intermediate. They disagree with serological results (Hourston, 1949), which show a close link among coho, spring and sockeye but a significant difference for chum. Preliminary serum protein data (Tsuyuki and Gadd, 1962) show smaller differences than myogen data. Hemoglobin data (Tsuyuki and Gadd, 1962) show a marked difference between spring and sockeye and support the myogen-based genus relationships.
- **Unexamined taxa:** Cherry salmon (O. masou) was not available. The authors predict its myogen pattern would be closer to Salmo than coho is.
- **Limitations:** Without refinement of the separation method, racial differences between Port Renfrew and Cultus Lake sockeye could not be clearly resolved.
