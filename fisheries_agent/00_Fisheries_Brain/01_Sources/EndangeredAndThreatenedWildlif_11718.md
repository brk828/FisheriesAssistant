# Alabama Sturgeon Listing Withdrawal: Endangered Status Proposal for *Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice (December 15, 1994), with a companion [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] announcement dated December 19, 1994, documents the withdrawal of the proposed rule to list the [[Alabama Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*) as an endangered species with critical habitat under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. The Service found insufficient information to justify listing a species that may no longer exist. The sturgeon is endemic to the [[Mobile River System]] and has declined substantially in population size and range over the past century. The decision rests primarily on the absence of evidence that the species still exists, following a single verified capture in December 1993. The species was placed in Category 2 of the candidate species list.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** Recognized since 1976 as a distinct, undescribed taxon (Ramsey 1976) most similar to the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*) of the Mississippi River system. Formally described as *S. suttkusi* by Williams and Clemmer (1991). Taxonomic status remains debated; the Service determined it warrants recognition as a species under the Act.
- **Morphology:** Relatively small sturgeon, maximum standard length about 72 cm (28 inches). Elongated, heavily armored, depressed body; attenuated caudal peduncle; long filament on the upper caudal fin lobe. Sexual dimorphism is slight.
- **Distribution (historical):** Mobile, Tensas, Alabama, Tombigbee, Black Warrior, Cahaba, Tallapoosa, and Coosa Rivers of the Mobile River system.
- **Longevity:** Congeners aged to 12 years (shovelnose, upper Mississippi), 8 to 27 years (shovelnose, Missouri River, 288 fish), and 10, 37, and 41 years (pallid sturgeon, Missouri River).
- **Diet:** Aquatic insect larvae dominant. In Haynes (1994), aquatic insects occurred in all 12 stomachs examined (true flies, mayflies, dragonflies, caddisflies); small fish in 5 stomachs and plant material in 4.
- **Reproduction:** Spawning habitat generally unknown; inferred to resemble shovelnose habitat (hard substrates in main channel or deep-water areas near channel-training devices). Spawning apparently occurs April to July.

Related taxa: [[Gulf Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser oxyrinchus desotoi*), listed as threatened September 30, 1991, and [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*S. albus*), listed as endangered September 6, 1990.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Mobile River system biodiversity:**
- High diversity and endemism. Aquatic endemism: snails 93 percent, mussels 40 percent, freshwater fish 25 percent.
- 17 mussels, fishes, 2 turtles, and 1 snail federally protected; 64 additional aquatic taxa are candidates.
- Documented extinction of 37 endemic snail taxa and 18 endemic mussel taxa.
- The Service characterizes the system as an "unstable and imperiled riverine ecosystem."

**Historical harvest:**
- U.S. Commission of Fish and Fisheries (1898) reported 19,500 kg (42,900 lb) of "shovelnose sturgeon" from Alabama: 18,000 kg (39,500 lb) from the Alabama River, 1,000 kg (2,300 lb) from the Black Warrior River, and 500 kg (1,100 lb) from the Tennessee River.

**Recent records:**
- 1985: five Alabama sturgeons captured (Burke and Ramsey 1985), including two gravid females and one juvenile about 2 years old.
- 1990, 1991, 1992: ADCNR and Corps searches (Tucker and Johnson 1991, 1992) found no specimens.
- December 2, 1993: one mature male caught alive in a gill net, Alabama River downstream of [[Claiborne Lock and Dam]]. Single verified record in about 8 years; it died and was necropsied.
- 1994: no specimens collected as of the notice date.

**Habitat associations:**
- Prefers relatively stable gravel and sand substrates in channels with swift currents.
- Burke and Ramsey (1985) reported a radio-collared fish frequenting areas shallow to about 12 m (40 ft) deep.
- Captures primarily from large channels of big rivers; a few from oxbow lakes.

**Threats (as assessed under Factors A to E):**
- **Factor A (habitat):** Dams (Burke and Ramsey 1985 estimated 57 percent of the historic range fragmented, with 15 percent (243 km, 152 mi) retaining potential habitat); channelization; gravel mining; silt loading; water-quality alteration; maintenance dredging (Corps removes 1.3 to 3.8 million cubic meters annually).
- **Factor B (overutilization):** Commercial harvest historically; possible incidental catch in 1980s paddlefish fishery (anecdotal only).
- **Factor C (disease/predation):** No known threats.
- **Factor D (regulatory):** Alabama law requires release of all sturgeon and prohibits possession without a scientific permit. The Service concluded this does not protect against other threats.
- **Factor E (other):** Reduced genetic diversity from isolation by dams; Tennessee-Tombigbee Waterway (TTW) potentially allows limited shovelnose movement, judged very low probability of genetic mixing.

**Flow regime:** Corps and Alabama Power Company maintain average daily minimum flows of approximately 149 cms (4,640 cfs) over any seven consecutive days and approximately 81 cms (2,667 cfs) over any three consecutive days downstream of Claiborne Lock and Dam. Average daily flows over the last decade ranged from 114 to 6,912 cms (3,800 to 244,000 cfs).

## Key Findings

**Decision and regulatory status**
- Proposed rule to list as endangered with critical habitat published June 15, 1993 (58 FR 33148).
- Deadline extended June 21, 1994 (59 FR 31970), then extended to December 15, 1994 (59 FR 47294 notice of reopened comment period).
- Service withdrew the proposed rule on December 15, 1994. Placed in Category 2 of the candidate species list.
- The Service will treat the Alabama sturgeon as a full species pending revision in peer-reviewed literature. It notes that even as a subspecies or DPS it would remain eligible for protection.

**Population status**
- Last confirmed record: one male captured December 2, 1993.
- Eight scientists were consulted September 13, 1994; five strongly supported continued existence, three did not address the question.
- The Service concluded it was premature to declare extinction; several decades of negative sampling would be needed.

**Taxonomic evidence**
- Williams and Clemmer (1991) described the species; Mayden and Kuhajda (in press, accepted for peer-reviewed publication) support distinctness.
- Schill and Walker (1994, unpublished) found cytochrome b sequences indistinguishable.
- Genetic Analyses Inc. (1994, Corps and Service study) found no measurable difference between pallid and shovelnose sturgeon but significant differences from the single Alabama specimen.
- Howell (1993, 1994), Blanchard and Bartolucci (1994), and Blanchard (1994) disputed the taxonomic validity (unpublished).

**Public comment process**
- Several thousand written and oral comments were received; several hundred supported listing; most opposing comments concerned economic impacts, which the Act excludes from listing decisions.
- Hearings: October 4, 1993 (Mobile); November 15, 1993 (cancelled due to a preliminary injunction issued November 9, 1993); January 13, 1994 (rescheduled to January 31, 1994, Montgomery).
- A scientific panel report (delivered November 5, 1993) was subject to court restriction (Alabama-Tombigbee River Development Coalition v. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Civ. No. 93-AR-2322-S).

**Management considerations**
- Maintenance dredging: the Service concurred with the Corps that current annual maintenance dredging does not adversely affect the sturgeon.
- Gravel mining: Service does not anticipate jeopardy from mining unconsolidated substrates.
- Coalbed methane produced water: Service does not anticipate impacts from properly permitted discharges.
- Impoundments on the Alabama River (late 1960s and early 1970s) likely played a significant role in decline.

**Economic and procedural points**
- Economic impacts cannot be considered under section 4(b)(1)(A) of the Act.
- The Service is exempt from the Regulatory Flexibility Act for section 4 listing actions.
- The Service stated that Federal listing would not necessarily halt navigation maintenance, but that section 7 consultation would apply.

**Key figures from the record**
- 1893 to 1898 commercial catch: 18,000 kg (39,500 lb) from the Alabama River.
- Alabama had the fourth largest number of federally listed species (88) of any State as of November 30, 1994.
- The Service's Daphne, Alabama, Field Office reviewed about 10,000 Federal actions from 1988 to 1993.

**Conclusion**
- Insufficient information exists to justify listing a species that may no longer exist. The Service stated that if new information demonstrates present existence, it may proceed under 50 CFR part 424.
