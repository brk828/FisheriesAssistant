# Observations on Growth and Reproduction of the Paddlefish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paddlefish

## Executive Summary

This 1959 study by Alfred Houser and Michael G. Ross, of the [[Oklahoma Fisheries Research Laboratory]] in Norman, examines the [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*) population in [[Fort Gibson Reservoir]], Oklahoma. Young-of-the-year fish collected in 1957 reached an average total length of 28.4 inches in their first year. A [[Linear Regression Growth Model]] placed hatching on May 4. The authors also suggest that paddlefish may have a periodicity in sexual maturation, which could explain why spawning succeeded in only some years.

## Background & Description

The paddlefish has drawn interest since early American exploration. Its commercial value peaked in the first decade of the twentieth century. Overharvesting, stream pollution, and dam construction were cited as causes of the subsequent decline (Larimore, 1950; Lachner, 1956).

In Oklahoma the species has never been a major commercial or sport fish, but it has appeared regularly in catches from the [[Arkansas River System]] and the [[Red River System]]. Unverified local reports indicate that paddlefish occurred in the [[Grand River]] before its impoundment in 1949 to form Fort Gibson Reservoir. Early reservoir collections confirmed their presence, and commercial fishermen reported that spawning during filling produced a fairly large population.

A 1951 Oklahoma Legislature law liberalized commercial fishing by allowing more efficient gear and out-of-state sales. Harvest rose immediately, and fishermen reported a rapid population reduction. Biologists working on the lake in 1954–1956 recorded only small, infrequent captures.

Before 1957 investigators generally held that paddlefish age and growth could not be reliably read from fin rays. Fin ray samples preserved incidentally during other work nonetheless provided a historical record for this population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir conditions and food supply.** During the 1957 growing season, unusually heavy rainfall kept the flood pool elevated for more than six weeks. The reservoir surface area more than doubled, and large areas of vegetation were inundated. Nutrients from this flooding produced plankton blooms, especially in the bay areas. The authors attribute the exceptional growth of the 1957 year-class to this abundance of plankton, the staple food of paddlefish.

**Winter growth.** Growth was minimal from mid-October 1957 to February 1958. Length increases measured in November, December, and February were under two inches, probably less than one inch.

**Current and spawning.** Following [[Stockard]] (1907), paddlefish spawn only in running water. In spring 1957 the current in Fort Gibson was noticeable in the main lake. Daily outflow as a percentage of storage in acre-feet averaged 8 percent in April and 14 percent in May, indicating substantial flow. According to this criterion, spawning should have been possible in 1950, 1951, 1952, and 1958, but not in 1953–1956.

**Maturation and life history.** Adams (1942) reported that female paddlefish do not mature until about seven or eight years of age, consistent with the Fort Gibson observations: the adults that spawned in 1957 were themselves spawned in 1950. The authors compare this to the [[Lake Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*), which also matures slowly and does not spawn every year. Cuerrier (1949) reported that male lake sturgeon spawn at two-year intervals and females at four to five years. Larimore reported finding two immature males larger than some mature males in one collection, and individuals without ripe gonads that were larger than mature fish of the same sex have been recorded.

## Key Findings

**Population history and year-classes**

- Sixteen 2-year-old paddlefish of the 1950 year-class were collected in summer 1952. Members of this class were captured occasionally thereafter, but no reproduction was evident until August 16, 1957, when a 17.3-inch paddlefish was taken.
- The 1957 year-class was represented by 23 fish measuring 15.8 to 29.4 inches, captured between August 16 and December 13, 1957. Commercial fishermen supplied information on 34 additional specimens in 1958, and enough fin ray samples were preserved to identify them as 1957 year-class fish.
- No individuals of the 1951, 1952, or 1958 year-classes were found, although spawning conditions were presumed suitable in those years.

**Aging methods**

- Microscopic examination of cross-sections of [[Dentary Bone]] and [[Pectoral Fin Rays]] from the 1957 collection revealed no annuli. The authors took this as evidence that these fish were spawned in 1957.

**Growth model and first-year growth**

- The [[Linear Regression Growth Model]] for 1957 fish, based on capture data through October 12, was: size = 7.72 + 0.1728 × days from August 16 (total length in inches).
- Extrapolation of the regression suggests hatching on May 4.
- Eight specimens collected in November and December averaged 28.4 inches. This length intersects the regression line on October 14, so the growing season was taken as May 4 to October 15, a total of 165 days.
- The average daily growth increment was 0.17 inches.
- The first-year average length of 28.4 inches approximated the 32-inch average of the 1950 year-class at the end of three years.

**Post-winter growth**

- Growth resumed in spring 1958, with an average length of 35 inches by July. After a mid-July rise in water level, growth accelerated, and the average reached 37.7 inches in early September.

**Reproductive periodicity hypothesis**

- The authors suggest that paddlefish may have a periodicity in sexual maturation, citing slow maturation, the lake sturgeon analogy, and the presence of large immature fish.
- If periodicity exists, it would explain the absence of spawning in 1951, 1952, and 1958 despite currents presumed similar to those in 1957.
- The authors state that their observations do not support environmental factors as the cause of these failed year-classes.

**Stated limitations**

- Information on many of the 1958 specimens was incomplete, and the periodicity conclusion rests on limited observations and literature review.
