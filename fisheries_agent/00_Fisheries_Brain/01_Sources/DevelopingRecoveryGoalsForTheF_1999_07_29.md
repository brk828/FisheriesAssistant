# Developing Recovery Goals for the Four Endangered Colorado River Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ConservationBiology #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This document is a **Scope of Work (SOW)** submitted by SWCA, Inc. (R.A. Valdez) and Ryel & Associates (R.J. Ryel) to the **Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program** in 1999. The project's goal is to establish **quantitative recovery goals** — specifically target population size and structure — for four endangered fish species native to the Colorado River Basin: the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]. Existing recovery plans (1990–1998) called for "self-sustaining populations" but lacked quantitative criteria. This project proposes a structured methodology combining **genetics-based minimum effective population size (Ne)** estimates with **habitat carrying capacity** assessments to generate defensible, sub-population-specific recovery targets for both the Upper and Lower Colorado River Basins. The final report was scheduled for completion by April 30, 2000.

## Background & Description

### Species Overview
Four cyprinid/catostomid species native to the Colorado River system are the focus of this recovery goal-setting effort:

- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — Family Cyprinidae; largest native cyprinid in North America; historically a top predatory fish of the Colorado River system.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Family Catostomidae; distinguished by a pronounced dorsal keel ("razorback").
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Family Cyprinidae; noted for a distinctive nuchal hump, adapted to swift canyon-bound river reaches.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*) — Family Cyprinidae; among the rarest of the four, historically found in mainstem river reaches.

All four species are federally listed as **Endangered** under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, with existing single-species recovery plans issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Colorado pikeminnow, 1991; razorback sucker, 1998; humpback chub, 1990; bonytail, 1990). None of these plans contained quantitative population-based delisting criteria — a gap this SOW project seeks to fill.

### Project Purpose
The recovery goals will define:
1. Target **population size** by sub-population.
2. Target **population structure** (young-of-year, juveniles, sub-adults, adults) based on fecundity and survival by age class.
3. Metrics to measure **progress toward downlisting and delisting**.

The project involves coordination with the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]], the Biology Committee of the Recovery Program, the **Lower Colorado River Basin Multi-Species Conservation Program (LCR MSCP)**, the **San Juan River Recovery Program**, the **Native Fish Work Group**, and the **Grand Canyon Adaptive Management Program**.

### Habitat & Environmental Drivers
Habitat carrying capacity assessments rely on:
- Channel geomorphology
- Existing habitat evaluations
- River hydrology and temperature regimes
- Historical and current fish population densities

Key regions referenced for empirical population data include the **Green River**, **Colorado and Gunnison Rivers**, **Grand Canyon**, and **Lake Mead/Lake Mojave** in the Lower Basin. The project explicitly notes that historic, pre-development population levels may not be determinable or achievable under current regulated-river conditions, so achievable densities must be estimated from contemporary studies.

## Key Findings

### Methodological Framework
- **Minimum Effective Population Size (Ne):** Defined as the number of adults necessary to maintain genetic viability and minimize inbreeding depression (per Soulé 1987; Allendorf et al. 1997).
  - A **maximum 1% inbreeding rate** is recommended for wild populations (Simberloff 1988).
  - A **minimum Ne of 1,000 adults** is a general guideline for animal populations (Lynch 1996), though this is expected to vary by species based on species-specific genetic characterization (Meffe 1986).
- **Genetics data sources** proposed for use include studies by Ammerman & Morizot (1989) and Gilpin (1993) for Colorado pikeminnow; Buth & Murphy (1984), Buth et al. (1987), Dowling & Minckley (1993), and Echelle (1991) for razorback sucker; Dowling & DeMarais (1993) and DeMarais (1986) for humpback chub; and Echelle (1991) and Minckley et al. (1989) for bonytail.
- **Metapopulation approach:** Where habitat is inadequate to support a minimum viable population within a single reach, sub-populations will be evaluated for their capacity for genetic exchange with nearby sub-populations, treating them as components of a broader metapopulation.

### Recovery Goal Structure
- Recovery goals will express targets as **densities of reproducing adults (number per mile)**, derived from the intersection of minimum effective population size and estimated habitat carrying capacity.
- **Population structure** (young-of-year, juveniles, sub-adults) will be back-calculated from fecundity and age-specific survival rates.
- A **temporal maintenance criterion** — the duration over which target numbers/structure must be sustained — will be built into each recovery goal.

### Project Deliverables & Timeline
- **Task 1:** Meetings with Recovery Team and Biology Committee (Biology Committee meeting held July 15–16, 1999).
- **Task 2:** Literature review on population structure/dynamics and genetics (June–November 1999).
- **Task 3:** Interviews with upper and lower basin biologists and geneticists.
- **Task 4:** Development of recovery goals (sub-population designation, Ne estimation, habitat carrying capacity, goal-setting).
- **Task 5:** Reporting — Preliminary Draft Report (Oct 1, 1999) → Draft Final Report (Dec 1, 1999) → independent scientific review (Mar 1, 2000) → **Final Report (April 30, 2000)**.

### Open Questions Posed to the Recovery Team
The memo raises five unresolved questions for Recovery Team deliberation at the August 25, 1999 meeting in Las Vegas:
1. Whether recovery goals should be split between upper and lower basins.
2. Appropriate sub-population/river reach designations.
3. What recovery goals should look like per sub-population/reach.
4. Whether the proposed process is scientifically appropriate and defensible.
5. What additional regional information should inform goal development.
