# Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program: Draft FY 95 Work Plan Package

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This is a December 14, 1994 memorandum from Pat Nelson, Coordinator of the Flooded Bottom Lands Restoration Program, to its Technical Advisory Group. It requests review of draft FY 95 proposals by January 5, 1995, and asks four questions: do the proposals fit what the group had in mind, do they address the program goal and objectives, what are the recommendations for the program, and are other studies needed. The attached package contains the November 30 meeting summary, the program goal and objectives, about 15 scope-of-work proposals, and a draft FY 95 budget summary. The proposals concern the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]]. They cover biology (nonnative-fish interactions, productivity, release size, juvenile monitoring), site restoration, contaminants (mainly selenium), hydrology, geomorphology, and site inventory. The aim is to recover the [[Razorback Sucker]] and other native fishes.

## Background & Description
**Program goal:** "To restore or enhance natural flood plain functions for recovery of endangered fishes in the upper Colorado River basin."

**Objectives:**
- Ensure short-term stabilization of razorback sucker populations.
- Determine the conditions that maximize long-term recruitment and persistence of razorbacks and other native fishes.
- Use drainable ponds with inflow/outflow control, from which nonnative fishes can be removed and excluded. Stock them with wild-caught [[Green River]] larvae, rear the fish to PIT-taggable size, and release them.
- Study nonnative effects on young razorbacks in simulated floodplains.
- Monitor fish communities, water levels, temperature, cover, geomorphology and riparian vegetation across a variety of floodplain sites.

**Core assumptions** (stated in the proposals):
- Floodplain wetlands are critical rearing habitat.
- Native fish declines relate to habitat loss.
- Loss of this habitat is tied to [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operations.
- Flooded bottomlands can be managed to maximize survival of young native fish.

**Target species:** razorback sucker (the most imperiled large-river fish with extant reproducing riverine populations), [[Colorado Squawfish]], and possibly [[Bonytail Chub]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Floodplain wetlands are described as shallow, warm, slow-water areas with higher nutrients, primary productivity and invertebrate densities than main-channel habitat. They support rapid growth of young fish.
- Dams, levees, dikes, rip-rap and tamarisk have cut off bottomlands, and spring flows have been reduced by upstream impoundments.
- **Nonnative predators of concern:** channel catfish, northern pike, green sunfish, smallmouth bass and [[Red Shiner]]. Red shiner is the most widespread and abundant fish in the basin and ranked second on a 1991 list of harmful nonnatives.
- **Contaminants:** selenium may be a problem at many sites. It bioaccumulates strongly in food chains.
- **Key sites:**
  - [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]]: [[Old Charley Wash]], [[Leota Bottoms]], Johnson Bottom. The refuge holds water rights to divert 26,000 acre-feet and has lower contaminant levels than other large sites.
  - [[Escalante Ranch]] (Green River).
  - Gravel pit ponds at 29-5/8 Road and [[Golf-Course West]] (Adobe Creek) near Grand Junction.
  - [[Walter Walker State Wildlife Area]].
  - [[Scott Matheson Wildlife Preserve]] ([[Moab Sloughs]], Colorado River miles 64-62).
  - Lower Green River nursery reaches in Canyonlands.
- The Duchesne River mouth is a postspawning staging site for the largest known riverine razorback population. Leota Bottoms is about nine miles upstream of it.

**Study designs:**
- **Release size:** a pilot mark-recapture of 130-150 mm fish (three lots, resampled every three weeks for three months) will inform a power analysis, set at 70% power. The main experiment compares three release sizes: about 100, 150 and 200 mm TL. The size classes are tied to the gape sizes of nonnative predators, with handling-mortality control cages of 50-100 fish per size.
- **Nonnative control:** four nursery habitats per reach, two treatments and two controls, in two lower Green River reaches. Treatments use 3- or 6-mm mesh exclosures that block red shiner of 35 mm TL or more and pass larvae under 20 mm TL. Larvae will be marked with tetracycline or alizarin in the otoliths.
- **Leota ponds (L-3, L-4):** treated with rotenone and stocked with wild razorback larvae. One pond receives river water with nonnatives and one does not. A further 3 depression and 3 terrace habitats will be sampled across the hydrograph.
- **Old Charley Wash:** larvae captured by light trap, razorback adults radio-tagged, and the pond drained in the fall for PIT tagging. Nonnatives are removed.
- **Productivity:** weekly nutrient samples (autoanalyzer), Hydrolab parameters, Surber and drift-net invertebrate sampling, and at least three natural floodplains of different sizes.
- **Hypothesis:** larger floodplains retain water longer and develop fuller production.
- **Gravel pit and Golf-Course West sites:** these test pond configurations ("bathtub" vs. graded) for reducing nonnatives. Gravel ponds warmer than the river by as much as 10.5 C were reported.

**Contaminants:** the planned work includes baseline selenium monitoring at the Walter Walker and Adobe Creek sites. It also includes stocking at least five razorback spawning pairs and evaluating egg viability and 30-day larval survival. Flannelmouth sucker and fathead minnow may serve as surrogates. Twelve sites are scheduled for contaminant screening in FY 95.

**Hydrology and geomorphology:** the planned work covers site screening, a stage-discharge relationship for each site, and baseline topographic maps. Restoration is scheduled to start in FY 95 for Leota L-7, Walter Walker, Golf-Course West, the 29-5/8 Road gravel pits and Moab Slough.

**Draft FY 95 budget summary:** component lines include contaminants $150K, hydrology $100K and geomorphology $100K. The legible total is about $1,536.25K, though line items are garbled by OCR and some figures are uncertain.
