## Executive Summary

A 1992 spot check survey conducted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department on Gap Creek, Prescott National Forest, Yavapai County, Arizona, found **no evidence of [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*)**, a population originally introduced into this system in 1974. Despite backpack electroshocking 13 pools (both above and below Government Springs) totaling over 2,299 seconds of shocking effort, zero fish of any species were sampled or observed. This represents a continued decline from previous surveys and confirms the likely **extirpation** of the Gila Trout population in Gap Creek, attributed to predation, seasonal flooding, and drought-driven dewatering.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*), a salmonid native to the American Southwest and a federally protected species of conservation concern.
- **Introduction history:** The Gap Creek population was introduced in 1974 as part of range expansion/conservation efforts for this imperiled trout.
- **Survey objective:** To backpack electroshock various pools above and below Government Springs on Gap Creek to determine the presence and viability of the introduced Gila Trout population.
- **Personnel:** Tom McMahon, Pat Crouch, Stan Mondragon, and Rob Bettaso (Arizona Game and Fish Department). No [[US Forest Service]] representatives were available to attend.
- **Equipment:** Coffelt Mark-10 backpack electroshocker, thermometer, pH meter, and dipnets.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature:** Average of 15°C (59°F) across 3 samples — within suitable range for coldwater salmonids.
- **Dissolved oxygen:** Average of 10.2 ppm across 5 samples — adequate for trout survival.
- **Streamflow:** Estimated at less than 0.5 cfs — critically low flow conditions.
- **Invertebrate community:** High populations of aquatic insects including mayflies, damselflies, belostomatids (giant water bugs), and dragonflies — indicating reasonable water quality despite absence of fish.
- **Amphibians:** High populations of [[Canyon Treefrog]] adults and tadpoles observed, suggesting the aquatic habitat remains viable for some vertebrate taxa even without fish.
- **Habitat stressors:** The stream has experienced **drought conditions** and **seasonal flooding** in recent years, leading to partial dewatering of significant stream sections — likely major drivers of the fish population collapse.

## Key Findings

- **Survey effort:** 5 pools sampled above Government Springs (375 seconds of shocking); 8 pools sampled below Government Springs (1,924 seconds of shocking). Total: 13 pools, ~2,299 seconds of backpack electroshocking effort.
- **Result:** **Zero fish of any species**, including Gila Trout, were sampled or observed in either survey section.
- **Population trend over time** (historical inventory data):
  - **1982:** >150 Gila Trout sampled or observed below Government Springs.
  - **September 1987:** 38 sampled + 32 observed (70 total).
  - **August 1990:** 4 sampled + 2 observed (6 total).
  - **June 1992:** 0 sampled or observed — population presumed **extirpated**.
- **Probable causes of decline:** Predation, seasonal flooding, and chronic drought conditions causing partial dewatering of the stream.
- **Management conclusion:** Restocking Gap Creek with Gila Trout is deemed **not feasible** under current climatic conditions, as the same environmental stressors that caused extirpation would likely undermine repopulation efforts.

### Recommendations

1. If a sport or conservation fishery is desired in Gap Creek, a hardier coldwater species is recommended, such as [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), or [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*).
2. If establishing Gila Trout populations in central Arizona remains a priority, alternative sites such as **Coldwater Creek** (Yavapai County) should be considered due to its consistent flow, stable temperatures, and relative isolation from public disturbance — factors favorable for long-term population viability.
