## Executive Summary

This report documents administrative and planning progress related to the **Alamo Dam** project on the **Bill Williams River**, Arizona, rather than presenting direct biological or morphological fish data. The study addresses coordination among the **Army Corps of Engineers**, the **Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife**, the **Arizona State Parks Department**, and the **Arizona Game and Fish Department** regarding the creation and management of the future **Alamo Reservoir** and the adjoining **17,000-acre Ocotillo Game Management Area**. Key outcomes include agreement on a General Plan granting the Arizona Game and Fish Department management authority over fish, wildlife, and recreational resources at the reservoir, along with preliminary planning for angler support infrastructure (a fish cleaning facility) and recreational zoning of the future lake.

## Background & Description

No specific fish species, morphological descriptions, or taxonomic data are provided in this source. The report is administrative/planning in nature and does not include field surveys, species inventories, or morphometric analysis. Future [[Alamo Reservoir]] fish community composition was not yet established at the time of this report, as the dam and reservoir had not yet been completed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System type:** Emerging impoundment (desert reservoir) formed by damming the [[Bill Williams River]], transitioning a riverine system into lentic reservoir habitat.
- **Anticipated habitat conditions:** High public recreational use expected on the future reservoir, necessitating sanitary and angler-support facilities (e.g., a proposed [[Fish Cleaning Facility]]).
- **Zoned water use:** Approximately 160 acres of open water were designated for water skiing and other water sports, with the remainder of the lake reserved from such activities — an early example of use-zoning to protect fish and wildlife habitat within a reservoir.
- **Adjacent terrestrial habitat:** The **Ocotillo Game Management Area** (17,000 acres) borders the reservoir and was slated for management primarily benefiting **small game and waterfowl**, with secondary consideration for other game species.
- **Hydrological variability:** Arable fields within the Ocotillo Game Management Area are subject to periodic flooding from the Bill Williams River, complicating long-term habitat and land-use planning. Boundary fencing across flood-prone washes was expected to require ongoing maintenance due to flood damage.
- **Management framework:** Post-dam river flow patterns needed to be observed before a stable management plan for the surrounding game and fish habitat could be finalized, indicating recognition of the reservoir's future role in shaping downstream and littoral ecological conditions.

## Key Findings

- Specifications and cost estimates for a **fish cleaning facility** at the Alamo Reservoir area were developed and submitted to the Army Corps of Engineers for inclusion as an official project feature.
- A **General Plan** was drafted by the Army Corps of Engineers and, after minor revisions, accepted by the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and the Arizona Game and Fish Department, formally enabling the Department to manage fish, wildlife, and recreation at [[Alamo Reservoir]].
- The Arizona Game and Fish Department agreed (October 5, 1965) to assume full responsibility for **fish, wildlife, and recreational management** across the entire Alamo Reservoir area, with the option to sublease administration of recreational facilities to a third party.
- A **lease agreement** for the Ocotillo Game Management Area and recreation sites was under review, with proposed changes returned to the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife for further coordination with the Corps of Engineers; finalization was expected during the next project segment.
- A **fish and wildlife management plan** for the reservoir and Ocotillo Game Management Area was in development, emphasizing habitat enhancement for **small game and waterfowl**, while considering broader game species needs.
- Recommended **boundary fencing** for the Ocotillo Game Management Area had not yet been approved, despite anticipated recurring flood damage to fence lines crossing seasonal washes.
- The study explicitly recommends **continuation of this project** as a cooperative, multi-program effort (Federal Aid Projects [[F-7-R]], [[W-53-R]], [[W-70-R]], and [[FW-16-R]]) to complete a comprehensive fish and wildlife management plan for the Alamo Dam/Reservoir system.
