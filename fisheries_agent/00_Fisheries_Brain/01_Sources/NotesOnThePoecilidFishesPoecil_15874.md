## Executive Summary

This 1966 taxonomic and distributional note by Minckley and Koehn documents field collections of two poeciliid fishes — [[Poecilia formosa]] (the Amazon molly) and [[Gambusia myersi]] — from the Rio Tamesi drainage of Tamaulipas, México. The paper expands known localities for both species beyond the sparse prior records (Darnell 1962; Hubbs and Hubbs 1932), catalogs associated fish fauna at each site, and presents a detailed morphological and color description of *G. myersi*, including gonopodial structure. The authors also raise significant taxonomic doubt about the validity of the name *Gambusia myersi*, arguing it may be a synonym of the widespread [[Gambusia affinis]] rather than a distinct, range-restricted endemic of the Rio Mante.

## Background & Description

### Poecilia formosa
- Dorsal fin ray counts: 27 of 30 specimens had **11 dorsal rays**; 3 had **12**.
- Compared to sympatric *[[Poecilia sphenops]]*, which mostly has **9 dorsal rays** (range 8–9; average 8.95, n=50) from the same localities — a useful diagnostic distinction between the two co-occurring *Poecilia* species.

### Gambusia myersi
- **Dorsal fin rays:** consistently **7** (counting the last ray split to its base) across 40 specimens — contrasts with Ahl's original description of 6–7, and differs from most Mexican *G. affinis* forms, which typically have **6**.
- **Anal fin rays:** consistently **9** in all specimens, versus 7–8 reported by Ahl for the nominal *myersi* type series.
- **Gonopodium:** possesses a very strong "elbow" in the gonopodial structure, unlike the reduced elbow seen in some southern *G. affinis* forms. Distinctively, the anterior and posterior branches of **ray four** meet at their distal ends — a trait shared with a *G. affinis* population from the Rio Pesqueria system (Nuevo León) and an undescribed *Gambusia* species from Cuba, suggesting this is not a unique diagnostic character.
- No dark lateral coloration or "gravid spot" was observed in any specimen, contrary to the type series examined by Ahl (1923).

### Coloration (G. myersi, from fresh/anesthetized specimens)
- Ground color: intense **canary yellow** in life, whitening on the belly, with blue reflections on the sides.
- Breeding males: intense **chalk-blue** reflection along the mid-side scale row; yellow-orange coloring on the ventral third of the caudal peduncle.
- **Dorsal fin:** yellow-orange in males, thin black distal margin, 3–5 rows of dark spots.
- **Caudal fin:** intense yellow at base, transparent yellow distally, with 2–4 vertical rows of dark spots.
- **Anal fin:** whitish in males (base/tip excepted); females show a variably developed dusky edge.
- **Pelvic fins:** unmarked in both sexes.
- **Pectoral fins:** transparent to opaque whitish; some males show a thin dark margin.
- Wide predorsal streak, thin postanal streak; dark sub-ocular bar variably present (best developed in large males); lower lip black in breeding males, dusky in females.
- Strong dark cross-hatching pattern on sides, back, and caudal peduncle present even in juveniles, combined with lateral spot series — described as making *G. myersi* "one of the most strikingly colored" species in the genus [[Gambusia]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types:**
  - *Rio Guayalejo* (main channel pools near highway crossings): slow current, mud-gravel bottom, abundant aquatic vegetation.
  - *Canals near Ciudad Mante*: clear water, relatively slow currents, mud banks with sand-gravel centers, depths of 2–4 ft, widths of 1–15 ft (mostly ~4 ft), unshaded by riparian vegetation.
- *Poecilia formosa* was consistently associated with **aquatic vegetation** and was invariably collected together with *[[Poecilia sphenops]]*.
- *Gambusia myersi* appears restricted to spring-fed and canal habitats associated with the **Rio Mante**, a tributary of the Rio Tamesi, and may be locally endemic (though this endemism is questioned by the authors).
- **Associated fish fauna** at Rio Guayalejo sites included: *[[Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus]]*, *Ictalurus* sp., *Notropis* spp., *[[Gambusia regani]]*, *[[Flexipenis vittatus]]*, *[[Poecilia sphenops]]*, *[[Poecilia latipunctata]]*, *[[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]]*, and *[[Cichlasoma steindachneri]]*.
- Canal habitats near Ciudad Mante shared nearly the same species assemblage as the Rio Guayalejo (minus catfish), plus two additional poeciliids: *[[Gambusia affinis]]* and *[[Xiphophorus variatus]]*.

## Key Findings

1. **Range extension:** This study substantially increases known collection localities for *Poecilia formosa* and *Gambusia myersi* within the Rio Tamesi system, previously known from only one or two records each (Darnell 1962; Hubbs and Hubbs 1932; Rosen and Bailey 1963).
2. **Taxonomic uncertainty of *Gambusia myersi*:** The authors present multiple lines of morphological evidence (dorsal/anal ray counts, absence of dark lateral pigmentation and gravid spot, gonopodial elbow strength) suggesting the Tamesi *Gambusia* form may not correspond to Ahl's original *G. modesta*/*G. myersi* description, and could instead represent a form of *[[Gambusia affinis]]*. They recommend either designating *G. myersi* a *nomen dubium* or formally synonymizing it with *G. affinis*, with the Tamesi population potentially requiring a new name if types cannot be relocated.
3. **Diagnostic dorsal ray counts** distinguish sympatric *Poecilia formosa* (11–12 rays) from *Poecilia sphenops* (8–9 rays) at shared localities.
4. **Distinctive gonopodial trait** (fused anterior/posterior branches of ray four) in the Tamesi *Gambusia* is not unique — it also occurs in a Rio Pesqueria *G. affinis* population and an undescribed Cuban *Gambusia*, weakening its use as a diagnostic character for *G. myersi*.
5. **Detailed live-color description** of *G. myersi* is provided for the first time in this level of detail, documenting sexual dichromatism (bright yellow/blue males vs. duskier females) and complex cross-hatched pigmentation patterns present even in juveniles.
6. **Community ecology:** *P. formosa* shows a strong ecological association with submerged aquatic vegetation and consistently co-occurs with *P. sphenops* across all sampled habitats, suggesting habitat overlap and shared microhabitat preference.
