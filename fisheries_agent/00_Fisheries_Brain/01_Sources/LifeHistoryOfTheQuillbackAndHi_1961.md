#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by David Vanicek on the Des Moines River (Boone County, Iowa) in 1960, compares the age, growth, and life history of the [[Quillback Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes cyprinus*) and the [[Highfin Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes velifer*). Utilizing electrofishing and seining techniques, researchers analyzed scale samples to determine age and growth patterns. The study found that while the length-weight relationship, body-scale relationship, and coefficient of condition are nearly identical between the two species, the Quillback is definitively a larger and faster-growing fish compared to the Highfin. The research also noted significant difficulty in distinguishing juvenile specimens (under 3 inches) from other sympatric species, specifically the [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*) and the rare [[Plains Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes forbesi*).

## Background & Description
- **Identification Difficulty:** Carpsuckers under 3 inches in length could not be reliably distinguished by species and were excluded from age/growth analysis.
- **Scale Structure:** Annuli (growth rings) were identifiable via incomplete circuli ("cutting over"), discontinuous circuli, and relative spacing between rings. The antero-lateral portion of the scale provided the clearest annuli identification.
- **Body-Scale Regression Equations:**
  - **Quillback:** $L = 2.67 + 1.075(S)$
  - **Highfin:** $L = 2.49 + 1.03(S)$
  - *(Where L = total length in inches; S = anterior scale radius x43)*
- **Length-Weight Relationship (Log-transformed):**
  - **Quillback:** $Log W = -1.4912 + 3.184 (\log L)$ (Weight increases slightly faster than the cube of length)
  - **Highfin:** $Log W = -1.3655 + 2.997 (\log L)$ (Weight increases almost exactly with the cube of length)
- **Growth Trend:** Initially, Highfin carpsuckers weigh slightly more than Quillbacks at a given length, but Quillbacks surpass them in weight as length increases.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Segregation:** Habitat preference shifted with seasonal water levels.
  - **High Water (June):** No distinct habitat preference detected between species.
  - **Mid Water (July):** [[Highfin Carpsucker]] favored riffle areas; [[Quillback Carpsucker]] favored deeper waters.
  - **Low Water (August):** Both species, including Quillbacks, shifted primarily to riffle areas.
- **Water Quality:** The river transitioned from deep and turbid in early summer to shallow (wadable) and clear by late summer. Secchi disk readings ranged from 4.5 inches (June) to 14 inches (July).
- **Spawning/Recruitment:** Annulus formation (indicating the end of the first year's rapid growth phase) occurred in June for Highfins and the first half of July for Quillbacks. This variability suggests an extended spawning period, corroborated by [[Buchholz 1957]] data on the related River Carpsucker suggesting a May-July spawning window.
- **Community Context:** Coexists with the more abundant [[River Carpsucker]] and the rare [[Plains Carpsucker]] in the same river reach.

## Key Findings
1. **Growth Rate Disparity:** The Quillback Carpsucker grows significantly faster and reaches a larger overall size than the Highfin Carpsucker, despite similar first-year growth patterns.
2. **Condition Factor (K):** The average condition factor (a measure of "plumpness") was nearly identical between species: **43** for Highfin and **42** for Quillback. Both species became more robust (higher K) as the summer progressed, regardless of age or length.
3. **Comparative Growth (Interspecific):** The [[River Carpsucker]] outgrows both the Quillback and Highfin up until the 6th year of life, at which point the Quillback's growth rate surpasses the River Carpsucker.
4. **Geographic Growth Variation:** Highfin Carpsuckers in the Illinois River, Oklahoma (Jenkins et al. 1952) grew substantially faster (reaching 13.5 inches by year 5) than those in the Des Moines River, likely due to a longer regional growing season.
5. **Age Determination Challenges:** The timing of annulus formation is highly variable within a single age class, meaning fish captured early in the "growing season" may be aged one year younger than their true biological age if the annulus has not yet formed.
6. **Regional Validation:** The Quillback's average condition factor (42) closely matched previous findings in Iowa ponds (44) by [[Carlander and Moorman 1949]], while the Highfin's factor (43) matched Oklahoma river populations (42) studied by [[Jenkins, Leonard, and Hall 1952]].
