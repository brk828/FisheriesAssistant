## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative letter from the International Boundary and Water Commission (USIBWC) to the Bureau of Reclamation, responding to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's (USFWS) Final Biological and Conference Opinion (BO) on Lower Colorado River Operations and Maintenance. The letter concerns river management activities spanning from Lake Mead to the Southerly International Boundary (SIB) with Mexico. Rather than presenting primary scientific data, this correspondence confirms agreement with a Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) comprising seventeen provisions designed to mitigate jeopardy to three federally protected species: the [[Bonytail Chub]], the [[Razorback Sucker]], and the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher (a non-fish avian species included for ecosystem context). No morphological, physiological, or population-specific biological data are presented in the source text itself; the letter references but does not reproduce the underlying biological findings of the USFWS BO.

No morphological data for the [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] are provided in this source document. The letter is procedural in nature and defers to the (unincluded) USFWS Biological and Conference Opinion for species-specific biological and physical descriptions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System**: [[Lower Colorado River]], spanning from Lake Mead downstream to the Southerly International Boundary (SIB) with Mexico.
- **Management Context**: River operations and maintenance activities conducted by the Bureau of Reclamation's Lower Colorado Regional Office are identified as having the potential to jeopardize the continued existence of the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], indicating these species inhabit reaches of the river directly affected by flow regulation, maintenance operations, and habitat alteration.
- **Co-occurring Species of Concern**: The Southwestern Willow Flycatcher, a riparian-obligate bird species, is jointly addressed in the same regulatory action, indicating overlapping riparian/aquatic habitat dependencies along the same river corridor.
- **Institutional Framework**: The species are subject to conservation planning under the developing [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP), suggesting a multi-species, ecosystem-level management approach to the Lower Colorado River corridor.

## Key Findings

- The USFWS issued a Final Biological and Conference Opinion (BO) on May 28, 1997, determining that proposed Lower Colorado River operations and maintenance activities (Lake Mead to SIB) would jeopardize the continued existence of the [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and Southwestern Willow Flycatcher.
- A Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) containing **seventeen (17) provisions** was developed to protect these species from jeopardy while allowing continued river operations.
- The USIBWC's review found **no factual errors** in the biological data presented in the BO and expressed **general agreement** with all seventeen RPA provisions.
- A public meeting on the BO was held on June 18, 1997, attended by USIBWC representation (Alton Goff, Yuma Field Office Manager).
- The correspondence signals intent for continued interagency cooperation on long-term conservation planning via the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]], indicating this BO served as a precursor or contributing input to that broader program.
- This document functions as a **procedural/regulatory artifact** under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (Section 7 consultation process) rather than a primary source of ichthyological research data.
