# Faunistic Research in Phreatic Groundwaters of the Maghreb and Egypt

#marine-biology #crustacea #groundwater-fauna

## Executive Summary

This paper reports the results of four collecting missions (1977–1980) by the [[Istituto Di Zoologia L'Aquila]] in phreatic groundwater systems of Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia and Egypt. The authors sampled 121 stations, mostly fresh or slightly brackish wells, plus some rain cisterns, recording chemical-physical data, geographic data and the full biocenosis of each. Copepods, isopods and amphipods are discussed in detail, and several taxa are of systematic or biogeographic interest, including a new amphipod subspecies and a new amphipod species, a harpacticoid genus record for the region, and isopods that appear to be transitional between marine and freshwater *Microcharon*. The fauna is mostly widespread stygophilic or stygoxenous forms, so truly groundwater-adapted taxa are few.

## Background & Description

The study belongs to a Mediterranean programme on freshwater-interstitial and phreatic faunas, funded by [[Consiglio Nazionale delle Ricerche]] contracts (CT 78.01343.04 and CT 79.00988.04). The authors' stated aim was to survey subterranean fauna, not surface waters, and salt lakes such as the Chotts were explicitly excluded from sampling.

Four missions were carried out:

- **Algeria** (Oct 1977): 50 stations, mainly wells in the pre-desert zone around Biskra, Touggourt and El Oued, plus the Djurdjura and Dirah/Ain-El-Hadjel areas.
- **Morocco** (May 1979): 51 stations, mainly wells between Chechaouen and Midelt, around Erfoud and Ouarzazate, and along the Casablanca–Marrakech road. A vehicle breakdown interrupted the first part of the route.
- **Tunisia** (Nov 1979): 9 stations, a low count attributed to dried or closed wells and piped water supplies, plus bad weather.
- **Egypt** (Oct 1980): 11 wells along the Nile, the Qena–Port Safaga road and the Red Sea coast.

Field parameters were measured with portable meters (YSI and Fisher), with water temperature, pH, salinity and external temperature all recorded. Samples were fixed in 5% [[Formalin]] and later stored in 70–80° alcohol.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most sampled wells were in heavily human-modified landscapes. Wells near the Nile and coastal Tunisia were scarce because people use rivers, canals and aqueducts. Many wells were abandoned, dried up or destroyed.

Groundwater depth varied considerably. Typical values included about 8 m in the Oasis des Ziban (Bou Saada–Biskra area), about 4.5 m near Touggourt, about 12.5 m near El Oued, roughly 15 m between Midelt and Erfoud, and about 28 m before Marrakech. Water temperatures ranged from about 7 °C (Jendouba, Tunisia) to about 27 °C (El Khobua, Algeria). pH was mostly 6.0–8.0. Sediments were typically fine to medium sand, with silt and clay in many sites.

The authors note that the Chotts (Melrhir, Mérouane, El Djerid) support highly specialised fauna, but they were outside the study scope. The [[Grand Erg Oriental]] and [[Nile River]] are important regional features for the sampled groundwater.

## Key Findings

**Scope and sampling**
- 121 stations were sampled in total, and each station record includes depth, water level, temperature, pH, sediment and the biocenosis list.
- Of these, 11 stations were recorded as azoic (no fauna). The records include 3 in Algeria, 6 in Morocco and 2 in Tunisia.
- Many taxa were identified only to family or order, and some groups (notably Diptera larvae and other insects) were still under study by outside specialists.

**Crustacea overall**
- Crustaceans (entomostracans and malacostracans) were the best-represented group, and copepods were the most abundant and frequent component.
- Other recurrent groups included oligochaetes, nematodes, rotifers, mites (Hydracarina), turbellarians, and insect larvae, mainly Diptera.

**Copepoda**
- The authors list 21 species and subspecies, but the stated breakdown (Cyclopoida 18, Arpacticoida 6, Calanoida 2) sums to 26. This discrepancy appears in the source and should be checked against the original.
- Most cyclopids are widespread stygophiles or stygoxenes, including [[Paracyclops fimbriatus]], [[Tropocyclops prasinus]], [[Eucyclops serrulatus]] and [[Macrocyclops albidus]].
- Eustygophilous forms include [[Cyclops abissorum mauritaniae]], [[Thermocyclops oblongatus]], [[Thermocyclops dybowskii]] and [[Microcyclops varicans]]. *Thermocyclops oblongatus* was well represented in coastal Moroccan, Tunisian and Egyptian systems but apparently absent from Algerian ones.
- Among harpacticoids, *Attheyella crassa*, *Canthocamptus staphylinus*, *Bryocamptus zschokkei* and *Nitocra lacustris* are common, widespread taxa. The authors note an unexpectedly low diversity of harpacticoids and the absence of strictly stygobiotic forms.
- Two new species of Ameiridae were found: [[Praeleptomesochra]] (a genus not previously recorded from regional groundwater) and a new [[Parapseudoleptomesochra]] species. The latter is close to *P. italica* and *P. hellenica* but is not yet formally named.
- Two calanoids were recorded: [[Calanipeda aquae-dulcis]] and [[Arctodiaptomus (Rhabdodiaptomus) salinus]]. The latter is typically a brackish or saline species, rarely found in groundwater, so its presence is ecologically notable.

**Isopoda**
- Three families were present: [[Asellid Isopods]], [[Microparasellid Isopods]] and [[Stenasellid Isopods]].
- Asellids were abundant in Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia. The authors assign the material to *Proasellus coxalis* cf. *peyerimhoffi* but doubt that the reported diagnostic characters separate this subspecies from the typical species, given their variability.
- Two lineages of *Proasellus* are recognised in North Africa: the recently colonising *P. coxalis* complex and the older *P. monstrosus* lineage, which includes more specialised subterranean forms.
- Microparasellids were found in Algeria (near Dirah and the Ziban oasis), Morocco (Sidi El Aydi) and Egypt (south-eastern desert). They appear to be absent from Tunisian phreatic systems.
- [[Microcharon marinus]] was found near Marrakech, far from the coast, which substantially extends its known range and suggests inland colonisation.
- [[Microcharon karamani]] and [[Microcharon zibani]], both described by Pesce & Tete (1978), show marine-type morphological characters. The authors interpret them as transitional forms, supporting a marine origin for the group.
- Stenasellids were represented by only two specimens from neighbouring wells at Bouchagroum, Algeria, one adult attributed to *Magnezia* and one juvenile not identifiable to genus.

**Amphipoda**
- Three genera were found: [[Salentinella]], [[Bogidiella]] and [[Metacrangonyx]].
- [[Salentinella angelieri]] was recorded from Derdara, Morocco, which is the first record of the genus from North Africa. The authors treat several synonymised names as forms of this variable species.
- [[Bogidiella ichnusae africana]] was described as a new subspecies from Bouchagroum, and is regarded as endemic to Algeria.
- [[Metacrangonyx spinicaudatus]] was described as a new species, regarded as endemic to Morocco, and is distinguished from related species by uropod form, accessory antennal flagellum and telson armature.

**Biogeographic and systematic significance**
- Records of stygobiont and eustygophile taxa from inland wells indicate that several marine-origin or coastal groups have penetrated continental groundwater.
- The authors describe the groundwater fauna of the region as mostly cosmopolitan or Palearctic, so few faunistic conclusions can be drawn from the widespread copepods.
- Their conclusion highlights the need for more detailed study of the new taxa, the *Proasellus* variability and the material still under specialist study.
