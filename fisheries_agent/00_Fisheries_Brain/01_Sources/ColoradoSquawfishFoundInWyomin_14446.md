# Colorado Squawfish Found in Wyoming

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A news report from the *Denver Post* (by Mary George) describes the first recorded [[Colorado Squawfish]] in Wyoming since 1962. A 27-inch adult was caught and released on August 20 from the [[Little Snake River]] just west of [[Baggs Wyoming]], close to the Colorado border. The [[Wyoming Game And Fish Department]] reported the capture. State and federal officials said the significance of the find was unclear and planned to sample the river for more fish. A [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] biologist said the find was not surprising, because adult fish move considerably. The article states that the find would not affect planned construction of a 60-acre-foot dam on a creek whose name is garbled in the scan.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The Colorado squawfish is an endangered freshwater cyprinid (common name used in the article).
- **Specimen:** One adult, 27 inches long, caught and released alive.
- **Date and place of capture:** August 20, Little Snake River, just west of Baggs, Wyoming, barely north of the Colorado state line.
- **Historical records:** The last previously recorded Wyoming specimen came from the [[Green River]] before the [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] closed, reported as 28 years earlier.
- **Reporting sources:** [[Wyoming Game And Fish Department]] (capture report); [[John Hamill]], wildlife biologist in [[Vernal Utah]], quoted on the find.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The [[Little Snake River]] flows into the [[Yampa River]], which the article describes as an important habitat for the endangered squawfish.
- Hamill stated that adult squawfish "tend to move around quite a bit," which he offered as a reason the find was not surprising.
- Officials considered two possibilities: the fish could be a straggler that moved upstream, or it could be part of a resident community in the area. The article does not resolve which is true.
- The article does not report water chemistry, population counts, habitat measurements, or other quantitative ecological data.

## Key Findings

1. **First Wyoming record since 1962:** An adult Colorado squawfish (27 inches) was caught and released on August 20 in the Little Snake River near Baggs.
2. **Significance unclear:** State and federal officials said they did not know whether the find indicates a straggler or a resident population.
3. **Planned follow-up:** Officials planned to sample the river for more squawfish.
4. **Mobility of adults:** Hamill attributed the find partly to adult movement, which he described as common.
5. **No project impact stated:** Hamill said the find would not affect plans for the 60-acre-foot dam on a creek whose name is illegible in the scan.
6. **Historical context:** The previous Wyoming record was from the Green River before the Flaming Gorge Dam closed, about 28 years before the article.
