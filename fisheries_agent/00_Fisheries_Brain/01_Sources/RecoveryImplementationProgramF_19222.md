# Water Acquisition Recommendations for Endangered Fish Recovery in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This Management Group report, prepared by the [[Water Acquisition Ad Hoc Subcommittee]] for the [[Recovery Implementation Program]], sets out recommendations for acquiring water rights to convert to [[Instream Flow]] for endangered fishes in the Upper [[Colorado River]] Basin. It was approved by the Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee at its winter meeting on February 21, 1990. The recommendations cover seven topics: general acquisition policy, the [[Yampa River]], the [[Little Snake River]], the [[15-Mile Reach]] and the Colorado River below the [[Gunnison River]], the [[White River]], the [[Farm Foreclosure Program]], and [[Conditional Water Rights]].

The document is a policy and planning report, not a research study. It contains no population, abundance, or fish survey data. Its main outputs are a set of numbered recommendations with deadlines, funding commitments, and rationale.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Committee asked the Management Group to develop ways to speed up water acquisition. On September 13, 1989, an ad hoc subcommittee was formed. It met on October 3 and November 3, 1989, and again in January 1990. Its recommendations were reviewed, modified, and adopted by the Management Group.

Key actors include the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS, "the Service"), the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB), the [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]] (River District), [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), the [[Bureau of Land Management]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Grand Junction Office), and the Wyoming and Utah water agencies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report deals with habitat for endangered fishes, but it names no species. Its ecological concerns are:

- **Yampa River:** The [[Juniper-Cross Mountain Conditional Water Right]] is described as the controlling right on the Yampa mainstem. Its acquisition could greatly benefit endangered fishes in the Lower Yampa River.
- **15-Mile Reach:** Flow needs are highest in July through September. Hydrology is complex, so a water availability study is needed before an instream flow filing.
- **Colorado River below the Gunnison:** Flow needs are uncertain until the [[Aspinall Unit]] [[Section 7 Consultation]] produces recommendations.
- **White River:** No flow recommendations exist. The habitat and life-stage use of endangered fishes is poorly defined, and research is needed first.
- **Wetlands:** Removing water rights from farm properties for instream flows may affect adjacent wetlands, and those impacts would need to be addressed.

## Key Findings

**1. General policy**
- The Management Group will report on water acquisition to the Committee before each meeting.
- A water rights engineer and appraiser will be retained "as needed," with services available by September 30, 1990 (OCR reads "1998").
- Every acquisition (purchase, donation, or instream flow appropriation) must be evaluated so that Program costs match expected benefits.

**2. Yampa River**
- The CWCB may use the Phase II interim flow recommendations for case-by-case acquisitions, provided the acquisition is smaller than the interim flows.
- Refined Phase III flow recommendations must be developed before the Board can file for instream flow appropriations. A filing requires the Board to find that water is available, that a natural environment will be preserved to a reasonable degree, and that flows can be met without material injury to other rights.
- The Yampa Basin Feasibility Study, jointly funded by the Program, CWCB, and River District for FY90 and FY91, aims to identify alternative Upper Yampa water projects consistent with endangered species protection. It is due in April 1992.
- The [[Cross Mountain Ranch]] Winter Unit holds senior absolute irrigation rights on the Yampa mainstem and about 27,088 deeded acres on 14.5 river miles. The unit was offered for $2.5 million. TNC will fund a two-part appraisal, due by March 1996.
- The Service, BLM, and TNC will sign a disposition agreement for the Cross Mountain Ranch water rights once an option to purchase is secured.

**3. Little Snake River**
- The FY90 budget allocates $37,598 of Section 7 funds for a technical evaluation and appraisal of conditional water rights in Colorado and Wyoming. The text also gives $37,588 in one place, so the exact figure should be verified.
- Under a cooperative agreement with Wyoming, Phase I modeling will assess the Pothook, Three Forks, and Powderwash conditional rights, estimated at two to three months. Modeling will not cover changing points of diversion, which would be costly and raise interstate legal questions.
- Phase II appraisal will assess the probability of development over time, assumed uses (agricultural, municipal, industrial), dollar value ranges, discount rates, and present-worth analyses. Standard appraisal methods will run alongside.
- Agreement execution is targeted for March 1, 1998 (as printed), with project completion by August 1, 1998.
- The River District may donate the Pothook and Powderwash rights. Action is deferred until the appraisal and CWCB review are complete.
- Firming up junior filings with senior conditional rights may help meet the Program's "sufficient progress" requirement, but that credit must be negotiated among participants.

**4. 15-Mile Reach and Colorado River below the Gunnison**
- The Service has issued July, August, and September flow recommendations for the reach between Palisades and the Gunnison. The CWCB found them adequate for an appropriation in some amount.
- A detailed water availability study is needed first. The CWCB will start it in 1996 (as printed), contingent on funding, with completion targeted for 1991. A filing decision follows in FY91.
- A new flow methodology for year-round recommendations began in FY90, with completion expected in FY92.
- The first priority is purchasing existing absolute water rights that can be converted to instream flows.
- Ruedi Reservoir leasing is the most immediate option for dry-year releases. This could happen without an instream flow appropriation. Joint evaluation is due by December 1996 (or 1998, as printed in the final list). Congressional cancellation of Ruedi repayment debt was judged unacceptable on the West Slope.
- Yamcolo Project return flows could reach the reach only in June and July, when their volume is too small to benefit habitat, so they were judged insufficient.
- Industrial Resources, Inc. holds a 1965-decree conditional right for 2,900 cfs in nine of ten years at Horsethief Canyon. The Management Group recommends no action on it.
- Acquisitions below the Gunnison are deferred until Section 7 flow recommendations are complete. Program-funded work plans are due by September 30, 1998.

**5. White River**
- No flow recommendations exist. Before they can be developed, research is needed.
- The Service will produce a research work plan by May 1, 1998 (as printed), with a literature review as the first task.
- The Service will report on the 404 Permit for the White River Dam and the basis of the earlier biological opinion by March 1, 1990 (as printed).
- The text says the White River Dam 404 Permit expires in December 1998.

**6. Farm Foreclosure Program**
- A recently passed federal law lets FWS receive foreclosure notices for USDA-administered farm loans. Associated water rights may be acquired at no cost, but removing them may affect adjacent wetlands.
- The Service will develop screening criteria (location, consumptive yield, minimum quantities) for field staff. The evaluations will include a simple historic consumptive use assessment.
- The Recovery Program Director will recommend action or no action to the Management Group after consulting the CWCB or Utah Department of Natural Resources. The CWCB and Utah Division of Water Rights will help convert approved rights to instream flows under state law.

**7. Conditional Water Rights**
- Colorado law is unsettled. The Division No. 5 Water Court has approved changing conditional rights for municipal, industrial, and agricultural use to instream flows, but the Colorado Supreme Court has not ruled. The TNC and CWCB are attempting such a conversion in the [[Black Canyon of the Gunnison]], and a final decree may take one to two years.
- The Program should not actively pursue conditional rights for change to instream flows until the courts resolve the issue. It also should not firm up junior instream appropriations until a technical and CWCB review is complete.
- The Program or CWCB may accept donations or purchases of conditional rights only if three conditions are met: (a) the rights have significant value given their priority and potential volume; (b) the deal does not interfere with higher-priority acquisitions; and (c) it does not impose substantial legal burdens to prove diligence.
