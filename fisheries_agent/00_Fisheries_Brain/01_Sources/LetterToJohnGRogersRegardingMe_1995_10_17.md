# Letter to John G. Rogers Regarding Memorandum of Agreement for Lower Colorado River Species Conservation Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #EndangeredSpeciesAct #ColoradoRiver

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1995 advocacy letter from Defenders of Wildlife to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, contesting a Memorandum of Agreement ("MOA") intended to precede development of a multi-species Habitat Conservation Plan ("HCP") for the [[Lower Colorado River]]. While the letter is fundamentally a legal and regulatory critique under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA), it contains substantive references to two endangered freshwater fish species native to the Lower Colorado River — the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]] — as the primary aquatic taxa of conservation concern. The letter argues that the MOA grants "up front" regulatory assurance to nonfederal water and power entities (Arizona, California, Nevada agencies) before any actual conservation plan or interim conservation measures ("ICMs") targeting these fish are implemented, potentially permitting continued habitat degradation (dam and reservoir operation) without corresponding recovery actions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: Both focal fish species inhabit the mainstem and associated waters of the [[Lower Colorado River]], a system heavily modified by dam construction, reservoir impoundment, and water/power diversion infrastructure operated under the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].
- **Anthropogenic pressures**: The letter explicitly identifies "dams and reservoirs" as the activities that "have brought these endangered species to the brink of extinction," implicating altered flow regimes, thermal regimes, and habitat connectivity loss as key drivers of decline.
- **Co-occurring species of concern**: The [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], a riparian-obligate bird, is cited as an example of a non-fish species dependent on riparian habitat along the same river corridor — illustrating that conservation measures narrowly targeted at fish (e.g., "fish hatcheries") would not address impacts to other listed taxa sharing the ecosystem.
- **Conservation management framework**: The fish exist within a regulatory ecological niche shaped by [[Section 7 Consultation]] (federal recovery duty) and [[Section 10 Habitat Conservation Plan]] (nonfederal take-avoidance duty) processes under the ESA — a distinction the letter argues is being improperly blurred by the MOA.

## Key Findings

- **Two ESA-listed fish species named**: The [[Bonytail Chub]] (Gila elegans) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus) are identified as the specific endangered fish of the Lower Colorado River requiring urgent conservation action; the MOA signatories "acknowledged" an "immediate need to work toward preventing the extinction" of both.
- **No binding commitment to species-specific action**: Despite naming these species, the letter finds the MOA contains "nothing... that commits the states to doing anything" concretely for the Bonytail Chub or Razorback Sucker — Interim Conservation Measures (ICMs) are only "intended" to be "developed," not mandated.
- **Regulatory assurance decoupled from species benefit**: Per the draft Sufficient Progress Agreement (SPA), "the success of ICMs and the level of funding dedicated... to ICM implementation are not intended to be the basis for gauging sufficient progress" — meaning regulatory protection for water/power projects is granted irrespective of actual conservation outcomes for the two fish species.
- **Dam operations implicated but insulated from change**: The Bureau of Reclamation's ongoing Section 7 consultation on [[Colorado River Dam Operations]] — which could have triggered a jeopardy opinion and forced operational changes benefiting fish habitat — is described as being "wrapped into" the HCP process and rendered "dormant" for the three-year MOA term, preventing near-term operational reform that might benefit the Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker.
- **Insufficiency of narrow fish-focused mitigation**: The letter notes that even full implementation of fish-specific ICMs would be ecologically insufficient, since other listed taxa (e.g., the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher) depend on the same riparian/riverine system and require distinct habitat protections not addressed by fish-focused measures.
- **Precedent-setting policy risk**: The letter frames the MOA as a potential template that decouples endangered species recovery obligations from actual, implemented conservation plans — a precedent it argues undermines the ESA's structural requirement (Section 10) that incidental take permits follow, rather than precede, an approved [[Habitat Conservation Plan]].
