# Review of 1993 Humpback Chub Propagation Study Plan

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #hatchery-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a critical review letter dated March 10, 1993, from C.O. Minckley (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Parker, AZ) to Bill Persons (Arizona Game and Fish Department) regarding a propagation study plan for [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) developed by Diann Smith. The reviewer expresses significant concerns about the complexity of the proposed plan, arguing that multiple experimental protocols jeopardize the primary objective of successfully collecting and transporting viable spawn from the [[Lower Colorado River]] to [[Bubbling Springs]] hatchery. Key criticisms include unnecessary field experiments, questionable water chemistry testing, and concerns about project leadership expertise.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) is an endangered species native to the [[Colorado River]] system. This correspondence addresses a 1993 propagation study plan following previous failures in spawn collection and transport. The plan proposed collecting eggs from wild fish in the [[Lower Colorado River]] (LCR) and transporting them to [[Bubbling Springs]] hatchery for rearing. The operation was designed to work within a 2-3 day window, with helicopter transport to the south rim of [[Grand Canyon]] (20 minutes flight time). Previous attempts had encountered problems with sperm diluent, sediment, heath tray issues, egg transport trauma, and temperature control.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Lower Colorado River]] represents the natural spawning habitat for this [[Humpback Chub]] population. The reviewer emphasizes that LCR water chemistry is inherently compatible with chub eggs, stating "How many millennia do the fish have to spawn there to prove it is compatible?" This reflects the fundamental principle that native water conditions should be optimal for the species' reproductive success. The plan proposed comparing LCR water versus [[Bubbling Springs]] water for egg incubation, which the reviewer considers inappropriate given the fish's evolutionary history in LCR conditions. Sediment concerns were raised in the original plan, though the reviewer dismisses these unless eggs remain in unaerated water. The reviewer suggests that Arizona Game and Fish personnel could pre-settle LCR water and prepare acclimation concentrations in advance, simplifying field logistics.

## Key Findings

**Critical Concerns with Study Design:**
- The plan is "too complicated" with the main goal "overshadowed by a presumption of failure and the proliferation of experiments"
- Multiple simultaneous experiments jeopardize the primary objective of spawn retrieval
- Proposed experiments include: sperm diluent necessity, LCR water vs. [[Bubbling Springs]] water compatibility, and dewatered vs. watered egg transport

**Water Chemistry Recommendations:**
- Use LCR water exclusively; abandon [[Bubbling Springs]] water experiments
- The proposed allocation (90% eggs in bubbling springs water, 10% in LCR water) could "very succinctly killed 90% of the spawn" if bubbling springs water proves incompatible
- Pre-settle LCR water before field operations; aerate with air stones and pure oxygen if needed
- Prepare acclimation concentrations in advance at [[Bubbling Springs]]

**Experimental Protocol Concerns:**
- Field experiments are inappropriate given the 2-3 day operational window
- The dewatering experiment could reduce viable spawn to 60% of original collection
- No clear decision criteria for sperm diluent testing (sample size: one fish, five, ten?)
- Unclear fate of eggs after experimental decisions are made

**Operational Recommendations:**
- Focus solely on successful spawn removal, not concurrent experiments
- Establish contingency plans for helicopter service interruptions
- Clarify number of target females and total egg collection goals
- Specify whether individual fish spawn will be kept separate and why
- Determine whether collection continues after reaching target numbers

**Leadership and Coordination Issues:**
- Reviewer requires an experienced hatchery person with hands-on spawning expertise to lead operations
- While acknowledging Diann Smith's commitment and effort, reviewer states she "does not have the expertise to conduct field spawning of humpback chubs"
- No coordination mentioned with [[Arizona State University]], BIO/WEST, or Owen Gorman's group
- Previous year's accidental coordination "seemed to work" and could improve efficiency

**Conservation Philosophy:**
- Population is "not an inexhaustible resource"
- Must prioritize "conserving what is left instead of repeatedly assuming we are not impacting the population by repeated removal of fish/spawn"
- Reviewer states if effort fails, will be "very adamant to see it repeated under similar circumstances"

**Review Process Concerns:**
- Timeline for review and incorporation of suggestions is "much too short to reasonably incorporate everyone's suggestions and still get in the field this spring"
