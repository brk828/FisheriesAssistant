# Growth Rates of Paddlefish Receiving Supplemental Feeding in Fertilized Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Polyodontidae #freshwater-aquaculture #Paddlefish

## Executive Summary
This 1964 field study by Hugh A. Swingle examined growth performance of juvenile [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*) collected from the Tallapoosa River and stocked into two fertilized experimental ponds receiving supplemental feeding (Auburn No. 2 fish food). One pond was co-stocked with [[Channel Catfish]], [[White Catfish]], and [[Largemouth Bass]]; the other with two [[Tilapia]] species (*Tilapia mossambica*, *Tilapia nilotica*) and Largemouth Bass. Over roughly 114–144 days, paddlefish exhibited substantial weight gains (139.7% and 162.1% aggregate increases, respectively), demonstrating that Paddlefish can achieve rapid growth in pond aquaculture settings under fertilization and supplemental feeding regimes, independent of species assemblage.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Polyodon spathula* (American Paddlefish), family [[Polyodontidae]].
- **Size range at stocking**: 28.5–43 inches in length; 1.50–11.25 pounds in weight.
- **Size range at recovery**: 38.0–57.25 inches in length; 6.94–26.50 pounds in weight.
- Growth was tracked via paired length and weight measurements at stocking and recovery, indicating substantial increases in both linear and mass dimensions over the study period.
- Individual growth was variable; smaller/younger fish (e.g., Fish 1 in Table 2, 28.5 in → 38.0 in) showed the highest percent weight increases (363%), while larger fish showed proportionally smaller percent gains despite greater absolute mass increases.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Source population**: Wild paddlefish collected from the **Tallapoosa River**, below Tallassee, Alabama.
- **Experimental habitat**: Two fertilized freshwater ponds (one of undetermined size, one 1.95-acre) enriched with fertilizer and supplemental feed (Auburn No. 2 fish food), simulating a productive, eutrophic pond ecosystem.
- **Co-occurring species**:
  - Pond 1: [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*).
  - Pond 2: [[Tilapia Mossambica]], [[Tilapia Nilotica]], Largemouth Bass.
- Paddlefish, typically planktivorous filter feeders in riverine/lacustrine systems, were raised here alongside benthic/piscivorous species, suggesting a mixed trophic pond environment where paddlefish likely exploited plankton blooms stimulated by fertilization, supplemented by direct feeding.
- This study represents an early application of **pond aquaculture techniques** to a normally riverine, plankton-feeding species, highlighting the potential for [[Paddlefish Aquaculture]] under managed nutrient conditions.

## Key Findings
- **Pond 1** (Channel Catfish, White Catfish, Largemouth Bass; stocked July 4, recovered Oct. 22 — 114 days):
  - 7 paddlefish; total weight increased from 34 lbs to 81.51 lbs (**+139.7%**).
  - Individual percent weight increases ranged from 103% (Fish 7) to 233% (Fish 1).

- **Pond 2** (Tilapia mossambica, Tilapia nilotica, Largemouth Bass; stocked June 19, recovered Nov. 10 — 144 days; 1.95-acre pond):
  - 10 paddlefish total; total weight increased from 47.25 lbs to 123.83 lbs (**+162.1%**).
  - Data reported for 4 representative fish; percent increases ranged from 127% to 363%.
  - Table 2 summary:
    | Fish | Stocked Length (in) | Stocked Weight (lb) | Recovered Length (in) | Recovered Weight (lb) | % Weight Increase |

- Paddlefish in the tilapia-stocked pond achieved a higher overall percent weight increase (162.1%) than those in the catfish/bass pond (139.7%), potentially reflecting differences in pond productivity, plankton availability, or stocking duration (144 vs. 114 days).
- Smaller/younger paddlefish showed disproportionately higher relative growth rates than larger individuals, consistent with typical fish growth curves where juvenile stages exhibit faster relative mass gain.
- Findings support the feasibility of using **fertilized, supplementally-fed ponds** for rapid growth of paddlefish, with implications for aquaculture, stock enhancement, and conservation propagation programs for this species.
