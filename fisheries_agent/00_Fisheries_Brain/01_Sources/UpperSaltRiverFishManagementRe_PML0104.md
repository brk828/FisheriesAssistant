# Upper Salt River Spot Creel Survey, 1986–1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Fish Management Report, prepared under [[Federal Aid In Sport Fish Restoration]] project F-7-M-33 (Statewide Fisheries Investigations), summarizes two seasons of spot creel surveys of catfish anglers on the [[Upper Salt River]] and [[Roosevelt Lake]]. The 1986 survey compared harvest rates and sizes between the upper river and the reservoir and aged [[Channel Catfish]]. The 1987 survey evaluated harvest rates under high and low river flows and tested bait success. The study found that [[Channel Catfish]] dominated the harvest, the [[Salt River]] reach had the best catch rates, and flow levels above about 4,000 cfs did not appear to limit harvest. Recommended follow-up work includes spring population surveys and creel surveys from April to August 1992.

## Background & Description

- **Report:** Upper Salt River Fish Management Report, Spot Creel Survey, 1986 and 1987, issued February 1991 by the Regional Fisheries Specialist (Region VI) with the Regional Fisheries Program Biologist. The scan does not clearly name the second author.
- **Funding context:** Sport Fish Restoration and boating access facilities, with the cover page referencing purchases of fishing equipment and motorboat fuel.
- **Study areas:**
  - 1986: Upper Salt River above and including Roosevelt Lake, May–July 1986.
  - 1987: Upper Salt River from [[Diversion Dam]] down to [[Salt Delta]], March–July 1987.
- **Objectives:**
  1. Compare harvest rates and sizes of catfish caught by anglers in the upper Salt River and Roosevelt Lake (1986 and 1987).
  2. Determine age and growth of channel catfish (1986).
  3. Evaluate harvest rates during high and low river flows (1987).
  4. Determine successful catfish baits (1987).
- **Method:** Random roving [[Creel Survey]] interviews, with harvest expressed as fish per hour (FPH).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regime:**
  - In 1986, flows were declining through the survey period.
  - In 1987, spring run-off flows of roughly 1,000 to 3,800 cfs (as read from the scanned text) were sampled in March–June, followed by summer flows declining from about 1,000 to 150 cfs in June and July.
- **Water temperature:** Ranged from 21 to 23 °C during the 1986 surveys.
- **Angler composition:** In 1986, most anglers interviewed were shore anglers (reported as 0.82) and were targeting channel catfish (reported as 0.96). In 1987, only shore anglers were interviewed, primarily on the Salt River.
- **Seasonal pattern:** The document concludes that the prime channel catfishing period is April through June.

## Key Findings

### 1986 Survey (May–July, Upper Salt River and Roosevelt Lake)

- **Overall harvest:** The average harvest rate was 0.12 FPH. Six flathead catfish were creeled, which is a small fraction of the catch.
- **Reach comparison:** The Salt River had the best harvest rate (0.50 FPH) and Roosevelt Lake had the worst (0.07 FPH).
- **Size structure:** Of the channel catfish measured (262 fish), most were 200–350 mm. The largest share fell in the 250–299 mm class.
- **Monthly results:** June had the highest harvest rate (0.59 FPH). The highest success rate was recorded in May.
- **Successful baits:** Channel catfish anglers reported the following baits as most successful: liver (about 43%), followed by worms (about 16%), minnows (about 14%), and one additional bait type (about 13%).
- **Age and growth:** Spines from harvested fish were analyzed by Kirk Young (ASU Zoology Department). Back-calculated lengths by age are summarized in [[Age And Growth Analysis]] (Table 3).

### 1987 Survey (March–July, Diversion Dam to Salt Delta)

- **Harvest rate:** The overall harvest rate was 0.36 FPH.
- **Flathead catfish:** These contributed only 0.01 FPH to the harvest.
- **Length frequency:** Table 4 gives monthly length-frequency data for channel catfish, with size classes running from 150 mm to over 760 mm. Samples were collected from March through July.

### Conclusions

- Flows below about 4,000 cfs do not appear to be a factor in channel catfish harvest rates below the Diversion Dam.
- Harvest rates were higher during the declining flows of 1986 (0.50 FPH on the Salt River) than during the higher spring flows of 1987 (0.36 FPH).
- April through June is the prime period for channel catfishing success.

### Recommendations

1. Conduct spring catfish population surveys to monitor channel and flathead catfish levels.
2. Conduct creel surveys from April to August 1992 to compare harvest rates with population survey data from the same period.
