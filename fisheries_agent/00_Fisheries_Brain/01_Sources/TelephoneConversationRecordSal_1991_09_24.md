## Executive Summary
This record documents a brief internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service communication reporting field survey results for the [[Gila Topminnow]] in Salt Creek, Arizona (Site S3, near Bylas). Conducted on September 23–24, 1991, by Stewart Jacks and Stuart Leon, the survey sampled the creek reach from a barrier structure to the headspring. Of approximately 250–300 fish collected, only 14 individuals (~5%) were identified as Gila topminnow, with the overwhelming majority being non-native [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia* sp.), highlighting the species' precarious status and the competitive/invasive pressure it faces within this remnant habitat.

## Background & Description
No morphological data (e.g., length, weight, coloration, meristics) were reported in this source. The document is a field survey communication focused on presence/absence and relative abundance rather than specimen-level morphological description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Desert spring-fed stream system (Salt Creek), a tributary-associated habitat within the [[Gila River Basin]].
- **Survey extent:** Sampling spanned from a physical barrier structure upstream to the natural headspring, suggesting habitat fragmentation management or an isolation strategy possibly intended to protect native fish from invasive competitors.
- **Spatial distribution within site:** Gila topminnow were found predominantly at the road crossing location, with only a single individual detected at the headspring itself, indicating uneven distribution across the sampled reach.
- **Co-occurring species:** [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis* or *G. holbrooki*), a widely introduced competitor known to displace native poeciliids such as the [[Gila Topminnow]] through competitive exclusion and possible predation on young.
- **Community composition:** Mosquitofish vastly outnumbered topminnow (~94–95% of the catch), consistent with well-documented patterns of native topminnow decline in the presence of invasive *Gambusia* across Arizona desert streams.

## Key Findings
- Total catch: approximately 250–300 fish across the two-day survey.
- Native [[Gila Topminnow]] comprised only 14 individuals of the total catch — a minor fraction of the community.
- The remainder of the catch consisted of non-native [[Mosquitofish]], underscoring significant biotic pressure on the native species.
- Topminnow occurrence was concentrated at the road crossing site, not evenly distributed throughout the barrier-to-headspring reach.
- Only one topminnow was recorded at the headspring, potentially indicating this refuge area holds a smaller or more marginal population segment.
- The data point to an ongoing conservation concern for the [[Gila Topminnow]] in [[Salt Creek]], consistent with its status as an endangered/threatened native fish in Arizona's Gila River drainage, and reinforce the need for continued monitoring and possible removal/control of invasive [[Mosquitofish]] populations.
