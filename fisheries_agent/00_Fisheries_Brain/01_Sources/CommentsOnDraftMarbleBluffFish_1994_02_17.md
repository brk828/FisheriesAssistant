## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper but an interagency review memo from the **Cui-ui Recovery Team** critiquing the draft "Marble Bluff Fish Facility (MBFF) Station Management Plan." The memo addresses fish passage operations for the endangered **[[Cui-ui]]** (*Chasmistes cujus*), a Lahontan Basin catostomid endemic to Pyramid Lake, Nevada, along with co-occurring migratory species **[[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]]** and **[[Tahoe Sucker]]**. The comments focus on fishway/ladder design, operational procedures, mortality events during the 1993 spawning run, and the plan's failure to integrate with the broader **[[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]]**. While largely administrative in tone, the memo contains embedded fish-passage engineering and mortality observations relevant to conservation hydraulics at fish facilities.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body measurements) are provided in this source. The document is procedural/administrative rather than descriptive. Species implicated are noted only taxonomically:
- **Cui-ui** (*Chasmistes cujus*) — endemic lake sucker, primary focus of the facility and recovery team.
- **Lahontan Cutthroat Trout** (*Oncorhynchus clarkii henshawi*) — threatened salmonid also dependent on the same fishway.
- **Tahoe Sucker** (*Catostomus tahoensis*) — noted as an additional user of the facility for accessing spawning habitat, a fact the reviewers state was omitted from the draft plan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The **[[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]** (MBFF) functions as a fish passage structure allowing anadromous-like lacustrine migrants to move between **[[Pyramid Lake]]** and spawning habitat in the **lower [[Truckee River]]**.
- **Cui-ui** rely on this facility to bypass a barrier (likely a dam/diversion) during their spawning migration, with run timing tied to spring runoff and reservoir operations (referencing **[[Stampede Reservoir]]** consultation).
- **Tahoe Sucker**, previously unaddressed in the plan, also depends on MBFF passage to reach **lower Truckee River spawning habitat**, indicating multi-species reliance on this single passage structure.
- **Lahontan Cutthroat Trout** are identified as requiring facility operation independent of cui-ui runs — the plan is criticized for not assuming annual operation is necessary regardless of cui-ui presence, since LCT and Tahoe sucker also require passage.
- The fishway/ladder system includes a "terminal ladder," gravel-filled sections, baskets to prevent fish stacking, an electric fish weir, and hatchery trucks — components of a managed fish-passage ecosystem intervening in a naturally landlocked (endorheic) basin migratory corridor.

## Key Findings

- **Scope/Planning Deficiency**: The draft plan lacks clarity — it blends elements of a facilities improvement plan, a runoff forecast document, and a single-year (1994) operational decision document, without a cohesive multi-year (recommended minimum 3-year) management framework.
- **Missing Species Consideration**: The plan omits **[[Tahoe Sucker]]** use of MBFF for spawning migration access.
- **Recovery Plan Disconnect**: No reference is made to how the station management plan aligns with tasks/objectives in the **Cui-ui Recovery Plan**, a significant compliance gap.
- **1993 Mortality Event**: Dead cui-ui were found predominantly in the **inundated portion of the terminal ladder**, but reviewers question whether this location reflects where the majority of deaths actually *occurred*, versus where carcasses simply accumulated — raising concern about undetected mortality elsewhere in the ladder.
- **Hydraulic/Engineering Concerns**:
  - Filling portions of the terminal ladder with gravel may inadvertently create a **turbulence or velocity barrier**, impeding upstream cui-ui migration.
  - Anti-stacking baskets may need to be extended further up the ladder based on mortality distribution patterns.
  - Chainlink fence handling logistics (two-person lift feasibility) depend on required fence length.
  - Any **electric fish weir** must be operationally tested *prior to* deployment during an active spawning run, not during it.
- **Operational Ambiguity**: Unclear function of "hatchery trucks" in facility operations.
- **Passage Objective Unclear**: Reviewers question whether fishway operation is intended primarily to enable **upstream passage** or to **prevent mortality**, and note the plan fails to address **downstream passage of spawners and larvae** — a critical life-history stage gap.
- **Fish Handling Building**: No 1994 operational procedures are specified for this facility component, a notable omission.
- **Process/Governance Critique**: The plan conflates Stampede Reservoir operational consultation (a separate regulatory process) with station management, and lacks documentation of interagency coordination in its development.
- **Tone Critique**: Reviewers note the plan is written in an unnecessarily negative, "justificatory" tone rather than a proactive, action-oriented format (recommended: bullet-point activity summaries with brief discussion).
