# Virgin Spinedace (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) — 90-Day Petition Finding

## Executive Summary
This document is a **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Federal Register notice** (March 16, 1993) announcing a positive 90-day finding on two petitions to list the **Virgin spinedace** as an [[Endangered Species]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. Petitions were submitted by the Bonneville Chapter of the [[American Fisheries Society]] and the Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance, both citing the unpublished report by [[Valdez et al. 1990]] as primary scientific basis. The Service determined that the petitions presented **substantial information** warranting a full 12-month status review, though no new taxonomic or morphological data are provided in this notice—it is primarily a regulatory and distributional summary.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological descriptions (e.g., fin ray counts, body proportions, coloration) are provided in this regulatory notice. The document focuses on:
- **Taxonomic classification**: Subspecies of [[Lepidomeda mollispinis]], within family [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows).
- Common name: **Virgin spinedace**, named for its association with the [[Virgin River]] drainage.

*Note: Morphological data on spinedace species typically includes presence of spines in the dorsal fin and small body size typical of desert cyprinids, but such specifics are not detailed in this source.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historical Range**: Extended from the **East Fork of the Virgin River** to at least **Bunkerville, Nevada** ([[Cross 1975]]).
- **Current Distribution**: Primarily restricted to **Utah**, with a significant range contraction.
  - Historical range in Utah: 233 km (145 miles)
  - Current range in Utah: 140 km (87 miles)
  - **Range decline: ~40%**
- **Outside Utah**: A small remnant population persists in the [[Virgin River]] near **Littlefield, Arizona**. Recent surveys found only **one individual** in the Arizona mainstem Virgin River (Herder, BLM, pers. comm. 1992).
- **Nevada**: No Virgin spinedace were collected during the same survey period — suggesting possible extirpation from Nevada portions of its historical range.
- **Habitat Type**: Warmwater desert stream/river system within the [[Colorado River Basin]] (specifically the [[Virgin River Drainage]]).

## Key Findings
1. **Regulatory Action**: FWS issued a positive 90-day finding on March 4, 1993, concluding that petitions to list the Virgin spinedace as endangered present **substantial information** that listing "may be warranted."
2. **Petitioners**: 
   - Petition 1 (June 15, 1992): Mark Holden, Bonneville Chapter, [[American Fisheries Society]].
   - Petition 2 (August 17, 1992): [[Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance]].
   - Both petitions requested **critical habitat designation**, though FWS notes designation of critical habitat is **not a petitionable action** under the Act; it would only be addressed if the subsequent 12-month review results in a proposed listing rule.
3. **Threats Identified** (contributing to population decline):
   - [[Flow Depletion]] / dewatering
   - [[Water Diversion]]
   - [[Habitat Fragmentation]]
   - Increased [[Sedimentation]]
   - Degraded [[Water Quality]]
   - Invasion by **nonnative species** (likely referencing introduced predatory/competitive fish such as red shiner or crayfish)
4. **Next Steps**: Under Section 4(b)(3)(B) of the Act, a **12-month finding** is required within one year of petition receipt (i.e., by mid-1993) to determine whether listing is definitively warranted.
5. **Public Comment Period**: Open through **June 14, 1993**, coordinated through the Utah Field Office (Salt Lake City), overseen by **Robert D. Williams**, State Supervisor.
6. **Key Scientific References**:
   - [[Cross 1975]] — M.S. thesis on ecological distribution of Virgin River fishes (Univ. Nevada, Las Vegas).
   - [[Valdez et al. 1990]] — Status report on Virgin spinedace, prepared for Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (BIO/WEST Report No. PR-197-1).
   - [[Valdez et al. 1992]] — Conference abstract (Desert Fishes Council) updating status in the Virgin River drainage, Utah.
