# Two New Species of Sonorella from the Pinaleno Mountains, Arizona

#malacology #gastropoda #helminthoglyptidae #sonorella

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes two new pulmonate land snail species, [[Sonorella Christenseni]] and [[Sonorella Macrophallus]], from the [[Pinaleno Mountains]] of southeastern Arizona. Species are separated mainly on genitalic characters, since shell differences are often insufficient for identification. The authors argue that the four [[Sonorella]] species now known from the Pinalenos are too genetically and morphologically distinct to have evolved from one ancestral population, and that they more likely arrived from different areas during the [[Pleistocene]].

## Background & Description

**Study area.** The [[Pinaleno Mountains]], dominated by [[Mount Graham]] (10,713 ft), lie in [[Graham County]], Arizona. The range has the largest continuous area above 9,000 ft of any mountain range in southeastern Arizona, with three peaks above 10,000 ft and several more above 9,000 ft. The range lies directly north of the [[Dos Cabezas Mountains]] and the [[Chiricahua Mountains]].

**Collecting history.** Malacological collecting before 1950 was rare. [[J. H. Ferriss]] made the earliest trip in 1913, and [[Sonorella Grahamensis]] was described from his material ([[Pilsbry and Ferriss 1919]]). [[Sonorella Imitator]] was described from material collected in 1954 by M. L. Walton and W. O. Gregg ([[Gregg and Miller 1974]]). [[W. B. Miller]] began collecting in the range in 1965. This paper is based on collecting trips from 1965 through 1978.

**Sonorella christenseni (new species).** The shell is depressed, heliciform, thin, glossy and tan, with one chestnut-brown spiral band. The umbilicus is contained about 7 times in the diameter. The holotype measures 20.6 mm in diameter and 11.0 mm in height, with an umbilicus diameter of 2.9 mm. The genitalia have a smooth, cylindrical, pointed verge and a large epiphallic caecum.

**Sonorella macrophallus (new species).** The shell is depressed, heliciform, thin and light brown, with a chestnut-brown band and regularly spaced hyphen-like papillae on the dorsal surface. The holotype measures 17.7 mm in diameter and 10.6 mm in height, with an umbilicus diameter of 2.5 mm. The verge is large and has a retractable (protrusible) terminal tip.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *S. christenseni* was found in a rock-slide on the north slope of [[Clark Peak]] at about 8,700 ft. *S. macrophallus* was found under rocks and in rock-slides in [[Wet Canyon]] at about 6,050 to 6,900 ft.
- **Sympatry:** *S. christenseni* lives sympatrically with *S. imitator*, and *S. macrophallus* lives sympatrically with *S. imitator* in Wet Canyon. No gradation in genitalic size or shape was found between *S. christenseni* and *S. imitator*.
- **Range:** *S. christenseni* is known only from its type locality. *S. macrophallus* has been found only in Wet Canyon. A previous report of *S. grahamensis* from Wet Canyon was determined by the authors to be an error.
- **Biogeography:** The authors attribute the species diversity to multiple dispersal events, with all three of [[Miller 1967]]'s [[Sonorella Complexes]] (the hachitana, binneyi and granulatissima complexes) represented in the Pinalenos.
- **Not addressed:** The document gives no data on climate, water chemistry, diet, reproduction timing or population size.

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species described:** *Sonorella christenseni* and *Sonorella macrophallus* are formally described.
2. **Genitalia separate the species:** *S. christenseni* is inseparable from *S. imitator* by shell alone, but the penis and verge of *S. imitator* are about twice the length and diameter of those of *S. christenseni*. *S. christenseni* has a much larger epiphallic caecum and penial sheath.
3. **Papillae as a diagnostic character:** Of 18 *S. macrophallus* specimens examined, 16 showed easily detectable papillae (the other 2 were old and worn). None of 12 *S. imitator* specimens had papillae, which corrects the earlier report by Gregg and Miller (1974).
4. **Protrusible verge:** The verge tip of *S. macrophallus* is terminal and protrusible. This is shared only with *Sonorella Binneyi* of the [[Chiricahua Mountains]], where the tip is subterminal.
5. **Holotype data:**
   - *S. christenseni* genitalia: penis 11.1 mm, verge 7.1 mm, penial sheath 6.2 mm, epiphallus 12.3 mm, epiphallic caecum 2.0 mm, vagina 8.6 mm, free oviduct 5.3 mm.
   - *S. macrophallus* genitalia: penis 16.1 mm, verge 10.7 mm, penial sheath 3.5 mm, epiphallus 8.7 mm, epiphallic caecum 1.5 mm, vagina 9.7 mm, free oviduct 8.0 mm.
6. **Type localities:**
   - *S. christenseni:* rock-slide, north slope of Clark Peak, ca. 8,700 ft, 32°43.5'N, 109°59.1'W.
   - *S. macrophallus:* south side of Wet Canyon, ca. 300 ft from Highway 366 (Swift Trail), ca. 6,050 ft, 32°39.1'N, 109°48.8'W.
7. **Evolutionary conclusion:** The genitalic differences among the four Pinalenos *Sonorella* species are too large to support a single ancestral population. The authors propose that ancestors arrived from different areas at different times during the Pleistocene.
8. **Etymology:** *christenseni* honors Dr. Carl C. Christensen of the Bishop Museum, who located the *S. christenseni* site. *macrophallus* (Greek *makros* + *phallos*) refers to the large, distended verge.
