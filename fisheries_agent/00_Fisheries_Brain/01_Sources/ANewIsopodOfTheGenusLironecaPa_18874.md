# A New Isopod of the Genus *Lironeca*, Parasitic on a Cichlid Fish of Lake Tanganyika

#isopoda #cymothoidae #crustacea #parasitology

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[Geoffrey Fryer]] describes *Lironeca tanganyikae* sp. nov., a parasitic [[Isopoda]] (family [[Cymothoidae]]) found in the mouth of the endemic [[Lake Tanganyika]] cichlid [[Simochromis Diagramma]]. Until this record, the only parasitic isopod known from African freshwaters was *[[Lironeca Expansus]]*, which parasitizes the citharinid fish *[[Eugnathichthys Eetveldii]]* in the [[Congo River]] system. The author shows that the genus *Ichthyoxenus*, under which the Congolese species had been placed, is not separable from the marine genus *Lironeca*, and argues that freshwater members of the genus have colonized inland waters independently several times. The two African species are separated morphologically, by host preference and by site of attachment on the host, and the paper discusses, without firm conclusions, how the genus reached Lake Tanganyika.

## Background & Description

**Prior knowledge.** *L. expansus* (originally assigned to *Ichthyoxenus*) had a wide distribution in the Congo and its tributaries but was otherwise unknown from African inland waters (see [[Brian and Dartevelle 1949]]).

**Material.** The author studied specimens provided through [[Gustave Marlier]] and [[P. L. G. Benoit]] of the [[Royal Museum for Central Africa]], Tervuren. The new species was named from these collections.

**Description of *L. tanganyikae* (adult female).**
- Length to at least 12 mm; strongly arched dorsal surface; oostegites bulge when the marsupium is full of eggs or embryos.
- Head partly hidden by the first free thoracic tergite; large, elliptical, lateral eyes.
- Antennule of seven segments, larger than the antenna; antenna of five segments with signs of distal fusion.
- Mandible with an elongate cutting blade and a reduced molar region; no lacinia mobilis.
- Maxillule reduced to a slender distal endite ending in four curved claws; maxilla much reduced.
- Maxilliped with hook-like spines on the distal segment.
- Thoracic legs similar, each ending in a powerful hook-like spine; first three pairs point anteriorly, last four posteriorly.
- Pleopods broad and leaf-like; light brown preserved colour with many brownish pigment spots.

**Adult male.** Length to at least 6 mm; more slender and less swollen than the female; distinctive penes and modified second pleopods.

**Juvenile from brood pouch.** Length 3.4 mm; strong melanophore pattern; antennule and antenna proportionally large; antennule bears aesthetasc groups absent in adults; antenna with eight distinguishable segments.

**Type material.** Holotype is a female deposited in the [[Royal Museum for Central Africa]] (registered M.T. No. 6669).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Host association.** All specimens came from the mouth of *Simochromis diagramma*, described as a littoral species (see [[Poll 1956]]).

**Taxonomic history of the genus.** The paper reviews the nomenclature. *Ichthyoxenus* (spelled variously as *Ichthyoxenos* and *Ichtyoxenus*) is treated as a synonym of *Lironeca*. Several authors continued to use *Ichthyoxenus* for freshwater species, but [[Miers 1880]] applied *Lironeca* to both freshwater and marine forms, as did [[Gusev 1963]] for a Russian species. Freshwater *Lironeca* also occurs in South America ([[Van Name 1936]]).

**Independent freshwater colonization.** The author argues that freshwater species assigned to *Ichthyoxenus* from China, Siberia, Japan, Indonesia, the Himalayas and Africa were derived independently from marine ancestors in widely separated localities. Dispersal between these areas is considered implausible, so a single freshwater genus would have had to arise repeatedly. The more parsimonious view is that *Lironeca* has colonized freshwater many times.

**Separation of the two African species.**
- *L. tanganyikae* and *L. expansus* differ in the contour of the last thoracic tergite and the abdominal tergites, and in pigment pattern (the latter considered unproven as a diagnostic mark because material was limited).
- *L. expansus* has seven antennal segments; *L. tanganyikae* has five in the material examined. The juvenile of *L. tanganyikae* suggests this character may be unstable.
- *L. expansus* lives in the opercular cavity, probably always on the gills, of a citharinid fish. *L. tanganyikae* lives in the mouth of a cichlid.
- The author notes that [[Dartevelle 1939]] and [[Brian and Dartevelle 1949]] misquoted [[Monod 1931]] on attachment site. Information from [[J. P. Gosse]] (1963) supports gill attachment in *L. expansus*.
- The two species are geographically separate, and their host and site preferences would make contact unlikely even if ranges overlapped.

**Origin in Lake Tanganyika.** The parasite is judged to have been originally derived from marine ancestors and is now almost certainly confined to Lake Tanganyika. Three possibilities are considered:
1. The genus is a remnant of a Jurassic marine fauna, as [[Moore 1903]] proposed for other Tanganyikan taxa. The author finds little support for this.
2. The genus is an old inhabitant of the lake and entered the basin from the Congo.
3. The genus arrived after the lake was reconnected to the Congo by the [[Lukuga River]] in comparatively recent times.

The absence of *Eugnathichthys* from the [[Malagarasi River]] is noted as a potential clue. The strict restriction of the parasite to one host species suggests a prolonged association with *S. diagramma* or its immediate ancestor, which would argue against recent colonization.

**Evolutionary questions.** Changes in host preference, site preference on the host, and the transition from riverine to lacustrine conditions (which would affect dispersal) are described as evolutionarily interesting, but the author states that any discussion is necessarily speculative.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Lironeca tanganyikae* sp. nov. is a parasite of the endemic Lake Tanganyika cichlid *Simochromis diagramma*.
- **Genus status:** *Ichthyoxenus* is not separable from *Lironeca*. Freshwater members of the genus have arisen from marine ancestors multiple times, so freshwater species should be placed in *Lironeca*.
- **Diagnostic differences from *L. expansus*:** tergite contour (thoracic and abdominal), pigmentation (tentative), antennal segment count (7 vs. 5, possibly unstable), host species, and attachment site (gills of citharinids vs. cichlid mouth).
- **Type specimen:** Female holotype, Royal Museum for Central Africa, Tervuren, M.T. No. 6669.
- **Origin hypothesis:** The parasite probably came from marine ancestors. Its arrival date in Lake Tanganyika remains unresolved, with the Lukuga River reconnection and an older origin both possible. The strict host specificity suggests a long association.
- **Correction of the literature:** Earlier reports that *L. expansus* occurs in the mouth were a misquotation; the species is attached to the gills.
