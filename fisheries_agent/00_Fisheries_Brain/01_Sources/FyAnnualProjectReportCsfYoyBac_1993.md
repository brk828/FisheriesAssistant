# FY93 Annual Project Report: CSF YOY Backwater Habitat — Colorado River Recovery Program

#ichthyology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This FY93 report documents Project #12 of the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], led by Kathleen H. Fenton (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation). The study focuses on quantifying **backwater habitat availability** for young-of-year (YOY) [[Colorado Squawfish]] (also known as [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]) across the Green, Colorado, and Gunnison Rivers using aerial videography and the Map and Image Processing System (MIPS). The project is a monitoring and remote-sensing effort rather than a direct morphological or population study, aiming to link river flow regimes to habitat availability for endangered fish nursery areas. Budget for FY93 was fully expended ($177,000), and video basemaps and habitat quantification products were delivered to Fish & Wildlife Service and Utah Division of Wildlife Resources.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data on [[Colorado Squawfish]] or associated endangered fishes were reported in this document. The study is habitat-focused, using remote sensing (aerial video and infrared imagery) rather than specimen-based biological sampling. Species addressed are implied to be YOY (young-of-year) individuals of the [[Colorado Squawfish]], part of the broader suite of endangered Upper Colorado River Basin fishes historically studied under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], which also includes species such as [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Backwater habitats — critical nursery areas for YOY endangered fish species in slow-moving, low-velocity river margins.
- **Geographic scope:** 
  - [[Green River]] (Ouray reach, Mineral Bottom, Island Park to Sand Wash, RM 0–345)
  - [[Colorado River]] (RM 0–185, RM 20–30, RM 55–65, Westwater Canyon, 15/18 Mile Reaches, Horsethief Canyon)
  - [[Gunnison River]] (entire monitored reach)
- **Flow relationships:** The project explicitly examines how variable river discharge (including high-flow events, e.g., May 27, 1993 special videography) affects the extent and configuration of backwater nursery habitat.
- **Habitat monitoring tools:** Aerial videography, color infrared photography, and GIS-based video basemaps were used to characterize channel morphology and habitat distribution, supporting integration into the broader **Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program (ISMP)**.
- **Ecosystem context:** This effort supports multiple RIPRAP action plans, including the [[Green River Action Plan]], [[Colorado River Action Plan]] (Gunnison and mainstem segments), and the [[General Recovery Program Support Action Plan]], all aimed at identifying flow regimes necessary for recovery of endangered Colorado River Basin fishes.

## Key Findings
- Aerial videography was successfully collected for **all five FY93 tasks**, covering the Green, Colorado, and Gunnison Rivers between March and September 1993.
- Habitat quantification fell **behind schedule** due to a **May 1993 revision** of the scope of work, reflecting a programmatic **de-emphasis on ISMP-based habitat quantification**.
- The **Ouray site basemap** was in final preparation stages, a prerequisite for habitat quantification analyses.
- Two **unplanned but noteworthy accomplishments**:
  - **High-flow videography** of the Green River (May 27, 1993), funded via a special $5,000 request.
  - **Color infrared aerial photography** of the Gunnison River (September 28, 1993), funded through scope-of-work budget surplus.
- Completed and submitted **1991 Colorado River 15/18-mile reach habitat quantification** data (carried over from FY92) to Doug Osmundson (U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service).
- **FY93 Budget:** Fully expended — $177,000 provided and $177,000 spent, with no funding discrepancy.
- **Recommendation:** The report calls for a **comprehensive assessment of remote sensing needs** (videography and photography) across the entire [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] to improve efficiency and cross-project utility.
