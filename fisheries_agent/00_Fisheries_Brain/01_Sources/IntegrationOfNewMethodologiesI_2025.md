# Integration of New Methodologies in Monitoring Native Fish Habitat and Populations in the Southwestern U.S.

#ichthyology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #native_fish_conservation

## Survey Objectives & Target Population

**Primary Sampling Purpose**: This study integrated emerging remote sensing and molecular technologies (UAV, terrestrial lidar, RTK survey, eDNA) to assess current riparian and in-stream habitat conditions on the [[Upper Verde River]] and compare them to 2009 baseline assessments. The objective was to quantify long-term trends in habitat suitability and native fish populations, particularly in the context of proposed fish barrier construction by the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation under Central Arizona Project mitigation efforts. The study aimed to evaluate whether declining baseflows and vegetation encroachment are driving native fish extirpation from upper river reaches.

**Target Population & Spatial Boundaries**: Target populations include six native fish species: [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster chrysogaster*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*). The spatial domain extends 50 km along the Upper Verde River from the confluence with Granite Creek (Paulden, AZ) downstream to Sycamore Canyon. The study area is naturally divided at Hell Canyon into an upper section (broad alluvial valleys, sand-cobble substrate) and lower section (canyon-bound reaches, cobble-boulder substrate). A subset of 44 long-term monitoring plots established by USFS in the early 1990s were resurveyed; 20 plots received geomorphology and vegetation resurveys, 17 were scanned with lidar, 10 imaged with UAV, and 29 sampled for eDNA.

## Gear Specifications & Standardization

**Equipment & Mesh/Gear Parameters**: **eDNA sampling** used a geostatic pump to draw 5 L of stream water through Whatman® 1827-047 Glass Microfiber Binder-Free Filters (1.5 μm pore size, 47 mm diameter, Grade 934-AH). Filters were analyzed via qPCR with species-specific assays for four native species (limit of detection: 10 mtDNA copies/rxn with >95% amplification success; 83.3% detection at 2 copies/rxn). Negative controls included filtered distilled water in DNA extraction and qPCR processes. **UAV surveys** employed a Sensefly Ebee X platform with multispectral sensor (four bands: green 550 nm, red 660 nm, red edge 735 nm, near-infrared 790 nm) flown at 70 m AGL with 85% latitudinal and 90% longitudinal overlap, yielding 14 cm spatial resolution images spanning ~10 ha per 25-minute flight. **Terrestrial lidar** used a Leica BLK360 scanner (830 nm wavelength, 60 m maximum range, 8 mm accuracy at 20 m); 15–25 individual scans per plot were mosaicked into point clouds of 30–60 million points. **RTK surveys** captured bathymetric profiles at ≤1 m intervals along 40-m reaches (20 m upstream and downstream of historic transects). **Riparian vegetation plots** followed modified Daubenmire methodology: five 5 m × 8 m plots along 40 m of streambank per transect, recording species, density, DBH, and height of woody stems.

**Deployment & Effort Standardization**: eDNA samples were collected in November 2021 and 2022 at well-mixed water near channel center, co-located with historic electrofishing sites. UAV flights were conducted in summers 2021 and 2022, centered on each transect location with missions planned in SenseFly eMotion 3 software. Lidar scans covered entire transect lengths (30–115 m) with consistent 40 m widths. RTK surveys characterized breaks in lateral and cross-channel morphology, defining habitat types (riffle, pool, run, cattail backwater, large rocks). Geomorphology transects measured vertical distance from a tape stretched between permanent stakes at typically 1–5 m intervals, recording bankfull, water's edge, water depth, and thalweg. Entrenchment ratio (ER = flood prone width / bankfull width) and width-to-depth ratio (W/D = bankfull width / average bankfull depth) were calculated from cross-sectional data.

**Selectivity & Bias Considerations**: eDNA assays were not available for [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Speckled Dace]], limiting species coverage. The method detects presence/absence but does not quantify population abundance, precluding statistical comparison with historic backpack electrofishing data (2006) and canoe electrofishing (2022). Lidar does not penetrate water, so subsurface channel bathymetry required traditional survey methods. UAV image classification used random forest machine learning trained on eight cover types (water, gravel bars, herbaceous, cattail, riparian woody, juniper, bedrock/bare ground, shadow); classification accuracy depends on training data quality. NAIP imagery (1 m resolution, 2007 and 2019) provided coarser spatial and spectral resolution than UAV but enabled broader spatial extent analysis. Vegetation plots (10 m × 40 m strips in 2009) were replaced by full floodplain UAV coverage, improving spatial representation but complicating direct comparison.

## Experimental & Spatial Design Framework

**Stratification & Allocation**: The study area was stratified into two sections divided by Hell Canyon: (1) upper section (Granite Creek to Hell Canyon) characterized by broad alluvial valleys, sand-silt-cobble substrates, and greater human development impacts; (2) lower section (Hell Canyon to Sycamore Canyon) with canyon-bound reaches, cobble-boulder substrates, and less anthropogenic disturbance. Plot selection from the original 44 USFS sites prioritized proximity, data completeness, and presence of riffle-run-pool habitat with established geomorphology transects. The upper section contained all cattail/backwater habitat surveyed. Proposed fish barrier locations coincide with section boundaries (upper barrier above Hell Canyon, lower barrier above Sycamore Canyon).

**Temporal Scheduling**: Long-term monitoring plots were established in the early 1990s and resurveyed in 1997, 2001/02, and 2009 by USFS; monitoring was discontinued after 2012 due to budget constraints. This study resurveyed plots in 2021/22 (UAV, lidar, vegetation, geomorphology) and 2023 (RTK bathymetry), providing a 13-year gap since the last comprehensive assessment. eDNA sampling occurred in November 2021 and 2022, coinciding with low-flow conditions. The study leveraged NAIP imagery from 2007 and 2019 to quantify riparian woody vegetation change over a 12-year period. Historic aerial photographs from 1997 provided additional context for 24-year vegetation trends.

**Detection & Bias Mitigation**: eDNA sampling included negative controls (filtered distilled water) in DNA extraction and qPCR to detect contamination. Species-specific qPCR assays were validated with known detection limits. UAV multispectral classification was ground-truthed with field-based vegetation plots using modified Daubenmire methods. Lidar-derived DEMs were compared to both 2009 and 2021/22 USFS single-line transect data to validate geomorphic change detection. RTK surveys increased the number and accuracy of survey points compared to traditional laser level methods, improving bathymetric characterization. Paired t-tests compared ER and W/D ratios between 2009 and 2021/22; two-sample unequal variance t-tests compared sections above and below Hell Canyon. Statistical significance was assessed at α = 0.05.

## Field Data Schema & Variable Definitions

**Primary Biological Measurements**: **Fish**: eDNA presence/absence for [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Spikedace]], [[Roundtail Chub]] (qPCR detection in triplicate reactions; positive detection = amplification in ≥5 of 6 replicates). Historic electrofishing data (2006, 2022) reported total fish counts and relative abundance by species but were not directly comparable due to methodological differences (backpack vs. canoe electrofishing). **Vegetation**: Woody stem species, density, diameter at breast height (DBH), height; importance values (IV = relative density + relative dominance, scaled 0–200). UAV-derived cover types: water, gravel bars, herbaceous (forbs, sedges/rushes), cattail (*Typha* spp.), riparian woody (all woody species combined), juniper (*Juniperus monosperma*), bedrock/bare ground, shadow (percent cover within riparian corridor and full image extent).

**Environmental & Spatial Covariates**: **Hydrology**: Stream discharge (120-day moving average from USGS gauge at Paulden; declined from 30.6 cfs in 1995 to 18 cfs in 2023, a 40% reduction). **Geomorphology**: Channel width, average depth, bankfull width, bankfull depth, thalweg depth, water's edge elevation, entrenchment ratio (ER = flood prone width / bankfull width; <1.4 = entrenched, 1.41–2.2 = moderately entrenched, >2.2 = slightly entrenched), width-to-depth ratio (W/D = bankfull width / average bankfull depth). Lidar-derived vertical change (mean, min, max deposition/erosion in cm). **Habitat**: RTK-derived habitat classification (pool, run, riffle, cattail backwater, large rocks) with percent cover per 40-m reach. Pools defined as depths >1.8 m with low velocity; runs as 0.5–1.8 m depth; riffles as <0.5 m depth with decreasing thalweg elevation. **Substrate**: Qualitative descriptions (sand, silt, cobble, boulder). **Vegetation**: Riparian woody cover change from NAIP imagery (2007–2019); cattail cover from UAV (9.4% of riparian corridor in 2021/22, nearly absent in 2007).

**QA/QC & Data Entry Protocols**: eDNA negative controls were run in triplicate to detect contamination. qPCR assays were validated with known standards. UAV images were processed in SenseFly eMotion 3 software; distorted edge areas were excluded from analysis. Lidar point clouds (15–25 scans per plot) were registered and mosaicked; DEMs were created centered on USFS transect locations. RTK survey points were collected at breaks in morphology and at ≤1 m intervals. Geomorphology data were digitized into Excel spreadsheets; ER and W/D ratios were calculated from cross-sectional profiles. Vegetation data were entered following Daubenmire protocols; importance values were calculated as IV = (relative density + relative dominance). NAIP imagery was classified using random forest machine learning trained on UAV-derived cover types; pixel-to-pixel change detection quantified woody vegetation encroachment. Statistical analyses (paired t-tests, two-sample t-tests) were conducted at α = 0.05.
