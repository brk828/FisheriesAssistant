# Notropis Xanthicara: A New Cyprinid Fish from the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, Coahuila, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 taxonomic paper in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* describes [[Notropis Xanthicara]], a new cyprinid minnow from the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] in [[Coahuila]], northern Mexico. The authors, W. L. [[Minckley]] and Gladys L. Little of the Department of Zoology, Arizona State University, place the species in the *lutrensis-ornatus* group of the subgenus *Cyprinella* of [[Notropis]]. The new form is most closely related to [[Notropis Rutilus]], from which it is distinguished mainly by pigmentation, especially a discrete blue-black lateral band, and by modest differences in fin, body and scale proportions. The authors also discuss its geographic isolation, its likely origin from a *rutilus*-like ancestor in the basin's springs and spring-fed streams, and its probable late Pleistocene age.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The paper places the Cuatro Ciénegas form within a wider assemblage of nominal and undescribed forms related to [[Notropis Proserpinus]] that occur in the middle [[Rio Grande]] and adjacent drainages. The other members named are:

- *N. proserpinus*, which lives in clearer waters of the lower [[Pecos River]] system and east to the Devil's River and San Felipe Spring, Val Verde County, Texas.
- *N. lepidus*, in spring-fed waters of the Nueces, Frio, Medina and Guadalupe systems in Texas.
- *N. rutilus*, in similar habitat in the Rio Salado and Rio San Juan, northern Mexico.
- Two undescribed species: one from the Rio Conchos basin, Chihuahua, and one from the semi-isolated Bolsón of Cuatro Ciénegas.

The work was supported by NSF Grants CB-2?7 and GB-4477X (OCR-uncertain digits).

**Diagnosis.** The new species is described as a slender, delicate minnow. Key diagnostic traits include:

- A discrete blue-black lateral band running from the darkened middle caudal rays, through a diffuse caudal spot, to the back of the eye, and continuing as a preocular bar.
- Fragile fin rays.
- A gently decurved lateral line with little associated pigment.
- A relatively unpigmented cornea.
- Pectoral and pelvic fins that are pointed, with the pelvic fins having an almost straight distal margin.

**Type material.** The holotype is a mature, tuberculate male, 45 mm standard length, collected in April 1961 from the Rio Puente Colorado, Cuatro Ciénegas basin, Coahuila. The holotype has 35 lateral-line scales and lacks a lateral canal on the three posterior scales on the left side, an aberration the authors note is rare in the series. Paratypes (including a male and female figured in Figure 1) and a large series of non-type material are held in several museums, including the University of Kansas (KU), the Museum of Zoology at the University of Michigan (UMMZ), and the Universidad de Nuevo León, with lot-by-lot counts and locality data tabulated in the source (summarized here, not reproduced).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** [[Notropis Xanthicara]] is allopatric with respect to other *Notropis* species. Intensive collecting in the Rio Salado de los Nadadores, east of the Cuatro Ciénegas basin, produced only *N. rutilus*. Collections from the north and northeast (outlet) sides of the basin yielded only *N. xanthicara*, although the authors note that the north side may once have held *N. rutilus* at higher frequency. Notably, the authors found *N. rutilus*-like specimens at two places within the basin, in [[Canal De La Ansostura]] and the [[Rio Salado De Los Nadadores]] system, both of which resemble *rutilus* in key characters.

**Habitat and behavior.** The new species is most abundant in the upper reaches of streams, just below their origins in limnocrenes (spring pools), where it concentrates at the transition between lentic and lotic conditions. In canals and streams it occurs in groups of three to 25 individuals along shear zones between current and backwater. By day it rests on the bottom but forages over the bottom and inspects nearly all floating objects at any depth. Fish rest in eddies in groups of four or more and are lethargic under diver lights. Breeding behavior is not documented. By contrast, *N. rutilus* concentrates above and below riffles, with aggregations of nuptial males in the swiftest riffles and females in associated eddies or pools.

**Co-occurring fauna.** The most common fishes associated with the new species include the [[Astyanax Fasciatus]] (Mexican tetra) and [[Dionda Episcopa]] (roundnose minnow). Both tend to favor faster water and are more active and aggressive. *N. xanthicara* has been taken with most other basin fishes, except those characteristic of marshes and larger lakes such as [[Cyprinodon Bifasciatus]] and [[Lucania Interioris]]. Other basin species mentioned include [[Gambusia]] species, [[Xiphophorus Gordoni]], [[Micropterus Salmoides]] (largemouth bass) and [[Lepomis Megalotis]] (longear sunfish), all differing from the new species.

**Endemism and evolutionary setting.** The Cuatro Ciénegas basin has produced endemic fishes, snails and other invertebrates. Endemic snails include taxa of distinctive subfamilies and genera ([[Taylor 1966]]), while some invertebrates do not differ from animals found elsewhere ([[Minckley 1969]]). The authors place *N. xanthicara* as intermediate between the ancient, pre-Pleistocene relict *Cyprinodon bifasciatus* and highly differentiated endemics, and conclude it probably dates to the later Pleistocene. The authors consider the basin's springs and spring-fed streams as an isolated setting in which *N. rutilus* or its progenitor was likely isolated and differentiated.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described.** *Notropis xanthicara* is a new cyprinid from the Cuatro Ciénegas basin, Coahuila, distinguished from *N. rutilus* mainly by a discrete lateral band and its slenderer body.

2. **Anal fin rays (meristics).** In 264 of 283 *N. xanthicara*, the anal fin had eight rays, with seven in two fish and nine in 17. In 447 *N. rutilus* counts, eight rays occurred in 371 fish and nine in 70.

3. **Standard length.** *N. xanthicara* ranged from 30.2 to 56.0 mm (149 fish), and *N. rutilus* from 30.0 to 60.0 mm (447 fish).

4. **Fin and body proportions.** The dorsal fin is usually as long as or longer than body depth in *N. xanthicara* (shorter in *N. rutilus*). The anal fin is slightly falcate in the new species, while the pectoral and pelvic fins are relatively small and pointed. Predorsal length averages 54.1% of standard length in *N. xanthicara* versus 53.3% in *N. rutilus*. Prepelvic length averages 52.2% versus 50.2%.

5. **Head and body measures.** Head length averages 26.6% of standard length (versus 26.1%), with a longer snout (8.0% versus 7.1%). Body depth averages 21.7% (versus 23.1%), and caudal peduncle depth 10.3% (versus 11.1%).

6. **Lateral line scales.** About 66% of 149 *N. xanthicara* have 34 lateral-line scales, and 21% have 33 (mean 33.9, range 33–36). In *N. rutilus* (447 fish), 42% have 34 and 40% have 33 (mean 33.6, range 32–36).

7. **Pigmentation is diagnostic.** The lateral band of *N. xanthicara* is discrete and blue-black, whereas in *N. rutilus* it is diffuse and highly variable. Scale margins in *N. rutilus* often carry melanophores along the decurved lateral line, which the new species lacks.

8. **Intermediate populations.** Some *N. rutilus* from the Rio Salado de los Nadadores show pigmentation approaching *N. xanthicara*, especially in non-breeding fish. Five specimens from [[Laguna De Los Fresnos]] (Santa Tecla system) show longer heads, shorter snouts and darker pigmentation. The authors refer these to *N. xanthicara* but defer judgment on their status pending more material.

9. **Possible hybridization.** The authors suggest the intermediacy in the Rio Salado population could reflect hybridization between *N. xanthicara* and a stream population of *N. rutilus*, but they regard the question as unresolved. They attribute the lighter pigmentation to water clarity in that stream.

10. **Origin and age.** *N. xanthicara* likely descended from *N. rutilus* or its progenitor in isolation in the basin's springs and spring-fed streams. Its differentiation is placed in the later Pleistocene.

11. **Sampling effort.** Fewer than 50 seining collections were made in Canal de La Ansostura since 1960, and *Notropis* was taken there only once. Canal manipulation in the north-side area may have reduced *Notropis* abundance.
