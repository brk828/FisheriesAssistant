# Pre-Impoundment Investigations of the Glen Canyon and Flaming Gorge Areas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Utah Department of Fish and Game completion report (Federal Aid Project F-4-B-5, Bulletin 60-3, dated September 12, 1960) documents pre-impoundment ecological surveys of the [[Colorado River]] in the [[Glen Canyon]] area and the [[Green River]] above the planned [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]]. The surveys covered fish, aquatic insects, algae, aquatic and riparian plants, water chemistry, and physical habitat, in advance of the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] reservoirs. The investigators recorded 17 fish species in the Glen Canyon area and 16 in the Flaming Gorge area. They recommended stocking game fish, rough fish eradication, post-impoundment monitoring, and improved access.

## Background & Description

The study was a federally funded job completion report prepared by fishery biologists Donald B. McDonald and Phil A. Dotson under project leader supervision. Its general objectives were to determine present ecological conditions, predict conditions in the two future reservoirs, and identify suitable game fish species. The specific objectives covered:

- Species present and their habitat conditions.
- Chemical and physical characteristics of river and tributary water, including diurnal and seasonal change.
- Types and distribution of bottom organisms and their value as fish food.
- Feasibility of establishing game fish in the proposed reservoirs.
- Availability and condition of spawning areas.
- Plankton forms and relative abundance, as time permitted.

Methods included seines, rotenone, explosives, set lines, gill nets, and stomach analysis for fish; dip nets for benthic fauna (preserved in 70% alcohol and identified by University of Utah biologists); algae preserved in lacto-phenol and identified by [[Seville Flowers]]. Water chemistry used the Winkler method for dissolved oxygen, color comparators for pH, and titration for carbon dioxide, alkalinity and hardness. Turbidity was measured with a Hellige turbidimeter.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Glen Canyon river and tributaries.** The [[Colorado River]] supports relatively few fish species, while tributaries hold a more diverse fauna. Most tributaries are intermittent, flash-flood prone and turbid during runoff. Fish diversity varied among tributaries: spring-fed streams such as Warm Spring Creek and Aztec Creek supported large, diverse populations, while the shallow, sandy [[Escalante River]] was nearly devoid of fish. The river was turbid, with a shifting sand bottom and little organic material, which together produced low productivity. Dissolved oxygen ranged from 7.2 to 13.4 ppm. No chemical conditions harmful to fish were detected.

**Flaming Gorge area (Green River).** Sixteen species were collected in the river drainage and 29 river miles below the dam site. Intensive collecting in the gorge was limited by deep, swift water. Dissolved oxygen in the Green River ranged from 4.7 to 7.5 ppm. The riparian vegetation was dominated by [[Sandbar Willow]] and [[Salt Cedar]] (tamarisk), and submerged aquatic plants were scarce.

**Habitat threats.** Sand scouring and high turbidity were judged to reduce bottom fauna and aquatic plants, and the investigators linked the scarcity of food and spawning areas to these factors. Flood and low-flow events caused temporary declines in fish populations in tributaries.

## Key Findings

**Glen Canyon fishes (17 species).** The most important species were:

- **[[Channel Catfish]]** was the most abundant fish in the Colorado and San Juan rivers, with specimens up to 24 inches in length.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** was the most abundant native sucker in the river. Its largest specimen was about 15 inches, and it fed mainly on algae.
- **[[Colorado River Squawfish]]** was recorded from one specimen, with numbers reported to have declined.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** was recorded only twice in the Glen Canyon area.
- **[[Fathead Minnow]]** and **[[Speckled Dace]]** were common in tributaries. Fathead minnows were new records for Utah, and two species, the Fathead Minnow and the [[Southwest Plains Killifish]], were first recorded in Utah during 1958.
- The [[Carp]] growth rate in Glen Canyon was slow. One large carp was about 4 years old.

**Flaming Gorge fishes and fisheries (16 species).**

- The [[Redside Shiner]] was the most abundant fish in the river, making up 49.2 percent of all fish collected in 1959.
- Flannelmouth suckers made up 14.7 percent of the river catch, and mountain suckers 4.1 percent.
- Bonytail chub made up 2.3 percent of the catch, with the largest specimen measuring 16.6 inches and weighing 1.2 pounds.
- Humpback suckers were rare; only a few adults were collected.
- Cutthroat and rainbow trout were collected from several tributaries, especially Carter Creek. Brown trout were not found in the river.
- Fish condition factors (K) were calculated for nine species. Bonytail chub K values declined with increasing size, and most other species showed no obvious trend.
- A disease survey found bacterial ulcers on flannelmouth and mountain suckers, and leeches and a tapeworm plerocercoid larva were also noted.

**Food habits.** Stomach analysis of 17 bonytail chubs showed a diet dominated by terrestrial insects, especially ants (58.7 percent of items), plus plant matter. Fish stomach analyses showed algae as the main food of flannelmouth suckers and channel catfish feeding on a wide variety of food.

**Aquatic insects.** A total of 66 genera of aquatic insects were collected in the Glen Canyon area. Insects were abundant in tributaries but sparse in the river, which the investigators attributed to scouring and shifting sand.

**Water chemistry.** Carbon dioxide was rarely present, never exceeding 3 ppm, and water was alkaline (pH 7.2 to 9.0). Turbidity values were as high as 3,500 ppm in the Colorado River.

**Management recommendations (Glen Canyon).**

1. Continue limnological and fishery studies of the lake during and after dam construction.
2. Do not stock forage fish until the value of existing species is known.
3. Consider rainbow trout and kokanee salmon for cold-water areas.
4. Limit warm-water stocking to largemouth bass and/or black crappie.
5. Investigate spawning areas and clear littoral vegetation near springs to improve kokanee spawning.
6. Ensure access roads and boat ramps at Warm Creek bay, Bullfrog Creek bay and Hite-White Canyon.

**Management recommendations (Flaming Gorge).**

1. Initiate a rough fish eradication program on the Green River and tributaries from Seedskadee Dam to Flaming Gorge Dam, ideally in fall 1962 by chemical treatment.
2. Consider a fish hatchery to sustain trout and kokanee in the reservoir.
3. Build additional access roads to the cold-water fishery below the dam.
4. Control downstream discharge, increasing it at about 400 cfs per hour until the normal maximum rate is reached.
5. Conduct post-impoundment studies for fewer than 5 years on the Flaming Gorge Reservoir.
6. Consider other game fish species for stocking.

**Overall stocking recommendation.** Largemouth bass, rainbow trout and kokanee salmon are recommended for Lake Powell, and the same species except largemouth bass for Flaming Gorge Reservoir.
