# Razorback Sucker Genetics and Propagation Program (Upper Colorado River Basin)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of correspondence, a program summary, and two Scope of Work (SOW) research proposals from the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) for Endangered Fishes of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The materials were written by J. Holt Williamson, Propagation Coordinator (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service), in 1989 to 1990. They cover the [[Propagation Genetics Management Plan]], refugia capture and rearing of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and a proposed multi-year genetic survey using allozyme and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analyses together with the development of non-lethal tissue sampling. The core message is that the species is critically imperiled, that accurate genetic baseline data are needed before broodstocks and reintroductions can be managed, and that coordination among researchers and funding bodies is currently weak.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is a large catostomid of big river systems. The source treats it as one of the four threatened or endangered big-river fishes of the Upper Basin, alongside [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]]. Per the proposals, many biologists regard its plight as second only to the bonytail chub.

The correspondence was addressed to university and agency researchers, including W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University), Tom Dowling, Paul Marsh, and Don Buth. Williamson, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service employee on loan to the program, had no staff or supervisory authority. His role was to identify propagation and genetics needs, write proposals, and recruit collaborators. Funding is channeled through the [[Tech Group]], the Management Group, and the Recovery Implementation Committee, with funds becoming available in October.

Methods named in the source include:
- **Allozyme electrophoresis** (starch gel electrophoresis of enzyme systems)
- **Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis**, for founder population size, maternal introgression, and relationships among groups
- **Non-lethal tissue sampling**, in two phases: (1) testing the effects of tissue sampling on the fish, and (2) allozyme resolution from those tissues
- **PIT-tagging** of all wild fish brought into refugia, with PIT-tag numbers used as data file names
- A proposed **centralized database** and a **Standardized Monitoring Program**

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Razorback sucker populations are depleted and in some cases in immediate danger of extinction. Adults are difficult to locate outside the spawning season, and young fish are rarely collected. Sampling is therefore concentrated on spring spawning aggregations, where genetic material is mixed and passed to the next generation.

The source describes an ongoing hypothesis that razorback suckers return annually to the same Upper Basin sites to spawn. If these are natal spawning areas, genetic evidence could support the "spawning migration hypothesis" and justify imprinting and chemoreception studies. The authors also state that genetic data can show whether the species is panmictic (one broadly distributed population) or structured into subpopulations, which would shape conservation decisions.

Threats and management concerns noted include:
- Hybridization and introgression, including possible interspecific hybridization within the Gila complex
- Inbreeding in captive and refugia populations
- Federal permitting restrictions, which favor non-lethal sampling ("sample nonlethally, or don't sample")

Study areas listed include Lake Mohave, the San Juan, Green, and mainstem Colorado Rivers, Lake Powell, Senator Wash Reservoir (Imperial Valley, CA), Niland SFH (CA), and Dexter NFH (NM). Refugia sites include the Ouray endangered fish experimental facility (Ouray National Waterfowl Refuge, near Vernal, UT).

## Key Findings

**Program goals (Propagation/Genetics Management Plan, 1990 summary):**
1. Avoid immediate population extinction.
2. Develop or maintain self-sustaining fish populations.
3. Maintain the capacity of populations to adapt to changing environments.
4. Maintain the capacity of populations for continued speciation.

**Refugia collection, 1990:**
- Seven adults from the Colorado River mainstem were taken to Dexter NFH, New Mexico.
- Eleven fish from the San Juan arm and four from the Dirty Devil arm of Lake Powell were taken to the Ouray facility.
- Gametes from Green River populations were collected at spawning sites and reared at Ouray in discrete lots to form genetically identified broodstocks.
- Offspring from Green River stock are being used in recovery-approved research and strategic reintroductions.

**Genetics survey proposals:**
- **FY 1990 (Proposal No. 18):** Total budget $33,300 ($20,000 sampling, $10,000 equipment, $3,300 other), excluding 17% overhead. Deliverables were problem identification, work plans, and data collection. The schedule set March 15, 1990 for work plans, May 15, 1990 for the sampling protocol, and September 30, 1990 for sample collection.
- **FY 1991 (Proposal No. 22):** Eleven tasks cover work-plan development, non-lethal sampling procedures, site selection, equipment, team training, sampling, RFPs, contracting, and analyses. Budget figures were left blank in the source. Analyses were projected for fall 1992, possibly extending into 1993.

**Stated scientific objectives and end products:**
- Baseline genetic data sets for as many wild and captive populations as feasible
- Non-lethal sampling procedures that prevent killing endangered fish
- Data on hybridization, introgression, and inbreeding, and on population structure
- Management tools and secure refuge populations, plus a peer-reviewed manuscript

**Coordination issues raised in correspondence:**
- Williamson wrote that communication among cooperators had been poor since the Arizona State University meeting. He asked for regular progress reports because the Recovery Implementation Committee would lose interest without information to report.
- A September meeting (19–21 September) was proposed to address: identifying all razorback sucker aggregations, non-lethal sampling progress, which allozyme systems to resolve, a sample "clearinghouse," and a periodic reporting system.
- Most FY 1990 funds were expected to go to sample collection, non-lethal technique development, and contracting, with formal analyses beginning in 1991.

**Open issues:** The source does not report any actual genetic results. The FY 1991 budget and several FY 1990 budget lines are blank in the scanned text, and the non-lethal sampling method was still under development.
