# Drug Sensitivity of Chondrococcus columnaris

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-pathology #myxobacteria #columnaris-disease

## Executive Summary

This study by Fijan and Voorhees (1969) systematically evaluated the *in vitro* drug sensitivity of **[[Chondrococcus columnaris]]**, the causative agent of **[[Columnaris Disease]]**, a significant bacterial pathogen causing mortality in both cold- and warmwater fish. Using 26 bacterial isolates (23 from the southeastern USA, 3 from the northwestern USA, including strains from blueback salmon and chinook salmon), the researchers assayed susceptibility to 28 antibiotics, 5 nitrofuran compounds, 11 sulfonamides, 5 other chemotherapeutics, 20 disinfectants, and 11 additional chemicals (herbicides, insecticides, heavy metal salts). The work aimed to establish an evidence base for developing effective treatment protocols against columnaris disease, which was diagnosed in 28% of 75 bacteriologically examined fish disease cases in the southeastern US during 1967–1968.

## Background & Description

While this paper is primarily a pharmacological/microbiological assay rather than a morphological study, key identification and culturing characteristics of *C. columnaris* are noted:

- **Growth medium**: Cultivated on **[[Cytophaga Agar]]** (Anacker & Ordal formulation: 0.05% tryptone, 0.05% yeast extract, 0.02% sodium acetate, 0.9% agar, pH 7.2–7.4)
- **Optimal incubation temperature**: 25°C
- **pH sensitivity**: Growth is not supported at pH 6.5 or below; survival in water is shortened at low pH
- **Salinity tolerance**: Highly salt-sensitive — only 1 of 15 tested cultures tolerated 0.5% NaCl; most cultures were inhibited at lower concentrations (corroborated by Wakabayashi & Egusa, 1966)
- **Serological typing**: Six strains analyzed serologically by Ordal belonged to two distinct serological types
- **Preservation**: Viable for at least one year when frozen at −70°C on Cytophaga agar plates

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Host range**: Isolated from nine species of fish plus a bullfrog tadpole across six southeastern US states; also isolated from **[[Blueback Salmon]]** and **[[Chinook Salmon]]** in the Pacific Northwest
- **Disease ecology**: Causes **[[Columnaris Disease]]**, an external bacterial infection affecting both cold-water (e.g., salmonids) and warmwater fish species
- **Environmental constraints**: Low salt tolerance (inhibited above 0.5% NaCl) suggests a strictly freshwater niche, contrasting with the higher NaCl tolerance (0.7%) of most host fish species
- **Related pathogen comparison**: Distinguished from **[[Cytophaga Psychrophila]]** (another fish-pathogenic myxobacterium, causal agent of cold-water disease) primarily by differential sensitivity to bacitracin and polymyxin B
- **Geographic distribution**: Documented in both southeastern and northwestern United States, indicating broad geographic range within North American freshwater fish populations

## Key Findings

### Antibiotic Sensitivity
- **Sensitive** (all 12–16 cultures tested): chloramphenicol, cephalothin, chlortetracycline, demethylchlortetracycline, dihydrostreptomycin, erythromycin, kanamycin (moderate), lincomycin, methacycline, oleandomycin, oxytetracycline, penicillin, phenethicillin, streptomycin, tetracycline, ampicillin, novobiocin
- **Resistant**: bacitracin, cloxacillin, colistin, methicillin, nafcillin, neomycin, nystatin, oxacillin, polymyxin B, ristocetin, vancomycin, viomycin
- Results were largely consistent with independent findings by Wakabayashi & Egusa (1966) on Japanese strains, except for **colistin**, likely due to disc concentration differences
- **[[Oxytetracycline]]** and **[[Chlortetracycline]]** previously confirmed effective for external columnaris disease control in aquarium fish

### Sulfonamides & Other Chemotherapeutics
- All 12 tested cultures were susceptible to 12 sulfonamides, 5 nitrofuran compounds, methenamine mandelate, nalidixic acid, and trichlobismonium chloride
- **Resistant**: para-aminosalicylic acid and isoniazid (anti-tuberculous drugs) showed no inhibitory activity
- Sulfamethizole, sulfathiazole, and sulfisoxazole produced wider inhibition zones than sulfisomidine, sulfadimethoxine, sulfadiazine, and sulfamerazine
- Narrowest zones occurred with trisulfapyrimidines; some colonies grew within inhibition zones with no clear resistance pattern — suggesting sulfonamides may not be optimal therapeutics
- **[[Methenamine Mandelate]]** proposed as a potentially effective acidic-environment treatment, given *C. columnaris*'s pH sensitivity

### Disinfectants & Dyes
- **[[Neutral Acriflavine]]** identified as the most promising disinfectant: MIC ranged from 0.076–0.75 µg/ml across cultures, 10–100× more active than triphenylmethane dyes
- Methyl violet MIC was uniformly 3.1 µg/ml
- **Malachite green** and **methylene blue** showed equal *in vitro* activity, but methylene blue recommended due to malachite green's higher fish toxicity (50–200× more toxic)
- **[[Dowicil 100]]** was the most active of four quaternary ammonium compounds tested; potentially viable given favorable fish toxicity data (no ill effect on fathead minnows at 20 µg/ml after 72 hrs)

### Heavy Metal Salts
- **Merthiolate** and **pyridylmercuric acetate (PMA)** were the most active heavy metal compounds
- Despite high efficacy, heavy metal compounds face regulatory obstacles preventing approval for use in fish culture in the US and other countries

### Herbicides
- **[[Diquat]]** showed greater activity against *C. columnaris* than **[[Paraquat]]** in twofold dilution assays
- Diquat's therapeutic threshold (6 µg/ml) for controlling columnaris in external lesions was later confirmed by Meyer (1968), though standard aquatic weed control concentrations were ineffective in this study

### Insecticides/Parasiticides
- Only concentrations well above levels toxic to fish showed any antibacterial activity — rendering this class impractical for treatment

### Salicylanilides
- All three tested salicylanilides shared identical MIC to formalin, potassium permanganate, and 2,6-diamino-3-phenylazopyridine
- Most salicylanilides deemed unsuitable due to high fish toxicity, except **4′-acetamido salicylanilide**, which showed low toxicity in 5 of 6 tested fish species

### Overall Treatment Recommendations
Ranked by promise for prolonged/indefinite external treatment of columnaris disease:
1. **Neutral acriflavine** (most promising)
2. Formalin, methylene blue, Diquat, Dowicil 100, 4′-acetamido salicylanilide, sodium chloride (moderately promising)
3. All other tested compounds — either insufficiently active or excessively toxic to fish

This work established a foundational antibiogram for *C. columnaris* and provided critical groundwork for subsequent *in vivo* efficacy trials in fish disease management, particularly informing the continued clinical use of **[[Oxytetracycline]]** and exploration of **[[Formalin]]**-based treatments in aquaculture.
