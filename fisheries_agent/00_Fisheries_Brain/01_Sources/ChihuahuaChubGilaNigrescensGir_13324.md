# Chihuahua Chub Recovery Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery plan (Region 2, Albuquerque, New Mexico, approved 1986) addresses the [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), a federally threatened minnow treated as endangered by the State of New Mexico. The plan's goal is to remove the species from the federal list by restoring it as a secure, stable, self-sustaining population within the [[Mimbres River]]. It is based on a 1980 management plan by [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]] biologists. The U.S. population is a relict occurring only on private land, and the chub's decline is attributed primarily to habitat loss from flooding, channelization and levee construction.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomy.** The species was first described from the Mimbres River as *Gila pulchella* (Baird and Girard, 1854), later placed in *Tigoma nigrescens* (Girard, 1856). Taxonomic revision resolved homonym conflicts and adopted *Gila nigrescens*, with the Rio Janos at Boca Grande, Chihuahua, as the type locality. The chub is distinct from the [[Rio Grande Chub]] (*Gila pandora*), which has also been called *G. nigrescens* by some authors.

**Morphology.** Adults average 5–6 inches and may reach 12 inches. The dorsal fin origin lies behind the pelvic fin, dorsal fin rays are usually 9, and there are usually 67–78 lateral line scales. Coloration is dusky brown above and whitish below, with orange-red breeding coloration around the mouth and lower fins. Post-larvae have a spot above the lateral line preceding the caudal fin.

**Biology.** The chub depends on deep pools with undercut banks or overhanging vegetation, which provide escape cover and foraging. It feeds much like a trout, taking surface terrestrial insects as well as aquatic invertebrates and possibly fish and vegetation. Spawning occurs in late April and May in quiet pools about 3–7 feet deep over matted aquatic vegetation. At [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], the highest reproductive success occurred in ponds with gravel beds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** The chub is endemic to the Guzman Basin. It was collected in 1851 from the Mimbres River, was not seen again until 1975 (about 20 individuals found by Bill Rogers), and is now confined to a reach from Allie Canyon downstream to the Mimbres Post Office plus a spring-fed tributary opposite [[Bear Canyon Reservoir]]. In Chihuahua, it was formerly known from at least 15 localities; a 1979 survey (Miller and Chernoff) found only 7 occupied, 3 with healthy populations.

**Associated fauna.** Six native fishes occur in the Guzman Basin, including the [[Rio Grande Mountain Sucker]], fathead minnow, beautiful shiner, Mexican stoneroller and an undescribed pupfish. In the Mimbres drainage the stoneroller was apparently never present and the beautiful shiner has been extirpated. Exotic species reported with the chub include carp, black bullhead, [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Longfin Dace]]. Historical accounts suggest the Mimbres once supported greater diversity, including catfish and gar.

**Habitat decline.** Historical descriptions (Antisell, 1856) describe pools and lagoons ringed by willows, with the river up to 2.5 feet deep and flowing 2.5 miles per hour in summer. Today the river terminus is usually dry. Diversions, uneven flows, flooding and repeated stream modifications by landowners have eliminated these conditions.

**Population status.** Until late 1978 the species numbered no more than 30–40 adults. Flooding in late 1978 and early 1979 displaced fish downstream and they were lost. Subsequent inventories found no more than 150 adults. Ten chubs were placed in Dexter National Fish Hatchery on March 17, 1979, and reproduced successfully; fish from the [[Rio Piedras Verdes]] population in Chihuahua were also held there.

## Key Findings

- **Goal:** Delist the chub by establishing a secure, self-sustaining population in the Mimbres River.
- **Recovery criteria:** (1) conservation easements or legal agreements on the spring-fed tributary where fish currently occur, and (2) two additional secure populations within the former Mimbres range.
- **Primary cause of decline:** Loss of habitat from flooding linked to watershed degradation, loss of riparian vegetation, and landowner channelization and levees, which must be repeated each spring near spawning time. Exotic fish were judged a minor factor by the plan, a view commented on by reviewers.
- **Core actions:**
  1. Protect occupied habitat and the spring-fed refuge, ideally with [[The Nature Conservancy]].
  2. Reclaim two habitats, including excavated 3–7 ft pools confluent with the river.
  3. Reintroduce chubs and monitor success.
- **Reintroduction sites:** Three areas were identified, including the box canyon below Cooney Place, the river near San Lorenzo, and a New Mexico Department of Game and Fish property about 3 miles upstream of San Lorenzo (added after review).
- **Regulatory measures:** Ban non-native fish introductions; prohibit taking minnows for bait (state regulation already restricts bait minnows downstream to Highway 90, except at Bear Canyon Reservoir); replace push-up irrigation diversions with permanent diversions that do not dewater downstream habitat.
- **Captive propagation:** Maintain a reserve gene pool at Dexter National Fish Hatchery, supplemented every two years with wild fish, and determine genetic relationships between U.S. and Mexican stocks.
- **Mexico:** The plan acknowledges the major portion of the range lies in Mexico, but no Mexican recovery actions were included; the plan encourages diplomatic protection efforts.
- **Implementation:** The schedule lists tasks by priority (Priority 1 for habitat protection of the spring tributary, costed at up to $50,000 in the first year), responsible agencies (USFWS Region 2, NMDGF, Army Corps of Engineers), and estimated costs.
- **Agency comments:** Reviewers (USFWS Director, State Game Commission, U.S. Forest Service, Bureau of Reclamation, Army Corps of Engineers, BLM, and Soil Conservation Service) requested clearer habitat requirements data, clarification of Section 7 consultation, land ownership and water quality details, and financial arrangements for landowners. Responses indicated the USGS operates gauge 08477110 at Mimbres, and that no upstream mining is known.
