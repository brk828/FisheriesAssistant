# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin - FY 1992 Work Plan

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #endangered-species #river-management

## Executive Summary

This document presents the Fiscal Year 1992 work plan and budget for the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. Approved by the Colorado River Implementation Committee on August 15, 1991, the plan allocates $2,988,900 across five recovery elements: habitat management (38.4%), research/monitoring/data management (20.4%), stocking native fishes (20.3%), program management (15.7%), and habitat development (2.6%). The program targets four endangered cyprinid and catostomid species: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). Major emphases include water rights acquisition, continuation of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] operation studies, investigations of [[Aspinall Unit]] operations on the [[Gunnison River]], and implementation of propagation and genetics management plans.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program represents a collaborative effort among federal agencies (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, Western Area Power Administration), state agencies (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming Departments of Natural Resources), environmental groups, and water users. The program operates under a Long Range Plan developed in December 1990 that identifies goals, objectives, and priority activities for recovery of endangered fishes in the Upper Basin.

The four target species are endemic cyprinids and catostomids of the [[Colorado River]] system. [[Colorado Squawfish]] is the largest minnow native to North America, historically occupying mainstem rivers. [[Razorback Sucker]] is a long-lived catostomid showing reproductive and recruitment failure throughout its range. [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] are members of genus *Gila*, with taxonomy requiring clarification through ongoing genetic studies.

The work plan development process involves technical and management committees reviewing approximately 65 project proposals submitted for FY 92 consideration. Ranking factors include consistency with Recovery Program goals, degree of urgency to avoid jeopardy, essentiality for recovery, timeliness of results, likelihood of success, relationship to other priority work, opportunity timing, and proposal quality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The endangered fishes occupy diverse riverine habitats in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], including the mainstem [[Colorado River]], [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Gunnison River]], and [[White River]]. Critical habitats include spawning areas, backwater nurseries for young-of-year fish, and flooded bottomlands used during spring high flows.

Flow regime alterations from dam operations represent primary threats to recovery. [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] on the [[Green River]] and the [[Aspinall Unit]] on the [[Gunnison River]] have fundamentally altered natural hydrographs, affecting spawning cues, larval transport, nursery habitat availability, and water temperature regimes. The program seeks to re-regulate dam operations to more closely mimic natural flow patterns while meeting water development needs.

Nonnative fish species pose significant threats through predation and competition. Species of concern include channel catfish, common carp, and northern pike. Studies indicate high mortality rates when hatchery-reared endangered fishes encounter established nonnative populations.

Habitat degradation from channel alteration, levee construction, exotic vegetation encroachment, and loss of flooded bottomland connectivity further limits recovery potential. Water diversions create passage barriers, fragmenting populations and blocking access to historical spawning areas.

Water temperature, turbidity, sediment transport, and pollutants influence reproductive success and recruitment. The program investigates relationships between flow magnitude/timing and these water quality parameters.

## Key Findings

**Budget Allocation and Program Structure:**
- Total FY 92 budget: $2,988,900 distributed across recovery elements
- Habitat Management: $1,146,500 (38.4%), including $183,000 Section 7 funds
- Research, Monitoring, Data Management: $610,700 (20.4%)
- Stocking Native Fishes: $607,300 (20.3%)
- Program Management: $468,300 (15.7%)
- Habitat Development: $77,000 (2.6%)
- Nonnative Fish/Sportfish Management: $79,100 (2.6%)

**Water Rights Acquisition (Section 7 Funds):**
- Section 7 fund balance as of October 1, 1991: $535,500
- New project depletion charge: $11.50/acre-foot (adjusted for inflation)
- Five projects totaling $183,000 funded with Section 7 funds in FY 92
- Water rights consultant services: up to $40,000
- [[Yampa River]] water availability study: $20,000
- Senior scientist review of instream flow methodologies: $70,000
- Policy/legal/institutional issues analysis: $50,000

**Flaming Gorge Dam Operations:**
- Five-year follow-up research period entering third year
- Studies using cooperative, systems-oriented approach
- Focus on testing hypotheses and refining flow recommendations for spring and winter periods
- Total FY 92 funding: $382,100
- Specific investigations: squawfish reproduction and larval abundance ($63,600), early life biology and development ($64,500), backwater invertebrate predation ($14,000)
- Biological Opinion anticipated for completion in early FY 92
- Outyear cost estimate: $900,000

**Aspinall Unit Investigations:**
- Five-year cooperative study to provide data for biological opinion
- Test flows released to mimic natural hydrograph
- FY 92 studies include: ichthyofaunal survey ($43,300), flow effects on squawfish larval production ($69,800), nursery habitat availability ($80,100), effects on young-of-year squawfish ($57,300)
- Total FY 92 funding: $265,600
- Outyear cost estimate: $1,000,000

**Propagation and Genetics Management:**
- Implementation of Propagation/Genetics Management Plan: $259,900
- Development of refugia facilities and captive populations for all four endangered species
- Back-up refugia populations (F₁ progeny from wild fish)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] genetic study: $30,000
- [[Razorback Sucker]] genetics: $45,500
- Operation and maintenance of [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] research hatchery: $100,000/year
- Ouray pond and water supply development: $860,000 (Reclamation drought relief funds)
- Horsethief refugia development: $130,000 ($100,000 drought relief, $30,000 Colorado funds)
- Outyear cost estimate: $2,000,000

**Stocking and Augmentation Studies:**
- Analysis of previously stocked [[Colorado Squawfish]] in [[Upper Colorado River]]: $67,400
- Assessment of squawfish in [[White River]] including Kenney Reservoir stockings: $46,500
- Augmentation plan development for [[Razorback Sucker]] in [[Colorado River]] and [[Green River]]
- Chemoreception, imprinting, and propagation studies: $75,500

**Taxonomy and Population Studies:**
- *Gila* taxonomy study: $314,700 to resolve uncertainty about humpback, bonytail, and roundtail chub species boundaries
- Focus on collection and genetic analyses using nonlethal techniques
- Museum and captive specimen analyses
- Humpback chub surveys in Desolation and Cataract Canyons conducted incidentally
- Outyear cost estimate: $482,000

**Habitat Studies and Restoration:**
- [[Upper Colorado River]] habitat suitability study (Palisade to Rifle reach): $58,600
- Assessment of historical but now unoccupied endangered fish habitat
- Prerequisite data for potential squawfish reintroduction
- Old Charley Wash wetlands restoration pilot project: $77,000
- Creation of flooded bottomland demonstration area on [[Green River]]
- Testing hypotheses about spring habitat requirements

**Gunnison River Restoration:**
- New emphasis area for FY 92
- Baseline fisheries data establishment
- Assessment of existing and potential habitat conditions
- Evaluation of passage barriers at Redlands diversion
- Ichthyofaunal survey from Delta (RM 55) to confluence with [[Colorado River]]
- Integration with Aspinall Unit studies
- Three-year study design

**Flow Recommendations and Hydrology:**
- [[Colorado River]] flow recommendations and quantification (Grand Valley): $43,600 (final year)
- Development of empirical relationships between squawfish habitat quantity and stream flow
- Video imagery and habitat measurements
- Backwater habitat availability investigation: $137,700
- Assessment using Map Image Processing System (MIPS)
- General hydrology support: $64,100/year
- Temperature data collection, stream network model maintenance, HYDROSS model operation
- [[Green River]] and [[Yampa River]] channel monitoring: $11,800 (final year of pilot study)

**Standardized Monitoring Program:**
- Ongoing population monitoring: $118,800/year
- Determination of status and trends
- Development of population indices
- Assessment of management program efficacy
- Backwater nursery habitat availability assessment using video mapping
- Larval fishes identification: $46,000
- Database management: $22,000/year

**Nonnative Fish Management:**
- Laboratory and field studies to assess nonnative fish impacts
- Building on previous work by Colorado and Larval Fish Lab
- Focus on practical management applications
- Studies of predator-prey and competitive interactions
- Evaluation of turbidity, vegetative cover, and multiple species presence effects
- Pike reproduction and diet studies expansion into Utah

**Recovery Goals Development:**
- Population Viability Analysis (PVA) for [[Colorado Squawfish]]: $18,900
- Development of scientifically defensible recovery goals
- Questions addressed: number of populations needed, population size requirements, location optimization, effects of deterministic factors, model assumptions

**Information and Education:**
- Multi-faceted public education program: $79,100/year
- 30-minute video for public television
- Denver Museum of Natural History display with aquarium
- Media training for program managers and researchers
- Endangered fish information on Colorado and Utah fishing licenses
- Recovery Program newsletter
- Historical information compilation

**Program Management Recommendations:**
- Program management costs limited to 15% of annual budget
- Current costs (15.7%) deemed reasonable given program complexity
- Staff requirements for participating agencies not originally anticipated
- Integration plan needed for all hatchery and propagation activities

**White River Work Plan:**
- Development of systematic work plan for defining flow recommendations
- Compilation of existing literature and available information
- Identification of existing and historical endangered fish populations
- Assessment of river significance relative to other reaches
- Methodology and time-frame for obtaining flow recommendations
