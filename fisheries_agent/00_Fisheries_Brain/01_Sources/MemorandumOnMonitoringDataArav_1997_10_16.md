## Executive Summary

This memorandum reports fish community monitoring data collected on 16–17 October 1997 along Aravaipa Creek, a perennial desert stream in the [[Gila River Basin]] (Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona). Six stations were sampled across a longitudinal gradient: three upstream of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] (Turkey Creek-Gauge, Chimney Rock, Guest House), one within the Wilderness Area (Hell Hole), and two downstream (Wagner-Gauge, White's Ranch). Sampling combined seining (upstream 100 m of each 200 m reach) and backpack electroshocking (downstream 100 m), standardized as catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) by area seined (per 10 m²) or by shocking time (per minute). A key finding is the notable resurgence of the non-native [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) at three of six stations, at densities markedly higher than in prior 1990 and 1993 streamwide surveys — attributed to an absence of scouring floods since winter 1995 and low baseflows in 1996–1997. The native fish assemblage, including federally listed [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), remained present throughout most sites, though loach minnow at the lowermost station (White's Ranch) showed a high incidence of parasitism (possibly yellow grub), and habitat quality there was degraded by sand/gravel infilling of rock-associated microhabitats.

## Background & Description

While this monitoring memo is primarily an abundance/distribution survey rather than a morphological study, it does document age-class structure (juvenile vs. adult) for each species at each station, and notes species-specific field/taxonomic identification challenges (e.g., "unidentified sucker," "unidentified fry" categories in the raw catch tables, reflecting difficulty distinguishing juvenile [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] in early life stages). All captured [[Red Shiner]] individuals were recorded as adults, suggesting either an absence of recent local recruitment at time of sampling or a bias toward sampling detectability of larger fish via seining.

Species documented across stations, by taxonomic group:
- **Cyprinidae:** [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*)
- **Catostomidae:** [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)
- **Ictaluridae:** [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus melas*)
- **Poeciliidae:** [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*, non-native)
- **Centrarchidae:** [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*, non-native)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Aravaipa Creek supports a diverse assemblage of native Sonoran Desert fishes occupying distinct mesohabitat types quantified at each station: **run**, **riffle**, **glide**, and **pool** (including off-channel pools). Habitat composition varied by reach:

- **Guest House (Station 1):** 49% run, 46% riffle, 5% glide — shallow, narrow channel (mean depths 0.12–0.14 m).
- **Chimney Rock (Station 2):** near-even mix of run (48%), riffle (35%), and glide (34%).
- **Turkey Creek/Gauge (Station 3):** dominated by run (93%), minimal riffle/pool.
- **Hell Hole (Station 4, within Wilderness Area):** run-dominated (95.3%), minimal riffle/glide.
- **Wagner/Gauge (Station 7):** most heterogeneous, with substantial glide (31.5%), riffle (30.2%), run (24.2%), and off-channel pool (14.1%).
- **White's Ranch (Station 8):** riffle-dominated (62.3%) with wide, deep glides (37.7%); notably, interstitial rock-associated refugia used by fish were infilled with sand and small gravel, indicating sedimentation-driven habitat degradation.

[[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] — both federally listed species — were most abundant in the upper and middle reaches, particularly at Turkey Creek/Gauge and Chimney Rock, consistent with their known preference for swift riffle/run habitats over sand-silt substrates. The presence of [[Red Shiner]], a generalist invasive cyprinid, in lower and middle Aravaipa Creek reflects reduced hydrologic disturbance (flood scouring), which normally suppresses this species' population growth in the system. [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Western Mosquitofish]], both non-native lentic-adapted species, were also detected in low numbers, generally associated with pool/glide microhabitats.

## Key Findings

- **Six stations sampled** across upper, middle, and lower/Wilderness Area reaches of Aravaipa Creek using paired seining (200 m reach, upstream 100 m) and backpack electroshocking (downstream 100 m; 150–250 V, 75–125 pps, 0.5–0.6 amps).
- **Red Shiner resurgence:** Captured at 3 of 6 stations — Hell Hole (11 individuals), Wagner/Gauge (7), White's Ranch (1) — all adults, captured via seining only.
  - 1997 rate: 19 shiners / 1.2 km sampled = **15.8 fish/km**
  - 1990 rate (Minckley, 23.5 km streamwide survey): 15–50 shiners = **0.6–2.1 fish/km**
  - 1993 rate (Bettaso et al. 1995, same 23.5 km segment): 1 shiner = **0.04 fish/km** (or 0.07 fish/km adjusting for ~60% sampling coverage)
  - **Conclusion:** Red Shiner abundance in 1997 is substantially higher than in 1990 or 1993, likely due to lack of flood disturbance since winter 1995 and low baseflows in 1996–1997.
- **Loach Minnow parasitism:** High incidence of apparent parasitism (possibly yellow grub) observed in Loach Minnow at White's Ranch (Station 8); voucher specimens (2 parasitized loach minnow + red shiners) sent to the [[ASU Museum]].
- **Habitat degradation at White's Ranch:** Interstitial rock/eddy habitats normally used by fish were filled with sand and small gravel, indicating sediment loading impacts on lower-reach habitat structure.
- **Longitudinal species composition (seine data, Table 7):**
  - Upper Aravaipa: dominated by *Meda fulgida* (56.8%), followed by *Agosia chrysogaster* (17.6%) and *Pantosteus clarki* (16.1%); total n=199.
  - Middle Aravaipa: dominated by *Agosia chrysogaster* (25.8%) and *Meda fulgida* (34.3%), with *Cyprinella lutrensis* present at 9.1%; total n=198.
  - Lower Aravaipa: dominated by *Agosia chrysogaster* (73.7%); *Cyprinella lutrensis* at 5.3%; total n=19 (low overall catch).
- **Longitudinal species composition (electroshocker data, Table 8):**
  - Upper Aravaipa: *Agosia chrysogaster* (17.6%), *Tiaroga cobitis* (31.6%), *Pantosteus clarki* (24.7%); total n=624.
  - Middle Aravaipa: *Agosia chrysogaster* dominant (47.5%); *Tiaroga cobitis* (20.1%); *Ameiurus natalis* (5.0%); total n=680.
  - Lower Aravaipa: *Agosia chrysogaster* dominant (66.2%); *Tiaroga cobitis* (16.9%); total n=420.
- **Equipment malfunction** at Hell Hole (Station 4) prevented summarization of electroshocker fish captures for that station (Table 4), limiting data completeness for the Wilderness Area reach.
- Data tables (1–8) enable direct comparison with [[W.L. Minckley]]'s historical longer-term Aravaipa Creek dataset, supporting the identification of the Red Shiner increase as a genuine ecological shift rather than a sampling artifact.
