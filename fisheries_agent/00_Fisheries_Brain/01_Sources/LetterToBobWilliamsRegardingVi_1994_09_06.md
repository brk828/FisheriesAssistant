# Virgin River Spinedace Conservation Agreement Negotiations

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #conservation-policy #[[Endangered Species Act]]

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 letter from Pamela Hyde (American Rivers, Conservation Associate) to Bob Williams (USDI Fish and Wildlife Service, Utah State Office) concerning negotiations over a proposed **conservation agreement in lieu of listing** for the [[Virgin River Spinedace]] under the Endangered Species Act. The letter is primarily a policy and procedural communication rather than a scientific study, but it contains important context on the species' known and suspected geographic range, its imperiled conservation status, and the scientific experts recognized as authorities on Virgin River fishes. It raises concerns about transparency, interagency (regional) coordination within the Fish and Wildlife Service, and the adequacy of public involvement in the negotiation process prior to finalizing a conservation agreement.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or physical descriptive data for *Lepidomeda mollispinis* (Virgin River Spinedace) are provided in this source. The document is procedural/administrative in nature and does not describe body form, coloration, fin structure, or other taxonomic characteristics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat**: The [[Virgin River]] drainage system, a tributary system associated with the Colorado River Basin.
- **Geographic distribution**:
  - Confirmed occurrence in **Utah** (the jurisdiction driving the current negotiations).
  - Suspected/likely occurrence in **Arizona** (supported by consultation with the Arizona Game and Fish Department).
  - Possible occurrence in **Nevada**, though this had not yet been confirmed or acted upon by relevant agencies (Nevada Department of Wildlife, USFWS Region 1) at the time of writing.
- **Conservation status context**: Described as "a fish that is in serious jeopardy," reflecting the basis for its proposed listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The proposed listing had already been published in the Federal Register prior to this letter.
- **Threats/drivers of conservation action**: Implied water resource management pressures from the [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]] in Utah, suggesting habitat modification or water withdrawal concerns in the Virgin River system as a driver for the conservation agreement discussions.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory mechanism**: A "conservation agreement in lieu of listing" was being negotiated as an alternative to formally listing the Virgin River Spinedace under the [[Endangered Species Act]], despite the species' proposed listing already being published in the Federal Register.
- **Process transparency concerns**: American Rivers raised concerns that negotiation meetings, while nominally open to the public, lacked proactive public notice, disadvantaging interested stakeholders who were not already aware of the process.
- **Interagency coordination gap**: Despite potential occurrence of the species in Arizona and Nevada, USFWS **Region 2** (Arizona) and **Region 1** (Nevada) involvement was unclear or absent, raising concerns about incomplete geographic/jurisdictional scope in the negotiations. The Arizona Game and Fish Department had reportedly been consulted, but the Service's Region 2 involvement remained unconfirmed.
- **Scientific expertise recommended**: The letter explicitly recommends engaging recognized ichthyological experts on Virgin River fishes, naming:
  - [[James Deacon]]
  - [[Wendell Minckley]]
  - [[Paul Marsh]]
- **Legal/procedural question raised**: American Rivers requested clarification on the statutory or regulatory authority underpinning the Fish and Wildlife Service's practice of negotiating conservation agreements as an alternative to formal ESA listing — noting this was a relatively novel regulatory tool with significant potential consequences.
- **Stakeholders involved**: Washington County Water Conservancy District, State of Utah, USDI Fish and Wildlife Service (Utah State Office), American Rivers, Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund, and Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance were all identified as parties with interest in or involvement in the negotiation process.
