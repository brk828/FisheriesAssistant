# Rotenone Piscicide Treatment Chronology: Virgin River Fish Eradication Operation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an operational record and work plan for a four-day [[Rotenone]] treatment (piscicide application) on the Virgin River system and its tributaries, drains, and irrigation diversions. It contains a project chronology, daily work assignments for five field teams, equipment lists, flow and dosing tables, and handwritten field notes from [[Bioassay]] observations. The treatment used [[Noxfish]] (rotenone formulation) applied at stations keyed to measured flow, with a detoxification station using [[Potassium Permanganate]] to neutralize toxicant before it reached downstream areas. The operation is described as a fish eradication project, with the stated goal of tracking toxicant movement by dye, monitoring caged fish, and protecting areas behind a barrier dam. The document is largely scan-degraded, so many numbers and fish counts are uncertain.

## Background & Description

The record is an undated-to-partially-legible project file, with the date most likely 1954 (OCR: "Of Nev 1954"). It is organized into:

- **Project chronology**: a time-stamped schedule across Days 1 to 4 showing when each spray, drip, and detox station starts or stops.
- **Team work assignments** (Teams A, F, G, K), each with personnel, equipment, and timing.
- **Field notes** (pages 8 to 16): handwritten observations of toxicant arrival, dye tracing, water temperature, and fish mortality at monitoring points.

Named sites include Washington Diversion, Mill Creek, Seegmiller Marsh and its outlets, Smoots Drain, Washington Fields outlet, Johnson Diversion, Twin Bridges, Ft. Pierce Wash, Duck Pond and its outlet, Man-O-War Bridge, Santa Clara River, a sewage outfall, a golf course outfall, I-15, and the barrier dam. The primary target is the mainstream Virgin River reach between Man-O-War Bridge and the barrier.

Treatment equipment listed for the drip stations includes 82 gal of rotenone (3 drums), 12 barrels with stands, 14 drip tubes, a metering pump, two vehicles, a quad, and radios. The bioassay team carried about 200 minnows and suckers, minnow cages, and a distribution unit.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The treatment targets an entire fish community in a regulated desert river system with irrigation diversions, drains, marshes, and urban outfalls. Flow was the operational driver. Drip rates were set against stream discharge, which ranged from about 3 cfs (Mill Creek, Santa Clara River) to 37 cfs (Twin Bridges) and 46 cfs (sum of Day 1 sites). Dye tracing was used to follow the toxicant plume and to determine when detox had to be discontinued if dye bypassed the station. Live cages were positioned at intervals to detect the arrival and passage of the toxicant.

Water temperature was recorded at some stations, including about 21°C in one bioassay note. Notes also describe substrate, including cobble and sand substrate, and marsh and riparian conditions. The field observations refer to dense vegetation (for example, tamarisk) and low optical clarity in some reaches, which would affect dye and fish observation.

The sewage outfall and golf course outlet were treated as drip sites. This suggests the operation was also intended to remove fish from urban-influenced drains and outfalls.

## Key Findings

**Operational plan**

- The operation spanned four days, with drip stations on Days 1 to 3, spray teams from Day 2 to Day 3, and detox from Day 2 (1400) to Day 4 (0400).
- Day 1 drip stations (Washington Diversion, Mill Creek, Johnson Diversion, Washington Fields outlet, Seegmiller Marsh outlet, Smoots Drain) totaled 46 cfs, with 10 drips over an 8-hour period.
- Day 2 stations included Twin Bridges (37 cfs, 2 drips), Ft. Pierce Wash (14 cfs, 2 drips), Duck Pond outlet (5 cfs), sewage outlet, Santa Clara River (3 cfs), golf course, and Man-O-War Bridge (5 cfs each).
- Day 3 had timed drip restarts from 0000 to 0800 at Ft. Pierce, Twin Bridges, Duck Pond, sewage outlet, Santa Clara River, golf course, and Man-O-War Bridge.
- Mainstream Virgin River spot treatment ran from Man-O-War Bridge to the barrier, starting at 1100 on Day 3.

**Detoxification**

- Potassium permanganate was applied at 1134 lb per detox station. The detox station was started on Day 2 at 1400 and stopped on Day 4 at 0400.
- The procedure was to discontinue detox if dye had bypassed the station. Field notes describe timing of arrival at downstream stations and a detox-related shutdown.

**Monitoring and bioassay**

- Live cages and minnow or sucker monitors were placed at Twin Bridges, I-15, the barrier dam, Man-O-War Bridge, and Lower Ft. Pierce Wash, and checked hourly during the expected toxicant window.
- Field notes record fish passage and mortality at multiple points. The entries include counts of "passes" (fish lost or moved) and mortality for a range of species, but many numbers are unreadable in the scan. Entries that can be read indicate significant mortality in caged fish and in fish at the barrier and Man-O-War Bridge.
- Probable species named in the notes include red shiner ("Notropis lutrensis," OCR "Mototropis Lutrensis"), suckers of the genus [[Catostomus]], and a sucker identified as [[Pantosteus]] sp. Identifications are uncertain due to scan errors.

**Coordination**

- Team G (6 persons) provided radio contact across all elements, managed river reach signing, supplied equipment, and tracked rotenone and potassium permanganate delivery rates.
- Team A (4 persons) ran the drip stations.
- Team F (bioassay) maintained live cages and replaced fish at the barrier dam.
- Team K (4 persons) ran the mainstream spray crew.

**Data limitations**

- Much of the document is scan-degraded, including several numeric entries, species names, and mortality counts. Quantitative results such as total fish killed or detox efficiency cannot be confirmed from the text.
- The 1954 date and the Utah location are inferred from partially legible text and site names, not stated explicitly.
