## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific or morphological study**; it is a legal/administrative Memorandum of Clarification (MOC) related to the 1995 Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) that established the framework for the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP). The MOC clarifies that the MOA's purpose is twofold: (1) to conserve habitat and promote recovery of "included species" listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) within the 100-year floodplain of the [[Lower Colorado River]], and (2) to accommodate existing water diversion and power production while optimizing future water/power development consistent with law. Critically, the MOC removes any implication that the MOA or MSCP planning provides "regulatory assurance" or exemption from ESA Section 7 consultation requirements. No species-specific morphological, physiological, or population data are presented in this excerpt; the attached species list (Attachment 2) is referenced but not reproduced in the extracted text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat scope**: The conservation program's geographic and ecological focus is the **100-year floodplain of the [[Lower Colorado River]]**, encompassing riparian and aquatic habitats subject to federal water and power management activities.
- **Anthropogenic pressures**: The dual mandate of the MSCP explicitly links species conservation with continued **water diversion** and **power production** operations, indicating that the ecological niche of included species is shaped by, and must be balanced against, hydrological management and infrastructure development along the river.
- **Regulatory ecological context**: Federal actions within this floodplain trigger **ESA Section 7 consultations**, meaning habitat conditions and species status are subject to ongoing federal oversight via the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], which evaluates conservation measures and program development status when formulating Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPAs) and Reasonable and Prudent Measures (RPMs).

## Key Findings

- The MOC formally interprets the 1995 MOA as establishing the [[Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP) with the joint goals of species/habitat conservation and continued water/power operations on the [[Lower Colorado River]].
- The document explicitly **disclaims any "regulatory assurance"** or exemption from ESA requirements for signatories — a key clarification of intent versus the original MOA language.
- During ESA [[Section 7 Consultation]], the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] is directed to consider (1) MSCP conservation measures and (2) MSCP program development status, but may still impose additional RPAs/RPMs if these are judged insufficient to meet ESA standards.
- All signatories reaffirm that the MOA/MSCP does **not abrogate** independent statutory responsibilities (Article II) and remains **subject to all applicable federal and state law** (Article V).
- Signatories include federal (United States of America), and multi-state agencies from **Arizona**, **California**, and **Nevada**, reflecting the tri-state jurisdictional nature of [[Lower Colorado River]] management, including agencies such as the [[Arizona Game and Fish Commission]], [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]], [[Colorado River Board of California]], and [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]].
