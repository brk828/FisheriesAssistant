## Executive Summary

This OCR-scanned excerpt from the *Proceedings of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia* (1860, pp. 472–475) is a taxonomic compilation by [[Charles C. Abbott]]. It contains formal descriptions of new fish species, a taxonomic note on a new echinoderm subgenus, a set of nomenclatural errata, and a brief description of an ammonoid cephalopod from the Rockford Goniatite bed. The fish descriptions cover one marine flying fish from Chile, four North American freshwater species, and one apodal (eel-like) species. Most descriptions give diagnostic characters, fin-ray counts, lengths, colour in alcohol, and habitat. Specimens are held in the museum of the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]].

## Key Findings

**1. Ammonoid cephalopod (Rockford, Indiana).** The description covers a laterally compressed, deeply septate ammonoid with six lobes and six saddles per septum. The dorsal lobe is infundibuliform and acutely pointed. The superior lateral lobe is halberd-shaped. Measurements are given for an imperfect, entirely septate specimen: length 2.55 inches, height 2.15 inches. The genus name is not legible in the OCR text.

**2. *Oligoporus*, a new echinoderm subgenus.** Abbott proposes this subgenus within *Melonites* because the type species of *Melonites* has eight double rows of pores per ambulacrum, whereas the new form has only four. The author notes that the difference may not yet justify full generic status, pending further species or better specimens. The name derives from Greek *oligos* ("few") and *opos* ("opening").

**3. Errata.** The note corrects several overlooked proof errors:
- Page 382: "second radial" becomes "second radials." A passage is substituted with "secondary radials," which rest partly on the second and partly on the first primary radials, or directly on the latter in the absence of the former.
- Page 382: a new entry, "Anal plate 1 (known), varying much in size and form," is inserted.
- Page 382: "Actinocrinus scilutus" is corrected to "Actinocrinus."
- Page 389: "Forbsiocrinus" is corrected to "Forbesiocrinus" in several lines.

**4. *Exocetus chiliensis*.** This is a flying fish with a head of medium size and no scales. The eye is unusually large, with a diameter entering the head length 2⅔ times. Fin-ray counts are D 16, P 15, V 6, A 15, C 20. The caudal fin is deeply forked, with the lower lobe one-third longer than the upper. Alcohol colours are a deep brown with purple tint on the dorsal surface, silvery white ventrally, and a chestnut-brown iris. The author notes that living colours were "evidently much brighter."

**5. *Catostomus texanus*.** The species is a large-headed sucker with a dorsal gibbosity carinated throughout its length. Fin-ray counts are D 15, P 16, V 10, A 7, C 18. Total length is 14 inches. Colour is a dull slate above, a white belly, and a yellow throat.

**6. *Catostomus chloropteron*.** Fin-ray counts are D 13, P 16, V 8, A 7, C 20. Total length is 7¾ inches. Colour is light blue on the back and upper sides, pearly white below, with bright yellow fins.

**7. *Gila affinis*.** The species has a slender body with a greatly attenuated tail. Fin-ray counts are D 10, P 16, V 8, A 9, C 28. Total length is 6⅛ inches. Colour is dull violet above and pale pink below the lateral line.

**8. *Semotilus hammondii*.** The body is much compressed and tapers rapidly to the tail. Fin-ray counts are D 9, P 18, V 9, A 9, C 24. Total length is 7 inches. Colour is deep umber brown above and bright yellow on the belly, with a black spot at the anterior base of the dorsal fin.

**9. Apodal fish (genus illegible; epithet *callisoma*).** The description outlines a head that is depressed and a snout that terminates in a rather obtuse tip. Nasal teeth number eight, vomerine teeth nine to thirteen, and palatine teeth are present in two rows. The dorsal fin is short and arises near the tips of the pectoral rays. The colour pattern consists of large, equidistant circular lateral spots and smaller dorsal spots. The throat is longitudinally marked with black lines.
