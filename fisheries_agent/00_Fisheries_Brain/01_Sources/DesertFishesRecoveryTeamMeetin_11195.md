## Executive Summary

This document is a formal recommendation letter from the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, addressing revisions to the draft **Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan** (dated March 23, 1990). The core issue is whether to formally designate the **San Juan River** as a recovery area for the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), for purposes of both downlisting (endangered → threatened) and delisting (removal from the endangered species list). The Team concluded that new biological and hydrological data justify including the San Juan River in recovery objectives, though members were split on whether it should be a mandatory criterion for downlisting versus an either/or alternative with the mainstem Colorado River. Unanimous agreement was reached that recovery in all three major subbasins (Green River, Colorado River, San Juan River) should be required for full delisting. The letter also recommends new criteria involving the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] in Arizona, and dam-passage remediation at [[Taylor Draw Dam]] on the White River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat**: Large, free-flowing warm-water river systems of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], including the San Juan, Green, Yampa, White, and mainstem Colorado Rivers.
- **Reproductive significance**: The San Juan River is identified as **one of only three documented reproductive areas** for Colorado Squawfish in the Upper Basin, alongside the Green River Basin (via [[Yampa River]] and [[White River]]) and the Colorado River mainstem.
- **Barrier-free range**: The San Juan River contains **183 miles** of currently barrier-free habitat, critical for migratory access between Lake Powell and upstream spawning/rearing areas.
- **Flow-recruitment relationship**: Hydrological data show a direct correlation between **maintained/enhanced flows** and successful downstream reproduction; drought years (1988–1989) correlated with reduced juvenile/larval collections.
- **Community context**: Compared to other Upper Basin rivers, the San Juan River supports a **higher relative abundance of native fish species** and a **lower presence of exotic/non-native fish species**, an ecologically favorable condition for native fish recovery.
- **Thermal regime**: As the southernmost tributary in the Upper Basin, the San Juan River's warmer water temperature characteristics are believed to favor **long-term growth** of Colorado Squawfish.
- **Threats**: Populations remain vulnerable to **oil spills**, **chemical fish eradication events**, and other catastrophic disturbances (cited precedents in the [[Yampa River]] and [[West Water Canyon]]).
- **Genetic considerations**: The San Juan River population, despite low numbers, may represent a **distinct genetic stock** isolated within its own subbasin, elevating its conservation value.
- **Comparative subbasin status**: Populations in the **Colorado subbasin** appear depressed, and the **Green River** population status is described as "not secure," reinforcing the relative importance of San Juan River habitat to overall species recovery.

## Key Findings

1. **New data supports San Juan River inclusion**: The Recovery Team concluded there is sufficient new biological and hydrological evidence to consider the San Juan River a viable recovery area for [[Colorado Squawfish]].
2. **Proposed downlisting criteria (Option 1 — mandatory)**: Self-sustaining populations required in **all three** areas — Green River Basin, Colorado River (Lake Powell to Palisade, CO), and San Juan River (Lake Powell to RM 183).
3. **Proposed downlisting criteria (Option 2 — either/or)**: Requires Green River Basin recovery plus **either** the Colorado River **or** the San Juan River, allowing flexibility if recovery proves difficult in one area.
4. **Unanimous delisting criterion**: Regardless of downlisting pathway chosen, **all three river systems** must show recovery for full delisting consideration.
5. **New delisting criterion proposed**: Reestablishment of a population in either the **Salt River** (Roosevelt Lake diversion dam to Apache Falls, 70 river miles) or the **Verde River** (Horseshoe Reservoir to Paulden, AZ, 150 river miles), with feasibility to be reevaluated after 1995 stocking programs conclude.
6. **White River / Taylor Draw Dam issue**: Recommendation that either scientific data confirm habitat loss above Taylor Draw Dam won't impair species recovery, or upstream/downstream fish passage be restored at the dam.
7. **Management recommendations**:
   - Develop a **comprehensive interagency management plan** for San Juan River recovery activities.
   - Extend existing Upper Basin protection measures to the San Juan River population.
   - Coordinate San Juan River recovery efforts with the **Lower Colorado River Basin** recovery activities and the broader [[Recovery Implementation Program]].
   - Evaluate potential **reoperation of Navajo Dam and Reservoir** to support flow regimes beneficial to squawfish recovery.
8. **Political/procedural note**: Arizona and New Mexico state representatives were absent from the vote but were anticipated to favor the stricter (mandatory three-area) downlisting criterion.
