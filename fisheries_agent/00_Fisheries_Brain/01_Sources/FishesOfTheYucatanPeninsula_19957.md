# Fishes of the Yucatan Peninsula

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae #pimelodidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This classic ichthyological report by Carl L. Hubbs (Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan) describes the fishes collected during the 1932 Yucatan biological explorations led by A. S. Pearse, together with older and aquarium-trade material. It treats both the shore/estuarine fauna and the fresh-water fauna of [[Yucatan Peninsula]] cenotes, caves, aguadas and cienagas. The author erects several new taxa, analyses the zoogeographic links to Florida and Middle America, and argues from the fish distributions that the northern cenote fauna is young and was repopulated after Pleistocene inundation. Fish of the [[Rhamdia Guatemalensis]] complex, which dominate the cenotes, are examined in detail.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Fishes of the Yucatan Peninsula, with 45 numbered collecting stations grouped under ten regional headings (Valladolid, Chichen Itza and Piste, Yuncu and Mukuyche, Merida, Motul, Miramar Spring, Progreso cienagas, Rio Champoton and Gulf of Campeche, Champoton aguadas, and Tuxpena in interior Campeche).
- **Sources:** 1932 expedition material (Carnegie Institution, Duke University, University of Michigan); older cienaga and cenote collections reexamined at the [[Smithsonian National Museum]] and the [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]]; aquarium-trade specimens from Progreso; and a Tuxpena aguada collection by C. L. Lundell.
- **Novelty count:** The shore and estuarine fauna comprised 27 genera, 29 species and 4 subspecies, of which 1 genus, 3 species and 3 subspecies were new. The fresh-water fauna comprised 11 genera, 18 species and 19 subspecies, of which 1 genus, 4 species and 12 subspecies were newly described.
- **Nomenclature:** Two new genera (*Garmanella* and *Allophallus*) and eight new species are proposed, plus 18 new subspecies in total as listed by the author.

Among the new taxa are *Fundulus grandissimus*, *Rivulus myersi*, *Garmanella pulchra*, *Menidia colei*, *Allophallus kidderi*, and *Cichlasoma heterospilum*, *C. champotonis* and *Herichthys pearsei*, along with a series of cichlid subspecies of *Cichlasoma urophthalmus* (e.g., *C. u. cienagae*, *C. u. mayorum*, *C. u. zebra*, *C. u. conchitae*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Coastal and estuarine waters:** Sandy northern and western coasts host distinctive forms. The author notes that the cienaga–Rio Lagarto–La Laguna band runs more than 300 km along the northern shore, is largely mangrove swamp, is fed by springs, and has few connections to the sea, so its waters are brackish and isolated.
- **Cenotes and caves:** The interior fresh waters are limestone sinkholes (open cenotes, roofed caverns) and aguadas. The author states the northern limestone plain has no stream systems or lakes of consequence, so fresh water is confined to coastal springs, tidal streams and cenotes.
- **Water chemistry examples:** Recorded salinities range from fresh (Miramar Spring, "absolutely fresh and clear") to brackish (Station 33 at 2.8 ppt; Station 34 at 8.4 ppt; Station 36 at 19.7 ppt). Temperatures in cenotes were mostly 23–27 °C, with some shallow pools near 29–32 °C.
- **Biogeography:** Fish records indicate affinities with Florida (e.g., *Chriodorus atherinoides*, *Floridichthys carpio*, *Garmanella pulchra* linked to *Jordanella floridae*) and with Middle America. Fresh-water fishes show Middle American affinity, not Nearctic.
- **Blind-fish question:** No blind cave fishes were found, consistent with the young age of the northern fauna. Incipient cave modifications (depigmentation, reduced eyes) appear in *Rhamdia* from running-water caves. Blind crustaceans, by contrast, are more advanced.
- **Human-mediated introduction:** Several cenote populations (e.g., *Mollienisia sphenops altissima*, *Cichlasoma meeki*, *C. octofasciatum*) were apparently planted by the International Health Board for mosquito control.

## Key Findings

1. **Endemism is high, especially in subspecies.** The outer (northern) peninsula fresh-water fauna showed 33% endemic species and 100% endemic subspecies (13 subspecies), against 0% species and 17% subspecies for the basal part (Table 3). Overall new-taxon percentages were 9% genera, 22% species and 63% subspecies for fresh-water fishes.
2. **Florida–Yucatan link in marine/inshore fishes.** Four shared forms (*Chriodorus atherinoides*, *Floridichthys carpio* subspecies, *Garmanella pulchra*, *Menidia colei*) suggest a connection, probably via a low sea-level period when the Campeche Bank approached the Florida bank. The author regards a true land bridge as less likely than a close approximation of the peninsulas.
3. **Middle American fresh-water origins.** Fresh-water fish faunas of Yucatan are of Middle American affinity; Yucatan forms were derived by dispersal through the base of the peninsula, with an estimated 16 to 18 separate immigrations for the whole peninsula.
4. **Young, isolated cenote populations.** The author infers that the northern fauna was repopulated after Pleistocene inundation, so speciation there is recent. Cenote populations are distinct, with little free interconnection.
5. **Rhamdia subspecies.** Within the *Rhamdia guatemalensis* complex, six subspecies are recognized (*oaxacae*, *muriei*, *depressa*, *sacrificii*, *decolor*, *stygaea*). Gill-raker number, barbel length and character indices separate the cenote and cave forms; the cave forms show only incipient cave adaptation.
6. **Mollienisia and Belonesox.** The author treats *Mollienisia sphenops altissima* as a deep-bodied peninsular subspecies, and *Belonesox belizanus maxillosus* as a robust-jawed subspecies, and records a sphenops × velifera hybrid from Miramar Spring.
7. **Taxonomic corrections.** Several older Progreso records were reidentified (e.g., *Garmanella pulchra* was earlier named *Jordanella floridae*; *Mollienisia* records assigned to *M. velifera* rather than *M. latipinna*). *Gambusia yucatana* is restored as a valid species distinct from *G. nicaraguensis*.
8. **Coastal records.** New or noteworthy records include the moray *Gymnothorax ocellatus saxicola* (first for the Yucatan fauna), *Micropogon furnieri* (not previously taken on the peninsula), and *Hypsoblennius* extending the Atlantic range southward from Texas.
