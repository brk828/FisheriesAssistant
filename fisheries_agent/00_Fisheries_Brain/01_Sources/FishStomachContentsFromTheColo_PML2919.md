# Fish Stomach Contents from the 1939 Colorado River Expedition (Appendix C)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is Appendix C of a report on fish stomach contents collected during the [[Colorado River]] expedition of 1939, analyzed by [[Donald B. McDonald]]. It lists the gut contents of fish taken at five mainstem canyon sites and two tributary sites in July of that year. Most stomachs held amorphous (highly digested) matter, but the record also shows aquatic insect larvae and nymphs, small fish, algae and human food scraps. Several specimens were empty.

## Background & Description

The appendix belongs to a study authored in part by [[Paul C. Marsh]], whose name appears on the cover page. The fish were collected by backpacking and boat crews along the river, and each entry gives the species, number of specimens, length range, the stomach condition (full, partly full, empty) and the food items found. Pages 3 and 4 contain only a garbled bibliography and fragments of unrelated text, so the main content is the stomach-content table on pages 1 and 2.

The species recorded are:

- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio carpio*), 3 specimens at White Canyon-area mainstem and Ticaboo Creek.
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), 2 specimens at Red Canyon.
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), the most frequently sampled species, at several mainstem and pond sites.
- [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), 5 specimens from Warm Spring Creek (tributary).
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), 2 specimens (6 in and 7 in) from a tributary pond.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampling covers a sequence of river miles along the [[Colorado River]], with mainstem sites from about mile 98 to mile 162.6 and tributary sites at mile 136.6 and mile 69.6. Stomachs at mainstem sites were dominated by amorphous matter. Insect groups identified include [[Mayfly]] nymphs (Heptageniidae), [[Caddisfly]] larvae (Trichoptera, including Hydropsychidae and Helicopsyche), [[Chironomid]] larvae, [[Dobsonfly]] larvae (Corydalidae) and grasshoppers. Tributary and pond fish fed more on invertebrates and small fish. The presence of bread, potato peelings, dried apricot and egg shells in catfish stomachs suggests scavenging near camps or human activity.

## Key Findings

- **Mainstem catfish diet:** Channel catfish (9 to 12 in) at several canyon sites had mostly amorphous matter, with mayfly nymphs, chironomid larvae, caddisfly larvae, dobsonfly larvae, fish tissue and one grasshopper. Human food items (bread or dough, potato peelings, apricot) were found in a few specimens.
- **Empty stomachs:** Several specimens were empty or nearly empty, notably 1 of 3 carp, 1 of 2 flannelmouth suckers, and some channel catfish (6 specimens at Cottonwood Canyon, 9 in, were largely empty).
- **Carp:** Two carp (8 and 9 in) at Ticaboo Creek were empty or nearly empty; the flannelmouth suckers held amorphous matter, with one specimen containing some algae.
- **Green sunfish (Warm Spring Creek):** All 5 specimens (4 to 5 in) had full or partly full stomachs. Contents were mostly caddisfly larvae (8 larvae, including Philopotamidae and Helicopsyche), along with dipterans, insect wings and one small fish.
- **Pond predators:** Channer catfish (10 to 25 in) from ponds near a mouth fed on cyprinids, grasshoppers, parts of carp (4 to 5 in) and unidentified large insects. Largemouth bass (6 and 7 in) had eaten dace and a cyprinid.
- **Overall pattern:** Fish in the main channel relied on detritus and benthic invertebrates, whereas fish in tributaries and ponds were more predatory on insect and fish prey.
