## Executive Summary

This document, authored by Owen T. Gorman (USFWS Pinetop Fishery Assistance Office, 1991), is a management and methodology protocol rather than a primary morphological or ecological study. It establishes proposed low-impact sampling guidelines for USFWS-Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Phase II research targeting the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] population in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR). The protocols govern hoopnet and minnow trap deployment frequency, soak time, and spatial distribution to minimize capture-related stress, predation, and behavioral disturbance to the resident chub population. The paper also addresses policy on exotic species (notably [[Common Carp]] and [[Channel Catfish]]) and prohibits baiting of nets to preserve unbiased habitat-use data.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphological descriptions (e.g., meristics, body shape, coloration) of the Humpback Chub or associated exotic species. It is a sampling protocol document; morphological data are not within its scope. Notable taxa mentioned include:

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — Federally listed endangered cyprinid; the target species of protection.
- **[[Common Carp]]** — exotic species, potentially present in the LCR for up to 100 years.
- **[[Channel Catfish]]** — exotic species; noted as a predator of chub, particularly under baited or prolonged hoopnet-soak conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution**: The resident LCR Humpback Chub population occupies a limited range of approximately **14 km** within the Little Colorado River.
- **Population size**: Resident population estimated in the **thousands of fish**; mainstem Colorado River population size is unknown.
- **Migratory behavior**: Adult chubs from the mainstem Colorado River migrate into and ascend the LCR to **spawn during spring months** (March–June).
- **Diel habitat use**: Chubs exhibit detectable diel (day/night) patterns of habitat use, which the 12-hour net-run protocol is specifically designed to capture within the first 24 hours of sampling.
- **Predation pressure**: [[Channel Catfish]] pose a predation risk to captive chubs in hoopnets, especially with extended soak times or baited traps.
- **Exotic species interactions**: [[Common Carp]] and [[Channel Catfish]] have coexisted with Humpback Chub in the LCR for up to a century without causing extinction, suggesting an established, if imperfectly understood, community-level equilibrium. Researchers emphasize the ecological importance of studying — rather than eradicating — exotic species interactions to properly understand net effects on the [[Humpback Chub]] population.
- **Sampling-induced bias risk**: Repeated capture may induce **learned avoidance behavior** in chubs toward hoopnets, biasing habitat-use and recapture-rate data.

## Key Findings

- **Hoopnet Protocol**: Nets set/soaked/run every **12 hours for the first 24 hours**, then every **24 hours** thereafter at the same location.
- **Site Rotation**: Nets not to be run at the same location for more than **3 consecutive days**, and not resampled more often than **every 4 weeks**.
- **Spatial Definition**: Nets within **100 meters** of one another are considered the same sampling location (same sub-population).
- **Spring Exception (March–June)**: During peak spawning migration, nets may be set every **3 weeks** (vs. 4) and left for up to **4 days** (vs. 3); researchers may compensate for lower catch rates by deploying more nets within the same 100 m radius.
- **Absence Exception**: When USFWS personnel are not present to assess habitat, nets may soak for a full **24 hours** between runs regardless of timing restrictions.
- **Minnow Trap Protocol**: Grids of **8–30 traps** covering **100–500 m²**, sampled no more than **3 days/month**; grids must be spaced at least **10 m** apart.
- **Rationale for Limits**: Protocols aim to cap chub captivity to **no more than 3 days per 4-week period**, balancing scientific data needs (diel activity, habitat use) against cumulative stress, handling trauma, and predation risk.
- **No Baiting Policy**: USFWS explicitly recommends **against baiting hoopnets** — baiting increases catfish-driven predation on chub, creates unnatural food-reward associations, attracts fish from wider areas, and biases habitat-use conclusions. Large chub catches have been achieved historically without bait.
- **Exotic Species Policy**: GCES research should **not function as a de facto eradication program** for exotic species; ad hoc removal is discouraged since (a) it would be reversed by recolonization from the mainstem Colorado River, and (b) it interferes with understanding exotic-native species ecological interactions. USFWS recommends **permitting/limiting exotic species take** and **PIT-tagging adult exotics** to study their movement and distribution in the LCR.
- **Underlying Conservation Goal**: All protocols are structured around minimizing capture/recapture frequency of the federally endangered [[Humpback Chub]] while still enabling USFWS to characterize diel and seasonal habitat-use patterns through coordinated, non-duplicative research efforts among [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) researchers.
