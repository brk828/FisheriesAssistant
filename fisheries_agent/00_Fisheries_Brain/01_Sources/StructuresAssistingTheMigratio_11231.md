# Structures Assisting the Migrations of Non-Salmonid Fish: USSR

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae #cyprinidae #clupeidae #percidae

## Executive Summary

This FAO Fisheries Technical Paper (No. 308, 1989, 97 pp.) by D.S. Pavlov reviews how the Soviet Union has tried to keep non-salmonid fish migrating past dams, reservoirs and water abstraction works. The focus is on [[Sturgeon]] (Acipenseridae), Clupeidae, Cyprinidae, Percidae and Siluridae in rivers draining into the [[Caspian Sea]], [[Azov Sea]] and [[Black Sea]], particularly the [[Volga River]], [[Don River]] and [[Kuban River]]. The paper covers two linked problems: (1) upstream spawning migrations blocked by dams, and (2) downstream drift of young fish lost in water intakes. It describes fish-passes and fish-lifts, fish-guiding devices, and a wide range of protective screens and behavioural barriers, and it draws biological principles from fish behaviour, swimming performance and hydraulics.

## Background & Description

- **Scope and authorship:** Prepared by a consultant at the Severtsov Institute of Evolutionary Morphology and Ecology of Animals (Moscow) after a 1984 recommendation of the FAO Working Party of Experts on Inland Fisheries of the Indo-Pacific region. Technical editing was by R. Hughes, J. Muir and T. Petr. A companion Latin American review by R. Quiros appeared as COPESCAL Technical Document 5.
- **Pressures on rivers:** Annual water consumption in the USSR reached 350 km³, with about 30,000 water abstraction devices. Reservoirs covered about 10 million ha, roughly a quarter of the area of all natural USSR lakes, and dams regulated a further 6 million ha. Stocks in the Aral, Azov and Caspian basins declined sharply, and several species are listed in the USSR Red Book.
- **Stocking:** Hatcheries released about 11 × 10⁹ young fish annually. In the Volga–Caspian Basin, roughly 90 million young Acipenseridae, 18 million Caspian inconnu and 0.6 million Kura salmon were stocked each year (Nikonorov, 1984). The author argues that replacing natural reproduction with stocking would impoverish the gene pool, so migratory paths must be preserved.
- **Species of concern:** Acipenseridae, Anguillidae, Clupeidae, Coregonidae, Cyprinidae, Percidae and Siluridae, with the paper concentrating on Acipenseridae, Clupeidae, Cyprinidae, Percidae and Siluridae.
- **Literature:** About 400 works on the topic had been published since the adverse effects of regulation were recognised.
- **Structure:** Fish-pass types are described in [[Fish-Pass]] and [[Fish-Lift]] sections; protection methods in [[Fish Protection]] sections; the conclusion is a synthesis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Behaviour and rheoreaction.** Fish orient against currents through optical, tactile, lateral-line and labyrinth organs. Threshold current velocity (V_thr) ranged from 1–30 cm/s, and critical velocity (V_cr) was 2–3 times lower for bottom dwellers than for pelagic species. Pelagic species rely on vision and bottom species on touch. Migrants swim at cruising speed; bottom sturgeon swim 0.5–1 body lengths/s and pelagic fish 3–4 body lengths/s.

**Migration patterns.** Pelagic and visually oriented species (shad, Atlantic salmon, bream, vobla) migrate by day or twilight. Tactile species (sheatfish, zander) migrate at night near the banks, while stellate sturgeon show a 24-hour pattern with maximum night movement. Active upstream migration alternates with active-passive downstream drift.

**Effects of regulation.** Regulation can eliminate or sharply reduce migratory populations. Examples include Stenodus leucichthys and giant sturgeon (Huso huso) in the Volga, and Aral barbel (Barbus brachycephalus) and bastard sturgeon (Acipenser nudiventris) in the Aral and Lake Balkhash basins. During initial filling of the Tsimlyanskoye Reservoir, the Don's current fell to 0.1 m/s, and sturgeon began spawning in the Severtskiy Donets tributary (current 1.5–2 m/s), where they had not previously bred. Seasonal redistribution of flows also prevents floodplain inundation needed for spawning.

**Drift and early life stages.** Sturgeon pre-larvae drift pelagically, though sterlet keep bottom contact. Only about 70% of Acipenser gueldenstaedtii, 50% of A. stellatus and 16% of Huso huso keep close to the bottom. Downstream migrants in the Ili River dropped from 19 species above the Kapchagayskoye Reservoir to one species below it.

**Water intakes.** Water intakes take young fish in large numbers. About 150 km³ of water is used annually for irrigation. In the Volga delta alone about 14 × 10⁹ young food fish are lost each year. Most entrapment occurs at night, with 60–97% of young fish entering in darkness in clear water. Up to 80% of juveniles in a river may be delayed near bank intakes. Artificial night lighting at intakes concentrates fish and increases losses.

**Other stressors.** Navigation (especially water-jet vessels), high-voltage power lines (which delay spawners), pollution and canalisation all reduce migration success.

## Key Findings

**Fish-passage structures**
- Fish-passes in the USSR were designed for dams below 10 m (pool-and-weir) or up to 40 m (pool-and-orifice). Mechanical and hydraulic fish-lifts can be used at any height.
- Three hydraulic fish-lifts were built: Tsimlyanskiy (Don, 1955), Volgogradskiy (Volga, 1961) and Volkhovskiy (Volkhov, 1967).
- Mechanical fish-lifts were built at Saratovskiy (Volga, 1969; up to 1 million fish transferred annually) and Krasnodarskiy (Kuban'; about 1 million fish annually).
- Sluice fish-passes operate at Kochetovskiy (Don), Nikolaevskiy and Konstantinovskiy (Don), the Volga flow-divider, and Fedorovskiy (Kuban'). At Kochetovskiy, up to 67% of Acipenseridae and 44% of other fish successfully negotiated the fishway.
- Floating mobile collectors include the Kochetovskiy barge and the Rizhskiy complex on the Daugava. The truck can carry fish for 500 km.
- More than twenty pass designs exist in the USSR. Only the Nizhne-Tulomskiy salmon pass from 1914–1965 remains operational from older designs.

**Biological design principles**
- Fish accumulate under dams, particularly at spillways and in whirlpools, and "search zones" form at the dam face. The inlet should sit at the outer boundary of this zone.
- Attracting flow velocities are generally 0.6–0.8 of critical velocity: 0.7–0.9 m/s for Acipenseridae, 0.9–1.2 m/s for Salmonidae, and 0.7–1.0 m/s for semi-anadromous species.
- The attracting plume must exceed the threshold velocity by about 0.15–0.2 m/s relative to the main flow.
- Fish-pass entrances should be parallel to the main flow or within 30° of it. Greater angles extinguish the attracting plume.
- In collectors, flow velocities are set 20–30% below critical velocity to retain fish when lock gates close.
- Lockage times are usually about 2 hours. Crowding screens are essential: without them, the number of fish entering the Volgogradskiy lift was insignificant.

**Downstream fish protection**
- Protection is organised on three principles: ecological (siting and timing), behavioural (light, electric, hydraulic and tactile-hydraulic stimuli) and physical (screens, circulatory separation, air-lifts).
- Ecological measures include siting intakes away from littoral and migratory paths, and at depth. Moving an intake head to 6 m depth in the Volgogradskaya Reservoir cut young-fish capture by a factor of 200. Diurnal regulation (abstraction only at night, or using accumulation basins) is widely practised.
- Light can recover visual orientation in young Cyprinidae and Percidae, with 84–91% protection reported for fish over 14 mm. Electrical barriers (ERZU-1) were found to be of little practical use.
- Screens are effective only for larger fish. Perpendicular screens are best suited to fish over 50 cm. Screens with diversion channels are most effective, with some vertical screens reaching 97–100% protection for young Salmonidae under 30 mm.
- Cone and three-dimensional screens are considered the best option, followed by rotating belt screens; flat screens have the greatest limitations.
- Diversion efficiency of 85–100% is achievable when the hydraulic indices are set within the stated limits (K_ne ≈ 0.88, K_div ≈ 2.74 for 98.2% efficiency, drift duration 4–18 s).
- Protection devices are ineffective for fish under about 10–12 mm; ecological siting remains the only practical option for the earliest stages.

**Conclusions and strategy**
- Fish-passes and protective structures in the USSR are considered effective, but the author notes that field data are too scarce to compare devices reliably.
- Priority actions include conserving spawning grounds (artificial spawning grounds of pebbles and panels have been built on the Volga and Kuban'), preserving migration routes, locating new dams away from lower river reaches, and studying downstream migration across systems.
- Pelagic species in reservoirs have the highest migratory coefficients (K = 0.15–0.24), and stocking them has generally failed. Reservoirs with low water turnover (1.0–2.6 times a year) retard drift most.
- Suggested alternatives to restoring full migration include making reservoir throughflow resemble natural lakes, preventing drift with protective devices, and stocking large fingerlings (10–15 cm) of pelagic species.
