# On New Species of Fishes from Colombia, Ecuador, and Brazil

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Characidae #taxonomy #neotropical

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic contribution from the [[Zoological Laboratory of Indiana University]] describes four new species of characid fishes from South American freshwater systems. [[Carl H. Eigenmann]] and [[Arthur Henn]] present detailed morphological descriptions of [[Rhoadsia minor]] from high-elevation streams in [[Ecuador]], [[Hemigrammus barrigonae]] and [[Hyphessobrycon metae]] from the [[Rio Meta]] in [[Colombia]], and [[Bryconamericus novae]] from the [[Rio Tocantins]] basin in [[Brazil]]. Each species description includes comprehensive meristic data, dentition patterns, coloration notes, and comparative analyses with related taxa.

## Background & Description

**[[Rhoadsia minor]]** (Type 95mm, I.U.M. 13419): Collected from [[Rio Blanco]] of the [[Rio Esmeraldas]] system at 4,000 feet elevation in [[Mindo]], Province of [[Pichincha]], [[Ecuador]]. Head 3.8; depth 2.8-3; D. 11; A. 28-30; scales 9-15 + 25-8; eye 1.2-1.4 in snout. Gill rakers 10 + 14. Slender and compressed with oblique profile. Predorsal area narrow without median scales. Occipital process elongate with five or six lateral scales. Dorsal placed slightly anterior to middle of entire length. Lateral line developed on first 14-15 scales. Dentition highly modified with development: maxillary in young without teeth, bearing five- to six-pointed incisors on premaxillary. Mature specimens have elongate maxillary reaching below posterior orbit margin, with 13 conical spike-like incisors plus two tricuspid incisors. Premaxillary teeth in two series; mandible with ten teeth per ramus. Coloration brilliant in life with anal combining bright red and yellow, dorsal paler yellow, oblong or deltoid black spot mid-laterally below last dorsal rays. Young specimens possess circular caudal base spot becoming obsolete by 40mm. Humeral spot behind operculum followed by vertical bar. Upper opercle silvery white, lower part deep black. Locally known as "doradillo" (little gilded one).

**[[Hemigrammus barrigonae]]** (Type 41mm, I.U.M. 13423): 65 paratypes 25-43mm from [[Barrigona]], [[Rio Meta]], collected by Manuel Gonzales. Head 4; depth 2.6-3; caudal peduncle depth 9-10; D. 11; A. 24-25. Scales 33-35 longitudinally, about 11 transversely. Lateral line pore development extremely variable, not equal on both sides of same specimen. Usually continuous series of at least 14 pored scales, then discontinuous. Lateral line on seventh transverse scale series. Eye 2.6 in head, equal to or slightly greater than interorbital. Compressed and rather deep with slender caudal peduncle. Predorsal area with variable median series of 9-12 scales (usually 10). Occipital process about 5 in distance to dorsal, bordered by three scales. Premaxillary with three tricuspid outer row teeth and five five- to six-pointed inner row teeth. Maxillary with four or five minute six-pointed teeth. Mandible with four or five six-pointed incisors per ramus. Gill-rakers 7+6. Dorsal origin equidistant from snout and middle caudal base. Anal origin at vertical from last dorsal ray. General coloration light olivaceous with bright silvery operculum, cheeks and snout. Broad horizontal lateral stripe from operculum to caudal base, expanded to ill-defined oval caudal spot. Indistinct humeral spot overlaid by lateral stripe. Conspicuous dark band along entire anal base.

**[[Hyphessobrycon metae]]** (Type 34mm, I.U.M. 13421): 66 paratypes 16-35mm from [[Barrigona]], [[Rio Meta]], collected by Manuel Gonzales. Head 3.5-4, equal to depth at dorsal origin; D. 11; A. 19-23. Scales about 30 longitudinally, 10 transversely; pores on 6 (rarely 7) scales; 9-10 scales between occipital and dorsal. Eye 2.3-2.6 in head, about equal to interorbital. Caudal peduncle equal to eye and 1.2 in its own depth. Slender and compressed. Predorsal area with median series of 9-10 scales; preventral area with median series of small (6) scales. Occipital process 6 in distance to dorsal, bordered by 2-3 scales. Fontanels very large. Maxillary five-sixths eye length. Premaxillary with three or four narrow tricuspid or broadly conical outer row teeth, five three- to four-pointed second series teeth, inner series of very small tricuspids or broadly conical teeth. Maxillary with two small tricuspids and one conical tooth. Mandible with four strong three- to five-pointed incisors per ramus followed by about seven minute conical or recurved lateral teeth. Gill rakers 6+5, short and strong. Dorsal origin equidistant from snout and middle caudal base, height 3.2-3.5 in length. Obscure dusky humeral spot. Operculum with dusky vertical semi-lunar area. Top of head deep black extending as line from occiput to dorsal. Narrow intense lateral stripe from upper operculum to below last dorsal rays, broadening to heavy black band on caudal peduncle and oblong caudal spot produced as triangle to middle caudal ray tips. Caudal lobe bases evidently tinged deep red in life.

**[[Bryconamericus novae]]** (Type 56mm, C.M. 3568a): 9 paratypes 30-57mm from below [[Cachoeira da Velha]] near [[Piabana]], [[Rio Novo]] of [[Rio Tocantins]], [[Lower Amazon]] basin, collected by J.D. Haseman. Allied to [[Bryconamericus hyphessus]] but less elongate with more arched dorsal profile and additional scale row below lateral line. Possibly identical with [[Bryconamericus diaphanus]]. Head 4; depth 3.2-3.6; D. 10; A. 18-20; scales 4-36-3; eye 2.4, slightly greater than interorbital. Gill rakers 6+5, upper limb minute. Slender and compressed with equally but slightly arched profiles. Preventral area without regular median scales; postanal area with 6 median scales; predorsal area with median series of about 10 scales. Occipital process short, one-seventh distance to dorsal, bordered by two lateral scales. Interorbital flattened or slightly convex. Frontal fontanel triangular, as wide as parietal and two-thirds as long. Maxillary slender, extending to suture between first and second suborbitals, four-fifths eye length. Maxillary with two or three minute tricuspid teeth at inner angle. Three to six conically tricuspid teeth in premaxillary outer series (variable number). Inner premaxillary series of four three- or four-pointed teeth. Mandible with four large five-pointed teeth and variable number of small lateral tricuspids. Scales cycloid, regularly imbricate. Anal sheath single row of bifid scales. Fourth lateral series horizontal, large scales with vertical height about three-fourths eye. Lateral line decurved, parallel with row below. Straw colored with darker head top but no predorsal line. Broad postdorsal color band to caudal base. Bright silvery cheeks and operculum. Broad horizontal silvery lateral stripe just above lateral line, terminating in dusky caudal stripe or spot. Faint vertically elongate humeral spot under third and fourth lateral line scales.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Rhoadsia minor]] inhabits small crystalline brooks at high elevation (4,136 feet) in the extremely humid sub-tropical forests of the western folds of [[Mt. Pichincha]], draining to the [[Rio Esmeraldas]]. This represents a dwarf mountain form occurring above 4,000 feet, contrasting with its relative [[Rhoadsia altipinna]] from the [[Chimbo-Chan Chan]] basin of the [[Guayas]] system, which occurs at lower elevations (700-800 feet) and attains larger size. The species was also reported from the hot dry valley of the [[Chota]] in northern [[Ecuador]] near the irrigated sugar plantation "Cabuyal" at approximately 4,900 feet elevation.

[[Hemigrammus barrigonae]] and [[Hyphessobrycon metae]] both occur in the [[Rio Meta]] system of [[Colombia]], representing additions to the [[Orinoco]] basin ichthyofauna.

[[Bryconamericus novae]] inhabits areas below waterfalls ([[Cachoeira da Velha]]) in the [[Rio Novo]], a tributary of the [[Rio Tocantins]] in the [[Lower Amazon]] basin of [[Brazil]].

## Key Findings

1. **[[Rhoadsia minor]]** is distinguished from [[Rhoadsia altipinna]] by lesser depth, relatively feeble dentition, more forward dorsal position, and smaller size. Mature at 95mm versus 140mm for R. altipinna. Maxillary development corresponds at all stages: at 40mm R. minor maxillary extends below vertical from anterior orbit corner, while R. altipinna maxillary extends only slightly beyond vertical from posterior nares. At 56mm R. minor maxillary reaches vertical from anterior third of eye; R. altipinna reaches vertical from anterior orbit corner. At maturity (95mm) R. minor maxillary reaches posterior orbit margin; R. altipinna at this size has maxillary reaching nearly to suture between first and second suborbitals. Scale counts show scarcely any difference between species.

2. **[[Hemigrammus barrigonae]]** exhibits extreme variability in lateral line pore development, not even equal on both sides of individual specimens. Examples include: one specimen with 16+3+1+11+2 pattern, another with 17+6+2+4+2+3, a 41mm specimen with 17+14+2 on left and 16+18+1 on right, a 34mm specimen with 24+8+3 and 20+8+7, a 37mm specimen with 15+19 and 16+18, and a 42mm specimen with 24+5+5 on left and 24+7+3 on right. This represents unusual bilateral asymmetry in lateral line development.

3. **[[Hyphessobrycon metae]]** shows variation in anal ray counts: A. 19-23, with frequency distribution of 19₁, 20₁₁, 21₂₂, 22₂₈, 23₄ (denominators represent number of individuals). The species exhibits distinctive coloration with deep black head top extending as line to dorsal, narrow intense lateral stripe broadening to heavy black caudal peduncle band, and oblong caudal spot produced as triangle to middle caudal ray tips.

4. **[[Bryconamericus novae]]** shows variable premaxillary tooth counts: three to six conically tricuspid teeth in outer series, with asymmetry possible (four on one ramus and five on other, or as in type, six on right and three on left). The species is allied to [[Bryconamericus hyphessus]] but differs in being less elongate with more arched dorsal profile and having an additional scale row below lateral line. Authors note possible identity with [[Bryconamericus diaphanus]] (Cope).

5. Dental modifications in [[Rhoadsia minor]] are ontogenetic: young specimens lack maxillary teeth entirely, while mature specimens develop elongate maxillary with 13 conical spike-like incisors plus two tricuspid incisors. Premaxillary develops two distinct series: outer series of one or two conical cusps projecting downward and outward without mandibular opponents, and inner series of five tricuspid incisors interlocking with mandibular incisors.
