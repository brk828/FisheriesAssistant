# Rucker Canyon Fisheries Survey (Cochise County, Arizona, 1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A single-day seine survey of [[Rucker Canyon]], Cochise County, Arizona, was conducted on July 30, 1997 by Arizona State University researchers Brian Bagley and Patti Sowka. Two stream reaches were sampled and three [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] species were recorded: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*), and [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*). Longfin Dace was the most abundant species at both sites. The survey also noted that [[Rucker Canyon Lake]] had been reduced to a braided channel by heavy siltation following a fire several years earlier.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Rucker Canyon is a stream system in southeastern Arizona. The survey records two conditions that affected the sampled habitat:

- **Post-fire siltation:** The field note states that [[Rucker Canyon Lake]] "remains nothing more than a braided channel" because of heavy siltation after a fire "several years ago." This points to lasting watershed disturbance following wildfire, although the document does not quantify sediment load or describe its effects on fish.
- **Timing of sampling:** The second site was sampled "just before heavy rains came," so the survey was cut short by weather. This may have limited the number of fish captured at that site, though the document does not say so directly.

The sampled reaches were the Red Rock Canyon road turnoff (upstream) and the second road crossing downstream of Rucker Canyon Lake. The contrast between these sites is the main spatial comparison the document allows.
