## Executive Summary

This document is a revised taxonomic **checklist of Texas fresh-water fishes** compiled by Clark Hubbs (1958), updating an earlier 1957 checklist. It enumerates **209 numbered species** across dozens of families, ranging from primitive lampreys ([[Petromyzontidae]]) and cartilaginous elasmobranchs that stray into coastal fresh waters, to gars, catfishes, minnows, sunfishes, darters, and euryhaline marine species that penetrate coastal streams. Rather than presenting new morphological or ecological research, the checklist's primary contribution is a **statewide distributional framework**, assigning each species to one or more of **eight modified "game areas"** of Texas (mapped in Map I), with directional qualifiers (N, S, E, W) indicating partial-area occupancy. The work also documents several **nomenclatural revisions** (e.g., *Notropis roseus* → *N. texanus*; *N. deliciosus* → *N. stramineus*; *Etheostoma whipplei*/*E. artesiae* → *E. radiosum*) and formally lists two newly described endemic species, *[[Gambusia geiseri]]* and *[[Gambusia heterochir]]*, plus the addition of *Gambusia senilis* and the marine straggler *Eucinostomus argenteus*.

## Background & Description

As a checklist rather than an anatomical study, morphological detail is limited to **diagnostic common names and taxonomic placement**, but several groups are noted for distinguishing traits:

- **[[Petromyzontidae]]** (lampreys) — jawless, eel-like body plan; *Ichthyomyzon castaneus* and *I. gagei* represent parasitic vs. non-parasitic (brook) lamprey life histories.
- **[[Acipenseridae]]** and **[[Polyodontidae]]** — *Scaphirhynchus platorynchus* (shovelnose sturgeon) and *Polyodon spathula* (paddlefish) represent basal chondrostean morphology (heterocercal tail, reduced ossification).
- **[[Lepisosteidae]]** (gars) — elongate body, needle-like teeth, four Texas species (*Lepisosteus spatula*, *L. platostomus*, *L. productus*, *L. osseus*) differentiated primarily by snout length and body proportions.
- **Blind cave catfishes** — *Trogloglanis pattersoni* (toothless blindcat) and *Satan eurystomus* (widemouth blindcat), both restricted to subterranean artesian waters of San Antonio (SE6), show troglobitic morphology: depigmentation and reduced/absent eyes and dentition, adaptations to a lightless aquifer habitat.
- **[[Cyprinodontidae]]** pupfishes — several undescribed forms (*Cyprinodon* sp., Pecos and Devil's River pupfish) noted as morphologically distinct but formally unnamed at time of publication.
- **[[Percidae]]** darters (*Etheostoma*, *Hadropterus*, *Percina*, *Ammocrypta*) — small, benthic, elongate bodies typical of riffle-dwelling darters, distinguished largely by pigmentation pattern (e.g., "orangethroat," "greenthroat," "redspot," "bluntnose").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The checklist implicitly encodes ecological information through **habitat classification and distributional codes**:

- **Freshwater residents** are assigned to one or more of the **eight Texas game areas**: (1) East Texas timber/blackland prairie, (2) coastal prairie, (3) South Texas brushlands, (4) main blackland prairie, (5) cross timbers/grand prairie, (6) Edwards Plateau, (7) High Plains, (8) Trans-Pecos.
- **Euryhaline/coastal species** (e.g., [[Carcharhinidae]] sharks, [[Dasyatidae]] rays, [[Sciaenidae]] drums, [[Gobiidae]] gobies, [[Mugilidae]] mullets, [[Syngnathidae]] pipefishes) are marked "coastal," reflecting their typical brackish/marine habitat with incursions into Texas coastal streams, following euryhalinity criteria of Gunter (1945).
- **Highly saline specialists**: species inhabiting the [[Pecos River]] (salinity up to 12.5 ppt chloride) are arbitrarily classified as freshwater fishes despite near-marine conditions, highlighting extreme physiological tolerance in taxa such as *Cyprinodon* pupfishes and *Gambusia* species.
- **Spring/artesian endemics**: Several narrow-range endemics are tied to specific spring systems — *[[Gambusia nobilis]]* (Pecos gambusia), *Gambusia gaigei* (Big Bend gambusia), *Gambusia heterochir* (Clear Creek gambusia), *Etheostoma fonticola* (fountain darter, Edwards Plateau springs), and *Cyprinodon bovinus* (Leon Springs pupfish, noted as **extinct** at time of writing) — illustrating the fragility of isolated spring-fed habitats on the [[Edwards Plateau]].
- **Subterranean/artesian niche**: *Trogloglanis pattersoni* and *Satan eurystomus* occupy a unique hypogean ecological niche in the San Antonio artesian aquifer, unusual among North American ichthyofauna.
- **Introduced species**: *Cyprinus carpio* (carp), *Carassius auratus* (goldfish), *Astyanax fasciatus* (banded tetra), and *Ambloplites rupestris* (rock bass) are explicitly marked as introduced to various areas, indicating anthropogenic range expansion.

## Key Findings

- The checklist documents **209 species** spanning marine stragglers, euryhaline estuarine fishes, and strict freshwater/spring endemics, reflecting Texas's unusually high ichthyofaunal diversity due to its transitional position between temperate, subtropical, and arid biogeographic zones.
- **Taxonomic revisions** from Hubbs (1957a) are formally incorporated: *Notropis roseus* → *[[Notropis texanus]]*; *N. deliciosus* → *[[Notropis stramineus]]* (per Suttkus 1958); *Etheostoma whipplei*/*E. artesiae* → *[[Etheostoma radiosum]]*.
- Two species new to science at time of publication are formally named: ***[[Gambusia geiseri]]*** (fountain gambusia, following Hubbs and Springer 1957) and ***[[Gambusia heterochir]]*** (Clear Creek gambusia, following Hubbs 1957b).
- ***[[Gambusia senilis]]*** (blotched gambusia) is added to the Texas/U.S. fauna based on Devil's River collections (Hubbs 1958), marking a new state/national record.
- ***Eucinostomus argenteus*** is added as a marine species collected incidentally in a coastal stream near Brownsville, illustrating the porous boundary between marine and freshwater ichthyofaunal designations in coastal Texas.
- The genus ***[[Gambusia]]*** emerges as a key example of Texas endemic radiation, with at least six species/subspecies (*nobilis*, *geiseri*, *gaigei*, *senilis*, *heterochir*, *affinis*) restricted to isolated desert springs, rivers, or widespread lowland waters.
- ***Cyprinodon bovinus*** (Leon Springs pupfish) is explicitly noted as **extinct**, representing one of the earliest documented extinctions of a North American freshwater fish tied to spring habitat loss/modification in the Trans-Pecos (Area 8).
- The distributional framework based on **eight game areas** provides a reusable biogeographic template for subsequent Texas freshwater fish studies, distinguishing this checklist from a purely taxonomic inventory by emphasizing **species-level range mapping** over broader distributional pattern analysis (cf. Hubbs 1957c).
- Multiple **species pairs/complexes among darters and shiners** (e.g., *Notropis oxyrhynchus*, *N. buccula*, *N. girardi* — all restricted to the upper Brazos/Red River drainages) suggest localized speciation events within Texas river systems, consistent with allopatric divergence in isolated Great Plains-derived stream fragments.
