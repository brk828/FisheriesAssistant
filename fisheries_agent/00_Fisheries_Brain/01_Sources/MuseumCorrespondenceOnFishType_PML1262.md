# Museum Correspondence on Fish Type Specimens and Catalog Reidentification (1976)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #museum-collections #type-specimens

## Executive Summary

This is a 1976 letter from [[Susan J. Karnella]], Museum Specialist in the [[Division of Fishes]] at the [[National Museum of Natural History]], [[Smithsonian Institution]], to [[Robert R. Miller]] at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]]. It concerns the loan and hand-delivery of several fish specimens and [[Type Specimens]] from the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM) collection, and the unresolved naming status of a few catalogued lots. The letter is a museum-administrative document, not a research report. It contains no ecological, habitat or population data. Its main value is in documenting specimen catalog numbers, [[Syntypes]] counts and nomenclatural uncertainty for Gila-group material.

## Background & Description

The letter is dated October 13, 1976, and is written on Smithsonian letterhead. Karnella responds to requests from Miller for several specimens. Her main points are:

- **USNM 143547** is catalogued under a name that the OCR renders illegibly (ending in "orri"). The specimen has undergone several name changes in recent years. Karnella encloses identification labels and asks Miller to report the correct identification when he returns the material, if it differs from the catalogued name.
- **Specimens held for hand-carrying to Ann Arbor** include:
  - a species name garbled by OCR (USNM 246, with 2 syntypes remaining; the sender notes that only one was apparently sent, possibly intentionally);
  - [[Gila Seminuda]] (USNM 16975, 5 syntypes);
  - [[Gila Robusta]] (USNM 15762, 4 specimens);
  - [[Gila Grahami]] (USNM 252, 1 syntype).
- **USNM 252** raises a catalog discrepancy. The card file lists only one specimen of this species, and the letter notes that no jar is present in the type or regular collection. The specimen may have been reidentified and placed elsewhere.

The second page is a continuation dated 1 October 1976. It is largely illegible. It includes a note of thanks, a request for patience ("sorry to be such a bother"), and a reference to a possible December return or visit. It also refers to a Gila robusta collection and a list of other items. Much of the text is too damaged to read reliably.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address habitat, distribution, ecology, water chemistry or any other environmental variable. The Gila taxa are named only as museum material, and no collection localities are legible. Any ecological interpretation would require the original collection records, which are not included.

## Key Findings

- **Catalog discrepancies:** USNM 143547 has an uncertain current identification after several name changes. USNM 252 has a card-file/jar mismatch, suggesting it was reidentified and moved.
- **Type material counts (as legible):** Gila seminuda 5 syntypes (USNM 16975); Gila grahami 1 syntype (USNM 252); a further 2 syntypes of an illegible species (USNM 246), with 1 apparently sent earlier.
- **Non-type material:** Gila robusta, 4 specimens (USNM 15762).
- **Loan logistics:** Several lots were held pending hand-carrying to Ann Arbor, reflecting the period's practice of transporting type material by hand rather than by post.
- **Nomenclatural follow-up:** Miller is asked to return the identification labels with the specimens and to report any corrected identification, particularly for USNM 143547.
