# The Trans Mojave-Sierran Shear Zone and Early Miocene Collapse of Southwestern North America

#tectonics #structural-geology #palaeomagnetism

## Executive Summary

This paper (Dokka, Ross & Lu, 1998) examines the [[Trans Mojave-Sierran Shear Zone]] (TMSSZ), a broad east-west dextral shear zone active between 21 and 18 Ma. The authors argue that the TMSSZ was the kinematic link between the [[Pacific-North American Plate Boundary]] and inland extensional systems. Oblique, transtensional motion on that boundary triggered gravitational collapse of the western edge of the North American plate. The collapsed region covered nearly 10^6 km² and moved 100-150 km southwest.

To test this, the authors built a [[Palinspastic Reconstruction]] of the Mojave Desert that removes the effects of the younger [[Eastern California Shear Zone]]. They used early Miocene markers to measure TMSSZ deformation. The reconstruction indicates that deformation spanned a c. 90 km wide band and that more than 80 km of dextral shear occurred.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** The TMSSZ passes through the southern Sierra Nevada and the Mojave Desert. It linked [[Metamorphic Core Complexes]] and detachment fault terranes in the Mojave, southeastern California, southern Arizona and Sonora, Mexico, with the transtensional plate boundary.
- **Prior work:** Plate tectonic models (e.g. Atwater 1970, 1989; Stock & Molnar 1988; Severinghaus & Atwater 1990) explained coastal Neogene deformation well. Their links to interior extensional terranes were weaker. The TMSSZ was recognised from regional field structural and [[Paleomagnetism]] data, and Dokka & Ross (1995) proposed the collapse model.
- **Three deformation intervals in the Mojave (late Cenozoic):**
  1. N-S opening of the c. 24-21 Ma [[Mojave Extensional Belt]] (MEB).
  2. E-W dextral shearing of the TMSSZ, c. 21-18 Ma.
  3. Right shear of the c. 13-0 Ma Eastern California Shear Zone (ECSZ), which truncated and disrupted the TMSSZ.
- **ECSZ:** It has accommodated 65 km of slip since its inception, within a c. 80 km wide belt. It accounts for between 9% and 23% of total Pacific-North American relative plate motion. It physically connects with the [[San Andreas Fault System]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

This document is a structural geology and tectonics paper. It does not address aquatic ecosystems, fish, invertebrates, plankton, water chemistry or aquatic habitat. The only "ocean" references concern the offshore Pacific plate, used to constrain plate motion, including magnetic anomaly mapping off central California. The environmental context is regional crustal tectonics: lithospheric thinning of 10-17 km (vertical) during early Miocene extension and associated magmatism.

## Key Findings

**1. Methodology and reconstruction**
- The reconstruction follows the protocol of Dokka & Travis (1990a) and Dokka (1993). Its resolution is c. ±1 km.
- Fault blocks were defined from mapped late Cenozoic faults, then restored using fault slip and palaeomagnetic declination constraints. Table 1 lists net slips for roughly 40 ECSZ-related faults, from under 1 km to 27 km. Most entries are right-lateral; a few are left-slip (e.g. the Lane Mountain-related and Garlic Spring values).
- Multi-strand fault systems (e.g. the Camp Rock system) were treated as single faults with composite net slip.

**2. TMSSZ geometry and deformation**
- Restoring markers to the c. 18 Ma configuration shows the TMSSZ was a c. 90 km wide, approximately E-W dextral shear zone.
- At its final stage it was oriented about N82°W. Its original trend was probably ENE (WSW-ENE in the 21 Ma restoration) and it rotated 30-40° clockwise.
- Deformation was asymmetric, with maximum apparent shear south of the zone's centre (Fig. 7). Shear strains decrease gradually toward the outer limits.

**3. Mechanism: clockwise rotation and second-order sinistral shear**
- First-order dextral shear was accomplished largely by clockwise vertical-axis rotation of large blocks.
- Second-order sinistral shear zones reactivated older [[Mojave Extensional Belt]] transfer zones. They produced local counter-clockwise rotations before throughgoing faults formed.
- Key examples:
  - [[Lane Mountain Fault]]: rocks rotated 23-57° counter-clockwise between 22 and 18 Ma (Valentine et al. 1993).
  - [[Kramer Hills]]: rotated c. 80° counter-clockwise before 21 Ma, then c. 45° clockwise at c. 21-18 Ma.
  - [[Baxter Wash Fault]]: >80° local counter-clockwise rotation, with c. 13-25 km of left-slip (Temple 1997).
  - [[Kane Springs Fault]]: megascopic folds tightened by sinistral shear during TMSSZ time.
- Using the relation NS ≈ wθ/180 (net slip, zone width, rotation), the Lane Mountain zone implies >22 km of distributed left-slip.

**4. Net slip estimates**
- Dokka & Ross (1995) estimated 41-66 km of right slip using average vertical-axis rotation. The authors consider this an underestimate by perhaps a factor of two.
- The improved 21 Ma reconstruction gives a minimum dextral net slip of 80 km. Net slip is expected to increase westward because each sinistral zone adds to total right slip.
- The paper suggests the true value may be closer to 150 km. This matches the combined width of the fault blocks between the continental edge and the [[San Andreas Fault System]] in central California (c. 150 km).

**5. Plate tectonic framework**
- Relative Pacific-North American motion in early Miocene time was oblique and transtensional.
- Transform-normal migration since c. 30 Ma is estimated at 340 ± 200 km (Atwater 1989), with as much as c. 175 km between 20 and 16 Ma.
- The Pacific plate off central California shows no sea-floor spreading (Lonsdale 1991), whereas the North American plate is highly extended. Dokka & Ross therefore concluded that the North American plate extended to maintain contact as the Pacific plate migrated WNW.

**6. Collapse model (Dokka & Ross 1995)**
- Collapse began near 24 Ma and continued to c. 18 Ma, moving the lithospheric fragment southwest along subhorizontal detachment faults and ductile shear zones.
- Strain was concentrated around the perimeter of the collapsed block: Arizona metamorphic core complexes, the Colorado River Extensional Corridor, the MEB and the TMSSZ.
- Cessation of NW motion of the northern region at c. 21 Ma changed the northern boundary to dextral shear.
- The [[Salinian Block]] and other slivers were progressively sliced off and transferred to the Pacific plate (Figs 11-12).

**7. Consistency tests**
- The >80 km TMSSZ slip falls within predicted plate divergence (175 ± 100 km; Stock & Molnar 1988).
- Extensional lengthening of southern Arizona-southeastern California since early Miocene is 86 ± 13 km (Spencer & Reynolds 1991). The Salton Trough lengthening may be greater.

**8. Conclusions (as stated by the authors)**
- Motion along the 21-18 Ma TMSSZ was dextral, with at least 80 km of net slip. Internal deformation was dominated by large clockwise rotations facilitated by syntectonic sinistral zones, some with slip >20 km.
- The original trend was probably ENE, rotated 30-40° clockwise.
- The TMSSZ is consistent with the unified collapse model, serving as the regional kinematic link between inland extensional belts and the transtensional plate boundary.
- The authors note that the TMSSZ lay undetected for a long time because of masking by younger deformation and its broad, subtle effects. Its discovery depended on palaeomagnetic declination analysis.
