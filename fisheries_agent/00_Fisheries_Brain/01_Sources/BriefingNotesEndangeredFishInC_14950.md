# Briefing notes--Endangered fish in Colorado River and tributaries and the operation of Glen Canyon Dam

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ColoradoRiverBasin #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 legislative briefing memo addressing water allocation requests (notably the [[Hopi Tribe]]'s Colorado River water claim) in the context of endangered fish conservation under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). Four fish species native to the [[Colorado River]] mainstem and tributaries are federally listed as endangered: the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Colorado Pikeminnow), [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The note details the causes of population decline, ESA regulatory mechanisms (critical habitat designation, recovery plans, Section 7 consultation, and Section 9 "take" prohibitions), and two major basin-scale recovery efforts: the [[Upper Colorado River Basin Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) and nascent [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] recovery/HCP planning. It also covers the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] Draft Environmental Impact Statement (DEIS), which evaluates flow-regime alternatives intended to reduce downstream ecological harm, including habitat for a proposed new population of Humpback Chub in the [[Grand Canyon]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Mainstem [[Colorado River]] and tributaries, both above and below [[Glen Canyon Dam]]; young fish depend heavily on **backwater habitats** for rearing.
- **Range reduction**: Dam and canal construction fragmented historic ranges and blocked spawning migration routes.
- **Thermal disruption**: Dams cool water immediately downstream, rendering habitat thermally unsuitable for native warm-water-adapted fish.
- **Habitat loss**: Backwaters critical for juvenile rearing have largely disappeared due to altered flow regimes.
- **Biotic interactions**: Introduced **non-native sport and bait fish** compete with native species for food and space, and may prey on eggs and juveniles of the endangered fish; recreational angling causes incidental mortality ("accidental take").
- **Additional stressors**: Pollution and parasites are cited as possible contributing factors.
- **Critical habitat**: Large portions of the mainstem Colorado River and tributaries were proposed (January 29, 1993) as [[Critical Habitat]] for one or more of the four species, with a final rule anticipated by March 15, 1994.

## Key Findings

1. **Four endangered species** define the conservation focus of Colorado River water management: Humpback Chub, Colorado Squawfish, Bonytail Chub, and Razorback Sucker.
2. **Primary decline drivers**: dam/canal construction (range reduction, migration blockage, thermal alteration), loss of backwater nursery habitat, and non-native species competition/predation, plus pollution and parasites.
3. **ESA regulatory framework** applied to these species includes:
   - Critical habitat designation (§4(b)(2))
   - Recovery plans (§4(f)(1))
   - Interagency consultation requirement to avoid jeopardy or critical habitat destruction (§7(a)(2))
   - Prohibition on "take" of listed species (§9(a)(1)(B))
4. **[[Upper Colorado River Basin Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP)**:
   - Established January 1988 via agreement among Colorado, Utah, Wyoming, the Secretary of the Interior, and the Western Area Power Administration.
   - 15-year recovery plan covering fish above [[Glen Canyon Dam]].
   - Five core elements: instream flow acquisition, habitat development/maintenance, endangered fish stocking, non-native species/sportfishing management, and research/data monitoring.
   - Funded via annual operating budget from states, Bureau of Reclamation, Fish and Wildlife Service, water development groups, federal appropriations, and water development depletion fees.
   - RIP recovery actions serve as the "reasonable and prudent alternative" satisfying ESA Section 7 consultation for new/historic water projects in the Upper Basin — contingent on continued implementation and fee payment.
   - Progress noted in habitat management, refugia/broodstock facilities, and instream flow needs determination; acquisition of instream flow rights in Colorado is complicated by disagreements over flow-need determination methodology.
5. **[[Lower Colorado River Basin]] activities**:
   - Arizona, California, and Nevada exploring a multi-species [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] (HCP) under ESA §10(a)(2), covering the three endangered fish species present in the Lower Basin (excludes Colorado Squawfish, extirpated there).
   - No unified recovery program yet exists for the Lower Basin; a draft recovery management program was pending.
   - Federal agencies (FWS, Bureau of Reclamation, National Park Service) coordinating with state/private entities on interim recovery measures.
   - Funding mechanism for a Lower Basin HCP identified as a key unresolved issue.
6. **[[Glen Canyon Dam]] Operations DEIS** (filed January 4, 1994, per the [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] of 1992):
   - Evaluates 9 operational alternatives affecting release rates and ramp rates.
   - Preferred alternative: max release 20,000 cfs; min release 8,000 cfs (day) / 5,000 cfs (night); ramp rates capped at +2,500 cfs / −1,500 cfs per hour.
   - Restricted/steady-flow alternatives include adaptive management, cultural resource protection, flood-frequency reduction, beach/habitat-building flows, and establishment of a **new Humpback Chub population in the Grand Canyon**.
   - Final EIS expected late 1994; public comment period through April 11, 1994.
