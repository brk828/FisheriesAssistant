# Bonytail Chub HACCP Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This document is a Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point (HACCP) plan for [[Bonytail Chub]] raised at the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] in Willow Beach, AZ. The plan covers the rearing sequence from egg hatching through stocking, and it focuses on the risk that [[Aquatic Nuisance Species]] (ANS) could hitchhike with the fish, water, or equipment and cause ecological harm in receiving waters. Fish are raceway-reared and distributed by truck for restoration use. The plan identifies 11 process steps, rates ANS hazards at each step, and lists the preventive measures applied.

## Background & Description

**Product description**
- Product: Bonytail Chub, raceway reared and distributed by truck.
- Intended use and consumer: restoration. The consumers are public waters and other facilities.
- Process handling noted in the description: crowding, netting, and/or handling (the OCR text is partly illegible).

**Plan structure**
The HACCP document is organized into five sections: product description, flow diagram, potential hazards, hazard analysis worksheet, and HACCP plan form. The flow diagram and plan form are largely unreadable in the scanned text, so the worksheet is the main source of process detail.

**Source hatchery**
Adults are spawned at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] in May. Larvae are then transferred to Willow Beach NFH.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Potential ANS hazards**
The plan lists aquatic species found in the hatchery water supply or local waters that could hitchhike into new waters:
- **Fish:** [[Common Carp]], [[Striped Bass]], and [[Red Shiner]].
- **Other vertebrates:** none identified.
- **Invertebrates:** snails.
- **Plants:** algae, specifically [[Spirogyra]].

**Water sources and controls**
- Well water is used to hatch eggs and to fill distribution tanks.
- Water passes through a [[UV Sterilization]] unit before use in the larval and fry stages.
- Other vertebrates are "not present in our water supply," so this hazard is ruled out.

## Key Findings

**Production timeline (from the process flow)**
1. Adults are spawned at Dexter NFH&TC in May, and larvae are brought to Willow Beach NFH.
2. Larvae are reared in aquaria for approximately 2 months.
3. Fry are moved to indoor [[Re-use Raceways]] and reared for approximately 3 months.
4. Fingerlings are moved to outdoor re-use raceways. Feeding is daily, and raceways are cleaned periodically.
5. Fish are monitored for growth monthly and split as needed.
6. Fish are harvested to indoor raceways.
7. Fish are held in indoor raceways for 48 hours.
8. Fish are loaded into distribution tanks.
9. Fish are stocked into public waters or transported to other facilities.

**Hazard analysis by step**

| Step | Significant ANS hazard? | Preventive measure | Critical control point? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Spawning and hatching | No (well water, homogeneous cultures) | Well water used for hatching | No |
| 2. Aquaria rearing (~2 months) | No | UV sterilization | No |
| 3. Indoor re-use raceways (~3 months) | Yes, for invertebrates | Tanks cleaned daily to remove ANS | No |
| 4. Outdoor re-use raceways | Yes, for fish and invertebrates | Thoroughly clean and dry raceways before fingerlings are introduced | No |
| 5. Daily feeding and periodic cleaning | No | None needed at this step | No |
| 6. Monthly growth monitoring | No | None needed at this step | No |
| 7. Splitting as needed | Yes, for fish, invertebrates, and plants | No preventive measures can be applied | No |
| 8. Harvest to indoor raceways | Yes, for fish, invertebrates, and plants | Use proper size mesh seine to grade out smaller ANS; visually pick out larger invertebrates and plants | Marked Yes (OCR-uncertain) |
| 9. Holding in indoor raceways (48 h) | Yes, for fish, invertebrates, and plants | Hand-pick to remove; proper flow pushes non-mobile ANS to rear screen | Marked Yes (OCR-uncertain) |
| 10. Loading into distribution tanks | No | Well water for filling; previous screenings removed potential hazards | No |
| 11. Stocking or transport | No | No ANS introduced at this step | No |

**Main conclusions**
- The main ANS risks come from fish, invertebrates, and plants that may already be present in the rearing system at the fingerling, harvest, and holding stages.
- Preventive measures are based on cleaning and drying raceways, sizing mesh seines, hand-picking, flow management, and UV treatment of incoming water.
- Steps 8 and 9 (harvest and holding) are the points where the plan most clearly applies controls to stop ANS from reaching the release stage.
- Some critical-control-point entries are hard to read in the scanned text, so the exact designations at those steps should be checked against the original form.
