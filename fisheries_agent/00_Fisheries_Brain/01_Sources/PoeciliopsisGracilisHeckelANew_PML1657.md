# Endangered Fish Interpretive Plan and the Introduced Poeciliopsis gracilis in California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines two items. The first is a proposal for an interpretive program on endangered native fishes of the American Southwest, focusing on the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Desert Pupfish]]. The second is a short scientific note reporting the first record of *Poeciliopsis gracilis* (Heckel), a livebearing poeciliid, from California. The scan is heavily degraded, so some numbers and names below are best readings of OCR text and are flagged where uncertain.

## Background & Description

**Interpretive proposal.** The plan covers a historical and current-status narrative on southwest fishes, including causes of decline and conservation efforts. It proposes fact sheets on the three target species for distribution to visitors, schools, and summer camps. Staff roles are named (Patti Sowka for fact sheets and presentations, and the Media Relations Department). Proposed options are:

1. Video monitors showing 3–5 minute loops of native fishes.
2. An audio system playing 2–3 minute narrative loops.
3. Traditional signage, with graphics developed in consultation with the graphics department.
4. Life-size 3-D models of the fish, modeled on the existing "Feel-the-Difference" displays on the children's trail.
5. An aquarium display of five native fishes in the lake, with a volume of 500–1,000 gallons or a scaled-down version depending on funding.

Presentation along the shoreline or an ADA-approved boardwalk (including near the alligator exhibit) is suggested.

**Scientific note.** Specimens of a poeciliid were collected in California on 27 June 1974 after being unreported there before. The fish were identified as *Poeciliopsis gracilis* (Heckel), a species often confused with *Gila topminnow* (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) in the Gila River system. The collected specimens were immature, with four to five dark spots on each side of the translucent body.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Site and timing:** The introduction appears recent, probably early 1974. The fish were not found in earlier collections from the site, or in periodic surveys of nearby Coachella Valley canals since 1964. The source of introduction was not investigated but is presumed to be the aquarium trade, based on a nearby tropical fish farm.
- **Water conditions:** Water temperature was about 22–24 °C during the sampling events.
- **Co-occurring fauna:** Table 1 lists fishes collected or observed on 20 January, 27 July, and 17 November 1974. Families recorded include Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae, Cyprinidae, Cichlidae, and *Tilapia* sp. The table's numeric entries are largely illegible in the scan, so no reliable totals are reported here. Time of day and air and water temperature were also recorded.
- **Reproduction and schooling:** At least a dozen additional juveniles, about 9–48 mm long, were collected on 12 November 1974, indicating a reproducing population. Many more were observed schooling with a group of what appears to be the shortfin molly (*Poecilia mexicana*).

## Key Findings

- The interpretive proposal recommends a multi-format program (video, audio, signage, 3-D models, and aquarium display) to raise public awareness of endangered southwest fishes.
- *Poeciliopsis gracilis* is documented as a new introduced species in California, with the first specimens collected in June 1974.
- Repeated sampling of nearby canals since 1964 did not find the species, which supports a recent introduction.
- Juveniles across a range of sizes collected in November 1974 point to successful local reproduction.
- The author notes that the introduction pathway was not investigated and is probably linked to the aquarium trade, which requires monitoring of ornamental fish release.
