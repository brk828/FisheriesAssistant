## Executive Summary
This 1902 taxonomic note, authored by Barton A. Bean (Assistant Curator, Division of Fishes, U.S. National Museum), documents a small collection of freshwater fishes gathered by H. H. Brimley (and Franklin Sherman, Jr.) from Cane River and Bollings Creek, North Carolina. The centerpiece of the paper is the formal description of a new minnow species, **[[Notropis brimleyi]]**, placed in the subgenus *Hydrophlox* Jordan. The paper also catalogs eight additional species collected during the same expedition, providing locality, date, and specimen count data — a valuable historical snapshot of the ichthyofauna of this North Carolina drainage system.

## Background & Description

### Notropis brimleyi (new species) — Type Description
- **Body form:** Elongate, rounded, *not compressed* (distinguishing it from its closest relative, [[Notropis coccogenis]])
- **Body depth:** Contained 5½ times in standard length (vs. 4½ times in *N. coccogenis*) — the key diagnostic proportion
- **Head length:** Equal to ¼ of total length (excluding caudal)
- **Eye diameter:** Contained 3½ times in head length; slightly exceeds snout length
- **Mouth:** Moderately oblique, large; maxillary reaches to a line at the front margin of the pupil
- **Interorbital space:** Broad, width exceeding eye diameter
- **Jaws:** Approximately equal
- **Teeth:** 2, 4-4, 2
- **Fin rays:** Dorsal (D.) 9; Anal (A.) 9
- **Scales:** 5/48/8 formula; 18 rows of scales anterior to dorsal fin

### Coloration
- Light green dorsally; darker on top of head
- Silvery white venter
- Dark dorsal stripe running from head to tail
- Plumbeous (lead-gray) lateral band from upper gill cover angle to caudal peduncle, broadening at the tail and extending onto the top of the caudal peduncle
- Dark scapular band present
- Dark punctulations (speckling) on upper body
- Scales edged in dark pigment
- Dorsal and caudal fins plain anteriorly, black posteriorly
- Other fins unpigmented

### Type Material
- **Holotype:** No. 50601, U.S.N.M.
- **Type locality:** Cane River, North Carolina; collected October 4, 1902
- **Type size:** 3⅜ inches total length; 3¼ inches to origin of middle caudal rays
- **Paratypes (cotypes):** Two specimens, 2¼ and 2¼ inches, same locality

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Small freshwater river and creek systems in the southern Appalachian region of North Carolina (Cane River and Bollings Creek)
- **Co-occurring species** collected in the same survey suggest a mixed riffle/pool habitat typical of headwater or piedmont streams, supporting:
  - Cyprinids (minnows): [[Notropis arge]], [[Hybopsis kentuckiensis]], [[Campostoma anomalum]]
  - Catostomids (suckers): [[Catostomus commersonii]], [[Catostomus nigricans]]
  - Percids (darters): [[Percina caprodes]]
  - Cottids (sculpins): [[Cottus ictalops]]
  - Cyprinid riffle specialist: [[Rhinichthys cataractae]]
- This assemblage is characteristic of clean, well-oxygenated, gravel/rock-bottomed Appalachian drainage streams.

## Key Findings

1. **New species established:** *Notropis brimleyi* is described as new, most closely allied to *Notropis coccogenis* (Cope) but distinguished primarily by a significantly more elongate (less compressed) body — depth 5½ vs. 4½ times in standard length.
2. **Taxonomic placement:** The new species is assigned to the subgenus *Hydrophlox* Jordan within *Notropis*.
3. **Faunal inventory:** The collection documents nine total taxa from the two North Carolina streams, offering an early 20th-century baseline record of stream fish diversity in this locality.
4. **Collection details:**
   - *Notropis brimleyi* (type + 2 cotypes) — Cane River, Oct. 4, 1902
   - *Notropis arge* — 11 specimens (2–4 in.), Cane River, Oct. 4
   - *Rhinichthys cataractae* — 2 specimens, Bollings Creek, Oct. 8, 1902
   - *Hybopsis kentuckiensis* — 24 specimens, Cane River
   - *Campostoma anomalum* — 1 small specimen, Cane River
   - *Catostomus commersonii* — 1 juvenile, Cane River
   - *Catostomus nigricans* — 1 specimen, Cane River
   - *Percina caprodes* — 1 specimen, Cane River
   - *Cottus ictalops* — 9 specimens (1–2¼ in.), Bollings Creek, Oct. 3, 1902
5. **Collectors:** H. H. Brimley served as the primary collector, with Franklin Sherman, Jr. assisting on at least the Bollings Creek collections.
6. **Publication context:** Published in *Proceedings of the U.S. National Museum*, Vol. XXVI, No. 1339.
