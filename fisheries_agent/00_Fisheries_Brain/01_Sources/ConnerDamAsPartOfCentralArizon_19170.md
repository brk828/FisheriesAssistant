# Conner Dam Proposal and Endangered Species Act Considerations for Gila River Native Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #water-policy

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (March 18, 1988) responds to a request for federal support of [[Conner Dam]] construction on the [[Gila River]] in New Mexico as part of the [[Central Arizona Project]]. The Bureau declined further consideration, citing economic infeasibility (costs exceed benefits) and conflicts with the [[Endangered Species Act]] due to two threatened native fish species: [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). The decision reflects compliance requirements with federal water resources planning regulations and species protection laws.

## Background & Description

The [[Hooker Dam Association]] of Silver City, New Mexico, requested federal intervention to support construction of [[Conner Dam]] or other mainstream [[Gila River]] dams as alternatives to the authorized [[Hooker Dam]]. The proposed dam was evaluated as part of the Bureau of Reclamation's Upper Gila Water Supply Study, which aimed to identify mechanisms for developing [[Central Arizona Project]] water allotment consistent with applicable laws.

**Species Status:**
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*): Proposed as threatened June 18, 1985; listed as threatened July 1, 1986
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*): Proposed as threatened June 18, 1985; listed as threatened October 28, 1986
- Critical habitat designation was proposed for both species but deferred; no final determination had been made as of January 1988

Both species are native cyprinids inhabiting the [[Gila River]] system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Gila River]] through the Safford and Duncan Valleys provides habitat for two threatened native fish species. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] listing of [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] represents a significant impediment to dam construction in this system. The proposed [[Conner Dam]] would have provided water supply and flood control benefits but would impact habitat for these protected species.

The Bureau's analysis involved coordination with multiple agencies including the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]], the State of New Mexico, and local stakeholders. Compliance requirements included the [[Endangered Species Act]], the Economic and Environmental Principles and Guidelines for Water and Related Land Resources Implementation Studies, and the [[National Environmental Policy Act]].

## Key Findings

**Economic Analysis:**
- Project costs considerably outweigh benefits
- Flood control benefits were insufficient to justify the multipurpose project economically
- More efficient and less expensive alternatives exist for developing New Mexico's [[Central Arizona Project]] water allocation

**Endangered Species Act Considerations:**
- No formal Section 7 consultation between the Bureau and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] had occurred regarding [[Conner Dam]]
- An [[Endangered Species Act]] exemption process requires: (1) completion of formal consultation resulting in a jeopardy biological opinion, (2) demonstration that no alternatives exist, and (3) proof that project benefits far outweigh consequences
- None of these conditions were met: alternatives exist, costs exceed benefits, and no consultation had been initiated
- Therefore, no basis exists for pursuing an [[Endangered Species Act]] exemption

**Regulatory Compliance:**
- The project cannot proceed without compliance with the [[Endangered Species Act]]
- Critical habitat designation for both fish species remained under evaluation with consideration of economic data and public comments

**Agency Decision:**
The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] determined that [[Conner Dam]] should not receive further consideration based on economic infeasibility and regulatory constraints related to threatened species protection.
