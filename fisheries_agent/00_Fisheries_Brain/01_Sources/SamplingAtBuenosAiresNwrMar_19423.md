# Memorandum: Bonytail and Razorback Sucker Sampling, May 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

A memorandum from [[Arizona State University]] personnel reports sampling of [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] at a wildlife refuge on 3-4 May 1998. The work covered fish from several refuge tanks and lakes, with measurements of length and weight, observations of spawning condition, and subsamples taken for growth analysis. Bonytail showed no detectable growth in length or weight since January 1988. Razorback suckers from three locations showed modest growth since stocking in March 1987. The memo closes by announcing late-summer sampling and inviting questions.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), a cyprinid, and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a catostomid. Both are freshwater fishes managed in refuge and stocked populations.
- **Sampling dates:** 3-4 May 1998.
- **Sampling team:** Personnel from [[Arizona State University]].
- **Purpose:** Assess population condition, growth rates, and reproductive status, and collect subsamples for laboratory analysis.
- **Stocking history referenced:** Razorback suckers were stocked in March 1987. A separate cohort was introduced in November 1986, though none of the sampled fish could be reliably assigned to it.
- **Gear:** Experimental [[Gill Net]] (about 45 minutes, set immediately after sunset) for bonytail at Road Camp tank; [[Trammel Net]] for razorback suckers at Mormon Lake, Rock Tank, and Tequila Tank.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning condition (bonytail):** Many males were tuberculate, showed typical spawning coloration, and were running ripe. No females with readily expressible gametes were caught. The memo states there was no evidence that spawning had occurred or was underway, contrary to earlier expectations.
- **Food availability (bonytail):** [[Food Limitation]] was ruled out. The proportion of empty stomachs matched earlier samples, and fish that had fed contained substantial quantities of food.
- **Density and growth (bonytail):** The author suggests [[Density-Dependent Growth]] (stunting from overcrowding) as one possibility. A similar lack of growth was recorded for bonytail in lakes at the ASU Research Park, where density is lower. The memo notes that sample sizes and collection methods differed between the two sites, so a definitive cause may not be identifiable.
- **Mortality management:** Roughly 200 razorback suckers were captured at Mormon Lake. Only 100 were processed on site; the remainder were released to avoid unnecessary mortality.

## Key Findings

**Bonytail (Road Camp tank)**
- Mean total length: 198 mm (SD = 19; range 157-238 mm).
- Mean weight: reported in the source with an OCR-corrupted value and sample size (approximately 51 g, SD = 16, range 25-100 g, n = 24; verify against original).
- No measurable growth in length or weight since January 1988; means, deviations, and ranges were essentially unchanged.
- Spawning was not confirmed. Males were ripe, but no ripe females were taken.

**Razorback Sucker, Mormon Lake**
- About 200 fish captured; 100 measured and processed on site.
- Mean total length: 206 mm (SD = 18; range 154-234 mm, n = 100).
- Mean weight: 92 g (SE = 24; range 44-158 g, n = 30).
- Growth since January 1988 was described as at most slight.
- All sampled individuals were attributed to the March 1987 stocking.

**Razorback Sucker, Rock Tank**
- 56 fish captured.
- Mean total length: 208 mm (SD = 14; range 184-251 mm).
- Mean weight: 84 g (SD = 19; range 64-148 g, n = 31).

**Razorback Sucker, Tequila Tank**
- 44 fish captured.
- Mean total length: 211 mm (SD = 17; range 192-274 mm).
- Mean weight: 88 g (SD = 23; range 62-155 g, n = 31).

**Overall growth trend**
- Fish in Rock and Tequila tanks increased in length by approximately 33% since January 1988.
- Total growth since the March 1987 stocking was described as essentially identical across the three razorback sucker locations.

**Planned work**
- Additional sampling is scheduled for late summer to early fall.
