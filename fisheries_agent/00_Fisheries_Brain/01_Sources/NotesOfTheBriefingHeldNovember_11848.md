# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Executive Review Committee Meeting Notes - November 1989

#ichthyology #river-ecology #dam-management #environmental-assessment

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] documents the November 8, 1989 briefing regarding the October 24, 1989 [[Executive Review Committee]] (ERC) meeting for [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES). The document addresses ongoing environmental studies related to [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, including a low flow test conducted October 6-9, 1989, and plans for an [[Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS) scoping process scheduled for January-February 1990. Multiple federal agencies participated, including [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[National Park Service]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

## Background & Description

The [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program involves multiple federal agencies coordinating research on the environmental impacts of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations on the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem. S. Robinson serves as [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s overall lead for GCES as Director of Colorado River Storage Project Initiatives and Studies. D. Patten serves as Senior Scientist for GCES. The program encompasses six areas of study coordinated with fall-winter field season work, with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] designated as lead agency with 2-3 [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] staff support.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] experiences fluctuating flow regimes that impact aquatic and riparian ecosystems. The October 6-9 low flow test at 5,000 cubic feet per second (cfs) provided opportunity to study ecosystem responses under controlled conditions. Study areas include [[Lee's Ferry]] and the greater [[Grand Canyon]] ecosystem. Environmental concerns center on dam operations affecting water quality, sediment transport, beach erosion, and aquatic habitat including trout spawning areas. The document references previous flood flow relief adjustments and proposes stepped series of interim flows as structure for the research program.

## Key Findings

**Administrative Decisions:**
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] announced EIS scoping sessions scheduled for January-February 1990, with BR as lead agency
- [[Western Area Power Administration]] requested Cooperative Agency status
- BR requested [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Park Service]] serve as Cooperators in Notice of Intent
- No formal relationship yet developed between WAPA EIS (marketing criteria) and Glen Canyon EIS; WAPA effort may require four years

**October 6-9 Low Flow Test (5,000 cfs):**
- Experiments conducted to calibrate [[USGS]] gage stations
- Water quality parameter changes measured
- Beach erosion and sediment transport changes quantified
- Trout spawning habitat quantified
- General channel characteristics above [[Lee's Ferry]] determined
- Experiments designed to contrast fluctuating flow regime before and after test flows
- Report preparation planned for test period

**Constituent Concerns:**
- Limited time for EIS preparation raised concerns about document adequacy and potential delays
- Environmental community clarified they are not responsible for 'quick-fix' EIS approach
- Both D. Patten and FWS indicated problems with adequacy of quick EIS
- Need for interim flows discussed, similar to previous flood flow operation adjustments
- Questions submitted by Cohen of [[Arizona Flycasters]] regarding Solicitor's opinion on dam operations

**Upcoming Activities:**
- Preliminary Draft Planning Aid Report prepared under [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]], sent to agencies for review
- Meeting scheduled November 27, 1 pm in Flagstaff at Forest Service District Ranger Office
- GCES Environmental and Economic Team meetings scheduled November 14-15 in Tempe
- Informal coalition meeting held October 22 at Grand Canyon by conservation groups concerned about greater Grand Canyon ecosystem
