# Native Ichthyofauna of the Yaqui River Basin, Sonora, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This survey assesses the current status of the freshwater fish fauna of the [[Yaqui River Basin]] in Sonora, Mexico. Sampling covered 29 localities in March and October 1987 and August 1988. Of 23 species collected, the authors report distribution and abundance data for the native species and observations on exotic species, including three new exotic records for the basin. Native fish communities had shifted in composition and abundance at most sites compared with earlier surveys. The authors attribute this mainly to exotic species, stream modification (dams and reservoirs), and poor management of riparian zones.

The authors propose that [[Notropis Formosus]] and [[Ictalurus Pricei]] be classed as endangered, that [[Cichlasoma Beani]] be considered for the same status, and that [[Gila Robusta]], [[Gila Purpurea]] and [[Campostoma Ornatum]] are threatened. They recommend declaring critical habitats as reserves.

## Background & Description

The native freshwater fish fauna of Sonora numbers about 30 species, most of them confined to the Yaqui basin. The basin covers roughly 73,000 km² and is about 740 km long. It occupies about 30% of Sonora and lies between 27–31°N and 108–111°W. It is commonly divided into four subbasins. The Upper Yaqui includes the Papigochic-Sirupa-Aros, Bavispe and Moctezuma-Nacozari systems. The Lower Yaqui is the fourth subbasin, draining south toward the [[Alvaro Obregon Reservoir]] (Oviachic).

Earlier work includes [[Hendrickson Et Al 1981]], a monographic study of the basin's zoogeography and population status. That study recorded 27 exotic species in the basin, 15 of them in the Sonoran portion. The present survey updates it roughly a decade later.

Sampling used seines (1 m and 5 m lengths, 1.6 and 3.2 mm mesh), cast nets and hand nets across lotic and lentic habitats. Electrofishing ([[Coffelt Bp-4]]) was used at the Rio Aros only. Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin and preserved in 50% isopropanol. Habitat variables recorded included channel size, current, depth, temperature, substrate and vegetation. Voucher material is held in the ichthyological collection of the [[Centro Ecologico De Sonora]] in Hermosillo.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Community composition.** The survey collected 2,095 specimens representing 23 species in 20 genera and 7 families. Native species made up 52.17% of the species count (12 species) and exotics 47.83% (11 species). The abstract gives 11 native and 12 exotic species, so the two counts differ and the text figures are reported here. The most abundant native genus was [[Poeciliopsis]], including [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]] and the unisexual form [[Poeciliopsis Monacha-Occidentalis]]. It was followed by [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] and [[Catostomus Bernardini]]. Among exotics, [[Tilapia]] sp. had the widest distribution and highest abundance, especially in the Lower Yaqui, Bacanora and Sahuaripa rivers.

**Species richness by site.** The Lower Yaqui had the highest richness, with 15 species. The Rio Chico and Rio Aros each had 10. The Arroyo Rebeico and Arroyo La Matanza had the fewest, with three species each.

**Threats.** The authors identify four pressures:
- Establishment and spread of exotic species, many introduced for sport or food fishing and aquaculture.
- Impoundments, including the large dams [[Lazaro Cardenas Dam]] (La Angostura), [[Plutarco Elias Calles Dam]] (El Novillo) and the Oviachic reservoir, plus smaller dams on the Sahuaripa, Bacanora, Bavispe and Moctezuma rivers.
- Inappropriate use of riparian areas for agriculture, grazing and mining.
- Water pollution and reduced water quantity and quality.

**Exotic interactions.**
- [[Ictalurus Punctatus]] hybridizes with *I. pricei* in the Rio Aros, which threatens the genetic integrity of the native catfish.
- [[Gambusia Affinis]] is suspected of driving *Poeciliopsis prolifica* declines at locality 27 and of contributing to the disappearance of *N. formosus* from the Rio Moctezuma and the Rio Bavispe.
- Tilapia may compete with or hybridize with *C. beani* in the Rio Chico.
- Largemouth bass ([[Micropterus Salmoides]]) and green sunfish were abundant at the Rio Aros alongside native species. They may prey on or displace native fish such as *G. robusta* and *N. formosus*.

**Parasites.** Two trematode groups and a copepod were recorded on native fish:
- [[Uvulifer]] sp. (Diplostomidae; black-spot disease) was heavily infesting *P. occidentalis* and *A. chrysogaster*.
- [[Clinostomum]] sp. (Clinostomidae; yellow-grub disease) occurred only on native hosts in the Sahuaripa and Lower Yaqui rivers, especially *C. bernardini*, *A. chrysogaster* and *C. ornatum*.
- Copepods probably of the genus [[Lernaea]] infested several native species at lower intensity.

## Key Findings

**1. Native fish declines.** Composition and abundance of native species changed at most localities compared with earlier records. Declines are linked to exotic species and habitat alteration.

**2. Status of focal species.**
- *Notropis formosus* and *Ictalurus pricei* are recognized as endangered in the United States. The authors show reduced distribution and abundance in Sonora and recommend endangered status in Mexico as well.
- *Cichlasoma beani* is proposed for endangered status, given its evident decline in the Yaqui basin and parts of the Mayo and Fuerte basins.
- *Gila robusta*, *G. purpurea* and *Campostoma ornatum* are classed as threatened. *G. purpurea* was not collected and may have been extirpated from the basin, so wider sampling is needed.
- The remaining native species appear secure in the basin, though *Codoma ornata* and *Dorosoma smithi* need further status assessment.

**3. Specific observations.**
- *Ictalurus pricei* was represented by only 8 specimens, all from the Lower Yaqui at Onavas (locality 18).
- *Poeciliopsis prolifica* persists in a small population in the Lower Yaqui (12 specimens), a population not recorded by earlier surveys.
- *Cichlasoma beani* was absent from the Lower Yaqui but persists at low abundance in the Rio Chico (4 specimens at locality 20).
- *Lile stolifera*, a euryhaline clupeid, was recorded from a single specimen near the river mouth.

**4. New exotic records.** Three exotic species are reported as new for the basin. Among them are *Dorosoma petenense* (threadfin shad), found at locality 24 near the Lower Yaqui delta (41 specimens), and *Pomoxis nigromaculatus* (black crappie), recorded near the Oviachic dam. The text lists *Dorosoma petenense* and *Pomoxis nigromaculatus* as the new records.

**5. Management recommendations.** The authors call for:
- Designating reserves with critical physical and biological features.
- Preventing further introduction of exotic species.
- Recovering or reintroducing native species that have been extirpated or greatly reduced.
- Protecting genetic diversity of native populations.
- Suggested reserve sites: the Rio Aros and Bavispe (for *N. formosus* and *I. pricei*), the Sahuaripa (for *C. ornatum* and *G. robusta*) and the Rio Chico (for *C. beani*).

**6. Research needs.** Further sampling is needed to confirm the status of *G. purpurea*, to resolve the *Clinostomum* identification, and to assess the effects of parasites and exotic species over time.
