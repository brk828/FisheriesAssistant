# The Behavior of Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictalurus

## Executive Summary

This is the first in a planned series by [[Antonios Pappantoniou]] on the behavior of [[North American Freshwater Fishes]], written for aquarists and drawing on the scientific literature and the author's own observations. The article argues that fish behavior is largely shaped by environment, with [[Temperature]] and [[Light]] identified as the most critical factors. It also introduces [[Taxis]] (orientation toward or away from a stimulus), including [[Rheotaxis]], the [[Optomotor Response]], [[Chemotaxis]] and [[Pheromones]]. The next article in the series will focus on catfish, beginning with [[Ictalurus]]. The article is the first of a series on North American freshwater fishes. Each later installment will cover a single species or a group of closely related fishes. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Ectothermy:** Fish rely on outside heat sources to maintain body temperature. Temperature governs biochemical and physiological activity, which in turn controls behavior.
- **Temperature preference:** Species differ. Fish adapted to swift-flowing streams prefer cooler water than those adapted to small ponds.
- **Daily and seasonal temperature change:** In summer, daily fluctuations can drive onshore-offshore movements in some lake fish. Seasonal temperature shifts help trigger reproductive physiology.
- **Light patterns:** Fish may be [[Diurnal]] (active by day), [[Nocturnal]] (active at night), or active in twilight. Seasonal light changes also contribute to reproductive timing.
- **Spawning cues:** The right combination of light and temperature is critical for spawning in most freshwater fish. Aquarists can induce spawning by providing that combination.
- **Crowding:** Some pheromones released under crowded conditions stunt the growth of nearby fish, a point the author highlights for aquarium management.

## Key Findings

**Core behavioral mechanisms**

- Much fish behavior falls under taxis. [[Rheotaxis]] is common in stream fish, which usually orient upstream (positive rheotaxis).
- The optomotor response lets a fish hold position by visually fixing on a stationary object on the stream bed or bank and swimming to stay in place relative to it. Together with rheotaxis, it allows fish to hold station in fast water.
- [[Chemotaxis]] is linked to the senses of smell and taste.

**Pheromones**

- In certain minnows, skin damage (from predation or attack) releases an alarm pheromone. Nearby minnows detect it and dive to the bottom.
- Crowding releases pheromones that stunt the growth of nearby fish.
- Catfish of the genus *Ictalurus* use pheromones to recognize individuals and to form a social hierarchy.

**Reproductive and social behavior**

- Reproductive behaviors (territoriality, courtship, spawning) are complex. The author states that general descriptions are difficult because exceptions would outnumber the species that fit a pattern.
