# Fish Collection from Cajon Bonito, Sonora, Mexico (18 July 2009)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This field report by [[C. O. Minckley]] documents a seine-based fish collection made on 18 July 2009 in the [[Cajon Bonito]], a northern tributary of the [[Yaqui River]] in [[Sonora]], Mexico. Fourteen seine hauls over roughly 1 km of stream yielded 102 individuals representing six native freshwater species. All fish were identified, counted and released. The collection confirmed the presence of all six species in the stream, including the [[Ornate Minnow]], which had not been listed for the Cajon in two prior regional sources. The [[Yaqui Catfish]] was not captured, which the author attributes to the difficulty of seining for that species.

## Background & Description

The collection was made on the [[Austin Property]] at the [[El Ojo Ranch]] headquarters, about 130 km east of Agua Prieta along Highway 2, followed by roughly 4 km south. The seining reach began just above the ranch corrals and extended about 1 km downstream to the road crossing near the main ranch house. Fourteen hauls were made roughly 20–30 m apart, each averaging about 10 m in length. All available habitats were sampled, with emphasis on slower-flowing water and pools.

The fish were identified, counted and released. The report was written by C. O. Minckley and assisted by [[Lee Simons]] of Las Vegas, Nevada.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Cajon Bonito is part of the Yaqui River system, which the report cites through [[Fishes of the Rio Yaqui Basin]] (Hendrickson et al. 1980) and [[Freshwater Fishes of Mexico]] (Miller 2005). These sources document a regional fauna that includes species such as the [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus*) and the Yaqui catfish (*Ictalurus pricei*). The beautiful shiner was not collected in this survey. The report notes that the Yaqui catfish is well documented in the system, but seining is a poor method for capturing it, so its absence here was expected.

Population structure suggested active reproduction and recruitment. Most Yaqui suckers and Mexican chub were young-of-the-year, and the report notes several larger suckers and chub that were seen but not captured.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** Six species were collected, all of which had been recorded previously in Cajon Bonito.
- **Dominant species:** The ornate minnow was the most abundant fish, with 58 individuals (about 57% of the catch).
- **Ornate minnow reproduction:** Individuals were spawning, showing black fins bordered in white and tubercles. Reproductive fish ranged from 50 to 70 mm total length.
- **Sonora topminnow:** All 16 individuals taken were adults over 40 mm.
- **Yaqui sucker and Mexican chub:** Most were young-of-the-year, with lengths given as under 100 (unit not clearly legible in the scan). One Mexican chub of about 15 cm was taken. Several larger individuals of both species were observed but not captured.
- **Mexican stoneroller:** Four fish, recorded at 70 mm total length or less.
- **Longfin dace:** A single adult was taken, the only one in the collection.
- **Range records:** The ornate minnow was not reported from the Cajon by Hendrickson et al. (1980) or Miller (2005). Its presence is supported by [[C. O. Minckley]]'s unpublished field notes.
- **Sampling limitation:** The absence of the Yaqui catfish and the beautiful shiner was attributed to sampling method and not to their absence from the system.
