# Memorandum on Spinedace Meeting in Flagstaff

#ichthyology #leuciscidae #spinedace #conservation-management

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum summarizing a February 26, 1993 interagency meeting held at Coconino National Forest Headquarters in Flagstaff, Arizona, regarding conservation and research planning for [[Spinedace]]. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the memo outlines a coordinated 1993 research agenda involving USFWS, the U.S. Forest Service, Arizona Game and Fish (AGF), Northern Arizona University (NAU), and Arizona State University (ASU). Key action items include substrate preference experiments at [[The Arboretum At Flagstaff]], a forest-wide re-survey of spinedace localities, genetic research status updates, and a proposed physical barrier in [[Silver Creek]] to mitigate trout predation/competition impacts near a critical springhead.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristic counts, coloration, body proportions) are presented in this source. The document is administrative/programmatic in nature. Morphological characterization of spinedace (genus *Lepidomeda*) would need to be sourced from taxonomic literature; this memo focuses exclusively on management and research coordination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Small stream systems within Coconino National Forest, Arizona, including a captive/experimental stream at [[The Arboretum At Flagstaff]] and wild populations in [[Silver Creek]].
- **Spawning ecology:** Spawning substrate preferences are unknown/untested for [[Spinedace]]; a 1993 field experiment was proposed to introduce a variety of substrates into the Arboretum stream to identify preferred spawning material.
- **Species interactions:** Non-native [[Trout]] identified as a potential threat to spinedace near the main springhead in Silver Creek, prompting discussion of physical exclusion barriers.
- **Hydrological disturbance:** Recent high-water/flood events reportedly damaged existing weir structures in Silver Creek, which were slated for repair by Arizona Game and Fish (AGF).
- **Distributional monitoring:** A re-survey of known spinedace localities across Coconino National Forest was requested to update presence/absence data and incidentally document all co-occurring fish species.

## Key Findings
- **Research Coordination:** [[Chuck Minckley]] (USFWS) agreed to serve as informal liaison among the various agencies and researchers (USFWS, USFS, AGF, NAU, ASU) working on spinedace conservation and research.
- **Proposed 1993 Field Projects:**
  1. Substrate preference trial at The Arboretum to test for a preferred spawning substrate among spinedace.
  2. Forest-wide re-survey of spinedace localities on Coconino National Forest, requested by [[Tom Caine]] (USFS), including collection of observational data on all fish species encountered.
- **Genetic Research Status:** Ongoing genetic work on spinedace by [[Tom Dowling]] (ASU) was flagged for status inquiry; Minckley agreed to investigate and report back to the group.
- **Silver Creek Barrier Proposal:** Discussion of installing a trout-exclusion barrier near the main springhead in [[Silver Creek]], timed to coincide with AGF's planned weir repairs (weirs damaged by recent high water). [[Jim Novy]] to be contacted regarding feasibility.
- **Outreach/Education:** A Heritage Fund proposal was submitted jointly by USFWS and [[The Arboretum At Flagstaff]] to the State of Arizona to develop a public interpretive exhibit on spinedace.
- **Meeting Attendees:** [[Tom Caine]] (USFS), [[Greg Goodwin]] (USFS), [[Scot Reger]] (AGF), [[Dean Blinn]] (NAU), [[Clay Runck]] (NAU), and [[Chuck Minckley]] (USFWS).

> **Note:** This source is a brief administrative memorandum rather than a primary research article; it lacks quantitative ecological, morphological, or population data. It is valuable primarily as a historical record of collaborative conservation planning for imperiled [[Spinedace]] populations in the Little Colorado River drainage.
