# Late Holocene Lacustrine Chronology and Archaeology of Lake Cahuilla, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-mussel

## Executive Summary

This paleolimnological study by [[Michael R. Waters]] (University of Arizona) reconstructs the late Holocene lake history of [[Lake Cahuilla]], a freshwater lake that periodically filled the [[Salton Trough]] of southern California when the [[Colorado River]] was diverted westward from its course to the [[Gulf of California]]. Using new stratigraphic sections, radiocarbon dating of charcoal and freshwater mussel shell, and historical and sedimentological evidence, the author identifies four lacustral intervals that reached the 12-m shoreline between A.D. 700 and 1580. Archaeological sites of the [[Lowland Patayan]] culture are associated with the 12-m shoreline and correlate with these intervals. The paper is primarily a geology and archaeology study; fish appear only incidentally (fishbone and freshwater fish remains at archaeological sites).

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** The Salton Trough is the landward extension of the depression flooded by the Gulf of California. It is about 225 km long, ranges from a few km to 110 km wide, and has more than 5400 km² lying below sea level. All streams in the trough are ephemeral.
- **Climate:** Arid Colorado Desert setting, average annual precipitation 6.4 cm/yr, summer temperatures up to 51°C, mild winters.
- **Colorado River:** Drains more than 629,270 km². Average annual discharge at Yuma, Arizona, 1903–1930 was 2 × 10⁴ hm³/yr (figure as given). Discharge declined after 1930 due to damming and withdrawals. Tree-ring-based reconstruction over the last 650 yr (Thomas et al., 1960) agrees closely with the 1903–1930 average.
- **Historical discovery:** [[William Blake]] reported the 12-m shoreline in 1854 and named the extinct lake "Lake Cahuilla" in 1907.
- **Prior chronologies:** [[Stanley]] (1962, 1965) proposed at least three lacustral intervals. [[Wilke]] (1978) proposed three intervals (100 B.C.–A.D. 600, A.D. 900–1250, A.D. 1300–1500), based largely on archaeological and tufa radiocarbon dates that the present author judges inadequate.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake formation mechanism.** Lake Cahuilla formed when the Colorado River, flowing into the trough through distributary channels across its delta, was captured or diverted westward. Channels overflowed natural levees and discharged down the delta's northern slope. Triggers include infrequent large floods or tectonic movement. Because the lake's level was controlled by river discharge and the delta's minimum crest altitude at [[Cerro Prieto]] (12 m), the author concludes it provides little paleoclimatic information.

**Hydrology:**
- Lake at 12 m: surface area over 5700 km², maximum depth 95 m.
- Roughly half the average annual Colorado River discharge (under present evaporation and precipitation) is needed to maintain the lake at 12 m.
- Filling time: about 12–20 yr if the lake received the entire river flow (Wilke, 1978).
- Desiccation: about 60 yr to dry completely at 1.8 m/yr once isolated from the river.

**Lacustrine sediments:** Mostly horizontally laminated, very fine sand to silt and clay, containing freshwater shellfish. Fluvial deposits are coarse sand with minor gravel. Interval 3 deposits are ripple-laminated and show unconformities.

**Gulf of California sedimentology:** Colorado River sediment supply builds mudflats; reduced supply (river diverted to the trough) allows shoreline formation. [[Thompson]] (1968) recorded three periods of sediment curtailment: 1050–250 B.C. (shoreline 1), an interval between A.D. 450 and 950 (shoreline 2, based on 17 radiocarbon dates), and the modern coastline (shoreline 3).

**Shoreline features:** The 12-m shoreline is well preserved and nearly continuous around the trough. Desert varnish and pavements are absent on beaches, and soil development is weak (torripsamments/entisols).

**Archaeology:** Numerous Lowland Patayan sites with locally made pottery, ground-and-chipped stone, and other features are associated with the shoreline. Sites contain freshwater fish bones and burned fishbone with shell.

## Key Findings

1. **Four lacustral intervals (A.D. 700–1580):** The lake reached 12 m during four episodes, each separated by complete or partial desiccation. Ages are considered accurate to about ±100 yr.
   - **Interval 1:** Began about A.D. 700 (filling); ended about A.D. 940.
   - **Interval 2:** Occurred between A.D. 940 and 1210, with an estimated total duration of about 250 yr; not directly dated.
   - **Interval 3:** Began about A.D. 1210, with at least 200 yr of deposition recorded. Lake fluctuated, dropping to near −40 m before interval 4.
   - **Interval 4:** Began A.D. 1430 and terminated about A.D. 1580, about 100 yr at 12 m plus 50 yr for desiccation.
2. **Earlier lakes:** Two intervals dated 2285 ± 120 and 2300 ± 120 ¹⁴C yr B.P. (about 400 and 420 B.C.) indicate no lacustral intervals between A.D. 1 and 700 are identified. Earlier, unquantified stands probably occurred before A.D. 1.
3. **Termination:** Spanish explorers recorded the Colorado River following its present course by A.D. 1540, which closely matches the independently derived termination date of about A.D. 1580. Mudflat deposition (Stage V) in the Gulf resumed, marking the end.
4. **Methodological critique:** Earlier radiocarbon clusters (Wilke, 1978) were judged inadequate because samples lacked stratigraphic context and many were not corrected for isotope fractionation. Charcoal is preferred over shell because of shell's initial ¹⁴C/¹²C ratio and surface contamination issues. Atmospheric ¹⁴C variation can shift calendar ages by up to 200 yr.
5. **Sedimentation rate:** Interval 1 yielded a rate of 0.3 cm/yr (preferred; range 0.3–0.5 cm/yr), applied to other units at locality A.
6. **Dating summary:** Fourteen new radiocarbon samples were analyzed from three stratigraphic localities (A, B, C). Table 1 lists 14 samples; six archaeological charcoal dates (Table 2) were used as a cross-check.
7. **Archaeological correlation:** The oldest dated site (A.D. 650, LJ-105, with freshwater fish bones) corresponds to interval 1. Other sites align with intervals 2–4. A dated site 55 m below sea level (A.D. 1640, UCLA-192) postdates the lake.

**Conclusions:** The Salton Trough was a region of environmental extremes, shifting between desert and lacustrine conditions during the late Holocene. Lake formation was geomorphically, not climatically, controlled. The hydrologic budget requires roughly half of the Colorado River's average discharge to sustain the lake at 12 m.
