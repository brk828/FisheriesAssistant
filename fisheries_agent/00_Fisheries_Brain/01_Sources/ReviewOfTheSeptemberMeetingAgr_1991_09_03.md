## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the Bureau of Reclamation's Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program, summarizing agreements reached at a September 3, 1991 meeting among Principal Investigators studying endangered fish in the [[Little Colorado River]]. While not a primary data-reporting paper, it establishes the sampling protocols, study locations, and ethical/administrative guidelines governing field research on the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fish species in the Grand Canyon region. The memo reflects a collaborative, multi-agency effort (FWS, Arizona Game & Fish, ASU, NPS) to standardize microhabitat and macrohabitat sampling methodology while minimizing harm to study populations.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristics, coloration) are presented in this source. The document is procedural in nature and focuses on study design rather than specimen-level biological description. Species affected are broadly categorized as:
- **Endangered native fish** (implied to include [[Humpback Chub]], a Colorado River basin endemic historically studied in the [[Little Colorado River]])
- **Native non-endangered fish**
- **Exotic (non-native) fish species**, which are subject to catch-and-release policy unless sacrificed for defined scientific study

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat/Study Sites**: Two designated 500-meter intensive microhabitat study sections within the [[Little Colorado River]]:
  - **Salt Trail Camp**
  - **Powell Canyon Camp**
- **Sampling Method**: Hoop nets deployed for fish capture, checked at minimum every 12 hours per [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) permit conditions; potential extension of check intervals pending data-driven justification from [[Arizona State University]] (ASU).
- **Macrohabitat Monitoring**: Continued at ASU hoop net sites, though sampling frequency may be reduced over time.
- **Baiting Restrictions**: Hoop nets are prohibited from being baited within the two intensive 500-meter microhabitat zones; baiting outside these zones is discouraged unless it is the only viable method for sample acquisition — indicating concern for minimizing artificial behavioral influence on native and endangered fish populations.
- **Young Life-History Stage Sampling**: To be jointly developed by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and FWS, targeting juvenile fish stages within the LCR ecosystem — critical for understanding recruitment and early survival within the [[Little Colorado River]] nursery habitat.
- **Exotic Species Management**: Reflects an ecological policy stance against an "eradication mentality," favoring return of exotic fish unharmed unless a specific scientific rationale justifies sacrifice — suggesting management awareness of exotic-native species interactions within this endangered fish habitat.

## Key Findings
1. **Standardized Study Sites**: Microhabitat research by the FWS will be concentrated in two defined 500-meter reaches (Salt Trail Camp and Powell Canyon Camp) within the [[Little Colorado River]], improving comparability and focus of ecological data collection.
2. **Protocol Documentation Requirement**: FWS is required to submit a written protocol detailing sampling procedures and objectives, enabling review of staffing needs and logistical requirements — an administrative control ensuring scientific rigor and reproducibility.
3. **Animal Welfare Assessment**: A new procedure, led by GCES, will formally evaluate the health/welfare impacts of the fish sampling program itself on [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fish, to be reviewed by the National Park Service and cooperating agencies.
4. **Hoop Net Check Interval**: Default check interval is every 12 hours per FWS permit; ASU tasked with providing an empirical basis to potentially justify longer intervals, balancing fish stress/mortality risk against logistical sampling constraints.
5. **Adaptive Management Documentation**: Any proposed change to established sampling protocols must be preceded by a "logic document" summarizing supporting data — ensuring transparency and accountability in adaptive study design.
6. **Exotic Species Policy**: Establishes a conservation-oriented catch-and-release default policy for exotic species, sacrificing individuals only under defined scientific study plans, reflecting an ecologically cautious, non-punitive approach to invasive species within the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] habitat.
7. **Baiting Restrictions as Sampling Control**: Prohibiting bait within core microhabitat study zones indicates a methodological effort to preserve natural fish distribution/behavior data integrity within the [[Little Colorado River]] endangered fish studies.
