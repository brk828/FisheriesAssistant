## Executive Summary
This 1959 *Nautilus* paper by Branson and Heard reports on land and freshwater snails collected on the Alaska Peninsula near [[Brooks Lake]] (Katmai National Monument) during summer 1958, alongside incidental but ecologically significant data on the malacophagous (snail-eating) feeding habits of several resident fish species. The study documents an unusually dense periphyton-associated gastropod community—dominated by [[Lymnaea Emarginata]], [[Menetus Cooperi Planospirus]], and [[Valvata Helicoidea]]—that forms a major dietary component for [[Dolly Varden Charr]], [[Round Whitefish]], [[Alaska Blackfish]], and [[Ninespine Stickleback]]. The authors speculate that this abundant molluscan food source may reduce predation pressure by Dolly Varden charr on juvenile [[Sockeye Salmon]] at Brooks Lake, a hypothesis of considerable fisheries management interest given the lake's importance to sockeye production.

## Background & Description
**Gastropod taxa recorded:**
- **[[Succinea Strigata]]** (Pfeiffer) — collected from a peat bog near the Naknek River, King Salmon area; widely distributed across Alaska.
- **[[Deroceras Laeve]]** (Müller) — small grayish-to-blackish terrestrial slug with a soot-colored foot; previously misidentified in regional literature as *Agriolimax (Limax) hyperboreus* (Westerlund) by Dall (1905), later synonymized with *D. laeve* by Waldén (1956). Captured incidentally in small-mammal traps baited with peanut butter.
- **[[Lymnaea Emarginata]]** (Say) — dominant pulmonate snail of the periphyton zone; specimens closely resembled the form *randolphi* (per Dall); individuals reached up to 25 mm in length.
- **[[Menetus Cooperi Planospirus]]** F. C. Baker — small planorbid snail, second most abundant gastropod in the periphyton community.
- **[[Valvata Helicoidea]]** Dall — abundant prosobranch snail associated with Chara beds.
- **[[Pisidium Idahoense]]** Roper — small freshwater bivalve (pea clam); only an empty double-valved shell was recovered, from a lake trout stomach.

**Fish species examined (gill-net captures):**
- 75–80 [[Dolly Varden Charr]] (*Salvelinus malma*) — stomachs individually examined; one 8 lb specimen contained 253 gastropods, another medium-sized fish contained 82 snails.
- 1 [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*) — stomach contained 1 *L. emarginata* and 1 empty *Pisidium idahoense* shell.
- Several [[Round Whitefish]] (*Prosopium cylindraceum*).
- Several [[Alaska Blackfish]] (*Dallia pectoralis*).
- Thousands of [[Ninespine Stickleback]] (*Pungitius pungitius*), sampled only in part.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
[[Brooks Lake]] is a deep, relatively oligotrophic lake (max depth 252 ft) where suitable gastropod habitat is restricted to shallow zones—the northern end and a peripheral shelf less than 10–25 ft deep—supporting dense periphyton growth dominated by the algal genus *Chara*. This littoral Chara-periphyton zone functions as the primary production hub sustaining an extremely dense gastropod assemblage (*Lymnaea emarginata*, *Menetus cooperi planospirus*, *Valvata helicoidea*), observed and sampled via SCUBA on August 6 and 19, 1958.

This gastropod community occupies a keystone trophic position, linking littoral primary productivity to multiple fish species across different feeding strategies:
- **[[Round Whitefish]]** and **[[Alaska Blackfish]]** consume large numbers of *Menetus cooperi planospirus*.
- **[[Dolly Varden Charr]]** feed heavily on *Lymnaea emarginata*, apparently taking *Menetus* only secondarily while foraging on the larger lymnaeid.
- **[[Round Whitefish]]** (a known established mollusk-eater) and **[[Ninespine Stickleback]]** are the only species observed preying on *Valvata helicoidea*, with whitefish consuming far more than stickleback.
- **[[Lake Trout]]**, not typically considered malacophagous, showed only incidental mollusk remains, likely ingested secondarily via predation on whitefish (a piscivorous link rather than direct grazing).
- **[[Alaska Blackfish]]**, being small-bodied, take only occasional and correspondingly small *L. emarginata* individuals.

## Key Findings
- Brooks Lake supports an exceptionally dense littoral gastropod population tied to Chara-based periphyton in shallow (<10–25 ft) zones.
- *Lymnaea emarginata* is the dominant prey item for Dolly Varden charr, with single stomachs containing up to 253 individual snails (some up to 25 mm long), often to the near-exclusion of other food items.
- Differential predation patterns exist among fish species: round whitefish and blackfish favor *Menetus cooperi planospirus*; Dolly Varden and round whitefish favor *Lymnaea emarginata*; round whitefish and stickleback are the primary consumers of *Valvata helicoidea*.
- Lake trout mollusk consumption appears indirect/incidental, likely acquired secondarily through piscivory on whitefish rather than direct benthic grazing.
- The authors propose a management-relevant hypothesis: the high availability of gastropod prey may reduce predatory pressure by [[Dolly Varden Charr]] on juvenile [[Sockeye Salmon]] in Brooks Lake, as charr may preferentially exploit abundant, easily obtained molluscan food over fish prey — a hypothesis flagged as requiring further verification.
- Taxonomic clarification is provided for regional slug records: specimens previously assigned to *Agriolimax hyperboreus* in Alaskan malacological literature are confirmed conspecific with [[Deroceras Laeve]] following Waldén (1956).

**Data limitations:** Only Dolly Varden charr stomachs were exhaustively analyzed; other species (round whitefish, blackfish, stickleback) were assessed from limited samples only, with detailed quantitative dietary analysis deferred to a separate publication.
