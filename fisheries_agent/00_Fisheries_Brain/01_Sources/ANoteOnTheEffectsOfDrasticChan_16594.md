# Effects of Drastic Changes in Total Dissolved Solids on Bulinus (Physopsis) africanus

#malacology #freshwater-ecology #planorbidae

## Executive Summary

This short paper by J. Heeg (Department of Zoology, University of Natal, Pietermaritzburg) examines how abrupt changes in [[Total Dissolved Solids]] (TDS) affect the aquatic pulmonate snail [[Bulinus Africanus]]. Snails from the [[Vernon Hooper Dam]] at Shongweni (native water 490 ppm TDS) suffered 46% mortality when moved directly to a laboratory culture medium of 126 ppm TDS. They also showed much greater variability in oxygen consumption. A two-stage [[Acclimation]] through intermediate media removed both effects. The author concludes that the species tolerates gradual change but not sudden dilution, and that snails should be acclimated to laboratory conditions before experiments.

## Background & Description

*Bulinus (Physopsis) africanus* is a freshwater pulmonate snail, studied here as a potential intermediate host context for schistosomiasis work. The study used schistosome-free individuals. The original observation came from a laboratory culture problem. Snails from the Shongweni dam suffered 34% mortality within three days of transfer to standard aquarium water, while snails collected the same day from a pool in the [[Mpolweni River]] at [[Krantzkloof Nature Reserve]], about eight km away, all survived. Both groups received the same diet and laboratory conditions, which pointed to a physiological shock caused by differences in dissolved solids. Because stressed snails could bias the planned experiments, the author set out to confirm this.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water chemistry:** Shongweni water differed markedly from both Krantzkloof water and the standard aquarium medium in TDS. The difference was mainly due to high sodium, chloride and bicarbonate. Shongweni water had 490 ppm TDS and conductivity of 837 µmho/cm, against 41 ppm TDS and 74 µmho/cm for Krantzkloof water and 126 ppm TDS for standard aquarium water. Sodium was 5.11 meq/l at Shongweni and 0.04 meq/l at Krantzkloof.
- **Media preparation:** An artificial Shongweni substitute (537 ppm TDS) and two acclimation media (308 ppm and 216 ppm TDS) were made by adding salts to water matured in a well-vegetated fishpond. Pond water measured 65 ppm TDS.
- **Natural dynamics:** The author notes that sudden large changes in dissolved solids should be rare in natural fresh water. Evaporation and inundation normally act gradually.
- **Distribution:** The species lives in both chemically different habitats, and snails of all sizes and egg masses were abundant in both. This supports its adaptability.

## Key Findings

1. **Direct transfer causes stress and mortality.** Moving Shongweni snails straight into standard aquarium water (126 ppm TDS) gave 46% mortality over two weeks, with most deaths in the first week (29 of 83 snails). Transfer to the Shongweni water substitute produced 4% mortality, and Krantzkloof snails transferred directly had 3%.
2. **Oxygen consumption variability rises sharply.** Mean oxygen consumption under direct transfer was 1.6 µl/mg dry tissue weight/h, only slightly above other treatments (1.3–1.4). However, the coefficient of variation was 66.3, compared with 25.0 after two-stage acclimation and 23.1 for Krantzkloof snails. The author describes this as more than two and a half times the variability of the other groups.
3. **Two-stage acclimation removes the effects.** Snails spent seven days in each of two intermediate media before transfer to standard water. This brought mortality to 4% (3 of 85 snails in the second and third weeks combined) and returned variability to levels seen in controls.
4. **Survivors adapt.** Snails that survived direct transfer showed variability after two weeks (CV 28.5) comparable to acclimated Shongweni snails and Krantzkloof snails, which suggests they had adjusted.
5. **Sample sizes.** Oxygen consumption groups contained 25 snails per Shongweni treatment, 22 Krantzkloof transfer snails and 20 Krantzkloof controls. The survivor group contained 12 snails. Mortality groups ranged from 62 to 85 snails.
6. **Implications for laboratory work.** The author concludes that snails from different localities, even nearby ones, can respond very differently to experimental conditions unless they are acclimated to constant culture conditions first. Otherwise results can be significantly affected.
7. **Comparison with other species.** [[Biomphalaria Pfeifferi]] is reported to be sensitive in fecundity to dissolved solids (Harrison 1966, 1968; Williams 1970). The author suggests it may respond more strongly to such changes than *B. (Physopsis) africanus*.
8. **Reconciling field distribution data.** The author suggests that the earlier finding of Schutte and Frank (1954), who found no link between snail distribution and dissolved solid composition, can be explained by the snails' capacity to acclimate.
