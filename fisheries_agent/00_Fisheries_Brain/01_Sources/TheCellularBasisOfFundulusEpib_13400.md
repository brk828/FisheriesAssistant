# The Cellular Basis of Fundulus Epiboly: Adhesivity of Blastula and Gastrula Cells in Culture

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fundulidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 study by J. P. [[Trinkaus]] examines whether [[Epiboly]], the most extensive morphogenetic movement in the teleost egg, is driven by the activity of blastoderm cells themselves. Dissociated cells from [[Fundulus Heteroclitus]] ([[Atlantic Killifish]]) blastulae and early gastrulae were cultured in vitro and scored for flattening on glass, used as a measure of [[Cell Adhesivity]]. Blastula cells remained spheroid, while most early gastrula cells flattened extensively. The author concludes that epiboly depends in part on increased adhesivity of the blastoderm's constituent cells, and proposes a revised concept of the mechanism.

## Background & Description

- **Prior hypothesis:** [[Lewis Gel Layer Hypothesis]] (Lewis, 1949a,b) held that the blastoderm is passively stretched by tension from the surface gel layer of the yolk sphere. Devillers (1951a,b) and Trinkaus (1951a,b,c) independently concluded that the primary expansive forces reside in the periblast and the blastoderm itself.
- **Model organism:** Fundulus heteroclitus, a teleost with a [[Periblast]] (syncytial yolk layer) and a deep cell mass beneath an [[Enveloping Layer]].
- **Staging:** Eggs were staged using the Oppenheimer (1937) series. Stages 8-9 are "blastula," stage 10 to 10½ is "late blastula," and stages 11 to 11¾ are "early gastrula." Middle and late gastrula stages were not studied.
- **Methods:** Blastoderms were dechorionated, periblast removed, and cells dissociated with EDTA using a micropipette. Cultures were maintained at 18-25°C in a medium of 0.5% glucose and 0.2% proteose-peptone in double-strength [[Holtfreter's Solution]], with penicillin G and streptomycin sulfate at 0.01%. Cells were photographed at intervals up to 6 hours, with some extended to 8-9 hours.
- **Flattening grades:** Cells were scored from grade 0 (spheroid, lobopodia present, no flattening) to grade 3 (maximally flattened with fan-shaped extensions and lacework). Grades in parentheses indicate a minority of flattened cells.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Eggs were grown in sea water, so achieving asepsis in culture was not possible. Antibiotics were therefore added.
- Cell density (180 to 450 cells per 0.001 ml) had no apparent effect on behavior. Temperature (18-25°C) also had no noted effect.
- The study ties cell behavior to the hypothesis that the periblast is the substratum over which the blastoderm spreads. Its rate limits blastoderm epiboly once the margins meet.
- Comparative data are drawn from [[Salmo]] (rainbow trout), where [[Devillers]] reported similar enveloping-layer behavior, and from other teleosts including Brachydanio, Serranus, Muraena, Noturus, Salvelinus, Schilbeodes, and Batrachus, all cited from the literature.

## Key Findings

1. **Blastula cells are non-flattening.** Across 39 cultures (7 experiments), blastula cells (stages 8-9) remained in grade 0 or (1)/1 during the first 2 hours (95% of cultures). Over 6 hours, 90% remained in grade 0 or (1)/1, with no extensive flattening.
2. **Early gastrula cells flatten strongly.** Across 74 cultures (12 experiments), 70% of cultures reached grades 2 or 3 during the first 2 hours. In 17 of the 25 cultures reaching grade 3, over 80% of cells flattened into a lacework. In 19 cultures (25%), grades (3) or 3 were reached within 40 minutes. No differences were found between stages 11, 11¼, and 11¾.
3. **Adhesivity correlates with flattening.** Cells in aggregates flattened far more than isolated cells. In nineteen grade 3 cultures examined at 1 hour, only one had more than 20% of individual cells flattened, while aggregated cells reached 50% (5 cultures), about 80% (5 cultures), and 100% (7 cultures). The author concludes that flattening indicates adhesivity.
4. **Adhesivity increases during late blastula stages.** Late blastula cells (stage 10-10½, 34 cultures, 5 experiments) were intermediate between blastula and early gastrula cells. Flattening increased over time in culture: at 8 hours, cultures previously in grade 0 or (1) shifted to grades 2 and (3). The author infers that the process is largely intracellular.
5. **Timing parallels in vivo development.** Control eggs reached stage 11¼ to 12 within 4-5 hours, suggesting the adhesivity change proceeds at about the same rate in dissociated cells and in the intact blastoderm.
6. **Deep cells contribute to flattening.** Because the enveloping layer is a small fraction of early gastrula cells, the extent of flattening implies that many deep cells also flatten. Histology shows deep cells become rhomboid and tightly packed, and hypoblast cells flatten on the periblast.
7. **Revised mechanism of epiboly.** The author proposes that epiboly begins with differential increases in cell adhesivity. Enveloping-layer cells adhere to deep cells, deep cells pack together and move over each other, and hypoblast and marginal enveloping-layer cells flatten on the periblast. Cell division is said to play no role, citing [[Kessel]] (1960).
8. **Periblast contraction and blastoderm.** Postequatorial periblast contraction contributes to epiboly. A detached blastoderm spreads equally well at all stages, so the mechanism is considered to be the same throughout epiboly.
9. **Broader implications.** Changes in adhesivity may drive other morphogenetic movements such as convergence, involution, and extension. The author suggests that external pulling forces may be less critical than cell activity, and questions whether archenteron extension in echinoderms (Dan and Okazaki, 1956; Gustafson and Kinnander, 1956) is truly passive.
10. **Open questions.** It remains unknown whether extracellular flattening factors are required, what causes the increase in adhesivity, and whether contact inhibition is involved.
