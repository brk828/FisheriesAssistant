# Results of an Opening Week Creel Census and Tagging Study on Three State-Owned Lakes

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #centrarchidae #largemouth-bass #creel-census #tagging-study

## Executive Summary

This 1955 study by the [[Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources]] examined the impact of intensive fishing pressure during the opening week of three newly stocked state-owned lakes. [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) were tagged with monel strap jaw tags prior to opening, and comprehensive creel censuses were conducted. Tag return rates were remarkably consistent across all three lakes (27.0%, 30.9%, and 27.3%), with 70% of the total first-week bass catch occurring within the first 30 hours of fishing. The study provides evidence that bass rapidly learn to avoid capture and that jaw tags may adversely affect growth rates. The authors recommend opening lakes on January 1 following the first spawn rather than during peak fishing season to distribute angling pressure more evenly.

## Background & Description

Kentucky's expanding lake-building program in the 1950s created numerous state-owned lakes ranging from 33 to 305 acres, stocked and managed for public fishing. These lakes generated intense local interest, with sportsmen's clubs and communities investing labor and money in land acquisition and clearing. The typical pattern involved stocking [[Largemouth Bass]] fry, waiting approximately one year for maturation and first spawn, then opening the lake to fishing—resulting in a "circus of fishing activity" directed at uneducated bass populations.

**Study Lakes:**

1. **Marion County Sportsmen's Club Lake** (53 acres, 19 acres at census): Located 5 miles southeast of Lebanon in central Kentucky's Knobs region. Small watershed (4.5:1 ratio), brown water, Secchi disk reading ~2 feet, methyl orange alkalinity 21 ppm, total hardness 35 ppm (as CaCO₃). One access point. Stocked 5/17/54 with 1,500 bass fry and 400 bluegill (2-6 inches) on 5/28/54.

2. **Smoky Hollow Lake** (43 acres): Located in [[Carter Caves State Park]], northeastern Kentucky. Large watershed (195:1 ratio, 8,400 acres) of high wooded hills with narrow bottomland farms. Very clear water, methyl orange alkalinity 124 ppm, total hardness 142 ppm. Multiple access points. Stocked 5/31/54 with 5,000 bass fry, 900 adult bluegill (7/28/54), and 730 additional bluegill (8/11/54).

3. **Bullock Pen Lake** (178 acres, ~150 acres at census): North central Kentucky, 30 miles south of Cincinnati-Covington-Newport urban area. Watershed 20:1 ratio, mostly farmland with good soils, heavy algal bloom. Methyl orange alkalinity 106 ppm, total hardness 156 ppm. Four good public access points plus private access. Stocked 5/18/54 with 15,000 bass fry, 3,166 adult bluegill (7/6 and 7/29/54), and 500 2-inch [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*) on 12/8/54.

**Tagging Methodology:**

All fish were captured by hook and line using small spinning lures (spinners and spoons with pork rind). Bass were extremely easy to catch before lake opening. The most productive 4-hour session yielded 108 largemouth bass, 1 Kentucky bass, 1 smallmouth bass, and 1 bluegill. Fish were tagged in the upper jaw with numbered monel strap tags (No. 1 and primarily No. 3 sizes) bearing "Game and Fish, Frankfort." Total length was measured to the nearest 0.1 inch on a standard measuring board.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three study lakes represented diverse ecological conditions across Kentucky's physiographic regions, from nutrient-poor brown-water systems to productive farmland impoundments. Water chemistry varied substantially: Marion County Lake showed low alkalinity (21 ppm) and hardness (35 ppm), while Smoky Hollow and Bullock Pen exhibited much higher values (124-106 ppm alkalinity, 142-156 ppm hardness), reflecting different watershed geology and land use.

The stocking protocol followed Kentucky's farm pond program: largemouth bass fry as the primary piscivore, with small numbers of adult [[Bluegill]] (20-50 per acre) stocked after bass establishment but before bass required a piscivorous diet. This approach aimed to delay the "almost inevitable" overcrowding with slow-growing intermediate-sized bluegill.

Wild fish populations were minimal except at [[Bullock Pen Lake]], which contained an unauthorized population of [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus melas*, 9-12 inches) intentionally planted by unknown persons. Dead bullheads with net marks were found near an access point during winter 1954-55.

The lakes opened to fishing approximately 14-15 months post-stocking, allowing bass to reach maturity and complete their first spawn. Opening times were adjusted to reduce initial pressure: 1:00 P.M. for Marion County and Smoky Hollow, 4:00 P.M. for Bullock Pen. Creel limits were reduced to five bass per fisherman.

## Key Findings

**Tag Return Rates:**

- Marion County Lake: 43 of 159 tagged bass returned = 27.0% during opening week
- Smoky Hollow Lake: 46 of 149 tagged bass returned = 30.9% during opening week  
- Bullock Pen Lake: 18 of 66 tagged bass returned = 27.3% during opening week

These percentages substantially exceeded typical seasonal returns from established lakes. For comparison, [[Eschmeyer]] (1942) reported 18.5% yearly returns at [[Norris Lake]], Tennessee; [[Manges]] (1950) reported 18.4% average over 5 years at Norris; [[Fisher]] (1953) reported 19.7% at 5,000-acre [[Millerton Lake]], California.

Long-term returns at Marion County Lake: 6 additional tags returned in 1955, 25 in 1956, totaling 46.5% of originally tagged fish recaptured.

**Temporal Distribution of Catch:**

- First day: 821 bass caught across three lakes = 54% of total first-week catch
- First 30 hours (including Bullock Pen's second day): 1,049 bass = 70% of total first-week catch
- Total first-week catch: 1,509 largemouth bass across three lakes (540 Marion County, 434 Smoky Hollow, 535 Bullock Pen)

**Catch Per Unit Effort Decline:**

Immediate and dramatic reduction in catch rates after opening day demonstrated across all lakes:

*Marion County Lake:*
- Day 1: 1.69 bass/fisherman, 0.90 bass/hour
- Day 2: 0.91 bass/fisherman, 0.48 bass/hour
- Day 4: 0.58 bass/fisherman, 0.48 bass/hour

*Smoky Hollow Lake:*
- Day 1: 2.38 bass/fisherman, 0.65 bass/hour
- Day 2: 1.08 bass/fisherman, 0.27 bass/hour
- Day 4: 0.68 bass/fisherman, 0.21 bass/hour

*Bullock Pen Lake:*
- Day 1: 2.43 bass/fisherman, 0.70 bass/hour
- Day 2: 2.00 bass/fisherman, 0.57 bass/hour
- Day 4: 0.95 bass/fisherman, 0.40 bass/hour

**Evidence of Bass Learning Capacity:**

Shift in vulnerability to artificial versus live baits over the opening week:

*Marion County Lake:*
- Day 1: 296 bass on artificial bait, 100 on live bait
- Day 4: 13 bass on artificial bait, 26 on live bait

*Smoky Hollow Lake:*
- Day 1: 170 bass on artificial bait, 68 on live bait
- Day 4: 26 bass on artificial bait, 37 on live bait

This pattern suggests bass developed distrust of artificial lures and became more selective based on experience, supporting observations by [[Aldrich]] (1938) and [[Bennett]] (1954).

**Recapture Behavior During Tagging:**

- 5 recaptures during 3 days of tagging at Marion County Lake
- 2 recaptures during 2 days at Carter Caves
- 1 recapture during 2 days at Bullock Pen
- Two tagged bass rose to artificial lures and were recaught the same day they were tagged; one crossed Marion County Lake (~400-500 yards) before recapture

**Evidence of Tag-Induced Growth Reduction:**

At Marion County Lake, mean length comparison (9/15-18/55):
- 43 tagged bass recaptured: mean length 10.4 inches
- 60 untagged bass captured: mean length 11.1 inches
- Original 108 bass tagged (7/19/55): mean length 9.9 inches

Tagged bass grew only 0.5 inches versus 1.2 inches for untagged bass over the same period, suggesting jaw tags adversely affected growth rate. This supports findings by [[Pechacek]] (1956) for cold-water fish in Wyoming, though contrasts with [[Cooper and Benson]] (1951) who found minimal growth effects in hatchery trout.

**Avid versus Casual Fishermen Performance:**

Fishermen making >50 trips/year ("avid") versus <50 trips/year ("casual"):

*Marion County Lake:*
- Day 1: Avid 0.86 bass/hour, Casual 0.95 bass/hour (casual slightly better)
- Day 4: Avid 0.61 bass/hour, Casual 0.33 bass/hour (avid nearly double)
- Overall week: Avid 1.61 bass/fisherman and 0.72 bass/hour; Casual 0.96 bass/fisherman and 0.65 bass/hour

*Smoky Hollow Lake:*
- Day 1: Avid 0.78 bass/hour, Casual 0.50 bass/hour
- Day 4: Avid 0.28 bass/hour, Casual 0.13 bass/hour
- Overall week: Avid 1.87 bass/fisherman and 0.51 bass/hour; Casual 0.89 bass/fisherman and 0.26 bass/hour

After opening day, avid fishermen consistently caught significantly more bass, providing further evidence that bass become wary and require greater skill to catch.

**Bluegill Catch:**

Bluegill entered the creel in only token numbers at two lakes due to low stocking densities:
- Marion County Lake: 21 bluegill total (0.05 per fisherman)
- Smoky Hollow Lake: 6 bluegill total (0.03 per fisherman)
- Bullock Pen Lake: 332 bluegill (1.12 per fisherman), representing a small but viable fishery

**Economic Data:**

Cost per fish to anglers (Marion County Lake):
- Day 1: $0.93 per fish, $0.71 per hour
- Day 4: $0.58 per fish, $0.48 per hour
- Week average: $1.32 per fish, $0.79 per hour

**Creel Census Completeness:**

- Marion County Lake: virtually complete (single entrance)
- Smoky Hollow Lake: 71 fishermen escaped first day through back trails; subsequent days fairly complete
- Bullock Pen Lake: only ~50% of fishermen checked due to multiple access points

**Management Recommendations:**

1. **Revised Opening Date:** Open lakes on January 1 following the first spawn of originally stocked largemouth bass rather than during peak fishing season. This would allow fishermen to "trickle out" to the lake, enabling bass to become wary before exposure to intensive fishing pressure, spreading the catch over greater time.

2. **Additional Species:** Given evidence that bass rapidly become difficult to catch, leaving many fishermen unsuccessful, Kentucky should continue experimenting with supplemental species: [[Blue Catfish]] (*Ictalurus furcatus*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus lacustris*), [[White Bass]] (*Morone chrysops*), [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*), and [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*) to find species that either retain catchability or add to inexperienced fishermen's success.

3. **Reduced Opening Day Pressure:** Continue policies of delayed opening times (afternoon rather than morning) and reduced creel limits (5 bass per fisherman) to moderate initial harvest.

**Study Limitations:**

The arbitrary division of avid versus casual fishermen (>50 vs. <50 trips/year) may be crude, as some fishermen may exaggerate trip frequency. However, the division effectively demonstrated substantial differences in catch success between groups. Census completeness varied by lake due to access point configurations, with Bullock Pen data representing only ~50% of actual fishing effort.
