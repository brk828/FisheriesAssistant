# Review of Glen Canyon Environmental Studies for Humpback Chub Recovery

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #dam-operations

## Executive Summary

This document is a review memo from Jim to Minck evaluating ongoing [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] projects focused on [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) recovery. The reviewer assesses research conducted by [[Arizona State University]]/[[Navajo Nation]], [[Arizona Fish and Game Department]], Biowest, and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]-Pine Top. The memo provides critical recommendations for redirecting research priorities to more cost-effectively accomplish recovery of [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Grand Canyon]], particularly in relation to [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations including water temperature, ramping rates, and current velocity modifications.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is an endangered cyprinid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system. Research has demonstrated a good, reproducing population in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), while reproduction elsewhere is apparently limited or non-existent. The species' spawning requirements remain incompletely understood, though conditions in the [[Little Colorado River]] meet these requirements. The adult phase represents the most adaptable life stage with respect to habitat requirements, while early life history stages (larvae, fry, juveniles) are likely more sensitive to environmental conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations significantly influence downstream conditions affecting [[Humpback Chub]] recovery. The dam could potentially be modified by installing an epilimnetic discharge to increase water temperature in the river, and discharge patterns could be altered. These modifications create research questions focused on understanding effects of temperature, ramping rates, and current velocity on the [[Humpback Chub]] population. Habitat variability in [[Grand Canyon]] is probably high enough to provide essential habitat for early life history stages if physical conditions (temperature, current velocity, ramping) are suitable. The reviewer notes that biotic factors affecting survival may become important only after physical conditions are optimized.

## Key Findings

**High Priority Research Recommendations:**

1. **EXPERIMENTAL DETERMINATION OF SPAWNING REQUIREMENTS** - Establish controlled artificial stream studies to determine spawning requirements, effects of temperature on hatching and survival, and response of larvae, fry, and juveniles to current velocity at various temperatures and ramping rates. This approach could most quickly accomplish understanding of spawning requirements compared to field observation alone.

2. **HORMONE INJECTION STUDIES** - Use hormone injection of mature, radio-tagged fish followed by release near suspected suitable spawning locations to yield important information about suitable spawning sites.

3. **OPTIMIZE TAGGING EFFORT** - Evaluate the minimum number of tagged fish necessary to provide essential information on population size and movements. The reviewer doubts that tagging more than about one-third of the population would yield much new information. It is critical to obtain information on population size and movements throughout one complete annual cycle; after establishing an annual pattern, sampling intensity can be decreased.

4. **MACROHABITAT EMPHASIS** - Place more emphasis on macrohabitat than microhabitat determinations for adults. It is more important to know whether [[Humpback Chub]] occupy eddy currents, backwater pools, deep water, or areas near rapids than to determine precise nose velocity preferences.

5. **PROTOCOL STANDARDIZATION** - Develop a workable protocol for hoop net sampling in the [[Little Colorado River]]. The [[Arizona State University]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] hoop net protocols are mutually incompatible, requiring modifications to permit optimum information development without excess fish handling.

6. **REORIENT PROPAGATION WORK** - Reorient work on propagation and early life history to develop basic information in an experimental stream followed by confirmation in the [[Little Colorado River]] and [[Grand Canyon]].

**Research Priority Hierarchy:**

- **First Priority**: Questions directed at defining spawning time, temperature, and location, and effects of temperature on egg development
- **Second Priority**: Factors affecting survival of larvae, fry, and juveniles, particularly temperature, current velocity, and ramping rates
- **Third Priority**: Population size, movements, and macrohabitat occupied through an annual cycle for adult life stages
- **Lower Priority**: Microhabitat occupied by adults (interesting but probably less critical to species survival)

The reviewer concludes it is not inconceivable that dam operation could be modified in ways that would permit establishment of a good [[Humpback Chub]] population throughout [[Grand Canyon]], and that extending the species' range by establishing additional breeding populations depends on understanding spawning requirements.
