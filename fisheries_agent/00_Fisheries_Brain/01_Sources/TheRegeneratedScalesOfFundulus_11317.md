# Regenerated Scales of Fundulus Heteroclitus Linné with a Preliminary Note on Their Formation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fundulus

## Executive Summary

This 1911 paper by Will Scott, reprinted from the *Proceedings of the Indiana Academy of Science*, reports experiments on [[Scale Regeneration]] in the [[Killifish]] ([[Fundulus Heteroclitus]]). Scott removed about six rows of scales from one side of each fish and followed the development of the replacement scales over 25 days. Regenerated scales first showed a cycloid sculpture, and the radiate sculpture of the inner field appeared by day 12. The work was motivated by the use of [[Scale Sculpture]] to read [[Age Determination]] and life-history events in fish, and by the hypothesis that regenerated scales could record injuries. The study was carried out at the [[Woods Hole Laboratory]] of the U.S. Fish Commission. The paper also comments on a conflict over [[Osmotic Pressure]] effects on marine fishes.

## Background & Description

The paper opens with a short review of scale-based fish study:

- **[[Hoffbauer]] (1899)** showed that the age of [[Carp]] could be determined from scale sculpture up to the third year.
- **[[Johnston]] (1905–1910)** showed that the age of [[Atlantic Salmon]] could be read from scale sculpture. Scale marks also recorded the migration of young salmon (parr) from river to sea and each return to spawn as sexually mature adults. The Scottish Fisheries Board supported this work.
- **[[Hutton]] (1910)**, working on scales of [[Wye River]] salmon, noticed unusual scales that he suggested were regenerated. If so, scale examination could reveal wounds and hazards an individual had survived.

Scott chose the Killifish for its abundance and hardiness. Many fish at the time of year of the study (August) were infected with a sporozoan parasite, which was often fatal, so careful selection of specimens was needed.

**Method:**
- Fish were covered with cheesecloth kept wet with sea water, and only a small area was exposed at a time.
- About six rows of scales were lifted from the left side between the posterior end of the dorsal fin and the anal fin, using a scalpel. The inner pocket wall was not to be damaged.
- Noticeable damage to the circulatory system led to sporozoan infection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Holding conditions:** Fish were kept in tanks with running sea water. Tank A held Series A, and tank B held Series B.
- **Parasites:** [[Sporozoan Infection]] was the main cause of mortality. It followed punctures or circulatory injury during surgery and was common in fish at that season.
- **Osmotic conditions:** Scott noted that no deaths could be attributed to the osmotic pressure of sea water. This contradicted [[Garrey]] (1905), who had reported such effects. It partly agreed with [[Sumner]] (1906), who repeated and extended Garrey's work. Scott suggested the conflict could be resolved if sporozoan infection had occurred unrecorded in Garrey's experiments.

## Key Findings

**Experimental series (sample sizes as reported):**

| Series | Date of operation | Fish (length) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | August 8 | 21 fish, 91–114 mm | No fish lost |
| B | August 3 | 12 fish, 72–86 mm | 2 lost (1 sporozoan infection, 1 caught in tank outlet) |
| C | August 4 | 5 fish, fully denuded except a few scales near fin bases | 2 died of sporozoan infection two days later; 3 survived |

Note: The caption for Plate II states that all operations were performed on August 3, which does not match the dates given in the text for Series A and C. The text dates are reported here.

**Regeneration timeline:**
- Scales regenerated to almost normal size within **25 days**. Scott noted that the rate probably varies among species.
- Scales could not be dissected out before **day 6**. At that point they were thin and fragile. The cycloid sculpture showed three irregular but fairly distinct lines enclosing a relatively large unsculptured area.
- The radiate sculpture of the inner field was absent on day 6. Faint traces appeared on **days 8 and 10**, and it was clearly developed by **day 12**.
- From day 12 onward, the sculpture resembled that of the periphery of a normal scale, except that the cycloid lines were slightly farther apart.

**Effect of age and injury:**
- Scales from the largest fish in Series A (114 mm), the smallest in Series B (72 mm), and a fish with all scales removed (Series C) showed no marked difference in differentiation (Plate III).
- Scott concluded that [[Age Determination]] and degree of injury do not greatly influence the regeneration process, if they influence it at all.

**Other conclusions:**
- Regenerated scales show a sequence of sculpture development that could in principle be used to estimate the time since an injury.
- The osmotic-pressure question was left open for further work. Scott stated that the histological study of regeneration had not yet been completed.
