# Age and Growth of Largemouth Bass in Clear Lake, Iowa

## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by J. Douglas Thompson as part of the Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit, examines the age and growth of 281 [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) collected from [[Clear Lake]], Iowa, between 1947 and 1963. Age was determined via the [[Scale Method]] (Van Oosten technique), revealing year classes from 1943–1962 (ages I–IX). The body-scale relationship was linear with a 1.00 inch intercept, and weight scaled to the 3.091 power of length, indicating slight positive allometric growth. The growth curve exceeded the median of 30 comparable populations reported in the literature. No evidence of [[Lee's Phenomenon]] was found, suggesting the sampling methods were not size-selective. Annual growth was correlated with fluctuating water levels (particularly a below-average growth period during 1957–1960 low-water years) but showed no correlation with water temperature or turbidity.

## Background & Description
- **Length measurement conventions:**
  - Total length (TL): snout tip to compressed caudal fin tip
  - Fork length (FL): snout tip to caudal fork
  - Standard length (SL): snout tip to hidden caudal base (natural bending groove)
- **Conversion factors** (based on 82–86 fish, 1.8–20.5 inches):
  - TL = 1.22 × SL
  - TL = 1.04 × FL
  - FL = 1.17 × SL
  - No trend in conversion factors detected across length groups.
- **Body-scale relationship:**
  - Linear regression of total length vs. scale radius (n=114)
  - Slope = 1.496; Intercept = 1.00 inch
  - Correlation coefficient, r = 0.989 (strong linear fit)
  - Scale growth assumed proportional to body growth after the first inch of length.
- **Length-weight relationship** (n=172):
  - Log W = −1.3124 + 3.0910 (Log L)
  - W = weight in hundredths of pounds; L = total length in inches/tenths
  - Slope (3.091) significantly differs from isometric value of 3.00 (t-test, 95% CI), indicating fish become **slightly plumper with increasing length**.
- **Condition factor (C):**
  - Formula: C = (W / L³) × 10⁵
  - Slight increase in mean condition factor observed from 15 to 20 inches in length.
  - Overall condition factor range across size classes: ~50.2–69.2 (means).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Clear Lake (Iowa)]] — a 3,600-acre shallow, eutrophic lake in north-central Iowa with a maximum depth of 20 feet.
- **Population status:** Largemouth bass are described as **not abundant** in Clear Lake, appearing only occasionally in angler catches.
- **Reproduction:** Presence of bass from nearly every year class (1945–1962) despite low overall stocking suggests **at least partially successful natural reproduction annually**.
- **Year class abundance:** Variation in numbers collected likely reflects differences in **fishing/sampling effort** rather than true abundance differences, though the 1946, 1947, and 1951–1957 year classes may have been comparatively more abundant.
- **Environmental correlations:**
  - Annual growth (via Hile's growth indexes) correlated with **water level fluctuations** — below-average growth coincided with a ~30% drop in lake volume (1957–1960).
  - No correlation found between growth indexes/condition factors and **average summer temperature** or **turbidity**.
- **Collection methods:** 30-, 500-, and 3,000-foot seines, gill nets, bag seines, fyke nets, electric shocker, and angling — reflecting a multi-gear, long-term monitoring approach typical of the [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]].

## Key Findings
1. **Age range:** Sampled bass ranged from age I to age IX, spanning year classes 1943–1962.
2. **Growth rate:** The Clear Lake largemouth bass growth curve was **above the median** of 30 other reported water bodies (Carlander, 1953), indicating comparatively fast growth despite low population abundance.
3. **No Lee's Phenomenon:** Absence of this apparent back-calculated growth decline pattern implies the sampling gear/methods did not selectively capture certain size classes, lending confidence to the age-growth data's validity.
4. **Allometric growth:** Weight increased disproportionately faster than length cubed (exponent = 3.091), meaning larger bass are proportionally heavier/plumper — statistically significant deviation from cubic (isometric) growth.
5. **Environmental driver of growth:** Water level was the only tested environmental variable significantly associated with annual growth variation; temperature and turbidity showed no relationship.
6. **Annuli determination challenges:** Distinguishing annuli became progressively harder with fish age; a notable proportion of scales required multiple independent readings (153 read twice, 72 thrice, 18 four times) before consensus, highlighting methodological difficulty in aging older individuals using the [[Scale Method]].
7. **Future research direction:** The study set the stage for continued NSF-funded work on largemouth bass distribution, food habits, parasites, and homing behavior in Clear Lake.
