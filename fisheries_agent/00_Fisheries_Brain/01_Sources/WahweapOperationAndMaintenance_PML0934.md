# Wahweap Operation and Maintenance – Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This project report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers the operation and maintenance of the [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] in Utah, which raises [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] for stocking in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The principal investigators were Wayne Gustaveson and Quentin Bradwisch of the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]]. The project is ongoing. In FY2000 the hatchery stocked or transferred 114,466 bonytail, and the report also documents the rearing of bonytail brood stock and razorback sucker. The document is a fiscal-year accomplishments report with a recommendation to continue ongoing fish culture. It was signed by Quentin Bradwisch on December 5, 2000.

## Background & Description

The project is funded under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] and supports the general recovery program through an action plan. Its stated purpose is to maintain and operate the hatchery so that it can help restore endangered fish populations in the upper Colorado River Basin, which includes the [[Green River]] and the [[Colorado River]].

The hatchery is a state facility in Page, Arizona, that serves the upper basin. Its work includes:

- Rearing bonytail for stocking in the upper basin.
- Rearing razorback sucker.
- Maintaining brood stock: bonytail brood fish and razorback sucker brood fish were held at Wahweap during the reporting period.
- Receiving hatchery-reared fry from other facilities for growout.

The project's relationship to the recovery program is listed under several action-plan items, including IV.A.4 and IV.A.4.a (genetic stocks in refugia), IV.A.5, and IV.C (operate and maintain the facility as approved). The OCR text is partly garbled, so the exact item descriptions should be checked against the original.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Bonytail and razorback sucker are endangered native fishes of the upper Colorado River system. Stocking hatchery fish into the large river is the main method for supplementing and restoring their populations. The report notes that the project's stocking program continues in both Utah and Colorado, and that survival and growth of stocked fish are tracked after release.

Fish that were stocked as fry or juveniles were tracked after release and monitored for growth. Tag retention was checked. The report notes that tag retention was normally good and that regular checks were done. The OCR text is unclear on exact tagging methods and retention rates, so those figures are not given here.

## Key Findings

**Stocking and transfers (FY2000)**
- Wahweap stocked and transferred **114,466 bonytail** in 2000.
- Stocking and transfers were made at several sites:
  - Green River, near Green River State Park.
  - Green River, at Dewey Bridge.
  - Colorado River, near Westwater Bridge (name partly garbled in OCR).
- Dates reported include a stocking on April 25, 2000, a transfer around July 12, 2000, and further transfers in late October to early November 2000.

**Incoming stock**
- Wahweap received **260,000 bonytail** from a national fish hatchery in 2000 (the hatchery name is garbled in the OCR text).
- Fry from the 1999 year class were reared at Wahweap. Over **200,000** of these were later stocked into the Green River.

**Growth and monitoring**
- Growth of the 1999 year class was monitored after stocking.
- The report states that growth increased by an additional 10–15 mm in the 2000 year class. The OCR text is partly unreadable, so this figure should be verified.

**Fish remaining at year end**
- At the end of fall 2000, **14,000 bonytail** (about 160 mm) of the 1999 year class remained at the station.

**Brood stock**
- Bonytail brood stock and razorback sucker brood stock were held over the winter at Wahweap, and this was noted as part of ongoing fish culture.

**Staffing and operations**
- A small crew, described as a four-person crew in part of the OCR text, was used for the fish culture and transport operations. The report also mentions a "[[Hatchery Stocking]]" routine that was run throughout the year.

**Budget**
- The FY2000 budget section is largely unreadable in the OCR text. The only clearly legible figure is that **$0.00** was spent on publication charges.

**Recommendations and status**
- The report recommends continuing ongoing fish culture for [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
