# Laughlin Lagoon Gill Netting and Seine Survey (April 1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A Nevada Department of Wildlife fish biologist, [[Mike Burrell]], ran overnight gill nets and two seine hauls in [[Laughlin Lagoon]] on April 14–15, 1986, to document the fish present. Sixteen fish were captured in gill nets across six species. [[Common Carp]] dominated, making up 7 of 16 fish (43%). The survey confirmed the presence of the endemic [[Razorback Sucker]] and the warm-water game species [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]] and [[Channel Catfish]]. The seine produced more than 1,000 very young fry, which the biologist judged to be largemouth bass, and he concluded that largemouth bass reproduce in the lagoon. The memo is a field trip report and internal correspondence from the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], not a peer-reviewed study. Parts of the handwritten source are illegible, so some values below are uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interdepartmental memorandum with an attached field trip report, signed 4/18/86. The subject line concerns razorback sucker in Laughlin Lagoon.
- **Field party:** Mike Burrell, Fish Biologist.
- **Survey purpose:** Fish sampling ("Fish Desutétt Sampling," probably "Fish Distribution Sampling" after OCR correction).
- **Methods:**
  - [[Gill Netting]]: Nets were set overnight, with a total of 12 hours per net. Net locations are shown on a diagram that is not included in the text.
  - [[Seine Hauling]]: Two seine hauls were made.
- **Water temperature:** Recorded as "679" in the scan, most likely 67.9 °F (OCR ambiguous).
- **Catch detail:** Individual fish were recorded by net number, species, fork length, total length, and weight in pounds and ounces. The record table lists about 17 individual entries, including several sucker, bass, carp and bluegill records. Only the summary is reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The lagoon supports a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fish. Warm-water game species and a non-game introduced species (carp) share the habitat with the endemic razorback sucker.
- The biologist described the lagoon as providing habitat for warm-water game species, including largemouth bass, bluegill and channel catfish.
- Water temperature was reported as warm, which is consistent with the warm-water species found.
- The early-stage fry were observed within two feet of an adult largemouth bass, a pattern consistent with parental guarding, although the memo does not explicitly describe nest guarding.

## Key Findings

**Catch summary (gill nets, 16 fish total):**

| Species | Number | % Composition | Fish per Net Hour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Largemouth Bass | 3 | 19% | 0.25 |
| Channel Catfish | 1 | 6% | 0.08 |
| Bluegill | 2 | 13% | 0.17 |
| Razorback Sucker | 2 | 13% | 0.17 |
| Sucker (unidentified, probably flannelmouth) | 1 | 6% | 0.08 |
| Common Carp | 7 | 43% | 0.58 |

- The biologist described common carp as the most numerous inhabitant of the lagoon by both number and biomass.
- Total weight is partly illegible in the scan. The summary weight figures are unreliable and are not reported here.
- The unidentified sucker was probably a [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], but this was not confirmed.

**Seine results:**
- The first haul produced no fish.
- The second haul produced more than 1,000 fry, believed to be [[Largemouth Bass]]. The fry were in a tight ball within two feet of an adult largemouth. They were at a very early developmental stage, which made identification difficult.

**Conclusions stated by the biologist:**
1. Laughlin Lagoon provides habitat for largemouth bass, bluegill and channel catfish.
2. The endemic razorback sucker is present in the lagoon, which is significant for its conservation.
3. Largemouth bass reproduce in the lagoon. This rests on the seine observation and on fingerling bass seen in past years. The biologist considered the fry ball next to an adult to be "ample evidence" of reproduction.
4. Common carp is the most abundant species in the lagoon.

**Limitations:** The sample is small (16 fish from two nets set overnight, plus two seine hauls). Fry identification was uncertain. The document is a field report and does not include statistical analysis or a formal management plan.
