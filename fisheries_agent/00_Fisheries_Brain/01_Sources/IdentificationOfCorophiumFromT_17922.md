# Identification of Corophium from the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Corophiidae

## Executive Summary
This 1967 taxonomic note, authored by D. W. Kelley based on identifications by Charles R. Hazel (California Department of Fish and Game), addresses the correct identification of two amphipod species — *Corophium spinicorne* and *Corophium stimpsoni* — collected during a zoobenthos survey of the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. Although not fish themselves, both amphipod species are highlighted as ecologically significant because they serve as an important food source for bottom-feeding fishes in the Delta. The paper provides a morphological identification key to distinguish these two species (and briefly lists four additional congeners) to support accurate ecological monitoring and future benthic survey work.

## Background & Description
The identification key is based on features of the antennae, head, and urosome:

**Primary diagnostic split — Urosome:**
- **Group A:** Segments of the urosome **separate** (applies to *C. spinicorne* and *C. stimpsoni*)
- **Group AA:** Segments of the urosome **fused into a single plate** (applies to *C. acherusicum*, *C. insidiosum*, *C. brevis*, *C. oaklandense* — not further differentiated in this key)

**[[Corophium spinicorne]]:**
- Setae on antennae **profuse**
- Front margin of head **smooth, rounded, convex**; no rostrum; does not protrude forward
- **No spine** on the first peduncular joint of the first antennae in males

**[[Corophium stimpsoni]]:**
- Setae on antennae **common but not profuse**
- Front margin of head in **both sexes protrudes forward**, evenly rounded, but lacks a distinct rostrum
- **Spine present** on the inside of the first peduncular joint of the first antennae in males

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat differentiation:**
  - *C. spinicorne* — a **tube-dwelling** species found on **solid substrate**.
  - *C. stimpsoni* — abundant in **fine sand** or a **mixture of fine and medium sand**.
- Both species occur within the **zoobenthos** community of the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]], a key estuarine system in California.
- Both species are described as **abundant** and function as important **prey items for bottom-feeding fishes**, linking benthic invertebrate community composition directly to fish diet and trophic ecology in the Delta.
- This work is situated within a broader ecological survey program: [[Ecological Studies of the Sacramento-San Joaquin Estuary]] (Kelley, compiler, 1966), part of California Department of Fish and Game Fish Bulletin 133.

## Key Findings
- Two *Corophium* species — *C. spinicorne* and *C. stimpsoni* — were identified as abundant components of the Delta zoobenthos and are ecologically important as fish food resources.
- A simple morphological key was developed to reliably distinguish these two species based on antennal setae density, head margin shape, and presence/absence of a male peduncular spine, since prior identifications risked confusion between similar-looking species.
- Habitat segregation exists between the two species: *C. spinicorne* occupies solid substrates as a tube-dweller, while *C. stimpsoni* prefers sandy sediments.
- The urosome segmentation (separate vs. fused) serves as the primary higher-level diagnostic character separating these two species from four other West Coast *Corophium* species (*C. acherusicum*, *C. insidiosum*, *C. brevis*, *C. oaklandense*), which are listed but not keyed out in detail.
- The paper underscores the importance of correct amphipod taxonomy for accuracy in future benthic and fish food-web studies in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]].
- Builds on earlier taxonomic work on the genus *Corophium* along the West Coast of America by [[Shoemaker]] (1941, 1947, 1949).
