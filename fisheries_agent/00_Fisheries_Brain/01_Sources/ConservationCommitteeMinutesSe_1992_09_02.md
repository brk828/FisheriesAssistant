## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Cuatro Ciénegas]] Basin (Central Coahuila District, Mexico)**: Described as a critical endemism hotspot. This spring-fed desert wetland complex is well known in aquatic ecology for harboring numerous endemic fish, invertebrate, and reptile taxa in isolated pools and channels — consistent with the endemism of the box turtle discussed here.
- **[[Cahuilan Box Turtle]]**: Occupies aquatic/semi-aquatic microhabitats within the Cuatro Ciénegas wetland system. Listed as:
  - **Endangered** under Mexican federal law.
  - **CITES Appendix I** (highest protection level, prohibiting international commercial trade).
- **Research methodology noted**: Field researcher Francisco Soberón Mobarak (under supervision of Rolando Mendoza, Museum of Zoology, National University of Mexico/UNAM) is conducting **radio-telemetry tagging** of turtles in this aquatic habitat to study movement and habitat use, requiring ~20 mini-radio transmitters and receivers (~$2,600 total cost).
- **Fishes of Mexico project (Dr. Robert Rush Miller)**: Represents a broader ichthyofaunal survey/taxonomic effort for Mexican freshwater systems, implying ongoing concern for aquatic biodiversity and endemism parallel to the Cuatro Ciénegas case, though no specific fish taxa or habitats are detailed in this document.
