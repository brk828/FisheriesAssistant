# Correspondence and Curriculum Vitae: Salvador Contreras Balderas (1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation #Mexican-fauna #research-infrastructure

## Executive Summary

This document comprises correspondence dated December 19, 1990, from Dr. Salvador Contreras Balderas ([[Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon]], Faculty of Biological Sciences) to Dr. W.L. Minckley ([[Arizona State University]]), along with Dr. Contreras Balderas's comprehensive curriculum vitae. The letter addresses critical infrastructure needs for ichthyological fieldwork and collection maintenance in Mexico following changes in national grant policies. The CV documents an extensive career (1936-1990) in Mexican ichthyology, herpetology, and aquatic ecology, including founding roles in graduate programs, species recovery plans, and conservation initiatives focused on [[Rio Grande Basin]] fishes and the endemic biota of [[Cuatro Cienegas]].

## Background & Description

Dr. Salvador Contreras Balderas (born February 19, 1936, Mexico City) earned his biology degree from [[Universidad de Nuevo Leon]] (1957-1961) with a thesis on the ichthyofauna of the [[Rio San Juan]]. He completed M.S. (1962-1966) and Ph.D. (1975) degrees at [[Tulane University]], with his doctoral dissertation examining zoogeography and evolution of *Notropis lutrensis* and *Notropis ornatus* (Pisces: Cyprinidae) in the Rio Grande Basin. His career centered at the Faculty of Biological Sciences, UANL, where he served as full-time professor since 1965, founded the Division of Graduate Studies (1975), and established the Institute of Fisheries Ecology and Wildlife (1984). He founded ichthyological (1965) and herpetological (1965) collections at UANL and co-founded the ornithological collection (1971).

The correspondence reveals that Mexican grant conditions changed dramatically by 1990, no longer providing vehicles, furniture, computers, or large equipment—only running expenses. The institution's field vehicles had been non-functional for three years without replacement, creating a critical impediment to continued research.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Dr. Contreras Balderas's research focused extensively on arid-zone aquatic ecosystems of northern Mexico, particularly:

- **[[Cuatro Cienegas]] Valley, Coahuila**: Endemic biota, environmental impacts, and conservation (multiple publications 1974-1989)
- **[[Rio Grande]] (Rio Bravo) Basin**: Ichthyofaunal surveys, community composition changes, and zoogeography across Mexican tributaries including [[Rio Conchos]], [[Rio Salado]], [[Rio San Juan]], [[Rio Alamo]], and [[Rio Soto La Marina]]
- **Interior drainage basins**: [[Nazas River]], [[Aguanaval River]], [[Parras Basin]] ichthyofauna
- **Coastal systems**: [[Laguna Madre]] (Tamaulipas), [[Bahia de La Paz]] (Baja California Sur)

His work documented anthropogenic impacts including species introductions, habitat degradation, water pollution, and community retrogression in semi-arid Mexican aquatic systems. He served as Chihuahuan Desert (Mexico) Area Coordinator for the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (1973-1988) and participated extensively in [[Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team]] activities (1978-1983).

## Key Findings

**Infrastructure Crisis (1990)**:
- Mexican research grants no longer fund field vehicles, major equipment, furniture, or computers
- UANL field vehicles inoperative for 3 years without replacement
- Critical need identified for Chevrolet Silverado-type crew vehicles or 4-wheel drive pickups
- Collection maintenance funding inadequate
- Request for assistance identifying funding sources for field/lab equipment and general expenses

**Career Accomplishments**:
- **Publications**: Over 36 peer-reviewed scientific papers (1967-1988), numerous technical reports, and popular articles
- **Species descriptions**: Multiple new species including *Algansea monticola* (1968), *Notropis aguirrepequenoi* (1973), *Dionda mandibularis* (1976), *Bramocharax caballeroi* (1985), and *Xiphophorus* sp. (1985)
- **Recovery plans**: Authored or co-authored U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service recovery plans for [[Big Bend Gambusia]] (*Gambusia gaigei*), [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon bovinus*), [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]], [[Clear Creek Gambusia]], [[Pecos Gambusia]] (1981-1984), and [[Pecos Shiner]] (*Notropis simus*, 1989)
- **Graduate education**: Founded doctoral curricula in Aquatic Ecology and Fisheries, and Microbiology at UANL (1983-1985); directed 33 bachelor's theses, 4 master's theses, and 1 doctoral dissertation (through 1990)

**Professional Leadership**:
- President, [[Sociedad Mexicana de Zoologia]] (1981-1983)
- President, [[Desert Fishes Council]] (1982-1984)
- Founding President, [[Sociedad Ictiologica Mexicana]] (1986-1990)
- Member, [[Sistema Nacional de Investigadores]] (National System of Researchers), Level I (1984-1990)
- Extensive committee service including endangered species committees for [[American Fisheries Society]] and editorial boards

**Conservation Documentation**:
- Published annotated lists of Mexican freshwater fishes in danger of extinction (1984, 1987)
- Documented exotic fish distributions and impacts in Mexico (1984)
- Monitored fish community changes and retrogression in northern Mexican biotopes (1975-1983)
- Emphasized environmental impacts of hydraulic works through technical reports to Mexican water resources agencies

The CV demonstrates sustained productivity despite infrastructure limitations, with research spanning systematics, zoogeography, ecology, conservation biology, and applied fisheries across diverse Mexican aquatic ecosystems from 1959 through 1990.
