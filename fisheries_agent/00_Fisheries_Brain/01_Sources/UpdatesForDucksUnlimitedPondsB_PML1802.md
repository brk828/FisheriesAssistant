# Lower Colorado River Native Fish Program Updates, 2004

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #centrarchidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This USFWS Mountain-Prairie/Region 2 fisheries update (C.O. Minckley and M.J. Thorson, USFWS-MFRO.PA-05-003) was presented to the [[Lower Colorado River Native Fish Group]] in October 2004 in Laughlin, Nevada. It reports on the restoration of endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] at several sites: [[Duck's Unlimited Ponds]], [[Beal Lake]], [[High Levee Pond]], [[Office Cove]], and [[Lake Havasu]]. The main issues were extreme summer water temperatures and low dissolved oxygen in the ponds, caused by [[Submerged Macrophytes]] and other biological oxygen demand, and the discovery of [[Largemouth Bass]], a nonnative predator, in High Levee Pond. Management responses included timed water exchange, planned herbicide and aeration work, and continued stocking.

## Background & Description

The [[Duck's Unlimited Ponds]] system covers roughly 40 acres and consists of five ponds ranging from about 4 to 14 acres. The system was renovated and stocked with fingerling Razorback Sucker in 2002–2003. Nonnative fish were soon found and the system was renovated again to remove them. [[Pond 1]] was targeted for a second renovation and restocking, completed in 2003. In April 2004, 10,000 fingerling Razorback Sucker were introduced into Pond 1, and the other ponds were isolated from it. A May 2004 population estimate found 6,573 Razorback Sucker remaining in Pond 1 after 30 days.

The document also summarizes the status of other sites:

- **Beal Lake:** Water quality was measured quarterly in 2004. No fish collections were made that year.
- **High Levee Pond:** Largemouth Bass were first found in May 2004 and have since reproduced.
- **Office Cove:** Planned for renovation in 2005 as a grow-out site for Razorback Sucker for Lake Havasu.
- **Lake Havasu:** Part of a larger stocking program for Razorback Sucker and [[Bonytail]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

In 2003 Duck's Unlimited ponds had water temperatures above 30°C and oxygen depletion. The causes were submerged macrophyte respiration and other biological oxygen demand.

**Sampling methods:** Water quality was measured bimonthly in Pond 1 (June–October) and Pond 2 (July–October). Three sites were sampled in each pond, taking readings at 1-meter intervals from surface to bottom. Most sites were 1.5 m deep or less, and none exceeded 2.0 m. Samples were taken in late afternoon and at or before dawn. Parameters measured were water temperature, depth, conductivity, pH, turbidity, and dissolved oxygen. The summary reports only temperature and oxygen.

**Management intervention:** A timer was installed on the well supplying the ponds. Water was introduced between 0000 and 0600 hours from 16 July through September. The schedule was changed to 0000–0400 hours on 15 September, and the practice was stopped on 30 September.

**Fish collections in Pond 1:** Fish were collected using an experimental multi-mesh net set in late afternoon and checked at dawn. Captured fish were weighed and measured and then released back into the pond. The document reports no nonnative fish from Pond 1 in these collections.

**Observed patterns (2004):**
- Water temperatures and oxygen levels in both ponds were strongly affected by the timed inflow and by [[Dissolved Oxygen]] changes at different depths and times of day.
- Dawn readings were generally lower in oxygen than afternoon readings, and surface–bottom differences were noted.
- Wind mixing did not appear to offset the effects of macrophyte respiration on temperature or oxygen.
- Oxygen concentrations recovered after inflow was increased, but the exact values were not clearly legible in the source.

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker growth and resilience**
- The average total length of Razorback Sucker in Pond 1 increased from about 170 mm in June to about 400 mm in October 2004.
- Sample sizes varied strongly between collection dates and were small overall, so the document interprets this as a general summer growth trend.
- The authors consider this growth evidence of the species' resilience under less-than-optimal conditions.

**Plans for FY2005**
- Continue water quality and fish monitoring: monthly from November to May, and bimonthly from June to October.
- Continue summer water introduction into Pond 1.
- Install a solar-powered [[Aerator]] in Pond 2.
- Reduce submerged macrophyte growth in Ponds 1 and 2 with the herbicide [[Sonar Herbicide]].
- Evaluate [[Bioremediation]] methods to reduce the biomass of organic material in Ponds 1 and 2.
- Conduct a population estimate for Pond 1 in November 2004.
- Renovate Pond 2 in winter–spring 2004–2005 and stock fingerling Razorback Sucker there in spring 2005.
- Discuss modifying Ponds 3–4, and possibly the whole system, with refuge staff and the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR). Ponds 3 and 4 were not monitored during this period.

**High Levee Pond: Largemouth Bass**
- Largemouth Bass were discovered in May 2004 and have reproduced.
- The annual monitoring survey was scheduled for 27 October 2004. All fish were to be measured, weighed, and marked by clipping or [[PIT Tagging]].
- Research planned for January 2005 focuses on native fish and on the impacts of coexistence with Largemouth Bass.
- The working hypothesis is that smaller native fish will decline sharply and recruitment will cease. If confirmed, and the native population becomes only large, non-recruiting adults, the system will be renovated and restarted in 2006.

**Beal Lake**
- Water quality data for 2004 are available and monitoring will continue quarterly.
- A survey was planned before April 2005.
- USBR was renovating the inflow structure, with completion expected in late winter or spring 2005.
- Restocking of Razorback Sucker from [[Lake Mohave]], reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], would follow renovation.

**Lake Havasu Fisheries Project**
- The goal of introducing 30,000 larger Razorback Sucker (over 300 mm) into Lake Havasu was met. The document notes that the total number introduced to date is not legible in the source.
- An estimated 3,000–5,000 fish were scheduled for release in 2005, pending harvest of current production at Dexter and [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]].
- No Razorback Sucker were taken by anglers from Lake Havasu in 2004.
- The Razorback Sucker roundup was scheduled for February. The Bonytail roundup was scheduled for spring (April–May) and was combined with the Razorback roundup to reduce the need for electrofishing.
- A proposal was submitted to the [[Central Utah Project]] for a [[Radio Telemetry]] study of Razorback Sucker in Lake Havasu for 2005–2006.
