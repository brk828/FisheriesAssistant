# Lake Mead Razorback Sucker Studies: 2001-2002 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This sixth-year annual report (July 2001 to June 2002) from [[BIO-WEST, Inc.]] for the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]] documents the continuing study of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mead]], Nevada and Arizona. The study, begun in 1996, sought to estimate population size, describe habitat use and life history, and document known spawning areas at [[Echo Bay]] and [[Las Vegas Bay]], and to search for new populations near the [[Colorado River Inflow Area]].

During the sixth year, 31 adults and 1 sub-adult were captured (18 in Las Vegas Bay, 14 in Echo Bay as reported in the summary; 32 total fish, 14 recaptures). The most notable result was a 617 mm female first tagged by [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]] in 1993 at Las Vegas Bay and recaptured at Echo Bay in 2002, the first fish documented at both sites. Nineteen fish had fin-ray sections removed for aging, bringing the total aged to 40. Lake elevation fell about 21 feet over the year (approximately 1,181 to 1,160 ft amsl), and this decline dried the historic [[Echo Bay Spawning Site]], shifting spawning to a new location on the south shore.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endemic large-river fish of the [[Colorado River Basin]], listed as endangered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in 1991. Its decline is attributed to mainstem dams, altered cool-water habitat, and predation and competition from non-native species such as [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Common Carp]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. Reservoir populations were historically dominated by long-lived adults that recruited only during the first years after impoundment, and they typically disappeared 40 to 50 years after reservoir creation.

[[Lake Mead]] was formed in 1935 with the closure of [[Hoover Dam]]. Razorback sucker were common through the 1950s and 1960s, declined in the 1970s, and were not collected by NDOW or the Arizona Game and Fish Department from 1980 to 1989. Following reports from anglers in 1990, NDOW collected 61 fish from 1990 to 1996 (34 at Blackbird Point, 27 at Echo Bay). Pond-reared fish were stocked in 1994 (26 at Las Vegas Bay) and 1995 (14 at Echo Bay), all PIT-tagged.

The study was funded by SNWA and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], with support from NDOW, the [[National Park Service]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. A 1998 cooperative agreement added objectives to search for new populations via larval light-trapping and to enhance juvenile sampling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake level.** Lake elevation rose to a high of 1,218 ft amsl in October 1998, then declined. It fell from roughly 1,181 ft in July 2001 to about 1,160 ft by June 2002, the lowest of the study. By the 2002 spawning season the western quarter of Echo Bay was exposed, leaving about 200 ft between the water's edge and the former spawning site, and water levels were 31 ft below the 1996 level and 56 ft below the 1998 high.

**Habitat change.** Sediment from [[Las Vegas Wash]] extended the wash delta into Las Vegas Bay, and the wash was estimated to deposit about 65 tons of sediment per day (T. Porta, personal communication). Inundated shoreline vegetation (bulrush, tamarisk, cattail, cottonwood) declined to very little by the sixth year, leaving only spiny naiad as submergent cover. Sand berms cut off Pearce Ferry Bay and Grand Wash Bay from the Colorado River, limiting boat access.

**Non-native fishes.** Fyke netting captured green sunfish, bluegill, largemouth bass, channel catfish, threadfin shad, striped bass, and common carp. Green sunfish dominated both bays (58.0% at Las Vegas Bay; 54.5% at Echo Bay). No juvenile razorback sucker were captured.

## Key Findings

**Population and capture**
- 31 adult and 1 sub-adult (407 mm) razorback sucker captured; 18 in Las Vegas Bay and 14 in Echo Bay; 14 of 32 total were recaptures.
- Recapture rates: 33.3% at Las Vegas Bay and 50.0% at Echo Bay.
- Population estimates (2001-2002, Model Mo): Echo Bay 45 to 66 fish; Las Vegas Bay 68 to 124 fish, with high year-to-year variability.
- Catch-per-effort (CPE) at Echo Bay fell from about 0.85 in year 1 to 0.22 in year 6. Las Vegas Bay CPE was 0.23 in year 6, up from 0.14 the previous year.
- Trammel netting effort totalled 188 net nights (76 Las Vegas Bay/Boulder Basin, 67 Echo Bay/Overton Arm, 45 Driftwood Island Bay/Colorado River inflow).

**Spawning and larvae**
- Echo Bay larval catch was 1,022 in 2002 (987 in 2001), with CPM of 1.67; Las Vegas Bay had 176 larvae (CPM 0.18).
- Spawning at Echo Bay moved from the dry cliff site to two coves on the south shore, showing spawning-site flexibility.
- No larvae were captured in the Colorado River inflow area in 2002, after 11 larvae in 2000 and 22 in 2001. Declining levels may have caused adults to abandon the area.
- The one "razorback" larva captured in the inflow area was identified as a flannelmouth sucker.

**Growth and age**
- Mean annual growth of recaptured fish was 10.9 mm (Las Vegas Bay 11.3 mm; Echo Bay 10.5 mm). These rates are far above those of other populations (about 2.0 mm/yr in Lake Mohave and the Green River).
- Fish recaptured within short intervals often showed negative growth, possibly due to netting or handling stress.
- Ages of the 19 fish aged in 2002 ranged from 6 to about 35 years. Aged fish were spawned between 1978 and 1996, with the greatest recruitment from 1985 to 1987.

**Telemetry**
- Four Floyd Lamb pond-reared fish were sonic-tagged and released at Driftwood Island Bay in January 2002. Their locations were lost after the lake dropped 8 to 10 ft, and two fish stopped moving at the same site.
- Average depths of tagged fish increased relative to previous years (70 ft vs. 56 and 51 ft for fish 79; 40 ft vs. 23 and 30 ft for fish 247), because shallow habitat was lost.

**Hypothesis on recruitment**
- Reduced annual lake-level fluctuation after the filling of [[Lake Powell]] (mid-1960s) may have let shoreline vegetation establish and then be inundated, providing cover for young razorback sucker. The authors suggest this combination of low annual and large multi-year fluctuations drives recruitment.

**Work plan for 2002-2003**
- Quantify spawning substrate with the Wolman pebble count method.
- Identify spawning areas at all three study sites through larval light trapping and tracking of four newly tagged fish.
- Describe adult habitat use at low lake levels.
- Test whether fish use Pearce Ferry Bay.
- Identify cover features in bays and coves that support recruitment by reviewing [[NDOW Videotape Surveys]].
