# English and Metric Length-Weight Relationships for Steelhead Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #hatchery-management #biometrics

## Executive Summary

This technical reference document, published September 11, 1970, as part of the Manual of Fish Culture (Section A, Part 3), provides comprehensive length-weight conversion tables for [[Steelhead Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) reared in hatchery conditions. The tables enable fish culturists to estimate fish weights from length measurements and calculate population inventories. Based on empirical data from [[Dworshak National Fish Hatchery]] in Ahsahka, Idaho, the document uses a condition factor of 0.0003405 specific to steelhead trout. The reference covers fish ranging from approximately 1 inch to over 10 inches in length, with both English (inches/pounds) and metric (centimeters/grams) units provided.

## Background & Description

The document establishes the fundamental growth equation for trout under hatchery conditions: **W = KL³**, where W represents weight in pounds, L represents length in inches, and K is the condition factor relating body height and width to length. According to Haskell (1959), normal trout growth under constant water temperature and adequate food supply exhibits a constant rate of increase in length, except during periods when metabolism is altered by factors such as disease or spawning.

The condition factor K = 0.0003405 was determined empirically from [[Steelhead Trout]] populations at Dworshak National Fish Hatchery. This factor is species-specific; the document explicitly states that other salmonid species with different condition factors require separate tables. The body form of hatchery trout remains constant until sexual maturity, allowing the tables to be extrapolated beyond the printed range by moving decimal points: for fish smaller than 1 inch, multiply columns 1 and 3 by 1000 and divide columns 2 and 5 by 10; for fish larger than 10 inches, divide columns 1 and 3 by 1000 and multiply columns 2 and 5 by 10.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The tables reflect hatchery-reared [[Steelhead Trout]] under controlled conditions at [[Dworshak National Fish Hatchery]], located in Ahsahka, Idaho. Hatchery environments provide constant water temperatures and adequate food supply, conditions that differ substantially from wild populations. The constant condition factor assumes fish maintain consistent body proportions throughout growth, which holds true for immature hatchery fish but may not apply to sexually mature individuals or wild populations experiencing variable environmental conditions.

The document represents standard fish culture practices of the early 1970s under the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, Division of Fish Hatcheries. These tables served as operational tools for hatchery managers to estimate biomass, calculate feeding rates, and plan stocking schedules without requiring individual weighing of large numbers of fish.

## Key Findings

**Length-Weight Conversion Tables**: The document provides extensive tabular data spanning 55 pages with the following columns:
- Weight of 1000 fish (pounds)
- Length (inches)
- Fish per pound (number)
- Weight (grams)
- Length (centimeters)
- Fish per kilogram (number)

**Sample Data Points**:
- At 1.0000 inches: weight of 1000 fish = 0.3405 pounds; 2936.857 fish per pound
- At 5.0000 inches: weight of 1000 fish = 2.2680 pounds; 200.000 fish per pound
- At 10.000 inches: weight of 1000 fish = 45.3592 pounds; 10.000 fish per pound

**Computational Precision**: Tables provide a minimum of four significant figures for computational purposes, though the document recommends rounding final answers to three figures. The fish-per-pound and fish-per-kilogram columns contain superfluous significant figures due to the wide range of magnitudes.

**Condition Factor Application**: The specific condition factor K = 0.0003405 applies only to [[Steelhead Trout]]. The document emphasizes that other trout species require different tables based on their respective condition factors, reflecting species-specific morphometric differences.

**Practical Applications**: The tables enable hatchery managers to:
- Estimate total biomass from length measurements
- Calculate daily length increases for growth monitoring
- Determine lot inventories without individual weighing
- Plan feeding schedules based on estimated weights
- Optimize stocking densities and schedules

**Limitations**: The relationship holds for immature hatchery fish under constant environmental conditions. Sexual maturity, disease, spawning activity, or variable environmental conditions may alter the condition factor and reduce table accuracy. Wild populations likely exhibit different length-weight relationships due to variable food availability and environmental stressors.
