# Mohave - Truly a Fisherman's Heaven

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This popular-press article describes [[Lake Mohave]], a 67-mile reservoir formed when [[Davis Dam]] was built to back up the [[Colorado River]] below [[Hoover Dam]]. The lake supports a sport fishery for [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] (the species is not specified in the text), along with crappie, bluegill and channel catfish. The article attributes the emergence of a trout fishery to a thermal stratification created by cold water released from Hoover Dam, and it reports exceptional catches in late 1962. It also surveys the recreation resorts around the lake. The document is anecdotal and promotional rather than research-based; it contains no formal sampling data.

## Background & Description

- **Lake setting:** The reservoir is described as elongated and deep, with sandy bars, coves and rocky points. Its shoreline is noted for scenery, sandy beaches and suitability for boating and water-skiing.
- **Origin:** Davis Dam was constructed to back up the Colorado River 67 miles below Hoover Dam, creating the lake.
- **Forage base:** [[Threadfin Shad]] are described as a forage fish growing to a maximum of eight inches. The article credits them with supporting the large and plentiful trout and bass populations.
- **Bait and lures:** Spinner-type lures, particularly the "Flatfish," were the most popular for trout. Traditional baits such as cheese, doughballs, corn and marshmallows also took trout. [[Waterdog]] (bait, identity not specified in the text) was described as the best bet for bass and was also popular for trout.
- **Fishing methods:** Trolling over sandy bars was the most popular method. Anglers also located trout by watching for concentrations of birds, which indicated feeding activity on freshwater shrimp and shad.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal stratification:** Water drawn from the bottom of Hoover Dam, from [[Lake Mead]], enters upper Lake Mohave about 10 degrees cooler, at 55°F, than the rest of the lake. This creates a visible "cold water front" near El Dorado Landing, below the [[Willow Beach Fish Hatchery]].
- **Habitat zones:**
  - Above the cold water front and in the deep original river channel: trout habitat, with the largest trout concentrations between the front and Hoover Dam.
  - Below the front, in warmer water: bass habitat. Lake Mohave is deep enough to provide wintering areas for bass, and its coves and rocky points give bass summer feeding grounds.
- **Seasonal movement:** Trout start moving down the lake in October and November and reach full lake distribution in early January.
- **Tailwater:** The Colorado River just below Davis Dam is regularly stocked with creel-sized trout. Lunker trout are taken in the swirling water near the dam, where they feed on fish remnants passing through the gates.
- **Recreation conflict:** The article states that fishing and water-skiing do not conflict, because the good fishing spots are not good skiing spots.

## Key Findings

- **Trout fishery emergence:** The article states that Lake Mohave was not a known trout fishery until catches began to appear in fall 1961. Stringers of good-sized trout appeared at [[Cottonwood Cove]] and Willow Beach that year.
- **Record season, 1962:** November 1962 was described as the best trout fishing ever on the lake.
  - On Thursday, November 8, no fewer than 42 trout, all over two pounds, were taken from one point visible from the Cottonwood Cove landing.
  - A week earlier, two limits of trout weighing roughly 2¾ to 4¾ pounds were brought into Lake Mohave Resort. A few days earlier, a limit included a 5¾-pound fish.
  - Reported large individual fish include a 6¾-pound trout taken by Mrs. Bill Davis on a waterdog in early November, an 8¾-pound trout caught from shore just downstream of Cottonwood Cove, and a 9½-pound trout caught on a waterdog. (OCR-read weights were corrected to the most likely fractions.)
  - One party took eighteen rainbows from Cottonwood Cove in a single morning.
- **Bass fishing:** Bass fishing in the warmer lower section is described as "out-of-this-world," with five-pound fish considered routine. The article does not provide catch statistics for bass.
- **Predictions:** Two years before the article, angler [[Jess Carrington]] predicted in *Arizona Wildlife* that Lake Mohave would become a great trout lake.
- **Resort development:** Four resorts are described, each with management changes or improvements:
  - **Lake Mohave Resort**, just above Davis Dam in Katherine Wash, managed by Ham Pratt. A restaurant and cocktail lounge were under construction, and it is described as the most popular skiing port on the lake.
  - **Cottonwood Cove**, 15 miles from Searchlight, Nevada, off U.S. 95 and 24 miles upstream of Davis Dam, managed by Bob Cole. It has a trailer park, boat dock and a public campground.
  - **Eldorado Landing**, renamed Crazy Canoe Cove by new operators Glenn and Marianne Massey, who plan improvements to attract skiers and boaters as well as anglers.
  - **Willow Beach**, ten miles below Hoover Dam, managed by Earl Gilmore. Modern boat slips and a restaurant were under construction, and a bay was dredged for new docks. The article predicts it will become one of the most popular fishing spots in Arizona.
