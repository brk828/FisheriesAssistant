# Evaluation of the effects of electrofishing on the native and endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin

## Executive Summary
This FY93 Annual Project Report (Colorado River Recovery Program, Project #29) documents a methodological study assessing whether **electrofishing**, a widely used capture technique for [[Endangered Colorado River Fishes]], causes injury or sublethal harm. The investigation was prompted by concerns raised in the fishery science literature (Snyder 1992) that electrofishing-induced injuries — particularly in salmonids — may be internal, difficult to detect, and more prevalent than historically recognized. Given decades of electrofishing use (since the 1960s) to monitor [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] endangered fishes, the study leverages historical **tag/recapture databases (1974–1992)** to statistically compare recapture rates and growth rates between fish initially captured via electrofishing versus alternative capture methods. The project experienced significant schedule delays due to contractual funding issues, pushing the final report to February 1994.

## Background & Description
This report does not describe specific morphological traits of the target species (no length, weight, meristic, or coloration data are presented). Instead, the relevant "morphological" concern is **physiological/traumatic injury**, specifically:
- **Internal injuries** potentially induced by electrical current exposure, which may not present with external, visually obvious wounds.
- Injury detection is proposed to require advanced diagnostic methods (e.g., **X-ray imaging**) rather than visual inspection alone, since recapture-record analysis alone may fail to reveal sublethal trauma.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], a system supporting multiple federally listed endangered fish species subject to long-term monitoring under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]].
- **Sampling ecology**: Electrofishing is the dominant capture technique used since the 1960s for population monitoring, research, and radiotelemetry tagging of endangered fishes in this basin.
- **Human interaction/management context**: The study sits within the "General Recovery Program Support Action Plan," under the objective to "establish sampling procedures to minimize adverse impacts to endangered fishes" (Section V.D.1), reflecting a conservation-management niche rather than a purely biological one.
- Long-term individual monitoring via **radiotransmitters** indicates some tagged fish have been tracked for multiple years post-electrofishing capture, suggesting general survivability, though sublethal effects remain understudied.

## Key Findings
- **Study Objective 1**: Determine whether endangered fish initially captured by electrofishing show significantly different recapture rates compared to fish captured by other techniques.
- **Study Objective 2**: Determine whether electrofishing-captured fish exhibit reduced growth rates relative to fish captured by alternative methods.
- **Literature basis**: Snyder (1992) — *Impacts of Electrofishing on Fish* (Larval Fish Laboratory Contribution 56) — is cited as the key review raising concern over previously underappreciated internal, sublethal injuries in salmonids from electrofishing, motivating this investigation in non-salmonid endangered [[Colorado River Fishes]].
- **Data source**: A rare-fish tag/recapture database spanning **1974–1992** was compiled, error-corrected, and cross-checked against original field sheets for questionable entries.
- **Progress status (as of FY93)**:
  - Task 1 (database compilation) — completed.
  - Task 2 (recapture/growth rate analysis) — analysis performed, results pending; behind schedule.
  - Task 3 (final report) — delayed to **February 1994** (originally scheduled for FY93/94).
- **Cause of delay**: A ~7-month contractual holdup — the Final Contract between Colorado State University and the Bureau of Reclamation was not signed until late May 1993 (expected ~November 1992), curtailing project start-up work.
- **Budget (FY93)**:
  - Funds provided: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service — $1,500; Colorado State University — $9,000 (Total: $10,500)
  - Funds expended: $10,500 (no variance)
- **Preliminary recommendations**:
  - Recapture-record analysis alone may not reveal all injury types; supplemental field studies (e.g., **X-ray during Standardized Monitoring**) are recommended to directly assess internal injury.
  - Standardization of **handling techniques** across research teams.
  - Formal training requirements for researchers and seasonal field crews in electrofishing and fish-handling protocols.
- **Reporting venue**: Initial findings to be presented at the **Biology Committee meeting** (invited research update), with the full final report due February 1994.
- **Investigators**: Frank K. Pfeifer (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, project lead); Robert T. Muth and John A. Hawkins ([[Larval Fish Laboratory]], Colorado State University).
