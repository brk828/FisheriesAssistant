# Etheostoma Chermocki, a New Species of Darter (Percidae) from the Black Warrior River Drainage of Alabama

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

Boschung, Mayden and Tomelleri (1992) describe [[Etheostoma Chermocki]], the **[[Vermilion Darter]]**, a new snubnose darter in the subgenus [[Ulocentra]]/[[Nanostoma]]. The species is known only from a short reach of Turkey Creek, a tributary of [[Locust Fork]] in the [[Black Warrior River]] drainage of the [[Mobile Basin]], Jefferson County, Alabama. It is separated from neighbouring [[Warrior Snubnose Darters]] mainly by fin and body colour, and breeding males are also morphometrically separable. The authors consider it most similar to the [[Duryi Group]] and recommend an ENDANGERED conservation status because its range is very small and its habitat is degraded.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The snubnose darters (subgenus Ulocentra sensu [[Bailey and Etnier 1988]], or Nanostoma sensu [[Page 1981]]) contained 12 and 14 described species, respectively, at the time of writing. Their subgeneric placement was still disputed, and the authors did not resolve the question. Most snubnose darters have restricted ranges, often endemic to one drainage, and several were still undescribed.

**Type material.** The holotype is UAIC 10288.02, an adult male of 52.5 mm standard length (SL), collected 9 March 1992 by Kuhajda, Tomelleri, Mayden and Boschung on Tapawingo Drive, near Pinson, Alabama, at an elevation of 600 feet. Paratypes come from numerous lots spanning 1962 to 1992, from Turkey Creek and its tributaries (including [[Dry Creek]]), Tapawingo Spring, and sites on Turkey Creek such as the Hwy 79 bridge and "The Narrows." The entire range lies on the Pinson 7.5-minute topographic quadrangle.

**Diagnosis.** The species is distinguished from other subgenus members by colouration of the spinous and soft dorsal, caudal, anal, and pectoral fins and the lateral body surface. Key features of breeding males include:
- a cherry-red ocellus in the first spinous dorsal membrane;
- a broad brick-red subdistal band on the remaining spinous dorsal membranes;
- two red-orange basicaudal spots separated by clear membrane;
- turquoise-and-black anal and pelvic fins and lemon-yellow pectorals;
- a vermilion venter contrasting with a wavy brick-red lateral band.

**Size and morphometrics.** The species reaches 60.2 mm SL (UF 44006). Sexes do not differ meristically, but they differ significantly in 12 of 16 morphometric traits (Table 1). Males have longer heads, deeper bodies, longer snouts, longer fin bases and rays, and wider trans-pelvic bases.

**Meristics.** Summary values for *E. chermocki* (N = 74) are:
- Lateral scale rows 44–52 (usually 46–48), mean 47.4 (SD 2.08);
- Transverse scale rows 11–14 (usually 13), mean 13.0 (SD 0.58);
- Caudal peduncle scales 16–19 (usually 17);
- Dorsal spines mean 10.7 (SD 0.52); dorsal rays mean 11.0 (SD 0.47);
- Anal rays mean 7.3 (SD 0.45); left pectoral rays mean 13.6 (SD 0.54);
- Branchiostegal rays 5; gill rakers 5–7 on each limb.

Scales are absent from the breast and nape but present on the cheeks and opercle. The lateral line is complete and nearly straight. Infraorbital, preoperculomandibular, and supraorbital canal pore counts are given in the source, with variation noted within each count.

**Sexual colour and seasonal change.** Males are brighter than females throughout the year, especially in spring. Colour descriptions are based on early March specimens. Breeding males have eight dark olive dorsal saddles and a flank pattern of brick-red spots and olive blotches. Females are drabber in early spring but brighten by late summer, with red-orange ocelli on the spinous dorsal fin and a lemon-yellow fin pattern.

**Etymology.** The epithet honours [[Ralph L. Chermock]] (1918–1977), who founded the University of Alabama Ichthyological Collection. The common name refers to the vermilion-coloured ventro-lateral flanks and belly of breeding males.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** *E. chermocki* lives in small to medium-sized streams, 3–20 m wide, with gravel bottoms. Pools of moderate current alternate with riffles of moderately swift water. Riffles are coarse gravel, cobble, and small rubble, while pool bottoms are rock (sometimes bedrock), sand, and silt. The most favourable habitat is swifter chutes with vegetation such as [[Watercress]] (*Nasturtium officinale*) and pondweed (*Potamogeton foliosus*).

**Associated fauna.** The authors list 15 species collected with the vermilion darter throughout its range, including [[Campostoma Anomalum]], [[Cyprinella Callistia]], [[Luxilus Chrysocephalus]], [[Notropis Stilbius]], [[Semotilus Atromaculatus]], [[Hypentelium Etowanum]], [[Moxostoma Duquesnei]], [[Gambusia Affinis]], [[Cottus Carolinae]], [[Lepomis Cyanellus]], [[Lepomis Macrochirus]], [[Micropterus Coosae]], [[Micropterus Salmoides]], [[Etheostoma Whipplei]], and [[Percina Nigrofasciata]].

**Range and threats.** The species occupies no more than about three miles of stream in urban and suburban areas. Parts of Turkey Creek are degraded by domestic pollution, especially silt from construction projects, and the darter now occurs only sporadically. It was collected in good numbers in the 1960s and 1970s at the Hwy 79 bridge site. On 31 July 1992, 10 man-hours of collecting at that site yielded one specimen. The authors infer that the species was formerly more widespread.

## Key Findings

- **New species.** *Etheostoma chermocki* is described as the Vermilion Darter, endemic to Turkey Creek, Jefferson County, Alabama.
- **Colour separates it from neighbours.** Both sexes differ from the undescribed [[Warrior Snubnose Darters]] in colour pattern. Warrior-form lateral bands are narrow, separate, and bordered by different reds and oranges, and females have dense melanophore bands that the vermilion darter lacks.
- **Morphometric separation in males only.** Using [[Principal Component Analysis]] (sheared PCA on log₁₀-transformed morphometrics), males of *E. chermocki* are completely separable from males of the four Warrior populations: [[Gurley Creek]] (Locust Fork), [[Mulberry Fork]] (Mill and Murphy creeks), [[Sipsey Fork]] (with Borden Creek), and [[Valley Creek]] (Fivemile Creek). Females overlap considerably with the Warrior populations. Male *E. chermocki* have a shorter snout, deeper body, taller spinous dorsal fin, shorter soft dorsal base, shorter caudal peduncle, and shorter anal spines and rays.
- **No meristic separation.** *E. chermocki* did not differ significantly from any Warrior population in meristic characters.
- **Seasonal colour.** Males are most colourful in early March, and females are most colourful in late July. Seasonal changes in fin and venter colour are documented.
- **Relationships.** The species is most similar to the [[Duryi Group]] of snubnose darters. It lacks a distinct premaxillary frenum and has vomerine teeth, a combination that [[Bailey and Etnier 1988]] used to define that group.
- **Conservation.** The authors recommend ENDANGERED status because of the very small range, urban and suburban location, and silt pollution. They note the population is "sufficiently small that immediate attention should be given to its protection."
- **Missed earlier recognition.** Specimens were identified as undescribed *Etheostoma* in earlier literature (e.g., [[Kuehne and Barbour 1983]]), and the authors regret not recognizing the species 20–30 years earlier, when protection might have been easier to secure.
