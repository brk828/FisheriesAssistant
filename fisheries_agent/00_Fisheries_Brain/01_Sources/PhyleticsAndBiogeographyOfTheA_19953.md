# Phyletics and Biogeography of the Aspinine Cyprinid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1984 systematic paper by [[Gordon Howes]] (Bulletin of the British Museum (Natural History) Zoology 47(5): 283-303) re-examines the Hwang-Ho dace, *Leuciscus mongolicus* (Kessler, 1876), which was prompted by specimens in the American Museum of Natural History. Anatomical study shows the species does not belong in [[Leuciscus]] and is erected as the new genus [[Genghis]]. The paper places Genghis as the sister lineage of the [[Aspinine Group]], broadens that group to include [[Aspiopsis]], [[Oreoleuciscus]], [[Tribolodon]] and [[Pogonichthys]], and discusses a possible trans-Pacific (eastern Asia–western North America) biogeographic link explained by vicariance rather than dispersal.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Skeletal and dissected material from a broad range of Old World and Nearctic cyprinids, including approximately 50 listed taxa (for example, *Aspius aspius*, *Aspius vorax*, *Elopichthys bambusa*, several *Gila* species, *Leuciscus* spp., *Ptychocheilus* spp., *Tribolodon brandti*).
- **Leuciscus concept:** The genus [[Leuciscus]] (Cuvier, 1817) is the most speciose Palearctic cyprinid group, with at least 36 nominal species. Its diagnostic characters are plesiomorphic, so the genus is judged non-monophyletic.
- **Genghis mongolicus:** A medium-sized, slender, non-barbelled cyprinid; the largest measured specimens reached 225.5 mm SL. Its type locality is Dalai Nor (Lake Dalai), Nei Monggol, China. Examined material came from Paotow (Baotou) on the Hwang-Ho, with 18 specimens listed in the description.
- **Nomenclature:** *Squalius mongolicus* Kessler, 1876 is the type species. Several synonyms are recognized, including *Leuciscus farnumi* Fowler, 1889. Oshima's (1926) *Leuciscus mongolicus* is a junior homonym, and the replacement name *jeholi* is proposed. *Leuciscus waleckii suiyuani* Mori, 1941 is treated as a synonym.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Biogeography within Asia:** A clear east–west dichotomy exists. [[Aspius]] occurs west, across much of Europe and south to the Tigris. [[Aspiolucius]] is sympatric with Aspius in the Amu Darya. Most aspinine genera lie east of the Mongolian Plateau, in the Sino-Korean region and the Amur basin. Genghis occurs in northern China, and Tribolodon along Yellow Sea, Sea of Japan and Pacific coasts, including Japan and Sakhalin.
- **Central Asian genera:** [[Aspiopsis]] and [[Oreoleuciscus]] are confined to a small area bordering Sinkiang and to the landlocked Upper Ob and Bya basins in Mongolia.
- **Trans-Pacific link:** The Tribolodon–Pogonichthys relationship supports earlier hypotheses (Miller, Hopkirk, Gosline, Howes) of close ties between western North American and Japanese/Chinese cyprinids. Insect groups (caddisflies, mayflies, proturans) show similar Pacific patterns.
- **Vicariance model:** The author notes that Bering land bridge dispersal was long assumed, but terrane accretion models better fit the pattern. The author concludes the data provide general support for vicariance, though only more congruent cladograms would confirm it.

## Key Findings

- **New genus erected:** *Leuciscus mongolicus* shares suites of derived characters with the aspinine group, including an elongate, laterally expanded pterosphenoid with a lateral shelf, a deeply concave sphenotic, a shallow orbitosphenoid keel, and an extensive gap between the branchial arch and the bucco-pharyngeal roof. It lacks the three defining aspinine synapomorphies (high vertebral number, numerous frontal and nasal pores, elongated pterosphenoid as defined), so it is assigned to [[Genghis]] gen. nov.
- **Diagnostic traits of Genghis:** A humped nuchal profile, mouth set obliquely at 45°, gill-rakers spinous with crenate medial membranes, and a deeply emarginate caudal fin. Vertebral count is 45, and the lateral line has 50-52 scales (Kessler's count of 54 included scales on the caudal base).
- **Sister-group relationship:** Genghis is the sister lineage of the aspinine genera (*Aspius*, *Elopichthys*, *Pseudaspius*, *Aspiolucius*, *Luciobrama*). Shared characters include crenate gill-rakers, an AAP origin from the pterosphenoid, and a triangular coronomeckelian bone.
- **Broadened aspinine group:** Genghis, Aspiopsis, Oreoleuciscus, Tribolodon and Pogonichthys join the aspinines in a monophyletic assemblage. Relationships among the three lineages (Genghis plus aspinines; Aspiopsis plus Oreoleuciscus; Tribolodon plus Pogonichthys) form an unresolved trichotomy.
- **Aspiopsis is distinct:** Previously synonymized with *Leuciscus* by Berg (1949) and Howes (1978), it is shown to be a separate genus. It is based on a single syntype, with 27 gill-rakers on the 1st ceratobranchial and about 70 lateral-line scales.
- **Shared derived traits:** Tribolodon and Pogonichthys share a fat-filled anterior extension of the subtemporal fossa, a club-shaped suboperculum process, and hypertrophied preural neural spines.
- **Pacific relationships:** Ptychocheilus shares features with Pogonichthys and Tribolodon, and its cladistic position among Nearctic taxa remains unresolved.
- **Leuciscus revision needed:** *Leuciscus lehmanni* and *L. schmidtii* share gill-raker morphology and an extensive branchial gap with Genghis, so they may be the sister group to the aspinines. A full revision of Leuciscus is recommended.
