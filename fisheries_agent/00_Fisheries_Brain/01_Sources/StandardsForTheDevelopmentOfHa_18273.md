# Standards for the Development of Habitat Suitability Index Models (103 ESM)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) manual part, Release No. 1-81 (April 10, 1981), sets standards for building [[Habitat Suitability Index]] (HSI) models for use with the [[Habitat Evaluation Procedures]] (HEP). An HSI is a 0–1.0 index of a habitat's capacity to support a species, and [[Habitat Units]] are computed as HSI multiplied by the area of available habitat. The document lays out a five-phase model construction process (set objectives, identify variables, structure the model, document the model, verify the model) and illustrates it with example models for the [[Gray Squirrel]], [[Red-tailed Hawk]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. Its aim is to improve the reliability and repeatability of HEP applications.

## Background & Description

The manual is the third in a series that began with a habitat-based methodology developed by the USFWS from 1974. The first volume (101 ESM) gave the rationale for habitat-based assessment, and the second (102 ESM) described the standardized field procedure for HEP. This volume provides guidance for building the models that generate HSI values.

Key definitions and scope:

- **Scope:** standards for terrestrial and inland aquatic evaluation species, suited to a typical federal water resource planning effort. Models may be graphical, word, or mathematical, and must document their rules and assumptions.
- **Linearity assumption:** HEP assumes a direct linear relationship between HSI and carrying capacity. Non-linear relationships must be converted to linear form for use.
- **Standard of comparison:** HSI = study area habitat conditions ÷ optimum habitat conditions for the same species.
- **Documentation:** Ellis et al. (1979) found that documentation increased the repeatability of HSI determinations.

Appendices provide worked examples:

- **Appendix A:** [[Gray Squirrel]] model (mast, cavity and canopy variables; limiting-factor HSI).
- **Appendix B:** [[Red-tailed Hawk]] prototype breeding-season model (grassland and deciduous forest cover types).
- **Appendix C:** hypothetical application of the red-tailed hawk model to 1,600 ha of available habitat, yielding an HSI of 0.59.
- **Appendix D:** [[Channel Catfish]] model with separate riverine and lacustrine equations.
- **Appendix E:** a cover type classification system adapted from [[Cowardin Classification]] (Cowardin et al. 1979).
- **Appendix F:** HSI models built using [[Discriminant Analysis]].
- **Appendix G:** glossary (not reproduced in the source text).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat use and variable selection.** Variables must be measurable, practical, and related to habitat capacity. Population variables are usually excluded because they are costly to measure and not directly indicative of habitat. Habitat is organized into four component types: seasonal habitat, life requisites (food, cover, water, reproduction), life stages, and cover types. Tree diagrams link these components to measurable variables.

**Spatial context.** Model applicability must be tied to a geographic unit, such as [[Ecoregions of the United States]] (Bailey 1976) for terrestrial models or [[Hydrologic Unit Maps]] (USGS) for inland aquatic models. The red-tailed hawk example uses interspersion distances (optimum within 1.2 km; unsuitable beyond 3.6 km) and composition targets (70% food area, 15% cover-reproductive area).

**Aquatic specifics for channel catfish.** The species occupies large riverine systems and reservoirs. Optimal conditions include warm temperatures, cover such as logs and boulders, and diverse velocities and depths. Spawning typically occurs late May through mid-July at about 21 °C. Key water-quality thresholds in the model include dissolved oxygen of 5 mg/l adequate and ≥7 mg/l optimal, and salinity of 2 ppt as the upper limit for embryo habitat.

**Gray squirrel resource thresholds.** Mast production should exceed 168 kg/ha (150 lb/ac) to sustain high densities. Seed crops below 145.7 kg/ha (130 lb/ac) sharply reduced juvenile survival in an Ohio study. Stands younger than 30–40 years produce insufficient mast or cavities.

## Key Findings

- **Index framework:** HSI = study area conditions ÷ optimum conditions; HSI ranges from 0.0 (unsuitable) to 1.0 (optimum). Word-rank and undefined-unit model outputs can be converted to HSI by dividing by the maximum rank or standard of comparison (for example, Excellent = 4 gives HSI 1.00; Good = 3 gives 0.75).
- **Acceptance levels:** Verification proceeds through four steps (author review, sample-data analysis, species-authority review, field testing). Acceptance level 1 requires steps 1–2, level 2 requires steps 1–3, and level 3 requires all four. The manual notes that testing should stop once further improvement is not possible given data limits.
- **Aggregation methods:** The document specifies limiting-factor (minimum), cumulative (capped at 1.0), arithmetic and geometric means (the geometric mean gives 0.40 versus 0.50 arithmetic for suitability values of 0.8 and 0.2), and weighted means. Averages become insensitive when four or more variables are used.
- **Model-building approaches:** Word models, mechanistic models (suitability index curves), pattern recognition models, Bayesian PATREC models, and multivariate statistical models (regression, discriminant analysis) are described. The PATREC example gives a potential density of 16.96 per 10 km² and an HSI of 0.85. Regression for Acadian flycatchers (Robbins 1978) is given as a multi-variable equation.
- **Red-tailed hawk worked example:** Food in grassland is calculated as the geometric mean of three suitability indices. The HSI equals the lowest life requisite value. In the hypothetical project area, food gives 0.59 and reproduction gives 1.0, so HSI = 0.59.
- **Gray squirrel model:** Food and cover/reproduction are each computed as the square root of the product of two variable indices. HSI is the lower of the two. Canopy closure above 75% for hard-mast trees (≥25.4 cm dbh) is considered optimal, and cover is optimal above 40% canopy closure with mean dbh ≥30.5 cm.
- **Channel catfish model:** Riverine HSI combines food, cover, water quality, and reproduction components. Lacustrine HSI adds an "other" component (storage ratio and fry flushing rate). Example data sets yield HSIs of 0.84, 0.40, and 0.38 (riverine) and 0.86, 0.30, and 0.20 (lacustrine). Results are not validated against field data, and the author notes the model is not a population model.
- **Limitations:** The manual acknowledges that the validity of assumed relationships between physiological or population responses and suitability is unknown, that data for most species are scarce, and that HSI values in a given water body may not reflect realized population size.
- **Documentation requirement:** Models must state applicability (geographic area, season, verification level), justify variables, explain aggregation, and give field measurement procedures.
