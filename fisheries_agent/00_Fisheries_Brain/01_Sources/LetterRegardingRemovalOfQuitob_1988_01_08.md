## Executive Summary
This 1988 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Endangered Species Office) addresses a taxonomic and conservation management conflict at the Audubon Research Ranch (Elgin, Arizona). In 1978, [[Boyd Kynard]] stocked [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*) into ponds at the Research Ranch and at Bog Hole Lake. These fish are not native to the Gila River drainage. The Service requests removal of the exotic *C. m. eremus* stock and its replacement with genetically appropriate [[Gila Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*), which is the historically native subspecies of the Gila drainage but has been extirpated from the U.S. for approximately 50 years. The letter outlines the risk of genetic contamination via escape of non-native pupfish, describes the Service's reintroduction plan, and proposes logistical and technical support for pond renovation.

## Background & Description
The source text contains no direct morphological, meristic, or anatomical data on either subspecies. However, it notes relevant **evolutionary/genotypic-phenotypic plasticity** relevant to population differentiation:
- Pupfish (genus *Cyprinodon*) are described as highly **plastic in both phenotype and genotype**.
- The letter explicitly disputes concerns that the Research Ranch population of *C. m. eremus* could have evolved into a distinct population within ~9 years of isolation, stating the likelihood of such rapid divergence is "extremely low."
- No specific external measurements, coloration, or fin-ray counts are provided in this document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range of *C. m. macularius***: historically inhabited the [[Gila River]] drainage in Arizona (U.S.); as of 1988, surviving populations persist only in ponds along the **lower Colorado River in Mexico**.
- **Native range of *C. m. eremus***: [[Quitobaquito Springs]], within [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]], described as a remote habitat with existing populations "doing well" in situ.
- **Threats to Quitobaquito population** (in native habitat): pesticide drift and exotic species introductions, though considered manageable given remoteness and NPS protection.
- **Introduced/translocated populations**: 
  - Audubon Research Ranch ponds (Elgin, AZ) — stocked 1978, thriving as of letter date.
  - Bog Hole Lake — also holds exotic *C. m. eremus* stock, subject of a parallel renovation effort with the U.S. Forest Service.
- **Habitat connectivity risk**: Ponds at Research Ranch and Bog Hole Lake are within the same drainage system targeted for *C. m. macularius* reintroduction, creating risk of escape and hybridization/contamination between subspecies.
- **Conservation infrastructure**: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] identified as an alternative refuge/backup site for maintaining *C. m. eremus* stock, potentially freeing Research Ranch ponds for native pupfish recovery work.

## Key Findings
- *Cyprinodon macularius eremus* (Quitobaquito Pupfish) is **not native** to the Gila drainage or the Research Ranch; its presence there is the result of a 1978 introduction by [[Boyd Kynard]].
- The historically native Gila drainage subspecies, tentatively classified as *Cyprinodon macularius macularius*, has been **extirpated from the United States for approximately 50 years**, with extant populations restricted to ponds along the lower Colorado River in Mexico.
- USFWS has obtained stock **genetically similar** to the native Gila River pupfish and is actively pursuing reintroduction into historic range.
- The presence of exotic *C. m. eremus* in the same drainage (Research Ranch ponds and Bog Hole Lake) poses a **genetic contamination risk** via potential escape, and is cited as the primary reason the Service is hesitant to proceed with reintroduction of the native subspecies.
- The Service formally requested that Audubon Research Ranch **remove the exotic *C. m. eremus* stock** and replace it with native *C. m. macularius*, offering to:
  - Coordinate with the **Arizona Department of Game and Fish** to renovate (detoxify) the ponds.
  - Supply native pupfish stock once ponds are renovated.
  - Coordinate parallel renovation of **Bog Hole Lake** with the **U.S. Forest Service**.
- The Service disputes the concern that the isolated Research Ranch pupfish population may have evolved into a genetically distinct population over 9 years, citing pupfish's known high plasticity but low probability of rapid speciation/divergence over such short timescales.
- As an alternative to maintaining exotic stock at Research Ranch, USFWS proposed relocating the "backup" *C. m. eremus* population to **Dexter National Fish Hatchery**, or maintaining a small stock (e.g., a 50-gallon aquarium) if Audubon wished to continue the strain in captivity.
- This document exemplifies a broader conservation principle in [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] efforts: prioritizing subspecies-level genetic integrity and drainage-specific reintroduction over incidental ex-situ population maintenance, especially for endangered desert fishes with fragmented historic ranges.
