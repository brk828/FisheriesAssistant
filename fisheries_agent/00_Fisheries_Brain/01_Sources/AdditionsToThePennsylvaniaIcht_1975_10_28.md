## Executive Summary

This 1975 faunal note documents three fish species newly recorded from the [[Susquehanna River Drainage]] in Pennsylvania: the [[Fourspine Stickleback]] (*Apeltes quadracus*), the [[Brook Stickleback]] (*Culaea inconstans*), and the [[Bluespotted Sunfish]] (*Enneacanthus gloriosus*). These findings represent range extensions or first confirmed records for the drainage, based on museum specimens and field collections spanning 1948–1974. The authors note that the origin of these populations — whether natural relict distributions or human-mediated introductions — remains uncertain, particularly for the aquarium-trade-associated Bluespotted Sunfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Fourspine Stickleback]]**: Typically a coastal/brackish-water species ranging from Labrador to Maryland, occasionally entering coastal streams. Previously known in Pennsylvania only from the Delaware River system (Bucks, Delaware, Lehigh, Northampton, Philadelphia Counties). Its presence in inland Susquehanna tributaries (Harvey's Lake inlet, Big Spring Creek) suggests either an unusual freshwater-adapted population or a disjunct/introduced occurrence.

- **[[Brook Stickleback]]**: A freshwater species native to Canada and the upper Mississippi/Missouri River systems; previously documented in Pennsylvania only from the Ohio River drainage in the northwest of the state. A "rare" population was noted in the Unadilla River (New York), part of the extreme upper Susquehanna drainage. This paper marks its **first record within the Pennsylvania portion of the Susquehanna drainage**, found in Sandy Run, Blair County.

- **[[Bluespotted Sunfish]]**: Native to lowland freshwaters from New York to Florida. Found here across several disconnected Susquehanna tributary sites (Mountain Creek, Stony Creek, Lake Winola, Harvey's Lake), suggesting a broader but patchy distribution than previously recognized. The species is also kept as an aquarium fish, raising the possibility of anthropogenic introduction via release by hobbyists.

## Key Findings

- Three species — **[[Fourspine Stickleback]]**, **[[Brook Stickleback]]**, and **[[Bluespotted Sunfish]]** — are formally added to the known ichthyofauna of the [[Susquehanna River Drainage]] in Pennsylvania.
- **Brook Stickleback** is reported for the first time from the Pennsylvania Susquehanna drainage, extending known range southward/eastward from Ohio drainage and New York (Unadilla River) populations.
- **Fourspine Stickleback**, formerly known in PA only from the Delaware River drainage, is now documented from two inland Susquehanna tributary sites (Harvey's Lake inlet and Big Spring Creek).
- **Bluespotted Sunfish** records span four separate localities across three counties (Cumberland, Dauphin, Wyoming, Luzerne), with specimens dating back to 1948, indicating an established if historically overlooked presence.
- A discrepancy in the historical record is noted: [[Fowler 1919]] listed Bluespotted Sunfish from York County, PA, but omitted it from his later 1938 and 1940 checklists; the authors could not locate a supporting specimen at the Academy of Natural Sciences, suggesting Fowler may have corrected an earlier error.
- The **origin of all three populations is undetermined** — natural occurrence versus accidental/deliberate introduction (especially plausible for the aquarium-trade Bluespotted Sunfish) cannot be ruled out.
- Voucher specimens are deposited across multiple institutions: the [[University of Maryland Appalachian Environmental Laboratory]], the [[Pennsylvania State University Museum]], and the [[Cornell University Fish Collection]].
- This work builds on a broader ongoing survey effort of Pennsylvania fish distributions by the authors dating to 1966, including related studies of the [[Schuylkill River]], [[Perkiomen Creek]], [[Codorus Creek]], [[Conewago Creek]], and [[Conowingo Creek]].
