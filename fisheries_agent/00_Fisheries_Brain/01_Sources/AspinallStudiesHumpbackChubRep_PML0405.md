# Westwater Canyon Humpback Chub Reproduction and Recruitment: 1992-1994 Progress Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This progress report from the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]] summarizes the first three years (1992-1994) of the [[Aspinall Studies]], a component of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]]. The study examines how [[Colorado River Flows]] affect spawning, nursery habitat, recruitment and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) populations in [[Westwater Canyon]], the largest known humpback population in the Upper Basin. Sampling occurred on nineteen occasions, including eight four-day community trips and eleven shorter early life history efforts. Native chubs reproduced successfully each year. Results are preliminary; the final report will provide full interpretation and geomorphic analysis.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), first described from Grand Canyon specimens collected in 1946 (Miller 1946). Found in canyon-bound reaches of the Colorado River (Black Rocks, Westwater, Cataract canyons) and the Green River (Desolation and Yampa canyons).
- **Associated species:** [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), native catostomids (flannelmouth and bluehead suckers), and non-native fishes including [[Channel Catfish]], [[Common Carp]] and cyprinids such as red shiner, sand shiner and fathead minnow.
- **Study goal:** Develop flow recommendations protecting spawning and nursery habitat for humpback chub in Westwater Canyon, providing the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service with data for its biological opinion on operation of the [[Aspinall Unit]] on the Gunnison River.
- **Study objectives:** (1) determine spawning and nursery requirements; (2) describe links between geomorphic sediment transport and nursery habitat formation; (3) identify reproductive isolating mechanisms; (4) assess recruitment.
- **Historical data sets:** (1) [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] studies beginning 1979; (2) seven years (1986-1992) of late-summer monitoring by the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources following the ISMP handbook; (3) this study, which targets early life stages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Westwater Canyon is a 14-mile stretch located 115 river miles upstream of the Green River confluence. Upthrust metamorphic gneiss creates a confined, deep channel. Habitat is predominantly runs, eddies and pools interspersed with riffles and rapids. Average gradient is 13.1 ft/mile (as given in source), compared with 6.2 ft/mile in [[Desolation Canyon]] on the Green River.

**Monitoring sites.** Three locations were sampled: Miners Cabin (RM 124.1), Cougar Bar (RM 121.5) and Hades (RM 120.0). Maximum depths measured in May 1994 were 21.8 m at Miners Cabin, 19.4 m at Cougar Bar and 10.6 m at Hades.

**Flow regime.** Mean daily flows since 1914 have ranged from 640 cfs (1934) to 13,200 cfs (1917). During the study, flows ranged from a low of 1,910 cfs (late July and early August 1994) to a high of 47,500 cfs on May 29, 1993. Winter flows have been above historic averages since the construction of the [[Aspinall Unit]]. Spring, summer and fall 1992 and 1994 flows were well below long-term averages, while 1993 flows were above average.

**Temperature and low-flow conditions.** In summer 1993, flows rarely dropped below 5,000 cfs and main-channel temperatures averaged 20°C or more from July 11 to September 21. In 1994, the lowest flow year, flows dropped below 2,000 cfs on several occasions and temperatures averaged 20°C or more from June 7 to September 16. Summer 1994 main-channel temperatures averaged 26.2°C with flows of 2,483 cfs.

**Sampling methods.** Community sampling used trammel nets (23 m x 2 m), boat-mounted electrofishing and shoreline hoop nets, mostly during crepuscular and nighttime hours. Early life history sampling used seines (1.5 mm mesh), larval drift nets, larval dip nets and larval light traps. Chubs over 150 mm TL were PIT tagged and scale samples were collected for age and growth analysis.

## Key Findings

**Population and recruitment**
- A total of 1,350 chubs were PIT tagged over the three years.
- Recapture rates were 19.8% for humpback chub and 12.9% for roundtail chub in the most recent sampling (as stated in the abstract).
- Native chubs reproduced successfully each year. The highest catch rate of young-of-year (YOY) chubs was 201.2 YOY/100 m² immediately upstream of Westwater Canyon on August 2, 1993.
- High 1993 flows appeared to delay non-native cyprinid reproduction, particularly within the canyon.

**Growth and survival**
- Recaptures indicate that over the long term both species grow little, at less than 0.05 mm/day for fish over 250 mm TL.
- Humpback chub growth rates over 260 mm TL averaged less than 0.05 mm/day, roughly 1.5 mm per growing season.
- Long-term monitoring at Miners Cabin shows average total length of both species decreasing since 1986, with the smallest averages in 1992 and slight increases in recent years.

**Species composition**
- A total of 19 species and two suspected hybrids were collected (Appendix Table 10).
- Native species outnumbered non-natives in both trammel net and electrofishing samples. Native catostomids never exceeded 58% of the total net catch, while channel catfish comprised at most 20%.
- Humpback chub became the most abundant species collected with trammel nets and electrofishing in July 1994, indicating a shift toward a more balanced representation with roundtail chub.

**Cohorts and age structure**
- In 1992, age-0 fish were not detected until July (average size about 23 mm). In 1993, they appeared earlier in June (average about 16 mm), and spawning apparently continued into early July.
- The 1993 cohort showed a steep growth slope between July 15 and September 1.
- Electrofishing was the most effective method for collecting age-1 and age-2 Gila spp., and juvenile catch rates were greatest between RM 121.5 and 119.8, where boulder outcrops along shallow shorelines under moderate flows appear to be preferred habitat.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Trammel net catch rates were not significantly correlated with flow or main-channel temperature; other variables (turbidity, water chemistry, photoperiod, time of day) likely influence catch rates.
- Recommendations include continuing sampling to estimate population size as recaptures increase, and gathering geomorphic evaluation of habitat formation to link flow to humpback chub nursery habitat in the low-gradient reach of Westwater Canyon.
