# Lake Powell Fisheries Investigations: Creel Census and Plankton Studies (February 1968 - January 1969)

#ichthyology #reservoir-ecology #Centrarchidae #multi-species-fishery #plankton-dynamics

## Executive Summary

This report documents the sixth segment of a nine-year cooperative fisheries investigation on [[Lake Powell]], conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[Utah Fish and Game Department]]. During February 1, 1968 through January 31, 1969, creel census was conducted at four major access points ([[Wahweap Marina]], [[Halls Crossing Marina]], [[Bullfrog Marina]], and [[Hite Marina]]) over 140 days. Creel clerks interviewed 6,354 angler trips producing 16,267 fish in 37,797 hours. Total estimated pressure was 32,401 angler trips yielding 81,896 fish in 191,468 hours. The creel composition was 47% [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), 21% [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), 17% [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), 9% [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*), 1% [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*), and 5% other species. Success averaged 0.43 fish per hour or 2.55 fish per trip, with average trip duration of 5.94 hours. Plankton studies identified 15 zooplankton and 21 phytoplankton genera, with 16 blooms noted throughout the study period.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Powell]] is the reservoir created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] as part of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]]. This investigation represents a cooperative effort financed through Section 8 funds to evaluate fishing pressure, harvest, and plankton production in this newly created reservoir system.

**Study Design**: Creel census employed stratified sampling with varying intensity by marina: six days per month at [[Wahweap Marina]], two to four days per month at other marinas, split equally between weekdays and weekends. Census extended from daylight to dark, with night fishing census during warmer months at Wahweap. Dock-based interviews captured length of fishing time, number and species of catch, area fished, angler residence, and bait type.

**Plankton Sampling**: Eleven sampling stations arranged at equal intervals through the reservoir. Samples collected using closing net (#20 mesh) sampling 100-foot vertical water column (five inches diameter). Samples preserved in 5% formalin. Quantitative analysis measured volume of concentrated organisms and actual counts of zooplankton in triplicate subsamples using [[Sedgewick-Rafter Counting Chamber]] at 3.5x to 20x magnification following [[Welch]] limnological methods.

**Aerial Survey**: Thirty aerial counts conducted mid-day at elevation sufficient to observe side canyons while identifying boat use (fishing, transit, other). Total of 3,497 boats observed (960 fishing, 1,492 transit, 1,045 other use).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir Characteristics**: [[Lake Powell]] exhibits typical reservoir zonation with distinct upper, middle, and lower sections. The reservoir receives input from the [[Colorado River]] system including the [[San Juan River]] arm. Access points distributed along reservoir length create distinct fishing pressure zones.

**Habitat Zones**: Multiple habitat types sampled including:
- Creek arms (Wahweap, Warm, Kane, Navajo)
- Main channel areas
- Side canyons (Cha Canyon, Rincon, Lake Canyon)
- River confluence zones (San Juan, Escalante)
- Upper reservoir areas (Tapestry Wall, Red Canyon, White Canyon, Hite)

**Water Column Structure**: Stratified sampling in August revealed strong vertical zonation. Upper 25 feet most productive; depths greater than 25 feet uniformly non-productive, indicating thermal stratification typical of desert reservoirs.

**Plankton Community**: Zooplankton dominated by copepods ([[Cyclops]], [[Diaptomus]]), cladocerans ([[Daphnia]], [[Bosmina]]), rotifers ([[Polyarthra]], [[Keratella]]), and protozoans ([[Ceratium]]). Nauplius stage arthropods most numerous. These three copepod/cladoceran genera constitute over half plankton volume.

**Phytoplankton Blooms**: Sixteen blooms documented:
- February: *Asterionella* and *Fragilaria* (very heavy, lower and middle reservoir)
- April: *Dinobryon* (very heavy, all reservoir)
- May-June: *Dinobryon*, *Pediastrum*, *Fragilaria* (light to moderate)
- July-August: *Mirocystis*, *Fragilaria*, *Aphanizomenon*, *Ulothrix* (heavy)
- September-October: *Aphanizomenon*, *Staurastrum*, *Mirocystis* (moderate to heavy)

Most prevalent phytoplankton genera: *Staurastrum* (117 occurrences from 116 samples), *Fragilaria* (66), *Cymbella* (43), *Asterionella* (53), *Aphanizomenon* (49).

## Key Findings

**Fishing Pressure Distribution**:
- [[Wahweap Marina]]: 66% of total pressure (21,225 trips, 56,568 fish, 143,600 hours)
- [[Halls Crossing Marina]]: 16% (5,133 trips, 14,314 fish, 21,630 hours) - 22% decrease from previous segment
- [[Bullfrog Marina]]: 16% (5,180 trips, 8,656 fish, 21,766 hours) - 73% increase due to improved access road and marina completion
- [[Hite Marina]]: 2% (863 trips, 2,358 fish, 4,472 hours) - 51% decrease

**Temporal Trends**: Peak pressure occurred May-June (11,846 combined trips). Pressure 19% higher in angler trips and 43% higher in fish harvested compared to 1967-1968 segment, representing natural recovery from previous low. However, still below peak 1966-1967 year (28,961 trips, 102,309 fish).

**Species-Specific Patterns**:

*Largemouth Bass*: 38,774 fish (47% of creel). Best fishing mid-reservoir (Rock Creek to Tapestry Wall). From Wahweap: San Juan area produced 26%, Last Chance 20%. From Halls Crossing: Rincon 33%, Bullfrog Bay 17%, Tapestry 16%, Lake Canyon 14%. From Bullfrog: Bullfrog Bay 45%, Escalante River 20%. Successful anglers used live waterdogs (54%) or lead-headed rubber worms.

*Channel Catfish*: 17,065 fish (21% of creel). Best fishing around Bullfrog area. From Halls Crossing: Bullfrog Bay 55%. From Bullfrog: Escalante River 33%, Moqui Canyon 27%. Successful anglers used live worms (69%).

*Bluegill*: 13,473 fish (17% of creel) - dramatic increase from 3% previous segment. From Halls Crossing: Bullfrog Bay 64%. From Bullfrog: Bullfrog Bay 61%. Species expected to increase further.

*Black Crappie*: 7,567 fish (9% of creel) - increased from 1% previous segment. Best fishing lower reservoir (Wahweap to Rock Creek). Navajo Creek produced 38% from Wahweap. Only in reservoir four years, spread to Escalante River area. Successful anglers used dollfly jigs and spinners (58%).

*Rainbow Trout*: 1,144 fish (1% of creel) - continued decline from 5% previous segment. Best fishing Wahweap Creek area (73% of trout checked). Successful anglers trolled with lures (52%).

**Success Rates by Marina**:
- Fish per hour: Wahweap 0.41, Halls Crossing 0.64, Bullfrog 0.39, Hite 0.47 (overall 0.43)
- Percentage successful trips: Wahweap 53%, Halls Crossing 60%, Bullfrog 49%, Hite 60%
- Fish per trip: Wahweap 2.59, Halls Crossing 3.01, Bullfrog 1.89, Hite 2.82 (overall 2.61)

**High-Productivity Areas**:
- Aztec Creek: 0.81 fish/hour
- Navajo-Warm Creek: 0.49 fish/hour
- Bullfrog Bay (from Halls Crossing): 1.11 fish/hour
- Escalante River (from Halls Crossing): 0.78 fish/hour
- Tapestry Wall (from Bullfrog): 0.69 fish/hour
- Rincon (from Bullfrog): 0.52 fish/hour

**Angler Origin**:
- Wahweap: 55% Arizona residents (Phoenix 10% of total), 21% California
- Other marinas: 58% Utah residents, 14% Colorado

**Aerial Survey Assessment**: Thirty flights estimated 30,368 angler trips from 3,497 boats observed, comparable to creel census estimate (32,401 trips). However, data deemed too inconsistent over three years to warrant continuation. Formula I (average boats × days × fishermen per boat) more reliable than Formula II (fishing boats only).

**Plankton Production**:
- 116 samples collected (no samples March, November, January due to weather/equipment failure)
- 15 zooplankton genera, 21 phytoplankton genera identified
- Largest volumes and numbers: Escalante and Rincon stations, April-June period
- Stratified sampling (August): upper 25 feet most productive, deeper water relatively non-productive
- Zooplankton: *Cyclops* (112 occurrences), *Daphnia* (112), *Diaptomus* (92), *Polyarthra* (94), *Ceratium* (78)

**Other Species Documented**: [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Kokanee Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*) - all rare.

**Management Recommendations**:
1. Continue creel census at all access points with special attention to trout harvest (size and numbers)
2. Discontinue aerial surveys due to data inconsistency over three years
3. Reduce plankton studies to volumetric type due to manpower and budget reductions
4. Plant rainbow trout in late fall/early winter, over 5 inches long, by truck (fall 1968 plants appeared successful based on January 1969 creel)
5. Mark portion of planted trout with plastic streamer tags to evaluate return to creel
