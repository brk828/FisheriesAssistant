# Hydroacoustic Surveys of Fish Abundance and Distribution in Twin Lakes, Colorado

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #hydroacoustics #population-assessment

## Executive Summary

This report presents results of hydroacoustic surveys conducted in September 1980 to assess fish abundance and distribution in [[Twin Lakes]], [[Colorado]], prior to operation of the [[Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant]]. Using echo-counting techniques with a 120 kHz Simrad EK-120 system, researchers estimated the [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*) population at 14,578 ± 10,432 fish (95% confidence interval: 4,145-25,010) in deep and intermediate strata. The study documented clear size-based vertical stratification, with large lake trout (mean target strength -37.5 dB, ~400mm) concentrated below the thermocline at 16-25m depth, while smaller targets occupied shallow water (3-7.5m). Horizontal distribution was highly contagious, with significantly higher densities in the southern portions of both lakes. This baseline assessment provides critical pre-operational data for evaluating powerplant impacts on the fishery.

## Background & Description

[[Twin Lakes]] consists of two connected montane drainage lakes of glacial origin at 2802m elevation in the Upper Arkansas River Valley. The upper lake covers approximately 196 ha with maximum depth 28m; the lower lake covers 589 ha with maximum depth 27m. The lakes are dimictic, chemically soft water systems with maximum surface temperatures reaching 18°C in late July to mid-August. The fishery comprises a self-reproducing [[Lake Trout]] population and put-and-take [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*). Both lake trout and [[Mysis Shrimp]] were introduced species. Zooplankton fauna consists of a mysis shrimp-copepod-rotifer association, while phytoplankton is dominated by diatoms and yellow-brown species.

The hydroacoustic system employed a 120 kHz transducer with 10-degree beam angle, 0.6 ms pulse length, and 6 soundings per second. The transducer was towed 2m below surface at 2 m/s boat speed. Seven transects were established (five in lower lake, two in upper lake) and surveyed during both day and night between September 15-18, 1980. Targets were stratified into three depth zones: shallow (3-7.5m), intermediate (7.5-16m), and deep (16-25m), corresponding to above-thermocline, transitional, and below-thermocline habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The vertical thermal structure strongly influenced fish distribution. The thermocline at approximately 12m depth created distinct habitat zones. [[Lake Trout]] demonstrated clear preference for deep, cold water below the thermocline, consistent with their thermal requirements. Gill net sampling by the [[Colorado Cooperative Fishery Research Unit]] confirmed that 95% of fish below the thermocline were lake trout, with 100% in midwater sets. Above the thermocline, species composition included [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), and suckers in addition to occasional lake trout.

The contagious (patchy) distribution of lake trout, particularly concentrated in areas 3, 4, and 5 of the survey grid, appeared associated with bottom topography. In area 3, fish aggregated near a ridge approximately 500m off the south shoreline creating a 5m vertical dropoff. This bathymetric feature likely provided preferred habitat or foraging opportunities. The southern portions of transects consistently showed higher fish densities than northern sections.

Diel vertical migration was limited compared to many limnetic fishes. Large targets (>-49 dB echo strength) showed broader vertical distribution at night but remained primarily in deeper water. Small targets in shallow water exhibited extreme patchiness and were predominantly detected near shore at night, possibly representing juvenile lake trout, rainbow trout, or suckers.

## Key Findings

**Population Estimates:**
- Combined deep and intermediate strata: 14,578 ± 10,432 fish (pooled night surveys)
- Night 1 & 2 estimate: 17,389 ± 13,130 (relative sampling error 34%)
- Night 3 estimate: 10,206 ± 7,249 (relative sampling error 32%)
- Day 1 estimate: 8,143 ± 8,081 (relative sampling error 45%)
- Lower lake contained approximately 85-90% of the population
- Shallow stratum estimate: ~26,800 targets (85% from area 7, high variability)

**Size and Vertical Distribution:**
- Shallow zone (3-7.5m): Mean echo strength -58.4 dB, corresponding to target strength -51.4 dB (~90-100mm fish)
- Intermediate zone (7.5-16m): Mean echo strength -50.8 dB, target strength -43.8 dB (~150-200mm fish)
- Deep zone (16-25m): Mean echo strength -44.5 dB, target strength -37.5 dB (~400mm fish)
- Largest targets: -28 dB echo strength (corresponding to ~1000mm fish)
- Clear size-based stratification with small targets concentrated at ~5m depth, large targets at ~21m

**Horizontal Distribution:**
- Significant density differences among transect areas (Mann-Whitney U, α=0.05)
- Highest densities: Areas 3, 4, 5 (southern lower lake and upper lake)
- Intermediate densities: Areas 6, 7 (upper lake)
- Lowest densities: Areas 1, 2 (northern lower lake)
- North-south gradient with higher concentrations in southern portions
- Association with bottom topography, particularly ridges and dropoffs

**Temporal Patterns:**
- Daily variability between night surveys was large but not statistically significant (Wilcoxson matched pairs, α=0.05)
- Diel variability relatively low compared to other lakes
- Daytime measurements tended toward lower range but not significantly different from night
- Vertical distribution broader at night but fish remained primarily in deep water
- Few small targets observed during day, precluding shallow-zone diel comparisons

**Sampling Efficiency:**
- Approximately 5% of lake volume below thermocline sampled during 3-day survey
- 95% confidence intervals ±72% of mean due to low density and contagious distribution
- Maximum technical bias <5% for deep strata using echo-counting techniques
- Species composition below thermocline 95% lake trout (gill net verification)
- Shallow stratum sampled <1% of volume, inadequate for population estimation

**Comparison with Previous Estimates:**
- Previous mark-recapture estimate: 73,556 (95% CI: 33,690-200,608) based on 6 recaptures
- Hydroacoustic estimate substantially lower, suggesting either mark-recapture error or population decline between studies
- Hydroacoustic method provided much narrower confidence intervals and higher precision

**Methodological Insights:**
- [[Twin Lakes]] ideally suited to hydroacoustic techniques: low density enabling echo-counting, large target sizes providing good signal-to-noise
- Complementary value of hydroacoustics (distribution, abundance) and gill nets (species composition, biological samples)
- Dual-beam system recommended for improved size discrimination at low densities
- Shallow/near-shore zones require specialized system design and higher sampling effort
- Stationary acoustic systems could evaluate gill net efficiency and provide ice-cover data
