# Desert Fishes Council Twentieth Annual Symposium: Final Details and Dues Statement (1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This circular, dated September 23, 1988 and signed by Executive Secretary [[E. P. Pister]] of the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC), announces final logistics for the Twentieth Annual Symposium, held November 16–19, 1988, in [[Death Valley]], California. It marks two decades since the Council's founding, which the letter ties to a December-era meeting at Death Valley on November 18, 1969. The circular covers lodging at [[Furnace Creek Ranch]], a barbecue, 1989 dues, commemorative merchandise, student paper awards, Mexican participation, the special "Battle Against Extinction" symposium, a field trip to [[Ash Meadows National Wildlife Refuge]], resolution rules, the daily agenda, and registration. It is an administrative document, not a research report, so its scientific content is limited to the conservation themes of the meeting.

## Background & Description

The Council is described on its letterhead as "Dedicated to the Preservation of America's Desert Fishes." The letter recalls milestones the Council helped shape during its first two decades:

- Formation of the Council and its Pupfish Task Force
- Court hearings and the Supreme Court decision concerning [[Devils Hole]]
- The [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973
- Greater involvement of Mexican colleagues and three symposia held in Mexico
- Acquisition of Ash Meadows

Logistical details include:

- **Lodging:** Furnace Creek Ranch accepted prepaid reservations through October 15, 1988. Rates were $59.95 per night for a cabin unit (described as becoming scarce) and $70.85 per night for a deluxe room.
- **Dues:** 1989 dues were due. They constitute the great majority of Council income and cover publishing, postage, and operating costs. Proceedings Volume XIX was expected at the symposium.
- **Merchandise:** Commemorative tee-shirts (organized by [[Pat Minckley]]) cost $10.00 for adults and $8.00 for children. Hats and pins were also on sale.
- **Barbecue:** A traditional Thursday evening barbecue required advance headcounts, since no supermarkets are located at Furnace Creek.
- **Registration:** Opened Tuesday, November 15, 1900–2100, outside the Visitors Center, and resumed Wednesday at 0800. Fees for registration, banquet, dues, and merchandise were to be "kept to an absolute minimum," as the Council remains non-profit.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The meeting's technical focus is the management and survival of endangered desert fishes in the American West and Mexico. The special symposium, titled "Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West," addressed:

- Patterns and results of environmental change, and the development of an environmental ethic
- Historical exploration and synthesis of arid-west fishes, including the Green River
- Conservation status of Mexican fishes, presented by [[Salvador Contreras]]
- Public awareness, legislation, and litigation
- Case studies on the [[Devils Hole Pupfish]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Cui-ui]]
- The [[Colorado River Fisheries Project]]
- Captive propagation and reintroduction, including the history of endangered species hatcheries, reintroduction of long-lived and short-lived species, and conservation genetics
- Habitat reclamation and alteration, criteria for refuges, and progress toward refuges
- Lessons from species that resist extinction

The field trip to Ash Meadows, scheduled for Saturday, November 19, was flagged as a major discussion item.

## Key Findings

**Administrative and logistical points**

- The symposium runs November 16–19, 1988, in Death Valley.
- Final deadline for paper titles was October 15, 1988. The Wednesday and Thursday regular sessions still had room for additional papers.
- Furnace Creek prepaid reservations closed October 15, 1988.
- Resolutions must be typed in the standard DFC format, prepared before arrival, and submitted at the meeting. Handwritten or non-standard resolutions "must be rejected."
- Each agency should send one representative for a general report, limited to 15 minutes.
- The business meeting is scheduled for Friday, November 18.

**Awards and participation**

- Two student paper awards were offered: the [[Carl Le Hubbs Award]] (best student paper) and the [[Frances Hubbs Miller Award]] (best paper by a Mexican student).
- The Council urged members to help Mexican colleagues cover travel expenses, citing economic difficulties in Mexico and the hospitality received at the previous year's meeting in Hermosillo.
- The first annual meeting of the [[Sociedad Ictiológica Mexicana]] was to convene in La Paz immediately before the Death Valley symposium. Its president, Dr. Salvador Contreras, was to report on it, and several Council members were founders planning to attend.

**Schedule**

- **Tuesday, November 15:** Evening registration.
- **Wednesday, November 16:** Agency reports and Paper Session I.
- **Thursday, November 17:** Paper Session II, Extinction Symposium I, and evening barbecue.
- **Friday, November 18:** Extinction Symposium II, a special U.S. Geological Survey program on Devils Hole, and the business meeting.
- **Saturday, November 19:** Field trip to Ash Meadows.

**Battle Against Extinction Symposium**

The tentative program comprised roughly two dozen presentations, including talks by Minckley, Rolston, Douglas, Miller, Holden, Contreras, C. Williams, Pister, Deacon, Wydoski, Scoppetone, Tyus, Johnson, Marsh, Echelle, Mette, Hendrickson, Rinne, Moyle, and J. Williams. The closing talk, "Lessons from Species that Resist Extinction," was to be given by Minckley. Speaker assignments for the opening talk were still pending.
