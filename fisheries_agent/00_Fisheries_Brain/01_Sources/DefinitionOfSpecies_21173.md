# Turtox Catalog Pages: Formalin Neutralization, Embryology Slides, Cat Skulls and the Species Concept

#ichthyology #marine-biology #embryology

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of pages from a [[Turtox]] biological supply catalog and its companion newsletter, *Turtox News*. It mixes four kinds of content: a short technical note on neutralizing [[Formalin]] for field preservation; sales listings for [[Whitefish]] and [[Asterias|Starfish]] embryology microscope slides; an advertisement for [[Cat Skull]] teaching specimens; and Part II of an essay by [[Hobart M. Smith]] on the [[Species Concept]]. The essay is the only substantive scientific argument in the text. Its main claim is that species are discrete units only at a given time level, and that allopatric populations should be recognized as species when they are completely distinct and genetically isolated.

## Background & Description

**Preservation note.** Neutralized formalin is described as standard practice in larger museums. It allows specimens to stay in formalin for two to three months or more during fieldwork without acid damage, and it gives institutions without alcohol a medium for indefinite storage. Neutralization does not remove the formalin odor. The recommended dilution for permanent preservation is about 3 to 5 percent formaldehyde by weight, depending on the material.

**Whitefish embryology slides.** Turtox's serial sections of whitefish blastula showing mitosis (No. E13.78, $1.25) are described as used in more than 5,000 schools. The catalog lists about 16 further whitefish slides covering:
- Second polar body formation (E13.62 to E13.64, $2.75 each for late formation and complete body)
- Cleavage spindles (E13.65 and E13.66, $1.65 each)
- Early cleavage and fibrillar cytoplasm (E13.70s, E13.75, $1.65)
- Blastula and gastrula (E13.77, $1.75; E13.79, $1.65)
- Primitive streak and somite stages (E13.81 to E13.83, up to $2.50)

**Cat skulls.** Cat skulls are presented as the most-used mammalian skull in zoology courses, partly because they show the "generalized" vertebrate skull and cost far less than human skulls. Perfect adult skulls are $3.00 each or $34.00 per dozen. Slightly imperfect skulls with no serious defects or missing teeth are $1.75 each or $20.00 per dozen. The catalog number is Turtox No. 1052TN1.

**Starfish embryology slides.** The [[Asterias]] series (about 15 slides, prices $0.55 to $2.00) covers sperm, ovary, eggs, polar body formation, cleavage, blastula, gastrula, bipinnaria, brachiolaria, and the young starfish after metamorphosis. The material was prepared at [[Woods Hole]] by Turtox embryologist [[Margaret H. Davidson]] from freshly collected specimens, using special fixatives rather than routine killing methods.

**Species essay (Part II).** Smith's essay, first in the July 1952 issue of *Turtox News*, builds on the earlier Part I. Its central points are:
- **Isochrony:** species are discrete only at a given time level. Populations merge into one another through time, consistent with the genetic continuity of life.
- **Allopatric populations:** geographically isolated, mutually non-interbreeding populations should be regarded as species, because many species evolved in isolation without intrinsic reproductive barriers.
- **Criterion:** a species is a combination of (1) complete distinction and (2) genetic isolation, either intrinsic or extrinsic. This applies to sympatric, parapatric and allopatric cases alike.
- **Cenospecies:** [[Cenospecies]] (after Blair, 1943) is proposed for intersterile complexes.
- **Recent contact:** formerly separate forms that now interbreed but remain geographically or ecologically segregated should be recognized as subspecies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The essay's examples are mainly freshwater and desert fish and reptiles:
- *Anoptichthyes jordani* and *Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus* (blind cavefish), now treated as subspecies, linked to [[Cueva Chica]] (Breder, 1942).
- *Gila orcutti* and *Siphatales mohavensis*, cyprinids that hybridize in the Mojave Desert, California (Hubbs and Miller, 1943).
- *Lampropeltis* king snakes (*L. californiae*, *L. getulus boylii*, *L. nitida*) and the western snakes *Masticophis piceus* and *Sonora miniata*, whose former species distinctions have coalesced.

The embryology material covers marine invertebrate development (Asterias, from Woods Hole) and freshwater fish development (whitefish). The text does not discuss habitat, water chemistry or population data for any species.

## Key Findings

- **Formalin treatment:** neutralized formalin with 3 to 5 percent formaldehyde by weight allows safe long-term storage of specimens, including during prolonged fieldwork. It does not eliminate odor.
- **Teaching slides:** Turtox offers large sets of whitefish and starfish developmental slides for embryology teaching. Whitefish mitosis slide E13.78 is widely used in schools. Starfish slides were prepared with special fixatives from fresh material.
- **Cat skulls:** perfect adult skulls cost $3.00 each ($34.00 per dozen). Imperfect but study-suitable skulls cost $1.75 each ($20.00 per dozen).
- **Species concept:** Smith argues that species are time-level units and that complete distinction plus genetic isolation defines a species. Allopatric forms that are distinct qualify as species even without intrinsic reproductive barriers.
- **Subspecies status:** where formerly distinct species now interbreed but remain segregated, they should be treated as subspecies. Where segregation has disappeared, they should not be separately named at the present time level.
- **Limits of the concept:** species are natural in most organisms, but not universal. Applying the term to phyletic series or non-Mendelian populations yields only approximations.
