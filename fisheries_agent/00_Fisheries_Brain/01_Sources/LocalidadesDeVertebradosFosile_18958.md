# Fossil Vertebrate Localities of the Mexican Republic

#ichthyology #marine-biology #vertebrate-paleontology

## Executive Summary

This 1969 catalogue by [[Angel Silva-Barcenas]], published by the [[Instituto De Geologia]] of the [[Universidad Nacional Autonoma De Mexico]] as *Paleontologia Mexicana* No. 28, maps and inventories the known fossil vertebrate localities of [[Mexico]]. It plots 87 localities on a 1:5,000,000 map and lists 174 genera and 236 species, arranged in 23 concentration tables by state. Most records are terrestrial mammals, but the catalogue also includes fishes, sharks, rays and turtles. The author argues that the record is patchy, that many older specimens have doubtful provenance, and that a general taxonomic and stratigraphic revision is needed.

## Background & Description

The document is a compilation of earlier work rather than a new field study, though the author visited localities in Coahuila, Tamaulipas, Michoacan, Oaxaca, Campeche and Yucatan to verify specimens. Its goal was a complete picture of the state of knowledge, to support later detailed studies. The author notes that the listed localities are probably not all the existing ones, but they are the most important in abundance or type of material.

**Historical context:**
- Early work ran from the late 1800s. [[Felix And Lenk]] (1890–1899) and [[Freudenberg]] (from 1910) shaped the stratigraphic approach to Mexican vertebrate paleontology.
- [[A. R. V. Arellano]] from 1946 linked vertebrate paleontology to stratigraphic problems in the Cuenca de Mexico.
- Key syntheses include [[Maldonado-Koerdell]] (1948, Quaternary vertebrates and fossil fishes) and [[T. Alvarez]] (1965, mammal catalogue), which the author describes as showing "anarchy" in nomenclature and synonymy.
- Later contributions came from [[Mooser]] (from 1958, including the Aguascalientes Fauna Cedazo) and [[Hibbard]] (1955, Becerra Formation).

**Stratigraphic framework:** Fossil ages are assigned to named units, including the Becerra, Gran Canal, Tacubaya, Hemphillian, and Mexicanense faunal intervals, and to Cretaceous, Tertiary and Permian horizons.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The catalogue is mainly a palaeontological and biostratigraphic record, so it offers only indirect environmental information. Notable points:

- **Pleistocene basins:** The greatest abundance and diversity occur in the Altiplano. The [[Cuenca De Mexico]] (Tequixquiac, with localities near 2,260 m elevation) and the Valle de Puebla (near the Presa de Valsequillo) are the richest. The author links locality elevation to former lake-water levels in the Pleistocene.
- **Western lake systems:** The [[Lago De Chapala]], Ameca and Atotonilco–Zacoalco–Sayula valleys in Jalisco hold large mammal and reptile assemblages, placed in the Gran Canal Formation.
- **Cave deposits:** [[Cueva De San Josecito]] (Nuevo Leon) is a major Pleistocene site with 74 genera and 75 species of birds, mammals, reptiles and amphibians.
- **Marine and coastal records:** Sharks and rays from Baja California and Chiapas, and fishes and sharks from Tamaulipas and Oaxaca, indicate marine or marginal-marine settings.

## Key Findings

**Overall inventory**
- 87 fossil vertebrate localities, 174 genera and 236 species, plotted at 1:5,000,000.
- 23 concentration tables, one per state (including the Distrito Federal and Estado de Mexico grouped together).
- The record is most complete for the Pleistocene and Pliocene. Oligocene, Eocene and Cretaceous material is sparse. Several states (Nayarit, Colima, Durango, Queretaro, Tabasco, Campeche, Quintana Roo) have no recorded finds.

**Fishes and other aquatic vertebrates (selected)**
- **Permian:** *[[Helicoprion Mexicanus]]* from the Hacienda Las Delicias, Coahuila, is the only Paleozoic record and the oldest vertebrate in the catalogue.
- **Cretaceous (Turonian):** *Ceratodus* sp. and *Lamna* sp. from Peyotes, Coahuila, and a dinosaur vertebra from Buiuelos.
- **Neocomian (Oaxaca):** *[[Belonostomus Ornatus]]*, *Thrissops* (?) and *Otomitla speciola* from Cerro de la Virgen.
- **Jurassic (Puebla):** *Gyrodus macraphthalmus* from Cerro Sangre de Dragon, Petlalcingo.
- **Tertiary sharks and rays (Baja California, Mision Vieja):** *Carcharhinus*, *Carcharodon*, *Galeocerdo*, *Isurus*, *Lamna*, *Myliobatis*, *Sphyrna* and *Galeus*.
- **Eocene (Tamaulipas, Presa del Azucar):** *Scylliorhinus* sp. and *Aetobatis* sp.

**Notable single records**
- **Dinosaurs:** A Hadrosauridae-type vertebra from Coahuila; a Cretaceous dinosaur from Baja California (Arroyo del Rosario, Morris, 1967). A later note reports an almost complete skeleton in the Olmos Formation (Upper Cretaceous) at Palau, Coahuila, probably a *Triceratops* according to Dr. Wilson of the University of Texas. The author stresses that such skeletons are otherwise known only as isolated fragments.
- **Paramylodon (?) sp.:** Reported by Hatt (1953) from Actun Spukil, Yucatan, the only vertebrate fossil record for the peninsula.

**Taxonomic and stratigraphic issues**
- The Neohipparion otomii holotype (Guanajuato) does not meet the ICZN requirement of a single individual. The author designates a left mandible (FO-10) as holotype and the other pieces as paratypes, and recommends deposition in an official institution.
- Many museum specimens have uncertain provenance; the author calls for a full revision including modern and classic studies.

**Stated conclusions**
- Current knowledge spans the stratigraphic column from the Permian (*Helicoprion*, Coahuila) to the Pleistocene, with the Tertiary best represented.
- Greater attention and dedicated collections are needed to support future stratigraphic and evolutionary work.
- A 1969 find of a mastodon bonebed near Chichihualco, Guerrero, offers a chance to resolve taxonomic confusion over earlier Osborn and Freudenberg identifications.
