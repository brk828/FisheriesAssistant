# Alabama Sturgeon: Withdrawal of Proposed Endangered Species Listing (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register rule (Vol. 59, No. 240, 15 December 1994, Part IX, Department of the Interior, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) withdraws the June 1993 proposal to list the [[Alabama Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*) as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and to designate its critical habitat. The Service concluded there was insufficient evidence that the species still persists. Only one specimen had been captured since 1985, despite searches in 1990–1993. The species is placed in Category 3 of the candidate list. The notice also responds to 40 numbered public issues, covering taxonomy, economic impacts, dredging, dams, water quality, and procedural concerns.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy:** The Alabama sturgeon was described by [[Williams and Clemmer]] (1991) as a distinct species in the genus *Scaphirhynchus*. A peer-reviewed study by [[Mayden and Kuhajda]] (in press) supported its distinctness. Critiques by Bartolucci et al. (1994) questioned the statistics. A [[Schill and Walker]] (1994) cytochrome b study found the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] and [[Pallid Sturgeon]] indistinguishable, and found no cytochrome b differentiation among related species. A [[Corps Genetic Study]] (1994) found no measurable nuclear DNA difference between pallid and shovelnose sturgeons, but significant differences between those two and the single Alabama specimen. The Service accepted the species as distinct, pending peer review.
- **Museum records:** [[Clemmer]] (1983) listed 23 specimens in museum collections. Williams and Clemmer (1991) located nine more, for a total of 32 specimens in museum, university, and private collections. Museum records show captures in February, March, April, May, June, November, and December, with most from spring.
- **Historic catch:** A 1898 [[U.S. Commission of Fish and Fisheries]] report listed a commercial harvest of about 18,000 kg (39,500 lb) from the Alabama River. A 1930s *Game and Fish News* article described the fish as "not uncommon."
- **Feeding:** The shovelnose sturgeon is an opportunistic feeder. Its diet in the Missouri River includes aquatic insect larvae, caddisflies, small fish, and plant material. The Alabama sturgeon is assumed to feed similarly, but no direct diet data exist.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range decline:** Burke and Ramsey (1985) estimated the historic range at 1,635 km (1,022 mi) across the Mobile River system (Black Warrior, Tombigbee, Alabama, Coosa, Tallapoosa, Mobile, Tensas, and Cahaba rivers). About 57 percent had been lost. Only about 15 percent (243 km, 152 mi) retained potential habitat, and that was considered marginal.
- **Recent captures:** Burke and Ramsey (1985) captured five Alabama sturgeons, two gravid females and one juvenile. Searches by the [[Alabama Department of Conservation and Natural Resources]] (ADCNR) and the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] (Corps) in 1990, 1991, and 1992 yielded no specimens. A single specimen was captured in December 1993. Burke and Ramsey's 1985 captures indicate reproduction in the mid-1980s.
- **Habitat pressures:** Impoundments built on the Alabama River in the late 1960s and early 1970s (including [[Robert F. Henry Lock and Dam]], [[Millers Ferry Lock and Dam]], and [[Claiborne Lock and Dam]]) altered flows, blocked spawning migrations, and reduced riverine habitat. Silt from land clearing, agriculture, urban development, and gravel mining also reduced stable substrates. Uncontrolled discharges of pollutants were noted. Commercial fishing may have contributed to early decline, but this is unconfirmed.
- **Flow requirements:** The proposed rule suggested minimum flows of about 90 cms (3,000 cfs) below the Henry and Millers Ferry locks. The Corps and [[Alabama Power Company]] (APC) operate under agreed minimums of about 149 cms (4,640 cfs) for any seven consecutive days and about 81 cms (2,667 cfs) for any three consecutive days downstream of Claiborne Lock and Dam. The Service concluded that the sturgeon's flow needs remain uncertain and that flow changes would not have been recommended.

## Key Findings

- **Withdrawal:** The Service withdrew the proposal because it could not substantiate the species' present existence. The [[Best Available Information Standard]] could not be met for listing.
- **Candidate status:** The species was placed in Category 3 of the candidate list. Category 2 is for species with insufficient information to propose listing.
- **Population evidence:** Only one specimen was captured in 1993, and none was confirmed in the 1990–1992 searches. Anecdotal reports exist but were not used as the basis for the decision.
- **Taxonomy:** Genetic and morphological studies (Mayden and Kuhajda; Corps genetic study) support distinctness, but the reviewers note that more specimens are needed for conclusive results.
- **Peer review:** The [[Peer Review Panel]] was formed in September 1993 and delivered a consolidated report on 5 November 1993. A preliminary injunction on 9 November 1993 barred the Service from using or disseminating the report. Hearings were canceled and rescheduled, and the comment period was extended through 31 January 1994.
- **Economic impacts:** Under section 4(b)(1)(A), economic impacts cannot be considered in listing decisions. The Service estimated the regional economic impact would have been minimal.
- **Dredging and channel training:** Corps maintenance dredging was judged not likely to adversely affect the sturgeon. The Alabama River has 67 channel training works at 16 locations. The Corps considers these used to the maximum practicable extent. The Service encourages continued evaluation of [[Channel Training Devices]].
- **Other issues:** Alabama state law protects the species from take but not other threats. Listing would have triggered Section 7 consultation and Section 6 funding. Boll weevil eradication and water quality standards would not have required significant changes. Recovery plans do not restrict private land use, though best management practices were encouraged.
- **Procedural concerns:** The Service acknowledged errors, including the 15-minute examination limit for the dead specimen, a mislabeled "Mobile River system population" reference in the June 1994 notice, and an erroneous statement about shovelnose diet change due to channelization. These were corrected.
- **Future action:** If new information demonstrates the species' present existence, the Service may propose listing again under 50 CFR part 424.
