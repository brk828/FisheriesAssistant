# Razorback X Flannelmouth Sucker Hybridization in the Colorado River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines a cover letter from the [[Lake Mead Limnological Research Center]] (University of Nevada, Las Vegas, December 1982) with a manuscript by Gene R. Wilde (Department of Biological Sciences, UNLV). The letter reports fall netting at [[Cottonwood Basin]] and a [[Razorback Sucker]] spawning observation, and promises a second paper on morphometric and meristic data. The manuscript identifies [[Razorback Sucker]] X [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] hybrids from [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]], proposes the Virgin River flannelmouth population as the most likely maternal or paternal parental stock, and reviews hybrid incidence across the upper and middle [[Colorado River]] system. The author concludes that hybridization is widespread and likely to continue, and that proposed razorback reintroductions could produce further hybrids with local catostomid populations.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** The manuscript's stated purposes are to (1) identify the Virgin River flannelmouth sucker population as the most probable parental stock of hybrids in [[Lake Mohave]], (2) report the razorback X flannelmouth hybrid from [[Lake Havasu]], and (3) discuss the distribution and incidence of this hybrid combination in the [[Colorado River]] system.
- **Taxonomic history:** Earlier reports of hybrids in [[Lake Mohave]] were attributed to a [[Utah Sucker]] combination (Seethaler, McAda and Wydoski 1979) and to a [[White Sucker]] combination (Gustafson and Pinckley). The author argues that the [[Utah Sucker]] and dusky mountain sucker can be eliminated as the second parent, because hybridization with either would have produced different lateral-line and oral/labial characters.
- **Diagnostic features:** The razorback sucker is named for its pronounced nuchal keel, which is usually poorly developed in hybrids, though it may be well developed in some specimens. Hybrids show a narrower caudal peduncle, 13 to 15 dorsal fin rays, and a somewhat higher lateral-line scale count than razorbacks.
- **Morphological evidence:** Two distinct hybrid morphs occur in [[Lake Mohave]], possibly representing reciprocal crosses. One resembles the razorback with a poorly developed keel; the other resembles the flannelmouth with a moderately developed keel.
- **Misidentification:** A photograph published by Douglas (1957) of a "razorback sucker" from [[Lake Havasu]] is identified here as a hybrid, based on its typical flannelmouth snout, inferior mouth, shallow posterior body, and lateral-line count of 86 to 87, which is above the razorback mean and just above the hybrid mean of about 85.2 (Hubbs and Miller 1953).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution and abundance**
- Hybrids occur wherever the razorback sucker is found in the upper and middle [[Colorado River]], and are often common there.
- Razorbacks have declined in the upper basin, while flannelmouth suckers are abundant and widespread. Relative abundances are reversed between the upper and middle basins.
- Early evidence: a single 1891 record (Jordan), one specimen in 1947, and six specimens in 1953. The 1953 collection contained 43 flannelmouth suckers and no razorbacks.

**Incidence of hybridization**
- Upper [[Colorado River]] system: incidence ranges from about 5% to a higher value that is illegible in the scan.
- Holden and Stalnaker (1975a) reported 43%.
- [[White River]]: Lanigan and Ferry (1981) collected one hybrid and no razorbacks (incidence reported as 100%).
- [[Yampa River]] and [[Gunnison River]]: razorbacks are rare. [[San Rafael River]] and [[Dolores River]]: razorbacks are absent. Hybrids have not been recorded from these tributaries but may occur.
- [[Glen Canyon Dam]] tailwater and the confluence of the Colorado and [[Paria River]]: three hybrids and one razorback were collected recently. Hybrids were also present in a 1976 Arizona Fish and Game collection of flannelmouth suckers from the Paria confluence, released below [[Glen Canyon Dam]].
- [[Lake Mohave]]: estimated incidence varies locally, and the overall figure is partly illegible in the scan (possibly about 16%).
- Hybrids have also been recorded in the Salt River drainage between razorbacks and the [[Sonoran Sucker]] (*C. insignis*).

**Impoundment effects**
- Before [[Lake Mohave]] formed behind [[Davis Dam]], razorbacks were common in the lower river. Flannelmouth suckers were probably present but uncommon.
- Hybridization probably occurred mainly in the first few years after impoundment and has since declined, possibly because flannelmouth suckers have been nearly extirpated from the lake. Introgressive hybridization may still be occurring.

**Management implications**
- Future river modifications and tributary alterations may increase hybrid incidence in some areas.
- Proposed [[Razorback Sucker]] reintroductions may hybridize with local catostomids.

**Spawning observations (Cottonwood Basin, November 1982)**
- Mike Bozek and Wilde netted [[Cottonwood Basin]] for four hours in November 1982 with trammel nets. Of the fish caught, 38 were recorded as males. The remaining sex counts are partly illegible in the scan.
- No razorbacks were taken in gill nets fished overnight.
- Razorback suckers were observed spawning in groups of three, from as early as mid- or late November at water temperatures near the threshold for spawning, which the author expected to fall with the onset of colder weather.

## Key Findings

1. **Hybrid identity:** Hybrids from [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]] are razorback X flannelmouth sucker combinations, based on morphological intermediacy and agreement with Hubbs and Miller (1953).
2. **Parental stock:** The Virgin River flannelmouth population is the most likely parental stock. Its caudal peduncle, dorsal ray count, and lateral-line counts best match the hybrids, whereas other flannelmouth populations described by Pinckley differ.
3. **Diagnostic caution:** The nuchal keel is an unreliable diagnostic character. Hybrids can have a well-developed keel, and misidentification has occurred in published photographs.
4. **Widespread hybridization:** Hybrids occur in all areas where razorbacks are found in the upper and middle [[Colorado River]] system, with incidence from about 5% to over 40% in reported samples.
5. **Trend:** Whether hybridization is currently increasing cannot be determined from available data. Scarce razorbacks, abundant flannelmouth suckers, and continued river modification favor continued or increased hybridization.
6. **Lake Mohave dynamics:** Hybridization was most likely heaviest soon after impoundment and may now be limited by the apparent loss of flannelmouth suckers, though introgression may still be occurring.
7. **Conservation concern:** Razorback reintroductions may produce hybrids with local catostomid populations, which the author flags as a management risk.
8. **Letter-stated plans:** A second paper on morphometric and meristic data was still in preparation in December 1982. The author also sought a copy of McAda and Wydoski (1980) for comparison.
