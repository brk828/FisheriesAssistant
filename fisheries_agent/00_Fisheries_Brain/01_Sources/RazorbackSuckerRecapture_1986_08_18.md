# Razorback Sucker Recapture (Gila River Drainage, 1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #RazorbackSucker

## Executive Summary
This 1986 field memo from the Center for Environmental Studies at Arizona State University documents the recapture of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) at two distinct sites within the Gila River drainage in Arizona. A total of 16 specimens were collected: two larger adults from an impoundment on [[Eagle Creek]] above the Phelps Dodge diversion dam, and fourteen smaller juveniles/subadults from a backwater on [[Bonita Creek]] near the San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation. The data provides morphometric baselines (length and weight) for a federally significant, imperiled catostomid species and documents its co-occurrence with both native and non-native ichthyofauna.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Razorback Sucker]] occupies distinct freshwater habitats within the Gila River system, captured here in two contrasting microhabitats:

1. **Eagle Creek Impoundment:** A lentic (still-water) habitat created by a diversion dam. Associated species collected via trammel net indicate a mixed native-nonnative assemblage:
    - [[Cyprinus carpio]] (Common Carp) – *Nonnative*
    - [[Gila intermedia]] (Gila Chub) – *Native*
    - [[Catostomus insignis]] (Sonora Sucker) – *Native*
    - [[Ictalurus natalis]] (Yellow Bullhead) – *Nonnative*
    - [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (Channel Catfish) – *Nonnative*

2. **Bonita Creek Backwater:** A lotic-adjacent backwater habitat near the San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation boundary, sampled via seine. This site shows a more intact native fish assemblage:
    - [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (Longfin Dace) – *Native*
    - [[Gila intermedia]] (Gila Chub) – *Native*
    - [[Pimephales promelas]] (Fathead Minnow) – *Nonnative*
    - [[Catostomus insignis]] (Sonora Sucker) – *Native*
    - [[Pantosteus clarki]] (Desert Sucker) – *Native*

The presence of Razorback Sucker in a backwater environment at Bonita Creek is ecologically significant, as backwaters are considered critical nursery habitat for the species' recruitment.

## Key Findings
- **Geographic Distribution:** Confirms the persistence of *Xyrauchen texanus* in two tributaries of the [[Gila River]] drainage (Eagle Creek and Bonita Creek) as of August 1986.
- **Population Structure:** The size range at Bonita Creek (150–251 mm TL) indicates the presence of younger or smaller individuals compared to the Eagle Creek specimens (270–285 mm TL), suggesting potential recruitment or differing age structures between the two sites.
- **Sympatric Species:** The species coexists with both native catostomids/cyprinids (e.g., [[Catostomus insignis]], [[Gila intermedia]]) and invasive ictalurids/cyprinids (e.g., [[Ictalurus punctatus]], [[Cyprinus carpio]]), highlighting the conservation challenge posed by nonnative species interactions in the [[Gila River Drainage]].
- **Collection Methodology:** Different capture techniques were utilized based on habitat type—trammel netting for the deeper impoundment waters at Eagle Creek versus seining for the shallow backwater at Bonita Creek.
- **Repository:** All 16 specimens were vouchered and housed at Arizona State University for further study.
