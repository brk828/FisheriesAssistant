# Native Fish Conservation in the Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1992 correspondence package from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Region 2, Albuquerque, N.M.). It includes a cover letter to [[Minckley]], Leader of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] at [[Arizona State University]], and an enclosed memorandum to the State Director of the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM), Phoenix, Arizona. The memorandum urges BLM to develop and implement an aggressive management plan for the [[Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area]] that emphasizes the conservation and recovery of native fishes. The area holds at least 15 native fish species, 11 of which are listed as threatened or endangered or are candidates for listing.

## Background & Description

- **Origin of correspondence:** The Service's cover letter responds to a letter from Dr. Minckley dated March 11, 1992, sent to the Regional Director on behalf of the entire [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]. The memorandum was prepared primarily in response to that letter.
- **Document dates:** Both the cover letter and the memorandum bear the date APR 16 1992.
- **Study area:** The [[Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area]] (NCA) includes the [[Gila River]] in its big-river form and the downstream section of [[Big Bonito Creek]].
- **Biological significance:** The Gila River in this area still retains big-river features that historically protected a variety of fish species endemic to the [[Colorado River Drainage]]. Big Bonito Creek supports a large population of fish native to the desert Southwest.
- **Stated pressures:** The memorandum notes continuing pressures on water resources, and therefore on native fish fauna, across the southwestern United States, and states that opportunities to restore and maintain these fishery resources are very limited.
- **Contacts:** Sam Spiller, Phoenix Ecological Services Field Office, (602) 379-4720.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native fish diversity:** At least 15 native fish species presently or historically occupied the Gila Box area or adjacent parts of the Gila River system.
- **Conservation status:** Eleven of these species are listed as threatened or endangered, or are candidates under consideration for federal listing.
- **Competition and predation:** The under-utilized [[Catfish Fishery]] in the area is said to compete with and prey upon the native fish population.
- **Livestock impacts:** [[Livestock Grazing]] and possibly other activities affect the watershed, the streams, and the fish fauna they contain.
- **Habitat management opportunities:** The memorandum identifies opportunities to reduce these impacts on the area's native flora and wildlife.

## Key Findings

1. The [[Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area]] has great potential for conservation and restoration of several native fish species.
2. At least 15 native fish species presently or historically occupied the Gila Box area or adjacent Gila River reaches. Eleven are listed as threatened or endangered, or are candidates for federal listing.
3. The Service encourages three management actions: maintaining existing native fish populations, reestablishing other species in the area, and possibly expanding the range of some or all of these species.
4. The [[Catfish Fishery]] competes with and preys on native fish and should be considered in management.
5. [[Livestock Grazing]] and other activities affect watershed and stream health, and these impacts can be minimized.
6. The Service requests that BLM develop and implement an aggressive management plan emphasizing the conservation and recovery of native species in the NCA.
7. The Service will continue to work with BLM and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] on conservation and recovery, and it requests continued communication from the recovery team.
