# Abundance and Bathymetric Distribution of Bahrain (Arabian Gulf) Reef Ichthyofaunas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pomacentridae

## Executive Summary

This study surveyed reef-fish assemblages at three reefs off [[Bahrain]] in the [[Arabian Gulf]] at 4-7 m, 7-10 m and 13-15 m depth, using SCUBA and the [[Species/Time Random Count Technique]]. A total of 55 species in 22 families was recorded. Species richness rose with depth, from 37 species at the shallowest station to 43 at the deepest. The authors attribute the low richness and equitability of the Bahrain reef fauna to stressful environmental conditions in the Gulf. Most species belong to the [[Western Indian Ocean Province]] or [[Indo-Polynesian Province]] of the [[Indo-West Pacific]], but several show restricted distributions, which the authors argue gives the Arabian Gulf ichthyofauna a degree of distinctiveness.

## Background & Description

Several checklists exist for the Arabian Gulf ichthyofauna, but little was known about the abundance of the species they include. A notable exception was a [[Artificial Reefs]] survey in [[Kuwait]] (Downing et al., 1985), which used in situ SCUBA fish censuses. The purpose of this study was to compare reef-fish assemblages across depths using a visual census technique.

Fieldwork was conducted by SCUBA divers at three stations north-east of Bahrain. Each station was visited twice during the study period, between June and December 1985.

- **Station 1** (4-7 m; sampled 9 May and 20 September): [[Porites]] buttresses rising 2-3 m above the bottom, with [[Acropora]] thickets covering 70-80% of the area.
- **Station 2** (7-10 m; sampled 6 June and 10 December): dominated by Acropora and Porites, with the largest heads rising 2-3 m above the bottom.
- **Station 3** (13-15 m; sampled 10 May and 13 December): small rocky ledges and terraces on a 1-2 m high reef edge, likely relict Pleistocene shoreline features.

Station selection followed the finding of [[Reef Structural Complexity]] and fish species diversity (Risk, 1972), so stations were chosen to represent the collective ichthyofauna of each depth interval.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Arabian Gulf is a stressful environment. Annual coastal sea temperatures off Bahrain range from 12 to 36°C, and salinities in the study area are high, at 40-43‰ (Vousden, in press). The authors link these conditions to the low species richness and equitability of the reef fauna compared with other Indo-West Pacific areas, such as the [[Seychelles]] (880 species, Goldman & Talbot, 1976) and the [[Red Sea]] (over 325 species, Randall, 1983).

The fauna is described as a depauperate Indo-West Pacific assemblage. Its distinctiveness comes from species with restricted ranges:

- **Probable Arabian Gulf endemics (5):** Pseudochromis persicus, Chaetodon nigropunctatus, Cheilodipterus arabicus, Halichoeres stigmaticus, Scarus persicus.
- **Gulf to north-western India (5):** Diplodus sargus, Sparidentex hasta, Neopomacentrus sindensis, Ecsenius pulcher, Parupeneus margaritatus.
- **Arabian endemics (6):** Cephalopholis hemistiktos, Scolopsis taeniatus, Chaetodon melapterus, Zebrasoma xanthurum, Cryptocentrus lutheri, Ostracion cyanurus.

The authors suggest these patterns may justify special [[Zoogeography]] status for the Arabian Gulf (Smith et al., in press).

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- The [[Species/Time Random Count Technique]] (Thompson & Schmidt, 1977) was used. Each diver recorded the first sighting of each species during a 40 min search, divided into eight 5 min intervals.
- Species first seen in interval 1 scored 8, interval 2 scored 7, and so on down to 1. Four replicates per station visit gave a maximum score of 32 per visit and 64 across the two visits.
- A preliminary check at Station 1 found 32 of 33 species in the first 40 min, compared with all 33 after 80 min, so 40 min was adopted as the standard.
- Seasonal scores did not differ significantly (P > 0.05, [[Wilcoxon Signed Rank Test]]), so visits were combined.

**Richness and composition**
- A total of 55 species in 22 families was recorded.
- Species richness increased with depth: 37 species at Station 1 and 43 at Station 3.
- The most diverse families were Pomacentridae (5 spp.), Carangidae (4 spp.), Haemulidae (4 spp.), Sparidae (4 spp.) and Gobiidae (4 spp.). The abstract lists Haemulidae in the top four in place of Carangidae, so the two accounts differ on this point.
- 26 species were common to all three stations.
- 10 species were found only at the deepest station, including Seriolina nigrofasciata, Sphyraena barracuda and Scarus sordidus.
- 12 species were found only at the shallower stations, including Lutjanus argentimaculatus, Acanthopagrus berda and Amphiprion clarkii.

**Abundance patterns**
- Ten species received the maximum score of 64 at one or more stations (Table 2).
- [[Pomacentrus Trichourus]] scored the maximum at all three stations.
- [[Pomacentrus Aquilus]] and [[Diplodus Sargus]] scored the maximum at both shallower stations.
- Several species peaked at the shallowest station (Amblygobius albimaculatus, Lutjanus fulviflammus, Pseudochromis dutoiti), while others peaked at the deepest (Scolopsis ghanam, S. taeniatus, Epinephelus malabaricus, Neopomacentrus sindensis).
- Abundance rose with depth in some species (e.g. Scolopsis taeniatus, Neopomacentrus sindensis) and fell in others (e.g. Siganus canaliculatus, Carangoides ferdau, Cryptocentrus lutheri). The authors consider these differences real and not sampling artifacts, based on the consistency of high and absent or rare records.

**Regional comparison**
- The 55 species recorded here compare with 72 species from Bahrain (Smith et al., in press), 70 from off [[Saudi Arabia]] (Basson et al., 1977) and 50 from Kuwaiti artificial reefs (Downing et al., 1985).

**Conclusions**
- Low richness and equitability in the Bahrain reef fauna likely reflect stressful Gulf conditions.
- Bathymetric zonation shapes both species composition and relative abundance.
- The Arabian Gulf fauna has enough restricted-range species to warrant special zoogeographic consideration.
