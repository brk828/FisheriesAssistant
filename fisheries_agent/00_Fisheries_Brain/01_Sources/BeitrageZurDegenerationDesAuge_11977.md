# Degeneration of the Eye in Cave-Dwelling Fish: Gene Number and Mode of Manifestation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This German-language doctoral study by Horst Wilkens (Zoologisches Staatsinstitut und Museum Hamburg, published 1970) investigates eye degeneration in Mexican cave fishes of the [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] complex. Crosses between the sighted surface form and two blind, depigmented cave forms (Pachon and Sabinos populations) were used to examine how eye-controlling genes segregate and how many genes are involved. The study reports that eye formation is controlled by a highly polygenic system with epistatic and balance effects, that lens, pupil and eyeball are hierarchically linked ("eye genes"), and that the minimum estimate is a tri- to tetrahybrid inheritance in F2 hybrids and a hexa- to heptahybrid inheritance in backcrosses to the cave fish. The two cave populations were found not to differ detectably in eye size, supporting the view that the Pachon population is not clearly the oldest or most degenerate.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** [[Astyanax Mexicanus]], a sighted, pigmented characid of the Rio Tampaon system, regarded as the ancestor of the cave forms.
- **Cave forms:** Originally described as *Anoptichthys jordani* (Hubbs and Innes, 1936) from [[Chica Cave]]; *A. hubbsi* and *A. antrobius* (Alvarez, 1946) from [[Sabinos Cave]] and [[Pachon Cave]]. Sapoc​u's crosses showed these are ecological races of one species, so a separate genus is not justified.
- **Geography:** Caves drain into the Rio Tampaon. Sabinos lies about 25 km upstream and Pachon about 90 km further north. The populations are thought to have been isolated sequentially as water levels fell.
- **Theoretical context:** The author contrasts the degenerative-evolution view of [[Kosswig]] (absence of selection allows neutral degeneration) with neo-Darwinian explanations invoking selection for energy saving, and with the immanent-law view of Vandel.
- **Material:** Fish collected on a 1964 Mexico expedition and reared in Hamburg. Chica fish were excluded because of strong heterozygosity. Females were induced to spawn with carp pituitary injections and later with Anteron. Fish were measured at 2.5 cm (Group I) and 6.0 cm (Group II) standard length, fixed in 4% formalin, and eyeball diameter was measured with an ocular micrometer (1 ME = 0.32 mm).
- **Scale:** The summary states that about 20,000 fish were examined.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Cave populations show the typical troglomorphic suite of reduced eyes and pigment, with a small eye that is progressively overgrown and sunk into the orbit as growth ends.
- Degeneration is accompanied by increased variability, which is typical of regressing organs. Left-right asymmetry of eye size was also common.
- Peters and Peters (1966, 1968) are cited for histological differences between the Pachon and Sabinos eye remnants, and for a gene-flow interpretation in [[Poecilia Sphenops]], a cave-dwelling livebearer with an incipient cave population.
- Gradient hypotheses from [[Breder]] and Rasquin (1947) and Sapoc​u (1957) are discussed, with the author concluding that eye degeneration does not form a clear phylogenetic gradient.
- Schemmel (1968) reported that taste-bud numbers are greatest in Pachon, then Sabinos, then the surface form. This is cited as partly contrary to a simple gradient.

## Key Findings

1. **Hybrid eye size (Group I, mean eyeball diameter, in ME):** Surface fish about 7.1 ME (8.8% of body length). Pachon fish about 2.00 ME and Sabinos fish 2.1 ME. F1 (Surface × Pachon) 5.17 ME, F1 (Surface × Sabinos) 5.12 ME. F2 (Pachon) 4.8 ME, F2 (Sabinos) 4.63 ME. Backcross to cave fish about 3.6 ME. Backcross to surface fish about 6.1 to 6.3 ME.
2. **Distribution patterns:** F2 and backcrosses to cave fish are normally distributed. The backcross to surface fish is bimodal, which suggests a non-additive genetic effect. F2 means are slightly displaced toward the surface form, while most other crosses lie closer to it.
3. **Eye structures:** Lens and pupil correlate closely, and eyeball correlates less strongly with lens and pupil. Pupil-to-lens allometry in hybrids of equal body length is positive, while lens growth in surface fish is isometric.
4. **Three peaks:** Lens and pupil size frequencies in F2 hybrids show three peaks that correspond to each other and recur across crosses. Some F2 eyes have no lens: 20.4% in Group I (n = 615) and 61.7% in Group II for F2RPa.
5. **Variability:** Variability of lens size at constant pupil size increases with the number of degenerative genes. Pupil variability at constant eyeball size is lowest in F1 and backcrosses to surface fish, and in backcrosses to cave fish.
6. **Left-right asymmetry:** Asymmetry is not strongly tied to degeneration. Only 0.17‰ of 4366 F2 fish lacked one eye. Most asymmetries are under 4%. The author attributes asymmetry to disturbed genetic balance rather than gene number.
7. **Population comparison:** No significant difference was found in eye diameter, lens/pupil frequencies, or implied gene number between Pachon and Sabinos. Pigment differences are attributed to one or two genes, and the author doubts that a phylogenetic gradient exists.
8. **Gene number estimates:** A tri- to tetrahybrid inheritance is estimated in F2 and a hexa- to heptahybrid inheritance in backcrosses to cave fish. The ratio of surface-sized eyes was 1:322 in F2Sa and 1:151 in F2Pa, and 1:53 and 1:110 in the backcrosses. The author notes these are minimum estimates, since linked "supergenes" and modifiers may inflate the count.
9. **Interpretation:** Eye genes act hierarchically and are better called "eye genes" than separate lens, pupil or eyeball genes. Eye size depends on which genes are combined, not only their number. Correlated allometry is considered necessary for functional vision and not subject to selection.
10. **Vision:** Hybrids with eyes at least as large as the F1 are sighted. Impairment appears only in smaller eyes, with overgrowth, lens and pupil loss, and blindness.
11. **Limitations:** The author stresses modifiability, uncertain measurements in lively fish, small numbers of certain classes, and a need for further work on Pachon versus Sabinos genetic identity.
