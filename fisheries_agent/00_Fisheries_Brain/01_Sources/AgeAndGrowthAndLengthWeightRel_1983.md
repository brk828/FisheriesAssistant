## Executive Summary

This study examines the age, growth, and length-weight relationship of [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) from the [[Coachella Canal]], an introduced population in southeastern California descending from fish stocked into Martinez Lake, Arizona in 1962. As the species is native to the [[Rio Grande-Mississippi River Complex]], this represents novel baseline data for a non-native population established in the [[Lower Colorado River]] drainage. Using rotenone sampling and pectoral spine sectioning for age determination, researchers found that flathead catfish comprised roughly 1.0% (94 of ~9,000 fish) of the total fish community sampled. Growth rates in the Coachella Canal population were comparable to, or exceeded, those reported for populations within the species' native range, while the length-weight relationship more closely resembled riverine rather than reservoir populations — consistent with the canal's lotic (flowing water) characteristics.

## Background & Description

- **Measurement protocol**: Total length measured from tip of lower jaw to tip of caudal fin (cm); weight recorded in pounds, later converted to metric units.
- **Age determination structure**: Pectoral spines, disarticulated and excised, cleaned of soft tissue with dilute potassium hydroxide, sectioned with a jeweler's saw (following [[Turner 1977]] methodology), polished, and examined under reflected light via binocular microscope.
- **Spine growth correlation**: Spine diameter at the sectioning point was highly correlated with fish total length (**r = 0.98**), described by the regression:
  - **Y = 13.6 + 137X**
- **Length-Weight Relationship**:
  - **Log W = −5.2500 + 3.1441 Log L**
  - This relationship more closely resembles riverine flathead catfish populations than reservoir populations, likely reflecting the canal's flowing-water (lotic) habitat conditions.
- **Back-calculation formula** used for length at annulus formation:
  - L' = C + (S'/S)(L − C)
  - Where L' = length at annulus "x", L = length at capture, S' = anterior spine radius at nth annulus, S = total anterior spine radius, C = intercept from least-squares regression of length vs. spine diameter.
- **Annulus formation timing**: No annuli were observed near spine edges in any age group, suggesting annulus formation occurs in January, the coolest month in the region, after which further growth occurs before the next annulus is laid down.
- **Age structure**: Age groups from 0 (young-of-year, clearly segregated by size-frequency distribution) through at least Age Group 8 were represented in the sample of 75 aged fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Introduced population inhabiting an engineered irrigation canal system (Coachella Canal), sampled across three isolated sections (blocked by nets and earthen dikes) during a water outage, including a concrete box siphon structure.
- **Sampling method**: Emulsifiable rotenone applied at >2.5 mg/liter active ingredient; affected fish collected by nets and seines.
- **Community composition**: Flathead catfish co-occurred with several other introduced and native fishes, including:
  - [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*)
  - [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*)
  - [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)
  - [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*)
  - [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*)
  - [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
  - All of these species are documented dietary components of flathead catfish in the [[Lower Colorado River]] (per [[Minckley 1982]]).
- **Density and biomass**:
  - Overall fish density: 0.55–1.08 individuals/m²
  - Biomass in open canal sections: 49.4 and 102.0 g/m²
  - Biomass in the siphon structure: 593.9 g/m² (flathead catfish comprised ~25% of this biomass)
- **Trophic role**: As a large-bodied piscivore, flathead catfish occupy an apex/near-apex predator niche within this introduced fish assemblage, consistent with their known predatory role in the broader lower Colorado River system.

## Key Findings

1. Flathead catfish represented approximately **1.0% of total fish abundance** (94 of ~9,000 individuals) sampled from the Coachella Canal via rotenone.
2. **Growth rates** of Coachella Canal flathead catfish were **comparable to or exceeded** those reported from native-range reservoirs and rivers (e.g., Grand Lake OK, Lake Texoma OK, Blue and Neosho Rivers KS, Kansas River) as documented in prior studies ([[Jenkins 1954]], [[McCoy 1953]], [[Cross and Hastings 1956]], [[Minckley and Deacon 1959]]).
3. The strong correlation (r = 0.98) between pectoral spine diameter and fish length validates the use of spine sectioning as a reliable, non-lethal-adjacent aging technique for this species.
4. Back-calculated lengths at annuli aligned well with independent length-frequency distribution data, supporting the validity of age assignments.
5. The **length-weight relationship** (Log W = −5.2500 + 3.1441 Log L) more closely resembles values typical of **riverine populations** rather than reservoir populations, suggesting habitat structure (canal flow regime) influences body condition patterns similarly to natural river systems.
6. This paper provides the **first published age, growth, and length-weight data** for flathead catfish from the introduced southeastern California population, filling a data gap for this non-native range extension following the species' 1962 introduction to the [[Lower Colorado River]] system.
7. No spine annuli were found near the spine margin in any age class, indicating a **consistent winter (January) annulus formation period**, coinciding with the coolest regional temperatures.
