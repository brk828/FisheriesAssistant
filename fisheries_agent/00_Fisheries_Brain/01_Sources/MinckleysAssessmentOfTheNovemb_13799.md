# Assessment of Humpback Chub Research Program Review - Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (1991)

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #program-review

## Executive Summary

This document is a comprehensive assessment letter from Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) to Dr. Duncan T. Patten evaluating a peer review of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) research program conducted under [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) on 20-21 November 1991. The review panel consisted of three external scientists (Drs. James E. Deacon, Hiram Li, and William J. Matthews) who evaluated ongoing research by four organizations: BIO/WEST, Arizona State University/Navajo Natural Heritage Program (ASU/NNHP), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGF), and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS). The assessment addresses scientific design quality, research progress, and management applicability for [[Humpback Chub]] conservation in the context of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. Major concerns included lack of research integration, insufficient experimental approaches to thermal and energetics questions, and inadequate coordination of early life-history studies.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is an endangered cyprinid endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system. The research program under review was designed to understand the species' biology and support conservation measures, including potential re-establishment of a second breeding population. The program operated under anticipated budget constraints of approximately 40% reduction.

The review panel evaluated three fundamental questions: (1) scientific design quality of proposed/ongoing research, (2) substantial progress by contracting organizations, and (3) direct benefit to [[Humpback Chub]] management and/or [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations management to avoid adverse impacts or enhance the species.

[[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations altered three basic features of the [[Colorado River]]: (a) water temperature, (b) discharge magnitude, and (c) temporal nature of discharge variations. These changes formed the environmental context for understanding impacts on [[Humpback Chub]] populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) serves as a critical tributary habitat for [[Humpback Chub]], with fish movements documented between the mainstem [[Colorado River]] and the LCR. The review noted remarkably large populations of chubs present in the system, with thousands of individuals handled during research operations with essentially no mortality—described as "a major achievement of this project."

The panel emphasized that management decisions for the species must address fundamental ecological questions applicable to any species: what limits reproduction, survival to recruitment, and adult life processes that ensure ongoing reproduction. These questions must be evaluated in the context of dam-induced environmental changes.

Critical life-history stages requiring study include:
- **Adult ecology**: Movement patterns, habitat use, food habits, reproductive behavior
- **Spawning**: Direct observation of spawning behavior and site selection
- **Early life history**: Larval and juvenile survival, predation, bioenergetics, habitat requirements

The review identified scale-dependent habitat considerations: adult habitat use should be analyzed at reach or macrohabitat levels, while larval and juvenile studies require much finer-scale microhabitat data.

## Key Findings

**Overall Program Assessment:**
- The program will satisfy many conservation requirements for understanding [[Humpback Chub]] biology and potential population re-establishment
- Major deficiencies identified: (a) lack of integration among four ongoing studies, (b) considerable effort in areas with less direct applicability to management, (c) lack of experimental approaches to thermal and energetics questions (especially for early life stages), and (d) apparent lack of planning toward ultimate synthesis
- Panel recommended authorizing intentional take for selected experimental studies, given large population size and demonstrated low handling mortality

**BIO/WEST Program:**
- Well conceived and progressing satisfactorily
- Radiotracking and tagging efforts effective
- Microhabitat quantification questioned unless paired with bioenergetics or experimental studies
- Food habits emphasis questioned for adults (condition indicates no food limitation) but remains critical for early life stages
- Recommended increased emphasis on documenting fish movements between mainstem and [[Little Colorado River]], with greater cooperation with ASU/NNHP
- Suggested use of sonic tags in LCR

**ASU/NNHP Program:**
- Tag-recapture and basic ecology in [[Little Colorado River]] well conceived and progressing satisfactorily
- Initial emphasis on tagging substantial numbers (by necessity) will shift to recapture and interpretation of movements after 1992 spawning season
- Direct spawning observation attempts strongly supported as necessary to program success
- Recommended experimental approach: hormone injection of near-ripe, radiotagged fish, release, then tracking to spawning sites
- Laboratory and field larval studies (emphasizing predation) considered unlikely to produce highly significant results as proposed; positive criticisms provided for streamlining
- Coordination problems with USFWS in LCR identified as requiring resolution through negotiations

**Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGF):**
- Program and progress deemed questionable, especially regarding sub-projects
- Monitoring at mouth of [[Little Colorado River]] deemed highly valuable and should continue "at all costs" (provides long-term data set)
- Early life-history studies in mainstem and LCR only recently started and should be re-thought
- Consensus that AZGF should test specific hypotheses rather than attempting to "do everything"
- Questioned activities included: organic drift evaluation, water quality monitoring, chub otolith analyses (beyond length-frequency calibration), trout studies, and whole fish community emphasis
- Most important contribution: high-quality data in spring monitoring effort in cooperation with other investigators

**U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS):**
- Adult habitat studies questioned regarding scale of study
- 1991 habitat mapping should have been immediately analyzed to document upstream-downstream heterogeneity in LCR before designating sampling reaches
- Sampling reaches should not be arbitrarily designated for convenience unless demonstrated representative
- Scale questions: proposed scales possibly too coarse for larvae and young-of-year, too fine for adults
- Artificial habitat use questioned at proposed scale (except perhaps for young-of-year)
- Sampling protocols should be integrated with ASU/NNHP to minimize costs in manpower and time

**Critical Early Life-History Integration Issue:**
- Greatest program risk identified: insufficient or diffuse data on larval/juvenile biology despite all groups having responsibility or interest
- Knowledge of early life stages critical to understanding [[Humpback Chub]] biology
- Information in danger of "falling through the cracks" without direction and integration
- **Two alternatives proposed:**
  1. Consolidate all early life-history studies under single leader from GCES central office with direction and power to integrate (strongly favored)
  2. Direct principal investigators to plan and execute integrated program specifically for early life history
- Under centralized direction: AZGF collaborates with BIO/WEST (mainstem) and ASU/NNHP (LCR); USFWS assigned habitat work and experimental bioenergetics studies with larvae/juveniles

**Review Process Recommendations:**
- External review panel approach validated as appropriate
- Future reviews should incorporate some new panelists while retaining continuity
- Future reviews should be more external to GCES management, funding, and permitting agencies
- **Major error identified**: Lack of private sessions between panel and individual principal investigators
- Meeting overcrowded with uninvited attendees (14 invited, room filled over capacity with ~20+ people including 4-5 GCES project office representatives, funding agency representatives, permitting personnel)
- Overcrowding resulted in uncomfortable conditions, brief questions, superficial clarifications, unsatisfactory results
- **Recommended protocol**: Half-hour presentation by principal investigator followed by at least one hour of private examination and clarification with panelists only
- Project manager orientation session at beginning remains necessary and acceptable

**Budget Context:**
- Anticipated 40% budget reduction in coming year
- Panel recommendations for experimental work may seem inappropriate given budget constraints
- Review conducted for scientific merits, not based on funding considerations
- Suggestion that some field operations be de-emphasized to accommodate experimental work
