# Pharyngeal Teeth in the Endangered Kendall Warm Springs Dace (Rhinichthys osculus thermalis)

## Executive Summary
This study by William R. Gould addresses a long-standing question about the taxonomic validity of the [[Kendall Warm Springs Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus thermalis*), an endangered subspecies of [[Speckled Dace]] restricted to a single thermal spring in western Wyoming. The original 1937 subspecies designation (Hubbs and Kuhne) relied on meristic traits (lateral line scale counts, fin ray counts) known to be plastic and influenced by water temperature, casting doubt on whether *R. o. thermalis* represents a genuinely distinct, genetically differentiated population rather than an artifact of its unusually warm (~29°C) habitat. To resolve this, the author examined **pharyngeal tooth formulas**, a previously unstudied trait believed to be under stronger genetic control and less susceptible to thermal plasticity. Comparisons between Kendall dace and nearby *R. o. yarrowi* populations (Duck Creek, Green River, Boulder Creek), including laboratory-reared fish held at controlled temperatures, demonstrated that Kendall dace have a significantly higher frequency of pharyngeal tooth formulas containing a "zero" in a minor row (86%) compared to *R. o. yarrowi* (only ~1%). Because this difference persisted regardless of rearing temperature, the study concludes that the dental distinction is genetically based rather than a thermally-induced artifact, supporting continued recognition of *R. o. thermalis* as a valid subspecies and its continued endangered listing. Preliminary mitochondrial DNA data corroborated this genetic distinctiveness.

## Background & Description
- **Trait studied:** Pharyngeal tooth formula (counted per [[Hubbs and Lagler Method]], 1958/1964), examined using a 10–45X dissecting microscope.
- **General cyprinid pattern:** Across *Rhinichthys osculus* populations from Arizona to Canada, the dominant pharyngeal tooth formulas range from **1,4-4,1 to 2,4-4,2**, showing remarkable dental uniformity across a huge geographic range.
- **Kendall Warm Springs Dace (*R. o. thermalis*):**
  - 86% of specimens exhibited a dental formula with a **zero in a minor row** (e.g., 0,4-4,0; 0,4-4,1; 1,4-4,0; 2,4-4,0).
  - Most common formula: **0,4-4,0** (62% of sampled individuals).
- **R. o. yarrowi (parental/surrounding populations):**
  - Only ~1% of specimens (across Duck Creek, Green River, and Boulder Creek sites) showed a zero-tooth minor row formula.
  - Dominant formulas were **2,4-4,2** and **1,4-4,2 / 2,4-4,1**, i.e., higher, more "complete" minor row tooth counts.
- **Comparative context:** Zero-tooth formulas are otherwise extremely rare in the species complex, reported only in *R. o. thermalis*, *R. o. nevadensis*, and *R. o. oligoporus* (the latter at only 13% frequency) — all isolated, spring/relict populations, consistent with a broader pattern of **tooth element reduction in isolated Rhinichthys populations** (Hubbs et al., 1974).
- **Related taxa comparison:** *R. cataractae* and *R. atratulus* almost universally possess two teeth in both minor rows (i.e., 2,4-4,2), representing the opposite extreme from Kendall dace.
- Reduced meristic traits historically used for subspecies diagnosis (lateral line scales; dorsal/anal/pectoral/pelvic fin ray counts; combined character index) are known to be **temperature-plastic** (Taning 1952; Harlow 1961), unlike the pharyngeal tooth trait examined here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Type locality:** Kendall Warm Springs, a single short thermal spring near Pinedale, Wyoming, at ~2,300 m elevation, discharging into the upper [[Green River]] over a barrier falls.
- **Isolation:** The falls prevents upstream immigration of *R. o. yarrowi* from the Green River, isolating the Kendall dace population for an estimated "several thousand years" (Hubbs and Kuehne, 1937).
- **Thermal regime:** Water temperature is unusually warm, approximately **29°C** in summer — markedly warmer than typical speckled dace habitats.
- **Comparative populations sampled:**
  - *R. o. yarrowi* — Duck Creek (downstream of Kendall Warm Springs)
  - *R. o. yarrowi* — Green River (upstream of Kendall Warm Springs)
  - *R. o. yarrowi* — Boulder Creek (upstream of Kendall Warm Springs)
- **Broader species range:** *Rhinichthys osculus* (species complex) occupies a vast range from the [[Columbia River Basin]] to the [[Colorado River Drainage]] in western North America, having diversified into at least 11 recognized subspecies across highly varied habitats (Schultz 1934; Kimsey and Fisk 1960; La Rivers 1962; Baxter and Simon 1970; Minckley 1973; Hubbs et al. 1974).
- **Conservation status:** Habitat is extremely restricted (single spring), rendering the population highly vulnerable to habitat degradation; listed as endangered by USFWS (1987).

## Key Findings
- **Zero-formula frequency:** 86% of *R. o. thermalis* specimens had a pharyngeal tooth formula with a zero in a minor row, versus only ~1% of all sampled *R. o. yarrowi* (Fisher's Exact Test, significant differences, P < 0.05).
- **No significant heterogeneity among R. o. yarrowi source populations** (Duck Creek, Green River, Boulder Creek) — all were statistically similar to each other and distinct from Kendall dace.
- **Two-teeth-in-minor-row formulas** (e.g., 2,4-4,2) were significantly more frequent in *R. o. yarrowi* than in Kendall dace, reinforcing the dental divergence.
- **Temperature-controlled rearing experiment:** Laboratory-reared *R. o. yarrowi* offspring (from Duck Creek stock) raised at 18°C, 24°C, and 28°C showed **no significant difference** in the proportion with zero-tooth formulas among temperature treatments — and if anything, the relationship between temperature and zero-formula frequency appeared **inverse** to what a thermal-plasticity hypothesis would predict.
- **Comparison of pooled lab-reared R. o. yarrowi vs. wild R. o. thermalis** still showed a significantly greater proportion of zero-formulas in Kendall dace, mirroring the direct field comparison.
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** Because pharyngeal tooth formula appears genetically controlled and is not attributable to thermal environment, and because the 86% vs. ~1% distinction exceeds the diagnostic threshold recommended by [[Mayr et al. 1953 Subspecies Criteria]], the data support retaining *R. o. thermalis* as a valid, genetically distinct subspecies.
- **Corroborating genetic evidence:** Preliminary mitochondrial DNA analysis (E. Vyse, Montana State University, pers. comm.) found restriction-enzyme evidence of only **one polymorphic mtDNA site in R. o. thermalis** versus **five in R. o. yarrowi**, along with other genetic differences — independently supporting genetic distinctiveness between the two forms.
- **Conservation implication:** Findings support continued endangered species listing for the [[Kendall Warm Springs Dace]], countering earlier concerns that its subspecific status was merely an artifact of thermally plastic meristic traits.
