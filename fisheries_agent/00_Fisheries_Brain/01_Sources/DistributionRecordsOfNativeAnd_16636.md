# Distribution Records of Native and Exotic Fishes in Pacific Drainages of Northern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1983 faunistic paper by [[Dean A. Hendrickson]] (Arizona State University) reports previously unpublished freshwater fish distribution records from Pacific drainages of Mexico, based on collections made in July 1980 and January 1981. The records are drawn from 30 numbered localities, with specimens deposited in the [[Collection of Fishes at Arizona State University]]. Key outcomes are the first record of [[Gila purpurea]] in the [[Rio Concepción]] system, range extensions of several cyprinids and catostomids farther south than previously reported, and evidence that numerous [[Exotic Fish Species]] have become established with undocumented effects on native fauna.

## Background & Description

The freshwater fish fauna of Mexico was described in the source as poorly studied and changing rapidly because of habitat alteration and introductions. Documenting natural ranges is a prerequisite for systematic and biogeographic work, and documenting establishment of exotics is needed to analyze their dispersal and interactions with native species.

Collections used [[Seine Netting]], gill nets, and [[Backpack Electrofishing]] gear. A species was reported only if it was a new record for a drainage or resolved confusion in the literature. Predominantly marine species occurring far inland were excluded, as their general distributions were already summarized by [[Castro-Aguirre 1978]].

Table 1 lists catalog numbers of specimens by locality for native and exotic taxa, including [[Carassius auratus]], [[Cyprinus carpio]], [[Poecilia reticulata]], [[Xiphophorus helleri]], [[Lepomis macrochirus]], [[Tilapia aurea]] and [[Micropterus salmoides]]. Specimens of one [[Xiphophorus]] form agreed with *X. maculatus* except for a midventral black caudal peduncle stripe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Localities span Sonoran and Sinaloan coastal-slope rivers (Mayo, Fuerte, Sinaloa, Culiacán, San Lorenzo), the Santiago-Lerma-Chapala system in Jalisco and Guanajuato, and smaller streams and lagoons such as [[Laguna Chapala]], [[Laguna de Sayula]] and [[Laguna El Rosario]]. Many collections were near major habitat alterations such as dams, including the Presa Huites and Presa Bacurato construction sites.

Native species were noted to be broadly sympatric in northwestern Mexico, co-occurring with [[Catostomus]] species of the [[Catostomus insignis-bernardini Complex]] and western ictalurids. Exotic species are associated with food and sport introductions (Tilapia and centrarchids). Native [[Cichlasoma beani]] now occurs syntopically with centrarchids and tilapia, and the native predator *Gila robusta* occurs with largemouth bass. Impacts of these exotics remain largely unstudied.

## Key Findings

- **Gila purpurea** is reported from the Rio Concepción system at locality 1 (La Atascosa, Sonora), the first record for that system. The congeneric *Gila ditaenia*, endemic to the Rio Concepción, was syntopic with it and occurred above and below the locality.
- ***Gila robusta*** collections at localities 3, 7, 8 and 11 agree with [[Smith et al. 1979]] mapping. Smith et al. also reported the species from the Rio Culiacán, which is not unexpected. The author found no records from the Rio Piaxtla, correcting an interpretation by [[Holden and Minckley 1980]].
- ***Agosia chrysogaster*** occurrences at localities 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 13 confirm its presence in the Mayo, Fuerte and Sinaloa rivers, where it appears rare in the Sinaloa. This extends the southern limit mapped by [[Minckley 1980]] as uncertain.
- ***Campostoma ornatum*** is documented from the Rio Mayo (localities 3 and 4), a record apparently unknown to [[Burr 1976]].
- The ***Catostomus insignis-bernardini*** complex (hypothesized by [[Miller 1976]] to be conspecific across Gila, Yaqui and Conchos basins) was collected at localities 2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 11, 12, 13, 15, 16 and 17, extending its range far south.
- **Unidentified *Ictalurus*** resembling *I. pricei* were recorded at localities 4, 7, 8, 11, 12, 14, 17, 18 and 19. Their systematics are being studied by J. Humphries and R. R. Miller.
- **Exotic species** are tabulated as established, with undocumented impacts on native fauna. Historical documentation is lacking, and specimen-based confirmation is needed for later analysis.

**Study scope:** 30 numbered localities across Sonora, Sinaloa, Zacatecas, Jalisco, Guanajuato and Mexico State, sampled 20 January to 30 July (1980 and 1981 seasons). Permits 6148 and 9091 were issued by the Dirección General de Regulación Pesquera, Mexico.
