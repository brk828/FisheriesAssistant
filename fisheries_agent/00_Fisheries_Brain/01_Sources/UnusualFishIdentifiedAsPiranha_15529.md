# Unusual Piranha Caught in Houghton Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #piranha

## Executive Summary

A news item reports that [[Robert Pennel]], of Houghton Lake and Dimondale, caught a [[Piranha]], an exotic South American fish, while fishing for [[Walleye]] off [[Long Point]] on [[Houghton Lake]]. The fish was identified at [[Betty's Pet and Garden Center]] after a [[Department of Natural Resources]] officer, [[Jean Zelenak]], brought it in. The store's staff attributed its presence to a release by a former aquarium owner, since piranha cannot survive a winter in the lake. The article is anecdotal and contains no scientific survey data.

## Background & Description

- **Catch:** On a Saturday around 2 p.m., Pennel and a companion were fishing for walleye off Long Point. The fish took a leech bait and reportedly jumped about six feet out of the water. Pennel first thought it was a bass.
- **Identification:** Pennel's companion suggested piranha. Pennel took the fish to the state police post, which contacted the DNR. Zelenak initially thought it might be a hybrid, then took it to Betty's Pet and Garden Center, where a resident piranha in a tank looked essentially the same.
- **Size and appearance:** The caught fish measured 8 to 10 inches. It was reddish in color and had feathery fins. [[Dawn Dusseau]], of Betty's, judged it to be an older fish with a problem in one eye and considerably worn teeth. The store's tank fish is young.
- **Growth reference:** Dusseau stated that piranha typically reach 24 inches in South American rivers, and that a fish would need at least two years to reach the caught fish's size in a tank. Growth is limited by the size of the environment.
- **Behavior (as reported by Dusseau):** Captive piranha are described as shy and not meat eaters. They eat flake food and retreat to the far end of the tank during cleaning. Dusseau said wild piranha will bite when confronted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Temperature tolerance:** Dusseau said piranha require water above 65°F. Below that temperature, they reportedly develop parasites and their immune systems shut down. She concluded that a fish released in summer would not survive winter in the lake.
- **Introduction pathway:** Zelenak suggested that the fish was likely released by an owner who "got tired of it or moved." This is a form of [[Exotic Species Introduction]] by aquarium release, which can place non-native species in waters where they cannot establish permanently.
- **Native range comparison:** The article contrasts the lake setting with the [[South American Rivers]] where piranha are native and grow much larger.

## Key Findings

1. **Identification:** A fish caught off Long Point was identified as a piranha, an exotic South American species, with a resident tank piranha at Betty's used as a comparison.
2. **Likely origin:** Officials believe the fish was released into the lake during summer by an owner. The article does not report any sampling or confirmation of other individuals.
3. **Survival limits:** According to Dusseau, piranha cannot survive the winter temperatures of the lake, so the establishment of a wild population is considered unlikely based on these statements.
4. **Individual condition:** The fish was an older individual (8 to 10 inches) with an eye problem and worn teeth, which Dusseau interpreted as signs of age.
5. **Behavior claims:** Captive piranha are described as shy and non-aggressive, while wild piranha are said to bite when threatened. These claims come from a store employee and are not supported by research cited in the article.
6. **Outcome:** Pennel planned to have the fish mounted as a trophy.

**Limitations:** The article is a local news report. It provides no scientific name, no sample size beyond a single fish, no data on water chemistry or lake ecology, and no formal verification of the species. Temperature and growth statements are attributed to a pet store employee and are not independently verified.
