## Executive Summary

This 1958–1959 completion report from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Project F-7-R-1) presents results of a comparative **creel census** conducted on three District 1 lakes in the White Mountain region: [[Show Low Lake]], [[Luna Lake]], and [[Woods Canyon Lake]]. The study was designed to evaluate stocking practices and management recommendations for the region's [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery, one of the most heavily used trout resources in Arizona (480,000 rainbow trout fry stocked across these lakes in 1957 alone). Data were collected via randomized creel checks, traffic counters, and total angler counts, tracking species composition, catch rates, angler origin, and size/growth of harvested fish. Findings indicate variable fishery quality across lakes tied to stocking density, presence of competing "trash fish" species (bluegill, bullhead), and lake productivity/sterility, leading to specific 1959 stocking adjustments for each system.

## Background & Description

Size and growth data were the primary morphological metrics collected (total length in inches, weight in pounds), reflecting growth rate and condition rather than detailed anatomical description.

- **[[Show Low Lake]] Trout Harvest:** Average total length 11.4 in; total harvest 13,306 fish (7,042 lbs; 68 lbs/acre). Monthly average length ranged from a low of 9.0 in (November) to a high of 11.7 in (July), reflecting fry recruitment and seasonal growth patterns. One-to-two inch fingerlings stocked April 1957 reached ~11.5 in average length about one year later — described as "very good" growth and condition.

- **[[Luna Lake]] Trout Harvest:** Average total length 12.8 in; total harvest 19,133 fish (15,306 lbs; 109 lbs/acre) — the highest yield per acre of the three lakes. Average length increased from 11.2 in (February) to a peak of 13.8 in (July), dipping to 12.6 in (August) due to recruitment of new spring fry, then recovering by November. Occasional large fish (16–21 in) recorded in January/December. Condition and growth were rated "excellent," with 1957 fry/fingerling plants averaging 12.5 in by spring 1958.

- **[[Woods Canyon Lake]] Trout Harvest:** Average total length only 8.0 in — markedly poor growth attributed to a sterile, newly formed lake bed and overstocking. Gill-net sampling in May 1958 showed average trout size of only 7.3 in, "well below the state average," with no measurable increase by July resampling. Harvest totaled 10,691 fish (1,798 lbs; 36 lbs/acre). [[Kamloops Trout]] (an experimental rainbow subspecies introduction, fin-clipped adipose-anal for identification) showed no growth advantage over standard rainbow trout, with only ~4% of marked fish returned to creel.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lake origins and hydrology:** All three water bodies are artificial impoundments used for both irrigation/mining storage and recreational trout fisheries.
  - [[Show Low Lake]]: Built 1951 by Phelps Dodge Corporation for mining runoff storage; 104 surface acres, 1,100 acre-foot minimum pool; drainage chemically treated pre-impoundment to remove rough fish.
  - [[Luna Lake]]: Originally an irrigation reservoir (550 acre-feet) on the Arizona–New Mexico border; dam raised in 1952 (+9.4 ft, +840 acre-feet) to maintain permanent pool; 155 surface acres at full pool, max depth 24 ft.
  - [[Woods Canyon Lake]]: Constructed 1956 under the federal aid lake development program; 1,014 acre-foot capacity, 51 surface acres; filled by heavy 1957 winter rains; described as relatively sterile, newly formed habitat.

- **Species interactions:** [[Show Low Lake]] exhibited an emerging multi-species community, with gill netting revealing increasing populations of [[Bluegill]] and [[Black Bullhead]] ("trash fish") competing with stocked trout, coinciding with an apparent lack of trout fingerling survival from the 1958 spring plant.

- **Trophic/productivity contrast:** Luna Lake's "natural food production" supported strong trout growth once fry-based stocking replaced catchable-size stocking after 1955. In contrast, Woods Canyon Lake's sterile new lake-bed substrate could not support the initial stocking density (120,000 fingerlings), producing stunted growth — a density-dependent food limitation effect.

- **Seasonal habitat use:** Across all lakes, fishing pressure (a proxy for accessibility/angler behavior tied to seasonal conditions) was concentrated April–September, with ice conditions in December suppressing catch rates at all sites.

## Key Findings

**Fishing Pressure & Harvest (1958):**

| Lake | Angler-Hours | Anglers | Trout Harvested | Other Species |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Show Low Lake]] | 39,370 | 11,279 | 13,306 | 2,774 bluegill; 320 bullhead |
| [[Luna Lake]] | 51,693 | 11,400 | 19,133 | none (100% trout) |
| [[Woods Canyon Lake]] | 11,216 | 3,251 | 10,635 (rainbow) + 26 Kamloops | — |

**Catch Rates (trout/man-hour):**
- Show Low Lake: 0.30 (up from 0.08 summer rate in 1957 → 0.17)
- Luna Lake: 0.36 (up from 0.18 in 1957)
- Woods Canyon Lake: 0.95 (highest, but reflecting overstocked/stunted population)

**Angler Origin:**
- Show Low Lake: 95% resident (Arizona), 5% nonresident; Maricopa/Pima counties dominant (52% of residents); nonresidents mostly New Mexico (47%) and California (25.1%).
- Luna Lake: Only 50.5% resident — heavy nonresident use reflecting proximity to New Mexico border (78.1% of nonresidents from NM).
- Woods Canyon Lake: 99.6% resident, overwhelmingly Maricopa County (73.2%).

**Stocking History Highlights:**
- Show Low Lake: cumulative fry/fingerling/catchable stocking 1954–1958, culminating in 287,500 fry in spring 1958.
- Luna Lake: progressive increase in fry stocking (50,000 in 1955 → 216,575 in 1958), plus experimental introduction of 82,000 [[Kokanee Salmon]] fry in 1958 — outcome to be evaluated in 1959.
- Woods Canyon Lake: 120,000 rainbow fingerlings (1957) proved excessive for sterile new lake, causing stunting; reduced 1958 stocking (90,000) plus experimental Kamloops trial (585 fish).

**Unit Cost Evaluation** (Hess unit system — hours fished & lbs harvested per dollar invested):
- Show Low Lake: 17.56 units/$1.00
- Luna Lake: 20.25 units/$1.00
- Woods Canyon Lake: 22.25 units/$1.00

**Management Recommendations:**
1. Hold Show Low Lake's 1959 fingerling quota at 1958 levels pending assessment of the expanding bluegill/bullhead population.
2. Increase Luna Lake's 1959 spring fingerling stocking by 10% and fall stocking by 20%; continue monitoring the introduced [[Kokanee Salmon]] population.
3. Reduce Woods Canyon Lake's 1959 fingerling density to 650 fish/surface acre and supplement with periodic 10-inch catchable plants to relieve stunting and encourage harvest of smaller fish.
4. Continue creel census programs at all three lakes in 1959, with reduced intensity at Woods Canyon Lake, to track effects of revised stocking strategies.

**Overall Fishery Quality Ranking (1958):** Growth/condition of trout population — Luna Lake (excellent) > Show Low Lake (good) > Woods Canyon Lake (poor).
