## Executive Summary
This source is an archaeological/geoarchaeological paper by Michael R. Waters (1985) examining the chronological placement and megafaunal associations of the **Sulphur Spring stage** of the Cochise Culture along Whitewater Draw, southeastern Arizona. The paper is concerned with radiocarbon dating of alluvial deposits, ground-stone and flaked-stone artifact assemblages, and the contested association between human artifacts and Pleistocene megafauna (mammoth, camel, horse, dire wolf, bison). **No fish taxa, fish morphology, fish ecology, or aquatic ecosystem data (e.g., water chemistry, plankton, invertebrates) are discussed anywhere in this text.** Consequently, no ichthyological or aquatic-science content can be extracted from this source.

## Key Findings
- Twenty-two radiocarbon dates (12 associated with artifacts, 10 from alluvium) place the **Sulphur Spring stage** between approximately **8,000 and 10,000 yr B.P.**, with a possible maximum age of **10,400 yr B.P.** based on a single date near Double Adobe.
- Radiocarbon dates from artifact-bearing deposits:
  - AZ FF:6:9: 8,390 ± 190 to 8,650 ± 180 yr B.P.
  - AZ FF:6:8: 8,140 ± 220 to 9,340 ± 180 yr B.P.
  - AZ FF:10:1 (Double Adobe): 8,760 ± 210 to 8,840 ± 310 yr B.P.
  - Correlative Double Adobe deposits: 8,270 ± 250 to 9,120 ± 270 yr B.P.
  - Nearby gravel with mammoth remains (no artifacts): 10,420 ± 100 yr B.P.
  - Lehner site (overlying Clovis horizon): 9,860 ± 80 and 9,900 ± 80 yr B.P.
- **No extinct faunal remains** were found in Sulphur Spring artifact-bearing deposits at AZ FF:6:8, AZ FF:6:9, or AZ FF:10:14.
- The only artifact–megafauna association occurs at AZ FF:10:1, but most bones are disarticulated and likely reworked from older sediments; only two articulated finds (camel leg bones; mammoth lumbar vertebrae) are considered primary, and the mammoth find dates to ~10,400 yr B.P., predating most dated Sulphur Spring artifact contexts.
- The author concludes that the Sulphur Spring stage generally **postdates** the extinction of Pleistocene megafauna in the region, except for a possible brief temporal overlap during the terminal Pleistocene, and is **chronologically and culturally distinct from the Clovis Culture**.
