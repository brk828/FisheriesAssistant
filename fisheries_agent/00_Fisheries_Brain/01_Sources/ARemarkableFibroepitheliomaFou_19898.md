# A Remarkable Fibroepithelioma in a Marbled Sole, *Limanda yokohamae*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #flatfish

## Executive Summary

This case report describes a tumor-bearing [[Limanda Yokohamae]] (marbled sole) collected by gill net in [[Ryotsu Bay]], [[Sado Island]], [[Sea of Japan]], on 15 February 1991. The large, hard, papilloma-like growth on the non-eyed side was diagnosed histologically as a [[Fibroepithelioma]], a mesenchymal-type tumor dominated by dermal connective tissue hyperplasia. The author reports that this tumor type had not appeared in the earlier fish tumor literature he cited. The likely cause remains unresolved, with mechanical contact with the bottom and contaminant exposure considered but not confirmed.

## Background & Description

The paper opens by noting that benign epithelial tumors in fishes are most commonly papillomas: papillary or folded epidermal outgrowths supported by proliferating dermal connective tissue. Papillomas are characterized by an increase in undifferentiated basal cells (stratum germinativum) and a reduction in mucous and clavate cells. The author cites earlier Japanese reports of [[Papilloma]]-type growths in:

- a Japanese newt, *Cynops pyrrhogaster* (Honma and Murakawa, 1967)
- a witch flounder, *Glyptocephalus stelleri* (Honma and Kon, 1968)
- a puffer, *Takifugu niphobles* (Honma and Chiba, 1982)

A viral pox-type epidermal growth in a tree sparrow, *Passer montanus* (Honma and Chiba, 1976), is also mentioned.

The specimen was supplied by Mr. Eikichi Noda of the Niigata Prefectural Cultural Fisheries Center. Its external appearance resembled a papilloma, but histology revealed a fibroepithelioma, following the classification of [[Peters and Watermann]] (1979) and [[Peters]] (1984).

**Specimen measurements (as reported):**
- Total length: 270 mm
- Standard length: 220 mm
- Head: 57 mm
- Width: 110 mm
- Body weight: 355 g
- Ovaries: fairly mature

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Collection and tumor location.** The fish was taken by [[Gill Net]] off Tsubakio, Ryotsu Bay. The tumor covered a large area of the cephalo-thorax on the non-eyed side, from near the dorsal margin of the head to the pectoral fin. Its midline followed the curved portion of the lateral line. The surface was white with capillaries visible and no melanin invasion.

**Tumor dimensions (as reported):**
- Cephalo-caudal axis: 65 mm
- Dorso-ventral axis: 55 mm
- Height: 15 mm
- Attached by a short, thin stalk, 7 mm in diameter, located 15 mm from the tumor's anterior margin

The tumor was very hard and tough, unlike a soft, typical papilloma, and hung from its stalk.

**Proposed causes.**
- The author cites [[Peters and Watermann]] (1979) and [[Peters]] (1984), who considered mechanical stimulus from swimming against a tank or container to be a possible or apparent cause of excessive connective tissue growth in reared fish.
- The present fish was wild-caught, with the tumor on the side that would contact the marine sandy mud bottom. The author considers this habitat alone unlikely to produce massive growth.
- [[Wedemeyer and Goodyear]] (1984) and [[Sindermann]] (1990) suggest chronic, sublethal contaminant exposure or pollution as factors in epithelial tumors. The author notes that water quality around Sado Island is still high, so the real stress mediator is not apparent.

## Key Findings

1. **Diagnosis.** The growth is a fibroepithelioma, not a papilloma. Its diagnosis follows [[Peters and Watermann]] (1979) and [[Peters]] (1984).

2. **Histology.**
   - The tumor was almost entirely massive hyperplasia of dermal connective tissue, covered by a papillary layer of thin epithelium.
   - The epithelium had normal keratinized, mucous, and Malpighian (basal) layers, with relatively poor development of clavate cells.
   - Dense collagen bundles ran in parallel or in a herringbone pattern (Azan stain).
   - Blood canals were scattered through the dermis.
   - Several large vacuole-like spaces with scant fibrous content were likely produced by necrosis.
   - No epidermal polyp-like growth or epidermal hyperplasia was seen.
   - No melanin invasion occurred, because the growth was on the non-eyed side.

3. **Novelty.** The author states that this type of mesenchymal tumor had not been reported in the cited earlier fish tumor literature, including [[Takahashi]] (1929) and [[Schlumberger and Lucké]] (1948).

4. **Etiology unresolved.** Mechanical stress from confinement may explain tumors in reared fish, but not this wild-caught specimen. Contaminant exposure is suggested in the literature but not demonstrated here. The author concludes no obvious stress agent was identified, given the high water quality around Sado Island.

5. **Regional significance.** The paper adds a further documented neoplasm to Japanese marine fish pathology, following earlier records in the newt, witch flounder, and puffer.
