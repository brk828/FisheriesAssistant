# A New Anoptichthys Cave Population (Characidae, Pisces)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

Wilkens and Burns (1972, *Annales de Spéléologie*) describe a new cave form of the [[Anoptichthys]] group, called the "Micosfish," from the [[Micos Cave]] (Cueva del Río Subterráneo). Its eyes and pigmentation are less reduced than those of the known cave forms [[Pachonfish]] and [[Sabinosfish]], and its organ variability is greater. The authors therefore infer that this population is phylogenetically younger. The cave also holds the epigean ancestor, [[Astyanax Mexicanus]], and [[Sphenops Molly]]. The Micosfish feeds on bat guano and also preys on the other fish, which appear undernourished.

## Background & Description

- **Species group:** Hypogean (cave) derivatives of the normal-eyed, normal-pigmented epigean characin [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] (Filippi). Previously described forms are:
  - [[Chicafish]] (*Anoptichthys jordani*, Hubbs and Innes 1936b)
  - [[Sabinosfish]] (*A. hubbsi*) and [[Pachonfish]] (*A. antrobius*, Alvarez 1946)
- **Morphology of known cave forms:** Except for *A. jordani*, these forms show almost complete eye reduction and loss of almost all melanin pigment (Peters and Peters 1966; Wilkens 1970a–c).
- **Genetic inference:** Low variability in *A. antrobius* and *A. hubbsi* suggests they are genetically homozygous. The authors describe this as typical of phylogenetically old troglodyte forms (see [[Troglomorphism]]).
- **Timing:** Cave-adapted states in these forms arose convergently since the beginning of the [[Pleistocene]], and there is no complete allelism between the cave forms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The Micos Cave is a subterranean water body with a major bat roost. Bat guano is an important food source.
- **Associated fish:**
  - *Astyanax mexicanus*: the epigean ancestor of the Micosfish, which feeds on bat guano.
  - *Poecilia sphenops* (Poeciliidae): appears undernourished.
- **Trophic interactions:** The Micosfish feeds on bat guano and also on other fish. The authors suggest it preys on *Astyanax* and *Poecilia* as they weaken from competition in darkness.
- **Hybridization context:** Recent work shows that *A. jordani*, the only cave form with greater eye and pigment variability, resulted from secondary hybridization between a true cave fish and the epigean ancestor (Wilkens, in press c).

## Key Findings

1. **A new cave population:** The Micosfish is a new *Anoptichthys* cave form from the Micos Cave.
2. **Less regressive than known forms:** Eyes and pigmentation are less reduced than in [[Pachonfish]] and [[Sabinosfish]], and variability in these organs is greater.
3. **Inferred youth:** Greater variability and less reduction suggest the population is phylogenetically younger than the other cave forms.
4. **Diet and predation:** The Micosfish consumes bat guano and other fish. It probably feeds on weakened *Astyanax* and *Poecilia*.
5. **Competitive stress:** *Astyanax* and *Poecilia* appear undernourished, which the authors attribute to competition in the dark cave environment.
6. **Evolutionary context:** Cave adaptation in this group is convergent and dates to the Pleistocene. Variability patterns in [[Chicafish]] point to secondary hybridization with the epigean ancestor.
