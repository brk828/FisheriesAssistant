# Fish Passage Project: Geronimo's Cave Irrigation Diversion Repair

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This project report documents the redesign and repair of an irrigation diversion at [[Geronimo's Cave]] on the [[East Fork of the White River]], within the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] in Arizona. The work was planned and carried out by the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]] with the [[Arizona Fishery Resources Office-Pinetop]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The old diversion was suspected of blocking upstream fish movement under some flow conditions and was causing bank erosion. The replacement used a [[Cross Vane Weir]] and [[Root-Wad Revetment]]s made of ponderosa pine, plus revegetation with native plants. Preliminary geomorphic observations suggest reduced channel impacts. The document reports no quantitative fish passage measurements; the authors state that future evaluation is needed to confirm improved passage.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** The East Fork of the White River is a tributary of the Salt River and lies within the [[Gila River Basin]]. It originates at Mount Baldy (11,403 ft) in the Mount Baldy Wilderness Area and flows 18 miles to its confluence with the North Fork of the White River at about 5,200 ft elevation near Fort Apache. The watershed drains 138 square miles.

**Fish community.** The river supports several aquatic and riparian species:
- [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), listed as threatened and managed for both conservation and recreational fishing.
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Rhinichthys cobitis*), federally threatened and the subject of the Tribe's Loach Minnow Management Plan.
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarkii*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), all native fishes.

**Management context.** The Tribe depends on the river for agricultural irrigation. Numerous diversions on the lower reaches are maintained each year by the Land Operations Division of the Tribal Public Works Department. Routine maintenance removes gravel bar and debris blockages and resets displaced boulders. The Geronimo's Cave diversion was identified as one of a few structures that could hinder upstream fish movement during certain flow regimes ([[Fish Passage]] concern).

**Project history.**
- **Spring 1998:** Tribe, Land Operations Division, and the Arizona Fishery Resources Office-Pinetop drafted a redesign. Permits and environmental clearance were obtained.
- **1998 (pre-construction):** Photographs show eroded stream banks and turbid flow around the diversion.
- **November 1998:** Project initiated under oversight of the Watershed Program and Hydrology Department.
- **Winter 1998:** Diversion repair and bank stabilization work carried out.
- **August 2000:** Report prepared by Leslie Ruiz, Project Coordinator (Document No. USFWS-AZFRO-PT-00-020).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Design rationale.** The [[Cross Vane Weir]] was chosen to direct flow into the irrigation ditch and away from the streambanks. The root-wad revetments were intended to deflect water from the eroded bank and slow near-bank velocities. Together they aim to restore [[Channel Morphology]] and improve fish passage.

**Bank stabilization.** Multiple channels were dug into the bank. Ponderosa pine trees were laid longitudinally with root wads pointing upstream into the flow. Native willow and grass species were planted along the bank.

**Revegetation.** The Watershed Program transplanted sedges (*Carex senta*) and cattails (*Typha latifolia*) and reseeded native wetland plants ([[Riparian Revegetation]]). Many transplants appeared to be thriving and seedlings were observed. Beavers damaged some planted cottonwoods, though others continued to grow.

**Pre-construction fish survey.** [[Electrofishing Survey]]s conducted by the Arizona Fishery Resources Office-Pinetop before work began collected only trout in the immediate restoration area.

**Channel observations.** Preliminary measurements at several diversions, including Geronimo's Cave, were made by a tribal summer intern to prioritize rehabilitation sites. The reported differences at Geronimo's Cave, compared with other diversions, are described in Key Findings.

## Key Findings

**Preliminary geomorphic results (Geronimo's Cave vs. other diversions):**
1. Channel gradient change is less dramatic than at other diversions.
2. No braiding occurs upstream of the channel, unlike other diversions.
3. No scour pool forms below the diversion, unlike other diversions.
4. Bank erosion around the diversion has stopped, unlike in the past.

**Post-construction status:**
- The redesigned diversion and bank features remained intact through spring and summer runoff events, reported as two spring runoff events in the later update.
- Planted vegetation appeared to be thriving, and some seeds had established.
- Geomorphic evaluations suggest reduced impacts to the channel (braiding, downcutting, and bank erosion).
- The stream was reported to be no longer eroding its banks, which the authors interpret as restored function for this section of the East Fork.

**Expected fish passage benefit (not yet measured):**
- The lower diversion gradient and better flow distribution are expected to help fish negotiate the structure.
- The report explicitly states that future evaluation must determine how far the design improves fish passage.

**Stakeholder response:**
- Tribal cultural advisors reviewed the project and approved of the new design and the restored channel's appearance.

**Ongoing initiatives:**
- The evaluation of irrigation diversions will feed into the Watershed Program's watershed analysis and management plan for the East Fork, including a list of priority sites for addressing diversion impacts. Completion was expected within the following year, with early drafts possibly available that winter.
- Annual fishery surveys by the Wildlife and Outdoor Recreation Division and the Arizona Fishery Resources Office continue under the Loach Minnow Management Plan.

**Overall conclusion:** The project reduced erosion and channel disturbance at Geronimo's Cave, and its effect on fish passage is expected but not yet quantified in this document.
