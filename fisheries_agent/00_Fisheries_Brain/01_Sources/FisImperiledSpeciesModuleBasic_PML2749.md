# Imperiled Species Refugium Program: Dexter NFH & TC, FY2000 Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This FY2000 [[Imperiled Species Module]] report documents the refugium culture of 15 imperiled freshwater fish species at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]]. Twelve species are listed as endangered and three as threatened or of special concern. Each species is held as a genetically managed backup population to supplement wild stocks in case of emergency or disaster. The report lists funding per species, culture methods, and numbers of fish distributed during the year. The facility total is $571,960, while the Roll-Up total for spent funds is $510,404.

## Background & Description

The program is a [[Refugium Culture]] effort. Each entry gives the species' domestic status, the wild source strain, the year the refugium population was started, and the funding allocated. Culture began between 1974 and 1999, depending on the species. The stated goal for every species is to "maintain a genetically diverse population to supplement the wild population in case of emergency and/or disaster." Several entries also note that the [[Genetic Diversity]] of a stock was a management target.

Species and strains covered in the report:

- **Beautiful shiner** (threatened): [[Beautiful Shiner]], Arroyo Moctezuma, Chihuahua, Mexico. Cultured since 1978. Funding $15,782.
- **Big Bend gambusia** (endangered): [[Big Bend Gambusia]], Big Bend National Park, Texas. Cultured since 1974. The only other known population is in Big Bend National Park. Funding $15,782.
- **Bonytail** (endangered): [[Bonytail]], Lake Mohave wild stock. Cultured since 1981. The report calls it "the most endangered fish in North America at the present time." Funding $185,818, the largest single allocation.
- **Chihuahua chub** (threatened): [[Chihuahua Chub]], Mimbres River strain. Cultured since 1979. Funding $35,253.
- **Colorado pikeminnow** (endangered, three strains): [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], Colorado River subbasin (cultured since 1991), Green River subbasin (since 1980), and Colorado River/Green River, UT strain (since 1982). Combined funding $105,212.
- **Comanche Springs pupfish** (endangered): [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]], Phantom Lake Springs. Cultured since 1999. Funding $5,260.
- **Desert pupfish** (endangered): [[Desert Pupfish]], Santa Clara Slough, Sonora, Mexico. Cultured since 1983. Funding $15,782.
- **Gila topminnow** (endangered): [[Gila Topminnow]], Sharp Spring, Arizona. Cultured since 1976. Funding $15,782.
- **Leon Springs pupfish** (endangered): [[Leon Springs Pupfish]], Leon Creek/Diamond Y Spring, Texas. Cultured since 1974. Funding $24,732.
- **Pahranagat roundtail chub** (endangered): [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]], Pahranagat Valley. Cultured since 1985. Funding $15,782.
- **Pecos gambusia** (endangered): [[Pecos Gambusia]], Phantom Lake Springs. Cultured since 1999. Funding $5,260.
- **Razorback sucker** (endangered): [[Razorback Sucker]], Lake Mohave wild stock. Cultured since 1981. Funding $78,909.
- **Virgin River chub** (endangered): [[Virgin River Chub]], Virgin River wild strain. Cultured since 1989. Funding not legible in the scan; the facility total implies $15,782.
- **Woundfin** (endangered): [[Woundfin]], Virgin River. Cultured since 1979. Funding $26,303.
- **Yaqui sucker** (listed as threatened in the header; "species of special concern" in the Roll-Up): [[Yaqui Sucker]], Rio Bavispe, Sonora, Mexico. Cultured since 1996. Funding $10,521.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Culture methods depend on the species' life stage and the season. Most species are reared in [[Earthen Ponds]] of 0.1 to 1.0 acre, with some lined ponds. Over-winter survival is managed in the [[Fish Culture Building]]. The typical schedule is as follows:

- **April through November:** outdoor earthen ponds (0.1 acre) for beautiful shiner, Big Bend gambusia, desert pupfish, Gila topminnow, Leon Springs pupfish, and woundfin.
- **December through March:** indoor holding for the species listed above, to prevent over-winter mortality.
- **January through December:** year-round pond culture for bonytail (0.25 to 1.0 acre), Chihuahua chub (0.1 to 0.25 acre), Colorado pikeminnow (1.0 acre lined ponds), Pahranagat roundtail chub, razorback sucker, Virgin River chub, and Yaqui sucker.
- **Aquaria:** Comanche Springs pupfish and Pecos gambusia are held year-round in [[Aquaria]] inside the Fish Culture Building.

Several species depend on a single or very small set of wild populations. The report notes this for Big Bend gambusia (Big Bend National Park is the only other known population) and Leon Springs pupfish (Leon Creek/Diamond Y Spring is the only other known population). Refugium stocks are therefore the main safeguard against losing a species. [[Captive Brood Stock]] management is also central: the bonytail brood stock is being replaced, and a Chihuahua chub brood-stock study is underway to develop a more genetically diverse hatchery stock.

## Key Findings

- **Program scale:** 15 species are held at Dexter, 12 endangered and 3 threatened or of special concern, with culture dating back to 1974.
- **Funding:** The facility report lists $571,960 in total. The Roll-Up lists $510,404 in total spent. The report does not explain the difference of $61,556. Individual allocations range from $5,260 (Comanche Springs pupfish and Pecos gambusia) to $185,818 (bonytail).
- **Distributions in 2000:**
  - [[Bonytail]]: 7,700 eggs and 352,694 fry/fingerlings sent to seven facilities. This is the largest distribution in the report.
  - [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (Colorado River, CO–Green River, UT strain): 128,050 fry/larvae sent to three facilities.
  - [[Woundfin]]: 8,512 fish sent to the Nevada Department of Wildlife in Boulder City (the scan reads "E,512," so the figure is inferred).
  - [[Leon Springs Pupfish]]: 50 fish sent to [[San Marcos National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] and 2,095 fish sent to Leon Creek/Diamond Y Spring.
  - [[Razorback Sucker]]: 82 fish sent to the Arizona Fish and Wildlife office (the scan reads "E2," so the figure is inferred).
  - [[Chihuahua Chub]]: 65 fish sent to the Mimbres River.
  - [[Big Bend Gambusia]]: 25 fish distributed.
- **No distributions in 2000:** Beautiful shiner, Colorado pikeminnow (Colorado River subbasin and Green River subbasin strains), Comanche Springs pupfish, desert pupfish, Gila topminnow, Pahranagat roundtail chub, Pecos gambusia, Virgin River chub, and Yaqui sucker.
- **Brood-stock work:** Dexter began developing a new bonytail brood stock in 2000, a five-year process. A Chihuahua chub brood-stock study was also started, with an expected duration of three to four years.
- **Program management:** The report is a facility-level status and funding summary for the [[Imperiled Species Module]] and was prepared as a basic report for FY2000.
