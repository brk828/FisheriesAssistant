## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Portland, Oregon) formally notifying Dr. W.L. Minckley of his selection to the **[[Cui-ui]] Recovery Team**. While not a primary scientific data source, the letter provides important context on the conservation status, management framework, and institutional recognition of the **Cui-ui (*Chasmistes cujus*)**, an endangered sucker (Catostomidae) endemic to **[[Pyramid Lake]]**, Nevada. The letter outlines the mandate, timeline, and composition of the recovery team tasked with revising the species' recovery plan based strictly on biological data.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Chasmistes cujus*, commonly known as the **[[Cui-ui]]**, a member of the sucker family **[[Catostomidae]]**.
- **Taxonomic Group Context**: Referred to in the letter as a "long-lived catostomid," indicating a life-history strategy of extended longevity relative to many other freshwater fishes.
- **Habitat**: Endemic to **[[Desert Lakes]]**, specifically associated with **[[Pyramid Lake]]** in Nevada — a terminal, endorheic desert lake ecosystem (not connected to the ocean).
- **Regulatory Status**: Listed under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**, necessitating a federally mandated recovery plan and dedicated recovery team.
- **Document Purpose**: Formal appointment letter establishing the **Cui-ui Recovery Team**, led by team leader **Thomas Strekal**, with **Chester Buchanan** (Reno Field Station) serving as Executive Secretary/Service liaison.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The Cui-ui is explicitly tied to **desert lake ecosystems**, a rare and ecologically fragile freshwater habitat type in the arid western United States.
- The recovery team's composition criteria emphasize expertise in three interlocking domains:
  1. **Cui-ui-specific biology**
  2. **Long-lived catostomid fish management**
  3. **Desert lake ecosystem dynamics**
- This triad suggests that Cui-ui population viability is understood to be closely linked to lake-level hydrology, water quality, and long-term demographic stability typical of long-lived fish species (e.g., episodic recruitment, delayed maturity, susceptibility to prolonged environmental disruption).
- No specific population counts, water chemistry data, or spawning metrics are provided in this letter; it is a procedural/administrative document rather than a primary research paper.

## Key Findings
- **Recovery Team Establishment**: Dr. W.L. Minckley was selected as a member of the Cui-ui Recovery Team, with Thomas Strekal appointed as team leader.
- **Team Mandate**: The team is explicitly defined as an **autonomous scientific/technical planning body** — it is NOT authorized to weigh socio-economic, political, or administrative factors against biological ones. Decisions must be based **solely on the latest biological information**.
- **Core Deliverable**: A revised **Cui-ui Recovery Plan** that incorporates a **quantifiable definition of recovery** (i.e., measurable biological criteria for delisting).
- **Timeline**:
  - First draft of recovery plan due: **January 31, 1990**
  - Final version due: **June 30, 1991**
  - Approximately six team meetings anticipated for the first draft; six more anticipated for the final version.
- **Institutional Support**: Travel expenses for team meetings covered by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Post-Plan Role**: Once the recovery plan is finalized, the team may continue to serve in an advisory capacity until the Cui-ui achieves recovery status sufficient for delisting under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**.
- **Significance**: This letter documents a key institutional/administrative milestone in the conservation trajectory of the **[[Cui-ui]]**, reflecting the formalized, science-driven approach mandated for endangered catostomid recovery planning in desert lake systems.
