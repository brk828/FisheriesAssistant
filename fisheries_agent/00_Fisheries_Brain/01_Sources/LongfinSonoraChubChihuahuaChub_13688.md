# Freshwater Fishes of the Southwestern United States and Northern Mexico: Species Accounts from the Atlas of North American Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of 16 species accounts from the *Atlas of North American Freshwater Fishes*. The accounts cover minnows, suckers, pupfishes, a topminnow and a catfish from the desert and grassland drainages of Arizona, New Mexico, Texas, California and northern Mexico. Each entry gives type locality, systematics, distribution and habitat, adult size, and biology. Most are compiled by [[W. L. Minckley]] between 1978 and 1980. The accounts repeatedly show the same pattern: restricted or fragmented native ranges, local extirpation from the United States, and displacement by introduced species. Several taxa are federally listed or considered at risk.

## Background & Description

The accounts are organized by genus and species, and each follows a fixed template:

- **Type locality**: the original collection site, often cited to [[Girard]] (1857) or other 19th-century authors.
- **Systematics**: taxonomic placement, subgeneric assignment and closest relatives. Several genera are monotypic or poorly resolved.
- **Distribution and habitat**: range, elevation and microhabitat.
- **Adult size**: typical and maximum standard length (SL) or total length (TL).
- **Biology**: spawning, diet and life history.

Species covered:

- **Cyprinidae (minnows and chubs)**: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), [[Rio Grande Chub]] (*Gila pandora*), [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*).
- **Catostomidae (suckers)**: [[Yaqui Sucker]] (*Catostomus bernardini*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), [[Rio Grande Sucker]] (*Catostomus plebeius*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*).
- **Cyprinodontidae (pupfishes)**: [[Conchos Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon eximius*), [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Red River Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon rubrofluviatilis*).
- **Poeciliidae (livebearers)**: [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*).
- **Ictaluridae (catfishes)**: [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*).

Several scan errors in the source were corrected in this note, including the names of *Cyprinodon macularius*, *Catostomus latipinnis* and *Poeciliopsis occidentalis*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic units.** Most ranges fall within the [[Gila River Basin]], [[Rio Yaqui Basin]], [[Rio Grande Basin]], [[Colorado River Basin]] and the endorheic basins of northern Chihuahua (Laguna Guzmán and Laguna Bustillos). Several species range across the US–Mexico border, and some type localities are placed on either side of it.

**Habitat associations.** Most species are tied to cover and to specific flow conditions:

- Pools near undercut banks, boulders or debris, used by Yaqui Chub, Rio Grande Chub, Yaqui Sucker and Sonora Sucker.
- Turbulent riffles with filamentous algae, used by Loach Minnow.
- Vegetated springs, brooks and backwaters, used by Gila Topminnow.
- Harsh, fluctuating environments with high salinity and temperature swings. Desert Pupfish and Red River Pupfish survive here because such conditions exclude predatory and competing fishes.

**Biotic interactions.**

- Introduced [[Gambusia Affinis]] (mosquitofish) typically replaces the Gila Topminnow where the two come into contact.
- Introduced *Rhinichthys* and [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) interact with the Loach Minnow, and dewatering has contributed to its decline in Arizona.
- Hybridization occurs in several genera: Rio Grande Chub with *Rhinichthys cataractae*, Flannelmouth Sucker with [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and with introduced *Catostomus commersoni*.
- Belostomatid hemipterans prey heavily on Sonora Chub concentrated in drought-isolated pools.
- Heavy external parasite infestation occurs in Rio Grande Chub crowded into intermittent pools.

**Human impacts.** The accounts repeatedly cite dewatering, stream channelization, introductions and the loss of spring habitat as drivers of decline.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic status**

- *Agosia chrysogaster* and *Tiaroga cobitis* are each monotypic genera, both presumed related to the *Rhinichthys*-like stock of western cyprinids.
- The Mexican populations of Longfin Dace are often considered a distinctive, undescribed form.
- Sonora Chub (*Gila ditaenia*) is placed in subgenus *Temeculina*. Miller (1945) linked it to *G. purpurea* and *G. orcutti*.
- Desert Sucker (*Catostomus clarki*), as currently constituted, is a complex that includes *Pantosteus intermedius*, *P. utahensis* and at least one undescribed form.
- Systematics of *Ictalurus* in Mexico is poorly understood. *I. meeki* may be a synonym of *I. pricei*.

**Size and life history**

- **Longfin Dace**: rarely exceeds 100 mm SL. Spawns year-round in saucer-shaped sand depressions. Young hatch in under four days in summer. Diet includes algae, aquatic insects and detritus.
- **Sonora Chub**: spawns February–April. Adults move little, even at high flow. Diet includes aquatic and terrestrial insects and algae.
- **Chihuahua Chub**: maximum about 200 mm. Biology is essentially unknown. Spawns late April–May in the Mimbres River.
- **Rio Grande Chub**: rarely exceeds 150 mm SL. Biology is essentially unknown.
- **Yaqui Chub**: rarely exceeds 100 mm. Spawns in March. Males are steely blue, females yellow-brown.
- **Loach Minnow**: rarely exceeds 60 mm TL. Diet is highly restricted to riffle-dwelling baetid mayflies and simuliid dipterans. Spawns late winter and early spring in the second summer of life, producing 250–1,200 ova per female.
- **Yaqui Sucker**: maximum 400 mm. Spawns May through mid-August.
- **Desert Sucker**: maximum about 330 mm. Herbivorous, scraping diatoms from stones. Spawns January–May, maturing in its third summer.
- **Sonora Sucker**: commonly exceeds 400 mm TL, maximum about 800 mm. Spawns February through July. Feeds on benthic invertebrates and seasonally on detritus.
- **Rio Grande Sucker**: typically 60–90 mm, rarely over 140 mm. Spawns February through April.
- **Flannelmouth Sucker**: 300–400 mm SL, about 500 mm TL maximum. Spawns in spring and early summer on riffles near 13°C over coarse gravel.
- **Conchos Pupfish**: rarely exceeds 40 mm SL. Some populations excavate feeding pits in soft substrate.
- **Desert Pupfish**: about 60 mm SL maximum. Can mature at 15 mm TL and complete its life cycle in one summer. Spawns April–October at 20°C.
- **Red River Pupfish**: rarely exceeds 45 mm SL. Tolerates temperatures from about −1 to 38°C and salinities above 3%.
- **Gila Topminnow**: females usually ≤50 mm SL, males typically 20–25 mm. Bears young year-round in constant-temperature habitats at northern range limits. Diet is detritus, algae and invertebrates.
- **Yaqui Catfish**: biology is little known. Males with swollen heads in breeding condition were recorded from the Rio Yaqui basin.

**Conservation status and range reduction**

- **Gila Topminnow**: now restricted in the United States, listed as endangered (USDI 1976), and common only in the Rio Yaqui basin.
- **Loach Minnow**: widely extirpated in Arizona, locally common in New Mexico.
- **Yaqui Chub**: nearly extirpated in the United States, surviving in one artesian well in the San Bernardino Creek drainage. Introduced and established in Leslie Creek, Arizona, in 1969.
- **Yaqui Sucker**: recently extirpated from the United States.
- **Yaqui Catfish**: introduced into the Santa Cruz River system in 1899, persisted until the 1950s, and is now extirpated from US waters.
- **Chihuahua Chub**: long thought extinct in the United States, recently rediscovered.
- **Rio Grande Chub**: formerly widespread in the upper Rio Grande and Pecos watersheds, with documented range reduction in the Pecos system.
- **Sonora Sucker**: widely extirpated in the southern half of its range, but overall still a common species.
- **Desert Pupfish**: established outside its native range in the Salton Sea, Anza-Borrego State Park and Boyce Thompson Arboretum.

**Notable records**

- A 1979 specimen of Desert Sucker from Salt River at Granite Reef Dam measured 212 mm SL.
- Records of Yaqui Chub from Morse Canyon in the Chiricahua Mountains are not supported by specimens.
