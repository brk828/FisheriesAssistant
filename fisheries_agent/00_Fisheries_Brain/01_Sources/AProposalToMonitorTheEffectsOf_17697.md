# Monitoring Interim Flows Effects from Glen Canyon Dam on the Aquatic Food Base in the Colorado River

#aquatic-ecology #freshwater-ecology #macroinvertebrates #benthic-algae #dam-impacts

## Executive Summary

This proposal by Dean W. Blinn ([[Northern Arizona University]]) and Lawrence E. Stevens ([[Grand Canyon National Park]]) requests $293,117 over 20 months (June 1992–February 1994) to monitor the effects of interim flow releases from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] on the aquatic food base in the [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The interim flows, implemented in August 1990, were designed to mitigate dam operation impacts by establishing minimum releases (141.5 m³/sec), reduced maximum flows (20,000 cfs), and slower ramping rates. The study focuses on lower trophic levels—benthic algae (particularly [[Cladophora glomerata]] and associated diatoms) and macroinvertebrates (Diptera, gastropods, [[Gammarus lacustris]])—which form the food base for both aquatic and terrestrial components of the fluvial ecosystem. The proposal builds on GCES Phase II findings that demonstrated strong negative effects of discharge fluctuations on benthic standing crop, positive correlations between drift and discharge, and 50–60% reductions in [[Cladophora]] biomass from 12-hour desiccation cycles.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River]] ecosystem below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] is strongly regulated by dam operations. Prior to interim flows, fluctuating discharges caused significant impacts on benthic communities through desiccation, freezing, scour, and drift. The Secretary of the Interior authorized interim flows beginning August 1990 to mitigate these impacts pending completion of the Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement. Interim flows consist of low-, medium-, and high-volume months with maximum discharge of 20,000 cfs, reduced daily fluctuation ranges, and reduced ramping rates. Minimum discharge of 141.5 m³/sec may be sustained for no more than six hours between 7:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m.

Historical studies established that the benthic assemblage changes with distance downstream from the dam. The clear, cold tailwaters reach is dominated by [[Cladophora glomerata]], associated periphyton (especially diatoms), [[Gammarus lacustris]], and chironomid midges, with oligochaetes, lumbricids, and Physella sp. snails. Below the sediment-laden [[Paria River]] near [[Lees Ferry]], benthic dominance shifts to crust-forming blue-green algae ([[Oscillatoria]] spp.), simuliid gnats, and different chironomid assemblages. Hofknecht (1981) described the "intertidal" zone as largely devoid of benthic life due to fluctuating water levels, while tributaries support diverse and densely colonized communities.

Leibfried and Blinn (1988) found chironomid larvae numerically dominant over [[Gammarus lacustris]] in drift, comprising up to 85% of drifting invertebrate standing crop. Chironomid drift was associated with [[Cladophora glomerata]] abundance rather than discharge fluctuations. Macroinvertebrate standing crop decreased below the [[Little Colorado River]] (RM 61) and was positively correlated with [[Cladophora]] standing crop spatially and seasonally.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The aquatic food base in the [[Colorado River]] corridor is affected by discharge duration, timing of low releases, and range of daily fluctuations from [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. Lower trophic levels provide essential resources for both aquatic and terrestrial components of the fluvial ecosystem, supporting a unique native fish fauna with several Federally listed species and a diverse riparian insectivore fauna.

Blinn and Leibfried (1988) demonstrated that [[Cladophora]] provides essential substrata for diatoms, which feed the macroinvertebrate assemblage. Pinney (1991) confirmed that [[Gammarus]] diet consists mostly of diatoms with little [[Cladophora]]. Blinn et al. (1991) found chironomid gut contents at [[Lees Ferry]] were predominantly diatoms. Usher and Blinn (1990) and Blinn et al. (1991) showed that 12-hour desiccation and rewatering cycles reduced benthic [[Cladophora glomerata]] standing stock by 50–60% over one week, with equally strong effects on associated macroinvertebrates.

The study area extends from [[Lees Ferry]] to [[Diamond Creek]], encompassing wide-reach and narrow-reach sites. Geomorphology and tributary sediment supplies strongly affect standing stock, production, drift, and species composition. Allochthonous upstream organic matter appears to be the primary energy source for downstream reaches. GCES Phase II synoptic studies suggested drift (algae, macroinvertebrates, detritus) is positively correlated with daily discharge fluctuation ranges.

## Key Findings

**Study Objectives:**

1. **Monitor standing stock** of lower trophic levels between [[Lees Ferry]] and [[Diamond Creek]] under interim flows, using dredging and Hess sampling at five wide-reach and five narrow-reach sites in pools and riffles (n=4 sampling trips seasonally).

2. **Monitor primary and secondary production** at low, medium, and high-volume monthly interim flows, focusing on [[Gammarus lacustris]] production using size-frequency methods (Benke 1984) at three sites: [[Lees Ferry]], 50m below [[Paria River]] confluence, and [[Diamond Creek]] confluence. Collections at 3-week intervals throughout interim flow periods.

3. **Monitor re-colonization rates** of benthic algae and macroinvertebrates following low-volume interim flow periods. Colonized rocks (n=120 at [[Lees Ferry]], n=120 below [[Paria River]]) will be re-submerged and harvested at 3-week intervals during high summer and winter interim flows to determine recovery rates.

4. **Monitor organic drift** on rise and fall of daily flow cycles at ten mainstem stations using four drift nets (10 cm orifice, 250 μm mesh) at surface and 0.6 depth, plus one large net (0.5 m orifice) for 15-minute collections. Drift biomass will be compared to discharge and to November 1990 and June 1991 synoptic experiments.

5. **Prepare monitoring data** for inclusion into GCES/NPS GIS database, generating themes for benthic algal and macroinvertebrate standing crop and species composition, integrated with fisheries and potentially terrestrial fauna information.

**Sampling Protocol:**
- Pool habitats sampled at five locations along three transects: thalweg, <1,000 cfs, lower-littoral (~5,000 cfs), mid-littoral (~10,000 cfs), and upper-littoral (28,000 cfs)
- Three pairs of samples collected for taxonomic/abundance and biomass determinations
- Samples sorted into 10 biotic categories: [[Cladophora]], blue-green algal crust, chironomids, [[Gammarus]], gastropods, oligochaetes, simuliids, lumbricids, other invertebrates, and detritus
- Abiotic parameters recorded: water temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, specific conductance, substratum, microhabitat conditions, Secchi disc, water velocity/stage, depth, date, site, time
