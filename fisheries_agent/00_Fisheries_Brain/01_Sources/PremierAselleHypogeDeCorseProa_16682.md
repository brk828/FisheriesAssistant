# Proasellus beroni: A New Blind, Depigmented Water Slater from a Corsican Cave

#crustacea #isopoda #proasellus #speleology

## Executive Summary

This 1968 paper by [[Jean-Paul Henry]] and [[Guy Magniez]] (*Annales de Spéléologie*, vol. 23, fasc. 2) describes *Proasellus beroni* n. sp., a blind (anophthalmic) and depigmented [[Water Slater]] (Crustacea, Isopoda). The species was collected in November 1967 during a biospeleological survey led by P. Beron in the [[Sabara Cave]] near Castiglione, central [[Corsica]]. The authors place it in the genus [[Proasellus]] and note close affinities with [[Proasellus Pavani]] from Italy and Slovenia. They treat it as a new species based on male pleopod structure, especially the first and second pleopods and the fourth pleopod, rather than as a subspecies of [[Proasellus Coxalis]]. The paper also reports that it is the first cave-dwelling asellid recorded from Corsica, and that it shares its cave with [[Stenasellus Racovitzai]], a species not previously known from cave habitats.

## Background & Description

**Context.** The authors review the Mediterranean island asellid fauna, summarising [[Codreanu]] (1961). Most epigean (surface) island asellids had been assigned to subspecies of *Proasellus coxalis* (Dollfus, 1892). Earlier records of *P. coxalis* came from Sicily, Sardinia, Capraja, Giglio, the Balearics and the Hyères islands. A specimen pair collected by E. Simon in 1889 near Porto-Vecchio (eastern Corsica) was reviewed, but the authors chose not to name a new subspecies because existing coxalis subspecies definitions are imprecise. They recommend a global revision of *P. coxalis*.

**Sampled population.** The type series comprised:
- 5 adult males, reported as 3.1 to 3.7 mm (the OCR reads "3,1 à 38,7 mm"; 3.7 mm is the most plausible value)
- 11 subadult and adult females, 3.2 to 4.1 mm (mean 3.8 mm)
- The largest female had about 25 nearly mature oocytes in each ovary.
- All specimens had guts filled with silty particles and dark plant debris.
- The material is held in the collections of the Laboratoire Souterrain du C.N.R.S.

**Morphology (selected characters):**
- **General habitus:** stocky body, not the elongated form typical of phreatic (groundwater) asellids. Yellow organs are very abundant on pereionite VII and the pleotelson.
- **Antennules:** short, not exceeding half the antennal peduncle. The flagellum has 5-6 articles and bears 3-4 olfactory lamellae in males and 1-2 in females.
- **Antennae:** slightly longer than the body. A 3.7 mm male had 40 articles on the antenna.
- **Mouthparts:** mandibles, maxillules and maxillae are typical of *Proasellus*. The maxilliped retinaculum has 5 hooks. The oostegite could not be studied because no ovigerous female was available.
- **Pereiopods:** sternal spines on the dactylopodites are notably long in both sexes, with 4 on pereiopod I and only 1-2 on pereiopods II to VII.
- **Male pleopod I:** subtrapezoidal protopodite with a 2-3 hook retinaculum. The exopodite is very short and subcircular, with 5 smooth setae on its distal margin. This resembles *P. pavani pavani* and *P. pavani orientalis*.
- **Male pleopod II:** hypertrophied, massive endopodite. The distal region is typical of *Proasellus*, but the endopodite tip differs from the Italian and Slovenian forms: a short apophysis and a downward-directed opening, versus a long apophysis and outward-directed opening in *P. pavani*.
- **Female pleopod II:** short, nearly glabrous, with two smooth setae. This resembles *P. pavani*.
- **Pleopod III (both sexes):** exopodite about 1.5 times as long as wide, with short smooth setae on the outer margin.
- **Pleopod IV (both sexes):** exopodite is a distinctive two-article structure. A subcircular, strongly projecting, fleshy respiratory area is found on the distal article. The authors consider this unusual for the genus, because *P. pavani* has a fully respiratory distal article.
- **Pleopod V (both sexes):** exopodite more than twice the length of the fleshy, oval endopodite.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** *P. beroni* lives in a subterranean cave (Sabara Cave). The authors describe it as the first cave-dwelling asellid recorded from Corsica.
- **Associated fauna:** it co-occurs with *Stenasellus racovitzai* Razzauti, 1925. That species was previously recorded in Corsica from the hyporheic zone (subsurface stream habitat) of the Golo tributaries (Casaluna and Tartagine rivers) by Angelier (1953) and Coineau (1964). The authors note that Stenasellus was not previously known from a cave biotope in Corsica.
- **Co-occurrence context:** the authors observe that asellid and stenasellid co-occurrence in a single cave is uncommon. Comparable examples include *Proasellus cantabricus* with *Stenasellus virei buchneri* at Cueva de Cullalvera (Santander, Spain), and *Proasellus ibericus* with *S. virei hussoni* at Goueil di Her (Haute-Garonne, France).
- **Biogeography:** the relationship to *P. pavani* reflects an ancient lineage. The authors argue that continuous land connection between the mountains of Slovenia, the Lake Garda region and Italy likely dates to pre-Quaternary periods. The lineage is believed to have been established underground for a long time.
- **Related Italian island record:** [[Proasellus Patrizii]] from a cave on Sardinia (Arcangeli, 1952) is also closely related to *P. coxalis*, and [[Karaman]] erected the subgenus *Coxasellus* to group coxalis and its close relatives.

## Key Findings

1. **New species:** *Proasellus beroni* n. sp. is described from the Sabara Cave near Castiglione, central Corsica. It is blind, depigmented, and is the first cave asellid recorded from Corsica.
2. **Affinity with *P. pavani*:** the closest relatives are *P. pavani pavani* (Italy, Brescia region) and *P. pavani orientalis* (Slovenia). Male pleopods I and II are considered the key taxonomic characters.
3. **Diagnostic differences:** the endopodite tip of male pleopod II (short apophysis, downward opening) and the two-article, respiratory-dominated pleopod IV distinguish the new species from *P. pavani*. The authors judge these differences sufficient for species status, although they admit that the separation would otherwise be marginal.
4. **Subgenus placement:** the authors do not assign the species to subgenus *Arcasellus* Karaman, 1952. Its pleopod IV structure would require changing the subgenus diagnosis, and the validity of Arcasellus itself needs confirmation.
5. **Coxalis complex:** many Corsican and Mediterranean epigean island asellids have been assigned to *P. coxalis* subspecies. The authors decline to name a new coxalis subspecies from E. Simon's 1889 material and call for a modern global revision of the species.
6. **Faunistic note:** the co-occurrence of *P. beroni* and *Stenasellus racovitzai* in a cave is considered rare for Mediterranean asellid-stenasellid assemblages.
7. **Planned work:** the paper cites forthcoming systematic and biogeographic papers by Henry and Magniez (1968b, 1968c) and a planned Stenasellus virei revision by Magniez (1968). These were not complete at publication.
