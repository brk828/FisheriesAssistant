# San Bernardino Ranch Management Plan Outline

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #conservation #habitat-restoration

## Executive Summary

This management plan outline for [[San Bernardino Ranch]] addresses the restoration and protection of aquatic habitats supporting native [[Rio Yaqui]] fish species. The document, dated January 15, 1980, identifies five springs, four ponds, canals, and four historical cienega areas requiring immediate management intervention. Critical priorities include eliminating exotic fish species (particularly [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Mosquitofish]], and other centrarchids), restoring degraded cienegas, and stabilizing [[San Bernardino Creek]] which has incised 15+ feet into the ground. The plan emphasizes reintroduction of native species including [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and native suckers while establishing cooperative management with [[The Nature Conservancy]].

## Background & Description

The San Bernardino Ranch contains multiple interconnected aquatic habitats in the [[Rio Yaqui]] drainage system near the Arizona-Mexico border. The property features five named springs ([[House Spring]], [[Tule Spring]], [[Cottonwood Spring]], [[Pipe Spring]], [[North Spring]]), four ponds (House, Tule, Twin, North), canal systems, and historically four cienega wetland areas. The ranch includes buildings recognized on the National Historic Register.

Historical cienegas once fed by House Springs, Twin Ponds, Pipe and Cottonwood springs, and North Spring have been converted to cultivated fields. The North Spring Cienega became a fallow field after use as a truck farm. [[San Bernardino Creek]] has undergone severe degradation, with the watercourse incised 15+ feet into ground and past organic deposits, creating a gully 50 feet wide that extends at least to Twin Ponds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The aquatic habitats support both native [[Rio Yaqui]] fish fauna and problematic exotic species. Native species targeted for conservation include [[Gila Topminnow]] (Poeciliopsis occidentalis), [[Bonytail Chub]], native suckers, [[Red Shiner]], [[Stoneroller]], and native catfish. Exotic species requiring elimination include [[Black Bullhead]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Mosquitofish]] (Gambusia affinis).

The creek system faces critical erosion with down-cutting and lateral cutting continuing at several feet per year. This erosion threatens to "pirate the flow of needed springs" and potentially move the ranch boundary into Mexico. The degradation of cienegas represents loss of critical wetland habitat that historically supported diverse aquatic communities.

## Key Findings

**Springs, Ponds, and Canals Management:**
- Testing required to determine potential flows, head pressures, flow interrelationships and interdependencies among five spring systems
- Recommended habitat allocations by species:
  - Ponds (House, Twin, North): [[Gila Topminnow]], chub, sucker, catfish
  - Springs (Cottonwood, Tule): topminnow, shiner, chub  
  - Canals (House, North): topminnow, [[Stoneroller]], sucker
- Immediate elimination of exotic fish species from the ranch and above House Pond required
- Reintroduction of native [[Rio Yaqui]] species following exotic removal

**Cienega Restoration:**
- Four cienega areas historically existed; three converted to cultivated fields, one to fallow field
- Water from four sources should be released onto fields to initiate cienega reformation
- Research needed on cienega formation and composition
- Tiered water flow recommended to saturate increasingly larger areas
- Native cienega vegetation replanting may be necessary

**San Bernardino Creek Stabilization:**
- Creek incised 15+ feet with 50-foot-wide gully
- Erosion continuing at several feet per year
- [[Soil Conservation Service]] (SCS) assistance recommended for erosion control plan
- Immediate action required to prevent further spring flow piracy and boundary loss

**Uplands Management:**
- No specific management needed except elimination of grazing
- Survey of plant and animal associations recommended

**Historic Sites:**
- Committee formation needed to protect National Historic Register buildings
- Ramsaurers scheduled to vacate house by end of March 1980
- Immediate occupancy by [[The Nature Conservancy]] or [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] required

**Immediate Priorities:**
- Cooperative agreement with The Nature Conservancy for interim aquatics management between sale to TNC and transfer to FWS
- Elimination of [[Mosquitofish]] and exotic fish from House and North ponds
- Cienega construction must begin immediately to prevent flowing spring water from causing additional downcutting
- Creek stabilization urgent to prevent loss of ranch to Mexico and spring flow piracy
- Historic sites protection following house vacancy
