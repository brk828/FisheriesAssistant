# Vascular Plants of the Aravaipa Creek Primitive Area

#botany #vascular-plants #flora-catalogue

## Executive Summary

This appendix is a catalogue of the vascular plant flora of the [[Aravaipa Creek Primitive Area]] in Arizona. Based on repeated collecting trips over one year and on personal observation, it lists **150 species in 128 genera across 58 families**. The inventory covers Aravaipa Creek from the confluence with Turkey Creek to Woods Ranch and adjacent hillsides, and it was extended to include specimens from just above and below these boundaries. The author states that the list is a partial inventory of the area's actual flora.

## Background & Description

- **Scope and methods:** Several collecting trips were made through the canyon during a year. Plants were recorded by personal observation, and collected, processed specimens were deposited at the [[Desert Botanical Garden Herbarium]] (DES) in Phoenix, Arizona.
- **Taxonomic authorities:** Identifications followed [[Kearney and Peebles]] (*Arizona Flora*), except Cactaceae, which followed [[Benson 1969]] (*The Cacti of Arizona*). Other groups follow recent revisions and monographs, mainly [[Lehr 1978]] (*A Catalogue of the Flora of Arizona*), which was used to update names and synonymy.
- **Arrangement:** Species are grouped by family, following the family sequence of [[Kearney and Peebles]], with genera alphabetical within each family. Each entry gives the author and, where available, a common name.
- **Limitations:** Plants without flowers could not be keyed to species, so they are listed only to genus. The author describes the catalogue as an effort toward completeness that "at best represents a partial list."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The catalogue reflects a mix of riparian and upland desert elements. Riparian and streamside taxa include:

- Trees and shrubs such as [[Fremont Cottonwood]] (*Populus fremontii*), [[Goodding Willow]] (*Salix gooddingii*), Bonpland willow (*Salix bonplandiana*), [[Arizona Walnut]] (*Juglans major*), [[Arizona Sycamore]] (*Platanus wrightii*), and [[Saltcedar]] (*Tamarix pentandra*).
- Herbaceous streamside plants such as water cress (*Nasturtium officinale*), water speedwell (*Veronica anagallis-aquatica*), and cardinal flower (*Lobelia cardinalis*).
- Aquatic or near-water plants such as duckweed (*Lemna* sp.) and cattail (Typhaceae).

Desert and upland elements include [[Saguaro]] (*Carnegiea gigantea*), [[Creosote Bush]] (*Larrea divaricata*), jojoba (*Simmondsia chinensis*), ocotillo (*Fouquieria splendens*), and many cacti, including several cholla (*Opuntia*) species. Desert shrubs such as four-wing saltbush (*Atriplex canescens*), desert broom (*Baccharis sarothroides*), and mesquite and paloverde legumes are also present.

## Key Findings

- **Total richness:** 150 vascular plant species, 128 genera, and 58 families.
- **Major groups:**
  - Pteridophyta (ferns): four species listed, including southern maidenhair fern (*Adiantum capillus-veneris*).
  - Gymnosperms: one species, one-seed juniper (*Juniperus monosperma*).
  - Angiosperms: monocots (e.g., grasses, Agavaceae, Typhaceae, Lemnaceae) and dicots, which make up the bulk of the list.
- **Most diverse families (by listed taxa):** Compositae (Asteraceae) and Leguminosae (Fabaceae), with Cactaceae also well represented through many *Opuntia* and other cactus taxa.
- **Notable entries:**
  - Canyon grape (*Vitis arizonica*) and Arizona sycamore (*Platanus wrightii*) for riparian canopy.
  - Ocotillo, jojoba, and creosote bush as signature desert species.
  - Passion flower (*Passiflora mexicana*) and tree tobacco (*Nicotiana glauca*) among less typical entries.
- **Incomplete coverage:** Non-flowering specimens are listed only to genus, and the author notes the catalogue is partial.
