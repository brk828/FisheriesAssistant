# Reintroduction of Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish: 1981 BLM NEPA Decision Record

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1981 [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) compliance package from the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM), Phoenix District, together with supporting correspondence. It covers a proposal to reintroduce the federally endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] and the state Group I [[Desert Pupfish]] into two spring sites on public land in the [[Lower Gila North]] (LGN) planning area: [[South Peoples Spring]] in [[Peoples Canyon]] and [[Grapevine Springs]]. The proposal was requested by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AG&F) and the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). The BLM recommended approval, and a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) was drafted. The stated goal is to delist both species within five years by establishing as many self-sustaining populations as possible.

## Background & Description

**Species involved**

- **Gila topminnow** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*): Listed as Endangered by USFWS (Federal Register 41(208): 47,180 - 47,198). Originally described by Baird and Girard in 1853 from the Santa Cruz River near Tucson. It was once described as the most abundant fish in Arizona. It is the only representative of the genus *Poeciliopsis* (16 species) in the United States.
- **Desert pupfish** (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*): Classified as AG&F Group I. First described by Baird and Girard in 1853 from the San Pedro River, a tributary of the Gila River. It has been totally extirpated from Arizona.

Both species are native to the Colorado River Drainage and historically occurred together.

**Sites**

| Site | Legal Location | Elevation | Notes |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Causes of decline**

- **Habitat loss and degradation:** Cienega habitats were lost to severe arroyo cutting and draining that began in the 1880s. Other habitats were lost through the development and modification of water resources.
- **Non-native fishes (primary cause):** Introductions of non-native fishes for vector control and sport fishing are described as the major reason for decline. Topminnow populations declined after the first introduction of [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis affinis*) in 1926. Desert pupfish were eliminated primarily by game fishes such as [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*).

**Status at time of document**

- Gila topminnow currently occurs in only seven small spring systems on private and public lands. USFWS later described this as "the seven to eight which now exist."

**Habitat requirements**

- Shallow pools, marshes, springs, backwaters, and sandy-bottomed streams.
- Quiet, shallow water with vegetation or debris, below 1500 m elevation.
- Diet of small invertebrates, algae, and detritus, depending on availability.

**Environmental setting of the sites**

- Combined area of both sites: approximately two acres, or 0.00006% of the total EIS area (0.0001% of LGN public lands).
- Sites require no surface disturbance or modification of present conditions.
- Both sites lie within two proposed [[Areas Of Critical Environmental Concern]] (ACEC) designated for threatened and endangered plants (MFP Step 2). The reintroduction is stated not to disturb plant species diversity or riparian habitat.
- Peoples Canyon falls within a [[Wilderness Study Area]] (MFP Step 2).

**Management recommendations (from the June 22, 1981 field trip memo)**

- Locate and set aside suitable natural habitats for topminnow and pupfish, free of exotic fishes, particularly mosquitofish and game fishes.
- If needed, remove exotics by electrofishing and/or ichthyotoxins. Barriers may be required to prevent reinvasion.

## Key Findings

**1. Purpose and need**
- The USFWS and AG&F requested BLM cooperation on June 17, 1981 and July 2, 1981, respectively.
- The goal is to delist both species from Endangered and Group I status within five years by establishing as many self-sustaining populations as possible.
- Brood stock is available from AG&F and the USFWS [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. The action could be completed by August 1, 1981.

**2. Alternatives**
- Only one alternative was considered besides the proposed action: no action. It was dismissed because it would negate the purpose and block eventual delisting.

**3. Critical element assessments (Section V)**
- A. No effect on any other known threatened or endangered species or their habitat.
- B. No surface disturbance, so an unconditional clearance for cultural resources was recommended.
- C. Within two proposed ACEC designations; a no-conflict decision was recommended.
- D. Within the Peoples Canyon Wilderness Study Area; introduction will not interrupt solitude or primitive recreation and will enhance wilderness values.
- E. No adverse socioeconomic impacts; no modification of present conditions; not subject to critical habitat designation.

**4. Rationale for decision**
- No adverse impacts were identified, and the action will not interrupt present multiple-use management.
- Successful re-establishment will benefit species perpetuation and support future BLM management of T&E species through delisting.

**5. Recommendations and decision**
- Reintroduce Gila topminnow and desert pupfish into South Peoples Spring and Grapevine Springs as soon as possible.
- BLM personnel should assist with stocking and future monitoring.
- The Decision Record grants AG&F and USFWS permission to reintroduce both species into suitable habitat on public lands in the LGN area, with BLM assistance. The signed decision text is partly illegible in the scan.
- Finding of No Significant Impact: the reintroduction will have no significant impacts on present multiple-use management.

**6. Cooperative agreement framework**
- AG&F proposed a similar agreement with the USFWS and the Forest Service for the Tonto National Forest. Critical habitat designation would not occur, and "vested rights and existing uses of the waters" would not be "limited or altered because of the reintroduction."
- AG&F would conduct stocking and monitoring with BLM and USFWS assistance. AG&F agreed to monitor populations after introduction.
- Potential additional sites include "several tanks and springs" within the Safford and Phoenix districts. BLM biologists found these suitable for topminnows.
- The USFWS stated that, with cooperation, "a hundred or more self-sustaining populations" might be achieved "in a few years."

**7. Legal authority and policy basis**
- Authority: [[USBLM Manual 6820]] via the [[Federal Land Policy And Management Act]] of 1976 (90 Stat. 2743).
- Consistent with [[USBLM Manual 6840]] (policy to attempt delisting of endangered species).
- Aligned with the goal of the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 (87 Stat. 926 as cited in the source; the document lists "80 Stat. 926").

**8. Conclusion**
- The BLM regards reintroduction as its "best opportunity" to cooperate with other agencies in managing T&E species and work toward delisting.
