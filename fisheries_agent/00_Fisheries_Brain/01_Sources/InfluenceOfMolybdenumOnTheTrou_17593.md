# Influence of Molybdenum on the Trout and Trout Fishing of Castle Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

In July 1963, 35 lb of sodium molybdate (containing 14 lb of molybdenum) were added to Castle Lake, California. Angler yields of [[Rainbow Trout]] in 1966 and [[Eastern Brook Trout]] in 1967 were then at record levels. Each year's yield was about 3.5 lb per acre above the 1960-1965 mean (12.8 lb/acre). The authors estimate the cost per additional pound of trout in the creel at under $0.10. The increase came mainly from high survival of the 1965 year class of both species, probably helped by larger standing crops of [[Zooplankton]] and [[Benthic Invertebrates]]. Other factors may have contributed, so a second molybdenum addition was made in June 1969 to verify the result.

## Background & Description

- The California Department of Fish and Game has studied Castle Lake since 1938. Until 1959 the work evaluated [[Trout Stocking]] practices. Since 1959, [[Charles R. Goldman]] (UC Davis) has studied the lake's limnology, and the Department has evaluated the effects of fertilization on trout and trout fishing.
- Goldman (1960) found [[Molybdenum]] to be the stimulating micronutrient limiting primary productivity. The chemical cost about $30 (the cost section gives $80 for the 35 lb).
- Castle Lake is a 50-acre glacial cirque lake at 5,600 ft. It drains only 200 acres, fed by snow and springs. The northern third is shallow (maximum 15 ft) and the rest is a deep bowl (maximum 122 ft).
- It is mesotrophic. Total dissolved solids are 20-30 ppm, Secchi readings 40-55 ft, and summer surface temperatures never exceed 75 F. A thermocline forms in June, and in some years oxygen is completely depleted below 85 ft.
- Only eastern brook trout and rainbow trout are present. Brook trout reproduce over sublacustrine springs. Rainbow do not spawn in the lake, and the tributaries are unsuitable for spawning, so annual stocking supports them.

**Methods:**
- A five-day-per-week [[Creel Census]] ran from 1960 through 1968. It covered all weekend days, major holidays and three weekdays per week, and virtually all anglers were contacted.
- Fish were measured to 0.1 in FL and weighed to 0.01 lb.
- Rainbow plants were standardized at 10,000 fingerlings (10-20 per ounce), released in mid-August. All were Shasta-strain domestic fish reared at Mt. Shasta Hatchery, with a distinctive fin clip for each year's plant, which allowed ages to be determined. Brook trout were not aged.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Trout diets in Castle Lake are dominated by zooplankton and benthos (Swift, 1970).
- Goldman (1966) attributed significant zooplankton increases in 1964-1965 to the molybdenum. Beatty (1968) found record bottom fauna densities in 1965-1967, but noted these might reflect natural fluctuation.
- Rainbow and brook trout appear spatially segregated, especially in summer. Brook trout stay near the substrate in August, while earlier-stocked rainbow range widely in surface waters, where they probably compete with newly stocked fingerlings.
- Historically, the wild brook trout contribution peaked in 1952-1954 and declined in 1958-1961. Wales and Borgeson (1961) attributed this to food competition and [[Predation]] on brook trout fry by rainbow, possibly worsened by large rainbow plants (15,000 in 1956 and 20,000 in 1958). Stabilizing plants at 10,000 coincided with a brook trout recovery.
- A record flood on December 22, 1964 (mean daily flow of 34,400 cfs at the Sacramento River gaging station near Shasta Lake) may have caused trout to leave California coldwater lakes. It probably reduced survival of the 1964 plant.

## Key Findings

**Angler success and yield**
- The 1960-1965 mean catch was 0.84 fish per hour (range 0.75-1.04) and the mean yield was 12.8 lb/acre (range 10.9-14.5).
- Yields were 16.7 lb/acre in 1966 and 16.6 in 1967, which is 30% above the mean. The 1966 success rate of 1.15 was 37% above the mean, and the 1967 rate of 1.07 was 27% above it.
- Yield and success dropped to pre-1966 levels in 1968.
- Angler use ranged from 2,759 hr (1961) to 3,985 hr (1962) and stabilized near 3,500 hr from 1963. It was not significantly correlated with success or yield. Yield and success were strongly correlated (r = 0.858, d.f. = 6).
- Before 1960, yields averaged about 10 lb/acre for mixed-species and brook-only fisheries and about 13 lb/acre with brook plus rainbow fingerlings. The 1966-1967 increase is about 3.5 lb/acre, or 168 lb for the lake.

**Species composition**
- Rainbow dominated the 1966 catch. Their yield of 11.2 lb was 32% above the 1960-1965 average of 8.5 lb.
- Brook trout dominated 1967. Their yield of 9.9 lb was 125% above the 1960-1966 average of 4.4 lb. Brook trout success in 1967 was high in all months except August.
- Brook trout length-frequency data indicate a strong 1965 year class, which entered the creel at 5-7 in in 1966 and was fully recruited in 1967. Monthly lengths, weights and condition did not differ significantly before and after fertilization.

**Rainbow plants**
- Harvest of the 1959-1965 plants ranged from 12% (1960 and 1964) to 35% (1965). The 1966 plant probably will not exceed 12%.
- About 89% by number and 83% by weight of rainbow caught in 1966 were age I fish from the 1965 plant. In other years age I fish averaged 59% by number (range 42-76%) and 45% by weight (range 25-63%).
- The 1965 plant did well at age I, but its contribution at age II and older was similar to other plants. This matched a lower age I-to-II survival. The average survival of age II and older fish was 0.085 (range 0.057-0.149) for the 1959-1965 plants, versus 0.342 for the 1956 plant.
- Rainbow lengths were somewhat higher after fertilization, but not significantly so.

**Alternative explanations tested**
- Angler effort was not correlated with plant harvest. Boat anglers were more successful than shore anglers in 32 of 34 months, but the rise in boat use was too small to explain the record yields.
- Size at release correlated strongly with percent harvested for the 1959-1965 plants (r = 0.880, d.f. = 5). The correlation was lost when the 1966 plant, which was large but returned poorly, was included. The only plant with no observed loss at release was the highly successful 1965 plant.
- Plant harvest was inversely correlated with August-October rainbow catch rates (r = -0.773, d.f. = 6), which suggests density-dependent suppression of later plants by strong year classes.
- The 1964 plant had a low harvest despite the 1964 zooplankton increase, probably because of the December 1964 flood.

**Cost and conclusion**
- The 35 lb of sodium molybdate cost $80, and application costs were minimal. The yield increase was almost 350 lb over two years, so the cost per additional pound was under $0.10, versus an average of $0.85 per pound of trout for the rainbow stocking program.
- The authors consider molybdenum enrichment the probable primary factor, but note a fortuitous combination of favorable factors is possible. Adding trace elements to high mountain lakes could be a valuable [[Lake Fertilization]] management tool, and a second addition in June 1969 is planned to verify the results.
