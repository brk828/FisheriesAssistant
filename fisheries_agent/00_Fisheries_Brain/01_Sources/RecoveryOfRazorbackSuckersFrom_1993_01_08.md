## Executive Summary
This memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Parker Fishery Resource Office documents an emergency salvage operation of five [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) individuals from a Colorado River Indian Tribe (CRIT) irrigation canal slated for imminent drying. All five fish were PIT-tagged prior to the operation, and morphometric, sex, and reproductive condition data were recorded. Two of the five fish died during handling/recovery (mortalities), while the remaining three were successfully relocated to permanent habitat ([[Twelve Mile Lake]] on CRIT land). Tissue samples (muscle plugs) from all fish, including the mortalities, were transferred to Dr. Tom Dowling's genetics laboratory at Arizona State University for analysis. This record is a valuable data point for population monitoring and genetic studies of this federally endangered species in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]].

- **Sexual Dimorphism/Condition**: All three males exhibited clear spawning readiness — described as "ripe," tuberculate (nuptial tubercles present, a secondary sexual characteristic), and actively running milt, indicating the fish were in **active reproductive/spawning condition** at the time of capture (early January), consistent with known winter-spring spawning periodicity for this species in the [[Lower Colorado River]].
- **Sexual Size Dimorphism**: Females (615mm/2.50kg and 573mm/2.35kg) were notably larger in both length and weight than the males (522–554mm, 1.75–2.05kg), a pattern consistent with typical [[Razorback Sucker]] sexual size dimorphism.
- All individuals were adult-sized, consistent with mature breeding-stock size for the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Context**: The fish were recovered from an anthropogenic irrigation canal system (CRIT Main Canal) rather than natural riverine or lacustrine habitat, highlighting the species' documented tendency to occupy and persist in altered/artificial aquatic habitats connected to the [[Colorado River]] system.
- **Habitat Vulnerability**: The canal was scheduled to be dried within a week of the recovery operation as part of routine agricultural/irrigation water management, representing a significant anthropogenic mortality risk for any fish trapped within such conduits.
- **Conservation Translocation**: Surviving fish were relocated to [[Twelve Mile Lake]], a permanent water body on CRIT lands, illustrating active habitat-based conservation intervention (salvage and translocation) for this endangered catostomid.
- **Genetic Monitoring Infrastructure**: The use of PIT-tagging and tissue archiving (muscle plugs sent to ASU) reflects an established, systematic population monitoring and genetic stock assessment program for [[Razorback Sucker]] in the region, likely linked to broader recovery efforts under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Key Findings
- Five [[Razorback Sucker]] individuals (3 males, 2 females) were salvaged from a drying agricultural canal on the CRIT reservation on 5 January 1993.
- All three captured males were in active spawning condition (tuberculate, ripe, running milt), indicating this canal habitat was being used near/during the species' reproductive period.
- Two mortalities occurred during the recovery operation; cause of death was undetermined at time of reporting.
- Despite the mortalities, the operation was framed as a net conservation benefit, since all five fish would likely have perished when the canal was scheduled to dry within the following week.
- Muscle tissue from all fish (including mortalities) was preserved and transferred to Dr. Tom Dowling (Arizona State University) for genetic analysis.
- Three surviving fish were successfully released into permanent habitat at Twelve Mile Lake on CRIT lands.
- The operation involved multi-agency collaboration among [[Arizona State University]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], reflecting an interagency approach to endangered fish recovery in the Lower Colorado River Basin.
