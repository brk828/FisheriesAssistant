# Reassignment of Endangered Species Management Responsibilities in Region 2

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-management

## Executive Summary

This 1988 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum from the [[Regional Office]] of [[Region 2]] (Albuquerque, NM) transfers day-to-day lead responsibility for endangered, threatened, and candidate species from the regional office to individual [[Ecological Services Field Stations]] and [[Fisheries Field Stations]]. Authority for endangered species matters moved from Fisheries and Federal Assistance to Fish and Wildlife Enhancement on March 28, 1988. Attachments assign a lead field station and, in many cases, supporting stations for each listed or candidate species, and summarize the duties of a lead station. The document is administrative rather than research-based; it contains no population data, sample sizes, or ecological measurements.

## Background & Description

The memo is addressed to project leaders in Region 2. It describes an organizational shift, with several stated goals:

- **Accelerated recovery:** The Regional Director expects that recovery of "at least some, and perhaps many, species" will speed up through stronger advocacy at the local level.
- **Broader partnerships:** Project leaders are expected to create opportunities for entities outside the FWS to support or undertake beneficial actions.
- **Cross-program coordination:** The reassignment is intended to increase coordination among Regional programs.

Attachment 1 contains the assignment tables. Each table lists a lead field station, the species or species group, a short list of supporting stations, and a footnote marker for species "not currently listed" (candidate species, species proposed for listing, or species under management agreements). Species occurring in more than one Region are flagged with a designated lead Region. Attachment 1 includes:

- Lead-by-station tables for Albuquerque, Clear Lake, Tulsa, Fort Worth, Corpus Christi, Phoenix, Dexter, San Marcos, and Pinetop, plus a table for refuges and wildlife field stations.
- An alphabetical index by species group (amphibians, birds, fish, invertebrates, mammals, plants, reptiles).
- A key to station abbreviations (for example ALB = Albuquerque, CC = Corpus Christi, PHX = Phoenix, DEX-NFH = Dexter National Fish Hatchery).

Attachment 2 summarizes the charges of a lead field station (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo does not report ecological data for any species. It notes, however, that the FWS's mission includes ecosystem-level protection: "As ecosystems are conserved for the direct benefit of threatened or endangered species, other species and species groups also benefit directly or indirectly." The species assignments cover several habitat types, reflected in the lead station names and refuge assignments:

- **Freshwater fishes** in desert and Southwestern drainages, such as [[Gila Trout]], [[Pecos Pupfish]], [[Chub, Bonytail]], [[Squawfish, Colorado]], [[Woundfin]], and [[Shiner, Beautiful]], managed largely by Phoenix, Dexter, Albuquerque, and San Marcos stations.
- **Aquatic and spring invertebrates** (springsnails, snails, isopods, and Texas cave invertebrates) assigned mainly to Albuquerque and Fort Worth.
- **Coastal and grassland species** in Texas and Oklahoma, including [[Attwater's Prairie Chicken]], [[Red-cockaded Woodpecker]], [[Piping Plover]], and [[Sea Turtles]], managed largely by Corpus Christi, Clear Lake, and Tulsa.
- **Plants** and **mammals**, such as the [[Ocelot]] and [[Jaguarundi]], which are assigned through refuge-based management.

Several species are assigned to "Support" stations that coordinate with the lead, which indicates shared ranges across multiple station areas. Species occurring in more than one Region have a designated lead Region.

## Key Findings

**Scope and timing of the reassignment**

- Responsibility for endangered species matters in Region 2 was transferred from Fisheries and Federal Assistance to Fish and Wildlife Enhancement on March 28, 1988.
- Assignments to the field are effective immediately, with individual exceptions to be considered based on vacancies and other circumstances.
- The Regional Director expected to inform colleagues and cooperators within two weeks that coordination and operation of listing and recovery management had been delegated to field stations.

**Regional Office retains certain functions**

- Lead responsibility for a few species was kept in the Regional Office because of national or international importance (for example, [[Falcon, American Peregrine]] and [[Bald Eagle]] entries list RO involvement).
- Initial drafting of proposed and final listing, delisting, and reclassification rules, plus related public notices, remains in the Regional Office.
- A small cadre of specialists will remain in the Regional Office to provide biological information and technical guidance for listing rules.
- [[Section 7 Consultation]] remains the responsibility of the Ecological Services Field Stations.

**Attachment 1: Assignment scale**

- Attachment 1 covers more than 100 listed, candidate, and proposed species and species groups.
- The Albuquerque lead table alone includes roughly two dozen entries, including several springsnails, cacti, and the [[Southern Spotted Owl]].
- Lead assignments are split among nine field stations plus refuges and fisheries stations, with supporting stations listed for many species.

**Attachment 2: Duties of a lead field station**

Each lead station is charged with:

1. Coordinating efforts, directly or through cooperators, that contribute to species recovery.
2. Securing annual authorization, work schedules, and funding from the Regional Director through the [[Work Activity Guidance]] process.
3. Developing and revising recovery plans, internally or with recovery teams.
4. Evaluating the effectiveness of completed recovery actions.
5. Collecting and reporting status information on assigned species and alerting the Regional Office to major changes. Status surveys for candidate species remain with Fish and Wildlife Enhancement.
6. Recommending a course of action for each species with a completed status survey.
7. Recommending candidate species for listing evaluation.
8. Preparing a one-page annual report on actions and species status.
9. Consulting routinely with scientific staff, in the Regional Office and elsewhere, on relevant biological information.
10. Keeping supporting stations and the Regional Office informed; supporting stations share a mutual responsibility to the lead.
11. Maintaining timely communication with state, federal, and local agencies, institutions, private cooperators, and interested parties.

**Standing expectation**

Project leaders are expected to take beneficial actions for their assigned species, within reasonable limits, even when no species-specific endangered species funding has been provided.
