# Establishing a Captive Broodstock for the Endangered Bonytail Chub (Gila elegans)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #conservation-genetics #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This paper evaluates the genetic status of a captive broodstock of the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), one of the most critically imperiled freshwater fishes in North America. Wild bonytail persist only as a declining population of large, old adults in [[Lake Mohave]] on the lower Colorado River, with scattered individuals in the upper Colorado River basin. In 1981, F1 progeny were produced at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] from at most 10 wild-caught adults, and a subsample (~2,300 individuals, ~350 surviving by 1997) was retained at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] as the "original captive broodstock." Using allozyme and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analyses combined with theoretical/simulation genetics, the authors estimate the **effective number of founders** contributing to this F1 stock at only **~3.5 to 8.5 individuals** — a drastic reduction from the nominal 10 wild founders. The study concludes that incorporation of additional wild-caught fish into any future broodstock is essential to preserve adequate genetic variation, and proposes quantitative guidelines for how such wild fish should be weighted in future breeding schemes. This work serves as a general model for genetic management of captive broodstocks in other endangered species.

## Background & Description

The source text is a population/conservation genetics study and does not describe external morphological traits of *Gila elegans* in detail. Genetic "morphology" data reported instead includes:

- **Allozyme loci examined:** sAat-A, Gpi-B, and Pgm-A (three polymorphic loci).
- **Six distinct three-locus genotypes** identified among 30 F1 progeny sampled from DNFH (see [[Table 2 Genotype Distribution]]).
- **Genotype 1** (heterozygous only at Pgm-A) dominated the sample (22 of 30 individuals, 73%), consistent with descent from a single dominant mating pair.
- **Significant excess of heterozygotes** at Pgm-A relative to Hardy-Weinberg expectations (3 aa : 25 ab : 2 bb).
- **mtDNA haplotypes:** Three haplotypes identified (a, b, c) via SSCP analysis of cytochrome b and NADH dehydrogenase subunit fragments.
  - Haplotype *a* vs *b*: ~0.7% sequence divergence (8 nucleotide differences) — suggestive of two historically divergent bonytail lineages.
  - Haplotype *a* vs *c*: 1 nucleotide difference.
  - Haplotype *b* vs *c*: 2 nucleotide differences.
  - All 20 sampled F1 individuals were monomorphic for haplotype *a*, while wild Lake Mohave fish showed all three haplotypes (a: 43.8%, b: 31.2%, c: 25%).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary habitat:** [[Lake Mohave]], an impoundment of the lower Colorado River, houses the majority of remaining wild adults — described as large, old individuals with apparent lack of recruitment in the wild.
- **Secondary habitat:** Scattered individuals persist in the upper [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Population structure:** Three overlapping cohort categories likely coexist in Lake Mohave:
  1. Truly wild fish (hatched before 1981 stocking)
  2. F1 hatchery fish released in 1981 (marked with oxytetracycline, deemed "surplus")
  3. F2 fish from unmonitored ("volunteer") reproduction of F1 fish in hatchery holding ponds, stocked 1981–1996
- **Stocking history:** 41,517 F1 and 161,371 F2 bonytail chub were stocked into Lake Mohave between 1981 and 1996.
- **Reproductive biology:** Wild-caught females were pair-mated in captivity; fecundity ranged from 5,850–37,700 eggs per female (mean 25,090), with fertilization proportions of 0.91–0.97 (one female failed to produce eggs) (see [[Table 1 Fecundity Data]]).
- **Conservation context:** Related congeners in genus *Gila* also show generally low genetic variation, suggesting a broader regional pattern of genetic depauperation among southwestern desert cyprinids (see [[Endangered Desert Fishes]]).

## Key Findings

- **Effective founder number ("high" estimate):** Based on original hatchery production records (relatively even contribution across 5 mating pairs), N_ef ≈ **8.48** — only 15.2% less than the theoretical maximum of 10 wild founders.
- **Effective founder number ("low" estimate):** Based on allozyme genotype ratios and strongly supported by mtDNA monomorphism, N_ef ≈ **3.48**, implying progeny descended from as few as 2–3 mating pairs with highly unequal contributions.
- **mtDNA supports the "low" estimate:** Simulation showed the observed monomorphic mtDNA sample (all 20 F1 = haplotype *a*) was 4.4× more probable under the "low" scenario than the "high" scenario, given the wild population's estimated haplotype frequency (0.438).
- **Cytonuclear disequilibrium:** Significant non-random association (P < .05) between mtDNA haplotype and Pgm-A genotype detected in wild Lake Mohave samples, possibly due to admixture and/or selection.
- **Effect of using finite F1 broodstock to found future stock:** With 30 pairs of F1 parents (60 individuals), retained genetic variation reaches 89.3% ("high") to 97.1% ("low") of the maximum possible; using >30 pairs yields diminishing returns (up to 150 pairs → only 97.6–99.4%).
- **Critical recommendation:** Because even the optimistic "high" estimate (~8.5 effective founders) is far below ideal, **incorporation of additional wild-caught fish is essential**. Adding even a single wild fish increases effective founders from 8.5→9.5 (+11.8%) under the "high" estimate, or 3.5→4.5 (+28.7%) under the "low" estimate.
- **Practical breeding guideline proposed:** Representation of new wild founders in future broodstock should fall between 1/(8.5 + N_w) and 1/(3.5 + N_w), where N_w = number of additional wild-caught fish incorporated.
- **Mating structure advice:** Wild × wild (W×W) matings should be avoided (to not "waste" two sets of rare wild gametes in a single cross); F1 × Wild matings are preferred to maximize genetic contribution of newly captured wild individuals.
- **Genetic framework used:** Effective founder number calculated via the *eigenvalue effective population size* framework (per [[Caballero 1994]]), aligning with standard practices in pedigreed endangered species management (per [[Ballou and Lacy 1995]]).
- **Broader implication:** This case study is proposed as a transferable model for genetic management of captive broodstocks in other endangered species pending recovery of stable wild populations — see also [[Population Bottleneck]], [[Inbreeding Depression]], and [[Genetic Drift in Small Populations]].
