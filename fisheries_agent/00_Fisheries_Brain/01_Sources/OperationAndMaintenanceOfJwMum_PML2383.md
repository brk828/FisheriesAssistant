# Colorado River Recovery Program FY 2002 Annual Project Report: J.W. Mumma Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This annual project report from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] covers FY 2002 operations of the [[J.W. Mumma Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility]] (NASRF) in Alamosa, Colorado. The facility rears [[Bonytail]] for stocking in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] for the [[San Juan River]] drainage, and also held [[Razorback Sucker]] for recovery ponds. The project is ongoing. Survival was reported as good, ammonia was not a problem this year, and the main constraint was pond space, which was relieved once improvements at [[Trinity State Junior College]] were completed. The report was signed by Principal Investigator J. David Schnoor of the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] and dated 15 December 2003.

## Background & Description

The project is a Colorado River Recovery Program support activity. It is linked to several action plan items:

- **General support:** conduct annual fish propagation; operate and maintain facilities.
- **[[Green River Action Plan]] (Yampa and Little Snake Rivers):** develop and implement a state stocking plan for Bonytail in the [[Yampa River]] within [[Dinosaur National Monument]].
- **[[Colorado River Action Plan]] (mainstem):** implement state stocking plans for Colorado Pikeminnow and Bonytail.
- **Colorado River Action Plan ([[Gunnison River]]):** implement a state stocking plan for Colorado Pikeminnow.

The principal investigators were David Schnoor and Jenn Logan, both of the Colorado Division of Wildlife, based in Alamosa. The study schedule is listed as ongoing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three target species are native to the Colorado River system and are federally listed for recovery. Propagation depends on pond and tank rearing, [[Hatchery Stocking]], and [[PIT Tagging]] of stocked fish to track survival. Water quality was a concern in earlier years; this year ammonia levels were not a problem. Fish were held in ponds and tanks at NASRF and at Trinity State Junior College (TSJC) ponds. Fish losses during the year came from handling during moves, sampling for fish health inspections, and predation. Dead fish were preserved in alcohol for possible genetic work.

## Key Findings

**Stock received and reared**

- **Bonytail:** Approximately 40,000 fry were received from Dexter, New Mexico, on 5/10/02 (date partly illegible in the scan). Survival was good. At the time of reporting, about 38,225 fish were on station, reported at roughly 345 fish per pound. Fish were smaller than usual because they could not be moved into ponds early in the year. Most were in ponds by the end of the year and expected to grow over winter.
- **Colorado Pikeminnow:** 5,000 fry were received from Dexter, New Mexico, in June 2002. Survival was described as "very" good. About 7,315 fish were on station, reported at roughly 500 per pound, and most had been moved to ponds. They were also small because pond space was not available early in the season.

**Stocking events**

- 19,845 [[Razorback Sucker]] fingerlings (about 399.5 per pound) were stocked into recovery ponds near Grand Junction, Colorado, on 10/18/2001.
- 16,614 Razorback Sucker fingerlings were stocked into recovery ponds near Farmington, New Mexico, in spring 2002 (date partly illegible).
- About 8,400 Bonytail were PIT tagged in September 2002 for an October river stocking (exact count partly illegible in the scan).

**Infrastructure**

- Pond improvements at TSJC were completed, which removed the bottleneck on pond space for Bonytail and Colorado Pikeminnow.

**Budget (FY 2002)**

- Funds provided: $50,000. Funds expended: reported as $50,000 (the difference line is also listed as $50,000; the figures are not internally consistent in the scan).
- Itemized expenses total about $43,670 by summation of the listed lines, including:
  - TSJC lease: $18,500
  - TSJC pond improvements: $12,190
  - Stocking vehicle use and maintenance: $5,200
  - Utilities: $2,240
  - Travel: $1,500
  - NASRF pond improvements: $1,440
  - Miscellaneous equipment: $950
  - Discharge chlorine: $850
  - Feed and equipment maintenance: $400 each
- About $30,000 of total expenses were one-time costs.
- Volunteer labor covered PIT tagging and about 10 hours per month of general labor on the three species (exact hours partly illegible).

**Recommendations and status**

- Continue culturing [[Bonytail]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] at NASRF to meet the stocking needs of the Recovery Program.
- Project status: on track and ongoing.
- PIT-tag data is to be submitted to the data manager by January.
