# Fish Remains from Arroyo Sequit Shellmound (LAn-52), Los Angeles County, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #zooarchaeology #Scombridae #Lamnidae #aboriginal-subsistence

## Executive Summary

This zooarchaeological report by W. I. Follett analyzes 459 fish remains recovered from the [[Arroyo Sequit]] Shellmound (LAn-52) in extreme western [[Los Angeles County]], [[California]]. The site, located on a bluff rising to nearly 75 feet elevation, was occupied between approximately 1000 B.C. and 1500 A.D. Of the total remains, 128 were identified to at least genus level, representing approximately 19 species from 14 families. The assemblage includes sharks, rays, and diverse teleost fishes ranging from a 6-inch [[Blacksmith]] to large pelagic species including [[Bluefin Tuna]] (up to 50 pounds) and sharks (100-200 pounds). The presence of strictly pelagic and deep-water species provides clear evidence of boat-based fishing by aboriginal inhabitants.

## Background & Description

The [[Arroyo Sequit Shellmound]] is an archaeological site situated on the east side of Arroyo Sequit creek, on the south side of U.S. Highway 101. The site occupies a large bluff rising gradually from the creek bed to approximately 75 feet elevation. Excavations were conducted by Freddie Curtis as part of archaeological investigations in [[Leo Carrillo Beach State Park]].

The fish remains collection comprises 459 specimens, most incomplete or fragmentary. For elasmobranch vertebrae, only the centrum persisted. Skeletal elements identified include teeth, vertebrae (precaudal and caudal), and various cranial bones (dentary, quadrate, maxillary, articular, metapterygoid, hyomandibular, frontal, and pharyngeal bones). The collection represents 14 families:

**Elasmobranchs:**
- [[Lamnidae]] (mackerel sharks): 7 teeth from [[Lamna ditropis]] (salmon shark, 3 specimens) and [[Isurus oxyrinchus]] (mako, 4 specimens)
- [[Carcharhinidae]]: 24 centra from [[Triakis semifasciata]] (leopard shark, identification questionable)
- [[Squatinidae]]: 2 centra from [[Squatina californica]] (Pacific angel shark)
- [[Rhinobatidae]]: 3 centra from [[Rhinobatos productus]] (shovelnose guitarfish)
- [[Myliobatidae]]: 1 tooth segment and 1 centrum from [[Myliobatis californica]] (bat stingray)

**Teleosts:**
- [[Serranidae]]: 2 bones from [[Paralabrax clathratus]] (kelp bass, identification questionable)
- [[Sphyraenidae]]: 11 bones from [[Sphyraena argentea]] (Pacific barracuda), including vertebra from fish approximately 34 inches long
- [[Carangidae]]: 3 vertebrae from [[Seriola dorsalis]] (yellowtail), largest from fish about 32 inches and perhaps 12 pounds
- [[Scombridae]]: 51 bones from four species including [[Scomber japonicus diego]] (Pacific mackerel, 23 specimens), [[Sarda lineolata]] (California bonito, 6 specimens), [[Thunnus thynnus]] (bluefin tuna, 3 vertebrae), and [[Thunnus alalunga]] (albacore, 17 vertebrae)
- [[Pomacentridae]]: 1 vertebra from [[Chromis punctipinnis]] (blacksmith), from fish about 6 inches long
- [[Labridae]]: 16 bones from [[Pimelometopon pulchrum]] (California sheephead)
- [[Scorpaenidae]]: 6 bones from [[Sebastodes]] species including [[Sebastodes paucispinis]] (bocaccio) and [[Sebastodes goodei]] (chilipepper)
- [[Batrachoididae]]: 1 vertebra from [[Porichthys myriaster]] (slim midshipman, identification questionable)
- [[Clinidae]]: 1 dentary from [[Heterostichus rostratus]] (giant kelpfish)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish assemblage represents species from multiple marine habitats along the southern [[California]] coast:

**Pelagic Species:** [[Thunnus thynnus]] (bluefin tuna) and [[Thunnus alalunga]] (albacore) are strictly pelagic. [[Lamna ditropis]] (salmon shark) and [[Isurus oxyrinchus]] (mako) are commonly pelagic but occasionally enter shallow water.

**Offshore Species:** [[Sphyraena argentea]] (Pacific barracuda), [[Seriola dorsalis]] (yellowtail), [[Scomber japonicus diego]] (Pacific mackerel), and [[Sarda lineolata]] (California bonito) ordinarily occur offshore but are not strictly pelagic.

**Deep-Water Species:** Adult [[Sebastodes paucispinis]] (bocaccio) and [[Sebastodes goodei]] (chilipepper) occur near the sea bottom in water too deep to be fished from shore.

**Shallow-Water Species:** Nine species commonly enter very shallow water: [[Triakis semifasciata]] (leopard shark), [[Squatina californica]] (Pacific angel shark), [[Rhinobatos productus]] (shovelnose guitarfish), [[Myliobatis californica]] (bat stingray), [[Paralabrax clathratus]] (kelp bass), [[Chromis punctipinnis]] (blacksmith), [[Pimelometopon pulchrum]] (California sheephead), [[Porichthys myriaster]] (slim midshipman), and [[Heterostichus rostratus]] (giant kelpfish).

All species represented, including sharks, guitarfish, and stingray, are edible. Most specimens represent average-sized individuals with no unusually large examples. Size estimates include: salmon shark and mako (100-200 pounds), bluefin tuna (up to 50 pounds), and most other species under 20 pounds. The smallest individual is a blacksmith approximately 6 inches long.

## Key Findings

**Stratigraphic Distribution:** The largest amount of material and greatest species diversity occurred at depths of 18-30 inches. Distribution by depth shows:
- 0-6": 4 species, 10 specimens
- 6-12": 6 species, 59 specimens  
- 12-18": 9 species, 40 specimens
- 18-24": 11 species, 110 specimens
- 24-30": 13 species, 157 specimens
- 30-36": 9 species, 23 specimens
- 36-42": 6 species, 44 specimens
- 40-48": 3 species, 4 specimens
- 48"-sterile: 2 species, 3 specimens

**Evidence of Boat-Based Fishing:** The presence of four species provides clear evidence of boat use by aboriginal fishermen:
1. [[Thunnus thynnus]] (bluefin tuna) - strictly pelagic
2. [[Thunnus alalunga]] (albacore) - strictly pelagic
3. [[Sebastodes paucispinis]] (bocaccio) - deep-water bottom species
4. [[Sebastodes goodei]] (chilipepper) - deep-water bottom species

Additionally, [[Lamna ditropis]] (salmon shark) and [[Isurus oxyrinchus]] (mako) were much more probably caught from boats than from shore.

**Shore vs. Boat Fishing:** Four offshore species ([[Sphyraena argentea]], [[Seriola dorsalis]], [[Scomber japonicus diego]], [[Sarda lineolata]]) were probably taken most frequently from boats but may have been caught occasionally from shore. Nine shallow-water species were equally likely caught from shore or boat.

**Taxonomic Composition:** The [[Scombridae]] (mackerels and tunas) dominate the identified assemblage with 51 specimens across four species. [[Triakis semifasciata]] (leopard shark) is represented by 24 centra, though species identification is questionable. [[Pimelometopon pulchrum]] (California sheephead) contributed 16 specimens including distinctive pharyngeal bones.

**Size and Weight Estimates:** Specific measurements include:
- [[Sphyraena argentea]]: 3rd precaudal vertebra 11.3 mm horizontal diameter, from fish about 34 inches total length and 5 pounds
- [[Seriola dorsalis]]: largest vertebra from fish about 32 inches and perhaps 12 pounds
- [[Thunnus thynnus]]: largest vertebra 21 mm horizontal diameter (approximately 4th caudal), from fish weighing perhaps 50 pounds
- [[Chromis punctipinnis]]: vertebra 2.3 mm horizontal diameter (approximately 4th caudal), from fish about 6 inches long

**Preservation:** Most remains are incomplete or fragmentary. For elasmobranchs, only vertebral centra persisted. Two [[Scomber japonicus diego]] vertebrae were partly charred, suggesting cooking or disposal in fire.
