# Lower Colorado River Reservoir Operations and Native Fish Recovery Appendices

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #reservoir-operations

## Executive Summary

This compilation is a set of appendices (E through J) to a Lower Colorado River environmental document. It covers historical and projected reservoir operations from [[Lake Mead]] to Mexico (Appendix E), the [[Yuma Desalting Plant]] (Appendix F), a 1995 native fish recovery program for the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] (Appendix G), a 1984 flood control agreement between the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] (Appendix H), 1995 records compiled under the Supreme Court decree (Appendix I), and a bibliography (Appendix J). The fish-focused material is concentrated in Appendix G, while Appendices E and F describe the flow and habitat regime that shapes the river's aquatic environment.

## Background & Description

The lower Colorado River system is a chain of regulated reservoirs and dams: [[Hoover Dam]]/Lake Mead, [[Davis Dam]]/Lake Mohave, and [[Parker Dam]]/Lake Havasu. Annually about 9 million acre-feet (maf) are released from Lake Mead, of which 7.5 maf serve Nevada, Arizona, and California entitlements and 1.5 maf are delivered to Mexico under the 1944 Treaty.

Appendix E defines the time-based flow terms (instantaneous, hourly, mean daily, mean monthly, midnight elevation), noting that they must not be used interchangeably when describing habitat. Appendix G is a recovery plan dated July 17, 1995, with the goal of preventing extinction of the Razorback Sucker (RBS) and Bonytail Chub (BTC) in the Lower Colorado River Basin, with emphasis on Lakes Mohave and Havasu.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regime:** Hoover releases ranged from a non-flood-year maximum mean daily release of about 25,000 cfs (March 1994, after turbine uprating) to a minimum of 800 cfs (January 1993). Davis Dam's minimum mean daily release was 1,600 cfs; Parker Dam's was 30 cfs.
- **Reservoir elevations:** Lake Mead's historical range runs from a minimum of 895 feet to a maximum of 1,229 feet. Lake Mohave's maximum is 646.5 feet, with a minimum of 630 feet without resetting intake stops. Lake Havasu's operating range is 445.8 to 450.5 feet.
- **Razorback Sucker habitat:** A Lake Mohave backwater rearing program starting in 1994 limited drawdown to no more than 2 feet in a 10-day period during spawning, keeping Mohave above elevation 640 feet from March 15 to June 15.
- **Yuma region:** Irrigation drainage from the Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation and Drainage District is the target feed for desalting. Without treatment, the water would be bypassed to the Cienega in Sonora, Mexico.
- **Vaquita and marine fishes:** Bibliography entries (Appendix J) document the [[Vaquita]] and [[Totoaba]] in the Gulf of California, both of which are threatened by gillnet fisheries.

## Key Findings

**Reservoir operations (Appendix E)**
- Projected 15-year operations (1996–2010) were simulated with the CRSS model using 1,000 traces, and were found to be "very similar" to the historical 15-year period for Hoover and Parker monthly releases.
- Projected Lake Mead end-of-year elevations show a 0.34 probability that the lake will be full, and a 0.99 probability of staying above elevation 1,130 feet over the next 15 years.
- Projected Hoover releases fall largely within 800,000 acre-feet monthly volume classes; 9 percent of the projected values fall in the 800,000 acre-foot class, plus or minus 50,000 acre-feet.
- The ESA consultation covers only the 5-year period 1996–2000.

**Yuma Desalting Plant (Appendix F)**
- The plant, about 6 miles west of Yuma, was essentially complete in 1992. Its full-scale capacity is 72.4 million gallons per day, designed to desalt about 73,000 acre-feet per year of irrigation drainage.
- At full capacity, about 97,000 acre-feet of saline drainage (2,900–3,200 ppm) feed the plant, yielding about 68,500 acre-feet of product water (250–300 ppm) and 28,000 acre-feet of reject stream (about 9,400 ppm).
- Blended water of 73,000 acre-feet is returned to the river for Treaty deliveries at the NIB, upstream of Morelos Dam.
- Pretreatment uses about 100 tons of lime and produces about 360 tons of calcium carbonate sludge per day.
- The plant is currently on standby. At one-third capacity it would yield about 26,200 acre-feet of usable blended water.

**Native fish recovery (Appendix G, July 1995)**
- *Razorback Sucker* objective: capture as much genetic variability as practical in Lake Mohave and replace the existing population by the year 2000 (minimum 50,000 adults); increase Lake Havasu to 25,000 adults.
- Between 1992 and 1994, 2,046 subadults (in 1994), 542 (in 1993), and 129 (in 1992) were stocked into Lake Mohave. In 1995, 21,146 swim-up fry were collected.
- *Bonytail* objective: reach 25,000 adults in each of Lakes Mohave and Havasu by 2005. About 160,000 fingerlings were stocked into Lake Mohave between 1981 and 1992; the Havasu population status is unknown.
- Limiting factors include low wild fry collection capacity, rearing facilities and personnel limits, and the need to verify population status.
- Funding needs were itemized in the FY1996 budget, with current efforts and unfunded needs compared for each task.

**Federal coordination (Appendices H and I)**
- A 1984 Field Working Agreement sets the Lake Mead flood control space schedule, with an available flood control storage of 1,500,000 acre-feet on 1 August, and a maximum release of 280,000 cfs at the Davis Dam gage.
- Appendix I compiles 1995 calendar-year records under the 1964 Supreme Court decree in *Arizona v. California*.

**Bibliography (Appendix J)**
- The reference list contains several hundred entries spanning fish, birds, mammals, reptiles, and invertebrates, along with personal communications with 21 agency and academic contacts.
