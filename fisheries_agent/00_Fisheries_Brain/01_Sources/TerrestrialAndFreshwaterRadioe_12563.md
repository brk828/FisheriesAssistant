# Terrestrial and Freshwater Radioecology: A Selected Bibliography (Supplement 2)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #radioecology

## Executive Summary

This document is the second supplement (March 1964) to a selected bibliography on [[Terrestrial and Freshwater Radioecology]], compiled by Alfred W. Klement, Jr. and Vincent Schultz for the U.S. [[Atomic Energy Commission]] Division of Biology and Medicine (TID-3910, Suppl. 2). It is not a research study; it is a reference list of roughly 1,200 numbered entries published through 1963, plus a preface explaining compilation criteria, a list of abbreviations for document sources, and an acknowledgment section. The entries cover radiation effects on organisms, radionuclide cycling in water and soil, tracer-based ecology, and fallout and environmental monitoring. Fish appear only in a subset of entries, mostly on radiotracer uptake and radiosensitivity.

## Background & Description

The bibliography follows the criteria of the March 1962 bibliography and Supplement 1 (February 1963). It draws heavily on [[Nuclear Science Abstracts]] (NSA), and it cross-references U.S. government report sources such as the Office of Technical Services (OTS), the Government Printing Office (GPO), and the Division of Technical Information Extension (DTIE). Preprints bearing laboratory report numbers were generally omitted. Author-name spellings from translations were not standardized, though listings for each author are usually grouped together alphabetically.

The preface and symbol list identify the source abbreviations used throughout, including APCA, CA, HMSO, IDS, IAEA, JPRS, NAS-NRC, RDA, and WR (Wildlife Review). The acknowledgment credits Dr. Charles F. Lytle for locating Soviet-language publications, and several U.S. and international libraries.

Entries are primarily in English, with substantial numbers in Russian, German, French, and Italian, many with English translation references.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography spans several interlinked research themes:

- **Radiotracer ecology:** use of [[Phosphorus-32]], [[Carbon-14]], [[Strontium-90]], [[Cesium-137]], [[Zinc-65]], and other isotopes to trace food webs, insect movement, and nutrient cycling. Many insect entries use P-32 to label individuals for dispersal studies.
- **Aquatic systems:** uptake and accumulation of radionuclides by freshwater algae, [[Aquatic Macrophytes]], mollusks, crustaceans, plankton, and fish; primary productivity measured by the carbon-14 method; and transport of radionuclides in rivers, estuaries, and sediments (including a 1963 conference proceedings with roughly 40 papers).
- **Nevada Test Site and other field sites:** studies of small mammals, mites, reptiles, birds, and vegetation recovery after nuclear detonations (e.g., Project SEDAN), and biotic community surveys of the [[Nevada Test Site]].
- **Fallout and monitoring:** global and regional fallout deposition, radioiodine in deer and caribou, strontium-90 in milk, soil, and plants, and drinking-water surveys.
- **Radiation biology:** radiosensitivity and lethal dose studies on insects, amphibians, fish embryos, plants, microorganisms, and mammals, including a number of [[Drosophila]] mutation studies.
- **Hydrology:** tracer-based river flow measurement, groundwater movement, and sediment transport using radioisotopes.

## Key Findings

The document's main content is a bibliographic inventory, with the following features relevant to fish and aquatic ecology:

- **Fish-related radioecology:** the list includes work on [[Strontium-90 Uptake in Fish]], calcium-45 and phosphorus-32 labelling of carp and sturgeon (largely Soviet studies cited via Karzinkin, 1962), iodine-131 thyroid studies in salmon and goldfish, and cesium-137 accumulation by brackish-water invertebrates. The Columbia River and Clinch River transport studies also address fish.
- **Freshwater organisms:** numerous entries cover radioisotope accumulation by algae (including Scenedesmus), mollusks (Unio, Lymnaea), crustaceans, and aquatic insects. Several Soviet studies (Timofeyeva-Resovskaya and colleagues) examine how EDTA and water chemistry alter uptake coefficients.
- **Key multi-author works:** the 1963 *Radioecology* symposium proceedings (Schultz and Klement, ed.; 746 pp.), the *Transport of Radionuclides in Fresh Water Systems* conference (Kornegay et al., 1963), and the IAEA *Radioisotopes in Hydrology* proceedings (1963, 469 pp.).
- **Nevada Test Site studies:** biotic community, mite, tick, and reptile surveys, and the close-in effects of Project SEDAN.
- **Reference scope:** the list includes items from the [[United Nations Scientific Committee on the Effects of Atomic Radiation]] (1962 report) and the Federal Radiation Council fallout estimates (1963).
- **Limits:** the document reports no new experimental data. Many entries are abstracts, lack page numbers, or note only a title, so the list should be read as a finding aid rather than a source of quantitative results.
