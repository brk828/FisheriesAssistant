# Dexter National Fish Hatchery Letter on Desert Fish Refugia and Brood Stock Genetics (1985)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This December 16, 1985 letter from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFE), New Mexico, to Dr. W. L. Minckley of Arizona State University, responds to [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] business. The writer declines to attend a recovery team meeting scheduled before the January 1987 AZ-NM Chapter meeting, comments on minutes from an October meeting, and reports on hatchery stocks of four endangered or sensitive fishes: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), and *Gila nigrescens*. The dominant concern is genetic integrity, since the brood stocks descend from very few wild fish.

## Background & Description

The letter is correspondence rather than research. It is addressed to Dr. Minckley at the [[Arizona State University]] Department of Zoology and copied to Jerry Burton and George Divine. The writer cannot attend a meeting held before the 29–31 January 1987 [[Arizona-New Mexico Chapter]] annual meeting in Silver City, because of prior commitments. If the meeting cannot be rescheduled, the writer asks to be told what information about DNFE operations is needed.

The letter covers four species held at the hatchery:

- ***Cyprinodon macularius*** (desert pupfish)
- ***Ptychocheilus lucius*** (Colorado pikeminnow)
- ***Gila elegans*** (bonytail)
- ***Gila nigrescens***

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Desert pupfish refugium.** The DNFE refugium population is about 5,000 fish. Pupfish found at [[Fort Huachuca]] may have come from the [[Arboretum]] refugium. The writer suggests contacting Jerry Burton, who helped Fort Huachuca obtain topminnows for its ponds in 1982 or 1983 and may have moved pupfish at the same time.

**Bog Hole renovation.** Renovating the [[Bog Hole]] is described as a formidable task because of its size, and its diversity and abundance of aquatic vegetation. The writer recommends:

- Dewatering, preferably by pumping onto dry land.
- Multiple applications of piscicide ([[Piscicide Application]]).
- Renovation of downstream habitat in the [[Upper Santa Cruz River]] if necessary.

**Colorado Basin wild stock.** Hatchery staff are working to obtain wild [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] from the Upper Colorado River Basin for the spring 1987 spawning.

## Key Findings

**1. Brood stock domestication and genetic diversity (*Ptychocheilus lucius*).**
- Domestication of brood stock is a key issue to stress.
- Staff are aware of the need to keep a genetically diverse stock. Evidence includes a large brood stock, although one female produced all the fish in one year, and future brood stock from three wild-caught females.
- Spawning practice uses 2–3 males per female, and a single male fertilizes eggs from no more than two females.

**2. Genetic integrity of the bonytail stock (*Gila elegans*).**
- Integrity is questionable mainly because few wild fish founded the current brood stock.
- In a worst case, a single pair produces all young in the ponds.
- Because bonytail are piscivorous and cannibalistic, the young-of-year harvested in fall may instead reflect survivors of a widespread spawning effort. The writer states that manual spawning is the only sure way to know.
- Fish from in-pond spawning were requested for stocking into [[Lake Mohave]], ponds at the [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], and the [[ASU Research Park]]. The letter asks that unwanted fish be declared and disposed of properly.

**3. *Gila nigrescens* stock.**
- Progeny from volunteer reproduction in the DNFE pond are few, so manual spawning and culture of young fish are needed to meet possible reintroduction demand.
- The entire brood came from ten fish, and supplemental wild stock is required to protect genetic integrity.

**4. Administrative items.**
- The writer cannot attend a meeting before the Chapter meeting and asks for a reply on scheduling or information needs.
- The letter closes with "See you in Silver City."
