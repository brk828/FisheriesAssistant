# Exotic Fish Survey of Aravaipa Creek, July 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

A memorandum from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) reports a survey of [[Aravaipa Creek]] conducted July 6-8, 1998, jointly with [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC) and the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM). The survey aimed to document the distribution and reproduction of the exotic [[Red Shiner]]. No red shiners were captured. The survey instead confirmed the establishment and reproduction of [[Yellow Bullhead]] and found [[Green Sunfish]] at abundances comparable to the bullheads. The author recommends resuming red shiner monitoring and addressing the threat these non-native species pose to native fish. She also calls for inter-agency cooperation because of manpower limits.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interagency memorandum dated July 20, 1998, from Heidi B. Blasius (AGFD Fisheries Assistant II) to Dave Gori (TNC).
- **Field crew:** Five AGFD staff and one University of Arizona researcher (Jeff Conn).
- **Prior context:** Red shiners had been found earlier in April 1998 by students of Dr. Minckley in the canyon and at the west end of the creek.
- **Sampling method:** Electrofishing moved downstream through all available habitat within a 200 m section (100 m above and 100 m below each station). Seining was used in limited areas, and block nets were used in fast currents. Dip nets were used in slow water. Stations were historic sites established by [[Arizona State University]] (ASU). Canyon localities were sampled with the same method.
- **Survey reach:** AGFD Region V sampled from the confluence with [[Parsons Canyon]] downstream to [[Sycamore Tree]]. TNC and BLM sampled from the Parsons Canyon confluence to Guest House.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey covered several tributary canyons, including [[Parsons Canyon]], [[Deer Canyon]], [[Paisano Canyon]], [[Booger Canyon]], [[Horse Camp Canyon]], [[Virgus Canyon]], [[Painted Cave Canyon]], and the Wagner Ranch, White's Ranch, and Sycamore Tree stations. Exotic species were recorded at each locality.

- **Exotic species:** Yellow bullheads and green sunfish were abundant at every canyon confluence. The author states that green sunfish numbers in the creek appear to equal those of yellow bullheads, although many green sunfish avoided capture.
- **Native fish:** The lower stations held many young-of-the-year [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]. A few juvenile [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Desert Sucker]] were also captured.
- **Predation and reproduction:** A 12 cm TL yellow bullhead dissected at Sycamore Tree had eaten an undigested sucker of about 6 cm. Most yellow bullheads were swollen with eggs, food, or both, which indicates active reproduction.
- **Invasion pathway:** The author notes that red shiners would most likely invade from the [[San Pedro River]], especially after periods of high flow.
- **Threat framing:** The memo treats the non-native species as a threat to native fish in the creek. This is part of the broader [[Invasive Species]] concern.

## Key Findings

**Red shiner status**
- No red shiners were observed or captured during the 1998 survey.
- Red shiners were found earlier in April 1998 in the canyon and at the west end.

**Yellow bullhead counts**
- Parsons Canyon (50 m below natural barrier): 2
- Deer Canyon: 10
- Paisano Canyon: 16
- Booger Canyon: 9
- Horse Camp Canyon: 10
- Virgus Canyon: 13
- Between Virgus and Painted Cave canyons: 5
- Painted Cave Canyon: 5

**Transect totals (per the summary table)**
- *Ameiurus natalis* (yellow bullhead): 109
- *Lepomis cyanellus* (green sunfish): 19
- *Gambusia affinis* (mosquitofish): 1

The field-text tallies do not sum exactly to the table totals, so the table totals are reported as published.

**Other findings**
- Green sunfish were present at Booger Canyon, Horse Camp Canyon, Virgus Canyon, and the area between Virgus and Painted Cave canyons.
- Yellow bullhead fry and adults, green sunfish, and mosquitofish were found at the lower stations on July 8.

**Recommendations**
1. Resume monitoring for red shiners.
2. Recognize and manage the threat posed by yellow bullheads and green sunfish to native fish.
3. Coordinate sampling across agencies, because of manpower limits.
4. Schedule future sampling after high-flow events, when red shiner invasion from the San Pedro River is most likely.
