# Metabolism of an Arid Region Canal Ecosystem

#aquatic-ecology #limnology #ecosystem-metabolism #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

Paul C. Marsh and Stuart G. Fisher measured whole-system and component metabolism in a 12-km reach of the [[Arizona Canal]] in central Arizona, USA, during 1983. The study used [[Diel Oxygen Curve Method|diel dissolved oxygen curves]] on four dates to estimate [[Whole-System Metabolism]], then partitioned gross production and respiration among algae, [[Macrophytes]], sediments and invertebrates. The canal was treated as a model lotic system to test whether longitudinal changes along a stream-like channel could be attributed to biotic or physical causes. The authors found that biotic components drove most metabolism, while only nitrate decline showed a consistent downstream pattern that could not be separated from physical change.

## Background & Description

Canals are conspicuous features of arid landscapes in the American Southwest. The [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] supply the [[Phoenix Metropolitan Area]], and their full flows are diverted into canals during most of the year. Southwestern canals are nearly uniform in morphometry, lack riparian vegetation, and have long periods of constant flow, which makes them a rare opportunity to study stream-like processes with little physical confounding. Before this study, canal metabolism had not been reported.

The study reach was 12 km long, east-west oriented, and lined with concrete. Mean channel width was roughly 13 to 19 m, and design capacity was about 53.9 m³/s. The sampled flow was about 14.2 m³/s on 14 November 1983. Bottom area was about 77.9% of the submerged surface, with sidewalls making up the rest. Dredging occurred one to three times per year, and the canal was drained for about four weeks each November to December.

Influent water chemistry depended on the mix of Salt and Verde river water. Salt River water had higher conductance (1140 µS/cm), Na⁺ (161 mg/L) and Cl⁻ (234 mg/L) than Verde River water (510 µS/cm, 30 mg/L Na⁺, 19 mg/L Cl⁻). During the study period, pH was about 7.5, conductance ranged from 250 to 835 µS/cm, and Cl⁻ ranged from 28 to 133 mg/L.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical setting.** Regional precipitation averages 23.4 cm per year. Water temperature ranged from about 10°C to 30°C, and summer daily ranges were under 5°C. Air temperatures reached above 43°C from late June into August. Inputs to the reach came only from [[Granite Reef Dam]] and occasional storm runoff.

**Biota.** Organic sediments up to about 15 cm deep covered only 14% of the bottom area, but they supported rooted macrophytes (*Potamogeton pectinatus*, *P. crispus*, *Zannichellia palustris*). Concrete surfaces were colonized by [[Cladophora Glomerata]] and an epiconcretic diatom assemblage, and blue-green algae (*Nostoc* sp. and *Oscillatoria* sp.) occurred in the middle reach.

Invertebrates were few and sparse, typical of Southwestern canals. Dominant groups included Turbellaria, Oligochaeta, Hydracarina, Ephemeroptera, Trichoptera, and Chironomidae. The introduced [[Corbicula Fluminea]] was conspicuous, reaching several thousand individuals per square metre in some areas. Most invertebrates were collector-gatherers or filterers. The only scraper noted was *Helicopsyche mexicana*. The canal supported a diverse and abundant fish community, with several species of angling quality, but no sport fishery existed. Fish and benthic roles were not assessed.

**Methods overview.** Whole-system metabolism was estimated at stations A, C and E, located 0, 5 and 12 km downflow of Granite Reef Dam, using modified Winkler titration at 2 to 4 hour intervals. Gross primary production (P_G) was computed as net production plus respiration, with reaeration estimated from night-time curves. Component measurements used [[Chamber Incubation Method|chamber incubations]] for algae and macrophytes, sediment cores, and areal coverage mapping.

## Key Findings

- **Gross primary production (P_G)** averaged **6.70 g O₂/m²/day** across the study period, with daily values from **3.34 to 8.94 g O₂/m²/day**.
- **Ecosystem respiration (R)** was reported in the abstract as roughly 8.3 g O₂/m²/day (OCR-affected; verify against source). Daily R ranged from **3.60 to 7.92 g O₂/m²/day**.
- The abstract describes the canal as **autotrophic**, meaning it exported or accumulated organic carbon. The P/R ratio value is garbled in the scan, so it should be checked before citing.
- **Epiconcretic algae** contributed about **80% of total P_G**, which the authors used to estimate an annual algal P_G of roughly 1,600 g O₂/m²/year (scan-uncertain value).
- **Macrophytes** contributed **less than 20%** (0.91 g O₂/m²/day) of P_G in November 1983. This is equivalent to an annual macrophyte P_G of about 400 g O₂/m²/year, which exceeded the rate reported for [[Silver Springs]], Florida.
- **Sediment respiration** was about **30% of whole-system R** (about 0.7 g O₂/m²/day). Annual sediment respiration was about 585 g O₂/m² by the authors' estimate.
- **Corbicula fluminea** could approach about 1.0 g O₂/m²/day where dense, but this contributed only about 0.1 g O₂/m²/day to whole-system R, which was insufficient to explain the observed discrepancy.
- **Summed subsystem measurements** overestimated P_G and underestimated R relative to whole-system values. The authors attributed this to chamber incubation artifacts and unmeasured components such as clam and macrophyte root respiration.
- **Longitudinal patterns:** Only **nitrate-N (NO₃-N)** showed a consistent downstream decline, independent of physical change. The decline was about **20 µg/L** over the reach in November 1983, and was smallest in the summer months. Net NO₃-N decline did not correlate directly with P_G, suggesting other processes such as denitrification or bacterial assimilation.
- **Soluble reactive phosphorus (SRP)** showed a statistically significant downstream decline on only one date (26–27 July). On 23 other dates, SRP was uniform along the reach (average difference under 10%). Ammonia-N showed no longitudinal pattern.
- **Physical changes** at 2–3 km and 8–9 km coincided with abrupt shifts in algal communities. The authors concluded that even small morphological changes can produce biotic responses that might otherwise be attributed to upstream effects.
- The study concluded that **biotic and abiotic mechanisms are confounded** in the canal. Discrimination of biological versus physical controls would require newer, more uniform canals such as those of the [[Central Arizona Project]], then under construction.
