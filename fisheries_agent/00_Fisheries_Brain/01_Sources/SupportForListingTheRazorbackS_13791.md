# Letter Regarding Proposed Endangered Species Listing for Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This letter from an Arizona State University zoologist to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, dated July 20, 1990, provides expert commentary on the proposed rule to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as endangered under the Endangered Species Act. While strongly supporting the listing, the author critiques the proposed rule's quality, noting errors in fact, misuse of literature, and omission of significant research. The letter raises critical concerns about geographic bias toward upper basin populations and lack of clarity regarding lower [[Colorado River Basin]] populations, hatchery-produced fish, and reintroduction programs throughout the [[Gila River Basin]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a big-river fish species native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The author has studied this species from 1973 to 1990, publishing 10 papers on its biology and participating in recovery efforts both as an academic researcher and through an Interagency Personnel Agreement Act appointment with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

The species has been subject to extensive conservation efforts including:
- Pioneering work at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]
- A 10-year reintroduction program (cooperative agreement between Arizona and the Service) resulting in stocking throughout much of the [[Gila River Basin]]
- Similar agreement between California and the Service on the lower [[Colorado River]]
- Use of backwaters, urban lakes and ponds, and other artificial habitats for rearing
- Ongoing genetic studies (mtDNA, allozymes, morphology) to determine relationships between upper and lower basin populations

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Razorback Sucker]] occurs in diverse habitats across the [[Colorado River Basin]]:
- Upper [[Colorado River Basin]] remnant stocks and habitats
- [[Lake Mohave]]
- Streams, lakes, and reservoirs throughout the [[Gila River Basin]]
- Aquaducts such as the [[Central Arizona Project Canal]]
- Both natural and artificial habitats including backwaters and urban water bodies

The species represents a "big-river fish" adapted to large riverine systems. Populations exist in both upper and lower basin waters, with genetic studies underway to assess relationships and innate variability between these populations. Genetic studies on the [[Lake Mohave]] population address possibilities of hybridization with other species.

## Key Findings

**Critique of Proposed Rule:**
- The proposed rule is "poorly written and of questionable content"
- Contains "cavalier use of both published and 'gray' literature, resulting in a number of errors in fact"
- Second- and third-hand reports recorded with no assessment of reliability
- Data freely interpreted, often differently than original authors intended
- Whole body of information ignored, including hatchery work, genetic studies, reintroduction programs, and ecological research

**Geographic Bias Concerns:**
- Proposed rule appears to emphasize upper [[Colorado River Basin]] almost exclusively
- Unclear intent regarding lower [[Colorado River Basin]] waters other than [[Lake Mohave]]
- Nothing specific stated about razorback suckers in [[Gila River Basin]] streams, lakes, and reservoirs
- Recovery implementation appears placed under the Colorado River Endangered Fishes Recovery Implementation Committee, which has no representation from lower [[Colorado River Basin]]
- Lower basin habitats historically excluded from committee consideration and funding recommendations

**Unresolved Management Questions:**
- How to separate hatchery-produced fish from those produced by natural reproduction?
- Are stocking sites to be excluded from protection under the [[Endangered Species Act]]?
- Which sites will be included/excluded and how will they be identified?
- What protection for populations in artificial habitats like the [[Central Arizona Project Canal]]?
- Why informal agreements mentioned only with Colorado and Utah, not Arizona, Nevada, and California?

**Recommendations:**
- Questions should be answered prior to final rule listing the species as endangered
- If not resolved before listing, must be considered before moving to recovery and management protocols
- Basin-wide unanimity should be achieved through internal agency review
- Lower basin research and recovery efforts must be properly cited and contextualized
- Long-standing investment and interest in lower [[Colorado River Basin]] must be recognized to avoid disruption
