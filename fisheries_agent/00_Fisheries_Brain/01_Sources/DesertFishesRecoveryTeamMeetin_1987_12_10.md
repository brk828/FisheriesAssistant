# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes (10–11 December 1987)

## Executive Summary
This document records the proceedings of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT) meeting held in Albuquerque, New Mexico, under Team Leader [[W. L. Minckley]]. The meeting addressed recovery planning, policy, and management status for multiple imperiled desert fish taxa within the [[Colorado River Basin]], [[Gila River Basin]], [[Rio Yaqui Basin]], and Mimbres closed basin. Major topics included substantive technical reviews of recovery plans for [[Meda Fulgida]] (spikedace) and [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (loach minnow), a working plan for [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] (desert pupfish), status updates on [[Yaqui Chub]] and [[Chihuahua Chub]] recovery, non-essential experimental population designations for [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail]] under the Endangered Species Act (ESA), and various habitat, genetics, and permitting issues affecting reintroduction programs across Arizona and New Mexico.

## Background & Description
The DFRT operates in an advisory capacity to the USFWS Region 2 Regional Director, coordinating recovery efforts for over 19 desert-adapted fish species and subspecies listed or proposed for listing under the ESA. Members present included representatives from the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Arizona State University (ASU), New Mexico Game and Fish Department (NMGFD), U.S. Forest Service (USFS), Comisión Estatal de Ecología Sonora (CES), and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), reflecting the multi-jurisdictional, binational scope of desert fish conservation spanning the U.S. Southwest and northern Mexico.

Species discussed span several families:
- **Cyprinodontidae**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), with disjunct populations in California (Salton Sea basin, Anza-Borrego), the Gila River basin (extirpated in the wild), Quitobaquito Spring (Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument), and Mexico (Santa Clara Slough).
- **Cyprinidae**: [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*).
- **Catostomidae**: [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and the candidate [[Zuni Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus yarrowi*).
- **Ictaluridae**: [[Yaqui Catfish]], recently acquired from Mexico and held at Dexter National Fish Hatchery for potential genetic and captive breeding evaluation.
- **Poeciliidae**: [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), discussed jointly with desert pupfish regarding reintroduction status on BLM lands.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Genetic conservation and gene flow**: A central ecological theme was the genetic integrity of reintroduced and translocated desert pupfish populations. Minckley proposed that historic Gila Basin populations likely experienced natural, discharge-driven gene flow among river-dwelling (not spring-isolate) populations, unlike pupfish in more xeric basins of Nevada and California. This supports an active management strategy of periodically mixing stocks among reintroduced, isolated populations to maintain panmixia, rather than preserving isolated pure lineages as pursued in California.
- **Introduced/translocated populations outside native range**: Desert pupfish populations established outside their native range (e.g., Research Ranch stock, Bill Williams River drainage) raised concerns about hybridization risk with Gila River basin reintroduction stock (Quitobaquito and Santa Clara Slough lineages). The DFRT reaffirmed opposition to out-of-range introductions, though it deemed the poorly-established Bill Williams River population a low priority for eradication given its apparent failure to persist.
- **Habitat degradation**: Riparian habitat along the Mimbres River (Chihuahua chub range) was reported as heavily over-grazed, with National Forest lands requiring an implementation plan and habitat inventory overlays (fish distribution, riparian condition, grazing pressure) to prioritize restoration and reintroduction sites.
- **Reservoir and flow regime effects**: The Gila River below San Carlos Reservoir was identified as unsuitable for establishing viable native fish populations under current reservoir operations, despite proposed use as an "experimental stream" for radiotelemetry studies.
- **Wastewater-subsidized perennial flow**: Effluent-dependent reaches such as the Gila River below Phoenix, and potentially the Hassayampa River below Wickenburg and Santa Cruz River below Tucson, were identified as candidate habitats for desert pupfish reintroduction due to anthropogenically maintained permanent flow.
- **Exotic species introductions**: Proposed stocking of triploid-certified grass carp (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) in McGaffey Lake (upper Little Colorado River drainage, NM) raised concern for the nearby candidate [[Zuni Bluehead Sucker]], compounded by scientific uncertainty regarding the reliability of triploidy-induced sterility.
- **Exotic fish impacts on natives**: Competition/predation from exotic fishes was cited as a major biological threat to Chihuahua chub recovery, alongside general scarcity of suitable habitat.

## Key Findings

### 1. Yaqui Fishes & Gila ditaenia Recovery Plans
- The multi-species Yaqui fishes recovery plan required better integration with the completed San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge (NWR) management plan due to inconsistent downlisting criteria between documents.
- A subgroup (Minckley, Hendrickson, Ben Robertson) was tasked with reconciling the plans, targeting completion by March 1988.
- The [[Gila Ditaenia]] (Yaqui chub) recovery plan was essentially complete pending the Regional Director's signature; USFS had not yet submitted comments despite the species occurring in the U.S. only on Coronado National Forest lands.

### 2. Desert Pupfish Out-of-Range Populations
- Long-standing (since 1984) unresolved recommendations regarding out-of-range desert pupfish populations remained unaddressed by the Regional Director.
- A proposed electrophoretic genetic study (Jeff Mitton, University of Colorado-Boulder) of Research Ranch, Quitobaquito, and Santa Clara Slough pupfish stocks was supported to clarify population relationships.
- DFRT reaffirmed need to remove Quitobaquito-derived pupfish from the Gila River basin to prevent genetic contamination of reintroduction stock.
- Bill Williams River drainage pupfish introductions (BLM lands) appeared to be failing naturally and were deemed a low priority for active removal.

### 3. Colorado Squawfish Experimental Population (Lower Colorado River)
- Proposed non-essential, experimental population status for [[Colorado Squawfish]] drew mixed responses; water users opposed due to concerns over water-use restrictions, while some scientists advocated for full "essential" protection.
- USBR recommended shifting the introduction reach downstream from Parker Dam to below Headgate Rock Dam.
- Fish dispersing from designated non-essential experimental areas would retain non-essential status under USFWS's interpretation of the amended ESA.
- Precedent cited: Verde/Salt River non-essential experimental squawfish stockings may legally extend into tributaries of the designated mainstem reach (Perkinsville–Horseshoe Lake; Hwy 40 bridge–Roosevelt Lake).

### 4. Bonytail Experimental Population & Radiotelemetry Proposal
- The non-essential experimental package for [[Bonytail]] was drafted but stalled pending resolution of preliminary review questions.
- ASU proposed a radiotelemetry study using surgically neutered (gonad-intact) bonytail, squawfish, and humpback chub in the Gila River below San Carlos Reservoir to gather movement/habitat preference data, motivated by the reach's unsuitability for establishing viable wild populations under current reservoir operations.
- USFWS clarified that neutering fish confers no regulatory advantage under the ESA — full protection or experimental/non-essential designation would still be required regardless of reproductive status.

### 5. Chihuahua Chub Recovery (Mimbres River, NM)
- Species is critically rare and cannot be studied in situ within the U.S.; cross-border research collaboration with Chihuahua, Mexico, was planned to study microhabitat requirements.
- USFS identified a potential rearing pond site (former river oxbow, Mimbres Ranger District) suitable for both Chihuahua chub and beautiful shiner, contingent on feasibility studies, water rights utilization, and 404 permitting given heavy bulldozer/flood disturbance history.
- Key threats: habitat scarcity, exotic fish competition, and private land ownership limiting habitat security; land acquisition (Archileta-Moreno properties) was recommended.
- DFRT supported drafting a formal letter (Propst, Whitney, Stefferud) to the Regional Director requesting Mexico research support, habitat acquisition, and habitat development funding.
- Existing recovery plan deemed too recent for formal revision but requires an implementation amendment.

### 6. Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan Framework (Agenda Item 8)
- Distinct regional strategies proposed:
  - **California**: Protect existing genetically pure, secure populations (e.g., Anza-Borrego State Park).
  - **Gila River Basin**: Since wild populations are extirpated, propose mixing Mexican and Salton Sea stocks to maximize heterozygosity for reintroduction, followed by periodic gene flow management among reintroduced populations.
  - **Quitobaquito Spring**: Considered relatively secure under NPS management, functioning as an informal "recovery plan" in itself.
  - **Mexico**: Encouraged as a key partner given it holds the largest, most viable wild populations.
- Historical context: fewer than 30 confirmed pupfish localities existed by the 1940s, with natural local extinction/recolonization dynamics under variable discharge conditions.
- Downlisting criteria were deemed premature to establish at this stage; a full draft plan was due from Burton by 15 January 1988.

### 7. Substantive Reviews: Spikedace & Loach Minnow Recovery Plans
- Full technical reviews of [[Meda Fulgida]] (spikedace) and [[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (loach minnow) recovery plans were conducted; detailed comments were to be forwarded directly to plan author Paul Marsh by 18 December 1987, with revisions largely paralleling between the two plans.

### 8. Additional Species & Administrative Actions
- **[[Lepidomeda Vittata]]** (Little Colorado spinedace): Now listed; a recovery plan draft was to be initiated by Gary Carmichael (USFWS Pinetop office), potentially jointly with Chuck Minckley.
- **East Fork Gila River land acquisition**: DFRT recommended supporting USFS land acquisition for spikedace, loach minnow, and associated species habitat.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]**: A recommendation was made for a genetic (electrophoretic) survey of recently acquired Mexican stock (housed at Dexter National Fish Hatchery) to verify purity prior to spring spawning.
- **Razorback sucker & bonytail/squawfish plans**: Status inquiries were pending from USFWS Region 6 (Denver).
- **Grass carp introduction proposal (McGaffey Lake, NM)**: Raised concern over impacts to the candidate Zuni bluehead sucker; Arizona was not consulted despite basin connectivity concerns, and clarification was requested from New Mexico Department of Game and Fish.
- **Topminnow/pupfish reintroductions on BLM lands**: Stalled due to administrative bottleneck at the BLM Arizona State Director level and an apparent oversight excluding pupfish from USFWS correspondence (addressed separately in the pending pupfish recovery plan).
- **Hassayampa Preserve cooperative agreement**: A three-party agreement (AGFD, USFWS, The Nature Conservancy) for using Palm Lake as a holding/grow-out area for Colorado squawfish, bonytail, and razorback sucker was endorsed with a letter of support.
- **Federal holding permits**: Ambiguity remained regarding whether loan permits were still required for private holders of listed species; Burton was tasked with clarifying policy.
