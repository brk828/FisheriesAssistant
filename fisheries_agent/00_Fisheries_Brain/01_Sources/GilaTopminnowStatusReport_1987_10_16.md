## Executive Summary

This document is an internal Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD) memorandum, dated October 16, 1987, transmitting the completed **1986–1987 Gila Topminnow status report**, authored by Lee Simons (Assistant Native Fish Biologist) under the supervision of Dean Hendrickson (Native Fish Program). The memo does not itself present raw biological, morphological, or population-count data; rather, it summarizes the administrative and monitoring context surrounding the [[Gila Topminnow]] conservation program in Arizona. Key content includes AZGFD's evaluation of a preliminary **downlisting proposal** submitted to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and an announcement of a shift in monitoring frequency for reintroduced populations. The memo also outlines the Department's plan to consolidate all historical and ongoing topminnow data (stocking records, monitoring results, site descriptions, and literature) into computerized databases for future distribution to interested parties.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The [[Gila Topminnow]] occupies both **natural** and **reintroduced (introduced)** populations across sites in Arizona, implicating a historically fragmented distribution consistent with its status as an endangered species restricted to isolated desert springs, cienegas, and small streams within the [[Gila River Basin]].
- Distinction is drawn between:
  - **Natural sites** — populations occurring in original, undisturbed habitat, monitored **at least twice annually**.
  - **Reintroduction sites** — populations established via active stocking efforts as part of species recovery, to be monitored **every other year** going forward (monitoring suspended in 1988).
- This differential monitoring intensity reflects a management prioritization strategy, likely driven by resource constraints and the relatively higher conservation value or fragility of the remaining natural populations, which are linked closely to habitat quality and connectivity within the broader [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]] conservation landscape.

## Key Findings

- A **final report** covering **1986 and 1987** monitoring efforts for both natural and introduced [[Gila Topminnow]] populations in Arizona has been completed by Lee Simons.
- AZGFD used data from this report to formally evaluate and comment on a **preliminary proposal to downlist** the Gila topminnow's federal conservation status, submitted to the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service** — indicating the species may have been under consideration for reduced protection status (e.g., from Endangered toward Threatened) at this time.
- The Department acknowledges potential **errors of omission** in documentation, particularly regarding early, poorly-documented introduction events, and solicits stakeholder review and correction.
- AZGFD is developing **four integrated computerized databases**, expected to be available by summer 1988:
  1. **Stocking database** — tracks all known introductions, enabling reconstruction of exact reintroduction phylogenies/histories.
  2. **Monitoring database** — tracks all field monitoring data over time.
  3. **Site description file** — descriptive, ownership, and contact information for each site, plus miscellaneous comments.
  4. **General literature database** — indexed by species, for broader research support.
- **Monitoring protocol change**: Reintroduction sites will be monitored only **biennially** (skipping 1988), while **natural populations continue to be monitored at least twice per year**, reflecting continued but resource-limited commitment to species recovery.
- Reintroduction (stocking) efforts are planned to continue, but implementation remains contingent on **bureaucratic and logistical constraints**.
- Status reports of this kind are intended to be issued biennially going forward, with the **next report anticipated in 1989**.
