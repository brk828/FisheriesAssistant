# Eagle Creek Fish Community Survey — Summer 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Southwestern-Native-Fishes

## Executive Summary
This memorandum, authored by Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) with C.O. Minckley (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) and Glen Knowles (ASU), documents a fish community assessment of [[Eagle Creek]], Graham and Greenlee Counties, Arizona, conducted the week of 05 July 1993. The primary goals were to (1) evaluate the status of the fish community following severe winter flooding, and (2) collect specimens of [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Sonora Sucker]] for genetic studies. Sampling occurred at five sites — Honeymoon Campground, above Sheep Wash, P-Bar Ranch, above the Phelps Dodge (PD) diversion structure, and below the PD pump station — using backpack electrofishing and seines. Of 20 historically known fish species in the drainage, 9 were captured. Native fishes (5 species) were abundant, including both adults and young-of-year, indicating successful recruitment despite habitat disturbance. Non-native fishes were notably rare (only 4 specimens across 3 species, excluding stocked [[Rainbow Trout]]), a pattern attributed to scouring effects of winter flooding. Target species [[Spikedace]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] were not collected, likely due to unsampled deepwater habitat above the PD diversion dam.

## Background & Description
The report is a community-level field survey rather than a morphometric study; no direct morphological measurements are provided. However, taxonomic identifications reference standard diagnostic distinctions among native Gila River basin fishes:

- **[[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)** — small-bodied [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] minnow, genetic study target.
- **[[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*)** — [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] with subterminal, cartilaginous-edged mouth typical of algae-grazing suckers.
- **[[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)** — larger-bodied catostomid, distinguished from Desert Sucker by mouth and lip morphology.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)** — deep-bodied native cyprinid, consistently captured across all five sites and both survey years (1987, 1993).
- **[[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)** — small, robust-bodied benthic cyprinid, ubiquitous across sites.
- **[[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)** — slender-bodied cyprinid with laterally compressed body; a habitat specialist of swift riffle habitats, not collected in 1993.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)** — distinguished by pronounced dorsal keel behind the head; part of an FWS reintroduction/stocking program in the drainage.

Non-native species noted include [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*, stocked), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*, juveniles only), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), and [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Eagle Creek is a Gila River basin desert stream subject to strong hydrologic disturbance regimes. Key habitat observations from this survey include:

- **Flood disturbance legacy**: Severe winter flooding degraded stream channels, caused bank erosion, and stripped riparian vegetation from much of the floodplain across all sample sites.
- **Riparian recovery**: Riparian vegetation was actively rejuvenating at the time of sampling; stable substrates supported luxuriant algal (green algae) and diatom growth, suggesting recovering primary productivity supporting the grazing sucker guild ([[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]]).
- **Anthropogenic structure damage**: The Phelps Dodge (PD) diversion dam was undermined and rendered non-functional by flooding; a replacement structure and rock-filled gabion channel stabilization were under construction at both the dam site and downstream pump station during the visit.
- **Deepwater refugium habitat**: The pool above the PD diversion dam, previously silted-in since 1983 due to lack of flushing flows, had scoured open and deepened substantially post-flood — flagged as a priority site for future entanglement-gear sampling, particularly for locating stocked [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Native fish resilience**: Native species (Longfin Dace, Roundtail Chub, Speckled Dace, Sonora Sucker, Desert Sucker) were abundant with evidence of successful reproduction (young-of-year present), indicating resilience to flood disturbance.
- **Non-native suppression**: Flood scouring is hypothesized to have substantially reduced non-native fish abundance, notably eliminating [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) from collections despite its historical presence, and leaving only trace occurrences of [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]], and juvenile [[Common Carp]].

## Key Findings
- **9 of 20** historically documented fish species were captured across five sites during the July 1993 survey.
- **5 native species** — Longfin Dace, Roundtail Chub, Speckled Dace, Sonora Sucker, Desert Sucker — were abundant at all or most sites, with both adult and young-of-year age classes present.
- **[[Spikedace]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]** were not captured; absence attributed to lack of sampling in deepwater habitat (the pool above the PD diversion dam).
- **Non-native fishes were rare**: only 4 specimens across 3 species (excluding stocked Rainbow Trout at Honeymoon Campground) — one Yellow Bullhead, one Channel Catfish, and two juvenile Common Carp.
- **Red Shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*)**, present in 1987 collections, was conspicuously absent in 1993 — interpreted as a flood-driven population crash.
- **Winter flooding** substantially altered physical habitat (channel degradation, bank erosion, riparian loss) but also appears to have benefited the native fish community by suppressing non-native competitors/predators.
- **Infrastructure impacts**: The PD diversion dam was destroyed by flooding and was under active reconstruction with gabion stabilization at the time of the survey.
- **Management recommendation**: The newly deepened pool above the PD diversion dam should be sampled with entanglement gear (e.g., gill nets) in a follow-up visit to assess presence of stocked Razorback Sucker.
- Voucher specimens and frozen genetic material from this survey are archived at [[Arizona State University]].
- Study builds on baseline data from [[Marsh et al. 1991]] (J. AZ-NV Acad. Sci. 23:107-116), enabling direct year-to-year (1987 vs. 1993) species occurrence comparison at each locality.
