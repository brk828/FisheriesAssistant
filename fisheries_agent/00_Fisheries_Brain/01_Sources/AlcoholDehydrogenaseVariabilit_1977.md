## Executive Summary
This study by Buth (1977) investigates the alcohol dehydrogenase (ADH) enzyme system in the [[Northern Hogsucker]] (*Hypentelium nigricans*) using starch gel electrophoresis. The research identified two distinct alleles (*Adh-A<sup>a</sup>* and *Adh-A<sup>b</sup>*) across 12 sampled localities spanning the Atlantic Slope, Ohio Basin, and Upper Mississippi Basin. The findings reveal a clinal pattern of geographic variability, with allele fixation occurring at the geographic extremes of the species' range, supporting existing zoogeographic hypotheses regarding the species' colonization history via stream capture and glacial deglaciation events.

## Background & Description
*Note: This study is biochemical/genetic in focus rather than morphological. Key structural findings pertain to the enzyme itself rather than the organism's gross anatomy.*

- **Enzyme System:** Alcohol Dehydrogenase (ADH) (E.C. 1.1.1.1)
- **Genetic Control:** The enzyme is controlled by a single gene locus.
- **Ploidy Note:** *H. nigricans* is a tetraploid species, but the ADH locus displays functional diploidization (behaving genetically as a single-locus diploid system despite the tetraploid genome).
- **Protein Structure:** The enzyme exhibits a **dimeric** structure, inferred from the presence of a heteropolymer band (a "hybrid" band of intermediate mobility) in heterozygous individuals.
- **Alleles Identified:**
  - *Adh-A<sup>a</sup>*
  - *Adh-A<sup>b</sup>*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution:** The [[Northern Hogsucker]] is one of the most widespread North American [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] fishes.
- **Range:** Spans from Minnesota to Ontario and New York, south along the Atlantic coast through the Carolinas, and throughout the Mississippi Valley to the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Habitat Type:** Inhabits streams and rivers across the [[Atlantic Slope]], [[Ohio Basin]], [[Upper Mississippi Basin]], and reportedly the [[Ozark Region]] and [[Great Lakes Basin]] (though the latter two were not sampled in this specific study).
- **Taxonomic Status:** No subspecies of *H. nigricans* are currently recognized, despite the wide geographic and genetic variability observed.

## Key Findings
1. **Clinal Variability:** The geographic distribution of the two ADH alleles supports a clinal pattern.
   - **Atlantic Slope (James/Roanoke/Santee drainages):** Fixation of the *Adh-A<sup>a</sup>* allele.
   - **Ohio Basin:** Both alleles present, with *Adh-A<sup>a</sup>* predominating (except in the eastern Appalachian region, which showed fixation of *Adh-A<sup>a</sup>*).
   - **Upper Mississippi Basin:** Fixation of the *Adh-A<sup>b</sup>* allele.
2. **Zoogeographic Correlation:** The allele distribution supports the hypothesis by [[R. E. Jenkins]] that the Ohio Basin represents the ancestral range of *H. nigricans*.
   - The Atlantic Slope populations likely originated via **stream capture** from the Ohio Basin, explaining the shared *Adh-A<sup>a</sup>* fixation with the eastern Ohio Basin populations.
   - The Upper Mississippi populations likely originated from western Ohio Basin stock following **deglaciation**.
3. **Founder Effect Hypothesis:** The fixation of the *Adh-A<sup>b</sup>* allele in the Upper Mississippi basin—despite the source population (Ohio Basin) containing both alleles—is hypothesized to be the result of a **founder effect** and/or **genetic drift** during the initial colonization following glacial retreat, rather than environmental selection (as no correlating environmental factors were identified).
4. **Unsampled Regions:** The study predicts that populations in the [[Ozark Region]] (unsampled) might show predominance or fixation of the *Adh-A<sup>b</sup>* allele, while the genetic makeup of [[Great Lakes Basin]] populations remains unknown and requires further study to determine if they were colonized by Ohio Basin or Upper Mississippi Basin stock.
5. **Methodology:** Findings were derived from liver tissue homogenates of 75 specimens, analyzed via vertical starch gel electrophoresis using a pH 8.6 boric acid–sodium hydroxide buffer system.
