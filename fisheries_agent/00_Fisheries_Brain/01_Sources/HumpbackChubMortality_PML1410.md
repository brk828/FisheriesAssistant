# Humpback Chub Mortality Examination, Grand Canyon (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #humpback-chub

## Executive Summary

This January 1991 memorandum from [[Frank Baucom]], a fish pathologist with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in Phoenix, AZ, reports a post-mortem examination of a dead [[Humpback Chub]] collected from the [[Grand Canyon]]. The fish carried an internal transmitter with a trailing antenna. The examiner found unhealed incisions, a dark enlarged gall bladder suggesting the fish had not been feeding, and a parasite-associated lesion. He attributed the death, speculatively, to improper healing of the sutures and excess handling. The memo recommends iodine treatment of incisions and antibiotic injections for future cases. The document does not give a scientific name, sample size, or quantitative data.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Interoffice correspondence, routed to the Albuquerque regional office (FR), the [[Fish Health Center]] in Pinetop, AZ (FAO), and Ronald D. Major.
- **Date of examination:** January 16 (year 1991, per the memo date of January 22, 1991).
- **Context:** The examiner viewed the specimen with several other fish pathologists at a local fish health meeting.
- **Specimen:** A single humpback chub, dead for approximately 48 hours before examination.
- **Scientific name:** Not stated in the document.
- **Equipment on fish:** An [[Internal Transmitter Tagging|internal transmitter]] and external tag with a trailing antenna.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses a single individual and does not describe population status, habitat conditions, water chemistry, or other ecosystem variables. Relevant context is limited to:

- The fish came from the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River, where the species is monitored.
- The examiner ruled out trauma from [[Electrofishing]] as a cause of injury.
- The presence of [[Lernaea Cyprinacea]] (anchor worm) indicates parasitic exposure, though the document does not assess its prevalence.

## Key Findings

**Gross pathology observed:**
- **Incision sites:** Erythema (redness) around the sutures and the antenna exit. Neither site had healed properly.
- **Gall bladder:** Enlarged and dark, which the examiner interpreted as evidence that the fish had not been feeding.
- **Dorsal region:** Focal inflammation near the dorsal fin, probably caused by *Lernaea cyprinacea*, plus abrasions on the dorsal area.
- **Electrofishing trauma:** None found.

**Cause of death (speculative):**
- The examiner stated it is difficult, if not impossible, to determine cause of death 48 hours post-mortem.
- He speculated that death resulted from improper healing of the sutures and excess handling during collection.

**Recommendations for future cases:**
- Treat surgical incisions with one of the iodine compounds.
- Give intraperitoneal injections of an antibiotic.

**Implications for tagging practice:** The memo implies that surgical implantation of transmitters, and the handling that accompanies it, may contribute to mortality if wounds do not heal. It does not quantify this risk or report any follow-up data.

**Limitations:** A single case, no sample size, no statistics, and no histology or laboratory culture results are reported. The cause of death remains unconfirmed.
