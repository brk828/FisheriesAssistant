## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 news release from the Southwest Center for Biological Diversity (SWCBD) and allied conservation groups announcing a lawsuit against [[Fort Huachuca]] for failing to comply with the National Environmental Policy Act and the Endangered Species Act (ESA) prior to a proposed military base expansion. The core scientific/ecological concern is that increased groundwater pumping and surface water diversion associated with the base expansion threaten to de-water the [[San Pedro River]], destroying critical recovery habitat for several endangered and threatened native fish species of the Gila River basin. While not a primary research paper, the release cites U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) biological opinions identifying the upper San Pedro River as one of the most promising recovery habitats for imperiled southwestern fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Upper [[San Pedro River]] basin (above Saint David, Arizona), part of the [[Gila River]] drainage system.
- **Riparian association**: The river corridor supports the most extensive surviving expanse of cottonwood/willow gallery (broadleaf riparian) forest in North America — described as one of the last relatively intact ecosystems on Earth.
- **Hydrological dependency**: Native Gila River fishes in this reach depend on perennial surface base flows sustained by groundwater contributions from the San Pedro Basin aquifer.
- **Threats to niche integrity**:
  - Groundwater pumping and surface water diversion in the Garden Canyon area (associated with [[Fort Huachuca]] and the city of Sierra Vista) is intercepting water that would otherwise sustain river base flows.
  - FWS projected that current water use rates would result in complete de-watering of the San Pedro River within approximately 20 years (from 1994), sooner if usage increases.
  - Co-occurring threatened taxon: the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (a riparian-obligate bird), underscoring the broader ecosystem-wide dependency on river hydrology.
- **Recovery habitat designation**: FWS Biological Opinions (1991 draft; 1994 final, document #2-21-90-F-119) identify the upper San Pedro River as among the most promising sites for reestablishment of native Gila River fishes within their historic range, a stated recovery strategy under the ESA.

## Key Findings

- The upper San Pedro River is legally and ecologically significant as designated recovery habitat for five imperiled Gila River basin fishes: [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- FWS (August 22, 1994 correspondence) formally warned that groundwater pumping and diversion linked to Ft. Huachuca's operations and expansion threaten to de-water the San Pedro River within roughly two decades absent intervention.
- The ESA's Section 2(b) purpose and Section 3(3) definition of "conserve" are invoked to argue that habitat degradation precluding species recovery — not just preventing extinction — constitutes a violation of the Act.
- The lawsuit alleges Ft. Huachuca Command violated ESA Section 7(a)(2)-(4) consultation requirements by proceeding with expansion plans without adequately assessing impacts on endangered/threatened species and their habitat.
- The San Pedro riparian corridor is framed as an ecosystem-level conservation priority, not solely a single-species issue, linking fish recovery potential to preservation of the broader [[cottonwood-willow riparian forest]] ecosystem and other listed species like the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher.
- No new empirical field data (population counts, water chemistry, flow measurements) are presented in this source; all quantitative/predictive claims (e.g., the ~20-year de-watering timeline) are attributed to prior FWS assessments rather than original findings in this release.
