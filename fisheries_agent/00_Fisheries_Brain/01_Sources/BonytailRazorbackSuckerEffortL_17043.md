## Executive Summary

This 1990 field report summarizes the third annual collaborative electrofishing and trammel-netting effort on [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada) targeting two Colorado River Basin native fishes of conservation concern: the endangered [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). No wild Bonytail were captured, though four hatchery-reared, fin-clipped survivors from a 1990 stocking event were recovered. A total of 1,529 Razorback Suckers were collected (1,370 newly tagged), yielding a preliminary population estimate of ~66,509 individuals (95% CI: 54,700–82,200), with catch-per-effort statistics indicating no measurable change in population status relative to prior years. [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*) abundance in samples was higher than in any previous survey year, spanning yearling to large adult size classes, raising ecological concern regarding predation pressure on native fishes.

## Background & Description

- **Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)**: Hatchery-origin recaptured individuals ranged 25.0–34.2 cm total length (TL); identified via left pectoral fin clip as part of a cohort of 1,142 fish produced at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], reared in Niland, California, and stocked at Katharine Landing on 24 February 1990.
- **Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**: Characterized by the distinctive dorsal keel typical of the species (not quantified numerically in this report); size/age data collected via tagging program but not detailed in text excerpt.
- **Striped Bass (*Morone saxatilis*)**: Size range spanned yearling to large adult; scale samples collected for age determination.
- No morphometric data provided for incidental catch species (carp, channel catfish, largemouth bass, bluegill, green sunfish, rainbow trout) beyond counts and catch rates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: [[Lake Mohave]] is an impounded reach of the lower [[Colorado River]], sampled across four camp/zone areas: Arizona Bay, Carp Cove, Three/Six Mile Coves, and Arrowhead Cove.
- **Native species status**: Both [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are endemic Colorado River Basin fishes under active recovery management, subject to hatchery supplementation (Bonytail) and wild population monitoring/tagging (Razorback Sucker).
- **Non-native community**: The lake supports an extensive introduced fish assemblage — [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Rainbow Trout]] — captured alongside natives in trammel nets, reflecting a mixed native-invasive fish community typical of altered Colorado River reservoirs.
- **Predation/competition concern**: The marked increase in Striped Bass abundance is ecologically significant given known predatory pressure of this species on smaller native fishes, including juvenile Razorback Sucker and Bonytail.
- **Sampling gear-specific catch composition**: Electrofishing effort (Table 1) focused on Razorback Sucker detection across three camps; trammel netting (Table 2) captured the full mixed-species assemblage. Supplementary USFWS gill netting upstream of Arizona Bay captured additional Striped Bass (13), Channel Catfish (2), and Carp (2), not included in the main totals.

## Key Findings

### Bonytail
- **0 wild individuals** captured.
- **4 hatchery-origin individuals** recovered (survivors of 1,142 stocked): TL = 25.0, 31.1, 34.2 cm, and one unspecified length captured in Cottonwood Cove.
- Confirms measurable short-term post-stocking survival at Lake Mohave.

### Razorback Sucker
- **Total captured: 1,529** (combined electrofishing + trammel netting).
- **1,370 fish tagged for the first time** in this effort.
- **Cumulative tagged total since 1980: ~8,087** (liberal estimate of tagged fish currently at large).
- **Recaptures**: 49 long-term (>1 year) + 107 short-term (7-day) recaptures.
- **Preliminary population estimate**: ~66,509 individuals (95% CI: 54,700–82,200).
- CPUE and abundance estimate show **no measurable change** in population status versus previous years.
- Electrofishing catch by camp (Table 1): Camp 2 = 726 fish (2.19 fish/min); Camp 4 = 462 fish (1.26 fish/min); Camp 5 = 19 fish (0.08 fish/min); **Total = 1,207 fish**, overall 3.13 fish/min.
- Trammel net catch (Table 2, all camps combined): **305 individuals (21.9% of total catch)**, CPE = 3.4 fish/100m²/24h.

### Striped Bass
- **Highest abundance recorded in any survey year to date** — individuals ranging from yearling to large adult.
- Trammel net catch: 111 individuals (8.0% of total catch), CPE = 1.3 fish/100m²/24h.
- Supplementary USFWS gillnetting (upstream of Arizona Bay): +13 individuals (not in main total).
- Recommendation: vital statistics and scale samples to be forwarded to Tom Liles, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Kingman, for further age/growth analysis.

### Management Implications
- Continued annual monitoring recommended by authors as essential for tracking [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] recovery status in [[Lake Mohave]].
- Rising Striped Bass numbers flagged as a trend warranting closer ecological scrutiny given potential predatory impact on recovering native fish populations.
- Tagging dataset still being refined; authors note a more precise population estimate for Razorback Sucker was in preparation at time of this report.
