# Bull Minnows of Maryland: An Assemblage of Eight Shallow-Water Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This educational article, part of the Education Series No. 80 reprinted from the *Maryland Conservationist* (Vol. XLIV No. 3:2-5), was written by [[Frank J. Schwartz]], Curator of Fishes at the [[Natural Resources Institute]], [[Chesapeake Biological Laboratory]], University of Maryland. It explains that the local term "bull minnow" does not refer to a single species but to an assemblage of eight small, stout fishes inhabiting shallow brackish waters around the [[Chesapeake Bay]]. Seven belong to the family Cyprinodontidae (killifishes), while the [[Mosquitofish]] belongs to Poeciliidae, the live-bearers. The article describes each species' size, coloration, reproduction, diet, distribution and value to fisheries.

## Background & Description

- **Misnomer:** Bull minnows are not true minnows (Cyprinidae). Unlike minnows, which have throat teeth, these fishes have jaw teeth and often a protruding jaw and bright colors.
- **The eight species:** [[Mosquitofish]], [[Variegated Minnow]], [[Rainwater Killifish]], [[Spotfin Killifish]], [[Striped Killifish]], [[Mummichog]], [[Marsh Killifish]] and [[Banded Killifish]].
- **Family assignment:** Seven species are Cyprinodontidae; the mosquitofish is Poeciliidae.
- **Mosquitofish:** Single-finned, soft-rayed live-bearers, 0.75 to just under 2 inches long. Flattened head, underslung lower jaw and upward-opening mouth suit surface feeding on mosquito larvae. Males have a prong-shaped anal fin used as an intromittant organ. Smaller females produce about 25 young; larger ones more than 200. Broods appear from May until September, and young can breed in their birth summer.
- **Killifishes in general:** Share a similar body plan. Males are usually striped or spotted; larger females are generally drab tan or vertically barred. Some reach 8 inches.

Species-level sizes given in the article: variegated minnow up to 3 inches; rainwater killifish 0.5 to 1 5/16 inches; spotfin killifish about 2 1/8 inches; marsh killifish 2 3/8 inches; striped killifish up to 8 inches; mummichog about 6 inches; banded killifish up to 5 inches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** Mosquitofish occur occasionally in Chesapeake Bay and tributaries south of Annapolis, in saltwater ponds near the Bay and near Chincoteague Bay. The article notes the variegated minnow occurs throughout the Bay, its tributaries, isolated potholes or pools and coastal bays.
- **Habitat tolerance:** Rainwater killifish are found in brackish or very salty water. Marsh killifish, considered freshwater, tolerate moderate salinities and inhabit nearly full-seawater areas along the Gulf Coast. Banded killifish ascend rivers such as the Potomac, Patuxent, Susquehanna, Chester and Choptank into fresh water. Striped killifish occur in brackish and highly saline waters.
- **Trophic role:** Mosquitofish control mosquito and insect larvae near the surface. Variegated minnows are mainly vegetarian, feeding on the bottom or on vegetation. Mummichog and banded killifish eat crustaceans, worms and other bottom organisms, and vegetation. Many of these species serve as forage for larger brackish, oceanic and sport fishes.
- **Economic value:** Mummichog and banded killifish are important bait for flounder, yellow perch, pickerel and other species. Bait dealers depend on them. Variegated and rainwater killifish have little economic value.
- **Conservation status:** Spotfin killifish is now rarely found in Maryland; marsh killifish is known from only three Maryland sites (Tonytank Pond on the Eastern Shore, the Potomac River near the Morgantown 301 Bridge, and the Susquehanna River near Havre de Grace).

## Key Findings

- The "bull minnow" label covers eight fishes, which the author argues form a fascinating part of Maryland's fish fauna.
- Seven species are killifishes (Cyprinodontidae); the mosquitofish is a live-bearing Poeciliid.
- Reproduction timing: most killifishes spawn April to August; the mummichog spawns several times from April to August, with a female producing a little over 450 adhesive eggs that hatch in 9 to 18 days depending on temperature.
- Variegated minnow males defend small territories and have iridescent peacock-blue coloration during spawning. Incubation takes about a week.
- Striped killifish is the third most abundant killifish in Maryland, forming huge schools in brackish and saline waters. Its young become sexually distinguishable at 1.5 inches.
- Banded killifish is the most abundant and widespread killifish in fresher Maryland waters, with 16 to 22 silver-blue bands on both sexes, and spawns from early spring to late summer.
- Rainwater killifish rank fourth in abundance; spotfin killifish is the rarest in the group.
- Rare species such as the spotfin and marsh killifish face decline, with few Maryland records.
- The article's key message is that these small fishes support sport fisheries, bait commerce and food webs, despite their modest size and lack of prominence.
