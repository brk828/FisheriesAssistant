## Executive Summary

This document is a **taxonomic checklist** rather than a single-species study, cataloging the freshwater fish fauna of the **Pecos River** (Texas/New Mexico) and providing a **family-level sequence** for Mexican freshwater fishes. The Pecos River list comprises **53 species** across 15 families, with the notable inclusion of a newly described species, *[[Ictalurus Chihuahua]]* (n. sp.), and several endemic, endangered, and extinct taxa restricted to the Pecos drainage. The checklist annotates conservation status (Endangered, Extinct, Introduced) and notes species with restricted distributions (e.g., "upper Pecos" or "lower Pecos" only). The Mexican freshwater fish family sequence lists 47 families following the classification systems of Robins et al. (1980) and Greenwood et al. (1966), reflecting a much broader taxonomic and biogeographic scope encompassing marine-derived and estuarine-tolerant families (e.g., [[Carcharhinidae]], [[Ariidae]], [[Mugilidae]]) alongside strict freshwater groups.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological descriptions are provided in this checklist-format source. However, taxonomic distinctions implied by the list include:

- **[[Ictalurus Chihuahua]]** — designated as a new species (*n. sp.*) within [[Ictaluridae]], distinguishing it from congeners *Ictalurus furcatus*, *Ictalurus lupus*, and *Ictalurus punctatus*.
- **[[Notropis Simus Pecosensis]]** — a subspecies-level designation, indicating localized morphological/genetic divergence from nominal *Notropis simus*, and flagged as Endangered (E).
- Genus-level diversity within **[[Cyprinidae]]** (18 taxa) and **[[Notropis]]** (7 species/subspecies) suggests significant morphological radiation typical of North American minnow assemblages.
- Presence of *[[Cyprinodon Bovinus]]*, *[[Cyprinodon Elegans]]*, and *[[Cyprinodon Pecosensis]]* — three congeneric pupfish species co-occurring in the same drainage, all marked Endangered, implying fine-scale ecological/morphological specialization (typical of desert spring-endemic Cyprinodontidae).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The Pecos River system supports a mix of large-river generalists (e.g., *[[Ictalurus Punctatus]]*, *[[Aplodinotus Grunniens]]*), headwater/upper-reach specialists (e.g., *[[Catostomus Commersoni]]*, *[[Semotilus Atromaculatus]]*, *[[Campostoma Anomalum]]* — all noted "upper Pecos" only), and lower-river/warmwater taxa (*[[Ictiobus Bubalus]]*, *[[Ictiobus Niger]]* — "lower Pecos" only).
- **Endemism and Endangerment**: Several taxa are narrow endemics restricted to spring/river habitats of the Pecos drainage, including the pupfishes (*Cyprinodon* spp.), *[[Gambusia Nobilis]]*, and *[[Notropis Simus Pecosensis]]* — reflecting the isolated, spring-fed nature of Chihuahuan Desert aquatic refugia.
- **Introduced Species**: *[[Morone Chrysops]]* (white bass) and possibly *[[Gambusia Geiseri]]* represent non-native introductions altering the native community structure.
- **Extinction**: *[[Notropis Amabilis]]* is marked as Extinct (EX) within this checklist context, indicating a documented loss of a native cyprinid from the Pecos system.
- **Trophic/Functional Diversity**: The fauna spans piscivores (*[[Micropterus Salmoides]]*, historically recorded from Delaware Creek in 1851), benthic invertivores/detritivores (Catostomidae — suckers, buffalofish), and small-bodied insectivorous cyprinids and cyprinodontids.
- **Mexican Freshwater Context**: The broader family sequence reflects a transitional freshwater-marine ichthyofauna typical of Mexican coastal drainages, including euryhaline/marine-derived families such as [[Carcharhinidae]] (requiem sharks), [[Ariidae]] (sea catfishes), [[Belonidae]] (needlefishes), and [[Gerreidae]] (mojarras) alongside true freshwater specialists like [[Goodeidae]] and [[Rivulidae]].

## Key Findings

- **53 total fish species** documented for the Pecos River, with *Morone chrysops* noted as an introduced addition to the native fauna count.
- **New species description**: *[[Ictalurus Chihuahua]]* n. sp. is introduced in this checklist within [[Ictaluridae]].
- **Conservation status flags** applied across the list:
  - **Endangered (E)**: *[[Notropis Simus Pecosensis]]*, *[[Cyprinodon Bovinus]]*, *[[Cyprinodon Elegans]]*, *[[Cyprinodon Pecosensis]]*, *[[Gambusia Nobilis]]*
  - **Extinct (EX)**: *[[Notropis Amabilis]]*
  - **Introduced (I)**: *[[Gambusia Geiseri]]* (possibly introduced), *[[Morone Chrysops]]*
- **Restricted distribution notes**: Several species are confined to either "upper Pecos" (*Campostoma anomalum*, *Catostomus commersoni*, *Semotilus atromaculatus*) or "lower Pecos" (*Ictiobus bubalus*, *Ictiobus niger*) reaches, highlighting longitudinal zonation within the river.
- **Historical record**: *[[Micropterus Salmoides]]* documented at Delaware Creek by Captain Pope in 1851, providing a rare historical baseline data point.
- **Cichlid presence**: *[[Cichlasoma Cyanoguttatum]]* is noted as occurring in the Texas portion only, suggesting a Rio Grande/Texas biogeographic influence rather than true Pecos endemism.
- **Mexican fauna classification**: 47 families are enumerated in phylogenetic/systematic sequence following **Robins et al. (1980)** and **Greenwood et al. (1966)**, serving as a taxonomic backbone for subsequent Mexican freshwater fish inventories.
- The juxtaposition of the Pecos checklist with the Mexican family sequence suggests this document served as a **regional biodiversity baseline** for comparative freshwater ichthyofaunal studies between the southwestern United States and Mexico.
