# Lime Creek Fish Barrier EA Comments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a public comment letter from [[Jerome A. Stefferud]] and [[Sally E. Stefferud]], dated April 19, 2010, responding to the draft [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA) for a [[Nonnative Fish Barrier]] on [[Lime Creek]] in the [[Tonto National Forest]]. The barrier would block upstream fish movement. The letter supports the project and urges the [[USDA Forest Service]] to also restore additional native fishes, including the Gila chub, desert pupfish and desert sucker where habitat exists. It is accompanied by an email thread with field observations from Jerome Stefferud's 1998 survey and comments from other biologists on the stream's suitability for native fish.

## Background & Description

The letter is addressed to Todd Willard, Project Leader for the Tonto National Forest. The authors note that the need for the barrier has been discussed for many years. They state that the draft EA identifies the barrier as an important conservation action for two native species:

- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)

The authors also recommend considering restoration of other native species that may be endangered or declining, including:

- [[Gila Chub]], described as possibly endangered
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarkii*; the scientific name is partly illegible in the scan and is inferred here)

The letter states that native fishes of the [[Gila River Basin]] have declined greatly and that protecting the remaining waters is critical to the survival of the native fish community.

The attached email thread, dated April 6 to 10, 2010, includes:

- Jerome Stefferud's description of a field visit to Lime Creek in December 1998, covering the reach from Lime Cabin Spring downstream 1.3 miles to a trail crossing.
- A comment from [[Rob Clarkson]] on the stream's flow regime and habitat.
- A request from [[Paul Marsh]] for information on the potential suitability of Lime Creek for native fishes, especially chubs.

Comments were due to the Forest Service by April 20, 2010.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stream and spring conditions (1998 survey, Stefferud):**
- Stream gradient was 2%.
- The stream was a B channel with cobble and boulder substrate.
- Past heavy grazing had widened the channel, removed the overstory and shrub cover, and left benches with no low-water channels or floodplain access.
- Surface water flowed in the channel and was continuous to the end of the survey site.
- Discharge from Lime Cabin Spring was less than 0.2 cfs.
- Pools were mostly scour pools.
- A holding pasture just south of Lime Cabin Spring had dense herbaceous riparian vegetation, with willow and cottonwood overstory. Mesquite occupied the benches.
- A 12-foot vertical barrier blocks upstream fish movement. Water from the spring barely reached Lime Creek.
- The pasture area was unfenced and showed heavy use on riparian vegetation.

**Flow and habitat (Clarkson):**
- Much of the lower stream was ephemeral, though some perennial pools were seen near the barrier site.
- Most of the stream lies above the barrier site, and the commenter had not seen habitats there.

**Fish records:**
- Gila topminnow were stocked into Lime Cabin Spring in 1992.
- A 1983 survey reported no topminnow at the spring.
- A 1996 fishery survey of Lime Creek from near Horseshoe Reservoir to Lime Cabin Spring reported the target fish downstream of the spring but none in the spring stream. The scan is partly illegible at this point.
- Longfin dace are present throughout Lime Creek, according to Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) surveys.

**Potential habitat for other natives:**
- Lime Creek may provide habitat for desert pupfish. It lies within the species' elevational range, and the associated floodplain could be used by pupfish (D. Weedman, AGFD, personal communication).
- A 2003 survey of a different reach was judged unsuitable for Gila chub. Surface water continued downstream about 5 miles. Stefferud recommended restoring native fishes, including chubs, and augmenting topminnow populations.

## Key Findings

1. **The barrier supports native fish conservation.** The authors endorse the EA's proposed barrier as protection for Gila topminnow and longfin dace.
2. **Broader restoration is recommended.** Gila chub, desert pupfish and desert sucker should be considered where suitable habitat exists.
3. **Habitat has been degraded by grazing.** Heavy grazing has widened the channel and reduced riparian vegetation.
4. **Spring discharge is very low.** Lime Cabin Spring discharges less than 0.2 cfs, and its water barely reaches Lime Creek.
5. **Topminnow status is mixed.** Topminnow were stocked in 1992 and absent in 1983. The 1996 survey results are partly illegible.
6. **Longfin dace are widespread.** They occur throughout Lime Creek.
7. **Suitability remains uncertain.** Clarkson's observations, the 2003 survey and the Stefferud assessment give differing views on habitat suitability, and most of the stream lies above the barrier site.
8. **Recommended actions** include augmenting Gila topminnow, restoring native fish (including chubs) in suitable reaches, and managing livestock use of the riparian zone.
