# Survival of Some Fishes Recently Introduced into the Salton Sea, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Sciaenidae #Gobiidae #salton-sea

## Executive Summary

This 1953 California Fish and Game report documents the early results of a fish introduction program into the [[Salton Sea]], an inland, saline, terminal lake in Riverside and Imperial Counties, California. Beginning in 1948, the California Department of Fish and Game conducted four expeditions to San Felipe, Mexico (western Gulf of California), transplanting roughly 10,000 marine game and forage fishes into the Salton Sea to test the feasibility of establishing a marine sport fishery. By 1952, recoveries confirmed survival and successful natural reproduction of two key introduced species — the [[Orangemouth Corvina]] and the [[Bairdiella]] — demonstrating that Gulf of California marine fishes could adapt to and reproduce within this artificial saline environment. The [[Longjaw Mudsucker]] was also found to have become well established, likely via accidental escape from bait-rearing operations rather than deliberate introduction.

## Background & Description

- **Orangemouth Corvina (*Cynoscion xanthulus*)**: Recovered specimen measured 22 inches (~56 cm), estimated live weight of 4–6 lbs at capture.
- **Bairdiella / Croaker (*Bairdiella icistius*)**:
  - Single stranded juvenile: 14 inches long.
  - Dip-netted juveniles (Aug 29, 1952): 2.7–4.0 inches long (11 individuals).
  - Seine/trawl-caught juveniles (Sept 4–5, 1952): 2.5–6.5 inches long, with densities up to 125 individuals per 100-foot bag seine haul at one east-central shore location.
- **Longjaw Mudsucker (*Gillichthys mirabilis*)**: All size ranges (juvenile through adult) documented around the periphery of the sea by summer 1951, indicating an established, multi-cohort population.
- Native/pre-existing species (striped mullet, mosquitofish, desert pupfish, occasional machete) were present at smaller, baseline population levels prior to the introduction program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The [[Salton Sea]] is California's largest inland water body — approximately 13 miles wide by 32 miles long (~275 sq mi), averaging <15 ft depth with isolated depressions to 60 ft, sitting at −237 ft below sea level. It is a closed-basin (no outlet), high-evaporation (78 in/year) system maintained near sea-water salinity, fed primarily by agricultural return flows via the [[Alamo River]] and [[New River]].
- **Pre-1948 fish community**: Striped mullet, mosquitofish, desert pupfish, and occasional machete (ten-pounder) constituted the native/established marine-tolerant fauna; freshwater species such as carp were confined to river-mouth areas of lower salinity.
- **Introduction pathway**: Marine game and forage fishes were sourced from San Felipe, Mexico, on the Gulf of California and transported for experimental stocking, testing whether Gulf of California marine species could colonize a hypersaline, landlocked analog habitat.
- **Trophic considerations**: Successful establishment of predatory species like [[Orangemouth Corvina]] and [[Bairdiella]] raises concerns about concentrated predation pressure on existing food resources — desert pupfish, mosquitofish, marine worms, and plankton — with the report noting future fishery success depends on food supply sustainability or introduction of new prey organisms.
- **Accidental introduction**: The Longjaw Mudsucker's establishment is hypothesized to stem from bait-rearing containers used by mullet fishermen corroding and releasing captive stock into marginal marsh areas of the sea, rather than a planned Department introduction.

## Key Findings

- Between 1948 and 1952, four expeditions to San Felipe, Mexico, delivered approximately **10,000 marine fish** of multiple species to the Salton Sea as experimental plants.
- **January 17, 1952**: A 22-inch, 4–6 lb [[Orangemouth Corvina]] was captured in a gill net near Mullet Island, confirming survival of adults from the original May 1950 or March 1951 plantings (roughly 1.5–2 years post-introduction).
- **July 11, 1952**: A 14-inch juvenile [[Bairdiella]] was found stranded on the east-central shore, suggesting successful spawning from the 1950/1951 adult plants.
- **August 29, 1952**: 11 juvenile Bairdiella (2.7–4.0 in) were collected at night via dip net at the U.S. Salton Sea Base dock, further corroborating natural reproduction.
- **September 4–5, 1952**: Systematic trawling and beach seining across representative sea sections yielded abundant young Bairdiella (2.5–6.5 in), with a peak density of 125 fish per 100-foot seine haul on the east-central shore — strong evidence that the original 1950 (57 adults) and 1951 (~24 adults) Bairdiella plants successfully reproduced in the wild.
- The [[Longjaw Mudsucker]] was independently confirmed established (all size classes present) by summer 1951 seining surveys, likely via accidental escape rather than formal introduction.
- Collectively, these recoveries provided the first solid evidence that transplanted Gulf of California marine fishes ([[Orangemouth Corvina]] and [[Bairdiella]]) could not only survive but **successfully reproduce** in the Salton Sea, supporting optimism for developing a future marine sport fishery — contingent on adequate forage/food-base availability under new predation pressures.
