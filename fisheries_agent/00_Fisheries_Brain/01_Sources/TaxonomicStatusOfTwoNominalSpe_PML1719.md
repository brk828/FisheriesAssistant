# Taxonomic Status of Two Nominal Species of Mexican Freshwater Fishes (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This short taxonomic note, published in *Copeia* (1991, No. 1, pp. 225-227), formally synonymizes two nominal cyprinid species from northern and central Mexico. The first is [[Hybognathus Punctifer]], which is placed as a synonym of [[Dionda Melanops]]. The second is [[Notropis Celayensis]], which is placed as a synonym of [[Yuriria Alta]]. The author bases both decisions on examination of type material, museum catalogue records, and comparative specimens. The note also documents museum-register confusion over the type locality of Garman's fish and summarizes the historic fish fauna of the [[Parras Valley]].

## Background & Description

**Scope.** The paper is a "Shorter Contribution" in ichthyology, written by a museum ichthyologist at the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology and accepted 13 April 1990. It addresses nomenclature and type-locality questions rather than ecology or population data.

**Taxon 1: *Hybognathus (Dionda) punctifer*.** [[Garman]] described this species in 1881 from five specimens. Four are catalogued at the [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]] (MCZ 24897), and one is at the University of Michigan (UMMZ 87096). The original locality label reads "Parras, and spring near Saltillo." Parras lies in the interior [[Nazas-Aguanaval Basin]], while Saltillo lies in the [[Rio Grande Basin]].

**Taxon 2: *Notropis celayensis*.** [[Alvarez]] described this species in 1958 from springs at Los Positos, northwest of Celaya, Guanajuato. The site is adjacent to a major tributary of the [[Rio Lerma Basin]]. Gilbert (1978) had already listed it as a junior synonym of *Hudsonius altus* (*Yuriria alta*).

**Morphological notes.** Six paratopotypes of *N. celayensis* (UMMZ 172461; 51-77 mm SL) have a small terminal maxillary barbel that the original description omitted. The dental formula (0,4-4,0 in one specimen) and pharyngeal arch shape match *Y. alta*. The dorsal and anal fin rays number eight, not nine as Alvarez reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Garman's type locality problem.** The MCZ catalogue register contains an apparent error. Entries for MCZ 24897 and 24898 appear to have been made after 1928, when the syntypes were still uncatalogued. Hubbs found only the five syntypes in 1928, and the second entry (MCZ 24898, two specimens) could not be located.
- **Dionda distribution.** No *Dionda* is recorded from the [[Nazas-Aguanaval Basin]]. Collecting at more than 50 stations over 85 years failed to yield the genus there. The author concludes that the type material came from Saltillo, where *Dionda* occurs in a spring.
- **Historic Parras Valley fauna.** The valley had a flood connection to the [[Laguna de Mayran]] and the historic terminus of the Rio Nazas, now the desiccated [[Bolson de Mapimi]]. The fauna showed high endemism (50%). It included:
  - [[Stypodon Signifer]] (monotypic cyprinid, now extinct)
  - an endemic form of [[Gila]]
  - [[Cyprinodon Latifasciatus]] (Parras pupfish, now extinct)
  - [[Cyprinella Garmani]] (widespread in the Nazas basin, unknown elsewhere)
  - [[Characodon Garmani]] (Parras Characodon, now extinct)
  - [[Astyanax]] (ubiquitous)
  
  Of these, only *Gila* and exotic species remain in the valley.
- **Buena Vista.** The type locality of *D. melanops* is Buena Vista, Coahuila, now La Joya del Refugio, about 8 km southeast of Saltillo. It is a historically significant site because of the 1847 Battle of Buena Vista. Only Astyanax from the Parras fauna is known from the Rio Grande tributary that drained the Saltillo spring.
- **Regional context.** The paper's broader setting is the [[Chihuahuan Desert]] fish fauna, which includes many endangered and vanished species. The author cites a regional review of vanishing desert fishes (Williams et al., 1985) and an extinction review (Miller et al., 1989).

## Key Findings

1. **Synonymy of *Hybognathus punctifer*.** Garman's name is a junior synonym of Girard's earlier *Dionda melanops*. Under a trinomial treatment, the name becomes *Dionda episcopa punctifer*, a synonym of *D. e. melanops*. The author notes that *D. melanops* may be elevated to full species status after a pending revision of the *D. episcopa* complex.
2. **Evidence for the Saltillo origin.** Topotypes (UMMZ 213903; about 100 young to adult specimens, collected 14 May 1953 by C. L. and L. C. Hubbs at Buena Vista) agree with the five *punctifer* syntypes and the Harvard syntype of *D. melanops* (MCZ 1809). Body shape, proportions, fin-ray counts, scale numbers, and small scattered melanophores all match, and the melanophores explain the name *punctifer*.
3. **Synonymy of *Notropis celayensis*.** *N. celayensis* is a junior synonym of *Yuriria alta*. The paratopotypes differ from *Y. alta* only in slenderer body form, which the author attributes to allometric growth. The taxon is common in streams and lakes of the Rio Lerma basin and the lower Rio Grande de Santiago.
4. **Correction to the original description.** The maxillary barbel and the eight-ray fin count were both missed or misreported in the original *N. celayensis* description.
5. **Register error.** The MCZ register contains a locality and specimen-count confusion for Garman's material that should be treated with caution in future work.
6. **Historical biogeography.** The Parras Valley fauna (including now-extinct *Stypodon*, *Cyprinodon latifasciatus*, and *Characodon garmani*) illustrates high local endemism and the extinction losses of northern Mexican desert springs.
