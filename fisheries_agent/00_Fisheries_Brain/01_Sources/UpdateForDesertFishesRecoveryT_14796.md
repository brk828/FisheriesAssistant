# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Program Update

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This program update from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Arizona Ecological Services office, addressed to the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], reports on candidate and listed fishes, listing and critical habitat actions, [[Recovery Plan]] progress, recovery actions, [[Freedom of Information Act]] requests, [[Habitat Conservation Planning]] efforts, and [[Section 7 Consultation]] cases. The document is dated January 15, 1992, but its content refers to events and deadlines in 1993, so the date appears to be a typographical error. Its purpose is to save meeting time by covering items in writing. The author also states the Team's broader purpose: to act as an advisory group to the Regional Director on the protection and recovery of candidate and listed fishes in Arizona and adjacent areas.

## Background & Description

The Team's stated role spans two groups of species.

- **Non-listed Arizona fishes:** advice on candidate status, survey priorities and methods, available information, status reports, and listing or reclassification decisions.
- **Listed fishes:** advice on [[Critical Habitat]] boundaries, delisting and downlisting criteria, recovery actions, taxonomic questions, and [[Section 7 Consultation]] information.

The species discussed include [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), [[Virgin Spinedace]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*), [[Virgin Chub]], [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), [[Apache Trout]], and [[Gila Trout]]. Several non-listed fishes, including *Gila ditaenia* and *Gila purpurea*, are also mentioned.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most of the species are restricted to small springs, creeks, and river reaches in the Gila, Verde, Virgin, and Colorado basins. The document describes many threats:

- **Water development and diversion:** a 5 cfs additional diversion on the North Fork White River (about 28 cfs already diverted); [[Central Arizona Project]] water exchanges and potential nonnative fish spread; and reservoir proposals such as Pigeon Creek Reservoir and Blue Ridge Reservoir.
- **Grazing and riparian degradation:** allotment management plans on the Diamond Bar (144,507 acres), West Bear/Del Rio, and Dos S allotments.
- **Recreation and trails:** the proposed [[Arizona Trail]] through [[Redrock Canyon]].
- **Flood damage and bank armoring:** January flooding on the Gila basin, with rip-rap proposals on [[Aravaipa Creek]].
- **Groundwater pumping and urban growth:** along the [[Verde River]].
- **Fire and timber:** the Quien Sabe prescribed burn and the Campbell Blue Creek timber sales.
- **Stocking:** gamefish stocking programs run by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

Habitat is often fragmented and extirpation is a risk. Reintroduced populations at [[Sycamore Spring]] were lost after an artificial water system failed and the site dried.

## Key Findings

**Candidate species**
- The [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] funded a status survey of *Gila intermedia*, contracted to [[The Nature Conservancy]]. The information is expected around mid-1994. The survey will inform the listing decision.
- A *Gila* taxonomy progress report was furnished for Team review.

**Listing actions**
- **Spikedace and loach minnow critical habitat:** the reproposal was delayed. The Regional Office's April 1, 1993 draft deadline will not be met. The White River and Eagle Creek populations are recommended for inclusion, requiring new economic analyses. The Team is asked whether areas should be added (e.g., the [[San Pedro River]] recovery area) or dropped, under current policy of including recovery areas.
- **Spikedace and loach minnow uplisting:** low priority, with no work scheduled for fiscal year 1993.
- **Virgin spinedace:** a petition to list as endangered was received. A 90-day finding has been recommended, and the 365-day finding is due June 1993.
- **Big river fishes:** critical habitat for razorback sucker, Colorado squawfish, humpback chub, and bonytail chub was proposed in the *Federal Register* on January 29, 1993. This met a court order for the razorback sucker. A 60-day comment period follows, and a final rule is expected by late 1993.

**Recovery plans**
- ***Desert pupfish:*** the draft plan was published for 60-day public comment, ending March 30, 1993. Target signature is September 30, 1993.
- ***Gila topminnow:*** the revision was contracted to the Arizona Game and Fish Department. It is slow, and a partial draft is planned before an April recovery team meeting. Key topics are delisting and downlisting criteria and the genetic framework for reintroductions. Final plan is targeted for early 1995 at best.
- ***Little Colorado spinedace:*** plan status unknown; a new author may be Chuck Minckley.
- ***Woundfin and Virgin chub:*** public review closed December 15, 1992. Reno FWS requested a reopened comment period because Salt Lake FWS did not send copies to key Nevada entities.

**Recovery actions**
- **Cottonwood Spring (Sonoita Creek):** the FWS plans to fence the spring to exclude livestock under the private land initiative, with two troughs outside the fence.
- **Sycamore Spring:** the reintroduced topminnow population was extirpated after the Forest Service did not repair a known water system failure. The Globe Ranger District agreed to evaluate four alternative reintroduction sites as mitigation.
- **Fresno Canyon (Sonoita Creek tributary):** a new mixed *Poeciliopsis* and *Gambusia* population was found in January 1992. *Poeciliopsis* dominated in January and June.
- **Gila ditaenia transplant:** shelved due to landowner difficulties.

**Freedom of Information Act**
- Six requests were received since June 1992 for spikedace and loach minnow files. The requesters included counties, a coalition, and environmental groups. Partial fee waivers of 100 free pages and $0.13 per additional page were granted. Two appeals have been filed. The Coalition selected about 1,900 pages.
- A law firm representing Phelps-Dodge requested material on Little Colorado spinedace and razorback sucker, and received 603 pages.

**Habitat conservation planning**
- **Verde River:** the effort has stalled. The FWS hopes the new Verde Watershed Association can provide a comprehensive water plan.
- **Prescott and Yavapai-Prescott Apache water settlement:** a Congressional bill would use federal CAP buyback money. The FWS is seeking to treat resulting development as a federal action under NEPA and Section 7, but this appears unlikely.

**Section 7 consultations (selected)**
- **White River (*Tiaroga*):** a draft jeopardy opinion was issued to Indian Health Service on August 26, 1992. The tribe disputes the reasonable and prudent alternatives and has revoked scientific collecting permits.
- **CAP nonnative fish:** consultation is ongoing and difficult. Fish barriers may be considered, but not on Aravaipa Creek.
- **Arizona gamefish stocking:** formal consultation was withdrawn pending revised data and is expected to finish by summer 1993.
- **Campbell Blue Creek timber sales:** formal consultation started November 1992 and was extended to March 27. Cumulative effects are a concern.
- **Diamond Bar allotment:** a non-jeopardy opinion was issued December 3, 1992. Conservation recommendations include a study of sedimentation from grazing 60 bulls on the East Fork Gila River.
- **Arizona Trail, Redrock Canyon:** a non-jeopardy opinion was issued December 23, 1992. The FWS recommends the Lampshire Canyon alternative, which would avoid nearly all topminnow effects, but the Forest Service has said it will not implement this.
- **Quien Sabe prescribed burn:** a non-jeopardy opinion was issued October 1991. The burn has not yet occurred. Monitoring shows the Cave Creek topminnow population is larger and more widespread than previously known.
- **Aravaipa Creek flood repairs:** an emergency consultation allowed 800 linear feet of rip-rap to protect a house after the bank eroded to within 5 feet of it. Further requests for 525 feet of rip-rap and road rebuilding are pending. The FWS favors a watershed approach.
- **Beaver Dam Estates (EPA):** a discharge permit was issued without Section 7 consultation because EPA was unaware the process applied to NPDES permits.
