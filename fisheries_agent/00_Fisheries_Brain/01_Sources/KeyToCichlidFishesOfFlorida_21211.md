# Key to the Cichlid Fishes of Florida

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a preliminary dichotomous identification key to the [[Cichlidae]] (cichlid fishes) of Florida, prepared by William F. Smith-Vaniz and James D. Williams of the National Fisheries Research Center, National Biological Survey, Gainesville, Florida. The key was circulated to selected biologists for testing, with requests for comments, original observations, and voucher specimens documenting apparently new distributional records. The authors planned to publish the key with brief species descriptions and spot distributional maps in a scientific journal. The accompanying cover letter (1 October 1993) provides the context for the key and notes a change in the authors' agency.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Florida hosts more cichlid species than any other state in the continental United States. Nonindigenous cichlids now form the fourth most speciose family in Florida fresh waters.
- **Establishment status:** At least 14 species are considered permanently established. [[Cichla Temensis]] is possibly established. Five additional species, included for completeness, were previously reported from Florida but apparently failed to become established or were eradicated.
- **Hybridization:** [[Tilapia]] species readily hybridize under certain conditions. Hybrids of [[Oreochromis Mossambicus]] x [[Oreochromis Aureus]] occur in some Florida locations and may be very difficult to identify.
- **Key characters and measurement methods:**
  - Longitudinal scale rows are counted from the first row of scales below the upper lateral line that touches the cleithrum and ends at the end of the hypural plate. The count is easiest made counting forward from the scale row immediately above the lower lateral line.
  - Gill-raker counts must include rakers at both ends of the gill arch. All rudimentary gill rakers on the lateral side of the first branchial arch should be counted.
- **Morphological features used in the key:** anal-fin spine count, dorsal fin profile, cheek and preopercle squamation, caudal fin shape and pattern, subocular and basicaudal spots, lower lip structure (presence of a fleshy [[Symphyseal Frenum]]), mandibular pore number, jaw tooth shape, gill-raker counts, pectoral and dorsal fin element counts, and breeding-male coloration.
- **Specimen handling:** Preserved or frozen specimens with precise locality and date data can be collected by the authors. Donated specimens are deposited in the [[Florida Museum Of Natural History]] as voucher material.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All species treated are nonindigenous to Florida freshwater systems, and the key was prepared in response to biologists' difficulty separating morphologically similar species.
- Several taxa are noted as having failed to establish, or as only possibly established, which underscores the dynamic status of introductions.
- The key is considered potentially useful beyond Florida, depending on which species have been introduced in a given region.
- Hybridization among tilapiine species is a identification and management concern in affected locations.

## Key Findings

1. **Dichotomous key structure:** The key uses paired couplets (1a/1b through 22a/22b) that separate taxa on fin spine counts, scale row counts, squamation, color pattern, and oral and jaw morphology.
2. **Initial split:** Couplet 1 separates species by dorsal fin profile and longitudinal scale rows (>60 versus <40). [[Cichla Ocellaris]] has 68-78 scale rows and no black head markings behind the eye. [[Cichla Temensis]] has 108-127 scale rows and black markings on the head behind the eye.
3. **Astronotus:** [[Astronotus Ocellatus]] is identified by anal fin III, 14-16, a densely scaled proximal half of unpaired fins, and 3 scale rows between the upper and lower lateral lines.
4. **Cichlasoma group (couplets 4 to 22):** Multiple species are separated by anal-fin spine number, subocular spot presence, caudal fin banding, lower lip frenum, and mandibular pore number. Key examples include:
   - [[Cichlasoma Bimaculatum]]: dark subocular spot present; anal-fin spines typically 4 (5 in 6% of 310 specimens examined); longitudinal scale rows 23-25, rarely 25.
   - [[Cichlasoma Cyanoguttatum]]: no dark subocular spot; anal-fin spines 4-6, rarely 4; longitudinal scale rows 26-29.
   - [[Cichlasoma Salvini]]: anal-fin spines 8-10; lateral stripe extends to the rear eye margin; mandibular pores 5.
   - [[Cichlasoma Urophthalmus]]: dark basicaudal blotch noticeably blacker than body bands; anal-fin spines 5 or 6.
   - [[Cichlasoma Citrinellum]]: anal-fin spines 6 or 7 (usually 7); breeding males typically with a swollen nuchal hump.
5. **Tilapiine group:** [[Sarotherodon Melanotheron]] has an emarginate caudal fin and longitudinal scale rows 24-26. The [[Oreochromis Aureus]] and [[Oreochromis Mossambicus]] group is separated by first gill arch raker counts (20-26 on the lower limb in O. aureus; 16-22 in O. mossambicus) and genital papilla color in life (translucent in O. aureus; chalky white in O. mossambicus). [[Tilapia Mariae]] has 5 or 6 dark blotches in adults and 7 dark bands in juveniles. [[Tilapia Zillii]] and [[Tilapia Sparrmanni]] are separated by caudal fin banding and pectoral and dorsal fin counts.
6. **Recorded species and status:** Species marked as previously reported but eradicated or not permanently established include [[Geophagus Surinamensis]], [[Cichlasoma Trimaculatum]], [[Tilapia Zillii]], and [[Tilapia Sparrmanni]], among others flagged in the key.
7. **Documentation plans:** The authors requested comments, original observations, and voucher specimens for new distributional records, and intended to publish the key with species descriptions and distribution maps.
8. **Institutional change:** As of 1 October 1993, the research center was moved from the US Fish and Wildlife Service to the new National Biological Survey within the Department of the Interior. The address and contact numbers remained the same, and the authors anticipated a change in research direction.
