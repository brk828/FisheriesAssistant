## Executive Summary

This document synthesizes a genetic analysis (conducted by Genean Genetic Laboratories) of [[Apache Trout]] (APT) populations across the White Mountains of Arizona, spanning both the **Fort Apache Indian Reservation (FAIR)** and the **Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest (FS)**. The Pinetop Fishery Assistance Office used these genetic results, combined with prior [[GAWS Surveys|General Aquatic Wildlife Survey (GAWS)]] morphometric/meristic data, to classify streams as genetically **pure**, **mostly pure (MP)**, **introduced pure (IP)**, or **impure/hybrid (IMP)** due to introgression with non-native [[Brown Trout]] (BNT) and [[Brook Trout]] (BKT). The overarching goal is species **delisting** under the Apache Trout Recovery Plan, which requires **30 self-sustaining pure populations**; the analysis identifies **46 streams** with pure APT populations potentially available for this purpose — exceeding the recovery target. Management recommendations include selective renovation (piscicide treatment to remove hybrids/competitors), barrier construction to prevent re-invasion, and broodstock-based restocking using fish from [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]] (WCNFH) or genetically pure donor streams such as [[Mamie Creek]] and [[Coyote Creek]].

## Background & Description

Formal morphological description of Apache Trout is not detailed in this document; instead, population status is inferred through two complementary lines of evidence:

- **Genetic analysis** (allozyme/molecular, performed by Genean Genetic Laboratories) — considered the higher-resolution standard, occasionally reclassifying populations previously deemed pure via morphology.
- **Morphometric and meristic measurements** (via GAWS surveys) — used to designate populations as "**Mostly Pure (MP)**," a category later found in some cases to be genetically hybridized, highlighting a **discrepancy between morphological and genetic classification methods**.

A notable case-in-point is **[[Sun Creek]]**, where two samples from the same stream returned divergent results — one hybrid, one pure — representing the only unresolved discrepancy in the entire genetic dataset.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** High-elevation, cold-water mountain streams and headwater tributaries, plus stocked lakes/reservoirs (e.g., [[Hurricane Lake]], [[Lee Valley Reservoir]], [[Christmas Tree Lake]]) within the historic native range of Apache Trout in the White Mountains.
- **Sympatric/competing species:** Non-native [[Brown Trout]] and [[Brook Trout]] are the primary hybridization and competition threats, having been introduced into several historically pure APT streams (e.g., Christmas Tree Lake, Sun Creek, Moon Creek, Flash Creek, Little Diamond Creek, Reservation Creek).
- **Forage species management:** [[Speckled Dace]] have been stocked as a forage base in some renovated systems (e.g., Hurricane Creek, Crooked Creek) to support reintroduced APT populations.
- **Population connectivity:** Several stream systems are managed as single interconnected populations due to hydrological connectivity — e.g., Hurricane Lake + Hurricane Creek; Christmas Tree Lake + Sun Creek + Moon Creek.
- **Barriers:** Natural and constructed fish barriers are central to the conservation strategy, isolating pure populations from downstream sources of hybridization and preventing re-invasion after renovation.
- **Hatchery role:** [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]] serves as the primary genetic reservoir (East Fork strain) for restocking renovated streams, exemplified by Hayground Creek, Home Creek, Lee Valley Creek/Reservoir, and Wildcat Creek (all stocked 1988–1990).

## Key Findings

1. **Delisting feasibility:** If genetic recommendations are implemented, **46 streams** with pure APT populations would be available — surpassing the Apache Trout Recovery Plan's requirement of 30 self-sustaining populations prior to delisting.

2. **Population linkage inference:** Genetic purity in one waterbody can be extrapolated to connected/source waters absent direct sampling:
   - [[Hurricane Lake]] (pure) → [[Hurricane Creek]] (inferred pure), treated as one population.
   - [[Coyote Creek]] (pure, sampled) → [[Mamie Creek]] (source population, inferred pure) → traced back to original **[[Ord Creek]]** stock (Fort Apache Indian Reservation), which was lost during 1977 renovation efforts. Mamie/Coyote Creek stock is recommended for **reestablishing APT in Ord Creek**.

3. **Sport fishery vs. refugium trade-off:** [[Christmas Tree Lake]], [[Sun Creek]], and [[Moon Creek]] contain introduced BNT/BKT but will **not be renovated**, being prioritized instead as sport fisheries over their value as genetic refugia; managed collectively as one population.

4. **Genetic-morphological discordance:** The Sun Creek sample discrepancy (one pure, one hybrid result) represents the sole unresolved conflict in the dataset; due to the sport-fishery designation, no removal action will be taken regardless of true status.

5. **Hatchery-origin purity:** APT in [[Hayground Creek]], [[Home Creek]], [[Lee Valley Creek]], [[Lee Valley Reservoir]], and [[Wildcat Creek]] all derive from the **East Fork strain** produced at WCNFH and are considered genetically pure by hatchery lineage.

6. **Remediation protocol for hybridized pure populations:** Where pure populations are contaminated by BNT/BKT, the recommended method is to (a) collect APT broodstock from the population, (b) hold them safely, (c) eliminate all remaining fish (chemical renovation), and (d) restock with the preserved broodstock.

7. **Classification framework:**
   - **Genetically Pure** — confirmed via lab genetic analysis (e.g., Boggy Creek, Crooked Creek, Deep Creek, East Fork White River, Elk Canyon Creek, Firebox Creek, Lofer Creek, Paradise Creek, Rock Creek, Diamond Creek North/South Fork, Hughey Creek, Paddy Creek).
   - **Introduced Pure (IP)** — pure APT introduced from a verified pure donor source (e.g., Coyote Creek from Mamie Creek; Mamie/Mineral Creek from Ord Creek stock).
   - **Mostly Pure (MP)** — morphologically/meristically consistent with purity but sometimes genetically hybridized upon further testing (e.g., Bear Wallow Creek, Centerfire Creek, Conklin Creek, Corduroy Creek, Fish Creek, Snake Creek, Stinky Creek).
   - **Impure/Hybrid (IMP)** — confirmed hybridized populations recommended for eradication and restocking (e.g., Grant Creek, Hannagan Creek, KP Creek).

8. **Active renovation program:** Several streams have already undergone renovation and restocking cycles with WCNFH-sourced APT, including [[Hayground Creek]] (1989/1990), [[Home Creek]] (1987/1988/1990), [[Lee Valley Creek]] (1987/1988/1990), and [[Wildcat Creek]] (1988/1990), each maintained with fish barriers to prevent re-invasion.

9. **Institutional roles:** Management responsibility is split between the **Fort Apache Indian Reservation** and the **Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest**, with [[Soldier Creek]] jointly managed by both agencies and recognized as a naturally pure population requiring only monitoring.
