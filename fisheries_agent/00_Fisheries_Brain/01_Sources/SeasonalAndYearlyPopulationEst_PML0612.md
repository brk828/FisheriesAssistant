# Desert Fishes Symposium Abstracts: Population Estimates and Gila Genetics

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document contains two conference abstracts from the Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council (1995 Symposium). The first, by Douglas and Marsh, reports seasonal and yearly population estimates for [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus*) in the [[Little Colorado River]] of the [[Grand Canyon]] region, Arizona. The second, by Dowling, Tibbets and Naylor, is a progress report on the evolutionary genetics of the genus *Gila* using mitochondrial DNA restriction-site and sequence data. The abstracts address the conservation status of the native Colorado River fishes, which are threatened by habitat disturbance from modern human activity. The source is partly degraded by OCR errors, and the Spanish-language abstract is a translation of the English text, so it is not reproduced here.

## Background & Description

**Conservation context.** The abstracts state that four native large-river fishes of western North America were listed as endangered at the federal level:

- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
- *Gila* species listed as endangered (the abstract names [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] in this category)

A fifth species, [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), had been proposed as a candidate for listing. The Flannelmouth Sucker was under review, and the Bluehead Sucker was considered secure. Life histories are described as "enigmatic" for most of these fishes, and their densities in most tributaries are relatively unknown.

**Study taxa.**

- *Catostomus latipinnis* ([[Flannelmouth Sucker]]), a catostomid sucker of the Colorado River basin.
- *Pantosteus discobolus* ([[Bluehead Sucker]]), a catostomid sucker of the same basin.
- *Gila* spp. (family Cyprinidae), studied as the genus [[Gila]], with emphasis on the mainstem Colorado River species *G. cypha*, *G. elegans* and *G. robusta*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site.** The population study was conducted at three locations within the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), a tributary of the [[Colorado River]] in the Marble Canyon region of northern [[Grand Canyon]], Arizona.

**Sampling design.** Adult Flannelmouth and Bluehead suckers were netted, [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging|PIT-tagged]], and released unharmed on a monthly basis at the three sites. The sampling period is recorded in the source, but the years are garbled by OCR and are not reported here.

**Analytical approach.** Population estimates were derived from [[Mark-Recapture]] data using an [[Open-Population Model]] that allows for immigration, emigration and ontogeny (growth through life stages). Estimates were normalized by fishing effort and compared within and between seasons and years.

**Genetic approach.** The *Gila* study uses [[Mitochondrial DNA]] restriction-site and sequence data, including the [[Cytochrome B]] gene, within the framework of the [[Gila Taxonomy Project]]. The abstract describes preliminary data from the third and final portion of that project.

## Key Findings

**Population estimation (Douglas and Marsh)**

- Seasonal and yearly population estimates were produced for Flannelmouth and Bluehead suckers in the Little Colorado River using a mark-recapture approach. The abstract does not give numerical estimates.
- Within-species variance in population estimates is interpreted as reflecting **longevity**.
- Between-species differences in population estimates are interpreted as related to **life history** differences.

**Conservation implications**

- The Flannelmouth Sucker was under scrutiny for federal listing status, and the Bluehead Sucker was regarded as secure. The population data are intended to inform these status assessments.
- Life-history and density data were limited for most of these fishes, and the study aims to fill this gap.

**Genetic study (Dowling, Tibbets and Naylor)**

- The study presents preliminary mitochondrial DNA results for the genus *Gila*, focusing on the mainstem Colorado River species. The abstract is truncated, so no specific genetic findings or sample sizes are reported.
