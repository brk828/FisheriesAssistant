# Summary of Fish Surveys of Salt River Project and Central Arizona Project Canals, Arizona (1998)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report by [[Paul C. Marsh]] of [[Arizona State University]] summarizes fish surveys of irrigation canals in central Arizona conducted in fall 1998 (October to December). It was submitted to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Phoenix Area Office, under a cooperative agreement, and forms part of the [[Central Arizona Project]] fish monitoring program. Surveys covered the [[South Canal]], [[Consolidated Canal]], [[Tempe Canal]], [[Arizona Canal]], and the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] with its [[Pima Lateral Feeder Canal]]. Sampling used seines, trammel and gill nets, hoop nets, backpack electrofishing, and visual observation. Across stations, a small number of species dominated catches, mainly the juvenile and adult fish of the taxa coded PACL, CYLU, CAIN, and GAAF, and the carp (CYCA).

## Background & Description

The report is a set of survey summaries, one table per canal reach or station. Each entry gives the date, gear type, total effort, and the number and percentage of juvenile and adult fish by species. Species are identified by a standard code set, which the source states follows "the established protocol." Scientific names are not spelled out for most codes, so this note keeps the codes as printed.

Sampling gear and effort, as recorded:

- **Bag seine** (15 x 6 ft, 0.25 in mesh) and **straight seine** (8 x 4 ft, 10 x 6 ft, or 15 x 6 ft; mesh from 0.125 to 0.5 in)
- **Trammel net** (150 x 6 ft, 1.5 in mesh), 0.5 hours
- **Experimental gill net** (125 x 6 ft, 0.5 to 2 in mesh), 5 hours
- **Hoop nets** (6 x 3 ft, 0.5 in mesh), 10 hours total (two nets set for 5 hours each)
- **Backpack electrofisher** (shocking runs of 194 to 400 seconds)
- **Visual observation** by 1 to 2 observers or people walking canal banks

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The surveys sit within a managed water-delivery system. Stations were referenced to structures including the [[Electric Fish Barrier]] on the South Canal and the Tempe Canal, [[Granite Reef Dam]], and [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]]. The electric barrier is a structure meant to limit fish movement along the canals. Reaches were sampled above and below these features to compare fish composition. Locations included a canal siphon, a turnout, a business-route crossing, a wash crossing near Mesa, and a drain outflow. The report gives no water-chemistry, temperature, or habitat data.

## Key Findings

**South Canal (Salt River Project), October to November 1998**

- **Below electric fish barrier, 5 Nov 1998, bag seine (5 hauls):** Two species made up most of the catch. PACL (106 juveniles, 35%) and CYLU (104 juveniles, 34%) were dominant, with CAIN, GAAF, and ICPU also recorded. Totals were 268 juveniles and 303 adults as printed.
- **2.8 miles below barrier at the River Road siphon, 5 Nov 1998, trammel net (0.5 h):** 61 fish. PACL 31 (51%), CAIN 26 (43%), CTID 4 (6%).
- **Same site, straight seine (4 hauls):** MISA was dominant (207 fish, 81%), followed by CYLU (46, 18%), with 2 GIRO-coded individuals (1%).
- **Below barrier at the RWCD turnout, straight seine (10 hauls):** 249 fish. PYOL (200, 80%), PACL (20, 8%), CAIN (17, 7%), and LEMA (10, 4%) were most common.
- **9 miles below barrier, visual survey, 24 Oct 1998:** PACL and CAIN were "abundant." ICPU and MISA were "common," and CYCA (koi), CTID, and PYOL were recorded as "uncommon" or "rare."

**Florence-Casa Grande Canal (Bureau of Reclamation), October 1998**

- **0.5 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam, seine (10 hauls, 8 x 4 ft):** 110 fish. AMNA and GAAF, CYCA, and CYLU were the main taxa.
- **0.5 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam, backpack electrofisher (400 s):** 124 fish. Taxa included CYCA, DOPE, AMNA (66, 16%), and CYLU (17, 6%).
- **1.5 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam, seine (12 hauls):** 52 fish. Taxa included AMNA, ICPU, GAAF, CYCA, and CYLU.
- **2.6 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam at China Wash, 17 Oct 1998:** Seine (6 hauls) caught 15 fish, 14 of them CYLU (93%). Seine (7 hauls, 10 x 6 ft) caught 116 fish, with ICPU (108 juveniles, 93%) the most abundant.
- **10.5 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam, 17 Oct 1998, seine (10 hauls):** 107 fish. DOPE, CYLU, AMNA, GAAF, ICPU, CAIN, and CYCA (mirror carp) were recorded.
- **15.2 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam, 17 Oct 1998:** Hoop nets (10 hours) caught 40 fish, including adult CAIN-type fish (27 adults and 40 total in the summarized rows; the source table is partly garbled). Gill netting (5 hours) caught a single CAIN, and the station is noted "No Fish" in one row.
- **Pima Lateral Feeder Canal at the Florence-Casa Grande junction (15.5 miles below Ashurst-Hayden Dam), 17 Oct 1998:** Seine (6 hauls) caught 192 fish, with CYLU at 185 (96%) and GAAF at 7 (4%). Backpack electrofishing (194 s) caught 79 fish, 78 of them CYLU (99%).

**Central Arizona Project and Arizona Canal, December 1998**

- **Arizona Canal at the Granite Reef electric barrier:** Visual survey (2 observers) recorded 12 fish, PACL and CAIN at 6 each.
- **0.25 miles above Beeline Highway, seine (3 hauls):** CYLU, with 200 fish (100%).
- **6.6 miles below Granite Reef Dam, visual survey:** CAIN and PACL "hundreds," CTID "dozens."
- **7.8 miles below Granite Reef Dam, below Evergreen Drain, seine (3 hauls):** 325 fish. CYLU (300, 92%), CAIN (20, 6%), and PACL (5, 2%).
- **11 miles and 14.2 miles below Granite Reef Dam, visual surveys (4 and 5 Dec 1998):** No fish recorded.

**Consolidated Canal, 24 October 1998**

- **Immediately north of Williams Field Road, seine (10 hauls, 10 x 6 ft):** 744 fish total (32 juveniles, 712 adults). GAAF (340, 46%) and CYLU (250, 34%) dominated, followed by TIZI (101, 14%), ICPU (27 juveniles, 4%), PACL (17, 2%), and smaller counts of CTID, AMNA, CYCA, MISA, and TIAU.

**Tempe Canal, 24 October 1998**

- **South Canal to Chandler Falls, visual survey (2 observers):** CAIN and PACL were "abundant," ICPU and CYCA (koi) "common," CTID "common" to "uncommon." Counts were not recorded.

**Overall pattern:** Catches were dominated by a few species, mostly CYLU, PACL, CAIN, and GAAF, with the largest seine catches at the Consolidated Canal and the South Canal. Fish were absent from several Arizona Canal reaches and from the 15.2-mile hoop-net station. The report is descriptive, with no statistical analysis, no species checklist beyond the codes, and no conclusions about population trends or management recommendations.
