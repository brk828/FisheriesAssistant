# The Nature Conservancy Correspondence on Aravaipa Creek Aquatic Biota Report

#ichthyology #conservation #rare-species

## Executive Summary

This July 1970 correspondence from The Nature Conservancy's Western Regional Office to Dr. W. L. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]] discusses management proposals for [[Aravaipa Creek]] and requests a semi-popular report on the creek's aquatic biota. The letter emphasizes limiting fish collecting activities and coordinating sample requests through Dr. Minckley, while seeking documentation of rare fishes for conservation fundraising purposes.

## Background & Description

The correspondence originates from The Nature Conservancy's San Francisco office, addressed to Dr. W. L. Minckley in the Department of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]] in Tempe, Arizona. The letter responds to Dr. Minckley's July 6th communication regarding management proposals for [[Aravaipa Creek]]. A committee meeting was scheduled for Thursday in Tucson to discuss these matters.

The Nature Conservancy expressed interest in obtaining color photographs of rare fishes inhabiting the stream for use in postcards as part of a fundraising campaign for the area. The organization offered funding assistance to expedite production of the requested report and photographs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Aravaipa Creek]] is identified as habitat for rare fish species requiring conservation management. The correspondence indicates concern about collecting pressure on these populations, leading to recommendations that fish sampling be limited and coordinated through a single authority (Dr. Minckley). The creek's aquatic biota warranted documentation for both scientific and public education purposes, suggesting significant conservation value of the aquatic ecosystem.

## Key Findings

- Agreement reached to limit fish collecting activities at [[Aravaipa Creek]]
- Dr. Minckley designated as coordinator for fish sample requests
- Urgent need identified for semi-popular account of Aravaipa aquatic biota with photographs
- The Nature Conservancy offered funding to expedite report production
- Color photographs of rare fishes specifically requested for postcard production and fundraising
- Timeline of several weeks established for completion of popular account
- Student assistance proposed to help compile the report, either locally or from the Bay Area
