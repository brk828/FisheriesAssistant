# Evolutionary Relationships of the Plagopterins (Teleostei: Cyprinidae) from Cytochrome b Sequences

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This molecular phylogenetic study uses mitochondrial cytochrome *b* (cyt *b*) sequences to test the relationships of the [[Plagopterini]], a tribe of minnows endemic to the [[Great Basin]] and the [[Lower Colorado River]] in southwestern North America. The plagopterins form a monophyletic group, but the genus [[Lepidomeda]] is paraphyletic because the Bonneville-basin and upper Snake River form [[Snyderichthys]] (the [[Leatherside Chub]]) nests within it. The authors recommend that *Snyderichthys* be considered a possible synonym of *Lepidomeda*, possibly divided into two species, and they estimate that the plagopterin lineage is at least 17 million years old. The study is published in *Copeia* (2002) and was produced by the team of [[Thomas E. Dowling]], [[C. Andrew Tibbets]], [[Wendell L. Minckley]] and [[Gerald R. Smith]].

## Background & Description

The plagopterins are a monophyletic group of North American cyprinids diagnosed by spinelike ossification of the first two rays of the dorsal and pelvic fins. Miller and Hubbs (1960) recognized six species in three genera: *Lepidomeda albivallis*, *L. mollispinis*, *L. vittata*, *L. altivelis*, *Meda fulgida* and *Plagopterus argentissimus*. These fishes are endemic to the middle and lower Colorado River drainage, and many now occupy restricted ranges.

*Snyderichthys copei* was originally placed in its own monotypic genus by [[Miller (1945)]]. [[Uyeno (1960)]] treated it as a subgenus of *Gila*. Its relationship to the plagopterins has been disputed, and [[DeMarais (1992)]] recommended resurrecting *Snyderichthys* on the basis of allozyme evidence linking it to *Lepidomeda*.

Competing morphological and molecular hypotheses frame the study:
- [[Coburn and Cavender (1992)]] used morphology and osteology to propose three North American cyprinid groups ("shiner," "chub" and "western" clades), placing plagopterins within a *Gila* clade of western minnows.
- [[Simons and Mayden (1997)]] used 12S rDNA and identified a distinct "creek chub clade" (*Couesius*, *Hemitremia*, *Margariscus*, *Semotilus*) to which plagopterins were more closely related than to *Gila*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors frame the group's biogeography around major geological events. The plagopterin lineage is linked to the formation of the southern Colorado Plateau margin (22 to 25 Ma), extensional faulting that produced the [[Basin and Range Physiographic Province]] (beginning more than 20 Ma), and later Plateau uplift and aridification. The shared ancestor of *Meda*, *Plagopterus*, *Lepidomeda* and *Snyderichthys* is estimated to have lived 17 to 40 Ma.

Several historical connections are discussed:
- The [[Bear River]] has moved between the Snake and [[Bonneville Basin]] systems. Its most recent connection to Lake Bonneville is dated to about 34,000 years ago.
- [[Keyes (1917, 1918)]] proposed the upper Snake River as a former Bonneville tributary flowing to the lower Colorado. This idea was largely rejected by geologists and biogeographers, though some faunal evidence was cited in its support.
- The Virgin and White River populations of *Lepidomeda* are linked to pluvial lake systems, and *L. albivallis* is from the pluvial White River.

Habitat isolation is also invoked. *Meda* is associated with hard-bottomed streams, which are separated by the sand-bottomed, erosive main stem of the lower [[Gila River]].

## Key Findings

**Sampling and sequencing**
- The study used 1140 bp of cyt *b*. Representatives included all extant plagopterins (with multiple *Snyderichthys* samples from the Snake, Bear and Bonneville basins), the three clades of Coburn and Cavender (1992), and outgroups *Abramis brama* and *Notemigonus crysoleucas*.
- Of the 1140 bp, 425 sites were parsimony informative. Variable characters were most common at third codon positions (335), followed by first (76) and second (14).

**Phylogenetic results**
- The unweighted parsimony search recovered 15 most-parsimonious trees of 2040 steps (CI = 0.34, RI = 0.54). Transversions weighted twice as heavily as transitions gave six trees of 2494 steps, with a similar overall topology.
- The plagopterins formed a monophyletic group (80% bootstrap in the unweighted analysis; 90% in the weighted analysis). Within it, a southern clade (*Meda* + *Plagopterus*, 99% bootstrap) and a northern lineage (*Lepidomeda* + *Snyderichthys*, 94%) were recovered.
- Within the northern lineage, Snake and Bear River *Snyderichthys* grouped with a trichotomy of *L. mollispinis* and *L. albivallis* (94% bootstrap). *Lepidomeda vittata* was sister to this clade (63%), and Bonneville *Snyderichthys* was the most basal lineage.
- Relationships of the chubs *Couesius*, *Margariscus* and *Semotilus* to plagopterins were weakly supported (below 50% in the unweighted analysis). Under parsimony, plagopterins fell within the creek chub clade with *Margariscus* as the closest relative.
- Neighbor-joining analysis using gamma-corrected Tamura-Nei distances recovered similar groupings, with the western, chub + shiner and plagopterin groups supported at 70%, 99% and 87% bootstrap, respectively.

**Taxonomic conclusions**
- The paraphyly of *Lepidomeda* (through *Snyderichthys*) and the complex geographic pattern of mtDNA lineages cast doubt on the morphologically diagnosed *Snyderichthys*.
- *Snyderichthys* should be considered a possible synonym of *Lepidomeda* and may be divided into two species, a northern form (upper Snake and Bear rivers) and a southern form (central and southern Bonneville basin). The southern form may be *L. altivelis* (Jouy). The authors have not yet found diagnostic morphological characters separating the northern form from *S. copei*.
- The authors consider two alternatives to a simple vicariance explanation: stochastic lineage sorting and introgressive mtDNA transfer, which is common among cyprinids. They judge lineage sorting less likely given the high divergence among lineages.

**Divergence times**
- A cyt *b* rate for cyprinids was estimated from fossil calibrations at 1.31% sequence divergence per pairwise comparison per Ma (0.66% per lineage per Ma) when forced through the origin. The unconstrained regression gave 1.05% per Ma (0.53% per lineage per Ma).
- The plagopterin lineage is at least 17 Ma old. The *Meda* + *Plagopterus* split is estimated at 19 to 34 Ma.
- *Meda* populations in the Verde and upper Gila basins diverged 0.9 to 3.4 Ma.
- *Snyderichthys* and *L. vittata* from a common progenitor 7.8 to 11.6 Ma and 6.3 to 8.6 Ma, respectively. The authors link this separation to Plateau uplift and Basin and Range extension.
- *Lepidomeda* and *Snyderichthys* share a sister relationship estimated at 1.3 to 7.4 Ma, which the authors find difficult to explain.

**Conservation and data**
- The study notes that several taxa, including *Plagopterus argentissimus* (woundfin) and *Meda fulgida* (spikedace), are federally listed. Specimens were collected under federal endangered-species permits.
- New sequences were deposited in GenBank under accession numbers AF452072 to AF452094.
