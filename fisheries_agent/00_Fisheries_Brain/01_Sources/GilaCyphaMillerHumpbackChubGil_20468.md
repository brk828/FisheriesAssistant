# Freshwater Fish Atlas Accounts: Humpback Chub, Roundtail Chub, and Bluehead Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document contains three species accounts from a North American freshwater fish atlas (compiled by P. B. Holden and W. L. Minckley, March 1980). It covers the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*). Each account gives the type locality, systematics, distribution and habitat, adult size, and biology. The accounts focus on [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes, with taxonomic revisions and range notes drawn from earlier literature.

## Background & Description

**Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*)**
- Type locality: [[Colorado River]] at the mouth of Bright Angel Creek, [[Grand Canyon]], AZ (Miller 1946).
- Systematics: Belongs to [[Subgenus Gila]]. Smith et al. (1979) showed that *G. cypha*, *G. elegans*, and *G. robusta* are distinct species in big-river habitats. Suttkus and Clemmer (1977) added morphometric data from Grand Canyon specimens.
- Adult size: 200–300 mm SL; maximum 320 mm SL.
- Biology: Little known. Often found in deep, swift water, but also common in canyon-shaded pools and along deep, ledge-shaded reaches with moderate current.

**Roundtail Chub (*Gila robusta*)**
- Type locality: Recorded as Zuni River, NM (Baird and Girard 1853). The atlas considers this almost certainly an error, most likely the [[Little Colorado River]] below Grand Falls.
- Systematics: Subspecies recognized include *G. r. jordani* (Pluvial White River, NV), *G. r. seminuda* (Virgin River, UT/AZ/NV), *G. r. robusta* (widespread in the upper and lower Colorado River basin), and *G. r. grahami* (Gila River tributaries, AZ/NM). Nominal forms also occur in Mexico. One subspecies is listed as unstudied.
- Adult size: 250–350 mm SL.
- Biology: Vanicek and Kramer (1969) studied age and growth, food habits, and spawning times in the [[Green River]] (UT and CO).

**Bluehead Sucker (*Catostomus discobolus*)**
- Type locality: Green River, WY (Cope 1872).
- Systematics: Previously called *Pantosteus delphinus*. Smith (1966) placed it in *Catostomus* and found that *C. discobolus* has priority, though some authors still use *Pantosteus*. It appears most closely related to *C. clarki*. Populations in the Little Colorado River show [[Introgression]] from *C. plebeius* of the adjacent Rio Grande drainage. The species [[Hybridization]] with *C. latipinnis* and with introduced *C. commersoni* in the upper Colorado basin.
- Adult size: 250–350 mm SL; maximum 400 mm SL.
- Biology: Little known. Spawns in late spring or early summer in water at least 16°C. Feeds by scraping organisms off rocks. Individuals occasionally live up to seven years.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution patterns:** The Humpback Chub is generally uncommon but locally concentrated. Its largest extant populations are in and near the mouth of the Little Colorado River and in Desolation Canyon of the Green River. Remains are known from archaeological sites along the lower Colorado River.
- **Range replacement:** The Roundtail Chub is replaced by *G. elegans* in the largest river channels. The Bluehead Sucker is replaced by *C. clarki* below Grand Canyon.
- **Habitat use:** The Roundtail Chub is locally abundant in large river channels or near cover such as boulders, overhanging cliffs, or vegetation in smaller streams. The Bluehead Sucker occupies a wide range of fluvial habitats, from cold, clear trout streams (less than 20°C) to warm, very turbid streams, and prefers riffles over rocky substrate.
- **Geographic extent:** The Roundtail Chub extends south through the [[Rio Yaqui]] basin to the Rio Piaxtla in Sinaloa, Mexico. The Bluehead Sucker also occurs in the Bear and Weber river drainages of the Pluvial Lake Bonneville basin.
- **Voucher specimens:** The atlas cites specimens from Grand Canyon (*C. discobolus*, male, 147 mm SL), Grant County, NM (*G. robusta*, 116 mm SL), and other localities, all held in the NCSM collection.

## Key Findings

- The Humpback Chub is a species of deep, swift, and canyon-shaded habitats, with its strongholds in the Little Colorado River and Desolation Canyon. Its biology is poorly known.
- Taxonomic work (Smith et al. 1979) confirmed three separate big-river species: *G. cypha*, *G. elegans*, and *G. robusta*.
- The Roundtail Chub's type locality is considered erroneous, and its subspecies arrangement across the Colorado and Gila systems and northern Mexico is still partly unresolved.
- The Bluehead Sucker's naming is contested (*Catostomus* versus *Pantosteus*), and hybridization and introgression with related species complicate its identity in the upper Colorado basin.
- Reproduction in the Bluehead Sucker begins in late spring or early summer at water temperatures of at least 16°C.
- The atlas describes all three species as poorly studied in biology, with most information limited to age, growth, and food habit studies in the Green River and general regional accounts.
