# Aspects of Juvenile and Adult Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*) Biology in Senator Wash Reservoir and the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen-texanus

## Executive Summary

This 1980 study proposal, prepared by W. E. Loudermilk (Assistant Fishery Biologist, [[California Department of Fish and Game]] Region 5), seeks to fill major data gaps on the [[Razorback Sucker]], a declining native fish of the [[Colorado River]] drainage. The proposal covers juvenile and adult biology in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]] (SWR) and the [[Lower Colorado River]] from [[Parker Dam]] to [[Morelos Dam]]. It is a joint effort of the California Department of Fish and Game, the [[Bureau of Land Management]], the [[Water and Power Resources Service]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. Twelve tasks are proposed over fiscal years 1980–1983, covering movement, spawning, habitat, population estimation, egg and larval survival, predation and status in the river. The authors argue that management decisions under federal and state law cannot be made without this information.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is one of relatively few fishes native to the Colorado River drainage. In the late 1800s and early 1900s it was distributed from the headwaters in Wyoming and Colorado to the river mouth in northwestern Mexico, and probably used smaller tributaries in good water years. It was an important food source for Native American tribes and was among the most abundant fishes in its range. Its abundance supported a commercial fishery in Saguaro Lake, Arizona, until the late 1940s.

Since about the turn of the century, abundance has declined and the range has contracted. The decline coincides with low-flow years, impoundment of tributaries and the mainstream, and introductions of exotic fauna. The species is listed as "endangered" by California, received increased legislative protection in Arizona, and has been proposed as "threatened" by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (1979 amendments).

Projects affecting remaining populations must assess and minimize impacts under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] and the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]]. The proposal stresses that prior life-history knowledge was fragmentary, citing earlier work on habitat, spawning and early development (e.g., [[Gustafson 1975]] on early development, sexual dimorphism and fecundity). Recent records of young fish (0.5–0.7 kg) in the Parker and upper Yuma divisions suggest some recruitment is occurring in the lower river.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Senator Wash Reservoir** is a semi-controlled system that the proposal considers well suited to study. The reservoir is closed to adult fish immigration and emigration, except through natural and angling mortality, yet adults and young of all predator fish present in the river occur there. The proposal states that SWR provides habitat adequate for spawning and for recruitment at least to the larval stage.

**Threats and pressures** include planned projects that will alter the chemical, physical and biological character of the lower river, such as [[Hoover Dam Modification]], [[Parker II Stabilization]], [[Yuma Division Plans]] and energy-related facility changes. The proposal argues that resource agencies cannot design projects that protect the species while information about it is lacking.

**Sampling context:** Water movement, temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, oxidation-reduction potential, light, depth, slope, substrate, periphyton, benthic invertebrates, phytoplankton and zooplankton are all to be measured in relation to spawning sites. Egg survival is to be linked to fungal and hydra presence, predation, water-level fluctuation, flow, turbidity, sedimentation and human disturbance.

## Key Findings

**Preliminary results reported (1980 program):**
- Nine razorback suckers held at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] were used to test a technique for implanting [[Ultrasonic Telemetry]] transmitters, and serve as control fish.
- Ultrasonic transmitters were implanted in six fish captured from SWR, and movement monitoring began after the February release.
- Telemetry data indicate crepuscular (twilight) activity peaks, with movement mainly associated with reservoir contours.
- Telemetry and follow-up diving surveys documented spawning, including the collection of viable eggs and larvae.
- The collection is described as only the second collection of young razorback suckers from the lower basin since the 1950 Willow Beach specimens.

**Proposed tasks (12 total):**
1. Complete the adult movement routes and patterns study in SWR.
2. Define [[Spawning Behavior]] in SWR, including pre-spawning movement, site selection, redd description and spawning-act specifics (number of fish, egg counts, predator association, time of day).
3. Locate and characterize spawning substrates and habitat conditions in SWR.
4. Estimate the adult population in SWR.
5. Develop sampling techniques suitable for mainstream use, to determine species status from Parker to Morelos dams.
6. Evaluate [[Egg Survival]] in SWR.
7. Document egg development to swim-up stage and larval activity patterns, if feasible.
8. Determine larval food habits, if feasible.
9. Determine predator food habits associated with larval survival.
10. Determine status in the Colorado River from Parker Dam to Morelos Dam, including age, growth and food habits from sacrificed adults (not to exceed 30 fish), supplemented by inventory data.
11. Use ultrasonic telemetry in the mainstream to determine habitat use and movement, documenting depth, temperature, dissolved oxygen, conductivity, redox potential and substrate.
12. Evaluate river spawning areas found in Task 11 under the same parameters as Task 3.

**Schedule and reporting:**
- The schedule spans FY 1980–81 through FY 1982–83, with mid-year and annual summaries and final reports for SWR and the Colorado River.
- Task summaries follow standard scientific format (Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions, Literature Cited).
- Quarterly progress reports are planned, and a draft final report is due within one quarter of contract completion.
- Joint funding under a cooperative agreement is recommended over sole-source funding, since several agencies will use the results.
