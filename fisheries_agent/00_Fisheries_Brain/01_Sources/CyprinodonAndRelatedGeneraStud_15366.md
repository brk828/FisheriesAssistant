# Cyprinodon and Related Genera Study and Maintenance Group

## Executive Summary
This document (est. 1991) describes the founding charter and operational protocols of the **Cyprinodon and Related Genera Study and Maintenance Group**, a hobbyist-professional network dedicated to the captive maintenance, documentation, and conservation of [[Cyprinodontidae]] (pupfish and related killifish genera). Rather than a taxonomic or morphological study, the source is a **conservation-management and quality-control (QC) framework** intended to preserve genetically pure, well-documented captive populations of legally keepable species, with the long-term goal of supporting reintroduction efforts and reducing collection pressure on wild populations. The group organizes itself geographically into three subgroups (Mexico, western US, eastern US/Caribbean) and mandates strict anti-hybridization, record-keeping, and population-exchange protocols.

## Background & Description
The source does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of individual species. However, it notes a taxonomically relevant observation:
- **Female pupfish across many species within these genera are morphologically very similar ("look almost identical")**, creating a significant risk of accidental cross-contamination between populations if fish are inadvertently mixed. This underscores the difficulty of field/tank identification at the species level within [[Cyprinodon]] and allied genera without careful lineage tracking.
- The included **Yearly Maintenance Report** template calls for standardized morphometric/life-history data collection per population, including:
  - Adult male and female size
  - 30-day and 60-day fry size
  - Egg incubation period and temperature
  - Spawning medium preferences

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Genera covered**: [[Adinia]], [[Crenichthys]], [[Cualac]], [[Cubanichthys]], [[Cyprinodon]], [[Empetrichthys]], [[Floridichthys]], [[Jordanella]], and [[Megupsilon]] — collectively comprising at least **56 species and subspecies** per the third edition of the *Killifish Master Index*.
- **Geographic distribution**: Species originate from localities within the continental United States, Mexico, and select Caribbean islands, often restricted to isolated springs, desert pools, and other narrow-range freshwater/brackish habitats typical of the family [[Cyprinodontidae]].
- **Conservation status**: Several species/subspecies within these genera are federally listed as **endangered**, restricting private aquarist access; the group explicitly focuses on **legal, non-listed species** as a proof-of-concept for responsible community-based species maintenance.
- **Ecological risk factors discussed**:
  - Unauthorized release of captive fish into the wild risks **hybridization** with closely related native populations or **competitive displacement** of native species.
  - The group mandates that any wild reintroduction only occur with the explicit approval of a Department of Fish & Game or equivalent regulatory agency.
- **Role in habitat conservation**: By supplying tank-bred fish to researchers, the group aims to reduce demand for wild collection, thereby lowering disturbance to sensitive natural habitats such as isolated spring systems associated with genera like [[Crenichthys]] and [[Empetrichthys]].

## Key Findings
1. **Purpose Structure**: The group's mission rests on three pillars — (1) maintaining pure populations under strict QC, (2) building a hobbyist/professional network, and (3) aggregating species-specific information.
2. **Anti-Hybridization Protocol**: Strict physical separation of related species/genera is required in fish-rooms; equipment (nets, rocks, gravel) must be heat-sterilized between tanks, and plants isolated for **at least twice the average egg incubation period** (e.g., 20 days for an 8–12 day incubation) to prevent accidental hybridization via hidden eggs.
3. **Population Genetics Management**: Recommended captive population size is **15–25 individuals per member** to buffer against skewed sex ratios and genetic bottlenecks. Group breeding (not pair breeding) is prioritized to maximize gene pool retention.
4. **Culling Criteria**: Individuals exhibiting deformities or deviations from the population's normal color pattern should be culled to preserve phenotypic authenticity relative to wild populations.
5. **Exchange Protocol**: Populations should be exchanged between maintainers **every 2–4 generations**, ideally incorporating wild-caught specimens periodically to prevent genetic drift from founder populations.
6. **Documentation Standards**: A standardized **Yearly Maintenance Report** captures population counts (by sex/age class), mortality, tank parameters (pH, hardness, temperature, salinity), diet, spawning substrate, incubation conditions, generational lineage (F1, F2, etc.), and cross-breeding history (e.g., an F2 × F5 cross yields F6 offspring).
7. **Institutional Redundancy**: Each species is targeted to be maintained by **3–5 independent keepers**, with mandatory communication at least once every 1–2 years to prevent attrition of institutional knowledge; records are duplicated across at least two locations and organized regionally (Mexico / western US / eastern US & Caribbean subgroups).
8. **Conservation Rationale**: The group explicitly positions itself as a non-commercial, volunteer-based alternative/supplement to state and institutional [[Refugium]] programs, intending eventual collaboration with agencies for species reintroduction (using [[Jordanella floridae]] as an illustrative future case study) and hybrid tracking via the American Killifish Association's Hybrid Register.
