# Notice to Pursue Legal Action: San Pedro River Dewatering and Endangered Species Act Violations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This August 16, 1994 letter from the [[Southwest Center For Biological Diversity]] (SWCBD), signed by Robin D. Silver, M.D., is a notice of intent to sue federal agencies. It alleges that they violated Sections 2, 7, and/or 9 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] and the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) through their contribution to the dewatering of the [[San Pedro River]] in [[Cochise County]], Arizona. The letter names five listed or proposed fish and one proposed bird species as harmed. It attributes the dewatering mainly to groundwater pumping and to growth and development that federal agencies fund or facilitate. The letter gives the recipients 60 days to remedy the violations before suit is filed in the United States District Court.

This is a legal correspondence, not a research study. It contains no original field data. Its content consists of legal arguments, quoted agency and government documents, and a list of addressees and violated statutes.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Formal notice under the Endangered Species Act of intent to pursue legal action against federal agencies for violations related to federally protected and proposed species in southeastern Arizona.
- **Addressees:** Federal officials, including the Secretary of Defense, the Commanding General of the [[U.S. Army Intelligence Center And Fort Huachuca]], the Secretary of Housing and Urban Development, the Secretary of the Interior, the Director of the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), the Secretary of Transportation, the Federal Highway Administration, the Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation, the Federal National Mortgage Association, and the Veterans Administration, along with regional and state officials.
- **Protected fishes named:**
  - [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), federally protected
  - [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), federally protected
  - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), federally protected
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), federally protected
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), federally protected
- **Protected bird named:** [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), proposed for federal protection. The letter says the listing was "illegally overdue."
- **Legal basis:** Section 2 (purposes and policy), Section 7 (interagency consultation and jeopardy prohibition), Section 9 (prohibited take), and NEPA Section 4332 (environmental impact statements).
- **Sponsoring contact:** Dr. Silver, SWCBD, P.O. Box 39629, Phoenix, AZ 85069-9629. Copied to Mark Hughes, Esq., Staff Attorney, Earthlaw.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Dewatering mechanism:** The letter states that [[Fort Huachuca]] and the Department of Defense, through their personnel, contractors, and support staff, are directly dewatering the San Pedro River by pumping groundwater from the aquifer that is the river's primary water source.
- **Growth as a threat:** The letter identifies "Cochise County's uncontrolled population growth and development" as the major threat to the river and the listed species.
- **Council On Environmental Quality findings (1981):**
  - Human groundwater overdraft is "now the major desertification force at work in this area."
  - The overdraft situation could "effectively exhaust the nearby aquifer by the year 2020."
  - The upper San Pedro River "could run dry - just as the Santa Cruz did" if overdrafting continues.
- **Habitat loss:** The USFWS proposed rule (July 23, 1993) states that "as much as 90 percent of lowland riparian habitat has been lost in Arizona." Loss is attributed to urban and agricultural development, water diversion and impoundment, channelization, livestock grazing, and hydrological change. The cottonwood-willow community "has declined most with modern river management."
- **Recovery habitat:** USFWS biological opinion documents (May 30, 1991 draft; April 15, 1994 final; document #2-21-90-F-119, concerning [[Central Arizona Project]] water delivery to the Gila River Basin) identify the upper San Pedro River basin above Saint David as "among the most promising recovery habitat for native Gila River Fishes."
- **Timeline of awareness:** The letter states that the need for protection of the flycatcher has been known since 1987, and that USFWS scientists had recommended emergency protection in writing for more than two years.

## Key Findings

1. **Alleged violations by agency:** The letter lists the Department of Defense and Fort Huachuca, the Department of Housing and Urban Development, the Department of the Interior and USFWS, the Department of Transportation, the Federal Highway Administration, the Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation, the Federal National Mortgage Association, and the Veterans Administration as violating Sections 2, 7, and 9 of the Endangered Species Act. Each agency is named under all three sections.

2. **Direct versus indirect contribution:** The letter argues that agencies violate the law whether their actions directly or indirectly contribute to dewatering. Fort Huachuca's groundwater pumping is cited as direct. The other agencies are cited as promoting, facilitating, funding, or underwriting growth.

3. **USFWS inaction:** The letter alleges that the Department of the Interior and USFWS "have failed to issue appropriate biological advice and have failed to advise others of the deleterious effects of their actions."

4. **Recovery requirement:** Quoting USFWS, the letter states that conservation under the Act "clearly requires recovery of that species and protection of ecosystems which would support that recovery." Loss of recovery habitat is therefore contrary to the Act's purposes.

5. **NEPA claims:** Fort Huachuca and the other agencies named above are alleged to violate NEPA's requirement for a detailed environmental impact statement. SWCBD is already litigating against Fort Huachuca for failing to evaluate effects of expansion on the San Pedro River. A similar suit against the other agencies is planned.

6. **Deadline:** If "satisfactory steps" to remedy the violations are not taken within 60 days, SWCBD intends to sue in the United States District Court.

7. **Stated rationale:** The letter invokes the legal principle that "the party who hires the killer, or who supplies the killer with the weapon, is just as guilty as the murderer," to argue that funding and facilitating agencies share liability for harm to listed species.
