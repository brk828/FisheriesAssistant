# Noxious Vegetation Control Program (F-2-D-2) Completion Report

#aquatic-botany #freshwater-ecology #pontederiaceae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 completion report, prepared by Don R. Luethy for the [[Florida Game and Fresh Water Fish Commission]], documents the Noxious Vegetation Control Program (Project F-2-D-2). The program targeted [[Water Hyacinth]] and other emergent and marginal aquatic plants that were covering fishing streams and lakes and harming fisheries. It was approved on April 3, 1952 and run under [[Federal Aid in Fish and Wildlife Restoration]] until June 31, 1953 (as stated in the report). It then continued with Commission funding on a somewhat enlarged scale. The core method combined [[Aerial Herbicide Application]] with [[Airboat Spray]] clean-up, using [[2,4-D Herbicide]] and [[2,4,5-T Herbicide]]. Sponsors paid for chemicals, and the Commission paid for operations.

The report is primarily an operational and cost record for herbicide control of aquatic vegetation, not a fish study. It is framed as a guide for other agencies considering similar programs.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Hyacinth had covered many favored fishing waters. Earlier hyacinth work by other agencies addressed drainage and navigation, leaving most inland waters untreated.
- **Demand:** The program drew weekly requests for new lakes and streams. Requesting parties had to fund all chemicals.
- **Governing principle:** The Commission supplied equipment, personnel and operations. Sponsors (sportsmen's clubs, county commissions, and others) supplied chemical funds, often held in [[Escrow Funding]].
- **Reporting basis:** The report is a required [[Federal Aid Completion Report]] for Project F-2-D-2, with tables covering roughly 31 treated waters.

The procedures covered:

1. **[[Preliminary Survey]]:** Checks on public access, whether all connected vegetation would be treated, sponsor funding, open-water access for aerial spraying, mat versus single-layer structure, and crop or liability waivers.
2. **Acreage determination:** Aerial photographs, a planimeter and a Crown Graphic camera were used. Readings were taken in triplicate and averaged.
3. **Aerial spraying:** A PA 18 Piper with a removable 40-gallon tank, and a larger Stearman as a supplement.
4. **Boat spraying:** An airsled fitted with a Bean 600-psi piston pump.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hyacinth structure:** Dense single-layer hyacinth cost about $4.00 per acre in chemicals. Multi-layer mats could cost up to $8.00 per acre and take months to sink.
- **Drift hazard:** The vapor of aerial 2,4-D ester can drift a considerable distance and is lethal to most farm crops. Perennial yard shrubs and citrus trees led to outright disapproval of a site until a release was obtained. No liability insurance for unintended crop kills had been obtained.
- **Wind and timing:** Spraying had to occur during brief dawn or late-afternoon calms. One 205-acre raft was only 120 acres treated before wind drift forced a stop, and the area had to be resprayed.
- **Seasonality:** Summer applications were reported to be about 5 to 10% more effective than winter ones. Frost and falling water levels reduced maintenance needs. Fall and winter reproduction by stolons and seeds was largely retarded.
- **Fish and habitat:** The report rejects the idea that untreated marginal vegetation should be left as fish cover. It also rejects the claim that hyacinth provides winter pasture. Both claims were judged to lack validity, and maintaining any margin was judged a constant expense.
- **Animal toxicity:** Apart from one [[Sodium Arsenite]] incident, no herbicides used were reported as toxic to animals. Claims of livestock deaths were handled by discussion or by publications.

## Key Findings

**Methods and equipment**
- The aerial unit uses a 40-gallon tank carrying 7.5 gallons of chemical and 32.5 gallons of diesel or kerosene carrier. This covers 15 acres at one-half gallon of chemical per acre, with a 30-foot swath over a four-mile run.
- The spray plane can treat 15 acres in 2.5 minutes of actual spraying. The report notes that a boat crew might need several days for the same area.
- Aerial spraying is restricted to projects where most vegetation is in the open. The threshold is more than 15 acres.
- The Stearman (300 or 450 hp, 140 to 160 gallons per load) can treat three to four times more acreage per trip than the Piper.
- The airsled measures about 5'6" at the bottom beam, 6'6" at the gunnel beam and 14' long, with a 30-inch freeboard, and is powered by a 108 to 150 hp aircraft engine.
- The Bean pump delivers 8 gallons per minute and is powered by a 3 hp engine.

**Recommended rates**

| Application | Chemical | Rate per acre |
|---|---|---|
| Aerial, hyacinth | 2,4-D ester (3.34 lb acid-equivalent), oil carrier | 0.5 gallon |
| Boat, hyacinth | 2,4-D amine (4.0 lb acid-equivalent), water carrier | 0.75 gallon |
| Water lily and water lettuce | 2,4-D amine, 50 gal water, detergent activator | 1 gallon |
| 2,4,5-T:2,4-D mixture (aerial) | 1 gal 2,4,5-T to 3 gal 2,4-D ester | 0.5 gallon |

- The report found the aerial mix of 2,4,5-T and 2,4-D to be the economical choice when woody plants such as water willow are mixed with hyacinth.
- Oil carriers were chosen because they resist rain washout and reduce runoff.

**Recommendations on other species**
- No firm recommendations were made for cattail, maiden cane, water willow, button bush, coontail or pondweeds, because of erratic results.

**Costs and results**
- Average chemical cost to control hyacinth was $2.99 per acre, with a range of $1.50 to $6.44 per acre.
- Average 2,4-D use to reach control was 0.64 gallons per acre. The report gives the range as 1.56 to 0.85 gallons.
- Total average cost was about $8.03 per acre, of which $5.04 was operational expense.
- Across the program, 104 lakes and streams were surveyed. Of these, 31 were controlled and maintained and 16 were in process of control.
- About 5,940 acres of hyacinth were killed and sunk, clearing an original 3,909 acres of infested water.
- One case, [[Lake Hamburger]] (60 acres of layered hyacinth), needed about 150 acres treated aerially and 70 acres by boat.
- The report records that about $1,404 in reimbursements was never collected.

**Failures and lessons**
- A [[Rotenone]] application over about 8 miles of canal (42 acres) by spray plane was successful. It was a separate fish-management job.
- Attempts at T.C.A. with 2,4-D caused a jelly-like mix and spray-system clogging. The liquid form was unstable, and production was stopped.
- Aerial [[Sodium Arsenite]] killed Potamogeton in a 20-acre lake, but a jammed valve released it onto nearby pasture. No further aerial arsenite treatments were made.
- Sponsors' depleted funds caused regrowth and wasted effort. Requiring escrow deposits resolved this.

**Administration**
- Staff were hired on performance, not political affiliation. The report credits this as a main reason for success.
- Publicity was deliberately limited to avoid overwhelming demand.
- The report notes that the two airsleds could handle clean-up, but maintenance on controlled lakes would soon absorb them. It states that the program must either expand or be limited to maintenance.
