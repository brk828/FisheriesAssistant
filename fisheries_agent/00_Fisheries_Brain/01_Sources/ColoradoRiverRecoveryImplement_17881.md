# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program: 1990 Committee Minutes, Reorganization and FY-91 Work Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This administrative packet, issued by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) in November 1990, documents the August 22, 1990 meeting of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (RIP) [[Implementation Committee]] for four endangered or rare Upper Colorado River fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The packet contains the meeting minutes, a reorganization plan for the program's committee structure, the draft FY-91 work plan and budget, Section 7 consultation correspondence, a Utah nonnative-fish review agreement, and a Congress-facing water rights resolution. The main outcomes were approval of the reorganization, approval of the FY-91 work plan (about $2.5M base budget, $2.697M total), conditional approval of up to $400,000 to acquire [[Cross Mountain Ranch]] water rights, and a decision to stop new program funding for the [[San Juan River]] pending resolution of the [[Animas-La Plata Project]].

## Background & Description

The RIP is a 15-year, multi-agency effort to recover the four species and allow water development in the Upper Colorado River Basin to continue. Its participants include the FWS, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), the states of Colorado, Utah and Wyoming, the [[Western Area Power Administration]], water users and conservation groups. The Implementation Committee oversees the program; the Recovery Program Director, [[John Hamill]], serves as non-voting staff.

The packet covers several interlinked topics:

- **Meeting minutes (August 22, 1990):** Governance, budget, reorganization, San Juan and Animas-La Plata issues, Cross Mountain Ranch acquisition, nonnative fish, Section 7 consultation, technical subcommittee reports and a planned Washington briefing.
- **Reorganization memo (July 31, 1990):** Proposes abolishing the Technical Group and replacing it with three technical committees (Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information and Education), with planning moved to a renamed Management Committee.
- **Research Advisory Group input:** Field biologists argue that biology is not driving the program, that the draft [[Road Maps]] lack focus and prioritization, and that funding is shifting to administrative projects.
- **FY-91 Work Plan:** Budget, ranked projects, Section 7 fund status and appendices describing each project.
- **Supporting correspondence:** Flaming Gorge consultation, Utah stocking review, rainbow smelt rejection, Section 7 depletion consultations, a BLM/FWS consistency meeting, the Governors' overhead waiver request, and a public-information press release.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The four species are endangered or rare native fishes of the Upper Colorado system. The packet treats their recovery as dependent on habitat flows, hatchery and refugia capacity, and control of nonnative fishes. Key ecological points include:

- **Flow management:** Flaming Gorge Dam operations drive Green River flows. FWS recommended a summer/fall target flow not to exceed 1,800 cfs (±25 percent at Jensen) to protect young-of-year Colorado squawfish nursery habitat. Winter and spring recommendations were deferred pending about five more years of study.
- **Nursery habitat and sediment:** Backwater habitat availability, flooded bottomlands and substrate conditions for spawning and larval emergence are identified as research priorities.
- **Nonnative fishes:** Warmwater nonnatives are considered competitors and predators of the endangered fishes. Rainbow smelt introduction to [[Lake Powell]] was rejected because it posed high downstream risk to native fishes and was unlikely to fix the collapsed striped bass fishery.
- **Hatchery and genetics:** Roughly 75,000 Colorado squawfish were stocked in 1982–1984 from Dexter National Fish Hatchery. In July 1990 three hatchery-spawned adults (22–24 inches) were found in the Colorado River, but their reproductive success remains unknown. Concern exists about limited genetic diversity among captive stocks.
- **Hydrology and water depletions:** Upper Basin depletions are mitigated through Section 7 consultations and the [[Section 7 Funds]] mechanism, which charged $10 per acre-foot, adjusted to $10.91 for FY-91.

## Key Findings

**Governance and reorganization**
- The Implementation Committee approved the reorganization: the Technical Group is abolished, and Biology, Water Acquisition and Information and Education committees are created. Only the Implementation Committee may create new technical committees, but the Management Committee may give interim approval for subcommittees.
- The Researchers' Advisory Group was sanctioned to work with the Biology Committee on an outreach program. The Biology Committee must document the time and resources it needs.
- Field biologists still say they lack a collective forum, and they recommended sub-basin working groups to set biological priorities and recovery goals.

**FY-91 budget and work plan**
- The recommended FY-91 budget is $2.5M, a 4.8 percent increase. Total funding with additional sources is $2,697,100: FWS $624,000; BR $1,636,500; Colorado $173,500; Utah $79,700; Wyoming $25,200; Section 7 $113,500; water users $44,700.
- Proposals totaled about $4.5M against the roughly $2.5M available. About 85 projects were submitted and ranked.
- The Implementation Committee approved the work plan pending a decision on the [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] studies, which were held until a September 10, 1990 FWS–BR meeting.
- The FWS contribution is $30,000 below the amount established for FY-91. A $150,000 increase is sought for FY-93, along with operation and maintenance funds for the Ouray propagation facility.
- Budget flexibility is shrinking because more of the annual budget is committed to ongoing projects.

**Water rights acquisition**
- The Committee approved, with modifications, a resolution to spend up to $400,000 at fair market value for the Cross Mountain water rights (Lily Park Pumps, 36.67 cfs, 1886 priority; Cross Mountain Pumps, 17.78 cfs, 1900 priority) on the [[Yampa River]].
- Condition 3 requires the [[Bureau of Land Management]] to seek congressional reimbursement if the rights are not converted to instream flows by the Colorado Water Court. The Water Court risk was judged low.
- Congress had appropriated $1 million for water acquisition in 1988, and concern existed that it remained unspent.
- The Yampa Basin feasibility study was delayed four months, with a consultant selected and a two-year timeline. The 15-mile reach water availability study ($47K, possibly up to $100K over two years) was funded.

**San Juan River and Animas-La Plata**
- The FY-89 and FY-90 San Juan management plan ($50K and $53K) was not completed as a management plan. The Committee agreed no further Recovery Program funds would go to the San Juan until related issues were resolved.
- Discussion of a separate San Juan recovery program versus inclusion in the RIP was contentious. Tom Pitts and others argued for a single Upper Basin program.

**Section 7 consultations and Flaming Gorge**
- Redlands Dam fish passage funds ($319,000 of Section 7 funds) are to be redirected to higher priority activities, subject to project-proponent approval.
- Section 7 funds available as of October 1, 1990 were projected at $523,641, including $44,516 in the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation account.
- FWS will reinitiate consultation after final spring and winter flow needs are determined.
- A BLM/FWS meeting (July 26, 1990) sought consistent small-depletion mitigation across Colorado, Utah and Wyoming. BLM will explore a prototype Green River Resource Management Plan with a lump-sum depletion payment.

**Nonnative fish and stocking**
- Utah will develop management plans for all its lakes and reservoirs, starting with those most likely to affect endangered fish habitat. FWS will review them under an agreement modeled on the Colorado arrangement.
- FWS reviewed 8 Colorado and Utah lake management proposals. It objected to channel catfish stocking in Kenney Reservoir (recommending sterile fish instead) and to saugeye introduction at Gurley Reservoir, and requested an environmental assessment before grass carp introduction.

**Overhead charges and outreach**
- The Governors of Colorado, Utah and Wyoming asked the Interior Secretary to waive the 17.65 percent FWS overhead charge on BR transfers. Charges were $50,601 (1989), $118,256 (1990) and a projected $119,100 (1991).
- The Information and Education Committee shifted the newsletter audience to the general public. A proposal to rename the Colorado squawfish is pending with the American Fisheries Society and the American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists.

**Unresolved issues**
- Recovery goals are not yet defined, and the packet notes that without them progress cannot be measured.
- Sediment subcommittee work was not prioritized. The Committee asked for the Sediment Committee to reconvene.
- Long-term facility needs for the propagation program depend on recovery goals not yet set.
- The Road Maps were approved only with the condition that they be revised and updated yearly.
