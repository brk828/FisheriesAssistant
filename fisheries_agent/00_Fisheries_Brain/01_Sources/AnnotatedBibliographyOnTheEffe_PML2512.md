# Annotated Bibliography on the Effects of Water-Level Manipulation on Lakes and Reservoirs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries #reservoir-ecology

## Executive Summary

This annotated bibliography, compiled by Triplett, Culver and Waterfield (Ohio Cooperative Fishery Research Unit, Ohio State University) under Ohio Department of Natural Resources Federal Aid Project F-57-R, catalogues published literature on how [[Water-Level Manipulation]] (drawdowns, flooding, stabilization, pulsed releases) affects [[Sport Fisheries]] and the ecological mechanisms behind them. The compilation is biased toward fish management in reservoirs and under-represents aquatic vascular plant control and western salinity problems. It contains roughly 350 numbered entries spanning 1936 to 1979, indexed by 100+ keywords, and draws on a [[Computerized Storage and Retrieval System]] built from library, [[Science Citation Index]] and agency reprint searches.

## Background & Description

**Purpose.** The parent study aimed to delineate potential applications of existing water-level manipulation techniques for Ohio reservoirs and to design evaluation studies for the most promising ones.

**Methods.** Searches covered card catalogues, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Reference Center (Denver), Water Resources Abstracts, the Ohio State mechanized information center, the Ohio DNR reprint collection and the Science Citation Index. Each entry carries author, title, citation, an abstract (author-written or prepared by the compilers) and keywords. Entries are alphabetical; a keyword index follows.

**Scope.** Most entries concern fish management; supporting references cover thermal regimes, vegetation, cover, productivity, water quality and food webs. The keyword index lists the most frequent topics, including abundance, angling, aquatic vascular plants, benthos, drawdown, largemouth bass, nutrients, production, reproduction, survival, temperature, water quality, year-class strength and [[Turbidity]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Reviewed literature links water-level regimes to fish through several pathways:

- **Food webs:** Drawdowns reduce littoral benthos and zooplankton; flooding of [[Terrestrial Vegetation]] releases nutrients and stimulates invertebrate production (e.g., [[Chironomidae]], [[Gammarus]], [[Daphnia]] shifts).
- **Spawning and nursery habitat:** Spring level changes strand eggs and nests, cause [[Nest Abandonment]] and alter spawning success for [[Northern Pike]], [[Walleye]], [[Sauger]], [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Black Crappie]].
- **Vegetation:** Stabilized levels shift flora from annuals toward perennials and reduce species richness; drawdowns control nuisance macrophytes but can cause mudflat and turbidity problems.
- **Water quality:** Drawdowns affect nutrient release, dissolved oxygen (oxygen depletion after flooding or ice-cover drawdowns), thermal stratification and turbidity.
- **Shoreline erosion:** Wave action on unprotected shorelines increases sediment input and degrades spawning gravels.
- **Population feedback:** Responses vary by species; winter drawdowns can reduce carp and sunfish while enhancing predator-prey balance.

## Key Findings

- **Drawdowns reduce or control undesirable species.** Winter and fall drawdowns reduced [[Common Carp]], [[Gizzard Shad]] and stunted sunfish in multiple reservoirs (e.g., Ridge Lake, Illinois; Lake Carl Blackwell, Oklahoma; Fort Randall, South Dakota). Carp spawning control via drawdowns was demonstrated at Fort Randall, with exposure of eggs at drawdowns of about 1.5 to 2 feet.
- **Flooding of terrestrial vegetation boosts fish production.** Flooded vegetation increased benthos (up to roughly three to four-fold in some studies), zooplankton and young-of-year survival, notably for [[Largemouth Bass]] on Bull Shoals and Beaver Reservoirs.
- **Year-class strength depends on spring conditions.** Multiple studies (Johnson 1957 on northern pike; Johnson 1961 on walleye; Nelson 1959 on sauger; Carlander and others) associate high spring water levels during spawning with strong year classes, while sudden drops strand eggs and larvae.
- **Stable levels can be harmful.** Stabilized reservoirs developed reduced fish production in several cases (Hulsey 1958; Eddy and Ruschmann reviews; Gorbunov 1962 on Russian reservoirs), while fluctuating reservoirs often showed more productive littoral zones.
- **Responses vary by species and life stage.** Largemouth bass often respond favorably to manipulation, while bluegill, crappie and catfish show mixed outcomes; many reports note that effects depend on timing (spring vs. summer or fall drawdowns).
- **Winter drawdowns can enhance angling.** Several cases (Kansas, Tennessee, Ohio, Lake Chautauqua, Lake Carl Blackwell) report improved sportfish harvest, though many studies were short term or lacked controls.
- **Cautionary results.** Some studies found no measurable effect (Stroud 1944, Beaver Reservoir, Missouri River), and others report that extreme drawdowns killed fish or caused kills of gizzard shad and bluegill (Lake Carl Blackwell, Lake Apopka).
- **Management recommendations.** Authors consistently advocate seasonal, species-specific manipulation plans, coordination among agencies (e.g., Corps of Engineers and state fish and wildlife agencies), and monitoring; Ostermann-era and Kansas programs (Gronen 1977–1979) are presented as models.
- **Scope of the bibliography.** Of the entries listed, many address Missouri River mainstem reservoirs, Tennessee Valley Authority impoundments, Oklahoma and Kansas lakes, and northern European reservoirs (Sweden, Norway, USSR). Keyword counts show largemouth bass, abundance, drawdown, survival and water quality as the most cited topics.
