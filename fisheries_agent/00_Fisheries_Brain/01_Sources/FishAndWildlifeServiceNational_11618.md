# National Recreational Fisheries Policy: First Public Review Draft (1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #recreational-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice (Vol. 53, No. 5, January 8, 1988) requests public comment on the first public review draft of a [[National Recreational Fisheries Policy]]. The [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) coordinated drafting in a multi-stakeholder forum that included federal, state, tribal, and local governments and private-sector constituency and industry groups. The Policy is a non-binding, umbrella statement of unified national principles, goals, and objectives for conserving, managing, and wisely using recreational fisheries and the resources that support them. Comments are due February 15, 1988. The final Policy is slated for adoption at a National Recreational Fisheries Conference during National Fishing Week in June 1988.

The notice also contains a separate, unrelated item: a notice of a Minerals Management Plan for Lake Mead National Recreation Area, based on that area's General Management Plan.

## Background & Description

**Scope and purpose.** The Policy applies to recreational fisheries at a national scale. Its stated purpose is to provide "a unified set of national guiding principles, goals, and objectives" for governmental (federal, state, tribal, and local) and private-sector entities. The notice stresses that the Policy "is not a Federal policy," is not a strategic or action plan, and is an umbrella document. Implementation is left to each interest, individually or cooperatively.

**Development history.**
- The drafting effort began April 29, 1987, in a meeting convened by the Service.
- An outline was distributed to more than 65 agencies, groups, and individuals. The International Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies distributed it to all 50 states.
- A final outline was produced at an October 13, 1987, meeting.
- A preliminary draft was written from that outline, and a working group then produced the public review draft.
- Drafting bodies named include the Service, National Marine Fisheries Service, U.S. Forest Service, U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, [[American Fisheries Society]], Sport Fishing Institute, National Wildlife Federation, [[Atlantic States Marine Fisheries Commission]], and [[Gulf States Marine Fisheries Commission]]. The notice states this is not an exhaustive list.

**Context stated in the Policy.** The Policy notes that nearly 60 million Americans, about one in four citizens, fish recreationally. Anglers have more than doubled in the past 30 years, and angler expenditures approach $30 billion annually. "Recreational fishing" is described as encompassing bass and trout anglers, big-game offshore anglers (marlin, tuna, sailfish), and casual anglers fishing ponds and rivers. Anglers fish for food, relaxation, and excitement.

**Contact.** Gary Edwards, Assistant Director, Fisheries, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Washington, DC.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Policy describes the resource base in broad terms. It refers to "thousands of species of finfish and shellfish" across coastlines, bays, estuaries, the Great Lakes, reservoirs, inland rivers, small ponds, and upland brooks. The document does not report species-level data, population counts, or site-specific measurements.

Key ecological concepts defined in the glossary:
- **Habitat:** the biological, physical, and chemical components constituting the fish's environment.
- **Fishery resources:** fish and the habitats on which they depend.
- **Stewardship, conservation, and wise use:** responsible management of fishery resources for public benefit.
- **Restoration** (recovering lost production) and **enhancement** (increasing production).
- **Mitigation:** avoidance, minimization, rectification, reduction, and/or compensation for adverse impacts.

The Policy emphasizes habitat tools, including [[Fish Stocking Programs]], instream and riparian habitat improvement, and [[Artificial Reefs]]. It also addresses genetic concerns, specifically wild and hatchery stock interbreeding and reductions in gene pools.

## Key Findings

The Policy is organized around four goals, each with objectives.

**Goal I: Conserve, Restore, and Enhance Fishery Resources.**
- Enhance and create fish stocks and habitat through stocking, habitat improvement, and artificial reefs.
- **Research and development:** Sustained fishing depends on sound science. Priorities include better population monitoring and trend forecasting, improved fish culture and health care, quantitative population genetics (for hatchery and wild stock interactions), standardized habitat evaluation, and refined restoration techniques. Private-sector research is encouraged.
- **Data bases:** Existing surveys should be evaluated continuously. The named surveys are the [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] Recreational Fisheries Statistics Survey and the Service's National Survey of Hunting, Fishing, and Wildlife-Associated Recreation. Surveys should add social and economic data (angler preferences, demographics, trip expenditures) and aim for comprehensiveness, timeliness, accuracy, and comparability.

**Goal II: Increase the Quality and Diversity of Recreational Fishing Opportunities.**
- **Enhance and diversify experiences:** Create fisheries in underused areas such as cities, suburbs, private waters, and government lands. Redirecting effort to underused species can relieve pressure on crowded fisheries.
- **Diversity tools:** [[Put-and-Take Fishing]], [[Catch-and-Release Fishing]], equipment limits, trophy fishing areas, and length and creel limits.
- **Non-consumptive use:** Opportunities for watching, studying, and photographing fish, visiting fish ladders, and touring hatcheries should be encouraged.
- **Access:** Increase public access while respecting private property rights. Boat ramps, fishing sites, and shore access should be considered in mitigation. The needs of children, senior citizens, and people with disabilities should be addressed.

**Goal III: Enhance Partnerships Between Governments and the Private Sector.**
- Coordinate the growing number of government agencies, councils, committees, and commissions to reduce overlap and fragmentation.
- Develop structured information exchange and joint conferences among agencies, constituency groups, and industry.
- Encourage early angler involvement in decisions affecting them.
- Use challenge grants, technical assistance, and cost sharing to promote private participation, including on public lands.
- Build public concern and participation, and improve [[Angler Ethics]], through educational programs and an angling code of ethics. The notice cites more than a century of anglers funding conservation through licenses, excise taxes, and user fees.

**Goal IV: Promote a Healthy Recreational Fishing Industry.**
- The industry's prosperity depends on healthy fishery resources, and the industry is expected to support conservation policies and projects.
- Provide a wide range of quality goods and services, marketing, and fishing information.
- Increase outreach to beginning anglers through instruction and information on opportunities and conservation.

**Conclusion.** The Policy is described as "a first step" toward a comprehensive national effort. All participating groups must translate its goals into productive action. Its goals are to provide improved fishing, more responsive and capable management, and a more informed and participatory public.
