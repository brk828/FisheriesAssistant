# Gila River Basin Native Fish Study

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

American Rivers, in collaboration with Dr. W.L. Minckley of Arizona State University, is undertaking a two-year historical and scientific study to map the presence and distribution changes of all native fish species in the [[Gila River]] basin over time. The project will incorporate existing fish occurrence data into a Geographic Information System (GIS) format to document the decline of native fishes and identify critical conservation areas. The [[Gila River]] basin holds the dubious distinction of being the only river basin in the world where all freshwater fish species comprising the native fauna are either extinct, federally listed as threatened or endangered, or recommended as candidates for federal listing.

## Background & Description

Approximately one-third of North American native fishes live in arid regions of the western continent. The [[Gila River]] basin represents a critical case study in native fish decline, with human settlement and development severely impacting aquatic systems. Land use changes have altered natural runoff patterns and increased erosion and sedimentation. Agricultural and municipal diversions have dried up or dramatically decreased seasonal flows in many streams. Dams, canals, and other structural alterations have changed natural flow regimes. The introduction of non-native species that compete for and dominate native fish habitat presents a growing problem.

A substantial historic record of native freshwater fishes exists for western North America, including the [[Gila River]] watershed, providing the foundation for this comprehensive analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The aquatic ecosystems of the [[Gila River]] basin have been fundamentally altered by multiple anthropogenic factors. Native fish species are struggling for survival in habitats modified by flow regime changes, sedimentation, and structural alterations. Non-native fishes adversely affect native fish survival either as indicators of habitat changes that allow invasion and establishment of introduced species, or through superior competitive or predatory abilities that directly destroy native populations.

The distribution of non-native fishes will be plotted alongside native species to assess their spread through time and geographic space. Changes in distributions of non-native species directly pertain to native fish ecology and conservation. The project will specially note data showing non-occurrence of species at sites where they originally occurred, documenting local extirpations.

## Key Findings

The project will produce the following outcomes and capabilities:

- **GIS-Compatible Database**: A comprehensive, documented database for freshwater fishes of the [[Gila River]] basin will be created and made available to agencies and interested parties
- **Historical Distribution Mapping**: Plotting fish locations over time on detailed drainage maps will reveal original distributions and track expansions and contractions of geographic ranges
- **Critical Habitat Identification**: The project will highlight tributaries and areas of the river system that still have significant populations of native species
- **Multi-Layer Analysis**: GIS techniques will allow transfer of data to other base maps (geologic, topographic, climatological, terrestrial vegetation) enabling evaluation of fish distributions relative to other ecosystem features
- **Management Applications**: Transfer of distributional data to maps depicting land ownership and use patterns will allow rapid assessment of biodiversity preservation challenges
- **Conservation Planning**: Potential applications include identifying which rivers, streams, or portions thereof have the habitat diversity and biotic integrity that would most benefit from aggressive management or restoration efforts
- **Conservation Unit Identification**: Results could identify conservation units necessary to assure minimum habitat size, quantity, and quality for maintenance of biodiversity within the basin

The project aims to formulate recommendations for preserving habitat critical for conserving aquatic biodiversity in the [[Gila River]] basin by learning to interpret species changes over the historic record.
