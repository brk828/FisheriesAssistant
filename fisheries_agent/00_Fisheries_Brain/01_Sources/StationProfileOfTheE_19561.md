## Executive Summary

This document is a **station profile** for the **Eagle Creek National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service anadromous fish production facility located near Estacada, Oregon, on Eagle Creek within the [[Columbia River]] basin. Established in 1956 under the **Mitchell Act**, the hatchery's mission is to mitigate anadromous salmonid losses caused by construction and operation of mainstem Columbia River dams. The facility rears and distributes **[[Coho Salmon]]** (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*) and **[[Steelhead Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) for release into Oregon waters, functioning as a compensatory mitigation hatchery rather than a research station. This profile documents infrastructure, staffing, budget, and fish distribution data for fiscal years 1991–1995 rather than species morphology or wild ecological data.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or physiological data on the reared species are provided in this source. The document is administrative/operational in nature, covering hatchery infrastructure rather than biological descriptions of [[Coho Salmon]] or [[Steelhead Trout]].

- Species reared: Coho Salmon (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*) and Steelhead Trout (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), both members of family **Salmonidae**.
- No egg or fry morphological data reported; egg and fry distribution figures for this station were **none** in FY1993.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Life history strategy:** Anadromous salmonids — spawned/reared in freshwater hatchery conditions, released as smolts/fry-to-adult stages into the [[Columbia River]] system for eventual outmigration to the North Pacific Ocean and return migration to spawn.
- **Habitat provided:** Combination hatchery/office/shop building, 75 raceways (8 ft × 80 ft production raceways), adult holding and spawning facilities, and a 70 kW hydro generator supporting station operations — an engineered freshwater rearing environment substituting for natural spawning/rearing habitat lost to dam construction.
- **Ecological role of the hatchery:** Serves as **mitigation infrastructure** compensating for habitat and population losses from mainstem Columbia River hydroelectric dam construction and operation, linking hatchery-origin fish into the broader [[Anadromous Fish Mitigation]] framework for the Columbia River Basin.
- **Distribution destination:** Fish are released into federal, other (non-federal), and state waters in Oregon; some fish are transferred to the Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife for acclimation rearing prior to release, indicating cooperative interagency stocking pathways.

## Key Findings

- **Establishment & Authority:** Founded in 1956 under the Mitchell Act; administered by USFWS Region 1; Project Leader Douglas K. Dysart (as of profile date).
- **Mission:** Mitigation of anadromous salmonid stocks lost to mainstem Columbia River dam construction/operation.
- **Species produced:** Coho Salmon and Steelhead Trout only; no egg or fry distribution reported for FY1993 (only larger juvenile/adult-stage fish distributed).
- **Fish distribution trends (excludes eggs/fry):**
  - FY1991: 3,726,699 total fish distributed (3,576,180 to "Other Waters," 150,519 to State Waters)
  - FY1992: 3,333,525 total fish distributed (3,241,993 Other Waters, 91,532 State Waters)
  - FY1993: 3,460,510 total fish distributed (1,503 FWS waters, 3,366,922 Other Waters, 92,085 State Waters)
- **Visitation:** 900 visitors recorded in FY1993, indicating modest public engagement/education role.
- **Facilities:** 75 production raceways (8×80 ft), adult holding/spawning facilities, on-site 70 kW hydro generator, and on-station housing (three duplexes) supporting a small permanent staff.
- **Staffing:** Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staffing remained stable at ~8.0 across FY1991–1995.
- **Budget trends:**
  - Operations budget rose from $390,701K (FY1991) to an estimated $430,000K (FY1995).
  - Maintenance budget rose sharply from $12,936K (FY1991) to an estimated $215,000K (FY1995), reflecting increased infrastructure investment.
  - No cost recoverables reported in any fiscal year (FY1991–1995).
- **Interagency cooperation:** Fish reared at Eagle Creek NFH were transferred to the **Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife** for acclimation rearing prior to release, illustrating a shared-responsibility stocking model within the [[Columbia River Fish Mitigation Program]].
