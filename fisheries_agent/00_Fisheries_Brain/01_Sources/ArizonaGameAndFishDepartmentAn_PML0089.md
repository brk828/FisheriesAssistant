# Arizona Game and Fish Department Regional Annual Reports (Regions II–IV, 1972–1982)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This compiled regional annual report from the Arizona Game and Fish Department spans roughly 1972 to 1982 and covers Regions II, III and IV. Its main subject is wildlife and fisheries management across the [[Colorado River]] valley, though the text is broad and addresses game species (antelope, [[Desert Bighorn Sheep]], [[Mule Deer]], [[Javelina]]), enforcement, habitat review and fish stocking. Recurring themes are personnel turnover, budget and manpower shortfalls, rapid human population growth that is converting desert habitat, and declining fawn recruitment in antelope and deer. Fisheries sections report creel census results, stocking, record fish, and fish-kill investigations on [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]] and related waters.

## Background & Description

The reports are organized by administrative region and by staff specialist (game management, fisheries, law enforcement, habitat evaluation). Each year's summary includes activity-hour tables, citation and fine totals, and survey results. The document is partly OCR-degraded and includes some administrative correspondence, but its core content is annual operational reporting.

Fish species named in the fisheries sections include:
- [[Tilapia]] (*Oreochromis mossambicus*), record fish from the Salinity Canal
- [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*), state record of 51 lb 8 oz from the Colorado River near Imperial Dam
- [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*), state record caught June 27, 1976 (reported 48 inches, weight garbled in scan)
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), with nesting surveys and stocking
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), increasingly important in creels and stocking
- [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), the primary stocked salmonid
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail Chub]], both listed or protected
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), introduced below Davis Dam for biological control of Simulid flies
- [[Northern Pike]] at Lake Mary, the primary fish managed there

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Key environmental pressures are:
- **Human population growth and land conversion:** Rapid development at Bullhead City and Lake Havasu City, residential and off-road vehicle use, and conversion of [[Mesquite Bosque]] on the Fort Mohave Indian Reservation to farmland. The report describes this as severely detrimental to small game habitat.
- **Water level and quality:** Lake Mead elevation dropped 3.05 m during the spawning period, destroying one largemouth bass nest and possibly affecting six others. Dissolved oxygen was consistently above 7.0 ppm in monitored Lake Mead columns. [[Fish Kill]] events at Alamo Lake (wind-driven turnover) and Painted Rock Lake (low water, high temperature and organic loading) are documented.
- **Water developments and canals:** The [[Central Arizona Project]] and Wellton-Mohawk Canal trapped and drowned deer. Mittry Lake dredging and tule eradication improved open water.
- **Federal regulatory changes:** Under the [[Endangered Species Act]], the USFWS restricted non-native stocking in drainages with endangered or threatened fish, reducing Lake Mohave trout stocking from 130,000 pounds to none and the below-Davis-Dam program from 300,000 pounds plus 500,000 fingerlings to 100,000 fingerlings per year.
- **Biological control:** Stocked rainbow trout below Davis Dam consumed mostly Simulid fly larvae (70% larvae, 29% adults in one stomach analysis; 95-plus percent of diet in another report).

## Key Findings

**Fisheries results**
- Creel census on Lake Mead, Lake Mohave and the Colorado River was conducted for 265 to 393 man-days across several reports, with angler contacts ranging from about 7,200 to over 10,000 per year.
- Region III catch rate was 0.40 fish/angler-hour versus 0.26 the previous year. Largemouth bass fell from 74.4% to 43.1% of the creel, channel catfish rose from 7.7% to 35.8%, and striped bass rose from 0.4% to 7.0%.
- Region IV creel census (1979–80) covered 11 areas with 2,781 anglers and a 0.77 fish/hour overall catch rate. Painted Rock Lake led at 2.56 fish/hour; Lake Havasu was lowest at 0.30.
- Striped bass checks at Temple Bar rose to 81.0% of the catch in one year, then fell to 24.1% in a later year as largemouth bass rose to 29.7%.
- Largemouth bass nesting surveys recorded 28 nests in two coves, with an estimated nest success of 52% documented and up to 89% possible.
- Rainbow trout at Katherine Landing averaged 25.4 mm smaller after stocking of smaller fish due to egg unavailability.
- Lake Mohave, the Colorado River, and Topock Marsh showed little change in overall catch rate or species composition in several surveys.
- Bonytail chubs (federally endangered) and razorback suckers were collected from Lake Mohave. Longfin dace were collected in the Truxton Wash drainage, described as the first native fish record from that area.

**Fish stocking**
- Region IV (1981–82) stocked seven species totaling roughly 120,000 fish, with channel catfish most numerous, followed by [[Redear Sunfish]], rainbow trout, [[Florida Bass]] and tilapia.
- Lake Mead received 26,599 largemouth bass (2,134 lb) in one year. Lake Mohave received 653,000+ rainbow trout (132,723 lb) in another.

**Enforcement and manpower**
- Region III reported 695 citations in one year (49% fish, 20% big game, 14% small game, 11% watercraft) and fines of $15,460.85, up 37%.
- Region IV adjudicated 1,048 charges in 1980–81, with $20,021.90 in game and fish fines and $10,098.80 in watercraft and miscellaneous fines.
- Expended manpower across Region III ranged from about 25,000 to 41,600 hours per year. Enforcement, watercraft and game management consistently ranked highest.
- Staff turnover was severe: Region IV had a nearly complete personnel turnover in 1972–73, and 21 commissioned officers were assigned to Region III in one year, with only 10 serving the full year.

**Game and habitat findings**
- Antelope fawn production declined steadily. Unit 10 fawns fell to 15.8 per 100 does (later 17.9%), and Unit 13B fawns dropped to 15.9 per 100 does in 1981. Permits were cut from 335 to 275 and later restored in response.
- Desert bighorn sheep counts reached record levels in some units (158, 15C and 15D), with 956 animals classified in 1980 and 617 in 1981.
- Deer hunt success in Region III was 23.7% in 1980, almost identical to 1979. Record low fawn survival was recorded in 1981–82.
- The proposed Lake Havasu marina and country club north of Lake Havasu City was opposed for threatened impacts to wildlife in northern Lake Havasu and Topock Gorge.
- Region III habitat review handled 240 documents in 1980–81 and 290 in 1981–82; Region IV handled 132 and 238 documents in 1980–81 and 1981–82.

**Administrative and policy issues**
- A 1976 Attorney General's Opinion clarified public access to state trust land, but a checkerboard ownership pattern left over 600 square miles of Region III closed to public hunting, although about 50% of it is state land.
- Watercraft enforcement legislation introduced in 1976 failed to pass.
- The implementation of a 40-hour work week reduced man-hours per program and made publicly complaint handling more difficult.
