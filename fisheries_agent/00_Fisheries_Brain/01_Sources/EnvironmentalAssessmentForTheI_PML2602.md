# Drachman Grazing Permit Draft Environmental Assessment: Alpine Allotment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This draft Environmental Assessment (EA) from the USDA Forest Service evaluates whether to issue a 10-year livestock grazing permit to R., R., and B. Drachman on the [[Alpine Allotment]] (about 8,000 acres) within the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]], Arizona. Three alternatives are analyzed: [[Alternative A]] (no grazing), [[Alternative B]] (current season and numbers, 92 cattle, with utilization standards), and [[Alternative C]] (stocking balanced with capacity, 59 cattle, preferred). The document focuses on soils, vegetation, riparian condition, water quality, [[Threatened and Endangered Species]], sensitive species, economics, social factors and cultural resources. Aquatic relevance centres on [[Apache Trout]] and [[Loach Minnow]] and their watersheds.

## Background & Description

- **Permittee and allotment:** The current permit (#OL2ZG) covers one allotment, the Alpine Allotment, grazed mainly May to October under a deferred system with typically 92 adult cattle. The current permit expires December 31, 1995.
- **Purpose and need:** Issue a permit that authorizes grazing at an appropriate level and timing to maintain satisfactory resource conditions, improve unsatisfactory ones, and comply with the [[Endangered Species Act]] and [[Clean Water Act]].
- **Decision maker:** The [[Forest Supervisor]] decides whether to issue the permit. A later [[Allotment Management Plan]] would be prepared by the [[District Ranger]].
- **Significant issues:** (1) Permitted livestock use should be in balance with grazing capacity; (2) grazing season should suit site-specific conditions. Scoping drew approximately 71 letters sent and 20 responses received.
- **Vegetation:** Ponderosa pine (about 3,270 acres), mixed conifer (about 3,720 acres), aspen (910 acres), and roughly 911 acres of meadow and riparian vegetation. Elevation ranges from 8,000 to 9,800 feet.
- **Capability classes:** About 3,451 acres full capacity, 1,169 acres potential capacity, and 3,397 acres no capacity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Soils and watershed.** Soil loss was modelled with the [[Universal Soil Loss Equation]] using [[Terrestrial Ecosystems Survey]] data. Satisfactory soil condition covers 81% of the allotment currently (projected 99% under Alternative A, 92% under B, 99% under C). Watershed condition is satisfactory under all alternatives, with [[Coleman Creek]] (an Apache trout stream) also rated satisfactory.

**Vegetation.** Late spring to early summer grazing has concentrated use in riparian areas and meadows, shifting composition from sedges and native cool-season grasses toward [[Kentucky Bluegrass]], rushes and forbs. Proper use is set at about 40% by weight for desirable species such as Arizona fescue and mountain muhly. Stocking must be based on usable, not total, forage.

**Riparian condition.** Only Coyote Creek was identified as riparian habitat for the permit (about 1.2 miles). Currently 100% of the allotment riparian area is unsatisfactory. Projections: Alternative A and C would move toward satisfactory conditions faster than Alternative B.

**Water quality.** The [[San Francisco River]] is listed as "limited" by the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] for fecal coliform. The allotment is not considered the cause, since pastures only drain ephemerally into the river. Best Management Practices apply to all range activity.

**Air quality.** Airsheds within the Alpine District are in attainment. No alternative is expected to degrade air quality.

**Wildlife and listed species.** Seven federally listed species (plus two with critical habitat) may be present:
- Endangered: [[Mexican Gray Wolf]], [[American Peregrine Falcon]]
- Threatened: [[Bald Eagle]], [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Loach Minnow]]
- Proposed endangered: [[Jaguar]]

Sensitive species total 54; 5 could trend toward listing if grazing exceeds capacity, including [[Northern Goshawk]], [[Northern Water Shrew]], [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]], [[Northern Leopard Frog]], and [[Narrowheaded Garter Snake]].

**Economics.** Estimated direct and indirect jobs and county payments: Alternative B provides about 8.4 jobs (with the other two permits) and $1,673 in payments to counties. Alternative C reduces jobs to 5.4. The permits account for 0.6% of Apache County's 1994 Forest Service receipts ($285,497). Cattle ranching supports about 220 direct and indirect jobs in Apache County (3.8% attributable to these permits).

**Social.** Ranching is a long-standing way of life, and the Drachman family has held permits on the Alpine Allotment since the late 1980s. Dispersed recreation (camping, hiking, fishing, hunting) occurs near riparian areas, and some recreationists object to livestock presence.

**Cultural resources.** No known standing ruins or historic structures are affected. No new facilities are authorized, and Section 106 consultation is required for future range structures.

## Key Findings

- **Preferred alternative:** [[Alternative C]] balances 59 adult cattle (July 15 to October 31) with allotment capacity, keeping utilization at 45% in riparian areas and 40% in uplands, and excluding livestock from Coleman Creek.
- **Alternative B** retains 92 cattle (May 1 to October 31) above capacity. Utilization standards may shorten the season, reduce payments and may not sustain a viable operation.
- **Alternative A** (no grazing) would produce the greatest habitat benefit in the short term but eliminates the ranching economy and social values.
- **Apache trout and loach minnow:** Alternative C is expected to improve watershed and habitat conditions; Alternative B would not adversely affect these species but would limit improvement.
- **Bald eagle:** The only active nest in the White Mountains of Arizona lies within the allotment near Luna Lake (young fledged in 1994 and 1995). The pasture with the nest tree would not be stocked during the breeding season (January to July).
- **Peregrine falcon:** One known eyrie lies within ten miles of the allotment; all alternatives are considered to comply with Recovery Task 1221.
- **Mexican spotted owl:** The allotment contains four designated management territories and 5,965 acres of critical habitat. Livestock would be removed before 40% use is exceeded, and no corrals or mineral sites would be placed in Protected Activity Centers.
- **Monitoring:** A paired-plot utilization monitoring plan, with at least two cages per pasture, will guide pasture moves; a 10-year term includes a comprehensive analysis after three years.
- **Public response:** About 71 letters were sent in scoping and 20 responses received. Twenty-four livestock permittees and 12 individuals were contacted, and 23 individuals or groups responded.
