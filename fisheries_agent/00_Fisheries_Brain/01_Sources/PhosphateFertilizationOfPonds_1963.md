## Executive Summary
This paper by Swingle, Gooch, and Rabanal (1963) synthesizes 20 years of Auburn University research on pond fertilization in the Southeastern United States. The study demonstrates that in ponds with a long history (15+ years) of N-P-K (Nitrogen-Phosphorus-Potassium) fertilization, nitrogen and potassium supplementation could be omitted without significant loss in fish production, provided phosphate was maintained. The research attributes this to residual nitrogen fixation by bacteria/algae and stored organic matter in bottom muds. The paper concludes that phosphate is the primary limiting nutrient for [[Phytoplankton]] production in mature ponds, and that phosphate-only fertilization is a cost-effective strategy (reducing costs by ~65%) for maintaining fish yields and controlling submersed aquatic weeds and mosquito populations.

## Background & Description
*Note: This paper is a pond management and limnology study; it does not focus on the morphological characteristics of the fish species themselves but rather on their production yields and interaction with the environment.*
- **Test Species Life Stages:** Fish were stocked as fingerlings/juveniles in February/March and harvested (drained) by November, indicating a growth period of approximately 210–272 days.
- **Stocking Densities:** Varied significantly by species and study year, ranging from 500 [[Common Carp]] per acre to 2,800 [[Channel Catfish]] per acre.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Trophic Dynamics:** The study reinforces the principle that fish production is directly correlated with [[Plankton Production]], establishing a foundational food chain: Fertilizer (N-P-K) → Phytoplankton/Algae → Zooplankton/Fish Food Organisms → Fish Biomass.
- **Weed Control Mechanism:** Fertilization induces a "shading effect" via phytoplankton blooms, which blocks sunlight and prevents the growth of rooted submersed aquatics (e.g., *Pithophora*, duckweed, bladderwort). This is an ecological control mechanism rather than a chemical herbicide.
- **Mosquito Vector Control:** The presence or absence of rooted aquatic vegetation is identified as the primary factor determining habitat suitability for *Anopheles quadrimaculatus* larvae (malaria vector). Clear, weed-free water managed via fertilization eliminates this niche.
- **Biological Control Agents:** [[Gambusia affinis]] (mosquitofish) and juvenile [[Bluegill]] are noted as effective biological predators of mosquito larvae, but *only* in the absence of emergent/submersed vegetation which provides refuge for larvae.
- **Nitrogen Fixation:** In mature ponds, bacteria and algae in the bottom substrate perform nitrogen fixation, utilizing stored carbohydrates and organic matter as an energy source, thereby naturally sustaining the nitrogen cycle without artificial input.
- **Algal Succession:** Cold-water filamentous algae dominate in late winter/early spring (shading out rooted weeds), followed by a succession to warm-water planktonic algae as temperatures rise.

## Key Findings
1. **Nitrogen Omission:** In ponds previously fertilized for 15 years, omitting Nitrogen (using 0-8-2 instead of 8-8-2) resulted in **no statistically significant difference** in fish production for [[Carp]], [[Goldfish]], or [[Channel Catfish]].
2. **Phosphate is Limiting:** Omitting Phosphate (0-0-0 treatment) caused a significant decrease in production across all four years of the study, confirming phosphate as the primary limiting nutrient in these aquatic systems.
3. **Residual Fertility:** Ponds with a history of fertilization that received *no* fertilizer during the test period did not drop to "never-fertilized" baseline production levels until the third and fourth years, indicating a 2-year buffer of residual fertility in the bottom muds.
4. **Stocking Density Impact:** Increasing channel catfish stocking density from 600 to 2,800 fish/acre drastically increased net production efficiency in fertilized ponds (e.g., +155.3 lbs/acre in 0-8-2 ponds), highlighting the importance of matching biomass to food availability created by fertilization.
5. **Comparative Yield Data (Table 1):**
    - **Carp (1959):** 0-0-0: 275.2 lbs/acre vs. 8-8-2: 848.8 lbs/acre.
    - **Channel Catfish (1962):** 0-0-0: 58.9 lbs/acre vs. 8-8-2: 310.9 lbs/acre.
6. **Cost Efficiency:** Utilizing phosphate-only fertilization (via triple superphosphate) reduced material costs by approximately 65% compared to standard 8-8-2 fertilizer while maintaining comparable fish yields in 8 of 14 test ponds.
7. **Delayed Response Risk:** A drawback of phosphate-only fertilization was a delayed plankton response (30–60 days) in some ponds, creating a window for the undesirable growth of the filamentous alga *Pithophora*.
8. **Species-Specific Algal Blooms:** Contrary to findings by Prowse (1961) in other regions, phosphate-only fertilization in Alabama ponds did not prevent nuisance blooms of *Microcystis* and led to dominance by *Anabaena*, rather than promoting diatoms.
