# Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council, 1992: Abstracts on Desert Fish Biology and Conservation

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This is the front matter and abstract section of the *Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council* for 1992, edited by Dean A. Hendrickson of the [[Texas Memorial Museum]] and published 30 March 1993 by the [[Desert Fishes Council]]. The available text covers three papers: a histological study of the digestive tract of *Gila atripinnis*, a radiotelemetry study of [[Razorback Sucker]] movement and habitat use in the [[Verde River]], Arizona, and a survey of [[Rio Grande Sucker]] and [[Rio Grande Chub]] distribution on the [[Carson National Forest]], New Mexico. The shared theme is the conservation and ecology of native freshwater fishes in arid western North America.

## Background & Description

The volume is a collection of research presented to the Desert Fishes Council, a body focused on the fishes of arid and semi-arid regions. The document provides bilingual (English/Spanish) abstracts for each paper.

- **Histological study (*Gila atripinnis*):** The aim was to describe the digestive tract histologically and relate its structure to feeding habits. Gross anatomy was described, and standard histological techniques were applied to sections stained with H&E (hematoxylin and eosin). The digestive plan resembles that of other vertebrate groups. The liver and pancreas suggested high metabolic activity directed toward energy acquisition, which the authors said was supported by experimental observations. The abstract gives no sample size.
- **Razorback Sucker radiotelemetry:** Eighteen fish over 355 mm total length were implanted with radiotransmitters and released into the Verde River between May 1991 and March 1992. Monitoring ran from May 1991 to May 1992. The authors are from the Arizona Game and Fish Department, Research Branch, in Flagstaff.
- **Catostomus plebeius and Gila pandora survey:** The first year of a two-year field study began in June 1992 on the Carson National Forest. The authors are from the USDA Forest Service in Taos, New Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Each study addresses a different aspect of freshwater fish ecology in arid landscapes:

- **Habitat selection:** The razorback sucker study found a preference for pool and glide habitats. The Rio Grande survey found that both study species occupied gradients of 2% or less over cobble, rubble or gravel. Within that habitat, *Gila pandora* preferred pools and *Catostomus plebeius* selected riffle/run areas.
- **Species interactions:** *Gila pandora* and *Catostomus plebeius* were found alongside trout (*Salmo trutta*, *Oncorhynchus mykiss*, *Oncorhynchus clarki virginalis*), brook trout (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), minnows (*Pimephales promelas*, *Platygobio gracilis*) and [[Common White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersonii*). The white sucker is described as non-native to the Rio Grande drainage.
- **Competition concern:** Where large numbers of *Catostomus commersonii* occurred, *Catostomus plebeius* was absent or present in low numbers. The authors say further research is needed to determine whether the white sucker is negatively affecting the native species.
- **Land management:** The Rio Grande survey is a Forest Service study, and its findings bear on [[Native Fish Conservation]] and habitat management in national forest waters.

## Key Findings

**Histology of the digestive tract (*Gila atripinnis*)**
- The digestive tract follows the general vertebrate structural plan.
- The liver and pancreas indicate high metabolic activity linked to energy acquisition.

**Razorback Sucker movement (Verde River)**
- 18 radio-tagged fish, all over 355 mm total length, were released from May 1991 to March 1992.
- Mean downstream movement rate was 1.11 km per week.
- Mean upstream movement rate was 0.76 km per week.
- Movement differed by time of day: fish were more active at night than during daylight hours.
- Fish preferentially used pool and glide habitats.

**Distribution of Rio Grande Sucker and Rio Grande Chub (Carson National Forest)**
- Both species were confined to the [[Rio Grande Drainage]].
- *Gila pandora* was sampled at 24 sites (n = 536) across seven watersheds. Two of these watersheds were not in the historical record.
- *Catostomus plebeius* was sampled at 14 sites (n = 149) across four watersheds. Historical records place it in six watersheds.
- *Catostomus plebeius* was not found in El Rito Creek or the Rio Grande del Rancho, although historical records report its presence there.
- Subjective assessment: *Gila pandora* appears stable, while *Catostomus plebeius* appears to be declining.
- Both species favored gradients of 2% or less and cobble, rubble or gravel substrates.
