# General Hydrology Support for the Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program FY-93

#ichthyology #endangered-species #hydrology #habitat-restoration #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This FY-93 annual report documents hydrological support activities provided by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Water Resources Division for the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]]. The project, ongoing since 1988, provides critical water temperature data collection, flow quantification, gage maintenance, and water rights acquisition support for endangered fish recovery efforts in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Activities during FY-93 were significantly influenced by high spring flows that both challenged operations (two thermographs lost) and provided opportunities for expanded high-flow monitoring and geomorphology studies. The program supports multiple RIPRAP (Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan) activities including flow and habitat restoration for [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]].

## Background & Description

The Division of Water Resources (DWR) provides comprehensive hydrological support for the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]], which aims to recover endangered fish species in the Colorado River Basin. The program operates thermographs at six locations on the [[Colorado River]] and nine locations on the [[Green River]] to collect water temperature data. This data supports water temperature modeling and research projects critical to understanding habitat requirements for endangered species.

The project involves adaptation of the HYDROSS hydrology model for the Colorado and Green Rivers to run on workstations, coordination with the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] on a Colorado River Decision Support System model, and incorporation of data from the [[Yampa River]] Feasibility Study. The program maintains gaging stations, including a critical gage at the head of the [[15-Mile Reach]] near Palisade, Colorado, and works with the U.S. Geological Survey, Bureau of Reclamation, and Colorado Division of Wildlife on gage operations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery program addresses flow and habitat restoration needs for endangered fish in the Colorado River Basin. Water temperature is a critical ecological parameter, with temperature data collection designed to establish long-term records for trend analysis. Temperature data from the recorder below [[Razorback Bar]] on the Green River was successfully used in mid-May to identify the beginning of [[Razorback Sucker]] spawning, enabling crews to be dispatched for spring sampling work.

The program supports identification and hydrologic profiling of wetlands and flooded bottomlands on the Colorado and Green Rivers, including [[Escalante Wetland]] and Old Charly Wash. Automatic cameras and water stage recorders were installed at wetland inlets to monitor flows that would flood these critical habitats. This work was coordinated with Reclamation and the Vernal CRFP office and presented to the [[Flaming Gorge]] Coordinating Committee.

Flow management from major facilities including [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] and the [[Aspinall Unit]] is coordinated to support endangered fish studies during spring runoff and post-runoff periods. The program also addresses water rights acquisition and passage at diversions including Redlands, Price-Stubs, and Government Highlands to ensure adequate flows for endangered species.

## Key Findings

**Temperature Data Collection (FY-93):**
- High spring flows resulted in loss of two thermographs: one at [[Escalante Refuge]] on the [[Gunnison River]] (washed out) and one at the mouth of the [[Yampa River]]
- Data was collected at these locations between early May (installation) and late June (loss)
- Thermograph below [[Razorback Bar]] on Green River was damaged in late September when a beaver bit the probe cable
- All other thermographs were successfully recovered and data downloaded
- Temperature database program was updated with improved accessibility and multiple graphic output formats
- Temperature data successfully used to identify beginning of [[Razorback Sucker]] spawning in mid-May, enabling timely crew dispatch

**Hydrology Modeling:**
- Participated in Colorado River Decision Support System model development with [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]]
- Data from [[Yampa River]] Feasibility Study incorporated into HYDROSS model
- Environmental baseline for Yampa River updated with newer data
- Attended numerous Technical Steering Committee meetings
- Provided Yampa River diversion depletions and return flow information to enable model calibration

**Flooded Bottomlands Study:**
- Installed automatic cameras and water stage recorders at inlets of [[Escalante Wetland]] and Old Charly Wash
- Monitored daily flow and stage at each location
- Results presented to [[Flaming Gorge]] Coordinating Committee in September

**15-Mile Reach Monitoring:**
- Provided contract assistance for gage operation and maintenance near Palisade, Colorado
- Second temporary gage installed at lower end of 15-mile reach for third consecutive year
- Information to be used supporting instream flow application for return flows in the 15-mile reach

**Flow Coordination:**
- Coordinated endangered fish study releases from [[Flaming Gorge]] during spring runoff and post-runoff periods
- Coordinated releases from [[Aspinall Unit]] to coincide with spring peak and facilitate biological studies
- 1993 coordination limited because runoff was so large the system operated at capacity for several months
- Participated in quarterly operation meetings for Aspinall Unit

**High Flow Work Coordination:**
- Coordinated and contracted for 1993 high flow hydrology, geomorphology, and aerial reconnaissance work
- Activities included: (1) Air video reconnaissance of Green River; (2) Oblique 35mm slides of Green River; (3) [[Razorback Bar]] high flow and post-high flow channel monitoring; (4) [[Yampa Canyon]] post-high flow channel monitoring

**Water Rights Acquisition:**
- Worked with water right specialist to gather information on water rights and stored water
- Developed scopes of work for 15-mile reach return flows analysis and [[Steamboat Lake]] appraisal
- Long-term water lease contract developed and negotiated with Colorado Division of Parks and Recreation for water from Steamboat Lake
- Worked to secure fish passage at Redlands, Price-Stubs, and Government Highlands diversions
- Negotiated with Reclamation on 1-year and 5-year contracts for additional water from [[Ruedi Reservoir]]

**Ruedi Reservoir Releases:**
- Prepared report to [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] and Aspen/Pitkin County Water and Power Authority on benefits of Ruedi releases for endangered fish
- 1-year contract implemented; 5-year contract placed on hold pending Reclamation resolution of long-term contracting issues
- Developed estimate of storage water necessary to fulfill flow recommendations for 15-mile reach

**Budget:**
- Funds Provided: $77,000
- Funds Expended: $77,000
- Difference: $0

**Program Recommendations:**
Work in 1993 was greatly influenced by high runoff conditions throughout the basin, causing some work to expand at the expense of lower priority items. The program demonstrated ability to coordinate extensive high flow hydrology work and participate in field work with Service biologists. Extreme events require priority adjustments and readiness to field crews quickly and continuously.
