## Executive Summary

This document set comprises administrative and technical correspondence for the **Cui-ui Recovery Team Meeting** (August 9-10, 1994, Reno, NV), organized by Tom Strekal (Bureau of Indian Affairs). The core scientific content is a Bureau of Reclamation technical review, "Review of Pyramid Lake Inflow Data Used for 1992 Cui-ui Recovery Plan," which critically reassesses the hydrological assumptions underpinning the [[Cui-Ui Recovery Plan]] (1992). The [[Cui-Ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), an endangered lake sucker endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, requires specific inflow volumes to persist over a 200-year stochastic modeling horizon. The review challenges the original plan's approach of using a fixed water acquisition target, recommending instead a long-term average inflow target framework, and it quantifies how much of the projected water need stems from assumed future drought conditions versus historical baseline flows.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphological data on the [[Cui-Ui]] (body length, weight, meristic counts, coloration). The document instead references demographic/population structural data collected during recovery monitoring, including:
- **Adult female population estimates** (used as the key demographic metric in population viability modeling; initial assumed population = 150,000 adult females).
- **Year-class structure** of the current population (to be reported by Scoppettone at the meeting).
- **Larvae survival rate** used in modeling: 0.002 (0.2%), a critical life-history parameter in the Cui-ui Population Index Model.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: [[Cui-Ui]] is endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], a terminal (endorheic) desert lake in the Lahontan Basin, Nevada, fed primarily by the [[Truckee River]].
- **Spawning migration**: The species undergoes an annual spawning run from Pyramid Lake up the lower Truckee River; spawning sites and run timing are monitored yearly.
- **Critical infrastructure for life cycle**: Fish passage and habitat are mediated by constructed structures including the [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]], the [[Truckee River]] delta, the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], and the [[Numana Dam]] fish ladder.
- **Water dependency**: Population persistence is tightly linked to long-term average annual inflow to Pyramid Lake. Base condition (historical, 1901-1989-derived stochastic) average annual inflow ≈ 400,000–437,130 acre-feet; recovery modeling suggests ~470,000 acre-feet average annual inflow needed for population persistence under immediate water acquisition.
- **Flow-spawning interaction**: The population model incorporates a negative relationship between certain high-flow conditions and cui-ui spawning success — i.e., excessively high inflow can suppress recruitment, producing non-monotonic responses to supplemental water (documented in the "maximum trace" case study, Table 2).
- **Threats/conflicts**: Water management conflicts between multiple stakeholders — [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Truckee-Carson Irrigation District]], [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]], and the Town of Truckee — directly affect flow regimes critical to cui-ui habitat, framed under legislative implementation of P.L. 101-618 (TROA, OCAP, WRAP).

## Key Findings

- **Recovery Plan Water Need Estimates (1992 Plan)**: Delisting cui-ui requires **70,000 acre-feet** of additional annual inflow to Pyramid Lake (immediate acquisition scenario) or **110,000 acre-feet** (phased acquisition at 5,000 acre-feet/year). Reclassification (endangered→threatened) requires **40,000 acre-feet**.
- **Baseline inflow assumptions**: The 1992 plan assumed a base condition average annual inflow of ~400,000 acre-feet; the reviewers found the true 200-trace average/median base condition inflow to be **437,130 / 439,740 acre-feet** — indicating the recovery plan's "critical" 30 traces (Table 2, avg. 400,520 / median 397,550 ac-ft) were drawn from unrepresentatively dry stochastic scenarios.
- **Stochastic modeling design**: 200 stochastic traces of 200 years of monthly streamflow data were generated by Bureau of Reclamation (Denver) and run through the Truckee-Carson water operations model; inflow distribution across traces was approximately normal (mean 437.13, std dev ~32.77 thousand acre-feet).
- **Drought attribution**: An estimated **~37,000 of the 70,000 acre-feet** delisting requirement is attributable to assumed future long-term droughts drier than the historical 1901-1989 record, rather than an intrinsic species requirement — a critical distinction for water management policy.
- **Flow-target vs. acquisition-target critique**: The reviewers found the recovery plan's approach of applying a fixed supplemental water volume (e.g., 80,000 ac-ft) regardless of trace conditions produces illogical outcomes — e.g., in a wet trace (526,740 ac-ft base), adding supplemental water actually *decreased* the modeled adult female cui-ui population at 200 years (2,063,960 vs. 2,227,611 without supplementation) due to negative high-flow effects on spawning.
- **Recommended methodology change**: Use long-term average inflow *targets* (candidate values: 440,000 / 460,000 / 480,000 acre-feet) rather than fixed supplemental water volumes, calculating needed supplementation as the shortfall between a trace's base condition average and the target, with probability of 200-year persistence (>0 adult female population) calculated per target.
- **Persistence criterion**: The recovery plan defines delisting readiness as demonstrating ≥0.95 probability of species persistence over 200 years, a high-probability threshold that skews the modeling analysis toward the driest stochastic traces (bottom 15% of the 200 traces).
- **Case-specific modeling parameter**: Larvae survival rate fixed at 0.002 in the Cui-ui Index Model used to generate Table 2 results.
