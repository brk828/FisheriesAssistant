# Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center FY1998 Annual Plan

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #ecosystem-management #adaptive-management #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document presents the Fiscal Year 1998 Annual Monitoring and Research Plan for the [[Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center]] (GCMRC), representing the first year of implementation of the Long-Term Monitoring and Research Strategic Plan (1998-2002). The plan addresses monitoring and research requirements under the [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]] (GCPA) and Glen Canyon Dam EIS (GCDEIS) to evaluate effects of dam operations on natural, recreational, and cultural resources in [[Grand Canyon National Park]] and [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]]. The study area encompasses approximately 293 river miles from the forebay of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to the lower boundary of Grand Canyon National Park. The plan integrates physical, biological, cultural, and socioeconomic resource programs within an adaptive management framework, with a proposed budget of approximately $7 million. Major program areas include conceptual modeling, physical resources (water and sediment), biological resources (native and non-native fish, riparian vegetation, threatened and endangered species), cultural resources, socioeconomic resources, and information technologies.

## Background & Description

The GCMRC was established to implement monitoring and research programs ensuring [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations comply with Section 1802 of the GCPA. The mission encompasses evaluating short- and long-term ecosystem resource impacts of dam operations and addressing information needs specified by the Adaptive Management Work Group (AMWG) and the Secretary of the Interior. The physical scope includes the [[Colorado River]] mainstem corridor and associated riparian and terrace zones, with limited investigations into tributaries such as the [[Little Colorado River]] and [[Paria River]]. Research scope includes resource impacts of dam operations up to inundation levels associated with flows of 100,000 cubic feet per second (cfs).

Stakeholder information needs were developed through workshops held between May and October 1996, involving approximately 20 stakeholders across eight six-hour sessions. Nine resource areas were identified: hydropower, water, sediment, fish and aquatic biology, vegetation, threatened and endangered species, terrestrial wildlife, cultural resources, and recreation. The Strategic Plan addresses monitoring and research for a five-year period (1998-2002), with annual plans drafted each April following AMWG review and Secretary of the Interior approval.

Independent reviews of past research concluded that several issues need addressing: (1) development of adaptive management and science processes, (2) development of conceptual models of Grand Canyon riverine ecosystems, (3) extensive synthesis of baseline resource conditions and changes associated with dam construction and operations, (4) ecosystem analyses to understand critical attributes and interdependencies, and (5) development of predictive models under differing dam operating criteria.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River]] ecosystem in Glen and Grand Canyons was drastically altered by construction of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] in 1963. Dam operations affect water temperature, sediment transport, flow regimes, and habitat availability throughout the system. The aquatic food base (algae and macroinvertebrates) forms the foundation of the ecosystem, supporting both native and non-native fish populations. Native fish species of concern include [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*). Non-native species of interest as predators/competitors include several species of Salmonidae, Ictaluridae, Cyprinidae, and Percichthyidae.

The riparian vegetation is composed of three distinctive communities: (1) upper riparian zone (URZ), (2) lower riparian zone (LRZ), and (3) hydro-riparian wetland community. These communities provide critical habitat for fish and wildlife, including threatened and endangered species such as the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*) and [[Kanab Ambersnail]] (*Oxyloma haydeni kanabensis*). The trout fishery in the Lees Ferry reach (25 km below Glen Canyon Dam) has become an important recreational resource due to high water clarity, coarse substrates, increased benthic standing biomass, and reduced water temperatures following dam closure.

Sediment resources are critical to ecosystem health. The main concern is whether the system receives sufficient sand and finer sediment from tributaries below the dam to maintain a positive sediment budget. If sediment supplies are inadequate, physical degradation of the ecosystem will gradually occur. The 1996 Beach/Habitat-Building Test Flow (experimental flood) provided data on sandbar dynamics, backwater rejuvenation, and impacts to various resources under high-flow conditions.

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Budget:**
- FY98 budget proposed at approximately $6.9-7.0 million total
- Approximately $5.0 million directed to monitoring and research programs
- Approximately $0.5 million for Upper Colorado Region BOR adaptive management program administration
- Approximately $1.5 million for GCMRC administrative programming
- Budget allocations: BOR Administration ($350K), USGS Overhead ($220K), Personnel ($1,200K), Information Technologies ($425K), Logistics ($450K), Operations ($500K), Biological Resources ($1,300K), Physical Resources ($950K), Cultural Resources ($1,100K), Socioeconomic Resources (included in totals)

**Conceptual Modeling Approach:**
- Conceptual model will be developed using modeling workshop process involving scientists and stakeholders
- "Looking outward matrix" approach to specify resources, attributes, and linkages between resources
- Two modeling workshops planned: initial workshop to develop explicit relationships among attributes, second workshop (3-6 months later) to refine model and develop strategic simulation model
- Model will identify key information gaps and suggest priorities for long-term monitoring and research
- Continued refinement over the five-year strategic plan period

**Physical Resources Program:**
- Continuation of streamflow and sediment monitoring at key locations: Glen Canyon Dam, Lees Ferry, Paria River, mainstem above LCR confluence, LCR near Cameron, Grand Canyon near Phantom Ranch, Havasu Creek near Supai, Diamond Creek
- GCMRC will maintain database for unit value streamflow data from all gaging stations
- Development of system-wide sediment budget is primary goal
- Critical unanswered questions relate to sediment inputs from ungaged tributaries, contributions from Paria River, and sediment budget of Marble Canyon
- Monitoring downsized and focused on reaches where sediment supply is most limited and where physical-resource issues are critical to other vital resources
- New integrated program to merge sandbar mapping, campsite area monitoring, and cross-section surveys
- Synthesis activities to interpret past research on river geomorphology, sandbar stability, and sediment transport
- Lake Mead delta resurvey planned using modern GPS-based protocol for comparison with 1962-63 survey

**Biological Resources Program:**
- FY98 program funded at FY97 levels, requiring careful evaluation of monitoring and research efforts
- Aquatic food base monitoring to focus on community structure, density, distribution, and linkages between nutrient levels, water quality, and community structure
- Native fish (especially [[Humpback Chub]]) monitoring to address spawning, larval transport, recruitment, habitat availability and use, food availability and diet, competitive and predator-prey interactions with non-native fish
- Data integration project on endangered and other native fish to identify factors limiting reproduction, development, recruitment, or survival
- PIT tag database development for population monitoring
- Trout fishery monitoring in Lees Ferry reach to focus on growth, survivorship, population structure, and contribution from natural reproduction
- Synthesis of existing trout fishery information and response to alternative dam operations
- Riparian vegetation monitoring to emphasize community structure, diversity, density, native and non-native species dynamics, habitat integrity
- Limited avifauna monitoring focused on [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] nesting population and general riparian avifauna (waterfowl, riparian obligate species, resident non-obligate species, migrant species)
- [[Kanab Ambersnail]] activities to address Biological Opinion and Recovery Plan requirements, including age-class distributions, food availability, habitat requirements

**Cultural Resources Program:**
- Four main activity areas proposed: (1) synthesize existing monitoring data, (2) develop risk assessment for cultural resources from varying flow regimes, (3) develop tribal programs to assess resource impacts, (4) develop appropriate data systems and technology
- Accommodation of Programmatic Agreement (PA) activities as requested by PA parties who are AMWG members
- Stage-to-discharge relationships at selected resource locations to determine inundation frequency and critical threshold levels
- Evaluation of geomorphic processes linking sediment loss, arroyo cutting, bank failure, and archaeological site erosion
- Investigation of high flows' ability to stabilize predam terrace deposits
- Tribal programs include Hualapai ethnobotanical resource assessment, tribal GIS resource mapping, student intern program
- Development of protocols for confidential and restricted cultural resource data
- Workshops to address tribal and agency concerns for appropriate treatment and storage of sensitive data

**Socioeconomic Resources Program:**
- FY98 emphasis on synthesis of campsite beach changes through time associated with differing flow regimes
- Research on user preferences, attitudes, and wilderness experience relative to differing flow regimes
- Monitoring of trout angler use and satisfaction through creel census and cooperative programs with fishing guides and Trout Unlimited
- Evaluation of monitoring protocols for long-term assessments of camping beach change
- Information needs include wilderness experience criteria, adequate beach quality for camping, safe power craft navigability, flow regimes to maintain 100,000 adult trout (age class II plus), waterfowl and wildlife use patterns

**Information Technologies Program:**
- Development of protocols for data collection, processing, and use
- Development of extensive databases across all resources and database management system (DBMS)
- Development of robust geographic information system (GIS) with multiple layers for all resources
- Development of databases for remotely sensed data
- Stakeholder direct access to selected data in DBMS and GIS
- Outreach programs to transport data and information to stakeholders
- Maintenance of unit value streamflow database
- Revision of Meta-data protocols and DBMS protocols to expand system capability
- Development of home page access for stakeholders
- Training programs for stakeholders on GCMRC DBMS and ARC view for GIS data
- Confidentiality protocols for archaeological-site data, tribal sensitive information, and endangered species locations

**Implementation Schedule:**
- May 15, 1997: Release of RFPs
- July 1, 1997: Receipt of Proposals
- August 1, 1997: Award of Proposals and Release of Logistics Plan
- September 1, 1997: Approval of NPS Permits
- April 1, 1998: First Progress Report due
- July 1, 1998: Second Progress Report due
- September 1, 1998: Final Draft Report due
- October 1, 1998: Final Report due with all contract deliverables

**Transition from FY96-97 Program:**
- Several TMRP projects to be discontinued after October 1, 1997, including sand-transport algorithm completion, rapid sandbar erosion processes study, Lake Powell water quality assessment, channel-bed form changes report, backwater rejuvenation evaluation, and initial integration reports
- Remaining TMRP components to be restructured to improve data integration across disciplines, create more efficient monitoring, provide data sets relatable to past efforts, and better meet stakeholder information needs
- Competitive request for proposals process to be used for FY98 monitoring and research studies
- Monies from terminated activities to be reallocated to conceptual modeling, synthesis, and new monitoring and research activities
