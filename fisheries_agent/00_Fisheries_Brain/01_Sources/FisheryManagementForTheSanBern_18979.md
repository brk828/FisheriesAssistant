# Fishery Management for the San Bernardino Ranch

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 memorandum from Bill Silvey to Al Essbach presents tentative fishery management recommendations for [[San Bernardino Ranch]] based on observations from 1976-1979. The property supports multiple native fish species of the [[Yaqui River]] drainage, including [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), [[Yaqui Shiner]] (*Notropis formosus mearnsi*), [[Yaqui Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis senoriensis*), [[Yaqui Sucker]] (*Catostomus bernardini*), and [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*). The document recommends extensive biological inventory prior to habitat alteration, fish renovation to remove exotic species, pond restoration, and reintroduction of native species complexes including [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*) and [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum pricei*). The property also contains an undescribed *Fontelicella* snail species known only from two springs on the ranch.

## Background & Description

[[San Bernardino Ranch]] is located adjacent to the international boundary between Arizona and Mexico. The property contains multiple spring-fed ponds, artesian wells, and stream habitats that historically supported a diverse native fish fauna from the [[Yaqui River]] drainage system. The ranch features at least five major pond systems (Pond 1-5), nine artesian well complexes (A1-A9), and [[Black Draw]], a stream with perennial flow. Water sources include multiple springs, both capped and uncapped artesian wells, and natural seepage areas creating cienega-type wetland habitats.

The native fish assemblage documented includes five species of recent or present occurrence: [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Shiner]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Sucker]], and [[Longfin Dace]] (Minckley, 1973). Adjacent downstream habitats formerly supported [[Yaqui Catfish]] and [[Mexican Stoneroller]] populations. The property also harbors an undescribed new species of *Fontelicella* snail known only from two springs on the ranch (Landye, 1973).

Exotic species documented on the property include [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Black Bullhead]] (referred to as "mudcats"), and [[Bullfrog]] (*Rana catebeiana*). These invasive species pose threats to native fauna and require eradication.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[San Bernardino Ranch]] represents a unique ecosystem at the northern extent of the [[Yaqui River]] drainage, supporting endemic fish species found nowhere else in Arizona. The property's multiple spring sources and artesian wells create diverse aquatic habitats ranging from lentic pond systems to lotic stream environments, providing opportunities to support the full spectrum of native Yaqui ichthyofauna.

Habitat degradation has occurred through multiple mechanisms. [[Typha]] (cattail) invasion has significantly reduced open water in several ponds, with Pond 3 losing approximately 50% of its former surface area. Livestock use has eliminated peripheral emergent vegetation around some impoundments. Recent burning of cattail growth by the landowner may have destroyed fish populations in Pond 2.

The presence of exotic predators, particularly [[Bullfrog]], poses significant threats to native fish populations. Exotic fish species compete with and prey upon native species, necessitating complete fish renovation in affected systems. The property's location on the international boundary creates management challenges, as some water systems flow directly into Mexico.

The undescribed *Fontelicella* snail species represents a conservation priority, with type localities located at springs adjacent to the south periphery of Pond 1. Any chemical treatment for fish renovation must exercise extreme caution to avoid impacts to these endemic invertebrates.

## Key Findings

**Priority Management Needs:**
- Extensive biological inventory of both terrestrial and aquatic fauna required prior to any habitat alteration
- Baseline data accumulation on overall biotic community, both past and present, identified as priority direction
- Overall faunal complex of the area virtually unknown at present

**Pond 1 (2 surface acres, ranch headquarters):**
- Multi-spring sources around periphery and within basin
- Only [[Yaqui Topminnow]] observed during 1979 survey despite landowner reports of [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], topminnow, and mudcats
- Recommend complete fish renovation and conversion to [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Shiner]], and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] complex
- Renovation difficult due to multi-spring sources; drawdown would facilitate chemical treatment
- Extreme caution required due to *Fontelicella* n. spp. type localities at springs on south periphery

**Pond 2 (formerly 15 x 150 feet):**
- Fed by uncapped artesian well (A1)
- Requires extensive backhoe or dragline operation to restore depth and surface area lost to [[Typha]] invasion
- No fish observed during recent survey; landowner burnt cattail growth recently, possibly destroying fish
- Recommend dredging to former size at 4-5 foot depth and restocking with [[Yaqui Topminnow]] and [[Yaqui Chub]]

**Pond 3 Complex (two impoundments):**
- Larger pond formerly 150 x 200 feet; extensive [[Typha]] invasion reduced open water to approximately 50% of former size
- Fed by capped artesian well with pump (A4)
- No fish noted during recent survey; exotic [[Bluegill]] and [[Black Bullhead]] recorded previously
- [[Bullfrog]] (*Rana catebeiana*) presence noted in 1979 survey
- Smaller impoundment approximately 20 x 20 feet; tadpoles (presumably *R. catebeiana*) present
- Recommend dredging large impoundment to eliminate [[Typha]], fish renovation on both ponds to eliminate exotics
- System capable of being dried to facilitate renovation
- Active continuous bullfrog eradication required to control exotic predator
- Restock with [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Yaqui Shiner]], [[Yaqui Chub]], and [[Yaqui Topminnow]]

**Pond 4 (150 x 50 feet stock tank):**
- Fed by capped artesian well (A8)
- Void of peripheral emergent vegetation due to livestock usage
- No fish noted in 1979 survey
- Recommend periphery planting with native semi-aquatic/aquatic vegetation excluding [[Typha]]
- Stock with [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Shiner]], and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] complex

**Pond 5 (15 x 15 feet):**
- Fed by capped artesian well (A9)
- Void of fish in 1979 survey; limited potential
- Recommend periphery planting with native vegetation exclusive of [[Typha]] and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] stocking
- Maintain artesian water supply

**A2-A3 Complex:**
- Two capped artesian wells with basal seepage creating minor ponded water
- Tailwater flows immediately across international boundary into Mexico
- Supported substantial [[Yaqui Topminnow]] population during 1979 survey
- Can be retained as is or waters diverted northward for cienega development

**A5 Complex:**
- Uncapped vertical artesian bore supporting approximately 5 square meters of semi-impounded water
- Outflow directed southeasterly onto agricultural area creating minor cienega-type habitat
- Only [[Yaqui Topminnow]] observed during 1979 survey
- Locality formerly supported minor [[Yaqui Chub]] population possibly entrapped in vertical bore by recently installed pipe riser
- Potential for significant improvement through masonry construction to stabilize wetted perimeter and enhance volume
- Outflow system capable of supporting expanded fishery complex with manipulation
- Creation of pool-run-riffle types would provide habitat suitable for [[Yaqui Sucker]] introduction and [[Yaqui Chub]] population expansion

**A6 Feature:**
- Capped artesian well with valve continuously open through landowner cooperation
- Supports high-density [[Yaqui Topminnow]] population
- Sporadic observations of [[Yaqui Chub]] noted previously though not observed during 1979 survey
- Recommend improvements similar to A5 complex with same species complex establishment

**A7 Artesian System:**
- Not investigated; no opinions or recommendations formulated

**Black Draw:**
- Perennial flow from above road crossing to international boundary at time of 1979 survey
- Supported moderate population of [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
- Lowermost reach above boundary remains permanent throughout year per landowner
- Presents opportunity for fishery enhancement and faunal expansion, particularly for [[Yaqui Sucker]] and [[Mexican Stoneroller]]
- Further analysis required

**Pond 1 Outflow Canals:**
- Potential for improvement to support lotic species [[Yaqui Sucker]] and [[Mexican Stoneroller]]
- Would incorporate entire known Yaqui ichthyofauna present or formerly resident within Arizona into [[San Bernardino Ranch]] biota

**Implementation Considerations:**
- Concepts presented constitute optimum management for native species
- May be beyond Department capability
- Specifics tentative, based on only four cursory surveys of property
- Recommendations address only aquatic habitat potentials
- Extensive biological inventory of both plant and animal life must be accomplished prior to alteration
- Intent of proposed acquisition: maintenance of a unique ecosystem
