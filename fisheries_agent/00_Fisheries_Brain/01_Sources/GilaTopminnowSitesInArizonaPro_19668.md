# Gila Topminnow Sites in Arizona: Provisional Extirpation Report 1995-96 Field Season

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Poeciliidae #conservation-biology #reintroduction

## Executive Summary

This technical report documents provisional extirpation determinations for [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) reintroduction sites in Arizona surveyed during the 1995-96 field season. Since 1981, the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), and [[U.S. Forest Service]] (USFS) have cooperated to recover this endangered species through reintroductions at more than 200 sites. The report applies extirpation criteria proposed in the draft revised Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan to evaluate failed reintroduction sites, allowing removal of Section 7 consultation obligations. Fifteen sites met extirpation criteria during 1995, seventeen additional sites met criteria prior to 1995, and eleven sites did not meet criteria but require additional management actions or restocking.

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] is a small poeciliid fish endemic to the [[Gila River]] drainage in Arizona and Mexico. Following decades of decline due to habitat loss, water diversion, and competition/predation from introduced [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), the species was listed as endangered. Recovery efforts beginning in 1981 involved extensive reintroductions to springs, stock tanks, and small streams throughout historical range. Many reintroductions failed due to poor site selection, with early criteria amounting to "little more than any available, possibly permanent water (whether artificial or not) within historical range."

Current reintroduction criteria (Abarca et al. 1994) specify: natural stream channels (Rosgen Type B or C); complex, heterogeneous habitat protected from major flash flooding; only native, nonpredatory fishes present; water quality meeting Arizona Department of Environmental Quality standards; minimal development potential; and location within the [[Gila River]] drainage.

Extirpation criteria require: (1) qualified biologists fail to collect topminnows in at least five consecutive surveys for natural populations or three for reintroduced populations; (2) surveys covered majority of available habitat; (3) surveys conducted during optimal detection periods (June-November); (4) preservation of mosquitofish voucher specimens if present; and (5) if site completely dry, surveys of surface waters above and below.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Reintroduction sites spanned diverse habitats across Arizona counties including Yavapai, Gila, Maricopa, Cochise, Graham, Pima, and Santa Cruz, at elevations from 2,120 to 4,900 feet. Sites included natural springs, spring-fed streams, artificial stock tanks, and modified spring systems. Many sites were located on [[Tonto National Forest]], [[Prescott National Forest]], [[Coconino National Forest]], [[Coronado National Forest]], BLM lands, and private property.

Common causes of population failure included: complete desiccation during drought; flash flooding and scouring; illegal introduction of [[Western Mosquitofish]]; cold winter temperatures; sedimentation and habitat degradation; cattle trampling and overgrazing; water diversion and spring modifications; and predation by non-native fishes or tiger salamanders.

Several sites showed initial success followed by failure. For example, [[Big Springs]] (Site #84) supported topminnows from 1985-1991 until severe flooding in August 1990 removed most fish and silted in pools. [[Sycamore Spring]] (Site #21) maintained populations from 1982-1992 until vandalism and flooding damaged water delivery systems. [[Yellowstone Tank]] (Site #23) supported topminnows from 1982-1987 before drying during moderate drought.

## Key Findings

**Sites Meeting Extirpation Criteria During 1995 (n=15):**

- [[Big Springs]] (Site #84): Flooding removed population; pool silted in; only small plunge pool remained by 1996
- [[Chalk Tank]] (Site #186): Reported dry following 1982 stocking; tank no longer exists
- [[Chaney Place]] (Site #109): Never officially stocked; rumored population never confirmed; now private land with hostile owners
- [[Deep Spring]] (Site #189): Dried year following 1982 stocking; steep drainage unsuitable
- [[Montezuma Tank]] (Site #202): Topminnows gone by July 1983; tank completely dry in 1993 and 1996
- [[Peoples Canyon]] (Site #28): Large flood in Fall 1989 removed all fish; outside historic range
- [[Rock Creek 3 Bar C]] (Site #212): Original population at C Weir destroyed by cold temperatures 1975-76
- [[Spring Fed Tank #78a]] (Site #78a): Dry immediately following 1982 stocking; no reliable water source
- [[Sycamore Spring]] (Site #21): Water delivery vandalized 1990; flooding damaged system 1992; USFWS declared extirpated September 1992
- [[Unnamed Spring]] (Site #146): Likely never stocked; unsuitable habitat
- [[Unnamed Spring Fed Tank #498]] (Site #39): Failed possibly due to low oxygen; tank dry in 1989 and 1996
- [[Unnamed Spring #3]] (Site #37): Desiccation caused failure by 1987; only tiny pool present in 1996
- [[White Tank #1]] (Site #69): Dry during both post-stocking visits (1985, 1996); ephemeral tank
- [[Yellowstone Tank]] (Site #23): Tank dried between 1987-1989; appears to dry during moderate drought

**Sites Meeting Extirpation Criteria Prior to 1995 (n=17):**

- [[Two Mile Spring]] (Site #13): Disappeared between 1985-1986; few small pools present
- [[Badger Springs]] (Site #180): Status unknown 1975-1991; second survey confirmed extirpation
- [[Frog Spring]] (Site #47): Did not persist long after 1982 stocking; limited habitat
- [[Hull Spring]] (Site #30): Completely dry in 1996; heavy cattle grazing
- [[Jubilee Spring]] (Site #27): Spring modified in 1987 for downstream drinker, causing drying
- [[McCann Spring Tank]] (Site #53): Gone by 1985; tank choked with vegetation
- [[Mesquite Tank #1]] (Site #62): Not present following stocking; likely dried
- [[Middle Mesa Tank]] (Site #58): Not collected after introduction; cold weather, drying, or salamander predation suspected
- [[Monkey Tank]] (Site #201): Dry both times monitored (1983, 1993)
- [[Oxbow Spring]] (Site #56): Failed by 1985-1987; cold temperatures in steep, wooded canyon suspected
- [[Rock Spring #1]] (Site #52): Gone by 1985; spring reduced to small puddle by 1994
- [[Sheep Spring]] (Site #34): Not collected since 1985; cattle trampling and pond silting
- [[Sheepshead Spring]] (Site #63): Floods removed topminnow by 1985; water now piped out
- [[Squaw Peak Spring]] (Site #220): Disappeared immediately after stocking; limited habitat
- [[Tucker Box]] (Site #78b): Steep canyon prone to flooding; no fish collected 1993
- [[Unnamed Spring #4]] (Site #50): Failed; no water present at stocking locality in 1994
- [[Unnamed Spring #7]] (Site #100): Never collected after introduction; all springs dry by 1992

**Sites Not Meeting Extirpation Criteria - Recommended for Restocking (n=11):**

- [[Campaign Creek]] (Site #91): Supported topminnows 1983-1989/1991; habitat remains suitable; essential to recovery
- [[Campbell Flat Spring]] (Site #25): Persisted 4-6 years; perennial water with thick vegetation; needs re-evaluation
- [[Cottonwood Artesian]] (Site #77): Supported population 11 years (1982-1992); conservation pools constructed December 1992; should be restocked
- [[Cottonwood Spring Creek]] (Site #55): Failed 1985 for unknown reason; USFWS completed Section 7 consultation 1990 but never restocked; suitable conditions in 1994 and 1996
- [[Government Spring]] (Site #33): Persisted 6-8 years; good surface flow; suitable for reintroduction
- [[Mud Spring Tank]] (Site #16): Mosquitofish illegally introduced 1985-1986; both species gone by 1991; abundant water and vegetation in 1993; verify mosquitofish absence before restocking
- [[O'Donnell Creek]] and [[Canelo Cienega]] (Sites #205, #274): Never collected after 1974 stocking; suitable habitat remains; remove/control [[Green Sunfish]]; coordinate reintroduction
- [[Red Creek]] (Site #112): Natural dispersal discovered 1987; did not persist long; investigate suitable portions for restocking
- [[Redfield Canyon]] (Site #211): Not collected since September 1978; supports other native fishes; consider reintroduction
- [[Tule Creek Unnamed Spring 1E]] (Site #74): Limited perennial habitat but close to successful [[Tule Creek]] site; maintain management and restock as needed
- [[Turkey Creek]] (Site #97): Never documented to support topminnows; historical locality for [[Gila Chub]] and [[Longfin Dace]]; reintroduce both topminnow and chub when acceptable

**Survey Methodology:** Surveys employed dipnetting, seining, visual observation, and electrofishing. Thoroughness and percent habitat surveyed were inconsistently documented historically. Estimates were based on personal knowledge of collectors, with surveys by Brooks, Simons, Bagley, Abarca, and Brown generally considered thorough and complete unless otherwise noted. Voucher specimens were preserved at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) when other poeciliids present.

**Mitigation Recommendations:** For [[Sycamore Spring]], already declared extirpated by USFWS, planning should continue to reintroduce topminnows to three alternate sites: Seven Mile Wash, Hess Canyon, and West Fork of Pinto Creek in [[Globe Ranger District]]. Pre-stocking evaluations found these sites suitable.
