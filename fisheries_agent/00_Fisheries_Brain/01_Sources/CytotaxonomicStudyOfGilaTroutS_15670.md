# Cytotaxonomic Study of Gila Trout, *Salmo gilae*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This cytogenetic study characterizes the karyotype of the [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*), a freshwater salmonid restricted to a few streams in New Mexico and Arizona. The diploid chromosome number is 2n = 56, with 49 metacentric or submetacentric and 7 acrocentric or telocentric chromosomes. The authors compare this karyotype with other western North American *Salmo* species and argue that chromosomal rearrangements, particularly [[Robertsonian Fusion]] and [[Pericentric Inversion]], are correlated with speciation in the genus. The paper was published in the *Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada* (1977, vol. 34, pp. 1041–1045) by R. J. Beamish and R. R. Miller.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Salmo gilae*, the Gila Trout, a native trout of the southwestern United States.
- **Distribution:** Limited to a few streams of New Mexico and Arizona (Miller 1972).
- **Study rationale:** Karyotypes are the physical basis of an organism's genetic system, so the first karyotype of *S. gilae* was expected to help assess its specific distinctness and its evolutionary origin.
- **Material:** Juveniles obtained from [[Sterling Springs Hatchery]] in Arizona. The stock originated from Main Diamond Creek, New Mexico, the type locality of the species. Four fish were sampled (fish 1–4).
- **Method:** Chromosome squashes were prepared from gill tissue using the McPhail and Jones (1966) technique, modified by Beamish et al. (1971), and examined with phase contrast microscopy. Karyotypes were established by comparing enlarged prints with permanent preparations, which were retained for later examination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addresses the taxonomic and evolutionary status of a geographically restricted trout. Its relevance lies in the relationship between chromosome structure and speciation within *Salmo*, rather than in direct habitat measurements. The paper provides no data on water chemistry, habitat conditions, or population ecology.

The authors contrast *Salmo* with other genera. Speciation in *Lepomis* (Roberts 1964) and *Esox* (Beamish et al. 1971) is not clearly associated with chromosomal change, whereas in *Salmo* it may be. Related western North American taxa examined include [[Apache Trout]] (*Salmo apache*), [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki*), [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*), and [[Golden Trout]] (*Salmo aguabonita*).

## Key Findings

- **Diploid number:** *S. gilae* has 2n = 56 chromosomes, comprising 49 metacentric/submetacentric and 7 acrocentric/telocentric chromosomes.
- **Count variation:** Of 60 squashes counted, 39 had 2n = 56. Counts ranged from 53 to 58 across fish, and the largest variation came from fish 3. The authors attribute much of this variation to interpretive errors (overlaps, loss, breaks, or added chromosomes from other squashes) and state that it should not be taken as the natural degree of variation in the species. Some well-spread squashes with counts above 56 suggest that real variation occurs in gill-tissue cells.
- **Modal karyotype:** At least one preparation from each fish had the modal count of 2n = 56 with 7 acrocentrics. Some squashes showed 57 or 58 chromosomes with 8 or 9 acrocentrics.
- **Unpaired chromosome:** An apparently unpaired chromosome was observed. It is consistent with the chromosomal polymorphism known in *Salmo* (Ohno et al. 1965; Roberts 1968) and with a single unpaired submetacentric reported in landlocked *S. salar* (Roberts 1968). The sample was too small to test whether heterozygosity relates to sex.
- **Comparison with *S. apache*:** The karyotype is very similar to that of *S. apache*, but *S. gilae* has one more acrocentric and one fewer metacentric/submetacentric chromosome. This may reflect a pericentric inversion in one metacentric or submetacentric chromosome.
- **Speciation hypothesis:** Table 3 presents hypothetical and present-day karyotypes for western North American *Salmo* species, built on a primitive *S. clarki* karyotype (2n = 64, 106 arms, 22 acrocentric/subtelocentric). Robertsonian fusions are modeled as reducing chromosome number by 2 and acrocentrics by 4 while keeping arm number constant, and pericentric inversions as reducing metacentric/submetacentric numbers while also decreasing arm number. The authors conclude that the close fit between observed karyotypes and predicted rearrangements strongly suggests speciation is linked to chromosomal change. They further propose that species may have arisen in isolation rather than through hybridization, and that species at the lower and right side of the table are probably more recent.
- **Sampling note:** Karyotypes were prepared from only four juvenile fish, so the conclusions about variation and sex-linked heterozygosity are tentative.
