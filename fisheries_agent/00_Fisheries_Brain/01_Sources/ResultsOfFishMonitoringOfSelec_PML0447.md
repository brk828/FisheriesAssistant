# Fish Monitoring of Selected Waters of the Gila River Basin, 1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This technical report, prepared by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (Phoenix Area Office) for the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], presents fish monitoring results for 1997 in target streams and canals of the Gila River Basin. The monitoring was required by a [[Biological Opinion]] (1994) on delivering [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water. That opinion found the project would jeopardize four threatened or endangered fishes, and the main concern was the transfer of non-native species from the lower Colorado River into Gila Basin drainages. Across the monitored waters, 1997 surveys recorded many species, a small native component, and no species new to the monitoring record. The authors state that data quality and comparability across years and sites remain under review, so only very general conclusions about assemblage structure are offered.

## Background & Description

**Regulatory context.** The [[Biological Opinion]] concluded that CAP water transport would jeopardize the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and would adversely modify designated critical habitat for the last three. A reasonable and prudent alternative directed a baseline study and long-term monitoring of non-native fish presence and distribution, with monitoring to begin in October 1994.

**Monitored reaches.** Six target areas were specified:
1. CAP aqueduct (Hayden-Rhodes, Fannin-McFarland, and Tucson aqueducts)
2. [[Salt River Project]] (SRP) canals (South and Arizona canals)
3. [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG)
4. [[Salt River]], between Stewart Mountain Dam and Granite Reef Dam
5. [[Gila River]], between Coolidge and Ashurst-Hayden dams
6. [[San Pedro River]], downstream from the U.S.-Mexico border

**Methods.** Sampling followed the 1996 revision of the [[Monitoring Plan]] (Revision No. 2). Streams were stratified by geomorphology or flow, and replicate quantitative stations were established. [[Electrofishing]] was the primary gear, though many canals required seines, dip nets, gill and trammel nets, minnow traps, trot lines, and angling. Canal sampling was opportunistic and tied to low-flow or dry-up periods. Station changes were made for right-of-entry reasons. Two stations were deleted: Hawk Spring Canyon (Gila, inaccessible) and Gage Station (San Pedro, no landowner access).

**Responsibilities.** Reclamation's Boulder City office led CAP sampling. The Arizona Game and Fish Department sampled the Gila, Salt, and San Pedro rivers and led multi-agency sampling of SRP canals above the [[Electrical Fish Barrier]]. Arizona State University (ASU) monitored the SRP and FCG canals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Species occurrence.** [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) was the only species captured in every stream and canal sampled, though not at every reach or station. [[Common Carp]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Largemouth Bass]] occurred in all waters except the FCG canal. [[Yellow Bullhead]] was absent only from the SRP canals.

**Native fishes.** Only four native species were recorded in 1997: [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Sonora Sucker]]. Roundtail chub was taken only in the SRP canals. Longfin dace was the native fish found at every San Pedro station. The native fraction was low overall.

**Non-native dominance.** Most species captured were introduced. All species caught in the CAP canal were non-native. Grass carp and other stocked species were recorded, and the report notes [[Non-Native Fishes]] as the core threat behind the monitoring program.

**Habitat and gear constraints.** The CAP canal is deep, swift, and monotonous, so the largest gear array was used there. Electrofishing was not used in lower reaches during the winter low-flow period, because of equipment breakdown. The Gila River lower reaches had high discharges in earlier years, and sampling efficiency was reduced. San Pedro sampling was affected by high, muddy flows.

**Assemblage patterns:**
- **CAP canal:** Sunfishes dominated the lower reach, and young-of-year [[Bluegill]] was especially abundant at San Xavier. Grass carp was taken only in reach 1, which the authors attribute to limited electrofishing in lower reaches.
- **Gila River:** Native suckers were numerically important in several instances. Yellow bullhead was captured at every station. [[Smallmouth Bass]], black crappie, and flathead catfish occurred only in the uppermost reach.
- **San Pedro River:** Upper reach: longfin dace, desert sucker, and mosquitofish co-dominated. Middle reach: mostly longfin dace. Lower reach: red shiner dominated, with very few fish overall.
- **FCG Canal:** [[Mosquitofish]] was most abundant above the barrier. Red shiner dominated below it.
- **Salt River:** Young-of-year native suckers and mixed-age largemouth bass made up most of the catch. Desert sucker captures fell sharply at the lower, deep-water station. No roundtail chub was found, despite its presence in nearby SRP canals.
- **SRP canals:** Red shiner, desert sucker, and channel catfish were top-ranked above the barrier. Tilapia species were recorded in both sides.

**Main conclusions and plans:**
- No species were recorded for the first time in 1997.
- Data quality, sampling design, and cross-year comparability remain under review, so the report offers only very general assemblage conclusions.
- A comprehensive statistical review was scheduled for the first half of 2000, followed by an update to the monitoring plan.
- A habitat data report was planned for 2000.
- Partial 1997 results were previously presented by Clark et al. (1998) and Marsh (1998). Marsh (1998) reported on SRP and FCG canal surveys for 1997–1998.
