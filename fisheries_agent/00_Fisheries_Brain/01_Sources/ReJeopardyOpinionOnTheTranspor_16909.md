# Central Arizona Project Water Delivery and the Gila River Biological Opinion

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This letter from the [[Central Arizona Water Conservation District]] (CAWCD), dated June 9, 1995, asks the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (the Service) to withdraw its April 20, 1994 [[Biological Opinion]]. That opinion concluded that continued transportation and delivery of [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water jeopardizes four threatened or endangered fish species in the upper [[Gila River Basin]] and adversely modifies designated critical habitat for three of them. CAWCD argues the opinion ignores or contradicts the best available scientific and commercial data, was produced without CAWCD participation in the [[Section 7 Consultation]] process, and places responsibility on the wrong federal project. The letter requests reinitiated consultation and reconsideration, and states that CAWCD will seek judicial review if no response is received by July 10, 1995.

## Background & Description

The letter is addressed to Secretary [[Bruce Babbitt]] of the [[U.S. Department of the Interior]] and to Director [[Mollie Beattie]] of the Service. It is a formal policy correspondence, not a research report, and contains no species-level biological data such as sample sizes, population counts or measurements.

The biological opinion concerns the risk that non-native fish moving from CAP water delivery systems could enter natural watercourses and travel upstream past dams and dry washes to reach habitat of native species. The opinion set out four [[Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives]] (RPAs) for the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]]:

1. Transfer $500,000 per year for 25 years to the Service to fund research and conservation in Arizona and New Mexico.
2. Construct and maintain a series of physical barriers to fish movement on the San Pedro River and Aravaipa Creek.
3. Monitor the presence and distribution of non-native fish in Arizona streams and waters for 100 years.
4. Develop and implement an information and education program on native fish conservation.

CAWCD estimates the RPAs will cost more than $150 million over the life of the CAP.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native-fish risk:** The opinion's central concern is that non-native fish escaping from subcontractor delivery systems could threaten native fish in the upper Gila River basin.
- **Pathways cited:** Escape routes named in the opinion include agricultural return flows, canals, sumps and ponds within irrigation districts receiving CAP water.
- **Irrigation systems:** CAWCD states that most or all irrigation systems identified in the opinion are funded or controlled by the United States and so fall under [[Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act]]. The [[San Carlos Irrigation Project]], comprising the [[Gila River Indian Community]] and the San Carlos Irrigation and Drainage District and operated by the [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], is cited as an example.
- **Historical introductions:** CAWCD argues that the non-native fish were introduced mainly through Service-funded or Service-controlled stocking programs, most of which never underwent Section 7 consultation.

## Key Findings

- **Disputed scientific basis:** CAWCD contends that most of its report, which it says contains scientific evidence refuting the opinion, was not considered in the biological opinion. The report itself is not reproduced in this document.
- **Information standards:** CAWCD invokes the [[Interagency Cooperative Policy on Information Standards Under the Endangered Species Act]] (59 Fed. Reg. 34871, July 1, 1994), which directs the Service to impartially evaluate information that disputes its positions.
- **Procedural objection:** CAWCD was not allowed to take part in the Section 7 consultation that produced the opinion.
- **Wrong target:** CAWCD argues the problem arises from the design, construction and operation of irrigation delivery systems rather than from the CAP itself, so the opinion is directed at the wrong federal project.
- **Burden shifting:** CAWCD argues the opinion shifts responsibility for past and ongoing Service stocking programs onto CAP water users.
- **Requested action:** Withdraw or reconsider the opinion, reinitiate consultation under 50 C.F.R. § 402.18(b), and find no jeopardy and no adverse modification of critical habitat.
- **Deadline:** Response requested by July 10, 1995, failing which CAWCD will pursue judicial review.
