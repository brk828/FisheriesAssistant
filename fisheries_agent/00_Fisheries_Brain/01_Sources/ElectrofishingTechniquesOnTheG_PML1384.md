# Electrofishing Techniques on the Green River: Pros and Cons

#ichthyology #marine-biology #trout

## Executive Summary

This technical note, by John A. Lund of the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]] (Flaming Gorge Fisheries Investigations, Dutch John, Utah), evaluates the use of [[Electrofishing]] to collect trout data on the [[Green River]] below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. The note finds that daytime electrofishing was largely ineffective because large trout avoided the boat in clear water, while [[Night Sampling]] was 5 to 10 times more effective and was the only practical option for obtaining enough fish for population estimates. Access limits and boat size further constrained sampling. The author concludes that a different boat design is needed for future [[Population Estimation]] work. Page 2 of the scan is largely illegible, so its conclusions are tentative.

## Background & Description

- **Fishery origin:** The trout fishery was created in 1964 following construction of Flaming Gorge Dam.
- **Early electrofishing use (1964–1976):** Sporadic use focused on age and growth, species composition, distribution, and migration.
- **Penstock modification (1977–1978):** The dam's penstocks were modified so that warmer water could be released from the reservoir into the river during summer months.
- **Research project:** A project was set up to assess the effects of these warmer releases on trout populations. The scan reads the start year as "L97l," so the exact year is uncertain. The project decided that population estimates or indices should be made using electrofishing gear.
- **Equipment (fall 1977):** Sampling used a boat from the Department of Water and Power Resources, consisting of a 16 ft "Johnboat" with a 70 h.p. jet outboard. Electronics were installed by [[Coffelt Electronics]] of Denver, Colorado. A Coffelt model WP-15 power control unit energized two positive booms fitted with single cable droppers. Page 2 mentions a negative electrode in the boat and several positive and negative electrodes, but the scan is too garbled to confirm the arrangement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water clarity:** Clear water conditions usually prevail in the river. The author links this to poor daytime capture success, particularly for larger trout.
- **Trout behavior:** Larger trout appeared to avoid the boat during the day. At night, trout seemed to occupy shallower water and may have been attracted to running lights directed at the water.
- **Access and reach:** Road access is limited. The tailrace boat ramp directly below the dam and the [[Little Hole]] boat ramp, 7 miles downstream, are the only access points in the first 15 miles of river. The [[Browns Park]] area covers the remainder of the river to the Colorado line and offers several more launch points.
- **Physical limits:** The size of the electrofishing boat and several small rapids restrict shocking efforts to areas close to access points.

## Key Findings

1. **Daytime sampling is ineffective:** Electrofishing during daytime largely failed to capture trout, especially larger fish that avoided the boat, likely because of clear water.
2. **Night sampling is far more productive:** Nighttime electrofishing was 5 to 10 times more effective than daytime trials, and it was the only option found that could capture enough trout for population estimates or indices.
3. **Access and boat constraints:** Limited launch points and the size of the boat, together with small rapids, restrict effective sampling to areas near access points.
4. **Boat design needs change (tentative):** Page 2 appears to conclude that a different boat is required for future population estimates and that a larger or aluminum hull should be used. The scan is largely illegible, so these details could not be verified.
5. **Management purpose:** The electrofishing program supports assessment of how warmer summer releases from Flaming Gorge Dam affect trout populations in the Green River.
