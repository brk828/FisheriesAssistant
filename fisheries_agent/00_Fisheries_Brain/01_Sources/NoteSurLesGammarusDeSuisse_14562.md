## Executive Summary
This 1957 note by H. B. N. Hynes documents the first confirmed occurrence in Switzerland of three freshwater amphipod taxa of the genus [[Gammarus]]: *Gammarus pulex pulex*, *Gammarus pulex fossarum*, and *Gammarus lacustris*. Collected during excursions associated with the first Plecoptera Symposium (Lausanne, September 1956), these findings fill a gap in the knowledge of Swiss freshwater crustacean fauna, a group previously neglected by Swiss biologists. The paper situates Switzerland as a biogeographically significant region for understanding the post-Pleistocene distribution and interspecific competition dynamics of European *Gammarus* species, and it proposes a thermal-ecology hypothesis to explain altitudinal replacement patterns between the two *G. pulex* subspecies.

## Background & Description
The paper is taxonomic/distributional rather than morphometric in focus, but distinguishes the following taxa primarily by subspecific/species identity and ecological affinity rather than detailed morphology:

- **[[Gammarus Pulex Pulex]]** — nominate subspecies; riverine/stream-dwelling form associated with lower altitudes and warmer, more thermally variable waters.
- **[[Gammarus Pulex Fossarum]]** (Koch) — cold-water ecotype/subspecies inhabiting springs and small headwater rivulets; dominant in thermally stable, cold environments.
- **[[Gammarus Lacustris]]** (Sars) — an entirely lacustrine species; typically found in isolated, high-altitude water bodies in Central Europe, in contrast to its broad lowland distribution in Northern Europe and North America.

Notably, one aged male *G. pulex pulex* was found in a spring population otherwise dominated by *G. pulex fossarum* — interpreted as either an upstream migrant from a lower river population or a downstream-displaced individual from the lake population (see Ecological Niche).

Two additional species not yet recorded in Switzerland but plausibly present are also discussed:
- **[[Gammarus Roeseli]]** (Gervais) — inhabits slow-flowing lowland rivers in France, Germany, and Austria.
- **[[Gammarus Pungens]]** (Milne-Edwards) — inhabits small streams and springs in Italy and southern France.
- **[[Gammarus Berilloni]]** (Catta) — a species of western European origin, gradually expanding eastward, having crossed France and currently invading northern Germany.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Gammarus Pulex Pulex]]**: Occupies rivers and streams at low altitude. Recorded in an artificial basin at L'Isle (Vaud), in the Venoge river at Cuarnens (Vaud), and in Lac Brenet (Jura vaudois).
- **[[Gammarus Pulex Fossarum]]**: Found abundantly in the springs of the Orbe near Vallorbe (Vaud), characteristic of consistently cold water habitats such as springs and rivulets.
- **[[Gammarus Lacustris]]**: Recorded at Lac Lioson (Préalpes vaudoises), a small calcareous mountain lake at 1850 m — an entirely lacustrine, high-altitude habitat. This species is widespread across northern Europe and North America but restricted to isolated high-altitude sites in Central Europe (cf. Lac Darbon, Haute-Savoie; Dussart 1948).

**Interspecific competition and thermal gradient hypothesis**: The author proposes that temperature is the principal factor governing altitudinal zonation between the two *G. pulex* subspecies. In regions where *G. pulex fossarum* is absent (e.g., England), *G. pulex pulex* extends up into spring habitats, suggesting competitive exclusion rather than strict physiological limitation. Where both forms coexist, *fossarum* dominates cold, thermally stable waters (springs, headwaters) while *pulex* dominates warmer, more thermally variable lowland waters.

**Case of inversion at the Orbe system**: A notable exception to the typical altitudinal pattern was observed at Lac des Brenets (1000 m), fed by the resurgence of the Orbe at 717 m elevation. Counterintuitively, the lake (higher elevation) contained only *G. pulex pulex*, while the spring-fed source (lower elevation) was dominated by *G. pulex fossarum*. Hynes attributes this inversion to water temperature variability rather than elevation per se: lake water experiences greater seasonal thermal fluctuation (cooling in summer, warming in winter as it percolates through rock), favoring *pulex*, whereas the spring water remains thermally stable and cold, favoring *fossarum*.

## Key Findings
- First documented records of *Gammarus pulex pulex*, *Gammarus pulex fossarum*, and *Gammarus lacustris* in Switzerland, despite prior records in neighboring France (Pacaud 1952) and Germany (Schellenberg 1942).
- *Gammarus lacustris* is likely more widespread in Switzerland than currently documented, given its known presence in comparable high-altitude habitats in Haute-Savoie (Dussart 1948).
- Switzerland is highlighted as a biogeographically important region for studying post-Pleistocene faunal history (Thienemann 1952) and interspecific competition (Hynes 1955) among European *Gammarus* taxa.
- Altitudinal replacement of *G. pulex pulex* by *G. pulex fossarum* is proposed to be driven primarily by thermal regime (stability and absolute temperature) rather than altitude itself, supported by an inversion case in the Orbe/Lac des Brenets system.
- The paper calls for further Swiss-based research into *Gammarus* ecology, noting the country's rich diversity of biotopes suitable for such study.
- Three additional European *Gammarus* species (*G. roeseli*, *G. pungens*, *G. berilloni*) are flagged as strong candidates for future discovery in Switzerland based on known ranges and eastward range expansion (particularly *G. berilloni*).
