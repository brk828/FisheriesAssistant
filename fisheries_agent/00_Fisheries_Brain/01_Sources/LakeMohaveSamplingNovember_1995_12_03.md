## Executive Summary
This report documents a fisheries survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel in [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada border) over a four-day period (22-25 November 1995). The primary objective was to capture and monitor the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) using trammel nets deployed in the Cottonwood East vicinity, extending uplake to Carp Cove. A total of sixteen razorback suckers were captured, six of which were recaptures with documented tagging histories dating back to 1994. A supplementary effort in Yuma Cove (Arizona Bay) targeted recently stocked native fishes, yielding seven additional razorback suckers and one [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), all of which were measured, sexed, PIT tagged, and released back into the main lake body.

## Background & Description
Specific morphometric data (length/weight distributions) were recorded but not fully preserved in the available OCR tables. However, methodological notes indicate the following standard measurements were taken for all captured native fish:
- **Total Length (TL):** Recorded for all individuals captured at Yuma Cove.
- **Sex Determination:** Performed on all captured [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] individuals, likely via external morphological cues (e.g., papillae, body shape) or physical examination during handling.
- **Tagging:** PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags were utilized for individual identification, allowing for long-term tracking of growth and movement between sampling events (e.g., recaptures spanning from 09/20/94 to 11/25/95).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Utilization:** Fish were sampled across a depth gradient of 1-10 meters, indicating that [[Razorback Sucker]] populations in [[Lake Mohave]] utilize a range of littoral and sub-littoral zones, including specific embayments like [[Cottonwood Cove]] and [[Carp Cove]].
- **Stocking and Refugia:** The data confirms an active repatriation program, with specific mention of fish "Stocked from Boulder City" (a hatchery/rearing facility) and "Stocked from Nevada Larvae," highlighting the reliance on hatchery-reared stock to bolster wild populations.
- **Site-Specific Management:** [[Yuma Cove]] in Arizona Bay was identified as a specific stocking site for native fish (including [[Bonytail Chub]]) introduced in Spring 1995, suggesting this cove serves as a designated nursery or acclimation habitat before fish disperse into the main lake.
- **Species Co-occurrence:** The capture of both [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] in the same net pull at Yuma Cove suggests overlapping habitat preferences between these two endangered Colorado River Basin natives.

## Key Findings
- **Total Catch:** 16 Razorback Suckers captured in the primary Cottonwood East/Carp Cove sampling area; 7 additional Razorback Suckers and 1 Bonytail Chub captured in the supplementary Yuma Cove effort.
- **Recapture Rate:** 6 of the 16 razorback suckers captured in the main survey area were previously tagged, providing valuable long-term growth and survival data.
- **Historical Tracking:** Recapture data reveals specific individual histories, including:
  - A fish stocked as larvae in Nevada Cove on 09/20/94, later recaptured.
  - Multiple fish stocked from the Boulder City facility into Cottonwood Cove, NV (e.g., 07/19/95 and 10/12/95 stocking dates), later recaptured at Carp Cove.
  - Individual capture spans indicating survival in the reservoir for over a year (e.g., initial tag 11/25/94, recapture 11/25/95).
- **Sampling Effort:** The primary survey utilized 6 trammel nets set at 1-10 m depths, cleared at maximum 6-hour intervals, totaling **30.06 units of effort** (where 1 unit = 100 m² of netting fished for 24 hours).
- **Conservation Action:** All fish captured during the Yuma Cove supplementary sampling were immediately measured, tagged, and released at the Yuma Cove berm back into the main body of [[Lake Mohave]], indicating this was a monitoring/data-collection exercise rather than a removal effort.
