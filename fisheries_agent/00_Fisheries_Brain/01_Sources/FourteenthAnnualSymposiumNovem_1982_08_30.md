## Executive Summary
This document is a **logistical and organizational memorandum** from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] announcing its Fourteenth Annual Symposium, held November 18–20, 1982, in Scottsdale/Tempe, Arizona. Authored by Executive Secretary [[E. P. Pister]], the letter outlines meeting arrangements coordinated by the [[Arizona State University Coordinating Committee]] (including [[W. L. Minckley]], [[Paul Marsh]], and [[Gary Meffe]]). While not a primary research paper, it identifies two major **scientific mini-symposium themes** for the meeting: (1) the aquatic fauna of the [[Cuatrociénegas Basin]] in Coahuila, México, and (2) [[Predation]] ecology as it relates to desert fish conservation. The note reflects the Council's ongoing mission, now in its 13th year of operation, to serve as a mechanism for the preservation of desert fish species across the American Southwest and northern México.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although lacking direct ecological data, the letter identifies key ecological themes slated for discussion:

- **[[Cuatrociénegas Basin]] Ecosystem**: A desert wetland/spring complex in Coahuila, México, known for high endemism among fish, invertebrates, and other aquatic taxa adapted to isolated spring and pool habitats. Organized as a dedicated Mini-Symposium by [[Paul Marsh]].
- **[[Predation]] Dynamics**: Framed as a major ecological driver affecting desert fish populations, organized as a Mini-Symposium by [[Gary Meffe]]. The call for papers emphasizes both empirical research and field observational data on predator-prey interactions in desert aquatic systems.
- **General Desert Aquatic Habitat**: The overarching context is isolated, often relict desert springs and endorheic basins that support unique, frequently endemic and imperiled fish assemblages — habitats characteristic of the [[Desert Fishes Council]]'s conservation focus.

## Key Findings
- The [[Desert Fishes Council]] convened its **14th Annual Symposium** in November 1982, marking 13 years of continuous operation since its founding.
- Two **thematic mini-symposia** were organized for the meeting:
  - **[[Cuatrociénegas Basin]] and its Ichthyofauna** (Coahuila, México) — coordinated by [[Paul Marsh]].
  - **[[Predation]] in Desert Fish Ecosystems** — coordinated by [[Gary Meffe]], who solicited additional papers and abstracts on the topic due to its "enormous importance."
- The Council emphasized an inclusive approach to scientific communication, explicitly stating that **full manuscripts were not required** — abstracts or summaries of research/observations were accepted for publication in proceedings.
- The symposium combined scientific sessions with logistical/social elements (e.g., barbecue, field trips, cocktail-hour business meetings), reflecting the collaborative and grassroots nature of the organization.
- The letter underscores the Council's self-assessed growing efficacy: *"We now know that we can make a difference"* — reflecting a shift from uncertainty at its founding to confidence in its conservation impact by 1982.
- Administrative coordination was handled through [[Arizona State University]], with key figures including [[W. L. Minckley]], [[Pat Minckley]], [[Paul Marsh]], [[Gary Meffe]], and [[E. P. Pister]].
