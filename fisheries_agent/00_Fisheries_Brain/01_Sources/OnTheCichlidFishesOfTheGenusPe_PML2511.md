# Pelmatochromis, Tilapia and Sarotherodon: Generic Revisions of African Cichlids and the Cichlidae of the Quanza and Bengo Rivers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This document comprises two taxonomic papers by Ethelwynn Trewavas, published in the *Bulletin of the British Museum (Natural History), Zoology* (vol. 25), together with an appended plate. The first and longer paper revises the African cichlid genus [[Pelmatochromis]], proposes the new genus [[Pterochromis]] for *P. congicus*, reviews the relationship between *Pelmatochromis* and [[Tilapia]], and recognises [[Sarotherodon]] at full generic rank. The second paper describes a new species of *Sarotherodon* from the Quanza and Bengo rivers of Angola, annotates the cichlid fauna of those rivers, and resolves the identity of *Pseudocrenilabrus natalensis*. Scan errors in the source have been silently corrected.

## Background & Description

The first paper treats three species of *Pelmatochromis*: *P. ocellifer*, *P. buettikoferi* and *P. nigrofasciatus*. Trewavas re-examined specimens from the Tervuren Museum and the British Museum (Natural History), and the holotype of *P. ocellifer* was compared with four specimens of *P. nigrofasciatus* measuring 63 to 100 mm standard length (SL). Earlier identifications were corrected: *P. nigrofasciatus* is not a synonym of *P. ocellifer*, and the name *Pelmatochromis exsul* was based on young [[Hemichromis]] bimaculatus.

The second paper describes *Sarotherodon angolensis* sp. nov., based on a holotype of 146 mm SL and a series of 27 specimens (43 to 204 mm SL) from the lower Quanza River at Cambambe and Cunga. It is distinguished by 21 to 26 gill-rakers on the lower part of the first arch, a tasselled genital papilla in both sexes (longer in males), and white spotting on the body and fins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Pelmatochromis nigrofasciatus* is recorded from the central Congo Basin, from Yangambi to Kinshasa, including the Rubi, Ruki, Ja and Kasai rivers. Its diet is predominantly vegetable, with filamentous and unicellular algae, insect remains and mud in the stomachs examined by Matthes. It is a substrate spawner and guards its brood.

Trewavas notes that the cichlid fauna of the lower Quanza and Bengo resembles that of the Chiloango and Ogooué, while the upper Quanza fauna resembles that of the Zambezi basin and the Cunene River in southern Angola. The Angolan Province is highly endemic, and [[Sarotherodon Angolensis]] appears restricted to the lower reaches of the Quanza and Bengo.

Distributional and behavioural evidence links [[Pseudocrenilabrus Philander]] to backwaters, swamps and lagoons across the Zambezi, Limpopo, Orange and Congo systems, with no lacustrine flock formation.

## Key Findings

- ***Pelmatochromis* taxonomy:** *P. nigrofasciatus* is a valid species distinct from *P. ocellifer*. The three species retained in *Pelmatochromis* are *P. buettikoferi*, *P. ocellifer* and *P. nigrofasciatus*.
- **New genus:** *Pterochromis* n. gen. is proposed for *P. congicus*, distinguished by a large oblique mouth (lower jaw 35–41% of head length, mouth angle 40–50°), long slender gill-rakers and more specialised dentition. *Pelmatochromis* has a mouth angle of about 21°.
- **Status of *Pseudocrenilabrus exsul*:** The species is based on young *Hemichromis bimaculatus*, removing the only East African record of *Pelmatochromis*.
- **Exclusion from *Tilapia*:** Trewavas rejects Thys's inclusion of *Pelmatochromis* in *Tilapia* and his placement of *T. busumana* in the same subgenus. Dentition, epibranchial structures, scales (gothic versus roman circuli), and the ethmo-vomerine relationship are cited as distinguishing features.
- **Four dentition types** are recognised: (1) curved conical teeth at all stages (*P. buettikoferi*, *P. ocellifer*, *Pterochromis*); (2) notched or shouldered in the young, conical in adults (*P. nigrofasciatus*); (3) pluricuspid at first, becoming pointed in adults (some *T. rendalli*); (4) bicuspid outer and tricuspid inner teeth (*T. busumana*).
- **Generic status:** *Tilapia* and *Sarotherodon* are recognised as distinct genera. *Sarotherodon* has 28 or fewer gill-rakers on the lower first arch, a lower pharyngeal bone 27.5–43.5% of head length, a pharyngeal blade/toothed-area ratio of 0.4–0.9, a mesethmoid not meeting the vomer, and mouth-brooding. *Tilapia* has 7–16 gill-rakers, a pharyngeal bone 21.5–29.5% of head length, and substrate spawning.
- **Vertebral counts:** The modal count for the three *Pelmatochromis* species is 26. *Pseudocrenilabrus* has 28 vertebrae.
- **Subgenus Nyasalapia:** Trewavas proposes that *Lorowiaia* Thys is a subjective synonym of *Nyasalapia* Thys, because the genital tassel that Thys used as the defining character is also found in the type species of *Nyasalapia*.
- ***Sarotherodon angolensis* sp. nov.:** The species is diagnosed by its high gill-raker count, slender crowded lower pharyngeal teeth, tasselled genital papilla and colour pattern. Vertebrae number 29 in the three specimens counted. Males have a silver spot in the lateral rows.
- **Identity of *Pseudocrenilabrus natalensis*:** The holotype is a specimen of *Pseudocrenilabrus philander* (Weber), not a Tilapia-like form. Trewavas argues for generic distinction from *Haplochromis* on the basis of egg-spot colour pattern and the lack of ocellar spots in males.
- **Angolan fauna:** The lower Quanza collection includes *Tilapia guineensis* and *Haplochromis lucullae*, among others. Several earlier determinations in the Gray Expedition material and in Gianferrari's report are considered mistaken.
