# Letter to Dr. W.L. Minckley Regarding Razorback Sucker Specimen Shipment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1988 letter from Harold M. Tyus, Project Leader of the [[Colorado River Fish Project]] (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Vernal, Utah), documents the transmittal of 40 [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) specimens to Dr. W.L. Minckley at Arizona State University. The specimens—derived from the [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] propagation program—were intended to support a collaborative assessment of morphological and genetic variability in hatchery-reared versus wild razorback sucker populations. The correspondence outlines specimen preservation methods, tagging protocols to preserve cohort identity, and data-sharing/curation arrangements consistent with federal permit requirements.

## Background & Description
No direct morphometric data are reported in this letter; rather, it describes the *design* of a morphological study to be conducted by the recipient:
- **Specimen composition**: 40 total razorback suckers split into two lots of 20.
  - Lot 1 (20 specimens): Preserved in **10% formalin solution** — designated for **morphomeristic comparisons** against wild-caught razorback suckers and fish from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
  - Lot 2 (20 specimens): **Frozen on dry ice** — designated for **electrophoretic analysis** and **mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis**.
- **Cohort identification**: Specimens represent **two year classes** from the Ouray propagation facility.
- **Individual marking**: A [[Carlin Tag]] was used to mark one 1988 year-class female and one fish sourced from Dexter NFH, with tagged individuals included in *both* sample lots to allow cross-referencing between preservation batches.
- Full **parentage and growth records** for the shipped fish were retained by the sending office (FWS Colorado River Fish Project) and were to be furnished to Minckley upon completion of his analyses.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large-bodied catostomid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically inhabiting mainstem river reaches with the characteristic dorsal keel adaptation associated with large-river flow environments.
- This document reflects the species' status as a focal taxon in **conservation aquaculture and species recovery efforts**, with propagation occurring at two federal facilities: the **Ouray NFH** (Utah) and **Dexter NFH** (New Mexico), both integral to the broader [[Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]].
- The comparative study design (wild vs. hatchery-reared, across multiple hatchery sources) implies ecological concern over **genetic and morphological divergence** in captive-reared stocks relative to wild Colorado River populations — a key consideration in reintroduction and augmentation programs for this federally listed endangered species.

## Key Findings
- Institutional collaboration was established between the **USFWS Colorado River Fish Project** and **Arizona State University** (Dr. Minckley's laboratory) to evaluate variability in propagated razorback sucker stocks.
- Study design integrated three complementary analytical approaches: **morphomeristics**, **protein electrophoresis**, and **mtDNA analysis**, reflecting a multi-method approach typical of late-1980s fish stock/population differentiation studies.
- Specimens were sourced from **two hatchery year classes** and included **cross-hatchery representation** (Ouray and Dexter NFH fish), enabling comparison of propagation program outcomes.
- Chain-of-custody and regulatory compliance were emphasized: post-analysis, surviving/remaining specimens were required to be returned to the FWS and curated in the **Fort Collins FWS collection**, per federal permit stipulations — indicating formal specimen tracking under [[Endangered Species Act]] permitting.
- The letter itself constitutes primary documentation of **specimen provenance and chain-of-custody** for a historical razorback sucker genetics/morphology dataset, valuable for retrospective analysis of hatchery contributions to wild population genetic assessments.
