# Native Fish Work Group Meeting Results - Razorback Sucker Restoration at Lake Mohave (January 1991)

#ichthyology #native-fish #Catostomidae #razorback-sucker #habitat-restoration #conservation

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum documents the January 10, 1991 Native Fish Work Group meeting regarding ongoing efforts to restore [[Razorback Sucker]] populations in [[Lake Mohave]]. The project involves fish renovation of [[Yuma Cove]], introduction of mature razorback suckers for spawning, and subsequent transfer of juveniles to [[Davis Cove]] for grow-out. The meeting covered successful completion of initial fish removal, planned spawner introduction, PIT tagging protocols, and construction of temporary barriers at Davis Cove. Participating agencies included Arizona Game and Fish Department, Bureau of Reclamation, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, National Park Service, and Arizona State University.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a native fish species requiring restoration efforts in [[Lake Mohave]]. The project utilizes modified backwater coves as spawning and rearing habitat. [[Yuma Cove]] serves as the primary spawning site, while [[Davis Cove]] is being developed as a grow-out facility for juvenile fish.

The restoration approach involves: (1) complete fish renovation using ichthyocide to remove non-native species, primarily [[Threadfin Shad]] and [[Carp]]; (2) introduction of mature razorback suckers (approximately 80 individuals: 60 males and 20 females) collected by beach seine; (3) monitoring spawning success; (4) PIT tagging of juveniles at approximately 10 cm length; and (5) transfer to Davis Cove for continued rearing.

Water level fluctuations in Lake Mohave significantly impact project timing. Elevations were projected to drop to 635 feet by July 1991 and further to 631 feet by late August or September, which would virtually dry up Yuma Cove, necessitating fish transfer before September.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project addresses the declining [[Lake Mohave]] razorback sucker population, with reviewers noting the urgency to "optimize sucker production since the Lake Mohave population may soon die off." The backwater cove habitat modification provides protected spawning and rearing areas isolated from predatory non-native species.

The first fish renovation at Yuma Cove on October 4, 1990 was successful, killing an estimated 800 to 1,200 fish comprised mostly of threadfin shad and carp. A second treatment was scheduled for January 11, 1991, with water quality testing on January 22, 1991 to ensure ichthyocide oxidation prior to razorback sucker introduction.

The temporary barrier design for Davis Cove consists of a log boom fitted with ¾-inch mesh nylon net weighted with steel chain and concrete blocks, designed to minimize site disturbance. The carrying capacity of both Yuma and Davis Coves remained uncertain, requiring assessment after the first production cycle to determine if additional spawning and rearing facilities would be necessary.

## Key Findings

**Fish Renovation Success**: The October 4, 1990 fish kill at Yuma Cove was complete, removing 800-1,200 fish (primarily threadfin shad and carp). Second treatment scheduled for January 11, 1991.

**Spawner Introduction Plan**: Approximately 80 razorback suckers (60 males, 20 females) to be collected by beach seine on January 24, 1991 and introduced to Yuma Cove. Dr. Paul Marsh ([[Arizona State University]]) coordinating collection.

**Spawning Monitoring**: Success to be checked in mid-February and March during Lake Mohave "roundup." Spawning success anticipated to be evident 30 days following spawner introduction. Backup plan involves stocking with razorback sucker fry from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico if on-site spawning unsuccessful.

**PIT Tagging Protocol**: PIT tags available under GSA contract at $3.25 per tag; monitors cost $1,100 each. [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Albuquerque Regional Office budgeted $20,000 for equipment. Dr. Marsh to receive 100 tags for tagging spawners prior to release. Juveniles to be tagged at approximately 10 cm length in July.

**Transfer Timeline**: Young suckers anticipated to reach 10 cm by July 1991. Recovery, tagging, and transfer to Davis Cove coordinated for July, prior to September when dropping lake levels (to 631 feet) would dry up Yuma Cove. [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] stocking barge potentially available for transport.

**Davis Cove Development**: Temporary barrier construction ($15,000) scheduled for mid-June installation. National Park Service conducting NEPA compliance (Environmental Assessment required). Permanent barrier concept discussed as contingency if temporary design proves unreliable.

**Management Plan**: Draft Management/Research Plan received supportive review comments with no dissenting remarks on the spawning/grow-out alternative. Comments incorporated; final comments due January 30, 1991. Plan emphasizes optimizing sucker production given declining Lake Mohave population.

**Production Capacity**: Actual production capacity of Yuma Cove to be examined in fall 1991 to determine adequacy and whether additional spawning and rearing facilities needed to augment production.

**Public Outreach**: National Park Service to provide public information signs at Davis Cove and include NFWG activities in interpretive programs. Bureau of Reclamation documenting project with slides and videos for potential presentation/program and brochure development.

**Next Meeting**: Scheduled for early May 1991 to review spawning progress, equipment requisition, barrier materials, NEPA compliance, and discuss fish salvage and renovation of Davis Cove.
