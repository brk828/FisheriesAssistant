## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service memorandum, not a primary research paper, detailing logistical arrangements for transporting and stocking **10,000 razorback sucker ([[Razorback Sucker]]) fingerlings** from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery & Technology Center (Dexter NFH&TC) to a net-enclosed grow-out impoundment at Davis Cove, [[Lake Mohave]]. The stocking effort represents a component of a broader native fish recovery and augmentation program for this endangered [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] species in the Colorado River system, coordinated among federal, state, university, and tribal/agency stakeholders under the [[Native Fish Work Group]].

## Background & Description
- **Species**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]].
- **Life stage**: Fingerlings (juvenile hatchery-reared stock).
- **Size at stocking**: Approximately **3 inches** in length.
- **Marking status**: None of the fish in this shipment were marked/tagged, which has implications for future tracking of survival, growth, and recruitment post-release.
- No additional anatomical, coloration, or meristic data are provided in this source, as the document is administrative rather than descriptive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: [[Davis Cove]], a net-enclosed grow-out impoundment within [[Lake Mohave]], a Colorado River reservoir spanning the Arizona-Nevada border, downstream of [[Davis Dam]].
- **Rearing strategy**: Fish were reared at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] and transported to a protected, predator-excluded enclosure to allow continued growth prior to eventual release into open lake waters ("Mohave proper"), a common conservation aquaculture strategy for endangered catostomids vulnerable to predation by non-native piscivores.
- **Population context**: An additional 10,000 fish from the same hatchery cohort were noted as available, indicating a total production lot of approximately 20,000 fingerlings, with the remainder potentially available for stocking in "pools" below [[Davis Dam]] or other sites.
- **Management framework**: Decisions regarding timing and methodology for releasing grow-out impoundment fish into the open lake were deferred to the [[Native Fish Work Group]], reflecting an adaptive, multi-agency management approach for this federally listed species.

## Key Findings
- **Stocking event**: 10,000 razorback sucker fingerlings (approximately 3 inches) were scheduled for transport from Dexter NFH&TC, arriving at Katherine's Landing (National Park Service facilities), Arizona, on **June 24, 1992**, with an estimated arrival window of 7:00–9:00 a.m. MST.
- **Logistics**: The Bureau of Reclamation (Boulder City, NV) was responsible for providing boat and fish-hauling tank support to move fish from the boat ramp to the prepared net-enclosure site at Davis Cove.
- **Unmarked cohort**: The absence of marking in this release cohort limits future ability to distinguish these fish from other stocked or wild individuals, an important caveat for subsequent population monitoring studies.
- **Surplus stock**: A second batch of 10,000 fingerlings of the same size and lot was available for stocking elsewhere, suggesting flexible allocation across multiple recovery sites in the lower Colorado River basin.
- **Multi-agency coordination**: The memo's distribution list (Dexter FWS, Bureau of Reclamation Denver, FWS Albuquerque and Phoenix, [[Arizona State University]], National Park Service, Arizona Game & Fish, Nevada Department of Wildlife) illustrates the broad interagency collaboration underpinning [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery efforts in [[Lake Mohave]], consistent with recovery actions under the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program.
- **Data gap**: This source contains no survival, growth, water quality, or genetic data — it functions purely as a stocking/transport coordination record, useful primarily for establishing a documented timeline of hatchery-to-field fish releases for this [[Endangered Species|endangered species]].
