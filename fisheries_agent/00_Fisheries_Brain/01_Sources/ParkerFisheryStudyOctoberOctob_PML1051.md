# Parker I Fishery Study (October 1985 to October 1986)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #game-fish

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes the appendices (A through E) of the Parker I Fishery Study, a one-year monitoring program on the [[Lower Colorado River]] between [[Parker Dam]] and [[Headgate Rock Dam]]. The study was prepared by Steven Hlebert and Stephen Grabowski of the Bureau of Reclamation's Division of Research and Laboratory Services (Denver, Colorado) for the Yuma Projects Office, and is dated March 1988. It covers sampling from October 1985 to October 1986 at 15 main-river and backwater sites in the [[Parker I Subdivision]].

The appendices document habitat descriptions, river flows, water quality (dissolved oxygen, temperature, conductivity, light extinction, Secchi depth, major ions), aquatic macrophytes, [[Electrofishing]] catches, larval fish tows, and recaptures of tagged fish. The work supports planned river modification and channel training under the [[Colorado River Modification Plan]].

## Background & Description

**Study area.** The sampling sites range from riprap-lined main-river banks to isolated backwaters. Site descriptions cover:

- **Main-river sites** with large riprap, cobble, or cattail-fringed banks, including a high-velocity riprap site at river mile 169.0 (velocities of 0.61 m/s or more).
- **[[Backwater Habitat]]** of several types (natural, large, medium, small), formed by J-dikes or old river channels. Most have riparian brush, tamarisk, or cattail shorelines.
- **[[Deer Island Lake]] area**, where an upstream dike breach in 1986 changed flow conditions. Repairs in March 1986 reduced flows to less than 0.03 m/s. The site was therefore treated as lotic rather than lentic.

**Sampling design.** Surveys occurred in October 1985, December 1985, February, April, June, August, and October 1986. Methods included electrofishing (with catch per unit effort expressed per minute and per 100 m of bankline), larval fish tows, and tagging with recapture. Macrophyte cover was scored using a combined cover-sociability scale based on [[Braun-Blanquet Sociability Scale]] classes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow regime.** Daily flows were regulated by [[Parker Dam]] and [[Headgate Rock Dam]]. Parker Dam releases ranged from about 15,100 to 21,800 ft³/s, and flows below Headgate Rock Dam were adjusted by gate changes during sampling days. Flows through Deer Island Lake were recorded at about 90 ft³/s on June 17, 1986, and 169 ft³/s on August 19, 1986.

**Water quality.** Figures show dissolved oxygen, temperature, conductivity, light extinction coefficients, and Secchi depths at main-river and backwater sites from October 1985 to October 1986. Light penetration was limited, with Secchi depths generally between about 0.6 m and 3.0 m, and "bottom" recorded at several sites. Major ions (sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium, bicarbonate, sulfate, chloride) were measured in December 1985 and June 1986 at several sites.

**Aquatic vegetation.** Macrophytes were sparse in most sampled areas. Curly-leaf pondweed covered part of Site 8 in October 1985 but was absent by October 1986. Stands of water hyssop (*Bacopa monnieri*) occurred at the backwater edge at Site 8. Cattail (*Typha* sp.) and [[Tamarisk]] dominated many shorelines, and screwbean mesquite and willows occurred along Site 10.

**Substrate and cover.** Riprap sites had little vegetation. Habitat structure in the water included rootwads, submerged logs, and woody debris, which were common at backwaters and vegetated riprap.

## Key Findings

**Fish community.** Electrofishing collected ten game-fish species: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Bluegill Sunfish]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Flathead Catfish]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. Nongame species included [[Common Carp]], mirror carp, goldfish, [[Threadfin Shad]], redbelly tilapia, bullhead, and armored catfish. Largemouth bass and sunfish were the most frequently captured game fish, and carp and shad were the most common nongame fish.

**Catch rates by habitat.** Catch rates were summarized by site and habitat type (riprap with and without vegetation, main-river cattails, riparian structure, backwater muck, and cobble below Headgate Rock Dam). Habitat summary tables (Tables 22–26) report game and nongame catch per 100 m of bank and kilograms per 100 m. Catch rates varied substantially among habitats and seasons. Several backwater and vegetated-riprap sites had higher catches than unvegetated riprap.

**Larval fish.** Larval tows (October 1985 to October 1986, at 14 to 15 sites, sampled in pairs) collected mostly shad, centrarchids, and tilapia. Densities were mostly 0 to 0.28 fish/m³, with the highest counts in October 1985 (243 shad in one tow).

**Tagged fish recaptures.** Recaptures of tagged largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, redear sunfish, and a softshell turtle were recorded, mainly in riprap and backwater habitats. Most recaptured fish showed small changes in measured value since tagging, with several showing no change. Changes ranged from about −0.31 to +0.47 (units as reported in the source).

**Aquatic macrophytes.** Cover ratings were generally low (mostly +.1 or 0) at Sites 11, 14, and 15 across the sampling dates. Algae and mosses were the most frequent non-vascular cover. Macrophyte cover was not recorded at some visits.

**Management implications.** The authors describe backwater, cattail-bank, and vegetated-riprap habitats as likely to become important features after river training structures are installed in the Parker II subdivision. Riprap with little vegetation was predicted to dominate newly riprapped reaches.
