## Executive Summary

This table catalogs nine terrestrial mammal taxa (or subspecies) identified as **umbrella and corridor design species** for regional conservation planning, likely associated with the Sonoran Desert Conservation Plan in Pima County, Arizona. These species were selected because their large home ranges, habitat breadth, and sensitivity to fragmentation make them effective proxies ("umbrella species") for designing habitat corridors that would also protect co-occurring biodiversity. Several taxa are federally listed as endangered under the [[Endangered Species Act]], and multiple species are documented as **locally extirpated** from Pima County, with historic records dating to the early-to-mid 20th century (e.g., 1920s–1952 records in the Catalina and Rincon Mountains).

These species function ecologically as **habitat connectivity indicators**: their large territorial and dispersal requirements mean that corridors designed to sustain their populations inherently protect a broader suite of co-occurring flora and fauna — the classic "umbrella species" conservation strategy.

## Key Findings

- **Federal Endangered Species Act listings** apply to: [[Sonoran Pronghorn]], [[Mexican Gray Wolf]], [[Ocelot]], [[Jaguar]] — all listed as "endangered."
- **No federal or state listing** currently applies to [[Mule Deer]], [[Mountain Lion]], or the [[Jaguarundi]] (though jaguarundi sightings remain unconfirmed in the region).
- **Local extirpation** is documented for the [[Mexican Gray Wolf]] and [[Grizzly Bear]] in Pima County, with last confirmed records around **1952** (wolf, Catalina Mountains) and the **1920s** (grizzly bear, Catalina/Rincon Mountains).
- The [[Mexican Gray Wolf]] has an experimental nonessential population reintroduced in the **Blue Primitive Area**, with unconfirmed reports of individuals in southern Arizona.
- The [[Jaguar]] has been confirmed via photographs near the Mexico/Arizona border and south-central Arizona, indicating potential recolonization or transboundary movement.
- Historic ranges for several species (e.g., [[Mexican Gray Wolf]], [[Chihuahuan Pronghorn]]) were likely broader than present-day distributions, with [[Chihuahuan Pronghorn]] formerly ranging east toward Tucson.
- The [[Sonoran Pronghorn]] population's distribution extends transboundary into Mexico's **Pinacate Biosphere Reserve**, highlighting the importance of international habitat corridor planning.
- This table underscores the conservation planning rationale of using wide-ranging, sensitive mammals as **umbrella/corridor design species** to guide regional land-use and habitat connectivity decisions in the Sonoran Desert ecosystem.
