# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Recommendations February 1989

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] Leader to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director outlines six critical recommendations regarding endangered fish recovery in the [[Colorado River]] system. Primary concerns include upgrading the listing status of [[Razorback Sucker]] from threatened to endangered, addressing habitat fragmentation threats to [[Colorado Squawfish]], and establishing protocols for non-native species introductions. The letter also announces leadership transition within the Recovery Team.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] convened February 23-24, 1989, to review three recovery plans and address management issues for endangered native fishes. Three species are central to these discussions: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, family [[Catostomidae]]), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) (both family [[Cyprinidae]]). 

The [[Razorback Sucker]] population faces critical challenges with numbers considerably less than [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]], and no recruitment occurring. A Region 2 Ten Year MOU for stocking razorback suckers in the Lower Basin in lieu of listing was set to expire May 29, 1991.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitat fragmentation represents a serious threat to [[Colorado Squawfish]] recovery and survival, with scientific evidence supporting this position. The species' existing unrestricted range requires protection, and fragmentation sometimes results in habitat loss.

Non-native species introductions pose additional concerns, specifically the [[Utah Department of Wildlife Resources]] proposal to stock [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Powell]]. The Recovery Team emphasizes the need to demonstrate "absence of potential conflict with rare or endangered species" before such introductions proceed.

Field collection methods including electrofishing, netting, and handling procedures require evaluation to minimize trauma and ensure survival of endangered fishes. Concerns exist regarding stress from collection and handling operations, particularly in the [[San Juan River]] and LCR (Lower Colorado River).

## Key Findings

1. **Listing Status Recommendation**: The Recovery Team reaffirms support for formal listing of [[Razorback Sucker]] and recommends upgrading from threatened to endangered status due to population vulnerability, low numbers compared to other species, and lack of recruitment.

2. **Management Plan Development**: The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] should complete the Razorback Sucker Management Plan scheduled for FY 1989 under the Recovery Implementation Program. Team members offered to participate in development and requested opportunity to review the draft. California indicated potential financial contribution.

3. **Non-Native Species Protocol**: The Team recommends that [[Utah Department of Wildlife Resources]], as Recovery Implementation Plan participant, demonstrate absence of potential conflict with endangered species before stocking [[Rainbow Smelt]] in [[Lake Powell]]. The Service and states should develop procedures, including studies, as directed in Recovery Implementation Plan section 4.4.3.

4. **Habitat Fragmentation**: The Team unanimously agrees that fragmentation of [[Colorado Squawfish]] range constitutes a serious threat to recovery and possibly survival. Scientific evidence supports this position, with additional documentation to be presented in the revised recovery plan.

5. **Carlin Tagging Policy**: Continued tagging has value in certain areas (San Juan and LCR). However, tagging should only be pursued when part of approved study design. Primary concern is collection and handling stress rather than tag use itself. The Service should evaluate existing and alternative procedures for electrofishing, netting, and handling to minimize trauma.

6. **Leadership Transition**: Steve Petersburg ([[National Park Service]]) nominated as Team Leader; Reed Harris ([[Bureau of Reclamation]]) proposed as Team Secretary. W.A. St. Amant resigned as Team Leader after serving as member since 1974 and Leader since 1980, accepting assignment to head California Department of Fish and Game's [[Desert Tortoise]] Management Team.

7. **Implementation Priority**: Final recommendation emphasizes expediting recovery plan finalization and calls for U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to take strong aggressive lead in implementation.
