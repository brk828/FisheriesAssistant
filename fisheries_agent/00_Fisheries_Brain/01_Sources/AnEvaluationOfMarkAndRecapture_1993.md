# An Evaluation of Mark and Recapture Methods for Determining Colorado Squawfish Recruitment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This FY93 annual report for the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project #18(4)) evaluated marking and mark-recapture methodologies for assessing recruitment, abundance, and overwinter survival of underyearling (age-0 and age-1) [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the [[Green River]]. Three marking techniques — dental inoculator tattoo-ink, syringe-injected elastic polymer, and fin clipping — were compared for mark retention, associated mortality, and predation vulnerability. The **syringe-injected elastic polymer** emerged as the superior method, demonstrating high retention (up to 250 days), low mortality, and ease of field application. A field pilot study conducted on the Green River (river mile 290–300) between 06 April and 22 April 1993 confirmed that mark-recapture methods could reliably estimate population size and overwinter survival, outperforming traditional catch-per-unit-effort ([[CPUE]]) approaches in accuracy and precision.

## Background & Description
While this report is primarily methodological rather than morphometric, key marking-related physical observations include:
- **Mark types tested on underyearling fish:**
  - Dental inoculator with tattoo-ink — visible dye mark, but associated with **greater mortality**.
  - Fin clipping — simple mechanical mark, but exhibited **poor retention** over the 142-day study period.
  - Syringe-injected elastic polymer (e.g., visible implant elastomer) — produced marks that were **readily visible**, retained for up to **250 days**, with **good survival** outcomes.
- Only two colors of elastic polymer were commercially available at the time of study: **"rocket red"** and **"arc yellow"** (supplied by Northwest Marine Technology), with additional colors in development.
- No morphological differences in predation vulnerability were detected between marked and unmarked underyearling fish for any of the three tested techniques.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Life stage focus:** Age-0 (young-of-year) and age-1 (underyearling) [[Colorado Squawfish]], a critical and understudied life stage for population recruitment modeling.
- **Habitat use:** Fish were studied in **backwater habitats** of the Green River, a key nursery habitat type for juvenile [[Colorado Squawfish]] within the Upper Colorado River Basin.
- **Movement ecology:** The study aimed to characterize:
  - Daily movement patterns of juveniles within a selected river reach.
  - Distribution patterns among available backwaters.
  - Downstream movement in relation to seasonal flow regimes (fall, winter, spring).
- **Flow-ecology linkage:** Overwinter survival of underyearling squawfish was hypothesized to be influenced by flow releases from [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], linking hydrological management directly to recruitment success.
- **Predation dynamics:** Controlled experiments assessed whether marking increased vulnerability to predation — a critical consideration for validating that marked individuals behave/survive comparably to unmarked wild fish.

## Key Findings
1. **Mark Longevity Experiment (completed 22 April 1992, extended monitoring through 04 August 1993):**
   - All three marks (dye tattoo, elastic polymer, fin clip) were retained long enough to support population estimates over a 21-day window.
   - Dental inoculator tattoo-ink caused **higher mortality** in marked fish.
   - Fin clips showed **poor retention** across the full 142-day trial.
   - Syringe-injected elastic polymer had the best combined performance: **good survival and retention through 250 days**.
   - **No significant difference in predation vulnerability** was found among marked and unmarked fish across all three techniques — supporting the validity of mark-recapture assumptions.

2. **Field Pilot Study (06–22 April 1993, Green River RM 290–300):**
   - Confirmed the elastic polymer mark was **easy to apply in field conditions**, caused **low mortality**, and produced **clearly visible marks**.
   - Demonstrated that mark-recapture methods could generate valid estimates of **population size** and **overwinter survival**.
   - The **closed population assumption** (a core requirement for standard mark-recapture models such as [[Lincoln-Petersen Estimator]]) was met under field conditions.
   - Mark-recapture estimates of survival were **more accurate and precise** than estimates derived from traditional [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPUE) methods.

3. **Programmatic Recommendations:**
   - Population estimates for underyearling [[Colorado Squawfish]] should be incorporated into the **annual ISMP monitoring program** in Green River reaches where juveniles are abundant.
   - Mark-recapture population estimates should run **concurrently with ISMP CPUE sampling for at least two years** to calibrate and cross-validate both methods, ensuring continuity of long-term trend data interpretation.

4. **Budgetary Status:** FY91 funds ($24,000 provided) were fully expended with no discrepancy, indicating the study remained on schedule and budget through its final reporting period.
