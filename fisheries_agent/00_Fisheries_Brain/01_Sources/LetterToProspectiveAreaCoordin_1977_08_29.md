## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary research paper but an administrative letter from James E. Deacon, Chairman of the [[Desert Fishes Council]], addressed to Dr. W. L. Minckley and other prospective **Area Coordinators**. The letter announces a restructuring of the Council's regional coordination system, aimed at improving conservation monitoring and reporting for threatened and endangered desert fish faunas across the arid western United States and northern Mexico. It establishes a two-year appointment term for area coordinators, a reporting structure (written report by September 1, verbal report at the November annual meeting), and designates Chuck Minckley (Northern Arizona University) as the coordinator-of-coordinators tasked with compiling a new [[Desert Fishes Council Newsletter]].

## Key Findings
- The [[Desert Fishes Council]] restructured its **Area Coordinator** program to increase organizational effectiveness in monitoring desert fish conservation status.
- **Chuck Minckley** (Dept. of Biological Sciences, Northern Arizona University) was appointed to coordinate communications among all regional Area Coordinators and to compile a new Council newsletter.
- Area Coordinators were given latitude to define their own regional priorities — whether focusing on a **single species**, a **comprehensive faunal review**, or a **specific taxonomic group** — reflecting a flexible, locally-driven conservation strategy.
- A formal reporting cycle was instituted:
  - Written report due to Chuck Minckley by **September 1**
  - Verbal report at the **Desert Fishes Council annual meeting** in **November**
- Area Coordinator appointments were set at a **two-year term**.
- Twelve named individuals were designated as prospective Area Coordinators for defined desert regions, including notable ichthyologists **W. L. Minckley**, **R. R. Miller**, **Salvador Contreras-Balderas**, **Phil Pister**, and **Carl Bond**, among others — figures central to the historical development of [[Desert Fish Conservation]] in North America.
- This letter reflects an early institutional effort (1977) to formalize a **network-based, regionally distributed conservation monitoring system** for threatened and endangered desert fishes, a precursor to modern collaborative conservation frameworks.
