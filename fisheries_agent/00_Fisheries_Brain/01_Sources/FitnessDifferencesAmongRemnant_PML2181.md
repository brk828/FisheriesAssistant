## Background & Description

**Sonoran Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*)**
- Endangered live-bearing fish of the family [[Poeciliidae]] (order Atheriniformes), native to the [[Gila River]] system in Arizona.
- Remnant natural populations have low genetic variation. A hatchery stock derived from [[Monkey Spring]] was being used in reintroductions within the Gila River drainage.
- Study populations: [[Monkey Spring]] (electrophoretically monomorphic, no detectable variation), [[Tule Spring]] (intermediate heterozygosity, a distinct subspecies *P. o. sonoriensis* per the authors' note), and [[Sharp Spring]] (most heterozygous, mean heterozygosity 3.7%).
- Co-occurring predator: [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), which the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service adopted in part for its current species recovery strategy.

**Malaria sporozoite serology (page 1)**
- Work performed at the [[Malaria Branch]], [[Centers for Disease Control]], Atlanta, GA.
- Blood-stage parasites and sporozoites of *P. vivax* from Kanchanaburi Province, Thailand (designated VK244, VK210, VK247) were tested. Some could not be distinguished morphologically from other species.
- Monoclonal and polyclonal antibodies (CDC, NMRI, New York University, Walter Reed Army Institute) recognized at least three CS protein epitopes, per unpublished data by R. A. Wirtz et al.
- Diagnostic primers and probes (e.g., probe 165R) for small-subunit rRNA were used to distinguish *P. vivax* from *P. ovale*.

**Subplate neurons (page 3 fragment)**
- [[Ghosh and Shatz]] describe [[Subplate Neurons]] in the developing [[Mammalian Telencephalon]] that are among the first postmitotic neurons of the [[Cerebral Cortex]].
- These neurons pioneer the first axon pathway through the [[Internal Capsule]] before cortical layer 5 and 6 neurons send projections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sonoran Topminnow populations**
- Remnant populations in Arizona show low genetic variation. Electrophoretic surveys indicated the [[Monkey Spring]] stock had none detectable.
- Monkey Spring water is thermally stable, while Tule and Sharp Spring fluctuate more, a difference the authors link to adaptive potential in fluctuating environments.
- Historical processes such as severe population bottlenecks, founder events, inbreeding, and migration are expected to reduce heterozygosity across loci.
- The authors suggest that mixing stocks could cause outbreeding depression, a decrease in fitness from negative interactions between differentially adapted genotypes.
- Remnant populations such as Monkey Spring should be maintained as isolated reservoirs of potential genetic variation.

**Experimental environment**
- Laboratory conditions were controlled through a flow-through incubator, which closely matched the thermally stable environment of Monkey Spring. The authors note the lab represented a novel environment and was not a true mimic of field conditions.

**Malaria transmission**
- Sporozoites were dissected from mosquito feeds and tested for antibody reactivity. Some antibodies were tested against nonreactive sporozoites from the same patient-mosquito feed, and some were tested in pools.

## Key Findings

### Sonoran Topminnow Fitness (Quattro & Vrijenhoek)
- **Study design:** Four fitness correlates (survival to 12 weeks, growth, early fecundity, developmental stability) were measured in three stocks. Ten gravid females per locality produced broods in the laboratory, and progeny were reared in individual containers for a 12-week test period.
- **Survival:** Heterogeneous among populations (Wilcoxon rank test, χ² = 8.98, P ≈ 0.02). Monkey Spring showed the poorest survival (mean 9.0 weeks, SE 0.5). Tule Spring and Sharp Spring averaged 10.4 weeks (SE 0.4), with Tule Spring equaling Sharp Spring.
- **Growth:** Analyzed separately for each sex because males grow more slowly after maturation. Growth differed among the three populations for both sexes (females: ANOVA F(2, 39) = ... P < 0.001; males: F(2, 61) = 17.98, P < 0.001). Monkey Spring fish grew slowest and Sharp Spring fish grew fastest.
- **Fecundity at 12 weeks:** Differed among stocks (ANCOVA, adjusted for standard length, P ≈ 0.005). Sharp Spring females produced more eggs per unit length than the larger Monkey Spring females, which yielded fewer but larger eggs.
- **Egg diameter:** No significant difference among stocks (ANOVA F = 0.11, P = 0.86).
- **Developmental stability (fluctuating asymmetry, FA):** Measured on bilateral meristic characters (sample sizes n = 25 for Monkey Spring, n = 19 for Tule Spring; the Sharp Spring sample is not legible in the OCR text). Three of seven characters differed significantly among stocks. Sharp Spring had the lowest FA and differed significantly from the homozygous Monkey Spring stock. Across populations, FA tended to decrease as mean heterozygosity increased.
- **Overall conclusion:** The most heterozygous stock (Sharp Spring) showed the highest survival, growth, fecundity, and developmental stability. The homozygous Monkey Spring stock performed worst even under the favorable thermally stable laboratory conditions.
- **Caveat:** Enzyme polymorphisms are only a small fraction of the genome and are not claimed to be the primary determinants of fitness. The correlation is interpreted as reflecting the historical demography of each population.

### Recovery Recommendations
- Using Sharp Spring topminnows for reintroduction into reclaimed habitats is suggested, because higher fecundity and survival should promote rapid establishment.
- Continuous genetic monitoring and periodic restocking may be needed to offset genetic drift at isolated localities.
- The authors conclude that small viviparous desert fish like the Sonoran Topminnow are ideal for testing general hypotheses about the genetic and evolutionary consequences of species recovery programs.

### Malaria Sporozoite Serology (Page 1)
- The IFAT panel used nonreactive sporozoites and anti-*P. vivax* monoclonal antibodies (NMRI NS3 and NS4; NYU 2F2; CDC CDC11; Walter Reed WRAIR 3, 7, 9, 21, 28). These recognized at least three CS protein epitopes.
- Antibodies to other *Plasmodium* species were also tested: *P. malariae*, *P. ovale*, *P. cynomolgi*, *P. knowlesi*, and *P. coatneyi*.
- Antibodies were protein A purified and tested at 2.5 or 5 μg/ml, and all were IFAT positive with the homologous sporozoite.
- Molecular methods: PCR amplification of 2.6 × 10³ sporozoites with 25 cycles, followed by cloning into pBluescript SK–. Sequences were deposited in GenBank (accessions M25759 for VK210 and M25760 for VK247). Sequencing of VK247 showed identical sequences on repeat PCR, ruling out sequencing error.

### Subplate Neurons (Page 3 Fragment)
- Subplate neurons are the first postmitotic cortical neurons and establish early axon pathways before the neurons of cortical layers 5 and 6 form their projections.
- Subplate cells disappear during postnatal life as adult connections are established. The authors suggest early axonal projections by subplate cells may be essential for permanent subcortical connections. The fragment is incomplete and the full results are not available in this extract.
