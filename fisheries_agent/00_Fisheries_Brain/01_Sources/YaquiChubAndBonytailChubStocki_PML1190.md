# Yaqui Chub and Bonytail Chub Stocking Memorandum (1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1986 interagency memorandum from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] manager to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Regional Director, [[Region 2]] (Albuquerque, New Mexico), reports the November 4, 1986 stocking of native southwestern fishes in Arizona. The stocked species were the [[Yaqui Chub]] and the [[Bonytail Chub]], both native to the Southwest. The memorandum also gives the hatchery's current holdings and the status of its Yaqui Chub stock. The OCR scan is badly degraded, so the stocking table cannot be fully reconstructed and the numbers below are limited to those that are legible.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum, dated November 6, 1986.
- **Subject:** "Yaqui Chub and Bonytail Chub Stockings."
- **Sender:** Hatchery Manager, [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Dexter, New Mexico.
- **Recipient:** Regional Director, Region 2, Albuquerque, New Mexico.
- **Distribution:** Multiple U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service staff, including biologists and hatchery personnel.
- **Target species:**
  - [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), a small cyprinid of the Yaqui River basin in Arizona and Mexico.
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), a large-bodied cyprinid of the Colorado River basin, recognized as a native southwestern fish.
- **Stocking table:** The original table lists stocking dates, numbers, mean lengths or weights, and site names, with stocking sites in Arizona. Most of it is illegible in the scan, so only the overall structure is reported here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native southwestern fishes:** The memorandum frames the stockings as conservation actions for native southwestern fishes, a group that has declined sharply under habitat change and non-native species pressure.
- **Source stock:** Fish for the Arizona release were collected at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], where field staff loaded and stocked them into refuge tanks.
- **Hatchery role:** Dexter National Fish Hatchery supplied the propagated fish and carried out the stocking, with close support from refuge biologists.
- **Propagation:** The Bonytail Chub fingerlings came from natural reproduction in two egg-collection ponds at the hatchery, which shows that the species could be propagated in captivity.

## Key Findings

1. **November 4, 1986 stocking event:** Dexter National Fish Hatchery personnel stocked native southwestern fishes in Arizona on this date.
2. **Yaqui Chub stock status:** The Yaqui Chub stocking represented the hatchery's entire stock of this species. The memorandum states that the station no longer holds *Gila purpurea*.
3. **Bonytail Chub propagation:** Fingerlings were produced by natural reproduction in the two hatchery egg-collection ponds, and the memorandum records the hatchery's current holdings as legible numbers only (figures partly garbled).
4. **Current holdings:** The memorandum records two holding figures for Bonytail Chub, one of about 11,372 excess fish. The size of the fish is given as roughly 15 fish per pound, though the scan makes this figure uncertain.
5. **Field logistics:** Fish were collected at Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge by Dan Langhorst and Mark Young, who also assisted with stocking at the refuge tanks.
6. **Acknowledgments:** Steve Dobrott, a biologist at Buenos Aires, led the stocking effort and was credited for helpful support during the visit.
7. **Distribution list:** The memorandum was sent to roughly ten U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service staff members, including regional and hatchery personnel, among them Paul Marsh.

**Data note:** The original table of stocking sites, dates, numbers, and sizes is largely unreadable. Any quantitative claim beyond the points above should be checked against the original paper file.
