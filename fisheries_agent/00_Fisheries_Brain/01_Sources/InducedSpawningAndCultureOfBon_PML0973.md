# Induced Spawning and Culture of Bonytail Chub

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Roger L. Hamman]] of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], documents the first successful spawning of wild-caught [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) in captivity and the culture of their progeny in 1981. The species is endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], has declined more sharply than any other native mainstem fish, and is listed as endangered. Hormone-induced spawning, artificial fertilization, and incubation at three water temperatures produced fry that were reared to fingerling size over 70 days. The author concludes that hatchery propagation may be needed to restore the species.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The [[Bonytail Chub]] is a cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River basin. It was once abundant in both the upper and lower basins. In recent years, the upper basin reports came only from the [[Green River]] in Utah. Occasional records exist from reservoirs of [[Lake Havasu]] and [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Conservation status:** The species is listed as endangered by Arizona, California, Nevada, Utah, and Wyoming, and by the U.S. Department of the Interior (1980).
- **Study aim:** To develop techniques for spawning and culturing the species, with data from the 1981 season at Willow Beach.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Broodstock source:** Six wild females and five wild males were collected from [[Lake Mohave]] near Cottonwood Cove, in collections spanning April–May 1979, April–May 1980, and March 1981 (the OCR-garbled dates were reconstructed from context).
- **Natural spawning cue:** Spawning is thought to occur when water temperature reaches about 18 °C. Earlier work reported June–July spawning in the upper Green River, but [[Jonez and Sumner]] (1954) observed May spawning in Lake Mohave. The 1981 spawning occurred in May at 20–21 °C.
- **Decline drivers:** The decline is linked to [[Dam Construction]], which created cold downstream water, reduced flows, and lost habitat. The author cites a 96% egg mortality at cold incubation temperatures as support for this link.
- **Hybridization risk:** The species can hybridize with [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). Hybrids of bonytail × roundtail and bonytail × humpback were produced in a hatchery (Hamman 1981).
- **Rearing food web:** Fry ponds and raceways were fertilized to produce a [[Plankton Bloom]]. Zooplankton, predominantly [[Cladocerans]], appeared about 10 days later. Declining zooplankton by the third week coincided with fish dispersing through the culture system.

## Key Findings

**Broodstock and induced ovulation**
- Five of six females ovulated 18–20 h after injection with 4 mg per kg body weight of acetone-dried [[Carp Pituitary Induction]] material. One female failed to ovulate after three injections.
- Males were producing active sperm before any female could be stripped. Breeding males showed light orange coloration at the pectoral and pelvic fin bases. Females did not show breeding colors.
- Anesthesia used [[Tricaine Methanesulfonate]] before intraperitoneal injection.

**Egg production**
- Egg diameter: 2.0–2.3 mm (mean 2.2 mm).
- Volumetric counts: 103–151 eggs/mL.
- Mean [[Fecundity]]: 25,090 eggs per female (total 125,450 for five females), or 21,514 eggs per kg body weight.

**Incubation and hatching (by water temperature)**

| Temperature | Hatching period | Egg survival | Swim-up survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20–21 °C | 99–174 h | ~90% | ~98% |
| 16–17 °C | 170–269 h | ~55% | ~96% |
| 12–13 °C | 334–498 h | ~4% | Lowest of the three |

- Newly hatched fry averaged 6.8 mm total length and 2.8 mg.
- Warm-water incubation gave the best egg and swim-up survival, supporting an optimum near 20–21 °C.

**Culture performance**
- Of the swim-up fry, 26,430 were placed in raceways B1 and B2, and about 80,000 were sent to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico.
- Fry doubled in length by day 28. Fry were fed commercial trout starter from day 21, and most accepted prepared feed by day 28.
- On day 70, 18,775 fingerlings (mean 49.5 mm TL, 974.6 mg) were harvested, a survival rate of 71%. Fish grew about 7-fold in length and 348-fold in weight over the 70 days.

**Water quality during culture**
- Temperature 12.8–24.4 °C; carbon dioxide 1.8–4.4 mg/L; ammonia nitrogen 0.01–0.12 mg/L; dissolved oxygen 7.6–10.0 mg/L; pH 7.8–9.0.

**Disease management**
- On day 42, [[Ichthyophthirus Multifiliis]] (white spot parasite) was identified on two dead fish. Three alternate-day [[Formalin Treatment]] doses at 15 mg/L were given. The parasite was believed eliminated by day 49, but weekly prophylactic treatments continued.

**Conclusions**
- The author concludes that the species' survival may depend on [[Hatchery Propagation]] for restocking selected habitats and supplementing wild populations. Hatchery conditions also offer a way to study reproduction, early life history, and behavior, which the author considers necessary for species survival.
