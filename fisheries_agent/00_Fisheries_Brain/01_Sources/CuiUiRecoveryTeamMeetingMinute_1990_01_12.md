# Cui-ui Recovery Team Meeting Minutes — Reno, Nevada (January 12, 1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #conservation-genetics #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This document records the minutes of a [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] meeting held in Reno, Nevada, on January 12, 1990. The central scientific finding presented was an electrophoretic (allozyme) analysis conducted by guest researcher Peter Brussard, aimed at resolving whether the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) hybridizes with the sympatric [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*) in [[Pyramid Lake]]. The analysis conclusively found no evidence of hybridization, a result with major implications for the species' conservation management, leading the team to disband a special task force previously overseeing spawning-run management. The meeting also addressed policy issues, including the potential effects of Senator Reid's Water Settlement Bill (S.1554) on water rights allocation and the biological definition of "recovery" for this endemic catostomid.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological measurements are reported in this text; the analysis relies on **molecular/genetic characterization** rather than external morphology.
- **Electrophoretic (allozyme) profile:**
  - 26 presumptive genetic loci were scored across 14 enzyme systems.
  - 14 loci showed **identical electrophoretic mobility** across pure [[Cui-ui]], pure [[Tahoe Sucker]], and purported hybrid individuals.
  - 12 loci exhibited **fixed or nearly fixed allelic differences** between the two pure parental groups (cui-ui vs. Tahoe sucker), providing strong diagnostic markers for distinguishing the taxa genetically.
  - Purported hybrid specimens shared **no Tahoe sucker-specific alleles** at these 12 diagnostic loci and were genetically **identical to pure cui-ui** across all 26 loci examined.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** [[Cui-ui]] is endemic to [[Pyramid Lake]], a terminal desert lake in the Lahontan Basin of Nevada, with dependency on the [[Truckee River]] for spawning migrations.
- **Sympatric species interactions:** Cui-ui co-occurs with the [[Tahoe Sucker]] in the same lake system, raising historical concern about potential hybridization between the two catostomid species.
- **Anthropogenic barriers:** [[Marble Bluff Dam]] is identified as a critical obstacle restricting free fish passage over the river delta, directly affecting spawning-run access and being explicitly targeted for future elimination as part of recovery criteria.
- **Water dependency and habitat threats:** Lake water levels are identified as a key limiting factor; a "minimum lake level" is required to sustain the population. The team also discussed how water-rights purchases under the pending [[Water Settlement Bill (S.1554)]] — intended partly for wetland acquisition — could compete with and potentially exhaust water available for cui-ui recovery efforts before recovery actions are implemented.

## Key Findings
1. **No hybridization detected:** Brussard's electrophoretic analysis of 26 loci across 14 enzyme systems found **no genetic evidence of hybridization** between cui-ui and Tahoe suckers; purported hybrids were genetically indistinguishable from pure cui-ui.
2. **Statistical confidence:** The calculated probability of hybridization having occurred was **0.041** (based on a sample of 6 fish across 26 loci); this would drop to **<0.01** with a larger sample (25 fish), and to as low as **1 in 1,000–10,000** when considering F1 hybrid probabilities specifically.
3. **Management implication:** Based on these genetic findings, the Recovery Team concluded there was **no continued need for the Special Task Force** previously established to oversee and manage cui-ui spawning runs for hybridization concerns.
4. **Policy/water rights concerns:** Senator Reid's [[Water Settlement Bill (S.1554)]] may require Environmental Impact Statements and could authorize water-rights purchases for wetlands that compete with cui-ui recovery water needs; the team recommended **coordination within the Fish and Wildlife Service** on water rights acquisitions.
5. **Recovery definition debate:** The team emphasized that species recovery must be defined via **biological criteria** (e.g., minimum lake level, natural spawning runs, unimpeded passage past [[Marble Bluff Dam]]) rather than solely through ongoing human management actions — with a stated goal of minimizing long-term anthropogenic intervention.
6. **Research need identified:** The team called for development of a comprehensive **bibliography of the Pyramid Lake–Truckee River ecosystem** to support future recovery planning.
