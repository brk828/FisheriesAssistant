# Fort Huachuca Groundwater Impacts on San Pedro River Ecosystem - Legal Correspondence

#conservation #riparian-ecology #environmental-law #endangered-species #groundwater-management

## Executive Summary

This June 1997 correspondence from Dr. Robin Silver of the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] to the Commander of [[Fort Huachuca]] documents serious concerns about groundwater depletion impacts on the [[San Pedro River]] ecosystem. The letter challenges the Army's Environmental Assessment for personnel expansion, arguing it violates [[NEPA]] (National Environmental Policy Act) by failing to analyze cumulative effects of maintaining over 30,000 military personnel and associated population in the watershed. The [[San Pedro River]] is described as the last living river in the Southwest, supporting 489 vertebrate species including 24 federally protected species, and recognized as a Globally Important Bird Area. USGS data shows 67% reduction in low flows from 1943-1992. A 1997 University of Arizona study confirms groundwater pumping outside the [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]] has captured water that would otherwise discharge to the river, with effects apparent since 1991-1992.

## Background & Description

The [[San Pedro River]] supports the most extensive surviving expanse of cottonwood/willow gallery forest in North America, one of the rarest forest types on the continent. The river hosts 489 species of birds, mammals, fish, amphibians, and reptiles, with nearly half of North America's 800 bird species frequenting the area at some point in their lives. In 1996, the [[American Bird Conservancy]] designated it a "Globally Important Bird Area."

[[Fort Huachuca]] maintains approximately 30,000-34,533 personnel (military, families, civilian employees, and retirees) who depend entirely on groundwater. According to the FY96 Impact Statement, 16,928 military personnel and family members constitute nearly half of [[Sierra Vista]]'s 37,815 population. The base supports approximately 44.8% of jobs in [[Cochise County]]. On-post activities account for approximately 28% of the area's total yearly water deficit, with off-post activities by Fort Huachuca-associated people responsible for most of the remainder.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]] (SPRNCA) provides critical habitat for multiple federally protected species:

**Threatened and Endangered Species:**
- [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] - uses SPRNCA as migratory stopover and breeding habitat
- [[Huachuca Water Umbel]] - found primarily within SPRNCA
- [[Desert Pupfish]] - requires river for recovery
- [[Gila Topminnow]] - native fish dependent on perennial flows
- [[Loach Minnow]] - federally protected native fish
- [[Spikedace]] - federally protected native fish
- [[Razorback Sucker]] - requires San Pedro for recovery
- [[Jaguar]] - documented on base in 1963 checklist

Historical base documents prove awareness of the problem since 1970. A July 1970 Department of the Army report stated: "The ground water in the area of the post well field is overdrawn, and a large cone of depression has been formed in the water table. Water levels in the area of influence (a radius of 1 to 2 miles) have continued to decline and will continue until and unless pumping is reduced."

The 1997 University of Arizona study by Sharma, MacNish, and Maddock found that "ground water inflow to the San Pedro River between Lewis Springs and the Charleston Bridge has diminished, starting in 1991 or 1992, with the only viable explanation being that pumpage outside the San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area has captured ground water that would have discharged to the river."

## Key Findings

**Legal and Regulatory Violations:**
- The Army never completed required cumulative effects analysis under NEPA for BRAC (Base Realignment and Closure) rounds in 1989, 1991, 1993, and 1995
- August 30, 1995 District Court ruling found the Army's Environmental Impact Statement "fails to satisfy the requirements of the NEPA as it fails to supply cumulative impact analysis on the River, the Riparian Area, and the associated ecosystem"
- Judge Marquez ordered: "In future environmental impact analysis, the Army must strive to address the cumulative impacts of continued expansion activities on the River and Riparian Area"
- April 1997 Environmental Assessment for Regionalization of Civilian Personnel Administrative Functions issued Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) without baseline cumulative analysis
- Army repeatedly delayed promised Master Plan EIS: announced for 1993, then October 1995, then April 1996, then August 1996 - never completed

**Hydrological Impacts:**
- USGS stream flow data shows 67% decrease in low flows over 50-year period (1943-1992)
- Fort Huachuca on-post activities responsible for approximately 28% of area's total yearly water deficit
- Off-post activities by Fort Huachuca-associated population responsible for most remaining deficit
- Cone of depression from Sierra Vista/Fort Huachuca pumping expanding toward river
- Nearly half (8,272 of 16,928) military personnel and families live off-post, outside cantonment boundaries

**Population and Growth Pressures:**
- Fort Huachuca area population: 34,533 people (September 1996)
- Cochise County total population: 112,300 (1996)
- Proposed action would add 150 employees, estimated 250-300 total people with multiplier effects
- 1996 Cochise County Board of Supervisors approved plan for uncontrolled growth
- Sierra Vista wastewater recharge project described as "short-term Ponzi scheme" that couples river survival to mandatory city growth

**Conservation Measures Criticized as Inadequate:**
- Fort Huachuca received FY94 Federal Water Conservation Award while violating environmental laws
- On-post conservation efforts do not address off-post impacts of associated population
- Agricultural land retirement has helped but low flows continue decreasing
- Proposed mountain front recharge contradicts flood prevention infrastructure
- Nature Conservancy's land acquisition does not assure water in river
- BLM beaver reintroduction project lacks adequate monitoring funding

**Institutional Failures:**
- US Fish and Wildlife Service concurred with Army determination of no off-post effects, failing stewardship responsibility
- Arizona Department of Water Resources removed requirements for developers to assure future water
- Federal agencies (BLM, Bureau of Indian Affairs, FWS) abandoned public trust responsibility for water rights
- Local Natural Resource Conservation District engaged in "inter-personal intimidation" against river advocates

**Piecemealing and Evasion:**
- September 1995: Army moved 158 additional National Guard troops using Categorical Exclusion
- Army breaks projects into small components to avoid cumulative analysis
- Tiering current action to other DoD actions while never establishing baseline is "deceptive and illegal"
- Court Orders pending for Critical Habitat designation for Southwestern Willow Flycatcher and Huachuca Water Umbel by July 20, 1997

The correspondence concludes that without dramatic reduction in Fort Huachuca's personnel and control of uncontrolled growth, "there will be no San Pedro River within the foreseeable future." The author announces intent to return to US District Court to challenge the Army's continued violations.
