# Depth, Temperature, Coloration and Tidal Spawning in Temperate Pacific Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Embiotocidae #Blenniidae #Leuresthes

## Executive Summary

This Southern California Academy of Sciences bulletin issue (Volume 75, 1974–1975) contains three field and laboratory studies on Pacific fishes. The first examines depth and thermal orientation of five [[Surfperch]] species at King Harbor, California. The second describes the signal value of coloration in five [[Hypsoblennius]] blennies from California to Peru. The third analyzes how tides and waves control the spawning runs of the Gulf of California [[Gulf Grunion]]. Common threads are behavioral responses to temperature, habitat structure and tidal or lunar cycles.

## Background & Description

**Surfperch depth and temperature study.** The study took place from August 1974 to July 1975 at King Harbor, California, using diver transects. It covers five surfperch (family [[Embiotocidae]]): [[Micrometrus Minimus]], [[Embiotoca Jacksoni]], *Rhacochilus vacca*, *Hyperprosopon caryi* and *Phanerodon furcatus*. The introductory portion of the paper is missing from the scan, so the full methods are not available.

**Hypsoblennius coloration study.** Losey examined five Pacific species of [[Hypsoblennius]] (family Blenniidae), small territorial blennies that shelter in tidal pools, subtidal reefs and algae beds. The species are *H. jenkinsi*, *H. gentilis*, *H. brevipinnis*, *H. robustus* and *H. sordidus*. Behavior was observed in the laboratory at the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, supported by subjective field observations in California, Mexico and South America. Fish were held in 150 L, 300 L and 550 L aquaria with running seawater, fed an overabundance of food, and observed under reversed photoperiod from a darkened blind.

**Grunion spawning study.** Thomson and Mulligan analyzed spawning runs of the Gulf grunion, *Leuresthes sardina*, at El Golfo de Santa Clara, Sonora, Mexico, from 1968 to 1973. Their aim was to compare its run periodicity with that of the California grunion, *L. tenuis*, which lives under a different tidal and wave regime. The paper is dedicated to Carl Hubbs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Thermal and depth structure.** Surfperch distributions reflect water temperature and the seasonal position of the [[Thermocline]]. Adults of most species move deeper in late summer and fall and shallower in winter and spring. The authors link the fall descent to rising surface temperatures and a deepening thermocline. Juveniles prefer warmer, shallower water.

**Habitat-linked coloration.** In Hypsoblennius, habitat type shapes color pattern. Species on disruptive, rocky substrates show [[Saddle Coloration]] patterns, while species on barren substrates such as bare rock or empty barnacle tests rely on stripes or mottle. Sympatric species show divergent courtship colorations, while allopatric species from similar habitats show similar codes.

**Tidal and wave regime.** The Gulf of California has mixed semidiurnal tides. The higher high tide shifts from early morning to afternoon across the spawning season, which changes the timing of grunion runs. The California grunion, in contrast, spawns only at night.

## Key Findings

### Surfperch Depth and Temperature Orientation (King Harbor)

1. Two of the five species differed significantly in bathymetric distribution from the other three. *M. minimus* occupied the shallow intertidal and subtidal fringe, *E. jacksoni* the middle subtidal, and *R. vacca*, *H. caryi* and *P. furcatus* the deeper rocks and rock-sand interface.
2. Depth distribution varied seasonally, except for *M. minimus*. Adults of each species moved deeper in late summer and fall and shallower in winter and spring.
3. The fall descent corresponded with periods of increased surface water temperature and a deepening thermocline. The three deeper species selected the coolest water available during this period.
4. Seasonal abundance decreased during fall, which may reflect emigration in response to warm thermal conditions.
5. Each species appears to occupy a preferred thermal range. For the deeper species, distributions centered at or below 16°C. *E. jacksoni* appeared more thermally tolerant. *M. minimus* either selected the warmest water available or tolerated whatever temperature prevailed in its preferred habitat.
6. Young are born from spring to late summer. Juveniles preferred warmer, shallower water than adults. Temperature orientation appears to play a key role in controlling depth distribution.

### Coloration as a Communication Signal (Hypsoblennius)

1. Coloration correlates with behavioral state and can signal submission, aggression and courtship. Its signal value is strongest in species with the most distinct color codes.
2. Trunk pattern and darkness, and the darkness of signal spots, were nonrandomly related to behavioral state in all species studied.
3. Three broad signal types recur across species: (a) submission and exploration, usually light and cryptic; (b) aggression and male courtship, usually dark and less cryptic; and (c) female courtship, usually light bland and probably noncryptic.
4. *H. brevipinnis* showed the greatest divergence between behavioral curves and therefore the highest potential for communication. Its lower postorbital spot is a ritualized signal organ, which may let it settle disputes quickly and spend less time exposed on barren substrate.
5. Sympatric species had divergent courtship colorations. *H. gentilis* and *H. jenkinsi* differ in courtship coloration, including the red chin and reticulate trunk of ripe male *H. gentilis* and the dorsal spot of courting *H. gentilis* females. *H. robustus* and *H. sordidus* show complete sympatry in Peru but very different aggressive colorations.
6. *H. robustus* and *H. jenkinsi* are considered a likely species pair that probably speciated in allopatry after a warm-water barrier split their ancestral population. They show little divergence in morphology, habitat, behavior or coloration.
7. Behavior is largely territorial. Side-roll, a submissive display, was answered by an attack in only one of 34 occurrences. Pelagic prejuveniles begin territorial defense within several days of settling. Courting males release a [[Sexual Pheromone]] that attracts other ripe males.
8. Cryptic function is linked to habitat. Saddle patterns dominate disruptive habitats. Stripe and mottle patterns serve as cryptic coloration in barren habitats, probably by reducing light-to-dark interfaces that signal movement to visual predators.

### Tidal Control of Grunion Spawning (Gulf of California)

1. Gulf grunion runs occur fortnightly from January to May, following new and full moons. The runs begin about four days after the new moon and about five days after the full moon. California grunion usually spawn from early March to August.
2. The analysis covers 53 runs observed at El Golfo de Santa Clara from 1968 to 1973. Tide data came from predictions for Puerto Peñasco, which were roughly synchronous with local tides but slightly smaller in amplitude.
3. Night spawning of the Gulf grunion follows the early-morning higher high tide (0300–0500), which dominates early in the season (January–February).
4. Mid-season, the two daily high tides become about equal in height, and daytime runs begin, typically in the late afternoon (1500–1700). When the afternoon high tide exceeds the morning high tide, only daytime runs occur.
5. *L. sardina* times its runs to the higher high tide, regardless of the time of day it occurs.
6. The accelerated spawning act during daytime runs is attributed to the low-amplitude sea waves typical of the period, rather than to increased daytime predation by sea birds.
7. Earlier work had attributed runs of *L. tenuis* to tides (Thompson, 1919; Clark, 1925). Walker (1949) hypothesized that run timing is set by the initiation of sex-product maturation at the second preceding moon phase, about 17.9 days before the midpoint of a run series.
