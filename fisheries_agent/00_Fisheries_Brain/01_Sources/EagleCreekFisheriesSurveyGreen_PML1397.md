# Eagle Creek Fisheries Survey, June 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #fisheries-survey

## Executive Summary

A fisheries survey of [[Eagle Creek]] in [[Greenlee County]], Arizona, was conducted on June 17–18, 1998, by researchers from Arizona State University, Brian Bagley and Paul Marsh. Seven sites were sampled between [[Honeymoon Campground]] and 3.4 km (2.1 mi) below the Eagle Creek pump station, using a DC [[Backpack Electrofisher]], dip nets and seines. The survey recorded fourteen fish species and crayfish at every site. The notable result is the absence of [[Spikedace]], which had not been collected since 1989, and of [[Loach Minnow]], which was not found for the first time since its rediscovery. The rediscovery site has been altered into pool habitat that the document describes as unsuitable for loach minnow.

## Background & Description

The document is a short field report with a methods summary, a results table of fish counts and [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (fish caught per minute of shocking), a site-specific note, and map pages. The map pages include a vicinity map, a U.S.G.S. index and a Forest Service map of the area, with a legend listing special information (nature trail, boat launching, wilderness or primitive area, trailer station) and boat motor limits of up to 1, 8 and 9 hp at various waters. The map text is partly illegible after OCR and is not reproduced here.

Sampling methods:
- Seven sampling locations along Eagle Creek.
- A DC backpack electrofisher, dip nets and seines.
- Shocking effort at each location was recorded in seconds. Seines and dip nets were run for five minutes at specified sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Eagle Creek supports a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fishes. The native and regionally sensitive taxa include [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Sonora Sucker]]. Introduced or non-native species include [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]]. [[Rainbow Trout]] were also collected.

The [[Middle Prong]] of Eagle Creek, sampled downstream of the [[San Carlos Indian Reservation]] boundary, had abundant longfin and speckled dace, and common Sonora sucker and crayfish.

Habitat change is a concern. The road crossing below Honeymoon Campground, where loach minnow were first rediscovered, has been modified into a pool habitat that the document considers unsuitable for that species.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** The document reports fourteen fish species across the reach. The species names in the text account for thirteen taxa, and one name appears to have been lost in scanning. The names are listed above.
- **Crayfish:** Present at all sample locations.
- **Previously recorded fishes:** The document states that all other fish species were recorded on previous trips.
- **Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*):** Not encountered, and not collected in Eagle Creek since 1989.
- **Loach minnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*):** Not encountered. This is the first survey since its rediscovery (the scanned year reads "1994") in which it was not collected.
- **Shocking effort at loach minnow habitat:** 1,218 seconds was spent in habitats where loach minnow had been collected in the past, with no capture.
- **Middle Prong:** Longfin and speckled dace were abundant, and Sonora sucker and crayfish were common.
- **Per-site data:** Table 1 gives fish counts and CPUE for each site, along with seining and dip net effort (for example, a seine for five minutes at one site and a dip net for five minutes at another). The scanned table is too degraded to reproduce site values reliably. Consult the original document for exact counts.
- **Management implication:** Habitat modification at the original loach minnow rediscovery site may have removed suitable habitat, and that should be considered in future monitoring and recovery work.
