# The Effect of Thermal Acclimation on Brain Cholinesterase Activity of the Killifish, *Fundulus heteroclitus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Fundulidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 study by Baslow & Nigrelli investigates the biochemical basis of thermal stress tolerance in the [[Killifish]] (*Fundulus heteroclitus*), focusing on brain [[Cholinesterase]] (ChE) activity as a proxy for central nervous system function during [[Thermal Acclimation]]. The authors hypothesize that failure of the CNS under thermal stress — implicated in both heat and cold death in poikilothermic fish — is mechanistically linked to alterations in ChE activity, an enzyme critical for terminating cholinergic neurotransmission at synapses. Using a colorimetric acetylcholine hydrolysis assay, they measured ChE activity across multiple marine and brackish-water fish species from both "northern" (cold-adapted) and "southern" (warm-adapted) thermal regimes, and performed detailed acclimation experiments on *F. heteroclitus*. Key findings include a homeostatic regulation of brain ChE activity (stabilizing near Q̸CHE ≈ 31 regardless of acclimation temperature), species-specific differences in thermal stability of the enzyme between warm- and cold-water fishes, and a seasonal endogenous rhythm in ChE activity independent of ambient temperature. Critically, the study demonstrates that in vitro heat-induced denaturation of brain ChE does **not** occur in vivo during heat death, decoupling enzyme thermal instability from the mechanism of heat death, while supporting a role for ChE-related autointoxication in cold death.

## Background & Description

This paper is biochemical/physiological rather than morphological in focus; however, relevant anatomical and tissue-level data include:

- **Brain tissue sampling**: Brains were extracted by removing the skull roof and severing at the optic nerves and medulla base, following spinal cord transection at the pectoral fin level.
- **Brain weight vs. body weight relationship**: As killifish body/brain size increases, ChE activity per unit brain tissue weight *decreases* — a pattern also documented in rat, [[Bluegill Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Golden Shiner]], and [[Common Goldfish]].
- **Comparative brain weights and total body weights** were tabulated across ten species (Table I), ranging from small killifish (29.7 mg brain, 3.6 g body) to large Nassau grouper (895.2 mg brain, 6940 g body).
- **Cold death syndrome morphology**: Described (per Fries 1952) as numbness, convulsion, and pectoral fin paralysis, with fins held rigidly at 90° to the body — a posture reproducible via anticholinesterase drug exposure ([[Weiss 1961]]), linking gross behavioral/postural pathology to ChE dysfunction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species range and thermal zonation**: The study contrasts "northern" cold-adapted species (acclimated ~15°C) — [[Common Killifish]], [[Northern Blackfish]] (*Tautoga onitis*), [[Common Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*), [[Northern Stargazer]] (*Astroscopus guttatus*) — against "southern" warm-adapted species (~25°C) from South Atlantic/South Pacific waters: [[Nassau Grouper]], [[Holbrook's Porgy]], [[Spot Snapper]], [[Fantail Filefish]], [[Tomato Clownfish]], [[Anemone Clownfish]].
- **Fundulus heteroclitus** occupies estuarine/coastal habitats with wide natural seasonal thermal variation (summer–winter range), making it an ideal model for studying [[Poikilotherm]] thermal acclimation due to its hardiness and tolerance of large temperature swings.
- **Thermal physiology and lethal limits**: Upper and lower lethal temperature limits in fish vary directly with acclimation temperature (Brett 1956), implicating CNS-level modification as an ecological adaptation to seasonal or geographic thermal regimes.
- **Seasonal endogenous rhythms**: Even under constant captive temperature (13°C, ±2°C over 5 months), killifish exhibited a spontaneous ~30% rise in brain ChE activity in late January/early February, mirroring natural seasonal minima in water temperature — suggesting an endogenous physiological clock tied to natural thermal cycles, independent of proximate temperature cues. This parallels seasonal oxygen consumption cycles in the [[Pacific Killifish]] (*Fundulus parvipinnis*; Wells 1935) and seasonal lethal-limit shifts in [[Goldfish]] (Hoar 1955).
- **Ecological implication**: Such seasonal biochemical variation may be adaptive for species inhabiting environments with strong seasonal thermal fluctuation, priming the CNS for anticipated cold stress.

## Key Findings

### 1. Species-Specific Enzyme Kinetics (Q₁₀)
- Q₁₀ (factor for enzymatic activity increase per 10°C rise) for brain ChE was stable across 10–35°C but rose sharply below 10°C in most species tested.
- Q₁₀ values across ten species ranged from **1.02 (Fantail Filefish) to 1.45 (Anemone Clownfish)** (Table I), comparable to values reported by Chadwick (1957) for cholinesterases generally.
- Baseline specific activity (Q₀,ₕ at 26°C) varied ~3.5-fold across species, from 28 (Nassau Grouper) to 99 (Fantail Filefish).
- Northern species (goldfish, blackfish, killifish) maintained stable Q₁₀ down to ~2°C before activity loss, while southern species (filefish, snapper) lost stability at higher (cooler) thresholds — indicating intrinsic species-level biochemical adaptation to native thermal regime.

### 2. Differential Thermal Stability of ChE (In Vitro Denaturation)
- Warm-water species (tomato clownfish, anemone clownfish, spot snapper, fantail filefish) showed **greater heat stability** of brain ChE at 35–40°C than cold-water species (goldfish, blackfish, killifish) (Table II).
- Killifish ChE was notably heat-labile: 100% activity loss after 60 min at 40°C, vs. minimal loss (~6–21%) in warm-water species under comparable exposure.

### 3. Heat Death Does Not Involve ChE Denaturation In Vivo
- Despite predicted ~30% ChE reduction based on in vitro kinetics, killifish heat-killed at 35–40°C showed **no measurable difference** in brain ChE activity compared to cold-adapted controls (Text-fig. 1).
- Conclusion: in vitro thermal lability of ChE does **not** translate to in vivo functional failure — heat death is not caused by ChE denaturation.

### 4. Homeostatic Regulation of ChE During Acclimation
- Brain ChE activity varies **inversely** with acclimation temperature when measured at a constant assay temperature (26°C) — a ~40% difference between 30°C- and 18°C-acclimated killifish.
- When measured *at the ambient/acclimation temperature*, ChE activity is actively regulated back toward a stable set-point of **Q₀,ₕ ≈ 31**, regardless of whether the fish is acclimated to 13°C or 30°C — evidence of a **homeostatic regulatory mechanism** in CNS enzyme activity (Text-fig. 4).
- Acute thermal stress causes transient overshoot: warming to 30°C acutely raises Q₀,ₕ to ~50 before settling back to ~31; cooling to 13°C acutely drops it to ~20 before rising back to ~31.

### 5. Asymmetric Acclimation Kinetics (Heat vs. Cold)
- Heat acclimation (reduction of elevated ChE activity to new stable level) is complete in **~6–7 days**.
- Cold acclimation shows a **~4-5 day lag** before ChE activity begins rising, with full stabilization taking **~11 days total**.
- Opercular (gill) beat rate parallels ChE changes: acute warming raises rate from ~100 to >230 beats/min, declining back to baseline over ~5 days in step with ChE reduction.
- Consistent with prior findings that fish adapt more readily to temperature increases than decreases (Brett 1956; Wells 1935 for *F. parvipinnis*).

### 6. Seasonal Variation Independent of Temperature
- Under constant 13°C for 5+ months, brain ChE activity rose spontaneously to >130% of October baseline in late January/early February (Text-fig. 5), coinciding with the natural period of lowest water temperatures — an apparent endogenous seasonal rhythm.
- Parallels seasonal oxygen consumption cycle in *Fundulus parvipinnis* (Wells 1935) and seasonal lethal-limit cycling in goldfish (Hoar 1955).

### 7. Physiological Interpretation: ChE and Cold Death
- Residual ChE activity compatible with survival under anticholinesterase drug exposure ranges 20–60% of normal (Weiss 1958/59/61; Nachmansohn & Feld 1947).
- Calculated residual ChE activity at lethal cold temperatures (using species-specific Q₁₀ and Q₀,ₕ) in goldfish falls to ~47-52% of acclimated levels — closely matching drug-induced survival thresholds.
- Supports hypothesis that **cold death**, but not heat death, in fish results from CNS failure due to insufficient ChE activity, causing autointoxication via accumulation of unhydrolyzed [[Acetylcholine]] at synaptic junctions.
- Cold death syndrome (numbness, twitching, convulsion, paralysis) is consistent with cholinergic autointoxication rather than generalized heat-driven enzyme denaturation.

### Summary Statement
Brain ChE activity in *Fundulus heteroclitus* is under tight homeostatic control during thermal acclimation, with distinct kinetics for heat vs. cold adaptation, species-specific thermal stability profiles reflecting native ecological thermal zones, and an endogenous seasonal component. The data support a mechanistic link between ChE physiological limits and **cold death** in fish, while ruling out direct ChE denaturation as a cause of **heat death**.
