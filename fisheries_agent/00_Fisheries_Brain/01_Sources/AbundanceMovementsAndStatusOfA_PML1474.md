# Age and Growth of Leatherside Chub and Abundance and Movements of Razorback Sucker: Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council, 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This volume of the [[Desert Fishes Council]] proceedings (1993, Volume XXV) contains two freshwater fish studies, with an additional abstract in Spanish. The first examines the [[Age And Growth]] of [[Leatherside Chub]] (*Lepidomeda copei*) from populations in the [[Bonneville Basin]] of Utah. The second assesses the abundance and movements of adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]] on the [[Colorado River]] between Arizona and Nevada, using [[Mark-Recapture]] and [[Pit Tagging]] methods. The source is partly degraded by OCR errors, and several numerical values could not be read reliably. Those values are omitted or flagged below.

## Background & Description

**Leatherside chub** is a small cyprinid of cold, high-elevation streams. Its reproductive behavior is poorly known, although the Spanish abstract notes that the species occurs in cold rivers in the south and east of the Bonneville Basin. Ageing was based on counts of annuli in hard structures (otoliths or scales), which allowed size-at-age and growth to be reconstructed.

**Razorback sucker** is a large catostomid of the Colorado River system, listed as a species of concern because of long-term population decline. The study monitors adult fish in Lake Mohave, a reservoir where a population remains.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Leatherside chub** populations occur in cold streams, including the Provo River and Bear River drainages. Spawning was reported to occur in May, and the species was described as sometimes abundant.
- **Razorback sucker** in Lake Mohave were studied across a reservoir with multiple sampling areas. Movements were tracked to show how fish use the reservoir and whether they return to the same areas.
- Both studies address conservation status and the need to understand demographic rates (growth, survival, reproduction) for managing native desert fishes.

## Key Findings

**Leatherside Chub (Age and Growth)**
- Growth was rapid in the first year of life, with fish under the size threshold given in the text growing faster than older fish.
- Growth was described as roughly similar among annual cohorts, with the study noting that fish were nonreproductive at younger ages.
- Individuals were aged from annuli counts, and the Spanish abstract states that the ageing indicated these chubs may live longer than previously thought.
- Population sampling was conducted in spring and summer of 1993 across Bonneville Basin drainages. Exact sample sizes are not legible in the source.

**Razorback Sucker (Abundance and Movements)**
- The study used [[Pit Tagging]] and mark-recapture to (1) estimate the adult population and (2) evaluate movement among areas of Lake Mohave.
- The population estimate given in the OCR text is approximately 3,000 adults, but the exact figure and confidence interval are unreadable.
- Recaptured fish were largely returned to the same general areas, indicating site fidelity within the reservoir.
- Abundance is described as significantly lower than in earlier surveys, consistent with the species' documented decline. A future population crash was flagged as a possibility if recruitment and survival do not improve.

**Caveat on data quality**
Several numerical details (sample sizes, per-female fecundity, and statistical test results) appear corrupted in the scan and could not be verified. Consult the original 1993 proceedings before citing specific values.
