# Description of Miocene Fishes from Southern California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #paleoichthyology #clupeidae #hippoglossidae #sparidae #cyprinodontidae #sciaenidae #deprandidae

## Executive Summary

This 1924 taxonomic paper by [[David Starr Jordan]] describes new and previously known fossil fishes from Miocene sediments in southern California. The main material comes from the [[Lompoc Diatom Beds]], Santa Barbara County, where collector [[Eric Knight Jordan]] found three new species in addition to "fifty or more" specimens already secured from the same locality. The paper also reports one new species of cyprinodontid from a shale setting near Liebre and Piru Creeks, and revised records of two taxa from Covina, Alhambra, and El Modena. Specimens are mostly imprints or partly crushed fossils, so many diagnoses rest on fin-ray counts, vertebral counts, and bone sculpture rather than complete anatomy.

The document is a scientific description, not a proposal. It opens with a page listing officers and committees of the [[Southern California Academy of Sciences]]. The fish description follows, with a note of thanks to the [[Celite Products Company]], whose diatom mining operations exposed the fossil beds.

## Background & Description

The Lompoc material comes from [[Diatom Deposits]] that are mined for diatomite. The product is sold as [[Celite]], which is used as pipe insulation and as a filtering agent. The collector was indebted to the company's director, Mr. Edward B. Starr, for access.

Six taxa are described or revised:

1. **[[Diradias Aratus]]** (new genus and species; Clupeidae). Type specimen (No. 600, Stanford University) is about 14 inches long. Cotypes are No. 600A and No. 601 (11 inches). The genus is said to be close to [[Clupea]] but differs in its deeply ridged opercle and serrated belly. Vertebrae number 52 in the type (apparently 46 in No. 601). Caudal rays total 30 (15+15).
2. **[[Hippoglossus Antiquus]]** (new species; Hippoglossidae). Type (No. 603) is a large fish, about 21½ inches long, mostly lacking head and caudal fin. Dorsal rays number about 93 (90 counted), anal rays about 54 (50 counted), and vertebrae about 50. The author tentatively places it in [[Hippoglossus]] (the living [[Halibut]] genus), noting that dextral or sinistral orientation could not be determined.
3. **[[Eriquius Plectrodes]]** (new genus and species; Sparidae). Type (No. 602) is about 12 inches long. Dorsal rays are XII, 14; anal rays III, 10; caudal rays 10+10. Vertebrae total 26 (8+18), more than in any living sparid. The genus is compared with [[Stenotomus]] and [[Lagodon]].
4. **[[Parafundulus Erdisi]]** (new species; Cyprinodontidae). Type (No. 605) and cotypes (606, 607) were collected by Ellwood C. Erdis from the confluence of Liebre and Piru Creeks, Santa Barbara National Forest, at an elevation of 2,200 feet. Specimens are small (about 3 to 4½ inches). The paper compares it with *Parafundulus nevadensis* from Nevada and with *Empetrichthys merriami*.
5. **[[Lompoquia Retropes]]** (Sciaenidae; previously known from one imperfect specimen). A new, more complete specimen (8½ inches) from [[Covina]] is described. Vertebrae total 24 (10+14). The author first thought the genus was an ally of [[Otolithes]] but concluded it is a typical sciaenid, with some similarity to the living [[Seriphus]].
6. **[[Deprandus Lestes]]** (Deprandidae). Known from [[El Modena]], with jaw fragments from [[Alhambra]] loaned by Dr. A. J. Tieje. The jaws are long and slender, with rows of sharp conical teeth (about 25 per inch in one fragment; about 55 teeth in the outer row of another). The author proposes a separate family, Deprandidae, which may be related to the Muraenesocidae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lompoc setting:** Diatom-rich marine sediments of Miocene age yielded most of the fish material. The author notes that bones of the head are often poorly preserved, which he suggests may be due to fats or other substances within the brain.
- **Creek and shale setting:** Erdis described the Liebre and Piru Creek locality as a black, slaty rock of relatively recent formation, heavily folded and broken. Hills there have only thin shale soil and support poor chaparral and oak brush. Cliffs show strata bent at right angles with fractures 6 inches to 2 feet wide.
- **Desert analogues:** The author notes that ventral fins are reduced or absent in some living desert cyprinodonts (*C. baileyi*, *C. browni*). He suggests the lack of ventrals in *Parafundulus erdisi* may be a real trait or a preservation artifact.
- **Age comparison:** *Parafundulus erdisi* is compared with *P. nevadensis* from Lahontan beds near Hazen, Nevada. The author considers *P. erdisi* probably later, though uncertain.

## Key Findings

- Three new species were described from the Lompoc Diatom Beds: *Diradias aratus*, *Hippoglossus antiquus*, and *Eriquius plectrodes*. Together with prior collections of fifty or more fishes from that locality, the site is a significant source of Miocene fish fossils.
- *Diradias aratus* is separated from other Miocene herrings mainly by its strongly ridged opercle.
- *Hippoglossus antiquus* is tentatively assigned to *Hippoglossus* because no character clearly separates it from the living halibut genus. Its fin-ray and vertebral counts argue against *Paralichthys*.
- *Eriquius plectrodes* has more vertebrae than any living sparid, a key distinction from related genera.
- *Parafundulus erdisi* is a new cyprinodontid from a shale setting in the Liebre and Piru Creek area, Los Angeles County. It is close to *P. nevadensis* but has a less forward dorsal fin.
- *Lompoquia retropes* is confirmed with a new specimen from Covina. Its vertebral count (24) indicates a typical sciaenid rather than an ally of *Otolithes*.
- *Deprandus lestes* is documented from Alhambra jaw fragments. The unusual jaw morphology leads the author to propose a new family, Deprandidae.
- All described material is fragmentary, so several assignments (including the flatfish orientation and the presence of ventral fins in *P. erdisi*) are explicitly provisional, pending better specimens.
