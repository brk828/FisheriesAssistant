# Asian Tapeworm in Bonytail Chubs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #parasitology

## Executive Summary

This June 20, 1994 memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Fish Health Biologist Jerry Landye to the Arizona Fish and Wildlife office reports fish health findings for [[Bonytail Chub]] and related fish from the [[Hassayampa Preserve]] in Arizona. Non-lethal sampling in April 1994 and subsequent necropsies in May and June identified the [[Asian Tapeworm]] (*[[Bothriocephalus Acheilognathi]]*) in longfin dace and in bonytail chubs held for stocking into [[Lake Havasu]]. The author recommends that no fish from the Hassayampa Preserve be stocked or transferred without deworming, and that any bonytail from Lake Havasu found infected be treated.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Health assessment of bonytail chubs from [[Palm Lake]] before they were to be transferred to Lake Havasu for release.
- **Sampling context:** [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] biologists had been netting the fish for about 19 hours before the health team arrived (the scanned text reads "lg hours").
- **Methods:** [[Non-Lethal Sampling]] of live fish (fecal smears for bacterial analysis, skin scrapings for parasites). Brown bullheads were sacrificed for kidney bacterial cultures and a full parasite check. Two fish that died in the net were frozen and not used for bacterial or viral analysis.
- **Recipients:** Copies to R2 (FR), AZFRO, the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] Fish Health Lab, and the file.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Palm Lake (Hassayampa Preserve):** Source population of bonytail chubs, which were being moved to Lake Havasu for release.
- **Aquarium losses:** Longfin dace at the Hassayampa Preserve aquarium were experiencing losses, which prompted the diagnosis that identified the tapeworm.
- **Parasite spread:** The memo treats the Hassayampa Preserve fish as a likely source of infection. Infected fish could carry the parasite into stocking programs and into Lake Havasu, so testing and deworming before transfer are the main controls.
- **Brown bullheads:** Present at the sampling site and found with external and gill protozoan infections, showing that other fish species at the site also carried parasites.

## Key Findings

**1. Initial health screen (April 28, 1994), Palm Lake**
- Brown bullhead: *Chilodenella* (light, on skin), *Trichodina* (light, on gill), and *Aeromonas* sp. (bacterial, from kidney).
- Bonytail chub: *Aeromonas* sp. on skin. The parasite entry for the bonytail is garbled in the scan and cannot be reliably reconstructed.
- Two fish that died during netting were frozen for later analysis and were not used for bacterial or viral testing.

**2. Longfin dace infection (May 9, 1994)**
- Fish from the Hassayampa Preserve aquarium were infected with the Asian tapeworm, *Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*, after experiencing losses.
- Diagnosis led to contact with the Hassayampa Preserve about obtaining frozen bonytail chubs for examination.

**3. Bonytail chub infection (received June 4, examined June 10)**
- Frozen bonytail chubs were examined stomach-by-stomach. Both fish were infected with the Asian tapeworm:
  - Larger fish (390 mm TL / 330 mm SL): one tapeworm.
  - Smaller fish (350 mm TL / 300 mm SL): three adult tapeworms.
- The recovered tapeworms were to be returned to the Hassayampa Preserve. The bonytails were being prepared for transfer to the Arizona State University fish museum.

**4. Recommendations**
- Assume any fish from the Hassayampa Preserve is positive for the Asian tapeworm.
- Do not use Hassayampa Preserve fish for stocking or transfer unless they are dewormed first.
- If any bonytail chub from Lake Havasu becomes infected, treat it for this tapeworm.
