## Executive Summary
This 1992 interoffice memorandum from BIO/WEST to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) outlines a collaborative sampling strategy for age-0 and age-1 [[Humpback Chub]] in the Grand Canyon reach of the [[Colorado River]]. The memo does not present original morphometric or population data, but instead lays out field methodology, hypotheses on ontogenetic habitat shifts, and logistical coordination for a mark-recapture program (fin punching) intended to track juvenile chub movement, growth, and habitat use between the [[Little Colorado River (LCR)]] confluence and Hance Rapid.

## Background & Description
The source contains no direct morphometric, meristic, or anatomical descriptions of *Gila cypha*. The only size-related information referenced is operational:
- Fin punching (marking) is planned for individuals **less than 175 mm total length (TL)**, used as a practical size cutoff to distinguish young-of-year and yearling [[Humpback Chub]] from larger juveniles/subadults.
- Length data collected by BIO/WEST and AGFD field crews are intended to be used to statistically separate **age-0, age-1, and possibly age-2** cohorts based on length-frequency distributions, rather than direct aging methods (e.g., otoliths).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The memo focuses heavily on habitat use patterns of early life-stage Humpback Chub within the Grand Canyon reach:
- **Primary nursery habitat**: Backwaters between the [[Little Colorado River (LCR)]] and Hance Rapid are identified as supporting the highest densities of age-0 Humpback Chub, making this reach critical for understanding recruitment, survival, and downstream transport.
- **Shoreline habitat**: BIO/WEST crews concurrently sample shoreline habitats, which are hypothesized to be used more heavily during periods of increased turbidity.
- **Turbidity–habitat linkage**: Increased turbidity is associated with increased shoreline activity by juvenile chub; the memo hypothesizes that backwater use may be similarly influenced by turbidity levels, though this remains untested at the time of writing.
- **Proposed ontogenetic habitat shift model**: The authors hypothesize a three-stage habitat transition during the first three years of life:
  1. Shallow nursery embayments and backwaters (earliest life stage)
  2. Transient use of backwaters and shorelines (intermediate stage)
  3. More consistent shoreline use, transitioning toward adult use of large eddies (later juvenile/subadult stage)
- This habitat-use transition is explicitly framed as important for informing **flow management recommendations** (timing and magnitude of dam releases) in the [[Glen Canyon Dam]]-regulated Colorado River system.

## Key Findings
- **Sampling recommendation**: AGFD is urged to intensify backwater sampling between the LCR and Hance Rapid, the apparent core nursery zone for age-0 [[Humpback Chub]]; BIO/WEST will reciprocally intensify shoreline sampling in the same reach.
- **Coordinated sampling design**: Joint BIO/WEST–AGFD trips are proposed to simultaneously sample backwaters and adjacent shorelines, enabling direct comparison of habitat use timing and environmental conditions (e.g., turbidity) between age-0 and age-1 cohorts.
- **Data sharing protocol**: BIO/WEST requests trip-by-trip length data (by backwater location) from AGFD captures to support cohort (age-class) delineation via length-frequency analysis.
- **Marking program**: BIO/WEST will begin fin-punching Humpback Chub <175 mm TL, with a formal protocol to follow by January 1993; AGFD and other field crews are asked to watch for these marks (fresh punches or regenerated/misaligned fin rays).
- **Cross-institutional marking awareness**: Note that [[Arizona State University (ASU)]] researchers are independently fin-clipping young chub in the LCR, and these marks must also be tracked to avoid confounding mark-recapture data.
- **Interagency coordination**: Ongoing communication between BIO/WEST field leads (Bill Masslich, Bill Leibfried) and AGFD's river crew (Glen Doster) is emphasized to align field logistics and data collection across the 1993 field season.
- **Broader management relevance**: The memo situates juvenile Humpback Chub habitat-use research within the larger [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES)]] program, aimed at informing dam operation and flow regime decisions to benefit this federally listed endangered species.
