## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary biological or morphological research paper but a formal comment letter submitted by the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]] Department of Fish and Game (DFG) in response to the [[Biological and Conference Opinion on Lower Colorado River Operations and Maintenance]] (BO), a regulatory document prepared by the [[United States Bureau of Reclamation]]. The letter addresses habitat degradation along the [[Lower Colorado River]] resulting from Reclamation's operational activities and critiques specific Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) provisions within the BO. No direct morphological, taxonomic, or population data on fish species are presented; instead, the letter provides ecological and policy context relevant to habitat conservation and restoration efforts affecting native aquatic and riparian species in this river system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The letter concerns the **[[Lower Colorado River]]** ecosystem, specifically the river section bounded by the **[[Colorado River Indian Reservation]]**.
- Habitats discussed include riparian and aquatic zones historically shaped by natural river dynamics, which have been altered by Reclamation's operational and maintenance activities (e.g., flow regulation, channelization).
- The **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]** is explicitly named as a species requiring off-site habitat protection, indicating the affected habitat supports both avian and presumably aquatic/riparian-dependent species, though no fish species are named directly.
- DFG emphasizes that current river conditions "neither represent, nor function like the natural systems of the past," implying significant habitat degradation with likely downstream ecological consequences for native fish and other aquatic fauna dependent on historic flow and habitat structure.
- The habitats in question hold dual ecological and cultural/spiritual significance to the [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]], linking indigenous stewardship practices to aquatic ecosystem management.
- The letter objects to the use of the southern international boundary (U.S.–Mexico border) as the terminus of the study area, suggesting ecological connectivity and management responsibility should extend beyond this administrative boundary — a relevant consideration for transboundary aquatic species and habitat continuity.

## Key Findings

- DFG supports RPA provisions 14 and 15, which mandate development of solutions to habitat devastation caused by Reclamation's activities on the Lower Colorado River.
- DFG objects to RPA provisions 5 and 11, which permit off-site habitat protection and restoration rather than requiring on-site remediation within the affected river reach.
- The letter argues that off-site mitigation allows Reclamation to be "released from its on-site responsibility" for ecological damage, undermining restoration of the specific historic habitats along the Lower Colorado River.
- DFG calls for the **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service** to proactively secure off-site habitat for the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher if off-site alternatives are to be pursued at all.
- DFG objects to limiting the study area's southern terminus at the international boundary, arguing this artificially constrains Reclamation's accountability for downstream ecological effects.
- Despite objections, DFG expresses qualified support for Reclamation's overall intentions in developing the BO and expresses hope for future restoration of historic Lower Colorado River habitat conditions.
- The letter underscores the intersection of indigenous cultural/spiritual values and aquatic/riparian habitat conservation, framing habitat restoration as both an ecological and cultural imperative for the Colorado River Indian Tribes.
