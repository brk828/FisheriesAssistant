# Virgin River Spinedace Collected from the Sevier River, Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This short field report by David A. White (Department of Zoology, [[Brigham Young University]], Provo, Utah) documents the capture of seven adult [[Virgin River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) from the [[Sevier River]] in Utah on 6 July 1967. The fish were seined about 3.2 miles west of Gunnison, Sanpete County. The fish were in good condition and matched museum specimens and published descriptions. The author argues that the population lies well outside the subspecies' known range, which is confined to the [[Virgin River Drainage]] of Utah, Arizona and Nevada. The most likely explanation is human introduction, probably as bait fish, rather than natural dispersal.

## Background & Description

The subspecies was described as belonging to the spiny-rayed cyprinids (Plagopterini) of the [[Colorado River System]] (Miller and Hubbs, 1960). Earlier reviews by Tanner (1936) and Miller (1963) place it in the Virgin River drainage. The collection record includes:

- **Sample size:** 7 adults (3 females, 4 males).
- **Collection method:** Seining.
- **Date:** 6 July 1967.
- **Locality:** Sevier River, 5.1 km (3.2 mi) W of Gunnison, Sanpete County, Utah.
- **Condition:** All seven fish were in excellent condition, with no significant morphological deviations from the [[Brigham Young University Ichthyological Collection]] specimens or from the literature descriptions.
- **Diagnostic feature:** Males had tubercles between the eyes and anterior to the external nares (the OCR text is partly garbled at this point).

## Key Findings

1. **Range extension:** Seven *Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis* were recorded in the Sevier River. This is outside the subspecies' previously documented range in the Virgin River drainage of Utah, Arizona and Nevada.
2. **Likely cause:** The author judges that the population most probably resulted from transport by people, probably as bait fish. Natural extension is considered unlikely because the Sevier and Virgin drainages are geographically separated.
3. **Morphological confirmation:** The specimens matched [[Brigham Young University]] reference material and published descriptions, so the identification is considered secure.
4. **Habitat context:** The collection site was a turbid, sluggish, sand-and-clay reach with pH 7.8 and disturbance from grazing and refuse. It also supported a diverse invertebrate assemblage and co-occurring non-native and native fish.
5. **Sex ratio of sample:** 3 females to 4 males, with breeding-related head tubercles evident in males.
