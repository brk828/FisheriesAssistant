# Age Estimation from Otoliths of Roundtail Chub (Gila robusta) from the Verde River, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report to the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] tests whether [[Otolith Age Estimation]] is a viable method for ageing [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) from the [[Verde River]], Arizona. Nineteen fish were aged, with estimates ranging from 2 to 5 years. Three independent readers reached a high degree of agreement. The authors judge otoliths more reliable than [[Scale Analysis]] for this species, but they state that the method is not yet validated, because no known-age fish were available. The report also compares Verde River sizes-at-age with published scale-based data from [[Turkey Creek]] and the [[Green River]].

## Background & Description

The roundtail chub is a cyprinid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. It was once widely distributed in warm streams and large tributaries. In Arizona it occurred in the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]] and major tributaries including the Black, San Pedro and Verde rivers, as well as the Little Colorado River and the Colorado mainstream near what is now [[Lake Powell]]. The species is now experiencing severe depletions across its natural range and has been recommended for candidate status by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) Desert Fishes Recovery Team.

Adults are typically 250 to 350 mm total length (TL), although mature individuals range from 150 to 650 mm. Roundtail chub are most abundant in deep main channels of large rivers, or near protective cover in smaller streams. Prior age and growth work includes scale-based studies by [[Bestgen 1985]] (Gila River, New Mexico), [[Vanicek And Kramer 1969]] (Green River), and [[Neve 1976]] (Fossil Creek, Arizona). Otherwise, the authors note, little ecological information exists for the species.

### Ageing Structures

The report reviews calcified structures used for fish ageing:

- **Scales** have been found generally unreliable for many endemic cyprinoid fishes, because they can be resorbed or regenerated during stress. Bestgen (1985) is cited as a dissenting view.
- **Otoliths** are permanent records of daily and annual growth, and are considered a useful and reliable ageing method. Their use for western North American cypriniforms was poorly documented before this study. The techniques here build on earlier work on [[Bonytail Chub]] (Marsh 1997).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study sampled the Verde River, a warm, regulated desert river system in central Arizona. The authors suggest that thermal differences between streams may partly explain observed size differences. Warmer water could support faster growth, but this was not tested directly.

Roundtail chub are described as a native species in decline. Age data are relevant to population dynamics, life history and responses to environmental factors. The authors state that such analyses require validated ages first.

## Key Findings

### Sample and Collections

- **Eighteen roundtail chub** were captured 12 to 16 February 1996 from the Verde River at Perkinsville and Childs, Arizona, and provided frozen by the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
- **One additional male** (230 mm) was collected in May 1997 from the Verde River at Perkinsville by [[Arizona State University]], bringing the total to **19 fish**.
- Fish sizes ranged from **220 to 371 mm TL** and **90 to 560 g** weight.

### Methods

- **Extraction:** Otoliths were removed using the [[Open-The-Hatch Method]] (Secor et al. 1991; Stevenson and Campana 1992), with a Dremel tool and fine forceps.
- **Structure:** [[Lapilli]] gave the most readable mounts and were used for ageing. Asterisci from two specimens were polished but did not yield acceptable mounts.
- **Mounting:** Otoliths were coated in 3 or 4 layers of Super Glue to reduce cracking, then fixed to slides with [[Crystalbond]] thermoplastic cement. This cement dries clear, can be reheated to reposition otoliths, and adheres to slides for long-term storage.
- **Polishing:** Whole otoliths were ground with 600, 1000, 1500 and 2000 grit wet/dry sandpaper, on one side and then the other, until rings were visible at 40× under a dissecting microscope. Finer grits gave the best mounts.
- **Reading:** Three experienced readers independently counted apparent annuli at 40× on a compound microscope. Age range, mean and estimated year class were derived from the three readings.

### Unsuccessful Enhancement Techniques

- **Burning whole otoliths** over a propane flame (about 10 to 20 seconds) darkened them but did not improve readability. In two cases it increased cracking.
- **Burning polished otoliths** did not increase cracking or improve contrast between annuli.
- **Alizarin red dye** applied to polished otoliths was not absorbed, and readability was unaffected.

### Age Results

- **Age range:** 2 to 5 years.
- **Dominant cohort:** Most fish (**74%**) were age 3, from the 1993 year class.
- **Low-confidence estimates:** Fish numbers 23 and 24 had two lapilli polished, but confidence in their ages is low.
- **Virtual annulus:** A "virtual" annulus was added at the otolith margin for fish captured in February. Roundtail chub spawn in spring, so the authors judged that assigning all fish a 1 January birthdate would be inappropriate.

### Size-at-Age (Verde River, Otolith-Based)

| Age | n | Mean TL ± SD (mm) | Range (mm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 14 | 245 ± 22 | 220–291 |
| 4 | 3 | 337 ± 49 | 281–371 |
| 5 | 2 | 314 ± 36 | 288–339 |

- No clear relationship between age and size was found. The authors attribute this partly to the small sample. Means are reliable only for age 3, so other size-at-age values should not be used unless sample sizes are stated.

### Comparison with Published Scale-Based Data

- **Turkey Creek, New Mexico** (Bestgen 1985, back-calculated lengths): 133 mm at age 3 (n = 58), 162 mm at age 4 (n = 24), and 186 mm at age 5 (n = 3). Verde River fish were much larger at each age. The authors say the difference may be real, or may reflect methodological invalidity.
- **Green River, Colorado and Utah** (Vanicek and Kramer 1969, empirical lengths): 165 mm at age 3 for bonytail and roundtail combined (n = 56), 230 mm at age 4 (n = 16), and 277 mm at age 5 (n = 13) for roundtail alone. These values are smaller than the Verde River values, which the authors attribute at least partly to slower growth in cooler water. The authors do not consider scale-based results verifiable or reliable.

### Method Assessment

- **Time cost:** Preparing one otolith took about 1.7 hours after techniques were established (figure partly garbled in the scan). A scale mount takes a few minutes. Reading takes 2 to 5 minutes for either structure.
- **Agreement:** Independent readers showed a high degree of concurrence. The authors state that agreement alone does not validate the method.

### Recommendations

- Future studies should use **known-age fish**, from a hatchery or mark-recapture work, to compare ageing structures and to **validate the otolith method**. The authors state this is required before age estimates can be considered correct.
- Once validated, back-calculated length-at-age could support analysis of growth histories, environmental influences and population dynamics.
