# Allozyme Genetic Characteristics of the Flannelmouth Sucker and Razorback Sucker, with Evaluation of Hatchery Stock and Introgressive Hybridization

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1984 report by [[Donald G. Buth]] and [[Robert R. Miller]] (UCLA Department of Biology) was submitted to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Endangered Species Office under a contract. It uses starch gel [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]] to compare genetic variation in the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The central concern is that the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] broodstock of *X. texanus*, derived from [[Lake Mohave]], may carry introgressed genes from *C. latipinnis*, and that [[Genetic Drift]] acting on a small hatchery population could amplify them. The authors conclude that introgression is present but rare, that the hatchery stock is no worse than the Lake Mohave source population, and they give recommendations for monitoring.

## Background & Description

- **Focal species:** *Xyrauchen texanus* is a large catostomid of the Colorado River basin's "big-river" fish assemblage. Its decline has prompted recovery action, including large-scale artificial propagation at Dexter Hatchery.
- **Related species:** *C. latipinnis* is a sympatric catostomid that hybridizes with *X. texanus*. Among the Colorado River species it is most often reported in hybrid records (Wick et al., 1983).
- **Management problem:** Hatchery broodstock is a small subsample of the wild population. Sampling error and [[Genetic Drift]] can shift allele frequencies, and this effect is strongest in small effective populations.
- **Study design:** Tissues (brain, liver, heart, skeletal muscle) from wild and hatchery fish were analysed at 31 presumptive loci in *C. latipinnis* (four samples, N = 15 to 39 per site) and 31 loci in *X. texanus* (three samples: Lake Mohave N = 18, Senator Wash Reservoir N = 14, Dexter Hatchery N = 51). Nomenclature followed the International Union of Biochemistry (1978) and locus names followed Buth (1983). Specimens were stored at -70 °C and vouchered at LACM and ASU.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hybridization context:** *X. texanus* is known to hybridize with *C. latipinnis*, *C. discobolus*, and *C. ardens* in the Colorado system. Hybrid reports most often involve *C. latipinnis*.
- **Lake Mohave:** One of the few places where *X. texanus* is locally abundant. Hybridization there had been suspected from morphology, and the Lake Mohave sample was not random, since fish suspected of being hybrids were selected for study.
- **Dispersal and gene flow:** Allozyme data show restricted gene flow among *C. latipinnis* populations. Interspecific gene flow appears low, consistent with a published case in syntopic cyprinids (*Notropis* spp., Dowling and Moore, 1984).
- **Genetic background:** *X. texanus* variation is below the mean for catostomid fishes but well above the lowest end of the spectrum. Several eastern North American *Moxostoma* species have much lower variation, and none are listed as endangered.

## Key Findings

**Intraspecific variation in *Catostomus latipinnis***
- Nine of 31 loci were polymorphic in at least one population. All genotype arrays conformed to Hardy-Weinberg expectations within populations.
- Between populations, five loci (Ck-A, G-3-pdh-A, Gpi-A, Gpi-F, Gpi-B) departed significantly from equilibrium. This reflects statistically significant gene-flow structuring, though no fixed differences were found.
- Example: the Virgin and Paria river samples lacked the G-3-pdh-A polymorphism found in the other two samples.
- Nei's genetic identity and distance were calculated for all pairs (Table 6). The Virgin River–Paria River pair is the most similar (identity 0.913 range); the Virgin River–Upper Colorado comparison shows the greatest divergence.

**Intraspecific variation in *Xyrauchen texanus***
- Eleven of 31 loci were polymorphic in at least one population.
- Significant between-population differences occurred at two loci: S-Mdh-A (minor polymorphism in Senator Wash Reservoir, absent elsewhere) and Gpi-A (minor polymorphism in Lake Mohave, absent elsewhere).
- At the S-Mdh-A locus, the Dexter Hatchery sample showed a significant deficit of heterozygotes and an excess of both homozygotes, an intra-sample departure from equilibrium.
- Nei's genetic identity between Lake Mohave and Dexter Hatchery was 0.999 (distance about 0.001), close to the identity of the two other comparisons.

**Interspecific differentiation**
- Four loci (Ck-A, Gpi-A, M-Mdh-A, S-Sod-A) showed species-specific or nearly species-specific allele distributions. These can separate *C. latipinnis* from *X. texanus* and act as "marker" loci for hybrid detection.
- Mean genetic identity between the two species was about 0.0 range, with mean genetic distance reported as 0.733 and identity as 0.480 (values as given in the source text).
- Gene number was identical for the two species at the enzyme systems examined, so differential gene expression was ruled out as a hybrid marker.

**Evidence for introgression**
- Ck-A and M-Mdh-A show allele distributions suggesting bidirectional gene flow. Gene flow from *C. latipinnis* into *X. texanus* was favored at M-Mdh-A, while flow from *X. texanus* into *C. latipinnis* was favored at Ck-A.
- Introgressed alleles were found in the Upper Colorado and Virgin River samples of *C. latipinnis*, and in both Lake Mohave and hatchery samples of *X. texanus*.
- No F1 hybrids were found. Any introgressed individuals would likely be backcrosses.
- The authors conclude that introgressive hybridization is real but rare, so the species maintain the integrity of their gene pools.

**Evaluation of hatchery stock**
- Introgressed genes from *C. latipinnis* occur at very low levels in both the Dexter Hatchery stock and the Lake Mohave source population.
- Relative to the presumed parental stock, the hatchery has fewer *C. latipinnis* genes than Lake Mohave. The authors judge the hatchery stock "at least as good as" the natural population for introgression.
- Genetic variability in *X. texanus* is comparable to that of *C. latipinnis* in the Colorado basin.

**Recommendations**
- Breeding stock should continue to be obtained in sufficient quantity from natural populations. The authors advise against breeding exclusively from the hatchery's own reared stock, because subsampling it repeatedly would reduce variation.
- Future broodstock could be screened for introgressed genes using the marker loci Ck-A and M-Mdh-A, whose heterozygous conditions are diagnostic. Tissue can be obtained by biopsy, roughly the size of a pencil eraser.
- Blood or fin tissue may be easier to collect than muscle biopsies. Glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (liver) and 6-phosphogluconate dehydrogenase (brain) showed useful allelic differences for future work.
- Key questions for future research: whether morphology predicts introgression, whether morphological and allozyme introgression are decoupled, and how rare introgression is in Lake Mohave using a random sample.

**Limitations**
- The Lake Mohave sample was non-random, and the authors note that the incomplete data for some enzyme systems were excluded from the report.
