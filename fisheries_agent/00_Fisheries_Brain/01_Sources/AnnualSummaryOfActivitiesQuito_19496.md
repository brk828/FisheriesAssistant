# Quitobaquito Desert Pupfish Annual Census and Monitoring, 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodon

## Executive Summary

The [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]] annual summary for 1992 reports on the endangered [[Quitobaquito Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*), an endemic desert pupfish inhabiting the spring outflows and pond at [[Quitobaquito Springs]]. Weekly inspections and an annual census were conducted on September 8 and 9, 1992. The census recorded 2470 fish in the pond and 667 and 469 fish in the channel and spring on the two census days. No [[Gambusia Affinis]] or other non-native fish were captured or observed. Staff concluded that the population is in good condition, with a healthy range of age and size classes.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Cyprinodon macularius eremus*, an endangered, endemic desert pupfish.
- **Setting:** [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]], a unit of the National Park System in Pima County, Southwestern Arizona. Quitobaquito Springs/Pond lies in the southwestern portion of the monument, adjacent to the U.S./Mexico International Border. Pond water is supplied by two springs north of the pond.
- **Management goals (National Park Service):**
  - Ensure the continued survival and well-being of the endangered Desert Pupfish.
  - Provide shallow water habitat for young [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense longifemorale*).
  - Provide varied habitat for aquatic crustaceans and microorganisms.
- **Permits:** Census conducted under [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811 and [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] License BRNT0000160.
- **Survey methods history:**
  - [[Dr. Boyd E. Kynard]] conducted census research in 1975, continuing almost yearly through 1981.
  - Mark-recapture ([[Mark-Recapture Method]]) was used up to 1985. Markable fish were ≥22 mm and submarkable fish were <22 mm. Left pectoral fins were clipped on markable fish, which required much handling.
  - A contract with the [[University Of Arizona]] in 1985 sought the most suitable sampling method.
  - From 1985, [[Dr. Bill Matter]] assisted in implementing a [[Depletion Trapping Method]], which estimates population from successive trapping runs. Fish were held in a screened tank in the pond during the census.
  - Staff observed that the mark-recapture and depletion methods provided population estimates that were too low.

### Weekly Inspections (1992)

[[Monument Resource Management]] staff inspected the Quitobaquito area once a week throughout 1992. Inspections covered the channel, the southwest and northeast springs, pond perimeter, pond outflow, trails, and the historic fig and pomegranate orchard. Staff recorded habitat use, areas of concentration, and age classes, and checked for non-native fish such as *Gambusia affinis*. Pupfish were never trapped or handled during weekly inspections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Pond habitat:** The 1992 census used 47 traps around the pond perimeter. Traps were emptied every two hours, six times over two days. Captured fish were held in one of three holding tanks at the northwest end of the pond until the census ended, about 28 hours for fish from the first run. Total mortality for the two days was 17 individuals.
- **Channel and spring habitat:** Nine traps were placed in the spring channel and three at the southwest spring. One 4-hour run was conducted each day. Fish were counted and released.
- **Vegetation:** The channel has become thickly vegetated with aquatic and terrestrial plants. This cover provides habitat for both fish and turtles. Vegetation is minimally cleared to keep the channel open and water flowing.
- **Non-native fish:** Staff monitored closely for [[Gambusia Affinis]] in the pond and channel. None were found.

## Key Findings

### Census Results (September 8 and 9, 1992)

| Location | Trapping Effort | Fish Trapped |
|---|---|---|
| Quitobaquito Pond | 47 traps, emptied 6 times over 2 days | 2470 total |
| Channel and spring, Day 1 | 9 channel traps + 3 at SW spring, 1 run of 4 hours | 667 (released) |
| Channel and spring, Day 2 | Same effort, 1 run of 4 hours | 469 |

- Approximately 65 fish from each pond run were measured for length.
- Length measurements in the channel: 15 fish from each trap holding at least 15 fish; all fish from traps holding 15 or fewer. Fifteen fish were measured from the three SW spring traps.
- **Size distribution sample sizes:** Pond, 395 individuals (September 1992); Channel, 290 individuals (September 1992).
- Capture numbers were **not consistently declining** across runs.
- **No non-native fish** were captured or observed in the pond or channel.
- Channel trap counts for September 8 ranged from 10 to 111 individuals per trap (traps #48 to #60, including #57, #58, and #60 at the SW spring).

### Population Trend Context

- 1975 high: **7294** individuals.
- 1981 low: **1800** individuals.
- Intervening years (1976 to 1980): range of **3000 to 6700** individuals.
- The reliability of these historical figures has been questioned.

### Conclusions

- The 1992 total catch in the pond, channel, and springs was considerably greater than in 1991. Possible explanations include a mild winter, a very wet spring, better catching luck, and more careful trap placement at known pupfish locations in the pond, especially on the second day.
- Visual observations indicated that the fish caught were only a small fraction of the total pupfish population in the pond and channel.
- Staff observed a healthy grouping of age and size classes.
- No non-native species or other exotic competitors were observed.
- **Overall assessment:** The present population of *C. macularius eremus* is in good condition.

### Future Plans

- Continue ongoing monitoring of the pupfish population at Quitobaquito.
- Establish consistent census techniques for the pond and channel.
- Conduct weekly examinations, with inspections and censuses in May and September, to monitor the population and check the pond for non-native fish species.
