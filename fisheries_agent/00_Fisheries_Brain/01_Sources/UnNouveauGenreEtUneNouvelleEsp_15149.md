# Haploginglymus bragai n. gen. et n. sp.: A New Troglobiont Amphipod Genus from Portugal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #amphipoda

## Executive Summary

This 1958 taxonomic paper by [[Amilcar Mateus]] and [[Emilia de Oliveira Mateus]], published by the [[Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre]] (Faculdade de Ciências do Porto) in the *Memórias e Estudos do Museu Zoológico da Universidade de Coimbra* (No. 252), describes a new genus and species of groundwater amphipod from Portugal. The taxon, *Haploginglymus bragai*, was collected from a well near a house in Leça da Palmeira, near Porto. The authors describe it as a monotypic genus that is distinct from [[Niphargus]], [[Crangonyx]] and [[Neoniphargus]]. The paper is written in French and is one of the few Portuguese studies of phreatic Amphipoda.

Note: the source is a crustacean (amphipod) study. No fish are described, so the fish-related fields are marked N/A.

## Background & Description

**Context of the study.** The authors state that phreatic (groundwater) fauna has been studied extensively in France, Germany, Italy and Yugoslavia, but that little work had been done in Portugal apart from the research of [[J. M. Braga]] on Isopoda and Syncarida. Only one amphipod had previously been recorded from Portuguese groundwater: *Pseudoniphargus africanus*, first known from North Africa and found in Portugal at Funchal (Madeira) and in the Lapa dos Morcegos, Setúbal district. Bibliography on the subject was very limited, mainly [[Schellenberg]]'s work (1943) on Portuguese freshwater amphipods.

**Material and methods.** The material consisted of "a few dozen" male and female specimens collected from a well near a house at Leça da Palmeira, collected by J. M. Braga in 1940. Animals were collected with a gauze net weighted and mounted on a metal arc, then preserved in 70% alcohol. Specimens were mounted whole or dissected under a stereoscope for microscope study, using Apathy gum syrup or Hoyer's medium. Drawings were made with a camera lucida. The holotype and paratypes are held at the Institute of Zoology in Porto.

**Morphological description.** The body is slightly robust, unpigmented, and reaches 9 mm. Eyes are absent. Key features include:
- Head: subpentagonal profile with rounded, weakly developed lower angles.
- Mesosome segment III is the largest; coxal plates I–IV are subquadrangular.
- Telson: cleft beyond its midpoint, with 3–4 terminal spines and 2 lateral setae, one of them plumose.
- Antennae I: flagellum of 20–26 articles, accessory flagellum of 2 articles. Antennae II: flagellum of 9–11 articles.
- Mouthparts: mandibles with a 4-toothed cutting edge; palp 3-articulated.
- Gnathopods I and II are subequal, with the propodus subtriangular.
- Pereiopods V–VII are larger than the anterior pereiopods.
- Uropod III: inner branch rudimentary with two terminal spines; outer branch uniarticulate with 9 transverse rows of spines.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species lives in groundwater (phreatic) habitats. The collection site was a well, and the authors describe the group as ecologically and zoogeographically interesting, especially given the Iberian Peninsula's distinct position among European Mediterranean countries. The authors note that marine amphipods of the Portuguese coast were comparatively well known, while freshwater (groundwater) species were "completely unknown" according to [[Schellenberg]] (1943). Also mentioned are related cave and groundwater fauna groups studied in Portugal, including [[Isopoda]] (e.g. *Stenasellus*, *Asellus*) and [[Syncarida]] (e.g. *Parabathynella lusitanica*).

No water chemistry, physical habitat measurements, population counts, or abundance data are reported.

## Key Findings

- **New genus and species:** *Haploginglymus bragai* n. gen. et n. sp. is described from Portugal. The genus name derives from Greek *haplos* (simple, unique) and *gynglymos* (articulation), referring to the uniarticulate outer branch of uropod III.
- **Diagnostic characters:** the genus is defined by (1) well-developed, detached inner lobes of the lower lip (labium); (2) subequal gnathopods I and II; and (3) a uniarticulate outer branch of uropod III.
- **Taxonomic position:** the authors considered the new taxon closest to *Niphargus* and *Crangonyx*, but it differs from both. They chose to erect a new intermediate genus rather than treat the form as a subgenus of either. Comparison with the extra-European genus *Neoniphargus* (*N. indicus*, after Stephensen 1931) also showed clear differences in mouthpart and gnathopod characters, as summarized in their comparison table.
- **Monotypic genus:** the genus is currently represented by a single species.
- **Dedication:** the species is named after [[J. M. Braga]], the authors' companion in groundwater exploration and a noted systematist.
- **Significance:** the paper notes that Portuguese groundwater Amphipoda were poorly known, and the authors aimed to intensify collection and study of this group.

The paper ends with a bibliography of about 60 entries, mostly on Portuguese cave fauna, Isopoda, Syncarida, arachnids and fisheries. Its references to sardine (*Sardina pilchardus*) fisheries are unrelated to the amphipod study.
