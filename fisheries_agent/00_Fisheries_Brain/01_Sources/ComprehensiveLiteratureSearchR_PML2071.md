# Stranding of Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker: Comprehensive Literature Search

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by Carol A. Pacey and Paul C. Marsh of [[Arizona State University]] for the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Lower Colorado Region) under Order Number 1425-98-PG-30-07540 (8 June 1998), is a preliminary step toward a long-term research program on the stranding of [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The work was prompted by a [[Biological Opinion]] from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]], which found that operation of the [[Colorado River]] jeopardized both endangered fishes. A reasonable and prudent measure required the agency to assess and reduce potential stranding.

The authors searched five major databases and assembled about 125 published works with potential relevance. The searches found almost no direct literature on stranding of these two species. The only notable hit paired *Xyrauchen* with "stranding." Most relevant work instead concerns drawdown, drought and intermittent-stream habitats.

## Background & Description

**Regulatory context.** The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation completed a biological assessment of its operation and maintenance of the Lower Colorado River (1996). The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service then issued a biological and conference opinion (1996). That opinion concluded that river operations jeopardized the continued existence of bonytail chub and razorback sucker. Its Reasonable and Prudent Measures included minimizing "take" of the two species. This literature review was the first step in that effort.

**Objective.** To assemble a comprehensive database of published information on stranding of bonytail chub and razorback sucker.

**Methods.**
- Databases searched: Biological Abstracts, Wildlife and Fisheries Review (WFR), Zoological Records, Applied Science & Technology Abstracts (ASFA) and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Reference Service. Coverage periods varied, starting from 1989, 1970, 1978, 1983 and 1861 respectively, through to date.
- Search terms: about eight key words (e.g., beach, drawdown, drought, intermittent, intermittent stream, pool, strand, tidepools) and about 19 fish names (e.g., bonytail, catostomid, chub, cyprinid, razorback, roundtail, sucker, *Xyrauchen*, freshwater fish). Fish names covered larval, juvenile and adult stages.
- Boolean "and" combined terms to narrow results. Boolean "not" was used to exclude plural variants where a database did not auto-search them.
- Terms yielding fewer than 50 hits were searched manually.
- Results were merged, alphabetized, de-duplicated and visually screened.

**Deliverable requirements.** The statement of work asked for a report listing the search terms, number of hits and a contact list for researchers working on stranding of these fishes. Each reference was to include author, date, title, journal and source. In a follow-up email (8 May 1998), [[Thomas A. Burke]] of the Bureau of Reclamation asked that the literature be organized by the key word that produced each hit, with a separate page per key word. The deadline was June 1998.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Keyword patterns.** The authors reported that "stranding" was mostly associated with mammal hits (e.g., intentional stranding of marine mammals). "Drawdown" was mostly associated with fish. The term "fish" produced both mammal and fish results, which were narrowed by pairing it with "freshwater fish."

**Habitat themes in the literature.** Most relevant work addressed:
- **Reservoir drawdown**, including fish response, sampling methods and shoreline habitat change.
- **Drought effects** on fish populations in lakes, streams, estuaries and wetlands.
- **Intermittent streams** and shrinking pools, where fish become trapped and die (e.g., [[Mundahl]] 1990 on heat death; [[Tramer]] 1977 on catastrophic mortality).
- **Stranding during flow reduction**, such as Bradford et al. (1995) on juvenile salmonids stranded during rapid flow decreases in winter.

**Native-species relevance.** Closely related native Gila species were found: [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*), discussed in Carpenter and Maughan (1993) on macrohabitat in Sycamore Creek, Arizona. The searches also returned Gila trout and Iberian chub, which are not relevant to the Colorado River system.

## Key Findings

**Search results**
- Initial searches returned **110,880 hits** (per the text). Table 2 totals appear to differ (the sum on one table page reads 81,800), so the table and text figures should be reconciled before citation.
- Excluding the broad first search term (except "tidepools") reduced the total to **4,024 hits**.
- More than **700 hits** were examined in detail.
- About **125 published works** with potential relevance make up the Comprehensive Literature Summary.

**Species-specific results**
- "Bonytail" and "razorback" produced **no hits** when combined with the broad first search terms.
- "Xyrauchen" with "stranding" produced **one noteworthy hit**: Madson (1994), "Between Two Worlds," *Wyoming Wildlife*.
- No study directly documented stranding of bonytail chub or razorback sucker in the Colorado River.

**Key relevant references (examples from the summary)**
- Bradford, M.J. et al. 1995. Stranding of juvenile coho salmon and rainbow trout during rapid flow decreases under winter conditions. *North American Journal of Fisheries Management* 15(2):473–479.
- Closs, G.P. and P.S. Lake. 1996. Drought, differential mortality and the coexistence of a native and introduced fish in a southeast Australian intermittent stream. *Environmental Biology of Fishes* 47(1):17–26.
- Mundahl, N.D. 1990. Heat death of fish in shrinking stream pools. *American Midland Naturalist* 123(1):40–46.
- Tramer, E.J. 1977. Catastrophic mortality of stream fishes trapped in shrinking pools. *American Midland Naturalist* 97(2).
- Zale, A.V. et al. 1989. The physicochemistry, flora and fauna of intermittent prairie streams: a review of the literature. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Biological Report 89(5):1–58.
- Carpenter, J. and O.E. Maughan. 1993. Macrohabitat of Sonora chub in Sycamore Creek, Arizona. *Journal of Freshwater Ecology* 8(4):265–278.

**Literature distribution**
- The drawdown and drought categories contain the most references.
- Intermittent-stream and pool categories contain many studies on fish trapped in shrinking pools.
- Stranding-specific references are few, and most concern non-target species or mammals.

**Deliverables and next steps**
- The report documents search terms, hit counts, the summarized literature (author, date, title, journal) and a contact list.
- Per Burke's request, references were to be reorganized by key word, with a separate page for each key word and its relevant references.
- The authors describe this work as a preliminary step to a long-term research program on stranding of these two endangered fishes.
