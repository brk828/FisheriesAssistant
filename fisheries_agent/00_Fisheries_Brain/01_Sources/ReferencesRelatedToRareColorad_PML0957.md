# Recovery Plan Literature Update for Colorado River Endangered Fishes (1980–Present)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1987 correspondence and reference package from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Division of Endangered Species (Denver, Colorado), addressed to Dr. Paul Marsh at Arizona State University. The Service was preparing to update the recovery plans for four endangered Colorado River fishes: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The plans, last issued in 1983, had been placed on hold pending the outcome of the Upper Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee effort. The letter requests the recipient's review of attached bibliographies of published and unpublished reports from 1980 onward, to identify additions, deletions and corrections for consideration by the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] before updates scheduled for March–April 1987.

## Background & Description

The document comprises:

- **Cover letter** (Colorado/Platte River Endangered Species Recovery Coordinator) requesting review of reference lists, page and report numbers, and copies of key reports.
- **Four reference lists**, dated January 20, 1987, covering literature from 1980 to present:
  - General "Related Literature" on rare Colorado River fishes and natural resource development (roughly 110 entries, including multi-volume energy development and habitat reports).
  - Humpback Chub update list (about 60 entries).
  - Colorado Squawfish update list (about 90 entries).
  - Razorback Sucker references (about 90 entries, including grey literature from Lake Mohave and the Gila River basin).

The reference lists cover fish biology, habitat requirements, hatchery and culture methods, reservoir and water development impacts, population monitoring reports (many by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] and [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]]), and non-native fish interactions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The literature describes the four species as rare, large-river fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin whose populations declined under habitat alteration by dams, reservoirs and water diversion. Key themes across the references include:

- **Habitat alteration and flow regime:** Impacts of energy development and water projects, including [[Taylor Draw Reservoir]] (environmental assessments, feasibility studies, draft EIS) and the [[White River Dam]] project. Studies address the role of flows in the Yampa River and the loss of stream passage.
- **Water quality and temperature:** Tolerance and preference studies for total dissolved solids and temperature, and effects of incubation temperature on embryo survival (Marsh, 1985).
- **Non-native species:** Predation by [[Largemouth Bass]] on stocked squawfish, choking mortality from channel catfish, and Kaeding's (1986) argument that introduced fishes negatively affect native species.
- **Hatchery and reintroduction:** Culture techniques, hatchery roles in recovery, and reintroduction into historic habitats (e.g., Arizona, Lake Mohave).
- **Population monitoring:** Long-term monitoring series under the [[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Program]] and trend-zone monitoring, plus radiotelemetry studies of habitat use.

## Key Findings

Main points and planned actions contained in the document:

1. **Recovery plans are being updated.** Plans were drafted in 1983 but held pending the Coordinating Committee effort; updates are scheduled for March–April 1987.
2. **Substantial new information exists since 1980**, especially for the [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. The Service requested reviewers to identify missing references, corrections and deletions.
3. **Reviewer tasks:** Supply page numbers, report numbers and copies of highlighted reports; the Service is abstracting selected reports for the Recovery Team.
4. **Razorback Sucker library:** The Service is maintaining a current library of razorback publications to support its recovery effort for that species, and will send the final reference list and abstracts on completion.
5. **Scope of literature:** Reference lists are dominated by state and federal agency progress reports, internal reports and conference abstracts, indicating that much of the recovery-relevant data is grey literature requiring active collection.
6. **Notable recurring research topics:** Habitat requirements and limiting factors (Haynes, Muth and Nesler 1985), life history and early development (Muth, Haynes, Kaeding), non-native predation and competition (Pimentel et al. 1985; Osmundson 1986), and reservoir impacts (Taylor Draw Reservoir).
7. **Contacts:** Richard Wydoski or the Colorado/Platte River Endangered Species Recovery Coordinator.
