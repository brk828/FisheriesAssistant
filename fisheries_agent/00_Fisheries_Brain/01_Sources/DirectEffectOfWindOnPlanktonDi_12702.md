# Direct Effect of Wind on Plankton Distribution

#limnology #zooplankton #crustacea

## Executive Summary

This 1966 study by Langford & Jermolajev investigates the **direct effect of wind action on horizontal distribution of freshwater zooplankton** in three small lakes (Clarke, Brewer, and Kearney) in Algonquin Park, Ontario. Building on earlier work by [[Langford 1938 Lake Nipissing Study]] that revealed marked horizontal variation in crustacean abundance, the authors conducted systematic sampling across multiple stations and depths (0, 1, 3, 5, 8, 10 m) under varying wind conditions. The central finding is that sustained wind produces a consistent pattern: **epilimnial accumulation of microcrustacean plankton at the leeward (downwind) end of a lake**, with a corresponding paucity at the windward end, accompanied by measurable displacement/tipping of the thermocline. This work is part of a broader limnological literature (citing [[D'Ancona 1955]], [[Mortimer 1952]], [[Southern and Gardiner 1932]], [[Ragotzkie and Bryson 1953]], [[Colebrook 1960]]) demonstrating that lake plankton distribution is fundamentally heterogeneous rather than uniform, with important implications for productivity studies relying on single-station vertical sampling.

## Background & Description

While this is primarily an ecological/behavioral study rather than a morphological one, the following taxon-specific distributional and developmental (life-stage) distinctions are noted:

- **Diaptomus spp.** (D. oregonensis, D. minutus): Adults and juveniles/copepodites showed differential vertical distribution — epilimnial concentrations often composed of **adults**, while hypolimnial concentrations were dominated by **juveniles/late copepodite stages**.
- **Cyclops spp.**: Species-level differentiation observed between depths —
  - *Mesocyclops edax*: juveniles and few mature females found in epilimnion.
  - *Cyclops bicuspidatus* and *Cyclops scutifer*: juveniles plus few adults found in hypolimnion.
  - *Tropocyclops prasinus*: adults found in shallow bay habitat at Brewer Lake.
- **Daphnia, Diaphanosoma, Bosmina, Ceriodaphnia**: Treated at genus level; distributional patterns tracked as whole populations, with occasional depth-stratified subpopulations (e.g., Diaphanosoma showing both surface and thermocline-region maxima).

No formal morphometric or anatomical descriptions were provided in this text; the "morphological" data are limited to life-stage (naupliar/juvenile/adult) distinctions used to interpret behavioral responses to wind-driven water movement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure**: All three study lakes are small (11–81 ha), moderately deep (max depth 11–15 m), dimictic lakes with clear thermal stratification into **epilimnion, thermocline, and hypolimnion** (see [[Thermal Stratification in Lakes]]).
- **Vertical niche partitioning**: Zooplankton genera exhibited depth-based niche differentiation, with epilimnion generally favored during calm conditions, and species/life-stage-specific hypolimnial refugia (particularly for Cyclops species and Diaptomus copepodites).
- **Wind-driven horizontal redistribution**: The central ecological mechanism examined is that **wind-induced surface/epilimnial currents transport zooplankton toward the leeward shore**, while organisms with sufficient locomotory power resist being swept back by deeper reverse currents — allowing them to accumulate at the lee end rather than being homogeneously redistributed. This is analogous to wind-driven distributional mechanisms described for the pelagic zooplanktivorous fish [[Oncorhynchus Nerka]] (sockeye salmon) in Nilkitkwa Lake, British Columbia ([[Johnson 1961]]).
- **Behavioral/locomotory role**: The authors propose that **differential swimming ability** determines whether a species shows leeward concentration (strong swimmers resisting reverse currents) versus more uniform epilimnial distribution (weak swimmers passively advected back and forth). All crustacean taxa examined in this study behaved as effective swimmers, showing leeward concentration rather than uniform distribution.
- **Exception/reverse current dynamics**: On Brewer Lake (28 July 1956), a rare **windward concentration** of adult Diaptomus, Cyclops, Daphnia, and Bosmina was observed during an episode of extreme thermocline displacement, attributed to unusually strong deep reverse currents within the epilimnion overriding the typical leeward-accumulation pattern.
- **Diurnal migration**: Referenced but not directly measured in these lakes; most taxa showed daytime epilimnetic maxima, with diurnal vertical migration likely restricted to the upper 8–10 m (per author discussion with Edmondson).

## Key Findings

1. **Consistent leeward accumulation pattern**: Under sustained, moderate-to-strong wind (~9.3–21.3 km/h persisting for hours), zooplankton (Diaptomus, Cyclops, Daphnia, Diaphanosoma, Bosmina, Ceriodaphnia) consistently concentrated in the **epilimnion at the leeward end** of all three lakes studied, regardless of lake shape or depth contour differences.
2. **Thermocline displacement as a wind indicator**: The degree of thermocline tipping/compression at the leeward end (visualized via isotherm displacement) correlated with the intensity and duration of wind, serving as a physical proxy for water movement strength — consistent with seiche-type dynamics described by [[Mortimer 1952]] and [[Colebrook 1960]].
3. **Calm-day distribution differs**: On calm days, plankton tended toward more central-lake concentration (Clarke Lake) or accumulation over the deepest basin areas (Brewer Lake), rather than clear leeward/windward asymmetry — though residual patterns from prior wind events (persistent stratification effects) could still be detected (e.g., Kearney Lake, 16 Aug 1958, showing opposing-end concentrations despite calm conditions).
4. **Species/life-stage specific depth partitioning under wind stress**: Even during leeward accumulation events, populations often split by life stage between epilimnion (adults) and hypolimnion (juveniles/nauplii), particularly for Diaptomus and Cyclops — suggesting depth-selection behavior is maintained even while horizontal position shifts with wind.
5. **One anomalous windward concentration**: A single exception (Brewer Lake, 28 July 1956) showed windward concentration of adult crustaceans during an especially strong wind event, hypothesized to result from strong reverse currents at the base of the epilimnion overriding the typical surface-driven leeward transport.
6. **Implications for sampling methodology**: The findings reinforce that **single-station vertical sampling is inadequate** for estimating whole-lake plankton productivity, since horizontal heterogeneity driven by wind can be substantial — echoing concerns raised in [[Langford 1938 Lake Nipissing Study]] and the broader critique by [[D'Ancona 1955]] of "homogeneous uniform distribution" assumptions in limnoplankton studies.
7. **Proposed mechanism**: Wind generates surface currents that passively transport plankton at epilimnetic depths toward the lee shore; motile crustaceans resist return via deeper reverse currents by maintaining preferred depth/light conditions, leading to net leeward accumulation — a mechanism paralleling wind-driven distribution reported for pelagic fish such as sockeye salmon ([[Oncorhynchus Nerka]]) in other systems.
