# Gene and Chromosome Homology in Fishes of the Genus Xiphophorus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Kallman & Atz]] (1967) examines gene and chromosome homology among poeciliid fishes of the genus [[Xiphophorus]]. The authors review and describe macromelanophore (large pigment cell) and micromelanophore (tail-spot) patterns, analyze their inheritance through laboratory crosses, and test whether the underlying loci lie on homologous chromosomes. The work is based on laboratory-bred strains derived from wild-caught and commercial stocks, many of which are hybrids of uncertain origin. The authors argue that the sex chromosomes of [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]], [[Xiphophorus Variatus]] and [[Xiphophorus Milleri]] are homologous and that the tail-spot loci of these three species are likely homologous as well.

## Background & Description

The genus Xiphophorus includes species that are interfertile to an appreciable degree, which makes them suitable for genetic experiments requiring hybridization. Two classes of pigment genes are central to the study:

- **Macromelanophore patterns**: large pigment cells (often 0.3 to 0.5 mm in diameter) that can occur over much of the body. These are controlled by dominant, sex-linked alleles in [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]] and related forms.
- **Micromelanophore tail-spot patterns**: small pigment cells clustered near the caudal fin base. Gordon recognized seven basic tail patterns in addition to wild type, forming a single autosomal dominant allelic series in maculatus.

The authors note that polygenic traits cannot easily be used to assess gene homology, so the study focuses on characters governed by a single major gene existing in at least two recognizably different allelic states.

Taxa and populations examined include:
- [[Xiphophorus Couchianus]] (two subspecies noted, no patterns recorded)
- [[Xiphophorus Variatus]] subspecies *xiphidium*, *variatus* and *evelynae*
- [[Xiphophorus Milleri]] from Lake Catemaco, Veracruz
- [[Xiphophorus Montezumae]] subspecies *montezumae* and *cortezi*, plus [[Xiphophorus Pygmaeus]] *nigrensis*
- [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]], with many inbred strains (e.g., Jp 30, Jp 163, Hp-1, Hp-2, Np, Gp)
- [[Xiphophorus Hellerii]] subspecies *hellerii*, *strigatus*, *guentheri*, and *alvarezi* (Rio Santa Domingo)

Fish were bred and maintained at the Genetics Laboratory of the [[New York Aquarium]] following established methods. Many were preserved in formalin or alcohol. Funding came from U.S. Public Health Service grants, and the Rio Chajmaic fish were collected on the 1963 [[American Museum of Natural History]] Guatemalan Expedition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The genus is Middle American and spans many river systems, including the Rio Panuco, Rio Tamesi, Rio Tecolutla, Rio Jamapa, Rio Coatzacoalcos, Rio Grijalva and Rio Usumacinta. Geographic range was linked to polymorphism: species and subspecies with extensive ranges (e.g., [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]], [[Xiphophorus Variatus]] *variatus*, [[Xiphophorus Hellerii]]) are more polymorphic, whereas forms with restricted ranges (e.g., [[Xiphophorus Couchianus]], [[Xiphophorus Pygmaeus]] *pygmaeus*, [[Xiphophorus Variatus]] *evelynae*) show few or no patterns. Gordon (1943) is cited for this pattern.

The authors also note that commercial swordtails and platyfish often carry genes introduced by introgressive hybridization, so pattern origins in domesticated stocks are uncertain. The "montezuma" swordtail, for example, is interpreted as a hybrid product rather than a natural variety.

## Key Findings

**Macromelanophore patterns**
- In [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]], five natural patterns (spotted, striped, spotted dorsal, nigra, spotted belly) and two domestic-stock patterns (fuliginosus, pepper-and-salt) were reviewed. Crossing over within the macromelanophore locus was recorded in two cases, suggesting a super-gene or suballelic series.
- In [[Xiphophorus Variatus]], the authors describe or name several patterns, including flecked (F1 and F2), punctatus (P1 and P2), lined (Li), and striped (Sr). Different spotted patterns were shown to be caused by different sex-linked alleles.
- In [[Xiphophorus Milleri]], the spotted ventral pattern (Sv) showed strictly paternal inheritance, indicating an XX-XY system with Sv on the Y chromosome.
- In [[Xiphophorus Montezumae]] *cortezi*, the spotted caudal (Sc) and spotted (At) patterns were inherited as separate, non-sex-linked autosomal factors. At was dominant. In inbred strain 38, 314 offspring were recorded (135 AtSc males and females combined, 5 + 3 + 1 + 2 + 59 + 25 + 84 + 135 across classes, with 165 males and 149 females in the table). The authors also documented a red body coloration in backcross hybrids with [[Xiphophorus Hellerii]] that always accompanied the Sc pattern, which they interpret as a linked gene from *cortezi*.
- In [[Xiphophorus Hellerii]] *guentheri*, two dominant autosomal spotting patterns were described: Db1 (Bx strain, 100% penetrance, inbred) and Db2 (Hx strain, striped arrangement). Crosses indicated these are likely different genes, though allelism was not formally tested.

**Tail-spot patterns**
- In [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]], the patterns one-spot (O) and dot (D) were confirmed as distinct alleles, and the complete-crescent (Cc) pattern was shown to be inherited together with dot or one-spot in three cases. The authors note these are the best evidence for either composite alleles or a super-gene.
- [[Xiphophorus Milleri]] has three tail-spot patterns: bar (B), point (Pt) and single spot (Ss), which form a single dominant autosomal allelic series.
- [[Xiphophorus Variatus]] *xiphidium* and *variatus* share the peduncular spot (Ps), crescent (C), and cut-crescent (Ct) patterns, with the upper cut-crescent (Cu) possibly a variant.
- [[Xiphophorus Hellerii]] from the Rio Chajmaic, Guatemala, is unusual: both sexes carry a ventral caudal spot (grave, Gr) that resembles the sword edge in males elsewhere. Seven matings produced 22 males and 205 females, all with grave.

**Chromosome homology**
- The sex chromosomes of [[Xiphophorus Variatus]], [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]] and [[Xiphophorus Milleri]] are homologous. Tests included backcrosses (e.g., Table X, Table XII) showing that macromelanophore genes of maculatus and milleri segregate on the same sex chromosome pair.
- The Db1 locus of [[Xiphophorus Hellerii]] Bx is not on the chromosome homologous to the sex chromosomes of maculatus (Gordon, 1958, cited).
- Tail-spot loci of maculatus and variatus *xiphidium* were concluded to be on homologous chromosomes (h-20 and h-30 crosses: 30 Co, 5 C, 4 wild type; h-38: 57 Co, 16 C, 8 wild type).
- Tail-spot loci of maculatus and milleri were concluded to be on homologous chromosomes after backcross tests (476 backcross hybrids: 224 single spot, 249 twin-spot, one both, two none). A non-disjunction event in pedigree 1691 was analyzed (chi-square = 8.3, p between 0.05 and 0.01).

**Conclusions and discussion**
- The sex-chromosome homology of three species strongly supports a common ancestor with an XX-XY sex system. The authors reject an earlier explanation of independent evolution from polygenic systems.
- Macromelanophore alleles of maculatus, variatus and milleri are not identical; differences persist in hybrids.
- Some patterns are shared across species (e.g., one-spot and single spot; dot and point; simple crescent), but others are unique (e.g., moon, comet, bar).
- The authors propose that the Rio Chajmaic swordtail population has freed the grave pattern from androgen control, an idea summarized in the 11-point Summary.
- Breadth of the study is limited by the lack of known ancestry for many domestic stocks and by incomplete penetrance of some genes (e.g., Sc).

**Summary (main points)**
1. Xiphophorus species are interfertile and polymorphic for major-gene pigment patterns, making them well suited for homology studies.
2. Eighteen macromelanophore and 16 micromelanophore patterns were reviewed, and a uniform nomenclature was applied.
3. Sex chromosomes of maculatus, variatus and milleri are homologous and carry homologous macromelanophore genes.
4. The tail-spot alleles of maculatus, variatus and milleri are highly probably homologous.
5. Some other pigment genes lie on non-homologous chromosomes.
6. Crossing over within the tail-spot locus of maculatus was observed in three cases, supporting a super-gene arrangement.
7. Restricted-range taxa are less polymorphic than widespread ones.
8. A non-disjunction case in a milleri x maculatus hybrid was documented.
