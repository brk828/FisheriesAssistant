# Artificial Key to the Adult Goodeids of Mexico

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a **dichotomous taxonomic key** (not a standard experimental study) designed to identify adult specimens of the family [[Goodeidae]], a group of viviparous freshwater fishes endemic to the highland drainages of central Mexico. The key by Webb & Smith uses external, macroscopically observable morphological traits — sensory pore configuration, anal- and dorsal-fin ray counts, fin-origin position, dentition (tooth cusp/shoulder morphology), body depth, caudal peduncle proportions, pigmentation patterns, and secondary sexual characteristics of breeding males — to discriminate among genera and species within [[Goodeidae]]. The excerpt covers portions of the key addressing the genera [[Girardinichthys]], [[Hubbsina]], [[Skiffia]], [[Allodontichthys]], [[Allotoca]], [[Xenotaenia]], [[Characodon]], and [[Ilyodon]].

## Background & Description

### Sensory system (cranial pores)
- **Character 1a**: Sensory system on top of head consists of exposed neuromasts only; supraorbital pores and canals absent (rarely a single canal present between two pores on snout in [[Skiffia bilineata]]).

### Fin-ray counts and fin position
- **Anal-fin rays**: Diagnostic ranges of 18–26 rays (base longer than longest rays, except in female [[Girardinichthys viviparus]]) versus 13–17 rays (base shorter than longest rays) separate major lineages.
- **Dorsal-fin origin**: Used repeatedly as a key character —
  - Well in advance of pelvic-fin insertion with 30–37 dorsal rays: [[Hubbsina turneri]].
  - Behind pelvic-fin insertion with 11–17 dorsal rays: remainder of the [[Skiffia]] group.
  - Set far behind vs. near/in advance of the midpoint of total body length (including caudal fin) separates further genera (e.g., [[Characodon]] vs. [[Ilyodon]] lineage).
- **Dorsal-fin notching in males**: Presence/absence and degree of an indented interradial membrane separating the first 4–5 rays distinguishes [[Skiffia bilineata]] (not notched) from other *Skiffia* species (notched).

### Body shape and proportions
- Body depth ratio (more than vs. less than one-third of body length) separates [[Girardinichthys multiradiatus]] (deep-bodied) from [[Girardinichthys viviparus]] (slender-bodied).
- Caudal peduncle height relative to length, and caudal peduncle length relative to head length, are used to separate [[Allotoca meeki]] and related taxa.
- Anterior head profile: strongly wedge-shaped and superior mouth cleft in [[Skiffia francesae]] vs. blunt profile and scarcely superior mouth in [[Skiffia multipunctata]].
- Dorsal profile at the nape: indented in [[Characodon audax]] vs. convex in [[Characodon lateralis]].

### Scale counts
- Lateral series scale counts distinguish *Skiffia* species: 35–40 scales with 11–14 dorsal rays in [[Skiffia lermae]] vs. 30–35 scales with 14–18 dorsal rays in the [[Skiffia francesae]]/[[Skiffia multipunctata]] pair.

### Dentition
- Outer-series jaw teeth morphology is a major generic-level character:
  - Prominent shoulders, becoming tricuspid near the symphysis in both jaws: [[Allodontichthys hubbsi]].
  - Shouldered but not tricuspid (except in large individuals): remaining *Allodontichthys* species.
  - Teeth flattened to form lateral shoulders or cusps vs. bifid teeth splits further genera (e.g., [[Xenotaenia]], [[Characodon]] vs. others).
  - Approximate tooth counts and attachment: ~25 strongly set outer teeth in a uniform row ([[Ilyodon furcidens]]) vs. ~45 weakly set outer teeth in a staggered row.

### Pigmentation and coloration
- Lateral stripe continuity: unbroken in [[Allodontichthys tamazulae]] vs. broken into irregular blotches in [[Allodontichthys zonistus]], the latter also having a mostly black or blotched dorsal fin.
- Blotch above anus: large and conspicuous blackish in [[Girardinichthys multiradiatus]] vs. small/inconspicuous or absent in [[Girardinichthys viviparus]].
- Scale pigmentation: posterior halves of scales above the lateral series bear dark pigment producing a speckled appearance in [[Xenotaenia resolanae]] (absent in related taxa).
- Breeding male coloration: mostly black with isolated silvery scale flecking and no yellow/orange in median fins in [[Characodon audax]] vs. non-black body, no silvery flecking, and prominent yellow/orange banding in median fins in [[Characodon lateralis]].
- [[Skiffia multipunctata]] males show a variable number of irregular black spots on the body sides, absent in [[Skiffia francesae]].

### Secondary sexual characters (breeding males)
- Body darkening without turning fully black: [[Girardinichthys multiradiatus]].
- Fully black breeding males: [[Girardinichthys viviparus]] and [[Characodon audax]].
- Anal-fin morphology in adult males (e.g., straight/curved base nearly parallel to midline, anterior lobe with labia concealing proximal 1/4–1/2 of anal rays) used to separate genera near [[Ilyodon]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- All taxa in this key belong to [[Goodeidae]], a family of small, live-bearing (viviparous) freshwater fishes restricted to the Mesa Central of Mexico, typically inhabiting highland streams, springs, and lake systems associated with the Lerma-Santiago drainage and adjacent endorheic basins.
- Several included species are narrow-range endemics with high conservation concern, notably [[Skiffia francesae]] and [[Characodon audax]], reflecting the historically fragmented, spring-fed habitats characteristic of the Mexican highlands.
- The genus [[Girardinichthys]] exhibits sexual dimorphism in body depth and anal-fin ray counts, consistent with viviparous reproductive strategies typical of the family, where modified anal-fin structures (gonopodium-like) in males relate to internal fertilization.
- Species such as [[Ilyodon furcidens]] show specialized dentition (numerous fine, uniformly arranged outer teeth), suggestive of algal/detrital grazing ecology common among goodeids in benthic stream habitats.

## Key Findings

- The key relies on a hierarchical combination of **meristic counts** (anal-fin rays, dorsal-fin rays, lateral scale series), **cranial sensory pore configuration**, **dentition morphology**, and **sexually dimorphic pigmentation/fin structure** to discriminate among genera and species of Mexican [[Goodeidae]].
- Absence of supraorbital head pores (exposed neuromasts only) is a major synapomorphy uniting several genera at the top of the key (couplet 1a), distinguishing them from goodeids with fully developed supraorbital pore systems (implied in the unlisted 1b branch).
- Anal-fin ray count thresholds (13–17 vs. 18–26) form a primary bifurcation separating [[Girardinichthys]] species from the [[Hubbsina]]/[[Skiffia]] lineage.
- Dorsal-fin origin position relative to the pelvic-fin insertion and to overall body midpoint is repeatedly diagnostic across multiple couplets, indicating its taxonomic importance across genera ([[Hubbsina turneri]], [[Skiffia]], [[Characodon]], and taxa near [[Xenotaenia resolanae]]).
- Dentition type (shouldered vs. tricuspid vs. bifid outer teeth) is used as a key generic-level character separating [[Allodontichthys]] from [[Xenotaenia]], [[Characodon]], and [[Ilyodon]] lineages.
- Coloration and pigmentation patterns in breeding males (black body coloration, silvery flecking, yellow/orange median fin bands, irregular black spotting) provide species-level diagnostic characters within [[Skiffia]] and [[Characodon]].
