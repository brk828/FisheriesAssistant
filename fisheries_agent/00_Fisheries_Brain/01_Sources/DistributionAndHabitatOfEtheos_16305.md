# Distribution and Habitat of Etheostoma atripinne in Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This study reassesses the distribution and habitat of the [[Cumberland Snubnose Darter]] (*[[Etheostoma Atripinne]]*) in Kentucky, using new field collections (June 1979 to September 1981) and museum and state collection records. The species' range is now defined as continuous from [[Fishing Creek]], Pulaski County, downstream in the [[Cumberland River]] system to the [[Little River]], Trigg County, Kentucky. The authors extend the known western (downstream) range by approximately 118 km, from the [[Red River]] to the Little River. The darter occupies cobble-pebble riffles in third- to fifth-order streams with low siltation and well-buffered, hard water. The authors also flag a likely competitive relationship with other members of the subgenus [[Nanostoma Subgenus]] and call for further collecting in high-quality western streams.

## Background & Description

*Etheostoma atripinne* is a small percid darter of the subgenus *Nanostoma*. Before this study, [[Burr 1980 Checklist]] recorded only six Kentucky localities, all in the Cumberland River basin, so the species was considered relatively uncommon in the state. It is, however, widespread in the Cumberland River of Tennessee (Etnier 1980).

Taxonomy is unsettled. Current evidence suggests *E. atripinne* is conspecific with *[[Etheostoma Simoterum]]* (the Tennessee snubnose darter), and D. A. Etnier was revising the systematics of both species at the time of writing. Page and Mayden (1981) had studied the biology of the species under the name *E. simoterum*. Nomenclature follows [[Common and Scientific Names of Fishes]] (Robins et al. 1980).

Methods included:
- **Seining:** Minnow seines (1.8 × 3.0 m, 3.2 mm square mesh) were used at quantitative sites. A representative riffle-pool section was seined for 10 minutes by a four-man team.
- **Chemical collecting:** Sodium cyanide, following [[Tatum 1968]], was used at some sites.
- **Site sampling:** Seven sites were sampled. Quantitative collections were made at Fishing, Sulphur, and Meshack creeks, with three riffle-pool habitats sampled at Meshack Creek.
- **Physical and chemical data:** Water quality, substrate, and macroinvertebrates were measured at Meshack, Fishing, and Sulphur creeks in summer 1979, following [[Harker et al. 1980 Survey]].
- **Vouchers:** Specimens are held at the [[Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission]], University of Tennessee, Cornell University, Southern Illinois University Carbondale, and the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology.
- **Data sources:** The authors also drew on state collection records from the [[Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources]] (KFW) ichthyological collection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat structure.** Historically, *E. atripinne* was characterized as a fish of small to medium streams with gravel riffles and slab-rock pools with current (Etnier 1980; Page and Mayden 1981). This study found it in streams from third to fifth order. It was taken almost exclusively in or very near riffles, although adults were observed in slower current directly above riffles at [[Meshack Creek]].

**Physical parameters (Table 1, six streams):**
- Riffle depth: 0.05–0.45 m; riffle width: 3–20 m.
- Gradient at survey stations: 0.7–3.2 m/km.
- Riffle velocity: 0.098–0.690 m/s.

**Substrate.** At the three intensively sampled sites, riffle substrate was at least 90% cobble and pebble, with cobble predominating at two sites. Pebble made up 66% or more of the fine fraction. Sand, silt, and clay were negligible at all sites. The exception was the most upstream [[Marrowbone Creek]] site, where bedrock chutes with slab boulder and cobble dominated. The darter was infrequently taken there.

**Water quality (Table 3, three intensively sampled streams):**

| Parameter | Fishing Creek | Meshack Creek | Sulphur Creek |
|---|---|---|---|
| pH | 8.3 | 8.1 | 8.2 |
| Conductivity (µmhos) | 209 | 279 | 264 |
| Total hardness (mg/l CaCO₃) | 90 | 154 | 150 |
| Total alkalinity (mg/l CaCO₃) | 96.6 | 139.6 | 154.6 |
| Dissolved O₂ (mg/l) | 11.5 | 10.4 | 8.7 |
| Turbidity (NTU) | 4.0 | 5.0 | 5.4 |

The authors describe these as well-buffered, hard-water streams with high dissolved solids, low turbidity, and high dissolved oxygen. Other streams with this darter were also clear and of high quality. Macroinvertebrate fauna at Meshack, Sulphur, and Fishing creeks comprised 7 to 10 orders, with Ephemeroptera predominating (Harker et al. 1980).

**Associated fishes.** Direct percid associates were *E. blennioides*, *E. caeruleum*, *E. flabellare*, *E. rufilineatum*, *E. spectabile*, *E. squamiceps*, an undescribed *E. (Nanostoma)* sp., and *Percina caprodes*. Frequently co-occurring species included *Campostoma anomalum*, *Hypentelium nigricans*, *Noturus flavus*, and *Cottus carolinae*.

**Competition.** Distributional evidence suggests competitive exclusion may operate between *E. atripinne* and other *Nanostoma* species. East of Fishing Creek, an undescribed *Nanostoma* species occupies the Rockcastle River, the Big South Fork (excluding the Little South Fork), and the upper Cumberland River above the falls. In Tennessee, *E. atripinne* is widespread except in the upper [[Caney Fork River]], which holds *E. etnieri*. Where the two species are sympatric, one greatly outnumbers the other.

## Key Findings

1. **Revised range.** *E. atripinne* is continuous from Fishing Creek (Pulaski County) downstream to the Little River (Trigg County). The western or downstream edge is extended about 118 km, from the Red River to the Little River.

2. **Eastern limit.** The easternmost and most upstream viable population is in Fishing Creek. The historic Kennedy (=Canada) Creek record (Wayne County, Kirsch 1893) could not be confirmed by recent collecting (Comiskey and Etnier 1972; Harker et al. 1980). Comiskey and Etnier (1972) suggested that this population may reflect a relatively recent faunal transfer from a more westerly Cumberland tributary.

3. **Abundance.** Of 2,484 individuals collected in five quantitative samples, 81 were *E. atripinne*, with a relative abundance of 0.3–8.9%. The authors note the species is "much more common in Kentucky than was previously known."

4. **Habitat specialization.** The species is restricted to cobble-pebble riffles in third- to fifth-order streams with gradients of 0.7–3.2 m/km, velocities of 0.098–0.690 m/s, and negligible fines.

5. **Water quality.** It inhabits well-buffered, hard-water streams (pH about 8.0–8.3) with low turbidity, high dissolved oxygen, and little or no siltation.

6. **Taxonomy.** *E. atripinne* is probably conspecific with *E. simoterum*, pending revision by D. A. Etnier. The authors note that the subgenus *Nanostoma* is poorly understood.

7. **Future work.** Few additional populations are expected east of Fishing Creek, despite nearly 100 years of collecting in the region. The authors recommend:
   - further collecting in high-quality streams west of the Little River to determine whether other populations exist;
   - further study of the ecology and distribution of the *Nanostoma* subgenus, including the competitive relationships suggested here.
