# Biological Opinion: Blue River Road - Bush Creek (Joy) Reroute

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #endangered-species-act #habitat-management

## Executive Summary

This biological opinion, issued October 14, 1997, addresses the realignment of Forest Road 281 near [[Bush Creek]] on the upper [[Blue River]], [[Greenlee County]], [[Arizona]]. The [[U.S. Forest Service]] (lead agency) and [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] proposed rerouting approximately 1,200 feet of road that currently crosses the [[Blue River]] and runs between the river and [[Bush Creek]]. The new 1,400-foot alignment would move the road to the toe of the hillside southwest of [[Bush Creek]], reducing chronic maintenance needs and adverse impacts to aquatic and riparian habitats. The opinion concludes **non-jeopardy** for both the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). While short-term construction impacts (sediment, vegetation removal) are anticipated, the realignment is expected to provide long-term benefits by reducing channelization, decreasing sediment inputs from repeated road repairs, and allowing riparian recovery in the area between old and new alignments.

## Background & Description

### Species Status

**[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was listed as threatened on October 28, 1986. This small, slender cyprinid with upwardly-directed eyes historically ranged throughout the Verde, Salt, San Pedro, San Francisco, and Gila River basins. Habitat destruction and competition/predation from nonnative species have reduced its range by approximately 85%. The species inhabits shallow, swift water over gravel, cobble, and rubble substrates, using interstitial spaces for resting and spawning. Life span is about 2 years; spawning occurs March-May (with some autumn spawning documented). The species feeds exclusively on aquatic insects. Recent genetic work indicates substantial differences between remnant populations, which should be managed as separate units. Although listed as threatened, a petition to uplist to endangered status has been found warranted but is precluded by higher-priority listing actions.

**[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) was listed as endangered on October 23, 1991. This large catostomid (over two feet length) with a distinctive sharp-edged dorsal ridge was once common throughout the [[Colorado River]] basin but is now rare. In the lower basin, a substantial population exists only in [[Lake Mohave]]. The species has been reintroduced to the [[Blue River]] through stocking (1986-1989) using hatchery stock from [[Lake Mohave]] via [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. Few recaptures have occurred; no razorback suckers were found during recent surveys (1994-1996), though the river is considered occupied. Adults inhabit mainstream and backwater areas; spawning occurs January-May in shallow water over large gravel or cobble with minimal fine sediment. Life span reaches approximately 50 years.

### Project Description

The existing alignment is a constructed dike of pushed-up river gravel that washes out during flooding, requiring frequent maintenance that severely perturbs the river and riparian habitats. The road currently crosses the [[Blue River]] via a low-water ford (Joy crossing) and runs between the river and [[Bush Creek]], crossing the creek near its confluence with the river. The new alignment would continue westerly after crossing the river, cross [[Bush Creek]] before turning southerly along the hillside toe to rejoin the existing road southwest of [[Bush Creek]]. Approximately half the project area is on private land, half on [[U.S. Forest Service]] land.

Construction would take approximately one week and involve: (1) preparing the new alignment using bulldozer and road grader; (2) removing material from the existing roadbed via front-end loader and trucks or scraper; (3) placing material on the new alignment; (4) constructing a ford crossing at [[Bush Creek]] on the new alignment; (5) installing a culvert for [[Blue River Fish Hatchery]] settling pond drainage. All equipment operation would be confined to within 35 feet of alignment centerlines. No water would be removed from the river; if needed, water would come from hatchery ponds. The west lane of the old roadbed would remain as a protective dike. No hardening/riprap would be installed initially (covered under separate consultation for normal maintenance).

**Unauthorized Work**: Before September 19, 1997, approximately 120 feet of the new alignment was constructed on private land, including culvert installation and approximately 3,200 square feet of [[Bush Creek]] disturbance and 1,000 square feet of [[Blue River]] floodplain disturbance. This work was addressed through [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] enforcement action and authorized under Nationwide Permit No. 32.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Blue River Ecosystem Status

The [[Blue River]] aquatic and riparian communities represent a badly damaged ecosystem in decline. Nine fish species (65% of native fauna) have been extirpated, though one ([[Razorback Sucker]]) is being reintroduced with limited success. Floodplain soils have been mostly lost; riparian vegetation is reduced to sparse stands with little reproduction or understory. The channel has been substantially altered into a wide, unvegetated expanse of cobble, gravel, boulder, and sand with a braided, shifting, shallow low-flow channel. Base flows become critically low during dry periods; surface flow may be lost in some areas. This degradation has removed much ecosystem resilience needed for long-term support. Human uses of the watershed and valley bottom are the major degradation factor, with roads (particularly within the floodplain) contributing significantly to past and ongoing habitat degradation and potentially precluding recovery.

### Fish Community

Recent surveys (1994-1996) found [[Loach Minnow]] distributed throughout [[Campbell Blue Creek]] and the [[Blue River]] system in suitable habitat, from middle reaches of [[Campbell Blue Creek]] downstream to the confluence. The species is relatively common but least abundant of five native species, rarely constituting more than 10% (often <5%) of fish populations. Other native species present include [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*), and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*).

Nonnative species adversely affect the native fish community through competition and predation. Species reported include [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), and [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*). The scarcity of large pools, paucity of habitat structure, and flashiness of flooding may make nonnatives susceptible to removal during large flood events.

### Road Impacts

The historic road on the [[Blue River]] floodplain has been implicated as one of the major factors in river erosion and destabilization (Leopold, 1946). The continued existence, use, maintenance, and repair of FR 281 is among the largest discrete human activities adversely affecting the river ecosystem and arguably the single largest Federal impact at present. Roads cause sediment input, contribute to bank and channel instability and erosion, remove or reduce riparian vegetation, constrict channels, and compact bank soils and stream substrates. Construction and protection of roads are among the leading causes of stream channelization.

## Key Findings

### Effects Analysis

**Long-term Benefits**: The most significant effect is alteration of how the road interacts with river channel function. The existing alignment is a dike within the active channel that channelizes both the river and [[Bush Creek]], constraining natural meander patterns. While only half the existing roadbed width would be removed initially (leaving the other half as protective dike), over time this dike is likely to be breached by floods, allowing the river and creek to find more natural channels favorable to healthy ecosystem function. The new alignment is expected to require less maintenance and cause smaller-scale channelization impacts than the existing alignment, though it is located on the outside of an apparent natural meander bend where cutting typically occurs.

**Short-term Adverse Effects**: 
- **Sediment**: Road construction would cause short-term sediment increase but long-term decrease compared to continued maintenance of the existing alignment. Sediment would result from vegetation removal, material movement, ford construction, culvert installation, and channel adjustment. Work in presently dry areas would limit sediment movement, but precipitation during construction could substantially increase sediment input. Late September is normally dry, but El Niño conditions were expected to increase fall rain. Fine sediment deposition adversely affects [[Loach Minnow]] habitat by filling interstitial spaces critical for the species' benthic habit and smothering eggs.

- **Riparian Vegetation**: Removal from the new alignment would reduce buffering, soil stabilization, bank building, and temperature moderation. However, the amount removed is expected to be small. Removal of disturbance from repeated old alignment repairs should enhance vegetation density and stability between alignments and between the old alignment and river base-flow channel. Disturbed riparian area along the new alignment is expected to be smaller than the area enhanced. Vegetation disposal into the riparian area between alignments would provide beneficial large woody debris, currently lacking in the [[Blue River]] system.

- **Toxic Substances**: Potential exists for petroleum products or other toxic substances to enter the river during construction, potentially killing [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], or their food base. Impaired riparian buffer increases this likelihood.

- **Unauthorized Work Effects**: Direct hatchery discharge into the river (bypassing settling pond) would carry sediment, excess nutrients, and other potentially harmful materials directly into the river. Magnitude of these discharges is unknown but assumed low to moderate.

**Indirect Effects**: Road improvement may encourage increased use, bringing increased impacts to streambanks and riparian vegetation, increased water demand, increased pollution, and other adverse effects to aquatic and riparian habitats.

### Cumulative Effects

Most land within the [[Blue River]] watershed is under [[U.S. Forest Service]] administration; activities affecting listed species are subject to section 7 consultation. Primary cumulative effects originate from private lands in the valley bottom: livestock grazing, cropping, residential development (removing water, adding to instability), and aquaculture operations (feeding predatory nonnatives into the river, diverting water, adding nutrient load). The [[American Fisheries Society]] (1991) position statement notes that accumulation of localized or small impacts from unrelated human actions poses serious threats to fisheries. Some habitat improvement efforts may only mitigate cumulative habitat alterations rather than result in cumulative increases in species status. This realignment only partially mitigates damage from the existing alignment and past/future accumulating habitat alterations, leaving substantial cumulative impacts requiring amelioration for species recovery.

### Consultation Conclusion

**Non-jeopardy determination** for both [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Implementation of the realignment is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of either species.

### Incidental Take Statement

**Anticipated Take**: Cannot be directly quantified due to low population data and inability to predict long-term effects. Take is indexed to total fish community and habitat. Anticipated take is exceeded if:
1. More than 20 dead fish (any species) found in project area or within 500 yards downstream during implementation
2. Machinery/vehicles operated outside 35 feet on both sides of alignment centerlines
3. Any toxic material spill occurs in [[Blue River]] or floodplain during implementation

**Reasonable and Prudent Measures** (4 objectives):
1. Minimize direct mortality of [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]
2. Minimize loss and alteration of habitat
3. Monitor fish community and habitat to document incidental take
4. Maintain complete and accurate records

**Terms and Conditions** (mandatory):
- Minimize activities within wetted channel
- No work requiring surface water entry during spawning season (March 1-June 1, September 1-October 31)
- No water removal from [[Blue River]]
- Prevent pollutants from entering surface waters
- Apply Best Management Practices from June 16, 1997 biological opinion
- Minimize riparian vegetation damage; salvage and replant willow poles
- Minimize channel and floodplain alterations
- Limit [[Bush Creek]] ford construction to 70-foot wide band (35 feet up/downstream from centerline)
- Monitor for dead/dying fish; notify Service immediately if >20 found
- Submit written report within 60 days of completion with photographs, compliance discussion, and monitoring results

### Conservation Recommendations (discretionary)

1. Explore options for additional realignment to remove road from floodplain entirely
2. Conduct aerial photography of FR 281 within [[Blue River]] drainage (Turkey Creek to Raspberry Creek confluence) within six months as baseline for future planning and assessment
3. Allow remaining dike (west lane) on old alignment to erode naturally during floods without reconstruction; harden riverside embankment of new alignment with riprap to prevent erosion as old dike erodes and channel migrates west

### Reinitiation Requirements

Formal consultation must be reinitiated if: (1) anticipated take level is exceeded; (2) new information reveals effects not considered; (3) action is modified causing unconsidered effects; or (4) new species listed or critical habitat designated that may be affected.
