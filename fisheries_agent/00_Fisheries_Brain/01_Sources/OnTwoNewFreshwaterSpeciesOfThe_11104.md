# Two New Freshwater Gammarus Species from North Africa (Crustacea, Amphipoda)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, published in the [[Beaufortia]] miscellaneous series of the University of Amsterdam (1975, Volume 23, No. 301), describes two new freshwater amphipod species of the genus [[Gammarus]] from Morocco. [[Gammarus Rouxi]] was found in a well near Fez alongside [[Gammarus Gauthieri]], and [[Gammarus Microps]] was the only amphipod recorded from a cave near Taza. The authors deliberately limit species descriptions to material where adult status is certain or morphology is clearly diagnostic, reflecting how poorly known the North African amphipod fauna remains.

## Background & Description

The authors note that the freshwater amphipods of northern Africa are poorly documented, and that many samples contain specimens that cannot be matched to existing literature. Prior work by [[Pinkster]] (1971) and [[Stock]] (1974) had already described one new freshwater gammarid from the region. Both new species belong to, or are compared with, the [[Gammarus Pulex Group]], a morphologically complex assemblage in which species separation relies on fine details of appendages and setation.

- **G. rouxi** was discovered during hybridization experiments by [[Goedmakers]] and [[Roux]] (summer 1972). Small adult couples (maximum length 10 mm in males, 6 mm in females) appeared among large G. gauthieri couples in a population assumed to be homogeneous.
- **G. microps** was collected by a British expedition. Its striking features are very long body size (largest male 24 mm; females to 16 mm) and reduced, nearly blind eyes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The two species occupy contrasting freshwater habitats: a surface well with an [[Oued]] (watercourse) setting for G. rouxi, and a subterranean cave (Ikhfou Ouan, about 50 km southwest of Taza) for G. microps. The reduced eyes and pigment loss in G. microps are consistent with a cave-adapted lifestyle, though the document does not analyse this explicitly. No water chemistry, physical parameters or community data are reported.

## Key Findings

**Gammarus rouxi nov. spec.**
- Type locality: oued crossing road P 24, upstream of Imouzzer du Kandar, Fez province, Morocco (16 June 1972); many specimens, many in precopulation.
- Types (holotype male, 2 allotype, about 50 paratypes) deposited in the Zoological Museum Amsterdam (cat. nos. Amph. 105.002 a, b, c).
- Very short antennae (first antenna about 1/4 of body length) give a robust appearance; first antenna flagellum 12 to 15 segments.
- Second antenna flagellum 7 to 8 segments, never swollen or compressed as in G. pulex.
- Seventh leg basal segment is distinctive: wide proximal part with a strongly constricted distal portion, plus long setae on inner surface and posterior margin.
- Telson lobes twice as long as wide; inner ramus of third uropod slightly longer than half the outer ramus.
- Separated from G. acalceolatus and G. ibericus by antennal length, inner-surface setae on legs 6 to 7, leg 7 shape, uropod setation and epimeral plate setation.
- Named in honour of [[Roux]] (University of Lyon), who collected the material.

**Gammarus microps nov. spec.**
- Type locality: Cave Ikhfou Ouan, about 50 km SW of Taza, Taza province, Morocco; 14 specimens in total.
- Holotype and 13 paratypes deposited in the British Museum (Natural History), London (cat. nos. 1974: 912 and 1974: 913).
- Eyes very small, in some specimens almost invisible through lack of pigment.
- First antenna flagellum up to 46 segments in the main flagellum, a diagnostic character.
- Second antenna flagellum 15 to 19 segments, with transverse setal rows on each segment.
- First gnathopod hand with transverse rows of spines on the inner surface, not previously observed in the G. pulex group.
- Urosome has a shallow saddle on segment 1 and no dorsal elevations; lateral spine group absent in segment 3.
- The name *microps* refers to the very small eyes.

**Methodological and taxonomic conclusions**
- Descriptions are restricted to adult or clearly diagnostic material, because developmental stages of known species could be confused with the new taxa.
- The authors state that systematic sampling is needed before North African freshwater amphipod taxonomy is resolved.
- Variability in both species is assumed to resemble that of the G. pulex group, but this is based on very limited material (one sample for G. rouxi; 14 specimens for G. microps).
