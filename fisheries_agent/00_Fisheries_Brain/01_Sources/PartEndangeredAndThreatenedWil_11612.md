# Title 50—Wildlife and Fisheries, Part 17—Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#legal-document #endangered-species-act #conservation-policy #federal-register

## Executive Summary

This document is a comprehensive compilation of Title 50, Code of Federal Regulations, Part 17, Subpart B, which contains the official lists of endangered and threatened wildlife (§17.11) and plants (§17.12) under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Published as a special reprint current to the date shown on the cover, it provides the regulatory framework for species classification, listing procedures, and the legal definitions that govern federal protection of imperiled species. The document includes extensive tables listing hundreds of species across taxonomic groups—mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, snails, clams, crustaceans, insects, and arachnids for wildlife; and numerous plant families—with their scientific names, common names, historic ranges, conservation status (Endangered or Threatened), Federal Register citation dates, and references to special rules or critical habitat designations. It also includes a supplementary list of species removed from protection due to recovery, extinction, or original listing error.

## Background & Description

The document establishes the regulatory structure for implementing the [[Endangered Species Act]] listing provisions. Section 17.11 addresses wildlife, while §17.12 addresses plants. The lists define species using scientific nomenclature following the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]] for animals and the [[International Code of Botanical Nomenclature]] for plants. Common names are provided for reference but cannot be relied upon for specimen identification due to regional variation.

Status designations use standardized symbols: "E" for Endangered, "T" for Threatened, "E (S/A)" or "T (S/A)" for similarity-of-appearance species, "XE" for essential experimental populations, and "XN" for nonessential experimental populations. The historic range column indicates known general distribution as reported in scientific literature, though present distribution may be greatly reduced. Listing a particular taxon automatically includes all lower taxonomic units (e.g., listing a genus includes all species and subspecies within that genus).

The document clarifies that data on historic range, Federal Register citations, special rules, and critical habitat are informational and nonregulatory, though they reference binding regulations elsewhere in the Code. The "When listed" column provides footnoted citations to Federal Register publications documenting the basis and effective dates of each listing decision. The compilation incorporates all published final rules through the document date, with minor corrections to the October 1, 1988 CFR compilation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listed species represent a cross-section of global biodiversity facing threats from habitat destruction, overutilization, disease, predation, inadequate regulatory mechanisms, and other natural or anthropogenic factors. Geographic coverage is worldwide, though emphasis is on species occurring in or imported to the United States and its territories.

For aquatic species specifically, the fish section includes numerous endemic species from restricted ranges—particularly desert pupfishes ([[Cyprinodon]] spp.) from isolated springs in [[Nevada]], [[California]], and [[Texas]]; cave-adapted species like the [[Alabama Cavefish]] and [[Ozark Cavefish]]; riverine species such as the [[Colorado Squawfish]] and various chubs, darters, and suckers from western and southeastern river systems; and anadromous species including the [[Shortnose Sturgeon]]. Freshwater mollusks (pearly mussels) are heavily represented from southeastern U.S. river systems, particularly the [[Tennessee River]] and [[Cumberland River]] drainages. Marine species include sea turtles (all species listed), marine mammals (whales, seals, manatees, sea otters), and limited marine fish representation.

Habitat contexts range from desert springs and thermal waters to large river systems, coastal estuaries, coral reefs, and open ocean. Many listed aquatic species are indicators of water quality degradation, habitat fragmentation by dams, and hydrological alteration. The document notes that prohibitions apply to all individuals of listed species wherever found, not just within designated critical habitat.

## Key Findings

**Regulatory Framework**: The lists establish legal protection for species determined to be endangered or threatened based on five statutory factors: habitat destruction/modification, overutilization, disease/predation, inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms, or other natural/manmade factors affecting continued existence. Delisting requires demonstrating recovery, extinction, or that original data were in error.

**Taxonomic Coverage**: The wildlife list encompasses 8 major vertebrate classes plus invertebrates. Fish species number over 100 entries, representing diverse families including [[Salmonidae]] (trouts), [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows and chubs), [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), [[Percidae]] (darters), [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers), [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons), and numerous others. Freshwater mussels (Unionidae) constitute a substantial portion of listed mollusks, with over 40 species of pearly mussels from southeastern rivers.

**Geographic Patterns**: Listed aquatic species show concentration in specific regions: southwestern desert springs ([[Ash Meadows]], [[Death Valley]] region); southeastern river systems ([[Tennessee]], [[Alabama]], [[Georgia]]); [[Colorado River]] basin; [[California]] coastal and Central Valley systems; [[Florida]] springs and coastal waters; and [[Hawaii]] for endemic stream species. Marine species have broader distributions but breeding populations may be geographically restricted.

**Special Designations**: Many species have designated critical habitat (referenced in separate sections) or special rules governing experimental populations, particularly for reintroduction programs. The [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Yellowfin Madtom]], and [[Red Wolf]] have experimental population designations in specific drainages or counties. Similarity-of-appearance listings protect look-alike species to aid enforcement.

**Removed Species**: The supplementary list documents species delisted due to recovery ([[Palau Dove]], [[Palau Fantail]], [[Palau Owl]]), extinction ([[Tecopa Pupfish]], [[Longjaw Cisco]], [[Blue Pike]], [[Sampson's Pearly Mussel]], [[Amistad Gambusia]]), or original data error ([[Mexican Duck]], [[Pine Barrens Treefrog]], [[Indian Flap-shelled Turtle]], [[Bahama Swallowtail Butterfly]]).

**Listing Chronology**: Federal Register citations span from 1967 (initial listings under predecessor legislation) through 1988, documenting the evolution of species protection. The numerical footnote system (1-342) provides traceable documentation for each listing action, essential for legal and administrative purposes.

**International Species**: The lists include foreign species (marked with historic ranges outside the U.S.) to regulate import/export and commercial trade, supporting international conservation agreements and preventing market demand from driving foreign species toward extinction.
