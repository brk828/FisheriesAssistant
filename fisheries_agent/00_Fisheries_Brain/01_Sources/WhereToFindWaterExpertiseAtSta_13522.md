# Where to Find Water Expertise at State Universities in Arizona

#water-resources #expertise-directory #hydrology

## Executive Summary

This second revised edition (May 1995) is Number Thirteen in a series of issue papers from the [[Water Resources Research Center]] at the [[University of Arizona]]. It is a directory of 173 people at Arizona's three state universities, [[University of Arizona]], [[Arizona State University]], and [[Northern Arizona University]], who have expertise in water-related fields. Information was gathered by surveying relevant university departments, including [[Cooperative Extension]], and is reported as the respondents provided it. The directory is a reference tool for locating experts, not a research study. It covers hydrology, agricultural economics, flood control, water law, water pollution, aquatic ecology, and many related fields. The directory is organized into four chapters that allow cross-referenced searches by name, general area, keyword, and geography or language.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and series context.** The directory is the third in a series intended to help the public find information about water in Arizona. Volume I, *Where to Get Free (Or Almost Free) Information About Water in Arizona*, guides readers to agencies and organizations. *Where to Get Technical Information About Water in Arizona* guides technical users to data bases and specialized libraries.

**Scope and method.** The directory lists 173 experts. All entries are as reported by respondents and are abbreviated to keep the publication manageable. Entries for each person may include university address and phone number, areas of expertise, geographic specialty, language proficiency, specialized teaching assignments, and the audiences the person will serve (for example, news media, educators, government agencies, or the general public).

**1995 revision.** The edition is completely revised. More than 20 new entries were added, and 15 were removed as people joined or left the university system. A searchable version is available on the World Wide Web through the [[Water Resources Research Center]] site, accessed with browsers such as Mosaic or Netscape.

**Chapter structure:**
- **Chapter One: Alphabetical Listing.** The core reference with full entries for each expert. Categories left blank mean the person did not provide that information.
- **Chapter Two: General Research Areas.** Broad fields such as [[Hydrology]], [[Water Quality]], [[Groundwater]], [[Riparian Ecosystems]], [[Wetlands]], [[Water Law]], and [[Drought]]. Each lists the experts in that field.
- **Chapter Three: Keywords.** Narrow specialties such as acid precipitation, benthos, fisheries, oxidation, subsidence, and wetlands, which narrow a search within a general area.
- **Chapter Four: Geographic Areas and Languages.** Regional expertise (for example, deserts, Middle East, Central America/Mexico) and special language skills (for example, Spanish, French, German, Arabic).

**Example search.** The directory's own example combines a field, two keywords, a region, and a language to find a hydrologist with expertise in water harvesting and irrigation in Middle Eastern deserts who speaks German. The search leads to [[Sol Resnick]], Director Emeritus, who is listed as available to the news media, educators, government agencies, and the general public, and who is not a full-time university employee.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The directory is not a fisheries or ecology study, but its aquatic and riparian expertise is relevant to aquatic ecosystem management in Arizona. Several listed experts cover aquatic and riparian topics. Entries mention aquatic ecosystems, riparian ecosystems, wetlands, instream flow, habitat, and water quality. Examples from the entries:

- **[[Fish]]-related expertise** appears in the Keywords chapter under Fish, which lists [[Thomas Dowling]], [[James Elser]], [[Kevin Fitzsimmons]], [[Nancy Grimm]], [[Paul C. Marsh]], [[O. Eugene Maughan]], [[W. L. Minckley]], and [[W. Linn Montgomery]]. Fisheries appears as a separate keyword with several of the same names plus [[Edella Schlager]].
- **Riparian ecosystems** and **riparian vegetation** are among the most frequently listed specialties. Examples include [[Dean W. Blinn]], [[Robert D. Ohmart]], [[Duncan Patten]], and [[Julie Stromberg]].
- **Aquatic and water quality chemistry** appears in entries for [[Lawrence Baker]], [[Stuart Fisher]], [[Nancy Grimm]], [[Milton Sommerfeld]], and [[Richard Foust]].
- **Constructed and artificial wetlands** are covered by [[Kennith Foster]], [[Glenn France]], [[Barbara Tellman]], and others.
- **Stream and watershed hydrology** is covered by [[Richard H. Hawkins]], [[Donald J. Baumgartner]], [[Phil Guertin]], [[Robert D. MacNish]], and others.

The entries also show that Arizona's water expertise spans arid-land ecology, desert hydrology, drought and water use, and urban and municipal water systems.

## Key Findings

- **Scale of the resource:** 173 experts at three Arizona state universities are listed, with contact details, areas of expertise, geographic and language specialties, and the audiences each will serve.
- **Update history:** The 1995 edition added more than 20 new entries and removed 15 entries for people who had joined or left the university system.
- **Breadth of expertise:** Chapter Two's general areas include Agriculture, Biology, Chemistry, Climate/Weather, Conservation, Drought, Ecology, Economics/Finance, Education, Floods, Geosciences, Groundwater, Hydrology, Institutions & Policy, Law, Methods, Modeling, Pollution, Recreation, Riparian Areas, Sediments, Soils, Surface Water, Toxic Substances, Wastewater, Water Quality, Water Supply, Water Treatment, Water Use, and Wetlands.
- **Aquatic and fish expertise:** Several experts list fish, fisheries, aquatic ecosystems, and invertebrates (see [[Ecological & Environmental Context]]), which is relevant to aquatic conservation planning in Arizona.
- **Audience focus:** Experts indicate whether they will serve consulting firms, educators, the general public, government agencies, groups seeking speakers, or the news media. Some, such as [[Robert Glennon]] and [[Donald Davis]], are listed only for certain audiences.
- **Geographic and language reach:** Chapter Four shows regional specialties ranging from Arizona and the Southwest to Africa, South America, the Middle East, and Southeast Asia. Languages range from Spanish and French to Arabic, Hindi, Farsi, and Ilokano.
- **Search method:** Users find experts by moving from a general area (Chapter Two) to a keyword (Chapter Three), then cross-checking geography or language (Chapter Four), and then reading the full entry in Chapter One.
- **Digital access:** The directory is searchable on the [[Water Resources Research Center]] website through a database interface, which is described as easy to use and able to search all collected data.
- **Related directories:** The series also includes *Where to Get Free (Or Almost Free) Information About Water in Arizona* and *Where to Get Technical Information About Water in Arizona*.
