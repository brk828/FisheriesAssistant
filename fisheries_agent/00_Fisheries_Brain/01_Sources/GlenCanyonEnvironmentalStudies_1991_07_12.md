## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific research paper**, but rather a set of administrative memoranda, meeting agendas, and mailing lists from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program (Bureau of Reclamation, Upper Colorado Region), dated 1991-1992. The materials organize a coordination meeting among researchers studying the aquatic ecology of [[Lake Powell]] and [[Lake Mead]], in support of an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) evaluating operational changes to [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. ## Key Findings

- The GCES identified a need to consolidate and review **past scientific research** on Lake Powell and Lake Mead to inform the [[Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement]], indicating that pre-1991 ecological data on these reservoirs was fragmented across multiple agencies and researchers.
- A formal **Reservoir Ecology Meeting** (August 13-14, 1991) was convened to:
  1. Review prior scientific studies on both lakes.
  2. Assess existing databases and long-term monitoring programs.
  3. Identify data gaps relative to EIS alternatives.
  4. Develop a prioritized plan for further scientific analysis.
- A **follow-up memo (July 1992)** from the Aquatic Coordination Team flagged two specific unresolved action items:
  1. Determining whether [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] had established protocols for repeated handling of endangered/threatened fish and its physiological effects.
  2. Locating documentation from a prior USFWS-commissioned study (referenced by Dr. Wendell Minckley) evaluating **exotic and native fish species** in the Colorado River and Lake Mead, needed to complete an **Exotic Fish literature review**.
- These findings collectively indicate that, as of the early 1990s, addressing the impacts of dam operations on native and endangered fish species required significant additional data synthesis, and that exotic species interactions were recognized as an important but insufficiently documented ecological concern in the [[Colorado River Basin]].
