# Harvest of Razorback Suckers from Lakeside Rearing Ponds, Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum from Tom Burke to the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) reports on the summer 1995 monitoring of juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] rearing in lakeside backwater ponds around [[Lake Mohave]]. It sets a harvest schedule for October and November, when the lake drops below elevation 638 feet, and describes a field decision to consolidate fish into [[Yuma Cove]] after poor survival and water-quality problems in other ponds. The memo also asks staff to help with the harvest.

## Background & Description

- **Subject species:** Juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]], stocked as larvae and reared in backwater ponds, and [[Bonytail Chub]], which were also stocked in several ponds.
- **Origin of the program:** The fish are reared in [[Lakeside Rearing Ponds]] managed under the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] Lower Colorado Regional Office in Boulder City, Nevada.
- **Pond system:** The memo lists 11 backwater ponds. Four contain Bonytail Chub (marked with asterisks in the original): [[Arizona Juvenile Pond]] (North Arizona Juvenile), Nevada EGG, North 9-Mile, and [[Mesquite Cove]]. The other ponds include [[South Sidewinder]], [[Willow Cove]], [[Dandy Cove]], Nevada Larvae, [[North Chemehuevi]], and [[Yuma Cove]].
- **Hydrologic control:** Lake Mohave stays full (above elevation 639 feet) through September, which extends the growing period. Harvest is scheduled for when the lake falls below elevation 638 feet.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water level management:** Delaying harvest until October and November gives the fish extra growing time. The lake level then drops and the ponds can be drained or accessed for harvest.
- **Pond conditions:** Some backwaters are ephemeral. Water quality in South Sidewinder was described as poor in mid-July 1995, which led staff to move fish.
- **Habitat quality:** Yuma Cove was noted as having a large expanse of good habitat, which was the reason for consolidating fish there.
- **Survival concerns:** In the previous year, Yuma Cove survival was only 12 percent of almost 3,000 larvae stocked. Also in the previous year, Arizona Juvenile and North Arizona Juvenile suffered total kills. In 1995 none of the backwaters had a total kill.

## Key Findings

**1. Harvest schedule**
- The planned harvest is in October and November, after the lake drops below elevation 638 feet.
- The memo gives harvest weeks of October 23, October 30, and November 20. The table linking specific ponds to specific weeks is garbled in the scan, so the pond-to-week assignment cannot be confirmed from this text.

**2. Summer 1995 monitoring results**
- Yuma Cove again had the poorest survival of the ponds.
- No backwater suffered a total kill as occurred the previous year.
- All four ponds that received Bonytail Chub reported some success.

**3. Yuma Cove sampling and consolidation**
- On July 7, Yuma Cove had more than 5,000 larvae stocked in spring, but no fish were caught in five overnight trap sets.
- During the same period, fish were trapped successfully in South Sidewinder, North Chemehuevi, Willow Cove, and Nevada Larvae.
- On July 13, Yuma Cove was trapped again and only one fish was caught.
- Field staff then moved finger-size fish from ephemeral backwaters into Yuma Cove.
- For the rest of the summer, 544 Razorback Suckers and 162 Bonytail Chub were transferred into Yuma Cove. Razorbacks were given different fin clips to identify their source pond (see [[Fin Clipping]]).
- On September 1, Yuma Cove sampling caught 15 razorbacks and one bonytail. The razorbacks came from five of the six ponds that were sampled.

**4. Growth data**
- The memo compares total lengths at the start of each month for juvenile razorbacks. The data are for 1995 (Yuma Cove, Arizona Juvenile, Dandy Cove, North Chemehuevi, and Willow Cove) and for Yuma Cove in 1992, which the memo describes as an excellent growth year.
- Selected July values (mm): Yuma Cove 1992 = 175; Yuma Cove 1995 = 130; Arizona Juvenile 1995 = 183; Dandy Cove 1995 = 168; North Chemehuevi 1995 = 162; Willow Cove 1995 = 180.
- September values: Yuma Cove 1992 = 260; Yuma Cove 1995 = 268; Arizona Juvenile 1995 = 248.
- Some August values are unclear in the scan and are not reported here.
- Based on 1995 data, the memo estimates that over 90 percent of fish should exceed 250 mm by the October and November harvest. The memo gives this as an expectation rather than a measured result.

**5. Action items**
- Staff are asked to take part in the pond harvest and to help with camping and logistics.
- Members must tell Burke how many people will attend and what equipment they can bring. He will follow up by October 1.
