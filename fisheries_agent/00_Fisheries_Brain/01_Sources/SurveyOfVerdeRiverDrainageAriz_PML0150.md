# Survey of Verde River Drainage, Arizona, for Loach Minnow (Tiaroga cobitis)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This final report, submitted by Brian Bagley to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] under federal contract #22410-0-M525, describes a fisheries survey of Verde River tributaries conducted from August 2000 through July 2002. The survey targeted [[Loach Minnow]], a federally threatened cyprinid last recorded from the Verde River system in 1938. No loach minnow were found. The survey did document a new population of [[Gila Chub]] in Wet Bottom Creek and a reestablished [[Gila Trout]] population at Dude Creek, and it recorded 23 fish species (8 native, 15 non-native). A cover letter dated November 23, 2002 identifies three streams with potential for native fish reintroduction.

## Background & Description

Loach minnow is endemic to the Gila River Basin of New Mexico, Arizona and Sonora, Mexico. It was listed as threatened in 1986, and it is now restricted to about a dozen remaining populations. Its decline is attributed to habitat alteration and competition and predation by non-native fishes.

The species is a small, stream-dwelling minnow reaching standard lengths of about 68 mm. It has an elongated, slightly compressed body that is flattened ventrally, a small terminal oblique mouth, and an olivaceous body that is blotched with darker pigment. Breeding males show bright red-orange coloration on the pelvic, pectoral and caudal fins and the mouth. Preferred habitat is shallow, turbulent riffles with gravel-to-cobble substrates in small to moderate creeks and rivers. The author also observed loach minnow persisting for extended periods in pools, such as at Pace Creek, New Mexico.

The [[Verde River]] drains approximately 16,900 square kilometers (the OCR-garbled unit is most likely km²). Its headwaters rise at about 2,135 m (7,000 ft), and its mainstem flows southeast for more than 301 km (187 mi) before joining the [[Salt River]]. The basin supports 12 native fish species, including [[Roundtail Chub]], Gila chub, [[Headwater Chub]], spikedace, [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], Sonora sucker, desert sucker, and reestablished populations of Gila trout, Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker and [[Gila Topminnow]]. Twenty-eight non-native species have been recorded, including rainbow, brown and brook trout, red shiner, catfishes, black bass, sunfishes, crappies and Mozambique tilapia.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Historical loach minnow records.** Loach minnow has been collected at only three Verde River locations: the mainstem near [[Beaver Creek]], Beaver Creek itself (collected in 1933), and a tributary near Chino. All three sites have been degraded. Beaver Creek was treated with fish poison in 1962 and is now surrounded by houses and agriculture, with a fish community dominated by non-native species. Tributaries near Chino have been invaded and have lost aquatic habitat.

**Detectability.** The author notes that the species is easily overlooked. In Eagle Creek, loach minnow was absent from collections for about 40 years after the 1950s, then was found again in 1994 in only a small reach. The North Fork of East Fork Black River population was not detected until 1996 despite frequent visits. The author therefore considers it possible that loach minnow persists in the Verde drainage.

**Non-native fishes.** Green sunfish were the most widespread non-native, recorded at 28 locations and most abundant in lower-elevation tributaries. Brown and rainbow trout were common at higher elevations, stocked since as early as 1942 and still augmented by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The author observed that wherever water appeared suitable for fishes, non-native species were present, which complicates native reintroductions.

**Disturbance.** The Dude Creek fire of 1990 eliminated non-native trout, and the stream remained fishless for eight years before Gila trout were stocked. The report also records drying of many reaches, cattle use, and flood-prone canyons.

## Key Findings

**Survey scope and effort**
- The contract called for 57 locations on 36 streams. Collections were made at **130 locations on 44 streams**.
- **23 fish species** were captured: **8 native** and **15 non-native**.
- **No loach minnow** were encountered.
- Sampling used [[Electrofishing]] (backpack shocker), seines, dip nets, kick seines and gill nets. Young-of-year and older fish were counted separately. Voucher specimens were deposited at the [[Arizona State University Museum]].

**Key discoveries**
- **New Gila chub population in Wet Bottom Creek.** Fish were present but uncommon. Five chubs were taken to Arizona State University for genetic study. Gila chub were also confirmed at Walker Creek, Spring Creek and Williamson Valley Wash. Prior records placed Gila chub at 5 Verde basin locations (Weedman et al. 1996).
- **Gila trout at Dude Creek.** Fish ranged from 10 to 25 cm and occupied only part of the creek in autumn 2000.
- **Roundtail chub** were collected from Deadman Creek, South Fork Deadman Creek, Weber Spring and West Clear Creek.

**Native species distribution**
- [[Longfin Dace]] was the most common native, recorded at **29 locations**.
- [[Speckled Dace]] was recorded at **20 locations**.
- Sonora sucker and desert sucker were locally abundant.
- Reestablished [[Gila Topminnow]], stocked in 1982, still maintained large numbers at Walnut Spring and [[Lime Creek]].

**Non-native species distribution**
- [[Green Sunfish]] was the most common non-native, recorded at **28 locations**.
- Other common non-natives included red shiner, fathead minnow, yellow bullhead, mosquitofish and smallmouth bass.
- Common carp, flathead catfish, channel catfish, black bullhead, largemouth bass, bluegill and rockbass were infrequent. The author suspects these are underrepresented because they favor deep pools that were not fully surveyed.

**Management recommendations (cover letter, November 23, 2002)**

The author visited more than 130 locations and identified three streams suitable for native fish reintroduction:
1. **Upper Lime Creek**: already holds longfin dace and has natural fish barriers and no non-natives. It is recommended for topminnow, and a strong topminnow population already exists in Lower Lime Creek. The habitat also suits a chub, especially near Little Cougar Canyon, where pools exceed 2 m deep.
2. **[[Horse Creek]]**: historically supported longfin dace and a topminnow population that persisted about 10 years. These were likely eliminated by flooding in the early 1990s. Topminnow could be restocked and periodically augmented after major floods.
3. **[[Apache Creek]]**: supports a large population of speckled dace. Pools in the middle reach appear suitable for a chub.

**Conclusions**
- The author concludes that loach minnow may still persist in the Verde drainage, so continued monitoring and survey of additional reaches is warranted.
- The Verde system is described as a cornerstone for Arizona's native fishes. The report states that protecting portions of the drainage from human and non-native impacts is essential for their future.
