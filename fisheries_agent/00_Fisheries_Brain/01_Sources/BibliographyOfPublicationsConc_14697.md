# Bibliography of Publications Concerning Tilapia mossambica (Peters)

#ichthyology #aquaculture #Cichlidae #tilapia #exotic-species

## Executive Summary

This administrative report presents a comprehensive bibliography of 213 selected references on [[Tilapia mossambica]] (Mozambique Tilapia), compiled by the California Department of Fish and Game, Region 5, Inland Fisheries in January 1967. The bibliography encompasses all significant papers known to the authors dealing with this cichlid species, including research on its biology, culture methods, ecological impacts, physiology, and global introductions. Sources were drawn from multiple institutional libraries including the Bio-Medical Library of UCLA, Crookshank Hall Library of Pomona College, Chino Fisheries Base Library, and the Resources Agency Library in Sacramento.

## Background & Description

[[Tilapia mossambica]] (Peters) is a cichlid fish species (Family [[Cichlidae]]) native to Africa that has been widely introduced throughout tropical and subtropical regions worldwide. The species exhibits mouthbrooding behavior, with males incubating eggs and fry in their mouths. The bibliography covers literature from multiple countries and regions where the species has been studied or cultured, including Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Philippines, Taiwan, India, East Africa, Southern Rhodesia, South Africa, Hawaii, Florida, Texas, Arizona, California, and various Pacific islands.

The species is notable for its adaptability to diverse aquatic environments including freshwater ponds, brackish water, rice paddies, and heated waters. References document its use in aquaculture, as live bait for tuna fishing, for aquatic weed control, and its role in malaria control through mosquito larvae consumption.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography documents [[Tilapia mossambica]] across multiple ecological contexts:

**Habitat Range**: The species demonstrates high temperature tolerance and euryhaline capabilities, surviving in both freshwater and brackish water environments. Studies document culture in ponds, rice paddies, irrigation systems, natural lakes (including [[Lake Victoria]], [[Lake Nyasa]]), rivers, and experimental facilities.

**Geographic Distribution**: Native to African waters, the species has been introduced throughout the Indo-Pacific region, Southeast Asia, Central and South America, and North America. References document introductions in Taiwan (1950s), Hawaii, Florida, Texas, Arizona, California, and numerous other locations.

**Ecological Roles**: The species serves multiple functions including aquaculture production, weed control in ponds, mosquito control for malaria prevention, live bait for skipjack tuna fishing, and as forage in polyculture systems with carp and other species.

**Environmental Concerns**: Several references address control and eradication efforts in brackish-water fishponds and concerns about competition with indigenous species, indicating recognition of potential invasive impacts.

## Key Findings

**Biological Research**: The bibliography includes extensive coverage of:
- Reproductive biology, mouthbrooding behavior, and parental care (Aronson 1956, Shaw 1956, Gill 1907)
- High temperature tolerance studies (Allanson and Nobel 1964)
- Growth rates and age determination (Koura and El-Boloch 1958, Le Roux 1961)
- Feeding habits and digestive physiology (Fish 1960, Negase 1964, Le Roux 1956)
- Fecundity studies (Lowe 1955c, Peters 1963)

**Aquaculture Applications**: Major themes include:
- Pond culture techniques and stocking densities across multiple countries
- Fertilizer trials and nutritional studies (Anonymous 1960a, Charpy 1958)
- Monosex culture methods (Kong 1962)
- Polyculture with carp and other species (Sarig 1955, Yashouv 1958)
- Culture in rice paddies (Chen 1953, Hofstede and Ardinawata 1950)
- Production in heated waters and thermal effluents

**Hybridization Studies**: Multiple references document interspecific hybridization experiments:
- Malacca tilapia hybrids (Hickling 1960, McConnell 1966a)
- Hybrid vigor and production enhancement (Bard 1960, Mortimer 1960)
- Interspecific crosses between various [[Tilapia]] species (Trewavas and Greenwood 1960, Whitehead 1960)

**Physiological Research**: The bibliography includes studies on:
- Osmoregulation and endocrine function (Reid and Ego 1962, Nandi and Bern 1960, 1963)
- Neurosecretory systems (Takasugi and Bern 1962, Yagi and Bern 1963, 1965)
- Radionuclide uptake and metabolism (Boroughs et al. 1956a, 1956b, 1957; Townsley 1963)
- Thyroid function and metabolism (Madanmohanrao 1961)
- Corticosteroid production (Bern 1963)

**Behavioral Studies**: References cover social behavior, color changes, sound production, breeding patterns, and ethological observations (Neil 1964, 1966; Rodman 1966; Baerends and Baerends-Van Roon 1950).

**Management Applications**:
- Use as live bait for tuna fishing (Hida et al. 1962, Shomura 1964, King and Wilson 1957)
- Aquatic weed control (Swingle 1957, Lahser 1967)
- Malaria control through mosquito larvae consumption (Hofstede and Botke 1950, Hora 1952b)
- Sport fishing potential (McConnell 1966a, Boles 1966)

**Geographic Introductions**: The bibliography documents introductions and establishment in:
- North America: California (St. Amant 1966a, 1966b), Arizona (McConnell 1965, 1966a), Texas (Brown 1961), Florida (Crittenden 1962)
- Asia: Taiwan, Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia, India, Ceylon, Malaya
- Pacific: Hawaii, Nauru, New Guinea
- Africa: widespread native and introduced populations
- Central/South America: Nicaragua (Riedel 1965), Venezuela, Haiti, Dominican Republic

**Technical Methods**: References include:
- Air transport techniques for live fish (Nemoto 1957, Vaas 1952)
- Marking experiments (Hickling 1959)
- Tank culture methods (Uchida and King 1962)
- Food assessment and acceptance studies (Harper 1960)

The bibliography represents a comprehensive compilation of scientific knowledge on [[Tilapia mossambica]] through the mid-1960s, covering basic biology, applied aquaculture, physiology, behavior, and management across its native and introduced range.
