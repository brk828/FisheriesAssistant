# Cui-ui and Lahontan Cutthroat Trout Spawning Runs at Marble Bluff Fish Facility — 1987

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #salmonidae #endangered-species #fish-passage

## Executive Summary

This report documents the 1987 spawning runs of the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) and threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*) at [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], [[Nixon, Nevada]]. The cui-ui spawning run totaled 5,301 individuals, representing 45 percent of the average run since 1980. The run began in mid-April and continued sporadically until mid-May. [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] numbered 371 individuals (after adjusting for 33 percent re-entry rate), approximately one-quarter the size of the 1986 run. Releases from [[Stampede Reservoir]] totaled 85,470 acre-feet to support attraction, spawning, incubation, and emigration flows. Larvae production was lower than expected, with only 1.4 percent (3.4 million larvae) of estimated eggs surviving to the larval stage. Facility mortality was exceptionally low: 16 cui-ui (0.3 percent) and one cutthroat trout (0.2 percent) died accidentally.

## Background & Description

The [[Cui-ui]] is an endemic sucker species found only in [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, listed as endangered. The [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] is a threatened subspecies native to the [[Lahontan Basin]]. Both species undertake spawning migrations up the [[Truckee River]], where passage is facilitated by the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]]. This facility includes a river trap at the base of [[Marble Bluff Dam]], passage infrastructure, and holding pens. The [[David Koch Cui-ui Hatchery]] receives adult cui-ui for artificial propagation.

**Cui-ui Morphometrics (1987):**
- Average male: 529 mm FL
- Average female: 577 mm FL
- Sex ratio: 1 male to 1.91 females
- Adults (>485 mm FL): sex ratio 1.00:2.10
- Young fish (<485 mm FL): sex ratio 1.00:0.49

**Lahontan Cutthroat Trout Morphometrics (1987):**
- Average male: 598 mm FL (23 mm larger than 1986)
- Average female: 508 mm FL (24 mm smaller than 1986)
- Sex ratio: 1 male to 1.8 females

The 1969 year class of cui-ui continued to dominate the spawning run, though several younger year classes were present. Length frequency distributions showed nearly the same relative abundance as 1986, with slight increases in average size (males +1 mm, females +11 mm).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrological Management:**
River access and freshwater inflow volume are the principal factors influencing cui-ui spawning run magnitude and timing. Water temperature may secondarily affect timing. The relatively small 1987 run size was attributed to:
- Low attraction flows (60,000 acre-feet) during January-April, within the estimated minimum range (52,000-87,000 acre-feet)
- Restricted passage over the river delta (water depth 1.5 feet shallower than required for unobstructed passage)
- [[Pyramid Lake]] elevation: 3,814.7 feet above m.s.l.

**Stampede Reservoir Releases:**
Despite 1987 being a drought year (46 percent of normal runoff), storage releases from [[Stampede Reservoir]] were critical for spawning success. Releases began February 19 and totaled 85,470 acre-feet, leaving 92,992 acre-feet in storage. The release strategy aimed to:
- Provide minimum attraction flows (January-April)
- Support spawning and incubation flows (May-June: 1,000 cfs)
- Facilitate larvae emigration
- Maintain water temperatures below 63°F during incubation

**Temperature Regime:**
Maximum water temperature during incubation reached 20°C, higher than the planned 17°C due to extraordinarily warm air temperatures. This likely contributed to:
- Lower-than-expected larvae survival (1.4 percent observed vs. 8.5 percent predicted)
- Rapid embryo development, with emigration beginning ~20 days after first adult passage (vs. 46 days in 1986)
- Preliminary results from the [[Seattle National Fishery Research Center]] confirmed 17°C as the upper limit for suitable incubation temperatures

**Habitat Conditions:**
The [[Truckee River]] delta was flooded, creating a bay that concentrated freshwater and potentially influenced adult maturation. Water depth over the delta (1.5 feet) was suboptimal, possibly causing some cui-ui to abandon or delay spawning runs. Many captured fish exhibited bird-inflicted wounds, indicating predation pressure during migration.

## Key Findings

**Cui-ui Spawning Run Characteristics:**
- Total run size: 5,301 individuals (45 percent of average since 1980)
- Run timing: mid-April to mid-May, without discernible peak
- Daily catches: ranged from a few to several hundred individuals
- Tagged fish recaptures: 98 individuals (2 percent of run) carried tags from 1982-1986 releases
- Spawning frequency: 88 percent of recaptured males and 79 percent of females had entered at least one other spawning run since 1982; 56 percent of males and 44 percent of females were also in the 1986 run
- Multiple spawning events: 16 percent of males and 3 percent of females had entered four spawning runs (including 1987) since 1982

**Larvae Production:**
- Estimated larvae recruitment: 3.4 million (1.4 percent of estimated eggs spawned)
- Survey period: May 2 to June 10, sampling every fifth day from 10 p.m. to 1 a.m.
- Sampling method: Two plankton nets (37 cm × 37 cm mouth, 150 cm long funnel, 1 mm mesh) with flow meters
- Peak emigration: ~250,000 larvae per day
- Emigration timing: began ~20 days after first adult passage, nearly complete by June 10
- Lower survival than predicted (8.5 percent expected at 20°C; 14 percent expected at 17°C)

**Population Estimates:**
Tag release/recapture data (adjusted for 7 percent annual tag shedding and 20-25 percent annual mortality) suggest the adult cui-ui population is probably less than 100,000 individuals. During 1983-1986, 54 percent of tagged females and 62 percent of tagged males entered at least one spawning run, while total run size was only 59,096 individuals.

**Lahontan Cutthroat Trout:**
- Adjusted run size: 371 individuals
- Run timing: April 1 to June 3
- Micro-tagged fish: 28 percent (vs. 49 percent in 1986)
- Hatchery contribution: 12 of 19 sacrificed tagged fish were [[Independence Strain]] from [[Lahontan National Fish Hatchery]], stocked in 1982-1983

**Facility Operations:**
- Exceptionally low mortality: 16 cui-ui (0.3 percent), 1 cutthroat trout (0.2 percent)
- Additional scientific sacrifices: 5 cui-ui, 19 cutthroat trout
- Larvae collected for survey: 2,903 (stored at [[Great Basin Complex]], Reno)
- Tribal hatchery contribution: 503 adult cui-ui transferred to [[David Koch Cui-ui Hatchery]]
- No cutthroat trout eggs taken due to immature fish and warm water conditions
- Equipment improvements (new crowder lip/sides, table/chute, bar grill in by-pass ladder) functioned as designed
- Personnel safety greatly improved by new walkway and river trap release mechanism

**Management Implications:**
The study demonstrates the critical importance of [[Stampede Reservoir]] storage releases for cui-ui spawning success. Without these releases, several spawning runs since 1980 would not have occurred or would have had much lower reproductive success. The need to refine temperature/survival relationships is evident, particularly accounting for above-normal air temperatures. The curvilinear relationship between attraction flow and spawning run size suggests that greater attraction flows would likely produce larger runs, even within drought conditions.
