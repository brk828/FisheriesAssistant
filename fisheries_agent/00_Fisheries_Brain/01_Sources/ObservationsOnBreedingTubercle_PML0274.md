# Nuptial Tubercles in Xyrauchen texanus and Berchemia scandens Locality Records

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This short two-part note contains (1) a description of the distribution and form of breeding tubercles in a single male [[Xyrauchen Texanus]] (Abbott) specimen, which suggests a closer relationship to [[Catostomus]] than to [[Chasmistes]], and (2) a brief locality note on the disjunct distribution of the woody vine [[Berchemia Scandens]] in [[Chiapas]]. The fish note is the aquatic component; the plant note is terrestrial and falls outside the aquatic scope.

## Background & Description

**Xyrauchen texanus (Razorback sucker)**, a catostomid fish, was the subject of an earlier observation by [[Branson]] (1961), who suggested that nuptial tubercle arrangement in *Xyrauchen* was probably similar to that in *Catostomus* or *Chasmistes*. On 9 September 1962, the first author collected a tuberculated male *Xyrauchen* measuring 393 mm standard length from the [[Green River]] at the Gates of Lodore Ranger Station, [[Dinosaur National Monument]], Moffat County, Colorado. The authors are Branson A. Brown (Eastern Kentucky State College, Richmond) and Clarence L. McCoy, Jr. (Carnegie Museum, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania).

**Berchemia scandens** (Hill) K. Koch (Rhamnaceae) is a climbing shrub. The document is a locality note on its geography, which shows a disjunction between Texas and Chiapas. Its occurrence in Chiapas was first reported by Miranda & Sharp (1950, *Ecology*) and was later repeated by Brizicky (1963, *Journal of the Arnold Arboretum*), based on references supplied by J. Paal C. Marsh.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish specimen came from a large river system in the Colorado Plateau region. The note does not report water chemistry, habitat conditions, population size, or reproductive timing. The tubercles are sexually dimorphic breeding structures, so their presence is relevant to the spawning season, though the document does not state this directly.

The plant record concerns biogeographic distribution. It is not an aquatic or ecosystem observation.

## Key Findings

**1. Distribution of nuptial tubercles in the male *Xyrauchen* specimen**

- Tubercles are restricted to the **anal fin** and to the **lower lobe of the caudal fin**.
- The head, body, and other fins are devoid of nuptial tubercles, apart from a few contact organs near the insertion of the anal fin.
- **Anal fin:**
  - The proximal tubercles of the first 3 anal rays range from 1.0 to 3.0 mm in size.
  - These are followed by elongate, ridge-like bodies 9.0–18.0 mm long and 1.5 mm wide.
  - Distal to the ridges, and on the remaining rays, are simple conical tubercles 1.0–4.0 mm in diameter.
- **Caudal fin:** Tubercles are restricted to the rays of the lower lobe, arranged in rows (the count is partly illegible in the scan, roughly 17 rows). Rows are double on the branched rays.

**2. Taxonomic inference**

- The authors state that this arrangement suggests a closer relationship between *Xyrauchen* and *Catostomus* than between *Xyrauchen* and *Chasmistes*.
- In *Chasmistes*, the body and head are heavily tuberculated, unlike *Xyrauchen*.
- The arrangement in *Xyrauchen* is essentially like that of some *Catostomus* species, such as *C. mocrehellus* (cited from Branson, 1961).

**3. Locality record for Berchemia scandens**

- The species shows a geographic disjunction between Texas and Chiapas.
- The Chiapas record was first reported in 1950 and reconfirmed in 1963 by the cited authors.
