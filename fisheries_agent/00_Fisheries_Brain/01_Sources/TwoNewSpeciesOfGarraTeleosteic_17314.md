# Two New Species of Garra (Teleostei: Cyprinidae) from the Arabian Peninsula

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by K. E. Banister describes two new cyprinid species of the genus [[Garra]] from the Arabian Peninsula. [[Garra Dunsirei]] is a microphthalmic, hypogean fish from an isolated sink hole in the [[Jabal Qara]] mountains of [[Dhofar]], Oman. [[Garra Lautior]] is a streamlined species from the [[Wadi Hadramut]] drainage of Yemen, previously held as "Garra: incertae sedis" by [[Krupp 1983]]. The paper also corrects specimen register numbers and scale counts in Krupp's revision, and argues that morphometric and meristic characters are too variable to support a dichotomous identification key for the region. With these additions, eight species of Garra are recognised from the Arabian Peninsula.

## Background & Description

The paper is a formal species description published in the *Bulletin of the British Museum (Natural History) Zoology* (52(1): 59–70, 1987), issued 29 January 1987 and accepted for publication 6 November 1985. Continued speleological exploration in Oman, reflected in earlier work on [[Garra Barreimiae]] (a blind cave population described by Banister in 1984), prompted the search for further subterranean fishes. The new species were compared against the revision of [[Krupp 1983]], which served as the regional reference.

### Garra dunsirei sp. nov.

- **Holotype:** BMNH 1984.3.6: 571, 68 mm SL, Tawi Atair (17°06'N, 54°34'E), Jabal Qara (also Jabal Samhan), Dhofar, Oman.
- **Paratypes:** BMNH 1984.3.6: 572–576, 34–49 mm SL.
- **Material:** The first four specimens (BMNH 1984.3.6: 577–580) were collected on 16 May 1980 by A. Dunsire and D. Green but were too poor for description. A further 19 live specimens were collected on 26 February 1983 by D. Maclelland; 13 were still alive in July 1985.
- **Description basis:** Holotype and five paratypes (34–68 mm SL), radiographed; two additional specimens cleared and stained with alizarin.
- **Key morphology:**
  - Eyes very small (eye diameter mean 3.3% SL, range 2.8–3.7%), not visible in ventral view.
  - Mental disc approximately circular; papillae poorly developed, with the papillar bed only just differentiated at 50 mm SL.
  - No snout tubercles in any specimen, though a 39.5 mm SL specimen was a gravid female.
  - Dorsal fin with 3 unbranched and 7 or 8 branched rays; anal fin with 3 unbranched and 5 branched rays.
  - Six or seven gill rakers on the lower limb of the first gill arch.
  - Pharyngeal teeth 2.4.5–5.4.2, with the posterior teeth of the innermost row thin and hooked.
  - Neural spines below the dorsal fin sharply bent backward, almost parallel to the centrum axis, a condition unique among regional Garra.
- **Colouration:** Preserved fish pale yellowish grey. Living fish dirty white, with cheeks and operculum reflecting greenish-gold.
- **Etymology:** Named for collector Andy Dunsire, who encouraged subterranean fish searches.

### Garra lautior sp. nov.

- **Holotype:** BMNH 1976.4.7: 398, 74 mm SL, Qasam area, Wadi Hadramut, Yemen (collector King-Webster).
- **Paratypes:** 12 specimens, 61–80 mm SL, from several Wadi Hadramut localities (including Al-Ghurf and Gheil Umar). Krupp (1983) had described six specimens; a [[British Museum (Natural History)]] search added 13 more.
- **Key morphology:**
  - Streamlined body with a dorsal profile that rises smoothly from the pointed snout, and a slender caudal peduncle about half as deep as long.
  - Eye diameter mean 4.8% SL; mouth width mean 5.7% SL.
  - Thick upper lip with many small papillae.
  - Scales well developed and slightly lobate; 12 scale rows around the caudal peduncle.
  - Vertebrae 27–30 excluding the Weberian apparatus; abdominal vertebrae 12 or 13.
  - Gill rakers 11–17 on the lower limb of the first gill arch, small and hooked.
  - Three interhaemal spine spaces correspond to the anal fin pterygiophores, unlike the four in the other regional species examined.
- **Colouration:** Preserved fish uniform sandy brown, darker dorsally, with a deeper brown post-opercular spot.
- **Etymology:** From Latin *lautus* ("neat"), alluding to the streamlined appearance.
- **Diagnosis:** Sympatric only with [[Garra Mamshuqua]]. It lacks snout tubercles and the conspicuous dorsal and post-opercular "Garra" marks of that species, and has a different mental disc shape.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Garra dunsirei** lives in a pool in a side passage about 200 m down the Tawi Atair sink hole. The surface drainage runs south to the [[Arabian Sea]]. The fish swim slowly but continually, usually head-down, and stay near the bottom, rarely approaching the surface even to feed. This contrasts with the rapid, surface-swarming behaviour of the blind [[Garra Barreimiae]] population. The fish show no preference for light or dark conditions, although a strong beam causes a brief startle response, after which they investigate illuminated patches. The author notes that food reaches the cave only during annual rains, and that the eleven growth rings on the holotype's scales may reflect annual spawning marks or food-driven growth changes; this remains unknown.

The paper stresses the isolation of *G. dunsirei*. No primary freshwater fishes have been recorded from this part of Dhofar. The nearest natural populations are about 400 miles away at Tarim in the Wadi Hadramut and about 450 miles northeast in the Omani Jabal Akhdar. Krupp (1983) attributed the internal drainage pattern to the ancient Jabal Qara range. Wetter periods may have allowed northward dispersal into a lake system once occupying the site of the [[Rub al Khali]]. However, whether northern fishes could reach the southern streams is unknown, so the hydrological affinities of the sink hole and the phylogenetic relationships of the species could not be determined.

**Garra lautior** is known only from the Wadi Hadramut drainage, Yemen. The sympatric localities are described by the collector as "a clear stream with stony shallows and deep holes" (Gheil Umar) and an "isolated muddy pot-hole below a dam" (Al-Ghurf).

## Key Findings

1. **Two new species described.** *Garra dunsirei* (hypogean, microphthalmic, Jabal Qara, Oman) and *Garra lautior* (Wadi Hadramut, Yemen) bring the number of known Arabian Peninsula Garra species to eight.

2. **Garra dunsirei is distinguished** by late papillar bed development (differentiated only at 50 mm SL), very small eyes (mean 3.3% SL), and uniquely backward-bent neural spines below the dorsal fin.

3. **Garra lautior is distinguished** by a streamlined body, three rather than four interhaemal spine spaces opposite the anal fin pterygiophores, and absence of snout tubercles.

4. **Tubercles as a diagnostic character.** Snout tubercles are present in both sexes of *G. mamshuqua* at all maturity stages (first appearing at 27 mm SL) and absent in *G. lautior*. This makes tubercles a sound diagnostic character for these sympatric species, though the author does not extend this to other species. The author also finds that the sympatric field data do not support Reid's (1978) hydrodynamic hypothesis for tubercle function in *Labeo*.

5. **Isolation of the cave fish.** The Jabal Qara sink hole fish lie roughly 400–450 miles from their nearest known relatives, and their origin remains unresolved.

6. **Scale count discrepancy.** Banister counted 12 scale rows around the caudal peduncle in *G. lautior*, whereas Krupp gave 14 or 16. A similar discrepancy appears for *G. buettikeri* (Krupp: 18 or 20; Banister: 16 or 18). The author judges this meristic feature unreliable for diagnosis.

7. **No identification key.** Overlap in morphometric and meristic ranges is too large for a reliable key, even when the species are first grouped by drainage. The author considers body shape, mental disc shape, papillae distribution and colour pattern the most useful characters, while noting that variation in [[Garra Tibanica]] may limit disc-based identification.

8. **Records requiring correction.** The Wadi Hadramut record of [[Garra Tibanica]] rests on Scortecci specimens from Bir el Manzil, whose coordinates Banister places well west of the Hadramut. The four *G. sahilia* specimens from Sayun and Shibam do not match the description (barbels and anus position differ), though their poor condition prevents firm conclusions. A list of register-number and locality corrections to Krupp (1983) is provided.

9. **Specimen material.** The paper draws on BMNH collections, including specimens from collectors such as Dunsire, Green, Maclelland and King-Webster, and Krupp's own material. Banister states that the 1983 revision included more specimens of *G. lautior* than Krupp had examined, and that his 13 specimens included 6 used by Krupp.
