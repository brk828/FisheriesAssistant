# Remarks about population structure of some wader species caught in Vendee, Atlantic coast of France

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Charadriiformes #shorebirds #wader-ecology #estuarine-ecology

> **Note:** This source concerns migratory shorebirds (waders) utilizing intertidal/estuarine habitats rather than fish taxa. Synthesized here per the paper's actual scope: avian population structure, migratory phenology, and morphometric race discrimination in a coastal wetland ecosystem.

## Executive Summary

Between May 1964 and May 1967, the Laboratoire des Vertébrés (I.N.R.A.) captured 3,667 individual waders representing 25 species in the [[Bay of the Aiguillon]], Vendée, on the Atlantic coast of France, using mist-netting and clap-netting techniques. The study focuses on four dominant species — [[Dunlin]] (*Calidris alpina*, n=1,590), [[Red Knot]] (*Calidris canutus*, n=843), [[Grey Plover]] (*Charadrius squatarola*, n=330), and [[Common Redshank]] (*Tringa totanus*, n=284) — plus two godwit species of special interest, [[Black-Tailed Godwit]] (*Limosa limosa*) and [[Bar-Tailed Godwit]] (*Limosa lapponica*). Using morphometric analysis (wing-length and bill-length distributions), the author attempts to disentangle sub-populations/geographic races (e.g., Icelandic, British, North Scandinavian, Siberian) using the intertidal mudflats as a shared wintering and staging ground, and examines seasonal patterns of juvenile vs. adult passage as indicators of breeding success and differential migration routes.

## Background & Description

Morphometric (biometric) analysis was the primary tool used to discriminate between geographically distinct breeding populations converging on the same wintering/staging grounds — analogous to stock discrimination methods in fisheries science.

- **Bill-length (Black-tailed Godwit, *Limosa limosa*):** Distribution of bill-length in 12 specimens trapped August–February matched known measurements for *L. l. islandica* (Icelandic race), not the continental race. A confirmed female specimen had a bill-length of 93 mm, supporting the Icelandic-origin hypothesis for wintering birds in West France.
- **Wing-length (Redshank, *Tringa totanus*):** Wing-length distributions varied seasonally and were used to distinguish local breeding birds from British/North European and Icelandic populations (see [[Ecological Niche]] below for seasonal modal shifts).
- **Wing/bill diagrams (Knot, *Calidris canutus*):** Combined wing-length and bill-length measurements identified three distinct adult morphological types:
  1. Long wing / medium bill
  2. Short wing / short bill
  3. Long wing / long bill
  Juveniles were consistently smaller than adults for any given sampling period, complicating — but not precluding — assignment to the same three putative source populations.
- **Wing/bill diagrams (Dunlin, *Calidris alpina*):** Strong sexual dimorphism complicated analysis. A distinct "Siberian II" population was defined based on the largest-bodied birds, contrasted against Icelandic, European, North Scandinavian, and "Siberian I" morphotypes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Bay of the Aiguillon]] functions as a critical intertidal stopover, wintering, and staging habitat along the East Atlantic Flyway, hosting sequential and overlapping waves of migratory wader populations from distinct breeding regions (Iceland, Britain, North Scandinavia, Siberia, Greenland/Arctic Canada).

- **Grey Plover (*Charadrius squatarola*):** Juveniles pass through France principally in October, but proportions vary strongly by year (>50% of October 1964 sample vs. only 20% in October 1965), suggesting inter-annual variation in breeding success. Winter juvenile proportions were also inconsistent (30% in January 1965 vs. 0% in January 1966 and 1967), leading the author to propose that juveniles may follow migration routes distinct from adults.
- **Redshank (*Tringa totanus*):** Temporal segregation of populations observed via wing-length:
  - **April–May:** Homogeneous sample matching British/North European adult breeders.
  - **June–July:** Bimodal distribution — local breeding birds (mode 1) and larger, likely British, birds (mode 2).
  - **December–January:** Trimodal distribution — local population, British/North European population (most numerous), and larger-bodied Icelandic population.
- **Knot (*Calidris canutus*):** Distinct temporal arrival patterns by morphotype:
  - Long-wing/medium-bill adults present in low numbers Aug–Sept, dominant in wintering samples, reappearing March–April — hypothesized as **Siberian breeding population**.
  - Short-wing/short-bill adults pass through in high numbers in October only — hypothesized origin **northwest (Greenland/Arctic Canada)**.
  - Long-wing/long-bill adults appear only in May.
  - Juveniles of the third type may pass in September; juveniles of the second type may pass in March (inferred from smaller juvenile size).
- **Dunlin (*Calidris alpina*):** Complex seasonal turnover of racial composition:
  - **July:** Numerous adults matching Icelandic population.
  - **Aug–Sept:** Scarce adults matching European/North Scandinavian populations.
  - **October:** Large influx, mostly North Scandinavian with some "Siberian II."
  - **Dec–Feb (wintering):** Near-equal mix of European, North Scandinavian, and "Siberian II" populations.
  - **March–April:** Return of European, North Scandinavian, and "Siberian I" populations.
  - **May:** Predominantly Icelandic birds, with some North Scandinavian and "Siberian I" individuals.

## Key Findings

- The [[Bay of the Aiguillon]] mudflats serve as a shared, temporally partitioned stopover/wintering site for multiple genetically/geographically distinct wader populations, detectable via biometric (wing/bill) analysis rather than direct genetic or ringing recovery data.
- [[Grey Plover]] juvenile:adult ratios show strong inter-annual variability, potentially reflecting fluctuating Arctic breeding success or juvenile-specific migratory routing distinct from adults.
- Wintering [[Black-Tailed Godwit]] in western France are most consistent with the Icelandic race (*L. l. islandica*) rather than the continental race, based on bill-length distributions and a confirmed 93 mm-billed female specimen.
- [[Common Redshank]] populations using the bay show clear seasonal succession from local breeders (spring/summer) to mixed British/North European and Icelandic contingents (winter), detectable through shifting wing-length modality (unimodal → bimodal → trimodal).
- [[Red Knot]] adults segregate into three discrete morphotypes (by wing/bill combination) tied to distinct arrival windows, suggestively linked to separate breeding origins (Siberia vs. Greenland/Arctic Canada), though the paper notes substantial uncertainty remains.
- [[Dunlin]] population structure is the most complex of the species studied, exhibiting high sexual dimorphism and rotating dominance among Icelandic, European, North Scandinavian, and two proposed "Siberian" (I and II) morphotypes across the annual cycle.
- The study demonstrates the utility of wing/bill morphometric diagrams as a proxy for identifying cryptic population structure and migratory connectivity in mixed-stock shorebird assemblages using a shared intertidal habitat.
