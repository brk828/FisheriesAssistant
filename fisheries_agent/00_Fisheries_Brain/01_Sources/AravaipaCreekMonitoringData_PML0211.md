# Aravaipa Creek Native Fish Monitoring, February–June 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

Interagency memoranda from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), with input from [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC) and the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), document fish and habitat monitoring at nine stations along [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona, from February to June 1993. Winter floods had reshaped the channel, so the February effort was cut short, and the March and May–June surveys were conducted under recovering conditions. The data show a native-dominated assemblage, with [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) the most abundant species at most sites. Several native species were breeding. No exotic species were recorded at the nine stations in March, although [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]] were collected nearby. A September 1993 letter announced a planned October survey to map the distribution of [[Red Shiner]] and other nonnative fishes.

## Background & Description

Aravaipa Creek flows through Graham and Pinal counties and forms part of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] (WA). The monitoring design divided the creek into three zones:

- **East end, upstream of the WA:** [[Turkey Creek]], [[Chimney Rock]], and the TNC guest house.
- **Within the WA:** [[Hell Hole]], [[Horse Camp]], and [[Painted Cave]].
- **West end, downstream of the WA:** [[Wagner Ranch]], [[White's Ranch]], and the [[Sycamore Tree Site]].

Fish were sampled by seining mainstream channels over 200 m with a 1/8-inch mesh 6-foot seine, and with a 1/4-inch mesh 15-foot seine when needed. Margins and side channels were sampled with dipnets. Backpack electroshockers (150–250 V, 75–150 pulses per second) were used in the WA, and shocker data represent the lower 100 m of each reach. Results are reported as [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPUE) per 10 square metres for seining and per minute of shocking time. Habitat was described by unit type (run, riffle, pool), length, width, depth, and estimated substrate composition, following the [[Habitat Assessment]] protocol.

Collectors were drawn from AGFD, BLM, TNC, and [[Arizona State University]] (ASU). Lead staff included Rob Bettaso, Dave Gori, and Jeff Simms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flood disturbance.** Heavy rains in January and February 1993 caused several floods that scoured the channel. Reported effects included:

- At Chimney Rock, a 100 m band was scoured and over 1 m of fine sediment deposited.
- At the guest house reach, most riparian vegetation was lost to scouring and to sediment plugs more than 2 m deep, which were already incising.
- The WA parking lot area incised about 2 m.
- At White's Ranch the channel shifted and cut into a terrace.
- The old sycamore stump was washed about 200 m downstream.

By June, riparian and aquatic vegetation was described as more abundant, and woody debris and root wads still provided much of the cover. See [[Flood Disturbance]].

**Water conditions.** Water was very turbid in February and March, with visibility nil in February and at Chimney Rock in July. Temperatures were:

- February: 9.5–15 °C (memo range 12–16 °C during sampling).
- March: 11–21 °C.
- June: 17–28 °C, with slight turbidity.
- July: 18–28 °C, with moderate turbidity.

**Land use and wildlife.** Heavy to moderate cattle use was noted at east and west end sites in June. Road construction above Chimney Rock was reported in July and was suspected to have reduced catches at the guest house and Chimney Rock. Black hawks, coati and javelina tracks, and canyon treefrogs were observed.

## Key Findings

**Sampling effort and effectiveness**

- The February 16–17 sampling was aborted after only three of nine sites were worked, because flows, velocity, and turbidity posed a safety threat. Seines were usable only along the margins. The effort was to be rescheduled for lower flows.
- Seine hauls were often empty, most dramatically at the WA and west end sites in March (see below).

**February 1993 (partial)**

- Station 2 (Chimney Rock): 189 fish caught. 44 of 95 seine hauls (46.3%) were empty. Habitat was four runs (65.4%) and riffles (33.2%), with pools only 1%. Flow was 8.26 cms.
- Station 3 (Guest House): 264 fish caught. 45 of 105 hauls (42.9%) were empty. Flow was 10.6 cms.
- White's Ranch: 75 fish caught along the margin. 2 of 15 hauls (13.3%) were empty. Flow was 47.4 cms.
- Sycamore Tree: only partially sampled, with 83 longfin dace, 4 desert sucker, 3 Sonoran sucker, 3 [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and 1 [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) taken. 3 of 11 hauls (27.3%) were empty.
- Breeding colours were seen on about 11 *Tiaroga cobitis*, and some *Pantosteus clarki* were running ripe.

**March 1993 (full sampling of all nine stations)**

- East end: Turkey Creek 348 fish (16 of 47 hauls empty, 34.0%); Chimney Rock 323 fish (22 of 67, 32.8%); Guest House 350 fish (31 of 66, 47.0%).
- Within the WA: Hell Hole 203 fish (20 of 39, 51.3%); Horse Camp 109 fish (22 of 50, 44.0%); Painted Cave 65 fish (76 of 95, 80.0%).
- West end: Wagner Ranch 96 fish (59 of 73, 80.8%); White's Ranch 255 fish (88 of 107, 82.2%); Sycamore Tree 424 fish (reported as 27 of 58 hauls empty, given as 6.6%; this percentage does not match 27/58 and may be a transcription error).
- Breeding was observed in *Tiaroga cobitis*, *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Pantosteus clarki*, *Catostomus insignis*, *Gila robusta*, and *Meda fulgida*. Several large *Pantosteus* were running ripe.
- Native species made up a greater share of catches at lower sites than on previous trips. No exotic species were recorded at the nine stations. Green sunfish and yellow bullhead were collected by A. Velasco near the Virgus Canyon confluence.

**May–June 1993**

- Sampling was performed from 19 to 21 May, with a second summary issued on 30 June. Reported totals include 348 fish at Turkey Creek and 1534 at Chimney Rock, the latter of which needs verification. Seine-haul empty rates ranged from about 13% to 37% at most east end and WA sites.
- Breeding was observed in *Gila robusta*, *Agosia chrysogaster*, *Pantosteus clarki*, *Meda fulgida*, and *Tiaroga cobitis*. Several large *Pantosteus* and *Gila* were running ripe.
- Two additional records of an uncertain taxon were noted at Sycamore Tree and Wagner Ranch. Their identification is illegible in the OCR text.

**July 1993 (June data)**

- The June 1993 sampling produced 512 fish at Turkey Creek, 537 at Chimney Rock (0 of 31 hauls empty), and 452 at the Guest House. West end totals were 583 at Wagner Ranch, 1019 at White's Ranch, and 2329 at Sycamore Tree (9 of 104 hauls empty, 8.7%).
- Breeding conditions were observed in *Gila robusta*, with one large individual running ripe.
- Over one hundred small red-spotted toads were noted at Sycamore Tree.

**Planned exotic fish survey (September 1993)**

- AGFD, BLM, and TNC planned an extensive survey on 23–24 October 1993. The goal was to assess the abundance and distribution of nonnative fishes, particularly [[Red Shiner]], from the headwaters to the [[San Pedro River]] confluence.
- The stated purpose was to guide long-term protection and management of Aravaipa's seven native fish species.
- The survey required at least 35 people. Agency staff numbered 12, so at least 23 more volunteers were needed. Each team of four was to cover about four miles of creek.
- Volunteers were to rendezvous at Wes Car Ranch on 22 October. Interested participants were asked to contact Rob Bettaso by 8 October 1993.
