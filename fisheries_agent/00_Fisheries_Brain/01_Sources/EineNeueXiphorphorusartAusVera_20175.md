# Xiphophorus andersi n. sp. from the Rio Atoyac, Vera Cruz, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, written in German with an English abstract, describes **[[Xiphophorus Andersi]]**, a new poeciliid species from the [[Rio Atoyac]] in [[Vera Cruz]], Mexico. The species is distinguished by a large head, a moderately slender body, a large dark spot at the base of the anal fin, and a short sword-like caudal extension in adult males. Its gonopodium lacks a claw on ray 5a. The authors report that the species combines characters of both the [[Platyfish]] and [[Swordtail]] species groups, so it does not fit cleanly into either. The species is known only from the type locality and is presumed to be endemic with a very small range.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Xiphophorus]] (described by Heckel, 1848) is among the best-studied poeciliid genera. It has been used in morphology, physiology, ethology, zoogeography, genetics and cancer research. The genus was revised by [[Rosen 1960]], which was incorporated into the family-level revision by [[Rosen & Bailey 1963]]. Since then, two taxa have been added: *X. couchianus gordoni* (Miller & Minckley 1963) and *X. helleri signum* (Rosen & Kallman 1969). The present paper adds a further species.

**Holotype and material**
- Holotype: male, SMF 15118, collected 24 February 1979 by E. Hnizicka at the Rio Atoyac near Finca St. Anita, Chico.
- Paratypes: two females (SMF 15119–20) and one juvenile (SMF 15121), all from the same locality.
- Etymology: named after Prof. Dr. F. Anders, Justus-Liebig-Universität Gießen, for his work on *Xiphophorus* as a model for the origin of cancer.

**Morphometrics (holotype, mm as given)**
- Total length (TL): 40.5; standard length (SL): 33.6
- Body depth (KH): 9.75; head length (KL): 9.30; caudal peduncle depth (StH): 6.20
- Eye diameter (Ag): 3.00; sword length (SchwL): 4.75
- Paratypes span TL 38.0–45.9 and SL 30.8–38.6.

**Meristics (diagnosis)**
- Dorsal fin rays: 12; caudal: 31–32; anal: 10; ventral: 6–7; pectoral: 13
- Lateral line scales: 27–28; transverse scales: 9
- Vertebrae: 29

**Morphology**
- Large head, moderately elongated body with a long caudal peduncle, and a rhombic dorsal fin.
- Adult males have the lower caudal rays (three ventral rays) extended into a short sword.
- Gonopodium: ray 5a has no claw; 7–9 well-developed distal teeth on ray 4p; the ramus of ray 4a is curved downward and rounded; ray 3 has a long, slender hook and 7–8 spines.

**Colouration**
- Olive-brown ground colour with a brown midline stripe formed by a reticulate pattern of small melanophores.
- Lower third of the dorsal fin is pigmented; other fins are colourless.
- A large dark spot in front of the anal fin, described as a "puberty spot" that becomes visible in mature fish through the translucent body wall.
- No sexual colour dimorphism was reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat:** The Rio Atoyac is a fast-flowing river about 6 m wide at the type locality, with a depth of roughly 2 m outside seasonal fluctuation. The water is brownish and turbid. The riparian vegetation includes hard-leaved shrubs, banana plants, yucca palms and grassy patches.

**Associated fishes:** *Heterandria bimaculata*, *Xiphophorus helleri*, *X. variatus variatus*, and several Characinoidae species.

**Distribution:** The species appears to be restricted to the type locality, which the authors interpret as a narrow endemic range.

**Relationships (as argued by the authors):**
- Distinguished from *X. helleri*, *X. clemenciae*, *X. pygmaeus*, *X. montezumae*, and *X. milleri* by the absence of a claw on ray 5a, which varies in the other species, and by the rounded ramus shape (versus *X. helleri* and *X. clemenciae*).
- Most similar in gonopodium structure and counts to [[Xiphophorus Variatus]] and *X. variatus evelynae*. It differs in body shape, rhombic dorsal fin and colouration, which the authors compare to *X. milleri*.
- Its caudal extension approaches the length found in *X. montezumae cortezi*.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Xiphophorus andersi* n. sp. is a valid new species of Poeciliidae from the Rio Atoyac, Vera Cruz, based on one male holotype, two female paratypes and one juvenile.
- **Diagnostic characters:** large head; moderately slender body; large dark anal-fin-base spot; short sword in adult males formed by three ventral caudal rays; gonopodial ray 5a lacks a claw.
- **Intermediate placement:** The species combines features of the swordtail group (sword, fin shape, elongated body) with features of the platy group (gonopodium structure, large head). The authors therefore place it between the *X. helleri*/*X. clemenciae* group and the *X. couchianus*/*X. maculatus*/*X. variatus* group, with links to the *X. milleri*/*X. montezumae*/*X. pygmaeus* group.
- **Systematic implication:** The authors argue that the traditional split of the genus into platy and swordtail groups, based on caudal extensions, does not hold for this species.
- **Conservation-relevant status:** Presumed endemism to one small site suggests a very restricted range, although no conservation assessment is provided in the paper.
