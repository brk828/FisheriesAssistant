## Key Findings

- **No fish or aquatic species data exists in this source.** This note is provided for completeness per instructions, but the paper is a geological petrology study with zero relevance to ichthyology or marine/aquatic biology.
- Identification of a previously undescribed **subvolcanic granite (granophyre)** north of Empalme, Sonora, intrusive into Tertiary potassic volcanics of the [[Sierra La Ventana]].
- Field relationships show transitional contacts between coarse-grained (grenu) and fine-grained (microgrenu) facies, interpreted as evidence of a genetic volcano-plutonic association rather than a recrystallized welded tuff (as previously proposed by earlier authors).
- Geochemical comparison (major oxide table) shows the granophyre has strong compositional affinity with the **Cerro El Vigía ignimbrite** and **Sierra La Ventana ignimbrite**, but differs markedly from the Laramide-age **Rancho El Bayo granodiorite** in Al₂O₃, MgO, CaO, and K₂O content.
- The granophyre is interpreted as the **first recognized volcano-plutonic complex** in this part of Mexico, belonging to a mid-to-late Cenozoic magmatic sequence contemporaneous with post-Miocene extension and the opening of the Gulf of California.
- This finding contributes to regional tectonic models correlating Sonoran magmatism with the broader **Basin and Range** extensional regime of northwestern Mexico and the southwestern United States.
