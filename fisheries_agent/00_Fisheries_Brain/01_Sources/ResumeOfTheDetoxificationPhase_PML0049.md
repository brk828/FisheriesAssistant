# Green River Rotenone Detoxification Phase Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes the detoxification phase of the [[Green River]] fish rehabilitation project. A [[Detoxification Station]] neutralized [[Rotenone]] with [[Potassium Permanganate]] (KMnO4) to protect native fish below the station. Operations were based on information presented at the 1963 session of the Technical Committee of the [[Colorado River Management Committee]]. The station operated for 82 hours, and sampling later found all native species present within [[Dinosaur National Monument]] except [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), which was uncommon and hard to collect. The author judged that KMnO4 can effectively detoxify rotenone in a fish rehabilitation program, and recommended a faster, more accurate field test for rotenone. The document is a general summary; a detailed report was expected by the Western Association meeting in June.

## Background & Description

The project was a cooperative effort. [[Utah]] and [[Wyoming]] worked with the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] to prepare the detailed rehabilitation report. The summary was prepared by [[Donald Andriano]].

Rotenone was the piscicide applied to the river. Its decay and detoxification were estimated from prior field experience and limited controlled experiments at the hydraulic laboratory of [[Utah State University]]. Key planning figures were:

- **Design flow:** The station was designed to handle up to 1,400 cubic feet per second (cfs) of river flow.
- **Location:** The station was about 35 miles below the last toxicant introduction station and about 25 miles below the dam. The last toxicant station was approximately 30 miles above the dam.
- **Expected decay:** Rotenone was expected to decay at 1 ppm per 16 miles of stream.
- **Initial detoxification assumption:** About 1 ppm of commercial rotenone could be detoxified with 2.3 ppm KMnO4.
- **Design duration:** Under minimum flow (1,400 cfs) and 1 ppm rotenone, the station would need to run 24 hours. This required about 827 pounds of KMnO4 per hour at 2.3 ppm.
- **Supply:** 14,000 pounds of KMnO4 were on site, enough for 24 hours at an average of 0.7 ppm rotenone passing the station.

The dispensing system used sheet-metal containers mounted on a bridge, with gravity-fed openings. A small electric motor rotated a disc beneath each opening to spread the chemical over a 50 to 75 foot diameter area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Monitoring used three methods:

- **Chemical checks:** Rotenone was measured above the station, and KMnO4 was checked by colorimetric analysis (Post method).
- **Bioassays:** Bioassays were run by the [[Public Health Service]] above and below the station.
- **Caged fish:** Live boxes with [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Green Sunfish]], and other fish were placed about one and two miles below the station.

Water conditions changed during the operation. A drop in water temperature and an unexpected, undetected decrease in river flow at least partly explained why the station had to run longer and handle higher rotenone concentrations than planned. The authors also suggested the rotenone decay rate may have been overestimated.

## Key Findings

- **Operation duration:** The station ran for 82 hours, much longer than the 24 hours originally planned.
- **Dosing:** KMnO4 was dispersed at 1.5 to 3 ppm per 1 ppm of rotenone. The ratio was reduced to conserve chemical, but the authors concluded results were satisfactory as long as the rotenone-to-KMnO4 ratio stayed at or above 1:1.7.
- **Supply:** Only 14,000 pounds of KMnO4 were on site. An additional 3,000 pounds were rushed to the project area.
- **Primary objective:** The authors stated the main goal, preventing loss of indigenous fish below the station, was achieved while the station was operating.
- **Post-closure incident:** After the station shut down, U.S. [[National Park Service]] personnel reported dead or distressed fish in Dinosaur National Monument. Some fish probably drifted from upstream, but limited rotenone may also have passed the station after the KMnO4 was used up.
- **Post-treatment sampling:** Sampling within Dinosaur National Monument found all native species present except Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*). This species was not common in the drainage and is difficult to collect, and the authors expected it to be found with more intensive sampling.
- **Taxonomic note:** Some fishery biologists questioned whether *Gila cypha* is separate from the [[Colorado River Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), because the size of the "hump" varies.
- **Conclusion:** The authors considered KMnO4 a chemical that could effectively detoxify rotenone in fish rehabilitation programs.
- **Recommendation:** A quicker and more accurate field test for rotenone was recommended to improve program success.
