## Executive Summary

This document is a **program status and funding needs report** (dated July 17, 1995) from the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] Native Fish Species Recovery/Conservation Program, later feeding into the **Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program (LCR MSCP)** Biology Subcommittee agenda (August 1996). It is not a morphological/taxonomic study but a management document tracking recovery efforts for two critically imperiled Colorado River endemics: the **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (RBS, *Xyrauchen texanus*) and the **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (BTC, *Gila elegans*). The overarching goal is to **prevent extinction** of both species in **[[Lake Mohave]]** and **[[Lake Havasu]]** through captive rearing, wild larval/fry collection, ephemeral pond rearing, and stocking programs, with associated budget shortfalls identified for each program component.

## Background & Description

Limited direct morphological description is provided; data are restricted to **life-stage size classes** used to track growth and survival through the rearing pipeline:

- **Larvae/swim-up fry**: collected at hatch, ~12mm at capture from Lake Mohave
- **Feeding fry**: grown to ~20–30mm in hatchery/lab conditions
- **Juveniles**: grown to ~100–125mm (WBNFH growout benchmark)
- **Subadults**: 250mm+ total length (TL) — threshold for release into ephemeral ponds or Davis Cove
- **Stocking-size adults/subadults**: minimum 300mm TL — target size for release into [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]] to bolster reproductive populations

No external morphological, meristic, or coloration data are reported. Size classes primarily reflect **hatchery/rearing benchmarks** rather than taxonomic description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use**: Both species occupy **reservoir and backwater habitats** within the regulated [[Lower Colorado River]] system, specifically [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]].
- **Reproductive habitat manipulation**: 
  - **[[Yuma Cove]]** — berm fortified (1991) and used for stocking sexually mature RBS adults (1991–1992), resulting in reproduction and limited recruitment of ~300mm fish by 1992.
  - **[[Davis Cove]]** — blocked off (1992) as a nursery/rearing area for undersized fish (<250mm).
  - **Ephemeral backwaters/ponds** — created and expanded (1993–1995, reaching seven operational ponds by 1995) to mimic natural nursery conditions for larval rearing, capturing wild genetic diversity.
- **Genetic conservation strategy**: Program specifically emphasizes **capturing wild genetic variability** from remaining Lake Mohave razorback sucker population via wild-caught fry rather than relying solely on captive broodstock, given the population consists largely of **aging, likely non-reproducing wild adults**.
- **Recruitment bottleneck**: Wild recruitment in the reservoir environment is minimal due to predation on larvae/small juveniles by non-native predators (implied context of backwater/ephemeral pond strategy), necessitating human-assisted rearing to bridge the survival gap to 250–300mm.
- **Multi-facility rearing network**: [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH), Boulder City Golf Course ponds (BR/NDOW/BC cooperative), [[Lake Mead Fish Hatchery]] (NDOW-LMH), and Dexter National Fish Hatchery (captive BTC broodstock) collectively support the rearing pipeline from larvae to stockable subadults.

## Key Findings

**Razorback Sucker (Objective 1)**
- Goal: Replace aging Lake Mohave population with 50,000 adults by 2000; increase Lake Havasu population to 25,000 adults.
- 1995: 20,146 swim-up fry collected — 10,800 sent to ephemeral ponds, 7,730 to WBNFH.
- Subadult (250mm+) stocking into Lake Mohave increased sharply: 129 (1992) → 542 (1993) → 2,189 (1994).
- Anticipated annual stocking under existing regime: only **2,500–5,000 subadults/year** — insufficient pace relative to 50,000-adult goal.
- 1996 larval collection goal: minimum 40,000 wild larvae to WBNFH; projected **net production ~10,000 fish at 300mm** from 40,000 larvae collected (25% survival to stocking size).
- 1995 cohort: 20,000 larvae collected → projected net production **~4,500 fish at 250mm** (~22.5% survival), with 1,500 mortality documented at WBNFH stage.

**Bonytail Chub (Objective 2)**
- Goal: Increase Lakes Mohave and Havasu populations to 25,000 each by 2005.
- ~160,000 fingerlings (5–150mm) stocked into Lake Mohave from 1981–1993 (peak year 1981: 41,500; 1990: 43,500), but wild recruitment remains low.
- Lake Havasu population status **unknown** as of 1995.
- FWS committed (1996) to stocking 25,000 (250–300mm) BTC into Lake Mohave and 6,000 subadults into Lake Havasu.

**Program Bottlenecks Identified**
1. Difficulty rearing RBS subadults beyond 250mm under existing facilities/strategy.
2. Insufficient rearing facilities/personnel to produce >5,000 RBS subadults (300mm) annually.
3. Lack of adequate wild larvae collection capacity (only ~20,000/year vs. 40,000–60,000 goal).
4. Absence of regular (quarterly) population/genetic monitoring — only annual surveys funded.
