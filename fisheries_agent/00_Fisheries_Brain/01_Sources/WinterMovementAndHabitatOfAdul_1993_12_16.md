## Executive Summary

This report, prepared for the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program]] FY93 Annual Report, synthesizes winter movement and habitat use data for adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in the Green River downstream of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], with comparative data from the [[Yampa River]]. The study integrates radiotelemetry findings with a broader literature review of winter fish ecology in riverine systems affected by flow regulation, ice dynamics, and water temperature. The ultimate goal is to inform recommendations for winter flow releases from Flaming Gorge Dam that support the recovery of these federally endangered species, particularly by addressing poor overwinter survival of age-0 Colorado squawfish, a life stage identified as critical to recruitment failure in the Upper Colorado River Basin.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphological measurements (e.g., length, weight, meristic counts) for either species. The focus is behavioral and habitat-based, relying on radiotagged adult individuals of both [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. No age-0 morphological data are given, though age-0 Colorado squawfish are referenced as a monitored life stage via the Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program ([[ISMP]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat Use — Green River (below Flaming Gorge Dam)
- Adult radiotagged fish primarily used **slow runs and slackwaters**.
- Depth range: **0.6 to 1.4 m**.
- Average velocity: **0.03 to 0.4 m/sec**.
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] used **backwaters under ice cover**, likely to forage on abundant minnows.
- [[Razorback Sucker]] remained primarily in **main channel habitats** during winter, avoiding backwater/ice-associated zones.

### Habitat Use — Yampa River
- Fish used **backwaters, slow runs, and embayments** during high-flow periods.
- Fish shifted to **pools, runs, and embayments** during low-flow periods.
- Most frequently used depth: **0.6 to 1.1 m**.
- Velocity range: **0.0 to 0.2 m/sec**.

### Site Fidelity and Movement
- Both species exhibited strong **fidelity to specific overwintering sites** in both the [[Green River]] and [[Yampa River]].
- Fish moved regularly between **several preferred locations within a given habitat type**, rather than remaining static or dispersing widely.

### Hydrological Context
- Comparative hydrology analysis of the Green River **pre- and post-Flaming Gorge Dam** reveals significant alteration in flow magnitude and variability.
- **Increased winter flow volume** post-dam **delays ice formation**.
- **Daily flow fluctuations** repeatedly break up ice cover, generating **large ice jams** and **thick frazil ice** accumulations.
- These altered ice dynamics **displace fish from preferred habitats** and can **fill/degrade nursery areas** used by young fish.

## Key Findings

1. **Recruitment bottleneck**: Failed recruitment of age-0 [[Colorado Squawfish]] to juvenile/adult stages is a primary driver of the species' endangered status in the Upper Colorado River Basin; the **first overwintering period** is identified as a critical early-life-history bottleneck.
2. **Species-specific winter habitat partitioning**: Colorado squawfish favor ice-covered backwaters (possibly for foraging on minnows), while razorback sucker favor stable main-channel habitats — indicating differing sensitivity to ice and flow disturbance.
3. **Habitat fidelity with micro-scale movement**: Both species show high fidelity to general overwintering areas but move among multiple specific "favorite spots" within those habitat types, in both the Green and Yampa rivers.
4. **Dam-induced ice regime alteration**: Flaming Gorge Dam operations have increased winter flow volumes and daily fluctuations, delaying ice formation but promoting repeated ice breakup, jam formation, and frazil ice — processes that can physically displace fish and damage nursery habitat.
5. **Management implication**: Stability of overwintering habitat (in terms of flow consistency and ice regime) is linked to survival of young Colorado squawfish and overall health of juveniles and adults, forming the basis for proposed **winter flow release recommendations** from Flaming Gorge Dam.
6. **Monitoring integration**: The study recommends continuing age-0 Colorado squawfish spring sampling in the Green River, potentially incorporating it into the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP), and comparing spring cohort strength to prior fall densities to assess overwinter survival relative to flow patterns.
7. **Comparative literature synthesis**: The paper incorporates global riverine literature on winter fish ecology (temperature, ice, flow regulation effects) to contextualize Green/Yampa River findings and support flow management recommendations.
