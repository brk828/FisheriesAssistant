# Identification of Poeciliid Specimens Collected in Fresno Canyon

## Executive Summary
This 1992 correspondence from a U.S. Forest Service Fishery Biologist to the Arizona Game and Fish Department documents the taxonomic identification of Poeciliid specimens collected in Fresno Canyon, Santa Cruz County, Arizona, on 27 January 1992. Of 96 total specimens examined under dissecting microscopy (8-60x power), 88 were identified as [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and 8 as [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). The finding is ecologically notable because it confirms the persistence of a native, imperiled Gila Topminnow population despite co-occurrence with a substantial predator load, including [[Green Sunfish]], a large [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Fathead Minnow]]. Identification relied on Minckley (1973) and supplementary diagnostic characters developed by Dean Hendrickson and Brian Bagley to distinguish the two superficially similar Poeciliid species, particularly given the faintness of diagnostic markings on the mosquitofish specimens.

## Background & Description
Diagnostic differentiation between *Gambusia affinis* (mosquitofish) and *Poeciliopsis occidentalis* (Gila Topminnow) was based on a comparative character key, summarized below:

**Gambusia affinis (Mosquitofish):**
- Caudal and dorsal fin spotting nearly always present
- Darkened "teardrop" mark nearly always present below the eye
- No darkened peritoneum in breeding females
- Mature females: silver-blueish coloration
- Mature males: silver-blueish coloration
- Origin of dorsal fin well posterior to anal fin origin in females
- Caudal fin generally squared in configuration

**Poeciliopsis occidentalis (Gila Topminnow):**
- Caudal and dorsal fin spotting never present; fin rays only outlined with melanophores
- Teardrop mark never present below the eye
- Peritoneum nearly always darkened (black) in breeding females
- Mature females: silver-tannish coloration
- Mature dominant males: black coloration; sub-dominant males silver-tannish
- Origin of dorsal fin nearly in line with origin of anal fin in females
- In males, distal tip of the gonopodium nearly aligns with, or extends beyond, the distal margin of the dorsal fin

Note: Many of these markings, especially in mosquitofish, were described as "quite faint and difficult to discern" without high magnification (8-60x dissecting microscope), underscoring the identification challenge posed by [[Cryptic Species Identification]] in Poeciliids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Location:** Fresno Canyon, Santa Cruz County, Arizona — part of the [[Gila River Drainage]] system, a critical native fish refuge in the southwestern United States.
- **Microhabitat partitioning:**
  - Poeciliids (Gila Topminnow and Mosquitofish) were collected exclusively in shallow glides (<0.8 ft deep) over sand substrate with floating algae — likely a refuge microhabitat.
  - Larger-bodied and predatory species — [[Green Sunfish]] (n=47), [[Largemouth Bass]] (n=1, ~450 mm), and [[Fathead Minnow]] (unspecified number) — were collected in deeper pools (1-3 ft).
- **Predator-prey dynamics:** The co-occurrence of a heavy predator load (notably a large adult Largemouth Bass and abundant Green Sunfish, both known predators of small native fishes) alongside a persisting Gila Topminnow population was noted as ecologically surprising, suggesting that shallow-water microhabitat segregation may serve as a critical survival mechanism for the native [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]] population against introduced predatory and competitive fish species.
- **Conservation significance:** The Gila Topminnow is a federally listed endangered species; discovery of a viable population in Fresno Canyon represents a significant finding for the species' persistence given ongoing pressure from non-native introduced species like *Gambusia affinis*, which is a known competitor and predator of topminnow larvae.

## Key Findings
- 96 total Poeciliid specimens collected on 27 January 1992 in Fresno Canyon; 88 identified as native [[Gila Topminnow]] and 8 as introduced [[Western Mosquitofish]].
- Identification employed Minckley (1973) taxonomic keys supplemented by additional diagnostic characters (Hendrickson & Bagley) targeting fin spotting, teardrop marks, peritoneal pigmentation, sexual coloration, dorsal/anal fin origin position, and gonopodium morphology.
- A surviving Gila Topminnow population persists despite substantial predation pressure from non-native [[Green Sunfish]] (n=47) and a large [[Largemouth Bass]] (~450 mm), plus [[Fathead Minnow]].
- Microhabitat segregation was observed: Poeciliids restricted to shallow (<0.8 ft), sand-substrate glides with floating algae, while predatory/competitor species occupied deeper pools (1-3 ft).
- This finding supports the broader conservation value of maintaining heterogeneous stream microhabitats to allow persistence of imperiled native poeciliid populations alongside introduced predatory fish assemblages.
