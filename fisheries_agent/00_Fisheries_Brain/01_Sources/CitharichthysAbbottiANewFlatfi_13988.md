# Citharichthys abbotti: A New Flatfish (Bothidae) from the Southwestern Gulf of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bothidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 taxonomic paper by C. E. Dawson of the [[Gulf Coast Research Laboratory]] (Ocean Springs, Mississippi), published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (vol. 82, pp. 355-372), describes [[Citharichthys Abbotti]] as a new species of [[Bothidae|bothid flatfish]] from coastal waters of [[Veracruz]] and [[Campeche]], Mexico. The species could not be identified with existing keys, and comparisons with its closest western Atlantic relatives, [[Citharichthys Spilopterus]] and [[Citharichthys Arenaceus]], revealed consistent differences in meristic and morphometric characters. The paper also reports the first known case of body reversal (a dextral juvenile) in the genus and critiques the tooth-based diagnostic characters used to separate *Citharichthys* from [[Syacium]].

## Background & Description

The author collected numerous specimens of a flatfish referable to the genus *Citharichthys* Bleeker. Existing revisions and keys ([[Parr 1931]], [[Norman 1934]], [[Gutherz 1967]]) did not allow identification, and the fish's apparent abundance on the Mexican coast raised the risk of confusion with related Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean forms.

**Type material:** The holotype is USNM 203735 (142.6 mm SL), collected 1 September 1967 from an eddy current at the mouth of [[Laguna de Zontecomapan]], Veracruz, over a sand bottom at depths up to 2 m. Paratypes are held in several institutions, including the [[United States National Museum]], the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]], the [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], the [[University of Miami Marine Science Institute]], and the [[Instituto Nacional de Investigaciones Biológico Pesqueras]].

**Diagnosis:** A *Citharichthys* lacking osseous protuberances or spines on the snout or orbital rims. Modal counts are 22 caudal vertebrae, 76 dorsal and 56 anal fin rays, 20 gill rakers on the first arch, and 44-45 lateral line scales. The species has no prominent or distinctive coloration. The first dorsal ray averages 8% of SL, body depth averages 47% of SL, and the upper jaw averages 37% of head length (HL).

**Key ranges (from the description):**
- Dorsal rays: 72-81 (usually 75-78)
- Anal rays: 52-60 (usually 55-56)
- Gill rakers: 18-23 total (usually 20)
- Lateral line scales: 42-49 (usually 44-45)
- Vertebrae: 31-33 (usually 32)
- Body depth: 44-51% of SL, greatest near the 10th or 11th anal ray
- Left pectoral fin: 9-11 rays (usually 10); right: 8-10 (usually 9); pelvic fins: 6 rays; caudal fin: 17 segmented rays

**Coloration (preserved):** The left (ocular) side is light sandy-tan, speckled with small brown blotches, with dark-margined scales and a dark spot at the upper opercular angle. The right (blind) side is mainly cream-white. Juveniles are lighter with fewer blotches.

**Etymology:** The species is named for Dr. Walter Abbott, who helped collect the type series.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution:** Presently known from the Gulf of Mexico shore and estuarine habitats along the Mexican coast, from Boca del Rio, Veracruz, south to Zacatal, Campeche. Specimens came from 0-2 m over substrates ranging from fine sand to dark brown mud. The author suggests it probably also occurs in inshore and estuarine habitats along the Caribbean coast of Mexico and northern Central America.

**Physical conditions:** A salinity of 14.8‰ and water temperature of 30.1 °C were recorded for the 5 June 1968 collection at [[Boca del Rio]] (GCRL 2733).

**Sympatry:** *C. abbotti* is sympatric with *C. spilopterus* along the Mexican Gulf Coast. Sampling sites included beaches, jetty tidepools (Alvarado), river mouths ([[Rio Jamapa]], [[Rio Coatzacoalcos]]), lagoons, and an offshore trawl station at 60 m depth where *C. spilopterus* was collected.

**Related species:** Three shallow-water *Citharichthys* occur in the Gulf of Mexico or Caribbean:
- [[Citharichthys Spilopterus]]: New Jersey to Brazil
- [[Citharichthys Arenaceus]]: West Indies to Brazil
- [[Citharichthys Uhleri]]: known only from the unique holotype from Haiti

## Key Findings

- **New species recognized.** *C. abbotti* is distinct from its congeners in a number of meristic and morphometric characters. The author concludes it "warrant[s] specific status."

- **Meristic differences.** Dorsal and anal ray means were 76.3 (SD 1.73, N = 132) and 55.8 (SD 1.46, N = 132) for *C. abbotti*. Compared with *C. arenaceus*, *C. abbotti* averages three more dorsal rays, two more anal rays, three fewer lateral line scales, and about three more gill rakers. Compared with *C. spilopterus*, it averages two fewer dorsal rays, three fewer anal rays, four more gill rakers, and one fewer caudal vertebra.

- **Significance.** Analyses of variance showed differences significant beyond the 1.0% level in all but three meristic instances. Proportional measurements overlapped considerably, but small differences were highly significant.

- **Intermediate and shared characters.** For fin ray counts, *C. abbotti* falls between *C. arenaceus* and *C. spilopterus*. It is inseparable from *C. arenaceus* in vertebral number and pectoral fin length, and from *C. spilopterus* in jaw lengths and lateral line scale count. - **Dentition.** *C. abbotti* has slightly more fixed teeth than its congeners, which are more regular and more strongly recurved posterolaterally. Its anterior teeth are less enlarged and less widely spaced than in the other two species.

- **First known reversal in Citharichthys.** A dextral (reversed) juvenile of 15.9 mm SL, from GCRL 2733, had 76 dorsal rays, 54 anal rays, and 22 caudal vertebrae, with no other abnormalities. The author notes that [[Gudger 1935]] did not include *Citharichthys* in his review of flatfish reversal. Gudger had suggested that mortality of reversed young in plaice "approaches 100 percent," and the author speculates that a similar situation may apply here, or that reversal is extremely rare in the genus.

- **Taxonomic caution on dentition.** Standard keys separate *Citharichthys* from *Syacium* by uniserial (*Citharichthys*) versus biserial (*Syacium*) upper jaw teeth. The author shows that *Syacium gunteri* and *Cyclopsetta chittendeni* also have two or three rows of movable teeth. He argues that future keys must distinguish fixed from movable teeth to avoid misidentification.

- **Uncertain status of C. uhleri.** The author did not examine *C. uhleri* directly. The reported counts (68 dorsal, 52 anal, 17-18 gill rakers, 21 caudal vertebrae, narrow interorbital, dark brown coloration) clearly separate it from both *C. abbotti* and *C. spilopterus*. It may prove to be the senior synonym of *C. arenaceus*, but this was not pursued.

- **Methods.** Measurements were taken with dial calipers or an ocular micrometer to the nearest 0.1 mm. Statistical analyses (ANOVA, covariance, regression) were run on an IBM 1130-2B 8K computer. Dentition drawings came from cleared, stained, and dissected specimens of 100-115 mm SL. Funding came from [[National Science Foundation]] Grant GB-6823.
