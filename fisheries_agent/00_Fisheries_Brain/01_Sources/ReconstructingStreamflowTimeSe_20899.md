# Reconstructing Streamflow Time Series in Central Arizona Using Monthly Precipitation and Tree Ring Records

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hydroclimatology

## Executive Summary

This paper reconstructs streamflow for three central Arizona gauges, [[Verde River]] at Tangle Creek (VERDTANG), [[Tonto Creek]] at Gun Creek (TONTGUN), and [[Salt River]] at Roosevelt (SALTROOS), from 1580 to the start of the historical gauge records. The method is a three-way interpolation model combining [[Multiple Discriminant Analysis]], [[Multiple Linear Regression]], and the [[Normal Ratio Method]], using a median estimate. Inputs are annual [[Tree Ring Indices]] and monthly precipitation from 30 stations. Adding precipitation reduced mean absolute error by roughly 60%, compared with roughly 30% for tree rings alone. Because interpolation shrinks extreme values, the author applies an asymmetric variance adjustment that removes a negative bias caused by positively skewed streamflow. Harmonic analysis of the reconstructed record finds a 70-year cycle and a 5.2-year cycle, and the author projects a major drought in the 2020s.

Note: the source is a peer-reviewed hydroclimatology article. It contains no fish species data, so the species and taxonomy fields are marked N/A.

## Background & Description

- **Motivation:** Greenhouse-gas-driven changes in precipitation and streamflow threaten water supplies in the [[Southwestern United States]], where demand is rising with population growth.
- **Problem:** Instrumental streamflow records are too short and too variable to separate natural low-frequency variability from human-caused change. [[Tree Ring Records]] extend the record back centuries.
- **Prior work cited:** [[Meko and Stockton]] (1984) reported a 15%–20% decline in the upper Colorado basin, and [[Revelle and Waggoner]] (1983) estimated roughly 40% lower Colorado River flow under a 2°C warming with 10% less precipitation. [[Smith and Stockton]] (1981) reconstructed Salt and Verde flows from tree rings, and [[Stockton]] (1975) worked on Bright Angel Creek and the upper San Francisco River.
- **Study gauges:**
  - VERDTANG: records complete since 1904; a regression linked it to the Bartlett gauge (correlation greater than 98%) for 1888–1904. [[Bartlett Dam]] was built in 1939.
  - TONTGUN and SALTROOS: records begin January 1913 and are complete through May 1990.
- **Data sources:** Streamflow was supplied by the [[Salt River Project]]. Tree ring indices came from 27 sites in central and eastern Arizona (Table 1), each averaged over at least four cores. Thirty precipitation stations were used (Table 2), with missing months filled by the three-way interpolation method.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrologic variability:** Southwestern streamflow is highly variable, and the author notes that natural cycles may mask or mimic climate-change signals.
- **Proposed natural cycles:** Meko and Stockton (1982) suggested a 30-year cycle in Rocky Mountain precipitation. [[Sellers]] (1960) suggested a 48–49-year cycle and an 11-year sunspot cycle in Arizona summer precipitation.
- **ENSO link:** The 5.2-year cycle may relate to the [[El Niño–Southern Oscillation]], which [[Schonher and Nicholson]] (1989) linked to winter precipitation in southern California. ENSO is only quasi-periodic, so weak correlations are expected.
- **Drought context:** The author suggests central Arizona may see a drought similar to the mid-twentieth-century drought (1953–1966) in the early next century. The 70-year cycle appears to capture major droughts around 1810 and 1735.
- **Reservoirs:** Storage changes at Bartlett and Horseshoe reservoirs on the Verde River were used to adjust gauge data.

## Key Findings

1. **Skill improves with precipitation.** Mean absolute error reduction versus the long-term mean was about 30% with tree rings only (1580–1863) and about 60% with both sources. Spring and annual streamflow reached similar skill.
2. **Summer is poorly reconstructed.** Tree rings showed no significant skill for summer streamflow, and precipitation only partly helped.
3. **Method comparison.** Multiple discriminant analysis beat multiple linear regression or the normal ratio method in 13 of 18 comparison sets. The median of the three methods was judged the most reliable.
4. **Hit rates.** Sign-correct reconstructions reached 72–79% for spring and 75–86% for annual values with tree rings alone. With precipitation added they reached 84–91% for spring and 87–91% for annual. Smith's (1981) reported skill values (64.5% and 62.1%) are not directly comparable because his evaluation was not independent.
5. **Negative bias and variance loss.** Positive skewness pulls reconstructions toward the mean. Above-normal flows are reduced more than below-normal flows are raised, so the mean reconstructed flow is too low. The MLR method keeps more variance but at a cost in skill.
6. **Variance adjustment.** Separate positive and negative multiplicative factors (Table 6) restore variance. Adjusted means (Table 7) move closer to observed means. For example, spring TONTGUN was 62,197 acre-feet observed, 54,764 reconstructed, and 64,771 adjusted.
7. **Extreme values.** Reconstructed maxima are roughly 1.3 million acre-feet (SALTROOS and VERDTANG spring), 1.7 million (VERDTANG annual), and 2.3 million acre-feet (SALTROOS annual). The long-term means are 269,558 (VERDTANG spring) and 649,669 acre-feet (SALTROOS annual).
8. **70-year cycle.** Significant at the 99.5% level for all three gauges, with variance explained of 5.6–8.0%. Correlations with reconstructed flow for 1589–1847 were 20.5% (VERDTANG), 14.6% (TONTGUN), and 20.4% (SALTROOS). Five-cycle F statistics for 1634–1989 supported a period of 70.8 years. The author states that no physical explanation is advanced.
9. **5.2-year cycle.** Significant at the 99.9% level in the 1848–1989 record. Correlations with reconstructed flow were weaker: 10.5%, 4.5%, and 5.6% for 1589–1847.
10. **Projected drought.** Extending the 70-year cycle suggests a major drought in central Arizona during 2020–2029.
11. **Data quality.** No significant inhomogeneities were found in the observed series. Reconstruction skill levels off with more than about three stations or sites per basin.
