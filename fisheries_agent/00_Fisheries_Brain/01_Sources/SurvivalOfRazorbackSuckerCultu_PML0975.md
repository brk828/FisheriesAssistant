# Survival of Razorback Sucker Cultured in Earthen Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This hatchery report documents survival and growth of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*[[Xyrauchen Texanus]]*) reared in earthen ponds during 1985 at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], New Mexico. The species is endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], has declined in the past century, and has been proposed as threatened, though it was not listed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service at the time. Fry and fingerlings were cultured in [[Earthen Ponds]] through three stages: [[Fry Culture]], [[Subfingerling Culture]], and [[Fingerling Culture]]. Survival ranged from 87.8% to 98.6% for fry, 92.7% to 95.8% for subfingerlings, and was 96.2% for fingerlings. Survival has improved since the program moved to Dexter in 1981, which the author attributes to better fertilization and stocking practices.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is one of the largest [[Catostomidae]] in western North America. It reaches total lengths near 1 m (3.3 ft) and weights of 5 to 6 kg (11 to 13 lb) (Minckley 1973). It is considered endemic to the Colorado River basin and is among the native fishes whose populations have declined over the past century (McAda and Wydoski 1980; Minckley 1983). Deacon et al. (1979) listed the species as threatened in several western states, but the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service had not listed it (Johnson and Rinne 1982).

A propagation program began in 1974 at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], Arizona, to test and develop spawning and rearing techniques (Toney 1974). The program moved to Dexter in 1981. Reproductive biology and early life history have been studied (Minckley and Gustafson 1983; Marsh 1985; Hamman 1985; Inslee 1982), but little was documented on survival under culture conditions from fry to fingerling size. This study addresses that gap.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The culture system relied on [[Earthen Ponds]] of 0.14 to 0.44 ha (0.34 to 1.08 acres), with average depths of 0.9 to 1.2 m (3.0 to 4.0 ft). Water temperatures were:

- **Fry rearing:** 8.3 °C (47.0 °F) to 21.6 °C (71.0 °F)
- **Subfingerling rearing:** 16.1 °C (61.0 °F) to 23.9 °C (75.0 °F)
- **Fingerling rearing:** 19.4 °C (67.0 °F) to 24.4 °C (76.0 °F)

Ponds were fertilized to encourage [[Natural Plankton]] as food, and fish received supplemental commercial feed. Fertilization used [[Alfalfa Pellets]] and [[Superphosphate Fertilization]]:

- **Initial treatment:** alfalfa pellets at 335 kg/ha (300 lb/acre) and superphosphate at 56 kg/ha (50 lb/acre)
- **Weekly maintenance:** alfalfa pellets at 112 kg/ha (100 lb/acre), applied weekly for five weeks in two fry ponds; three weekly applications in the other two, with superphosphate at 28 kg/ha (25 lb/acre) in one of them

The fry and subfingerling ponds also received pre-stocking treatments, and one subfingerling pond received a single alfalfa application before stocking.

## Key Findings

### Fry Culture (Table 1)
- Swim-up fry (276,120 total reported) were stocked in February and March 1985 into four ponds.
- Stocking density was 251,000/ha (101,000/acre) to 269,000/ha (109,000/acre).
- Fry ranged from about 15.6/g (71,000/lb) to 20.7/g (94,000/lb) at stocking.
- Rearing lasted 69 to 78 days.
- Survival ranged from 87.8% to 98.6%.
- Final fish size ranged from 508 to 724 fish/lb (per Table 1).
- No significant handling mortality was observed after transplanting.
- Fry received a commercial trout starter diet beginning on day one, supplemented by natural plankton. Feeding rate was not calculated.

### Subfingerling Culture (Table 2)
- Young fingerlings were stocked into three ponds (0.35 to 0.40 ha).
- Stocking density was 126,000/ha (51,000/acre) to 143,000/ha (58,000/acre).
- Rearing lasted 50 to 70 days.
- Survival ranged from 92.7% to 95.8%.
- Final size ranged from 135.3/kg (61.5/lb) to 165.0/kg (75.0/lb) (per the text).
- The pond that received a single pre-stocking alfalfa treatment had the highest survival (95.8%) and the highest food conversion ratio (2.67). The other two ponds had [[Food Conversion Ratio]] values of 1.90 and 1.34.
- Fish were fed a commercial trout diet (No. 2 and 3) beginning the day they were transplanted.

### Fingerling Culture (Table 3)
- Subfingerlings (about 52,000 total, 1930 estimated per text) were stocked into two ponds (0.40 ha each).
- Stocking density was 66,000/ha (26,000/acre) to 68,000/ha (27,000/acre).
- Initial size was 34.8/kg (15.8/lb).
- Rearing lasted 50 to 55 days.
- Total survival was 96.2%.
- Food conversion ratios were 2.13 and 2.64 in the two ponds.

### Historical Trend and Program Outlook
- Since the program moved to Dexter in 1981, overall survival from fry to fingerling was 10.8%, 14.1%, 35.7%, and 23.6% for 1981 through 1984 (unpublished data cited by the author).
- Overall survival for 1985 is printed in garbled OCR text that appears to read 91.8%. This figure should be verified against the original scan.
- The author credits improved [[Stocking Density]] and fertilization rates for the gains.
- The author argues that [[Fish Propagation]] will be central to recovery. Hatchery stock can be used for [[Native Fish Restocking]] and to supplement wild populations. Continued work on [[Reproductive Biology]] and [[Early Life History]] is expected to support long-term survival of the species.
