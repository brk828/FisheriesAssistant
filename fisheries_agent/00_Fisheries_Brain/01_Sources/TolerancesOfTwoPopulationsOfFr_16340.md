#ichthyology #marine-biology #Palaemonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 study examines the differential tolerance of two geographically distinct populations of freshwater shrimp, [[Palaemonetes kadiakensis]], to five chlorinated hydrocarbon insecticides: DDT, toxaphene, aldrin, endrin, and dieldrin. One population was collected from Twin Bayou (Sunflower Co., Mississippi), a waterway receiving agricultural runoff from cotton fields treated with these insecticides, while the reference population came from the pesticide-free [[Noxubee National Wildlife Refuge]]. Shrimp exposed to insecticide-laden runoff (Twin Bayou) exhibited 36-hour median tolerance limit (TL₅₀) values 1 to 3 times higher than the unexposed Noxubee population, suggesting some adaptive or physiological shift in tolerance. However, despite this elevated tolerance relative to their own reference population, Twin Bayou shrimp remained substantially more susceptible to all five insecticides than co-occurring fish species from the same bayou, which were shown in a companion study to be highly resistant (except to DDT). This discrepancy raises questions regarding trophic-level differences in bioaccumulation and detoxification, particularly in light of documented fish kill events that the shrimp population survived.

## Background & Description

The paper does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of *Palaemonetes kadiakensis*. Behavioral/physiological indicators of insecticide intoxication are noted instead of morphology:
- Test animals showing toxic stress became **hypersensitive**, exhibiting erratic jumping behavior, occasionally leaping from the test solution.
- Shrimp displaying intoxication symptoms could become trapped on the sides of test jars by surface tension.
- Assessment of mortality relied on behavioral response to **antennal and leg stimuli**; unresponsive individuals were classified as dead.
- Shrimp that escaped the test solution and were exposed to air survived several hours (barring desiccation) and could recover fully when returned to solution, indicating some resilience to acute environmental stress independent of chemical toxicity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species**: [[Palaemonetes kadiakensis]] (freshwater shrimp), family Palaemonidae.
- **Habitat**: Freshwater bayous and refuge waters in Mississippi, USA.
  - **Twin Bayou** (Sunflower Co., Miss.): An agriculturally-impacted waterway receiving pesticide runoff (DDT, toxaphene, endrin, methyl parathion) from adjacent cotton fields.
  - **[[Noxubee National Wildlife Refuge]]** (Noxubee Co., Miss.): A nonagricultural, presumably pesticide-free reference site.
- **Trophic Role**: Shrimp are presumed to be an important prey item for local fish populations, notably [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*).
- **Co-occurring species**: Twin Bayou hosts four fish species demonstrated in a related study ([[Ferguson et al. 1964]]) to be highly resistant to chlorinated hydrocarbon insecticides, with the notable exception of DDT.
- **Historical exposure context**: The Twin Bayou shrimp population had survived two severe fish kill events (late July and mid-August 1964), which were apparently caused primarily by DDT contamination.
- **Water chemistry**: Test water had pH 7.4 and hardness of 24 ppm; experiments conducted at 68 ± 4°F.

## Key Findings

- **Total sample size**: 2,650 shrimp tested — 930 from Noxubee Refuge and 1,720 from Twin Bayou.
- **Testing protocol**: Groups of 5 shrimp were exposed to 0.5 liter of test solution (insecticide diluted from a 1% acetone stock) in quart jars for 36 hours; mortality was checked at 6, 12, 24, and 36-hour intervals. No control mortality occurred.

- **36-hour TL₅₀ values (ppb) for each insecticide by population** (see [[Chlorinated Hydrocarbon Insecticide Toxicity]]):

| Insecticide | Noxubee (ppb) | Twin Bayou (ppb) |
|---|---|---|
| DDT | 4.5 | 10.0 |
| Toxaphene | 57.5 | 170.0 |
| Aldrin | 85.0 | 185.0 |
| Dieldrin | 50.0 | 135.0 |
| Endrin | 6.5 | 9.5 |

- **Population comparison**: Twin Bayou shrimp tolerated 1–3× higher insecticide concentrations than Noxubee shrimp across all five compounds, but the authors caution against calling this difference true "resistance."

- **Cross-species comparison**: Even the more sensitive Noxubee shrimp population showed greater tolerance to toxaphene, aldrin, endrin, and dieldrin than fish from untreated waters — yet Twin Bayou shrimp were still far more susceptible to all five insecticides than fish inhabiting the same contaminated bayou.

- **Ecological paradox**: Despite their comparatively low insecticide tolerance relative to sympatric fish, Twin Bayou shrimp survived massive fish die-off events attributed largely to DDT toxicity. Proposed explanations include:
  - Lower trophic position may reduce exposure to effects of **biological magnification** (bioaccumulation up the food chain).
  - Possible **rapid detoxication** mechanisms in shrimp.
  - Possible **mechanical exclusion** of pesticides (e.g., reduced uptake via exoskeleton or gills).

- **Related research**: This study complements [[Ferguson et al. 1964]] (BioScience 14: 43-44), which documented insecticide resistance in three freshwater fish species from the same watershed, reinforcing the trophic-level contrast in tolerance highlighted here.

- **Broader implication**: The findings suggest that agricultural runoff can differentially shape insecticide tolerance across trophic levels within the same aquatic ecosystem, with lower invertebrate prey (shrimp) remaining comparatively vulnerable even as top consumers (fish) develop pronounced resistance — a dynamic relevant to studies of [[Pesticide Bioaccumulation]] and [[Aquatic Food Web Toxicology]].
