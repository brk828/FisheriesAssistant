#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This study by Ridenhour (1957) examines the population dynamics of [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*) in [[Clear Lake]] and the adjacent [[Ventura Marsh]] in north-central Iowa between 1941 and 1955. Using gill nets, fyke nets, wire traps, and angling, 445 pike were collected and analyzed for age, growth, condition, food habits, and mortality. The research was prompted by a large stocking event (>15,000 fingerlings) in October 1953 following the installation of a carp trap/control structure in 1949 that severed the historical connection between the lake and its primary spawning marsh. Key findings indicate that growth rates in the lake were comparable to other North American waters, but pike in Ventura Marsh grew faster during their second year. The 1953 stocked year class exhibited notably poor condition, likely linked to two significant mortality events observed in 1954 and 1955. Tagging studies allowed for angler harvest estimates, revealing substantial catches in 1954 (2.02 pike/acre) largely attributable to the 1953 stocking effort.

## Background & Description

### Body-Scale Relationship (Lee's Method)
Regression of total body length (L) on anterior scale radius (R) revealed a statistically significant difference between habitats (F = 4.33, p<0.05), likely reflecting differential growth at the time of scale formation rather than differential scale growth rates.

- **Clear Lake** (n=208): $L = 1.383 + 2.278R$ (r = 0.857)
- **Ventura Marsh** (n=182): $L = 2.757 + 1.969R$ (r = 0.524)

### Length Conversions
Based on 390 pike (8.0–37.6 inches TL):
- Standard Length (S.L.) = 0.91 × Fork Length (F.L.)
- Fork Length = ~0.86 × Total Length (T.L.) *(implied ratio: TL = 1.16 SL; TL = 1.06 FL)*

### Length-Weight Relationships
A significant difference (99% confidence) existed between habitats, attributed to marsh fish being heavier-bodied for their length during early life stages compared to stocked lake fish.

- **Clear Lake** (n=188): $\log W = -2.823 + 3.122 \log L$
- **Ventura Marsh** (n=219): $\log W = -2.335 + 2.744 \log L$

### Condition Factor (K)
Utilizing the formula $C = W \times 10^5/L^3$ (converted to K via $K = C/37.204$):
- **Overall Lake average** (excluding 1953 year class): Mean C = 22.89
- **Overall Marsh average**: Mean C = 22.93
- **Stocked 1953 year class (in Lake)**: Mean C = 18.98 (Significantly lower, p<0.01)
- **1953 year class (remaining in Marsh)**: Mean C = 22.91
- General condition of [[Clear Lake]] pike was lower than reported for [[Esox lucius|Northern Pike]] in other regions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat
[[Clear Lake]] is a shallow, eutrophic, non-stratified lake (~3600 acres, max depth ~20 ft) featuring *Scirpus* and *Potamogeton* vegetation. [[Ventura Marsh]] (600 acres) served historically as the primary natural spawning ground until a carp trap/control structure separated the two water bodies in 1949, disrupting natural recruitment into the lake.

### Trophic Role & Food Habits
Northern Pike serve as a principal predatory species in the food web, critical to the predator-prey dynamics of [[Clear Lake]].
- **Lake diet** (n=10 stomachs): Predominantly fish remains (40%) and carp (10%); 50% were empty. One instance of crayfish predation was noted.
- **Marsh diet** (n=121 stomachs): Heavily reliant on **carp** (47.9%), specifically utilizing the 1954 carp hatch through the 1954–1955 growing seasons.
- **Parasites**: Black grubs were common in marsh specimens; cestodes and nematodes were also identified in the digestive tracts of both lake and marsh pike.

### Reproduction & Stocking
Natural reproduction was historically sufficient in [[Ventura Marsh]], but the 1949 carp trap eliminated most natural recruitment to the lake. Only one wild young-of-year pike (1954 year class) was found in [[Clear Lake]] between 1952–1955, making artificial stocking (fry, fingerlings, adults) the primary recruitment source (see Table 13).

## Key Findings

1. **Growth Rates**: Comparable to other regions overall, though [[Ventura Marsh]] pike grew significantly faster in their second year of life than [[Clear Lake]] pike (F = 23.84, p<0.05).
2. **Sexual Dimorphism in Growth**: Female pike grew significantly faster than males during the second year of life (F = 5.16, p<0.05), though not significantly different at the first annulus.
3. **Stocking Impact on Condition**: Mass stocking of fingerlings from the marsh into the lake had a clear adverse effect on fish condition for at least two years post-stocking.
4. **Angler Tag Returns**: Highest return rates occurred within the first fishing season post-tagging (9.6%–33.5%), decreasing substantially in subsequent years (e.g., 6.7% and 0.9%).
5. **Harvest Estimates**: Petersen-based estimates using tag ratios yielded catches of approximately 7,372 pike in 1954 (2.02 pike/acre) and 1,015 pike in 1955 (0.28 pike/acre).
6. **Mortality Events**:
    - **Summer 1954 (Lake)**: Mortality primarily affecting the stocked 1953 year class, potentially linked to their poor physical condition.
    - **May 1955 (Lake)**: A more severe mortality event (estimated 252–293 pike) correlated with recent handling at the carp trap and poor condition of the 1953 year class; two large females died from egg-binding.
    - **Summer 1955 (Marsh)**: Attributed primarily to abnormally high water temperatures (up to 96°F surface) rather than low oxygen, evidenced by hemorrhagic enteritis in specimens examined pathologically.
7. **Movement**: Pike tagged in [[Ventura Marsh]] and released at the lake dispersed widely, distributing themselves across most of [[Clear Lake]].
