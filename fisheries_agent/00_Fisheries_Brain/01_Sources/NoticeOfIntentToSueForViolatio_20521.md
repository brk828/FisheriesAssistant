# Notice of Intent to Sue for ESA Violations - Lower Colorado River Species Conservation Program

#ichthyology #conservation-law #cyprinidae #endangered-species #federal-policy

## Executive Summary

This legal notice, dated February 6, 1996, from [[Defenders of Wildlife]] to the [[U.S. Department of the Interior]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[Bureau of Reclamation]], alleges violations of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) in connection with a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) for development of a [[Lower Colorado River Species Conservation Program]]. The document details how federal agencies violated ESA Section 7 consultation requirements by executing the MOA in August 1995 without public notice, NEPA compliance, or proper ESA review. Four endangered fish species—[[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)—along with multiple threatened and endangered bird species, are affected by [[Bureau of Reclamation]] dam and reservoir operations that continue unabated despite ongoing harm to listed species and their designated critical habitat.

## Background & Description

The [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] supports four federally listed endangered fish species, all native to the basin and listed between 1967 and 1991, with critical habitat designated in 1994. The basin also provides habitat for threatened and endangered bird species including [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (*Rallus longirostris yumanensis*), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*), [[Brown Pelican]] (*Pelecanus occidentalis*), [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*), and [[Peregrine Falcon]] (*Falco peregrinus*), plus more than two dozen candidate species. Many species have recovery plans at various stages of implementation.

The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] operates as "water master" in the basin, operating and maintaining numerous dams, reservoirs, and facilities for flood control, river regulation, navigation, water storage, water delivery, and hydroelectric power generation. These operations have caused the decline and endangerment of numerous species through significant alteration and degradation of the ecosystem's hydrological cycle.

In 1994, following critical habitat designation for the four endangered fish species, [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] requested [[Bureau of Reclamation]] evaluate effects of Lower Colorado River operations on these fish and their critical habitat, initiating "informal" consultation under ESA Section 7(a)(2). BuRec agreed to prepare a biological assessment (BA) to determine whether formal consultation would be necessary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Lower Colorado River]] ecosystem has been significantly altered by dams, reservoirs, levees, and water withdrawals that have modified its natural hydrological cycle. The document emphasizes that threatened and endangered species need immediate, effective action to avoid extinction. The impacts of [[Bureau of Reclamation]] operations on listed species and critical habitat are well-documented and acknowledged by BuRec, FWS, and fish and wildlife experts.

Recovery plans for the endangered fish species identify BuRec's dam and reservoir operations as major causes of endangerment. The [[Bonytail Chub Recovery Plan]] (September 1990) acknowledges high threat levels and sets prevention of impending extinction as its first basic goal, with eventual reintroduction into historic habitat in the Lower Basin as a recovery objective. The [[Humpback Chub Recovery Plan]] (September 1990) similarly identifies BuRec operations as a major cause of endangerment and considers future reintroduction a principal recovery goal. The [[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan]] (August 1991) stresses the role BuRec facilities played in causing decline and calls for reintroduction into historic range. The [[Yuma Clapper Rail Recovery Plan]] (February 1983) ties recovery directly to operation of BuRec dams and reservoirs.

Critical habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] has been designated (59 FR 13374, 1994), with documentation emphasizing that BuRec operations adversely affect both listed species and their critical habitat.

## Key Findings

**Legal Violations Alleged:**

1. **Section 7(a)(2) Violations - Predetermined Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives**: The MOA obligates FWS to consider the MOA as the automatic "reasonable and prudent alternative" (RPA) for every biological opinion issued in the basin during the MOA term. This predetermination violates ESA requirements that every Section 7 consultation be fact-specific, location-specific, and result in unique RPAs based on best available science tailored to particular circumstances and impacts of each federal action.

2. **Failure to Consult on MOA Itself**: DOI violated Section 7 by failing to consult with FWS on potential effects of the MOA itself on threatened or endangered species or critical habitat before execution. The MOA execution was an action "authorized, funded, or carried out" by DOI requiring consultation.

3. **Unlawful Delay of Biological Assessment**: BuRec and FWS unlawfully abandoned required Section 7 process and "shelved" preparation of BuRec's BA until at least 1998, violating the 180-day completion requirement (50 CFR §402.12(i)). This three-year extension is unreasonable given time-sensitive ESA purposes and dire status of endangered species.

4. **Misuse of Informal Consultation**: The "informal" consultation process does not apply where it is abundantly clear that agency actions do adversely affect listed species and critical habitat. Formal consultation is required, and FWS must prepare a biological opinion forthwith. BuRec and FWS are engaged in "informal" consultation as means of bypassing strict statutory requirements.

5. **Section 7(d) Violations**: Until completion of formal consultation, BuRec may not make any "irreversible or irretrievable commitment of resources" (ESA Section 7(d), 16 U.S.C. §1536(d)). BuRec operations continue unabated and unchanged, adversely affecting threatened, endangered, and candidate species and their critical habitat.

**Recovery Plan Violations:**

6. **Sections 4 and 7(a)(1) Violations - Failure to Prioritize Recovery**: The MOA goal is to "accommodate current water diversions and power production" while "working toward" recovery, reversing ESA requirements that federal agencies give endangered species priority over primary missions (TVA v. Hill, 437 U.S. 153, 185 (1978)). Federal agencies must take action to recover species, even if this means modifying dam and reservoir operations to restore habitat.

7. **Conflict with Recovery Plans**: The MOA and Conservation Program may commit DOI to actions that violate published recovery plans or preclude their implementation. Section 4(f) requires FWS to implement recovery plans; Section 7(a)(1) requires other agencies to assist. The MOA removes key opportunities for federal agencies to implement recovery actions as part of RPA implementation.

8. **Improper Delegation of Recovery Authority**: The MOA puts a multi-party "Steering Committee" in the recovery driver's seat rather than FWS. FWS cannot delegate recovery discretion to a committee comprised of federal and non-federal representatives and cannot foreclose consideration of potentially viable options such as modification of dam or reservoir operations.

**Section 9 and 10 Violations:**

9. **Unlawful Interim Regulatory Assurance**: FWS cannot lawfully grant non-federal entities "interim regulatory assurance" for a three-year development period in advance of completing a Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP). Section 10(a)(2) is clear: "No permit may be issued by the Secretary authorizing any [incidental] taking . . . unless the applicant therefor submits to the Secretary a conservation plan."

10. **Lack of Public Notice**: Section 10(c) requires publication of Federal Register notice for each application for exemption or permit, inviting submission of written data, views, or arguments. DOI gave no public notice before signing the MOA, provided no opportunity for public comment, and conducted neither NEPA compliance nor ESA Section 7 review.

**Ongoing Harm:**

The document emphasizes that DOI's violations are causing present and substantial harm to endangered species. Survival of these species continues to be jeopardized, critical habitat continues to be destroyed and adversely modified, and individuals continue to be "taken" in violation of ESA Section 9. BuRec operations continue unabated and unchanged, and BuRec is failing to ensure operations are not harming endangered species, affecting critical habitat, or reducing chances for recovery in the wild.

**Demands:**

[[Defenders of Wildlife]] demands two actions: (1) DOI must rescind the MOA, and (2) BuRec must immediately initiate and timely complete "formal" consultation with respect to all listed species and designated critical habitat in the [[Lower Colorado River]] vis-à-vis BuRec operations. In the meantime, BuRec must cease making "irreversible or irretrievable commitment[s] of resources" per ESA Section 7(d). Deadline for compliance: February 19, 1996, with litigation threatened if actions not taken.

The notice reserves the right to assert claims based on NEPA, ESA, Administrative Procedure Act, and Federal Advisory Committee Act, while expressing hope that litigation can be avoided through development of a legal alternative that recovers species and ecosystems while accommodating water and power interests where consistent with the primary goal of endangered species recovery.
