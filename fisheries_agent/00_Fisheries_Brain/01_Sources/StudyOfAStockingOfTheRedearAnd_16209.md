## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by Darrell E. Louder and William M. Lewis of Southern Illinois University, examines the relative success of a combined stocking of [[Redear Sunfish]] and [[Bluegill]] in Lake Murphysboro, an artificial impoundment in Jackson County, Illinois. Using creel census data, spillway trapping counts, and scale analysis, the researchers compared catch rates, population density, size structure, and growth rates between the two species. The study concludes that while the Redear Sunfish exhibits lower catchability by anglers, it demonstrates faster growth and a better condition factor than the Bluegill, making the two species compatible for combination stocking with [[Largemouth Bass]].

## Background & Description
- **Growth Comparison (Scale Analysis):** The [[Redear Sunfish]] was determined to be a faster-growing fish than the [[Bluegill]] across all measured age classes.
  - **Redear Sunfish Calculated Total Length (1955):** Reached 10.2 inches by Age VIII (n=56).
  - **Bluegill Calculated Total Length (1955):** Reached 9.3 inches by Age VIII/IX (n=92).
  - **Redear Sunfish (1956):** Reached 8.8 inches by Age VI (n=160).
  - **Bluegill (1956):** Reached 5.9 inches by Age VI (n=120).
- **Condition Factor:** A coefficient-of-condition study indicated that the Redear Sunfish was significantly more "plump" (robust body condition) compared to the Bluegill.
- **Size Structure (Spillway Escapement):**
  - Redear Sunfish population was skewed toward larger, catchable fish (511 fish measured 6–10 inches vs. 99 fish at 1–5 inches).
  - Bluegill population was skewed toward smaller, juvenile fish (375 fish measured 1–5 inches vs. 69 fish at 6–9 inches).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Lake Murphysboro is a 160-acre artificial impoundment with a maximum depth of 30 feet, filled in 1950 via surface runoff and a small stream inflow.
- **Community Assemblage:** The lake supports a community including [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[White Crappie]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Warmouth]], and [[Yellow Bullhead]].
- **Population Density:** The higher rate of Redear Sunfish loss over the lake spillway (relative to Bluegill) suggests the Redear population density may have been higher than initially assumed based on angler catch rates alone.
- **Age-Class Composition:** Both species displayed a similar spread of age classes within the lake, suggesting stable, multi-year recruitment for both populations.
- **Compatibility:** The study suggests that Redear Sunfish and Bluegill are ecologically compatible, supporting their combined use in stocking programs alongside predatory species like the Largemouth Bass.

## Key Findings
1. **Catch Rate Discrepancy:** Bluegill were caught at a significantly higher rate than Redear Sunfish in both survey years (1955: 1.29 vs. 0.26 fish/hour; 1956: 0.78 vs. 0.09 fish/hour), despite comparable or higher population density of Redear.
2. **Catchability Issue:** The primary "weak point" identified for the Redear Sunfish is its low catchability by standard angling methods (worms and cockroaches) compared to the Bluegill.
3. **Growth Rate:** The Redear Sunfish consistently grows faster and larger at each annulus (age mark) than the Bluegill, a difference considered significant enough to impact fishery management decisions.
4. **Physical Condition:** Redear Sunfish exhibited a superior coefficient of condition, indicating better health/plumpness relative to their length compared to Bluegill.
5. **Management Implication:** Despite differing catch rates, the compatibility of growth and age structure between the two species supports the continued practice of stocking Redear Sunfish and Bluegill together with Largemouth Bass in Southern Illinois impoundments.
