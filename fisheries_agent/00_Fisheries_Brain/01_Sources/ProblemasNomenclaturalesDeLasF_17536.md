# Nomenclatural Problems of the Mexican Forms of the Genus Astyanax (Pisces: Characidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic commentary, published in Zoologia Informa (1988, no. 38), examines the nomenclature of the freshwater characid genus [[Astyanax]] in Mexico. The authors argue that the genus is taxonomically confused: undescribed forms are present, published names are assigned inconsistently to species or subspecies, and non-taxonomic publications have added to the confusion. The paper reviews the available nominal species, the historical usage of names (including the troglobitic forms once placed in [[Anoptichthys]]), and the uncertainty over type localities. The authors propose provisional names based on current usage and recommend that non-taxonomists send specimens to specialists and deposit voucher material in ichthyological collections.

## Background & Description

The authors note that the Characidae and related families contain many very similar forms, often allopatric and sometimes sympatric, which are easily overlooked or confused. Since 1963, one author (SCB) had recognized undescribed Astyanax in central and northern Mexico that did not match the names of [[Regan (1908)]] or [[Alvarez (1950, 1970)]]. A new species, [[Astyanax armandoi]], was described by [[Lozano-Vilano and Contreras-Balderas (1990)]].

The paper lists the available nominal taxa, which include:
- *Tetragonopterus mexicanus* Filippi (1853), type locality "City of Mexico"
- *Tetragonopterus aeneus* Günther (1860), Oaxaca
- *Astyanax argentatus* Baird and Girard (1854), Rio Nueces, Texas
- *Tetragonopterus oaxacanensis* Bocourt (1868), Oaxaca
- *Tetragonopterus nitidus* Bocourt (1868), Taxco, Guerrero
- *Tetragonopterus fulgens* Bocourt (1868), Cuernavaca, Morelos
- *Tetragonopterus finitimus* Bocourt (1868), Orizaba, Veracruz
- *Tetragonopterus streetsii* Cope (1872), Coatzacoalcos basin, Veracruz
- *Tetragonopterus macrophthalmus* Regan (1908), Motzorongo, Veracruz
- *Tetragonopterus angustifrons* Regan (1908), "Mexico"
- *Astyanax fasciatus altior* Hubbs (1936), Progreso, Yucatan
- *Astyanax armandoi* Lozano-Vilano and Contreras-Balderas (1990), near Pénjamo, Chiapas/Tabasco

The authors also report work on at least four further undescribed endemic forms: two in central Coahuila, one in Durango-Coahuila, and one in Oaxaca, three of which are already at risk of extinction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The current working classification, which the authors describe as unsettled, is as follows:
- *Astyanax mexicanus* (or *A. fasciatus mexicanus*): northeastern forms from central Texas to the upper Papaloapan, or only as far as Punta del Morro; also Pacific slope forms north of and around the [[Balsas River]].
- *Astyanax aeneus* (or *A. fasciatus aeneus*): tropical Mexican forms from the upper Papaloapan and the Armería–Coahuayana area southward, possibly to Central or South America.
- *Astyanax armandoi*: a stream and spring form near Pénjamo, Chiapas–Tabasco.
- Cave-dwelling, eyeless forms from San Luis Potosí and Tamaulipas, historically called *Astyanax jordani* or *Anoptichthys jordani*.
- *Astyanax altior* from cenotes and aguadas in northern Yucatan.

Distribution is shaped by historical drainage connections and collecting routes. For example, the type locality of Filippi's *T. mexicanus* may have been on the Veracruz–Mexico City or Mexico City–Acapulco routes, which cross the upper Papaloapan and Balsas basins.

Legal protection is also relevant. The Mexican environmental law (LGEEPA, 1996) protects only named species, so undescribed threatened forms receive no legal protection. The authors state that this has contributed to recent extinctions of several taxa.

## Key Findings

- **Nomenclatural confusion:** Names of Mexican *Astyanax* have been applied inconsistently. Some authors treat the northern form as *A. mexicanus*, others as a subspecies of *A. fasciatus*, and others as a synonym of *Anoptichthys*.
- **Type localities are unreliable:** The type locality of *A. argentatus* was changed by Jordan and Evermann (1896–1900), and early collectors often recorded only country or continent. The type material of most nominal forms is scattered across museums (USNM, MNHN, BMNH) or lost.
- **Possible name changes:** If Filippi's material came from the Balsas route, *A. argentatus* could replace *A. mexicanus* for the northern form. If it came from the Veracruz route, *A. mexicanus* could become a synonym of *A. aeneus*. The status of the Balsas form is still unresolved.
- **Cave forms:** Sadoglu and McKee showed that the difference between *Anoptichthys* and *Astyanax* rests on two allelic gene pairs (albinism and anophthalmia), so *Anoptichthys* was considered invalid and *A. jordani* was treated as an ecological race of *A. mexicanus*.
- **Yucatan form:** The Yucatan form *A. altior* is distinctive and probably deserves species status, but it has not been re-evaluated since its description.
- **Recommended provisional names** (by usage, not priority): *A. mexicanus* north of Punta del Morro (excluding the Nazas–Aguanaval and Cuatro Ciénegas complexes); *A. aeneus* south of Punta del Morro and outside the Balsas basin; *A. altior* for northern Yucatan cenote forms; *A. armandoi* for the Pénjamo form. The Balsas form should be called *Astyanax* sp. "Balsas" or *A.* cf. *aeneus* Balsas until it is resolved.
- **Practical recommendations:** Non-taxonomists should have specimens checked by specialists, give exact localities, and deposit voucher specimens in ichthyological collections. Undescribed threatened forms should be formally named so that they can be protected.
