# Arnett Creek Fish Barrier: Construction Drawings and Specifications

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fish-conservation

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of U.S. Forest Service construction drawings (Region 3, Southwestern Region) for a [[Fish Barrier]] on Arnett Creek, an ephemeral or intermittent stream in the [[Tonto National Forest]], Globe Ranger District, Pinal County, Arizona. The project is labelled "Arnett Creek Native Fish." The drawings describe a gabion-basket wall anchored into a cliff wall and bedrock, with supporting clearing, excavation and anchoring specifications. The document contains no species list, population data or monitoring results. Its ecological purpose is implied by the map legend, which shows proposed and relocated barrier sites in relation to areas of potential [[Fish Toxicant]] treatment.

## Background & Description

The package has three sheets (title sheet, vicinity map, construction details) plus a general-notes page. It is a construction and design document, not a research report.

- **Project name:** Arnett Creek Fish Barrier (Arnett Creek Native Fish project).
- **Agency:** [[USDA Forest Service]], Region 3, Tonto National Forest, Globe Ranger District.
- **Map source:** Tonto GIS, JPL, dated 9/95 (vicinity map).
- **Drawing files:** dated 09/06 (site plan and detail sheets).
- **Specifications:** reference Forest Service Section 630 (Clearing and Grubbing; Gabions) in the publication *Forest Service Specifications for Construction of Roads and Bridges* (cited as EM-7720-100).

The vicinity map legend identifies:
- Proposed and relocated fish barrier locations.
- Areas of moderate and high potential for treatment with fish toxicant, separated by perennial or intermittent water classes.
- Watershed boundary and analysis area.
- Vegetation classes: forested mixed broadleaf, scrub/shrub mesquite, scrub/shrub mixed broadleaf, and forested cottonwood.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydrology:** The stream is mapped as perennial or intermittent water. The average stream width at the barrier site is 31 ft (the scanned value reads "3l'", interpreted as 31 ft).
- **Riparian vegetation:** The vicinity is dominated by mixed broadleaf forest, mesquite scrub, and cottonwood stands. A willow tree in an eroded area is slated for removal.
- **Site geology:** The barrier is anchored into an existing cliff wall and bedrock, with boulders placed along the structure.
- **Management context:** The legend links barrier sites to fish-toxicant treatment zones. This indicates the barrier is meant to block fish movement, most likely to protect native fish from non-native species, though the document does not state this explicitly.

No water chemistry, flow measurements, invertebrate data, plankton data or fish survey results are included.

## Key Findings

**1. Site plan and barrier geometry**
- A [[Gabion Basket]] wall sits across the stream, with a splash pad of approximately 50 ft downstream.
- The wall is about 13 ft high and 26 ft wide, according to the section drawing.
- Excess excavation is placed behind the wall on the upstream side.
- Backfill goes to the top of the bottom basket.
- The top of the wall matches the stream edge; the corner of the wall is anchored into the bank.

**2. Clearing and grubbing (Section 630)**
- The contractor must remove one willow tree, approximately 12 inches in diameter.
- The trunk is cut into 2 sections and disposed of on site as designated by the engineer.

**3. Excavation**
- Excavation is incidental to the gabion work.
- Excess material is disposed of at locations shown on the drawings or designated by the engineer.

**4. Gabion specifications**
- Basket sizes are set by the contractor and may vary to suit site conditions.
- Rock comes from a commercial source or as shown on Sheet 2.
- Each gabion basket is secured with cable and anchored into the cliff wall and boulders at 3 ft on center.

**5. Anchoring system**
- The specified system is a [[Williams Form Engineering Company]] pre-stressed, hollow-core, spin-lock rock bolt system, or an approved equal.
- Each anchor is designed for a load of 50,000 (units are illegible in the scan).
- Washers, nuts, bolts and grout must come from the same manufacturer as the anchor bolts, and installation follows the manufacturer's specifications.
- Anchors use two steel eyebolts with galvanized steel cable and cable clamps.
- Steel reinforcing bar (#8 rebar, grouted in place into bedrock) is spaced at 6 ft on center.
- Galvanized steel cable (5/8 in. diameter) runs through the basket and into the cliff wall, spaced at 3 ft on center.
- Reinforcing steel is specified to ASTM A-615.

**6. Documentation status**
- The sheets list project name, designer, checker and reviewer fields, with signature and date blocks. Several fields are partly illegible in the scan.
- Scale is noted as "not to scale" for the site plan and detail sheets.
