## Executive Summary

This document is a **Challenge Cost-Share Agreement** (No. 0301-93-039) between the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] (U.S. Forest Service) and the [[Center for Environmental Studies]] at Arizona State University (project coordinators [[Paul Marsh]] and [[W. L. Minckley]]). Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the source is an administrative/legal instrument establishing a collaborative research program to study **native fish communities** inhabiting lotic (flowing water) habitats on Forest Service lands. The agreement authorizes compilation of existing distributional data and new field inventories to support Endangered Species Act (ESA) compliance and Forest Service management obligations. The project was budgeted at $8,000 total (split $4,000 Forest Service / $4,000 Cooperator) and scheduled for completion by March 30, 1995.

## Background & Description

No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic descriptions of individual species are provided in this source. The document references only an aggregate category:

- **12 species of native fishes** occurring on Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest lands, each falling into one of three regulatory categories:
  - Listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]
  - Candidates for listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]]
  - Designated as [[Sensitive Species]] by the Regional Forester

No common names, scientific (binomial) names, size, coloration, or diagnostic morphological traits are specified in the text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** [[Lotic Systems]] (rivers, streams, and other flowing-water habitats) within [[National Forest]] lands managed by the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, Arizona.
- **Geographic context:** Terrestrial-freshwater interface habitats under federal management in east-central Arizona; not a marine or oceanic basin — inland desert/mountain freshwater drainage systems typical of the [[American Southwest]].
- **Conservation status driver:** Species distribution and habitat association data are explicitly identified as **currently inadequate or scattered** across agencies, motivating the need for coordinated data compilation and field survey.
- **Data gaps noted:** "Some lotic systems on Forest Service lands presently lack current or adequate information on the status of native fish communities," highlighting incomplete baseline ecological knowledge for at-risk native fish populations.

## Key Findings

- The Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests manage habitat for **12 native fish species** with elevated conservation concern (ESA-listed, ESA candidate, or Regional Forester Sensitive status) — precise species identities not enumerated in this document.
- The Forest Service's statutory obligations stem from the **[[Endangered Species Act]]** and the **[[National Forest Management Act]]**, requiring reliable data on distribution, habitat association, and population trends for these species.
- The agreement establishes a **two-phase research plan**:
  1. **Compilation phase** — Cooperator (ASU Center for Environmental Studies) will synthesize existing occurrence/distribution data for native fishes on Forest Service lands and identify remaining information gaps.
  2. **Field inventory phase** — Cooperator will conduct new lotic habitat surveys where baseline fish community data are lacking.
- Deliverables specified in the Plan of Work (Attachment B) include:
  - A final report summarizing existing distributional data.
  - GIS-compatible maps of native fish occurrence data.
  - A final report on new survey results, with associated spatial/GIS data.
  - Voucher specimens (as available) for Forest Service educational/training use.
- Total project budget: **$8,000**, split evenly between Forest Service in-kind/cash contributions and Cooperator-incurred costs (salaries, travel, equipment use).
- This agreement exemplifies a **federal-university partnership model** for addressing data deficiencies affecting imperiled freshwater fish conservation, linking directly to broader themes in [[Southwestern Native Fish Conservation]] and [[Endangered Species Act Compliance]] on public lands.
