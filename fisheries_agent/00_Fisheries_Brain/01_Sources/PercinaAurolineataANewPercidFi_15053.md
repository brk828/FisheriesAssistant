# Percina aurolineata: A New Goldline Darter from the Alabama River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This manuscript describes *Percina aurolineata*, a new darter of the [[Subgenus Hadropterus]] from the [[Alabama River Drainage]], and compares it with the other three members of the subgenus: [[Percina Sciera]], [[Percina Nigrofasciata]] and [[Percina Lenticula]]. The description rests on 59 specimens. The new species is closest to *P. sciera*, from which it differs in average lateral-line scale and vertebral counts and in colour pattern. The paper also extends the ranges of *P. lenticula* and *P. sciera*, reports on co-inhabiting populations, and documents natural hybridization between *P. nigrofasciata* and *P. sciera* below a dam on the [[Pearl River]].

## Background & Description

The authors, Royal D. Suttkus (Tulane University) and John S. Ramsey (University of Puerto Rico), based the work on collections made mostly at night between 1957 and 1966 (the 59 *aurolineata* specimens were taken in 1962, 1965 and 1966). The holotype (TU 39573) is an adult male, 70.0 mm standard length, collected on 18 June 1966 from the [[Coosawattee River]] about 4.2 miles southwest of Ellijay, Gilmer County, Georgia. The first specimen was taken on 17 March 1957 from the [[Cahaba River]], Bibb County, Alabama. The common name given is Goldline Darter.

**Diagnosis:** *P. aurolineata* is the most slender of the four species in the subgenus. Key traits include:
- Lateral-line scales 59–74 (usually 64–72).
- Vertebrae 40–44 (usually 41–43).
- Dorsal soft rays 10–13 (usually 10 or 11).
- Narrow least fleshy interorbital width and short upper jaw.
- Mid-dorsal saddles absent or indistinct.
- A dorsolateral stripe that is bright amber or russet in nuptial males and is straight anteriorly, not turned toward the occiput.

**Colour and morphology:** The dark lateral band consists of seven to nine linked blotches. In males the belly is fully scaled. Females may have bare patches on the belly. The preopercle has a serrate margin. Of 59 specimens, 51 have one to three pored scales on the caudal fin base (mean 1.72). In life, nuptial males show bright amber, canary-yellow and russet markings, which are absent in *P. sciera*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*P. aurolineata* lives in the main channels of rivers, as does *P. lenticula*. Collection sites were rivers 50–200 feet wide, with "white water" rapids 4 inches to 3 feet deep over bedrock, boulder, rubble and gravel. The river weed *Podostemum* occurred at all localities, and water willow (*Justicia*) formed extensive beds at the three Cahaba sites. No fish was seen within plant clumps. Water temperatures during sampling ranged from 12.5 to 25 °C.

The authors suggest the species once occurred throughout the upper Alabama drainage. Pollution and extensive impoundment have probably removed it from much of that range. Its known occurrence is restricted to areas above the [[Fall Line]].

Associated fishes at the type locality included about 12 species, while about 40 taxa were recorded from the Cahaba localities, including [[Percina Palmaris]], *Percina lenticula* and *Percina nigrofasciata*.

Habitat differences among darters were also noted. *P. sciera* tolerates a wider range of stream sizes and bottom types, including sand and silt where aquatic plants are absent. *P. nigrofasciata* is abundant in small Coastal Plain and Piedmont streams and, in rivers, is almost always found over bedrock, boulder, rubble or gravel where *Podostemum* or *Justicia* grows. At [[Talking Rock Creek]], *P. palmaris* dominated the riffles while *P. nigrofasciata* was common along the banks in still water, which the authors interpret as its winter habitat.

## Key Findings

**1. Taxonomic status and closest relative.** *P. aurolineata* is closest to *P. sciera*, and the two form a close group with *P. nigrofasciata*, distinct from *P. lenticula*. The authors interpret the complementary distributions of *P. aurolineata* and *P. sciera* as evidence that the new species is an eastern isolate of the *sciera* stock. It probably invaded the upper Alabama system from the Tennessee drainage when the two were connected. It is less heat-tolerant than *P. sciera*, which makes a return movement unlikely.

**2. Meristic and morphological differences.**
- Vertebral counts: *aurolineata* from the Coosawattee averages 42.4 and from the Cahaba averages 41.1. *P. lenticula* averages 42.9–43.5. *P. nigrofasciata* averages 40.5–40.7.
- Lateral-line scales: *P. aurolineata* averages 67.3 (Coosawattee) and 67.6 (Cahaba). *P. sciera* averages 63.3–66.1. *P. nigrofasciata* averages 55.0–59.2. *P. lenticula* has the highest values. *P. sciera* is nearly intermediate between *aurolineata* and *nigrofasciata*.
- Branchiostegal membrane ratio: holotype 0.86, ten paratypes 0.84–1.00 (mean 0.91). *P. lenticula* 0.87–1.05 (mean 0.94). *P. sciera* from the Tombigbee 0.76–1.00 (mean 0.87).
- Body proportions show no range overlap between *aurolineata* and *sciera* in caudal peduncle depth, least fleshy interorbital width, or upper jaw length.

**3. Colour pattern as a diagnostic.** *P. sciera* has well-defined median dorsal saddles, even in darkened nuptial males, and its dorsolateral blotches curve toward the occiput, often forming a V-shape. *P. aurolineata* lacks these saddles, has a single linear stripe that runs to the occiput, and shows bright yellow to russet nuptial colours.

**4. Geographic variation.** Coosawattee specimens have higher average counts of vertebrae, dorsal spines, dorsal soft rays, pectoral rays and anal soft rays, but fewer caudal peduncle scale rows. *P. lenticula* from the higher-elevation Etowah River shows a parallel pattern.

**5. Range extensions and co-occurrence.**
- *P. aurolineata* and *P. lenticula* co-occur in the Cahaba River and may co-occur elsewhere in the Alabama system.
- *P. sciera*, *P. lenticula* and *P. nigrofasciata* co-occur in the Pearl, Leaf and Tombigbee rivers.
- *P. sciera* and *P. nigrofasciata* co-occur in the Black Warrior River.
- Catch records are tabulated for these co-occurrences; the authors also list *P. palmaris* records from the Cartecay and Coosawattee sites.

**6. Hybridization at Pools Bluff.** Below the low dam at Pools Bluff Sill on the Pearl River, a sample of 46 night collections yielded:
- *P. nigrofasciata*: 28 specimens in 11 collections.
- *P. sciera*: 789 specimens in 29 collections.
- Hybrids: 11 specimens in four collections.

No *P. nigrofasciata* turned up in 37 monthly collections from a sand bar 1.4 miles east of Bogalusa, where 26 *P. sciera* were taken in ten collections. Seventy-three fish species have been recorded at Pools Bluff Sill.

**7. Conditions favouring hybridization.** The authors identify five conditions at Pools Bluff: (1) a barrier (dam); (2) restricted preferred spawning areas; (3) a presumably marginal habitat for *P. nigrofasciata*; (4) overlap in spawning time, which runs from mid-February to about mid-April; and (5) extreme relative abundance, with *P. nigrofasciata* scarce and *P. sciera* common.

**8. Hybrid intermediacy.** For lateral blotch shape (ratio of length to height), hybrids are intermediate between the parental species, as are their numbers of lateral blotches and scale counts. Counts of vertebrae and fin rays were too similar between the parents to test intermediacy. The authors note that earlier workers (Hubbs and Black, 1954; Crawford, 1956) found no evidence of intergradation. This study documents natural hybrids for the first time in these data.

**9. Seasonal and ecological notes.** *P. lenticula* adults typically occupy the deepest and fastest parts of rapids (one Leaf River site was recorded at 10 ft/s and 2.5 ft deep), which probably explains their scarcity in collections. High water in December 1965 at the Leaf River site limited sampling to the margins, which may explain the lack of *P. lenticula* there.
