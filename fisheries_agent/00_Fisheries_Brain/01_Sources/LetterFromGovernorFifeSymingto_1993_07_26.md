## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific paper but a **policy and administrative letter** from Arizona Governor Fife Symington to U.S. Secretary of the Interior Bruce Babbitt, dated July 26, 1993. It concerns the governance framework surrounding the **Glen Canyon Dam Draft Environmental Impact Statement (EIS)** and the associated [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]]. While the letter contains no direct morphological, physiological, or population data on fish species, it is highly relevant contextual material for [[Ichthyology]] and [[Aquatic Ecosystem Management]] research, as it documents the political and regulatory apparatus governing flow regimes, monitoring programs, and wildlife jurisdiction in the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] — a system inhabited by federally significant native and endemic fish taxa such as the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]].

The letter raises concerns about the future role of state agencies (specifically the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) in the **adaptive management process** that will determine dam operations following the Record of Decision, expected by the end of 1994. It also flags a jurisdictional dispute regarding state versus federal authority over wildlife permitting in the Grand Canyon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no species-specific ecological data are presented, the letter implicitly concerns the management of habitat conditions for the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem within the [[Grand Canyon]], which is shaped directly by the operational regime of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. Key ecologically relevant management elements referenced include:

- **Flow regulation**: Dam operations set by the Secretary of Interior (as of August 1992) govern downstream flow, temperature, and sediment regimes — all primary drivers of habitat suitability for native Colorado River fish and the basis of ongoing [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]].
- **Adaptive Management Process**: A formal federal process intended to generate recommendations for dam operations post-Record of Decision, with implications for long-term monitoring of aquatic and riparian resources.
- **Cooperating Agencies Forum**: A multi-stakeholder body (state, federal, Tribal) historically involved in balancing "environmental and economic demands," including presumably fish and wildlife conservation objectives, whose future authority is described as being at risk of curtailment.
- **Jurisdictional habitat authority**: Dispute over whether Arizona's [[Title 17]] wildlife statutes grant the state regulatory authority over activities affecting wildlife (including fish) within Grand Canyon lands, versus exclusive federal authority via the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[National Park Service]].

## Key Findings

- The **Glen Canyon Dam Draft Environmental Impact Statement** was slated for public release in late 1993, with a federal Record of Decision legally mandated by the end of 1994 under the [[Grand Canyon Protection Act]].
- Until the Record of Decision, [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations were governed by interim provisions established by the Secretary of the Interior in August 1992.
- The letter documents a **governance conflict**: the Department of Interior signaled that the **Cooperating Agencies** (including state wildlife agencies) would lose formal decision-making input after the Record of Decision, with future dam operation recommendations to be made exclusively by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[National Park Service]].
- This exclusion was justified via interpretation of the **Federal Advisory Committee Act (FACA)**, which the Governor disputes as an appropriate basis for removing state oversight of dam operations affecting shared wildlife resources.
- A second major conflict concerns **wildlife permitting authority**: the Interior Solicitor's Office asserted that state and Tribal permits are not required for activities in the Grand Canyon, directly challenging Arizona's claimed authority under [[Title 17]] statutes over wildlife (including fish) management.
- The Governor explicitly frames this as a precedent-setting conflict between state and federal authority with implications extending beyond Glen Canyon Dam to other federal lands in Arizona.
- No specific fish population, water quality, or flow-experiment data are included; the letter should be read as **procedural/political context** for the broader body of [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] scientific literature on native fish conservation (e.g., [[Humpback Chub]] recovery efforts) in the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River.
