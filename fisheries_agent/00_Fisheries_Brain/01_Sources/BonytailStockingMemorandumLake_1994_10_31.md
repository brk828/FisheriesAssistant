## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum detailing the third scheduled 1994 stocking event of the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) into the lower Colorado River system. The operation involved the transport and release of juvenile bonytail from the Dexter National Fish Hatchery to two distinct reservoir sites: [[Lake Mohave]] (via Willow Beach) and [[Lake Havasu]] (via Windsor Beach). The report focuses on logistics, fish condition upon release, and survival rates immediately following transport, serving as a critical record for the [[Bonytail Recovery Program]].

## Background & Description
While not a morphological study, the memo provides specific size-class data for the stocked cohort, relevant to tracking growth and survival rates in [[Repatriation Studies]]:
* **Size Range:** Predominantly small juveniles, averaging **2–3 inches** in length.
* **Size Outliers:** A minor subset of the population measured **4–6 inches**.
* **Condition:** The population was noted to be largely healthy at the time of release, with negligible size-based mortality observed during transport.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The document highlights the artificial reintroduction of a critically endangered fish into fragmented habitat remnants of its historic range.
* **Native Range Context:** The [[Bonytail Chub]] is native to the Colorado River Basin, historically adapted to swift, turbid, warm-water riverine environments.
* **Release Habitat:** The stocking occurred in reservoir "coves" (Twin Coves North and South) within [[Lake Havasu]], representing a lentic (still-water) habitat rather than the species' historic lotic (flowing-water) niche.
* **Secondary Site:** A portion of the cohort (~56,500 fish) was diverted to [[Lake Mohave]], where standard protocol involves "nose tagging" prior to release, likely for future tracking and mark-recapture population studies.
* **Management Context:** This action represents a proactive conservation intervention, indicative of the species' status as functionally extirpated in the wild without hatchery supplementation.

## Key Findings
* **Total Stocked Population:** An estimated 56,500 juvenile bonytail were initially transported from the Dexter hatchery.
* **Distribution Split:** Fish were split between Lake Mohave (destined for tagging) and Lake Havasu (immediate release).
* **Havasu Release Volume:** A total of 39,658 fish were released into Lake Havasu, split evenly between two sites:
    * Twin Coves North: ~19,829 fish
    * Twin Coves South: ~19,829 fish
* **Survival Rate:** Transport and release mortality was recorded at **less than 1%**, indicating high efficacy in the hatchery-to-wild transport protocol used by the [[Parker FRO]] (Fish Resource Office) and [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]].
* **Operational Timeline:** The entire logistics chain—from truck rendezvous at Kingman to fish being fully waterborne in the coves—was completed within a single day (0830 to 1030 hours), demonstrating an efficient rapid-deployment stocking model.
* **Interagency Collaboration:** The operation required coordination between the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM), and hatchery personnel, utilizing both Service and BLM pontoon boats for the final release phase.
