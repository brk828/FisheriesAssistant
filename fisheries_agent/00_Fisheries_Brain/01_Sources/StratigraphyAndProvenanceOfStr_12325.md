# Stratigraphy and Provenance of Strata Along the San Marcos Fault, Central Coahuila, Mexico

This document is a geological research paper, not an ichthyological or aquatic-ecosystem study. It contains no fish or other organismal data, so the aquatic taxon tags do not apply. The closest biological content is fossil fauna (ammonites, pelecypods, fusulinids, sponges) used only for dating and correlation.

## Executive Summary

McKee, Jones, and Long (Department of Geological Sciences, University of Texas at Austin, with Jones also affiliated with the University of Wisconsin-Oshkosh) use stratigraphy, petrology, geochemistry, and Rb-Sr geochronology to reconstruct the history of the [[San Marcos Fault]] in central Coahuila. They estimate up to 3,000 m of coarse Upper Jurassic and Lower Cretaceous detritus was shed from the south side of the fault, which was the uplifted northern margin of [[Coahuila Island]]. The principal source was the late Paleozoic, arc-proximal [[Las Delicias Basin]] and its flanking [[Las Delicias Arc]] and intrusions. The authors tie the fault's activity to transform motion along the [[Mojave-Sonora Megashear]] and to the Tithonian shift of seafloor spreading from the Gulf of Mexico to the Caribbean.

## Background & Description

**Objectives.** The authors aimed to (1) evaluate evidence for the San Marcos fault and its displacement history, (2) interpret Upper Jurassic and Lower Cretaceous stratigraphy in relation to displacement, and (3) present petrologic, chemical, and age data for the pre-Jurassic source rocks.

**Regional structures.** Several north-northwest lineaments in northeastern Mexico (the [[La Babia Fault]], [[Boquillas-Sabinas Lineament]], and [[Sabinas Fault]]) may be one structure. The San Marcos fault, striking about N62°W, is the northern margin of Coahuila Island. The authors chose the name because the fault is exposed in Valle San Marcos. Alternative names include the Sierra Mojada–China lineament, the Caltam lineament, and the Monclova fault. The paper also corrects Longoria (1985), who misidentified the San Marcos fault with a N40°W structure. Of the larger postulated features to the south (Saltillo-Torreón fracture zone, Torreón-Monterrey fault, Mojave-Sonora megashear), only the San Marcos fault has a clear displacement record.

**Stratigraphic units.** Key formations, with the authors' interpretations:
- **La Casita Formation** (Jurassic): includes the submarine Las Palomas and Sierra El Granizo beds, and the eolian Colorado beds and marine Tanque Cuatro Palmas beds.
- **[[San Marcos Formation]]** (Neocomian): coarse alluvial fan deposits, about 1,000 m thick at Valle San Marcos.
- **[[La Mula Formation]]**: finer, distal, mostly marine strata. The authors reserve the term for these and propose that the Sabinas Gulf depocenter be called the [[La Mula Basin]] to avoid confusion with the Late Cretaceous [[Sabinas Coal Basin]].
- **Padilla and Barril Viejo Formations**: a carbonate tongue that splits the San Marcos into upper and lower members.
- **[[La Virgen Formation]]**: sabkha evaporites.
- **[[Cupido Formation]]**: shelf carbonates marking the sea's final advance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paleoenvironments are marine and nonmarine, not aquatic-ecosystem data in the ecological sense:
- **Submarine slope and debris flows** (Las Palomas beds) were deposited below storm wave-base and contain polymictic boulder conglomerates.
- **Coastal eolian dunes** (Colorado beds, Potrero Colorado) reached tens of meters in height on the alluvial surface of Coahuila Island.
- **Wave-cut platforms** cut into eolian sands during the Jurassic transgression. The authors cite [[Bradley and Griggs]] (1976) for analogous platforms in California, several hundred meters wide.
- **Sabkha and lagoonal settings** produced salt hopper and desiccation features in the Las Palomas top and evaporites of the La Virgen Formation.
- **Fossil fauna** are used for age control and correlation, not ecology. Ammonites (*Proniceras* and *Substeueroceras*) indicate the Tithonian. Other records include pelecypods (*Lucina kellumi*-like forms), septarian concretion-bearing siltstones, fusulinids (*Polydiexodina*), and sponges (*Amblysiphonella*, *Guadalupia*).

Sediment dispersal runs from the south-southwest, as shown by pebble imbrication at Loma La Herradura and the composition of boulders.

## Key Findings

1. **Fault-proximal detritus volume.** As much as 3,000 m of coarse detritus was shed north from the San Marcos fault scarp. Valle San Marcos alone has nearly 3,000 m of fault-proximal Jurassic and Cretaceous detritus.

2. **Multiple displacement episodes.** Two Jurassic submarine conglomerate units (Las Palomas and Sierra El Granizo beds) record separate displacement pulses. The Tanque Cuatro Palmas and Colorado beds record Tithonian inactivity. Neocomian rejuvenation produced the San Marcos Formation. A mid-Neocomian carbonate tongue (Padilla and Barril Viejo) marks a pause in faulting.

3. **Provenance.** Distinctive clasts (turbidite components, spotted schist, fusulinid-bearing limestone, hemipelagic limestone) match the Las Delicias basin and [[Coahuila Island]]. No major source change is documented from the lowest to highest exposed units, so the source area did not change much over the roughly 3,000 m of deposition.

4. **Triassic granitic source.** Rb-Sr data from granite cobbles at Sierra Mojada yield an isochron age of 225 ± 4 Ma, interpreted as Triassic crystallization. Valle San Marcos gneiss gives 230 ± 3 Ma (initial ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr = 0.7082 ± 0.0005). The Potrero de La Mula pluton is 213 ± 14 Ma. The authors compare these to a K-Ar biotite age of 208 ± 4 Ma from the Acatita tonalite (Denison and others, 1969).

5. **Devonian clasts.** Two schist cobbles from Potrero Colorado give model ages of 370 ± 4.5 Ma and 387 ± 4 Ma, older than any other dated rock in the region. The authors infer an older, unidentified terrane near the source area.

6. **Rancho Australia microdiorite.** This alkalic rock was not dated successfully because the radiogenic Sr was swamped by common Sr. Its composition suggests a link to Cenozoic igneous rocks rather than pre-Jurassic basement.

7. **Chemistry.** Eight igneous samples are mostly calc-alkaline, while the Rancho Australia sample is alkaline. Alteration is widespread, so the authors stress that major-element chemistry supports field identifications.

8. **Tectonic model.** The authors suggest the San Marcos fault was part of the transform system linking Gulf of Mexico and Pacific spreading ridges (per [[Klitgord and Schouten]], 1986). Jurassic displacement ended near the Tithonian magnetic anomaly M-21, when spreading shifted to the Caribbean. Early Neocomian displacement may reflect isostatic adjustment.

9. **Laramide and post-Laramide overprint.** Tertiary (Laramide) reverse movement raised Jurassic strata against Cretaceous ones. Left-lateral, post-Laramide thrusting at Sierra Mojada involves Tertiary Ahuichila alluvium.

10. **Nomenclature.** The authors recommend "San Marcos Formation" over "Arkose" for the coarse Neocomian body and recommend "La Mula Formation" for its distal equivalent. They do not acknowledge the La Gloria or Zuloaga formations at Valle San Marcos because no such limestone has been located there.
