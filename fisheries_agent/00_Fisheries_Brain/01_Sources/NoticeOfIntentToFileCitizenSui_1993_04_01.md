## Executive Summary

This document is a **legal notice letter** (not a primary scientific study) sent by attorneys representing the Southwestern Water Conservation District and the Animas-La Plata Water Conservancy District to the U.S. Secretary of the Interior and the Director of the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service. The letter provides sixty-day notice of intent to file a citizen suit under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA), alleging that the Service failed to comply with the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) when designating **critical habitat** for four endangered fish species native to the Colorado River Basin: the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (referred to in the original text as "Colorado squawfish"), [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]] (collectively termed the "Endangered Fish"). While the document contains no direct morphological, physiological, or population-data science, it is scientifically relevant in that it identifies the taxa involved, their listed status, and the specific river systems designated as critical habitat.

## Background & Description

No morphological, anatomical, or physiological data for the four species are provided in this source. The document is strictly regulatory/legal in nature. For species-level morphological detail, cross-reference dedicated taxonomic sources for:
- [[Razorback Sucker]] — large-bodied catostomid, named for its pronounced dorsal keel.
- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] — largest North American cyprinid, historically an apex piscivore in the basin.
- [[Bonytail Chub]] — streamlined cyprinid adapted to swift-water riverine habitats.
- [[Humpback Chub]] — cyprinid distinguished by a pronounced nuchal hump, adapted to canyon-bound whitewater habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat designation**: The Service proposed critical habitat encompassing the **San Juan, Yampa, Colorado, and Green Rivers**, all major tributaries/mainstems within the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Ecological context of listing**: All four species are large-river endemics historically associated with warm, turbid, free-flowing riverine conditions; their imperilment is broadly linked to flow alteration, impoundment, and water development within these river systems (implied by the letter's focus on the economic impact of habitat designation on water development projects).
- **Regulatory niche**: The designation of critical habitat under 16 U.S.C. § 1533 is positioned as a "major federal action" with substantial implications for water resource management in southwestern Colorado and the broader Colorado River Basin, reflecting the intersection of species ecology and regional water allocation policy.
- Related concepts: [[Critical Habitat Designation]], [[Colorado River Basin]], [[Water Resource Development]], [[San Juan River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Green River]].

## Key Findings

- The four species collectively termed the "Endangered Fish" — [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]] — were subject to a proposed critical habitat rule published in the Federal Register on **January 29, 1991**.
- The Service asserted that it need not prepare an Environmental Assessment (EA) when promulgating rules under Section 4(a) of the ESA, a position contested by the Water Districts as lacking statutory basis.
- Precedent cited: **Pacific Legal Foundation v. Andrus** (657 F.2d 829, 1981) — Sixth Circuit held no EA required for *listing* an endangered species.
- Contrasting precedent: **Douglas County v. Lujan** (Civil No. 91-6423-HO, D. Or., Dec. 22, 1992) — held that the EA/NEPA exemption does *not* extend to *critical habitat designation*, reasoning that "the NEPA process serves the purpose of ESA."
- The Secretary of the Interior has a non-discretionary statutory duty under 16 U.S.C. § 1533(b)(2) to consider **economic and other relevant impacts** (social, biological, administrative) when designating or excluding critical habitat.
- The letter frames the scale of proposed critical habitat across the San Juan, Yampa, Colorado, and Green Rivers as carrying "enormous" economic impact on water development in southwestern Colorado and statewide.
- No population counts, abundance trends, or biological survey data for the four fish species are presented in this document; its significance is primarily as a legal/administrative record concerning the regulatory history of critical habitat designation for these Colorado River Basin endangered fishes.
