# Rock Creek Fish Barrier Feasibility Investigation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This feasibility report, prepared by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (Phoenix Area Office) with the [[Tonto National Forest]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], evaluates a reinforced-concrete [[Fish Barrier]] on [[Rock Creek]] in Gila County, Arizona. The barrier would keep [[Nonnative Fishes]] out of a stream that could serve as a replication site for the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]]. The work is part of conservation measures offsetting nonnative aquatic organism transfers into the Gila River basin under the [[Central Arizona Project]]. The preferred site is a solid bedrock reach about 2,000 feet upstream of the Spring Creek confluence. The feasibility-level construction estimate is $201,000 (computed total $200,622). Access is limited to helicopter delivery. The report recommends proceeding with design and construction scheduling, subject to [[National Environmental Policy Act]], [[Endangered Species Act]], and [[Clean Water Act]] compliance.

## Background & Description

**Purpose.** Reclamation aims to protect high-priority streams that can prevent extinction of, stabilize, or replicate rare native fish stocks. Rock Creek was selected from six Tonto Creek drainage streams investigated in April and May 2006: Greenback Creek, Gun Creek, Houston Creek, Spring Creek, [[Buzzard Roost Canyon]], and Rock Creek. Selection criteria were biological value for native fishes, length of stream protected behind a barrier, physical site characteristics, and other considerations.

**Stream character.** Rock Creek is a perennial tributary of [[Spring Creek]], which flows into [[Tonto Creek]]. It is ungaged; streamflow during the October 2006 site visit was estimated at about 1 cubic foot per second. About 3 miles of perennial stream lie upstream of the study area. Buzzard Roost Canyon is a perennial tributary of Rock Creek. The stream has not been renovated.

**Fish community.** The report lists the following species:
- Nonnative: [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*)
- Native populations: [[Headwater Chub]] (*Gila nigra*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*)
- Possibly present: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)

The target species are the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), using a White River population, and an undetermined population of [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Location.** The site lies in the SE¼ of Section 1, Township 8 North, Range 12 East, on the USGS Buzzard Roost Mesa quadrangle, between Payson and Young. It is at about 4,400 ft elevation, roughly 2,000 ft upstream of the Rock Creek–Spring Creek confluence and about 40 ft higher in elevation. UTM coordinates are approximately 3,768,600 N, 493,200 E (latitude 34°3'0" N; the longitude in the scan is partly illegible, reported as about 111°3' W).

**Channel.** Channel and gravel alluvium are interrupted by bedrock intrusions. The site has solid rock channel and banks, swept clean of alluvium. Surveys covered biological, NEPA, and engineering characteristics.

**Hydraulics and flooding.** The design flood is likely the 100-year flood. After several floods, sediment is expected to fill the upstream side to the crest, raising the local water surface. No improvements would be affected, and due to the steep channel gradient, backwater effects should not extend far upstream.

**Access.** No roads reach the site. The nearest roads end about 2 miles away across difficult terrain. Road construction was judged too costly and disruptive, so helicopters would be needed.

## Key Findings

**Barrier design**
- Reinforced concrete structure with a 4-foot drop and a sloping splash pad or apron.
- Total height above existing thalweg about 5 ft; crest length about 10 ft.
- Anchor bars drilled and grouted into bedrock to tie the structure to the channel and abutments.
- A center notch passes base flow and flows up to about 15 cfs, so base flow achieves the full 4-foot drop and hits the downstream apron.
- A small channel on the left abutment would be sealed; an outlet hole was also considered.
- The splash pad prevents a plunge pool that could aid fish leaping and keeps shallow, high-velocity flow below the barrier.
- Aesthetic treatment (coloring, texturing) is possible and must be coordinated with Tonto National Forest.

**Construction logistics**
- Helicopter delivery is required. A Bell Long Ranger (load capacity of about 800 to 1,000 lb) is assumed, and the final choice rests with the contractor.
- Concrete could come from Payson or Young (two plants at Young). Trucks from Young would drive about 5 miles on gravel roads to a helicopter staging area roughly 2 miles from the site. Concrete from Payson would pass through Young or stage near Tonto Creek, about 13 air miles away.
- Expected on-site equipment: helicopter, generators, pumps, rock drills, power saws and hand tools, and concrete vibrators. Generators and fuel must stay above flood levels to prevent hydrocarbon release.
- A work camp must be sited where the helicopter can drop supplies, pending agreement with the Forest Service.

**Material weights (assumed)**
- Concrete: 6 yd³ × 4,000 lb/cy = 24,000 lb
- Rebar and anchors: 1,700 lb
- Forming materials: 1,000 lb
- Miscellaneous tools, generators, pumps: 4,000 lb
- Total: 30,800 lb

**Environmental compliance**
- **NEPA:** Requires draft and final Environmental Assessments and possibly public meetings. Estimated cost is about $50,000, with Tonto National Forest as co-lead. The duration figure in the source is illegible.
- **ESA:** Requires a Biological Assessment. Consultation with FWS is expected to go smoothly, as it did for Reclamation's Aravaipa Creek and Fossil Creek barriers. Estimated time is 3 to 6 months, and cost is about $20,000.
- **CWA Section 404:** Requires a jurisdictional delineation from the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and a 404 permit application ([[Section 404 Permit]]). Mitigation for impacts to "waters of the U.S." was already completed through Reclamation's acquisition of stream/riparian habitat along the [[San Pedro River]]. Processing takes 3 to 6 months, at an estimated cost of about $10,000.

**Recommendations**
- Rock Creek is a strong candidate for a barrier, given its biological value and potential as a replication stream for loach minnow and spikedace.
- The site has stable bedrock and permits a relatively small structure. Its main drawback is the lack of access, which raises costs but also limits human influence.
- Reclamation supports proceeding with investigations and recommends developing a design and construction schedule.
