## Executive Summary

This study by Daniel W. Coble investigates the physiological basis of **false annulus** (accessory check) formation on the scales of [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*). Adult bluegill were held in laboratory tanks at 24°C from November 21, 1967 to February 2, 1968, under controlled feeding regimes. The experiment tested two independent hypotheses for false annulus formation: (1) diet restriction/starvation, and (2) handling stress (anesthesia, measuring, weighing, scale sampling) during otherwise heavy feeding. Results showed that fish starved completely for **10 days or more** reliably formed false annuli, while fish given a reduced (but nonzero) ration during restriction periods generally did not. Separately, fish that were **handled** midway through the experiment—despite continuous heavy feeding—also formed false annuli, implicating physiological stress responses (e.g., elevated oxygen consumption, energy diversion) rather than food availability alone. The positional correspondence between treatment timing and false annulus location on the scale (measured via anterior scale radius) provided strong evidence linking specific stressors to specific circulus disruptions. The paper concludes with a broader discussion of confounding factors reported in the literature — temperature, disease, dissolved oxygen, and photoperiod — that may independently or interactively drive false annulus formation in wild bluegill populations.

## Background & Description

- **Scale structure**: The bony layer of a fish scale is a collagen matrix (circuli) that calcifies after formation (per [[Wallin 1957]]). Circulus formation/calcification proceeds from the anterior end of the scale posteriorly; multiple circuli may be at different completion stages simultaneously.
- **True annulus formation**: Occurs when scale growth rate declines/stops, then resumes—creating a new circulus that cuts across previously incomplete circuli.
- **False annulus (accessory check) identification criteria**:
  - Out of place within the overall scale growth pattern.
  - Not as well-formed/complete as a true annulus.
  - Circuli **inside** the false annulus are more widely spaced than those just **outside** it (per [[Burress 1949]]).
  - Often accompanied by a thin dark line marking its position.
  - In this study, false annuli were most discernible at the **anterior end** and **antero-lateral corners** of the scale; not detectable in the lateral fields or posterior half.
- **Growth measurement**: Anterior scale radius was used to precisely localize the false annulus relative to the portion of scale grown during the experiment; widely spaced circuli marked the region of rapid growth formed during the study period.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study population**: Adult bluegill collected from drained ponds at Missouri Department of Conservation's Little Dixie Wildlife Area (October 1967), held in outdoor stock tanks, then moved to indoor fiberglass troughs (50 × 24 cm compartments) at a controlled mean temperature of 24°C (SD 1.5°C).
- **Diet**: Mealworms (*Tenebrio molitor*), ration scaled from 10/day up to 30/day (~3% body weight/day for a 100g fish), with individualized reductions for fish that would not consume the full ration.
- **Relevance to wild populations**: False annuli are common on bluegill scales in natural populations and complicate age determination, sometimes indistinguishable from true annuli ([[Bennett 1948]]). The author argues that complete food deprivation for 10+ days is unlikely in wild bluegill, suggesting food scarcity alone is probably not the dominant cause of false annuli in nature. Alternative ecological drivers discussed include:
  - **Low dissolved oxygen** — diel fluctuations common in bluegill habitats in summer; documented in prior studies with false annuli occurrence (Bennett, Thompson & Parr 1940; Sprugel 1952, 1954; Carlander & Sprugel 1955), including vegetation-clogged marsh habitats prone to DO swings.
  - **Photoperiod** — linked to reproductive cycles, growth, and food conversion efficiency in [[Green Sunfish]] and coregonids ([[Hogman 1968]]); true annuli formed near spring equinox and false annuli near fall equinox in coregonids independent of temperature or feeding changes.
  - **Temperature** — reduced food consumption/conversion efficiency documented at low temps in bluegill ([[Anderson 1959]]), though no evidence of temperature involvement in this specific experiment.
  - **Disease** — commonly reduces appetite but not implicated in this or related studies.
  - **Spawning checks** — some false annuli misattributed to spawning, but occurrence in immature fish argues against this in several studies (Bennett, Thompson & Parr 1940; Burress 1949; Hogman 1968; Regier 1962).

## Key Findings

- **Handling-induced false annuli**: Among bluegill fed heavily throughout the entire experiment, those handled midway (anesthetized, measured, weighed, scale-sampled) formed false annuli; unhandled fish did not (Table 1).
- **Starvation threshold**: Among fish subjected to a period of zero food, those starved ≥10 days reliably formed false annuli (5 of 6, excluding one anomalous case); shorter starvation periods (3–6 days) did not produce false annuli.
- **Restricted (non-zero) ration largely ineffective**: Fish receiving a reduced ration (3 mealworms/day, ~1/10 max ration) during the restriction period generally did NOT form false annuli, with one exception (a fish fasted for 10 days on the restricted ration did form one).
- **Temporal correlation (Figure 1)**: The position of the false annulus along the anterior scale radius correlated strongly with the timing of the causal treatment (handling or starvation) within the heavy-feeding period:
  - Handled-only fish: false annulus formed at ~37.5% through the experimental scale growth, corresponding to 43% of the heavy feeding period elapsed before handling.
  - 21-day starved fish: false annulus formed at ~65% through scale growth, corresponding to 61% of the heavy feeding period elapsed before starvation began.
  - 10- and 15-day restricted fish: false annulus formed near the midpoint of experimental scale growth, consistent with restriction occurring about halfway through the heavy feeding period.
- **Growth not a reliable predictor**: False annulus formation was not clearly or consistently related to overall fish growth rate (instantaneous growth rate G); a fish could grow well overall yet still form a false annulus if growth was interrupted and resumed.
- **Mechanistic interpretation**: False annuli likely result from either (a) the fish not eating for a sufficient period, or (b) the fish eating but not allocating energy to scale growth (e.g., due to handling stress elevating oxygen consumption per [[Fry 1957]], diverting energy away from growth processes).
- **Consistency with prior literature**: Findings align with unpublished experiments by Bruno von Limbach (cited in Bennett 1948), where bluegill starved ≥3 weeks between feeding periods formed "clear false annuli," and with [[Van Oosten 1961]] on starvation-induced accessory annuli in young whitefish (*Coregonus clupeaformis*).
- **Contrast with Hogman (1968)**: In coregonids, false annuli formed seasonally (fall) tied to photoperiod even without observed changes in feeding or growth deviation, suggesting multiple independent pathways to false annulus formation across taxa.
- **Conclusion**: No single factor (food restriction, handling/stress, temperature, disease, dissolved oxygen, or photoperiod) fully explains false annulus formation across all studies; the phenomenon likely arises from any disruption—nutritional or physiological—that halts or slows scale-matrix formation and calcification, followed by renewed growth.
