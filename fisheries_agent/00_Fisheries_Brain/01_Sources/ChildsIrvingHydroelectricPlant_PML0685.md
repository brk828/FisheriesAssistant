# Razorback Sucker Biological Report: Childs & Irving Hydroelectric Plant Relicensing, Fossil Creek, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This biological report, prepared by [[EnviroNet Inc]] for [[Arizona Public Service]] in October 1998, supports the [[Federal Energy Regulatory Commission]] (FERC) relicensing of the [[Childs & Irving Hydroelectric Plant]] on [[Fossil Creek]], about 5 miles southwest of Strawberry, Arizona. It responds to a request from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] regarding potential impacts to the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), listed as endangered in 1991.

Razorback suckers are not native to Fossil Creek. Roughly 10,000 young-of-year fish were accidentally introduced into Fossil Creek and Fossil Springs in October 1988, intended for [[Stehr Lake]]. The report's conclusion is that the project is expected to have **no effect** on the species, and that Fossil Creek has essentially no potential for razorback sucker recovery. The proposed action is not expected to adversely modify [[Critical Habitat]] along the [[Verde River]].

The findings rest mainly on a value-based expert opinion process: six experts were questioned and their responses compiled. The report also addresses the no-action baseline, the proposed minimum flow releases, and a full-flow decommissioning alternative, the latter provided for FERC's NEPA analysis.

## Background & Description

**Legal status.** The razorback sucker was listed as endangered by the federal government in 1991 and is classed as "Wildlife of Special Concern in Arizona" by the state. Critical habitat was designated in 1994 for 15 reaches of the Colorado River system. In the Verde River, it covers the river and its 100-year floodplain from the Prescott National Forest to Horseshoe Dam, including the Childs reach and the Fossil Creek mouth. Fossil Creek itself is not designated critical habitat.

**Species description.** The razorback sucker is one of the largest catostomids in western North America, reaching lengths near one meter and weights of 5–6 kg. Historically it was abundant in mainstem Colorado basin rivers, using backwaters, sloughs, oxbows, and floodplains. Adults in the upper basin spawn over gravel and cobble bars in mainstem rivers and major tributaries in spring, and larvae drift downstream into floodplain nursery habitat.

**Historical setting.** Razorback suckers were once found in the Verde River upstream to Perkinsville. They were extirpated from the Verde system before 1960, and the last Verde collection was from Peck's Lake in 1954. The species was never historically recorded from Fossil Creek or Fossil Springs. Earlier surveys, including [[Chamberlain]] (1904) and [[Neve]] (1976), recorded no razorback suckers.

**Introduction history.** Stocking of young-of-year fish in the Verde began in 1981 under a 10-year Memorandum of Understanding between USFWS and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD). From September 1981 to September 1983, 20,650 young-of-year fish were released in the Verde near Childs. Fish intended for Stehr Lake were inadvertently released into Fossil Creek in October 1988. Stocking since the late 1990s has used fish averaging about 300 mm.

**Study approach.** The recovery question was addressed through a [[Value-Based Approach]] (Delphi technique, interdisciplinary approach, consensus building). Six experts were selected from USFWS, USFS, AGFD, and EnviroNet lists: [[Dean Blinn]], [[Dean Hendrickson]], [[Paul Holden]], [[Paul Marsh]], [[William Miller]], and [[John Rinne]]. Each answered a questionnaire on existing conditions, recovery potential, and habitat impacts under three flow scenarios.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fossil Creek fish community.** AGFD surveys (1994–1996) documented five native species below the diversion dam: [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], and [[Longfin Dace]]. Nonnative fish include [[Green Sunfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Yellow Bullhead]]. Crayfish are abundant.

**Fossil Springs.** Springs maintain a constant temperature of about 72°F year-round. The water is clear and carbonate-rich, and deposits [[Travertine]] that coats substrate and removes nutrients from solution.

**Verde River confluence.** The Childs plant discharges about 41 cfs into the Verde. The tailrace supports vegetation and some cover, but the flow is strong and offers little juvenile habitat. The Fossil Creek mouth consists of a cobble/boulder bar with pool and run habitat. Green sunfish were abundant in the mouth pools. No backwaters, side channels, or downstream nursery habitat were identified.

**Environmental flows.** The baseline is about 2.2 cfs in the diversion reach. The proposed action adds 10 cfs in the Irving reach and 5 cfs in the Childs reach. The full-flow alternative returns about 43 cfs baseflow.

## Key Findings

**Expert consensus on recovery.** Experts generally agree that Fossil Creek has essentially no potential for razorback sucker recovery at any flow regime. Four of five experts rated recovery potential as zero or non-existent in the final question. The limiting factors identified include:
- lack of required habitats, linked to channel morphology
- inadequate food resources and lack of extensive vegetative cover
- flashy hydrograph and steep gradient
- lack of spawning substrate and suitable spawning conditions
- lack of recruitment potential and no historical habitat
- presence of nonnative predators and competition with native suckers
- razorback suckers not collected in creeks this small
- lack of persistence of stocked fish, and poor recovery success in the Verde and Salt Rivers

**Persistence of stocked fish.** The last confirmed record was one individual seen by Hendrickson in November 1989. [[Barrett]] (1990, thesis 1992) collected 13 sub-adults in 1990 but no larvae or juveniles. AGFD surveys from 1994–1996 found no razorback suckers. Experts generally conclude the 1988 stocking no longer persists.

**Responses to the recovery questions.**
- **Larvae/juvenile survival:** Four of five experts report no or low potential, with predation by nonnatives the main concern.
- **Adult survival:** Four of five report low to no potential. One expert (Holden) rated potential as fair to good, citing adult survival in captivity.
- **Spawning and recruitment:** Four of five report very low potential, with constant spring temperatures, marginal substrate, and peak flows as limits.
- **Sustaining a population for 10 years:** Four of five report no to low potential.
- **Nonnative effects:** High or great impact on survival, recruitment, and space, with food competition considered less certain.
- **Nonnative habitat expansion:** Low to considerable, depending on flow scenario. Full flows could favor catfish and smallmouth bass.
- **Food base:** Three experts said the food base is inadequate for all life stages. Travertine deposition removes nutrients and reduces invertebrate habitat.
- **Recreation:** Low impact under existing and minimum flows. Full flows could increase recreation and introduction risks.
- **Travertine:** Opinions are mixed. Four of five experts believe deposition would reduce spawning habitat by coating cobbles and gravels.
- **Overall recovery:** Essentially no potential at any flow regime. Rinne alone rated full-flow recovery potential as "very high" for the native fish community, while stating that the razorback is not a meaningful focus.

**Dissenting views.** Dr. Blinn noted that discharge is not the primary issue, and that predation by nonnatives and crayfish is a far greater threat. Dr. Holden expected that spawning and larval survival might be possible under some conditions, but later clarified that this is unlikely. Dr. Rinne stated that restoring full flow would be a unique opportunity for a native fish community, while noting that the razorback recovery question is a poor framework for decisions.

**Verde River critical habitat.** Critical habitat in the Childs reach includes the 100-year floodplain, which was estimated from the 1993 floods. The Fossil Creek mouth provides all three primary constituent elements (water, physical habitat, and biological environment), but no downstream nursery habitat was identified. The report found that the proposed action would result in no adverse modification of critical habitat.

**Anticipated impacts.**
- *Proposed action:* No effect on the species or its recovery, and no change in Fossil Creek habitat beyond a 10 cfs release at the dam. Transferring 3 cfs from Childs to the confluence is expected to have no detectable effect.
- *No action:* No change. Recovery potential is considered non-existent.
- *Decommissioning (full flow, 43 cfs):* Expected to bring no improvement in recovery potential. Travertine dams may build up over about 2 miles of channel near Irving, and the Childs tailrace would be lost. Fish movement from Childs to the confluence is possible, and nonnative fish may move upstream from the Verde.

**Conclusion.** The report concludes that the proposed action would have no effect on the continued survival or recovery of the razorback sucker, and no adverse modification of critical habitat. Its reasoning rests largely on expert opinion. Several experts cited limited verifiable data and noted that predictions of travertine effects and flow responses are highly uncertain.
