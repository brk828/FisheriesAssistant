# Benthos of South Lake, St Lucia After a Period of Stable Salinities, and the Life Cycle of Echinoparyphium montgomeriana

#marine-biology #benthic-invertebrates #parasitology

## Executive Summary

This document is a two-part scientific journal extract (*South African Journal of Zoology*, 1983, vol. 18, pp. 311–325). The first paper, by Blaber, Kure, Jackson and Cyrus, reports a year-long monthly survey of the [[Benthic Invertebrates]] of [[South Lake]], [[St Lucia Estuary]], conducted from August 1981 to July 1982 after several years of stable salinities near 35‰. The second paper, by Appleton, Donnelly and Eriksson, describes a new trematode species, *Echinoparyphium montgomeriana*, from [[Durban]] and documents its larval stages in a freshwater snail that is also an intermediate host of bilharzia parasites. The benthos paper is the main body of work; the trematode paper is partly available (the text is cut off in the Methods section).

## Background & Description

**Benthos survey (Blaber et al. 1983).** St Lucia is the largest estuarine system in southern Africa. Earlier benthic surveys covered 1948–1951 (Day, Millard & Broekhuysen 1954), 1964–1965 (Millard & Broekhuysen 1970) and 1972–1973 (Boltt 1975). Salinities in the South Lake compartment have been more stable than in the rest of the system, where values above 100‰ have been recorded. The survey was carried out alongside a study of the [[Feeding Ecology of Benthic Fish]] and was restricted to four sites where fish could also be collected. Benthic invertebrates are an important food source for fish, birds and reptile predators.

**Trematode paper (Appleton et al. 1983).** *Echinoparyphium montgomeriana* was first described as a cercaria by Taplin (1964) from *Bulinus tropicus* and *Bulinus forskali* at Montgomery Park, Johannesburg. Its cercariae have two unusual features: a brush of needle-like spines in the prepharyngeal sac, and branched excretory canals. The present paper describes the adult fluke and its eggs, and records prevalence in the [[Bulinus africanus]] population of the Amanzimtoti River. The adult lives in the alimentary tract of birds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study sites and substrata.** Samples were taken monthly at about 1 m depth at four sites in South Lake. The two eastern sites (Nkazana and Game Guard Camp) were sandy, and the two western sites (Gilly's Point and Makakatana) were muddy. Sampling used a Zabolocki-type [[Ekman Grab]] with a surface area of 0,0236 m² to 4,5 cm depth, with four grabs per sample. Samples were sieved (1 mm, then 0,5 mm), preserved in 10% formalin, stained with Phloxin, dried at 60 °C and weighed to 0,01 mg. Molluscs were weighed shell-free after dissolving the shells in nitric acid.

**Salinity.** Salinity varied little during the sampling period, between 31 and 44,5‰, close to seawater (35‰). Salinity peaked at 44,5‰ in February 1982. Before the survey, salinity had been below 35‰ for several years, and 1972–1973 had salinities above 55‰.

**Trophic role.** [[Assiminea]] is eaten by mullet (Mugilidae; Blaber 1976) and by the mud crab *Scylla serrata* (Hill 1979), and is probably taken by benthic fish such as *Rhabdosargus sarba*. The mysid *Mesopodopsis africana* is part of the zooplankton, so its benthic counts are underestimated.

**Snail-parasite system.** *Bulinus africanus* in the Amanzimtoti River (about 30 km south of Durban) is the intermediate host of *Schistosoma mattheei*, the bilharzia parasite of cattle (Donnelly 1981). Echinostome infections were always more prevalent than schistosome infections in this snail population, which raises the possibility of interspecific competition for the limited snail resource.

## Key Findings

### Benthos of South Lake (1981–1982)

- **Overall biomass:** The yearly mean standing crop was 2,63 g m⁻² (dry mass). Muddy substrata averaged 4,19 g m⁻² and sandy substrata 1,07 g m⁻². No definite seasonal pattern in total biomass was found.
- **Dominant species:** The bivalve *Solen cylindraceus* and the polychaete *Marphysa macintoshi* contributed most of the standing stock. *Solen* peaked in summer at the muddy Gilly's Point site, and *Marphysa* had major peaks in autumn and winter and low values in summer.
- **Taxa richness:** 37 taxa were recorded, compared with 23 in 1972–1973. Taxa found at all four sites included *Solen cylindraceus*, *Dosinia hepatica*, *Marphysa macintoshi*, *Glycera tridactyla*, *Dendronereis arborifera*, *Hymenosoma orbiculare*, *Apseudes digitalis*, *Grandidierella lignorum*, Cumacea and *Mesopodopsis africana*. Fourteen taxa were rare.
- **Substratum preferences:** Cumacea were more numerous on sand, while *Apseudes digitalis*, Nemertea, *Solen* and *Dendronereis* were more abundant in mud. *Grandidierella lignorum* was more common on mud than sand in St Lucia, although earlier work at Lake Sibaya suggested the opposite.
- **Sampling limitations:** *Solen cylindraceus*, *Hymenosoma orbiculare* and *Mesopodopsis africana* are likely underestimated because of their mobility and grab avoidance. *Brachidontes virgiliae* is underestimated because it lives in weed beds.

### Changes Since 1972–1973

- **Assiminea collapse:** *Assiminea* fell from more than 1 000 animals m⁻² in 1972–1973 to fewer than 10 m⁻² in 1981–1982, though many empty shells were found. *Nassarius kraussianus* showed a similar decline.
- **Bivalve and polychaete shifts:** *Solen* increased from fewer than 10 m⁻² to more than 200 m⁻². *Prionospio sexoculata* and Nematoda almost disappeared. The authors suggest this may reflect interspecific interactions with larger forms such as Nemertea, *Marphysa* and *Glycera*.
- **Biomass comparison:** On sandy substrata, biomass ranged from 1,08 to 3,26 g m⁻² in 1972–1973 and from 0,56 to 3,23 g m⁻² in 1981–1982 (mean 1,07 g m⁻²). On muddy substrata, values rose from 0,013–0,235 g m⁻² to 1,07–8,55 g m⁻² (mean 4,19 g m⁻²). The overall mean rose from 0,6 g m⁻² (Boltt 1975) to 2,63 g m⁻².
- **Interpretation:** The authors suggest the lake was recolonised after 1973 by rapidly maturing forms, which were then gradually replaced by slower-growing species such as *Solen* and *Dosinia* as conditions stayed stable.

### Species Diversity

- The Shannon-Wiener index (H) was highest at Gilly's Point (H = 2,45, SE = 0,09). The two eastern sites were similar (H = 1,34 and 1,46). The lowest value was at Makakatana (H = 1,11).
- Gilly's Point and Makakatana had the same species count (28), so the difference in diversity came from evenness of individuals among species rather than from species number. The authors attribute the difference to substratum: Makakatana has small rocks and some macrophytes, while Gilly's Point is uniform mud.

### Echinoparyphium montgomeriana (Appleton et al. 1983)

- **New species:** *E. montgomeriana* is described as a new species from Durban. Its adult fluke is a parasite of the alimentary tract of birds.
- **Hosts:** Larval stages are common in freshwater planorbid snails, especially *Bulinus africanus*, the intermediate host of *S. haematobium* and *S. mattheei* in the area.
- **Seasonal cycle:** The intra-molluscan stages show a seasonal transmission cycle.
- **Methods:** Adults were recovered from feral pigeons (*Columba livia*) fed infected snails 15–29 days earlier. Eggs were recovered from bird faeces using the formol-ether concentration technique (Allen & Ridley 1970). Measurements were taken to 0,001 mm.
- **Prevalence:** Adult and cercarial prevalence was recorded in the *B. africanus* population of the Amanzimtoti River over 21 months. The text provided ends before the prevalence results are reported.
