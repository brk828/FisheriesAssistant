# Literature Review of Historic Razorback Sucker Work at Senator Wash Reservoir, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This final report, prepared by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] Research Branch under a [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] cooperative agreement, compiles and synthesizes the unpublished historic surveys, studies and correspondence on [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Senator Wash Reservoir]]. Razorback suckers have existed in the reservoir since its completion in 1966, but most data from the 1970s onward were never published. The review covers reservoir regulation, stocking, spawning studies, genetics, telemetry, habitat, water quality and raw sampling records, and it draws on about 215 cited documents arranged chronologically in a bibliography. The authors conclude that Senator Wash Reservoir held the most abundant California population of the species, but recruitment past the larval stage was essentially absent, and the adult population was small, aging and at risk.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large catostomid (sucker) of the lower [[Colorado River]] basin, listed as endangered.
- **Study area:** [[Senator Wash Reservoir]], a pump-storage and irrigation-water reservoir completed in 1966 in Imperial County, California, operated by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] for irrigation of farm fields. Its lowest operating level is 210 ft elevation and maximum pool is 251 ft.
- **Associated sites:** [[West Pond]] (experimental native fish population and grow-out site, established 1974 for [[California Black Rail]] habitat), the [[Blythe Municipal Golf Course Pond]] (grow-out facility, 1983–1989), [[Squaw Lake]] (field base and telemetry site), and the [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]].
- **Scope of the document:** A literature review and synthesis (Abstract, Synthesis of Historic Surveys, Razorback Sucker Predictive Growth Curves, Raw Data, Study Proposals, and an annotated bibliography of about 215 items).
- **Funding and partners:** [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (CDFG), [[U.S. Bureau of Land Management]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Water and Power Resources Service]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir operation:** Water levels fluctuate seasonally and daily. Maximum drawdown and pump-up are six feet per day, and pump-up is restricted to 1000–1800 hours (non-peak power). Low levels (210 ft) occur at least twice a year during summer and winter irrigation demand. Trash racks with four- to six-inch openings were installed to limit fish being pumped out. In 1996 the reservoir was lowered to 235 ft for forty-five days for a penstock epoxy coating.

**Physical and chemical conditions:**
- In 1970, the reservoir was described as the only oligotrophic lake in the Imperial Division, with an average depth of 25 ft and maximum depth of 39 ft.
- March 1980 Hydrolab readings: 15.5–17.5 °C, dissolved oxygen 8.5–11.6 ppm, conductivity 1350–1450 µS/cm.
- April 1980: 16.5–18.25 °C, dissolved oxygen 8.0–10 ppm, conductivity 1200–1250 µS/cm.
- September 1980: 27.2–28.2 °C, pH 7.6–8.1, conductivity 1370–1385 µS/cm.

**Substrate:** Spawning gravel at two sites measured 52.5% gravel, 47.1% sand, 0.4% fines, and 35.7% gravel, 63.6% sand, 0.7% fines (February 1981). Bottom sampling found silty-clay with some gravel near the boat ramp and silty-clay without gravel near the dam.

**Predators and competitors:** Non-native fishes were identified as the main predation threat. Green sunfish were the primary predator of eggs, larvae and fingerlings. Channel catfish, carp, bluegill, largemouth bass, smallmouth bass, redear sunfish and yellow bullhead were also recorded. Smallmouth bass and tilapia were illegally introduced in 1979–80. Flathead catfish were increasing by 1982.

**Hybridization:** Electrophoretic work by [[Donald Buth]] (UCLA) found low genetic variation and 0% [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] genes in the Senator Wash Reservoir sample, compared with 1% in Dexter Hatchery progeny and 3% in Lake Mohave razorback suckers.

## Key Findings

**Population status**
- A 1980–81 mark-recapture estimate gave 54 ± 22 (95% confidence limits) adults. In November 1982, [[Linda Ulmer]] (CDFG) estimated 40–50 adults. In March 1983 she estimated 25 adults aged 17–18 years.
- Sex ratio from September 1980 trammel netting: 3 females to 1 male. Potential predators to razorback suckers: 482 to 24.
- A 1977 report noted that Senator Wash Reservoir was the most abundant California population, but later records showed no recruitment beyond the larval stage.

**Reproduction and spawning**
- Spawning was observed in 1979–1981 over lap-zone gravel, cobble and mixed substrate, cleaned of silt by wave action, at 10–18 ft depth and 15–18 °C. Fish reused the same sites across years.
- January–February 1980 was the first documented evidence of successful reproduction, when eggs and larvae were collected.
- Egg incubation at 20–23.5 °C in reservoir water gave a 46% hatch rate versus 30% in tap water. Higher survival occurred at warmer temperatures (20–25 °C). Hatch time was 3 days at 21 °C versus 15–25 days at 13 °C.
- Larval behaviour: swim-up at 2–4 days, bottom use by day 5, and shelter use afterward. Larvae showed no defensive behaviour toward predators.
- Egg and larval mortality in February–March 1980 was attributed to reservoir operation and water-level fluctuation. Recruitment failure was attributed to predation, mainly by non-native fish, and physical conditions.

**Telemetry and movement (1980–1981)**
- Sonic-tagged fish were tracked in 24-hour periods, three times a month. Preliminary results showed activity in water under 25 ft in the western one-third of the reservoir, movement parallel to contours, and activity peaks at dusk and dawn.
- Mean depth by season (Helfrich 1985): winter 10.57 m, spring 12.94 m, summer 15.78 m, fall 18.53 m, spawning season 11.14 m, rest of year 14.55 m.
- Mean depth was 9.03 m at night and 17.29 m during the day. Females were deeper during spawning, winter and spring; males were deeper in summer and fall. Depth was greatest during pumping activity and waning moon.
- A final movement report was never produced; only preliminary results survived.

**Stocking history**
- Stockings into Senator Wash Reservoir include 82 fish (February 10, 1987; mean TL 200 mm), 70 fish from Niland (February 26, 1988; mean TL 276.7 mm), 1600 fish from the Blythe Golf Course Pond (March 4, 1988; mean TL 170 mm), and 548, 1073, 692 and 36 fish in 1990. Stockings from 1988 were marked by removing the left pelvic fin.
- Many stockings are unverified by records.

**Conservation recommendations and policy**
- The recovery plan (1980/1981) kept the species under endangered status until viable populations were maintained in Senator Wash Reservoir, Lake Mohave and the lower Colorado River.
- The 1985 California management plan aimed for self-sustaining populations in Senator Wash Reservoir and West Pond, with biannual or annual monitoring.
- The 1989 recommendation limited future grow-out ponds to the Niland Native Fish Ponds, Imperial National Wildlife Refuge and Cibola National Wildlife Refuge.

**Data gaps:** Many projects were incomplete, cancelled, or never published. Examples are the 1981 movement report, a planned 3-year follow-up study, and the 1981 filming proposal. The authors emphasize that the historical record should be preserved and synthesized as a reference for future management.
