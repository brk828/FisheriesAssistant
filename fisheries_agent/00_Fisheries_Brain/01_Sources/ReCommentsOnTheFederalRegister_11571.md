# RE: Comments on the Federal Register publication that proposes to designate a nonessential experimental population of the endangered Colorado squawfish

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #ESA

## Executive Summary

This document combines a 1987 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) *Federal Register* proposed rule and a critical comment letter from ichthyologist [[W. L. Minckley]] regarding the reintroduction of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) into the Lower [[Colorado River]] between Imperial Dam and Parker Dam (Arizona/California). The FWS proposed designating this reintroduced population as a "nonessential experimental population" under Section 10(j) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (1982 Amendments), which would relax Section 7(a)(2) protections and permit regulated take under state law. Minckley's letter (representing Arizona State University's Department of Zoology) strongly challenges the "nonessential" designation, arguing the reach is historically and ecologically critical to species recovery and should instead be classified "experimental essential."

## Background & Description

The source texts are regulatory/policy documents and do not provide direct morphometric or anatomical descriptions of *Ptychocheilus lucius*. Notable biological/life-history points mentioned:

- Large-river cyprinid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], including the Gila River system.
- Historically documented in the Salt, Verde, and San Pedro Rivers (Gila River tributaries) and likely other Arizona drainages.
- Reintroduced stock originates from captive-bred broodstock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, New Mexico); fish are grown to fingerling size at [[Page Springs State Fish Hatchery]] (Cornville, Arizona) before stocking.
- A concern is raised that hatchery-reared fish may lack innate migratory imprinting/capabilities compared to wild-spawned fish, potentially affecting spawning migration behavior — though Minckley notes they "may establish new routes or behaviors."
- Comparative note on a congener, [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*): observed to coexist successfully with introduced [[Tilapia]] (cichlids) in artificial ponds (golf course ponds, Blythe, CA; urban lakes, Tempe, AZ), but disappeared from Lake Mohave backwaters when subjected to introduced centrarchids — illustrating differential predator/competitor interactions among native and non-native fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historic range**: Once widespread throughout the Colorado River system, including the Gila River system in Arizona (Salt, Verde, San Pedro Rivers). Last Arizona specimen collected in the early 1950s; extensive subsequent sampling has failed to relocate the species in the state.
- **Habitat preference**: Large river channels with associated isolated and semi-isolated riverine marshlands and backwaters — considered by many researchers as primary habitat for recovery efforts. The Colorado River Indian Reservation reach ("Parker II Division," not yet channelized) and NWR-enhanced backwaters are highlighted as particularly suitable.
- **Causes of historic decline**: Dewatering, dam construction (blocking migration routes), and competition with introduced exotic fish species.
- **Current reintroduction sites**:
  - [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] (Arizona) — already designated experimental populations.
  - Proposed: [[Lower Colorado River]] reach from [[Imperial Dam]] to [[Parker Dam]] (Yuma and La Paz Counties, AZ; Imperial, Riverside, San Bernardino Counties, CA).
- **Isolation**: Proposed population site is ~500 river miles from the nearest native population (upper Lake Powell) and ~300 river miles from the nearest experimental population (Verde River near Perkinsville, AZ).
- **Community interactions**: Changes in the introduced fish fauna of the lower Colorado River since the 1970s may now be more favorable to native fish survival than in earlier decades, per Minckley's anecdotal pond/lake observations of [[Bonytail]] vs. cichlids and centrarchids.
- **Land management context**: A significant proportion of the proposed reintroduction reach (52 of 147 river miles, per Minckley) falls under [[National Wildlife Refuge]] (NWR) jurisdiction, where full ESA protections (including Section 7(a)(2)) remain in force regardless of "essential/nonessential" designation. Additional river miles fall under Colorado River Indian Tribes jurisdiction.

## Key Findings

1. **Regulatory mechanism**: Under Section 10(j) of the 1982 ESA Amendments, translocated/reintroduced populations within historic (but currently unoccupied) range may be designated "experimental," and if "nonessential," are treated as threatened rather than endangered — exempting them from Section 7(a)(2) consultation requirements (except on NWR/National Park lands).

2. **Proposed action**: FWS and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] planned annual stocking of up to 100,000 hatchery-reared Colorado squawfish over 10 years into the Lower Colorado River reach (Imperial Dam to Parker Dam), sourced entirely from Dexter NFH captive broodstock (no wild removals).

3. **Take provisions**: Under the proposed special rule (50 CFR 17.84(f)), take of this nonessential experimental population would be allowed consistent with state law for educational/scientific/propagation purposes, incidental catch-and-release during permitted recreational fishing, and during state-regulated open seasons — with violations of state law also constituting ESA violations.

4. **Scientific critique (Minckley letter)**:
   - Objects to "nonessential" status, arguing the Lower Colorado River reach was the historic center of abundance for the species and contains uniquely suitable recovery habitat (isolated backwaters, marshlands).
   - Notes that the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team assigned low recovery priority to reaches with "nonessential" designations (per Las Vegas meeting minutes), potentially marginalizing this reach's contribution to species recovery.
   - Raises concern about enforcement complexity given patchwork jurisdiction (state-regulated sport fishing vs. full ESA protection on NWR lands within the same river reach).
   - Highlights an "enigma" scenario: if a "nonessential" population becomes self-sustaining while wild "essential" populations decline, a reclassification problem would arise.
   - Advocates for "experimental essential" status instead, citing federal/tribal land control over the reach as sufficient safeguard against take pressures, comparable to or exceeding protection under full endangered status.

5. **Broader recovery strategy**: The reintroduction is part of the FWS [[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan]], which emphasizes establishing multiple isolated populations to buffer against catastrophic loss of any single population — with parallel restocking efforts already underway in the Salt and Verde Rivers.

6. **Taxonomic/regulatory listing update**: The proposed rule would amend 50 CFR 17.11(h) to add a distinct entry for the Salt/Verde River drainages and Lower Colorado River (Imperial to Parker Dam) populations as experimental, separate from the entire-range endangered listing.

7. **Administrative determinations**: FWS determined the rule was not "major" under Executive Order 12291 and would not significantly affect a substantial number of small entities under the Regulatory Flexibility Act; no new recordkeeping requirements were imposed under the Paperwork Reduction Act.
