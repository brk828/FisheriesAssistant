# Limnology of the Upper North Platte River System of Wyoming: 1977 Report of Findings

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation report (GR-79-1, July 1978) presents the 1977 limnological survey of the four uppermost reservoirs of the [[North Platte River]] system: [[Seminoe Reservoir]], [[Kortes Reservoir]], [[Pathfinder Reservoir]] and [[Alcova Reservoir]]. Sampling occurred in June and late August/early September 1977 and covered physical-chemical conditions, water chemistry, heavy metals, nutrients, chlorophyll, benthic fauna, bottom sediments and zooplankton. The study was intended to support Lower Missouri Region hydroelectric planning and the management of the upper system's fishery. The authors found that the reservoirs were alkaline, well oxygenated and low in toxic metals, and that nutrients and productivity build up through the middle of the system, which the authors link to the exceptional trout fishery of the [[Miracle Mile]]. Flushing by reservoir operations was identified as a major driver of zooplankton loss and cold-water habitat loss.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** The four reservoirs form an integrated multipurpose system providing irrigation, hydroelectric power, flood and sediment control, and recreation.
- **Seminoe Reservoir** (most upstream): capacity 1255 million m³ (1 017 297 acre-ft) at spillway; surface elevation 1937.6 m (6357 ft). Fishery mainly walleye and trout. Fed by the North Platte and [[Medicine Bow River]], plus Austin and Saylor Creeks.
- **Kortes Reservoir:** capacity 5.9 million m³ (4765 acre-ft); confined to a narrow, precipitous canyon; acts as a regulating reservoir for Seminoe releases. The river below Kortes, the "Miracle Mile," is renowned as one of the most productive trout streams in the Western United States.
- **Pathfinder Reservoir:** capacity 1253 million m³ (1 015 888 acre-ft); surface elevation 1783.1 m (5850.1 ft); completed in 1909 as the Bureau's first construction in the basin; stores water for the North Platte Project.
- **Alcova Reservoir:** capacity 227 million m³ (184 295 acre-ft); surface elevation 1676.4 m (5500 ft); a regulating reservoir fed by Pathfinder via Fremont Canyon and the Fremont Canyon Powerplant.
- Previous limnological work was limited (Public Health Service 1951; Neel 1953, 1966; LaBounty et al. 1976 on Seminoe).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling design.** Fieldwork ran June 6–10, June 22–24 and August 29–September 2, 1977, at roughly 20 stations (Table 1), including inflow and outflow river sites. Methods followed APHA standards, with a Hydrolab multiparameter probe, Van Dorn samplers, Secchi disk and limnophotometer, Clarke-Bumpus zooplankton nets, Ponar/Ekman benthic dredges and Parsons and Strickland chlorophyll methods.

**Physical structure.** All reservoirs were set up for stratification in June, but by August–September they were nearly isothermal and warm. Cold hypolimnetic water was released downstream, and 1977 drought drawdown at Pathfinder may have made flow patterns atypical.

**Water chemistry.** Calcium, magnesium and sodium were the dominant cations; bicarbonate (112–208 mg/L) and sulfate were the main anions. Carbonate was not detected. Bicarbonate levels suggest carbon is not limiting productivity. TDS and conductivity were persistently high, mainly from the Medicine Bow River, and were not significantly retained by the reservoirs, with Pathfinder possibly trapping some phosphorus-nitrogen (P-N) fraction.

**Heavy metals.** Copper, manganese, iron and zinc were detected; lead was not. Values were judged unlikely to be toxic to most aquatic organisms, though iron and copper declined downstream.

**Nutrients and sediments.** Nutrients were not trapped by Seminoe or Kortes but accumulated in the middle of the system, peaking in the North Platte arm of Pathfinder. Benthic organic content and metal content in sediments tracked the percentage of fine particles (organic content vs. fines <75 µm, r = 0.93; vs. clay-size fines, r = 0.92).

**Benthos.** Only chironomids and oligochaetes were found. Densities across the four reservoirs ranged from 205 to 8309 individuals/m², classed as relatively abundant by Northcote and Larkin's criteria.

**Zooplankton.** Rotifers, copepods and cladocerans were found, with copepods and cladocerans dominant. Abundance in August was about 1% of June levels at Alcova, Pathfinder and Kortes.

**Flushing.** Water retention times were 4.6 days (Kortes), 37.3 days (Alcova), 85.2 days (Pathfinder) and 657.4 days (Seminoe) for June–August 1977.

## Key Findings

1. **Stratification and temperature:** Reservoirs stratified by June and became isothermal by late August due to high flushing; Seminoe showed only slight stratification.
2. **Dissolved oxygen:** Always sufficient to support aquatic life at all depths, with no significant stratification.
3. **pH and conductivity:** All four reservoirs were alkaline (pH about 7.1–8.6) and relatively saline; conductivity rose in August, likely from evaporation.
4. **Redox:** Eh values exceeded 200 mV throughout, indicating an oxidized state.
5. **Trace metals:** Concentrations were well below toxic levels, with no lead detected.
6. **Nutrients:** Phosphorus and nitrogen accumulated in the middle of the system (Kortes and Pathfinder); the authors call for further data.
7. **Productivity:** Chlorophyll a was highest in the North Platte arm of Pathfinder (over 780 mg/m² in August, the highest areal value recorded). Kortes and Alcova productivity was limited by high flushing.
8. **Algae:** The blue-green alga *Aphanizomenon flos-aquae* dominated in August.
9. **Light:** The Zeq/Secchi ratio averaged 2.67 ± 0.73 across 11 readings, within the recommended range of about 3.
10. **Sediments:** Organic content and metal content were directly correlated with the percentage of fines.
11. **Zooplankton:** August abundance dropped to about 1% of June abundance at Alcova, Pathfinder and Kortes, attributed to flushing.
12. **Hydrology:** 1977 was a water-short year; Pathfinder's volume fell to levels not seen for several decades.
13. **Miracle Mile productivity:** The authors propose three non-exclusive explanations: nutrient passage through Kortes, Pathfinder acting as a "fish hatchery" for the Miracle Mile, and Kortes's low retention time.
14. **Recommendations:** Continue the study for at least two more years, focus on nutrient loading and unloading, document system-operation effects on zooplankton, and monitor the effects of a pelagic planktivore introduced by the Wyoming Fish and Game Department in winter 1977–78 (a [[Pelagic Planktivore]] intended to feed walleye and rainbow trout).
