# Marble Bluff Fish Facility Operation Plan (1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1995 operation plan, prepared by the U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Region 1, Northern Nevada Fishery Resource Office) for the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]], sets out management strategies and physical modifications to improve fish passage and reduce mortality at the facility. It also serves as the Biological Assessment reinitiating intra-Service Section 7 consultation. The facility passes endangered [[Cui-ui]], threatened [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] and [[Tahoe Sucker]] from [[Pyramid Lake]] upstream to spawning habitat in the lower [[Truckee River]]. The 1994 season produced heavy mortality (about 2,589 cui-ui recorded in Table 1), so the plan adds crewing, terminal-structure and river-trap changes, and regulatory controls for the 1995 run.

## Background & Description

- **Historical decline:** Altered flow and channel characteristics of the Truckee River since the early 1900s caused extirpation of the Pyramid Lake strain of LCT and a critical decline of cui-ui. All three species are obligatory river spawners that spend most of their lives in Pyramid Lake.
- **Current LCT stock:** The LCT now in Pyramid Lake was first stocked in the early 1970s from other Nevada and California lakes and is maintained through hatchery production.
- **Authorization:** Construction of [[Marble Bluff Dam]], the Pyramid Lake Fishway and the Numana Dam fish ladder was authorized by 70 Stat. 775 (August 1, 1956) under the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Washoe Project. Construction was completed in 1976.
- **Responsibilities:** The Bureau of Reclamation operates and maintains the dam. The Service operates and maintains the facility and manages the spawning runs. [[Lahontan National Fish Hatchery]] maintains the fishway, fish handling building and sluice gate and assists with trapping. NNFRO manages fishery resources, trapping, run enumeration and improvement recommendations.

### Facility Components
- **Marble Bluff Dam:** About 175 ft long and 40 ft high with a baffled concrete apron, built to provide grade control and divert water to the fishway.
- **Fishway:** Extends 3.1 miles from the fish handling building to Pyramid Lake. It consists of a clay-lined earthen canal (3 ft deep, 8 ft wide) and four concrete ladders. It operates at 30–50 cfs when lake level is 3,784–3,810 ft msl. The original [[Ice Harbor Dam]] design (27 weirs) was modified from 1978 to 1983 by blocking orifices and adding wooden weirs to reduce height, turbulence and velocity barriers.
- **Fish handling building:** Contains a V-trap, holding pens and sorting table. All fish are counted, checked for tags, and randomly sampled for length and sex before release upstream.
- **River trap:** A concrete channel, V-trap and fish elevator (raised 40 ft) accessible only when the Truckee River delta permits passage. The trap entrance is 4 ft by 12 ft.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Migration routes:** Fish reach the dam by either the fishway or the Truckee River delta. Two factors govern access: lake elevation and river discharge.
- **Delta threshold:** Estimated minimum lake elevation for delta passage was 3,810–3,815 ft msl (Buchanan, 1987, 1988). Delta passage nonetheless occurred at 3,797 ft and lower during 1993 and 1994.
- **Delta advantages:** Runs tend to be earlier and larger, passage is less stressful than the fishway, and it is relatively non-selective across sexes and age classes (Buchanan and Strekal, 1988).
- **Stable meander:** The Truckee River below the dam currently shows a stable meander pattern that intermittently allows passage at lower lake levels.
- **Predation:** White pelicans caused heavy losses at the terminal structure and especially at the dam base. Pelicans were estimated to possibly consume one cui-ui per day, implying 1,800 to 18,000 cui-ui consumed in the six-day event.
- **Larval outmigration:** The fishway also serves as a downstream route for larval cui-ui. Stranded larvae were found after flows were stopped early on May 26, 1994, with an unknown number, possibly several hundred to several thousand, killed.

## Key Findings

### 1994 Season Evaluation
- **Terminal structure:** Volume was reduced and the entrance canal narrowed to 3 ft deep and 8 ft wide. This limited the number of fish entering the structure. A record **36,000** cui-ui migrated the fishway, and more than **10,000** were transported by hatchery truck.
- **False bottoms:** Installed in lower exposed weirs to limit stacking to 3 ft. Chain attachment at the centre caused the structure to tip, trapping and killing about **200** cui-ui in one peak event.
- **Exclusion gate:** A steel gate under the bridge required a backhoe to install and remove, and scouring of about 10 ft undermined it. About **300** cui-ui deaths were attributed to its design and location.
- **Mortality totals (Table 1):** Recorded losses were **2,589** cui-ui in total: river trap 1,200, bypass chute 16, fishway 462, emergency gate 911. The largest single day was 5/10 (1,050).
- **Stampede Reservoir constraint:** A May 20, 1994 cutoff for upstream adult movement was set to protect reservoir storage specified in a draft Biological Opinion. The gate was used to exclude adults from the fishway from May 12.
- **Bypass culvert:** Completed but never implemented, though it was intended to allow river trap operation without the fishway.
- **Early termination:** Fishway flows ended May 26, 1994, with 140 adults still inside.

### River Trap Events (1994)
- The trap operated 21 of 33 days (April 17 to May 19). Quick-release designs failed, and the gate was opened manually with a comealong.
- Rain on May 4 triggered migration of several hundred thousand cui-ui, which remained at the dam base for six days.
- A four-hour set on May 6 overfilled the trap. Crowding on May 10 left cui-ui vertical and stacked in the channel, and roughly **1,000** dead cui-ui were removed. Incidental take was exceeded and operations stopped pending review.
- The 19,000 cui-ui captured in the river trap were released upstream to spawn. Heavy pelican predation occurred throughout.

### 1995 Operations Plan
- **Timing:** Improvements to be completed by late February 1995, with fishway flows starting the first week of March for Tahoe sucker and LCT runs. Flows continue through larval outmigration.
- **Continuity:** Operations continue even if the incidental take limit from the 1995 Biological Opinion is exceeded.
- **Staffing:** Three four-person crews rotate through 10-hour shifts (2400–1030 and 1330–2400 hours), replacing the earlier on-call system, with a start on April 16, 1995.
- **Fishway measures:** Weirs were replaced, valves serviced, sand removed, drop logs added below the bridge to limit scour, and the canal excavated to 2–3 ft deep and 10 ft wide. A tilting weir was planned to regulate entry into the terminal ladder. Two hatchery trucks stand by as an emergency measure.
- **River trap measures:** Sets are limited to one hour at the start of the season and 30 minutes maximum with 24-hour operation. Backup hoist and motors are available, and a crane contract and 24-hour electrician are in place. Lighting and a 10 ft by 3 ft tilting weir were planned, along with a vertical slot weir backup, a ladder for access, and portable video cameras.
- **Predation control:** Bird netting remains over the terminal structure and hazing methods are used at the terminal structure and river trap.
- **Outlook:** The plan states that design limitations and current cui-ui population estimates will remain problematic until more fundamental, comprehensive redesigns are implemented.
