# Unregulated Activities Adversely Affecting Listed Fish in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #EndangeredSpecies #Conservation

## Executive Summary
This 1993 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services Office) documents multiple unregulated, unauthorized heavy-machinery activities occurring in Arizona stream channels that are adversely impacting federally listed fish species. The document is not a primary research paper but a regulatory/administrative record identifying compliance failures under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (Sections 7 and 9) and the [[Clean Water Act]] (Section 404). Four separate incidents are reported: road repair on Aravaipa Creek, dam reconstruction on Eagle Creek, streambed alteration on the Blue River, and heavy equipment use in the White River on the Fort Apache Indian Reservation — all affecting habitat of the [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The memo emphasizes chronic understaffing at AESO as a barrier to enforcement and consultation compliance.

## Background & Description
The source document does not provide direct morphological data (e.g., body measurements, meristics, coloration) for the species discussed. Species mentioned are identified taxonomically only:
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*) — small-bodied cyprinid minnow native to Gila River Basin.
- **Loach Minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — small benthic cyprinid adapted to riffle habitats.
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — large-bodied catostomid, notable for a pronounced dorsal keel ("razorback") anterior to the dorsal fin (background knowledge; not detailed in text).

*Note: Morphological detail is outside the scope of this administrative memo; see [[Meda Fulgida]], [[Tiaroga Cobitis]], and [[Xyrauchen Texanus]] species accounts for detailed characteristics.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Stream Channel Dependency**: All three species rely on flowing, structurally intact stream channel habitat (riffles, runs, and substrate) within Southwestern desert rivers.
- **Documented Occupied Habitats**:
  - **Upper [[Aravaipa Creek]]** (Graham County) — occupied by [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]; site of road/utility repair using heavy machinery directly in the channel, with materials harvested from the streambed.
  - **[[Eagle Creek]]** (Greenlee County) — occupied by [[Spikedace]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]; site of diversion dam reconstruction by Phelps Dodge Corporation within proposed critical habitat for razorback sucker.
  - **Upper [[Blue River]]** (Greenlee County) — occupied by [[Loach Minnow]] and part of its proposed critical habitat; observed fill placement, channel material removal, and road blading directly in/along the stream.
  - **Mainstem and North Fork [[White River]]** (Fort Apache Indian Reservation, Navajo/Gila Counties) — heavy machinery activity within and upstream of loach minnow habitat; **dead loach minnow directly observed** in the Canyon Day area, indicating acute mortality from disturbance.
- **Threat Mechanism**: Heavy equipment operation in-channel causes direct habitat destruction (substrate disturbance, sedimentation, altered hydrology), channel material extraction, and fill placement — all degrading riffle/run habitat critical to these rheophilic (current-loving) species.
- **Regulatory Niche**: The ecological vulnerability of these species is compounded by an "unregulated" institutional gap — actions by county governments, FEMA, the Army Corps of Engineers, Phelps Dodge Corporation, the U.S. Forest Service, and the Bureau of Indian Affairs are proceeding without required Section 7 consultation or Section 404 permitting review.

## Key Findings
- **Widespread Non-Compliance**: At least four independent instances of heavy machinery use in listed-fish-occupied streams occurred in Arizona within a span of weeks (June–July 1993), none of which involved prior contact with AESO.
- **Direct Mortality Observed**: Tribal Game and Fish biologists reported **dead loach minnow** in the Canyon Day area of the White River following machinery activity — direct evidence of lethal impact.
- **Critical Habitat Overlap**: Eagle Creek activity occurred within proposed critical habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]]; Blue River activity occurred within proposed critical habitat for [[Loach Minnow]].
- **Multi-Agency Federal Nexus**: Federal involvement likely spans FEMA funding, U.S. Forest Service lands, Bureau of Indian Affairs assistance, and Army Corps of Engineers Section 404 permitting — yet no consultations were initiated by any of these agencies.
- **Root Cause Identified**: The memo explicitly attributes the lack of enforcement to insufficient AESO staffing relative to competing high-priority workload (formal/informal Section 7 consultations, FOIA requests, litigation, and critical habitat rulemaking).
- **Resource Request**: The authors state that addressing these unregulated impacts, together with upcoming listing/recovery actions for other native fish (including [[Virgin River]] fishes) and critical habitat designations, will require **at least one additional full-time equivalent (FTE) position** at AESO.
- **Systemic Conservation Threat**: The memo concludes that unregulated adverse impacts constitute "a major contributing factor to the ongoing decline" of Arizona's listed fish species, implicating a broader pattern of unpermitted Section 7/Section 9 violations across the state.
