## Executive Summary
This document is an annual compliance report submitted by the California Department of Fish and Game (Inland Fisheries Division) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, detailing collection activities for the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) conducted under federal Permit No. BOLSBC. The report documents the sampling of both wild and refugium populations across several sites in Riverside, Imperial, and San Diego Counties, California, on June 7–8, 1992. Collected specimens were intended to support electrophoretic (genetic) analysis being conducted by graduate student Jason Dunham under the supervision of Dr. W.L. Minckley, with results to be submitted for publication. No morphological, ecological, or population-trend data are directly reported; the letter is primarily administrative, confirming permit compliance and specimen provenance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The [[Desert Pupfish]] is documented as occupying a fragmented range of both **wild (natural) populations** and **refugium (artificial/managed) populations**, reflecting its status as an endangered species reliant on conservation intervention:

**Wild Populations:**
- [[Salt Creek]], Riverside County — 30 fish collected
- [[San Felipe Creek]], Imperial County — 30 fish collected

**Refugium Populations (managed conservation sites):**
- [[Thousand Palms Oasis]] / Simone/McCallum Pond, Riverside County — 50 fish
- Pond at [[The Living Desert]], Riverside County — 35 fish
- [[Palm Canyon Pond]], Anza-Borrego Desert State Park, San Diego County — 50 fish
- [[Cleveland Street Drain]], Riverside County — 30 fish
- [[Salton Sea]] shoreline pool ([[Cleveland Pool]]), Riverside County — 30 fish

This distribution illustrates the species' ecological reliance on isolated desert water sources — natural springs and creeks, artificial ponds, agricultural drainage channels, and Salton Sea-associated shoreline pools — a hallmark of the [[Desert Pupfish]]'s adaptation to fragmented, often anthropogenically altered desert aquatic habitats in the [[Salton Sea Basin]] and Colorado Desert region.

## Key Findings
- A total of **255 Desert Pupfish** were collected across 7 sites (60 from wild populations, 195 from refugium populations) on June 7–8, 1992, under USFWS Permit No. BOLSBC.
- Sampling spanned three counties: Riverside, Imperial, and San Diego, California.
- Collected specimens were designated for **electrophoretic analysis** (a genetic/protein variation technique) conducted by graduate student **Jason Dunham**, under the supervision of **Dr. W.L. Minckley** (Arizona State University), indicating a genetic population-structure study of wild vs. refugium [[Desert Pupfish]] stocks.
- Dunham and Minckley had, as of the report date, submitted a manuscript for publication based on this genetic work — representing a key downstream scientific output not detailed within this letter.
- The report underscores active, permit-regulated conservation management of the [[Desert Pupfish]] as a federally listed endangered species, involving coordinated monitoring between California Department of Fish and Game and multiple U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regional offices (Portland, Ventura, Phoenix).
- The maintenance of multiple **refugium populations** in artificial or semi-natural ponds highlights an ex-situ conservation strategy to buffer against the loss of naturally occurring wild populations in Salt Creek and San Felipe Creek.
