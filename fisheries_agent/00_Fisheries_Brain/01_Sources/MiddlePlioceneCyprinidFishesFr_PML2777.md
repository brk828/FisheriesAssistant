# Middle Pliocene Cyprinid Fishes from the Bidahochi Formation, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #haemulidae

## Executive Summary

This document contains two Copeia contributions. The main paper, by Uyeno and Miller, describes four cyprinid (minnow) species from the Middle Pliocene [[Bidahochi Formation]] of northeastern Arizona. Three of the four are new, including a new genus, [[Euomus]]. The fossils are dated at 4.1 million years by [[Potassium-Argon Dating]]. The authors use them to trace the evolutionary history of the [[Colorado River Basin]] fish fauna, arguing that the river already offered the swift-water habitat that modern relatives occupy. A shorter companion note by Courtenay reviews the systematic status of [[Haemulon boschmae]], a marine grunt from the Atlantic coast of South America.

## Background & Description

**Rationale.** The authors note that Pleistocene ichthyofaunas are largely similar to Recent ones, in contrast to the major mammalian and avian turnover of the same period. Many fish species and some genera went extinct around the close of the Pliocene or early Pleistocene. The authors argue that studying Pliocene fishes should clarify the origins of the living North American freshwater fauna.

**Study area and age.** The [[Bidahochi Formation]] extends across the upper [[Little Colorado River]] basin. Its fish fossils come from the basal part of the upper member, which records mixed lacustrine and fluvial deposition. [[Potassium-Argon Dating]] of a basalt unit gave 4.1 million years, which agrees with earlier mammal and mollusk-based age estimates of Middle Pliocene (Hemphillian) age.

**Localities.** Fossils come from four localities, three of which are described in detail:
- **Roberts Mesa (UMMP-ARIZ-2-60, 61):** the main source of material, with most specimens collected from 1950 to 1961.
- **White Cone Peak (UMMP-ARIZ-360):** a site with a known local fauna of mollusks, fishes and mammals.
- **Coliseum Diatreme (UMMP-ARIZ-4-60):** a crater deposit of thin-bedded limey clay and tuff, interpreted as a small pond connected to the main lake.
- **Steamboat Wash:** exact location unknown, possibly higher in the section.

**Material.** Specimens are housed in the [[Museum of Northern Arizona]], the [[University of Michigan Museum of Paleontology]], and the [[United States National Museum]]. Most material consists of isolated bones (dentaries, cleithra, pharyngeals, hyomandibulars), which the authors describe as the most diagnostic elements. Only the family Cyprinidae is represented among the abundant remains.

**Taxonomic summary.** Four taxa are recognized:
1. *Ptychocheilus prelucius*, a new species
2. *Euomus navaho*, a new species in a new genus
3. *?Gila cristifera*, a new species
4. *Gila cf. G. robusta*, an identification left open

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Lacustrine and fluvial setting.** The lower member of the formation is largely lacustrine, while the upper member is mostly fluvial. The fish fauna of the lake is linked to the fluvial system of the basin.
- **Diatreme pond.** The two species from the Coliseum Diatreme lived in an isolated pond that was probably connected to the lake by a stream or estuary. The authors found no clear habitat-based ecological differences between those two species.
- **Swift-water adaptation.** The authors conclude that by Middle Pliocene time, the [[Colorado River]] had already developed the swift-river habitat to which modern relatives such as [[Gila Robusta]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] are adapted.
- **Extinct lacustrine suckers.** The absence of [[Catostomidae]] (suckers) from the Bidahochi beds is notable. The authors cite paleontological evidence that suckers appeared in North America before minnows. The early sucker line *Amyzon*, known from lacustrine deposits elsewhere in the West, does not appear to have survived the Miocene. *Catostomus latipinnis* is known only from early Pleistocene deposits in the same region, so the sucker lineage may have arrived after the Bidahochi beds were laid down.
- **Lake disappearance.** The authors suggest that the disappearance of the large Bidahochi lake, through downcutting and drainage changes, may have contributed to the extinction of several minnow lineages.

## Key Findings

**Ptychocheilus prelucius (new species)**
- Holotype: a nearly complete right dentary (UMMP G2.7819, MNA), 48 mm long, with 15 sensory pores.
- The ventral surface of the dentary is broader than in living species and forms a right angle with the dorsal process. The species name combines the Latin *pre-* ("before") with *lucius*, implying that the fossil is ancestral to the living [[Colorado Squawfish]], *Ptychocheilus lucius*.
- Pharyngeals have a dental formula of 2,5. Living *P. lucius* and two related species have 2,5-4,2.
- Referred material includes hyomandibulars with the strongly developed lateral ridge that characterizes *Ptychocheilus*.
- The authors judged the species sufficiently distinct from the living form to name it, while retaining a close ancestral relationship.

**Euomus navaho (new genus and species)**
- Diagnosed from the cleithrum alone. Its mesial wing is deflected posteriorly into a high ridge at about 45° to the horizontal ramus, unlike other American minnows, where the wing points forward.
- Name derivation: the genus name comes from Greek *eu* ("good") and *omos* ("shoulder"). The species name honors the Navajo, with the spelling "navaho" used in agreement with usage among anthropologists.
- Holotype: a complete left cleithrum (UMMP 45502), 49 mm along its greatest dimension, collected by R. R. Miller and party on 17 July 1961 at Roberts Mesa.
- Paratypes include several complete or nearly complete cleithra. Additional non-type material comes from White Cone Peak and the Coliseum Diatreme.
- Cleithra are the most abundant remains at Roberts Mesa, so the authors tentatively assign several other bones (dentaries, a preopercle, hyomandibulars, pharyngeals) to this species.
- Estimated meristic counts from one partial specimen: dorsal 9 or 10, anal 8 or 9, pectoral 12, pelvic about 10 or 11.

**?Gila cristifera (new species)**
- Holotype: a complete left cleithrum (MNA G2.7704) from White Cone, collected by J. F. Lance, presumably in 1953.
- Diagnostic feature: a moderately developed free ridge between the mesial wing and the mesocoracoid contact. The authors state that this ridge is absent from all North American cyprinids they examined, but always well developed in the Catostomidae.
- Holotype measurements (mm): greatest dimension 41; vertical ramus 30; greatest horizontal length 30; horizontal ramus 25; anterior edge 11.5; anterior surface width 7.
- Name derivation: *cristifera*, "bearing a crest," after the free ridge.
- Non-type material includes about 20 cleithra from White Cone and Roberts Mesa.
- The authors place the species tentatively in *Gila* rather than erect a new genus. They note that *Gila* is probably the most generalized genus among those considered.

**Gila cf. G. robusta (Steamboat Wash specimen)**
- A nearly complete fish (UMMP 47116), about 97 mm standard length. It has 43 or 44 vertebrae, within the range of *G. robusta* subspecies, though too few for *G. r. elegans* and *G. cypha*.
- The opercle has an angular posterodorsal projection that appears in some Roberts Mesa specimens and in living *G. atraria*.
- Tentatively referred to *G. robusta* because it combines features of living Colorado River forms.
- The authors conclude it probably does not belong to the same species as *?G. cristifera*. Its shoulder girdle is not broad enough and the horizontal ramus lacks the distinctive shape of that species.
- A Coliseum Diatreme posterior body fragment shows a caudal peduncle and fins resembling *G. cypha*. It is provisionally referred to *Gila cf. G. robusta*.

**Unidentified bones.** Dentaries, opercles, preopercles, hyomandibulars and frontals represent at least one additional species, possibly including *Euomus* or *?Gila cristifera*. The authors note that a fifth species is probably represented among the pharyngeals.

**Evolutionary conclusions.**
- *Ptychocheilus* in the middle Colorado basin was already moving toward the specialized living form, *P. lucius*.
- The fauna included a now-extinct minnow genus (*Euomus*) with sucker-like feeding habits and a second extinct minnow (*?Gila cristifera*).
- The Colorado River basin's fish fauna may have been more diverse then, possibly because of a larger lake and more ecological niches.

**Haemulon boschmae (companion note by Courtenay)**
- The pomadasyid fish *Pristipoma boschmae* Metzelaar (1919), whose synonym *Isacia lacis* Miles (1953) was described from Colombian Caribbean material, is placed in the genus *Haemulon*.
- It is the fourteenth species of *Haemulon* recognized from the Atlantic and occurs along the northeastern coast of South America, overlapping the range of its close relative *H. striatum*.
- Key diagnostic character: the scale rows below the lateral line run parallel to the body's long axis in *H. boschmae*, rather than oblique as in *H. striatum*.
- The note also reports new specimens from Surinam, the Guianas and the Venezuelan coast.
