# Fall Fish Count Summary 1988–1994: Volunteer Monitoring of Arizona Stream Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department technical report (Nongame and Endangered Wildlife Program, Technical Report 81, June 1995) summarizes six years (1988–1994) of data from the [[Fall Fish Count]] (FFC), a volunteer-based survey of fish communities in Arizona streams. The FFC yielded collections at more than 140 sample sites. The authors assessed whether these data could reveal abundance trends for native fishes, and concluded that the data are mainly useful as point-in-time records of species occurrence and relative abundance, not as reliable population trend indicators. The report recommends continuing the project with improved survey methods.

## Background & Description

The FFC began in 1988 as a volunteer effort to gather long-term data on fish communities in Arizona streams. From 1992 it received Section 6 funding from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to monitor target species listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], including:

- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*)
- [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*)
- [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*)
- [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*)
- [[Mexican Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma ornatum*)

Sampling used hoopnets, gillnets, dipnets, minnow traps, electrofishers and seines. Volunteer participation was 61 (1988), 80 (1989), 62 (1990), 49 (1991), 34 known participants (1992), 42 (1993) and 57 (1994), with support from groups such as [[Sabino Canyon Volunteers]] and [[Red Rock Volunteer Naturalists]]. Vouchers were deposited in the Arizona State University Vertebrate Museum.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sites span the [[Colorado River Basin]] and closed basins across Arizona, plus several New Mexico sites in the [[Gila River]] system. Major sampled waters include the [[Gila River]], [[San Pedro River]], [[Santa Cruz River]], [[Agua Fria River]], [[Tonto Creek]], [[Oak Creek]], [[Sycamore Creek]] and [[Aravaipa Creek]].

Native species recorded include longfin dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*), Sonora sucker (*Catostomus insignis*), desert sucker (*Pantosteus clarki*), and speckled dace (*Rhinichthys osculus*). Dominant nonnatives include red shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*), fathead minnow (*Pimephales promelas*), green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*) and smallmouth bass (*Micropterus dolomieu*).

Flooding was an important factor. High precipitation and flows in January and February 1993 exceeded previous period-of-record maxima at several gages in the Gila and Verde drainages. The authors suggest this may have favored native over nonnative fishes, since many nonnatives are thought to tolerate floods less well ([[Meffe 1984]]; [[Minckley and Meffe 1987]]).

## Key Findings

**Target species occurrence**
- Target species were found at 46 sites in 1988, 41 in 1989, 28 in 1990, 18 in 1991, 15 in 1992 and 15 in 1993. 1994 totals were unavailable because of delinquent reporting.
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] has not been found in any FFC survey. No new localities for target species were recorded.
- [[Roundtail Chub]] has not been collected at Tonto Creek since 1988.

**Sampling effort**
- Sites visited fell from 57 (1988) to 41 (1992), then rose to 46 in 1993. Data from 33 sites were received for 1994, with about 20 more surveyed but unreported at report completion.
- Of 141 FFC sites, 93 contained at least three years of data, 49 of those with three consecutive years, and 30 had been sampled in 1994. Only 16 of the 30 recently sampled sites had enough information to compute CPUE.

**Trends at selected sites (16 analyzed)**
- Native catch rates or relative abundance rose at 10 sites in 1993, mainly from longfin dace and, to a lesser extent, Sonora and desert suckers.
- Sonora and/or desert suckers were first recorded in 1993 or 1994 at eight sites.
- Nonnatives (mosquitofish, fathead minnow, red shiner, green sunfish) declined at six sites in 1993.
- Site-level patterns were mixed: [[Gila River]] near Box-O Wash and Sycamore Creek showed increasing native proportions, Oak Creek and Tonto Creek showed increasing nonnative and decreasing native trends respectively, and [[San Pedro River]] near Cascabel showed a decrease in native abundance.

**Methodological limitations**
- Effort, gear type and habitat sampled varied among years, so CPUE was used as an index. Dipnet-only sites could not yield CPUE and were excluded.
- The authors note that trend interpretations based on four years are of questionable value, and that many years of comparable data are needed.

**Recommendations**
1. **Sample continuity:** continue sites currently sampled and re-establish surveys at sites with two or more years of historical data, prioritizing historical over new sites.
2. **Consistent effort:** use the same gear types, effort minimums and habitats at each site.
3. **Data recording:** record each gear type's catch and effort separately (shocking seconds, meters seined, etc.).
4. **Data reporting:** require all volunteer data by February 1 each year.

The authors conclude that the FFC's central strength is its point-in-time documentation of species occurrence, which is useful for management. They recommend continuing the program on that basis while improving methods.
