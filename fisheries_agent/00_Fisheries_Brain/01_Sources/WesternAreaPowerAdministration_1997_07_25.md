## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific study but a set of **inter-agency regulatory comments** submitted by the Western Area Power Administration (Western) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) and Bureau of Reclamation regarding the **Final Biological and Conference Opinion** on Lower Colorado River (LCR) Operations and Maintenance (Lake Mead to the Southerly International Boundary), issued under Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act (ESA). The correspondence critiques the FWS opinion's treatment of two management stressors affecting endangered native fish populations in the LCR: **daily fluctuating flows** from hydropower/water operations and **non-native species introductions/predation**. Western argues the Biological Opinion disproportionately emphasizes flow fluctuation as a negative factor while under-addressing the more significant threat posed by non-native predators and competitors, and requests clarification on augmentation requirements for two endangered cyprinid/catostomid species: the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]].

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (meristics, body measurements, coloration) are presented in this source. The document is administrative/regulatory in nature and references the species only by common name in the context of population augmentation and habitat management:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — endangered catostomid endemic to the Colorado River Basin; referenced regarding augmentation stocking requirements (RPA Item A.1).
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) — endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River Basin; referenced with explicit uncertainty about required augmentation specifications, including **numbers, source population, and genetic suitability** of stock used for reintroduction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The Lower Colorado River between Lake Mead and the Southerly International Boundary, a heavily regulated river system subject to dam operations (including [[Parker Dam]]) and hydropower-driven daily flow fluctuations.
- **Spawning and nursery habitat**: The Biological Opinion identifies a "fluctuation zone" (exposed/dewatered daily due to hydropower operations) as potentially unsuitable for spawning from January through May, though Western disputes the strength of the causal link between fluctuating flows and reduced spawning success.
- **Documented recruitment despite fluctuation**: The source notes that "successful recruitment apparently did occur below [[Parker Dam]] under the influence of daily fluctuating water releases" (BO p. 154), used by Western as evidence against attributing recruitment failure solely to fluctuating flows.
- **Non-native species interactions**: Non-native predators and competitors are identified as benefiting from the *stable* conditions produced by non-fluctuating flows (BO p. 130) — i.e., stability may favor non-natives over natives, an ecological trade-off central to the dispute.
- **Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives (RPA) framework**: Management measures discussed include preventing new non-native introductions, isolating natives from non-natives in controlled/refuge facilities, and a research mandate (RPA 4) on habitat use/preference — but no explicit RPA for active non-native population control in open river reaches.

## Key Findings

- Western Area Power Administration formally disputes the FWS Biological Opinion's causal attribution of poor native fish spawning/recruitment success to **daily fluctuating flows**, arguing the opinion lacks supporting rationale/documentation (critiquing BO p. 137 and RPA Item 1, p. 172).
- Three counterarguments are raised against the "fluctuating flows are harmful" narrative:
  1. Daily fluctuations disrupt stable conditions that otherwise favor **non-native predators/competitors** (BO p. 130).
  2. Fluctuations may *discourage* native spawning in unsuitable, frequently dewatered elevations rather than directly harming existing spawn.
  3. Documented successful recruitment occurred **below [[Parker Dam]]** despite daily fluctuating releases (BO p. 154).
- Western contends the **non-native species problem** is treated with far less regulatory weight in the RPA than fluctuating flows, despite being characterized as a greater threat to the continued existence of endangered natives (BO p. 134).
- Existing RPAs address non-natives only via prevention of new introductions, native/non-native isolation in controlled facilities, and a general research mandate (RPA 4) — no active removal/control program is mandated for open-river non-native populations.
- Outstanding clarification is requested on **RPA Item A.1 (augmentation of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]])**, specifically stocking numbers, genetic source suitability, and requirements unique to the bonytail.
- Western asserts its own status as an "other party with action flexibility" under RPA Item 13.b and requests formal inclusion in assessing discretionary operational flexibility, citing a prior request (letter dated November 1, 1996) for Section 7 consultation participation.
- The letter documents Western's formal request for **independent Section 7 ESA consultation** (per 50 CFR 402.14) due to its operational integration with Reclamation's LCR facilities and shared impact on the same biological communities.
