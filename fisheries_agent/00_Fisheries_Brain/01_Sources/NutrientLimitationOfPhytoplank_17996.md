# Nutrient Limitation of Phytoplankton in a Central Arizona Reservoir

#phytoplankton #limnology #aquatic-ecology

## Executive Summary

This paper reports an in situ enclosure experiment on [[Phytoplankton]] in [[Lynx Lake]], a small, oligotrophic reservoir in central Arizona. Lake water was isolated in transparent [[Polyethylene Bag Enclosures]] and dosed with [[Nitrogen]], [[Phosphorus]] and [[Carbon]], alone and in every combination, during summer 1974. Phytoplankton increases in cell number, [[Chlorophyll A]], [[Dissolved Oxygen]] and pH occurred only in bags that received both nitrogen and phosphorus. The authors conclude that nitrogen and phosphorus together limit phytoplankton growth in the lake, while carbon does not, and that species composition did not change across treatments.

## Background & Description

Lynx Lake had year-round low nutrient concentrations (orthophosphate phosphorus below 0.01 mg/l; nitrate nitrogen 0.92 mg/l) and a phytoplankton community limited in both abundance and diversity. The authors place this within a literature on [[Nutrient Limitation]] in which phosphorus, nitrogen, silicate and carbon have each been identified as limiting in different lakes. Reported examples include phosphorus limitation in lakes in Africa, the Arctic, Canada, Oregon, Minnesota and Michigan, and conflicting results in which nitrogen, phosphorus or carbon were found limiting depending on season or site.

The study aimed to determine whether the sparse standing crop of Lynx Lake could be stimulated by in situ additions of nitrogen, phosphorus or carbon.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study site.** Lynx Lake lies near Prescott at 1676 m elevation in the [[Bradshaw Mountains]]. It is about 225 m wide, 900 m long, with a maximum depth of 18 m.

**Experimental design.** Eight polyethylene bags (43 cm diameter, 1.0 m long, 0.004 mm film) were partially submerged and held on floating wooden frames, with wire hoops and metal strips keeping the open end above the surface. Treatments were:
- Control (no addition)
- Single additions: N, P, C
- Pairwise additions: NP, NC, PC
- Combined addition: NPC

Nominal additions were 15.0 mg/l carbon (as NaHCO3), 1.5 mg/l nitrogen (as NaNO3) and 0.1 mg/l phosphorus (as Na2HPO4). Bags were sampled every two days for 13 to 14 days.

**Methodological limitations.** The authors note that enclosures can alter eddy diffusivity, which may matter more over longer experiments. Light penetration was not monitored. Temperature in bags and lake remained identical. Prior work by [[Goldman]] and others suggested minimal effects on light and thermal structure, and a short-term design was judged less susceptible to diffusivity artefacts.

## Key Findings

1. **Phytoplankton response was restricted to N+P treatments.** Cell numbers rose noticeably only in NP and NPC bags. Peak populations, reached on day 10, were 2 to 3 times the control. Single nutrients and carbon combinations had no significant effect.

2. **Chlorophyll a increased about six-fold** in NP and NPC bags relative to control, peaking at 13.8 mg/m³ about eight days after addition.

3. **Dissolved oxygen and pH tracked productivity.** Dissolved oxygen peaked six to eight days after addition and reached supersaturation in NP and NPC bags. A small DO increase also occurred in the P-only bag. pH rose to 9.9 in NP and NPC bags, while other treatments stayed between 8.2 and 8.7. The authors attribute the high pH to carbon dioxide drawdown by photosynthesis, since lake alkalinity was 90 mg/l.

4. **Nutrients were depleted in all enriched bags.** Orthophosphate fell to control levels within 6 to 8 days in every P-added bag, showing that phosphorus removal can occur without a phytoplankton response. Nitrate fell fastest in bags receiving both N and P, reaching control levels within eight days.

5. **Carbon did not limit growth.** Carbon additions alone or with N or P did not significantly alter standing crop.

6. **No species shift.** [[Chlorella]] was dominant across treatments, with [[Scenedesmus]] and [[Cosmarium]] also prevalent. Several [[Synedra]] diatom species occurred in low numbers. The authors found no evidence of treatment-specific species changes, which contrasts with several earlier studies and with the [[Single Limiting Nutrient Hypothesis]]. They suggest that responses may have been expressed as changes in yield rather than in species composition.

7. **Conclusion.** Nitrogen and phosphorus jointly limit phytoplankton in Lynx Lake, and carbon does not. The authors interpret this as evidence that the lake is co-limited by N and P, consistent with its low nutrient status and sparse phytoplankton community.
