# Desert Pupfish Permit Amendment & Transport Record

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This document set is not a primary research paper but a series of regulatory permit applications, amendments, and correspondence (1987–1992) concerning the interinstitutional transfer of the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) from a captive broodstock maintained by Dr. [[Wendell L. Minckley]] at [[Arizona State University]] to [[The Phoenix Zoo]]. The stock, described as progeny of legally obtained wild-collected fish, was authorized by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) for transport (~10 miles) and release into the zoo's cheetah exhibit water moat for conservation, public education, and captive propagation purposes. The record documents an initial acquisition of 50 individuals (8 September 1992) out of an authorized 100, under permit #LA87-04 and its 1992 amendment. The file also references a prior catastrophic population loss event at the same facility, providing an important husbandry case study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Taxonomic classification**: Order [[Cyprinodontiformes]], Family [[Cyprinodontidae]], Genus *Cyprinodon*.
- **Native range context**: Species is endemic to Sonoran Desert aquatic habitats (implied by "Southwestern fish" designation and endangered status); wild stock originally sourced from natural habitat prior to captive breeding at ASU.
- **Captive habitat used in this case**: An artificial water moat associated with a zoo cheetah exhibit, retrofitted with a multi-grade gravel filter to support the species.
- **Documented threat — invasive species interaction**: A prior population crash at The Phoenix Zoo was caused by accidental introduction of native [[Bluegill]] and [[Largemouth Bass]], likely via eggs transported through the water pumping system into the moats. This resulted in total extirpation of the pupfish population from that exhibit, illustrating predation/competition pressure from non-native or incidentally introduced fish species as a major risk factor for small endangered pupfish populations in artificial refugia.
- **Remediation measures**: Moats were drained, chemically treated (poisoned), dried, and resealed to eliminate residual unwanted fish prior to restocking — a standard eradication protocol for safeguarding reintroduction sites.

## Key Findings
- **Permit history**: Original USDI permit #LA87-04 (1987) amended in 1992 to authorize transport of up to 100 Desert Pupfish from Dr. Minckley's ASU stock to The Phoenix Zoo.
- **Transport logistics**: Fish were transported in large plastic trash cans over a distance of approximately 10 miles, with capture and transport overseen directly by Dr. Minckley.
- **Regulatory conditions imposed by USFWS**:
  - Recipient (Phoenix Zoo) must properly care for specimens.
  - Any mortality must be reported to the Endangered Species Division within 24 hours.
  - No disposal or release of specimens without prior written USFWS approval.
  - Annual activity reports required, due by 1 December each year, including any research data relevant to species conservation/recovery.
- **Actual transfer outcome**: On 8 September 1992, 50 desert pupfish were received by the Phoenix Zoo and deposited into the cheetah exhibit moat (of the 100 authorized).
- **Personnel and institutional roles**:
  - **Reg Hoyt**, Animal Collection Curator at Phoenix Zoo (>10 years marine/freshwater aquaria experience), responsible for husbandry.
  - **Dr. Wendell L. Minckley** (ASU), recognized authority on desert fishes, served in an advisory capacity and directly managed capture/transport.
  - Phoenix Zoo indicated willingness to participate in cooperative breeding programs and studbook contributions.
- **Conservation rationale**: Stocking the cheetah exhibit moat was framed as serving dual purposes — enhancing public awareness of endangered Southwestern fish species and providing an additional captive breeding/propagation site for *C. macularius*.
- **Historical mortality event**: Complete loss of a previously held Desert Pupfish population at the same facility due to inadvertent introduction of predatory/competitive sportfish species (Bluegill, Largemouth Bass), underscoring biosecurity vulnerabilities in shared water systems for endangered species refugia.
- **No costs to USFWS**: All transport costs were borne by The Phoenix Zoo.
