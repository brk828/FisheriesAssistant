# Aravaipa Canyon Fish Sampling and Wilderness Permit Correspondence (2000)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a short file of correspondence and survey results from [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness]] in Arizona, dated 2000. It contains three items:

1. A [[Bureau of Land Management]] letter (2 February 2000) from the [[Safford District Office]] to [[W.L. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]] about a day-use permit and visitor rules for the canyon.
2. A summary of an [[Fish Sampling]] trip (27–29 November 2000) that reports catch counts for nine sites along Aravaipa Creek, with the October trip having been postponed because of flooding.
3. An email (23 October 2000) from [[Peter Unmack]] reporting [[Fish Sampling]] results from 21 October 2000, broken down by sampling location.

The fish data show a community dominated by [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) across most sites, with native suckers and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) prominent at several others. Several non-native species also appear in small numbers.

## Background & Description

**Permit and access.** The BLM letter grants Permit #AC?-0-266 for day use (the first characters are illegible in the scan). The permit sets two conditions:

- Permit holders must notify the office before the trip if party size, length of stay or reservations change. Changes can be made by telephone through the BLM Information Receptionist.
- Permit holders must register at the parking lot fee station on arrival. Failure to register results in being counted as a "No Show."

Non-compliance can lead to penalties including a one-year suspension from the canyon. The letter states a **50 person per day** canyon limit, intended to maintain a high-quality experience. The letter was signed by Steve Knox, Outdoor Recreation Planner, and enclosed an Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness brochure.

**Sampling trip.** The fish sampling summary states that the October trip was postponed because of moderate flood events that lasted five days. The trip that did run covered 27–29 November 2000 (the scan reads "11127 - 11129100"). Neither flood exceeded a two-year flood event, but both lasted longer than a typical summer monsoon increase in flow.

**Email report.** The October email reports results of an [[Fish Sampling]] effort on 21 October 2000, led by Peter Unmack of ASU. It notes that no *Cyprinella* (Red Shiner) were collected. The creek had risen about three feet in the week before sampling. Minckley estimated the flow at approximately 1000 cfs based on that degree of rise.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Aravaipa Creek is a perennial desert stream flowing through a wilderness canyon. Its fish assemblage contains a mix of native cyprinids and catostomids and several non-native taxa.

The sampling data show these ecological patterns:

- **Flow disturbance:** The flood events in the autumn 2000 sampling period were longer than a typical monsoon flow increase, though they did not reach a two-year recurrence level. Such prolonged high flows can affect fish distribution and sampling success, and they explain the postponement of the October trip.
- **Native species:** Native taxa include [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Gila Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*). Several of these are of conservation concern in the Gila basin.
- **Non-native species:** [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) and *Gambusia* (mosquitofish) appear in low numbers at some sites. These are non-native to the Gila basin and are of management interest.
- **Hybridization:** The October summary lists one "hybrid sucker" at the upper end site, which points to possible hybridization among sucker taxa.

## Key Findings

### Permit and visitor management
- Permit #AC?-0-266 is a day-use permit.
- The canyon limit is 50 persons per day.
- Visitors must notify the BLM before any change and register at the fee station on arrival. Penalties include a one-year suspension.

### Fish catch by site (November 2000 trip)

Counts are reported as given. Percentages are as listed in the source.

| Site | Total fish | Dominant taxon (count, %) | Other notable taxa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chimney Rock | 429 | *Agosia chrysogaster* 317 (73.9%) | *Tiaroga cobitis* 93 (21.7%) |
| Hell Hole Canyon | 116 | *Pantosteus clarki* 35 (30.2%) | *Meda fulgida* 33 (28.4%); *Agosia* 31 (26.7%) |
| Painted Cave | 127 | *Agosia* 60 (47.2%) | *Pantosteus* 42 (33.1%) |
| Wagner Ranch | 197 | *Agosia* 107 (54.3%) | *Tiaroga* 49 (24.9%); *Pantosteus* 27 (13.7%) |
| Guest House | 152 | *Agosia* 66 (43.4%) | *Tiaroga* 40 (26.3%); *Catostomus insignis* 16 (10.5%) |
| Turkey Creek | 117 | *Tiaroga* 74 (63.2%) | *Meda* 21 (17.9%); *Agosia* 18 (15.4%) |
| Horse Camp | 73 | *Catostomus insignis* 21 (28.8%) | *Pantosteus* 15 (20.5%); *Lepomis cyanellus* 16 (21.9%) |
| White Ranch | 94 | *Agosia* 64 (68.1%) | *Tiaroga* 21 (22.3%) |
| Sycamore Tree | 435 | *Agosia* 356 (81.8%) | *Cyprinella lutrensis* 32 (7.4%); *Pantosteus* 32 (7.2%) |

Across these nine sites, *Agosia chrysogaster* is the most frequent taxon at six, *Tiaroga cobitis* is dominant at Turkey Creek, and *Pantosteus clarki* is dominant at Hell Hole Canyon. The per-species counts listed for Sycamore Tree sum to about 421 of the reported 435, so some records may be missing from the summary.

### October 2000 email sampling (21 October)
- The email lists counts for about ten locations, from the upper end at the TNC house down to Sycamore Tree. Locations include Chimney Rock, Turkey Creek confluence, Parsons Canyon confluence, House Canyon confluence, Virgus Canyon region, Woods Ranch (trail end) and White's Ranch.
- The highest counts are at Chimney Rock, with *Agosia* 294, *Pantosteus* 11, *Meda* 39, *Tiaroga* 32 and *Catostomus* 13.
- At the upper end (TNC house), counts include *Agosia* 50, *Pantosteus* 56, *Tiaroga* 12, *Catostomus* 23 and one hybrid sucker.
- At Sycamore Tree, *Agosia* is recorded at 142, with *Pantosteus* 7, *Tiaroga* 6, *Lepomis* 5 and *Gambusia* 2.
- No *Cyprinella* were collected in the October sampling.

### Flow conditions
- Creek stage rose about three feet in the week before the 21 October sampling.
- Flow was estimated at about 1000 cfs by Minckley.
- Flood events in the period lasted about five days, longer than typical summer monsoon increases, but did not exceed a two-year flood.
