# A Creel Census of Streams in District II

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1962 completion report presents the third consecutive year of creel census data on [[Oak Creek]] (Arizona), focused on managing a stocked [[Rainbow Trout]] fishery, alongside a limited supplementary census of the [[Channel Catfish]] fishery at [[Lee's Ferry]] on the [[Colorado River]]. An estimated 83,000 fishing trips occurred on Oak Creek in 1962, with overall angler success of **0.432–0.435 fish/man-hour**, falling short of the department's target of 0.5 fish/man-hour despite planting 139,000 catchable-sized trout. A 1.1-mile "artificial lure only" zone (Section III) was instituted on July 1, 1962, reducing fishing pressure in that section by an estimated 38% while producing slightly higher and more evenly distributed angler success compared to the "any lure" area (Section II). At Lee's Ferry, a limited March–June census found low fishing pressure (~3.5 anglers/weekend day) targeting Channel Catfish, with a success rate of 0.8 fish/man-hour; this fishery was expected to be fundamentally altered by the impending closure of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and filling of [[Lake Powell]] in 1963.

## Background & Description

Limited morphological data are reported, as this is a fisheries-management creel census rather than a taxonomic study:

- **Channel Catfish (Lee's Ferry):** The overwhelming majority of fish captured measured **8–12 inches** in length; one outlier of **10 pounds** was recorded.
- **Squawfish (Colorado Pikeminnow):** A single specimen was noted incidentally among catfish catches; no size data given.
- **Rainbow Trout (Oak Creek):** No individual size/weight data reported; fish are referenced only as "catchable sized" hatchery-reared stock used for put-and-take stocking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Oak Creek** functions as a heavily stocked, put-and-take trout stream subject to intense recreational angling pressure. Fish are planted approximately weekly; angler success spikes sharply the day after planting before dropping to a lower baseline for the remainder of the week (see [[Fish Stocking Dynamics]]), indicating rapid depletion of newly introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] under high fishing pressure.
- The **"artificial lure only" area** (Section III) represents an experimental regulatory niche restricting bait type (flies, spinners, spoons permitted; natural/prepared baits such as worms, salmon eggs, cheese, dough, corn forbidden) to test effects on angler distribution, pressure, and catch equity.
- **Lee's Ferry** represented a warmwater riverine fishery dominated by Channel Catfish, occupying a transitional reach of the [[Colorado River]] soon to be inundated by [[Lake Powell]] following completion of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], fundamentally converting the habitat from lotic river to reservoir/tailwater system.
- Angler demographic ecology: non-resident anglers comprised 19.5% of Oak Creek fishermen in 1962 (up from 13.1% in 1961, 18.3% in 1959); juvenile anglers (<14 yrs) made up 28.1% of Section II and 24.5% of Section III anglers, indicating minimal exclusionary effect of the lure restriction on youth participation.

## Key Findings

- **Fishing pressure trends (Oak Creek):** Estimated total trips — 60,000 (1959), 92,000 (1961), 83,000 (1962). Pressure ratio relative to 1959: 1.53× (1961), 1.38× (1962).
- **Angler success:** Overall 1962 success = 0.432–0.435 fish/man-hour, below the 0.5 target despite record stocking (139,000 fish, +23,000 over 1961).
- **Stocking-success relationship:** A stable empirical relationship was derived across three census years — approximately **1.9 catchable trout per fishing trip** are required to sustain 0.5 fish/man-hour success (values: 1.87 in 1959, 1.89 in 1961, 1.94 in 1962), enabling predictive stocking models (see [[Stocking Rate Formula]]).
- **Artificial Lure Only Area (Section III) effects:**
  - Fishing pressure reduced ~38% relative to expected "any lure" baseline (2,344 actual trips vs. 3,790 projected, Jul–Oct).
  - Slightly higher average success: 0.466 fish/man-hour (Section III) vs. 0.430 (Section II) during Jul–Oct.
  - More equitable success distribution across the week between plantings, though first-day-post-planting catch rates were paradoxically *lower* than in Section II.
  - Negligible discriminatory impact on juvenile or non-resident anglers.
- **Bait usage:** In the "any lure" section, 97.3% of anglers used natural/prepared bait; only 2.7% used artificial lures — underscoring the behavioral shift required by the new regulation.
- **Day-after-planting effect:** Across all study years, angler success was consistently highest the day immediately following stocking, then declined sharply (e.g., 1962 Section II: 0.63 → 0.26 fish/man-hour, a 59% drop).
- **Lee's Ferry fishery:** Average of 3.5 anglers/weekend day; success = 0.8 fish/man-hour; average trip length 4.9 hours; nearly all anglers resided in nearby Page, AZ. Fishing pressure declined sharply as summer air temperatures reached 120°F, ending the census.
- **Management recommendation:** Extend "artificial lure only" designation to the entire Oak Creek trout fishery (Red Rock Crossing to headwaters, including tributaries) from May 1–Labor Day, 1963, alongside a planned stocking of ~125,000 catchable trout, adaptively managed through the season.
