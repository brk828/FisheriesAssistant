## Executive Summary

This document is a **U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) budget allocation report**, not a biological or morphological study. It catalogs **Operations and Maintenance (O&M) funding and Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staffing levels** across the National Fish Hatchery System for Fiscal Years 1992 and 1993. The data is organized by subactivity codes distinguishing **anadromous hatchery operations (1311)**, **inland/Great Lakes hatchery operations (1312)**, **hatchery maintenance and rehabilitation (1313)**, and **hatchery construction (2830)**, among other minor codes (e.g., 1412 Fish Research, 2850 Dam Safety, 2855 Bridge Safety, 2895 Emergency Construction, 4710 Lower Snake River Compensation Plan). Reimbursable funding from partner agencies (NMFS, TVA, Army Corps of Engineers) is explicitly excluded. The report spans over 50 individual hatchery stations, fish technology centers, and fish disease control facilities nationwide, with a **grand total of $47,063K and 432 FTEs in FY1993**, compared to **$42,485K and 441 FTEs in FY1992**.

Because the source is a fiscal/administrative record rather than a species-level scientific study, standard ichthyological categories (morphology, ecological niche) are reinterpreted below as **hatchery system characteristics** and **programmatic/ecological context**, consistent with the actual content of the document.

## Background & Description

This report contains no direct biological descriptions of fish species. It functions purely as an institutional funding ledger. Species reared at each station (e.g., Chinook and coho salmon at Pacific Northwest anadromous stations such as [[Coleman NFH]] and [[Leavenworth NFH]]; landlocked Atlantic salmon at [[Craig Brook NFH]] and [[Green Lake NFH]]; warmwater and coolwater species at southern/midwestern stations like [[Tishomingo NFH]] and [[Uvalde NFH]]) are implied by station geography and hatchery type designation (Anadromous vs. Inland/Great Lakes) rather than explicitly enumerated.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report's structure reflects the **functional ecological classification of hatchery operations** rather than wild population ecology:

- **Anadromous Hatchery Operations (Subactivity 1311)**: Stations supporting anadromous salmonids that migrate between freshwater and marine environments, concentrated in the Pacific Northwest ([[Columbia River Basin]], [[Snake River Basin]]) and New England (Atlantic salmon restoration). Examples include [[Nisqually Hatchery]], [[Entiat NFH]], [[Quinault NFH]], [[Winthrop NFH]], and [[Green Lake NFH]].
- **Inland/Great Lakes Hatchery Operations (Subactivity 1312)**: Stations supporting non-anadromous freshwater fisheries, including Great Lakes stocking programs ([[Iron River NFH]], [[Genoa NFH]], [[Jordan River NFH]], [[Pendills Creek NFH]]) and interior river/reservoir sport fisheries ([[Dexter NFH]], [[Neosho NFH]], [[Wolf Creek NFH]]).
- **Lower Snake River Compensation Plan (Subactivity 4710)**: A distinct mitigation program ([[L Snake River Comp Plan]], [[Dworshak NFH]], [[Hagerman NFH]], [[Kooskia NFH]]) funded to compensate for anadromous fish losses caused by hydroelectric dam construction on the Snake River — an important ecological-mitigation linkage to [[Hydroelectric Dam Impacts on Anadromous Fish]].
- **Fish Disease Control and Technology Centers**: Facilities such as the [[Bozeman Fish Technology Center]], [[Fish Disease Control Lab]], and [[Lamar Fishery Tech Center]] represent supporting infrastructure for hatchery health management rather than direct rearing ecology.
- **Historic/Interpretive Hatcheries**: [[DC Booth Historic Fish Hatchery]] appears twice (South Dakota), reflecting a dual operational and historic-interpretive role within the system.

## Key Findings

- **Total O&M funding increased** from $42,485K (FY1992) to $47,063K (FY1993), despite a **slight decrease in total FTEs** (441 → 432), suggesting increased per-FTE funding intensity or capital/maintenance cost growth outpacing staffing.
- **Hatchery Construction (2830)** spending was highly uneven across stations — e.g., [[Coleman NFH]] saw construction funding rise sharply from $1,608K (FY1992) to $2,702K (FY1993), and [[DC Booth Historic Fish Hatchery]] jumped from $63K to $2,188K, indicating major one-time capital projects.
- The **[[L Snake River Comp Plan]]** (Subactivity 4710) represented one of the single largest station allocations in the dataset ($10,649K, 13 FTEs reported), reflecting the scale of federal mitigation commitments tied to hydropower development in the Columbia/Snake River system.
- **Maintenance and Rehabilitation (1313)** funding fluctuated significantly year-to-year at several stations (e.g., [[Winthrop NFH]] increased from $36K to $578K), likely reflecting episodic infrastructure repair cycles rather than steady-state ecological operations.
- Staffing (FTEs) remained essentially flat or slightly declined at most large anadromous stations (e.g., [[Coleman NFH]] steady at 20 FTEs), suggesting operational stability despite budget volatility.
- The explicit **exclusion of reimbursable funding** (NMFS, TVA, COE) means total federal investment in these hatcheries is understated relative to actual combined program spending — an important caveat for any downstream funding or population-support analysis tied to [[Federal Fish Hatchery Funding Trends]].
- The dataset provides a valuable structural snapshot for tracking the historical **federal fish hatchery infrastructure investment** pattern in the early 1990s, useful for cross-referencing with [[Pacific Salmon Recovery Programs]] and [[Great Lakes Fisheries Restoration]] initiatives of the same era.
