# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Native Fish Research Coordination Meeting

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #humpback-chub #research-planning

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, dated November 24, 1992, outlines coordination plans for a December 1, 1992 meeting of principle investigators conducting native fish research in [[Grand Canyon]]. The document focuses on integrating research efforts across four organizations studying [[Humpback Chub]] (Gila cypha) and planning the 1993 spawning season fieldwork. Dr. W.L. Minckley agreed to moderate the meeting, which was structured to facilitate one-on-one discussions between research groups and develop unified permit requests and data integration strategies.

## Background & Description

The GCES program coordinated endangered fish research through four primary contractors: [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), and [[Bio/West]]. Each organization conducted complementary studies on [[Humpback Chub]] biology, habitat use, and population dynamics in the [[Colorado River]] system below [[Glen Canyon Dam]].

The meeting was restricted to field researchers (maximum three representatives per organization) who had conducted work in [[Grand Canyon]] within the previous year. A separate meeting for administrators was scheduled for December 2, 1992. Attendees included: Rich Valdez, Bill Masslich, and Bill Leibfried (Bio/West); Owen Gorman and Stuart Leon (USFWS); Michael Douglas and Paul Marsh (ASU); Rob Clarkson, Glen Doster, and A.T. Robinson (AGFD); with W.L. Minckley as moderator.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Research focused on understanding [[Humpback Chub]] biology in relation to [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and flow fluctuations. Studies addressed critical life history stages including spawning, larval development, and juvenile survival in both mainstem and tributary habitats. The program examined interactions between native and nonnative fish species, habitat availability during flow fluctuations, and the effects of altered thermal regimes on endangered fish populations.

Key habitat types under investigation included spawning areas, backwaters, nursery habitats, and tributary mouths as potential reintroduction sites. Research also evaluated behavioral responses of larval and juvenile fish to flow fluctuations and thermal conditions.

## Key Findings

**Research Coordination Objectives:**

The meeting agenda structured interactions to maximize information exchange through:
- 9:00 AM: Group introductions and 10-minute presentations from each organization
- 10:00-11:00 AM: First paired meetings (ASU+Bio/West; AGFD+USFWS)
- 11:00 AM-12:00 PM: Second paired meetings (ASU+USFWS; Bio/West+AGFD)
- 1:00-2:00 PM: Third paired meetings (ASU+AGFD; USFWS+Bio/West)
- 2:00-4:30 PM: Full group synthesis and discussion

**Required Information from Each Group:**

1. Planned data collection for 1993 field season
2. Information needs from other researchers' 1991-1992 field seasons
3. Critical data gaps for understanding [[Gila cypha]] biology in [[Grand Canyon]]
4. Permit requirements for unified permit applications

**Arizona Game and Fish Department Study Objectives:**

- Temporal and spatial distribution patterns for larval fish
- Propagation of native fishes
- Changes in backwaters and nursery habitat during fluctuations
- Behavioral response of larval and juvenile natives to flow fluctuations
- [[Otolith]] studies
- Age and growth for native species
- Thermal tolerance studies
- Diet analysis of young fish

**Arizona State University Study Objectives:**

- Population genetics
- Spawning habitat identification and evaluation
- Spawning success assessment
- Survivorship and growth of post-larval fish
- Nonnative fish impacts through digestive rate experiments, stomach contents analysis, and predation verification

**U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Study Objectives:**

- Identify preferred habitat of [[Gila cypha]] in [[Little Colorado River]]
- Identify spawning habitat
- Evaluate flood hydrograph effects
- Identify tributaries for possible reintroductions

**Bio/West Study Objectives:**

- Resource use (food and habitat)
- Reproductive capacity and success in mainstem
- Survivorship of early life stages in mainstem
- Native-nonnative interactions
- Life history schedule and population model development

**Data Integration Framework:**

The program outlined a long-term monitoring and data integration plan spanning 1993-1997, with the goal of producing an integrated native fish report synthesizing findings from all four research programs. The flow chart identified needs for additional studies specific to management alternatives and ongoing data collection to support [[Endangered Species Act]] compliance and adaptive management decisions.
