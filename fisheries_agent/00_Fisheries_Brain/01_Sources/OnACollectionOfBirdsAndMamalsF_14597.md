# Birds and Mammals of the Colorado Delta, Lower California (1905 Collection)

#ornithology #mammalogy #rodentia

## Executive Summary

This report, written by [[Witmer Stone]] with field notes by [[Samuel N. Rhoads]], documents a February 1905 collection of 258 bird specimens and 117 mammal specimens from the [[Colorado Delta]] in northwestern Lower California. The expedition was made for the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]]. Rhoads' field work was hampered by unusually heavy rain, cold and high water, which kept animal life largely inactive. The report confirms the presence of many species, comments on subspecific identity and taxonomy, and includes field observations on behavior and habitat. The document contains no fish data.

## Background & Description

**Expedition route and dates.** Rhoads left [[Yuma, Arizona]] in an open rowboat on February 4, 1905, accompanied by H. E. Wilder of Riverside and a guide. The party descended the [[Colorado River]] to the mouth of the [[Hardy River]], a slackwater bayou about ten miles above the head of the Gulf of California. They reached the Hardy on February 8, where they acquired a Cocopah guide, Frank Tejano. They then ascended the Hardy to the base of the [[Cocopah Mountains]], reaching Bruce's ranch on February 15, Pescadoro Slough on February 18, and Cocopah Major Mountain on February 19. The final leg was overland to Calexico, California, after five days of difficult travel through mud and sloughs.

**Conditions.** More than half of the days were rainy, and total precipitation was reported as greater than usually falls in this desert region over several years. On February 13 the temperature reached 34°F, with ice in the coffee pot. The report describes the conditions as "peculiarly unfavorable to collecting of any sort."

**Format.** Species accounts give the number and locality of specimens, identifications (some confirmed by specialists, including [[W. H. Osgood]] and [[J. A. G. Rehn]]), and quoted field notes from Rhoads. The report also includes a supplementary list of species observed but not collected, and a list of additional birds observed by Rhoads.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Mammals.** Rodent communities were concentrated by habitat:
- The [[Desert Cotton Rat]] (*Sigmodon hispidus eremicus*, 19 specimens) was abundant in overflow lands where tules and reeds provided permanent food. Rhoads described it as "essentially littoral," not an upland species.
- The [[Lower California Harvest Mouse]] (*Reithrodontomys longicaudus pallidus*, 6 specimens) was found only in fine grass on higher banks that were still temporarily flooded during peak spring tides and freshets.
- The [[White-throated Wood Rat]] (*Neotoma intermedia albigula*, 5 specimens) built large bush nests at several delta locations. Its habits were compared to those of mesquite rats near Corpus Christi, Texas, and it fed on bark, twigs, seeds, and mesquite pods.
- The [[Desert Mouse]] (*Peromyscus eremicus*) was abundant in the Cocopah Mountains, where Rhoads' notes describe it as a rock-loving mountain species.
- The [[Sonora White-footed Mouse]] (*Peromyscus sonoriensis*) was the dominant low-ground form, with 19 specimens from the Hardy mouth and 5 from the Colorado 35 miles below Yuma. Rhoads reported it was "excessively abundant in the bottoms," repeatedly springing traps and preventing capture of other small rodents.
- Two [[Pocket Mouse]] species were recorded: the [[Slender-nosed Pocket Mouse]] (3 Cocopah and 1 Colorado specimens) and the [[Spiny Pocket Mouse]] (11 specimens from the Cocopah Mountains). Rhoads observed that both nest in burrows at the base of low bushes and are closely associated with kangaroo rats. He suspected brief intermittent hibernation in February, based on few captures on the coldest nights.
- The [[San Felipe Kangaroo Rat]] (*Dipodomys merriami arenivagus*, 3 specimens) came from one burrow at the base of Mount Major.

**Carnivores and ungulates.** Traces of [[Coyote]] (5 skulls, 4 skins) were abundant, with Rhoads' companion reporting they were "overabundant and easily trapped." The [[Arizona Hare]] was uncommon, which Rhoads attributed to heavy coyote predation. [[Raccoon]] occurred along the river. Signs of [[Mule Deer]] (*Odocoileus hemionus eremicus*), [[Pronghorn]] (*Antilocapra americana mexicana*, tracks only), [[Beaver]] (*Castor canadensis frondator*, trapped in winter on the lower Colorado), [[Cougar]], [[Bobcat]], and [[Badger]] were recorded, though many were seen only as tracks or reports.

**Marine mammals.** A single [[Gill's Dolphin]] skull (*Tursiops gillii*) was found on the Hardy River, 50 miles above the Gulf.

**Birds.** The avifauna included desert and riparian species such as [[Gambel's Quail]], [[Gila Woodpecker]], [[Verdin]], [[Phainopepla]], and [[Abert's Towhee]]. Mesquite groves and their mistletoe berries supported many birds. Rhoads noted that Gambel's Quail were "subsisting almost wholly on the mistletoe berries," and that Gila Woodpeckers and Phainopeplas also fed on them.

**Waterbirds.** Water-associated birds were the most conspicuous in the field. [[American White Pelican]] and [[Mexican Cormorant]] were abundant and fished together. At Mount Major, pelicans regularly gathered in a flood-formed lagoon, sometimes numbering about 500, while hundreds of cormorants dived nearby. Rhoads also noted [[Great Blue Heron]] as "very abundant," [[Western Gull]] "very abundant" on the Colorado and its tributaries, and [[Mallard]] as "abundant." Several other ducks, geese, and shorebirds were seen in smaller numbers.

## Key Findings

**Collection and taxonomy**
- The collection comprised 258 birds and 117 mammals, identified by Stone.
- Stone supported the subspecific status of the Lower Californian woodpecker *Dryobates scalaris lucasanus* and argued against raising it to species rank, as [[William Brewster]] had proposed.
- Stone noted that *Peromyscus sonoriensis* may be more closely related to the eastern *P. gossypinus* than to *P. leucopus*. He based this view on habitat and behavior alone, not anatomy.
- Stone reported that three specimens of *Perognathus fallax pallidus* from Mission Creek, California, had been separated as a distinct race by Rhoads in 1895, but the name remained in manuscript until the specimens were referred to *P. penicillatus augustirostris*.
- Stone questioned the separability of the *Dipodomys* race *arenivagus* and of the *Agelaius phoeniceus sonoriensis* race from *neutralis*.
- Stone found it "very unlikely" that three coyote species occur together in the San Pedro Mártir Mountains, as Elliot had stated.

**Behavior and natural history**
- Several rodent observations were recorded in the field, including the relationship between pocket mice and kangaroo rats, and a possible brief hibernation in February.
- [[Costa's Hummingbird]] was found breeding in February. One specimen showed "bodily marks of protracted incubation" on February 21.
- [[Rufous Hummingbird]] was engaged in courtship in February, which Rhoads compared with breeding behavior observed near Puget Sound in April 1903.
- [[Abert's Towhee]] was described as resembling a female cardinal grosbeak in appearance and habit. Stone concluded that the systematists had correctly placed it in *Pipilo*.
- A [[Verdin]] was found sitting in its gourd-shaped roosting nest with its tail projecting out of the opening so it could void excrement onto the ground and keep the nest clean.

**Conditions affecting collecting**
- Extreme rain, cold and high water limited field work. Rhoads wrote that animal life "was at a standstill and the results of field work most discouraging."
- Floods drove deer to the uplands, so they were rarely seen, though old tracks were abundant.

**Summary of wildlife records**
- Mammal records include 9 rodent species, several carnivores (coyote, raccoon, cougar, bobcat, badger, ringtail, and fox), and 1 cetacean.
- Bird records (in the main list and supplementary lists) cover numerous families, including raptors (such as [[Bald Eagle]], [[Osprey]], [[Golden Eagle]], and [[Caracara]]), waterfowl (such as [[Lesser Snow Goose]] and [[Red-breasted Merganser]]), and passerines.
- Rhoads' supplementary list of additional species includes common and widespread birds such as [[Turkey Vulture]], [[Red-tailed Hawk]], and [[Killdeer]].
