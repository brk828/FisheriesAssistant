# Proposal for Native Fish Restoration in the Blue River Drainage

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This scoping report, issued in October 2004, announces a joint proposal by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and the [[Forest Service]] to restore native fish in the [[Blue River]] drainage of eastern Arizona. The agencies would eliminate nonnative predatory fish from 5 tributary streams of the upper drainage and protect native fishes in the mainstem Blue River. The [[Environmental Assessment]] is being prepared with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The report's purpose is to inform interested parties and solicit public comment that will help define the issues the Environmental Assessment must address.

## Background & Description

**Threat context.** The document states that the introduction and spread of [[Nonnative Fishes]] is one of the most pervasive threats to native fish communities in the southwestern United States. Nonnative fishes have reduced or eliminated native populations through predation, competition, hybridization, and other factors.

**Regulatory drivers.**
- **[[Central Arizona Project]] Biological Opinion (2001):** The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service issued a revised opinion on delivering Central Arizona Project water to the Gila River basin and the risk of spreading nonnative aquatic species. It addressed possible serious and adverse effects on several listed native Arizona fishes. Its conservation measures direct Reclamation to build a fish barrier in the Blue River and fund native fish recovery and protection in the Gila River basin.
- **[[Endangered Species Act]], Section 7(a)(1):** Federal agencies must use their authorities to conserve threatened and endangered species.
- **Forest Service policy:** Recover listed species so special protections are no longer needed, prevent nonlisted species from becoming threatened through Forest Service actions, and support reintroduction onto suitable unoccupied habitat where it promotes recovery.
- **[[National Forest Management Act]] (1976):** The Forest Service must provide for national forest biological diversity and maintain viable populations.
- **[[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] Plan (1987, as amended):** The plan sets goals, objectives, standards, and guidelines for viable native fish populations and recovery of federally listed species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal targets the native coldwater and warmwater fish assemblage of the Blue River and its tributaries. The main species named is the [[Gila Trout]], which the Forest Service would work to recover in 5 upper Blue River tributaries. The document does not report population counts, survey data, or water quality measurements.

The Environmental Assessment is expected to examine the following:
- effects on biological resources, including special status and non-target species
- effects on cultural resources
- effects on water quality
- effects on primitive areas
- effects on the potential [[Wild and Scenic River]] designation of the Blue River
- effects on recreation
- effects on public safety and health

## Key Findings

**Proposed action**
- Eliminate nonnative predatory fish from 5 tributary streams in the upper Blue River drainage.
- Protect native fishes in the mainstem Blue River.
- Reclamation would construct a fish barrier on the Blue River.
- The Forest Service would implement Gila trout recovery in the 5 upper tributaries.

**Decisions pending**
- The co-lead agencies (Reclamation and the Forest Service) must choose among implementing the proposed action, modifying it, or taking no action.
- Under the no-action alternative, the Federal agencies would undertake no effort to enhance the Blue River native fishery.

**Public comment**
- Scoping comments help identify significant issues and mitigation for the Environmental Assessment.
- Comments submitted during scoping do **not** confer appeal eligibility, because of recent changes to Forest Service appeal regulations (36 CFR 215).
- To become eligible to appeal, a person must submit substantive comments during the **30-day comment period** that follows release of the draft Environmental Assessment (36 CFR 215.13).
- Interested parties can request a copy of the draft Environmental Assessment by asking to be placed on the mailing list.
