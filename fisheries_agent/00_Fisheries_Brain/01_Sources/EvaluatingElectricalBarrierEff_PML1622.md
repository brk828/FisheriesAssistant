# Electrical Barrier Evaluation for Preventing Downstream Entrainment of Endangered Fish in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a project proposal, circulated via the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] listserver, for a study evaluating an electrified weir wall intended to divert [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] away from the irrigation canal at the [[Tusher Wash Diversion Dam]] on the [[Green River]], Utah, and back into the river mainstem. The study has two phases: laboratory pilot experiments to characterize physiological responses to electric fields, and behavioral trials in a hatchery raceway system to measure passage success and barrier avoidance under varying flow and electrical settings.

## Background & Description

The proposal is attached to an email from Kevin McAbee (Instream Flow Coordinator, Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program) on 27 May 2014, which forwards a draft "Tusher Wash electrical barrier study" developed by Jackson Gross from input at January and February stakeholder meetings. It was prepared for discussion under agenda item 3a of a June 2014 webinar.

The project's main objective is to prevent downstream passage of three endangered Colorado River fishes, [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), while evaluating barrier effectiveness and characterizing species responses to an electric field. The Green River is described as 730 miles long, and these species depend on its mainstem for critical habitat and passage to spawning locations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Native Colorado River fishes have declined largely due to anthropogenic activity. The three focal species were once widespread but are now among the country's most imperiled.
- River regulation, including dams, altered flow regimes and reduced passage, is identified as a major driver. The Colorado River is described as one of the most regulated rivers in the country, in an arid climate, supplying water to over 30 million people across the southwestern United States and northwestern Mexico.
- The basin has two active spawning locations of Colorado Pikeminnow and Razorback Sucker, the largest and most productive Colorado Pikeminnow population, two known population centers of [[Humpback Chub]], and stocked populations of all four species (USFWS letter, 2010).
- The Tusher Wash Dam diverts water into the Green River Canal Company Main Canal. Entrainment of endangered fish has been documented there (Recovery Program FY 2013 Annual Project Report).
- The USFWS requires that projects minimize all forms of take to listed species and that the electric barrier be operated to reduce injury and mortality. Any solution must not adversely modify habitat in the Green River stretch.
- Electrofishing is a established sampling tool, but research on endangered Colorado River fishes has shown negative effects once electrical parameters exceed a threshold.

## Key Findings

**Proposed Design**
- A 3 ft high weir wall retrofitted with aluminum flat bar electrodes on the top quarter, built in a fiberglass raceway at approximately 40° downstream angle to flow, with electrode configuration modeled in [[COMSOL Multiphysics]].
- Traditional barriers operate at voltage gradients of 0.23 V/cm at frequencies below 10 Hz. The authors suggest higher voltages may be needed during high flows, while lower gradients could reduce both injury and operating costs.
- Conductivity will be held constant at the mean Green River value from 1992 to 2012.

**Experimental Design**
- Behavioral trials use two raceways, an experimental (electrified) and an acclimation (never electrified) raceway built to look visually identical. Fish receive at least a 48-hour acclimation after wild capture.
- Movement is tracked for 24 hours using [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tags]] detection arrays and overhead videography, analyzed with [[Noldus EthoVision XT]].
- Each treatment combination (flow rate, electrical setting, size class, species) is run in triplicate.
- Passage success (percentage) and barrier avoidance (percent occupancy) are the primary response variables; individual and group responses are both recorded.
- Mainstem return is excluded from the scope.

**Pilot Studies (Appendix 1)**
- Homogeneous fields are generated with [[Smith-Root Portable Electroanaesthesia System]] in a 742 L tank.
- Responses are scored into four categories: no response, twitch, taxis and tetanus. Treatments follow an incomplete Latin Square design, analyzed by multinomial logistic regression.
- Internal injury is ranked by vertebral injury (three classes, adapted from Muth and Ruppert, 1996) and hemorrhaging (three classes).

**Expected Results and Hypotheses**
- Passage success is expected to rise with flow rate and fall with field strength, with a possible tetanus threshold that could sweep fish over the barrier.
- Preliminary bighead and silver carp (1–60 cm) work reported 100% of fish could be swept at gradients below 1 V/cm at frequencies considered safe for people, with no incapacitation.
- The authors hypothesize that below 3 Hz, fish can be repelled at 1–2 ft from the wall without incapacitation.
- Physiological responses are expected to intensify with voltage, waveform and exposure time, with larger fish responding more strongly.

**Constraints and Open Issues**
- Hatchery pumps have previously achieved 1.8 ft/s approach velocity; it is uncertain whether they can reach the highest test flow. Solutions would require higher-capacity pumps or a lower wall.

**Relevance**
- The project addresses conservation needs identified by the Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program, including reducing entrainment at Tusher Wash Diversion.
