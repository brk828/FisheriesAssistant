# Gila Topminnow Reintroduction and Lineage Selection Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #topminnow

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of internal memoranda from [[Dean Hendrickson]] to members and consultants of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], written in 1989 and 1990. It sets out a draft plan for choosing which [[Gila Topminnow]] stocks (lineages) to reintroduce where, as input to the revised [[Recovery Plan]]. The plan is explicitly a starting point for discussion. It was prompted by a [[Forest Service]] proposal to stock about a dozen new sites, and it rests partly on subjective judgment. Key data from the 1989 survey by Bagley et al. were not yet available to the author.

## Background & Description

The Gila topminnow is a small fish of the Gila basin, and its surviving populations are geographically isolated. The team defines a **lineage** as a geographically isolated set of populations. Under the team's working definitions, the U.S. has three to five extant lineages, depending on how they are grouped (Vrijenhoek would combine them into one). The memoranda reflect a team consensus reached at a December 7, 1989 meeting, which proposed:

- **Revised recovery criteria:** recovery to be judged by the level of "security" for each genetic lineage, with genetic studies as a high-priority task.
- **Refugia:** stocks from each natural population to be secured in refugia or aquaria at the earliest opportunity.
- **Two habitat classes:** primary sites (most permanent and natural, long persistence expected, no other fish species) and secondary sites (more ephemeral, may hold other fish species).
- **Monitoring:** sampling at least twice a year in natural habitats and primary sites, and at least once every two years at secondary sites.

The working lineage list includes:

1. **San Rafael Valley lineage:** Sharp Spring, Sheehy Spring and the uppermost Santa Cruz populations.
2. **Sonoita Creek lineage:** Sonoita Creek, Redrock Canyon, Monkey Spring and Cottonwood Spring populations.
3. **Ciénega Creek lineage:** a single continuous population on BLM land.
4. **Bylas Springs complex:** Medicine/Bos, Middle Spring and Salt Creek populations.
5. **San Carlos and San Pedro drainages:** an elusive or extinct Ash Creek population and possibly extant San Pedro populations. These are included because searches are incomplete, especially in Sonora.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The main threat named is [[Gambusia]] (mosquitofish) contamination and competition, which makes it hard to maintain topminnow populations. Habitat quality varies widely, and the author notes that "Sonoita seem likely to separate more" in habitat and in its ability to resist Gambusia. The plan divides the Gila basin into roughly two dozen sub-basins, and marks those with known or possibly extant native populations. Sub-basins with no remaining native stock (for example, [[San Pedro River]], Avra-Altar, Salt, Agua Fria, Hassayampa, San Simón) could receive reintroductions. The [[Lower Gila]], below Phoenix, was suggested as an area where lineages should mix, as they probably did historically.

Other threats and contextual points noted:

- Long-term development trends threaten habitats throughout the species' range.
- Mexican populations need protection and demonstrated security, and Mexico needs a written implementation plan.
- Earlier stockings did not follow the scheme. Examples include Sharp Spring fish introduced at Redrock, where Bylas fish should have gone, and widespread scattering of Monkey Spring fish.

## Key Findings

**Stocking priorities.** The memo proposes this order for future stockings:

1. [[Bylas Springs Complex]]
2. [[Ciénega Creek]]
3. [[Cottonwood Spring]], [[Redrock Canyon]] and [[Sonoita Creek]] (grouped)
4. [[San Rafael Valley]]

**Proposed stocking sequence.** Stockings 1 to 10 alternate between the Bylas complex and Ciénega Creek. Stockings 11 to 20 alternate among Cottonwood, Sonoita and Redrock. Stockings 21 to 25 use San Rafael fish. Later stockings are to be evaluated against 1 to 25 and adjusted as needed.

**Replication ranking (by number of introduction sites with extant populations):**
- [[Monkey Spring]] (or stock containing those genes; "many" sites)
- [[Sharp Spring]] ("several")
- Ciénega Creek ("several")
- Middle Spring ("a couple")

Monkey Spring is excluded from the schedule because of its many successful introductions already.

**Site and sub-basin rules:**
- Any sub-basin with an extant native population should receive only fish from that population.
- No stockings should occur in the San Pedro or San Carlos sub-basins until surveys rule out remnant native populations.
- Sub-basins without native stock should be open to any lineage. The author argues that single-lineage assignment would leave some lineages never stocked.
- Each site should receive a single lineage. Intentional mixing at one site should wait for detailed field experiments on genetics and fitness.

**Habitat and gene-flow design.** The plan is designed to mimic historic gene flow. Fish move among primary and secondary sites under qualified management. Movement from natural populations to primary sites is one-way for now. The team suggested three primary and 10 secondary sites per lineage to start.

**Key recommendations and open questions:**
- Survey the Sonoran portions of the [[San Pedro River]] and the Santa Cruz as a very high priority.
- Conduct a thorough search of the U.S. native range for undiscovered remnant populations before downlisting.
- Conduct a [[Genetic Lineage]] study to test differentiation among Monkey, Cottonwood, Sonoita and Redrock, which the author notes is an assumption.
- Establish a single centralized data depository for all stocking and monitoring data before downlisting.
- Form a [[Management Implementation Committee]] to define the bureaucratic security criteria (numbers of populations and persistence periods) for [[Downlisting]].
- Note that [[Delisting]] is unlikely, because it requires eliminating the Gambusia threat.
- Sharp Spring fish are considered secure at Dexter and Heron. The author suspects Heron persists in a different location than recorded.

**Status of the documents.** The memo is a draft. The author asked for team comments by January 31, 1990, and noted that the 1989 survey appendix and the [[Bagley et al. Survey]] database were still incomplete.
