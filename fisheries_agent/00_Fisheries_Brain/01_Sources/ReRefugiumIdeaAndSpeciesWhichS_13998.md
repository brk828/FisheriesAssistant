# Coordinated Refugia Program for Nevada Endangered Fishes (1975 Correspondence)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 letter from [[James E. Deacon]], Professor of Biology at the [[University Of Nevada Las Vegas]], replies to [[Clark Hubbs]] of the University of Texas. It supports Hubbs' proposal for a coordinated refugium program for rare Nevada fishes. The letter lists refugia already in development or completed, names the Nevada species Deacon considers priorities, and suggests additional contacts. It is correspondence, not a research study, so it reports no new field data.

## Background & Description

The letter is a two-page reply dated April 17, 1975. Deacon agrees that the refugium idea is sound and notes that substantial work has already begun. He says the list of existing refugia is not exhaustive. The copy list (cc) includes more than ten regional fish biologists, among them [[Robert R. Miller]], [[W. L. Minckley]], [[Anthony Echelle]], and [[Carl L. Hubbs]].

The species named are mostly small, range-restricted fishes of desert aquatic systems, several of them endemic to single springs or river systems. Common names are not given in the document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Existing refugia (Nevada):** Artificial or natural refugia were in varying stages of development or completion for eight taxa: *Cyprinodon diabolis*, *Empetrichthys latos*, *Crenichthys baileyi*, *Gila robusta jordani*, *Moapa coriacea*, *C. nevadensis mionectes*, *C. nevadensis pectoralis*, and *Crenichthys nevadae*.
- **Hatchery work:** The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] had successfully reared *[[Xyrauchen Texanus]]* and *Ptychocheilus lucius*, and was attempting *Gila elegans* that spring. (The OCR rendering "Tlow Beach" has been corrected.)
- **Out-of-state refugium:** The [[Phoenix Zoo]] had developed preliminary plans for a refugium area on its grounds. Funding from the [[New York Zoological Society]], channeled through Bob Miller, had gone into the project.
- **Priority species (Nevada):** Deacon ranked the candidates for inclusion in the program. The top priorities were *Moapa coriacea*, *Gila robusta jordani*, and *Empetrichthys latos*. The list also included the [[Ash Meadows]] stock of *Rhinichthys osculus*, *Chasmistes cujus*, *Cyprinodon diabolis*, the *C. nevadensis* subspecies, *Pantosteus clarki intermedius*, *Lepidomeda albivallis*, the [[Virgin River]] stock of *Rhinichthys osculus* (the OCR rendering "unite River" has been corrected), *Eremichthys acros*, *Gila elegans*, *Xyrauchen texanus*, a *Plagopterus* taxon, *Lepidomeda mollispinis*, *Crenichthys baileyi*, *Crenichthys nevadae*, and *Relictus solitarius*. Deacon said the list was illustrative and might be incomplete. Including the garbled entries, the list covers roughly 18 taxa.
- **Contacts:** Deacon named five additional people who could help: [[Dave Koch]] (Desert Research Institute, Reno), [[Jim Yoakum]] ([[Bureau Of Land Management]], Reno), [[Tom Trelease]] and [[Dale Lockard]] ([[Nevada Department Of Fish And Game]], Reno), and [[Jim LaBounty]] ([[Bureau Of Reclamation]], Denver).

## Key Findings

1. **Refugia are already under way.** Eight Nevada taxa were in various stages of refugium development, and hatchery propagation had succeeded for at least two species.
2. **A coordinated program is needed.** Deacon agrees with Hubbs that a coordinated approach would be highly desirable. He notes that existing efforts are scattered across agencies, zoos, and hatcheries.
3. **Species are ranked by priority.** *Moapa coriacea*, *Gila robusta jordani*, and *Empetrichthys latos* head the list of desirable candidates. Several are endemic to restricted Nevada habitats.
4. **Funding and partners exist.** The Phoenix Zoo project had outside support from the New York Zoological Society. Federal, state, and university biologists were identified as potential collaborators.
5. **Limits of the document.** The letter gives no population counts, habitat measurements, or outcome data. Its claims rest on the author's own knowledge in 1975.
