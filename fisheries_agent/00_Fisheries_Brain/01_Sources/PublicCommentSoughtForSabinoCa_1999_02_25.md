## Executive Summary
This 1999 USDA Forest Service news release announces a public comment period for a proposed fish management project in Sabino Canyon, Coronado National Forest, Arizona. The project's goal is to support recovery of the native [[Gila Chub]], a cyprinid fish endemic to the American Southwest, by eradicating the nonnative and predatory [[Green Sunfish]] from Sabino Creek and Rose Canyon Lake. The plan involves piscicide treatment (antimycin and rotenone) at strategic times of year to eliminate the invasive predator while minimizing risk to humans, mammals, and birds. The document is primarily a regulatory/management communication rather than a peer-reviewed scientific study, but it contains ecologically relevant data on species interactions, habitat barriers, and chemical control methods.

## Background & Description
- No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, size) for [[Gila Chub]] or [[Green Sunfish]] is provided in this release.
- The document instead focuses on functional/ecological classification: gill-breathing aquatic organisms (fish) are specified as the target of the piscicide treatments, distinguishing them physiologically from non-target mammals and birds, which are not affected by antimycin or rotenone at treatment concentrations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native species**: [[Gila Chub]] — an Arizona-native freshwater fish species, historically present in Sabino Canyon's aquatic system.
- **Invasive species**: [[Green Sunfish]] — a nonnative predator, historically introduced into [[Sabino Creek]] and [[Rose Canyon Lake]], now the primary ecological threat to the local Gila chub population.
- **Predator-prey dynamic**: Green sunfish are described as the "main predator" of Gila chub, directly threatening the survival of the entire canyon population.
- **Habitat structure and refugia**: A natural rock barrier known as **Bridge Nine** in Sabino Creek currently blocks upstream migration of Green sunfish, inadvertently protecting what is believed to be the **source population** for all Gila chub in [[Sabino Canyon]]. This barrier is ecologically critical — its failure or bypass could result in total loss of the Gila chub population in this system.
- **Connectivity**: [[Rose Canyon Lake]] is a tributary-connected waterbody to Sabino Canyon, also colonized by Green sunfish, requiring separate but coordinated treatment.
- **Water chemistry / seasonal ecology**: Treatment timing is tied to seasonal hydrology — low water levels in late June/early July or late September/early October are optimal for Sabino Creek treatment, maximizing piscicide effectiveness and minimizing downstream dilution/spread.

## Key Findings
- **Management objective**: Eliminate Green sunfish from Sabino Creek and Rose Canyon Lake to protect and enable recovery of the native Gila chub population.
- **Toxicants used**:
  - **Rotenone**: A naturally occurring botanical piscicide derived from tropical/subtropical plants in the pea family (Fabaceae); historically used for centuries by Indigenous peoples in the East Indies, Africa, India, and Central/South America for fish harvesting.
  - **Antimycin**: Derived from a naturally occurring fungus; decomposes rapidly into nonlethal byproducts in flowing water; approved for use by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).
  - Both toxicants selectively target **gill-breathing aquatic animals** and are not harmful to humans, mammals, or birds at treatment concentrations.
- **Treatment logistics**:
  - Sabino Creek: four-day treatment window, either late June–early July or late September–early October (dependent on low water levels).
  - Rose Canyon Lake: treatment scheduled after October 31, coinciding with the closure of motorized public access via a gate.
  - Public notice signage required prior to treatment.
- **Collaborative agencies**: Project jointly proposed by the [[Coronado National Forest]] (USDA Forest Service, Santa Catalina Ranger District) and the [[Arizona Department of Game and Fish]].
- **Public process**: Public comments were solicited through March 30, 1999, with information available at the Santa Catalina Ranger District office in Tucson, AZ.
- **Conservation risk framing**: Biologists explicitly warn that if Green sunfish breach the Bridge Nine barrier and migrate upstream, it could result in the **extirpation of the entire Gila chub population** in Sabino Canyon — underscoring the urgency of the proposed eradication project.
