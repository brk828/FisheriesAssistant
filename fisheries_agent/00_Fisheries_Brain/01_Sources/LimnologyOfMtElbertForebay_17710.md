# Limnology of Mt. Elbert Forebay, 1978-79

#limnology #plankton #benthos

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation report presents the preoperational limnological baseline for the [[Mt. Elbert Forebay]], a pumped-storage reservoir north of [[Twin Lakes]], Colorado. Sampling ran from November 1978 through September 1979. The forebay was classed as a cold monomictic impoundment. It was richer in dissolved solids, nitrogen, phosphorus and trace metals than Twin Lakes, but held far fewer phytoplankton, zooplankton and benthos. Elevated [[Heavy Metals]] (notably iron and zinc) are offered as a possible cause. The data are intended to be compared with post-operation data to evaluate the effects of the [[Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant]] on the Twin Lakes aquatic environment.

## Background & Description

**Purpose and setting.** The forebay stores water pumped to a higher elevation during low demand and releases it for generation at peak demand. It lies about 137 m above Twin Lakes on a lateral moraine at the eastern foot of the Sawatch Range. Before construction the site had sagebrush, sandy soil, aspen stands and served as an elk wintering ground.

**Construction and liner.** Construction ran from 1975 to 1976, with a 1.5 m compacted sandy-silt bottom liner. The forebay was first filled in 1977-78 to a maximum depth of 8 m (19 percent of total capacity) with water pumped from Twin Lakes. Throughout the study it had no inlet or outlet, so precipitation was its only water input. The report gives conflicting dates for the [[Chlorinated Polyethylene Liner]]: the Summary says the forebay was drained in fall 1979 for installation, while the Introduction says the liner was added in fall 1980.

**Morphometry.**
- At maximum water surface elevation (2940 m a.s.l.): 115 ha surface area, 14 234 000 m³ capacity, average depth 13 m.
- During sampling (2926 m a.s.l.): 47 ha, 2 716 000 m³, average depth just under 6 m.

**Planned operation.** Water from [[Turquoise Reservoir]] will enter through the 2275 mm diameter, 17.2 km [[Mt. Elbert Conduit]]. Water will be mixed in the forebay during initial pumping cycles, then released to Twin Lakes during generation. The overall study is part of the [[Fryingpan-Arkansas Project]].

**Methods (summary).**
- Monthly and semimonthly surveys from November 1978 to September 1979 (Table 1).
- Physical parameters were measured with an electronic multiparameter probe. Water was sampled from the surface and at 1 and 3 m with a Van Dorn sampler.
- Trace metals (copper, zinc, iron, manganese, lead) and nutrients (orthophosphate, total phosphorus, TKN, nitrate, nitrite, ammonia) were analyzed following [[Water Data Acquisition Methods]] standards.
- Chlorophyll a was filtered and extracted following Parsons and Strickland (1963).
- Plankton were collected with a No. 20 silk net (76 µm mesh) and preserved in 2% formalin.
- Benthos were collected in triplicate per station with a [[Ponar Dredge]], sieved at 600 µm, preserved in 10% formalin, then counted and weighed (wet and dry biomass).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Thermal and ice regime.** The forebay was ice-covered from November 1978 through April 1979. Ice formed about a month earlier than on Twin Lakes, which the authors attribute to the forebay's shallower depth and smaller volume. Twin Lakes was ice-covered from 20 December 1978 to 15 May 1979. Surface temperatures ranged from 0 °C under ice to 16.5 °C on 15 August 1979. The April profile showed [[Inverse Stratification]] under ice (1.4 °C at surface, 3.8 °C at bottom). The August profile was isothermal. This pattern supports classification as cold monomictic. Twin Lakes showed strong stratification in August, with a bottom temperature of 8.4 °C.

**Water chemistry.** The forebay is dilute, with conductivity below 120 µS/cm. Its mean conductivity was 101 µS/cm (range 85-118), against 66 µS/cm (range 48-81) for Twin Lakes. The higher value is attributed to dissolution of substrate solids during filling, plus evaporation and freezing concentration in a stagnant basin. Major ions were dominated by calcium, bicarbonate and sulfate. Forebay ion concentrations averaged about 1.5 times those of Twin Lakes. Using the [[Hart Freshness Classification]] (Hart et al., 1945), the forebay ranks among the best 5 percent of US waters for supporting good fish populations.

**Nutrients and trophic status.** Nitrogen and phosphorus were generally higher in the forebay. Total phosphorus was about the same in both waters. Both are low, and the forebay is described as [[Oligotrophic Conditions]] and probably phosphorus limited. Nitrate peaked just after ice-off and fell sharply in the fall.

**Biological context.** The two waters differed more biologically than chemically or physically. Lower biomass in the forebay is linked to food limitation, possible turbidity limiting light, and [[Benthic Colonization]] constrained by a hardpacked clay substrate. Predation on cladocerans by [[Mysis Relicta]] is a major factor in Twin Lakes.

## Key Findings

**1. Physical limnology**
- Maximum ice thickness: 740 mm (forebay, 5 April 1979); 790 mm (Twin Lakes, 4 April 1979).
- Maximum snow depth: 460 mm (forebay, 13 March 1979); 250 mm (Twin Lakes, 21 February 1979).
- Forebay snow readings averaged about 50 percent deeper than Twin Lakes, attributed to drifting snow near the penstock raft rather than meteorology.

**2. Chemical limnology (22 analyses)**
- Mean total dissolved solids: 68 mg/L in the forebay, 48 mg/L in Twin Lakes.
- Mean calcium 13.9 mg/L; bicarbonate 37.0 mg/L; sulfate 15.9 mg/L.
- Trace metals, forebay vs Twin Lakes (averages):

| Metal | Forebay | Twin Lakes |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 0.0085 mg/L | 0.0028 mg/L |
| Iron | 0.324 mg/L | 0.078 mg/L |
| Lead | 0.0071 mg/L | 0.0028 mg/L |
| Zinc | 0.025 mg/L | 0.0029 mg/L |
| Manganese | 0.005 mg/L | 0.0097 mg/L |

- Forebay metals exceeded Twin Lakes by about 3x (copper), 4x (iron), 2.5x (lead) and 8.6x (zinc). Manganese was the only exception.
- Nutrients (forebay vs Twin Lakes): total phosphorus 0.004 vs 0.003 mg/L; nitrate 0.052 vs 0.017 mg/L; TKN 0.172 vs 0.09 mg/L; ammonia 0.019 vs 0.013 mg/L.
- Metals were unfiltered, so dissolved versus particulate fractions are unknown. The authors judge them biologically unimportant given high dissolved oxygen and pH above 7.0.

**3. Phytoplankton**
- Total abundance was more than 100 times greater in Twin Lakes (7124 vs 68.6 No./L average).
- Forebay peaks occurred in late November 1978, then February, May and July. Community shifted from [[Asterionella]] to [[Synedra]] dominance, with a more diverse assemblage in July and early August.
- Forebay averages: Asterionella 42.2 No./L (range 0-238); Synedra 9.8 No./L; Dictyospherium 8.5 No./L.

**4. Zooplankton**
- Total average: 34.8 No./L in the forebay vs 91.0 No./L in Twin Lakes.
- Forebay averages: [[Diaptomus]] 13.5 No./L; copepod nauplii 10.3 No./L; [[Daphnia]] 4.6 No./L; Cyclops 3.3 No./L.
- Twin Lakes had almost no cladocerans, presumed due to predation by Mysis relicta.

**5. Chlorophyll a**
- Twin Lakes averaged 4.45 and the forebay 0.93 (units as reported in the text; Table 3 uses µg/L). Highest values were recorded in mid-August 1979, generally at the deeper sampling depths.

**6. Benthos**
- April 1979, forebay: 43 chironomids/m² (0.0069 g/m²) and 43 oligochaetes/m² (0.0099 g/m²). Twin Lakes, April: 1867 chironomids/m² (1.1663 g/m²) and 603 oligochaetes/m² (0.2250 g/m²).
- August 1979, forebay: 153 chironomids/m² (0.01366 g/m²). Twin Lakes, August: 2256 chironomids/m² (2.8094 g/m²).
- Colonization was sparse. The authors suggest the hardpacked clay substrate and the absence of pumping operations were the main causes. [[Brinkhurst]] (1974) is cited on substrate as a key component of benthic production.

**7. Anticipated effects of operation (speculative)**
- Stratification may become possible in summer as forebay depth increases. Pumping direction could induce stratification (colder water sinks, warmer water floats).
- Ice formation will be delayed, and water-level changes will produce ice breaks and pressure ridges.
- Chemistry in the two waters should gradually approach equilibrium, though the forebay may stay high in metals and in chemicals from the liner and Turquoise Lake.
- Cladocerans, especially Daphnia from Turquoise Lake, could establish in the forebay. If Mysis colonizes the forebay, predation could occur there. Conversely, the forebay could act as a "nursery" supplying cladocerans to Twin Lakes.

**8. Future studies**
- Biweekly surveys will continue after operation begins. Five continuous monitoring stations (four on Twin Lakes, one on the forebay) will fill data gaps.
- Nets and floats in the forebay and Twin Lakes will measure fish mortality and transport.
- Related Twin Lakes reports cover [[Lake Trout]], [[Catostomids]], hydroacoustic fish surveys and creel census data. A companion [[Heavy Metals Pollution]] study covers the Upper Arkansas River.
