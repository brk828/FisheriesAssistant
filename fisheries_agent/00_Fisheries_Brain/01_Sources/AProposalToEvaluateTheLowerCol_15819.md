# A Proposal to Evaluate the Lower Colorado River Ecosystem

## Executive Summary
This document is a **conservation/research proposal** (not a primary field study) submitted by American Rivers (AR) and Conservation International (CI) to evaluate the historical and current ecological status of the [[Lower Colorado River]] ecosystem, spanning from Hoover Dam to the [[Gulf Of California]]. The proposal highlights the severe anthropogenic alteration of the river system via dams, diversions, and levees, which has eliminated the natural sediment and nutrient flow to the [[Colorado River Delta]] and upper Gulf of California. The region was designated a Mexican "Biosphere Reserve" (Reserva de la Biosfera Alto Golfo de California y Delta del Río Colorado) on 10 June 1993. The proposed bi-national, multi-disciplinary study aims to define pre-1935 baseline ecological conditions, evaluate causes of ecosystem decline, and assess restoration/management alternatives for both fish and non-fish endangered taxa, with a total requested budget of $478,318 over 27 months (1 September 1994 – 1 January 1997).

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide morphological, meristic, or anatomical descriptions of any species. It is a policy and funding proposal rather than a taxonomic or biological field study. Species are referenced only by common name in the context of endangerment and habitat loss:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — described as now **extirpated** from the lower Colorado River mainstem.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*) — described as "rare"; a big-river cyprinid.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — a "big-river" catostomid species with designated critical habitat in the lower mainstem.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — small-bodied cyprinodontid; subject of an existing (unintegrated) recovery plan.
- **[[Totoaba]]** (*Totoaba macdonaldi*) — large sciaenid endemic to the upper Gulf of California, listed as endangered.

No morphometric data, size classes, coloration, or diagnostic features are included in the source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **River-delta-marine continuum**: The lower Colorado River ecosystem functions as a continuous hydrological and ecological gradient from the mainstem river, through the delta, into the estuarine/marine environment of the upper [[Gulf Of California]]. Historically, spring runoff delivered "millions of tons of silt and nutrients" that sustained delta and near-shore Gulf productivity.
- **Fish habitat degradation**: Damming (beginning with Hoover Dam, 1935), diversion for irrigation (Palo Verde Valley, Colorado River Indian Tribal lands, Yuma Valley, Imperial Valley, Mexicali Valley) and municipal water supply (Los Angeles, San Diego, Phoenix, Tucson) have eliminated natural flow regimes required by native "big-river" fishes such as bonytail and razorback sucker.
- **Backwater/marshland dependency**: At least three major wildlife refuges along the lower river depend on adequate flows to maintain backwater and marshland habitats critical for both fish and avian species (e.g., [[Yuma Clapper Rail]]).
- **Critical habitat designation**: Portions of the lower mainstem are legally designated critical habitat for bonytail and razorback sucker, though no recovery/management plans have yet been implemented there.
- **Estuarine/marine endemics**: The upper Gulf of California supports endemic and endangered taxa tied to delta freshwater input, including the [[Vaquita]] dolphin, [[Totoaba]], green sea turtle, leatherback turtle, and [[Elegant Tern]] — all indirectly dependent on the historic freshwater/nutrient regime of the Colorado River.
- **Fisheries linkage**: Declining shrimp and shellfish fisheries in the Gulf are explicitly linked to loss of freshwater and sediment/nutrient delivery from the river, underscoring the trophic connectivity between the riverine and marine components of the ecosystem.

## Key Findings
- The Colorado River from Hoover Dam to the Gulf of California is one of the most heavily engineered river systems in the world, with dams, diversion canals, levees, and channelization dramatically altering natural flow and sediment transport.
- Cessation of natural spring flooding has eliminated silt/nutrient delivery to the delta and upper Gulf, degrading what was "perhaps the most biologically rich region in the southwest."
- Multiple fish species tied to the mainstem lower Colorado River are in severe decline or extirpated: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (extirpated), [[Bonytail]] (rare), [[Razorback Sucker]] (critical habitat designated but unmanaged), and [[Desert Pupfish]] (recovery plan exists but not integrated regionally).
- Non-fish endangered/endemic taxa dependent on the same freshwater-delta-Gulf system include the [[Vaquita]], [[Totoaba]], green sea turtle, leatherback turtle, [[Elegant Tern]], and [[Yuma Clapper Rail]].
- Existing single-species recovery plans (pupfish, clapper rail) have **not been integrated** into a unified, ecosystem-level management strategy.
- The three lower-basin U.S. states are developing a multi-species [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] covering Lake Powell to the Mexican border, but details remain unformulated at time of writing.
- The proposal calls for a **bi-national, multi-disciplinary scientific team** to: (1) reconstruct baseline pre-1935 riverine/deltaic/Gulf ecological conditions, (2) evaluate causes of ecosystem and species decline, and (3) generate restoration/conservation alternatives.
- Funding is sought from U.S. federal agencies (Bureau of Reclamation, Fish and Wildlife Service) and private sources, with NAFTA side-agreement obligations cited as additional justification.
- Total proposed budget: **$478,318** over 27 months, covering personnel, travel/advisory meetings, operations, and indirect costs (~40% of direct costs).
- The study explicitly frames fish conservation (bonytail, razorback sucker, desert pupfish, Colorado squawfish) as inseparable from broader ecosystem management encompassing the delta and northern Gulf of California — a direct precursor to modern binational [[Colorado River Delta]] restoration efforts.
