## Executive Summary

This 1976 report documents a large-scale fish mortality event in the [[St Lucia Lake System]], Natal, South Africa, in which an estimated **100,000+ fish** died between May and July 1976. The [[University of Natal Mullet Research Programme]] collected data during the event. The mortality affected numerous estuarine and marine-derived species (Table 1) but notably spared the euryhaline freshwater cichlid [[Sarotherodon Mossambicus]]. The authors, [[S.J.M. Blaber]] and [[A.K. Whitfield]], attribute the die-off to a lethal combination of abnormally low salinity and low water temperature, causing [[Osmoregulatory Failure]], compounded by secondary [[Fungal Infection]] of skin lesions. The event coincided with unusually high catchment rainfall that drastically reduced lake salinities.

## Background & Description

Dying and dead fish exhibited consistent pathological signs prior to death:

- **Skin lesions and haemorrhaging** over large areas of the body
- Haemorrhaging most concentrated around:
  - Caudal peduncle
  - Fin bases
  - Opercula
  - Mouth
- Affected lesions frequently covered by a **brownish, fur-like growth**, microscopically identified as a **fungus** (secondary fungal infection)
- Behavioral/physiological symptom: **disorientation**, with dying fish typically **swimming upside down** — consistent with osmoregulatory stress and neurological impairment

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat context:** The [[St Lucia Lake System]] is an estuarine lake complex (comprising the estuary, North Lake, South Lake, and False Bay) that supports both marine-derived euryhaline species and freshwater species, functioning as a critical nursery and foraging habitat for juvenile marine fish.

**Salinity regime shift (Table 2):**

| Station | Jan 1976 (‰) | Jul 1976 (‰) |
|---|---|---|
| N.P.B. Jetty (estuary) | 9.9 | 3.5 |
| Charters Creek (South Lake) | 10.3 | 2.3 |
| Bird Island (North Lake) | 5.9 | 1.0 |
| Lister Point (False Bay) | 6.8 | 1.2 |

- Salinities dropped sharply system-wide due to unusually high rainfall in the catchment area.
- Recorded water temperatures in June 1976 (False Bay and South Lake, surface and bottom) were **12°C**.
- Prior ecological records (Millard & Broekhuysen 1970; Wallace 1974) indicate none of the affected species (Table 1) had previously been recorded in salinities below **7‰**, despite such low-salinity zones existing in the lake.
- Species affected were of **marine origin**; the freshwater/euryhaline cichlid *Sarotherodon mossambicus* was unaffected, highlighting a differential physiological tolerance to combined low-salinity/low-temperature stress between marine-derived and freshwater-derived taxa.
- A comparable prior event: mass mortality of *[[Pomadasys Commersonni]]* in Lake Nhlange, Kosi (salinity 3‰) in July 1975, attributed to the same low-salinity/low-temperature mechanism.

## Key Findings

- **Affected species** (Table 1) included:
  - *[[Argyrosomus Hololepidotus]]* (Kob) — one of first species to die
  - *[[Johnius Belengeri]]* (Mini-kob) — one of first species to die
  - *[[Caranx Ignobilis]]* (Giant kingfish)
  - *[[Hilsa Kelee]]* (Kelee shad)
  - *[[Leiognathus Equulus]]* (Slimy)
  - *[[Liza Macrolepis]]* (Largescale mullet)
  - *[[Mugil Cephalus]]* (Flathead mullet)
  - *[[Pomadasys Commersonni]]* (Spotted grunter)
  - *[[Rhabdosargus Holubi]]* (Cape stumpnose)
  - *[[Rhabdosargus Sarba]]* (Natal stumpnose)
  - *[[Terapon Jarbua]]* (Thornfish)

- **Causal hypothesis:** Combined low salinity + low temperature caused lethal [[Osmoregulatory Failure]]; surviving stressed fish were then vulnerable to secondary fungal infection of skin lesions.

- **Experimental support:** Prior laboratory work by Blaber (1973, 1974) on *[[Rhabdosargus Holubi]]* demonstrated that the specific salinity–temperature combination observed at St Lucia in June–July 1976 is lethal to this species, and that severe osmoregulatory stress predisposes fish to skin lesions (supporting the fungal secondary-infection hypothesis).

- **Behavioral mechanism of mass die-off:** Fish were likely unable to escape unfavorable conditions because low salinity/temperature conditions were relatively uniform across the *entire* lake system, and reduced activity at low temperatures further limited fish mobility and avoidance behavior.

- **Selective mortality:** Only species of marine origin died; the euryhaline freshwater cichlid *Sarotherodon mossambicus*, abundant in St Lucia, was unaffected — suggesting a physiological tolerance threshold difference correlated with evolutionary/ecological origin (marine vs. freshwater lineage).

- **Precedent:** A similar mortality event in *[[Pomadasys Commersonni]]* at Lake Nhlange, Kosi (1975), supports low salinity + low temperature as a recurring regional mortality mechanism in South African estuarine systems.
