# Public Forum on Reasonable and Prudent Alternative Provision 13(b) — Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #endangered-species-management #freshwater-ecology #colorado-river-basin

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1998 administrative letter from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation announcing a public forum on **Provision 13(b)** of a **Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA)**, issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] under **Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]** on April 30, 1997. The RPA governs Reclamation's operation and maintenance of the [[Lower Colorado River]] (LCR) and is aimed at increasing water availability and management discretion for **fish and wildlife conservation purposes**. The letter does not contain primary morphological, taxonomic, or ecological field data on fish species; rather, it documents a regulatory and stakeholder engagement process tied to species conservation planning. Findings and outcomes were slated to feed into the **[[Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP)**, a long-term habitat and species conservation planning effort for the LCR.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although no specific species accounts are given, the document situates its subject matter within a broader ecological and hydrological management context:

- **Habitat in question**: The [[Lower Colorado River]] (LCR), a highly regulated river system subject to reservoir operations, water diversions, and multi-jurisdictional water allocation under the **Colorado River Annual Operating Plan (AOP)**.
- **Conservation driver**: Listed and unlisted species inhabiting the LCR and its associated riparian/aquatic habitats are the implicit ecological focus, as the RPA and MSCP are designed to protect species while balancing continued water and power use.
- **Water as a limiting resource**: The core ecological issue addressed is water allocation flexibility — specifically, identifying "discretionary action flexibility" that Reclamation could direct toward **fish and wildlife purposes**, implying that water quantity/timing is a key limiting factor for aquatic and riparian species in this reach of the [[Colorado River]].
- **Multi-species framing**: The [[Multi-Species Conservation Program]] indicates a management approach targeting an assemblage of species and habitats along the LCR corridor, rather than a single-species focus, consistent with ecosystem-level conservation planning under the Endangered Species Act.

## Key Findings
- The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] issued a **Biological Opinion** (April 30, 1997) establishing an **RPA** for Bureau of Reclamation operations on the LCR under [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 consultation.
- **RPA Provision 13(a)** required Reclamation to report on existing discretionary action flexibility for fish and wildlife water needs; this was completed October 30, 1997.
- **RPA Provision 13(b)** required Reclamation to collaborate with stakeholders to identify *additional* opportunities to increase discretionary water management flexibility for fish and wildlife purposes on the LCR.
- Reclamation conducted a series of **stakeholder meetings** to identify partnership opportunities that could yield additional water for conservation purposes.
- A public forum was scheduled for **August 4, 1998**, at McCarran International Airport (Las Vegas, NV), immediately following the 1999 Colorado River Annual Operating Plan meeting, to present findings and solicit further input.
- A summary document addressing Provision 13(b) was due to the Service by **October 31, 1998**.
- Results from this effort were intended to directly inform the **[[Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (MSCP)** Steering Committee, supporting long-term conservation planning for listed and unlisted species in the LCR while preserving existing water and power infrastructure uses.
- The process reflects an **adaptive, multi-stakeholder regulatory framework** rather than a controlled ecological study, underscoring the intersection of water resource management and species conservation in arid-region river systems like the [[Colorado River]].
