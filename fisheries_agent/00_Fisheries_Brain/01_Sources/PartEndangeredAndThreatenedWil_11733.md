# U.S. List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants (Special Reprint, 50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #Cyprinidae #Unionidae

## Executive Summary
This document is a special reprint of 50 CFR 17.11 (wildlife) and 17.12 (plants), current as of October 31, 1996. The Division of Endangered Species, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, prepared it. It is the [[Endangered Species Act]] list of every species determined to be Endangered (E) or Threatened (T), plus species treated as listed because they look similar (S/A). Each entry gives the common and scientific name, historic range, the vertebrate population listed, status, the Federal Register citation ("When listed"), and cross-references to critical habitat and special rules. A closing section lists species removed from the lists. The wildlife part is organized by taxon group: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, clams, snails, insects, arachnids and crustaceans.

## Background & Description
- **Legal framework:** The listing rules define a "species" by the common name, scientific name and listed vertebrate population together. "Entire" means all populations across the range are listed. Differently classified geographic populations of one species appear as separate entries.
- **Status symbols:** E, T, E/T (S/A), and XE or XN for essential or nonessential experimental populations. XN entries include the black-footed ferret, gray wolf, red wolf and [[Colorado Squawfish]] populations.
- **Non-regulatory columns:** Historic range, footnotes, synonyms and spelling may be revised without public notice. Species and status can change only through the Part 424 procedures.
- **Footnotes:** Footnote numbers refer to Federal Register notices. The "When listed" column also uses D (delisted) and E (emergency rule). Entries are numbered 1 to 597, from 1967 to October 1996.
- **Taxon rule:** Listing a taxon covers all lower taxonomic units, as with the gibbon genus *Hylobates*.
- **Special reprint symbols:** "+" marks entries added since the October 31, 1995 republication, and "*" marks entries amended by a published rule.
- **Access:** The list was available by e-mail and on the FWS web site.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The aquatic and semi-aquatic entries fall into several groups.

- **Western North American desert and spring fishes:** These are mostly endemic cyprinids and cyprinodontids of isolated springs and river basins. Examples are the [[Devils Hole Pupfish]], [[Borax Lake Chub]], Ash Meadows species, [[Spikedace]], spinedaces, springfishes and Yaqui fishes (Yaqui catfish, Yaqui chub, Gila topminnow).
- **Colorado River basin big-river fishes:** These include the [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]].
- **Southeastern U.S. fishes:** The darters, madtoms, shiners and chubs of the Tennessee, Cumberland and Mobile Basin streams are listed. Examples are the amber, bayou, boulder, duskytail, Etowah, goldline and snail darters, plus the Cape Fear shiner and Cahaba shiner.
- **Cave and subterranean taxa:** The [[Alabama Cavefish]] and [[Ozark Cavefish]], with cave crayfish, shrimp and isopods.
- **Anadromous and estuarine fishes:** [[Chinook Salmon]] (Sacramento River winter run; Snake River spring/summer and fall runs), Snake River [[Sockeye Salmon]], the sturgeons, [[Tidewater Goby]] and delta smelt.
- **Freshwater invertebrates:** These include many [[Freshwater Mussels]] (pearlymussels, pigtoes, clubshells, combshells, riffleshells and others), springsnails and other snails, crayfishes, fairy shrimps, amphipods, isopods, and the Hine's emerald dragonfly.
- **Marine and oceanic taxa:** [[Sea Turtles]], [[West Indian Manatee]], baleen and sperm whales, Steller sea lion, Hawaiian monk seal, Caribbean monk seal and Mediterranean monk seal.

## Key Findings
**Fishes (examples with status and critical habitat as printed)**
- The [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*; AZ, CA, CO, NV, UT, WY) is E, with critical habitat cross-referenced at 17.95(e). The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) is E with critical habitat. The [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) is listed for its entire range except the Salt and Verde River drainages in Arizona, where a separate XN experimental entry applies.
- Several Chub entries are threatened, including the Chihuahua, Hutton tui, slender, Sonora and spotfin chubs. The Mohave tui, Oregon, Owens tui and Virgin River chubs are E.
- The Alabama cavefish (*Speoplatyrhinus poulsoni*) is E, and the Ozark cavefish (*Amblyopsis rosae*) is T. The Yaqui catfish (*Ictalurus pricei*) is T, with critical habitat and a special rule.
- Sturgeons listed are the pallid sturgeon (*Scaphirhynchus albus*), shortnose sturgeon (*Acipenser brevirostrum*), Gulf sturgeon (*A. oxyrhynchus desotoi*) and the Kootenai River population of white sturgeon.
- [[Chinook Salmon]] entries are separated by run and basin. These are the Sacramento River winter run, and the Snake River spring/summer and fall runs, which are natural populations. The Snake River sockeye is a separate entry limited to the Idaho stock. Critical habitat is cross-referenced to Part 226.
- Other listed fishes include the totoaba (*Cynoscion macdonaldi*) of the Gulf of California, Apache and greenback cutthroat trout, the Gila trout and Lahontan cutthroat trout. Foreign fishes include the Ala Balik (Turkey), Ayumodoki (Japan), Asian bonytongue and the giant Mekong catfish.

**Other aquatic taxa**
- [[Sea Turtles]]: green, hawksbill, Kemp's ridley and leatherback are E, with 222.41 cited as a special rule. Loggerhead is T. Green and olive ridley turtles have split populations (breeding colonies E, elsewhere T). Hawksbill and leatherback also carry critical habitat references.
- Marine mammals: the whales (blue, bowhead, finback, humpback, right, sei and sperm) are E. The gray whale entry is limited to the western North Pacific. Manatees, the Hawaiian and Mediterranean monk seals, and the Chinese and Indus River dolphins are also listed.
- Reptile and amphibian entries include the American crocodile and Chinese alligator. Amphibians include the San Marcos salamander and Texas blind salamander.
- Freshwater invertebrates are numerous, dominated by mussels from the Southeast and Midwest. Crustacean listings include the California freshwater shrimp (*Syncaris pacifica*) and the Shasta crayfish (*Pacifastacus fortis*).

**Delisting**
- Delisting requires one of three grounds: extinction, recovery, or original data in error. Error is subdivided into better data, taxonomic revision, amendment of the Act, and newly discovered populations.
- Under 50 CFR 424.11(c), listing factors are habitat destruction or modification, overutilization, disease or predation, inadequate regulation, and other natural or manmade factors.
- Aquatic and related delisted entries include the Amistad gambusia (*Gambusia amistadensis*, extinct), the American alligator (recovered), the eastern North Pacific gray whale population (recovered), the Atlantic and Gulf populations of the brown pelican (recovered), and the dusky seaside sparrow (extinct).
