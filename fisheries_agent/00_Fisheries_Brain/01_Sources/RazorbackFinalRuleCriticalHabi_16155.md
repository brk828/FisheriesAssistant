# Draft Final Rule to Designate the Razorback Sucker as an Endangered Species

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species-act #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum and supporting materials dated August 1991 regarding the draft final rule to list the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as an endangered species under the Endangered Species Act. The memorandum from Regional Director, Region 6, to the Director responds to comments on critical habitat designation and addresses a 60-day notice of intent to sue filed by the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund for failure to list the species within 1 year of the proposed rule. The document includes detailed responses to public comments regarding critical habitat designation and explains the Service's determination that critical habitat is "not determinable" at the time of listing.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]] inhabiting the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The species was proposed for listing as endangered, prompting public comment and regulatory review. The document represents internal Fish and Wildlife Service correspondence and regulatory materials prepared in response to the proposed listing rule.

The memorandum references a court decision (*Northern Spotted Owl v. Lujan*) that caused the Service to scrutinize critical habitat findings more closely. Under Section 3 of the Endangered Species Act, critical habitat is defined as specific areas within the geographical area occupied by the species at listing that contain physical or biological features essential to conservation and that may require special management considerations or protection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document notes limited information on specific habitat needs of the [[Razorback Sucker]]. While habitat occupied by the species has been identified and spawning has been documented in several areas, there is uncertainty whether these areas adequately meet life history needs, particularly given little or no recruitment to adult populations.

The Service identifies that the razorback sucker cannot perpetuate itself in the wild if there is little or no recruitment. The document references concerns about nonnative fish introductions as a threat factor and notes that the Service can limit introduction of nonnative species through agreements with states or by withholding Federal funds or fish from Federal hatcheries for stocking proposals with potential to adversely impact the species.

A [[Recovery Implementation Program]] is mentioned as providing protections alongside species listing. Conservation measures are currently provided in biological opinions for three species of endangered fish in the [[Colorado River Basin]] that do not have critical habitat designated.

## Key Findings

**Critical Habitat Determination**: The Service finds that critical habitat for the [[Razorback Sucker]] is "not presently determinable" at the time of listing. The Service will reexamine whether critical habitat designation is prudent during the period of attempting to determine critical habitat.

**Rationale for Not Determinable Finding**: The Service is unable to adequately determine the constituent elements within specific areas that are critical to survival and recovery. Though spawning areas have been documented, it is questionable whether these areas adequately meet life history needs given little or no recruitment. The Service states it would not be in the best interest of the species to identify or use characteristics of existing habitats as the basis for critical habitat when unable to identify specific areas and habitat characteristics needed to bring about recruitment.

**Public Comment Summary**: Fourteen commentors expressed concern about critical habitat designation. Ten supported designation; four opposed. Supporters cited reasons including: (a) five believed critical habitat was determinable and would provide habitat protection benefits; (b) two thought it would limit evaluation area for determining impacts; (c) two thought it would help protect against nonnative fish introductions; (d) one thought conservation measures could not be implemented without designation; (e) one questioned whether designation would preclude restoration efforts.

**Service Responses to Comments**: The Service clarified that critical habitat designation is not necessary to implement conservation measures, does not limit the area of evaluation for impacts (species are protected throughout their range once listed), and would be unlikely to preclude restoration efforts since such efforts aim to benefit the species.

**Legal Context**: Section 4(b)(6)(C) of the Act allows postponement of final critical habitat determination for 1 additional year from proposed rule publication if prompt determination of endangered or threatened status is essential to species conservation. The Service considers prompt determination of endangered status for the [[Razorback Sucker]] essential, as proposed species status provides only limited consideration under Section 7(a)(4) conference requirements.

**Regulatory Authority**: Under Section 4(b)(2), critical habitat designations must be based on best scientific data available and must consider economic and other relevant impacts. The Service's regulations (50 CFR 424.12(a)(2)) state critical habitat is not determinable if information sufficient to perform required impact analyses is lacking or if biological needs are not sufficiently well known to permit identification of an area as critical habitat.
