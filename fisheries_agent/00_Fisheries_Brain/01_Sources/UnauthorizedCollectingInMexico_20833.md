# USFWS Letter on Unauthorized Collecting at Cuatro Cienegas

#ichthyology #conservation #endangered-species #cuatro-cienegas

## Executive Summary

Official correspondence from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director to the Desert Fishes Council Executive Secretary dated January 14, 1987, regarding unauthorized collection of endangered fish and reptiles at [[Cuatro Cienegas]], [[Coahuila]], [[Mexico]]. The letter addresses violations of both Mexican Federal law and U.S. laws (Endangered Species Act and Lacey Act), and requests assistance in informing researchers about international permit requirements.

## Background & Description

A recent meeting between [[USFWS]] and the Mexican counterpart agency ([[SEDUE]]) revealed Mexican concerns about unauthorized collecting of flora and fauna. A specific case involved a research associate from an Arizona university who was found collecting endangered fish and reptiles at [[Cuatro Cienegas]] in the State of [[Coahuila]]. 

Mexican Federal law absolutely forbids collecting any species of flora or fauna in the Republic of Mexico without first acquiring valid Mexican permits. Additionally, no biological material may be imported into the U.S. without Mexican permits and, usually, U.S. permits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Cuatro Cienegas]] is recognized for its unique biodiversity. Agencies of the Mexican Government were working to have [[Cuatro Cienegas]] declared a national sanctuary. Concerns existed that further damage and loss would occur to this site's unique diversity before adequate permanent on-site protection could be provided.

## Key Findings

- The unauthorized collecting case constituted serious violations of both U.S. laws (the [[Endangered Species Act]] and the [[Lacey Act]]) and Mexican Federal law
- [[USFWS]] committed to fully support Mexico in the enforcement of all permit requirements
- Organizations, institutions, and agencies were requested to assist in informing and cautioning persons and institutions about international permit requirements
- Mexican Government agencies were actively working toward declaring [[Cuatro Cienegas]] a national sanctuary to provide permanent protection for the site's unique diversity
