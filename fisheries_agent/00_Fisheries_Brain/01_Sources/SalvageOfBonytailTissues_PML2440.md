# Salvage of Mohave Bonytail Tissues

#ichthyology #marine-biology #mohave-bonytail

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum, dated 14 September 1993, from the [[Gila Taxonomy Project]] at the [[National Museum Of Natural History]] sets out a plan to salvage frozen tissue from Mohave bonytail specimens. The tissue is held at Dexter and at [[Arizona State University]]. Gary Carmichael and Jim Hutchison requested the salvage, and the memo asks Dexter personnel to begin removing tissue from frozen specimens promptly. The removed tissue is to be stored in ultracold conditions and shipped to collaborators. The main concern is preserving DNA and enzymes for later [[Genetic Analysis]] and [[Enzyme Studies]]. The document is an administrative plan and does not report research results.

## Background & Description

The memo concerns the Mohave bonytail, a native fish of the Colorado River system. The document does not give a scientific name, body measurements, or other morphological details.

The task has three parts:

- **Tissue removal.** Frozen tissue is removed from specimens held at Dexter. Specimens held at ASU are to be handled separately at a later date, in coordination with morphological work.
- **Storage.** Removed tissue is placed in nine-tube vials and returned to ultracold freezers at Dexter until shipping.
- **Shipping and extraction.** Tissue is shipped to collaborators, including a person at the Gila Taxonomy Project who is finishing work on other samples. Later extraction steps are led by a separate team.

The memo notes that the specimens are rock-hard when frozen, which makes tissue removal difficult. Preserving DNA and enzymes requires that specimens stay as cold as possible during the process. The author suggests a serrated knife, flame-sterilized between specimens, and slow cutting through thin slices as possible methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not address habitat, population status, water chemistry, or other ecological conditions. Its context is laboratory handling and curation of museum specimens.

## Key Findings

- **Salvage approved and requested.** Gary Carmichael and Jim Hutchison asked that tissue salvage proceed under a joint protocol with the Gila Taxonomy Project.
- **Staged workflow.** Tissue removal at Dexter is to be done as an early, separate step at the earliest convenience of Dexter personnel. Once removed and stored in ultracold conditions, tissue can be shipped at a later date, which makes the workflow convenient for all parties.
- **Urgency.** The author asks that Dexter begin as soon as possible so that some tissue can be shipped to the collaborator who is finishing other work.
- **Sample tracking.** Vials and the tissues retained at Dexter must be carefully cross-referenced to their source carcasses using labels and records.
- **Coordination.** Shipping of fresh tissue from the other holding sites (Carmichael and Hutchison, and ASU) is to be coordinated separately. The author plans to follow the later stages of the process, including the extractions.
- **Enzyme and DNA priority.** Speed and cold handling are priorities because DNA and enzyme quality depend on minimal thawing.
- **Permit context.** The memo refers to a permit and other procedural requirements from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), but the text is too garbled to report the details.
