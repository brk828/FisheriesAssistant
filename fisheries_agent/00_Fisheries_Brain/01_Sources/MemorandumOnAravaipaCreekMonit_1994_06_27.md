## Executive Summary

This memorandum, authored by W. L. Minckley, Tony Velasco, and Ross Timmons (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology), summarizes fish and habitat monitoring conducted on 25-26 June 1994 along [[Aravaipa Creek]], a perennial desert stream in Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona. Six sites were sampled by seine — three upstream ("east end") and three downstream ("west end") of the [[Aravaipa Canyon Wilderness Area]] — to characterize fish assemblages, physical habitat structure, and associated wildlife. The data document persistence of a native-dominated fish community, including breeding activity in the imperiled [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), alongside notable geomorphic changes (channel aggradation) and encroachment of nonnative aquatic vegetation (*Nasturtium*, watercress) at multiple stations.

## Background & Description

Physical/morphological notes are habitat- and condition-based rather than anatomical measurements of individual fish, but several breeding/secondary sexual characteristics were recorded:

- **[[Meda Fulgida]] (Spikedace):** Numerous individuals observed with brassy coloration, indicative of breeding condition.
- **[[Pantosteus Clarki]] (Desert Sucker):** At least one adult retained nuptial tuberculation, suggesting a late/extended breeding season.
- **[[Tiaroga Cobitis]] (Loach Minnow):** Frequently collected in breeding condition; several seine hauls captured apparent breeding pairs.
- **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]] (Longfin Dace):** Tuberculate adults (a secondary sexual breeding characteristic) collected occasionally at both east and west end sites.

**Thermal conditions:** East end daytime air temperatures averaged 35°C (range 20–40°C); west end averaged 42°C (range 32–48°C), reflecting a strong upstream-to-downstream thermal gradient across the [[Aravaipa Canyon]] corridor.

**Vegetation dynamics:** Dense stands of *Nasturtium* (watercress, nonnative) dominated east end sites, constricting channel width to <1 m at the Chimney Rock and east end guest house stations — a potential concern for [[Habitat Fragmentation]] of native fish habitat.

**Geomorphic change:** The White's Ranch site showed marked aggradation since the prior year's sampling, with a ~2 m deep plug of sand and gravel burying previously embedded boulder substrate — evidence of active channel instability/sediment transport in the lower reach.

**Associated wildlife observed concurrently with fish sampling** (indicating broader riparian ecosystem integrity):
- Zone-tailed hawk (*Buteo albonotatus*) — 2 individuals, east end guest house site.
- Great blue heron (*Ardea herodias*) — congregation of 9–10 individuals foraging above Geldmacher's crossing (west end), notably arrayed side-by-side lengthwise with the creek.
- Sonoran mud turtle (*Kinosternon sonoriense*) — downstream of Normandy Ranch.
- Yellow-billed cuckoo (*Coccyzus americanus*) — heard at Normandy Ranch.
- Purple martin (*Progne subis*) — west end.
- Sonoran softshell turtle (*Trionyx spiniferus*, syn. *Apalone spinifera*) — irrigation pool at White family property.
- Fireflies (Lampyridae) — observed at Turkey Creek (east end) and Aravaipa Ranch (west end), evenings.

## Key Findings

- **Native fish community persistence:** [[Aravaipa Creek]] continued to support a diverse native fish assemblage including [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*).
- **Active breeding documented in imperiled species:** Breeding condition and apparent breeding pairs of *Tiaroga cobitis* (federally listed [[Loach Minnow]]) and brassy-colored *Meda fulgida* ([[Spikedace]], federally listed) were recorded — significant for conservation monitoring given both species' imperiled status in the Gila River Basin.
- **Nonnative species present:** Codes recorded for nonnative taxa include *Cyprinus carpio* (common carp), *Cyprinella lutrensis* (red shiner), *Pimephales promelas* (fathead minnow), *Ameiurus melas* and *A. natalis* (bullheads), *Gambusia affinis* (western mosquitofish), *Lepomis cyanellus* (green sunfish), and *Micropterus salmoides* (largemouth bass) — indicating ongoing risk of competition/predation pressure on native cyprinids and catostomids, particularly relevant to [[Invasive Species Impacts on Native Fish]].
- **Habitat differentiation between reaches:** Canyon-bound stations exhibited higher overstory cover (up to 40%) and run-dominated flow, while west end stations were shallower, wider, and riffle-dominated with minimal vegetative cover — reflecting geomorphic and canopy differences along the creek's longitudinal gradient.
- **Sediment aggradation as a habitat stressor:** The substantial infilling of boulder habitat at White's Ranch by sand/gravel represents a potential threat to benthic-specialist natives such as *Tiaroga cobitis*, which depend on coarse substrate interstices.
- **Access constraints:** A new gate at the Brandenburg campsite now restricts vehicular access to west end sites to local residents only, a logistical note relevant to future monitoring continuity.
- **Standardized sampling methodology:** All sites sampled via 200 m seine hauls using 1/8-inch mesh, 6-foot seines, enabling comparability with historical Aravaipa Creek datasets referenced in the memo (e.g., "aggraded noticeably since last year's sampling").
