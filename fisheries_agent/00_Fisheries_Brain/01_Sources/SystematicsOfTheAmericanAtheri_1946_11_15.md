## Executive Summary

This 1946 systematics paper by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Luis R. Rivas]] resolves a long-standing taxonomic enigma surrounding *Atherina microps* Poey, 1860, a species originally described from Cuba but left unplaced at the genus level (noted as "undetermined genus" within Atherinopsinae by [[Jordan and Hubbs 1919]]). Through careful re-examination of Poey's original diagnosis and comparison with recent material, the authors demonstrate that *Atherina microps* is conspecific with what was then known as *Melanorhinus boekei* Metzelaar, 1919, and its junior synonym *Mugilops marinus* Meek and Hildebrand, 1923. Because Poey's name has priority, the valid combination becomes **Melanorhinus microps**. This resolves the genus *Mugilops* Meek and Hildebrand as a synonym of *Melanorhinus* Metzelaar, 1919 — a synonymy first published by Myers and Wade (1942) but here given full taxonomic and nomenclatural treatment. The paper also examines fin-ray meristics across type specimens, discusses discrepancies in dorsal spine and anal ray counts (attributed to counting error by Poey using low-power optics), and situates *Melanorhinus* ecologically and morphologically among other surface-dwelling atherinopsine genera, drawing comparisons to *Iso* (Indo-Pacific) and the querimana stage of *Mugil* (mullet).

## Background & Description

### Diagnostic Features (from Poey's original description, translated/interpreted)
- **Head**: Small, contained 5 times in total length
- **Body**: Deep — somewhat deeper than the head is long
- **Eye**: Small, 4 times in head length
- **Mouth**: Much smaller than in *Atherinomorus stipes* (cited as "Atherina laticeps"); highly protractile
- **First dorsal fin**: More anterior than in *A. laticeps*
- **Second dorsal and anal fins**: Longer than in *A. laticeps*
- **Ray formula (Poey)**: D. 5-I, 15; A. I, 19
- **Anus**: Positioned close to the anal fin
- **Coloration**: Silvery body; greenish back; scales partly dotted with brown; head deep blue; lateral silvery band not sharply defined

### Fin Structure
- First dorsal fin: cluster of short, flexible spines, well separated from second dorsal
- Second dorsal and anal fins: each with one short spine, one unbranched soft-ray, followed by branched rays
- Pectoral fin: pointed; uppermost ray very short, second ray long and unbranched, remaining rays branched

### Scale Morphology (diagnostic for genus)
- Lateral scales very deep — much higher than long
- Basal field: few, nearly straight, vertical circuli
- Postero-lateral angles evenly rounded; **no radii**
- **Exposed (apical) field**: crossed by many very fine, closely-set, nearly strictly vertical circuli — this trait sharply distinguishes *Melanorhinus* from *Atherina araea* (smooth exposed field) and is shared convergently with the querimana postlarval stage of *Mugil*

### Opercle and Jaw Characters
- Opercle in *Melanorhinus*: extremely high and short; articulating socket positioned much closer to upper edge than lower edge (diagnostic vs. *Atherina araea*, which has extroverted premaxillary dentition and elevated mandibular rami)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat inference**: Morphological adaptations — compressed body (especially belly), truncated head, very high pectoral fins — are interpreted as adaptations to **surface life in or near the surf zone**
- **Convergent ecomorphs**: Close morphological and presumed ecological counterparts include:
  - *Iso* (Indo-Pacific genus, Atherininae) — similar surf-dwelling habits
  - The **querimana stage** of *Mugil* (mullet postlarvae) — surface-schooling behavior and near-identical apical scale circuli structure, which may function to enhance silvery sheen for surface camouflage/schooling
  - *Notocheirus hubbsi* Clark, 1937 — the "most bizarre member" of the related atherinopsine group, sharing surface-adapted traits
  - Related genera *Eurystole* and *Nectarges* (per Myers and Wade, 1942) also show body compression and pectoral fin elevation
- **Geographic distribution**: Tropical Atlantic (Caribbean, Cuba, Panama, St. Martin/Dutch West Indies); sister species *Melanorhinus cyanellus* occupies the tropical Pacific

## Key Findings

1. **Priority resolution**: *Atherina microps* Poey, 1860 has nomenclatural priority over *Melanorhinus boekei* Metzelaar, 1919, and *Mugilops marinus* Meek and Hildebrand, 1923 — all are conspecific, and the valid name becomes *Melanorhinus microps* (Poey).

2. **Generic synonymy confirmed**: *Mugilops* Meek and Hildebrand, 1923 is a junior synonym of *Melanorhinus* Metzelaar, 1919 (following Myers and Wade, 1942; independently confirmed by Hildebrand in personal communication).

3. **Counting error identified**: Discrepancies between Poey's original ray counts (6 dorsal spines, 19 anal rays) and later specimens (7 spines typical; up to 25 anal rays) are attributed to Poey's likely use of a hand lens rather than proper high-power magnification — "rather high power with good illumination is required for reliable counting" of the small, closely-crowded rays.

4. **Species-level differentiation from Pacific congener**: *M. cyanellus* (Pacific) is distinguished from *M. microps* (Atlantic) primarily by dorsal spine count — 8–9 spines in *M. cyanellus* vs. 6–7 in *M. microps*.

5. **Museum specimen misidentification uncovered**: Seven specimens in USNM (no. 37530) originally labeled "Atherina microps? Cuba, 1885, Prof. F. Poey" were determined NOT to be cotypes, but rather represent a distinct species, *Atherina araea*, based on vertebral counts (43–44 vs. 39), opercle shape, jaw bone structure, and scale surface texture (smooth exposed field vs. vertically-circuli'd field in *Melanorhinus*).

6. **Taxonomic placement within Atherinidae**: The authors argue *Melanorhinus* should remain within subfamily **Atherinopsinae** rather than being elevated to its own monotypic subfamily "Melanorhininae" as proposed by Myers and Wade (1942, p. 140), since doing so would obscure genuine phylogenetic relationships with *Eurystole*, *Nectarges*, and *Notocheirus*.

7. **Biogeographic/evolutionary inference**: The convergence between *Melanorhinus* (Atlantic/Pacific American atherinopsines), *Iso* (Indo-Pacific atherinines), and mugilid querimana postlarvae represents independent evolutionary origins toward a shared surface-dwelling ecomorph, rather than close phylogenetic kinship.

8. **Type specimen data compiled** (Table 1) for *Atherina microps* types (Poey), *Melanorhinus boekei* types (Metzelaar), and *Mugilops marinus* type (Meek and Hildebrand, both original count and Hubbs/Rivas recount) — establishing the comparative meristic basis for synonymy.
