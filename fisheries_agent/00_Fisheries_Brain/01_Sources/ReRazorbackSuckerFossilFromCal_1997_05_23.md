# Razorback Sucker Fossil from California — Correspondence Summary

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1997 email exchange between Geoff Hoetker and Dr. W.L. Minckley relaying information from Stewart & Reeder (1993), which describes the only published fossil record of the genus **[[Xyrauchen]]** (Razorback Sucker). The fossil specimen (SBCM A73-1) was recovered from the Colorado Desert west of the Salton Sea, California, and was originally misclassified as part of the [[Palm Spring Formation]]. Subsequent geological work by Paul Remeika reassigned the fossil-bearing beds to a newly named informal unit, the **[[Diablo Formation]]**, composed of Colorado River-derived fluvial sandstones. This reassignment places the specimen in the **early Pliocene**, pushing back the known fossil record of large, river-adapted *Xyrauchen* lineages to more than three million years — the oldest documented occurrence of the genus at the time of writing.

## Background & Description
- Specimen **SBCM A73-1** is described as **"well-preserved"** and **"nearly complete"**, a rarity for fossil catostomids.
- Completeness of the specimen was considered sufficient to potentially resolve whether it represents:
  - An earlier, distinct species within **[[Xyrauchen]]**, or
  - An extension of the known range of the modern taxon **[[Xyrauchen texanus]]** into the early Pliocene.
- No direct osteological measurements, meristic counts, or diagnostic morphological descriptions are provided in this correspondence; detailed morphological analysis was reportedly underway by Dr. Kenneth Gobalet (California State University, Bakersfield) and Mark Reeder at time of writing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The fossil-bearing sediments are interpreted as part of the **deltaic plain of the ancestral [[Colorado River]]**, deposited along the western margin of the **[[Salton Trough]]** in southern California.
- This depositional context supports the inference that early *Xyrauchen* populations were already associated with **large, sediment-laden, river-fed depositional systems** — consistent with the modern Razorback Sucker's ecological affinity for large, turbid, main-stem river habitats such as the **[[Colorado River Basin]]**.
- The distinction between the **fluvial Diablo Formation** and the **lacustrine [[Borrego Formation]]** is ecologically significant: the fossil derives from riverine/deltaic sediments, not lake deposits, reinforcing the species' long-standing association with flowing river systems rather than standing water bodies.

## Key Findings
- **Only published fossil record of the genus *Xyrauchen*** (as of this correspondence), first noted in Minckley, Hendrickson & Bond (1986:581) and detailed in Stewart & Reeder (1993).
- Specimen **SBCM A73-1** collected by Ruth Coyle from the **north side of the San Felipe Hills**, near the western boundary of Imperial County, California, north of State Highway S-3.
- Originally mapped by **Dibblee (1954)** as part of the **Palm Spring Formation**.
- **Paul Remeika (1991)** and **Remeika & Lindsay (1992)** determined the fossil-bearing beds were not genetically equivalent to the true Palm Spring Formation (restricted to the Vallecito Badlands, Anza-Borrego Desert State Park) and formally proposed the informal **[[Diablo Formation]]** for these Colorado River-derived fluvial sandstones.
- Age reassignment places the specimen in the **early Pliocene**, extending the documented lineage of large, river-adapted *[[Xyrauchen]]* back **more than 3 million years**.
- Specimen completeness raises the possibility of identifying either:
  1. A **new/earlier species** within *Xyrauchen*, or
  2. A **temporal range extension** of *[[Xyrauchen texanus]]* into the Pliocene.
- Ongoing study at time of writing was being conducted by **Dr. Kenneth Gobalet** (CSU Bakersfield) and **Mark Reeder**.
