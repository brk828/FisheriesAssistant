# Potential Impacts of PIT Tagging on a Critically Endangered Small-Bodied Fish: A Trial on the Surrogate Mountain Galaxias

#ichthyology #Galaxiidae #biotelemetry #conservation

## Survey Objectives & Target Population

**Primary Sampling Purpose**: This study evaluated the effects of 9-mm PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags on survival, growth, and tag retention in [[Mountain Galaxias]] (Galaxias olidus) to assess the suitability of PIT telemetry for monitoring small-bodied galaxiid fishes, particularly threatened species within the Mountain Galaxias complex. The Mountain Galaxias served as a surrogate for 11 conservation-concern species identified in a recent taxonomic revision (Raadik 2014), 9 of which are known from only a few or single locations.

**Target Population & Spatial Boundaries**: The target population comprised adult [[Mountain Galaxias]] (72-100 mm length to caudal fork [LCF], mean = 80 mm) collected from three streams (Kangaroo, Lees, and Pierces creeks) in the [[Cotter River]] catchment, Australian Capital Territory, Australia. Fish were collected on two dates (June 9 and 23, 2017) using backpack electrofishing. The species is endemic to southeastern Australia and has become highly fragmented due to introduced [[Brown Trout]] and [[Rainbow Trout]]. The study aimed to inform monitoring protocols for the 15 species within the Mountain Galaxias complex, which exhibit similar morphology.

## Gear Specifications & Standardization

**Equipment & Mesh/Gear Parameters**: Fish were collected using a Smith-Root LR-24 backpack electrofisher and transported in aerated buckets. PIT tags were HPT9 model (Biomark, Inc.): 9 mm long × 2.12 mm diameter, weighing 0.065 g. Tags were implanted using pre-loaded 12-gauge needles and an MK25 implanter (Biomark). Tag mass represented 0.8-2.3% (mean = 1.6%) of fish body mass in air, with four instances exceeding the commonly recommended 2% threshold. Fish were anesthetized using clove oil (clove bud oil dissolved in 100% ethanol at 2 drops/mL, then mixed with water at 10 drops in 250 mL, yielding final concentration of 4 drops/L).

**Deployment & Effort Standardization**: All surgical equipment was sterilized with Betadine solution before and after tagging each fish. Tags were inserted into the midpoint of the abdominal cavity to the right of the ventral midline, with the needle directed posteriorly to reduce risk of organ damage. The implantation site was swabbed with Betadine before and after surgery. Fish were held until anesthesia effects reversed (cessation of paired fin movement, loss of equilibrium, no response to external stimuli). A single operator performed all tagging procedures. The study used a purpose-built implanter rather than internally sprung syringe applicator for greater control over needle penetration and tag injection.

**Selectivity & Bias Considerations**: The study acknowledged that tagger experience was limited but noted that fish mortality occurred mid-way through the tagging process and the single tag expulsion occurred in one of the last fish tagged, suggesting skill was easily learned. The document recommends future studies include a sham-surgery group (needle insertion without tag implantation) to isolate surgical procedure effects from tag presence effects. Fish were randomly assigned to tagged (n=34) or control (n=34) treatments. The study noted that release into wild conditions may intensify tag effects through greater predation pressure and interspecific competition, though comparable survival between aquaria and field studies has been reported previously.

## Experimental & Spatial Design Framework

**Stratification & Allocation**: The experimental design was a randomized before-after, control-impact (BACI) design. Sixty-eight fish were randomly assigned to tagged (n=34) or control (n=34) groups. Fish were housed in three separate 120-L aquaria (900 × 380 × 460 mm) with separate filtration units. Maximum density was 23 fish per tank, with approximately 50% from each treatment per tank (11-12 fish). This density was considered appropriate as [[Mountain Galaxias]] is a schooling species. Nine lengths of PVC pipe (100 mm long × 20 mm diameter) were arranged in each aquarium to provide shelter and refuge.

**Temporal Scheduling**: Fish were collected on two dates (June 9 and 23, 2017), tagged on July 7, 2017, and monitored for 90 days post-tagging. Food was withheld for 2 days prior to and 1 day after tagging. Fish were observed 5-7 times per week to check for survival and tag loss. Lengths and weights were measured at 0, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, and 90 days post-surgery. Water temperature was maintained at 16-21°C and monitored with HOBO Pendant temperature data loggers. A 10-hour photoperiod (0630-1630 hours Australian Eastern Standard Time) was controlled by artificial lighting. Fish were fed a mixed diet of commercially available frozen bloodworms and brine shrimp ([[Artemia]] spp.) 5-7 times per week.

**Detection & Bias Mitigation**: The study used visual inspection and weighing to detect mortality and tag loss. At each measurement, fish were visually inspected for signs of illness or disease, and the implant location on tagged fish was checked for signs of healing or infection. Fish were measured to the nearest millimeter LCF and weighed to the nearest 0.1 g. Tag weight was subtracted from total weight of tagged fish. Anesthetic was not used for post-tagging measurements. Necropsies were performed on fish that died to check for needle-related internal damage. The study acknowledged that individual fish in the control group could not be uniquely identified, limiting the ability to include individual-nested-within-tank random effects in statistical models.

## Field Data Schema & Variable Definitions

**Primary Biological Measurements**: **Length**: measured to nearest millimeter as length to caudal fork (LCF). **Weight**: measured to nearest 0.1 g; tag weight (0.065 g) subtracted from total weight of tagged fish. **Condition**: calculated as (weight/length^3.25) × 10^6. **Survival**: binary (alive/dead) recorded 5-7 times per week. **Tag retention**: binary (tag present/expelled) checked at each observation. **Growth**: change in weight over time, analyzed using log-transformed weight values. Initial fish size range: 72-100 mm LCF (mean = 80 mm); weight range: 2.20-8.40 g (control mean = 3.79 g; tagged mean = 4.49 g).

**Environmental & Spatial Covariates**: **Water temperature**: maintained at 16-21°C, measured with HOBO Pendant temperature data logger. **Salinity**: water treated with NaCl at 5‰ to reduce risk of fungal and bacterial infection. **Photoperiod**: 10-hour light cycle (0630-1630 hours). **Tank**: categorical variable (tanks 1, 2, 3) included in statistical models to account for potential tank effects. **Time (day)**: measurement day (0, 7, 14, 21, 28, 42, 90) included as random effect in growth models. Water was sourced from municipal supply and dechlorinated before use.

**QA/QC & Data Entry Protocols**: All surgical equipment was sterilized with Betadine solution before and after tagging each fish. Visual inspection for signs of illness, disease, healing, or infection was conducted at each measurement. Necropsies were performed on deceased fish to check for internal damage. Statistical analyses used R version 3.4.0. Survival and tag retention rates were calculated with 95% confidence intervals using the "binom.test" function. Growth was analyzed using linear mixed-effects models ("lmer" function in lme4 package) with log-transformed weight as response variable to normalize data and stabilize variance. The model equation was: log10(weight) = β0 + β1Tagged + β2After + β3(Tagged × After) + β4Tank + Time(day), where the interaction term β3(Tagged × After) estimated whether mean weight of tagged fish differed from control fish after tagging, accounting for pre-tagging differences.
