# Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement and Strategy Five-Year Assessment

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Leuciscidae #conservation-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This five-year assessment evaluates conservation actions conducted for [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) during the initial term (1995-2000) of the Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement and Strategy (VSCAS). The VSCAS was developed in 1995 as a collaborative effort among resource agencies and private landowners to prevent federal listing under the Endangered Species Act. By 1994, Virgin spinedace distribution had declined to approximately 60-63% of historic range (140 km of 231.6 km historically), primarily due to habitat destruction from water depletions, livestock grazing, nonnative species interactions, and inadequate regulatory mechanisms. The assessment documents substantial progress in threat reduction through flow restoration, habitat enhancement, nonnative fish control, and population monitoring across the [[Virgin River Basin]].

## Background & Description

[[Virgin Spinedace]] historically occurred in the [[Virgin River]] mainstem and several tributaries across southwestern Utah, northwestern Arizona, and southeastern Nevada, encompassing approximately 231.6 stream kilometers. The species is not adapted to lacustrine environments and was eliminated from reaches inundated by reservoir construction (Gunlock, Baker, Schroeder, and Quail Creek reservoirs).

**Threats at time of proposed listing (1994):**

**Habitat destruction and modification:** Water diversions and depletions eliminated Virgin spinedace from approximately 60 km of historic habitat including East Fork [[Beaver Dam Wash]], [[Santa Clara River]] below [[Gunlock Reservoir]], Mogatsu Creek, [[Ash Creek]] near Toquerville, Leeds Creek, and mainstem Virgin River between Quail Creek diversion and Pah Tempe Springs. An additional 31 km experienced water depletions altering habitat conditions. Livestock grazing caused de-vegetation of riparian areas, stream bank erosion, siltation, and water quality degradation in multiple reaches.

**Nonnative species:** [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) selectively prey on Virgin spinedace. [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) below Schroeder Reservoir prevented upstream migration and recolonization. [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) posed predation threats. [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*, formerly *Notropis lutrensis*) threatened through competition and predation. Crayfish (Astacidae) threatened populations in lower tributary reaches. [[Asian Tapeworm]] (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*) posed potential disease threats.

**Regulatory inadequacy:** More than half of Virgin River lands were privately owned. State agencies lacked authority to protect habitat despite rapid human development. No regulations guided nonnative fish stocking and introductions.

**Other factors:** Drought could de-water tributaries and isolate populations. Mining contaminants, agricultural return flows with pesticides and herbicides, livestock waste, and recreational use (off-highway vehicles, hiking, tubing) degraded habitat quality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Virgin River Basin]] is characterized by high temporal variability in flow and habitat availability. Virgin spinedace populations exhibit large annual fluctuations reflecting this environmental variability rather than necessarily indicating population declines. The species requires flowing water habitats and is eliminated from reservoir-inundated reaches.

**Baseline conditions** were established using four primary attributes: (1) basin hydrology based on 20-year averages and hydrologic simulation models from gauging stations; (2) channel geomorphology quantified through sediment transport regime and effective discharge; (3) water rights and depletions documented through water budgets and stream gauge records; and (4) ecology defined by species presence and habitat use in aquatic (mainstem and tributaries) and riparian (shoreline and 100-year floodplain) habitats.

**Habitat requirements:** Research conducted during the VSCAS term identified specific winter habitat use patterns, thermal regime requirements, and spatial distribution of habitat types utilized by Virgin spinedace. Multi-spectral imagery linked fish locations to habitat availability within a GIS framework.

**Genetic considerations:** Protocols for reintroduction emphasized using nearest-neighbor source populations to maintain genetic integrity and releasing sufficient numbers to prevent inbreeding depression and loss of rare alleles through genetic drift.

## Key Findings

**Seven required conservation actions were substantially implemented:**

**1. Establish existing conditions as baseline (COMPLETED):**
- Baseline conditions described for Virgin River Basin including hydrology, geomorphology, water rights/depletions, and ecology
- Stream Channel Alteration (SCA) permit review process modified to prevent Virgin spinedace impacts
- 170+ SCA application reviews conducted by Utah Division of Wildlife Resources (UDWR) and U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) from 1995-1999
- Standard protective conditions established for permits including: wet cement exclusion from streams, vegetation protection, low-flow work periods, sediment control, proper riprap sizing and placement
- Special mitigation measures required for projects with direct spinedace impacts, including fish passage structures, intake screening, and spawning season work restrictions

**2. Re-establish population maintenance flows (SUBSTANTIALLY COMPLETED):**
- **Virgin River Narrows:** Washington County Water Conservancy District (WCWCD) restored and maintains 3 cubic feet per second (cfs) flow below Quail Creek diversion, restoring 4.5 km of habitat; small Virgin spinedace population now persists
- **Santa Clara River below Gunlock Reservoir:** Preparations completed to provide 3 cfs flow restoring 31 km of habitat; $2,285,000 secured; pipeline engineering plans and specifications completed; irrigation company agreements negotiated; environmental assessment underway; awaiting completion of Shivwitz Water Right Settlement negotiations (expected early summer 2000)
- **LaVerkin Creek:** WCWCD purchased water rights providing 5 cfs year-round flow, restoring 11.4 miles (18.3 km) of habitat
- **Total flow restoration:** Approximately 46.9 km of stream habitat to be restored, exceeding the 39 km VSCAS requirement and achieving 93% of historic range (exceeding 80% goal)
- [[Zion National Park]] implemented Water Rights Settlement Agreement assuring adequate instream flows in North Fork and East Fork Virgin River

**3. Enhance and maintain habitat (EXCEEDED REQUIREMENTS):**
- Actions conducted across 54.8+ stream km, exceeding 26 km requirement
- **Bureau of Land Management (BLM) land acquisitions:** 3.4 km of Virgin River and tributaries acquired including Anderson Exchange (0.6 km), Stowe Exchange (0.8 km), Mendenhall Exchange (1.6 km), and Lowe Exchange (0.4 km)
- **Virgin Town Park Management Agreement:** Protective fencing on 0.8 km restricting OHV use and livestock grazing; parking barriers; restroom facility; native tree plantings; night gate closure
- **Four Areas of Critical Environmental Concern (ACEC) designated:** 28.1 stream km total including 6.4 km lower Virgin River, 7.2 km Santa Clara River, and 14.5 km West Fork Beaver Dam Wash; prescriptions include collaboration on VSCAS implementation, riparian protection, rangeland health standards, closure to fuelwood/mineral sales, right-of-way avoidance, motorized travel restrictions
- **Mining and fire rehabilitation:** Goldstrike Mine (400 acres), Silver Reef Mine (63 shafts closed), East Reef Mine, Greek Peak prescribed burn (700 acres), Cedar Pockets fire (4,000 acres), Pahcoon fire (5,500 acres)
- **Grazing management:** Health assessments completed for four allotments on 11.3 km North Fork Virgin River
- **LaVerkin Creek fish passage:** Graduated steps/waterfalls constructed below Jones diversion providing 5.6 km connected habitat (damaged by July 1999 flood, repair planned)
- **LaVerkin Creek sinkhole repair:** Natural sinkhole that drained entire stream flow repaired with earthen dam and diversion pipe (later naturally plugged by September flood)
- **Santa Clara Reserve Cooperative Agreement:** Restricts camping and OHV use
- **Protective fence:** 0.8 km Santa Clara River below Gunlock Reservoir
- **Virgin River Resource Management and Recovery Program (VRRMRP):** Committed $700,000 for acquisition of 4.8 km habitat along Virgin River, LaVerkin Creek, and Ash Creek with conservation easement
- **Zion National Park:** Banned recreational tubing on North Fork Virgin River following study showing impacts to native fish distribution, abundance, community structure, and larval fish recruitment

**4. Selectively control non-indigenous fish (SUBSTANTIALLY COMPLETED):**
- **Fish Stocking and Transfer Procedures Policy:** UDWR approved and implemented statewide policy requiring consistency with sensitive species conservation
- **Stocking cessation:** Brown trout stocking ceased below Veyo on Santa Clara River
- **Beaver Dam Wash regulations:** Nevada Division of Wildlife (NDOW) implemented artificial lures only for sportfishing on Beaver Dam Wash and Schroeder Reservoir (March 1998)
- **Private pond permits:** 22 permits reviewed in Washington County; only bluegill, largemouth bass, and rainbow trout introductions approved; denials included channel catfish, smallmouth bass, yellow perch, walleye, carp, white bass, black crappie, white crappie, and fathead minnow
- **Rainbow trout removal:** NDOW selectively removed rainbow trout from Beaver Dam Wash tributary below Schroeder Reservoir using backpack electroshockers; log and rock barrier constructed to prevent re-invasion
- **Red shiner eradication program:** Systematic five-phase plan developed and implemented using rotenone treatments:
  - Phase 1: Three treatments between Washington Fields and Johnson diversions (March, August, December 1996; October 1999); $141,345 cost
  - Phase 2: Fort Pearce Wash barrier constructed; chemical treatment December 1997; temporarily successful but three red shiners captured post-flooding
  - Phase 3: Washington Fields drains/canals barrier constructed; chemical treatment December 1999 successful
  - Phase 4: Webb Hill fish barrier planned; draft EA and hydrological studies completed
  - Phase 5: Scouting and mapping scheduled for 2000
- **Green sunfish control:** Analysis of removal options initiated for Santa Clara River as part of Baker Reservoir EA; implementation planned after flow restoration below Gunlock Reservoir

**5. Maintain genetic viability (PROTOCOLS DEVELOPED):**
- NDOW developed and implemented reintroduction protocol for Beaver Dam Wash tributary
- Source fish selected from nearest-neighbor populations (Mormon Well and Lytle Ranch on Beaver Dam Wash)
- Multiple releases: 274 fish (February 1997), 408 fish (December 1997), 400 fish (1998)
- High numbers released to prevent inbreeding depression and genetic drift
- Monitoring observed numerous adults but no recruitment detected; 2000 evaluation report planned
- Basin-wide transfer protocols still need formalization

**6. Population and habitat monitoring (EXCEEDED REQUIREMENTS):**
- **Annual population monitoring:** Six consecutive years (1994-1999) at 12 standardized stations using block nets and backpack electroshockers (10 stations) or seines (2 stations)
- Virgin spinedace observed at each station each year except Motoqua (1994) and LaVerkin Creek (1998)
- Successful reproduction observed at all stations in at least 3 years; 10 of 12 stations showed reproduction in at least 4 of 5 years
- Large annual fluctuations observed, likely reflecting temporal habitat variability rather than population declines
- Population size structure determined through length frequency histograms; density estimates calculated as fish per 100 m²
- **Quail Creek monitoring:** Utah State University (USU) monitored four sites below Quail Creek diversion five times annually (1996-1998) to assess response to restored flows
- **Virgin River Fishes Database:** USU developed and maintained database; pre-1991 data modified for multi-pass depletion analysis compatibility
- **Long-term trend analysis:** USU examined trends at six standardized locations (Near Ash Creek, Above Quail Creek, Below Hurricane Bridge, Above Washington Fields diversion, Twin Bridges, Atkinville Wash)
- **Winter habitat use study:** USU research determined specific habitat types and locations utilized during winter
- **Thermal regime characterization:** USU monitored temperatures above and below Quail Creek Reservoir (winter 1998, summer 1999) at six locations; cross-section profiles evaluated thermal mixing zone
- **Habitat mapping:** USU used multi-spectral imagery to delineate spatial distribution of habitat types at monitoring sites; integrated with GIS framework
- **NDOW monitoring:** Beaver Dam Wash tributary reintroduction site monitored 1997-1999
- **Arizona Game and Fish Department surveys:** Mormon Well and Beaver Dam Wash near Virgin River confluence
- Total monitoring cost: $186,965

**7. Develop mitigation plan and protocols (COMPLETED):**
- Plan completed as part of VRRMRP
- Recovery banking system established using "recovery units" as currency
- Quantifies, allocates, and administers recovery units based on habitat requirements for Virgin spinedace, [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), and [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila seminuda*)
- Provides mechanism for water development while promoting species recovery
- VRRMRP provides 10-year funding source for Virgin River fish conservation

**Additional significant actions:**
- **Recreational impact studies:** University of Nevada Las Vegas study documented that wading and tubing affect native fish distribution, abundance, community structure, and larval recruitment; lower community diversity in high recreation areas
- **Zion Narrows hiking study:** Invertebrate biomass and densities decrease with increased hiking; current hiking volume diminishes river ecosystem integrity and may negatively impact fish productivity
- **Virgin River Instream Flow Study:** BLM completed field work identifying and quantifying water-dependent resources on 193 km of public land segments
- **Basin-wide instream flow study:** VRRMRP committed funds for comprehensive study plan
- **Shunes Creek diversion EA:** Zion National Park issued EA for water diversion satisfying private water right; UDWR provided technical input; alternatives include infiltration gallery, fish passage, intake/outlet screening, and spawning season work restrictions; no final decision

**Threat reduction assessment:**

**Habitat threats:** Significantly reduced through baseline establishment, SCA permit review modifications, flow restorations, livestock exclosures (1.6 km protected), grazing assessments, ACEC designations (28.1 km), land acquisitions (8.2 km), and habitat connectivity restoration. Natural hydrology cannot be completely restored but water depletion threats substantially reduced.

**Nonnative species threats:** Reduced through stocking regulations, rainbow trout removal, ongoing red shiner eradication (three phases completed), and private pond permit restrictions. Green sunfish control analysis initiated.

**Regulatory inadequacy:** Addressed through VSCAS multi-agency cooperation, modified SCA review process, and fish stocking/introduction regulations.

**Other threats:** Mining threats reduced through closures and cleanups; recreational threats reduced through Virgin Town Park agreement, Santa Clara Reserve agreement, ACEC OHV restrictions, and Zion tubing ban; natural sinkhole threat eliminated through LaVerkin Creek repair.

**Population status:** Further declines appear halted; populations generally stable with successful reproduction documented; large annual fluctuations likely reflect habitat variability rather than true population changes; time lag expected between threat reduction and population responses.

**Recommendations for second five-year term:**
1. Complete Santa Clara River flow restoration negotiations and implementation
2. Identify and prioritize additional reaches for flow restoration (East Fork Beaver Dam Slope, Mogatsu Creek, Santa Clara River above/below Veyo and above Gunlock Reservoir, lower Santa Clara River, Leeds Creek, Quail Creek, Lower North Creek)
3. Conduct habitat enhancement in unimproved occupied reaches (Beaver Dam Wash below Schroeder Reservoir, Littlefield, East Fork, Santa Clara River above Gunlock Reservoir, lower Santa Clara River)
4. Expand green sunfish control analysis and implement removal methodologies
5. Continue stepwise red shiner eradication after barrier construction
6. Evaluate Beaver Dam Wash reintroduction success and develop basin-wide transfer protocols
7. Revise VSCAS based on information acquired during initial term

**Conclusion:** The VSCAS successfully reduced or eliminated major threats warranting listing. Upon completion of scheduled actions (particularly Santa Clara River flow restoration), Virgin spinedace will occupy approximately 93% of historic range, exceeding the 80% goal. Substantial new information on population variability, habitat use, and recovery needs acquired. VSCAS revision recommended to incorporate current knowledge. Extension for additional five-year term warranted based on progress toward recovery goals.
