# Rainbow Trout Stocking and Recapture in the Edersee

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This short German fisheries report, reprinted from *Der Fischwirt*, describes the release of 470 [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) into the [[Edersee]] reservoir in [[Hesse]], Germany, on 4 December 1961. The fish were individually marked and tracked through recaptures during their first months in the reservoir. Within a few months, 38 marked fish (8.1%) had been recaptured across the lake and in distant tributaries. Most recaptures occurred in March and April, which the authors interpret as spawning migrations into tributaries. The authors conclude that the species can reproduce in the Edersee's inflows and that the reservoir has room for it alongside the existing [[Northern Pike]] stock, which would raise the fishery's yield value and enrich tributary fish fauna.

## Background & Description

- **Study type:** Stocking and tag-and-recapture study, reported as preliminary results after the first summer.
- **Authors and partners:** K. Müller, in cooperation with Dr. G. Haß (fisheries referent at the Hessian Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry) and master fisher H. J. Seidlitz. The authors thank local anglers for their reports.
- **Release:** 470 rainbow trout released at Rehbach, Edersee, on 4 December 1961.
- **Size range:** 25–55 cm total length. All fish were measured.
- **Marking method:** Each fish received a numbered aluminium tag attached to a nylon thread. The thread was anchored in the dorsal musculature below the dorsal fin, so each recapture could be identified individually.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Reservoir setting:** The Edersee is a dammed reservoir (*Talsperre*) with a managed fish community. The authors state that a [[Northern Pike]] stock is already present and that the reservoir still has space for rainbow trout.
- **Tributaries as habitat:** Recaptures in distant inflows showed that trout could reach and use tributaries far from the stocking site. The authors see the tributaries as the key to natural reproduction.
- **Reproductive state at release:** Some fish had already spawned by the time of release. Others, especially individuals around 30 cm, still carried ripe gonadal products. The authors therefore infer two groups in the stocked population: an early-spawning and a late-spawning component.
- **Seasonal movement:** Recaptures were concentrated in the mouths of tributaries (Bäche) in spring. The authors describe this as a spawning migration.

## Key Findings

1. **Initial recapture:** The first marked trout was caught on 15 January 1962 at Niederwerbe (34 cm). On 17 January, two more marked fish were caught at the same site. They were recorded and released again. One was seen again in mid-March and released once more.
2. **Recapture total:** 38 recaptures were recorded after the first summer, equal to 8.1% of the 470 fish stocked. A further fish was recaptured in the Orke tributary on 20 April 1962.
3. **Spatial spread:** Recaptures came from across the whole lake and from distant tributaries, which the authors describe as surprisingly rapid dispersal.
4. **Seasonal concentration:** 80% of recaptures fell in March and April. The authors attribute this to spawning migrations into tributary mouths.
5. **Spawning observed:** Spawning trout were observed in March and April at Niederwerbe, Asel, Herzhausen and Bringhausen.
6. **Reproductive potential:** The authors conclude that the Edersee's tributaries allow natural reproduction of rainbow trout.
7. **Management recommendation:** The authors argue that rainbow trout can coexist with the existing pike stock. They expect stocking to increase the fishery's yield value and enrich the fish fauna of the tributaries.
