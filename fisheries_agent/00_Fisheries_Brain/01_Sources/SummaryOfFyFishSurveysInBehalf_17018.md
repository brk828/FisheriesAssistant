# Central Arizona Project Fish Monitoring FY 1999 Summary

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #invasive-species #canal-management

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes fish population monitoring conducted in the [[Gila River Basin]], Arizona during fiscal year 1999 (October 1998 - September 1999) under Cooperative Agreement 1425-97-FC-32-00780 between the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[Arizona State University]]. Sampling was performed by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD), ASU, and USBR across seven water systems: [[San Pedro River]], [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], [[Central Arizona Project Canal]], and three [[Salt River Project]] canals. A total of 27 taxa were captured, with only four native species (15% of total) documented: [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Desert Sucker]]. Native fish proportions varied dramatically among stations, from 0% in the CAP Canal to 74.3% in the San Pedro River. The monitoring employed standardized protocols detailed by Clarkson (1996 a-c), though implementation challenges included variable electrofishing efficiency and inconsistent area measurements.

## Background & Description

The monitoring program targets fish populations in selected waters of the Gila River basin as part of long-term assessment efforts. Sampling occurred between September 29, 1998 and January 28, 1999 across multiple station types. Waters sampled included: (1) San Pedro River downstream from the U.S.-Mexico international boundary (8 of 9 stations sampled), (2) Gila River between Coolidge Dam and Ashurst-Hayden Diversion (10 of 12 stations), (3) Salt River between Stewart Mountain Dam and Granite Reef Diversion (all 3 stations), (4) CAP Canal at selected pump plants (7 stations), (5) SRP North (Arizona) Canal (multiple stations), (6) SRP South Canal (multiple stations), and (7) Florence-Casa Grande Canal (6 stations). Additional opportunistic sampling was performed on SRP Consolidated and Tempe canals.

Sampling methods varied by location and included backpack electrofishing, boat-mounted electrofishing, seining, gill nets, hoop nets, trammel nets, minnow traps, angling, and visual observation. Backpack electrofishing was the primary quantitative method for river stations, while canal sampling employed diverse gear types. Protocol deviations were noted, particularly regarding area coverage estimation and electrofishing technique variability among users.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Gila River Basin]] represents a critical freshwater ecosystem in the southwestern United States, characterized by highly modified hydrology due to dam construction, water diversion, and canal systems. The basin supports both native desert-adapted fish species and numerous non-native species introduced through various pathways. Electric fish barriers have been installed on some canal systems (SRP canals, FCG Canal) to prevent fish movement, creating distinct upstream and downstream fish assemblages.

Native fish species in the basin are adapted to variable desert stream conditions but face competition and predation from non-native species. The four native species documented represent remnant populations of historically more widespread taxa. [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] (Family [[Catostomidae]]) are benthic feeders adapted to rocky substrates, while [[Longfin Dace]] (Family [[Cyprinidae]]) occupies flowing water habitats. [[Roundtail Chub]] is a larger-bodied cyprinid now rare in most locations.

Non-native species dominance reflects decades of introductions for sport fishing, aquaculture, and accidental releases. [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), and various centrarchids ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]]) are widespread. Tilapia species, particularly in canal systems, represent recent invasions with potential for rapid population growth.

## Key Findings

**Species Richness and Native Fish Status:**
- Total of 27 taxa captured during FY 1999 monitoring, including Lepomis hybrids
- Only 4 native taxa (15% of total species): [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]]
- No stream or canal produced more than 3 native species
- CAP Canal contained zero native species
- Native species comprised 18-25% of all species among stations (except CAP Canal)

**San Pedro River (12 species total, 3 native):**
- Native fish comprised 74.3% of total catch (highest proportion of all systems)
- [[Longfin Dace]] most abundant overall (64% of total numbers) and at upper/middle reaches
- Native fish comprised 59% of upper reach, 96% of middle reach, 39.9% of lower reach
- [[Desert Sucker]] at 6 of 8 stations; [[Sonora Sucker]] only at lower two stations
- [[Western Mosquitofish]] most abundant non-native (13% of catch)
- [[Red Shiner]] comprised 4.3%, black bullhead 4.1%, green sunfish 2.2% of total catch

**Gila River (15 species total, 3 native):**
- Native fish only 22.4% of total catch
- [[Red Shiner]] most abundant species overall (36% of total catch)
- [[Desert Sucker]] comprised 14%, [[Sonora Sucker]] 9%, [[Longfin Dace]] only 0.4% of total
- [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] found at 8 of 10 stations across all four reaches
- [[Channel Catfish]] most abundant in lower reach, followed by [[Western Mosquitofish]] and yellow bullhead
- Native proportions by reach: 13% (reach 1), 29% (reach 2), 27% (reach 3), 11% (reach 4)

**Salt River (13 species total, 3 native):**
- Native fish comprised 44.7% of total catch
- [[Desert Sucker]] most abundant species overall (35%) and at two upper stations
- [[Sonora Sucker]] contributed 10%; [[Roundtail Chub]] rare (one individual)
- [[Largemouth Bass]] second most abundant overall (17%) and most abundant at lower station
- [[Western Mosquitofish]] 15% of total; blue tilapia 14%
- [[Channel Catfish]] and flathead catfish both rare (<1% of catch)

**Central Arizona Project Canal (12 taxa, all non-native):**
- Zero native species captured
- [[Bluegill]] most abundant overall (53% of total numbers)
- [[Common Carp]] second most abundant (22%), followed by hybrid Lepomis (8%)
- Centrarchids predominant among samples
- Grass carp, carp, [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], redear sunfish, [[Largemouth Bass]], and striped bass in all 3 reaches
- No species found at all 7 stations; [[Bluegill]] and striped bass at 6 of 7 stations

**SRP North (Arizona) Canal (17 species, 3 native):**
- Native fish 17.4% above electric barrier, 14.3% below barrier
- Undetermined Tilapia (primarily young-of-year) overwhelmingly predominant above barrier (57% overall)
- [[Red Shiner]] second most abundant (15% of total)
- [[Desert Sucker]] 7%, [[Sonora Sucker]] 6%, [[Roundtail Chub]] 9% of overall catch
- Native sucker abundances likely underestimated due to sub-sampling of large aggregations
- Below barrier: [[Red Shiner]] predominant in upper reach, [[Sonora Sucker]] in middle reach, [[Desert Sucker]] in lower reach
- Goldfish only species found below but not above barrier

**SRP South Canal (16 species, 3 native):**
- Native fish 24.8% above barrier, 27.1% below barrier
- Tilapia species most abundant overall (56% of total catch)
- [[Sonora Sucker]] second most abundant (11%), [[Roundtail Chub]] 10%, [[Desert Sucker]] 5%
- [[Red Shiner]] 9%, [[Channel Catfish]] 5% of total
- Below barrier: [[Desert Sucker]] predominant at upper station, [[Red Shiner]] at middle and lower stations
- Grass carp, [[Western Mosquitofish]], and redbelly tilapia found below but not above barrier

**Florence-Casa Grande Canal (8 species, 2 native):**
- Native fish only 1.7% above barrier, 1.3% below barrier (lowest proportions)
- [[Red Shiner]] predominant species (75% of catch)
- [[Western Mosquitofish]] 10%, [[Channel Catfish]] 8%, yellow bullhead 3%
- Total of 8 [[Sonora Sucker]] at five stations; [[Longfin Dace]] at one site (2 individuals)
- Only artificial stream where [[Longfin Dace]] encountered
- [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Red Shiner]], and [[Western Mosquitofish]] at 5 of 6 stations

**Opportunistic Sampling:**
- SRP Consolidated Canal: 10 species including native [[Desert Sucker]] (17 specimens, fourth in abundance)
- [[Western Mosquitofish]] most abundant, followed by [[Red Shiner]], redbelly tilapia, [[Channel Catfish]]
- Only location where two Tilapia species (redbelly and blue) found together
- SRP Tempe Canal: 5 species observed (qualitative data only)
- Native [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] both abundant
- [[Channel Catfish]] common; "Koi" variety carp and stocked grass carp observed

**Sampling Protocol Issues and Recommendations:**
- Area of sampling coverage highly variable and poorly estimated across sites
- No actual measurement of sampled area; visual assessment by individual electrofishers
- Multiple persons with different perceptions responsible for area determination
- Electrofishing technique and efficiency highly variable among users
- Data comparability among samples, reaches, and stations compromised
- Recommendations include: intensive training for all personnel, standardized equipment demonstrations, sketch maps for each station visit, improved communication with canal operators regarding scheduled outages, examination of boat-mounted electrofishing as standard technique during normal flow conditions, obtaining keys to locked canal gates
