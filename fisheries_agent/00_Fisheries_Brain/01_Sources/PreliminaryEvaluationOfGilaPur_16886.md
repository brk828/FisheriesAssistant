## Executive Summary
This 1987 report by Galat and Gerhardt presents a preliminary evaluation of the food habits, gut morphology, and reproductive characteristics of the federally endangered [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) at the San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge (SBNWR), Cochise County, Arizona. Sixty-nine specimens were collected from Black Draw Wash (San Bernardino Creek) across three sampling dates (October, January, and March) using multiple gear types. Contrary to earlier characterizations by [[Minckley 1973]] describing *G. purpurea* as primarily herbivorous, this study's gut morphology and gut content data indicate the species is more accurately described as **omnivorous and euryphagous**, with a diet dominated by aquatic invertebrates (particularly chironomid dipterans and zygopteran damselflies) supplemented by filamentous algae and occasional vascular plant material.

## Background & Description
- **Standard Length (SL):** Range 20–139 mm; mean ± SD = 51.3 ± 20.6 mm (N = 69)
- **Wet Weight:** Range 0.2–80.7 g; mean ± SD = 6.3 ± 10.8 g
- **Gut Length:Body Length Ratio:** Mean ± SD = 83.9 ± 11.4%; range 57.7–109.8%
  - This ratio is notably below the >100–200% typically seen in herbivorous fishes (per [[Moyle and Cech 1983]]), supporting a more carnivorous/omnivorous digestive strategy than previously assumed.
- **Gill Rakers:** Low count, mean ± SD = 10 ± 0.5 (N = 20); short, widely spaced, and morphologically undifferentiated — consistent with a non-specialized planktivorous/herbivorous feeding apparatus.
- **Reproductive Morphology:**
  - Mature ova diameter: range 0.6–1.1 mm; mean = 0.85 mm (SD = 0.6, N = 15)
  - High intra-ovarian variability in ova size/developmental stage suggests **fractional spawning** behavior
  - Ovary weight:body weight ratio: 4.2–11.5%, relatively stable between January and March samples (though confounded by size/gear/site differences)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*Gila purpurea* occupies a narrow, fragmented range restricted to only three known wild population locations within the Yaqui and Sulfur Springs basins in the United States:
1. [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] (Cochise County, AZ)
2. [[Leslie Creek]], Swisshelm Mountains
3. A small pond adjacent to [[Turkey Creek]], Chiricahua Mountains

The species inhabits desert spring-fed streams and washes, exemplified by the Black Draw Wash/San Bernardino Creek system. As an omnivore in this habitat, *G. purpurea* fills a generalist trophic role, consuming both aquatic macroinvertebrates and algal/plant material, likely opportunistically based on seasonal availability (e.g., plant material was prominent in January samples but absent in March samples from a Mexican border site influenced by thermal springs).

## Key Findings
- **Diet composition:** 67% of examined fish had food present in guts; most guts were not full, and a large proportion of contents were amorphous/unidentifiable.
- **Dominant invertebrate prey:**
  - Aquatic Diptera (primarily [[Chironomidae]]): most frequent prey item (27% of fish with food), averaging 43% ± 33% SD of relative gut volume when present
  - Odonata (exclusively Zygoptera/damselflies): second most frequent (20%), averaging 26% ± 28% SD of relative gut volume when present
- **Minor invertebrate prey items:** Ephemeroptera (9.1%), Ostracoda (9.1%), Coleoptera (4.5%), Collembola (2.3%), Cladocera (2.3%), terrestrial insects (2.3%)
- **Plant material:**
  - *Mougeotia* (filamentous Chlorophyte alga) was the most commonly recorded filamentous alga (23% of fish)
  - Other unidentified filamentous algae also observed in 23% of fish
  - Vascular plants were present but uncommon (9.1%)
  - Strong seasonal variation: abundant in January samples, absent in March (Mexican border) samples
- **Revised trophic classification:** Findings contradict [[Minckley 1973]]'s earlier characterization of *G. purpurea* as "entirely algae and a few terrestrial insects and arachnids." This study concludes the species is **omnivorous**, not strictly herbivorous, based on convergent evidence from gut length:body length ratios, gill raker morphology, and actual gut content analysis.
- **Study limitations:** Sampling occurred only in October, January, and March — missing the spring-summer breeding season — limiting the representativeness of fecundity data. Small sample sizes in both this study and Minckley's prior work limit generalizability.
- **Uncertain digestive capacity:** It remains unknown whether *G. purpurea* can effectively digest plant material; its moderate gut length suggests it would not be an efficient herbivore even when algae is consumed.
