# White Catfish (Ictalurus catus) of the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ictaluridae #freshwater-ecology #population-study

## Executive Summary

This study investigated the biology and vital statistics of [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*) in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]] during 1952-1955. The research employed extensive tagging programs (totaling 4,864 tagged fish across three studies) to assess movement patterns, mortality rates, and population dynamics. Key biological parameters were determined including age-growth relationships using vertebral analysis, spawning timing and size at maturity, diet composition, and survival rates. The study was conducted following the abolition of commercial catfish fishing in September 1953 due to signs of overfishing, and aimed to provide management information for the remaining sport fishery.

## Background & Description

[[White Catfish]] support a substantial sport fishery in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. Prior to September 1953, they also supported a commercial fishery that was abolished when the population showed signs of overfishing. This represents the third and final report of a study designed to obtain background information for effective management of the Delta catfish resource.

The study employed three separate tagging periods between 1952-1954, using different tag types (opercular strap tags, disk-dangler tags, and staple tags) at multiple sites throughout the delta system including [[Antioch Bridge]], [[Old River]], [[Burns Cutoff]], [[Sandmound Slough]], [[Sutter Slough]], [[Cache Slough]], [[Disappointment Slough]], and the [[Sacramento River]].

Fish were captured using typical commercial fyke (hoop) nets, with only healthy-appearing individuals tagged without size selection. All measurements reported are fork lengths (FL).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and Distribution**: [[White Catfish]] in the delta system exhibited variable movement patterns depending on release location. Fish released at [[Sutter Slough]], [[Burns Cut]], and upper [[Sacramento River]] typically moved considerable distances upstream. Those from [[Antioch Bridge]] and [[Cache Slough]] showed marked upstream movement but most recaptures occurred within 5 miles of tagging sites. Fish from [[Disappointment Slough]] and [[Old River]] showed least tendency to leave tagging areas, dispersing rapidly within apparent home ranges.

**Diet Composition**: Analysis of 121 stomachs from the [[Antioch Bridge]] area (1953-1954) and 29 from [[Fremont Weir]] revealed:
- **Primary food**: Amphipods (*Corophium* spp.) were the most important food throughout the year in the lower Delta
- **Seasonal items**: [[American Shad]] (*Alosa sapidissima*) parts were conspicuous during shad runs at [[Fremont Weir]]
- **Other prey**: Unidentified fishes, insects, clams, crayfish (*Pacifastacus* sp.), and mysid shrimp (*Neomysis mercedis*)
- **Detritus**: Unidentified animal and plant debris formed approximately 50% of stomach contents from both areas

**Water Temperature and Spawning**: Spawning activity was closely tied to water temperature, commencing when temperatures reached 70°F in late June and continuing into July.

## Key Findings

**Age Determination Method**: The seventh spinal vertebra proved suitable for age determination, avoiding problems with pectoral spines where first and second annuli are often obliterated by bone deterioration. The body length-vertebral radius relationship was curvilinear:

L = 0.76760 + 0.056651R - 0.000051R²

Where L = fork length (inches), R = vertebral radius (mm × 0.22), with correlation coefficient = 0.972.

**Growth Rates**: Mean fork lengths at each year of life (based on 463 fish collected at [[Fremont Weir]] in October 1954-1955):
- Age I: 3.1 inches
- Age II: 5.2 inches  
- Age III: 6.9 inches
- Age IV: 8.4 inches
- Age V: 9.9 inches
- Age VI: 11.5 inches
- Age VII: 12.9 inches
- Age VIII: 13.8 inches

Average annual increments ranged from 0.9 to 3.1 inches, with largest growth in first year. Delta [[White Catfish]] grow appreciably slower during the first three years of life than those in the [[Patuxent River]], Maryland.

**Length-Weight Relationship**: Based on 443 fish from [[Fremont Weir]]:
Log L (mm) = -5.65374 + 3.33063W (grams)

**Spawning Biology**:
- **Timing**: Spawning commenced when water temperatures reached 70°F in late June, continuing into July
- **1953 observations**: No spent fish observed June 8-23 (64-66°F); 17 of 32 mature females spent/partially spent July 1 (66-71°F); 16 of 20 mature females spent July 21 (72°F)
- **1954 observations**: 9 of 27 mature females spent July 25 (70°F); all 138 fish spent by July 29 (77°F)

**Size at Maturity** (based on 762 fish examined):
- 6.0-6.4 inches: ~10% mature
- 6.5-6.9 inches: ~40% mature  
- 7.0-7.4 inches: ~60% mature
- 7.5-7.9 inches: ~85% mature
- 8.0-8.9 inches: 90-95% mature
- Over 9 inches: nearly 100% mature
- No difference in size at maturity between males and females

**Fecundity**: Linear relationship between body weight and egg production within common size range (100-600 grams): approximately 600 eggs per 100 grams body weight.

**Tagging Results**:
- **First study** (Jan-May 1952): 2,865 opercular strap tags and 601 disk-dangler tags released at six sites
- **Second study** (Dec 1952-Jan 1953): 1,499 fish tagged (250 staple tags and 249-250 disk-danglers at each of three sites)
- **Third study** (Jan-Apr 1954): 2,000 fish tagged with disk-danglers only at four sites
- Disk-dangler tags showed sixfold greater return than opercular strap tags in first study
- Disk-dangler returns exceeded staple tag returns 278 to 220 in second study

**Vulnerability**: Vulnerability to sport fishery increased with size. Average first-year returns by size group:
- Less than 7.5 inches: 9.2% (84 of 913 tagged)
- 7.5-8.4 inches: 14.6% (232 of 1,590 tagged)
- 8.5 inches and larger: 21.1% (208 of 985 tagged)

Fish 8.5 inches and larger considered fully vulnerable to sport fishery.

**Mortality and Survival Rates** (assuming 60% tag return rate):

| Age Class | Survival (s) | Total Mortality (a) | Fishing Mortality (u) | Natural Mortality (v) |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| II+ to III+ | 0.50 | 0.50 | 0.15 | 0.35 |
| III+ to IV+ | 0.44 | 0.56 | 0.24 | 0.32 |
| IV+ onward | 0.43 | 0.57 | 0.33 | 0.24 |

For fully vulnerable fish (over 8.5 inches FL, Age IV+):
- **Total annual mortality**: 57%
- **Fishing mortality**: 33%
- **Natural mortality**: 24%
- **Annual survival**: 43%

**Management Implications**: The study provided critical baseline data following abolition of commercial fishing. High fishing mortality (33%) on fully vulnerable fish indicated continued need for monitoring of the sport fishery to prevent overfishing of the [[White Catfish]] population in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]].
