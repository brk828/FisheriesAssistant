# Razorback Sucker Monitoring in Lake Mohave, Arizona and Nevada (1995-1996)

#ichthyology #endangered-species #catostomidae #population-monitoring #repatriation

## Executive Summary

This report documents monitoring of the critically imperiled [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) population in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona and Nevada, from November 1, 1995 to October 31, 1996. The adult population has declined from 60-75,000 individuals in the late 1980s to approximately 25,000, representing the largest remaining population of this species. Natural recruitment is precluded by predation on early life stages by non-native fishes. During the reporting period, 1,883 razorback suckers were captured, including 534 recaptures (28.4% recapture rate). The study employed [[PIT Tags]] (passive integrated transponder tags) for individual identification and [[Trammel Netting]] as the primary capture method. A repatriation program involving larval harvest, protected rearing, and stocking of juveniles at target size (30 cm TL) showed promising results with an estimated 56.2% survivorship of 4,580 cove-reared fish stocked between 1992-1995.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large-bodied catostomid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Adults in Lake Mohave have been monitored routinely since the late 1960s, with sampling traditionally conducted in autumn (November) and spring (March). The species is characterized by exceptional genetic diversity in the adult population. Wild adults are captured primarily using trammel nets (typically 91 m long × 1.8 m deep, with 3.8 cm inner and 20-30 cm outer mesh) set near shore at depths of 1-10 meters along gravel spits, reefs, small embayments, and gently sloping shorelines. Catch rates are greatest between dusk and dawn, with nets tended at approximately 6-hour intervals.

Individual fish are measured (total length to nearest mm), weighed (optional given existing weight-length database), sexed, examined for parasites, disease, deformity, injury, and general health, and scanned for tags. Since 1991, PIT tags have been used exclusively for marking, replacing earlier Carlin tags (1982-1983) and Floy anchor tags (used extensively until 1990). All data are maintained in a comprehensive tagging database by [[Arizona State University]] under auspices of the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG), representing multiple agencies including [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]], [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[U.S. National Park Service]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] that provides critical habitat for the largest remaining population of razorback suckers. However, the ecosystem has been fundamentally altered by the presence of non-native predatory fishes that prevent natural recruitment by consuming early life stages. The adult population is aging without replacement, creating an urgent conservation crisis. The lake was subdivided into six geographic areas for movement analysis: River (river miles 43-63), Uplake (26-43), [[Arizona Bay]] (24-26), Nevada Side (13-22), Arizona Side (13-22), and Downlake (0-13).

The repatriation program addresses this recruitment failure through a multi-stage process: (1) winter-spring (January-April) harvest of naturally-produced larvae from wild adults, (2) transfer to protected environments ([[Boulder City]] facilities or [[Willow Beach]] hatchery) for initial feeding and growth beyond the critical post-yolk sac period, (3) stocking of small juveniles into off-site and near-site rearing locations including natural and semi-natural backwaters adjacent to Lake Mohave, artificial ponds (Boulder City Golf Course, Phoenix Zoo, Colorado River Indian Tribes facilities), and federal/state hatcheries, and (4) harvest, PIT tagging, and release into the lake when juveniles reach the target size of 30 cm TL (typically late summer-autumn of age 0). This approach was selected as the most practical method to preserve the exceptional genetic diversity of the adult population.

## Key Findings

**Adult Population Monitoring (Wild Fish):**
- Total captures: 1,883 razorback suckers during the 1995-96 season
- Adult recaptures: 479 individuals (26.2% of 1,828 wild fish captured; 25.4% of total captures)
- Recaptures represented all five years since PIT tagging initiation in 1991
- Nearly 30 recaptured fish had been at large at least 60 months (5 years)
- Three fish retained capture histories dating to 1990 (originally Floy tagged), representing 72 months (6 years) between initial capture and last recapture
- One fish was captured on four separate occasions (maximum observed)
- 26 fish had been captured three times (two recaptures)

**Growth and Measurement:**
Total length changes between capture and recapture were not useful for assessing individual adult growth, as changes were indistinguishable from measurement error (±2.5% of TL). For example, one female grew only 1.5 cm over 6 years (61.5 to 63.0 cm TL, approximately 2.5% increase), and one male showed 2.2 cm growth over 6 years (54.0 to 56.2 cm TL, approximately 3.9% increase).

**Movement Patterns (Wild Adults):**
- 47% of recaptures occurred within the same geographic area (within-area movement)
- 33% moved between adjacent areas (excluding Arizona-Nevada side movements due to open water separation)
- 80% of all recaptures were within or close to the area of initial capture
- [[Nevada Side]] accounted for 57% of movement records, [[Arizona Bay]] for 53%
- Nevada Side and Arizona Bay combined accounted for 60% of total records
- Movement probability decreased with distance to succeeding areas
- 89% of records accounted for when movements among Nevada Side, Arizona Bay, and other areas are included

**Repatriation Program Results:**
- Total repatriated fish recaptures: 55 individuals (10.3% of recaptures; 2.9% of total captures)
- Source locations: [[Yuma Cove]] (n=33), [[Boulder City Municipal Golf Course]] pond (n=10), [[North Chemehueve]] (n=5), [[Arizona Juvenile]] (n=4), [[Dandy Cove]] (n=2), [[Nevada Larvae]] (n=2)
- Stocking period: November 1992 to November 1995
- Recapture locations: widely distributed between Nine-mile and Black Butte, with most from adult spawning areas in Arizona Bay

**Time at Large and Growth (Repatriated Fish):**
- Longest time at large: 3.3 years (40 months) for four individuals released at Yuma Cove in November 1992 and recaptured March 1996
- These four fish averaged 35.4 cm TL at stocking and 52.7 cm at recapture (17.3 cm total growth, approximately 5.2 cm per year)
- Greatest length increase: 41.5 cm (from 23.0 cm at stocking in October 1994 to 64.5 cm in February 1996)
- Largest recaptured repatriate: 64.6 cm female (stocked at 37.7 cm in July 1993)
- Smallest recaptured individual: 20.5 cm at stocking (September 1994), 42.5 cm when recaptured 14 months later
- Two fish had multiple recapture histories, demonstrating successful integration into the population

**Multiple Recapture Examples:**
1. Fish stocked January 13, 1993 from [[Davis Cove]] (28.3 cm, 292 g): captured 2 months later (28.4 cm, 314 g), recaptured 36 months after first recapture (52.0 cm, 1600 g, male), total time at large 38 months
2. Fish reared in Arizona Juvenile, stocked August 18, 1993 (36.0 cm, 504 g): recaptured 7 months later (36.8 cm), recaptured again 31 months at large (49.5 cm, 1380 g, male)

**Population Estimates and Survival:**
- Using the adult population estimate of 25,000 wild razorback suckers and recapture ratios, estimated number of repatriated fish in population: approximately 2,575 individuals
- Total cove-reared fish stocked (October 14, 1992 to December 21, 1995): 4,580 individuals
- Estimated survivorship of repatriated juveniles: 56.2% (2,575/4,580)
- Approximately 64% of surviving repatriated fish in 1996 attributed to juveniles released during 1995
- Additional 252 fish stocked through September 12, 1996 (no recaptures recorded from these most recent stockings)

**Predictive Factors:**
Analysis attempted to relate recapture success with two variables: (1) size at stocking (total length) and (2) total number stocked. Neither factor proved to be a strong predictor:
- Mean total length at stocking vs. percent recaptures: r² = 0.1297 (poor relationship, unexpected given previous studies showing clear relationship between size and predation susceptibility)
- Number stocked vs. success: r² = 0.4881 (approximately 50% of variation explained, unsatisfactory result)
- Further analyses with larger datasets and exploration of other variables, combinations, or transformations indicated

**Program Assessment:**
The report concludes that while much remains to be accomplished before the goal of replacing the remnant population with juveniles representing the genetic diversity of the adult stock can be realized, results to date are encouraging and demonstrate significant progress. The findings fully support continuation of the repatriation program. The Native Fish Work Group goal is to repatriate 50,000 razorback suckers to Lake Mohave to replace the aging adult stock, which is expected to effectively disappear within the next decade without intervention.
