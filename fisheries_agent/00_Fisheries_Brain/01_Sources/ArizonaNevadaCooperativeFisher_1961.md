# Arizona-Nevada Cooperative Fisheries Survey, 1961 Report

## Executive Summary
This report summarizes the 1961 cooperative creel census and stocking program conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[Nevada Fish and Game Department]] across the [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], and Colorado River (Below [[Davis Dam]]) fisheries. The study tracked warm water fisheries (dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Black Crappie]]) and cold water fisheries (dominated by hatchery-stocked [[Rainbow Trout]]). A significant focus was placed on a cooperative study of [[Threadfin Shad]] spawning biology in Lake Mohave, correlating spawning events with specific water temperature thresholds. Data indicates a shift in stocking strategy—moving from concentrated "put and take" planting to distributed stocking—which successfully increased average fish size ("lunkers") and poundage returns, despite a slight decrease in the overall catch rate (fish per man-hour).

## Background & Description
The report provides limited specific morphological data, focusing primarily on stocking sizes and catch metrics rather than detailed anatomical descriptions.
* **[[Rainbow Trout]]:** Stocked at variable lengths depending on location and management goals, including 4-inch, 6-inch, 8-inch, 9-inch, and 10-inch specimens.
    * *Marking:* 4-6 inch fish were fin-clipped; 8-10 inch fish were jaw-tagged to track migration and returns.
* **[[Largemouth Bass]]:** The average length recorded in the [[Lake Mead]] creel census was **14.24 inches**.
* **[[Threadfin Shad]]:** Life history stages identified included mature (spawning) adults and newly hatched larval shad, though specific length/weight morphometrics were not detailed in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
* **Cold Water Fishery ([[Willow Beach]] & [[Davis Dam]]):** [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Cutthroat Trout]] were stocked in the tailwater fishery below [[Davis Dam]] and in the upper reaches of [[Lake Mohave]]. Distributing stocking locations away from the base of the dam (a shift from "put and take" management) resulted in fish surviving longer, growing larger, and contributing more significantly to angler poundage, despite a drop in the immediate catch rate (Fish/man-hour fell from 0.57 in 1960 to 0.41 in 1961).
* **Warm Water Fishery ([[Lake Mead]] & [[Lake Mohave]]):** Dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], and [[Channel Catfish]]. [[Lake Mead]] serves as the primary reservoir habitat for these species, while [[Lake Mohave]] showed an ecological shift toward trout dominance in the angler harvest.
* **[[Threadfin Shad]] Spawning Ecology:** Spawning in [[Lake Mohave]] (specifically the Katherine Wash area) was triggered when water temperatures reached **71°F**, occurring between June 6–12, 1961. Larval shad were found distributed widely in the [[Eldorado Canyon]] area in water temperatures ranging from **68°F to 75°F**. Crucially, larvae were **not** found in the colder water mass above the thermal front, suggesting temperature acts as a limiting barrier for shad distribution and larval dispersal in the reservoir.

## Key Findings
1. **Stocking Strategy Shift:** Nevada Fish and Game changed rainbow trout stocking locations to [[Eldorado Canyon]] (from [[Willow Beach]]), resulting in "lunker" trout appearing in the creel and improved poundage return compared to the rapid, small-fish turnover of previous concentrated plants.
2. **Species Composition Shift in Lake Mohave:** The angler harvest composition in [[Lake Mohave]] shifted dramatically from 1959 to 1961. [[Largemouth Bass]] dropped from 46% to 11% of the catch, while [[Rainbow Trout]] rose from 47% to 87% (Note: 1961 data affected by changes in census scheduling).
3. **Lake Mead Trends:** [[Black Crappie]] catches declined in 1961 relative to previous years, while [[Largemouth Bass]] continued to dominate the harvest (80% of total catch), with [[Channel Catfish]] remaining stable at 10%.
4. **Angler Demographics:** Residency data indicates that [[Lake Mohave]] and the area [[Below Davis Dam]] attracted a significantly higher percentage of "Other States" anglers (up to 81.78% at Katherine Wash) compared to [[Lake Mead]], suggesting Mohave/Davis Dam was a more significant tourist/out-of-state destination fishery.
5. **Total Stocking Volume:** In 1961, a combined total of **223,096** trout (Rainbow and Cutthroat) weighing 35,592 lbs were stocked in Lake Mohave, and **102,083** trout weighing 18,792 lbs were stocked below Davis Dam, involving cooperative efforts between Arizona Game & Fish, Nevada Fish & Game, and the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] (BSFW).
