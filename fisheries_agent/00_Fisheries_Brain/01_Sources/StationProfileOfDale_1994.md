# Station Profile: Dale Hollow National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
Dale Hollow National Fish Hatchery (NFH) is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Celina, Tennessee, established under the [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] of 1934 (48 Stat. 401). Construction began in 1964, with the station becoming fully operational in 1966. The hatchery's core mission is to produce and distribute [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], and [[Lake Trout]] to mitigate the ecological impacts of Federal water development projects and to support fisheries management on Federal lands across Tennessee, Alabama, and Georgia. A significant infrastructure expansion (approximately 34 additional raceways) was formalized in 1990 through a cooperative agreement between the Fish and Wildlife Service, the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]], and the [[Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Dale Hollow NFH functions as an artificial propagation node supporting cold-water [[Salmonidae]] populations in the southeastern United States, specifically addressing habitat loss from Federal dam and reservoir construction.

- **Primary Function:** Mitigation stocking to offset the loss of natural riverine spawning/rearing habitat caused by Federal water development projects (e.g., dam construction impacting the [[Cumberland River]] system).
- **Distribution Range (Stocking):**
  - **Tennessee:** Primary distribution for Federal lands management.
  - **Alabama:** Rainbow trout stocked in Federal waters.
  - **Georgia & Arkansas:** Recipient of fingerling rainbow trout for further growout at other National Fish Hatcheries.
- **Habitat Type:** The hatchery itself utilizes a raceway system fed by a water source suitable for cold-water salmonid rearing, characteristic of the [[Dale Hollow Reservoir]] tailwater region, an area renowned for trophy trout fisheries.
- **Trophic Role:** As stocked fish, these salmonids introduce a managed apex/mid-level predator into recipient tailwater and reservoir ecosystems, targeting recreational fisheries.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Basis:** The hatchery operates under the authority of the 1934 [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]], linking its mission directly to mitigating impacts of Federal water infrastructure.
- **Infrastructure Expansion:** A 1990 Statement of Cooperation between the FWS, [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]], and [[Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency]] led to the planned addition of ~34 raceways, significantly increasing rearing capacity.
- **Production Trends:** Data indicates a decrease in the *number* of fish produced from 1990 (1,864,113) to 1991/1992 (~1.3 million), while the *total weight* of production remained relatively stable or slightly increased (214,124 → 216,971), suggesting a shift toward stocking fewer, larger (higher weight-per-fish) fingerlings/yearlings rather than a greater quantity of smaller fry.
- **Multi-State Role:** The hatchery serves a broader regional network, supplying fingerling rainbow trout to sister National Fish Hatcheries in Georgia and Arkansas in addition to its direct stocking duties in Tennessee and Alabama.
- **Staffing Growth:** Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staffing increased from 6.0 in 1991 to an estimated 10.0 in 1994, corresponding with increased operational funding to support the raceway expansion project.
- **Facilities:** The station includes basic support infrastructure — 2 residences, an equipment house, an oil house, an aeration house, and a combined office/tank room/visitor center/aquarium — indicating both operational and public outreach functions.
