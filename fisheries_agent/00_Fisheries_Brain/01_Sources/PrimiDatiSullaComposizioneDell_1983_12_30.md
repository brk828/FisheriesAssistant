## Executive Summary
This study by Pesce & Maggi (1980, published 1983) presents the first data on the composition of phreatic (groundwater) biocoenoses of [[Sardinia]], based on a survey of 51 collection stations comprising artificial freshwater/brackish wells in inland karst areas, coastal sandy-arenaceous zones, hyporheic habitats, and one spring. The work is not fish-focused but centers on **stygofauna** — obligate and facultative groundwater invertebrates — including cyclopid and harpacticoid copepods, asellid and stenasellid isopods, microparasellid isopods, amphipods, syncarids, water mites, ostracods, and gastropods. The authors document a marked ecological and biogeographic contrast between inland karstic phreatic waters and coastal interstitial systems, and discuss a "regression model" of marine-to-freshwater faunal colonization along with preliminary biogeographic patterns across northern vs. southern Sardinia.

## Background & Description
While the paper is taxonomic/faunistic rather than morphometric, it identifies numerous specialized subterranean lineages with adaptive traits typical of stygobiont life (depigmentation, reduced eyes, elongation — implied by taxonomic identity rather than described in detail here):

- **Copepoda (Cyclopidae):** *Diacyclops languidoides zschokkei*, *Diacyclops languidoides hypnicola*, *Megacyclops brachypus*, *Thermocyclops dybowskii*
- **Copepoda (Harpacticoidea):** *Nitocrella stammeri*, *Nitocrella cf. subterranea*, *Parapseudoleptomesochra minoricae*, *Attheyella crassa*, *Elaphoidella* sp., *Ceuthonectes* sp.
- **Isopoda (Asellidae):** *Proasellus coxalis* and multiple *Proasellus* populations showing varying degrees of interstitial/phreatic adaptation
- **Isopoda (Stenasellidae):** *Stenasellus assorgiai*, *Stenasellus nuragicus*, *Stenasellus* n.sp. (undescribed, new to science)
- **Isopoda (Microparasellidae):** *Microcharon marinus*, *Microcharon cf. boui*, *Microcharon* sp. (undescribed, new to science)
- **Amphipoda:** *Bogidiella vandeli*, *Bogidiella silverii* (new species), *Salentinella angelieri*, *Niphargus longicaudatus*
- **Syncarida:** *Sardobathynella cf. cottarellii*
- **Other groups:** Hydracarina (Trombidiidae, Hydrachnellae), Ostracoda, Gastropoda

Three taxa are formally reported as **new to science** and currently endemic to Sardinia: *Bogidiella silverii*, an undescribed *Microcharon* sp., and an undescribed *Stenasellus* sp.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study distinguishes two principal groundwater habitat types with contrasting physicochemical and faunal profiles:

**Inland karstic phreatic stations:**
- Depth: 6.5–23.5 m; water column 0.5–2.0 m
- Temperature: stable, 16.9–17.4 °C
- pH: ~6.9–7.1
- Salinity: <0.5‰
- Substrate: very fine sands or clayey sands
- Fauna: low diversity, low abundance, dominated almost exclusively by true [[Stygobiont]] and eustygophile (obligate/near-obligate groundwater) species

**Coastal sandy-arenaceous stations:**
- Depth: shallower, 1.5–5.5 m; water column 0.5–3.8 m
- Temperature: warmer and more variable, 17.5–19.6 °C
- pH: 6.8–7.2
- Salinity: weakly brackish, 1.9–2.5‰
- Substrate: medium-to-coarse sands, often organogenic (foraminifera, ostracods, bivalves, gastropods, echinoids)
- Fauna: richer and more diversified overall, but with few true stygobionts and a high proportion of [[Stygophile]] and stygoxene (epigean-derived) elements

A small number of **hyporheic stations** (rivers Liscia, Palasole, Posada) hosted distinct, small-bodied stygobiont assemblages dominated by cyclopid and harpacticoid copepods.

The authors propose that ecological instability, excess trophic input, and surface-derived pollution in coastal systems drive the rarefaction of strict stygobionts there — a hypothesis previously advanced by Ronneberger (1975) and supported by Danielopol (1981) for interstitial faunas of Euboea, Greece, and corroborated by the authors' own unpublished data from Greece and Turkey.

## Key Findings
- **51 stations** sampled across Sardinia: mostly artificial wells (freshwater inland, weakly brackish coastal), plus hyporheic sites and one spring.
- Clear **ecological dichotomy** between species-poor but stygobiont-dominated inland phreatic waters and species-rich but epigean/stygophile-dominated coastal interstitial waters.
- Several taxa (*Megacyclops brachypus*, the *Diacyclops languidoides* group, *Nitocrella stammeri*, *Parapseudoleptomesochra minoricae*, *Microcharon cf. boui*, genus *Ceuthonectes*) are recorded for the **first time from Sardinian groundwater**, though previously known from the island's cave/hyporheic environments.
- Evidence supports the **"Regression Model"** (Stock, 1977, 1980) whereby thalassoid (marine-derived) groups — Thermosbaenacea, Microparasellidae isopods, some Cirolanidae, Mysidacea, Atyidae — became trapped in littoral interstitial habitats during Tertiary marine regressions and subsequently adapted to continental/mesohaline conditions, explaining current distributions coinciding with once-submerged areas.
- **Biogeographic test:** distribution patterns partly support the traditional hypothesis of faunal differentiation between northern/central and south-western Sardinia (e.g., *Bogidiella*, *Niphargus*, *Stenasellus*, *Diacyclops languidoides* complex, *Parapseudoleptomesochra minoricae*, *Ceuthonectes* show localized/restricted distributions consistent with this model).
- Conversely, *Microcharon marinus*, *Salentinella angelieri*, *Nitocrella stammeri*, and *Attheyella crassa* are **homogeneously distributed** across the island, interpreted as more recent colonizers whose spread does not yet reflect deep vicariance patterns.
- The findings are explicitly preliminary; further sampling and taxonomic work on collected but unstudied material are expected to refine the biogeographic and ecological hypotheses presented.
