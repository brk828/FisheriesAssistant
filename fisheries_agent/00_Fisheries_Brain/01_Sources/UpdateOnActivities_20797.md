# American Rivers Board Update on Conservation and Organizational Activities (May 1999)

#river-conservation #salmon-recovery #dam-removal #habitat-restoration #water-policy

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from [[American Rivers]] Executive Director Rebecca R. Wodder to the Board of Directors provides a comprehensive update on organizational activities as of May 24, 1999. The document covers major conservation initiatives focused on river restoration, salmon recovery, dam removal campaigns, and water policy reform across multiple river systems in the United States. Key conservation efforts include legal challenges to hydropower licensing, advocacy for [[Snake River]] dam removal to restore salmon populations, water metering enforcement in [[Washington State]], and protection of the [[Hanford Reach]] on the [[Columbia River]]. The organization also reports significant development achievements including major donor gifts, foundation grants, and successful media outreach for the 14th annual Most Endangered Rivers report.

## Background & Description

[[American Rivers]] is a national river conservation organization working on multiple fronts to protect and restore river ecosystems across the United States. The organization operates through regional offices (including a Northwest Office) and employs legal, scientific, and advocacy strategies to address threats to river health. The memorandum documents activities spanning several major river systems:

The [[North Fork Skokomish River]] in [[Washington State]] is the site of the Cushman hydroelectric project, where inadequate flows and lack of fish passage have decimated native [[Salmon]] and [[Steelhead]] runs. [[American Rivers]] filed an appeal before the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals challenging FERC's new license for this project, with pro bono representation from Wilmer, Cutler and Pickering law firm.

The [[Snake River]] was designated as the #1 Most Endangered River in the 1999 report. The organization is leading efforts through the Save our Wild Salmon coalition (52 groups) to advocate for partial removal of four dams on the Lower Snake River. Rob Masonis authored the publication "Restoring Salmon by Restoring Rivers: The Case for Partially Removing Four Dams on the Lower Snake River."

The [[Missouri River]] is the focus of the "Rivers of Lewis and Clark" campaign, with field operations based in [[Montana]]. Mark Albers was hired as Field Representative to work with the [[Bureau of Land Management]] on grazing practices along Wild and Scenic segments and with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] on dam operations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses multiple ecological threats to river systems and anadromous fish populations:

**Salmon and Steelhead Decline**: Recent listings of [[Puget Sound]] chinook and other anadromous fish in metropolitan areas prompted the Salmon Collaboration (led by Bill Ruckelshaus) to recommend early action. Katherine Ransel successfully included instream flow recommendations requiring that no more water be withdrawn from troubled salmon streams until instream flow standards are set and met.

**Water Withdrawal and Agricultural Impacts**: The [[Yakima River Basin]] has the highest farmgate values in the nation after California's Central Valley. Katherine Ransel convinced irrigation interests to recommend universal surface and groundwater metering to the Secretary of Interior and State of Washington, along with strong enforcement programs including stream masters and strategic penalties.

**Polluted Runoff**: [[American Rivers]] worked with the Resource Studies Center to develop a 10-year, $1.1 billion plan called the Mississippi River Stewardship Initiative to reduce polluted runoff into the [[Upper Mississippi River]]. The plan was developed by farmers, bargers, and environmentalists and includes increased federal funding for technical and financial assistance to landowners, water quality monitoring networks, improved interagency coordination, and expanded outreach.

**Habitat Protection**: The [[Hanford Reach]] encompasses 90,000 acres on the North Slope. Secretary of Energy Richardson announced consolidation of these lands under [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] management as a national wildlife refuge, following advocacy by [[American Rivers]], [[The Nature Conservancy]], and the Lower Columbia Audubon Society.

## Key Findings

**Legal and Policy Actions**:
- Filed Ninth Circuit appeal challenging FERC license for Cushman hydroelectric project due to inadequate flows and lack of fish passage
- Filed lawsuit against [[Washington State]] on March 18, 1999 for failing to implement mandatory water metering laws
- Secured recommendations for universal surface and groundwater metering in [[Yakima River Basin]] with enforcement programs

**Dam Removal and Hydropower Reform**:
- Published comprehensive case for removing four [[Snake River]] dams to restore salmon populations
- Contracted former Army Corps economist to develop economic mitigation plan for Snake River navigation, with completion anticipated by end of May 1999
- Hired Steve Brooke, Maine-based dam removal expert, to direct "Rivers Unplugged" campaign
- Received $35,000 grant from Town Creek Foundation to launch dam removal campaign
- Secured $350,000 two-year grant from Charles Stewart Mott Foundation for Hydropower Reform Coalition work in Great Lakes and Southeast

**Habitat Protection and Restoration**:
- Secured 90,000-acre [[Hanford Reach]] protection as national wildlife refuge under [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] management
- Developed $1.1 billion Mississippi River Stewardship Initiative to reduce polluted runoff over 10 years
- Established instream flow protections for [[Puget Sound]] salmon streams preventing further water withdrawals until standards are met

**Organizational Development**:
- Brett Swift permanently joined Northwest Office staff (effective July 1) after six years as Assistant Director of Water Policy Project at Lewis & Clark School of Law
- Mark Albers hired as Montana Field Representative for "Rivers of Lewis and Clark" Campaign, based in Helena
- Received second $100,000 gift in FY1999 from Ted and Jennifer Stanley (lifetime giving: $510,000)
- Exceeded $600,000 in major gifts from individuals by mid-May with adjusted goal of $690,000
- Received $850,000 challenge grant proposal submitted to Richard King Mellon Foundation for Resource Center
- Received $75,000 three-year challenge grant from Charles Engelhard Foundation for Montana field work

**Media and Public Outreach**:
- Released 14th annual America's Most Endangered Rivers report on April 12, 1999 with national coverage in The New York Times, USA Today, The Economist, ABC News, CNN, and National Public Radio
- Press conference broadcast live on C-Span for first time
- Tripled web traffic in April 1999 with significant increase in online memberships
- Placed op-ed "Setting Our Rivers Free" in The New York Times (May 8) on dam removal
- Developing traveling exhibit with [[National Geographic]] on [[Missouri River]] covering Lewis and Clark story, dam impacts, and restoration campaign
- Producing History Channel documentary on Missouri River restoration with teacher's guide for 80,000 teachers nationwide
- Member/supporter count as of April 30: 23,797

**Partnerships and Collaborations**:
- Co-founded Save our Wild Salmon coalition (52 groups)
- Partnered with Resource Studies Center on Mississippi River planning
- Working with [[National Geographic]] on Missouri River exhibit
- Coordinating with History Channel on documentary and educational materials
- White House appointed Owen Dutt as river navigator for [[Upper Mississippi River]] (designated American Heritage River in August 1998)
