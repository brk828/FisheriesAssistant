# Distribution of Macrofauna and Fish in the Lucina and Moravka Streams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1950s hydrobiological study by Milan Straškraba (Hydrobiological Laboratory, Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences, Prague) compares the bottom macrofauna, fish and fish food of two Silesian streams, the [[Lucina Stream]] and the [[Moravka Stream]], which are tributaries of the [[Odra River]]. Sampling took place in July 1952 at 11 stations on the Lucina and 8 on the Moravka. The author found that breakage lines in invertebrate composition coincided with breakage lines in fish zones and with hydrographic zones. Because the physical gradients change gradually while the faunal changes are abrupt, the author proposes that biotic interactions, particularly predation by [[Brown Trout]] on [[Rivulogammarus Fossarum]], drive the sharpest faunal transitions.

## Background & Description

- **Study design:** Sampling covered stations spaced roughly 2.5 km apart, each representing 500–800 m of stream. Macrofauna was collected qualitatively by hand-picking, disturbing the bottom and lifting stones over a wire net, with about 5 persons working 2.5 hours per station. Fish were collected by [[Electrofishing]] (electric shock apparatus). Temperature, current velocity, pH, alkalinity and oxygen were also measured. Main sampling ran 18–25 July 1952, with supplementary observations on 29 June–6 July 1953.
- **Streams:** The Lucina is 32 km long and changes from montane to lowland character. The Moravka is 27 km long and remains montane to submontane. Both arise from the northern slopes of the Silesian Beskydy Mountains in tertiary Godula sandstone, which accounts for their low calcium content.
- **Reaches:** Based on slope, substrate, current speed and maximum temperature, the author divided both streams into five comparable reaches: source, headstream, mountain zone, submountain zone and lowland zone (Table 3). The Lucina's lowland zone (18 km, stations 8–11) is absent from the Moravka.
- **Scope:** The paper summarises results from a larger unpublished Czech report (559 pp.) and a series of separate papers. The macrofauna tables (Tables 4–5) list 61 taxa, including planarians, amphipods, isopods, molluscs, mayflies, stoneflies, caddisflies, beetles, dragonflies and chironomids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water chemistry.** Source waters were very soft. In the main Lucina source, bicarbonate hardness was 0.5 German degrees, total hardness 1.5 degrees, Ca 5.0 mg/l and Mg 3.5 mg/l. In an adjoining source, bicarbonate hardness was 0.8 degrees and Ca 6.5 mg/l. [[Dugesia Gonocephala]] was the only planarian found, and the author links the absence of [[Crenobia Alpina]] to these low hardness values, since that species was never recorded below 0.5 degrees bicarbonate hardness.

**Zonation of macrofauna.** The author quantified faunal breaks using "changes" (taxa starting, ending or changing abundance between adjacent stations) and "transits" (taxa persisting without considerable change). Their ratio indicates where the largest faunal shifts occur. The method is similar to the "faunistical analysis" of Ilies (1958) on the River Fulda.

- **Lucina:** Five zones were identified: (1) station 1, (2) station 2, (3) stations 3–4, (4) stations 5–8, and (5) stations 9–11. Changes peaked between stations 1–2 (20 changes, ratio 20.0), 2–3 (17 changes, ratio 3.6) and 8–9 (13 changes).
- **Moravka:** Four zones were identified: (1) station 1, (2) station 2, (3) stations 3–4, and (4) stations 5–8, with station 8 slightly distinct. Sharp breaks occurred at 1–2 (19 changes, ratio 9.5) and 2–3 (18 changes, ratio 1.5).
- **Coefficients of difference:** For the Lucina, zones 1 and 2 scored 10.0, zones 2 and 3 scored 3.8, zones 3 and 4 scored 0.7 and zones 4 and 5 scored 0.9. For the Moravka, zones 1 and 2 scored 19.0, zones 2 and 3 scored 3.1 and zones 3 and 4 scored 0.7. Lower reaches are therefore more uniform than upper ones.

**Fish zonation.** A total of 20 fish species were caught across both streams. The Lucina held 17 species, while the Moravka held 9, of which 5 were abundant. Neither Cottus gobio nor Cottus poecilopus occurred in the Lucina, although both are present in neighbouring rivers. The author could not explain this absence.

- **Lucina fish zones (after Balon, 1954):** (1) stations 1–2, no fish; (2) stations 3–4, with brown trout, stone loach and minnow; (3) stations 5–8, adding gudgeon and chub; (4) stations 9–11, with barbel, nase, bleak, pike, roach, dace and perch.
- **Moravka fish zones:** (1) stations 1–2 (upper part), no fish; (2) station 2 (lower part) to station 4, with brown trout, carpathian sculpin and minnow; (3) stations 5–8, adding common sculpin and stone loach.

**Amphipod distribution and fish predation.** [[Rivulogammarus Fossarum]] is absent from the sources, where [[Niphargus Tatrensis]] occurs instead. It begins occurring less than 100 m below the source and peaks about 10 m downstream. In the Moravka, its abundance drops sharply at a high weir near station 2 (Heřmanice), where brown trout become common. Stomach analysis of 28 brown trout from that station found 347 Rivulogammarus remnants, making it the dominant food item. Egg counts per female were highest at the station just above the sharp drop, which the author takes to indicate optimal conditions there.

**Comparative evidence.** The author cites other studies in which fish predation limited gammarids. Pentland (1930) reported trout controlling Gammarus distribution in North America. Uéno (1934) and Hutchinson found Gammarus lacustris abundant in Himalayan lakes lacking fish. Decksbach (1952) observed similar pelagic Gammarus populations in Ural lakes that declined after fish introduction. Janković-skaja's samples from Issykskoje Lake (1788 m a.s.l.) and Son-Köl lake also contained no fish.

## Key Findings

1. **Coinciding breakage lines:** Breaks in hydrographic conditions, macrofauna and fish zones occurred at nearly the same positions in both streams (Fig. 3).
2. **Abrupt faunal change versus gradual physical change:** Invertebrate composition changed far more suddenly than physical conditions such as slope, substrate and temperature. The author attributes this discrepancy to biotic interactions.
3. **Predation as a driver:** The sharp decline of Rivulogammarus at the trout zone boundary, together with stomach data and egg counts, supports the hypothesis that fish feeding restructures amphipod populations.
4. **Fish zone correspondence:** The fish zones match the classical lower trout zone (stations 2–8) and a minnow zone (Lucina stations 3–4 and 5–8), with the upper trout zone barely present (Lucina station 3).
5. **Cross-taxa comparison:** The fish zone pattern agrees with the biocoenotic classification of Illies (1961), which links fish zones to epirhithron, metarhithron and hyporhithron.
6. **Geographic and chemical limits:** The absence of [[Crenobia Alpina]] is attributed to low bicarbonate hardness (below 0.5 °dH), and the absence of [[Polycelis Felina]] is attributed to historic biogeographic barriers.
7. **Future work:** The author describes these results as a step toward explaining zonation through interspecific interactions. The original report and the separately published papers contain the full data.
