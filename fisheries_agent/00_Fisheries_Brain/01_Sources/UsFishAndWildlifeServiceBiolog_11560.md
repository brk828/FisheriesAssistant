# USFWS Biological Opinion on 1987 Rangeland Grasshopper and Mormon Cricket Control Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #pesticide-toxicology #wildlife-conservation #aquatic-ecology

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service biological opinion evaluates the Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS) 1987 rangeland grasshopper and Mormon cricket cooperative management program across 17 western states. The consultation addresses potential impacts of three insecticides—carbaryl, malathion, and acephate—on 73 listed endangered and threatened species including mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, and plants. The Service concludes that malathion poses unacceptable risks to most listed fish species and several terrestrial species due to its high aquatic toxicity (LC50 30 micrograms/liter for [[Bluegill]]). Aerial ultra-low-volume (ULV) application threatens insect-pollinated plant species through pollinator loss. The opinion mandates buffer zones (1/4 mile to 3 miles depending on species and chemical), monitoring requirements, and reasonable and prudent alternatives to avoid jeopardy determinations for 32 species.

## Background & Description

APHIS conducts grasshopper and Mormon cricket control when populations exceed 10 individuals per square meter (8 per square yard), deemed "economic" thresholds requiring treatment. The program employs three primary insecticides: carbaryl (Sevin 4 oil and bait formulations), malathion (91-95% concentrate), and acephate (Orthene 75 soluble powder), applied primarily via aerial ULV spraying. Biological control using *Nosema locustae* (a microsporidian protozoan) is also authorized. Application rates are carbaryl at 8 fluid ounces per acre, malathion at 8 fluid ounces per acre, and acephate at 4 ounces per acre. The biological assessment prepared by APHIS initially addressed 58 species but omitted several listed species including [[New Mexican Ridge-Nosed Rattlesnake]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Pecos Bluntnose Shiner]], [[Sneed Pincushion Cactus]], and [[McKittrick Pennyroyal]].

All three chemicals are cholinesterase inhibitors affecting nerve synapse function. Carbaryl demonstrates low to moderate mammalian toxicity (LD50 150-710 mg/kg), low avian toxicity (LD50 780-2,290 mg/kg), moderate fish toxicity (LC50 0.05-5.3 ppm), and extreme toxicity to aquatic invertebrates (LC50 0.0028-0.0080 ppm). Malathion shows relatively low mammalian (LD50 53-775 mg/kg) and avian toxicity (LD50 150-403 mg/kg) but moderate to high fish toxicity (LC50 0.030-0.070 ppm) and high aquatic invertebrate toxicity (LC50 0.0008-0.001 ppm). Acephate exhibits low to moderate mammalian toxicity (LD50 150-530 mg/kg) but degrades to methamidophos, which is 40-70 times more toxic (LD50 7.5-14 mg/kg for mammals).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project area encompasses 17 western states including portions of Alaska, covering diverse habitats from arid rangelands to riparian corridors. Listed fish species occupy highly restricted ranges: [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] now occupies only 0.3% of historic lake habitat and less than 10% of historic stream habitat in the Lahontan Basin. [[Pecos Bluntnose Shiner]] occurs sporadically in only 175 miles of the Pecos River mainstream, representing severe range contraction. [[Loach Minnow]] presently occupies an estimated 236 linear miles, only 15% of historic range in the Gila River system.

The Service establishes risk thresholds at 1/10 LD50 for mammals and birds and 1/20 LC50 for aquatic organisms when evaluating endangered species impacts—more conservative than EPA's standard 1/5 LD50 criterion. Using the most sensitive reference species, expected environmental concentrations in streams are 13.8 micrograms/liter for carbaryl, 0.0026 milligrams/liter for acephate, and 16.0 micrograms/liter for malathion. The malathion concentration exceeds the 1/20 LC50 reference value of 1.5 micrograms/liter by more than 10-fold, indicating unacceptable risk even with buffer zones.

Spray drift modeling indicates pesticides can reach water bodies despite buffer zones. Empirical models show drift is not completely eliminated at 500 feet (1/4 mile). Wild bees have effective foraging ranges up to 3 miles from nest areas, and bee mortality has been documented 5 miles from spray zones. Recovery of wild insect pollinators with low fecundity rates may require up to 4 years post-spraying.

## Key Findings

**Jeopardy Determinations (32 species):** The Service concludes the proposed action is likely to jeopardize: [[Utah Prairie Dog]] (malathion and acephate exposure through cicada consumption and plant contamination); [[Interior Least Tern]] and [[Piping Plover]] (food base reduction, behavioral effects from cholinesterase inhibition during critical nesting period May-August); [[Desert Tortoise]] (malathion and acephate/methamidophos exposure through contaminated vegetation); [[Wyoming Toad]] (all three pesticides threaten aquatic larvae and terrestrial adults feeding on arthropods).

**Fish Species (21 jeopardy determinations for malathion):** [[Borax Lake Chub]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Hutton Tui Chub]], [[Parhranagat Roundtail Chub]], [[Desert Dace]], [[Foskett Speckled Dace]], [[Kendall Warm Springs Dace]], [[Pecos Gambusia]], [[Pahrump Killifish]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Pecos Bluntnose Shiner]], [[Spikedace]], [[Big Springs Spinedace]], [[White River Spinedace]], [[Hiko White River Springfish]], [[White River Springfish]], [[June Sucker]], [[Modoc Sucker]], [[Warner Sucker]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Apache Trout]], and [[Woundfin]]. The 1-mile malathion buffer is deemed insufficient for these species due to extreme aquatic toxicity and restricted habitat ranges.

**No-Jeopardy Determinations (fish with adequate buffers):** [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] are not likely to be jeopardized with 1/4-mile buffers and monitoring, given their occurrence in large water bodies where dilution reduces pesticide concentrations.

**Plant Species (18 jeopardy determinations):** Nine cactus species including [[Sneed Pincushion Cactus]], [[Lee Pincushion Cactus]], [[Purple-Spined Hedgehog Cactus]], [[Kuenzler Hedgehog Cactus]], [[Knowlton Cactus]], [[Peebles Navajo Cactus]], [[Siler Pincushion Cactus]], [[Uinta Basin Hookless Cactus]], and [[Mesa Verde Cactus]] face jeopardy through loss of halicitid bee pollinators. Additional species threatened include [[Jones Cycladenia]], [[Rhizome Fleabane]], [[Malheur Wire-Lettuce]], [[Mancos Milk-Vetch]], [[Clay Phacelia]], [[McKittrick Pennyroyal]], [[Dwarf Bear-Poppy]], [[Gypsum Wild-Buckwheat]], and [[Arizona Cliffrose]]. The 1/4-mile buffer is inadequate given 3-mile bee foraging ranges.

**Reasonable and Prudent Alternatives:** (1) Malathion and acephate prohibited within 1/4 mile of [[Utah Prairie Dog]] towns in Iron and Millard Counties, Utah. (2) Piping plover: 1/4-mile no-ULV-spray buffer around nesting water bodies; 500-foot no-treatment zone for carbaryl bait or Nosema. (3) Interior least tern: 2.5-mile upstream/downstream no-aerial-ULV zone plus 1/4-mile lateral buffer; 500-foot no-treatment zone around colonies in Kansas, Nebraska, New Mexico, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, and Texas. (4) Desert tortoise: no malathion or acephate in Beaver Dam Slope, Washington County, Utah. (5) Wyoming toad: 1/4-mile buffer each side of Little Laramie River, Albany County, Wyoming. (6) Fish: 1-mile malathion exclusion for 21 restricted-range species; 1/4-mile buffer adequate for carbaryl and acephate. (7) Plants: 3-mile no-aerial-ULV buffer around 18 insect-pollinated species; alternative use of carbaryl bait or Nosema.

**Monitoring Requirements (seven components from 1986 opinions):** (1) Abundance of [[Peregrine Falcon]] prey species before and after spraying in treated and untreated areas. (2) Carbaryl, malathion, or acephate levels in prey species. (3) Acetylcholinesterase levels in kestrels before and after treatment. (4) Pesticide levels in water near sprayed areas. (5) Acetylcholinesterase levels in fish from treated and untreated areas. (6) Incidence of lordosis in [[Colorado River]] and [[Green River]] larval fishes. (7) Abundance of aquatic macroinvertebrates in Green and Colorado Rivers before and after treatment. Monitoring scope of work must be developed by APHIS and concurred with by the Service in writing prior to program initiation.

**Buffer Zones for No-Jeopardy Species:** [[Bald Eagle]]: 1-mile treatment-free radius around eyries (no Nosema, no flyovers); 2.5-mile no-aerial-ULV zone upstream/downstream with 1/4-mile lateral river buffer; 1/4-mile buffer around foraging lakes. [[Peregrine Falcon]]: 1-mile treatment-free radius around eyries, hack sites, augmentation sites (no flyovers); 10-mile radius no-aerial-ULV zone (considered "no-drift" zone). [[Black-Footed Ferret]]: consultation with local FWS offices required prior to any control program; plans for adequate protection to be adopted.

**Conference Report on Proposed Species:** [[Mount Graham Red Squirrel]]: no control in dense Engelmann spruce/fir habitat above 9,400 feet, Graham County, Arizona. [[Black-Capped Vireo]]: exclusion of aerial ULV in foraging/nesting habitat; contact FWS Tulsa office and Joseph Grzybowski (Central State University) to identify nesting areas in Oklahoma. [[Little Colorado Spinedace]]: 1/4-mile buffer for carbaryl/acephate, 1-mile buffer for malathion in East Clear Creek, Chevelon Creek, Silver Creek, Nutrioso Creek (approximately 115 stream miles occupied). [[Virgin River Chub]]: same buffers from Mesquite, Nevada, to LaVerkin Springs, Utah (40 stream miles). [[Bruneau Hot Spring Snail]]: no aerial pesticide within 1/4 mile of Hot Creek, Owyhee County, Idaho (T. 8S., R. 6 E., Sections 2-4; T. 7S., R. 6E., Sections 33-35). [[Pawnee Montane Skipper]]: no aerial ULV within 1/4 mile of habitat in Douglas County, Colorado. [[Blowout Penstemon]] and [[Toad-Flax Cress]]: 3-mile no-aerial-ULV buffer or use carbaryl bait/Nosema.

**Incidental Take Statement:** Implementation of all reasonable and prudent alternatives should result in zero incidental take for all listed species pursuant to Section 7(b)(4). For proposed species, incidental take provisions become effective only upon final listing.

**Conservation Recommendations:** (1) Whooping crane: APHIS contact FWS Southeastern Idaho Refuge Complex (Pocatello) for Idaho actions, Helena field office for Montana/Wyoming actions, and Dave Lockman (Wyoming Game and Fish) to ensure no cranes in treatment areas. (2) New Mexico ridge-nosed rattlesnake: no chemical pesticides in Animas Mountains, Hidalgo County, New Mexico. (3) Plant research: implement scientific study of floral biology and pollination ecology for all threatened/endangered plant species in spray areas, including identification of pollinators, foraging ranges, life histories, pollinator risk assessment, floral characteristics, fecundity documentation, self-compatibility evaluation, pollen limitation studies, and precise phenology documentation.
