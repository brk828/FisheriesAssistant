# Subterranean Population of Garra barreimiae from Oman, with Comments on Regressive Evolution

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document combines two papers from the *Journal of Natural History* (1984). The first, by K. E. Banister of the [[British Museum (Natural History)]], reports a hypogean (cave-dwelling) population of the cyprinid [[Garra Barreimiae]] from a deep cave in the [[Jabal Akhdar]] mountains of [[Oman]]. The author documents depigmented fish with rudimentary, embryonic eyes and argues that the concept of [[Regressive Evolution]] is untestable and should be replaced by [[Heterochrony]], particularly [[Neoteny]]. The second, by G. Fernández-Leborans, describes a new marine hypotrich ciliate, *Pleurotricha planensis* sp. nov., from seawater off the Spanish Mediterranean coast. The fish paper is the dominant content and forms the basis of this note.

## Background & Description

**Study population (Garra).** The cave lake lies on the south flank of the Jabal Akhdar, 9 km E.S.E. of Al-Hamra. Specimens were collected in May 1980 by A. Dunsire and M. Gallagher. The Museum registrations cited are BMNH 1980.11.27: 1–115, 1981.6.29: 4–15, and 1981.9.28: 26 and 56 (live specimens). The 56 living fish were divided into four colonies to guard against a single catastrophe. One colony died within four months; the others were still alive on 3 June 1983.

**Morphology.** Morphometric and meristic characters match epigean [[Garra Barreimiae]] (Fowler and Steinitz, as treated by Banister and Clarke, 1977). Hypogean fish differ mainly in being depigmented, lacking externally visible eyes, and having weak squamation. The epigean [[Garra Longipinnis]] is much rarer and is known from only one locality in the range. The author treats the hypogean and epigean populations as conspecific.

**Eye development.** Fish above 24 mm SL lack external eyes. Smaller individuals (about 11–14 mm SL) show a dark eye patch in the centre of the orbit, surrounded by very fluid orbital fat. Three external trajectories were seen as fish grew: the eye receded and was occluded by fat; a narrow tunnel formed between eye and surface; or a clear skin layer persisted over the eye, possibly the conjunctiva. By about 30 mm SL there was no external trace of the eye.

**Ciliate (Pleurotricha planensis).** Specimens were collected from seawater at Morro del Gos, Castellón (Mediterranean Sea), cultured in 100 ml filtered seawater with sterile wheat grains, and stained with ammoniacal silver carbonate. Measurements are based on about 40 specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cave conditions:** The cave population lives in a stable, low-density subterranean habitat. The author links this to K-selection, which he argues favours neoteny.
- **Comparative cave fauna cited:** [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] (Mexican characin), [[Phreatichthys]] and [[Barbopsis]] (Somalia), [[Typhlogarra]] (Iraq), [[Caecobarbus]], [[Trogloglanis Pattersoni]] (North American catfish), and the synbranchid eel [[Ophisternon Infernale]]. Invertebrate examples include the isopod *Platyarthrus hoffmannseggii* and the goby *Typhleotris californiensis*.
- **Light exposure:** Exposure to light did not change eye size or structure. Melanin developed in the skin, and dorsal fin markings and the post-opercular blotch became recognizable.
- **Mortality events:** Four months after establishment, the colony's fish (then 42–54 mm SL) died within 12 hours, with no change in aquarium conditions. Dissection showed advanced sexual maturity; one female of 52 mm SL carried eggs of 1.1 mm diameter.

## Key Findings

**Garra barreimiae cave population**

- **Optic lobes:** Fish that died shortly after capture had very poorly developed optic lobes, especially the dorsal part (optic tectum).
- **Regrowth under light:** Twelve fish that died after four months in light had optic lobes nearly as large as epigean fish, showing substantial brain regrowth in that time. Four of the ten fish that died were examined and all showed the same enlargement. (The text gives both twelve and ten for the deceased fish; the discrepancy is in the source.)
- **Eyes:** No development of eye or optic nerve was detected in the cave fish. The eye stays embryonic, lies close to the brain stem on the floor of the orbit, and the interocular distance stays about 1 mm from small to large fish (15 mm and 36 mm SL), while the skull grows. Preliminary histology showed a very small optic nerve, eye muscles and, in four eyes, a small lens. Histology is described as preliminary.
- **Pit and tube:** In one specimen, a hollow sac runs from a skin pit through adipose tissue to the cornea. Its walls are lined with germinal epithelium.
- **Theoretical conclusion:** The author argues that [[Regressive Evolution]] and [[Degeneration]] are unfalsifiable and should be dropped. Cave characters are treated as apomorphies that result from convergence.
- **Proposed mechanism:** The author proposes a testable hypothesis that cave characters result from [[Neoteny]] within [[Heterochrony]]. Juvenile hypogean fish have embryonic eyes, and adults keep the same eye structure. Other organs, such as scales and pigment, are described as developing in relation to gonadal development.
- **Critique of prior work:** The author criticises assumptions of spurious relationships, such as Ercolini et al. (1982) equating [[Phreatichthys]] with the goldfish *Carassius auratus*, and Wilkens (1973) treating *Ophisternon infernale* as a mere ecological race of *Synbranchus marmoratus*. The author also argues that degree of regression cannot indicate phylogenetic age.
- **Genetics:** Only a few genetic experiments on blind [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] exist, so genetic extrapolation is considered unreliable.

**Pleurotricha planensis (ciliate)**

- **Body:** Elongated, dorsoventrally flattened, 102–108 µm × 57–60 µm.
- **Macronucleus:** Two oval or slightly elongated nodes, each 26.4–27 µm × 14.4–15.6 µm, joined by a fine thread 10.2–11.4 µm long.
- **Micronuclei:** Two, spherical, 1.8–2.4 µm in diameter, one beside each macronuclear node.
- **Oral ciliature:** The adoral zone of membranelles (AZM) is 81–90 µm long and has 28–35 membranelles. The paroral formation is also present, and the fibrillar systems correspond to those of other hypotrichs seen under transmission electron microscopy.
- **Status:** Described as a new species; holotypes deposited at the Laboratorio de Biología General, Universidad Complutense de Madrid.
