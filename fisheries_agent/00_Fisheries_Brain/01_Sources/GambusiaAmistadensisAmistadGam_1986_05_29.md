# Gambusia amistadensis (Amistad Gambusia)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #extinction #freshwater-fish #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1986 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) letter of intent addressed to Dr. W.L. Minckley, notifying him of the agency's proposal to **delist the Amistad gambusia (*Gambusia amistadensis*) as extinct**, removing it from the List of Threatened and Endangered Species. The species was endemic to a single natural population in [[Goodenough Spring]], Val Verde County, Texas. The spring was inundated by the creation of [[Amistad Reservoir]], extirpating the wild population. All subsequent captive refuge populations also failed, ultimately due to genetic/ecological contamination by the closely related and invasive [[Gambusia affinis]] (western mosquitofish). This record represents a formally documented anthropogenic extinction event tied to reservoir construction and interspecific hybridization/competitive displacement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Type locality/sole habitat**: [[Goodenough Spring]], a freshwater spring system in Val Verde County, Texas, within the Rio Grande drainage.
- **Habitat loss mechanism**: The spring was permanently inundated by the impoundment of the [[Amistad Reservoir]] (a U.S.-Mexico border reservoir on the Rio Grande), eliminating the species' only known natural habitat.
- **Captive/refuge populations**: Following extirpation in the wild, captive populations were maintained but ultimately failed. Failure was attributed to **contamination by [[Gambusia affinis]]** (western mosquitofish), likely through introgressive hybridization, competitive exclusion, or accidental introduction into refuge tanks/ponds — a common threat to isolated, narrow-range *Gambusia* species.
- **Conservation context**: This case is a documented example of extinction driven by combined **habitat destruction (dam/reservoir construction)** and **invasive congener contamination**, a recurring pattern among endemic desert and spring-dwelling fishes in the American Southwest (cf. [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Pecos Gambusia]]).

## Key Findings
- *Gambusia amistadensis* is known from a **single natural population** at Goodenough Spring, Texas — an extreme case of micro-endemism.
- The species' habitat was destroyed by the **Amistad Reservoir impoundment**, a direct anthropogenic cause of extirpation in the wild.
- All **captive/refuge populations subsequently died out or were compromised** by contamination from *Gambusia affinis*, eliminating the last known living representatives of the species.
- As of the 1986 letter, USFWS considered the species **extinct** and was preparing formal Federal Register proposal documentation to delist it from the Endangered Species list.
- The letter explicitly solicits informal expert comment from Dr. W.L. Minckley (a noted authority on southwestern U.S. fishes) prior to formal rulemaking, indicating the agency sought scientific verification before finalizing the extinction determination.
- This case is frequently cited in ichthyological and conservation literature as one of the clearest documented **fish extinctions caused by dam/reservoir construction** in North America.
