# Biological Opinion on Draft Revised Black Canyon Habitat Management Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-conservation #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Biological Opinion evaluates the Bureau of Land Management's draft revised **Black Canyon Habitat Management Plan (HMP)** under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The consultation addresses three imperiled freshwater fish species — the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]], the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]], and the threatened [[Spikedace]] — within the 406,080-acre Black Canyon management area in Arizona. The Service concluded that adoption of the HMP as a *planning document* is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of any of the three species, though implementation of specific management actions will require additional Section 7 review. The opinion catalogs known populations, habitat conditions, historical declines driven by habitat destruction and nonnative species introductions, and anticipates minimal to no incidental take from the plan's adoption itself.

## Background & Description

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*): Small livebearing fish, 1–2 inches (2.5–5 cm) in length; family [[Poeciliidae]]. Listed endangered March 11, 1967. No critical habitat designated.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*): Small-bodied fish; family [[Cyprinodontidae]]. Listed endangered March 31, 1986. Critical habitat designated at Quitobaquito Spring (Pima County, AZ) and three sites in Imperial County, California.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*): Small, silvery cyprinid; family [[Cyprinidae]]; named for a well-developed spine on the dorsal fin. Listed threatened July 1, 1986 (currently under consideration for uplisting to endangered). Critical habitat proposed (June 18, 1985) for portions of the Verde and Gila Rivers and Aravaipa Creek, still pending at time of writing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat associations:**
- Gila topminnow occupies small, often spring-fed or perennial desert streams and cienega habitats within the [[Gila River]], Sonora, and de la Concepción River drainages (Arizona, New Mexico, Sonora, Mexico). Prefers perennial flow with minimal nonnative fish competition, as exemplified by [[Tule Creek]].
- Desert pupfish historically inhabited the lower Gila River system, lower [[Colorado River]] system, and Rio Sonoyta system; now restricted to a handful of natural localities in Arizona, California, and Mexico (Rio Sonoyta, Laguna Salada, lower Colorado River delta).
- Spikedace historically ranged throughout the upper Gila River drainage above Phoenix, including the [[Agua Fria River]] (documented near Rock Springs in 1943); currently persists in only four locations: Aravaipa Creek, upper Gila River (NM), Verde River, and Eagle Creek.

**Threats:**
- Habitat destruction: water diversion, stream downcutting, backwater draining, vegetation clearing, channelization, water impoundment, groundwater pumping, pesticides.
- Biological threats: competition and predation from nonnative fishes, notably [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), nonnative catfish, and green sunfish.
- Spikedace decline attributed to habitat destruction plus competition/predation by introduced fish (Propst et al. 1986; Rinne 1991).

## Key Findings

- **Rangewide status:** Gila topminnow status is "poor and declining" rangewide; only 93 naturally occurring populations plus ~20 reintroduced populations remain in the U.S. Reintroduction success is generally poor — most transplant populations fail within a few years, but the Tule Creek and Cow/Humbug Creek populations have persisted 12 years, making them "vital to survival and recovery" of the species.
- **Desert pupfish status:** Improving rangewide due to changes in the Salton Sea basin, but only one natural population exists in Arizona and it has been invaded by nonnative catfish. Reintroduction efforts have had very limited success.
- **Spikedace status:** Being considered for uplisting to endangered due to increasing rangewide threats. Historic Agua Fria River habitat is "seriously deteriorated," particularly from loss of perennial flow; spikedace do not currently exist in the Black Canyon management area, though reintroduction is a recovery plan objective.
- **Biological Opinion conclusion:** Adoption of the HMP as a planning document is **not likely to jeopardize** the Gila topminnow, desert pupfish, or spikedace. Long-term effects of the plan are expected to be beneficial (reintroduction, habitat restoration, threat abatement), consistent with existing recovery plans (USFWS 1984, 1991, 1992).
- **Incidental take:** No take anticipated from adoption of the HMP itself; however, implementation of specific actions (e.g., fish barriers, exotic fish eradication, riparian exclosures, habitat improvements) could cause short-term adverse impacts or incidental take of Gila topminnow or desert pupfish, requiring further Section 7 consultation before implementation.
- **Management recommendations in HMP:** Native fish reintroduction, monitoring/inventory, habitat improvement, fish barriers, exotic fish eradication, [[Coordinated Resource Management Plans]], riparian/spring exclosures, vehicle access regulation, land exchanges, burro management, cottonwood/willow plantings, prescribed burns, wildlife water catchments, mining pollution abatement, and bat roost gating — reflecting a broader multi-species habitat strategy encompassing riparian/wetland systems, desert tortoise habitat, sensitive bat habitat, and chaparral.
- **Reinitiation triggers:** Per 50 CFR 402.16, formal consultation must be reinitiated if incidental take limits are exceeded, new information reveals unconsidered impacts, the action is modified to cause new effects, or a new species/critical habitat designation becomes relevant.
