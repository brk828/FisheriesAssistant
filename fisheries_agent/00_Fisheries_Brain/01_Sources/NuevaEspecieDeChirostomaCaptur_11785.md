# Chirostoma riojai sp. n. (Atherinidae) from Laguna de Santiago Tilapa, Estado de México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 taxonomic description, published in *Ciencia* (Mexico) by A. Solórzano and Y. López of the [[Instituto Nacional De Investigaciones Biologico Pesqueras]], introduces [[Chirostoma Riojai]] as a new species of [[Atherinidae]] (silversides). The fish was collected in 1963 from the [[Laguna De Victoria]], also called [[Santiago Tilapa]] lagoon, in the state of Mexico. The authors judged the specimens distinct from all known *Chirostoma* forms and named the species after the recently deceased hydrobiologist [[Enrique Rioja]]. The description rests on a holotype and 48 paratypes, with morphometric and meristic comparisons against six congeners.

## Background & Description

**Study site.** The [[Laguna De Victoria]] is a small, spring-fed water body of no more than 6 to 7 hectares, about 6 km from the village of Santiago Tilapa. It lies in a small closed basin. The authors describe it as a remnant of a former lake that flooded the whole [[Valle De Victoria]]. It probably formed from tributary streams of the [[Lerma Basin]] following [[Tectonic Movements]]. The lagoon was visited several times in 1963 for collecting.

**Type series.**
- **Holotype:** a female of 59.0 mm standard length, collected 31 October 1963 by A. Solórzano and R. Cruz.
- **Paratypes:** 48 specimens of 33.0 to 78.5 mm standard length. Of these, 33 were measured for the variation table.
- **Catch records:** four collections in 1963 (6 Sept, 16 Oct, 23 Oct and 31 Oct) yielded 6, 10, 15 and 18 specimens. The holotype came from the 31 October catch.
- **Deposition:** three paratypes went to the Laboratorio de Hidrobiología, ENCB-IPN. Two went to the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, two to Tulane University, and two to the British Museum of Natural History. The remainder are held at the central laboratory of the [[Instituto Nacional De Investigaciones Biologico Pesqueras]].

**Diagnosis.** Small fish, with the largest measured specimen at 78.5 mm standard length.

**Morphology.** The body is compressed. Maximum depth is 5.1 to 6 times in standard length. The head is small, at 3.4 to 4.3 times in standard length. The mouth is small and protractile, and the teeth are small and pointed, in two rows. Scales are cycloid with smooth free margins.

**Coloration.** Living or freshly preserved fish are greenish, darker on the back. After formalin fixation they turn grey, with a whitish-yellow belly. Some females show a faintly visible dark peritoneum. The lateral band becomes dark in preserved specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors place the species within the [[Lerma Basin]] fauna, one of the characteristic *Chirostoma* areas. They suggest that its isolated population in the Victoria lagoon records former tributary streams of that basin. The lagoon's isolation, they argue, allowed the local population to evolve into the described form. The text gives no data on water chemistry, temperature, food, reproduction or associated fauna.

**Comparative material.** Six specimens of *Chirostoma arge* (38.9 to 58.5 mm) from the Río Aguascalientes near Belén del Refugio, Jalisco (March 1961, UMMZ 179762) were measured for comparison.

**Closest relatives and key separating characters:**

- [[Chirostoma Bartoni]]: similar in dorsal and anal ray counts and scale number (39 to 48). Differs in having about 20 gill rakers on the lower limb (versus 11 to 14) and usually more than 40 vertebrae (39 to 43, versus 37 or 38). Standard length fits 4 to 4.5 times in *C. bartoni*, versus 3.4 to 4.3 in *C. riojai*.
- [[Chirostoma Regani]]: 42 to 44 vertebrae, 46 to 55 lateral scales, anal rays I,17 to 20, and 15 to 18 lower-limb gill rakers. All differ from *C. riojai*.
- [[Chirostoma Patzcuaro]]: 45 to 52 scales, 18 to 22 lower gill rakers, more than 40 vertebrae, and scales with notched margins (smooth in *C. riojai*).
- [[Chirostoma Melanaccus]]: also 37 vertebrae, but 15 to 17 lower gill rakers and anal rays I,14 to 17.
- [[Chirostoma Jordani]]: shares scale and vertebra counts. Differs in 13 to 17 lower gill rakers (usually 15), anal rays I,15 to 20 (usually I,18), and pectoral rays 11 to 12.
- [[Chirostoma Arge]]: the closest affinity, but with a deeper body (maximum depth 4 to 5 times in standard length versus 5.1 to 6), 7 to 8 interdorsal scales (versus 8 to 11), anal rays usually 16 to 17 (versus 13), pectoral rays usually 12 to 13 (versus 10), and larger teeth.

## Key Findings

- *Chirostoma riojai* is a new species from a single small lagoon, Laguna de Victoria or Santiago Tilapa, Estado de México. The type locality lies 6 km from Santiago Tilapa and about 3 km from San Miguel Almaya.
- The description is based on a holotype (59.0 mm) and 48 paratypes (33.0 to 78.5 mm), with 33 specimens used for the morphometric variation table.
- The species is separated from its congeners mainly by low lower-limb gill raker counts (11 to 14), low vertebra counts (37 or 38) and few lateral scales (39 to 43).
- The nearest relatives are *C. bartoni*, *C. arge* and *C. jordani*. Each is separated by specific counts of gill rakers, anal rays, body depth and interdorsal scales.
- The authors interpret the lagoon as a relict of ancient Lerma tributaries, with the fish population isolated and evolving in place.
- The species is named in honour of [[Enrique Rioja]] for his contributions to Mexican hydrobiology.
- The lagoon is partly choked with vegetation, a point of conservation relevance for this restricted-range fish.
