# Aspects of the Reproductive Biology of *Etheostoma tippecanoe* With Comments on Egg-Burying Behavior

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #Nothonotus #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This study (Warren, Burr & Kuhajda, 1986) presents the first detailed account of the reproductive biology and spawning behavior of the [[Tippecanoe Darter]] (*Etheostoma tippecanoe*), a small percid fish in the subgenus [[Nothonotus]]. Field collections from the [[Green River]] (Kentucky) in July 1984 combined with aquarium spawning trials revealed that breeding individuals segregate by size, depth, and current velocity, with larger, brightly colored males and gravid females occupying fast, deep, gravelly riffles. Aquarium observations documented an egg-burying spawning mode involving full female burial in gravel substrate, contiguous L-shaped egg clusters attached to stones, and rapid male abandonment post-spawning. These behavioral traits support a phylogenetic placement of *E. tippecanoe* as closely allied to the egg-burying members of Nothonotus (e.g., [[Etheostoma camurum]] and [[Etheostoma rufilineatum]]), rather than the egg-clumping species (e.g., [[Etheostoma maculatum]]).

## Background & Description
- **Breeding size threshold:** Individuals >19 mm SL classified as reproductively mature/breeding.
- **Sampled males:** 4 ripe males, 24–31 mm SL (mean nuptial male SL = 28.1 mm, range 22.5–30.8 mm, N=15).
- **Sampled females:** 5 gravid females, 20–26 mm SL (mean gravid female SL = 25.8 mm, range 21.2–27.2 mm, N=14).
- **Age at maturity:** All sampled breeding individuals were 12 months old (age-1 spawners).
- **Egg diameter classes:**
  - Class I (mature): 0.88–1.32 mm, yellow to translucent orange.
  - Class II: 0.64–0.84 mm, white.
  - Class III (undeveloped): <0.48 mm, small, white.
- **Fertilized egg diameter:** Averaged 1.44 mm (N=2, measured 29 July).
- **Hatchling length:** Averaged 5.09 mm TL (individual measurements 5.05 and 5.12 mm TL).
- **Hatching time:** ~211 hr (9 days) at 22–25 °C.
- **Male coloration:** Breeding males intensified in orange coloration during spawning, with dark blue vertical bars becoming obscured — consistent with descriptions in [[Trautman 1981]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat segregation by reproductive status:**
  - Gravid females and large, brightly colored males (>19 mm SL) occupied a **deep run (0.35 m)** with fast current, gravel/pebble/cobble substrate, adjacent to *Justicia americana* growth.
  - Immature individuals occupied a **shallow (0.08–0.15 m), slower riffle** area.
- **Comparative population structure:** A Licking River population (pre-breeding season, June 1984) showed homogeneous size-class distribution within riffle habitat, suggesting the Green River segregation pattern reflects **prespawning/spawning behavior** rather than permanent year-round habitat partitioning (contrasting with year-round segregation reported for [[Etheostoma rufilineatum]] and [[Etheostoma camurum]] by Stiles 1972).
- **Territoriality:** The largest aquarium male (31 mm SL) established and defended a territory (initially under a filter, later relocated near air stones between two vertical stones), displaying dominance and agonistic chasing behavior toward other males; tolerated minor intrusion only from the smallest male (24 mm SL).
- **Substrate/spawning site selection:** Spawning occurred at a site selected by the dominant male — between two vertical stones — with eggs recovered 4 cm deep in substrate attached to a flat stone.

## Key Findings

### Spawning Behavior
- Female initiates burial via snout-thrusting and body vibration until fully buried except for the caudal fin.
- Male assumes head-to-head position over buried female; multiple females (up to 3) observed burying near/around the dominant male simultaneously.
- Male spawning act signaled by pectoral fin flaring and wide gaping (interpreted as milt release) — **no physical contact** observed between male and female during spawning.
- Females withdrew from substrate 5–10 min post-spawning and were observed undergoing **periodic reburial**.
- Male abandoned the spawning site relatively quickly following spawning bouts.
- Eggs were arranged in **rows of two, forming contiguous L-shapes**, attached to a submerged flat stone (20×20×8 mm), with 6 eggs recovered from one site.

### Egg/Ovarian Data (N=14 gravid females)
| Egg Class | Mean Count | Range |
|---|---|---|
| Class I (mature) | 30.4 | 1–58 |
| Class II | 22.8 | 0–72 |
| Class III (undeveloped) | 109.5 | 24–191 |
| **Total ova** | **162.7** | **55–236** |

- Largest ova count from a 27.2 mm SL female: 58 Class I + 62 Class II ova.
- **Gonadosomatic Index (GSI):** mean = 965 (range 344–1618).
- **No significant correlation** (P > 0.01) between number of mature (Class I) ova and adjusted body weight (r = 0.583) or SL (r = 0.423) — attributed to low size variation among mature 1-year-old spawners.
- Authors suggest possible **fractional spawning** strategy, cautioning that ovarian egg counts may not equal true annual fecundity (per [[Gale and Deutsch 1985]] framework).

### Phylogenetic/Behavioral Implications
- Reproductive behavior (dominance hierarchies, agonistic displays, mounting, spawning-site selection, timing, periodic female reburial, rapid male abandonment) closely parallels [[Etheostoma camurum]] and [[Etheostoma rufilineatum]] (per Mount 1959; Stiles 1972).
- **Multiple female burial around a single dominant male** more closely resembles *E. rufilineatum* than *E. camurum* (Stiles 1972 noted multiple burial only in the former).
- Findings support classifying *E. tippecanoe* as an **egg-burying** species within Nothonotus, contrasting with earlier inferred classification based only on secondhand observation (Page 1983, citing Trautman 1981).
- Egg-attachment/clumping-within-substrate behavior (as in *E. tippecanoe* and *E. camurum*) is proposed as a **phylogenetic precursor** to above-substrate egg-clumping seen in advanced Nothonotus (e.g., *E. maculatum*), supporting Page's (1985) transitional hypothesis regarding male territory establishment beneath stones as a key evolutionary step.
- Findings suggest *E. tippecanoe* occupies a phylogenetically intermediate/close position to the ancestral origin of above-substrate egg-clumping, contradicting Zorach's (1972) view of the species as an early offshoot of the Nothonotus lineage.

### Methodological Notes
- Aging performed via scale annuli counts (dorsum, junction of spinous/soft dorsal fins); month-scale aging anchored to July as "month zero" (breeding month).
- GSI calculated as ovary weight / adjusted body weight × 1000 (per [[Page 1974]] methodology).
- Aquarium conditions simulated natural current regime via air-stone-generated rolling water motion (~15 cm/sec).
