# Eagle Creek Fisheries Survey (June 7-9, 1999)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A three-day fisheries survey of [[Eagle Creek]] in Greenlee County, Arizona, was conducted June 7-9, 1999, by Arizona State University researchers (Brian Kesner, Tim McLaughlin, Paul Marsh and Brian Bagley). Sampling at seven sites along a roughly 6.5 km (about 4 mi) reach, from [[Honeymoon Campground]] to Bat Cave, documented 14 fish species, all previously recorded in the creek. [[Crayfish]] were present at every site. Two sensitive native species, [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], were not found. Spikedace have not been collected since 1989, and loach minnow have not been collected for the second consecutive survey year since their 1994 rediscovery.

## Background & Description

The survey was a short field effort, documented in a field report with a summary table of catch and effort by site. The reach lies downstream of the [[Phelps Dodge Eagle Creek Pumping Station]], which is the reference point for site distances. A location map (scale 1:250,000; 1 inch = 4 miles; contour interval 200 ft / 61 m) accompanies the report. Later pages of the source are largely illegible scanned map and table fragments and contain no additional survey data.

**Sampling methods**
- Gear: [[Smith-Root DC Backpack Electrofisher]], dip nets, and 0.5 cm mesh [[Seines]].
- Coverage: seven locations.
- Constraint: the electrofisher broke down early on the first day, so most sites were sampled with seines (recorded as seine hauls per site).

**Species collected (14 total)**
- Salmonids: [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarkii*)
- Cyprinids: [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*)
- Catostomids: [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarkii*)
- Ictalurids: [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*)
- Poeciliids: [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)
- Centrarchids: [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*)

Several of these are non-native or disturbed-habitat species, including carp, red shiner, fathead minnow, mosquitofish, channel catfish, yellow bullhead and smallmouth bass; the source does not classify them explicitly.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey is framed around the conservation status of native fishes in Eagle Creek. The notable absences were:

- **[[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)**: not encountered, and not collected in Eagle Creek since 1989, despite earlier records.
- **[[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)**: not encountered despite a concentrated search at the first road crossing below Honeymoon Campground. The species was rediscovered in 1994, and this is the second straight year without a capture.

The reported species list includes the native fishes Gila chub, longfin dace, speckled dace, Sonora sucker and desert sucker, and the non-native or introduced species listed above. All 14 species had been recorded on previous trips, so the survey confirms persistence of the existing community rather than documenting new occurrences. The source gives no water chemistry, flow, temperature or habitat measurements.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 14 fish species were collected across the reach. All had been recorded previously.
- **Crayfish:** present at all seven sample locations.
- **Mosquitofish** were the most numerous fish in the table, with counts of 168, 150 and 350 at three sites.
- **Red shiner** was also abundant at several sites (174 and 130 recorded at two sites).
- **Speckled dace** counts ranged from 8 to 60 at different sites, with 46 and 29 also recorded.
- **Spikedace** were not found and have not been collected since 1989.
- **Loach minnow** were not found, despite concentrated effort below Honeymoon Campground, extending a two-year absence since the 1994 rediscovery.
- **Electrofishing** was limited by an early equipment failure, so seining was the main method. Electrofishing shock times of 158 and 195 seconds are recorded for the final two sites in the table.
- **Sampling effort:** seine hauls per site varied, with the table giving counts such as 10, 26, 12 and 17 at the first sites.
