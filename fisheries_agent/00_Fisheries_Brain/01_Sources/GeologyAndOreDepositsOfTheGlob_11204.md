# Geology and Ore Deposits of the Globe-Miami District, Arizona

#geology #economic-geology #globe-miami-district #porphyry-copper

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 342 (N. P. Peterson, 1962) presents a detailed geologic study of the [[Globe-Miami District]] in Gila County, Arizona. The district's rocks range from Early Precambrian to Recent, and its economic importance rests on [[Disseminated-Copper Deposits]] (chiefly the [[Miami-Inspiration Deposit]]) and on the [[Old Dominion Vein System]]. Through the end of 1958 the district had produced about 6,121 million pounds of copper plus minor gold, silver, lead and zinc, valued at more than $1 billion. The paper is not about fish or aquatic biology; the only water-related content concerns groundwater and surface flow in the [[Gila Conglomerate]] and [[Pinal Creek]] system.

## Background & Description

**Location and access.** The district lies in the lower foothills of the [[Pinal Mountains]] and [[Apache Peaks]], mostly within the Globe, Inspiration and northern Pinal Ranch 7½-minute quadrangles. [[Globe]] (1950 population 6,419) and [[Miami]] (4,320) are the main towns, with about 18,000 people in the district as a whole. Access is via U.S. Highways 60-70 and 70, a branch of the Gila Valley, Globe and Northwestern Railroad, and State Route 88 (Apache Trail).

**Climate and vegetation.** Mean annual temperature at Globe is about 61.3 °F and mean annual precipitation about 17 inches, with summer cloudbursts and winter steady rains. Vegetation is typical of the transition zone between central Arizona mountains and the desert.

**Physiography.** The district sits in the middle of Arizona's mountain region, divided by Globe Valley into the Pinal Mountain foothills (southwest) and the Globe Hills (northeast). Pinal Peak rises to 7,850 feet.

**Previous and current work.** F. L. Ransome mapped the area in 1901-1902 and again in the 1910s (USGS Professional Paper 115, 1919). Fieldwork for this report ran from 1943 to 1953, at 1:12,000 on photogrammetric base maps.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper addresses the physical and hydrologic environment rather than biota.

- **Drainage:** Most of the area drains north to the [[Salt River]] via [[Pinto Creek]] and [[Pinal Creek]]. Pinal Creek flows underground for much of its course; a substantial permanent underflow rises near Pringle Ranch. On 28 March 1945 surface flow was absent for about 10 miles below Globe, while the flow at a pumping plant was estimated at 6,000 to 7,000 gallons per minute, of which 1,500 to 2,000 gallons per minute is pumped for use at Inspiration.
- **Groundwater:** The [[Gila Conglomerate]] is the district's principal aquifer. Wells 500 to 800 feet deep in the northeastern fan yield commonly 110 to 150 gallons per minute, while the southwestern fan has very low storage and transmissibility. Mine water from the east contains about 2,300 ppm dissolved solids, mainly calcium sulfates.
- **Water-use pressure:** The paper notes that the Globe Valley groundwater reserve is being rapidly depleted after a decade of deficient precipitation and greatly increased domestic use.
- **Geologic hazards and structure:** Faulting persisted into Quaternary time, and the [[Miami Fault]] has a throw of about 1,500 feet near the Miami-Inspiration ore body.

## Key Findings

**Stratigraphy and geologic history**

- Oldest unit: [[Pinal Schist]] (Early Precambrian), formed by metamorphism of shale and feldspathic sandstone during the [[Mazatzal Revolution]]. It is intruded by [[Ruin Granite]], [[Madera Diorite]] and related bodies.
- [[Apache Group]] (Late Precambrian): Scanlan conglomerate, Pioneer formation, Barnes conglomerate, Dripping Spring quartzite, Mescal limestone and basalt, with the Pioneer about 200 to 270 feet thick and the Dripping Spring 350 to 650 feet thick in measured sections.
- Paleozoic: [[Troy Quartzite]] (Middle Cambrian, 400 to possibly 700 feet), [[Martin Limestone]] (Late Devonian, 150 to 350 feet), [[Escabrosa Limestone]] (Mississippian, up to 365 feet) and [[Naco Limestone]] (Pennsylvanian, about 500 feet maximum). No Mesozoic sediments are preserved.
- Intrusions: a Late Cretaceous to early Tertiary igneous episode produced the [[Solitude Granite]], [[Willow Spring Granodiorite]], [[Lost Gulch Quartz Monzonite]], [[Diabase]] sills and dikes, [[Schultze Granite]] with granite porphyry facies, and diorite porphyry.
- Tertiary volcanism: [[Dacite]] (interpreted here as a welded tuff, not a single lava flow, and at least 1,200 feet thick) overlies the [[Whitetail Conglomerate]].
- [[Gila Conglomerate]] (Pliocene to early Pleistocene), locally more than 4,000 feet thick, with olivine basalt flows.

**Mineral deposits**

- Disseminated-copper deposits (chiefly Miami-Inspiration, Castle Dome, Copper Cities, Cactus) account for more than 80 percent of district metal value. Protore is pyrite and chalcopyrite; the ore is supergene chalcocite and covellite.
- Miami-Inspiration is by far the largest deposit: about 275 million tons of ore produced, yielding about 4.82 billion pounds of copper (Miami 2.13 billion lb, Inspiration 2.70 billion lb) from 1911 to 1958.
- Vein deposits: the [[Old Dominion Vein System]] alone produced copper valued at about $152 million. Other vein systems include Great Eastern, Buckeye-Black Oxide, Dime, Stonewall, Big Johnnie, Maggie, Josh Billings and Buffalo.
- Copper deposits formed by circulating groundwater (for example Van Dyke, Carlota, Warrior, Live Oak and Keystone) account for about $7 million in production.
- Gold and silver are minor (Old Dominion ore averaged 0.014 oz gold per ton; Iron Cap 0.006 oz per ton).

**Production statistics**

- 1878-1910 (estimated): 27,718 oz gold, 5,664,431 oz silver, 811,199,664 lb copper, value about $51.05 million.
- 1911 to 1953: 158,630 oz gold, 10,671,978 oz silver, 6,120,996,830 lb copper, 4,511,860 lb lead, 384,586 lb zinc, value $1,031,968,666.
- Copper accounts for more than 98 percent of total value. Molybdenum recovery by Miami Copper Co. since 1938 averaged about 523,000 lb per year in the last seven years reported.
- About 300 million tons of ore had been mined to the end of 1953.

**Structure**

- Three blocks: [[Globe Hills Block]], [[Globe Valley Block]] (a graben filled with Gila conglomerate) and [[Inspiration Block]] (containing all disseminated-copper deposits).
- Faulting began after the Pennsylvanian and its fundamental pattern formed before diabase intrusion. The Old Dominion vein fault has apparent vertical separation of 50 to 680 feet.

**Economic outlook**

- The paper notes that large areas of younger rock (Tertiary and Quaternary) overlie older rocks within the mineral belt, which may conceal additional ore bodies. The mineral belt is about 6 miles wide and trends northeastward.
