# Cuatro Ciénegas Basin Fauna: Correspondence and Minckley's Faunal Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #mammalogy #malacology

## Executive Summary

This item combines two parts. The first is a 1993 letter from thesis student [[Alessandra Pellegrini]] (University "La Sapienza", Rome) to [[W.L. Minckley]] at [[Arizona State University]]. She asks about a forthcoming book on environmental change in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], the source and reliability of a mammal checklist, and the absence of deer from that checklist. The second part is a Spanish-language faunal summary prepared by Minckley. It describes the basin's aquatic fauna as highly endemic, especially its fishes and freshwater snails, and it gives a mammal checklist of roughly 64 taxa.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence:** Pellegrini wrote a second letter in September 1993, after an April letter. She spent three months in the valley, supported by [[PROFAUNA A.C.]] and the [[World Wildlife Fund]] (USA). Her thesis, roughly equivalent to a master's thesis, proposes an ecologically sound [[Management Plan]] for the area. She uses a [[Geographic Information System]] as an experimental tool. Her fieldwork included visits to marshes, ponds, lakes, creeks and canals, informal talks with local stakeholders, and weekend interviews with visitors to "La Becerra" and "Los Mezquites" about recreational use.
- **Faunal summary:** Minckley wrote the summary as a brief overview of biological studies in the basin. He states that it is likely superficial for specialists but useful for newcomers. Most available information concerns aquatic animals and habitats, since few studies cover terrestrial animals. Invertebrate data come from [[Dr. D.W. Taylor]] for gastropods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Aquatic endemism:** The summary says the basin's aquatic habitats hold abundant fauna, especially compared with other desert areas. Ten of 18 fish species are endemic, spanning 8 families and 13 genera (see [[Endemism]] and [[Fish]]).
- **Reptiles:** Six aquatic and semiaquatic reptile species are known. Three are endemic turtles, one turtle has a wider distribution, and two [[Natrix]] snakes represent peripheral or relict populations. Several terrestrial vertebrates (*Eumeces*, *Coluber*, some anurans) require mesic habitats hundreds of kilometres to the south.
- **Freshwater snails:** Only 17 species are known, and collecting is incomplete. Five new genera and 12 species in 3 new subfamilies have been identified. One new genus has a different local form in each spring sampled. Five species and one subspecies are recognized. Minckley notes that comparable local evolution in aquatic snails is unknown elsewhere in the Western Hemisphere.
- **Mammals:** The checklist separates species collected within the valley from those recorded within about 30 km around it. The list includes bats, rodents, carnivores and ungulates, with many subspecies designations.

## Key Findings

- **Endemism:** The basin is a centre of endemism for fish (10 of 18 species endemic), snails (5 new genera, 12 species in 3 new subfamilies), and turtles (3 endemic species). Minckley describes this concentration of unique genera and species as unmatched elsewhere in North America.
- **Mammal checklist:** The list includes about 64 taxa across 17 families. The largest groups are Vespertilionidae (about 13 taxa) and Cricetidae (about 10). Heteromyidae has about 7 and Mustelidae about 6. The list uses symbols, and the letter asks what the cross marks mean relative to the asterisks. The summary does not clearly explain the symbol key in the OCR text.
- **Questions raised by Pellegrini:**
  - When and how the checklist was compiled, and its confidence level.
  - Why some taxa carry crosses instead of asterisks.
  - Whether *Nasua narica molaris* actually reached the basin in recent decades, since she doubts it.
  - Why white-tailed and mule deer are absent, and whether they were never collected.
- **Proposed outputs:** Pellegrini plans a [[Management Plan]] for the valley that uses GIS. She asks Minckley for a copy of his forthcoming book on environmental change and for input on multiple-use management of the basin.
- **Status of correspondence:** The item contains no reply from Minckley. Its value lies in the faunal summary and the open questions about the mammal checklist.
