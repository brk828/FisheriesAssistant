# Reintroduction Guidelines for Federally Listed Aquatic Species: Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) packet, dated July–September 1997, concerns a proposed set of [[Reintroduction Guidelines]] for federally listed aquatic species, prompted by the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] stocking program. The Commission was asked to approve the guidelines, which were adopted on August 8, 1997 and then circulated to interested parties on September 8, 1997. The guidelines allow reintroductions under full protection of the [[Endangered Species Act]] when landowners, land managers and grazing permittees concur and sportfishing or other wildlife conflicts are absent, and default to [[Experimental Nonessential Population]] status otherwise. Commentators, including the [[Desert Fishes Council]] and [[The Nature Conservancy]] of Arizona, supported approval with qualifications.

## Background & Description

- **Program origin:** In August 1995 the Commission authorized a [[Memorandum of Understanding]] with the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) to stock 20 to 30 or more self-sustaining populations of each species by 2004 on BLM lands. Reintroductions were to achieve downlisting objectives in recovery plans.
- **Species status:** The Gila topminnow is federally listed as endangered; in Arizona it is restricted to fewer than a dozen natural sites plus about 12 reintroduced sites (figure as stated: "approximately izre" in OCR, likely "about a dozen"). The Draft Revised Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan identifies 55 successful reintroductions as necessary for downlisting. The desert pupfish is also federally listed as endangered, due to habitat loss, pollution and non-native fishes; its recovery plan requires that at least 20 populations be re-established.
- **Authorizing direction:** All reintroductions under the MOU were to follow the nonessential experimental population clause (ESA Section 10(j)). No guidance existed for actions outside the MOU.
- **Problems identified (Memo, July 14, 1997):**
  1. USFWS needs about two years to promulgate a 10(j) rule, and the Arizona Field Office had not allocated time to new 10(j) rules for more than three years.
  2. ESA precludes 10(j) designation where a site is not isolated from occupied habitat, for example where floods could mix stocks.
  3. Some private landowners and public land managers do not want less-than-fully-protected populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Proposed sites:** BLM requested stocking of Gila topminnows in Empire Gulch, about one mile upstream of a natural, fully protected population in Cienega Creek. The Arizona Nature Conservancy (TNC) requested stocking on its Muleshoe Ranch Cooperative Management Area (CMA) and a private landowner requested a site on Sonoita Creek upstream of a natural population on private land. Flood-driven mixing rules out 10(j) status at both the Empire Gulch and Sonoita Creek sites.
- **Sportfishing coexistence:** Topminnow and pupfish populations occur near significant sportfisheries (Kayler Spring near Roosevelt Lake, Sonoita Creek below Patagonia Lake, and Roper Lake State Park, among others) with no reported impacts. The Department states that each site is screened by fisheries staff to avoid conflicts.
- **Habitat and species interactions:** The Desert Fishes Council noted that these species are poor competitors with many non-native sport fish, and that the chance of native fish being lost to a sportfishery is extremely remote. Commenters also noted that [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) should be excluded from stocked waters (MOU stipulation C.7).
- **Management scale:** Sites are to be biologically viable but containable as management units, each subject to site-specific [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) compliance.

## Key Findings

- **Proposed guidelines (adopted August 8, 1997):**
  1. The Director may approve reintroductions under full ESA protection (with prior notice to the Commission) if the land management agency and/or landowner concurs, any directly affected grazing allotment owner/lessee concurs, and there are no conflicts with sportfishing or other wildlife management.
  2. Where concurrence exists but conflicts are possible, the Director may approve as fully protected or as experimental nonessential if conflicts can be resolved without effect on threatened or endangered species or sportfishing; if conflicts are significant, the Director may decline, approve as nonessential experimental, or recommend a fully protected population to the Commission.
  3. All other reintroductions proceed as experimental nonessential populations.
- **Commission action:** At the August 1995 meeting the Commission unanimously approved the MOU, with a motion requiring experimental nonessential designation for all MOU reintroductions. The Commission also required that the Department bring significant conflicts to it for discussion.
- **Process:** Guidelines operate within the Department's 12-Step Process for Re-establishment of Nongame and Endangered Species.
- **Stakeholder comments (summarized, not exhaustive):**
  - [[Desert Fishes Council]] (E. Phil Pister, August 6, 1997) supported approval but asked for deletion of guideline 1b, which gives a private grazing lessee effective veto power, and noted that experimental nonessential populations do not count toward recovery or downlisting.
  - USFWS Arizona Ecological Services (Sam F. Spiller, August 1997) supported the guidelines, said a 10(j) rule could take two years or more, and recommended that conservation plans under Section 10 or Safe Harbor agreements address private-land sites.
  - Jack C. Turner (Desert Fishes Council) argued that the Commission holds wildlife in public trust and that endangered species should receive protection at least equal to that for harvested species; he objected to the term "customers" for the public.
  - The Arizona Nature Conservancy (Leslie N. Corey, June 30, 1997) requested that fully protected populations be considered, while accepting that experimental nonessential status may suit some sites.
- **Open issues raised in the packet:** Whether USFWS will count experimental nonessential populations toward recovery; the potential for legislative scrutiny of sportsmen's funds (Chairman Porter's concern, August 1995); and the question of fully protected versus nonessential designation on a site-by-site basis.
