# Desert Fishes Council Proceedings, Volume XXV (1993): Leatherside Chub Life History and Razorback Sucker Decline

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is front matter and two abstracts from the *Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council*, Volume XXV, from the 1993 Annual Symposium held 10 to 14 November 1993 at the Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León, Monterrey, Mexico. The volume was edited by [[Dean A. Hendrickson]] (Texas Memorial Museum, University of Texas, Austin) and published 10 March 1994. The [[Desert Fishes Council]] is dedicated to the conservation of desert fishes.

The volume contains two fish studies, both in English with Spanish summaries:

1. A life-history study of the [[Leatherside Chub]] (*Gila copei*) from the [[Provo River]] drainage, Utah, covering reproduction, age, and growth.
2. A population study of adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona and Nevada, which concludes that the population is declining toward possible collapse.

## Background & Description

**Leatherside Chub (*Gila copei*)** is native to the southern and eastern parts of the [[Bonneville Basin]], where it occurs in quiet pools of cold streams and rivers. The abstract notes that although the species can be abundant, its reproductive behavior and its age and growth patterns were poorly known.

**Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)** is a large catostomid of the desert Southwest. In this study it was treated as an adult population in a reservoir, with attention to abundance, movement, site fidelity, and stock status.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Leatherside chub habitat:** Cold, quiet pools in streams and rivers of the Bonneville Basin. The study sampled the [[Provo River]] and [[Spanish Fork River]] drainages in central Utah.
- **Razorback sucker habitat:** [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir spanning Arizona and Nevada. The study identified three general areas of concentration within the lake and included the [[Cottonwood East]] area in its catch comparisons.
- **Reproductive timing:** Gonadosomatic indices indicated that spawning peaked in May for the leatherside chub.
- **Population context:** The razorback sucker population is described as dependent on replacement, since natural recruitment has not been detected.

## Key Findings

### Leatherside Chub (*Gila copei*), Provo and Spanish Fork Rivers

Fish were collected in 1978–1979 for reproductive data and in spring/summer 1993 for age and growth data. Methods included:

- Gonadosomatic index analysis to determine the timing of reproduction ([[Gonadosomatic Index]]).
- Counting annuli on thin-sectioned [[Otolith]]s for [[Age and Growth Analysis]].
- [[Back-Calculation]] of size at age.

Key results:

- **Spawning peak:** May, based on gonadosomatic indices.
- **Fecundity:** Mean of 1,813 ova per female (N = 9). Ova number increased with female length ([[Fecundity]]).
- **Maximum age:** 9 years in the 1993 collection.
- **Reproductive age:** Reproductive adults ranged from 2 to 9 years, with a median of 6.
- **Adult size:** Standard lengths of adults ranged from 65 to 107 mm. Chubs under 65 mm standard length were nonreproductive.
- **Growth pattern:** Rapid growth in the first year, then roughly equal annual gains in length through age 5.
- **Conclusion:** The authors suggest that leatherside chub may be more long-lived than previously thought.

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*), Lake Mohave

The multi-agency study has sampled adult razorback suckers in Lake Mohave since 1974. Its objectives were:

1. Population abundance estimation.
2. Evaluation of movement patterns and site fidelity.
3. Acquisition of stock status information.

**Tagging and capture effort:**

- About 3,000 Floy tags and 600 Carlin tags were deployed from 1980 to 1990.
- More than 4,000 [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tagging]] (PIT) tags were deployed from 1991 to 1993.
- Total captures over 14 years exceeded 12,000. Total recaptures exceeded 1,000 fish, and about 40% of recaptures were long-term (more than 30 days) ([[Mark-Recapture Estimation]]).

**Movement and site fidelity:**

- Most sequential, long-term recaptures showed that fish returned to the same one of three general concentration areas.
- Return intervals were irregular and exceeded one year.

**Abundance estimates:**

- Annual abundance estimates are near 20,000 fish.
- Time-integrated multiple mark-recapture estimates (14 years of data, through 1993) indicated:
  - 73,500 individuals in the earlier period (the period label is partly garbled in the OCR).
  - 59,500 fish in the 1990s.
  - 23,300 fish in the most recent three-year estimate, 1991–1993.

**Catch trends:**

- Seasonal [[Trammel Net Sampling]] catch per unit effort and proportion of total catch were unchanged from 1974 to 1984 at Cottonwood East.
- Both statistics were progressively and significantly lower since 1985.
- The authors state that two independent lines of evidence agree, so a population decline is indicated despite large inter-annual variance.

**Demographic status:**

- Most fish exceed 40 years of age, meaning they were produced in the early 1950s.
- Natural recruitment has not been detected.
- The authors anticipate a [[Population Decline]] or crash. Without replacement, razorback suckers are expected to disappear from Lake Mohave.
