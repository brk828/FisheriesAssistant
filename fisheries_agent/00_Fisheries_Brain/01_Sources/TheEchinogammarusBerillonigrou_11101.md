# Echinogammarus berilloni-Group: Taxonomy and Ecology of Predominantly Iberian Amphipods (Crustacea)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 taxonomic monograph by S. Pinkster (University of Amsterdam) shows that the name [[Echinogammarus Berilloni]], long treated as one highly variable species, is in fact a collective name for a group of at least 10 closely related freshwater amphipod species. Only one, *E. berilloni* s.str., has a wide range across France, Belgium, Luxembourg, the southern Netherlands and south-western Germany. The other species are confined to small areas, mainly in northern Iberia. The paper gives redescriptions, a dichotomous identification key based on adult males, distribution maps, water chemistry for each station, and a zoogeographical discussion of Iberian freshwater amphipods.

Note: the document is a scientific paper, not a fish study. Its subject is freshwater crustaceans, so the taxon tag is [[Gammaridae]] rather than a fish family.

## Background & Description

- **Study group:** the [[Echinogammarus Berilloni-Group]], defined by large elongate eyes, gnathopod hands of unequal size, setae-armed legs, a short carpus on leg 2 relative to the propodus, absence of calceoli, and a comb-like setal row on the third segment of the mandible palp.
- **Historical context:** the variability noted by Spandl (1926), Margalef (1956) and Pinkster (1969) led to systematic sampling in 1969–1971 across all drainage systems of the Iberian Peninsula and much of France.
- **Taxonomic actions:** the new species *E. aquilifer* was described in 1969. The paper names *E. calvus* (new rank, previously *Gammarus berilloni* subsp. *calvus*) and erects seven further new species: *E. meridionalis*, *E. longisetosus*, *E. tarragonensis*, *E. feminatus*, *E. echinosetosus*, and *E. margalefi*. *E. zebrinus* is cited from Pinkster & Stock (1971).
- **Diagnostic traits:** the key separates species by the flag-like setal brush on antenna 2, the presence or absence of medial palmar spines on the gnathopods, the length of setae on legs 5–7 and the epimeral plates, and the shape of the telson lobes.
- **Adult-only basis:** morphological characters change during development, so only sexually mature males were used for the diagnostic comparisons. Females and juveniles are generally hard to identify.
- **Variability:** stable characters include eye shape, antennal setation, absence of calceoli and mandible-palp structure. Variable characters include flagellar segment counts, epimeral plate corner angles, telson spination and the number of setae, which increase with age.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and water chemistry**

- *E. berilloni* inhabits middle stream and river courses and has never been found in upper reaches. It is replaced upstream by *E. aquilifer* (western Pyrenees), *E. calvus* (spring zone of the Rio Ebro), *Gammarus duebeni* (western Brittany) and members of the [[Gammarus Pulex-Group]].
- Vincent (1971) found that *E. berilloni* was never recorded where Ca²⁺ was below 15 mg/l. At 4 °C it could not compensate for calcium loss during moulting when external Ca²⁺ was below 20–25 mg/l. At 15 °C the minimum was about 6 mg/l. Dense populations occurred in Brittany waters with under 6 mg Ca/l.
- Recorded pH ranged from 4.5 (Brittany) to 8 (Vendée). Summer temperatures near 31 °C were recorded in parts of Spain.
- *E. berilloni* tolerates salinities up to 80% seawater (about 24.8‰ S) and occurs in north-western Spanish estuaries and on sandy beaches. It also withstands substantial organic pollution.
- *E. calvus* prefers fast-running upper and middle reaches. It was found at Cl⁻ 2–70 mg/l and Ca 8–134 mg/l.
- *E. meridionalis* is known from only two Portuguese sites in Leiria province, both slow-running streams with Ca 13–81 mg/l.
- *E. echinosetosus* and *E. margalefi* occur in highly mineralized, Ca-rich waters. *E. margalefi* ranges from 1600 m altitude (summer temperatures under 11 °C) to lowland water at 25.5 °C, suggesting a wide temperature tolerance.
- *E. aquilifer* lives under stones in narrow, clear, fast upper reaches of mountain streams.
- *E. zebrinus* is restricted to the Mont-de-Marsan area of south-western France but tolerates Ca²⁺ from 3.3 to 69.8 mg/l and a range of bottoms.

**Biogeography**

- The Iberian Peninsula has two main amphipod distribution areas: a southern one of the [[Echinogammarus Simoni-Group]] and a northern one of the *E. berilloni*-group. Their overlap is narrow.
- *E. lusitanus* (simoni-group) is isolated in soft, acidic waters in the north-western corner of the peninsula.
- The author argues that the *E. berilloni*-group originated in the western Pyrenees. *E. berilloni* s.str. is the most recent invader, as shown by its occurrence in downstream reaches.
- Zonation is clear in the western Pyrenees. In more extreme interior climates it is weak, and only one species usually occurs per river system.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic revision:** the *E. berilloni* group contains at least 10 species. Nine are restricted to small areas in northern Iberia or adjacent France, while *E. berilloni* s.str. has a wide distribution.
2. **New species and rank changes:** *E. aquilifer* (1969) is confirmed. The paper describes *E. meridionalis*, *E. longisetosus*, *E. tarragonensis*, *E. feminatus*, *E. echinosetosus* and *E. margalefi* as new species, and raises *E. calvus* to species rank.
3. **Identification key:** a dichotomous key separates the species on adult-male characters, mainly antennal flagellum setation, gnathopod spination and leg 5–7 setae.
4. **Absence in southern Iberia:** despite intensive sampling, no members were found in southern Iberia, where they appear to be replaced by the African-derived simoni-group.
5. **Ecological partitioning:** species sort along stream zones, from *E. aquilifer* in upper western Pyrenean reaches to *E. berilloni* in lower reaches. Overlap zones (about 4 km in the Nive) suggest limited competition.
6. **Calcium as a limiting factor:** Vincent (1971) showed that low temperature combined with low Ca²⁺ restricts *E. berilloni*, though this does not explain its distribution in Brittany or its absence from the Vilaine and Loire systems.
7. **Physiological races:** the author infers that adaptive races of *E. berilloni* exist for high and low Ca²⁺ and pH, as already reported for *Gammarus pulex*.
8. **Tolerances:** the species tolerates high salinity, temperature and organic pollution, consistent with its inferred marine invasion history.
9. **Relict fauna:** the author proposes that the Iberian *Eulimnogammarus* species and the *Gammarus pulex*-group are relics of a formerly widespread Eurasian limnic fauna.
10. **Unresolved record:** *E. pacaudi*, previously known from the northern Pyrenees to the Garonne, was discovered in an irrigation ditch near Chilches (Castellón), far from its known range.
11. **Sample base:** more than 500 samples were examined for *E. berilloni*. The *E. longisetosus* description used 45 samples. Each species description includes station-level data on Cl⁻, Ca²⁺, pH and temperature.
