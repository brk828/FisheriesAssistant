## Executive Summary

This note documents a 1949 stomach content study of angler-caught [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*) from Donner Lake, California, conducted incidentally during research on the [[Kokanee Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*) fishery. The study aimed to assess whether lake trout, a species introduced to California waters beginning in 1895 (Lake Tahoe) and subsequently established in Donner Lake, exert significant predation pressure on kokanee. Analysis of 27 non-empty stomachs (of 31 collected) revealed that fish prey—overwhelmingly the [[Tui Chub]] (*Siphateles bicolor*)—dominated the diet by volume (98.2%), while kokanee itself was notably absent from identified prey items. The findings suggest seasonal prey-switching dynamics linked to thermal stratification and tui chub spawning migrations.

## Background & Description

- **Sample size:** 44 lake trout checked over a 37-day productive fishing period (near-total season catch); 31 stomachs collected for diet analysis.
- **Average fork length:** 17.7 inches
- **Fork length range:** 11.0–28.0 inches
- **Total catch weight:** ~110 lbs (~0.1 lb per surface acre in the 750-acre lake; lake surface area fluctuates 650–862 acres due to reservoir storage use)
- **Stomach condition:** 4 of 31 stomachs (12.9%) were empty at capture

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** [[Donner Lake]], a high-elevation reservoir/lake in Nevada County, California, used for water storage, with fluctuating surface area (650–862 acres).
- **Introduction history:** Lake trout were first introduced to California in May 1895 via a planting of 65,000 fry in [[Lake Tahoe]]; concurrent plantings occurred in Donner Lake.
- **Seasonal context:** The 1949 fishing season opened May 1, roughly two weeks post ice-out, coinciding with the spring overturn. The last lake trout of the season was caught June 6, as the lake was entering thermal stratification. Legal season extended to October 31, but catch productivity was concentrated in the initial 37 days.
- **Prey availability and thermal stratification:**
  - [[Tui Chub]] inhabit deep water until stratification is established and surface temperatures reach ~60°F, after which they migrate to shallow water to spawn.
  - Surface temperature recorded June 8, 1949 was 57°F, just prior to this shift.
  - Tui chub remain in shallow habitat until approximately the fall overturn, making them seasonally inaccessible to lake trout for roughly four months of the year.
  - During periods of tui chub inaccessibility, the author hypothesizes that lake trout predation on thermocline-dwelling [[Kokanee Salmon]] may increase beyond what the spring stomach data reflect.

## Key Findings

**Table 1 — Foods Consumed by Lake Trout in Donner Lake (n=27 stomachs)**

| Class of food | Frequency of occurrence | % Frequency of occurrence | % Total volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fish (total)** | 27 | 100 | 98.2 |
| [[Tui Chub]] (*Siphateles bicolor*) | 16 | 39.2 | 89.2 |
| [[Lahontan Redside]] (*Richardsonius egregius*) | 1 | 3.7 | 2.4 |
| [[Lahontan Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus* sp.) | 2 | 7.4 | 1.4 |
| [[Piute Sculpin]] (*Cottus beldingi*) | 1 | 3.7 | 1.2 |
| Salmonids (unspecified) | 2 | 7.4 | 0.6 |
| Unidentified fish | — | 40.7 | — |
| Surface foods (insects) | 3 | 11.1 | 0.04 |
| Bottom foods | 1 | 3.7 | 0.24 |

- **Fish dominate the diet:** Fish prey accounted for 98.2% of total stomach volume and occurred in 100% of non-empty stomachs, confirming lake trout in Donner Lake are primarily piscivorous.
- **Tui chub is the primary prey item:** *Siphateles bicolor* alone constituted 89.2% of total volume consumed, despite appearing in only 39.2% of stomachs — indicating large individual prey mass per feeding event.
- **No direct evidence of kokanee predation:** Kokanee salmon were not identified among prey items in this spring sample, suggesting either low predation rates during this period or limited spatial/temporal overlap between lake trout and kokanee during spring conditions.
- **Invertebrate and bottom foods are minor contributors:** Surface insects and bottom foods together comprised less than 0.3% of total dietary volume, underscoring the near-total reliance on fish prey.
- **Seasonal predation dynamics inferred:** The author (Kimsey) suggests that spring sampling likely underrepresents kokanee predation, as tui chub—the dominant prey—are only available in deep water (and thus accessible to lake trout) outside their ~4-month shallow-spawning period; during tui chub's shallow residency, lake trout may shift more heavily toward kokanee in the thermocline.
- **Management implication:** These findings informed early assessments of lake trout impact on introduced kokanee fisheries in California's Sierra Nevada lakes, highlighting the need for multi-seasonal diet sampling to capture full predatory impact.
