# Hybridization in Native Cyprinid Fishes: Gila ditaenia and Gila sp. in Northwestern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study describes putative hybrids between the [[Sonora Chub]] ([[Gila Ditaenia]]) and an apparently undescribed congener, [[Gila Sp]], in the [[Río de la Concepción Basin]] of Sonora, Mexico. Hybridization appears localized and does not seem to threaten the genetic integrity of *G. ditaenia*, which is federally listed as threatened in the United States. Based on morphological evidence, the authors conclude that *Gila* sp. is likely endemic to the Concepción basin rather than introduced, as previously believed. The authors relate the pattern to habitat differences, with *G. ditaenia* using streams and *Gila* sp. using ciénega marshes, and to loss of ciénega habitat over the past century.

## Background & Description

*Gila ditaenia* is abundant in Mexico, where its conservation status appears relatively secure ([[Hendrickson And Juárez-Romero 1990]]). In Arizona, it is restricted to a few kilometers of intermittent stream in Sycamore (Bear) Canyon near the international boundary and is listed as threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]] ([[United States Fish And Wildlife Service 1986]]).

*Gila* sp. was originally reported as an introduced population of *G. purpurea* and later referred to *G. eremica* ([[DeMarais 1991]]). The authors argue it is distinct from *G. eremica*, which shows negligible morphological variation within or between basins.

**Methods**
- Standard length (SL) was used throughout.
- 25 morphometric variables were measured on preserved specimens of *G. purpurea* (Río Yaqui basin), *G. intermedia* (Gila River basin), *G. ditaenia*, *G. eremica* (Ríos Mátape and Sonora basins), *Gila* sp., and putative hybrids.
- Only individuals 50 to 126 mm SL were analyzed. Sexes were pooled after removing two sexually dimorphic fin-length variables.
- Data were analyzed by principal components analysis (PCA), sheared to remove overall size effects ([[Humphries Et Al 1981]]; [[Bookstein Et Al 1985]]), using the covariance matrix of log10-transformed measurements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat partitioning:** *G. ditaenia* shows a marked preference for streams. *Gila* sp. appears restricted to spring- and seepage-fed ciénegas with dense vegetation and almost no current. The authors suggest that *Gila* sp. avoids dispersing through flowing water, which may have isolated it in ciénegas.
- **Habitat loss:** The rarity of *Gila* sp., and possibly its hybridization with *G. ditaenia*, may reflect ciénega loss from natural and human-induced drainage over the past century ([[Hendrickson And Minckley 1985]]).
- **Vegetation overgrowth:** Declining water tables may have allowed vegetation to overgrow Ciénega la Atascosa. This could have pushed *Gila* sp. into less suitable habitat and into contact with *G. ditaenia*.
- **Biogeography:** Stream captures across basin-and-range structure may explain shared distributions in Sonoran drainages ([[Minckley Et Al 1986]]). The Concepción basin shares *Agosia chrysogaster* and two *Poeciliopsis* species with the Río Sonora, and *G. ditaenia* and an undescribed *Poeciliopsis* complete its known native ichthyofauna.
- **Introduction concern:** Early absence of *Gila* sp. from Concepción collections, nearby *G. eremica* sources, roadside sites, and widespread transfaunation in the adjacent United States ([[Courtney And Stauffer 1984]]) led the authors to suspect introduction. Hybridization between native and introduced congeners is common ([[Hubbs 1955]]).

## Key Findings

**1. Morphometric separation of *G. ditaenia* and *Gila* sp.**
- PC1 (size) accounted for 96.5% of total variation.
- Sheared PC2 (H2, shape) accounted for 1.8% and loaded most heavily on caudal-peduncle length and vertical-fin lengths. All three were greater in *G. ditaenia* than in *Gila* sp.

**2. Lateral-line scale counts**
- *Gila* sp.: mean 56 (range 51–60).
- *G. ditaenia*: mean almost 68 (range 62–76).
- Lateral-line counts combined with H2 gave strong separation. Putative hybrids were generally intermediate.

**3. Geographic variation**
- *G. ditaenia* showed no conspicuous geographic variation across five widely separated localities.
- Ojo Caliente (1971): the sample was mostly putative hybrids. Three fell within the *G. ditaenia* range and two were clearly or almost within the *Gila* sp. range.
- Ciénega la Atascosa (1981 and 1983): the two species were essentially sympatric. The 1983 collection contained only two possible hybrids.
- Arroyo upstream of la Atascosa (1986): only *G. ditaenia*, with no hybrid influence detected.
- Ciénega la Atascosa (1986): vegetative overgrowth had eliminated open water, and no *Gila* sp. was found.
- La Providencia (1986): one individual appeared to be a hybrid. All other specimens were clearly *G. ditaenia*.
- The authors interpret hybridization as local and limited. Specimens from sites with suspected hybridization were no more variable than those far away, indicating little or no introgression of hybrid characters.

**4. Comparison with related species**
- PC1 (size) accounted for 95.2% of variation. Sheared H2 and H3 accounted for 2.1% and 1.6%.
- *G. eremica* and *Gila* sp. separated from the other species on H2, mainly as a contrast between upper-jaw length and caudal-peduncle length.
- *Gila* sp. was completely separated from *G. eremica* of the Ríos Mátape and Sonora basins on H3.
- The authors conclude that *Gila* sp. is distinct from other regional species and likely endemic to the Concepción basin. Its phenotypes do not trend toward *G. ditaenia*, so genetic exchange cannot explain its differences.

**5. Limitations**
- Collections near the hybridization zone are too few for temporal or geographic interpretation.
- Specimens from La Providencia (1983), on which the 1983 *Gila* sp. records were based, appear to be lost.

**6. Material examined (summary)**
- Lots are catalogued in the Arizona State University collection (ASU). The list covers 8 species-level localities, with counts ranging from 4 to 31 specimens per lot. Examples include *G. ditaenia* at Arroyo Agua Caliente (ASU 11031, 24 specimens, November 1986), Arroyo la Atascosa (ASU 11039, 30 specimens), and Ciénega la Providencia (ASU 11034, 27 specimens). *Gila* sp. lots include Ciénega la Atascosa (ASU 9004, 8 specimens) and Ojo Caliente (ASU 6466, 12 specimens, September 1971).
