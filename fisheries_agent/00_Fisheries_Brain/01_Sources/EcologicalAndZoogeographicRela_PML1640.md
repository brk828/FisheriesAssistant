# Ecological and Zoogeographic Relationships of Amphibians and Reptiles of the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin

#herpetology #zoogeography #amphibians-reptiles

## Executive Summary

This paper analyzes the herpetofauna of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], an interior, closed-drainage valley in Coahuila, Mexico. The fauna comprises 66 native species of amphibians and reptiles, plus introduced species. Ecologically, the fauna is dominated by desert forms, but aquatic, semiaquatic and riparian species add substantial diversity. Geographically, most species have [[Chihuahuan Desert]] affinities, while the Tamaulipan (lowland) element is smaller but includes most of the Basin's endemics. The author characterizes the fauna as a mosaic of relict populations and successive faunal invasions. The analysis is explicitly provisional because the fauna is poorly sampled.

## Background & Description

The Basin lies at an average elevation of about 740 m and is ringed by mountains rising to roughly 2,500 m. It is often assumed to be typical of the [[Chihuahuan Desert]], but the author argues that this impression is "vastly misleading" once aquatic habitats are considered. Its spring-fed wetlands form a hydrologically distinct island of aquatic habitat within the desert.

Biological exploration began in 1939. Systematic herpetological study expanded after a 1950s University of Kansas expedition, led by [[W. L. Minckley]], whose work centered on fishes and other aquatic organisms. The author's own studies date from two expeditions in the 1970s, followed by repeated short trips. Nearly 100 scientific papers had been published on the Basin's biota, but several species were still known from only a handful of specimens.

**Scope and definitions**
- The herpetofauna is defined as species occurring naturally below 1,000 m in the Basin and the adjacent Sacramento Basin.
- Above 1,000 m, species such as *Sceloporus grammicus*, *Sceloporus poinsetti* and *Gerrhonotus liocephalus* are montane relicts of the northern Chihuahuan Desert and are excluded.
- Two species, *Hemidactylus turcicus* and *Bufo marinus*, are introduced. *Drymarchon corais* is tentatively included on a single specimen. Captive *Gopherus* records are regarded as escaped pets.

**Taxonomic composition (per the abstract):** 8 amphibians, 4 turtles, 23 lizards and 31 snakes, totaling 66 native species. The body text OCR reads "28" lizards, which does not reconcile with the 66-species total; the abstract figure is used here.

**Ecological categories**
1. **Desert:** widespread, not habitat-limited.
2. **Riparian:** not aquatic, but restricted to watercourses or high subsurface water.
3. **Semiaquatic:** marsh, sedge-mat and lagoon-margin species that tolerate temporary dry periods.
4. **Aquatic:** require permanent standing or flowing water.

**Geographic categories**
- **Chihuahuan:** with subcategories for Basin endemics, Chihuahuan Desert endemics, Chihuahuan–Great Plains species and widespread species.
- **Tamaulipan:** with subcategories for Basin endemics, Gulf-edge species and species extending across the Great Plains to the desert edge.
- **Ubiquitous:** coast-to-coast distributions extending onto the Mexican plateau.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Desert and aquatic gradient.** Of the 66 species, 41 (62%) are desert forms, 13 (20%) are riparian, 6 (9%) are semiaquatic and 6 (9%) are aquatic. Six of the eight amphibians are desert species. The only riparian amphibian is [[Bufo Valliceps]]. Among snakes, the aquatic forms are *Nerodia erythrogaster* and *N. rhombifera*. The aquatic amphibian is a *Rana* (*pipiens* group) species, and the aquatic turtles are *Trionyx ater*, *T. spiniferus* and [[Pseudemys Scripta Taylori]]. No lizards are aquatic. The author estimates that aquatic and wetland habitats raise herpetofaunal diversity by roughly a third.

**Relict populations.** About 39% of the fauna consists of relict mesic-adapted populations isolated at the desert edge. These include *Eumeces tetragrammus*, *Coluber constrictor*, *Micrurus fulvius* and *Sistrurus catenatus*, which are relictual but morphologically undifferentiated.

**Faunal boundary.** The Tamaulipan–Chihuahuan transition is unusually sharp here. Of the Tamaulipan species, 77% (14 species) reach their western limit in the Basin, around Puerto Salado. Sixteen Chihuahuan species (38%) reach their eastern limit at the Basin's edge. Hybridization between *Cnemidophorus gularis* and *C. scalaris* occurs where they meet.

**Differentiation patterns.** Divergence ranges from the highly distinct [[Coahuilan Box Turtle]] (*Terrapene coahuila*), whose phylogenetic position remains debated, to intergrading *Pseudemys scripta taylori*, which intergrades with *P. s. elegans* near Puerto Salado. *Trionyx ater* hybridizes with the invading *T. spiniferus*. The author describes the overall pattern as "a mosaic of differentiation" that results from successive faunal invasions.

## Key Findings

- **Fauna size and composition:** 66 native amphibian and reptile species (8 amphibians, 4 turtles, 23 lizards, 31 snakes) plus two introduced species, *Hemidactylus turcicus* and *Bufo marinus*.
- **Ecological profile:** 62% desert, 20% riparian, 9% semiaquatic, 9% aquatic. Aquatic, semiaquatic and riparian species together account for roughly 40% of the fauna.
- **Geographic profile:** 64% (42 species) Chihuahuan, 27% (18 species) Tamaulipan, 9% (6 species) ubiquitous.
- **Endemism:** Only about 10% of the fauna is endemic. Two Chihuahuan forms are Basin endemics: *Gerrhonotus lugoi* and *Cnemidophorus scalaris pallidus*. Four Tamaulipan Basin endemics are *Trionyx ater*, *Terrapene coahuila*, *Pseudemys scripta taylori* and *Scincella lateralis* ssp. Most Basin endemics have Tamaulipan affinities.
- **Relict populations:** 39% of the fauna. The text also states that about 11% of species are differentiated at a taxonomically recognizable level, though this figure does not match the five species listed.
- **Biogeographic conclusion:** The Basin's herpetofauna is "superficially typical" of the Chihuahuan Desert, but its aquatic habitats and position at the eastern edge of the Chihuahuan Desert Province produce a diverse, layered assemblage shaped by successive invasions.
- **Provisional status:** Several species are known from few specimens, and genetic comparisons of relict populations are needed before the invasion history can be reconstructed.
