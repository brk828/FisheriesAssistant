# Central Arizona Project Fish and Wildlife Enhancement Opportunities Meeting Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #native-fish #riparian-habitat #water-policy

## Executive Summary

This document summarizes a July 19, 1993 meeting convened by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] [[Arizona Projects Office]] to discuss fish and wildlife enhancement opportunities associated with the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP). The meeting brought together federal agencies, state wildlife managers, water districts, and conservation organizations to identify potential enhancement projects under the authority of the [[Colorado River Basin Project Act]] (P.L. 90-537) and the [[Federal Water Project Recreation Act]] (P.L. 89-72). Five general categories of enhancement opportunities were identified for further investigation: (1) [[Lower Colorado River]] mainstem/backwaters/[[Cienega de Santa Clara]], (2) inland native fish enhancement, (3) authorized project feature areas ([[Charleston Dam]] and [[Hooker Dam]] areas), (4) [[Agua Fria River]]/[[Lake Pleasant]]/urban fishing/recharge, and (5) recharge in [[Tucson]]. The document includes detailed legal authorities, cost-sharing requirements (75% Federal/25% local for fish and wildlife enhancement), water contracting procedures, and various implementation scenarios.

## Background & Description

The [[Central Arizona Project]] is a major water conveyance system transporting [[Colorado River]] water to central Arizona. Section 301(a) of the Colorado River Basin Project Act authorizes the Secretary of the Interior to "construct, operate, and maintain the Central Arizona Project" for multiple purposes including "conservation and development of fish and wildlife resources, enhancement of recreation opportunities." The meeting was organized by Bruce Ellis, Chief of the Environmental Division, in response to discussions with environmental resource organizations and the Governor's Advisory Committee on CAP Environmental Working Group.

The [[Federal Water Project Recreation Act]] of 1965 (amended 1974) provides the regulatory framework for enhancement projects, requiring that: (1) enhancement must be associated with the Federal water project, (2) benefits must meet or exceed construction costs, (3) a local cost-sharing partner must cover capital costs, and (4) the local partner must pay for and be responsible for all operation and maintenance (O&M). Cost-sharing is set at 75% Federal/25% local for fish and wildlife enhancement versus 50/50 for recreation. The [[Reclamation Readjustment Act of 1992]] (P.L. 102-575) allows 50% of O&M costs to be funded by Reclamation under certain circumstances, though no appropriations had been allocated as of the meeting date.

Bob Michaels noted that approximately $15 million might be available nationwide by 1995 under the new act. The CAP authorization does not specify a fixed amount for environmental enhancement, though the 1964 PSWP Supplemental Report identified approximately $11 million (equivalent to $50 million in 1993 dollars) for fish and wildlife enhancement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Multiple enhancement opportunities were discussed spanning diverse aquatic habitats:

**[[Cienega de Santa Clara]]**: Glen Gould described this wetland in [[Sonora]], [[Mexico]], created by highly saline drainage water from the [[Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation and Drainage District]] transported via bypass drain. Prior to discharge initiation, approximately 500 acres of vegetation existed; the wetland has expanded to over 6,100 acres. The water is considered too saline to deliver to Mexico under international agreement. [[Bureau of Reclamation]] was studying whether operation of the [[Yuma Desalinization Plant]] would significantly impact existing resources, with preliminary answers anticipated by end of 1993.

**Native Fish Enhancement**: Multiple participants emphasized the need for reintroduction of native fish and stream restoration. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] indicated Section 7 consultation would be needed to determine impacts to native fish for various proposals. Concerns were raised about ensuring activities do not adversely impact inland native fish species. Specific locations mentioned included the upper [[Gila River]] and streams in authorized project feature areas.

**Recharge Projects with Riparian/Wetland Components**: Grant Ward ([[Central Arizona Water Conservation District]]) identified several recharge projects: [[Agua Fria River]], participation in [[Salt River Project]]'s [[Granite Reef Underground Storage Project]], [[San Xavier District]]/[[Santa Cruz River]], and [[City of Tucson]]. CAWCD was awaiting determination from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] regarding impacts to native fish before proceeding. Mark Myers described studies in the Tucson area including preliminary design work on a recharge project incorporating riparian habitat restoration desired by the [[San Xavier District]] ([[Tohono O'odham Nation]]). Multiple benefits were identified: wetlands creation/recharge in [[Canada del Oro]], effluent wetlands creation/recharge in [[Brawley Wash]] in [[Avra Valley]], creation of flyway habitat along the [[Santa Cruz River]].

**Urban Fisheries**: Proposals included opening portions of CAP to angler access and stocking with game fish, urban fishery opportunities in both [[Phoenix]] and [[Tucson]], supplemental water to [[Picacho Reservoir]] (especially in winter for waterfowl), improvement of water quality in [[Lakeside Lake]] (near Tucson), and increased flows on the [[Salt River]] between [[Stewart Mountain Dam]] and [[Granite Reef Dam]] for improved sports fishery.

**[[Lower Colorado River]] Mainstem**: Opportunities included creation of backwaters, flattening out flows, and enhancing habitat between [[Laguna Dam]] and [[Yuma]]. Concerns were raised about how far downstream water would flow if left in the mainstem and potential for uncontrolled diversions in Mexico.

**[[San Pedro River]]/[[Sierra Vista]]**: Discussion of arresting the increasing cone of depression that will eventually affect the river through effluent recharge/wetland projects or delivery of CAP water to [[Sierra Vista]] to replenish the groundwater table. Rob Smith stated for the record that not everyone supported this approach.

Environmental concerns emphasized that enhancement should not support subdivision development, must endure in perpetuity, should avoid creating "riparian disneyland," and must ensure water additions to streams do not degrade systems by picking up and transporting toxics downstream.

## Key Findings

**Five Priority Enhancement Categories Identified**:
1. [[Lower Colorado River]] mainstem/backwaters/[[Cienega de Santa Clara]]
2. Inland native fish enhancement
3. Authorized project feature areas (e.g., [[Charleston Dam]] and [[Hooker Dam]] areas)
4. [[Agua Fria River]]/[[Lake Pleasant]]/urban fishing/Phoenix area recharge
5. Recharge in [[Tucson]]

**Legal and Administrative Framework**:
- Authority for CAP fish and wildlife enhancement derives from [[Colorado River Basin Project Act]] (P.L. 90-537) Sections 102 and 301(a)
- Current interpretation requires expenditure of CAP appropriations to be associated with authorized project features (either currently constructed, to be constructed in future, or in lieu of features not constructed for water supply)
- Authorized geographic locations include: [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]], [[Orme Dam]] (replaced by Modified [[Roosevelt Dam]] and New [[Waddell Dam]]), [[Buttes Dam]] (on [[Gila River]] between [[Coolidge Dam]] and CAP canal crossing), [[Hooker Dam]] (New Mexico on Gila River system), [[Charleston Dam]] (on [[San Pedro River]] near [[Sierra Vista]]), [[Tucson Aqueduct]], [[Salt-Gila Aqueduct]], and related works
- Section 309(b) provides approximately $110 million appropriation authority for distribution systems under 100% cost recovery with 20% cost-sharing requirement; unclear if this can be used for environmental enhancement

**Cost-Sharing Requirements**:
- 25% of separable costs allocated to fish and wildlife enhancement borne by non-Federal bodies
- Not less than 50% of operation, maintenance and replacement costs borne by non-Federal bodies
- 75% of separable costs and all joint costs allocated to recreation and fish and wildlife enhancement borne by United States as nonreimbursable
- If no additional costs to CAP result from enhancement, no cost sharing necessary
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] had not yet developed procedures for seeking appropriations O&M assistance for environmental enhancement

**Water Availability and Contracting**:
- Approximately 60,000 acre-feet of Municipal and Industrial (M&I) priority water not under contract (declined or forfeited); approximately 40,000-45,000 acre-feet expected to be retained for settling Indian water rights claims
- 29.3% of CAP agricultural water not yet under contract; uncertain how much will be contracted due to agricultural sector financial difficulties
- Agricultural water available after M&I and Indian uses satisfied; availability will diminish as M&I and Indian use builds to full amount (projected 2025-2030)
- CAP water contracting not limited to particular project features; can be used anywhere within CAP service area either directly or via exchange
- Colorado River water (not transported via CAP) can be used anywhere along mainstem Colorado River within Arizona, exchanged, or transported from mainstem

**Water Costs**:
- Fixed cost O&M approximately $30 million per year, or $20 per acre-foot if 1.5 million acre-feet available for delivery
- Agricultural contractors expected to pay most costs in early years but financial difficulties may require redistribution among remaining participants
- Energy costs estimated at $37 per acre-foot, paid only on water delivered

**Implementation Process**:
1. Planning process with public involvement to identify and evaluate potential activities
2. Compliance with Federal environmental statutes ([[National Environmental Policy Act]], [[Endangered Species Act]], [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]])
3. Selection process requiring Arizona Projects Office report documenting costs, benefits, cost-sharing arrangements
4. Approval by Regional Director and Commissioner of Reclamation
5. Appropriations process taking at least two years from Commissioner approval to Congressional funding
6. Implementation upon receipt of appropriations and cost-sharing partner funds

**Role of [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]**:
- Consultation required under [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] (1946)
- USFWS provides report assessing possible damage, mitigation necessities, and opportunities for wildlife development and improvement
- USFWS estimates costs and benefits of recommended measures
- Implementation requires positive benefit-cost ratio

**Six Implementation Scenarios Outlined** with varying requirements for water service contracts, cost-sharing under Federal Water Project Recreation Act, and CAP separable enhancement facility repayment contracts under 9(d) program, ranging from mainstem use with no new facilities to Central Arizona use with both mainstem and 9(d) facilities constructed.

**Follow-up Meetings Scheduled**:
- July 30, 1993: [[Lower Colorado River]] mainstem/backwaters/[[Cienega de Santa Clara]]
- August 16, 1993: Authorized project feature areas
- Additional meetings for remaining three categories postponed pending establishment of overall management process for the enhancement program

**[[Central Arizona Water Conservation District]] Environmental Policy** (draft): Some water should be allocated for environmental enhancement; capital portion should be made non-reimbursable; O&M costs should not be paid by water contractors/subcontractors but by local cost-share partner.

**[[Cienega de Santa Clara]] Issues**: Use of Colorado River water destined for CAP to maintain the Cienega would result in: (1) reduction in CAP supply, (2) potential effect on CAWCD's repayment ability unless corresponding reduction in repayment obligation, (3) possible adverse effect on California water users who currently use Arizona's unused entitlement, (4) delivery of Colorado River water to Mexico in excess of 1.5 million acre-feet annual treaty commitment.
