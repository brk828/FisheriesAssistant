# Notropis asperifrons: A New Cyprinid Fish from the Mobile Bay Drainage of Alabama and Georgia, with Studies of Related Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 taxonomic paper by [[Royal D. Suttkus]] and [[Edward C. Raney]] (Tulane Studies in Zoology, Vol. 3, No. 1) describes *[[Notropis Asperifrons]]*, a new minnow from the [[Mobile Bay Drainage]] of Alabama and Georgia. The authors compare it with four closely related lowland and upland shiners, provide a dichotomous key to nine small *Notropis* species sharing a typical tooth formula (2,4–4,2), 7 or 8 anal rays and a dark lateral band, and reassess the nomenclatural history of [[Notropis Xaenocephalus]], whose original type series proved to include *N. asperifrons*.

## Background & Description

- **Study group:** Nine small *Notropis* from the eastern Gulf drainages, from Apalachicola Bay (Florida and Georgia) to Mobile Bay (Alabama and Mississippi). Many are common and some serve as bait fishes, but their systematics had been confused.
- **Prior work:** [[Bailey Et Al 1954]] clarified *N. roseus*, *N. petersoni*, *N. xaenocephalus* and *N. chalybaeus*; [[Suttkus And Raney 1955]] described *N. baileyi* and *N. hypsilepis*.
- **Type material:** Holotype CU 28262, an adult female, 50 mm standard length, from Holly Creek at Ramhurst, Murray County, Georgia (June 12, 1952). The type series comprises 91 specimens (28–60 mm SL) from 12 localities in the Alabama River system. An additional 69 specimens (22–55 mm SL) came from nine localities in the Black Warrior River system.
- **Diagnosis:** Small species, to 60 mm SL, with 2,4–4,2 teeth and 7 anal rays. Dorsal 8, pectoral 13–14, pelvic 8, caudal 19 rays. Lateral line complete; anterior lateral-line scales (especially 2 and 3) elevated. Typical lateral-line scales 36; circumferential scales before dorsal 19–21; caudal peduncle 12. Mouth inferior; prominent basicaudal spot continuous with the lateral band; mid-dorsal streak obsolescent.
- **Nuptial tubercles:** Males bear dense white tubercles on the snout, larger than those on the body, plus dense tubercle bands on the upper surface of the first seven or eight pectoral rays, which may aid in gripping females during spawning.
- **Etymology:** *asperifrons* from Latin *asper* (rough) and *frons* (forehead), referring to the tuberculate snout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range:** Apparently restricted to the Mobile Bay drainage: the upper Coastal Plain, Piedmont and mountain-type headwater tributaries of the Coosa River in the Alabama River system, and Piedmont streams near Tuscaloosa, Cullman and Oneonta in the Black Warrior system.
- **Habitat:** Clear streams about 4 to 50 feet wide, with rubble, bedrock or sand bottoms, flowing through wooded areas.
- **Associations:** Collected alongside *N. xaenocephalus*, *N. roseus*, *N. chrosomus* or *N. baileyi* at several sites. Sympatric with *N. roseus* in the Black Warrior and Alabama systems.
- **Evolutionary context:** The authors propose that *N. roseus*-like stock gave rise to *asperifrons*, *hypsilepis* and *xaenocephalus*, possibly via upstream invasions of lowland populations. *N. chalybaeus* and *N. roseus* probably diverged early, with *chalybaeus* later reinvading the western lowlands. *N. petersoni* likely derived from a Pleistocene invasion of the Atlantic Coastal Plain by roseus stock and shows tolerance of saline water, which aided its coastwise dispersal into peninsular Florida.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *N. asperifrons* is separated from *N. xaenocephalus* by an inferior mouth, elevated anterior lateral-line scales, a longer and slimmer body, a more acute muzzle, shorter fins, an elongate caudal spot, obsolescent predorsal marks, a much lower circumferential scale count, and a lower pectoral ray count.
- **Tooth variation:** The holotype has the typical 2,4–4,2 formula, but counts vary. Of 27 counts in the study, 2,4–4,2 occurred 15 times, with reductions in the main row being unusual. The authors note the reduction trend also appears in northern *N. roseus*.
- **Nomenclature:** Jordan's 1877 description of *N. xaenocephalus* combined two species. The lectotype of *N. xaenocephalus* is USNM 20116 (50.1 mm SL). The lone *N. asperifrons* in that series became paratype USNM 164969, and a 45.5 mm specimen from a second Jordan series became paratype USNM 164968.
- **Misidentifications:** Gilbert (1891) overlooked *N. asperifrons* and misidentified it, *N. roseus* and *N. baileyi* material from the Black Warrior system.
- **Ontogeny:** Jordan's two "forms" of *N. xaenocephalus* were judged to represent size and probably age extremes, based on 677 specimens from 28 collections.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** In *N. asperifrons*, male pectoral and pelvic fins are longer; the male pelvic tip extends beyond the anus, unlike in females.
- **Key:** A dichotomous key separates nine species on anal ray count, mouth position, lateral band, basicaudal spot, nuptial tubercles and range. It includes [[Notropis Hypsilepis]], [[Notropis Baileyi]], [[Notropis Petersoni]], [[Notropis Roseus]] and [[Notropis Chalybaeus]], among others.
- **Taxonomic conclusion:** The authors deliberately leave the Flint River population of *N. roseus* unnamed, pending range-wide study, though they consider it possibly worthy of subspecific status.
