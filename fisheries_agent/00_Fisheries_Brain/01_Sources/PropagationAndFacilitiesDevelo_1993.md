## Executive Summary

This FY93 annual report documents the **Propagation and Facilities Development Project** (Project No. 24) of the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], focused on captive breeding, refuge maintenance, and experimental rearing of four endangered big-river fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. Work was conducted at two facilities — the **Ouray Hatchery** (broodstock/refuge propagation) and the **Horsethief Ponds** (grow-out, refugia, and disease/rearing trials). The report emphasizes artificial propagation as an essential recovery tool for species that have lost the ability to spawn, reproduce, or recruit successfully in the wild, and details significant progress alongside substantial setbacks from disease outbreaks, bird predation, and gas supersaturation events.

## Background & Description

While this report is primarily programmatic rather than morphometric, it notes specific **morphological anomalies** observed in a cohort of hatchery-reared [[Razorback Sucker]] originating from Colorado River arm (Lake Powell) broodstock:

- **Misshapen mouths**
- **Crooked spines**
- **Shortened gill covers**

These defects were suspected to result from **formalin treatments for *Costia* (Ichthyobodo)** during early life stages, though a genetic origin could not be ruled out. Of an original 108 fish in this group, only 25 remained, and these were deemed unsuitable for the proposed experimental stocking into the [[Gunnison River]] and [[Colorado River]] due to poor expected long-term survival.

Size/age classes referenced for [[Razorback Sucker]] production lots:
- Sub-adult (Green River, 1989–1991 year classes): 398 individuals
- YOY (1993 year class, Escalante Bar paired matings): 7,802 individuals across 13 lots
- Sub-adult (1992 San Juan year class): 1,664 individuals, ~75% survival over one growing season

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report situates these species within the **Upper Colorado River Basin** ecosystem, encompassing the Green, Yampa, San Juan, and main-stem Colorado rivers, as well as the Colorado River arm of **Lake Powell**. Key ecological and husbandry-relevant observations include:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** broodstock were sourced from multiple wild populations: Yampa River/Echo Park, Green River/Razorback Bar, and Lake Powell (San Juan and Colorado arms) — reflecting genetically distinct management units.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** showed heightened sensitivity to environmental stressors relative to co-occurring native and non-native species; in a nitrogen supersaturation event, all 13 bonytail adults in an affected pond died from **gas bubble disease**, while trout, fathead minnows, green sunfish, roundtail chub, and bluehead sucker in the *same pond* survived — indicating species-specific tolerance thresholds and cautioning against using tolerant species as sentinel indicators for T&E species health.
- **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (Colorado squawfish) juveniles (6–8 inches) were identified as **preferred prey for great blue herons**, resulting in the loss of ~2,000 "surplus" fish before pond netting was upgraded.
- Chemoreceptive **imprinting studies** used river-specific water sources (Gunnison River, Uncompahgre River) on fertilized razorback sucker eggs, suggesting olfactory imprinting research relevant to natal homing and stream-side spawning behavior (e.g., Escalante Bar).
- Wild adult **feral fish** were noted entering ponds via water inlets, prompting recommendations for filter socks and more frequent pond inventory/culling.

## Key Findings

**Refuge & Broodstock Status (End of FY93):**
- Razorback Sucker wild adults held at Ouray Hatchery:
  - Yampa/Echo Park: 1 male
  - Green River/Razorback Bar: 12 fish (sex uncertain for several; 2 lost during handling/spawning)
  - Lake Powell/San Juan: 9 (5M, 4F) — transferred to UDWR April 1993
  - Lake Powell/Colorado: 5 (3M, 2F)

**Propagation Output:**
- 7,802 YOY Razorback Sucker (1993 year class, Green River/Escalante Bar) produced across 13 paired-mating lots; 6 lots met minimum YOY criteria (500 fish) for overwinter survival benchmarks.
- Additional fish distributed for research: chemoreception imprinting studies (Dexter-origin Colorado squawfish and razorback sucker), spring habitat study (Old Charley Wash, 2,700 fry), contaminant studies (~3,600 eggs; 620 fry to Columbia Research Lab for irrigation drain water toxicity), and cage-culture technology development (14,352 fry).

**Horsethief Ponds Activity:**
- ~1,100 PIT-tagged Colorado Pikeminnow (F1 generation, Grand Valley wild stock) established as refugia population.
- 4,400 additional Colorado Pikeminnow used for rearing-protocol trials.
- 108 Razorback Sucker (Colorado River arm/Lake Powell progeny) — significant morphological defects reported (see above).
- 50 Bonytail Chub adults (F1, Dexter NFH broodstock) — **all lost to an "Ich" (*Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*) outbreak within 5 days** in August 1993.
- 13 wild-caught Razorback Sucker adults (Grand Valley area and Etter's Pond) added to Horsethief refugia.

**Disease & Environmental Challenges:**
- **Bacterial septicemia**: controlled via oxytetracycline injections in adult fish transferred between hatcheries.
- **"Ich" (*Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*)**: endemic in ponds, exacerbated by warm water; controlled in Colorado Pikeminnow with moderate losses (July), but caused total mortality in the Bonytail Chub cohort (August).
- **Lernaea (anchor worm)**: minor incidence, comparable to wild river populations.
- **Nitrogen gas supersaturation**: caused by pumping Colorado River water into ice-covered ponds; disproportionately lethal to Bonytail Chub (13 adults lost to gas bubble disease) versus other co-housed species.
- An **INAD (Investigational New Animal Drug) permit** application was pending for standard fish culture chemicals, with interim off-label prescriptions arranged via local veterinarian.

**Predation Management:**
- Complete pond netting (2" mesh polypropylene) proved effective against great blue heron predation after initial failures with perimeter-only netting.

**Budget:** FY93 funds provided and expended: **$219,000** (no variance).

**Recommendations for FY94:**
1. Attempt additional paired matings to complete a full 5×5 genetic matrix for Green River razorback sucker broodstock at Ouray (anticipated to take up to 5+ years based on San Juan/Colorado River Arm precedent).
2. Maintain backup populations for all fish held at Horsethief Ponds to mitigate disease risk.
3. Use excess Colorado Pikeminnow to test upper pond-capacity limits and prophylactic treatment intervals.
4. Avoid introducing fish during cold months (immune suppression); prioritize additional Bonytail Chub rearing trials before introducing wild [[Humpback Chub]] to Horsethief for refugia.
5. Increase pond rotation/inventory frequency to three times annually; continue filter sock use at water inlets.
6. Request that the Propagation Subcommittee formalize a fish disposition policy document for Biology Committee action.
