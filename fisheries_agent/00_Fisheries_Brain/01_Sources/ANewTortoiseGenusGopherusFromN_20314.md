# A New Tortoise, Genus Gopherus, from North-central Mexico

#herpetology #testudines #gopherus

## Executive Summary

John M. Legler ([[University of Kansas Museum of Natural History]], 1959) describes a previously unrecognized tortoise, *Gopherus flavomarginatus*, from north-central Mexico. The species is based on a [[Holotype]] and five [[Paratype]] specimens, mostly from [[Lerdo]], Durango, with additional material from Chihuahua and Coahuila. The new species is distinguished by its large, flat-topped and flared shell, pale laminae with dark centers, a longer intergular than interhumeral seam, and a relatively wide skull. Legler also argues that historical reports of *Gopherus polyphemus* from Chihuahua by [[Duges]] actually refer to this new species, and that its range extends the genus onto the Mexican highlands.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The study was undertaken during taxonomic work on the genus *Gopherus*. Specimens from north-central Mexico were found to belong to an unrecognized species. The paper raises the count of recognized *Gopherus* forms to four, alongside [[Gopherus Agassizii]], [[Gopherus Berlandieri]], and [[Gopherus Polyphemus]].

**Type material.**
- Holotype: [[U.S. National Museum]] (USNM) 61253, an adult stuffed specimen with a disassociated skull, collected 30 to 40 miles from Lerdo, Durango, by [[Elswood Chaffee]] in 1918.
- Paratypes (5): USNM 61254 (adult, skull in place, same locality as holotype); USNM 60976 (adult, Lerdo, May 1918); University of Illinois 42953-4 (two adult carapaces, Carrillo, Chihuahua, 1 August 1958); University of Kansas 39415 (adult carapace, 8 mi E and 2 mi S Americanos, Coahuila, 19 May 1954).

**Diagnostic features.**
- Flat-topped, posteriorly flared shell; carapace height slightly more than 50% of its length.
- Pale laminae with contrasting dark centers, especially on lateral margins.
- Intergular seam longer than interhumeral seam; truncate, non-pronglike gular projections.
- Single large, rectangular axillary scute, wider below than above.
- Skull greatest width slightly more than 80% of condylobasilar length; head width 1.3 to 1.7 times hind foot diameter.
- Enlarged osteodermal scales on the forearm in seven to eight longitudinal rows, and one enlarged femoral spur (the inner spur about half the size of the outer).

**Coloration.** Carapace ground color is pale yellow to straw in small specimens and pale brown in large ones. The plastron is pale, with black or dark brown blotches in younger animals that fade with age.

**Size.** The largest specimen (USNM 61254) exceeds the largest known *G. agassizii* by more than 50 mm in length.

**Anomalies.** The type series shows a high incidence of anomalies. Three specimens have a supernumerary central lamina, and all Durango specimens are anomalous in their marginal scutes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** The species is known from the three type localities and from an indefinite locality, "Bolson de Mapimi," Chihuahua, cited by Duges. Legler suggests it probably occurs across several internally drained basins in northeastern Chihuahua, western Coahuila, and northern Durango. The region lies within the [[Basin and Range Province]] and the [[Mapimi Biotic Province]]. The range is likely bounded by the [[Sierra Madre Occidental]] to the west, the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]] to the east, and the Mexican plateau to the south. Its northern limit is unclear.

**Range relationships.** The range does not appear to overlap that of any other *Gopherus*, although it closely approaches that of *G. berlandieri* in central Coahuila. Reported distances include about 410 miles east of Alamos, Sonora (*G. agassizii*), about 100 miles west of Monclova, Coahuila (*G. berlandieri*), and about 200 miles west of Monterrey, Nuevo León (*G. berlandieri*).

**Local knowledge.** In September 1958, near [[Cuatro Cienegas]], Coahuila, Legler questioned residents and an American rancher. Most had seen few or no tortoises. Older herders said tortoises ("Tortuga del Monte") were common in the country beyond the Sierra de La Madera and Sierra de La Fragua, west of Cuatro Cienegas, and these were probably this species.

**Historical records.** Duges (1888, 1896) twice reported *G. polyphemus* from Chihuahua. His description of two living animals agreed generally with the new species, and he noted that one animal had a yellow iris and a larger one a brown iris. Legler concludes these records most likely refer to *G. flavomarginatus*.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Gopherus flavomarginatus* is a large tortoise from north-central Mexico, distinguished by its flared, flat-topped shell, pale-and-dark coloration, and wider skull.
- **Closest relatives:** *G. polyphemus* is considered the closest relative, differing by its unflared shell and coloration. *G. agassizii* is the closest Mexican relative, differing by its narrower head and lack of contrasting plastron coloration. *G. berlandieri* has a highly arched shell and pronglike gular projections with a deep notch.
- **Diagnostic traits:** A longer intergular seam, a single rectangular axillary scute, and femoral spurs that are developed in other species to varying degrees.
- **Range extension:** The genus extends onto the Mexican highlands, and the species probably occupies several internally drained basins.
- **Resolution of historical records:** Duges's reports of *G. polyphemus* from Chihuahua most likely refer to this species.
- **Museum provenance:** Locality data for the Durango specimens were confirmed through correspondence and accession records at the National Museum. Legler notes that [[Leonhard Stejneger]] apparently never studied them.
- **Future work:** Legler expects hatchlings to be dark brown to nearly black, with laminae acquiring paler borders as they grow. This remains untested.
