# Green River Fish Barrier Dam Hydraulic Model Study

#ichthyology #marine-biology #rough-fish

## Executive Summary

This 1967 hydraulic model study, carried out by the [[Colorado State University Engineering Research Center]] for Fermelia and Johnson of Rock Springs, Wyoming, tested the proposed [[Green River Fish Barrier Dam]] in [[Wyoming Game And Fish Commission]] planning. The dam and its [[Fish Trap]] are intended to stop the upstream movement of "rough" fish and to remove the need to poison the entire river fish population every three or four years. The study used a 1:25 [[Froude Criterion]] model of half the spillway and fish trap boundary, plus a separate test facility for [[Fish Screen]] head losses.

The spillway performed satisfactorily across the tested range, up to 26,200 cfs, which exceeds the 15,000 cfs design flood. Modifications were proposed to reduce construction cost: removing [[Chute Blocks]], shortening the apron by 12 ft, and using a solid or dentated [[End Sill]]. Wing wall changes removed a downstream eddy that could have held migrating fish. Screen loss coefficients were measured for four bar spacings and four inclinations. The report also recommends [[Riprap]] for scour protection.

## Background & Description

The proposed dam is a weir with an [[Ogee Crest]] and a downward-sloping apron ending in a wedge-shaped end sill. The crest is 153 ft long, with four piers that may be needed to access the trap. The spillway design flood is 15,000 cfs. The fish trap is at the river's right abutment. Downstream migrants are guided by crest screens to a [[Collection Gallery]], then through one of three gates to an angled screen that directs them into the trap. Upstream migrants enter through either the spillway gallery or the channel gallery and are led by current to screens that route them into the trap.

The study had two parts. The first was a model study of spillway hydraulics and the trap boundary, with objectives covering the [[Spillway Rating Curve]], boundary pressures, water surface profiles, gallery outflow velocities, [[Stilling Basin]] optimization, and maximum scour and riprap needs. The second tested screen head losses at prototype dimensions and velocities, including the effect of tilting screens into the flow.

Model criteria: the Froude criterion set the geometric scale at 1:25. Only half the spillway was modeled, as a mirror image of the prototype, at a scale large enough to give accurate flow patterns. This meant the interior of the fish trap could only be assessed qualitatively. Scale ratios from [[Table I]] include:

- Length: 1:25 (25 ft prototype = 1 ft model)
- Velocity: 1:5
- Time: 1:5
- Discharge: 1:3125

The model was built mainly of plywood, with sheet metal molded over wooden frames for the spillway crest and apron, and sand for the river bed and banks. Water came from a pump, with discharge measured by a calibrated orifice. Spillway pressures were read from piezometers on manometer boards. Screens were made from steel frames with 1/2-in. bars on 3/4-in. centers, with alternate bars removed to create clear spacings of 1/4 to 1 1/2 in.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not identify any fish species, so species-specific biology, life history and population data are absent. The ecological purpose is control of "rough" fish movement above the dam.

Flow patterns were assessed in terms of fish behavior:

- **Eddies as resting areas:** A large eddy near the channel bank could hold fish and keep them from following the main current into the trap. It was removed by changing the wing walls.
- **Hydraulic jump position:** At minimum tailwater, shortening the basin moved the [[Hydraulic Jump]] 2 to 4 ft downstream. This reduced the chance of fish moving upstream over the crest.
- **Surface boils:** A boil formed in the stilling basin at low tailwater, up to about 0.5 ft high at 5,300 cfs and about 1.5 ft at 15,400 cfs. It was absent at maximum tailwater and could hinder fish entry if it persisted.
- **Turbulence and scour:** Downstream scour was tested with and without end sills and riprap, to protect the channel and the trap transition.
- **Pier scour:** With a skewed dam axis, scour around the piers increased with skew angle. The report expects this to be minor if the piers are aligned parallel to the flow in the prototype.

## Key Findings

**Spillway hydraulics**
- The spillway performed satisfactorily for prototype discharges and at flows above those expected in the prototype, up to 26,200 cfs.
- Boundary pressures were positive at all piezometers for discharges below about 16,000 cfs. Negative pressures appeared at A3, A4, B3 and B4 above that.
- The minimum recorded pressure was -1.7 ft of water at piezometer A3 at 26,200 cfs. The report accepts pressures above -10 ft of water as satisfactory, a rule of thumb set to prevent [[Cavitation]].
- Pressures with the trap intake gates open were of the same order as with the gates closed.

**Stilling basin and end sills**
- The original basin worked within the full tailwater range. Shortening the apron by 10 ft, then 12 ft, did not significantly change performance.
- Removing the chute blocks had little effect at maximum tailwater. At minimum tailwater the jump moved downstream and its toe became more oscillatory. This was judged neither detrimental nor a concern.
- Three end sills were tested: one solid and two dentated (#1 and #2). All contained the jump within the basin at the tested discharges. At 15,000 cfs, the jump was swept out at tailwater 43.8 ft (solid), 43.0 ft (dentated #1) and 43.5 ft (dentated #2).
- At 5,100 cfs the jump could not be swept out by lowering tailwater. At 10,000 cfs it was swept out at about 41.5 ft.

**Fish trap**
- The exterior trap boundary worked for all discharges, with streamlines flowing outward from the channel gallery.
- Parallel wing walls eliminated the large eddy. A 20° flared arrangement also removed it and performed satisfactorily.
- The interior water surface was very undulatory. The boil was not eliminated, and the report recommends a larger-scale model for interior modifications.

**Dam axis skew**
- Skew angles of 10°, 20° and 30° were examined qualitatively, with flow approaching from the fish trap side.
- Pier scour increased with skew angle, but skew had little other effect on spillway performance.
- At 30°, the crest length would be about 177 ft, a 15.7% increase over 153 ft.
- At 10° or less, the rating curve in Fig. 7 still applies if the crest length stays at 153 ft.
- The report recommends installing the dam normal to the flow or at no more than 10° skew.

**Scour and riprap**
- The maximum scour hole was about 15 to 20 ft downstream of the original basin, and about 25 to 30 ft downstream of the end sill with the 10 ft and 12 ft shortened aprons.
- With the 12 ft shortened apron and 15,000 cfs at 45.5 ft tailwater, the scour hole was about 8 to 12 ft downstream of the solid sill, 6 to 10 ft downstream of dentated sill #1, and immediately downstream of dentated sill #2.
- Solid sill and dentated sill #1 performed clearly better than dentated sill #2.
- Maximum average end sill velocity was about 10 fps, at minimum tailwater and 18,000 cfs. Under [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] data, this calls for riprap with most stones about 15 in. in diameter.
- Recommended protection: graded riprap, 2 ft thick over gravel or a reverse filter, extending 50 ft downstream from the end sill and up the banks into the trap-to-channel transition.

**Fish screens**
- Head losses were measured for approach velocities from about 1 to 7 fps. Screens were tested for 3/4-, 1-, 1 1/2- and 2-in. center-to-center spacings, at inclinations of 0°, 10°, 20° and 30° from vertical.
- Bars vibrated transversely at approach velocities above about 2.5 fps for 3/4-in. and 1-in. spacings, and above 3 fps for 1 1/2-in. and 2-in. spacings. This removed paint and caused abrasion grooves at the frame supports.
- Average loss coefficients K for each spacing (Table III, [[Head Loss Coefficient]]):

| Bar spacing (c/c) | Per-angle K (0° / 10° / 20° / 30°) | Average K |
|---|---|---|
| 3/4 in. | 5.29 / 5.20 / 5.53 / 5.14 | 5.29 |
| 1 in. | 1.93 / 1.85 / 1.91 / 1.77 | Printed value unclear in source (per-angle values average about 1.87) |
| 1 1/2 in. | 0.66 / 0.70 / 0.72 / 0.78 | 0.72 |
| 2 in. | 0.46 / 0.45 / 0.45 / 0.46 | Printed as 0.26, which conflicts with the per-angle values |

- Head loss was quite sensitive to water depth. Standing waves required averaging trough and crest elevations.
- The coefficients apply to clean screens only. They do not account for flow-area loss from trash accumulation.
- Recommendations: use the average K for each spacing at any inclination within the tested range, and rigidly support the bars in the prototype to reduce fatigue and wear.

**Overall recommendations**
- Adopt the recommended spillway and fish trap design shown in Fig. 28, including the wing wall modification.
- Remove the chute blocks and shorten the apron by 12 ft.
- Use a solid or dentated #1 end sill.
- Install 15-in. riprap, 2 ft thick, over gravel or a reverse filter, extending 50 ft downstream.
- Keep the dam normal to the flow or at 10° skew at most.
- Conduct larger-scale testing of the trap interior and of skew angles above 10°.
