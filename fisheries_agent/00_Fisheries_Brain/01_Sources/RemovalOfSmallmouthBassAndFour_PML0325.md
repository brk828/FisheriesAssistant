# Removal of Smallmouth Bass and Four Other Centrarchid Fishes from the Upper Colorado and Lower Gunnison Rivers, 2004–2006

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service final report (Burdick, January 2008; Recovery Program Project 126) documents a three-year lethal removal of [[Smallmouth Bass]] by boat and raft [[Electrofishing]] in main channel habitats of the Upper [[Colorado River]] (Rifle to Beavertail Mountain, and Price-Stubb Dam to Westwater, Utah) and a 2.3-mile reach of the [[Lower Gunnison River]]. The aim was to reduce smallmouth bass abundance to benefit native endangered fishes such as the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]. Four other centrarchids (largemouth bass, green sunfish, black crappie, bluegill) were also removed at the request of Colorado Division of Wildlife for an isotope provenance study.

Smallmouth bass catches peaked in 2005 and fell in 2006, while [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Green Sunfish]] increased. A mark-recapture estimate for 2006 gave 3,197 (95% CI 1,097–5,297) smallmouth bass >100 mm, about 91 per mile.

## Background & Description

- **Target species:** [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*), a nonnative piscivore first documented in quantity in the Upper Colorado River in 2003, when 318 fish were captured in a 39-mile reach.
- **Co-removed centrarchids:** [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Black Crappie]].
- **Background threat:** At least 67 nonnative fishes have been introduced into the Colorado River system. Warmwater gamefish, including centrarchids, are considered major contributors to the decline of native "big river" fishes. Smallmouth bass were identified as the number one predatory threat in the Upper Colorado River basin at a 2005 summit.
- **Study design:** 2004–2005 used repeated removal passes with no population estimate. In 2006, a marking pass with [[Mark-Recapture]] estimation was added. The study area was divided into river segments and sub-reaches (Table 1), with 8 sub-reaches between Price-Stubb Dam and Westwater, one in the Lower Gunnison, and four between Rifle and Beavertail Mountain.
- **Estimator:** [[Chapman Modification of the Petersen-Lincoln Estimator]] with probability of capture after White et al. (1982) and CV after Pollock et al. (1990).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Barriers:** Price-Stubb Dam is an effective upstream barrier. [[Redlands Diversion Dam]] (built 1918, with a fishway completed 1996) also blocks upstream movement on the Gunnison.
- **Source uncertainty:** Smallmouth bass may have arrived from an off-channel source, possibly [[Rifle Gap Reservoir]], which holds a smallmouth bass fishery. Walleye, northern pike, and yellow perch also occur there. Unauthorized introductions cannot be ruled out.
- **Hydrology:** 2005 was the highest water year since 1999, with two discharge peaks. 2002 was the driest year in the record considered. Cumulative degree-days above 14 °C were lowest in 2005 at both Cameo and the stateline gauge.
- **Turbidity:** Catch rates for juvenile and adult smallmouth bass were higher after rainstorms and turbid conditions, which the author attributes to fish moving into shallow shoreline habitat that is sampled by electrofishing.
- **Bass recruitment:** The 2005 year class of age-0 smallmouth bass largely failed to recruit to 2006. The author discusses [[Overwinter Survival]] linked to size and lipid reserves, and to temperature and discharge.
- **Other nonnatives:** Walleye, northern pike, yellow perch, and gizzard shad were also collected. Twelve adult gizzard shad were seen in 2006, likely the first records in the Grand Valley area.

## Key Findings

**Abundance and catch**
- Total smallmouth bass removed, Price-Stubb Dam to Westwater plus the Lower Gunnison: 1,165 (2004), 1,366 (2005), 751 (2006). The 2006 total declined 45% from 2005 and 36% from 2004.
- Between Rifle and Beavertail Mountain: 21, 230, and 79 fish in 2004, 2005, and 2006, a 66% decline from 2005 to 2006.
- Largemouth bass rose steadily: 277 (2004), 589 (2005), 1,094 (2006) in the Grand Valley reaches, up 86% from 2005 and 295% from 2004.
- Green sunfish: 643, 1,051, and 1,395 fish across the same years. Black crappie: 7, 41, and 70. Bluegill: 111, 316, and 307.
- Mean catch rate, Grand Valley: smallmouth bass 6.91 (2004), 7.83 (2005), 4.64 fish/hr (2006). Largemouth bass 1.64, 3.37, and 6.76 fish/hr. Aggregate green sunfish/black crappie/bluegill 4.51, 8.07, and 10.94 fish/hr.

**Concentration areas (highest to lowest, after three years)**
1. Corn Lake to the Colorado/Gunnison River confluence
2. Colorado/Gunnison confluence to Fruita State Park
3. GVIC Dam to Corn Lake
4. Redlands Dam to the Colorado/Gunnison confluence (Lower Gunnison)

**Population estimate (2006)**
- Smallmouth bass >100 mm, 15- and 18-mile reaches plus 2.3 miles of the Lower Gunnison: 3,197 (95% CI 1,097–5,297), about 91 fish/mile. Probability of capture was 0.033 and CV was 33.5%.
- Percentage removed: 5.7% at the point estimate, with exploitation between 3.4% and 16.5%.
- Back-projected 2004 and 2005 estimates were 8,088 and 8,152 fish >100 mm. The author treats these as projections, not measurements.
- The CV never met the 20% precision guideline from Pollock et al. (1990).

**Size structure**
- In 2006, mean total length rose significantly (α = 0.05) in 3 of 5 river segments. The author attributes this partly to a scarcity of fish 100–180 mm in the Grand Valley and 100–200 mm from Rifle to Beavertail.
- Largemouth bass were mostly small: 92% were under 254 mm in the Price-Stubb to Westwater reach. Of 59 fish over 254 mm, 37 were caught between Corn Lake and Redlands Parkway, or between Fruita State Park and Loma Boat Landing. Skipper's Island backwater (RM 154.0) held consistently high densities of large largemouth bass.
- In 2006, 42.9% of Grand Valley smallmouth bass were over 255 mm, and 1.4% were over 357 mm.

**Early-life bass and the 2005 cohort**
- Smallmouth bass 50–100 mm produced in 2005 disappeared before the summer of 2006, which the author calls the most notable finding. The cause is not identified.
- About 67% of the 2005 cohort reached 75 mm by late August 2005, versus 36% in 2006. The author hypothesizes that fish under about 75 mm lack the lipid reserves to survive winter.
- Evidence is described as "insufficient" and "suggestive" only, based on three years of data.

**Conclusions**
- Smallmouth bass abundance was lowest at the end of the 2006 season. It is premature to say whether the decline will continue.
- Largemouth bass numbers continued to increase despite lethal removal. The author suggests isolated, lacustrine-like backwaters may supply juveniles.
- Catch rates were higher in turbid water.

**Recommendations**
1. Double removal passes (4 to 8) in 2007–2008, concentrating effort in high-density reaches and reducing it elsewhere.
2. Suspend electrofishing during Colorado Pikeminnow spawning (mid-to-late June through mid-to-late July downstream of Price-Stubb Dam).
3. Repeat mark-recapture estimates at least every three years.
4. Continue sampling Rifle to Beavertail Mountain.
5. Sample young smallmouth bass in spring before runoff to assess overwinter mortality.
6. Prevent escapement of piscivores from Rifle Gap Reservoir; a screen on Rifle Creek is suggested.
7. Collect otoliths from age-0 and age-1 bass during removal passes.
8. Target in-river features (beaver lodges, stumps, rip-rap) and sample during high turbidity.

**Key uncertainties**
- Spawning timing and duration across flow regimes.
- The cause of the 2005 cohort's loss.
- The number of passes needed to reach a target exploitation rate.
- Whether electrofishing is a cost-effective long-term removal method.
