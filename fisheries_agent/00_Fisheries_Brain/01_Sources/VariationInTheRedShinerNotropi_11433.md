## Executive Summary
This study by James C. Underhill (University of Minnesota) investigates meristic variation in the **red shiner**, *Notropis lutrensis* (Baird and Girard), a cyprinid minnow. Between 1955 and 1958, 570 specimens were collected using a 25-foot, quarter-inch mesh seine from the Missouri River and Vermillion River (and an associated ox-bow lake) in southeastern South Dakota. Nine meristic characters were analyzed across multiple sample sites and years. The central finding is that *N. lutrensis* exhibits comparatively **low variability** in these characters relative to a related species, the [[Bigmouth Shiner]] (*Notropis dorsalis*), and that environmental/temperature differences among habitats had minimal influence on meristic character expression, contrary to expectations from prior literature (e.g., Tåning 1952).

## Background & Description
Nine meristic characters were measured following the methodology of [[Hubbs and Lagler 1958]]:

- **Lateral line scales**
- **Scales above lateral line**
- **Scales below lateral line**
- **Scales before the dorsal fin** (predorsal scale count)
- **Circumferential (body) scale count**
- **Dorsal fin ray count**
- **Anal fin ray count**
- **Pectoral fin ray count**
- **Pelvic fin ray count**

Sample sizes per site (N) ranged from approximately 50 to 117 individuals. Means and standard errors were calculated for each character across nine distinct samples (labeled by station/year, including samples A, B, C from the Vermillion River in 1956, and additional samples from 1957 and the ox-bow lake).

**No sexual dimorphism** was detected in any of the nine characters examined.

Overall meristic counts were relatively stable across sampling sites, with the notable exception of **predorsal scale counts** (scales before the dorsal fin), which showed statistically significant variation between certain populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat types sampled:**
  - [[Missouri River]] (main channel) — characterized by **lower and more thermally stable** temperatures.
  - [[Vermillion River]] — intermediate temperature regime; three distinct 1956 sampling stations (A, B, C) plus a separate 1957 sample.
  - **Ox-bow lake** (associated with the Vermillion River system) — exhibited the **highest temperatures** at spawning time among the three habitat types.

- **Thermal gradient at spawning:** Lake (highest) > Vermillion River (intermediate) > Missouri River (lowest).

- Despite documented environmental differences (particularly temperature, a factor previously implicated by [[Tåning 1952]] as a primary driver of meristic part formation), the study found that habitat-driven temperature variation had **little measurable effect** on the meristic characters of *N. lutrensis*.

- Collections were made across river and lentic (ox-bow lake) habitats, indicating the species' tolerance for a range of freshwater lotic and lacustrine conditions within the same drainage system.

## Key Findings
1. **Low overall variability**: *Notropis lutrensis* shows a lower level of meristic variability compared to the congeneric [[Bigmouth Shiner]] (*Notropis dorsalis*), as previously documented by Underhill and Merrell (1959).
2. **No sexual dimorphism** was detected in any of the nine meristic characters studied.
3. **Predorsal scale count anomaly**: Among Vermillion River samples A, B, and C (1956), only the predorsal scale count showed significant differentiation — samples A and B did not differ significantly from each other, but Sample C differed significantly from both.
4. **Temporal effect**: The 1957 Vermillion River sample had a significantly **lower** average predorsal scale count than all other samples, while a separate 1957 sample from the main channel had a significantly **greater** predorsal scale count than all other samples — highlighting inconsistency and caution warranted when using this character for interbasin comparisons.
5. **Temperature vs. meristic variation**: Although the [[Missouri River]], [[Vermillion River]], and the ox-bow lake habitats differed markedly in thermal regime (especially at spawning time), this did not translate into strong or consistent meristic differences, suggesting temperature was **not a dominant driver** of variation in this species/system, contrasting with expectations set by earlier meristic variation theory (Tåning 1952; Jordan 1891).
6. **Methodological caution**: The study recommends caution when using **predorsal scale counts** specifically as a diagnostic character for distinguishing populations across different river basins, given its demonstrated instability relative to other meristic traits.
