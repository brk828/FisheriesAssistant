# Arizona Game and Fish Department Response on Balancing Native and Sport Fish Management

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is a 2005 letter from [[Duane L. Shroufe]], Director of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], replying to Dr. [[Paul C. Marsh]] and Jerome and Sally Stefferud. They had written a Perspective article in *Fisheries* magazine arguing for segregated management of native and sport fishes. The letter accepts that native fish conservation and sport fishing are both important public responsibilities. It describes the department's [[Integrated Fisheries Management Plan]] for the [[Little Colorado River Watershed]], which divides watersheds by management need, and invites the authors to join an ongoing evaluation process. It stresses that balancing these roles depends on partnerships with agencies, landowners and the public.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Official correspondence (letter), not a research study. The letter is dated December 2, 2005, and its continuation page is dated December 1, 2005.
- **Sender:** [[Duane L. Shroufe]], Director, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The letter is copied to Governor [[Janet Napolitano]] and Deputy Director Steve Ferrell.
- **Recipients:** Dr. Paul C. Marsh (Chandler, Arizona) and Jerome and Sally Stefferud (Phoenix, Arizona).
- **Trigger:** A Perspective article by the authors in *Fisheries* magazine, which the letter describes as often offering "thought-provoking comments and views on many fisheries issues."
- **Department structure:** The Commission has seven members, including chairman W. Hays Gilstrap. The [[Nongame Branch]] leads native wildlife conservation, and sport fish management is handled separately. The letter describes the Nongame Branch as nationally recognized for native wildlife conservation.
- **Department roles, as stated:**
  - Conserving and restoring Arizona's wildlife resources, including native fishes.
  - Providing recreation. The letter cites roughly five million user days of recreation and nearly $1 billion in value to the state's economy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter frames the issue as a tension between two responsibilities:

1. **Native aquatic wildlife conservation:** The letter calls native fishes "incredibly valuable to our culture, heritage, and health of our ecosystems." The mission includes restoring and conserving native species for present and future generations.
2. **Sport fishing opportunities:** Sport fishes are described as "assets worthy of conservation," and the letter notes that some watersheds can support sport fishing with minimal effect on native fish management.

The letter argues that some watersheds should be used for native aquatic conservation, while others can be managed for sport fishing without harming native fish. The approach is therefore spatial, dividing the landscape by management priority rather than treating all waters the same.

## Key Findings

**1. Segregated management is not new to the department.**
The department began a "pioneering" watershed-based approach in 1997. It culminated in the [[Integrated Fisheries Management Plan]] for the Little Colorado River watershed, finalized in 2001. The plan:
- Involved the department, other agencies and the public.
- Divided the watershed into native fish management areas and sport fish management areas, each with its own management needs.

**2. The plan is under evaluation and will be accelerated.**
The approach is being used to evaluate the watershed plan over the next three years. The department states that it wishes to accelerate the process to improve implementation of native aquatic wildlife restoration. The authors are invited to participate, as they were in the 1997 effort.

**3. Implementation depends on partnerships.**
The letter states that implementation relies on federal and state partners, land managers, private landowners, businesses and the public. It cautions that success without this partnership is unlikely.

**4. Dual roles require balance.**
The letter describes the central challenge as balancing native fish conservation with sport fishing in practice. It describes the goal as providing "the widest possible range of native aquatic and sport fishing opportunities," with the stated obligation of wildlife professionals to manage resources in context with human values, communities and ecosystem functions.

**5. Requested action.**
The director encourages the authors to continue working with department staff on native fish conservation, focusing on areas where conservation and recreation can coexist.

**Limitations of the document:** The letter contains no species list, population data, sampling results or ecological measurements. Its numerical content is limited to the recreation and economic figures cited above, and the specific native and sport fish species are not named.
