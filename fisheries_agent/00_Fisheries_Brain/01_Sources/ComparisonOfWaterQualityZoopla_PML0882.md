# Comparison of Water Quality, Zooplankton Density, and Cover in Razorback Sucker (Xyrauchen texanus) Spawning Areas of Lake Mead and Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This BIO-WEST Inc. report (PR-749-2, December 2001), prepared for the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]], tests whether nursery areas of [[Razorback Sucker]] differ in water quality, zooplankton density, and aquatic cover between [[Lake Mead]] and [[Lake Mohave]]. Razorback sucker recruitment is documented in Lake Mead (Las Vegas Bay and Echo Bay) but not in Lake Mohave for roughly 40 years, despite Lake Mohave holding the largest known population. Five locations were sampled in March and May 2000. [[Las Vegas Bay]] differed significantly from the other bays in nearly every water quality parameter and had the highest nutrient and productivity levels. Zooplankton densities were low across all sites. Cover differed most strongly between Las Vegas Bay and Echo Bay (high cover) and the other sites. The authors conclude that cover and productivity, which vary with lake level, are the most promising factors to monitor in future work.

## Background & Description

**Species context.** The razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Catostomidae) is a remnant native fish of the lower [[Colorado River]] reservoirs. Hypotheses for failed recruitment include predation by nonnative fishes, inadequate food supply, and altered flow and temperature regimes. Lake Mead adults are fewer, but the population is younger, indicating recruitment has occurred several times since the 1970s.

**Study design.** Five locations were chosen:
- Lake Mead: [[Las Vegas Bay]] (LVB), [[Echo Bay]] (EB), and [[Trail Rapids Bay]] (TRB, chosen as a control physically similar to Echo Bay).
- Lake Mohave: [[Arizona Bay]] (AZB) and [[Tequila Cove]] (TQC).

Las Vegas Bay, Echo Bay, Tequila Cove, and Arizona Bay were known razorback sucker spawning areas. Each location had five sites, each a 200 m × 20 m shoreline rectangle.

**Methods summary.**
- Water quality was measured with a Hydrolab Datasonde (temperature, dissolved oxygen, nitrate, ammonium, total dissolved solids). Total phosphorus came from grab samples analysed by NEL Laboratories.
- Zooplankton was collected with a 30 × 90 cm net, 153 µm mesh, in horizontal tows of about 6 m at 0–0.5 m depth (about 430.5 L sampled per tow), preserved in 10% formalin, and identified mainly to order and family.
- Cover (mainly dead or live vegetation) was mapped from the boat, with underwater video and snorkeling in deeper areas.
- Analysis used ANOVA and Tukey's HSD test.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Nutrient history.** Lake Mead supplies drinking water to Las Vegas and is mostly mesotrophic, with Las Vegas Bay eutrophic. The formation of [[Lake Powell]] after [[Glen Canyon Dam]] (1963) reduced phosphorus loading by up to 96%. [[Las Vegas Wash]] now supplies 60% or more of Lake Mead's phosphorus. Productivity in the upper lake fell to less than 21% of pre-1963 levels, and Boulder Basin supplies over 50% of total reservoir productivity. Lake Mohave's main nutrient source is hypolimnetic outflow from [[Hoover Dam]].

**Las Vegas Wash inflow.** Wash water forms a density current that moves nutrients into Las Vegas Bay. Baker et al. (1977) recorded 800 kg/d of phosphorus and 1,100 kg/d of nitrogen. By 1993, loads were 110 kg/d of phosphorus, 1,100 kg/d of nitrates, and 4,800 kg/d of ammonia plus ammonium.

**Zooplankton background.** Historical densities in Boulder Basin ranged from 846/m² to 6,180/m² over a year. Lake Mohave densities were lowest near Hoover Dam and peaked at Davis Dam. Marsh and Langhorst (1988) found very low numbers in Arizona Bay.

**Lake level.** Figures 19 and 20 show mean monthly elevations for Lake Mohave (1975–2000) and Lake Mead (1925–2000). The authors link fluctuating levels to cover and productivity.

## Key Findings

**Water quality**
- Las Vegas Bay differed significantly from the other bays in nearly every parameter. Nitrate-N and ammonium were highest at LVB Site 1, closest to the Las Vegas Wash inflow (p < 0.001 in March and May for nitrate).
- Total phosphorus was detectable only at LVB sites 1 and 2 in March (≥0.01 mg/L). Because each site was sampled once, no statistical conclusions were drawn.
- Total dissolved solids were highest at LVB sites, and dissolved oxygen was highest at LVB and EB in March. TQC had the lowest oxygen values.

**Zooplankton**
- Total zooplankton densities showed no consistent trend between March and May. Tequila Cove had significantly higher densities than EB and TRB, and LVB was higher than Echo Bay in March (p < 0.02).
- Densities of small zooplankton (< 0.03 mm) did not differ consistently among locations, apart from LVB sites in March.
- Composition differed: Bosminidae dominated AZB and TQC in March, while copepods dominated EB, LVB, and TRB. Arizona Bay had the highest share of copepod eggs and nauplii combined in May.
- Average total zooplankton density was below the 10–20 items/L threshold that Papoulias and Minckley (1990) linked to larval survival, suggesting food may limit larvae at all sites.

**Cover**
- Las Vegas Bay had 17.3% average cover in March and 16.2% in May. Echo Bay had the highest average percent cover in March (about 22.2%).
- Arizona Bay averaged 6.8%, but sites 2 and 3 had much higher cover than the rest. TRB and TQC had very little cover.
- LVB and EB had cover levels 2.4 to 24.0 times higher than the other sites in March and May, which the authors consider the major difference between the two lakes.

**Discussion and interpretation**
- Las Vegas Bay has the highest productivity, but its zooplankton density was not consistently higher than in the other bays. Productivity alone may not explain recruitment.
- Cover, which is tied to lake level, may be a major factor. The authors suggest that cover and vegetation inundation could explain why recruitment occurs in certain years.
- Sampling with a 153 µm mesh may have missed rotifers; studies using 80 µm mesh were recommended. The sampling was also only two "snapshots" and may not reflect the whole spawning season.

**Recommendations**
- Continue monitoring cover and productivity at Echo Bay and Las Vegas Bay, plus the three other locations, over multiple years and across water levels.
- Monitor the Colorado River inflow area if a spawning population is confirmed there.
- Combine productivity and cover monitoring with larval and adult razorback sucker sampling to identify the years and conditions that allow recruitment.
