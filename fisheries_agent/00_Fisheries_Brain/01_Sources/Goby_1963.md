## Executive Summary
This entry, authored by Carl L. Hubbs for the Encyclopaedia Britannica (1963), provides a general taxonomic and ecological overview of the [[Gobioidei]], a large suborder of spiny-rayed fishes comprising the [[Gobiidae]] (true gobies) and [[Eleotridae]] (sleepers). These fishes are predominantly small, bottom-dwelling, marine and warm-water species distinguished by distinct pelvic fin morphology. The note highlights extreme size variation within the group—from some of the smallest known vertebrates to sleepers exceeding a foot in length—and describes distinctive reproductive behavior, habitat specialization, and several notable representative species.

## Background & Description
- **Dorsal fin**: Separate first dorsal fin bearing a few flexible spines (usually six).
- **Pelvic fins**: Key taxonomic distinguishing feature:
  - In [[Eleotridae]] ("sleepers"): pelvic fins set close together but not fused.
  - In [[Gobiidae]] (true gobies): pelvic fins united into a sucking disc, aiding attachment to substrate.
- **Size range**: Most species measure 1–4 inches in length.
  - Some sleepers exceed 12 inches.
  - *[[Pandaka pygmaea]]* (Philippines) is among the smallest known vertebrates, at roughly 0.5 inch.
- **Notable morphological adaptations**:
  - *[[Periophthalmus]]* (mudskippers): bulging eyes, adapted for amphibious life on mud flats, resting with the front body out of water.
  - *[[Gillichthys mirabilis]]* (long-jawed goby / mudsucker): upper jaw elongates in adults, extending beyond the gill opening.
  - *[[Typhlogobius californiensis]]*: blind, pink-colored species, lacking pigmentation and functional eyes, consistent with its burrow-dwelling lifestyle.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **General habitat**: Primarily marine and warm-water environments; mostly bottom-dwellers.
- **Distribution patterns**:
  - Many species, e.g., *[[Bathygobius]]*, are restricted to tropical shore edges.
  - *[[Gobius capito]]*: inhabits European rock pools, reaching about 10 inches.
  - *Periophthalmus* species: found on mud flats around the Indian Ocean and East Indies.
  - *Gillichthys mirabilis*: burrow-inhabiting species native to Southern California, notably hardy and used as the region's chief bait fish.
  - *Typhlogobius californiensis*: found in a striking symbiotic/commensal relationship, cohabiting burrows with a blind shrimp (*[[Callianassa]]* sp.) under intertidal stones in Southern California.
- **Reproductive ecology**:
  - Male gobies guard the eggs, exhibiting paternal care behavior.
  - Eggs are oval and encapsulated, attached via short adhesive threads.
  - Egg-laying substrates include discarded bivalve shells and rock/crevice surfaces, as depicted with the [[Frillfin Goby]] (*Bathygobius soporator*) guarding eggs on an empty bivalve shell.

## Key Findings
- The Gobioidei represents one of the most numerous groups of spiny-rayed marine fishes, with several hundred known species.
- Pelvic fin structure (free vs. fused into a sucking disc) serves as the primary morphological distinction between [[Eleotridae]] and [[Gobiidae]].
- The suborder includes both the largest size extremes among small fishes—containing *Pandaka pygmaea*, one of the smallest known vertebrates—and species with specialized amphibious or symbiotic lifestyles.
- Paternal egg-guarding behavior is a defining reproductive strategy across goby species.
- Ecological specialization is pronounced, including mud-flat amphibious life (mudskippers), burrow commensalism with shrimp (*Typhlogobius*), and use as bait fish in regional fisheries (*Gillichthys mirabilis*).
