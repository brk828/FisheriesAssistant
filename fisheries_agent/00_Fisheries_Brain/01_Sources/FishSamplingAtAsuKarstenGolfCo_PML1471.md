# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, Autumn 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

Personnel from [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), Center for Environmental Studies, sampled the fish of the North and South lakes at [[ASU Karsten Golf Course]] (ASUGC) on 05–06 October and 10 November 1992. The goal was to assess the status of stocked native fishes: [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Only three native fish were captured in total (two razorback suckers and three bonytail on 10 November, plus one razorback sucker on 05 October, per the source's catch summary), no juvenile natives were found, and no Colorado squawfish were captured. Non-native species dominated the seine catch. The author attributes the low native catch to several possible factors that future recapture of PIT-tagged fish may help resolve.

## Background & Description

- **Origin of stocked fish:** Bonytail, Colorado squawfish, and razorback sucker were propagated at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Research Center, Dexter, New Mexico, a U.S. [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] facility, and stocked at ASUGC in September–October 1988. Individuals from these initial stockings were approximately 4 years old at the time of sampling.
- **Additional bonytail transfer:** 549 [[PIT Tagging|PIT-tagged]] bonytail averaging 381 mm total length (TL) were moved from Dexter to ASUGC in April (year given as 1991 in the source).
- **Further transfer:** On 10 November 1992, seven bonytail measuring 230–320 mm TL were moved from the ASU Animal Care Facilities to ASUGC. These fish came from the original autumn 1988 Dexter group and were not PIT-tagged before stocking.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods**

- **Gill and trammel nets:** Set at sunset on 06 October and 10 November to capture larger fish. Nets were retrieved after 3–5 hours of fishing to limit capture stress and potential mortality.
- **Seine:** A 3 × 1.2 m straight seine with 0.3 cm mesh was pulled around the lake perimeter after dark on 05 October to collect smaller fish.
- **Handling:** Native fish were measured (TL, mm), weighed (nearest 2 g), checked for general health, condition, and external parasites, scanned, PIT-tagged if no tag was present, and released. Non-native fish were removed, except white amur, which had been stocked by ASUCC. No native fish mortalities occurred.

**Factors proposed for low native catch**

The author lists four possible explanations:
1. General inactivity of fish in cooler autumn water temperatures.
2. Cumulative mortality since stocking, reducing the numbers available for sampling.
3. Fish occupying specific local areas that were not effectively sampled.
4. Unknown factors affecting catch rates at the times sampling occurred.

## Key Findings

- **Native catch was very low.** Entanglement nets captured bonytail, razorback sucker, and several non-native fishes, but no Colorado squawfish. The author notes this species has proven difficult to collect in other urban lakes. The first night's native catch was one razorback sucker, which prompted extra sampling on 10 November; that visit added one more razorback sucker and three bonytail.
- **No native fish were PIT-tagged during sampling.** Data for the individual native fish are summarized in a table in the source (Table 2, not reproduced here).
- **Non-native seine catch was large.** The seine yielded nearly 1,500 specimens of three non-native species (Table 3 in source). [[Red Shiner]] was predominant in both lakes, followed by [[Threadfin Shad]] and juvenile [[Green Sunfish]].
- **No evidence of native recruitment.** No juvenile native fish were captured. The author concludes that, if native reproduction has occurred, there is no evidence it reached the juvenile stage.
- **Future work:** Recapture of PIT-tagged fish should show which of the four proposed factors is operating. Planned work includes light-trap sampling for larval fish in winter–spring, and general collecting with entanglement nets and seines in spring–early summer.
