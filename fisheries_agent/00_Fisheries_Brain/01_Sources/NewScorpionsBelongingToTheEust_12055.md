# Seven New Scorpion Taxa of the *Vejovis* Eusthenura Group from Baja California, Mexico

#arachnology #scorpionida #vejovidae

## Executive Summary

This 1970 taxonomic paper by [[Stanley C. Williams]] in the *Proceedings of the California Academy of Sciences* describes seven new species and subspecies of scorpions in the genus [[Vejovis]], all belonging to the [[Vejovis Eusthenura Group]]. The taxa are *Vejovis waeringi*, *V. coloradensis*, *V. terradomus*, *V. galbus*, *V. viscainensis*, *Vejovis hoffmanni fuscus*, and *Vejovis diazi transmontanus*. The material derives from two field expeditions (1968 and 1969) through the [[Baja California Peninsula]], during which about 50,000 scorpions were collected, several thousand of them from the eusthenura group. The author argues that *V. viscainensis* represents a distinct systematic branch of the group. The document contains no aquatic or marine data; it concerns desert arachnids.

## Background & Description

The eusthenura group occurs in western North America, with greatest diversity in the [[Baja California Peninsula]] and the desert regions of southern California and Arizona. Members are mostly uniform pale yellow and occupy sandy habitats. Most are burrowers, and some appear above ground only during a restricted period, presumably the mating season. Several species seem to have more than one sexually mature instar.

Group members are defined by features such as:
- metasomal segment V longer than the carapace or movable finger;
- elongate pedipalp fingers and a narrow palm with low or obsolete keels;
- well-developed, coarsely crenulate to serrate dorsal and dorsolateral metasomal keels;
- densely granular carapace and tergites;
- pectines of about 13 to 22 teeth, smaller and with fewer teeth in females;
- adult males with genital papillae and a completely divided genital operculum.

Species-level diagnosis relies on pedipalp finger elongation, palm width, scalloping of the pedipalp fingers, vesicle hirsuteness and surface texture, metasomal keel structure, length-to-width ratio of metasomal segment I, and carapace setation. Specimens were sampled with the [[Ultraviolet Detection Method]], then killed, preserved, and processed following [[Williams 1968 Scorpion Preservation Methods]]. Acknowledgments credit the American Museum of Natural History, the California Academy of Sciences, Arizona State University, the National Science Foundation (grant GB-7679), and the Mexican Department of Wildlife for collecting permits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes terrestrial habitats only. Recurring points include:
- **Substrate:** sandy desert soils, coastal sand dunes, volcanic canyon bottoms and oases.
- **Burrowing behaviour:** sex ratios strongly skewed toward males in several samples led the author to infer that these species spend most of their time in burrows, with surface activity mainly limited to courtship and mating periods.
- **Biogeographic isolation:** the volcanic-area subspecies is interpreted as an invasion of a sandy-desert species into canyon and oasis habitats, with local colour-race formation in each known locality.
- **Endemism:** *V. viscainensis* is considered endemic to the [[Viscaino Desert]], and *V. diazi transmontanus* is known only from a dune locality east of the [[Sierra de la Giganta]].

## Key Findings

**1. *Vejovis waeringi* (new species)**
- Holotype male 46.0 mm total length; allotype female 54.0 mm.
- Type series of 233 paratopotypes (179 males, 54 females) collected at Okies Landing, 27 miles south of Puertocitos, 12 June 1968. An additional 53 paratypes are listed from San Felipe, Puertecitos and Rio Hardy.
- Males measured 32 to 47 mm and females 30 to 58 mm. Pectine teeth ranged 12 to 14 in females and 15 to 18 in males.
- Known only from the upper gulf region of Baja California Norte, between Rio Hardy Fishing Camp and Okies Landing.
- Male-biased sex ratio interpreted as predominantly subsurface living with courtship surface migrations. Named after [[Erik Kjellsvig-Waering]].

**2. *Vejovis coloradensis* (new species)**
- Holotype male 46.0 mm; allotype female 52.0 mm.
- Type locality: California Agricultural Inspection Station, 2 miles west of Andrade, Imperial County, 6 July 1969. Twelve further paratypes are listed.
- Found in open sandy habitats of the [[Imperial Valley]] (Colorado Desert, southern California) and northern Baja California, but not abundant in the samples.
- Separated from *V. confusus* and *V. waeringi* mainly by palm proportions, vesicle hairiness and keel crenulation. Named for the [[Colorado Desert]].

**3. *Vejovis terradomus* (new species)**
- Holotype male 44.0 mm. No allotype was designated because maturity of the females was doubtful.
- Nine paratopotypes (6 males, 3 females) from 1 mile southwest of Rancho Canipolé, Baja California Sur, 5 May 1969.
- Males measured 33 to 41 mm, females 23 to 35 mm. Pectine teeth were 14 to 15 in females and 18 to 19 in males.
- Known only from the area between Rancho Canipolé and Loreto. The author infers mostly burrow-bound life. Name means "soil dweller."

**4. *Vejovis galbus* (new species)**
- Holotype male 34.0 mm; allotype female 38.0 mm.
- Eighteen paratopotypes (9 males, 9 females) collected 5 miles south of Loreto, 16 May 1969.
- Males measured 18 to 35 mm and females 19 to 37 mm.
- Known only from the type locality. Pedipalp fingers are relatively short for the group, and the name refers to its uniform yellow colour.

**5. *Vejovis viscainensis* (new species)**
- Holotype male 44.0 mm; allotype female 49.0 mm (allotype from a different site, 14 miles south of Guerrero Negro, 16 April 1969).
- Holotype and nine paratopotypes (5 males, 4 females) from 2 miles northwest of Miller's Landing, 21 June 1968.
- Males measured 28 to 42 mm and females 27 to 39 mm. Pectine teeth were 16 to 17 in males and 14 to 15 in females.
- Diagnosed by very fine crenulation of metasomal keels, with about 25 to 30 crenulations on each dorsal keel of segment II, roughly double the number in other group members. The author regards it as a distinct branch of the eusthenura group.
- Distribution spans the [[Viscaino Desert]] from Miller's Landing to San Angel, 13 miles west of San Ignacio. Records include 20 males and 10 females at one site (1 mile east of Las Bombas), supporting predominantly subterranean life.

**6. *Vejovis hoffmanni fuscus* (new subspecies)**
- Holotype male 34.0 mm; allotype female 31.0 mm.
- Twelve paratopotypes (10 males, 2 females) collected 24 miles northeast of San José de Comondú, 15 May 1969.
- Males measured 12 to 36 mm; the single female reported measured 22 mm. Most specimens appeared to be subadults.
- Distinguished from *V. hoffmanni hoffmanni* by distinct dusky dorsal marbling and more hairs on the vesicle. Endemic to the volcanic area of [[Comondú Volcanic Area]].
- The author interprets it as a sandy-desert species that invaded volcanic canyon bottoms and oases.

**7. *Vejovis diazi transmontanus* (new subspecies)**
- Holotype male 39.0 mm, with no allotype designated.
- Ten paratopotypes (7 males, 3 females) collected on coastal sand dunes at Punta San Telmo, Baja California Sur, 26 May 1969.
- Males measured 19 to 40 mm and females 18 to 23 mm; none of the females appeared mature.
- Differs from *V. diazi diazi* chiefly by the absence of red coloration on the vesicle. Known only from the type locality.

**Overall conclusions**
- Seven new taxa are added to the eusthenura group, and the author contends that the group's systematics are only now becoming clear.
- Several taxa have very restricted ranges, several are known only from type localities, and most show male-biased samples, consistent with burrowing habits.
- The paper cites related works on *Vejovis* faunas of Coahuila and Baja California (Williams 1968a, 1970) as companion studies.
