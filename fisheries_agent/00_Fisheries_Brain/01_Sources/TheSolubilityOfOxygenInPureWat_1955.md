# The Solubility of Oxygen in Pure Water and Sea-Water

## Executive Summary
This 1955 study by Truesdale, Downing, and Lowden re-measured the solubility of dissolved [[Oxygen]] in pure water and [[Sea-Water]] across a temperature range of 2–40°C using a refined [[Winkler Method|Winkler titration technique]] with an amperometric end-point. The authors found that widely accepted solubility values (largely derived from older gasometric determinations by Fox) were too high by as much as 4%, with the discrepancy greatest at low temperatures (~3–4% at 0°C) and smallest at high temperatures (~0.3% at 35°C). When the newly determined, lower solubility values were applied to calculations of oxygen re-aeration rates in stirred water, the results conformed much better to the [[Adeney & Becker Law]] of aeration than when older accepted values were used. This has direct implications for calculating [[Dissolved Oxygen Budget|oxygen balances]] in polluted rivers and estuaries — the paper is foundational to water-quality and self-purification modeling that underpins habitat suitability assessments for aquatic organisms, including fish.

## Background & Description
- **Core problem**: Accurate knowledge of oxygen solubility is essential for calculating the rate of oxygen solution into water, a primary driver of self-purification in polluted streams and estuaries. The rate of solution in well-mixed water is proportional to the deficit between the saturation concentration and the actual dissolved oxygen concentration.
- **Historical discrepancy**: Published solubility values from various workers (A.P.H.A., Winkler, Roscoe & Lunt, Fox, Bohr & Bock, Jacobsen, Royal Commission on Sewage Disposal) differed by up to 0.57 p.p.m. (~6% of solubility) across 0–35°C, with most reference tables relying on gasometric measurements by [[Fox (1909)]].
- **Method employed**: A modified [[Winkler Titration Method]] with amperometric iodine end-point detection, cross-validated against a new gasometric reference procedure (Wheatland & Smith) to confirm accuracy.
- **Experimental design**:
  - Pure water: deionized/distilled water in a 10-litre aspirator, deoxygenated with nitrogen or supersaturated with oxygen, then equilibrated with CO₂-free, water-vapour-saturated air at controlled temperature (±0.05°C) while being stirred at 12.7 r.p.m.
  - Saline water: natural sea-water collected from the Thames Estuary (Knock John Buoy), filtered, sterilized (heated to 80°C twice), and diluted to target salinities of 15.0 and 30.0 parts per thousand; stirred at 38 r.p.m. (with confirmatory runs at 400 r.p.m.).
  - The [[Azide Modification]] of the Winkler method was used for sea-water to eliminate nitrite interference.
- **Empirical equations derived**:
  - Pure water saturation concentration (p.p.m. O₂) as a function of temperature T (°C):
    `C'_0 = 14.161 − 0.3943T + 0.007714T² − 0.00006467T³`
  - Salinity correction (linear with salinity S, parts per thousand):
    `C'_S = C'_0 − S(0.0841 − 0.00256T + 0.0000874T²)`
  - Coefficients tabulated at 0.1°C intervals (0–40°C) for practical lookup of solubility at any temperature/salinity combination (Table III).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Aquatic self-purification**: The solubility values underpin calculations of stream and estuarine re-aeration, a process central to the recovery of oxygen-depleted, polluted waters — directly relevant to habitat quality for [[Fish]] and other aquatic organisms.
- **Estuarine relevance**: Sea-water for the saline experiments was drawn from the [[Thames Estuary]], a system subject to organic pollution and oxygen depletion; the study explicitly ties its findings to practical "oxygen balance" calculations for this estuary.
- **Practical impact**: Applying the newly measured (lower) solubility values reduced the calculated daily oxygen absorption of the Thames Estuary by approximately 40 tons — a substantial revision affecting pollution-control and water-quality management models that indirectly govern conditions for [[Aquatic Life]] and [[Fish Habitat|fish habitats]].
- **Temperature and salinity as environmental drivers**: The work reinforces the well-established ecological principle that dissolved oxygen carrying capacity decreases with increasing temperature and increasing salinity — both key variables shaping distribution and stress physiology of [[Fish]] and other [[Aquatic Organisms]] in estuarine and marine environments.
- **Residual biological oxygen demand**: Despite sterilization efforts, sea-water retained a small residual 5-day [[Biochemical Oxygen Demand|B.O.D.]] of ~0.1–0.2 p.p.m., illustrating the persistent influence of microbial/organismal respiration on oxygen budgets even in "sterilized" experimental water — a reminder of the biological confounders present in real aquatic systems.

## Key Findings
- **Overestimation of accepted values**: Prior "accepted" oxygen solubility tables overestimated true solubility by ~0.4 p.p.m. (3–4%) at low temperatures (~0°C), decreasing to ~0.025 p.p.m. (<0.3%) at 35°C.
- **Improved aeration law fit**: Using the new, lower solubility values in re-aeration rate calculations produced straight-line (correct) semi-logarithmic plots of oxygen deficit vs. time, consistent with the [[Adeney & Becker Law]], whereas old values produced curved, non-conforming plots (Fig. 1).
- **Agreement with Jacobsen**: The new results agreed well with earlier (and previously criticized) measurements by [[Jacobsen (1905)]], also based on the Winkler method, suggesting Jacobsen's values were more reliable than long-assumed.
- **Salinity effect confirmed linear**: Measurements at three salinities (0, ~15, and 30 parts per thousand) confirmed that oxygen solubility declines linearly with increasing salinity across the tested range, consistent with prior assumptions.
- **Method validation**: A modified micro-Winkler titration (20 ml sample, amperometric end-point) gave results statistically indistinguishable from the standard 250 ml Winkler procedure (9.601 ± 0.026 p.p.m. vs. 9.606 ± 0.024 p.p.m.), validating the smaller-scale technique for routine use.
- **Air composition check**: Measured atmospheric oxygen content (20.88–20.97% by volume, CO₂-free) was close to, but slightly below, the standard reference value of 20.99% used in International Critical Tables, possibly reflecting real local/secular variation in air composition.
- **Practical management implication**: Recalculated oxygen solubility reduces the estimated oxygen absorption of the Thames Estuary by ~40 tons/day, materially affecting pollution-loading and dissolved-oxygen management models used to protect aquatic ecosystems.
- **Comprehensive reference table**: The paper provides a detailed lookup table (Table III) of oxygen solubility coefficients for temperatures 0–40°C and salinities 0–0.8 (and by extension up to sea-water salinity), enabling precise calculation of saturation dissolved oxygen concentration for any combination of temperature and salinity — a tool still foundational to modern [[Dissolved Oxygen]] standards in fisheries and water-quality science.
