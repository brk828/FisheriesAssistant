# Un siècle de recherches belges sur la floristique et l'écologie des algues

#phycology #limnology #algae #history-of-science

## Executive Summary

This historical review by [[Jean-Jacques Symoens]] (Université d'Elisabethville; *Bulletin de la Société royale de Botanique de Belgique*, vol. 95, 1963) traces roughly a century of Belgian research on the floristics and ecology of algae, written for the centenary of the [[Royal Botanical Society of Belgium]]. It follows the three classical stages of biological knowledge: inventory of flora, study of organism–environment relations, and analysis of trophic chains and biological production. The paper also reviews Belgian contributions to overseas phycology in Indonesia, the Congo, Rwanda-Burundi and Antarctica, and closes with a critique of weaknesses in Belgian phycology.

## Background & Description

The article is a retrospective essay rather than a research study. It draws on earlier histories, notably [[Evens]]'s 1944 *Geschiedenis der Algologie in België*, and gives a bibliography of 20 pre-1943 works and about 97 later works on Belgian algae, plus further entries on Central African phycology.

Key historical actors include:
- **Early inventories (1803–1867):** Characeae and cryptogam floras by Kickx, Crépin, Michot and Marissal. Kickx's posthumous *Flore cryptogamique des Flandres* (1867) raised the Belgian algal count to just over 400 species.
- **[[Henri Van Heurck]]:** an Antwerp industrialist and self-taught diatomist. He co-produced the *Synopsis des Diatomées de Belgique* (1880–85), with an atlas of 132 plates and 3100 figures.
- **[[Emile De Wildeman]]:** a professional botanist who produced the *Flore des Algues de Belgique* (1896) and tome I of the *Prodrome de la Flore belge* (1898). By 1895 he had about 85 publications.
- **[[Ernest Rousseau]]:** founder of the [[Overmere Hydrobiological Station]] (1906) and the *Annales de Biologie lacustre*. He died in 1920.
- **[[Jean Massart]]:** a leading Brussels plant-ecologist whose *Esquisse de la Géographie botanique de la Belgique* (1910) shaped later ecological work.
- **[[Paul Van Oye]]:** an ecologist who worked on Desmidiaceae and pH, on tufa (*crons*) in Gaume, and on tropical plankton in Java and the Congo.
- **[[Walter Conard]]:** a flagellate specialist who studied brackish waters near Lillo and the Ry Colas stream in the Ardenne.
- **[[Jean-Jacques Symoens]]:** author of the 1957 synthesis *Les eaux douces de l'Ardenne et des régions voisines*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stages of biological knowledge.** The author frames regional study as (1) inventory, (2) relations between environment and distribution, and (3) quantitative trophic relations and production. He notes that these stages overlap in practice.

**Decline and revival.** After the founding of the [[Royal Botanical Society of Belgium]] (June 1862), algal floristics stagnated because the field was dominated by amateurs and microscopy was costly. Ecological work revived before World War II with the introduction of pH measurement and phytosociology.

**Regional types and tufa.** Symoens distinguished three chemical water types in Ardennes streams:
- *Condrusien* (calcareous; tufa forming, high biogenic capacity)
- *Ardennais*
- *Fagnard* (acidic, low biogenic capacity)

Tufa formation is linked to [[Phormidium Incrustatum]] (= *Lyngbya calcarea*), which forms tufa crusts, with growth stages described by Gomont as varieties *incrustatum* and *cataractarum*.

**Saline and peatland systems.** Conard and Kufferath studied the Lillo brackish waters, and Conard's 1942 Ardennes sphagnum survey identified sphagnophilous taxa such as *Heliochrysis sphagnicola*. Desmidiaceae were used as indicators of acidic peat conditions.

**Historical ecology.** Gilliet's work on Charophyceae and Symoens's work on Ardennes flora raised the question of whether cold-water (orophile) algae are relicts of the Dryas Recent period. The author judged that birds disperse algae efficiently, so past factors may play only a secondary role.

**Tropical and overseas systems.** Van Oye found that tropical algal plankton follows rainfall periodicity. Damas found extreme cyanobacterial dominance in Rwandan lakes. In the Katanga reservoirs, Macis reported algal blooms of *Microcystis*, *Synedra* and *Melosira*.

## Key Findings

- **Inventory milestones:** The first comprehensive Belgian inventory (Prodrome, 1898–1903) listed 322 genera, 1228 species and about 1500 forms, of which 668 were Bacillariophyceae. Kufferath's 1930 table gave 2631 forms, although about 30 flagellates were later removed from the algae.
- **Ardennes count:** Symoens raised the known algal forms in the Ardennes and neighbouring regions from about 1300 to more than 1500.
- **Marine plankton:** A station off Ostende (7–8 September 1906) collected 72 planktonic algal species; dominant taxa included *Chaetoceros* spp. and *Biddulphia* spp.
- **Production model:** Huet's formula P = 10kB, where P is fish production (kg/ha/year), k is a coefficient, and B is biogenic capacity (scale I to X), was applied to Belgian waters.
- **Algal–biocoenosis links:** Symoens (1937) correlated algal associations with biogenic capacity. *Melosira varians*–*Diatoma vulgare* and *Cladophora glomerata* associations marked high-capacity condrusien streams (VI–IX), while *Eunotia exigua*–*Pinnularia subcapitata* and *Microspora tumidula* marked fagnard streams with zero capacity.
- **Ry Colas study:** Conard's 1942 stream study recorded about 300 algae and worked with about 15 specialists, making it a model of team limnology.
- **Lake Tanganyika:** Symoens recorded mass blooms of *Anabaena flos-aquae* in late dry season (September–October) in the northern basin.
- **Katanga reservoirs:** Annual fish production was estimated at about 2400 t for N'Zilo and 5000–6000 t for Mwadingusha (Symoens, 1961).
- **Antarctic:** The [[Belgica Expedition]] (1897–99) produced algal results; Van Heurck's last work covered its diatoms (1909), and De Wildeman wrote on the other algae (1900, 1935).
- **Congo flora:** [[Desmidiaceae]] studies by Van Oye, and Kufferath's Congo and Tanganyika plankton work, form a major part of the Central African record.
- **Critique of Belgian phycology:**
  1. Lack of team research. Most work was by isolated scientists, unlike the [[Freshwater Biological Association]] at Windermere.
  2. Insufficient technical modernity. Plankton studies stayed at the microscope level, quantitative nanoplankton analysis was rare, and modern production methods (pigment extraction, ¹⁴C uptake) were not adopted.
  3. Culture work declined after World War II.
- **Central African research future:** The author argues that long-term limnological work requires permanent institutions, such as IRSAC and the Universities of Lovanium and Elisabethville, with African researchers participating in studies of their own countries.
- **Bulletin contribution:** The *Bulletin* published about 128 articles (32% of Belgian algal floristics and ecology) and 6 articles (12% of Congolese phycology).
