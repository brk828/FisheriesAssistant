# Apparatus for Sampling Gut Contents of Large, Living Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

Crossman and Hamilton (1978) describe a field apparatus for flushing and retaining gut contents from large, living fishes using water supplied by a 12-volt portable bilge pump. The device was developed for [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy*) in Nogies Creek, Ontario, a small population under intensive study whose individuals must be returned alive. It was also applied to [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). The authors report large sample numbers, no detectable mortality attributed to the treatment, and a validation trial in which dissected stomachs of 20 largemouth bass contained no detectable food after sampling.

## Background & Description

- **Problem:** Traditional diet studies require killing fish, which is unacceptable for valuable populations. Stomach flushing, known as [[Gastric Lavage]], offers a non-lethal alternative.
- **Prior methods reviewed:** White (1930); Seaburg (1957) and Seaburg & Moyle (1964); Gengerke et al. (1973); Baker & Fraser (1976), who injected water through the anus for small fish; Legler (1977) for turtles; and Foster (1977), who used a [[Pulsed Gastric Lavage]] approach with a hand-pumped agricultural sprayer.
- **Design components:** (1) a holding device that keeps the anaesthetized fish head-down so the stomach empties; (2) control of water volume and pressure; (3) capture of flushed contents.
- **Apparatus parts (Fig. 1, 12 labelled components):**
  - Inclined plexiglass board with padded hinged gates, a catch basin, and a leather mouth-restraint strap.
  - Cotton drawstring sample bag labelled with sample number, date and tag number.
  - Standard 12-volt bilge pump housed in a perforated plexiglass box slung over the boat gunwale.
  - Low-pressure rubber hose, pistol-grip garden hose valve, and rigid polyethylene delivery tube.
  - Angler's mechanical hook removers ("disgorgers"), preferred over surgical forceps.
- **Size range:** Effective for muskellunge 250–1136 mm TL and largemouth bass over 200 mm TL.
- **Informal name:** The board was nicknamed "the Barf-board," from Board Allowing Regurgitation by Fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The study population of muskellunge in Nogies Creek is small and intensively monitored, so non-lethal sampling is essential.
- Water is pumped from the creek, and the rigid box helps keep floating vegetation from clogging the pump.
- Fish are held in fresh water in the boat after sampling to recover from anaesthesia before release.
- Food items in muskellunge can be detected externally by palpation during abdominal massage, aiding complete sampling.
- For smaller fish (muskellunge under 225 mm TL; largemouth bass under 200 mm), a 50 cm³ plastic syringe with fine tubing was used instead, as in several published accounts.

## Key Findings

- **Sample totals at Nogies Creek:**
  - Muskellunge: 835 samples, including 135 multiple samples (maximum of four samples from each of two individuals).
  - Largemouth bass: 1253 samples, including 365 multiple samples (maximum of four samples from each of two individuals).
- **Mortality:** The authors have no evidence of mortality directly caused by the treatment, and many individuals were sampled repeatedly.
- **Validation:** Of 20 largemouth bass sampled with the method, then killed and dissected, no detectable food items were found in the stomachs.
- **Effectiveness:** The apparatus is described as highly effective, simple to use, and non-injurious to fish.
