## Executive Summary

This paper by T. T. Macan examines the community structure of Corixidae (water boatmen) in two small, rich lowland lakes ("meres") in Shropshire, England — [[Crose Mere]] and [[Sweat Mere]] — comparing findings to prior surveys of Danish lakes, particularly [[Esrom Lake]]. Although the taxon studied is an aquatic insect family rather than fish, the study's core aim mirrors classic ichthyofaunal survey work: using indicator species composition of Corixidae to characterize lake trophic status, size, and successional stage of littoral vegetation. The paper also resolves a taxonomic confusion between *Sigara striata* and *Sigara dorsalis* (previously misnamed *S. lacustris* by the author). Results broadly confirm a known ecological succession of corixid species tied to organic matter accumulation in reed-beds, with some Shropshire-specific deviations (e.g., presence of *Callicorixa praeusta* and *Sigara lateralis* in unusual microhabitats).

## Background & Description

While the paper is taxonomic/ecological rather than anatomical, it addresses a key nomenclatural/morphological distinction:

- **[[Sigara Dorsalis]] vs. [[Sigara Striata]]**: Comparison of Danish and British material revealed that the name *S. striata* had been erroneously applied to two distinct species. The British species, previously misnamed *S. lacustris* by Macan (believing it undescribed), is in fact *S. dorsalis*, a name originally assigned by Leach in 1818 to a British specimen but later synonymized incorrectly with *S. striata*.
- No detailed morphological descriptions (body size, coloration, structural traits) of individual corixid species are given in this text; species identification is treated as established via prior taxonomic literature (Macan, 1954a, 1954b).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper centers on habitat associations and successional dynamics of corixid species within eutrophic lake and reed-swamp environments:

- **General habitat gradient**: Corixid species distribution correlates with degree of wave exposure, substrate type (sand vs. organic detritus), and stage of reed-bed to fen succession.
- **Documented Danish succession** (as organic matter accumulates in *Phragmites* beds):
  *S. striata* (≈ [[Sigara Dorsalis]] in Britain) → *[[Hesperocorixa Linnei]]* → *[[Hesperocorixa Sahlbergi]]*
  - *S. dorsalis*/*S. striata*: found from exposed sandy substrate to areas with moderate organic accumulation; most tolerant of wave action among larger corixids.
  - *H. linnei*: replaces *S. dorsalis* as organic matter increases.
  - *H. sahlbergi*: dominant where reed-bed transitions into fen (most organically enriched, most sheltered).
  - *[[Sigara Falleni]]*: not strictly part of this successional sequence; favors moderately exposed sites with fine organic sediment deposition (e.g., boat harbours, inflow ditches); in Britain associated mainly with hard-water lakes and rivers.
  - *[[Callicorixa Praeusta]]*: associated with productive (eutrophic) conditions but seemingly restricted to lakes below a certain size threshold — abundant in small [[Blelham Tarn]] (0.11 km²) but scarce in larger, otherwise similar [[Esthwaite Water]] (1.0 km²); also scarce in the larger Danish lakes studied.
- **Crose Mere findings** (10 stations, single collection date 6 Nov 1961):

  | Species | Count |
  |---|---|
  | *Sigara falleni* | 145 |
  | *Callicorixa praeusta* | 74 |
  | *Sigara dorsalis* | 20 |
  | *Sigara lateralis* | 13 |
  | *Sigara distincta* | 6 |
  | *Sigara concinna* | few |
  | *Sigara fossarum* | few |
  | *Arctocorisa germari* | few |
  | *Corixa punctata* | few (in spring-fed pool, not true lake biotope) |

  - *S. lateralis*, atypical for lake habitats, was found in a cattle-fouled sandy bay — likely attracted by organic pollution, as it is otherwise common in small polluted ponds.
  - *S. distincta* and *S. fossarum*, which typically replace *S. dorsalis* in softer-water, moderately productive lakes (e.g., [[Windermere]]), were notably scarce in both Shropshire meres and the Danish lakes — an anomaly given the meres' calcareous, productive nature.
  - *C. praeusta* dominated 2 of 10 stations; *S. falleni* dominated the remainder (excluding the sandy bay and an alder-swamp station with no corixids present).

- **Sweat Mere findings** (4 stations, collected 24 Feb 1962; see Table 1):
  - No corixids recovered from the deepest open-water zone.
  - Near open water: species composition and proportions matched Crose Mere.
  - Mid-reed-swamp (*Typha*): *H. sahlbergi*, *H. linnei*, and pond-associated *Corixa punctata* present.
  - Deepest into reed-swamp (*Carex paniculata* pools): *H. sahlbergi* predominant.
  - Excluding *C. punctata* (attributed to the mere's small size), the observed succession closely parallels the Danish pattern.

## Key Findings

- **Indicator value confirmed**: Corixid species composition in the Shropshire meres broadly supports Macan's (1955) claim that corixid assemblages reflect general lake type/trophic status, paralleling patterns from Danish lowland lakes (notably [[Esrom Lake]]).
- **Taxonomic correction**: *Sigara lacustris* (Macan) is a synonym error; the valid name for the British species previously conflated with *S. striata* is *[[Sigara Dorsalis]]* (Leach, 1818).
- **Lake-size effect on *C. praeusta***: Reinforces prior evidence (Macan, 1954b) that this species' abundance is tied not just to productivity but also to small lake size, a pattern consistent between the Lake District (Blelham vs. Esthwaite) and now the Shropshire meres.
- **Anomalous scarcity**: *S. distincta* and *S. fossarum*, common in soft-water, moderately productive English lakes like [[Windermere]], were unexpectedly rare in both hard-water Shropshire meres and Danish lakes, suggesting water chemistry (hardness) may modulate their distribution independent of organic enrichment stage.
- **Microhabitat/pollution association**: *S. lateralis* occurrence in a cattle-fouled sandy bay suggests sensitivity to localized organic pollution rather than general lake conditions, consistent with its known preference for small polluted ponds.
- **Succession confirmed in situ**: Sweat Mere data (Table 1) directly demonstrate the reed-swamp-to-fen succession sequence — *S. dorsalis*/*S. falleni* near open water → *H. linnei*/*H. sahlbergi* mid-swamp → *H. sahlbergi* dominant in the most terrestrialized *Carex* pools — matching the Danish successional model with only minor site-specific variation (e.g., *C. punctata* presence attributed to Sweat Mere's small scale).
- **Methodological note**: Findings are based on single-visit collections (one day each per mere), limiting seasonal/temporal generalization, though spatial consistency within Crose Mere (10 stations) supports robustness of the reported patterns.
