## Executive Summary

This document is a critical policy response, authored on behalf of the Colorado River Wildlife Council, opposing a proposal to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] (*Osmerus mordax*) into [[Lake Powell]] as a forage-fish solution to declining predatory fish populations. The author argues that the proposal is scientifically premature, poorly supported by limnological data, and likely to produce unintended ecological consequences, drawing on historical precedent from other non-native fish introductions (e.g., [[Inland Silverside]], [[Threadfin Shad]]). A secondary section reproduces a resolution from the 7th International Symposium of [[Astacology]] (Lausanne, 1987) addressing the spread of the crayfish plague pathogen *[[Aphanomyces astaci]]* via the international trade in live crayfish, using it as a cautionary parallel to fish introductions.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data on rainbow smelt are provided in this text; the paper is a management/policy critique rather than a taxonomic or anatomical study. The only biologically descriptive content concerns disease agents:

- ***Aphanomyces astaci*** (crayfish plague / "aphanomycosis") — an oomycete parasite pathogenic to native European crayfish (*Astacus* spp.), introduced to Europe circa 1860 via imported [[Signal Crayfish]] (*Pacifastacus leniusculus*) from the western United States.
- Signal crayfish act as an asymptomatic reservoir/carrier of the pathogen, tolerating infection without the lethal effects seen in naïve native *Astacus* populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Rainbow Smelt Introduction Context (Lake Powell / Colorado River System):**
- Proposed role: forage species intended to stabilize declining or cyclic predatory fish populations and potentially act as a "buffer" species reducing predation pressure on native fishes.
- Reservoirs are characterized as inherently unstable, human-managed systems where water management rarely prioritizes fish or fishery outcomes, undermining expectations that a new introduction could stabilize fish populations.
- Concern that smelt introduction could paradoxically **sustain elevated predator populations**, thereby *increasing* rather than decreasing predation pressure on small, vulnerable populations of native and threatened/endangered ([[T&E Species]]) fishes.
- Uncertainty flagged regarding whether adequate deep-water plankton resources exist in the reservoir to support a self-sustaining smelt population — a basic trophic prerequisite left unverified by ongoing limnological studies.

**Precedent for Range Expansion and Human-Assisted Spread:**
- [[Rainbow Smelt]]: documented spread down the Mississippi River as far south as Kentucky (Burr and Mayden 1980), illustrating potential for extensive unplanned dispersal from an initial introduction site.
- [[Inland Silverside]]: introduced to Clear Lake, northern California in 1967; by 1981 had become abundant throughout southern California reservoirs and intermediate waters — a documented case of rapid, extensive range expansion.
- Angler behavior (e.g., use of live wells) identified as an accelerating vector for unintentional fish translocation, implicated in the spread of inland silverside, threadfin shad, and white bass throughout California waterways.
- Ecological risk assessments for reservoir introductions are argued to require evaluation of both upstream and downstream connected systems, not just the target reservoir.

**Crayfish Plague Case Study (European Astacology Resolution, 1987):**
- Highlights risks of live animal trade (food, bait, pet trades) as a vector for pathogen introduction across regions and taxa.
- *Aphanomyces astaci* introduction via signal crayfish decimated native *Astacus* populations in Europe, exemplifying "grave menace to native ... populations from introduction of undesirable exotic" congeners.
- Cross-taxon disease transmission risk noted: crayfish-borne parasites and diseases can also expose co-occurring fish populations.

## Key Findings

- **No demonstrated net benefit:** The claim that smelt introductions have been unequivocally "beneficial ... without negative impacts" in the Missouri River system is disputed as unsupported by sufficient evidence.
- **Premature proposal:** Ongoing reservoir limnological studies had not yet produced results (e.g., confirmation of adequate deep-water plankton biomass) needed to justify introduction.
- **High probability of uncontrolled spread:** Historical analogs (rainbow smelt in the Mississippi, inland silverside in California) indicate introduced forage fish are likely to disperse well beyond the target reservoir, aided by both natural movement and human vectors (angler live-wells).
- **Buffer-species hypothesis is double-edged:** A forage species intended to reduce predation on native fish could equally sustain elevated predator biomass, worsening predation pressure on native and T&E fish populations.
- **Inadequate T&E species risk assessment:** The proposal's evaluation of threatened and endangered species impacts is characterized as inadequate and internally contradictory; given the precarious status of T&E species in the system, any non-zero risk is argued to warrant prohibition of the introduction.
- **Systemic critique of reservoir fisheries management:** The commentary argues that instability in reservoir fish populations stems largely from water management practices, not fishery management failures, and that public expectations should be recalibrated accordingly rather than solved via novel species introductions.
- **Crayfish plague as a cautionary parallel:** The Lausanne Astacology resolution formally recommends that governments halt importation of live crayfish (except for regulated research/restocking) and ensure disease/parasite-free status, reinforcing the broader precautionary principle argued in the smelt case — that species introductions carry disease and ecological transmission risks disproportionate to demonstrated benefits.
- **Guiding precautionary philosophy:** The response closes by invoking Aldo Leopold's warning that "[[Exotic Wildlife]] has served as a perfect alibi for postponing the practice of game management," framing introductions as a substitute for, rather than a solution to, sound management.
