# Habitat Conservation Measures for Cui-ui Recovery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #chasmistes

## Executive Summary

This 1994 [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Service) document, prepared by the Nevada Ecological Services State Office and the Northern Nevada Fishery Resource Office, is the "action plan" required by the [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]] (Second Revision) for [[Cui-ui]] (*[[Chasmistes Cujus]]*). It evaluates habitat conservation measures that could supply the water equivalent of 110,000 acre-feet per year needed for recovery. The evaluation covers instream flow, fish passage over the [[Truckee River Delta]], and restoration of the lower river's riparian canopy. Water quality and fish passage facility operation are addressed in separate documents.

The Service concludes that the measures differ widely in certainty. Only [[Water Rights Acquisition]] is treated as a secure benefit. The document recommends acquiring up to 110,000 acre-feet of water rights while pursuing non-acquisition measures, and using [[Recoupment]] of excess [[Newlands Project]] diversions as an interim source.

## Background & Description

**Recovery objective.** The Recovery Plan sets the goal of giving cui-ui at least a 0.95 probability of persisting for 200 years. The recovery plan treats habitat as sufficient if the average annual discharge of the [[Truckee River]] into [[Pyramid Lake]] is supplemented by 110,000 acre-feet (or equivalent). Supplementation must reach at least 5,000 acre-feet per year if begun by 1994. The document defines "secure" as ensuring benefits for at least 200 years, and "current average annual discharge" to include reasonably certain future diversions.

**Scope.** Six habitat conservation categories were evaluated for feasibility and contribution to the target. Much of the information comes from Appendix D of the Recovery Plan, updated with data from the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Reclamation) and an unpublished report by David de Bruyn (April 1992).

**Historical context.** The document notes that extensive modification of Truckee River discharge and channel configuration during the 20th century created suboptimal spawning conditions for cui-ui.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Diversion pressure.** On a long-term average, about 125,000 acre-feet of Truckee River water (roughly 20 percent of average annual total discharge) is diverted to the Newlands Project under the [[Operating Criteria And Procedures]] (OCAP). About 100,000 acre-feet go to the [[Carson Division]] and 25,000 acre-feet to the [[Truckee Division]], including delivery losses. This is the largest single diversion from the river.

**Thermal and riparian conditions.** Based on a Reclamation temperature model (calibrated in 1984 at the Marble Bluff Fish Facility), restoring a mature [[Cottonwood-Willow Community]] could lower water temperatures by about 1°F under normal air temperatures. This would reduce the minimum spawning flows needed in May and June by about 300 cfs.

**Delta barrier.** The Truckee River delta blocks upstream cui-ui passage when Pyramid Lake elevation is generally below 3,812 feet mean sea level (msl).

**Land use.** The Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe may expand farming in the historic flood plain under its [[Orr Ditch Decree]] rights, which could degrade riparian communities and remove reaches from restoration consideration.

## Key Findings

### 1. Reduce Truckee River diversions to the Newlands Project (non-acquisition)

| Measure | Estimated benefit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bench/bottom land criteria | 6,000 acre-feet/yr (Truckee River share) | Based on a 320,000 acre-foot demand; requires Federal District Court approval |
| Lahontan Reservoir monthly storage targets in OCAP | ~7,000 acre-feet/yr | Varies with Carson discharge, demand, and reservoir storage |
| Lining Truckee Canal | 21,000 acre-feet/yr (Reclamation); 15,000 acre-feet/yr (de Bruyn) | Estimates differ |
| Lining smaller canals (Truckee Division, Soda Lake area) | ~5,250 acre-feet/yr | de Bruyn estimate |
| Metering farm headgates | 10,000 acre-feet/yr | de Bruyn estimate |
| Lining 45 miles of main canals | 40,600–81,900 acre-feet/yr lost to seepage | Draft Reclamation efficiency study (Sept. 29, 1993) |
| Reduced carry-over storage | ~20,000 acre-feet/yr | Lowers May–June storage target from 215,000 to 150,000 acre-feet and October target from 80,000 to 10,000 acre-feet |
| Fallon Naval Air Station water use reduction | ~5,000 acre-feet/yr (2,750 acre-feet remain in river) | Preliminary estimate |
| Upper Carson River irrigation management | Not estimated | Would apply Alpine Decree allocation and improve efficiency |

The bench/bottom criteria would reduce project-wide duty on 57,000 acres from 3.685 to 3.539 acre-feet per acre per year. The document notes that 2,750 acre-feet from the Fallon measure would stay in the river, and that the reductions depend on assumptions about delivery efficiency (70 percent).

### 2. Rehabilitate the lower Truckee River

- Restoring the historic cottonwood/willow canopy could provide benefits equivalent to about 36,000 acre-feet of water. Because the benefit occurs only during spawning runs under normal air temperatures, the average annual equivalent is lower.
- Full restoration along the lower river is uncertain because of current and planned farming in the flood plain. Sufficient restoration sites may not be available.
- Maturation takes about 25 years and is vulnerable to fire, flood, and land-management changes.
- The document concludes these benefits should not be counted until a mature canopy is established.

### 3. Improve fish passage over the Truckee River Delta

- Lowering the passage threshold from 3,812 to 3,800 feet msl might yield 25,000–35,000 acre-feet/year of inflow to Pyramid Lake (Figure D-6, Recovery Plan).
- Because the hydrologic assumptions may overstate benefits, the Service considers 15,000–20,000 acre-feet/year more reasonable.
- These benefits should not be counted until secured.

### 4. Implement the Truckee River Operating Agreement (TROA)

- The Secretary of the Interior is authorized by Public Law 101-618 to negotiate [[Truckee River Operating Agreement]] (TROA).
- As drafted, TROA would slightly reduce overall discharge to Pyramid Lake but increase spawning-period flows. The net benefit is about 5,000 acre-feet/year.
- Storage of acquired water in upstream reservoirs could add value but is uncertain while negotiations continue.

### 5. Acquire water rights

Acquisition gives the most assured benefits because of ownership security and delivery control through upstream storage. It is authorized by Section 5 of the [[Endangered Species Act]] and subsection 207(c) of Public Law 101-618. Acquisition under 207(c) requires an approved TROA. Currently there is no authority to store acquired water in upstream reservoirs.

Excluded from consideration: Orr Ditch Decree rights in Truckee Meadows (tied to TROA negotiations) and the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe's Claims No. 1 and 2 (likely to be used for agriculture).

Available sources:

- **Upstream of Reno:** ~6,000 acre-feet (limited by California demand).
- **Reno to Derby Dam:** ~3,000 acre-feet.
- **Downstream of Derby Dam:** ~5,000 acre-feet.
- **Truckee Division:** ~19,000 acre-feet of headgate demand, plus ~6,000 acre-feet from avoided conveyance losses.
- **Carson Division:** ~100,000 acre-feet of headgate demand, saving about 100,000 acre-feet/year in the Truckee River. Including Truckee Division retirement would raise the saving to 125,000 acre-feet/year.

### 6. Recoup excess water from the Newlands Project

Under Public Law 101-618, the Secretary and [[Truckee-Carson Irrigation District]] are negotiating recoupment of about one million acre-feet diverted in excess of OCAP from 1973 through 1987. Preliminary estimates suggest about 40,000 acre-feet/year can be recouped over 25 years. Because recoupment is temporary and ends within 25 years, it cannot provide the 200-year security the Recovery Plan requires. It is, however, a useful interim source for meeting the 5,000 acre-feet/year incremental objective while permanent measures are implemented. It would allow about 8 years to complete the acquisition NEPA process.

### Recommendation

- The document states that over 200,000 acre-feet of water rights and equivalent benefits are potentially available, but the certainty of each measure varies greatly.
- Selection should weigh **certainty of benefits** and **rate of achievement**.
- **Strategy:** Secure up to 110,000 acre-feet of water rights while continuing to evaluate and secure non-acquisition benefits. Acquisition would likely take 20 to 30 years, and any equivalent benefits obtained first would reduce the acquisition target.
- **Interim measure:** Begin recoupment immediately to meet the 5,000 acre-feet/year minimum. Recoupment benefits must be replaced with long-term benefits once it ends.
- At an acquisition rate of 5,000 acre-feet/year, cui-ui could be recommended for removal from the endangered species list in 22 years.
