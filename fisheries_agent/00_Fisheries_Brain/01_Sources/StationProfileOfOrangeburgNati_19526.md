# Station Profile: Orangeburg National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
The Orangeburg National Fish Hatchery, established in 1912 under the Appropriation Act (36 Stat. 1441), is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Orangeburg, South Carolina. Originally constructed to support the [[Farm Pond Program]] by rearing warmwater sportfish for stocking private waters, the station's mission has since shifted toward conservation-focused aquaculture. Current production emphasizes [[Striped Bass]], [[Redbreast Sunfish]], and the federally endangered [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], primarily for restoration of depleted native stocks in South Carolina's river systems, coastal streams, and impoundments. The hatchery maintains an active Cooperative Agreement with the State of South Carolina, funded through the Service's Federal Aid Program, to support sturgeon restoration work.

## Background & Description
This document is a station/administrative profile rather than a biological or morphological study; no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data for any reared species are provided in the source text. Species reared historically and currently include:

- **[[Channel Catfish]]** and **[[Blue Catfish]]** (family Ictaluridae)
- **[[Bluegill]]** and **[[Redear Sunfish]]** (family Centrarchidae)
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (family Centrarchidae)
- **[[Redbreast Sunfish]]** (family Centrarchidae)
- **[[Tilapia]]** (family Cichlidae) — introduced for aquatic vegetation control
- **[[Striped Bass]]** (family Moronidae)
- **[[Shortnose Sturgeon]]** (family Acipenseridae) — federally listed as endangered

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Facility footprint:** 56 acres at the main station (deep well and reservoir water supply) plus a 204.78-acre substation (acquired 1961) operating on gravity-flow reservoir water.
- **Infrastructure:** 30 production ponds, 12 circular tanks, holding house, office/aquarium house, and supporting maintenance/storage buildings.
- **Historical role:** Supplied warmwater sportfish ([[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]]) to private waters and small impoundments under the now-discontinued Farm Pond Program; later expanded to include [[Blue Catfish]], [[Redbreast Sunfish]], and [[Tilapia]] (for vegetation control in ponds).
- **Current ecological role:** Functions as a conservation hatchery supporting restoration of anadromous and riverine species in South Carolina river systems, coastal streams, and lakes/impoundments — notably the depleted [[Striped Bass]] population and the endangered [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], the latter under a state-federal cooperative restoration program.
- **Distribution range:** Fish produced at Orangeburg NFH have been distributed to waters in Georgia, North Carolina, and South Carolina.

## Key Findings
- Hatchery mission transitioned from general warmwater sportfish production (pre-Farm Pond Program era) to targeted conservation aquaculture for [[Striped Bass]], [[Redbreast Sunfish]], and [[Shortnose Sturgeon]].
- The station plays a direct role in an endangered species recovery program (shortnose sturgeon) via a formal Cooperative Agreement with the State of South Carolina, funded under the Service's Federal Aid Program.
- Total fiscal year 1990 distribution across Federal, State, and International waters equaled **1,681,350** fish/units, with the largest share (1,310,778) attributed to "Other Federal Waters" — indicating substantial output scale for restoration stocking.
- Staffing grew from 7.0 FTEs (1991–1992) to a projected 9.0 FTEs (1993), alongside rising operations budgets (from $258.3K in 1991 to an estimated $340.6K in 1994) and a notable increase in maintenance funding by 1993 ($248.2K), reflecting expanded institutional investment in the hatchery's conservation mission.
- The dual-water-source design (deep well + reservoir at main station; gravity-flow reservoir at the substation) supports flexible production management across warmwater and anadromous species requirements.
