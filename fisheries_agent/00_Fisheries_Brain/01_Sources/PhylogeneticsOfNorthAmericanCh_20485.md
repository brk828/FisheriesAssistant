# Phylogenetics of North American Chubs (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #freshwater-biology #phylogenetics

## Executive Summary

This document is a graduate research proposal by Mark Henry Sabaj (University of Illinois, Illinois Natural History Survey) proposing to resolve long-standing phylogenetic ambiguities among North American [[Cyprinidae|minnows]], specifically the "chub" clade and two problematic western genera, [[Rhinichthys]] and [[Agosia]]. The study integrates two novel and largely unexploited data sets — **mitochondrial DNA sequences of the cytochrome b gene** and **reproductive strategy/behavior data** — to independently test relationships previously hypothesized from osteological and scale (lepidological) characters by [[Cavender and Coburn]] (1992) and [[Coburn and Cavender]] (1992). The proposal outlines a sampling strategy spanning 14 genera of North American chubs plus western cyprinids, and includes a field expedition to the Gila River and Little Colorado River drainages in Arizona to collect several state- and federally threatened species.

## Background & Description

While this proposal is primarily phylogenetic/methodological rather than a morphological description of a single species, it summarizes taxonomically diagnostic morphological work relevant to chub classification:

- **Family Cyprinidae** comprises >2,070 species worldwide (Nelson, 1984) and ~300 species in ~50 genera in North America (Burr and Mayden, 1992), making it the most diverse North American fish family.
- Cladistic analyses based on **osteology and scale (lepidological) morphology** (Chen et al., 1984; Howes, 1987; [[Cavender and Coburn]], 1992) divide Cyprinidae into two major subfamilies:
  - **Leuciscinae** (includes essentially all North American cyprinids, informally the "phoxinins")
  - **Cyprininae**
- Within Leuciscinae, [[Coburn and Cavender]] (1992) used 116 morphological characters across 48 taxa to define three phoxinin clades:
  - **Western clade** (20 genera)
  - **Chub clade** (16 genera)
  - **Shiner clade** (9 genera)
- The genus *[[Notemigonus crysoleucas]]* (golden shiner) is anomalously placed outside the phoxinin group, in the Eurasian-affiliated leuciscin clade, and is used as an **outgroup** in cladistic analyses.
- **Diagnostic osteological characters** distinguish the chub clade, but *[[Rhinichthys]]* and *[[Agosia]]* (both nominally "western" genera) share several chub-clade osteological synapomorphies, creating persistent taxonomic ambiguity (Cavender and Coburn, 1987; Mayden, 1989).
- Sensitivity analyses by Coburn and Cavender (1992) show that inclusion/exclusion of 18 scale characters and two Asian cyprinid taxa dramatically shifts the placement of *Rhinichthys* and *Agosia*:
  - Deleting scale characters nests *Rhinichthys + Agosia* within the chub clade.
  - Including scale characters but excluding two Asian taxa submerges the entire chub clade within an expanded western clade in a trichotomy with *Rhinichthys* and *Agosia*.
  - The shiner clade remains stable as sister to chub+western taxa across all analyses.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Target taxa occupy North American freshwater drainages, with specific focus on the **American Southwest** (Gila River and Little Colorado River systems, Arizona) and **Midwestern (Illinois) waterways**.
- Several southwestern species are habitat specialists with restricted, threatened distributions:
  - *[[Gila robusta robusta]]* (Colorado roundtail chub) — upper Gila River and Little Colorado River; State Threatened.
  - *[[Lepidomeda vittata]]* (little Colorado spinedace) — upper Little Colorado River; State and Federally Threatened.
  - *[[Meda fulgida]]* (spikedace) — upper Gila River; State and Federally Threatened.
  - *[[Tiaroga cobitis]]* (loach minnow) — upper Gila River; State and Federally Threatened.
  - *[[Rhinichthys osculus]]* (speckled dace) — upper Gila River and Little Colorado River; not listed.
  - *[[Agosia chrysogaster]]* (longfin dace) — upper Gila River; not listed.
- Non-listed, widespread taxa used for comparison include *[[Notemigonus crysoleucas]]* (golden shiner) and *[[Pimephales promelas]]* (fathead minnow), both collected in the upper Gila River despite being more broadly distributed generalist species.
- Nongame [[Catostomidae|suckers]] were also proposed as voucher specimens from the same habitats.
- The field expedition was explicitly timed (late May) to coincide with **spawning seasons** of southwestern chub species, enabling direct behavioral observation and video documentation of reproductive strategies in situ — reflecting the ecological importance of spawning habitat (e.g., pebble nest substrates) discussed in related work on *[[Nocomis]]* and *[[Semotilus]]* pebble-nest builders.

## Key Findings

- **Unresolved phylogenetic problem**: The systematic position of *[[Rhinichthys]]* and *[[Agosia]]* relative to the North American chub clade remains unresolved using morphological/osteological data alone; Coburn and Cavender (1992) explicitly called for further investigation.
- **Proposed dual approach**: The study proposes to test chub clade relationships using two independent, non-osteological data sets:
  1. **Cytochrome b mtDNA sequences** — building on precedent from Schmidt and Gold (1993), who sequenced the complete cytochrome b gene in the cherryfin shiner (*[[Lythrurus roseipinnis]]*), and Song (1994), who used cytochrome b sequences to resolve intergeneric relationships in Percidae.
  2. **Reproductive strategies** — building on Johnston and Page (1992), who identified **eight reproductive strategies** across 95 cyprinid species and mapped them onto a morphology-based phylogeny, but concluded a full cyprinid phylogeny based solely on behavior was not yet feasible.
- **Preliminary molecular work**: Using PCR primers originally designed for percid studies, the author and Choon-Bok Song had already begun sequencing cytochrome b in two species of *Rhinichthys* and one chub clade representative, *[[Nocomis micropogon]]*.
- **Taxonomic scope**: The proposed study would sample representatives of **all 14 genera** of North American chubs, multiple *Rhinichthys* species, the monotypic *Agosia*, and several western cyprinids — the broadest combined morphological/molecular/behavioral sampling of this group proposed at the time.
- **Conservation relevance**: Several southwestern target taxa carry State Threatened (ST) and/or Federally Threatened (LT) status, requiring permitting through the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Arizona Game and Fish Department; collection amendments limited take to 5 individuals per listed species (4 frozen for molecular work, 1 formalin-preserved voucher).
- **Prior related work** by the author on reproductive behavior in pebble-nest-building minnows (*[[Nocomis]]*, *[[Exoglossum]]*, *[[Semotilus]]*) established methodological precedent for behavioral phylogenetics within Cyprinidae, including description of a novel spawning behavior, "retroflexure," in female creek chubs (*[[Semotilus atromaculatus]]*).
