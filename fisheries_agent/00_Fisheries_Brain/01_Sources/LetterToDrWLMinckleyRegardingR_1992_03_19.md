## Executive Summary
This document is a 1992 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) to Dr. W.L. Minckley, Leader of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), responding to a March 4, 1992 DFRT recommendation. The letter addresses the proposed reclassification of two southwestern cyprinid fishes — [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — from threatened to endangered status, and discusses the need for captive propagation, refugia, and continued biological/habitat research. The Service concurs that reclassification "may be warranted" but defers action due to competing listing priorities, while committing to species monitoring support and potential emergency refugium capacity at the Dexter National Fish Hatchery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are native freshwater fishes of the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, associated with the [[Gila River Basin]] drainage system in Arizona and New Mexico.
- Referred to collectively as part of the imperiled "native fish" fauna of southwestern desert streams under threat of extinction.
- Habitat requirements are acknowledged as insufficiently understood at the time of writing, with the letter explicitly calling for "additional research...on the biology and habitat requirements of both species."
- Wild population security is treated as precarious enough to warrant contingency planning for emergency captive refugia.

## Key Findings
- The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) reviewed the status of [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] and **agreed with DFRT that reclassification from threatened to endangered may be warranted**.
- A proposed rule for reclassification was anticipated but **delayed due to higher-priority listing actions** elsewhere in the Service's workload.
- The Service concurred with DFRT that the following actions are needed:
  - Development of **captive propagation techniques** for both species.
  - Establishment of **short-term refugia**.
  - Additional **research on biology and habitat requirements**.
- As of this letter, the Service was already **holding and/or culturing 16 species of threatened or endangered fish** at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], making captive management of spikedace/loach minnow a lower immediate priority relative to other listed species.
- The Service requested DFRT's **continued monitoring** of wild spikedace and loach minnow populations.
- A contingency commitment was made: if wild population security became compromised, the Service would establish a **temporary refugium at Dexter or elsewhere**.
- The letter underscores the **collaborative governance structure** between the Service, Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), and academic/DFRT leadership (Dr. W.L. Minckley, Arizona State University) in southwestern native fish conservation.
