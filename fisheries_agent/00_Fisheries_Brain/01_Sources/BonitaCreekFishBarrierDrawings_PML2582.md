# Bonita Creek Fish Barrier: Design, Cost Estimate, and Value Engineering Study

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-barrier-engineering

## Executive Summary

This document is a project file for the [[Bonita Creek Fish Barrier]], a concrete in-stream structure designed by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Phoenix Area Office. It contains construction drawings, a cost estimate, geotechnical logs, hydraulic and volume computation sheets, and correspondence for a [[Value Engineering]] (VE) study held the week of 15–19 May 2006. No fish species are named in the text, so the fish-related content concerns the barrier's function and its design. The cost table totals $1,332,567.17. The VE team was tasked with generating and developing alternatives to the baseline design.

## Background & Description

The Bonita Creek Fish Barrier is a grade-control and [[Fish Barrier]] structure built across a [[Bonita Creek]] channel. The drawing set shows:

- **Crest wall:** a fish barrier crest wall of structural concrete with a broomed finish on top and a smooth, rubbed finish on the downstream face.
- **Scour protection:** a cutoff/scour wall, with a [[Riprap]] apron on sand and gravel bedding. Apron length varies along the alignment, and riprap depths are tabulated by station.
- **Abutments:** rock-anchored abutments with abutment keys set into [[Bedrock]] (noted as "abutment rock").
- **Joints:** construction, contraction, and optional construction joints, with water stops and rounded edges.
- **Channel work:** the existing river thalweg is to be realigned between clear limits to match the notch in the barrier, with a [[Modified Thalweg]] profile.
- **Survey control:** stations reference a control line in the [[Arizona State Plane Coordinate System]], East Zone. Sheets cover stations from roughly 2+20 to 2+90.

The file also records project administration: a VE study report draft, a study team, and a meeting schedule.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The text gives little direct biological information. It does state that the structure's purpose is to act as a fish barrier in Bonita Creek, and the team included a biologist from [[Arizona State University]] (Paul Marsh) and a hydraulic engineer with a fisheries background (Brent Mefford, [[Technical Service Center]]).

**Geology (from geotechnical section C-C' and logs DH-1 to DH-3 and TP-1 to TP-3):**

- **[[Alluvium]]:** a mix of mostly gravel and sand with cobbles, boulders, and minor fines, with boulders up to about 2 ft in diameter. It fills the present channel to an unknown depth.
- **[[Conglomerate]]:** forms the canyon walls and underlies the alluvium. It is calcium-carbonate cemented (strong reaction with HCl), thinly to thickly bedded (0.2 to 2.0 ft), and contains boulders up to about 2 ft.
- **Overlying hard unit:** a gray, fine-grained, slightly weathered, hard rock about 14 ft above the channel on the right abutment. Fractures are randomly oriented and spaced 0.33 to 3.0 ft apart. Contacts with the conglomerate dip slightly downstream.

The geology bears on foundation design, abutment key depth, and scour protection, which are the main environmental-engineering constraints.

**3. VE methodology**
The study followed a standard [[Value Engineering Job Plan]] with five phases:
1. **Information:** identify components, functions, and costs; build a [[Function Analysis System Technique]] (FAST) diagram; find value mismatches.
2. **Creative:** unconstrained brainstorming on high-cost or mismatched components.
3. **Evaluation:** test ideas for viability against functions, limits, and criteria.
4. **Development:** refine proposals and compare them with the baseline, including life-cycle cost.
5. **Presentation:** present proposals, draft the report, and issue the final report.

Function-analysis rules require two-word functions (an active verb and a measurable noun), with "be" and "provide" avoided.
