# Two New Darters (*Etheostoma lugoi* and *E. segrex*) from Central Coahuila, Northern Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, published in *Ichthyological Exploration of Freshwaters* (Vol. 8, No. 2, 1997) by Steven M. Norris and W. L. Minckley, describes two new darters of the subgenus [[Oligocephalus]] from streams of the Chihuahuan Desert in central Coahuila, Mexico. Both species occur within or near the [[Bolsón de Cuatro Ciénegas]], an intermontane basin with exceptionally high biotic diversity, relict taxa and endemism. *Etheostoma lugoi* is confined to the largely isolated western part of the basin, while *E. segrex* lives in headwaters of the [[Río Salado de los Nadadores]], which flows east into the [[Río Grande]] (Rio Bravo del Norte) system. The authors use cranial osteology alongside conventional darter characters to place the species, and discuss ecology, conservation status and biogeography. Both species are considered highly threatened by water-resource exploitation.

## Background & Description

**Study region.** The Chihuahuan Desert of northern Mexico features thick Cretaceous limestones with subterranean solution channels and springs. Many regional rivers (e.g., Río San Juan, Río Salado) are spring-fed, some comparable to the [[Edwards Aquifer]] of Texas. Surface and groundwater are under growing pressure from population growth, agriculture, industry and development stimulated in part by the [[North American Free Trade Agreement]] (1993). The regional aquatic biota is strikingly endemic and is in a precipitous, possibly irreversible decline.

**Taxonomic placement.** The subgenus *Oligocephalus* contains about 20 species and is considered a cohesive but problematic group. The new species lack several defining characters of the subgenus (breeding males are not highly colored; females exceed males in size; supratemporal and infraorbital canals are always incomplete), yet share its general pigmentation (including a triad of spots at the caudal-fin base) and morphology (fusiform body, blunt snout). They are assigned provisionally to *Oligocephalus* pending a thorough phylogenetic evaluation, and are phenotypically closest to [[Etheostoma grahami]].

**Methods.** Counts and measurements follow [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1958), with several custom measurements (caudal peduncle length, pre-second dorsal-fin length, postorbital length, vertical scale series). Osteology used cleared-and-stained specimens (Taylor & Van Dyke method); vertebral counts came from radiographs. Institutional codes follow Leviton et al. (1985).

**Etheostoma lugoi (tufa darter).** Holotype UMMZ 233089 (35.4 mm SL, nuptial male), collected 10 April 1961 from Río Puente Chiquito, Coahuila. Paratypes were drawn from multiple lots at Río Mesquites and tributaries (ASU, UMMZ, KU, UANL, EUNAM-P collections). Small (adults to about 37.6 mm SL), slender-bodied, with seven pairs of branchiostegal rays (vs. six in related species), a blue throat in nuptial males, subdued body markings, and a scaleless pectoral-fin base.

**Etheostoma segrex (Rio Salado darter).** Holotype UMMZ 233090 (41.0 mm SL, nuptial male), collected 5 April 1961 at El Carrizo de la Montaña, Río Salado de los Nadadores. Distinguished by well-defined banding on the first dorsal fin, green-flank pigmentation, a small patch of scales at the pectoral-fin base, a bony splint on the prootic that divides the anterior opening of the trigeminofacialis chamber, and a more elongate body than *E. grahami*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Basin ichthyofauna.** Table 1 lists roughly 20 fish species of the Cuatro Ciénegas area (characins, minnows, suckers, catfishes, killifishes, livebearers, cichlids, sunfishes and percids). Many are endemic or undescribed differentiated populations. Faunal origins combine invasions by northern temperate taxa, southern/tropical taxa and older Mexican elements.
- **Western basin habitat (*E. lugoi*).** Found over open, unvegetated, complex bottoms of gravel- to cobble-sized tufa and stromatolite fragments, in channels 1.5–15 m wide, 30 cm–1.2 m deep, with currents of 10–25 cm/s. Water levels fluctuate only a few centimeters over three decades, which allows this habitat to persist. Only two fishes are closely associated: juvenile *Ictalurus* sp. cf. *lupus* and Mexican tetra (*Astyanax mexicanus*). Flathead catfish (*Pylodictis olivaris*) were recorded only four times in 38 years.
- **Río Salado habitat (*E. segrex*).** Riffles 1.5–3 m wide and 10–25 cm deep, moderate turbulence over gravel and small cobble, often among aquatic vegetation (*Potamogeton*, *Zannichellia*, *Cladophora glomerata*) in currents of 0.1–0.3 m/s. Associated species include *Cyprinella rutila*, *Astyanax mexicanus*, *Ictalurus* sp. cf. *lupus*, [[Gambusia marshi]] and juvenile [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]].
- **Historical change.** An 1846 military account describes the Río Salado de los Nadadores as a "large and rapid stream," far better watered than today. Base flow measured 0.1–0.2 m³/s in 1989–96, no more than half the 1958–70 discharge. Diversion, canal construction, groundwater extraction, grazing and farming have reduced habitat, and the upper canyon stream is now overgrown by reeds (*Phragmites*).
- **Threats.** An introduced alien cichlid, possibly *Hemichromis* sp., was observed in the Río Churince system (August 1996, March 1997). Darters do not occupy that isolated system, but the species could spread to others. Physical channel modification or minor changes in discharge or water quality would likely harm *E. lugoi*.
- **Parasites.** Acanthocephalan cystacanths (*Leptorhynchoides thecatus*) were found in the body cavities of dissected *E. lugoi* specimens.
- **Conservation measures.** Much of the Bolsón de Cuatro Ciénegas was designated a protected area in 1994 by the Mexican government.

## Key Findings

- **Two new species** are described: *Etheostoma lugoi* and *E. segrex*, both assigned to *Oligocephalus* provisionally.
- ***E. lugoi* diagnosis:** maximum observed SL about 37.6 mm (female) and 35.1 mm (male); seven branchiostegal ray pairs; blue nuptial throat; lateral-line pored scales 23–39 (mean 29.6); modal dorsal-fin rays 10; scaleless pectoral-fin base.
- ***E. segrex* diagnosis:** maximum observed SL about 43.8 mm (female) and 41.1 mm (male); lateral-line pored scales 31–42 (mean 37.6); modal dorsal-fin rays 9; scaled pectoral-fin base; unique prootic splint in 4 of 6 skeletal preparations.
- **Relationships:** *E. lugoi*, *E. segrex* and *E. grahami* form an "*E. grahami* group," sharing small body size and absence of dark green flank markings in males.
- **Biogeography:** *E. lugoi* is restricted to the southwestern quarter of the basin (Río Mesquites and tributaries, notably Río Puente Chiquito). *E. segrex* is known only from the uppermost Río Salado de los Nadadores, a Río Grande tributary, with the closest downstream population from Río Sabinas.
- **Conservation status:** both species are considered at high risk. Their narrow distributions and dependence on spring-fed flows make them vulnerable to water development; the authors state that continued water-resource development "will doom" *E. segrex* to extinction if it occurs nowhere else in its drainage.
- **Etymology:** *lugoi* honors José "Pepe" Lugo Guajardo, a long-time Cuatro Ciénegas resident dedicated to its conservation who died in August 1996; *segrex* (Latin, "separate") alludes to the geographic segregation of the species.
- **Key-based identification:** the paper provides a dichotomous key to Mexican *Etheostoma* (*Oligocephalus*), separating *E. australe*, *E. pottsi*, *E. lepidum*, *E. grahami*, *E. lugoi* and *E. segrex* by anal spine number, opercle scaling, branchiostegal ray count and pectoral-fin scaling.
