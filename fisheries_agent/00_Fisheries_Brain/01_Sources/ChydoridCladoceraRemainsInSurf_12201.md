# Chydorid (Cladocera) Remains in Surficial Sediments of Danish Lakes

#paleolimnology #Chydoridae #Cladocera #Danish-lakes

## Executive Summary

This 1969 paleolimnological study examines surficial sediment assemblages of [[Chydoridae]] (a family of benthic microcrustaceans, Cladocera) across 80 Danish lakes to establish ecological relationships between chydorid species diversity and lake typology. Using [[Ekman Dredge]] sampling and standard sediment processing techniques, the author quantified chydorid skeletal remains (head shields, shells, post-abdomens) and applied the [[Shannon-Weaver Diversity Index]] to relate assemblage composition to water transparency, primary production, water color (humic vs. clear), and alkalinity. The study establishes that species diversity of chydorid remains correlates strongly with trophic status, offering a potentially more robust proxy for reconstructing paleo-productivity than raw fossil abundance counts, which are confounded by sedimentation rate and spatial distribution effects. Key indicator taxa identified include [[Chydorus sphaericus]] (dominant in eutrophic, high-production clear-water lakes) and [[Alonella]] (associated with low-alkalinity, low-productivity, and humic-water lakes).

## Background & Description

Note: This study concerns Cladocera (chydorids), not fish; morphological data pertain to identifiable skeletal remains used for taxonomic and quantitative analysis in sediment cores.

- **Remains counted**: Head shields, carapace/shells, and post-abdomens — each fragment counted as one individual animal.
- **Minimum sample size**: 150 identifiable remains per lake to ensure statistical reliability.
- **Identification basis**: Taxonomic keys and morphological criteria established primarily by [[D. G. Frey]] (1958, 1959, 1960), including head pore morphology used for phylogenetic/taxonomic distinctions within Chydoridae.
- **Key taxa referenced**:
  - **[[Chydorus sphaericus]]**: Capable of semi-planktonic existence, using planktonic blue-green algae as substrate — a morphological/behavioral adaptation allowing survival in eutrophic lakes lacking littoral flora.
  - **[[Alonella]]**: Genus lacking this planktonic flexibility; more dependent on littoral substrate and macrophyte-associated habitat.
  - **[[Pleuroxus]]**: Genus tracked in stratigraphic (core) analysis at Grønne Langsø, showing relative percentage shifts from 5% to 40% across sediment depth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat range**: 80 Danish lakes spanning oligotrophic (e.g., Grønne Langsø) to "ultra-eutrophic" (e.g., Frederiksborg Slotssø) conditions, sizes from small bogs/ponds to 4,063 ha, all near sea level (max elevation in Denmark: 147 m), minimizing confounding effects of altitude/climate/latitude present in prior studies (cf. [[DeCosta 1964]]).
- **Water color classification**:
  - **Humic (colored-water) lakes**: 18 lakes; Color values (Ohle units) 9–192; low transparency (0–82%); high species diversity (2.36–3.62 H').
  - **Clear-water (non-humic) lakes**: 59 lakes; further subdivided by productivity.
- **Transparency–Diversity relationship**: Significant positive correlation in clear-water lakes (r = 0.682, p < 0.005) between % transmittance and Shannon-Weaver diversity index; attributed to available habitat heterogeneity.
- **Production–Transparency relationship**: Strong inverse correlation (Spearman r = −0.94, p < 0.001) between phytoplankton primary production (g C/m²/yr) and transparency, excluding three atypical acidic lakes ([[Store Gribsø]], [[Lille Gribsø]], [[Ellesø]]).
- **Production–Diversity relationship**: Negative correlation (r = −0.89, p < 0.001) between species diversity and trophic status — high-production lakes show low chydorid diversity, oligotrophic lakes show high diversity.
- **Alkalinity niche of Alonella**: Restricted to low-alkalinity (0–0.69 meq), acidic, low-productivity lakes (10 lakes with >30% Alonella surveyed in Tab. 2, e.g., Geddesø pH 4.3).
- **Dominance niche of Chydorus sphaericus**: Strongly associated with high phytoplankton production; comprises up to 92.2% of assemblage in the most eutrophic lakes (e.g., [[Sønderso]]), vs. means of 23.0% and 20.5% in low-production clear-water and humic lakes respectively.

## Key Findings

- **Species diversity as trophic proxy**: Chydorid species-diversity indices (Shannon-Weaver H') in surficial sediments are significantly correlated with lake trophic status, transparency, and primary production, suggesting diversity indices may serve as more reliable paleolimnological proxies for past productivity than raw fossil abundance (which is confounded by sedimentation rate and spatial clustering).
- **Compositional stability**: Proportional (percentage) composition of sedimentary chydorid populations is statistically more stable/consistent than absolute abundance (citing [[Mueller 1964]] and [[Goulden 1968]]), supporting use of relative % data in stratigraphic interpretation.
- **Indicator taxa**:
  - High **[[Chydorus sphaericus]]** percentages (>85%) mark highly eutrophic clear-water lakes; difference between high- and low-production lake means is highly significant (p < 0.001).
  - **[[Alonella]]** is significantly reduced in productive clear-water lakes (mean 0.7%) versus less productive clear-water lakes (mean 14.0%) and humic lakes (mean 24.5%), all differences p < 0.001.
- **Limits of diversity indices alone**: A sediment core from Grønne Langsø shows that despite a large shift in relative Pleuroxus abundance (5% → 40%) between two stratigraphic levels, the Shannon-Weaver index remained nearly unchanged (3.06 vs 3.08), indicating that diversity indices alone are insufficient for full paleoecological reconstruction — detailed autecological knowledge of individual taxa is required.
- **Atypical lakes**: Three lakes (Store Gribsø, Lille Gribsø, Ellesø) deviate from the general transparency–production relationship due to unique acidic, bog-influenced, or unexplained limnological conditions, and were excluded from primary statistical correlations.
- **Methodological contribution**: This study extends [[DeCosta 1964]]'s latitudinal approach by controlling for geographic/climatic variables (using a compact, low-relief country), enabling clearer identification of water-color- and trophic-based ecological relationships in chydorid assemblages.
- **Broader significance**: Findings support and refine the paleolimnological framework of [[Frey]] (1960) linking cladoceran remains to lake production, while cautioning that fossil abundance alone (per Frey 1962; Goulden 1964, 1966) is an imperfect production proxy without accounting for sedimentation dynamics.
