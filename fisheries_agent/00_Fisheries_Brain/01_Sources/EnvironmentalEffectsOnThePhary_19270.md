# Environmental Effects on the Pharyngeal Mill of Astatoreochromis alluaudi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 paper by P. H. Greenwood (published in the *Proceedings of the Linnean Society of London*, vol. 176) examines whether diet and calcium supply, rather than only genetics, shape the pharyngeal mill (pharyngeal bones, teeth and apophysis) of the cichlid [[Astatoreochromis Alluaudi]]. The main evidence is a single aquarium-raised specimen, "Specimen N.Y.", reared on an almost snail-free diet. Its pharyngeal mill is the least developed yet recorded for the species. The author concludes that the reduction reflects both mechanical use and dietary calcium, and that the apophysis is a more fundamental, genetically determined feature than [[Wickler Hypothesis|Wickler (1963)]] proposed. The paper also suggests that the two described subspecies may not be genetically distinct and should be treated as phenetic variants of one polytypic species.

## Background & Description

*Astatoreochromis alluaudi* is the only species in its genus. It belongs to the [[Haplochromis Group]] of [[Cichlidae]] and differs from *Haplochromis* mainly by having four or more anal fin spines. Its dorsal and anal fins carry increased spinous rays (dorsal XIX, 7; anal V, 6 in the specimen examined).

The [[Pharyngeal Mill]] comprises the lower pharyngeal bones, their teeth, and the [[Pharyngeal Apophysis]] on the skull base. Earlier work showed that hypertrophy of the mill varies geographically:

- Lake Victoria fish show maximal development. Their main food is the thick-shelled gastropod [[Melanoides Tuberculata]].
- Fish from the Koki lakes and [[Lake Edward]] show a weakened mill, especially in the pharyngeal bones.

The author originally proposed that bone responds adaptively to crushing prey, following [[Wolff's Law]], and that a genetic tendency toward hypertrophy interacts with this response. Later data suggested that a dietary [[Calcium Deficiency]] may also inhibit mill development in fish eating thin-shelled snails or mixed insect and snail diets.

Two subspecies were recognised:

- *A. a. alluaudi* from Lake Victoria, with massive pharyngeal bones and apophysis.
- *A. a. occidentalis* from [[Lake Edward]], [[Lake George]] and [[Lake Nakavali]], with weaker bones and a slightly reduced apophysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Origin and rearing of the aquarium specimen.** The parental stock was taken as very small fry from the mouth of a brooding female near Jinja, Lake Victoria, in 1953. The fry were given to Dr Lester R. Aronson, carried to Ibadan (Nigeria), and then shipped to the American Museum of Natural History. The fish was between the first and tenth filial generation, so its distance from wild stock is unknown. During transport the fish were not fed; later they received powdered dry food. In New York they were fed alternately on dry food and wet mash for about seven months. Attempts to feed them thin-shelled *Planorbis* snails failed. The fish repeatedly mouthed the snails, churned them, and usually let them sink and lose interest. Feeding trials continued for several months without obvious improvement.

**Dietary calcium.** Shell crushing means that snail-eating fish also ingest calcium. Bone calcium in the aquarium fish was about 20% lower than in a wild Lake Victoria specimen. Its values were similar to those of an insectivorous species, [[Haplochromis Empodisma]], from Lake Victoria.

**Comparative populations.** Snails are very uncommon in [[Lake Nabugabo]], a small lake about 4 miles west of Lake Victoria, and are very localised there. A Lake Nabugabo specimen showed a comparable degree of reduction and was raised under comparable low-snail conditions.

**Juvenile feeding.** Lake Victoria juveniles begin eating snails at a standard length of about 20 mm, so mechanical and calcium influences would act early in post-larval development.

## Key Findings

### 1. Comparison of the pharyngeal apophysis

The apophysis is formed from the prootic, basioccipital and parasphenoid. In *A. a. alluaudi* its articular surface covers about two-thirds of the post-orbital skull base in adults. Measurements in millimetres:

| Measurement | *A. a. alluaudi* (Lake Victoria) | Specimen N.Y. |
|---|---|---|
| Neurocranial length | 29.0 | 28.0 |
| Length of basioccipital facet | 6.5 | 2.0 |
| Length of parasphenoid facet | 3.8 | 2.5 (OCR reads "255") |
| Horizontal length of articular surface | 7.0 | 4.0 |
| Breadth of articular surface | 7.0 | 4.0 |
| Width of apophyseal base (prootic breadth) | 8.0 | 8.0 |

The basioccipital facets are the most reduced element. In Specimen N.Y. they are almost circular, whereas in wild *A. a. alluaudi* they are pyriform. Prootic facets are absent in Specimen N.Y. Its articular surface is about 1.5 times smaller than that of [[Haplochromis Ishmaeli]] and [[Haplochromis Pharyngomylus]].

### 2. The aquarium specimen

- **Specimen:** an adult male, 112 mm standard length (SL).
- **Pharyngeal bones:** comparable to those of a 123 mm SL *A. a. occidentalis* from Lake Nakavali. They are much less massive, with fewer and less molariform enlarged teeth. The total tooth count is not appreciably different.
- **Level of reduction:** the mill is still enlarged relative to generalised *Haplochromis*. It is comparable to [[Haplochromis Obtusidens]]. The reduction is therefore relative, not a return to the generalised condition.
- **Bone thickness:** the neurocranial bones are thinner. The prootic was easily damaged during dissection, unlike in wild fish.
- **Oral dentition and general morphology:** identical to wild *A. a. alluaudi*, apart from the area influenced by the apophysis.

### 3. Main effects of a virtually mollusc-free diet

The author lists five results:

1. Pharyngeal bones are much less massive.
2. Fewer enlarged pharyngeal teeth, and those present are less flattened and molariform.
3. Overall dimensions of the apophysis are reduced, especially the articular surface.
4. The reduction arises from smaller basioccipital facets, which have lost their pyriform outline and resemble those of unrelated *Haplochromis*.
5. Despite this hypotrophy, the structures remain larger than in generalised *Haplochromis*, so the reduction has not reached the limit possible within the genus complex.

### 4. Calcium content of bone

| *A. alluaudi* (wild, Lake Victoria) | 22.6 | 20.5 | 10.6 | 59.8 |
| *A. alluaudi* (Specimen N.Y.) | 17.4 | 16.6 | 8.6 | 48.6 |
| *H. empodisma* (wild, Lake Victoria) | 17.2 | 16.6 | 8.6 | 48.6 |

The analysis was carried out on the supraoccipital bone. The author suggests the aquarium fish was not exercised against hard objects, and that its diet was adequate, since it was in good condition.

### 5. Proposed mechanisms

The author concludes that full mill development depends on two factors:

- **Physical factors:** strong musculature and the stress of crushing prey.
- **Physiological factors:** an adequate calcium supply for bone to respond to physical stimuli.

Moderately enlarged pharyngeal bones and a basically enlarged apophysis are considered genetically determined. Further hypertrophy would depend on hard-shelled prey and the calcium it supplies. Whether pressure or calcium deficiency causes the dentition differences is still unknown.

### 6. Critique of Wickler (1963)

Wickler proposed that the [[Tilapia Type Apophysis]] (formed from the parasphenoid alone) is polyphyletic. He based this on breeding behaviour, especially that of [[Tropheus]] and [[Petrochromis]], which have a Haplochromis reproductive pattern but a Tilapia-type apophysis. He attributed the apophyseal convergence to feeding on aufwuchs (algal films). Greenwood rejects this, citing several counterexamples:

- [[Tylochromis]] has a Tilapia-type apophysis but massive pharyngeal bones.
- [[Geophagus Braziliensis]] has massive pharyngeals with a Tilapia-type apophysis.
- Several algal-grazing *Haplochromis* from Lake Victoria (*H. obliquidens*, *H. lividus*, *H. nigricans*, *H. nuchisquamulatus*) have apophyses no smaller than those of comparable insectivores. The basioccipital facets of *H. obliquidens* are as large as those of the generalised *H. brownae*.

Greenwood therefore concludes that apophyseal structure reflects a fundamental dichotomy in cichlid phylogeny. He considers the Haplochromis-type reproductive behaviour of *Tropheus* and *Petrochromis* to be convergent, which is the reverse of Wickler's conclusion.

### 7. Status of the subspecies

The subspecies differ mainly in pharyngeal bones and dentition. Their genetic basis is now doubtful. The author recommends dropping the subspecific divisions unless marked genotypic differences can be shown, and treating the species as phenetically polytypic. The Lake Nabugabo specimen and the aquarium fish both show marked reduction, which weakens the link between the subspecies and their diets.

### 8. Limitations and further work

The author states that conclusions from a single specimen need checking, and that only experimental work can separate environmental from genetic effects. The paper is intended as a preliminary report to encourage further study.
