# Whiteriver Water Treatment Plant Operating Cost Analysis

#water-treatment #utility-infrastructure #operating-cost-analysis

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1991 operating cost analysis for a proposed surface water treatment plant serving [[Whiteriver]] and North Whiteriver on the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]]. It was prepared by the [[Indian Health Service]] (IHS) Eastern Arizona District Office for the [[White Mountain Apache Tribal Utility Authority]]. Currently 5,820 people and 34 businesses are served by nine wells and one spring. The wells are adequate in winter but unreliable in summer. The proposed plant would draw surface water from the [[North Fork of the White River]] and deliver up to 480 million gallons per year (MG/yr) by the design year 2022. The authors recommend approval for construction and operation.

The document does not address fish, other aquatic organisms, plankton, or aquatic habitat. Its subject is the engineering, chemical use, staffing, and cost of a municipal water supply.

## Background & Description

- **Existing system:** Submersible pumps in nine drilled wells tap the alluvium beside the North Fork, supplemented by a spring. The spring line is an 8-mile concrete/steel pipeline delivering 350 gpm (183 MG/yr). It is described as a reliable source of clean, clear, contaminant-free water for several decades.
- **Proposed facility:** The plant would be sited near Rainbow City, north of Whiteriver. It would include:
  - An intake structure along the North Fork stream bed with a screened pump intake.
  - Three 60-horsepower source pumps, each rated at 700 gpm, lifting raw water 262 feet.
  - Three filter units, with two proposed for Phase I. Each is rated at 700 gpm, for 2,100 gpm total at design capacity.
  - A mixed-media filter (gravel, sand, anthracite) preceded by an adsorption clarifier.
  - Chlorination, fluoridation, and storage through the 1-million-gallon Rainbow City tank and related tanks totaling about 4.2 MG.
- **Service area:** About 94% of existing homes and all businesses would be served by the plant. The remaining northern homes would continue to be served by two wells.
- **Treatment chemicals:** Alum, synthetic polymer, or both are added for coagulation. Chlorine is applied both pre- and post-filtration. Fluoride is added post-filtration.
- **Waste handling:** Backwash water flows to abandoned on-site sewage lagoons (dry since 1984) and is held for 37.4 days before discharge to the regional sewer system. Settled sludge may be removed and used as landscape soil. The document states that no toxic contaminants will be present in the sediments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document's environmental content is limited to the plant's operational footprint:

- **Water source:** All existing wells tap alluvium beside the North Fork. The proposed intake would withdraw surface water from the North Fork, and the document does not evaluate effects on instream flow or downstream biota.
- **Raw water quality:** The analysis treats turbidity and coagulation demand as design variables. Coagulation aids are intended to remove "harmful viruses, cysts, and other living organisms." Monthly turbidity levels are tabulated in Table C-1, which is not reproduced here.
- **Discharge:** Backwash discharge is managed through settling and sewer connection, with a stated discharge rate of 31,086 gpd initially and 106,686 gpd at design.
- **Spring protection:** The spring area is described as a protected, fenced compound. The Tribe is encouraged to continue chlorinating and fluoridating spring flow.

## Key Findings

### Water Demand and Capacity

| Parameter | Initial (~1992) | Design (2022) |
|---|---|---|
| Water required | 319 MG/yr | 480 MG/yr |
| Spring output | 183 MG/yr | 183 MG/yr |
| Filter capacity | 1,400 gpm | 2,100 gpm |
| Average daily plant run | 5 hours | 10 hours |
| Average daily production | 408,000 gal | 1,260,000 gal |
| Average yearly production | 149 MG/yr | 460 MG/yr |

- Current demand is 319 MG/yr. Existing production totals 332 MG/yr, of which 13 MG is used by northern homes.
- The plant would initially treat 135.2 MG for domestic use and 13.8 MG for backwash. If the spring fails or filtration is required by law, it would treat the full 319 MG without modification.
- The design allows 50% growth over a 30-year period.

### Chemical Use and Cost

| Chemical | Initial use/yr | Initial cost/yr | Design use/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine (pre + post) | 4,970 lb | $3,479 | 15,345 lb |
| Fluoride (hydrofluosilicic acid) | 5,475 lb | $1,370 | 16,863 lb |
| Alum | 25,192 lb | $6,046 | 77,774 lb |
| Polymer | 252 lb | $1,008 | 778 lb |

- Appendix C gives total initial chemical cost as $11,921 per year. Table 5-1 lists chlorine at $3,497, which differs from the $3,479 in Appendix C. Appendix C is used here.
- Polymer is priced at $4.00/lb, about 16 times the alum price of $0.24/lb. Polymer use is about 1/100th of alum by mass.

### Electrical Costs

- Plant electricity: **$23,179/yr** initially, rising to **$64,538/yr** at design capacity.
- Replaced well electricity: **$27,406/yr**, from the Diamond Creek, Orchard, and East Whiteriver wells, plus the Post Office Canyon wells in the conclusions.
- **Net saving: $4,227/yr** initially.
- Appendix D gives a range of $23,179 to $35,419 per year for the initial operating scenario, depending on whether one or two pumps run. The electricity rate used is $0.039071/kWh.

### Personnel

- The Utility Authority has 18 staff. None hold Level II certification.
- The plant requires a Level II state-certified operator, with a second staff member also recommended for certification. The labor budget would increase by about $5,000 to support higher salaries.
- Table B-2 projects 15 people needed for O&M after plant construction and concludes the requirement rises by one person. The Conclusions section states manpower remains at the same level. The two statements are inconsistent in the source.

### Capital and Replacement Costs

| Component | Estimated cost |
|---|---|
| Intake structure, 12-inch line, source pump | $225,063 |
| Water treatment units | $270,000 |
| Treatment plant building, piping, foundation, pumps | $187,700 |
| **Total construction** | **$682,763** |

- Short-term replaceable/repairable items total **$56,000**, depreciated over 7 years at **$8,000/yr** ($667/month).
- Full replacement after 50 years is estimated at $682,763 in 1992 dollars. The authors judge this unlikely, because ongoing maintenance is expected.

### Alternative: Additional Wells

- The alternative would require up to 16 additional wells with combined capacity of 800 gpm, spread over a 10-mile reach of the North Fork, at $20,000 to $30,000 per well.
- Each well would need individual visits, maintenance, and repair.

### Conclusions and Recommendations

The authors recommend that the plant be approved for construction and accepted for operation by the [[White Mountain Apache Tribe]]. Their reasons are:

1. Higher production capacity and a central location.
2. Lower electricity costs ($4,227/yr less).
3. Consolidated operations and improved reliability.
4. No net increase in staff, according to the Conclusions section.
5. Close proximity to pressure reducing valves and other North Fork components for faster servicing.
