## Executive Summary

This 1993 preliminary project report from the U.S. EPA Environmental Research Laboratory (Gulf Breeze, FL), in cooperation with the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service National Fisheries Contaminant Research Center, describes an ongoing study evaluating whether common, non-listed surrogate fish species — the [[Sheepshead Minnow]] (*Cyprinodon variegatus*) and [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) — can reliably predict contaminant sensitivity in endangered pupfish species, specifically the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon bovinus*). The research was conducted under a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Endangered Species Permit. Due to limited availability of desert pupfish, only a single chemical (carbaryl) could be tested comparatively before the study pivoted to using Leon Springs pupfish as a substitute test organism for a broader suite of contaminants (carbaryl, copper sulfate, nonylphenol, pentachlorophenol, and permethrin).

## Background & Description

The source text is a toxicology/risk-assessment report and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of the species involved. Test organisms were juvenile/adult fish sourced from hatchery or wild-collected stocks:
- **Desert Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — federally listed endangered species; limited numbers available for testing.
- **Leon Springs Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon bovinus*) — federally listed endangered species; ~800 individuals received Nov 12, 1993, from a captive/refuge population.
- **Sheepshead Minnow** (*Cyprinodon variegatus*) — congeneric, non-listed surrogate species used as a standard EPA/ASTM test organism.
- **Fathead Minnow** (*Pimephales promelas*) — standard freshwater surrogate test species with documented "similar sensitivity" to sheepshead minnows.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- [[Desert Pupfish]]: inhabits isolated desert springs, marshes, and small streams in the southwestern U.S. and Mexico (Sonoran Desert region); tolerant of extreme temperature, salinity, and oxygen fluctuations typical of desert spring habitats.
- [[Leon Springs Pupfish]]: endemic to a highly restricted spring system in Texas (Leon Springs/Diamond Y Spring area), representing a critically endangered, range-limited population.
- [[Sheepshead Minnow]]: euryhaline species common in estuarine and coastal marsh habitats along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts of North America; widely used as a standard toxicity-testing surrogate due to ease of culture and broad environmental tolerance.
- [[Fathead Minnow]]: freshwater species widespread across North America; standard USEPA/ASTM bioassay organism for freshwater toxicity testing.

The study's ecological rationale centers on using widely available, ecologically similar congeners or functionally analogous species as **surrogates** for contaminant risk assessment when testing endangered species directly is constrained by permitting, availability, or conservation concerns.

## Key Findings

- **Test design**: Acute static toxicity tests followed ASTM protocols; 1-gallon jars with 3 L test solution, 4 replicates per treatment, 5 fish/jar, mortality recorded over 96 hours. LC50 values estimated via the [[Trimmed Spearman-Karber Method]] (Hamilton, Russo & Thurston 1977).
- **Carbaryl exposure concentrations**: control, solvent control, 1.0, 1.7, 2.9, 4.8, and 8.0 mg/L.

### Carbaryl 96-h LC50 Comparison
| Species | 96-h LC50 (mg/L) | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| [[Desert Pupfish]] (*C. macularius*) | 7.38 (reported as 7.4) | 6.04–9.01 |
| [[Sheepshead Minnow]] (*C. variegatus*) | 5.17 (reported as 5.2) | 4.61–5.80 |
| [[Fathead Minnow]] (*P. promelas*, historical data) | 4.2–7.5 (range) | N/A |

- **Interpretation**: The desert pupfish was **slightly more resistant** to carbaryl than the sheepshead minnow, with non-overlapping 95% confidence intervals suggesting a statistically meaningful difference in sensitivity. However, the desert pupfish LC50 fell within the range previously reported for fathead minnows, indicating that surrogate species may still provide a reasonably conservative/comparable estimate of sensitivity for regulatory risk assessment purposes.
- Mortality proportions in both datasets were **not monotonically increasing** with concentration, requiring adjustment prior to Spearman-Karber estimation — a data-quality caveat noted in the raw bioassay results.
- Due to limited desert pupfish availability, comparative testing with the remaining four chemicals (copper sulfate, nonylphenol, pentachlorophenol, permethrin) could not be completed with that species.
- The study pivoted to the **Leon Springs Pupfish** as an alternative endangered test species; preliminary (non-definitive) exposures to carbaryl, copper sulfate, nonylphenol, and pentachlorophenol were completed November 15–26, 1993, with permethrin testing scheduled for the following week.
- **Definitive toxicity testing** with Leon Springs pupfish was planned for Spring 1994, contingent on fish availability, to establish more statistically robust LC50 estimates.
- This work supports the broader regulatory goal of validating **surrogate species testing frameworks** to estimate contaminant risk for [[Endangered Species Act]]-listed fish without requiring extensive direct testing on rare or protected populations.
