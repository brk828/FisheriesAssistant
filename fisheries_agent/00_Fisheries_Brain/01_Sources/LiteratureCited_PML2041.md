# Lower Colorado River Riparian Revegetation and Aquatic Fauna: Annotated Literature Cited

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is the **Literature Cited** section of a 1986 final report on revegetation work in riparian habitat, prepared for the Department of Fish and Game and associated with a 25-acre plot project. The OCR text is badly degraded, so the title and author block are only partly legible. The reference list runs about 20 pages and contains several hundred entries, judged by page count rather than an exact tally. It is not a research study, and it reports no original sample sizes or statistics.

The bibliography is dominated by work on the [[Lower Colorado River]]. Its main themes are riparian vegetation and [[Revegetation]], avian and mammalian wildlife use of floodplain habitat, and the ecology of native and introduced fishes. Fish-related citations focus on endangered and threatened species, especially the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]].

## Background & Description

The reference list is organized alphabetically by first author, spanning roughly 1850 to 1988. It includes:

- **Historical and exploratory accounts**, such as Emory (1848), Ives (1861), Whipple (1856) and Cooper (1869), which describe the Colorado River region before large-scale water development.
- **Agency reports** from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the California Department of Fish and Game, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Peer-reviewed journal papers** in *Condor*, *Southwestern Naturalist*, *Journal of Wildlife Management*, *Copeia* and *Desert Fishes Council* proceedings.
- **Unpublished theses** from Arizona State University, Utah State University and the University of California.

Most citations are for work by B. W. Anderson and R. D. Ohmart, who appear as authors or co-authors on a large share of the entries. Their studies cover revegetation, bird community structure and habitat use.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The cited literature describes a river system heavily modified by agriculture, dams and invasive plants. Several entries address:

- **Riparian vegetation change**, including invasion by [[Salt Cedar]] (*Tamarix*) and [[Honey Mesquite]], and the dynamics of marshland formation along the river.
- **Wildlife use of revegetated and invaded habitat**, with a focus on birds such as the [[Yuma Clapper Rail]], riparian breeding birds, and winter ducks.
- **Mammals**, including [[Coyote]] ecology and food habits, [[Mule Deer]] habitat use, pocket gophers, and feral burros.
- **Water quality and contaminants**, such as [[Selenium]] in fish and avian reproduction, and reconnaissance of irrigation drainage.
- **Invasive aquatic species**, including the [[Asiatic Clam]] and [[Bullfrog]], and the predation effects of introduced fishes.

The fish-related entries show that the river's native fauna is in decline. Recovery programs for endangered species, including reintroduction and larval rearing, are a recurring theme, often tied to the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Key Findings

The document contains no original data, but its reference list identifies the following main research and management themes:

1. **Native fish decline and conservation.** The most important fish citations concern:
   - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): status reviews in the lower and upper basins, early development, larval growth and survival in ponds, and management plans for California and Arizona.
   - [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): life history, instream flow needs and spawning migration.
   - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*): status reports and endemic subspecies, including *C. m. eremus*.
   - Woundfin and other endangered species: reintroduction efforts described at the [[Desert Fishes Council]].

2. **Introduced fishes.** Entries on [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*) in the lower river, mosquitofish and bait fishes, and trophic relationships among introduced species show that non-native fish are a major factor in native species decline.

3. **Riparian vegetation and revegetation.** Multiple citations address planting for wildlife, vegetation mapping, and the spread of salt cedar and honey mesquite. These form the core context for the 1986 revegetation work.

4. **Avian and mammalian habitat relationships.** Bird community studies use discriminant analysis, species diversity indices and habitat-attribute comparisons. Mammal studies cover food habits and population dynamics.

5. **Water quality.** Reconnaissance of irrigation drainage and selenium levels in fish fauna highlight contaminant risks to aquatic and avian wildlife.

6. **Historical background.** Early exploration, Spanish and Mexican settlement, and mining histories provide context on the river's pre-dam condition.
