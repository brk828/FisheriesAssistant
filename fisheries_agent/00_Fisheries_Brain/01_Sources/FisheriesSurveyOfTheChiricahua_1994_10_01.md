# Fisheries Survey of the Chiricahua Mountains: Turkey Creek and Coronado Ranch

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #Salmonidae #Poeciliidae #FreshwaterFisheries #RioYaquiBasin

## Executive Summary
This October 1994 survey, conducted by Arizona State University researchers, assessed fish assemblages in [[Turkey Creek]] (Chiricahua Mountains, Arizona) and a series of artificial ponds on the adjacent [[Coronado Ranch]]. Electrofishing of Turkey Creek revealed an overwhelmingly depauperate stream fish community, with fish detected at only one of seven sampled reaches. In contrast, gill-net and trammel-net surveys of six ranch ponds documented a mixed native-introduced assemblage dominated by the native [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*) alongside stocked/introduced sportfish and forage species. The findings highlight the precarious status of native cyprinids in a landscape increasingly dominated by non-native centrarchids, salmonids, and poeciliids, and informed subsequent pond renovation planning for native species conservation.

## Background & Description
The source is a field survey report and does not provide detailed morphometric or anatomical descriptions of individual specimens. Species were identified taxonomically at the point of capture:

- **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*): Native cyprinid, small-bodied; small individuals reportedly capable of passing through 1/2-inch gill net mesh, suggesting a slender body form conducive to escapement.
- **[[Bluegill]]** (*Lepomis macrochirus*): Introduced centrarchid, laterally compressed sunfish body plan.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*): Introduced salmonid, stocked for recreational purposes.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*): Introduced piscivorous centrarchid.
- **[[Mosquitofish]]** (*Gambusia affinis*): Small-bodied introduced poeciliid, live-bearer.
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): Native cyprinid, suspected but not confirmed present (no specimens captured).
- **[[Grass Carp]]** (*Ctenopharyngodon idellus*): Large-bodied introduced herbivorous cyprinid, suspected but not confirmed present in multiple ponds.

No length, weight, or meristic data were reported for any captured individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Turkey Creek]]**: A montane desert stream within the Chiricahua Mountains, part of the greater [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] drainage. The stream exhibited extremely low fish biomass/diversity across ~2.5+ km of surveyed habitat and 1,700+ cumulative shocking seconds, with only a single productive site (near a road crossing in T18S R29E Sec.16) yielding fish (3 bluegill, 1 Yaqui chub). This scarcity may reflect naturally low carrying capacity, intermittent flow, habitat degradation, or predation/competition pressure from downstream-invading non-natives.
- **[[Coronado Ranch]] ponds**: A series of artificial impoundments (ranging from small guest ponds to larger tanks) functioning as isolated lentic habitats. These ponds supported the bulk of documented fish abundance, particularly the native Yaqui chub, which numerically dominated two ponds (Lodge Pond: 33 individuals; Barn Pond: 69 individuals).
- **Species interactions**: Ponds host a layered assemblage of native cyprinids (Yaqui chub, longfin dace) co-occurring or overlapping with introduced piscivores (largemouth bass), competitors/omnivores (bluegill, grass carp), and small forage fish (mosquitofish) — creating a conservation conflict between native fish refugia and stocked recreational/production species.
- **Yaqui Chub status**: As a species of conservation concern within the Rio Yaqui basin, its persistence in isolated stock ponds (absent confirmed presence in the flowing creek except for one individual) suggests these artificial waters may serve as important, if precarious, refugia.

## Key Findings
- **Turkey Creek electrofishing effort**: 7 sites sampled across ~2.5 km stream length and >1,700 shocking seconds; only 1 site yielded fish (3 [[Bluegill]], 1 [[Yaqui Chub]]).
- **Lodge Pond** (50m x 40m, 25.5 net-hours): 33 Yaqui chub; longfin dace and grass carp suspected but unconfirmed.
- **Barn Pond** (20m x 20m, 8 net-hours): 69 Yaqui chub, 1 rainbow trout; longfin dace suspected.
- **House Pond 1** (60m x 30m, 24 net-hours): 20 bluegill, 2 largemouth bass; grass carp, channel catfish, and rainbow trout suspected.
- **House Pond 2** (30m x 40m, 24 net-hours): 2 bluegill captured, with many additional undersized bluegill observed but not netted; grass carp, channel catfish, rainbow trout, and largemouth bass suspected.
- **Guest House Pond** (20m x 30m, 3 net-hours): No fish captured; small Yaqui chub suspected to have evaded 1/2-inch mesh gill net.
- **Lisa Tank** (50m x 70m, 3 net-hours): 2 largemouth bass, numerous mosquitofish; grass carp, channel catfish, rainbow trout, and bluegill suspected.
- **Big Tank** (100m x 70m, 22 net-hours, trammel net): Mosquitofish only confirmed; largemouth bass, grass carp, channel catfish, bluegill, and rainbow trout suspected.
- **Management note**: At the time of survey, all ranch ponds except Lisa Tank and Big Tank were under consideration for renovation, likely reflecting conservation prioritization for native [[Yaqui Chub]] populations over ponds dominated by non-native sportfish.
- **Overall pattern**: Native fish diversity in the natural stream channel was nearly absent, while artificial impoundments — despite being non-native habitat — currently harbor the largest known populations of the native Yaqui chub in this survey area.
