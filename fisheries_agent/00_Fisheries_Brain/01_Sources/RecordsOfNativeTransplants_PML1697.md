# Pertinent Records of California Fishes, 1939–1955

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

The source is a scanned, partly legible set of pages from a historical fish-survey publication. The only readable substance is a continuation table, "Table 1 (Continued): Pertinent Records of Some [[California Fishes]], 1939–1955." It lists field records of [[Cyprinodon]] (pupfish) populations, with localities, dates, survey results and remarks. Most other pages are scan debris and cannot be reliably transcribed. The note therefore records only what can be read with reasonable confidence, and marks uncertain items as such.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Historical faunal survey table (continuation page), covering records from 1939 to 1955.
- **Taxon:** Multiple *Cyprinodon* entries, including forms labelled with the epithet abbreviations *C. n.* and *C. n. amargosae*-like names. Several OCR-corrupted names could not be confirmed, so exact subspecies identities are uncertain.
- **Table structure:** Each row gives a species or population, a locality, a collection date (roman-numeral volume and year notation), a result (Positive or Negative, or a survey note), and remarks.
- **Other pages:** Pages 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 contain only fragmentary text or noise. An earlier table fragment (apparently the start of the same record series, with references to the "Museum" or "Records of Some California Fishes, 1939–1955") is also partly visible. These pages do not yield reliable data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The localities visible in the table are inland desert waters, consistent with the genus's known habitat in isolated springs and saline or alkaline streams. Legible place names include:

- **[[Death Valley]]** region, including a "Salt Creek" or "Salt-Cr." locality
- **[[Shoshone Spring]]**, likely in [[Inyo County]]
- Other spring or valley localities, partly illegible

The remarks column suggests population-status assessments rather than habitat measurements. Entries such as "abundant by 1939" and "No fish seen" indicate that fish presence was checked at a given site and time. No water chemistry, temperature or habitat-measurement data are legible.

## Key Findings

- **Record count:** The continued table contains roughly ten *Cyprinodon* entries. Exact row numbers cannot be confirmed because of scan quality.
- **Survey results:** Entries are coded as Positive (fish present), Negative (fish absent), or a qualitative note. A legible example is "abundant by 1939."
- **Temporal span:** Records run from the late 1930s to 1955, with dates in the 1939–1941 range most legible.
- **Taxonomic signal:** Several populations are grouped under *Cyprinodon* with subspecies or variant epithets. This implies a taxonomic review of desert pupfishes in the region during this period.
- **Data quality:** Scan corruption prevents reliable transcription of most species names, localities and dates. Any citation or quantitative use should be checked against the original publication.
