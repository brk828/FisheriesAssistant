# The Freshwater Fishes of China in Coloured Illustrations (Volume 3)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This third volume of *The Freshwater Fishes of China in Coloured Illustration* is a photographic and descriptive field guide to 142 freshwater fish species in 17 families, focused mainly on northern China. Coverage includes the cold-water fauna of the [[Northeast China]] region, the arid northwest (including the [[Xinjiang Region]] and [[E-erqisi River]] drainage), and selected species from southwest [[Yunnan Province]]. Each species has a colour photograph of a living specimen in a simulated natural setting, plus a short bilingual (Chinese and English) account of its habitat, behaviour, distribution and use. The editors state that the book is intended to support rational use and protection of fish resources, as well as research, education and ornamental interest. The document is the front matter, plate descriptions and index of the book, dated 1992 in the preface and 1993 in the publication details.

## Background & Description

**Publication details**
- Volume 3 of a multi-volume series; the editors' preface is dated August 1992.
- Publication year 1993; ISBN 7-5323-2960-7/Q.44.
- Describes fishes "mainly from the northern part of China," reflecting the status of freshwater fish fauna and fish resources in the northeast and northwest.
- Yunnan's fauna is described in more depth in [[Volume 2]]; this volume adds further Yunnan species.

**Format of species accounts**
- Photographs taken from living specimens in simulated natural backgrounds.
- Entries combine scientific and artistic description of "natural appearance and behaviour."
- Each entry gives habitat, distribution, spawning season, size and economic use where known.

**Regional context in the preface**
- The **northeast** is high-latitude, frigid-temperate to temperate, with many cold-water groups: Petromyzonidae, Acipenseridae, Salmonidae, Thymallidae, Osmeridae, Esocidae, Gasterosteidae, Cottidae, and the Cyprinidae genera *Phoxinus* and *Leuciscus*.
- The **northwest** is inland Asian, with arid, continental climate, high sunshine, large day-night temperature swings, mountains, meadows, deserts and Gobi. Major waters include the Huanghe River, E-erqisi River, Tarim River, Yili River, Qinghaihu Lake, Bosten Lake and Wulungu Lake.
- The fauna near the [[Qinghai-Xizang Plateau]] is dominated by Schizothoracinae and *Triplophysa* (Cobitidae). The Gansu Corridor and areas west of it have a relatively poor fauna of saline-alkali- and cold-tolerant Cobitidae and Cyprinidae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Biogeographic highlights**
- The E-erqisi River is described as the only drainage in China flowing into the Arctic Ocean. Its fauna includes *Thymallus arcticus arcticus*, *Esox lucius*, *Tinca tinca*, *Rutilus rutilus lacustris*, *Leuciscus idus*, *Leuciscus baicalensis*, *Aspius aspius*, *Perca fluviatilis*, *Acerina cernua* and *Cottus sibiricus altaicus*.
- The Tumen River is the site of the first Chinese record of *Acipenser medirostris*, which is otherwise endemic to the Sea of Japan area.
- Anadromous migration is a recurring theme: the lamprey, several sturgeons, salmonids and two cyprinids (*Leuciscus brandti* and *L. hakonensis*) move between fresh water and the sea. The preface notes the two cyprinids are "very rare in the world."

**Habitat associations (from species accounts)**
- Cold, fast, gravel- or stone-bottomed streams: many *Schizothorax*, *Garra*, *Glyptothorax*, *Nemacheilus* and *Triplophysa* species.
- Slow currents, backwaters and vegetated margins: *Carassius*, *Esox*, *Misgurnus*, *Pungitius* and many bitterlings.
- Large rivers and deep pools: *Acipenser schrenckii* (lives its whole life in freshwater), *Lota lota* and *Diptychus maculatus*.
- Lakes and saline or mineralised waters: Qinghaihu Lake fishes such as *Gymnocypris przewalskii* and *Triplophysa alticeps*.
- Subterranean habitat: *Sinocyclocheilus anophthalmus* lives in caves, with degenerate eyes.

**Conservation and resource notes**
- Several species are called rare or endangered in the text, e.g., *Oncorhynchus gorbuscha* ("one of the most precious and rare salmons in China"), *Mesogobio tumenensis* and *Gobocypris rarus*.
- Many species are important commercial or food fishes. Examples are *Oncorhynchus keta* (a major commercial salmon in Heilongjiang and Jilin), *Gymnocypris przewalskii* (main commercial fish of Qinghai) and *Gymnodiptychus dybowskii*.
- The book explicitly links its content to the rational utilisation and protection of fish resources.

## Key Findings

**Scope and structure**
- 142 species, 17 families, each with a colour photograph and bilingual text.
- Entries are numbered sequentially (1 to 142). Plate-based cross-reference index lists species by name with page numbers.
- Numerous Yunnan and Southwest China species are included, supplementing earlier volumes.

**Notable species and records**
- ***Lampetra japonica*** (arctic lamprey): anadromous as adult; larvae spend at least three to four years as sedentary filter feeders, and adults die after spawning.
- ***Acipenser schrenckii*** (amur sturgeon): restricted to the [[Heilong River]] system; spawns over gravel in deep water, May to September.
- ***Acipenser medirostris***: lateral scutes 22 to 36, gill rakers fewer than 30; first recorded in China in the Tumen River.
- ***Acipenser nudiventris***: native to the Black, Caspian and Aral seas; introduced to Lake Balkhash in 1933 to 1934, and reached the Ili River system in China.
- ***Oncorhynchus keta*** (chum salmon): migrates upstream 30 to 50 km per day from September to October without feeding.
- ***Oncorhynchus masou*** (masu salmon): spawns in the Tumen River in August; the female may spawn with several males; adults die within 2 to 3 days of spawning.
- ***Triplophysa siluroides***: described as the largest loach in the world, weight about 500 to 1200 g.
- ***Triplophysa stoliczkae***: one of the highest-living fishes, recorded at an elevation of 5,200 m on the [[Qinghai-Xizang Plateau]].
- ***Triplophysa scleroptera***: maximum weight about 160 g and total length 223 mm; migrates and spawns over sand beds after ice-breakup in March.
- ***Tinca tinca***: grows up to 700 mm; in China restricted to the E-erqisi River system.
- ***Aspius aspius***: weight over 8000 g; predatory once above 200 to 300 mm.
- ***Carassius carassius***: described as the most tolerant of extreme limnological conditions; endemic to Xinjiang in the book's account.
- ***Gymnodiptychus dybowskii***: weight up to 3000 g; spawns February to April.
- ***Diptychus maculatus***: widespread in the Ili and Tarim basins; length up to 700 mm.
- ***Sinocyclocheilus anophthalmus***: cave-dwelling, eyeless, transparent body; known only from Yiliang County, Yunnan.
- ***Sinocyclocheilus angustiporus***: found in the Lanpanjiang River system and its branches in Yunnan.

**Species-group summary (142 species)**
- **Cyprinidae** (carps, minnows, barbs, gudgeons, bitterlings) make up the largest share, including *Danio*, *Barilius*, *Rasbora*, *Barbodes*, *Sinilabeo*, *Gobio*, *Schizothorax* and *Acheilognathus*.
- **Cobitidae** (loaches) include *Triplophysa*, *Nemacheilus*, *Barbatula*, *Misgurnus* and *Botia*.
- **Siluriformes** (catfishes) include *Glyptothorax*, *Exostoma*, *Pareuchiloglanis* and *Pseudobagrus*, several of which have adhesive thoracic discs.
- **Salmonids and other coldwater groups** include *Oncorhynchus*, *Salvelinus*, *Brachymystax*, *Thymallus*, *Esox*, *Lota*, *Perca*, *Cottus* and *Pungitius*.

**Key regional statements**
- The E-erqisi River is the only Chinese drainage to empty into the Arctic Ocean.
- The Tumen River hosts several anadromous and rare species.
- Yunnan's freshwater fauna is among the richest in China, and this volume adds to earlier coverage there.

**Purpose and use**
- Intended as a reference for fish resource management, conservation, scientific research and education, and for aquarium or ornamental interest.
