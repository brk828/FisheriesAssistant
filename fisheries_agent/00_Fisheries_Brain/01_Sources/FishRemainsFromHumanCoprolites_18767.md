# Fish Remains from Human Coprolites and Midden Deposits, Lovelock Cave, Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report by W. I. Follett identifies fish remains recovered in 1968 and 1969 from human coprolites (fossilized feces) and midden deposits at [[Lovelock Cave]], Churchill County, Nevada. Remains come from two coprolite series, "AN" and "LX," plus midden refuse. Three species are identified: the [[Tui Chub]] (the dominant taxon), the [[Tahoe Sucker]], and the [[Cui-ui]]. The fish were most likely taken from [[Humboldt Lake]] and the lower [[Humboldt River]], and from [[Pyramid Lake]], [[Winnemucca Lake]], and the lower [[Truckee River]]. Radiocarbon dates span roughly 2740 B.C. to A.D. 1805. The paper supplements an earlier 1965 analysis by Follett and discusses seasonal exploitation of fish and the implications of the species' ranges.

## Background & Description

**Site.** Lovelock Cave (site NV-Ch-18) lies in a limestone outcrop of the West Humboldt Range at about 4,240 feet (1,292 m) above sea level. It is about two miles east of the bed of Humboldt Lake, the terminus of the Humboldt River, and 15 miles south-southwest of Lovelock.

**Sampling method.** Each coprolite was split roughly in half. One half was kept intact for future [[Pollen Analysis]] and [[Radiocarbon Dating]]. The other half was rehydrated, and its fish remains and other constituents were separated for study. Identification relied mainly on lower [[Pharyngeal Teeth]], which are the most distinctive elements. For the Tahoe Sucker, a quadrate was used because no pharyngeal was found. Length is total length, and weight is an estimate derived from pharyngeal or quadrate length.

**Collections.** The two coprolite series each contain 15 specimens. The AN series was collected in September 1968 and May 1969 at grid locations S10/W50 and S10/W55. The LX series was collected in 1968–1969 at grid location N50/W30. Collections are deposited in the [[Lowie Museum of Anthropology]], Berkeley. Midden samples came from the West Alcove (WA), including WA Sample 46:1029 and 46:1044.

**Context.** The study is part of the [[Lovelock Cave Coprolite Analysis Project]] and draws on work by [[Heizer and Napton]], [[Cowan 1967]], and [[Ambro 1967]]. The [[Northern Paiute]] used local names for these fish (for example, "tui-pagwl" for the tui chub and "kuyui" for the cui-ui).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Chronology.** Radiocarbon dates place the AN series at about A.D. 1800 to 2740 B.C. and the LX series at about A.D. 700 to A.D. 50. The oldest date from probable cultural material at the site is 2740 B.C. ± 110 (I-3962), from vegetal material in the AN unit. Dated human remains are 1420 B.C. ± 100 and 1450 B.C. The LX material includes a probable mixing event, marked by an empty cache pit at 56 inches depth.

**Habitat and range.** Tui chubs, especially smaller individuals, and Tahoe suckers were probably caught in Humboldt Lake or the lower Humboldt River. Larger tui chubs and cui-ui were probably taken from Pyramid Lake, Winnemucca Lake, or the lower Truckee River. The cui-ui's presence cannot be explained by a high stage of [[Lake Lahontan]]. Morrison (1965) placed the last Lake Lahontan stage more than 10,000 years before the present, well before the earliest known human occupation.

**Seasonality.** Follett suggests that differences in pharyngeal counts between the AN and LX locations may reflect seasonal occupation. The AN location may have been occupied in late summer or early fall, when small tui chubs were abundant in receding pools of the Humboldt Sink. The LX location may have been occupied in winter, when fish were scarce or when stored dried fish were eaten.

**Feeding behavior.** Among 144 tui chub pharyngeals, 32 retained a complete tooth series. Their teeth are readily dislodged, so this is strong evidence that fish heads were swallowed whole. The 1965 material supported the same conclusion.

## Key Findings

**Overall counts**
- More than 5,800 remains, representing at least 98 fish, were recovered from 14 coprolites (eight AN, six LX). The paper's table lists seven AN coprolites with fish remains.
- About 47 remains, representing at least seven fish, were recovered from midden refuse in the West Alcove.

**Tui Chub, *Gila (Siphateles) bicolor***
- **Coprolites:** 144 pharyngeals (32 complete, 112 incomplete), 3.1–9.1 mm long. They represent at least 97 fish, about 45–130 mm long and perhaps 0.7–20.8 g each, with a total estimated weight of about 471 g. Of these pharyngeals, 125 came from seven AN coprolites and 19 from six LX coprolites.
- **Midden:** three pharyngeals, representing three fish about 22, 25, and 32 cm long.
- The fish is bony but palatable (Kimsey 1954).

**Tahoe Sucker, *Catostomus tahoensis***
- **Coprolites:** one incomplete quadrate (8.4 mm), representing a fish about 21 cm long and perhaps 120 g, from coprolite AN-11.
- **Midden:** none.

**Cui-ui, *Chasmistes cujus***
- **Coprolites:** none.
- **Midden:** two cleithra (one representing a fish about 55 cm long and perhaps 1.8 kg), one articulated fragment, about 30 vertebrae representing fish up to about 61 cm long and perhaps 2.5 kg, three hypural fans, and pelvic fins.
- The cui-ui was the principal food supply of the Pyramid Lake [[Northern Paiute]] (Powers 1877).

**Absent taxa**
- [[Lahontan Speckled Dace]] (five pharyngeals in the 1965 collections) was not found in this material.
- [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] was not found in the 1965 or present collections, nor in the earlier Loud and Harrington (1929) collections. The author considers explanations for this absence conjectural.

**Comparison with 1965 collections**
- 1965: about 298 tui chubs, five Lahontan speckled dace, and three Tahoe suckers in 29 whole coprolites, plus midden remains of about 22 tui chubs, seven Tahoe suckers, and eight cui-ui.
- Present study: fewer remains overall. Fish sizes fall between the largest and smallest in the 1965 collections.
- Two 1965 coprolites yielded 123 and 101 tui chub pharyngeals. Coprolite AN-2 (45 pharyngeals) approaches that abundance.
- The AN series produced 125 pharyngeals and the LX series 19. In 1965, the entrance lot yielded 458 pharyngeals, while the interior lot yielded 24.

**Conclusions**
- The species represented are consistent with lake and river fishing in the Humboldt and Truckee systems.
- Differences in pharyngeal counts between locations may reflect seasonal occupation patterns, which are inferred rather than demonstrated.
- The cui-ui's presence at the site cannot be linked to a high Lake Lahontan stage because that stage predates human occupation.
