# Geographic Variation Within the Long-Tailed Group of the Glossy Snake, *Arizona elegans* Kennicott

#herpetology #snakes #colubridae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 study by [[Fleet and Dixon]] (*Herpetologica* 27:295–302) examines geographic variation in the long-tailed group of the [[Glossy Snake]] (*Arizona elegans*). The authors analyze ventral scale counts, caudal scale counts, and dorsal body blotch counts across four north-south geographic samples, including a population of uncertain status from [[Trans-Pecos Texas]]. Their analysis indicates that *A. e. blanchardi*, the Trans-Pecos population, and *A. e. elegans* form a continuous population with unrestricted gene flow from northern Kansas and southern Nebraska to southern Tamaulipas, Mexico. They therefore propose that *blanchardi* be placed in the synonymy of *A. e. elegans*.

## Background & Description

The long-tailed group of *Arizona elegans* was defined by [[Klauber]] (1946) as subspecies in which males averaged tail lengths of 14.9% or more of total length. At the time of the study, this group comprised five subspecies: *A. e. elegans*, *A. e. expolita*, *A. e. philipi*, *A. e. blanchardi*, and *A. e. arenicola* (Dixon 1959, 1960).

[[Dixon]] (1959) had examined seven specimens from the Big Bend region of Trans-Pecos Texas and suggested they were intergrades among *philipi*, *expolita*, and *blanchardi*. Since then, the authors examined 15 additional specimens from Trans-Pecos Texas, 75 from New Mexico, and 12 from Mexico. A total of 203 specimens were examined across all localities.

Key characters used were:
- Number of ventral scales
- Number of caudal scales
- Number of dorsal body blotches

Statistics included range, mean, and standard error. Sexes were analyzed separately for ventral and caudal counts. Means were compared using the [[Kramer Multiple Comparison Method]] (SNK procedure for unequal sample sizes), with significance set at P < .05.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Trans-Pecos Texas specimens lie at the limits of the distributions of *expolita*, *philipi*, *blanchardi*, and *elegans*, within or near 150 miles of the area. The authors show that these specimens were intermediate between *blanchardi* to the north and *elegans* to the south. The subspecies *arenicola* was excluded from further discussion because it intergrades with the nominate race in the [[Rio Grande Embayment]] region of Texas and Mexico and lies outside the problem area.

The study area spans a broad latitudinal gradient from central Kansas to southern Tamaulipas, Mexico. Specimens were drawn from Kansas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Texas, Coahuila, Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, Chihuahua, Durango, San Luis Potosí, Zacatecas, and Arizona. ## Key Findings

- **Ventral scales:** Means for the four geographic samples differed significantly from those of *philipi* and *expolita*. Ventral counts of *philipi* and *expolita* did not differ significantly from each other, although [[Klauber]] (1946) had used dorsal blotch number as the sole separating character. Female ventral means did not differ significantly among the four samples where testing was possible. In males, the Kansas-Oklahoma-Panhandle sample differed from the Plains-Texas sample, but this difference disappeared when Plains-Texas was compared with the nearest Panhandle sample. Ventral counts were highest in both sexes in the Plains-Texas sample.
- **Caudal scales:** Caudal means for both sexes differed significantly between *expolita* and *philipi*, a distinction not recognized by earlier workers. The *philipi* caudal mean did not differ significantly from Trans-Pecos Texas or Mexican *elegans* samples, which the authors attribute to chance because there is no evidence of geographic contact. No significant differences in caudal means were found among the four geographic samples.
- **Body blotches:** Dorsal blotch means differed significantly between *philipi* and *expolita*, and both showed a clinal decrease from north to south (Dixon 1959). The *expolita* mean did not differ significantly from Mexican *elegans*. Within the four north-south samples, blotch numbers declined southward, with adjacent means differing significantly only between Trans-Pecos Texas and Mexican *elegans*. When blotch number was plotted by latitude, an apparent cline was observed from southern Tamaulipas to central Kansas (Fig. 4).
- **Conclusion:** The authors found no variation large enough to indicate genetic isolation. They conclude that *A. e. blanchardi*, *A. e. elegans*, and the intermediate Trans-Pecos Texas sample constitute one continuous population with unrestricted gene flow, and recommend placing *blanchardi* in the synonymy of *A. e. elegans*.
- **Limitations:** The apparent difference between Trans-Pecos Texas and Mexican *elegans* blotch counts may reflect a lack of specimens from northern Coahuila. The Mexican *elegans* sample showed weaker clinal variation than other samples, possibly due to small sample size.
