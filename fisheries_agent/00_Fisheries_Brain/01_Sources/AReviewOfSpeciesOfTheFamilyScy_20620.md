# A Review of Scyphacidae Isopods in the New World

#crustacea #isopoda #oniscoidea #scyphacidae #marine-biology

## Executive Summary

This 1972 taxonomic review by George A. Schultz, published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* (Vol. 84, No. 56, pp. 477-488), revises the terrestrial isopod family [[Scyphacidae]] in the New World. The family consists of beach-dwelling [[Oniscoidea]] (terrestrial isopods) that occupy beach niches on sea coasts. Eight species in four genera are recorded from the New World. The paper defines the family, reassigns misplaced taxa, clarifies distribution records, and provides a dichotomous key to the New World species.

## Background & Description

**Scope and taxonomy.** The author examined specimens in the [[National Museum of Natural History]] (Smithsonian Institution) and the [[American Museum of Natural History]]. Vandel (1962) treated Scyphacidae as a subfamily of [[Oniscidae]], but Schultz considers it a distinct family.

**Diagnostic characters.** The family is defined (modified from Vandel, 1962) by:
1. Four flagellar articles on the flagellum of antenna 2.
2. Primitive cephalon, almost always with a supra-antennal line.
3. Maxilliped with four palp articles plus endite, or five defined articles.
4. Dactyl organ present on peraeopods (absent in *Scyphax*).
5. Primitive male genital apophysis and endopod of pleopod 1.

**Genera.** The New World fauna comprises:
- *Deto* Guérin: two species in southern South America.
- *Armadilloniscus* Uljanin: four species in North America (one on the east coast and in Bermuda, three on the west coast).
- *Scyphacella* Smith: monotypic, *S. arenicola*.
- *Detonella* Lohmander: one species, *D. papillicornis*.

The genera *Scyphax* and *Scyphoniscus* are not represented in the New World.

**Taxonomic corrections.**
- [[Richardson]] (1904) counted "about seven" antennal articles in *Detonella papillicornis* and placed it in Trichoniscidae. Lohmander (1927) corrected this to four or five and moved it to Scyphacidae. The author confirmed four articles in the type specimen (USNM 28772).
- Verhoeff (1942) reported seven articles in *Detonella sachalina*, which the author considers unlikely. Specimens labelled *D. sachalina* in the USNM proved to be *Trichorhina*, which has two articles.
- *Alloniscus* Dana (1856), placed in Scyphacinae by Vandel (1968), has three antennal articles and is closer to Oniscinae or Philosciinae. Its revision is deferred to a separate paper.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Species occupy beach niches, typically the upper beach and maritime drift. *Armadilloniscus ellipticus* is common on well-drained sand beaches, often under flat cover such as boards, rocks, or boxes left on the beach for long periods, and it may be submerged at high tide.
- ***Deto marina*** was collected from under rocks in seaweed at the upper beach at Willunga, South Australia. It is most abundant in New Zealand and southern Australia.
- ***Armadilloniscus lindahli*** occurs on beaches from Tomales Bay, central California, to Isla Cedros, Baja California. It is unique among west coast species in being able to roll into a ball like a pill bug.
- ***Armadilloniscus coronacapitalis*** was collected under rocks at the high-tide line at Tomales Bay.
- **Distribution limits:** *Scyphacella arenicola* ranges from Woods Hole, Massachusetts, south to Miami, Florida. Its Miami record (USNM 42585) predates 1918, and no Florida record has been made since, so it may reflect former distribution. *Detonella papillicornis* ranges from the Bering Island and the Aleutians to Puget Sound, Washington.
- **Sampling gaps:** The author notes more New World species probably await discovery in the West Indies and South America. Schultz found *S. arenicola* absent from the extensive beaches near Beaufort, North Carolina.

## Key Findings

**Species accounts and specimen data**
- ***Scyphacella arenicola* Smith (1873):** Long recorded but never properly illustrated. The dissected specimen is a male 3.4 mm long from Norfolk, Virginia, the longest male in the collection. The largest female measured 4.4 mm (Choptank River, Maryland). No females were gravid.
- ***Detonella papillicornis* (Richardson, 1904):** The type specimen (USNM 28772) is a male, 3.8 mm long. Small tubercles occur on the cephalon and anterior peraeonal segments, and the eyes sit on conspicuous bumps. Its described range extends from Bering Island to Puget Sound. Lohmander's (1927) earlier descriptions covered appendages in detail but did not illustrate the whole animal. Verhoeff's *D. lohmanderi* (1942) is treated as a synonym.
- ***Deto bucculenta* (Nicolet, 1849):** Collected at Bay of Valparaiso, Chile. It has not been cited from the New World since Van Name (1936). Hurley (1950) recorded a species he called *D. bucculenta* from New Zealand.
- ***Deto marina* (Chilton, 1884):** Specimens illustrated are from Willunga, South Australia. Stebbing's (1900) record from the Falkland Islands, cited as *Trichoniscus magellanicus*, is re-identified by Vandel (1952) as *D. marina*. No South American specimens were examined.
- ***Armadilloniscus ellipticus* (Harger, 1878):** Bermuda specimens show more noticeable cephalic tubercles than North Carolina specimens. Specimens from Virginia are plentiful in the National Museum. Subspecies cannot be assessed without a larger series.
- ***Armadilloniscus lindahli* (Richardson, 1905):** Includes *Scleropactes cedrosensis* Mulaik (1960) as a synonym.
- ***Armadilloniscus holmesi* Arcangeli (1933):** Originally described as *tuberculatus* (Holmes and Gay, 1909), but the name was preoccupied. Range runs from Friday Harbor to Bahia Magdalena, Baja California.
- ***Armadilloniscus coronacapitalis* Menzies (1950):** Distinguished by large cephalic tubercles.

**Dichotomous key to New World Scyphacidae (summary)**
1. Uropodal bases flattened and expanded: go to 2. Uropodal bases not expanded: go to 5.
2. Eyes moderately large (10 or more ocelli), with elongate peraeonal tubercles in rows: *A. ellipticus*. Eyes small (7 or fewer ocelli): go to 3.
3. Rostrum broad or truncate, able to roll into a ball: *A. lindahli*. Rostrum pointed: go to 4.
4. Large cephalic tubercles, peraeon with two or more conspicuous tubercle rows: *A. coronacapitalis*. Only small cephalic tubercles, relatively smooth peraeon: *A. holmesi*.
5. Anterolateral cephalic processes large and angular: go to 6. Processes small, inconspicuous: *S. arenicola*.
6. Peraeonal segment I expanded laterally: *D. bucculenta*. Not expanded: go to 7.
7. Cephalon and peraeon covered with short spines: *D. marina*. Relatively smooth, never spinose: *D. papillicornis*.

**Conclusions**
- Eight species in four genera are recognised in the New World, and more are likely to be found in the West Indies and South America.
- Several earlier records are corrected or synonymised, and the Scyphacidae is confirmed as a distinct family defined by four flagellar articles.
- The author plans further comparison of *D. papillicornis* with *D. sachalina* from Sakhalin Island once adequate material is available.
