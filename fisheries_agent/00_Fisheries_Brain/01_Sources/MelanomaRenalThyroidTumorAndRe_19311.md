# Melanoma, Renal Thyroid Tumor and Reticulo-Endothelial Hyperplasia in a Non-Hybrid Platyfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This histopathological case report describes a melanoma in a **non-hybrid** [[Spike-Tailed Platyfish]] (*Xiphophorus variatus xiphidium*). Melanomas in this genus are usually associated with interspecific hybrids, so the finding was unexpected. The same fish had a [[Thyroid Tumor]] in the pharyngeal region and kidney, an abnormal [[Pituitary Gland]], and marked hyperplasia of the [[Reticulo-Endothelial System]]. The authors also report large masses of melanin-laden macrophages in the kidney, a feature they state had not been previously described. A second, melanotic (pre-tumor) specimen of the same species is described for comparison.

## Background & Description

**Context.** Melanomas consistently arise in certain [[Platyfish-Swordtail Hybrids]] and other interspecific hybrids of xiphophorin fishes. Thyroid tumors develop in some species and strains maintained in iodine-poor water. Genetic factors are important in both kinds of atypical pigment and thyroid growth. The authors note that in these fish, melanosis precedes melanoma.

**Study animals.**
- **Melanomatous specimen:** an adult male *X. v. xiphidium* from the sixth laboratory-reared generation of fish collected in 1939 from the Rio Purificacion, Tamaulipas, Mexico (strain 53). Identification was verified by Dr. Donn E. Rosen of the American Museum of Natural History. It measured 28 mm standard length and 12 mm depth. It carried the dominant sex-linked gene *Sp* (macromelanophore spotting), and much of its body was intensely black.
- **Melanotic specimen:** a second adult male, also laboratory-reared, measuring 27 mm standard length and 11 mm depth. It carried *Sp*, and its body was almost completely black.

**Gross anatomy.** The melanomatous fish had an externally visible swelling under the dorsal fin on the right side, about 8 × 6.5 × 2 mm with a smooth surface. The thyroid tumor was not visible externally.

**Methods.**
- Melanomatous fish: fixed in [[Bouin's Fluid]], decalcified in formic acid, paraffin-embedded, and sectioned at 3, 7 and 10 µm. Stains were Harris's hematoxylin and eosin, Masson's trichrome, or Heidenhain's iron hematoxylin. Some sections were bleached with hydrogen peroxide or potassium permanganate plus oxalic acid to reveal nuclei within melanin-laden cells.
- Melanotic fish: formalin-fixed, decalcified, paraffin-embedded, sectioned at 7 and 10 µm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Slow formalin fixation left the hypophysis poorly preserved.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Husbandry and iodine.** Thyroid tumors in this group have been linked to domesticated (laboratory) conditions, sometimes with known [[Iodine Deficiency]]. The authors state that every previously reported case of displaced thyroid tissue in fish came from a domesticated environment, and all were freshwater species.
- **Genetic background.** The *Sp* macromelanophore gene requires genetic buffering to control growth. The authors suggest that this buffering was presumably disrupted in the melanomatous fish, possibly by mutation or inbreeding. Melanomas had previously been found only in interspecific hybrids, the domesticated Fu strain of *X. maculatus* (Kosswig, 1938), and some *X. montezumae cortezi* carrying the *Sc* factor (unpublished data).
- **Survey scope.** The authors have examined thousands of wild and laboratory-reared poeciliid fish, including *X. v. xiphidium*. Melanomas were found only in the exceptions listed above.
- **Endocrine influence.** [[Stolk Thiouracil Study|Stolk (1959)]] reported that melanoma incidence increased in thiouracil-fed (hypothyroid) platyfish-swordtail hybrids and decreased in thyroxine-fed (hyperthyroid) ones. Pituitary hormones are known to regulate pigment cell formation and melanin synthesis in fish, so the authors suggest that the abnormal endocrine state may have influenced the melanoma.

## Key Findings

### 1. Melanoma (melanomatous specimen)
- The melanoma was microscopically similar to those previously described in platyfish-swordtail hybrids (Reed & Gordon, 1931; Gordon & Smith, 1938).
- It consisted mainly of lightly pigmented [[Melanocytes]]. Cells near muscle formed whorls, with small spindle-shaped nuclei (about 2 × 5 µm). Elsewhere nuclei were round, oval or irregular, mostly under 5 µm in diameter, with prominent nucleoli.
- Mitotic figures were rare. Some nuclei showed median or budlike constrictions, possibly indicating amitosis, and some giant nuclei were present.
- The tumor had destroyed much of the right-side body musculature and infiltrated the dermis. The epidermis was thickened, and scales were displaced.
- Metastases are very rare in fish melanomas, and none were reported here.

### 2. Thyroid tumor (melanomatous specimen)
- **Pharyngeal thyroid:** markedly hyperplastic, extending into the bases of the gills. It consisted of small to large follicles filled with dense, acidophilic colloid.
- **Renal thyroid:** many large, colloid-filled follicles similar to the pharyngeal tissue, plus swollen cystic follicles nearly devoid of colloid. Small amounts of thyroid tissue were also found in the choroid gland of the eye. No thyroid tissue was seen elsewhere.
- Colloid was extensive in both pharyngeal and renal masses. The authors describe these growths as more like colloid goiter than adenoma, as in man.
- Renal tissue was less disturbed than in renal thyroid tumors of *X. maculatus* (Baker et al., 1955).

### 3. Pituitary hyperplasia (melanomatous specimen)
- The anterior pituitary showed a homogeneous overgrowth of light-staining basophils, which elongated the gland and made the whole organ misshapen.
- The authors had previously seen some hypertrophy of pale basophils in *X. maculatus* with goitrous thyroids or regenerating thyroid tissue after radioiodine treatment. In those fish, however, the change was less extensive than in this specimen.

### 4. Reticulo-endothelial hyperplasia (melanomatous specimen)
- **Melanin-laden macrophages:** spherical cells 5–10 µm in diameter, with nuclei compressed at the periphery by melanin. These formed large masses in the kidney and in an anterior growth ventral to the vertebral column. That mass surrounded the urinary ducts and compressed the air bladder. Scattered cells were also found in the liver, around the brain, and in brain blood vessels.
- **Primitive reticular cells:** pale cells with indistinct outlines and round nuclei (2–5 µm), forming nodules in the kidney and among pharyngeal thyroid follicles.
- The authors conclude that this was a reactive hyperplasia, not metastasis. The masses were too large to be explained by cell migration alone and may have damaged the air bladder and kidney through pressure.

### 5. Melanotic specimen
- **Melanosis:** melanophores occurred in the dermis and along muscle septa in the posterior body. Melanocytes had multiplied enough to compress some muscles, with slight musculature destruction and epidermal thickening. The authors cannot say whether this would have become a melanoma.
- **Thyroid:** the pharyngeal thyroid appeared normal, with 20–175 µm follicles. None contained colloid, which the authors attribute to formalin artifact. No thyroid tissue was found in the kidney, but pearl-like basophilic concretions were present, similar to those in regressing thyroid tumors treated with potassium iodide (Berg et al., 1954).
- **Pituitary:** appeared normal, similar to *X. maculatus*, though poorly preserved.
- **Macrophages:** the same type of melanin-laden macrophage mass was found ventral to the vertebral column and around the urinary ducts and bladder. Numerous masses were in the kidney, and some lined the air bladder.

### 6. Melanin disposal (discussion)
- In the kidney, [[Melanin-Laden Macrophages]] appeared unable to clear melanin as fast as the melanocytes produced it, through intracellular digestion or urinary excretion.
- Other routes include phagocytosis by macrophages in the immediate area, which may later be sloughed through the epidermis (as seen in fish and amphibians), and lymphatic transport to lymph nodes or other macrophage-rich sites.
- Normally pigmented poeciliid fish also carry small numbers of melanin-containing macrophages in the kidney and other lymphoid tissues, supporting these disposal routes.
- The kidney is the richest lymphoid and main hemopoietic organ in teleosts, so it is the likely site where much of the melanin was taken up.

### 7. Relation between hyperplasia and tumors (discussion)
- The authors conclude that the reticulo-endothelial hyperplasia was a reaction to the melanoma and thyroid tumor. Excess melanin production may have caused it, or melanin phagocytosis may have been secondary.
- Mammalian studies (Old et al., 1960) also report increased reticulo-endothelial activity during tumor growth.

### 8. Summary of conclusions
- The melanoma is described in a non-hybrid xiphophorin, which is unusual.
- The fish had a colloid-rich thyroid tumor in the pharynx and kidney, an abnormal pituitary, and extensive reticulo-endothelial hyperplasia.
- The two specimens, one with melanoma and one with melanosis, show a progression from melanosis to melanoma within the same species.
- The authors provide new evidence that much of the melanin produced by pigment cells is taken up by phagocytes in the fish kidney.
