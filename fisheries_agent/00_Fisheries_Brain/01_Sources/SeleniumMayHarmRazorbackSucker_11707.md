## Executive Summary

This U.S. National Biological Survey Research Information Bulletin reports findings from a 1992 investigation into selenium contamination in [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) populations in the middle Green River near Jensen, Utah. Conducted in cooperation with the Colorado River Fishery Project (CRFP), the study found elevated selenium concentrations in eggs, milt, and muscle tissue of wild-caught razorback suckers compared to fish from uncontaminated reference sites and hatchery stock. The paper raises concern that agricultural irrigation drain water entering the Green River via [[Ashley Creek]] and [[Stewart Lake]] is a source of selenium bioaccumulation that may be impairing reproductive success and contributing to the species' documented lack of recruitment, thereby complicating recovery efforts for this endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fish.

## Background & Description

- **Sampled specimens:** 8 adult razorback suckers (3 females, 5 males) captured at Razorback Bar, Green River, Utah (April 29 – May 5, 1992).
- **Female size range:** 1,485–1,798 g; 540–549 mm length.
- **Male size range:** 1,127–1,814 g; 471–546 mm length.
- **Sampling method:** A 0.5-mm diameter muscle plug was removed from the back of each adult for selenium analysis; eggs collected from females and milt from males.
- **Tissue moisture content:** Ranged widely, 67.6%–95.3% across individuals, relevant to interpreting dry-weight selenium concentrations.
- Concurrent research suggested selenium concentrations are consistent across anterior, middle, and posterior muscle plug sampling locations on the same fish, supporting muscle-plug sampling as a non-lethal proxy technique.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** [[Razorback Sucker]] inhabits large, turbid rivers of the [[Colorado River Basin]]; in the Green River, adults congregate at tributary mouths (e.g., [[Ashley Creek]]) prior to spawning.
- **Spawning behavior:** Pre-spawning aggregation occurs at the mouth of Ashley Creek, a tributary carrying selenium-laden irrigation drain water into the Green River; this reach also receives contaminated drainage from [[Stewart Lake]].
- **Larval habitat:** Larval razorback suckers have been found predominantly in shallow, low-current backwater areas (such as the Ashley Creek confluence), which are also zones of elevated selenium exposure via waterborne and dietary pathways.
- **Threats context:** Species decline across the Colorado River system attributed to a combination of dams, habitat destruction, degraded water quality, and interactions with non-native fishes — with this study adding selenium contamination from irrigated agriculture as an underexamined stressor.
- **Population status (1979–1986 surveys):** Low adult abundance, minimal growth, and no apparent recruitment observed in the Green River population.

## Key Findings

- **Egg selenium concentrations:** 3.7–10.6 ppm (dry weight) in three females sampled in 1992; a separate 1991 sample from the Green River measured 28 ppm.
- **Reference comparison (eggs):** Hatchery-reared razorback suckers (Dexter National Fish Hatchery, NM) had 2.8 ppm selenium in eggs; other fish species from uncontaminated U.S. sites ranged 2.0–3.1 ppm.
- **Toxicological benchmarks:** 
  - [[Fathead Minnow]] larvae hatched from females with 24 ppm ovarian selenium showed increased edema and lordosis.
  - [[Bluegill]] larvae hatched from females with 18 ppm ovarian selenium showed reduced survival.
  - Implication: Green River razorback sucker egg selenium levels (up to 28 ppm) fall within or exceed ranges associated with adverse effects in other species.
- **Milt selenium concentrations:** <1.1–6.7 ppm in five males, compared to 2.0–3.8 ppm in milt from uncontaminated-site fish populations.
- **Muscle plug selenium concentrations:** 4.4–32 ppm in females; 3.6–26 ppm in males.
- **Correlations:** Strong correlation between muscle-plug selenium and gonadal selenium — eggs (r = 0.99) and milt (r = 0.87) — suggesting muscle biopsy could serve as a non-lethal predictive tool for gonadal selenium burden in this endangered species.
- **Source attribution:** Selenium contamination linked to irrigation drain water entering via Ashley Creek and Stewart Lake, concentrated in the same river reach used by adults for pre-spawning staging.
- **Conservation implication:** Authors conclude selenium contamination may be contributing to the decline of the razorback sucker and could interfere with ongoing recovery efforts, though a definitive causal link to reduced reproductive success in this specific dataset remained undetermined and warranted additional study.
