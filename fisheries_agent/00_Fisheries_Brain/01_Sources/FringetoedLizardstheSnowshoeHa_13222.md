# Fringe-toed Lizards—The Snowshoe Hares of Desert Dunes

#herpetology #desert-ecology #Phrynosomatidae

## Executive Summary

This article describes the specialized morphological adaptations of fringe-toed lizards (genus *Uma*) that enable efficient locomotion on desert sand dunes. Three species inhabit the warm deserts of the southwestern United States: [[Coachella Valley Fringe-Toed Lizard]], [[Colorado Desert Fringe-Toed Lizard]], and [[Mohave Fringe-Toed Lizard]]. These desert-dune endemics face significant conservation challenges due to habitat loss and alteration, with varying federal and state protection status. The article emphasizes their ecological specialization and vulnerability to anthropogenic threats.

## Background & Description

Fringe-toed lizards possess distinctive morphological adaptations for life on sand dunes. The primary adaptation consists of fringes formed by projecting, pointed scales on the toes of the rear feet. These structures function analogously to snowshoes, allowing the lizards to scurry across loose windblown sand without sinking, leaving characteristic small, dimpled footprints.

Additional adaptations for sand-dwelling include an underslung lower jaw, earflaps, and valves in their noses, which prevent sand from entering their heads when they dive into dunes. This diving behavior serves as an effective predator-avoidance strategy: when pursued, lizards may dash over a dune crest, then suddenly dive and wiggle into the sand, leaving only a surface dimple and confounding predators.

Behavioral adaptations include thermoregulatory strategies where lizards barely ease their heads out of the sand upon waking, allowing the sun to warm the blood in their brains before completely emerging from overnight shelter beneath the sand.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fringe-toed lizards are desert-dune endemics with highly restricted distributions across the warm deserts of the southwestern United States. Their extreme habitat specialization makes them vulnerable to habitat alteration.

The [[Colorado Desert Fringe-Toed Lizard]] occurs in Arizona in extremely arid areas south and east of [[Yuma]], found mostly on military lands encompassing the [[Yuma Dunes]] and [[Mohawk Dunes]]. These populations are isolated, and monitoring procedures are being developed to track population trends in these areas.

Major threats to fringe-toed lizards include:
- Cropland conversion
- Pesticides
- Urbanization
- Off-road vehicles
- Habitat loss

## Key Findings

**Conservation Status:**
- [[Coachella Valley Fringe-Toed Lizard]]: Listed as federally threatened and California threatened due to habitat loss
- [[Colorado Desert Fringe-Toed Lizard]]: Federal candidate species and Arizona state candidate for listing
- [[Mohave Fringe-Toed Lizard]]: Arizona candidate list (not federally listed)
- Both Mohave and Colorado Desert species are considered "species of special concern" in California

**Habitat Specialization:**
Fringe-toed lizards demonstrate extreme niche specialization, restricting them to windblown sand dune habitats. This specialization, while enabling efficient exploitation of dune environments, increases vulnerability to habitat alteration and fragmentation.

**Management Actions:**
Monitoring procedures are under development for populations on military lands in Arizona to track trends in isolated populations of the Colorado Desert fringe-toed lizard.
