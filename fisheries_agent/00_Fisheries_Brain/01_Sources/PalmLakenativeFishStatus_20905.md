# Palm Lake Native Fish Status Assessment 1992

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] documents a sampling effort conducted on October 26-27, 1992 at [[Palm Lake]] ([[Hassayampa River Preserve]]) to assess the survival of endangered native fish species stocked in 1989 and 1990. The assessment followed a summer mortality event (low oxygen "kill") in May-June 1992. Trammel net sampling successfully collected 32 [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and 1 [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), indicating significant survival of stocked endangered fish despite the oxygen depletion event. No [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) were collected, though their survival is considered likely.

## Background & Description

Three endangered native fish species were previously stocked into [[Palm Lake]]:
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) - stocked April 1989
- [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) - stocked April 1989  
- [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) - stocked February 1990

The lake experienced a low oxygen mortality event during May-June 1992, prompting this assessment to determine endangered fish survival. The sampling was conducted by Paul Marsh ([[Arizona State University]]), Rob Bettaso, and Kerry L. Young (Native Fish Program Manager, AGFD).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Palm Lake]] is managed under a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) agreement between [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] for endangered fish management purposes. The lake experienced a "summer kill" event attributed to low oxygen conditions in May-June 1992, raising concerns about the survival of stocked endangered species.

The presence of 41 [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), a non-native species, indicates established populations of introduced fish in the system.

## Key Findings

**Sampling Methods:**
- Trammel nets deployed: two 30m × 2.7m nets and one 100m × 2.7m net
- Sampling period: October 26, 1530 hrs through October 27, 1000 hrs
- Nets checked at two-hour intervals
- Target species held in live-cars and released after processing

**Bonytail Collection Results:**
- Total collected: 32 individuals
- Mean total length: 354mm (range: 274-434mm)
- Mean weight: 344 grams (range: 218-632 grams)
- All specimens released alive

**Colorado River Squawfish Results:**
- Total collected: 1 individual
- Total length: 367mm
- Weight: 378 grams
- Released alive

**Razorback Sucker Results:**
- None collected during sampling effort
- Survival considered "extremely unlikely" if [[Bonytail]] survived the oxygen depletion
- Absence attributed to sampling methodology rather than mortality

**Methodological Considerations:**
- [[Colorado River Squawfish]] noted as difficult to capture with trammel nets
- Comparison to [[ASU Research Park]] where squawfish caught by anglers but rarely in trammel/electrofishing efforts
- Suggests actual squawfish population likely higher than catch indicates

**Management Conclusions:**
- "Significant proportion" of stocked endangered fish survived the summer mortality event
- Interagency meeting scheduled for January 14, 1993 to assess Palm Lake management strategy
- Meeting to address past management, current status, and future direction for endangered fish use
