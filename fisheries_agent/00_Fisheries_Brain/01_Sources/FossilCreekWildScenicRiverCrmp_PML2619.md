# Fossil Creek Wild & Scenic River CRMP: Public Comment, Proposed Alternative, and Public Coverage

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fishes

## Executive Summary

This package combines three items on the [[Comprehensive River Management Plan]] (CRMP) for [[Fossil Creek]], a designated [[Wild and Scenic River]] in Arizona:

1. **A public comment letter** (April 27, 2011) from fishery scientist [[Paul C. Marsh]]. It disputes the classification of recreation as an [[Outstandingly Remarkable Values]] (ORV) item, calls the [[Forest Service]] proposal too liberal, and supports the "Proposed Alternative Scenario" while urging stepwise, more restrictive limits. It also raises unresolved safety, maintenance, and funding questions for [[Forest Road 708]].
2. **The Forest Service Proposed Alternative Scenario and Proposed Action** (dated March 28, 2011). It sets goals, management actions by river segment, desired conditions, and a three-phase implementation plan. Its central tool is recreation control through a summer shuttle, day-use limits, and paved or hardened parking.
3. **A news report** (Payson Roundup, April 29, 2011) on the public workshop. Residents expressed mixed support and concern, mainly about shuttle fees, access, road conditions, and emergency response.

Fish and aquatic habitat is one of five ORVs. Its protection is the main rationale for the restrictions, which are aimed at protecting native fish populations, the [[Fish Barrier]] that excludes non-native fish, and [[Travertine]] formation.

## Background & Description

**Study context.** Marsh is a professional fishery scientist with more than three decades of experience with southwestern native fishes. He has been involved in restoring flows and native fishes in Fossil Creek since the 1990s. His letter was addressed to [[Lynn Humphrey]], Fossil Creek Planning Team Leader.

**Waterway.** The Payson article describes a roughly 15-mile stretch of stream from the spring near the foot of the steep [[Fossil Springs]] Trail to the confluence with the [[Verde River]] near Childs and its natural hot springs. The article describes the creek as one of the world's six pristine streams with extensive travertine.

**Designation.** The stream is one of two Wild and Scenic segments in Arizona. The plan balances recreation, water quality, and wildlife.

**Management zones.** The plan divides the corridor into segments with different ROS (Recreation Opportunity Spectrum) classifications:
- **Northern Wild Segment:** Primitive ROS; year-round camping allowed; no fires.
- **Fossil Springs Recreational Segment:** Semi-Primitive ROS; limited dispersed camping (about 5 or fewer sites outside the riparian zone); group size under 25; two composting toilets; a helipad rescue site just outside the corridor.
- **Old Dam to Waterfall Wild Segment:** Primitive ROS; day use only; no designated trails; described as a wildlife refugium.
- **Middle Fossil Creek Recreational Segment:** Roaded-natural experience along Fossil Creek Road; the main recreation area with paved parking, toilets, kiosks, and creek access points.
- **Southern Wild Segment:** Primitive ROS; year-round dispersed camping with monitoring.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Native fish and amphibians.** The Desired Conditions describe native fish populations that "continue to thrive" and self-sustaining populations of re-introduced native fishes. Non-native fish are present only below the fish barrier. Lowland leopard frogs are re-colonizing Fossil Creek downstream. Garter snake and native frog and springsnail populations are described as abundant and diverse. No specific fish species are named in the source material.

**Threats identified.**
- Recreation impacts: litter, dispersed camping, fires, vegetation removal, and trampling.
- Sedimentation and erosion from unpaved roads, especially [[Childs Road]] (FR 502) near Purple Mountain.
- Non-native species, which may require physical or chemical removal from the fishery and stock tanks.
- Groundwater pumping that could reduce creek flows, including demand from Strawberry.
- Wildfire, which could affect riparian and water quality.
- Dust from roads, which affects residents and water quality.

**Water and travertine.** Water quality is to be maintained or enhanced to meet the creek's classification as an Outstanding Arizona Water. Travertine step-pool formation depends on natural flows. Research is planned on recreation effects (swimming, kayaking), with possible limits on kayaking if impacts prove unacceptable.

**Infrastructure and access.** Marsh describes [[Forest Road 708]] as steep, narrow, winding, unguarded, prone to rockfall and erosion, slippery when wet or icy, and dangerous even under good conditions. The Payson article reports about 100 informal parking spaces along the canyon bottom road and frequent cars parked in haphazard spaces.

## Key Findings

### Points raised in the public comment letter (Marsh)

- **Recreation is a use, not a value.** The letter argues that recreation should be removed from the ORV list. The ORVs are geology, fish and aquatic habitat, wildlife, history and traditional uses, and water. Recreation can negatively affect these values.
- **Support for the Proposed Alternative, with reservations.** Marsh supports the Proposed Alternative over the Forest Service proposal, which he considers too liberal and likely to cause "unnecessary and potentially irreversible damage." He also feels the alternative may not be restrictive enough.
- **Stepwise implementation.** Key elements should be implemented in steps to determine appropriate limits on users, vehicles, paved parking, and constructed amenities.
- **Road safety and funding.** He asks who will maintain Forest Road 708, at what cost and frequency, who pays, whether the Forest Service can assume responsibility, and how the road will handle projected traffic. He says these questions must be answered before any management plan is adopted.
- **Shuttle practicality.** He considers the shuttle system impractical.

### Forest Service Proposed Action and Proposed Alternative

- **Primary goal:** Maintain and enhance ORVs mainly by managing recreation impacts through access control, user limits, seasonal restrictions, and hardened recreation sites.
- **Water protection:** Limited day-use visitors in Middle Fossil Creek; toilets; rerouted waterfall trail; paved and repaired roads; campfire prohibition; and [[Best Management Practices]] on roads, trails, and recreation sites.
- **Visitor limits:** The Proposed Alternative estimates a limit of 75 cars and/or 300 people at one time. The Proposed Action proposes 500 to 1,200 people per day through the [[Shuttle System]], with a weekday shuttle and a reservation-based system. Shuttle fees are $5 to $10 per person, according to the Payson article.
- **Shuttle and winter management:** The shuttle would operate in summer, with private vehicles allowed in winter. Shuttle stops would include toilets, shade, kiosks, and creek access at several locations (Strawberry Road, Fossil Creek Road, Fossil Springs TH, Waterfall TH, Irving, Tonto Bench, Bridge Area, Homestead, Old Corral, Sally May, and Purple Mountain). Feasibility would be studied separately.
- **Fish and aquatic management:** Maintain the fish barrier. The [[Biological Opinion]] requires maintenance by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] for 100 years (the Proposed Action says the agency should continue when the commitment lapses). Maintain the ability to remove non-native species. Prohibit felt-soled waders and encourage lug-soled waders (Forest Plan). Establish closure orders for sensitive or threatened and endangered species habitat. Protect mesquite stands. Helipads must avoid black hawk nesting sites.
- **Water measures:** Continue the water gauge at the bridge, consider adding water quality monitoring, study groundwater pumping effects, pave or reroute Childs Road, and quantify federal reserved water rights.
- **Other resource measures:** Continue cultural inventory and tribal ceremonial access. Support travertine monitoring. Plan fuel treatments that protect the riparian corridor first. Restrict livestock from the WSR corridor. Remove invasive plants, especially blackberry, in the Fossil Springs Botanical Area.
- **Corridor-wide rules:** Year-round fees and permits; no glass containers; no campfires or charcoal or briquette grills; no firearms except for legal hunting; no picnic tables, shade canopies, or bridges except kiosks, parking, and toilets.

### Phased implementation

- **Full implementation** could take up to three years and depends on funding. Current management remains in place until the CRMP is finalized.
- **Phase I (immediate):** Day-use-only policy; permanent campfire ban; fees and permits after public involvement and Resource Advisory Committee approval; entry gates on Fossil Creek Road and Strawberry Road; limit Middle Fossil visitors to existing parking; add portable toilets and trash pickup; install composting toilets at Fossil Springs; harden bridge approaches; evaluate roads for erosion control; seek ADEQ permits.
- **Phase II (construction):** Build paved parking, entrance stations, kiosks, toilets, and trail improvements, scheduled outside summer.
- **Phase III (adaptive management):** Adjust visitor numbers based on ORV monitoring. The Proposed Action adds shuttle implementation for May to September.

### Public comments (Payson Roundup)

- **Attendance and mixed reaction:** The lightly attended workshop (seven Forest Service staff) produced mixed support and dismay. No final decision has been made, and the final plan could take another year.
- **Use data:** Average weekend use in 2010 was about 650 per day, peaking above 1,200 on busy weekends. Weekday use during summer averaged 150 to 200.
- **Visitor origin:** A Forest Service survey found that on peak weekends, Valley residents make up 58% of visitors and Rim Country residents only 2%.
- **Emergency response:** Tonto Rim Search and Rescue reported 14 rescue operations in the canyon the previous year, two-thirds involving injured or dehydrated hikers hauled up the Fossil Springs Trail. Pine-Strawberry Fire Board chair Forrest McCoy said ambulances must travel the road frequently and that the road remains dangerous.
- **Local concerns:** Residents objected to shuttle fees, especially for weekday access and for those who already buy a Tonto Pass. Others said the road has long been promised repair, and that litter and dust are serious problems.

### Discrepancies in the source material

- **Summer season dates:** The Proposed Alternative gives April 1 to October 15 for summer management. The Proposed Action gives May to September. The Payson article gives May through October.
- **Parking capacity:** The Proposed Alternative cites about 75 spaces. The Proposed Action cites about 100. The Payson article cites about 100 informal spaces.
- **Date of summary table:** The summary table is dated "March 28, 2017," which appears to be an OCR or typographical error for 2011.
