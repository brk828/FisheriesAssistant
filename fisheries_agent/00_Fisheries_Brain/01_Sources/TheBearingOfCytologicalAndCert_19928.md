# Progress Report: Cytological and Physiological Studies of Characeae Morphology and Taxonomy

#characeae #freshwater-algae #cytology #taxonomy #polyploidy

## Executive Summary

This progress report covers the first six months (through January 1, 1961) of NSF Research Grant G13261 investigating the [[Characeae]] (stoneworts), a group of complex aquatic plants. Principal Investigator Arland Hotchkiss from the [[University of Louisville]] Department of Biology reports extensive chromosome counting across multiple genera and species collected from sites spanning North America (Kentucky, Indiana, Quebec, Ontario, New York, Connecticut, Rhode Island, California), Mexico, and Australia. The research establishes baseline chromosome numbers for the family, explores polyploidy patterns, and examines taxonomic relationships through cytological evidence. Key collaborators include graduate student Donald Tindall (studying [[Nitella flexilis]] ecology and life history in [[Doe Run]], Kentucky) and Dr. Richard Wood ([[University of Rhode Island]]), who provided Australian specimens. The report documents 21 species with confirmed chromosome counts and proposes significant taxonomic revisions based on correlations between ploidy levels and reproductive strategies (monoecious vs. dioecious).

## Background & Description

The [[Characeae]] are simple aquatic plants with worldwide distribution, primarily in freshwater habitats. They share pigmentation, food reserves, and cell morphology with green algae but possess distinctive complexity in stem nodes and internodes, whorled branchlets, and elaborate gametangial structures. Taxonomically, they are placed either in Class Charophyceae of the Chlorophyta (following G.M. Smith) or in a separate Division Charophyta (following Groves, Bullock-Webster, and Zaneveld after Sachs).

The family contains a single Order [[Charales]] with one extant family [[Characeae]], subdivided into two tribes:

**Tribe [[Nitelleae]]** (entirely ecorticate, two tiers of 5 crown cells):
- [[Nitella]]: 153 species; forked branchlets, two stem branches per node, laterally compressed oospore, terminal antheridia
- [[Tolypella]]: 13 species; monopodial branchlets, more than two stem branches per node, terete oospore, lateral antheridia

**Tribe [[Chareae]]** (mostly corticated, single tier of 5 crown cells):
- [[Chara]]: 116 species (largest genus)
- Four additional genera with 1-4 species each: [[Protochara]], [[Nitellopsis]], [[Lychnothamnus]], [[Lamprothamnium]]

Important taxonomic characters include cortication degree, stipulode arrangement, branchlet structure, spore characteristics, and reproductive organ distribution (monoecious vs. dioecious).

Fossil [[Characeae]] are well-documented from the Devonian forward, with gyrogonites (fossilized oogonia with lime shells) known from late Triassic to recent times.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Collections were made from diverse freshwater habitats including:
- Lakes: [[Lake Wawasee]] (Indiana), [[Keuka Lake]] (New York), [[Lake James]] (Indiana), [[Crystal Lake]] at Fort Knox (Kentucky)
- Ponds: sinkhole ponds in [[Meade County]] and near [[Doe Run]] (Kentucky), various Rhode Island ponds
- Streams and rivers: [[Doe Run]] (Kentucky), [[Virgil Creek]] (New York), Ottawa River (Quebec)
- Artificial habitats: hatcheries, reservoirs

The [[Doe Run]] population of [[Nitella flexilis]] provides stable year-round conditions with constant water temperature, quality, and flow, enabling study of seasonal variations and developmental stages under natural conditions. This site supports investigation of potential protandrous winter forms versus monoecious spring/summer forms.

Water chemistry, habitat characteristics, and ecological relationships are being documented alongside cytological work, particularly for the [[Nitella flexilis]] study site.

## Key Findings

**Chromosome Numbers and Polyploidy Series:**

The research establishes three distinct polyploid series:

1. **[[Chara]] Corticatae**: 14:28 series
   - Haploid (N=14): [[Chara aspera]], [[Chara braunii]] var. schweinitzii, [[Chara sejuncta]]
   - Diploid (N=28): [[Chara contraria]], [[Chara delicatula]], [[Chara globularis]], [[Chara vulgaris]], [[Chara zeylanica]] var. elegans
   - Strong correlation: dioecious species are haploid (14), monoecious species are diploid (28)

2. **[[Chara]] Ecorticatae**: Uniformly N=14
   - [[Chara braunii]] group, [[Chara australis]], [[Chara brittonii]]
   - Both monoecious and dioecious species have 14 chromosomes
   - No polyploidy beyond the 14-chromosome level detected

3. **[[Nitelleae]]**: 6, 9, 12, 18 series
   - N=12: [[Nitella axillaris]], [[Nitella clavata]], [[Nitella microcarpa]] ssp. megacarpa, [[Nitella opaca]]
   - N=18: [[Nitella acuminata]] varieties, [[Nitella flexilis]], [[Nitella gracilis]], [[Nitella intermedia]]
   - N=9: Some [[Nitella]] species (possibly separate 9:18 series or indication of base-3 multiple)
   - [[Tolypella]]: N=12 ([[Tolypella intertexta]], [[Tolypella prolifera]])

**Protochara Findings:**
Australian collections of [[Protochara]] species consistently show N=14, agreeing with previous counts (Macdonald and Hotchkiss, 1955).

**Lamprothamnium Counts:**
[[Lamprothamnium macropogon]] shows N=28 (three collections) with one anomalous count of 30+.

**Methodological Advances:**

Successful fixation protocol established using 3:1 absolute alcohol:glacial acetic acid or Carnoy's solution (100 parts absolute alcohol:16 parts glacial acetic acid:50 parts chloroform) for at least 12 hours, then transfer to 70% alcohol with refrigeration. Australian and Mexican materials showed perfect preservation using this method.

Aceto-orcein squash technique refined: antheridia teased into stain drops, warmed, squashed under coverslip, made permanent by transfer through alcohol series to Euparal mounting medium.

**Cytological Observations:**

- Evidence of somatic chromosome pairing observed in some preparations, suggesting homologous chromosomes may associate more than randomly
- Endopolyploidy documented in [[Chara leptopitys]]: larger antheridia contained cells with 35, 42+ chromosomes in paired groups alongside normal 14-chromosome cells
- Distinct "typical" cytological pictures distinguish [[Nitella]] from [[Chara]], and within [[Chara]], species like [[Chara braunii]] differ markedly from [[Chara vulgaris]] and [[Chara contraria]]
- Interphase nuclei show 2-3 large chromocenters and several smaller ones

**Taxonomic Implications:**

The research supports major taxonomic revisions:

1. **Separation of Ecorticatae from Corticatae**: The lack of correlation between polyploidy and sex expression in ecorticate species (all N=14 whether monoecious or dioecious) versus strong correlation in corticate species (dioecious=14, monoecious=28) indicates fundamentally different genetic mechanisms controlling sex determination. This suggests ecorticate species may warrant generic separation from corticate [[Chara]].

2. **Status of [[Chara braunii]]**: While Gillet (1959) proposed transferring this species to genus [[Charopsis]] based on a chromosome count of 12 and placement in [[Nitelleae]], the present study's consistent count of 14 contradicts affinity with [[Nitelleae]] (base 6 or 9). However, the cytological evidence regarding sex-ploidy correlation supports generic distinction from corticate [[Chara]], though not placement in [[Nitelleae]].

3. **Diminished importance of stipulode characters**: Since monoecious diplostephanous [[Chara brittonii]] (N=14) groups cytologically with haplostephanous ecorticate species, stipulode number appears less taxonomically significant than cortication and ploidy-sex correlations.
