# Check-list of the Fresh-water Fishes of Oregon and Washington

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1929 taxonomic check-list of freshwater fishes recorded in [[Oregon]] and [[Washington]] and adjacent regions. It was compiled by [[Leonard P. Schultz]] from published literature, supplemented by a few unpublished records of introduced species observed by D. R. Crawford and the author. The list was reviewed with input from [[Carl L. Hubbs]]. It is numbered 1 to 98 across 19 families (Roman numerals I–XIX) and flags introduced species. The author states the list is provisional and expects future work to remove some taxa and add others.

## Background & Description

- **Publication:** Publications in Fisheries, University of Washington, College of Fisheries, Vol. 2, No. 4, pp. 43–50, January 1929. Published by the University of Washington Press, Seattle.
- **Scope:** Freshwater fishes of Oregon and Washington, including anadromous species (e.g., [[Eulachon]], [[Steelhead]], [[Pacific Salmon]]) and a marine lamprey recorded in the region.
- **Method:** Compilation of published literature plus unpublished introduction records. Taxonomic input came from [[Carl L. Hubbs]].
- **Taxonomic notes:** The list includes many trinomial forms and species the author flags as uncertain. Examples include:
  - [[Marine Lamprey]] (*Entosphenus tridentatus*), which may split into two subspecies (*E. t. tridentatus* north of the [[Columbia River]]; *E. t. ciliatus* south of it, to southern California).
  - [[Sockeye Salmon]] landlocked form, noted as possibly *Oncorhynchus nerka kennerlyi* ("little red fish"), abundant in [[Lake Washington]].
  - Several trout forms "that may prove not to be valid species," including *Salmo eremogenes* (taken only in [[Crab Creek]] by Evermann and Nichols) and *Salmo clarkii jordani*/*declivifrons*/*bathoecetor*, the latter from the [[Olympic Peninsula]].
  - Kendall (1920) argued that the rainbow and steelhead should be treated as a single species, *Salmo gairdnerii*. The document notes rainbow vertebrae = 63 and steelhead = 60.
  - Hubbs considered *Leuciscus cooperi* and *Leuciscus siuslawa* "at most, two forms of *R. balteatus*."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native and introduced species:** The list explicitly marks introduced species, including:
  - *Alosa sapidissima* (shad)
  - *Cristivomer namaycush* (Great Lakes trout / Mackinaw trout)
  - *Salvelinus fontinalis* (eastern brook trout)
  - *Cyprinus carpio* (common carp)
  - *Carassius auratus* (goldfish)
  - *Tinca vulgaris* (green tench)
  - *Ameiurus nebulosus* (bullhead)
  - *Ictalurus punctatus* (channel catfish)
  - *Esox vermiculatus* (little pickerel), introduced into Crab Creek, Washington
  - *Perca flavescens* (yellow perch)
  - *Roccus saxatilis* (striped bass)
  - *Aplites salmoides* (largemouth black bass)
  - *Micropterus dolomieu* (smallmouth black bass)
  - *Pomoxis annularis* (white crappie)
  - *Eupomotis gibbosus* (pumpkinseed sunfish)
  - Salmonid plantings: *Salmo shasta* was "planted in this region by many of the state hatcheries."
- **Early planting record:** A footnote reports that 375,000 fry were planted in [[Lake Washington]], Seattle, in February 1889, but they probably did not establish, as no definite record of their occurrence there at that time could be found.
- **Anadromy and life history:** The document notes that the steelhead and coastal rainbow return to the sea. The young of one listed species (*Lota maculosa*, "Ling") are described as found in both fresh and salt water.
- **Geographic distribution:** Several taxa are restricted to particular drainages, including the [[Klamath Basin]] (*Catostomus rimiculus*, *C. snyderi*, *Chasmistes copei*, *C. stomias*), the [[Snake River]] (*Catostomus pocatello*), the [[Pit River]] (*Mylopharodon conocephalus*, *Ptychocheilus grandis*), and [[Lake Chelan]] (cited in the bibliography).

## Key Findings

1. **Checklist scope:** 98 numbered entries across 19 families. Families include lampreys, sturgeons, shad, smelts, whitefishes, salmonids, suckers, minnows, catfishes, pikes, percopsids, perches, temperate basses, surfperches, sunfishes, codfishes, sculpins, flatfishes, and sticklebacks.
2. **Salmonids dominate the taxonomic detail:** The Salmonidae family (Family VI) is divided into steelhead/rainbow, cutthroat, and special-region trout series, and it carries the most notes on taxonomic uncertainty.
3. **Introductions:** At least 16 introduced taxa are flagged, showing that many non-native fishes (carp, goldfish, centrarchids, bullheads, striped bass) were established in the region by 1929.
4. **Lake Washington planting:** 375,000 fry were planted in February 1889 without a confirmed establishment record.
5. **Taxonomic revisions pending:** The author explicitly states that several names (e.g., the trout "from special regions," the rainbow/steelhead complex, the Oregon chub complex, and the Cottus sculpins) need further study and revision. The author expects future work to change the list.
6. **Information sources:** The list relies on the historical literature listed in the bibliography (Bean, Evermann, Gilbert, Hubbs, Jordan, Kendall, Snyder, and others, 1881–1928), with additional input from Hubbs and Crawford.
