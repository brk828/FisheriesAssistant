# Topminnow and Pupfish Survey - Southwestern Arizona January 1991

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This intra-office memo documents a survey of 15 [[Gila Topminnow]] (POOCOC - *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] sites in Southwestern Arizona conducted 5-8 January 1991. The survey assessed current fish populations, evaluated potential reintroduction sites, and documented habitat conditions across multiple spring systems, tanks, and creek locations. Key sites included the [[National Audubon Society Research Ranch]] near Elgin, [[Monkey Spring]], [[Sonoita Creek]], [[Roper Lake State Park]], [[Watson Wash]], [[Big Spring]], and three hot springs on the [[San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation]].

## Background & Description

The survey was conducted by Assistant Native Fish Biologist Matthew R. Brown as part of ongoing monitoring efforts for two imperiled native fish species in Arizona. [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, abbreviated POOCOC in field notes) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) are native desert fishes requiring specialized aquatic habitats in the arid Southwest. The survey evaluated existing populations and assessed potential sites for species reintroduction, with particular attention to the [[San Pedro River]] drainage where stocking decisions await completion of headwater surveys.

Other fish species documented included GAAF (likely [[Gambusia affinis]] - mosquitofish), AGCH (likely [[Agosia chrysogaster]] - longfin dace), RHOS (likely *Rhinichthys* species), guppies, [[Red Shiner]]s, and bullheads. Amphibian presence, particularly leopard frogs and bullfrogs, was also noted at multiple sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The surveyed habitats represent critical refugia for desert-adapted fishes in an arid landscape. Sites included natural springs, constructed tanks (water storage structures), thermal springs, and creek systems. Habitat permanence emerged as a critical factor, with some tanks experiencing complete drying (e.g., [[O'Donnell Canyon]]) while thermal springs maintained year-round flow.

Non-native species pose significant threats to native fish populations. [[Red Shiner]]s and guppies were found at [[Watson Wash]], and the potential presence of bass at [[Post Canyon]] tank was noted as a concern for reintroduction efforts. The presence of bullfrogs (identified by croaking behavior) at [[Watson Wash]] represents another non-native threat, while native lowland leopard frogs were documented at [[Big Spring]].

Habitat disturbance was evident at several sites. [[Big Spring]] experienced flash flood damage with extensive siltation. [[Middle Spring]] on the [[San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation]] dried completely in the previous year. Winter die-off was observed at [[Monkey Spring]], a thermal spring where such mortality was unexpected.

Infrastructure issues affected conservation efforts. The barrier at [[Bylas Springs]] continued to leak water near a salt cedar stump and through the west side, with water audible running underground approximately 2 m from the barrier structure.

## Key Findings

**Site-Specific Results:**

- **[[Finley Tank]]** ([[National Audubon Society Research Ranch]]): Large tank (20 m × 20 m) contained abundant [[Desert Pupfish]]. Renovation plans discussed with Director Gene Knoder, but stocking delayed pending [[San Pedro River]] headwater surveys for native fish presence.

- **[[Post Canyon]] Tank**: 40 m × 10 m tank with permanent water and cleaned silt trap appeared suitable for [[Gila Topminnow]] reintroduction, though potential bass presence remains a concern.

- **[[O'Donnell Canyon]] Tank**: 50 m × 10 m tank contained no fish and deemed unsuitable for reintroduction due to drying.

- **[[Monkey Spring]]**: Only a few POOCOC sampled in siphon and headspring. Unexpected amount of winter die-off observed in this thermal spring system.

- **[[Sonoita Creek]]**: Three separate sites sampled, none contained POOCOC. GAAF, AGCH, and RHOS present in moderate numbers. Plans to resample [[Nature Conservancy]] preserve site in spring.

- **[[Cottonwood Spring]]**: POOCOC present in moderate numbers. Leopard frogs sighted nearby.

- **[[Roper Lake State Park]]**: Both Upper and Lower Headquarters Ponds contained abundant [[Gila Topminnow]]. Two bullheads captured in Upper Pond. Small tadpoles captured and leopard frogs sighted. No pupfish observed.

- **[[Watson Wash]]** (thermal spring, BLM land near Thatcher): POOCOC present along with non-native guppies and [[Red Shiner]]s. Bullfrogs identified by croaking behavior.

- **[[Big Spring]]** (BLM land near Pima): Flash flood damage from previous summer caused extensive siltation above and below dam. Only one POOCOC captured. Lowland leopard frogs abundant in pool 15 m above dam.

- **[[San Carlos Apache Indian Reservation]] Hot Springs** (near Bylas): [[Middle Spring]] dried completely in previous year with no fish present (water depth < 1 cm except in hoof prints). Both GAAF and POOCOC caught at [[Bylas Springs]] and [[Salt Creek]]. POOCOC comprised 6.7% and 3.6% of fish fauna, respectively. Barrier at [[Bylas Springs]] continues to leak near salt cedar stump location and through west side, with underground water flow audible 2 m from barrier.

**Management Implications:**

Stocking decisions for [[San Pedro River]] drainage sites (including [[Finley Tank]]) postponed pending completion of headwater surveys to determine presence of existing native fish populations. This conservative approach follows recommendations from Sally Stefferud and aligns with [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] protocols discussed at the December 1990 meeting led by Dr. Minckley.
