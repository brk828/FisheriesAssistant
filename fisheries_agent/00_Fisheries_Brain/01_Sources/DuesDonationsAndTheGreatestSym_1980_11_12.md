# Dues, Donations, and the Greatest Symposium Ever Held

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes #conservation #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative communication from the **[[Desert Fishes Council]]**, dated November 12, 1980, authored by Executive Secretary E. P. Pister. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research data, the letter reports on the outcomes of the Council's 1980 annual symposium held in **Monterrey, Nuevo León, Mexico**, hosted by Dr. Salvador Contreras B. and colleagues from the **University of Nuevo León**. The letter emphasizes the symposium's scientific and international significance, financial and administrative matters (dues increase proposal), and publication status of Council proceedings. It serves as an important historical record of the institutional development of desert fish conservation science and cross-border (U.S.–Mexico) scientific collaboration in ichthyology.

## Background & Description

No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic species-level data are presented in this source. The document is administrative and organizational in nature and does not describe specific fish species, their physical traits, or diagnostic features.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no specific ecological data (habitat parameters, water chemistry, population data) are given, the document references significant desert aquatic habitats central to the Council's conservation mission:

- **[[Cuatrociénegas]]**, Coahuila, Mexico — described as a site of a "magnificent field trip," this basin is renowned among ichthyologists for its unique endemic desert fish fauna and relict aquatic ecosystems, historically a focal point of **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** research and conservation efforts.
- The broader geographic scope of attendees (Nuevo León to Michigan and Maine) reflects the wide biogeographic interest in **[[Desert Fish Conservation]]** spanning arid-land aquatic ecosystems of the southwestern U.S. and northern Mexico.
- Institutional stakeholders mentioned include the U.S. Department of the Interior (four agencies, likely including **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]**), Mexico's Departamento de Pesca, state Fish and Game Departments (Colorado, California), the **[[Environmental Protection Agency]]**, **[[California Academy of Sciences]]**, and **[[The Nature Conservancy]]** — indicating an interdisciplinary, multi-agency approach to desert aquatic habitat conservation.

## Key Findings

- The 1980 **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** symposium in Monterrey was described as the organizational highlight of its twelve-year history, marking a significant advance in binational (U.S.-Mexico) collaboration in fishery science.
- Over **100 attendees** represented **15 universities** (U.S. and Mexico), multiple federal and state agencies, NGOs, and private consultants and citizens.
- **32 technical presentations** were delivered, with **12 contributed by students** from the University of Nuevo León, reflecting strong Mexican academic engagement in desert fish research.
- Dr. Salvador Contreras B., Dean of the Graduate School of Biological Sciences at the University of Nuevo León, was elected **president-elect** of the Council, symbolizing deepening U.S.-Mexico institutional ties in desert fish conservation.
- A field excursion to **[[Cuatrociénegas]]**, Coahuila — a globally significant desert wetland ecosystem — was a highlighted event, underscoring the Council's continued focus on this critical habitat for endemic desert fish taxa.
- The Council faced financial constraints affecting publication of the **1979 and 1980 Proceedings** and a **1971–1977 compendium**, prompting a proposed dues increase from historical levels to **$10.00** (unchanged since the Council's founding 12 years prior, i.e., since approximately 1968).
- The letter reflects the operational and funding challenges faced by grassroots scientific and conservation societies dedicated to imperiled desert aquatic fauna.
