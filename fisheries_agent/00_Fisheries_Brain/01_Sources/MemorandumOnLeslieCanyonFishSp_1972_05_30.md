## Executive Summary

This 1972 internal memorandum from the Arizona Game and Fish Department outlines the fish fauna and management/stocking plans for [[Leslie Canyon]], a drainage in southeastern Arizona containing a single known area of permanent water — a spring located at a proposed dam site. The document records the historical introduction and subsequent extirpation of the [[Yaqui Sucker]], describes the two extant native/established species, and details planned introductions of sport and forage fishes contingent on construction of a lake impoundment. This memo is significant as a historical record of desert stream fish management and the deliberate manipulation of native [[Desert Fish Assemblages]] in the arid Southwest.

## Background & Description

The memo does not provide detailed morphological descriptions, as it is a management/stocking communication rather than a taxonomic study. Species are identified only by common and scientific (binomial) names:

- **[[Yaqui Sucker]]** (*Catostomus bernardini*) — a catostomid, described as a "peripheral species."
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — a small native cyprinid.
- **Mosquitofish** (*Gambusia* sp.) — small live-bearing poeciliid, likely introduced for mosquito control.
- **[[Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides*) — planned centrarchid sport fish introduction.
- **[[Channel Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — planned ictalurid introduction.
- **[[Redear Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis microlophus*) and **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*) — planned centrarchid introductions.
- **[[Threadfin Shad]]** (*Dorosoma petenense*) — proposed forage fish, contingent on future need.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Salmo gairdneri*) — proposed for seasonal (winter) stocking, conditions permitting.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context**: The Leslie Canyon drainage contains only one documented area of permanent water — a spring near a proposed dam site — indicating an otherwise intermittent, arid-adapted stream system typical of southeastern [[Arizona]] desert drainages.
- **Yaqui Sucker**: Described as a "peripheral species" that formerly inhabited waters of southeastern Arizona at the edge of its native range (its core range being in the Río Yaqui basin of Mexico). It was artificially introduced into the Leslie Canyon spring, persisted for a few years, then disappeared — mirroring its broader disappearance from Arizona waters generally, suggesting range contraction or extirpation pressures (e.g., habitat limitation, competition, or drought) affecting [[Peripheral Fish Populations]].
- **Longfin Dace**: Currently one of only two species persisting in the spring; a hardy native cyprinid well-suited to intermittent desert stream conditions.
- **Mosquitofish**: Occupies the spring habitat, likely serving a vector-control ecological role, common in stagnant/spring-fed desert waters.
- **Planned lake community**: If impounded, the memo outlines an intent to establish a classic warmwater sport-fishery assemblage (bass–catfish–sunfish), a common management template for constructed reservoirs, which would substantially alter the trophic structure of the native spring ecosystem.
- **Forage fish consideration**: Threadfin shad proposed as a future forage base to support predatory sport fish (largemouth bass) if the lake is built, reflecting standard reservoir fisheries management practice of the era.
- **Seasonal cold-water use**: Rainbow trout stocking is proposed only for winter months "if conditions are favorable," implying seasonal thermal tolerance limits of a coldwater salmonid in an otherwise warm desert-adapted system.

## Key Findings

- Leslie Canyon drainage has only **one known permanent water source** — a spring at the dam site.
- The **Yaqui sucker** was previously introduced into the Leslie Canyon spring but failed to establish a lasting population and has since disappeared, consistent with its broader disappearance from Arizona.
- Only **two fish species** currently inhabit the Leslie Canyon spring: the **longfin dace** and a **mosquitofish** species.
- Department plans, contingent on construction of a lake at Leslie Canyon, call for introduction of **largemouth bass, channel catfish, redear sunfish, and green sunfish**.
- **Threadfin shad** may be introduced later as a forage fish if deemed necessary.
- **Rainbow trout** stocking during winter months is under consideration, dependent on favorable water conditions.
- The memo illustrates a broader historical pattern of Arizona Game and Fish Department policy favoring introduction of sport and forage fishes into desert drainages, often at the expense of native and peripheral species such as the [[Yaqui Sucker]].
