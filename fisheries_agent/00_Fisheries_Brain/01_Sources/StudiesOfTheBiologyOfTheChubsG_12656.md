# Studies of the Biology of the Chubs (Genus *Nocomis*, Family Cyprinidae) of Northeastern United States

## Executive Summary
This 1946 Cornell doctoral thesis by Ernest Albert Lachner (under Professor E.C. Raney) examines the taxonomy, distribution, life history, growth, and food habits of three freshwater cyprinid species: [[Nocomis biguttatus]] (Hornyhead Chub), [[Nocomis micropogon]] (River Chub), and [[Nocomis leptocephalus]] (Carolina Chub). The study is based on morphometric analysis of adult specimens, scale-based age determination of 1,709 individuals, and stomach content analysis of 607 specimens. Findings establish diagnostic morphological keys, document pronounced sexual dimorphism and nuptial tuberculation patterns, quantify differential growth rates between sexes and species, and characterize a shared benthic invertebrate-dominated diet.

## Background & Description
- **Pharyngeal teeth**: Primary diagnostic character separating species groups.
  - *N. biguttatus*: teeth in two rows (I,4-4,I or I,4-4,0).
  - *N. micropogon* and *N. leptocephalus*: teeth in a single row (4-4).
- **Caudal spot**: Intense, large, round, and distinct in *N. biguttatus*; small/round or diffuse/indistinct in *N. micropogon* and *N. leptocephalus*.
- **Predorsal scales**: 19–27 (avg. 22.5) in *biguttatus*; 16–23 (avg. ≤20) in the single-row-toothed species.
- **Circumferential scales**: 31–35 (avg. ~33) in *biguttatus*; 27–32 (avg. ≤31) in others.
- **Snout**: Short and rounded in *biguttatus* (>9x in standard length); in *micropogon* longer and pointed (~8x SL); in *leptocephalus* shorter and blunt (>9x SL).
- **Dorsal fin insertion**: More anterior in *micropogon* (reaches nostril when measured forward from origin) vs. more posterior in *leptocephalus* (reaches eye).
- **Body/head depth**: *Leptocephalus* has shorter, deeper head and deeper body with more decurved lateral line compared to *micropogon*.
- **Coloration**: 
  - *Biguttatus*: light vermilion postocular spot; orange dorsal/anal fins; red caudal fin in young; mid-dorsal light line and intense dark lateral band in nuptial males.
  - *Micropogon/leptocephalus*: no postocular spot; olive-yellow dorsal/anal fins; amber caudal fin in young.
- **Nuptial tuberculation** (sexual dimorphism):
  - *Biguttatus*: >45 antrorse tubercles from internasal area to occiput; no occipital/interorbital swellings.
  - *Micropogon/leptocephalus*: <40 erect tubercles from internasal to interorbital area; occipital and interorbital swellings present.
  - *Micropogon*: tubercles concentrated on internasal area, sides/front of snout.
  - *Leptocephalus*: tubercles concentrated between internasal and interorbital area, approaching occiput.
- **Maximum recorded standard lengths**:
  - *N. micropogon*: male 188 mm, female 127 mm.
  - *N. biguttatus*: male 124 mm, female 118 mm.
  - *N. leptocephalus*: male 145 mm, female 102 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Rivers and streams of eastern United States, typically associated with riffle habitats; *N. leptocephalus* especially common in sandy Piedmont streams of Virginia and North Carolina.
- **Distribution**:
  - *N. biguttatus*: Western limits from northern Arkansas/northeastern Oklahoma to Colorado/Wyoming and eastern North Dakota (Red River); eastward to western Lake Ontario basin, Hudson River drainage (NY), northern Ohio River system; north to Lake Superior and Green Bay drainages (WI) and Straits of Mackinac.
  - *N. micropogon*: Wabash River system and Lower Peninsula of Michigan to Lake Ontario basin (west of Rochester); southward on Atlantic slope from Susquehanna (NY) to James River (VA); upper James River system; western Appalachian uplands to Tennessee River tributaries of Georgia/Alabama.
  - *N. leptocephalus*: Atlantic slope from James River, Virginia, southward; upper New River system of western Virginia.
  - Ranges of *biguttatus*/*micropogon* and *leptocephalus*/*micropogon* overlap significantly (James and New River systems).
- **Abundance**: In Lake Ontario drainage streams (NY), *micropogon*, *biguttatus*, and [[Notropis cornutus chrysocephalus]] (central common shiner) co-occur as the most abundant fish species.
- **Nesting behavior**: Males construct conspicuous dome-shaped pebble nests above riffles during late April–May; eggs deposited among pebbles for scavenger protection. Nests serve as communal spawning sites for multiple other fish species, indicating a keystone ecological role in stream reproductive ecology.
- **Trophic ecology**: Primarily benthic invertivores; diet dominated by aquatic Insecta, Crustacea, and Mollusca, with incidental plant material (filamentous algae, chiefly [[Cladophora]], and vascular plants) likely ingested alongside animal prey.
  - Key prey taxa: *Simulium* (black-fly larvae), *Chironomus* (midge-fly larvae), *Heliocopsyche* (caddis-fly larvae), non-burrowing Ephemeroptera (mayflies), and *Cambarus* (crayfish).
  - Ostracods and Cladocera consumed only by young-of-year during July.
  - Diet composition nearly identical between *micropogon* and *biguttatus*, with no significant seasonal dietary shift across summer months.

## Key Findings
- **Taxonomic key**: A dichotomous key distinguishing *N. biguttatus*, *N. micropogon*, and *N. leptocephalus* was developed based on pharyngeal teeth, caudal spot, scale counts, snout proportions, and cranial measurements.
- **Sexual maturity**: All three species mature and spawn at age 3; faster-growing *biguttatus* of both sexes mature at age 2. Males mature at greater length than females in all species.
- **Sex ratios** (male:female): *biguttatus* 1.0:1.3; *micropogon* 1.0:0.9; *leptocephalus* 1.0:2.3.
- **Age determination**: Based on scale analysis of 1,709 specimens (NY, PA, VA, NC). Annuli form with growth resumption in late April/May; circuli more widely spaced during rapid growth (June–July) vs. slower growth (August–September).
- **Longevity**: *N. micropogon* attained greatest age (one male at 5 summers, n=454). *N. leptocephalus*: one male and 12 females reached 4 summers (n=819). *N. biguttatus*: only one female reached 4 summers (n=401). Four-summer age group never exceeded 1.5% of population in any species; females survived longer than males in each species.
- **Growth rates**: Males grew significantly faster than females by end of first year (statistically significant) across all three species. Growth increment slightly greater in June–July than August–September.
  - *N. micropogon* (Sandy Creek, NY): males reached 63/103/138 mm SL; females 57/94/111 mm SL at end of years 1/2/3.
  - *N. biguttatus*: males 56/90/123 mm SL; females 49/86/106 mm SL.
  - *N. leptocephalus* (James River, VA): males 47/77/116 mm SL; females 40/62/86 mm SL.
  - Annual growth increment greatest in year 1 for *micropogon* and *biguttatus*, declining sharply thereafter; *leptocephalus* showed roughly equal annual increments across three years.
- **Diet study**: Based on 607 stomachs (470 with food content) from Sandy Creek, NY (July–Sept 1939, June 1940). Diet almost entirely benthic invertebrates, with high dietary overlap between *micropogon* and *biguttatus*.
- **Reproductive ecology**: Pebble nest construction by males represents a significant nest-associate spawning strategy, facilitating reproduction of multiple co-occurring cyprinid species.
