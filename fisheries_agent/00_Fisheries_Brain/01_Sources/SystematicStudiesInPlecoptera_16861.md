# Systematic Studies in Plecoptera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #plecoptera #nemouridae #perlodidae

Note on scope: the source is an entomological monograph on stoneflies (order Plecoptera), an aquatic insect order, not fishes. The tags above follow the system template; the taxon tag is #plecoptera.

## Executive Summary

William E. Ricker's monograph *Systematic Studies in Plecoptera* (Indiana University Publications, Science Series No. 18) revises North American stoneflies in three parts: the genus [[Nemoura]] (Part I), the family [[Perlodidae]] (Part II), and miscellaneous revisions (Part III). It builds on earlier work by [[Needham and Claassen Monograph]] (1925), [[Claassen Catalog]] (1940), and [[T. H. Frison]]. The paper erects several new subgenera and species, synonymizes multiple names, and proposes a revised phylogeny of the order, including a new genus, *Utaperla*. Distribution records are summarized, with detailed locality data given only for new species.

## Background & Description

- **Context:** Stonefly study in North America slowed after [[Hagen]] and Provancher, revived with Nathan Banks and J. G. Needham, and was advanced by Peter W. Claassen (monograph 1925; nymph volume 1931; catalog 1940), T. H. Frison (Illinois fauna, 1942 bulletin), and John F. Hanson.
- **Materials:** Ricker drew on the Illinois Natural History Survey collection and Frison's notes, plus loans from many museums (e.g., [[Canadian National Collection]], [[Cornell University Collection]], [[Quebec Provincial Museum]], [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], [[United States National Museum]]).
- **Methods:** Diagnostic characters include wing venation, wing pigmentation, gill presence and position, cerci shape, male subgenital and subanal structures, supra-anal process form, 10th tergite form, female 7th and 8th sternites, and nymphal spination.
- **Size ranges given:** Nemoura males about 4–15 mm and females 5–17 mm (body-to-wing-tip).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Several species are tied to specific stream types. [[Amphinemura varshava]] occurs in cool spring creeks, whereas [[Amphinemura delosa]] frequents warmer streams but avoids the largest rivers.
- In [[Megarcys]] (Arcynopteryx), Ricker's observations at Mt. Rainier and near Cultus Lake, B.C., indicate habitat partitioning: *yosemite* in the highest reaches of glacier-fed creeks with silt load and sparse vegetation (6,000–7,000 ft); *irregularis* in lower reaches from tree line to sea level; *subtruncata* in small-to-medium creeks up to near tree line.
- Gills and cerci are linked to habitat and taxonomy, and many species are described as "rather uncommon," with *Ostrocerca dimicki* noted as possibly the exception among the subgenus.
- Distribution is broadly cordilleran in the west and eastern in the east. Several Nemoura subgenera are absent from America, notably *Protonemura* and *Nemurella*.

## Key Findings

**Phylogeny and higher classification**
- A revised phylogenetic tree (Figure 1) places [[Peltoperla]] zipha as an intermediate form with a well-developed cowl but an entire 10th tergite.
- *Calliperla* is an intermediate between Isogeninae and Isoperlinae, and *Isoperla clio* retains traces of isogenine ancestry.
- The new genus *Utaperla* (Paraperlinae) fills a gap by having a head capsule only slightly elongated, which removes the need for a hypothetical ancestral group.

**Nemoura (Part I)**
- Four European subgenera are recognized; two occur in America: *Amphinemura* (with *N. venosa* Banks, four cervical gills) and *Nemoura sensu stricto*.
- Eleven morphological character systems are used for subgenera and species groups.
- New species: *Amphinemura delosa*, *A. linda*, *A. mockfordi*, *A. varshava*; *Lednia tumana* (subgenus Lednia, new, from Glacier National Park, Montana); *Nemoura (Malenka)* tina.
- Subgenera established as new include *Lednia*, *Malenka*, *Ostrocerca*, *Podmosta*, *Prostoia*, *Shipsa*, *Soyedina*, and *Visoka*. Nemoura (N.) normani is described from Mackenzie and Alaska.
- Synonymies: *N. venosa* Banks is restricted (the eastern complex was previously confused with it), *N. nigritta* Provancher is retained, *N. stylata* Banks is synonymized with venosa, and *N. divergens* is synonymized with *N. similis*.
- Name status: *Nemoura incerta* Provancher is a nomen nudum.

**Perlodidae (Part II)**
- Subfamily Isogeninae is treated as primitive, with Perlodinae and Isoperlinae more specialized.
- Genus *Arcynopteryx* (North American Perlodes of Needham and Claassen) is split from true *Perlodes*. Megarcys (with *A. signata*, *A. irregularis*, *A. subtruncata*, *A. watertoni* new, *A. yosemite*) and Frisonia are among its subgenera.
- Genus *Isogenus* contains many subgenera; Ricker notes an "ancestral" Isogenus would combine submental gills, lacking a 7th-sternite lobe, lateral stylets, and serrate mandibles. *Setvena* is considered the closest existing predecessor of that ancestor.
- Species described as new include *Isogenus krumholzi*, *I. tostonus*, and *I. hansoni*, with new subgenus *Tadamus* for Japanese species.
- Isogenoides species recognized include *I. varians*, *I. hansoni*, *I. doratus*, *I. elongatus*, *I. frontalis* (two subspecies), and *I. olivaceus*.

**Nomenclature and synonymy highlights**
- *Isogenus alameda*, *I. innubilus*, *I. nubes*, *I. phaleratus*, and *I. sorptus* are listed among North American species of uncertain relationship; *I. nubes* is considered unrecognizable because its type is lost.
- Diura is recognized with *D. bicaudata* (Linné) and *D. nanseni* (Kempny) as the main Holarctic taxa, with American records including *D. washingtoniana*.

**Scope of the paper**
- The bibliography is not reproduced; Claassen (1940) is the reference for synonymy through 1939.
- Exotic (Asian and European) species are listed in summary form only.
