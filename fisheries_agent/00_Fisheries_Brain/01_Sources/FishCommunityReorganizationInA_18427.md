# Fish Community Reorganization in a Perturbed Stream, The Virgin River, Utah-Arizona-Nevada

#ichthyology #stream-ecology #community-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is a research proposal submitted by [[Arizona State University]] to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 2 on December 5, 1988. The proposal, titled "Fish Community Reorganization in a Perturbed Stream, The Virgin River, Utah-Arizona-Nevada," seeks funding to study fish community dynamics in the [[Virgin River]] system spanning Utah, Arizona, and Nevada. The project directors are Drs. W.L. Minckley, Michael E. Douglas, Thomas E. Dowling, and Paul C. Marsh from the Department of Zoology and Center for Environmental Studies. The proposal emphasizes the timely nature and importance of this work for both scientific understanding and management of critical endangered species resources.

## Background & Description

The [[Virgin River]] represents a perturbed stream system requiring investigation of fish community reorganization processes. The proposal was submitted as an unsolicited request for support to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director Michael Spear in Albuquerque, New Mexico. The research team consists of four principal investigators from Arizona State University with expertise in zoology and environmental studies. The proposal routing form indicates this is a new project (not a continuation or renewal) with a proposed start date of January 1989 and end date of December 1993, representing a multi-year research effort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Virgin River]] system spans three states (Utah, Arizona, and Nevada) and represents a perturbed stream environment requiring study of community reorganization dynamics. The proposal emphasizes the management implications for endangered species resources in this system. The research involves biosafety considerations (chemicals/recombinant DNA) and radioactive materials, suggesting comprehensive ecological and genetic methodologies. The project requires both office and laboratory facilities (wet/dry) at Arizona State University, with space allocation split between the Department of Zoology (75%) and the Center for Environmental Studies (25%).

## Key Findings

The proposal requests total funding of $1,357,727 over the multi-year project period. The budget structure includes direct costs of $1,005,000 and indirect costs of $352,727, with an indirect cost rate of 35% applied. The project is structured as a grant rather than a contract or subcontract. Four copies of the proposal were submitted (original plus three copies) to the funding agency. The proposal emphasizes coordination potential with other regions within the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service due to the project's broad significance. The research team represents multiple academic ranks including Professor, Academic Professional, Assistant Professor, and Associate Research Professor, indicating a comprehensive expertise base for investigating fish community dynamics in perturbed stream systems.
