# Spinal Deformity in Threadfin Shad from Concrete-Lined Canals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This short research note reports an unusually high incidence of spinal deformity (lordosis) in [[Threadfin Shad]] (*[[Dorosoma Petenense]]*), a [[Clupeidae]] species introduced to Arizona and California in the 1950s. Deformities were found at 21.3% to 40.7% of shad from three [[Concrete-Lined Canals]], far above the rates in [[Earthen Canals]] and [[Apache Lake]]. The authors conclude that canal lining is unlikely to be the cause, because the shad were older than the canal's period of use and the concrete canals in Maricopa County are dewatered each year, forcing fish to re-immigrate from upstream sources.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** Threadfin shad (*Dorosoma petenense*) has spread widely through warmwater habitats in [[Arizona]] and [[California]]. Prior reports have not described spinal deformities in this species.
- **Context:** Spinal anomalies are known in other clupeids and fishes, but frequencies in wild populations are generally a few percent or less.
- **Study question:** Whether shad from concrete-lined canals show a higher frequency of spinal deformity than fish from earthen canals and a reservoir.
- **Authors:** Paul C. Marsh (Center for Environmental Studies, Arizona State University) and William E. Rinne (Bureau of Reclamation, Lower Colorado Region).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitats sampled:** Three concrete-lined canals (Coachella Canal, CA; South and Tempe canals, AZ), earthen canals (Coachella and Eastern canals), and [[Apache Lake]] reservoir.
- **Canal management:** Concrete canals in Maricopa County are dewatered annually, and shad rarely survive these periods. The Coachella Canal was first used in November 1980, so its 1981 shad had to come from confluent waters.
- **Recruitment:** Most fish in the concrete canals appear to immigrate each year from upstream rivers or their impoundments.
- **Comparative rates in wild fishes:** Wild clupeids typically show <1.0% deformity, including [[Atlantic Menhaden]], [[American Shad]], and [[Blueback Herring]]. Salmonids reach about 10%, and young [[Roundtail Chub]] up to 16%. Laboratory or hatchery stocks have exceeded 70%.
- **Developmental timing:** Causal factors generally act on eggs or early embryos.

## Key Findings

**Sampling and methods**
- Shad were collected by hand, dip net, and seine from the Coachella Canal (CA, 2 Nov 1981), and the South and Tempe canals (AZ, 10-11 Oct 1981).
- Comparison samples came from the earthen Coachella Canal, the earthen Eastern Canal (3 Nov 1964), and Apache Lake (12 May 1967).
- Specimens were measured for total length (TL, nearest mm), weighed (nearest 0.01 g), examined externally for spinal anomalies, and radiographed to check vertebrae.
- Collections were compared using percent deformed, [[Condition Factor]] (K), and the [[Length-Weight Relationship]].

**Deformity rates**
- Concrete-lined canals had the highest rates: Tempe 21.3% (132 deformed), South 26.7% (92 deformed), and Coachella 40.7% (88 deformed).
- Earthen Coachella Canal had 6.1% deformed (13 of 213 fish). The Eastern Canal had no deformed fish among 98 examined. Apache Lake had a low rate, which the authors describe only as a minor incidence.
- All deformed individuals showed mild to relatively severe lordosis (dorsoventral spinal curvature). Figure 1 illustrates the range from severe to normal curvature in Coachella Canal fish (122-131 mm TL).

**Statistical comparisons**
- Within each population, there were no significant differences in mean TL, weight, K, or length-weight relationships between normal and deformed fish.
- Differences among populations could not be attributed to the incidence of deformity.

**Interpretation and conclusions**
- Concrete lining may have played some role in the higher incidence, but the authors argue that it cannot be the main cause:
  - Nearly all shad from the concrete Coachella Canal were older than 2.0 years, based on length frequencies (Johnson, 1969, unpublished dissertation).
  - The canal was first used in November 1980, so 1981 shad must have come from confluent waters.
  - Maricopa County concrete canals are dewatered annually, so their shad must immigrate from upstream.
- The authors therefore conclude that the deformities "must have resulted from some factor unrelated to canal lining."
- The note's data are limited: the sample sizes are unevenly distributed across populations, and some mean values in the source table are unreadable after OCR, so they are not reproduced here.
