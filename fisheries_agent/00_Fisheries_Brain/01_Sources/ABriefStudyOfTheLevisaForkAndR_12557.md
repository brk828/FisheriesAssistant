# A brief study of the Levisa Fork and Russell Fork of the Big Sandy River

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #riverine-ecosystems #Cyprinidae #Percidae #Catostomidae #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae #Petromyzontidae #coal-mining-pollution

## Executive Summary

This 1956 field survey by James B. Kirkwood documents the fish fauna and water quality of the **Levisa Fork** and **Russell Fork** of the [[Big Sandy River]] in eastern Kentucky and western Virginia. Using electric shocking (electro-seining) across five study sites, researchers captured **553–583 fish representing 44 species**, weighing an estimated **81.4 pounds**. The study highlights the ecological impact of **coal mining pollution** — particularly coal washer effluent, acid mine drainage, and sedimentation — on a stream system otherwise supporting a moderate warmwater sport fishery, including [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Kentucky Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], and an unsampled but locally significant [[Walleye]] population. The work builds on and compares against a 1937 baseline survey by Minor E. Clark, noting both species gains and losses over the two decades.

## Background & Description

The **Levisa Fork** originates in Buchanan County, Virginia, and flows northwestward through Pike, Floyd, and Johnson Counties, Kentucky, before joining the Tug Fork at Louisa, Kentucky to form the [[Big Sandy River]], which empties into the Ohio River at Catlettsburg, Kentucky. The basin spans **4,281 square miles**, with 2,280 square miles in Kentucky.

Major tributaries include Paint Creek, John's Creek (site of Dewey Lake), Beaver Creek, Mud Creek, Shelby Creek, and the **Russell Fork**, which originates in Virginia and joins Levisa Fork at Millard, Kentucky.

The study area covered:
- **Levisa Fork**: Prestonsburg, KY to the Virginia border (73.5 miles)
- **Russell Fork**: Millard, KY to Virginia (16.8 miles)

### Five Study Sites (October 1956 conditions)
- **Area A** — Levisa Fork, 4 mi upstream of Prestonsburg (Floyd Co.): 100 ft wide, avg. depth 5 ft, silt/sand/gravel/boulder bottom, dark coal-stained water with surface oil, 65.2°F.
- **Area B** — Levisa Fork, 5 mi upstream of Allen (Floyd Co.): 90 ft wide, avg. depth 2 ft, sandy/gravel/boulder bottom, surface oil, 65.2°F.
- **Area C** — Confluence of Russell Fork and Levisa Fork (Pike Co.): 70 ft wide, avg. depth 3 ft, gravel/boulder bottom, clear water, 63.5°F.
- **Area D** — Levisa Fork, 1 mi downstream of Virginia border (Pike Co.): 60 ft wide, avg. depth 2.5 ft, boulder/gravel bottom, black water from coal washing, 64.4°F.
- **Area E** — Russell Fork at "east end bridge," Elkhorn City (Pike Co.): 70 ft wide, avg. depth 5 ft, boulder bottom, clear water, 60.5°F.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Water Chemistry (Lamar, Krieger & Collier 1955; USGS 1956)
- **pH**: 6.3 – 8.3
- **Dissolved solids**: avg. 143 ppm (max 367 ppm)
- **Hardness**: 40–155 ppm (avg. 72 ppm)
- **Sulfate**: avg. ~60 ppm (30–135 ppm)
- **Specific conductance**: 79.9–768 micromhos (avg. 231)
- **Water temperature**: freezing point to 89°F
- **Average discharge (1929–53, Paintsville)**: 2,287 cfs; 1952 avg.: 3,183 cfs
- **Suspended sediment load (Oct 1952–Apr 1955)**: 1 to 34,800 tons/day; max daily mean concentration 960 ppm (Dec 12, 1952)
- **Sediment composition**: 4% sand, 46% silt, 50% clay

### Pollution Sources
The dominant environmental stressor identified was **coal mining pollution**:
- Coal washing residue entering Levisa Fork from **7 mines in Kentucky** (3 also affecting Russell Fork), plus an unknown number in Virginia.
- Observations on January 11, 1957 confirmed **black, coal-laden water** entering Kentucky from Virginia via Russell Fork, with 3 Kentucky mines actively discharging coal washer effluent.
- A sand dredge operator in Floyd County reported that of the top 12 feet of streambed, only ~1/3 was usable sand; the remainder was ~50% coal and 50% rock/debris, with the top 4 feet "mostly coal."
- Clark (1937) had previously identified sewage, oil, mine water, sawdust, and silt as principal pollutants, alongside heavy losses of virgin forest to logging.

This pollution regime — chronic sedimentation, acid mine drainage, and organic/oil contamination — represents a significant confound in interpreting [[Fish Community Structure]] and [[Species Richness]] in Appalachian coal-region streams.

### Land Use
- **Primary industry**: coal mining
- **Secondary**: natural gas extraction, small-scale lumber
- **Agriculture**: limited to bottomlands and rolling hills in lower stream reaches

## Key Findings

### Sampling Effort & Catch Composition
- **Method**: Electric shocker (electro-seining), conducted October 23–25, 1956, across 5 sites.
- **Total catch**: 553–583 fish (reported inconsistently in text), ~44 species, estimated total weight **81.4 lbs**.
- **Area B** yielded the highest diversity: 248 fish, ~29–30 species, including 26 of 29 total [[Channel Catfish]] captured study-wide.
- **Area E** (Russell Fork, Elkhorn City pool) was a known **walleye fishing hole** (1.5 acres, avg. depth 5 ft) but sampling was hindered by rough bottom and limited boat/crew resources.
- **Longnose Gar** were not effectively immobilized by the shocker and were undersampled across all sites.
- **Rock Bass** were captured only once, likely due to behavioral avoidance (seeking cover under rocks/logs when shocked).

- [[Channel Catfish]] alone comprised **9.35%** of total weight (29 individuals, avg. 0.26 lbs each).
- **Gizzard Shad** (26 individuals, avg. 0.79 lbs) were notably large, described as an "advanced age group."
- **White Crappie** and **Largemouth Bass** captured at Area E were likely stocked by Kentucky's Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources, consistent with Clark's (1937) note of bass stocking as early as 1936.

### Comparison to 1937 Baseline (Clark Survey)
**New species recorded in 1956 not found in 1937:**
[[Northern Brook Lamprey]], [[American Brook Lamprey]], [[Mountain Madtom]], [[Shorthead Redhorse]], [[Spotted Chub]], [[River Chub]], [[Spotfin Shiner]], [[Kentucky Bass]], [[White Crappie]], [[Gilt Darter]], and [[Freshwater Sculpin]].

**Species from 1937 not recovered in 1956** (Table III): Goldeye, Bigmouth Buffalo, Smallmouth Buffalo, Silver Redhorse, Streamlined Chub, Mountain Blacknose Dace, Northern Creek Chub, Rosyside Dace, Southern Redbelly Dace, Northern Black Bullhead, Flathead Catfish, American Eel, [[Walleye]], Northern Johnny Darter, Northern Rainbow Darter, Banded Fantail Darter.

Notably, **Walleye** — despite being absent from both the 1937 and 1956 collections — was reported as common in late winter/early spring angler creels, especially from Russell Fork, indicating a sampling gap due to seasonal timing rather than true absence.

### Fisheries Management Implications
- The presence of Smallmouth Bass, Kentucky Bass, Channel Catfish, and (anecdotally) Rock Bass and Walleye suggests the streams can support a **moderate sport fishery** despite pollution pressure.
- The author concludes that **coal mine pollution** (acid mine water, coal washer waste, sedimentation) is the most serious threat to fish populations in the watershed, and recommends pollution abatement as the primary management priority.
- Incomplete sampling of deep-water habitats (Areas A and E) likely underrepresented [[Game Fish]] abundance, suggesting true game fish biomass may exceed the observed 10.56%.
