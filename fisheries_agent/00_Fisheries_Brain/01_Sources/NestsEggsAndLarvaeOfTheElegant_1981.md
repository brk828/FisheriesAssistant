#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary
This study reports the first documented nests, eggs, and larvae of the **Elegant Madtom** (*[[Noturus elegans]]*), a small catfish endemic to the Barren and upper Green River systems of Kentucky. Three nests were discovered on 22 June 1980 in Fallen Timber Creek at a water temperature of 20°C, each guarded by a parent (males confirmed in two cases). One clutch of 25 eggs was removed and successfully incubated, hatching in approximately 12 days. The paper provides quantitative data on egg size, larval development, fecundity, and clutch size, establishing that *N. elegans* has notably smaller clutch sizes than most congeneric [[Madtom]] species, likely correlated with its small adult body size.

## Background & Description
- **Eggs**: Lemon-yellow, spherical, adhesive, clustered in a mass.
  - Egg diameter: 4.1–4.4 mm (mean = 4.2 mm, n=5)
  - Yolk diameter: 2.9–3.0 mm (mean = 2.9 mm, n=5)
- **Hatchlings**: Averaged ~7 mm TL; externally similar to other *[[Noturus]]* and *[[Ictalurus]]* hatchlings.
- **Sac fry (Nest 1)**: Mean 10.9 mm TL (range 10.6–11.5 mm, n=11)
  - Dense melanophore concentration on head and body
  - Fin rays developed
  - 4 pairs of rudimentary barbels present
  - Yolk sac reduced to about one-third of size at hatching
  - Cephalic lateral-line system formed
- **Larvae (Nest 2)**: Mean 14.7 mm TL (range 14.0–15.3 mm, n=6)
  - Body form, opercular membrane, and fin ray/spine complement resembling adult morphology
  - Cephalic lateral-line system well developed
  - Yolk sac fully absorbed
  - Pigmentation well advanced, though the adult barred pattern had not yet developed
- **Guardian males**: 53 mm SL and 61 mm SL; stomachs found empty upon capture, indicating non-feeding behavior during brood care.
- **Maximum recorded adult size**: 64 mm SL (per Taylor 1969), among the smallest in the genus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Shallow riffle areas of small, low-gradient streams with gravel and bedrock/slab-rock substrate.
  - Stream width: 2–5 m; depth: 12–40 cm at nest sites (~12 cm at nest depth)
  - Nests situated under flat rocks (10–14 cm long, 12–24 cm wide) in shaded riffle zones
  - Nest depressions excavated by guardian parent (~6 cm across)
- **Distribution**: Barren River and upper Green River systems, Kentucky; also recorded from Ruin Creek, Little Sandy River system, Elliott County (eastern Kentucky range extension).
- **Parental care**: Contrary to earlier assumptions (Menzel and Raney 1973) that madtoms do not guard broods post-hatching, this study — along with related work on *[[Noturus exilis]]*, *[[Noturus albater]]*, and *[[Noturus miurus]]* — confirms that male *Noturus* actively guard nests for several days after hatching and refrain from feeding during this period.
- **Interspecific interactions**: Fantail darters (*[[Etheostoma flabellare]]*) were observed invading an exposed, abandoned nest and predating on unguarded sac fry.

## Key Findings
1. **Nesting behavior**: Three nests found under flat rocks above riffles at 20°C water temperature; each attended by a guardian parent (2 confirmed males).
2. **Clutch size and fecundity**: 
   - Direct nest counts: ~30 sac fry (Nest 1), ~20 young (Nest 2), 25 eggs (Nest 3).
   - Ovarian oocyte counts from 8 females (44–64 mm SL) ranged from 19–42 mature oocytes (mean = 30.8).
   - *N. elegans* exhibits smaller clutch sizes than most other *Noturus* species:
     - *[[Noturus exilis]]*: mean 50 eggs (16 nests)
     - *[[Noturus albater]]*: 42 eggs (1 nest)
     - *[[Noturus miurus]]*: mean 66.2 eggs (6 nests)
     - *[[Noturus leptacanthus]]*: smallest known clutch size in genus, mean 17.6 eggs, despite larger adult size (~80 mm SL)
   - Small clutch size in *N. elegans* is likely linked to its small maximum adult body size (64 mm SL).
3. **Incubation and hatching**: A clutch of 25 eggs incubated at 20°C hatched in approximately 12 days (286.5 hours), though a laboratory power failure may have caused premature/artificial hatching due to disrupted aeration.
4. **Parental care confirmed**: Guardian males remained with broods post-hatching and did not feed, corroborating similar findings in other *Noturus* species and refuting prior claims of no post-hatch parental care in madtoms.
5. **Developmental staging**: Growth from hatchling (~7 mm TL) to sac fry (~10.9 mm TL) to larvae (~14.7 mm TL) documented, showing progressive pigmentation, lateral-line development, and yolk sac absorption, with adult-typical barred pigmentation developing later in ontogeny.
