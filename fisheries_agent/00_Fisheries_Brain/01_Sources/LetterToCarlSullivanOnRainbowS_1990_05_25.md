# Letter to Carl Sullivan on Rainbow Smelt Introduction into Lake Powell

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Osmeridae #fisheries-management #introduced-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1990 letter from Paul L. Shafland, President of the American Fisheries Society's (AFS) [[Introduced Fish Section]], to AFS Executive Director Carl Sullivan. It conveys the Committee on Purposeful Introductions' critical review of a proposal by [[Gustaveson Et Al]] to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] (*Osmerus mordax*) into [[Lake Powell]]. The letter is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, but a policy/risk-assessment communication addressing whether the proposed introduction poses unacceptable biological risks and whether the socioeconomic rationale behind it (sportfishery expansion) is sound. The Committee, led by Al Zale (Chair) and Bob Howells, found no specific unacceptable biological risks but expressed skepticism regarding the long-term sustainability of Lake Powell's "new reservoir sportfisheries expansion" model, independent of the smelt introduction itself.

## Background & Description

No morphological data on [[Rainbow Smelt]] are provided in this correspondence. The source is administrative/policy-focused rather than a taxonomic or anatomical description. For morphological characterization, see primary references such as [[Gustaveson Et Al]]'s original introduction proposal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target Habitat:** [[Lake Powell]], a large artificial reservoir on the Colorado River, spanning Utah and Arizona.
- **Proposed Role:** Rainbow smelt were proposed as an introduced forage/sport fish species intended to support and expand the reservoir's sportfishery.
- **Governing Ecological Context:** Lake Powell is characterized in the letter as having experienced a "new reservoir sportfisheries expansion scenario" — a transient period of high productivity and fish abundance typical of newly impounded reservoirs, which the Committee doubts can be sustained indefinitely regardless of management interventions (including smelt introduction).
- **Risk Assessment Scope:** The [[Committee On Purposeful Introductions]] evaluated potential biological risks of introducing a non-native forage fish into an existing reservoir fish community, implicitly considering trophic interactions, competition, and ecosystem stability within the [[Colorado River]] basin's aquatic community.
- **Regulatory/Ecological Governance:** Decision-making authority rests with the [[Colorado River Fish And Wildlife Council]], reflecting the region's approach to managing introduced species within a shared interstate reservoir ecosystem.

## Key Findings

- The AFS Committee on Purposeful Introductions (chaired by Al Zale, with Bob Howells) conducted a critical review of the proposed [[Rainbow Smelt]] introduction into [[Lake Powell]] and **identified no specific unacceptable biological risks**.
- The Committee therefore **did not object** to the introduction on biological risk grounds.
- However, the review found the proposal **failed to convincingly demonstrate** that Lake Powell's current "new reservoir sportfisheries expansion scenario" could be sustained long-term, regardless of the smelt introduction or other management actions.
- The letter suggests that the perceived benefits of introduction may be **exaggerated**, and that anticipated fishery gains should be weighed against more realistic long-term expectations.
- Final decision-making authority is explicitly assigned to the **[[Colorado River Fish And Wildlife Council]]**, with AFS recommending deference to that body's professional judgment.
- The letter frames Utah's solicitation of independent, critical peer review as a **positive precedent** in fisheries management — praised as evidence of serious, professional handling of a potentially controversial introduction decision.
- Distribution of the review included key AFS leadership: Jay Stauffer (President-Elect), Peter Moyle (Past President), Dawn Jennings (Secretary/Treasurer), and Hiram Li (Newsletter Editor), indicating broad institutional awareness of the case as a model for future purposeful introduction reviews.
- The correspondence underscores a broader theme in [[Introduced Species Management]]: that lack of identified biological risk does not necessarily validate the socioeconomic or fisheries-management rationale for an introduction.
