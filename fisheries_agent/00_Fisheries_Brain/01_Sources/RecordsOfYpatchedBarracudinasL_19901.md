# Records of Y-Patched Barracudinas, Lestidium prolixum, Outside Wakasa Bay, Sea of Japan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Paralepididae #bathypelagic-fishes #Aulopiformes

## Executive Summary

This report documents three specimens of [[Lestidium prolixum]] (Y-patched barracudina) caught by motor trawlers outside [[Wakasa Bay]], central west Honshu, Japan, in April-May 1991. These records represent the northern extreme of the species' distribution in the [[Sea of Japan]]. The bathypelagic fish were captured at 200 m depth while fishing for [[Watasenia scintillans]] (firefly squid). All specimens were immature adults ranging from 179.3 to 288.3 mm total length. The findings suggest northward range extensions of warm-water fishes into the Sea of Japan.

## Background & Description

[[Lestidium prolixum]] belongs to the family [[Paralepididae]] (order [[Aulopiformes]]), bathypelagic fishes that resemble barracudas and form large shoals. The species was originally described by Harry (1953) based on specimens from Japanese waters, distinguished by having more lateral-line segments and higher numbers of pores per lateral-line section than other Pacific species.

**Morphological characteristics:**
- Body slender, elongate, strongly compressed
- Deepest point midway between pectoral and pelvic fins
- Head and snout long, snout broad and anteriorly projected
- Mouth large with 3 canines and more than 70 minute retrose teeth on maxillary
- Two rows of teeth on mandible
- Eye and pupil large, vertically oval
- Dorsal fin rays: 10; Anal fin rays: 30-31; Pectoral fin rays: 11-12
- Second dorsal fin adipose
- Tubular luminous organ in mid-ventral line
- Four pores above and below each lateral line scale
- Distinctive Y-shaped dark blotch on occiput
- Body semi-transparent with brilliant silver belly due to guanin deposition
- Dark peritoneum visible from mouth cavity

The luminous organ structure differs from that of [[Lestidium japonicum]] (now referred to genus [[Lestrolepis]]) as reported by Haneda (1958, 1964).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lestidium prolixum]] is very common off southern Japanese Islands, from [[Suruga Bay]] to the [[Okinawa Islands]], particularly off southern [[Kyushu]]. Tabeta (1972) reported mass strandings on northern Kyushu beaches facing the [[Straits of Tsushima]] during winter months, peaking in December.

The [[Straits of Tsushima]] mark the entrance to the [[Sea of Japan]], with a depth of approximately 150 m that has been considered a barrier for invasion and migration of barracudinas and other bathypelagic fishes from northern Kyushu into the Sea of Japan. Prior to recent reports, L. prolixum was not included in checklists of fishes from the Philippines, Korea, or the Sea of Japan.

**Collection details:**
- Location: 10 km off southwestern coast of [[Cape Echizen]], [[Wakasa Bay]]
- Dates: 22 April 1991 (2 specimens) and 1 May 1991 (1 specimen)
- Depth: 200 m
- Method: Motor trawlers fishing for [[Watasenia scintillans]]
- Total lengths: 179.3 mm, 288.3 mm (third specimen not specified)
- Standard lengths: 170.0 mm, 283.5 mm
- All specimens immature; hermaphroditism status undetermined

Many aulopiforms are synchronous hermaphrodites; ovotestis has been observed in [[Lestidium pseudosphyraenoides]] by Mead (1960).

## Key Findings

1. **Northern Range Extension**: These three specimens represent the first confirmed records of [[Lestidium prolixum]] from the middle regions of the [[Sea of Japan]] and likely mark the northern extreme of the species' distribution in this basin.

2. **Barrier Penetration**: The presence of L. prolixum in [[Wakasa Bay]] demonstrates that the species has successfully crossed the [[Straits of Tsushima]], previously considered a depth barrier (approximately 150 m) for bathypelagic fish migration into the Sea of Japan.

3. **Pattern of Warm-Water Species**: The northward occurrence of L. prolixum follows a pattern of range extensions by warm-water (tropical and subtropical) fishes into northern Sea of Japan waters, similar to recent records of [[Caristius macropus]] (manefish), [[Beryx decadactylus]], [[Rexea prometheoides]], and [[Chaetodermis penicilligera]] near [[Niigata]] and [[Sado Island]].

4. **Taxonomic Confirmation**: Specimens were definitively identified as L. prolixum based on diagnostic features including lateral-line segment counts, pore numbers, and the characteristic Y-shaped dark blotch on the occiput.

5. **Immature Specimens**: Despite adult size (170-283.5 mm standard length), all three specimens were sexually immature, preventing determination of hermaphroditism status in this population.

6. **Associated Fishery**: The specimens were incidental catch in the [[Watasenia scintillans]] (firefly squid) fishery, suggesting potential overlap in depth distribution (200 m) during the spring season (April-May).
