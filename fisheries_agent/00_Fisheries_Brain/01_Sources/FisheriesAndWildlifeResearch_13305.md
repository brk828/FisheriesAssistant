# Fisheries and Wildlife Research: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Research Divisions, Fiscal Year 1982

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This is the 1982 annual research summary of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Research Divisions, covering fiscal year 1982 (October 1, 1981 to September 30, 1982). The volume is a compilation of many programs rather than a single study. Its fish-related content centers on [[Environmental Contaminant Evaluation]] in fishes, [[Great Lakes Fishery Laboratory]] assessments of cisco and lake trout, acidification effects on salmonids in the Northeast, and [[Hybrid Grass Carp]] as a sterile aquatic-plant control tool. The rest of the report covers bird damage control, endangered species propagation, and wildlife pest management.

The research was carried out at 12 major facilities, about 80 satellite field stations, and 50 Cooperative Research Units. The program had 751 full-time permanent employees, with a budget of about $36 million in appropriated funds and $7 million in other funds.

## Background & Description

The Research Divisions (Division of Wildlife Ecology-Research, Division of Fishery Ecology-Research, and the Office of Cooperative Units) act as the fact-finding arm of the FWS. Their work supports management of sport fishery and wildlife resources and collaborates with the Departments of the Interior, Agriculture, Defense, and Health, Education, and Welfare, the [[Environmental Protection Agency]], the Atomic Energy Commission, and state agencies. Results are shared through talks, workshops, processed reports, and journal articles.

The report is organized by program and facility: Animal Damage Control (at the [[Denver Wildlife Research Center]]), Environmental Contaminant Evaluation (at the [[Columbia National Fisheries Research Laboratory]]), [[Patuxent Wildlife Research Center]] (endangered species and contaminants), [[Great Lakes Fishery Laboratory]], Inland Fisheries and Reservoir Management stations, Migratory Birds, Mammals and Nonmigratory Birds, and Cooperative Units. Sections on publications, a directory, and a species index are included but were not reproduced here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Much of the report addresses how human activity affects aquatic and terrestrial wildlife:

- **Acidic precipitation** lowers pH in northeastern lakes and streams. Fish and sediment chemistry show increasing metal loads and stress in acidified waters.
- **Irrigated agriculture** in the [[Central Valley]] of California changes river chemistry and fish communities.
- **Industrial and legacy contaminants** (PCBs, dioxins, DDT and metabolites, toxaphene, selenium, and trace metals from mine tailings) occur in fish, eggs, and predators across many regions.
- **Pesticide use** near wetlands, orchards, and roosts creates risks for waterfowl, raptors, and other wildlife.
- **Great Lakes fisheries** face overfishing, invasive prey competition (alewife), stocking limits, and contaminant concerns.

## Key Findings

### Contaminants in Fish

- **Toxaphene** was detected for the first time in fish from Alaska. Pooled rainbow trout and Dolly Varden from the Kenai River exceeded 200 ng/g.
- **Mirex** was detected for the first time in [[Striped Bass]] (30 ng/g, Cooper River, South Carolina).
- **Lindane** reached 700 ng/g in a largemouth bass from the Loxahatchee National Wildlife Refuge, Florida, while bullheads from the same site had none detected.
- **Total DDT** in striped mullet from the Colorado River near Yuma, Arizona, reached 2,900 ng/g, with 36-40% as the isomer most common in the applied insecticide.
- **Endrin** reached 111 ng/g in a bloater from Lake Michigan. Measurable endrin appeared in all Lake Michigan samples in 1982.
- **Selenium** in largemouth bass and bluegills from North Carolina cooling reservoirs was generally about 10 times or more above the nationwide geometric mean (about 0.5 µg/g). The highest mean residue was 7.8 ng/g in male bluegills from Hyco Reservoir.
- **Dioxins and furans** were found at sites ranging from a few ng/kg to a few µg/kg (ng/g). The most contaminated samples came from the Tittabawassee and Saginaw rivers (Michigan) and the Niagara River (New York). Some Great Lakes fish exceeded the FDA advisory limit of 25 ng/kg for 2,3,7,8-TCDD. Two Niagara River common carp contained 53 and 417 ng/kg.

### Trace Metals and Wood Preservatives

- **Big River, Missouri:** About 50,000 cubic yards of lead-, cadmium-, and zinc-rich sediment entered the river in 1977. Elevated residues were found up to 60 miles downstream. Metals were loosely bound to fine sediments and readily available. Pocketbook mussels accumulated up to 120 ng/g lead and 20 µg/g cadmium in 8 weeks.
- **Wood preservatives:** Tetrachlorophenol was highly toxic, with LC50s of 85-170 ng/L. CCA was moderately toxic. The addition of copper did not increase PCP toxicity. Impurities in industrial-grade PCP and PCB formulations strongly influenced toxicity.

### Dust-control Oils and Salmonids

- Intermittent 48-hour exposures to road-dust oil runoff significantly reduced the survival and growth of cutthroat trout. Dissolved naphthalenes were the most biologically available fraction. The Forest Service discontinued oil use for dust control after these studies.

### Striped Bass Decline

- Adults, eggs, and young contained many organic and inorganic contaminants, but no single contaminant was clearly responsible for stock declines.
- In a laboratory mixture study, survival of young striped bass decreased in fresh water and 2 ‰ salt water after 30 days at two and four times the mixture concentration. In 5 ‰ salt water, swimming and feeding were impaired.

### Selenium Effects on Reproduction

- Ovaries consistently held higher selenium than other tissues. Larvae from crosses with a Hyco female developed abnormally.
- Rainbow trout fry exposed to 50 or 100 µg/L selenite showed reduced growth and 47% and 82% mortality at 90 days.

### Acidification and Metals in Fish

- **Northeastern headwaters:** Of 226 headwater lakes and streams surveyed, about 8% had pH below 5, and about 20% had pH between 5 and 6. The mean pH of 95 historically sampled lakes declined from 6.07 to 5.37.
- **Atlantic salmon:** Small tributaries had pH minima below 5.0 and aluminum above 400 µg/L. Egg and fry mortality was 11% in a small stream (pH minimum 4.9) and 2% in a larger stream (pH minimum 5.4).
- **Wisconsin lakes:** Mercury in walleye from acidic lakes ranged from 0.41 to 1.74 µg/g. About one-third exceeded the FDA action level of 1.0 µg/g. White suckers showed serum calcium depression in lakes with pH 5.6 or lower.

### Great Lakes Fisheries

- **Bloaters, Lake Huron:** The average catch of adults per unit of effort rose from less than 0.2 in 1973 to 11 in 1981. The trawlable stock exceeded 3,000 metric tons in 1981. Commercial harvest remained closed while the stock rebuilt.
- **Bloaters, Lake Michigan:** Catch per unit of effort rose from 2 in 1977 to 217 in 1981. Trawlable stock increased from about 6,800 metric tons (1973) to 14,000 (1980). Commercial fishing reopened in Illinois and Wisconsin waters.
- **Lake trout forage, Lake Michigan:** The 1979 standing stock was about 1,600 metric tons. Trout consumed more than 3,000 tons of forage fish, mainly alewives (71%), rainbow smelt (18%), and slimy sculpins (11%). Current consumption was judged not to be harming prey stocks.
- **Lake trout reproduction, Lake Huron:** Swim-up fry captured in 1982 provided the first evidence that hatchery-reared lake trout are reproducing naturally in Lake Huron.
- **Coregonine stocking:** Past fry plantings were too small to show gains. Effective stocking in European lakes is several hundred to several thousand fry per hectare. Lake Ontario would need 1.1-2.6 billion lake whitefish fry annually.
- **Toxaphene:** Lake trout from Lake Michigan contained 6-11 µg/g, and fish from Lakes Superior and Huron generally contained 3-5 µg/g. Technical toxaphene was acutely toxic to *Bosmina* (48-h EC50 1.4 µg/L).

### Hybrid Grass Carp

- Hybrids were all triploid and therefore likely sterile. None were caught by hook and line in over 2 years of fishing pressure of 5,000-7,000 man-hours per hectare per year.
- Survival was 70% with growth to 300 g after one season, and 50% with growth to 900 g after two seasons. Fecal pellets contained over 99% aquatic plant material.
- Electrofishing broke the backs of 90% of exposed hybrids in one pond experiment, though most recovered within about 8 months.

### Animal Damage Control

- **Blackbirds and sunflowers:** Sunflower losses to birds in North Dakota, South Dakota, and Minnesota totaled about 75.3 million pounds in 1980 (up 33% from 56.8 million in 1979). Losses in Stutsman County, North Dakota, were a major cause. Marsh proximity strongly increased damage in 1979 and 1980.
- **Corn:** Bird damage in ten major corn States rose from 1.1% to 2.4% of ears damaged and from 8.7% to 18.5% of fields between 1971 and 1981. Estimated 1981 losses were about $20 million.
- **Livestock feed and starlings:** Starlings ate about 1.5 g of feed per visit. Feed in 3/8-inch or larger pellets and granular meal reduced starling losses.
- **Proadifin HCl:** This synergist significantly lowered LD50 or TI50 values for four test chemicals in coturnix quail, with the largest effect on phencyclidine (TI50 lowered 27.5-fold).
- **Coyotes:** Captive coyotes preferred kids over lambs in pen trials. Bait collars containing 5 mg sodium monofluoroacetate killed coyotes in five of six tests.
- **Rodent control in the Philippines:** Annual preharvest losses fell from 3.7% to 0.5% after continuous low-concentration baiting, adding rice valued at $266 million.

### Endangered Species

- **Whooping crane:** Captive breeding produced 28 eggs. Twenty-five of 26 inseminated eggs (96%) were fertile, the highest fertility yet for captive whooping cranes.
- **California condor:** Photo-identification found only 20 individuals in 1982. The rate of new discoveries had almost stopped, so few or no additional condors may exist in the wild.
- **Masked bobwhite:** 2,850 captive-bred birds were released in Mexico over three years.
- **Bald eagles:** Of 650 archived liver samples, 47 eagles had 11-98 ppm lead, probably from lead poisoning. Record production of 13 captive-bred eaglets was translocated to seven States.

### Other Notable Findings

- **Lead and kestrels:** Chronic dietary lead (0-50 ppm) did not affect American kestrel reproduction. Dosing hatchlings at 25-625 mg/kg caused growth retardation and hematological effects.
- **Fire ant bait (nifluridide and Amdro):** Technical Amdro was highly toxic to fish (LC50s 70-120 µg/L). The field bait did not readily dissolve, and no invertebrate or fish mortality occurred in test containers.
- **Copper and apple snails:** Copper was toxic to juvenile snails in the lab, but field data indicated it did not cause the population decline in the Loxahatchee canals.
- **Waste PCB transformer oil:** Growth in rainbow trout was reduced at 1.5 µg/L waste PCBs, and mortality at 6 µg/L was 82% versus 37% for technical PCBs at 90 days.
- **Endrin in Washington orchards:** Over 90 birds and mammals died. Brains of 42 birds and one mammal were analyzed, and 18 contained lethal endrin levels.
- **Brodifacoum (Talon, Volid):** Secondary poisoning killed six of 25 radio-tracked screech owls in orchards, though urban use posed minimal risk to barn owls.
