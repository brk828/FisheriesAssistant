## Executive Summary
This 1980 paper by Melvin L. Warren, Jr. documents the discovery of a disjunct, apparent relict population of the [[Banded Pygmy Sunfish]] (*Elassoma zonatum*) in the Cypress Creek watershed of the Green River system, Muhlenberg County, Kentucky. This finding represents a significant range extension of approximately 160 km east of the species' previously known Kentucky distribution (which was confined to the Gulf Coastal Plain in the extreme western part of the state) and constitutes a new record for the [[Green River]] drainage. The discovery was incidental to fieldwork conducted for an environmental impact statement related to a proposed channel modification project. The author links the species' occurrence to remnant [[Bald Cypress]] (*Taxodium distichum*) swamp habitat, suggesting that suitable habitat historically extended across the western third of Kentucky before wetland drainage and channelization reduced it to isolated pockets.

## Background & Description
The source text is ecological/distributional in nature and does not provide detailed morphometric data (e.g., standard length, fin ray counts, coloration patterns) for *Elassoma zonatum*. It is referenced descriptively as a "diminutive sunfish," consistent with its common name, the [[Banded Pygmy Sunfish]], within the family Elassomatidae (historically classified with Centrarchidae).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**Habitat Association:**
The species shows strong fidelity to structurally complex, low-flow or lentic wetland habitats, specifically:
- Dense beds of submerged aquatic vegetation (e.g., *Chara* sp., *Potamogeton* sp.).
- Thick floating surface mats of duckweed (*Lemna* sp.), watermeal (*Wolffia* sp.), and *Spirodela* sp.
- Muck/silt substrates covered in decaying organic plant matter, logs, and stumps.
- Negligible to minimal water flow (beaver-dammed pools, cattail marshes, cypress swamp sloughs).
- Strong biogeographic correlation with the historic range of [[Bald Cypress]] swamps in Kentucky.

**Collection Sites (Cypress Creek Watershed):**
1. **Station 1 (Cattail Marsh):** Dense *Typha* sp., *Eleocharis* spp., and *Leersia oryzoides*, with open water pockets of duckweed and *Chara*. Depth 0.15–1.3 m. Yielded 2 specimens near a deep pool edge.
2. **Station 2 (Old Channelized Cypress Creek):** Pools created by beaver dams and log jams (0.45–2 m deep, 8 m wide channel); minimal in-channel vegetation but riparian root cover. Sampled via sodium cyanide. Yielded 1 specimen.
3. **Station 3 (Cypress Swamp Tributary):** Beaver-dam pools within a bald cypress swamp; substrate of muck and decaying plant material; shallow (~0.6 m) and narrow (~3 m). Yielded 2 specimens; represented the only significant open water within the swamp.

**Species Associates:**
*Elassoma zonatum* co-occurred with a range of warm-water, vegetation-associated fishes, including:
- [[Grass Pickerel]] (*Esox americanus vermiculatus*)
- [[Bowfin]] (*Amia calva*)
- [[Lake Chubsucker]] (*Erimyzon sucetta*)
- [[Warmouth]] (*Lepomis gulosus*)
- [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*)
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) — noted as particularly abundant in shallow open-water areas
- [[Ribbon Shiner]] (*Notropis fumeus*)
- [[Slough Darter]] (*Etheostoma gracile*)
- [[Longear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis megalotis*)
- [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*)
- [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*)
- [[Pirate Perch]] (*Aphredoderus sayanus*)

## Key Findings
- **Range Extension:** Confirmed presence of *Elassoma zonatum* in the Green River system (Cypress Creek watershed) extends its known Kentucky range ~160 km east of prior Gulf Coastal Plain records, representing a new drainage record.
- **Relict Population Status:** The population is interpreted as an apparent relict, isolated by historical habitat loss rather than representing a continuous or expanding range.
- **Habitat-Vegetation Correlation:** The distribution of *E. zonatum* closely tracks the historical distribution of [[Bald Cypress]] swamps in Kentucky (per Braun 1943), suggesting these habitats were once broadly connected across the western third of the state.
- **Anthropogenic Threats:** The Cypress Creek watershed has already undergone substantial channelization (~35% of Cypress Creek, ~44% of Little Cypress Creek, dating to ~1920), compounded by ongoing surface mining and a proposed rechanneling project and a wastewater disposal plant — all posing risks to this relict population's remaining wetland habitat.
- **Absence in Adjacent Drainages:** The species' apparent absence from the neighboring Tradewater and Cumberland river drainages may reflect insufficient survey effort rather than a true biogeographic gap, suggesting further undiscovered populations may exist in the [[Gulf Coastal Plain Province]] habitats of western Kentucky.
- **Collection Methodology Note:** Specimens were successfully collected using both seining (1.8 x 3.0-m, 0.032-m mesh) and sodium cyanide application (per Tatum 1968), with low water conditions in autumn aiding fish concentration in isolated pools.
