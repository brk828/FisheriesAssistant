# Fossil Creek Spikedace and Loach Minnow Stocking, November 2007

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cobitidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department document reports the stocking of 125 [[Spikedace]] and 125 [[Loach Minnow]] into [[Fossil Creek]], a perennial, travertine-forming tributary of the [[Verde River]] in Central Arizona. The fish were released on November 2, 2007, above a fish barrier and diversion dam. They came from the captive breeding population at the [[Bubbling Ponds Native Fish Hatchery]] (Aravaipa lineage). The release followed a fall 2007 habitat survey that identified suitable habitat for five federally listed species. The fish appeared healthy on release, though one loach minnow was injured or in poor health and one stocked spikedace was eaten by a chub. The populations are to be augmented around fall 2008 and monitored, with further stocking planned downstream of the diversion dam and for other listed species.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** Fossil Creek is a perennial, travertine-forming tributary of the Verde River. In 2004, a fish barrier was built about 7.5 km above the confluence with the Verde River, and the stream was chemically renovated to remove nonnative fishes. This renovation created a fishless environment for restoring native species.

**Habitat survey.** In fall 2007, the aquatic habitat from the constructed fish barrier to the headwater springs was surveyed (Carter 2007). The survey aimed to locate suitable habitat for five federally listed species:
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*)
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

**Source stock.** Both stocked species were raised in the captive breeding program at the [[Bubbling Ponds Native Fish Hatchery]], which is operated by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD). The stock is of the Aravaipa lineage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stocking site.** Spikedace and loach minnow were released into habitat above the [[Diversion Dam]] that the fall 2007 survey had identified as suitable. The renovated, nonnative-free reach is the restoration target, so the fish were placed where no competing or predatory nonnative fishes were expected.

**Transport and acclimation.** Fish were transported in 0.6% saline solution with Stress Coat® to reduce handling stress. At the stocking site they were tempered slowly for about 1.5 hours to match stream temperature and water chemistry before release.

**Observed mortality and predation.** The fish appeared healthy and behaved normally on release. Two problems were noted:
- One loach minnow appeared injured or in poor health when the team arrived at the stream.
- One newly released spikedace was observed being eaten by a chub. This indicates that at least one chub was present in the stocking reach, which is relevant to predation risk for stocked natives.

## Key Findings

- **Stocking event (November 2, 2007):** 125 spikedace and 125 loach minnow were released above the diversion dam in Fossil Creek.
- **Personnel:** The release was carried out by staff from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (Tony Robinson, David Ward, Codey Carter), the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Shaula Hedwall), and the [[Center for Biological Diversity]] (Michelle Harington).
- **Husbandry protocol:** Fish were transported in 0.6% saline with Stress Coat® and tempered for about 1.5 hours before release.
- **Initial condition:** Fish appeared healthy and acted normally on release. One loach minnow was injured or in poor health on arrival.
- **Predation:** One newly released spikedace was observed being consumed by a chub, which points to predation as an early risk for the reintroduced population.
- **Survey basis:** The fall 2007 habitat survey (Carter 2007) identified suitable habitat for five federally listed species across the reach from the fish barrier to the headwater springs.
- **Planned augmentation:** The populations are expected to be augmented around fall 2008 and monitored over time.
- **Future stocking plans:**
  - Stock spikedace and loach minnow in habitat downstream of the diversion dam.
  - Stock [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] into potentially suitable habitat in the renovated portions of Fossil Creek.
