# Taxonomy, Distribution and Speciation of the Aphanius dispar Species Complex, with Description of Aphanius sirhani n. sp.

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This synoptic revision by [[Wolfgang Villwock]], [[Adolf Scholl]] and [[Friedhelm Krupp]] (German-language paper) re-examines the [[Aphanius Dispar]] species complex (Cyprinodontidae) using morphology, meristics, enzyme electrophoresis and crossbreeding experiments. The authors designate a lectotype for *A. dispar* and conclude that at least two species exist: the nominate *A. dispar dispar*, widely distributed from NE Africa to NW India, and a new species, *A. sirhani*, known from the Azraq oasis in the Wadi Sirhan depression of NE Jordan. A third taxon, *A. d. richardsoni*, is retained as a subspecies restricted to the [[Dead Sea]] basin. *A. sirhani* is argued to be the closest relative of *A. dispar* and possibly the oldest branch of the *A. dispar* species-flock.

## Background & Description

The authors frame the paper as a demonstration of how combined methods can resolve taxonomically complex groups. The reference species *A. dispar* was described by [[Ruppell]] (1828) from the Red Sea, where the sexes were originally described as separate species by later authors (e.g. Heckel 1849). The nomenclatural history includes numerous synonyms (e.g. *A. ginaonis*, *A. stoliczkanus*, *A. richardsoni*) and conflicting assignments, with [[Hoedeman]] (1951) proposing a separate genus, which most later workers rejected.

**Morphology and methods.** About 300 specimens were examined for 18 morphometric characters, but these were judged unreliable for delimitation because intra-population variation often exceeded interspecific differences and they depend on age, condition and habitat. Meristic characters (dorsal, anal and caudal fin rays; lateral-line scale counts; gill rakers) were considered more useful.

**Diagnostic features (selected):**
- *A. dispar dispar*: D 8-11, A 9-12, MLR 24-28, GR (11) 14-16 (17). Lectotype: ♂, 28.9 mm SL, Red Sea (SMF 821.1). Males show dark cross-banding on the caudal fin and longitudinal streaks in dorsal and anal fins; females show about 10 greyish-brown flank bars.
- *A. d. richardsoni*: fewer meristic differences from the nominate form; differs mainly in body proportions (shorter dorsal and anal fins) and a labyrinthine flank pattern. Its Boulenger (1907) diagnostic features (rounded caudal fin, 12-14 teeth per jaw) were found not to be significant.
- *A. sirhani*: D 9-12, A 10-13, LL 25-29, GR 9-13. Males carry 8-11 dark cross-bands on a pale background; females carry 8-12 black spots. Holotype: ♂, 36.4 mm SL (ZMH 6444), Azraq Oasis, 31.3.1980. Short pelvic fins (max. about 10% SL), and a distinctive frontal scalation pattern shared with *A. dispar* and *A. fasciatus*.

**Jaw and pharyngeal morphology.** Jaw bones of *A. sirhani* resemble *A. dispar* more closely than *A. fasciatus*, notably the narrowed maxillary bone, interpreted as a sister-group relationship (Hennig sense). Pharyngeal teeth in *A. sirhani* are slightly more numerous than in the comparison species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*A. dispar* occupies standing or slow-flowing inland waters and marginal marine habitats, tolerating high temperatures and salinity, and it finds few competitors in such habitats. At the type locality of *A. ginaonis* (Iran) constant temperatures of 37-40 °C were recorded.

**Azraq (type locality of *A. sirhani*).** The Azraq oasis lies at the northern end of the Wadi Sirhan depression (about 300 km long, 30-50 km wide). The historic lake covered about 4000 km²; the present catchment is 12,800 km², with about 5 km² of perennial pools and streams. Annual rainfall is below 100 mm, mostly in December and January, and seasonal playas (Qa'a) fill briefly and leave salt crusts. Springs are fed mainly by precipitation from the Hauran–Jabal Druz mountains. Water temperatures range from about −10 °C to +46 °C. Other fish at Azraq include [[Tilapia Zillii]], [[Tilapia Aurea]], [[Barbus Canis]] and [[Clarias Gariepinus]], most probably introduced; the authors found no evidence that any fish other than *A. sirhani* is native there.

**Biogeographic history.** The authors propose that the *A. fasciatus*/*A. dispar* group originated in the Middle Miocene Mediterranean–Indian Ocean connection, and that a population reached the Wadi Sirhan from the Mesopotamian Basin in the Late Miocene or Pliocene, where it diverged in isolation. *A. dispar* later spread via the Persian Gulf to the Indian Ocean and, after the opening of the [[Strait of Perim]] in the Pliocene, into the Red Sea. Pleistocene pluvial phases allowed entry into the Jordan–Dead Sea system (via Lake Lisan) and the Mediterranean (via the Suez region). Steinitz's and Por's alternative hypotheses are critically discussed; the authors consider Por's Pliocene marine-relict scenario and Steinitz's Oligocene-based interpretation unlikely given the magnitude of divergence.

## Key Findings

- **Lectotype designated** for *Lebias dispar* Ruppell, 1828: ♂, 28.9 mm SL, SMF 821.1.
- **Two species recognised:** *A. dispar* (with nominate subspecies *A. d. dispar* and *A. d. richardsoni*) and the new species *A. sirhani*.
- **Diagnostic traits** of *A. sirhani* include its body form, colour pattern, short pelvic fins and frontal scalation; it lacks the strongly rounded dorsal and anal fins of *A. dispar*.
- **Enzyme electrophoresis** (about 20+ enzyme systems tested): eight enzymes were monomorphic and identical across *A. dispar* populations and *A. sirhani*. *A. sirhani* differs from the Siwa population of *A. dispar* at ADH, GOT-1, GOT-2, MPI and 6-PGD. Genetic similarity (Nei's I) of *A. sirhani* with *A. dispar* Siwa is about 0.30 (comparable to the intraspecific value of 0.75 between Siwa and Ain Fashkha populations is not directly equivalent), and is lower with Ain Fashkha. Similarity with *A. fasciatus*, *A. iberus* and *A. chantrei* is clearly lower.
- **Crossbreeding:** *A. sirhani* × *A. chantrei* gave hybrids, but reciprocal crosses produced no offspring; more than 80 F1 hybrids were recorded from one direction, nearly all dying between days 8 and 10. *A. sirhani* × *A. fasciatus* produced only 5 F1 individuals, which died after about 14 days. Hybrids of *A. dispar* Ain Fashkha × *A. sirhani* (11 of 13 surviving F1 hybrids) were all male-like in pattern and had disorganised testes, interpreted as near-sterility; intraspecific *A. dispar* crosses showed variable F2 success that reflects population-level genetic divergence.
- **Conclusion on relationships:** *A. sirhani* is closer to *A. dispar* than to *A. fasciatus*, *A. iberus* or *A. chantrei*, and the authors regard it as likely the oldest branch of the *A. dispar* species-flock.
- **Distribution:** *A. dispar dispar* is widespread from NE Africa (Red Sea, Sudan, Egypt) to NW India and the Persian Gulf; *A. d. richardsoni* is endemic to the Dead Sea basin; *A. sirhani* is known only from Azraq. Earlier reports of *A. d. richardsoni* from Syria are considered misidentifications.
- **Open questions:** whether enzyme differences are real or sampling artefacts; the diagnostic significance of the Azraq pattern across the wider Wadi Sirhan depression; and the limited hybrid sample sizes.
