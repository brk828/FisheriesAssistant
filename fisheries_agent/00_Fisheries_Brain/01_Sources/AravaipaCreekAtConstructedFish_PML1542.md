# Aravaipa Creek Fish Survey Field Data Sheets (2005)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of handwritten and typed field data sheets from fish surveys of [[Aravaipa Creek]] in [[Pinal County]], [[Arizona]]. Surveys were conducted on 3 August 2005 and 14 September 2005 by P.C. Marsh, R. Clarkson (OCR reads "Gfarkson") and J.A. Stefferud. The sampling was organized around a [[Constructed Fish Barrier]], with separate counts for sites above and below the upper barrier and below the lower barrier. Tallies cover six species, with [[Longfin Dace]] the most abundant native fish in every block. Most pages are illegible or only partly readable after OCR, so the note reports only the counts and percentages that are legible.

## Background & Description

The document consists of roughly a dozen scanned pages. Most are standardized field forms with fields for site, date, locality, drainage, state, county, land ownership, forest, latitude and longitude, bank characteristics, vegetation, weather, air and water temperature, [[Dissolved Oxygen]], conductivity, pH, turbidity, and time. Gear fields record voltage, amperage and frequency, which points to [[Electrofishing]] as the capture method. Collector names and photo numbers also appear on several sheets.

The first page is a species count table with three blocks:

- **Aravaipa Creek at constructed fish barrier** (3 August 2005): the species list is partly garbled, and the block total is 415 (100.00%).
- **Below upper barrier**: a total is not legible, but the percentages (e.g., 67.03%, 18.28%, 5.38%, 4.66%) are consistent with a sample of about 279 fish.
- **Above upper barrier**: counts and percentages are partly legible (e.g., 72.62%, 7.64%, 6.20%, 4.90%).

A final page lists the 14 September 2005 sample below the lower barrier in Pinal County, Arizona, with collectors Marsh and Clarkson.

Other pages are handwritten notes, sketches and field observations. Many are illegible after OCR and are not summarized.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The surveyed reach contains a native assemblage of small-bodied cyprinids and catostomids, with [[Nonnative Fish]] recorded at the lower barrier site. The [[Constructed Fish Barrier]] divides the creek into sections, which allows the fish community above and below to be compared. The presence of [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Desert Sucker]] is relevant to conservation monitoring in southwestern streams. [[Red Shiner]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]] appear as lower-frequency taxa.

Water-quality fields (air and water temperature, pH, DO, conductivity, turbidity) were recorded on multiple sheets, but most readings are illegible or incomplete in the scan, so no values are reported here.

## Key Findings

- **Dominant species:** [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) was the most abundant species in every legible block. It made up 76.39% of the barrier-site total (317 of 415 fish) and roughly 67% of the below-upper-barrier sample.
- **Sonora Sucker:** *Catostomus insignis* (30 fish, 7.23%) was recorded at the barrier site, and 13 fish (4.66%) were recorded below the upper barrier. [[Sonora Sucker]] is an important native catostomid in this creek.
- **Desert Sucker:** *Pantosteus clarki* (19 fish at the barrier site) was present across sites. Its share was about 5.38% below the upper barrier and 4.90% above it.
- **Loach Minnow:** *Tiaroga cobitis* (15 fish, 5.38%) was recorded below the upper barrier. The lower-barrier sheet also lists loach minnow, with a count of 13 (alignment with the species list is partly unclear).
- **Red Shiner and Yellow Bullhead:** *Cyprinella lutrensis* and *Ameiurus natalis* were recorded in small numbers (around 1 to 5 fish per block).
- **Lower barrier (14 September 2005):** Counts of 200, 13, 2 and 1 were recorded for loach minnow, desert sucker, Sonora sucker and nonnative fish. The count-to-species mapping is only partly legible.
- **Sampling design:** Surveys were stratified by barrier position (above upper barrier, below upper barrier, and below lower barrier), which makes the sheets useful for comparing fish community composition across the barrier.
- **Data quality:** Most handwritten entries, water-quality readings, and site descriptions are illegible after OCR. Verification against original forms is recommended before any quantitative reuse.
