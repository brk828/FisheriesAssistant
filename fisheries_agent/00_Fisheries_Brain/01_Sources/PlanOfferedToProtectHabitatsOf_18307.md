# Plan Offered to Protect Habitats of Northwest Salmon and Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary
This 1994 policy report (via AP/NY Times) details a Clinton Administration initiative, known as **"Pacfish,"** aimed at protecting anadromous fish habitats on Federal lands in eastern Oregon, eastern Washington, Northern California, and Idaho. The proposal seeks to establish riparian protection buffer zones to mitigate habitat degradation caused by logging, livestock grazing, and recreation. The plan is a direct response to significant, documented declines in native Pacific anadromous fish stocks and aims to preempt the need for additional listings under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The species addressed are **anadromous fish**, meaning they migrate from the ocean upstream into freshwater rivers and streams to spawn. Their survival is highly dependent on specific [[Riparian Habitat|riparian]] and in-stream conditions, including:
- **Cool water temperatures**: Critical for survival; shade from streamside vegetation prevents overheating.
- **Pool frequency and width-to-depth ratios**: Essential structural habitat features for healthy aquatic ecosystems.
- **Woody debris**: Provides structural complexity and cover within streams.
- **Stream-bank stability and low bank angles**: Reduces erosion and sediment intrusion.
- **Low siltation**: Excessive silt from erosion (caused by logging/grazing) degrades spawning gravel and rearing habitat.

The plan explicitly links land-use practices (logging and grazing) to habitat degradation via increased erosion and loss of riparian shade cover, directly impacting fish survival rates.

## Key Findings
- **Population Decline**: Recent reports (as of 1994) found that approximately half of the ~400 stocks of native Pacific anadromous fish show significant population declines, and 106 stocks are already **extinct**.
- **Protection Zone Structure (Buffer Strips)**:
  - **300 feet**: Buffer width on either side of *fish-bearing* streams (permanent).
  - **100 feet**: Buffer width for *intermittent* streams (those that dry up seasonally).
  - **50 feet**: Buffer width for "non-key" watersheds (permanent streams without fish) and around ponds/reservoirs/wetlands larger than one acre.
- **Geographic Scope**: The plan applies only to Federal lands east of the Cascade Range; it does not apply to private lands.
- **Relation to [[Northern Spotted Owl]] Protections**: The buffer strip strategy is modeled after similar protective zones established for old-growth forests containing the northern spotted owl in western Oregon and Washington, though this plan does not add new conservation measures within the owl's range.
- **Economic Impact Estimates**:
  - Estimated implementation cost: **$20 million** (preferred option); more extensive alternative options considered could cost up to **$34 million**.
  - Estimated logging loss: **58 million board feet per year**.
  - Estimated grazing reduction: **42,000 fewer "animal units" per month** (1 animal unit = 1 cow + calf, or 5 sheep).
- **Regulatory Context**: The proposal was issued as an environmental assessment to comply with the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA). It represents an interim measure that will remain in place until a longer-term Environmental Impact Statement is completed.
- **Public Process**: A 45-day public comment period was scheduled before the plan could become formal government policy.
- **Institutional Conflict**: Sources indicated the plan represents a shift in internal Forest Service policy, favoring fisheries science and wildlife/fish protection biologists over the traditional timber supply program.
