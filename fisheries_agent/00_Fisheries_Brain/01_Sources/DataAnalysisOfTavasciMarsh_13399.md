## Executive Summary

This report presents a short-term limnological survey of **[[Tavasci Marsh]]**, a shallow freshwater wetland situated adjacent to the **[[Verde River]]**, separated from it by a limestone hill. Data were collected over three days (5–7 December 1972) using a **Hach unit** (turbidity, nitrogen, phosphorus) and a **Hydrolab** (water temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductance). No fish species, fish morphology, or fish population data are reported in this source — the study is exclusively a **water chemistry and nutrient-cycling assessment** of the marsh ecosystem, which is dominated by *Typha* (cattail) and grasses and is characterized as **highly eutrophic**. The marsh is hypothesized to be hydrologically connected to the Verde River, with an additional spring input, and exhibits strong diel photosynthesis/respiration signals.

## Background & Description

*No fish or faunal morphological data are present in this source.* The paper instead documents the **physical and chemical morphology of the aquatic habitat** itself:

- **Water depth:** Very shallow marsh basin; bottom sediments described as biologically active, contributing to photosynthesis/respiration cycles.
- **Substrate/vegetation structure:** Dominated by dense stands of **[[Typha (Cattail)]]** and marsh grasses; decomposing typha detritus is a major organic input.
- **Water temperature range:** 2.5°C (06:30–07:30, 6 Dec) to 12.5°C (13:30, 6 Dec); nighttime ice formation observed along shoreline on 6 Dec.
- **Turbidity:** Initial anomalously high readings (95, 63, 75, 50 NTU-equivalent) attributed to Hydrolab placement disturbance and nearby waterfowl activity; subsequent stabilized readings ranged 0–43, mean = **18.9**.
- **Conductance:** High readings interpreted as indicative of **very soft water** (low total dissolved solids/salt ion content).
- **Flow regime:** Measurable current through the marsh, estimated at **0.75–1.0 m/min**, suggesting through-flow rather than stagnant conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although no fish community data are given, the report characterizes the **[[Eutrophic Wetland]]** niche of Tavasci Marsh in detail:

- **Hydrological connectivity:** Hypothesized surface/subsurface linkage to the **[[Verde River]]** via a limestone-hill divide; a feeder spring was sampled directly (turbidity 0, phosphorus 0.80 ppm, nitrogen 0.25 ppm), though comparative sampling at the spring mouth was not completed due to time constraints.
- **Nutrient sources:** Two dominant inputs sustain marsh eutrophication:
  1. Decomposing **dead Typha detritus**, releasing nutrients back into the marsh for uptake by living plants.
  2. **Cattle waste products**, abundant in the surrounding marsh area.
- **Dissolved oxygen dynamics:** DO expressed as percent saturation ranged from **54% to 146%**, indicating strong photosynthetic oxygenation during daylight (supersaturation) balanced against respiratory drawdown — though it is unclear whether values were altitude-corrected.
- **pH dynamics:** Mean pH 7.6, range 7.5–8.4 (~1 unit diel swing), attributed to the interplay of photosynthesis (raising pH via CO₂ drawdown) and respiration (lowering pH).
- **Weather influence:** Clear skies with steady northward wind (5 Dec) versus 75–100% cloud cover (6–7 Dec) correlated with sub-0°C to ~2°C ambient air temperatures, influencing water temperature and ice formation patterns.

This habitat profile is consistent with a **[[Marsh Ecosystem|shallow, nutrient-rich marsh]]** functioning as a biogeochemical "hot spot" within the broader Verde River riparian corridor — the kind of productive, structurally complex wetland habitat that (in comparable systems) often supports fish nursery or foraging habitat, though this particular dataset does not sample or report on fish directly.

## Key Findings

- **Nutrient status:** Both phosphorus (0.02–1.30 ppm) and nitrogen (0.22–1.00 ppm, mean 0.60 ppm) occurred at levels high enough that neither nutrient is considered **limiting** to primary production in this system — a hallmark of eutrophic conditions.
- **Diel nutrient cycling:** Phosphorus showed elevated daytime concentrations with rapid nighttime uptake by plants, indicating tight coupling between the **[[Nutrient Cycling|nutrient pool]]** and active photosynthetic biomass.
- **Overall trophic classification:** The report concludes Tavasci Marsh is **"very eutrophic and active in gas and nutrient exchange,"** with strong day/night biogeochemical signals (pH, DO, phosphorus) reflecting high rates of photosynthesis and respiration.
- **Hydrology as a driver:** The marsh's current (~0.75–1.0 m/min) is identified as ecologically important for **circulating nutrients and oxygen** throughout the shallow basin.
- **Sediment role:** Shallow depth allows bottom sediments to actively participate in photosynthetic/respiratory gas exchange, rather than acting as an inert nutrient sink.
- **Data limitations:** Initial turbidity readings were likely artifacts of instrument placement and waterfowl disturbance; DO percent-saturation values carry uncertainty due to unclear altitude correction; comparative spring-mouth vs. spring-source sampling was not completed.
- **No direct fish/ichthyological data** (species composition, abundance, morphology, or habitat use) are reported anywhere in this source — any fish-related inference would require linking this chemical/habitat dataset to a separate faunal survey of the **[[Verde River Watershed]]**.
