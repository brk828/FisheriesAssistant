# RNA-DNA Ratios as Indicators of Recent Growth Rates of a Fish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #biochemistry #fish-physiology

## Executive Summary
This 1970 study by Frank J. Butow tested whether the ratio of ribonucleic acid to deoxyribonucleic acid (RNA-DNA ratio) in fish tissue could serve as a biochemical index of recent growth rate, using [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*) as a model species. Two laboratory experiments manipulated feeding rate (0%, 2%, 6% body weight/day) and duration (15, 30, 45 days), plus a starvation/refeeding trial (14 days deprivation, 6 days refeeding), while measuring RNA-P and DNA-P content of defatted, whole eviscerated fish tissue. Results confirmed that RNA-DNA ratios closely track feeding condition and growth rate: ratios were lowest in starved, weight-losing fish and highest in rapidly growing, well-fed fish. Notably, ratios responded rapidly (within 1–4 days) to refeeding after starvation, driven almost entirely by changes in RNA concentration rather than DNA, supporting the use of this ratio as a near-real-time indicator of metabolic/growth status in fish populations — with potential application to [[Fish Growth Rate Estimation]] and broader aquatic productivity studies pioneered in [[Zooplankton Nucleic Acid Studies]].

## Background & Description
- **Species used:** Medium-sized, young-of-the-year [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*), family Cyprinidae.
- **Tissue sampled:** Whole, eviscerated (liver-free) fish, defatted and dried via chloroform–methanol–ether extraction ("dry, fat-free tissue," DFFT).
- **Body size changes tracked:**
  - Standard length (mm) and fresh weight (g) measured to nearest mm/0.01 g.
  - Under 14-day starvation, standard length remained essentially unchanged, but body weight steadily declined (sharpest single-interval loss at day 8, roughly 3× the day-6 loss).
  - Upon refeeding, weight gain was detectable after 1 day; length increase was not detectable until ~3 days.
- **DNA-P concentration:** Remained relatively stable across treatments (a hallmark of DNA as a stable per-cell marker), ranging roughly 74–117 µg/100 mg DFFT, but slightly *elevated* in starved/weight-losing fish — attributed to reduced cytoplasmic volume per cell (more cells per unit tissue weight) rather than actual DNA synthesis.
- **RNA-P concentration:** Highly variable and the primary driver of RNA-DNA ratio changes; increased with feeding rate and growth, declined with starvation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** *Notemigonus crysoleucas* is a freshwater cyprinid; specimens were held in a controlled laboratory system of 30-gallon fiberglass tanks (subdivided into 15-gallon compartments) at 22.0–24.0°C with continuous water exchange (~every 8 hr).
- **Feeding ecology (experimental):** Fish were fed granular trout feed at controlled rations (0%, 2%, 6% of body weight/day) to simulate a gradient of natural feeding/growth conditions, analogous to environmental food availability fluctuations experienced by wild [[Golden Shiner]] populations.
- **Broader applicability:** The RNA-DNA technique was framed as a tool with cross-taxon relevance for [[Aquatic Productivity Measurement]], building on prior work applying nucleic acid ratios to marine phytoplankton and zooplankton growth estimation (Sutcliffe 1965; Holm-Hansen et al. 1968), suggesting utility for fishery biologists and oceanographers studying [[Growth Rate Biomarkers]] in wild populations without repeated sampling.
- **Comparative RNA levels:** Golden shiner whole-body RNA concentrations (~16.5–43.4 µg/mg) fell within the range reported for whole, dried amphipods (~2.0–44.0 µg/mg; Sutcliffe 1965), indicating broad conservation of RNA-based growth signals across aquatic taxa.

## Key Findings
1. **Feeding rate and duration directly affect RNA-DNA ratio:**
   - 15-day trial: 0%, 2%, 6% feeding → mean weight changes of −16.1%, +2.2%, +21.3%; corresponding RNA-DNA ratios of 2.18, 2.83, 4.32.
   - 45-day trial: weight changes of −35.4%, +45.6%, +90.9% → ratios of 1.36, 3.03, 3.08.
   - Starvation for 30 and 45 days produced ratios of 1.66 and 1.36 with weight losses of 20.6% and 35.6%.

2. **Ratio "leveling off" and decline at high growth over time:**
   - At 2% feeding, ratios plateaued (3.15 → 3.03) over 30–45 days despite continued weight gain (19.9% → 45.6%).
   - At 6% feeding, ratios actually *declined* (3.91 → 3.08) over 30–45 days despite continued strong weight gain (57.9% → 90.9%), suggesting ratio reflects *recent* rather than cumulative growth.

3. **Rapid ratio response to starvation/refeeding cycle:**
   - During 14-day starvation: ratio declined from 3.56 (day 4) to 2.30 (day 14), with a transient, non-significant rebound at day 8 (3.42).
   - Upon refeeding: ratio rose quickly — 3.45 (day 1) → 4.93 (day 2) → 5.07 (day 3) → peak 5.73 (day 4) → 5.00 (day 5) → 5.14 (day 6), indicating a response detectable within 24 hours and peaking within ~4 days.

4. **RNA, not DNA, drives ratio changes:**
   - DNA-P remained comparatively stable (~74–117 µg/100 mg DFFT) across all treatments; slight increases in starved fish attributed to shrinking cytoplasmic volume rather than DNA synthesis (per [[Leslie 1955]], [[Hotchkiss 1955]]).
   - RNA-P fluctuated much more dramatically (e.g., 183.7 to 433.7 µg/100 mg DFFT across starvation/refeeding), confirming RNA as the responsive, growth-linked nucleic acid component.

5. **Proposed diagnostic thresholds:**
   - A field sample of ~20 golden shiners with mean RNA-DNA ratio ≈ 2.0 would indicate a population currently losing weight.
   - A mean ratio ≈ 4.0 would indicate rapid growth — establishing a practical reference framework for field application of the technique, contingent on species-specific calibration.

6. **Methodological caveats:**
   - Orcinol reaction (RNA-P quantification, per [[Schneider 1957]]) is subject to interference from chromogenic substances, notably problematic in liver tissue (hence use of eviscerated fish); DNA-P was measured via the Burton diphenylamine method ([[Burton 1956]]).
   - The study recommends future inclusion of a cold acid extraction step to remove potential interfering substances, which was omitted here.

7. **Comparative physiological support:**
   - Findings align with growth hormone studies in fish liver ([[Venugopalan 1967]]) and regenerating rat liver tissue, both showing RNA concentration peaks coinciding with periods of most rapid tissue growth, reinforcing the RNA-DNA ratio as a conserved biochemical correlate of active growth across vertebrate taxa.
