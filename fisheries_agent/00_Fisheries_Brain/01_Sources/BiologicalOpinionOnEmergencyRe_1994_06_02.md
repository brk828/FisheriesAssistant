# Biological Opinion: Emergency Repairs to Hank and Yank Tank, Sycamore Creek

## Executive Summary
This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service biological opinion (Consultation #2-21-94-F-183) addresses emergency repairs to **Hank and Yank Tank**, a concrete impoundment of Yank's Spring on **Sycamore Creek**, Santa Cruz County, Arizona. The tank and spring support a small population of the threatened **[[Sonora Chub]]** (*Gila ditaenia*), a desert-adapted cyprinid endemic to the Rio de la Concepción drainage. Following severe flood damage in January 1993 that destabilized the streambank and exposed leaking pipe fittings threatening to drain the tank, the U.S. Forest Service and FWS undertook emergency repair actions. The Service concluded the repairs were **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the Sonora chub nor destroy or adversely modify its designated **[[Critical Habitat]]**, and determined that **no incidental take occurred**.

## Background & Description
Direct morphological data on *Gila ditaenia* are not detailed in this document; it references [[Miller 1945]] for original species description and [[Minckley 1973]] (*Fishes of Arizona*) for anatomical/taxonomic treatment. Key taxonomic points noted:
- Genus: *Gila*, subgenus described by Miller (1945) alongside the species description.
- Family: [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows).
- Species is treated as morphologically and ecologically distinct enough to warrant its own subgenus within *Gila*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution**: Endemic to the Rio de la Concepción drainage of Sonora, Mexico, and Arizona, USA. In the U.S., restricted entirely to **[[Sycamore Creek]]**, two of its tributaries, and **Yank's Spring** — the *only* known U.S. locality.
- **Habitat preference**: Adults concentrate in the **deepest pools** of Sycamore Creek. Habitat use is strongly associated with **bedrock-sand substrates**; notably, habitats supporting chub through summer **lacked floating cover** (Carpenter & Maughan 1993).
- **Hydrology**: Sycamore Creek is an **interrupted stream** during low-flow years, existing as isolated pools; it becomes continuous to the International Border (~5 miles downstream) only during heavy rainfall events — an important refugial dynamic for the species.
- **Spawning**: Correlated with **post-flooding periods**, occurring at variable times throughout the year (Carpenter & Maughan 1992).
- **Diet**: Poorly documented but likely includes aquatic and terrestrial insects and algae, with specific taxa identified as **Chironomids, Simuliids, and Ephemeroptera** (Carpenter & Maughan 1992; Minckley 1973).
- **Artificial refuge habitat**: Hank and Yank Tank (a man-made concrete impoundment) supports a small chub population and functions as an artificial refugium, raising unresolved questions about long-term management value versus its non-native/artificial status.
- **Co-occurring species of concern**: The tank and surrounding spring/stream also historically supported three ranid frogs: the Category 1 **[[Tarahumara Frog]]** (*Rana tarahumarae*), Category 2 **[[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]** (*Rana chiricahuensis*), and Category 2 **[[Lowland Leopard Frog]]** (*Rana yavapaiensis*). Notably, from 1980–1985 Hank and Yank Tank was the only area in Sycamore Canyon with abundant leopard frogs, potentially linked to favorable zinc:cadmium ratios (5–30× higher than downstream reaches), which may mitigate heavy-metal leaching toxicity implicated in Tarahumara frog decline (Hale and Jarchow 1988).
- **Threats**: Habitat modification, predation/competition from non-native fishes, grazing-induced erosion and siltation, cyclical drought/flood stress, watershed development, mining, and pollutants (Service 1986, 1992).

## Key Findings
- **No jeopardy determination**: The emergency pipe repair was found not likely to jeopardize the Sonora chub or adversely modify its [[Critical Habitat]].
- **No incidental take**: The Service determined no take of Sonora chub occurred during repair operations; SCUBA-based repair attempts by Dr. Paul Marsh (ASU) were brief (~5 minutes) and low-disturbance despite 18–26 inches of bottom debris in the tank.
- **Critical habitat definition**: Includes Sycamore Creek from Yank's Spring downstream to the Mexico border, plus lower 1.25 miles of Peñasco Creek and lower 0.25 miles of an unnamed western tributary, with a 25-ft riparian buffer on each side. Notably, **Yank's Spring/Hank and Yank Tank itself is excluded from critical habitat designation**.
- **Population data gap**: No quantitative estimates of Sonora chub abundance in the tank/spring area exist, representing a significant baseline data gap.
- **Repair history**: Two remediation attempts occurred — an unsuccessful internal plug attempt by Dr. Paul Marsh (Dec 1993) and a successful fitting-tightening fix by USFS staffer Tom Newman (late Dec 1993) — though underlying bank instability and pipe exposure remain unresolved long-term risks.
- **Cumulative/future risk**: Continued bank erosion and pipe exposure could destabilize the tank further; additional flood control/bank stabilization work may require Clean Water Act Section 404 permitting.
- **Conservation recommendations**: (1) Evaluate feasibility of maintaining the tank as refugia for Sonora chub and recovery habitat for leopard frog/Tarahumara frog species; (2) stabilize the tank while minimizing downstream sediment transport and pollutant discharge during construction; (3) maintain records of flood events affecting tank stability.
- **Regulatory status**: Sonora chub listed as **Threatened** in 1986 ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 1986]]); no substantial improvement in species status has occurred since listing, per this 1994 opinion.
- **Reference works**: Detailed species biology and management guidance available in the [[Sonora Chub Recovery Plan]] (Service 1992).
