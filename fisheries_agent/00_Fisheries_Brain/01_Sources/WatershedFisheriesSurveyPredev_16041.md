# Watershed Fisheries Survey: Predevelopment Surveys (Project F-7-R-9, Job R-4, 1966)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report (Project F-7-R-9, Work Plan 8, Job R-4, 1 January to 31 December 1966), authored by Louis H. Carufel, is the third and final year of predevelopment surveys on nine proposed or completed lakes and eighteen to nineteen streams. The study sought criteria for estimating the potential aquatic productivity of proposed [[Lake Fertility]] developments and for deciding where stream improvement is needed. The report proposes guidelines covering [[Water Quality]], [[Basin Morphometry]], [[Area Geology]], [[Soil Types]], [[Stream Flow]] and other factors, to be applied case by case. Stream recommendations are largely for trout management, with warm water options on some reaches.

## Background & Description

The Game and Fish Department had built or rebuilt 17 fishing lakes in recent years, with another under way. A minimum goal of 50 new lakes by the year 2000 was set to meet demand from a rapidly expanding population. Earlier reports (F-7-R-7 for 1964, F-7-R-8 for 1965) established that fish production correlates with water chemistry, morphometry, temperature, flow, geology, land use and biological factors, and that these factors estimate production better in combination than singly.

Objectives were to determine the need for, and estimate the results of, proposed fishery developments in two categories: (1) impoundments and (2) stream improvements.

Lake sites studied were [[Bear Canyon Lake]], [[Black Canyon Lake]], [[Blue Ridge Reservoir]], [[Chevelon Canyon Lake]], [[Knoll Lake]], [[Lynx Lake]], [[Parker Canyon Lake]], [[Willow Springs Lake]] and [[Woods Canyon Lake]]. Comparative data came from nine established lakes, including Ashurst, Big, Kinnikinick, Luna, Lyman, Upper Lake Mary, Pena Blanca, Rainbow and Show Low lakes.

Methods followed [[Limnological Methods]] described by Lagler (1956) and Welch (1948). Water chemistry used a Hach D.R. colorimeter, soils were analyzed by the University of Arizona Soils Laboratory, temperatures were logged with 33-day Ryan recording thermometers, and flow was measured with a Pigmy Gurley Current Meter. Base and flood flows were both sampled.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Proposed criteria for lake productivity** (guidelines, applied on the merits of each site):

- **Total alkalinity:** above 40 ppm appears best for production.
- **Phosphate and nitrogen:** above 0.5 ppm preferred.
- **Total soluble salts:** suggested above 200 ppm for soil and above 100 ppm for water (less sulfate reading). Soil samples can estimate production when water samples are unavailable.
- **Harmful elements:** heavy metals such as copper and iron may restrict plankton and fish production.
- **Shoal area:** 30% or greater at the 15-foot contour suggested.
- **Slope:** gradual or moderate slopes outperform steep basins.
- **Mean depth:** lakes under 25 feet tend to have greater shoal area and gradual slopes.
- **Elevation:** lakes above 6,000 feet suggested for trout.
- **Geology:** sites in rock formations with reasonable limestone, feldspar and basalt content.
- **Stream flow:** large inflow helps, but excessive inflow and outflow can dilute and flush nutrients; small drainages may have low mineral concentration but a build-up of nutrients from slow exchange.

**Thermal and flow dynamics.** Base flow (groundwater) carries less volume but more minerals than flood flow (surface runoff), which dominates inflow but has had less time to leach soil minerals. Thermoclines in six lakes sat roughly 9 to 28 feet deep in summer. [[Blue Ridge Reservoir]] drawdown for diversion disrupted stratification, while [[Chevelon Canyon Lake]] was affected by wind action.

**East Verde River thermal shift.** Pumping from Blue Ridge Reservoir cooled the East Verde in spring and summer (5 to 22 °F lower than 1965 in spring) and warmed it in fall (62 to 68 °F, 5 to 17 °F higher). Eight additional miles of the East Verde became suitable trout habitat. Continued study on long-term effects was recommended.

## Key Findings

**Water quality**
- All study areas were judged suitable for sustaining fish life.
- Five of nine lakes averaged total alkalinity of 43 to 82 ppm; the other four averaged 26 to 35 ppm. The higher-alkalinity lakes were Black Canyon, Blue Ridge Reservoir, Chevelon Canyon, Lynx and Parker Canyon.
- Kemmerer (1965) found Arizona lakes more productive at alkalinity above 85 ppm with total phosphate above 0.36 ppm; poor production occurred with calcium carbonate below 30 ppm and phosphate below 0.23 ppm.
- Total soluble salts in water were lower in 1966 than in the prior two years, possibly from heavy winter and spring runoff diluting nutrients.

**Morphometry and stratification**
- 1966 morphometric results matched 1965. Stratification was measured in six of nine lakes.

**Biological data (condition factors and growth)**
- [[Bear Canyon Lake]]: grayling planted May 1965 grew to 7.0 to 10.0 inches; cutthroat trout stocked September 1965 grew slowly (5.0 to 6.0 inches), possibly from grayling competition.
- [[Black Canyon Lake]]: rainbow trout condition factor 34.0 to 47.0; brook trout 32.0 to 56.0.
- [[Blue Ridge Reservoir]]: rainbow trout 32.0 to 41.0; brook trout 38.0 to 39.0.
- [[Knoll Lake]]: trout condition factor fair (33.0 to 35.0).
- [[Lynx Lake]]: trout condition factor poor to good (27.0 to 39.0).
- [[Parker Canyon Lake]]: trout condition factor 35.0 to 40.0.
- [[Woods Canyon Lake]]: trout condition factor 31.0 to 42.5.

**Streams (21 limnological surveys; management suggested for 15)**
- Ten streams suggested for rainbow trout, three for native trout (Gila trout, *Salmo gila*) and two for brown trout (*Salmo trutta*). Five suited warm water species; one needed more data.
- [[Ash Creek]]: 250 native trout stocked September 1965; 39 recaptured in 1966 (4.5 to 7.5 inches), about 70% had doubled length. Gabion structures recommended.
- [[Beaver Creek]]: rainbow and brown trout, suckers and speckled dace present. Brown trout suggested for the upper reach; bass for the lower reach.
- [[Black River]]: rainbow trout are most abundant trout; no significant limnological changes across sampled sections.
- [[West Clear Creek]]: 56 rainbow and 48 brown trout collected; access identified as the main limitation.
- [[East Clear Creek]]: flood scour extended about 1.5 miles below Blue Ridge Dam; 83 trout (72 rainbow, 11 brown) collected. Release of 0.5 to 2.0 acre-feet daily from Blue Ridge Reservoir suggested to sustain flow.
- [[Verde River]]: 989 fish counted; suckers 47%, red plains shiner 34%. Stream temperatures 68 to 73 °F. Warm water management suggested, including [[Northern Pike]].
- [[Trout Creek]]: electrofishing composition suckers 50%, speckled dace 25%, river chub or boneytail 25%. Channel catfish and spotted bass plants proposed.
- [[Marijilda Creek]]: rainbow trout (8.0 to 15.0 inches) and brook trout (6.0 to 10.0 inches) present; access identified as the main limitation.
- [[Salome Creek]]: poor watershed cover and access limit development; more data on temperature and flow needed.

**Overall conclusion:** no important changes were found in the criteria for estimating production at proposed lake sites, and the 1965 discussion (Carufel 1965) was deemed adequate for the 1966 report.
