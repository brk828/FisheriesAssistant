# Verde River Spikedace Surveys, Fall 2005

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 2006 transmittal letter from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) to Dr. Paul Marsh of [[Arizona State University]], enclosing the report of a collaborative survey for the endangered [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) on the upper [[Verde River]]. Surveys ran from October to November 2005 and covered the river from [[Granite Creek]] to just downstream of [[Sycamore Creek]]. The survey team included personnel from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], Arizona State University, the [[U.S. Bureau Of Reclamation]], the [[U.S. Forest Service]], and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, along with members of the public.

No spikedace were detected in any reach. Teams instead recorded presence or absence of all native and nonnative fish species, and the appended methods and data sheets standardize how future surveys should be conducted.

## Background & Description

**Purpose.** A September 21, 2005 meeting of the Arizona Game and Fish Department, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and U.S. Forest Service agreed on a concentrated survey of the upper Verde River. Five teams were formed to sample independently in fall 2005.

**Study area.** The upper mainstem was divided into five reaches:
- Reach 1: Granite Creek to [[Verde Ranch]]
- Reach 2: Verde Ranch to [[Bear Siding]] (not sampled, because no team was available)
- Reach 3: Bear Siding to [[Perkinsville]]
- Reach 4: Perkinsville to Sycamore Creek
- Reach 5: Downstream of Sycamore Creek

**Target species.** Spikedace is a small, endangered cyprinid. Its habitat is shallow, low-velocity water, and the survey focused on that habitat type. The species is the subject of recovery efforts, which is why the letter thanks participants for their "continued efforts towards recovery of endangered species."

**Field methods (Appendix A).** Each team followed a common protocol:
- **Seining** was the preferred gear. Seines had mesh of 1/8 inch or smaller, and each haul was limited to 1.5 to 2.0 times the seine width. Suitable areas were sampled with two passes, with extra passes if spikedace-associated species kept appearing.
- **Backpack electrofishing** was used where seining was not effective. Settings were kept low enough that fish recovered from tetany. Larger suckers and chub that were seen but not captured could be recorded as observed if identified with confidence.
- **Habitat targeting.** Teams concentrated on water about one meter deep or less, in shear zones along gravel sand bars, quiet eddies on the downstream edge of riffles, and broad shallow areas above gravel bars. All suitable habitat was sampled, not just representative reaches.
- **Data.** Teams recorded all species caught, both native and nonnative, along with seine haul counts, stream length, and shocking time. Individual counts per species were not recorded.

**Holding and transport.** If spikedace were captured, one person per team was to monitor them in holding containers, and fish were to be transported in aerated containers inside the vehicle to [[Bubbling Ponds]] hatchery. Staff there were to be notified of all survey dates.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey documented a fish community dominated by a few widespread species:

- **Native fishes** recorded across reaches included [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*). The [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) is listed in the data sheets but was not recorded.
- **Nonnative fishes** included [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), which was the most common species. Others were [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), and green sunfish.
- **Invertebrates and amphibians** noted included crayfish, including *Orconectes virilis* in one segment, and bullfrog tadpoles.

The survey also described the physical setting. Suitable spikedace habitat was interspersed with swift, deep water and slow, deep pools, which are unsuitable for the species. Water depth and velocity increased from upstream to downstream in Reach 3. Reach 5 was dominated by long, deep pools. Red shiners were present in virtually every suitable spikedace habitat, and fish numbers were generally low.

## Key Findings

1. **No spikedace were detected** in any reach by any survey team.
2. **Survey coverage:** Reach 2 was not sampled. Other reaches were sampled between October 19 and November 2, 2005.
3. **Seining effort by segment:**
   - Reach 5 (Sycamore Creek downstream, September 30, 2005): 63 seine hauls and about 1.0 mile of river surveyed.
   - Perkinsville to Sycamore Creek, Segment One (October 24, 2005): 43 seine hauls, with shocking time recorded, and about 5 miles surveyed.
   - Perkinsville to Sycamore Creek, Segment Two (October 25, 2005): 11 seine hauls, with 21 minutes 47 seconds of shocking, and about 5.0 miles surveyed.
   - Reach 3 (Bear Siding to Perkinsville, October 19 to 20, 2005): seine hauls not available.
   - Burnt Ranch to Forest Road 638 (November 1, 2005): 54 seine hauls and about 3.0 miles surveyed.
   - Granite Creek to Burnt Ranch (November 1, 2005): 45 seine hauls.
   - Forest Road 638 to Muldoon Canyon (November 2, 2005): 66 seine hauls, no shocking, and about 1.4 miles surveyed.
   - Muldoon Canyon to Verde (November 2, 2005): 26 seine hauls, no shocking, and about 2.1 miles surveyed. Very few fish were caught.
4. **Species richness:** Reach 4 supported the most species (9 total). Reach 3 supported the fewest native species (2).
5. **Habitat condition:** Most of the reach below Sycamore Creek lacked suitable habitat, and Reach 5 was surveyed only to the point where suitable habitat ended.
6. **Monitoring standard:** The standardized data sheets, methods, and transport protocol in the appendix are intended as the baseline for future spikedace surveys on the Verde River.
