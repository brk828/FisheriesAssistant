# Comparative Study of Physical-Chemical Variables of Streams Using Multivariate Analyses

#ichthyology #marine-biology #stream-ecology

*Note: The document contains no fish-specific data; the tags above reflect its freshwater ecosystem focus.*

## Executive Summary

The document combines two items: a short appendix reporting water-chemistry sampling from [[Lake Pamvotis]] in Ioannina, Greece (18 October 1979), and a multivariate study by Cushing et al. (1980) on physical-chemical variables from 34 stream stations on 26 streams. The stream study tests whether running waters can be grouped into discrete classes analogous to the [[Oligotrophy-Eutrophy Classification]] used for lakes. Cluster and [[Canonical Discriminant Analysis]] of 15 variables yielded five stream groups (ARID, ROCK, OREG, EAST, WOOD). Watershed area, phosphate, total dissolved solids, solar radiation, precipitation, channel-length-to-watershed ratio and litter input were the most influential variables. The authors conclude that a combined physical, chemical and biological classification is not yet feasible. No fish species are reported.

## Background & Description

**Lake Pamvotis sampling (appendix):** Samples were collected from the lake centre near Ioannina, under calm, sunny conditions, in cooperation with the authors of the wider project. Water was filtered, deep-frozen and analysed later by standard autoanalyzer methods at the Max-Planck-Institut für Limnologie. Dissolved oxygen, temperature and conductivity were measured in the field. Table 3 gives values for orthophosphate, total phosphorus, ammonium, nitrate and related parameters for several depths. The lake was described as very dense with *[[Microcystis Aeruginosa]]* down to about 1 m, and as rather eutrophic.

**Stream study (Cushing et al.):** The study grew out of [[Lotic Ecology]] research, which had lacked a generally accepted classification comparable to lake typologies. Earlier schemes were based on fish zonation (Thienemann, 1925), invertebrate indicators (Maitland, 1966), physical features (Ricker, 1934; Horton, 1945; Strahler, 1957) and combined criteria (Pennak, 1971). The authors used data from streams in Canada, Scotland, Sweden, Germany and the United States, and noted that biological variables could not be included because methods differed among the source studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Data set:** 15 physical-chemical variables from 34 stations on 26 streams. Variables include watershed area, base flows, gradient, storm frequency, precipitation, nitrate-nitrogen, phosphate-phosphorus, total dissolved solids, temperature fluctuation, and degree days. Table 1 lists station, location and data source for each stream.
- **Transformation:** Data were log-transformed as log(y+1) and standardised to zero mean and unit variance. Clustering used a nonhierarchical divisive procedure ([[McIntire Clustering Program]]). A five-cluster solution was chosen after partitioning into up to twelve clusters.
- **Regional contrasts:** [[ARID Stream Group]] sites (25–30) were high in solar radiation and phosphorus with low precipitation. [[ROCK Stream Group]] sites (2–5, 11) had greater base flows and precipitation and lower phosphorus. [[OREG Stream Group]] sites had low nitrate. [[EAST Stream Group]] and [[WOOD Stream Group]] sites (18 combined) shared small watersheds, low insolation and high litter input, and separated by base flow, nitrate and gradient.
- **Context:** The authors acknowledge the [[River Continuum Project]] as the framework for future work.

## Key Findings

**Lake Pamvotis (Table 3, 18 October 1979):**
- Lake is eutrophic without strong nutrient enrichment, and the water column is warm, consistent with other Greek lakes.
- Dense *Microcystis aeruginosa* bloom reaches about 1 m depth.
- Silica enrichment is noted as possibly from siliceous material in the catchment.

**Stream classification (Cushing et al. 1980):**
- Cluster analysis yielded five groups: ARID (6 sites), ROCK (5 sites), OREG (6 sites), EAST and WOOD (18 sites combined).
- The first canonical axis explains 83.54% of among-group variability, with strong positive loadings (>0.7) for watershed area, phosphate, total dissolved solids and solar radiation, and strong negative loadings (<−0.6) for annual precipitation, channel length to watershed area ratio and terrestrial litter input.
- The second axis explains 9.77% (reported as 9.8%), linked to maximum diurnal temperature fluctuation and nitrate-nitrogen.
- The third axis explains 4.82% (reported as 4.8%), linked to summer base flow.
- The first two axes together retain 93.31% of among-group variability; three axes retain 98.13%.
- The authors conclude a universal classification including biological variables is not yet possible. Biological data are needed, and a second phase with [[River Continuum Project]] sites in Pennsylvania, Michigan, Idaho and Oregon was planned.
