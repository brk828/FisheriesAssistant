# Growth of Game and Pan Fish in Massachusetts Lakes and Ponds (1946-1950)

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #Percidae #Centrarchidae #Esocidae #age-growth-studies

## Executive Summary

This report presents age and growth data for six species of warm-water game and pan fish collected from 155 ponds in eastern and north central [[Massachusetts]] during 1946-1950. A total of 1,850 individual fish were analyzed using scale reading techniques to determine age structure and growth rates. The study provides growth standards for [[Yellow Perch]], [[White Perch]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Chain Pickerel]], [[Largemouth Black Bass]], and [[Smallmouth Black Bass]]. Growth rates varied considerably among ponds, suggesting wide variation in pond balance and overcrowding conditions. The data support fisheries management recommendations including increasing the minimum legal length for [[Chain Pickerel]] from 12 to 14 inches.

## Background & Description

The study was conducted by the [[Massachusetts Division of Fisheries and Game]] as part of a comprehensive lake and pond fisheries survey program. Fish samples were collected using seines and gill nets. Each specimen was measured for total length (nearest 0.1 inch) and weight (nearest gram, converted to ounces). Scale samples were taken from standardized body regions for each species and mounted on cellulose acetate slides for age determination.

Age was determined by counting annuli (year-marks) on scales, which form due to seasonal variation in growth rates. During early growing season, rapid growth produces widely-spaced circuli; later in the season, retarded growth produces crowded circuli. The demarcation between years forms the annulus. This technique allowed researchers to establish age-specific length and weight data for each species.

The study built upon previous work analyzing 1,734 fish from 75 ponds surveyed between 1942-1945. Growth standards derived from pooling data across all lakes provide benchmarks for evaluating individual pond conditions, identifying overcrowding, and assessing management effectiveness.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study encompassed warm-water fish communities in Massachusetts lakes and ponds, representing diverse environmental conditions across 155 water bodies. Growth rate variation among ponds of the same species indicated corresponding variation in pond balance and fish population density.

[[Yellow Perch]] showed considerable growth variation across 112 ponds, with relatively few large individuals (only 7% exceeded 9.25 inches; 24 fish ≥12 inches from 14 ponds, with 9 from [[Lake Saltonstall]] in [[Haverhill]] alone). This scarcity of large perch suggests widespread overcrowding or harvest pressure.

[[White Perch]], a brackish water species, exhibited great variation in growth relative to age across 65 ponds, indicating variable success in freshwater adaptation and frequent overcrowding in many lakes.

[[Black Crappie]] reproduction was limited despite introductions into 140 ponds; evidence of successful reproduction and growth was obtained from only 54 ponds. Only 93 specimens were available for analysis.

[[Chain Pickerel]] distribution across age classes (34% two-year-olds, 21% three-year-olds, 29% four-year-olds, 16% five-year-olds) suggested that harvest rates were not excessive and that rapid growth compensated for current harvest levels. Fish over 2 pounds were relatively uncommon.

[[Largemouth Black Bass]], though officially stocked in only 40 of 251 surveyed ponds, were collected from 67 ponds, indicating extensive unofficial stocking. This non-native species showed little growth variation among ponds through age four.

[[Smallmouth Black Bass]] were better represented in net catches than largemouths (108 fish from 43 ponds), though this may reflect greater net vulnerability rather than true abundance.

## Key Findings

**[[Yellow Perch]]** (873 specimens from 112 ponds):
- Four- and five-year-old fish (age groups IV+ and V+) comprised 52% of sample
- Greatest average length increases (1.0 inch) occurred during both third and fourth years
- Greatest weight increase (1.8 ounces) occurred in sixth year
- Fish >9.25 inches were scarce (7% of collection)
- Only 24 fish ≥12 inches total length were found

**[[White Perch]]** (488 specimens from 65 ponds):
- Three- and four-year-old fish (8.1-8.9 inches, 4.3-5.9 ounces) comprised 42% of sample
- Greatest increases in length (2.0 inches) and weight (2.6 ounces) occurred between ages two and three
- During third year, fish grew from 6.1 to 8.1 inches and 1.7 to 4.3 ounces
- Weight approximately doubled while growing from 7.0 to 9.0 inches (based on 113 fish analyzed 1942-1950)

**[[Black Crappie]]** (93 specimens):
- Three-year-olds (7.9 inches, 4.0 ounces) comprised 46% of sample
- Greatest increases in length (1.3 inches) and weight (2.4 ounces) achieved during fourth growing season (III+ to IV+)
- Limited reproductive success: evidence found in only 54 of 140 stocked ponds

**[[Chain Pickerel]]** (252 specimens from 85 ponds):
- Fish aged 2-6 years comprised 89% of sample (34% age II+, 21% age III+, 29% age IV+, 16% age V+)
- Greatest increases in length (2.6 inches) and weight (6.3 ounces) occurred at age three (III+)
- Three-year-olds grew from 13.0 to 15.6 inches and 8.2 to 14.5 ounces
- Most efficient forage conversion occurred in fish 13-15.6 inches (1-3.5 inches above 12-inch minimum legal length)
- Growth from 12 to 15.2 inches accompanied by 119% weight increase
- **Management recommendation**: Increase minimum legal length from 12 to 14 inches to allow harvest during peak growth period and improve pan fish control
- Uniform age distribution suggests harvest rates not excessive; rapid growth compensates for current harvest

**[[Largemouth Black Bass]]** (155 specimens: 119 from 19 ponds in western/central Massachusetts, 36 from 21 additional ponds):
- Non-native species; collected from 67 ponds despite official stocking in only 40, indicating widespread unofficial stocking
- Fish through age four comprised 84% of sample
- Little growth variation evident among ponds or regions through age four

**[[Smallmouth Black Bass]]** (108 specimens from 43 ponds):
- One-year-old fish absent from collection (likely due to net selectivity)
- Greatest length increase (2.0 inches) during third year: 8.2 to 10.2 inches
- Greatest weight gain (10.9 ounces) apparently in sixth year (insufficient sample size for certainty)
- More easily caught in nets than [[Largemouth Black Bass]]

**Species Frequency** (1950 survey, 30 ponds):
1. [[Yellow Perch]]: 30 collections
2. [[Chain Pickerel]]: 28 collections
3. [[Pumpkinseed]]: 25 collections
4. [[Bluegill]]: 24 collections
5. [[Golden Shiner]]: 19 collections
6. [[Largemouth Black Bass]]: 16 collections
7. [[Brown Bullhead]] and [[White Sucker]]: 15 collections each

**Methodological Notes**:
- Scale reading technique using cellulose acetate imprints allowed precise age determination
- Annuli counting based on circuli spacing patterns reflecting seasonal growth variation
- Growth standards derived from pooled data enable evaluation of individual pond conditions
- Seine and gill net sampling methods have known deficiencies; more quantitative methods needed
- Creel census and tagging studies recommended for assessing harvest rates and population dynamics
