# Osteology and Relationships of Ceratioid Anglerfishes of the Family Oneirodidae, with a Review of the Genus Oneirodes Lütken

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Oneirodidae

## Executive Summary

This Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County Science Bulletin (No. 18, 1974, by Theodore W. Pietsch) presents a detailed osteological description of [[Oneirodes Acanthias]] as a basis for comparing nine of the 13 genera of the deep-sea ceratioid family [[Oneirodidae]]. It diagnoses the family and the genus [[Oneirodes]], provides keys to genera and species, and revises the genus. Of 23 recognized species, seven are described as new. The study also infers phenetic and phylogenetic relationships among genera from 30 osteological characters, examines distribution relative to water masses, and reviews the structure and function of the ceratioid esca (lure).

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Osteology and taxonomy of Oneirodidae, based on roughly 450 known metamorphosed male and female Oneirodes specimens, including all type material.
- **Family:** Oneirodidae is the largest and most diverse of the 11 ceratioid families, with 13 genera and nearly 50 species (over half of all described ceratioids). The genus count in the text is given as 33 ceratioid genera overall.
- **Genus Oneirodes:** Following the revision, 23 species are recognized. Eighteen nominal forms of the former *O. eschrichtii*-group were synonymized with [[Oneirodes Eschrichtii]]; four others (*O. bulbosus*, *O. anisacanthus*, *O. heteronema*, *O. theodoritissieri*) are valid. *O. inimicus* is a synonym of *O. carlsbergi*, and *O. thysanophorus* of *O. flagellifer*. The *O. eschrichtii*- and *O. flagellifer*-groups are not recognized; the *O. schmidti*-group is retained.
- **Diagnostic character:** Species are separated mainly by esca morphology, which is species-specific; male-based identification has not been achieved, so species are defined on females.
- **Bertelsen's prior work:** [[Bertelsen 1951]] (Dana Report 39) had listed 33 species, placing 28 in three groups (the *eschrichti*-, *flagellifer*- and *schmidti*-groups).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Depth:** Members occupy 300–3000 m but are commonly found between about 800 and 1500 m. No evidence of diel vertical migration was found.
- **Distribution:** Contrary to earlier thought, species are not cosmopolitan. Most are confined to discrete areas defined by physico-chemical and biological parameters, consistent with recognized [[Zoogeographic Regions]] for midwater organisms. Only *O. eschrichtii* has a nearly cosmopolitan range.
- **Water masses:** Nine of 12 well-known species are limited to a single water mass; three cross boundaries. Examples: *O. thompsoni* and *O. bulbosus* in Pacific Subarctic Water; *O. acanthias*, *O. basili* and a southern California population of *O. eschrichtii* in Transition Water of the California Current; *O. notius* in Subantarctic Water.
- **Ontogenetic migration:** Larvae live in upper 200 m; metamorphosed stages descend, with females most numerous between 1500–2000 m. The authors suggest ontogenetic vertical migration in [[Ocean Current Gyres]] and countercurrents helps maintain restricted distributions. The *O. notius* length-latitude relationship is cited as support.
- **Sympatry:** Sympatric forms are most common in the eastern tropical Pacific and eastern tropical Atlantic.

## Key Findings

**Osteology**
- The description of *O. acanthias* covers cranium, mandibular, palatine, hyoid and opercular arches, branchial arches, vertebrae (19–21 centra in 24 specimens), caudal skeleton, fins and pectoral girdle. Skin spines are present only in *Oneirodes*.
- The genus is characterized by a pharyngobranchial of the first arch, a coracoid with a posteroventral process, and microscopic skin spines.

**Systematics**
- *Oneirodes* is considered the least derived oneirodid genus, close to the ancestral form of the family. [[Oneirodes Luetkeni]], *O. carlsbergi* and *O. rosenblatti* are the least derived species. Members of the *O. schmidti*-group are the most derived.
- Key genus-level evolutionary trends: elongation and depression of jaws, palatine arch and cranium; shortening of the hyoid arch; widening of the illicial trough; anterior displacement of frontals. [[Dolopichthys]] and [[Bertella]] show the extreme elongation state; [[Pentherichthys]] the extreme illicial widening.
- *Chaenophryne*, *Pentherichthys* and *Lophodolos* are each distinct and show little relationship to other oneirodids. Phenetic similarity is highest between [[Danaphryne]] and [[Microlophichthys]] (87%) and between *Dolopichthys* and *Bertella* (87%).
- Four genera (*Phyllorhinichthys*, *Tyrannophryne*, *Chirophryne*, *Ctenochirichthys*) are known only from one or two adolescent specimens and were not examined osteologically.

**Taxonomic highlights**
- *Oneirodes acanthias* (147 females, 11.5–167.0 mm) is resurrected as a valid species; [[Oneirodes Thompsoni]] was reinstated from synonymy with *O. acanthias*.
- *O. eschrichtii* (52 females, 10.0–213.0 mm) has a disjunct, morphologically distinct southern California population; the 213 mm specimen was a ripe female.
- Seven new species: *O. rosenblatti*, *O. kreffti*, *O. myrionemus*, *O. macrosteus*, *O. notius*, *O. dicromischus*, *O. basili*.
- The species list in the key includes *O. macronema* (single specimen, 27.0 mm) and *O. melanocauda* (known only from larvae), with the latter possibly representing an undescribed ceratioid genus.

**Esca function**
- The esca is a luring apparatus unique to Ceratioidei: it attracts prey through bioluminescence (symbiotic Gram-negative bacteria, per [[O'Day 1972]]), senses nearby organisms through innervation, and moves via illicial muscles.
- Two proposed functions: attraction of prey and attraction of a species-specific male.
- Experiments on shallow-water [[Antennariidae]] showed escal regeneration with morphological fidelity; the author infers deep-sea anglerfishes can also regenerate escae.

**Sampling methods**
- Most collections used nonclosing midwater nets (e.g., 10-ft [[Isaacs-Kidd Midwater Trawl]]), so capture depth is uncertain; vertical distributions were estimated from maximum fishing depth.
