## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) letter of concurrence responding to a U.S. Forest Service request for Section 7 consultation under the Endangered Species Act regarding the proposed **Tenney Timber Sale Improvement Project** on the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests (Apache and Greenlee Counties, Arizona). The letter does not present original field data, population surveys, or morphological/ecological research; rather, it documents a regulatory determination on project effects to three federally listed species: the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (terrestrial, non-fish), the [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), and the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). The Service concurred with the Forest Service's determination that the project "is not likely to adversely affect" the Apache Trout and would have "no effect" on the Loach Minnow, contingent on specific project constraints (no road construction, foot-based hand-thinning, and a buffer zone around watercourses).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no direct habitat-use data for the fish species are given, the letter implies critical ecological context through the mitigation measures required to protect aquatic habitat from upland timber management activities:

- **Riparian Buffer Zone**: No thinning permitted within 132 feet of either side of any drainage depicted as a watercourse on USGS 1:24,000 topographic quadrangle maps, indicating recognized sensitivity of stream/riparian corridors to sediment and disturbance from forestry operations.
- **Watershed Context**: The species inhabit or are associated with drainages within the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, a montane forest ecosystem in eastern Arizona — habitat historically occupied by native [[Apache Trout]] populations (Little Colorado River and White Mountains drainages) and [[Loach Minnow]] (Gila River basin tributaries).
- **Threat Pathway Considered**: Potential indirect effects to aquatic habitat from ground disturbance, road use, and vegetation removal (sedimentation, altered hydrology) associated with the timber sale, mitigated by the project's design constraints (no new roads, existing-road-only vehicle travel, hand-tool thinning by foot crews).
- **Co-occurring Species Considered**: The consultation jointly addresses a terrestrial apex/indicator species (Mexican Spotted Owl) and two threatened aquatic species, reflecting the multi-taxa environmental review required under the [[Endangered Species Act]] for forest management projects affecting mixed terrestrial-aquatic ecosystems.

## Key Findings

- The Service **concurred** with the Forest Service's "is not likely to adversely affect" determination for the **Mexican Spotted Owl**, based on "Dispersal Habitat Rule" calculations, judging effects as insignificant.
- The Service **concurred** with a finding of "is not likely to adversely affect" for the **Apache Trout** (*Oncorhynchus apache*), a threatened species.
- The Service concluded there would be **"no effect"** on the **Loach Minnow** (*Tiaroga cobitis*), a threatened species.
- These aquatic species findings were based on **verbal (unconfirmed in writing)** project information and the following key assumptions/conditions:
  1. No roads will be constructed or repaired for the project.
  2. All vehicle travel restricted to standard 2- or 4-wheel drive vehicles on **existing roads only**.
  3. Thinning performed by **hand-held chainsaws**, operated by crews working on-foot from vehicles on existing roads.
  4. **No thinning within 132 feet** of either side of any watercourse depicted on USGS 1:24,000 topographic maps.
- The Service noted that if any of these assumptions proved incorrect, additional **informal or formal consultation** could be required — flagging the contingent, assumption-dependent nature of the "no adverse effect" findings.
- Because the effect determinations were "no effect" or concurrence with "not likely to adversely affect," **formal consultation was not legally required**; the Alpine Ranger District (Forest Service) confirmed it did not wish to pursue formal consultation, and the original June 3, 1994 request for formal consultation was formally recorded as **withdrawn**.
- No population abundance, distribution, or habitat quality survey data for Apache Trout or Loach Minnow are presented in this letter — it is purely a regulatory/administrative concurrence document, useful primarily as a historical record of ESA compliance process rather than a source of primary species data.
