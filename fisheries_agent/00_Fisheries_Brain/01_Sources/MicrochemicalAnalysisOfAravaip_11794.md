# Microchemical Analysis of Aravaipa Creek and Adjacent Aquatic Habitats

## Executive Summary
This quarterly progress report (October 1976) documents a microchemical water quality investigation of [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona, and its tributary canyons, conducted in collaboration with ecological fieldwork led by Dr. W. Minckley. The study is **not a fish-specific investigation**; rather, it characterizes dissolved and total (sediment-bound) trace element concentrations across the creek and adjoining drainages (Turkey Creek, Hell Hole Canyon, Horse Camp Canyon, Virgus Canyon) to inform land and water use management. Because Aravaipa Creek is a well-documented refugium for native desert fishes, this geochemical baseline data is ecologically relevant to habitat quality assessment for the resident aquatic fauna, even though no fish specimens or taxa were sampled or analyzed in this report. Key findings show that while dissolved element concentrations in the mainstem creek met drinking/recreational water standards, several tributaries (notably [[Hell Hole Canyon]] and [[Turkey Creek]]) exhibited anomalously high dissolved and total metal concentrations, some attributable to mining influence and storm-driven sediment transport.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **System type**: Perennial desert stream ([[Aravaipa Creek]]) draining into intermittent/ephemeral tributary canyons within the Gila River basin of Arizona.
- **Habitat structure**: Six mainstem sampling sites spanning elevations from 2490–3225 ft, plus six tributary/seep sites (3375–2850 ft) including [[Turkey Creek]], [[Hell Hole Canyon]], [[Horse Camp Canyon]], and [[Virgus Canyon]].
- **Water quality gradient**: Dissolved element concentrations in the Aravaipa mainstem remained within recommended drinking/recreational standards, suggesting suitable baseline conditions for aquatic life. However, tributary inputs (iron-rich seeps, manganese enrichment, sodium-rich seeps) episodically degrade local water quality, which could stress sensitive native fish populations known to inhabit this drainage.
- **Sediment dynamics**: Precipitation runoff substantially elevated total (particulate-bound) metal loads at downstream sites (e.g., total iron rose ~30× at the Turkey Creek confluence, reaching 5850 mg/L), indicating strong hydrologic coupling between storm events, sediment transport, and trace metal bioavailability in this habitat — a factor relevant to fish and invertebrate exposure risk.
- **Mining influence**: Hell Hole Canyon showed extreme total metal concentrations (e.g., total Pb 5500 µg/L, total Mn 21,600 µg/L, total Zn 8670 µg/L), with metals elevated up to 1000× dissolved levels, strongly implicating historical or active mine drainage as a contamination source entering the Aravaipa system.

## Key Findings
- **Sampling design**: Water sampled once (Oct 9–11, 1976) in duplicate at each of 6 mainstem and 6 tributary/seep sites; analyzed for Ca, Mg, Na, K, Cr, Cu, Cd, Fe, Mn, Pb, and Zn via flame/flameless atomic absorption spectrophotometry, following USEPA (1974) extraction protocols.
- **Dissolved vs. total fractions**: Both dissolved (0.45 µm filtered) and total extractable (acid-digested) element concentrations were measured, allowing differentiation of soluble vs. particulate/sediment-bound metal pools.
- **Mainstem water quality**: No dissolved elements in Aravaipa Creek exceeded recommended drinking or recreational water standards (EPA, 1972).
- **Tributary anomalies**:
  - [[Horse Camp Canyon]]: Elevated dissolved iron exceeding public water supply standards; total sodium ~5× dissolved sodium.
  - [[Turkey Creek]] (source and seep near confluence): High dissolved iron; excessive manganese at both sampled sites; large sodium concentration at the seep.
  - [[Hell Hole Canyon]]: Extreme total metal loads (Cu, Cr, Cd, Fe, Mn, Pb, Zn), with Pb, Mn, Fe, and Cd elevated >1000× dissolved levels, Cu and Zn >100×, and Cr ~10× — interpreted as evidence of mine-derived contamination.
- **Spatial patterns in Aravaipa mainstem**:
  - Total iron varied strongly by site and storm timing: 300 mg/L (Site 1, upper reach) to 5850 mg/L (Site 2, below Turkey Creek) after runoff; downstream sites below Hell Hole Canyon and Virgus Canyon measured ~200 mg/L (pre-runoff arrival), while Site 5 (below Virgus Canyon mouth) and Site 6 (Sycamore Tree, 700 mg/L) reflected differential runoff arrival timing.
  - Manganese followed a pattern parallel to iron, with Sites 2 and 5 showing concentrations ~10× higher than other mainstem sites; copper, chromium, and lead were similarly elevated at these two sites.
- **General trend**: Total element concentrations consistently exceeded dissolved concentrations throughout the system, consistent with the relative insolubility of most monitored elements under alkaline stream conditions — indicating that the majority of trace metal loading in Aravaipa Creek is particulate/sediment-bound rather than truly dissolved.
- **Management implication**: Precipitation runoff events and specific tributary inputs (especially those influenced by mining, i.e., Hell Hole Canyon) can substantially and rapidly alter trace element loading in Aravaipa Creek, a finding directly relevant to land and water use management decisions for this ecologically significant desert stream system, which is noted elsewhere in the broader Minckley research program as critical habitat for native [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]].
