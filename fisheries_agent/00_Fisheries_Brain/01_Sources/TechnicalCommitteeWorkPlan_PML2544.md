# Technical Committee Work Plan for Threatened and Endangered Fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a work plan for the [[Technical Committee]] of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee]]. It guides the committee and its sub-committees in preparing and integrating the hydrologic and biological analyses needed to develop a [[Conservation Plan]] for threatened or endangered fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The plan must fully maintain state [[Water Rights]] systems and the [[Colorado River Compact]] and its tributary compacts. The work is organized into four phases, divided between a [[Hydrology Subcommittee]] and a [[Biology Subcommittee]], with iterative exchange between them. The document contains no species list, site data or numerical results; its content is procedural.

## Background & Description

The parties to the Upper Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee agreed to cooperate on ways to conserve threatened or endangered fishes while preserving water rights and compact obligations. They anticipated drafting a conservation plan that achieves both aims.

The work plan defines the major activities for each sub-committee and states, in general terms, what is to be accomplished, for what purpose, by whom, and by when. Responsibility for executing the plan rests with the Technical Committee's "steering group." Each sub-committee may prepare a more specific work plan, provided it is consistent with the overall plan. The document is marked as a draft.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fishes in question are described only as "threatened or endangered" and are not named. The study area is defined by stream reaches of special concern, including occupied and unoccupied reaches important to the fishes and their migratory routes, along with the reservoirs that regulate flow. The planned analyses cover:

- **Hydrologic parameters:** flow, chemistry and temperature characteristics of the identified reaches.
- **Habitat and life-cycle requirements:** characteristics that may be important to the specific needs of the fish, to be confirmed through a review of existing conclusions.
- **Water demand and reservoir operations:** future depletions and operating criteria that could affect the hydrology of these reaches.
- **Historical conditions:** baseline hydrology under 1960 and 1980 development conditions.

The work plan treats hydrology and biology as linked. Future flow and temperature conditions are to be judged against fish population needs. The plan also considers the economic effects of reservoir operation on power generation and water users.

## Key Findings

The document sets out a four-phase plan, with its main elements summarized below.

**Phase 1: Hydrologic Modeling and Future Conditions (Hydrology Subcommittee, with Biology input)**
1. Identify stream reaches of special concern, including occupied, unoccupied and migratory reaches.
2. For these reaches, specify the hydrologic parameters to be analyzed.
3. Review the available hydrologic models and select a single model or a combination. Changes to the models, including biological and area constraints, may be needed. Select the reaches and reservoirs to model, the period(s) of record for input data, and confirm that input hydrology is compatible across models (for example, virgin versus modified flows).
4. Project future water demands on a monthly basis. Make projections for the year 2000 for river basins above each critical reach, and for high, medium and low levels of depletion beyond 2000. These depletion levels are not additive.
5. Model future hydrologic conditions for the selected reaches, summarize the results in writing, and interpret them with the biology sub-committee. This step may be iterative.

**Phase 2: Historical Hydrologic Conditions (Hydrology Subcommittee)**
1. Characterize virgin flow conditions using the natural flow estimates in the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]]'s [[CRSS Model]].
2. Characterize hydrology assuming 1960 development conditions, including reservoir operations as of 1960, remained constant over time.
3. Repeat this for 1980 development conditions.
4. Prepare written summary comparisons of the three conditions for the Biology Subcommittee.

**Phase 3: Assessment of Biological Information (Biology Subcommittee)**
1. Identify data needs, determine what essential data exist, and assess confidence in them. This covers the characteristics and reliability of each data base, where the data are located, and whether data sets can be combined or made comparable. Results go into a written document.
2. Review all conclusions on the fishes' habitat and life-cycle requirements to confirm what is and is not known, and rate the certainty of each conclusion. Select the parameters to analyze, decide how much analysis is needed for useful judgments, and summarize in writing.
3. Identify and analyze all potential means of conservation. This includes:
   - a comprehensive list of possible flow and non-flow alternatives;
   - an analysis of whether currently available data support each alternative and what would be needed to implement it;
   - an assessment of how likely each alternative is to achieve the desired result.

**Phase 4: Development of a Conservation Plan (both sub-committees)**
1. Using the Phase 1 to 3 results, the Biology Subcommittee assesses whether the fish population would be sufficient or insufficient under projected future flows. It also assesses whether temperature variation is adequate or inadequate. Where conditions are inadequate, the sub-committees identify alternatives to correct them and any research needs. The evaluation is iterative between the two sub-committees.
2. The Hydrology Subcommittee identifies conflicts with state water rights systems or compact allocations, and the economic impacts of alternative reservoir operations and streamflow levels on power operations and water users.
3. Draft a conservation plan that uses the alternatives identified in step 2 to conserve the fishes while maintaining state water rights and the compacts on the Colorado River and its tributaries.

**Caveats**
- The phase divisions are for organizational convenience. The document states that Phase 2 need not wait for Phase 1 to finish. Some tasks are sequential or iterative, and the work plan will need close monitoring as the effort progresses.
- Sub-committee work plans must remain consistent with this overall plan.
