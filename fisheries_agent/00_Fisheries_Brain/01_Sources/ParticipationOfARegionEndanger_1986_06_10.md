## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (June 10, 1986) rather than a primary research paper, but it contains administratively significant data regarding the conservation status and management structure for the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularis*). The memo announces the establishment of the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT)** by FWS Region 2, formed in response to the recent federal listing of the desert pupfish as an endangered species. Because the species' range spans both Region 1 and Region 2 jurisdictions, the memo requests that Region 1 nominate a biologist (proposed: Ed Lorentzen, Sacramento Field Office) to serve as a team consultant or member, ensuring coordinated recovery planning across regional boundaries.

## Key Findings
- The **desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularis*)** was recently federally listed as an endangered species, prompting the need for a coordinated interregional recovery strategy.
- FWS Region 2 established the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT)** to: 
  1. Draft new recovery plans for listed species lacking them,
  2. Revise outdated recovery plans,
  3. Advise the Service on implementation of recovery actions, and
  4. Serve as an expert resource on issues affecting listed/proposed southwestern fish species.
- Because *C. macularis* populations occur in **both Region 1 (California) and Region 2**, effective recovery planning requires cross-regional collaboration.
- Region 2 proposed that DFRT oversee desert pupfish recovery, contingent upon Region 1 designating a representative — reflecting an institutional model for **multi-jurisdictional endangered species recovery planning**.
- **Ed Lorentzen** (Sacramento Field Office) was nominated to serve as a consultant or team member, tasked specifically with contributing to the California-based population and habitat sections of the desert pupfish recovery plan.
- The correspondence involved key figures in southwestern desert fish conservation, including **Dr. W.L. Minckley**, a prominent authority on [[Desert Fishes]] biology, underscoring the scientific credibility embedded in the DFRT's advisory structure.
- This memo exemplifies the **administrative and interagency coordination processes** underlying endangered species recovery planning under the U.S. Endangered Species Act, distinct from direct field or laboratory research data.
