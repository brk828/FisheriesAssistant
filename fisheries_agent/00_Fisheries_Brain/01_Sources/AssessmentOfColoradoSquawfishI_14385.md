## Executive Summary

This FY94 Annual Project Report for the Colorado River Recovery Program presents findings from a 1992–1993 study assessing the distribution, movement, and habitat use of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the White River, Colorado/Utah. Using radio telemetry and mark-recapture methods, researchers documented extremely high densities of wild adult squawfish congregating in the tailrace waters below [[Taylor Draw Dam]] and above [[Kenney Reservoir]]. The study confirmed long-distance spawning migrations between the White River and the Green/Yampa river spawning grounds, along with strong home-range fidelity. Concurrent fish community sampling revealed a concerning long-term shift from endemic to non-native (exotic) species dominance downstream of the dam.

## Background & Description

The report does not provide detailed morphometric data (e.g., length-weight relationships) but notes the species is a large-bodied, long-distance migratory cyprinid. Individuals were large enough to be surgically implanted with radio transmitters, indicating adult body sizes typical of mature *Ptychocheilus lucius*, one of the largest North American minnows. All 12 tagged fish were classified as wild adults, distinguishing them from the hatchery-reared juveniles that were a secondary (but largely unmet) study focus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary Habitat Use**: Main channel run habitat was preferred across fall, winter, spring, and summer months.
- **Cover Type**: Cutbanks were the dominant habitat cover used year-round.
- **Substrate**: Gravel substrate was the primary substrate type occupied across all seasons sampled.
- **Depth Preferences** (seasonal averages):
  - Fall: 240 cm
  - Winter: 413 cm
  - Spring: 123 cm
  - Summer: 191 cm
- **Temperature Ranges** (no winter data collected):
  - Fall: 7–18°C
  - Spring: 12–14°C
  - Summer: 15–28°C
- **Migration Ecology**: Adult squawfish exhibited long-distance spawning migrations from the White River to spawning sites in the [[Green River]] (Desolation Canyon) and [[Yampa River]] (Yampa Canyon), averaging 644 km round-trip (range 438–745 km) over ~97 days (range 46–167 days).
  - Migration initiation occurred ~32 days (range 6–50 d) after spring runoff began (14 May), at ~14°C water temperature.
  - Yampa Canyon spawning arrival: ~23 July (range 9 July–12 August), 38 days (range 24–58 d) post-migration start.
  - Desolation Canyon spawning arrival: ~30 July (range 21 July–12 August), 45 days (range 36–58 d) post-migration start.
  - Return to origin: ~17 September (range 16 August–29 October), 97 days (range 46–167 d) total.
- **Home Range Fidelity**: All 12 radio-tagged fish returned to or near their pre-migration home range below Taylor Draw Dam and above Kenney Reservoir, supporting strong site fidelity in this population.
- **Community Context**: Species composition sampling documented a shift from endemic-dominated to exotic-dominated fish assemblages in the White River below Taylor Draw Dam, a pattern consistent with previous studies and indicative of ecosystem-level changes tied to impoundment.

## Key Findings

1. **Population Density**: Mark-recapture population estimates indicated 26 wild adult Colorado squawfish (1992) and 37 (1993) within a 0.4 km reach below Taylor Draw Dam — among the highest recorded densities in the Upper Colorado River Basin.
2. **Artificial Carrying Capacity**: Taylor Draw Dam tailrace waters appeared to create an artificially high carrying capacity for squawfish; the 1993 population decline was linked to fish redistributing downstream in response to turbine operations.
3. **Radio Telemetry Results**: 12 wild adult squawfish were radio-tagged (6 in Sept/Oct 1992, 6 in April 1993). All 12 fish undertook long-distance spawning migrations into the Green and Yampa rivers and successfully returned to the White River.
   - 7 fish migrated north into the Green/Yampa rivers.
   - 5 fish migrated south into the Desolation Canyon area of the Green River.
   - 2 fish were recaptured in Kenney Reservoir, having moved upriver within 10 days of release, suggesting potential for fish passage utilization.
4. **Fish Passage Feasibility**: Findings support the feasibility of a fish passage facility at Taylor Draw Dam, as squawfish demonstrated strong motivation to access historic habitat above Kenney Reservoir.
5. **Species Composition Shifts**:
   - 1992: Utah portion of White River — 26% endemic / 74% exotic; Colorado portion — 56% endemic / 44% exotic (n=2,369 fish captured).
   - 1993: Utah portion — 45% endemic / 55% exotic; Colorado portion — 63% endemic / 37% exotic (n=1,975 fish captured).
   - Colorado squawfish comprised less than 1.0% of total catch in both years.
6. **Management Recommendations**:
   - Maintain Taylor Draw Dam operations to preserve high squawfish densities below the dam.
   - Investigate fish passage feasibility given demonstrated upstream movement potential.
   - Continue radio telemetry monitoring (transmitters operable through September 1994) to assess squawfish response to newly installed hydroelectric turbine operations.
   - Monitor species composition changes below the dam related to potential prey base impacts from altered flow regimes.
7. **Study Limitation**: A lack of available juvenile hatchery-reared Colorado squawfish prevented full assessment of hatchery-fish presence, abundance, growth, and habitat use objectives.
