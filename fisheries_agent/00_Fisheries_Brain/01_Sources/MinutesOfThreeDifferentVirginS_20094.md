# Virgin Spinedace Conservation Coordinating Team: Meeting Minutes and Correspondence (1994–1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a faxed cover sheet and three draft minutes from meetings of the [[Virgin Spinedace]] Conservation Coordinating Team, transmitted by the [[Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund]] on February 9, 1995. The meetings (July 7, September 30, and August 31, 1994) brought together state, federal, university, local water-district, and land-management representatives to set minimum instream flow targets, identify habitat enhancement sites, and draft a [[Conservation Agreement]] for the species in the [[Virgin River]] basin. The discussions centered on restoring roughly 80 percent of historical habitat, defining channel-forming flows, managing non-native species, and reconciling water-supply needs with recovery goals.

## Background & Description

The minutes document an ongoing, multi-agency effort to conserve the Virgin spinedace, a small cyprinid fish of the Virgin River system. The participating organizations included:

- [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (multiple staff)
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]
- [[Bureau of Land Management]]
- [[Washington County Water Conservancy District]]
- [[Utah State University]] (Dr. Thomas Hardy, Bryan Atkinson, Craig Addley)
- [[Nevada Department of Fish and Game]] and Nevada Department of Conservation and Natural Resources
- [[Arizona Department of Fish and Game]]
- [[National Park Service]]

The fax cover indicates the correspondence was sent to "Dr. Minckley" and was the first page of minutes from three meetings. Participant lists are summarized here rather than reproduced in full.

Note on sources: the scanned text contains OCR errors. Some numeric values (notably the mileage remaining after the priority reaches) are garbled and are not reported with certainty. The August 31 meeting header reads "1996" in the scan, but the document footer and sequence indicate 1994.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow regime and channel maintenance**
- The team considered channel-forming flows essential to maintaining channel characteristics and integrity in the basin.
- The group agreed that a conceptual approach to determining channel-forming flows, with periodic re-evaluation over a 2–10 year period, might suit the Virgin River basin. This approach drew on a conceptual article by Hill et al. (1991) on instream and out-of-channel flows.
- Peak flows were considered natural and should not be captured or diverted.
- Current flows below Gunlock were found to be exceptions rather than the norm for the basin as a whole.

**Water diversions and depleted reaches**
- The [[Quail Creek Diversion]] reach was identified as problematic for inclusion in the conservation strategy because of limited water availability.
- Other diversion and reservoir features mentioned include the [[Washington Fields Diversion]], the [[Johnson Diversion]], and [[Schroeder Reservoir]].

**Non-native species**
- [[Green Sunfish]] in the upper [[Santa Clara River]] were identified as a problem and a potential benefit for inclusion in the conservation agreement.
- Stocking of trout at Schroeder Reservoir raised potential conflicts that needed revised wording.

## Key Findings

**1. Recovery target and priority reaches (July 7, 1994)**
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Henry Maddux) proposed a feasible habitat conservation target of 80 percent of historical levels.
- Achieving this target would require approximately 24 miles of restored habitat, including several key areas.
- Priority areas were identified as:
  - Below the Quail Creek Diversion to Pah Tempe.
  - Santa Clara River below Gunlock to its confluence with the Virgin River.
- Flows from the Washington Fields Diversion downstream to the Johnson Diversion, and the area below Schroeder Reservoir, were also considered capable of contributing to the target. Flows combined with non-native species control were considered alongside these measures.

**2. Flow definitions and methodology (September 30, 1994)**
- Team members discussed the definition and significance of high flows, and emphasized that the target flows were a starting point rather than fixed figures.
- The team agreed to remove the target high-flow column from the draft flow table and place it in the Conservation Agreement text.

**3. Empirical minimum flow analysis (August 31, 1994)**
- [[Craig Addley]] of Utah State University presented empirical analyses relating [[Virgin Spinedace]] population estimates to channel characteristics and flows. The analyses covered both adult and young-of-the-year fish.
- USU provided a written rationale for its draft site-specific flow recommendations.
- The team reviewed the minimum instream flow recommendation table and finalized initial recommended target minimum flows for specific reaches. The table was attached to the minutes.
- Language designating some sites as "priority" was flagged for critical review in the next draft. Participants stressed that priority designations and strategies are flexible and would be addressed during the three-year implementation period.

**4. Habitat enhancement sites (August 31, 1994)**
The team identified the following reaches for evaluation of potential enhancement:
- Santa Clara River above and below Gunlock Reservoir
- Lower North Creek
- Lower Leverkin Creek
- Virgin River above the Sheep Bridge Crossing
- Lower Santa Clara River
- Lower Ash Creek
- Virgin River (general)

Bob Douglas, Rick Friedlett, and Sheridan Hanson were tasked with identifying potential problems, problem statements, and a general approach for these reaches.

**5. Process and next steps**
- Comments on the August draft were due to Leo Lentsch by August 24. The team generally considered the draft close to final, and only a few members provided formal comments.
- The team agreed to critically examine the "priority" language in the next draft.
- Strategies in the Conservation Plan were acknowledged to be adaptable over the three-year implementation period.
