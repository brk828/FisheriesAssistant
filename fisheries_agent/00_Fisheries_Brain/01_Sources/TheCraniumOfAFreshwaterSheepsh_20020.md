# A Sheepshead from Postglacial Marl: A Semifossil Cranium of Aplodinotus grunniens from Burt Lake, Michigan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aplodinotus

## Executive Summary

[[Carl L. Hubbs]] reports a well-preserved semifossil cranium of the [[Freshwater Sheepshead]] (*Aplodinotus grunniens*) found while digging a boat well at [[Burt Lake]], [[Cheboygan County]], Michigan, in summer 1937. The skull was buried in postglacial marl, and the author argues it provides the first concrete, objective evidence that the species once occurred in the waters of Burt Lake or one of its postglacial precursors. Specialists agree the fish lived in postglacial time, and the marl and root-fiber evidence suggest shallow water, most plausibly during the [[Lake Nipissing]] phase. The find supports the hypothesis that the species penetrated northward through the [[Inland Waterway]] when it was a broad, shallow, turbid strait connecting [[Lake Michigan]] and [[Lake Huron]].

## Background & Description

**Discovery and provenance.** The cranium was uncovered by J. H. Greenman about thirty feet from the water's edge and six feet below the surface of level ground, lying under about two feet of black muck or turf and about four feet of marl, directly on solid red clay. Traces of white marl remain in some skull cavities. The site is on [[Greenman Point]], near the north end of the east shore of Burt Lake (Section 14, T. 36 N., R. 3 W., Burt Township). The superficial muck was identified in an unpublished soil survey as Edwards muck associated with Granby sand.

**Specimen.** The skull measures 156 mm from the tip of the vomer to the end of the supraoccipital crest, which is large compared with typical present-day adults. Bones of the species from Indian burials and middens show that much larger sizes were attained in prehistoric times. A Recent cranium from [[Lake Erie]] (122 mm long, illustrated on Plate I) differs only slightly. The thicker, coarser bone of the fossil is attributed to age, estimated at about twenty years from winter lines on membrane bones. Bilateral asymmetry and Recent variation make foramen and sculpture differences of little significance.

**Custody.** The specimen was deposited at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] by its discoverer, after [[Max M. Peet]] recognized its scientific value.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Age and lake correlation.** Consulted specialists, including [[Frank Leverett]], [[George M. Stanley]], P. S. Welch, and Lee R. Schoenmann, agreed the fish lived in postglacial time, probably many centuries ago. Stanley favored correlation with [[Lake Nipissing]] because marl and peat of Nipissing age occur near present lake levels, while such deposits are scarce in [[Lake Algonquin]] sediments. Root or stem fibers penetrating the foramina indicate shallow water, which rules out both high Algonquin stages.

**Biogeography.** The sheepshead is described as an essentially southern species (Barney, 1926). The author recorded it as far south as the Rio de la Pasión, a tributary of the Río Usumacinta in El Petén, Guatemala. In the Great Lakes region it is common in southern [[Lake Michigan]], [[Lake Erie]], and [[Saginaw Bay]] of [[Lake Huron]]. A single published record exists for the mouth of the Au Sable River. Commercial catch records show occasional northward straggling to the Beaver Islands, northern Lake Huron shores in Michigan, and St. Martin Bay near the [[Straits of Mackinac]].

**Paleoenvironment.** During most of postglacial time the Inland Waterway was a strait linking Lakes Michigan and Huron (Leverett and Taylor, 1915). The author describes it as broad, shallow, and probably turbid, conditions favorable to the species. The species presently occurs farther north in cool, shallow, often turbid waters. Its use of the southern outlets of the glacial Great Lakes is confirmed by this find (Barney, 1926; Greene, 1935).

**Absence today.** The species is almost certainly absent from the Burt Lake waters at present. The author notes an expectation (Barney, 1926) that it might occur in [[Lake Superior]], but no evidence supports this.

## Key Findings

- A semifossil sheepshead cranium (156 mm) from postglacial marl at Burt Lake provides objective evidence that *Aplodinotus grunniens* once inhabited the Inland Waterway region.
- The skull was buried six feet below the surface, under about two feet of muck and four feet of marl, on red clay.
- Specialists consensually date the fish to postglacial time, probably many centuries ago; Nipissing correlation is considered most plausible.
- Root fibers in the foramina indicate shallow water, excluding both high Lake Algonquin levels.
- Comparison with a Recent [[Lake Erie]] cranium (122 mm) showed only slight differences, mostly attributable to the fossil's estimated age of about twenty years.
- The find supports earlier hypotheses (Barney, 1926; Greene, 1935) that the species dispersed northward through southern outlets of the glacial Great Lakes.
- The sheepshead is now essentially absent from the Burt Lake area, so the record documents a former range extension.
- The author notes the study of prehistoric sheepshead remains from Indian burials and middens was in progress and that comparative data on larger prehistoric sizes exist.
