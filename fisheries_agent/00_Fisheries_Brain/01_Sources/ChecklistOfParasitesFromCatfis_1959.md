# Checklist of Parasites from Catfishes of Northeastern Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #parasitology #Ictaluridae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1959 survey by Clarence E. Harms documents the parasite fauna of three catfish species — [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish), [[Ictalurus melas]] (black bullhead), and [[Ictalurus natalis]] (yellow bullhead) — collected across northeastern Kansas between 1956 and 1957. A total of 135 fish were sampled from 12 ponds, 2 lakes, 3 rivers, and 5 creeks spanning eight counties (Jackson, Shawnee, Wabaunsee, Douglas, Franklin, Miami, Linn, and Chase), covering all months except November, December, and January. The study found that 124 fish (91.9%) harbored at least one parasite, indicating a very high infection prevalence among freshwater catfish populations in the region. The checklist enumerates 24 parasite taxa spanning six major groups: [[Trematoda]], [[Cestoda]], [[Acanthocephala]], [[Nematoda]], [[Hirudinea]] (leeches), and [[Copepoda]].

## Background & Description
This paper is a parasitological checklist rather than a morphological description of the host fishes; however, key identifying data on parasite life-stage morphology (used for host-parasite classification) are recorded via the symbol system employed in the original table:

- **†** = Adults and sub-adults
- **‡** = Metacercariae
- **\*** = Plerocercoids
- **|** = Other larvae (undifferentiated larval forms)

These symbols were used to denote which life-cycle stage of each parasite was recovered from each catfish host species, distinguishing definitive-host infections (adults) from intermediate/paratenic-host infections (larval stages such as metacercariae and plerocercoids).

No morphometric or anatomical data on the catfish hosts themselves (length, weight, meristics) are reported in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Host species:** [[Ictalurus punctatus]], [[Ictalurus melas]], and [[Ictalurus natalis]] — three sympatric ictalurid catfishes native to central North American freshwater systems.
- **Habitat types sampled:** 12 ponds, 2 lakes, 3 rivers, and 5 creeks — representing both lentic (still-water) and lotic (flowing-water) freshwater habitats.
- **Geographic scope:** Eight counties in northeastern Kansas, reflecting a regional-scale survey of the [[Kansas River Basin]] watershed ecosystem.
- **Temporal coverage:** Collections spanned nearly the full year (all months except November–January), suggesting an attempt to capture seasonal variation in parasite prevalence, though seasonal breakdown is not detailed in this excerpt.
- **Parasite guild structure:** The catfish hosts serve as definitive or intermediate hosts for a diverse assemblage of helminths and ectoparasites, reflecting their position in freshwater food webs (as both predators of invertebrates/small fish and prey/hosts for parasites with complex life cycles involving invertebrate intermediate hosts such as copepods, mollusks, and oligochaetes).
- **Trophic transmission implications:** Presence of taxa like [[Clinostomum marginatum]] (a metacercarial trematode) and [[Proteocephalus ambloplitis]] (a cestode with plerocercoid stages) indicates these catfish act as intermediate/paratenic hosts within multi-host parasite life cycles linking fish, invertebrates, and piscivorous birds or larger fish.

## Key Findings
- **High overall prevalence:** 124 of 135 catfish (91.9%) were infected by at least one parasite species, indicating parasite infection is the norm rather than the exception in these populations.
- **Taxonomic breadth:** The checklist identifies 24 distinct parasite taxa across six phyla/classes:
  - **Trematoda (9 taxa):** [[Cleidodiscus floridanus]], [[Phyllodistomum caudatum]], [[Phyllodistomum lacustri]], [[Phyllodistomum carolini]], [[Crepidostomum ictaluri]], [[Plagioporus sp.]], [[Alloglossidium corti]], [[Pisciamphistoma stunkardi]], [[Clinostomum marginatum]]
  - **Cestoda (5 taxa):** [[Corallobothrium fimbriatum]], [[Corallobothrium giganteum]], [[Corallobothrium sp.]], [[Proteocephalus ambloplitis]], [[Proteocephalus sp.]]
  - **Acanthocephala (2 taxa):** [[Neoechinorhynchus sp.]], [[Pomphorhynchus bulbocolli]]
  - **Nematoda (5 taxa, including unidentified larvae):** [[Spinitectus gracilis]], [[Camallanus oxycephalus]], [[Dichelyne robusta]], [[Contracaecum sp.]], and unidentified larval nematodes
  - **Hirudinea (2 taxa):** [[Placobdella montifera]], [[Piscicolaria reducta]]
  - **Copepoda (2 taxa):** [[Achtheres pimelodi]], [[Ergasilus versicolor]]
- **Multi-host applicability:** Several parasite taxa (e.g., *Corallobothrium* spp., *Proteocephalus* spp., *Phyllodistomum* spp.) were recorded across more than one catfish host species, suggesting limited host specificity among many of these parasites within the Ictaluridae.
- **Life-stage diversity:** The use of four distinct symbols (adult/sub-adult, metacercariae, plerocercoids, other larvae) demonstrates that catfishes in this system serve as hosts for parasites at multiple points in their life cycles — acting as both definitive hosts (for adult trematodes, cestodes, nematodes) and intermediate/paratenic hosts (for larval and metacercarial stages).
- **Regional baseline value:** This checklist represents an early (1959) baseline survey of catfish parasite fauna in Kansas, valuable for later comparative studies on [[Host-Parasite Coevolution]], parasite biogeography, and freshwater fish health monitoring in the [[Great Plains]] region.
- **Methodological note:** The author indicates a more detailed report was intended for future publication, implying this checklist is a summary companion to a fuller quantitative parasitological analysis (e.g., prevalence and intensity data per host species and locality).
