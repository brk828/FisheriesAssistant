# Bonytail Chub Recovery Program: Issue Paper and Unfunded Needs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (April 1998) transmits an issue paper on the [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) recovery program in the lower [[Colorado River]] Basin. Decisions were reached at a March 13, 1998, meeting at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH), following a January 27, 1998, teleconference from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery And Technology Center]] (DNFHTC). The paper proposes actions to prevent further loss of scarce BTC genetic material, collect wild adults from [[Lake Mohave]] in spring 1998, and continue development of a new captive broodstock. It lists unfunded needs, mainly staffing (PCS costs), fish health support, genetic analysis, and broodstock facilities. Funding is requested from Region 2 management. Budget shortfalls in the region's fisheries program and upcoming retirements at WBNFH are identified as threats to the effort.

## Background & Description

- **Listed species:** Four mainstream Colorado River fishes are listed as endangered by the Fish and Wildlife Service: [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Regional responsibility:** BTC, [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] occur primarily in the lower Colorado River Basin, where Region 2 is responsible for recovery.
- **Conservation status:** Of these species, BTC is in the greatest danger of extinction in the wild and is the focus of the paper.
- **Legal basis:** Actions fall under Recovery Objectives 11 and 13 of the [[Bonytail Chub Recovery Plan]]. The paper states that collecting free-swimming BTC in spring 1998 is critical to fulfilling Federal responsibilities to protect the species from extinction.
- **Stock composition:** Lake Mohave BTC include wild and propagated fish. Captive stock at DNFHTC includes 1981 F1 (year-class) fish.
- **Facility context:** DNFHTC also maintains [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Yaqui Catfish]] family lots, which limit available tanks.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- BTC is a large-river fish of the mainstream Colorado River. The paper describes the threat as the continued loss of genetic material from both wild and captive populations, not habitat conditions.
- Lake Mohave is the main location for free-swimming BTC collection.
- Wild-caught fish are in poor physical condition on capture. They require stabilization at WBNFH, a training period onto prepared diets, and fish health treatment before transfer to DNFHTC.
- Genetic status of both captive and wild stock is unknown and is considered a key constraint on broodstock decisions.

## Key Findings

**Proposed actions (1998)**

1. **Milt cryopreservation as a safety net.** Milt from several 1981 F1 BTC at DNFHTC will be frozen and shipped to WBNFH in April 1998. It will be used to fertilize eggs from any ovulating, free-swimming BTC collected from Lake Mohave in May 1998, if needed. Unused milt will be disposed of as determined later. No lake-caught fish at WBNFH will receive hormone injections to induce ovulation in 1998. *Unfunded needs: none.*
2. **Lake Mohave adult collection (May 1998).** Biologists will collect additional adult BTC under existing permits and established protocols. Fish will be held at WBNFH until stable and then moved to DNFHTC.
3. **Broodstock development at DNFHTC.** Development of a new BTC broodstock continues in 1998, guided by the DNFHTC Bonytail Chub Hatchery Broodstock Development Plan (draft, May 1, 1996).

**Unfunded needs and costs (as stated in the paper)**

| Need | Stated cost / detail |
|---|---|
| Filling WBNFH Hatchery Manager and Assistant Manager positions (both retiring before July 1, 1998); reporting or detail of the new manager by late April 1998 | About $25,000 for PCS costs, if needed |
| Fish Health Biologist on site at WBNFH for treatment and stabilization of wild-caught fish | About $500 for per diem (M&IE only) and fuel; housing provided by WBNFH |
| No more than 10 paired matings of captive broodstock in April 1998 (1981 year class and wild captures), in isolated recirculating units | About $10,000 for equipment, supplies, and feed |
| Assembly and interpretation of raw genetic data by September 30, 1998, to inform spring 1999 broodstock decisions | About $5,000 |
| Genetic status determination for DNFHTC broodstock and WBNFH wild fish | About $40,000 |
| Transport of BTC from WBNFH to DNFHTC (health inspections, fuel, per diem, overtime) | $1,000 |
| Bonytail maintenance plan for 1999 and beyond, building on the 1996 draft plan and recommendations from Phil Hedrick et al. (ASU 1997) | To be identified by the FY 99 budget cycle |

**Stated itemized total:** The paper's cost summary table is incomplete in the source. The itemized figures listed above sum to roughly $81,500, excluding the undetermined broodstock plan cost.

**Management and policy concerns**

- Imminent retirements of the WBNFH Manager and Assistant Manager (March 28 and July 3, 1998, per the cover memo) threaten managerial oversight of the recovery program.
- Region-wide fisheries budget shortfalls in FY 1998 may prevent full implementation of BTC recovery strategies and PCS moves for key positions.
- The paper argues that the urgency of preventing further loss of scarce BTC genetic resources justifies special consideration. The cover memo states that the shortfalls may also affect recovery of other listed fish.
