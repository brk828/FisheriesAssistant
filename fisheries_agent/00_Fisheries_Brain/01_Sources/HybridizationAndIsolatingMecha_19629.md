# Hybridization and Isolating Mechanisms in Catostomus commersonii and C. macrocheilus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 study by J. S. Nelson, based on a University of British Columbia PhD thesis, examines natural hybridization between the [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersonii*) and the [[Largescale Sucker]] (*C. macrocheilus*) in the northern interior plateau of British Columbia. Using morphological indices, discriminant function analysis, field observations of spawning, and controlled artificial crosses, the author found hybrids in 9 lakes where both species occurred. Presumed F1 hybrids averaged 7% of the combined populations. Evidence showed no backcrossing or swamping, and ethological (mate-choice) isolation was inferred to be the principal barrier to hybridization. The author concludes that the two species are distinct, that hybridization has likely occurred for several hundred years without reinforcement, and that the species represent an irreversible discontinuity.

## Background & Description

The two species are similar catostomids that came into sympatry after post-Pleistocene range expansion. *C. commersonii* has glacial refugia in the Mississippi basin and Atlantic drainages and spread westward through the [[Peace River]] system, whereas *C. macrocheilus* persisted in the [[Columbia Basin]] refugium. The nomenclature note in the paper adopts *commersonii* in preference to alternative spellings, following the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature.

Identification relied on several meristic and morphometric characters measured on preserved material: dorsal-pelvic distance to caudal peduncle depth ratio, dorsal ray count, gillraker count, lateral-line and diagonal scale counts, and peritoneal and side color scores. Dorsal ray number was fixed by about 20 mm fork length, and gillraker counts were corrected for body size. The author noted that the body ratio was the only character with a distinct gap between species in juveniles (5-18 cm fork length), and that a character index combining five characters was used to identify individuals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area.** Most lakes and streams lay between 640 and 762 m elevation in the Endako (Skeena), Nechako (Fraser) and Crooked (Peace) river valleys. Spawning creeks had rapid flow over gravel. The [[Cluculz River]], outlet of Cluculz Lake, was the main diversified habitat, with flows of 2000 to 3100 dm³/sec in late May and velocities from 0.14 m/sec to about 0.9 m/sec. Several inlet creeks (e.g. Norman, Sucker, Stearns, Decker, Richmond) were small and relatively uniform, with flows from roughly 60 to 1700 dm³/sec. Several creeks were modified by farming, bulldozing, pollution (Burns Creek) or poisoning in early 1950s eradication programs.

**Spawning temperature.** Temperature appeared to be the main trigger for the spawning migration. Both species began migrating at about 6-9 °C and spawned at about 10-12 °C.

**Associated fauna.** Species such as [[Redside Shiner]], [[Northern Pikeminnow]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Burbot]] and [[Coastal Sculpin]] were present in most creeks, though not in the Cluculz River.

**Zoogeography.** *C. commersonii* is widespread across North America, while *C. macrocheilus* is confined to western North America. The two are sympatric in the upper Fraser, Peace and Skeena drainages. Sympatry is considered natural, and the author concluded that the distribution implies the overlap has existed for at least several hundred years.

## Key Findings

- **Hybrid occurrence:** Hybrids were found in 9 of the 11 lakes examined where both species occurred. Presumed F1 hybrids averaged 7% of combined populations; the sample of 499 suckers from hybrid-positive areas contained 7.2% hybrids.
- **Immature hybrid proportions** ranged from 17% (31 fish, Burns Lake) and 13% (69 fish, Cluculz River) to 2.5% (77 fish, Summit Lake). Overall, immature hybrids constituted about 5% of all immature specimens when populations without hybrids were included.
- **Adult hybrids:** Presumed adult hybrids made up about 4% of the total population where hybrids were found, with individual proportions of 15% in Decker Creek and 6-9% in several other creeks.
- **Backcrossing and swamping:** No evidence was found for backcrossing or introgression. The author concluded that swamping was not occurring and that hybrid reproduction is selected against.
- **Temporal isolation:** Absent in the Cluculz River and all inlets. Fully ripe females of both species overlapped for at least 12 days.
- **Habitat isolation:** Absent in inlets, but present in the Cluculz River. *C. commersonii* spawned over shallow coarse gravel (areas 5 and 7), whereas *C. macrocheilus* spawned over deep sand or fine gravel (areas 6 and 8). Only *C. commersonii* spawned in area 7, while 332 matings, mainly of *C. macrocheilus*, were observed in area 6 (25-28 May 1964).
- **Ethological isolation:** Inferred to be the most important mechanism reducing initial hybridization. Mating is promiscuous, with several males per female, and small *C. commersonii* males were observed entering spawning acts with larger *C. macrocheilus* males. The main cause of hybridization is considered to be males of one species joining spawning acts with a male and female of the other.
- **Morphological differences:** Color and size differences exist, and the author suggests they may favour conspecific matings, but no direct evidence was obtained, and mate choice tests in fenced areas were inconclusive.
- **Hybrid embryo mortality:** No significant difference among the four crosses in pre-hatching mortality. The hybrid cross with *C. commersonii* females had the highest mean mortality, but this was not significant (t10 = 1.89, p > 0.08). Hatching time of that cross was significantly shorter than its reciprocal and the *C. commersonii* control.
- **Egg size:** *C. commersonii* eggs (mean 2.1 mm) were significantly smaller than *C. macrocheilus* eggs (mean 2.5 mm); hybrid eggs were intermediate.
- **Hybrid inviability:** Post-hatching mortality of the *C. commersonii*-female hybrid cross was significantly higher than its reciprocal at 3 days post-hatching (t10 = 2.48, p < 0.05), though neither hybrid cross differed significantly from controls. One deformed mouth was observed in hybrids over 36.5 mm.
- **Hybrid sterility:** Presumed hybrid females were backcrossed to *C. commersonii* males, and offspring showed no obvious deformities. Testes of 21 presumed hybrids were indistinguishable from parental species in sections, and presumed male hybrids produced milt. Sex ratio was similar to parental species.
- **Reinforcement:** No evidence was found for reinforcement of isolating mechanisms despite likely hybridization for several hundred years. The author suggests mechanisms developed in allopatry and that selection against hybrids is slow.
- **Speciation conclusion:** The author considers the two species to be distinct and their status to be an irreversible discontinuity, in line with the [[Biological Species Concept]].
