# FY 2000 Fish Surveys of Salt River Project and Florence-Casa Grande Canals, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes fiscal year 2000 (1 October 1999 to 30 September 2000) fish sampling in three canal systems of the [[Gila River Basin]] in central Arizona. The work was done by [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR) under a long-term fish monitoring plan. Sampling covered the [[Salt River Project]] North Canal (SRPn), the SRP South Canal (SRPs), and the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]] (FCG), which includes the Pima Lateral Feeder Canal.

Nineteen taxa were captured, with no new species recorded. A [[Striped Bass]] taken at the River Road siphon was the first ASU collection of this species anywhere in the SRP canal system. Native fishes were present in SRPn and SRPs but absent from FCG. Non-native species dominated the community in FCG and were prominent elsewhere. The authors stress that canal samples are opportunistic and largely qualitative, so comparisons among canals are limited. They also identify communication lapses about canal dewatering schedules as a recurring problem that reduces sampling quality.

## Background & Description

The monitoring followed protocols in the Long-term Monitoring Plan for Fish Populations in Selected Waters of the Gila River Basin, Arizona (Clarkson 1996). It was submitted by Paul C. Marsh of ASU's Department of Biology to Robert W. Clarkson of the BR Phoenix Area Office, as partial fulfillment of Cooperative Agreement 1425-97-FC-32-A0780.

Sampling used several gear types, selected for each reach:

- [[Electrofishing]], including backpack units and a boat-mounted unit (BR PxAO)
- Seines (straight, bag, and mesh-size variants)
- Hoop nets, trammel nets, and experimental gill nets
- Dip nets and visual observation on foot

Sampling dates in the report:

- FCG: 24 October 1999
- SRPs: 13 November 1999
- SRPn: 7 January 2000 (an opportunistic electrofishing sample, not routine monitoring)

Fish were identified with standard species codes (for example CAIN, PACL, CYLU, ICPU, GIRO, MISA, GAAF). Those codes are given in the source tables and are not expanded here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The canals are managed systems. Water levels are controlled, and periodic dry-ups occur for maintenance. Fish assemblages reflect flow, sampling effort, gear, and local fish availability. The report notes that these conditions make it hard to compare stations directly.

Native and non-native species are distinguished throughout the report:

- **Native taxa:** [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarkii*). The text also mentions [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) as a past FCG resident.
- **Non-native taxa:** [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), Striped Bass (*Morone saxatilis*), and undetermined Tilapia species (*Oreochromis* spp.).

Native species made up between 0% and 27% of the total species count at the stations sampled. The number of non-native taxa was 3 at FCG, 14 at SRPs, and 9 at SRPn.

Operational communication is a key constraint. Salt River Project schedules for dry-up periods were often late, hard to interpret, or changed without notice. The [[San Carlos Irrigation District]] (SCID) communication lapses also affected FCG timing, though the report describes that impact as minor. Electrofishing data from above the fish barriers were collected by the Arizona Game and Fish Department and reported separately, so the present results may understate total fish communities.

## Key Findings

**Overall monitoring results**

- A total of 19 taxa, including undetermined Tilapia, was captured across the three canal systems.
- SRPn yielded 11 species, SRPs yielded 17, and FCG yielded 3.
- Three native taxa were collected, all from SRPn and SRPs. None was found in FCG.
- The Striped Bass record at River Road siphon is the first ASU collection of this species in the SRP canal system.

**Salt River Project South Canal (SRPs), 13 November 1999**

- Seventeen species were recorded, including three native species.
- Native fishes made up 74% of the 306-fish catch.
- Sonora Sucker was the most abundant species (36.9%). Roundtail Chub was second (21.2%), and Desert Sucker contributed 15.4%.
- Non-native fishes made up 24% of the catch. Red Shiner was the most abundant non-native (7.5%), followed by Tilapia spp. (4.9%) and Largemouth Bass (4.5%). Other non-natives each contributed no more than 1.6%.
- Native suckers were present at all stations. Roundtail Chub was present at all stations except the downstream-most triple junction site.

**Florence-Casa Grande Canal (FCG), 24 October 1999**

- Only three species were captured: Red Shiner, Channel Catfish, and Mosquitofish. No native fishes were found.
- The text reports a sample of 373 fish with Red Shiner at 55.0%, Mosquitofish at 28.2%, and Channel Catfish at 16.9%. The text dates this sample to October 2000, but the tables date the sampling to 24 October 1999. The discrepancy is unresolved in the source.

**SRP North Canal (SRPn), 7 January 2000**

- Routine stations were not sampled because of canal dry-up timing. An opportunistic boat-electrofishing sample covered the reach from the Indian Bend Wash siphon to the Pima Road bridge.
- Eleven species were captured, including two natives.
- Native fishes made up 54.5% of the 68-fish catch. Sonora Sucker was 50.0% and Desert Sucker was 4.4%.
- Red Shiner was the most abundant non-native (19.1% of numbers), followed by Common Carp (11.8%) and Largemouth Bass (5.9%).
- Non-native fishes were concentrated near the Indian Bend Wash siphon. Native suckers were distributed throughout the reach.

**Sampling-quality limitations**

- Canal samples are mostly opportunistic and qualitative. The report warns that results cannot be treated as directly comparable across canals.
- Across stations, natives made up 54% of numbers in 6 samples (range 12–99%), 74% in one sample, and 0% in 4 samples.

**Recommendations**

- Improve communication between canal operators (SRP, SCID) and monitoring staff (ASU, BR) so sampling can coincide with scheduled and unscheduled outages. The authors describe these lapses as a significant concern since the program began nearly five years earlier.
- Change the monitoring protocol to allow routine sampling in years without dry-ups. The authors expect dry-ups to become less common because canal management no longer requires annual dewatering for maintenance.
- Use boat-mounted electrofishing combined with hoop and/or entanglement netting in selected areas.
