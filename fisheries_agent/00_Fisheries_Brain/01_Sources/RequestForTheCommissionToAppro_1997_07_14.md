# Guidelines for Stocking Federally Listed Aquatic Species: Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinodontidae #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1997 administrative memorandum from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) to its Commission, requesting approval of guidelines governing the reintroduction ("stocking") of two federally listed aquatic species: the [[Gila Topminnow]] and the [[Desert Pupfish]]. It is not a primary biological research paper but a management/policy record; however, it contains valuable ecological and conservation-status data on these species, their habitats, and the regulatory framework (ESA Section 10j "experimental nonessential population" designation) used to manage reintroduction efforts. The memo outlines a tiered decision framework balancing endangered species recovery goals against potential conflicts with sportfishing interests and landowner/land-manager concurrence.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide morphological, meristic, or anatomical data for either species. As a policy/management memorandum, it focuses exclusively on regulatory and habitat-management concerns rather than physical taxonomy. ## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Both species inhabit isolated desert aquatic habitats — springs, cienegas, and small perennial streams — characteristic of the arid Southwest (Arizona).
- **Named localities associated with topminnow populations**:
  - [[Cienega Creek]] (natural, fully-protected population; Empire Gulch proposed reintroduction site ~1 mile upstream)
  - [[Sonoita Creek]] (natural fully-protected population on private land; proposed upstream reintroduction site)
  - [[Kayler Spring]] (near [[Roosevelt Lake]])
  - [[Ad Wash]] (upstream of [[Lake Pleasant]])
  - [[Roper Lake State Park]]
  - [[Muleshoe Ranch Cooperative Management Area]] (Cienega Creek Drainage region; managed jointly by AGFD, BLM, and The Arizona Nature Conservancy)
- **Population isolation as a conservation constraint**: A key ecological/regulatory issue noted is that ESA Section 10j ("experimental nonessential population") status *cannot* be applied where a reintroduction site is hydrologically connected to an existing wild population, because flood events can cause co-mingling of experimental and fully-protected stocks (documented at both the Empire Gulch/Cienega Creek and Sonoita Creek sites).
- **Interspecific interactions**: The memo emphasizes that sites are screened by Department fisheries biologists to avoid stocking topminnow or pupfish into waters where **sportfish predation** would occur, indicating that introduced/established sportfish (predatory, non-native) represent a significant ecological threat to these small-bodied native fish.
- **Co-occurrence with sportfisheries**: Despite proximity to significant sport fisheries (e.g., near [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[Patagonia Lake]]), no documented predation or stocking conflicts have arisen to date, suggesting some degree of spatial/habitat separation between topminnow/pupfish habitat and open-water sportfish habitat.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory driver**: A 1995 Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between AGFD and the [[Bureau of Land Management]] set a goal of establishing 20–30+ self-sustaining populations of [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]] on BLM lands by 2004, in support of ESA-mandated species downlisting objectives.
- **Section 10j bottleneck**: Designating reintroductions as "experimental nonessential populations" (ESA Section 10j) requires USFWS rulemaking, which in practice takes ≥2 years and was effectively stalled due to competing priorities (e.g., California condor, black-footed ferret, Mexican wolf rules), impeding topminnow/pupfish recovery progress.
- **Biological/legal constraint on 10j designation**: Section 10j designation is legally precluded when a proposed reintroduction site is not hydrologically isolated from an existing wild population, since flood-driven mixing would compromise the "nonessential" status. This directly affected proposed reintroductions at Empire Gulch (Cienega Creek) and a Sonoita Creek private property site.
- **Stakeholder-driven reintroduction requests**: Three concrete reintroduction proposals prompted the policy review:
  1. BLM's request to stock Gila topminnow at Empire Gulch (Cienega Creek drainage).
  2. The Arizona Nature Conservancy's (TANC) request to stock both species at multiple sites within the [[Muleshoe Ranch Cooperative Management Area]].
  3. A private landowner's (via TANC) request to reintroduce Gila topminnow on private Sonoita Creek property.
- **No documented sportfish conflict**: The memo asserts that no past or planned topminnow/pupfish reintroduction has created conflict with sportfishing, and that neither species was a focal issue in the Department's prior sportfish stocking program ESA Section 7 consultation — attributed to careful pre-screening by both Nongame/Endangered Wildlife and Sportfish Management program staff.
- **New tiered management guidelines proposed** (and recommended for Commission approval):
  1. The Director may approve **fully protected** reintroductions when the land manager/owner and any affected grazing lessee concur, and no sportfishing/wildlife conflicts exist.
  2. Where conflicts exist but are resolvable without harm to listed species or sportfishing resources, the Director may approve as fully protected (with notice to the Commission) or as experimental nonessential.
  3. Where conflicts are significant, the Director may deny the reintroduction, approve it as experimental nonessential, or refer it to the Commission for a decision on full protection.
  4. All other cases default to experimental nonessential status.
- **Conservation significance**: This guideline framework represents an attempt to reconcile ESA recovery/downlisting obligations for two endangered desert fish species with practical land-management, regulatory, and stakeholder (rancher, landowner, sportfishing) constraints in Arizona.
