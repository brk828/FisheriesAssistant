## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific research paper but an internal **National Park Service (NPS) memorandum** (dated May 31, 1990) addressing procedural and administrative concerns regarding research on the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) conducted as part of the Glen Canyon Dam Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) process, specifically under the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Phase II program. The memo raises concerns about the integrity, oversight, and interagency coordination of the research program rather than presenting biological or ecological data on the species itself.

## Background & Description

- The [[Humpback Chub]] is a federally significant fish species native to the [[Colorado River]] system, particularly within [[Grand Canyon National Park]].
- Research on this species was being coordinated through the **Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES)** program, established to inform the **Glen Canyon Dam EIS**, a major federal environmental review process addressing dam operations and downstream ecological impacts.
- Dr. Duncan Patten (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) served as a key scientific advisor responsible for developing an **Integrated Study Plan** for humpback chub research.
- The memo was authored by the Superintendent of [[Grand Canyon National Park]] and addressed to Rick Gold of the **Bureau of Reclamation, Upper Colorado Region**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The [[Humpback Chub]] inhabits fast-flowing, turbulent waters of the [[Colorado River]] within Grand Canyon, a habitat historically shaped by natural flow and sediment regimes prior to regulation by [[Glen Canyon Dam]].
- As a species of major conservation concern, the humpback chub was central to environmental studies assessing how dam operations (flow regulation, temperature changes, sediment transport alteration) affect native fish populations.
- The NPS, as the principal land management agency for [[Grand Canyon National Park]], holds responsibility for protecting in-park aquatic resources potentially affected by upstream dam management decisions.

## Key Findings

- **Research Process Concerns**: Multiple agencies had not submitted required research proposals to Dr. Patten, undermining the coherence of the humpback chub study plan.
- **Lack of Interagency Review**: The Bureau of Reclamation's Request for Proposals (RFP) for humpback chub research was released without review or input from cooperating agencies, including the NPS, despite prior requests (March 9, 1990 memo) for such involvement.
- **Contracting Issues**: The research contract was awarded through a **Small Business Administration (SBA) mechanism**, which the NPS argued excluded qualified individuals and institutions with stronger scientific credentials from participating.
- **No Academic Standards Required**: The RFP reportedly contained no academic or professional qualification requirements for either the principal investigator or the proposing organization — a significant concern given the scientific complexity of humpback chub research.
- **Recommended Solution**: The NPS proposed establishing an **Intergovernmental Personnel Act (IGPA)** position (25–50% of a full-time equivalent) to provide stronger oversight, ensuring that contracted research aligned with the **Integrated Study Plan** developed by Dr. Patten.
- **Stakeholders Noted**: Dr. Duncan Patten (Arizona State University) and Dave Wegner (Glen Canyon Environmental Studies office, Flagstaff, AZ) were copied on the memo, indicating direct coordination between NPS, academic science advisors, and GCES program management.

> **Note:** This document contains no direct biological, population, or habitat data on *Gila cypha*. Its value lies in documenting the **science-policy and interagency management context** surrounding humpback chub conservation research during the Glen Canyon Dam EIS process.
