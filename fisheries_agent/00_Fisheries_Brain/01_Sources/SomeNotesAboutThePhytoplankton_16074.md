# Phytoplankton Community of the River Lima: Reference State and Trophic Status

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bacillariophyceae

## Executive Summary

This 1990 study by [[Maria Manuela Cerqueira da Silva]] (Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre", Faculdade de Ciências do Porto) characterises the phytoplankton of the [[River Lima]] over one annual cycle (monthly sampling, May 1987 to April 1988). It was part of an ecological characterisation of the river within the [[Plano de Desenvolvimento Regional do Alto Minho]] and was supported by the [[Programa Integrado de Desenvolvimento Regional do Alto Minho]] (PIDR-AM). The main goal was to establish the reference state of the phytoplanktonic community.

The author concludes that the river phytoplankton is typically lotic, with high specific diversity, [[Bacillariophyceae]] as the dominant group, and low microalgal density and biomass. The system is oligotrophic, tending towards mesotrophy at Portucel (L2) and Ponte da Barca (L5).

Note: this document contains no fish data. Its content is limited to phytoplankton and water-quality indicators.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** the Lima basin in northern Portugal, with seven sampling stations (L1–L7) spanning the estuary to the Lindoso hydroelectric plant (see [[Lima Hydrographic Basin]]).
- **Sampling stations:**
  - L1: estuary
  - L2: downstream Portucel
  - L3: Lactolima (Bertiandos)
  - L4: Etar (Ponte da Barca)
  - L5: near the Ponte da Barca water intake
  - L6: mouth of the [[Tamente River]], a tributary
  - L7: [[Lindoso Hydroelectric Plant]]
- **Sampling frequency:** monthly, May 1987 to April 1988.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods**

- Running-water samples were taken at the surface near the margins in plastic bottles.
- Estuarine samples were collected with a [[Ruttner Bottle]] at low tide (surface) and at high tide (surface and bottom).
- Quantitative counts followed the [[Utermöhl Method]] (1958) using an inverted microscope, with samples preserved in iodine solution.
- Qualitative samples were taken with a 30 µm plankton net.
- Photosynthetic pigments were extracted with acetone and measured spectrophotometrically (Strickland and Parsons, 1968). [[Chlorophyll a]] and phaeopigments were calculated using the Lorenz equations (1967).
- Indices used: the [[Pigmentary Diversity Index]] (Margalef, 1960), and the [[Carlson Trophic State Index]] (1977).

**Context**

- Chlorophyll *a* was used as the community indicator because it is common to all phytoplankton groups. Phaeopigments indicate the physiological state of the cells.
- Heavy January rains produced flooding in the river, which was linked to a rise in the PDI in February.

## Key Findings

**Pigments and biomass**

- Chlorophyll *a* was generally low, and lowest in February and March at most sites.
- The annual maximum was 41.69 mg/m³ at L2 in July. It coincided with a flagellate bloom (unidentified species).
- Chlorophyll *a* mean values ranged from 1.31 to 5.90 mg/m³. The author reports these as oligotrophic by Weber's criteria and oligo-mesotrophic by OECD (1980) criteria.
- Station L5 showed the most regular chlorophyll values, attributed to a less current zone.

**Diversity and trophic state**

- The Pigmentary Diversity Index was broadly uniform across sites and months, except for a marked rise in February.
- Trophic State Index values ranged from 13.8 to 67.2, indicating an oligotrophic system.

**Density**

- Maximum density was 14,107 cells/ml at L2 in July. Minimum was 25 cells/ml at L2 in March.
- Mean annual density ranged from 501 to 1,821 cells/ml. This indicates oligotrophy by Weber's criteria, and oligotrophy with a tendency to mesotrophy at L2 and L5 by Margalef's criteria.

**Composition**

- Bacillariophyceae was the most frequent and dominant group, especially in spring, autumn and winter.
- Flagellate blooms occurred in July at L1 and L2. Chlorophyceae increased in summer, reaching about 51% at L3 and 71% at L4.
- Single-species dominance included:
  - *Fragilaria crotonensis*: 78% (June, all stations) and 81% (July, all river except L1 and L2)
  - *Melosira ambigua*: 75% (October), 85% (November) and 85% (December), all stations
- Diatoms typical of oligotrophic waters included *Ceratoneis arcus*, *Tabellaria flocculosa* and *Tabellaria fenestrata*.
- Brackish species (*Amphiprora alata*, *Bacillaria paradoxa*) appeared at L2 in summer.

**Estuary**

- Chlorophyll *a* was low across the annual cycle. A June increase coincided with peak density.
- Density was usually higher at low tide.
- Bacillariophyceae dominated. Phytoflagellates bloomed in July and September, when temperatures were higher, and Dinophyceae were present less frequently.
- Marine-affinity species were recorded, especially at high tide. These included *Ceratium furca*, *Skeletonema costatum*, *Chaetoceros* spp. and *Coscinodiscus radiatus* (about 20 taxa in total).

**Conclusions**

- The phytoplankton community has high species diversity, with Bacillariophyceae dominant. Chlorophyceae, Cyanophyceae and Dinophyceae increase in warmer periods.
- The river is oligotrophic, tending towards mesotrophy at L2 (Portucel) and L5 (Ponte da Barca).
- Compared with earlier results, the river currently maintains good water quality. Some pollution sources already identified should be controlled.
