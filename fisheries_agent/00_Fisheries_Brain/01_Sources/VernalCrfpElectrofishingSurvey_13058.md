# Vernal CREP Electrofishing Survey Field Data Sheets

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 28-page set of field datasheets from a spring electrofishing survey, labelled "Vernal CREP Electrofishing Survey." Each sheet logs one sampling effort on a river reach. The forms capture location (river, stratum, river mile [[River Mile Index]] start and stop), effort (date, start and end times, duration, water temperature, collectors), and catch data for [[Target Species]], [[Non-Target Species]], and [[Predator Species]]. The only target species named in the legible text is the [[Razorback Sucker]], which appears in the header of page 25. Most handwriting and scan text is degraded, so the document contains no summarized results, population estimates, or analysis. Its value lies in the raw field protocol and the individual fish records.

## Background & Description

The survey uses a standardized form with these sections:

- **Effort record:** river, stratum (letter codes such as E, F, and G appear), RMI start and stop, distance, date, start/end time, duration (often recorded as hours or minutes), water temperature at start and end, and collector initials.
- **Non-target catch:** counts of juveniles and adults by species.
- **Predator catch:** species, total length (TL, mm), and stomach contents, with entries such as plant material and invertebrate remains.
- **Target catch (individual fish):** species, tag number, recapture status (Y/N), sex, reproductive condition (see below), TL (mm), weight (g), river mile, RMI, water temperature, habitat, and comments.
- **Habitat (back of sheet):** depth, velocity, and primary and secondary substrate for all ripe fish.

Reproductive condition codes are defined on every page:
- **R:** ripe, with expressible sex products
- **T:** tuberculated
- **N:** neither condition applies (both codes may be recorded together)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sheets show that the survey sampled riverine habitat at multiple river miles within several strata, with reach lengths and distances noted per effort. Recorded substrates and comments include cobbles, gravel, and shallow areas, and sampling in some passes was done in shallow water. Water temperatures were recorded at the start and end of each pass, which indicates the survey tracked thermal conditions during spring sampling. Several pages reference radio-tracked fish ("From Radio Sheets"), suggesting telemetry was used alongside electrofishing to locate individuals. The legible data sheets also record mortality ("Died") for at least one specimen, and one comment notes that fish were kept on ice for later handling.

## Key Findings

- **Survey type and structure:** The document is a multi-page set of [[Electrofishing Survey]] field records. It contains no summary tables, analysis, or conclusions.
- **Target species:** The [[Razorback Sucker]] is the only species named in the legible headings. Individual target entries are recorded with tag numbers, sex, reproductive codes, TL (mm), and weight (g). Several legible entries show total lengths in the hundreds of millimetres, with examples around 460–560 mm.
- **Reproductive monitoring:** Many entries carry R (ripe) or T (tuberculated) codes, which indicates the survey tracked spawning condition during the spring sampling period.
- **Recapture and tagging:** Each target fish has a tag field and a recapture (Y/N) field, so the survey was designed to support mark-recapture and individual tracking. Some pages have only partially legible tag numbers.
- **Non-target and predator catch:** Each page lists non-target species with juvenile and adult counts, and predator species with TL and stomach contents. Predator stomach contents are often recorded as plant material or invertebrate remains, but the text is too degraded to report specific species or counts.
- **Habitat measurements:** Depth, velocity, and substrate are supposed to be recorded for ripe fish. Legible entries mention substrates such as cobbles and gravel, plus shallow-water conditions.
- **Data quality:** The scans are heavily degraded, with many partially illegible entries and handwritten corrections. Specific values (dates, river mile ranges, tag numbers, and measurements) should be verified against the original forms before any reuse.
- **Missing information:** The river name, location, and survey year are not clearly legible. The dates visible in the scan appear to fall in late spring, but the year is uncertain. The species list for non-target and predator taxa cannot be reliably reconstructed.
