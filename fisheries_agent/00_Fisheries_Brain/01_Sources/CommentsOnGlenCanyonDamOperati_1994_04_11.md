## Executive Summary

This document is a legal comment letter submitted by the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund regarding the Draft Environmental Impact Statement (DEIS) for [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations. It addresses the regulatory and ecological status of four endangered fish species native to the Colorado River system: the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]]. The letter argues that the dam's operational history has caused severe habitat degradation and that the DEIS's "Preferred Alternative" (Modified Low Fluctuating Flow, MLFF) fails to meet the requirements of the Endangered Species Act (ESA). It advocates for alternative flow regimes, sediment augmentation, and temperature control to support the recovery of these native species, citing scientific findings from Clarkson et al. (1994).

## Background & Description

*Note: The source document is a legal/policy text and does not provide direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, size ranges) for the species discussed. It focuses instead on population status and habitat requirements.*

- **Species Identified:** [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]].
- **Taxonomic Status:** All four species are listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act (ESA), with critical habitat designated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (March 21, 1994).
- **Life Stages:** The document specifically highlights vulnerability during "early life stages," suggesting susceptibility to environmental stressors like flow fluctuations that impact larvae and juveniles disproportionately.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary Habitat:** The Colorado River mainstem and tributaries between [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and [[Lake Mead]], including the San Juan Arm of [[Lake Powell]] (critical habitat for Razorback Sucker).
- **Critical Micro-habitat:** **Backwater habitats** are identified as crucial nursery areas. These are formed by sediment deposition and are necessary for the survival of endangered fish larvae.
- **Sediment Dynamics:** The dam blocks the downstream transport of sediment. Tributary inputs alone represent only **23%** of the historic sediment budget below the dam, insufficient to maintain necessary backwater habitats under current flow alternatives (MLFF).
- **Thermal Regime:** Water released from the dam is significantly colder than historic natural conditions. This thermal alteration limits the growth and survival of native fish, favoring nonnative species competitors instead.
- **Protected Status:** The habitat is considered highly recoverable due to its location within [[Grand Canyon National Park]], which shields it from additional development pressures.

## Key Findings

1. **Regulatory Failure:** The Biological Opinion for the DEIS's preferred alternative (MLFF) did not adequately consider the impact on newly designated **critical habitat**, necessitating further consultation under ESA § 7.
2. **Flow Fluctuation Mortality:** Daily fluctuating flows are identified as a direct cause of mortality for early life stages of endangered fish (citing Tyus and Karp, 1989; [[Clarkson et al. 1994]]). This occurs by trapping fish in isolated pools, exposing them to desiccation and predation.
3. **Sediment Limitation:** The lack of sediment augmentation prevents the formation of backwater habitats, which are essential for fish recovery. The MLFF alternative does not address this deficit.
4. **Temperature Recommendation:** A **Multi-Level Intake Structure** is recommended to modulate release temperatures. Warmer water during critical life history periods is expected to increase growth and survival rates of young native fish.
5. **Alternative Flow Regime:** The **Seasonally Adjusted Steady Flow (SASF)** alternative is identified as the operational strategy best suited to avoid adverse modification of critical habitat, serving as a bridge until sediment and temperature issues are resolved.
6. **Ecosystem Trade-offs:** A caution is raised (citing [[Clarkson et al. 1994]]) that warmer water releases must be carefully managed alongside sediment/flow releases to prevent inadvertently benefiting nonnative competitor species over the native endangered fish.
7. **Historical Impact:** [[Glen Canyon Dam]] is cited as a primary driver of habitat alteration/destruction for these species, but its modification is also seen as the primary tool for their potential recovery.
