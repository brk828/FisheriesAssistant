## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Mountain-Prairie Region, to the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund, dated June 16, 1992. It does not contain primary morphological or ecological field data; rather, it communicates an administrative decision to **expand the scope of a critical habitat designation effort** originally focused solely on the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) to include three additional federally listed Colorado River fishes: the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, also known as [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]), the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The rationale cited is the significant overlap in habitat requirements and scientific data among these four species, administrative efficiency, alignment with an emerging "ecosystem approach" to endangered species management, and streamlining of the associated economic analysis. The letter also revises the projected timeline for the proposed critical habitat rule from December 1992 to September 1993.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While explicit habitat parameters (e.g., flow regime, substrate, depth) are not detailed, the letter implies a shared ecological framework among the four species:

- **Geographic Range:** All four species are endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], a large southwestern U.S. river system spanning multiple states under FWS Mountain-Prairie Region jurisdiction.
- **Habitat Overlap:** The FWS explicitly states that "the different scientific data bases generally have information on all four fishes or at least more than one," indicating substantial spatial and ecological co-occurrence among the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]].
- **Conservation Status:** All four species are treated as federally listed (endangered) fishes requiring [[Critical Habitat Designation]] under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Ecosystem Approach:** The letter references a policy shift toward evaluating habitat needs collectively across co-occurring species rather than isolating single-species habitat requirements — reflecting recognition that these fishes share niche space within the same riverine ecosystem.

## Key Findings

- **Administrative Decision:** The FWS formally expanded the critical habitat designation effort from a single-species focus (razorback sucker) to a multi-species effort covering four Colorado River fishes.
- **Justification for Expansion:**
  - Commonality of existing scientific/habitat data across species reduces duplicative technical effort.
  - Efficient use of already-dedicated FWS personnel and resources.
  - Consistency with contemporaneous Washington-level policy favoring an [[Ecosystem Approach]] over a species-by-species regulatory strategy.
  - Simplification of the required economic analysis by evaluating shared habitats jointly rather than parsing habitat importance to a single species.
- **Schedule Revision:** The target date for publishing the proposed rule on critical habitat was pushed back from **December 1992** to **September 1993**, reflecting the increased scope of the expanded, multi-species effort.
- **Regulatory Context:** This correspondence illustrates a procedural precedent in U.S. endangered species law/policy — treating co-occurring imperiled species within a single watershed as a bundled critical habitat rulemaking, foreshadowing later multi-species critical habitat designations for the [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes.
- **Stakeholder Communication:** The letter was sent in response to inquiries/legal interest from the Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund, indicating external legal/advocacy pressure on the pace and scope of critical habitat rulemaking for these species.
