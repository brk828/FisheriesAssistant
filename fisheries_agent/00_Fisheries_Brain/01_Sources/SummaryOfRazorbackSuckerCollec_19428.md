# Summary of Razorback Sucker Collections Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge 17-18 April 1989

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This report documents the status of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) larvae stocked into eight tanks at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] in March 1989. Sampling conducted 17-18 April 1989 recovered fish from four of eight tanks (Pozo Nuevo, Mosca, Suffrido, Carrizo Dam), with total lengths ranging 19-33 mm and weights 0.08-0.32 gm. Statistical comparisons revealed significant differences in growth among tanks based on parental size combinations. Predatory invertebrates (Ranatra, Lethocerus, Naucoridae, anisopteran odonates) and [[Tiger Salamander]] (*Ambystoma* spp.) larvae were common across sites. Dense aquatic vegetation (*Potamogeton* spp.) precluded effective sampling in three tanks.

## Background & Description

Embryos were produced at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Dexter, New Mexico, in March 1989 from controlled crosses of fast- and slow-growing male and female [[Razorback Sucker]]. Larvae were shipped to Arizona State University and stocked into selected tanks at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] on 16 March 1989. Four parental combinations were tested: large female × small male (L-S), small female × small male (S-S), large female × large male (L-L), and unspecified crosses.

Sampling methodology employed 1/8" and 1/4" straight seines (10 and 20 ft) and bag seines (30 ft). Each tank was seined for approximately one hour. Fish were measured for total length (TL, mm) and weight (gm).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Tank Conditions (April 1989):**

- **Pozo Nuevo** (L-S cross): Water level 0.3 m lower than March; turbid; maximum depth 0.5 m (mostly <0.2 m); solid/slightly muddy bottom; predatory invertebrates exceptionally abundant; *Ambystoma* larvae and adults common; no algal mats or aquatic macrophytes.

- **Blanco**: No fish recovered; shallow throughout (maximum depth <0.3 m); water described as "rich, pea-green soup"; solid bottom with central soft area; few predatory invertebrates; no vegetation.

- **Mosca** (L-S cross): Water level 0.25-0.3 m lower than March; tank divided by narrow earthen berm; maximum depth 0.5 m; predatory invertebrates common-to-abundant; solid-to-moderately soft bottom; no vegetation.

- **Cordova**: No fish recovered; water level receding but satisfactory; maximum depth 0.5 m; relatively clear water; *Potamogeton* so dense as to preclude effective seining.

- **Suffrido** (L-L cross): Water level stable; maximum depth 0.75 m; moderately turbid; predatory invertebrates common; solid bottom; no vegetation.

- **Big Bertha**: No fish recovered; water level receding (ca. 1 m below wire); maximum depth >0.75 m; relatively clear; *Potamogeton* too dense for efficient seining.

- **Carrizo**: Water level down slightly; maximum depth 1 m; relatively clear; heavy cattle use; *Potamogeton* precluded successful seining.

- **Carrizo Dam** (S-S cross): Water level down 0.25 m since March; maximum depth 0.75 m; turbid; predatory invertebrates common; *Ambystoma* common; dense algal mats around perimeter; solid bottom; fish associated with gravelly area near wash inflow.

## Key Findings

**Growth Data by Tank:**

- **Pozo Nuevo** (L-S): n=26; mean 23.54 ± 1.45 mm TL (range 19-27 mm); 0.145 ± 0.03 gm (range 0.08-0.17 gm)

- **Mosca** (L-S): n=323; mean 26.0 ± 1.44 mm TL (range 24-29 mm); 0.16 ± 0.03 gm (range 0.10-0.21 gm)

- **Suffrido** (L-L): n=4; mean 30.75 ± 1.71 mm TL (range 29-33 mm); 0.25 ± 0.03 gm (range 0.22-0.29 gm)

- **Carrizo Dam** (S-S): n=50; mean 29.20 ± 1.77 mm TL (range 21-32 mm); 0.24 ± 0.05 gm (range 0.12-0.32 gm)

**Statistical Comparisons (t-tests, independent samples):**

- Carrizo Dam (S-S) vs Suffrido (L-L): t(52)=1.686, p=0.0939 (not significant)
- Carrizo Dam (S-S) vs Pozo Nuevo (L-S): t(74), p=0.0000 (highly significant)
- Carrizo Dam (S-S) vs Mosca (L-S): t(80), p<0.001 (highly significant)
- Suffrido (L-L) vs Pozo Nuevo (L-S): t(28), p=0.0000 (highly significant)
- Suffrido (L-L) vs Mosca (L-S): t(34)=4.758, p=0.0001 (highly significant)
- Pozo Nuevo (L-S) vs Mosca (L-S): t(36)=-9.260, p=0.0000 (highly significant)

**Key Observations:**

1. Fish from large female × large male crosses (Suffrido) and small × small crosses (Carrizo Dam) achieved significantly greater sizes than large female × small male crosses (Pozo Nuevo, Mosca).

2. Within the L-S cross category, Mosca fish significantly outgrew Pozo Nuevo fish despite identical parental combinations.

3. Dense *Potamogeton* beds prevented sampling in three tanks (Cordova, Big Bertha, Carrizo), making fish presence/absence uncertain.

4. Predatory invertebrates were common-to-abundant in most tanks, representing potential mortality sources.

5. Water levels declined 0.25-1.0 m across most tanks between March and April.

6. An oily film was noted on Suffrido Tank surface (possibly fuel oil), but was not considered problematic enough to prevent stocking.

7. Transport conditions: larvae received directly at stocking sites; container temperature maintained at 21°C throughout; no mortality during transport or at stocking.
