# Distributional Records for North American Fishes, with Nomenclatural Notes on the Genus Psenes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #nomeidae

## Executive Summary

This short paper, published in the *Journal of the Washington Academy of Sciences* in 1946, reports rare or range-extending records for North American fishes drawn from the unidentified backlog of the [[U.S. National Museum]]. It covers freshwater and marine species, with freshwater forms listed first. The paper also offers nomenclatural notes on the marine genus [[Psenes]], arguing that several names have been misapplied and that the Atlantic and Indo-Pacific forms need revision.

## Background & Description

The author reduced a large backlog of unidentified fishes in the [[U.S. National Museum]] over roughly a year and a half and found specimens that extended beyond their published ranges. The records are arranged systematically, with freshwater species first and marine species after. Both introduced and native species are included.

The paper covers about 11 species across several families:

- **Freshwater:** [[Thoburnia Rhothoeca]] (sucker), [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] (cyprinid), [[Hesperoleucus Mitrulus]], [[Rhinichthys Osculus]], [[Chrosomus Oreas]] (minnows), and [[Ameiurus Natalis]] (catfish).
- **Marine:** [[Psenes Regulus]], [[Psenes Pellucidus]], [[Psenes Maculatus]], [[Psenes Cyanophrys]] (Psenes), and [[Urophycis Chesteri]] (hake).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Several records concern drainage connections and biogeographic boundaries:

- **Potomac and Kanawha drainages:** [[Thoburnia Rhothoeca]] was previously known from the James and Roanoke systems. Miller's material extends its known range to the Kanawha and Potomac drainages. The Potomac record is a single half-grown specimen from Dry River, a tributary of the North Fork of the [[Shenandoah River]].
- **Gila River faunal boundary:** [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] is the only known species of its genus and had not been recorded north of the [[Gila River]]. The fish fauna of the Gila River is described as distinct from the rest of the [[Colorado River Basin]], apart from a few channel forms and *Plagopterus argentissimus*.
- **Pit River fauna:** [[Hesperoleucus Mitrulus]] is described as a distinctive element of the upper [[Pit River]] fauna of the [[Sacramento River]] system, to which Goose Lake was once tributary.
- **Southern California drainages:** [[Rhinichthys Osculus]] was newly found in the [[Cuyama River]] and [[Santa Clara River]] basins, where earlier surveys had not detected it.
- **Open-ocean distribution:** Miller notes that oceanic fishes typically have wide distributions, which explains the occurrence of *Psenes regulus* off the Carolinas.

## Key Findings

**Freshwater records**

- **[[Thoburnia Rhothoeca]]:** Range extended to the Kanawha and Potomac drainages on the basis of museum specimens collected by [[Marshall McDonald]]. The Kanawha material is three adults from Wolf Creek, which enters the [[New River]] about 10 miles above the Virginia–West Virginia boundary. The Potomac record is one half-grown specimen (U.S.N.M. no. 182071).
- **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]:** Fourteen young to small adults (U.S.N.M. no. 181685) were collected by Frank Stephens in Big Sandy Creek (River), Mohave County, Arizona, on July 1902. This extends the species north of the Gila River into the Colorado River Basin.
- **[[Hesperoleucus Mitrulus]]:** Two adults (U.S.N.M. no. 132089), collected by J. O. Snyder in Big Valley near Bieber, Lassen County, California, establish the species in the Pit River system. Fin counts (dorsal 8, anal 7) and scale radii were used for identification, but Miller notes these characters also occur in some headwater populations of *symmetricus*. The two species may intergrade.
- **[[Rhinichthys Osculus]]:** Five forms of the species (subgenus *Apocope*) show a frenum of varying development. Notable collections include:
  - 388 young to adult fish (U.S.N.M. no. 132342), seined October 14, 1945, in Elizabeth Lake Canyon, Los Angeles County.
  - 144 young to adult fish (U.S.N.M. no. 132346), from Castaic Creek.
  - Four adults (U.S.N.M. no. 132351), from the Santa Clara River, October 21, 1945.
  - Miller considers introduction by bait fishermen a serious possibility, since the genus was absent from earlier Santa Clara Basin collections despite thorough sampling.
- **[[Chrosomus Oreas]]:** The first New River record, based on 12 specimens (36.5–54 mm standard length; U.S.N.M. no. 107582) collected July 16, 1938, from Little River and Sugar Run above Snowville, Pulaski County, Virginia. The New River sample is very slightly finer-scaled than Roanoke material.
- **[[Ameiurus Natalis]]:** First Oregon record, based on one adult (U.S.N.M. no. 108817) from McBee Lake, Benton County, collected by Stuart J. Couper. Chapman's 1942 list of introduced Pacific Northwest fishes had omitted this species.

**Marine records and nomenclature**

- **[[Psenes Regulus]]:** Four small to large adults taken by the [[Albatross Expedition]] off North and South Carolina, extending a previously single record from off Beaufort, N.C. Dorsal fin rays are XI, I, 15 in all four, and the specimens were probably taken near the surface. Goode and Bean's 1895 record under *P. pellucidus* and *P. maculatus* was attributed to a misidentified specimen and is considered erroneous.
- **[[Psenes Pellucidus]]:** Lütken's 1880 type locality (Straits of Sunda, Java) places the species in the Indo-Pacific. The American form may be distinct, but Miller lacks sufficient material to decide. Atlantic specimens (Cape May, N.J., and east of Cape Henry, Va.) are listed in Tables 1 and 2.
- **[[Psenes Maculatus]]:** Lütken's description indicates palatine and vomerine teeth, which are absent in *Psenes*. Miller considers it improbable that the species belongs to *Psenes*. He therefore uses the informal name "*maculatus*" for the Atlantic form, pending revision, and notes that a valid name may already exist.
- **[[Psenes Cyanophrys]]:** Hubbs (1929) added this species to the continental U.S. fauna. Miller reports the most northern American record: one large adult (117 mm standard length; U.S.N.M. no. 64120) from No Man's Land, off Martha's Vineyard, Massachusetts, collected by Vinal N. Edwards in 1904.
- **[[Urophycis Chesteri]]:** Previously thought to range from the Gulf of St. Lawrence to off Cape Lookout, N.C. Longley's 29 specimens from Tortugas, Florida, could not be located for verification. The southernmost verified record is one adult (207 mm standard length; U.S.N.M. no. 132240) taken about 165 miles east of Charleston, S.C., at 316 fathoms. It has approximately 90 lateral-line scales.

**Taxonomic and nomenclatural conclusions**

- Miller argues that the Atlantic *Psenes* forms are poorly understood and that current names are unreliable.
- He proposes a tentative grouping of the species into the *pellucidus*, *cyanophrys*, and *maculatus* groups, based on fin-ray counts, scale numbers, and body proportions.
- He calls for a full revision of *Psenes* and its close allies.
