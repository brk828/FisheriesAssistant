# Quantitative Nucleic Acid Histochemistry of the Yolk Sac Syncytium of Oviparous Teleosts

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[W. E. Bachop]] and [[F. J. Schwartz]] tests whether the giant nuclei of the [[Yolk-Sac Syncytium]] (periblast) in oviparous teleost embryos carry more DNA than diploid nuclei. Using [[Feulgen Reaction]] staining and [[Microspectrophotometry]], the authors measured relative DNA content in [[Muskellunge]] embryos and [[Zebra Fish]] yolk-sac larvae. Periblast nuclei in both species contained roughly four times or more the DNA of presumably diploid nuclei, with some reaching about 20 times. The authors argue that yolk resorption hypotheses should account for this somatic polyploidy and syncytialization.

## Background & Description

The muskellunge (*Esox masquinongy ohioensis*, Esocidae) is a freshwater predatory fish. Earlier work by Bachop (1965) reported periblast nuclei in its embryos that appeared to have higher chromatin content based on size and dye staining. This study aimed to verify that observation quantitatively.

The authors reviewed the principles behind [[Microphotometry]]. Beer's law links light absorption to dye concentration, but dye content depends on both concentration and nuclear volume. Measuring whole asymmetrical nuclei is difficult because of the [[Schwarzschild-Villiger Effect]], stray light passing around the nuclear border. The authors used the [[Two-Wave Length Method]] (Patau, 1952; Mendelsohn, 1958), which corrects for this artifact and does not require homogeneous chromatophore distribution. They also addressed objections that the Feulgen reaction is non-stoichiometric, citing Pollister and Ornstein (1955) and Kasten (1960) in support of its use for relative DNA estimation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Muskellunge embryos:** Fertilized eggs were incubated in hatchery jars, with water temperatures rising from 13.3°C to 18.3°C. Embryos were sampled at 95 h (1/3 developed), 163 h (2/3 developed) and 238 h (just hatching). Embryos were fixed in ethanol-acetic acid (3:1) for 4 h at about 25°C.
- **Zebra fish larvae:** Yolk-sac larvae were sampled on the day of hatching and processed by the same general protocol, with modifications to hydrolysis time and refractive index.
- **Yolk-sac anatomy:** The periblast lies between the yolk and the embryonic blood vessels. Yolk-derived substances reach the embryonic bloodstream without passing through the embryonic gut, which develops separately into the adult gut.

## Key Findings

- **Muskellunge (two-wave length method):** At 95 h, most periblast nuclei held about four times the dye content (presumed DNA) of non-periblast nuclei. At 163 h, the majority still showed about fourfold excess. The number of periblast nuclei too irregular to measure increased by this stage.
- **Replicate precision:** In a replicate test on two nuclei (10 replicates each), the periblast nucleus mean was 82.0 (SD 2.4, SE 0.8) and the non-periblast nucleus mean was 19.8 (SD 0.6, SE 0.2). Instrument and operator variation was much smaller than the difference between nucleus types.
- **Histograms:** Frequency distributions were plotted for 25 periblast and 20 non-periblast nuclei at 1/3 development, and for 40 periblast and 20 non-periblast nuclei at 2/3 development.
- **Zebra fish (scanning integrating microdensitometry):** Twenty giant periblast nuclei were compared with five small non-periblast nuclei each, from the same larvae. Total optical density ratios ranged from 4:1 to 20:1. The 20:1 ratio was found in trilobed and bilobed nuclei.
- **Interpretation:** Excess DNA is organized as extra chromosome sets (polyploidy) without an apparent increase in DNA per chromosome, according to Bachop and Price (1971). The authors propose that yolk-resorption models should include a functional role for somatic polyploidy and syncytialization, rather than assuming resorption through small diploid endodermal nuclei.
