## Executive Summary
This document is a regulatory permit amendment issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque, NM) to Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University), authorizing specific research activities under Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811. The amendment permits the capture and artificial spawning (stripping) of up to 100 ripe female [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) from [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona, along with milt collection from adult males, for the purpose of captive propagation and conservation research. This is not a primary scientific research paper but an official correspondence documenting authorized conservation/broodstock collection methodology for an endangered fish species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Habitat referenced: [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] in Arizona/Nevada, representing critical remaining habitat for wild [[Razorback Sucker]] populations.
- The species is federally listed as endangered, indicating a severely reduced and imperiled wild population — consistent with known threats to Colorado River Basin native fishes, including habitat alteration (damming/reservoir creation), non-native predatory/competing fish species, and lack of recruitment.
- The permitted activity (egg/milt collection followed by immediate release of adults at the capture site) reflects a conservation strategy designed to minimize harm to the wild population while supporting ex-situ propagation.

## Key Findings
- USFWS authorized Dr. W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) to capture up to 100 ripe female [[Razorback Sucker]] from [[Lake Mohave]] for artificial spawning (egg and milt stripping), without use of supplemental hormone injections.
- All adult fish (male and female) must be released unharmed at their original capture site immediately after gamete collection — a catch-and-release broodstock protocol.
- Collected eggs are to be transported in suitable containers to Arizona State University for controlled hatching and subsequent research, explicitly for species recovery purposes.
- This amendment builds upon and retains all other conditions from the original subpermit dated January 15, 1993.
- The permit holder is required to carry the amendment, original subpermit letter, and permit while conducting authorized field activities.
- Distribution of this amendment to multiple FWS offices (Law Enforcement, Office of Management Authority, Ecological Services, Fisheries & Federal Assistance, Arizona Fishery Resource Offices) reflects standard interagency compliance and oversight protocol for endangered species research permits under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
