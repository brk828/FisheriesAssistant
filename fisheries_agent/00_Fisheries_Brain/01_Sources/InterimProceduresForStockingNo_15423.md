# Interim Procedures for Stocking Nonnative Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #endangered-species #fisheries-management #conservation-policy #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document presents interim procedures developed cooperatively by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the states of [[Colorado]], [[Utah]], and [[Wyoming]] to regulate stocking of nonnative fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The procedures aim to ensure that all future nonnative fish stocking is consistent with recovery of four endangered Colorado River fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The interim procedures were implemented on a trial basis during spring and summer 1994, with final procedures scheduled for completion by March 1995. The document establishes specific criteria for routine stocking, case-by-case review processes, and locations where stocking is prohibited to protect endangered species habitat.

## Background & Description

The procedures fulfill requirements established in the [[Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species]] in the Upper Colorado River Basin for developing protocols to review proposed fish introductions. The Upper Basin encompasses waters in Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, excluding the [[San Juan River Basin]]. The document recognizes that both habitat alteration and nonnative species proliferation contributed to the decline of endangered Colorado River fishes, though the relative contribution of each factor is difficult to quantify definitively.

The procedures were developed based on several key principles: (1) implementation should support the Recovery Program goal of establishing naturally self-sustaining populations of endangered fishes, (2) the Recovery Program should serve as the "reasonable and prudent alternative" for certain water development projects, (3) nonnative stocking should be confined to areas where conflicts with endangered species can be demonstrated to be absent, and (4) both Service and State statutory responsibilities must be respected. The document emphasizes that the Recovery Program was not intended to abolish nonnative fish stocking but to guide it toward consistency with endangered species recovery.

The procedures identify three categories of stocking situations: (a) routine stocking that is acceptable without additional review, (b) stocking that is unacceptable, and (c) stocking requiring case-by-case evaluation. Table 1 in the document provides detailed specifications for 13 nonnative species or groups currently managed through stocking, including all salmonids (family [[Salmonidae]]), [[Triploid Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*), [[Redside Shiner]] (*Richardsonius balteatus*), [[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*), [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*), [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*), [[Mosquito Fish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Utah Chub]] (*Gila atraria*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The procedures recognize that habitat and biological communities have been significantly altered in the Upper Basin, with both habitat loss and nonnative species effects responsible for endangered fish declines. Critical habitat designations and occupied habitat areas are mapped and protected from nonnative stocking. The document establishes the 6,500 feet mean sea level contour line as a key elevation threshold, chosen by professional fishery biologists as providing reasonable confidence that stocking certain warmwater nonnatives in isolated ponds above this elevation will not adversely impact listed species due to unsuitable coldwater stream conditions.

Floodplain criteria are central to the procedures. The 40-year and 100-year floodplains related to critical habitat were to be mapped and made available by October 1994. As an interim measure, "5 feet above ordinary high water line" was established to approximate the 50 to 100 year floodplain, based on professional judgment of FWS and State hydrologists. The [[Ordinary High Water Line]] (OHWL) is defined as the water level representing the water surface elevation during a normal annual high water event, physically marked where perennial hydrophytic plant life converges with bare substrate.

Specific ecological justifications are provided for each species in Table 1. For example, salmonids are considered low-risk due to their preference for cold water and limited potential for interaction with warmwater endangered species. [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] serves as an effective barrier, with deep reservoir releases precluding virtually all warmwater fish escapement. Species like [[Smallmouth Bass]] are restricted to areas separated from occupied endangered species habitat by waters containing established reproducing populations, based on the premise that additional stocking will not contribute more fish to occupied habitat than already supplied by self-sustaining populations.

## Key Findings

**Routine Stocking Criteria**: All salmonids can be stocked throughout the Upper Basin. Triploid grass carp require certificates of triploidy and must be stocked in isolated ponds/reservoirs within critical habitat above the 40-year floodplain, screened ponds, or isolated/screened ponds at or below 6,500 feet MSL located at least 5 vertical feet above OHWL of non-critical habitat waters. Species-specific restrictions apply to other nonnatives based on elevation, isolation, screening, and separation from occupied habitat.

**Prohibited Stocking**: No stocking of nonsalmonid, nonnative species is permitted in riverine habitat occupied by endangered Colorado River fishes. Occupied endangered fish habitat is displayed in Figure 1 of the document, including critical habitat reaches in the [[Green River]], [[Colorado River]], [[Yampa River]], and other tributaries.

**Case-by-Case Review Process**: Stocking of species in Table 1 outside specified locations, additional species including [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), [[Yellow Perch]] (*Perca flavescens*), [[Tiger Muskie]] (*Esox lucius* x *Esox masquinongy*), and [[Wipers]] (*Morone chrysops* x *Morone saxatilis*) require case-by-case evaluation. Proposals must include: purpose and location, species and numbers with rationale, potential for escapement and survival in critical habitat, potential impacts to threatened/endangered species with control measures, and monitoring plans.

**Section 7 ESA Consultation**: The review process distinguishes between proposals requiring [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (those involving Federal funds, permits, or approvals) and those that do not. For Federal actions, formal consultation includes "may affect" determinations, jeopardy analyses, and biological opinions with reasonable and prudent alternatives if necessary. Non-Federal proposals follow a multi-tiered State review process involving agency comments (30 days), Director-level review (30 days), and potentially State Wildlife Commission hearings.

**Previously Approved Waters**: Nine Colorado waters with approved lake management plans since inception of the Recovery Program are exempted from further review unless substantive plan changes occur: [[Rio Blanco Reservoir]], [[Hollenbeck Reservoir]], [[Mark Mesa Reservoir]], [[Chipeta Lake]], [[Crawford Reservoir]], [[Narraquinnep Reservoir]], [[Puett Reservoir]], [[Summit Reservoir]], and [[Totten Reservoir]].

**Species Not Currently Managed**: Table 2 identifies 21 nonnative fish species already occurring in the Upper Basin but not currently managed through stocking, including [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), various minnows, suckers, bullheads, and [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*). Any proposals to stock these species require case-by-case evaluation following American Fisheries Society Position Statement guidelines covering rationale, search, preliminary impact assessment, publicity and review, experimental research, evaluation, and monitored introduction.

**Reporting Requirements**: All nonsalmonid fish stocking actions, including routine and case-by-case approvals, must be reported to the Recovery Program by December 31 of the stocking year, including species, location, number, size, Table 1 criteria invoked, and written proposals for case-by-case reviews.

**Trial Period Evaluation**: The interim procedures were implemented spring-summer 1994 to assess effectiveness and operational efficiency. Components tested included availability of floodplain maps, Table 1 criteria, and case-by-case review processes. States agreed to evaluate and implement viable control options for nonnative fishes in occupied habitat during the trial period, including amending research permits to allow nonnative removal and modifying harvest restrictions. Final procedures were scheduled for fall 1994 revision and March 1995 finalization, with [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] to develop regulations for aquaculture industry stocking in 1996 and beyond.
