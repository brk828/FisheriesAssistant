## Executive Summary
This document is a 1997 public comment letter from the Yuma Valley Rod and Gun Club (YVRGC) addressed to the Bureau of Reclamation (BR) regarding the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Biological Opinion (BO) and the associated Reasonable and Prudent Alternative (RPA) for Lower Colorado River (LCR) operations (LC-2000). Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research, the letter critiques regulatory Provision #2 of the RPA, which would prohibit BR from developing additional backwaters for sport fisheries. The author argues this restriction conflicts with the BO's own findings regarding the importance of backwater habitat for the recovery of two federally endangered fish species: the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The letter serves as a valuable secondary source, citing specific pages of the BO that document the ecological role of backwaters and the historical decline of this habitat type on the LCR.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., length, weight, meristics, coloration) for the [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] is provided in this source. The document is policy-focused rather than a biological survey. For morphological characterization of these species, refer to the underlying USFWS Biological Opinion (BO) cited within this letter (specifically pages 39, 41, 47, 82–83, 89, 94–96, and 132).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The letter, via its citations of the BO, establishes the following ecological context for the target species:

- **Critical Habitat Type:** Backwaters and marshes along the LCR are identified as critical habitat for both the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Life Stage Use:** Backwaters provide essential slow-water refugia for **both young and adult fish**, which are increasingly scarce due to habitat loss ("fewer places for young and adult fish to find slow water areas").
- **Water Quality Dynamics:** Bank percolation provides some "freshening" of backwater habitats, but this process is often insufficient to counteract high evaporation rates, which can degrade water quality in isolated backwaters.
- **Habitat Structure Preference:** Backwaters and marshes featuring "more open inlet structures" are noted as superior habitat, providing critical organic material inputs to the aquatic system.
- **Historical Habitat Loss:** Significant backwater habitat above Imperial Dam was lost during the early 1980s, attributed to high flow events that transported large amounts of silt into these areas, effectively filling them in.
- **Co-occurring Endangered/Sensitive Species:** The same backwater and adjacent wetland habitats support the endangered **Yuma Clapper Rail** and **Southwestern Willow Flycatcher**, as well as general waterfowl populations, particularly near the [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] (INWR).
- **Non-native Species Interaction:** The BO itself acknowledges that non-native fish species are the primary beneficiaries of existing BR-implemented habitat projects on the LCR, raising concerns about competitive exclusion or resource use by non-natives over the target endangered natives.

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory Conflict:** The YVRGC contends that RPA Provision #2 (barring new backwater/sport fishery development) is inconsistent with the BO's own documented reliance of endangered fish on backwater habitats.
- **Executive Order Conflict:** The letter asserts that Provision #2 does not comply with **Executive Order 12962** (dated 6/8/1995), which relates to recreational fisheries.
- **Disputed Historical Claim:** The letter directly challenges a statement on BO page 134 asserting that past mitigation for LCR channelization projects provided no benefit to the [[Bonytail Chub]] or [[Razorback Sucker]]. The author argues there is insufficient evidence that these species were even present at the mitigation sites to have utilized them, questioning the validity of the conclusion.
- **Recommendation:** The YVRGC urges BR to re-evaluate the Biological Opinion thoroughly, arguing that restoration projects between Imperial Dam and the northern boundary of the INWR could integrate with, rather than contradict, long-term RPA recovery goals.
- **Mitigation Obligations:** The letter concedes that BR retains responsibility for mitigating habitat loss affecting the Southwestern Willow Flycatcher, both retroactively and for future operational impacts.
- **Stakeholder Concern:** Represents a sport-fishing stakeholder perspective (YVRGC) advocating for continued backwater development for recreational purposes, in tension with endangered species habitat protection mandates under the RPA.
