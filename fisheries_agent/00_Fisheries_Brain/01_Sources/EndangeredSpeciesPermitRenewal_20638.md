# Virgin River Fishes Subpermit Renewal and Collection Records (1992–1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #virgin-river-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a December 20, 1993 letter from [[Utah State University]] (Institute for Natural Systems Engineering) to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] requesting renewal of [[Endangered Species Subpermit PRT-676811]]. The renewal would cover population monitoring and life history studies of the [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]], and [[Virgin River Spinedace]] across the Virgin River Basin in Utah, Arizona, and Nevada. The work is aimed at determining instream flow requirements, a priority item in the [[Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan]]. Most of the attachments are site-by-site collection tables for 1992 and 1993, plus [[Geographic Information System Mapping]] figures showing sampling stations and spinedace distribution.

## Background & Description

- **Requester and permit:** The letter responds to a Region 2 correspondence dated October 25, 1993. It enclosed cover sheets of subpermits issued by Regions 1, 2, and 6 under which 1993 collections were authorized.
- **Target species:** Woundfin (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and Virgin River roundtail chub (*Gila robusta seminuda*) are the focal listed species. Virgin River spinedace is included because it had been petitioned for listing. Its collection covers all perennial tributaries of the Virgin River.
- **Study scope:** The original study plan from the previous year's permit application continues through December 31, 1994. Work is supported by U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service staff and fisheries agency personnel from Utah, Arizona, and Nevada.
- **Protocols:** The study plan was reviewed by the existing Virgin River Fishes Recovery Team. Sampling follows the protocols in the Recovery Plan for these species.
- **Data management:** Collection data were incorporated into the [[Virgin River Fishes Data Base]], which was sent to U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 6 and to state fish and game offices in Arizona, Nevada, and Utah.

### Collection Table Structure

The attached tables use the following fields:
- Stream ID code (alpha characters) followed by river mile, read moving upstream
- Date of collection and area sampled
- Number of depletion samples from a single habitat
- Total fish collected, and counts for woundfin, red shiner, roundtail chub, spinedace, speckled dace, flannelmouth sucker, desert sucker, and miscellaneous species (the latter detailed in the database)

The key lists 25 stream codes, including [[Beaver Dam Wash]] (BDW), [[East Fork Virgin River]] (EFV), [[North Fork Virgin River]] (NFV), [[North Creek]] (NC), [[Santa Clara River]] (SC), [[Moody Wash]] (MW), [[Magotsu Creek]] (MGC), [[Shunes Creek]] (SHC), and the [[Virgin River]] main stem (VR).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Basin extent:** Sampling covered the Virgin River from its headwaters to its terminus at [[Lake Mead]], plus its tributaries. GIS base maps locate all collection sites.
- **Stream conditions:** Spinedace distribution mapping distinguishes four categories: occupied by spinedace; perennial but not occupied; naturally dry; and dewatered due to diversions. Dewatering by diversions is a key concern for instream flow work. The maps cover Beaver Dam Wash and East Fork, the Santa Clara River, Moody Wash, and Magotsu Creek, Quail, Leeds, Ash, La Verkin, and North Creeks, and the North Fork, East Fork, and Shunes Creek systems.
- **Sampling design:** Collections used depletion sampling of single habitats. Reported areas per sample range from under 10 m² at some small sites to several hundred m² at larger reaches, and sampling dates span April through September in 1992 and 1993.

## Key Findings

- **Permit purpose:** Renewal is requested to continue population monitoring and life history studies for the three fishes through December 31, 1994.
- **Instream flow priority:** Determining instream flow requirements for these species is a priority in the Virgin River Fishes Recovery Plan.
- **Reporting commitments:**
  - A status and distribution report on spinedace in the Virgin River Basin would be sent in early January 1994.
  - A final technical report on main stem Virgin River species and instream flow recommendations would be completed during 1994.
- **1992 collections:** The 1992 summary tables (pages 3–13 of the attachment) list hundreds of site records across the basin, sampled mainly June through September 1992 under several federal permits (PRT-676811, SP 90-11, PRT-704930, PRT-702631). Examples include:
  - NC200 (09/21/92, 550 m² area): 1,175 total fish, with 168 and 638 in two species columns.
  - Large main stem catches such as VR151.30 (06/22/92, 50 m² area): 997 total fish, with 952 in one species column.
- **1993 collections:** The 1993 table (page 14) covers stations sampled in July 1993 under federal permits. Notable records include:
  - VR133.50 (07/06/93): 1,758 total fish, including 1,022 spinedace and 408 speckled dace.
  - VR122.80 (07/07/93): 54 woundfin.
  - VR127.80 (07/07/93): 59 woundfin.
  - Woundfin also appear at VR119.40, VR120.90, and VR102.50, but in small numbers.
- **Distribution maps:** Figures 1 and 3–6 map spinedace presence and absence, perennial reaches, naturally dry reaches, and diversion-dewatered reaches across the basin. Figure 1 gives the basin-wide overview, and Figures 3–6 give station-level maps by tributary group.
- **Data sharing:** Results were shared with federal and state agencies through the Virgin River Fishes Data Base, and GIS maps were provided to support the permit review.
