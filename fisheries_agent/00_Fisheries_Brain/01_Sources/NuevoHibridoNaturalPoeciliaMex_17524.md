# New Natural Hybrid *Poecilia mexicana* x *P. latipunctata* from the Rio Tamesi, Tamaulipas, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This short report formally describes the first recorded natural hybrid between two poeciliid fishes, [[Poecilia Mexicana]] and [[Poecilia Latipunctata]]. A single adult male was collected in the [[Rio Guayalejo-Tamesi]] near [[Llera De Canales]], [[Tamaulipas]], Mexico, in 1964 and deposited in the [[Universidad Autonoma De Nuevo Leon]] fish collection. The two putative parent species are morphologically very similar, so the hybrid is difficult to confirm quantitatively. It was recognized in the field mainly by its nuptial male coloration, a blend of the coloration of both parents. The author notes that the collecting site is strongly disturbed by seasonal floods and gravel extraction, and suggests that hybridization may be linked to such environmental disturbance.

## Background & Description

**Taxa involved**
- *Poecilia mexicana* (the parent with gray to black males, yellow ventral parts and yellow distal caudal fin).
- *Poecilia latipunctata* (the parent with bright gray-to-blue males, dark bars on the flanks and dark blue spots on the dorsal and anal fins).
- The hybrid is an F1 individual. Its parentage was inferred from morphology and coloration, not from genetic analysis.

**Specimens examined** (catalogue numbers from the source; counts as stated)
- Hybrid: UANL 413, one male, 42.7 mm standard length (SL), collected 7 May 1964 (Col. S. Contreras et al.).
- *P. mexicana*: UANL 414, 137 specimens, 15.0-52.1 mm SL.
- *P. latipunctata*: UANL 412, 549 specimens, 12.4-44.1 mm SL.

**Methods**
The comparison was descriptive. Ranges and means of morphometric and meristic characters were compared among the putative hybrid and both parents. Morphometric data came from 10 males of each parent species plus the single hybrid. The author notes that F1 hybrids often show intermediate traits, traits resembling one parent, traits more extreme than either parent, or mixed traits. Hybrids are frequently sterile and often masculinized (citing Contreras, in preparation).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area**
The [[Rio Guayalejo-Tamesi]] near the national highway bridge (Highway 85) is described as:
- Clear, hard water, 0.5-1.0 m deep, with deeper pools.
- Bottom of silt over gravel or rubble, gray to bluish.
- A highly dynamic channel. [[Seasonal Flooding]] causes pools to connect, disconnect and shift position readily.
- Numerous excavations for construction materials (sand, gravel, cobble). The environmental impact of this [[Gravel Extraction]] has not been measured.
- Water temperature during observations was 27 °C.
- Coordinates given: N 23°17', W 99°02'.

**Disturbance and hybridization**
The author reports that natural hybrids in Mexico are appearing more often and are associated with environmental disturbance. The author therefore considers hybrids to be useful ecological indicators. The paper also proposes that hybrids may appear mainly during periods of strong environmental impact that alter the river channel.

## Key Findings

**1. Hybrid identification and morphology**
- The hybrid male is intermediate or mosaic in many characters, and resembles the parents in others. Within the 14 characters considered, the morphometric and meristic values of the hybrid overlap strongly with both parents, so no single character separates the three entities reliably.
- The gonopodium is slightly shorter in the hybrid than in both parents (215.7 vs. about 223 in *P. latipunctata*, per the source table).
- The hybrid exceeds both parents in maximum body height, minimum caudal peduncle depth and pectoral fin length. The author suggests these may reflect hybrid vigor.
- The hybrid is more similar to *P. mexicana* in interorbital distance, pelvic fin length, pectoral ray count and peduncle scale count.
- The hybrid is more similar to *P. latipunctata* in depressed dorsal fin length, eye diameter, predorsal distance and dorsal ray count.

**2. Nuptial coloration (field recognition)**
- The hybrid's body is intense bright blue, with dark blue margins on the scales forming longitudinal rows of spots, a trait of *P. latipunctata*.
- The ventral parts (throat, chest, belly) are yellow, a trait of *P. mexicana*.
- The dorsal fin is blue with dark spots and a thin yellow border. The distal third of the caudal fin is yellow, with blue spots on the basal two-thirds.
- Residual traces of these markings are visible in the paper's Figure 1 (center).

**3. Parental species coloration (for comparison)**
- *P. mexicana* males are variable: gray to black bodies with dark scale margins, yellowish ventral parts and often yellowish lateral spots. The distal third of the dorsal and caudal fins is yellow.
- *P. latipunctata* nuptial males are less variable: bright gray to blue bodies with dark lateral bars and a usually silvery belly. Their dorsal and anal fins carry numerous dark blue spots.

**4. Conclusions and cautions**
- Because the parents are so similar, the hybrid is hard to detect morphologically. This may explain why it has not been reported earlier, despite many collections in the area (Darnell, 1962).
- Recognition in the field requires careful observation and notes, and good familiarity with both parent species.
- Hybrids may occur mainly during strong environmental disturbance that alters the river channel. This remains a hypothesis.
- The author formally reports the first record of this hybrid combination and its locality, after the combination had been cited in several publications following letter inquiries.

**5. Context and prior records**
- Earlier world hybrid checklists (Slastenenko, 1957; Schwartz, 1972, 1981) did not list this combination. Schultz and Miller (1971) report being aware of the record.
- Relevant literature includes Darnell (1962) on the fishes of the Rio Tamesi and coastal lagoons, and Menzel and Darnell (1973) on the systematics of *P. mexicana* in northern Mexico.
