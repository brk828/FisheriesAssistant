# Arizona State-Wide Fishery Investigations: Periodic Survey of Region VII Waters, 1966

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This is the Job Completion Report for the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] Fish Management Division's State-Wide Fishery Investigations (Federal Aid Project F-7-R-9 and FS-2, January 1 to December 31, 1966). The work covered reservoirs and streams in Region VII, chiefly [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[San Carlos Lake]], and two mountain creeks stocked with Arizona native trout. The main methods were night electrofishing and cove [[Rotenone]] sampling, supplemented by a reconnaissance of black crappie. The report found good [[Largemouth Bass]] spawning survival, no young-of-the-year [[Black Crappie]] at either reservoir, and high water levels following heavy runoff. It recommends continued summer cove sampling, electrofishing in September or October, continued monitoring of the native trout, and [[Diquat]] treatment for weed control at [[Riggs Flat Lake]].

## Background & Description

The project was carried out by the Fish Management Division under Project Leader Bruce and Assistant Project Leader Essbach, with Gruenewald as Coordinator. Staff named on the report include Robert W. White, Levi Packard, Wes Martin, Don Smith, T. O. Barnes, Ted Knipe, Don Wingfield and Louis H. Carufel. Don Wingfield, Regional Supervisor, signed the final section, and Wm. W. Taylor (Federal Aid Coordinator) approved it.

The stated objective was to determine current physical, chemical and biological conditions in the region's waters so that management techniques could be evaluated. Sampling used a large shocker on Roosevelt Lake and a small shocker on San Carlos Lake, with a small back-pack unit used on the creeks. Work was done at night. Cove sampling used rotenone applied to small, representative cove areas, with weights, lengths and numbers of fish recorded.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water levels:** Both reservoirs were high during the year. The report attributes this to a heavy runoff in the spring of 1965 and 1966. Roosevelt Lake storage, from Salt River Project readings, ran roughly from 1.2 to 1.36 million acre-feet (values are approximate because of scan quality). San Carlos Lake acre-feet readings are also tabulated for 1965–1966. The high water delayed the San Carlos electrofishing by about two months, and the report suggests it may have spread largemouth bass more widely.
- **Species assemblage:** Roosevelt Lake cove samples contained largemouth bass, green sunfish, channel catfish, bluegill, carp, buffalo, mosquitofish, yellow bullhead and large schools of shad. Bluegill, green sunfish and carp were abundant throughout the lake, and shad were concentrated in the eastern coves.
- **Predator-prey balance:** The ratio of largemouth bass young-of-the-year to green sunfish young-of-the-year was about 1:1 in the sampled coves, which the authors regard as a generally good predator–prey balance.
- **Black crappie:** Crappie were scarce. An intensive study by the Research Division in 1966 used hoop nets, gill nets, electrofishing, rotenone, trammel nets and SCUBA. It found an adult population present but not numerous, and young crappie were not abundant.
- **Arizona native trout:** Trout were introduced into Ash and Grant Creeks in the Graham Mountains in 1965. The aim was to establish a pure strain of [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gila*) and to track its growth and survival.

## Key Findings

### Roosevelt Lake
- **Cove rotenone sampling (June 27–28, 1966):** Five cove areas were treated, each averaging about 2,500 sq ft (the text also refers to four cove sections in one passage). The total treated surface area is partly illegible in the scan. Largemouth bass, green sunfish, channel catfish and other species were counted by total length, with ranges reported for each group. Largemouth bass young-of-the-year were abundant, and most fish were in the 1–3 inch range.
- **Electrofishing (November 1966):** The large shocker was used on seven nights. The report gives 750 bass sampled, and separately 324 largemouth bass are reported from electrofishing, with the 8–10 inch size group predominant. Comparison counts were 498 bass in 1965 and 585 in 1964. Only 24 crappie were taken.
- **Crappie:** No young-of-the-year crappie were found in the 1966 cove sampling. Only one crappie was found in 1965. The report states that crappie remain at a very low level.
- **Largemouth bass:** Bass were less abundant during rotenone sampling than in the previous year, possibly because high water spread the fish more widely.

### San Carlos Lake
- **Cove rotenone sampling (September 28, 1966):** Largemouth bass, yearling largemouth bass, green sunfish, bluegill and yellow bullhead were recorded. Length and weight ranges are tabulated (for example, adult largemouth bass 2.9 to 3.9 inches and yearlings 7.0 to 8.8 inches, with averages given).
- **Electrofishing (November 1966 and January 1967):** Some planted crappie were found. Crappie reproduction was not detected during cove sampling.
- **Recommendation:** Add a crappie plant in San Carlos Lake during 1967 unless good reproduction is indicated by summer cove sampling.

### Ash and Grant Creeks
- **Survey (September 1966):** 39 native trout were examined in Ash Creek and 28 in Grant Creek. The trout had been introduced at 2.5 inches on September 9, 1965. Lengths reached 5.3 inches in Grant Creek.
- **Distribution:** Most fish were found near the stocking sites, but some had moved considerable distances, especially in Ash Creek.

### Recommendations
1. Continue summer cove sampling on Roosevelt and San Carlos Lakes to evaluate annual reproduction and track year-to-year changes.
2. Continue electrofishing on both reservoirs, ideally in September or October.
3. Continue sampling Grant and Ash Creeks to maintain growth and survival records for the native trout.
4. Plan for weed control at Riggs Flat Lake in the summer of 1967, using [[Diquat]].
