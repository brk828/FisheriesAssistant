# Lower Colorado River Native Fish Recovery Correspondence and Reports (2010 to 2011)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This compiled document is a set of 2010 to early 2011 materials from the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) on the recovery of two endangered Colorado River fishes, [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. It includes a database-change email, fisheries recapture histories, a [[Davis Cove]] sampling report, larval collection goals, hatchery summaries, stocking records, a distribution list, and meeting agendas. The main points are the database update for backwater growth-study fish, a 2010 rearing season at [[Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility]], the January 2010 Davis Cove harvest and release, and planning for 2011 larval collection and stocking. Much of the document is tables and scan debris, so it is summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Background & Description

The document centers on two endangered, long-lived fishes of the Lower Colorado River:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*, Catostomidae): the most frequently discussed species. Individuals were PIT-tagged, tracked by tag frequency (134 kHz and 400 kHz), and stocked or repatriated.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*, Cyprinidae): reared at hatcheries for repatriation to [[Lake Havasu]] and the Parker Strip. Bonytail over 300 mm total length were wire-tagged.

The document also mentions [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] in a 2011 presentation list and a habitat-use study, but no data for them are reported.

Agencies and organizations named include the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (USBR), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), National Park Service (NPS), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD), Nevada Department of Wildlife (NDOW), USGS, and several consulting firms. Key facilities are [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and [[Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Backwater and reservoir habitat.** [[Davis Cove]] operates as a native fish sanctuary and nursery pond on [[Lake Mohave]], established in 2005. Other sites named for stocking or sampling include Yuma Cove, Owl Point, Wrong Cove, Liberty Cove, Redtail Cove, Teouila Cove, Nine Mile Area, and Valhala Cove. Backwaters are important as nursery habitat, and the document notes that the Davis Cove function would shift toward producing larger fish.

**Riverine release areas.** Fish were repatriated "below Hoover Dam" on the [[Colorado River]]. Released bonytail went to [[Lake Havasu]] and the Parker Strip.

**Non-native fish.** Non-native fish were excluded at Achii Hanyo by continuous water filtration. During the January 2010 Davis Cove sampling, no non-native fish were observed or contacted.

**Water level.** The Davis Cove report notes that a higher water level in January 2010 likely dispersed young fish, reducing catches of the small-size net.

## Key Findings

### 1. NFWG Database Update (June 25, 2010)
- The NFWG database was changed to include backwater data for PIT-tagged, growth-study fish (2007 to date). Previously, backwater fish data had been excluded from the main database.
- Older backwater data, including Davis Cove fish data and growth-study data from fish held at Willow Beach NFH, were **not** updated.
- For backwater growth-study fish, the record now shows the original backwater origin in the "History" column, with subsequent captures for growth monitoring.
- Data sent to the database should be labeled by capture type (e.g., "recapture," "new tag," "YOY") to keep history accurate.

### 2. Below Hoover Dam Recapture Activity (2010)
- An August 17, 2010 USBR Fisheries crew electrofished below Hoover Dam and contacted **30** endangered razorback suckers. The reported tag status is 26 with 134 kHz PIT tags, 2 with 400 kHz tags, and 2 with no tag detected. The original numbers are partly garbled in the scan, so these counts are approximate.
- Tag histories were listed for 30 fish (Table 1) with repatriate and release events from 2005 to 2010 at sites including Willow Beach, Bubbling Ponds, Dexter NFH, Yuma Cove, BC Wetlands Park, and Placer Cove.
- Several fish had no prior history and received a 134 kHz tag in the field.

### 3. Davis Cove Sampling (January 12, 2010)
- Davis Cove had been used as a sanctuary since 2005 and had received multiple stockings. Minimal harvest efforts for larger fish (>450 mm) occurred in 2007, 2008, and 2009.
- Gill nets (2-inch and 3-inch mesh, and a 0.5-inch mesh set for one hour) were used. Hoop nets were not used.
- A total of **47** razorback suckers were contacted:
  - **39** were released into Lake Mohave, average length **466 mm**.
  - **5** more (329 to 408 mm) were returned to the backwater.
  - **3** were found dead (390, 394, and 430 mm).
- **26** released fish had no tag and were tagged before release. They likely came from the March 2007 stocking.
- No bonytail were contacted.
- Most fish were caught in the 2-inch mesh. The fish were skinny, and the goal was to remove larger fish to improve the condition of the remaining population.

### 4. Achii Hanyo Native Fish Rearing Facility (2010 Season)
- The facility is a satellite of Willow Beach NFH, located on the Colorado River Indian Tribes Reservation, about five miles south of Parker, Arizona. It has **seven** earthen ponds (0.17 to 1.36 acres, about 5 surface acres, about 23 acre-feet).
- Initial pond densities ranged from 10 to 168 lbs/acre-foot. Harvest densities ranged from 151 to 737 lbs/acre-foot.
- Survival to harvest was **64%** for bonytail and **52%** for razorback sucker overall. Several ponds had bonytail survival of **85%**.
- **3,093** bonytail reached 300 mm or more (about **34%** of surviving bonytail). Among razorbacks, about **97%** of survivors reached the target size, and **234** reached 400 mm or more.
- About **42,000** recruits were produced across all ponds in 2010.
- The facility did not reach its 2010 production goal. The last two years averaged **3,836** bonytail over 300 mm, and the document sets a 2011 need of **4,128** bonytail over target size to reach a 4,000-fish annual average.

### 5. Lake Mohave Harvested Backwater YOY and Experimental Fish (2010)
- Harvested backwater YOY totaled **3,204** fish (Dandy Cove 42; Arizona Juvenile 3,162), released mainly at Dandy Cove and Arizona Juvenile lakeside sites.
- Experimental fish from Jim Stolberg's study totaled **742**, released at Yuma Cove backwater and North Nine Mile sites. Of these, **308** were released at N. Nine Mile and Valhala Cove lakeside.

### 6. Razorback Sucker Larval Collection Goals (2010)
- The 2010 larval collection target for Lake Mohave was **33,000** razorback sucker larvae, distributed across 9-Mile, Yuma, and other sites. Weekly goals ran from January through May, with peak targets in March.
- Historic larval capture totals are reported by year and site (Nine Mile, Tequila, Yuma, AOP, 1991 to 2010). The total larvae captured from Lake Mohave to date is **733,905**.

### 7. Razorback Stocking from Willow Beach (End of 2009 to 2010)
- All razorback suckers were PIT-tagged by the USBR team and stocked by Willow Beach staff.
- The total stocked in December 2009 was **6,383**. In 2010, **3,660** had been stocked into Lake Mohave to date.
- Stocking sites included Wrong Cove, Liberty Cove, Redtail Cove, Owl Point, Yuma Cove, Teouila Cove, and Nine Mile Area. Average stocked length was roughly **374 to 381 mm**.
- Staff stocked fish into Wrong Cove instead of Liberty Cove and Redtail Cove, an error noted in the record.

### 8. Willow Beach NFH FY 2010 Summary
- Bonytail were transferred from Dexter NFH and Uvalde to Willow Beach and Achii Hanyo. Bonytail were stocked at Williams Refuge, Cattail Cove, River Island State Park, and La Paz County Park, mostly at 325 to 1,000 mm.
- As of the summary, **965** razorbacks were available for backwater stocking. An additional group of razorbacks was stocked after 10/1/2010 (count partly garbled in scan).

### 9. Meeting Agendas and Scheduling
- **NFWG meeting (January 13, 2011, Laughlin NV):** review of 2010 activities (larval collections, lakeside rearing, adult monitoring, database), 2011 scheduling (helicopter surveys, larval and adult collections, backwater stocking, March roundup), and business items including quagga mussel research, razorback genetics, and razorback sources.
- **CRAB meeting (January 12–13, 2011, Laughlin NV):** researchers from the Mexican border to Lake Powell present 2010 findings in five- to ten-minute talks, capped at 15 minutes. Topics include bonytail survival, razorback telemetry, flannelmouth habitat use, and humpback chub.
- The distribution list includes roughly 80 names from state, federal, tribal, and consulting agencies (not reproduced).
