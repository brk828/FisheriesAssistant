# Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program (LCR MSCP): Questions & Answers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #conservation-planning #endangered-species-act #habitat-conservation-plan

## Executive Summary

The Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program (LCR MSCP) is a proposed 50-year coordinated conservation approach for the [[Lower Colorado River]] basin involving federal, state, tribal, and private stakeholders. The program aims to conserve habitat and work toward recovery of threatened and endangered species while accommodating current water diversions and power production. More than 50 federal- or state-listed, candidate, and sensitive species will be addressed, with initial focus on core species including endangered [[Bonytail]] (Gila cypha), [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (Empidonax traillii extimus), [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (Rallus longirostris yumanensis), and [[American Peregrine Falcon]] (Falco peregrinus anatum). The program area extends from below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to the Southerly International Boundary with Mexico within the 100-year floodplain.

## Background & Description

The LCR MSCP represents a partnership among multiple agencies and organizations responding to the need to balance legal use of [[Lower Colorado River]] water resources with conservation of threatened and endangered species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). Participating entities include:

**Federal Agencies**: [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[National Park Service]]

**State Agencies**: [[Arizona Department of Water Resources]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Colorado River Board of California]], [[California Department of Fish and Game]], [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]], [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]]

**Tribal Nations**: [[Chemehuevi Tribe]], [[Cocopah Indian Tribe]], [[Colorado River Indian Tribes]], [[Fort Mojave Indian Tribe]], [[Fort Yuma Quechan Tribe]], [[Hualapai Tribe]]

**Water and Power Entities**: [[Arizona Power Authority]], [[Central Arizona Project]], [[Coachella Valley Water District]], [[Los Angeles Department of Water and Power]], [[Imperial Irrigation District]], [[Metropolitan Water District of Southern California]], [[Nevada Power Company]], [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]], and numerous other water districts and power authorities

The program will prepare a combined Environmental Impact Statement/Environmental Impact Report/Biological Assessment (EIS/EIR/BA) to fulfill requirements of NEPA, CEQA, federal ESA, and California ESA. The [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] serve as joint federal lead agencies under NEPA, while the [[Metropolitan Water District of Southern California]] is the designated CEQA lead agency.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The biological scope encompasses aquatic, wetland, riparian, and upland habitats within the [[Lower Colorado River]] basin. The program focuses on moving threatened and endangered species toward recovery and preventing future listings of "at risk" species.

**Core Species** (initial focus):
- **Endangered**: [[Bonytail]] (Gila cypha), [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus), [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (Empidonax traillii extimus), [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] (Rallus longirostris yumanensis), [[American Peregrine Falcon]] (Falco peregrinus anatum)
- **Threatened**: [[Bald Eagle]] (Haliaeetus leucocephalus) - being considered for delisting
- **Sensitive Species**: [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (Gila elegans), [[California Black Rail]] (Laterallus jamaicensis coturniculus), [[Yellow-billed Cuckoo]] (Coccyzus americanus) - under review for ESA listing, [[Occult Little Brown Bat]] (Myotis occultus), [[Threecorner Milkvetch]] (Astragalus tricarinatus), [[Sticky Buckwheat]] (Eriogonum viscidulum), [[Colorado River Toad]] (Incilius alvarius), [[Osprey]] (Pandion haliaetus), [[Desert Rosy Boa]] (Lichanura trivirgata gracia), [[American White Pelican]] (Pelecanus erythrorhynchos), [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (Kinosternon sonoriense), [[California Brown Pelican]] (Pelecanus occidentalis californicus), [[American Bittern]] (Botaurus lentiginosus), [[Arizona Bell's Vireo]] (Vireo bellii arizonae), [[Belted Kingfisher]] (Megaceryle alcyon), [[Clark's Grebe]] (Aechmophorus clarkii), [[Cooper's Hawk]] (Accipiter cooperii), [[Elf Owl]] (Athene cunicularia), [[Gila Woodpecker]] (Melanerpes uropygialis), [[Gilded Flicker]] (Colaptes chrysoides), [[Long-eared Owl]] (Asio otus), [[Merlin]] (Falco columbarius), [[Northern Harrier]] (Circus hudsonius), [[Sharp-shinned Hawk]] (Accipiter striatus)

The program will employ an ecosystem-based approach for interim and long-term compliance with applicable endangered species and environmental regulations. Conservation measures are expected to include maintaining or creating backwaters (wetlands) and reestablishing native riparian habitat essential to species recovery.

## Key Findings

**Program Purposes**:
1. Conserve habitat and work toward recovery of threatened and endangered species; reduce likelihood of additional ESA listings
2. Accommodate current water diversions and power production; optimize opportunities for future water and power development
3. Provide basis for federal ESA and California ESA compliance via incidental take authorizations

**Environmental Compliance Documentation**:
The EIS/EIR/BA will contain: (1) Proposed Action and [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] for ESA Section 10 permit application; (2) Alternatives; (3) No Action Alternative; (4) [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s Biological Assessment for ongoing and future actions within its legal authority. The document will provide basis for ESA compliance through Section 7 consultation for federal actions and Section 10 permit for non-federal actions.

**Preliminary Environmental Issues Identified**:

- **Biological Resources**: Overall effects expected to be positive due to conservation focus on species and habitat within 100-year floodplain
- **Hydrology and Water Quality**: Conservation measures and flow regimes may alter onsite water resources; Section 404 Clean Water Act permit may be required from [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]]
- **Floodplains and Wetlands**: Overall beneficial impacts expected, especially for maintaining/creating backwaters and reestablishing native riparian habitat
- **Municipal and Industrial Uses**: May be affected by conservation measures requiring additional water; program intends to accommodate these uses while protecting species
- **Cultural Resources**: Program will evaluate potential effects on archaeological resources, traditional cultural properties, Indian Sacred Sites, and Indian Trust Assets
- **Socioeconomics**: May have overall beneficial effects; extent unknown until specific alternatives identified
- **Recreation**: May affect localized recreational uses including motorized boating, off-highway vehicle use, and angling
- **Water and Hydroelectric Power Uses**: May be affected by conservation measures involving discretionary release patterns; program intends to accommodate these uses
- **Agricultural and Other Land Uses**: Current agricultural operations may be impacted; land use and cropping patterns would change with voluntary conversion of agricultural lands to native riparian habitat or transfer of water rights
- **International Impacts**: Potential trans-boundary impacts to Mexico will be identified and analyzed; project will not affect water delivery pursuant to Mexican Water Treaty
- **Environmental Justice**: Anticipated that program will not result in disproportionately high and adverse effects on minorities and/or low-income populations

**Public Scoping Process**:
Public scoping meetings scheduled for June-July 1999 in [[Lake Havasu City]], [[Laughlin]], [[Henderson]], [[Yuma]], [[Phoenix]], [[Blythe]], and [[Ontario]]. Written comments requested by July 27, 1999. Draft EIS/EIR expected completion by June 2000.

**Related Documentation**:
Program will utilize [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Description and Assessment of Operations, Maintenance, and Sensitive Species of the Lower Colorado River (Final Biological Assessment, August 1996) and [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Biological and Conference Opinion on Lower Colorado River Operations and Maintenance (April 1997).
