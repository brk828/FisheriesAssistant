# Razorback Sucker Genetic Research at Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #conservation-genetics #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This letter to George Divine (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) summarizes a three-year (1992–1994) experimental program evaluating repatriation strategies for the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in a [[Lake Mohave]] backwater ("Yuma Cove," Arizona Bay). The study, conducted under the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG) program, compared three propagation methods — (1) natural in-backwater spawning by stocked wild adults, (2) manual stripping/fertilization of gametes, and (3) direct capture of wild larvae from natural lake spawning — using mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) haplotype diversity as a proxy for genetic representativeness. The key finding was that only wild-caught larvae, reared in backwaters, adequately preserved the genetic diversity of the parental adult population, whereas both backwater spawning and manual stripping methods produced genetically under-representative cohorts.

## Background & Description

While the letter is primarily genetic/methodological rather than morphological, it includes growth benchmarks for juvenile razorback suckers from the 1992 cohort:
- **Larval emergence:** Present by late February 1992 following January stocking of adults.
- **Growth Rate (1992 cohort):**
  - Maximum total length (TL) of **5.3 cm** by June 1992.
  - Maximum TL of **39.0 cm** by November 1992.
- **Target repatriation size:** Juveniles reared in backwater facilities are grown to a target of **30.0 cm TL** before release back into [[Lake Mohave]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** [[Razorback Sucker]] adults inhabit open reservoir waters (e.g., Arizona Bay, Lake Mohave) and utilize adjacent shallow backwaters (e.g., Yuma Cove) as spawning and nursery habitat.
- **Reproductive Ecology:** Adults form spawning aggregations detectable via routine surveys and aerial (helicopter) overflights, indicating spatially and temporally predictable breeding behavior within the reservoir.
- **Life History Strategy:** Wild larvae are naturally produced via in-lake spawning, then captured and reared in controlled backwater environments or hatchery facilities (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Boulder City NV; USFWS Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery) prior to repatriation — a conservation strategy designed to boost recruitment for this imperiled [[Catostomidae]] species of the Lower [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Conservation Context:** This work directly supports repatriation efforts for a species facing significant population decline, emphasizing that genetic diversity in captive-reared/repatriated fish must mirror wild population diversity to avoid unintentional genetic bottlenecking of the reintroduced stock.

## Key Findings

### Experimental Design (1992–1994)
| Year | Method | Sample Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1992 | Backwater natural spawning | 60 males + 28 females stocked in Yuma Cove; 15 juveniles sampled for genetics |
| 1993 | Manual stripping & fertilization | 20 females stripped (~50 mL ova each), fertilized with sperm from 2–3 males; ~200,000 fertilized ova stocked; 17 juveniles sampled |
| 1994 | Wild larvae captured directly | Larvae from natural in-lake spawning collected and reared in backwaters; 14 larvae sampled |

### Genetic (mtDNA Haplotype) Results
| Cohort/Sample | N (sample size) | Haplotypes Found | Haplotype Diversity (Nₐ) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1987–1988 wild adult reference | 49 | — | **0.67** |
| 1992 backwater cohort | 15 | 5 | **0.33** (under-representative) |
| 1993 parental females (source) | 18 of 20 | 16 | **0.89** |
| 1993 manual-stripped cohort | 17 | 9 | **0.53** (under-representative) |
| 1994 wild larvae cohort | 14 | 10 | **0.71** (representative; parental Nₐ ≈ 0.67–0.69) |

### Core Conclusions
1. **Natural backwater spawning (1992)** by stocked adults resulted in significant under-representation of genetic diversity — likely because far fewer than the 28 stocked females actually contributed offspring to the sampled cohort, given mtDNA's strictly maternal inheritance.
2. **Manual stripping/fertilization (1993)**, despite drawing from a highly diverse parental pool (Nₐ = 0.89), also produced an under-representative cohort (Nₐ = 0.53), because only about half of stripped females' gametes successfully contributed to the surviving cohort, causing over-representation of some maternal lineages and complete loss of others.
3. **Direct capture of wild larvae (1994)** from natural in-lake spawning, followed by backwater rearing, produced a cohort (Nₐ = 0.71) that closely matched wild adult population diversity (Nₐ ≈ 0.67–0.69) — the only method validated as genetically representative.
4. **Overall Recommendation:** Wild-caught larvae are substantially more reflective of true population-level genetic variability than larvae generated via controlled backwater spawning or artificial propagation (manual stripping), supporting a shift in [[Native Fish Work Group]] protocol toward direct larval capture as the preferred repatriation method for maintaining genetic diversity in restored [[Razorback Sucker]] populations.
