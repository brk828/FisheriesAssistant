## Executive Summary
This 1954 paper by Kemp and Hubbs documents the **first published record of the [[Cypress Minnow]]** (*Hybognathus hayi* Jordan) in Texas. Prior to this report, the species' known range was largely restricted to the Tennessee River system and Mississippi River lowlands (Arkansas and Louisiana), with no confirmed records in Texas or Oklahoma. The authors report four new collection localities from Red River tributaries in Harrison and Marion counties, northeast Texas, plus a fifth locality in Bowie County reported by George A. Moore. The paper also corrects a historical misidentification of Oklahoma specimens previously attributed to *H. hayi*.

## Background & Description
- Original description by [[David Starr Jordan]] (1884/1885) based on specimens collected by O. P. Hay from Memphis, Tennessee, and multiple Mississippi localities (Vicksburg, Edwards, Vaughans, Grenada, and the Pearl River at Jackson).
- No new morphological/meristic data presented in this note; the paper is a distributional/faunistic record rather than a taxonomic redescription.
- Species is distinguished from the closely related **[[Hybognathus nuchalis]]** (Mississippi Silvery Minnow), with which it had previously been confused — Oklahoma records originally attributed to *H. hayi* by Ortenburger and Hubbs (1926) were later reidentified as *H. nuchalis* by Hubbs and Ortenburger (1929).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat preference:** The cypress minnow appears to prefer **quiet water over soft bottom substrates**.
- The largest sample in this study (Locality 3) was collected from a **roadside ditch directly connected to Black Cypress Bayou**, reinforcing the lentic (still-water) habitat association.
- Corroborating field observations from [[John D. Black]] (Arkansas fish survey, personal communication 1953) confirm the species is most commonly found in similar quiet-water habitats.
- **Absence from flowing water**: The species was notably absent from numerous other collections taken from flowing water within the same regions, suggesting it is **nearly limited to lentic (quiet water) habitats**, distinguishing its niche from riverine cyprinids.
- Associated habitat type: Cypress bayous, lowland swamps, and lake margins — consistent with common name "cypress minnow."

## Key Findings
- **First confirmed Texas records** for *Hybognathus hayi*:
  1. [[Caddo Lake]], Harrison County — September 9, 1950
  2. [[Black Cypress Bayou]], 3 miles NE of Jefferson — July 13, 1953
  3. Black Cypress Bayou, same locality — September 18, 1953 (largest sample; roadside ditch habitat)
  4. Black Cypress Bayou, 1.5 miles north of Jefferson — September 18, 1953
  5. Additional specimen: [[Barkman's Creek]], 5 miles north of Wamba, Bowie County (reported by George A. Moore)
- Prior Texas ichthyological surveys ([[Jurgens and Hubbs]] 1953; [[Knapp]] 1953) had **failed to record** the species in the state.
- [[George A. Moore]] (1952) similarly did not record the species from Oklahoma, despite geographic proximity (Bowie County shares a border with Oklahoma).
- The species' known range is expanded to include **northeast Texas Red River tributaries** (Harrison, Marion, and Bowie counties), extending its documented distribution beyond the Tennessee River system and Mississippi River lowlands (Arkansas, Louisiana).
- Clarifies taxonomic confusion between *H. hayi* and *[[Hybognathus nuchalis]]* in the historical Oklahoma fish fauna literature.
- Reinforces habitat-based species identification cues: association with **quiet, soft-bottomed water bodies** as a diagnostic ecological trait separating it from congeners occupying flowing-water habitats.
