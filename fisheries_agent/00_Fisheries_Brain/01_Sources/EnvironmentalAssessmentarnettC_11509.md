# Arnett Creek/Telegraph Canyon Native Fish Re-establishment Project Environmental Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This USDA Forest Service Environmental Assessment (EA), dated April 16, 1996, evaluates a proposal to re-establish a native fish community in [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]] on the [[Tonto National Forest]] (Globe Ranger District), in partnership with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD). The project would construct a [[Fish Barrier]] to block upstream re-invasion by exotic [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Mosquitofish]] from [[Queen Creek]] during high flows, and would use piscicides ([[Rotenone]] and [[Antimycin A]]) to remove exotic fish from the Arnett Creek watershed before native fish are stocked.

Five alternatives were developed by the Interdisciplinary (ID) Team: Alternative A (No Action) and Alternatives B through E (action alternatives). B and C differ in barrier location (Site 1 vs. Site 2); D and E differ in the stocked species, adding [[Gila Chub]] and [[Gila Topminnow]]. The agency's preferred alternative is **Alternative C**, which the EA states "contributes to a reversal in the decline of native fish, supports the re-establishment of fish for which there is suitable habitat, and protects the greatest amount of habitat from re-invasion by exotic fish."

## Background & Description

**Project origin and purpose.** In 1992 the Forest Service and AGFD identified an opportunity to re-establish native fish in Arnett Creek by removing two exotic, aggressive species known to prey on native fish. The stated purpose is to assist AGFD in re-establishing native fish in Arnett Creek and Telegraph Canyon by providing suitable habitat. Native fish were historically present in the Arnett watershed but are no longer present. The project implements the [[Tonto National Forest Land Management Plan]] direction that warmwater, non-game streams are to be managed to support Gila sucker (a synonym of [[Desert Sucker]]) and [[Longfin Dace]].

**Management context.** The area lies within Management Areas 2E and 2F of the LMP and adjoins the Proposed [[Picketpost Mountain]] Research Natural Area (RNA). A separate EA addresses the proposed Geological, Botanical, and Zoological Area designation, mineral withdrawal, and livestock management changes.

**Decision authority.** The Forest Supervisor, Tonto National Forest, decides on barrier construction, piscicide use, and associated mitigation and monitoring. AGFD decides on fish stocking and exotic fish removal.

**Scoping and public involvement.**
- Scoping letters were mailed to over 300 people on September 23, 1993.
- An ID Team was formed August 24, 1994.
- A comment request was mailed June 22, 1994; an ID Team meeting reviewed public comments January 13, 1995.
- Funding is largely non-appropriated grant and donation funds, most of which must be expended or returned in 1996.
- AGFD Heritage Fund support was provided through an IPAM grant.

**Study area.** Arnett Creek is roughly 31 square miles in watershed area, with elevations from 4,600 ft in the headwaters south of Superior to 2,400 ft at the [[Queen Creek]] confluence near the [[Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum]]. The analysis area includes 3.5 miles of Arnett Creek and 0.7 miles of lower Telegraph Canyon.

**Issues addressed.** Nine issues were identified, including: piscicide effects on humans, wildlife, aquatic life, and water quality (Issue 1); the instream flow water right (Issue 2); monitoring with Management Indicator Species (Issue 3); reintroduction of Gila topminnow and Gila chub (Issue 6); barrier site selection (Issue 7); and treatment of private land waters (Issue 8).

**Piscicide background (as stated in the EA).** Antimycin A was discovered in 1945 and is marketed as Fintrol. Rotenone is derived from tropical plants in the pea family, including *Derris elliptica*. Both are registered by the EPA for fisheries use.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and channel.**
- Arnett Creek is primarily a "C" stream type under the Rosgen classification, with short "F" and "D" reaches.
- Average streamflow is 3.0 cfs (based on 29 instantaneous measurements); median flow is 0.42 cfs.
- Flow exceeds 1 cfs about 35% of the time and 0.1 cfs 100% of the time. Baseflow is approximately 0.2–0.3 cfs.
- The greatest recorded flow is 33.6 cfs; a January 1993 indirect flood estimate exceeded 3,000 cfs.
- Channel gradient averages about 1%; bankfull width is about 38 ft and depth 2.2 ft; floodplain width averages about 80 ft.
- Water quality is a calcium sulfate type and is protected for warm water fishery and full-body contact recreation.

**Instream flow right.** The Tonto National Forest filed an instream flow application in October 1992. Water requirements range from 0.3 cfs (June–October) to 3.0 cfs (January–April). Arizona Department of Water Resources reportedly intends to approve a permit conditioned upon native fish reintroduction.

**Riparian vegetation.**
- The upper 2.5-mile perennial reach supports a multi-storied broadleaf forest dominated by Goodding and Bonpland willows, velvet ash, and Fremont cottonwood. The EA describes this type as rare within the Lower Sonoran Desert climate zone.
- Tree canopy is 77%, basal area 10,451 inches, and species density 2,076 stems/acre, indicating a young community.
- Oleander is the only non-native plant in sufficient quantity to threaten the rare community; volunteers and Forest Service staff cut it in 1994, but it resprouted.

**Upland vegetation.** More than 240 vascular plant species have been documented. The area contains several Sonoran Desertscrub associations, including Semidesert Grassland on the mountain top, Chaparral, Oak Scrubland, Jojoba Scrubland, and Saguaro and Palo Verde Scrubland.

**Wildlife.** The riparian zone supports:
- Common black hawks (nesting at two locations)
- [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (a riparian obligate; green sunfish predation is suspected to limit it)
- Pocketed free-tailed bats
- Bell's vireo and peregrine falcon (foraging)
- Javelina, mule deer, quail, and mountain lion
- Chuckwalla and desert tortoise in the general vicinity

The EA notes that [[Yavapai Leopard Frog]] (Candidate) has been recorded in the area, and narrow-headed garter snake (Candidate) is within its probable range.

**Mining and geology.** Approximately 170 existing mining claims were identified in the sections containing Arnett and Telegraph Creeks (BLM records, August 28, 1995). There are no oil or gas leases or active mines in the project area.

**Grazing.** The Arnett Creek riparian pasture (about 1.2 miles) was created in 1993. In May 1995, about 15 head grazed it for three days, with utilization monitoring showing less than 5% use by weight. Lower Telegraph Canyon lies within the Superior Grazing Allotment, which is permitted for 315 adult cattle.

**Existing fish community.** Surveys found only green sunfish and mosquitofish present. Leopard frog adults or tadpoles occurred only in pools with no-to-low green sunfish densities.

## Key Findings

**Fish habitat inventory** (21,732 ft surveyed; 10,338 ft dry):
- Watered habitat: 40.7% low-grade riffles, 39.7% runs, 19.6% pools.
- Median residual pool depth: 0.8 ft. Median watered width: 7 ft.
- Bank stability averaged 3 (50–79% covered). Average embeddedness was 3.5.
- Only 3.5% of banks were undercut.
- Instream cover was scarce: 83% of units had no small woody debris cover, 62% no terrestrial vegetation cover, and 52% no substrate cover.
- Backwater pools occupied about 3% of the channel; side channels about 8%.

**Habitat suitability for native species (EA assessment):**

| Species | EA conclusion |
|---|---|
| [[Longfin Dace]] | Excellent candidate; tolerates flash floods; runs and shallow pools comprise nearly 60% of habitat; reach elevation (under 2,600 ft) is within its range (under 4,900 ft) |
| [[Desert Sucker]] | Good candidate; riffles and pools (~60% of habitat) provide adequate, though not optimal, habitat; reach elevation 2,354–2,600 ft within its 300–6,560 ft range |
| [[Sonora Sucker]] | Not recommended for stocking at this time; lacks deep or rocky pools and large instream cover |
| [[Gila Chub]] | Doubtful persistence in current conditions; may be reconsidered if undercut banks and deep pools develop; the only known historical native record in the drainage |
| [[Gila Topminnow]] | Not preferred at present; unlikely to persist given flashy runoff and lack of cover; federally Endangered |
| [[Loach Minnow]] | Habitat not present (creek too small; lacks turbulent rocky riffles) |
| [[Spikedace]] | Habitat not suitable (creek too small) |
| Speckled dace | Doubtful habitat (lacks swift, well-developed riffles) |

**Barrier site comparison:**

| Criterion | Site 1 (Alternatives B, D) | Site 2 (Alternatives C, E) |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Intermittent reach, T2S R12E Sec 8 NE¼SW¼ | Ephemeral reach, T2S R12E Sec 7 SW¼NW¼ |
| Valley / floodprone width | ~125 ft / ~30 ft | ~150 ft / ~90 ft |
| Access | Helicopter for heavy materials; ATVs or small backhoe along game trails | Abandoned road rehabilitation |
| Critical criteria (2–4) | Conforms | Conforms |
| Habitat protected from reinvasion | Does not include all available habitat | Maximized |
| Cost | Lower barrier cost | Higher barrier cost (offset by lower transport cost) |

The EA identifies Site 2 as the preferred site from a fisheries standpoint. The barrier is a gabion basket wall with splash pad, designed for a 3–5 ft vertical drop.

**Alternative comparison:**

- **Alternative A (No Action):** No barrier, no piscicide, no reintroduction. The EA states this may jeopardize the instream flow right, which is conditioned on native fish reintroduction. Green sunfish predation on leopard frogs would continue.
- **Alternatives B and C:** Stock longfin dace and desert sucker. Both are expected to contribute to reversing their declines, but not to Gila chub recovery or Gila topminnow recovery.
- **Alternatives D and E:** Add Gila chub and Gila topminnow. The Gila topminnow is federally Endangered (ESA 1973, as amended). If exotic fish are found after stocking, spot-treatment would not be approved because it would take Gila topminnow.
- **Alternative C (Preferred):** Barrier at Site 2, longfin dace and desert sucker.

**Piscicide safety and degradation (as reported):**
- Antimycin A half-life: 310 hours at pH 6.0 and 1.5 hours at pH 10 (water at 12 °C in lab tests).
- Rotenone half-life in field tests: 0.94 days in warm water (24 °C) and 10.3 days in cold water (5 °C).
- Complete degradation is expected in 1–7 days in Arnett Creek under warmer conditions.
- [[Potassium Permanganate]] is kept on site to neutralize the piscicide if needed. Treatment is timed for low-flow seasons, so use is considered unlikely.
- Human risk estimate for Antimycin A: total maximum daily intake of 115 micrograms, equal to 1/17,000 of the rat oral LD50. A 150 lb person would need to consume 608 lb of raw fish containing 100 ppb rotenone to reach a toxic dose.

**Macroinvertebrates and cumulative effects.** Some taxa (Cladocera, Copepoda, Amphipoda, Ephemeroptera, Trichoptera) may decline temporarily and recover within a year. Recolonization is expected from untreated upstream waters. Big Dry Creek (Gila National Forest, 1985) showed only minimal, short-term effects after Antimycin A treatment.

**Mitigation highlights (Alternatives B–E):**
- Pre-treatment intensive survey to confirm exotic fish distribution and absence of native fish.
- Timing applications to minimize effects on lowland leopard frog; rescuing overwintering tadpoles if numerous.
- Treatment during low-precipitation seasons; state-certified applicator on site; notification of the public in the renovation zone; protective clothing and eye gear.
- Macroinvertebrate sampling before and after treatment.

**Monitoring commitments:**
- Fish population: annual survey of treated areas for four years, then every four years for at least eight years.
- Fish habitat: surveys every four years for 12 years, comparable to pre-project methods.
- Fish barrier: structural integrity assessed annually for four years, then every four years for at least eight years.
- Riparian photopoints: six established in 1995; additional points planned for 1996; replicated every four years for 12 years.
- The site will be re-evaluated within 9 years to determine whether Arnett Creek and Lower Telegraph Canyon can support other native species.

**Other effects:**
- **Private lands:** Two parcels (Sections 22, 26, and 27, T2S R12E) contain permanent waters that may need treatment. Permission has been obtained from the landowners.
- **Public safety:** The EA concludes no effects to humans are expected under the action alternatives.
- **Recreation:** Removal of green sunfish is expected to benefit leopard frog populations and wildlife viewing over the long term. Arnett Creek is not considered a significant sport fishery.
- **Air quality:** Not significantly affected by any alternative.
- **Mining:** Longfin dace and desert sucker are not federally listed, so no ESA restrictions on mining are expected. Alternative D could bring future Biological Opinion restrictions, though the EA states these would probably modify rather than stop projects.
- **Special use permit:** The 1929 permit to the Boyce Thompson Southwestern Arboretum (726 acres) is outdated and in need of revision. The fisheries project would not affect it.

**Conclusion.** The EA concludes that native fish re-establishment is consistent with the LMP and the proposed Geological, Botanical, and Zoological Area, and that Alternative C best meets the project purpose while minimizing reinvasion risk.
