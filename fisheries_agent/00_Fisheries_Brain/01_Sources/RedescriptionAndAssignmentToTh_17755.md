# Redescription and Assignment to the New Genus Lirceolus of the Texas Troglobitic Water Slater, Asellus smithii (Ulrich) (Crustacea: Isopoda: Asellidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #asellidae #isopoda #crustacea #troglobite #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic study redescribes the [[Texas Troglobitic Water Slater]], originally described as *Caecidotea smithii* by Ulrich in 1902 from a single incomplete specimen. Based on numerous specimens collected from the [[San Marcos Artesian Well]] beginning in December 1973, the authors establish the new genus *Lirceolus* to accommodate this distinctive troglobitic isopod. The species exhibits unique morphological features including an unusual maxilla 1 structure with 8 plumose apical setae (versus 4-5 in all other known [[Asellidae]]), extreme reduction of pleopods 4 and 5, and an oblique suture on pleopod 3 exopod shared only with the genus *Lirceus*. The detailed redescription includes comprehensive illustrations of morphological characters and establishes the systematic position of this cave-adapted crustacean.

## Background & Description

### Historical Context
Dr. C. H. Eigenmann first collected this species in September 1899 during a 3-day visit to [[San Marcos]], [[Texas]], while attempting to obtain specimens of the cave salamander *[[Eurycea rathbuni]]* from the U.S. Fish Commission artesian well. He collected only "the front half of a new species" along with other subterranean mollusks and arthropods. Eigenmann (1900) listed the species as "*Caecidotaea smithii* n. sp." (nomen nudum), and Ulrich (1902) subsequently published the formal description based on this single incomplete specimen lacking the telson, uropods, and ends of the 2nd antennae.

Until the present study, no additional specimens had been reported. Beginning December 1973, regular sampling of the [[San Marcos Artesian Well]] using nets attached to the pipe outlet yielded numerous specimens enabling complete redescription.

### Morphological Description
**Body:** Small troglobitic isopod reaching at least 3.7 mm length. Body slender, approximately 3× as long as wide. Completely blind and unpigmented. Width increases gradually posteriorly to pereonite 6, then decreases. All coxae visible in dorsal view. Head nearly twice as wide as long with nearly straight anterior margin lacking rostrum; postmandibular lobes weakly developed.

**Telson:** Unusually small and orbicular with width and length subequal; caudomedial lobe barely evident.

**Antennae:** Antenna 1 slightly longer than head with flagellum of 2-4 segments, last 2 segments each bearing esthete. Antenna 2 approximately as long as body with flagellum of about 30 segments.

**Mouthparts:** Mandible with 3-cuspate incisors and 2-cuspate lacinia mobilis; spine row with 13 spines on each mandible; palp with unusually long 1st segment and compact spination on 3rd segment. Maxilla 1 outer lobe with 10 apical spines inserted on distomedial shoulder, separated by wide gap from much longer circumplumose seta at distolateral corner and naked seta near distal end of posterior surface; inner lobe with 8 plumose apical setae (unique among [[Asellidae]]). Maxilliped with slender palp segments having sparse setation.

**Pereopods:** Pereopod 1 propus not enlarged; palm without processes; flexor margin of dactyl with 5-6 long slender spines. Pereopods 1 and 4 similar in males and females. Pereopods 2-7 similar; basis with distinctive bulbous-based setae on margin; carpus and propus with long slender spines on one or both margins; dactyl with 2 spines on flexor margin.

**Pleopods:** Male pleopod 1 larger than pleopod 2; protopod slightly shorter than exopod with 5 retinaculae; exopod oval, slightly less than 2× as long as wide. Male pleopod 2 with small basal spur; labial spur and catch lobe absent; exopod with oblique suture as in *[[Lirceus]]*; endopod slender, about 3.6× as long as wide with oblique fissure. Female pleopod 2 suboval, slightly more than half as wide as long with margins without setae. Pleopod 3 exopod with margins unarmed except for 2 setae at distomedial corner. **Pleopods 4-5 with exopod and endopod fused into single fleshy uniramous structure**—extreme reduction unknown in other [[Asellidae]]; groove encircling margin indicates line of fusion of rami.

**Uropods:** Narrow linear rami bearing long setae; endopod about 1.8× as long as exopod.

### Generic Diagnosis of *Lirceolus* new genus
Small, blind, unpigmented. Head without lateral incisions. Mandibular palp well developed. Maxilla 1 outer lobe with apical spines (10) inserted on distomedial shoulder; inner lobe with 8 plumose apical setae. Maxilliped with slender palp segments having sparse setation. Pereopods 1 and 4 similar in males and females. Male pleopod 2 with small basal spur; labial spur and catch lobe absent. Pleopod 3 exopod with oblique suture as in *Lirceus*. Pleopods 4 and 5 with exopod and endopod fused into single fleshy ramus.

**Type-species:** *Caecidotea smithii* Ulrich

**Etymology:** *Lirceus* (a spring in Greece according to Rafinesque 1820) + Latin diminutive suffix "-olus", referring to resemblance to *Lirceus* and small size. Gender masculine.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat
*[[Lirceolus smithii]]* is a troglobitic (obligate cave-dwelling) species inhabiting subterranean aquatic environments. All known specimens originate from the [[San Marcos Artesian Well]] in [[San Marcos]], [[Texas]]. The species was collected along with other subterranean mollusks and arthropods from this artesian system.

### Physiological Adaptations
The species exhibits classic troglomorphic adaptations including complete absence of eyes and body pigmentation. The extreme reduction of pleopods 4 and 5 is interpreted as an adaptation to the low metabolic demands of troglobitic life. The authors note that "the small size of *L. smithii*, together with the well known low metabolism of troglobites, suggests that this species requires minimal respiratory surface."

### Collection Methods
Regular sampling beginning December 1973 employed nets attached to the outlet pipe of the artesian well, yielding numerous specimens after 74 years of collection absence since the original 1899 specimen.

## Key Findings

### Taxonomic Revision
1. **New Genus Established:** *Lirceolus* new genus erected to accommodate *smithii* based on distinctive morphological characters not fitting existing asellid genera.

2. **Unique Maxilla 1 Structure:** Discovery of 8 plumose apical setae on maxilla 1 inner lobe represents a unique condition among [[Asellidae]]. All previously known asellids possess either 4 apical setae (*Asellus* (*Asellus*)) or 5 apical setae (all other Asellidae). This finding was so unexpected that maxilla 1 of several specimens was examined to confirm constancy. Additionally, spines of outer lobe positioned on oblique subterminal shoulder rather than terminally as in other asellids.

3. **Extreme Pleopod Reduction:** Pleopods 4 and 5 exhibit complete fusion of exopod and endopod into single fleshy uniramous structure—a condition unknown in any other [[Asellidae]]. Groove encircling margin indicates line of fusion.

4. **Phylogenetic Relationship:** The oblique suture on pleopod 3 exopod is shared only with genus *[[Lirceus]]*, suggesting either that *Lirceus* may be ancestral to *Lirceolus* or that the two genera share a common ancestor.

5. **Complete Redescription:** First comprehensive morphological description based on numerous complete specimens, replacing Ulrich's 1902 description based on single incomplete specimen. Detailed illustrations (Figures 1-26) document all major morphological features.

6. **Specimen Deposition:** Representative series deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]], [[Smithsonian Institution]].

7. **Size Range:** Body length up to at least 3.7 mm.

8. **Sexual Dimorphism:** Pereopods 1 and 4 similar in males and females (unlike many asellids with sexually dimorphic pereopods).

9. **Synonymy Clarified:** Complete synonymy provided documenting taxonomic history through various generic assignments (*Caecidotaea*, *Caecidotea*, *Conasellus*, *Asellus*) from 1900-1973.
