# Additional Range Expansion in Florida of the Introduced Walking Catfish

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clariidae

## Executive Summary

This short paper by [[Walter R. Courtenay Jr]] documents continued range expansion of the introduced [[Walking Catfish]] (*Clarias batrachus*) across Florida between April 1975 and August 1978. The author reports new county records, a long-distance invasion of the [[Peace River]] system, and persistence through a severely cold winter (1977). The species now occupies roughly 25% of Florida's freshwater area, and the author projects that it could spread through nearly three-fourths of peninsular Florida within about a decade.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Walking Catfish]] (*Clarias batrachus*), an air-breathing exotic clariid native to south-east Asia, ranging from eastern India to Vietnam (Smith, 1945).
- **Introduction:** Apparently introduced to the United States from Bangkok, Thailand. Earlier reviews by the author and colleagues ([[Courtenay & Miley 1975]]; [[Courtenay et al. 1974]]) traced its history and predicted rapid range expansion in Florida.
- **Traits of concern:** Under low-water conditions, the species concentrates with native fishes and preys heavily on them, sometimes killing large [[Largemouth Bass]] without eating them. Its high fecundity was also cited as a factor in its potential environmental impact.
- **Tolerance:** The species tolerates moderate salinities (Courtenay, 1970; Courtenay et al., 1974).
- **Study context:** The paper was supported in part by a U.S. Department of the Interior, Fish and Wildlife Service contract (14-16-0009-78-021).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Key sites and waterbodies named in the text:**
- [[Caloosahatchee River]] and Fort Myers ([[Lee County]])
- [[Big Cypress Swamp]] (Collier County), reaching west to Alligator Bay near the Gulf of Mexico
- [[Everglades National Park]] (Dade and Monroe Counties), including Taylor Slough, Shark Slough and the Pa-Hay-Okee overlook road
- [[Rookery Bay]] near Naples (Collier County)
- [[Lake Istokpoga]] (Highlands County), about 38 km north-west of [[Lake Okeechobee]]
- [[Peace River]] (DeSoto County, near Punta Gorda), Lake Effie (Polk County), and [[Prairie Creek]]
- [[Fisheating Creek]], a western tributary of Lake Okeechobee, identified as the likely invasion route to the Peace River
- [[Manatee River]] and [[Little Manatee River]] (Bradenton-Sarasota area)
- [[Loxahatchee River]] (northeastern Palm Beach County)
- Gibsonton and Riverview on the eastern shore of [[Tampa Bay]] (Hillsborough County), with an established population since 1968

**Climate and refuges:** Winter temperatures in the Tampa Bay area are generally much lower than in subtropical southern Florida, where the species first established. The author notes that warm springs and mild deep waters in rivers, canals, ponds and lakes provide winter refuges for pioneering populations.

**Barriers:** Flood-control structures and wide, shallow swamps, which restrict other exotic fishes, have not been effective against this species.

## Key Findings

**Range expansion timeline and counts**
- **April 1975:** Established as reproducing populations in 10 Florida counties (Courtenay, 1975).
- **Early 1976:** A fish kill of unknown origin in Big Cypress Swamp was about 90% Walking Catfish by number, indicating it already formed a major share of fish biomass.
- **Mid-1976:** Found in Everglades National Park canals and culverts, but not yet in the park's major undisturbed habitats.
- **1977:** Numerous individuals observed on an overland migration in Fort Myers; a specimen was collected from Lake Effie (Polk County).
- **August 1978:** Found in Rookery Bay and in canals of southeastern Dade County, extending the lower east coast range by about 215 km (from north of Fort Pierce to near Homestead).
- **June-July 1978:** A commercial fisherman caught an adult in the lower Peace River; numerous individuals were seen in the Bradenton-Sarasota area.

**Current distribution**
- As of August 1978, the species occurs in 18 Florida counties (Broward, Charlotte, Collier, Dade, DeSoto, Glades, Hendry, Highlands, Hillsborough, Lee, Manatee, Martin, Monroe, Okeechobee, Palm Beach, Polk, Sarasota, St Lucie), with possible presence in Hardee and southern Indian River counties.
- Coverage grew from about 18% of the state in April 1975 to somewhat more than 25% of Florida's area.
- The north-south range in southwestern peninsular Florida exceeds 250 km.

**Peace River invasion**
- The Peace River record is about 50 km from the Caloosahatchee mouth via Charlotte Harbor. The author judges that route unlikely.
- The more probable route is overland through Fisheating Creek and Prairie Creek, a distance of about 54 km through fresh water.
- Range expansion probably came from two sources: the southern Florida population (west and north) and the Hillsborough County population (east, via the Little Manatee River).

**Cold tolerance**
- Thousands of Walking Catfish died in the cold winter of 1970 in southern Palm Beach and northern Broward counties.
- The severe winter of 1977 caused only a few reported cold kills in southern Florida, and the Tampa Bay population survived.
- The author suggests cold-resistant segments may have been selected, and that expansion will accelerate in warm and wet years.

**Projections and conclusions**
- The author believes the species can spread across nearly three-fourths of peninsular Florida, possibly within the next decade.
- The author suggests the present and future range of Clarias may indicate the potential range for many established or future introduced tropical aquarium fishes.

**Note added in proof:** Specimens were observed north of downtown Naples on 27 October 1978, about 15 km north-west of Rookery Bay (W. F. Lanni, pers. comm.).
