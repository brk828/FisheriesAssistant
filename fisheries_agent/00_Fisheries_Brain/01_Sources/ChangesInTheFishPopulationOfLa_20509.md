# Changes in the fish population of Lake Murray following the reduction of gizzard shad numbers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This note documents a rare capture of a [[Colorado River Squawfish]] on the San Juan arm of [[Lake Powell]], reported to the Arizona Game and Fish Department. The species, once abundant throughout the [[Colorado River]] drainage, has become critically rare and is listed under the U.S. federal endangered species list. The account, relayed by fisheries biologist Bill Silvey, highlights the drastic population decline caused by dam construction and introduction of non-native game fish, and serves as a public-awareness piece cautioning anglers to identify and release the species if caught.

## Background & Description
- **Body shape:** Elongated, "pike-like" body form (basis for common name "squawfish"/pikeminnow).
- **Mouth:** Large mouth, terminal and predatory in form.
- **Dentition:** No teeth (relies on pharyngeal teeth typical of [[Cyprinidae]], not oral teeth).
- **Historical maximum size:** Reportedly reached up to **6 feet (~1.8 m)** in length and **100 lbs (~45 kg)** in weight in native, undammed river conditions.
- **Reported specimen size:** The individual caught near Lake Powell weighed only **~5.5 lbs**, illustrating a dramatic reduction in typical body size compared to historical records — likely reflecting altered river conditions (clearer, colder, dam-regulated water) rather than genetic change.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Historical range:** Once a "plentiful occupant" of the [[Colorado River]] drainage, including the [[Salt River]] near Phoenix, Arizona, where early residents reportedly caught them in large numbers ("tons") in muddy river conditions.
- **Habitat preference:** Adapted to warm, turbid ("muddy"), free-flowing river systems characteristic of the pre-dam Colorado River Basin.
- **Current range:** Considered extirpated ("extinct") in waters south of [[Lake Powell]]; last confirmed catch in the Salt River was circa **1950**.
- **Remaining range:** Found in the San Juan River arm of Lake Powell and presumably other upper Colorado River drainage waters in southern Utah.
- **Threats:**
  - **Habitat alteration:** Dam-building has eliminated the muddy, free-flowing river conditions the species evolved to exploit, replacing them with clearer, more stable reservoir/regulated-river environments.
  - **Introduced species:** Introduction of exotic game fish (likely predatory/competitive salmonids and centrarchids) is cited as a major contributing factor in the species' decline.
  - **Incidental/accidental harvest:** Anglers unfamiliar with the species risk illegally killing an endangered fish, as illustrated by the case in this report where a fisherman intended to eat his catch, unaware of its identity or legal status.

## Key Findings
- The [[Colorado River Squawfish]] is federally listed as an **endangered species**, with penalties for willful killing including fines up to **$20,000** and up to **one year in jail**.
- Sightings/captures are exceedingly rare — fisheries biologist Bill Silvey noted receiving only about **one specimen per year** brought in by confused anglers.
- The species is functionally **extirpated south of Lake Powell**, with the last known Salt River capture dated to approximately **1950**.
- Current surviving populations appear restricted to portions of the upper [[Colorado River]] drainage, including [[Lake Powell]] and the [[San Juan River]] arm.
- Public identification guidance issued: anglers should look for an **elongated, pike-like body**, a **large mouth**, and **absence of teeth**, and should release any suspected specimen unharmed to avoid legal repercussions.
- The case underscores broader themes in [[Freshwater Fish Conservation]] regarding the impacts of **dam construction** and **non-native species introduction** on native riverine fish populations in the American Southwest.
