## Executive Summary
This 1994 proposal letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) to the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT) outlines an experimental field study to test whether water chemistry acts as a barrier limiting the upstream distribution of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR). Prior water chemistry zonation studies found no drastic differences in chemical variables immediately above and below [[Atomizer Falls]], challenging a prior thesis (Mattes 1993) suggesting CO2 toxicity or physical habitat features prevent chub from occupying upper reaches. The proposed methodology involves translocating caged wild-caught chub (both age-0 and age-1) above the falls to directly monitor survival and behavior under real river conditions, effectively using the fish as bio-indicators to settle the "definitive" question of habitat suitability for potential population augmentation.

## Background & Description
The source document does not provide detailed external morphological descriptions (e.g., fin ray counts, nuchal hump measurements) of the [[Humpback Chub]]. However, it distinguishes between specific **life stages** relevant to the experimental design:
*   **Age-0 Fish:** Juvenile specimens, housed in smaller 1.5'×1'×1' fine-screened, covered hatching boxes to prevent escape/predation.
*   **Age-1 Fish:** Slightly older juveniles, housed in larger 4'×4'×4' soft-sided, 1/4 inch mesh live cars.
*   **Excluded Life Stages:** The study explicitly excludes adult chub (due to U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service permitting concerns regarding "take") and does not examine eggs or larvae, which the authors note may react differently to water chemistry than the juvenile stages tested.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The research focuses on the specific ecological and chemical constraints defining the [[Humpback Chub]]'s niche within the LCR:
*   **Geographic Range:** The study targets the reach between **Blue Spring** (21 km above the LCR mouth) and **Atomizer Falls** (14.8 km above the mouth), a distance of approximately 6 km.
*   **Physical Barriers:** The proposal raises the hypothesis that **Atomizer Falls** may act as a physical migration barrier, separate from chemical constraints, a possibility not fully considered in Mattes (1993).
*   **Co-occurring Species:** Notably, [[Speckled Dace]] already inhabit the reaches *above* Atomizer Falls, suggesting the barrier (whether chemical or physical) may be species-specific and not a blanket habitat exclusion zone.
*   **Water Chemistry Variables:** The niche limitation hypothesis is tied to specific abiotic factors: **Carbon Dioxide (CO2)** levels (primary suspected limiting factor per Mattes 1993), Temperature, pH, Dissolved Oxygen, Conductivity, and Alkalinity.
*   **Comparative Tolerance:** Previous CO2 tolerance conclusions were based on surrogate species data from [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), and [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*), rather than direct data on humpback chub.
*   **Habitat Features:** Alternative limiting factors proposed by Mattes (1993) include water clarity, physical cover, and depth in the upper reaches.

## Key Findings
*   **Chemical Homogeneity:** Prior AGFD longitudinal studies (Angradi et al. 1992; Robinson et al. 1993) found that water chemistry variables were **not drastically different** immediately above and below Atomizer Falls, casting doubt on the strict "chemical barrier" hypothesis.
*   **Experimental Design:** The study proposes a translocation experiment using 40 age-1 and 40 age-0 chub, divided into groups of 10, and transported via helicopter to four sites: RK 15.0 (above Atomizer), RK 17.5, RK 20.0 (near Blue Spring), and a control site near Salt Trail Canyon.
*   **Monitoring Protocol:** Survival and behavior will be monitored intensively for the first 4–6 hours and at least three times daily over a 72-hour period. Water quality (Temp, pH, DO, conductivity) will be logged hourly via Hydrolabs, while alkalinity and CO2 will be checked three times daily via Hach titrator kits.
*   **Statistical Analysis:** Results regarding survival/mortality across the different river kilometer sites are intended to be analyzed using a **G-test of homogeneity**.
*   **Conservation Implication:** A successful survival outcome in the upper reaches would support the augmentation of [[Humpback Chub]] populations in the broader [[Grand Canyon]] riverine ecosystem, providing a new area for population expansion beyond the currently occupied range below the falls.
*   **Regulatory Constraints:** The experiment necessitates permit amendments for the "worst-case" take of 80 additional humpback chub, requiring coordination with the Navajo Nation, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and potentially Grand Canyon National Park.
