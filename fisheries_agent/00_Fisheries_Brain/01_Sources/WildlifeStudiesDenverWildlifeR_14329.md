# Effects of Pesticides on Fish and Wildlife - 1964 Research Findings

#wildlife-toxicology #environmental-contamination #pesticide-research #aquatic-ecology

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive 1964 report from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] documents extensive research on pesticide effects across multiple taxa and ecosystems. The [[Denver Wildlife Research Center]] shifted emphasis from general surveillance to intensive investigation of new pesticides as potential substitutes for persistent chlorinated organic insecticides. Studies encompassed analytical methodology development, acute and chronic toxicity testing, field monitoring, and residue analysis in wildlife refuges, forests, and aquatic habitats. Key findings demonstrate widespread pesticide contamination in aquatic food chains, differential species susceptibility, and persistence of [[DDT]] and metabolites in multiple environmental compartments. Research covered birds of prey ([[Osprey]], [[Bald Eagle]]), waterfowl ([[Black Duck]], [[Canada Goose]]), songbirds, grouse, pelicans, fish, and aquatic invertebrates across diverse geographic locations.

## Background & Description

The 1964 research program represented a strategic pivot in pesticide-wildlife investigations. The [[Denver Wildlife Research Center]] established a cooperative agreement with the U.S. Forest Service Chemical Evaluation Laboratory to evaluate nonpersistent insecticides as replacements for [[DDT]] in forest pest control. Research activities were organized into six categories: (1) improved analytical methodology for pesticide residue identification, (2) toxicity testing in captive wildlife, (3) field toxicity studies with wild grouse, (4) residue monitoring in endangered species, (5) surveillance and monitoring activities, and (6) miscellaneous observations.

Analytical capabilities included paper chromatography (sensitivity 5×10⁻⁷ grams), gas chromatography with electron capture detector (sensitivity 1×10⁻⁹ grams), and gas chromatography with microcoulometric detector (sensitivity 1×10⁻⁸ grams). New methods were developed for analyzing [[DRC-1339]] (an avicide), [[Baytex]] (organophosphate), and blood cholinesterase activity across multiple species.

The research addressed both terrestrial and aquatic ecosystems, with particular attention to [[National Wildlife Refuges]] including [[Tule Lake]], [[Lower Klamath]], [[McNary]], and [[Deer Flat]]. Studies examined pesticide persistence in forest environments following spruce budworm control operations and rangeland applications for grasshopper control.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Aquatic ecosystems demonstrated particular vulnerability to pesticide contamination through bioaccumulation in food chains. At [[Tule Lake National Wildlife Refuge]], fish-eating birds died following exposure to [[toxaphene]] in waste agricultural water; fish contained up to 8 ppm toxaphene while dead birds contained 3-650 ppm in tissues. Water sampling across four refuges revealed that suspended material contained much greater insecticide concentrations than filtered water, with sediments showing variable accumulation patterns.

The role of aquatic invertebrates in pesticide metabolism emerged as significant. Evidence suggested invertebrates capable of metabolizing chlorinated hydrocarbons could reduce contamination levels; at [[Tule Lake]], suspended material in incoming agricultural water averaged 20.3 ppm [[DDT]], decreasing to 6.0 ppm in marsh areas and 1.5 ppm in sediments. However, [[Deer Flat Refuge]] showed anomalously high sediment contamination (40.0 ppm average) correlated with scarcity of invertebrates.

Forest ecosystems demonstrated complex pesticide dynamics following [[DDT]] application for white fir sawfly control at Knox Mountain, California (0.75 lb/acre). Residues in forest litter remained elevated (6.1 ppm) three months post-treatment. Dead and dying insects accumulated very high [[DDT]] concentrations, creating concentrated contamination sources for insectivorous birds. Soil showed modest increases (0.08 to 0.4 ppm), while vegetation residues decreased progressively from initial high levels.

The ratio of [[DDT]] to [[DDE]] (metabolite) varied systematically between environmental compartments: soil, litter, and plants showed predominantly [[DDT]], while animal tissues progressively accumulated greater proportions of [[DDE]] over time, indicating differential metabolism and degradation pathways.

## Key Findings

**Analytical Methodology Advances:**
- Three methods developed for [[DRC-1339]] analysis: volumetric (±0.02% accuracy), gravimetric (±0.1% accuracy), and paper chromatographic (±10% at 0.1-0.2 ppm levels)
- [[DRC-1339]] metabolism in rats produced N-acetyl derivative and carboxy acid metabolite, identified via infrared spectroscopy
- Colorimetric method developed for [[Baytex]] detection measuring 4-methylthio-m-cresol hydrolysis product
- Rapid Florisil column cleanup procedures established for environmental samples and tissue extracts

**Acute Toxicity Determinations:**
- [[Sharp-tailed Grouse]]: [[dieldrin]] LD₅₀ = 6.4 mg/kg (3.3-12.5, p=0.05); [[malathion]] critical level ~220 mg/kg
- [[Mule Deer]]: dimethoate at 100 mg/kg reduced blood cholinesterase 66% with no gross symptoms; 200 mg/kg produced salivation, pupil contraction, tremors, 77% cholinesterase reduction
- [[White Pelican]]: much more susceptible to [[toxaphene]] than [[DDT]]; 50 ppm toxaphene diet caused tremors, convulsions, death in 4-6 weeks; 50 ppm [[DDT]] diet eliminated parasites but caused no mortality

**Field Toxicity Studies:**
- Wild [[Sharp-tailed Grouse]] showed similar toxicity to captive birds: [[dieldrin]] LD₅₀ = 6.9 mg/kg (4.4-10.9, p=0.05)
- Radiotracking provided behavioral data on intoxicated birds and facilitated carcass recovery for residue analysis
- Residues in visceral organs correlated well with symptom severity

**Endangered Species Residues:**
- [[Bald Eagle]] carcasses (Colorado, Oklahoma): 2.08-158 ppm [[DDT]] and metabolites in fat; one specimen contained 44 ppm [[dieldrin]]
- [[Whooping Crane]] eggs: 1.80-2.48 ppm total [[DDT]] and metabolites; 0.509-0.611 ppm [[endrin]]; trace levels [[dieldrin]], [[aldrin]], [[heptachlor epoxide]]
- Immature whooping crane tissues showed detectable but low contamination levels

**Refuge Contamination Patterns:**
Average residues in water (suspended material): [[Tule Lake]] 6.0 ppm, [[Lower Klamath]] 7.3 ppm, [[McNary]] 9.3 ppm, [[Deer Flat]] 12.5 ppm. Sediment residues: [[Tule Lake]] 1.5 ppm, [[Lower Klamath]] 0.9 ppm, [[McNary]] 0.4 ppm, [[Deer Flat]] 40.0 ppm. Fish residues remained relatively low (0.5-3.3 ppm) despite environmental contamination. Black-crowned night heron mortality at [[Deer Flat]] in 1963 showed residues up to 25 ppm [[DDT]], 4 ppm [[dieldrin]], 5 ppm [[toxaphene]].

**Pesticide Persistence on Forage:**
Following [[DDT]] application (1 lb/acre in fuel oil) for spruce budworm control on [[Salmon National Forest]], Idaho:
- Wheatgrass: 384.1 ppm immediately post-spray, declining to 211.2 ppm after 3 months
- Balsamroot: 312.9 ppm declining to 149.3 ppm
- Big sagebrush: 148.2 ppm declining to 59.1 ppm
- Douglas-fir needles: 79.3 ppm declining to 35.3 ppm
- Deer rumen contents (collected 1 month post-spray): 27.0-185.3 ppm total [[DDT]] residues

**Malathion Field Applications:**
- Rangeland spraying (8 oz/acre) for grasshopper control in Nebraska: bird census decreased 16% on treated area vs. 57% increase on untreated area
- Young turkey blood plasma cholinesterase dropped from 0.60 to 0.43 (pH change/hour), recovering to pre-treatment levels after 68 days
- Forest application (0.75 lb/acre in oil) in Montana: minimal effect on songbird numbers (19.5 birds/hour pre-spray vs. 22.0 post-spray)
- Insectivorous bird species decreased 74% on malathion-treated areas vs. 40% on check areas at Tuolumne Meadows

**Parathion Marsh Studies:**
- Single treatment (0.85 lb/acre): water residues decreased from 0.40 ppm to 0.01 ppm in 8 days, undetectable by day 14
- Bottom mud: 0.06 ppm persisting 22 days
- Emergent vegetation: up to 14 ppm initially, ~2 ppm after 29 days
- Mosquito fish mortality under heavy treatment; populations recovered quickly
- No effects observed on mallard ducks or bullfrogs

**Forest Ecosystem Dynamics (Knox Mountain, California):**
Following 0.75 lb/acre [[DDT]] application, insect residues showed dramatic temporal pattern: 206.2 ppm (0-day), 83.7 ppm (1-2 days), 19 ppm (1 month). Songbird residues increased post-treatment with species-specific patterns: [[Mountain Chickadee]] and [[Oregon Junco]] showed highest 3-month residues (11.7 and 6.0 ppm respectively), while [[Robin]] residues decreased below pre-treatment levels. [[Deer Mouse]] showed low persistent contamination (0.08 ppm at 3 months).

**Metabolic Studies:**
[[DDT]] metabolism in deer and rats produced previously unidentified "Compound X" (degraded form of [[DDE]]), detected via gas chromatography with electron capture detector. The metabolite appeared in blood following [[DDT]] administration but not from [[DDMU]] or [[DDNU]] (DDE homologs). Additional unidentified materials detected may represent further degradation products.

**Artificial Propagation Success:**
[[Sharp-tailed Grouse]]: 312 eggs, 239 fertile (76.6%), 148 chicks reared (61.9% of fertile eggs). Greater [[Prairie Chicken]]: 41 fertile eggs, 24 hatched, 17 reared to maturity. Improved facilities and techniques overcame previous propagation problems, providing birds for toxicity testing.

**Rocky Mountain Arsenal Remediation:**
Decontamination of three industrial lakes removed [[aldrin]] and [[dieldrin]] contamination responsible for annual waterfowl mortality over 10-15 years. Operation involved drainage, removal of 4-6 inches of contaminated soil (up to 18 inches in high-concentration areas), analysis of ~800 samples, and installation of closed cooling system with 650-million gallon sump. Total cost: $265,000.
