# Deadman Creek Survey Memo (1–5 April 1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This inter-office memo from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (dated 10 April 1991) reports a reconnaissance of the upper [[Deadman Creek]] drainage, focusing on the [[South Fork Deadman Creek]], in the [[Mazatal Wilderness]]. The survey aimed to verify reports of an unidentified trout in the South Fork. Field work confirmed the presence of [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) near the confluence and documented [[Speckled Dace]] from the South Fork, a first record for the drainage. Trout were not confirmed. High water flows limited the survey, so the author recommends a return visit in late April to early May or late October to early November, with a five-day pack-supported crew.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Verify reports of trout in the South Fork of Deadman Creek.
- **Prior history:** The Eckstein family reported catching trout in the South Fork, last seen about 20 years earlier. In 1989, [[Pat Crouch]], [[Leonard Cooper]] and the author attempted a survey from the Club Trail crossing downstream. They could not reach the lower creek with survey equipment but found Speckled Dace on both June and August trips. The memo notes that no fish had previously been reported from this drainage.
- **Local report:** A geology graduate student from Northern Arizona University reported trout in the lower South Fork in 1988 and 1989.
- **Participants (April 1991):** Craig Eckstein (Payson Ranger District, Tonto National Forest) and the author, with a Tonto NF cooperating party.
- **Access:** Two routes to the confluence were identified. The author recommends the [[Lost Spring Trail]], which follows a ridge and an old fence before descending to just above the confluence. The alternative is an old mining trail along the north shore of Deadman Creek. Access to Lost Spring is via the [[Willow Spring Trail]] from the sheep bridge or the North Peak trail. The ride from camp on Deadman to the North Peak trailhead took 6.5 hours.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and canopy**
- The South Fork is a deep, narrow canyon with nearly total closed canopy, dominated by alder and sycamore.
- Main Deadman Creek is more open, with less developed canopy of sycamore, alder and [[Fremont Cottonwood]].
- South Fork flow was estimated at 50–60% of main Deadman flow above the confluence.

**Water temperature**
- August 1989, 1230 hrs, South Fork at Club Trail crossing: 22 °C (surface).
- August 1989, large downstream pool in the perennial flow area: 19 °C (surface). The author noted the water below the surface felt much cooler.
- 4 April 1991, morning surface temperature at the net site: 10 °C.

**Wildlife observations (April 1991)**
- Leopard Frog along Deadman just above the confluence. Eckstein reported frogs along the upper Deadman and possibly Leopard Frogs in the upper [[Wet-Bottom Drainage]] near [[Fuller Seep]].
- Probable Mud Turtles in the creek, per Eckstein.
- Three [[Black Hawk]] sightings and one [[Zone-Tailed Hawk]].
- A Black Hawk nest in a cottonwood in mid-channel, about 0.5 miles below the confluence.
- The confluence and North Fork Deadman area were considered potential [[Peregrine Falcon]] habitat.

**Historical reports**
- Eckstein reported sunfish in large pools downstream of the South Fork confluence in past years. These remain unconfirmed.

## Key Findings

1. **Roundtail Chub confirmed:** Four *Gila robusta* were captured in one gill net set in the first pool above the confluence (set 1700 hrs, 3 April 1991; pulled 0800 hrs, 4 April 1991). All four were preserved in [[Formalin Preservation]] as [[Voucher Specimens]]. The smallest specimen had opercle damage possibly caused by a turtle.
2. **Speckled Dace documented:** Found on both 1989 trips, representing the first fish record for the drainage. Voucher collection was recommended for the next survey.
3. **Trout unconfirmed:** Historical and anecdotal reports (1968-era recollection, 1988 and 1989 student report) were not verified during the 1991 survey, which was constrained by high water.
4. **Survey constraints:** High flows prevented an adequate survey. Access required long pack-in routes.
5. **Recommended follow-up:**
   - Resurvey the South Fork in late April to early May, or late October to early November, when temperatures are cooler and flows are lower.
   - Plan for at least five days in the field.
   - Use pack animals for supplies and equipment. The crew would carry only water and limited food.
   - Consider including a herpetology specialist for frogs and turtles, and a raptor surveyor for the confluence and North Fork area.
6. **Distribution notice:** Copies were sent to the Non-Game Branch, Fisheries Branch, and several Tonto National Forest district offices, including the Cavecreek and Payson Ranger Districts.
