# Allozyme Divergence Among Populations of the Razorback Sucker, *Xyrauchen texanus* (Catostomidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This working draft of a final report to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (Award # O0-FC-40-09610) by Donald G. Buth and collaborators at UCLA evaluates allozyme (protein electrophoresis) variation in the [[Razorback Sucker]], a declining endemic of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The study had two parts: (I) methods development for scoring enzyme expression in non-lethal tissues (skeletal muscle and fin), and (II) a geographic survey of allozyme variation across 10 to 12 sampling localities to test for introgression, regional divergence and population structure. The authors found little allozyme evidence for major population structure, could not recognize specific stocks with any marker locus, and identified introgression of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] alleles at the Etter Pond sample as the main concern, leading them to recommend against Etter Pond as a broodstock source.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker has declined throughout the Colorado River basin. Most extant populations are thought to number only hundreds or a few thousand individuals, with [[Lake Mohave]] possibly holding tens of thousands (W. L. Minckley, pers. comm.). Local reproduction has been noted, but no population is effectively recruiting, and populations are often dominated by aging adults.

Recovery includes artificial propagation at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico, using breeding stock from Lake Mohave. An earlier allozyme study (Buth et al., 1987) found only low-level introgression: 1% of the hatchery stock genome versus 1.5 to 2.8% in native Lake Mohave stock. That study identified nine diallelic and two triallelic loci and diagnostic enzymes separating razorback from flannelmouth sucker. Before hatchery stock is released more widely, the authors argued, the genetic composition of other extant populations must be examined.

The study was run in conjunction with a [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) study by Dowling and Minckley (Arizona State University), since allozymes and mtDNA measure different parts of the genome and provide dual tests of hybridization and divergence.

Two previous-work corrections were noted: the locus "mIcdh-A" was part of a duplicated system in this [[Allotetraploid]] species and is renamed mIcdh-A2; and sMdh-A1 and sMdh-A2 were more clearly scored from fin tissue, revealing a null allele.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Range and sampling:** Samples came from the upper Colorado River mainstem and neighboring impoundments, the Green and Yampa Rivers, Lake Powell (including the San Juan arm), Lake Mohave, and Senator Wash Reservoir (across the state line in California). Localities are coded by river mile where given (e.g., Green River at River Mile 311).
- **Sample sources:** Samples were supplied by agency personnel in Colorado and Utah. Lake Mohave specimens were obtained during a [[PIT Tagging]] operation by ASU personnel, using non-lethal tissue sampling.
- **Conservation constraint:** Because geographic comparisons were restricted to non-lethal tissues, the team had to establish which enzyme systems were expressed in muscle and fin. Liver biopsy was judged to carry greater risk to the fish than warranted.
- **Comparison taxa:** To identify introgressed genes, 67 [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (in addition to 73 from Buth et al., 1987) and related catostomids were screened, including *Catostomus (Pantosteus) discobolus* and two *C. commersoni*.
- **Heterozygosity context:** All samples fell below the teleost mean heterozygosity (H = 0.051; Nevo, 1978) but were average for tetraploid catostomids.

## Key Findings

**Part I: Gene expression and methods**

- Optimal buffer and tissue combinations were identified for 46 enzyme systems, resolving 94 loci in razorback suckers. Eight adults and four dissected razorbacks were used for buffer tests; six to eight specimens were used for the 11-tissue comparison.
- Fin alone is a very limited tissue source. Nearly half of the gene products scored across all tissues can be resolved when both fin and muscle are examined (Table 3: 48 loci with both tissues versus 38 with muscle only and 21 with fin only).
- Four additional polyallelic loci were resolved in Lake Mohave specimens, including Pgk-1 (muscle/liver) and Mpi-A.
- A screening protocol using muscle and fin was developed (Table 5) and applied to geographic samples.

**Part II: Geographic patterns**

- Of 38 loci screened, 17 were polyallelic in one or more samples. Most variants were uncommon and geographically restricted; the only substantial variation was at Ck-A, G6pdh-A and Tpi-B.
- Sampling totals (Table 6): Green/Yampa 51, Etter Pond 55, Upper Colorado/Highline 11, Lake Powell 22, Lake Mohave 51, Senator Wash Reservoir 14 (from Buth et al., 1987).
- Average heterozygosity (Table 7): Green/Yampa 2.1%, Etter Pond 1.5%, Upper Colorado/Highline 1.7%, Lake Powell 3.2%, Lake Mohave 1.7%, Senator Wash Reservoir 0.9%.
- Percent polyallelic loci ranged from 5.4% (Etter Pond, 2/37) to 28.9% (Green/Yampa, 11/38).
- Green and Yampa samples were not significantly different (chi-square = 38.46, df = 44, p = 0.707), and both Lake Powell samples were also homogeneous (chi-square = 9.1, p = 0.760).
- The Upper Colorado grouping was tentatively pooled but became significantly heterogeneous (p = 0.00004) after three 1993 specimens were added, because of Ck-A expression in those fish.
- Etter Pond and Lake Mohave remained distinct from all other samples. No fixed or complete allelic differences were found among samples for any locus.
- Senator Wash Reservoir had the lowest heterozygosity. Its eggs-based collection may reflect few parents, so its reduced variation may not represent the wider population.

**Introgression at Etter Pond**

- Etter Pond razorbacks showed a flannelmouth-type Ck-A genotype pattern (26 FF, 27 RF, 0 RR for Ck-A; Table 8/9), along with mIcdh-A2, mMdh-A1 and Ada-A2 markers. None of the Etter Pond fish were F1 hybrids.
- Limited mtDNA variation at Etter Pond (reported as none, T. Dowling, pers. comm.) suggests that perhaps only one female contributed genetically to the sample.
- The authors judged introgression the most parsimonious explanation, given the non-sister relationship between razorback and flannelmouth suckers. They also noted asymmetrical introgression has precedent in salamanders (Sharbel et al., 1995).

**Conclusions and recommendations**

- Razorback populations are not depauperate in allozyme variation, but there is little evidence of major structure, and specific stocks cannot be recognized with any marker locus.
- Hybridization remains the critical concern for artificial propagation. Etter Pond is eliminated as a recommended stock source because of introgression and a possibly small founding population.
- A conservative approach would treat Green/Yampa, Lake Powell and/or Lake Mohave as equally legitimate brood sources. Lake Mohave has the highest mtDNA variation and the lowest flannelmouth Ck-A frequency, but criteria beyond allozymes would be needed to justify maintaining more than one stock.
- Management is complicated because populations can be statistically distinguished only with large samples, and most individuals cannot be assigned to their source population with comparable certainty.
