# Rio Grande Silvery Minnow (*Hybognathus amarus*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-fish #endangered-species #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

This 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service proposed rule sought to list the [[Rio Grande Silvery Minnow]] (*Hybognathus amarus*) as an **endangered species** with designated **critical habitat** under the Endangered Species Act of 1973. Historically one of the most abundant and widespread fishes in the [[Rio Grande Basin]], the species has been reduced to approximately **5% of its historic range**, now surviving only in a 274 km (170 mi) reach of the Middle Rio Grande between [[Cochiti Dam]] and the headwaters of [[Elephant Butte Reservoir]], New Mexico. The species has been completely extirpated from the [[Pecos River]] and from the Rio Grande downstream of Elephant Butte Reservoir. Primary threats include dam-driven flow modification, dewatering, channelization, water quality degradation, and competition/predation from nonnative fish introductions.

## Background & Description

- **Body form:** Stout, silvery minnow
- **Eyes:** Moderately small
- **Mouth:** Small, slightly oblique
- **Maximum size:** Adults reach up to 90 mm (3½ in) total length (Sublette et al. 1990)
- **Dorsal fin:** Distinctly pointed; fin origin located slightly closer to snout tip than to base of tail (Pflieger 1975)
- **Coloration:** Silver body with emerald reflections; belly silvery-white
- **Fins:** Plain (unmarked)
- **Barbels:** Absent
- Species was first described by Girard (1856) from specimens collected near Fort Brown, Cameron County, Texas
- One of seven North American species in genus *[[Hybognathus]]* (Pflieger 1980)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Main-stem large-river cyprinid requiring flowing water, though it can persist in reduced form in irrigation return flows, diversion dam leakage pools, and irrigation ditches/drains during zero-flow periods
- **Historic range:** From Española, New Mexico, downstream to the Gulf of Mexico; also historically present in the [[Pecos River]] from Santa Rosa, NM to its confluence with the Rio Grande
- **Current range:** Restricted to 274 km (170 mi) of the Middle Rio Grande, from Cochiti Dam (Sandoval County) to headwaters of Elephant Butte Reservoir (Socorro County)
- **Reproductive requirements:** The proposed critical habitat is described as the only known area retaining physical characteristics necessary for spawning, rearing of young, and adult recruitment
- **Interspecific interactions:**
  - Displaced entirely in the Pecos River by the introduced congener [[Plains Minnow]] (*H. placitus*), first documented 1968; displacement was complete within less than a decade (Cowley 1979) and possibly involved hybridization
  - Predation pressure from nonnative species including [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*), [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*), [[White Bass]] (*Morone chrysops*), [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Brown Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus melas*, *A. nebulosus*), [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*), and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*)
  - Predation also from native predators [[Rio Grande Chub]] (*Gila pandora*) and introduced [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), particularly upon subadults
  - Related extinct/extirpated taxa: Two other native Rio Grande main-stem cyprinids, the [[Phantom Shiner]] (*Notropis orca*) and [[Bluntnose Shiner]] (*Notropis simus*), have already gone extinct — signaling systemic ecosystem collapse in this river system

## Key Findings

**Population Status**
- Species occupies only ~5% of its historic range as of 1993
- Completely extirpated from the Pecos River (last collected 1968 near Roswell, NM) and from the Rio Grande downstream of Elephant Butte Reservoir

**Primary Threats (per ESA Section 4(a)(1) factors)**
1. **Habitat destruction/modification:** Construction of five main-stem dams and two dams on the Rio Chama tributary since 1916 (Elephant Butte Dam); altered natural flow regimes; elimination of meanders/oxbows downstream of Elephant Butte; channel desiccation during low-flow years
2. **Overutilization:** None documented; scientific collecting permits expected to become more restrictive
3. **Disease/predation:** Fish crowding into isolated pools during low/no-flow periods increases susceptibility to protozoan parasite *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis* (ich) and external copepod parasites (*Lernaea*); increases exposure to introduced predatory fish and avian/mammalian predators
4. **Inadequacy of regulatory mechanisms:** New Mexico state listing (Group 2, endangered) provides only take prohibitions, not habitat protection; state water law lacks instream flow rights provisions for fish/wildlife; state bait-fish regulations permit live minnow use, facilitating species introductions
5. **Other factors:** Recurrent annual dewatering, especially in the 179 km (111 mi) reach from Isleta Diversion Dam to Elephant Butte Reservoir headwaters; construction of the 96 km (60 mi) San Acacia–Elephant Butte conveyance channel (1958) diverts flows below 57 m³/s (2,000 ft³/s), exacerbating dry-channel conditions

**Proposed Critical Habitat**
- Main stem of Rio Grande from State Highway 22 bridge (south of Cochiti Dam, Sandoval County) downstream to the Atchison Topeka & Santa Fe Railroad crossing near San Marcial, Socorro County (~262 km / 163 mi)
- Essential constituent elements identified:
  - Stream morphology sufficient to supply flowing water, food, and cover for all life stages
  - Water quality sufficient to prevent stagnation (avoiding elevated temperature, decreased oxygen, CO₂ buildup)
  - Water quantity sufficient to prevent isolated pool formation (which restricts movement, increases predation exposure, and concentrates pathogens)

**Regulatory Outcome**
- Comment period closed April 30, 1993; public hearings held April 27–28, 1993 in Albuquerque and Socorro, NM
- Proposed action: list *H. amarus* as **Endangered** under the Endangered Species Act, with concurrent critical habitat designation
- Federal agencies (e.g., [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]]) required to consult under ESA Section 7 regarding dam operation/maintenance affecting the species
