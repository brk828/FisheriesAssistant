# Cypress Minnow (Hybognathus hayi) — Illinois Status Survey

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1989 status survey by [[Melvin L. Warren Jr]] and [[Brooks M. Burr]] re-evaluates the distribution, abundance, and conservation status of the [[Cypress Minnow]] (*Hybognathus hayi*) in Illinois, a species previously believed extirpated from the state since 1940 (per [[Philip W. Smith]] 1979). Renewed collections in 1984 and 1986 confirmed the species' persistence at two localities within an 8-km reach of the middle [[Cache River]], Johnson and Pulaski counties, and possibly at [[Horseshoe Lake]], Alexander County. Historical populations in the [[Big Muddy River]] and [[Clear Creek]] drainages are confirmed extirpated. The study recommends listing the cypress minnow as Endangered in Illinois and designating the middle Cache River as critical habitat, citing wetland drainage for agriculture as the primary driver of habitat loss and range fragmentation.

## Background & Description

- **Adult standard length (SL):** 55–97 mm; species likely lives only 3–4 years.
- **Sexual maturity:** Reached at age 1, at SL ~55 mm (males) and 57–64 mm (gravid females).
- **Intestine:** Long, coiled; shorter on average than the closely related [[Mississippi Silvery Minnow]] (*Hybognathus nuchalis*), consistent with a largely herbivorous diet (sand, detritus, algae, and vegetable matter found in gut contents).
- **Tuberculation:** Males exhibit a simple tubercle pattern, consistent with communal, non-guarding spawning behavior (no parental care).
- **Growth/size classes (Illinois data):**
  - Mid-May: mean SL 19.3 mm (n=7, SD=1.7 mm)
  - July: mean SL 27.1 mm (n=22, SD=6.9 mm)
  - Late Aug–early Sept, young-of-year (YOY): mean SL 56.5 mm (n=45, SD=4.4 mm)
  - Late Aug–early Sept, age 1+: mean SL 90.3 mm (n=5, SD=5.9 mm)
- **Egg characteristics (Kentucky data):** Mature ova 0.5–0.9 mm diameter (mean 0.7 mm), yellow, distributed throughout ovary length; ovaries averaged 30.8% of body weight in gravid females (range 23.1–38.7%), containing 1,500–2,500 eggs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat associations:** Sluggish backwaters of streams, oxbows, and cypress lakes/swamps over soft substrates (sand overlain with silt/detritus or mud); logs, brush, and debris common; submerged aquatic vegetation typically absent; water depth ~0.5 m in historical Illinois sites.
- **Microhabitat at extant sites:** Captured specifically at depths of 0.6–1.3 m over 45–60 cm of soft mud/detritus ([[Limekiln Slough]]); also in a shallow (15–45 cm), duckweed-covered wetland pool with emergent vegetation and ~20 cm silt substrate near the [[Cache River]] (Highway 37 site).
- **Reproductive/migratory behavior:** Inferred communal spawning with eggs scattered over substrate, no parental care. Adults hypothesized to migrate from deep river/slough habitats into shallow adjacent wetlands or shoreline coves to spawn (by mid-April in Illinois), then return to deeper water — a pattern paralleling related species [[Eastern Silvery Minnow]] (*Hybognathus regius*) and [[Brassy Minnow]] (*Hybognathus hankinsoni*).
- **Diet:** Likely herbivorous/detritivorous — sand, detritus, algae, and vegetable matter dominate gut contents.
- **Co-occurring species at Site 1 (Cache River/wetland):** Ranked assemblage dominated by *Gambusia affinis*, *Notropis emiliae*, *Pomoxis annularis*; cypress minnow ranked 6th of 16 species.
- **Co-occurring species at Site 2 (Limekiln Slough):** Dominated by *Gambusia affinis*, *Lepomis macrochirus*, *Centrarchus macropterus*; cypress minnow ranked 7th of 13 species.
- **Regional range:** Lower Ohio River drainage (to southwestern Indiana, Green River KY, excluding Cumberland system) and Mississippi River basin from southern Illinois to the Gulf Slope, west to Sabine/Red rivers (TX/OK/LA), east to Escambia River (FL/AL).

## Key Findings

- **Historical Illinois range (7 sites, 1930s–1940):** [[Big Muddy River]], [[Beaucoup Creek]], [[Little Muddy River]], [[Horseshoe Lake]], and [[Cache River]] drainages, plus [[Clear Creek]] drainage.
- **Confirmed extirpations:** Big Muddy River and Clear Creek drainages — no specimens found despite targeted resurveys.
- **Extant populations:** Restricted to an 8-km reach of the middle Cache River (Johnson/Pulaski counties) at two sites — Cache River/adjacent wetland near the Highway 37 bridge, and Limekiln Slough at Limekiln Spring — plus a possible remnant population at Horseshoe Lake (single specimen taken in 1984, none since; sampling constrained by deep muck and obstructions).
- **Rarity indicators:** Of 25 total sites sampled, cypress minnows were found at only 2. No adult specimens were captured during the 1988 survey — only YOY individuals — suggesting adults occupy deeper main-channel/slough habitat inaccessible to seining, migrating to shallow wetlands only briefly to spawn.
- **Spawning timing:** Based on length-frequency progression and comparison to hatching size in *H. regius* (5.0–6.0 mm TL), Illinois cypress minnows are estimated to spawn by **mid-April**.
- **Capture frequency at Horseshoe Lake Spillway:** Only 2 specimens collected in 52 years of intermittent sampling, suggesting either a small population in the lake or low susceptibility to washover capture.
- **Regional status comparison:**
  - Indiana: Not collected since early 1940s; known from only 4 localities; no active search effort (per [[Gerking 1945]]).
  - Kentucky: Listed as Threatened; especially vulnerable in the Green River drainage (2 localities, both threatened by mining pollution).
  - Missouri: Rediscovered after presumed extirpation; now listed as Endangered.
  - Arkansas: Declined to an undetermined extent but stable populations persist across lowlands.
  - Tennessee: No evidence of historical decline.
- **Primary threat identified:** Wetland drainage for agricultural conversion, implicated in the loss of Big Muddy River and Clear Creek populations; ongoing threats to the Cache River population include continued wetland development, potential toxic spills, and siltation from poor agricultural practices.
- **Conservation recommendations:**
  1. List *Hybognathus hayi* as **Endangered** in Illinois.
  2. Designate the **middle Cache River** (from the Chicago and Eastern Illinois Railroad bridge downstream to the mouth of Limekiln Slough) as **critical habitat**.
  3. Prevent further habitat modification throughout the middle Cache River drainage.
  4. Note existing partial protections: part of the Limekiln Slough area is an Illinois Nature Preserve; land along the Cache River at the Highway 37 bridge is owned by the Illinois Department of Conservation.
- **Taxonomic/comparative note:** Closely related to and historically confused with *Hybognathus nuchalis* ([[Mississippi Silvery Minnow]]); distinguished via intestinal morphology and habitat preference (Fingerman and Suttkus 1961).
