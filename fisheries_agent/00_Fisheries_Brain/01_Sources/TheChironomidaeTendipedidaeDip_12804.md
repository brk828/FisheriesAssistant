# Chironomidae (Diptera) of Alabama with Descriptions of Six New Species

#entomology #chironomidae #diptera

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[J. S. Dendy]] and [[James E. Sublette]] (published 1959 in the *Annals of the Entomological Society of America*) reports the non-biting midge family [[Chironomidae]] from Alabama. The authors report 50 species from the state, of which 26 had not previously been recorded there. Six new species are described. The study began with invertebrate fish food sampled in experimental ponds at the Agriculture Experiment Station of the Alabama Polytechnic Institute, [[Auburn Alabama]]. The paper also gives a key to the North American species of [[Pentaneura]], describes the immature stages of two species, and adopts a revised nomenclature based on [[Freeman Nomenclature]] (1955–1958).

## Background & Description

**Study context.** The midge fauna was first found in a survey of invertebrates available as food for fishes in experimental ponds. A literature search showed very few earlier records of Chironomidae from Alabama, so the paper presents new collection data and descriptions. Most material was collected by Dendy. Specimens from a 1942 project at [[Wilson Dam]] were supplied by J. N. Belkin, and the work was partly supported by National Institutes of Health Grant RG4594, a study of Chironomidae distribution in relation to domestic sewage pollution.

**Nomenclature.** The authors follow Freeman's generic and subgeneric names, except that they adopt the 1800 names under ICZN Opinion 152 instead of Meigen (1803). They also retain *Tanytarsus* ad interim, sensu Edwards (1929), while a suspension of Coquillett's 1910 type fixation is requested.

**Measurements and abbreviations.** Descriptions use standard ratios: wing length (W.L.), leg ratio (L.R.), antennal ratio (A.R.), and the basitarsus-to-tarsal-segments ratio (Ta1:Ta2,3). Thoracic chaetotaxy is recorded as dorsomedian (D.M.), dorsolateral (D.L.), and prealar (P.A.) bristles.

**Taxonomic groups covered.** The checklist is arranged by subfamily: Tanypodinae (including [[Pentaneura]] and [[Procladius]]), Orthocladiinae (including [[Cricotopus]], [[Nanocladius]] and [[Psectrocladius]]), and Chironominae (tribes Tanytarsini and Chironomini, with genera such as [[Tanytarsus]], [[Polypedilum]], [[Tendipes]] and [[Glyptotendipes]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fish food.** Chironomid larvae form an important part of the invertebrate food base for fishes in experimental ponds, which motivated the survey.
- **Bryozoan hosts.** Many species were reared from colonies of the freshwater bryozoans [[Plumatella]] and [[Pectinatella Magnifica]]. The new species [[Tendipes Pectinatellae]] was found in colonies from a fish pond near Smiths, Lee County, Alabama. Its larvae and pupae lived mostly in burrows within 5 mm of the jelly surface, and deeper burrows contained no midge larvae.
- **Aquatic plant host.** [[Nanocladius Alternantherae]] larvae were collected on [[Alternanthera Philoxeroides]] (alligator weed). The species emerges mainly in late fall in Louisiana, with a smaller spring emergence.
- **Louisiana material.** Several species came from [[Cane River Lake]] and [[Natchitoches Louisiana]], collected by tent trap over multiple seasons. Tent-trap records indicated maximum emergence of *N. alternantherae* between August 15 and December 15.
- **Reservoir material.** Comparative material of [[Glyptotendipes Meridionalis]] came from [[Lake Texoma]] (Denison Reservoir), Oklahoma and Texas.

## Key Findings

1. **Regional checklist.** The paper reports 50 Chironomidae species from Alabama. Of these, 26 species were new state records. Notable species include *Pentaneura (Ablabesmyia) aequifasciata*, *Cricotopus belkini*, *Nanocladius alternantherae*, *Tendipes (Cryptochironomus) pectinatellae*, *T. (C.) directus*, and *Glyptotendipes (Phytotendipes) meridionalis*.

2. **Six new species described:**
   - *Pentaneura (Ablabesmyia) aequifasciata*: holotype male from Wilson Dam, Alabama (July 13, 1942). Wing length 2.26 mm in the male. It is separated from related species in a key by its three fore-tibial bands, 10–12 anterolateral bristles, and slender, straight aedeagal blades.
   - *Cricotopus belkini*: holotype female from Wilson Dam (June 24, 1942), total length 1.7 mm. It differs from related species by darker legs, longer fore tibia, and light cerci.
   - *Nanocladius alternantherae*: holotype male from Auburn (April 8, 1957). Wing length 1.33 mm. Larval, pupal and adult stages are described. Forty-two paratypes are listed.
   - *Tendipes (Cryptochironomus) directus*: holotype male from Auburn (September 3, 1957). Distinguished by its linear dististyles and the lack of a lateral apical shoulder on the superior appendage.
   - *Tendipes (Cryptochironomus) pectinatellae*: holotype male from Phenix City, Alabama (September 11, 1957). Wing length 2.11 mm. The mature larva, pupa and egg mass are described. Fifty males and 21 females emerged from one container.
   - *Glyptotendipes (Phytotendipes) meridionalis*: holotype male from Lake Texoma, Oklahoma (July 8, 1950). Wing length 2.7 mm in the male, with a table comparing it with *G. barbipes*, *G. paripes* and *G. lobiferus*.

3. **Immature stages.** The larval and pupal stages of *N. alternantherae* and *T. (C.) pectinatellae* are described for the first time in this paper.

4. **Pentaneura key.** A dichotomous key separates the North American species of *Pentaneura (Ablabesmyia)*, including *cinctipes*, *annulata*, *monilis*, *mallochi*, *idei*, *basalis*, *prudens*, *peleensis* and *illinoensis*. The authors note possible transposition of figures in Walley (1928) and mixed paratype series for *P. illinoensis*.

5. **Taxonomic revisions.** *Tendipes (Tribelos)* is tentatively placed in *Tendipes* because it differs from *Endochironomus* in having separated R1 and R4+5 veins and only one middle-tibial spine. Several species are recombined, including *Micropsectra dubia* and *Tendipes (Endochironomus) nigricans*.

6. **Unresolved material.** Some females are left undescribed because males are missing, including specimens of *Pentaneura* spp. and a *Tanytarsus* group with "at least eight new species" that are reserved for a later revision of the North American Tanytarsini.
