## Executive Summary
This document is a 1984 letter from ichthyologist Robert R. Miller (Curator of Fishes, University of Michigan Museum of Zoology) to W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) concerning ongoing taxonomic research on desert pupfishes (genus [[Cyprinodon]]) from northern Mexico. The letter describes a collaborative research effort involving morphometric, meristic, and osteological analyses of pupfish populations collected at a newly discovered hot-spring locality ("Julimes") in 1983, and their comparison to the previously described [[Balneario San Diego]] population. Miller strongly suspects the Julimes pupfish is conspecific with the undescribed taxon *[[Cyprinodon megacephalus]]* (Minckley and Minckley, manuscript), and urges Minckley to finalize and publish the formal species description. The letter also references ongoing work toward description of *[[Cyprinodon albivelis]]*.

## Background & Description
- **Study taxa**: Undescribed/newly discovered [[Cyprinodon]] population from **Julimes** hot spring, compared against the **Balneario San Diego** population and putative hybrids ("thorny ones") from a reservoir below the "cabanas" site.
- **Hybridization note**: Individuals described as "thorny ones" collected from a reservoir near the cabanas are suspected by Miller to be hybrids, implying morphological intermediacy or anomalous spination/morphology relative to parental populations.
- **Methodological approach to morphology**:
  - **Shape analysis**: Modern geometric morphometric ("shape-analysis") techniques developed by the **Michigan Morphometrics Group**, spearheaded by **Fred Bookstein**, were to be applied — noted as a major methodological contribution then under peer review.
  - **Meristics**: Traditional counting methods (fin rays, scales, etc.) to be performed by Miller himself.
  - **Osteology**: Skeletal analysis to be conducted by Julian Humphries.
- **Sexual dimorphism reference**: Miller requests black-and-white photographs distinguishing male (♂) and female (♀) specimens of *C. albivelis* from both the **Santa Maria basin** and **Papigóchic basin** populations, implying documented or expected sexual dimorphism relevant to species description.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Thermal/hot-spring systems in the Chihuahuan desert region of northern Mexico.
- **Key localities**:
  - **[[Julimes]]** — newly discovered hot-spring locality (collected 1983), noted as ecologically/morphologically similar to Balneario San Diego.
  - **[[Balneario San Diego]]** — previously known hot-spring pupfish locality used as comparative reference population.
  - **Reservoir habitat** (below the "cabanas") — site yielding putative hybrid individuals, suggesting habitat overlap or connectivity between divergent lineages.
  - **[[Santa Maria Basin]]** and **[[Papigóchic Basin]]** — additional drainage basins relevant to *Cyprinodon albivelis* distribution.
- **Implication**: These isolated desert spring systems represent classic allopatric speciation contexts typical of the genus [[Cyprinodon]], with hot-spring endemism and potential hybrid zones where isolated populations come into secondary contact (e.g., the reservoir hybrids).

## Key Findings
- Miller collected new *Cyprinodon* material from **Julimes** in 1983, a hot-spring locality ecologically comparable to Balneario San Diego.
- Strong taxonomic hypothesis: the **Julimes pupfish population is likely conspecific with *Cyprinodon megacephalus*** (Minckley and Minckley, unpublished manuscript name).
- Evidence of probable **hybridization** among Cyprinodon populations in a reservoir habitat near the cabanas ("thorny ones").
- Research division of labor established for the 2-month collaborative visit of David Soltz:
  - Soltz: morphometric (shape-based) measurements using novel geometric morphometrics.
  - Miller: meristic counts.
  - Julian Humphries: osteological examination.
- Reference to a **major forthcoming methodological contribution** from the Michigan Morphometrics Group (Fred Bookstein et al.), then under review, marking an early application of modern geometric morphometrics to pupfish taxonomy.
- Miller was concurrently completing a major taxonomic monograph/book (~300 pages at time of writing), likely his broader work on [[Cyprinodon]] systematics of the Chihuahuan Desert region.
- Active urging for Minckley to finalize and publish the *Cyprinodon megacephalus* manuscript with co-author "Chuck," to ensure proper priority and credit for the species discovery.
- Description of *[[Cyprinodon albivelis]]* was in progress, requiring additional photographic documentation of sexual dimorphism across the Santa Maria and Papigóchic basin populations.
