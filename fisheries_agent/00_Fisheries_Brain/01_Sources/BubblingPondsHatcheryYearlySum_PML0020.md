# Bubbling Ponds Hatchery Yearly Summary (2003)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This yearly report from the [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]] (BPH) covers administration, production, stocking and project work for 2003. The hatchery's main output was [[Razorback Sucker]] for stocking in the [[Colorado River]] system, with 17,869 fish released between 250 and 400 mm in total length. Smaller numbers of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] were reared for stocking in the [[Verde River]], and 623 wild [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) were brought in from Sabino Creek to protect that population from wildfire effects. Predation, especially by birds and otters, was the main cause of fish loss. A new Bureau of Reclamation contract supports culture of 20,000 razorback suckers to 300 mm over four years.

## Background & Description

**Administration.** The Fish Culturist II is responsible for hatchery administration. Funding from a new [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] contract made a three-year limited full-time position possible, with the new hire expected to start in early 2004. Support during the year came from [[Page Springs Hatchery]], Regional Personnel, the Bureau of Reclamation, the [[United States Fish And Wildlife Service]] and summer interns.

**Contract scope.** The Bureau of Reclamation contract calls for culturing 20,000 razorback suckers to 300 mm over a four-year period, for stocking below [[Lake Havasu]].

**Species in culture.**
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: the principal production species. Fish health was very good in 2003.
- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]: reared in small and large groups. Large fish reached stocking size early because densities were low.
- [[Gila Chub]]: 623 wild fish from Sabino Creek were held at the hatchery as a conservation refuge.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Predation and pests.** Predation was the major cause of loss. A bird netting program was installed over lined ponds to reduce avian predation. Otter numbers appear to be increasing, with more sightings and scat found across the facility.

**Disease.** No fish were lost to [[Ich]] (*Ichthyophthirius*), *Trichodina*, or other parasites in 2003. No chemical treatments were needed for razorbacks. Bacterial infections were present but caused minimal losses. Pikeminnow were treated for ich. Pikeminnow appear to tolerate bacterial infection better than razorbacks.

**Density and growth.** Available space limits how many fish can be cultured for annual stocking. Growth of smaller pikeminnow is expected to slow as they become more crowded.

**Wildfire and refuge.** The Gila chub were removed from Sabino Creek in [[Sabino Canyon]] on [[Mount Lemmon]] because of fires and their aftermath. Fish were treated with formalin for parasites.

**Stocking locations.** Stocked sites include the [[A-7 Backwater]] on the Colorado River, [[Lake Mohave]], and the Verde River. Target sites for future stocking are listed in the pond inventory.

## Key Findings

**1. Razorback sucker production and stocking (2003).**
- 17,869 razorback suckers (250–400 mm) were stocked in 2003.
- Colorado River system: 17,492 fish. A-7 Backwater received 14,058 and Lake Mohave received 3,433.
- Verde River: 378 fish.
- Table One lists ten stocking events from February to December 2003, with average sizes of 275–335 mm, all at the A-7 Backwater or Lake Mohave. Individual entries are partly garbled by OCR, so they should be checked against the original.
- Cumulative stocking in the Colorado River portion totals 38,542 razorback suckers.

**2. Lake Mohave stocking goal reached.** The target of 10,000 fish from BPH was met. Since 1999, 13,200 razorback suckers have been stocked in Lake Mohave. No further stocking there is planned until obligations below Lake Havasu are met.

**3. Health management.** Razorback health was exceptionally good, with no chemical treatments and no ich, *Trichodina* or parasite losses. Most losses were due to predation.

**4. Colorado pikeminnow.** Large fish reached 400 mm or more early because of low densities. The remaining large fish are to be planted in the Verde River in January. Smaller fish are growing well but will slow as crowding increases. Both groups were treated for ich.

**5. Gila chub refuge.** 623 fish were collected from Sabino Creek. High mortality occurred in the first 2.5 weeks, mostly from handling during capture. 578 fish remained at the time of reporting, with no mortality for 48 days. The fish are gaining weight on freeze-dried krill, shrimp, bloodworms and pellets, and prefer the freeze-dried feed. They will stay at the hatchery until creek conditions improve.

**6. Facility projects.**
- The spring pond supplying the hatchery was modified to improve fish production and snail habitat.
- Annual ditch maintenance, semiannual ground maintenance and wildlife trail work were completed or continued.
- Bird lines and netting were placed over all lined ponds.
- A new drain line was installed for fish isolation tanks.
- A new water line now supplies two lower ponds and a second truck filling station.

**7. Pond inventory and planned stocking (Table Two).** Eight production ponds (P1–P8) were listed. P3 was empty and reserved for splitting fish from P5 or Willow Beach fish. The main planned releases are:
- P4: 10,000 razorbacks at 75 mm, for A-7 Backwater in spring 2005.
- P5: 8,500 razorbacks at 90 mm, for A-7 Backwater in winter 2005.
- P6: 10,000 pikeminnow at 125 mm and 500 razorbacks at 200 mm, for the Verde River in winter 2006.
- P7: 6,500 razorbacks at 225 mm, for the Verde River and A-7 Backwater in spring 2004.
- P8: 2,000 pikeminnow at 200 mm and 2,000 razorbacks at 350 mm, for the Verde River and A-7 Backwater in winter 2004 and 2005.
- P1: 1,500 pikeminnow and 500 razorbacks at 400 mm, for the Verde River in January 2004.
- P2: 3,000 razorbacks at 240 mm, for the A-7 Backwater in spring 2004.
