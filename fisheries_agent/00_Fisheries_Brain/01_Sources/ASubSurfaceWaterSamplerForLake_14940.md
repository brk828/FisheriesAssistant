# A Sub-Surface Water Sampler for Lake, Pond and River Studies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #fisheries-science #sampling-methodology #aquatic-ecosystem

## Executive Summary

This paper (Watten, 1983) describes the design, construction, and cost of a novel **spring-activated gate-valve water sampler** intended to overcome deficiencies in traditional sub-surface water sampling instruments such as the [[Kemmerer Sampler]] and [[Van Dorn Sampler]]. The device is engineered to obtain undisturbed water samples at known depths in lakes, ponds, and rivers without restricting water flow during descent or disturbing local water stratification upon activation — key criteria for reliable [[Water Quality Sampling]] in [[Limnology]] and aquatic ecosystem monitoring. A second, appended fragment describes an unrelated but thematically related instrument: the **Quatrefoil Larval Fish Light Trap**, a device used to passively capture larval fish attracted to a submerged light source, addressing criteria for effective field-based [[Larval Fish Sampling]].

Although this is fundamentally an engineering/methodology paper rather than a species-specific ichthyological study, both instruments described are directly relevant to fisheries science: the water sampler supports water-chemistry and phytoplankton studies underpinning fish habitat assessment, while the light trap is a direct tool for capturing early life-history stages of fish for ecological and population studies.

## Background & Description
*(Instrument design / structural characteristics, in place of organismal morphology)*

**Sub-Surface Water Sampler:**
- Cylindrical sampling chamber: 32.6 cm (14.25 in) length, 7.3 cm (2.88 in) ID PVC pipe; capacity 1.87 L (63.2 oz).
- Closure devices: two 7.62 cm (3 in) ABS plastic **gate valves** (recreational-vehicle waste-valve type) threaded to each end of the chamber, offering **no flow restriction** when open.
- Gate valves aligned radially and secured with 1.27 cm polyethylene hose fitting adapters.
- Modifications: brass valve handles, brass wear plate-valve stem bushing, and a 3.6 mm diameter cross-drilled hole in the valve stem (12.2 cm from handle end) for the release pin.
- Lower assembly: 7.62 cm × 10.16 cm PVC reducing fitting acting as a flow director and support base.
- Closure mechanism: stainless steel impact release rod with welded perforated release-pin plates, actuated by a **230 g (8.1 oz) lead coil messenger**; twin tension springs (9.85 cm × 1.11 cm) close both valves simultaneously upon pin release.
- Asymmetric release-pin lengths (upper pin 7 mm longer) allow independent, sequential valve setting.
- Drain system: 48 cm length of 1.27 cm ID plastic tubing; all water-contact parts constructed of inert plastic.
- Total instrument weight and materials are lightweight and low-cost (~$176, 1983 USD), comparable to commercial Kemmerer/Van Dorn models (see [[Table 1 - Sampler Parts and Cost]]).
- Convertible to horizontal orientation via external trigger mechanism and tail-fin stabilizer for use in flowing (lotic) waters.

**Quatrefoil Larval Fish Light Trap** (appended fragment):
- Constructed of plexiglas tube (7.62 cm OD × 0.32 cm wall) and sheet (0.64 cm thick).
- Total weight with battery pack: 1.8 kg (4 lbs).
- Height: 40.4 cm (storage) / 44.8 cm (deployed with lamp).
- Top plate diameter: 19 cm; bottom plate diameter: 24.3 cm.
- Collection pan: stainless steel bowl, 24 cm diameter × 8.6 cm deep, with a 7 cm opening covered by 120-micron mesh (#12 bolting cloth) polyester screen to retain larvae while allowing water flow.
- Pan secured via four stainless steel spring clips for easy specimen removal.
- Light distribution device: plexiglas rod (4.27 cm diameter) conducting light from a submersible lamp assembly.
- Lamp assembly: T-3¼ No. 1490 miniature bayonet-base bulb wired via waterproof microphone cable, housed in a machined aluminum alloy (2024-T4) housing sealed with silicone rubber, rendering it fully submersible.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sub-Surface Water Sampler**: Designed for use across [[Lentic Ecosystems]] (lakes, ponds) and [[Lotic Ecosystems]] (rivers), particularly where **repeated sampling in small water bodies** requires minimal disturbance to natural water column stratification — a critical factor in accurately characterizing dissolved gases, phytoplankton distribution, and water chemistry gradients relevant to fish habitat quality.
- **Quatrefoil Light Trap**: Targets the **larval fish life-history stage**, exploiting positive phototactic behavior documented broadly across larval fish and associated planktonic taxa (Verheijen 1958; Faber 1981). Applicable in situ either resting on the substrate or suspended within the water column, extending its ecological sampling range across the entire water column in [[Larval Fish Ecology]] studies.
- Both instruments serve foundational roles in field-based [[Aquatic Ecosystem Monitoring]], supporting downstream fisheries and limnological research by ensuring representative, undisturbed sample collection.

## Key Findings

- Standard samplers (Kemmerer, Van Dorn) suffer from design flaws: the Kemmerer's rubber-stopper valves restrict water flow during descent, risking non-representative samples; the Van Dorn's external elastic tubing valves are prone to failure.
- Both traditional designs disturb the surrounding water column upon activation and are susceptible to premature triggering.
- The new spring-activated gate-valve sampler eliminates flow restriction during descent (valves offer no obstruction when open) and closes both valves **simultaneously** via a robust impact-release mechanism, reducing water disturbance at depth.
- Independent valve-setting capability (via differential release-pin lengths) improves field reliability and ease of operation.
- The instrument is constructed entirely of inert plastic in water-contact areas, preserving sample chemical integrity.
- Cost (~$175.92, 1983 USD) is comparable to commercial Kemmerer and Van Dorn samplers, while offering improved performance and simplified drain/maintenance systems (see [[Table 1 - Sampler Parts and Cost]]).
- The design can be adapted for horizontal deployment in flowing water via addition of a tail-fin stabilizer, broadening its applicability to riverine studies.
- The companion **Quatrefoil Larval Fish Light Trap** meets key field criteria (per Kindschi et al. 1979) for effective larval fish capture: easy larval entry with difficult escape, large capture-area-to-trap-size ratio, broadly distributed light source, ruggedness, reliable submersible electronics, portability, and economic feasibility.
- The light trap's waterproof, sealant-based lamp housing allows deployment both on the substrate and mid-water column, expanding its utility for sampling larval fish distributions across depth strata.
