# Evolutionary Relationships of the Atherinopsinae (Pisces: Atherinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This systematic revision uses [[Cladistics]] (Hennig's phylogenetic systematics) to test the relationships of the New World silverside subfamily [[Atherinopsinae]] within the family [[Atherinidae]]. Based on osteological comparisons of cleared-and-stained and dissected specimens, the study diagnoses the subfamily with five synapomorphies, supports its sister-group relationship with the subfamily [[Menidiinae]], and rejects the earlier hypothesis that it is closely related to the Old World [[Atherininae]]. The Atherinopsinae is split into two tribes: the northern [[Atherinopsini]] (four genera) and the southern [[Basilichthyini]] (two genera). Several haemal, premaxillary and jaw-mobility traits are concluded to have evolved independently in the two tribes, and the author argues this is a case of parallel evolution.

## Background & Description

The silverside family [[Atherinidae]] has resisted resolution for over a century. [[Jordan and Hubbs 1919]] applied the name Atherinopsinae broadly to nearly all American silversides. [[Schultz 1948]] restricted the subfamily using a derived haemal-spine condition and placed the excluded genera in Menidiinae. [[Patten 1978]] judged the Atherinopsinae monophyletic but unrelated to the Atherininae and questioned the two-subfamily division.

Of the eight genera Schultz included, seven remain recognized. [[Hubbsiella]] was synonymized with [[Leuresthes]] by Moffatt and Thomson (1975). The validity of some genera, notably [[Colpichthys]] and [[Atherinopsis]], had been questioned (Todd, 1976). The subfamily as revised here comprises six genera: [[Atherinops]], [[Atherinopsis]], [[Basilichthys]], [[Colpichthys]], [[Leuresthes]] and [[Odontesthes]]. [[Austromenidia]] is treated as a junior synonym of Odontesthes.

Material came from the Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County (LACM) and six other collections. At least two males and two females of each genus were cleared and counterstained, with the exception of [[Basilichthys]], represented by one male. The species list (about 140 catalog entries across Atheriniformes, Beloniformes and Cyprinodontiformes) is summarized here rather than reproduced in full.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Marine and freshwater range.** North American atherinopsines are marine, found along the outer coast and islands from Vancouver Island to the Gulf of California. South American members occur in temperate marine and fresh waters, including high Andean streams.
- **Grunion reproduction.** [[Leuresthes]] species (California grunion *L. tenuis* and Gulf grunion *L. sardina*) are lunar spawners. They bury eggs at the surf line on sandy beaches during extreme spring and early-summer high tides. Embryos develop in sand for about two weeks, and high tides return to trigger hatching (Walker, 1952). The eggs lack filaments, unlike those of other atherinopsines and most atherinomorphs.
- **Feeding and straining.** The undescribed Gulf of California species and [[Colpichthys]] regis have hairlike teeth on the third pharyngobranchials, which the author suggests strain fine sand particles.
- **Outgroups.** Comparisons drew on the Atherinomorpha, including [[Cyprinodontiformes]], [[Beloniformes]] and other atheriniforms.

## Key Findings

**Diagnosis of the subfamily.** Five synapomorphies support the monophyly of the Atherinopsinae:
1. Baudelot's ligament ossified.
2. No enlarged toothplates on the proximal fourth ceratobranchial; instead, paired toothplates run along its length.
3. Medial bony shelf on the palatine.
4. Ventral projection of the nasal bone contacts the lachrymal.
5. Ventral bony knob on the lateral ethmoid.

**Sister group.** The Menidiinae is the sister group, supported by seven synapomorphies (e.g., scapula-cleithrum bony struts, hammer-shaped palatine head, reduced supraorbital pores to four). The Menidiinae is itself diagnosed by three traits, including fused proximal toothplates on the fourth ceratobranchial. The author concludes that Schultz (1948) was correct to separate the two subfamilies but wrong to link the Atherinopsinae with the Atherininae.

**Two tribes.**
- *Atherinopsini* (North America): supraoccipital process trifid, paired vomerine dorsal projections, and haemal funnel formed by bifurcated haemal spines. Four genera are recognized: Atherinops, Atherinopsis, Colpichthys and Leuresthes.
- *Basilichthyini* (South America): fenestrated basioccipital, extrascapular composed of two elements, and haemal arches expanded into broad hypophyses. Two genera are recognized: Basilichthys and Odontesthes.

**Internal relationships.**
- Atherinops + Colpichthys form one group, diagnosed by bicuspid jaw teeth and an anteriorly angled quadrate.
- Atherinopsis + Leuresthes form another, defined by a spinelike medial process on the lateral ethmoid and a contorted third epibranchial.
- Colpichthys regis and the undescribed Gulf of California species are proposed as sister species, sharing a notched maxillary dorsal process, hairlike pharyngobranchial teeth and widened fourth-ceratobranchial toothplates.
- Within the South American tribe, Basilichthys is diagnosed by a rounded maxillary process. Odontesthes lacks a mesethmoid, has a fenestrated opercle (except *O. incisus*), and has a thin, spinelike premaxillary dorsal process with protractile jaws.

**Convergence and parallelism.** The haemal funnel is an example. The two tribes' haemal modifications are nonhomologous, but both carry the swimbladder into a modified funnel. The author judges that the traits are not convergent because they arose from a shared ancestral developmental potential, and that they fit the definitions of parallel evolution by Simpson (1961) and Mayr (1969). Jaw protrusion is also treated as having evolved more than once, independently in the North American grunions and in Odontesthes.

**Taxonomic outcome.** Two tribes are named (Atherinopsini and Basilichthyini), each after the first genus described in it. Approximate species counts are given as about 6 for Basilichthys and about 10 for Odontesthes, and both genera are flagged as needing generic revision.

**Key generic diagnosis.** A dichotomous key separates the six genera. Atherinops has frilled skin folds between the maxilla and the mouth corner and equal-cusped teeth. Colpichthys has a notched maxillary process and expanded fourth-ceratobranchial toothplates. Leuresthes has greatly protractile jaws, reduced vomer and minute or absent jaw teeth.
