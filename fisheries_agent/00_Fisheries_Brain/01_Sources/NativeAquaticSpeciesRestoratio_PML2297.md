# Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility Operation and Maintenance: FY 2003 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Colorado River Recovery Program annual project report (Project No. 29d, FY 2003, ongoing) covers the operation and maintenance of the [[Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility]] (NASRF) in Alamosa, Colorado. The facility produces endangered fishes for stocking in the upper Colorado River Basin and the San Juan drainage. The report is signed by Principal Investigator K. David Schnoor of the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] on 18 November 2003. It covers culture of [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], pond and tank inventories, stocking records, losses, and a FY 2001 budget status.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** Native Aquatic Species Restoration Facility operation and maintenance, under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]].
- **Principal investigators:** David Schnoor and Jenn Logan, Colorado Division of Wildlife, Alamosa.
- **Study schedule:** Ongoing.
- **Relationship to 2003 recovery plan (RIPRAP):** The work supports several recovery actions, including securing refugia-based presumptive stocks of razorback sucker, operating and maintaining facilities, the Mumma NASRF program, and state stocking plans for Colorado pikeminnow and bonytail. It also relates to the Green River Implementation Plan for the four endangered fishes.
- **Facility:** The J.W. Mumma NASRF is currently rearing fish for stocking in the upper Basin and is being adapted for stocking in the San Juan drainage. Improvements at the TSJC ponds were completed, and the report also refers to the NASRF pond system and recovery ponds near Farmington, New Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Source stocks:** Bonytail fingerlings were received from Dexter, New Mexico. Colorado pikeminnow fry (8,000) were also received from Dexter, New Mexico, on 6/02/03.
- **Pond and tank conditions:** Pond space limited growth, so some pikeminnow remained small. Ponds at TSJC were not feeding well. Heavy bird predation early in the year and fishing activity at the TSJC site contributed to losses.
- **Parasite control:** Formalin was used to treat a parasite outbreak in the TSJC ponds, shortly before PIT tagging of the smaller ponds.
- **Losses:** Fish losses were attributed to moving fish, lethal sampling for fish health inspection, disease and predation. Dead fish were preserved in ethanol for possible genetic work.
- **Facility infrastructure:** Pond and tank covers were planned for the three smaller TSJC ponds this fall. The two larger ponds were not to be covered. Transfer of fish between ponds through pipes was also noted as a management option.

## Key Findings

**Culture and stocking**
- NASRF received about 8,000 bonytail on 5/26/03 from Dexter, New Mexico. Survival was good, and roughly 1,990 fish were reported on hand in the ponds at NASRF. The scan of this figure is partly illegible.
- About 8,000 Colorado pikeminnow fry were received from Dexter on 6/02/03. About 2,000 fish were on hand, weighing approximately the stated size. They were expected to be ready for stocking in November.
- 574 razorback suckers were stocked into recovery ponds near Farmington, New Mexico.
- PIT-tagged bonytail and pikeminnow were stocked in the Green River and the San Juan drainage. The total number of bonytail stocked from 10/02 to 9/03 is partly illegible in the scan but is reported as roughly 14,000.

**Inventory as of 30 September 2003**
- The inventory table lists about 14 entries across pikeminnow (2002 and 2003 year classes), bonytail (2001 to 2003 year classes), and razorback sucker (2001 year class), across NASRF ponds and tanks, TSJC ponds and Farmington recovery ponds.
- The largest listed counts are 11,000 and two entries of 7,000, including tanks 1 to 4 at NASRF.
- The table entries for numbers and locations are partly garbled in the scan, so exact pairings are not reliable.

**Recovery at TSJC**
- Of an 8,000-plus group of bonytail stocked at TSJC ponds, 562 fish were recovered.
- Only about 900 of another group were recovered from the TSJC ponds. Losses were attributed to heavy bird predation, angling and disease treatment.
- Two of the largest 2001 fish died during a formalin treatment.

**Recommendations and status**
- Continue culturing bonytail and pikeminnow at NASRF to meet Colorado's stocking needs.
- PIT-tag bonytail and pikeminnow for fall stocking.
- Project status: ongoing fish culture.

**FY 2001 budget status**
- About $70,000 was expected from the Bureau of Reclamation (BOR). Only $17,000 was transferred to the NASRF budget, and that amount was not received. The account was also not reimbursed from Colorado's recovery program contribution.
- Total recorded expenditures were $138,681.82. The largest categories were labor ($50,000.00), TSJC lease ($18,000.00), NASRF pond improvements ($13,839.27), feed ($5,560.00) and equipment maintenance ($5,263.92).
- Approximately $37,000 of total expenses were one-time expenditures.
- The line items sum to $138,681.80, a $0.02 difference from the stated total.
