# Fish Monitoring of Palm Lake, Hassayampa River Preserve

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #native-fish-restoration

## Executive Summary

This monitoring report documents the status of native fish species stocked at [[Palm Lake]], [[Hassayampa River Preserve]], Arizona, sampled on 2-3 April 1990. The assessment evaluated survival and condition of [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) stocked in April 1989, as well as [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) introduced to springs in August 1989. [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) were stocked in February 1990. Trammel netting and seining revealed healthy populations of target species with evidence of reproductive condition in males, though the presence of non-native [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*) poses potential predation concerns.

## Background & Description

[[Palm Lake]] at the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] serves as a refuge and grow-out facility for endangered native Arizona fishes. Initial stockings occurred on 20 April 1989 with 688 [[Bonytail Chub]] averaging 203 mm total length and 35 [[Razorback Sucker]] averaging 250 mm. On 19 August 1989, [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]] were introduced to nearby spring habitats. Most recently, 1,136 [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] averaging 101.6 mm TL were stocked on 3 February 1990.

The monitoring employed trammel nets (50 ft. and 75 ft.) placed across the deepest lake section and checked at 2-3 hour intervals over 24 hours, supplemented by seining and backpack electrofishing in spring pools. The ultimate goal is to grow fish to large sizes for eventual translocation to wild riverine habitats in the [[Salt River]] and/or [[Verde River]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] provides both lentic (lake) and spring habitats supporting different native fish assemblages. Spring pools are physically separated by barriers that prevent upstream migration from the main lake. The upper spring pool measures 6 m x 4 m. Heavy aquatic vegetation characterizes the lake margins.

Non-native species present include [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) abundant in the main lake and [[Yellow Bullhead]], both representing potential threats to native fish populations through predation and competition. Crayfish populations are also present and were observed both as prey items and as predators of small fish.

## Key Findings

**Trammel Net Results (24-hour sampling):**
- 29 [[Bonytail Chub]] captured (plus 9 recaptures), total 38 captures
- 9 [[Razorback Sucker]] captured (one escaped)
- All fish appeared healthy and free of external parasites
- Seven male razorbacks in running ripe condition
- One male bonytail in running ripe condition; two other males highly tuberculate
- [[Razorback Sucker]] caught primarily during evening and early morning hours

**Size Data:**
- [[Bonytail Chub]]: 290-395 mm TL (average 318.9 mm); 210-612 g (average 301.5 g)
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: 305-511 mm TL (average 447.0 mm); 354-1,530 g (average 1,202.7 g)

**Spring Pool Monitoring:**
- Upper spring: Five adult female [[Desert Pupfish]] captured; one additional individual observed being consumed by crayfish
- Unexpected capture of one male [[Gila Topminnow]] in upper spring (not stocked there; barriers prevent upstream migration)
- Lower spring: Healthy [[Gila Topminnow]] population established
- One adult female [[Desert Pupfish]] in lower spring
- No [[Western Mosquitofish]] found above barrier between springs and main lake

**Non-native Species Concerns:**
- Two [[Yellow Bullhead]] captured with distended abdomens
- Stomach contents: crayfish; one individual gravid with eggs
- [[Western Mosquitofish]] abundant in main lake margins
- No [[Desert Pupfish]] or [[Gila Topminnow]] detected in main lake body

**Colorado Pikeminnow:**
- Not captured in trammel nets or seines
- Likely still too small for trammel nets but too large/mobile for seining in heavily vegetated habitat

**Management Continuity:**
- Francisco Abarca assumed Nongame Fish Biologist position
- Periodic monitoring to continue
- Future plans include stocking large fish from [[Palm Lake]] to wild [[Salt River]] and/or [[Verde River]] habitats
