# Lake Mohave Native Fish Work Group Agenda and Razorback Sucker 2008 Field Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This OCR-degraded set of documents combines three items: a Lake Mohave Native Fish Work Group meeting agenda (January 2009, Laughlin, NV), a Colorado River Aquatic Biologists (CRAB) meeting agenda (January 7-8, 2009), and a 2008 field and database summary of [[Razorback Sucker]] roundups at [[Lake Mohave]], prepared by Carol Pacey of the Native Fish Lab, Marsh & Associates, LLC. Many words are illegible, so several figures below are uncertain and are flagged as such. The material focuses on the monitoring, rearing, repatriation, and population estimation of the endangered Razorback Sucker in the Lower Colorado River system.

## Background & Description

**Meeting agendas.** The Native Fish Work Group agenda covers a review of 2008 activities, including [[Larval Collections]], [[Lakeside Rearing]], [[Adult Monitoring]], and [[Database Management]]. It also reviews the status of sites such as [[Willow Beach Hatchery]], [[Bubbling Ponds]], and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], and sets schedules and stocking plans for 2009.

The CRAB agenda (Laughlin, NV, January 7-8, 2009) lists presentations on Colorado River reaches from Mexico to [[Lake Havasu]], [[Lake Mead]], and [[Lake Powell]]. Topics include the [[Razorback Sucker Roundup]] on Lake Mohave, the history of [[Striped Bass]] management in the Colorado River, and the status of [[Humpback Chub]] in the Grand Canyon. Agency contributors include USGS, AZG&F, NVDOW, UDWR, and USFWS. The agenda also includes election of officers and new business.

**Field summary.** The 2008 Razorback Sucker report presents capture data from Lake Mohave roundups, including wild and repatriated fish. Repatriated fish were reared in off-site facilities or in lakeside backwaters, and many were marked with PIT (passive integrated transponder) tags.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species status:** The Razorback Sucker is a native catostomid of the Colorado River that has declined sharply. Repatriation of hatchery-reared fish and PIT-tag-based monitoring are used to assess recovery.
- **Habitat:** Lake Mohave and other Colorado River reservoirs serve as monitoring and repatriation sites. Lakeside backwaters are used for rearing and release.
- **Management pressures:** The agenda refers to [[Striped Bass]] management and non-native fish, which bear on native species survival. The CRAB agenda also refers to the confluence of the Little Colorado River (LCR) and non-native fish.
- **Methods:** Population estimates use mark-recapture methods, specifically the Chapman modification of the Petersen method (Ricker 1975 and Seber 1973 are cited).
