## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents a September 1991 riverside planning meeting among BIO/WEST researchers and collaborators (Valdez, Williams, Holden, Masslich, Yard, Ryel, Leibfried) to define the 1992 Work Plan for the [[Grand Canyon Humpback Chub]] studies. Rather than presenting novel morphological or ecological data, the document outlines methodological priorities for the ongoing multi-year investigation, including expanded spatial sampling near the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) confluence, refinement of adult and juvenile habitat quantification protocols, initiation of food habits studies using nonlethal stomach pump techniques, and decisions regarding radiotelemetry expansion into downstream reaches. The plan reflects an adaptive, data-driven management approach tied to [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and their ecosystem impacts.

## Background & Description

- **Size threshold for radiotransmitter implantation**: Humpback chub must reach approximately **550 g** body mass to accommodate an **11-g radiotransmitter** (~2% body weight ratio), a standard constraint in [[Radiotelemetry]] studies of riverine cyprinids.
- Of 44 unique humpback chub captured in Reaches 2 and 3 during the study's first year (Oct 1990–Sept 1991), only **3 individuals** met this size threshold, indicating a population skewed toward smaller/juvenile size classes in these downstream reaches.
- Juvenile and age-0 humpback chub were distinguished from adults primarily by capture method efficacy: electrofishing and minnow traps were effective for young fish, suggesting smaller body size and different habitat associations compared to radiotagged adults.
- No detailed external morphometric or meristic data are provided in this document; the focus is methodological rather than descriptive.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Adult habitat**: Radiotagged adult [[Humpback Chub]] in the mainstem Colorado River near the LCR confluence occupy identifiable macrohabitat types, with ongoing efforts to quantify **microhabitat parameters** (depth, velocity, substrate, cover) relative to flow regime changes driven by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations.
- **Juvenile habitat**: Age-0 and juvenile humpback chub (present May–September) were strongly associated with **talus slopes and rocky shorelines**, and notably absent from sand beaches and vertical rock walls — indicating substrate-specific habitat selection distinct from adults.
- **Spatial distribution**: Intensive sampling confirmed a core aggregation of humpback chub in **Study Reach 1** (Colorado River, Kwagunt Rapid RM 56 to Red Canyon RM 76.5), with evidence of significant numbers extending both upstream of Kwagunt Rapid and downstream past Lava-Chuar and Tanner Canyon.
- **Downstream distribution (Reaches 2 and 3, RM 76.5–226)**: Humpback chub occurrence was concentrated in four discrete areas — near [[Shinumo Creek]] (RM 108), RM 127, near [[Havasu Creek]] (RM 156), and near [[Pumpkin Springs]] (RM 213) — suggesting patchy, localized population structure outside the main LCR aggregation.
- **Trophic ecology**: A food habits study using nonlethal **stomach pump techniques** was planned to characterize diet and food availability relative to river flow, extending also to major piscivorous competitors/predators: [[Brown Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Striped Bass]].
- **Tributary use**: Tributary inflow zones (Bright Angel, Shinumo, Kanab, Tapeats, Havasu creeks) were identified as ecologically significant transition habitats, sampled up to the high-water line, in coordination with Arizona Game and Fish (AGF) and USFWS.
- **Abiotic drivers**: Turbidity was flagged as a key environmental covariate to be measured in coordination with GCES (M. Yard) and correlated with humpback chub telemetry/surveillance data.

## Key Findings

- **Sampling expansion**: Reach 1 sampling boundaries will be extended both upstream (above Kwagunt Rapid) and downstream (below Lava-Chuar/Tanner Canyon) without additional resource investment, by reallocating existing effort from the Kwagunt–60-Mile survey segment.
- **Radiotagging effort reduction**: Annual radiotagged adult sample size may be reduced from **60 to 45–50 fish**, based on preliminary data sufficiency, reducing handling stress on large individuals while preserving study power.
- **Juvenile ecology gap identified**: A dedicated juvenile habitat characterization protocol was drafted, targeting depth, velocity, substrate, and cover variables, plus downstream dispersal, survival, predation, and food resource questions for young humpback chub.
- **Nonlethal diet sampling methodology**: Stomach pump methodology was slated for validation on [[Roundtail Chub]] in the Dolores River (October 1991) prior to application on humpback chub in the Grand Canyon (target: November 1991–January 1992), reflecting a conservation-minded, minimally invasive approach.
- **Radiotelemetry expansion deferred**: Despite interest in extending radiotelemetry into Reaches 2 and 3, the group opted to first conduct detailed analysis of existing stratified random sampling data to avoid compromising the primary objective of estimating range-wide occurrence, distribution, and abundance (Red Canyon to Diamond Creek, RM 76.5–226).
- **Targeted-area sampling rejected (for now)**: Despite identification of four high-catch locations in Reaches 2/3, the group decided against shifting to a focused/targeted sampling design until the adequacy of the current stratified random approach could be statistically evaluated.
- **Interagency coordination**: Continued collaboration with AGF and USFWS on tributary sampling and mapping was emphasized as essential for a comprehensive Grand Canyon fish community picture.
- **Adaptive study design**: The memo exemplifies iterative, field-informed refinement of a long-term monitoring program directly tied to evaluating **Glen Canyon Dam operational impacts** on native fish ecology.
