# Establishing a Captive Broodstock for Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This genetics-focused paper evaluates the founder base of a [[Captive Broodstock]] for the federally endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). The hatchery stock descends from at most 10 wild parents collected from [[Lake Mohave]] in 1981. Using [[Allozyme Variation]], [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) data and production records, the authors estimate the [[Effective Number Of Founders]] at 3.48 to 8.48, far below the maximum of 10. They then model how many F1 parents and additional wild fish should be used to build a future broodstock. The paper recommends about 30 pairs of F1 parents and random, phenotype-blind mating, and it stresses that any surviving wild fish are genetically valuable.

## Background & Description

The bonytail chub is among the most imperiled North American freshwater fishes. Wild individuals are mostly large, old adults persisting in Lake Mohave and scattered in the upper Colorado River basin. Little recruitment has occurred since [[Davis Dam]] closed in 1954, which formed Lake Mohave, and non-native sport fishes became established. Habitat destruction, water development and non-native fishes drove the decline.

In 1981, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) produced an F1 cohort at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (WBNFH) from wild Lake Mohave adults. About 2,300 of these fish were later held as broodstock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH), and approximately 350 adults remained there. The USFWS plans to establish a future captive broodstock from these fish and any newly captured wild individuals. The stated goal is the most complete and representative genetic sample of the remaining species.

Wild-caught parents and tissue were not retained, and F1 fish were not marked. Genetic data from random F1 and F2 samples are therefore the only means of estimating the original parental contributions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat loss:** The species' decline is attributed to habitat degradation and alteration linked to water developments and the introduction of non-native fishes.
- **Recruitment failure:** Recruitment in the wild has been minimal since the 1954 closure of Davis Dam.
- **Predation and cannibalism:** Under pond conditions, adult bonytail chub were observed to prey on their own progeny. F2 fish were not enhanced, and survivors were exposed to predators in Lake Mohave.
- **Captive selection:** Selection in captivity may cause genetic change and maladaptation for survival or reproduction in the wild, particularly across consecutive hatchery generations and under unequal parental contribution.
- **Classification of wild-caught fish:** Three groups now occur in Lake Mohave: truly wild fish (hatched before stocking began in 1981), F1 fish released in 1981, and F2 fish released after 1981. These are difficult to tell apart.

## Key Findings

### 1. Original Parental Contributions (1981)
- Five adult males and six previously captured wild females were used. Female 1 produced no eggs.
- The five contributing females produced 5,850 to 37,700 eggs (mean 25,090).
- Fertilization proportions ranged from 0.91 to 0.98 (mean 0.95).
- Proportional contributions (p_i) ranged from 0.049 to 0.290 (mean 0.2). This is the "high" estimate, which represents the maximum retention of variation.

### 2. Allozyme Evidence
- 30 F1 fish from DNFH were examined at three polymorphic loci. Twenty-four F2 juveniles from the 1985 year class were examined at six loci (Minckley et al. 1989).
- At Pgm-A, allele a was significantly more frequent in F2 fish (p < 0.01), and a third allele c appeared only in F2 fish.
- The F1 sample showed a significant heterozygote excess at Pgm-A.
- Of 30 F1 fish, 22 carried one three-locus genotype, which is most likely the product of a single mating. Under Hardy-Weinberg assumptions, the authors estimate that one such mating is 3.32 times more likely than more than one.
- This evidence supports the "low" estimate, which assumes three effective matings with unequal contributions (p1 = 0.733, p2 = p3 = 0.1335).

### 3. mtDNA Evidence
- All 19 F1 fish analyzed for mtDNA carried haplotype a.
- Of 16 additional wild-caught carcasses, 7 (0.438) carried haplotype a and 9 (0.562) carried haplotype b. This shows substantial wild variation that was not captured or passed on.
- Simulations show that a monomorphic sample of 19 is unlikely under the high estimate (0.026 when haplotype frequency is 0.433) but more likely under the low estimate (0.107). The authors therefore conclude that the F1 ancestry was more likely to come from fewer matings and more unequal contributions.

### 4. Effective Number of Founders
- **Original broodstock:** N_e,f = 8.48 under the high estimate (84.8% of the maximum of 10). N_e,f = 3.48 under the low estimate.
- **Future broodstock with 30 pairs of F1 parents (60 individuals):** the ratio of N_e,f to the maximum is 89.3% (high) and 97.1% (low). Adding more than 30 pairs yields only small gains; 150 pairs gives 97.5% and 99.4%.
- **Heterozygosity retention:** Using about 30 pairs retains roughly 90% of the maximum variation, which is the basis for the recommendation.

### 5. Incorporating Wild Fish
- One additional wild fish raises N_e,f from 8.48 to 9.58 (+11.8%) under the high estimate, or from 3.48 to 4.48 (+28.7%) under the low estimate.
- Guideline: a wild fish's representation should be at least 1/(8.48 + N_w) (high) and no more than 1/(3.48 + N_w) (low), where N_w is the number of additional wild fish.
- A mating scheme illustrating one wild fish requires about nine F1 × wild matings to match another 1981 wild fish's expected contribution, or about 0.23 of total progeny.
- Marsh (1997) estimated that from 17 Lake Mohave fish of uncertain origin, "not less than 10% ... perhaps as many as two-thirds" were wild.

### 6. Stocking History (1981–1996)
- Stocking totaled 41,517 F1 fish (all marked with [[Oxytetracycline Marking]]) and 161,371 F2 fish (text figure; the summary table totals 202,699, a discrepancy to verify).
- F2 fish came from unmanaged "volunteer" spawning in holding ponds, with more than 50 adults in observed spawning bouts. Only a small percentage were marked (value garbled in the scan).
- Marking methods used across years included oxytetracycline, fin-clipping, passive integrated transponders (PIT), coded wire nose tags and sonic transmitters.

### 7. Recommendations
- Use about 30 pairs of F1 parents for the future broodstock.
- Choose parents at random and independent of phenotype, unless a genetic or congenital problem is identified, to prevent differential reproductive selection.
- Use wild fish while F1 stock is still available. Mate wild fish with F1 fish rather than wild × wild, unless more than about five wild fish are captured.
- Certify wild status by mating a candidate to five F1 fish and estimating age from otoliths before sacrificing it, so its genetic contribution is retained. If wild fish are few, recaptured F1 or F2 fish may be used, with F2 contributing about half as many genes as an F1 parent.
