# External Morphology of Adult and Copepodid Stages of Diaptomus clavipes

#copepoda #diaptomus #freshwater-zooplankton

## Executive Summary

This 1967 *University of Kansas Science Bulletin* paper by Kamate and [[Kenneth B. Armitage]] describes the external anatomy of all copepodid stages (CI–CVI) and the adults of [[Diaptomus clavipes]], a freshwater calanoid copepod collected from ponds at the University of Kansas. The species was chosen for study because it co-occurs with [[Diaptomus siciloides]]. The authors report stage-by-stage changes in body length, segmentation, and appendage armature, and they argue that divergence in the male [[Antennule]] and the [[Fifth Swimming Leg]] is the main morphological basis of speciation in the genus. Detailed quantitative comparison with other diaptomids was deliberately omitted.

## Background & Description

*Diaptomus* is a freshwater and saline calanoid genus with a worldwide distribution. The authors note 78 recognized species in North America, but few congeneric species co-occur in large numbers. Most such co-occurrences involve species placed in different subgenera. The genus is divided into 14 subgenera (Wilson, 1959), and European workers often treat many of these as separate genera, so reported co-occurrence rates depend on the taxonomic system used.

Development proceeds through five or six [[Naupliar Stages]] and six [[Copepodid Stages]], the last being the adult. Before this study, the most extensive description of all post-embryonic stages was that of *D. siciloides* by [[Comita and Tommerdahl]] (1960). Specimens were collected from local ponds, preserved in 10% formalin, dissected with microneedles under a binocular microscope, and mounted in glycerine or Turtox C. M. C-S non-resinous mountant. Drawings were traced from projected images using a B & L VH microprojector. Lengths were measured with an ocular micrometer, from the rostrum to the distal tips of the caudal rami, excluding caudal setae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*D. clavipes* and *D. siciloides* occurred together in the same local ponds. The authors note that their adult and larval structures are strikingly similar, but they differ in size and in the structure of the male right [[Antennule]] and the [[Fifth Swimming Leg]]. The female left antennule and the antennae, mandibles, maxillulae, maxillae, maxillipeds, and swimming legs 1–4 are similar in both species.

Co-occurring species may still occupy different niches ([[Pennak]], 1957). [[Hutchinson]] (1951) proposed that size differences between congeneric copepods may reduce competition through differences in food selection. The paper cites [[Fryer]] (1954) as an example: the gut of *Arctodiaptomus laticeps* (1.54–1.65 mm) contained *Melosira*, while *Eudiaptomus gracilis* (1.14–1.23 mm) contained minute algae and tripton. Physiological differences at the species level in copepods are poorly studied. The authors infer them from differences in size, breeding cycle, and food preference, and they treat the morphological differences as reinforcing evidence.

## Key Findings

**Body size and segmentation by stage** (lengths from rostrum to caudal ramus tips):

| Stage | Body length | Body segments | Abdominal segments |
|---|---|---|---|
| CI | ~0.46 mm | 6 | 1 |
| CII | ~0.58 mm | 7 | — |
| CIII | 1.04–1.08 mm | 8 | 2 |
| CIV | ♂ ~1.21 mm; ♀ ~1.40 mm | 9 | 3 |
| CV | ♂ 1.56–1.64 mm; ♀ ~1.76 mm | 10 | ♀ 3; ♂ 4 |
| CVI (adult) | ♂ 1.28–2.2 mm; ♀ 1.57–2.5 mm | — | ♂ 5; ♀ 3 |

Sexual dimorphism appears by CIV, when males and females differ in size and fifth-leg structure.

**[[Antennule]] development:** Apparent segment counts rise from 10 in CI to 18 in CII, 23 in CIII, and 25 in CIV and later. The authors infer the fused segments by tracking setae and aesthetae into later instars. In CV, spines appear on segments 17–20 of the male right antennule. The adult male right antennule is geniculate between segments 18 and 19.

**[[Antenna]]:** The exopod has nine segments and 12 setae in all stages. The terminal endopod segment has 11 setae in CI–CIII, 14 in CIV, and 15 in CV–CVI.

**[[Mandible]]:** The masticatory process has 11 teeth in CI, reduced to 8 in all later stages. The distal endopod segment has 6 setae in CI–CV and 8 in CVI.

**[[Maxilla]] and [[Maxilliped]]:** The maxilla has about 18 endites in CI, 23 in CII, and reaches its definitive form with 24 endites in CIII. The maxilliped attains its definitive form with its full complement of segments and setae in CIV.

**Swimming legs 1–4:** Leg 1 develops its definitive form in CV, and leg 4 is bilobed in CII. Legs 2–4 gain segments progressively, with the exopod and endopod becoming two- or three-segmented by CV. The terminal exopod seta of leg 3 is stout and serrated along its length.

**[[Fifth Swimming Leg]]:** The female CVI leg has a triangular, pointed terminal exopod segment with a bladelike, finely toothed medial margin. The adult male fifth legs are asymmetrical. The right exopod is elongated and bears a large sickle-shaped terminal spine. The left endopod is elongated and bears three small subterminal spines.

**Comparison with *D. siciloides*:** The males differ in antennule armature on segments 8, 15, 16, 17, 18, 22 and 23. The female left antennules are similar except for size and the number of setae on segment 2. The authors conclude that morphological speciation mainly involves divergence in the male antennule and fifth legs. In CV, the female exopod of *D. clavipes* bears one large, thick spine and three smaller spines of similar size. In *Mixodiaptomus laciniatus* the three smaller spines vary more in size, and *Eudiaptomus vulgaris* has only two clearly evident spines. The authors propose that these structural differences may reduce or prevent interbreeding among species that share habitat, a condition they describe as [[Sympatry]].

**Limitations:** The authors state that detailed comparison was not attempted because of the tabulation required and insufficient comparative data. Some segment assignments for the antennule are inferred rather than directly observed.
