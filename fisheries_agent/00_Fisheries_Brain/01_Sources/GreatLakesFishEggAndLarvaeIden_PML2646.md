# Great Lakes Fish Egg and Larvae Identification Workshop Proceedings

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-larvae

## Executive Summary

This document is the front matter and reference body of the proceedings of a workshop on the identification of Great Lakes fish eggs and larvae, edited by John Bonnell, sponsored by the [[National Power Plant Team]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and published in Ann Arbor, Michigan, in 1976. Its core product is a species-by-species table (Table 1) that cross-references published egg and larval descriptions for roughly 150 fish species, with a family-level key to yolk-sac and larval characteristics and an extensive bibliography of about 200 numbered references.

## Background & Description

The workshop addressed a practical problem: identifying fish eggs and early life stages collected in plankton and impingement surveys, particularly around power plant intakes and in Great Lakes waters. The proceedings compile literature sources so that investigators can match unknown eggs and larvae to known descriptions. The text is largely a reference listing, and the OCR scan contains substantial table debris that is not reproduced here.

Key structural elements include:
- **Table 1**: Species-by-species citations of egg and larval descriptions, spanning the Petromyzontiformes through the Cottidae.
- **Family characteristics key** (by [[Alice J. Lippson]]): diagnostic traits for yolk-sac and free-swimming larvae of [[Petromyzontidae]] (lampreys), [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons), [[Lepisosteidae]] (gars), [[Amiidae]] (bowfins), [[Anguillidae]] (eels), [[Clupeidae]] (herrings), [[Salmonidae]] (trouts and salmon), [[Osmeridae]] (smelts), [[Umbridae]] (mudminnows), [[Esocidae]] (pikes), [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows and carps), [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), [[Ictaluridae]] (catfishes), [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes), [[Percidae]] (perches) and others.
- **Reference list**: general works (e.g., Hubbs 1943 terminology of early stages; Balon 1975 developmental intervals) and species-specific sources, including taxonomic keys for the Great Lakes and Lake Erie.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species covered span anadromous, catadromous, freshwater and estuarine taxa of the Great Lakes basin and adjacent rivers, reservoirs and the Chesapeake Bay. Cited references include studies of spawning habitat, nest construction, fecundity and early-life ecology (for example, sea lamprey larval stages, lake sturgeon spawning in the Nottaway and Moose River systems, and walleye and sauger spawning in Lake Winnebago and Lewis and Clark Lake). Identification is complicated by overlapping characters among related species (for example, alewife versus blueback herring, walleye versus sauger, and white versus black crappie), which is why the proceedings emphasize comparative keys and electrophoretic or pigment-based separation methods.

## Key Findings

- The compilation lists egg and larval literature for about 150 species, organized by family, with the dominant sources for each group identified in Table 1.
- Family-level larval characters are provided, such as the presence of a mound-like adhesive organ in gars, the distinct two-chambered gas bladder in cyprinids and suckers, and the posterior vent position in salmonids.
- Several species are noted as having little or no published egg or larval description (for example, entries marked "Not seen" or with only spawning or fecundity data), highlighting identification gaps.
- Key methods for separating similar taxa include myomere and pyloric caeca counts, scale formation patterns, pigment patterns, and electrophoresis of muscle proteins (e.g., striped bass versus white perch).
- Standard developmental terminology follows Hubbs (1943), dividing early life into embryo, prolarva, postlarva, larva, juvenile and young-of-year stages.
