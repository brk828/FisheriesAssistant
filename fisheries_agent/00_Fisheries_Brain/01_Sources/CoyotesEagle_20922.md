# Mixed Archive: Hantavirus Reservoir Research, Historical Wildlife Diaries, and Ecological Coursework

#hantavirus #wildlife-biology #herpetology

## Executive Summary

This scanned, OCR-degraded archive combines several unrelated items rather than a single study. The main components are: a methods paper on identifying [[Hantavirus]] rodent reservoirs; a 1995 national [[Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome]] case update; a student's statistics and probability exercise set; a [[Chuckwalla]] literature list; a late-nineteenth-century Arizona hunting and natural history diary; a course syllabus; cardiac physiology essay answers; a résumé and a wage-dispute letter; and a hydrology figure from the [[El Coronado Ranch]]. Fish appear only sporadically (trout, carp, catfish, salmon, and fish stocking notes). No ichthyological study is present.

## Background & Description

- **Hantavirus:** Enveloped, negative-strand RNA viruses in the genus Hantavirus of the family [[Bunyaviridae]]. They carry three RNA segments (S, M, L). The first recognized form was Korean hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome, recorded in 1950. The paper cites at least twelve distinct strains, each generally carried by a specific rodent species in the family [[Muridae]]. Transmission occurs through aerosolized rodent excreta. Two diseases are attributed to the virus: hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome (HFRS) and [[Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome]] (HPS).
- **Reservoir-identification paper (abstract):** The authors describe a four-step process: (1) define the likely transmission area and trap rodents there; (2) collect blood and organs; (3) prepare and test organ samples for seropositivity, for example by hantavirus AG-ELISA; (4) accept or reject each specimen by comparing its genome, specifically the R and S segments, with the patient's strain. A match identifies the rodent as the vector. A mismatch means the transmission location must be reanalyzed.
- **Chuckwalla literature list:** About twenty references on *Sauromalus obesus*, covering thermoregulation, social behavior, body size, karyotypes, mitochondrial DNA phylogeography (with [[Dipsosaurus dorsalis]]), and the lizard mite [[Hirstiella pyriformis]]. Key entries include Abts (1987, *Ecological Monographs* 57(3): 215–232), Berry (1974, *Univ. California Publ. Zool.* 101: 1–60), and Lease (1976b, *J. Herpetol.* 10(2): 85–95).
- **Course syllabus (BIO 419, Research Colloquium in Biology and Society):** A 3-credit weekly seminar. Grading uses three 50-point essays (due 24 February, 7 April, and 8 May), plus weekly unscored writing and participation. Instructor: Dr. Ron Rutowski. Readings include McCain and Segal, Cromer, and Collins and Pinch. Seminar topics include funding of science, sociobiology, and the Human Genome Project.
- **Cardiac physiology essays (Chapter 9):** Answers cover heart valves, the three heart-wall layers, intercalated disks and gap junctions, the [[Sinoatrial Node]] pacemaker, and the [[Cardiac Conduction System]] (AV node, bundle of His, Purkinje fibers).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historical wildlife diary:** Undated and OCR-garbled entries (apparently late nineteenth century) record hunting, trapping, and sightings across southeastern Arizona. Species include deer, quail, ducks and geese, mountain lions, bears, bighorn sheep, antelope, javelina, wild turkey, rattlesnakes, and scorpions. Fish entries mention carp, catfish, salmon, and trout, including fish culture and stocking, and a Colorado River catch of "some 50 lbs." One entry notes that carp were being introduced to the local water supply.
- **Hydrology figure (El Coronado Ranch, Chiricahua Mountains, Arizona, 1997):** Major-ion concentrations were regressed against discharge (cfs) at 12 sub-watershed sites, with N = 118 per regression. Reported R² values are very low: Na 0.087, Ca 0.061, Mg 0.096, K 0.002.
- **[[Biosphere 2]] (from the résumé):** Water-chemistry work included the Winkler titration method, total dissolved carbon by coulometer, and pH analysis. A study tested the "Proton Hypothesis" (calcium concentration versus photosynthesis rate in calcareous algae).

## Key Findings

**1. CDC hantavirus update (PROMED, 1 June 1995)**
- 110 confirmed cases of HPS in 23 states.
- Gender: 48 female (43.6%).
- Race: 72 White (65.5%), 35 Native American (31.8%), 3 Black (2.7%).
- Ethnicity: 11 Hispanic (10.0%).
- Case fatality rate: 50.9% (56 deaths).
- Age: mean 35.2 years, range 11–69.
- CDC committed to monthly updates.

**2. Reservoir-identification method**
- Rodent vectors are found by matching viral genome segments to the patient's strain, with specimens accepted or rejected on that basis.
- The authors argue that the "true number and disease potentials" of hantaviruses is "doubtlessly much higher than currently appreciated."

**3. Statistics exercises (student work)**
- *Tree spacing (quadrat data):* mean 1.200000, variance 0.989899, interpreted as random spacing.
- *Expected vs. observed tree counts per quadrat (N = 100):* 0 trees, 30 expected vs. 27 observed; 1 tree, 36 vs. 39; 2 trees, 22 vs. 22; 3 trees, 9 vs. 11; 4 trees, 2 expected (observed value not legible). The author concludes the distribution is random.
- *Tiger beetles (Southern vs. Northern North American distributions):* N = 76 cases, mean 3.289, SD 3.032, skewness 1.174, kurtosis 1.217.
- *Trout length:* captured trout measured 23.21 cm, computed as 20.5 + 5.75 × 0.4721. The stated probability of a trout ≤ 8.425 cm is .0179, but the z-score calculation (z = −2.1) gives an area of .4821 from the mean. These two figures do not agree, and the source does not resolve the discrepancy.
- A proposed field study would test whether calling male anurans in a pond are randomly, regularly, or clumped in their spatial arrangement.

**4. Course and career records**
- The BIO 419 seminar series included speakers on the Endangered Species Act, daily lives of scientists, and the Human Genome Project.
- The résumé lists a B.S. in Biology (GPA 3.34) from [[Arizona State University]], graduation May 1998 (also given as December 1997), and a NASA-funded Biosphere 2 internship.
- An October 12, 1997 letter from a former employee demanded a final paycheck for 16.5 hours at $6.50/hour, plus a missing deduction voucher, and threatened a labor-board complaint.

**5. Cardiac physiology (essay answers)**
- The SA node fires 70–80 action potentials per minute, faster than other pacemaker tissue, so it sets the heart rhythm.
- The AV node is the only route for impulses between atria and ventricles, which produces an AV nodal delay that ensures atria contract before ventricles.
