# Ice Fishing Creel Census on a Heavily Fished Trout and Salmon Lake: Branch Lake, Maine (1951-52)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Game report, prepared by [[W. L. Minckley]], [[Stuart E. DeRoche]] and [[Lyndon H. Bond]] (dated June 18, 1952), evaluates whether [[Ice Fishing]] harms trout and salmon populations. The study ran as the [[Dingell-Johnson Project]] F-7-R-1 on [[Branch Lake]], Ellsworth, Maine, over the 1951 and 1952 seasons. Using an intensive [[Ice Fishing Creel Census]] and a less intensive [[Open Water Creel Census]], the authors found that the 1951-52 ice fishing seasons "did not seriously deplete the existing fish populations." They recommended that the department be consulted before any lake or pond is closed to ice fishing.

## Background & Description

**Problem.** Sportsmen disagreed about whether ice fishing can "fish out" a lake. Ponds had been opened and closed in public hearings without quantitative data.

**Study site.** [[Branch Lake]] covers 2,703 acres and is typical of general oligotrophic Maine lakes. Its proximity to large population centers was expected to produce heavy fishing pressure.

**Species present:**
- [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*)
- [[Landlocked Salmon]] (*Salmo salar sebago*)
- [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*)
- [[Eastern Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*)
- [[Chain Pickerel]] (*Esox niger*), present but not intensively fished

**Regulations.** Anglers were permitted 5 lines per person in winter and 2 lines per person in open water.

**Methods.**
- Ice census: one or more biologists were present on the lake every day of the season. Anglers were contacted across the entire lake, including from all landings and camps. Catches were weighed, measured and scale-sampled at the end of each day, and lines fished and departure time were recorded on a log sheet per fishing party.
- Open water census: observers worked from only one landing ([[Hanson's Landing]]), checking anglers entering and leaving it. Cottage and other landings were not checked. Sampling stopped once the same number of fish had been counted as in the entire ice season (reached after 8 days).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Branch Lake is an oligotrophic lake supporting a salmonid fishery under heavy angler pressure, with chain pickerel as a secondary, lightly fished species.
- Small [[Eastern Brook Trout]] were taken in shallow shoreline waters and at stream outlets during 1952. Anglers targeting them increased, and many small fish entered the catch.
- Age and growth analysis (total length and scale analysis) showed no significant difference in size or age classes of [[Lake Trout]] between the winter and spring/open-water catches.
- The report contains no data on water chemistry, temperature, invertebrates, plankton or habitat beyond ice thickness and weather fields on the log sheet.

## Key Findings

**1951 season (ice fishing, February 1 to March 21, 1951)**
- 298 anglers fished 118 lines for 1,312.25 line hours (OCR reads "7312.25"; the 1952 summary totals confirm 1,312.25).
- 28 fish were caught; the species breakdown lists 1 eastern brook trout, 1 brown trout and 1 chain pickerel.
- Reported catch per unit of effort: 0.00.
- Total catch weight: 100.5 lb; average 3.75 lb per fish; 0.0372 lb per acre (OCR reads "0.9372," corrected using 100.5 lb / 2,703 acres).

**1951 open water (April-May, 8 days)**
- 163 anglers fished 175 lines for 120.75 line hours and caught 29 fish.
- Species: [[Lake Trout]] 18, [[Brown Trout]] 1, [[Landlocked Salmon]] 10.
- Catch per unit of effort: 0.025 (OCR reads "0.025)").
- Total weight: 98.4 lb; average 3.39 lb per fish.

**1952 season (ice fishing, February 1 to March 27, 1952)**
- 267 anglers fished 112 lines for 218.50 line hours (OCR reads "1120" and "7218.50") and caught 98 fish.
- Species: [[Lake Trout]] 14, [[Eastern Brook Trout]] 78, [[Brown Trout]] 1, [[Chain Pickerel]] 5.
- Catch per unit of effort: 0.0136.
- Total weight: 71.8 lb; average 0.732 lb per fish; 0.0266 lb per acre.
- Cause of decline in average weight: anglers fishing shallow shoreline waters and stream outlets caught many small [[Eastern Brook Trout]], which lowered the average fish weight relative to 1951.

**1952 open water (April 19 to May 11, 8 days)**
- 291 anglers fished 308 lines for 1,947.50 line hours and caught 51 fish.
- Species counts were lost to OCR; species present were [[Lake Trout]], [[Eastern Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Chain Pickerel]] and [[Landlocked Salmon]].
- Total weight: 155.6 lb; average 3.05 lb per fish.
- Catch per unit of effort: 0.0258.

**First-week comparison (ice vs. open water)**

- No [[Landlocked Salmon]] were checked during either winter season.

**Combined two-year totals (Table 7 and Summary)**
- Winter (ice): 126 fish, 565 anglers, 1,530.75 line hours, CPUE 0.0087, total 172.3 lb, average 1.37 lb per fish.
- Open water: 80 fish, 454 anglers (reported as "4S"; 163 + 291 = 454), CPUE 0.0257, average 3.17 lb per fish.
- Open water total weight is printed as "251;" lb. The per-year weights (98.4 + 155.6) sum to 254.0 lb, which matches the stated 3.17 lb average (254.0 / 80 = 3.175).
- Reported open water effort (3,115.25 line hours) does not reconcile with the per-year figures (120.75 + 1,947.50 = 2,068.25). Treat with caution.

**Conclusions**
- The 1951-52 [[Ice Fishing]] seasons "did not seriously deplete the existing fish populations" at [[Branch Lake]].
- The [[Creel Census]] would continue under project F-8-R-1, "Lake and Stream Investigation."

**Recommendations**
- The [[Maine Department Of Inland Fisheries And Game]] should be consulted before any lake or pond is closed to ice fishing.

**Data quality notes**

- The 1951 species list (1 + 1 + 1 = 3) does not add up to the stated 28 fish. The source does not reconcile this.
