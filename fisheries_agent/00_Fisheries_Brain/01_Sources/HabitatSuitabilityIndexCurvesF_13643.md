# Habitat Suitability Index Curves for Humpback Chub of the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1990 paper by [[Valdez Et Al.]] (BIO/WEST, Inc.) presents habitat suitability index (SI) curves for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the Upper [[Colorado River]] Basin. The curves were built from previously collected microhabitat data (1964–1986, 17 principal investigators, nearly 100 investigations) and refined through consensus by a panel of [[Species Experts]] at two week-long workshops. The product is a set of 14 riverine SI curves covering four life stages and three microhabitat parameters (water depth, velocity, substrate). The experts classed these as "interim SI curves" and recommended using only the adult curve set until more data are gathered.

## Background & Description

The [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM) quantifies fish habitat as a function of flow. Its biological component is the SI curve, which is integrated with hydraulic data in the [[Physical Habitat Simulation]] (PHABSIM) model to compute weighted usable area (WUA) by species, life stage and reach. Microhabitat data for swift, deep, turbid rivers are costly and difficult to collect, which made assimilation of existing data necessary.

The four categories of SI criteria, defined by [[Armour et Al.]] (1984) and [[Bovee]] (1986), are:
- **Category I:** based on non-field information (literature, professional judgment).
- **Category II:** frequency analysis of used conditions ("utilization functions").
- **Category III:** corrects Category II for habitat availability.
- **Category IV:** conditioned curves for specific conditions such as cover or season.

Humpback chub curves relied mainly on Category I and II criteria. Earlier curves were produced by [[Holden]] (1977) and [[Valdez and Nilson]] (1982) and were incorporated. Humpback chub is one of four endemic mainstem fishes of the basin threatened with extinction, alongside the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail]] (insufficient data for curves) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (candidate species). The Colorado squawfish, humpback chub and razorback sucker were the focus of curve development, which the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) initiated in 1984 under the Endangered Species Act.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Turbidity and flow:** High turbidity, severe turbulence and swiftness of occupied habitats make precise microhabitat measurement difficult.
- **Cover:** Traditional overhead cover is not typical here. The experts identified turbidity (concealment), lateral structure (vertical rock walls, talus), and instream structure (rocks, sand bars, rock jetties) as likely important, and recommended that cover be recorded in future studies.
- **Water temperature:** Excluded as a microhabitat parameter because it varies seasonally and does not offer a full range of choices at any given time.
- **Habitat constraint:** Curves were not to be used in current PHABSIM analyses where data came largely from habitats the model cannot simulate (backwaters, concavities, isolated pools, embayments, gravel pits, eddies).
- **Sympatric species:** Humpback chub larvae and young-of-the-year are hard to distinguish from [[Bonytail]] and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*).

## Key Findings

**Methods and workflow**
- Database assimilated by FWS and BIO/WEST: study narratives for nearly 100 investigations were sent to experts.
- Workshop 1 had 17 attendees (3 organizers, 5 species experts, 9 participants). Workshop 2 had 15 (3 organizers, 6 experts, 6 participants). Only experts held decision-making authority; consensus was four of five (Workshop 1) or four of six (Workshop 2).
- Nine partition criteria were applied via dendrograms: life stage, river, strata, year, gear, time of year, time of day, sample design, habitat parameters.
- Statistical tests: Kolmogorov-Smirnov for depth and velocity; Chi-square for substrate and habitat (P < 0.05). Curves were fitted with exponential polynomial, exponential density, or generalized Poisson models.
- Category I curves were to be built by a [[Delphi Technique]], but experts did not submit them, stating they lacked the data or knowledge to judge habitat needs.

**Results: 14 SI curves (four life stages)**

| Life stage | Length | Confidence | Key habitat | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Larvae | <21 mm | D | Curves borrowed from larval Colorado squawfish; depth and velocity only | Not recommended (89% of data from backwaters) |
| Young-of-the-year | 21–74 mm | C | Silt and sand, backwaters and eddies; mean depth 2.1 ft | Not recommended (49% backwaters) |
| Juvenile (Green River) | 75–259 mm | C | Silt and sand, backwaters, eddies, runs; mean depth 2.3 ft, velocity 0.6 fps | Restricted (29% backwaters) |
| Juvenile (Colorado/Yampa) | 75–259 mm | C | Boulders and bedrock with some sand and silt; mean depth 11.1 ft | Applicable July–September |
| Adult | ≥260 mm | B | Boulders and bedrock with silt and sand; mean depth 10.3 ft, velocity 0.6 fps | Recommended; applicable March–November |

**Key data points from Table 1**
- Larvae: 1,498 depth and 1,512 velocity observations; mean depth 1.4 ft; mean velocity under 0.1 fps.
- Adults: 286 depth observations (range 2.5–40.1 ft), 274 velocity observations (range 0.0–3.9 fps).

**Adult curve set details**
- Confidence rating B because data came from mixed gear types (electrofishing, nets, angling, radiotelemetry).
- Depth curve was modified to favour all depths above about 7.2 ft, because sampling gear is less effective in shallow water.
- Gear limits: electrofishing effective to about 10 ft, radiotelemetry reliable only to about 12 ft.
- No spawning curve, as spawning behaviour was not well known.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- The curves are "interim" and the experts recommend refining them as more data become available.
- Only the adult curve set is presently recommended for use.
- The pre-existing data assimilation and expert-panel method is recommended for similar projects.
- Open workshops gave equal opportunity for expert input.
- Most experts and participants felt a third workshop should have been held to address PHABSIM use, WUA generation, and the biological soundness of streamflow-to-habitat relationships.
- Further research is needed on juvenile and larval habitat, and cover should be recorded as a microhabitat parameter.
