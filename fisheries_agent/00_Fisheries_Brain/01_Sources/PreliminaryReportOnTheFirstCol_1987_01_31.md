# Preliminary Report: First Collecting Trip to Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This preliminary field report documents a fish collection survey conducted by Jim Starr and Mike Perkinson on 31 January – 1 February 1987 at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], Arizona. The survey targeted two federally significant Colorado River Basin native fishes maintained as refugia populations: the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). Sampling occurred across four discrete water bodies — [[Mormon Lake]], [[Big Bertha Tank]], [[Road Camp Tank]], and [[Kings Tank]] — using bag seines, trammel nets, and gill nets. Results indicate measurable growth in both length and weight for both species relative to prior baseline data, suggesting the refuge tanks are functioning as viable growth habitat for these imperiled species.

## Background & Description

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bag seine (3 hauls) | 6 | 116.0 | 106.2 | 16.3 |
| Trammel net | 10 | 196.6 | 181.3 | 93.7 |
| **Combined** | **16** | **166.4** | **153.1** | **64.7** |

- Individual size range (seine): TL 106–126 mm; Wt 12–21 g
- Individual size range (trammel net): TL 181–222 mm; Wt 78–126 g
- Larger individuals were disproportionately captured via trammel net, consistent with gear selectivity for larger-bodied fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both target species are native, large-river fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]] now largely extirpated from their historic riverine range; the tanks at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] serve as **off-channel refugia** for conservation propagation and monitoring.
- Multiple isolated, human-constructed tanks (impoundments) within the refuge — [[Mormon Lake]], [[Big Bertha Tank]], [[Road Camp Tank]], [[Kings Tank]] — each support distinct size-structured populations of *Gila elegans*, indicating variable growth/survival conditions across sites.
- Sampling gear (bag seine, trammel net, gill net) was matched to habitat and fish size class; larger razorback suckers and bonytail were preferentially captured by trammel/gill nets, while seines captured a broader size range including smaller/juvenile fish.
- Presence of small (<75 mm TL) *Gila elegans* in Big Bertha Tank suggests possible in-tank recruitment or recent stocking of juveniles, relevant to assessing self-sustaining refuge population potential.

## Key Findings
- **Razorback Sucker growth**: Average weight increased from a prior baseline of 23.9 g to 64.7 g; average total length increased from 140 mm to 166.4 mm, indicating substantial somatic growth in the [[Mormon Lake]] population between sampling periods.
- **Bonytail Chub growth**: Average total length increased from 76 mm to 105.3 mm, and average weight increased from 6.2 g to 10.0 g, across the surveyed tank populations.
- A total of **152 *Gila elegans*** and **16 *Xyrauchen texanus*** were captured and measured across the four sites during this trip.
- Qualitative observations (not fully quantified in this report) were also recorded regarding capture methodology efficacy, fish spatial distribution within tanks, and overall tank habitat condition — flagged for further investigation in subsequent trips.
- This report establishes baseline morphometric data (TL, FL, weight) for future comparative growth-rate monitoring of these two endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fishes within refuge habitats.
