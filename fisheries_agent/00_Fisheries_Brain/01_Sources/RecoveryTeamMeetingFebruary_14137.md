# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Memorandum (January 1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director announces a [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting scheduled for February 23-24, 1989, in Phoenix, Arizona. The meeting's purpose is to resolve outstanding technical issues concerning recovery plans for three endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] fish species: [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). The document coordinates logistics for state and federal agency representatives involved in species recovery planning.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] comprises representatives from multiple state wildlife agencies (Arizona, California, Utah, New Mexico, Colorado) and federal agencies (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, National Park Service). The team is responsible for developing and reviewing recovery plans for endangered native fish species in the [[Colorado River Basin]].

The three focal species are all members of the family [[Cyprinidae]]:
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)

These species are characterized as "big river fishes" adapted to large riverine habitats in the Colorado River system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery planning process addresses conservation of native fish species in the [[Colorado River Basin]], encompassing both upper and lower basin regions. The [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] is specifically mentioned as the focus of a concurrent meeting on "big river fishes" recovery organized by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

The recovery planning effort involves coordination across multiple jurisdictions and agencies, reflecting the interstate nature of the [[Colorado River]] system and the wide historical range of these endangered species. The meeting location in Phoenix, Arizona, positions participants centrally within the species' range.
