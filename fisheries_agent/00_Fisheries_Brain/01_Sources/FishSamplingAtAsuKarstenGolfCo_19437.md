# ASU Karsten Golf Course Fish Survey, September 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

Arizona State University personnel sampled the two lakes (North Lake and South Lake) of the [[Arizona State University Karsten Golf Course]] in Tempe, Arizona, in late September 1989. The survey assessed the status of three imperiled native fishes stocked in 1988: [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Bonytail appeared to be doing well. Colorado squawfish were captured in very low numbers, and no razorback suckers were collected alive. Twelve fish species now occupy the water features, most of them non-native fishes that likely entered through [[Salt River Project Canals]]. Excessive algae and aquatic vegetation prompted a permit for up to 125 [[Triploid Grass Carp]], although supply problems had delayed stocking.

## Background & Description

The lakes were initially devoid of fishes. Aquatic biota could reach them by three routes: inflowing [[Salt River Project Canals]], unauthorized or authorized stockings, and terrestrial or aerial colonization by animals such as the [[Bullfrog]] and winged insects. The first samples, taken 2–3 September 1988, already contained non-native [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]], along with invertebrates and several macrophyte species.

Native fishes were stocked through cooperation among ASUGC, Arizona State University, Resort Management of America, Sun Angel Foundation, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. Stocking records:

- **Colorado Squawfish:** about 1,500 juveniles into North Lake, 7 September 1988
- **Bonytail:** 1,500 young into South Lake, 13 October 1988
- **Razorback Sucker:** 2,000 juveniles into South Lake, 24 October 1988

The stocked fishes were intended as research material for studies of growth, diet, and interactions with non-native fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.** Trammel and experimental gill nets captured larger fish. Nets were set for under two hours to limit stress, and large fish were filmed with a videocamera before release. Seines (6.0 and 6.1 × 1.2 m, 6.35 mm mesh) collected smaller individuals and covered essentially the full perimeter of each lake: five sectors in South Lake and four in North Lake. All seined fish were preserved in formalin, and voucher specimens were deposited in the ASU Museum.

**Vegetation and habitat.** Floating algae, emergent [[Cattail]], and submergent macrophytes (pondweeds and spiny naiad) were notably denser in North Lake than in South Lake. The authors attributed the difference in fish size structure partly to this cover. Cattail was described as difficult to control and capable of becoming occlusive within one or two growing seasons, so the authors recommended immediate removal if it was deemed undesirable.

**Health observations.** Bonytail appeared healthy, and no unusual infestations by the parasitic copepod *Lernaea cyprinacea* were observed.

**Growth.** Bonytail, Colorado squawfish, and razorback sucker grew faster at ASUGC than in other artificial and semi-natural habitats where they had been stocked.

## Key Findings

### Entanglement Net Catch
- [[Bonytail]] were the only fish captured in quantity by entanglement nets, with a mean total length of 22.7 cm (range 13.3–65.1 cm). Weights were not recorded. The sample size for this length summary is garbled in the scanned text.
- A single [[Colorado Squawfish]] (31.5 cm TL) was captured. Other individuals became disengaged while nets were lifted. The authors described capture success as unexplainably low in similar habitats.
- No [[Razorback Sucker]] were collected alive. Two moribund individuals were taken from South Lake, which indicates the species persists. Previous sampling at this site and at ASU Research Park had yielded substantial numbers, so the absence was described as surprising and disappointing. Additional sampling was planned for autumn.

### Seine Catch
- More than 3,500 fish were collected in 98 seine hauls.
- **South Lake:** 2,194 individuals in 49 hauls (catch per unit effort of 55 fish/haul). The catch was dominated by [[Mosquitofish]] (70%), [[Threadfin Shad]] (15%), and [[Red Shiner]] (14%). [[Carp]], [[Sonora Sucker]], and [[Yellow Bass]] were each represented by only one individual. Three [[Bonytail]] (17.6, 22.2, and 30.6 cm TL; 45, 92, and 235 g) were seined. Twenty-one [[Green Sunfish]] were also taken.
- **North Lake:** 1,475 individuals in 48 hauls (about 31 fish/haul). Dominant species were [[Red Shiner]] (46%), [[Green Sunfish]] (32%), [[Mosquitofish]] (14%), and [[Threadfin Shad]] (7%). Carp and bonytail were represented by 11 and 2 specimens, respectively. The two bonytail measured 7.4 and 9.0 cm TL and weighed 3.8 and 5.9 g.
- South Lake fish were mostly large adults, whereas North Lake fish were mostly small juveniles, which the authors linked to denser cover in North Lake.

### Species Inventory
- Table 2 lists 12 fish species occupying the water features. Three are native and were intentionally introduced: [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The native [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] are believed to have arrived via canals.
- The remaining species, including [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Yellow Bass]], and [[Green Sunfish]], are believed to have been passive invaders from [[Salt River Project Canals]].

### Management Conclusions
- **Bonytail:** The stocked population appeared to be doing well, with 31 fish ranging from 7.8 to 35.1 cm TL captured with modest effort.
- **Colorado Squawfish:** Capture success was low, but at least some and perhaps many individuals were believed to survive.
- **Razorback Sucker:** Status was unknown beyond the two moribund individuals, and further sampling was planned.
- **Aquatic vegetation:** Excessive algae and plant growth occurred in both lakes. The Arizona Game and Fish Department granted a permit for up to 125 triploid [[Triploid Grass Carp]]. Supply problems had delayed stocking. Any effects on native fishes were unknown and would be monitored if the carp were introduced.
- **Cattail:** Immediate treatment for removal was recommended if the plant was considered undesirable.
