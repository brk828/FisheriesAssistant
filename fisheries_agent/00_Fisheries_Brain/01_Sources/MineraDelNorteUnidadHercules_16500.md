# Minera del Norte, Unidad Hércules: Corporate Induction Booklet

#ichthyology #marine-biology #industrial-mining

## Executive Summary

This is an institutional induction booklet for [[Minera Del Norte]], S.A. de C.V., Unidad Hércules, an open-pit [[Iron Ore Mining]] operation in the desert of Sierra Mojada, Coahuila, Mexico. It introduces new employees to the site's location, company history, community services, mining and metallurgical processes, and management directory. The document contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, or aquatic ecosystem data. Its only water-related content concerns process water use and tailings management, so the species and habitat fields are recorded as N/A.

## Background & Description

**Welcome message.** The booklet opens with a welcome from [[Bernardo Portillo Acuña]], Director of Operations, Área Fierro. It stresses teamwork, resilience, and a quality-oriented culture, with the statement that quality is produced by people rather than machines.

**Location.** The unit lies in the municipality of Sierra Mojada, Coahuila, about 12 km from the Chihuahua state boundary. Neighboring properties are Rancho Los Comanches (north), Ejido La Gloria (south), Rancho Santa Teresa (east), and Rancho Las Cruces (west).
- Coordinates: 28° 02' 30" N, 103° 46' 30" W
- Elevation: 1,432 m a.s.l.
- Nearest town: Ciudad Camargo, Chihuahua, 160 km away, 90 km of it unpaved to the Camargo–Ojinaga highway junction.
- Nearest Coahuila town: Ocampo, about 4 hours by unpaved road.

**History.** Mining began in 1960. Workers, many from nearby ranches and towns, lived first in wooden houses in La Colonia de Abajo, then La Colonia de Enmedio. The modern mining town of Hércules emerged in the mid-1970s. The document also says the operation began in the 1930s with 32 men; this conflicts with the 1960 start date and is reported here as written.

**Population and housing.**
- About 1,300 workers and employees (the document says most live on site).
- 449 company houses, with drainage, potable water, and electricity (one page cites 439).
- Population of about 3,300 residents.

**Community infrastructure.** The company provides a clinic-hospital, staff and worker dining halls, a church (patron saint San Cayetano, celebrated 7 August), a cinema-theater, a self-service store, guest house, public lighting, five-channel television, a telephone booth, mail service, and a 2 km paved airstrip. The site was connected to the national rail line in December 1995.

**Education and recreation.** Services include a kindergarten, primary and secondary schools (morning and afternoon shifts), libraries, and adult education (INEA and IDEA). Sports facilities include a lit baseball field, a football field, 3 basketball courts, 2 tennis courts, 8 fronton courts, a children's sports unit, and a swimming pool. Cultural courses cover crafts, sewing, cooking, and similar subjects.

**Mining equipment.** The booklet highlights a Joy rock drill, described as a historic monument, used in exploration campaigns during 1955–1960 and displayed in town. The drill had an 18 hp gasoline engine, a 3-speed transmission, and a drilling depth of about 150 m. Current exploration rigs reach 600 m with 2¼-inch diameter bits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document describes the site as lying in the Coahuila desert, but it contains no biodiversity surveys, species records, or aquatic habitat assessments. Water is discussed only in an industrial context:
- The concentrator uses about **1,038 L/s** of process water.
- About **60%** of process water is recovered and recirculated, including water from spills and from the tailings dam.
- Tailings are pumped to the [[Presa De Jales]] (tailings dam), which the document refers to but does not characterize.
- Pit and waste handling involve blasting with diesel, emulsion, and [[Ammonium Nitrate]] mixtures. The document does not assess their environmental effects.

Environmental monitoring, impact assessments, and biological data are absent from this booklet.

## Key Findings

**Mining operation**
- Four open-pit mines (Prometeo, Teseo, Sama, and Tiber-Elektra) work cyclically, 24 hours a day, in three shifts, seven days a week.
- Planned extraction is **360,000 tonnes of iron ore per month**.
- Bench height is 13 m, ramp width 27 m, and ramp gradient 7%.
- Equipment includes rotary drills (9", 9⅞", and 12¼" diameter), Cat 992-C loaders (10 m³), Cat 777 and Terex 3311-D haul trucks (77–85 t), and auxiliary equipment.
- Waste stripping produces about **1,500,000 tonnes per month** of overburden, deposited in waste dumps (terreros).

**Crushing and concentration**
- A gyratory crusher (Allis Chalmers) takes up to 48-inch feed and reduces it below 7 inches.
- The concentrator processes an average feed of **586 tonnes per hour**, 300 days a year.
- Monthly concentrate output averages **300,000 tonnes**.
- Concentrate grade: **65% Fe and 0.14% P**.
- The process involves grinding to 100% passing 150 mesh, cycloning, desliming, flotation, and magnetic separation to remove gangue.
- Major equipment includes Dominion mills (586 t/h and 293 t/h), Krebs cyclones, Wemco flotation cells, Eimco hydroseparators, and Wemco and Eimco thickeners.

**Magnetic Concentration Plant, Stage II**
- Reprocesses tailings dams by hydraulic mining.
- Screens material at 0.6 mm and removes oversize impurities.
- Three stages of magnetic separation:
  - Low-intensity separators recover the most magnetic ore.
  - High-gradient separators at **1,100 Gauss** recover medium-susceptibility ore.
  - High-gradient separators at **2,200 Gauss** recover weakly magnetic ore.
- Concentrates are pumped back to the concentrator; final tailings go to the tailings dam.

**Ferroducto (slurry pipeline)**
- Length: **381.8 km** from Hércules to [[Altos Hornos De México]] in Monclova, Coahuila. The booklet describes it as the second-largest in the world.
- Pipe diameter: 14 in (Hércules–Monclova) and 8 in (Perla–Hércules).
- Capacity: **606 tonnes per hour**; flow **476 m³/h**; slurry density **2.04 kg/L**.
- Two pumping stations, one at a desert station 134.3 km away.
- Storage: two 3,500-tonne tanks plus one for off-specification concentrate.
- Fully automated and supervised by computer from the Hércules control center.

**Staff and management directory:** The booklet lists seven managers: Operations, Quality Assurance, Central Maintenance, Technical, Personnel Administration, Finance, and Labor Relations.

**Overall significance:** The document is a corporate and operational introduction. It offers no fish, aquatic, or ecological research findings. Its environmental content is limited to water use and recirculation rates.
