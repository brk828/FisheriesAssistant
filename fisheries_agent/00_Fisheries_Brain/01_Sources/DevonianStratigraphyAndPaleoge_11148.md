# Devonian Stratigraphy and Paleogeography in Gila, Graham, Greenlee, and Pinal Counties, Arizona

#stratigraphy #paleontology #devonian-strata

## Executive Summary

This 1968 University of Arizona doctoral dissertation by Gordon Leroy Pine is a petrographic and stratigraphic study of [[Devonian Strata]] in east-central and southeastern Arizona. Twenty-four localities were examined, and the work identifies a topographic high near Bylas that divides the study area into an eastern terrigenous unit, the [[Morenci Shale]], and a western carbonate (dolomite-dominated) unit, the [[Martin Formation]]. The author reconstructs a gradual marine transgression, probably from the west, over a region of low relief. The dolomite is interpreted as secondary, formed by seepage-refluxion of magnesium-rich waters. The study also reviews a century of conflicting interpretations of Devonian paleogeography, including the proposed [[Mazatzal Island]], and concludes that the sea encroached from the west in the western half of the study area and from the east or southeast in the eastern half.

## Background & Description

The dissertation concerns a region bounded roughly by Globe-Miami on the north, Mammoth on the south, Morenci on the east, and Superior on the west. It was designed as a petrographic investigation, since earlier regional correlations of Arizona Devonian rocks had been made largely without detailed petrographic analysis. The work follows [[Wright]] (1964) in southeastern Arizona and [[Teichert]] (1965) in central Arizona.

Stratigraphic nomenclature has changed repeatedly. Ransome (1903) first proposed the Globe Limestone, and Ransome (1916) later extended the name Martin Limestone into the Ray and Miami areas. Huddle and Dobrovolny (1952) revised the unit to the Martin Formation with three members. Teichert (1965) recognized the [[Beckers Butte Member]] and the [[Jerome Member]], and the author adopts Teichert's terminology for the western area. Lindgren's (1905) term Morenci Shale is retained for the eastern area.

The study rests on several laboratory methods:

- **Field work:** Sections were measured with a Jacob's staff and clinometer. About 1,100 samples were collected.
- **Classification:** Rocks were classified after [[Folk Classification]] (1959 and later) with modifications for carbonate-terrigenous mixtures.
- **Thin sections:** About 700 thin sections were prepared. Point counts used traverses spaced 2.0 mm apart, with about 200 points per section.
- **Staining:** The [[Dickson Staining Technique]] (1965) distinguished calcite, ferroan calcite, and dolomite.
- **Insoluble residue:** About 100 samples were dissolved in 10 percent hydrochloric acid.
- **X-ray diffraction:** About 180 samples were analyzed by [[X-Ray Diffraction]] for qualitative mineralogy.
- **Chemistry:** About 135 samples were analyzed for CaO and MgO by [[EDTA Titration]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Devonian strata record a warm, shallow, low-energy sea with low current activity and low turbulence, inferred mainly from the abundance of formerly carbonate mud. Key depositional settings include:

- **Beckers Butte Member:** Channel-fill deposits of conglomerate and orthoquartzite occur in areas such as Castle Dome, Sleeping Beauty, and Steamboat Mountain. Elsewhere the member is predominantly dolomite and was deposited in a shallow marine subtidal setting. Insoluble residue ranges from 13.5 to 81 percent. The marine waters probably had higher than normal salinity, which may explain the scarcity of fossils.
- **Fetid Dolomite Unit:** Laminated dolomite with organic-rich laminae is interpreted as an algal stromatolite structure formed in an intertidal to possibly supratidal setting. Organic matter was derived from marine organisms, with no contribution from land plants (Breger, in Teichert, 1965).
- **Aphanitic Dolomite Unit:** Birds-eye dolomite, intraclasts, and brachiopod or ostracod remnants suggest a warm, shallow, quiet but occasionally turbulent marine setting, with some subaerial exposure. Brachiopod shells were delicate and unoriented, consistent with little turbulence.
- **Upper Unit:** Carbonate mud is abundant, indicating low energy, but local winnowing and fossil-debris alignment show brief currents. Orthoquartzite and mud-shale layers occur, and oolitic hematite occurs near the base at the Job Corps Camp locality.
- **Morenci Shale:** Mud-shale and siltstone, with carbonate strata locally at the base, indicate a terrigenous, low-energy environment fed largely from the Defiance Positive area.

Fish remains are mentioned only as incidental evidence. Fish teeth occur in quartzitic sandstone beneath the Martin Limestone at Superior (Stoyanow, 1936), and fish fossils are reported from the Pine-Payson region. The dissertation does not study fish communities.

## Key Findings

- **Stratigraphic subdivision:** A topographic high near Bylas separates the Devonian strata into a predominantly terrigenous eastern portion (Morenci Shale) and a predominantly dolomite western portion (Martin Formation).
- **Thickness:**
  - Martin Formation: 80 to 460 feet, reflecting the relief of the pre-Devonian surface rather than post-depositional erosion.
  - Morenci Shale: about 140 to 225 feet.
  - Beckers Butte Member: 15 to 90 feet, generally 20 to 30 feet.
  - Fetid Dolomite Unit: about 6 to 26 feet.
  - Aphanitic Dolomite Unit: 12.5 to 188 feet, generally 100 to 150 feet.
  - Upper Unit of the Jerome Member: about 70 to 255 feet, generally more than 175 feet.
- **Contacts:** Lower and upper contacts have negligible relief at nearly all localities. The Martin Formation overlies Cambrian and Precambrian quartzites and the Cambrian Abrigo Formation. It is overlain by the Mississippian Escabrosa Limestone. The Morenci Shale overlies the Ordovician Longfellow Limestone and is overlain by the Mississippian Modoc Limestone.
- **Dolomitization:** All dolomite is secondary, replacing original aragonitic or calcitic material. Magnesium enrichment is attributed to evaporation of seawater and precipitation of calcium, followed by seepage-refluxion of dense brines.
- **Chemistry:**
  - Fetid Dolomite Unit: MgO of 18 to 23.5 percent, with calcite usually below 5 percent. Insoluble residue is usually below 10 percent.
  - Aphanitic Dolomite Unit: MgO mostly 15 to 20 percent, excess calcite generally 3 to 7 percent, and insoluble residue from 1 to 44 percent (most samples 5 to 20 percent).
  - Upper Unit: insoluble residue from about 1.5 to 67 percent, with MgO ranging from nearly zero to about 20 percent.
  - Morenci Shale carbonate strata: insoluble residue from 44 to 62 percent, MgO from near zero to about 21 percent.
- **Mineralogy:** Insoluble residue is dominated by quartz, followed by illite-like clay, with minor feldspar and chlorite. Talc occurs at Ray and Superior, probably from proximity to igneous activity. Corrensite was identified at Globe Hills.
- **Paleoenvironment:** The Fetid Dolomite Unit is intertidal to possibly supratidal, the Aphanitic Dolomite Unit is intertidal and subtidal, and the Upper Unit is subtidal. The Beckers Butte Member was deposited in a shallow subtidal setting.
- **Diagenetic evidence:** Dedolomitization, marked by replacement of dolomite by calcite, occurs at Apache Trail, Pinal Creek, and Putnam Wash. The author cannot identify the sulfate source.
- **Age:**
  - Beckers Butte Member: Middle Devonian, supported by a psilophyte flora (Teichert and Schopf, 1958).
  - Upper Unit of the Jerome Member: Fingerlake and Chemung, based on brachiopods, corals, and conodonts.
  - Morenci Shale: Cassadaga or Conewango, equivalent to the Box Member of the Percha Shale of New Mexico.
- **Source areas:** The Defiance Positive area was probably the main terrigenous source. Highs near Globe and Bylas and a high near the northwestern study boundary supplied smaller amounts. Coarser quartz in the Beckers Butte Member and Roblas Canyon orthoquartzites suggests an additional unknown source to the northwest.
- **Sea encroachment:** The sea transgressed from the west in east-central Arizona and from the south or southwest in southern Arizona. The author concludes that Eardley's Mazatzal Island interpretation is not supported, and that by the close of the Devonian, most of the region was covered by marine waters except for a few islands.
- **Nomenclature issues:** Stoyanow's Sycamore Creek and Island Mesa names were not adopted. Teichert (1965) argued that the former was preoccupied and that the latter's molluscan fauna occurs in a dolomite, so the name should not be used.
- **Paleogeographic maps:** Isopach, lithofacies, percent terrigenous, percent limestone, and sand-shale ratio maps were constructed from the study data and from [[Wright]] (1964), [[Teichert]] (1965), and other sources. The data are acknowledged to be sparse and uneven, so the maps are interpretive.
