## Executive Summary
This document is a brief correspondence (dated 4 November 1987) from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University) to Doug McQueen (ASU Research Park Planning Director) detailing a fish stocking event. The Arizona Game and Fish Department planted approximately 4,600 [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) into the northernmost pond at the ASU Research Park. The report confirms high survival rates immediately following transport and stocking, with a follow-up survey planned for the following spring to assess growth, referencing "remarkable growth" observed in a prior cohort stocked one year earlier.

## Background & Description
Specific biometric data provided for the stocked cohort:
* **Species:** *Gila elegans* ([[Bonytail]]), a member of the [[Cyprinidae]] (minnow) family.
* **Size Distribution:** Predominantly juvenile/sub-adult specimens.
    * **Average Size:** Approximately 120 fish per pound, correlating to an average length of roughly 3 inches (7.6 cm).
    * **Maximum Size:** Largest individuals recorded at approximately 10 inches (25.4 cm) in length.
* **Note:** The wide size range (3 in. avg vs 10 in. max) suggests a mixed cohort or fish sourced from different rearing classes/hatchery batches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
* **Native Range Context:** *Gila elegans* is native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically found in swift, turbid, warm-water mainstem rivers.
* **Current Habitat (Study Site):** The subject fish were relocated to an artificial, isolated pond environment at a research park, likely serving as a refugial or grow-out population outside their native riverine habitat.
* **Stressors:** The report specifically monitors survival relative to "stresses of transport and stocking," indicating the fragility of the species during translocation efforts, a common concern in [[Endangered Species Recovery Programs]].
* **Growth Rates:** The pond environment appears to support significant growth rates, as referenced by the "remarkable growth" of fish stocked in a previous cohort (c. 1986), suggesting the artificial pond provides favorable forage/conditions compared to native riverine habitats.

## Key Findings
1. **Successful Stocking Event:** 4,600 bonytail were successfully introduced into the Research Park pond on 3 November 1987.
2. **Zero Immediate Mortality:** A follow-up visit on 4 November 1987 (by Paul Marsh and Kirk Young) found no evidence of mortality, suggesting the fish acclimated well to the new environment immediately post-transport.
3. **Interagency Collaboration:** The project involved coordination between multiple entities, including:
    * Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD)
    * Arizona State University (ASU) Center for Environmental Studies
    * U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Albuquerque
    * Dexter National Fish Hatchery (NFH), New Mexico
4. **Longitudinal Monitoring:** The pond is slated for resampling in Spring 1988 to compare growth rates against the previous year's stocking cohort, indicating this is part of a longer-term [[Population Monitoring]] study for the species.
