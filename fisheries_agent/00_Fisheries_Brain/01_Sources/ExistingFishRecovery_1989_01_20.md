## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative roster (dated January 20, 1989) listing the composition of five federally-recognized **fish recovery teams** operating in Arizona and the broader Southwest under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS). Rather than presenting morphological or ecological data directly, the source provides a snapshot of the **institutional and interagency conservation infrastructure** assembled to recover threatened and endangered native fish species, including [[Apache Trout]], [[Woundfin]], [[Colorado River Fishes]] (multi-species complex), the broader [[Desert Fishes]] assemblage, and [[Gila Trout]]. The document reflects the collaborative, multi-agency approach (FWS, state game and fish departments, universities, tribal enterprises, and federal land management agencies) characteristic of Endangered Species Act recovery planning in the arid Southwest during the late 1980s.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While the text does not describe habitat use or trophic ecology directly, it implicitly maps the **geographic and hydrological scope** of recovery efforts:
- **[[Apache Trout]] Recovery Team** — White Mountains of Arizona, associated with the White Mountain Apache Tribe's Recreation Enterprise lands; cold headwater streams.
- **[[Woundfin]] Recovery Team** — Virgin River system (Utah/Nevada/Arizona border region); warm desert stream specialist.
- **[[Colorado River Fishes]] Recovery Team** — Mainstem and tributaries of the Colorado River basin, spanning California, Utah, Nevada, and Arizona jurisdictions; large-river endemic fishes.
- **[[Desert Fishes]] Recovery Team** — Broadest geographic scope, including Sonora, Mexico, reflecting transboundary desert spring/stream ecosystems.
- **[[Gila Trout]] Recovery Team** — Gila River headwaters, New Mexico/Arizona border; isolated montane trout streams, with hatchery support from Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Mescalero National Fish Hatchery.

## Key Findings
- **Five distinct recovery teams** were active in Arizona-associated fish conservation as of January 1989: Apache Trout, Woundfin, Colorado River Fishes, Desert Fishes, and Gila Trout.
- Each team follows a consistent governance structure: an **FWS coordinator**, a **team leader** (often from a state agency or university), core **members** (representing state fish and game departments, federal land agencies, and tribal enterprises), and in some cases external **consultants** (academic researchers and hatchery managers).
- The **Desert Fishes Recovery Team**, led by **W.L. Minckley** (Arizona State University), had the broadest interagency and international membership, including a representative from **Sonora, Mexico** (Lourdes Juarez-Romero), reflecting the transboundary nature of desert fish conservation.
- **Dean Hendrickson** (Arizona Game and Fish Department, AGFD) appears as a member on all five teams (Apache Trout, Woundfin, Colorado River Fishes, Desert Fishes, and Gila Trout), indicating a central coordinating role for AGFD in statewide native fish recovery efforts.
- Academic consultants — notably **W.L. Minckley** and **Paul Marsh** (both ASU), **Jim Deacon** (UNLV), and **Paul Holden** (Biowest) — provided technical expertise across multiple teams, illustrating strong university-agency partnerships in Southwestern fisheries conservation.
- National Fish Hatcheries (**Dexter NFH**, **Mescalero NFH**) were directly integrated into recovery planning, particularly for [[Gila Trout]], indicating active captive propagation/reintroduction components of the recovery strategy.
- The document underscores the **multi-jurisdictional complexity** of Southwestern native fish conservation, involving federal (FWS, BLM, BR, FS, NPS), state (AGFD, Utah DWR, Nevada GF, California DFG, New Mexico GF), tribal, and international (Sonora, Mexico) stakeholders working under a shared recovery team framework.
