## Executive Summary

This document is the general information and program packet for the **21st Annual Symposium of the Desert Fishes Council**, held November 16–18, 1989, at the Albuquerque Hilton Hotel in Albuquerque, New Mexico. While largely administrative (schedules, restaurant guides, logistics), the proceedings section (pages 17–24) lists **45 research and management papers** presented across multiple sessions, covering the conservation status, systematics, ecology, and management of desert and southwestern fishes and associated aquatic invertebrates. The meeting reflects the collaborative, binational (U.S.–Mexico) nature of desert fish conservation efforts involving federal and state agencies, universities, and Mexican research institutions. A capstone field trip to the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]** concluded the symposium, underscoring the applied conservation/propagation focus of the Council.

## Background & Description

The **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** is a conservation-focused scientific organization "Dedicated to the Preservation of America's Desert Fishes." The 1989 symposium was hosted in partnership with the University of New Mexico (Department of Biology, Museum of Southwestern Biology), the **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]** (Endangered Species Division, Fisheries Division, Dexter National Fish Hatchery), the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, and several Mexican institutions (Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores, Centro Ecológico de Sonora, Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León).

The meeting format included:
- Agency reports session
- A dedicated session on **permits and collecting in Mexico**, reflecting binational research cooperation on shared desert fish faunas (e.g., Rio Grande, Sonora, Baja California drainages)
- Four general research/management paper sessions (43 platform papers plus 2 posters)
- A business meeting and student meeting
- A field trip to the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]**, a key federal facility for propagation and refugia of endangered southwestern fishes

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The papers presented span a broad range of arid-land and desert aquatic ecosystems across the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, including:

- **Great Basin** systems (Truckee River, Warner Basin, White River, Ash Springs, Condor Canyon)
- **Colorado River basin** tributaries (Little Colorado River, Gila River, Little Snake River)
- **Rio Grande basin** (historical catfish fauna, Catostomus plebeius complex)
- **Virgin River / Beaver Dam Wash** (Utah) habitat and trophic studies
- **Baja California** highland streams (Sierra San Pedro Mártir trout habitat, Rio Santo Domingo hydrology)
- **Sonoran and Sinaloan river basins** (Rio Mayo, Rio Fuerte) and water quality assessment via macroinvertebrates and fish in the Rio Sonora
- **Snake River (Idaho)** springs, notably concerning invasive **[[Potamopyrgus antipodarum]]** (New Zealand mud snail) impacts on native snail fauna, and the critically endangered, undescribed **[[Bruneau Hot Springs Snail]]**
- **Nuevo León, Mexico** desert springs (Cyprinodon and Xiphophorus conservation crises at Sandia and Mesquital/Apodaca)

Key ecological themes include habitat use and diel food partitioning among coexisting native cyprinids and catostomids, biotic interactions structuring fish assemblages, invasive species impacts, hybridization between native and introduced trout, and water quality bioassessment using benthic macroinvertebrates and fish community structure.

## Key Findings

Because this document is a **symposium program** rather than a full research article, "findings" are represented by paper titles/abstracts rather than reported results. Key research threads presented include:

**Conservation Status & Systematics**
- Comprehensive review of North American fishes endangered, threatened, or of special concern (1989) — Johnson, Williams, Hendrickson, Contreras-Balderas, et al.
- Status updates for **[[Sonoran Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) in Arizona
- Virtual extinction of two undescribed *Cyprinodon* species from Sandia, Nuevo León, México
- Recovery efforts for Apodaca and Mesquital platyfish (*Xiphophorus* sp.) in Nuevo León
- Morphometric comparisons between *Cyprinodon nevadensis shoshone* and *C. n. amargosae*
- Systematic relationships among native **[[Cutthroat Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus clarki*) of the southern Rocky Mountains
- Population structure and possible cryptic species within *Catostomus plebeius*
- Relationships within the *Gila robusta* complex

**Genetics & Hybridization**
- Mitochondrial DNA assessment of **[[Gila Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) phylogenetics and population status
- Ribosomal DNA restriction fragment variation among cutthroat, redband, and brown trout
- Preliminary biochemical analysis of hybridization between native and nonnative trout in Arizona
- Genetic diversity and competitive interactions in sexual vs. clonal *Poeciliopsis* lineages
- Genetic diversity in subdivided (fragmented) fish populations

**Ecology & Habitat Use**
- Diel food utilization by **[[Virgin River Spinedace]]** and **[[Speckled Dace]]** in Beaver Dam Wash, Utah
- Habitat partitioning among spinedace, speckled dace, and **[[Desert Sucker]]**
- Potential dietary overlap between **[[Spikedace]]** and invasive **[[Red Shiner]]**
- Trophic ecology of *Oncorhynchus mykiss nelsoni* in Sierra San Pedro Mártir, Baja California
- Wetland use by fishes in the arid Southwest
- Effects of biotic interactions on Great Basin stream fish assemblage structure (experimental study)
- Alarm reactions in southwestern fishes

**Invasive Species & Water Quality**
- Rapid spread and ecological impact of the invasive New Zealand mud snail (*Potamopyrgus antipodarum*) on native snail fauna in the Middle Snake River, Idaho
- Biotic condition index monitoring in Box Canyon Creek, Idaho
- Status of the undescribed, monotypic **[[Bruneau Hot Springs Snail]]** and its imperiled spring habitat
- Water quality evaluation of the Rio Sonora, México, via benthic macroinvertebrates and fish assemblages

**Management & Recovery Programs**
- Management of Truckee River flows to enhance **[[Cui-ui]]** spawning
- Monitoring of the Little Colorado River **[[Humpback Chub]]** population (1989)
- Radiotelemetry studies of **[[Razorback Sucker]]** in the Gila River, Arizona
- Population estimates of Razorback Sucker in Lake Mohave
- "Block'n shock" sampling technique for **[[Colorado Pikeminnow]]** (squawfish) in spring backwaters
- Range extension documentation of endangered Colorado pikeminnow and humpback chub in the Little Snake River, Colorado
- Impact of natural perturbations on Gila trout recovery and implications for southwestern lotic habitats
- Habitat monitoring at Ash Springs and Condor Canyon, Nevada, for White River springfish and Big Spring spinedace
- Conservation status of Hiko White River Springfish (*Crenichthys baileyi grandis*)
- Public lands management strategy for special-status fishes (BLM)
- Potential impacts of fish barriers on native fish populations
- Exotic fishes database for monitoring alien fish distribution (USFWS)
- Status of Idaho sockeye salmon
- Protection strategy for threatened/endangered species of the San Marcos River, Texas
- Conservation status of threatened fishes in the Warner Basin, Oregon

**Regional Faunal Surveys**
- Historical question: "How many catfishes inhabited the pristine Rio Grande Basin?" — Robert Rush Miller
- Fishes of the Rio Mayo and Rio Fuerte basins, Sonora and Sinaloa, México
- Systematics and distribution of *Poecilia sulphurophila*
- Pleistocene desert climate history of Hispaniola and biogeographic implications
- Karyotype and chromosomal variability of *Oncorhynchus mykiss nelsoni* (San Pedro Mártir trout)
- Biology and conservation guidelines for the San Pedro Mártir trout

This symposium collectively illustrates the multi-disciplinary (genetics, systematics, ecology, hydrology, management) and binational scope of desert fish conservation science as practiced through the **[[Desert Fishes Council]]** network in the late 1980s.
