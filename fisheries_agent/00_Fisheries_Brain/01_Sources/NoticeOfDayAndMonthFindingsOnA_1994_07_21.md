## Executive Summary
This is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regulatory notice (Federal Register, July 21, 1994) documenting 90-day and 12-month petition findings regarding the reclassification status of two small cyprinid fishes, [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), from "Threatened" to "Endangered" under the Endangered Species Act (ESA). The petition, submitted September 17, 1993 by a coalition including the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] and [[Biodiversity Legal Foundation]], prompted a Service review concluding that reclassification to endangered status is biologically warranted but administratively precluded due to higher-priority listing actions. This is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper but a legal/administrative document synthesizing prior biological studies (e.g., Minckley 1973, Propst et al. 1986/1988, Rinne 1991) on population status, genetics, and threats.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide direct morphological descriptions (e.g., meristics, body proportions, fin ray counts) of [[Spikedace]] or [[Loach Minnow]]. Both are characterized only generally as:
- **Common description**: "Two small minnows" (Family [[Cyprinidae]])
- **Taxonomic identity**:

  - Loach Minnow — *Tiaroga cobitis* (Girard)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Both species inhabit "relatively fast flowing water in perennial stream stretches" within isolated, fragmented reaches of the upper [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona and New Mexico.
- **Historic range**: Formerly distributed throughout most of the Gila River basin; both species have undergone dramatic range contractions since the late 1800s.
- **Current known populations**:
  - **Spikedace**: Isolated stretches of the [[Gila River]] (Grant and Catron Counties, NM); [[Aravaipa Creek]] (Graham and Pinal Counties, AZ); East Fork and mainstem [[White River]] (Navajo and Gila Counties, AZ); [[San Francisco River]] and [[Tularosa River]] and Dry Blue Creek (Catron County, NM); and the [[Blue River]] and San Francisco River and Campbell Blue Creek (Greenlee County, AZ).
  - **Loach Minnow**: Five remaining populations noted (implied parallel distribution across similar Gila basin tributaries).
- **Threats driving decline**:
  - Water development: impoundment, diversion, channelization, groundwater pumping
  - Watershed degradation/erosion from livestock grazing, timber harvest, road building, recreation
  - Introduction and spread of nonnative fish species (e.g., competitive interactions cited between spikedace and [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) per Rinne 1991)
- **Genetic population structure**: Allozyme analyses (Tibbets 1992) indicate significant genetic differentiation among fragmented populations:
  - 4 of 5 remaining loach minnow populations analyzed — each genetically distinctive
  - All 4 remaining spikedace populations analyzed — each quite different, with the [[Verde River]] population being the most genetically distinct
  - Findings suggest a conservation need to preserve each fragmented population to maintain overall species-level genetic diversity

## Key Findings
- **Listing history**: 
  - Spikedace listed as Threatened: July 1, 1986
  - Loach Minnow listed as Threatened: October 28, 1986
  - Critical habitat designated for both species: March 8, 1994
- **1991 five-year status review** (per ESA §4(c)(2)) found both species' status had become "more precarious" due to substantial increases in threats over the prior five years, meeting biological criteria for Endangered status.
- **Recommending bodies**: The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] both recommended reclassification to Endangered for both species.
- **1994 Petition Finding (June 30, 1994)**: The Service concluded reclassification to Endangered is **warranted** but **precluded** by work on other higher-priority listing actions receiving expeditious progress — a "warranted but precluded" (candidate) status under the ESA.
- **Benefits assessment**: Reclassification to Endangered would confer only "incremental" benefits (formal recognition of precarious status; increased penalties for ESA §9 violations) beyond existing Threatened-species protections, and was judged lower priority than extending protection to currently unlisted species.
- **Definitional framework**: 
  - Threatened = "likely to become an endangered species within the foreseeable future throughout all or a significant portion of its range"
  - Endangered = "in danger of extinction throughout all or a significant portion of its range"
- **Key cited literature**: Minckley (1973) *Fishes of Arizona*; Propst et al. (1986, 1988) USFWS Endangered Species Reports on spikedace and loach minnow distribution/status/biology in New Mexico; Propst and Bestgen (1991) *Copeia*; Rinne (1991) *Southwestern Naturalist* on habitat competition with [[Red Shiner]]; Tibbets (1992) allozyme variation study presented to the [[Desert Fishes Council]].
