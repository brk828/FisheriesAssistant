# The Odonata of Cape Cod, Massachusetts

#ichthyology #marine-biology #odonata #entomology #freshwater-ecology

> **Note:** This source is an entomological faunal survey of dragonflies and damselflies (Odonata), not a fish-specific study. Fish are mentioned only incidentally as habitat co-occupants (e.g., bullheads, sunfish) and as a proxy for lake classification ("sport fishes" ponds). The note below is synthesized to reflect the paper's actual taxonomic focus: [[Odonata]] biogeography and habitat ecology.

## Executive Summary

Gibbs & Gibbs (1954) conducted the first systematic Odonata survey of [[Cape Cod]], Massachusetts, based on 115 collections at 55 localities during summers 1950–1953. The study recorded **72 species**, of which 19 were new records for Cape Cod and 2 (*[[Enallagma Recurvatum]]* and *[[Ladona Deplanata]]*) were new to both Massachusetts and New England. The authors framed Cape Cod as a biogeographically significant "meeting ground" between northern (glacial relict) and southern (coastal plain, range-expanding) Odonata faunas, shaped by its origin as glacial moraine/outwash plain, its position at the terminus of Wisconsin glaciation, and the oceanographic influence of the Labrador Current and Gulf Stream. Of the 72 species collected, 46 were of southern coastal-plain affinity, supporting the hypothesis of coastal plain habitat continuity with Long Island and New Jersey rather than a hard barrier at the Plymouth-Woods Hole moraine proposed by Howe (1921).

## Background & Description

While the paper is not morphology-centric, it includes a notable **taxonomic redescription** distinguishing three closely related *[[Ladona]]* species based on genitalic and pigmentation characters:

- ***[[Ladona Exusta]]***: Penis (median lobes) convex distally, not extended; abdomen becomes white-pruinose with gray, non-pruinose thoracic dorsum; supratriangle of forewing consistently crossed by a crossvein; dark basal wing markings not divided by a clear streak.
- ***[[Ladona Deplanata]]***: Penis concave distally with extended ends; becomes uniformly gray-pruinose on both thorax and abdomen; supratriangle crossed in only ~25% of specimens; dark basal wing markings generally smaller, often divided by a clear streak (inconsistent character).
- ***Ladona Julia*** (comparative, not collected in this study): Penis convex distally with more extended lobes; abdomen white-pruinose only on anterior segments; thoracic dorsum white; supratriangle crossed; wing markings small but lacking the clear streak seen in *deplanata*.

Additional taxonomic notes:
- ***[[Libellula Auripennis]]*** vs. ***[[Libellula Needhami]]***: Recently split species (Westfall 1943) distinguished by habitat segregation rather than gross morphology alone — *needhami* restricted to brackish/coastal habitats, *auripennis* found in inland ponds and coastal zones.
- ***[[Libellula Incesta]]*** vs. ***Libellula Vibrans***: Penis examination of specimens field-identified as *vibrans* by color pattern revealed all were actually *L. incesta*, suggesting historical *vibrans* records for the region are misidentifications.
- Female *[[Enallagma Laterale]]* were described for the first time from tandem pairs collected at Jabinette's Pond.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study identifies **five principal Odonata habitat types** on Cape Cod, each supporting distinct assemblages:

1. **Large glacial lakes** — sandy shorelines, minimal boggy margins, often stocked with sport fishes; associated with species requiring higher wave action/oxygenation:
   - *[[Argia Moesta]]*, *[[Enallagma Exsulans]]*, *[[Dromogomphus Spinosus]]*, *[[Epicordulia Princeps]]* — found exclusively in large lakes.

2. **Kettle hole ponds** — glacial outwash depressions formed around melting ice blocks; show a continuum from sandy-bottomed ponds to true bogs, hosting the most cosmopolitan assemblage (e.g., *[[Enallagma Doubledayi]]*, the most abundant damselfly on the Cape).

3. **Sphagnum bogs** — cold-stenothermal/northern relict species associated with quaking bog mats:
   - *[[Enallagma Cyathigerum]]*, *[[Nehalennia Gracilis]]*, *[[Gomphaeschna Furcillata]]*, *[[Dorocordulia Lepida]]*, *[[Libellula Quadrimaculata]]*, *[[Nannothemis Bella]]* — all northern-affinity species whose survival southward is attributed to the thermal/habitat constancy of bogs.

4. **Small sandy streams** (associated with cranberry bogs) — running-water specialists:
   - *[[Agrion Maculatum]]*, *[[Amphiagrion Saucium]]*, *[[Sympetrum Semicinctum]]* — collected only near flowing water.

5. **Brackish tidal habitats** — salinity-tolerant or salt-marsh breeding species:
   - *[[Erythrodiplax Berenice]]*, *[[Libellula Needhami]]* — restricted to brackish/tidal margins.

**Fish co-occurrence notes** (incidental to the Odonata focus):
- Deer Pond (Falmouth Twp.) supported a heavy population of bullhead (*[[Ameiurus Nebulosus]]*).
- Jabinette's Pond (Yarmouth Twp.) had abundant sunfish (*[[Lepomis Gibbosus]]*).
- Larger lakes generally were "often populated with sport fishes," used as a defining habitat criterion distinguishing lakes from smaller ponds.
- Locality 50 was the "Ponds of Sandwich Fish Hatchery," visited for Odonata collecting but not detailed further.

**Climatic/hydrological sensitivity**: Anomalously high spring rainfall in 1953 raised pond water levels ~1.5 ft above normal through early August, submerging emergent vegetation used by nymphs for transformation. This coincided with sharp declines in previously common species (*[[Leucorrhinia Frigida]]*, *[[Celithemis Elisa]]*, *[[Celithemis Martha]]*, *[[Lestes Disjunctus]]*, *[[Lestes Forcipatus]]*) at Flashy Pond, illustrating strong habitat-structure dependence of nymphal emergence success.

## Key Findings

- **72 species** collected across 115 collections/55 localities (1950–1953); 19 new Cape Cod records; 2 new North American regional records (*[[Enallagma Recurvatum]]*, *[[Ladona Deplanata]]*).
- **13 previously reported species** were not recollected by the authors.
- Refutation/refinement of Howe's (1921) "moraine barrier" hypothesis: of 28 species Howe claimed were excluded from Cape Cod by the Plymouth–Woods Hole moraine, the authors collected 18, mostly as breeding populations (only *[[Pantala Flavescens]]* appeared to be a transient stray).
- Coastal plain affinity species (e.g., *[[Enallagma Laterale]]*, *[[Ischnura Kellicotti]]*, *[[Enallagma Recurvatum]]*, *[[Enallagma Pictum]]*) show a distribution centered on the northern Coastal Plain (Cape Cod, Long Island, New Jersey), supporting a biogeographic continuity argument rather than a strict barrier model.
- Several rare or localized species exhibited "focal" distributions — abundant at one pond on one date, then absent days later (e.g., *[[Enallagma Laterale]]* and *[[Enallagma Recurvatum]]* at Jabinette's/Flax Pond), suggesting narrow, possibly early-spring-restricted flight seasons that were undersampled by the June–September collecting window.
- Documented probable **female dimorphism** in *[[Anax Longipes]]*: one female had an abdomen colored similarly to *[[Anax Junius]]* females, while another observed individual had a brick-red abdomen; unlike *A. junius*, *A. longipes* females were observed ovipositing without a guarding male.
- Species checklist and phenology (first/last capture dates) established a baseline for 72 species across Agrionidae, Lestidae, Coenagrionidae, Gomphidae, Aeshnidae, Corduliidae, and Libellulidae — foundational for later Cape Cod odonate biogeography and coastal plain relict-species studies.
