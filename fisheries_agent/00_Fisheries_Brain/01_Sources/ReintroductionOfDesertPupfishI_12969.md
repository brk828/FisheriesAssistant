# Plan for the Reintroduction of the Desert Pupfish into Phoenix District Waters

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinodontidae #pupfish #reintroduction

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal reintroduction plan for the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) into selected waters on the Phoenix District of the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM). Developed under a Memorandum of Understanding between BLM Arizona and the Arizona Game & Fish Department (AG&FD), the plan aims to restore this extirpated species to its historic range within the [[Gila River]] drainage. The species is classified as Group I (extirpated from Arizona, possibly reestablishable) on Arizona's Threatened and Unique Wildlife list. Sixteen waters were initially selected for reintroduction, with monitoring protocols established for 3 years post-stocking.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) formerly occupied much of the [[Gila River]] drainage but was extirpated from the basin until recovery efforts by W. L. Minckley and R. R. Miller in 1973. The species is listed as Group I on the State of Arizona Threatened and Unique Wildlife list, indicating extirpation from Arizona with potential for reestablishment.

All proposed reintroduction sites are located within the historic range of the pupfish. The plan was developed under authority of a Memorandum of Understanding between the U.S. Department of the Interior, Bureau of Land Management (BLM) Arizona and the Arizona Game and Fish Department, approved August 10, 1982, concerning nongame fish reintroductions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sixteen waters on the Phoenix District were selected for initial reintroduction efforts. These waters were identified as suitable [[Desert Pupfish]] habitat during field inspections conducted on June 22 and August 24, 1981, by Phoenix District and AG&FD personnel. Sites are listed in descending order of priority in the plan's attachments.

No adverse impacts to existing wildlife or other multiple uses of the waters were identified. The plan explicitly states that presence of pupfish will not be used by either party to hinder present or future demands on the waters, though BLM may choose to manage some waters for habitat protection or improvement under Habitat Management Plans (HMPs).

An Environmental Assessment (EAR# AZ-020-1-246, July 15, 1981) was prepared covering the reintroduction action, with a Decision Record containing a Finding of No Significant Impact.

## Key Findings

**Reintroduction Strategy:**
- AG&FD will provide and stock [[Desert Pupfish]] into identified waters on a priority basis
- Stocking to occur as soon as possible following approval by AG&FD Regional Supervisors and Phoenix District Manager
- U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service will acquire collecting permit for pupfish from Mexico (not available until spring following October 1982 correspondence)
- Additional reintroduction sites may be appended upon written request and approval

**Monitoring Protocol:**
- Monitoring will occur twice annually for the first 3 years
- Early spring monitoring to evaluate over-winter survival
- Fall monitoring to document reproduction and habitat condition
- After initial 3-year period, monitoring will occur once annually in spring to determine continued existence of populations
- AG&FD responsible for species monitoring with some BLM assistance
- Each agency provides its own personnel and funding

**Public Involvement:**
- AG&FD will carry out public information effort allowing for public involvement and comment
- BLM will communicate with local range and other water users before stocking
- BLM and AG&FD personnel may meet in field with local users in some cases

**Conservation Goals:**
- Reintroductions will increase distribution of pupfish within its range
- Aims to aid in restoration and perpetuation of the species
- Intended to ensure species does not become federally threatened or endangered
