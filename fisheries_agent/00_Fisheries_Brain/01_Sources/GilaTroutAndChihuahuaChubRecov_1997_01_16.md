# Gila Trout and Chihuahua Chub Recovery Team — Annual Meeting Summary (1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #freshwater-ecology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document summarizes the 1996 annual meeting of the [[Gila Trout]] and [[Chihuahua Chub]] Recovery Team, convened by the USFWS New Mexico Ecological Services and Fishery Resources Office. The meeting reviewed 1996 field sampling activities across multiple Gila trout populations (McKnight Creek, Black Canyon, Little Creek), discussed barrier construction needs to prevent nonnative salmonid reinvasion, evaluated potential renovation streams (Meadow Creek, Trout/Cow/Snow creeks), and addressed the contentious issue of establishing Gila trout populations in Arizona (Verde River drainage) under an "experimental, nonessential" designation. The Recovery Team also revisited strategic frameworks for downlisting Gila trout from endangered to threatened status.

## Background & Description
- **McKnight Creek population sampling (April 1996):** 173 individual [[Gila Trout]] were collected, weighed, and measured during spawning-stock assessment (data recorded in referenced attachment #1, not reproduced in this text).
- Comparatively few "ripe" (reproductively mature) adults were collected during the spawning run, indicating a constrained breeding population size that year.
- No morphological data provided distinguishing the putative **Spruce lineage** of Gila trout from the **Apache trout** lineage in the Verde River drainage — this taxonomic ambiguity remains unresolved and is noted as a research need for [[Chitty Creek]] stocking decisions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[McKnight Creek]]:** Primary broodstock source population; sampled for spawning-egg collection, transferring ~400 fertilized, water-hardened eggs and ~20 unspawned adults to [[Mescalero National Fish Hatchery]].
- **[[Black Canyon]] drainage:** Surveyed at nine sites from ~1.6 km downstream of Black Canyon Campground to Falls Canyon. No fish detected at the three most upstream sites; only **native fishes** (non-Gila trout) found at remaining sites. Site under active livestock grazing pressure, with **considerable habitat degradation** noted — a concern for long-term population viability.
- **[[Little Creek]]:** Sampled at two locations in June; yielded few fish, suggesting a depressed or marginal population status.
- **[[Meadow Creek]]:** Currently supports only **speckled dace** (*Rhinichthys osculus*) in upper reaches; nonnative salmonids suspected in lower reaches. Identified as a candidate renovation stream pending full stream inventory, in conjunction with planned USFS prescribed burns.
- **Sapillo Creek tributaries** ([[Trout Creek]], [[Cow Creek]], [[Snow Creek]]): Briefly discussed as additional potential Gila trout renovation habitat.
- **[[Verde River]] drainage / Spruce-Dry watershed (Arizona):** High wildfire risk in extremely rugged terrain; agency rescue of fish during fire events considered operationally infeasible, elevating urgency for establishing a secondary population of the **Spruce lineage** elsewhere.
- **[[Chitty Creek]]** (tributary to [[Eagle Creek]]): Candidate site for Gila trout introduction; unresolved question of whether Gila trout or Apache trout was the historically native salmonid.
- **[[Mineral Creek]]:** Targeted for renewed NEPA process to support future Gila trout establishment.

## Key Findings
1. **Population sampling outcomes (1996):** McKnight Creek yielded a functional broodstock (173 fish handled; ~400 eggs + 20 adults to hatchery); Black Canyon and Little Creek surveys revealed low fish abundance and, in Black Canyon's case, habitat degradation from livestock grazing with no trout detected at upstream sites.
2. **Barrier construction priority:** The Recovery Team recommended an engineering evaluation (involving fish-barrier specialists) of the stream reach near USFS Road 150 bridge to design a barrier preventing reinvasion by nonnative salmonids — flagged as an urgent unresolved action item since the USFS had not yet responded.
3. **Arizona/Verde drainage controversy:** AZGF would only support Gila trout establishment in Arizona under an "experimental, nonessential" ESA classification, requiring a minimum 24-month approval process. The Recovery Team judged this timeline incompatible with the urgent fire risk facing the sole extant **Spruce lineage** population, and expressed concern that using this designation purely for political expediency could set an "undesirable precedent."
4. **Four formal recommendations** were adopted to secure the Spruce lineage:
   - Engage Zane Grey Chapter of [[Trout Unlimited]] to negotiate AZGF flexibility.
   - Encourage AZGF to begin its "12-step" evaluation process for Verde tributaries and Chitty Creek.
   - Request USFS reinitiate NEPA process for Gila trout establishment in Mineral Creek.
   - Evacuate/rescue Gila trout from Spruce Creek to Mescalero NFH for use as brood stock or future stocking.
5. **Downlisting strategy:** The Recovery Team reaffirmed prior identification of multiple strategies toward downlisting Gila trout from endangered to threatened status, beginning with the "traditional" approach requiring secure replication of each relictual population per the existing Recovery Plan (additional strategies referenced but not detailed in this excerpt).
6. **Taxonomic uncertainty:** The historic native salmonid of the Verde River drainage remains ambiguously classified as either Gila trout (Spruce lineage) or Apache trout, complicating both Arizona stocking decisions and Chitty Creek source-population selection.
