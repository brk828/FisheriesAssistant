# Middle-American Poeciliid Fishes of the Genera Carlhubbsia and Phallichthys, with Descriptions of Two New Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

Rosen and Bailey (1959) revise two Middle-American poeciliid genera, [[Carlhubbsia]] and [[Phallichthys]], which had previously been grouped with several other genera in the subfamily [[Poeciliopsinae]] on the basis of a shared asymmetrically folded male gonopodium. The authors describe two new species, *Carlhubbsia stuarti* and *Phallichthys fairweatheri*, recognize *Phallichthys amates* with two provisional subspecies, and argue that the Poeciliopsinae is an artificial assemblage that should be dissolved. Carlhubbsia is instead shown to share suspensorial and gonopodial characters with the Cuban endemics [[Quintana]] and [[Girardinus]], pointing to a mainland origin for the Antillean group.

## Background & Description

The study is based on preserved material mostly held at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ), with additional specimens from the Chicago Natural History Museum, the New York Zoological Society Genetics Laboratory and the United States National Museum. Much of the Guatemalan and Honduran material was collected by the Rev. Gerald Fairweather, Myron Gordon, Carl L. Hubbs, Laurence C. Stuart and associates. Methods included standard fin-ray, scale and vertebral counts, proportional measurements expressed per thousand of standard length, cleared-and-stained skeletons, camera lucida drawings and X-ray comparisons, following [[Miller]] (1948) and the terminology of [[Rosen and Gordon]] (1953).

Genus descriptions emphasise:
- **Carlhubbsia**: dextrally folded gonopodium, 15 to 20 spine-like processes on ray 3, uncini on gonapophyses I and II (and sometimes III) forming an uncinar plane, a greatly dilated primary gonactinostal complex, and 28 to 30 vertebrae. Confined to the Atlantic drainages from the Isthmus of Tehuantepec to southern Guatemala.
- **Phallichthys**: a more simply specialised gonopodium, narrow primary gonactinostal complex, uncini on gonapophyses II and III placed distally, and 27 to 30 vertebrae. Found on the Atlantic lowlands from western Panama to northern Guatemala and British Honduras.

Standard Carlhubbsia and Phallichthys taxa are summarised in the following table.

| Taxon | Status in this paper | Key distinguishing feature |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** Carlhubbsia stuarti was recorded from muddy, sandy-mud bottoms with moderate current at depths up to 5 feet. Carlhubbsia kidderi preferred quiet to slow water, soft silt or organic litter, dense vegetation and shallow protected lagoons, at warm or hot temperatures. Phallichthys fairweatheri occupied quiet or stagnant water, often with soft mud and hydrogen sulphide. Phallichthys amates amates ranged from no current to swift flow, at 22° to 28° C in shallow water. P. amates pittieri was reported from swift rocky streams at about 1,200 m elevation.

**Distribution.** Carlhubbsia stuarti is known from the Rio Polochic and Lake Izabal, Guatemala. C. kidderi occurs in the Rio Champotén (Campeche, Mexico) and in the Rio San Pedro de Martir and Rio de la Pasión systems of El Petén and Alta Verapaz, which drain to the [[Usumacinta River]]. The two species are allopatric, separated by about 45 miles across the Yucatan Peninsula. Carlhubbsia kidderi and Phallichthys fairweatheri are sympatric in El Petén. Phallichthys fairweatheri is known from three areas: El Petén, the New and Rio Hondo systems of British Honduras (probably one stock), and northern Guatemala.

**Behaviour and laboratory notes.** Phallichthys amates and P. fairweatheri move in short, hesitant dashes and feed mainly on the bottom. Attempted hybridisation of P. fairweatheri with P. amates failed in 10 heterotypic pairings, while homotypic pairs produced young. P. fairweatheri reproduced poorly in captivity and matured more slowly (eight months or longer) than P. amates (three to four months).

## Key Findings

- **Dissolution of the Poeciliopsinae.** The gonopodial folding shared by Carlhubbsia, Phallichthys, Poeciliopsis, Aulophallus, Phalloptychus and Xenophallus is judged an independently derived feature, having arisen at least five times within the Poeciliidae (and in Jenynsiidae and Anablepidae). The authors conclude the subfamily is artificial and should be disrupted.
- **Phallichthys is allied with Poeciliopsis and Aulophallus.** These are considered a natural group with a shared suite of gonopodial and suspensorial traits. Poecilistes is synonymised with Poeciliopsis.
- **Carlhubbsia belongs with the Cuban endemics.** Carlhubbsia shares a constellation of characters (terminal hook on ray 3, spines on ray 3, weak retrorse serrae on ray 4p, serrae on ray 5p, three specialised gonapophyses, uncini in one plane) with [[Quintana]] and [[Girardinus]]. The authors interpret this as evidence of common descent and mainland representation of the Antillean group.
- **Girardinus is broadened.** Glaridichthys, Toxus, Dactylophallus and Allodontium are reduced to synonymy with Girardinus, which equals the tribe Girardinini; the tribe names Girardinini and Quintanini are considered unnecessary at current limits.
- **Dentition is unreliable.** Tooth structure varies strongly within Poeciliopsis and Girardinus and is largely uniform between Carlhubbsia and Phallichthys, so it should be given less weight in poeciliid systematics.
- **Sensory canals are not diagnostic.** Canal patterns are similar across the genera and closely resemble those of Mollienesia, Girardinus and Xiphophorus, so they are not treated as evidence of relationship.
- **Two new species described.** Carlhubbsia stuarti (holotype UMMZ 146084, 38.3 mm SL, 290+ paratypes and additional material) and Phallichthys fairweatheri (holotype UMMZ 172456, 29.7 mm SL) are diagnosed by gonopodial, suspensorial, meristic and pigmentary characters, with comparative tables (Tables 1–20) given in the original.
- **Provisional subspecies of P. amates.** Pittieri and amates cannot be separated by gonopodial structure; they differ mainly in dorsal and pectoral ray counts and dorsal fin length, and their ranges are separated by an unsampled coastal stretch of northern Honduras and eastern Nicaragua.
- **Zoogeographic conclusion.** The authors propose that the ancestors of Quintana and Girardinus reached Cuba from Central America via coastal northward spread toward the Yucatan Peninsula, and that the Cuban endemics were isolated long ago, in contrast with the more recent poeciliin and gambusiin invasions of the West Indies.
