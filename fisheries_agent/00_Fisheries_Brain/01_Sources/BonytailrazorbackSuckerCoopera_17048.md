# Bonytail-Razorback Sucker Cooperative Effort Lake Mohave March 1991

#ichthyology #endangered-species #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #population-monitoring

## Executive Summary

This summary report documents a collaborative fish monitoring effort conducted 16-22 March 1991 on [[Lake Mohave]] by [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (USBR), and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS). The primary objectives were to capture [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), tag and recapture [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), and collect data on [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*) and other fish community members. This was the fourth collaborative effort in an ongoing monitoring program. The report includes capture data, PIT tag numbers, recapture information, and movement patterns for razorback suckers.

## Background & Description

The cooperative monitoring program involves three agencies working different areas of [[Lake Mohave]]: ASU focused on [[Cottonwood East]], USBR concentrated on the [[Six-Mile]] vicinity, and USFWS worked [[Arizona Bay]] up-lake to [[Owl Point]]. Multiple capture methods were employed including trammel nets, gill nets, and electrofishing. All captured [[Razorback Sucker|razorback suckers]] were implanted with PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags for long-term individual identification and movement tracking.

The [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) is an endangered cyprinid species, while the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is an endangered catostomid. Both species are native to the Colorado River system. The monitoring program aims to assess population status, spawning activity, movement patterns, and survival of these imperiled fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] system between Arizona and Nevada. The fish community includes both native endangered species ([[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) and introduced species ([[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Carp]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Bluegill]]).

Sampling in March 1991 was conducted during the razorback sucker spawning period, though spawning was essentially complete in the lower lake when sampling occurred. Inclement weather, particularly high winds, limited sampling effectiveness compared to previous years. The presence of predatory [[Striped Bass]] in increasing numbers represents a potential threat to native fish populations.

Water conditions and habitat characteristics were not detailed in this summary report, though specific sampling locations included shallow coves and backwater areas where razorback suckers aggregate for spawning.

## Key Findings

**Bonytail Captures:**
- Zero bonytail captured during March 1991 sampling
- Five individuals captured in trammel nets in May 1991 (subsequent sampling)
- Two adults transferred to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] for brood-stock at [[Dexter, New Mexico]]
- Two juveniles (presumably re-introduced young) were PIT tagged and released
- One specimen died in field, frozen for ageing and genetic analysis

**Razorback Sucker Captures:**
- Total of 733 adult razorback suckers captured (well below 1,529 fish taken in 1990)
- ASU: 100 razorbacks in trammel nets over 6 days
- USBR: 46 by electrofishing and trammel nets (limited to 3 days due to weather and equipment failure)
- USFWS: 587 razorbacks using combination of trammel nets, gill nets, and electrofishing

**Recapture Data:**
- 37 recaptures among 733 adult razorback suckers handled
- 4 recaptures yielded no information due to data errors
- 16 fish recaptured 18 times during immediate sampling period (short-term recaptures)
- 21 long-term recaptures recorded

**Movement Patterns:**
- 13 fish at large for 12 months showed local or no movement (recaptured in same area as initial capture): 3 from [[Six-Mile]] area, 1 from [[Cottonwood East]], 9 from [[Arizona Bay]]-up-lake area
- 4 fish recaptured after 24 months at large exhibited little or no overall movement (2 each from [[Six-Mile]] and [[Arizona Bay]]-up-lake)
- One fish recaptured in [[Six-Mile]] area nine years after being tagged in same vicinity
- Five fish showed distant movements:
  - Two moved up-lake: one from [[Six-Mile]] to upper [[Arizona Bay]] (16 km, 12 months); one from [[Cottonwood East]] to [[Arizona Bay]] (9.6 km, 24 months)
  - One moved down-lake from [[Arizona Bay]] to [[Six-Mile]] (11.2 km)
  - Two moved 6.4 km west across [[Cottonwood Basin]] from [[Six-Mile]] to [[Cottonwood East]] (12 months)

**Striped Bass:**
- 116 individuals captured (compared to 111 in 1990) - most abundant in collective samples of any previous year
- Size range from yearling to large adult
- Vital statistics and scale samples collected for [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]

**Cottonwood East Trammel Net Statistics (ASU):**
- [[Carp]]: 371 fish (63.5%), CPE = 6.942 fish/100m²/24-hour period
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: 100 fish (17.4%), CPE = 1.871
- [[Striped Bass]]: 63 fish (10.8%), CPE = 1.179
- [[Channel Catfish]]: 40 fish (6.9%), CPE = 0.749
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: 6 fish (1.0%), CPE = 0.112
- [[Rainbow Trout]]: 3 fish (0.5%), CPE = 0.056
- [[Bluegill]]: 1 fish (0.2%), CPE = 0.019
- Total: 584 fish, CPE = 10.928

**Future Plans:**
- Comprehensive 5-year report planned following spring 1992 sampling
- Razorback sucker database expected to exceed 10,000 fish by end of 1992
- Continued monitoring and investigation of [[Lake Mohave]] fish community needed
