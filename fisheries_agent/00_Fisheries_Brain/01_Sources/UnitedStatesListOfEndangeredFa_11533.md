# United States List of Endangered Fauna (May 1974)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #endangered-species #federal-legislation

## Executive Summary

This document presents the official United States List of Endangered Fauna published in May 1974 by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. It implements the Endangered Species Act of 1973 (Public Law 93-205), which became effective December 28, 1973, replacing the previous 1969 Act. The list encompasses endangered species across all animal taxa, including extensive coverage of bony fishes (Class Osteichthyes), with particular emphasis on North American freshwater species. The document includes 33 fish species among hundreds of endangered fauna, providing scientific names, common names, and geographic distributions. Notable ichthyological entries include [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser brevirostrum*), and multiple endangered cyprinids, salmonids, and cyprinodontids from western United States waters.

## Background & Description

The 1973 Endangered Species Act introduced significant changes to U.S. wildlife conservation law: (1) coverage expanded to encompass all species of animals and plants; (2) "species" now includes subspecies and smaller taxonomic units, plus viable population segments; (3) two endangerment categories were established—"Endangered Species" (in danger of extinction throughout all or significant portions of range) and "Threatened Species" (likely to become endangered in foreseeable future); (4) separate "Native" and "Foreign" designations were eliminated in favor of combined listings.

The bony fishes listed represent multiple orders:
- **Acipenseriformes**: [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser brevirostrum*) from USA/Canada Atlantic Coast
- **Salmoniformes**: Seven trout species including [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*), [[Greenback Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki stomias*), [[Apache Trout]] (*Salmo sp.*)
- **Cypriniformes**: Eleven species including [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), [[Moapa Dace]] (*Moapa coriacea*)
- **Siluriformes**: Four catfish species including [[Giant Catfish]] (*Pangasianodon gigas*) from Thailand
- **Atheriniformes**: Ten species of pupfish, killifish, and topminnows, including [[Devil's Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*), [[Pahrump Killifish]] (*Empetrichthys latos*), [[Big Bend Gambusia]] (*Gambusia gaigei*)
- **Perciformes**: Five darter species including [[Fountain Darter]] (*Etheostoma fonticola*), [[Maryland Darter]] (*Etheostoma sellare*)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listed fish species occupy diverse aquatic habitats across North America and globally. Western United States desert springs and streams harbor numerous endemic cyprinodontids adapted to isolated thermal and mineral springs in [[Nevada]], [[California]], [[Texas]], and [[Arizona]]. These include multiple *Cyprinodon* pupfish species and *Empetrichthys* killifish restricted to single spring systems like [[Devil's Hole]].

Large river systems support endangered cyprinids and catostomids: [[Colorado River]] system species include [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]] (*Gila robusta jordani*), [[Colorado River Squawfish]], and [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopherus argentissimus*). The [[Cui-ui]] inhabits [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. Anadromous [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] ranges along Atlantic coastal rivers from Canada to USA.

Cold-water salmonids occupy western mountain streams: [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], and three subspecies of cutthroat trout (*Salmo clarki*) represent isolated populations in [[Arizona]], [[New Mexico]], [[Colorado]], [[California]], and [[Nevada]]. The [[Longjaw Cisco]] (*Coregonus alpenae*) inhabited [[Lakes Michigan]], [[Huron]], and [[Erie]].

International species include Asian catfishes from [[Thailand]] (*Pangasius sanitwongsei*, *Pangasianodon gigas*), Japanese endemics (*Hymenophysa curta*, *Tanakia tanago*, *Coreobagrus ichikawai*), and Turkish species (*Salmo platycephalus*, *Acanthorutilus handlirschi*).

## Key Findings

**Legislative Framework**: The Endangered Species Act of 1973 prohibits import, export, interstate commerce, harassment, harm, or capture of listed species within U.S. jurisdiction and territorial seas. Restrictions apply to live/dead specimens, parts, and products. Two permit types allow exceptions: (1) Economic Hardship permits (one-year grace period for existing businesses); (2) Scientific or Propagation permits for qualified researchers demonstrating valid conservation purposes.

**Fish Species Documentation**: The list documents 33 endangered bony fish species with official Federal Register publication dates (footnoted as dates in March 1967, October 1970, December 1970, March 1972, and June 1973). Each entry provides scientific binomial/trinomial nomenclature, common name, and geographic distribution.

**Geographic Patterns**: North American freshwater fishes dominate the list, with concentrations in:
- Western desert springs (Nevada, California): 8 cyprinodontid species
- Colorado River basin: 4 large-river cyprinids
- Western mountain streams: 7 salmonid subspecies
- Southeastern USA: 5 percid darters, 1 gambusia

**Taxonomic Coverage**: Beyond fishes, the complete list encompasses 226+ animal species across all vertebrate classes plus invertebrates (1 gastropod snail). Mammals represent the largest group with extensive Australian marsupials, primates, carnivores, and cetaceans. Birds include 108 species. The document demonstrates comprehensive ecosystem-level conservation approach mandated by the 1973 Act.

**Administrative Information**: Contact information provided for Office of Endangered Species (202/343-5687) and Division of Law Enforcement (202/343-9242). Complete regulations available in Code of Federal Regulations Title 50, Chapter I, Subchapter B, with updates published in Federal Register.
