## Executive Summary

This memo reports the results of a fish sampling survey conducted by Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies at the ASU Karsten Golf Course (ASUGC) lakes on 1–2 December 1994. The survey's primary objective was to assess the status of three federally significant native fish species — [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — that had been stocked into the ASUGC North and South Lakes in 1988 (all three species) and again in 1991 (bonytail only). All stocked fish originated from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery And Technology Center]] in Dexter, New Mexico, operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

Despite targeted sampling effort, no native species were captured. The total catch (22 individuals) consisted entirely of five non-native species. Catch numbers were notably low compared to historical ASUGC surveys, and the cause of this decline was unknown at the time of reporting. The authors hypothesize that native species likely remain present in the lakes but were simply not captured due to overall reduced catch efficiency during this survey event.

## Background & Description

The memo is administrative/survey-focused and does not provide morphological descriptions of individual specimens. However, taxonomic identity of all captured non-native species was confirmed to species level:

- **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*) — Centrarchidae

No length, weight, or age-class data were reported in the source text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Artificial lentic (lake) habitat associated with a golf course, representing a managed refugial population site rather than natural riverine habitat.
- **Stocked native species:** [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] are all Colorado River basin natives listed as endangered; ASUGC lakes were apparently used as a conservation stocking/refuge site, consistent with efforts by the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery And Technology Center]] to establish captive/refuge populations outside the main river system.
- **Non-native assemblage:** The captured community (threadfin shad, carp, yellow bullhead, yellow bass, green sunfish) reflects a typical warm-water, human-altered lentic fish assemblage common in the southwestern U.S., often established via unauthorized introductions or incidental stocking.
- **Community interactions:** The coexistence of predatory/competitive non-native species (e.g., green sunfish, yellow bass, carp) alongside stocked endangered natives raises ecological concern regarding predation and competition, though this is not explicitly discussed in the source.
- **Sampling gear ecological implications:** Use of trammel nets (300 ft) and experimental gill nets (125 ft) targets a range of body sizes and species, set overnight (12-hour soak) to maximize capture probability in lentic habitat.

## Key Findings

- Total catch: **22 individuals**, composed of **5 non-native species**; **zero native species** (bonytail, Colorado squawfish, razorback sucker) were captured.
- Catch distribution: **9 individuals in North Lake**, **13 individuals in South Lake** (100% non-native in both).
- Catch levels were **notably reduced** compared to prior ASUGC surveys; cause undetermined.
- Researchers infer native species **likely persist** in the lakes despite non-detection, attributing the absence to low overall catch efficiency rather than extirpation.
- Recommendation made to **increase sampling effort** in the next scheduled survey (spring, per memo—though noted inconsistently as both "1994" and implied "1995").
- This survey represents part of an ongoing **monitoring program** for endangered Colorado River fish species maintained in a refuge population setting at [[ASU Karsten Golf Course]].
