# Central Arizona Project Fish Monitoring: Summary of Fish Surveys, 1997–1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #canal-fish-community

## Executive Summary

This report summarizes fish surveys conducted in October 1997 and January 1998 on canal systems in central Arizona. The work covered [[Salt River Project]] canals (the [[South Canal]] and [[Arizona Canal]]) and the [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]], a [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] facility. Sampling was carried out during routine dry-up periods by [[Arizona State University]] personnel under a cooperative agreement with the Bureau of Reclamation. Field crews used seines, trammel and gill nets, hoop nets, dip nets, and visual observation at multiple stations. Species were recorded by code, and counts were separated into juvenile and adult classes. A non-native species was found in an area where it had not previously been recorded. No publications resulted from the work, and no voucher specimens were submitted to the Bureau of Reclamation.

## Background & Description

The survey was submitted by Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Department of Biology, Tempe) on 30 April 1998, as a partial fulfilment of Cooperative Agreement Number 1425-97-FC-32-00780. The report was addressed to Robert W. Clarkson of the Bureau of Reclamation's Phoenix Area Office. It is part of the [[Central Arizona Project]] fish monitoring program.

Field work was carried out by Brian E. Bagley, Paul C. Marsh, Robert W. Clarkson, Terry C. Inman, and Patricia A. Sowka (ASU), with the Bureau of Reclamation participating in some expeditions. Sampling data were entered and verified using KeyEntry software ([[Southern Computer Systems]]). Tables summarize sampling stations, reaches, gear, effort, and species counts by juvenile and adult class.

Access to the canal properties was secured through legal right-of-entry, and a collection permit was issued by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The surveyed canals are managed water-conveyance systems. Sampling was timed to dry-up periods, when water levels are low enough to capture fish in the reaches. Stations were located relative to engineered features, including the [[Electric Fish Barrier]] on the South Canal and the Arizona Canal, the [[Granite Reef Dam]], the [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]], the [[China Wash Barrier]], and various diversions and turnouts such as the [[Pima Lateral]] and the RWCD turnout.

Sampling gear varied by reach and included:
- Straight seines (multiple mesh sizes, 10 × 4 to 10 × 6 ft)
- Bag seines (30 to 35 ft)
- Trammel nets (75 to 90 ft)
- Experimental gill nets (125 ft)
- Hoop nets (6 to 8 ft)
- Dip nets
- Visual observation, used where netting was not practical

Effort was recorded as hauls, hours, or hoops set. Visual observations were not quantified as hauls, and several counts from these were recorded as approximate ("many," "some").

## Key Findings

**Survey scope and accomplishments**
- Annual monitoring was completed on identified reaches of the South Canal (October 1997) and the Florence-Casa Grande Canal (October 1997), plus the Arizona Canal (January 1998).
- Field expeditions covered several reaches on 25–28 October 1997 and 12 January 1998.
- A non-native fish species was found by ASU personnel in an area where it had not previously been recorded (Section A.3.3). The species is not named in the source text.
- All monitoring data were entered and verified; a hard copy and data sheets were delivered to the Bureau of Reclamation.

**South Canal, 25 October 1997 (Table 1)**
- Below the electric fish barrier, bag seine and dip net sampling yielded 202 fish. Species PACL made up 172 (85%) of these.
- At 2.5 miles below the barrier, trammel net sampling (3 hours) yielded 17 fish.
- Experimental gill net sampling (2.5 hours) at the same station yielded 68 fish. The most common species was MISA, with 48 (71%) individuals.
- At 4 miles below the barrier, seine and dip net sampling yielded 8 fish. Species PYOL made up 5 (63%) of these.
- At 9 miles below the barrier, seine sampling was dominated by CYLU, present in numbers too great to count. Totals were not tabulated.

**Florence-Casa Grande Canal, 27–28 October 1997 (Table 2)**
- At Ashurst-Hayden Dam, dip net sampling yielded 34 fish. CYLU made up the largest share, with 21 (62%) adults.
- At 2.6 miles below the dam (China Wash Barrier), straight seine sampling (15 hauls) yielded 126 fish. Species CYLU made up 118 (94%).
- At 14.5 miles below the dam, seine sampling yielded 31 fish, with CYLU at 15 (48%) and ICPU at 13 (42%).
- At 15.2 miles below the dam, hoop net sampling (5.5 total hours) yielded a small catch, with AMNA as the only species recorded.
- At the Pima Lateral diversion, seine sampling (10 hauls) yielded 108 fish across species. Experimental gill net sampling (2 hours) yielded 46 fish, mostly CYLU and GAAF.
- On 18 October 1997, hoop net sampling at the Pima Lateral (4 total hours) and seine sampling at the Pima Lateral Feeder Canal (2 hauls) yielded 4 and 31 fish, respectively.

**Arizona Canal, 12 January 1998 (Table 3)**
- Electric barrier station: seine sampling (3 hauls) yielded 15 fish.
- At 0.5 miles below Granite Reef Dam, trammel net sampling (5 hours) yielded 3 fish.
- At 0.6 miles below the dam, seine sampling (2 hauls) yielded 287 fish. Species PACL made up 252 (87%).
- At 0.9 miles below the dam, seine sampling (2 hauls) yielded 186 fish. Species PACL made up 166 (89%).
- At 2.8 miles below the dam, visual observation recorded about 50 PACL.
- At 3.2 miles below the dam, visual observation recorded about 200 CYLU.
- At 4.3 miles below the dam (Beeline Highway), seine sampling (5 hauls) yielded 251 fish.
- At 4.8 miles below the dam, visual observation recorded about 60 fish across three species.
- At 5.0 miles below the dam, seine sampling (1 haul) yielded 16 fish.
- At 6.9 miles below the dam, visual observation recorded 54 individuals of one species.
- At 8.2 miles below the dam, visual observation recorded about 104 fish, mostly unidentified and large in number.
- At the Evergreen Drain (8.2 miles), seine sampling (2 hauls) yielded 248 fish, with PACL at 207 (83%). Hoop net sampling and observation recorded about 20 fish.
- At 14.2 miles below the dam (Indian Bend Wash), trammel net sampling (0.5 hours) recorded no fish caught or seen.

**Protocol deviations and outputs**
- No voucher specimens were submitted to the Bureau of Reclamation, contrary to protocol.
- No publications have come from this research.
