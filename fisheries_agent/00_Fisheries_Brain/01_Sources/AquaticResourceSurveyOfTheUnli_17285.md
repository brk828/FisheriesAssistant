## Executive Summary

This 1981 baseline report characterizes the aquatic resources of the unlined [[Coachella Canal]] in southeastern California prior to its partial lining as part of the Colorado River Salinity Control Project. Despite the stated objective of inventorying "fisheries and invertebrate populations," **no fish data were collected or reported**; the study instead documents physical-chemical water quality, phytoplankton, zooplankton, macrophytes, and benthic macroinvertebrates at two stations (Station A near Checkdrop 5, Station B between Siphons 2 and 3) during a scheduled water outage/dewatering event (10–11 November 1980). The canal, fed by the [[Colorado River]] via the All-American Canal, exhibited water quality similar to the lower Colorado River system, with supersaturated dissolved oxygen, low nutrient concentrations, and phosphorus-limited conditions. Benthic invertebrate and macrophyte communities were most productive in vegetated, sand-silt habitats (Potamogeton beds, Phragmites stands), while mid-channel and gravel-silt habitats supported far lower diversity and biomass. The authors predict that canal lining will reduce macrophyte-associated habitat but may enhance periphyton- and plankton-based production due to increased substrate availability and water clarity.

## Background & Description

Not applicable in the traditional ichthyological sense, as no fish specimens were collected or described. Morphological/taxonomic identification in this study was instead applied to:

- **Phytoplankton**: 18 genera identified, dominated by [[Bacillariophyta]] (diatoms, 12 genera). *Cyclotella glomerata* was numerically dominant at both stations.
- **Zooplankton**: Depauperate assemblage — protozoan *Centropyxis aculeata*, rotifer *Euchlanis* sp. (dominant), and developmental stages of cyclopoid and harpacticoid copepods.
- **Macrophytes**: Two submergent species — [[Sago Pondweed]] (*Potamogeton pectinatus*) and [[Water-Milfoil]] (*Myriophyllum exalbescens*); emergent stands of *Phragmites* and *Typha* along banks.
- **Benthic Invertebrates**: 18 taxa identified (Table 3), spanning Turbellaria, Nematoda, Nematomorpha (*Paragordius* sp.), Oligochaeta, Amphipoda (*Hyalella azteca*), Decapoda (*Procambarus clarkii*), Hydracarina (*Lebertia* sp.), Pelecypoda (*Corbicula manilensis*, the Asiatic clam), and Insecta (Ephemeroptera, Odonata, Trichoptera — *Smicridea* sp., *Nectopsyche* sp. — and Diptera/Chironomidae: *Chironomus*, *Cricotopus*, *Cryptochironomus*, *Thienemanniella*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Coachella Canal]] functions as a lotic (flowing-water), slow-current freshwater habitat analogous to a large lowland river system, drawing physicochemical similarity to the lower [[Colorado River]]. Habitat heterogeneity strongly structured community composition:

- **Vegetated sand-silt habitat (Station A, above checkdrop)**: Potamogeton beds supported the highest invertebrate diversity (8 taxa) and biomass, dominated by *Corbicula manilensis* and oligochaetes, with notable Turbellaria, two Trichoptera species, and chironomids.
- **Gravel-silt mid-channel habitat (Station A, below checkdrop)**: Atypical, sparsely vegetated; supported only *Corbicula*, chironomids, and oligochaetes at low densities — consistent with the generally low benthic production typical of large river mid-channels ([[Hynes 1970]]).
- **Detritus-silt habitat adjacent to Phragmites stand (Station B)**: Dominated by insects (four orders), especially chironomids (most numerous and diverse group), with low numbers of *Corbicula* and oligochaetes.
- **Plankton community**: Nutrient-limited (N:P ratios >20 indicating phosphorus limitation per [[Schindler 1977]] criteria), with rotifers and diatoms dominating — a pattern typical of slow-moving waters ([[Hynes 1970]]). Downstream increases in phytoplankton density (particularly *Cyclotella glomerata*, a ~10-fold increase from Station A to B) coincided with nutrient drawdown, suggesting active downstream primary production and nutrient uptake by algae/macrophytes.

The study frames canal lining as an ecological disturbance: loss of soft sand-silt substrate will likely eliminate *Potamogeton* and *Phragmites* habitats (the most productive benthic zones), while concrete substrate may instead favor periphyton growth — as observed by [[Marsh 1980]], who documented dense periphyton-associated populations of the lepidopteran *Parargyractis confusalis* at Checkdrop 5 (not detected in this study's samples). Increased water clarity from lining could also enhance planktonic primary production despite persistently low nutrient concentrations.

## Key Findings

- **No fish were surveyed or reported**, despite the project's stated fisheries objective — a notable gap between stated aims and delivered data.
- Water quality (temperature ~19.7–19.8°C, DO 9.7–10.1 mg/L [supersaturated], pH 8.3, conductivity ~1220–1230 µmhos/cm) closely resembled the lower [[Colorado River]] system.
- Nutrient levels were low (Total P: 12.4–20.2 µg/L; Total N: 360–408 µg/L) and phosphorus-limited (N:P ratios 20.2–29.0), per [[Vollenweider 1970]] and [[Schindler 1977]] criteria.
- All phosphorus and nitrogen fractions declined from Station A to Station B, likely reflecting biological uptake and higher downstream productivity.
- Phytoplankton was dominated by diatoms (12 of 18 genera); *Cyclotella glomerata* increased roughly tenfold from Station A (278 #/mL) to Station B (2372 #/mL).
- Zooplankton community was extremely depauperate (only 3 taxa/groups detected), dominated by the rotifer *Euchlanis* sp.
- Submergent macrophyte cover (*Potamogeton*, *Myriophyllum*) covered <10% of canal area at Station A post-dewatering; emergent *Phragmites*/*Typha* formed dense bank stands.
- 18 benthic invertebrate taxa were recorded, broadly consistent with a concurrent survey by [[Marsh 1980]], except that Marsh's periphyton-associated caddisfly-like lepidopteran *Parargyractis confusalis* was absent from this study's collections.
- Vegetated, side-channel habitats (Potamogeton beds, Phragmites stands) supported markedly higher invertebrate abundance and diversity than open mid-channel or atypical gravel-silt substrates.
- The Asiatic clam *[[Corbicula manilensis]]* was a dominant and widespread benthic taxon across nearly all sampled habitats, including depauperate mid-channel zones.
- Authors predict canal lining will reduce macrophyte-dependent habitat/production but may promote periphyton- and plankton-based productivity via new hard substrate and improved water clarity; they recommend a follow-up survey of the lined canal for comparative assessment.
