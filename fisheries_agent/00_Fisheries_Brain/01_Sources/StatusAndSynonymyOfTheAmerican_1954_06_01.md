# Status and Synonymy of the American Percid Fish *Hadropterus scierus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #freshwater-biology #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1954 taxonomic revision by Hubbs and Black resolves long-standing nomenclatural confusion surrounding the darter **Hadropterus scierus** Swain, 1883 (the "Dusky Darter"), now placed in the genus [[Percina]]. The authors critically re-examine three previously named taxa: *H. scierus* itself, the putative subspecies *H. scierus serrula* Jordan and Gilbert, 1886, and the nominal species *Hadropterus maxinkuckiensis* Evermann, 1900. Using morphological character analysis (preopercle serration, gill-membrane union, scale counts, fin-ray counts), the paper demonstrates that: (1) the serrated preopercle long used to justify generic/subgeneric separation (as genus *Serraria*) is not diagnostic, as it varies ontogenetically and occurs variably in the related [[Hadropterus nigrofasciatus]]; (2) *H. s. serrula* lacks valid diagnostic grounds as a distinct Texas subspecies, though a Guadalupe River system population is recognized as warranting subspecies status (formally described concurrently by Clark Hubbs, 1954); and (3) *H. maxinkuckiensis* is not a relative of *H. scierus* at all, but is a synonym of **Hadropterus maculatus** Girard, based on re-examination of the type specimen. A formal lectotype designation is made for *H. scierus serrula* to stabilize its type locality at the Sabine River, Longview, Texas.

## Background & Description

### Preopercle Serration
- Historically used as the primary diagnostic character separating *H. scierus* (as genus *Serraria*, Gilbert 1884) from [[Hadropterus]].
- **Highly variable**: absent in young in some populations, strengthening with age in others (contra earlier claims that serrations are lost in age).
- Also present, variably, in cotypes and synonyms (*Alvordius spillmani*) of the genotype species *H. nigrofasciatus*, invalidating its use for generic/subgeneric distinction.
- Geographic variation is not consistent north-to-south, but strong local variation occurs within Texas drainages (e.g., stronger serration in Brazos River system populations).

### Gill-Membrane Union
- Degree of union of gill membranes is a "reasonably good" specific-level character distinguishing typical *H. scierus* (more connate) from *H. nigrofasciatus*, but too variable intra- and interspecifically for generic/subgeneric use.
- Quantified via relative measurements following [[Hubbs and Raney 1939]] methodology.

### Fin-Ray Counts (Dorsal + Anal Rays)
Table data comparing total dorsal + anal ray counts:
| Population | Average |
|---|---|
| *H. nigrofasciatus* – Florida | 34.0 |
| *H. nigrofasciatus* – Georgia | 33.5 |
| *H. nigrofasciatus* – Alabama | 34.0 |
| *H. nigrofasciatus* – Mississippi | 34.4 |
| *H. scierus* – Tennessee/N. Alabama | 36.7 |
| *H. scierus* – Texas (N. of Guadalupe R.) | 34.0 |
| *H. scierus* – Guadalupe/San Marcos Rivers | intermediate |

- Typical (Ohio River system) *H. scierus* has, on average, **more dorsal+anal rays** than *H. nigrofasciatus*.
- This distinction **breaks down in Texas**, where the Guadalupe River subspecies shows intermediate counts and other Texas races approach *nigrofasciatus* values.

### Scale Counts (Lateral Line Series)
- Reported ranges across the literature: 62–73 scales depending on locality/author (Table 2 compilation from Swain 1883, Jordan & Gilbert 1886, Jordan 1890, Evermann & Clark 1920, Forbes & Richardson 1909, Jordan & Evermann 1896, Meek 1896).
- Guadalupe River populations average **~68** scales — slightly higher than typical northern *scierus* and higher than other Texas races.
- No strong geographic pattern supports recognizing a distinct "fine-scaled" Texas subspecies overall, except for the Guadalupe form.

### Breast Scalation
- Breast scale coverage ranges from **wholly scaleless to completely scaled**; extreme conditions are rare in all populations.
- Anteromedian breast area typically scaleless; posterior/lateral breast more often scaled.
- Originally used (Jordan & Gilbert 1886) to distinguish Texas "serrula" (naked breast) from Indiana *scierus* (more scaly breast), but shown to overlap extensively.

### Coloration
- Typical *H. scierus*: more blotched, less banded pattern than *H. nigrofasciatus*.
- Texas "serrula" specimens described as paler with more sharply defined markings — attributed by the authors to turbid water conditions at type locality rather than true subspecific distinction (clear-water San Marcos River specimens are boldly marked).
- Degree of fusion of lateral blotches is highly variable with no clear geographic trend.

### Type Specimen Reassessment — *H. maxinkuckiensis*
- Re-examination of the type specimen, original description, and figure reveals it matches **Hadropterus maculatus** Girard, not *H. scierus*:
  - Head, body, fin, and caudal-base coloration matches *H. maculatus*.
  - **Smooth preopercle** and **free gill membranes** (both diagnostic against *scierus*).
  - Slightly larger-than-usual scales, but within known range of variation for *H. maculatus*.
  - Union of dorsal fins and large mouth also consistent with *H. maculatus* variation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Riffle-dwelling percid darter inhabiting clear to turbid freshwater streams and rivers; type locality Bean Blossom Creek, Indiana.
- **Distribution**: Ohio River system (Indiana, Tennessee, northern Alabama) eastward through Illinois, Kentucky, Ohio, Arkansas, Oklahoma, and multiple Texas river systems (Sabine, Trinity, Lampasas, Brazos, San Jacinto, Guadalupe/San Marcos, Nueces).
- **Sympatry**: *H. scierus* and [[Hadropterus nigrofasciatus]] co-occur in parts of the central Gulf lowland; no evidence of intergradation observed, supporting their status as distinct species despite morphological overlap in this region.
- **Microhabitat/water clarity effects**: Coloration pattern (paleness/blotch confluence) appears linked to local water clarity conditions (e.g., turbid Sabine River vs. crystal-clear San Marcos River), suggesting phenotypic plasticity rather than fixed geographic subspecific variation.
- Related species *H. maculatus* (with which *H. maxinkuckiensis* is synonymized) occupies Lake Maxinkuckee, Indiana inlet habitats and shows "much fluctuation in color pattern" that has "defied analysis into subspecies."

## Key Findings

1. **Preopercle serration is not a valid generic/subgeneric character** — it is developmentally and geographically variable and occurs in the type species of [[Hadropterus]] itself, invalidating the genus *Serraria* Gilbert, 1884, as based on this trait.
2. **Gill-membrane union** is a useful but imperfect specific-level character separating *H. scierus* from *H. nigrofasciatus*; the distinction weakens in Texas populations.
3. ***Hadropterus scierus serrula* Jordan and Gilbert, 1886 is invalidated** as a distinct subspecies for most of its originally described range — the diagnostic characters (scale count, coloration, breast scalation) do not hold up under expanded sampling, except for a genuinely distinct population in the **Guadalupe River system**, which is formally recognized as a new subspecies (*apristis*, described concurrently by Clark Hubbs, 1954).
4. **Lectotype designation**: The authors formally designate the syntype series from the **Sabine River at Longview, Texas** (USNM Cat. No. 36481) as the lectotype series for *H. scierus serrula*, correcting earlier erroneous type locality designations (Jordan and Evermann had incorrectly cited "Red River, Fulton, Arkansas").
5. **A historical error is identified and corrected**: Jordan and Evermann's erroneous inclusion of southern Arkansas within the range of *serrula* stemmed from an overlooked page heading in the original 1886 publication.
6. ***Hadropterus maxinkuckiensis* Evermann, 1900 is synonymized with *Hadropterus maculatus* Girard**, not with *H. scierus* as long assumed — a taxonomic correction based on direct type specimen examination (contradicting earlier treatments by Evermann, Blatchley, Schrenkeisen, and Fowler).
7. Potential for further subspecies recognition is flagged for the **Brazos River system**, where preopercular serration appears unusually well-developed, pending additional material and analysis.
8. The paper underscores the broader principle that **rudimentary/serrated structures (like preopercle serration) can represent atavistic or recrudescent ancestral characters** rather than derived diagnostic traits — drawing a comparative parallel to ctenoid scale evolution in the poeciliid genus [[Xenodexia]] and the cyprinodontid genus [[Lamprichthys]].
