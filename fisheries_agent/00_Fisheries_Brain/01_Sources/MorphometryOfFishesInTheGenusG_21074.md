# Colorado River Gila Taxonomy: Morphometric and Biochemical Study Review (1988)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1988 file of correspondence and two draft manuscripts by [[Mark J. Rosenfeld]] on the taxonomy of endangered [[Colorado River Gila]] chubs (*Gila* spp., Cyprinidae) of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The manuscripts apply [[Morphometrics]] (photographic measurements analyzed with [[Principal Components Analysis]]) and [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]] to test whether the morphospecies [[Humpback Chub]] (*G. cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*G. elegans*) and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*G. robusta*) are distinct, and whether hybridization occurs. Reviewers [[W. L. Minckley]] and [[Michael Douglas]] judged the drafts premature and statistically flawed. The [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] circulated the drafts for comment. The authors' conclusions are provisional.

## Background & Description

- **Species complex:** The Upper Basin *Gila* were poorly understood. Original descriptions rested on very few specimens: *G. cypha* on two whole fish and part of another, *G. elegans* on one individual, and *G. robusta* on "several" animals.
- **Subspecies:** *G. robusta* has been split into *G. r. robusta* (wide-ranging), *G. r. seminuda* (endemic to the [[Virgin River]]) and *G. r. jordani* (restricted to the Pluvial White River). Some authors regarded *G. elegans* as a *G. robusta* subspecies until the 1970s.
- **Diagnostic characters:** The authors cited occipital (nuchal) hump shape and median fin ray counts. Hump development may be influenced by environment, and fin ray counts overlap among taxa.
- **Conservation status:** *G. cypha*, *G. elegans*, *G. r. seminuda* and *G. r. jordani* are protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. *G. elegans* was nearly extinct, with fewer than 25 individuals recorded since 1970.
- **Hybridization:** Intermediate "intergrade" fish occur throughout the Upper Basin. They were attributed to human-caused disturbance, but may predate habitat change.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat change:** Reduced abundance was attributed to habitat degradation. Prior distributions are hard to reconstruct because earlier surveys focused on Lower Basin fishes.
- **Habitat and morphology:** The authors suggested that fast-flowing habitat selects for fewer gill rakers, and that lake-dwelling ancestry may select for more. Hump depth may also respond to habitat conditions.
- **Sampling sites:** Specimens came from [[Black Rocks]] and [[Westwater Canyon]] on the mainstem Colorado, several [[Green River]] sites (Whirlpool Canyon, Three Canyon, Coal Creek, Steer Ridge, Flat Canyon), the [[Little Colorado River]] near its mouth (the type locality of *G. cypha*), the Virgin River at Bloomington, Utah, the San Rafael River (Cottonwood Creek), Lake Mojave hatchery stock, and Newark Valley, Nevada.
- **Fish movement:** Fish are known to move between Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon, which are about 15 river miles apart.

## Key Findings

### Reviewer Correspondence (January 1988)

- **Minckley letter (27 January 1988):** Minckley said the drafts were not ready for review. Key analyses were incomplete: size had not been removed from the PCA, and the electrophoretic dataset was not finished. Not all enzymes were assayed in all samples, and [[Little Colorado River]] humpback data were still missing. He was "alarmed" that conclusions were drawn before the evidence was analyzed. He said that the methods (PCA, UPGMA and related techniques) were valid for taxonomy in *Gila*.
- **Douglas comments:** Douglas identified several problems:
  - PCA data were not corrected for size. He called this "entirely inadequate" and said the results were uninterpretable.
  - Discriminant function analysis (DFA) was misspelled and its results were not shown.
  - The term "significant" was used loosely.
  - Promised analyses, including sexual dimorphism, were deferred without reason.
  - The manuscript did not cite Suttkus and Clemmer's extensive data on *G. cypha* from Grand Canyon.
  - He called the approach "analysis by innuendo."
- **Radant memo (7 January 1988):** Randy Radant, Chief of Aquatic Management, asked fish researchers to review the drafts and return comments by 19 February 1988.
- **Rosenfeld cover note:** Rosenfeld acknowledged that the manuscript was incomplete. It lacked the Little Colorado River humpback analyses and the discriminant and single-character statistics.

### Morphometric Study

- **Sample:** 628 Upper Basin *Gila* were examined using photographs of live fish, which avoided sacrifice. These included 149 fish from Black Rocks (USFWS), Utah Division of Wildlife Resources collections from the Green River (1985–1986) and Westwater Canyon (1985–1987), and 73 *G. r. seminuda* from the Virgin River. Lower Basin comparisons were 68 *G. cypha* from the Little Colorado River and 50 hatchery *G. elegans*.
- **Method:** Thirty-three distance measurements were log-transformed. PCA used the STAT-80 FACTOR routine. A loading of ±0.3 was treated as significant.
- **Results:**
  - Axis 1 (about 81% of variance) was a size factor. Standard length loaded 0.97 to 0.99.
  - The first three axes explained about 91% of variance in both analyses.
  - Excluding nuchal hump depth (PCA-NOHUMP) gave essentially the same significant measures.
- **Species separation:** Compared with *G. r. robusta*, *G. cypha* had a shorter, more depressed head, a deeper mid-body and a relatively larger mouth. Black Rocks fish clustered into a *G. r. robusta* group and a "*G. cypha*–intergrade" group, with a gap between them. Westwater Canyon fish mostly fell in the *G. cypha*–intergrade category. Green River fish were mostly *G. cypha*. Whirlpool Canyon fish were all *G. robusta*. Coal Creek fish were *G. cypha*.
- **Weaknesses:** The authors noted that sexes were not recorded, meristic counts were not used, and DFA equations may not capture the full range of variation.

### Biochemical (Allozyme) Study

- **Sample:** 131 Upper Basin fish were analyzed, with sample sizes ranging from 3 to 29 per population. Each population was treated as an operational taxonomic unit (OTU).
- **Loci:** Of 24 presumptive loci, 9 were monomorphic and 15 were polymorphic.
- **Variation:** Polymorphism in Colorado River *Gila* ranged from 20% to 40%. *G. bicolor newarkensis* was 7% polymorphic. Heterozygosity was highest in the San Rafael River *G. r. robusta* (0.10) and lowest in Westwater Canyon *G. cypha* (0.02).
- **Phenetic clustering (UPGMA, Nei 1978 distance):**
  - *G. bicolor* separated from Colorado River *Gila* at 0.54 units (Analysis I), 0.14 (Analysis II) and 0.44 (Analysis III).
  - Colorado River OTUs were closely related, with clusters often separated by only 0.02–0.10 units.
  - Dexter hybrids (F2 backcross stock, n = 3) clustered with Little Colorado River *G. cypha*.
- **Diagnostic alleles (suggested):** Acon-A-b and PEP-A-b (*G. r. robusta*), Cabp-b (*G. robusta*), Pgm-A-c (*G. elegans*). These are considered tentative and require multi-locus support.
- **Westwater Pgm-A anomaly:** Westwater *G. cypha* and *G. r. robusta* lacked Pgm-A-a and were nearly fixed for Pgm-A-b. The authors suggested possible selection.
- **Hybridization evidence:** Westwater *G. cypha* and *G. r. robusta* showed minimal muscle-protein differentiation, which may indicate extensive hybridization. Cytogenetic data (unpublished) showed heteromorphic nucleolar organizing regions in sympatric populations, which suggests hybridization.

### Stated Conclusions (Morphometric Study)

1. *G. r. robusta* is a morphologically distinct subspecies.
2. Most intergrades and *G. cypha* belong to one species, distinguishable from *G. r. robusta* in the mainstem Colorado and Green rivers.
3. Green River fish were mostly *G. cypha*.
4. Westwater Canyon and Black Rocks are frequented by *G. cypha* and *G. r. robusta* from the same or closely related gene pools.
5. Hybridization between *G. cypha* and *G. r. robusta* may be rare or nonexistent.
6. A hybrid origin for *G. r. seminuda* is not indicated.
7. Further resolution may come from combining morphometric and meristic data, plus biochemical markers.

### Proposed Future Work

- **Mitochondrial DNA:** The authors proposed mtDNA restriction-fragment analysis to test species status. They noted that mtDNA evolves five to ten times faster than amino acid substitutions in proteins. At least three populations would be needed: allopatric *G. r. robusta* (e.g., San Rafael River), *G. cypha* (Little Colorado River) and *G. elegans* (Lake Mojave).
- **Expanded electrophoresis:** Tissues from muscle, liver, eye, heart, kidney and spleen would be sampled to assay 50 or more loci.
- **Sympatric surveys:** Black Rocks, Westwater Canyon and Desolation Canyon on the Green River would be sampled to assess hybridization.
