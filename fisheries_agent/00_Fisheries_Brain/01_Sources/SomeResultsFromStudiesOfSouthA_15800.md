# Some Results from Studies of South American Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This Indiana University Studies monograph (No. 20, March 1914, by [[Carl H. Eigenmann]]) reports taxonomic and evolutionary results from large South American freshwater fish collections, mainly from Colombia and Ecuador and from Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay. It has three parts: a discussion of repeated (convergent) character evolution in the [[Characidae]], a description of the new subfamily [[Glandulocaudinae]] with its strong sexual dimorphism, and a comparison of the faunas of transandean Colombia and southeastern tropical America. A final section describes new genera and species. The author argues that many diagnostic characters arose independently several times, so many genera are polyphyletic.

## Background & Description

- **Collections:** Fishes from Harvard University (collected by Louis Agassiz in Brazil) and the [[Carnegie Museum]] of Pittsburgh (largely gathered by John Haseman), plus Colombian and Ecuadorian material collected under Eigenmann's direction.
- **Context of the monograph:** Eigenmann's *American Characidae* volume recognizes 53 genera and 275 species. Of these, 33 genera and 171 species were described during its preparation, many by Eigenmann or his students, chiefly Marion Durbin Ellis.
- **Historical baseline:** Günther's 1864 review included 7 genera and 43 species of the relevant groups. Within the South American Tetragonopterinae, Günther recognized 5 genera and 37 species.
- **Subfamilies discussed:**
  - [[Tetragonopterinae]]: small, scaled characids with differentiated premaxillary teeth; the dominant tropical American group, ranging from the United States to Patagonia.
  - [[Rhoadsiinae]]: two genera and three species from Transandean Ecuador, Colombia and Panama. Adults have enlarged, thorn-like jaw teeth; juveniles resemble the minute genus *Cheirodon*.
  - [[Iguanodectinae]]: a few species from the Essequibo to Paraguay, with united gill membranes and broad multicuspid incisors.
  - [[Stethaprioninae]]: deep, compressed fishes with a procumbent predorsal spine, which the author considers a poor indicator of common ancestry.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The Tetragonopterinae are described as abundant in individuals and species and as occupying the widest range of environments in tropical America.
- Glandulocaudine distribution is disjunct. Genera such as [[Pterobrycon]], [[Gephyrocharax]] and [[Stevardia]] occur in northwestern South America west of the Eastern Cordilleras of Colombia. Other genera occur in southeastern Brazil and Uruguay. The subfamily is not known from the Amazon basin.
- Some species have very restricted ranges. Four *Gephyrocharax* species occupy contiguous territory in Colombia and Panama, and three *Glandulocauda* species are each found in a different river of southeastern Brazil. Exceptions include [[Coelurichthys]] iporangae and possibly *Pseudocorynopoma doriae*, which have wider ranges.
- The author notes that a widely distributed species can be stable across most of its range but unstable at a particular locality, which suggests local environmental or selective pressures.

## Key Findings

**1. Convergent character evolution is widespread.** The same characters arose independently many times in different lineages. Examples include:
- Lateral line becoming incomplete, the most frequently changed character.
- Tooth rows changing from two series to three, or from three to two.
- Maxillary teeth developing along the entire margin, or disappearing.
- Caudal fin becoming scaled.
- Adipose fin disappearing in several lineages.
- Conical teeth appearing behind the front tooth series.

**2. Many genera are polyphyletic.** Because characters are repeatedly and independently acquired, genus definitions often list shared derived traits rather than the traits of a single ancestor. Species of different genera in one isolated area can therefore be more closely related to each other than to their own genus-mates elsewhere. For example, *Astyanax feste* and *Bryconamericus peruanus* from the Pacific slope of Ecuador are more closely related than *feste* is to *Astyanax anterior* of the upper Amazon. *Bryconamericus*, *Hemibrycon*, *Deuterodon* and the larger genera are considered probably polyphyletic.

**3. Lateral line case studies.**
- [[Ctenobrycon hauxwellianus]]: nearly 1,400 specimens from the Amazon between Montalegre and Tabatinga all had a complete lateral line. Of 19 specimens from the Lagoa de Parnagua (Paranahyba basin), 7 were complete, 6 were mutilated, and 6 "stuttered" (scales without pores). A stuttering line is characteristic of *Psellogrammus* of the Paraguay basin, so the species is effectively transitioning in the Paranahyba basin.
- [[Phenacogaster]]: complete lateral line in nearly all specimens from Guiana to the Beni, but permanently reduced to 8 scales in a closely allied form of the upper Paraguay basin, which was placed in the genus *Vesicatrus*.

**4. Caudal and maxillary changes.** The naked caudal is treated as the primitive state. A scaled caudal appears to be developing now in *Astyanax* and in some small *Hyphessobrycon*. Maxillary teeth along the whole margin are seen in *Astyanax nicaraguensis* (up to 11 teeth).

**5. Adipose fin loss.** Losses of the adipose fin produced several genera, including *Hasemania* (from *Hyphessobrycon*), *Lebiasina* (from *Piabucina*), *Carnegiella* (from *Gasteropelecus*) and *Nematobrycon*. Among *Lebiasina* from Ecuador, some individuals still have an adipose fin, while others do not. The genera intercross in northwestern Colombia.

**6. Glandulocaudinae: a new subfamily.** The subfamily is defined by two notched premaxillary tooth series, a single mandibular and maxillary series, and an oblique mouth with large pectorals. Its most notable feature is sexual dimorphism: males of most species have modified fins, opercles, scales or a caudal glandular pouch. The author also notes that similar outcomes can arise through different means, such as a prolonged opercle in *Stevardia*, a prolonged scale in *Pterobrycon*, and pectorals tipped with black. The subfamily is key-separated into nine genera:
- *Stevardia*, *Diapoma*, *Pterobrycon*, *Pseudocorynopoma*, *Hysteronotus*, *Gephyrocharax*, *Microbrycon*, *Glandulocauda*, and *Coelurichthys*.

It has 16 numbered species, including *Stevardia albipinnis* (originally described by Gill in 1858 from Trinidad as three genera representing male, female and young).

**7. Faunal resemblance.** Transandean Colombia and southeastern tropical South America share genera or related pairs such as *Salminus* (one species west of the Eastern Cordilleras, one in the La Plata basin) and *Pseudochalceus* (western Ecuador) with *Hollandichthys* (southeastern Brazil). *Bryconamericus* is most diverse along the Ecuadorian and Colombian Cordilleras and in the La Plata basin. The author also reports that several types absent from northwestern Colombia are absent from southeastern Brazil to Buenos Aires.

**8. New taxa described.** The paper describes eight new species, one new variety and one new genus:
- *Stevardia aliata* (Glandulocaudinae), from Colombian rivers.
- *Pseudocorynopoma heterandria*, from Brazil; 3 males and 2 females (paratypes) plus a holotype female of 80 mm.
- *Bleptonema paraguayensis* and *B. amazone*, from Corumbá and Santarém.
- *Parecbasis cyclolepis* (gen. nov., sp. nov.), from the Rio Madeira area.
- *Fowlerina franciscensis*, from the São Francisco basin; first collected by Hartt in 1867.
- *Agoniates anchovia*, from Villa Bella.
- *Astyanax scabripinnis paranae* (var. nov.), with 229 specimens from one Iguaçu lot.
- *Pygidium hasemani*, a small catfish from Santarém.

**9. Conclusions.** The author concludes that:
- Evolution is currently active in many species, which are unstable in some areas.
- Insignificant characters, such as caudal scaling, can indicate relationship better than conspicuous organs with selective value.
- Members of a long-established genus sometimes revert to the characters of their ancestral genus, and members of one brood can belong to different recognized genera.
