# Intralacustrine Speciation in Lake Titicaca and Lake Lanao

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 German-language paper by [[Curt Kosswig]] and [[Wolfgang Villwock]] (Zoologisches Institut, Hamburg) examines whether two young, morphologically diverse fish assemblages, the *Orestias* of [[Lake Titicaca]] and the cyprinids of [[Lake Lanao]] on [[Mindanao]], arose through speciation inside the lakes ("intralacustrine speciation"). The authors conclude that the Titicaca *Orestias* radiation may partly reflect intralacustrine processes but also long-term allopatric isolation on the [[Altiplano]]. For Lake Lanao they reject in-lake speciation as the main explanation: the lake is too young and small, and the cyprinids reached Mindanao from several separate ancestors via glacial land bridges.

## Background & Description

**Theoretical framework.** The authors outline two views on speciation in large lakes. One holds that spatial isolation of populations, followed by accumulation of genetic differences, drives the formation of sister species. The other holds that biological, ecological or ethological isolation (for example, differing spawning sites and times) is decisive. They cite [[Lake Ohrid]], where pigmentless forms are apparently forming in deep water, and [[Lake Baikal]], where many fish and crustacean groups have diverged. In [[Salmonidae]] of postglacial northern-hemisphere lakes, populations can differ in spawning sites and times while remaining potentially interfertile. In [[Cichlidae]], genetically based [[Preferential Mating]] could split a "species" into reproductively isolated subgroups. A single mutation affecting a behavioural trait could thus be the decisive step, with lake age mattering mainly because it allows repeated isolation events and greater morphological divergence over time.

**Titicaca and Lanao.** Both lakes had been described in the literature as examples of intralacustrine speciation. The authors question this for both.

**Lake Titicaca and the Altiplano.** The genus *Orestias* was said to form a species swarm of about 20 species (Tschernavin 1944). Villwock later reduced the number to about 12. The lake itself is young, formed at the end of the Pliocene or early Pleistocene (Moon 1939). The [[Altiplano]] is much older. In the Miocene it lay near 600 m elevation in a tropical to subtropical climate, with faunal links to the [[Amazon Basin]]. Uplift with the [[Andes Mountains]] raised it to 3800–4000 m and cooled it, eliminating much of the older fauna. Cold-resistant survivors colonised the vacated niches. The Altiplano carried a network of interconnected waters that later separated. Today, part of the drainage flows to [[Lake Poopo]] and part to the Atlantic via the Amazon. *Orestias* occurs throughout these basins. The authors estimate about 15–25 million years for the genus to diversify across an area of roughly 300,000 km². They suggest that most diversification was driven by spatial isolation, with secondary contacts after separation.

**Lake Lanao.** The lake covers about 300 km² and reaches depths of roughly 100 m. Depths of 300 m reported by Wood (1963) are considered doubtful. It was dammed by a lava flow only about 10,000 years ago. Even if it is older, the cyprinid radiation could only have arisen in the Pleistocene, when glacial sea-level lowering linked Mindanao with neighbouring islands and Borneo. The Lanao assemblage comprises about 20 cyprinid species in four genera, three of them endemic. Other fishes include the introduced [[Tilapia]], [[Clarias]], [[Channa]], [[Anabas]], and [[Cyprinus carpio]]. An attempt to introduce [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus*) failed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lanao morphotypes.** Herre (1924) described many cyprinid species from the lake and placed some in separate genera: *Mandibularca*, *Ospatulus*, and later *Spratellicypris* (Herre & Myers 1931). The authors group the species into five types:

1. **Generalised type:** *Barbodes binotatus* and *B. tumba*, with specialised-jaw forms (*Mandibularca*, *Ospatulus*) and *B. clemensi*.
2. **Pelagic species** with terminal to superior mouths, including *Spratellicypris*, *B. lindog*, and *Hampala* sp. *Barbodes manalalk* (to 25 cm) is considered a predatory species of open water.
3. **Coarse-scaled, high-backed forms**, some dark-coloured, possibly related to *Ospatulus* or *Cephalocompsus*.
4. **Deep-water forms**, similar in shape to *B. tumba* but reddish-silver to mostly red.
5. **Dwarfs**, sexually mature at 3.5 cm body length, known only from the southern shore.

The jaw specialisations of *Mandibularca* and *Ospatulus* are considered less functionally distinct than specialisations in genera such as *Gyrinocheilus* and *Garra*. The group 2 and 3 forms differ more strongly in habitus from *B. binotatus* than the Herre genera suggest.

**Dapao comparison.** *Lake Dapao* lies about 400 m higher than Lanao and has no connection to it. At least three Lanao species also live there, including *B. tumba* and *B. baoulan* (a red deep-water form). The authors add *B. clemensi*. A claim by Wood (1963) about a 30 cm *Spratellicypris* in Dapao is considered unfounded.

**Colonisation history.** The authors consider it likely that at least 5–6 different *Barbodes* ancestors colonised Mindanao, rather than one. Glacial land bridges linked Mindanao to the Sulu Archipelago, Palawan, Mindoro, and Borneo. Populations from several glacial periods may have arrived with differing gene pools, which could explain the high variability.

## Key Findings

- **Titicaca:** *Orestias* diversification may have been driven partly by spatial isolation across the Altiplano over 15–25 million years, not solely by processes inside the lake. Villwock's count of about 12 species (down from Tschernavin's roughly 20) is used. Lake Titicaca (around 8000 km²) is a collection basin for previously isolated species as well as a site of possible in-lake speciation.
- **Lanao:** The lake's cyprinid diversity is judged too young and too small, and its habitat zonation too weak, for in-lake speciation to explain it. The authors call the proposal "unlikely" or "refuted" and argue that the cyprinids originated in several separate ancestors that were already present in Mindanao's waters before the lake formed.
- **Unresolved questions:** The origin of similar species within each Herre morphotype remains unknown. Many of Herre's species may be extreme variants of a single reproductive community. Whether this variation came from multiple invasions with hybridisation, or from biological isolation similar to the salmonid type, requires several years of variation-statistical studies by the newly founded University of Mindanao.
- **Broader implication:** Intralacustrine speciation is possible in principle, as in Lakes Ohrid and Baikal, but young and small lakes such as Titicaca and Lanao may not be convincing examples. Multiple colonisation during glacial periods can account for much of the diversity.
