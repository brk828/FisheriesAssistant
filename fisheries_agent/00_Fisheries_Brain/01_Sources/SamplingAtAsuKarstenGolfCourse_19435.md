# Sampling Assessment of Native Fishes at ASU Karsten Golf Course, April 1989

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #native-fish-recovery

## Executive Summary

This report documents sampling conducted on 17-18 and 25 April 1989 to assess the status of three native [[Colorado River]] fish species stocked into water features at [[Arizona State University]] Karsten Golf Course (ASUGC). Approximately 1500 juvenile [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) were stocked into North Pond (7 September 1988), 1500 [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) into South Pond (13 October 1988), and 2000 juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) into South Pond (24 October 1988). Sampling employed trammel and experimental gill nets (270 linear meters total) and seine hauls (45 total). Results showed excellent survival and growth rates for all three native species, substantially exceeding growth observed at other semi-natural rearing facilities. Non-native species were present but at relatively low densities compared to other area waters.

## Background & Description

The study was conducted by personnel from the Center for Environmental Studies at Arizona State University, with support from [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], Resort Management of America, and Arizona State University Sun Angel Foundation. The golf course ponds are supplied by the [[Salt River Project]] canal system.

**Sampling Methods:**
- Trammel and experimental gill nets set evening of 17 April 1989: 160 linear meters (4 nets) in South Pond, 110 m (3 nets) in North Pond
- Nets run approximately 12 hours later on 18 April 1989
- Seine sampling (3 x 1.2 m straight seine, 0.3 mm mesh) conducted 25 April 1989: 25 hauls in South Pond, 20 hauls in North Pond

**Target Species:**
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): ~1500 juveniles stocked North Pond, 7 September 1988
- [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*): ~1500 young stocked South Pond, 13 October 1988
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): ~2000 juveniles stocked South Pond, 24 October 1988

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The ASUGC ponds represent artificial habitat for rearing endangered [[Colorado River]] native fishes. These water features are connected to the Salt River Project canal system, which harbors an abundant and diverse non-native fish fauna. The intake facility structure may limit access by larger canal-dwelling fishes, though invasion by non-native species is expected over time.

**Movement Patterns:**
- [[Bonytail]] showed substantial movement from South to North ponds (55 of 81 captured in North Pond despite stocking in South Pond)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] showed limited movement in opposite direction (31 of 32 in North Pond where stocked)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] showed no movement from South Pond where stocked (all 136 captured in South Pond)

**Non-native Species Present:**
- [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*): young and reproductive adults in both ponds
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*): young and reproductive adults in both ponds
- [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*): young of year in South Pond only
- [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*): young of year in South Pond only (native species)
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*): one specimen (208 mm TL, 140 g) in North Pond

## Key Findings

**Entanglement Net Captures (17-18 April 1989):**
- Total native fish captured: 249 individuals
- [[Bonytail]]: 81 total (26 South Pond, 55 North Pond)
- [[Colorado Squawfish]]: 32 total (1 South Pond, 31 North Pond)
- [[Razorback Sucker]]: 136 total (all in South Pond)
- Non-native: 1 [[Yellow Bullhead]]

**Bonytail Growth and Condition:**
- South Pond: 213 ± 21 mm TL (range 174-244 mm), 95 ± 27 g (range 47-132 g)
- North Pond: 186 ± 33 mm TL (range 124-250 mm), 70 ± 44 g (range 18-188 g)
- Mean total lengths significantly different between ponds (t[79] = -3.79, p = 0.0005)
- All fish in good health; two specimens (188 and 244 mm TL) tuberculate
- Minimal parasitism by copepod *Lernaea cyprinacea* (few fish, 1-2 parasites per host)
- Growth substantially exceeded other sites: ASUGC bonytail at 13 months averaged larger than [[Arizona State University Research Park]] (154 mm TL) and [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] (143 mm TL)

**Colorado Squawfish Growth and Condition:**
- Overall: 151 ± 18 mm TL (range 123-192 mm), 31 ± 13 g (range 15-66 g)
- All fish in good condition, no parasitic infestations
- Growth considerably exceeded ASURP (85-102 mm TL at 13 months)
- Captures particularly valuable as this species proven difficult to capture at ASURP

**Razorback Sucker Growth and Condition:**
- South Pond: 253 ± 39 mm TL (range 179-370 mm), 235 ± 110 g (range 74-690 g)
- Generally good health; one minor *Lernaea* infestation, one caudal spinal anomaly
- Growth exceeded other sites: ASURP fish 225 mm at similar age, BANWR ponds averaged <200 mm first year

**Seine Sampling Results (25 April 1989):**
- Total catch: 245 fish, four species
- [[Mosquitofish]]: 215 individuals (dominant species)
- [[Red Shiner]]: 26 individuals
- [[Carp]]: 2 individuals (new occurrence at ASUGC)
- [[Desert Sucker]]: 2 individuals (new occurrence at ASUGC, native species)
- Catch per unit effort: 5.51 fish/haul overall (9.28 South Pond, 0.80 North Pond)
- CPUE lower than first-year ASURP (26-34 fish/haul), but ASURP sampled in October after summer reproduction
- [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]] first collected September 1988; among first invaders at ASURP
- Expected substantial increase in numbers and diversity by autumn 1989

**Comparative Analysis:**
- Growth rates at ASUGC generally exceeded those at [[Arizona State University Research Park]] and [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]]
- Differential growth may relate to food habits and fish density (planned for future examination)
- Specimens available from multiple locations for comparative food habits analysis
- Next scheduled sampling: autumn-winter 1989
