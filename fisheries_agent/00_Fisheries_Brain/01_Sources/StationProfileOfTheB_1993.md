## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Bowden National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Elkins, West Virginia (Region 5), established in 1958 under the Public Law of the 19th Congress. The hatchery serves a dual conservation and recreational mission: (1) rearing [[Chesapeake Bay Striped Bass]] to support restoration of self-sustaining anadromous populations in the Chesapeake Bay and its tributaries, and (2) producing [[Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], and [[Rainbow Trout]] for stocking federal waters to support sport fisheries. The document is primarily an administrative/operational profile rather than a biological study, providing funding, staffing (FTE), and fish distribution data across fiscal years 1991–1993.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Primary conservation target:** Striped bass reared at Bowden NFH are part of a **restoration program** aimed at reestablishing self-sustaining wild populations in the [[Chesapeake Bay]] and its tributary systems, indicating historical population decline in this anadromous stock.
- **Sport fishery support:** Brook, brown, and rainbow trout are propagated specifically for stocking **federal waters**, supplementing recreational angling opportunities, likely in cold-water streams and reservoirs within West Virginia and Maryland.
- **Distribution states (FY1993):** West Virginia and Maryland received hatchery-produced fish.
- **Water source allocation:** Distribution data separate "Federal Waters," "Other Waters," "State Waters," and "Private Waters," though in the reported years nearly all fish were released into "Other Waters" (non-FWS federal waters), suggesting cooperative stocking arrangements with state agencies.
