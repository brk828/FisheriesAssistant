# Spawning of Desert Sucker (Peer Review File, Manuscript F-14-89)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This file is a peer review package for manuscript F-14-89, "Spawning of Desert Sucker," by [[Jeffrey P. Hill]] and [[William J. Matter]], submitted to [[The Southwestern Naturalist]]. The package contains the reviewer evaluation form, the associate editor's cover letter, the reviewer's letter, and a short manuscript excerpt on the spawning biology of the [[Desert Sucker]] (*[[Catostomus clarki]]*).

The reviewer, [[Paul C. Marsh]] of [[Arizona State University]], recommended against publication. In his view the paper offers no useful new information and no quantitative data that could support a revision. The editor's letter sets out the journal's review criteria and asks reviewers to state clearly any major criticisms.

## Background & Description

The [[Desert Sucker]], *Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarki*, is a native catostomid of the southwestern United States. According to the manuscript, it occurs in the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona and New Mexico and in the [[Colorado River]] below the [[Grand Canyon]] and some of its tributaries, citing [[Minckley 1973]] and [[Lee et al. 1980]].

The manuscript states that little is known about the species' life history and ecology. It notes earlier reports of seasonal movement in an Arizona stream ([[Siebert 1980]]) and observations of feeding by the species ([[Schreiber 1978]]). [[Fisher et al. 1981]] are cited for work on feeding relationships. [[Minckley 1973]] suggested that spawning resembles that of related suckers, with spawning in spring and early summer, though details were not available.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study site was [[Bass-Hotsprings Creek]], a tributary of the [[San Pedro River]] on [[Muleshoe Preserve]], managed by [[The Nature Conservancy]]. Fish were collected in January, March, June, and August 1987 using DC [[Electrofishing]] in eight pools and eight riffles. The manuscript describes the habitat as a [[Sonoran Desert]] stream system. Juveniles were found in shallow water (under 15 cm deep), with seven in riffles and four in pools.

The reviewer's comments and the editorial correspondence focus on the scientific content and the journal's standards. They do not add ecological data about the site beyond what the manuscript reports.

## Key Findings

**Manuscript findings (as reported by the authors):**
- Sex and condition were recorded for captured fish. Males with breeding tubercles were common in March, and the authors describe them as spawning in March. Tubercles were less prevalent on males in June. Fish with tubercles were also observed in August.
- Adult females collected in June were reported as spawning or near spawning.
- Young-of-the-year fish were collected in June, ranging in size from roughly 27 to 36 mm (the OCR is unclear on this range).
- The authors conclude that spawning at Bass-Hotsprings Creek began as early as March and was completed by June, a single spring spawning period. They note this matches other *Catostomus* species ([[Moyle 1976]]).
- The fish count reported in the excerpt is not legible in the scan, so no specific sample total is given here. The manuscript is described as small in scope.

**Review outcome:**
- The reviewer's form and letter, dated 2 November 1989, recommend rejection ("Publication is not recommended for the reasons described below"). The reviewer states that the paper contains no useful new information and no quantitative data, and that revision to an acceptable standard is unlikely.
- The reviewer's form includes a yes/no question on whether the reviewer wishes to be identified to the author. The answer is marked "No."
- The editor's cover letter, dated 31 October 1989, asks for reviews within three weeks. It asks reviewers to be frank and concise, and to state major criticisms clearly when a manuscript is judged unsuitable.
