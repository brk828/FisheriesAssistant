# Long Range Plan for Recovery Implementation Program, Rare and Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

This draft Long Range Plan (October 3, 1991) outlines the comprehensive recovery strategy for four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*, proposed for listing). The plan establishes a logical framework progressing from establishing recovery goals through determining population status and trends, evaluating controlling variables (physical, chemical, biological factors limiting populations), and implementing management activities to overcome these obstacles. The document emphasizes that all four species have declined significantly, with the [[Razorback Sucker]] showing complete recruitment failure—all captured individuals exceed 20 years of age, predicting extinction within 40 years without intervention. The plan integrates habitat restoration, flow management, nonnative species control, genetic management, and experimental augmentation as key recovery strategies.

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program addresses species recovery across the upper [[Colorado River]] and [[Green River]] basins exclusively, though it acknowledges necessary research in the Lower Basin and [[San Juan River]] (e.g., genetic population identification). The plan differs from individual species recovery plans by identifying activities necessary for all four species simultaneously within this geographic scope.

**Current Recovery Goals (as of 1991):**

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**: First priority is preventing extinction in the wild. All recently captured individuals are estimated at 20+ years old with no evidence of successful recruitment. Formal quantitative recovery goals to be developed once populations become self-sustaining.

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]**: Naturally self-sustaining populations in: (a) [[Green River Basin]] (Green River from Colorado River confluence to Yampa River confluence, lower 220 km of Yampa, lower 240 km of White River); (b) Colorado River from Palisade, Colorado to Lake Powell; (c) Salt and/or Verde River; (d) lower 305 km of San Juan River. Removal of fragmentation threats, legal protection of essential habitats, migration routes, streamflows, and water quality.

- **[[Humpback Chub]]**: Protection or restoration of five viable, self-sustaining wild populations plus two refugia populations as catastrophe safeguards. Potential locations include Little Colorado/Colorado rivers in Grand Canyon, Black Rocks/Westwater Canyon, Yampa/Green rivers in [[Dinosaur National Monument]], Gray and Desolation Canyons, and Cataract Canyon.

- **[[Bonytail Chub]]**: Short-term goal is preventing extinction; quantitative recovery goals to be developed once extinction threat is removed.

The plan identifies 55 fish species in the Upper Basin, of which 42 are nonnative—a critical factor in native fish decline.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The plan identifies three major categories of controlling variables affecting endangered fish populations:

**Physical Factors:**
- **Channel Structure**: Extensive channelization from levees, dikes, riprap, and vegetative (tamarisk) encroachment has eliminated floodplains, wetlands, bottomlands, and side channels. Sediment accretion is filling pools and slow runs, reducing habitat diversity.
- **Flow Regime**: Altered by upstream impoundments ([[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Aspinall Unit]]), reducing spring floods that historically inundated floodplains—critical for spawning, rearing, feeding, and thermal refuge.
- **Sediment Dynamics**: Changes in sediment supply and transport capacity have altered substrate characteristics necessary for successful reproduction.
- **Passage Barriers**: Dams and diversions have fragmented populations, restricting access to spawning sites and preferred habitats. Barriers restrict gene flow, potentially causing inbreeding depression and genetic drift. Restoration opportunities identified at Redlands (50 miles), Price-Stub/roller dam (80 miles), and Taylor Draw (50 miles).
- **Temperature**: Cold water releases from mainstem impoundments have contributed to warmwater fish extirpation, particularly in the Green River below [[Flaming Gorge]] to the Yampa confluence.

**Chemical Factors:**
- **Contaminants**: Irrigation drainwater contains heavy metals (including selenium), pesticides, and fertilizers. Department of Interior Irrigation Water Quality Program investigations (1991-1994) are assessing impacts in Colorado, Gunnison, Green, and San Juan rivers, with preliminary indications of localized problems.

**Biological Factors:**
- **Nonnative Fishes**: Of 55 species present, 42 are nonnative. Many are piscivorous or compete for resources. Nearly as many North American fish extinctions attributed to introduced species as to physical habitat losses.
- **Sportfishing Mortality**: Estimated 5-20% mortality of adult [[Colorado Squawfish]] in Yampa/Green system based on tagged fish harvest data (approximately 10% angler harvest).
- **Sampling/Handling Mortality**: Concern exists but impacts not yet quantified.
- **Hatchery Fish Impacts**: Potential negative effects on wild populations via predation, competition, disease, parasites, and genetic introgression.

**Reproduction/Recruitment Failure:**
The plan emphasizes that population decline must ultimately result from factors interfering with successful reproduction and recruitment. For [[Razorback Sucker]], complete recruitment failure represents the most critical immediate threat.

## Key Findings

**Strategic Framework:**
The plan establishes a four-step recovery strategy: (1) Establish Recovery Goals, (2) Determine Status and Trends, (3) Evaluate Controlling Variables, (4) Conduct Management Activities. Success is measured by population responses matching recovery goals; failure indicates overlooked controlling variables requiring reassessment.

**Population Status Assessment (Objective 2):**
- Population Viability Analysis (PVA) scheduled for [[Colorado Squawfish]] in FY 1992 (results September 1992) to refine recovery goals.
- Genetics studies of [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] populations underway with FY 1993 results expected to define populations, delineate geographic boundaries, and enable population-based management.
- Systematics, taxonomy, and genetics investigation of *Gila* (chub) species began FY 1989, scheduled completion FY 1994.
- Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program under evaluation (1991) to improve confidence in abundance indices and data collection methods.
- Larval drift assessment ongoing in Yampa/Green, Colorado/Gunnison, and San Juan systems.
- No current work quantifying recruitment, mortality, or production rates.

**Critical Research Needs (Objectives 1-4, Section II.B):**
- **Channel Structure**: Channel monitoring program (begun FY 1990) collecting video and ground data at regular intervals. Studies include spring habitat restoration (Flaming Gorge), Upper Colorado River habitat assessment, [[Old Charley Wash]] wetlands restoration, and White River [[Colorado Squawfish]] assessment.
- **Flow Requirements**: Flow recommendations developed or in development for Yampa, Green, and Colorado rivers. Instream flow senior scientist to be contracted FY 1992 to evaluate studies and resolve technical issues.
- **Sediment/Substrate**: Need to determine substrate characteristics for successful reproduction, flows to prepare/maintain spawning substrate, and sediment variables important to other life stages. Colorado River Water Conservation District and Service Research branch may initiate flow/sediment regime studies FY 1992.
- **Passage**: Service report on passage issues completed December 1990. Opportunities to restore passage in Gunnison and Colorado above Palisade being explored outside Recovery Program.
- **Temperature**: Much prior work completed on temperature requirements and modeling; no current hypothesis-testing studies.
- **Contaminants**: Service Irrigation Water Quality Program data collection ongoing; by 1994 should identify nature/degree of problems, remedial measures, and flow regime effects.
- **Nonnative Fishes**: Issue paper to be finalized soon. Service has informal agreement with Colorado (Utah in development) to review state nonnative stocking proposals. Studies include Flaming Gorge/Aspinall investigations, White River assessment, and monitoring program.
- **Sportfishing**: Past creel censuses inconclusive; need reliable mortality estimates.
- **Sampling Impacts**: No studies conducted to estimate sampling efficiency or percentage of population sampled annually.
- **Hatchery Fish Monitoring**: FY 1992 projects approved to quantify hatchery fish in Colorado and White rivers; no studies assessing impacts of stocked endangered fishes.
- **Chemoreception/Imprinting**: Study began 1991, continues through 2000, testing hypothesis that [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] show spawning site fidelity.

**Management Activities (Section II.C):**
- **Habitat Restoration**: [[Old Charley Wash]] wetlands restoration, spring habitat management (Flaming Gorge), channel monitoring ongoing FY 1992. No current work addressing main channel habitat loss from siltation/vegetative encroachment.
- **Flow Management**: Activities underway to facilitate water acquisition. Flaming Gorge releases being managed; Ruedi Reservoir releases augmenting Colorado River flows (15-mile reach) in late summer/fall.
- **Passage Restoration**: Efforts underway exploring opportunities at Redlands and Price-Stub diversion structures.
- **Temperature Enhancement**: Exploring options for thermal enhancement via Flaming Gorge re-regulation.
- **Contaminants**: Recommendations for reduction/elimination expected 1994.
- **Nonnative Control**: No action taken yet; institutional and political barriers identified. Issue paper with Colorado Division of Wildlife/Colorado State University recommendations forthcoming. White River below Taylor Draw dam closed to fishing when squawfish present.
- **Sampling Mortality**: No action taken to reduce mortality.
- **Stocking Management**: Hatchery fish should not be stocked where wild fish reside or where access to wild populations exists. Habitat should be restored prior to stocking.

**Genetics Management (Objective 5):**
- Recovery Program genetics management plan established: representative samples being removed from wild populations and held in refugia.
- Backup refuge populations and broodstock being developed from captive populations.
- Experimental/research and refuge facility developed at Ouray, Utah.
- Facility for refuge populations of [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] being developed FY 1992 at Horsethief Wildlife Management Area, Colorado.
- Consolidated facilities needs report scheduled January 1992.
- Criteria for reintroduction/augmentation development ongoing, scheduled completion FY 1992.

**Razorback Sucker Colorado River Subbasin Action Plan (Section IV):**
The plan provides detailed species-specific guidance for [[Razorback Sucker]] in the Colorado River subbasin as an example. Highest priority goal: prevent extinction by arresting and reversing declines. Three theories for decline: (1) 100% egg/larval predation (being tested at Lake Mohave); (2) channelization and flow alteration eliminating flooded lowlands used for feeding, rearing, gonadal maturation (being tested at [[Old Charley Wash]]); (3) contaminants (selenium, pesticides, heavy metals) interfering with reproduction/recruitment (results expected 1994). All three theories may be correct—razorbacks may require flooded bottomlands free from predators and contaminants for successful reproduction. Historically-occupied floodplains like Walter Walker Wildlife Area identified for potential acquisition and management.

**Implementation Status:**
The plan notes that while many management activities are being explored, few are actually being implemented as of 1991 (exceptions: flow management below Flaming Gorge and Ruedi Reservoir augmentation flows). The Species/Sub-basin Matrix (Section III) is left blank for future completion to outline priority and status of objectives/tasks for each species and sub-basin. Species Action Plans for species other than [[Razorback Sucker]] and for other sub-basins will be developed once format and content are agreed upon.

**Not Included:**
The plan explicitly notes that a section on "Other Recovery Actions" (water acquisition, information and education, Federal reservoir Section 7 consultation, long-range planning, annual reporting, program technical support including data management and hydrology/temperature monitoring and modeling) is not included but could be added later.
