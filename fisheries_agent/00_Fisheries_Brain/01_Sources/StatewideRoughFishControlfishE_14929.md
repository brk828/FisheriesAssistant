# Fish Eradication in Loy Lake

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #clupeidae

## Executive Summary

This job completion report documents a complete fish eradication operation conducted at [[Loy Lake]] in [[Grayson County]], [[Texas]] on April 1, 1963 as part of the Statewide Rough Fish Control program (Federal Aid Project F-14-D-7). The 42-acre lake (230 acre-feet) was treated with 1.5 PPM of 5% emulsifiable [[Rotenone]] to remove the existing fish population, achieving approximately 95% mortality. The operation was conducted to eliminate an undesirable fish community dominated by [[Gizzard Shad]] (58% of population) and stunted [[Bluegill]] (18%). Following treatment, the lake was planned for restocking with [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Redear Sunfish]].

## Background & Description

[[Loy Lake]] is a 42-acre impoundment near Denison in [[Grayson County]], [[Texas]]. Pre-treatment surveys revealed a fish community dominated by undesirable species, with [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) comprising 58% of the total fish population and stunted [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) accounting for 18%. No endemic species were present at the time of treatment. The lake level was 3 inches below normal during treatment and remained there for at least 3 weeks afterward.

The watershed contains approximately 20 ponds and a small stream on private lands known to contain native fish populations. These could not be treated due to access restrictions, creating potential for reinfestation, particularly of sunfish species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The treatment was conducted under specific environmental conditions: water temperature of 70°F at 9:30 AM, increasing to 72°F by mid-afternoon, with sustained 20 MPH winds from the south throughout the day. These conditions influenced chemical distribution and fish response patterns.

The fish community observed during eradication included 14 species across multiple families:
- **[[Clupeidae]]**: [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*) - most abundant
- **[[Cyprinidae]]**: [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*)
- **[[Ictaluridae]]**: [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus natalis*)
- **[[Fundulidae]]**: Topminnows (*Fundulus* sp.)
- **[[Poeciliidae]]**: [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)
- **[[Centrarchidae]]**: [[Black Bass]] (*Micropterus* sp.), [[Warmouth]] (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*), [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*)

## Key Findings

**Treatment Application**: The entire 42-acre lake was treated on April 1, 1963 with 120 gallons of 5% emulsifiable [[Rotenone]] at a concentration of 1.5 PPM. Chemical application took 3 hours using two outboard motor boats equipped with gravity flow drums, applying chemical directly into prop-wash in a criss-cross pattern. Shallow water areas were treated using high-pressure spray.

**Fish Response Timeline**: [[Gizzard Shad]] began surfacing 15 minutes after shallow water spraying. Several sunfish were also observed shortly after treatment. The majority of affected fish were [[Gizzard Shad]].

**Notable Specimens Recovered**: 
- Largest fish: 42-pound [[Carp]]
- Eight to ten additional [[Carp]] weighing 20-25 pounds
- 30-35 [[Black Bass]] observed, largest weighing 6 pounds 1 ounce
- Few [[Channel Catfish]] seen, ranging 8-22 pounds
- Very few [[White Crappie]] observed

**Public Recovery Effort**: Approximately 50 boats were on the lake immediately after treatment completion, increasing to 100-125 boats by mid-afternoon with approximately 2,000 people on shore. This large crowd achieved excellent recovery of edible fish, though no quantitative estimate could be made.

**Post-Treatment Assessment**: Four days after treatment, an 8-12 inch border of dead fish surrounded the lake, mostly [[Gizzard Shad]] and small sunfish. Follow-up sampling on April 9-10, 1963 confirmed treatment effectiveness:
- 1,150 feet of gill nets set overnight captured only 4 fish (2 [[Black Bullhead]], 1 [[Warmouth]], 1 [[Green Sunfish]])
- Extensive seining (26 x 6 foot bag seine, 1/4 inch mesh) produced only 3 [[Mosquitofish]] and 1 [[Bluegill]]
- Two to three live fish (believed [[Green Sunfish]]) observed in shallow water

**Treatment Efficacy**: Total fish population reduced by at least 95%.

**Management Recommendations**: Following vegetation treatment under Project F-15-D, restocking plan recommended:
- 200 [[Largemouth Bass]] fry per acre (from state hatcheries)
- 50 [[Channel Catfish]] fingerlings per acre (from state hatcheries)
- 10 adult [[Redear Sunfish]] per acre (secured locally)

**Reinfestation Risk**: Approximately 20 ponds and a small stream on the watershed contain native fish but could not be treated due to private land ownership. Slight reinfestation expected, particularly sunfish species.

**Project Cost**: Total expenditure of $905.76, with rotenone ($492.00) representing 54% of total cost. Personnel costs totaled $355.34 for 21.5 person-days of effort.
