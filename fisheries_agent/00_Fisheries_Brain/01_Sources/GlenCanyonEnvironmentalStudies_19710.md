# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Reservoir Work Group Notes (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #reservoir-limnology

## Executive Summary

This August 1991 memorandum from [[Bureau of Reclamation]] staff in the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program records the outcomes of a two-day Reservoir Work Group meeting (August 13–14, 1991) on [[Lake Powell]] and [[Lake Mead]]. Participants agreed that large volumes of limnological and biological data exist but are scattered across file cabinets and magnetic tapes. The group called for a common database, a systematic and coherent monitoring approach, and assessment of reservoir impacts for the [[Environmental Impact Statement]] (EIS) on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operational alternatives.

## Background & Description

The meeting was organized in two parts:

- **Day One (Scientific Discussions):** Reviews of prior studies on Lake Powell and Lake Mead, followed by discussion of data bases, monitoring programs, and quality control needs.
- **Day Two (EIS Alternatives):** Presentations on EIS objectives, the dam operating alternatives, and anticipated Lake Powell level changes, followed by identification of data gaps and a work plan.

Presenters included Reclamation staff from Salt Lake City, Denver, and Boulder City, researchers from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), the [[U.S. Geological Survey]] (USGS), the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]] (UDWR), and the Lake Mead National Recreation Area (LMNRA). The author, Dave Wegner, described the meeting as a good start toward pulling together managers and researchers working on both reservoirs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake Powell**
- Sediment and nutrient dynamics: Resuspension of sediments in the upper lake is expected to affect nutrient and metal cycling. Over 97% of phosphorus is retained in the lake, and nutrients are bound in sediments that could fuel future [[Algal Blooms]] when resuspended. Estimated sediment accumulation is 300 years to the penstock tube height and an additional 400 years to the top of the dam.
- Stratification: Temperature drives conditions in the epilimnion, and salinity drives conditions in the hypolimnion. Penstock influence may vary seasonally with reservoir water density.
- Groundwater: More than 10 million acre-feet of water are stored in the [[Navajo Sandstone]], and its geochemical relationships need evaluation.
- Contaminants: Upper Basin irrigation and water quality studies have identified selenium and other mineral hotspots that will reach the lake. Lake Powell acts as a major cation and anion trap, and lead is the only trace element observed leaving the lake.
- Riparian zone: About 90% of riparian vegetation is [[Tamarisk]], an exotic species. Native species are slowly re-invading.

**Lake Mead**
- Las Vegas Wash: Most water quality work has focused here. Chlorophyll a and plankton have increased, and low reservoir levels reduce dilution.
- Remote sensing: Satellite imagery has shown limited success for mapping [[Chlorophyll A]] because of atmospheric interference.
- Littoral zones: Cove and littoral areas are important for bass spawning. Bass nest at depths near 10 feet, and waves can reduce nesting success where water is shallower than 5 feet.
- Use pressure: LMNRA reports more than 9 million visitors per year, with navigation and wetland development concerns in the upper lake.

## Key Findings

**Fisheries (Lake Powell, per UDWR)**
- Threadfin Shad show three-year abundance peaks (1981, 1984, 1987, 1990), each smaller than the last, making the forage base harder to predict.
- [[Striped Bass]] have limited open-water recruitment. Mature adults are pushed into the metalimnion, where food is reduced. Natural reproduction is linked to high dissolved oxygen on the substrate.
- Gamefish status: [[Largemouth Bass]] declining; Walleye peaked and then declined; [[Smallmouth Bass]] expanding; Crappie essentially gone; Striped Bass in a boom-bust-rebound pattern.
- Only 3–4 [[Razorback Sucker]] were collected over the previous four years, mostly in the upper San Juan drainage.
- No plankton samples had been collected by the agencies at the time, though some were gathered by outside researchers.
- Fish sampling at 11 points in Lake Powell began in fiscal year 1991, using [[Common Carp]] as a consistent indicator species.

**Limnology and Monitoring**
- Hydrolab samples have been collected monthly since August 1990, with nutrients, basic chemistry, and biological parameters measured every other month. Samples are also drawn from the dam's draft tubes.
- Data from before 1980 are mainly held in computer archives. Algal dynamics are measured only spottily.
- The [[LARM Model]] is archived and not used in current long-term modeling. Reclamation may be overestimating the evaporation coefficient in the [[CRSS Model]].
- Presenters called for a coordinated data base and survey approach involving all resource agencies, with a systematic baseline for future comparison.

**Lake Mead History**
- The reservoir filled from 1935 to 1941. Earlier fisheries work (Moffett, 1943; Jonez, 1954) provided a baseline before Lake Powell.
- The [[Black Bass Study]] ran 1975–1980 and a test fertilization project in Overton Arm ran for three years in the 1980s.
- Water quality studies span 1951 (Pritchard) to 1986–87 (Spring Canyon). The Denver Office holds the database on computer tape.

**EIS Resources of Concern**
- Biological: bass spawning areas; endangered and native fish habitat; riparian and wetland vegetation; bighorn sheep access.
- Limnological: metal fate and effects; nutrient and primary productivity; stratification changes; water quality for salinity and allocated water.
- Physical: navigation in Lakes Mead and Powell; the river reach below Separation Rapids; cultural resources.
- [[Multiple Level Withdrawal]] (MLW): Objectives must be defined (target temperature, timing, and species). Salinity, temperature, and organic matter routing should be modeled, MLW applications reviewed in the literature, and effects on forage fish and benthic organisms evaluated.

**Recommendations and Responsibilities**
1. Establish a long-term, systematic reservoir evaluation and monitoring program for Lakes Powell and Mead (GCES, USFWS, Lower and Upper Colorado Regions, LMNRA, Glen Canyon NRA).
2. Assess sediment quality in both reservoirs for the EIS, with sampling during a planned September/October 1991 Powell trip (GCES, USFWS, Reclamation).
3. Initiate a cultural resources survey from Separation Canyon to Lake Mead (GCES, Hualapai Tribe, NPS).
4. Compile a list of threatened and endangered species that water level changes could affect (GCES, Hualapai Tribe, LMNRA, USFWS).
5. Systematically review existing reservoir water quality and limnological data (GCES, Denver Office).
6. Review the quality of released water as lake levels drop (GCES, Denver Office, AGF).
