# Aspects of the life history of the Ozark madtom, Noturus albater, in southeastern Missouri (pisces; ictaluridae)

## Executive Summary
This study by Mayden, Burr and Dewey (1980) documents the life history of the [[Ozark Madtom]] (*Noturus albater*), a small catfish endemic to clear, high-gradient Ozark streams in southeastern Missouri and northern Arkansas. Collections were made from Big Sinking Creek (Current River drainage) in 1979 and Big Creek (St. Francis River drainage) in 1978–1979. The paper covers sexual dimorphism, fecundity, sex ratios, nesting behavior, egg and larval development, age/growth, food habits, and parasitism/predation. Key findings include male-guarded nests under large flat rocks, a mean clutch size of ~40 adhesive eggs, laboratory hatching in 196 hours at 25°C, rapid post-hatch development to adult-like morphology by 7 days, and a diet dominated by chironomid (midge) larvae. Both sexes were found to live at least 2+ years, with sexual maturity attained around age 2.

## Background & Description
- **Sexual dimorphism (breeding season):**
  - Genital papillae: males enlarged and elongated with slight tissue swelling; females relatively short but with pronounced swelling of adjacent tissue that obscures papilla shape.
  - Head musculature: breeding males show enlarged head muscles and swollen lips (a trait described in other *[[Noturus]]* species by Taylor 1969), presumably linked to nest excavation/burrowing under rocks. Mature females lack this trait.

- **Egg morphology:**
  - Spherical, yellow eggs; diameter 3.5–4.0 mm (mean 3.7 mm, N=3).
  - Yolk diameter 2.9–3.0 mm (mean 3.0 mm, N=3).
  - Eggs possess temporary adhesive properties, clinging to each other in a rounded mass but not to substrate.

- **Developmental sequence (25°C rearing):**
  | Stage | Age | Length (TL) | Key Features |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Prehatchling | ~100 hr | 6.0–6.2 mm | Blood visible, myomeres developed, tail/pectoral fins free of yolk sac, eye pigment developed, two barbel pairs present |
  | Hatchling | 0 days (196 hr to hatch) | 8.0–8.2 mm | Mostly translucent, melanophores on head/anterior myomeres, caudal/anal fins forming, nostril barbels forming |
  | Larva | 4 days | 9.2 mm | Melanophores over much of body; pectoral, caudal, anal, dorsal fin rays developed; pelvic fin buds formed; dorsal/pectoral spines evident; 4 barbel pairs well-developed; yolk sac ~half of initial size; begins feeding on crushed food pellets |
  | Larva | 7 days | 11.8–12.0 mm | Adult-like body form, opercular membrane, branchiostegal rays, and fin rays; lateral line present; yolk sac nearly absorbed; well-pigmented (barred adult pattern not yet developed) |

- **Age/size classes (from April collections, N=69):**
  - Age class 0 (≤9 months): 38–68 mm SL (mean 53.0 mm)
  - Age class 1+: 73–79 mm SL (mean 75.8 mm)
  - Age class 2+: 81–89 mm SL (mean 84.8 mm)

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Clear, moderate- to large-sized, high-gradient Ozark streams; most commonly found in fast gravel riffles, occasionally in shallow rocky pools. Rarely found in areas lacking rocky substrate, which is used as daytime cover — consistent with prior observations by [[Pflieger 1975]] and [[Taylor 1969]].

- **Nesting ecology:**
  - Nesting observed 4 July 1979 at Big Creek; stream width 5–18 m, depth 15 cm–1.5 m; water temp 28°C, air temp ~30°C.
  - Nests located at riffle heads or shallow pools (45–67 cm) with current, under large flat rocks (18–26 cm wide, 30–45 cm long).
  - Nest cavity depth: 10–20 cm; substrate lined with fine gravel/sand, free of debris.
  - Three nests documented, each guarded by a single male (captured males were 70.4–76.5 mm SL, age class 2).
  - Guardian males had empty stomachs, supporting the hypothesis (based on unpublished observations of other *Noturus* spp.) that nest-guarding males do not feed during this period.

- **Trophic role:** Primarily insectivorous, nocturnal feeder. Diet dominated by aquatic Diptera (particularly Chironomidae, 65.7% by occurrence, present in 90% of stomachs), with Ephemeroptera (20.3%) as a secondary component. Feeding intensity is greatest at night, consistent with prior work by [[Curd 1960]] and Taylor (1969).

- **Predator-prey interactions:**
  - Egg predation observed by *[[Notropis zonatus]]*.
  - Larval/hatchling predation observed by *[[Etheostoma caeruleum]]* and *[[Percina evides]]*.
  - Additional species congregating near exposed nests (though not directly observed feeding) included *E. flabellare*, *[[Lepomis megalotis]]*, *[[Micropterus salmoides]]*, and the crayfish *[[Orconectes ozarkae]]* — suggesting broader opportunistic predation pressure on exposed nest contents.

- **Parasitism:** 
  - 4 of 54 specimens showed a single *[[Lernaea]]* scar each (external parasitic copepod).
  - 2 of 20 dissected specimens harbored encysted parasitic nematodes (1 and 3 cysts, respectively), attached to the outer stomach wall.

## Key Findings
- **Fecundity:** Ripe females exhibited three discernible ova size classes: small (0.3–0.7 mm, white), intermediate (1.2–1.6 mm, white), and mature (2.1–2.3 mm, yellow-orange), randomly distributed within ovaries.
  - Mature ova counts in three females (77.1–81.3 mm SL): 109, 110, 116 (mean = 111.7).
  - Significant positive correlation between ova number and standard length (r = 0.898, p = 0.006, N = 7) — larger females produce more eggs.
  - Intermediate-sized ova (from 4 additional females, 56.0–76.0 mm SL) ranged 45–95 (mean = 76.0), potentially representing a later or partial spawning cohort.

- **Sex ratios:** 
  - Big Creek (mostly age class 0): females:males = 1.8:1 (35:19).
  - Big Sinking Creek (mixed age classes): females:males = 1.4:1 (10:7).

- **Clutch size and hatching:** Mean clutch size ~40 eggs; laboratory-reared eggs (N=34) hatched in 196 hours (8 days) at 25°C with 100% hatching success.

- **Maturity:** 
  - Females: only larger 1+ individuals and the single 2+ female showed full reproductive maturity (swollen papillae, mature yolky ova); smaller 1+ females were immature, possibly maturing later in the season.
  - Males: sexual maturity attained by age 2 (2nd–3rd summer), evidenced by nest-guarding males; only larger 1+ males showed papilla enlargement.

- **Longevity:** Both sexes documented to live at least 2+ years based on age-class analysis.

- **Comparative context:** This study builds directly on developmental and reproductive data generated for other *Noturus* species (Burr and Mayden, 1982), reinforcing patterns of nest-guarding, non-feeding brooding males, and rapid larval development shared across the genus [[Noturus]] and family [[Ictaluridae]].
