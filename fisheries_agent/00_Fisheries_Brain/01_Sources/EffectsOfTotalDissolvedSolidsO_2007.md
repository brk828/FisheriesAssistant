# Effects of Total Dissolved Solids on Aquatic Organisms: A Review of Literature and Recommendation for Salmonid Species

#ichthyology #salmonidae #water_quality #toxicology #conservation

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** This review examines multiple salmonid species with varying sensitivity to Total Dissolved Solids (TDS). Species sensitivity ranking (most to least sensitive to CaSO₄ TDS): [[Chum Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus keta*) > [[Steelhead]] (*O. mykiss*) > [[Arctic Grayling]] (*Thymallus arcticus*) > [[Dolly Varden]] (*Salvelinus malma*) > [[Arctic Char]] (*Salvelinus alpinus*). Additional species examined include [[Pink Salmon]] (*O. gorbuscha*), [[Coho Salmon]] (*O. kisutch*), [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), and [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*). Age, maturity, fecundity, and growth parameters not reported in this review. Critical life stage sensitivity occurs at fertilization and early embryonic development, with reduced sensitivity post-fertilization through juvenile stages.

**Habitat Associations:** Salmonid species occupy freshwater rivers, streams, and lakes across Alaska and North America. Natural TDS concentrations in world rivers average approximately 120 mg L⁻¹, with bicarbonate as the major anion (mean 68 mg L⁻¹ in North American rivers) and calcium as the most common cation (mean 21 mg L⁻¹). Water with TDS >1000 mg L⁻¹ is considered brackish. TDS concentrations and composition are determined by drainage geology, atmospheric precipitation, and water balance (evaporation-precipitation). Changes in natural TDS often result from industrial effluent, altered water balance, or salt-water intrusion.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** Not reported.

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** TDS affects entire aquatic food webs. Bierhuizen and Prepas found significant negative correlation between chlorophyll-a (primary production estimate) and concentrations of Na⁺, Mg²⁺, SO₄²⁻, HCO₃⁻, and CO₃²⁻. Hallock and Hallock reported substantial marsh community changes when TDS increased from 270 to 1170 mg L⁻¹, with near elimination of [[Coontail]] (*Ceratophyllus demersum*) and [[Cattails]] (*Typha* sp.). Derry et al. found salinity and aquatic biodiversity inversely related in lake water, with ionic composition determining species dominance: rotifer *Brachionus plicatilis* and copepod *Cletocamptus* sp. prevailed in Cl⁻-dominated water, while calanoid copepods *Leptodiaptoums sicillis* and *Diaptomus nevadensis* dominated SO₄²⁻/CO₃²⁻-dominated waters. Aquatic invertebrate toxicity varies widely by species and ion type: [[Chironomid]] larvae (*Chironomus tentans*) showed significant effects above 1100 mg L⁻¹ CaSO₄, with 10-day LC50 of 2,035 mg L⁻¹. For [[Ceriodaphnia dubia]], 48-h LC50 ranged from 390 mg L⁻¹ (KHCO₃ + K₂SO₄ mixture) to >5000 mg L⁻¹ depending on ionic composition. [[Daphnia magna]] showed 48-h LC50 of 735 mg L⁻¹ for NaHCO₃ and >5000 mg L⁻¹ for NaCl.

**Environmental Drivers:** Ion composition determines TDS toxicity more than total concentration. Mount et al. found relative ion toxicity: K⁺ > HCO₃⁻ = Mg²⁺ > Cl⁻ > SO₄²⁻, with Ca²⁺ and Na⁺ producing no significant toxicity in isolation. However, calcium emerged as the primary ion inhibiting salmonid egg hatch when exposure occurred during fertilization. Stekoll et al. identified Ca²⁺ as the primary ion responsible for inhibiting hatch of salmonid eggs exposed during fertilization. Brannock et al. found fertilization rates in [[King Salmon]] and [[Pink Salmon]] significantly lower with exposure to calcium or sulfate at 2500 ppm TDS equivalent, while potassium and magnesium showed no detectable differences from control. Spermatozoa activity was inhibited by small quantities of potassium chloride (19.2 mg L⁻¹) or potassium carbonate (106.2 mg L⁻¹). Erickson et al. found potassium chloride addition markedly increased copper toxicity, while calcium chloride and sodium chloride substantially reduced it.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** Not reported for specific populations.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** Elevated TDS poses significant threat to salmonid reproduction and recruitment. Stekoll et al. exposed [[Coho Salmon]] embryos to elevated TDS during different life stages from post-fertilization to button-up fry, finding no significant mortality increase with higher TDS concentrations post-fertilization. However, when coho salmon were exposed at fertilization, higher concentrations resulted in reduced hatch rates, delayed hatch, and long-term effects on growth and development. Eggs exposed to 1875 and 2500 ppm TDS at fertilization showed high mortality rates between eyed and alevin stages; at 2500 ppm, 50% mortality occurred among the 50% that had been fertilized. Brix and Grosell reported LOEC for [[Arctic Grayling]] ranging from 254 to >2782 mg L⁻¹ TDS and LOEC for [[Dolly Varden]] ranging from >1704 to >1817 mg L⁻¹. Stekoll et al. reported LOEC of 1875 mg L⁻¹ for [[Arctic Char]]. Chapman et al. exposed embryonic and juvenile [[Rainbow Trout]] (*O. mykiss*) to synthetic TDS mixtures modeled after Alaskan mine effluents, finding no significant effects up to 2000 mg L⁻¹ for post-fertilization exposures. Ketola et al. found exposing salmonid embryos to high calcium concentrations (≥520 mg L⁻¹) during water hardening (post-fertilization) decreased survival: 38% survival at eye-up for *S. fontinalis* exposed to 2229 mg L⁻¹ CaSO₄, 35% survival for *S. salar* exposed to 1395 mg L⁻¹ CaCl, and 4% survival for *O. mykiss* exposed to 1500 mg L⁻¹ CaSO₄. Mount et al. examined [[Fathead Minnow]] toxicity, finding one ion combination (KHCO₃) had 96-h LC50 <510 mg L⁻¹, seven combinations resulted in 96-h LC50 <1000 mg L⁻¹, and numerous combinations resulted in 96-h LC50 >2000 mg L⁻¹. Diversity of aquatic species declines as osmotic tolerances are exceeded with increasing salinity. TDS causes toxicity through increases in salinity, changes in ionic composition, and toxicity of individual ions.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations:** Authors recommend different limits for individual ions rather than TDS for salmonid species, based on ion effects on fertilization and egg development. Three regulatory approaches identified: (1) Set standard low enough to protect all species and life stages exposed to most toxic ions (may be unnecessarily restrictive but simpler to implement); (2) Set standard to protect most species and life stages for most ions (less restrictive but may lead to adverse effects); (3) Define different limits for different ion categories or combinations, with lower limit during fish spawning if sensitive salmonid species are present (most complicated but provides greatest protection with least unnecessary restriction). Approach (3) recommended as optimal, requiring potential dischargers to determine effluent composition and identify downstream species and life stages. Current TDS monitoring standards should be reconsidered to monitor specific ions for future risk assessments. Mount et al. research provides toxicity data for different ions and combinations; most toxic to *C. dubia*, *D. magna*, and fathead minnows (all producing LC50 <1000 mg L⁻¹): KHCO₃ + K₂SO₄, KHCO₃ + KCl, K₂SO₄ + KCl, KCl, KHCO₃, K₂SO₄, MgCl₂ + KHCO₃, KHCO₃ + NaHCO₃, MgSO₄ + KHCO₃. Regulations require periodic TDS monitoring, measured by filtering sample through 2.0 µm pore size filter, evaporating remaining filtrate, and drying to constant weight at 180°C.

**Biological Reference Points:** For salmonids at fertilization: LOEC ranges from 254 mg L⁻¹ (Arctic grayling, lower bound) to >2782 mg L⁻¹ (Arctic grayling, upper bound); 1875 mg L⁻¹ (Arctic char); >1704 to >1817 mg L⁻¹ (Dolly Varden). Significant effects on coho salmon at 1875 and 2500 ppm TDS during fertilization. Calcium concentrations ≥520 mg L⁻¹ decrease survival during water hardening. For invertebrates: *C. tentans* significant effects above 1100 mg L⁻¹ CaSO₄, LC50 2,035 mg L⁻¹. For aquatic plants/algae: productivity decline >1400 mg L⁻¹; *Selanastrum capricornutum* EC20 = 551.3 mg L⁻¹ CaSO₄; nitrogen fixation in bluegreen bacteria limited at ~2450 mg L⁻¹. Natural background: world rivers mean salinity ~120 mg L⁻¹; North American rivers bicarbonate mean 68 mg L⁻¹, sulfate mean 20 mg L⁻¹, calcium mean 21 mg L⁻¹, sodium and silica each mean 9 mg L⁻¹.

This review synthesizes toxicology literature to inform water quality standards for [[Alaska]] waters, particularly regarding [[Mining]] effluent impacts on [[Salmonid]] reproduction. The research demonstrates that fertilization represents the most sensitive life stage for salmonids exposed to elevated TDS, with calcium identified as the primary toxic ion. Statistical models developed by Mount et al. predict toxicity of major ions to multiple test species, providing foundation for ion-specific rather than total TDS regulatory approaches. The work was funded by [[Alaska Department of Environmental Conservation]] and [[Alaska Science and Technology Foundation]], involving collaboration among [[University of Alaska Fairbanks]], [[University of Alaska Southeast]], resource agencies, and mining industry representatives.
