# Effects of Different Flow Regimes on Periphyton Standing Crop and Organic Matter Transport, and Nutrient Loading Rates, for the Colorado River Tailwater from Glen Canyon Dam to Lee's Ferry

#ichthyology #marine-biology #periphyton #aquatic-ecology

## Executive Summary

This draft final report (March 1995) by Andrew D. Ayers and Ted McKinney of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] assesses how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations and the [[Interim Flow Regime]] affect the food base of the Colorado River tailwater. The study covered seston (particulate organic matter, POM), periphyton (epilithon and microfauna), submerged macroalgae ([[Chara]] sp.), and water nutrients, sampled between 1991 and 1994. The work was funded by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] through the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program. Because of budget reductions, nutrient loading rates were not directly assessed; only preliminary water sampling was conducted in summer 1994.

## Background & Description

The report frames dam discharge as a driver of the lotic food web. Seston particles supply benthic invertebrates and can augment or replace periphyton at the base of the food web (Kondratieff and Simmons 1984). Dam operations may alter seston, periphyton and water quality in the tailwater below Glen Canyon Dam. Earlier work by [[Angradi et al. 1992]] established a pre-interim-flow baseline.

The study had three stated objectives:
1. Determine daily and seasonal variation in seston concentration and loading in the tailwater.
2. Determine seasonal variation and the effects of dewatering on periphyton and submerged macroalgae.
3. Measure water nutrient quality in the [[Lake Powell]] forebay, dam penstocks, and tailwaters.

The dominant alga is [[Cladophora glomerata]], a filamentous green alga that now dominates epilithon on cobbles. [[Chara]] sp. was previously sparse, but since about 1992 it has expanded onto sandy/silt substrate in the lower reach, reflecting a shift from heterotrophy toward autotrophy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Seston (FPOM and CPOM):** Fine particulate organic matter (FPOM, 0.7–750 µm) and coarse particulate organic matter (CPOM, >1000 µm) were sampled monthly. Angradi et al. (1992) reported CPOM at about three percent of total POM, mostly Cladophora.
- **Dewatering zones:** Periphyton was sampled in the permanently inundated zone (5,000 cfs) and the fluctuating zone (8,000 cfs). Daily flow fluctuation exposes the fluctuating zone to air, mostly at night.
- **Nutrients:** The total inorganic nitrogen to orthophosphate ratio was high (>80) in earlier data, suggesting possible phosphorus limitation of downstream algal productivity. Measured total phosphorus in water (0.002–0.004 mg/l) was about 10% or less of the critical concentration range (0.042–1.0 mg/l; [[Wong and Clark 1976]]).
- **Invertebrates:** [[Gammarus lacustris]] and chironomids were the main invertebrates in CPOM. Gammarus drift peaked on the descending limb of the daily hydrograph.
- **Lake Powell:** Surface FPOM and chlorophyll a were highest in the forebay, consistent with phytoplankton concentration in the euphotic layer.

## Key Findings

**Sampling and analysis**
- FPOM was sampled monthly from April 1993 to December 1994 at five locations: Lake Powell surface, penstock, 25 m (or 75 m) depth, Glen Canyon Dam draft tubes, and Lee's Ferry.
- Periphyton was collected monthly from littoral zones beginning in 1991 at river miles -14.0 and -13.5, with -9.0 added in 1994 and -4.1 added in 1993. Samples (n=15 per sampling) were taken from cobbles using a 4.15 cm² cylinder.
- Chara sp. was sampled quarterly at river mile -3.5 from January 1994.

**Seston results**
- FPOM biomass and chlorophyll a differed significantly among months, locations, and month-by-location combinations (p < 0.01).
- Lake Powell surface biomass was significantly higher than all other locations. Penstock and 75 m biomass were relatively constant and significantly lower than other locations.
- Lee's Ferry biomass peaked in August–September 1993 and was higher than draft tube biomass (p < 0.05).
- Angradi et al. (1992) found that FPOM export from the reach exceeded import during winter–spring, while import exceeded export in summer–fall. The present study found export higher than import generally, and export from Glen Canyon exceeded draft tube input in late summer–fall of 1993.
- CPOM biomass was significantly higher during peak flow (p < 0.05), and lowest in winter. Samples of 750–1000 µm contained no biomass in six months (April–September 1994).
- CPOM was 0.4% to 52.4% of total POM at Lee's Ferry. The lowest percentages were in December 1993–February 1994.
- Cladophora and its epiphytic diatoms averaged 95.7% of CPOM by volume. Chara sp. averaged 4.3%.
- Gammarus lacustris averaged 0.029 animals/m³; chironomid larvae averaged 0.019 animals/m³ and adults 0.09 animals/m³.
- Total phosphorus in CPOM was significantly higher in March–April 1994 than in other months. Total nitrogen was lower in June–July 1993 than in May 1993.

**Periphyton results**
- Periphyton biomass was consistently higher at 5,000 cfs than at 8,000 cfs (p < 0.01). The difference narrowed over time, from 1991 to 1994.
- Biomass was highest at -14 mile and lowest at -4.1 mile (p < 0.01). Seasonal minima occurred December–February.
- Chlorophyll a at -9 mile was significantly lower than at -14 mile at both elevations (p < 0.01). Chlorophyll a levels were not significantly different between elevations in January–October 1994 (p > 0.05).
- Through mid-1993, fluctuating-zone chlorophyll a was lower than in the inundated zone. Later differences were not apparent. Lower mean fluctuating-zone chlorophyll a in January–October 1994 approached significance (p < 0.07).

**Chara results**
- Chara biomass differed among months and between elevations (p < 0.01). At the fluctuating zone, biomass in July was significantly lower than in April.
- Chara pheophytin a was higher in the 5,000 cfs zone in July than in other months.

**Nutrients**
- Dissolved silica ranged from 6.5 mg/l (Lake Powell surface) to 12 mg/l (springs). Dissolved NO3 ranged from 0.115 mg/l (Lake Powell surface) to 2.10 mg/l (-8 mile spring).
- Total phosphorus ranged from 0.002 mg/l (Lake Powell surface) to 0.004 mg/l (draft tubes, -14 mile, Lee's Ferry, and springs).

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Interim flows may have reduced periphyton productivity in the fluctuating zone, but differences between zones diminished after 1993, suggesting the interim regime may have favorably affected primary productivity overall.
- Algal uptake of phosphorus may limit primary productivity in the tailwater, though data are limited.
- Future work should include assessment of nutrient loading rates and more periphyton sampling, since the authors note the cobble-based sampling method may underestimate chlorophyll a.
