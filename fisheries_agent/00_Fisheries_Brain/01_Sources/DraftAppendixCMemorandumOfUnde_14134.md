# Memorandum of Understanding: Instream Flows for Rare Colorado River Fishes (Muddy Creek Project)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is a draft (dated 6/28/89) Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service) and the [[Colorado River Water Conservation District]] (River District). It commits the River District to release up to 3,000 acre-feet of water from [[Muddy Creek Reservoir]] to the 15-mile reach of the Colorado River, providing instream flows for the [[Rare Colorado River Fishes]] under the [[Recovery Implementation Program]]. The flows are interim and are intended to offset the depletion impacts of the Muddy Creek project and to satisfy the Service's "sufficient progress" determination in the project's [[Biological Opinion]]. The MOU sets annual water-availability criteria, a hydrologic similarity test based on the 1953–1982 period of record, a delivery obligation, and termination provisions.

## Background & Description

- **Parties:** U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Colorado River Water Conservation District. Signature blocks are unsigned in this draft; signatories are listed as Galen L. Buterbaugh (Regional Director, Service) and Theodore Brooks (President of Board of Directors, River District).
- **Program context:** In January 1988 the Secretary of the Interior, the Governors of Colorado, Wyoming and Utah, and the Administrator of the [[Western Area Power Administration]] signed a Cooperative Agreement (dated January 21–22, 1988) creating the Recovery Implementation Program and an Implementation Committee.
- **Recovery goal:** Recover and delist the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], and manage the [[Razorback Sucker]] so it no longer needs Endangered Species Act protection. The four species are collectively termed the "rare Colorado River fishes."
- **Offset mechanism:** Depletion impacts of water projects are offset by (a) a one-time contribution of $10 per acre-foot of the project's average annual depletion, and (b) legal protection of instream flows under State law.
- **Decision rule:** The Service must find that progress toward legal protection of instream flows is sufficient to offset large depletion projects before issuing a favorable biological opinion under [[Section 7 Consultation]] of the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- **Purpose:** Provide flows from Muddy Creek Reservoir to the 15-mile reach for the rare Colorado River fishes.
- **Authorities cited:** Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act (16 U.S.C. Sec. 661); Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956 (16 U.S.C. Sec. 742f(a)(4)); Endangered Species Act of 1973 (16 U.S.C. Sec. 1531); and the Cooperative Agreement for the Recovery Implementation Program.
- **Governing lease:** Muddy Creek Reservoir operations reference a lease with the [[Denver Water Board]] dated March 3, 1987.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The 15-mile reach (Grand Valley Irrigation Company Diversion Dam to the Gunnison River confluence) is described as important to the continued survival and recovery of the rare Colorado River fishes.
- The Service's goal is to protect flows in the 15-mile reach to maintain habitat for these fishes. The MOU flows could increase flow by 50 cfs for a 30-day period and contribute incrementally to that goal.
- Water releases are also linked to related reservoirs and operations meetings with the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] at [[Ruedi Reservoir]] and [[Green Mountain Reservoir]] (per the Muddy Creek Final EIS, Chapter 5.0).
- Protection of instream flows through the 15-mile reach is anticipated to be accomplished by the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB).
- The initial reservoir fill period (3 to 4 years) takes priority over hydrologic evaluation, and operations are driven by structural integrity requirements.
- Hydrologic data: the 1953–1982 period of record for Muddy Creek is based on synthesized data, not gauged records.

## Key Findings

**Water release commitment**
- Annual release of 3,000 acre-feet to the 15-mile reach during July 15 to October 15, as requested by the Service.
- Maximum release rate: 100 cfs, measured at the outlet works of Muddy Creek.
- Delivery to the head of the 15-mile reach is deemed to occur via the radial gate at the Orchard Mesa check structure.
- Non-contractual uses (e.g., Muddy Creek fishery releases, Colorado River recreation flows) are subordinate to these releases.

**Annual availability determination**
- After the May 1 snowmelt runoff forecast, the River District estimates releases needed for contractual obligations, wetlands mitigation, and reservoir losses.
- Water is considered available if the October 15 target storage is 50,000 acre-feet or more.
- The Service notifies the River District by June 15 of the delivery pattern.
- After a downstream river call (by the Division Engineer) stops Muddy Creek and Green Mountain Reservoirs from storing water, the River District recalculates available water within 15 days, using the same 50,000 acre-foot October 15 target.

**Hydrologic performance criteria**
- Hydrologic simulation of 1953–1982 conditions indicates the 3,000 acre-foot release would be available in three out of five years as a long-term average.
- Reference values: mean annual flow of 64,400 acre-feet; average drought deficit of 44,000 acre-feet for the 1953–1982 period.
- A period is judged hydrologically not similar if its average drought deficit exceeds 44,000 acre-feet.
- After 20 years of actual streamflow data, the 1953–1982 statistics will be reevaluated; criteria may be adjusted with consent of both parties if results differ significantly.
- The first performance evaluation occurs at the end of the fifth year after the River District declares Muddy Creek Reservoir operational.

**Compliance and enforcement**
- If runoff is similar but releases are not made in three of five years, the parties determine the reasons for the deficiency.
- If the Service finds the River District non-compliant, the Service will notify the [[Bureau of Land Management]] and the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] so they can act on subsequent permits requiring delivery.

**Alternative protection and substitution**
- If the River District develops another method to protect the 3,000 acre-feet flow (through CWCB or an alternative acceptable to the Service) within 6 years of execution, the Service will develop an alternative legal protection method with the River District.
- The River District may substitute alternate water sources for Muddy Creek water with the Service's concurrence.

**Termination and duration**
- The MOU is an interim water source. It terminates when the Service acquires water equivalent to the July 15 to October 15 Muddy Creek supply for the 15-mile reach through the Recovery Program, excluding the 10,000 acre-feet Ruedi/Green Mountain release under the June 15, 1987 biological opinion.
- The 15-mile reach is the second priority for water rights acquisition under the Recovery Program. If it is no longer a priority, acquiring 3,000 acre-feet of water rights elsewhere in the Upper Colorado River Basin satisfies the MOU.
- If the MOU remains in effect after 15 years, consultation will be reinitiated to assess the continuing need for the water and other offset options.
- During the MOU, the Service will not use Muddy Creek credited water to offset depletions of other projects.

**Modifications**
- Either party may propose modifications at any time; changes take effect upon written concurrence by both parties.
