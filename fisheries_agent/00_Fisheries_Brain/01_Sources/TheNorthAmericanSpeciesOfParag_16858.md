# North American Species of Paragnetina (Plecoptera, Perlidae)

#entomology #plecoptera #perlidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[William E Ricker]] (1949) revises the five North American species of the stonefly genus [[Paragnetina]] in the family [[Perlidae]]. It resolves a tangle of generic and specific names, argues that the American species belong in a single genus, and describes one new species, [[Paragnetina Fattigi]]. The paper also provides a dichotomous key for males and females, redescriptions, synonymies, type designations and distribution records drawn from museum and state survey collections.

## Background & Description

The author defines the subfamily Perlinae by three features: male subanal lobes that are little modified, a cleft tenth tergite with thickened posterior angles forming forward-pointing "genital hooks," and three normal ocelli. Most Perlinae are Old World species, and a broader revision was described as needed but left to future workers.

All five American species share a set of traits:

- Short genital hooks that are not cleft at the tip, with a well-developed callus (a shield-like "Schwiele") on the inner side.
- A median backward-pointing lobe on the male eighth tergite (small in *kansensis*).
- In females, a subgenital plate that is slightly to moderately produced, with a small median notch.
- A posterior extension of the male fifth tergite in *media*, *immarginata* and *fumosa*, but not in *kansensis* or *fattigi*. The author suggests this could later support subgeneric separation.

Key morphological contrasts among the species include:

- **[[Paragnetina Fattigi]]**: males are 14 mm long (19 mm to wing tip). The holotype has two small lobes on the seventh tergite and a larger eighth-tergite lobe than in *kansensis*. Its pleurites six to nine bear spinule rows, which *kansensis* lacks on six.
- **[[Paragnetina Fumosa]]**: males are 12 mm long (16–17 mm to wing tip) with uniformly smoky wings. Females are 17–20 mm long (25–28 mm to wing tip). The female subgenital plate is produced over one-fourth to one-third of the ninth sternite, with a narrow notch.
- **[[Paragnetina Immarginata]]**: larger than *fumosa*, with the genital hooks bearing terminal ventral projections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species are associated with flowing freshwater habitats. The type locality of *fattigi* is at flat shoals on the [[Flint River]] near Concord, Georgia. Records for *fumosa* include creeks and rivers across Georgia and Alabama, such as Nancy Creek, Chickamuga Creek and the Towaliga River. The paper notes that [[Paragnetina Media]] is common in the warmer brooks and rivers of the southern Ontario uplands but absent from colder trout streams. Distributions are influenced by the [[Appalachian Mountains]]: male *kansensis* have not been taken east of the range.

Range summaries:

- **[[Paragnetina Immarginata]]**: from the James Bay region of Quebec south and east through Ontario, Quebec, the Maritime Provinces, New England, New York, western Pennsylvania, and the Carolinas and Georgia. Possible New Orleans records would extend the range southwest.
- **[[Paragnetina Fumosa]]**: from the District of Columbia south to Georgia and Alabama, overlapping with *immarginata* from Virginia through Georgia.
- **[[Paragnetina Kansensis]]**: known only from Kansas, Illinois and Indiana in the males examined, plus one female from Douglas County, Kansas.
- **[[Paragnetina Media]]**: a predominantly northern species, recorded from northern Saskatchewan, northern Ontario, central Quebec, Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan, central Pennsylvania, southern New England, Virginia, North Carolina and the Ozarks of southern Missouri.

Material examined is drawn largely from the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]] and Cornell University collections. Many *media* specimens were collected by [[Frison]] and associates across Minnesota, Wisconsin, Michigan and New York, and the paper lists dozens of county-level records.

## Key Findings

**Nomenclature**

- The name [[Togoperla]] is not suitable for the American species. Its type species, *limbata*, has a greatly produced subgenital plate with no parallel among American species. The use of *Togoperla* in America therefore rests on uncertain associations, and the author recommends abandoning it for [[Paragnetina]].
- [[Banksiana]] (and its base [[Banksiella]]) is a synonym of *Paragnetina*. Klapálek's type specimen belongs to the *immarginata*–*fumosa* group, not to *kansensis*, which was the nominal type.
- [[Eccoptura]] is associated with *Perla xanthenes* as now recognized. It has priority over *Croconeuria* Needham and Claassen, though current usage places both in [[Acroneuria]].
- *Perla lurida* Hagen is a synonym of *immarginata*, based on examination of the female holotype from New Orleans.
- *fumosa* was synonymized with *immarginata* by [[Needham And Claassen]] (1925). Ricker reinstates it as a distinct species based on smaller size, darker wings, lack of terminal ventral projections on the genital hooks, and a more narrowly notched female subgenital plate.

**Typification**

- A neotype male and neallotype female of *immarginata* were designated from Ithaca, New York (male collected September 9, 1889; female September 28, 1907), to fix the identity of Say's 1823 name.

**New species**

- [[Paragnetina Fattigi]] n. sp. is described from a male holotype collected August 1, 1945, at Flat Shoals, Concord, Georgia, by P. W. Fattig. It is named for Dr. P. W. Fattig of Emory University, a prolific insect collector. The female is unknown; two lighter-colored Georgia and Florida females that resemble *kansensis* were tentatively not associated with it.

**Keys and diagnoses**

- A key separates the males by fifth-tergite shape, genital hook form and the seventh and eighth tergite lobes. The female key uses subgenital plate shape, pronotal color and ocellar triangle color.

**Taxonomic status**

- The paper argues that a comprehensive revision, likely by a worker with access to Old World material, is needed to settle the generic limits of the group.
