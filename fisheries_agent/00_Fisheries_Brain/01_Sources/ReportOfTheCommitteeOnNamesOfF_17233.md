# Report of the Committee on Names of Fishes (1951–1952)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #nomenclature #fisheries-science #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This document is the annual report of the **[[American Fisheries Society]] Committee on Names of Fishes**, chaired by Reeve M. Bailey, presented following the Society's Memphis meeting. The report addresses proposed changes to standardized common names for various North American fish species, as originally published in the 1948 [[Special Publication No. 1]] ("A List of Common and Scientific Names of the Better Known Fishes of the United States and Canada"). The Committee reviewed member feedback on proposed name changes, voted to retain 8 names, change 10, delete 1, and add 1 new species to the list. A separate list of 5 names remains under active reconsideration pending further input. The report also documents a significant procedural motion: the creation of a parallel naming committee within the **[[American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists]] (ASIH)**, proposed by ASIH President Dr. George S. Myers, to harmonize fish nomenclature between the two organizations.

## Background & Description

This is not a species-specific ecological or morphological study, but rather an **institutional nomenclature governance document**. It reflects the ongoing effort by the American Fisheries Society to standardize [[Common Names of Fishes]] across scientific, commercial, and recreational stakeholder groups in the United States and Canada.

Key procedural elements:
- Species/name changes were proposed by AFS members and circulated for comment.
- Petitions and member comments were mimeographed and distributed to Committee members prior to balloting.
- Decisions on 8 additional names were postponed pending further evidence.
- The Committee solicited membership opinion on whether to **capitalize approved common names** of fishes, following the convention used in ornithology.

The taxa discussed span multiple families, including catostomids (carpsuckers), merlucciids (hakes), pleuronectids and paralichthyids (flounders), scombrids (tunas), salmonids (Pacific salmons), and scorpaenids/sebastids (rosefish/ocean perch).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While this document is primarily administrative, the taxa referenced span diverse habitats:

- **[[River Carpsucker]]** (*Carpiodes carpio*) — freshwater riverine habitats.
- **[[Silver Hake]]** (*Merluccius bilinearis*) — North Atlantic continental shelf waters.
- **[[American Plaice]]** (*Hippoglossoides platessoides*), **[[Witch Flounder]]** (*Glyptocephalus cynoglossus*), **[[Georges Bank Flounder]]** (*Pseudopleuronectes americanus dignabilis*) — cold-water demersal fishes of the Northwest Atlantic, notably the Georges Bank region.
- **[[Summer Flounder]]** (*Paralichthys dentatus*) and **[[Fourspot Flounder]]** (*Paralichthys oblongus*) — Atlantic coastal and shelf flatfish.
- **[[Little Tuna]]** (*Euthynnus alletteratus*) — pelagic warm-water Atlantic scombrid.
- **[[Coho Salmon]]**, **[[Sockeye Salmon]]**, and **[[Chinook Salmon]]** (*Oncorhynchus* spp.) — anadromous North Pacific salmonids, subject to renaming from regional common names ("silver salmon," "red salmon," "king salmon") toward standardized scientific-common name pairings.
- **[[Rosefish]]/[[Ocean Perch]]** (*Sebastes* spp.) — North Atlantic groundfish targeted by commercial fisheries, with common name ambiguity affecting market and regulatory communication.
- **[[Skipjack]]** (also called "river herring" or "golden herring") — anadromous/clupeid species under naming review.

The push for standardized nomenclature reflects the practical ecological and economic need for consistent species identification across **fisheries management, commercial harvest reporting, and sport fishing** contexts, where regional common name variation can create confusion in stock assessment and regulation.

## Key Findings

- **Names retained without change** (approved unanimously or with ≤1 dissenting vote):
  - River Carpsucker — *Carpiodes carpio*
  - Silver Hake — *Merluccius bilinearis*
  - American Plaice — *Hippoglossoides platessoides*
  - Summer Flounder — *Paralichthys dentatus*
  - Fourspot Flounder — *Paralichthys oblongus*
  - Georges Bank Flounder — *Pseudopleuronectes americanus dignabilis*
  - Witch Flounder — *Glyptocephalus cynoglossus*
  - Little Tuna — *Euthynnus alletteratus*

- **Names under active reconsideration** (pending further member input on geographic distribution, prevalence, and user class):
  | List # | Current Approved Name | Suggested Substitute |
  |---|---|---|
  | 91 | Skipjack | River herring / Golden herring |
  | 104 | Silver salmon | Coho salmon |
  | 105 | Red salmon | Sockeye salmon |
  | 107 | King salmon | Chinook salmon |
  | 474 | Rosefish | Ocean perch |

- **Committee decisions on the broader name list**: 8 retained, 10 changed, 1 deleted, 1 new species added (specific identities of the changed/deleted/added names beyond the retained list are not detailed in this excerpt).

- **Procedural proposal**: A motion was raised and **passed** to approve the creation of a corresponding **Committee on Common Names within the ASIH**, composed of the same individuals serving on the AFS Committee, in order to jointly standardize fish nomenclature between the two societies. This aligns with Section 15 of the AFS Bylaws, which directs the Committee on Names to "coordinate its activities with those of other societies and organizations throughout the world."

- **Open question raised to membership**: Whether approved common names of fishes should be capitalized in publications, mirroring the taxonomic convention already established in [[Ornithology]].

- **Historical significance**: This report represents an early institutionalized effort at cross-organizational standardization of [[Fish Common Names]] in North America, a precursor to modern unified nomenclature systems maintained jointly by AFS and ASIH.
