# Seasonal Variations in Growth of the Asiatic Clam *Corbicula fluminea* in a Southern Illinois Fish Pond

#malacology #freshwater-biology #corbiculidae

## Executive Summary

This study by J. K. Buttner and B. C. Heidinger, from the [[Cooperative Fisheries Research Laboratory]] at [[Southern Illinois University]], Carbondale, measured daily and seasonal growth of the invasive [[Asiatic Clam]] ([[Corbicula fluminea]]) in a 0.06 ha [[Southern Illinois Fish Pond]]. Clams were caged on pond substratum during summer, fall and winter and then recovered and measured. Growth in length and weight varied with season and clam size, and water temperature and [[Phytoplankton]] composition were recorded as environmental covariates. The authors describe the clam's biology as poorly known and note that few earlier studies addressed growth or seasonal variation in growth.

## Background & Description

*Corbicula fluminea* (Müller) was first collected in the United States in 1938 from the Columbia River, Washington. It has since become established in all river drainage systems except the Great Lakes and Northeast. The species is regarded as a nuisance: it may outcompete native mollusks, causes economic problems in irrigation canals and power plants, and undergoes periodic die-offs. Its ecological range and economic importance are documented in earlier work, but its basic biology is poorly understood, and seasonal growth data were lacking.

The study aimed to determine the daily and seasonal growth rates of *Corbicula* and the effect of clam size on absolute and relative growth in a southern Illinois pond.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** A 0.06 ha fish pond in southern Illinois. Cages were placed in 0.5 m of water on the pond substratum.
- **Cage design:** Two open-top cages, each 0.3 m² and 9 cm deep, filled to 3 cm with pond substratum. Twenty-five clams were placed in each cage (50 per season).
- **Water temperature:** Surface temperatures were recorded daily between 0700 and 0900. Summer and fall averages differed significantly from winter (P = 0.001). Fall averaged 11.2 °C, and winter averaged 3.0 °C. The summer average appears in the scanned text as "y.7 °C" and is uncertain. On 2 February, when the pond was ice-covered, the water temperature was 4.7 °C.
- **Phytoplankton:** Sampled with an integrated column sampler and identified by the method of [[Vollenweider 1969]]. Densities stayed relatively constant across all observations. Chlorophytes dominated in summer, chrysophytes in winter, and fall assemblages mixed the two groups.
- **Predation and disturbance:** Low fall recovery was attributed to [[Muskrat]] activity. Muskrat tracks were seen along the shoreline, and digging marks were found in the substratum of the test cages. Clams not recovered were presumed lost, because no labeled empty shells were collected.

## Key Findings

### Study design and recovery

| Duration of run (days) | 62 | 40 | 40 |
| Clams stocked (no.) | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| Density stocked (no./m²) | 78 | 78 | 78 |
| Density stocked (g/m²) | 256 | 273 | 299 |
| Clam recovery (%) | 82 (±1.8) | 60 (±3.4) | 98 (±1.4) |

Overall recovery was 82% in summer, 60% in fall and 98% in winter. Stocked lengths ranged from about 14 to 32 mm across seasons, and weights ranged from about 0.95 to 11 g. Several values in the scanned table are garbled.

### Growth rates

- **Summer and fall:** Growth rates ranged from 0.012 mm/day and 0.054 g/day. Summer and fall length growth did not differ significantly (P = 0.29).
- **Winter:** Growth rates ranged from about 0.008 mm/day to 0.009 g/day. Winter growth was significantly lower than summer and fall (P = 0.001).
- **Growth in winter:** Clams kept growing even when the water averaged 3.0 °C. A crystalline style was present in most clams examined on 2 February, when the pond was ice-covered.

### Effects of size

- **Relative weight gain:** Smaller clams gained significantly more weight per day, relative to their body weight, across all observations (P = 0.01).
- **Absolute weight gain, summer:** Larger clams gained significantly more weight per day than smaller clams (P = 0.046 as printed).
- **Absolute weight gain, fall and winter:** Absolute gain was similar across clam sizes in these seasons. Smaller clams gained relatively more weight per day in fall (P = 0.01).

### Environmental relationships and comparison with literature

- The authors note that summer and fall growth were similar even though water temperatures differed. Earlier work by Sickel and others also reported winter growth, but at greatly reduced rates. Other studies cited reported growth or feeding only above certain temperatures, such as 10 °C or 24 °C. The authors conclude that feeding and growth occur mainly when clams are actively feeding.
- The authors state that the limited growth data in the literature are quite variable, and that their findings for *Corbicula* add to that record.
