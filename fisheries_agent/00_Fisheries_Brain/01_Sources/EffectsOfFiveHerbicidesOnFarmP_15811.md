## Executive Summary

This 1959 study by Alfred W. Eipper (Cornell University, Dept. of Conservation) evaluated the efficacy and fish toxicity of five aquatic herbicides — **Delrad** (dehydroabietylamine acetate), **Fermate** (ferric dimethyldithiocarbamate), **Phygon-XL** (2,3-dichloro-1,4-naphthoquinone), **copper sulfate**, and **sodium arsenite** — applied across 30 central New York farm ponds (70 total applications, 1953–1956). The work targeted control of filamentous green algae and rooted [[Submersed Aquatic Vegetation]] while monitoring effects on a diverse assemblage of warm-water and cold-water pond fishes. Delrad, Fermate, and copper sulfate at 0.5 p.p.m. were similarly effective against unbranched filamentous algae, but copper sulfate was safer and cheaper, making it preferable where small fish are present. Sodium arsenite (4 p.p.m.) was the only compound effective against branched algae (*Hydrodictyon*, *Cladophora*) and rooted aquatics, with no observed fish toxicity. The study is as much a cautionary methodological paper as an efficacy study, highlighting confounded variables in unreplicated field herbicide trials.

## Background & Description

This paper is not a morphological study of fish anatomy; however, it does record **size-class data** relevant to species vulnerability to herbicide toxicity:

| Species | Size Range Tested (inches) |
|---|---|
| [[Golden Shiner]] | 0.5–4 |
| [[White Sucker]] | 1.5–4 |
| [[Largemouth Bass]] | 1–12 |
| [[Bluegill]] | 1–6 |
| [[Channel Catfish]] | 9–16 |
| [[Trout]] (brook, rainbow, brook x brown hybrid) | 2–15 |
| [[Fathead Minnow]] | 0.75–2.5 |

Small fish (≤3 inches, especially young-of-the-year [[Golden Shiner]] and [[White Sucker]]) were identified as the most sensitive size class to herbicide toxicity, and the paper explicitly notes that light-to-moderate kills of small fish may have gone undetected due to methodological limitations (visual carcass counts within 48 hours).

Notably, **Fermate** exposure was associated with a distinct pathological effect: complete blindness in one [[Northern Pike]], one [[Largemouth Bass]], and two common sunfish 23 days post-treatment, plus severe fin erosion in 3-inch trout during preliminary aquarium trials — suggesting direct tissue damage rather than acute lethal toxicity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Small central New York farm ponds (0.1–0.7 acre, mean 0.3 acre; max depth 7–9 ft), a managed freshwater lentic ecosystem subject to fisheries research since 1949.
- **Water chemistry** (from 16 ponds, pre-treatment):
  - pH: 7.6–8.8 (mean 8.4)
  - Total alkalinity: 60–170 p.p.m. (mean 104 p.p.m.)
  - Surface temperature: 63–83°F (mean 76°F)
  - Bottom temperature: 60–79°F (mean 74°F)
- **Plant community**: Dominated by unbranched filamentous green algae (*Spirogyra*, *Zygnema*), branched algae (*Hydrodictyon*, *Cladophora*), and rooted aquatics (*Potamogeton*, *Elodea canadensis*, *Myriophyllum*, *Lemna*, *Chara*, *Marsilea*, *Typha*). 30–100% of pond surfaces were algae-covered pre-treatment (except sodium arsenite ponds).
- **Fish assemblage**: Mixed cold-water ([[Brook Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], hybrid trout) and warm-water species ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Carp]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Brown Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[White Sucker]]) — reflecting typical stocked farm pond management practice in the [[Fish Management Techniques]] research program initiated in 1949.
- **Trophic/ecological interactions**: Herbicide-induced algal die-off was frequently followed by algal regrowth within 2–3 weeks; sodium arsenite treatments were distinctively followed by dense [[Phytoplankton Bloom|phytoplankton blooms]] that suppressed filamentous algae regrowth, indicating a shift in primary producer community structure post-treatment.
- **Temperature dependence**: Efficacy of Delrad and copper sulfate against algae declined below ~65°F, implicating temperature as a confounding ecological variable in field herbicide efficacy.

## Key Findings

- **Algicide efficacy at 0.5 p.p.m.**: [[Delrad]], [[Fermate]], and [[Copper Sulfate]] were equally effective (85–97% kill) against unbranched filamentous green algae; none of these three adequately controlled branched algae (*Hydrodictyon*, *Cladophora*) at tested concentrations.
- **Sodium arsenite superiority for branched algae/rooted plants**: At 4 p.p.m., [[Sodium Arsenite]] achieved ~97–100% control of filamentous algae, *Hydrodictyon*, *Cladophora*, and rooted aquatics across 10 ponds, with maximal effect delayed 7–10 days post-treatment (vs. ≤4 days for the other three compounds).
- **Fish safety hierarchy**: Copper sulfate showed clear safety and cost advantages over Delrad and Fermate for ponds with small fish; no fish kills were recorded from copper sulfate at ≤1 p.p.m. under alkalinities ≥60 p.p.m. (though a partial golden shiner kill occurred at >1 p.p.m. in a low-alkalinity 45 p.p.m. pond, suggesting alkalinity-dependent copper toxicity).
- **Fermate tissue toxicity concern**: Non-lethal but pathological effects (blindness, fin erosion) raise concern about sub-lethal tissue damage, making Fermate less suitable where fish are a management priority.
- **Phygon-XL inconclusive**: At 0.25 p.p.m., neither fish nor algae were killed; testing was judged too limited (only 7 ponds) to draw firm conclusions about efficacy or safety.
- **Delrad size-specific toxicity thresholds** (Table 4): Maximum safe concentrations varied strongly by species/size — e.g., golden shiners 0.5–1.5 in. safe below 0.25 p.p.m., while bass (2–12 in.), bluegill (1–6 in.), catfish (9–16 in.), and trout (4–10 in.) tolerated >0.5–1 p.p.m.
- **No oxygen-depletion confound**: All recorded fish kills occurred within 36 hours of treatment and lacked symptoms typical of oxygen-depletion kills, supporting direct herbicide toxicity as the causal mechanism.
- **Methodological critique**: The 10-pond Delrad dataset (Table 5), despite extensive physical-chemical-biological data collection, was judged fundamentally uninterpretable due to lack of replication and experimental controls — each pond effectively represented an unreplicated, non-comparable single test, a key cautionary conclusion of the paper for future [[Aquatic Herbicide Field Trial Design]].
- **Application method matters**: The "float method" (Delrad 70) only killed algae within a 20-ft radius of the float due to restricted water circulation from dense algal mats, underscoring delivery-method limitations distinct from chemical efficacy per se.
