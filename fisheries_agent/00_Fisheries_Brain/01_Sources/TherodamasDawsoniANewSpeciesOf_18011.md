# Therodamas dawsoni New Species Of Parasitic Copepod From The West Coast Of Panama

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Ergasilidae #parasitic-copepod #Cyclopoida

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic description establishes *Therodamas dawsoni* as a new species of parasitic copepod (Cyclopoida: Ergasilidae) from the Pacific coast of [[Panama]]. A single female specimen was collected from the pectoral fin base of a stargazer fish, [[Dactyloscopus thysannotus]], at Chiriqui, Panama on 21 June 1971. The species is distinguished from the two other known *Therodamas* species by its shorter anterior head/neck region (one-fourth vs. one-half body length), different leg seta formula, and egg sac length equal to body length. The genus exhibits unique morphological modifications including a neck-like separation of anterior cephalic appendages from mouthparts, which may warrant future subfamily-level separation within [[Ergasilidae]].

## Background & Description

The genus *Therodamas* was originally described by Krøyer in 1863 based on *T. serranus* parasitic on [[Serranus]] sp. from the Danish West Indies. Wilson (1917) initially assigned the genus to [[Lernaeidae]], but Thomsen (1949) reassigned it to [[Ergasilidae]] based on morphological examination of additional specimens from Uruguay. The family placement is confirmed by the morphology of appendages typical of ergasilids.

**Holotype measurements** (female, USNM 141308):
- Total length: 2.17 mm
- Head length (to posterior margin of neck): 0.56 mm
- Head width (at lobes): 0.42 mm
- Neck width: 0.31 mm
- Genital segment and abdomen length: 0.30 mm
- Genital segment width: 0.32 mm
- Abdomen length: 0.09 mm
- Abdomen width: 0.16 mm

**Morphological characteristics:**
- Anterior head with lateral lobes posterior to second antenna at neck junction
- Neck appearing somewhat contracted in specimen
- Posterior head expanding to broad shoulders with no clear head-thorax demarcation
- Genital segment with 2 transverse rows of short hairs on ventral surface
- Abdomen bilobed with deep postmedian incision
- Caudal ramus on lateral edge of abdomen bearing 4 terminal setae
- First antenna 5-segmented with several long setae per segment
- Second antenna a stout claw with broad spine near inner midmargin
- Mouthparts very small, separated from other head appendages by long neck
- First maxilla simple lobe with 2 setae
- Second maxilla 2-segmented with comblike setae arrangement
- Maxilliped absent
- Legs 1-4 biramose with specific spine and seta formula
- Legs 5 and 6 absent
- Egg sacs as long as body containing 50-75 eggs

**Leg spine and seta formula:**

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The single specimen was collected attached to the base of the pectoral fin of [[Dactyloscopus thysannotus]] (stargazer) at [[Chiriqui]], Panama on the Pacific coast. The collector examined over 2,000 specimens of the host fish and found only this single parasite, indicating either extreme rarity of the copepod or that *D. thysannotus* is not the preferred host species.

The genus *Therodamas* appears to have a broad geographic distribution, with *T. serranus* from the Caribbean (Danish West Indies), *T. sphyricephalus* from Uruguay (Atlantic), and now *T. dawsoni* from the Pacific coast of Panama. Previous species were found on different host taxa: [[Serranus]] sp. and "Tachyurus barbus" (likely [[Ariidae]]).

## Key Findings

1. **New species description**: *Therodamas dawsoni* represents the third described species in the genus and the first from the Pacific Ocean.

2. **Diagnostic characters distinguishing from congeners**:
   - Anterior head/neck comprises only ~25% of total body length (vs. ~50% in *T. serranus* and *T. sphyricephalus*)
   - Higher number of setae on terminal ramal segments of legs
   - Egg sacs equal to total body length (vs. considerably shorter in *T. sphyricephalus*)

3. **Taxonomic placement**: Morphological examination confirms genus belongs to [[Ergasilidae]] rather than [[Lernaeidae]], agreeing with Thomsen's 1949 reassignment.

4. **Unique morphology**: The striking neck-like separation of anterior cephalic appendages from mouthparts is unique among ergasilids and may warrant future subfamily-level taxonomic revision.

5. **Host specificity**: Extremely low prevalence (1 parasite from >2,000 host fish examined) suggests either rarity or host misidentification.

6. **Male unknown**: Only female specimen available; male morphology remains undescribed.
