# Morphology and Morphometry of Lernaeocera lusci

#ichthyology #marine-biology #pennellidae

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[J. C. Eiras]] and [[P. J. T. Santos]] (Instituto de Zoologia «Dr. Augusto Nobre», Faculdade de Ciências do Porto, 1990) documents the morphological and morphometric variability of the parasitic copepod [[Lernaeocera Lusci]] infecting [[Trisopterus Luscus]] (bib) from the Atlantic west coast of Portugal. The authors conclude that species of the genus *Lernaeocera* should not be diagnosed on traits such as trunk curvature, abdomen shape, or holdfast features, because these vary too much to be reliable. The study was supported by the [[Instituto Nacional de Investigação Científica]] as part of an ecology research programme.

## Background & Description

*Lernaeocera* copepods belong to the family [[Pennellidae]] and their adults mainly parasitize fishes of the family [[Gadidae]]. Two species are recorded from Portugal: *L. lusci* on *T. luscus* and [[Lernaeocera Caparti]] on [[Merluccius Merluccius]] (hake). Earlier authors described new species on conspicuous features such as the holdfast and trunk shape, but these traits are now considered unsuitable for diagnosis unless supported by more reliable characters. Although the high morphological variability of *L. lusci* had been noted by previous workers, the authors state that no detailed morphological and morphometric study of the species had been made before.

The adult female has several named body regions: a proboscis, a dorsal antler and lateral branches (together the holdfast), a neck, a sigmoid trunk, a genital constriction, and an abdomen. The **antennary plates**, fused at the base and made of two simple or subdivided branches, are regarded as characteristic of the species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Host:** *Trisopterus luscus* (bib), a gadid fish.
- **Attachment:** Parasites normally anchor in the gill arches. The paper also reviews earlier reports of attachment to other host sites, and one specimen in this study was attached near the host's tail.
- **Geography:** Samples came from the north-west Portuguese Atlantic coast. Earlier records from [[Mediterranean Sea]] hosts of the genus *Merluccius* are also cited.
- **Sampling source:** Fish were collected by local fishermen, and the parasites were taken from them.

## Key Findings

**Sample and staging**
- 37 adult females of *L. lusci* were randomly sampled from *T. luscus*.
- Using the Sproston and Hartley (1941) nomenclature as modified by Evans et al. (1983), all specimens were fully developed females at stage X, with external egg strings present.

**Holdfast variability**
- The dorsal antler was present in all specimens, with a mean diameter of 0.5 ± 0.2 mm.
- Branching ranged from small buds to complex Y-shaped subramifications. Both types occurred in 43% of specimens. Branch number ranged from one to six, most commonly three to four.
- Lateral holdfast branches occurred in only 26% of specimens, with a mean length of 1.1 ± 0.4 mm and a diameter of 0.4 ± 0.1 mm. The right lateral branch was usually shorter than the left.

**Body form**
- Neck length varied widely (1.6–5.5 mm). Longer necks (4.0–5.5 mm) were often bent, with flexion up to nearly 90°.
- The proboscis is triangular, and the antennary plates were present in all specimens.
- The trunk is sigmoid. Its maximal width is 3.2 to 7.0 times the minimal neck width.
- The genital constriction was well marked in some specimens and barely visible in others.
- The abdomen is usually subcylindrical with a blunt end. Its long axis forms an angle of more than 90° with the posterior genital complex, in some cases approaching 180°.

**Atypical attachment**
- One fully developed female was found anchored in the host's tail. Its overall measurements fell within the range of gill-embedded specimens.
- It had a balanced holdfast with a dorsal branch of 2.1 mm and lateral branches of 1.9 mm (left) and 1.3 mm (right), all with subramifications.
- One cephalic antler had pierced the skin and sat outside the body in a small open wound. The authors judge this attachment less efficient than gill-arch anchorage.

**Taxonomic conclusions**
- Following [[Kabata 1979]], the authors accept three species: [[Lernaeocera Branchialis]] (no antennary plates), *L. lusci* (with antennary plates), and [[Lernaeocera Minuta]] (small body size).
- Antennary plates also vary considerably, as shown by scanning electron microscopy.
- The validity of *L. caparti* (Machado-Cruz, 1959) is doubted. The original description predates current diagnostic criteria, and the presence or absence of antennary plates was not reported. The authors propose designating it *Lernaeocera* sp., and state that the only valid species reported from Portuguese waters is *L. lusci*.

**Overall conclusion**
- *L. lusci* shows high morphological plasticity, and the holdfast is its most variable structure. Species descriptions in this genus should not rely on trunk curvature, abdomen shape, or holdfast features.
