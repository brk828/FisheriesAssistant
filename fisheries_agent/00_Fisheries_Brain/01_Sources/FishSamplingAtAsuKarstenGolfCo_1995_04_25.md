# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, April 1995

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #ConservationBiology

## Executive Summary
This report documents a fisheries survey conducted by Arizona State University personnel (B. Bagley, G. Knowles, and P. Sowka) at the [[ASU Karsten Golf Course]] (ASUGC) lakes on 24–25 April 1995. The primary objective was to assess the status of three native, imperiled Colorado River basin fishes — [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — which had been experimentally stocked into the ASUGC lakes in 1988 and 1991 as part of a conservation/repatriation effort using hatchery stock from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] in New Mexico. The survey recovered only a single native individual (one male razorback sucker), reinforcing a multi-year trend of declining native fish catch rates in this closed system, most plausibly attributable to cumulative post-stocking mortality.

## Background & Description
Limited individual-level morphological data were reported, restricted to the single native fish captured:

- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
  - Sex: Male
  - Total length: 374 mm
  - Condition: Tuberculate (breeding condition, reproductively active male)
  - Overall health: Appeared healthy at time of capture

No morphometric data were reported for the non-native species captured (threadfin shad, carp, yellow bullhead, yellow bass, green sunfish), nor for [[Bonytail]] or [[Colorado Squawfish]], neither of which were recovered in this sampling event.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: ASUGC lakes are small, closed (non-flow-through) lentic impoundments associated with a golf course, functioning as isolated experimental refugia for stocked native Colorado River basin fishes.
- **Community composition**: The extant fish community sampled was dominated by non-native species — [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Common Carp]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bass]], and [[Green Sunfish]] — with native [[Razorback Sucker]] present only in trace numbers.
- **Stocking history**: All three target native species ([[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]]) were originally stocked in September–October 1988, with a supplemental bonytail stocking in April 1991, both sourced from the same hatchery.
- **Sampling gear/effort**: Two gear types were deployed per lake (North Lake and South Lake): one 150-ft trammel net and one 125-ft experimental gill net, set overnight (~7-hour soak time) on 24–25 April 1995.
- **Trophic/competitive context**: The dominance of non-native piscivores/omnivores (carp, sunfish, bullhead, bass) in a closed system without recruitment inputs suggests likely competitive and/or predatory pressure on stocked juvenile native fishes, compounding potential post-stocking mortality.

## Key Findings
- **Total catch**: 36 individuals across six species (threadfin shad, carp, razorback sucker, yellow bullhead, yellow bass, green sunfish).
- **Native fish recovery**: Only **one native individual** was captured — a single male [[Razorback Sucker]] (374 mm TL, tuberculate, healthy) — with **no bonytail or Colorado squawfish** recovered.
- **Trend**: Catch per unit effort (CPUE) of native species has remained low since Autumn 1992, across six total survey events over four years.
- **Probable cause**: Given the small, closed nature of the ASUGC lakes (precluding recruitment or immigration from outside sources), the authors conclude that **cumulative mortality since the original 1988/1991 stocking events is the most probable explanation** for depressed native CPUE, rather than sampling gear inefficiency or transient environmental factors.
- **Alternative explanations considered but deemed less likely**:
  1. Fish occupying microhabitats not effectively sampled by the deployed nets.
  2. Unknown site- or time-specific factors affecting capture probability during this particular survey.
- **Management implication**: Continued low recovery rates of stocked native Colorado River basin fishes in isolated habitats like ASUGC raise concerns about the long-term viability of such off-channel refugia for [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]] conservation programs.
- **Next steps**: A follow-up survey of ASUGC was scheduled for Autumn 1996 to continue monitoring native fish persistence.
