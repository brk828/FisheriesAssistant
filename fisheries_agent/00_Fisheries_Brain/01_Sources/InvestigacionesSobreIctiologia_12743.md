# Catálogo de los Peces de la Región Neártica en Suelo Mexicano (Investigaciones sobre Ictiología Mexicana I)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish

## Executive Summary

This 1947 article by [[Fernando De Buen]], published in the *Anales del Instituto de Biología de México* (vol. XVIII, no. 1), is the first part of a series on Mexican ichthyology. It presents an annotated checklist of freshwater and freshwater-entering fish species of the Nearctic region in Mexico, excluding the Rio Grande del Sur subregion, and proposes a zoogeographic division of Mexico's continental fish fauna into regions, subregions, provinces and sections. The catalogue lists the species under their families, with synonyms, type localities and Mexican collection sites. The author also reviews earlier Mexican ichthyological literature, from [[Jordan And Evermann]] (1896–1900) through [[Seth Eugene Meek]] (1904) to [[Carl Tate Regan]] (1908), and frames the fauna as the meeting ground of Neotropical and Nearctic faunas.

## Background & Description

- **Author and series:** De Buen, writing as part of "Investigaciones sobre Ictiología Mexicana," with an acknowledgement that [[Carl L. Hubbs]] revised the nomenclature in a brief visit.
- **Scope:** Freshwater fishes and marine species that ascend rivers or reach river mouths. Marine species that only enter brackish water are included selectively; the author states that the list is incomplete and will grow with later research.
- **Historical sources cited:** [[Jordan And Evermann]] (1896–1900, four volumes on North and Central American fishes); Meek (1904, *The Fresh-water Fishes of Mexico, North of the Isthmus of Tehuantepec*), based on expeditions with F. E. Lutz in 1901 and alone in 1903 across roughly sixteen Mexican states; Regan (1908) in *Biologia Centrali-Americana*; Eigenmann (1910); Jordan, Evermann and Clark (1930); Beltrán (1934); Hubbs (1936) on Yucatán freshwaters; and De Buen's own 1940 mimeographed list.
- **Methods noted:** Systematic catalogue format with synonymy and localities; zoogeographic analysis based on family and genus distributions; comparison of provinces across river basins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The author describes Mexico's continental fish fauna as highly varied, with cave-dwelling forms, marine fishes that enter brackish lagoons and rivers, and two opposing faunal communities: Neotropical fishes advancing north from the south, and Nearctic fishes moving south through the Río Bravo and Colorado corridors. Relict "faunal islets" (*islotes faunísticos*) mark past range contractions.

Geological history shapes this distribution. The Tethys Sea and the joining of the North Atlantic (Nearctic) and Gondwanan (Neotropical) landmasses set up the mixing zone. Uplift of the Sierra Madre and volcanic activity created the Central Mexican highlands, the Lerma and Balsas drainages, and lake systems such as Chapala, Cuitzeo, Pátzcuaro and Zirahuén.

Habitat types described include lowland rivers with warmer, less oxygenated water; upland streams with cold, oxygenated water suited to salmonids; isolated desert springs and cienegas; saline lakes; and Yucatán cenotes. Migration is described as trophic (feeding, driven by thermotropism) or nuptial (spawning, driven by rheotropism). Examples include mullets, salmonids and Dorosoma.

## Key Findings

**Zoogeographic framework (proposed by De Buen):**
- **Región Neártica:** Subregions: Cortés (with Provincia Desértica, Colorado and Sonora sections; Provincia del Bravo with sections Soto la Marina, San Juan, Conchos, Santa María, Yaqui and Nazas); Provincia Sur California and Provincia Sinaloa; Subregión Río Grande del Sur (Provincias Gran Lerma, Valle de México and Santiago, the last including sections Tepic, Ameca and an interchange section, Mezquital).
- **Banda de Transición:** Provincia Balsas (sections Balsas oriental, Balsas occidental, Armería) and Provincia Pánuco (sections Tampico, Valles, Moctezuma).
- **Región Neotropical:** Provincia Yucateca and Provincia Ístmica (sections Champotón, Usumacinta, Veracruzana, Tehuantepec).

**Checklist summary:** The catalogue covers roughly 30 families and well over 150 taxa, with many subspecies and synonyms. The largest groups are Cyprinidae (*Notropis*, *Gila*, *Dionda* and related genera), Catostomidae (*Catostomus*, *Moxostoma*, *Carpiodes*), Ictaluridae, Poeciliidae (*Gambusia*, *Poeciliopsis*, *Mollienesia*), Goodeidae and Cyprinodontidae. Key marine-origin families are Ariidae, Centropomidae, Sciaenidae and Engraulidae.

**Notable records:**
- *Lepisosteus osseus*, *L. platostomus* (Río Bravo basin) and *L. spatula* near Tampico.
- *Dorosoma cepedianum* (Río Bravo to Río de la Antigua, Veracruz) and *D. smithi* (Pacific slope, Piaxtla and Yaqui).
- *Salmo clarkii*, a mountain trout from the Sierra Madre Occidental, and *Salmo gairdneri nelsoni* from northern Baja California.
- *Micropterus salmoides* introduced and spreading in northeastern Mexico.
- *Xyrauchen texanus* in the Colorado River, the only Catostomid there.
- *Cichlasoma beani*, a relict population on the Presidio and Chametla rivers.
- *Tetrapleurodon spadiceus*, the only Petromyzontid, restricted to the Lerma basin and Lake Chapala.
- Goodeid species endemic to the Río Grande del Sur subregion, including the *Algansea* "islet."

**Principal conclusions:**
1. Mexico's continental fish fauna is a mixture of Nearctic and Neotropical elements, with transitional zones where the two communities meet.
2. Cyprinids reach their southern Nearctic limit in Mexico and show two invasion routes (Río Bravo, broad and deep; Colorado, modest).
3. The Río Grande del Sur subregion is faunistically distinct, with endemic Goodeidae, Atherinidae and Cyprinidae.
4. Taxonomy remains provisional. Many names are based on older literature, and subspecies and hybrids need revision.
5. The author plans further papers on the Neotropical zone and the Río Grande del Sur subregion.
