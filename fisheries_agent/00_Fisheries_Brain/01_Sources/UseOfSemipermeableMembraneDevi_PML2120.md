# Semipermeable Membrane Devices for Bioavailable Organochlorine Pesticides in Irrigation-Impacted Streams

#ichthyology #ecotoxicology #freshwater-ecology #organochlorine-pesticides

## Executive Summary

This study by Petty, Huckins, Martin and Adornato (1995, *Chemosphere* 30(10)) used [[Semipermeable Membrane Device]] (SPMD) samplers to measure the bioavailable fraction of [[Organochlorine Pesticides]] in streams carrying [[Irrigation Drainwater]] in the [[Lugert-Altus Watershed]] of southwest Oklahoma. SPMDs were deployed at six sites in 1992 over two consecutive 45-day intervals (spring and summer). Residues of [[Toxaphene]], the [[DDT]] complex ([[DDT]], [[DDE]], [[DDD]]), [[Dieldrin]] and [[Endrin]] were detected at most impacted sites. Calculated ambient toxaphene concentrations ranged from 300 to 7,000 ng/L, and at three sites they equaled or exceeded the reported 96-hour [[LC50]] for [[Channel Catfish]]. The authors conclude that SPMDs provide a practical way to estimate dissolved, bioavailable contaminant concentrations.

## Background & Description

**Pesticide context.** Organochlorine (OC) pesticides were widely used from 1940 to the mid-1970s. U.S. DDT consumption peaked at 70,000 metric tons in 1963, and aldrin/dieldrin use reached about 9,000 metric tons in the mid-1960s. Toxaphene was applied at about 7,300 metric tons per year into the mid-1980s, mainly on cotton. Because OCs are persistent, transported through the atmosphere and bioaccumulate, they occur in fish, marine mammals and humans. They have also been linked to reproductive problems in raptors such as [[Bald Eagle]] and to bat mortality. Measuring the bioavailable (dissolved) fraction is difficult because inputs are episodic and non-point.

**The SPMD sampler.** The SPMD is a layflat [[Polyethylene]] tube containing a thin film of [[Triolein]], a high-molecular-weight neutral lipid. The authors state that the polyethylene's free volume allows organic molecules to dissolve into and diffuse through the membrane, which mimics transfer across biological membranes. The device therefore integrates the readily bioavailable contaminant fraction over time. Each sampler in this study held 1 mL (0.91 g) of triolein in an 86 cm tube with 387 cm² of surface area.

**Study area.** The Lugert-Altus Watershed is intensively farmed, with cropland covering 98% of the land surface. Irrigation water is delivered from [[Altus Reservoir]] through canals. The only natural streams, [[Bitter Creek]] and [[Stinking Creek]], receive irrigation return flows and drain to the [[Salt Fork of the Red River]] and [[North Fork of the Red River]]. (The watershed area figure is garbled in the scanned text and is not reported here.) Fish and wildlife resources are limited by farming, but the upper and lower ends of the watershed and the reservoir are important habitat for migratory birds. The [[Texas Horned Lizard]] and the Texas kangaroo rat, both candidate species for protection under the [[Endangered Species Act]], also occur in the area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Irrigation impacts:** Sites downstream of the creek mouths and lower river reaches received agricultural drainwater. Sites on the upper North and upper Salt Forks, presumed unaffected, were largely free of residues.
- **Persistence:** The authors note that these compounds have been banned in the U.S. for more than 20 years, yet residues remained at measurable levels. They attribute this to the persistence of OCs in aquatic systems, especially where aquatic resources are limited.
- **Endocrine effects:** [[Dieldrin]], the DDT complex and toxaphene are reported to have reproductive and endocrine-disrupting effects. These "environmental hormones" have been linked to decreased fertility and gender alterations in birds, fish and mammals.
- **Toxicity benchmark:** The 96-hour LC50 for fingerling channel catfish of 2.8 µg/L for toxaphene was used as the benchmark for risk.
- **Regional sampling:** Two-season sampling supports the idea that residue levels fluctuate seasonally with agricultural and irrigation activity.

## Key Findings

### Methods and quality control
- **Deployment:** SPMDs were placed in shrouds of galvanized steel, oriented horizontally with stream flow, at six sites. They were deployed for two consecutive 45-day intervals: spring (late May through June) and summer (July through early August) 1992.
- **Processing:** Samples were cleaned by hexane dialysis, [[Gel Permeation Chromatography]] and [[Florisil]] chromatography, then analyzed by capillary [[Gas Chromatography]] with [[Electron Capture Detector]] (GC-ECD). Toxaphene extracts received additional silica gel fractionation.
- **Trip blanks:** One blank per site (six total) showed no OC residues above the limits of detection (LODs).
- **Recovery:** Performance evaluation materials showed excellent recovery except for hexachlorobenzene, 2,4'-DDT and oxychlordane. Process blanks and SPMD controls were free of residues.

### Residue concentrations in SPMDs (µg/g triolein, uncorrected)
- **Toxaphene:** Highest in the lower North Fork (20 µg/g); lowest in the lower Salt Fork (4.9 µg/g). Bitter Creek averaged about 13 µg/g in spring and 18 µg/g in summer.
- **DDT:** Ranged from 1.6 µg/g (lower North Fork) to 0.24 µg/g (lower Salt Fork). Bitter Creek summer DDT was 1.1 µg/g, compared with about 0.7 µg/g in spring.
- **DDE:** Ranged from 3.5 µg/g (Bitter Creek) to 0.64 µg/g (lower Salt Fork).
- **DDE:DDT ratio:** Normally greater than 3:1. The lower North Fork ratio was 1:1 in spring and about 2:1 in summer, which the authors suggest may indicate recent DDT use.
- **Dieldrin and endrin:** Highest at Bitter Creek, followed by Stinking Creek and the lower North Fork. Concentrations were generally 0.5 to 1.0 µg/g.
- **Seasonality:** Most residues were lower in summer than in spring. Toxaphene and DDT at Bitter Creek were exceptions.
- **Replicates:** Replicate SPMDs at Stinking and Bitter Creeks in spring gave very similar concentrations.

### Calculation approach
- The sampling rates at 26 °C were 4.5 L/d for DDT, 4.2 L/d for DDE and 6.9 L/d for DDD. The authors corrected these for an average fouling impedance of 31% (F = 0.69).
- A membrane–lipid partition coefficient of 0.1 was assumed, giving a distribution of about 63% in the lipid and 31% in the membrane.
- For toxaphene, dieldrin and endrin, the high K_OW values mean equilibrium was approached or reached within 45 days, so an equilibrium model was applied.
- The authors state that uptake kinetics data are still incomplete and that studies were ongoing at the Midwest Science Center.

### Conclusions
- SPMDs can sequester enough contaminant mass for bioassays, and they offer a biologically meaningful estimate of organism exposure and toxicity.
- SPMD residues can be used to calculate bioavailable ambient concentrations, which the authors describe as a key element for risk assessment, including the risk of gender alteration.
- The authors state that few other water sampling methods provide comparable access to dissolved-phase concentrations.
- Organochlorine pesticide levels at several sites may be high enough to concern resource managers.
