## Executive Summary

This document is a taxonomic description of a new species, *Ucania browni*, published in the *Proceedings of the United States National Museum* (Vol. XXXIII, No. 1672). The scan is heavily degraded by OCR errors, so some figures are partly illegible. The authors state that the specimens were received from [[Herbert Brown]] of [[Tucson]], and that the fish differ from all previously described species. ## Background & Description

The document introduces the species as new and proposes the name *Ucania browni* in honour of the collector, Herbert Brown. The description is based on preserved specimens in [[Formalin Preservation]]. Key morphological characters are:

- **Body proportions:** head about 3.3 times in standard length (without caudal fin); body depth 3.3 in length (3.5 in males).
- **Caudal peduncle:** depth 1.9 in head length; breadth 1.6 in head length.
- **Head and mouth:** eye and interorbital width partly illegible in scan. Maxillary about equal to pupil length, with the tip reaching slightly below the upper rim of the pupil. Teeth sharp and pointed.
- **Gill and gut:** gill membranes connected across a short distance; gill openings not restricted above. Intestine not longer than the body.
- **Fins:** pectoral fin 1.4 in head length; ventrals very small (2.75 in head length); anal fin 1.3 in head length in females and equal to head length in males. The male anal fin is not modified into an intromittent organ. Dorsal fin ray count is partly illegible.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** body depth and anal fin length differ between sexes, as listed above (see [[Sexual Dimorphism]]).

## Key Findings

- **New species proposed:** *Ucania browni* is described as new to science. The authors state that it differs from all previously described species.
- **Diagnostic characters:** the species is defined by its body proportions, short and pointed dentition, connected gill membranes, and a non-modified male anal fin.
- **Colour pattern in preserved fish:**
  - Females are olivaceous, with about 8 large dark blotches along each side forming a broken lateral band.
  - In adults, the blotches continue downward on the body. In half-grown specimens, three diffuse clusters of pigment spots lie on the belly just in front of and above the anal fin.
  - The dorsal fin is dusky, with a dark blotch between the sixth and tenth rays.
  - The caudal fin is pale, with two crescent-shaped cross bands at its base.
  - The pectoral fins are obscurely marked, and the ventral and anal fins are pale.
- **Type material source:** specimens were received from [[Herbert Brown]] of Tucson, and the species is named after him (see [[Ucania Browni]]).
- **Data quality:** the scan is heavily corrupted. Several counts and measurements (fin ray counts, eye and interorbital measurements) cannot be read reliably and should be checked against the original publication.
