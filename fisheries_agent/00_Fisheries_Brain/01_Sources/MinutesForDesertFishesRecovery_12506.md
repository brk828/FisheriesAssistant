# Desert Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes - April 1987

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinodontidae #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #endangered-species #recovery-planning

## Executive Summary

Minutes from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT) meeting held April 1-2, 1987 at [[Kofa National Wildlife Refuge]], Yuma, Arizona. The primary focus was organizational preparation for a draft recovery plan for [[Desert Pupfish]] (*[[Cyprinodon macularius]]*), including discussion of subspecific forms, refugium populations, reintroduction sites, and downlisting criteria. Additional topics included [[Aravaipa Watershed]] renovation, [[Bonytail Chub]] (*[[Gila elegans]]*) reintroduction, and [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affecting endangered fish populations.

## Background & Description

The meeting addressed four recognized subspecific forms of [[Desert Pupfish]]: *C. m. macularius* (Gila form), *C. m. sonoytae* (Quitobaquito form), *C. m. califoricum* (Salton form), and *C. m.* ? (Rio Sonoyta form). The team reviewed distribution and status of each form and discussed maintenance of refugium populations in [[Arizona]] and [[California]].

Concern was expressed regarding maintenance of subspecific forms outside historic range at [[Bog Hole]], [[Research Ranch]], and [[Fort Huachuca]]. The team recommended destruction of existing populations with subsequent reintroduction of historic forms before serious recovery efforts could be initiated.

[[California Fish and Game Department]] has a management plan for existing Salton refugium populations and exploration of potential reintroduction sites. Mexico has an approved plan for inventory of fishes of [[Sonora]] including reconnaissance surveys for pupfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Recovery efforts for riverine habitats were deemed improbable, leading to recommendation of active management at the threatened level rather than full delisting. The team emphasized that all existing populations must be secure and replicated prior to consideration for downlisting.

Replication strategy involves introducing, maintaining, and reproducing pupfish populations within proper subspecific range and within a "selective arena" - diversity of habitats experiencing annual fluctuations in ambient parameters.

For [[Quitobaquito]] form, availability of sites is very restricted. Habitats outside but within vicinity of [[Organpipe National Monument]] need to be located. Tinajas in [[Cabeza Prieta National Wildlife Refuge]] have been identified for this purpose.

The [[Aravaipa Watershed]] renovation project addresses chronic source of non-native fish introductions from stock tanks. [[Green Sunfish]] and other non-natives have been problematic. A 2-year eradication program using chlorine as ichthyotoxin was planned to begin May 18, 1987, utilizing [[Bureau of Land Management]] fire helicopter for volunteer transport.

## Key Findings

**Desert Pupfish Recovery Plan Structure:**
- Part 1: Status assessment and subspecific form documentation (assignments due July 1st)
- Part 2: Divided into maintenance of existing populations and enhancement through downlisting
- Four considerations: survey needs, research, reintroduction, and downlisting criteria

**Recovery Philosophy:**
- Downlisting recommended as goal rather than delisting
- Active management program at threatened level most appropriate
- Status mix favored: protected, essential, and nonessential experimental populations
- Geographic spread and habitat diversity emphasized for reintroduction sites

**Survey Needs:**
- Inventory of potential reintroduction sites needed for all forms
- [[Bureau of Land Management]] lands in Arizona have suitable sites inventoried with mixed status
- [[U.S. Forest Service]] lands ([[Tonto National Forest]] and [[Coronado National Forest]]) require similar inventory
- [[National Park Service]] to be invited to review draft plans and participate in future meetings

**Research Needs:**
- Basic biological information adequate for recovery purposes
- Species interaction and reasons for decline or local extirpation remain unexplored
- Population response to management technique not well defined

**Downlisting Criteria:**
- Complicated by pending subspeciation description
- Divided by political boundaries for assignment purposes
- FWS Region 1, CFGD, and BLM assigned California (Salton form)
- ASU and AGFD assigned Arizona and Mexico (Gila, Quitobaquito, and Rio Sonoyta forms)
- Numerical goals (e.g., 30 populations for 3 years) to be considered in detail at next meeting, especially for [[Gila Topminnow]]

**Bonytail Chub Actions:**
- [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] to finalize recovery plans for "big river" fishes mid-April
- [[Bonytail Chub]] to be declared "functionally extinct"
- Reintroduction proposed for [[Gila River]] between [[Coolidge Dam]] and [[Ashurst-Hayden Dam]]
- Nonessential experimental classification recommended due to regulated flows for irrigation
- Research program in manipulated environment needed to justify continued maintenance of refugium populations at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]

**Glen Canyon Dam Concerns:**
- [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] recommendation for multi-level outlet on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] considered
- DFRT found [[Glen Canyon Environmental Study]] data insufficient to support recommendation
- Greatest concern: elevating water temperatures could eliminate barrier to upstream movement by non-native predators from [[Lake Mead]]
- Threat of loss of bonytail population at mouth of [[Little Colorado River]] considered too great
- LCR population considered largest, most productive within current range
- Friendly jeopardy with mitigation to fund monitoring and backup populations at Dexter NFH suggested

**Database Development:**
- Hendrickson proposed creation of Dbase 2 accounting system to track all reintroduction activities for listed fishes within DFRT responsibility
- Proposal for implementation and maintenance of fish record database to be submitted to FWS Region 2

**Interagency Coordination:**
- Letters to be forwarded to U.S. Forest Service Region 3, U.S. Army, and National Audubon regarding off-site migration concerns
- FWS Region 2 to draft letters to BLM and USFS regarding inventory and evaluation of potential pupfish introduction sites
- Recommendations regarding acceptable status (fully protected or essential) needed from land management agencies
