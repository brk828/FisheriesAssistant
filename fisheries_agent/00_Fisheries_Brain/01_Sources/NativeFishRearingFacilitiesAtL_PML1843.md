# Native Fish Rearing Facilities at Lake Mohave: Letter to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This is a 1979 letter from a Professor of Zoology at [[Arizona State University]] to the Regional Director of the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], Lower Colorado River Regional Office. The letter responds to a proposal for developing native fish rearing facilities at [[Lake Mohave]]. The author supports the effort, recommends moving quickly, and offers cooperation in planning and documentation. The species in focus are the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail]].

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Formal correspondence (letter), not a research paper.
- **Date:** 6 November 1979 (read from a scanned, partly garbled header).
- **Sender:** Professor of Zoology, Arizona State University, Tempe, Arizona.
- **Recipient:** Edward M. Hallenbeck, Regional Director, U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, Lower Colorado River Regional Office, Boulder City, Nevada.
- **Subject:** Feasibility of native fish rearing at Lake Mohave. The letter replies to the recipient's letter of 30 October 1979, which proposed the effort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter identifies the following points:

- **Target species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], both native fishes of the lower Colorado River system.
- **Site:** Lake Mohave, where backwaters are identified as usable habitat. The letter says one site, and possibly more, could be developed.
- **Habitat work:** Construction of barriers and preparation of habitats are recommended. The letter gives no further detail on the design or type of habitat.
- **Prior research:** The author states that research already completed shows that minimally prepared facilities can produce large numbers of razorback suckers. The letter says bonytail production has not yet been attempted at this scale.

## Key Findings

1. **Feasibility is supported by prior work.** The author states that earlier research has already shown that minimally prepared facilities can produce large numbers of razorback suckers.
2. **Bonytail propagation is untested.** Bonytail could also be produced, but the letter notes that this had not yet been attempted.
3. **Early construction is recommended.** The author recommends that construction of barriers and preparation of habitats begin as soon as possible, and that backwaters be used at least initially.
4. **Multiple sites may be used.** One site at Lake Mohave is likely, and more are possible.
5. **Procedure:** The author urges a vigorous effort with minimal preliminary meetings and procedural delays.
6. **Author's commitment:** The author offers cooperation and assistance in the planning phases, along with full participation in documenting the effort.
