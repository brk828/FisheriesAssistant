# Nueva Especie de Evarra Capturada en los Canales de San Gregorio Atlapulco (Valle de México)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1955 paper by [[Leopoldo Navarro]] (Laboratorio de Hidrobiología, Escuela Nacional de Ciencias Biológicas, I.P.N., Mexico City) formally describes **Evarra bustamantei**, a new cyprinid fish from the canals of San Gregorio Atlapulco in the southern Valley of Mexico. The species was separated from [[Evarra Eigenmanni]] and [[Evarra Tlahuacensis]] on morphological grounds, with the anal-ray count proving decisive after the type of E. tlahuacensis was re-examined by [[L. P. Woods]] at the Chicago Natural History Museum. The name honors [[Miguel Bustamante Y Septién]], the first Mexican naturalist to describe a fish.

## Background & Description

- **Collection:** During a field trip on 11 January 1955, Carlos García Ortiz and Leopoldo Navarro G. collected small cyprinids of the genus *Evarra* in canals of San Gregorio Atlapulco, Xochimilco lacustrine region.
- **Initial identification:** Two forms were sorted in the lab. One matched *E. eigenmanni* Woolman; the other resembled *E. tlahuacensis* Meek but differed in anal-ray count.
- **Type material:**
  - Holotype: one female, 46.8 mm standard length, from the San Gregorio Atlapulco canals.
  - Paratypes: 54 specimens (40.9–58.2 mm SL). Of these, 20 were measured for the variation table; further series came from collections of 27 March 1954 (17 specimens) and 25 April 1954 (17 specimens).
- **Diagnosis:** Blunt snout, subterminal mouth, head 4.0–4.4 in SL, body depth 4.8–5.1 in SL, D 8, A 8, lateral line scales 76–90, predorsal scales 40–48, rounded fin margins.
- **Morphology:**
  - Large, robust head without spines or scales; frontal region flat and broad.
  - Lower jaw projects slightly beyond the upper; only the grey-pigmented upper lip is visible in the subterminal mouth.
  - Pharyngeal teeth 4–4, with hooked tips; secondary-row teeth variable in number.
  - Anal fin with 8–9 rays (count in the table: 7 in 19 specimens, 8 in one); pectoral rays 14–17.
- **Coloration:** Dark brown dorsum with a conspicuous black dorsal line; a broad dark lateral band ending in a black caudal spot. Belly and cheeks are silvery in life, turning yellowish in preservative. Preserved specimens take on a greenish opaque tone.
- **Etymology:** Named for Miguel Bustamante y Septién, who described a fish from the Valley of Mexico in 1837.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The type locality is a canal system within the Xochimilco lacustrine zone, Valley of Mexico (described in the paper as the "Southern Region").
- The paper provides no data on water chemistry, habitat quality, diet, reproduction, abundance or population status. The ecological content is limited to locality and the fauna of the Valley of Mexico.
- Coexistence with *E. eigenmanni* in the same canals is implied by the simultaneous capture of both forms.

## Key Findings

1. **Recognition of a new species:** *Evarra bustamantei* differs from *E. eigenmanni* in fin shape (rounded vs. pointed), mouth position (subterminal vs. terminal), head spination (absent vs. numerous small spines), coloration (less silvery, greenish with yellow belly in preservative), and lateral-line scale count (76–90 vs. 74–88).
2. **Separation from *E. tlahuacensis*:** Anal rays are 8 in the new species versus 14 in the single known *E. tlahuacensis* type. Dr. Woods confirmed the type has 14 principal anal rays with no sign of damage or abnormality, ruling out a description error. The type of *E. tlahuacensis* also has 95 lateral-line scales.
3. **Morphometric variation:** Measurements from 20 specimens (including holotype) are summarized in Table I, with frequency counts for meristic traits (dorsal rays, anal rays, pectoral rays, scale counts, branchial spines, pharyngeal teeth).
4. **Type distribution:** Paratypes were deposited at the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (2), U.S. National Museum (2), Stanford University Museum of Zoology (2), and Chicago Natural History Museum (2).
5. **Taxonomic note:** The paper is written in Spanish with an English summary, and its bibliography cites Meek (1902, 1904), Woolman (1894), Regan (1908), Álvarez (1950) and Bórquez (1953).
