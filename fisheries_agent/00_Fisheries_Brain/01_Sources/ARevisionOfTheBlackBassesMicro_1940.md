# A Revision of the Black Basses (Micropterus and Huro) with Descriptions of Four New Forms

#ichthyology #Centrarchidae #Micropterus #Huro #taxonomy

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** This monograph revises the taxonomy of North American black basses, increasing recognized forms from 2-3 species to 7 distinct taxa across two genera: [[Micropterus]] (smallmouthed and spotted basses) and [[Huro]] (largemouthed bass). The genus Micropterus now includes three species: M. punctulatus (spotted bass) with three subspecies (punctulatus, henshalli, wichitae), M. coosae (redeyed bass), and M. dolomieu (smallmouthed bass) with two subspecies (dolomieu, velox). Four new forms are described: [[Micropterus punctulatus henshalli]] (Alabama spotted bass), [[Micropterus punctulatus wichitae]] (Wichita spotted bass), [[Micropterus coosae]] (redeyed bass), and [[Micropterus dolomieu velox]] (Neosho smallmouthed bass). Age and maturity data not systematically reported; largest M. p. punctulatus recorded at 17.5 inches (3 pounds 15 ounces) from Norris Lake, Tennessee. M. coosae reaches maximum 215 mm standard length (10.25 inches total). Growth parameters and fecundity not reported. Glossohyal teeth variably present/absent across taxa; gill-rakers typically 2+5 to 2+7 on first arch; pyloric caeca typically unbranched in Micropterus (7-9 in M. coosae), mostly bifid in Huro. Vertebral counts diagnostic: M. punctulatus and M. coosae typically 14+18=32; M. dolomieu typically 15+17=32.

**Habitat Associations:** [[Micropterus punctulatus]] occupies streams and rivers from southern Ohio to eastern Texas, Tennessee River drainage. [[Micropterus punctulatus henshalli]] inhabits Alabama River system in Mississippi, Alabama, Georgia; more elongate form at comparable sizes. [[Micropterus punctulatus wichitae]] restricted to West Cache Creek, Wichita Mountains, Oklahoma; swift-water habitat. [[Micropterus coosae]] is an upland stream specialist in southeastern drainages (Alabama to Savannah rivers), generally in uplands; locally known as "redeyed bass" and "highly regarded by local fishermen because of their gameness." Maximum recorded weight 2 pounds. [[Micropterus dolomieu velox]] inhabits swift tributaries of Arkansas River in northeastern Oklahoma and western Arkansas; "especially gamey fish" with streamlined form. [[Micropterus dolomieu dolomieu]] originally ranged from Quebec to northern Minnesota south to Arkansas and Tennessee River drainage of Alabama. [[Huro salmoides]] originally distributed from southern Canada throughout Great Lakes and Mississippi Valley to northeastern Mexico and Florida, north along coastal plain to Virginia; now widely introduced.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** Not reported.

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** [[Micropterus coosae]] noted as "a stream fish, it apparently cannot reproduce in a pond; or if it reproduces the spawn die. The young after reaching a length of several inches can stand pond water and grow rapidly." Flesh quality described as "good quality, somewhat drier than that of the large mouthed bass." Can be caught on worms, artificial lures, or live minnows. Interspecific interactions suggested by evidence of hybridization (see below).

**Environmental Drivers:** Color pattern variations correlate with habitat and age. Young M. punctulatus exhibit dark lateral stripe terminating in conspicuous basal caudal spot; adults develop ventrolateral dark streaks along scale rows. M. coosae young show transverse bands that disappear rapidly with age; in life, young have brick-red soft dorsal, caudal, and anterior anal fins with white margins; sides show bronzy olive and bright metallic blue in alternating streaks. M. d. velox young less strongly barred than typical dolomieu, bars broader and less elevated, tending to form hollow rhombs on caudal peduncle. Southeastern variants of M. d. dolomieu in Tennessee River mountains are "especially slender and have somewhat produced muzzles," with more blue-gray ground color and dusky golden markings. Scale counts vary geographically: M. p. henshalli has 68-77 lateral-line scales vs. 55-72 in typical punctulatus.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** Not reported. Document notes that "all too often the introduction of exotic species has exterminated the local forms," suggesting vulnerability of native populations to stocking programs.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** Authors recommend restricting stocking in Alabama River system waters to native species only: [[Micropterus coosae]] (redeyed bass), [[Micropterus punctulatus henshalli]] (Alabama spotted bass), and [[Huro salmoides]] (largemouthed bass). Evidence suggests potential threats from: (1) introduction of non-native bass subspecies/species that may hybridize with or displace native forms; (2) habitat modification (document notes impounded waters favor different species than historical clear waters); (3) overstocking of ponds with sunfish causing emaciation in bass populations. Hybridization documented between M. dolomieu and M. punctulatus (2 specimens), and suspected between M. coosae and M. p. henshalli. Possible extensive past hybridization may have influenced speciation patterns.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations:** (1) Restrict bass stocking in Alabama River system to native species (M. coosae, M. p. henshalli, H. salmoides) to prevent extermination of local forms. (2) Recognize that M. coosae cannot successfully reproduce in ponds; young must reach "several inches" before pond introduction. (3) Maintain awareness of subspecific variation when implementing stocking programs to avoid genetic contamination. (4) Consider that M. d. velox represents "an especially gamey fish" valuable for sport fisheries in Ozark swift-water habitats. (5) Acknowledge that recent fish surveys reveal M. punctulatus (not M. dolomieu or H. salmoides) is the most abundant bass in upper Ohio River and adjacent Ohio waters, requiring reassessment of historical distribution assumptions.

**Biological Reference Points:** Diagnostic meristic characters provided for all forms (Tables I-VII): **M. punctulatus complex** - dorsal rays typically X,12; anal rays III,10; pectoral rays 15-16; lateral-line scales 55-77 (varies by subspecies); vertebrae 14+18=32. **M. coosae** - dorsal X,12; anal III,10; pectoral 16; lateral-line scales 67-77; vertebrae 14+18=32; differs from punctulatus in color (bars vs. lateral stripe in young), more robust caudal peduncle, rounded predorsal contour. **M. dolomieu velox** - dorsal typically IX-X,13 (sum 21-23 in 76% of specimens); anal III,10-11; lateral-line scales 67-77; vertebrae 15+17=32; more elongate than typical dolomieu, especially in young (depth 3.8-4.3 vs. 3.2-3.8). **M. d. dolomieu** - dorsal typically X,14 (sum 24-25 in 93% of specimens); more robust form. **H. salmoides** - distinguished by pyloric caeca mostly bifid near base; sharply angulate spinous dorsal almost separated from soft dorsal; no scales on interradial membranes of anal and soft dorsal; 9-12 cheek scale rows; dorsal XIII,12-13; anal III,11; lateral-line scales 59-68.

[[Nomenclatural Revision]] - Micropterus punctulatus (Rafinesque 1819) replaces M. pseudaplites Hubbs 1927 based on re-examination of Rafinesque's original description from Ohio River (prominent black spots, 13 anal rays, 15 pectoral rays diagnostic). Huro Cuvier 1828 restored as valid genus for largemouthed bass, replacing Aplites (based on Lepomis pallida now considered complex of dolomieu and punctulatus). [[Type Locality]] - M. dolomieu type (Paris Museum 5243) re-examined by Pellegrin: dorsal X,13; anal III,11; pectorals 18-18; scales 10-74-17; confirms application to northern smallmouth; Lake Champlain hypothesized as probable type locality. [[Variational Analysis]] - Age-related changes documented: orbit decreases relatively with age in all taxa; jaw length increases; spinous dorsal becomes relatively lower; body depth increases (especially in dolomieu); predorsal contour changes from straighter to more rounded.
