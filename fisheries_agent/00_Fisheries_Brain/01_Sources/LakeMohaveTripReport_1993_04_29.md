## Executive Summary

This document is an interagency memorandum exchange between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Mountain-Prairie Region and the Parker Fishery Resources Office (Arizona) regarding a spring 1993 field trip to [[Lake Mohave]] to sample the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The correspondence documents observations on reservoir productivity, adult fish condition, spawning aggregation behavior, and a high mark-recapture rate (18%). A substantial portion of the reply from the Parker Project Leader (C.O. Minckley) details the history, rationale, and preliminary results of the [[Yuma Cove Project]], a grass-roots recovery effort using a natural, berm-enclosed cove as a spawning and rearing habitat for razorback sucker, along with the related [[Davis Cove]] grow-out facility used for bonytail (*Gila elegans*) and razorback fingerlings. The report highlights unresolved concerns about larval recruitment failure due to predation and forage limitation in the open reservoir, and raises genetics-management questions (effective population size, broodstock contribution) relevant to the species' recovery and delisting.

## Background & Description

Although this is not a morphometric study, several condition-related morphological observations are recorded for adult razorback sucker sampled in Lake Mohave:

- **Body condition:** Most captured fish were large-bodied and of advanced age, in generally good condition.
- **Parasite/disease load:** *Lernaea* (anchor worm) infestations and eye infections were noted as minimal.
- **Fin condition:** Only a small number of female fish showed seriously eroded anal fins, an indicator sometimes associated with spawning stress.
- **Secondary sexual/reproductive characteristics:** Most fish exhibited nuptial tuberculation (a breeding characteristic in male [[Catostomidae]]), but relatively few "ripe" (gravid/spawning-ready) individuals were caught, leading biologists to conclude sampling occurred early in the spawning run.
- **Species comparison:** Bonytail (*Gila elegans*) fingerlings are referenced as a co-stocked species alongside razorback sucker fingerlings in the [[Davis Cove]] grow-out facility, though no morphological detail is given for this cyprinid.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** [[Lake Mohave]] is a main-channel reservoir on the lower [[Colorado River]], functioning as habitat for adult razorback sucker populations of the lower basin.
- **Trophic/productivity observations:** The reservoir was described as appearing "sterile" in sampled areas, with negligible primary and secondary productivity observed and a complete absence of small fish (of any species) during sampling — suggesting either seasonal/thermal effects on productivity or a genuine lack of forage and nursery habitat.
- **Recruitment bottleneck:** Predation on eggs and emerging larval razorback sucker in the open lake is characterized as "more than a problem, it is a disaster." Insufficient food (proper size and abundance at the proper time) for larval fish is proposed as an additional/compounding limiting factor — potentially limiting production both in the open reservoir and in the semi-enclosed [[Yuma Cove]].
- **Thermal regime:** The upper reaches of Lake Mohave remain near 55°F (~12.8°C) year-round, which may influence the timing and location of spawning across the reservoir.
- **Spawning habitat:** A dense spawning aggregation of razorback sucker was observed directly in front of a levee at [[Yuma Cove]], notable because no comparable aggregation was seen elsewhere during this particular sampling trip. However, correspondence clarifies that spawning aggregations occur at multiple other locations throughout Lake Mohave (repeatedly observed by USBR biologist Tom Burke), and are influenced by water temperature, wave action, substrate, and an inflow "flume" of variable length.
- **Spawning season:** The spawning season in Lake Mohave is estimated to extend over several weeks, potentially 12 or more.
- **Managed spawning/rearing habitat:** The [[Yuma Cove Project]] is a berm-enclosed cove used as a controlled spawning and larval-rearing environment, intended to mimic "natural" conditions while reducing predation pressure relative to the open reservoir. [[Davis Cove]] serves as a secondary grow-out facility for fingerlings/sub-adults that need to reach larger size (fish were not expected to reach 300 mm in their first growing season in Yuma Cove alone).
- **Forage research:** Ongoing PhD research (Mike Horn, Arizona State University) is investigating forage limitation issues affecting larval/juvenile razorback sucker production in Yuma Cove and Lake Mohave.

## Key Findings

- **Recapture rate:** 18% of adult razorback sucker sampled in spring 1993 were found to be previously tagged — described as unusually high. Recaptures are typically dominated by fish tagged during the ongoing survey, with some proportion from earlier tagging years; full tag-history analysis was not yet available at time of writing.
- **Recruitment failure in open lake:** No small fish of any kind were observed anywhere in the sampled portions of Lake Mohave, reinforcing concern about egg/larval predation and potential forage limitation as major recruitment bottlenecks for wild-spawned razorback sucker.
- **Yuma Cove Project history:**
  - Preliminary work began in the mid-1980s using a natural, wave-formed berm; adult razorback sucker were introduced in two separate years, spawning occurred, but the berm breached in both years and fish/fingerlings escaped into the lake with unknown survival.
  - The **Native Fish Group**, a multi-agency work group (Arizona State University, University of Arizona, Arizona Game and Fish, Nevada Department of Wildlife, National Park Service, Bureau of Reclamation, USFWS), was formed in 1990, coinciding with physical reinforcement of the berm.
  - In 1991, spawning occurred but **no recruitment** resulted; the reinforced berm held.
  - In 1992, adult razorback sucker stocked into Yuma Cove spawned successfully, producing at least **300 fingerlings/sub-adults**.
  - A similar effort was underway again in 1993.
- **Davis Cove grow-out facility:** Established because fingerling razorback sucker were not expected to reach 300 mm in a single growing season. In 1992, smaller fish from Yuma Cove plus bonytail and razorback fingerlings from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] were stocked into Davis Cove; survival/growth data were not yet available at time of writing (expected later in 1993).
- **Open management/genetics questions raised:** Unanswered questions about the Yuma Cove Project's formal plan (goals, objectives, timeframes), its relationship to species recovery/delisting, number and genetic contribution of founding broodstock, effective population size (Nₑ) implications, relative cost-per-spawning-adult compared to traditional hatchery production (e.g., Dexter NFH&TC), and target stocking numbers needed for statistically sound comparison and eventual restoration stocking.
- **Urgency:** Program leaders flagged urgency in evaluating and scaling these efforts due to concern that the existing aging adult razorback sucker population in the lower Colorado River basin could experience die-off before adequate recruitment of young adults is achieved.
- **Key contacts/expertise identified:** Tom Burke and Gordon Mueller (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation) and Paul Marsh (Arizona State University) were identified as holding the most current expertise on the Yuma Cove Project.
