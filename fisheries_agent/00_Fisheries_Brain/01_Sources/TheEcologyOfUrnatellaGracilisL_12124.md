# The Ecology of Urnatella gracilis Leidy (Phylum Endoprocta)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endoprocta

## Executive Summary

This 1961 limnological paper by John G. Weise reviews the distribution and ecology of [[Urnatella Gracilis]], a freshwater endoproct (phylum Endoprocta), using bottom collections from the [[Ohio River]] made during the author's work for the [[Aquatic Life Resources Project]]. The species is found over a roughly 500-mile stretch of the Ohio River, almost exclusively in flowing water with coarse substrates, and tolerates wide ranges of dissolved oxygen, temperature, pH, alkalinity and turbidity. The paper also compiles historical records and notes morphological differences between river populations.

Note: the document is an invertebrate study, not a fish study; no fish species are treated.

## Background & Description

- **Taxon:** [[Urnatella Gracilis]] is one of only two species in the genus; the other, *U. indica*, was described by Seshaiya (1946/1947) from southern India. Together they constitute the reported freshwater endoproctan fauna of the world.
- **Morphology:** Colonies are branched, with calyces borne on the branches. Ohio River colonies showed extensive branching (one colony had 5 main branches and many side branches producing 24 calyces). The maximum number of segments was 8 in Ohio River specimens versus 18 in specimens examined by [[Leidy]].
- **Historical description:** Leidy (1851) described the species from the [[Schuylkill River]] in Pennsylvania.
- **Earlier authors' remarks:** [[Davenport]] (1904) stated that Illinois River specimens branched more profusely than the original Pennsylvania specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** Published records include the Schuylkill River (Pennsylvania), Scioto River (Ohio, via a unionid shell), Illinois River near Havana (Kofoid, Richardson), Licking River (Kentucky, Williams 1930), Lake Erie locations (Rogick 1936), Clinton River (Michigan), Grand River above Ionia (Michigan), Tippecanoe River near Pulaski (Indiana), and the Meuse River, Belgium (Damas 1938), the only record outside North America. Two observational reports (Lake Dallas, Texas; Mississippi River near Fairport, Iowa) are also cited.

**Ohio River collections.** Weise's collections were made at Miles 113.7, 326.0, 443.8, 458.0, 596, 598, 600.0, 617.0, 618.0 and 630.0 (miles downriver from Pittsburgh), spanning about 500 miles. All collections were in unidirectional currents of roughly 1/2 to 5 mph.

**Substrate and associates.** The species was attached to rocks, sticks and molluscan shells, notably *Amblema* shells and occasionally *Pleurocera canaliculatum*. Up to 1,000 or more specimens were counted on a single 3-inch rock. None were found on sand or mud. [[Hydra Americana]] was associated with it on almost all occasions. Other associates included *Paludicella articulata*, *Canthocamptus* sp., *Spongilla* sp., *Vorticella* sp., *Carchesium* sp., *Stentor* sp., a colony of *Cordylophora lacustris* and a floscularid rotifer.

**Plankton and diet.** The potamoplankton comprised more than 50 species of river diatoms, blue-green and green algae, protozoans, rotifers, cladocerans and copepods. Specimens fed on the diatom *Melosira varians* and the green algae *Pediastrum duplex* and probably *P. simplex*. [[Richardson]] (1921) had also reported diatom feeding in the Illinois River.

**Water chemistry (Table 1, Ohio River, 11 sampling events, Miles 113.7 to 617.0):**
- Dissolved oxygen: as low as 4.2 mg/L by day at polluted Miles 617 and 630, with saturation reaching a daytime low of 44.6%; night values were presumably much lower.
- pH at collection sites: 7.1 to 7.8. Seven-year data from the [[Ohio River Valley Water Sanitation Commission]] gave a range of 4.8 to 8.9.
- Water temperature on collection dates: 13.7 to 30°C; temperatures as low as 1.0°C have been recorded along the collecting area.
- Alkalinity: upper river total alkalinity of 8 mg/L (seven-year data); lower river up to 142 mg/L; at collection sites 93 to 94 mg/L.
- Turbidity: 2 to 2400 mg/L at collection sites. The highest value would seem destructive to small filter-feeders, yet those sites supported large numbers of the species.

## Key Findings

- *Urnatella gracilis* tolerates wide physical and chemical variation, including low dissolved oxygen, high turbidity and a range of pH and alkalinity.
- It occurs only in flowing water on coarse substrates (gravel, rubble, boulders), never on sand or mud.
- Its population spans roughly 500 miles of the Ohio River, with densities of up to 1,000+ individuals on one rock.
- Diet includes diatoms and green algae drawn from the river plankton.
- Ohio River colonies differ morphologically from the original Pennsylvania material (more branching, fewer maximum segments: 8 versus 18), which Weise suggests may reflect ecophenotypic variation and recommends comparing populations if Philadelphia material is available.
- The species' known range is broad in North America, with a single European record (Meuse River, Belgium).
