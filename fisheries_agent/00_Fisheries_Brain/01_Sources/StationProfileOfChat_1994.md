## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the **Chattahoochee Forest National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Suches, Georgia. Established in 1938 through an agreement between the U.S. Forest Service and the Bureau of Fisheries, the hatchery was originally constructed by the Civilian Conservation Corps (1936–1939). Operational authority transferred fully to the Fish and Wildlife Service in 1955, and the current infrastructure was built in two phases (1957–1961 and 1964–1965). The hatchery's sole production focus is [[Rainbow Trout]], reared to mitigate ecological damage from Federal water development projects in Georgia and to support fisheries management on Federal lands. The facility relies on gravity-fed, self-cleaning water intakes drawing from Rock Creek and Mill Creek.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide morphological or biometric data specific to the [[Rainbow Trout]] population reared at this facility (e.g., length, weight-at-age, coloration, meristic counts). Available data instead pertains to hatchery production output:

- **1990:** 1,191,959 fish produced; total weight 161,956 lbs
- **1991:** 1,070,091 fish produced; total weight 132,780 lbs
- **1992:** 978,715 fish produced; total weight 152,536 lbs

Notably, despite a declining trend in total fish numbers across 1990–1992, average individual weight appears to have increased in 1992 relative to 1991, suggesting a shift toward rearing larger/older stock or changes in stocking strategy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water Source:** Gravity-fed supply via self-cleaning intakes on **Rock Creek** and **Mill Creek**, indicative of a coldwater stream ecosystem suitable for [[Salmonidae]] rearing.
- **Habitat Type:** Constructed raceways and rearing tanks (46 raceways, 8 rearing tanks) simulating coldwater lotic conditions required for rainbow trout physiology.
- **Ecological Role:** The hatchery functions as a **mitigation facility**, compensating for habitat and population impacts caused by Federal water development projects (e.g., dams, reservoirs) in Georgia. Stocked trout also support recreational and management fisheries on Federal lands, indicating an anthropogenic-enhancement role within the regional aquatic ecosystem rather than a natural self-sustaining population.
- **Geographic Scope:** All distribution of reared [[Rainbow Trout]] is confined to the state of **Georgia**.

## Key Findings

- The hatchery has operated continuously since 1938, transitioning fully to Fish and Wildlife Service management in 1955.
- Physical infrastructure includes a Hatchery House with residences, lab, visitor center, food storage, lime mill house, generator house, office building, 8-stall garage/shop, visitor kiosk, 8 rearing tanks, and 46 raceways.
- Staffing (FTEs) remained stable at 5.0 from 1991–1993, increasing to 6.0 in the 1994 estimate.
- Operations funding showed a declining trend from $277.8K (1991 actual) to a projected $252.6K (1994 estimated), with a notable spike in maintenance funding in 1991 ($471.0K) followed by sharp declines in subsequent years.
- Production data (1990–1992) shows a **declining total number of fish stocked** (from ~1.19 million to ~978,715), while total biomass weight fluctuated, dipping in 1991 before rising again in 1992 — potentially reflecting changes in stocking size classes or production targets.
- The facility exclusively produces and distributes [[Rainbow Trout]] within Georgia, serving mitigation and Federal lands management objectives rather than commercial aquaculture purposes.
