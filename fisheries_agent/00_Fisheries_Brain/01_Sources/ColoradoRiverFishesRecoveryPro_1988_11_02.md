## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific research paper but an administrative memorandum from John Hamill, Director of the Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region 6), dated November 2, 1988. It convenes an informal coordination meeting among members of the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] and consultants at the 1988 [[Desert Fishes Council]] Symposium in Death Valley. The memo outlines administrative and policy topics relevant to the recovery of four federally significant Colorado River fish species: the [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. While it contains no direct morphological or ecological data, it provides valuable context on conservation priorities, interbasin coordination challenges, and management planning efforts of the late 1980s.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: The memo references the Colorado River system broadly, distinguishing between the **Upper Basin** and **Lower Basin** management contexts, which reflects differing habitat conditions and population statuses across the river's range.
- **[[Lake Mojave]]**: Identified as a key habitat/refuge area of specific concern for Lower Basin recovery efforts, likely functioning as a reservoir refugium population site for endangered Colorado River fishes such as the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Habitat fragmentation**: The memo explicitly raises concerns about the **fragmentation of Colorado Squawfish habitat**, indicating that connectivity loss within the river system was a recognized ecological threat by 1988.
- **Basin-scale ecology**: The document implies differing ecological/demographic conditions for these species between the Upper and Lower Colorado River Basins, necessitating separate but coordinated recovery strategies.

## Key Findings
- A coordination meeting was proposed for November 17, 1988 (7–10 PM) during the Desert Fishes Council Symposium in Death Valley to align Upper and Lower Basin recovery efforts.
- Key discussion topics included:
  1. Lower Basin concerns regarding **Bonytail Chub** and **Humpback Chub** recovery plans, including the role of [[Lake Mojave]] as a refuge/management site and differences in recovery approach between basins.
  2. Concerns about **fragmentation of Colorado Squawfish habitat** and issues with the "Bonytail chub protocol," referenced from an earlier communication (August 10, 1988 letter to Jim St. Amant).
  3. The need to develop a formal **[[Razorback Sucker]] Management Plan**.
  4. Broader **Upper/Lower Basin coordination** on endangered fish recovery efforts.
- This memorandum illustrates the institutional and administrative dimension of endangered species recovery — highlighting that management coordination, interagency communication, and basin-scale policy alignment were as critical to Colorado River fish conservation as direct field research during this period.
