# Yuma Cove Razorback Sucker Stocking (January 1991)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #razorback-sucker #conservation

## Executive Summary
This memorandum documents a cooperative fish-relocation effort conducted by [[Arizona State University]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], and the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] on 23-24 January 1991. Adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) were captured from [[Lake Mohave]] near Arizona Bay and translocated into the [[Yuma Cove]] backwater as part of a broader endangered species recovery and stocking initiative. A total of 90 razorback suckers (30 female, 60 male) were introduced into the backwater, while 20 "excess" individuals (1 female, 19 male) were tagged and returned to Lake Mohave. The operation included individual biometric data collection, health assessment, and permanent marking via [[PIT Tag]] technology.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Xyrauchen texanus* (Razorback Sucker), family [[Catostomidae]]
- **Measurements taken per individual:**
  - Total length (nearest mm)
  - Body weight
  - Sex determination
  - General health and condition assessment
  - Presence/absence of external parasites
  - Presence of pre-existing tags
- **Reproductive condition:**
  - Most captured males were "running ripe" (actively expressing milt)
  - Only 3 of the females readily expressed eggs, suggesting variable reproductive readiness across the female cohort
- **Size/condition note:** Captured individuals were reported as typical in size and condition compared to previously documented adult razorback suckers from [[Lake Mohave]]

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat:** Adult razorback suckers were located along Lake Mohave shorelines in proximity to the Yuma Cove backwater, indicating a habitat association between open reservoir shoreline zones and adjacent backwater refugia.
- **Target habitat for stocking:** [[Yuma Cove]] backwater — a lentic, likely low-flow environment presumed suitable for adult holding and potential [[Larval Razorback Sucker]] recruitment monitoring.
- **Co-occurring species (incidental catch):**
  - [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) — fewer than a dozen individuals captured incidentally
  - [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — single individual captured incidentally
- **Ecological context:** Low incidental bycatch suggests a relatively species-poor or spatially discrete capture zone, and/or effective net selectivity for larger-bodied catostomids along the shoreline habitat.
- **Conservation framing:** This effort reflects ongoing endangered species management for the razorback sucker, a species of significant conservation concern in the [[Colorado River]] basin, using translocation and backwater refugia as a recovery strategy.

## Key Findings
- **Capture method:** Trammel net seining, net dimensions 91 x 1.8 m, with 2.5-cm inner mesh and 30.5-cm outer mesh, deployed along shorelines where razorback suckers were visually observed near the backwater.
- **Total individuals processed:** 110 adult razorback suckers (30 F + 60 M stocked = 90; plus 1 F + 19 M returned = 20)
  - **Stocked into Yuma Cove backwater:** 30 females, 60 males
  - **Released back into Lake Mohave ("excess"):** 1 female, 19 males
- **Marking protocol:**
  - All stocked fish received a permanently implanted [[PIT Tag]] (Passive Integrated Transponder) injected into the abdominal cavity prior to release
  - "Excess" fish released back into Lake Mohave were marked with white spaghetti tags labeled "BR. BOX 427. BOULDER CITY, NV" plus a unique 5-digit ID number
  - 5 individuals bore pre-existing spaghetti tags from prior capture events; these tags were removed before re-release
- **Reproductive status:** Most males were reproductively active (running ripe); only 3 females readily expressed eggs, indicating asynchronous or limited female reproductive readiness during the sampling window
- **Bycatch:** Incidental catch was minimal — fewer than 12 [[Common Carp]] and 1 [[Channel Catfish]]
- **Planned follow-up monitoring:**
  - 21 February 1991 — site visit to survey the backwater for presence of larval razorback suckers
  - 16–23 March 1991 — coincident adult sampling event
- **Data availability:** Full summary statistics, and PIT/spaghetti tag identification numbers, along with individual-level data, were noted as forthcoming/available upon request at time of writing.
