# Pesticide-Resistant Fishes and Their Ecological Consequences

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #ecotoxicology

## Executive Summary

This 1960s-era conference paper by [[Denzel E. Ferguson]] (Department of Zoology, Mississippi State University) reviews evidence that insecticide resistance in natural fish populations is a serious ecological hazard rather than a benign curiosity. Resistance was first documented in [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) from intensively sprayed cotton land in the Mississippi Delta and has since been demonstrated in six fish species. Resistant fish tolerate very high body burdens of [[Endrin]] and other chlorinated hydrocarbons, which can transfer residues to predators and, potentially, to humans. The author argues that resistance is genetically based and that the conditions for its wider spread already exist.

## Background & Description

Pesticide resistance was well established in insects and bacteria, but its occurrence in vertebrates had received little attention. Biologists initially welcomed any factor that protected nontarget organisms from pesticide exposure. The paper states its purpose as reviewing recent evidence that pesticide-resistant vertebrates pose a major hazard in natural ecosystems.

The first documented case involved DDT-resistant mosquitofish from a heavily sprayed cotton-producing area of the Delta, identified through comparative bioassays of populations from contaminated and untreated sites. Earlier related work included resistance in amphibians (see [[Boyd et al. 1963]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Affected species:** Resistance has been demonstrated in black bullhead, yellow bullhead, golden shiner, mosquitofish, bluegill and green sunfish. Other co-occurring species are presumed to be resistant, though this is not confirmed.
- **Geographic scope:** Known cases are restricted to the Mississippi Delta, but the author expects the phenomenon to be found elsewhere.
- **Breadth of resistance:** Most populations resist several insecticides; mosquitofish resist nearly 20 compounds. Resistance ratios range from 2–3-fold up to 1,500-fold.
- **Exposure settings:** The most contaminated sites are drainage ditches receiving runoff from large treated cotton acreages, where resistance appears essential for survival. Runoff after heavy rain kills both susceptible and many native fish.
- **Cross-resistance:** Protection extends to compounds not used in cotton-insect control and allows survival under simultaneous multi-pesticide exposure. Resistance is highest for stable chlorinated hydrocarbons (cyclodienes such as endrin, chlorinated camphenes such as toxaphene), though organophosphate resistance also occurs.
- **Solubility effects:** Low pesticide solubility combined with high resistance makes some resistant fish nearly impossible to kill.

## Key Findings

**1. Extreme tolerance and survival**
- Golden shiners survived several days in water containing free aldrin crystals.
- Certain mosquitofish survived a 3-week exposure to 1,000 p.p.b. endrin (1 p.p.b. being the 48-hr TL for susceptible mosquitofish).

**2. Biomagnification risk**
- Resistant mosquitofish tolerated up to 214.28 p.p.m. endrin in their tissues after a 2-week exposure to 500 p.p.b. endrin.
- One such fish released enough endrin into 10 liters of tapwater to kill five susceptible mosquitofish, while surviving itself.
- Resistant green sunfish that ate endrin-laden mosquitofish (24.93 p.p.m.) survived, whereas susceptible sunfish died in 15.5 hours. Regurgitated mosquitofish showed no digestive damage, suggesting endrin was largely adsorbed to the surface.
- Susceptible green sunfish died in an average of 2 hours after eating live mosquitofish containing 1,041.66 p.p.m. endrin.

**3. Field pool experiment**
- In a woodland pool (15 ft diameter, 18 in. maximum depth) free of resident fish, 41 susceptible green sunfish were released. After 16 days, 39 mosquitofish exposed for one week to 1,000 p.p.b. endrin were added.
- Within one hour, 35 surface strikes were observed; no mosquitofish remained after 2 hours.
- After 16.5 hours, 11 dead sunfish were counted; 18 were dead by day 5. None of the dead sunfish had mosquitofish in their stomachs; one contained 0.86 p.p.m. endrin.
- On day 8, seven living green sunfish were recovered by seining and rotenone, with no mosquitofish found.

**4. Predator loss**
- Heavily contaminated environments may lack top piscivores such as [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). Susceptible green sunfish and bass died when fed field-collected resistant mosquitofish.

**5. Resistance paradox**
- Resistance levels do not rise with trophic level. Mosquitofish are most resistant, followed by golden shiners, green sunfish and yellow bullheads, even though green sunfish prey on mosquitofish. The author suggests residues in the food chain exert minimal selection when the consumer is itself resistant; heavy runoff exposure is the stronger selective force.

**6. Genetic basis**
- Resistance is genetically based: progeny of resistant fish kept resistance over four generations without insecticide exposure.
- Rare resistant individuals occur in susceptible populations and supply material for selection.
- Artificial selection over consecutive generations of mosquitofish produced strains with higher tolerance.

**7. Human health concern**
- Resistant green sunfish are eaten by people in the Delta. Edible portions of laboratory-exposed fish, prepared for cooking, contained 11.09–26.48 p.p.m. endrin.
- Only zero tolerances exist for endrin, and single-dose LD50 values for several homeotherms are below 10 mg/kg body weight, so these residues are a serious concern.
- The author warns that resistant fish could become a dangerous food product for consumers, including humans, and that similar problems may apply to other chlorinated hydrocarbons.

**Conclusion:** Resistance is currently geographically limited, but time, continued pesticide use and exposed populations are the conditions that could allow it to spread. The effects of resistant fish on piscivorous vertebrates remain to be determined, though preliminary observations suggest they may be serious.
