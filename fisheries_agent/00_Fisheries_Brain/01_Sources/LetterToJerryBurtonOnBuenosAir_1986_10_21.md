# Letter to Jerry Burton on Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge Pond Renovation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1986 letter from W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) to Jerry Burton (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Office of Endangered Species) documents a field operation at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] conducted with Paul Marsh's Fisheries Management class. The primary goal was to re-sample previously surveyed ponds to confirm fishlessness and to renovate additional tanks by removing non-native and undesirable species (mosquitofish, fathead minnow, yellow and black bullheads) using the ichthyocide **Fintrol** (antimycin A). The renovated and fishless tanks are proposed as growout habitat for endangered Colorado River basin big-river fishes, specifically [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (referred to as "squawfish"). The letter also proposes a longer-term strategy to renovate the entire [[Aguirre Lake]] drainage as broodstock and production habitat, contingent on continued USFWS funding and ichthyocide supply.

## Background & Description
This document is administrative/operational in nature and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of the species involved. However, taxonomic identity corrections are noted:
- An **Airport Tank #3** population initially identified as **[[Yellow Bullhead]]** was corrected upon re-sampling to **[[Black Bullhead]]**, indicating the practical difficulty of field-level ictalurid identification and the importance of confirmatory re-sampling.
- Target restocking species are grouped functionally as "big-river fishes" — [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (squawfish), [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — reflecting their shared large-bodied, riverine-adapted morphology suited to large, complex water bodies like [[Aguirre Lake]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target removal species (non-native/undesirable competitors or predators):**
  - [[Western Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) — small-bodied, live-bearing, known predator of larval native fishes.
  - [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) — competitor/prey-base species often introduced as baitfish.
  - [[Yellow Bullhead]] and [[Black Bullhead]] (Ictaluridae) — benthic omnivore/predators capable of persisting through chemical treatment due to burrowing/hardiness.
- **Renovation method:** Treatment with the ichthyocide **Fintrol** (antimycin A) at concentrations exceeding 50 ppb, elevated due to concerns over reduced shelf-life/potency of the chemical stock.
- **Target species for restocking (endangered natives):**
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) — prioritized for immediate stocking into three confirmed fishless tanks.
  - [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — reserved for spring stocking.
  - [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] ("squawfish", *Ptychocheilus lucius*) — considered a candidate for the larger [[Aguirre Lake]] drainage habitat.
- **Habitat context:** The Refuge's stock tanks and the larger [[Aguirre Lake]] drainage are framed as artificial/semi-natural growout and broodstock habitats functioning as ex-situ refugia for Colorado River basin endangered fishes, distinct from their historic riverine habitat.
- **Non-target ecological note:** [[Cordova Tank]] was found to have a large bullfrog (*Lithobates catesbeianus*) population, flagged as a potential predation/competition risk, leading to a recommendation to avoid its use for stocking.
- **Multiple-use synergy:** Maintaining water levels in the Aguirre Lake drainage for fish production is also proposed to benefit waterfowl habitat, aligning fish conservation with broader Refuge management goals.

## Key Findings
- Seven Refuge tanks were re-sampled and confirmed **fishless**, making them immediately available for stocking: [[Road Camp Tank]], [[Big Bertha Tank]], [[Kings Tank]] (recommended for initial [[Bonytail Chub]] stocking), plus [[Mormon Lake]], [[Hito Tank]], [[Rock Tank]], and [[Cordova Tank]] (held in reserve, with Cordova Tank flagged for bullfrog infestation).
- Three additional tanks were chemically renovated with Fintrol at >50 ppb: [[Little Mormon Tank]] (fathead minnow, mosquitofish), [[State Tank]] (mosquitofish, fathead minnow, yellow bullhead), and [[Airport Tank]] #3 (mosquitofish, black bullhead — corrected from an earlier yellow bullhead misidentification).
- Follow-up re-sampling (~1 month later) was recommended to verify whether bullheads were successfully eradicated, as bullheads are more resistant to ichthyocide than other species treated, which appeared to die immediately.
- A concern was raised regarding the reduced **shelf-life/potency of the Fintrol ichthyocide stock**, prompting compensatory over-dosing.
- Total project cost was **$398.02**, covering state and personal vehicle mileage, fuel, and food for a 14-person field crew; reimbursement was directed to Paul C. Marsh at the ASU Center for Environmental Studies.
- A significant logistical failure occurred: the state motor pool van broke down, requiring a tow back to Tempe and eliminating return mileage charges.
- A strategic recommendation was made to prioritize renovation of **all tanks in the [[Aguirre Lake]] drainage**, coupled with USFWS-funded pumping to sustain water levels, positioning this larger water body as a production and broodstock facility for endangered big-river fishes and as enhanced waterfowl habitat.
- The ASU team (Minckley/Marsh) offered continued manpower and technical expertise for a multi-year renovation program, contingent on USFWS providing ichthyocide supplies and expense reimbursement.
