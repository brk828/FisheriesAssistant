# Second progress report on the tagging of white catfish (Ictalurus catus) in the Sacramento-San Joaquin delta

## Executive Summary
This is the second progress report from a California Department of Fish and Game study (Dingell-Johnson Project F-2-R) investigating [[White Catfish]] (*Ictalurus catus*) population dynamics in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. The research had two primary goals: (1) develop a valid index for annual harvest rate to inform fishery conservation, and (2) characterize movement/migration patterns. The study compared tag retention and return rates among three tag types — [[Opercular Strap Tag]], [[Disk-Dangler Tag]], and [[Staple Tag]] — across multiple tagging cohorts (1952 and 1952-53 releases). Findings indicate disk-dangler and staple tags perform similarly well and far outperform opercular strap tags, that white catfish exhibit no regular seasonal migration but rather sporadic, directionless local movement, and that a minimum ~17.2% of the Delta's white catfish population was harvested annually by combined sport and commercial fisheries in 1953.

## Background & Description
While this paper is not primarily morphological, it includes relevant biometric and tag-attachment data:

- **Fork length range (tagged fish):** 6.1–12.4 inches; mean = 8.2 inches
- **Fork length range (fish with returned tags):** 6.3–12.0 inches; mean = 8.3 inches
- **Size-selective return bias:** Chi-square test showed a statistically significant difference (P<0.003) between size distribution of tagged fish vs. fish from which tags were returned. Fish under 8.5 inches were under-represented in returns; fish over 8.5 inches were over-represented — cause unexplained.

### Tag Construction & Placement
- **Materials:** Cellulose nitrate disks (0.5 in diameter, 0.040 in thick) attached via 0.020-inch [[Tantalum]] wire.
- **Disk-Dangler Tag:** Disk hangs freely from attachment point.
- **Staple Tag:** Disk positioned snugly against the body (designed to reduce entanglement with gill nets/weeds/obstructions).
- **Opercular Strap Tag:** Attached to the operculum; prone to being obscured by mucus buildup and eventual detachment.
- Both disk-dangler and staple tags were originally developed for tagging studies on [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*) (Calhoun, 1953).
- Application time: ~30 seconds per fish by experienced handlers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Tidal sloughs and channels of the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta, a mixed freshwater-estuarine system.
- **Capture method:** [[Fyke Net]] (locally "hoop net") — 7 circular iron hoops (45 in to 28 in diameter), No. 15 cotton twine webbing with 2¾-inch stretched mesh, funnels at hoops 2, 3, and 5. Nets treated with heated coal tar to reduce webbing deterioration and reportedly increase catch efficiency for white catfish.
- **Tagging localities:** Three stations — [[Antioch Bridge]], [[Disappointment Slough]], and [[Cache Slough]] — selected based on prior evidence (from the first study, using six stations) that fish dispersion from tagging points was thorough enough to require only three release sites.
- **Movement patterns:** Of 194 fish with determined movement (second study, first-year returns): 68 moved upstream (avg. 10.4 miles), 76 moved downstream (avg. 8.7 miles), and 50 were recaptured near the release point. Combined with first-study data (352 total fish), results confirm **sporadic, non-directional movement** with no evidence of regular seasonal migration.
- **Fishery context:** White catfish supported both commercial (~20 licensed fishermen, 174,452 lbs in 1952, valued at $35,763) and recreational fisheries (California sport fishery in 1953: ~225,000 anglers caught ~7,500,000 catfish statewide). The commercial fishery was legislatively eliminated in September 1953 due to evidence of stock decline.

## Key Findings

### Tag Retention (Hatchery Holding Trial)
- All 20 [[Disk-Dangler Tag]]s remained attached after 22 months (some irritation at attachment points, attributed to excessive handling).
- 10 of 41 [[Opercular Strap Tag]]s were lost after 22 months; most remaining strap tags were loose and obscured by mucus.

### First Study — Tag Returns (Released Spring 1952)
| Period | Strap Tag Return % | Disk-Dangler Return % | Total % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 2.6% | 16.3% | 5.0% |
| Year 2 | 0.03% | 0.3% | 0.07% |

Disk-dangler tags showed ~10x the return rate of strap tags in year 2, confirming strap tag inferiority.

### Second Study — Tag Comparison (Disk-Dangler vs. Staple, 1952–53 release)
- Total tagged: 1,499 fish (749 disk-dangler, 750 staple) across three stations.
- **Overall 12-month return rate:** 15.1% (227 of 1,499 tags)
- **By station:** Antioch Bridge 21.0%; Cache Slough 13.2%; Disappointment Slough 11.2%.
- **By tag type:** Disk-dangler 16.3% vs. staple 14.0% — difference **not statistically significant** at 5% level.
- Monthly return distribution showed no strong evidence of differential tag shedding over time; variation attributed to fluctuating angling effort.
- Note: Staple tag placement near the dorsal fin was separately found **unsatisfactory for large steelhead rainbow trout** (*Salmo gairdneri gairdnerii*) in a related study, suggesting species-specific tag performance.

### Harvest Estimation
- Raw first-year return (commercial + sport): 223 tags directly returned (~15.1%), plus 4 recovered by project personnel.
- Evidence of substantial **non-return of tags by commercial fishermen**: only 7 of ~20 licensed commercial operators returned any tags, though they accounted for ~44% of total 1953 commercial landings by weight.
- Correcting for estimated commercial non-return (via simple proportion), commercial returns were adjusted from 28 to 63 tags, raising total returns to 258 (17.2%).
- **Estimated minimum 1953 harvest:** ~17.2% of available white catfish (sport-only harvest estimated at ≥13.0%). True figure likely higher due to unaccounted non-returns.

### Movement/Migration Summary (Combined First & Second Studies, n=352)
| Category | Number | Avg. Distance (mi) |
|---|---|---|
| No movement | 70 | — |
| Upstream | 153 | 10.1 |
| Downstream | 129 | 8.7 |

- Conclusion: No regular seasonal migration pattern detected; movement is best characterized as **sporadic and directionless**, consistent with earlier findings (Pelgen, 1954).

### Management Implications
- Findings support continued use of [[Disk-Dangler Tag]] as a reliable, low-shedding tag for long-term catfish tracking studies.
- A subsequent (third) experiment using only disk-dangler tags was initiated following elimination of the commercial fishery, intended to isolate a cleaner index of sport-fishery harvest rate.
- Publicity campaigns (posters, prize drawings via the Foothill Sportsmen's Club of Oakland, personal outreach) were credited with maximizing tag return compliance among sport anglers.
