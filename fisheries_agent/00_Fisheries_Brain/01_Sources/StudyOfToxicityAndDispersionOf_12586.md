## Executive Summary

This 1948-1949 study by the New Hampshire Fish and Game Department evaluated the efficacy of **5% emulsifiable rotenone** applied via **fixed-wing aircraft** (biplane) as a piscicide for pond/lake reclamation—a fisheries management technique used to eradicate undesirable fish populations. Conducted on **White Lake, Tamworth, NH** (123 acres), the research tested toxicity, dispersion rate through water depth (0-30 ft), and downstream ecological effects on plankton and bacteria. Results confirmed that a concentration of **1:2,000,000** (0.5 ppm) rotenone, applied aerially, achieved sufficient toxicity and vertical dispersion to kill target fish species down to 30 feet within 24 hours, with lethal residual effects persisting for at least 18 days. The study also documented significant shifts in the plankton community structure, with **[[Planktonic Crustacea]]** declining sharply while algae and protozoans increased following treatment.

## Background & Description

While this paper is primarily an applied toxicology/limnology study rather than a morphological survey, it notes species-specific physiological responses (equilibrium loss, respiratory activity) as proxies for toxicant sensitivity:

* **[[Brook Trout]] (Salvelinus fontinalis):** Demonstrated the lowest resistance to rotenone exposure among test species; lost equilibrium fastest at surface temperatures (66°F).
* **[[Common Sunfish]] (Lepomis gibbosus):** Exhibited "slightly greater resistance" than trout, losing equilibrium later, though eventual mortality outcomes at depth were equivalent.
* **[[Common Shiner]] (Notropis cornutus) & Golden Shiner:** Used as primary bioassay indicators for dispersion studies; consistently succumbed to rotenone faster than sunfish across all tested depths, indicating higher relative sensitivity (e.g., 17 min vs. 39 min at surface).
* **Test Organisms (Study #4):** Included freshwater clams (*Elliptio*), snails (*Physa*), tadpoles, and planktonic crustacea (*Daphnia*, *Diaptomus*), used to assess broader non-target toxicity in controlled lab conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

* **Habitat:** [[White Lake]] is a 123-acre, oligotrophic-leaning salmonid water body within the Saco River Watershed, characterized by a slightly acidic pH (6.0–6.5), a maximum depth of ~54 ft, and significant shallow sandy shoal habitat (<10 ft).
* **Thermal Stratification:** The lake exhibited thermal stratification at the time of study (66°F surface to 51°F at 30 ft), which directly influenced the rate of chemical toxicity and equilibrium loss in test fish (colder, deeper water showed delayed toxic onset).
* **Trophic Interactions:** The study is notable for documenting non-target ecological cascades:
    * **[[Planktonic Crustacea]]** (Cyclops, Bosmina, Holopedium, Diaptomus) were highly sensitive to rotenone, disappearing entirely from samples within 39 days post-treatment, corroborating findings by Hamilton (1942) that rotenone acts broadly on aquatic invertebrates.
    * **Chlorophyceae (Algae)** and **Rotifera** populations increased following reclamation, likely due to reduced grazing pressure from the eliminated crustacean zooplankton (a trophic cascade effect).
    * **Protozoa** (specifically *Amoeba* and *Peridinium*) shifted from rare to predominant occurrence post-treatment.
    * **Bacterial levels** (measured via Most Probable Number/MPN of coliform) remained unaffected by either the rotenone or the resulting fish decomposition, maintaining "superior quality" water standards.

## Key Findings

1.  **Effective Aerial Application Rate:** A concentration of 1 part 5% emulsifiable rotenone to 2,000,000 parts water (0.5 ppm) was sufficient for full reclamation via aircraft application on large, maneuverable lakes.
2.  **Application Efficiency:** The plane successfully treated the entire 123-acre lake surface in 1 hour 35 minutes (first pass) and completed the total job (2 applications) in 5 hours 22 minutes, using 410 gallons of rotenone across 14 flight trips.
3.  **Vertical Dispersion Confirmed:** Toxicity was not limited to the surface; lethal concentrations dispersed effectively down to 30 feet (bottom depths), though the onset of toxicity was slower at depth due to lower water temperatures.
4.  **Species-Specific Sensitivity:** [[Common Shiner]] and [[Brook Trout]] were more sensitive to rotenone exposure than [[Common Sunfish]], suggesting varying resistance thresholds among freshwater fish families (Cyprinidae/Salmonidae vs. Centrarchidae).
5.  **Persistence of Toxicity:** Rotenone remained lethal to test fish for at least 18 days post-application, confirming long residual efficacy for reclamation purposes.
6.  **Non-Target Impact:** The chemical caused a total elimination of planktonic crustacean groups but did not significantly alter the *total* plankton count per liter, as algae and protozoan populations compensated for the crustacean decline.
7.  **Lab vs. Field Discrepancy:** A parallel laboratory experiment (Study #4) showed reduced toxicity efficacy, attributed to the higher pH (7.3) of the tap water used, aligning with prior findings by Leonard (1938) that pH significantly influences rotenone's toxic action.
