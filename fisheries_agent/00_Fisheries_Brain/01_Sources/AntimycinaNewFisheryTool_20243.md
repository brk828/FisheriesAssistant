# Antimycin—A New Fishery Tool

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fishery-management #chemical-control #carp-control

## Executive Summary

[[Antimycin]] is a newly developed antibiotic fish toxicant discovered in 1945 at the [[University of Wisconsin]] Department of Plant Pathology. By 1963, researchers identified its high toxicity to fish at concentrations harmless to other animals. The [[Fish Control Laboratory]] at [[La Crosse]] conducted extensive testing on over 30 freshwater fish species from 1963-1966. [[Antimycin]] shows exceptional promise for controlling undesirable fish species, particularly [[Common Carp]], with concentrations as low as 1-5 parts per billion proving lethal. The compound degrades rapidly in water (1-14 days, typically 4-7 days) and appears harmless to other aquatic life, waterfowl, and mammals at fish-killing concentrations. The [[Wisconsin Conservation Commission]] entered a $60,000 contract for comprehensive field testing during 1966-1967. The product was approved by the U.S. Department of Agriculture Pesticide Regulations Division and marketed as FINTROL by [[Ayerst Laboratories]].

## Background & Description

[[Antimycin]] is an antibiotic produced by microscopic plants, similar to penicillin production by mold. The name derives from its original intended use as an anti-fungal agent. The [[Wisconsin Alumni Research Foundation]] developed the compound for potential agricultural disease treatment. In 1963, Philip Derse and Dr. Frank Strong reported [[Antimycin]]'s high fish toxicity and suggested fishery management applications.

The compound kills fish by interfering with cellular respiration after absorption through gills. The action is irreversible—brief exposure proves fatal. [[Antimycin]] is formulated as FINTROL, consisting of the antibiotic coated on plain sand. This dry formulation resembles ordinary sand, imparts no odor or color to water, and can be applied by hand or seed spreader. The sand gradually releases [[Antimycin]] as it sinks.

Species susceptibility varies significantly:
- Most susceptible: [[Common Carp]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Green Sunfish]] (1-5 parts per billion)
- Less susceptible: [[Longnose Gar]], [[Bowfin]], [[Goldfish]], [[Bullheads]] (require slightly higher concentrations)
- This differential susceptibility enables selective removal of pest species while preserving gamefish

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Testing occurred across diverse environments including laboratory conditions (hard and soft water, cold to warm temperatures, egg to adult life stages) and field sites in ponds and streams across seven states. The [[Fish Control Laboratory]] tested over 30 freshwater fish species.

The October 1965 trial at [[Veterans Memorial Park]] pond in [[West Salem, Wisconsin]] demonstrated field effectiveness. The 5-acre pond received 10 parts per billion [[Antimycin]] to eliminate all fish except [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Black Bullhead]]. Within 24 hours, mortality included:
- 5,994 [[Common Carp]] (4-27 inches)
- 60 emaciated trout
- Thousands of [[Fathead Minnow]] (too numerous to count)
- Small numbers of [[Northern Pike]], [[Central Mudminnow]], [[White Sucker]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Bluegill]], [[White Crappie]]
- Only [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Black Bullhead]] survived

The trial revealed severe pond degradation—only 122 gamefish versus thousands of carp and minnows. Semi-tame [[Mallard]] ducks consumed dead minnows without ill effects. [[Antimycin]] degraded completely within 6 days.

Environmental safety testing demonstrated no harm to aquatic plants, insects, tadpoles, frogs, salamanders, turtles, or water snakes at fish-killing concentrations. Terrestrial animal testing (mallards, ringneck pheasants, pigeons, chickens, quail, mice, rats, rabbits, guinea pigs, dogs, lambs) showed no adverse effects at fish-lethal concentrations.

Degradation rates vary with water chemistry—faster in hard, alkaline water than soft, acid water. Potassium permanganate can accelerate degradation when desired. Rapid degradation preserves fish-food organisms, enabling quick restocking and reducing the typical lag between reclamation and quality fishing.

## Key Findings

**Efficacy and Selectivity:**
- [[Antimycin]] kills fish at extraordinarily low concentrations (1-5 parts per billion for susceptible species)
- 1 part per billion equivalent to 1 ounce chocolate syrup in 10 million gallons milk
- Differential species susceptibility enables selective pest removal while preserving desirable species
- Successfully eradicated large [[Common Carp]] and [[Green Sunfish]] populations in Nebraska pond while [[Northern Pike]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] survived
- Kills fertilized eggs of [[Common Carp]], [[Goldfish]], and suckers—effective against spawning congregations
- No repelling effects observed; carp do not attempt escape or seek bottom springs

**Environmental Fate and Safety:**
- Degrades in 1-14 days (typically 4-7 days) in natural waters
- Degradation faster in hard, alkaline water versus soft, acid water
- Can be accelerated with potassium permanganate addition
- Minimal impact on fish-food organisms
- No observed toxicity to aquatic invertebrates, amphibians, reptiles, or aquatic plants at fish-killing concentrations
- Large safety margin between fish-lethal and mammalian/avian-toxic concentrations

**Application and Formulation:**
- FINTROL formulation (antibiotic on sand carrier) easily applied by hand or seed spreader
- Gradual release as sand sinks provides sustained treatment
- Potential for depth-specific formulations to treat specific water layers
- No odor or color imparted to treated water
- Successfully tested in streams ([[Vernon County, Wisconsin]])—first chemical appearing safe for stream use

**Management Implications:**
- [[Wisconsin Conservation Commission]] $60,000 contract for 1966-1967 comprehensive testing
- Focus on [[Common Carp]] control in Wisconsin lakes and streams
- Planned trials include under-ice applications and spawning congregation treatments
- Approved by U.S. Department of Agriculture Pesticide Regulations Division
- Edibility of [[Antimycin]]-killed fish under investigation by [[Ayerst Laboratories]], [[University of Wisconsin]], and U.S. Food and Drug Administration
- Enables rapid water body restocking due to quick degradation and preserved food base

**Target Species for Control:**
[[Longnose Gar]], [[Bowfin]], [[Common Carp]], [[Goldfish]], minnows, suckers, [[Bullheads]], [[Green Sunfish]]
