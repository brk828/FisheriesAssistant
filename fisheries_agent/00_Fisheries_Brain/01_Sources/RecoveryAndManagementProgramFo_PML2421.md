# Gila Complex Taxonomy and Recovery Program Solicitation (1987 USFWS Correspondence)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1987 letter from the Denver Regional Office of the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Service) to Dr. R. R. Miller of the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology. It asks Dr. Miller to lead an advisory group of taxonomists addressing the unresolved taxonomy and conservation status of the [[Gila Complex]] in the [[Colorado River Basin]]: [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. The Service's stated concern is that the humpback chub's taxonomy has become confused, which complicates recovery and management. The letter poses five key questions, asks for recommendations on a meeting location and date, and offers travel funding for a centralized meeting. The letter is dated February 1987 and was copied to several Service, state and federal staff.

## Background & Description

The three species are native freshwater cyprinids (family Cyprinidae) of the Colorado River system, grouped as the Gila complex. The letter refers to them as "roundtail, bonytail, and humpback chub" and as "Gila spp." Scanning errors garble the binomial names in the source, so the scientific names above are supplied from the taxonomic group the letter describes rather than read from the text.

The letter identifies a core problem: the taxonomy of the humpback chub has become confused, and questions remain about whether the three forms are distinct species. The Service stresses that, as the federal agency responsible for protecting and recovering these species, it needs authoritative answers before it can manage them effectively.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter is framed around environmental change in the Colorado River basin. The Service asks whether genetic or morphological characteristics of the Gila complex have changed in recent times, and whether any such changes relate to environmental perturbations. It also asks how much the genetic integrity of the species must be protected to preserve the Gila complex and its Colorado River system.

The letter does not report field measurements, population counts, sample sizes, water chemistry, or habitat data. Its ecological content is limited to these questions about genetic integrity, morphological change, and environmental disturbance.

## Key Findings

The letter sets out a research and advisory agenda rather than reporting results. Its main points are:

1. **Species validity:** Are roundtail chub, bonytail, and humpback chub three distinct species, and are their taxonomic classifications adequately supported by data collected in recent years?
2. **Variation within species:** Is the genetic and morphological variation within these species adequately described, and do current data support keeping their existing taxonomic status?
3. **Diagnostic criteria:** What key characteristics separate the species, and can definitive criteria be developed to identify species and [[Hybridization|species hybrids]]?
4. **Recent change:** Has there been an identifiable change in the genetic and/or morphological characteristics of the Gila complex in recent times, and if so, how are these changes related to environmental perturbations?
5. **Conservation priority:** To what degree must the genetic integrity of the species be protected to preserve the Gila complex and the Colorado River system?

**Requested action:** The Service asks the recipient to form and lead a select advisory group of taxonomists. The group's goals are to:
- summarize the current state of knowledge for each question;
- develop a consensus response to each question;
- identify the specific work needed to resolve unanswered questions;
- recommend a meeting location and date, and propose group members.

**Support offered:** The Service will pay travel expenses for group members to attend a centralized meeting. [[Harold Tyus]] of the [[Colorado River Fisheries Project]] was named as the Service's coordinator and contact for further information.

**Document status:** The letter is an unsigned-copy correspondence with an illegible OCR signature. It was circulated to the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, the Colorado River Fishes Project, the Service's regional offices, and other staff, indicating a coordinated, multi-agency effort.
