# Description of a New Species of Killifish, Lucania browni, from a Hot Spring in Lower California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Fundulidae #killifish #species-description

## Executive Summary

This 1907 publication by [[David Starr Jordan]] and [[Robert Earl Richardson]] describes a new species of killifish, *[[Lucania browni]]*, collected from a hot spring in northeastern [[Lower California]] (Baja California). Six specimens were obtained by [[Herbert Brown]] from water at 128°F (53°C), representing a remarkable adaptation to extreme thermal conditions. The species is distinguished from other *Lucania* by shorter ventral fins and distinctive coloration patterns. The fish inhabited water with 25% soluble salts, demonstrating tolerance to both high temperature and salinity.

## Background & Description

*[[Lucania browni]]* is a small killifish of the family [[Fundulidae]]. Morphometric measurements: head 3.3 in length without caudal; depth 3.3 (3.5 in males); D. 10, A. 10, scales 28 (25); depth of caudal peduncle 1.9 in head; breadth of head 1.6; eye 3; interorbital space equal to eye; nose 4 in head. Mouth very small and quite oblique; maxillary equals pupil. Teeth sharp, pointed, equal, in a single row on each jaw. Gill membranes connected for short distance. Intestine not longer than body. Dorsal inserted slightly behind ventrals, nearer base of caudal than end of snout. Anal fin not modified into intromittent organ in males; length when depressed 1.3 in head in females, equal to head in males. Pectoral 1.4 in head; ventrals very small, 2.75 in head.

Coloration in formalin: Females olivaceous with about 8 large darker blotches forming broken lateral band in adults; blotches continued downward as cross bands in half-grown specimens; 3 diffuse clusters of punctulations on belly above anal fin; dorsal punctulated with dusky, with conspicuous dark blotch between sixth and tenth rays; caudal pale with two crescentric cross bands at base; ventrals and anal pale; pectorals obscurely punctulate. Males darker with less distinct side blotches, fins more dusky, dorsal and anal heavily tipped with dusky behind.

The species is oviparous. Two largest specimens were gravid females with eggs 1 mm in diameter.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collection locality was 20 feet below the outbreak of a hot spring on the eastern margin of a salt lake between the [[Coast Range]] and [[Cocopah Range]] in northeastern [[Lower California]], approximately 40 miles south of the international border and below sea level.

Water temperature was 128°F (53°C), measured with laboratory instrument by engineer [[Godfrey Sykes]] in presence of Brown and Dr. [[D.T. MacDougal]] (director of [[Carnegie Botanical Laboratory]]). Surface covered with thick growth of green and sulphur-colored algae except for 2-3 foot wide open strip. Fish darted in and out under algae. Flow was 10-12 feet wide at measurement point, 20 feet from spring source.

Water chemistry analysis by Prof. [[R.E. Forbes]]: 25% soluble salts total, including 2% common salt (NaCl), 0.5% calcium chloride, and trace lithium. Water teemed with insect life. A second parallel spring 20 feet away with equal water volume contained no fish.

Specimens survived nearly two days in approximately one quart of native spring water in closed can during February, remaining lively through cold night before being preserved in formaldehyde.

## Key Findings

- Six specimens collected, ranging 0.50 to 0.84 inch in length
- Type specimen: Cat. No. 57838, [[U.S. National Museum]]; cotypes: Stanford University No. 20171
- Species distinguished from other *[[Lucania]]* species by shorter ventral fins and coloration details
- Remarkable thermal tolerance: fish living at 128°F (53°C)
- High salinity tolerance: 25% soluble salts
- Oviparous reproduction confirmed with gravid females
- Species named for collector [[Herbert Brown]] of Tucson, Arizona
- Habitat specificity: present in one spring but absent from nearby parallel spring of similar volume
- Associated with dense algal mats in extreme environment
- Demonstrated survival capacity outside native water for extended period
