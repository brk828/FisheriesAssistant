# Morphometric Variation of Larvae and Juveniles of the Piranhas *Serrasalmus spilopleura* and *S. marginatus* (Characidae: Serrasalminae) of the Paraná Basin, Brazil

#ichthyology #marine-biology #serrasalmidae

## Executive Summary

This study describes the larvae and juveniles of two sympatric piranha species, [[Serrasalmus Spilopleura]] and [[Serrasalmus Marginatus]], from the upper [[Paraná River]] floodplain in Brazil. Early larvae are separated by myomere count (32–35 in *S. spilopleura* versus 36–39 in *S. marginatus*), while later stages and juveniles are separated by snout shape, postmedian body region and caudal peduncle. Using the [[Truss Method]] of geometric shape analysis combined with [[Sheared Principal Components Analysis]], the authors discriminated the two species at all developmental stages, including preflexion and flexion larvae that are otherwise hard to identify.

## Background & Description

Larval identification is difficult because larvae and adults often differ in habitat, feeding and behaviour, reflecting ontogenetic change and planktonic specialization. Most prior larval morphometric work used bivariate linear measures with little allometric information. The truss method (Strauss & Bookstein, 1982) is more sensitive to changes in size and body shape but has rarely been applied to fish larvae.

Information on early development in *Serrasalmus* was scarce, coming mainly from aquarium-reared fish and a few field reports, such as larval behaviour of *S. spilopleura* (Sazima & Zamprogno, 1985) and diet and ontogeny in Venezuelan floodplain serrasalmids (Machado-Allison & Garcia, 1986).

The aims were to:
1. Separate larvae and juveniles of the two species;
2. Identify morphometric characters discriminating them at each ontogenetic stage using truss shape analysis and sheared PCA;
3. Describe the general morphology of larvae and juveniles.

Developmental stages followed [[Ahlstrom & Ball Terminology]] and Kendall et al. (1984):
- Preflexion: 7.0–8.4 mm SL (*S. spilopleura*); 7.7–8.5 mm SL (*S. marginatus*)
- Flexion: 8.3–10.9 mm SL (*S. spilopleura*); 8.4–10.6 mm SL (*S. marginatus*)
- Postflexion: 10.5–14.7 mm SL (*S. spilopleura*); 10.4–13.7 mm SL (*S. marginatus*)

Juveniles were defined by pigmentation pattern, complete fin-ray development and onset of scaling.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study area and sampling.** Monthly samples were collected from March 1993 to February 1994 at 36 stations along the Paraná, Ivinhema and Baía rivers of the upper Paraná floodplain. Work was conducted through [[Nupélia]] at the Universidade Estadual de Maringá.

**Sample sizes.**
- *S. spilopleura*: 20 larvae (7.0–14.2 mm SL) and 7 juveniles (18.0–27.1 mm SL)
- *S. marginatus*: 22 larvae (7.1–14.2 mm SL) and 8 juveniles (18.6–26.6 mm SL)

**Methods.** Larvae were identified to genus using [[Ahlstrom & Moser Larval Identification Technique]] together with fin-ray and meristic counts. Measurements were taken under a stereoscopic microscope with a micrometric ocular (larvae) or calipers (juveniles). Myomere differences were tested by ANOVA (Sokal & Rohlf, 1981). Vertebrae were counted in dissected adults.

**Sheared PCA.** Following Humphries et al. (1981), data were log10-transformed and covariance matrices were computed for each landmark series. Variable numbers were 16 (series 1), 19 (series 2 and 3) and 20 (series 4). PC1 was read as a size factor only if all its coefficients were positive, and later components as shape. The Shear software package (MacLeod, 1990) was used.

## Key Findings

**Meristic separation (larvae).**
- Myomere number differed significantly between species (F = 193.80; p < 0.0001; df = 227).
- *S. spilopleura*: mean 34.56 (range 32–35).
- *S. marginatus*: mean 37.01 (range 36–39).
- Adults had 34–35 vertebrae (*S. spilopleura*) and 37–38 (*S. marginatus*), which supports the larval separation.
- Jégu & Santos (1988) reported 37 vertebrae for *S. spilopleura* from the Araguaia-Tocantins basin, against 32–35 in the Paraná material.

**Morphological differences.**
- Preflexion and flexion stages were separated mainly by myomere number and snout, postmedian and caudal peduncle shape.
- Differences were most marked in postflexion larvae.
- Juveniles of *S. spilopleura* had a rounded snout, convex head profile, greater body depth and little lateral compression.
- Juveniles of *S. marginatus* had a longer snout, concave head profile and extreme lateral compression.
- Fin-ray counts, keel-spine counts and pigmentation overlapped too much to be used as discriminators.

**Multivariate shape discrimination.**
- PCA score plots separated two groups: *S. spilopleura* and *S. marginatus*, at all four series.
- Series 1 (preflexion): PC1 explained 51.5% and PC2 17.2% of variation. PC1 had negative coefficients for some distances, which the authors attributed to shape rather than size (correlations with SL not significant, p > 0.15).
- Series 2 (flexion): PC1 48.3%, PC2 21.4%.
- Series 3 (postflexion): PC1 88.8%, PC2 4.7%.
- Series 4 (juveniles): PC1 86.3%, PC2 5.4%.
- Snout length and the postmedian and caudal peduncle distances were discriminating in all series.

**Larval development descriptions.**
- *S. spilopleura*: 7.3 mm SL preflexion larva with 35 myomeres; 8.4 mm flexion; 9.7 mm late flexion with 16 dorsal rays; 11.4 mm postflexion with completed dorsal, caudal and anal fins; 14.7 mm late postflexion (16 dorsal, 19 caudal, 34 anal, 6 pelvic rays); 27.1 mm juvenile (12 pectoral rays, serrated keel with 31 spines).
- *S. marginatus*: 7.2 mm SL preflexion larva with 37 myomeres; 8.7 mm flexion; 9.6 mm late flexion with 16 dorsal rays; 11.6 mm postflexion; 13.7 mm late postflexion (16 dorsal, 21 caudal, 37 anal, 5 pelvic rays); 26.6 mm juvenile (12 pectoral rays, serrated keel with 33 spines).

**Conclusions.**
- The sheared PCA combined with truss shape analysis discriminates the species even in preflexion and flexion larvae, which are otherwise highly similar.
- The authors suggest the similarity reflects close phylogenetic relationship and convergent development.
- Discriminating variables were similar in series 2–4, and most matched those separating adults.
- Series 1 differed from the others, probably because preflexion larvae are nearly embryonic in form and their landmarks are not homologous with later series.
- Reversals in median-region variables (series 2 and 4) were probably due to allometric growth.
- Using three landmark series, rather than one as in Strauss & Fuiman (1985), may explain the difference in results.
