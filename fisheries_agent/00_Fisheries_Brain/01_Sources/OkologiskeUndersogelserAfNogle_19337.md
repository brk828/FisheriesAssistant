# Ecological Investigations of East Jutland Streams: Planarians and Leeches

#invertebrate-zoology #freshwater-ecology #Planaridae #Hirudinea

## Executive Summary

This is Part 2 of a 1963 Danish study by [[Bent Lauge Madsen]] on invertebrates of small streams in [[East Jutland]]. It covers the common freshwater planarian and leech fauna and relates their distribution to stream temperature, particularly maximum summer temperature. Laboratory thermal-resistance experiments test how survival depends on exposure and acclimation temperature. The paper concludes that the field distributions of the species track their experimentally determined thermal limits. Most of the text is in Danish, with an English summary.

## Background & Description

The paper follows [[Madsen 1962]] (physiographic and chemical conditions of the same streams). It adds two streams not covered earlier: Tjærbæk in Toholt Skov and the [[Gudena River]] at Åbro. Tjærbæk is a summer-cold, stony, uncontaminated forest brook resembling Kvitbæk but with faster flow and larger stones. Gudena samples came from rich bankside vegetation and stony bottoms. Because the two streams were sampled in a cold summer (1961), their temperature ranking is provisional.

Target taxa:
- **Planarian:** [[Dugesia Gonocephala]] (syn. *Euplanaria gonocephala*), a rheophilic (current-loving) species.
- **Leeches:** [[Glossiphonia Complanata]], [[Helobdella Stagnalis]], and [[Erpobdella Octoculata]], plus several rarer species recorded in single samples.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Temperature of the streams.** Temperatures were measured on single dates in 1961, not as a full series.
- 4 June: Granslev 4 12.1 °C; Tjærbæk 14.3 °C; Garverbæk 14.8 °C.
- Lillea: 11.8 °C (23 May), 17.4 °C (4 June), 15.3 °C (29 July).
- Gudena on the same dates: 12.5 °C, 16.2 °C, 15.8 °C.

The author suggests Gudena is probably cooler than Lillea but notes that planned thermograph series were needed to confirm this.

**Maximum temperatures (Table 1, ranked):** 7.6, 8.0, 11.1, 13.1, 14.6, >14.3 (Tjærbæk, provisional), 16.5, 16.5, and 21.1 °C (Lillea). Mean maxima for the warmest month were lower, e.g. 10.6 °C for Skovkilden (max 11.1 °C) and 17.6 °C for Lillea.

**Habitat relationships:**
- *D. gonocephala* is concentrated in the spring-fed Skovkilden area, Torup Skov brooks, and Tjærbæk.
- Shading and substrate matter. The species avoids light (per van Oye 1942), and sparse numbers in Garverbæk may reflect algae-covered stones there.
- Chemistry was judged favourable (weakly alkaline water, good oxygen) and did not explain the distribution.
- Leeches are restricted to the warmer reaches. Bennike (1943) reported no leeches in streams with summer temperatures under 11 °C; here the lower limit was somewhat higher.
- Stone size and substrate influence leech abundance. *G. complanata* prefers stones and is most common in Lillea and Tjærbæk, where large stones occur.

**Seasonality.** [[Cocoon Seasonality]] of *D. gonocephala* showed no cocoons in January and February. The first was found on 22 March at 7 °C. Cocoons peaked from May through August, with no samples from September onward. The last record was on 29 December at 5.8 °C.

## Key Findings

**Distribution and abundance (Table 1 totals):**
- *D. gonocephala*: 587 specimens, strongly concentrated in the coldest streams.
- *G. complanata*: 66 specimens. Ranges furthest into cold streams (Granslev 4 is its coldest occurrence).
- *H. stagnalis*: 116 specimens, found only in Lillea and Gudena.
- *E. octoculata*: 203 specimens, found only in Lillea and Gudena, where it was "very common."

Rare species: one *Bdellocephala punctata* and a few *Dendrocoelum lacteum* in Lillea; one *Theromyzon tessulatum* in Lillea; one *Piscicola geometra* in Gudena; a few *Erpobdella testacea* in Lillea and Gudena. Ussing (1929) also recorded *G. heteroclita* and *T. tessulatum* from Lillea.

**Thermal resistance of planarians ([[Dugesia Gonocephala]]):**
- Negligible mortality between 10 and 20 °C.
- Survival at exposure of 20 °C was 0–5%.
- Decisively lethal at 27 °C, in agreement with [[Blasing 1953]].
- Acclimation strongly affects [[Lethal Temperature]]. Animals acclimated for 72 h at 20 °C showed a small but significant lethal effect at 24.7 °C only after about 2.5 days.
- Animals sampled directly from the field (5.5 °C) died much faster at 25.3 °C than animals acclimated at 20 °C.

**Thermal resistance of leeches:**
- *G. complanata*: no mortality at 24.6 °C. In a small test (n = 6) acclimated at 24.6 °C, all animals died within 12 h at 33 °C; the lethal limit is estimated near 30 °C.
- *H. stagnalis* and *E. octoculata*: no lethal effect below 30 °C. Lethal effects at 33.9 °C depended on acclimation. Decisively lethal at 35.1 °C after 2.5 days' acclimation at 29.8 °C, with *H. stagnalis* probably less tolerant than *E. octoculata*.
- Leech trials used 35 animals split between parallel aquaria; trials where parallels disagreed were discarded.

**Correlation of distribution and tolerance:** Field distribution and experimental limits correlate for all three main species. *D. gonocephala*, confined to the coldest streams, has the lowest lethal temperature. *G. complanata* extends into the planarian range but has its main distribution in warmer water and tolerates temperatures lethal to *D. gonocephala*. *E. octoculata* tolerates the highest temperatures. The author notes that field limits lie well below lethal limits, that death is a crude criterion (reproduction and enzyme function may be affected at lower temperatures), and that acclimation shifts limits but does not erase species differences (citing [[Brett 1944]]).

**Research programme proposed:** Analyse single-factor effects (temperature, oxygen tension, ion content) on community species, then their combined effects, and finally changes in the quantitative and qualitative composition of the whole community.
