# K-Ar Geochronology and Geologic History of Southwestern Arizona and Adjacent Areas

#geology #geochronology #earth-science #basin-and-range

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Shafiqullah]] and co-authors reviews 174 K-Ar age determinations from 154 localities in southwestern Arizona and adjacent areas, and uses them to reconstruct the region's geologic history from the Precambrian to the Pliocene. Many rock units previously dated only by lithologic correlation receive isotopic ages, which in several cases require revised age assignments. The authors recognize four major episodes: Precambrian convergent-margin accretion and batholith emplacement, Laramide magmatism and plutonism (about 75 to 50 Ma), the mid-Tertiary orogeny (roughly 36 to 12 Ma), and the [[Basin and Range Disturbance]], which continues in eastern and central Arizona. The note is a research digest, so its key findings are the dated chronology, the tectonic interpretations, and the drainage and erosion-rate constraints.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** The study area extends from southern Nevada south to latitude 30° N and from the Mohave Desert of eastern California east to longitude 110° W. Data are presented with decay constants and analytical details so that ages can be recomputed.
- **Method:** [[Potassium-Argon Dating]] relies on the closed-system assumption. Argon loss from heating or incomplete degassing can reset ages, so mineral blocking temperatures matter. Biotite resets at about 225 °C or higher, while pegmatitic muscovite is more retentive. Extrusive rocks date eruption, while plutonic cooling ages may postdate emplacement.
- **Physiography:** The region lies in the southern Basin and Range province and comprises three subdivisions: the central mountain region (mainly Precambrian and Tertiary rocks), the eastern mountain region (Paleozoic through Tertiary rocks, more intensely Laramide-deformed), and the Sonoran Desert section. The [[Colorado Plateau]] lies to the northeast, bounded by an escarpment capped by Permian Kaibab Limestone.
- **Previous work:** Earlier syntheses (for example [[Wilson]] in the 1920s–1960s) assigned many volcanic rocks to the Cretaceous. Recent isotopic work reassigned most of them to the mid-Tertiary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper concerns geology rather than aquatic ecosystems, so biological content is minimal. Its environmental relevance is in three areas:

- **Paleoclimate:** Red beds, evaporites, buried desert landforms, and weathering patterns indicate arid climate through most of the Cenozoic. Pre-29 Ma sand dunes are preserved in the Sil Murk Formation.
- **Drainage history:** The [[Colorado River]] and [[Gila River]] systems and the [[Gulf of California]] form the modern drainage. Internal drainage prevailed during early Basin and Range subsidence, with evaporites accumulating in basins such as Luke and Picacho.
- **Hydrologic and land-use effects:** Ground-water withdrawal in the Picacho Basin has caused subsidence of nearly 300 km² by 2 to 4 m over 25 years, and fissures have formed near basin-margin faults.

## Key Findings

**Precambrian history**
- The older Precambrian was an evolving convergent margin with accretion to the southeast at about 2 km/m.y. This ended about 1.4 b.y. ago with intrusion of transcontinental batholiths.
- Younger Precambrian rocks accumulated in an epicontinental sea. Diabase sills intruded between 1,100 and 1,200 m.y. ago, marking the last Precambrian magmatism.
- Precambrian K-Ar ages fall into four groups: pegmatitic muscovite ≥1,400 m.y.; biotite and muscovite of 1,320–1,460 m.y.; biotite of 1,080–1,180 m.y.; and a group that matches no known thermal event.

**Paleozoic and Mesozoic**
- K-Ar ages within the Paleozoic are reset, and there is no evidence of Paleozoic magmatism in Arizona.
- A 153–154 m.y. Jurassic date (Desert Museum andesite porphyry, Tucson Mountains) forces revision of the Recreation redbeds, previously considered Cretaceous, to at least mid-Jurassic.

**Laramide (about 75–50 Ma)**
- Magmatism began about 75 Ma with an easterly progressing arc and ended by 50 Ma with leucocratic two-mica granites.
- The [[Gunnery Range Batholith]] covers nearly 15,000 km². Biotite ages of 53.1 and 52.5 Ma were measured on the western part. Muscovite from Adobe Blanco yielded 53.2 Ma.
- Of 28 Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora porphyry copper deposits, 27 have Laramide ages. Porphyry emplacement spanned 72 to 54 Ma.

**Eocene magma gap and erosion**
- A roughly 15 m.y. quiescent interval followed the Laramide, with erosion and deep denudation. Drainage then ran northeast onto the Colorado Plateau.

**Mid-Tertiary orogeny**
- Magmatism culminated in calc-alkalic ignimbrites and calcic latites in late Oligocene to early Miocene time.
- The magmatic peak migrated west at 3–4 cm/yr: about 32 Ma in western New Mexico, 26 Ma in the eastern mountain region, and 21 Ma in the Sonoran Desert.
- Mid-Tertiary rotation of crustal blocks created an angular unconformity over a period of about 6 m.y. between 17 and 12 Ma. In the Kofa Mountains, three lava flows show progressive tilting (53°, 30°, and flat-lying).
- The [[Cabeza Prieta Volcanics]] (about 16–18 Ma) fill canyons cut in the Gunnery Range granite. These buried canyons preserve arid landforms.

**Basin and Range disturbance**
- Basin subsidence began about 15 Ma and essentially ceased in the Sonoran Desert by about 8 Ma. Basaltic volcanism was prolific early and waned between 9 and 4 Ma.
- Post-4 Ma volcanism is restricted to four Plio-Pleistocene fields: Sentinel Plain-Arlington, Pinacate, San Carlos-Peridot, and San Bernardino.
- The [[Pinacate Volcanic Field]] holds more than 500 cinder and agglutinate cones and ten maar tuff rings, and is the most recently active field.
- Mantle-derived basalts show initial ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr ratios of 0.703–0.705.

**Drainage evolution and erosion rates**
- The [[Bouse Formation]] was dated at 5.5 Ma, and its deposition precedes integration of the Colorado and Gila rivers into the Gulf of California.
- The lower Colorado River cut its gorge at about 390 m/m.y. during 5.9–3.8 Ma and has downcut only about 30 m/m.y. since then.
- Tributaries (Virgin and Little Colorado) downcut at about 100 m/m.y. Denudation of the Colorado Plateau drainage is about 160 m/m.y., with isostatic rebound of about 130 m/m.y.
- The upper Gila River is actively downcutting; the lower Gila has been near its equilibrium profile for about 3 m.y.
