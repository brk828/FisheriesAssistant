## Executive Summary

This document is a **FY93 Annual Project Report** (Colorado River Recovery Program, Project #23) rather than a primary morphological or ecological field study. It serves as an addendum to a genetics management report concerning **coordinated hatchery facilities** for four endangered fish taxa native to the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (historically "Colorado Squawfish"), and [[Humpback Chub]]. The project, led by Principal Investigator Richard S. Wydoski (USFWS), aimed to define presumptive genetic stocks, assess population status/trends, and quantify propagation and hatchery infrastructure needs to support the Recovery Implementation Program (RIP). Fourteen presumptive stocks were identified across the basin. Critically, the report documents a **genetic contamination event**: a candidate razorback sucker broodstock from the Colorado River (Etter Pond, near De Beque, CO) was found via genetic analysis to consist of **hybrids with flannelmouth suckers**, disqualifying them from RIP broodstock use.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide direct morphological descriptions, meristic data, or anatomical measurements for the species discussed. However, it implicitly references **taxonomic/genetic identity** as the key characteristic under scrutiny:

- **Hybridization Identification**: Adult fish collected for a prospective [[Razorback Sucker]] broodstock (Colorado River, upstream of Westwater Canyon; source population at Etter Pond, De Beque, CO) were determined via **genetic analysis** to be hybrids between [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (Catostomidae) — illustrating the real-world importance of molecular verification over morphological field identification in catostomid stock assessments.
- No length, weight, coloration, or fin-ray data are reported; population assessments instead relied on **length-frequency analyses** to infer recruitment and stock stability (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: All species occupy the mainstem and major tributaries of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], including the [[Green River]] and the Colorado River mainstem upstream of Westwater Canyon.
- **Life-stage ecology**: The report explicitly flags major knowledge gaps regarding the **ecological requirements of different life stages** (e.g., larval, juvenile, adult) for these endangered fishes — identified as a primary source of uncertainty in long-term (>5 year) management planning.
- **Habitat Restoration Interaction**: Unknown fish responses to proposed **habitat restoration projects** were cited as a limiting factor in establishing long-term propagation and stocking needs.
- **Captive/Broodstock Habitat**: Propagation strategy relies on ex-situ refugia — notably the [[Ouray Endangered Fish Facility]] (pond-based holding/culture) and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] / Fish Technology Center, which maintains [[Bonytail Chub]] broodstock originating from **Lake Mohave** (Lower Colorado River Basin), reflecting a basin-spanning genetic refugia strategy since the species is nearly extirpated in the upper basin.

## Key Findings

- **Stock Identification**: 14 presumptive genetic stocks of endangered fishes were identified across the Upper Colorado River Basin.
- **Population Status/Trends**:
  - [[Razorback Sucker]]: Declining rapidly in the upper basin; some recruitment detected via length-frequency analysis.
  - [[Bonytail Chub]]: Near extirpation in the upper basin; broodstock recovery dependent entirely on Lake Mohave-derived fish propagated at Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
  - [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] & [[Humpback Chub]]: Populations relatively small but considered **stable**, based on length-frequency analyses and juvenile recruitment collections.
- **Genetic Contamination Event**: Candidate Colorado River razorback sucker broodstock (from Etter Pond) confirmed as [[Razorback Sucker]] × [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] hybrids — excluded from RIP use, underscoring the necessity of genetic verification in broodstock development.
- **Propagation Priorities** (1 = highest priority, 4 = lowest):
  1. Razorback Sucker
  2. Bonytail Chub
  3. Humpback Chub
  4. Colorado Pikeminnow (Squawfish)
- **Infrastructure Gap**: An estimated **21.5 acres** of ponds are needed for culturing/holding endangered fishes RIP-wide; existing plus planned 1994–1995 construction at the [[Ouray Endangered Fish Facility]] will yield only ~11 acres — a **10.5-acre shortfall** relative to immediate needs.
- **Management Consensus**: The Biology Committee agreed that all future augmentation/restoration stocking should first proceed as **field experiments**, evaluated prior to any large-scale stocking efforts.
- **Uncertainty Drivers for Long-Term Planning** (>5 years): lack of definitive stock identification, incomplete genetic study results, unknown life-stage ecological requirements, and unknown fish responses to habitat restoration.
- **Policy Recommendations**: Adoption/review of four key governance documents — Genetics Guidelines for Propagation of Endangered Fish, Disposition Policy for Captive-Reared Endangered Fish, Stocking Policy for Endangered Fish in the Upper Colorado River Basin, and the Coordinated Hatchery Status Report — plus an annual, phased review of captive-rearing needs tied to Statements of Work (SOWs).
