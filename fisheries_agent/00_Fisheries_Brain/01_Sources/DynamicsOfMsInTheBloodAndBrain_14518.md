# 42. Dynamics of MS-222 in the blood and brain of freshwater fishes during anesthesia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-anesthesia #pharmacokinetics #Ictaluridae #Salmonidae #Lepisosteidae

## Executive Summary

This 1970 study by Joseph B. Hunn (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, Fish Control Laboratory, La Crosse, Wisconsin) investigated the pharmacokinetics of [[MS-222]] (tricaine, a widely used fish anesthetic) in the blood and brain of 11 freshwater fish species during induction of anesthesia. Fish were exposed to MS-222 solutions (100–1,000 mg/L) for 1–11 minutes, and blood/brain concentrations of free and acetylated MS-222 were measured. The drug rapidly crossed the gill epithelium and the blood-brain barrier, reaching measurable brain concentrations within 1 minute. Evidence of drug metabolism (acetylation) was found in all species. Brain concentration of free MS-222 increased with depth of anesthesia up to loss of reflex, after which it either continued to rise slightly or declined slightly as fish approached medullary collapse. This work extended prior findings in the dogfish shark ([[Squalus acanthias]]) to freshwater teleosts and holosteans, establishing key dose-response and species-sensitivity benchmarks for [[Fish Anesthesia]] protocols.

Blood samples were collected via caudal puncture; brain tissue was obtained after severing the spinal cord. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

While not an ecological study per se, the species selection spans a broad taxonomic and ecological range of North American freshwater fish inhabiting the upper [[Mississippi River]] system and associated Iowa/Wisconsin waters:

- **Primitive holosteans**: [[Shortnose Gar]], [[Longnose Gar]], and [[Bowfin]] — ancient lineages with unique gill/respiratory physiology, tested for comparative drug uptake.
- **Salmonid**: [[Rainbow Trout]] — a cold-water hatchery species and standard bioassay organism.
- **Esocid**: [[Northern Pike]] — a piscivorous ambush predator of vegetated wetlands.
- **Cyprinid**: [[Common Carp]] — a benthic omnivore, hardy and tolerant.
- **Catostomid**: [[Spotted Sucker]] — benthic detritivore/invertivore.
- **Ictalurids**: [[Black Bullhead]] and [[Channel Catfish]] — bottom-dwelling omnivores of rivers and warm ponds.
- **Moronid**: [[White Bass]] — pelagic schooling predator.
- **Centrarchid**: [[Bluegill]] — a common warm-water panfish.

This taxonomic breadth allowed the author to assess whether gill and blood-brain-barrier permeability to MS-222 is a conserved physiological trait across divergent freshwater fish lineages, relevant to standardizing [[Fish Anesthesia]] and handling protocols across [[Fisheries Management]] and hatchery contexts.

## Key Findings

- **Rapid gill uptake:** MS-222 crossed the gill epithelium and entered the bloodstream within 1 minute of exposure in all 11 species, far exceeding background levels of primary aromatic amines.
- **Species variation in blood uptake ratio:** The ratio of peak whole-blood MS-222 concentration to anesthetic solution concentration ranged from 0.14 ([[Shortnose Gar]]) to 0.83 ([[Rainbow Trout]]), indicating substantial interspecific variability in gill permeability/uptake efficiency.
- **Blood-brain barrier penetration:** MS-222 rapidly crossed the blood-brain barrier; in 7 of 11 species, brain concentration exceeded whole-blood concentration after just 1 minute of exposure. By 3 minutes, brain concentrations exceeded blood concentrations in all species.
- **Drug metabolism (acetylation):** Acetylated MS-222 was detected in the blood of all species, confirming that freshwater fish metabolize the drug. Most species showed <20% acetylation; the [[Bowfin]] was an outlier with 34% acetylated drug after only 1 minute of exposure — the highest of any species tested.
- **Dose thresholds for anesthesia:** A minimum brain concentration of approximately 100 mg/kg free MS-222 was required for loss of reflex in susceptible species (e.g., [[Rainbow Trout]]), while more resistant species like the [[Black Bullhead]] required approximately 200 mg/kg.
- **Brain concentration dynamics during deepening anesthesia:** Free MS-222 brain concentration increased with depth of anesthesia up to loss of reflex; beyond this point (toward medullary collapse), concentrations either rose slightly further or declined slightly, suggesting an approach to metabolic/distributional equilibrium.
- **Caudal puncture blood sampling limitation:** Blood MS-222 concentrations obtained via caudal puncture (largely venous blood) never reached the concentration of the surrounding anesthetic solution, even after 11 minutes, likely reflecting incomplete arterial-venous equilibration.
- **Comparative cross-species confirmation:** These freshwater teleost/holostean results corroborate earlier work on the [[Dogfish Shark]] (*Squalus acanthias*) by Stenger and Maren (1968) and Maren, Embry, and Broder (1968), showing that MS-222 rapidly and consistently reaches the central nervous system across cartilaginous and bony fish taxa via gill absorption.
- **Excretion pathway (from cited literature):** Nonpolar MS-222 and its N-acetyl derivative are primarily excreted via the gills during recovery (95% in dogfish shark; 79–85% in rainbow trout), while polar metabolites are excreted renally.
- **Practical implication:** Anesthesia depth and recovery in fresh water are tightly linked to the concentration of free (non-acetylated) MS-222 in blood and brain, supporting brain/blood free-drug concentration as the key physiological correlate for anesthetic staging in [[Fish Control]] and hatchery practice.
