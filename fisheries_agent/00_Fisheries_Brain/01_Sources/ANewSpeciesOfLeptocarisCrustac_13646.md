## Executive Summary

This paper (Zamudio Valdéz & Reid, 1990) describes a new species of harpacticoid copepod, **[[Leptocaris stromatolicolus]]**, collected from within stromatolites in two evaporitic (saline) lakes and a marsh in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, México. This discovery represents only the **second record** of the genus [[Leptocaris]] from inland (continental) waters of the Americas, the first being *L. brevicornis* from Lake Ilopango, El Salvador. The species is diagnosed primarily by the **uniarticulate (single-article) endopod of leg 1** in both sexes—a rare trait among congeners—and by differences in swimming leg setation from its closest relative, *L. mangalis*. The study highlights the unique, highly mineralized, ionically distinctive waters of Cuatro Ciénegas as a novel habitat type for the genus, which is otherwise typically associated with marine/brackish sand and phytal substrates.

## Background & Description

**Female** (holotype/paratypes):
- Body length (excluding caudal setae): 420–628 µm
- Body cylindrical; anal somite tapering posteriorly, nearly twice the length of the preceding somite
- Prosomite 1 with scattered hairs; succeeding somites bear 1–3 pairs of small, sometimes irregularly sculptured papillae
- Genital field with a small sclerotized area anteriorly and paired internal rounded refringent vesicles
- Anal operculum subtriangular with smooth margin; single dorsal hair row beneath operculum
- Caudal rami ~2× longer than broad; one stout broadened terminal seta, three lateral setae, one medial seta, one dorsal seta at midlength
- Antennule: 5 articles, spinule row on article 1, esthetasc on article 3
- Antenna: allobasis with long slender outer seta; exopod reduced to two small setae
- Mandible palp reduced to a single smooth seta
- Maxilliped **absent**
- **Diagnostic trait**: Endopod of leg 1 composed of a **single article** (vs. typical two articles in most congeners)
- Legs 1–4: exopods of three articles; endopods of legs 2–4 with two articles
- Setal formulae documented for legs 1–4 (see original figures 12–16)
- Leg 5: single plate, indistinctly separated from somite, each side with three smooth setae (outermost longest)

**Male**:
- Body length: 440–500 µm
- Similar habitus to female but with more numerous somatic setae/papillae; dorsal papillae on prosomites 4 and 5 more prominent and complexly sculptured
- Antennule: geniculate, 6 articles; articles 3–5 form a strong hook; article 1 with comb of spinules and spatulate spine; esthetascs on articles 3 and 6
- Endopods 2 of legs 2 and 3 sexually modified: shorter, stouter, bent laterally at proximal third, tapered tip with setule comb
- Leg 5: single fused plate distinct from somite; slender outer seta plus three stout inner setae with whiplike tips
- Leg 6: trapezoidal protrusion, more developed/ornamented (spinule rows) on left side of somite

**Taxonomic placement**: Belongs to the *brevicornis*-group of Kunz (1983), characterized by five setae/spines on exopod 3 of legs 3–4 and four setae on endopod 2 of legs 2–3 — considered the most "primitive" setal condition in the genus, though *L. stromatolicolus* also exhibits some derived features (subtriangular anal operculum, spinule rows on antennule article 1, sculptured male papillae).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat type**: Interstitial spaces within **stromatolites** (microbial mat/carbonate structures) in shallow evaporitic desert lakes, plus one marsh record.

**Collection sites** (all within the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Chihuahuan Desert):

1. **Laguna La Puente Zumbadora (La Playita)**
   - Shallow evaporitic lake; max depth 1.8 m; area 15.2 ha
   - Conductivity: 3650 µSiemens; Salinity: 1.3‰; pH 7.5
   - Temperature: 23–28°C

2. **Laguna Grande**
   - Shallow evaporitic lake; depth <1 m; area 30.5 ha
   - Bottom: firm sand, nearly unvegetated
   - Conductivity: 7000 µS; Salinity: 70.0‰ (hypersaline); pH 7.2
   - Dominant ions: Ca²⁺ (560–620 mg/l), SO₄²⁻ (2700–3100 mg/l), Na⁺ (350–395 mg/l), Cl⁻ (291–322 mg/l), Mg²⁺ (225–299 mg/l), CO₃²⁻ (121–125 mg/l)
   - Total dissolved solids: 4–5 g/l
   - Temperature: 24–29°C

3. **Unnamed marsh** (single specimen)
   - Dense algal beds; salt deposition on grass stems

**Water chemistry context**: Spring waters in the basin are initially dominated by SO₄²⁻ and Ca²⁺; evaporation and CaCO₃ precipitation drive a shift toward SO₄²⁻/Mg²⁺ dominance, transitioning water type from sulfato-carbonate to sulfato-chloride (per [[Minckley and Cole 1968]]).

**Broader niche of genus Leptocaris**: Most of the ~10 New World congeners occur in marine or brackish coastal habitats (sand, mud, phytal substrates including decomposing mangrove leaves), often as euryhaline/eurythermal specialists in "difficult" isolated brackish pools or beach sands. Only two congeners — *[[Leptocaris brevicornis]]* and *L. sibirica* — were previously known from continental (freshwater/brackish) Eurasian waters. The stromatolite microhabitat in Cuatro Ciénegas appears to provide a novel interstitial niche, and the authors note no prior records of the genus from waters with such unusual ionic proportions.

## Key Findings

- **New species described**: *Leptocaris stromatolicolus* sp. nov., named for its stromatolite-dwelling habitat.
- **Biogeographic significance**: Second inland-water record of genus *Leptocaris* in the Americas (after *L. brevicornis* in Lake Ilopango, El Salvador).
- **Key diagnostic character**: Single-article endopod of leg 1 in both sexes — shared only with *L. mangalis* among congeners, but distinguished from that species by exopod/endopod setal counts on legs 1, 3, and 4, and by antennule article number in females.
- **Habitat novelty**: First documented occurrence of the genus within a **stromatolite** interstitial habitat, and within waters of the distinctive Ca-SO₄-dominated ionic composition found in [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]].
- **Endemism context**: Cuatro Ciénegas basin is noted for high aquatic endemism (Minckley 1969; Minckley & Cole 1968), and this finding adds to the record of undescribed crustacean diversity in its microhabitats.
- **Taxonomic framework**: Species placed within Kunz's (1983) *brevicornis*-group, though it exhibits a mosaic of "primitive" and "derived" morphological traits, complicating current species-group classification within *Leptocaris* (per [[Fleeger and Clark 1980]]).
- **Collection method**: Stromatolite core sampling (4.6 cm diameter corer, 8 cm depth), sieved through 1000 µm and 44 µm sieves, rose bengal stained, 4% formalin fixed.
