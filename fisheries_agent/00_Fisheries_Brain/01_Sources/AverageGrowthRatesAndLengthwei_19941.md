# Average Growth Rates and Length-Weight Relationships for Fifteen Species of Fish in Oklahoma Waters

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This report, by Alfred Houser and Michael G. Bross of the [[Oklahoma Fishery Research Laboratory]], consolidates statewide average growth rates and length-weight relationships for fifteen freshwater fish species in Oklahoma. It is a summary of excerpts and revisions from eight earlier published reports (1953–1962), plus newly analyzed data for [[River Carpsucker]]. The stated purpose is to provide a reference set that fishery biologists can use to compare the condition of local populations against statewide averages.

## Background & Description

- **Program origin:** Beginning in 1952 with a study of [[Channel Catfish]] growth by Hall and Jenkins, the laboratory compiled average growth rates for all major fish species in the state. By the time of this report, eight published reports had presented statewide averages for fourteen species, and one further species was added.
- **Species covered (15):** [[Largemouth Bass]], [[White Crappie]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Longear Sunfish]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Warmouth]], [[Orangespotted Sunfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Black Bullhead Catfish]], [[White Bass]], [[River Carpsucker]] and [[Freshwater Drum]].
- **Source reports:** Cited publications include Jenkins and Finnell (1954, channel catfish), Hall, Jenkins and Finnell (1954, crappies), Houser (1960, freshwater drum), Houser and Collins (1962, black bullhead), Jenkins and Elkin (1957, white bass), Jenkins, Elkin and Finnell (1955, sunfishes), Jenkins and Hall (1953, largemouth bass) and McCoy (1955, flathead catfish).
- **Methods:**
  - All lengths are total lengths recorded in inches.
  - Growth rates were calculated by the [[Direct Proportion Method]], assuming an intercept of zero.
  - A logarithmic [[Length-Weight Relationship]] equation was calculated for each species. Tabulated values and graphs cover the normally expected length ranges.
- **Acknowledgments:** The authors credit the original publication authors, numerous biologists and students, and Mrs. Gerry Dillard, who prepared the figures and manuscript.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling base:** Data come from population sampling by Oklahoma Fishery Research Laboratory personnel over a 13-year period. Fish were collected from reservoirs, lakes, ponds and streams across the state.
- **Largest flathead catfish sample:** The McCoy (1955) study covered 1,456 fish from 15 reservoirs (501 acres and larger), 15 smaller lakes and eight streams, across twenty-one Oklahoma lakes.
- **River carpsucker sample:** A total of 2,358 fish were aged: 1,910 from reservoirs, 18 from lakes (11 to 501 acres), 11 from ponds and 419 from streams. The length-weight equation was calculated from 2,565 fish.
- **Use of the data:** The report notes that growth averages, together with slowest and fastest growth records, allow a population's condition to be compared quickly against the statewide average. Length-weight equations allow estimation of missing length or weight values.
- **Supporting agencies:** The Oklahoma Wildlife Conservation Department and the University of Oklahoma Biological Survey. The advisory committee included Roy Stafford, Mutt Standefer, George L. Cross, S. W. Jackson Jr., Carl D. Riggs and Lloyd Swearingen.

## Key Findings

**Largemouth Bass (Table 1, Table 2, Figures 1–3)**
- Statewide average growth was given for ages 1 to 9, with slowest and fastest rates for each age.
- At age 5, the average length was 17.1 inches (2.78 lb), against slowest 12.9 inches (1.2 lb is not reported; slowest weight listed) and fastest 22.8 inches (7.06 lb).
- The length-weight table covers 6.2 to 24.0 inches, from 47 g to 3,791 g (8.351 lb). The equation is given as log(10W) = −2.56229 + 3.24884 log L (W in grams, L in inches).

**White Crappie (Table 3, Table 4, Figures 4–6)**
- At age 5, the average was 11.9 inches and 0.926 lb. At age 8, the average was 15.0 inches and 2.021 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 3.0 to 19.0 inches.

**Black Crappie (Table 5, Table 6, Figures 7–9)**
- At age 5, the average was 11.6 inches and 0.894 lb. At age 7, the average was 15.2 inches and 2.185 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 3.0 to 19.0 inches.

**Bluegill (Table 7, Table 8, Figures 10–12)**
- At age 5, the average was 7.3 inches and 0.286 lb. The fastest growth at age 5 was 10.2 inches and 0.906 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 1.5 to 9.5 inches.

**Redear Sunfish (Table 9, Table 10, Figures 13–15)**
- At age 6, the average was 10.1 inches and 0.739 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 2.5 to 12.0 inches.

**Longear Sunfish (Table 11, Table 12, Figures 16–18)**
- At age 4, the average was 5.2 inches and 0.118 lb. The fastest growth at age 4 was 6.1 inches and 0.197 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 2.0 to 6.5 inches.

**Green Sunfish (Table 13, Table 14, Figures 19–21)**
- At age 5, the average was 8.9 inches and 0.490 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 2.0 to 10.5 inches.

**Warmouth (Table 15, Table 16, Figures 22–24)**
- Growth was tabulated for ages 1 to 6. The length-weight table covers approximately 2.0 to 11.0 inches.

**Orangespotted Sunfish (Table 17, Table 18, Figures 25–27)**
- Growth was tabulated for ages 1 to 3. At age 3, the average was 3.9 inches and 0.039 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 1.5 to 4.5 inches.

**Channel Catfish (Table 19, Table 20, Figures 28–30)**
- Growth was tabulated for ages 1 to 14. At age 5, the average was 16.1 inches and 1.360 lb. At age 10, the average was 24.8 inches and 5.930 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 4.0 to 35.0 inches, up to 19.177 lb.

**Flathead Catfish (Table 21, Table 22, Figures 31–33)**
- Growth was tabulated for ages 1 to 19. At age 5, the average was 22.8 inches and 5.03 lb. At age 10, the average was 33.4 inches and 17.42 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 4.0 to 50.0 inches, reaching 64.79 lb at 50 inches.

**Black Bullhead Catfish (Table 23, Table 24, Figures 34–36)**
- Growth was tabulated for ages 1 to 4 or more (table layout partly illegible in the source).
- The length-weight table covers 1.8 to 15.5 inches, with weights in grams.

**White Bass (Table 25, Table 26, Figures 37–39)**
- At age 5, the average was 17.1 inches and 2.64 lb. Fastest growth at age 5 was 18.9 inches and 4.30 lb.
- The length-weight relationship uses two equations, one for lengths of 3.0 to 16.0 inches and one for longer fish.

**River Carpsucker (Table 27, Table 28, Figures 40–42)**
- At age 5, the average was 17.0 inches and 2.454 lb. At age 2, the average was 10.1 inches and 0.482 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 3.0 to 24.0 inches. The equation is log W = −0.80013 + 3.12621 log L (W in grams, L in inches).

**Freshwater Drum (Table 29, Table 30, Figures 43–45)**
- At age 5, the average was 17.5 inches and 2.50 lb. At age 14, the average was 33.3 inches and 23.00 lb.
- The length-weight table covers 3.0 to 25.0 inches, with weights up to 7.794 lb.

**Cross-species summary:** Tables for all fifteen species report slowest, average and fastest growth by age. Length-weight tables convert between length and weight for each species. The equations are log-linear, with the constants varying by species and by the unit scale applied.
