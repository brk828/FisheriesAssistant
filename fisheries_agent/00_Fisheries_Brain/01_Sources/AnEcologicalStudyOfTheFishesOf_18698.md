# Seasonal Variation of Fishes in the Fall and Verdigris Rivers, Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes a year-round study of the fish assemblage of the [[Fall River]] and [[Verdigris River]] in Wilson and Montgomery Counties, southeastern Kansas, conducted from March 1954 to February 1955 as part of a larger survey of the rivers' physical, chemical, and biological characteristics. The study was published in the *Emporia State Research Studies* (Vol. 5, No. 3, March 1957) by Kansas State Teachers College and funded by the Standard Oil Company (Indiana). The author, [[Claire Schelske]], documented 42 species in 10 families across four stations and examined how their monthly occurrence changed over the year.

## Background & Description

The study was conducted by the Department of Biology at Kansas State Teachers College, Emporia, and was companion to a study of Cladocera and Copepoda by [[Carl W. Prophet]]. The objectives were to determine the number of fish species present and the extent of seasonal variation in collections in each river. The author notes that few year-round studies of Kansas stream fishes had been made previously. Identifications were verified by [[Frank Cross]] and [[John Breukelman]].

The literature review covers Kansas ichthyology from the 1880s onward (e.g., Gilbert, Cragin, Graham, Breukelman, Cross) and comparable stream studies in Ohio, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, and Missouri. Notable prior Kansas checklists include Breukelman's 1940 catalogue of 56 species and subspecies in 10 families at Dyche Museum, and Bishop's 1935 key listing 99 species in 21 families.

Collection used two types of small drag seines (a 0.25-inch mesh knotted twine seine, 4 x 20 feet, and plastic screen seines 8 to 12 feet long) in pools and riffles. Pool seining used a V-shaped sweep at roughly 70° to the bank, while riffle seining involved disturbing the stream bottom upstream of a stationary seine.

The study area lies in the ecotone between grassland and deciduous forest biomes. Channels were 40 to 140 feet wide, with banks 10 to 40 feet high and stream gradients of about 2.0 feet per mile. Dominant riparian trees included cottonwood, sycamore, and black willow; aquatic vegetation included water-primrose, arrowhead, and duckweed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Four stations were sampled: two on the Fall and Verdigris rivers above their confluence, and two on the Verdigris below it. Thirty-four collections were made at each station over 12 months.

Seasonal patterns were documented for several groups:
- **Darters** were generally absent from collections in June, July, and August, when water temperatures were high. The orangethroat darter was collected least often when temperatures exceeded 22.6°C.
- **Percids** were largely absent from summer collections, except the [[Slenderhead Darter]], which was the only percid taken in all months.
- **Channel catfish, flathead catfish, spotted bass, and longnose gar** showed clearly seasonal occurrence.
- **Minnows** (Cyprinidae) showed no clear seasonal variation; nine of 13 species were present in at least 10 of 12 months in the primary station data.

Between-river differences were also noted. Darters were more common in the clearer water of the Fall River, whereas sunfish were more common in the Verdigris, where discharge and turbidity varied more. Suckers were not collected in the Fall River.

The literature review draws on concepts such as stream gradient and longitudinal succession ([[Longitudinal Succession]]), home range and territoriality in stream fishes, and the effects of flood and water-level fluctuation on spawning success, for example in [[Smallmouth Bass]] and channel catfish.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** A checklist of 42 species in 10 families was compiled. Minnows (18 species) dominated, followed by sunfishes (7), catfishes (6), suckers (6), perches (5), and one each of gars, herrings, livebearers, killifishes, and silversides.
- **Station counts:** Stations I, II, VIII, and IX yielded 29, 27, 31, and 31 species, respectively.
- **Cumulative completeness:** At Stations I, II, and IX, no new species appeared after the eighth month. At Station VIII, one new species was added in the 12th month.
- **Widespread species:** Of the 42 species, 20 (47.6 per cent) were present in 10 of 12 months. Only 7 species occurred in all four seasons.
- **Restricted species:** Twenty-five of the 42 species were not collected in more than two seasons.
- **Monthly richness:** October had the highest monthly total (29 species, 69.0 per cent); January had the lowest (15 species, 35.7 per cent).
- **Dominant abundance example:** The [[Red Shiner]] was present in 109 of 131 collections at all stations.
- **Additional records:** Breukelman (1940b) reported five species from the Verdigris not collected in this study's core set. Two species were collected by the author: one [[Freckled Madtom]] (Station I, July 30, 1955) and one [[Redfin Darter]] (Verdigris River, November 5, 1954).
- **Comparison with prior work:** Of 26 species reported from the Verdigris by Breukelman (1940a), 21 were collected by the author.
