# Threatened Fishes of Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report, reprinted from the *Proceedings of the Utah Academy of Sciences, Arts, and Letters* (vol. 51, part 2, 1974), catalogs the fish species and populations in Utah that are endangered, threatened, or of indeterminate status. It was prepared by the [[Utah Fishes Committee]] of the Bonneville Chapter, American Fisheries Society. Thirteen fishes are covered: ten endangered, one threatened, and two indeterminate. The authors attribute nearly all declines to human activity, chiefly [[Dam Construction and Reservoirs]], [[Water Diversion]], hybridization, and competition or predation by [[Introduced Species]]. The report also sets out research priorities and management recommendations.

## Background & Description

The report uses definitions from the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973:

- **Endangered:** in danger of extinction throughout all or a significant portion of its range.
- **Threatened:** likely to become endangered within the foreseeable future.
- **Indeterminate:** knowledge is lacking, but the species is thought to be rare.

Because fish ranges follow river systems rather than political boundaries, the authors distinguish between species that are endangered globally and [[Endangered Populations]] that are rare only within Utah.

Each species account covers five topics: description, documentation (range and abundance), factors influencing decline, recent studies, and recommendations. The taxa fall into three regional groups:

**Cutthroat trout ([[Cutthroat Trout]] subspecies; Salmonidae)**
- [[Colorado Cutthroat Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus clarkii pleuriticus*): endangered subspecies. Known in Utah only from the Little West Fork of the Blacks Fork River, Summit County.
- [[Utah Cutthroat Trout]] (*O. c. utah*): endangered subspecies, originally described from Utah Lake. Known Utah populations include Water Canyon, Reservoir Canyon, an isolated tributary of Little Cottonwood Creek, and Birch Creek near Beaver.
- [[Snake Valley Cutthroat Trout]] (*O. clarkii* sp.): endangered. Recently recognized as a distinct form. Found in the Trout Creek drainage and a population in the Deep Creek Range headwaters.

**Colorado River fishes (Cyprinidae and Catostomidae)**
- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): endangered. Once occupied the entire large-river system; Utah is now its stronghold.
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*): endangered. Holden (1973) considered it the most endangered species in the Colorado basin.
- [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*): endangered. Its validity as a species is questioned because intergrading specimens are common with bonytail.
- [[Humpback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): endangered. Found in the Green and Colorado Rivers and Lake Powell.

**Virgin River fishes**
- [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*): endangered subspecies. Taxonomy is uncertain.
- [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*): endangered. A streamlined, nearly scaleless minnow of swift, turbid, warm water.
- [[Virgin River Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*): threatened.

**Bonneville Basin fishes**
- [[Least Chub]] (*Iotichthys phlegethontis*): endangered. A small cyprinid, now found in only a few springs in Snake Valley.
- [[June Sucker]] (*Chasmistes liorus*) and [[Webug Sucker]] (*Catostomus fecundus*): indeterminate. Their taxonomy is confused, and they may be hybrids.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The species are adapted to specific habitats. Many are endemic to small areas or to particular parts of a drainage, and they are less able to adapt to new conditions. The authors note that generalist and introduced species often thrive in disturbed habitats where specialists decline, adding competitive pressure.

Key regional points:

- **[[Colorado River Basin]]:** Species were historically widespread in large rivers. Reservoir construction, flow and temperature changes below dams, and competition from introduced [[Channel Catfish]] have reduced them. Reproduction is largely confined to the Green River, especially [[Desolation Canyon]] and areas below Ouray.
- **[[Virgin River System]]:** Its native fauna is very specialized. Habitat loss from irrigation, reservoir construction such as [[Lake Mead]], and the introduction of [[Redside Shiner]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] are the main threats.
- **[[Bonneville Basin]]:** Native fishes here are poorly understood. Water diversion from streams draining the Wasatch Range has been a major factor.
- **Hybridization:** Introduced cutthroat strains (usually Yellowstone) and rainbow trout are the major cause of cutthroat decline. Water manipulation is often a second factor.

## Key Findings

- **Scale of loss:** The 13 listed fishes make up about 40% of Utah's native fishes, indicating heavy alteration of aquatic environments.
- **Colorado River system:** The four endangered Colorado basin species make up 57% of the large-river species native to that drainage.
- **Virgin River system:** The three Virgin River fishes constitute 50% of the native fish fauna there. Together, these two river systems are described as "endangered."
- **Human causes:** Most decimating factors were created by humans, mainly water demand, dams, and introduced species.
- **Population data:** Holden (1973) found only 53 humpback suckers in four summers of collecting in the upper basin. Squawfish reproduction is poor outside the Green River. Bonytails have shown no confirmed recent reproduction.
- **Recovery efforts:** The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] held squawfish and humpback suckers. At the time of writing, over 3,500 juvenile humpback suckers and 6,000–7,000 squawfish had been hatched. Utah State University began a radio telemetry study of humpback suckers and squawfish in [[Dinosaur National Monument]].
- **Virgin River chub:** Its recent, drastic decline in Utah and Arizona is the basis for endangered status. The causes are unknown.
- **Virgin River spinedace:** Its decline in the Santa Clara system was linked to redside shiner introduction and [[Largemouth Bass]] spread from Gunlock Reservoir.
- **Taxonomic uncertainty:** The status of the [[Humpback Chub]], [[Virgin River Chub]], and Utah Lake suckers is unclear. Hybridization complicates identification.

**Priority recommendations (in order):**
1. Least chub: continue distribution and abundance studies.
2. Bonytail chub: clarify population status, especially reproduction.
3. Virgin River chub: determine the cause of recent decline.
4. June and Webug suckers: complete a taxonomic analysis of Utah Lake suckers.

**Other recommendations:** Survey all possible sites for the cutthroat subspecies, especially the south slope of the Uinta Range, where [[Oil Shale]] development may threaten the Colorado cutthroat. Protect pure cutthroat streams. Monitor the Desolation Canyon area, protect Green River spawning sites, and fund life history studies. Keep the Virgin River free of introduced species and avoid altering the Virgin River system, including the proposed dam and blockage of La Verkin Spring. Monitor Bear Lake fishes.
