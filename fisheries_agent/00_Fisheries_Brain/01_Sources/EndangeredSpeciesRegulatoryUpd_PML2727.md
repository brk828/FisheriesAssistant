# Endangered Species Regulatory Update: Southwest Rules of July 1997

#conservation-biology #endangered-species #us-fish-and-wildlife-service

## Executive Summary

This letter from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, dated July 24, 1997) informs interested parties of three final rules published in the [[Federal Register]] in July 1997 concerning endangered species in the Southwest. The rules cover a proposed listing that was withdrawn, a critical habitat designation, and a new endangered listing. They are:

1. Withdrawal of the proposed threatened listing of the [[Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard]].
2. Designation of critical habitat for the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]].
3. Extension of endangered status to the [[Jaguar]] throughout its range.

The letter was signed by Field Supervisor [[Sam F. Spiller]].

## Background & Description

The letter is a regulatory notice, not a research report, and it provides no morphological, taxonomic, or biological description of the species. The three species named are:

- **[[Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard]]** (*Phrynosoma mcallii*): a lizard whose proposed threatened listing was under review.
- **[[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]** (*Empidonax traillii extimus*): a bird already listed as endangered, for which critical habitat was designated.
- **[[Jaguar]]** (*Panthera onca*): a large cat whose listing was extended across its entire range.

The governing legal framework is the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The letter notes that section 4 of the Act requires the Service to consider economic and other relevant impacts before finalizing the size and configuration of critical habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat and geography:** The willow flycatcher's critical habitat consists of 18 units totaling 964 kilometers (599 river miles) across Arizona, California, and New Mexico. The river-based extent suggests riparian habitat, though the letter does not describe vegetation or habitat type directly.
- **Threats to the jaguar:** A primary threat in the United States is illegal shooting. A minimum of 64 jaguars were killed in Arizona since 1900, and the most recent recorded kill in Arizona was in 1986. Loss and modification of habitat are likely contributors to the decline.
- **Population status:** Only a few individual jaguars are known to survive in the United States (Arizona and New Mexico). Their presence there is believed to depend on the jaguar's status in northern Mexico. Documented observations north of the border are as recent as 1996.
- **Threat status of the lizard:** The Service stated that some threats to the [[Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard]] are less serious than when the proposed rule was published. A [[Conservation Agreement]] is expected to further reduce threats, and data indicating population decline were judged inconclusive.
- **Conservation agreements:** The jaguar listing does not undermine the conservation agreement developed by the States of [[Arizona]] and [[New Mexico]]. It is intended to strengthen the efforts made under that agreement.

## Key Findings

- **Lizard listing withdrawn (July 15, 1997; 62 FR 37852):** The proposed threatened listing of the [[Flat-Tailed Horned Lizard]] was withdrawn for three reasons: threats are less serious than previously assessed, a conservation agreement will reduce threats further, and population-decline data are inconclusive.
- **Willow flycatcher critical habitat finalized (July 22, 1997; 62 FR 39129):** The [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], already listed as endangered, received critical habitat comprising 18 units and 964 km (599 river miles) in Arizona, California, and New Mexico. Economic and other relevant impacts were considered before the final decision.
- **Jaguar listed as endangered throughout its range (July 22, 1997; 62 FR 39147):** The [[Jaguar]] is now endangered in the United States, Mexico, and Central and South America. Illegal shooting is the primary U.S. threat, with at least 64 killed in Arizona since 1900.
- **No critical habitat for the jaguar:** Critical habitat was found "not prudent" and was not designated.
- **Access to rules:** Full texts of these and all other Federal Register rules are available from the Government Printing Office access site. Those without internet access or printed volumes may request copies from the Service.
