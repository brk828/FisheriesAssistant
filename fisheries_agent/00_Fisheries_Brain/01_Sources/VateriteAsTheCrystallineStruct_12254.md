# Vaterite as the Crystalline Structure of Otoliths of Xyrauchen texanus

## Executive Summary
This study characterized the crystalline structure of sagittal otoliths in the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a member of the family [[Catostomidae]]. Using X-ray powder diffraction (Debye-Scherrer method), the authors found that all nineteen sampled otoliths—from both wild and hatchery-reared individuals—were composed **entirely of vaterite**, an unstable polymorph of calcium carbonate. This is a notable departure from the typical teleost pattern, in which otoliths are almost universally composed of [[Aragonite]], with rare exceptions reported as [[Calcite]]. The finding adds *Xyrauchen texanus* to a short list of fish taxa known to deposit vaterite in their otoliths, and supports the hypothesis that otolith crystalline polymorphism may be taxon-specific despite a conserved organic matrix (otolin) across fish groups.

## Background & Description
- **Structure analyzed:** Sagittae (otoliths) — excised, cleaned of soft tissue, dried, and ground to powder prior to analysis.
- **Composition:** Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) deposited on an organic matrix of **otolin**, a protein with molecular weight near 150,000 Da (Degens et al., 1969).
- **Crystalline polymorph identified:** [[Vaterite]] — confirmed via crystalline d-spacing analysis, distinct from the two more common and thermodynamically stable polymorphs, aragonite and calcite.
- **Stability note:** Vaterite is inherently unstable and typically undergoes progressive transformation (vaterite → aragonite → calcite) in the presence of water at room temperature/pressure (McConnell, 1960). Its persistence in these otoliths is therefore biologically and chemically notable.
- **Sample composition:** 19 otoliths total, comprising:
  - Wild-caught adult razorback suckers (~30 years of age)
  - Hatchery-reared young-of-the-year individuals
  - Hatchery-reared three-year-old individuals

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered catostomid fish native to the Colorado River Basin.
- **Wild population source:** [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona–Nevada border, sampled via trammel netting (Minckley, 1983).
- **Hatchery source:** Fish propagated and reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Dexter, New Mexico (Hamman, 1985), representing a conservation/aquaculture context for this endangered species.
- **Conservation context:** Study supported in part by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Endangered Species Office, reflecting the species' status as an imperiled native sucker of southwestern U.S. rivers.
- **Comparative taxa referenced for otolith mineralogy:**
  - [[Bichir]] (Polypteridae) — statoconia vaterite, statoliths aragonite
  - [[Bowfin]] (Amiidae) — statoconia vaterite, statoliths aragonite
  - [[Gars]] (Lepisosteidae) — statoconia vaterite, statoliths aragonite
  - [[Sturgeon]] (Acipenseridae) — statoliths of vaterite
  - [[Steelhead Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) — ~27% of young fish had vateritic otoliths, with both aragonite and vaterite deposited as daily growth rings (Campana, 1983)

## Key Findings
- All 19 sagittal otoliths examined from *Xyrauchen texanus*, regardless of wild or hatchery origin or age class, were composed **entirely of vaterite** — a first documented report of this polymorph as the sole otolith structure in this catostomid species.
- This contrasts with the dominant literature consensus that teleost otoliths are typically composed of **aragonite** (Irie, 1955; Carlstrom, 1963; Degens et al., 1969), with **calcite** reported only rarely (Devereux, 1967).
- Vaterite is otherwise extremely rare in nature, previously documented mainly in:
  - Gastropod repair tissues and egg shells (Wilber & Watabe, 1963; Hall & Taylor, 1971)
  - Statoconia/statoliths of a limited number of "primitive" fish groups (bichir, bowfin, gars, sturgeon)
  - Otoliths of some hatchery-reared steelhead trout (Campana, 1983), suggesting environmental/rearing conditions may promote vaterite deposition.
- The specific polymorph of CaCO₃ deposited in otoliths appears influenced by local physicochemical conditions, including:
  - **High pH** (Rowlands & Webster, 1971)
  - **Presence of specific amino acids** — glycine, serine, or glutamate — which can alter precipitation form from solution (Kitano & Hood, 1965; Bischoff, 1968; Nathan, 1971)
- Although the organic matrix protein **otolin** appears biochemically uniform across fish taxa (Degens et al., 1969), the **crystalline polymorph deposited onto that matrix may be taxon-specific** (Carlstrom, 1963), as evidenced by this study's consistent findings across age classes and rearing environments within a single species.
- These results raise open questions about the physiological or environmental mechanisms controlling polymorph selection in otolith biomineralization, particularly relevant to [[Otolith Aging Methods]] and species-specific applications of otolith microchemistry/microstructure in fisheries science.
