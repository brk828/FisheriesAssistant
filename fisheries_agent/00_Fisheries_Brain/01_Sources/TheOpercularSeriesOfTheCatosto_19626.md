# Opercular Series of the Catostomidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This classic morphological study by [[Edward M. Nelson]] (Osborn Zoological Laboratory, Yale University) examines the opercular series, the set of gill-cover bones, of the sucker family [[Catostomidae]]. The work is based on 83 preserved specimens across 15 genera. The author classifies the genera into three well-defined groups plus a fourth group of mixed affinities, using combinations of opercular, interopercular and branchiostegal character states. The analysis also reassigns [[Hypentelium]] to the [[Moxostomatini]] and questions the generic identity of a fossil "Catostomus" from the Eocene of the Gobi.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** The study forms part of a broader investigation into catostomid morphology and evolution. The author notes that a previously described Eocene fossil operculum, referred to *Catostomus* by Hussakof (1932), is based on a feature (a short, slender anterior-upper process) that is not unique to catostomids.
- **Materials and methods:** 83 preserved specimens were examined. The right opercular series of each was removed, cleaned to a bare osteological preparation, and several specimens were dissected to study element relationships. Measurements were taken with millimeter calipers and a transparent protractor, and ratios were used to detect differences and correlations.
- **Specimen sources:** Mainly the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ), with additional material from the University of Missouri, the American Museum of Natural History and the Wisconsin Conservation Department. One extra specimen was examined as an X-ray.
- **Elements described:** The operculum (with an articular process, an opercular arm and an auricular-type corner), the articular process (cup-like medial depression forming a ball-and-socket joint with the hyomandibular), the suboperculum, the interoperculum (with a variable preopercular process), and the branchiostegal rays.
- **Musculature:** Three functional parts of the dilator operculi muscle, plus the levator operculi, adductor operculi and superior hyohyoideus muscles, are described in terms of their origins, insertions and roles in opercular movement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Several opercular traits appear to be adaptive. The anterior concavity of the operculum in *Hypentelium* and *Pantosteus* is interpreted as a convergent adaptation to swift-current habitats.
- The notched interoperculum found in *Hypentelium* occurs only among recognized moxostomatins and is not obviously adaptive.
- The study also draws comparisons with other fish groups (for example the characin *Citharinus*), where similar opercular modifications occur, using [[Gregory's Fish Skulls]] (1933) as a reference.

## Key Findings

- **Specimen base:** 83 specimens in total (table of 15 genera; largest counts include *Carpiodes* 12, *Catostomus* 15, *Pantosteus* 10, *Erimyzon* 8, *Moxostoma* 8, *Thoburnia* and *Xyrauchen* 2 each). The table notes one additional X-ray specimen.
- **Three well-defined groups:**
  1. *Catostomus* group: *Catostomus*, *Chasmistes*, *Pantosteus*, *Xyrauchen*. Character assemblage OP1, IOP1, B1, 3B1 (*Pantosteus* tends toward OP3).
  2. *Carpiodes* group: *Carpiodes*, *Ictiobus*, *Megastomatobus*. Assemblage OP2, IOP1, B2, 3B2 (*Thoburnia* has more of an OP1 structure).
  3. *Moxostoma* group: *Moxostoma*, *Placopharynx*, *Hypentelium*, *Thoburnia*. Assemblage OP3, IOP2, B3 (with variations).
- **Mixed group:** *Myxocyprinus*, *Cycleptus*, *Minytrema* and *Erimyzon*, each with its own mixture of characters. *Megapharynx* and *Lagochila* probably belong with the third group, based on superficial observation only.
- **Character states:** The operculum is classified as OP1, OP2 or OP3; the interoperculum as IOP1 or IOP2; the branchiostegals as B1, B2 or B3 (with 3B1 to 3B3 for the third ray).
- **Reassignment of Hypentelium:** Although externally similar to catostomins, *Hypentelium* is morphologically (and genetically) a member of the Moxostomatini, supported by its Weberian apparatus and notched interoperculum. The author suggests it may instead belong to the Thoburniini of Hubbs.
- **Fossil identity:** The Gobi "*Catostomus*" is incorrectly designated. It is probably a catostomid, but its generic identity cannot be determined until more material is available. The operculum most closely matches *Myxocyprinus* (the Chinese sucker) among living forms.
- **Summary conclusion:** Opercular morphology is a useful systematic tool for catostomids, though the author notes it must be combined with other evidence, since many fish groups share similar opercular features.
