## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the White River National Fish Hatchery (WRNFH), a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1972 under Act 86, Statute 369, located near Bethel, Vermont. The hatchery's sole mission is to produce juvenile [[Atlantic Salmon]] for restoration stocking into the [[Connecticut River]] watershed, supporting a multi-state (VT, NH, MA, CT) anadromous fish restoration program. The FY93 report highlights significant improvements in fish health management—particularly control of bacterial and parasitic disease—alongside infrastructure challenges with a newly constructed infiltration gallery. The facility also serves a substantial public outreach function, hosting approximately 15,000 visitors annually.

## Background & Description

The source text is an administrative/operational station profile rather than a morphological study; no direct anatomical, meristic, or morphometric data on [[Atlantic Salmon]] are provided. Fish are referenced only by life-history stage relevant to hatchery production and stocking:

- **Unfed fry** — newly hatched, pre-feeding life stage
- **Feeding fry** — post-yolk-sac absorption, actively feeding juveniles
- **Fingerlings/juveniles** — reared in outside concrete rearing units prior to distribution

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target species**: [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*), an anadromous salmonid native to the [[Connecticut River]] system.
- **Restoration context**: The hatchery exists specifically to support reintroduction of Atlantic salmon to a river system where the species had been extirpated, functioning within a broader interstate/interagency restoration program (cooperating state and federal agencies stock fry into "suitable habitat").
- **Distribution waters (FY93)**: Fish distributed across federal, state, and private waters spanning Vermont, New Hampshire, Massachusetts, and Connecticut — reflecting the species' historical anadromous range within the Connecticut River drainage.
- **Facility infrastructure supporting the niche**: 80 outside concrete rearing units, an infiltration gallery with pump house (groundwater/surface water supply), a water reuse pump facility, and a dedicated tagging facility (for marking fish prior to release, likely for stock tracking in the restoration effort).
- **Disease ecology**: Two significant fish health pressures are documented as ecological/pathogenic stressors within the rearing environment:
  - [[Aeromonas Salmonicida]] (causative agent of Furunculosis), a bacterial pathogen affecting reared Atlantic salmon.
  - [[Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis]] ("Ich"), a protozoan ectoparasite historically causing major mortality in the rearing pools.

## Key Findings

- **Disease control success (Furunculosis)**: Collaboration with Drs. Rocco Cipriano and Larisa Ford of the National Fisheries Research Center (Leetown, WV) led to staff training in early detection of *Aeromonas salmonicida*. This reduced furunculosis-related mortality from ~8,800 fish lost in 1990 to only 200 fish lost in the reporting period — a >97% reduction.
- **Parasite mitigation (Ich)**: Application of epoxy paint to outside rearing pools combined with daily cleaning disrupted the life cycle of *Ichthyophthirius multifiliis*, substantially reducing associated mortality, which had previously been a major operational problem.
- **Production output (FY93)**:
  - Over 2,299,000 unfed fry stocked — an **all-time high** for the hatchery.
  - 176,000 feeding fry stocked.
  - 589,596 fish distributed in FY93 (excluding eggs/fry), compared to 303,408 in FY92 and 500,000 in FY91.
- **Public engagement**: ~15,000 visitors toured the facility during FY93, indicating a significant outreach/education role alongside production.
- **Infrastructure challenges**: The newly completed infiltration gallery experienced operational failure due to four faulty 14" valves that would not properly close during backflush cycles, impairing backflushing effectiveness. Contractor remediation attempts failed; the issue was ultimately resolved through direct intervention by the Regional Engineering Office with valve replacement.
- **Funding/staffing trends (FY91–FY95 est.)**: Operations budget rose from $536,695 (FY91) to a projected $530,000 (FY95, likely a data scaling inconsistency in the original table); Maintenance budget increased substantially from $93,366 (FY91) to a projected $150,000 (FY95). FTE staffing remained relatively stable (~9.6–10.6). Cost recoverables (funded via NMFS) grew from $22,500 (FY91) to a projected $33,000 (FY95).
- **Administrative context**: Facility operates within USFWS Region 5, under the authority of Act 86, Statute 369 (August 10, 1972), situated within Vermont's single at-large Congressional District.
