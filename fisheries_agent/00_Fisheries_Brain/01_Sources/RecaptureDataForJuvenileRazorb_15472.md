# Recapture Data for Juvenile Razorback Suckers in Lake Mohave, 1993

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This field memorandum reports recapture data for hatchery-reared juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]]s released into [[Lake Mohave]] on the [[Colorado River]]. The 1992 year-class was reared at [[Yuma Cove]], partly moved to [[Davis Cove]], and released after [[PIT Tagging]]. Five tagged fish were recaptured during the [[Razorback Sucker Roundup]] (March 13–19, 1993). All five had been at large for 62 to 116 days and had moved between 3.5 and 21 river miles. Four of the five were recovered in or near known spawning areas, and one was taken alone in shallow water.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large catostomid native to the Colorado River system. The document uses it as a model species for stocking evaluation, and it is listed as endangered.

Fish were raised in [[Yuma Cove]], an isolated backwater pond on Lake Mohave, and marked with PIT tags. The stocking history was as follows:

- **1992 rearing:** 297 juveniles were reared in and harvested from Yuma Cove.
- **Summer 1992 transfer:** 167 fish were moved to Davis Cove.
- **Direct release:** the remaining 130 fish were PIT tagged and released directly into Lake Mohave.
- **January–February 1993:** 23 juveniles recaptured from Davis Cove were PIT tagged and released into Lake Mohave.
- **Total:** the 1992 [[Year-Class]] at large in Lake Mohave was 153 fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Lake Mohave is a reservoir on the lower Colorado River, bordered by Arizona and Nevada. Recapture locations ranged from river mile 1 (Davis Cove) to river mile 45.5 (Nevada side), and the fish moved both up and down lake. Sites named in the records include [[Shoshone Cove]] (river mile 3.5), [[Carp Cove]] (about river mile 19), and [[Eldorado Canyon]] (Nelson's Landing, mile post 39).

Recaptured fish were taken during the spring spawning period. Several were ripe and tuberculate males, and one was infected with anchor worm (*Lernaea*), a copepod parasite. Four of the five fish were captured among adult razorback suckers at known or presumed spawning sites. One fish, captured in the back of a cove in shallow water (under 3 feet) over soft substrate, was alone.

**Recapture summary (March 13–19, 1993 Roundup):** Five of the 153 fish at large were recaptured, which is about 3.3%.

**Growth and condition:** All recaptured fish increased in length and weight. Growth over 62–116 days ranged from +1 mm to +19 mm in total length, and gains in weight ranged from +22 g to +81 g. Fish 7F7D182A12 also carried an old right pectoral fin clip (RPI).

**Movement:** Fish moved between about 3.5 and 21 river miles from their release or origin points. Most movement was up lake, away from the release site at river mile 24.

**Sex and reproductive status:** Two recaptured fish were identified as ripe males (7F7D303C05 and 7F7D182A12). Fish 7F7D182A12 remained ripe and tuberculate at recapture. Fish 7F7D1A4A5F was also a ripe, tuberculate male.

**Habitat association:** Four of the five recaptures were made among adult razorback suckers at spawning or presumed spawning sites. The fifth (7F7D174A23) was alone in shallow, soft-bottomed water.

**Data notes:**
- The release date for fish 7F7D18215D is printed as November 23, 1993. The 112 days at large and the March 1993 recapture both point to November 23, 1992, so the 1992 date is used here.
- Fish 7F7D174A23 is reported at 62 days at large. The dates given (January 13 to March 17, 1993) span 63 days.

**Contacts:** Data questions should go to the personnel named in each record (Baucom, P. Marsh, C.O. Minckley, B. Bristow). The [[Razorback Sucker Roundup]] was the main recapture effort, and the methods used were [[Electrofishing]], [[Trammel Netting]], and [[Gill Netting]].
