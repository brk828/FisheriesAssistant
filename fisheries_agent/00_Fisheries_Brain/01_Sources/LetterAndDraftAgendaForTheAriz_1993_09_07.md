## Executive Summary
This document is administrative/organizational correspondence (a letter and draft agenda) for the 1993 Arizona Fisheries Summit, convened by the [[Coconino National Forest]] under the [[USDA Forest Service]]. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, the text outlines the logistics and objectives of a stakeholder meeting focused on the management and recovery of [[Native Fish|Arizona's native fish]] populations. The stated goal was to shift discussion away from historical causes of decline toward identifying barriers to recovery and building interagency commitment. Keynote framing was provided by presenter [[John Rinne]], who posed the central ecological question: "[[Southwestern Aquatic Ecosystems]] - Are They Sustainable?"

## Key Findings
- **Purpose**: The summit aimed to increase participant commitment to native fish management and to identify structural/institutional barriers preventing population recovery, rather than re-litigating causes of decline.
- **Discussion Topics Proposed** for breakout sessions:
  - **[[Marketing Native Fish]]**: Emphasized the necessity of public awareness and support for native fish conservation, framing outreach as key to building non-traditional constituencies, partnerships, funding, and agency accountability.
  - **[[Research Needs]] for Native Fish**: Identified critical gaps in understanding of biology, life history, habitat requirements, and limiting factors for native species; called for coordinated resource-sharing among agencies to reduce duplication of research effort.
  - **[[Native Fish Sanctuaries]]**: Proposed formal designation of a stream network dedicated to protecting Arizona's aquatic biodiversity.
  - **[[Interagency Relations]]**: Raised concerns about communication and trust between agencies, advocating a resource-first (vs. bureaucracy-first) management philosophy.
- **Format**: The meeting used an open, participant-driven structure — beginning with the prompt "If you could do ANYTHING for native fish, what would it be?" — with no fixed time limits per topic, allowing flexible depth of discussion based on group interest.
- **Organizational Context**: Organized by [[Tom Cain]], Fisheries Biologist with the [[Coconino National Forest]], reflecting a Forest Service-led, multi-agency collaborative approach to native fish conservation in Arizona during the early 1990s.
- **Significance**: While lacking hard biological data, this document is valuable as a historical record of early 1990s interagency conservation planning efforts for Arizona's imperiled native fish fauna, illustrating the socio-institutional dimensions of species recovery efforts.
