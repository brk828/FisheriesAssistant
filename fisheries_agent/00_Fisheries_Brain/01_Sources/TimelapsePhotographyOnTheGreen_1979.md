## Executive Summary
This 1979 report by Richard P. Kramer of the Utah State Division of Wildlife Resources describes a novel field methodology for estimating recreational boat fishing pressure on the [[Green River]] below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]. Rather than presenting species-level ichthyological data, the paper documents the deployment of time-lapse Super 8 cameras to replace less reliable raft-launch expansion surveys. The technique, adapted from earlier work by Bob McDowell on the [[North Platte River]] in Wyoming, used three fixed camera stations along a tailrace river corridor to photographically count rafts and estimate angler/boater use of the fishery — a critical input for managing pressure on a heavily fished tailwater trout fishery.

## Background & Description
Not applicable — this study does not address fish morphology, taxonomy, or physical characteristics of any species. The "morphology" documented in this paper pertains instead to **equipment design**:

- **Camera system**: Kodak "Analyst" Super 8 movie camera with a 4:1 zoom lens, mounted in a waterproof case on a tree-mounted bracket.
- **Intervolometer**: A daylight-functional add-on unit extending the built-in time interval up to five minutes between frames; included a photosensitive switch to halt filming at night and resume at daylight, conserving film and battery life.
- **Power supply**: Camera used 4-AA alkaline batteries (up to one month life); intervolometer used 3-AA alkaline batteries (over one month life).
- **Frame rate**: Approximately one frame every two minutes, requiring weekly film changes.
- **Playback**: Bell and Howell QX95 movie projector used to review and count rafts from developed film.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study site is the [[Green River]] tailrace immediately below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]], a regulated cold-water river reach in the [[Colorado River Basin]] known for its tailwater trout fishery. Key habitat/site-selection criteria documented for camera placement — which double as indirect descriptions of the river's physical character — include:

- River sections wide and straight enough to keep rafts continuously in view, avoiding double-counting.
- Current velocity sufficient that even the fastest watercraft (kayaks) required at least two minutes to transit the monitored zone, ensuring capture on film.
- Flow strong enough to discourage rafters from loitering or making frequent shoreline stops, which could confound raft counts.
- Cameras positioned high on canyon walls on the **south side of the river** to minimize sun glare and reduce vandalism risk.

Three monitoring zones were established: 
1. Tailrace to [[Little Hole]]
2. [[Little Hole]] to [[Brown's Park]]
3. [[Swallow Canyon]] raft ramp within [[Brown's Park]] (launch/take-out point monitoring)

These zones collectively span the primary recreational and fishing pressure corridor of the regulated Green River tailwater, directly relevant to management of resident trout populations subject to angling pressure.

## Key Findings
- Time-lapse photography provided a more reliable alternative to traditional raft-launch expansion surveys for estimating boating/fishing pressure on regulated rivers.
- The technique builds on precedent methodology developed by Bob McDowell, who operated up to ten camera units on the [[North Platte River]] in Wyoming since 1975.
- Three camera stations were sufficient to characterize use patterns across the Green River tailrace, [[Little Hole]] reach, and [[Brown's Park]], including dedicated monitoring of raft launch/take-out activity at [[Swallow Canyon]].
- Photosensitive intervolometers significantly reduced operational costs by preventing unnecessary nighttime filming, conserving both film and battery consumption.
- Each camera required only weekly film changes at a two-minute frame interval, with 18 rolls of film used per camera across the full fishing season.
- **Cost data**: Film and processing totaled $92.52 per camera for the season; each camera-intervolometer unit cost $320.00 (excluding waterproof housing); the projector used for film review cost $216.00.
- This methodology represents an early application of automated remote photographic monitoring for freshwater fisheries pressure assessment, with direct implications for managing angling effort on tailwater fisheries such as the Green River below [[Flaming Gorge Dam]].
