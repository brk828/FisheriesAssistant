## Executive Summary
This document is a 1978 internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) authorizing the transfer of fifty young [[Razorback Sucker|razorback suckers]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) from Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery (NFH) to the State of California's Chino Fish and Wildlife Base. The stated purpose was to hold the fish for eventual release and/or use as future broodstock. Notably, the memo explicitly states that no endangered species permits were required at the time because the species had **not yet been federally listed** — a historically significant detail, as the razorback sucker was later listed as endangered under the U.S. Endangered Species Act (1991). This record functions as an early administrative data point in the conservation and captive propagation history of the species.

## Key Findings
- **Fifty juvenile razorback suckers** were authorized for transfer from **Willow Beach NFH** to **Chino Fish and Wildlife Base**, California, on the request of the State of California.
- The stated management purpose was twofold: (1) potential future **release** into the wild, and (2) establishment/support of a **captive broodstock program**.
- At the time (March 1978), *Xyrauchen texanus* was **not yet a federally listed endangered species**, so no Endangered Species Act permitting was necessary for the transfer — contrasting sharply with its current protected status.
- The memo demonstrates early interagency coordination (California Department of Fish and Game, USFWS Regional and Area Offices) in what would become a long-term, multi-decade effort to conserve the [[Razorback Sucker]] through hatchery propagation and interstate cooperation.
- This document serves primarily as a **historical/administrative record** rather than a source of primary biological or ecological data, but it is valuable for reconstructing the timeline of conservation actions preceding the species' formal endangered listing.
