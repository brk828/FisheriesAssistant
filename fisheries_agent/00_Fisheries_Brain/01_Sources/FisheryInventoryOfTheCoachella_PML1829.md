# Fishery Inventory of the Coachella Canal, Southeastern California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae #centrarchidae #canal-ecology

## Executive Summary

This final report, prepared by [[Minckley]] of the Arizona State University Department of Zoology for the Water and Power Resources Service, inventories the fish fauna and standing crops of the earthen upper 48.7 miles of the [[Coachella Canal]] in November 1980. Three sample sections were isolated with blocking nets and sampled by [[Rotenone Sampling]]. Sixteen fish species were recorded, all introduced to the region. [[Channel Catfish]] dominated numerically in most sections, and total standing crops were high relative to other low-desert and stream systems. The study was designed to inform fishery evaluation and to predict the effects of concrete lining on canal ecology. Objectives 1 through 5 (species composition, relative abundance, standing crop, whole-canal estimation, and age/growth and length-weight relations) are reported here; benthic invertebrate, macrophyte and nutrient objectives were deferred to a separate report.

## Background & Description

Water conveyance canals in the American Southwest extend habitat for aquatic and water-dependent organisms, yet their biology is poorly documented because canals are operated for delivery rather than fisheries. The authors note several sampling difficulties: high-velocity designs, infrequent de-watering, check and siphon structures, and dense aquatic plants that impede netting and seining.

The Coachella Canal was completed in 1948 and has operated continuously since. Its capacity declines from 70.8 m³/s (2,500 cfs) at the turnout from the [[All American Canal]] to 36.8 m³/s (1,300 cfs) about 137.8 km (86 miles) downflow. The upper 78 km was lined with concrete in 1980 under the [[Colorado River Salinity Control Project]]. Flow drains toward the [[Salton Sea]], a closed, saline basin.

Three sample sections were selected:
- **Section A:** a pool-like area below Check Drop 5 plus a straight reach; 403 m long, 20 m wide, 0.825 ha (2.04 acres) surface area, sand/silt substrate, and common reed along banks.
- **Section B:** a straight reach with no control structures; 650 m long, 20.5 m wide, 13,325 m² (3.22 acres), with sago pondweed and water milfoil on the sandy bottom.
- **Section C:** a double-barreled concrete box [[Inverted Siphon]] (Siphon No. 2), each barrel 165 m long and 3 m wide, total sampled area 1,023 m² (0.25 acre), with debris such as tires and bottles on the floor.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The canal fauna consisted entirely of introduced species that are mostly river-adapted or tolerant of slow, warm, turbid water. Threadfin shad concentrated below Check Drop 5, consistent with their known tendency to aggregate below control structures in the American Southwest. Channel and flathead catfish were most abundant in the dark, swift siphon, where [[Riverine Fishes]] would be expected. Sunfishes and black bass, which are poorly adapted to swift water, were scarce in the earthen channel.

Water depths during sampling were under 0.75 m except in the plunge pool below Check Drop 5 (about 3.5 m). Sampling in the siphon was hampered by darkness, so standing crop there is a minimum. Red shiners, which pass through 2.54 cm mesh, were consistently present. Poeciliids (mosquitofish and sailfin molly) occurred along shorelines but were not recovered.

The author anticipates that concrete lining will reduce spawning holes and crevices used by catfishes and reduce habitat for sunfishes, while the forage assemblage may persist if adequate food reaches the lined channel.

## Key Findings

**Species composition**
- Sixteen species were observed or collected. St. Amant et al. (1974) had taken 11 species, one of which (golden shiner) was not found in this study. Thirty species were known from waters supplying the canal.

**Relative abundance (Table II)**
- Channel catfish were about 90% of fishes in Sections B and C, and about 22% in Section A, where threadfin shad dominated (65.5% of Section A catch below Check Drop 5).
- Channel catfish density: 0.120/m² (A), 0.209/m² (B), 0.850/m² (siphon C).
- Flathead catfish: 4.5% of siphon catch, about 0.049/m² there; yellow bullheads occurred only in Section A.
- Bluegill were uniform across sections (0.13 to 0.32 individuals/m²).
- Carp comprised up to 5.1% of total fishes (0.003 to 0.056/m²); one goldfish was taken.
- Red shiners comprised 2.3 to 11.5% of total fishes (0.016 to 0.037/m²).

**Standing crop (Table III)**
- Section B supported the lowest standing crop, about 49 g/m² (437 lb/acre), which the authors judge close to actual biomass because second-day pickup was thorough.
- Section A reached about 102 g/m² (910 lb/acre), with over 74% from threadfin shad and carp concentrated below Check Drop 5.
- The siphon (Section C) had an over-estimated standing crop of about 594 g/m² (5,300 lb/acre), mainly large fishes.
- Extrapolating about 50 g/m² across the 156 surface hectares (385.5 acres) of the canal gives a minimum of roughly 78,000 kg (85 tons) of fishes, excluding fish in check drops, turnouts, inlets, and the six siphons.
- Game fishes (sensu St. Amant et al. 1974) made up about 66,000 kg (72 tons), or 84.3% of total fish.

**Size, age, and condition**
- Small individuals of most species indicated successful reproduction, especially in channel and flathead catfish, largemouth bass, and bluegill.
- Channel catfish showed strong modes at 26–28 cm and 32–34 cm total length in Section A, which differed from the unimodal 30–34 cm distribution in Sections B and C; the cause is unknown and may reflect stocking.
- Length–weight correlations were weak (r = 0.71 for channel catfish, n = 110; r = 0.65 for flathead catfish, n = 94), so no length–weight relationships or condition factors were computed.
- Qualitatively, all fishes were in good condition with substantial visceral fat, and no diseased fishes were seen.

**Comparison with other systems**
- St. Amant et al. (1974) estimated about 3,000 game fish per mile (0.09 fish/m²); the present data gave 0.015 and 0.229 fish/m² for Sections A and B and about 1.002 fish/m² in the siphon.
- Largemouth bass averaged 55.7 ± 9.6 (± 1 SE) per segment in the earlier survey.
- Standing crops of 49 to 102 g/m² fall near the upper range reported for low-desert Arizona streams (up to about 120.6 g/m² in Aravaipa Creek) and above most stream values from other regions (typically under 30 g/m²).

**Conclusions**
- The canal supports unusually large fish numbers and biomass, dominated by desirable sport and food species.
- Fishes were in good condition, and many were in a size range acceptable for sport and food.
- The author expects that concrete lining will favor river-adapted catfish and reduce sunfish and bass, while spawning habitat for catfish may decline.
