## Executive Summary
This document is **Information Letter No. 47** from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife, addressed to members of the **Lower Colorado River Drainage System Endangered Fishes Recovery Team**. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research data, the letter serves as an administrative planning communication concerning the establishment of **broodstock holding facilities** for the [[Colorado River Squawfish]] (now taxonomically referred to as [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]) and coordination on the **recovery plan** for the [[Razorback Sucker]]. Key logistical concerns include site selection for captive holding, water temperature suitability, food supply, and disease risk management. The letter also documents a personnel transition within the Recovery Team.

## Background & Description

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Limited ecological inferences can be drawn from the operational concerns raised in the letter:

- **Thermal Tolerance / Habitat Preference**: The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] was explicitly avoided as a holding site for squawfish broodstock due to its **low water temperature**, implying that [[Colorado River Squawfish]] require warmer water conditions than what this hatchery could provide — consistent with the species' native adaptation to warm, turbid, large-river conditions typical of the [[Lower Colorado River]].
- **Trophic Considerations**: Reference is made to existing (though limited) information on the **food/diet of squawfish**, indicating an established, if incomplete, understanding of feeding ecology relevant to captive rearing.
- **Disease Ecology**: The letter notes a **knowledge gap regarding disease** in squawfish populations, an important consideration for captive broodstock management and conservation risk assessment.
- **Distribution Context**: Field survey efforts are referenced in relation to the **Grand Canyon** region, suggesting this area was a focus of population survey work for these endangered fishes.

## Key Findings
- **Conservation Action**: Plans were underway to capture and establish a **captive broodstock population** of Colorado River squawfish, with fish expected to be collected by **late July to early August**.
- **Redundancy Planning**: The Recovery Team decided to split broodstock holding across **two separate facilities** to mitigate risk from potential accidents at a single location (a basic conservation risk-management strategy).
- **Site Exclusion**: The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] was excluded as a preferred holding site primarily due to unsuitably **low water temperatures** for this warmwater species.
- **Knowledge Gaps Identified**: 
  - Existing information on squawfish **food/diet** was available but incomplete.
  - **No information on disease** in squawfish was known to exist, flagging a critical research need for successful captive management.
- **Recovery Planning Documents**: A **first draft of the Colorado River Squawfish Recovery Plan** was anticipated for review at the upcoming meeting, alongside detailed discussion of the **Razorback Sucker Recovery Plan**.
- **Survey Standardization**: Efforts were underway to finalize standardized **survey forms** for field data collection, coordinated through Jim St. Amant, with Recovery Team member feedback being incorporated.
- **Personnel Change**: **Tom Robinson** (Arizona Game and Fish Department, regional fisheries specialist, Yuma) replaced **Joe Stone** on the Recovery Team, bringing hatchery propagation experience relevant to future fish culture components of the recovery plan. Joe Stone transferred to Pinetop but retained involvement in **Grand Canyon survey work**.
