# Russian-English Glossary of Names of Aquatic Organisms and Other Biological and Related Terms

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-glossary

## Executive Summary

This is Circular No. 65 of the [[Fisheries Research Board of Canada]] (Biological Station, Nanaimo, B.C.), compiled by W. E. Ricker in August 1962. It is a bilingual reference list intended to help translators render Russian scientific articles on aquatic biology, ichthyology and fisheries into English. The glossary covers fish and other aquatic animals, plus limnological, oceanographic, osteological, physiological, disease and fishing-gear terms. Entries run alphabetically in Cyrillic, with Russian headword, English equivalent and, where relevant, Latin binomial. The coverage is weighted toward larger food and commercial fishes of the USSR, and the note is a lexicon rather than a research study.

## Background & Description

**Scope and purpose.** The foreword states that the glossary is meant "to be of assistance in translating scientific articles in the fields of aquatic biology and the study of fishes and fisheries." Definitions were drawn from many sources, principally the text volume of [[Commercial Fishes of the USSR]] and Berg's [[Freshwater Fishes of the USSR]], along with works by Nikolsky, Galkin, Borisov and Ovsiannikov, and Martinsen.

**Conventions.**
- Recognised English names are given first where a North American standard exists, following Bailey et al. (1960), a list of common and scientific names of fishes from the United States and Canada.
- Unrecognised names are given as transliterations or translations in quotation marks.
- Scientific names are included for identification, but are not standardised, so one species may appear under several names.
- Stress marks were added only where local pronunciation could be determined.

**Contributors.** Dr. R. E. Foerster (Nanaimo), Dr. D. E. Bevan (University of Washington), Mr. D. E. McAllister (National Museum of Canada, osteology), Dr. V. Loosanoff (US Fish and Wildlife Service), and Mr. L. S. Bogdanov (Institute of Oceanology, USSR Academy of Sciences) contributed definitions or corrections. Mr. B. Goreloff typed an earlier Russian text.

**Structure.** The body is an alphabetical Russian–English list of roughly 1,000 pages-worth of entries spanning pages 8 to 166 of the circular, followed by an addendum (pages 161 to 173) of supplementary entries. The front matter contains a foreword and a reference list of about 40 works, including [[Glossary of Marine Conservation Terms]] (Galtsoff 1956) and [[Ricker Handbook of Fish Population Statistics]] (Ricker 1958).

**Usage notes.** The compiler stresses that equivalents may mislead because English and Russian usage are not fully established. Terms in fish population biology follow Ricker (1958), and some fishing-gear terms follow Dolbish (1958).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The glossary spans several subject areas relevant to aquatic ecosystems:

- **Fish and fisheries biology:** stock, recruitment, year-class, mortality and fishing-effort terms, plus catch-per-effort concepts. Key entries include the Russian terms for stock (сток/стадо), year-class (поколение), natural and fishing mortality coefficients, and "catchability" (уловистость).
- **Life history and ecology:** spawning migrations of anadromous fishes (for example, "winter salmon" and "summer salmon" of the hiemal and vernal types), feeding and growing periods, benthos and pelagic zones, and prey and food-chain terms.
- **Limnology and hydrology:** river, lake and reservoir features such as riffles, backwaters, oxbows, bays and reaches, plus the "Birge's theory" entry and Ricker's stock-related terms.
- **Non-fish aquatic fauna:** invertebrates (crustaceans, molluscs, annelids, cnidarians, echinoderms), marine mammals, sea birds and water birds (largely from *Ptitsy SSSR*), and aquatic plants and algae.
- **Fish diseases and parasites:** a substantial group of entries covers bacterial, fungal, protozoan, trematode, cestode, nematode and copepod infections, for example *Ichthyophthirius* (ichthyophthiriasis), *Gyrodactylus* (gyrodactylosis) and *Costia* (costiasis).
- **Oceanography and fishing technology:** gear types (trawls, seines, gill nets, traps, weirs), vessel terms, and marine-dictionary material.

The circular makes no ecosystem-wide measurements. Its ecological content is definitional.

## Key Findings

This document is a glossary and contains no original data, measurements or sample sizes. Its main outputs are:

1. **Scope.** About 1,000 headword entries are organised alphabetically in Cyrillic with English equivalents, scientific names, and brief definitions. Coverage is strongest for [[Sturgeon]], [[Salmon]], [[Herring]], [[Cyprinid Fishes]], gobies and sculpins, and [[Sharks]] and [[Rays]].
2. **Naming conventions.** English names follow Bailey et al. (1960) where a North American name exists. Unrecognised names are quoted, and the compiler warns that equivalents are provisional.
3. **Anadromous salmon terminology.** Atlantic salmon are classified by run type: grilse (тиина/тинка), "winter salmon" (зимняя семга, hiemal type), "summer salmon" (летняя семга, vernal type), and "autumn salmon" (осенняя семга). Spawning-related terms (kelt, smolt, parr) are also defined.
4. **Population dynamics terms.** Key entries include stock, year-class, recruitment (пополнение), replacement level (уровень воспроизводства), instantaneous rates of natural mortality, total mortality and fishing mortality, and the catchability coefficient.
5. **Morphological and osteological terms.** Standard fish anatomy terms are provided: fin rays, fin types, scale types (cycloid, ctenoid, placoid, ganoid), bones of the skull and gill apparatus, and length measurements (fork length, standard length, snout-to-vent length).
6. **Disease terms.** Over 60 parasitic and bacterial disease entries are given, including *Myxobolus* infections, *Dactylogyrus* and *Gyrodactylus* monogenean diseases, and *Ichthyophthirius* (white spot).
7. **Source credibility.** The compiler discloses that the list is incomplete. The circular says "large gaps will be evident" for non-fish aquatic animals and plants, and that Russian names of many species came from single sources.
8. **Addendum.** Pages 161 to 173 add roughly 300 further entries, chiefly sculpins (Cottidae), gobies, lumpsuckers, snailfishes, greenlings, and eels, with additional fish and invertebrate names.
