# Lake Mohave Fishery Job Progress Report, 1979

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Nevada Department of Wildlife job progress report (Project F20-15, Job 301, Statewide Fisheries Program) covers the [[Lake Mohave]] sport fishery from January 1 through December 31, 1979. Creel surveys recorded lower angling pressure than in 1978, with fewer anglers, fishing hours and fish checked. Stocked trout poundage dropped by about 22 percent, and [[Largemouth Bass]] nesting was disrupted by falling lake levels. The report recommends continued trout stocking at current levels, further bass population study, and monitoring of bass tournaments.

## Background & Description

- [[Davis Dam]] on the [[Lower Colorado River]] was built from 1942 and completed in 1951. [[Lake Mohave]] reached the tailrace of [[Hoover Dam]] in May 1951.
- The reservoir was created to meet irrigation needs, regulate erratic releases from Hoover Dam, and produce hydroelectric power. As a result, water levels and exchange flow fluctuate widely. The upper reach (Black Canyon) alternates between lotic and lentic conditions.
- Impoundment modified the original trout fishery below Hoover Dam and created a second-story fishery of warmwater gamefish.
- Management objectives: develop sport-fishing information; advance the premium trout fishery and minimize the [[Put-and-Take Fishery]] aspect; determine the impact of increased use on the largemouth bass population; and maintain the quality of the warmwater fishery.
- Four jobs were run: Job 301-1 ([[Creel Survey]]), Job 301-2 ([[Fish Marking Study]]), Job 301-3 (General Research, including bass transect and forage studies), and related work with the [[Lake Mead Fish Hatchery]] (Job 304-4).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water fluctuation:** Lake levels dropped 3.1 feet (0.94 m) between April 5 and April 13, 1979. Sixteen bass nests were examined on April 5, and none could be found on April 13. Nesting success was rated only fair.
- **Habitat:** Rising water later flooded shoreline vegeetation, and pondweed and spiny naiad gave cover for bass fry and fingerlings. Class 0 bass survival was rated fair. Average transect depth was 5.9 feet (1.8 m), ranging from 2 to 15 feet.
- **Food web:** A 1978 analysis of bass fry and fingerlings from Lake Mohave and Lake Mead found that crustaceans were the most important food for fish under 50 mm total length. Chironomid insects dominated the diet of fish over 50 mm. Fish were rare as food items but made up most of the food volume when present.
- **Forage and temperature:** Water temperature and forage organism abundance were measured to correlate with trout stocking recommendations and to identify desirable forage species for the riparian sections.
- **Pressures on angling:** The report cites the closure of the [[Willow Beach]] campground in July 1979 (34 percent drop in campground day use), increased angling on Lake Mead, and energy shortages and inflation.

## Key Findings

**Creel survey results (82 survey days, three boat landings: Willow Beach, Cottonwood Cove, Katherine Landing)**
- Anglers contacted: 41,339, with 61,475 fish caught.
- Catch rate: 1.49 fish per angler and 0.3x fish per hour (the hour total is partly illegible in the scan).
- Compared with 1978: creel survey days down 25 percent, anglers checked down 40 percent, fishing hours down 42 percent, fish checked down 54 percent, fish per angler down 24 percent, fish per hour down 21 percent.
- Likely causes: the departure of the creel clerk in September 1979, reduced rainbow stocking from Lake Mead Hatchery, campground closure, increased Lake Mead angling, and energy costs.
- A ten percent angling questionnaire showed a 5 percent rise in expanded angler days for 1978 compared with 1977.

**Catch composition and angler preference**
- Trout share of catch fell, which the report attributes to a 22 percent reduction in stocked trout poundage.
- Carp share rose because of a one-day spear fishing contest at Willow Beach in 1979, where 793 carp were taken.
- Angler preference changed only at Cottonwood Cove.

**Trout stocking (Job 301-1 and 301-2)**
- Lake Mead Hatchery (Nevada): 331,634 lb rainbow trout (a 62 percent decrease) and 81,361 lb cutthroat trout, totaling 411,995 lb (a 55 percent decrease).
- [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]: 1,141,085 lb rainbow trout (a 7 percent increase over 1978).
- Combined stocking: about 1.55 million lb, a 22 percent decrease.
- Harvest at Willow Beach: 21,794 rainbow trout taken by anglers during 34 creel days (1.45 trout per angler). In 1978, 6,564 trout were taken over 46 days (1.96 per angler). This is a 57 percent drop.
- Average rainbow length checked: 11.1 inches, up from 10.7 inches (27.1 cm) in 1978.
- Return rate for stocked rainbow (Nevada data only): 27 percent by number. The weight-based figure is illegible in the scan. The report notes that Arizona harvest data are not included, so true returns are probably higher.

**Largemouth bass and fishery monitoring**
- Lakewide: 926 bass anglers contacted, 1,527 bass caught in 4,925.25 hours (1.65 bass per angler, 0.31 per hour).
- Cottonwood Cove (41 days): 213 anglers, 1.71 bass per angler, 0.32 per hour.
- Willow Beach (34 days): 93 anglers, 126 bass, 0.21 bass per hour.
- Katherine Landing (7 days): 130 anglers, 182 bass, 1.40 per angler, 0.34 per hour.
- Average bass length: 14.0 inches (35.6 cm), up from 13.5 inches (34.3 cm) in 1978. Age classes I and II made up 21 percent of the harvest and class III made up 61 percent (in 1978, class III was 49 percent).
- Bass club tournaments (10 contest days): 160 contestants caught 391 largemouth bass, 2.44 per angler.

**Fish marking study (Job 301-2)**
- About 10,000 cutthroat trout were clipped and released at Mile 50 in February 1979. None were recaptured during the year.
- Seventeen marked rainbow and cutthroat trout from earlier plants were examined, plus two brook trout.
- A 1976 cutthroat release reached 26 inches and 6 pounds 9 ounces. The Mile 15 cutthroat release from September 1977 contributed little to the downlake fishery, and its growth (0.64 inch over 24 months) lagged behind other Cottonwood Basin plants (0.75 inch for the March 1976 release).
- The January 1978 [[Hoover Dam]] platform release of rainbow trout gave excellent early returns and contributed much to 1978 harvest at Willow Beach. The report notes that platform stocking gives quick returns but adds little to a trophy fishery.

**Bass research (Job 301-3)**
- Bass transect surveyed March 5 to September, 12 days in total. Nesting was observed from March 26 to May 24.
- Fry and fingerling collection for the Lake Mead study yielded 179 bass (143 fry and 36 fingerlings) from four sites: Study Cove (103 bass), Mile 51 (25), Mile 52 (49), and Mile 57 (2). Stomach analysis is under contract with the [[University of Nevada, Las Vegas]].
- Gill netting (four sets, Mile 22 to Mile 26, 12 hours, August 31, 1979) produced carp, rainbow trout, largemouth bass, channel catfish and threadfin shad (Table 4).

**Recommendations**
- Continue cooperation with [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] on stocking rate, area and season for 1980.
- Continue stocking [[Nevada Source Trout]] at present levels.
- Continue efforts to obtain quality [[Eastern Brook Trout]] for experimental stocking, and continue experimental stocking of [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]].
- Introduce aquatic insects (Trichoptera, Ephemeroptera and Plecoptera) into the canyon areas of Lake Mohave, using stock from the Colorado River drainage.
- Continue bass studies to determine causes of population fluctuation.
- Monitor [[Bass Tournaments]] when possible to assess their impact on the resource.

Prepared by Backstrand, Game Agent II, February 29, 1980.
