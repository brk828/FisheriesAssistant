# Scaphirhynchus suttkusi, a New Sturgeon from the Mobile Basin of Alabama and Mississippi

#ichthyology #marine-biology #acipenseridae

## Executive Summary

This 1991 taxonomic paper by [[James D. Williams]] and [[Glenn H. Clemmer]] formally describes *Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*, the Alabama Sturgeon, from the [[Mobile Basin]] of Alabama and Mississippi. The species is morphologically closest to the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*S. platorynchus*) and differs from it in several plate and fin ray counts and head and fin-placement measurements. Its populations have declined because of habitat loss from impoundments and dredging. The authors argue for urgent conservation action and propose a biogeographic explanation for its disjunct distribution shared with other fish species pairs.

## Background & Description

- **Discovery:** The first Mobile Basin *Scaphirhynchus* record was an 1880 Alabama River specimen at the Smithsonian (USNM 43675), originally catalogued as *Acipenser*. The first published record was [[Chermock 1955]], who identified a Tombigbee River specimen as *S. platorynchus*. The present study rests on 32 specimens from the Mobile Bay drainage.
- **Holotype:** TU 135000, adult male, 552 mm standard length (SL), collected 18 March 1977 from an overflow pool of the Alabama River at Dixie Landing, Monroe County, Alabama.
- **Paratypes:** Numerous lots from the Alabama, Cahaba, Coosa and Tombigbee river systems, with collections dated 1953 to 1985.
- **Diagnosis:** Distinguished from *S. platorynchus* by a combination of characters, including a larger eye (head-width ratio 6.5 to 8.2, usually 7.0 to 8.0, versus 7.7 to 14.4 in *S. platorynchus*), dorsal fin rays 29 to 34 (usually 30 to 31 versus 30 to 36, usually 33 to 34), and more posterior dorsal and anal fin placement.
- **Size:** Attains about 720 mm SL. Maximum size of both *S. suttkusi* and *S. platorynchus* rarely exceeds 80 cm fork length and 2.3 kg, whereas [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*S. albus*) reaches 168 cm and 28 kg.
- **Coloration:** Light brown to tan dorsally, cream ventrally, with a light olivaceous wash in life.
- **Etymology:** Named for [[Royal D. Suttkus]] of Tulane University. The proposed common name is Alabama sturgeon.
- **Methods:** Plate and fin ray counts and measurements follow [[Bailey and Cross 1954]]. Ten meristic and 36 mensural characters were examined; 6 meristic and 14 mensural variables were significant and used in principal components analysis. Data were drawn from 27 *S. suttkusi* and 45 *S. platorynchus*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat and feeding.** The species inhabits the main channels of large Coastal Plain rivers below the [[Fall Line]]. Stomach contents of four individuals contained larval aquatic insects (most abundant by volume and number), oligochaetes and mollusc shell fragments. Fishermen catch it on trot lines with bait such as poultry parts and fish, which suggests scavenging.

**Reproduction.** Gravid females were collected from late March through early May. Eggs measured 2.4 to 3.0 mm in diameter in spring females. A 720 mm SL female collected in November 1953 had smaller eggs (1.8 to 2.1 mm). Twelve individuals, including several gravid females, were captured in one gill net set at the mouth of the Cahaba River on 21 March 1969, suggesting directional spawning movements.

**Habitat loss.** Mainstream impoundments for navigation and channel dredging for barge traffic converted riverine habitat into reservoirs. [[Burke and Ramsey 1985]] found about 40% of historic habitat relatively undisturbed, but only 15% of suitable habitat supported a strong population.

**Biogeography.** Four fish species (*S. platorynchus*, *Notropis blennius*, *N. shumardi*, *N. wickliffi*) and five others share a disjunct pattern: they occur in the Mississippi and Mobile basins but are absent from intervening Gulf drainages. Three explanations are considered: a past connection between the Mississippi and Mobile rivers, continuous ancestral distribution followed by extinction in intervening drainages, and the existence of the ancient [[Appalachian River]].

**Conservation status.** Populations of all three *Scaphirhynchus* species declined over the past 50 to 75 years. *S. albus* is federally listed as endangered. *S. suttkusi* was listed as endangered by the [[American Fisheries Society]] and was under review by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. *S. platorynchus* has been extirpated or assigned a conservation status in 8 of the 24 states where it occurs.

## Key Findings

- **New species confirmed:** *S. suttkusi* differs significantly from *S. platorynchus* in several plate and fin ray counts and in head and fin-placement measurements.
- **Meristic differences:** Dorsal plates were significantly higher in *S. suttkusi* (mean 17.2 versus 16.2). Dorsal fin rays (mean 30.8 versus 33.1) and anal fin rays (mean 19.9 versus 21.2) were also significantly different. Plates between the anus and anal fin origin (mean 5.0 versus 4.4) and plates posterior to the anal fin (mean 7.8 versus 8.7) reflect the more posterior anal fin placement of *S. suttkusi*.
- **Mensural differences:** *S. suttkusi* has a larger orbit and a more posterior dorsal and anal fin placement. Caudal peduncle dimensions are smaller, and dorsal, lower caudal lobe and pectoral fins are proportionally shorter.
- **Multivariate analysis:** Principal components I and II explained 64% of meristic variance. For mensural variables, components I and III explained 67%.
- **Distribution:** The species is endemic to the Mobile Basin below the Fall Line. Museum specimens come from the Coosa, Cahaba, Alabama and upper Tombigbee rivers. Commercial and biologist records extend to the Black Warrior, lower Tombigbee, Mobile and Tallapoosa rivers. One questionable Mississippi record exists from the Tombigbee River near West Point.
- **Cline in *S. platorynchus*:** Northern populations had lower plate counts and higher pectoral and pelvic fin ray counts than southern populations. Southern *S. platorynchus* did not differ from *S. suttkusi* in pectoral or pelvic rays.
- **Conservation warning:** The authors state that without a major conservation effort within the next decade, both [[Pallid Sturgeon]] and *S. suttkusi* risk being lost.
