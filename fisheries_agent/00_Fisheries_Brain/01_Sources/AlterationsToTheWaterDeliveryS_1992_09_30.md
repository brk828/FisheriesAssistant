## Executive Summary
This document is a 1992 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) memorandum responding to a Bureau of Land Management (BLM) request regarding modifications to the water delivery system at Howard Well, Graham County, Arizona. The action reroutes livestock water supply from Howard Well to a separate source (Martin Well), thereby increasing water flow into a pond inhabited by the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*). The Service concurred that the change would have a **beneficial effect** on the species by eliminating water depletion from Howard Well. The memo does not address vegetation management (e.g., cattail encroachment) but notes its relevance to long-term habitat monitoring.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Isolated desert well/spring-fed pond system, characteristic of the fragmented, relict aquatic habitats occupied by [[Desert Pupfish]] populations in the arid Southwestern United States.
- **Hydrology:** The pond supporting the pupfish population is sustained by discharge from Howard Well, estimated at **7 gallons per minute (GPM)**.
- **Water source separation:** The project explicitly prevents hydrological connection between Howard Well and Martin Well to avoid mixing water sources — Martin Well water will instead be diverted via buried pipeline solely to livestock watering sites, disconnected from the Howard Well pipeline network (which will be capped).
- **Vegetation dynamics:** The pond contains cattails and other aquatic/emergent vegetation. While not managed under this specific action, vegetation encroachment is recognized as a potential future concern for habitat quality and will require separate justification and monitoring for control efforts.
- **Threats/pressures:** Historic depletion of the pond's water supply due to diversion for livestock use represented a stressor on available aquatic habitat volume for the pupfish population.
- **Land management context:** The habitat lies within BLM-administered land in [[Graham County, Arizona]], situated within a broader landscape used for livestock grazing operations, indicating overlapping land-use and conservation stakeholder interests (BLM, USFWS, Arizona Game and Fish Department).

## Key Findings
- The BLM proposed eliminating depletion from **Howard Well** by redirecting livestock water supply to **Martin Well**, allowing the full ~7 GPM flow from Howard Well to enter the pond housing the endangered [[Desert Pupfish]].
- The USFWS **concurred with a "beneficial effect" finding** under the Endangered Species Act consultation process for this project.
- No direct connection between Howard Well and Martin Well water systems will be permitted, to prevent potential water quality mixing effects; the old Howard Well–livestock pipeline connection will be disconnected and capped.
- Management of cattails and other emergent vegetation in the pond is explicitly **outside the scope** of this action, though it is acknowledged as an ongoing habitat consideration.
- **Continued monitoring** of the water delivery system and pond conditions was emphasized as important both for verifying the beneficial effect of this action and for supporting future vegetation control decisions.
- This action exemplifies a small-scale, site-specific water management intervention aimed at habitat conservation for a critically endangered fish species in an arid, spring-fed ecosystem — reflecting broader challenges of managing water rights and endangered species habitat in the [[Sonoran Desert]] region.
