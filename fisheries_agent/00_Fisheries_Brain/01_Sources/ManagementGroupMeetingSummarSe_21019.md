# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program Management Group Meeting Summary - September 13, 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species #river-management #conservation

## Executive Summary

This document is a meeting summary from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] Management Group meeting held September 13, 1989, in Lakewood, Colorado. The meeting addressed recovery efforts for rare and endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River Basin]], focusing on water rights acquisition, biological opinions, instream flow studies, and coordination among federal and state agencies. Key discussions included releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]], the [[Yampa River]] habitat study, multiple biological opinions for water development projects, and strategies for protecting instream flows for endangered fish recovery.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] is a collaborative effort involving the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), Bureau of Reclamation, state agencies from Colorado, Wyoming, and Utah, water developers, environmental groups, and [[The Nature Conservancy]]. The program aims to recover rare and endangered fish species in the Colorado River Basin while allowing water development to proceed. The Management Group provides oversight and decision-making for program implementation.

The meeting was attended by representatives from multiple agencies and stakeholder groups including FWS Region 6, state water conservation boards, Bureau of Reclamation, Western Area Power Administration (WAPA), water development interests, and environmental organizations. John Hamill served as Program Director.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The recovery program addresses habitat needs and streamflow requirements for rare and endangered fishes in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Specific river reaches under consideration include:

- The **15-mile reach** of the [[Colorado River]], where releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] were being made to supplement flows
- The [[Yampa River]], where Phase I habitat use and streamflow needs assessment had been completed
- The [[Little Snake River]] in Colorado, where instream flow protection was being considered for releases from Sandstone Reservoir

The program recognizes limiting factors and threats to endangered fish populations by species and river reach, requiring coordinated recovery actions including research, studies, and management interventions. Water availability, sediment dynamics, and nonnative fish stocking were identified as key ecological concerns requiring attention.

Instream flow studies were being conducted to develop consistent basinwide methodology for determining flow requirements. The program emphasized coordination between researchers (Tyus and Kaeding mentioned specifically) to ensure consistent approaches.

## Key Findings

**Water Management Actions:**
- Releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] to supplement flows in the 15-mile reach were proceeding without adverse public comment
- Technical issues regarding the proposed lease/agreement for protecting Ruedi releases were being resolved, with concerns about using [[Green Mountain Reservoir]] for substitute releases
- A public negotiating session for the Ruedi lease was scheduled for October 1989

**Biological Opinions and Consultations:**
- [[Muddy Creek]] Biological Opinion was on hold pending River District comments
- Sandstone Biological Opinion meeting scheduled for October 2, 1989, between Wyoming and FWS to address Wyoming's concerns
- Price-San Rafael Biological Opinion draft scheduled for September 15, 1989, with provisions for crediting accretions from the [[Uintah Basin Unit]]
- FWS to review nonnative fish stocking proposals in coordination with Utah and Wyoming

**Research and Planning:**
- Phase I report "Habitat Use and Streamflow Needs of Rare and Endangered Fishes, Yampa River, Colorado" was distributed
- Phase II products (interim flow recommendations) scheduled for October 1, 1989
- Sediment Ad Hoc Work Group meeting scheduled for September 25, 1989, to develop discussion paper
- FWS to develop 5-year "road maps" for each recovery element, focusing on identifying limiting factors/threats by species and river reach

**Water Rights Acquisition:**
- Cross Mountain Ranch water rights acquisition approved by Management Group; [[Bureau of Land Management]] preparing letter of intent to purchase from [[The Nature Conservancy]]
- Hinman Park conditional water rights on [[Elk River]] (Yampa River Basin) offered as donation by Public Service Company
- [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] evaluating senior water rights in Yampa River near Maybell (Roberts Ditch)
- Water availability study needed for 15-mile reach before instream flow appropriation

**Infrastructure:**
- Gage installation approved for 15-mile reach at site immediately below [[Orchard Mesa Power Plant]]
- Kenney Reservoir project funding shortfall ($18,800 Colorado share) to be evaluated for Section 7 funding

**Program Coordination:**
- Subcommittee formed to explore expediting water rights acquisition and instream flow protection (chair: Tom Pitts)
- Bennett and Hamill to meet with key researchers to improve work plan process
- Guidance for FY-89 annual project reports to be distributed
- [[Yampa Basin Feasibility Study]] scope of work and contract under development
- Next Management Group meeting scheduled for November 9, 1989
