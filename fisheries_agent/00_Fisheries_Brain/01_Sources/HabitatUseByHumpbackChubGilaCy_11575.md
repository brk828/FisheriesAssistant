# Habitat Use by Humpback Chub in the Little Colorado River and Other Tributaries of the Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #habitat-study #conservation-proposal

## Executive Summary

This technical proposal, submitted by the [[Pinetop Fishery Assistance Office]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] in July 1990, outlines a comprehensive multi-year research program to quantify habitat use by the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Little Colorado River]] and other tributaries of the [[Colorado River]]. The [[Humpback Chub]] was described in 1946 and has experienced dramatic range contraction, now restricted to isolated canyon populations. The species is listed as endangered, with the [[Little Colorado River]] population representing the primary spawning aggregation for the lower [[Colorado River]]. The proposal addresses Conservation Measures requiring research on [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operational impacts and establishment of a second spawning aggregation. The study proposes detailed microhabitat and macrohabitat assessments across multiple tributaries, streamflow analysis, and evaluation of dam operations effects on critical habitats over a five-year period (1990-1994) with a total budget of approximately $1.78 million.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) was originally described from a single specimen of unknown location in [[Grand Canyon]] (Miller 1946). Archaeological evidence from remains in Indian ruins near [[Hoover Dam]] confirms the species as a long-term resident of the [[Colorado River]] system (Miller 1955). Historically, [[Humpback Chub]] reached greatest abundance in inaccessible canyon areas of the mainstem [[Colorado River]] and the [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[White River]], and [[Little Colorado River]] (Smith 1960, Sigler and Miller 1963, Holden and Stalnaker 1970, 1975, Vanicek et al. 1970).

Within its native range, the species is now severely restricted to: the [[Green River]] in Desolation, Gray and Labyrinth Canyons and [[Dinosaur National Monument]]; the [[Yampa River]] in [[Dinosaur National Monument]]; the [[Colorado River]] in Black Rocks, Westwater, and De Beque Canyons; Marble and Grand Canyons; and the lower 13 km of the [[Little Colorado River]]. This dramatic reduction in distribution and population density led to endangered species designation.

Available habitat use information primarily concerns juveniles and adults captured during April through October (Valdez et al. 1987). Adult [[Humpback Chub]] have been reported to associate with fast current and/or deep channels (Holden and Stalnaker 1975, Kidd 1977, Seethaler et al. 1979). However, Valdez et al. (1982) and Fish and Wildlife Service (1986) reported preferred habitat as waters less than 9.1 m deep, over silt, sand, boulder or bedrock substrates, at water velocities less than 30 cm/s. In the [[Little Colorado River]], Minckley et al. (1981) reported the species occupied diverse habitats including pools adjacent to eddies, large pools with little or no current, and areas below travertine dams.

Previous research concentrated on locating and describing population extent; limited information exists on life history and ecology. In the lower [[Colorado River]], most or all successful spawning occurs in the [[Little Colorado River]] (Kaeding and Zimmerman 1983, Minckley 1987). Continued survival of lower river populations appears contingent upon this population's persistence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Little Colorado River]] system represents critical habitat for [[Humpback Chub]] reproduction and recruitment in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. The proposed study area extends from the [[Colorado River]] confluence upstream 21 river kilometers to [[Blue Springs]]. Additional tributaries proposed for study include [[Paria River]], [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Tapeats Creek]], [[Deer Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]].

[[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations control the [[Colorado River]] environment in [[Grand Canyon]], potentially affecting tributary confluence zones, water quality parameters (temperature, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity), and habitat availability. The dam's experimental flow releases (scheduled through July 1991) provide opportunities to quantify these impacts on [[Humpback Chub]] habitat.

The study recognizes that tributary-mainstem interfaces represent discontinuities in the stream continuum (Gorman 1986), complicating habitat-fish relationships. Water chemistry, substrate complexity, channel morphology, and underlying geologic strata all influence habitat suitability. The [[Little Colorado River]] exhibits variable discharge patterns influenced by snowmelt and summer monsoon precipitation, creating dynamic habitat conditions.

Native fish assemblages in these systems also include other cyprinids and potentially introduced species, requiring multi-species habitat assessments to understand community dynamics and competitive interactions affecting [[Humpback Chub]].

## Key Findings

**Study Objectives:**

1) Identify and quantify preferred habitats of juvenile and adult [[Humpback Chub]] and other fish species in the [[Little Colorado River]]

2) Identify and quantify seasonal habitat use patterns of juvenile and adult [[Humpback Chub]] and other fish species in the [[Little Colorado River]]

3) Identify and quantify [[Humpback Chub]] spawning habitat in the [[Little Colorado River]]

4) Identify potential [[Humpback Chub]] habitats within various [[Colorado River]] tributaries and evaluate suitability for recovery efforts

5) Develop discharge-frequency and flow-duration curves at locations of interest in the [[Little Colorado River]] to determine flood stage effects on [[Humpback Chub]] habitats

6) Identify information and future research required for possible enhancement of environmental conditions to protect and promote fish and wildlife populations in the [[Little Colorado River]] and other tributaries

**Hypotheses to be Tested:**

- H₁: Juvenile and adult [[Humpback Chub]] are uniformly distributed throughout all available habitats
- H₂: Habitat use patterns of juvenile and adult [[Humpback Chub]] do not vary seasonally
- H₃: Habitat use patterns among differing age classes of [[Humpback Chub]] do not vary from uniform
- H₄: Habitat availability and consequent habitat use does not vary over a continuum of flow volumes

**Methodology:**

**Microhabitat Assessment:** Characterization conducted concomitantly with seasonal fish sampling (20-day trips). The [[Little Colorado River]] sampling area subdivided into three 7-kilometer subreaches with four randomly selected 1-kilometer segments per subreach each season. Multiple sampling gears employed:

- **Trammel nets:** Placed perpendicular to flow, subdivided into 1-m subsections. Measurements include water velocity, depth, substrate complexity, temperature profiles, turbidity, dissolved oxygen, pH, and conductivity at 1-m intervals. Fish data includes total length, standard length, biomass, depth and orientation in water column. Habitat availability quantified along transects 15 m apart.

- **Seines:** Used in ephemeral backwaters, shoreline runs, side channels, eddies, and slackwater areas. Point measurements at 1-m intervals along transects spaced 3-5 m apart. Water quality sampled at two points per area.

- **Hoop nets:** Habitat quantified via five point measurements at 1-m intervals along transects perpendicular to net axes, with transects at 5-m intervals along lead nets.

- **Minnow traps:** For larvae/fry collection. Measurements along X-Y coordinate axes with 2-m length, points at 0.5-m intervals (total 4 m² area per trap).

- **In situ observations:** Direct underwater observation when conditions permit, particularly for spawning habitat determination. Measurements along X-Y coordinate plane centered on fish location.

**Macrohabitat Assessment:** Initial quantification along 22 permanent transects at 1-km intervals from confluence to river km 21 during low flow. Measurements of water velocity (at 0.6 depth using Marsh McBirney current meter), depth (metric wading rod), and substrate (Modified Wentworth Particle Size Scale) at 1-m intervals. Each point represents average for 1000 m². Additional transects placed where geologic strata or channel morphology changes between successive transects.

Confluence zone impacts from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] experimental releases investigated along permanent transects at 50-100 m intervals extending 1200 m upstream, measured before, during, and after releases and seasonally at different mainchannel flows.

Tributary surveys use analogous methods with permanent transects at 1-km intervals from confluence to areas precluding fish passage, with additional transects where geology or morphology changes.

**Streamflow Analysis:** Development of discharge-frequency and flow-duration curves for the [[Little Colorado River]] system. If 50+ years of streamflow records available, direct frequency analysis conducted. For shorter periods, statistical analysis of precipitation data assesses representativeness. If data not representative, stochastic models generate synthetic streamflow sequences using either precipitation-based transfer functions or direct daily runoff models.

Water surface profiles computed using HEC-2 Water Surface Profiles Program (U.S. Army Corps of Engineers) for one-dimensional steady, gradually varied flow in natural channels. Flow-duration curves and equal velocity curves developed for each macrohabitat cross-section to delineate potential fish habitats.

**Statistical Analysis:** Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests compare frequency distributions of depth, velocity, and substrate at fish capture locations versus availability to determine proportional use. Preferred ranges compared among age classes and seasons using ANOVA with contrast analysis (for depth) and Kruskal-Wallis tests with Dunn's procedure (for substrate and velocity).

Principal Components Analysis with varimax rotation evaluates habitat attributes at presence/absence locations. Stepwise discriminant function analysis determines variables distinguishing presence from absence, with resultant function used to predictively classify tributary areas for habitat suitability.

Resolved patterns compared to existing upper [[Colorado River]] basin habitat information and used to test habitat suitability index (HSI) models developed for upper basin populations (Valdez et al. 1987).

**Project Timeline (Milestones):**

- **1990:** Project personnel identified, base camps established, reconnaissance visits completed, detailed sampling regime formulated
- **1991:** Preliminary models for seasonal [[Humpback Chub]] habitats, spawning habitat quantified, [[Little Colorado River]] habitat availability quantified, experimental release impacts assessed, subreach sampling areas redefined
- **1992:** Refinement of [[Little Colorado River]] seasonal and age-specific models through continued data acquisition, habitat availability quantified in other tributaries, impacts of increased discharges determined
- **1993:** [[Little Colorado River]] models finalized, [[Humpback Chub]] habitat in tributaries identified, graduate student academic questions completed
- **1994:** Final report submitted with all objectives quantitatively addressed

Research will aid identification of suitable habitat for establishing and maintaining a second spawning aggregation of [[Humpback Chub]] in [[Grand Canyon]], addressing critical conservation needs for this endangered species.
