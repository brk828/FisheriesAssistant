# Native Fish Work Group Meeting — Lake Mohave Backwater & Repatriation Program (1990)

#ichthyology #freshwater-conservation #catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This 1990 Bureau of Reclamation memorandum documents a scheduled meeting of the **[[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG)**, an interagency body coordinating conservation efforts for imperiled native fishes of the **Lower Colorado River**, specifically within **[[Lake Mohave]]**. The meeting, hosted by the **National Park Service (NPS)** at Katherine's Landing, Arizona, centered on three interconnected management issues: (1) review of a draft **Environmental Assessment (EA)** for a proposed berm modification at **Yuma Cove**, (2) methodologies for distinguishing **hatchery-reared fish** from the **relic (wild) population** to evaluate stocking augmentation success, and (3) results of a survey identifying additional candidate **backwater habitats**, including the newly identified **Davis Cove** site. The involvement of prominent Colorado River fish biologists **Dr. Paul Marsh** and **Dr. Wendell Minckley** underscores the scientific weight given to population monitoring and marking/tracking methodology for repatriated fish.

## Background & Description
- **Context**: The document is administrative/procedural (a meeting notice and agenda) rather than a primary research paper, but it reflects active, ongoing **native fish recovery efforts** in the Lower Colorado River Basin during a critical period of decline for endemic large-river fishes.
- **Species implicated**: Although not explicitly named in the OCR text, the "relic population" and "reared fish" language strongly indicates management of the **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a federally listed endangered catostomid endemic to the Colorado River Basin, and possibly the **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*). Both species were subjects of augmentation/repatriation stocking programs in Lake Mohave during this era.
- **Key management sites**:
  - **[[Yuma Cove]]** — subject of a proposed berm modification, evaluated via a draft EA.
  - **[[Davis Cove]]** — a newly identified backwater candidate located between Katherine's Landing and Davis Dam, discovered through Reclamation's field reconnaissance (May 22–23, 1990) using 1977 NPS aerial photographs.
- **Institutional actors**:
  - **Bureau of Reclamation (Reclamation)**, Lower Colorado Regional Office — lead agency for habitat engineering and hydrological management.
  - **National Park Service (NPS)** — drafting the EA and hosting the meeting; represented by **Kent Turner**.
  - **Dr. Paul Marsh** and **Dr. Wendell Minckley** — leading ichthyologists on Colorado River native fish biology, tasked with addressing fish-marking and monitoring methodology.
  - **Gordon Mueller** and **Tom Burke** — Reclamation staff responsible for backwater survey work.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: The focus on "**backwaters**" reflects the recognized importance of low-velocity, shallow, vegetated off-channel habitats in reservoirs like Lake Mohave for **larval and juvenile native fish recruitment**, especially for **[[Razorback Sucker]]**, which rely on such nursery habitats absent in the main reservoir body dominated by non-native predatory fish.
- **Berm modification (Yuma Cove)**: Physical alteration of berms is a common habitat engineering technique used to control water exchange, temperature, and predator access between backwaters and the main reservoir — directly affecting spawning and rearing success.
- **Population dynamics concern**: The central ecological question — whether **hatchery-reared fish can be traced to and distinguished from the relic (wild) population** — reflects a foundational challenge in conservation aquaculture: assessing whether augmentation stocking is contributing net population growth or simply replacing/mixing with a declining wild stock. This has direct implications for genetic management and **[[Population Augmentation]]** strategy design.
- **Landscape-scale survey**: Use of historical (1977) aerial photography to identify additional backwater candidates demonstrates a **habitat-mapping approach** integrating remote sensing with field verification for conservation site selection across the reservoir.

## Key Findings
- The **NFWG meeting** was scheduled for **June 25, 1990**, at Katherine's Landing, Arizona, jointly coordinated by NPS and Reclamation.
- A **draft EA** for berm modification at **Yuma Cove** was under review, with NFWG input solicited prior to public release.
- **Reclamation's May 1990 field survey** identified **Davis Cove** as a prime candidate for inclusion in the **backwater habitat program**, based on 1977 aerial imagery analysis.
- **Drs. Marsh and Minckley** were specifically tasked with addressing methodology for **distinguishing reared vs. relic native fish**, deemed "crucial" to evaluating whether **rearing fish to larger size before release** improves survivorship and detectable contribution to the Lake Mohave population.
- The agenda allocated dedicated time for:
  - EA presentation/discussion (Kent Turner)
  - Monitoring needs (Marsh & Minckley)
  - Backwater survey results (Mueller & Burke)
  - A **field boat trip to Davis Cove** for direct site evaluation by attendees.
- The meeting exemplifies a **multi-agency, multi-disciplinary approach** (engineering, ichthyology, environmental compliance) to endangered native fish recovery in a heavily modified reservoir ecosystem.
