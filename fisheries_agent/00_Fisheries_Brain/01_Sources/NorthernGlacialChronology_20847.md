# Book Reviews: Australian Limnology, Northern Hemisphere Glaciations, and Books Received

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #quaternary-glaciation

## Executive Summary

This *Science* book-review section (vol. 236, 19 June 1987) contains three items: a review of a limnology book on Australia's inland waters by [[Sally MacIntyre]], a review of *Quaternary Glaciations in the Northern Hemisphere* by [[H. E. Wright]], and a "Books Received" list. The document has no fish-specific content. Its aquatic relevance lies in the limnology review and in the Quaternary review's treatment of glacial and lake-forming history. The excerpt of the limnology review is incomplete, so the book's title and full scope are not given.

## Background & Description

**Review 1: Australian limnology (reviewed by Sally MacIntyre, Marine Science Institute, University of California, Santa Barbara).** The reviewer praises the quality of the figures and the clarity of the text. She criticises two omissions: no map of the continent for readers unfamiliar with its geography, and no chapter giving a hydrological and geographical perspective. She notes Australia's climatic extremes by comparison: the northern tip resembles Bengal, the southernmost region resembles Norway, and the greatest expanse is comparable to the Sahara Desert. She judges the book valuable for students needing an introduction to limnology and for researchers seeking data from Australian inland waters and insights from its distinctive habitats.

**Review 2: *Quaternary Glaciations in the Northern Hemisphere* (reviewed by H. E. Wright, [[Limnological Research Center]], University of Minnesota).** The book is the product of the [[International Geological Correlation Programme]] Project 24, edited by [[V. Gravenor]] (surname OCR-uncertain), [[D. Q. Bowen]], and [[G. M. Richmond]]. It was published by Pergamon in 1986 as volume 5 of *Quaternary Science Reviews*, with x + 514 pages, illustrations, loose charts and tables, and a boxed price of $110. Chapters are by regional specialists, with 15 papers on the United States (including Alaska and Hawaii), one on Mexico, four on Canada, 19 on Europe, four on the USSR, one on Japan and three on China. Wright calls it a massive international synthesis of the stratigraphy and chronology of Northern Hemisphere glacial events.

**Books Received.** The list contains roughly 20 titles across the sciences, including *Invertebrate Zoology* (5th ed., Robert D. Barnes), *Ecology and Design in Landscape* (A. D. Bradshaw et al., eds.), *Earth* (Anne H. and Paul R. Ehrlich) and *Mediterranean Marine Avifauna* (MEDMARAVIS and Xavier Monbailliu, eds.). Most titles are outside aquatic ecology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Glacial history and lake systems:** The Quaternary review covers the glacial and proglacial setting that shaped many Northern Hemisphere lakes and drainage networks. One example is a Siberian ice-dammed proglacial lake that drained south to the Caspian Sea.
- **Stratigraphic nomenclature:** [[Richmond]] and [[Fullerton]] propose replacing the terms "Nebraskan" and "Kansan" with "pre-Illinoian." The change follows the reinterpretation of the [[Pearlette Ash]] as three ashes from a Yellowstone Plateau source.
- **Dating methods:** The review discusses [[Potassium-Argon Dating]] (K-Ar), [[Paleomagnetism]], [[Fission-Track Dating]], [[Thermoluminescence Dating]], [[Radiocarbon Dating]], pollen stratigraphy and micromammal evolution as tools for glacial chronology.
- **Marine correlation:** Glacial sequences are compared with the [[Marine Oxygen-Isotope Stratigraphy]] record, which is the master chronology used to correlate continental and ocean-core events.
- **Limnological focus:** The Australian review is relevant to inland-water ecology, though the excerpt gives no species or hydrological data.

## Key Findings

**From the limnology review (MacIntyre):**
- The book is a useful introduction to limnology and a source of data on Australian inland waters.
- Its main gaps are the missing continental map and the missing hydrological and geographical chapter.

**From the Quaternary review (Wright):**
- Glacial nomenclature across the mid-continent has become very complex. The reviewer notes that Fullerton lists almost 70 named tills for the area from Indiana east to New Jersey, with more than 100 references.
- The Pearlette ash dates (K-Ar) of 0.61, 1.27 and 2.01 million years undermined the older glacial chronology.
- Proposed chronology (Richmond and Fullerton):
  - Pliocene Alpine glaciation: more than 1,650,000 years ago
  - Early Pleistocene: 1,650,000 to 788,000 years ago
  - Middle Pleistocene: 788,000 to 132,000 years ago
  - Sangamon (restricted): 132,000 to 122,000 years ago
  - Eowisconsin: 122,000 to 79,000 years ago
  - Wisconsin: 79,000 to 65,000 years ago
  - Middle Wisconsin: 65,000 to 35,000 years ago
  - Late Wisconsin: 35,000 to 10,000 years ago
- Eleven glacial episodes, mostly from the Rocky Mountains with K-Ar dates, match 11 marine oxygen-isotope cold stages over the last 900,000 years. The Laurentide chronology lacks the control to make an equivalent match.
- Last glacial maxima: Laurentide ice sheet 22,000 to 21,000 years ago; Alpine glaciers about 22,000 years ago; Cordilleran ice sheet about 15,000 years ago. North American ice-volume maximum preceded the marine isotopic maximum (18,000 years ago) by a few thousand years.
- The European loess sequence at Stranzendorf and Krems in Lower Austria is said to span the Quaternary back to the Gauss/Matuyama reversal (2,840,000 years ago).
- The reviewer concludes that the volume is a milestone in Quaternary research, though the final word on the problems and terminology has not been written.
