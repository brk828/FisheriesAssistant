# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies - Action Items from the February 10, 1992 Meeting

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #GilaCypha #ConservationManagement

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1992 administrative memorandum from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, summarizing action items resulting from a Principal Investigators' meeting concerning endangered fish studies in the [[Colorado River]] system, particularly the [[Little Colorado River]] and Grand Canyon reaches. The primary species of concern is the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*). The memo does not present primary morphological or ecological data but instead coordinates ongoing research efforts among multiple institutions — [[Bio/West Inc]], [[Arizona State University]], [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] — regarding population demographics, larval thermal tolerance, food/drift studies, and otolith chemistry research tied to the [[Glen Canyon Dam]] Environmental Impact Statement process under the Grand Canyon Protection/Conservation Act.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body proportions, or anatomical descriptions) are provided in this administrative source. The only anatomical/physiological research references relate to:
- **Otolith edge chemistry** — used as a tool to infer age, growth, or environmental history of [[Humpback Chub]] individuals (efficacy under review, at risk of being discontinued due to insufficient data).
- **Stomach content sampling** — via stomach pumping, restricted to ≤10 individuals per quarter, for dietary analysis of *Gila cypha*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat**: [[Little Colorado River]] mouth and mainstem [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon]], representing critical habitat for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] population.
- **Population structure focus**: Adult demographic studies targeted specifically at the *Gila cypha* population inhabiting the Little Colorado River, led by [[Arizona State University]].
- **Trophic ecology**: Food and drift studies planned in the mainstem river to characterize prey availability/diet, though these were flagged for reduction in scope due to resource and coordination concerns.
- **Thermal ecology**: Larval thermal tolerance studies (originally under [[Arizona State University]], proposed transfer to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]) aimed at understanding temperature-dependent survival of larval *Gila cypha*, relevant to managing [[Glen Canyon Dam]] cold-water discharge effects on downstream spawning/rearing habitat.
- **Multi-agency spatial coordination**: Sampling efforts in the mainstem coordinated between [[Bio/West Inc]] and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]; Little Colorado River mouth efforts coordinated with [[Arizona State University]].
- **Geospatial mapping**: Development of standardized survey protocols and GIS-based habitat mapping for the Little Colorado River mouth, led by [[Bio/West Inc]], to support consistent ecological monitoring.

## Key Findings
- **Sampling constraints**: The [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] recommended limiting invasive stomach-pumping sampling of *Gila cypha* to a maximum of 10 individuals per quarter; [[Bio/West Inc]] proposed supplementing data using stomach samples from incidental mortalities.
- **Institutional handoff of thermal tolerance research**: Larval thermal tolerance work was slated to move from [[Arizona State University]] to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], contingent on AGFD's capacity, cost, and timeline evaluation.
- **Justification required for site selection**: The [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] was required to justify selection of two "representative sites" before the March sampling effort could proceed; provisional approval was granted for February sampling only.
- **Permitting**: A one-month temporary research permit for FWS efforts was to be issued by the [[Navajo Fish and Game Department]].
- **Research consolidation**: Tributary study efforts were directed to be consolidated, and the number of graduate students involved reviewed for potential reduction — reflecting resource-efficiency concerns raised in the endangered fish program review.
- **Data and mapping integration**: [[Little Colorado River]] mapping efforts were to be coordinated with the broader [[GCES Geographic Information System]] development, with a standardized survey protocol to be established.
- **Research scope reduction**: [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] committed to reducing food/drift studies in the mainstem and geochemistry work in the Little Colorado River, citing need to focus effort and resources.
- **Otolith chemistry at risk**: The otolith edge chemistry research program was flagged for discontinuation if it failed to yield adequate, useful data in a timely manner.
- **Expertise gap identified**: A need was identified to acquire additional larval fish expertise to support ongoing studies, indicating a recognized deficiency in the program's technical capacity.
- **Programmatic coordination mechanisms established**:
  - Principal Investigators to meet with [[Duncan Patten]] at least twice annually.
  - The [[GCES Aquatic Coordination Team]] (ACT) to continue coordinating administrative, budgetary, and permitting matters.
  - Continued pursuit of radio communications infrastructure in the Little Colorado River, pending [[National Park Service]] approval.
  - Emphasis on maintaining a shared, interactive database across all Principal Investigators to support management decision-making under the Conservation Act objectives.
- **Broader context**: This memo followed critical reviews by the [[Research Advisory Panel]] and the [[National Academy of Sciences]], underscoring the need for improved inter-agency coordination in endangered fish research supporting Glen Canyon Dam operational decisions.
