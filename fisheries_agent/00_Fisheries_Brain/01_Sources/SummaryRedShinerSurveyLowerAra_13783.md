# Red Shiner Survey, Lower Aravaipa Creek, October 1990

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #invasive-species #conservation

## Executive Summary

This report documents a comprehensive survey of [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona, conducted on 20 October 1990 to determine the geographic distribution of introduced [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), first discovered in the stream in early October 1990. The survey covered approximately 8.0 km from 4.8 km upstream of the [[Aravaipa Wilderness Area]] boundary downstream to where the stream percolated into its bed 3.2 km upstream from the [[San Pedro River]] confluence. Seven field parties sampled seven sections using 3.2-mm mesh seines. The survey confirmed that red shiners had achieved substantial penetration of Aravaipa Creek, with adults found in all sections and the most upstream occurrence approximately 4.0 km inside the Wilderness Area boundary. Native fish populations, including federally listed [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), were documented at low densities. Historical data tables (1943-1990) show long-term fish community composition trends across upper, middle, and lower creek reaches.

## Background & Description

[[Aravaipa Creek]] is a perennial desert stream in Pinal County, Arizona, supporting native fish assemblages including threatened and endangered species. The survey was coordinated among multiple agencies: [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD), [[U.S. Bureau of Land Management]] (USBLM), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), and [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC). A total of 33 field participants from professional and nonprofessional backgrounds participated.

The stream was divided into seven sections (Section I through Section VII, upstream to downstream). Each section was assigned to a party of three to five persons, including at least one individual familiar with the fish fauna. Sampling was conducted using 3.2-mm mesh seines, 1.0 to 3.0 m long and one meter deep. Nonnative fishes were removed from the stream, while field-identifiable native fishes were returned unharmed. Young-of-the-year (YOY) specimens too small for field identification were preserved in 10% formalin for laboratory identification by W. L. Minckley at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Aravaipa Creek]] historically supported a native fish assemblage dominated by [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), and [[Sonoran Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*). The stream also provides habitat for federally protected species including [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]. The last major survey prior to this event was conducted in April 1989 by an ASU ichthyology class, and the AZGFD October 1989 fish count also failed to record red shiner, suggesting the invasion occurred in early or middle 1990.

The general absence of juvenile shiners in the survey may indicate a late summer penetration, coincident with summer rains and flooding, by shiners originating from the abundant [[San Pedro River]] population. Red shiners were confirmed in the San Pedro River approximately 0.5 km up- and downstream from the mouth of Aravaipa Creek on 21 October 1990, and in the [[Gila River]] mainstream 1.6 to 3.2 km upstream from Winkleman.

Nonnative fishes collected included [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). Most nonnative fishes were concentrated in the lowermost sections (Sections V through VII).

## Key Findings

**Red Shiner Distribution and Abundance:**
- Adult red shiners (defined as >35 mm total length) were taken in small numbers, between three and ten individuals per section in all but Section II and Section VII
- The most upstream occurrence was approximately 4.0 km upstream from (inside) the Aravaipa Wilderness Area boundary (eight adults recognized and preserved)
- Only one red shiner was a "large" individual (>60 mm) indicative of a second summer of life; all others could have hatched in spring 1990
- Large numbers of larvae/juveniles were preserved from Section II (Wilderness Boundary to La Jolla Ranch/TNC headquarters)
- Red shiners were abundant in the San Pedro River near the Aravaipa Creek confluence and in the Gila River upstream from Winkleman

**Native Fish Status:**
- Only four [[Spikedace]] adults and a single adult [[Loach Minnow]] were noted by all field parties combined
- YOY [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*, <30 mm TL) and loach minnow (<15 mm TL) were identified from preserved samples from Section I
- YOY loach minnow (>15 individuals, 9-14 mm TL) and YOY spikedace (at least three, <14 mm TL) were caught in Section II
- [[Longfin Dace]] were clearly dominant overall, followed by [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Sonoran Sucker]]
- "A few" adult roundtail chubs were taken in Sections I-III

**Historical Context:**
- The invasion is dated to early or middle 1990, as the species was not recorded in April 1989 or October 1989 surveys
- Red shiner is confirmed to be well established in waters of the region and to have succeeded in substantial penetration of Aravaipa Creek

**Historical Data Tables (1943-1990):**
The report includes comprehensive tables showing fish abundance and percentage composition for three creek sections:
- Upper Aravaipa Creek (headwaters to above Turkey Creek confluence): 26 years of data (1965-1990)
- Middle Aravaipa Creek (Turkey Creek confluence to Wagner Ranch): 24 years of data (1950, 1965-1990)
- Lower Aravaipa Creek (Wood's Ranch to San Pedro River): 30 years of data (1943, 1963-1990)
