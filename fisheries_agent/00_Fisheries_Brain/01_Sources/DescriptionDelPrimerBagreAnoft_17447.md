# Blind and Depigmented Catfish of the Sierra de Santa Rosa, Coahuila

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ameiuridae

## Executive Summary

This 1954 paper by [[Jorge Carranza]] describes *Prietella phreatophila*, a new genus and species of blind, depigmented ameiurid catfish, the first such fish recorded from Mexico. The type series of 65 specimens was collected on 29 August 1954 from an artificial well at "El Potrero," Muzquiz, Coahuila. The genus is distinguished from surface-dwelling ameiurids mainly by the absence of eyes and pigmentation, a non-spiny first dorsal ray, and an adipose fin confluent with the caudal fin. Its closest known relative is *Satan*, one of two previously described blind ameiurids from Texas.

## Background & Description

**Discovery.** [[Candido Bolivar y Pieltain]] and the author visited several wells, tunnels and a flooded mine in the Muzquiz region in late August 1954. The find was made possible by [[Carlos Prieto]], who sponsored the trip, and the information came from Henry J. Sanford, manager of the Compañía Carbonífera de Sabinas. The author notes that the blind fish reported in the mine could not be confirmed, which does not invalidate the report.

**Type locality.** The well lies at 260 m altitude, about 50 m from the Arroyo Salinas, a tributary of the Río Sabinas. It is roughly 3 m in diameter, with the water table about 2.5 m below the surface. Local residents use it as a backup drinking-water source for Nueva Rosita.

**Genus and species.** The genus *Prietella* is named in honour of Carlos Prieto. The species name *phreatophila* combines the Greek *phréar* (well) and *philos* (friend), reflecting its life in subsurface waters. The genotype is *Prietella phreatophila* nov. sp.

**Holotype and paratypes.** The holotype is an adult female, 54.6 mm standard length, held in the author's private collection. There are 65 paratypes measuring 33.5–54.7 mm standard length; 19 were measured and some were kept alive in aquaria. Paratype deposits include institutions in Mexico, London, Paris, Washington, Ann Arbor and Stanford.

**Morphology (selected measurements, as proportions of standard length unless noted).**
- Head length 2.6–3.2; head depth 6.0–8.1; body depth 4.5–6.7.
- Eight barbels in the typical ameiurid arrangement. In 5 of the 65 specimens one barbel is bifurcated, and one specimen has four nasal barbels instead of two.
- Gill rakers on the first lower arch 8–11 (mode 9); 3 on the upper branch in the 20 specimens measured.
- Dorsal fin usually 2 unbranched and 5 branched rays; anal fin usually 14 rays, 5 unbranched; pectorals usually 1 spine and 9 branched rays; pelvics usually 1 unbranched and 7 branched.
- Lateral-line pores on the head are well developed but smaller than in *Satan eurystomus*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water temperature:** 29 °C on the visit day; a nearby pumped well read 30.5 °C. A letter from Sanford reported 30.7 °C on 6 December 1954. The source says temperature remains fairly constant through the year within certain limits.
- **Habitat:** A groundwater (phreatic) habitat in a limestone karst area, with connections to underground cavities.
- **Feeding:** No planktonic organisms were found in the stomachs examined. The author suggests this may indicate that plankton is scarce in the habitat.
- **Behaviour:** Individuals swim upside down when they reach the surface and normally hold a vertical, head-up posture with slight fin movements. On the bottom they lie on their sides, sometimes nearly inverted, or rest head-up against submerged objects, using the tail as support.
- **Relatives:** [[Trogloglanis]] and [[Satan]] (both from artesian waters of Texas) are the other known blind, depigmented ameiurids. Comparison with [[Ameiurus]], [[Schilbeodes]] and [[Noturus]] informed the proposed evolutionary scheme for the three genera.

## Key Findings

- The first known blind, depigmented ameiurid from Mexico is described as a new genus and species, *Prietella phreatophila*.
- The 65-specimen type series was collected from a single artificial well in Coahuila on 29 August 1954.
- Diagnostic characters include absence of eyes and pigmentation, a non-spiny first dorsal ray, a weak pectoral spine smooth along its posterior edge, an adipose fin confluent with the caudal fin, and lateral-line pores that do not reach the dorsal fin origin.
- Unlike [[Schilbeodes]] and [[Noturus]], which share the confluent adipose fin, the new genus lacks strong dorsal and pectoral spines and the pectoral poison gland.
- *Satan* is considered the closest living relative, and the differences from *Trogloglanis* and *Satan* are set out in Table I.
- The stable groundwater temperature of about 29–31 °C and the absence of plankton in the stomachs were recorded.
- The author notes possible evolutionary significance in the occurrence of extra barbels, which may increase sensory capacity and survival.
