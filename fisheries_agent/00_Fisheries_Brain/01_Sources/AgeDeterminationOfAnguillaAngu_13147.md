# Age Determination of the European Eel (Anguilla anguilla) and Methods for Marking and Tagging

#ichthyology #marine-biology #anguillidae

## Executive Summary

This FAO/EIFAC Occasional Paper (No. 21, 1988) reviews the methods used to determine the age of the [[European Eel]] (*Anguilla anguilla*) and related species, based on a circular survey of EIFAC Working Party on Eel members and the published literature of the previous decade. It also includes a companion review by Jan Nielsen on [[Marking And Tagging Methods]] for eel. The authors identify four main ageing approaches: grinding or slicing, burning and cracking, clearing of whole otoliths in alcohol, and advanced technologies. Only a few methods have been validated. [[Burning And Cracking]] is judged the most suitable available method and is proposed as a reference method for future studies.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** Age estimation of [[European Eel]] from sagittal [[Otoliths]], the only structure routinely used by eel workers. Scales were long discarded as unsuitable (Smith and Saunders, 1955; Jellyman, 1979), a finding confirmed by Dekker (1986) using [[Tetracycline Labelling]]. Vertebrae, fin rays and opercula have received little work.
- **Context:** Fisheries management decisions rely on ageing calcified structures, but eel ageing is especially difficult; results from different methods can vary considerably.
- **Origin:** Prepared for the EIFAC Working Party on Eel (Perpignan 1985; reviews presented at Bristol, UK, 1988). Published by FAO, Rome, 1988; printed by the Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries, The Netherlands.
- **Standard terms** (after Prince and Pulos, 1983; Anon., 1985): *annual zone*, *annulus*, *check*, *focus*, *opaque zone*, *translucent zone*, *radius*, *validation* (accuracy of true age), and *verification* (precision/reproducibility of estimated age).
- **Light conventions:** In transmitted light opaque zones appear dark and translucent zones bright; in reflected light the reverse holds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Eel life history cues:** Ageing relies on annual translucent (winter) and opaque (summer) zones in the otolith, formed in relation to growth and season. Supplementary translucent zones (checks) are linked to starvation, low or abruptly changing temperatures, and handling (Liew, 1974).
- **Growth variability:** Eels studied were often slow-growing (pond and temperate populations) or faster-growing (brackish water, Norway). Slow growth reduces dorso-ventral otolith growth, which complicates ageing in some methods.
- **Habitats cited:** Gulf of Lion lagoons (France), Italian lagoons (Comacchio, Lesina, Varano), Sardinian hypersaline lagoon, Irish rivers (Shannon, Tweed), River Severn (England), Lake Constance, Lake Fertő (Hungary), Imsa River (Norway), Kolderveen pond (Netherlands).
- **Migration and stocks:** Descent of silver eels, elver ascent and stock monitoring are used to test age estimates against population-level data.

## Key Findings

- **Four technique families:**
  1. *Grinding or slicing*: the most common method, with almost every institute using its own modification; validation documented in very few cases.
  2. *Burning and cracking*: first described by Christensen (1964); evidence indicates a good approximation of true age and accuracy in several tests.
  3. *Whole otoliths cleared in alcohol*: rapid and inexpensive, reusable after clearing; unsuitable for very slow-growing eels.
  4. *Advanced technologies* (scanning densitometry, electron microprobe, SEM): useful mainly for research, too costly and laborious for routine ageing.
- **Validation evidence (selected):**
  - Liew (1974) demonstrated annual translucent/opaque zone formation in American eels, validating the acetate peel technique.
  - Berg (1985, 1987) validated sliced otolith annuli using recaptured eels up to 5 years after tagging.
  - Moriarty (1983) and Vellestad and Jonsson (1988) gave indirect validation for burning and clearing respectively.
  - Vellestad and Jonsson (1988) estimated mean age at descent of silver eels at 8 years (regression) against 7.9 years from otolith ages in the Imsa River.
- **Burning and cracking performance:**
  - Moriarty and Steinmetz (1979) found it overestimated young eels but was best of five methods for older eels.
  - Vellestad and Nesje (1988) on 15-year-old Kolderveen eels: about 26% aged correctly, probably underestimated owing to unknown introductions.
  - Dekker (pers. comm.) found the last annulus correctly identified in over 90% of tetracycline-labelled burnt and cracked otoliths.
  - Repeat readings differed by one year or less in over 80% of cases (Moriarty, 1983; Vellestad, 1985).
- **Comparisons:**
  - Moriarty and Steinmetz (1979): no method performed equally well for young and old eels; differences of three to ten years between methods; grinding best for young eels, burning and cracking best for old ones.
  - Vellestad (1985): burning and cracking gave ages 1.25 years higher than alcohol clearing; only 8 otoliths (3.7%) yielded the higher estimate when cleared.
  - Jarmatz et al. (1986): no systematic difference between burning and cracking and sliced otoliths; burning preferred for speed.
  - Lecomte-Finiger et al. (in prep.): of 1800 otoliths from five Gulf of Lion lagoons, 26–47% were unreadable whole in one lagoon; grinding increased readability there.
- **Growth check against tagging:** Penaz and Tesch (1970) and Tesch (1977) found 2.8 cm/yr from otolith readings versus 2.0 cm/yr from internal magnetic tags, considered nearly similar given possible tagging effects.
- **Scanning method limits:** The scanning densitometer (Deelder, 1976) underestimated age of slow-growing eels, and interpretation difficulty merely shifts to graph reading.
- **Recommendation and conclusion:** Burning and cracking is the best available method and is proposed as the reference method for further studies. Every technique should be re-tested when applied to new populations or habitats. Precise age estimates are essential to fish biology and population dynamics, and multiple readings or independent interpreters should be used where validation is not possible.
- **Research needs:** Objective criteria to separate opaque and translucent zones, possible automated image analysis, and further validation of grinding, slicing and clearing methods.
