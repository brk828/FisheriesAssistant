# Santa Cruz River Native Fish Survey Data, May 1993

#ichthyology #native-fish #Cyprinidae #Poeciliidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence and data package documents fish sampling conducted on May 6, 1993 at four sites along the [[Santa Cruz River]] in [[Santa Cruz County]], Arizona. The survey was conducted by the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]] (ADEQ) and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) personnel. The primary species collected was [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), with significant observations of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) at [[Santa Gertrudis Lane]]. Follow-up observations on May 11, 1993 confirmed additional topminnow presence at the same location, with fish utilizing thermal refugia under filamentous algae.

## Background & Description

The survey targeted native fish populations in the [[Santa Cruz River]] system near the U.S./Mexican border. Four sampling locations were established:

1. **Near Guevavi Ranch** (9.4 miles north of border, elevation 3500 feet)
2. **Rio Rico at Calabasses Road Bridge** (one mile downstream of [[Sonoita Creek]] confluence, elevation 3403 feet)
3. **Santa Gertrudis Lane** (elevation and coordinates not specified in data sheets)
4. **Chavez Siding Road** (elevation 3140 feet)

Sampling methodology employed a Smith-Root backpack electrofisher. At each site, two 80-foot replicate sections were sampled with varying substrate types (sandy, mixed sand and rocks, stones). Fish were measured for maximum total length (mm), counted, and inspected for sores, parasites, lesions, and deformities.

[[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) was the dominant species collected. [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, family [[Poeciliidae]]) is a federally endangered species, making its observation particularly significant for [[Section 7 Consultation]] requirements under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water Quality Parameters:**

- **Guevavi Ranch site:** Temperature 17.5°C, dissolved oxygen 7.8 mg/L, pH 8.14
- **Rio Rico/Calabasses Road:** Temperature 24.0°C, dissolved oxygen 6.60 mg/L, total chlorine 0.20 µg/L, free chlorine 0.06 µg/L
- **Chavez Siding Road:** Temperature 28.0°C, dissolved oxygen 7.42 mg/L, total chlorine 0.08 µg/L, free chlorine 0.00 µg/L

Temperature gradient increased downstream from 17.5°C to 28.0°C. Chlorine detection at two sites suggests wastewater influence.

**Habitat Characteristics:**

Substrate varied from sandy to mixed sand/rock/stone. Current velocities ranged from slow to fast. Water depths ranged from 1 inch to 10 inches. The presence of thick filamentous green algae at [[Santa Gertrudis Lane]] provided critical thermal refugia for [[Gila Topminnow]], with water temperatures 5.5°C cooler under algae mats (18.5°C) compared to adjacent open water (24°C).

**Reproductive Activity:**

Approximately 200 fry (6-10 mm) of [[Longfin Dace]] were observed but not captured at multiple sites, indicating active reproduction during the survey period.

## Key Findings

**Longfin Dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*) Population Data:**

- **Guevavi Ranch Site:**
  - Replicate 1: N=60, mean length 34.5 mm (range 14-67 mm, SD 15.0)
  - Replicate 2: N=36, mean length 32.8 mm (range 16-54 mm, SD 13.8)
  - Total counted: 196 individuals in Replicate 1, plus ~200 fry observed

- **Rio Rico/Calabasses Road:**
  - Replicate 1: N=50, mean length 50.0 mm (range 15-74 mm, SD 18.1)
  - Replicate 2: N=30, mean length 70.1 mm (range 23-82 mm, SD 11.0)
  - Total counted: 73 individuals, plus ~200 fry (6 mm) observed

- **Chavez Siding Road:**
  - Replicate 1: N=50, mean length 46.1 mm (range 17-78 mm, SD 19.5)
  - Replicate 2: N=50, mean length 33.7 mm (range 18-77 mm, SD 15.7)
  - Total counted: 361 individuals in combined replicates

**Gila Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) Observations:**

- Males and females observed in small pool beside road crossing at [[Santa Gertrudis Lane]] on May 6, 1993
- Additional topminnow found on May 11, 1993 approximately 100 feet downstream from road crossing along east bank in 10-inch deep water
- Fish were utilizing thermal refugia under thick filamentous green algae where water temperature was 5.5°C cooler than adjacent open water
- Specimens were caught and examined but not preserved during initial survey; positive identification via preserved specimens still required for formal documentation

**Management Implications:**

The discovery of [[Gila Topminnow]] populations necessitates [[Section 7 Consultation]] with [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. AGFD planned to incorporate monitoring visits to [[Santa Gertrudis Lane]] during summer 1993 surveys. The behavioral use of algal mats as thermal refugia by topminnow suggests microhabitat complexity is critical for persistence of this endangered species in thermally variable river systems.
