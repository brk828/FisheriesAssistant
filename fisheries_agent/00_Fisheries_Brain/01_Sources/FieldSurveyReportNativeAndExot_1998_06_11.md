## Executive Summary
This document is a field-survey correspondence (dated June 11, 1998) from Arizona Game and Fish personnel (Heidi Blasius) to Dr. W.L. Minckley, reporting on a two-day electrofishing and seining survey of [[Aravaipa Creek]], Arizona. The primary objective was detection and removal of the invasive [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), a species of concern for native fish conservation in the Gila River drainage. No red shiners were detected during the survey. However, the crew removed 23 [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), another non-native species, and documented abundant larval/juvenile native fishes, including all seven native species historically known from the system, with [[Rhinichthys osculus]] (Speckled Dace) present in lower relative abundance than other native taxa.

## Background & Description
No formal morphometric or meristic data are provided in this correspondence; the report is observational/survey-based rather than a morphological study. Species identification was made in the field via visual/electrofishing capture:
- **Yellow Bullhead (*Ameiurus natalis*)**: Identified as a distinct exotic species within the survey reach; individuals were captured, confirmed, and euthanized ("humanely destroyed") rather than measured or described morphologically.
- **Speckled Dace (*Rhinichthys osculus*)**: Noted present but in lower numbers relative to co-occurring native species; no size classes, counts, or morphological details recorded.
- **Native larval/juvenile fishes**: Described only generically as "hundreds" of larval and juvenile individuals, presumed native due to absence of adult red shiners in catch, but not identified to species or measured.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat surveyed**: [[Aravaipa Creek]], a perennial desert stream tributary in southeastern Arizona, sampled across multiple reaches:
  - [[Turkey Creek]] confluence zone (250 m downstream and upstream toward Chimney Rock), sampled in systematic 25-meter increments.
  - Reach between **Deer Canyon** and Turkey Creek.
  - Downstream hike from a point near **Beer Canyon** (approx. Station 4) back toward the confluence.
- **Community structure**: The creek supports a historically intact assemblage of seven native fish species, all observed in "many numbers" during this survey — indicative of a functioning, biodiverse desert stream ecosystem, one of the most significant such systems in the American Southwest for native fish conservation.
- **Invasive species pressure**: Two non-native species are explicitly referenced:
  - *Cyprinella lutrensis* (Red Shiner) — target of active surveillance and removal efforts; a well-documented invasive competitor/predator of native Southwestern cyprinids and cyprinodontids.
  - *Ameiurus natalis* (Yellow Bullhead) — confirmed established in the reach, captured and removed as part of exotic species control efforts.
- **Reproductive activity**: Abundant larval and juvenile native fish observed across multiple reaches suggests active, successful in-stream spawning at the time of survey (early-to-mid June).

## Key Findings
- **No red shiners detected** across ~250 m below the Turkey Creek confluence, sampled reaches toward Chimney Rock, and the lower reach near Beer Canyon/Station 4 — a positive indicator for native fish conservation status at the time of survey, though absence of detection does not confirm absence of the species.
- **23 Yellow Bullhead removed total** (2 on Day 1, 21 on Day 2) via electrofishing/seining and euthanized on-site as part of ongoing exotic species management.
- **All seven native fish species** historically associated with Aravaipa Creek were observed in high numbers during the survey, indicating a robust native assemblage.
- **[[Rhinichthys osculus]]** (Speckled Dace) was present but in comparatively lower abundance relative to the other six native species — a notable observation worth monitoring in future surveys.
- Field data sheets from the survey exist and were offered for further review/collaboration with Dr. Minckley.
- The surveyor requested guidance on future management strategy for Aravaipa Creek, indicating this was part of an ongoing, iterative [[exotic species removal]] and native fish monitoring program.
- Follow-up trips further into Aravaipa Creek were planned to continue red shiner surveillance and exotic species removal.
