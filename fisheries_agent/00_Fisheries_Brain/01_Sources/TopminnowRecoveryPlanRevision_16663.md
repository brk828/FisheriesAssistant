# Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan Revision (1989 Team Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum from Dean Hendrickson, Native Fish Biologist with the [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]], dated December 21, 1989, outlines the consensus of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] on revised downlisting criteria and other major parts of the [[Gila Topminnow]] Recovery Plan. It asks team members and consultants to review the outline and return comments by January 31 so the revision can proceed. The memo is a planning document, not a research study, and contains no new field data.

## Background & Description

The memo is addressed to Desert Fishes Recovery Team members and consultants, and copied to the Department's Deputy Director. The author volunteered to write the plan revision and wanted to confirm his understanding of the December 7 meeting before drafting. The scientific name is not stated in the memo; the species list in the frontmatter reflects the subject organism.

The revision is framed around genetic structure, habitat security, and population management in the native range of the fish, which lies within the [[Gila River Basin]] of Arizona and Sonora.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Genetic lineages:** Until genetic studies are complete, differentiation is assumed to follow drainage relationships. Four provisional lineages are proposed: (1) Sharp and Sheehy Springs plus the Santa Cruz River populations in San Rafael Valley; (2) Monkey Springs, Cottonwood Springs, and Sonoita Creek; (3) Ciénega Creek; (4) the Bylas Springs complex (Bos/Medicine and Middle Springs, and Salt Creek). The memo notes that no genetic data exist yet for Sonoita Creek.
- **Habitat classification:** Sites are sorted into primary (most permanent and most natural) and secondary (more ephemeral or less natural) reintroduction categories, using criteria similar to those proposed by Sally Stefferud. Invertebrate and botanical indicators of surface-flow permanency are named as potential evaluation tools.
- **Threats:** The memo cites [[Gambusia]] (mosquitofish) as a major threat and notes long-term development trends threatening habitats across the range.
- **Basins without natural populations:** Sub-basins such as the San Pedro, San Simón, Avra-Altar, Agua Fria, and Hassayampa are candidates for reintroduction. The Gila below Phoenix was specifically suggested for mixed-lineage stocking, as likely occurred historically.
- **Out-of-range populations:** Populations outside the native range, such as those in the Bill Williams drainage, are to be removed or allowed to go extinct and will not count toward recovery.

## Key Findings

**Revised downlisting criteria (as agreed at the December 7 meeting):**

1. **Security per lineage:** Recovery is judged by the level of security provided to each genetic lineage, with the number of lineages yet to be determined. Stocks from each natural population are to be placed in refugia or aquaria at the earliest opportunity.
2. **In situ protection first:** Security relies mainly on protecting and restoring natural habitats, supplemented by reintroduced populations that persist for specified periods. Acquisition or management agreements covering all habitats with remaining natural populations are required.
3. **Management Implementation Committee (GTMIC):** A committee including every land or wildlife management agency with jurisdiction over sites, including Mexico, will define bureaucratic security terms, subject to Recovery Team approval.
4. **Primary and secondary sites:** The team suggested three primary and 10 secondary sites per lineage as a starting point. Primary sites should not hold other fish species; secondary sites may.
5. **Active fish movement:** Qualified personnel will move fish among sites to mimic historic gene flow, shown in a draft flow diagram.
6. **Monitoring:** Sampling by qualified professionals at least twice a year in all U.S. natural habitats and primary sites, and at least once every two years at secondary sites. Sonora requires an annual program and demonstrated population stability over a period the GTMIC will set.
7. **Data and survey requirements:** A centralized depository for stocking and monitoring data must exist before downlisting, and a survey of the entire U.S. native range must search for undiscovered remnant populations.
8. **Research priorities:** Genetic differentiation among Arizona populations; interactions with pupfish, Gambusia, and invertebrates; habitat evaluation criteria; disease and parasite impacts; and effective population size for intensive genetic management.
9. **Delisting:** An option to delist is included, but the memo acknowledges delisting is unlikely because eliminating the Gambusia threat is nearly impossible. Delisting would also require protection and demonstrated security for Mexican populations.

**Open questions for reviewers:**

- **A.** Should sub-basins lacking native stocks (San Pedro, San Simón, Avra-Altar, Hassayampa, Agua Fria) accept mixed-lineage stocking or be reserved for a single lineage?
- **B.** Should backup primary and secondary sites be specified per population, or only per lineage/watershed?
- **C.** Should the stocking flow diagram be one-way (natural populations to primary sites) or allow return movement, and do arrow widths reflect the intended relative importance of gene flow?
- **D.** Mexico must provide a written plan for implementing recovery actions, likely focused on monitoring and protected areas.

Comments were due January 31 (1990).
