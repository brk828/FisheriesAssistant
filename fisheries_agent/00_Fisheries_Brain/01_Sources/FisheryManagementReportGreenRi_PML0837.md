# Fishery Management Report: Green River, Colorado and Utah (October 11-13, 1953)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service fishery management report, issued by the Cooperative Fishery Unit under Robert L. Inucdo (Fishery Management Biologist), describes a three-day fish sampling survey of the [[Green River]] and the [[Yampa River]] in Colorado and Utah, conducted October 11-13, 1953. The survey was led by Dr. Joseph Banks of [[Colorado State University]], with student volunteer crews. The report's main conclusion is that the 1953 collections are **not comparable** to earlier [[Green River]] collections from 1951 and 1952, and that the methods and crew performance were inadequate for quantitative inference. The document is largely a critique of sampling practice, not a population study.

## Background & Description

- **Organizer and personnel:** Dr. Joseph Banks (Colorado State University) led three crews of students. Dr. Harold Eagen of Colorado State University observed. Bureau staff included Philip Sharpe (Fish and Wildlife Service, Fort Collins), William Melander (Fish and Wildlife Service, Laramie), and Dr. D. R. Franklin (Unit Leader, Cooperative Fishery Unit, Logan).
- **Crew assignments:**
  - Crew 1 (Miller, with Melander as Bureau inspector): sampled at the Gates of Lodore on October 12, then moved to the Browns Park Bridge area on October 13.
  - Crew 2 (Babcock, no Bureau inspector): sampled at Mantle Ranch on the Yampa River on October 12, then worked Echo Park.
  - Crew 3 (Banks, with Franklin and Sharpe as inspectors): sampled at [[Split Mountain Campground]] on the night of October 11 and morning of October 12, then joined Crew 2 at Echo Park on October 13.
- **Equipment:** Banks's crews used standard AC/DC 2500-watt backpack-style shockers that supplied non-pulsed AC through three electrodes. Sharpe supplied a boat-mounted shocker with a 1500-watt AC generator and a pulse generator, which supplied rectified, pulsed DC through three electrodes.
- **Sampling reach:** Stations were distributed across river miles (RM) roughly 201 to 248, including Lodore I and II, Browns Park, Split Mountain, Echo Park I and II, Yampa I and II, and lower Pool Creek. Individual station RM ranges are summarized in the report's Tables 1 and 2 (not reproduced).
- **Preservation:** Fish from Lodore Canyon and Browns Park were preserved in 10 percent formalin and sent to [[Colorado State University]]. Fish from Mantle Ranch were not preserved because of the absence of formalin and containers. Fish from Echo Park and Split Mountain were taken to Logan for transfer before shipment to Colorado State University.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Target species:** Named taxa in the report are limited to [[Gila]] (chub) and [[Pantosteus]] (suckers). Several other species were noted as rare in the area; these names are garbled in the scan and are not reliably recoverable.
- **Habitat constraints:** At [[Split Mountain Campground]], only about 20 percent of the river was shallow enough to wade. At Echo Park, the Green River above the Yampa confluence and the Yampa River itself were too deep to wade, so the boat shocker was required.
- **Disturbance:** The Split Mountain campground reach is repeatedly disturbed by tourists. The crew sampled only the area immediately adjacent to the campground, even though the shocker boat obtained good catches of Gila and Pantosteus about 1/4 mile upstream of the disturbed zone.
- **Gear bias:** The report states that catches reflect the capacity of each gear type and the electrical characteristics of the equipment more than the true fish community. The boat shocker took 74 percent of the total catch at the Echo Park-Yampa confluence area, including the only Gila taken from the Echo Park-Yampa area. At Split Mountain, the boat shocker collected the only Gila taken from the entire study area.
- **Unsampled areas:** Deep pools could be worked only from the boat, so deeper habitats were underrepresented.

## Key Findings

1. **Sampling was not standardized or comparable.** The report explicitly states that Bureau personnel did not alter sampling procedures to match earlier 1951-1952 collections, and that the 1953 catches therefore cannot be treated as directly comparable. The authors note that if they were comparable, some collections would deserve great weight.
2. **Field crews were inadequately instructed and equipped.** The student crews were volunteers who were not properly briefed, and the authors describe their effort as "perfunctory," with a "casual and careless" attitude and little interest in identifying species present. The report notes that this does not discredit Dr. Banks personally and that the crews were typical of student field trips.
3. **Equipment was outdated.** The reviewers judged the standard backpack shockers obsolete compared with modern units that rectify AC, produce pulsed output, and offer full control of electrical level. Adding a boat-mounted shocker with these capabilities produced a much more adequate sample. The report also recommends adding nets of various types to improve sampling adequacy.
4. **Gear and access choices shaped the results.** Wading restrictions, deep water, and campground disturbance all limited coverage. The authors conclude that the catch composition at Split Mountain and Echo Park would have looked very different without the shocker boat.
5. **Overall quality is low.** The authors judge that the collections were not made with any real desire to determine which fish species and numbers were present, and that the results should not be used to draw quantitative conclusions without caution.
6. **Specimen handling was incomplete.** Not all catches were preserved in the field, and some specimens were routed through Logan before reaching [[Colorado State University]]. This affects the traceability of the 1953 collections.
