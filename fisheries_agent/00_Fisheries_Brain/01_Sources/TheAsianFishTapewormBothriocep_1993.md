# The Asian Fish Tapeworm, Bothriocephalus acheilognathi, in Fishes from Nevada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #parasitology #Cestoda #Bothriocephalidae

## Executive Summary
This research note documents the presence and spread of the introduced [[Asian Fish Tapeworm]] (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*) in fish populations from the [[Muddy River]] and the cooling pond of the [[Moapa Power Plant]] in Clark County, Nevada, as well as in commercial bait fish sold in the Las Vegas area. Of 73 wild-caught fish examined, infection rates were notably high in [[Gila Robusta]] (91%) and moderate in [[Cyprinella Lutrensis]] (21%), the latter representing a **new host record** for this cestode. Screening of 79 bait minnows from four Las Vegas bait shops revealed infection in [[Notemigonus Crysoleucas]] (8%) but not in [[Pimephales Promelas]], implicating commercial baitfish trade (sourced from California, Arkansas, and Missouri) as a vector for continued parasite introduction into new watersheds, including the Lake Mead drainage.

## Background & Description
- **Parasite identity:** *Bothriocephalus acheilognathi* (Yamaguti, 1934) — a pseudophyllidean cestode, commonly known as the [[Asian Fish Tapeworm]].
- **Taxonomic classification:** Considered one of the most pathogenic pseudophyllidean cestodes affecting cultured [[Carp]] populations in Europe.
- **Diagnostic methodology:**
  - Digestive tracts dissected in saline solution and examined under a binocular dissecting microscope.
  - Cestodes excised from the intestinal tract, enumerated, and fixed in AFA (alcohol-formalin-acetic acid).
  - Specimens stained with Semichon's carmine and mounted on glass slides for identification.
  - Identification confirmed via standard taxonomic keys and comparison against reference specimens in the senior author's collection.
- **Voucher specimens:** Two whole mounts (HWML 35094, HWML 35095) and two histological slides of infected fish intestine (HWML 35096, HWML 35097) deposited at the Harold W. Manter Collection, University of Nebraska State Museum.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Geographic range expansion:** This study extends the known range of *B. acheilognathi* to the [[Muddy River]], Clark County, Nevada — a new locality record. The Muddy River drains into [[Lake Mead]], raising concern for downstream parasite dispersal.
- **Habitat types sampled:**
  - Natural riverine habitat (Muddy River)
  - Anthropogenic thermal habitat (cooling pond, Moapa Power Plant)
  - Commercial bait shop holding tanks (Las Vegas area)
- **Host community from Nevada waters (Muddy River / cooling pond):**
  - [[Cyprinella Lutrensis]] — 3/14 infected (21%) — **new host record**
  - [[Gila Robusta]] — 10/11 infected (91%)
  - [[Gambusia Affinis]] — 0/17 infected
  - [[Poecilia Mexicana]] — 0/26 infected
  - [[Crenichthys Baileyi]] — 0/5 infected
- **Bait fish shop survey (Las Vegas):**
  - [[Notemigonus Crysoleucas]] (golden shiner) — 3/38 infected (8%)
  - [[Pimephales Promelas]] (fathead minnow) — 0/41 infected
  - Bait fish originated from commercial fish farms in California, Arkansas, and Missouri, indicating potential interstate transport of infected hosts.
- **Known prior hosts in western U.S.** (per Heckmann et al. 1986): *N. crysoleucas*, *P. promelas*, [[Ctenopharyngodon Idella]] (grass carp), *G. affinis*, *G. robusta*, [[Rhinichthys Osculus]], [[Lepidomeda Mollispinis]], [[Plagopterus Argentissimus]] (woundfin), and [[Ptychocheilus Lucius]] (Colorado pikeminnow).
- **Introduction pathway:** Originally introduced to the United States via shipments of grass carp (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) imported from China for aquatic vegetation control (Hoffman and Schubert, 1984). Subsequent spread across the U.S. attributed to translocation of infected fish, including through the baitfish trade.

## Key Findings
- **New host record:** *Cyprinella lutrensis* (red shiner) is documented for the first time as a host of *Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*.
- **New locality record:** The Muddy River, Clark County, Nevada, is newly confirmed as an infected locality for the Asian fish tapeworm.
- **High infection prevalence in Gila robusta (91%)** suggests this species may be a highly susceptible or reservoir host in thermally altered habitats like the Moapa Power Plant cooling pond.
- **Baitfish as a vector:** Detection of *B. acheilognathi* in commercially sourced golden shiners sold as live bait represents a documented pathway for introducing the parasite into previously uninfected water bodies, particularly Lake Mead, where infected bait may be released or discarded by anglers.
- **Historical context:** This finding builds on earlier reports of *B. acheilognathi* in the [[Virgin River]] (Utah/Nevada) by Heckmann et al. (1986, 1987), reinforcing a pattern of range expansion of this invasive parasite in the southwestern United States.
- **Conservation implication:** Given some hosts (e.g., woundfin, *Plagopterus argentissimus*) are endangered species, continued spread of *B. acheilognathi* via bait trade poses a potential threat to imperiled native fish populations in Nevada, Utah, and Arizona.
