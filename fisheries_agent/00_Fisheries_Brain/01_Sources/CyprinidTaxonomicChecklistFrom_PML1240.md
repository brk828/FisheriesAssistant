# Cyprinid Taxonomic Checklist from a U.S. Bureau of Fisheries Page

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a scanned, heavily garbled page from an early twentieth-century [[U.S. Bureau of Fisheries]] taxonomic publication. It is a numbered checklist of freshwater fish names (species and genera), each followed by its author, year, and original publication reference. The page covers two genera in the minnow family (Cyprinidae): [[Ptychocheilus]] (pikeminnows, numbered in the 800s range) and [[Gila]] (chubs and related fishes, numbers 800 onward), plus the genus [[Notemigonus]] (number 301). The text contains no abundance data, measurements, sample sizes, or statistics.

## Background & Description

The page is a nomenclatural record rather than a field study. Each entry lists:

- A running species number and the valid name (e.g., Ptychocheilus umpquae Snyder).
- The describing authority (e.g., Baird and Girard, Agassiz, Snyder, Girard, Meek, Rafinesque).
- Citation details (journal, volume, year, and page), such as Proceedings of the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia, Bulletin of the U.S. Bureau of Fisheries, and the Journal of Physiology.
- Brief type-locality or range notes, such as "Zuni River," "Gila River," "Columbia River," and "Chihuahua, Mexico."

OCR errors are numerous. Several names and dates are only partly legible, so they are reported here as read, without correction beyond obvious scan errors.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The page gives only indirect ecological information, mainly through locality and range notes:

- **Columbia River** and **Umpqua River** systems in Oregon are associated with [[Ptychocheilus Oregonensis]] and [[Ptychocheilus Umpquae]].
- **Gila River** and tributaries, including the Zuni River in the southwestern United States, are associated with several [[Gila]] species. One legible note describes a Gila taxon as "very common up to" a certain point in the river system.
- **Chihuahua, Mexico** is named as a locality for one Gila species (Gila minacae, described by Meek).
- The Colorado basin appears as a broad regional reference for the Gila-group records.

Because the text is largely name lists and citations, habitat, behavior, and population status are otherwise not addressed.

## Key Findings

- The page lists more than a dozen named taxa across at least three genera, but the exact total cannot be confirmed because much of the OCR text is corrupted.
- **Ptychocheilus** entries include:
  - [[Ptychocheilus Oregonensis]] (Richardson), a pikeminnow from the Pacific Northwest.
  - [[Ptychocheilus Umpquae]] (Snyder, 1908, Bulletin of the U.S. Bureau of Fisheries), tied to Oregon coastal streams.
- **Gila** entries (the largest group on the page) include:
  - Gila robusta (Baird and Girard), noted in relation to the Gila River and its tributaries.
  - Gila elegans (Baird and Girard), associated with the Zuni River and Colorado region.
  - Gila emoryi (Baird and Girard), listed with a Gila River locality.
  - Gila minacae (Meek), recorded from Chihuahua, Mexico.
- **Notemigonus** (genus number 301) is cited with Rafinesque as author, but the species name is not legible.
- The authorities most often cited are [[Baird and Girard]], with additional entries by Snyder, Girard, Agassiz, Meek, and Rafinesque.
- The page gives no population counts, sample sizes, measurements, or conservation status.
