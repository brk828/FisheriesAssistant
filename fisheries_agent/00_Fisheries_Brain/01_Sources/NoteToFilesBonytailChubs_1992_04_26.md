# Note to Files — Bonytail Chubs (4/26/92)

## Executive Summary
This internal memo documents a mortality event affecting endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) at Palm Lake, a refugium property managed by The Nature Conservancy (TNC) near Wickenburg, Arizona. Following a report of dead fish on 4/20/1992, Arizona Game & Fish Department (Non-Game Branch) personnel, led by Francisco Abarca, mobilized a salvage crew. The suspected cause of mortality was low dissolved oxygen (DO) in the lake. A rescue operation successfully recovered approximately 20 live and documented 15 dead bonytail chub, with surviving fish transported to the Bubbling Ponds State Fish Facility for safekeeping. The note also outlines the origin of these refugium populations, tracing them back to the 1981 year class of pit-tagged fish reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]]. The author raises internal concerns regarding communication gaps, noting that the lead station (Parker Office of Fisheries Assistance) was not involved in the on-site decision-making.

## Background & Description
- **Species:** Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*)
- **Tagging:** The 1981 year class stocked in Arizona were **PIT tagged** (Passive Integrated Transponder), allowing for individual tracking and identification of "F1" generation wild-origin broodstock.
- **Origin:** Fish were F1 generation offspring derived from wild-caught adult bonytail chub, propagated at the Dexter facility.
- *Note: The source document is a management/administrative memo and does not provide detailed morphometric or anatomical data.*

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type:** The bonytail chub in this account do not occupy natural riverine habitat but rather **artificial refugium lentic environments**:
  - **Palm Lake** (TNC property, Wickenburg, AZ) — designated refugium for multiple listed species (up to 5 listed species reported present).
  - **Golf Course Pond** (Arizona) — a secondary stocking location.
- **Water Quality Sensitivity:** The mortality event highlights the species' vulnerability to **low dissolved oxygen (DO)** conditions in closed/still-water systems, a significant departure from the historic lotic (flowing river) habitat of *Gila elegans* in the [[Colorado River Basin]].
- **Reproductive Success in Refugia:** Notably, the population at **Golf Course Pond** successfully reproduced in captivity/semi-wild conditions, resulting in an unknown but believed "sizeable" self-sustaining population — an important indicator of the suitability of certain pond environments for recruitment.
- **Conservation Role:** These sites function as genetic and demographic **refugia**, intended to relieve rearing space pressure at the primary hatchery ([[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]]) while establishing wild/semi-wild population footholds.

## Key Findings
1. **Mortality Event:** Bonytail chub die-offs were first reported at Palm Lake on ~4/20/1992; only 2-3 dead fish initially observed by TNC staff, later revised upward.
2. **Suspected Cause:** Field investigation attributed the deaths to **low dissolved oxygen** levels in Palm Lake, rather than disease or contamination.
3. **Salvage Operation Results (as of 4/22/92):**
   - ~20 live bonytail chub captured and transported to **Bubbling Ponds State Facility**.
   - ~15 dead bonytail chub recovered.
   - Collection efforts continued with a goal to recover the majority of remaining fish by April 23, 1992.
4. **Population History:**
   - 700 F1 bonytail chubs (1981 year class) were moved from Dexter NFH&TC in April 1991.
   - Distribution: 350 to Golf Course Pond, 250 to Palm Lake, and remainder (~400) retained at Dexter as broodstock.
   - A third planned stocking near **Grand Junction, Colorado** failed due to a power outage and backup generator malfunction at Dexter, resulting in loss of those targeted fish.
5. **Administrative/Institutional Concern:** The author notes a critical **breakdown in communication**, specifically that the lead station for this species (**Parker Office of Fisheries Assistance**) did not participate in the on-site salvage decision-making process. This is flagged as an area needing improvement in inter-agency coordination between [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[The Nature Conservancy]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
6. **Follow-up Expected:** The author indicates further documentation and decisions regarding the final disposition of salvaged fish are forthcoming.
