# Fish Fauna of the Thorn Forest on Pacific Slope of Mexico (Sonora & Sinaloa)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Poeciliidae

## Executive Summary
This document, derived from a 1988 letter by Dr. Robert Rush Miller (Curator of Fishes) to Dr. Daniel H. Janzen, summarizes the freshwater and estuarine fish fauna of the [[Thorn Forest]] biome on the Pacific slope of Mexico, spanning the states of [[Sonora]] and [[Sinaloa]]. The region extends from the lower [[Rio Yaqui]] south to the [[Rio Piaxtla]], with the Tropic of Cancer marking an approximate southern boundary. Thirteen families comprising approximately 34 species are documented, ranging from sea level to about 500 m elevation. The fauna is notable for its mix of northern-affinity primary freshwater fishes, Middle American/Neotropical secondary freshwater fishes, and marine-derived vicarious and peripheral species. Special emphasis is placed on the livebearer genus [[Poeciliopsis]], which includes a remarkable complex of unisexual (all-female) hybrid and gynogenetic forms studied extensively by Dr. Robert C. Schultz.

## Background & Description
While the source is primarily a faunal/biogeographic list rather than a morphological treatise, key taxonomic and reproductive distinctions are noted:

- **Primary Freshwater Fishes** (true freshwater, cannot tolerate salinity): Cyprinidae (minnows, 5 spp.), Catostomidae (suckers, 1 sp.), Ictaluridae (catfishes, 1 sp.) — predominantly of northern (Nearctic) affinity.
- **Secondary Freshwater Fishes** (tolerate low salinity): Poeciliidae (livebearers, 7 spp.) and Cichlidae (1 sp.) — of Middle American or Neotropical origin.
- **Vicarious Group** (marine-derived, now permanent freshwater residents): Clupeidae (herrings, 2 spp.), Atherinidae (silversides, 1 sp.), Syngnathidae (pipefishes, 1 sp.), Eleotridae (sleeper gobies, 2 spp.), Gobiidae (true gobies, 2 spp.).
- **Peripheral Group** (marine invaders, do not reproduce in freshwater): Elopidae (tenpounders, 1 sp.), Ariidae (marine catfishes, 1 sp.), Mugilidae (mullets, 3 spp.), Bothidae (lefteye flounders, 1 sp.), Soleidae (soles, 1 sp.).

The most morphologically and reproductively distinctive taxon is the genus **[[Poeciliopsis]]** (Poeciliidae), which alongside six recognized bisexual species includes four extraordinary **unisexual (all-female) forms**:
- Three are **diploid hybrids**, arising through interspecific hybridization between parental Poeciliopsis species.
- One is a **gynogenetic triploid**.

Notably, one of the naturally occurring unisexual "species" was successfully recreated in the laboratory via controlled hybridization of the appropriate parental species — an effort that took 12 years to accomplish, as documented by [[Robert C. Schultz]] (1969).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The thorn forest drainages studied include the [[Rio Yaqui]], [[Rio Fuerte]], [[Rio Sinaloa]], [[Rio Culiacan]], [[Rio San Lorenzo]], [[Rio Elota]], and [[Rio Piaxtla]], grouped biogeographically into two drainage regions (per Miller 1986):
- **Yaqui/Mayo drainage region**
- **Sinaloan Coastal drainage region**

Ecological zonation of the fauna reflects a gradient of salinity tolerance and freshwater dependency:

1. **Primary freshwater fishes** occupy headwater and upland freshwater habitats, generally isolated from marine influence, and show northern (Nearctic) zoogeographic affinities (e.g., *Agosia*, *Campostoma*, *Gila*, *Notropis*, *Catostomus*, *Ictalurus*).
2. **Secondary freshwater fishes** (Poeciliidae, Cichlidae) occupy similar freshwater habitats but can persist in slightly brackish conditions, reflecting Middle American/Neotropical zoogeographic origins.
3. **Vicarious species** occupy river mouths, estuaries, and permanent freshwater stretches near the coast (e.g., *Pseudophallus starksi* recorded in permanent fresh water of the Rio Quelite), representing marine lineages that have become freshwater residents.
4. **Peripheral species** are euryhaline marine fishes that penetrate short distances upstream into fresh water but require marine or estuarine conditions to reproduce, and typically have broad geographic distributions (e.g., *Mugil cephalus*, *Elops affinis*).

This structure illustrates a classic freshwater-to-marine ecological continuum along Pacific coastal drainages of the [[Sonoran Desert]]-adjacent thorn forest biome, governed by [[Zoogeography|zoogeographic affinity]] and salinity tolerance.

## Key Findings
- **34 species in 13 families** are documented from the Pacific thorn forest region of Sonora and Sinaloa, occurring from sea level to ~500 m elevation.
- Only **3 families** (Cyprinidae, Catostomidae, Ictaluridae) represent true (**primary**) freshwater fishes, mostly of northern zoogeographic affinity.
- **2 families** (Poeciliidae, Cichlidae) are **secondary freshwater fishes** with Middle American/Neotropical affinities and some salinity tolerance.
- **5 families** are classified as **vicarious** (marine-derived, now essentially freshwater): Clupeidae, Atherinidae, Syngnathidae, Eleotridae, Gobiidae.
- **5 families** are **peripheral** marine invaders that penetrate fresh water but do not reproduce there: Elopidae, Ariidae, Mugilidae, Bothidae, Soleidae.
- The genus **[[Poeciliopsis]]** stands out as the most biologically remarkable group, containing a complex of **unisexual all-female lineages** — three diploid hybrids and one gynogenetic triploid — originating via interspecific hybridization.
- [[Robert C. Schultz]]'s research program (summarized in Schultz 1977, *Evolutionary Biology* 10:277–331) on unisexual *Poeciliopsis* represents the key scientific reference for this fauna, including the successful laboratory recreation of a naturally occurring unisexual hybrid form.
- The regional fish fauna is drawn primarily from [[Robert Rush Miller]]'s 1986 survey (Table 1), which delineates two major drainage groupings: the **Yaqui/Mayo** and **Sinaloan Coastal** regions.
- The letter notes a lack of published literature on Mesoamerican dry forest fish biology, highlighting this region as understudied despite its unique zoogeographic mixture of temperate and tropical freshwater fish lineages.
