# Annual Report of Hydrobiological and Ichthyological Research Units in Brno, 1966

#ichthyology #hydrobiology #limnology #waterfowl #fisheries #zoobenthos #parasitology #czechoslovakia

## Executive Summary

This annual report summarizes research activities conducted during 1966 by multiple hydrobiological, ichthyological, fisheries, fish disease, and waterfowl research units affiliated with the [[Natural History Club]] in Brno, Czechoslovakia. The report covers work by university departments, Academy of Sciences institutes, museums, and agricultural research stations. Research spans aquatic invertebrate production ecology, fish population dynamics, waterfowl ecology, pond aquaculture, and fish parasitology across various freshwater habitats including streams, reservoirs, and carp ponds in [[Moravia]], the [[Beskydy Mountains]], and the [[Carpathian Mountains]].

## Background & Description

The report represents the first coordinated annual review of scientific institutions in Brno that consolidated around the Hydrobiological and Ichthyological Section of the Natural History Club in 1962. These institutions trace their origins to prominent scientists including Professor E. Babák, Professor O.V. Hykeš, academician V. Laufberger, and others who established Brno as a center for aquatic research. The report aims to facilitate information exchange, prevent duplication of effort, and promote international collaboration in aquatic sciences.

Participating institutions include:
- Chair of Zoology and Anthropology, [[University J.E. Purkyně]]
- Institute of Vertebrate Zoology, [[Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences]] (Ichthyological and Ornithological Departments)
- [[Moravian Museum]] Department of Zoology
- Chair of Fisheries and Hydrobiology, [[University of Agriculture]]
- Biological Station of the University of Agriculture at [[Lednice]]
- Chair of Parasitology and Invasive Diseases, Veterinary Faculty

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Research focused on multiple freshwater ecosystems:

**Running Waters**: Studies concentrated on brown trout streams including the [[Loutka River]], [[Svratka River]], [[Hadivka Brook]], and [[Rokytná River]]. The [[Loutka River]] showed average zoobenthos biomass of 13.5 g/m². In [[Hadivka Brook]] (average discharge 40 l/sec), brown trout spawning runs yielded 214 individuals in 1966 versus 95 in 1965, with yearly net production estimated at 26.9 g/m² (juvenile production 22.6 g/m², adult production 4.3 g/m²), representing 1.6% of mean biomass.

**Reservoir Impacts**: The [[Vir Reservoir]] on the [[Svratka River]] demonstrated significant downstream effects. Organic drift below the dam consisted of 76.2% planktonic individuals swept from the reservoir, with only 12.7% rheobiontic organisms. Plankton individuals remained viable 55 km downstream. The reservoir altered fish communities, increasing oligothermic species (particularly [[Brown Trout]]) while decreasing [[Chondrostoma nasus]] populations. Growth rates and condition factors increased in multiple species post-impoundment.

**Pond Ecosystems**: Carp pond research at [[Lednice]] examined productivity and aquaculture optimization. Nitrogen fertilization (ammonium sulphate) positively influenced natural food development and carp fry production. Carp fry populations significantly influenced aquatic biocenoses, consuming large [[Daphnia]] spp. within 2-3 weeks, followed by mass reproduction of [[Acanthocyclops vernalis]] and nanoplankton, with primary production increasing to 7-9 mg O₂/l/8 hours.

## Key Findings

**Aquatic Invertebrate Production**:
- [[Loutka River]] zoobenthos averaged 13.5 g/m² biomass
- Three-year quantitative measurements of zoobenthos and drift completed
- Organic drift in [[Svratka River]] below [[Vir Dam]] dominated by potamoplankton (76.2%) with only 12.7% rheobiontic organisms
- [[Rotatoria]] prevailed in streamline drift; [[Chironomidae]] and [[Ephemeroptera]] larvae abundant
- Two [[Beskydy Mountains]] trout brooks showed total zoobenthos biomass of 12.0 g/m² average
- Length-weight relationships established for main biocenosis components

**Fish Population Dynamics**:
- [[Brown Trout]] ([[Salmo trutta]]) biomass in [[Loutka River]] ranged 26-211 kg/ha across three sections
- [[Hadivka Brook]] spawning run: 214 brown trout trapped (vs. 95 in 1965)
- Net production: 26.9 g/m² (juvenile 22.6 g/m², adult 4.3 g/m²)
- [[Chondrostoma nasus]] in [[Rokytná River]]: 4.2-14.2 individuals per 100 m²; biomass 112.1 kg/ha; yield 11 kg/ha
- Sex ratio balanced in younger fish, more females in older age classes
- Maturity attained at 5-7 years; average absolute fertility 8,251 eggs (200-300 mm standard length)

**Reservoir Effects on Ichthyofauna**:
- [[Vir Reservoir]] increased oligothermic fish populations
- [[Brown Trout]] occurrence increased dramatically (previously practically null)
- [[Chondrostoma nasus]] populations decreased markedly
- Growth rates increased in multiple species, most notably brown trout
- Higher condition factors observed in all investigated species
- Total angler catches remained stable despite species composition shifts

**Lamprey Research**:
- Investigations on [[Lampetra danfordi]] and [[Lampetra gracilis]] relationships in [[Carpathian Mountains]]
- Studies on larval stage duration and metamorphosis
- [[Lampetra planeri]] and [[Lampetra mariae]] distribution north of Carpathians
- Evidence suggests [[Lampetra mariae]] spread through Baltic drainage from northeast
- Both species present between Warsaw and Cracow; only [[Lampetra planeri]] in Carpathian Mountains

**Pond Aquaculture**:
- Nitrogen fertilization (ammonium sulphate) increased natural food production
- Carp fry production 1.5-2.5 times higher in fertilized ponds
- [[Daphnia pulicaria]] populations 1.5-2.5 times higher with fertilization
- Zoobenthos numbers increased 2.7-3 times, biomass 1.5-1.8 times
- [[Chironomidae]] numbers increased 4.3-5 times, biomass 2.1-2.4 times
- Adverse effects on [[Tubificidae]]
- Carp fry consumed large [[Daphnia]] within 2-3 weeks
- Primary production reached 7-9 mg O₂/l/8 hours after filtrator consumption

**Coregonid Culture**:
- [[Coregonus lavaretus maraena]] study confirmed importance of large [[Cladocera]] in summer diet
- Fat content differences between juvenile/adult and male/female fish
- Nutritive value and food industry utilization potential assessed

**Waterfowl Ecology**:
- [[Mallard]] ([[Anas platyrhynchos]]) age structure, movements, mortality, migrations, and wintering quarters evaluated
- Body weight variations documented throughout year for both sexes
- [[Aythya ferina]] and [[Aythya fuligula]]: clutch size and breeding season factors determined
- Diurnal activity in [[Aythya ferina]] identified as endogenous
- [[Netta rufina]]: parasitic breeding frequent; clutch size and breeding losses quantified
- [[Graylag Goose]] ([[Anser anser]]) and [[Mallard]] breeding density in pollarded willows studied
- Monthly censuses of ~140 localities across Czechoslovakia (September-April)
- January 1966: coordinated census of entire [[Elbe River]] and [[Danube River]] waterfowl populations

**Fish Parasitology**:
- Toxicity testing of antiparasite baths (ammonia, chloro-amine, malachite green) on carp fry
- Lowest condition coefficient and resistance in spring months
- Malachite green (0.5 mg/l for 48 hours) effective against mycosis in aquarium fish
- [[Silurus glanis]] (sheatfish) fry parasites: [[Ichthyophthirius multifiliis]], [[Ancylodiscoides siluri]], [[Diplostomum spathaceum]], [[Proteocephalus osculatus]], [[Silurataenia siluri]], [[Piscicola geometra]]
- Aquatic snail distribution in [[Dyje River]] inundation area documented
- [[Galba truncatula]] occurrence confirmed

**Methodological Advances**:
- New quantitative sampling method for organic drift developed
- Electro-fishing techniques refined for population studies
- Experimental approaches to population density effects on zoobenthos planned
- Artificial breeding experiments initiated for [[Mallard]]
- Cage experiments for benthic organism importance in carp fry diet
