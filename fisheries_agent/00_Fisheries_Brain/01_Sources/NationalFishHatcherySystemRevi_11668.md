# National Fish Hatchery System Review: Correspondence, Proposals, and Population Viability Modelling Materials (1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This compiled document, dated 1994, is a set of correspondence, meeting records, proposals, and technical reference material prepared for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) [[National Fish Hatchery System]] Review. Its central theme is a proposed shift in the role of federal hatcheries from producing sport and commercial fish toward [[Ecosystem Management]], endangered species propagation, and restoration of native stocks. Key elements include the restoration of [[Lake Trout]] in the Great Lakes, a proposed native mussel holding and propagation facility at [[Neosho National Fish Hatchery]], and a set of recommendations on stocking policy, mitigation, tribal roles, and genetics. The appendices also include the [[Population and Habitat Viability Analysis]] (PHVA) reference packet, a data form for the [[Mississippi Sandhill Crane]], and a technical paper describing the [[VORTEX]] simulation model (Lacy 1993).

## Background & Description

**Correspondence and review framework.** A letter dated 31 May 1994 from the FWS Fort Snelling office to Dr. Fred P. Meyer records the Great Lakes/Big Rivers Region's input to the National Fish Hatchery System Review. A memorandum from the Assistant Director, Fisheries, states that the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation would conduct an independent assessment through a panel of 15 scientists, co-chaired by Dr. Robert Putz and Dr. Fred Meyer. The panel was to deliver evaluations and recommendations to the FWS Director by the end of September 1994. The panel would not establish policy or close facilities.

**Meeting records.** Appendix A is the group memory summary of a 5 May 1994 meeting in Region 3. It lists field office and regional office participants and records discussion on issues A through Q, covering mollusk rearing, lake trout restoration, Federal Aid stocking criteria, recreational and tribal stocking, genetic engineering, and broodstock testing.

**Review objectives.** The review was to:
- Identify options for future hatchery directions within ecosystem management.
- Recommend hatchery roles, policies, and practices that protect wild stocks and native species.
- Evaluate the legal and regulatory basis of the system and its operational effectiveness.
- Recommend operational changes, transitional strategies, or alternative funding for hatcheries not contributing to the desired direction.

**Panel and reference materials.** Appendix B contains a Captive Breeding Specialist Group (CBSG/IUCN/SSC) PHVA workshop packet (December 1993). It covers workshop objectives, preparation, data needs, meeting logistics, and cost estimates. The CBSG estimated each PHVA workshop at **$10,000 to $15,000**. The packet also states that CBSG had conducted PHVA workshops for **more than 50 species in 22 countries**.

**Associated proposals.** Appendix C is a FY96 construction worksheet for a native mussel facility at Neosho NFH (MO), with a total project cost of **$550,000** (direct $407,000; indirect $143,000). Appendix D is an unreadable OCR budget/cost page. Appendix E is a briefing statement on native mussel status, prepared by Charles Surprenant on 5/3/92. Appendix F is a proposal to change the lake trout production strategy, dated 18 April 1994. Appendix G is a 25 May 1994 summary of early life-history (egg) stocking of lake trout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lake trout in the Great Lakes.** The text describes lake trout as a Great Lakes native in serious decline. Four of the Region's six hatcheries were involved in restoration. Hatchery-reared yearlings show high survival after planting, but they are impaired in their ability to locate suitable spawning sites. Hatchery fish tend to spawn inshore on shallow, unstable sites. Sea lamprey is described as a secondary impactor, with harvest the major issue. Stocking has been reported to have restored natural reproduction in Lake Superior, but the document states that success has not been achieved in Lakes Michigan and Huron. In Lake Michigan, Surprenant's reference to lake trout stocking in areas without reproduction is noted as a view held by some sources.

**Native mussels and zebra mussels.** The briefing statement reports that of 297 freshwater mussel species native to the U.S. and Canada, **71.7%** are endangered, threatened, or of special concern, **23.6%** are stable, and **7.1%** are possibly extinct. It projects that **65 species** may become extinct within 10 years. In Region 3, of **94** native mussel species, **6** are possibly extinct, **16** endangered, **9** threatened, **19** of special concern, and **44** stable, so **53%** are extinct, endangered, threatened, or of special concern. Zebra mussels have covered most native mussels in the [[Illinois River]], where mussels are reported to be dying. The project justification cites the National Biological Survey prediction that **20 unionid species** would become extinct from the zebra mussel invasion.

**Mississippi Sandhill Crane.** The data form describes a wild population confined to Jackson County, Mississippi, estimated at **80-90 cranes** (1989 census). Preferred habitat is savanna and openings at swamp edges. Savanna declined from **74% to 14%** between 1942 and 1981 in the 6,475 ha analysed. The form states that **19,000 acres** were placed under the Mississippi Sandhill Crane National Wildlife Refuge by 1990, with a projected goal of **22,000 acres**. Winter counts recorded **2.3 juveniles per 100 adults**. Mean age of first reproduction was **3 years for males** and **4 years for females**, based on Florida sandhill data. The captive clutch size was reported as **1.7**.

**Whooping crane modelling.** Lacy (1993) uses the [[Whooping Crane]] as a worked example. The wild population grew at a mean exponential rate of **r = 0.040** (1938-1991), with annual SD(r) of **0.141**. Life-table analysis predicted **r = 0.052**, and VORTEX projected **r = 0.046** (SD 0.081). The simulated 1991 population was **151 ± 123**, against an observed **N = 146**, starting from **18 cranes** in 1938.

## Key Findings

**Policy direction for federal hatcheries (letter and meeting records)**
- The Service proposes that federal hatcheries focus on restoration ecology, holding and propagating native, candidate, and listed aquatic species, and acting as "zoos" during exotic invasions before reintroduction.
- A PHVA should precede hatchery propagation of any species.
- The shift away from producing sport or commercial fish is expected to be incremental and may take about a decade. The 5-year training transition is also noted.
- Future hatcheries need more genetics expertise, evaluation of stocking success, and feedback loops. The workforce should be more diverse, with more women and minorities.
- The review should involve a forum of all interested parties to set ecosystem goals.
- Federal Aid stocking (e.g., Dingell-Johnson projects) should be reviewed for conflicts with ecosystem restoration. Preparing policy or criteria for this review is proposed.

**Lake trout restoration**
- A new strategy would produce larger fish (10 per pound) with an expected **three to six times better survivability**, with implementation targeted for August 1994.
- The proposal would reduce yearling production from **5.3 million** (20-25/lb) to **3.5 million** (10-20/lb), with about **275,000 lbs** of yearlings in future production. Minnesota agreed to accept **half the number** of fish if they were doubled in size.
- Egg stocking in artificial turf incubators began in fall 1992 (Green Bay FRO stocked **2.3 million eggs** on Jacksonport Reef in 1992; Alpena FRO stocked **1.6 million eggs** off Grindstone City in 1993). The plan calls for **4 million eggs per year** over six years, alternating between Lakes Michigan and Huron.
- Sanctuaries have been established in key spawning areas in Lakes Michigan, Superior, and Huron. Tribal and state harvest has been reduced.

**Native mussel propagation at Neosho NFH**
- The FY96 proposal would build raceways (8' x 80' x 4' deep), quarantine areas to prevent zebra mussel contamination, and a reuse pumping station. Total cost is **$550,000**, ranked **third** of all proposed construction in Region 3.
- The Service's strategy depends on zebra mussel populations crashing after high densities, allowing reintroduction. The document notes that this strategy is contingent on funding.

**Stocking policy and Federal Aid (5 May 1994 meeting)**
- Federal Aid does not currently use FWS criteria when approving Sport Fish Restoration stocking projects. Sport Fish Restoration funds pay for nearly all rearing and stocking in IL, IN, MI, MO, OH, and WI, but not IA.
- In states, **90%** of hatchery output goes to maintenance stocking (Paul Pajak).
- The regional Assistant Director recommended that Federal Aid adopt ecosystem criteria for sport fish proposals, while noting political opposition.
- Recreational stocking should rarely occur, except for restoration and subsistence, and stocking should not supplement overharvested waters without management.
- Put-and-take stocking should be limited to education programs, lost habitats, mobility-impaired anglers, and urban settings.

**Tribal and trust responsibilities**
- Hatcheries should serve as training centers for tribes, which should be encouraged to develop their own hatcheries.
- Stocking for recreational angling on Indian reservations is supported under the trust responsibility.

**Genetics and broodstock**
- Broodstock is tested for disease resistance but not for long-term fitness.
- Genetic engineering is generally considered incompatible with the ecosystem focus, with limited small-scale exceptions (e.g., possible use for BKD resistance in salmon).

**PHVA and VORTEX modelling**
- PHVA workshops are described as building consensus, assessing extinction risk, and testing management options with simulation models. Workshop costs are **$10,000 to $15,000**.
- VORTEX (Version 5.1) is a Monte Carlo stochastic simulation model covering demographic stochasticity, environmental variation, catastrophes, genetic drift (Recessive Lethals and Heterosis inbreeding models), density dependence, harvest, supplementation, and metapopulations. Lacy (1993) notes that it is most applicable to long-lived, low-fecundity species such as mammals, birds, and reptiles.
- The sample simulation in the appendix reports **86%** extinction probability for Population 1 at year 100 (14 of 100 survived), and **79%** extinction for the metapopulation (21 of 100 survived). The authors note that the sample is illustrative, not a field estimate.

**Open issues identified in the meeting records**
- Whether Federal responsibility for mollusk holding ends in extinction, or in reintroduction, must be clarified.
- Dollar sources for hatchery work are unresolved; partners should contribute.
- Whether bringing in alien lake trout strains is adverse remains unresolved. The Senecaville strain is reported to withstand sea lamprey better than the Marquette strain.
- Hatchery quality is measured at release ("what goes out the door"); recruitment to habitat is not evaluated.
