# Gila Specimen Photograph Correspondence, 1975

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila

## Executive Summary

This is a one-page cover letter dated 30 December 1975 from [[James E Deacon]], Chairman of the Department of Biological Sciences at the [[University Of Nevada Las Vegas]], to Dr. [[Robert R Miller]] of the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]]. It accompanies an enclosed photograph of a [[Gila]] fish captured by seine at a creek on 1 August 1975. The letter is a courtesy and documentation exchange between institutions. It contains no quantitative research data, no formal taxonomic identification, and no analysis. The second page of the scan is unreadable.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Personal business letter on departmental letterhead, with a typed signature block and reference initials ("WJED:ms").
- **Sender:** James E. Deacon, Chairman, Department of Biological Sciences, University of Nevada Las Vegas (Las Vegas, Nevada).
- **Recipient:** Dr. Robert R. Miller, University of Michigan Museum of Zoology (Ann Arbor, Michigan). The salutation is "Dear Bob," which suggests a personal professional acquaintance.
- **Enclosure:** One photograph, described as "as stated."
- **Subject specimen:** A fish of the genus [[Gila]]. The species is not named in the legible text.
- **Capture method:** The fish was in a seine net. The OCR text is garbled, but it appears to describe a seine of approximately 12 ft by 6 ft with 1/8-inch mesh.
- **Collection event:** Taken at Shin0umu Creek on 1 August 1975. The OCR is ambiguous, so the creek name may be misread.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter has little ecological content. The only environmental information is:

- The specimen came from a flowing creek, which is consistent with the typical freshwater habitat of the genus [[Gila]], though the text does not state this directly.
- The fish was photographed while still in the seine, and the text says it was "briefly" lifted out of the water for pictures. This suggests the fish was handled minimally and returned, although the letter does not say so explicitly.
- No water chemistry, habitat measurements, population counts, or abundance data are provided.

## Key Findings

1. **Specimen documentation:** A photograph of a [[Gila]] specimen, taken at a creek on 1 August 1975, was sent to the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology.
2. **Purpose of the exchange:** The sender offered duplicate prints and colored slides on request. This suggests an informal sharing of visual records between museum and university staff.
3. **Capture and handling:** The fish was taken in a seine and lifted out of the water only briefly for photography.
4. **Limitations:** The letter does not identify the species, give specimen counts, state a collection purpose, or describe any voucher deposit or catalogue number. Page 2 of the scan is unreadable, so any content it contains is unknown.
