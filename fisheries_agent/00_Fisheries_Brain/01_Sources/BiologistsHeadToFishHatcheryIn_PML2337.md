# Dexter Hatchery Fish Die-Off After Backup Water Valve Error

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A newspaper report from the *Roswell (N.M.) Daily Record* (Wednesday, April 8, 1987) describes the deaths of endangered fish at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. About two weeks before publication, an electrician repairing an incubator mistakenly turned off the hatchery's backup water valve. The hatchery's backup supply is town municipal water, which is treated with chlorine, a chemical deadly to fish. Officials said the loss did not push the species toward extinction but was a setback to recovery. Hatchery manager Jensen called for a dedicated backup water system and a standby generator for the holding house.

## Background & Description

The Dexter facility is described as the only hatchery in the world with the sole purpose of preserving, maintaining and producing endangered or threatened species. It currently holds 16 endangered species collected across the United States and Mexico. The lost fish included [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]], which were described as very rare. They came from an upper basin population distinct from other stocks being raised at Dexter.

Key people quoted in the article:
- **Jensen**: hatchery manager, who described the deaths as a disaster.
- **[[Galen Buterbaugh]]**: director of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in Denver, who said the loss was "a blow to the program."
- **Solting**: a senior fisheries biologist with the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], who said the loss "cuts to the core" of conservation safety.

The article does not state how many fish died.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery's primary water supply is natural ground water, pumped into 48 ponds and tanks through the hatchery's own system. The backup supply is municipal water, which is chlorinated. Chlorine is lethal to fish, so the switch to municipal water when the backup valve was closed by mistake is central to the event. The article presents hatchery production as a safeguard for wild populations. Solting warned that removing too many fish from the wild could jeopardize the wild population.

## Key Findings

- **Cause of loss:** About two weeks before publication, an electrician repairing an incubator mistakenly turned off the backup water valve. Chlorinated municipal water then reached the fish.
- **Species affected:** The deaths involved endangered razorback suckers and Colorado squawfish from an upper basin population that is genetically distinct from other captive stocks.
- **Scale of impact:** Buterbaugh said the loss did not push the species toward extinction but also did not advance recovery. The exact number of fish lost is not reported.
- **Infrastructure gap:** Jensen said he had asked for a better standby system for about 10 years. He identified the lack of a standby generator for the holding house as the operation's weakest point.
- **Recommendations and requests:** Jensen called for a dedicated backup water system for the holding house and hatchery incubators, and for funding that matches the demands placed on the facility.
- **Facility significance:** Dexter holds 16 endangered species from across the United States and Mexico, making any loss of stock a conservation concern.
