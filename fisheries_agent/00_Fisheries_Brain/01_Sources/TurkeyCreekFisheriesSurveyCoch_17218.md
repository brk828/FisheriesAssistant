# Turkey Creek Fisheries Survey, Cochise County, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Centrarchidae #invasive-species

## Executive Summary

A fisheries survey conducted November 14-15, 1997 along [[Turkey Creek]] on the west side of the [[Chiricahua Mountains]], [[Cochise County]], Arizona documented four fish species. The survey was motivated by reports of [[Fathead Minnow]] (Pimephales promelas) in Lodge Pond. Sampling confirmed the presence of this non-native species in both Lodge Pond and upper Turkey Creek, along with [[Green Sunfish]] (Lepomis cyanellus), another invasive species. Native species [[Yaqui Chub]] (Gila purpurea) and [[Longfin Dace]] (Agosia chrysogaster) were also documented. The presence of invasive species poses significant threats to native fish populations.

## Background & Description

The survey was conducted by Arizona State University personnel (Brian Bagley and Patti Sowka) across multiple sampling localities along Turkey Creek and associated water bodies on and adjacent to El Coronado Ranch (EC Ranch) and Forest Service land. Sampling locations included:

- Turkey Creek at 0.4, 1.0, and 1.2 miles downstream of end of F.S. road 41
- Turkey Creek at border of EC Ranch and Forest Service land
- Turkey Creek 0.5 miles downstream of Guest House ponds
- [[Turkey Pen Creek]] 0.5 miles above EC Ranch road
- Lodge Pond, EC Ranch
- Lilly Pad Pond below Lodge Pond
- Pond adjacent to horse pasture 100m downstream from Lodge Pond
- Guest House Ponds (upper and lower), EC Ranch
- Big Tank, EC Ranch

Sampling methods included gas-powered Smith-Root DC backpack electrofisher, dip nets, seines, and minnow traps deployed for 24 hours.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Turkey Creek contains several natural falls that act as barriers to upstream fish movement during most flow conditions. This barrier system makes it unlikely that [[Fathead Minnow]] colonized the upper creek naturally, suggesting deliberate stocking near the top of Turkey Creek as the probable introduction mechanism.

The creek system includes both natural stream habitat and artificial impoundments (ponds and tanks) on EC Ranch. Big Tank receives water diverted from Turkey Creek, creating connectivity between natural and artificial habitats that facilitates spread of invasive species.

While some native fish species can coexist with [[Fathead Minnow]] for extended periods (as demonstrated at Boyce Thompson Arboretum where desert pupfish, Gila topminnow, and fathead minnow have coexisted for more than a decade), their presence remains concerning for native fish conservation.

## Key Findings

**Species Composition:**
Four fish species were collected during the survey:
- [[Yaqui Chub]] (Gila purpurea) - native
- [[Longfin Dace]] (Agosia chrysogaster) - native
- [[Fathead Minnow]] (Pimephales promelas) - non-native
- [[Green Sunfish]] (Lepomis cyanellus) - non-native

**Fathead Minnow Distribution and Abundance:**
- Confirmed present in Lodge Pond, EC Ranch
- Present near upper end of permanent water in Turkey Creek, 0.4 miles downstream from end of F.S. road 41
- Low numbers in Turkey Creek proper
- In Lodge Pond, fathead minnow outnumber [[Yaqui Chub]] 5:1, indicating establishment and potential competitive displacement

**Green Sunfish Distribution and Impact:**
- Locally abundant in Turkey Creek
- Very abundant in Big Tank (fed by water diverted from Turkey Creek)
- Due to predatory nature, likely to have noticeable negative impact on native fish fauna

**Conservation Concerns:**
The presence of both invasive species poses threats to native fish populations through competition ([[Fathead Minnow]]) and predation ([[Green Sunfish]]). The mechanism of introduction remains unclear but deliberate stocking is suspected given the presence of natural barriers to upstream movement.
