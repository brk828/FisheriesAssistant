## Executive Summary

This document outlines a proposed interagency monitoring and research program for two endangered Colorado River Basin fishes—the [[Razorback Sucker]] and the [[Bonytail]]—in [[Lake Mohave]]. Initiated by the National Biological Service (NBS) in 1996, the program aims to assess the success of ongoing stocking efforts for both species by standardizing and expanding existing monitoring protocols rather than replacing them. The plan integrates passive and active capture methods, PIT-tag scanning, and a proposed telemetry research component (using surrogate "reservoir chub" for tag-attachment trials) to better understand bonytail movement and seasonal availability. The program is structured around bimonthly field surveys, standardized data collection, and a Core Work Group comprising NBS, ASU, FWS, BOR, AGF, NDOW, and NPS. A draft monitoring protocol and summary report with funding recommendations were scheduled for completion by September 1996.

## Background & Description

While this source is primarily a program/methods document rather than a morphological study, it specifies several biometric and tagging criteria used to differentiate size classes and monitor individuals:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]**
  - Size-class threshold set at **450 mm** — individuals **under 450 mm** are measured (length, weight) and scanned for PIT tags and other external tags; sex is recorded when determinable.
  - Adult razorback suckers (presumably ≥450 mm) are recorded by count, PIT tag number, and possible sex, but not necessarily measured/weighed to reduce handling stress.
  - All fish are scanned for **PIT tags** and examined for **external tags** as part of standard processing.

- **[[Bonytail]]**
  - All captured individuals are weighed and measured, and fitted with PIT tags if not already tagged.
  - A surrogate species — referred to as "reservoir chub" (likely roundtail/bonytail-complex fish) — was proposed as a stand-in for sonic transmitter/telemetry attachment trials, numbering 10 individuals.
  - Target handling time for tagging/processing (net-to-release) was **under 3 minutes**, with a goal of avoiding removal of the fish from water entirely where feasible.

No detailed external morphological descriptions (coloration, fin ray counts, meristics) are provided in this source; the document is methodological/administrative in nature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the lower Colorado River, serving as habitat for remnant and reintroduced populations of two federally listed native fishes.
- **Spawning behavior**: Both target species form **spawning aggregations** at identifiable sites; razorback sucker spawning sites were prioritized using **helicopter surveillance data** provided by the Bureau of Reclamation, indicating spatially discrete, trackable spawning locations.
- **Seasonality**: Razorback sucker sampling was scheduled to begin **late January**, coinciding with spawning season, and continue bimonthly for a minimum of 10 weeks; bonytail sampling and telemetry work were designed to determine **seasonal habitat use** beyond the spawning period, since bonytail are described as difficult to locate at other times of year.
- **Population status context**: Both species are subjects of active **stocking/reintroduction programs**, implying depleted or non-self-sustaining wild populations in Lake Mohave — consistent with their status as [[Endangered Colorado River Fish]].
- **Interspecific handling consideration**: The use of a non-target "reservoir chub" surrogate for telemetry attachment trials reflects conservation caution around handling stress in the rarer, more sensitive bonytail.

## Key Findings

- **Program Rationale**: A combined, structured interagency monitoring effort was judged the most effective and economical way to assess both the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]] stocking programs simultaneously, rather than running separate species-specific efforts.
- **Sampling Design**: Bimonthly 3–4 day field trips using **seining, fyke nets, and trammel nets**; **electrofishing explicitly excluded**, likely due to stress/injury risk to sensitive native fish.
- **Fish Welfare Protocols**: Net checks mandated on a maximum **6-hour cycle** to minimize stress and mortality; razorback sucker collection at any single spawning site capped at **2 consecutive days**; fish held in live cars to avoid repeat handling within a trip.
- **Bonytail Disposition Uncertainty**: Three possible dispositions were under consideration for captured bonytail — release, retention for propagation, and use in research (telemetry) — with propagation-bound fish recommended for temporary release in **Yuma Cove** pending transport logistics coordinated with **Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery**.
- **Telemetry Research Component**: A dedicated proposal for sonic-tag telemetry on bonytail (or surrogate reservoir chub) was scheduled for Core Work Group review by mid-February 1996, with proposal finalization and tag-attachment training by mid-April 1996, aiming to refine future sampling site selection based on tracked movement data.
- **Data Management**: Standardized field data (fish counts, PIT tag numbers, lengths, weights, sex when possible, sampler ID, GPS coordinates, time/date, weather, gear type) would be logged in a spreadsheet format compatible with ASU's existing database, backed up frequently, and shared with participating agencies on request.
- **Institutional Framework**: A "Core Work Group" of designated leads was established across NBS, ASU, FWS, BOR, AGF, NDOW, and NPS to coordinate funding, permits, field logistics, and technical support.
- **Regulatory Compliance**: All fish handling was required to follow **Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) 1988 guidelines**, with the potential need for bonytail-specific handling protocols pending FWS guidance.
- **Deliverables Timeline**: A draft monitoring protocol/report was due to the Core Work Group by **September 1, 1996**, with a finalized report and identification of FY97–99 funding sources due by **September 30, 1996**.
