# Alamo Lake Tilapia Survey (June 1997)

## Executive Summary
This trip report documents a collaborative fish-sampling effort by Arizona State University (ASU) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) on June 23–24, 1997, at [[Alamo Lake]], La Paz County, Arizona. The survey aimed to verify a reported occurrence of nest-building [[Tilapia]] in the reservoir. Multiple nesting sites were confirmed visually, and juvenile Tilapia specimens were collected and preserved for species identification, though adult specimens were not definitively confirmed to species level. A broader community of native and non-native freshwater fishes was also documented via trammel and gill netting across three distinct coves.

## Background & Description
- **Nest structure**: Circular depressions approximately **1 m in diameter** and **0.75 m deep**, consistent with substrate-nesting behavior typical of [[Tilapia]] (Cichlidae), which are known for constructing and guarding bowl-shaped spawning nests.
- **Adult Tilapia**: Species-level identification not confirmed in the field; adults fled upon approach of survey personnel, preventing capture and morphological assessment.
- **Juvenile Tilapia**: Collected via 25' seine from nesting areas; specimens preserved for formal taxonomic identification at the ASU fish collection.
- Other captured species retained standard diagnostic morphology for their taxa (e.g., [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Common Carp]], [[Threadfin Shad]]) — no morphological anomalies noted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Nesting habitat**: Nests were concentrated in the first major cove upstream of Spencer's Wash (near a boat launch), suggesting preference for sheltered, shallow littoral zones with soft substrate suitable for excavation — a classic reproductive niche for cichlid species like Tilapia.
- **Habitat variability**: A second cove near Cholla Road showed abandoned/exposed nests, likely due to reservoir water-level drawdown, indicating sensitivity of nesting success to hydrological fluctuation.
- **Absence in unnamed cove**: No nests were found in an unnamed cove upstream of Rawhide Wash on the opposite side of the reservoir, suggesting patchy or localized distribution of nesting Tilapia within Alamo Lake rather than lake-wide colonization.
- **Co-occurring fish community**: The Tilapia nesting coves were shared habitat with several established sport and non-native fish species, including [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Common Carp]], and [[Threadfin Shad]] — reflecting a mixed native/introduced fish assemblage typical of Southwestern U.S. reservoirs.
- **Trophic/reproductive interaction potential**: The presence of nest-guarding Tilapia alongside centrarchids (e.g., bluegill, largemouth bass) and catfish raises questions about potential competitive interactions for spawning habitat and food resources within [[Alamo Lake]].

## Key Findings
- Approximately **12 Tilapia nests** were visually confirmed in the primary survey cove (upstream of Spencer's Wash), supporting prior reports of nest-building Tilapia in Alamo Lake.
- **Juvenile Tilapia** were successfully collected and preserved from the nesting area for positive taxonomic identification, to be housed in the **Arizona State University fish collection**.
- **One adult Tilapia** was captured via hook-and-line (bait: worms) in the Cholla Road cove, though this was insufficient for full species confirmation.
- Netting effort (150' trammel net, two experimental gill nets, 25' seine) across three cove sites yielded a diverse catch:
  - Channel catfish (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — 8 total across sites
  - Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) — 4 total
  - Bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*) — 2
  - Flathead catfish (*Pilodictis olivaris*) — 1
  - Largemouth bass (*Micropterus salmoides*) — 1
  - Redear sunfish (*Lepomis microlophus*) — 1
  - Threadfin shad (*Dorosoma petenense*) — 1
- Evidence of **nest abandonment** correlated with reservoir water-level decline in at least one cove, suggesting environmental/hydrological drivers affect Tilapia reproductive success at this site.
- No Tilapia nests were detected in the Rawhide Wash-adjacent cove, indicating non-uniform distribution of the species within the reservoir.
- This report establishes a baseline record of a likely **non-native Tilapia introduction/establishment** in Alamo Lake, warranting further monitoring for invasive potential and ecological impact on native and sport fish populations.
