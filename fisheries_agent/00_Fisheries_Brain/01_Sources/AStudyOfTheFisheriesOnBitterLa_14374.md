# A Study of the Fisheries on Bitter Lake NWR

#ichthyology #fisheries-management #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #warmwater-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This 1983 study by [[Theophilus D. Inslee]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] investigated persistent failures in establishing self-sustaining sport fish populations at [[Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge]] (NWR) in New Mexico. Despite decades of stocking warmwater species—primarily [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), and various sunfishes—into six shallow man-made lakes (Units 3, 5, 6, 7, 15, 16), fish populations remained poor with minimal carryover. The study monitored water quality parameters (dissolved oxygen, pH, temperature, total dissolved solids) from June through October 1983 and conducted population sampling following a stocking of 5,400 channel catfish. Results revealed multiple severe limiting factors: extreme salinity (TDS reaching 35 ppt in Unit 5, equivalent to seawater), critically low dissolved oxygen caused by dense [[Chara]] beds, very limited deep-water habitat (estimated <10% of surface area suitable for fish), high summer temperatures, and heavy predation by waterfowl. Marked stocked fish suffered high mortality (estimated >80%), exhibited lesions and starvation, and were largely absent from creels. The study concluded that these impoundments, designed primarily for waterfowl habitat, are fundamentally unsuitable for sustaining quality sport fisheries under existing conditions.

## Background & Description

[[Bitter Lake NWR]] consists of six interconnected shallow impoundments (Units 3, 5, 6, 7, 15, 16) totaling approximately 586 surface acres in [[Chaves County]], [[New Mexico]]. The units were constructed primarily as waterfowl habitat with water supplied from springs and upflow areas within the lakes, supplemented by runoff during heavy rainfall. Spring flow is affected by surrounding irrigation, decreasing during spring/summer and increasing in fall/winter. A 1970 Fishery Management Program (Appendix I) identified numerous data gaps and problems including [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) control, water depth limitations, [[Chara]] control, and need for more saline-resistant species.

Physical characteristics at study initiation (1983):
- **Unit 3**: 136 acres, almost dry
- **Unit 5**: 98 acres, at least one foot below full, completely choked with *Chara* sp.
- **Unit 6**: ~80% dry, not studied
- **Unit 7**: 109 acres, one foot below full; 3-5 feet deep along dike (75-125 feet wide), remainder 12-20 inches gradually shallowing
- **Unit 15**: 79 acres, nine inches below full; mostly <24 inches deep except five spring holes (5-8 feet deep)
- **Unit 16**: 82 acres, >90% dry, not studied

All units featured profuse aquatic vegetation, primarily *Chara* sp. with some *Najas* sp. and *Potamogeton* sp., filling the entire water column where present. Unit 15 had open water along south and east dikes (30-100 feet wide) and around spring areas; elsewhere vegetation was removed by carp activity, leaving silty water.

Historical stocking efforts included channel catfish, largemouth bass, [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[White Crappie]] (*Pomoxis annularis*), [[Walleye]] (*Stizostedion vitreum*), and [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*). [[White Bass]] (*Roccus chrysops*), introduced in 1959, was the most successful species, apparently reproducing naturally. Carp dominated all units despite previous eradication attempts (1960, 1966, 1967).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Water Quality Parameters

**Total Dissolved Solids (TDS)**: The most critical limiting factor. Initial readings (June 23, 1983) and progression through October 6:

- **Unit 5**: 21,870 ppm increasing to 35,240 ppm (35 ppt, equivalent to ocean salinity)
- **Unit 7**: 12,110 ppm increasing to 16,690 ppm (16 ppt)
- **Unit 15**: 7,420 ppm increasing to 15,820 ppm

Spring water in Unit 15 measured 3,700 ppm lower TDS than static lake water, indicating concentration by evaporation. According to Allen (1969) and Perry (1969), Unit 7 and 15 TDS levels were at the upper end of tolerance before growth is critically reduced in channel catfish; Unit 5 exceeded tolerance for all but euryhaline species. The 1970 baseline showed much lower TDS (Unit 3: 11,090 ppm; Unit 5: 9,950 ppm; Unit 7: 8,852 ppm; Unit 15: 7,420 ppm), attributed to seasonal variation.

**Dissolved Oxygen (DO)**: Identified as "probably the greatest limiting factor." Extreme diurnal variation due to photosynthesis and respiration:
- Dawn readings: 0 ppm at bottom, 0.2 ppm at surface
- Afternoon readings: supersaturated in upper waters
- Bottom waters remained low even during daytime in weedy areas
- Weed-free areas also affected due to small size and shape

Heavy weed growth created anoxic conditions at night and early morning, rendering most surface area unsuitable for sport fish. Estimated habitable area: <10 acres in Unit 15, <4 acres in Unit 7.

**Temperature**: Ranged 23°C (start) to 31.5°C (September 9), declining to lower 20s by mid-October. Within optimal range for warmwater species growth and reproduction.

**pH**: Remained alkaline (8.1-9.5) throughout study, well-buffered, never reaching limiting levels despite variation from heavy weed growth.

**Chemical Composition** (1970 baseline): High concentrations of sodium (1,609-2,299 ppm), calcium (661-1,043 ppm), magnesium (134-274 ppm), sulfate (2,074-3,147 ppm), and chlorides (3,182-5,895 ppm). Chlorides were identified as detrimental, causing higher egg mortality in [[Centrarchidae]], supporting atypical invertebrate fauna, and potentially causing physiological stress in hatchery fish from less saline waters.

### Habitat Limitations

- **Depth**: Approximately 50% of water <1-2 feet deep; maximum depths 5-6 feet only near dikes
- **Substrate**: Silt and organic detritus up to 2 feet deep in many areas
- **Vegetation**: Dense *Chara* growth reaching ~2 feet height, leaving ≤2 feet open water above plants in deeper areas
- **Turbidity**: Water colorless and very clear (3-5 feet transparency) due to salt precipitation, stimulating excessive *Chara* growth

### Biological Community

**Fish Species Present** (1968-1969 sampling, 37 experimental gill nets + 27 trap nets):
- **Dominant**: Carp (small, stunted, poor condition; thousands of large individuals observed)
- **Present**: Channel catfish, white bass, green sunfish, bluegill, white crappie, largemouth bass, [[Gizzard Shad]] (*Dorosoma cepedianum*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*), [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia* sp.), [[Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon* sp.), [[Killifish]] (*Fundulus* sp.)
- **Reproducing**: Carp, green sunfish, gizzard shad, cyprinodontids, white bass
- **Not reproducing significantly**: Largemouth bass, other centrarchids

**Predators**: Heavy predation pressure from waterfowl and other predators:
- October 21, 1983: 36 [[American White Pelican]]s observed fishing spring areas
- November 4, 1983: 547 pelicans present
- Also: [[Great Blue Heron]]s, [[Great Egret]]s, numerous duck species (mallards, gadwall, teal, etc.), 5,000 [[Sandhill Crane]]s
- Aquatic: Large populations of turtles (snapping, red-eared)
- Weed reduction by overwintering ducks left fingerlings vulnerable in shallow water

**Parasites**: [[Argulus]] sp. (fish louse) most prevalent on fish observed in creels.

## Key Findings

### Stocking and Mortality

**August 1983 Stocking**:
- **Unit 7**: 1,080 channel catfish (613 lbs total); 10% marked by dorsal spine removal
- **Unit 15**: 4,320 channel catfish (2,454 lbs total); 10% marked
- **Unit 5**: No stocking due to extreme TDS

**Mortality Observations**:

| Date  | Unit | Dead Fish | Marked Fish | % Marked |
|-------|------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| 8/25  | 15   | 68        | 9           | 13.2%    |
| 8/25  | 7    | 33        | 5           | 15.2%    |
| 8/29  | 15   | 457       | 49          | 10.7%    |

- Mortalities began within 2 days of stocking
- Fish exhibited lesions (estimated 20% within 2 weeks in Unit 7), starvation (empty stomachs), poor body condition, and stress (surfacing early morning)
- Fish schooled and remained lethargic in stocking areas (canal in Unit 7, open water in Unit 15)
- Unit 7 fish would not bite despite heavy fishing pressure
- Estimated >80% mortality of stocked fish based on observations, though accurate counts impossible due to predator removal

### Population Sampling (Gill Nets, August-September 1983)

**Unit 15** (before stocking, 8/20):
- 2 nets, 24 hours: 9 channel catfish, 15 carp, 40 green sunfish

**Unit 15** (after stocking, 8/30):
- 2 nets, 24 hours: 45 channel catfish (6 marked = 13.3%), 3 carp, 12 green sunfish
- 24 fish (53%) showed lesions

**Unit 7** (8/31):
- 2 nets, 24 hours: 0 channel catfish, 0 carp, 59 green sunfish

**Unit 5** (9/1):
- 2 nets, 24 hours: 0 fish of any species captured

Body condition: Carp and green sunfish normal or above; channel catfish poor. Largest green sunfish only 2 ounces (too small for creel). Poor catfish condition attributed to overstocking relative to limited habitable water and available food.

### Creel Observations

- **Heaviest fishing pressure**: Spring holes in Unit 15 (despite requiring wading gear) and canal in Unit 7
- **Spring holes**: Supported highest channel catfish concentrations; fish readily bit; reports of 15-fish limits in <30 minutes
- **Over 300 fish observed in creels**: Zero marked fish found (indicating August-stocked fish absent)
- **Fish caught**: Smaller than August stockings, likely from April stocking with minimal growth
- **Species composition** (1969 refuge estimate): 60% channel catfish (8-10 inches), 20% largemouth bass (6-8 inches), 5% green sunfish, remainder white bass/crappie/bullhead
- **Condition**: Fair body condition, no lesions or sickness, *Argulus* sp. common
- **Hypothesis**: As water quality worsened seasonally, fish concentrated in best available habitat (spring holes, canal)

### Limiting Factors Summary

1. **Salinity**: TDS 16-35 ppt by fall, exceeding tolerance for freshwater species
2. **Dissolved Oxygen**: Anoxic conditions in >90% of surface area due to dense vegetation
3. **Habitat**: <10% of surface area suitable for fish life; extremely shallow water
4. **Vegetation**: Dense *Chara* beds blocking access to food organisms and creating DO problems
5. **Carp Competition**: Thousands of large carp competing for food, causing turbidity
6. **Predation**: Heavy pressure from pelicans, herons, turtles; vulnerability increased when ducks reduce vegetation in fall/winter
7. **Overstocking**: Stocking rates exceeded carrying capacity of limited habitable water
8. **Design**: Impoundments built for waterfowl, not fisheries

### Management Implications

- Units 3, 6, 16 had insufficient water for summer fish survival
- Units 5, 7, 15 fundamentally marginal for sport fisheries due to physical and chemical limitations
- Spring flow reduction and increased salt concentration during irrigation season creates seasonal water quality crisis
- Despite poor conditions, eastern New Mexico has limited public fishing water, suggesting continued stocking with modified policies and species selection
- 1970 report recommendations (carp control, deepening canals, *Chara* control, year-round fishing, saline-tolerant species like [[Striped Bass]] or [[White Perch]]) remain relevant but largely unimplemented
