# Eradication of the Fish Population of Lake Pauline

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #Catostomidae #lake-renovation

## Executive Summary

This Job Completion Report documents a total fish population eradication project at [[Lake Pauline]], Texas, conducted under Federal Aid Project F-14-D-7 (Statewide Rough Fish Control) from March 6 to June 30, 1963. Pre-treatment surveys revealed a rough fish population exceeding 90 percent, with [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*) constituting over 50 percent of netting samples. The lake, tributary streams, and watershed ponds were treated with rotenone powder on May 2, 1963, at an equivalent rate of approximately 4 pounds of 5 percent rotenone powder per acre-foot of water. The treatment successfully eradicated most species, though [[Black Bullhead Catfish]] (*Ictalurus melas*) and [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) showed some survival. Post-treatment restocking with [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) was initiated in mid-May 1963.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Pauline]] required renovation due to an unbalanced fish community dominated by rough fish species. The [[River Carpsucker]] (*Carpiodes carpio*) was the primary rough species, accounting for approximately 50 percent of netting samples, with total rough fish composition exceeding 90 percent. The project was conducted by the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department under Project Leader Lonnie Peters.

Pre-treatment surveys involved netting and seining of the lake proper as well as private tanks and ponds on the watershed. Landowner permission was obtained for treating all watershed water bodies. Fathometer transects were run across all lake sections at approximately 100-yard intervals to determine average depths for volume calculations. Surface area measurements were obtained from aerial photography supplied by the Quanah ASC office.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The treatment targeted the entire aquatic ecosystem of [[Lake Pauline]], including the main lake body, tributary streams, and watershed ponds. Special precautionary measures were implemented to protect infrastructure: two screens were constructed and installed at the intake canal of the [[West Texas Utilities Company]] electric plant to prevent dead fish from entering and potentially overloading the rotating screens.

The lake proper was treated using two barge-mounted treating units, while tributary streams and areas inaccessible to heavy spray equipment received treatment with 5 percent liquid rotenone using backpack sprayers. Other stream areas and small ranch ponds were treated with a John Bean spray unit. Treatment progressed from the upper (shallow) end of the lake to the lower end, concluding with deeper areas along the dam.

## Key Findings

**Treatment Application and Public Response:**
- Rotenone application began at approximately 7:30 a.m. on May 2, 1963, and concluded at approximately 4:00 p.m.
- Treatment rate: equivalent to 3-4 pounds of 5 percent rotenone powder per acre-foot
- Approximately 5,000 people witnessed the treatment, with about 450 motor boats on the lake at peak observation
- Fish of all species began surfacing in treated areas by 10:30 a.m.

**Fish Mortality Patterns:**
- Most fish sank to the lake bottom soon after death, leading some observers to initially question treatment success
- By May 4, bloated fish began rising to the surface
- By May 6, the entire north shoreline had a solid windrow of dead fish
- Estimated 200,000 to 300,000 pounds of dead fish drifted to shore by May 6
- Most game fish killed were assumed salvaged by spectators, as relatively few dead game fish were noted the day following treatment

**Post-Treatment Surveys:**
- First survey (May 7-8): Gill nets (1,800 feet, mesh sizes 1 to 3.5 inches) produced only eleven [[Black Bullhead Catfish]]
- Toxicity persistence: less than five days (live minnows placed in test cage on May 7 afternoon survived until May 8 afternoon)
- Second survey (early July): Several [[Carp]] specimens were captured, suggesting some survival or delayed mortality effects
- The absence of carp in the first survey may indicate temporary immobilization from rotenone effects

**Treatment Efficacy:**
- Excellent results achieved on all species except [[Black Bullhead Catfish]] (*Ictalurus melas*) and [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
- These resistant species were greatly reduced in number
- Overall renovation considered successful
- [[Largemouth Bass]] restocking initiated in mid-May 1963

**Recommendations:**
Periodic surveys recommended following complete restocking to determine survival and growth of restocked species and to assess the duration of renovation effects.
