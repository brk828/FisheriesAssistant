# Survey of the Fishes of the Pecos, Canadian and Arkansas Drainages in New Mexico

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 field survey by James E. Sublette of [[Eastern New Mexico University]] documents the freshwater fish fauna of the [[Pecos River]], [[Canadian River]] and [[Arkansas River]] drainages in New Mexico. It was commissioned by the [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]] during a period of legislative and federal attention to endangered fishes. Sampling ran from March to August 1975 at 76 numbered stations. The survey records more than 60 species and compares current distributions with earlier records, chiefly those of [[William J. Koster]] (1957). Many native species have contracted in range, several are rare or possibly absent, and introduced species are expanding.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** Support state and federal endangered-species work by updating the known distributions of New Mexico fishes.
- **Support:** Contract from the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, with some support from Eastern New Mexico University. Field collections were made largely by ENMU students.
- **Methods:** Minnow seines, bag seines, hoop nets, fyke nets, minnow traps and gill nets were used. A hand-generator electroshocker ([[Electrofishing]] with a telephone generator) gave little success. Minnow seines and gill nets were most productive. Gill nets arrived late, so their sampling is limited.
- **Limitations:** High water prevented adequate sampling of salmonids, so no population estimates were made for trout. Upper-elevation streams received little effort.
- **Preservation:** Specimens were fixed in 10% formaldehyde, washed, and stored in 40% isopropyl alcohol. Vouchers are curated at the ENMU Natural History Museum.
- **Data sources:** Specimens and records were drawn from the survey, earlier literature, Department of Game and Fish reports, and museum collections at UNM, NMSU and Texas A&M.
- **Appendices:** Appendix I lists station descriptions, gear, water quality and species counts. Appendix II maps distributions for each species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat gradient.** Species distributions follow elevation and water character:
- *Lower Pecos* (Eddy and Chaves counties): saline, warm, turbid water with high chloride and sulfate levels. Examples include Station 8, a spring with 13,312 ppm total hardness, 10,448 ppm chlorides and 15,000 µmho specific conductance at 26 °C.
- *Intermediate and upland streams* (Lincoln, Mora, Colfax, San Miguel): cooler, clearer, faster water that supports Rio Grande chub, longnose dace and creek chub.
- *Canadian and Arkansas reaches* (Quay, Union, Harding): shallow, slow water with killifish and Arkansas River shiner.

**Human disturbance.** Several stations showed signs of pollution or altered flow. Station 12 near Artesia had a strong sewage odor and large numbers of sewage midge larvae (*Chironomus stigmaterus*). Station 19 near Hagerman also had a strong sewage odor. Dead fish were seen below 10 Mile Dam (Station 7). The authors link the loss of sand shiner below Artesia to degradation of the lower Pecos.

**Introductions and stocking.** Stocking of hatchery fishes, bait minnows and game fishes has reshaped the fauna. Key examples:
- Introduced bait minnows such as [[Red Shiner]] and [[Fathead Minnow]] are widespread.
- [[Mosquito Fish]] were stocked extensively and are absent from cold, fast headwaters.
- Rock bass at [[Blue Spring]] were probably introduced in early-twentieth-century stockings.
- Silversides (*Menidia beryllina*) have become established below Carlsbad.
- The Pecos sucker (*Catostomus plebeius*) in the Rio Hondo appears to have been introduced with trout plantings.

**Conservation sites.** [[Blue Spring]], [[Bitter Lakes National Wildlife Refuge]] and the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] hold key populations of endangered or rare species. The hatchery propagates several endangered species.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomy and families.** The survey covers 15 families. Cyprinidae dominates, with about 20 taxa. The text reports roughly 60 or more taxa, plus about eight previously recorded species considered probably extirpated (for example, American eel, Sacramento perch, threadfin shad and red drum).

**Endangered and rare species:**
- **[[Pecos Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon* n. sp. "Pecos"): Being described by Dr. Antonio Echelle of Baylor University. It is locally abundant with a limited range. Station 8 yielded 26 specimens, and "hundreds" were sighted at [[Bottomless Lakes State Park]] (Station 20).
- **[[Pecos Gambusia]]** (*Gambusia nobilis*): Only two essentially natural localities are known, Blue Spring and Bitter Lakes. Blue Spring yielded 54 specimens (13 males and 35 females, plus other counts).
- **[[Greenthroat Darter]]** (*Etheostoma lepidum*): Known from only three localities (Blue Spring, Rio Penasco, and Bitter Lakes).
- **Brook stickleback** (*Culaea inconstans*): Only known population is in Merrick Lake, Vermejo Park. It was not verified during this survey.
- **Blue sucker** (*Cycleptus elongatus*): Not collected despite several attempts. It may no longer occur in the state.

**Range changes:**
- **Range reduction:** [[Roundnose Minnow]] (*Dionda episcopa*) is known from only three localities. The rarest species in the survey include suckermouth minnow (new Pecos record) and redbelly dace (one specimen at Coyote Creek).
- **Range contraction, lower Pecos:** Rainwater fish (*Lucania parva*), *Dionda episcopa* and *Astyanax fasciatus* are all reduced, possibly through hatchery introductions.
- **Log perch** (*Percina macrolepida*): Lower Pecos populations appear eradicated, but significant new populations were found below and within Lake Sumner.
- **Range expansion:** Sand shiner (*Notropis stramineus*) has expanded toward the headwaters of the Pecos and Canadian. Red shiner is still expanding.

**Hybridization:**
- *Gila pandora* hybridizes with *Rhinichthys cataractae* in the Rio Penasco. Dr. Koster reports observing this hybrid on several occasions.
- Hybrid sunfish (*Lepomis* spp.) were found in the lower Pecos and Black River.
- White bass and striped bass hybridize at Bitter Lakes National Wildlife Refuge.

**Parasitism:** One mature *Gila pandora* from Last Chance Canyon was so heavily infested with black grubs that it appeared almost black.

**Notable station data:**
- Station 7a, Pecos River: *Notropis lutrensis* (299 individuals, breeding colors).
- Station 13b, Rio Penasco: *Gila pandora* (165) and *Rhinichthys cataractae* (60).
- Station 52, Ute Creek: *Fundulus zebrinus kansae* (159).

**Survey gaps:** Several previously recorded species were not collected: blue catfish, headwater catfish, yellow and brown bullhead, flathead catfish, warmouth, redear sunfish, spotted bass and black crappie. The authors suggest additional collecting, especially between Lake MacMillan and Lake Avalon, and in impoundments that were not sampled.

**Management recommendations** (implicit in the text):
1. Monitor and protect Blue Spring and Bitter Lakes populations of endangered species.
2. Continue propagation of endangered taxa at the Dexter hatchery.
3. Verify uncertain records (Merrick Lake stickleback, Bottomless Lakes Pecos gambusia, and Pecos *Fundulus* records).
4. Carry out seasonal surveys of the Pecos and of impoundments, which were not sampled.
