# Fish Surveys in Southern Arizona, January-February 1991

#ichthyology #native-fish-conservation #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This intra-office memo documents fish surveys conducted at multiple sites in southern Arizona between January 30 and February 10, 1991, focusing on [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) populations. The survey assessed both natural populations and reintroduction sites, documenting successes at [[Quitobaquito Springs]] and failures at several stocked locations. Additional surveys examined native fish assemblages at [[Aravaipa Creek]] and evaluated potential reintroduction sites including [[St. David Cienega]]. The report reveals mixed outcomes for conservation efforts, with some populations thriving while others have been extirpated, often due to competition from exotic species, predation by bullfrogs, or habitat limitations.

## Background & Description

The survey covered approximately 15 sites across southern Arizona, including federal (BLM, Forest Service, National Monument), state, and private lands. Sites included natural springs, constructed ponds and tanks, stream reaches, and cienega habitats. Many locations had been previously stocked with [[Gila Topminnow]] or [[Desert Pupfish]] as part of conservation reintroduction efforts dating back to 1982-1989.

Target species included two federally protected native fish: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*, Family [[Cyprinodontidae]]) and [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, Family [[Poeciliidae]]). Additional native species documented included [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), [[Speckled Dace]], and catostomid suckers.

Survey methods included seining, electrofishing, experimental gill nets, and trammel nets. Visual observations were also conducted where water clarity permitted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The surveys revealed significant ecological challenges facing native fish conservation in southern Arizona:

**Exotic Species Competition**: [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia* spp.) were found at multiple sites and appear to have displaced [[Gila Topminnow]] populations at [[Martin Well]] (site 123) and [[St. David Cienega]]. Goldfish and other exotic forage fish were present at several locations.

**Predation Pressure**: Bullfrogs were abundant at [[Howard Well]] (site 83), [[Martin Well]] (site 123), and other sites, likely contributing to native fish declines. At [[Howard Well]], pupfish were concentrated away from bullfrog aggregations.

**Habitat Modifications**: At [[Quitobaquito Springs]] (site 128), a new concrete canal system replaced an earthen canal that had become choked with vegetation. The modification provided extensive cover and pupfish colonized it in large numbers. Conversely, excessive aquatic vegetation at [[Arivaca Pond]] (site 127) and [[Serendipity Ranch]] ponds limited fish populations and sampling effectiveness.

**Hydrological Factors**: Several sites experience periodic drying, preventing establishment of permanent fish populations. High-gradient streams like [[Josephine Canyon]] contain numerous barriers preventing recolonization after flooding events.

**Cattle Impacts**: Sites within cattle exclosures (e.g., [[Howard Well]], [[Martin Well]]) showed different ecological conditions than grazed sites, with exclosures supporting higher bullfrog populations.

## Key Findings

**Successful Populations**:
- **Site 128 ([[Quitobaquito Springs]])**: [[Desert Pupfish]] population thriving; fish more abundant in new concrete canal than in pond; males in reproductive color; annual census showed large numbers moved into canal
- **Site 127 ([[Arivaca Pond]])**: [[Gila Topminnow]] present with abundant juveniles but relatively few adults; population decreased from 1989 peak
- **Site 85 ([[Cold Spring Seep]])**: One pond maintained abundant [[Gila Topminnow]] population with lush aquatic plant growth

**Extirpated or Failed Reintroductions**:
- **[[Josephine Canyon]]**: No fish found; second reported extirpation of 1982 topminnow stocking; numerous barriers prevent recolonization
- **[[Bear Canyon]]**: No topminnow found above or below 1982 stocking site; second reported extirpation; [[Longfin Dace]] abundant; recommended for restocking 1-2 miles above previous site
- **Site 133 ([[Buehman Canyon]])**: No [[Desert Pupfish]] found from July 1989 stocking; [[Longfin Dace]] extremely abundant; moderate leopard frog tadpoles present
- **Site 83 ([[Howard Well]])**: [[Desert Pupfish]] numbers extremely reduced since September 1990; fair numbers in shallow water away from thermal well and bullfrog concentrations; fourth reported extirpation of [[Gila Topminnow]]
- **Site 123 ([[Martin Well]])**: Only mosquitofish and sunfish found among 300+ fish examined; illegal topminnow reintroduction unsuccessful; bullfrogs plentiful
- **[[Sopori Wash]]**: No fish found in approximately 5 miles surveyed despite 1988 reports of mosquitofish or topminnow
- **[[Ash Canyon]]**: No fish found; habitat appeared sterile, possibly due to mining activity and/or periodic drying

**Other Findings**:
- **Site 109 ([[Chaney Place]])**: Only mosquitofish present; investigated for possible illegal topminnow introduction; no bullfrogs observed; cattle use present
- **[[Serendipity Ranch]]**: Three large ponds fishless despite probable recent presence of goldfish and forage fish; aquatic invertebrates extremely rare; vegetation choked
- **[[Little Outfit Tank]]**: Only goldfish and bullfrogs; never stocked with topminnow; nearby Forest Service tank periodically dry
- **[[Clyne Ranch Bass Pond]]**: Many 1.5-pound [[Largemouth Bass]] and 0.5-pound bluegill caught; hundreds of small bluegill; experimental gill nets ineffective for small fish
- **[[St. David Cienega]]**: 80-acre bulrush-covered cienega evaluated for topminnow reintroduction; mostly <1 cm deep and fishless; south pond >1 meter deep with mosquitofish; difficult to renovate due to size and complexity
- **[[Aravaipa Creek]]**: 225 native fish collected outside canyon via seining and electrofishing; single small mosquitofish reported (first upper-end record, specimen not kept); reduced [[Spikedace]] numbers compared to 2 months prior; [[Loach Minnow]] common; no [[Roundtail Chub]]; [[Speckled Dace]] only near headwaters; both sucker species in low numbers with mostly large individuals indicating age class failure
