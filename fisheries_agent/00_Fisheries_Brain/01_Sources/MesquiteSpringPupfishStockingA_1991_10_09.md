## Executive Summary
This record documents a 1991 telephone conversation between BLM (Tim Hughes) and USFWS (Sally Stefferud) staff concerning the translocation of [[Desert Pupfish]] from [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]] into [[Mesquite Spring]]. The conversation highlights critical species-management concerns regarding non-target fish contamination during seining operations, as well as unrelated but concurrent infrastructure and habitat-protection issues at [[Tule Creek]], including pipeline installation and enforcement of an exclosure against unauthorized mining equipment intrusion. While not a primary research paper, the note captures operational conservation actions affecting an endangered desert fish species.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, coloration, body measurements) are provided in this source. The document references three co-occurring small-bodied freshwater fish taxa relevant to field identification during seining operations:
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — target species for translocation/stocking.
- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — non-target species present at source site, live-bearing poeciliid, must be excluded from stocking effort.
- **[[Fathead Minnow]]** (*Pimephales promelas*) — non-native cyprinid, considered undesirable/potentially invasive contaminant in the source population, must be excluded.

Field personnel were explicitly cautioned to visually distinguish [[Desert Pupfish]] from these co-occurring species during seine collection to prevent inadvertent introduction of non-target or invasive taxa into the recipient habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Source Population Habitat:** [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]] serves as a mixed-species aquatic refuge/source population site containing [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Fathead Minnow]] in sympatry.
- **Recipient Habitat:** [[Mesquite Spring]] is identified as a translocation/reintroduction site intended to establish or supplement a population of [[Desert Pupfish]], implying it is an isolated desert spring habitat suitable for the species' tolerance of extreme conditions (typical of *Cyprinodon* spp.).
- **Tule Creek Ecosystem:** A separate riparian creek system associated with an **exclosure** (fencing structure to prevent livestock/human/vehicle disturbance) protecting aquatic habitat, likely for endangered fish or associated riparian species. The presence of a **mining claim** and unauthorized heavy equipment crossing indicates anthropogenic pressure on this habitat, raising concern for potential "take" of endangered species under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Infrastructure Interaction:** A new pipeline (**Tule Creek Pipeline**, installed Oct. 3, 1991) crosses the creek, requiring structural supports, indicating direct physical infrastructure intersecting aquatic habitat.

## Key Findings
- [[Desert Pupfish]] were scheduled for translocation via seining from [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]] to [[Mesquite Spring]] on Oct. 10, 1991.
- Field staff were explicitly warned to prevent co-collection of **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (a co-occurring native species of conservation concern) and **[[Fathead Minnow]]** (a non-native competitor/predator risk) during the stocking operation, reflecting standard precautions against contaminating translocated founder populations.
- The **[[Tule Creek Pipeline]]** was successfully installed and operational as of Oct. 3, 1991, though it required additional structural supports at the creek crossing.
- The **exclosure** protecting aquatic/riparian habitat along Tule Creek was reported closed (i.e., secured) again after apparent breach.
- Unauthorized crossing of the creek by heavy equipment associated with an active **mining claim** posed a potential threat of "take" of endangered species, prompting BLM to plan direct notification of the claimant regarding **Endangered Species Act** liability to deter future habitat disturbance.
- This record exemplifies routine field-level interagency coordination (BLM–USFWS) involved in active management of endangered desert fish populations and their habitats in Arizona.
