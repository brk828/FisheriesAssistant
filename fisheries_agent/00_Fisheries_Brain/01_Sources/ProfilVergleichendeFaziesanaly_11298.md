# Tertiäre Süßwasserkarbonate der westlichen Schwäbischen Alb und des Hegau: Faziesanalyse, Paläoökologie und paläogeographisches Umfeld

#ichthyology #marine-biology #nonmarine-carbonates

## Executive Summary

This German-language dissertation (Profil 9, Stuttgart 1996, by [[Gunter Schweigert]]) studies the genesis of nonmarine Tertiary carbonates along the southern margin of the western [[Schwäbische Alb]] and in the [[Hegau]]. Its focus is the Lower Miocene algal tufa of [[Engelswies]] near Meßkirch and the Middle Miocene [[Riedöschingen Travertine]] near Blumberg. The work proposes a new classification of nonmarine carbonates, based on a triangular diagram of biotic versus abiotic precipitation, and interprets the carbonates in terms of paleoenvironment, paleoclimate and stratigraphy. The document contains no fish content; its biological focus is on microbes, invertebrates (gastropods, ostracods, crustacean coprolites, freshwater crabs), plants and mammals.

## Background & Description

The study addresses nonmarine carbonates (lake limestones, tufas, travertines, sinters, calcretes) of Tertiary age. Its comparative base includes the Quaternary "Sauerwasserkalke" of [[Stuttgart]] (Koban & Schweigert 1993a, b), as well as Tertiary deposits of the Ulmer Alb, Nördlinger Ries, Randecker Maar, Steinheimer Becken and the Upper Rhine Graben.

The author defines the terms used:
- **Seekalk** (lacustrine limestone): carbonate from standing waters, characterized by limnic fauna and flora.
- **Kalktuff** (tufa): precipitated from nonthermal, supersaturated flowing water; highly porous due to dissolved plant remains.
- **Kalksinter** (sinter): inorganic precipitate in restrictive environments (thermal springs, dark fissures, steep slopes).
- **Travertin** (travertine): deposits of mineralized or moderately thermal springs, with a characteristic microbial inventory.
- **Kalkcaliche / pedogenic carbonate**: an independent category that overprints other types.

Major features used for identification include the "Dichothrix-Morphotyp" (bushy microbial layers), the "Renalcis-Morphotyp" and the "Polybessurus-Morphotyp" (microbial fabrics), and the "Microcodium" structure, reinterpreted as calcified plant rootlets, probably of palms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stratigraphic framework.** After the Oligocene and Lower Miocene marine retreat, the Lower Freshwater Molasse was deposited; the Upper Marine Molasse transgression is followed by the "Graupensandrinne" (an erosional channel with Grimmelfingen sands and brackish Kirchberg beds). Upper Freshwater Molasse includes "Juranagelfluh" conglomerates. Volcanism in the Hegau (Deckentuffe, dated about 12.6–13 Ma) produced maars, sinter-bearing springs and lakes.

**Paleoclimate.** Plant fossils indicate warm-temperate to subtropical conditions, with mean annual temperatures of roughly 10–14 °C inferred for the middle Badenian, and a shift toward seasonal drought after the Ries impact (about 15.1 Ma). The author argues against a simple climatic control of carbonate precipitation and notes that calcretes occur in both arid and humid settings.

**Engelswies lake.** Brotia escheri and Theodoxus cyrtocelis indicate residual salinity (optimum about 3 ‰, tolerance 2–9 ‰). The endemic freshwater crab *Proballaya quenstedti* is the probable producer of internally structured crustacean coprolites (*Helicerina ruttei* n. sp., described in a separate paper). Cyanobacterial oncoids, including *Rivularia*- and *Phormidium*-type crusts, dominate the shallow shore. Potamogeton-type leaf sheaths point to a water depth of about 2–4 m, and large mammal and small mammal faunas (MN 5, Karpatium) are preserved.

**Hilzingen–Weiterdingen area.** Thermal sinter terraces, a dolomitic "Erbsenkalk" and a Philippsberg travertine with chironomid pupal tubes are combined with a lacustrine interpretation. The Philippsberg travertine is considered to be warm-season precipitated, based on the chironomid life cycle.

**Hohenkrähen and Hinterried.** Dolomitic laminites with probable crustacean or insect fecal pellets record a deep, ion-enriched lake. The Hinterried maar (upper Miocene, probably older Pannonian) has an estimated water depth of at least 90 m and an Auwald-type flora with *Glyptostrobus*, *Celtis*, *Schisandra geissertii* and *Podocarpium*.

## Key Findings

- **Classification.** A genetic triangle links biotic and abiotic precipitation with porosity and continuity, showing a succession from pure sinters (Riedöschingen) through travertines (Riedöschingen, Weiterdingen) and tufas (Wannenberg) to lacustrine limestones dominated by biotic precipitation (Wannenberg, Engelswies).
- **Sinters** form in harsh settings (thermal or mineralized water, darkness, steep relief). Pure sinters are bound to volcanic areas but may occur as diagenetic cements.
- **Travertines** are restricted to volcanic areas, show cyclic bedding driven mainly by seasonal climate, and contain almost no autochthonous fauna except chironomid larvae/pupae.
- **Tufas** form from cool, supersaturated water with extensive plant growth; they are highly porous and dominated by plant-related facies types (leaf, reed, moss, characean tufas).
- **Lacustrine limestones** are mostly detrital; laminites are typical of distal, poorly bioturbated bottom waters.
- **Calcretes** such as the "Albstein" and the Hoppetenzell "Algenkugel-Konglomerat" were misinterpreted as lacustrine. Their pisoids are pedogenic, not algal oncoids. The "Limnocodium" genus is a synonym of *Microcodium*.
- **Engelswies** lake limestone (Karpatium, MN 5) yields more than 60 plant taxa, about 25 gastropod and crustacean records, and 25 mammal records; *Heliconia europaea* and *Zanthoxylum schillii* were described from here.
- **Crustacean coprolites** at Engelswies are the first ones definitely attributed to brachyuran crabs, and the crab *Proballaya quenstedti* is considered endemic to the Graupensandrinne.
- **Schellenfeld** and **Kalte Herberge** are similar lacustrine carbonates with mammal faunas (MN 5) and detrital onkoids, respectively.
- **Riedöschingen travertine** (up to 17 m thick, Middle Miocene) is a dome built around a thermal fissure, showing sinter, Renalcis-type fabrics, Dichothrix bushes, gas-bubble layers and paleo-"water level" structures. Its age is inferred from radiometric dating of nearby volcanites and the comparison with Béttingen.
- **Wannenberg** (near Tengen) shows that tufas and lacustrine carbonates can develop on a slope. A younger, Sarmatian to Pannonian age is inferred.
- **Hinterried maar**: lacustrine carbonates with a flora dominated by riparian elements, probably of Upper Miocene age.
- **Conclusions.** Nonmarine carbonates record the interplay of volcanism, karst hydrology, climate and biota. Calcrete and tufa facies can serve as paleoclimatic and paleogeographic indicators, and similar facies models may apply to marine carbonates such as oncoids, peloids and laminites.
