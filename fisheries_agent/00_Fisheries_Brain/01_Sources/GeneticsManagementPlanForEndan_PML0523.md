# Genetics Management Plan for Endangered Fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin (Revised, 1999)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This revised [[Genetics Management Plan]] from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6, Denver, June 21, 1999) guides the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for four endangered fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]. The plan (1) identifies and classifies endangered fish stocks, (2) sets criteria for genetics decisions, (3) ranks priorities among species and stocks, and (4) recommends management actions by species and stock.

Eleven presumptive stocks were recognized: three of Razorback Sucker, five of Humpback Chub, and three of Colorado Pikeminnow. Bonytail was not assigned an upper-basin stock because adults are captured very rarely, so broodstock must derive from [[Lake Mohave]] fish. Stocks were delineated conservatively because all are considered potentially important to recovery. The [[Biology Committee]] reviews the plan annually under [[Adaptive Management]].

Species propagation priorities were: Razorback Sucker 1, Bonytail 2, Humpback Chub 3, and Colorado Pikeminnow 4 (1 = highest). The recommended breeding strategy is 25 females mated with 25 males to produce 25 pedigreed family lots, with an estimated inbreeding rate of 1% per generation as acceptable for maintaining genetic diversity.

## Background & Description

The four species are endemic to the Upper Colorado River Basin and were listed as endangered under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended. Declines were attributed to altered streamflow regimes, habitat alteration, temperature change, water diversions, barriers to fish passage, and competition or predation from nonnative fishes.

The Recovery Implementation Program is a joint effort of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, the Western Area Power Administration, the states of Colorado, Utah and Wyoming, upper-basin water users, and environmental organizations. It has five elements, including propagation and stocking of captive-reared fish.

The plan's philosophy, mission, and goals (Box 1) are to maintain the genetic integrity of wild and captive-reared fish, prevent irreversible losses of genetic diversity, prevent immediate extinction, conserve diversity in wild stocks, and produce genetically diverse fish for augmentation. Implementation occurs through an Annual Operations Plan, broodstock plans, case-by-case stocking plans, and facilities operations plans. Companion documents include [[Genetics Management Guidelines]] (Williamson and Wydoski 1994) and the [[Coordinated Hatchery Facility Plan]] (Wydoski 1994).

Stocks are defined as [[Evolutionarily Significant Units]] or self-sustaining groups. Identification draws on distribution, spawning-site fidelity, movement, geographic separation (criteria not met by stocks separated by less than about 100 river miles), molecular genetics, and morphometrics. Genetic tools include allozyme electrophoresis and mitochondrial DNA RFLP analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Dams fragment habitat and block movement, and interchange among stocks is limited. Reduced interchange among stocks during spawning can still cause genetic homogenization, so the plan classifies stocks conservatively. Stocks in the Desolation-Gray Canyons and the Yampa River show large phenotypic variation in Humpback Chub, raising concern about hybridization with [[Roundtail Chub]].

Razorback Sucker spawning concentrates in the middle Green River between the Duchesne and Yampa rivers, with a known spawning bar near RK 311.5. Larvae have been collected in the Lake Powell inflow. Historic bottomland floodplains that supported Razorback Sucker are now disconnected by levees, and reconnecting them would also benefit nonnative warmwater fishes, requiring control measures.

The Redlands Fish Ladder on the Gunnison River, operating since 1996, passed 23 Colorado Pikeminnow through October 1998, showing the value of fish passage.

Genetic work indicates Upper Colorado River Razorback Sucker mitochondrial diversity is lower than in the lower basin (Lake Mohave had 32 haplotypes; Lake Powell 7; Green River 4; Upper Colorado River 1). Historic gene flow among basin populations led to a recommendation to treat Razorback Sucker as one breeding population, with broodstock sourced first locally, then from nearest neighbors, then from Lake Mohave.

## Key Findings

**Stock status (Table 2 summary)**
- **Razorback Sucker:** Status is critical in the upper basin. Old adults in current presumptive stocks are declining rapidly. Middle Green River recruitment occurs but is insufficient for self-sustainment. The Upper Colorado River stock was extirpated above Price-Stubb Diversion Dam and Redlands Diversion Dam; about 600 fish were found in Etter Pond near Debeque, Colorado.
- **Bonytail:** Considered extirpated from the upper basin; no stock identified.
- **Humpback Chub:** Stocks apparently stable but small and isolated in deep canyons. The Yampa and Desolation-Gray stocks show possible roundtail hybridization. Black Rocks is estimated at approximately 2,500 adults (value as given in source).
- **Colorado Pikeminnow:** Generally stable. The Upper Colorado River stock (about 600 fish, increasing) is very low, and the Gunnison stock is extremely low and declining. Yampa/Green stock estimated at about 1,400; Desolation-Gray at about 1,000 adults.

**Priorities (Tables 3 and 4)**
- Razorback Sucker, priority 1: Middle Green/Yampa (Razorback-Jensen Bar), Lower Green, and Upper Colorado stocks all have broodstock development underway.
- Bonytail, priority 2: Lake Mohave-derived broodstock at Dexter National Fish Hatchery, New Mexico.
- Humpback Chub, priority 3: Yampa and Desolation-Gray stocks ranked 2 and 4 respectively within the species, pending genetic workshops on hybridization risk.
- Colorado Pikeminnow, priority 4: Upper Colorado River stock ranked priority 1 among pikeminnow stocks because of its low numbers.

**Breeding strategy and inbreeding**
- Recommended strategy: 25 × 25 paired matings yielding 25 family lots (effective population size goal). A 5 × 5 diallele cross (25 family lots) is used currently but is being phased out to reduce inbreeding risk.
- Acceptable inbreeding rate: 1% per generation (effective population size of 50).
- The current Razorback Sucker broodstock does not yet meet the 25 × 25 requirement; adults are hard to hold and spawning collections are limited.

**Facilities and production**
- Razorback Sucker broodstock: Grand Valley Endangered Fish Facility, Ouray National Fish Hatchery, and Horsethief Ponds.
- Colorado Pikeminnow broodstock (17 family lots) held at Grand Valley and Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- Bonytail broodstock: Dexter National Fish Hatchery, with a new year class of 354 fish planned.
- Pond water needs of roughly 144 acre-feet annually were noted as necessary to maintain broodstocks.

**Recommended actions**
- Hold 25 × 25 Razorback Sucker matings and expand Lake Mohave contributions if upper-basin fish are unavailable by 2002.
- Continue Bonytail reintroduction in Professor Valley (approximately 2,000 young-of-year stocked in 1996; about 2,206 in 1997).
- Hold a genetics workshop in 1999 on Humpback Chub hybridization risk across the Yampa, Desolation-Gray, Black Rocks, Westwater, and Cataract stocks.
- Maintain periodic monitoring (3–5 years) of Colorado Pikeminnow Yampa and Desolation-Gray stocks; continue Interagency Standardized Monitoring.
- Implement a stocking plan for the Palisade to Rifle reach, Colorado, where stocking has been delayed by production shortfalls.
- Investigate movement of hatchery-raised pikeminnow from the Colorado River into the Gunnison before stocking.
