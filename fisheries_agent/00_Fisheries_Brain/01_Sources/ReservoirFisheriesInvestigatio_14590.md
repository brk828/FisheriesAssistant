# Glen Canyon Unit Colorado River Storage Project Reservoir Fisheries Investigations - Creel Census and Plankton Studies (1967-1968)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #reservoir-ecology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This report documents the fifth year of a six-year cooperative fisheries investigation on [[Lake Powell]], the reservoir created by [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] conducted creel census and plankton studies from February 1, 1967 to January 31, 1968. An estimated 26,967 angler trips produced 53,908 fish in 129,186 hours of effort across four major access points: [[Wahweap Marina]], [[Hall's Crossing Marina]], [[Bullfrog Marina]], and [[Hite Marina]]. The creel was dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] (69%), followed by [[Channel Catfish]] (20%), [[Rainbow Trout]] (5%), and other species. Success averaged 0.42 fish per hour or 2.0 fish per trip. Plankton studies identified ten zooplankton and twenty phytoplankton genera, with significant blooms occurring in March, October, and January. The upper reservoir proved most productive for both fish and plankton.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Powell]] is a large reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] created by the [[Colorado River Storage Project]]. This investigation represents a cooperative effort between Arizona and Utah fish and game departments, funded under Section 8 of the project. The study employed systematic creel census at all major access points, with varying sampling intensity: six days per month at [[Wahweap Marina]], four days per month at [[Hall's Crossing Marina]], and two days per month each at [[Bullfrog Marina]] and [[Hite Marina]]. Census days were split equally between weekdays and weekends, conducted from daylight to dark, with extended night fishing census at Wahweap during warmer months.

The fish community included multiple sport species: [[Micropterus salmoides]] (largemouth bass), [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish), [[Salmo gairdneri]] (rainbow trout), [[Pomoxis nigromaculatus]] (black crappie), [[Lepomis macrochirus]] (bluegill), and [[Lepomis cyanellus]] (green sunfish). Additional species present but rarely caught included [[Salmo trutta]] (brown trout), [[Stizostedion vitreum]] (walleye), [[Gila robusta]] (bonytail), [[Ptychocheilus lucius]] (Colorado River squawfish), [[Cyprinus carpio]] (carp), and [[Ictalurus melas]] (black bullhead).

Plankton sampling utilized a closing net of #20 mesh to sample 100-foot vertical water columns at twelve stations arranged at equal intervals throughout the reservoir. Samples were preserved in 5% formalin and analyzed quantitatively using Sedgwick-Rafter counting chambers under binocular microscope at 3.5x to 20x magnification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Powell]] exhibited distinct spatial patterns in both fish distribution and plankton productivity. The upper reservoir (from [[Escalante River]] to [[Hite]]) demonstrated greater plankton abundance and supported higher fish catches. Water stratification was evident, with the upper 25 feet being most productive due to greater light penetration and warmer temperatures. Peak plankton production occurred during June, July, and August, corresponding to maximum sunlight and temperature.

The zooplankton community was dominated by [[Daphnia]], [[Cyclops]], [[Diaptomus]], [[Polyarthra]], and [[Ceratium]], with nauplius stages of arthropods being most numerous. [[Daphnia]] constituted over half the plankton volume in all samples, representing the largest planktonic organism. Phytoplankton was dominated by diatoms ([[Asterionella]], [[Fragilaria]]) from the Yellow-green Algae phylum and desmids ([[Staurastrum]]) from the Green Algae phylum.

Three major phytoplankton blooms occurred: (1) March - heavy [[Asterionella]] bloom in upper half of reservoir; (2) October - moderate blooms of [[Fragilaria]], [[Eudorina]], and [[Staurastrum]] in upper reservoir; (3) January - reservoir-wide [[Fragilaria]] bloom from [[Wahweap]] to [[Hite]], with suspected [[Oscillatoria]] (Blue-green Algae) bloom at [[Bullfrog]].

Fishing pressure distribution reflected accessibility and angler preference: [[Wahweap Marina]] (64%), [[Hall's Crossing Marina]] (25%), [[Hite Marina]] (6%), and [[Bullfrog Marina]] (5%). Anglers primarily originated from Arizona (55.9% at Wahweap), Utah (28% at Hall's Crossing, 67% at Bullfrog), and Colorado (50% at Hite).

## Key Findings

**Fishing Pressure and Harvest:**
- Total estimated pressure: 26,967 angler trips producing 53,908 fish in 129,186 hours
- Species composition: 36,939 largemouth bass (69%), 10,887 channel catfish (20%), 3,001 rainbow trout (5%), 642 black crappie (1%), 1,585 bluegill (3%), 415 green sunfish (1%), 439 other fish (1%)
- Overall success rate: 0.42 fish per hour, 2.0 fish per trip, average trip duration 4.79 hours
- Success by marina: [[Wahweap]] 0.43 fish/hour, [[Hall's Crossing]] 0.44 fish/hour, [[Bullfrog]] 0.39 fish/hour, [[Hite]] 0.46 fish/hour
- Successful angler percentages: Hall's Crossing 67%, Wahweap 57%, Bullfrog 57%, Hite 47%

**Spatial Distribution:**
- Best catch rates by marina: [[Wahweap]] - [[Last Chance Creek]] (0.57 fish/hour); [[Hall's Crossing]] - [[Tapestry Wall]] (0.64 fish/hour); [[Bullfrog]] - [[Red Canyon]] (0.63 fish/hour); [[Hite]] - [[White Canyon]] (0.58 fish/hour)
- [[Wahweap Creek]] received heaviest pressure (33% of trips) but declining from previous year due to reduced trout fishery
- Anglers shifted to [[Kane Creek]], [[Last Chance Creek]], and [[Rock Creek]] for bass fishing

**Temporal Trends:**
- 40% decrease in pressure at [[Wahweap Marina]] compared to previous year
- Success declined from 0.52 fish/hour (1966-67) to 0.42 fish/hour (1967-68)
- Rainbow trout declined from 27% of creel (1966-67) to 5% (1967-68)
- Channel catfish increased from 8% to 20% of creel, representing ~2,500 additional fish

**Bait Effectiveness:**
- Live waterdog/salamander most successful: 66% of largemouth bass, 64% of channel catfish, 14% of rainbow trout
- Prepared baits (corn, cheese, liver, salmon eggs): 45% of rainbow trout, 30% of channel catfish, 7% of largemouth bass
- Artificial baits: 27% of largemouth bass, 7% of channel catfish, 41% of rainbow trout

**Aerial Survey Validation:**
- Twelve aerial counts conducted July 1967 - January 1968, observing 1,126 boats
- Formula #2 (fishing boats × days × fishermen per fishing boat) estimated 11,254 angler trips vs. 11,218 from creel census
- Full study period estimate: 26,645 trips (aerial) vs. 26,967 trips (creel census)
- Method requires additional year of validation

**Plankton Community Structure:**
- Ten zooplankton genera identified: [[Ceratium]], [[Asplanchna]], [[Brachionus]], [[Keratella]], [[Lecane]], [[Polyarthra]], [[Daphnia]], [[Bosmina]], [[Diaptomus]], [[Cyclops]]
- Twenty phytoplankton genera across three phyla: Chlorophyta (Green Algae), Chrysophyta (Yellow-green Algae), Cyanophyta (Blue-green Algae)
- Most prevalent zooplankton: [[Daphnia]] (115/118 samples), nauplius stages (116/118), [[Cyclops]] (111/118), [[Diaptomus]] (92/118)
- Most prevalent phytoplankton: [[Fragilaria]] (78/118 samples), [[Asterionella]] (74/118), [[Staurastrum]] (78/118)
- Upper reservoir and upper 25 feet of water column most productive
- Peak production: June-August corresponding to maximum sunlight and temperature

**Management Recommendations:**
1. Continue creel census at all access points to further evaluate pressure and harvest
2. Continue aerial surveys to determine value as supplement to creel census
3. Continue plankton studies with basic 100-foot vertical control samples; add stratified samples in specific areas to correlate with trout distribution
4. Plant portion of 1968 trout from trucks to compare success of air vs. truck plants
5. Consider catchable-size rainbow trout plants if subcatchables and fingerlings fail to appear in creel
