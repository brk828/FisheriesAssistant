## Executive Summary

This memorandum from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) reports the results of a multi-agency trammel net survey conducted 10–14 May 1993 in [[Lake Mohave]], spanning the Cottonwood East Area (Arizona) and Six Mile Area (Nevada) of the reservoir on the lower [[Colorado River]]. The primary conservation objective was monitoring and salvage of two federally listed native fishes, the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), amid a catch dominated overwhelmingly by non-native [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*). Fifteen bonytail chub and thirty-nine razorback suckers were captured, measured, tagged, and in the case of bonytail either transferred to conservation hatcheries or released with PIT tags. The survey illustrates the persistent management challenge of detecting and recovering critically low-density native big-river fishes within a reservoir ecosystem heavily dominated by introduced species.

## Background & Description

**Gila elegans (Bonytail Chub)**
- Sample size: N = 15
- Mean total length: 47.5 ± 2.7 cm
- Size range: 40.0–50.2 cm
- One individual (smallest) received a fin clip (left pelvic, "LP₁") in addition to PIT tagging.

**Xyrauchen texanus (Razorback Sucker)**
- Sexes distinguished and measured separately:

| Sex | N | Mean TL ± SD (cm) | Range (cm) |
|-----|----|-------------------|------------|
| Male | 12 | 57.2 ± 3.1 | 51.0–63.5 |
| Female | 12 | 62.1 ± 4.5 | 55.5–67.5 |

- Females exhibited notably greater mean total length and greater variance than males, consistent with sexual size dimorphism reported elsewhere in this species (see [[Razorback Sucker]] biology).
- Three individuals were recaptures (previously tagged), indicating some level of established survival/residency in the reservoir; all captured fish were PIT-tagged and released back into the lake.

No morphometric data beyond total length were recorded for the other captured taxa (*Cyprinus carpio*, *Ictalurus punctatus*, *Micropterus salmoides*, *Lepomis macrochirus*, *Morone saxatilis*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat sampled:** Nearshore littoral zone — interior shorelines, bars, and points over gravel substrate at 0.5–5 m depth, sampled via trammel nets.
- **Sampling effort:** Total of 33.40 net-units (1 unit = 100 m² of netting set for 24 hours), accumulated over a 5-day period (10–14 May 1993).
- **Community composition:** The catch was dominated (86.0% of total catch) by the introduced [[Common Carp]], reflecting the broader trend of non-native fish dominance in [[Lake Mohave]] and other lower Colorado River reservoirs. Other non-natives present included [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*, 6.3%), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*, 2.4%), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*, 0.4%), and [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*, 0.1%).
- **Native species representation:** The two native, federally endangered species — [[Bonytail Chub]] (1.8%) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (2.9%) — together made up less than 5% of the total catch, underscoring their rarity relative to the introduced fish community within this altered reservoir ecosystem.
- **Conservation management interface:** Bonytail chub captured were either fin-clipped/PIT-tagged and released, or transported live to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery for eventual transfer to the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] brood stock program, directly linking wild population sampling to ex-situ conservation breeding efforts.

## Key Findings

- **Catch per effort (CPE)** for the full assemblage was 25.582 (fish per unit effort); *Cyprinus carpio* alone contributed a CPE of 21.138, more than 80% of total effort-adjusted catch.
- **Native fish CPE** was low: *Gila elegans* CPE = 0.449; *Xyrauchen texanus* CPE = 0.719 — reflecting sparse, but detectable, remnant/reintroduced populations of these endangered [[Colorado River]] endemics in [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Sex ratio balance** in the razorback sucker sample was even (12 males : 12 females), with females averaging larger body size.
- **Recapture evidence** (3 of 39 razorback suckers) indicates some individuals persist and are re-encountered across sampling events, useful for future demographic and survival analyses.
- **Direct conservation action:** The smallest bonytail chub captured was specifically marked (fin clip + PIT tag) and released, likely to allow future field identification, while larger individuals were removed from the wild to bolster the captive brood stock at Dexter NFH&TC — a strategy balancing in-situ monitoring with ex-situ genetic conservation.
- **Multi-agency collaboration:** The survey was a coordinated effort among Arizona State University, the Bureau of Reclamation, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Nevada Department of Wildlife, and Arizona Game and Fish Department, exemplifying interagency management of endangered big-river fishes in the [[Lower Colorado River]] basin.
