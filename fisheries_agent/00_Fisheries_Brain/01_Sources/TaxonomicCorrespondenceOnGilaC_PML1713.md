# Taxonomic Correspondence on Gila cypha, Gila elegans, and Gila robusta Identifications

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This 1978 letter from [[Robert R Miller]], Curator of Fishes at the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]], responds to [[Royal D Suttkus]] of the [[Tulane University Museum Of Natural History]]. Miller reviews the identification of three juvenile fish from the Grand Canyon region that Suttkus and Glenn Clemmer had illustrated as [[Gila Cypha]], and questions whether they are that species. Miller concludes that the specimens are more likely [[Gila Robusta]] and possibly [[Gila Elegans]], and he suspects that the fish are misidentified. The letter also covers the loan of specimens, a request for [[Thorichthys]] material, and a request for a size series of [[Cichlasoma Steindachneri]] from Laguna de Champayán. An attached table compares meristic and morphometric data for six juveniles.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Personal letter (correspondence), with an enclosed comparison table.
- **Date:** April 17, 1978 (the letter header reads "1978" after OCR correction).
- **Authors and recipients:** Miller to Suttkus; copies to Glenn H. Clemmer and Gerald R. Smith.
- **Enclosure:** A table titled "Certain Meristic and Morphometric Juveniles of Gila cypha, G. elegans, and G. robusta." It gives data for six specimens, expressed in thousandths of standard length (SL) for morphometric values.
- **Specimens in the table:**
  - *Gila cypha* (TU 99151; UMMZ 180090), Green River, Utah.
  - *Gila elegans* (TU 101404; UMMZ 194865), Green River below Dome, Utah.
  - *Gila robusta* (TU 99135; UMMZ 161782), Ashley Creek, Utah.
- **Characters measured:** Dorsal, anal, and gill raker counts; vertebrae; standard length; head length; eye diameter; snout length; preanal length; head depth; dorsal and anal basal lengths; predorsal length; anal origin to caudal base; pectoral, pelvic, and upper jaw lengths; body depth; and caudal peduncle depth.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Type locality dispute:** The [[Little Colorado River]] (basin) is the type locality for both [[Gila Elegans]] and [[Gila Robusta]]. Miller notes that the actual capture locality cited by Smith, Miller, and Hubbs is not the Zuni River, New Mexico, but the base of Grand Falls, a short distance up the Little Colorado River. Miller argues that it is implausible for these two species to have disappeared from the Little Colorado system while only [[Gila Cypha]] survived there.
- **Range and distribution:** Miller states that the [[Humpback Chub]] has not been found in recent collections from throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]]. He also notes that a particular ray count was rare among specimens he had examined since 1945.
- **Regional context:** Miller argues that the [[Grand Canyon]] chubs are a complex of closely related forms. He calls the *Gila robusta* complex "tricky" taxonomically.
- **Coastal Mexican fauna:** The letter mentions [[Cichlasoma Steindachneri]] collected on 29 December 1952 from Laguna de Champayán near Tampico. Miller suggests that a coastal, brackish-water form may exist in the Tampico estuary area.

## Key Findings

**1. Identification of the Grand Canyon juveniles**
- Miller reviewed the three fish illustrated as Fig. 9 in Suttkus and Clemmer's paper. He suspects that two, and possibly all three, are not *G. cypha*.
- The specimen identified as *G. elegans* is excluded from that species based on the anal ray count and vertebral count, which together do not support the identification. Miller notes that *G. elegans* was only a tentative determination.
- Miller states that the fish identified as *G. robusta* agrees with typical *G. robusta robusta* except for a few features. These include 15 gill rakers (unusually low for the species), a short dorsal basal length, a long predorsal length, a short anal origin to caudal base, and a long pelvic length.

**2. Meristic benchmarks**
- For typical *G. cypha*, Miller reports the following counts from specimens throughout the range below Grand Canyon:
  - Dorsal rays: 9 (rare), 10 (most common), 11 (uncommon).
  - Anal rays: 9 (13 specimens), 10 (52 specimens), 11 (7 specimens). The typical formula is D. 9, A. 10.
- Gill rakers on the second arch are rarely 13 and are typically 14–16.
- Vertebrae of 20 were found in only one of 50 fish examined.

**3. Morphometric differences**
- Miller states that the suspect *G. cypha* has a larger eye, greater preanal length, shorter dorsal and anal basal lengths, a longer predorsal length, a greater distance between anal origin and caudal base, a longer pectoral fin, and a much longer upper jaw than expected for good *G. cypha*.
- The table's measured values are summarized in the enclosure rather than reproduced here.

**4. Critique of the original paper**
- Miller notes that Suttkus and Clemmer cited only two of his publications on *G. cypha* (and one co-authored work), though he has referred to the species in 16 separate publications, only two of which appeared in 1976–1977.
- Miller suggests that reviewer selection was poor. He notes that Jim Deacon, an ecologist, is appropriate for comment on reproduction, age, and growth, but that Jim Williams, though a fine systematic ichthyologist, lacks long experience with Colorado River fishes.

**5. Other administrative and research matters**
- The three *Gila* specimens loaned by Suttkus will be returned.
- Miller reports that the Barbour–Mills revision of *Algansea* is in press, and he expects to provide a copy.
- Miller had earlier requested Mexican *Thorichthys* specimens, which are needed for a revision of the cichlid group. He had received no reply.
- Miller requests a size series of about 200 *Cichlasoma steindachneri* from Laguna de Champayán, ranging from smallest to largest, from the 1952 Darnell and Lineron collection. He notes that the existing sample of 11 fish is "intriguing but inadequate" for resolving whether a brackish-water coastal form exists in the Tampico area.
