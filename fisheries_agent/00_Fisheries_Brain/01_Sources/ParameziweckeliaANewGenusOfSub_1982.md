# Parameximeckelia, a New Genus of Subterranean Amphipod Crustacean (Hadziidae) from Northern México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hadziidae #amphipoda #stygobiont

## Executive Summary
This paper (Holsinger, 1982) is **not** about fish but about a stygobiontic (subterranean groundwater) amphipod crustacean. It formally establishes the new genus **[[Parameximeckelia]]**, erected to accommodate *Mexiweckelia particeps* Holsinger, originally described in [[Holsinger & Minckley 1971]] from a groundwater outlet in the Cuatro Ciénegas basin, Coahuila, México. Comparative morphological analysis demonstrated that *M. particeps* differs substantially from the other two species of [[Mexiweckelia]] (*M. colei* and *M. mitchelli*), warranting generic separation. Based on a high number of retained plesiomorphic (ancestral) characters, *Parameximeckelia* is identified as the most primitive genus within the family [[Hadziidae]] known from continental North America, and by extension the most primitive within the hadziid generic complex of the western Caribbean region. The paper also situates this genus within the broader biogeographic context of subterranean and anchialine hadziid amphipods across México, Texas, and the Caribbean.

## Background & Description
*Parameximeckelia particeps* is a small, unpigmented, eyeless stygobiont (body lengths of examined specimens 4.0–5.5 mm), exhibiting classic troglomorphic reduction consistent with subterranean groundwater life.

**Diagnostic features of the genus:**
- **Eyes/pigment:** Absent (typical subterranean facies).
- **Interantennal lobe:** Distinct, rounded anteriorly.
- **Antenna 1:** Longer than antenna 2; exceptionally elongate at ~80% of body length (vs. ~50% in [[Mexiweckelia]]); bears esthetascs on some flagellar segments; accessory flagellum 1-segmented (vs. vestigial/absent in *Mexiweckelia*).
- **Antenna 2:** Peduncular segment 4 with a few dorsal spines.
- **Mandible:** Molar prominent and triturative; molar seta absent from left mandible; palp lacking.
- **Maxilla 1:** Inner plate with apical plumose setae (vs. naked setae in *Mexiweckelia*); outer plate with 9 apical serrate spines (vs. 7 in *Mexiweckelia*); palp 2-segmented.
- **Maxilla 2:** Inner plate broader than outer, with oblique row of naked setae; both plates with coarse apical setae.
- **Maxilliped:** Inner plate with bladelike spines and naked/plumose setae; outer plate with rows of bladelike spines; palp 4-segmented.
- **Lower lip:** Outer lobes well developed; inner lobes absent; mandibular processes long and slender.
- **Gnathopods:** Not sexually dimorphic (a key distinguishing character from *Mexiweckelia*). Propod of gnathopod 1 subrectangular; palm short, slightly oblique, with double row of un-notched spine teeth. Gnathopod 2 propod elongate, longer than gnathopod 1. Segment 5 of both gnathopods unlobate, non-pubescent, bearing clusters of small **rastellate setae** — a feature unique within Hadziidae, otherwise typical of family [[Crangonyctidae]].
- **Pereopods:** 3 and 4 subequal with shallow coxal plates; 6 and 7 subequal, longer than pereopod 5, exceeding 50% of body length. Coxal gills large, subovate, present on pereopods 2–8; sternal gills absent.
- **Brood plates:** Small, not fully developed/setose in examined specimens.
- **Pleonal plates:** Posterior margins straight with 3–4 stiff setae (vs. single seta in *Mexiweckelia*); posterior corners not produced.
- **Pleopods:** Biramous, peduncles with 4–5 coupling spines.
- **Uronites:** Free (unfused), bearing groups of dorsolateral spines (more numerous than in *Mexiweckelia*, up to 14).
- **Uropods 1–2:** Biramous, not sexually dimorphic; uropod 1 peduncle with ventrolateral spines.
- **Uropod 3:** Comparatively long, biramous (magniramus type), rami 1-segmented with plumose marginal setae.
- **Telson:** Longer than broad, deeply incised nearly to base, apical lobes and lateral margins with slender spines — more deeply incised and more heavily armed than in *Mexiweckelia*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Subterranean freshwater groundwater outlet/spring, part of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] hydrological system in Coahuila, northern México — a groundwater-fed desert wetland complex renowned for endemic stygobiont and phreatic fauna.
- **Life history mode:** Fully stygobiontic (obligate subterranean groundwater dweller), lacking eyes and pigmentation, consistent with life in dark, low-energy phreatic/interstitial environments.
- **Biogeographic context:** Part of a broader radiation of subterranean hadziid amphipods across the Nearctic–Caribbean transition, including:
  - [[Mayaweckelia]] (Yucatán Peninsula caves, southern México)
  - [[Mexiweckelia]] (caves/phreatic waters, northern México)
  - [[Allotexiweckelia]] and [[Texiweckelia]] (Edwards Aquifer, south-central Texas, USA)
  - [[Weckelia]] and [[Paraweckelia]] (Cuban caves)
  - [[Alloweckelia]] (Puerto Rico cave)
  - [[Saliweckelia]] (interstitial hyperhaline habitats, Netherlands Antilles)
  - [[Metaniphargus]] (brackish caves, wells, springs of the Greater/Lesser Antilles)
  - [[Protohadzia]] (shallow marine waters off Puerto Rico and Bimini lagoon, Bahamas) — notably the only eyed, fully marine hadziid known from the Caribbean region.
- **Trophic role:** Presumed detritivore/scraper based on molar morphology (prominent, triturative molar structure typical of grazing/processing organic detritus in groundwater systems).

## Key Findings
- **New genus erected:** *Parameximeckelia*, type species *Mexiweckelia particeps* Holsinger, by monotypy; gender feminine. Name derives from Greek "para" (beside/near) + *Mexiweckelia*, referencing the related Mexican subterranean amphipod genus.
- **Generic separation rationale:** *P. particeps* differs from *Mexiweckelia* in at least eight major characters, including antenna 1 length (~80% vs. ~50% of body length), accessory flagellum development, maxilla 1 setae/spine counts, absence of gnathopod sexual dimorphism, presence of rastellate setae, pleonal plate setation, uronite spine counts, and telson incision depth.
- **Phylogenetic significance:** *Parameximeckelia* possesses the greatest number of plesiomorphic characters among all continental North American hadziid genera, making it the most primitive member of the family [[Hadziidae]] in this region and in the broader western Caribbean hadziid complex.
- **Unique character:** Presence of rastellate setae/spines on the gnathopods is unique to this species within Hadziidae, though this feature is common in the unrelated family [[Crangonyctidae]] — suggesting possible convergent or retained ancestral trait.
- **Taxonomic corrections:** The paper corrects several errors in earlier descriptions of *Mexiweckelia colei* and *M. mitchelli* (e.g., setae type on maxilla 1, omitted ventrolateral spines on uropod 1, mislabeled plumose vs. naked setae on uropod 3, incorrect molar seta description).
- **Broader taxonomic context (Addendum):** Citing Barnard & Karaman (1982), two former species of *Texiweckelia* were elevated to generic rank (*Texiweckeliopsis* and *Holsingerius*), bringing the total number of continental North American hadziid genera to seven, five of which are monotypic — underscoring the high degree of endemism and generic-level diversification among Mexican and Texan subterranean amphipods.
