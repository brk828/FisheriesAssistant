## Executive Summary

This 1991 U.S. Bureau of Reclamation memorandum documents a three-day fish sampling survey (October 21–23, 1991) conducted across eight sites within the San Carlos Irrigation Project (SCIP), downstream of the [[Ashurst-Hayden Diversion Dam]] on the [[Gila River]], Arizona. The survey's objective was to determine whether nonnative fish species transported via the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) canal system had been introduced into SCIP waters, which are hydrologically connected to native Gila River fish habitat. Sampling coincided with the annual system dry-up, concentrating fish in isolated pools and improving capture efficiency via seining, backpack electrofishing, and gillnetting.

A total of **5,130 fish** representing **10 species** were collected. The assemblage was overwhelmingly dominated by nonnative species — [[Western Mosquitofish]] (38.3%) and [[Red Shiner]] (35.3%) together comprised over 73% of the catch. Critically, only **one native fish** was collected across all sites: a single [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), highlighting severe displacement of native ichthyofauna by introduced species in this reach of the Gila River drainage. No CAP-associated species of special concern (e.g., striped bass, grass carp) were detected, suggesting no new introductions via CAP water delivery had yet occurred at the time of sampling.

## Background & Description

Limited individual morphometric data were recorded; the survey focused on species presence/abundance rather than detailed morphology. Notable size data include:

- **Threadfin Shad** (*Dorosoma petenense*): highly abundant in John Deere Sump, total length range 25–60 mm (juveniles/young-of-year dominant).
- **Common Carp** (*Cyprinus carpio*): average total length 235 mm (adults captured via gillnet).
- **Green Sunfish** (*Lepomis cyanellus*): one large individual recorded at 230 mm total length.
- Other species (mosquitofish, red shiner, fathead minnow, bullhead, catfish) were counted/estimated without systematic length measurements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The survey sampled a range of lentic and lotic freshwater habitats within an irrigation-dominated system:

- **Riverine pools** (Olberg Siphon, Gila River below Ashurst/Hayden Dam) — remnant ponded water in the dry riverbed, sampled at water temperatures of 19.5–23°C.
- **Reservoir habitat** (Picacho Reservoir) — shallow, low-water conditions (3.75 ft gauge depth), supporting a modestly diverse assemblage of five species.
- **Irrigation sumps** (John Deere Sump, Southside Canal Sump, Japanese Camp/9-60 Sump) — artificial standing-water bodies acting as refugia during system dry-up; John Deere Sump held the highest fish density (2,365 individuals), dominated by threadfin shad and red shiner.
- **Barrier-adjacent pools** (China Wash electrical fish barrier) — pools both upstream and downstream of an exposed electrical fish barrier; associated with incidental mortality of [[Bullfrog|bullfrogs]] (*Rana catesbeiana*) and crayfish caught in the de-energized electrode array.
- **Lotic river habitat** (Gila River below Ashurst/Hayden Dam) — diverse mesohabitats (riffles, runs, pools, backwaters) sampled at ~20 cfs flow and 20°C, representing the only site yielding the sole native fish record.

Non-fish fauna noted incidentally included [[Bullfrog]] tadpoles/juveniles (~30 individuals at Picacho Reservoir; adults at China Wash) and freshwater shrimp (*Palaemonetes* sp.) at Southside Canal Sump, plus crayfish at China Wash.

The dominance of [[Western Mosquitofish]] and [[Red Shiner]] — both widely introduced, highly tolerant nonnative species — reflects classic degraded/altered lentic irrigation habitat conditions, where native Sonoran Desert fishes such as [[Longfin Dace]] have been nearly extirpated from this reach.

## Key Findings

- **10 fish species** collected across **8 sites**; total catch = **5,130 individuals**.
- **Nonnative dominance**: Mosquitofish (38.3%) and Red Shiner (35.3%) accounted for ~73.6% of total catch.
- **Native fish near-absence**: Only **1 Longfin Dace** collected (0.02% of total catch), found at the Gila River site immediately below Ashurst/Hayden Diversion Dam — the only site with lotic riverine habitat diversity (riffles/runs/pools/backwaters).
- **No CAP-specific invasive species detected**: Species restricted to CAP waters and absent from the Gila River (e.g., [[Striped Bass]], [[Grass Carp]]) were **not found** in SCIP waters, indicating no confirmed cross-contamination via CAP water delivery at time of survey.
- **Highest abundance site**: John Deere Sump (2,365 fish), driven by extremely high threadfin shad and red shiner numbers (700+ each in some hauls).
- **Species richness by site** varied from a single species (Olberg Siphon: mosquitofish only) to five species (Picacho Reservoir; Japanese Camp Sump area).
- **Incidental bycatch mortality**: Bullfrogs and crayfish found dead in the de-energized China Wash electrical barrier array, suggesting the barrier's electrical field caused non-target mortality even without full water flow.
- **Methodology**: Standardized seining (20×4 ft, 1/8-inch mesh; ~30–40 ft hauls), backpack electrofishing (12-volt Smith/Root shocker), and gillnetting (100-ft "Green Meanie," 1.5-inch mesh) were used, with effort recorded (shocking seconds, net soak time, haul counts) to allow semi-quantitative comparison across sites.
- **Management implication**: The survey supports continued monitoring of the CAP–SCIP interface for nonnative species introductions that could further threaten native Gila River fish populations, given the fish community's near-total replacement by nonnatives in this reach.
