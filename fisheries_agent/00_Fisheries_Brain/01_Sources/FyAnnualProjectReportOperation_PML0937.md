# Wahweap State Fish Hatchery Operation and Maintenance, FY2002 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Colorado River Recovery Program annual report (Project 29c, FY2002) covers the operation and maintenance of the [[Wahweap State Fish Hatchery]] at Big Water, Utah. The hatchery rears [[Bonytail]] for stocking in the Upper Colorado River Basin, in Utah and Colorado, and maintains a backup brood stock of [[Razorback Sucker]]. Work was ongoing and on track, and the budget status reports 100% of FY2002 work completed. The principal investigators were Wayne Gustavson and Quent Bradwisch, Utah Division of Wildlife Resources.

## Background & Description

The project supports the [[Recovery Program]] for four endangered Colorado River fishes: [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. Its stated link to the recovery plan includes:

- **General Recovery Program support:** Secure and manage presumptive genetic stocks in refugia, operate and maintain facilities, and conduct annual fish propagation.
- **Green River Action Plan (mainstem):** Implement the stocking plan for the four endangered fishes.
- **Colorado River Action Plan (mainstem):** Implement the bonytail and other State stocking plans, including a State stocking plan for the four endangered fishes in Utah.
- **Colorado River Action Plan (Gunnison River):** Implement the Colorado Pikeminnow and Razorback Sucker State stocking plans.

Wahweap Hatchery is identified in the plan as facility IV.C.3.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The hatchery sits within the regulated Colorado River system, where the four endangered fishes are being stocked to support self-sustaining populations. Rearing took place in outdoor ponds, and growth was monitored using minnow traps, seining and cast nets. Pond productivity was increased by fertilization, which raised the amount of food available to young fish. The report notes that the bonytail fry received in 2002 were wild-spawned, with an unknown parentage. This created a genetic and policy concern, which is discussed under Key Findings.

The fish stocked for the Utah plan (about 10,000, per the garbled source) were pit tagged according to the plan.

### Production and Rearing

- Wahweap received and reared **30,000** bonytail fry from Dexter National Fish Hatchery.
- All age-one fish spawned to some extent in the hatchery ponds during the year.
- Weekly growth was monitored in the rearing ponds.
- Fish were stocked in the Colorado River and Green River, and the state fair display fish were released into the Green River near Jensen.

### Public Outreach

Wahweap provided endangered Colorado River fishes for display at the Utah State Fair in Salt Lake City. Bonytail, humpback chub and razorback sucker were on display for two weeks. Two bonytail were later transferred to Colorado for display at the Colorado State Fair.

### Losses and Shortcomings

- **30,000 bonytail** were destroyed in 2002. These fish were wild-spawned, their exact parentage was unknown, and they were about 100 mm and in very good condition. They were destroyed under the Recovery Program fish policy because of the uncertain origin.
- Fish losses during the year were attributed to moving, tagging, and sampling for fish health inspection.

### Fish Remaining on Station (Fall 2002)

The report lists the following Colorado River fish held at the station:

- 25,000 fish, 120 mm and larger
- 55,000 fish, 120 mm and larger
- 30,000 fish, 70 mm and larger
- 600 fish, 250 mm and larger
- 947 adult fish (size partly illegible)

### Recommendations

- Continue rearing bonytail at Wahweap to meet the integrated stocking plan.
- Maintain the razorback sucker backup brood stock, supplied for either species if requested by the Biology Committee.
- Pit tag 11,000 bonytail and stock them in the fall.
