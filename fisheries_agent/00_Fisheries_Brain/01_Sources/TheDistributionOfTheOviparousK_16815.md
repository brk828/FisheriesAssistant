## Executive Summary

This 1975 paper by Kenneth Relyea synthesizes the known geographic distribution of oviparous killifishes (family [[Cyprinodontidae]]) within the state of Florida. Florida hosts an exceptionally rich cyprinodontid fauna, with 19–21 species present, three of which are endemic to the state. The genus [[Fundulus]] is particularly well-represented, comprising 10–12 species — nearly half of all Fundulus species known in the United States (46 species across 10 genera nationally, 8 genera occurring in Florida). The paper documents species-by-species range data, taxonomic uncertainties, disjunct populations, and probable glacial relict distributions, drawing on museum collection records (Jacksonville University, University of Florida, Florida State University, Tulane University, USNM) and prior literature (Miller 1955b, Brown 1957, Moore 1968, Rosen 1964/1974).

## Background & Description

The paper is primarily distributional/zoogeographic rather than morphometric, but it notes several diagnostic and taxonomically relevant morphological features:

- **[[Fundulus chrysotus]]** vs **[[Fundulus cingulatus]]**: distinguished by gill raker shape — *F. chrysotus* has pointed gill rakers, while *F. cingulatus* has blunt gill rakers.
- **[[Fundulus majalis]]** and **[[Fundulus similis]]**: considered by some authors (including the author) to be conspecific based on morphological and karyological similarity (Chen, 1971 chromosome study found no karyological distinction).
- **[[Fundulus grandis]]** and **[[Fundulus saguanus]]**: the latter is treated as a distinct, geographically isolated (allopatric) form derived from *F. grandis* stock, illustrating recent evolutionary divergence.
- **[[Cyprinodon hubbsi]]**: endemic pupfish morphologically/taxonomically distinct from the widespread **[[Cyprinodon variegatus]]**, though populations (e.g., Lake Okeechobee) are sometimes difficult to assign without close examination.
- Taxonomic subspecies structure noted for **[[Floridichthys carpio]]** (F. c. carpio in Florida vs. F. c. barbouri and F. c. polyommus in Yucatan) and **[[Cyprinodon variegatus]]** (subspecies C. v. artifrons in Yucatan, C. v. riverendi in Florida Keys/Cuba).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Florida's cyprinodontid fauna occupies a broad spectrum of freshwater-to-marine habitats:

- **Freshwater specialists**: [[Fundulus chrysotus]], [[Fundulus cingulatus]], [[Fundulus seminolis]] (Seminole killifish, endemic to Florida peninsula, common in the Suwannee River), [[Jordanella floridae]] (Florida flagfish, peninsula endemic), [[Leptolucania ommata]] (ocellated killifish), [[Lucania goodei]] (bluefin killifish, common in Dead Lakes/Apalachicola drainage), and the [[Fundulus notti]]/[[Fundulus lineolatus]] "star head topminnow" complex (pond-dwelling).
- **Stream-dwelling counterpart**: [[Fundulus olivaceus]] (blackspotted topminnow) occupies flowing-water habitats analogous to the pond-dwelling *F. notti* complex, ranging into the Choctawhatchee River in the Florida panhandle.
- **Brackish/estuarine species**: [[Adinia xenica]] (diamond killifish, protected brackish waters), [[Fundulus confluentus]] (marsh killifish, brackish and freshwater, more freshwater-associated in south Florida), [[Fundulus jenkinsi]] (salt marsh topminnow, rare, brackish specialist), [[Lucania parva]] (rainwater killifish, brackish/occasionally freshwater, including isolated spring populations at Alexander Springs and Juniper Springs in the St. Johns River drainage).
- **Euryhaline coastal species with disjunct Gulf/Atlantic populations**: [[Fundulus grandis]] (gulf killifish) and its Florida Bay/Keys relict form [[Fundulus saguanus]]; [[Fundulus heteroclitus]] (mummichog, temperate species overlapping *F. grandis* in northeastern Florida); the [[Fundulus majalis]]/[[Fundulus similis]] complex (striped/longnose killifish).
- **Rare/poorly known**: [[Rivulus marmoratus]] (mangrove rivulus) — likely the rarest Florida killifish, possibly abundant in salt-influenced Everglades habitats, but with an uncertain full distribution; also occurs in Cuba and the Bahamas.
- **Lake endemics**: [[Cyprinodon hubbsi]] (Lake Eustis pupfish) is restricted to at least six large central Florida freshwater lakes (Eustis, Harris, Dora, Weir, Griffin, Yale), with a notable ecological "enigma" of absence from similar lakes (e.g., Lake Placid, Big Lake Tohopekaliga).
- Contrasted with the more widely distributed and ecologically flexible [[Cyprinodon variegatus]] (sheepshead pupfish/minnow), ranging continuously from Massachusetts to Texas in both fresh and brackish waters.

## Key Findings

- Florida contains 19–21 species of oviparous cyprinodontids, with 3 species endemic to the state ([[Cyprinodon hubbsi]], [[Fundulus seminolis]], [[Jordanella floridae]]).
- The genus **[[Fundulus]]** dominates Florida's killifish diversity (10–12 species), representing nearly half of all U.S. Fundulus species.
- Several species pairs/complexes show classic patterns of **disjunct Gulf-vs-Atlantic coastal distributions** in Florida, attributed to Pleistocene (Wisconsin) glacial history:
  - [[Fundulus grandis]] (Gulf/south Florida) vs. [[Fundulus heteroclitus]] (Atlantic, temperate) — overlapping/hybridizing zone between Amelia Island and Brevard Co.
  - [[Fundulus saguanus]] treated as a **glacial relict**, now allopatric to *F. grandis*, restricted to Florida Bay, the Keys, and Cuba.
  - [[Fundulus majalis]]/[[Fundulus similis]] complex shows an analogous grading pattern between Matanzas Inlet and Flagler Beach, paralleling the *F. grandis*/*F. heteroclitus* transition zone.
  - [[Lucania parva]] Florida Keys populations may similarly represent an isolated glacial relict, allopatric to both Atlantic and Gulf populations.
- The related Yucatan species **[[Fundulus grandissimus]]** may represent another glacial relict within the *F. grandis* species complex, highlighting a broader Gulf-Caribbean biogeographic pattern.
- Several taxonomic uncertainties remain unresolved, including the specific status of the [[Fundulus notti]]/[[Fundulus lineolatus]] complex (Brown 1958 recognized 3 subspecies: *F. n. lineolatus*, *F. n. notti*, *F. n. dispar*; Rivas 1966 elevated *lineolatus* to full species) and possible mislabeling/collection-mixing issues affecting historical **[[Floridichthys carpio]]** (as *Cyprinodon mydrus*) records from the Florida panhandle (Carrabelle, Pensacola).
- Notable range gaps exist for multiple brackish species along the southwest Florida Gulf coast between Marco (Collier Co.) and Cape Sable/Florida Bay, suggesting either genuine absence or a critical under-sampled zone relevant to zoogeographic interpretation.
- The paper calls for further ecological, systematic, and distributional research on several poorly studied taxa, notably [[Fundulus cingulatus]] ("least studied" Florida freshwater killie), [[Lucania parva]] spring populations, and [[Rivulus marmoratus]].
