# Fish Distribution (Numbers and Weight) by Program and Agency — FY 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-hatcheries #aquaculture #fisheries-management #salmonidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] administrative report summarizing **fish and fish egg distribution data for Fiscal Year 1992** across the national federal fish hatchery system. Rather than presenting original ecological or biological findings, it provides a standardized coding framework (species, agency, program, water type, and facility designator codes) and a station-by-station statistical accounting of hatchery output. The **report total** for FY 1992 was:

- **83,428,710 eggs** shipped
- **208,896,734 fish** distributed (numbers)
- **6,510,962 lbs** total fish weight distributed

Data are broken down by **hatchery station** (e.g., [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[Coleman National Fish Hatchery]], [[Quinault]], [[Quilcene]], [[Gavins Point]], [[Garrison Dam]]), **program code** (e.g., Restoration/Mitigation, Endangered/Threatened Species, Management Assistance), and **agency code** (e.g., [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Army Corps of Engineers]], [[State Government]] agencies).

## Background & Description

The report was compiled by the Division of Fish Hatcheries (contact: Roger Schulz) to standardize reporting of fish and egg distribution across the National Fish Hatchery System and cooperating agencies. It establishes three parallel coding systems used to classify distribution records:

- **Agency Codes** — identify the recipient or cooperating agency for stocked fish, spanning military branches ([[Air Force]], [[Army]], [[Navy]]), land/water management agencies ([[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Corps of Engineers]]), tribal entities ([[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], Indian Tribe non-BIA), state and local governments, universities, and private/foreign entities.
- **Water Type Codes** — classify the receiving habitat: Aquarium, Estuarine, Hatchery (Federal/Other), Lake/Pond, Reservoir, Stream/River/Canal, Research, and Tailwater.
- **Program Codes** — classify the management purpose of the stocking action:
  - **RM** – Restoration/Mitigation
  - **ES** – Endangered/Threatened Species
  - **MA** – Management Assistance on Federal and Indian Land
  - **RE** – Research and Development
  - **FP** – Farm Pond Program
  - **OT** – Other
  - Egg Shipment and Technology/Health categories also appear in the broader framework.
- **Facility Designator Codes** (for broodstock reporting) distinguish National Fish Hatcheries (NFH), State Fish Hatcheries (SFH), university/research labs, foreign hatcheries, and Indian Reservation facilities.

A **species code table** (pages 3–4) lists roughly 150 species codes, covering a broad taxonomic range including:
- Anadromous/salmonid species: [[Chinook Salmon]] (fall, spring, summer, winter, late-fall runs), [[Coho Salmon]], [[Chum Salmon]], [[Sockeye Salmon]], [[Pink Salmon]], [[Atlantic Salmon]] (including landlocked form), [[Steelhead]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Lake Trout]], [[Cutthroat Trout]], [[Golden Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Splake]] and other hybrid trout crosses.
- Warmwater sportfish: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Spotted Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Blue Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[White Bass]], [[Striped Bass]] and hybrid striped bass, [[Walleye]], [[Sauger]], and Saugeye hybrids, [[Muskellunge]] and Tiger Muskie.
- Federally listed/imperiled species: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]], [[Leon Springs Pupfish]], [[Pecos Pupfish]], [[Big Bend Gambusia]], [[Woundfin]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Sucker]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Fountain Darter]], [[Snail Darter]], [[Leopard Darter]], [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], [[Pallid Sturgeon]], [[Atlantic Sturgeon]].
- Forage/other native species: [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Freshwater Drum]], gars, mooneye, mudminnow, stickleback species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The dataset reflects the operational scope of the **National Fish Hatchery System** and its role in three principal ecological management functions:

1. **Restoration/Mitigation (RM):** The dominant program category by volume, used to offset habitat losses from dam construction and hydropower operations (e.g., stations tied to [[Bonneville Power Administration]] and [[Corps of Engineers]] projects) and to restore anadromous fish runs blocked by dams — evident in massive [[Chinook Salmon]], [[Coho Salmon]], and [[Steelhead]] releases at Pacific Northwest stations such as [[Quinault]], [[Quilcene]], [[Coleman]], [[Little White Salmon]]/Willard, [[Winthrop]], and [[Entiat]].
2. **Endangered/Threatened Species (ES) propagation:** Concentrated at stations serving arid Southwestern watersheds, most notably [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico), which reported distribution of highly imperiled Chihuahuan Desert fishes (Big Bend Gambusia, Comanche Springs Pupfish, Leon Springs Pupfish, Yaqui species). Other ES activity appears at [[San Marcos]], [[Mescalero]], and [[Lahontan]] (cutthroat trout conservation), reflecting habitat contexts such as spring-fed desert systems and closed alkaline lake basins.
3. **Management Assistance (MA) and Farm Pond Program (FP):** Support state, tribal, and private pond/reservoir fisheries, illustrated by widespread stocking of Channel Catfish, Largemouth Bass, and Bluegill at stations like [[Private John Allen]], [[Tishomingo]], [[Uvalde]], and [[Inks Dam]] — largely serving warmwater lentic (lake/pond) habitats rather than natural riverine systems.

Water type codes indicate that hatchery output was distributed across a full spectrum of freshwater and transitional habitats — streams/rivers/canals, lakes/ponds, reservoirs, estuarine waters, and tailwaters below dams — underscoring the cross-habitat scale of federal fish culture operations in FY 1992. The presence of estuarine codes and species like [[Striped Bass]], [[American Shad]], [[Weakfish]], and [[Snook]] indicates linkage to diadromous and coastal fishery restoration efforts (e.g., [[Orangeburg]], [[Bo Ginn]], [[Bears Bluff]] stations in the southeastern U.S.).

## Key Findings

- **Total FY1992 output:** 83,428,710 eggs; 208,896,734 fish (individuals); 6,510,962 lbs biomass distributed system-wide.
- **High-volume walleye production:** [[Gavins Point]] station alone distributed **21,928,348 Walleye** (fry-stage, low aggregate weight), reflecting large-scale fry stocking for reservoir fisheries under COE/BOR/state programs.
- **Garrison Dam** station distributed **5,823,950 Rainbow Trout equivalents/Walleye** and large numbers of Northern Pike, Sauger, and hybrid Walleye x Sauger (Saugeye) for Corps of Engineers reservoir mitigation.
- **Pacific salmon restoration hubs:** [[Coleman]] (26,552,957 fish, mostly Chinook/eggs), [[Carson]] (~2.57M fish), [[Quilcene]] (~2.97M fish, largely Chinook and Coho), and [[Quinault]] (~3.5M fish) represent major anadromous restoration/mitigation nodes tied to Columbia River and Pacific Northwest hydropower systems.
- **Endangered species stronghold:** [[Dexter]] station's distribution records (Comanche Springs Pupfish, Leon Springs Pupfish, Big Bend Gambusia, Yaqui Catfish/Chub/Sucker/Topminnow) confirm its role as a primary federal refugium/propagation center for critically endangered Southwestern desert fishes under the Endangered/Threatened Species (ES) program.
- **Atlantic salmon and diadromous restoration:** Stations such as [[Craig Brook]], [[Green Lake]], [[Nashua]], [[North Attleboro]], [[Richard Cronin]], and [[Pittsford]] report Atlantic Salmon (ATS), Landlocked Atlantic Salmon (LAS), and American Shad distributions tied to Northeastern river restoration programs.
- **Broad agency participation:** Recipient agencies span nearly the full breadth of federal, state, tribal, and private stakeholders — including [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]], [[Corps of Engineers]], [[Tennessee Valley Authority]], [[State Government]] fish and wildlife agencies, universities, and private landowners under the Farm Pond Program — indicating the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service hatchery system's function as a cross-jurisdictional supplier for conservation, mitigation, and recreational fisheries management.
- **Species code standardization:** The report's codified species list (~150 codes) reflects the taxonomic diversity managed under the National Fish Hatchery System in FY1992, spanning coldwater salmonids, warmwater centrarchids/ictalurids, esocids, percids, acipenserids, and numerous federally listed cyprinodontiform and cyprinid species.
