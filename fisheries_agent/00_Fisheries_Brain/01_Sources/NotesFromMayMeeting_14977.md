# Biological Subcommittee ICM Proposals: Lower Colorado River MSCP Meeting of May 20, 1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fish-conservation

## Executive Summary

This is an administrative memorandum from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] Habitat Branch (Larry Riley), dated May 28, 1997, with enclosed notes from the May 20, 1997 meeting of the [[Biological Subcommittee]] of the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (LCRMSCP). The meeting identified potential [[Interim Conservation Measures]] (ICMs) for FY 1997-98 funding. Participants suggested 22 ICMs, and a balloting process ranked seven as top proposals. The [[Steering Committee]] was asked to finance at least two of these and planned a final selection at its June 26 meeting in Las Vegas. The memo also records a plan led by the Bureau of Reclamation to address the [[Willow Flycatcher]] in the [[Lake Mead]] delta.

## Background & Description

The memo describes the origin and process of the ICM effort:

- **Purpose:** Identify ICMs for financing. The 22 suggested ICMs were judged "excellent," and contact people were asked to prepare more detailed summaries.
- **Steering Committee response:** The Steering Committee asked for more detail on the proposals. Some members criticised the process: the use of two break-out groups limited participation in both interest areas, and the gap between meetings (August to May) was a concern. The memo commits to more frequent meetings and broader participation.
- **Breakout groups:** An avian group (willow flycatcher and yellow-billed cuckoo) and a fish and aquatic resources group worked in parallel. Flipcharts were scribed and facilitated by B. Werner (avian) and L. Riley (fish and aquatic).
- **Ranking method:** Participants used a balloting procedure to rank-order the proposals.

Each proposal in the notes includes a title, description, target species, contact, funding estimate and timeframe. The notes are a transcription of the flip-chart sheets, not a formal research document.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The projects target the lower Colorado River system, which supports both riparian and aquatic native fauna:

- **Riparian and avian habitat:** Willow flycatcher and yellow-billed cuckoo depend on riparian willow habitat. Threats discussed include [[Brown-Headed Cowbird]] parasitism of nests, [[Salt Cedar]] invasion, fire hazard, and loss of water to habitat. Revegetation and habitat restoration are presented as multi-year efforts, with existing willow habitat potentially usable in the near term.
- **Aquatic and wetland habitat:** Backwaters, wetlands and channels in the [[Imperial Division]] have degraded since the 1983 flooding. The memo estimates that about 50% of that area has declined and proposes restoration toward pre-1983 conditions. Water supply to the [[Limitrophe Division]] (Gadsden Bend and Gadsden Pond) is also at issue.
- **Native fish:** Big river fishes, including [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]], are the focus of rearing, genetics and population work. The notes mention that [[Selenium]] work may be relevant to the [[Yuma Clapper Rail]] recovery plan.
- **Amphibians and small fish:** The [[Relict Leopard Frog]], believed to number only a few hundred individuals, and the [[Desert Pupfish]] require refugia and population work.

### Avian Group Proposals

The avian breakout produced a first-cut list of 16 projects, plus five strategic categories: protecting existing populations, cowbird trapping, status surveys, riparian revegetation, and fire management. Ten were detailed:

- **Surveys and monitoring (Lake Mead to SIB):** Continue surveys at or above current levels, with more work needed in the Yuma area south of I-8. Contacts were Bob McKernan (San Bernardino County Museum) and Tracy McCarthy (AGFD). Could start immediately.
- **Nest monitoring:** Monitor occupied willow flycatcher areas and identify the extent of cowbird parasitism. Estimated at $25,000 for 3 months.
- **Region-wide fire threat and hazard assessment:** A multi-agency effort in which each agency supplies staff for its own lands.
- **Cowbird trapping:** Contracted through USBR or USFWS, $50,000.
- **Deer Island habitat restoration:** Contact Fred Phillips (CRIT). Total $150k, perhaps $75k from ICM funds and $75k from USBR. Existing willow habitat could become usable in the near term, with revegetation usable in 2 to 3 years.
- **Bill Williams River NWR:** Protect habitat, remove salt cedar, and plant willow poles. Contact Nancy Gilbertson (USFWS). $30k through partnership with FWS. New willows usable in 2 to 3 years.
- **Havasu NWR (Topock Marsh):** Protect habitat, clear salt cedar and manage water levels. Contact Greg Wolfe (USFWS). $40k.
- **Yuma clapper rail program:** Develop a management plan aimed at delisting, possibly including selenium work and review of the existing recovery plan. Contact Randy Wilson (AGFD). $100k over 3 years. Habitat usable in 3 to 4 years.
- **Hualapai habitat protection:** Replant Gooding willows and protect benches on the Colorado River within the Grand Canyon. Contact Dawn Bascomb. $115k.
- **Limitrophe siphons:** Build siphons from the Bypass Drain to maintain water to Gadsden Bend and Gadsden Pond. Contact Dave Smith (BLM). $15k.

### Lake Mead Delta Willow Flycatcher Action Plan

Reclamation is leading an action plan with four components:

a) Collect occurrence, breeding and nesting information in the delta this season to support risk assessment.
b) Monitor water supply and forecasts to assess effects on Lake Mead surface elevations.
c) Monitor nests through the season.
d) Hold meetings of biologists and interested parties, as needed, to evaluate risks and develop strategies if risks arise.

A willow flycatcher meeting may be called within about three weeks if nesting risks emerge in Lake Mead.

### Fish and Aquatic Resources Proposals

The fish and aquatic breakout produced 13 projects. Key entries are below.

- **Lake Mohave razorback sucker juvenile rearing:** Rear the 1996-97 cohort to releasable size, with stocking to Lake Mohave by March 1998. Contact Tom Burke (USBR). $100k.
- **Achii Hanyo Fish Rearing Facility (CRIT ponds, Poston):** Operation and maintenance of ponds developed by USFWS (Parker FRO) and CRIT. Suited to razorback sucker and bonytail, and possibly amphibians. Contact C.O. Minckley (USFWS). $100k for personnel, supplies, vehicles, utilities and O&M. Facility expected online within a month. Rearing a cohort to stockable size takes 10 to 12 months. Potential partners include the AZ Heritage Fund, the AZ Water Protection Fund and NFWF.
- **Relict leopard frog refugium:** Use Boulder City Wetlands Park ponds and other sites north of Lake Havasu. $5-10k for seasonal labour at existing sites. Results could appear within one year.
- **Desert pupfish refugium:** Build large isolated refugia, mainly on National Wildlife Refuges. Contacts Kirk Young (AGFD) and C.O. Minckley. $80k. Functional within 2 years.
- **Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery renovation (AGFD):** Renovate and line 2 ponds for razorback sucker rearing. $20k. Functional within 1 year.
- **Razorback sucker genetic analysis (ASU):** Analyse archived samples from reared juveniles to confirm that genetic diversity is being maintained. Contacts Tom Burke, Paul Marsh and W.L. Minckley. $75k.
- **Relict leopard frog genetics and field survey:** Microsatellite analyses to test distinctness from lowland leopard frog, plus augmented field surveys. Contacts Ross Haley (NPS) and Jeff Howland (AGFD). $15k for genetics and $20k for surveys.
- **Imperial Division restoration:** Dredge to restore the reach from the northern boundary of Imperial NWR to Imperial Dam toward pre-1983 conditions, including channels, backwaters and wetlands. Contact Jim Romero (USBR). Up to $4 million, implemented in increments. Current funding is $580k from the AZ Water Protection Fund plus matching Reclamation funds, with a $420k proposal to the CA Riparian Conservation Fund.
- **Relict leopard frog research and monitoring (NPS, Lake Mead NRA):** Determine habitat needs and population trends, with the aim of avoiding listing. $60k per year for three years.
- **Flannelmouth sucker status surveys (below Davis Dam):** Assess the status of an isolated population and the need for protection. Contacts C.O. Minckley and Mike Burrell (NDOW). $15-20k over 2 years.
- **Bonytail basic research:** Maintain the ASU bonytail database, characterise broodfish genetically and track wild movements. Contacts C.O. Minckley and Paul Marsh. $100k, ongoing.
- **Big River Fishes Management Plan completion (USFWS):** Finalise a revised plan for razorback sucker, bonytail, humpback chub and Colorado squawfish. Funding is listed as "moral support" only.

### Overall Status

- Seven proposals were ranked by vote. Two or more are to be financed, with a final decision at the June 26, 1997 Steering Committee meeting.
- Several projects can start immediately. Others have timeframes of 1 to 4 years, or require further planning.
- The memo commits to more frequent meetings and wider participation in future ICM work.
