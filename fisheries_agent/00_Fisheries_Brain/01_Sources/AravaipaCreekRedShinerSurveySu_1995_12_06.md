# Aravaipa Creek Red Shiner Survey — Summary Report (November 1995)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #NongameFishSurvey

## Executive Summary

This report documents a targeted eradication-monitoring survey conducted by the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), in cooperation with The Nature Conservancy (TNC), Bureau of Reclamation (BOR), Bureau of Land Management (BLM), ADEQ, NRCS, and academic partners, to detect the presence of nonnative [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) in the lower reach of [[Aravaipa Creek]], a critical native fish refugium tributary to the [[San Pedro River]] in the Gila River Basin, Arizona. The survey replicated methodology from an October 1993 baseline survey (Attachment B) but was restricted to the lower ~6 km segment between Brandenburg Wash and the terminus of surface flow.

Thirteen biologists (reduced to seven on day two) conducted approximately 60 person-hours of seining across nine sampling sites, totaling 964 seine hauls (12.9% empty). **No red shiners were detected**, a positive outcome suggesting the species has not become established or has been effectively extirpated from this reach. A robust native fish assemblage was documented, alongside two established nonnative species: [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]]. A large, deep, heavily vegetated diversion pond near the end of surface flow was flagged as a potential nonnative fish refuge warranting follow-up gill-net sampling.

## Background & Description

Limited direct morphometric data were recorded, as the survey's primary methodology emphasized presence/absence and relative abundance over individual biometrics (per protocol, "no measurements of fish length or weight will be required").

- **[[Green Sunfish]]** (*Lepomis cyanellus*, LECY): A concentration of ~35–45 individuals was collected from a single isolated deep pool at Sampling Location 1; the largest specimen measured **143 mm**.
- **Incidental herpetological note**: A [[Leopard Frog]] specimen was captured, photographed, and measured (snout-vent length = 80 mm) at Sampling Location 1, where the species was noted as "fairly common" (~25 individuals observed along the reach).
- All captured nonnative fish were preserved in 10% formalin per protocol for potential further morphological/taxonomic analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Aravaipa Creek is a spring-fed, perennial desert stream supporting one of the most intact native fish assemblages in the American Southwest, historically threatened by nonnative invasive species such as the red shiner. Habitat sampled included:

- **Run/riffle/pool sequences** with boulder-dominated substrate limiting seine effectiveness (as low as 10% channel coverage at some sites).
- **Braided, shallow channel sections** near the terminus of surface flow, where the creek was suspected to transition to subsurface flow approximately 6 km above the San Pedro confluence.
- **A large artificial diversion pond** (~200 m², >2 m deep, heavily vegetated margins) identified as a distinct lentic microhabitat with elevated potential to harbor nonnative fish populations — only juvenile green sunfish and a single longfin dace were found in the ~10% of margin sampled.
- **Isolated deep pools** connected to the main channel, which appeared to serve as refugia for green sunfish, indicating habitat partitioning between lotic-adapted natives (e.g., [[Loach Minnow]], [[Speckled Dace]]) and lentic-tolerant nonnatives.

The native community — [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonoran Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Loach Minnow]] — reflects a functioning warmwater desert stream guild adapted to flashy, low-gradient hydrology, with [[Longfin Dace]] (AGCH) overwhelmingly dominant in raw abundance across nearly all sites.

## Key Findings

- **Red shiner not detected** anywhere in the surveyed 6 km reach in November 1995, contrasting with the species' historical concern as an invasive threat in the Gila Basin.
- **964 total seine hauls** conducted across 9 sampling sites; 124 hauls (~12.9%) were empty.
- **Native species detected** (7 total): [[Longfin Dace]] (AGCH), [[Sonoran Sucker]] (CAIN), [[Roundtail Chub]] (GIRO), [[Spikedace]] (MEFU), [[Desert Sucker]] (PACL), [[Loach Minnow]] (TICO), and [[Speckled Dace]] (RHOS, noted qualitatively at Site 1 but not quantified in counted hauls).
- **Nonnative species detected** (2 total): [[Green Sunfish]] (LECY) and [[Yellow Bullhead]] (AMNA), both present at low-to-moderate densities across multiple sites, with green sunfish notably concentrated in isolated deep pools and the diversion pond.
- **Approximate combined "every-fifth-haul" counts** (relative abundance index, not absolute population):
  - AGCH (Longfin Dace): ~2,036
  - PACL (Desert Sucker): ~66
  - CAIN (Sonoran Sucker): ~36
  - TICO (Loach Minnow): ~21
  - AMNA (Yellow Bullhead): ~21
  - GIRO (Roundtail Chub): ~12
  - MEFU (Spikedace): ~9
  - LECY (Green Sunfish): ~4 (in counted hauls; substantially more in uncounted/targeted hauls, e.g., 18 at Site 6, ~35–45 at Site 1)
- **Habitat concern identified**: A large, deep diversion pond near the end of surface flow was flagged as a likely nonnative fish reservoir; the report explicitly recommends **gill-net sampling** to properly characterize species composition and abundance there, since seines were ineffective in this deep, vegetated lentic habitat.
- **Methodological consistency**: Survey design closely followed the [[October 1993 Protocol]] (Attachment B), enabling comparison of native/nonnative distributions over a two-year interval, though the 1995 effort was geographically restricted to the lower creek segment only.
- **Citizen science emphasis**: The 1993 protocol document highlights a strong volunteer/interagency collaborative model (AGFD, TNC, BLM, ASU student societies, BOR, ADEQ, NRCS, USFWS), reflecting a broader conservation partnership approach to monitoring the [[Aravaipa Creek]] native fish community.
