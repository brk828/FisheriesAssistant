# Escape of Hybrid Grass x Bighead Carp into Central Arizona

## Executive Summary
This 1983 case report documents the unintentional escape of 1,100 stocked F1 hybrid triploid [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) x [[Bighead Carp]] (*Aristichthys nobilis*) from a controlled aquatic vegetation-control study in the Phoenix metropolitan area, Arizona. The hybrids, stocked in August 1982 by the Salt River Project (SRP), Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGF), and University of Arizona (UA), were intended to be contained within a canal reach and a pond using barrier grates. Upon attempted retrieval in January 1983, only 13 of 1,100 fish (12 from the canal, 1 from the pond) were recovered. Investigators concluded that fish escaped through structural failures in the containment grates (erosion, bent bars, and a gap at the pond outflow), compounded by high runoff events that raised water levels near or above grate tops. The escape raises concerns about potential downstream dispersal via the Salt, Gila, and Colorado river systems, and about the reliability of triploidy-based sterility assurances as a biocontrol safeguard. The paper also corrects historical records of grass carp and silver carp (*Hypophthalmichthys molitrix*) introductions in Arizona.

## Background & Description
- **Age/Origin:** Spawned in 1981 by Malone's Chinese Fish Farms, Lonoke, Arkansas.
- **Size at stocking (August 1982, N = 36):**
  - Total length: 193–289 mm
  - Head width: 25.4–38.2 mm
  - Weight: 67–206 g
- **Ploidy status:** All stocked fish were putative **triploids** (presumed sterile), selected from a mixed brood stock via size-based separation due to differential growth rates between triploid and diploid individuals.
- **Brood stock ploidy analysis:** Independent examination (cell nuclear diameter and electrophoretic techniques) of up to 400 fish from the source 1981 brood found **92–98% triploid hybrids**; the remaining 2% diploid fish were identified as **gynogenetic grass carp** and **androgenetic bighead carp**.
- **Containment specifications:** Vertical bar grates with 25.4-mm spacing were used to physically restrict fish; erosion and bar-bending expanded some openings to ~32 mm, permitting all but the largest individuals to pass.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Intended habitat:** Stocked into two enclosed experimental sites:
  - A 1.2-km reach of Lateral 9.5, Consolidated Canal, Mesa, Arizona (N = 500)
  - A 0.01-hectare pond on SRP property in Phoenix adjacent to the Grand Canal (N = 600)
- **Purpose:** Cooperative biocontrol study (SRP-funded) to evaluate hybrid grass x bighead carp efficacy for **aquatic vegetation control** in SRP-operated irrigation canals, following on from the original introduction reported in [[Marsh And Minckley 1982]].
- **Connectivity/dispersal risk:** The Phoenix-area canal system is interconnected and, during high runoff, links via storm drains to the [[Salt River]], then to the [[Gila River]] and [[Lower Colorado River]]. Documented flow events (August/October 1982, January–March 1983) inundated the Salt and Gila river channels for ~150 km downstream to Painted Rock Reservoir; subsequent reservoir releases connected flow to the [[Colorado River]] at Yuma, Arizona.
- **Barriers to further spread:** Upstream reaches of the Salt and Verde rivers were presumably blocked from fish access by Granite Reef Diversion and other upstream dams.
- **Historical Arizona distribution context:** Prior grass carp introductions (from Hong Kong, 1965, via AZGF) were held at Page Springs Hatchery and later destroyed; additional experimental stocks at UA and Northern Arizona University (NAU) were also destroyed. Unauthorized stockings persisted at Arizona City (possibly extant in an urban lake) and Sun Lakes (destroyed 1978, alongside two silver carp specimens found as a contaminant species).

## Key Findings
- **Escape event:** Of 1,100 hybrid carp stocked, only **13 individuals (1.2%)** were recovered during the January 1983 drawdown and seining effort (12 from canal, 1 from pond); the fate of the remaining ~1,087 fish is unknown.
- **Cause of escape:** Multiple containment failures were identified:
  1. A gap at the base of the pond outflow grate, through which centrarchid fishes were directly observed escaping.
  2. Erosion around the concrete support apron of the downstream canal grate.
  3. Bent bars enlarging grate spacing from 25.4 mm to ~32 mm.
  4. High runoff causing canal water levels to approach or exceed grate heights.
- **Alternative explanations dismissed:** SRP initially favored theft or poisoning as causes for the fish's disappearance, but physical evidence of structural grate failure and direct observation of fish escaping supported the escape hypothesis.
- **Sterility caveat:** Although stocked fish were selected as 100% triploid via size-sorting, the source brood population contained a small but confirmed fraction (2%) of fertile diploid hybrids, underscoring risk in relying solely on presumed triploidy for containment safety.
- **Ecological risk:** Potential for downstream spread of hybrid carp into the Salt, Gila, and Colorado river systems exists due to hydrological connectivity during flood/runoff events, though the ultimate ecological consequences remain **unknown**.
- **Broader significance:** The authors emphasize this event as a "dangerous precedent" — despite awareness of liability and efforts toward security by involved agencies, containment still failed, highlighting the need for more robust safeguards in future biocontrol fish introductions, particularly for species capable of significant environmental disturbance.
- **Taxonomic/historical corrections:** The paper clarifies and corrects the mapped distribution records of [[Grass Carp]] and [[Silver Carp]] in Arizona from Guillory and Gasaway (1978) and Guillory (1980), invalidating two previously mapped localities (near the San Pedro/Gila confluence and near Roosevelt Lake) due to unverifiable secondhand reporting.
