# Letter to Peter B. Moyle on Colorado River Cichlids and Exotic Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cichlidae #invasive-species #Colorado-River

## Executive Summary
This February 1991 letter from ichthyologist [[W.L. Minckley]] to [[Peter B. Moyle]] provides expert testimony on the status of introduced cichlids and other exotic fishes in the lower [[Colorado River]] basin (Arizona/California/Sonora/Baja California Norte). Minckley documents extensive hybridization among introduced *Oreochromis* species, a striking temporal shift in dominant phenotypes between the 1970s and 1980s, and the apparent decline or absence of several other exotic and native taxa. The letter also references ongoing taxonomic work (a Master's thesis by [[Paul Barrett]]), collaborative electrophoretic and molecular research plans, and offers data on several imperiled native big-river fishes.

## Background & Description
The letter is a piece of primary-source scientific correspondence rather than a formal publication, but it contains substantive faunistic and taxonomic data based on voucher specimens held in the Arizona State University (ASU) fish collection. Minckley discusses:

- **Introduced Cichlids (Tilapiine complex):** *Oreochromis aureus* (blue tilapia), *O. mossambicus* (Mozambique tilapia), *O. hornorum* (Wami tilapia), and *[[Tilapia zillii]]* (redbelly tilapia) are confirmed present in the lower Colorado River via voucher specimens, including apparent interspecific hybrids. Equivocal evidence (based mainly on coloration) suggests the true *[[Oreochromis niloticus]]* (Nile tilapia) may also be present, though this identification is not confirmed with rigorous data.
- **Taxonomic Resource:** A Master's thesis by Paul Barrett (ASU, under Minckley's direction) sorts out the identification of these tilapiine taxa and provides key morphological characters and literature citations useful for species accounts.
- **Planned Publication:** Minckley, Walter Courtenay, and Barrett intend to publish a paper on lower Colorado River cichlids pending completion of electrophoretic analyses derived from early-1980s data.
- **Other exotics:** *[[Poecilia mexicana]]* group (shortfin/Amazon molly complex) collected near Yuma but not recorded since the 1970s, likely obscured by the abundance of the congener *[[Poecilia latipinna]]* (sailfin molly).
- **Bigmouth Buffalo:** No confirmed records of *[[Ictiobus cyprinellus]]* (bigmouth buffalo) exist for the lower Colorado River, and Minckley questions the origin of claims of its presence there.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Lower Colorado River mainstem and associated waters near Yuma, Arizona, spanning the Arizona/California and Sonora/Baja California Norte border region.
- **Hybridization dynamics:** Minckley states he is "convinced" that extensive hybridization has occurred among all sampled *Oreochromis* species in this system, complicating field identification. He notes cichlids are "so damned variable (and similar) that one looks like the other depending on size, sex, sexual condition."
- **Phenotypic shift:** A survey comparison shows a dramatic ecological turnover — the *O. mossambicus* phenotype, common in 1976, had virtually disappeared by the early 1980s. By the ASU team's last major Yuma-area survey (1983–1987), essentially all captured tilapiines were phenotypically *O. aureus* or *aureus*-like.
- **O. hornorum status:** This phenotype was never abundant in the system but was clearly documented as present in the early 1970s.
- **Molly complex interactions:** The rarity of recent *Poecilia mexicana* group records may reflect a detection/sampling bias caused by the numerical dominance of the introduced *P. latipinna* rather than true extirpation.
- **Native big-river fish context:** Minckley references ongoing conservation-relevant data availability on several imperiled/managed native Colorado River fishes, indicating active research and reintroduction programs for these species:
  - *[[Gila elegans]]* (bonytail)
  - *[[Xyrauchen texanus]]* (razorback sucker)
  - *[[Ptychocheilus lucius]]* (Colorado squawfish / Colorado pikeminnow)
  - *[[Catostomus latipinnis]]* (flannelmouth sucker) — new data available
  - *[[Cyprinodon macularius]]* (desert pupfish) — conceptual/ecological ideas offered

## Key Findings
- **Confirmed cichlid taxa in lower Colorado River (via ASU voucher specimens):** *Oreochromis aureus*, *O. mossambicus*, *O. hornorum*, and *Tilapia zillii*, plus inter/intrageneric hybrids.
- **Possible but unconfirmed taxon:** *Oreochromis niloticus*, based on equivocal coloration data only.
- **Temporal phenotype replacement:** Complete shift from *O. mossambicus*-dominant to *O. aureus*-dominant phenotypes in the Yuma-area lower Colorado River between 1976 and the early 1980s, sustained through 1983–1987 surveys.
- **Rarity of O. hornorum:** Never common, but verified present as early as the early 1970s.
- **No evidence for bigmouth buffalo** (*Ictiobus cyprinellus*) in the lower Colorado River; the source of reports placing this species there is questioned by Minckley.
- **Poecilia mexicana group** persists only as historical specimens from the 1970s near Yuma; current status uncertain due to potential confusion with abundant *P. latipinna*.
- **Collaborative research infrastructure:** ASU had (as of 1991) developed molecular genetics capacity to revisit earlier evolutionary studies via mtDNA and allozyme analyses, with an active request to Moyle for *[[Pogonichthys]]* (splittail and related minnows) tissue samples for this purpose.
- **Related publication in progress:** *Battle Against Extinction* (in galley proofs as of March 1991), a book exceeding 700 pages, addressing broader conservation issues relevant to this fauna.
- **Regional taxonomic project:** Minckley's revision of *Fishes of Arizona* was expanding in scope toward a broader *Fishes of the American Southwest* treatment at the time of writing.
