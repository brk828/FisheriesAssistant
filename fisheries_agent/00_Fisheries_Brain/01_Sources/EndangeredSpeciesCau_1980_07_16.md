## Executive Summary
This note documents a historical news report from the *Lake Powell Chronicle* (July 16, 1980) detailing an incidental capture of an endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) by an angler, Don Stacey, at the Wahweap Gravel Pile in Lake Powell. The event is notable because the species is typically associated with the confluence of the [[Little Colorado River]] and [[Colorado River]], rather than the open, impounded waters of Lake Powell itself. Wildlife officials confirmed the species' rarity in the reservoir, noting that only approximately six individuals are captured there annually, with the majority of the population residing in free-flowing river reaches above or below the dam. The report also highlights procedural protocols established by the [[Utah Department of Wildlife Resources]] for handling incidentally caught endangered fish.

## Key Findings
- **Incidental Bycatch:** Confirms that endangered Humpback Chub are occasionally caught by recreational anglers in Lake Powell, despite the species' primary range being upstream/downstream river sections.
- **Population Distribution:** Utah Fish and Game rangers estimated only about six Humpback Chub are captured in Lake Powell annually, reinforcing that the core population resides in adjacent riverine habitat rather than the lake itself.
- **Conservation Protocol:** Wayne Gustaveson (Utah DWR) emphasized a strict conservation protocol:
    1. Live-caught Humpback Chub should be released immediately.
    2. Dead specimens should be turned over intact (uncleaned/ungutted) to rangers to preserve scientific data (sex, condition, spawning status).
- **Data Loss Due to Handling:** In this specific case, the sex of the specimen could not be determined because the fish had already been gutted and cleaned by the angler prior to being handed over to National Park Service rangers, representing a loss of valuable biological data for endangered species monitoring.
