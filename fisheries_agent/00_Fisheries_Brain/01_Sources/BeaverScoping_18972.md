# Southwest Center for Biological Diversity Letter on San Pedro River Beaver Reintroduction Concerns

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #cyprinidae #catostomidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This May 1995 correspondence from the [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]] to the [[Bureau of Land Management]] and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] raises critical concerns about proposed [[Beaver]] ([[Castor canadensis frondator]]) reintroduction to the [[San Pedro River]]. The letter documents a 67% decrease in low flows (to approximately 1 cubic foot per second) from 1943-1992 and argues that groundwater pumping from [[Fort Huachuca]] expansion poses the greatest threat to river survival. The organization questions whether beaver reintroduction during early riparian recovery could negatively impact federally listed species and native fish populations while potentially benefiting exotic species.

## Background & Description

The [[San Pedro River]] has experienced dramatic rejuvenation following cattle removal, with early-stage recovery of cottonwood/willow riparian habitat. The river reach between [[Palominas]] and [[Charleston]] within the [[San Pedro River National Conservation Area]] is a gaining reach where groundwater discharges into the stream. However, hydrological data reveals severe flow reduction over five decades, with low flows now at approximately one cubic foot per second.

Native fish species historically present include [[Razorback Sucker]] ([[Xyrauchen texanus]]), [[Colorado Squawfish]] ([[Ptychocheilus lucius]]), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] ([[Catostomus latipinnis]]), [[Roundtail Chub]] ([[Gila robusta]]), and [[Gila Chub]] ([[Gila intermedia]]). Federally listed species requiring recovery include [[Spikedace]] ([[Meda fulgida]]), [[Loach Minnow]] ([[Tiaroga cobitis]]), [[Gila Topminnow]] ([[Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis]]), and [[Desert Pupfish]] ([[Cyprinodon macularius]]).

Exotic species established in the system include [[Bullfrog]] ([[Rana catesbeiana]]), [[Common Carp]] ([[Cyprinus carpio]]), [[Black Bullhead]] ([[Ameiurus melas]]), [[Green Sunfish]] ([[Lepomis cyanellus]]), [[Mosquitofish]] ([[Gambusia affinis]]), [[Fathead Minnow]] ([[Pimephales promelas]]), [[Yellow Bullhead]] ([[Ameiurus natalis]]), [[Bluegill]] ([[Lepomis macrochirus]]), and [[Largemouth Bass]] ([[Micropterus salmoides]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[San Pedro River]] ecosystem is in a transitional recovery phase dominated by cottonwood/willow establishment. Dense stands of mature understory willows have not yet fully recovered after only a few years of young tree re-establishment. The [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] ([[Empidonax traillii extimus]]), a federally listed species, depends on this developing but immature riparian habitat.

The primary threat identified is deficit groundwater pumping associated with massive expansion of [[Fort Huachuca]] and the [[Sierra Vista]] complex, which directly impacts river baseflow. The gaining reach characteristic means the river depends on aquifer discharge, making it vulnerable to groundwater depletion.

Beaver dam construction would create ponded conditions that could: (1) require higher aquifer levels for discharge, (2) increase open water surface area and evaporative losses, (3) reduce natural flooding regimes, (4) favor lentic-adapted exotic species over native riverine fishes, and (5) impact young riparian vegetation critical for avian species recovery.

## Key Findings

**Primary Management Concerns:**
- 67% decrease in [[San Pedro River]] low flows from 1943 through 1992
- Current low flows approximately 1 cubic foot per second
- Groundwater pumping from [[Fort Huachuca]]/[[Sierra Vista]] expansion identified as greatest short-term threat to river survival
- Riparian recovery still in early cottonwood/willow establishment phase with immature understory

**Native Fish Conservation Risks:**
- Beaver damming could eliminate flowing habitat required by native riverine species including [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], and [[Gila Chub]]
- Ponded conditions may impede recovery of federally listed [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Desert Pupfish]]
- Reduced flooding from beaver dams could negatively impact species dependent on natural flow regimes

**Exotic Species Facilitation:**
- Beaver ponds likely to increase competitive advantage of exotic species adapted to lentic conditions
- Exotic species present include [[Bullfrog]], [[Common Carp]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Largemouth Bass]]

**Riparian and Avian Impacts:**
- Beaver herbivory on young cottonwood/willow trees could damage developing habitat for [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]]
- Potential increased competitive advantage for [[Brown-headed Cowbird]] ([[Molothrus ater]]) over recovering neotropical migrant songbird populations
- Risk to immature riparian vegetation during critical recovery phase

**Hydrological Concerns:**
- Beaver dams would require higher aquifer discharge levels in gaining reach
- Increased open water surface area would increase evaporative water loss
- No increase in groundwater recharge from beaver activity
- Actions contraindicated during period of documented flow decline
