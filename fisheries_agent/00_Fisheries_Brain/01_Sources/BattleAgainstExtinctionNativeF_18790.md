# Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #desert-fishes #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific research paper but a promotional/newsletter piece (from "BOOKLEAVES," a publication of the Friends of the ASU Libraries) announcing the book *Battle Against Extinction: Native Fish Management in the American West*. The book, edited by W.L. Minckley (Arizona State University) and James E. Deacon (University of Nevada), compiles scattered literature on the plight of native fish species inhabiting the arid American West. It emerged from a 1988 symposium of the same name and is described by reviewers (including former Secretary of the Interior Stewart L. Udall and Walter Courtenay Jr. of Florida Atlantic University) as a landmark contribution to [[Conservation Biology]]. The announcement also promotes a fundraising "gourmet grazing" event to benefit ASU library science collections and graduate fisheries education, tying culinary programming to awareness of [[Desert Fish Conservation]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Native fish populations discussed inhabit isolated aquatic habitats within the arid and semi-arid American West, including desert springs, streams, and rivers — environments popularly assumed (incorrectly) to be devoid of fish life.
- These species face severe interspecific and human-driven competition for habitat due to:
  - Colonization and exploitation of desert lands by humans.
  - Water development and diversion for agricultural, urban, and industrial use.
  - Consequent depletion and fragmentation of already-scarce freshwater resources.
- The book frames these fishes within a broader **ecosystem context**, emphasizing that conservation of individual species is inseparable from conservation of habitat and regional [[Biodiversity]].
- The source references the [[Desert Fishes Council]] as a key institutional body concerned with the ecology and management of these species.

## Key Findings
- **Central thesis**: Native desert fishes of the American West are undergoing widespread, human-caused decline and are in active competition for dwindling aquatic habitat, driven primarily by water depletion associated with human settlement and land use.
- **Publication significance**: *Battle Against Extinction* is characterized as consolidating "nearly all the scattered literature and new knowledge" on Western native fish conservation into a single volume, representing a major synthesis for the field of [[Conservation Biology]].
- **Editorial/institutional support**: The book was supported by grants from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] and [[Arizona State University]], underscoring institutional investment in desert fish research and preservation.
- **Public engagement strategy**: The associated "gourmet grazing" fundraising event illustrates an applied conservation-funding model, using public culinary events to generate revenue for scientific library collections (ASU's Daniel E. Noble Science and Engineering Library) and to support graduate education in fisheries science.
- **Notable endorsements**: Former Secretary of the Interior Stewart L. Udall and ichthyologist Walter Courtenay Jr. (Florida Atlantic University) publicly praised the book's ecosystem-level framing and its "positive recommendations for maintenance of biodiversity through conservation."
- **Limitation of this source**: As a newsletter/press release rather than a peer-reviewed scientific article, it contains no primary data, species accounts, or quantitative findings; it functions solely as a secondary announcement and fundraising vehicle for the underlying scientific volume.
