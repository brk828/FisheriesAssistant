# Chromosomal NOR Phenotypes of 21 Western North American Cyprinids

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This cytogenetic study, published in *Cytologia* (1992) by J. D. Jenkin, Y. C. Li and J. R. Gold, documents chromosomal nucleolus organizer region (NOR) phenotypes in 21 cyprinid taxa from western North America: 19 described species and 2 undescribed forms. All 21 possessed 2n = 50 chromosomes and at least two pairs of NOR-bearing chromosomes. The authors use NOR phenotypes to test the phylogenetic hypothesis of [[Coburn and Cavender Phylogeny]] (1992) for western cyprinids and argue that NOR variation is low in the west compared with the east, possibly reflecting [[Chromosomal Canalization]] or character-state reversal and [[Introgressive Hybridization]] driven by the region's tectonic and hydrographic history.

## Background & Description

Western North American cyprinids are a distinctive fauna. Fifteen of roughly twenty genera there are monotypic, and most are absent east of the [[Continental Divide]]. The region has the oldest known North American cyprinid fossils, dating from the mid-to-late Oligocene through the Pleistocene, including early representatives of *Acrocheilus*, *Gila*, *Mylopharodon* and *Ptychocheilus*. Late Cenozoic tectonism and aridity fragmented river basins, producing endemic, species-poor lineages, while some genera (e.g., *Gila*, *Rhinichthys*, *Ptychocheilus*) later became widespread.

Systematic relationships among western cyprinids were poorly resolved before this work. Earlier hypotheses drew on morphology and allozymes for pairs of genera and on regional revisions of *Algansea* and *Rhinichthys*. The phylogeny of [[Coburn and Cavender Phylogeny]] (1992) placed most western taxa in a "western" clade containing a [[Gila Clade]], with a large basal "California-Columbia" polytomy, and placed *Richardsonius* in the eastern "shiner" clade.

Specimens were mainly field-collected by seine. Four taxes (*G. elegans*, *G. nigrescens*, *P. argentissimus*, *P. lucius*) came from the Dexter National Hatchery, New Mexico, with wild parental stocks from the Colorado, Rio Grande and Yampa drainages. Chromosome preparations came from kidney tissue of colchicine-injected fish or from fin-derived fibroblast cultures. NORs were visualized with the AgNOR method of [[Howell and Black Silver Staining]] as modified by [[Gold and Ellison Silver Staining]], and NOR positions were measured with the BANDSCAN digitizer program. Voucher specimens were deposited at TCWC (Texas A&M) and UCLA Ichthyological Research Collections.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addresses cytogenetic and evolutionary questions rather than habitat ecology. Sampling sites span coastal and interior drainages, including the Umatilla, Santa Clara, Guadalupe, Putah Creek, Pit, Umpqua, Siuslaw, Aravaipa, Bonita and Stampede Reservoir systems, and a pond at Slaughter Ranch in Arizona. The authors link the region's fragmented, arid, tectonically active history to isolation, extinction, and hybridization among basin faunas, with interbasin transfers documented for several genera. Recent [[Mitochondrial DNA Introgression]] evidence (DeMarais et al. 1992) shows mtDNA transfer among western cyprinid lineages, supporting hybridization as a plausible mechanism.

## Key Findings

- **Sample scope:** 21 taxa (19 described, 2 undescribed) examined; 17 collected wild by seine and 4 from Dexter National Hatchery.
- **Karyotype:** all individuals had 2n = 50 chromosomes, typical of North American cyprinids.
- **NOR multiplicity:** all 21 taxa had multiple NOR-bearing chromosomes: 15 species had two pairs, 4 had three pairs, and 2 had four pairs.
- **Common NOR state:** one pair of A NOR chromosomes occurred in all 21 taxa, and 17 taxa also had a second pair of A NORs.
- **Contrast with the east:** among 80 eastern species, 67.5% had a single NOR pair, 30% had two and 2.5% had three. The high multiplicity in the west is therefore unusual.
- **Novel records:** chromosome numbers were reported for the first time for *A. alutaceus*, *G. elegans*, *G. nigrescens*, *G. pandora*, *G. purpurea*, *P. oregonensis*, *P. umpquae*, *P. sp. cf. umpquae*, *R. cobitus*, *R. osculus*, *R. sp. cf. osculus* and *R. balteatus*.
- **Rare NOR state:** an NOR on the long arm of a bi-armed chromosome (state H in the authors' notation) appeared in *R. sp. cf. osculus* and *R. balteatus*. Among North American cyprinids it was previously known only from *Notropis hemigymnus*.
- **Phylogenetic inference:** the authors propose that the AA state is plesiomorphic for the western clade, which supports placing *Richardsonius* in the western rather than the shiner clade. Inferred synapomorphies are the addition of an F NOR in *Rhinichthys* (*R. cobitus* + *R. osculus*) and a transition from A to D NOR in the subgenus *Gila* (*Gila*).
- **Rhinichthys from the Siuslaw:** the Siuslaw population differs from *R. osculus* (Bonita Creek) in NOR state and is not referable to *R. evermanni* or *R. falcatus*. The authors suggest it may merit species status.
- **Conservatism:** the AA NOR phenotype is found in seven of nine genera examined. Western NOR variation is low compared with the east, where interspecific and intergeneric differences are common.
- **Explanations offered:** the authors consider [[Chromosomal Canalization]] (Bickham and Baker 1979) and character-state reversal or [[Introgressive Hybridization]] as possible causes of the conservatism, but note that both are difficult to test empirically.
