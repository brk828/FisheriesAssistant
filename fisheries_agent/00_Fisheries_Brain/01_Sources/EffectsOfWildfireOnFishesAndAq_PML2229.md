# Effects of Wildfire on Fishes and Aquatic Ecosystems: Picture Fire Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes a forwarded email thread and an attached research summary on the effects of the [[Picture Fire]] on fishes and stream habitat in the Tonto National Forest, Arizona. The fire burned approximately 12,500 acres between 6/17/2003 and 6/26/2003. Researchers from the [[Rocky Mountain Research Station]] and Tonto National Forest monitored six stream sites before and during the [[Monsoon Season]] to track post-fire changes in fish populations, invertebrates, habitat and water quality. Post-monsoon sampling showed heavy ash and sediment deposition, very high turbidity at several sites, and marked declines in [[Headwater Chub]] counts at the sites sampled both before and after the rains. The study is ongoing and is planned for journal publication and a 2004 [[American Fisheries Society]] Arizona Chapter presentation.

## Background & Description

**Correspondence.** Forest Fisheries Biologist Robert Calamusso (Tonto National Forest) emailed a summary of the research on 24 September 2003. Jerry Stefferud forwarded it to colleagues, noting that the Picture Fire burn area included streams containing [[Gila Nigra]] (headwater chub).

**Research team.** John Rinne and Cody Carter (RMRS), with Terry Tolbert and personnel from the Tonto National Forest, conducted the study on the effects of catastrophic fire on native and nonnative fishes.

**Study design.** Six monitoring sites were established after the fire but before the monsoon rains. Each site included about 50 m of pool habitat and 50 m of riffle habitat. No instream ash input was seen during the initial pre-monsoon trips.

**Methods:**
- Fish sampled with pulsed DC [[Electrofishing]], identified to species and measured; meristic counts made for *Gila* species.
- Macroinvertebrates sampled with a Surber sampler.
- Stream cross sections to measure base flow and flood flows.
- Pool and riffle volumes measured.
- [[Pebble Count]]s to track substrate change.
- Water chemistry samples (major cations and anions).
- Turbidity, pH, conductivity, temperature and dissolved oxygen measured with a Hydrolab instrument.
- Automated water samplers at three sites (2 samples/day); Hydrolab loggers at two sites (hourly data).
- Digital photos of each habitat unit.

Monsoon-season revisits collected all data except electrofishing, which was limited to sites where water was low enough to sample fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The six sites form a single watershed sequence, from upper to lower stream:

1. **[[Turkey Creek]]** (highest site) at J Slash X Ranch: all fish native Arizona species.
2. **[[Rock Creek]]** at the Ord Mine site (second highest; receives water from Turkey Creek), split into two sub-sites.
3. **[[Spring Creek]]** above its confluence with Rock Creek (reference site for the study).
4. **Rock Creek** at its confluence with Spring Creek.
5. **Spring Creek** below its confluence with Rock Creek.
6. **Spring Creek** at the end of Forest Road 484 (lowest site; about 2 miles below the Rock Creek confluence).

Sites 1 to 2 are upper reaches with the most intense visible impacts. Sites 4 to 6 are lower reaches where nonnative species were more common and where turbidity stayed high through much of the monsoon season.

**Nonnative species.** [[Salmo Trutta]] (brown trout), [[Lepomis Cyanellus]] (green sunfish) and [[Ictalurus Natalis]] (yellow bullhead) were recorded at several sites alongside native species.

## Key Findings

**Fire extent and design.** The Picture Fire burned about 12,500 acres. Six sites were sampled before the monsoon and revisited during and after it.

**Post-fire impacts by site:**
- **Turkey Creek:** After the monsoon, tremendous ash and sediment filled pools and banks. Flood flows then scoured much of the ash, rearranged the channel and uprooted streamside vegetation. Pool habitat was greatly reduced, and several pools scoured into riffles. A visual survey on 8/11/2003 observed no fish, although many had been seen from the bank on earlier trips.
- **Rock Creek:** Large amounts of ash and sediment were deposited in pools and on the banks. Visual surveys saw many fish during the monsoon, but the authors judged visual counts inaccurate at this site because of poor depth and habitat complexity. Channel morphology did not appear significantly changed.
- **Spring Creek (above confluence):** Flood evidence was seen, but ash and large sediment deposits were limited to the lower reach.
- **Rock Creek at confluence:** Large ash and sediment deposits; turbidity reached 41 NTU.
- **Spring Creek below confluence:** Large ash and sediment deposits; turbidity reached 27 NTU.
- **Spring Creek at FR 484:** Large ash and sediment deposits. Turbidity stayed moderate to high through most of the monsoon, with pre-monsoon turbidity of 1–3 NTU. Later evidence of a bankfull flood was seen, though the channel was less scoured than upstream.

**Turbidity and water quality.**
- Turkey Creek post-monsoon turbidity was very low (0 NTU).
- Spring Creek above confluence was very low (1 NTU) when revisited during the monsoon.
- The lowest site had the highest sustained turbidity: 10 to roughly 100+ NTU (the upper limit is partly illegible in the source).
- Hydrolab and automated sampler data were collected and are being analyzed.

**Trends noted by the authors.** Headwater chub counts fell sharply at Turkey Creek between July and August (199 to 54). Desert sucker and speckled dace also declined there. Brown trout and green sunfish were found at several sites, but the source does not report pre- and post-fire trends for them.

**Planned work (fall 2003 onward):**
- Full re-sampling of all sites in fall 2003, plus additional off-site pool and riffle units to account for effects of earlier sampling.
- Macroinvertebrate samples processed by Professor Dennis Miller and students at Western New Mexico University, reported with the [[Eptindex]] and/or other metrics.
- Water chemistry analyzed by Steve Overby and staff at the RMRS Chemistry Lab.
- Automated sampler water filtered and weighed for suspended solids.
- Cross-section data entered into [[Winxspro]] to calculate base and flood discharge.
- [[Burn Severity]] maps analyzed to quantify the area and slope of each severity class in the watershed.
- Data compilation and analysis in fall and early winter 2003–2004.
- Planned publication in the primary literature and presentation at the February 2004 American Fisheries Society Arizona Chapter meeting.
- Long-term monitoring to track the effects of [[Wildfire]] on fishes and aquatic ecosystems.
