# Contaminated Fish Stockings into Topock Marsh 1977

#ichthyology #fish-management #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #percidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence documents three fish stocking events at [[Topock Marsh]], Arizona in 1977 where shipments from [[Tisomingo National Fish Hatchery]] in Oklahoma inadvertently introduced multiple non-target species alongside requested fish. The incidents involved [[Channel Catfish]] (March 30), hybrid sunfish (October 19), and resulted in the unintentional stocking of at least seven additional fish species, bullfrog tadpoles, and crayfish. Arizona Game and Fish Department attempted to filter unwanted species but did not reject the loads. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service acknowledged the error and committed to preventing future occurrences.

## Background & Description

[[Topock Marsh]] received fish from [[Tisomingo National Fish Hatchery]] in Oklahoma during 1977. The primary stockings included:

**March 30, 1977**: Approximately 27,360 [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) weighing 2,280 pounds were stocked at four locations. Fish ranged from 3-12 inches with modal length at 6.5 inches. Approximately 225 fish died during stocking. About 10,140 channel catfish were marked by adipose fin removal at the Glory Hole site.

**October 19, 1977**: Approximately 41,000 hybrid fingerling sunfish (bluegill × green sunfish cross) weighing approximately 100 pounds were planted by dipnet in three areas. Fish ranged from 0.5 to 3 inches in length. Water temperature acclimation was necessary due to 15°F difference between holding tank (61°F) and marsh (76°F). Approximately 200 fish died during the process.

Stocking locations included: (1) outlet structure on south dike, (2) south dike dredge channel, (3) [[Willow Lake]] dredge channel, and (4) Glory Hole. Underwater vegetation was sought at each locality to provide cover for released fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Topock Marsh]] is located within the [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]] along the [[Colorado River]] system. The marsh represents critical wetland habitat in the arid Southwest.

The contaminated stockings introduced species not native to the [[Colorado River]] basin, including:
- [[Slough Darter]] (*Etheostoma gracile*) - 1 specimen
- [[Golden Redhorse]] (*Moxostoma erythrurum*) - 2 specimens
- [[Logperch]] (*Percina caprodes*) - 2 specimens
- [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*) - 1 specimen
- [[Bluntnose Minnow]] (*Pimephales notatus*) - 2 specimens
- [[Stoneroller]] (*Campostoma anomalum*) - 2 specimens
- [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) - 1 specimen

Additional non-target organisms included bullfrog (*Lithobates catesbeianus*) tadpoles, bullhead catfish species, possibly [[Flathead Catfish]], unidentified minnows (referred to as "lizard or striped minnows" by hatchery personnel), and crayfish.

The March stocking also transported unknown numbers of [[Green Sunfish]], bullhead catfish, bullfrog larvae, and crayfish in the same tank as channel catfish.

## Key Findings

**Stocking Protocol Failures**: Hatchery workers were aware of contamination but did not prevent non-target species from being loaded. Workers expressed they were "hurried in the capturing, holding, and loading of the requested fish."

**Detection and Response**: USFWS Biologist Buddy Jensen and Wildlife Manager Jim Warnecke became aware of contamination only after approximately 100 pounds of fish had already been planted during the October stocking. They attempted to capture and preserve all non-target specimens found thereafter.

**Documentation**: Five minnow specimens and six hybrid sunfish were preserved as record specimens from the October stocking. Jensen preserved additional specimens from subsequent stockings at lakes on the Indian Reservation below Parker, Arizona, where two or three more non-request species were discovered in the same tank truck.

**Institutional Response**: George Divine, Senior Staff Assistant for Inland Fisheries (USFWS), formally apologized in February 1978 correspondence. The hatchery manager was contacted and made aware of the error. Other managers were instructed to ensure only requested fish leave the hatchery.

**Management Decision**: Contrary to initial recollection, Arizona Game and Fish Department did not reject the contaminated loads but attempted to "filter" unwanted species during the stocking process.

**Extent of Introduction**: The total number of contaminant individuals stocked was described as "small" but actual quantities remain unknown. Jensen indicated he did not capture all specimens but likely obtained representatives from each species present.
