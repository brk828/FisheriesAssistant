# Endangered Species Listing Actions for Fiscal Year 1990: Gila Topminnow

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Phoenix Field Office) dated March 28, 1990. It addresses a proposed administrative action to reclassify the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) from "Endangered" to "Threatened" status. The Field Supervisor formally requests that this reclassification (downlisting) be removed from the FY1990 schedule of listing actions, citing a declining population trend and a failure to meet the biological criteria required for downlisting under the existing recovery plan. This serves as a case study in the administrative and scientific process governing the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
* **Habitat Type:** Historically native to desert aquatic habitats within the [[Gila River]] basin, including springs, cienegas, and low-gradient streams in Arizona and New Mexico.
* **Biogeography:** Part of the broader [[Desert Fishes]] assemblage of the American Southwest, an ecosystem characterized by isolated, fragmented aquatic habitats highly sensitive to water diversion, groundwater pumping, and introduction of non-native species (e.g., mosquitofish).
* **Population Status:** The document explicitly states the species' status was "currently declining" as of late 1989/early 1990, indicating ongoing threats to its habitat and population viability despite prior conservation efforts.
* **Institutional Stakeholders:** The management of this species involves the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) for captive propagation/refugia populations and the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] (Arizona) for habitat protection.

## Key Findings
1. **Reclassification Rejected:** A proposal to downlist the Gila topminnow from Endangered to Threatened was deemed premature and incorrectly scheduled for FY90 action.
2. **Failure to Meet Recovery Criteria:** The species' current population status does not satisfy the biological benchmarks set forth in the existing (pre-1990) Recovery Plan for downlisting.
3. **Recovery Plan Revision:** The Recovery Plan governing this subspecies was undergoing revision at the time of writing, with expectations that the *new* criteria for downlisting to "Threatened" would be **more stringent** than the previous standards.
4. **Expert Consensus:** The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], the scientific body advising on this species' conservation (led by W. L. Minckley), voted **unanimously** in December 1989 to recommend *against* proceeding with the downlisting.
5. **Administrative Outcome:** The Field Supervisor (Sam F. Spiller) requested the Regional Director formally remove the reclassification proposal from the Region 2 schedule of proposed listing actions for Fiscal Year 1990, effectively maintaining the species' status as strictly "Endangered."
