# Further Studies on the Metalimnetic Oxygen Maximum, with Special Reference to Its Occurrence Throughout the World

#ichthyology #marine-biology #limnology #phytoplankton #oxygen-dynamics #lake-ecology #cyanobacteria

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive limnological study examines the phenomenon of metalimnetic oxygen maxima in 54 lakes worldwide, documenting 98 separate instances where oxygen concentrations exceeded 12.7 mg/L (the saturation value at 4°C). The research establishes a classification system for plus-heterograde lakes and identifies four distinct types of oxygen maxima based on timing, depth, temperature, and dominant phytoplankton. The study focuses particularly on "[[Oscillatoria]] lakes" in northern [[Indiana]], where [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] produces dense blooms at 10-12°C and 1-3% surface light intensity, achieving photosynthetic efficiencies of 0.8-1.7% at maximum production depths. Primary production rates in [[McLish Lake]] and [[Myers Lake]] ranged from 0.7 to 1.7 g C/m²/day during late summer. The research demonstrates that lake morphometry (particularly area and relative depth) significantly influences oxygen maximum development in US lakes, while edaphic stability appears more important in Alpine lakes.

## Background & Description

### Definition of Plus-Heterograde Lakes

The author proposes that the term "plus-heterograde" should apply only when metalimnetic oxygen exceeds the vernal prestratification saturation value at 4°C (12.7 mg/L at sea level), representing the maximum oxygen achievable through purely physical means. A **plus-heterograde lake** maintains this oxygen profile throughout most of summer stratification until fall circulation begins.

During spring circulation, water becomes uniformly saturated through surface aeration and turbulent distribution. At 4°C, saturation equals 12.7 mg/L. When hypolimnetic oxygen is consumed by decomposition and epilimnetic oxygen decreases through atmospheric equilibration, a plus-heterograde curve results if metalimnetic photosynthesis produces oxygen exceeding the spring saturation value.

### Geographic Distribution

The study compiled data from 54 lakes across multiple regions:
- **United States**: 12 lakes in [[Indiana]], 13 in [[Wisconsin]] (primarily glacial ice-block lakes)
- **Alpine Region**: 14 lakes in [[Switzerland]] and [[Austria]]
- **Japan**: 12 mountain lakes
- **Other**: Single examples from [[Poland]] ([[Hancza]]) and [[Russia]] ([[Belowod-See]])

The highest oxygen concentrations recorded were:
- [[Knights Lake]] (Wisconsin): 36.5 mg/L at 4.5 m (385% saturation, 152% absolute saturation)
- [[Otter Lake]] (Wisconsin): 35.7 mg/L at 4.5 m (416% saturation, 156% absolute saturation)
- [[McLish Lake]] (Indiana): 33.2 mg/L at 6 m (340% saturation, 117% absolute saturation)

### Morphometric Characteristics

Lake morphometry varied considerably:
- **Area**: 3 to 6,848 ha (median 20.2 ha)
- **Maximum depth**: 6 to 143 m (median 22 m)
- **Relative depth** (z_r): 1.28% to 11.76% (median 3.31%)

The relative depth parameter, calculated as z_r = 50z_m/√(A/π), proved particularly significant. In US lakes, 86% had volume developments greater than 1.00, indicating basin shapes conducive to stable stratification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Stability and Oxygen Accumulation

Maximum stability occurs when the thermocline approaches the center of gravity of the water mass. The study calculated stability values for six lakes:

**[[McLish Lake]]** exhibited highest stability:
- Absolute stability (S): 7,902 meter-tons
- Relative stability (S_a): 576 g·cm/cm²
- Center of gravity: 5.9 m
- Oxygen maximum depth: 6.0 m

The thermocline position relative to the center of gravity proved critical. In [[Myers Lake]] and [[McLish Lake]], the thermocline lay just above the center of gravity, while in [[Otter Lake]] it was 2.1 m below (19.6% of maximum depth).

The clinolimnetic coefficient of eddy conductivity (after subtracting molecular thermal conductivity of 0.12 × 10⁻²) showed minimal turbulence at oxygen maximum depths:
- [[Myers Lake]] (1952-1955): 0.17, 0.09, 0.50, 0.24 g·cm/sec × 10⁻²
- [[McLish Lake]] (1961): 0.05 g·cm/sec × 10⁻²

### Light and Temperature Conditions

Different oxygen maximum types occurred under distinct environmental conditions:

**Pseudo-metalimnetic maxima**:
- Depth: Very shallow (2-3 m)
- Temperature: Warm (20-25°C)
- Light: 1% level at 2.9-5.2 m
- Timing: June-July

**Diatom maxima**:
- Depth: 3.5-5.0 m
- Temperature: 15-20°C
- Light: 9-28% surface intensity (minimum 5% for production)
- Timing: Throughout summer

**[[Oscillatoria]] maxima**:
- Depth: 6-8 m
- Temperature: 10-12°C
- Light: 1-5% surface intensity
- Timing: Late summer climax

### Phytoplankton Communities

**Diatom-dominated lakes** ([[Knights Lake]], [[Otter Lake]], [[Beasley Lake]], [[Marl Lake]]):
- [[Fragilaria]]: 0.2-38.7 × 10⁴ individuals/L
- [[Asterionella]]: up to 9.8 × 10⁴ individuals/L
- [[Dinobryon]]: 0.1-7.8 × 10⁴ individuals/L
- [[Ceratium]]: minor component

**[[Oscillatoria]]-dominated lakes** ([[Myers Lake]], [[McLish Lake]]):
- Nearly monospecific blooms of [[Oscillatoria agardhii]]
- Dense concentrations 1 m below oxygen maximum
- Sharp decline at lower bloom edge
- Minimal zooplankton grazing (except [[Nassula aurea]] ciliate in 1952)

Alpine lakes featured [[Oscillatoria rubescens]] rather than O. agardhii, associated with documented eutrophication since the early 20th century.

## Key Findings

### Classification of Metalimnetic Oxygen Maximum Types

The study establishes four distinct types:

**Type I: Pseudo-metalimnetic oxygen maximum**
- Develops temporarily under secondary thermoclines in small, protected lakes
- Forms during June-July calm periods
- Disappears when wind-induced circulation incorporates the layer into epilimnion
- Very thin oxygen-rich strata
- Example: [[Wyland Lake]] (19.3 mg/L at 2 m, 11 July 1955)

**Type II: Temporary sub-climax oxygen maximum**
- Occurs in deep, transparent lakes
- Maximum in June-July, then declines
- Spring plankton community persists temporarily in metalimnion
- Thick oxygen layer with uniform concentrations
- Disappears despite adequate light, possibly due to nutrient depletion
- Example: [[Still Lake]] (14.8 mg/L at 7 m, 2 June 1961)

**Type III: Permanent climax oxygen maximum**
- Persists throughout summer stratification
- Highest values in August-September
- Stable metalimnetic phytoplankton community
- Two subtypes based on dominant organisms

**Type IIIa: Diatom maximum**
- Temperature: 15-20°C
- Light: 5-28% surface intensity
- Dominated by [[Fragilaria]] and [[Asterionella]]
- Examples: [[Waupaca Chain]] lakes

**Type IIIb: [[Oscillatoria]] maximum**
- Temperature: 10-12°C
- Light: 1-5% surface intensity
- Dominated by stenothermal blue-green algae
- [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] in US lakes
- [[Oscillatoria rubescens]] in Alpine lakes

### Morphometric Relationships

**US Lakes (Indiana and Wisconsin)**:
- Optimal conditions: <30 ha area, relative depth ~4%
- Frequency of oxygen maxima by size:
  - <30 ha: 21% (1:4.9 lakes)
  - 30-150 ha: 5.5% (1:18.8 lakes)
  - 150-750 ha: 4.1% (1:24.7 lakes)
- Statistical significance: P <0.001 for difference in relative depth between lakes with and without maxima
- Small lakes (<30 ha) showed no significant morphometric difference, suggesting most small glacial lakes have high relative depth

**Alpine Lakes**:
- No clear correlation between morphometry and oxygen maxima
- Highest oxygen values in largest lakes (contrary to US pattern)
- Edaphic stability (mountain protection from wind) more important than morphometry
- Mean relative depth (3.14%) similar to US lakes but with higher variability (coefficient of variation 75% vs. 37% for US)

**Japanese Lakes**:
- Highest relative depths (mean 7.70%)
- Oxygen maxima correlated with small area but not relative depth
- Most uniform group (coefficient of variation 22%)

### Primary Production in [[Oscillatoria]] Lakes

**[[Myers Lake]]** (1954-1955):
- Daily production: 1.7 g C/m²/day during extensive bloom
- Maximum metalimnetic production: 280 mg C/m³/day at 8 m
- Photosynthetic efficiency at maximum: 1.7%
- Overall efficiency: 0.32%
- 73% of net oxygen production occurred in metalimnion
- 46% of gross production occurred in metalimnion

**[[McLish Lake]]** (1960-1962):
- Daily production: 0.7 g C/m²/day
- Maximum metalimnetic production: 130 mg C/m³/day at 6 m
- Photosynthetic efficiency at maximum: 0.8%
- Overall efficiency: 0.18%
- Light at maximum production depth: 3% surface intensity

### Photosynthetic Quotient and C¹⁴ Methodology

The study determined a photosynthetic quotient (PQ) of 1.1 for blue-green algae-dominated systems, closely matching the value of 1.08 reported for [[Synechococcus]]. Using this PQ:
- Mean carbon equivalent from oxygen data: 0.72 g C/m²/day
- Mean phototrophic C¹⁴ uptake: 0.72 g C/m²/day

The remarkable agreement suggests C¹⁴ measurements in these lakes approximate gross production rather than net production, possibly due to low excretion of soluble metabolites by filamentous [[Cyanophyta]].

### Anomalous Carbon Uptake in [[Oscillatoria]] Blooms

**Dark carbon uptake** at 7 m depth in [[McLish Lake]]:
- 38-47% of light-absorbed carbon
- Paralleled [[Oscillatoria]] population density
- Negligible microplankton (<5μ) activity throughout water column
- All uptake attributed to [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] (>5μ)

**Imbalance between carbon uptake and oxygen production**:
- Maximum C¹⁴ uptake at 7 m (light <1%)
- Maximum oxygen production at 6 m
- Suggests supplemental, light-independent carbon fixation

**Possible chemosynthetic pathway**:
- High sulfate concentrations (>100 mg/L)
- H₂S present in hypolimnion up to lower bloom surface
- [[Nakamura]] (1938) described CO₂ assimilation in [[Oscillatoria neglecta]] using H₂S as hydrogen donor
- [[Pringsheim]] (1963) considered [[Oscillatoria]] closely related to [[Beggiatoa]] (colorless blue-green algae)
- [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] reported from polluted waters (Fall Creek, Indianapolis; Norrviken, Sweden)
- Nearly colorless O. agardhii var. isothrix found in H₂S-rich Stockholm waters

The author suggests [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] may possess metabolic pathways similar to [[Beggiatoales]], supplementing photosynthesis with chemosynthesis in the metalimnion where H₂S and low oxygen concentrations coexist.

### Ecological Succession and Lake Typology

The study proposes successional relationships among oxygen curve types:
- **Orthograde** ↔ **Plus-heterograde** ↔ **Clinograde**
- **Minus-heterograde** closely related to clinograde

Most plus-heterograde lakes are **mesotrophic**:
- US lakes: 0.7-1.8 g C/m²/day
- Alpine lakes: 0.1-1.2 g C/m²/day (preliminary data)
- [[Yoshimura]] (1938) classified 8 of 12 Japanese plus-heterograde lakes as mesotrophic

The oxygen curve type represents a summer climax condition, taken preferably in August or early September in north temperate regions, showing no further typological change until fall circulation begins.

### Regional Differences in Lake Evolution

**Indiana and southeastern Wisconsin**:
- Geologically older (earlier glacial retreat)
- More level terrain, greater eutrophy
- Absence of very shallow small lakes
- Agricultural influence: erosion, fertilizers accelerate eutrophication
- Soil drainage eliminated many shallow lakes

**Northeastern Wisconsin**:
- Heavily forested, less agriculture
- Low nutrient levels (calcium ~1/10 of southeastern Wisconsin)
- Less morphological alteration from sedimentation
- Small lakes remain deep (e.g., [[Lake Mary]]: 1.2 ha, 21.5 m)
- Very small lakes convert to bogs
- Higher variability in relative depth (coefficient of variation 140%)
- Prediction: more oxygen maxima likely with further study

### Absolute Saturation and Bubble Formation

The concept of absolute saturation incorporates hydrostatic pressure effects:

[O₂]ₐ = [O₂]ₛ(1 + 4.78p_v + 0.462z)

Where [O₂]ₛ is surface saturation (altitude-corrected) and p_v is water vapor pressure.

**Extreme supersaturation examples**:
- [[McLish Lake]]: 117% absolute saturation (33.2 mg/L at 6 m)
- [[Knights Lake]]: 150% absolute saturation (36.5 mg/L at 4.5 m)
- [[Otter Lake]]: 156% absolute saturation (35.7 mg/L at 4.5 m)

Despite exceeding the "bubbling point," no in situ bubble formation has been observed in undisturbed deep-water strata. Small bubbles forming would be reabsorbed passing through undersaturated upper layers. This contrasts with large bubbles readily observed during photosynthesis by vascular plants like [[Vallisneria]] and [[Elodea]], where intercellular oxygen accumulation precedes bubble eruption.

### Factors Preventing Competitive Exclusion

Laboratory experiments with [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] var. isothrix from Swedish waters showed:
- Massive growth at higher temperatures and light intensities than found in nature
- Rapid domination by [[Scenedesmus]] or [[Chlorella]] in contaminated cultures

This suggests the metalimnetic bloom develops **in the absence of competition** rather than in response to optimal conditions. The "eddy trap" at the plane of minimum turbulence concentrates organisms where:
- Extreme conditions (low light, low temperature) exclude most species
- [[Oscillatoria agardhii]] tolerates but does not prefer these conditions
- Limited food value reduces zooplankton grazing
- Only [[Nassula aurea]] ciliate observed feeding on [[Oscillatoria]]

Alternative hypothesis of antibiotic/antialgal substance excretion remains untested.
