# Sabino Canyon Gila Chub Renovation Project

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #gila-chub

## Executive Summary

This document is a February 25, 1999 public scoping letter from the [[Santa Catalina Ranger District]] of the [[Coronado National Forest]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. It proposes a native fish renovation in [[Sabino Canyon]] to aid recovery of the [[Gila Chub]], a native Arizona fish. The plan is to remove the non-native [[Green Sunfish]], a predator, from [[Sabino Creek]] and [[Rose Canyon Lake]] using the piscicides [[Antimycin]] and [[Rotenone]]. The letter also invites public comment, due March 30, 1999.

## Background & Description

**Species status.** The Gila chub is designated a candidate species by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and a sensitive species by the Forest Service. The proposal states that the chub may become federally listed as threatened or endangered if its population declines further.

**Purpose and need.** The project seeks an environment free of predatory non-native fish, with a stated target of 100% removal of Green Sunfish. The stated goal is to let the Gila chub re-establish and expand its range, maintaining population viability and lowering the risk of extirpation.

**Project scope.**
- Treatment areas: portions of Sabino Creek and Rose Canyon Lake. The legal description covers T.13S, R15E, Sections 2, 3, 9 and 35, and T.12S, R16E, Section 16.
- Initial treatment: Sabino Creek from [[Bridge Nine]] to [[Sabino Dam]], about 3 miles. It is expected to remove up to 95% of non-native fish, with follow-up treatments as needed to reach 100%.
- Follow-up: after treatment, the reintroduction of other extirpated fishes will be evaluated, including the [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Longfin Dace]].
- Closure: the Arizona Game and Fish Department will impose a state commission fishing closure from 1/2 mile upstream of the end of the paved road to the Forest boundary below Sabino Dam.

**Previous efforts.** The Arizona Game and Fish Department previously used [[Backpack Shocker]] units in Sabino Creek. These reduced the Green Sunfish population but did not achieve complete removal.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Threat to the chub.** The chub and sunfish co-occur in about 1.5 miles of downstream Sabino Creek reaches. Studies found Gila chub densities were about 90% lower in downstream reaches where Green Sunfish were present, compared with upstream reaches where they were absent. Chubs under 4.0 cm total length were absent where sunfish occurred.

**Natural barrier.** A large rock barrier, Bridge Nine, currently blocks Green Sunfish from moving upstream. Sunfish have crossed similar barriers downstream, either during high flows or through transplanting by recreationists. The proposal expects that the chub population above Bridge Nine, where sunfish are absent, may be the sole source of fry for the whole Sabino Creek population. If sunfish pass Bridge Nine, the proposal says the chub would probably be extirpated.

**Toxicant characteristics (as stated in the proposal).**
- [[Rotenone]]: a botanical piscicide from tropical and subtropical plants of the pea family. It inhibits cellular oxygen use rather than suffocating fish. The proposal describes it as generally nontoxic to people, mammals and birds at treatment concentrations. It suits larger, slow-moving waters because it requires more units per water volume.
- [[Antimycin]]: derived from a naturally occurring fungus and lethal to gill-breathing animals at very low concentrations. The proposal describes it as the safest and most effective option for flowing waters. It is approved by the [[U.S. Environmental Protection Agency]] and reportedly breaks down rapidly into non-lethal components. The proposal cites use in Arizona and New Mexico for [[Apache Trout]] and [[Gila Trout]] restoration, with "no detectable environmental consequences," and reports no long-term effect on aquatic insect fauna. These are the proposal's own claims, not independently verified results here.

**Detoxification and monitoring.** Water leaving the Forest must be neutralized. A detoxification station below Sabino Dam will apply [[Potassium Permanganate]] for at least 24 consecutive hours. Rose Canyon Lake will detoxify naturally over 2 to 4 weeks.

## Key Findings

**Treatment schedule and access**
- Sabino Canyon treatments are planned for a four-day consecutive window in late June to early July, or late September to early October, during low or no-flow periods.
- Rose Canyon Lake treatment occurs after October 31, when the gate closes to public motor access.
- The Rose Canyon Lake and Sabino Creek area from Bridge Nine to the Forest boundary will be closed during treatment and for 10 to 14 days afterward.

**Application methods**
- Rotenone will be used in still water at Sabino Lake. Antimycin will be used in flowing Sabino Creek above Sabino Lake and dam. Rose Canyon Lake will receive rotenone, applied once.
- Toxicant is released from drip stations, refilled about 3 to 4 times per hour. Treatments in Sabino Creek are simultaneous across stations.

**Salvage and recovery**
- The day before application, crews will electrofish to salvage Gila chub and mud turtles, relocating them to pools above Bridge Nine.
- Some fish will remain in the system for nutrient cycling. Drip jugs will be removed after treatment.
- Recolonization is expected from populations above Bridge Nine and from tributaries not treated. Rose Canyon Lake will be restocked in early January for trout fishing.

**Public process**
- Comments are due by March 30, 1999, to the Santa Catalina Ranger District, Attention: Steve Romero.
- Public notice signs and barricades will be placed to inform visitors about closures and project status.

**Principal conclusion:** The proposal treats complete removal of Green Sunfish as essential to Gila chub recovery. It argues that the chub's survival depends on keeping the sunfish from passing Bridge Nine.
