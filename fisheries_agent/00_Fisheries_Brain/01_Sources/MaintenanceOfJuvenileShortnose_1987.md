## Executive Summary
This 1987 interagency memorandum from Jack E. Williams (USFWS) to the Klamath Basin Sucker Working Group proposes an emergency genetic conservation action for the [[Shortnose Sucker]] (*Chasmistes brevirostris*), a species whose genetically pure populations were critically restricted to [[Upper Klamath Lake]]. The proposal outlines a plan to capture a small number of wild spawning adults, artificially spawn them, and transport the resulting fry to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico to establish an ex-situ "genetic reserve," minimizing further impact on the dwindling wild population. This document also references the imminent federal listing of the species under the Endangered Species Act.

## Background & Description
- **Species Identity:** *Chasmistes brevirostris*, the [[Shortnose Sucker]], a catostomid fish endemic to the [[Klamath Basin]].
- **Conservation Status (as of 1987):** Considered functionally near extinction in its pure genetic form; a Federal Register notice proposing [[Endangered Species Act]] listing for both the Shortnose Sucker and the [[Lost River Sucker]] was published on August 26, 1987.
- **Population Context:** Genetically pure specimens were known to exist only in very small numbers, restricted to [[Upper Klamath Lake]], suggesting significant hybridization or population decline elsewhere in the basin.
- **Proposed Methodology:**
  1. Capture approximately six adult shortnose suckers during their spring spawning run (river migration).
  2. Strip adults of ova and milt (artificial spawning) and immediately return live adults to the river to preserve the wild breeding population.
  3. Hatch eggs locally in the Klamath Basin (Klamath Falls or Chiloquin, Oregon).
  4. Air-transport resulting fry to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico for long-term maintenance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** The species relies on a lake-river migratory life history, utilizing [[Upper Klamath Lake]] as primary habitat and adjoining rivers/tributaries for spawning runs.
- **Threat Basis:** The urgency of the proposal stems from the assessment that pure shortnose suckers were "in danger of dying out in the next several years," implying critically low recruitment or high mortality in the wild population at the time.
- **Conservation Strategy:** This represents an early application of **ex-situ conservation** (genetic banking/refugia) for an endangered fish species, aiming to establish an assurance population physically isolated from the threats present in the Klamath Basin (e.g., habitat degradation, water quality issues, or hybridization pressure implied by the emphasis on "genetically pure" individuals).
- **Institutional Collaboration:** The effort involved coordination between the [[Klamath Basin Sucker Working Group]], the Oregon Department of Fish and Wildlife (ODFW), California Department of Fish and Game (CDFG), U.S. Forest Service (USFS), Bureau of Indian Affairs (BIA), and the Klamath Tribes (TKT).

## Key Findings
- **Critical Rarity:** As of 1987, genetically pure *Chasmistes brevirostris* were confirmed only in Upper Klamath Lake and in very small numbers, marking one of the earliest documented urgent conservation concerns for the species.
- **Non-lethal Broodstock Collection:** The proposed protocol emphasized a minimum-impact strategy—stripping gametes from wild adults and returning them alive to the river—to avoid further depleting the wild spawning population.
- **Genetic Reserve Rationale:** Establishing a captive population at Dexter National Fish Hatchery was intended as "insurance against loss in the wild," alongside providing specimens for **electrophoretic (genetic) studies** to better characterize the species' genetic distinctiveness.
- **Regulatory Timing:** The proposal directly preceded formal federal recognition of the crisis, coinciding with the August 26, 1987 Federal Register notice proposing endangered status for both the [[Shortnose Sucker]] and [[Lost River Sucker]].
- **Interagency Consensus:** The memo indicates broad agreement among Working Group members regarding the imminent extinction risk, framing the Dexter relocation as a time-sensitive action requiring immediate hatchery preparation for the following spring.
