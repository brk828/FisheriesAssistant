# Humpback Chub Research Permit Request — Little Colorado River

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #[[Humpback Chub]]

## Executive Summary
This document is a formal permit request submitted by [[Arizona State University]]'s Center for Environmental Studies to the [[Grand Canyon National Park]] Resources Management Division, seeking authorization to conduct field research on the ecology and conservation of the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the [[Little Colorado River]]. The proposed work, conducted under a cooperative agreement with the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program, targets the confluence zone of the Little Colorado and Colorado rivers. Rather than presenting new morphological or ecological data directly, the letter outlines a multi-year (1991–1995) sampling program designed to characterize the abundance, distribution, movement, and inter-river relationships of humpback chub populations, with explicit emphasis on non-lethal collection methodology.

## Background & Description
The source document is administrative/regulatory in nature (a permit request) and does not report morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data for *Gila cypha*. No length, weight, coloration, or distinguishing structural features (e.g., the diagnostic nuchal hump characteristic of the species) are described in this text. Morphological characterization would need to be drawn from the broader [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies Phase II Draft Integrated Research Plan]] referenced in the letter, which is not included here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat association**: The study targets the confluence area of the [[Little Colorado River]] and the mainstem [[Colorado River]] within [[Grand Canyon National Park]], extending from the river mouth upstream to the Park boundary.
- **Population connectivity**: A central ecological question addressed by the proposed research is the relationship between humpback chub populations residing in the Little Colorado River versus the mainstem Colorado River — implying the species utilizes both riverine habitats, potentially with seasonal or life-stage-dependent movement between them.
- **Movement ecology**: Two-way weirs and traps are proposed for use upstream of the Park boundary specifically to quantify short-term (seasonal) and long-term (annual) movement patterns of humpback chub, suggesting the species exhibits measurable migratory or dispersal behavior within this river system.
- **Co-occurring ichthyofauna**: The research also encompasses "other fishes" within the study area, situating the humpback chub within the broader context of the Grand Canyon's native fish assemblage ([[Grand Canyon Ichthyofauna]]).
- **Seasonal activity pattern**: Sampling intensity is structured around presumed seasonal biological activity — most intensive in spring (March–May), reduced in summer (June–August), and further reduced in autumn (September–November) and winter (December–February) — implying peak activity/detectability of the species in spring, consistent with likely spawning-related aggregation behavior typical of *Gila* species.

## Key Findings
*(Note: As this is a permit request rather than a results-reporting paper, the following are proposed research parameters and methodological commitments rather than empirical findings.)*

- **Regulatory authorizations in place**: Principal investigators [[Paul C. Marsh]] and [[Michael E. Douglas]] held existing collection permits from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (No. MRSHOGO0015) and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 2 (CPRT-676811).
- **Field personnel**: Responsible parties proposed for the Park permit include Marsh, Douglas, [[C.O. Minckley]], [[Brian Bagley]], and [[Pat Ryan]] (Navajo Natural Heritage Program), serving as field supervisory staff.
- **Sampling methods (within Park boundary)**: Trammel nets and hoop nets — selected specifically for being effective yet non-lethal to the target species.
- **Gear-clearing intervals**: Trammel nets checked/cleared at intervals of ≤6 hours; hoop nets checked at ~12-hour intervals, minimizing stress and incidental mortality risk to captured fish.
- **Expected mortality**: No intentional sacrifice of humpback chub was planned; only limited, unavoidable incidental mortality was anticipated.
- **Sampling methods (upstream of Park boundary)**: Addition of two-way weirs and traps to enable quantitative assessment of fish movement patterns.
- **Study duration and effort**: Field activity planned from July 1991 through summer 1995, with personnel deployed 1–3 weeks at 1–3 week intervals, totaling approximately 8 months of fieldwork per year.
- **Anticipated research value**: The project was expected to generate a temporal and spatial profile of humpback chub abundance, distribution, and movement; clarify the ecological relationship between Little Colorado River and mainstem Colorado River populations; and strengthen resource management agencies' capacity to conserve the humpback chub and other native components of the [[Grand Canyon Ichthyofauna]].
