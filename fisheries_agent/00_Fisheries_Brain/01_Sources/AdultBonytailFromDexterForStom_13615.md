## Executive Summary
This document is an internal USFWS/BIO-WEST memorandum (with an accompanying fish stocking request form) concerning a proposed methodological study rather than a primary field research paper. The memo requests up to 100 adult [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to serve as a **surrogate species** for developing and validating a **nonlethal stomach pumping technique**. This technique is intended to assess the food habits of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) population in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the Colorado River, as requested by the Aquatic Coordination Team (ACT) of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program. The study evaluates both the safety (potential damage to the pharyngeal mill) and efficacy of the stomach pumping method.

## Background & Description
- **Rationale for surrogate selection:** Adult [[Bonytail]] were chosen as a proxy for [[Humpback Chub]] due to close anatomical similarity in two key digestive structures:
  - **Pharyngeal tooth structure** (the [[Pharyngeal Mill]]) — critical because the study explicitly aims to assess potential physical damage to this structure from the stomach pumping procedure.
  - **Intestinal system** morphology — relevant to ensuring the pumping technique does not cause internal injury and that food bolus recovery is representative.
- These shared traits reflect the close phylogenetic relationship among *Gila* species native to the Colorado River Basin, including [[Bonytail]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target species habitat:** [[Humpback Chub]] inhabit swift, turbid mainstem reaches of the Colorado River within the [[Grand Canyon]], a habitat characterized by regulated flows from Glen Canyon Dam — the focus of the broader GCES research program.
- **Surrogate species source population:** Captive-reared adult [[Bonytail]] held at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (a federal refugium population for this now critically endangered species in the wild).
- **Supplementary wild population:** Wild [[Roundtail Chub]] from the [[Dolores River]] were proposed for inclusion in the study design to reduce the number of hatchery [[Bonytail]] that would need to be sacrificed, indicating a conservation-conscious experimental design balancing scientific need against protection of a captive endangered species stock.
- **Study logistics:** Proposed venues for conducting the stomach pumping trials included the Dexter hatchery itself or transport to the [[Logan Experimental Fish Hatchery]] in Utah, reflecting institutional coordination between BIO-WEST, USFWS, and Arizona-based research collaborators (Arizona State University, Arizona Game and Fish Department).

## Key Findings
- A **nonlethal stomach pumping methodology** was under development as a less invasive alternative to lethal sampling for studying diet and food habits of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]].
- Up to **100 adult Bonytail** were requested from Dexter National Fish Hatchery, with an anticipated need to **sacrifice no more than 10 individuals** (for direct examination of pharyngeal mill damage), leaving **~90 fish unharmed**.
- The study explicitly targets two evaluation criteria mandated by GCES/ACT:
  1. **Structural safety** — degree of damage, if any, to the pharyngeal mill from the pumping procedure.
  2. **Technique efficiency** — how effectively stomach contents can be retrieved nonlethally.
- Use of a **combined sampling approach** (hatchery [[Bonytail]] + wild [[Roundtail Chub]]) was proposed to minimize losses of the valuable captive endangered [[Bonytail]] stock while still generating a robust dataset.
- This work represents a methodological precursor supporting long-term diet and food-habit studies of [[Humpback Chub]] in the [[Grand Canyon]], situated within the broader Glen Canyon Dam adaptive management and environmental studies framework.
