# Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council, Volumes I–XV (1969–1983): Subject, Author, and Systematic Index

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a cumulative index to the first fifteen volumes of the [[Proceedings of the Desert Fishes Council]], covering 1969 to 1983. It was compiled by Cynthia Deacon Williams and Jack Edward Williams. The index has three parts: a Subject Index, an Author Index, and a Systematic Index. It is a finding aid rather than a research study, so it reports no new field data. It does, however, show how the Council's published work is organized, which species and places dominated desert fish conservation in the period, and which management and legal topics recurred. The index is bilingual, with an introduction in English and Spanish.

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Council was founded to advance the preservation of America's desert fishes. Its proceedings were the main venue for this work across the period. The indices make the volumes searchable by topic, author, and taxon.

**Scope and conventions**

- The Subject and Systematic indices include all vertebrate and invertebrate taxa. Plants are excluded.
- Amphibians, invertebrates, mammals, and reptiles appear under the subject heading "Non-fish Taxa."
- Entries are keyed to the primary language of each paper. Duplicate entries for second-language abstracts are omitted.
- Spelling errors in the original papers were corrected when the entries were made.
- Entries are cited by volume (Roman numeral) and page number(s).

**Standardized taxonomic names used in the indices**

- *Cyprinodon milleri* is entered as *Cyprinodon salinus milleri*.
- *Cyprinodon salinus* is entered as *Cyprinodon salinus salinus*.
- *Gila mohavensis* is entered as *Gila bicolor mohavensis*.
- All *Pantosteus* taxa are entered as *Catostomus*.
- *Salmo irideus nelsoni* is entered as *Salmo nelsoni*.
- *Sarotherodon zilli* is entered as *Tilapia zilli*.

**Index structure**

- **Subject Index** (Part I, from page 1): organized by agencies, places, topics, and management themes.
- **Author Index** (Part II, from page 9): lists contributors alphabetically, with volume and page references.
- **Systematic Index** (Part III, from page 21): lists genera and lower taxa, with synonyms and standardized names.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The index shows that most research and management attention in these volumes focused on small-range endemic fishes of isolated desert waters. These fishes are sensitive to water withdrawal, groundwater change, and introduced species.

**Key places**

- [[Ash Meadows]], Nevada, is the most frequently indexed locality. It appears in the Subject Index under Ash Meadows and in many Systematic Index entries for pupfishes and other endemics.
- Other indexed localities include [[Amargosa Canyon]] (California), [[Aravaipa Creek]] (Arizona), [[Cuatro Ciénegas]] (Coahuila), [[Owens Valley Native Fishes Sanctuary]] (California), [[Death Valley Region]], the [[Colorado River]], and the [[Sonoran Desert]].

**Main threats and pressures**

- **Habitat alteration**, both physical and biological. The index lists many entries under "Physical" and "Biological (Exotics)" alteration.
- **Introduced species**, including [[Gambusia affinis]] (mosquitofish), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), and *Tilapia zilli*. Exotics are a major category in the Subject Index.
- **Hybridization** between native and introduced or related taxa. Examples include *Cyprinodon bovinus* × *C. variegatus*, *Gambusia nobilis* × *G. affinis*, and *Salmo apache* × *S. gairdneri*.
- **Water rights and groundwater** use, which appear under the legal and regulatory headings.

**Management and legal context**

The Laws and Regulations section lists the [[Endangered Species Act]], the [[National Environmental Policy Act]], groundwater rights entries, and the [[Desert Pupfish National Monument]]/Wildlife Refuge proposal. It also lists court cases, including *Pyramid Tribe v. Truckee Carson Irrigation District* and *United States v. Cappaert et al.*. Agency entries show that the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[U.S. Bureau of Land Management]], the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[U.S. Forest Service]], and several state agencies were frequent participants.

## Key Findings

**Structure and content of the Proceedings (1969–1983)**

- The index covers Volumes I through XV and is organized into three parts: Subject, Author, and Systematic indices.
- Subject headings include Culture and Propagation, Distribution, Genetics, Habitat Alteration, Hybridization, Introductions, Laws and Regulations, Life History, Limnology, Models, Non-fish Taxa, Parasites and Diseases, Politics, Recovery Efforts, Reviews, Status Reports and Surveys, Taxonomy/Systematics, and Zoogeography.
- The Desert Fishes Council's own business appears in the index as attendance lists, business and operations records, and resolutions. Resolutions appear most heavily in Volumes IX through XV.

**Focal taxa in the Systematic Index**

The Systematic Index includes many genera and lower taxa. The most prominent focal species, by number of entries, are as follows:

- [[Devils Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*): the most heavily indexed taxon, with entries under culture, recovery, introductions, and population studies.
- [[Ash Meadows Pupfish]] (*Empetrichthys latos*): appears under recovery efforts, introductions, and culture.
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*): appears under recovery, introductions, and exotics.
- [[Mohave Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor mohavensis*): appears under recovery, introductions, and status reports.
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): appears under culture, hybridization, recovery, and status reports.
- [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*): appears under recovery, status reports, and life history.
- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): appears under recovery, life history, and status reports.
- [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*): appears under recovery, introductions, and taxonomy.

**Non-fish taxa**

The index also lists amphibians (for example, *Batrachoseps* and *Bufo*), mammals (for example, *Microtus californicus scirpensis*), reptiles, and invertebrates. Invertebrates include snails (*Assiminea*, *Tryonia*), crustaceans (*Artemia*, *Cambarellus*, *Hyalella*), and zooplankton (*Bosmina*, *Daphnia*, *Ceriodaphnia*). Under "Non-fish Taxa," invertebrates have entries for management and food-web topics.

**Recovery teams and programs**

The Recovery Efforts section names several teams and task forces:

- [[Arizona Trout Recovery Team]]
- [[Colorado Fishes Recovery Team]]
- [[Warm Springs Pupfish Recovery Team]]
- [[Pupfish Task Force]]

Recovery Efforts is one of the largest subject headings in the index. Its main subheadings are *Cyprinodon*, *Gila*, *Salmo*, *Catostomus*, and *Empetrichthys*.

**Frequently cited authors**

Several contributors appear across many volumes. Examples include E.P. Pister, W.L. Minckley, D.V. Lockard, J.E. Deacon, R.R. Miller, and N.B. Armantrout. These names are useful entry points for tracing the Council's research over time.

**Status and survey coverage**

Status reports and surveys cover the [[Bonneville Basin]], the [[Lahontan Basin]], the [[Chihuahuan Desert]], the [[Colorado River]], the [[Death Valley Region]], [[Baja California]], and the [[Sonoran Desert]].
