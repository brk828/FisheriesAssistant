# Executive Order 13112 - Invasive Species

#policy #invasive-species #ecosystem-management #federal-regulation

## Executive Summary

Executive Order 13112, issued February 3, 1999, establishes comprehensive federal policy for preventing introduction and controlling spread of [[Invasive Species]] in the [[United States]]. The order creates the [[Invasive Species Council]] co-chaired by the Secretaries of Interior, Agriculture, and Commerce, mandates development of a National Invasive Species Management Plan, and requires all federal agencies to identify and minimize actions that may introduce or promote invasive species. The order specifically references the [[Nonindigenous Aquatic Nuisance Prevention and Control Act]] and establishes coordination with existing bodies including the [[Aquatic Nuisance Species Task Force]].

## Background & Description

The executive order defines "invasive species" as an alien species whose introduction does or is likely to cause economic or environmental harm or harm to human health. An "alien species" is any species not native to a particular ecosystem, including seeds, eggs, spores, or other biological material capable of propagating that species. "Introduction" encompasses intentional or unintentional escape, release, dissemination, or placement of a species into an ecosystem as a result of human activity.

The order establishes the [[Invasive Species Council]] with membership including the Secretaries of State, Treasury, Defense, Interior, Agriculture, Commerce, Transportation, and the Administrator of the Environmental Protection Agency. An advisory committee representing stakeholders (state, tribal, and local government agencies, academic institutions, scientific community, nongovernmental organizations, trade groups, commercial interests, and private landowners) provides information and advice to the Council.

The order draws authority from multiple statutes including the [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA), [[Nonindigenous Aquatic Nuisance Prevention and Control Act]] of 1990, [[Lacey Act]], Federal Plant Pest Act, Federal Noxious Weed Act of 1974, and [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The order recognizes that invasive species cause significant economic, ecological, and human health impacts across diverse ecosystems. It emphasizes ecosystem-based approaches, defining "ecosystem" as the complex of a community of organisms and its environment. The order prioritizes restoration of [[Native Species]] and habitat conditions in ecosystems that have been invaded.

Control measures include eradicating, suppressing, reducing, or managing invasive species populations; preventing spread from areas where they are present; and taking steps such as restoration of native species and habitats to reduce effects and prevent further invasions. The order specifically acknowledges existing coordination mechanisms including the [[Aquatic Nuisance Species Task Force]], the Federal Interagency Committee for the Management of Noxious and Exotic Weeds, and the Committee on Environment and Natural Resources.

The order applies to all U.S. territories including the 50 States, District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, Guam, and all possessions, territories, and the territorial sea of the United States. It explicitly maintains existing obligations under 16 U.S.C. 4713 regarding [[Ballast Water]] programs, recognizing this critical pathway for aquatic invasive species introductions.

## Key Findings

**Federal Agency Duties**: Each federal agency whose actions may affect invasive species status must: (1) identify such actions; (2) use relevant programs and authorities to prevent introduction, detect and respond rapidly, monitor populations, restore native species and habitats, conduct research, and promote public education; and (3) not authorize, fund, or carry out actions likely to cause or promote introduction or spread of invasive species unless benefits clearly outweigh potential harm and all feasible measures to minimize risk are taken.

**Invasive Species Council Responsibilities**: The Council shall provide national leadership by: overseeing implementation and coordinating federal activities; encouraging planning at local, tribal, state, regional, and ecosystem-based levels; developing recommendations for international cooperation; developing NEPA guidance on prevention and control; facilitating development of coordinated monitoring networks; establishing information-sharing systems utilizing the Internet; and preparing the National Invasive Species Management Plan.

**Management Plan Requirements**: Within 18 months of order issuance, the Council must prepare the first National Invasive Species Management Plan detailing performance-oriented goals, objectives, and specific measures of success. The plan must include review of existing and prospective approaches for preventing introduction and spread, identify pathways by which invasive species are introduced, recommend measures to minimize introduction risk, and provide for science-based risk evaluation processes. The plan shall be updated biennially with concurrent evaluation and reporting on success in achieving goals.

**Risk-Based Approach**: The order mandates a coordinated and systematic risk-based process to identify, monitor, and interdict pathways involved in invasive species introduction. Recommended measures must provide for science-based evaluation of risks associated with introduction and spread.

**Stakeholder Engagement**: The Management Plan shall be developed through a public process in consultation with federal agencies and stakeholders. The advisory committee shall recommend plans and actions at local, tribal, state, regional, and ecosystem-based levels, acting in cooperation with stakeholders and existing organizations addressing invasive species.

**Implementation and Accountability**: Within 18 months after measures are recommended in any edition of the Management Plan, each federal agency whose action is required must either implement the recommendation or provide explanation of why action is not feasible. The Council shall assess effectiveness of the order at least once every 5 years and report to the Office of Management and Budget on whether revision is needed.

**Legal Authority**: The order revokes Executive Order 11987 of May 24, 1977. Exemptions from requirements may be granted by the Secretary of State or Secretary of Defense when necessary for foreign policy or national security reasons.
