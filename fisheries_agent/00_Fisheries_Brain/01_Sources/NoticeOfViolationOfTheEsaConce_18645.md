# Notice of Endangered Species Act Violations Concerning Razorback Sucker Critical Habitat Designation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species #critical-habitat

## Executive Summary

This legal notice, dated October 25, 1991, from the [[Sierra Club Legal Defense Fund]] formally notifies the U.S. Secretary of the Interior and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] of violations of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA) concerning the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The notice addresses the failure to designate appropriate [[Critical Habitat]] for this endangered species concurrent with its listing, and warns that federal actions, including the [[Animas-La Plata Project]] and [[Navajo Dam]] reoperation, may destroy or adversely modify the species' habitat. The notice was sent on behalf of the [[Colorado Environmental Coalition]] and [[Southern Utah Wilderness Alliance]].

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) was designated as an endangered species by the Secretary of the Interior on October 23, 1991, published in the Federal Register at 56 Fed. Reg. 54,957. Under the [[Endangered Species Act]], "critical habitat" consists of specific areas essential to species conservation that may require special management considerations or protection (16 U.S.C. § 1532(5)). The ESA requires the Secretary to designate critical habitat "concurrently" with the final decision to list a species, to the "maximum extent prudent and determinable" (16 U.S.C. § 1533(a)).

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a member of the family [[Catostomidae]] (suckers) endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The species inhabits multiple river systems and reservoir habitats throughout the southwestern United States.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice identifies extensive aquatic habitats that should be designated as [[Critical Habitat]] for the [[Razorback Sucker]], including:

**River Systems:**
- [[Colorado River]]
- [[Duchesne River]]
- [[Gila River]]
- [[Green River]]
- [[Salt River]]
- [[San Juan River]]
- [[Verde River]]
- [[Yampa River]]

**Reservoir Systems:**
- [[Lake Havasu]]
- [[Lake Mead]]
- [[Lake Mojave]]
- [[Lake Powell]]
- [[Senator Wash Reservoir]]

**Artificial Waterways:**
- [[Central Arizona Project]] canal

The notice emphasizes that federal actions, particularly the [[Animas-La Plata Project]] and associated reoperation of [[Navajo Dam]], pose threats to the species through potential destruction or adverse modification of habitat. Under *[[Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill]]* (437 U.S. 153, 184 (1978)), the Secretary and [[Bureau of Reclamation]] have a duty to afford the [[Razorback Sucker]] "the highest of priorities."

## Key Findings

1. **Listing Without Critical Habitat Designation**: The Secretary published the final rule designating the [[Razorback Sucker]] as endangered on October 23, 1991, but failed to concurrently designate [[Critical Habitat]], violating 16 U.S.C. § 1533(a)(3).

2. **Violation Characterization**: The failure to designate critical habitat is deemed "arbitrary and capricious, contrary to law, and an abuse of discretion."

3. **Required Habitat Designation**: Critical habitat must include all identified river systems (8 rivers), reservoir systems (4 lakes plus Senator Wash Reservoir), and the [[Central Arizona Project]] canal. Failure to designate all these water bodies would constitute additional ESA violations.

4. **Federal Project Impacts**: The [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s [[Animas-La Plata Project]] and [[Navajo Dam]] reoperation are identified as federal actions that may destroy or adversely modify the [[Razorback Sucker]]'s critical habitat.

5. **Legal Notice Requirements**: This notice satisfies the 60-day notice requirement under the citizen suit provision of the ESA (16 U.S.C. § 1540(g)(2)). If violations are not corrected within 60 days, a citizen suit will be filed.

6. **Conservation Priority**: The notice invokes the legal precedent that endangered species must receive "the highest of priorities" in federal decision-making, as established in *[[Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill]]*.
