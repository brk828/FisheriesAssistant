## Executive Summary

This volume compiles Occasional Papers 639–669 from the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, spanning mammalogy, herpetology, ornithology, entomology, malacology, and — most relevant here — **ichthyology and paleoichthyology**. Six papers within this range treat fish taxa directly: the phylogenetic relationships of Cretaceous [[Ichthyodectidae|ichthyodectid]] teleosts to the modern [[Chirocentrus|wolf herring]] (No. 650); a major description of fossil fishes from Plio-Pleistocene [[Lake Idaho]] (No. 654); the description of two new [[Cyprinodon|pupfish]] species from the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] (No. 659); an Oligocene mudminnow bearing on [[Esocoidei]] relationships (No. 660); a zoogeographic revision of the [[Notropis|cyprinid]] *Notropis (Opsopoeodus) emiliae* (No. 664); and descriptions of two new fishes — a [[Gila (Genus)|Gila]] chub and a [[Catostomus|sucker]] — from the isolated [[Owens River Basin]] of California (No. 667). Collectively these papers document deep-time teleost phylogeny, endemic radiation in isolated desert-spring systems, and fine-scale zoogeographic differentiation across North American freshwater basins.

## Background & Description

The volume is fundamentally a bound index/table of contents for 31 separate monographs (Nos. 639–669), most of which concern non-fish taxa (mammals, insects, birds, reptiles, mollusks). The fish-relevant contributions are:

- **No. 650** (Cavender): *The Caudal Skeleton of the Cretaceous Teleosts Xiphactinus, Ichthyodectes, and Gillicus, and its Bearing on Their Relationship with Chirocentrus.* Examines caudal skeletal morphology in the extinct predatory [[Ichthyodectidae|ichthyodectids]] *Xiphactinus audax*, *Ichthyodectes*, and *Gillicus arcuatus*, comparing them with the living wolf-herring *Chirocentrus dorab* ([[Chirocentridae]]) and other clupeomorph-adjacent taxa (*Diplomystus*, *Knightia*, *Thrissops*, *Allothrissops*, *Denticeps*, *Elops*, *Albula*, *Hiodon*).
- **No. 654** (Miller & Smith): *New Fossil Fishes from Plio-Pleistocene Lake Idaho.* Describes an extensive fossil fauna including new cyprinids (*Acrocheilus xestes*, *Mylopharodon*, *Ptychocheilus oregonensis*), catostomids (*Catostomus arenatus*, *Chasmistes spatulifer*, *Chasmistes batrachops*, *Deltistes ellipticus*, *Deltistes owyhee*), a centrarchid (*Archoplites taylori*), and an ictalurid (*Ictalurus vespertinus*).
- **No. 659** (Miller): *Two New Fishes of the Genus Cyprinodon from the Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, Coahuila, Mexico.* Describes *Cyprinodon atrorus* and *Cyprinodon bifasciatus*, endemic pupfishes from an isolated Mexican desert spring system, with hybridization data relative to *C. variegatus*, *C. nevadensis*, and *C. eximius*.
- **No. 660** (Cavender): *An Oligocene Mudminnow (Family Umbridae) from Oregon with Remarks on Relationships within the Esocoidei.* Describes the fossil *Palaeoesox fritschei* and discusses relationships among *Umbra*, *Dallia*, *Novumbra*, and *Esox*.
- **No. 664** (Gilbert & Bailey): *Systematics and Zoogeography of the American Cyprinid Fish Notropis (Opsopoeodus) emiliae.* Revises the pugnose minnow complex, recognizing subspecies *Notropis emiliae emiliae* and *N. e. peninsularis*, and reviewing related genera (*Hybopsis*, *Phenacobius*, *Exoglossum*, *Semotilus*).
- **No. 667** (Miller): *Two New Fishes, Gila bicolor snyderi and Catostomus fumeiventris, from the Owens River Basin, California.* Describes an endemic tui chub subspecies and sucker species from a hydrologically isolated Great Basin drainage.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cretaceous marine ichthyofauna** (No. 650): *Xiphactinus*, *Ichthyodectes*, and *Gillicus* were large predatory teleosts inhabiting Late Cretaceous epicontinental seas; their caudal skeletal architecture links them phylogenetically to the modern tropical marine/estuarine [[Chirocentrus dorab|wolf herring]], illuminating early teleost diversification alongside clupeomorphs (*Diplomystus*, *Knightia* — Eocene, Green River/Wyoming formations).
- **Plio-Pleistocene Lake Idaho** (No. 654): A large paleolake on the Snake River Plain supported a diverse endemic fish fauna of cyprinids, catostomids, centrarchids, and ictalurids — an important source for understanding the evolutionary history of western North American freshwater fish lineages (e.g., ancestral *Chasmistes*, *Deltistes*, and *Catostomus* lineages tied to modern Klamath Basin suckers).
- **Cuatro Ciénegas Basin** (No. 659): An isolated Chihuahuan Desert spring-fed wetland in Coahuila, Mexico, famous for high endemism; *Cyprinodon atrorus* and *C. bifasciatus* evolved in sympatry/parapatry with other endemics (*Gambusia marshi*, *Lucania interioris*, *Cichlasoma*, *Ictalurus lupus*), illustrating rapid speciation in desert refugia.
- **Oligocene Oregon (John Day Formation)** (No. 660): Fossil mudminnow *Palaeoesox fritschei* provides a calibration point for divergence within [[Esocoidei]] (pikes and mudminnows), bridging the biogeographic gap between Holarctic *Esox*, Beringian *Dallia pectoralis*, Pacific Northwest endemic *Novumbra hubbsi*, and Eurasian *Umbra*.
- **Southeastern/Midwestern USA drainages** (No. 664): *Notropis emiliae* zoogeography spans Great Lakes, Mississippi, and Gulf Coastal Plain drainages, with distinct peninsular Florida subspecies (*N. e. peninsularis*) reflecting Pleistocene coastal isolation.
- **Owens River Basin** (No. 667): A hydrographically isolated Great Basin drainage in eastern California (once tributary to Pleistocene Lake Manly/Death Valley system) hosting endemic derivatives of the tui chub (*Gila bicolor*) and Owens sucker (*Catostomus fumeiventris*), parallel to endemism patterns in the nearby Lahontan Basin (*Gila bicolor mohavensis*).

## Key Findings

- **Phylogenetic linkage of Ichthyodectidae to Chirocentridae** (No. 650): Caudal skeleton comparisons support a relationship between the extinct Cretaceous predators *Xiphactinus*, *Ichthyodectes*, *Gillicus* and the living wolf-herring *Chirocentrus*, refining understanding of early teleost (Clupeomorpha-adjacent) evolution.
- **New Plio-Pleistocene taxa from Lake Idaho** (No. 654): Description of *Archoplites taylori* (extinct Sacramento perch relative), *Ictalurus vespertinus*, *Deltistes ellipticus*, *Deltistes owyhee*, *Catostomus arenatus*, and *Chasmistes spatulifer*, substantially expanding the fossil record of western catostomids and centrarchids and clarifying ancestry of modern Klamath Basin suckers (*Deltistes luxatus*, *Chasmistes* spp.).
- **Two new pupfish species with evidence of hybridization** (No. 659): *Cyprinodon atrorus* and *C. bifasciatus* are described as morphologically and ecologically distinct endemics of Cuatro Ciénegas, with documented hybridization zones — an early molecular/morphological case study in desert-spring speciation.
- **Oligocene fossil resolves Esocoidei relationships** (No. 660): *Palaeoesox fritschei* is identified as a basal or transitional umbrid, supporting a Holarctic diversification model for Esocoidei (pikes + mudminnows) with *Novumbra*, *Dallia*, *Umbra*, and *Esox* as extant representatives.
- **Subspecific differentiation in a small cyprinid** (No. 664): *Notropis emiliae* is split into two subspecies (*emiliae* and *peninsularis*) based on morphometric and meristic characters (dorsal fin position, pharyngeal teeth, nuptial tuberculation, melanophore patterning), with zoogeographic patterns tied to Pleistocene drainage history across the Great Lakes, Mississippi Valley, and Florida peninsula.
- **New endemic chub and sucker in an isolated Californian basin** (No. 667): *Gila bicolor snyderi* (a tui chub subspecies) and *Catostomus fumeiventris* (Owens sucker) are formally described as endemics of the Owens River Basin, paralleling similar endemism in adjacent Lahontan (*Gila bicolor mohavensis*) and Death Valley system fishes (e.g., *Cyprinodon radiosus*, *Rhinichthys osculus*, golden trout), reinforcing the pattern of rapid ichthyofaunal diversification in hydrologically isolated Great Basin drainages.
