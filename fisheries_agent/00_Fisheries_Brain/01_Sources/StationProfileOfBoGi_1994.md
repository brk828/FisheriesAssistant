## Executive Summary

This station profile describes the [[Bo Ginn National Fish Hatchery and Aquarium]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Millen, Georgia, established in 1950 under authority of 63 Stat. 615. Originally constructed to produce fingerling [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Bluegill]], and [[Redear Sunfish]] for stocking farm ponds across 28 counties in eastern Georgia, the hatchery's mission has since expanded to include federal water stocking (military installations and National Wildlife Refuges) and restoration culture of anadromous and imperiled species — specifically Phase I and II [[Striped Bass]] and [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] — to rebuild depleted stocks in major river systems of Georgia and South Carolina. The document is primarily an administrative/operational profile rather than a species biology paper, but it provides quantitative data on production distribution, funding, and facility infrastructure relevant to hatchery-based fisheries management and conservation aquaculture.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Freshwater pond culture species**: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], and [[Channel Catfish]] are reared as fingerlings for stocking farm ponds and federal freshwater impoundments, functioning within warmwater pond/reservoir sport-fish and forage-fish ecological roles.
- **Anadromous restoration species**: [[Striped Bass]] (Phase I and II rearing) are cultured for reintroduction into major river systems in Georgia and South Carolina, targeting depleted anadromous stocks that migrate between riverine spawning habitat and coastal/estuarine waters.
- **Imperiled riverine species**: [[Shortnose Sturgeon]] (and [[Atlantic Sturgeon]], per distribution data) are cultured for restoration of depleted populations in the same river systems, reflecting their historic range in Atlantic slope drainages connecting to the Northwest Atlantic basin.
- **Distribution range**: Hatchery production is distributed across six states — Georgia, South Carolina, Florida, Virginia, North Carolina, and Louisiana — indicating a regional role in supporting both recreational pond fisheries and interstate riverine restoration efforts.
- **Facility habitat structure**: The hatchery operates 27 ponds (24.9 surface acres) and four 8' x 80' raceways, providing controlled freshwater rearing environments distinct from the natural riverine and estuarine habitats targeted for restocking.

## Key Findings

- **Establishment & Expansion**: Bo Ginn NFH was established in 1950 (construction begun 1951; operations began 1952) with 9 ponds; expanded to 27 ponds (24.9 surface acres) by 1981, when 4 raceways were also added.
- **Mission Shift**: Original mission (farm pond stocking in 28 eastern Georgia counties) has broadened to include Federal water stocking and anadromous/imperiled species restoration ([[Striped Bass]], [[Shortnose Sturgeon]]).
- **Infrastructure**: Facilities include office, shop/garage, residence, two pump houses, 27 ponds, metal storage house, paint/oil house, equipment house, public display aquaria, holding house, fertilizer house, and 4 raceways.
- **Funding Trends (Operations, in $K)**: 1991: 215.5 → 1992: 215.2 → 1993: 196.4 (allocation) → 1994: 204.2 (estimated), showing relatively stable but slightly declining operational funding.
- **Maintenance Funding**: Declined sharply from 43.0 (1991) to 18.6 (1992) to 6.0 (1993/94 est.), suggesting reduced capital investment in later years.
- **Staffing**: Full-time equivalents (FTEs) declined slightly from 5.5 (1991) to 5.2 (1992) to 5.0 (1993).
- **Production Distribution (Fiscal Year Totals)**:
  - 1990: 753,684 fish total (84,213 FWS waters; 240,671 other federal waters; 428,800 state waters)
  - 1991: 1,790,506 fish total (105,648 FWS; 458,938 other federal; 1,225,920 state) — marked increase, largely driven by state water stocking.
  - 1992: 1,516,078 fish total (327,144 FWS; 87,114 other federal; 1,101,840 state) — continued high state-water distribution despite reduced "other federal" contribution.
- **Geographic Reach**: Stocked species were distributed to six states (Georgia, South Carolina, Florida, Virginia, North Carolina, Louisiana), reflecting the hatchery's regional significance beyond its home state.
- **Species Portfolio**: Core production species remain [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], and [[Atlantic Sturgeon]], spanning both recreational warmwater fisheries and endangered/anadromous species restoration programs.
