# Systematic Notes on North American Siluroid Fishes of the Genus Schilbeodes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic revision of the small, venomous catfishes called madtoms (genus [[Schilbeodes]]) corrects nomenclature for four eastern North American species and describes a new subspecies. The authors, [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Edward C. Raney]], show that the name *insignis* properly belongs to the Mississippi Valley species, which was known as *exilis*. The eastern species is therefore renamed *Schilbeodes marginatus*, with a new subspecies, *S. m. atrorus*, described from the upper Kanawha and Roanoke systems of Virginia. Two other names are also revised: *gilberti* is retained for a rare Roanoke species, and *gyrinus* gives way to the older name *mollis* (Hermann, 1804). The paper is based on material from the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology, Cornell University, and the United States National Museum.

## Background & Description

Madtoms are small, poorly known freshwater catfishes of the eastern United States. The authors place all North American members of the family with an adnate adipose fin, except the stonecat (*Noturus flavus*), in the single genus *Schilbeodes*. They do not recognize the genus *Rabida* as distinct, because the pectoral spine ranges from smooth to serrated through a nearly unbroken series of intermediate forms.

The nomenclatural history is complex:

- **Priority of Pimelodon.** [[Charles Alexandre LeSueur]] introduced the name *Pimelodon* in 1819 for a species of *Schilbeodes*. The authors judged it unavailable until 1896, when it was republished in binomial form, so *Schilbeodes* (Bleeker, 1858) is retained.
- **Eastern madtom.** Long known as *S. insignis* (or *Rabida insignis*), it is renamed *S. marginatus* (Baird). The authors regard the Atlantic form as the valid marginatus, restricting the type to the Susquehanna River population.
- **Mississippi Valley madtom.** Known as *S. exilis*, it takes the name *S. insignis* (Richardson), based on LeSueur's *Pimelodus livrée*.
- **Priority of Hermann's name.** Johannes Hermann's 1804 *Silurus mollis* predates Mitchill's *Silurus gyrinus* (1818) and is used for the tadpole madtom.

The four taxa and their revised names are:

| Name in current use | Proposed designation |
|---|---|
| *Schilbeodes* or *Rabida insignis* | *S. marginatus marginatus*; *S. marginatus atrorus* (new subspecies) |
| *Schilbeodes* or *Rabida exilis* | *S. insignis* |
| *Schilbeodes* or *Rabida gilberti* | *S. gilberti* |
| *Schilbeodes gyrinus* | *S. mollis* |

Diagnostic characters include fin coloration (sharply bicolored, black-bordered fins in *atrorus*), number of anal rays, the proportions of the pectoral spine and fin, and the caudal ratios.

### Schilbeodes marginatus atrorus (new subspecies)

- **Diagnosis.** Vertical fins sharply bicolored, pale at the base and broadly bordered with black. Anal rays number 16 to 20 (average 18.17). The pectoral spine is long and strong, 1.6 to 1.9 in head length.
- **Size.** Typically exceeds 100 mm standard length, unlike *S. insignis*.
- **Holotype.** 112 mm standard length, collected by Hubbs and family on August 22, 1936, in Wolf Creek, Bland County, Virginia (UMMZ No. 139452). There are 15 paratypes.
- **Etymology.** From Latin *ater* (black) and *orus* (border).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All species are riffle inhabitants. Holotype and paratypes were collected under stones on riffles, and Reed Creek specimens came from rubble and boulder bottoms 1.5 to 2 feet deep. Water ranged from very clear to very silty. The authors confirmed the venom of the pectoral spine, as in related madtoms, through a field experiment at the type locality.

*Schilbeodes marginatus atrorus* lies within a montane fauna that crosses the [[Alleghenian Divide]] into the upper [[Kanawha River]] system. Other members include *Thoburnia rhothoeca*, *Chrosomus oreas*, *Nocomis leptocephalus*, *Notropis albeolus*, and a subspecies of *Campostoma anomalum*. The upper Kanawha fauna is separated from the Ohio River fauna by [[Kanawha Falls]].

The Mississippi Valley species, *S. insignis*, is rare or local over much of its range, possibly on the verge of extirpation. Its present center of abundance is the [[Ozark Upland]], where it is usually common under stones and dead leaves.

## Key Findings

1. **Nomenclatural reassignment.** The name *insignis* is shifted from the eastern species to the Mississippi Valley species, and the eastern species becomes *S. marginatus* (Baird). The authors conclude that the two named forms are specifically distinct.

2. **Geographic color gradient.** Fin coloration in *S. marginatus* forms a gradient:
   - Fins nearly unicolor with dusky margins in northern and southern Atlantic drainages.
   - Intermediate patterns in the Potomac, James, Chowan, Tar, and Neuse systems.
   - Sharply bicolored fins in the Roanoke River system (*atrorus*, slightly atypical).
   - Very sharply bicolored fins, typical *atrorus*, in the upper Kanawha (New) River system.

   Individual variation causes intergradation. In a sample of 52 Catawba River specimens, 2 or 3 had fins as sharply bicolored as Roanoke *atrorus*.

3. **Measurements.** Table III separates *S. marginatus* (20 to 31 specimens) from *S. insignis* (20 specimens). Average caudal ratio 1 is 1.5 in *marginatus* versus 2.0 in *insignis*. Average caudal ratio 2 is 2.6 versus 3.4. Average pectoral spine in head is 2.0 versus 2.5. Average head length in standard length is 3.8 versus 4.05.

4. **Meristic counts.** Anal rays of *S. marginatus* range from 15 to 20. The northern population averages 17 to 18 in analysis, and the southern population has a slightly higher average. Anal ray counts for *S. gyrinus* from southern New York and New Jersey average 15.50 across 20 specimens.

5. **Atrorus distribution.** The subspecies occupies the upper Kanawha (New) River system above Kanawha Falls and the Roanoke River system in Virginia. Its range interrupts that of the typical subspecies, an example of medial rather than peripheral differentiation.

6. **Gilberti status.** *S. gilberti* was known only from its original types until four specimens were collected from the Roanoke River at Glenvar, Virginia, on March 31, 1940. It is distinctive in having a short, broad head, an extremely short pectoral spine, a very low adipose fin, orange caudal borders, and very small eyes. A South Carolina record of *Rabida gilberti* by Fowler is referred to *S. marginatus*.

7. **Mollis priority.** Hermann's 1804 description *Silurus mollis* is interpreted as referring to the tadpole madtom (*gyrinus*). The authors argue that Hermann had a species with 12 anal rays and a "very soft and flabby" belly. The name *S. mollis* is therefore used for the species formerly called *S. gyrinus*, which is 14 years older than Mitchill's name.

8. **Distributional and taxonomic cautions.** Many older records are misidentifications. The Platte River locality for *occidentalis* and the "Ohio River" records for *marginatus* are errors. Some Great Lakes and Minnesota records of *S. insignis* remain doubtful.
