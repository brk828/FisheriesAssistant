# A key to the snooks, genus Centropomus (Centropomidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centropomidae

## Executive Summary

This work by Reeve M. Bailey presents a revised dichotomous taxonomic key to all twelve recognized species of the genus **[[Centropomus]]** (snooks), building on the posthumous systematic review by Rivas (1986) and earlier taxonomic literature (Regan, Meek & Hildebrand, Chavez, Fraser, Greenfield). The key was originally compiled in 1978 for identification of Guatemalan inland fishes and was substantially revised through 1997 to incorporate Rivas' methodology, meristic counts, and morphometric measurements. The genus comprises exclusively New World tropical and subtropical fishes that are primarily coastwise and estuarine, with most species capable of at least occasional freshwater incursion, though none are considered permanent freshwater residents. A central biogeographic theme is the existence of **transisthmian species pairs** — closely related Atlantic and Pacific counterparts separated by the Isthmus of Panama — reflecting a shared evolutionary history disrupted by the closure of the Central American seaway.

## Background & Description

The key relies on a suite of meristic (countable) and morphometric (proportional) characters to separate species groups and individual species within *Centropomus*:

### Diagnostic Characters Used
- **Anal soft ray counts** (5–8, modally 6 or 7) — primary character separating the [[Pectinatus Species Group]] (7 rays) from all others (6 rays)
- **Pectoral fin ray counts** (13–17)
- **Scales in row above the lateral line** (44–92, varies enormously by species group)
- **Gill raker counts** (excluding and including rudiments, first arch)
- **Scales around caudal peduncle** (18–31)
- **Scales between second dorsal-fin origin and lateral line** (6–16)
- **Preopercular spine size/number on posterior edge** (larger/fewer vs. smaller/more numerous, increasing with age)
- **Second anal spine morphology**: length (% SL), curvature (straight, slightly curved, or incurved), and relative position of tip versus third anal spine tip and caudal fin base
- **Pelvic fin length relative to vent**
- **Dorsal spine 3 length** (% SL) and shape of first dorsal fin (rounded vs. triangular)
- **Fleshy interorbital width** (% SL)
- **Coloration**: distal black pigmentation of spinous dorsal and pelvic fins; pigmentation of membrane between anal spines 2–3
- **Opercular flap extension** relative to vertical from dorsal fin origin
- **Lacrimal serration strength**

### Species Groups Defined by Key Characters

| Species Group | Anal Rays | Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|---|
| [[Pectinatus Species Group]] | 7 (rarely 8) | Pectoral rays ~13-15; 2nd anal spine straight/forward-curved; distal spinous dorsal/pelvic fins black |
| [[Undecimalis Species Group]] | 6 | Scales above lateral line 67-92; pectoral fin falls well short of pelvic tip; small numerous preopercular spines (27-73) |
| [[Ensiferus Species Group]] | 6 | Scales above lateral line 46-59; pectoral fin reaches near pelvic tip; large few preopercular spines (15-22); strong lacrimal serrations |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Centropomus* species occupy **coastal, estuarine, and euryhaline** habitats throughout the tropical and subtropical New World, spanning both the [[Atlantic Ocean]] and [[Pacific Ocean]] basins. Several species regularly penetrate freshwater systems:

- *C. undecimalis* enters freshwater as far inland as the Usumacinta basin, El Quiché, Guatemala
- *C. parallelus* enters freshwater habitats including **Lake Okeechobee** (Florida) and **[[Lake Nicaragua]]**
- *C. nigrescens* is noted to enter freshwaters on the Pacific coast

## Key Findings

- **Twelve recognized species** of *Centropomus* (per Rivas 1986) are organized into three or four **transisthmian species pairs**, reflecting vicariant speciation across the Isthmus of Panama:
  - [[Pectinatus Species Group]]: *C. pectinatus* (Atlantic) ↔ *C. medius* (Pacific)
  - [[Undecimalis Species Group]]: *C. undecimalis* (Atlantic) ↔ *C. viridis* (Pacific)
  - [[Ensiferus Species Group]]: *C. ensiferus* (Atlantic) ↔ *C. robalito* (Pacific)
  - Possible additional pair: *C. nigrescens* (Pacific) with *C. poeyi* (Atlantic), though relationship is uncertain
- Two Pacific species (*C. unionensis*, *C. armatus*) belong to the ensiferus group but lack clear Atlantic counterparts
- *C. mexicanus* and *C. parallelus* form a pair of **closely related, sympatric Atlantic species** rather than a transisthmian pair
- All 12 species of *Centropomus* are now confirmed to occur in **Mexican waters**, spanning both coasts
- Range extensions confirmed by Castro-Aguirre (in press) place *C. unionensis* in Oaxaca and the Gulf of California (Bahía de La Paz, Baja California Sur; Laguna Santa María y Altata, Sinaloa)
- *C. armatus* has disjunct confirmed records (Mazatlán, Mexico and Punta Arenas, Costa Rica) with a probable but undocumented continuous range between
- Eight of the twelve species occur in **Guatemala**, evenly split between Pacific and Caribbean coasts (four each); *C. poeyi* and *C. mexicanus* are notably absent from the Caribbean coast of Central America
- The key distinguishes species groups primarily via **anal soft ray counts** (7 vs. 6) and further subdivides using scale counts, gill raker counts, second anal spine morphology, and fin coloration
- Maximum body size differs sharply between groups: **undecimalis group** species can exceed 900 mm total length, while related forms with shorter/weaker second anal spines remain under 650 mm max length
- This key represents a synthesis and refinement of Rivas' (1986) posthumous systematic review, incorporating his morphometric methodology into a practical field-identification tool
