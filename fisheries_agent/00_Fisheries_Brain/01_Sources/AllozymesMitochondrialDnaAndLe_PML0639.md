# Allozymes, Mitochondrial DNA, and Levels of Phylogenetic Resolution in North American Minnow Species (*Notropis*: Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1989 study by Dowling and Brown, published in *Systematic Zoology*, compares [[Allozyme Analysis]] and [[Mitochondrial DNA]] (mtDNA) restriction-site data to infer relationships among four [[Notropis]] minnow species. Allozymes resolved a grouping of *N. cornutus* and *N. chrysocephalus* as a monophyletic lineage, consistent with morphology. mtDNA data supported monophyly of each species but failed to resolve relationships among species. The authors evaluate four explanations for this failure: stochastic lineage extinction, high sequence divergence, rate heterogeneity with homoplasy, and introgressive hybridization. They conclude that rate heterogeneity and/or introgression are the most plausible causes, and that assumptions of rate homogeneity and strict vertical inheritance should be scrutinized when interpreting mtDNA data.

## Background & Description

*Notropis* is the largest and most diverse group of fishes in North America, with most species in the eastern United States and the greatest diversity in southern states. Phylogenetic relationships within the genus remained largely unresolved prior to this work, despite earlier [[Morphological Systematics]] and protein electrophoresis studies.

The four focal species, along with the outgroup *Pimephales notatus* (Bluntnose Minnow), are:

- **[[Notropis Cornutus]] (Common Shiner)**: subgenus *Luxilus*; ranges from Ontario and Quebec south to northern Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Virginia.
- **[[Notropis Chrysocephalus]] (Striped Shiner)**: subgenus *Luxilus*; more southern range, from central Michigan, southern Ontario, and New York south to the Gulf of Mexico. Long considered a subspecies of *N. cornutus*.
- **[[Notropis Photogenis]] (Silver Shiner)**: subgenus *Notropis*; widely distributed in the Midwest, from southern Michigan and Ontario south to southern Tennessee.
- **[[Notropis Rubellus]] (Rosyface Shiner)**: classified in subgenus *Hydrophlox*, but the authors posit affinity with *Notropis*; subspecies *N. r. rubellus* (Atlantic slope and midwest) and *N. r. micropteryx* (Tennessee-Cumberland drainages).

*N. cornutus* and *N. chrysocephalus* hybridize extensively with introgression in sympatric zones. *N. rubellus* hybridizes with both, although one study found 36 of 41 F1 hybrids incapable of producing gametes. Hybridization involving *N. photogenis* is rare or unreported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling covered 12 populations across Michigan, New York, Virginia, Ohio, and Tennessee, drawn from drainages including the Muskegon, Susquehanna, James, Raisin, Scioto, and Tennessee rivers. Tissues were collected fresh or kept on ice for no more than 24 hours. Biogeographic evidence suggests *N. rubellus* was absent from Atlantic Coast drainages before or during the Pleistocene, and that glaciation affected much of the current range of *N. cornutus* and *N. rubellus*. Transient glacial lakes linking Great Lakes tributaries with Atlantic drainages are proposed as a route for recent colonization.

## Key Findings

**Methods**
- mtDNA was digested with 15 six-base-recognizing [[Restriction Endonuclease]] enzymes, producing cleavage at a total of 130 sites.
- Allozymes: 24 presumptive gene loci were resolved in 54 individuals (five per population from eleven populations); 20 loci were polymorphic, with 53 alleles resolved.
- Analyses used cladistic parsimony (PAUP), distance-based methods (UPGMA, Distance Wagner), and bootstrapping (PHYLIP).

**Allozyme results**
- Cladistic analysis by locus and by allele both placed *N. cornutus* and *N. chrysocephalus* in a monophyletic clade. Bootstrap support was strong (93 and 97 per 100 replicates for the two lineages in the allele-based analysis, with *N. rubellus* at 99 and 97).
- The *N. rubellus*–*N. photogenis* node was weakly supported (64/100).
- Allele-based tree length was 43 steps with a consistency index of 0.951.
- UPGMA clustering agreed with the cladistic topology, except that Virginia *N. cornutus* clustered outside *N. chrysocephalus*, attributed to homoplasy.
- Allozyme distances ranged from 0.00 to 1.09.

**mtDNA results**
- Of 130 cleavage sites, 50 were autapomorphic, 15 plesiomorphic, and 65 informative.
- Parsimony produced four minimum-length trees of 118 steps (consistency index 0.551).
- Each species was monophyletic, but relationships among *N. cornutus*, *N. chrysocephalus*, and *N. photogenis* formed an unresolved trichotomy distinct from *N. rubellus*.
- mtDNA sequence divergence ranged from 0 to 14%, with most comparisons below 12%.

**Sequence divergence across the genome**
- The mtDNA molecule showed about a five-fold difference in evolutionary rate among quarters. Pairwise divergence estimates were 3.7% and 6.5% for the slow first and third quarters, and 15.6% and 15.0% for the fast second and fourth quarters.
- The conserved first region (sites 1–27) includes the 12S and 16S rRNA genes, which the authors suggest could serve as phylogenetic markers across cyprinid genera.

**Explanations for mtDNA unresolved relationships**
- *Stochastic lineage extinction*: judged unlikely, because divergences were large and each species formed a resolved monophyletic unit.
- *High sequence divergence*: judged unlikely, since most divergences fell below the 10–15% range where saturation becomes problematic.
- *Rate heterogeneity*: plausible; the Distance Wagner analysis showed extensive branch-length variation, and relative rates may be elevated within *N. rubellus*.
- *Introgressive hybridization*: plausible; up to 60% of morphologically identified *N. chrysocephalus* in some populations carried *N. cornutus* mtDNA (Dowling et al., 1989).

**Conclusions and future work**
- The authors state that the two explanations may be impossible to distinguish, and propose maximum-likelihood analyses (Smouse et al., in prep.) to estimate rate variation.
- Analysis of mtDNA for the subgenus *Luxilus* (Dowling et al., in prep.) gave topologies consistent with morphology and allozymes.
- The study argues that mtDNA rate homogeneity and strict maternal inheritance should be scrutinized before mtDNA is used as a definitive phylogenetic marker.
