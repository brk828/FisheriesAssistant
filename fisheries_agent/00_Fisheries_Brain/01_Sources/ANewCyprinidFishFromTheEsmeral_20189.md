# Esmeralda Fossils and Entomological Notes from the Biological Society of Nevada Occasional Papers

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation of short taxonomic papers from the [[Biological Society of Nevada]] Occasional Papers describes four new taxa from the Pliocene freshwater deposits of southeastern and western Nevada, plus a comparative taxonomic note on a predaceous beetle and a new ichneumonid wasp from Idaho. The main fish content is the new cyprinid *Gila esmeralda* and the new salmonid *Salmo esmeralda*, both based on fragmentary fossils from the [[Alum Mine]] near [[Silver Peak]]. The set also includes a new fossil frog, *Rana plax*, and an editorial comment on Nevada's state bird selection. The fossil fish papers stress that the type material is incomplete and that better specimens may have been removed from the site in the 1920s.

## Background & Description

**Gila esmeralda (Cyprinidae, fossil)**
- Described by [[Ira La Rivers]] as a new species from a specimen of about five inches standard length, preserved only in its posterior half.
- Anal fin supported by 8 main rays, all but the first subdivided distally; fin length 20 mm.
- Caudal fin seemingly has about 21 main rays; each lobe about 30 mm long.
- Dorsal fin has 8 detectable main rays; fin length 23 mm. The dorsal fin tip overlaps the anal fin origin.
- The caudal peduncle is described as 1/7 to 1/8 as wide as the body is long.
- Compared with *Gila lineatus* (originally *Leuciscus lineatus*, Lucas 1900), it differs in dorsal fin overlap and in a heavier, more robust caudal peduncle. Ray counts are also lower.
- The author notes that the Western genus *Richardsonius* is now generally combined into *Gila*.

**Salmo esmeralda (Salmonidae, fossil)**
- Based on a single essentially entire right mandible impression, 115 mm (about 4.5 inches) long and 21 mm wide, with large tooth sockets along its anterior outer edge.
- Fragments of a maxilla, vomer and one frontal are also present.
- The source gives the fish's standard length as "about 36 inches," which is inconsistent with the 115 mm mandible and is probably a scan or typesetting error.
- Attributed to *Salmo* rather than *Oncorhynchus*, though *Oncorhynchus* cannot be fully excluded because diagnostic features are missing.

**Rana plax (Ranidae, fossil frog)**
- Known from a ventral impression in diatomite, including a pelvic girdle, right hind leg and parts of the anterior limbs.
- Right ilium about 30 mm; urostyle 29 mm long, 4 mm wide; left femur 30 × 4 mm; left tibio-fibula 30 × 3.5 mm.
- Named for its more robust bones than [[Rana Johnsoni]], to which it was first assigned (Ira La Rivers, 1953 description).

**Phaeogenes arogae (Ichneumonidae)**
- New species described by [[Arthur R. Griffins]] and [[John E. Henry]] from Idaho to make the name available for later biology papers.
- Holotype female, 5.8 mm long; body black with yellowish-brown antennae, legs and abdominal tergite apices.
- Associated with *Aroga websteri*, a sagebrush defoliator.

**Enoclerus cowani (predaceous beetle, comparative note)**
- The text compares *E. cowani* with *E. lecontei*, *E. eximius*, *E. acerbus* and *E. cupressi* on elytral markings, coloration, distribution and host plants.
- *E. cowani* is associated with Douglas fir and is reported as a predator of *Dendroctonus pseudotsugae*, a bark beetle.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Esmeralda lake system:** The fish fossils indicate large freshwater lakes in Esmeralda time with cyprinid fishes resembling those of the modern [[Lahontan Basin]]. The author states that the same genera likely inhabited both systems.
- **Matrix:** The *Gila* paratype sits in a muddy vitric tuff, and the type in a rhyolitic tuff. The *Salmo* matrix is a massive rhyolitic welded tuff without bedding planes.
- **Alum Mine site:** Once productive, the site is now largely depleted, producing only fragments after careful searching. Collectors reportedly removed many specimens in the 1920s, and their location is unknown. The Coaldale Junction locality (7 miles SE) is described as more productive.
- **Rana plax site:** The Trinity Mountains diatomite workings yielded plant remains and little else.
- **Predaceous beetles:** The *Enoclerus* species are separated by range and host plant. *E. eximius* occurs along streams and canyons with alder and willow; *E. acerbus* in sagebrush and salt-desert shrub of the northern Great Basin; *E. cupressi* in coastal central California with cypress; and *E. cowani* in the Oregon Coast Range.

## Key Findings

- **Two new fossil fish species** were described: *Gila esmeralda* (cyprinid) and *Salmo esmeralda* (salmonid), both from the Alum Mine, Esmeralda County, Nevada.
- **Type data:** *G. esmeralda* type collected 10 April 1957; paratypes collected 30 January 1966 (Ira La Rivers and Jerry Gray). *S. esmeralda* collected 29 January 1966. Type and paratypes of *G. esmeralda* are held by the [[Biological Society of Nevada]], the University of Nevada at Reno and the Nevada State Museum.
- **Taxonomic revision:** The author argues that *Lineatus* fits better in *Gila* than *Richardsonius*, though fossil material cannot resolve modern diagnostic characters.
- **Material limitations:** Both fish descriptions rest on incomplete material. The author estimates that much better specimens existed in the original collections.
- **New frog:** *Rana plax* is distinguished from *R. johnsoni* by more robust bones (femur and tibio-fibula about 4 mm and 3.5 mm wide at the ends).
- **New wasp:** *Phaeogenes arogae* is described from one female holotype, linked to a sagebrush defoliator.
- **Beetle comparison:** *E. cowani* is separated from congeners mainly by elytral markings and host plant range.
- **Editorial comment:** The text criticizes the selection of the Mountain Bluebird as Nevada's state bird, based on the authors' view of the selectors' knowledge of Great Basin biology.
