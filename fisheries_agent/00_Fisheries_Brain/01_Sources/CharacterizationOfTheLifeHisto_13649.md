# Characterization of the Life History and Ecology of the Humpback Chub in the Grand Canyon - Trip Report #5, 1991

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #endangered-species #radiotelemetry #spawning-ecology #grand-canyon

## Executive Summary

This trip report documents field research conducted May 8-26, 1991, investigating [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) life history and ecology in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the [[Colorado River]]. The study employed multiple gear types (trammel nets, electrofishing, hoop nets, gill nets) and [[Radiotelemetry]] to monitor fish movements, particularly during the spawning season. A total of 68 humpback chub were handled (33 adults, 35 juveniles), with 6 recaptures. Three fish received radio transmitters; five previously tagged fish were tracked. Key observations included adult movement into the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) for spawning, appearance of 35 juveniles in mainstem habitats below the LCR confluence, evidence of spawning activity (gravid females, tuberculated males, fin abrasions), and suspected bird predation on one radio-tagged individual. The research expanded known humpback chub distribution to include mainstem areas near [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]]. One adult female mortality occurred during netting operations.

## Background & Description

The [[Humpback Chub]] is an endangered cyprinid endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. This investigation, conducted by BIO/WEST Inc. for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], focuses on characterizing life history and ecological requirements within the [[Grand Canyon]] reach. The study area extends from approximately River Mile (RM) 30 to RM 225, encompassing multiple habitat types including mainstem river, the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence (RM 61.4), and various tributary inflows.

Adult humpback chub are large-bodied cyprinids; fish handled during this trip ranged from 296-404 mm total length (TL) for adults suitable for radio transmitter implantation. Juveniles captured ranged 43-176 mm TL. The species exhibits distinctive morphology including pronounced nuchal humps in adults and tuberculation during spawning condition. Reproductive adults display vivid coloration, firm abdomens (pre-spawn), and characteristic abrasions on fins and vents (post-spawn).

The [[Little Colorado River]] serves as critical spawning habitat. During May 1991, LCR conditions transitioned from high turbidity (spring runoff) to clearing water with conductivities reaching 4,500 µmhos and temperatures 18-21°C by trip end—conditions that impaired telemetry effectiveness but indicated base flows from Blue Springs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area encompasses a dynamic riverine ecosystem influenced by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations, tributary inputs, and seasonal flow regimes. During Trip #5, mainstem flows fluctuated from 5,000 to 26,000 cfs, creating variable habitat conditions that influenced fish distribution and behavior.

**Habitat Utilization**: Juvenile humpback chub (n=35) were captured primarily in mainstem shoreline habitats characterized by slow to moderately fast runs and eddies with boulder and sand substrates, from the LCR confluence (RM 61.4) downstream to Lava Canyon (RM 65.5). Radio-tagged adults in the LCR consistently occupied eddies created by instream boulder cover within 1000 m of the confluence, suggesting staging or active spawning behavior.

**Tributary Confluences**: Adult concentrations at [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]] suggest these sites may serve as attempted spawning locations. Seven adults captured near tributary inflows exhibited spawning characteristics (tuberculation, abrasions). The LCR plume and mixing zone appeared particularly important, with one gravid female expressing eggs during processing and one egg collected from substrates in this area.

**Sympatric Species**: [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) and [[Bluehead Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*) were abundant, with large spawning aggregations observed in the LCR plume and tributary streams. Flannelmouth sucker movement into [[Kanab Creek]] was heavily flow-dependent; >60 individuals utilized flooded portions during 26,000 cfs flows, but only one fish was captured during 5,000 cfs stable flows. [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*) were documented in Reach 3 for the first time (RM 215-219), suggesting seasonal movement from [[Lake Mead]].

**Predation Risk**: One radio transmitter was recovered 30 m up the LCR, approximately 15-20 vertical feet above water surface, with a white feather adhered to it. An [[Osprey]] was sighted in the area on consecutive days, strongly suggesting avian predation on the tagged fish.

## Key Findings

**Population Monitoring**: 68 humpback chub handled; 33 adults, 35 juveniles. Six recaptures bearing [[Carlin Tag]], [[Floy Tag]], or [[PIT Tag]]. Two juveniles escaped during processing. One adult female mortality occurred in trammel net operations—only the second incidental mortality in the investigation.

**Spawning Phenology & Behavior**: 
- Adult catch rates in mainstem declined, particularly large adults in LCR confluence area, indicating upstream spawning migration into LCR
- One gravid female expressed eggs during processing at LCR mouth
- Three post-spawn females exhibited flaccid abdomens and conspicuous fin/vent abrasions
- Five females showed spawning coloration, slight tuberculation, firm abdomens but no spawning abrasions
- One heavily tuberculated male captured at RM 61.7; four males at LCR confluence lacked expressible gametes
- Larval fish (primarily catostomids, some cyprinids) became conspicuous in LCR on May 20

**Radiotelemetry Results**: 
- 3 new transmitter implants (frequencies 40.650, 40.660, 40.670 MHz)
- 5 previously tagged fish contacted; 4 resided in LCR within 1000 m of confluence throughout trip
- 2 fish initially in mainstem (May 9-10); one moved into LCR by May 17
- 24-hour monitoring conducted on one mainstem fish; prolonged observations documented diel vertical movement patterns
- One fish recaptured 65 days post-implant (originally tagged March 11, 1991); primary incision healed, fish active and healthy, slight swelling/redness at antenna exit
- 2 transmitters assumed active were not contacted
- Lower remote telemetry station operational May 13; successfully logged free-ranging tagged fish but may allow undetected passage in deep channel at high flows

**Juvenile Distribution**: 35 juveniles (43-176 mm TL) captured in mainstem from LCR confluence to Lava Canyon—unprecedented catch rates suggesting movement or transport from LCR during spring runoff. Previous electrofishing in this reach did not yield similar juvenile densities.

**Range Extension**: Distribution expanded to include mainstem near [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], and [[Havasu Creek]] confluences. Seven adults captured at or near these tributary mouths; several exhibited spawning condition (tuberculation, abrasions), suggesting potential spawning attempts at multiple sites beyond the LCR.

**Environmental Conditions**: LCR turbidity decreased consistently throughout trip; water regained characteristic blue color by May 16 (base flow conditions). Conductivity increased to 4,500 µmhos, severely limiting telemetry effectiveness. Temperatures: 18-21°C.

**Methodological Challenges**: 
- Increasing LCR conductivity reduced telemetry range; coordination with LCR sampling teams (AGF, ASU) increasingly critical
- Limited mainstem telemetry data as most tagged fish occupied LCR
- Reduced capture rates of implantable-size fish due to spawning migration; only 3 implants completed vs. target numbers
- Lower telemetry station may not achieve 100% detection due to deep channel configuration, particularly at high flows

**Recommendations**: (1) Extend remote telemetry station operation beyond June 15 pending NPS approval; (2) Increase netting effort to capture implantable fish; (3) Maintain coordination between mainstem and LCR research teams; (4) Continue electrofishing in LCR region to track juvenile densities; (5) Extend electrofishing below Lava Chuar Rapid (RM 65.5) to determine extent of juvenile movement/transport.
