# Biological Evaluation of Spur Cross Ranch, Maricopa County, Arizona

#environmental-assessment #habitat-survey #endangered-species #desert-ecology #riparian-ecology

## Executive Summary

SWCA, Inc. Environmental Consultants conducted a biological evaluation of the approximately 2,154-acre [[Spur Cross Ranch]] project site in [[Maricopa County]], [[Arizona]], adjacent to the southern boundary of the [[Tonto National Forest]]. The evaluation was commissioned by The Pinnacle Group to support a planned community development. The study mapped vegetation communities and assessed habitat suitability for special-interest species. Based on the 1996 Development Master Plan Amendment, approximately 726 acres (34%) of upland and xeroriparian habitat would be directly impacted through grading and physical disturbance, while 1,145 acres (53%) would be designated as natural open space. An additional 37% of Rural Residential areas (283 of 768 acres) would likely remain undisturbed. No long-term direct impacts to [[Mixed Broadleaf Riparian]] or aquatic habitats are anticipated. Species-specific surveys were conducted for bats, fish, and [[Cactus Ferruginous Pygmy-Owl]]. The evaluation addressed 17 special-interest species, including 10 listed or proposed for listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Background & Description

The project site encompasses all of Sections 5 and 6 and portions of Sections 4, 7, 8, and 9, Township 6 North, Range 4 East of the Gila and Salt River Base and Meridian. The property includes [[Elephant Mountain]] and portions of two major drainages: [[Cave Creek]] and [[Cottonwood Creek]]. Topography is steep and heavily dissected with numerous rock outcrops. Elevations range from approximately 2,200 feet along Cave Creek at the southeastern end to about 3,926 feet at the top of Elephant Mountain. The region is underlain by metavolcanic rocks, volcanic rocks, basaltic rocks, metasedimentary rocks, and older surficial deposits.

Field reconnaissance was conducted July 24-26, 1996, with additional surveys on October 8-9, October 9-10, November 18, and November 25-26, 1996. Due to access limitations, field visits were restricted to the eastern half of the property, though the eastern and southern slopes of Elephant Mountain were scanned with binoculars. Vegetation communities were mapped according to Brown (1994). Species-specific surveys followed established protocols: mine features were evaluated for bat roosting habitat following Kingsley et al. (1991); electrofishing was conducted in Cave Creek on November 18 by biologists from SWCA, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS); [[Cactus Ferruginous Pygmy-Owl]] surveys followed AGFD protocol with 51 call stations during the first survey and 120 call stations during the second survey, with calls broadcast at approximately 150-meter intervals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project site is located in the [[Arizona Upland Subdivision]] of the [[Sonoran Desertscrub]] biotic community. One upland habitat type, two riparian habitat types, and three wetlands were identified. The upland habitat is dominated by triangle-leaf bursage (*Ambrosia deltoidea*) with common associates including foothill paloverde (*Cercidium microphyllum*) and jojoba (*Simmondsia chinensis*). Saguaros (*Carnegiea gigantea*) are abundant with densities exceeding 20 per acre in some areas. No agaves were observed.

Riparian habitats include:
- **[[Xeroriparian Mixed Scrub]]**: Associated with ephemeral water supply throughout most drainages including Cottonwood Creek, dominated by mesquite, catclaw, desert broom, and burrobush.
- **[[Mixed Broadleaf Woodland]]**: Occurs along Cave Creek and a lower tributary, best developed at the southern end where surface water was present, dominated by Goodding willow (*Salix gooddingii*) and Fremont cottonwood (*Populus fremontii*).

Three wetlands occur along approximately 1,000 feet of Cave Creek at the southern end, characterized by surface water with cattails (*Typha* sp.) and bulrushes (*Scirpus* sp.). Jurisdictional status had not been determined by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers at the time of the report.

Nine mine adits and shafts were surveyed; five were considered potentially suitable for bat occupation. Approximately 10 [[California Leaf-Nosed Bat]] (*Macrotus californicus*) were observed in Adit #3 on July 24, and two additional adits contained bat droppings on November 18, 1996. The rocky terrain provides numerous potential natural roost sites including crevices and rock overhangs.

## Key Findings

**Habitat Impacts**: Approximately 726 acres of upland and xeroriparian habitat (34% of project area) would be directly impacted through grading and physical disturbance. About 1,145 acres (53%) would remain as undisturbed natural open space. An additional 283 acres (37% of Rural Residential areas) would likely not be directly impacted. No long-term direct impacts to Mixed Broadleaf Riparian or aquatic habitats are anticipated. Direct impacts to aquatic habitats from bridge construction and utility crossings would be temporary and localized. Potential indirect impacts include increased nutrients and other constituents from golf course and residential runoff, to be addressed through Stormwater Management and Golf Course Management Plans.

**Special Status Species Evaluations** (17 species assessed):

*Mammals*:
- [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]] (*Leptonycteris curasoae yerbabuenae*): Project area outside late summer/fall range; extremely unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.
- [[California Leaf-Nosed Bat]] (*Macrotus californicus*): Observed in project area (approximately 10 individuals in mine adit); unlikely to be adversely impacted as no known adits/shafts affected by development.

*Birds*:
- [[American Peregrine Falcon]] (*Falco peregrinus anatum*): No known nest sites; may occasionally forage; extremely unlikely to be adversely impacted.
- [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (*Strix occidentalis lucida*): Habitat not typical; extremely unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.
- [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*): Habitat not typical for breeding; may occasionally visit; extremely unlikely to be adversely impacted.
- [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*): Habitat not typical for breeding; may rarely occur during migration; unlikely to be significantly impacted.
- [[Harris' Hawk]] (*Parabuteo unicinctus*): Likely to occur; 726 acres of habitat would be impacted but population-level impacts expected to be insignificant given widespread distribution and abundance in urban settings.
- [[Cactus Ferruginous Pygmy-Owl]] (*Glaucidium brasilianum cactorum*): None detected during surveys (51 and 120 call stations); unlikely to occur despite suitable habitat; unlikely to be adversely impacted.

*Reptiles and Amphibians*:
- [[Desert Tortoise]] (*Gopherus agassizii*): One individual observed in conglomerate cave on November 26; likely occurs elsewhere in project area; 726 acres of direct impact plus up to 441 acres potentially unavailable due to fencing, but population-level impacts expected to be insignificant.
- [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] (*Rana chiricahuensis*): None observed; project area below typical elevational distribution (4,600-8,000 feet); extremely unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.
- [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (*Rana yavapaiensis*): Approximately five individuals observed in small pools along Cave Creek at northern end; unlikely to be adversely impacted based on no anticipated long-term direct impacts to aquatic habitats.

*Fish*:
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*): None observed during electrofishing surveys on November 18; sunfish (predatory species) relatively common; unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*): None observed during electrofishing; extremely unlikely to occur given current distribution; extremely unlikely to be adversely impacted.
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*): Found at northern and southern ends of Cave Creek during electrofishing; unlikely to be adversely impacted based on no anticipated long-term direct impacts to aquatic habitats.

*Plants*:
- [[Arizona Hedgehog Cactus]] (*Echinocereus triglochidiatus arizonicus*): None observed; habitat not similar to known occupied sites; extremely unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.
- [[Hohokam Agave]] (*Agave murpheyi*): No agaves observed; unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.
- [[Arizona Agave]] (*Agave arizonica*): No agaves observed; habitat not typical (no oak-juniper woodland or mountain mahogany-oak scrub); unlikely to occur or be adversely impacted.

**Fish Survey Results**: Electrofishing conducted November 18, 1996 by SWCA, AGFD, and USFWS documented green sunfish, yellow bullhead, fathead minnow, and longfin dace in Cave Creek. No federally listed fish species were detected.

**General Wildlife**: Large mammals observed included mule deer (*Odocoileus hemionus*) and collared peccary (*Tayassu tajacu*), with at least three peccaries observed in mine adit #5. Red-tailed hawk (*Buteo jamaicensis*) was the only raptor observed. Plant species list (Appendix A) and wildlife species list (Appendix B) provided but noted as not comprehensive.

**Management Recommendations**: Developer preparing Stormwater Management Plan and Golf Course Management Plan to address potential indirect impacts to aquatic habitats from increased nutrients and other constituents. SWCA to be included in development of these plans.
