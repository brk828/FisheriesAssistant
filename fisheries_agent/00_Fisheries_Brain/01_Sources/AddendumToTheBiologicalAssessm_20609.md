# Addendum to the Biological Assessment: Nonnative Fish Transport by the CAP Aqueduct into the Santa Cruz River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum (June 1995, Phoenix Area Office) transmits an addendum to the March 1994 [[Biological Assessment]] on whether nonnative fishes carried by the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) aqueduct could invade the [[Santa Cruz River]] Basin. The 1994 assessment found "no effect" on listed species, based on a "low probability" of invasion. The [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] did not concur in a December 6, 1994 letter, citing limited project information. The addendum adds new data and answers FWS requests. It covers proposed groundwater recharge and transport projects that would use untreated CAP water, streamflow records from five USGS gauges, the threat posed by specific nonnative fishes, and a proposal to move CAP water to the San Xavier District Farm through natural arroyos. Reclamation asks FWS whether further consultation is needed.

## Background & Description

The memorandum was sent to Sam Spiller, State Supervisor of the Arizona Ecological Services office of the Fish and Wildlife Service. Copies went to the Central Arizona Water Conservation District, Arizona State University researchers (W.L. Minckley and Paul Marsh), and the director of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

The addendum has several parts:

- **Groundwater recharge and natural transport:** Figures and tables describe about ten proposed recharge projects in the [[Tucson Active Management Area]]. Five would use untreated CAP water for surface recharge.
- **Santa Cruz River hydrology:** USGS discharge records from five gauges were analyzed for consecutive days of flow.
- **Species analysis:** The assessment considers fishes present in the CAP system or historically in the Gila River Basin.
- **Distribution and storage:** Table 2 updates CAP turnouts by use class and status.
- **Attachment 1:** A proposal to deliver CAP water to the San Xavier District Farm through the [[Reach 6 Pipeline]] and natural arroyos.

Recharge projects discussed include the [[Pima Mine Road Recharge Project]], the [[Avra Valley Recharge Project]], the [[Lower Santa Cruz River Replenishment Project]], the [[Canada del Oro Recharge Demonstration Project]], and the [[Upper Santa Cruz In-Channel Recharge Project]]. The Upper Santa Cruz project was being considered as an interim measure for Tucson's CAP treatment and pumping operations during a moratorium on direct use of treated CAP water.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document centers on the risk of CAP-introduced fishes escaping into stream reaches used by threatened and endangered species.

**Hydrology.** Upper gauges near Lochiel and Nogales, in the vicinity of endangered fish populations, have flowing water 85.4% and 87.5% of the time (44 and 58 years of record). Flow exceeded 1,000 consecutive days at both sites, so these reaches are considered near-perennial and able to support reproducing fish. Downstream gauges at Continental, Tucson, and Cortaro have surface flow less than 15% of the time (27 to 66 years of record). At Continental (38 years), flow lasted 150 consecutive days from September 1983 to February 1984. Most flow events are receding flows, and fishes are expected to show the greatest movement during these periods.

**Nonnative threats.** [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Yellow Bullhead]] are piscivorous at larger sizes. Flathead and channel catfish have been documented to severely deplete reintroduced [[Razorback Sucker]] populations through predation. The [[Red Shiner]] has been implicated in the widespread decline of native Arizona fishes, including [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]. The ictalurids have been recorded in the upper basin only at single localities in [[Sonoita Creek]], and the red shiner at two localities there. Mainstem perennial habitats are considered potentially suitable for one or more of these species.

**Recharge as an escape route.** Recharge and transport projects using untreated CAP water may periodically establish CAP-derived fish populations in natural drainage channels. The document calls this "perhaps the most probable" escapement route, with establishment sites in or near the mainstem Santa Cruz River. Surface recharge is expected to run on wet/dry cycles, more often in summer and less often in winter.

**Physical setting.** A May 4, 1995 helicopter overflight from near Eloy to the International Boundary found no in-channel irrigation sumps or ponded areas in major ephemeral reaches. Golf course ponds and stock tanks adjacent to the channel likely lie within the 100-year floodplain.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory status:** The 1994 BA's "no effect" finding was not accepted by FWS. The addendum supplies new data and requests advice on whether further consultation is required.
- **Recharge projects:** About ten projects are proposed in the Tucson AMA. Five would use untreated CAP water. Reported volumes include:
  - Pima Mine Road: 18,000 acre-feet per year (af/yr)
  - Avra Valley: 15,000 to 30,000 af/yr
  - Lower Santa Cruz River: about 90,000 af/yr, which could be recharged at the Sanders Road alternative
  - Canada del Oro: 20,000 to 30,000 af/yr
  - Upper Santa Cruz In-Channel: about 1,000 acre-feet per month, possibly starting summer 1995
- **Escapement risk:** Untreated CAP water discharged to channels is identified as the most probable route for fish establishment in the Santa Cruz system.
- **Hydrologic thresholds:** Upper reaches flow most of the time and can support reproduction. Downstream reaches flow less than 15% of the time, but long flow events (up to 150 consecutive days at Continental) allow fish movement.
- **Predatory and competitive threats:** Ictalurids and the red shiner pose the most serious threats to listed species if they gain widespread access. Sonoita Creek is considered a more likely invasion source than the Tucson-area Santa Cruz.
- **San Xavier proposal (Attachment 1):** Reclamation proposes delivering CAP water through blowoff valves on the 7.4-mile, 72-inch Reach 6 pipeline into natural arroyos, which drain to farm areas on both sides of I-19. Water would be captured in charcos. Area B would serve as a pilot.
  - Area A: 3 blowoffs, 1.9 miles of pipeline
  - Area B: 5 blowoffs, 1.6 miles
  - Area C: 8 blowoffs, 3.7 miles
- **Claimed advantages:** The system is already in place, is flexible, provides recharge, and costs substantially less than a new pipeline. It may also create wetlands.
- **Identified disadvantages:** Flows in arroyos are hard to control, flood potential must be addressed, silt and biological growth may need clearing, and mine-tailing contamination requires testing.
- **Pilot program needs:** Soil sampling, vegetation surveys, horizontal and vertical control, aerial and infrared photography, and hazardous materials testing of mine wastes.
- **Decision status:** No decision has been made on the San Xavier proposal, and Reclamation is seeking FWS advice before proceeding.
