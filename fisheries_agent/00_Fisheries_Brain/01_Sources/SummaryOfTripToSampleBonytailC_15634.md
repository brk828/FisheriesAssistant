# Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker Sampling Survey, Lake Mohave, March 1988

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species #conservation

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents an intensive interagency sampling effort conducted March 5-13, 1988, at [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona-Nevada, targeting the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Camp Five, stationed at [[Arrowhead Cove]], was responsible for sampling the lower 10-12 miles of the lake. The effort involved multiple federal agencies (Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Land Management, Bureau of Reclamation, Geological Survey) and academic institutions (University of Arizona, Arizona State University). The camp successfully captured one bonytail chub (the only one captured across all camps) and 61 razorback suckers using trammel nets and electrofishing methods. A total of 281 fish representing nine species were documented. The single bonytail chub specimen, a 444mm female, died in captivity at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and was preserved for further analysis including otolith aging and tetracycline mark examination.

## Background & Description

[[Lake Mohave]] is a reservoir system supporting populations of endangered native cyprinids and catostomids alongside introduced sport fish species. The [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) is an endangered cyprinid of conservation concern, while the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large-bodied catostomid also requiring protection and monitoring.

Camp Five established operations at [[Arrowhead Cove]] (site #26) on the Arizona side, approximately 2.25 river miles above [[Katherine Landing]]. The site was selected for its accessibility via dirt road, protection from wave action, limited visitor traffic, and documented presence of razorback suckers. The sampling area encompassed the lower 10-12 miles of Lake Mohave.

The captured bonytail chub was a female measuring 444mm total length and weighing 599 grams. Examination revealed extremely small but developing ova, an extremely pale liver, fin erosion on all fins, and parasitic *Lernaea* infestations at the base of dorsal, anal, and pelvic fins and under the opercular flap. Bloody discharge from the vent was noted both at death and during post-mortem examination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sampling effort occurred within a multi-camp, lake-wide survey coordinated by Drs. Minckley and Marsh of [[Arizona State University]], representing the largest sampling effort yet conducted on [[Lake Mohave]] for these endangered species. The ecosystem supports a mixed fish assemblage including native endangered species and numerous introduced species.

The fish community documented at Camp Five included:
- Native endangered species: [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]]
- Introduced sport fish: [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*)
- Introduced rough fish: [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*)
- Introduced panfish: [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*), [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*)

A potential threat to razorback suckers was identified: bow fishermen visiting [[Arrowhead Cove]] during the survey period. With very few carp present in the cove, razorback suckers may become targets for bow fishing during daylight hours when they are visible.

Sampling methods included trammel nets operated continuously for 24 hours and electrofishing. To minimize stress on razorback suckers, nine individuals electrofished on March 12 were held in pens for five hours and released after the evening sampling period. Two of these nine fish had been previously tagged on March 6. All nine fish appeared to swim and function normally after release, suggesting the holding protocol was successful in reducing repeated sampling stress.

## Key Findings

**Species Capture Summary (March 6-12, 1988):**
- **Bonytail Chub:** 1 individual (trammel net) - the only bonytail captured across all camps
- **Razorback Sucker:** 61 individuals (4 trammel net, 57 electrofishing)
- **Carp:** 77 individuals (75 trammel net, 2 electrofishing)
- **Largemouth Bass:** 68 individuals (66 trammel net, 2 electrofishing)
- **Rainbow Trout:** 40 individuals (35 trammel net, 5 electrofishing)
- **Channel Catfish:** 26 individuals (25 trammel net, 1 electrofishing)
- **Bluegill:** 3 individuals (1 trammel net, 2 electrofishing)
- **Striped Bass:** 2 individuals (trammel net only)
- **Green Sunfish:** 3 individuals (trammel net only)
- **Total:** 281 fish captured

**Bonytail Chub Specimen Details:**
- Total length: 444mm
- Weight: 599 grams
- Sex: Female with extremely small but developing ova
- Parasites: *Lernaea* present at base of dorsal, anal, and pelvic fins and under opercular flap
- Condition: Fin erosion on all fins; extremely pale liver; bloody discharge from vent
- Status: Unknown whether wild or stocked individual
- Fate: Died March 12, 1988, at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] after minimal handling (daily antifungal and antibacterial treatments March 8-11)
- Preservation: Frozen for transport to [[Arizona State University]] for otolith aging and examination of bone structure for tetracycline marks

**Conservation Concerns:**
- Bow fishing activity observed in [[Arrowhead Cove]] poses potential threat to razorback suckers, particularly given low carp abundance in the cove
- Razorback suckers may be mistaken for or targeted as alternative to carp during daylight hours

**Operational Success Factors:**
- Interagency cooperation among Fish and Wildlife Service, University of Arizona, Bureau of Land Management, Bureau of Reclamation, and Geological Survey
- Portable radios provided by Bob Hall enhanced safety and efficiency
- Communication support from BLM's Kingman Resource Area Office
- Coordination by Drs. Minckley and Marsh enabled lake-wide sampling effort

**Razorback Sucker Handling Protocol:**
- Nine razorback suckers held for 5 hours after electrofishing to prevent repeated sampling
- All nine fish (including two previously tagged on March 6) swam and functioned normally after release
- Protocol demonstrated successful stress reduction approach
