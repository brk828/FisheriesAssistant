# Turkey Creek Fisheries Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

A fisheries survey of [[Turkey Creek]] and nearby ponds in Cochise County, Arizona, was conducted November 14–15, 1997, by Brian Bagley and Patti Sowka of [[Arizona State University]]. Four fish species were collected. The survey was motivated by a recent report of [[Fathead Minnow]] in [[Lodge Pond]]. The fathead minnow was confirmed there and also found in Turkey Creek, where it is present in low numbers. The document flags the fathead minnow and the [[Green Sunfish]] as threats to the native fish fauna, particularly the [[Yaqui Chub]].

## Background & Description

The survey covered the west side of the [[Chiricahua Mountains]] on [[Forest Service]] land and the [[El Coronado Ranch]] (EC Ranch). Sampling localities included:

- Several reaches of Turkey Creek, including sites 0.4 and 1.0 miles downstream of the end of F.S. road 41, Pole Bridge Canyon (1.2 miles downstream), the boundary between EC Ranch and Forest Service land, and a site 0.5 miles below the Guest House ponds.
- Turkey Pen Creek, 0.5 miles above the road to EC Ranch.
- Ponds on EC Ranch: Lodge Pond, Lilly Pad Pond, a pond adjacent to a horse pasture (100 m below Lodge Pond), upper and lower Guest House Ponds, and [[Big Tank]].

Sampling used a gas-powered [[Smith-Root Backpack Electrofisher]] (DC), dip nets, seines, and minnow traps. Electrofishing effort was recorded in seconds, seine effort in hauls, and minnow traps were set for roughly 24 hours at the pond sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native species:** Yaqui chub and longfin dace were the native fishes collected. Yaqui chub was the most abundant native species in Lodge Pond.
- **Non-native species:** Fathead minnow and green sunfish are non-native. Their presence is the main concern of the survey.
- **Dispersal barriers:** Several falls in Turkey Creek act as barriers to upstream fish movement during most flows. The authors therefore suggest that fathead minnow were likely deliberately stocked near the top of the creek, although the source is unclear.
- **Coexistence evidence:** The document notes that some native fishes can coexist with fathead minnow for long periods. [[Boyce Thompson Arboretum]] has held [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and fathead minnow together for more than a decade. The authors still consider the fathead minnow's presence a cause for concern.
- **Diversion:** Big Tank is fed by water diverted from Turkey Creek. Green sunfish are very abundant there.

## Key Findings

- Four species were collected: Yaqui chub (*Gila purpurea*), longfin dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*), fathead minnow (*Pimephales promelas*), and green sunfish (*Lepomis cyanellus*).
- Fathead minnow was confirmed in Lodge Pond and found near the upper end of permanent water in Turkey Creek, 0.4 miles downstream of the end of F.S. road 41. Numbers in Turkey Creek are low.
- In Lodge Pond, fathead minnow already outnumber Yaqui chub 5:1.
- Green sunfish are locally abundant in Turkey Creek and very abundant in Big Tank. The authors expect a noticeable predatory impact on the native fish fauna.
- The origin of the fathead minnow is unknown. Deliberate stocking is considered likely, given the upstream barriers.
- The document does not give a formal management plan. Its conclusions are that the non-native fathead minnow and green sunfish pose a threat to native fishes and that their spread warrants concern.
