# Palombitrema heteroancistrium n. gen., n. sp.: A New Monogenean from Costa Rican Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #dactylogyridae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 paper by C. E. Price and W. A. Bussing, published in the [[Proceedings of the Helminthological Society of Washington]], formally proposes a new genus and species of monogenean gill parasite, *Palombitrema heteroancistrium*, recovered from the gills of [[Astyanax fasciatus]] (Cuvier) in Costa Rica. The parasite was first suspected to be a deviant species of [[Cleidodiscus]] but was judged sufficiently distinct to warrant its own genus, placed within the Dactylogyridae, subfamily Ancyrocephalinae.

## Background & Description

Before this study, only four freshwater monogenetic trematodes were known from Central America, all from Costa Rican hosts. [[Price 1938]] described two *Cleidodiscus* species from the gills of *Rhamdia rogersi*, and [[Price and Bussing 1967]] described two further *Cleidodiscus* species from *Astyanax fasciatus*, which were the first valid *Cleidodiscus* forms from Central America. The present paper addresses a third, distinct form from the same host.

**Methods:** Host fish were collected in Costa Rica, frozen for several hours, and preserved in 3.5% formalin before shipment. Parasites were treated following [[Price 1966]]. Measurements were taken with a calibrated filar micrometer ocular and illustrations with a camera lucida; values are given in microns as mean followed by minimum and maximum.

**Type Material:** Ten specimens were studied. The holotype is deposited in the USNM Helminthological Collection (No. 62987), Washington, D.C.; the first paratype is in the Museo de Zoologia, Universidad de Costa Rica (No. UCR-1); remaining paratypes are in the authors' collections.

**Species Description:**
- Robust, moderate-sized dactylogyrid with thin cuticle; length 502 (454–529), greatest width 100 (88–105).
- Two pairs of eyespots; posterior pair larger and farther apart.
- Short, stout peduncle setting the haptor off from the body.
- Two pairs of dissimilar anchors: ventral anchor 33 (30–34) long; dorsal anchor 23 (21–25) long with an unusual structure for the subfamily.
- Ventral bar 38 (36–40); dorsal bar unusually V-shaped with poorly developed sclerotized discs, 36 (34–37).
- Seven pairs of hooks: five ventral pairs similar to one another, and two dorsal pairs (6 and 7) that are markedly diverse. Pair 7 is the largest at 38 (37–40).
- Cirrus with an estimated length of 42; accessory piece in two joined portions, overall length 27 (25–30).
- Heavily sclerotized, irregularly shaped vagina opening in the left body half.
- Vitellaria granular, forming lateral bands in two specimens and irregular patches in the rest.

**Etymology:** The genus honors Professor Arturo Palombi of Napoli, Italy, for his work on European Monogenea. The species name derives from the Greek *hetero-* ("different") and *ancistr-* ("hook" or "spine"), reflecting the variation in hook morphology and size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The parasite occupies the gill filaments of its host. The source gives host and locality data for a single site: Rio Montenegro, Guanacaste Province, about 23 km northwest of Cañas along the Interamerican Highway. The paper focuses on taxonomy and does not report population density, prevalence, seasonality or water chemistry. The host [[Astyanax fasciatus]] is a freshwater characin, and the related host *Rhamdia rogersi* was used in earlier Costa Rican monogenean work.

## Key Findings

- *Palombitrema* is established as a new genus within Dactylogyridae, Ancyrocephalinae, with *P. heteroancistrium* as the type species.
- The genus is closely related to [[Cleidodiscus]] (shared ventral bar and ventral hooks 1–5) but differs in three key features:
  1. The accessory piece is two-piece, a feature not found in any *Cleidodiscus* species.
  2. The dorsal hooks are strongly different in shape and size, whereas *Cleidodiscus* dorsal hooks are similar.
  3. The large, heavily sclerotized vagina is absent from all known *Cleidodiscus* species.
- The dorsal anchor and dorsal bar are unusual for the Ancyrocephalinae.
- The study adds to the small known Central American monogenean fauna and supports the view that the two genera are apparently related but distinct.
