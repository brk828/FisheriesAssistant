## Executive Summary

This document is a **draft policy letter**, not a primary scientific study, from the Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team (the "Team") to the Regional Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 6). The letter addresses the Team's review of a draft **Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP)** for endangered fishes of the Colorado River Basin. Rather than presenting morphological or ecological data, the text conveys the Team's advisory concerns regarding the pace and structure of recovery efforts for basin-wide endangered fish species, with specific reference to the [[Razorback Sucker]] recovery plan. Key concerns include continued water depletion in the Upper Colorado River Basin, absence of measurable positive population responses, and the need for a more rigorous, stepwise recovery framework linking specific actions to population-level outcomes.

## Key Findings

- The Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team convened April 12–13, 1993, to discuss **proposed critical habitat**, the **[[Razorback Sucker]] recovery plan**, and the **draft RIPRAP**.
- The Team's advisory role is to inform the Regional Director on basin-wide issues affecting **endangered fish recovery** throughout the Colorado River Basin.
- Despite implementation of recovery plans and the Upper Basin RIPRAP, the Team found **no significant positive species population responses**, alongside continued **net water depletion** in the Upper Basin.
- The Team **supports consolidated recovery planning documents** (like the RIPRAP) and recommends developing an analogous plan for the **Lower Colorado River Basin**.
- A major critique: the draft RIPRAP **lacks a clear nexus between specific recovery actions and measurable population responses**.
- The Team recommends a **step-down approach**, wherein defined goals and milestones must be achieved before progressing to subsequent recovery elements.
- The Team asserts that RIPRAP elements are **jointly essential**—partial implementation of selected elements is expected to be insufficient to achieve full recovery effect.
- The RIPRAP should serve as a **working framework**, not as the sole determinant of "sufficient progress" or "reasonable and prudent alternatives" under regulatory review; such determinations should be grounded in actual **population response data**.
- The RIPRAP originated from consensus-based "**road maps**" developed by basin biologists identifying priority recovery elements, developed independently of formal legal/regulatory sufficiency measures.
