# Fisheries Survey of the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests: Blue River and San Francisco River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This trip report documents a fisheries survey of the lower [[Blue River]] and portions of the [[San Francisco River]] within the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, Arizona, conducted 6–10 July 1995. Using electrofishing, dip nets, and seining, researchers from Arizona State University and the U.S. Forest Service sampled multiple localities to assess distribution, abundance, and catch-per-unit-effort (CPUE) of native and non-native fish species. Ten species were documented, including seven native [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]] (rainbow trout, longfin dace, speckled dace, loach minnow, Sonora sucker, desert sucker) and non-native taxa (red shiner, flathead catfish, channel catfish, common carp). Notably, native cyprinids and catostomids dominated riffle/run habitats, while non-native carp and catfish were confined to deep, difficult-to-sample pools on the San Francisco River. The survey highlights significant sampling limitations in deep-pool habitats and recommends more intensive gear (e.g., gill nets) and management action regarding non-native species.

## Background & Description

While this report is primarily a distributional/abundance survey rather than a morphometric study, several qualitative and size-based observations were recorded:

- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*): Only a single individual captured, at Cienaga Creek; likely a stocked or transient individual given the marginal warm-water habitat.
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*): Most abundant and widely distributed species; most collected fish across sites "appeared to be juveniles."
- **[[Speckled Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys osculus*): Present in moderate numbers at nearly all major localities.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*): Less abundant than other cyprinids but consistently present at all large sampling localities; a benthic riffle specialist.
- **[[Sonora Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*) and **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Pantosteus clarki*): Recorded in relatively high numbers (tens of individuals per site), largest size classes noted at Cienaga Creek locality.
- **[[Flathead Catfish]]** (*Pylodictis olivaris*): Captured individuals weighed approximately 2–3 lbs.
- **[[Common Carp]]** (*Cyprinus carpio*): Larger individuals observed/captured near the Blue-San Francisco confluence, estimated at 8–10 lbs (captured via arrow fishing by local individuals, not survey gear); additional carp visually confirmed in deep pools at the Forest Service boundary but not captured.
- **[[Red Shiner]]** (*Cyprinella lutrensis*): Non-native, found in low numbers (1–2 individuals) at multiple San Francisco River sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat structure:** Both rivers exhibited a mosaic of riffle-run-pool sequences with varying substrate composition:
- **Riffles:** boulder-to-cobble substrate, primary habitat for [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Speckled Dace]].
- **Runs:** cobble-to-gravel substrate.
- **Pools:** sand-to-silt substrate; served as refugia for larger suckers, and critical habitat for non-native predatory/invasive species (carp, catfish).

**River characteristics:**
- The **Blue River** was narrower, shallower (~0.2 m in runs, 0.2–0.75 m in riffles), flanked by steep canyon walls (~200 m).
- The **San Francisco River** was wider (~20 m average) and deeper (0.2–3.0 m in runs; up to 5.0 m in pools), with a canyon up to half a mile wide at points.

**Species-habitat associations:**
- [[Rainbow Trout]] restricted to the single coolest-water locality sampled (Cienaga Creek confluence with Blue River), alongside the highest recorded abundance of Sonora sucker — suggesting this reach offers relatively higher-quality, cooler perennial flow.
- [[Longfin Dace]] were habitat generalists, abundant at nearly all sites except one isolated, likely spring-fed pool disjunct from the San Francisco River.
- [[Common Carp]], [[Flathead Catfish]], and [[Channel Catfish]] were restricted to deep pools on the San Francisco River — habitats poorly suited to electrofishing/seining, resulting in likely undersampling of these non-native populations.
- An isolated, possibly spring-fed pool (~20 m x 3 m) disjunct from the San Francisco River supported only non-native flathead catfish, channel catfish, and common carp — no native species were found there, suggesting either founder effects from isolation or exclusion/predation dynamics.
- **[[White Rock Spring]]**, a small nameless tributary to the San Francisco River, held only [[Longfin Dace]] in isolated pools, representing a habitat with the sole perennial water in that canyon reach.
- Dry, ephemeral tributaries (e.g., **Pat Creek**) lacked perennial water and supported no fish, though possible seasonal barriers exist in box canyon sections during runoff.

## Key Findings

- **Ten fish species** documented across the lower Blue River and San Francisco River to the Forest Service boundary: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Common Carp]].
- **Native species dominance** in riffle/run habitats contrasts with **non-native dominance** in deep pool habitats, indicating a habitat-mediated segregation between native desert fishes and invasive species.
- **Rainbow trout** presence was highly restricted (single locality, single individual), suggesting this species is either a marginal stocked population or transient rather than an established reproducing population in this reach.
- **Sampling gear limitations**: Electrofishing and small seine nets were largely ineffective in deep pools (>1 m), which likely biased results toward underrepresenting non-native carp/catfish populations and any large-bodied native suckers using pool refugia.
- **Juvenile skew**: Most fish collected across the survey "appeared to be juveniles," possibly reflecting a summer recruitment pulse or sampling bias toward shallow nursery habitats.
- **Human harvest observed**: Local individuals were seen arrow-fishing for carp near the Blue-San Francisco confluence, indicating an existing informal harvest pressure on non-native carp populations.
- **Management recommendations**:
  1. A more intensive follow-up survey is needed, specifically targeting deep pools and major habitats missed by this trip's methods.
  2. Deep pools on the San Francisco River housing carp and flathead catfish should be sampled with gill nets, and management action should be considered regarding these established non-native populations, given their potential ecological impact on native [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]] such as [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Desert Sucker]].
- **Barriers/connectivity**: Several tributaries (e.g., Pat Creek) showed no perennial water and only potential seasonal flow barriers in box canyon sections, limiting fish access from the mainstem during dry periods.
