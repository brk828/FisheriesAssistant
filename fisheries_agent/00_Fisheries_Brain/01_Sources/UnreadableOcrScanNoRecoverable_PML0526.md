# Induced Spawning of Tilapia aurea with Gonadotrophins

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This preliminary report tests whether exogenous gonadotrophins can induce out-of-season spawning in mature female [[Tilapia Aurea]] held at the [[Dor Fish Culture Research Station]], Israel. Seven experimental series compared [[Carp Pituitary]] (CP), [[Luteinizing Hormone]] (LH), [[Human Chorionic Gonadotrophin]] (HCG), and the combinations CP + HCG and CP + LH. The author, Stephen Dadzie, reports that HCG alone was the most effective and consistent single agent, and that CP + HCG gave the best results overall. CP + LH produced no spawning and caused fighting-related injuries. The author concludes that the intact pituitaries of the test fish may have confounded the assessment of LH and CP.

## Background & Description

Tilapia are valued as a source of cheap animal protein and as a way to use the productive potential of freshwater. Two problems limit their culture: uncontrolled spawning, which slows growth, and the need for pure, unhybridized stocks. Hybridization is one approach to producing fast-growing [[Monosex Tilapia Culture]], but it requires two species to be kept apart until they mate. The author proposes that induced spawning with exogenous gonadotrophins could shorten the waiting period before mating.

The study builds on earlier induced-spawning work in Brazil, Russia, India, and at the Dor station. Figures in the original show silver carp larvae, which are not part of the tilapia experiments and are omitted here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Source fish:** Mature male and female T. aurea were raised in ponds at the Dor station.
- **Holding conditions:** Selected fish were moved to tanks at 25–30 °C and fed ad libitum on commercially prepared pellets.
- **Acclimation:** One to two days of acclimation preceded injection.
- **Hormone sources:** Carp pituitaries were alcohol-preserved and collected from pond-raised carp at the station. Their potencies had been predetermined in an earlier study (Yashouv et al., 1968). Pituitaries collected in May were the most potent and were used in most cases. Those collected in September were less potent, and dosage was adjusted for them.
- **Injection protocol:** The first injection (one-third of the total dose) was given on the second acclimation day. The second (the remaining two-thirds) followed 6–7 hours later. Later injections were given at 24-hour intervals.
- **Controls:** Control fish received 0.25 ml of 0.75% saline in the same proportions as the treated fish.

## Key Findings

**Experimental series (summary of the Results section)**

| Series | Treatment | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 and 5 | Carp pituitary (CP) | Males showed secondary sexual characters. Territorial behaviour and spawning occurred only in Series 1 (May pituitaries). Series 5 (September pituitaries) did not produce spawning. Controls did not spawn. |
| 2 | Luteinizing hormone (LH) | Two of three females spawned, on days 14 and 21 after the last injection. The third had immature oocytes at termination (day 46), when a control fish spawned. |
| 3 | Human chorionic gonadotrophin (HCG) | Secondary sexual characters and spawning behaviour appeared soon after the first injection. All three females spawned after the third injection. Controls did not spawn. |
| 4 | CP + HCG | All females spawned on day 3 (four injections). |
| 6 | CP + HCG | All females spawned after the first day (two injections). Controls did not spawn in either CP + HCG series. |
| 7 | CP + LH | No spawning after seven days of injection. Apparent aggression proved to be fighting among fish of both sexes, causing serious skin injuries, and the experiment was abandoned. |

**Main conclusions**

1. **HCG** was the most effective and consistent single hormone for inducing spawning in T. aurea, and every treated female spawned.
2. **CP + HCG** gave the best combined results, even when the carp pituitaries came from September collections of lower potency. This matches a synergistic effect of carp pituitary and pregnancy urine reported for out-of-season carp spawning (Yashouv & Berner-Samsonov, 1969).
3. **LH and CP alone** acted slowly, which made their effects hard to judge.
4. **Confounding factor:** Because the test fish retained their own intact pituitaries, the spawning seen after LH or CP treatment may have been driven partly by the fish's own gonadotrophins. The author states that this makes it difficult to assess the effectiveness of LH and CP.
5. **Combined CP + LH** produced no spawning and caused aggression and injury, so it was not a usable protocol.
