# Biological Opinion on Effects to Loach Minnow and Apache Trout from Campbell and Isabelle Timber Sales

#ichthyology #endangered-species #cyprinidae #salmonidae #habitat-assessment #forest-management

## Executive Summary

This biological opinion, issued May 7, 1993, by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] evaluates the effects of the [[Campbell Timber Sale]] and [[Isabelle Timber Sale]] on threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*) in the upper [[Campbell Blue Creek]] basin, [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]], Arizona. The proposed action involves harvest of 8.95 million board feet of timber using tractor logging, road construction and closure, reseeding, sediment tank construction, fuel treatment, prescribed burning, and mechanical scarification. The opinion concludes non-jeopardy for both species but anticipates incidental take through direct mortality and habitat modification from increased sedimentation, altered stream flows, and prescribed burning effects. Mandatory terms and conditions include 132-foot buffer zones on watercourses, limited stream crossings, daily fish mortality surveys, and extensive monitoring of fish populations, turbidity, stream morphology, and flow regimes for five years post-harvest.

## Background & Description

### Loach Minnow

[[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was listed as threatened on October 28, 1986. Critical habitat was proposed in June 1985 for portions of multiple rivers including [[Campbell Blue Creek]] from [[Coleman Creek]] confluence downstream to the [[Blue River]]. The species' range has been reduced by more than 85 percent due to competition and predation by non-native fish and habitat destruction. Only five populations remain in Arizona and New Mexico. The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Albuquerque Regional Office and [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] have recommended uplisting to endangered status due to deteriorating conditions throughout its range.

[[Loach Minnow]] are bottom-dwelling inhabitants of shallow, swift water over sand, gravel, cobble, and rubble substrates. They utilize interstitial spaces between and in the lee of larger substrate for resting and spawning. The species is rare or absent from habitats where fine sediments fill interstitial spaces. Some studies indicate filamentous algae may be an important habitat component. Life span ranges from 1.5 to 3 years, with most surviving about 2 years. They feed exclusively on aquatic insects. Spawning occurs primarily March through May, with recent reports confirming autumn spawning under certain circumstances. Eggs are attached to the underside of rocks forming cavity roofs on the downstream side; limited data suggest males may guard nests during incubation.

Distribution in [[Campbell Blue Creek]] extends from near [[Cat Creek]] downstream to the mouth, based on records from 1978-1992.

### Apache Trout

[[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*) is a medium-sized interior Western trout with yellow to yellowish-olive ground color, small dark spots scattered over dorsal and lateral surfaces, and conspicuous cream or pale yellowish fin tips. The species was listed as endangered in 1967, then downlisted to threatened on July 16, 1975, following successful conservation efforts. A special rule allows state-regulated recreational take.

Native to headwaters of the [[White River]], [[Black River]], and [[Little Colorado River]] in Arizona's [[White Mountains]], the species once occupied an estimated 600 stream miles but was reduced to fewer than 30 stream miles of genetically pure populations by 1983. Introduction of non-native [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*), and [[Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*) caused competition for space and hybridization, particularly genetic swamping by rainbow trout. The 1983 recovery plan established a delisting goal of 30 pure strain, self-sustaining populations on [[U.S. Forest Service]] and [[White Mountain Apache Reservation]] lands.

[[Apache Trout]] are not found on or downstream from the [[Campbell Timber Sale]] area. They occur in [[Coleman Creek]] on the northeastern boundary of the [[Isabelle Timber Sale]], originating from a 1981 stocking. Despite 1.9 miles of stream reach in unsatisfactory riparian/aquatic condition, [[Coleman Creek]] supports a small self-sustaining population considered important to recovery efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Watershed Conditions

The [[Campbell Blue Creek]] watershed has been subjected to numerous timber sales, livestock grazing, roads, gravel and mineral mining, and other activities. Turbidity and sedimentation are relatively high: Forest Service data show average turbidity of 17.4 nephelometric turbidity units (NTU) in high flow event grab samples from 1980-1985 and 19.8 NTU in automatic sampling during 1984-85. The Arizona state standard for maximum turbidity in cold water aquatic systems is 10 NTU.

Riparian vegetation along upper [[Campbell Blue Creek]] and [[Cienega Creek]] is in poor condition. Stream channel downcutting has occurred and the watertable has dropped 8 to 10 feet. Existing roads within the [[Campbell Timber Sale]] area are in poor condition with severe erosion causing increased runoff and sedimentation. Except for streams and roads, watershed conditions in the upper basin are generally satisfactory, but lower portions show poor conditions that may contribute to sediment loads.

The upper 1.9 miles of [[Coleman Creek]] are in unsatisfactory condition for riparian and aquatic values. Portions have recently been fenced to exclude livestock grazing. Streambank erosion and sediment inflow from the watershed are concerns.

### Project Description

The [[Campbell Timber Sale]] covers approximately 5,416 acres with harvest on 2,793 acres (52 percent), yielding approximately 3.298 million board feet (MMBF) of sawtimber and 700 cords of pulpwood. Harvest is expected in 1994 using tractor logging with shelterwood seed cuts, group shelterwood seed cuts, intermediate cuts, and sanitation salvage cuts. Buffer zones of 132 feet where no cutting is allowed would be maintained on all creeks and drainages.

Road activities include 0.22 miles of new construction, 13.42 miles of existing roads used and kept open, 13.49 miles used then closed, and 25 miles closed using [[Knutson-Vandenberg Act]] (KV) funds. All closed roads and skid trails would be reseeded. Small roadside sediment tanks would be constructed.

Fuel treatment would increase average fuel loading from 12.8 to 14.3 tons per acre, including 51 acres hand piling, 51 acres machine piling, 202 acres slash pile burning, 1,138 acres slash hazard broadcast burning, 542 acres lopping slash to 2 feet, 599 acres regeneration broadcast burning, and 1,527 acres forage improvement broadcast burning using KV funds. All prescribed burning would occur October-November with 132-foot unburned buffer zones on drainage bottoms.

The [[Isabelle Timber Sale]] covers approximately 7,796 acres with harvest on 1,995 acres (26 percent), yielding approximately 5.66 MMBF of sawtimber using tractor logging. Activities include 1 mile of new road (closed after sale), 22.3 miles of existing roads reconstructed and maintained, 110 acres mechanical scarification, and 553 acres prescribed burning. No buffer zones along drainages are specified, though no cutting units are immediately adjacent to [[Coleman Creek]] or [[Buckalou Creek]]. Cutting units do abut [[Campbell Blue Creek]] and [[Castle Creek]].

### Monitoring Proposed

For the [[Campbell Timber Sale]]: continuation of flow gauge on [[Campbell Blue Creek]], annual surveys of [[Loach Minnow]] distribution and habitat conditions for five years, pre- and post-harvest macro-invertebrate sampling at six sites, conducted in cooperation with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

For the [[Isabelle Timber Sale]]: no monitoring of stream flow, sediment, wildlife, fish, or riparian vegetation is proposed.

## Key Findings

### Effects Analysis

**Direct and Indirect Effects:**

1. **Sedimentation**: Both timber sales would add to already elevated turbidity and sediment loads in [[Campbell Blue Creek]]. Because [[Loach Minnow]] is a bottom-dwelling species using interstitial spaces of stream substrate, it is vulnerable to sediment deposition. The 132-foot buffer zone in the Campbell sale would partially alleviate this effect. Disturbed area on Campbell sale is estimated to increase from 4.02 percent to 10.25 percent (6.23 percent increase).

2. **Altered Flow Regimes**: Increases in peak flow would result in channel morphology alterations and increased channel erosion, potentially altering availability and distribution of [[Loach Minnow]] habitat. Increases result from decreased water retention in soils and increased overland flows. Water yield increases predicted at 4.5 percent for Campbell sale with 6.1 percent increase in peak flows; cumulative increases from Campbell, Isabelle and 4 other sales predicted at 2.9 percent water yield and 4.1 percent peak flow. These are substantially lower than nearby studies showing 29-110 percent increases, apparently due to small harvest volume per acre.

3. **Reduced Base Flows**: Corresponding decreases in base flows and low summer flows would occur. Low flows in [[Campbell Blue Creek]] may be a limiting factor on the [[Loach Minnow]] population; even small decreases may have serious adverse effects. No data available on expected low flow decreases.

4. **Prescribed Burning Effects**: Broadcast and prescribed burning would accentuate increases in peak flows, decreases in base flows, and increases in sediment. Runoff and ash deposition may result in mechanical damage or toxicity to fish. A 1989 wildfire in New Mexico caused a major fish kill of endangered [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*).

5. **Apache Trout Effects**: Effects would occur through same mechanisms as for [[Loach Minnow]]. Sediment fills resting, hiding, and spawning areas. Increased peak flows cause habitat damage; decreased low flows reduce habitat availability and may cause direct mortalities. Steep streamside topography on [[Coleman Creek]] prevents harvest or burning immediately adjacent, likely ameliorating most adverse effects from Isabelle sale.

### Biological Opinion Conclusion

**Non-jeopardy** for both [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Apache Trout]]. The proposed actions are not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of either species and are not likely to destroy or adversely modify proposed critical habitat of [[Loach Minnow]].

### Incidental Take Statement

**Level of Take**: Anticipated incidental take would be deemed exceeded if:

1. More than 10 dead fish (any species) found in [[Campbell Blue Creek]] or [[Coleman Creek]] within or downstream from sale area during logging, post-sale, or fuel treatment activities
2. Turbidity, sediment, water yield, or peak flow increases exceed predicted levels by greater than 15 percent
3. Stream channel downcutting and bank erosion increases over pre-harvest levels

If anticipated take is exceeded, consultation must be reinitiated immediately.

### Reasonable and Prudent Measures (Mandatory)

Four objectives for minimizing incidental take:

1. Conduct all actions to minimize direct mortalities of [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Apache Trout]]
2. Conduct all actions to minimize modification and loss of habitat
3. Maintain complete and accurate records of actions that may result in take
4. Monitor species and habitats to document incidental take levels

### Terms and Conditions (Mandatory Requirements)

1. **Stream Crossings**: Mechanized vehicles within channels of [[Campbell Blue Creek]], [[Coleman Creek]], [[Cienega Creek]], [[Buckalou Creek]], and [[Castle Creek]] only at designated crossings. Maximum seven total crossings, no more than three on [[Campbell Blue Creek]] (excluding Highway 666). Crossing width limited to minimum necessary.

2. **Buffer Zones**: All perennial, intermittent, and ephemeral drainages (all watercourses on USGS topographic maps) within sale areas considered watercourses. No timber harvest, vehicle use, post-harvest, or fuel treatment within 132 feet buffer on either side (measured from channel center). Only exception: designated crossings.

3. **Pollutant Prevention**: Activities conducted to ensure no pollutants (excluding sediment) enter any watercourse in [[Campbell Blue Creek]] drainage.

4. **Daily Fish Surveys**: During all activities, downstream areas of [[Coleman Creek]] and [[Campbell Blue Creek]] surveyed for dead or dying fish. Daily checks if work occurring and surface flow present within 0.5 miles of activity.

5. **Written Records**: Maintained for harvest and post-harvest activities, documenting differences between planned and actual activities. Reports submitted to Service within 90 days of completion of harvest, within 90 days of burn activities, and as yearly summary.

6. **Fish and Habitat Monitoring**: Conducted before and after commencement of on-ground activities. Habitat monitoring includes stream channel morphology, substrate embeddedness, riparian vegetation. Conducted once prior to sale, once following completion of all activities, and minimum once every two years during activities. Fish monitoring includes presence and distribution of [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Apache Trout]], with age structure data for [[Apache Trout]]. Minimum twice yearly during activities, then once yearly for minimum five years post-activities. Protocol mutually acceptable to Forest Service, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and Service. Results furnished within 90 days after each monitoring effort.

7. **Flow Gauge Operation**: Continued operation of flow gauges on [[Campbell Blue Creek]] and [[Castle Creek]] through life of fish monitoring. Data furnished every three months during sale activities, yearly thereafter. Service notified by telephone immediately if data indicate incidental take levels exceeded.

8. **Turbidity and Sediment Monitoring**: Turbidity levels monitored in [[Campbell Blue Creek]] as close to downstream end of Campbell sale area as reasonably possible. Sediment levels may be inferred from turbidity data. Channel shape, size, and stability monitored to detect increased downcutting or bank erosion. Monitoring continues through life of fish monitoring. Reports every three months during sale activities, yearly thereafter. Service notified by telephone immediately if data indicate incidental take levels exceeded. (Note: Sediment and turbidity monitoring not required for Isabelle sale due to lack of appropriate baseline and future stream-flow data.)

### Conservation Recommendations (Discretionary)

The Service supports use of KV funds to restore and enhance riparian and instream habitat in upper [[Campbell Blue Creek]]. Recommends that fencing, instream structures, and other erosion control and watershed restoration actions be cooperatively planned by Forest Service, [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], Service, and appropriate academic specialists. These activities were not included in this biological opinion and will likely require additional section 7 consultation.
