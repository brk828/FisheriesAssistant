# Growth and Survival of Larval Razorback Suckers Fed Five Formulated Diets

## Executive Summary
This study (Tyus & Severson 1990) evaluated the growth and survival of larval [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered [[Catostomidae|catostomid]] endemic to the [[Colorado River Basin]], when reared on five commercially available formulated dry diets as first foods. Sac fry were reared from 5 to 45 days posthatch under controlled hatchery conditions at the USFWS experimental hatchery in Ouray, Utah. Diets **LIV** and **B-250** produced the highest survival (78%) but lowest weight gain, while diets **A-250** and **4200** produced the highest weight gain but lowest survival (32% and 20%, respectively). Diet **AP-100** was intermediate in both metrics. Final total lengths (11–27 mm) and growth rates were comparable to larvae reared on live [[Brine Shrimp|Artemia salina]] nauplii under limited-ration conditions. The authors recommend LIV or B-250 as first foods for hatchery propagation of this critically declining species, supporting recovery efforts under the multi-agency [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]].

## Background & Description
- **Species:** *Xyrauchen texanus*, a monotypic genus within [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), endemic to the Colorado River system.
- **Developmental stage examined:** Sac fry to 45-day-old larvae.
- **Hatching:** Eggs began hatching 112 h postfertilization at 20°C; all fry emerged by 145 h.
- **Initial size:** Mean initial fish weight ≈ 0.005 g (5 d posthatch).
- **Final size (45 d posthatch):**
  - Total length range: 11–27 mm TL (overall average 18.3 mm TL)
  - Mean weight range across diets: 0.026–0.092 g
- **Diet-specific outcomes (mean weight, TL average, survival):**
  | Diet | Mean Weight (g) | Avg. TL (mm) | TL Range (mm) | Survival (%) |
  |------|------------------|--------------|----------------|----------------|
  | AP-100 | 0.036 | 16.5 | 13–23 | 59.2 |
  | A-250 | 0.092 | 22.5 | 18–27 | 34.7 |
  | 4200 | 0.066 | 19.5 | 16–23 | 20.0 |
  | LIV | 0.026 | 15.2 | 11–20 | 78.3 |
  | B-250 | 0.031 | 17.9 | 13–25 | 78.3 |
- Comparable wild/live-food-reared larvae (Papoulias 1988, fed *Artemia salina*) reached 11.8–28.8 mm TL (average 18.8 mm TL) over 50 d, consistent with the ranges observed here.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native habitat:** Upper [[Colorado River Basin]], primarily the Green River near Jensen, Utah; the species is a river-dwelling catostomid facing severe recruitment failure in the wild.
- **Conservation status:** Not federally listed under the Endangered Species Act at time of writing, but a candidate species and protected under state statutes; wild adult population estimated at only ~1,000 individuals (Lanigan & Tyus 1989).
- **Natural larval diet:** In rearing ponds, larvae first consume organisms <0.1 mm in width (rotifers, chironomids); larger fry (>18 mm TL) consume organisms up to 0.3 mm in width (Papoulias 1988).
- **Rearing environment (this study):**
  - Water source: heated, degassed, aerated, single-pass well water.
  - Sac fry tank: 21°C, DO 5.4 mg/L, pH 7.7, hardness 440 mg/L, flow 18.95 L/min.
  - Rearing compartments: 20.3°C, DO 6.1 mg/L, pH 7.7, hardness 440 mg/L, flow 1.90 L/min.
  - Fish held in 2.5-L breeder baskets (10-µm mesh) within 29.1-L compartments, completely randomized design, triplicate lots of 40 fish per diet.
- **Feeding regime:** Hand-fed at initial 5% body weight/day, divided into 6–8 feedings/day; ration declined to 0.9–2% body weight/day by day 40, suggesting a food-limited (non-ad libitum) feeding regime relative to optimal live-food studies.
- **Comparative rearing context:** Performance benchmarked against pond-based propagation (Dexter National Fish Hatchery, New Mexico) using natural plankton foods (Hamman 1987), and against live-food (*Artemia*) laboratory trials (Papoulias 1988).

## Key Findings
- **Survival-growth trade-off:** A clear inverse relationship existed between weight gain and survival across diets — diets yielding the fastest growth (A-250, 4200) had the lowest survival, while diets yielding the highest survival (LIV, B-250) had the slowest growth.
- **Best performing diets:** [[LIV Diet]] and [[B-250 Diet]] achieved 78% survival, recommended as optimal first foods despite lower growth.
- **Density effects:** No significant effect of fish density (survival) on weight gain *within* diets (ANCOVA, P = 0.36), but a weak inverse relationship when all diets were pooled (r = –0.67, P < 0.01).
- **Proximate composition:**
  - Protein content was similar across diets (49.8–57.0%).
  - Fat content varied substantially (6.4–30.2%) and did not by itself predict survival or growth outcomes — LIV (highest fat, 30.2%) and B-250 (lower fat, 14.6%) both produced the best survival, while A-250 (20.1% fat) and 4200 (6.4% fat) both produced high growth/low survival.
- **Particle size:** Diet particle size alone did not explain differences in growth or survival (regression: r = –0.36, P = 0.19 for survival; r = 0.44, P = 0.10 for growth). LIV had very fine, flour-like particles (avg. 50 µm, 100% <103 µm) while B-250 had coarser particles (avg. 240 µm, 87% >153 µm) — yet both yielded equally high survival, indicating factors beyond dry particle size (e.g., palatability, wet particle behavior, nutritional composition) are important.
- **Growth comparability to live food:** Growth under the limited formulated-diet ration was comparable to that achieved with live *Artemia* nauplii in other laboratory trials, suggesting dry diets are a viable, cost-effective alternative to pond-based natural food rearing.
- **Management implication:** Findings support intensive hatchery culture of razorback sucker larvae using commercial dry diets as a lower-cost alternative to natural pond rearing, aiding recovery efforts for this endangered [[Colorado River Basin]] endemic.
- **Research gaps identified:** Authors recommend further study of food palatability, wet (versus dry) particle size, and diet-specific nutritional requirements to optimize both growth and survival simultaneously.
