# Repatriation of Native Fishes to Fossil Creek: Monitoring and Stocking, 2010

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) report summarizes the 2010 monitoring and stocking actions for the second phase of the [[Fossil Creek]] [[Repatriation Program]]. Five native fishes were stocked there from 2007 to 2009. In 2010, three species were stocked on October 27 (Gila topminnow, spikedace and loach minnow), and surveys targeted all five. No [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] were found during 2010 surveys. Only [[Longfin Dace]] showed clear signs of persistence and reproduction. Whether the other repatriated species have established or persist remains unresolved. The authors attribute the poor results to several possible causes, including the 22 January 2010 flood, and recommend that the [[Fossil Creek Native Fish Working Group]] review results after the 2011 season.

## Background & Description

The repatriation program aims to create self-sustaining populations of threatened and endangered (T&E) species in Fossil Creek. The exception is razorback sucker, for which only rearing habitat is expected; juveniles are expected to disperse downstream to the [[Verde River]]. Post-stocking monitoring has four objectives:

1. Verify persistence since stocking.
2. Detect recruitment of young fish.
3. Track relative abundance over years.
4. Determine whether fish have dispersed beyond stocking locations.

Stocking and monitoring are guided by an annual implementation plan. That plan is based on the long-term monitoring plan, the original [[Repatriation Plan]] (Clarkson et al. 2007) and prior monitoring results.

**Study site:** Fossil Creek flows south to southwest from the Mogollon Rim to the Verde River. Perennial flow begins at [[Fossil Springs]] and runs about 23 km to the confluence. A [[Fish Barrier]], funded by the Bureau of Reclamation, was built about 7 km upstream of the Verde River in 2004. Following that barrier, nonnative fish were removed with [[Antimycin Treatment]] and native fish were returned. The stream is divided into five reaches:

| Reach | Extent (approximate length) |
|---|---|
| 0 | Fossil Springs to Fossil Springs diversion dam (~0.6 km); added in this report |
| 1 | Diversion dam ("sunfish barrier") to High Falls (~3.7 km) |
| 2 | High Falls to Irving waterfall (~2.0 km) |
| 3 | Irving waterfall to Sally May Wash confluence (~5.0 km) |
| 4 | Sally May Wash confluence to fish barrier (~4.5 km) |

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods, in brief:**
- **Spikedace and loach minnow:** Snorkel surveys on 16–17 August 2010 covered Reaches 0 and 1. A single snorkeler made one upstream pass of 25 m at 100-m segments, and all habitat types were sampled.
- **Gila topminnow:** [[Minnow Trapping]] (7 to 11 traps per reach, set for at least two hours) was combined with [[Seine Sampling]] and dip netting, from August to September 2010. The TM-4 stocking site was not surveyed because it had dewatered in 2008.
- **Razorback sucker:** Snorkeling of all deep (>1.0 m) pools and runs in Reaches 3 and 4 was conducted on 29–30 June 2010.
- **Longfin dace:** AGFD did not survey for this species in 2010. Data came from [[Marsh and Associates]] and AGFD Region I.
- **Non-target taxa:** Chub spp. (roundtail and headwater chub were not distinguished), [[Crayfish]] (*Orconectes virilis*) and [[Sonora Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense*) were recorded where observed.

**Stocking protocol:** Fish were transported from the [[Bubbling Ponds Native Fish Conservation Facility]] in 55-gal drums (or 100-qt coolers for Reach 3 topminnow), with aeration, salt at 3–5 ppt and an ammonia detoxifier. At the site, buckets were tempered to within 1.5°C of stream temperature by gradual water exchanges. Fish were then released individually. Transport was by helicopter or truck.

**Environmental conditions:** Flood events strongly affected the stream. The 22 January 2010 flood likely exceeded 11,000 cubic feet per second (cfs), with debris marks about 5 m above the water level. Earlier floods in December 2007 and January 2008 also occurred. The January 2008 flood likely exceeded 10,000 cfs. Fossil Creek's gradient is also considered: its large-scale gradient may be steeper than that of historical habitats of the T&E species, which would mean higher current velocities.

## Key Findings

**Monitoring results (2010):**
- **Spikedace and loach minnow (16–17 August):** Snorkel surveys covered about 25% of the 4.3 km of Reaches 0 and 1 (reported as 1,213 m). No spikedace or loach minnow were observed.
- **Other fishes at those sites:** Speckled dace were the most prevalent taxa. Headwater chub, desert sucker and Sonora sucker followed. Headwater chub and desert sucker appeared slightly more common in Reach 0 than in Reach 1. Table 2 lists site counts for each species.
- **Gila topminnow (August–September):** No Gila topminnow were captured or observed. Table 3 summarizes trap and seine catches, including chub, desert and Sonora sucker, and speckled dace.
- **Razorback sucker (29–30 June):** 109 runs and pools were snorkeled, covering about 43% (4,039 m) of the 9,500 m of Reaches 3 and 4. Snorkel time totaled 15.25 h. No razorback sucker were observed. Chub were the most prevalent taxon, followed by desert sucker, longfin dace, Sonora sucker and speckled dace. Speckled dace, chubs and suckers were more prevalent in Reach 3, while longfin dace were slightly more prevalent in Reach 4.
- **Longfin dace:** Marsh and Associates captured 276 longfin dace in one trap in 2010. Longfin dace were also observed in many sites during razorback sucker snorkel surveys.

**2010 stocking (all on 27 October):**

| Species | Stocked | Mortality during transport and tempering | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gila topminnow | ~6,500 (2,099 in Reach 1; 4,420 in Reach 3) | 40 of 2,139 (Reach 1); 10 of 4,430 (Reach 3) | Reaches 1 and 3 |
| Spikedace | Two stocking sites (2,502 transported) | 55 of 2,502 | Two sites in Reaches 0–1 |
| Loach minnow | Two stocking sites (777 transported) | 3 of 777 | Two sites in Reaches 0–1 |

- Longfin dace were not stocked. Over 100 were captured during 2010 monitoring, which suggested the population was increasing on its own.
- Fish behaved normally upon release for all three species.

**Interpretation and persistence assessment:**
- **Longfin dace:** Monitoring from 2008 to 2010 indicates that the species is persisting and has likely established a population in Fossil Creek.
- **Gila topminnow:** In 2009, one fish was captured in May and nearly 500 in September, but none were found in 2008 or 2010. Whether the species has established is unclear.
- **Loach minnow:** Over 2,000 were stocked from 2007 to 2009, but only five were observed in 2009 and none in 2010. [[Marsh et al. 2010]] reported two loach minnow in 2009 and none in 2010.
- **Razorback sucker:** Stocked from 2007 to 2009, but only two were observed in October 2008, and none in 2010. Lack of detection may reflect sampling limits or the 2010 flood.
- **Spikedace:** Stocked in 2007–2009, but none were detected in 2008, 2009 or 2010.

**Possible reasons for low detection:**
- Rarity of the species and limits of current sampling methods.
- Negative interactions with native species, which may not be major, since most species returned during phase one co-occur with the T&E species elsewhere.
- Flooding, which affects established and newly stocked populations alike, but does not fully explain the rarity of all native species in the Gila basin.
- Gradient and current velocity, which may limit the four T&E species relative to the more widespread chub, dace and suckers. A [[Gis Analysis]] of historical gradient is proposed to test this.
- Habitat suitability, which has not been measured quantitatively in Fossil Creek. Visual assessments suggest suitable habitat, but no data confirm it.

**Recommendations:** 2011 will be the fifth year of phase two. After 2011 surveys and data analysis, the Fossil Creek Native Fish Working Group should decide whether species have established and whether to continue stocking and monitoring.
