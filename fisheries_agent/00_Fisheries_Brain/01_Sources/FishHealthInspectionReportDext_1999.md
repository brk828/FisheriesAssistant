# Fish Health Inspection Report — Dexter NFH and West Turkey Creek Wild Fish Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #fish-health #conservation-hatchery

## Executive Summary

This document comprises two linked U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) Fish Health Inspection Reports (Form 3-226), conducted by the [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]] under biologist M. Landye and Director John C. Thoesen. The first report (Case #99-75) covers routine pathogen screening of hatchery-reared stock at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, NM), a facility central to the recovery of endangered Southwestern cyprinids such as the [[Bonytail Chub]]. The second report (Case #99-07) is a **wild fish inspection** of feral, non-salmonid species collected from [[West Turkey Creek]] on the El Coronado Ranch (Pearce, AZ), within the Chiricahua Mountains "Sky Island" region, owned/managed by Josiah Austin.

Both inspections screened for [[Asian Tapeworm]] (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*) and *[[Edwardsiella Tarda]]*/*[[Edwardsiella Ictaluri]]* bacterial pathogens, in addition to standard viral and bacterial panels. No definitive positive findings are legible in the surviving OCR data, consistent with these being clearance/monitoring inspections rather than disease outbreak investigations. The report explicitly disclaims predictive value: it is **not evidence of future disease status**.

## Background & Description

No morphometric or anatomical data are reported in this document type — Form 3-226 is a pathogen surveillance instrument, not a taxonomic or morphological record. However, the species/lot codes allow limited inference:

- **Dexter NFH lots ("B/C")**: Repeated lots of tens of thousands of individuals obtained as eggs (E) from Dexter broodstock, consistent with mass propagation of an endangered cyprinid such as the [[Bonytail Chub]] — a fusiform, small-headed, deep-bodied chub adapted to swift mainstem river currents in the Colorado River Basin, though reared here in a captive refuge population.
- **West Turkey Creek wild fish**: Species sampled as fish (F) directly from the stream, including:
  - *Agosia chrysogaster* (**[[Longfin Dace]]**) — a small, robust-bodied desert cyprinid with a subterminal mouth adapted for benthic grazing/detritivory in intermittent streams.
  - **YAC** — likely the **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*), a federally endangered Sky Island cyprinid with a stout, laterally compressed body.
  - **LMB** — [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), a non-native centrarchid piscivore.
  - **FHM** — [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), a hardy, small-bodied cyprinid tolerant of degraded water quality.
  - **WMF** — an unresolved code, tentatively a poeciliid or centrarchid (e.g., Warmouth or Western Mosquitofish); species identity could not be confirmed from OCR degradation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Dexter NFH**: A controlled hatchery environment using **spring and well** water supply, per the "Type of Water Supply" checkbox. This isolates broodstock from wild pathogen vectors and supports the captive propagation/refuge role of the facility for imperiled Southwestern fishes.
- **West Turkey Creek**: A **stream/impoundment** habitat within a Sky Island montane drainage in the Chiricahua range of southeastern Arizona. This creek supports a mixed native–nonnative fish assemblage — native desert cyprinids ([[Longfin Dace]], [[Yaqui Chub]]) co-occurring with introduced sportfish and baitfish ([[Largemouth Bass]], [[Fathead Minnow]]), a common conservation concern in isolated desert stream systems where invasive species threaten endemic ichthyofauna.
- Both sites are explicitly classified as **non-salmonid** systems, and the wild fish inspection is explicitly flagged as **feral** (wild-caught), contrasting with the **hatchery** classification of the Dexter lots.

## Key Findings

- **Pathogens screened**: [[Asian Tapeworm]] (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*), [[Edwardsiella Tarda]], and [[Edwardsiella Ictaluri]].
