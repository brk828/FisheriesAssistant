# Annotated Bibliography: Research in Economically Important Species of California Fish and Game (Supplement No. 3, July 1964)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is the third supplement to an annotated bibliography compiled by the State of California, Department of Fish and Game, and submitted to the Legislature under House Resolution No. 70 (1959 Regular Session). It catalogues research publications, bulletins, popular articles and short notes on economically important fishes, shellfish, marine mammals, marine turtles, upland game, waterfowl, furbearers and big game in California and adjacent waters. The entries are organized into Fish (General, Marine Fishes, Sharks, Herring-like, Flatfishes, Bass, Silversides, Jacks, Mackerel-like, Tunas, Surfperches, Rockfishes, Miscellaneous), Crustaceans, Mollusks, Marine Mammals, Anadromous Fishes (including [[Salmon and Steelhead]], [[Shad]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Sturgeon]]), Inland Fishes (including [[Kokanee]], [[Black Bass]], [[Catfish]], [[Threadfin Shad]]), Big Game, Upland Game and Game.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Annotated bibliography (supplement), California Department of Fish and Game, July 1964, Fish Bulletin series and Inland Fisheries Administrative Reports cited throughout.
- **Purpose:** Cover research published on economically important species to support fisheries and game management.
- **Structure:** Entries are grouped by taxon and habitat. Each entry gives author, year, title, source, and a summary of findings. Transmittal letters from the Director are addressed to the Chief Clerk of the Assembly and the Secretary of the Senate.
- **Entry types:** Peer-reviewed papers (e.g., *Calif. Fish and Game*, *Copeia*, *Journal of the Fisheries Research Board of Canada*), departmental reports, and popular articles from *Outdoor California*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography repeatedly links fish and game productivity to habitat and water conditions. Key themes include:

- **Logging and sedimentation:** Logging damage to California streams (Calhoun, 1962; Calhoun & Seeley, 1963) involved streambed use as roadways, heavy equipment in streams, steep-slope tractor logging and removal of streamside vegetation. In 1962, 33 streams were damaged, mostly in north coast counties, all on private land. Sediment smothers eggs, fish and fish food.
- **Water development:** Reports assess effects of dams and diversions on the Middle Fork Feather River, Box Canyon, Yuba and Bear Rivers, North Fork Stanislaus River, and Trinity River. The Middle Fork Feather River study concluded that the planned project would destroy existing fisheries below Nelson Point with no known mitigation.
- **Water quality and temperature:** Studies address dissolved oxygen in spawning gravels (Coble, 1961; Sheridan, 1962; Vaux, 1962), low-oxygen avoidance (Whitmore et al., 1960), and temperature effects on [[Striped Bass]] survival and [[Pacific Sardine]] development.
- **Pollution:** Sewer effluent and [[Red Tide]] mortality in southern California (Young, 1964; Reish, 1963) and pesticide toxicity to salmonids (Katz & Chadwick, 1961; Katz et al., 1961) are documented.
- **Artificial habitat:** Artificial reefs (Carlisle et al., 1964) found that quarry rock is the best reef material.
- **Upland and big game habitat:** Brush manipulation, fertilization of browse, bitterbrush stocking density, and wetland management for waterfowl are covered.

## Key Findings

**Fisheries and harvest data**
- Angling effort in 1961 (Abramson, 1968a/1963b): 14.8% (San Francisco Bay-Delta), 55.8% (fresh water) and 29.4% (ocean) of effort; the 1963b article gives 15%, 56% and 29% of angling days.
- Estimated 118,070 persons purchased 134,936 special California angling licenses in 1961 (random sample of 7,493 stubs).
- California marine commercial landings: 725,253,471 pounds valued at $74,214,467 (1961); 663,106,607 pounds valued at $80,922,632 (1962).
- [[Pacific Sardine]] landings 1959-60: 128 boats landed 15,418 tons (central California) and 20,335 tons (southern California), plus 8,113 tons in Baja California; 1960-61 California fleet landed 26,948 tons and Mexican fleet 46,706 tons.
- [[Pacific Mackerel]] 1958-59: 39.4 million fish weighing 24.2 million pounds landed at California canneries; sixty percent were under one year old.
- [[Kelp Bass]] fishery: size limit of 10½ inches imposed in 1953, raised periodically to 12 inches by 1959; 1961 and 1962 catches compared favorably with better postwar years.
- Dover sole landings averaged 9 million pounds annually, 1950-1959.
- Pacific halibut fishery restored to high productivity under international regulation; economic weaknesses analyzed.
- Sport catch in Mission Bay (1961) averaged 0.156 fish per hour of fishing.
- Striped bass catch rates: 0.07 fish per hour in the Delta (1954) to 0.43 fish per hour in Upper San Francisco Bay (1959).
- Eagle Lake rainbow trout (1961-62): 2,291 and 1,058 anglers checked; 608 trout caught from 11,465 hours; catch rate about 0.05 fish per hour (about 18 hours per fish).

**Salmon and steelhead**
- Shasta River 1958 king salmon count: 6,089 king salmon, 871 adult steelhead and 147 silver salmon counted; counts of 9,875 (1959), 10,698 (1960) and 8,764 (1961) were cited.
- Central Valley 1961 king salmon spawning stock: an estimated 259,000 spawned, with 256,000 (99%) using the main Sacramento River and major tributaries.
- Sacramento River chinook disease: water temperatures of 46° to 48°F appear to trigger the epizootic; temperatures over 55°F can control it.
- Robertson Creek spawning channel: a pink salmon eyed-egg transplant produced 95% fry output (91% of eggs taken).
- Juvenile chinook critical velocity between 8 and 13 feet per second; maximum observed swimming speed 26.7 feet per second (fishway research).
- Endrin toxicity: 96-hour TLm of 0.27 ppb for coho salmon (Katz & Chadwick, 1961); 0.51 ppb for coho in a separate test (Katz et al., 1961).
- Ocean mortality estimated at q = 0.32, or S = 73% annually (Parker, 1962).
- Potential profit: 38 cents per pound or $8.45 per fish (1959 prices) used for net-benefit calculations.

**Striped bass and other anadromous species**
- Young-of-the-year striped bass abundance fell from a 1953-54 peak to a 1959 low and rose to a 10-year high in 1962; no apparent relation with temperature, runoff, salinity, light or spawning stock size.
- Larval striped bass survival enhanced by low salinities; larval mortality greater at higher temperatures.
- Threadfin shad introduced to Hawaii established spawning populations in three reservoirs; spawning observed in California at 58°F and 64°F, below the commonly reported 70°F.

**Mackerel, tunas and other pelagic fishes**
- Skipjack and albacore: Bell (1963) aged 247 bluefin tuna 51-142 cm long as 0 to 5 years old. A bluefin tagged at Guadalupe Island in February 1958 was recovered off Japan in April 1963, 5,800 miles away.
- Albacore stomach analysis (Iverson, 1962): 544 fish from 1950-57, mainly fish, squid and crustaceans.
- Partyboat skindiver catches (Wood, 1964): 157 divers caught 406 fish of six kinds off central California; 8,898 divers caught 21,187 fish, mollusks and crustaceans of 24 kinds off southern California.
- Pollution effects (Young, 1964): dead or abnormal fish and mollusks near sewer outfalls (Santa Monica Bay, White Point, Los Angeles-Long Beach Harbor) in 1956-58.

**Shellfish, invertebrates and marine mammals**
- Paralytic shellfish poisoning (Kawabata et al., 1962): 20 persons affected and one died after eating Chlamys scallops at Ofunato City in May 1961.
- Oyster drill control (Haydock, 1964; Lindsay, 1963): barrier and chemical methods mostly ineffective beyond one month; furnace oil with Lindane and orthodichlorobenzene with Sevin gave ghost shrimp control at $54 per acre.
- Raft-cultured oysters in Massachusetts grew twice as fast as bottom-grown oysters, with survival six times greater, but earned $3.75 per bushel versus $4.50 for bottom-grown oysters.
- California sea lion count: 18,000 in southern California in 1961, the highest of 10 censuses since 1931; fewer than 1,000 in northern California.
- Sea otters observed at Año Nuevo Island in July 1963, 40 miles north of their usual haunts.

**Management tools and methods**
- Rotenone is the chemical most widely used for rough fish control (Meyer, 1963).
- Underwater tagging gun described (Ebert, 1964).
- Acrolein-based [[Aqualin]] tested for goldfish control: at least 1 ppm recommended, 3 ppm for large bodies of water.
- Shasta Lake sturgeon creel census (Fisk, 1963): 25 legal and 104 sublegal white sturgeon reported; night fishing judged unlikely to adversely affect the population.
- Trout stocking in urban areas could provide 1,000,000 or more fishing days annually, perhaps 3,000,000; statewide cost estimated at $500,000 minimum and possibly $3,000,000 per year.
- Pay-as-you-fish schemes increased angler use from 7,000 to 50,000 days annually at Murray Lake (San Diego).

**Big game, upland and waterfowl**
- Deer: highs and lows in abundance reflect range carrying capacity, weather, logging and similar conditions; a record 2,484 deer were taken by depredation permit in 1961.
- Quail: Gambel quail population mortality was 71% per year (Raitt & Genelly, 1964).
- Beaver: 1,600 taken in the 1962-63 trapping season, second to muskrat in furs sold.
- Bitterbrush stocking recommended at no more than 2,200 plants per acre.

**Recurring conclusions**
- Fish and wildlife protection must be considered in early water-project planning; the department recommends mitigation funds and protective flows.
- Stronger legal tools are needed, including amendment of the Forest Practice Act and possibly new water-rights laws.
- Several topics are flagged for further research, including the effects of erosion on streams, the relation of egg size to egg number in salmonids, and biological indices of lake productivity.
