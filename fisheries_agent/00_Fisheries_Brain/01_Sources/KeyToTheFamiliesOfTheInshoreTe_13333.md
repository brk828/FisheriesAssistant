# Key to the Families of the Inshore Teleosts of the Upper Gulf of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #dichotomous-key

## Executive Summary

This document is a provisional "working key" by Donald A. Thomson and William H. Eger (Department of Zoology, University of Arizona, July 1964) for identifying 66 fish families likely to occur in the shallow inshore waters of the [[Upper Gulf of California]], between [[Guaymas]] and [[Puerto Peñasco]], Sonora, Mexico. The key uses external morphological characters arranged as a [[Dichotomous Key]] with four master subkeys based on pelvic fin position, pelvic fin ray count, and dorsal fin continuity. The authors stated that the key had not been tested against Gulf specimens, because they had no access to regional collections, and they invited students to report errors found during use.

## Background & Description

The authors note that [[Walker 1960]] estimated that 120 fish families, comprising at least 586 species, occur in the [[Gulf of California]]. Of these, about 60 species in roughly 10 families are deep-water forms, leaving 526 species on the continental shelf. The key excludes deep-water fishes, offshore pelagic fishes, and the 16 families of [[Elasmobranchs]]. The line drawings were adapted from the [[Handbook of Hawaii Fishes]] (Gosline and Brock, 1960), which the key itself was modified from. Where illustrations of properly identified Gulf species were unavailable, representative genera were used. English and Mexican vernacular names (for example, "Pez volador" for flyingfish, "Agujón" for needlefish) appear beneath each drawing.

The key character choices were guided by the provisional checklist of Gulf fishes compiled by [[Walker and Norris]] (manuscript), which the authors acknowledged for making it available.

The key is organized around the basic external anatomy of a spiny-rayed fish, illustrated with the [[Opaleye]] (*Girella simplicans*). Terms such as spiny dorsal fin, soft dorsal fin, lateral line, cheek, isthmus, caudal peduncle, and pelvic fin position are used throughout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is a taxonomic identification tool and contains no measurements of water chemistry, habitat conditions, abundance, or population data. Its ecological relevance lies in the regional scope: the shallow shelf waters of the upper Gulf, a region the key targets for species likely to be encountered there. The inshore taxa it covers include reef-associated, estuarine-tolerant, and nearshore pelagic families, such as:

- **Snooks** (*Centropomus* sp.) and **Mullets** (*Mugil cephalus*), which enter brackish water
- **Gulf Grunion** (*Leuresthes sardina*) and **Pacific Sardine** (*Sardinops caerulea*), both schooling fishes
- **Flyingfishes**, **Needlefishes**, and **Halfbeaks**, which occupy the surface zone
- **Wrasses**, **Parrotfishes**, **Groupers**, and **Snappers**, which are associated with rocky or reef habitats
- **Clingfishes** and **Blennies**, which are small benthic forms

## Key Findings

**Scope and structure of the key**
- The key covers 66 families out of the 120 known from the Gulf of California.
- It is divided into four master subkeys:
  - **Subkey A:** Pelvic fins abdominal, with more than 5 soft rays (families such as [[Holocentridae]], [[Clupeidae]], [[Engraulidae]], [[Elopidae]], and [[Chanidae]]).
  - **Subkey B:** Pelvic fins thoracic with 5 or fewer soft rays, and dorsal fin in two or more separate parts (for example [[Gobiidae]], [[Carangidae]], [[Sphyraenidae]], and [[Scorpaenidae]]).
  - **Subkey C:** Pelvic fins thoracic or jugular with 5 or fewer soft rays, and continuous dorsal fin (for example [[Labridae]], [[Scaridae]], [[Serranidae]], [[Haemulidae]], and [[Pleuronectidae]]).
  - **Subkey D:** Pelvic fins totally absent (for example [[Syngnathidae]], [[Muraenidae]], [[Balistidae]], [[Tetraodontidae]]).
- Each couplet offers two contrasting character states and directs the user to a family, or to the next couplet.

**Taxonomic coverage**
- Representative species are named for each family. Where possible, these are Gulf species. Where not, a related genus was used.
- Vernacular names in English and Spanish are provided where available. Examples include:
  - *Mugil cephalus* (Mullet, Lisa)
  - *Sphyraena barracuda* (Great Barracuda)
  - *Lutjanus* sp. (Snapper, Pargo)
  - *Sterolepis gigas* (Black Sea Bass, Mero)
  - *Diodon hystrix* (Porcupine Fish)

**Limitations stated by the authors**
- The key has not been tested thoroughly against specimens because the authors lacked access to Gulf fish collections.
- Several families are represented by a single genus, and some drawings were substituted with genera, not species.
- The authors described it as a "working key" and asked students to report apparent errors or inconsistencies.

**Sources used**
- The bibliography lists eight works on Gulf and Pacific marine fishes, including:
  - [[Barnhart 1936]], *Marine Fishes of Southern California*
  - [[Breder]] (1928, 1928a, 1936), covering fishes from Panama to Lower California
  - [[Meek and Hildebrand]] (1923–1928), *The Marine Fishes of Panama*
  - [[Roedel 1953]], *Common Ocean Fishes of the California Coast*
  - [[Walker 1960]], on the distribution and affinities of the Gulf fish fauna
  - Walker and Norris (manuscript), an annotated checklist of Gulf fishes
