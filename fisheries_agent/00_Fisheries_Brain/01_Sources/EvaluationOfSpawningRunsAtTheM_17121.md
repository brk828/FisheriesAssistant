# Evaluation of Spawning Runs at the Marble Bluff Fish Facility, Nixon, Nevada (1978–1985)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report (Great Basin Complex, Reno; Mark E. Coleman, February 1986) reviews eight years of spawning-run data from the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] (MBFF) on the lower [[Truckee River]], which links [[Pyramid Lake]] to its upstream spawning grounds. MBFF was built in 1975 to let [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (LCT) and [[Cui-Ui]] bypass the Truckee River delta. The report covers run size, timing, sex ratios, length frequency, fishway performance, hatchery propagation, and the [[Tahoe Sucker]], and it documents how fishway and river-trap modifications changed passage success. Run sizes for the two endangered species remained small for LCT and highly variable for cui-ui. The authors attribute LCT shortfalls mainly to a lack of Truckee River imprinting and attribute cui-ui shortfalls largely to fishway design, with sediment, bird predation and larval mortality over the dam as secondary concerns.

## Background & Description

Pyramid Lake supported large populations of cui-ui and LCT that were central to the Paiute food supply and later to commercial and sport fisheries. Derby Dam (1905), built for the [[Newlands Project]], diverted Truckee River flows and encouraged delta formation, which blocked fish access. By 1943 the original Pyramid Lake LCT strain was extinct and cui-ui had declined considerably. Restocking followed through state, tribal and federal hatcheries, and management passed to the Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe in 1976.

MBFF has four components: Marble Bluff Dam, a fish handling building with a vee-trap and sorting table, a river trap at the dam base, and the Pyramid Lake Fishway. The fishway is 4.8 km long, uses modified 1-on-10 [[Ice Harbor Fishway]] ladders, and was designed for flows of 20 to 50 cfs. Operations run seasonally from late February or early March to late June or early July, with traps checked Monday through Friday.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lahontan cutthroat trout.** The introduced Summit Lake-origin strain is a spring spawner. Fish mature at 3 or 4 years, average 6 to 7 years of longevity (some reach 9), and weigh 1.5 to 2.5 kg at maturity. Historically the original strain included a winter run, and a 19 kg world-record cutthroat was caught in Pyramid Lake in 1925.

**Cui-ui.** Cui-ui are the last member of the genus *Chasmistes* and are endemic to Pyramid Lake. They are lacustrine adults that must spawn in streams. They live long (aged to 41 years), begin spawning at 6 to 8 years, and peak from 10 to 17 years. Larvae emigrate to the lake immediately after swim-up, so imprinting is probably absent.

**Tahoe sucker.** These fish use MBFF to reach lower Truckee River spawning grounds. Ages were underestimated in earlier work; opercle aging indicates fish up to 16 years old.

**Environmental drivers.** Water temperature above 13 °C causes high LCT egg mortality, and juvenile LCT lethal temperatures lie at 21.8–23.0 °C. Gas supersaturation (nitrogen ~100% in 1982), pollution from Reno-Sparks sewage effluent, bird predation by herons, cormorants and pelicans, and sedimentation of the impoundment all affect passage and survival.

## Key Findings

**Lahontan cutthroat trout**
- Annual MBFF runs were small: 42 fish in 1980 to 887 in 1985. The 1976 run was 560 fish.
- Runs typically lasted about 12 weeks, from March to early June, with peaks from mid-April to mid-May.
- Mean annual fork lengths since 1979 ranged from 458 to 568 mm.
- Females outnumbered males in 6 of the past 7 runs; the average ratio was about 1.0:1.5 males to females.
- Primary suspected cause of small runs: lack of Truckee River imprinting. Numana Hatchery returns (29 tag recoveries at MBFF versus 1 at Sutcliffe) support the imprinting hypothesis.
- Sutcliffe effluent-stream runs were much larger: 4,000 (1981), 9,081 (1982), 23,507 (1983), 6,153 (1984), 16,560 (1985).
- In 1983 to 1985 at least 75% of MBFF trout were captured in the river trap.
- 20–30% of fish reaching the building showed bird wounds.
- Egg takes rose sharply after 1983: 21,000 (1980), 0 (1981), 7,000 (1982), 27,000 (1983), 416,000 (1984), 475,000 (1985).

**Cui-ui**
- Largest run: 13,804 fish in 1982; runs of 5,000 or more occurred in five of the last six years.
- Runs peaked in May, except in late April in 1985, and lasted from the second week of April to the third week of June.
- Fishway ladder design was the main limiting factor. Modifications raised catches from 33 (1978) to 4,978 (1980), but 47 were taken in 1981 despite full modification.
- Large runs correlated with high Jan–Apr Truckee River discharge (for example, 675,700 acre-feet in 1983).
- Fish entered the building via the river only in 1984 and 1985, when the Pyramid Lake rise (about 7 m) inundated the delta.
- The 1969 year class dominated spawning runs and was 97% of the 1983 run processed at the building.
- Mean fork lengths rose yearly: males 471 to 528 mm; females 500 to 567 mm (1978–1985).
- Only 7.4% and 5.8% of the offshore prespawning aggregate reached the building in 1982 and 1983. Radio tracking of over 50 fish (1982–1984) showed mean upstream movement of only 2.1 km.
- Larval mortality from plunging over MB Dam ranged from 3.1% to 8.9% (adjusted), with no clear relation to river flow. The authors consider this serious because the species is endangered.
- Hatchery production: 7.7–11.7 million larvae stocked annually 1982–1985, nearly equal to all earlier plants combined.

**Tahoe sucker**
- Run sizes: 36,379 (1982), 7,997 (1983), 28,468 (1984), 16,800 (1985).
- Sex ratios of 1.0:1.4 (1982), 1.0:1.2 (1983) and 1.0:1.3 (1984).
- Spawning fish ranged from 100–400 mm FL, averaging about 260 mm (males) and 290 mm (females).

**Facility modifications and operations**
- A 1984 building by-pass ladder (larger cells, sub-surface openings) was not thoroughly tested because it was needed for release.
- River trap changes in 1984 and 1985 (flow settings, a recessed then elevator-mounted vee with 11.5 cm opening) raised cui-ui retention from about 30% to about 70%.
- Bird deterrence used a propane cannon and cracker shells, keeping birds at least 25 m from the trap entrance.
- Sedimentation after the 1983 floods and a shifting fishway-entrance bar are chronic problems requiring dredging and possible jetties.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- Post-1975 access gave cui-ui and LCT their first regular Truckee River access in about 50 years.
- Cui-ui passage has not been adequate for recovery; a new facility was estimated at over $17 million in a November 1984 Station Development Plan. A May 1985 design conference proposed alternatives that the authors recommend evaluating.
- Recommended interim steps include fishway and trap modifications and using fishway-held adults for hatchery spawning.
