# Distribution of Native and Introduced Fish in the Seven Creeks River System, Victoria

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percichthyidae #freshwater-fish #murray-darling

## Executive Summary

This 1979 study by P. L. Cadwallader, published in the *Australian Journal of Ecology*, documents the distribution of fish in the Seven Creeks River system, a tributary of the [[Goulburn River]] in the [[Murray-Darling Basin]]. A 1975-76 survey at sixty collecting stations recorded seventeen species, eleven native and six exotic. Habitat, cohabiting species and diets are described, and historical records are used to explain present distributions. Siltation is identified as the main cause of decline for native [[Murray Cod]] and [[Macquarie Perch]]. The upper-reach populations of Macquarie perch and [[Trout Cod]] derive from fish released in 1921-22. The only demonstrated adverse effect of an introduced species on a native species is the fragmentation of the range of [[Common Mountain Galaxias]] by [[Brown Trout]], whose populations were sustained by hatchery releases.

## Background & Description

The study addresses a gap: no comparable published distribution study existed for an Australian river system. The Seven Creeks was chosen partly because it is one of few localities where the rare [[Trout Cod]] occurs. The data were intended to support a breeding programme for re-establishing the species in suitable waters within its former range.

The system is about 100 km long and rises at roughly 600 m in the granitic [[Strathbogie Ranges]]. It flows through the [[Gooram Falls]] gorge, then across sedimentary deposits to join the Goulburn River at about 120 m above sea level. Downstream of Euroa the main channel divides into anabranches. Land has been largely cleared for sheep and cattle grazing, with only narrow fringing native vegetation remaining along parts of the main channel. Storage reservoirs, including [[Polly McQuinns Weir]], supply the Euroa town water system.

Mean annual rainfall is 976 mm at Strathbogie (1902-77) and 650 mm at Euroa (1883-1977), with winter maxima. Main-channel discharge peaks in July to September. Summer flows are much reduced and some tributaries dry to isolated pools. Water is alkaline, moderately conductive and slightly coloured, and *E. coli* densities are high, indicating organic waste inputs.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods.** Fish were collected at sixty stations between 17 December 1975 and 14 April 1976 by electrofishing, rotenone, gill netting and trap netting. Additional surveys ran in 1976-77 (main channel, stations 14-28, using SCUBA and seines) and 1977-78 (during an invertebrate survey). Recorded variables included width, depth, substrate, cover, macrophytes, shade, altitude, gradient and [[Stream Order]] (after Abell 1961). Stomach contents were analysed numerically.

**Habitat associations.**
- [[Common Mountain Galaxias]] occurred at 22 stations, mainly in first- and second-order shaded streams.
- [[Pygmy Perch]] occurred at 8 stations, usually in still or sluggish water among aquatic macrophytes.
- [[Brown Trout]] occurred at 19 stations, mainly first to fourth order, associated with boulders, debris and shade.
- [[River Blackfish]] was the most widespread native fish, at 25 stations, mainly fourth-order streams. It was absent from the headwaters and most small tributaries.
- [[Trout Cod]] occurred at three main-channel stations in the Gooram Falls section, plus one third-order stream in 1977-78.
- [[Macquarie Perch]] occurred at ten fourth- to fifth-order main-channel stations, with the largest numbers in deep holes.
- [[Western Carp Gudgeon]] was found at two stations in 1975-76 and later at stations 14 and 16.
- Exotic species ([[European Perch]], [[European Carp]], [[Goldfish]]) were concentrated in the lower main channel, with [[Smelt]], [[Silver Perch]], [[Golden Perch]] and [[Mosquito Fish]] restricted to the lowest reaches.

**Feeding.** Aquatic insect larvae (Ephemeroptera, Diptera, Trichoptera) dominated most diets. Crustaceans (copepods for pygmy perch; shrimps and yabbies for trout, European perch, carp and golden perch) were important for others. Trout diets included tadpoles and river blackfish. Overlap was documented between native and introduced species, including river blackfish in trout stomachs, and a little black cormorant containing western carp gudgeon.

**Water chemistry.** Samples from sites C and D (January 1976 to January 1977) showed pH values near neutral to alkaline, low hardness and low nutrients, with nitrate and phosphorus often undetectable. Turbidity and suspended solids were elevated in the lower reaches.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** Seventeen species were recorded, 11 native and 6 exotic (Table 4). Two species, [[Murray Cod]] and [[Flat-Headed Galaxias]] (the latter found after the 1975-76 survey), were not caught in the 1975-76 survey.
- **Siltation:** Clearing and overgrazing deposited silt in lower reaches. Silt fills deep holes that are preferred by Macquarie perch and Murray cod, and blankets gravel spawning beds. The researcher considers siltation the probable main cause of their decline.
- **Introduced populations:** The upper-reach Macquarie perch and trout cod populations descend from fish released in 1921 and 1922, drawn from Cathkin on the Goulburn River. Over 250 small fish were released in those two years, and the Gooram Falls release is the first record of these species above the falls.
- **Trout cod decline:** The species has contracted since 1921-22 and no longer occurs above Polly McQuinns Weir. Its range is limited to about 8 km of stream. Continual trout releases may have contributed. Reproduction was confirmed by recently hatched fish within the Gooram Falls.
- **Introduced species:** Western carp gudgeon first appeared after introduction into farm dams in the mid-1960s. Brown trout were stocked from 1886 onwards, with 120,000 released in 1964 alone. Stocking ceased in about 1969-75.
- **Trout effect on galaxiids:** The only confirmed adverse effect of an introduced species on a native species is the fragmentation of [[Common Mountain Galaxias]] range by brown trout.
- **Historical records:** Trout reached 4.6 kg, and a Murray cod of 25.4 kg was caught in 1926. By 1937 cod and Macquarie perch were described as rare.
- **Management implication:** The author argues that long-term detrimental change is more likely where introduced fish are sustained by hatchery releases, independent of natural recruitment, than where populations are regulated by environmental factors.
