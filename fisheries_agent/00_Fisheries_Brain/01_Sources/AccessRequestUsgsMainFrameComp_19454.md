# ASU Humpback Chub Research Logistics and Base Camp Authorization Request

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies to Glen Canyon Environmental Studies requests authorization for computer access, updates base camp locations for [[Humpback Chub]] research in the [[Little Colorado River]] gorge, and seeks resolution of logistical support items. The letter, dated 08 April 1991, addresses operational needs for upcoming field research on [[Gila cypha]] including PIT tag equipment, radio communications, and tribal authorization for camp sites.

## Background & Description

Arizona State University/NNHP (ASU/NNHP) is conducting research on [[Humpback Chub]] ([[Gila cypha]]) in the [[Little Colorado River]] system. The research requires establishment of base camps and various logistical support systems. A cooperative agreement for humpback chub studies was returned to the Bureau in Salt Lake City on 5 April 1991. Field operations are scheduled to begin within the next few months following this correspondence.

The research involves monitoring and tagging operations requiring access to real-time flow data from gauges at Cameron and at the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence. The study area encompasses the Little Colorado River gorge, with specific focus on river kilometers 3.0 and 10.75.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Little Colorado River]] serves as critical habitat for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]]. The research area includes [[Powell Canyon]] and [[Salt Trail Canyon]] locations. Base camp site selection required coordination with the Hopi Nation and Navajo tribes due to cultural and jurisdictional considerations.

The study area features characteristic Colorado Plateau geology including talus slopes and Bright Angel Shale formations. Water surface elevations in the study reach are documented at approximately 2920 feet. The research encompasses both the target species ([[Humpback Chub]]) and other native fishes being considered for PIT tag implantation.

## Key Findings

**Computer Access Request**: Authorization requested for ASU researchers to access USGS mainframe computer in Tucson via modem, specifically to obtain real-time flow information from ADAPS and QWDATA systems. Training session with USGS Flagstaff to be arranged.

**Base Camp Locations**: Two base camp sites identified:
- North side of [[Little Colorado River]] at approximately RK 3.0 (upstream from [[Powell Canyon]])
- North side of river at approximately RK 10.75 at mouth of [[Salt Trail Canyon]]
- Original [[Powell Canyon]] mouth location abandoned in response to Hopi Nation request
- Tribal authorization from Hopi and Navajo required before field operations commence

**Unresolved Logistical Items**:
- Purchase of PIT tags and scanners for ASU/NNHP use
- Decision on implanting other native fishes with PIT tags
- Installation of radio-communications system for [[Little Colorado River]] researchers
- Provision of equipment storage space in Flagstaff
- Identification and hiring of dedicated ASU/NNHP liaison in Flagstaff

**Previous Correspondence**: Logistical needs previously communicated in letters dated 18 December 1990, 12 February 1991, and 21 February 1991.

**Administrative Status**: Cooperative agreement for [[Humpback Chub]] studies signed and returned to Bureau on 5 April 1991, awaiting rapid turn-around for final authorization.
