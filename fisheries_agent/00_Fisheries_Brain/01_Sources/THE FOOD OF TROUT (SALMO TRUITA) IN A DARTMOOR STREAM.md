# The Food of Trout (*Salmo trutta*) in a Dartmoor Stream

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #freshwater-ecology #dietary-analysis

## Executive Summary

This study by [[J.M. Elliott]] investigates the diet of [[Salmo trutta|brown trout]] in the **Walla Brook**, a slightly acidic (pH 6.4–6.8) Dartmoor tributary, examining stomach contents relative to [[Invertebrate Drift]] and [[Benthos|benthic]] invertebrate populations. Using electrical stunning, 208 trout were sampled across three age classes (0+, 1+, 2+) during both day and night over a 17-month period. The research demonstrates strong **ontogenetic dietary partitioning** (reducing intraspecific competition) and provides critical evidence for **nocturnal drift-feeding behavior**, quantified via a novel "Availability Factor" (AF) metric comparing dietary proportion to benthic abundance.

## Background & Description

While this is primarily a trophic ecology paper rather than a morphological description, key size-class data used to stratify sampling includes:

| Age Class | Symbol | Mean Length |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| 1st year | 0+ | < 7 cm |
| 2nd year | 1+ | 7–12 cm |
| 3rd year+ | 2+ | > 15 cm |

- **Sampling method**: [[Electrical Stunning|Electrofishing]] (electrical stunning machine)
- **Stomach preservation**: 10% formaldehyde, pipetted directly into stomach cavity
- **Dry weight determination**: Oven-dried at 65°C for 3 days (Allen 1951 method); freeze-drying validated as equivalent
- **Fragment identification**: Head capsule size used to estimate original body size of partially digested prey (e.g., [[Chironomidae]] larvae)
- Comparative *S. salar* parr sampled: 5.5–6.9 cm (n=6), diet resembling 0+ trout

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat Structure
- **Substrate**: Small stones and gravel
- **Dominant macrophyte**: *Myriophyllum spicatum* (site 2, per Elliott 1967)
- Site selected for co-located sampling of fish, drift, and benthos

### Dietary Niche Partitioning (Key Mechanism for Reducing Competition)

| Age Class | Principal Food Item(s) | % Contribution to Biomass |
|-----------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **0+** | *Baetis* spp. nymphs | 23% |
| **1+** | Terrestrial invertebrates + Oligochaeta | 22.5% each |
| **2+** | Large *Limnephilidae* larvae (>5mm, stone-cased) | 50% |

- Total mean biomass scaled with trout size: **2+ trout ≈ 3.5× that of 1+; ≈7× that of 0+**
- Large prey items (Dytiscidae adults, large Ecdyonuridae, large Oligochaeta, large Tipulidae, stone-cased Trichoptera) were **exclusive to 1+ and 2+ classes** — 0+ trout physically unable to utilize these.
- Notable: [[Cannibalism]] observed — trout fry found in stomachs of 1+/2+ fish, especially March–April 1964 (peaking during [[Downstream Movement of Trout Fry|downstream fry migration]]).

### Seasonal Diet Shifts

**Winter (Nov–April) dominant taxa:**
- *Protonemura meyeri*, *Isoperla grammatica* (Plecoptera)
- Small *Limnephilidae* larvae (<5mm)
- *Hydropsyche instabilis*

**Summer (May–Oct) dominant taxa:**
- *Ephemerella ignita* nymphs
- Helmidae (*Helmis maugei*, *Limnius tuberculatus*, *Latelmis volckmari*)
- Chironomid pupae
- Emerging aquatic insects
- Terrestrial invertebrates

**Year-round staples:** *Baetis* spp., large *Limnephilidae*, *Rhyacophila* spp., *Polycentropus* spp., Chironomidae, *Simulium* spp.

### Diel Feeding Pattern (Drift-Feeding Hypothesis)

Central finding: trout exploit a **nocturnal surge in invertebrate drift** (per companion paper [[Elliott 1967 - Invertebrate Drift]]).

- Night sampling conducted ≥4 hours post-sunset (accounting for [[Gastric Evacuation Rate|digestion/gastric evacuation time]])
- Evidence of night-feeding: significant increase in emerging Plecoptera, Trichoptera, and Chironomidae in stomachs at night vs. day
- Behavioral corroboration: large trout observed stationary, facing upstream in midwater/surface at night during summer
- Comparison to [[Scotopic Vision in Fish]]: cites Brett (1957) showing coho salmon feed at 0.0001 ft-candles; trout presumed similarly capable

## Key Findings

### 1. Availability Factor (AF) Methodology
Elliott calculated **AF = d/b**, where *d* = % biomass in diet, *b* = % biomass in benthos (aquatic invertebrates only, normalized to 100%). AF = 1 indicates prey taken in strict proportion to benthic abundance.

### 2. Three Distinct Availability Groups (Table 6)
- **Group A**: AF < 1 by day, AF ≥ 1 at night (e.g., *Baetis* spp., *Rhyacophila* spp., Dytiscidae) — became newly accessible only under cover of darkness.
- **Group B**: AF > 1 in both day and night (e.g., *Leuctra* spp., *Isoperla grammatica*, Chironomidae pupae) — consistently over-represented relative to benthos.
- **Group C**: AF < 1 in both day and night (e.g., *Ephemerella ignita*, Helmidae) — chronically under-utilized despite benthic abundance, possibly due to protective morphology/behavior.

### 3. Case-Bearing Trichoptera Anomaly
Large stone-cased *Limnephilidae* larvae were **never recorded in drift** yet formed the dominant prey biomass for 2+ trout — indicating direct benthic foraging/visual detection rather than drift capture, uniquely exploited only by the largest size class (stone cases possibly limiting exploitation by smaller trout despite easy visibility).

### 4. Species Never Predated
*Leptophlebia vespertina* and *Paraleptophlebia submarginata* nymphs were **never found** in any stomach despite presumed presence in the system — anomalous absence unexplained.

### 5. Sericostoma Paradox
*Sericostoma personatum* larvae were **never found intact** in stomachs, though empty cases occurred in the lower gut — suggesting complete digestion or selective case-discarding behavior.

### 6. Terrestrial Subsidy
[[Terrestrial Invertebrates|Terrestrial invertebrate]] input (via surface drift) was a **major food source only in summer**, supporting broader theory of allochthonous energy subsidies to lotic salmonid populations (linking to foundational "drift-feeding" concept).
