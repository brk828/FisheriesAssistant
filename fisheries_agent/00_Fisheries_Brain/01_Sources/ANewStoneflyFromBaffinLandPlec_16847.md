# Capnia hantzschi (Baffin Land Stonefly)

#entomology #aquatic-invertebrates #capniidae #plecoptera #arctic-biology

## Executive Summary
This 1938 taxonomic paper by William E. Ricker (International Pacific Salmon Fisheries Commission) describes a new species of winter stonefly, **Capnia hantzschi**, from [[Baffin Land]] (Baffin Island), Canada. The species was collected from [[Nettilling Lake]] and the nearby locality of Tikerakdjuak. Although the genus [[Capnia]] (family [[Capniidae]]) is not a fish taxon, stoneflies are ecologically significant aquatic macroinvertebrates whose nymphal stages inhabit freshwater systems and serve as key indicators of water quality and prey resources within fish-bearing Arctic lake ecosystems. The description is based on male and female adult specimens, with the species named in honor of Arctic explorer Bernard Hantzsch, who collected the original specimen during his final expedition.

## Background & Description

### Male (Holotype)
- **Body length (dried):** 5 mm
- **Forewing length:** 1.8 mm (abbreviated, reaching only to the 1st abdominal segment)
- **Hindwing length:** 1.5 mm
- **Coloration:** Black body with a mid-dorsal brown line on the abdomen; legs dark brown
- **Head:** As wide as the prothorax; occiput rugulose (finely wrinkled); interocellar distance 2.0× the eye-to-ocelli distance
- **Pronotum:** As long as wide, sides curved, angles rounded, slightly rugulose near the anterior marginal groove and mid-line
- **Wings:** Dusky, abbreviated (brachypterous — reduced flight wings, common in cold-adapted winter stoneflies)
- **7th abdominal tergite:** Bears a hemispherical, prickle-covered tubercle on the posterior 2/3 of the segment, projecting over part of the 8th segment
- **Supra-anal process:** Shining black; broad base tapering to a slender, blunt, decurved tip fitting beneath the 7th tergite's tubercle; bears a short blunt subterminal inferior tooth
- **Paired elongate processes:** Light-colored, positioned above the supra-anal process base, separated medially by a narrow space; connected to the main process at posterior ends and near tips, otherwise separated by a visible air space (lateral view)
- **9th sternite:** Posterior margin produced and rounded
- **Sub-anal lobes:** Broad, emarginate on outer side, tips bluntly rounded

### Female (Allotype)
- **Body length:** 7.5 mm; to wingtip: 9.0 mm
- **Wings:** Fully developed (not abbreviated), 7.4 mm long, dusky
- **Venation details:**
  - Sc (subcosta) meets R (radius) before the cord
  - 1–2 costal crossveins present in addition to the humeral crossvein
  - R sinuate beyond the origin of Rs
  - Rs and M each fork once
  - Cu₁ and Cu₂ unforked
- **8th sternite:** Short subgenital plate set slightly anterior to the hind margin, notched at the tip, with a V-shaped band of dark pigment anterior to the plate
- Other abdominal segments unmodified

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Freshwater lake environment in the Arctic — specifically [[Nettilling Lake]] on Baffin Land, a high-latitude subarctic/Arctic freshwater system
- **Life stage collected:** Adults (nymphal/aquatic stage not described in this paper)
- **Seasonality:** Specimens collected in July (summer emergence period), consistent with typical stonefly adult emergence timing at high latitudes
- **Sexual dimorphism in flight capability:** Males exhibit brachyptery (abbreviated wings), while females possess fully developed wings — a pattern seen in several cold-climate Capniidae, possibly linked to differing dispersal strategies between sexes
- **Trophic role:** As with other Capniidae, larvae are presumed aquatic detritivores/grazers, forming a component of the invertebrate food web in Arctic lakes that likely support local fish populations (e.g., Arctic char) as a prey resource, though this is not explicitly discussed in the source text
- **Biogeography:** Distribution spans the Canadian Arctic (Baffin Land); closely related species occur in Northern Europe, suggesting a Holarctic distribution pattern for the genus [[Capnia]]

## Key Findings
- **New species description:** *Capnia hantzschi* n. sp. is formally described from both male and female specimens collected on Baffin Land.
- **Taxonomic relationship:** Most closely related among described American species to *Capnia nearctica* Banks, sharing the diagnostic 7th tergite tubercle in males, but distinguished by differences in the shape of the supra-anal process.
- **Biogeographic connection:** Related forms exist in northern Europe, exemplified by *Capnia tenuis* Bengtsson, supporting a broader Holarctic/circumpolar distribution for tubercle-bearing *Capnia* species.
- **Type series:** Holotype male and allotype female collected at Nettilling Lake (July 7 and 11, 1925, respectively) by J. D. Soper; additional paratypes (1 male, 2 females) from the same series, plus a historically significant single male paratype collected in 1910 at Tikerakdjuak by Bernard Hantzsch.
- **Etymology/Historical note:** The species is named after Bernard Hantzsch, an Arctic explorer who collected the earliest known specimen during his final (fatal) polar expedition, linking the taxonomic description to a notable chapter in Arctic exploration history.
- **Repositories:** Type specimens are deposited in the [[Canadian National Collection]] (Ottawa) and the Zoologisches Museum der Universität Berlin.
