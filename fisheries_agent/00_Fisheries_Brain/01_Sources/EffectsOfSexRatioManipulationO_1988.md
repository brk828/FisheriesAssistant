# Effects of Sex-Ratio Manipulation on Territoriality and Spawning Success of Male Pupfish (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary
This experimental study by Kodric-Brown (1988) manipulated the operational sex ratio (female density, with male density held constant) in outdoor tank populations of the [[Pecos River Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*) to test how skewed adult sex ratios affect [[Territoriality]], [[Alternative Mating Tactics]], and male reproductive success. Three treatments were used: male-biased (2:1), even (1:1), and female-biased (1:2) male:female ratios. Results showed that male pupfish facultatively adjust their breeding tactic (territorial vs. satellite) and time allocation (courtship vs. aggression) in direct response to female availability. Smaller, competitively subordinate males were the most behaviorally plastic, switching to the higher-fitness territorial tactic only when female-biased conditions reduced male-male competition. The study demonstrates that the operational sex ratio, independent of density, shapes breeding-system structure, variance in mating success, and the intensity of [[Sexual Selection]].

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Cyprinodon pecosensis* ([[Pecos River Pupfish]]), described by Echelle & Echelle (1978); genus is part of the family [[Cyprinodontidae]].
- **Size**: Adults range 20–50 mm standard length (SL).
- **Size classes used experimentally**:
  - Small: <2.5 cm SL
  - Medium: 2.5–3.5 cm SL
  - Large: >3.5 cm SL
  - Proportions held constant across treatments/replicates: 30% small, 40% medium, 30% large.
- **Sexual dichromatism**: During breeding season, males develop bright blue nuptial coloration; females retain cryptic olive-brown coloration with black vertical bars (shared non-breeding coloration of both sexes).
- **Reproductive biology**: Annual life history (most individuals breed in a single season); promiscuous mating system; no parental care; external fertilization; cryptic demersal eggs laid singly on substrate.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Source population collected from Mirror Lake, Bottomless Lakes State Park, Chaves County, New Mexico — a naturally high-density population with a **territorial breeding system** over substrates suitable for oviposition.
- **Breeding structure**: Because breeding sites are limited under high density, small males often become **satellites**, hovering in the water column above territories rather than defending them directly — a documented alternative reproductive tactic in [[Cyprinodon]] (Kodric-Brown 1977, 1986).
- **Experimental habitat conditions**: Outdoor circular galvanized tanks (7.35 m²), 60 cm water depth, continuous filtration through gravel, sand substrate (2 cm depth) for oviposition, ambient outdoor conditions in Tucson, Arizona (18 June–2 August 1982); water temperature 23–29°C during observation hours (0530–1100h).
- **Trophic/behavioral niche**: Diet ad libitum (Tetramin fish food) in experimental setting; natural niche presumably omnivorous/detritivorous typical of desert pupfishes.
- **Sex-ratio ecology**: Discussion draws parallels to other species with documented skewed adult sex ratios and alternative mating tactics, including [[Guppies]] (*Poecilia reticulata*), the [[Angel Blenny]] (*Coralliozetus angelica*), [[Dascyllus Marginatus]] (coral-dwelling damselfish), and the scorpionfly *Panorpa latipennis* — illustrating cross-taxa relevance of operational sex ratio to breeding system dynamics.

## Key Findings

### Experimental Design
- Treatments: 22 males per tank in all treatments; females varied — 11 (male-biased, 2:1), 22 (even, 1:1), 44 (female-biased, 1:2).
- 14-day experimental series, 3 replicates per treatment.
- Behavioral data: 9 daily 30-min observation sessions per replicate; 10-min focal-animal event recordings on 102 territorial males total (35 male-biased, 33 even, 34 female-biased).

### Breeding System Structure
- All treatments retained both **territorial** and **satellite** males — sex ratio did not eliminate either tactic, only shifted proportions.
- % Territorial males increased with female availability: male-biased 49.1% → even 53.2% → female-biased 59.5% (F=6.72, P=0.03); significant only between male-biased vs. female-biased treatments.
- % Satellite males decreased with increasing females: 15.9% (male-biased) → 16.4% (even) → 10.0% (female-biased) (F=9.13, P=0.021).
- Territorial:satellite ratio was highest in female-biased treatment (6.0) vs. male-biased (3.4) and even (3.2).

### Territory Size & Male Size Interactions
- Sex ratio significantly affected territory size distribution (G²=21.2, df=8, P=0.0007).
- In the female-biased treatment, 78% of males defended small territories (<800–1600 cm²) vs. 46% (even) and 44% (male-biased).
- Critically, **only in the female-biased treatment could small males secure territories** (19.6% of territories held by small males); in male-biased and even treatments, only medium/large males held territories (G²=18.01, df=4, P=0.0012).

### Spawning Success
- Satellite spawning success was strongly, positively related to female availability (F=24.62, P=0.003): 11-fold higher in female-biased vs. male-biased treatment.
- Territorial male spawning success showed a similar increasing trend with female availability (linear contrast F=6.95, P=0.05), though the direct ANOVA was marginal (F=4.59, P=0.074).
- Small males that switched from satellite to territorial tactic increased spawning success ~1.5× (0.45 vs. 0.63 spawnings/male/30 min).

### Agonistic Behavior
- % interrupted spawnings was higher in both male-biased and female-biased treatments vs. even-sex-ratio (F=17.24, P=0.004) — suggesting elevated competition at both extremes.
- Fight frequency by territorial males was highest in male-biased treatment (F=14.93, P=0.027; linear contrast F=13.80, P<0.05), consistent with intensified [[Male-Male Competition]] under scarce females.

### Time-Budget / Trade-offs
- Courtship frequency increased significantly with female availability (F=4.93, P=0.009; linear contrast P<0.001), highest in female-biased, lowest in male-biased treatment.
- Courtship *duration* did not differ across treatments.
- Agonistic behavior frequency and duration were both highest in male-biased treatment and lowest in even-sex-ratio treatment (frequency F=6.12, P=0.003; duration F=3.13, P=0.047).
- These patterns support a clear **behavioral trade-off between courtship and aggression**, mediated by female availability.

### Interpretation
- Smaller, competitively inferior males exhibited the greatest behavioral plasticity, consistent with size-dependent competitive ability documented in prior *Cyprinodon* work (Kodric-Brown 1977, 1986; Loiselle 1982).
- The **time-constraint hypothesis** (synchronized egg-laying overwhelming the ability of dominant males to monopolize matings) was favored over a sperm-depletion hypothesis to explain increased small-male territoriality under female-biased conditions, since territorial male spawning success didn't differ significantly between treatments despite increased courtship effort.
- Findings parallel experimental work on the scorpionfly *Panorpa latipennis* (Thornhill 1986), where small-male mating success also scaled with female availability but never surpassed large-male success.
- Overall, operational sex ratio independently affects: (1) proportion of males adopting primary vs. secondary breeding tactics, (2) variance in male reproductive success, and (3) intensity of [[Sexual Selection]] — even when density is held constant, distinguishing sex-ratio effects from density effects.
