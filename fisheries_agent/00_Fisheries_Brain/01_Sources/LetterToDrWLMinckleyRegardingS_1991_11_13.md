## Executive Summary
This document is a 1991 administrative letter from the Bureau of Land Management's Safford District Office to Dr. W.L. Minckley, a prominent ichthyologist at Arizona State University, regarding the finalization of a Habitat Management Plan (HMP) for the [[San Pedro National Riparian Conservation Area]]. Rather than a primary research paper, the letter serves as a meeting call for an ad hoc biological resources technical committee to resolve six major planning issues, several of which directly concern native aquatic species conservation, refuge habitat conversion, and riparian ecosystem management along the San Pedro River in Arizona. ## Ecological & Environmental Context
Although no specific species accounts are given, the letter identifies several ecologically significant habitat elements relevant to native aquatic fauna in the San Pedro River system:

- **[[San Pedro National Riparian Conservation Area]]**: A federally managed riparian corridor in southeastern Arizona recognized as critical habitat for native desert fish and riparian-dependent wildlife.
- **"Young Block" gravel pit ponds**: Proposed for conversion into a native fish refuge, suggesting these anthropogenic aquatic habitats may serve as refugia for native species threatened by nonnative fish introductions or habitat degradation in the main river channel.
- **[[St. David Cienega]]**: A wetland/spring-fed habitat proposed for partial development into a native fish refuge, indicating its ecological value as a stable, perennial aquatic habitat type historically important to desert fish persistence.
- **Riparian vegetation dynamics**: The tension between planting cottonwood (*Populus fremontii*) and willow (*Salix* spp.) versus rehabilitating abandoned farmland and controlling invasive salt cedar (*Tamarix* spp.) reflects broader riparian habitat management concerns that indirectly affect aquatic ecosystem quality, shading, bank stability, and invertebrate/fish food resources.
- **Prescribed burning**: Raised as a potential land management tool within the conservation area, with implications for watershed hydrology and downstream aquatic habitat.
- **Multiple-discipline ecological research**: Identified as a necessary component of the HMP, underscoring the interconnectedness of terrestrial, riparian, and aquatic systems in desert conservation planning.

## Key Findings
- The BLM identified **six major unresolved issues** requiring committee discussion before finalizing the Habitat Management Plan for the [[San Pedro National Riparian Conservation Area]]:
  1. Development of an **aquatic species recovery objective** parallel to existing terrestrial species objectives — signaling a gap in aquatic-specific conservation goals within the draft HMP.
  2. Conversion of the **"Young Block" gravel pit ponds** into a **native fish refuge**, an early example of using artificial/degraded habitats for native fish conservation.
  3. Development of part of the **St. David Cienega** into a native fish refuge, highlighting cienegas as priority habitats for desert fish conservation.
  4. Conflict between **riparian revegetation** (cottonwood/willow planting) and **farmland rehabilitation/salt cedar control**, reflecting habitat restoration trade-offs affecting the riparian-aquatic interface.
  5. The potential role of **prescribed burning** as a management tool within the conservation area.
  6. The need for **multiple-discipline ecological research** to inform adaptive management under the HMP.
