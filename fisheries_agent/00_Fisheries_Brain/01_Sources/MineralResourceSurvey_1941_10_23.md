## Executive Summary

This document, *Peat Deposits in Polk and San Jacinto Counties, Texas* (Mineral Resource Survey Circular No. 38, University of Texas Bureau of Economic Geology, 1941–1943), is a **geological and economic resource survey**, not an ichthyological or fisheries study. It describes the formation, stratigraphy, and chemical composition of peat deposits in two distinct wetland types near the Trinity River: **old meander scar bogs** (e.g., the Calvin Yates, Urbana, and Blanchard bogs) and **"bay-galls"** (perched-water forested wetlands, e.g., the Gerlach-Griffin bog). No fish species, fish populations, or fisheries data are mentioned anywhere in the source text. The only faunal references are incidental notes on terrestrial/semi-aquatic vertebrates (snakes, frogs, opossum, raccoon) observed in the bay-gall habitat. This note is therefore structured to document the **aquatic/wetland habitat context** relevant to potential fish habitat assessment, while explicitly flagging the absence of direct fish science data.

## Background & Description

**Not applicable — no fish or aquatic organism morphology is described in this source.**

The document instead characterizes the physical/chemical morphology of the peat substrate itself:
- **Meander scar bog peat** (e.g., [[Calvin Yates Bog]]): vertically stratified — upper 6–8 inches light brown/yellowish-gray, highly fibrous, low silt; middle zone (12–36 in.) more decayed, brown, spongy, silty; deeper zones dark brown to black, finely divided, high silt.
- **Bay-gall peat** (e.g., [[Gerlach-Griffin Bog]]): dark brown, lower silt content than river meander bogs; becomes finely divided and saturated below 3 feet; associated with sphagnous moss layers resembling commercial florist peat moss.
- Water content of peat samples ranged ~76–88% (average, wet basis); ash content ranged ~9–33% depending on depth and location.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although fish are not documented, the source describes two freshwater wetland ecosystem types that constitute potential aquatic/riparian fish habitat in the broader Trinity River system of Polk and San Jacinto Counties, Texas:

- **Meander Scar Bogs**: Formed adjacent to the Trinity River floodplain terrace, fed year-round by terrace seep water over the Lagarto clay formation. Sluggish drainage supports marshy vegetation (button willow, weeping willow, tall flaggy grass, water lilies, smartweed, love-vine, ferns, mosses). Subject to flooding only during extreme flood events, suggesting limited but episodic hydrologic connectivity to the main river channel — a factor relevant to fish access and larval/juvenile refuge potential.
- **"Bay-Galls"**: Perched-water wetlands sustained by springs emerging from the unconsolidated sands/gravels of the [[Willis Formation]] overlying impermeable Lagarto clay. These form dense, U-shaped, spring-fed marshes (e.g., the 8-acre Gerlach-Griffin bog) with poor drainage via nearly stagnant branches. Vegetation includes bay trees, magnolia, tupelo gum, myrtle, holly, sphagnum moss, ferns, grasses, and lilies.
- **Associated Fauna** (non-fish): snakes, frogs, opossum, and raccoon were noted inhabiting the bay-gall marsh; hogs reportedly avoid venturing far into the marshy interior due to unstable, "quaking" peat substrate.
- **Water Chemistry Context**: Hydrogen sulfide odor was detected in several peat samples upon sampling, indicating locally anoxic, reducing conditions typical of stagnant, organic-rich wetland water — a chemical condition generally unfavorable to fish survival without adequate dissolved oxygen refugia.

## Key Findings

- **No fish species, fish habitat surveys, or fisheries observations are present in this source.** The circular is exclusively a geological/economic assessment of peat resource potential.
- The described wetlands ([[Meander Scar Bogs]] and [[Bay-Galls]]) are hydrologically isolated from the main river channel most of the year, flooding only during extreme events — implying minimal permanent fish connectivity, though they may serve as ephemeral floodplain habitat during high-water events.
- Stagnant, low-oxygen, hydrogen-sulfide-bearing water chemistry documented in both bog types would likely limit fish occupancy except possibly by highly hypoxia-tolerant species (not identified in text).
- The spring-fed, perennial water supply from the [[Willis Formation]] and terrace seeps maintains consistently saturated wetland conditions, which could support amphibian and invertebrate communities more readily than fish populations.
- Given the absence of fish data, this source is best classified as a **geological/peat resource report** with tangential relevance to wetland habitat characterization rather than an ichthyological reference. Researchers seeking fish community data for the Trinity River drainage in Polk/San Jacinto Counties should consult dedicated fisheries or aquatic ecology surveys, as none are provided here.
