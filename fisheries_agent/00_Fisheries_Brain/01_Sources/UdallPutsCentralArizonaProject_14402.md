# Udall puts central Arizona project in phase one

#ichthyology #marine-biology #water-resources

## Executive Summary

This is a February 1964 newspaper page (Monday, February 17, 1964) with a regional map and articles on the revised [[Pacific Southwest Water Plan]], released by [[Stewart L. Udall]], Secretary of the Interior. The plan's first phase, estimated at about $3.1 billion, centers on the [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) and related works such as [[Bridge Canyon Dam]] and [[Marble Canyon Dam]]. The page also reports sharp criticism from Arizona leaders, [[Paul Fannin]] and [[Barry Goldwater]], who feared the plan could delay CAP authorization. A small advertisement for [[Central Arizona Newspapers Inc.]] securities appears on the page.

The document contains no fish or ecological field data. Its only aquatic-relevant content is a brief mention of fish and wildlife facilities and the water supplies of the [[Lower Colorado River]].

## Background & Description

The page includes three components:

- **Regional map (January 1964):** The [[United States Bureau of Reclamation]] (Department of the Interior) map shows existing and proposed features, including storage reservoirs, aqueducts, dams, and irrigation projects across the Southwest and into Mexico. Labeled features include the [[California Aqueduct]], [[Colorado River Aqueduct]], [[Imperial Aqueduct]], [[All-American Canal]], [[Coachella Canal]], [[Glen Canyon Dam]], [[Hoover Dam]] (Boulder Dam), [[Davis Dam]], [[Parker Dam]], [[Mono Lake]], [[Owens Lake]], and [[Salton Sea]]. Map legends distinguish existing projects, projects under construction, initial plan items (with and without further study), and other programs.
- **News article, "Udall Puts Central Arizona Project in..."** by Elson Irwin, Journal City Editor, describing the revised plan and its political reception.
- **Advertisement:** An offering by Central Arizona Newspapers Inc. (publisher of the *Arizona Journal*) of common stock units at $25.00 per unit. The notice states that the offer is made only by prospectus to Arizona residents.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The page is about water policy and infrastructure, not ecosystems. The environmental content is limited to:

- **Water supply for the Lower Colorado River:** The plan aims to protect economies that depend on **7.5 million acre-feet** per year of consumptive use from the Lower Colorado River.
- **Water salvage and seepage reduction:** Lining the All-American and Coachella canals would save **500,000 acre-feet** now lost to seepage.
- **Inter-basin transfer:** The Initial Plan would convey **1.2 million acre-feet** of surplus water from northern to southern California. Enlarging the California Aqueduct would move more [[Feather River]] water into southern California.
- **Fish and wildlife (Point 9):** The plan lists facilities for "conservation and development of fish and wildlife," including urgently needed **wintering waterfowl habitat in the Pacific Flyway**, and expanded public fishing and hunting opportunities to meet rising demand.
- **Recreation:** The plan would address rapidly expanding needs for water-oriented outdoor recreation.
- **Virgin River:** The Dixie Project would build a dam on the headwaters of the [[Virgin River]] to protect water for the St. George area of Utah.

No fish species, population counts, water quality measurements, or habitat surveys are reported.

## Key Findings

**1. Plan scope and cost**
- The revised [[Pacific Southwest Water Plan]] was unveiled by Secretary Udall on a Sunday and released in Washington.
- The total construction cost of the Initial Plan is estimated at **$3,126,000,000**, of which **95 percent** would be fully reimbursable. Other passages cite the plan as "$3.1 billion" or "$3,100,000,000."
- Each feature would be repaid **within 50 years** after it became revenue producing.
- Phase One would create a **$1.7 billion basin power revenue account** to construct the CAP and related works.
- Phase Two would cost **$1.4 billion** and follow in **15 to 20 years**.

**2. Authorization strategy**
- The report would allow Congress to authorize the CAP as an independent project, provided the basin account is written into the bill.
- The Interior Department strongly recommends a **package plan** instead.

**3. Stated objectives (ten points)**
The article lists ten objectives, including:
1. Meet the most immediate and urgent water needs of the Pacific Southwest.
2. Provide a framework for comprehensive long-range planning.
3. Establish a development fund as a financial foundation.
4. Protect economies dependent on 7.5 million acre-feet from the Lower Colorado River.
5. Maximize use of existing supplies through water salvage and wastewater renovation.
6. Authorize long-delayed water and power developments.
7. Improve the well-being and economic status of American Indians in the area.
8. Meet growing demand for water-oriented outdoor recreation.
9. Provide fish and wildlife facilities, including wintering waterfowl habitat in the Pacific Flyway.
10. Set aside past controversies and build a united approach to water problems.

The report states that the Initial Plan does **not** provide a full solution for the region's total future needs, which require long-range, comprehensive regional planning.

**4. Phase One components**
- Central Arizona Project and Bridge Canyon Dam.
- Lining of the All-American and Coachella canals to save **500,000 acre-feet** of seepage losses, which would ease California's loss from the Colorado River lawsuits.
- Enlargement of the California Aqueduct to carry more Feather River water to southern California.
- Dixie Project dam on the Virgin River for St. George, Utah.

**5. Political opposition**
- **Governor Paul Fannin** said the plan threatens the CAP. He argued that the report could be used to hinder and delay CAP authorization, which the plan claims to support. He said Udall had refused to provide a separate, favorable report on the CAP bill, and that a favorable report is all that is needed for full congressional consideration.
- **Senator Barry Goldwater**, speaking in Nogales, said he did not understand how a fellow Arizonan could take the stand Udall had taken in the dispute with California. He said the regional plan "might be good on paper" but would never work because California would not give up northern river water to other states. He argued the CAP should be built independently.
- The article notes that the Arizona–California water dispute had lasted about **40 years**, and that the plan was intended to end these hostilities.

**6. Other content**
- A photo caption shows Udall presenting his book *The Quiet Crisis* to Italian Industry and Commerce Minister Guiseppe Medici at the Interior Department.
- The securities advertisement offers 2,400,000 shares of Class "A" voting common and 2,200,000 shares of Class "B" non-voting common, sold in units at $25.00 each, and is restricted to Arizona residents by prospectus.
