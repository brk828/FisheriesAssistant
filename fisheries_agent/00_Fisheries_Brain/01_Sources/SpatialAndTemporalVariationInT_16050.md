# Spatial and Temporal Variation in the Distribution of Fishes of the LaBranche Wetlands Area of the Lake Pontchartrain Estuary, Louisiana

#ichthyology #marine-biology #estuarine-ecology #LaBranche-Wetlands #Lake-Pontchartrain

## Executive Summary

This study (Cashner, Gelwick & Matthews 1994) characterizes the fish assemblages of the [[Bayou LaBranche]]–[[Bayou Trepagnier]] system within the [[LaBranche Wetlands]], a 14,000-acre fresh-brackish marsh and wooded swamp along the southwestern shore of [[Lake Pontchartrain]], Louisiana. Using boat-mounted electrofishing at six stations during May 1989 and June 1990, the authors collected 10,644 fish specimens representing 38 species in 19 families. Combined with historical rotenone (LADEQ, 1986) and net/trawl (Thompson & Verret, 1980) records, the cumulative known fauna of the system totals 52 species in 26 families. The study demonstrates strong spatial dissimilarity between the two bayous (driven largely by conductivity/salinity differences) and substantial temporal variability within stations across years, using Jaccard's Index (JI), Percent Similarity Index (PSI), and Detrended Correspondence Analysis (DCA). The findings establish an important ecological baseline for monitoring anthropogenic change in Gulf Coastal Plain estuarine wetlands.

## Background & Description

- **Study system**: The [[LaBranche Wetlands]] lie along the south shore of Lake Pontchartrain and are considered the most productive wetlands in the Lake Pontchartrain basin (Binet & Ensminger, 1992).
- **Bayou Trepagnier**: Smaller tributary (9–14 m wide, ≤1 m deep) near Norco, LA; receives treated effluent from the Shell Oil Company Norco Manufacturing Complex; flows through cypress-tupelo, red maple, elm, and ash swamp with dense canopy, abundant woody snags, slow current, and little aquatic vegetation.
- **Bayou LaBranche**: Larger (17–27 m wide, 1.5–2.4 m deep), more open with direct sunlight exposure, supports submerged/emergent vegetation (coontail, alligator weed, duckweed) and marsh vegetation (*Juncus*, *Phragmites*, *Panicum*) near its mouth; connects directly to Lake Pontchartrain.
- **Historical sampling context**: Prior data came from [[Thompson and Verret 1980]] (nekton survey, nets/trawls, 1978) near Station T-8, and the [[Louisiana Department of Environmental Quality]] (LADEQ) rotenone surveys (1986) at five sites, including one comparative site in Mississippi Bayou (Lake Maurepas system).
- **Collecting methods**: Six stations (T-4, T-5, T-6 on Bayou Trepagnier; T-7, T-8, T-21 on Bayou LaBranche) sampled via a Coffelt Mark 20 boat-mounted electrofishing unit, suited to the high-conductivity waters (>2,000 micromhos) typical of these bayous. Each collection targeted ~30 minutes of active shocking time.
- **Voucher specimens**: Deposited at the University of New Orleans Vertebrate Collection ([[UNOVC]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water chemistry**:
  - Conductivity in May 1989: Bayou LaBranche ~3,900 micromhos vs. Bayou Trepagnier 2,400–2,550 micromhos.
  - Conductivity in June 1990: much lower and more similar between bayous (1,200–2,000 micromhos).
  - Water temperatures reached up to 32.5°C.
  - Dissolved oxygen was frequently low, especially in upper Bayou Trepagnier stations (2.8–3.4 ppm in 1989) and in Bayou LaBranche in 1990 (2.1–3.7 ppm), with diurnal fluctuation (lower in morning, higher in afternoon).
- **Salinity gradient influence**: Despite low conductivity during 1990 sampling, brackish-water species (e.g., *Trinectes maculatus*, *Pogonias cromis*, *Microgobius gulosus*, *Leiostomus xanthurus*, *Anchoa mitchilli*, *Elops saurus*, *Brevoortia patronus*) persisted in Bayou LaBranche due to its direct connection to Lake Pontchartrain — a hydrological linkage absent in upper Bayou Trepagnier.
- **Community differentiation**: The two bayou systems supported ecologically and physicochemically distinct fish faunas, reflecting differences in size, canopy cover, vegetation, and — most critically — conductivity/salinity regime.
- **Anthropogenic pressures**: The LaBranche Wetlands and broader Lake Pontchartrain basin face ongoing human disturbance (industrial effluent inputs, land-use change), underscoring the value of this study as an ecological baseline (Flowers & Isphording, 1990; Moore, 1992).

## Key Findings

### Faunal Composition
- 10,644 specimens, 38 species, 19 families collected via electrofishing (1989–1990).
- Combined with LADEQ (1986) and Thompson & Verret (1980) data: **52 species, 26 families** total known from the system.
- Dominant families (numerically): [[Lepisosteidae]] (gars), [[Clupeidae]] (herrings), [[Fundulidae]] (topminnows), [[Poeciliidae]] (livebearers), [[Centrarchidae]] (sunfishes).
- Bayou Trepagnier: 23 species collected via electrofishing.
- Bayou LaBranche: 34 species collected via electrofishing.
- 19 species shared between bayous; 15 species unique to Bayou LaBranche.
- LADEQ rotenone sampling captured 13 additional species (mostly brackish-water, transient, low abundance) not caught by electrofishing.

### Community Similarity (Spatial)
- Overall bayou-to-bayou dissimilarity: Jaccard's Index (JI) = 0.50; Percent Similarity Index (PSI) = 22.1%.
- Within-bayou station comparisons (1989): JI = 0.40–0.59 (higher similarity).
- Between-bayou station comparisons (1989): JI = 0.18–0.39 (lower similarity), except T-6 vs. T-21 (JI = 0.55), reflecting T-6's intermediate position.
- Similar spatial patterns held in 1990, though the uppermost Bayou LaBranche station (T-21) showed increased similarity to upper Trepagnier stations that year.

### Community Similarity (Temporal)
- Substantial inter-year (1989 vs. 1990) differences at most stations; only ~half or fewer of total species recorded in both years at most sites.
- Highest inter-annual stability: Stations T-6 (Trepagnier) and T-7 (LaBranche), with elevated PSI/JI.
- Comparison of Station T-8 across decades (Thompson & Verret 1978 vs. LADEQ/authors 1986–1990): JI = 0.611, PSI = 58.2%, indicating substantial long-term turnover.
  - Notable abundance shifts: *Micropogonias undulatus* (1097 → 46), *Fundulus grandis* (318 → 13), *Lucania parva* (0 → 123), *Cynoscion arenarius* (0 → 23).

### Multivariate Analysis (DCA)
- Axis 1 eigenvalue = 0.901; Axis 2 = 0.169 — captured most variance in fish assemblage composition.
- Fish assemblages distributed along a gradient from small-stream/freshwater (upper Trepagnier) to larger-stream/brackish (lower LaBranche) conditions.
- Separation of upper Trepagnier vs. lower LaBranche assemblages by ~4.00 DCA units on Axis 1, indicating near-complete faunal turnover across the gradient.
- Station T-6 (lowermost Trepagnier) occupied an intermediate position between the two bayou clusters.
- The Mississippi Bayou (Lake Maurepas system) LADEQ sample was distinctly separated from all LaBranche/Trepagnier collections, suggesting each associated wetland has a unique fauna warranting individual assessment.
- Species ordination revealed tight clusters, e.g., a group of eight largely freshwater taxa (*Notropis maculatus*, *Ictiobus cyprinellus*, *Sciaenops ocellatus*, *Ameiurus melas*, *A. natalis*, *Aphredoderus sayanus*, *Centrarchus macropterus*, *Pomoxis nigromaculatus*) aligning with the Mississippi Bayou site.

### Ecological Habitat Groups (assigned from spatial distribution/abundance mapping)
1. **Group 1 – Widespread throughout system**: *Lepisosteus oculatus*, *Cyprinodon variegatus*, *Fundulus grandis*, *F. pulvereus*, *Gambusia affinis*, *Poecilia latipinna*, *Mugil cephalus*, *Lepomis macrochirus*.
2. **Group 2 – Bayou Trepagnier only**: *Atractosteus spatula*, *Amia calva*, *Anguilla rostrata*, *Notemigonus crysoleucas*, *Fundulus chrysotus*, *Lepomis symmetricus*.
3. **Group 3 – Throughout Bayou LaBranche (and downstream Trepagnier)**: *Elops saurus*, *Brevoortia patronus*, *Dorosoma cepedianum*, *Lucania parva*, *Menidia beryllina*, *Morone mississippiensis*, several *Lepomis* spp., *Micropterus salmoides*, *Leiostomus xanthurus*, *Microgobius gulosus*, *Trinectes maculatus*.
4. **Group 4 – Lower LaBranche/lower Trepagnier only (T-6, T-7, T-8)**: *Myrophis punctatus*, *Alosa chrysochloris*, *Dorosoma petenense*, *Anchoa mitchilli*, *Ictalurus punctatus*, *I. furcatus*, *Strongylura marina*, *Syngnathus* sp., *Aplodinotus grunniens*, *Micropogonias undulatus*, *Gobiosoma bosc*.
5. **Group 5 – Scarce/occasional**: *Lepisosteus platostomus*, *Heterandria formosa*.
6. **Group 6 – Lowermost site only (T-8, near Lake Pontchartrain)**: *Ophichthus gomesi*, *Ictiobus niger*, *Syngnathus scovelli*, *Lepomis cyanellus*, *Dasyatis sabina*, *Cyprinus carpio*, *Pylodictis olivaris*, *Gobiesox strumosus*, *Cynoscion nebulosus*, *Pogonias cromis*, *Archosargus probatocephalus*.
7. **Group 7 – Mississippi Bayou only (T-10, Lake Maurepas system)**: *Notropis maculatus*, *Ictiobus cyprinellus*, *Ameiurus natalis*, *A. melas*, *Aphredoderus sayanus*, *Centrarchus macropterus*, *Pomoxis nigromaculatus*.

### Interpretation
- The two bayous represent opposite ends of a freshwater-to-brackish environmental gradient largely driven by conductivity and connectivity to Lake Pontchartrain.
- High temporal variance within stations suggests strong fish mobility, tidal/salinity-driven transient occupation, and dynamic short-term community turnover rather than stable, fixed assemblages.
- Each wetland subsystem (e.g., LaBranche/Trepagnier vs. Mississippi Bayou) appears to harbor a distinct fauna, arguing for site-specific monitoring approaches.
- These results establish a baseline dataset for detecting future anthropogenic or natural environmental change in the [[Lake Pontchartrain Basin]] and broader [[Gulf Coastal Plain]] wetland systems.
