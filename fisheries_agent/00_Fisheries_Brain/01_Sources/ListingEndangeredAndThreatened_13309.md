# Listing Endangered and Threatened Species and Designating Critical Habitat; Amended Procedures To Comply With the 1982 Amendments to the Endangered Species Act

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #endangered-species-act #regulatory-framework

## Executive Summary

This document presents the final rule amending 50 CFR Part 424, establishing revised procedures for listing endangered and threatened species and designating critical habitat under the [[Endangered Species Act]] (ESA). Published October 1, 1984, by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and [[National Marine Fisheries Service]], the rule implements changes mandated by the [[Endangered Species Act Amendments of 1982]]. The amendments fundamentally restructure the listing process to ensure decisions are based solely on biological considerations, prohibiting economic factors from affecting endangered or threatened status determinations. The rule streamlines timelines, consolidates public participation requirements, and allows separation of critical habitat designation from species listing when appropriate.

## Background & Description

The original regulations governing listing, delisting, and reclassification of species were adopted February 27, 1980 (45 FR 13022) and codified at 50 CFR Part 424. The [[Endangered Species Act Amendments of 1982]], enacted October 13, 1982, necessitated substantial procedural revisions. A proposed rule was published August 8, 1983 (48 FR 36062), with public comment closing October 7, 1983. The final rule adopts the proposal with minor modifications based on comments from federal agencies, state conservation departments, environmental organizations, industry groups, and scientific experts.

Key definitional clarifications include:
- **Candidate species**: Any species under consideration for listing but not yet proposed (distinguished from proposed species)
- **Critical habitat**: Specific areas with physical or biological features essential to species conservation requiring special management, including areas outside current range when essential
- **Species**: Includes subspecies and distinct vertebrate population segments
- **Conservation**: All methods necessary to bring species to the point where ESA protections are no longer required

The rule establishes that taxonomic groups above species rank may be listed only if all constituent species individually qualify. Listing decisions must rely on standard taxonomic distinctions and biological expertise.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The regulatory framework addresses five factors for listing determinations under Section 4(b)(1)(A) of the ESA:
1. Present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment of habitat or range
2. Overutilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes
3. Disease or predation
4. Inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms
5. Other natural or manmade factors affecting continued existence

Critical habitat designation criteria focus on physical and biological features essential to conservation, including:
- Space for individual and population growth and normal behavior
- Food, water, air, light, minerals, or other physiological requirements
- Cover or shelter
- Breeding, reproduction, and rearing sites
- Habitats protected from disturbance or representative of historic distributions

The rule recognizes that critical habitat designation may not be prudent when: (1) identification increases threats to species through taking or vandalism, or (2) designation would not benefit the species. Critical habitat is "not determinable" when information is insufficient for required analyses or biological needs are inadequately known.

Areas outside current geographic range may be designated as critical habitat only when essential to species conservation. Critical habitat cannot be designated in foreign countries or areas outside U.S. jurisdiction, based on legislative history and Solicitor opinions.

## Key Findings

**Petition Procedures**: Within 90 days of receiving a petition to list, delist, or reclassify a species, the Secretary must determine whether the petition presents "substantial scientific or commercial information" indicating the action may be warranted. "Substantial information" is defined as that amount leading a reasonable person to believe the measure may be warranted. Within 12 months of a positive 90-day finding, the Secretary must: (1) publish a proposed rule, (2) determine the action is not warranted, or (3) determine the action is warranted but precluded by other listing priorities. The 12-month period may be extended only with demonstration of expeditious progress on other listings.

**Mandatory Time Limits**: Final action on listing or critical habitat proposals must occur within 1 year of publication, reduced from the previous 2-year limit. A single 6-month extension is permissible only when substantial disagreement exists among knowledgeable scientists regarding data sufficiency or accuracy. This extension is not available for economic analyses related to critical habitat.

**Separation of Listing and Critical Habitat**: While concurrent listing and critical habitat designation remains the general requirement, listing may proceed without critical habitat designation when: (1) designation is deemed "not prudent," (2) prompt listing is essential but critical habitat analysis is incomplete, or (3) critical habitat is "not determinable" within the 1-year period. When critical habitat is not determinable, an additional 1-year extension is allowed, after which critical habitat must be determined "to the maximum extent prudent."

**Biological vs. Economic Considerations**: The rule implements Congress's clear intent that listing decisions be made "solely on the basis of the best scientific and commercial information" without reference to economic impacts. Economic considerations apply only to critical habitat designation, not species status determinations. The Conference Report explicitly prohibits economic factors from delaying species listing.

**State Agency Coordination**: When the Secretary adopts a rule conflicting with state conservation agency comments or fails to act on a state agency petition, written justification must be provided to the agency explaining the decision.

**Emergency Provisions**: The Secretary may issue emergency regulations immediately for any emergency posing significant risk to species well-being. Emergency rules take effect upon Federal Register publication and remain valid for 240 days unless standard procedures are completed during that period.

**Periodic Review**: At least every 5 years, the Secretary must review each listed species to determine whether delisting or reclassification is warranted. Reviews may occur more frequently based on petitions or new data.

**Public Participation**: Proposed rules require: (1) Federal Register publication, (2) notification to affected state agencies and counties, (3) notification to affected federal agencies and private parties, (4) international notification through the Secretary of State, (5) notification to scientific organizations, and (6) newspaper publication in affected areas. Comment periods are minimum 60 days for listing/critical habitat proposals, 30 days for other proposals. Public hearings must be held if requested within 45 days of proposal publication.

The rule received extensive public comment addressing concerns about: timing of critical habitat designations, standards for determining substantiality of petitions, scope of economic analyses, notification procedures, and the relationship between candidate species identification and legal protections. The Services clarified that candidate species receive no legal protection under the ESA but should be considered in environmental planning under laws like [[NEPA]].
