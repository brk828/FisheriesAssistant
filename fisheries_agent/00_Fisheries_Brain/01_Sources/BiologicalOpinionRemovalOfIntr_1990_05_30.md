## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Biological Opinion issued in response to a Section 7 consultation request from [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]] regarding the discovery and proposed removal of unauthorized, translocated fish populations from the [[Aguajita Springs Complex]]. Three species — [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius* ssp.) — were discovered in a small, ephemeral pool at Aguajita in May 1990. The presence of these fish, likely introduced by human transport from Mexico's [[Rio Sonoyta]], posed a significant genetic and ecological threat to the endangered [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*) population located only one mile away at [[Quitobaquito Springs and Pond]], which constitutes federally designated critical habitat. The FWS concluded that the proposed eradication action (trapping, netting, and habitat destruction if necessary) was not likely to jeopardize the Quitobaquito Pupfish and issued an incidental take statement anticipating the take of approximately 25 desert pupfish present at Aguajita.

## Background & Description

The source document is a regulatory and management opinion rather than a morphological study; however, it references key taxonomic distinctions:

- **Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*)**: A polytypic species comprising at least two relevant subspecies:
  - ***C. m. eremus*** (Quitobaquito Pupfish) — endemic to [[Quitobaquito Springs and Pond]]; listed as endangered March 31, 1986; described by [[Miller and Fuiman 1987]] as a distinct subspecies based on morphological and genetic differentiation.
  - ***C. m. macularius*** (nominate subspecies) — found in the [[Rio Sonoyta]] drainage in Sonora, Mexico.
  - The pupfish found at Aguajita Springs Complex were of **unknown subspecific origin**, raising concern about potential hybridization.
- **Mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*)**: Noted behaviorally as "extremely adept at hiding in small crevices, mud, or debris," complicating complete eradication efforts via netting/trapping.
- **Longfin Dace (*Agosia chrysogaster*)**: Native to the Rio Sonoyta basin; no detailed morphological data provided.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: The Aguajita Springs Complex is described as "a very small spring and its outflow," isolated from all other water bodies, and historically fishless. The pool in question was formed only in **August 1988** during heavy rains and never exceeded **one-third of a meter (~33 cm) in depth** — an extremely ephemeral, marginal habitat.
- **Biogeographic context**: The complex sits approximately one mile east of [[Quitobaquito Springs and Pond]], within the broader [[Rio Sonoyta]] basin ecosystem straddling the U.S./Mexico border in the Sonoran Desert.
- **Species origins and native ranges**:
  - [[Longfin Dace]] is native to the Rio Sonoyta basin; its presence at Quitobaquito would likely be ecologically benign for pupfish.
  - [[Mosquitofish]] is **non-native** to both the Rio Sonoyta and Gila River drainages and has documented associations with the decline of native southwestern fishes (citing [[McMahon and Miller 1985]] and [[Meffe 1985]]).
  - The suspected vector of introduction to Aguajita was **human transport**, likely originating from the Rio Sonoyta in Mexico, facilitated by the site's use by monument visitors and Mexican nationals for water and recreation.
- **Threat pathway**: Ecologists identified a specific anthropogenic risk mechanism — as the shallow, temporary Aguajita pool dries or fills with sediment, well-intentioned people may "rescue" fish and relocate them to the nearby, more permanent Quitobaquito Pond, thereby introducing both a predatory/competitive invasive species (mosquitofish) and a genetically distinct pupfish stock into critical habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Biological Opinion determination**: The proposed fish removal project was found **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the endangered Desert Pupfish, nor to destroy or adversely modify its designated [[Critical Habitat]].
- **Primary conservation concern**: Genetic integrity of the [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] subspecies was identified as the central conservation issue — hybridization between *C. m. eremus* and *C. m. macularius* stocks would undermine long-term subspecies conservation.
- **Invasive species risk**: Mosquitofish introduction into Quitobaquito Spring and Pond was explicitly flagged as **detrimental** to pupfish survival, consistent with broader literature on Gambusia's negative impacts on native southwestern fish (McMahon & Miller 1985; Meffe 1985).
- **Management action approved**:
  1. All desert pupfish removed from Aguajita would be frozen and transferred to [[Arizona State University]] for NSF-sponsored genetic research.
  2. Removal via nets and traps was deemed potentially insufficient (especially for mosquitofish); FWS recommended that if the spring flow and pool had not naturally dried by **June 20, 1990**, the flow should be artificially diverted and the pool filled with sand/gravel to guarantee complete eradication.
- **Incidental Take Statement**: FWS anticipated incidental take of **all desert pupfish present** in the Aguajita Springs Complex, estimated at approximately **25 individuals** (with the possibility of a higher actual count), under Section 7(b)(4)/7(o)(2) provisions.
- **Reinitiation triggers**: Formal consultation would be reinitiated if incidental take limits were exceeded, new information revealed unanticipated effects, the action was modified, or new species/critical habitat designations became relevant.
- **Interagency coordination**: The action involved cooperation between [[Organ Pipe Cactus National Monument]], the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
