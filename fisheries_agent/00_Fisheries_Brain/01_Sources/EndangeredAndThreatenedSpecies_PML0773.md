# Endangered and Threatened Species: Review of Plant and Animal Taxa (1997 Proposed Rule)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This *Federal Register* notice (Vol. 62, No. 182, September 19, 1997) is the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's annual review of plant and animal taxa that are candidates for, or have been proposed for, listing as endangered or threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. It is a multi-taxa compilation rather than a fish study. Fish appear among the freshwater taxa in the tables, alongside mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, freshwater mussels, snails, insects, crustaceans, and plants.

The notice reports the status of 2 major groups of taxa: [[Candidate Taxa]] (Table 1) and former candidates or proposed taxa no longer under review (Table 2). It also reports progress on listing actions, responses to [[Recycled Petitions]], and the Service's [[Listing Priority Guidance]]. Its stated purpose is to give advance notice of potential listings to resource managers and to solicit information for setting listing priorities.

## Background & Description

The notice serves several functions:

- **Candidate Notice of Review:** The Act requires the Service to identify species that are endangered or threatened, based on the best available scientific and commercial information. The Service keeps a candidate list to provide advance notice of possible listings to planners and developers, to solicit information, and to set listing priorities.
- **Annual notice of findings on recycled petitions:** Under Section 4(b)(3)(B) of the Act, the Service must respond to petitions with one of three findings: not warranted, warranted, or warranted but precluded.
- **Annual description of progress on listing actions:** The Service had not published these descriptions annually in the past, but states it intends to do so beginning with this notice.

Table 1 lists the candidate and proposed taxa. Its columns cover the category code (PE, PT, C, C(w), and others), listing priority number, lead Region, scientific name, family, common name, and historic range by state abbreviation. Table 2 lists taxa removed from candidate or proposed status, with codes explaining why (for example, taxa found to be more abundant or widespread than believed, or taxa removed for other reasons). Several Table 2 entries are fish, such as certain bull trout populations and the Gulf Coast hog-nosed skunk and other fauna.

The notice notes that prior notices of review were published in 1975, 1980, 1983, 1985, 1990, 1993, and 1996, with animal notices in 1982, 1985, 1989, 1991, 1994, and 1996. This notice supersedes all previous animal, plant and combined notices.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The notice gives limited ecological detail for individual taxa. It does note several features relevant to aquatic systems:

- **Freshwater mussels (Unionidae):** Several candidate and former candidate mussels are listed, including the Cumberland elktoe, Cumberlandian combshell, oyster mussel, and rough rabbitsfoot.
- **Springs and cave systems:** Many snails (for example, Pyrgulopsis and Tryonia springsnails and Hydrobiidae) and cave amphipods and crustaceans occur on the lists. Their known ranges are mostly in the southwestern United States.
- **Freshwater fish:** Shiners, trouts, chars, graylings, sturgeon, and chubs appear in the tables. Several of these occur in the upper Missouri, Columbia, Klamath and Sacramento systems, and in southeastern rivers such as the Alabama system.
- **Other aquatic-associated fauna:** Hawaiian damselflies (Megalagrion) and Comal Springs riffle beetles are also included.
- **Wetland and bog habitats:** The notice mentions the southern population of the [[Bog Turtle]] (*Clemmys muhlenbergii*), proposed for listing as threatened due to similarity of appearance. It is not counted in the totals.

The notice encourages State agencies, other Federal agencies and affected parties to consider these taxa in environmental planning, including [[National Environmental Policy Act]] analyses.

## Key Findings

**Overall status (as of September 3, 1997):**
- 51 plants and 20 animals are proposed for endangered status.
- 20 plants and 8 animals are proposed for threatened status.
- 107 plant and 100 animal candidates await preparation of proposed rules.
- 18 taxa that were classified as proposed or candidate in the 1996 notice are no longer in those categories (Table 2).
- Table 1 includes 207 candidate taxa and 99 taxa for which proposed listing rules have been published.

**Listing progress (February 28, 1996 to September 3, 1997):**
- Final listing actions for 152 taxa.
- Proposed listing actions for 23 taxa.
- One proposed delisting action.
- Withdrawals of proposed rules for 13 taxa.

The Service attributes the small number of proposed listings to its [[Listing Priority Guidance]]. This guidance followed the lifting of the [[Listing Moratorium]] on April 26, 1996 (imposed April 10, 1995, under Public Law 104-6) and the restoration of listing funding. It ranks work in this order:
1. Emergency situations.
2. Resolving the status of outstanding proposed listings. The large backlog from the moratorium meant that only 23 new proposals could be processed.
3. Processing new proposals to add species.
4. Processing administrative findings on petitions.
5. Lowest priority: final or proposed [[Critical Habitat]] designations, delistings, and reclassifications from endangered to threatened.

**Recycled petitions:** The Service reviewed the 18 recycled petitions that came due in this period. It made continued "warranted but precluded" findings on all of them. These taxa remain candidates, marked "C(w)" in Table 1.

**Requests for information:** The Service asks the public to submit information on:
1. Taxa that should be removed from the list.
2. Taxa that should be added.
3. Areas that should be designated as critical habitat, or cases where critical habitat would not be prudent.
4. Threats to included taxa, and their immediacy and magnitude.
5. Taxonomic or nomenclatural changes.
6. Suggested common names.
7. Mistakes, such as errors in historic ranges.

**Notable fish entries:**
- Alabama sturgeon (*Scaphirhynchus suttkusi*).
- Atlantic salmon, distinct population segment in seven Maine rivers.
- Bull trout, Columbia River and Klamath River populations.
- McCloud River redband trout.
- Arctic grayling, Upper Missouri River fluvial population.
- Arkansas River shiner (native population) and Topeka shiner.
- High Rock Spring tui chub, listed as a former candidate.

**Taxonomic note:** The notice corrects an earlier error. *Sclerocactus brevispinus* was mistakenly included as a candidate in 1996 because it was a part of *S. glaucus*, a threatened species. It was not a separate species.
