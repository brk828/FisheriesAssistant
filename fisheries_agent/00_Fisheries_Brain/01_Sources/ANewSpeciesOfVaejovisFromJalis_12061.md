# A New Species of Vaejovis from Jalisco, Mexico

#arachnology #scorpiones #vaejovidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, published in the *Pan-Pacific Entomologist* (vol. 62, no. 4, 1986, pp. 355–358) by Stanley C. Williams, describes and names [[Vaejovis Chamelaensis]], a new scorpion species from the [[Estacion de Biologia Chamela]] in [[Jalisco]], Mexico. The species is placed in the [[Eusthenura Group]] of the genus *Vaejovis* and is considered one of the smallest members of the genus, with mature males up to 14.5 mm in total length. The document is a terrestrial arthropod study. It contains no fish or aquatic-ecosystem data beyond a note that the site lies about 2 km from the Pacific Ocean.

## Background & Description

During July and August 1985, Williams spent two weeks surveying the scorpion fauna of the station's wildlife preserve. He studied 385 scorpions representing four species. The most common was [[Centruroides Elegans]]. Smaller numbers of [[Vaejovis Subcristatus Occidentalis]] and [[Vaejovis Increpidus Increpidus]] were also recorded. The new species was initially mistaken for juveniles of the other species because of its very small adult size. Measurements follow Williams (1980), a reference on the scorpions of Baja California.

**Diagnosis:**
- Minute body size; total length of mature males up to 14.5 mm.
- Base color golden-yellow, with variegated black carapace markings and dusky-outlined metasomal keels.
- Pedipalp chela length-to-width ratio of 3.8; palm slightly deeper than wide.
- Primary-row denticles on the fixed and movable fingers subdivided into 5 subrows.
- Telson with a distinctly enlarged subaculear tooth.

The species is related to [[Vaejovis Puritanus]] and [[Vaejovis Viscainensis]], which share the 5-subrow fixed-finger denticle pattern. It differs from both by its small size, by having 5 (not 6) subrows on the movable finger, and by the presence of the subaculear tooth.

**Type series and measurements:**

| Measure | Holotype (male) | Allotype |
|---|---|---|
| Total length (mm) | 14.3 | 16.3 |
| Carapace length/width (mm) | 1.87/1.23 | 1.94/1.23 |
| Metasoma length (mm) | 6.24 | 5.99 |
| Telson length (mm) | 1.74 | 1.94 |
| Chela length (mm) | 2.29 | 2.35 |
| Pectine teeth (left/right) | 11/10 | 10/10 |

The allotype is described as slightly larger, with 10 pectine teeth per comb, less robust chela denticles, and a more elongate vesicle. Seven topoparatypes (males) had total lengths of 13.5–14.0 mm and pectine counts of 10–11 (mode 11).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality:** 122 km north of Manzanillo, about 2 km from the Pacific Ocean, at roughly 100 m elevation.

**Climate:** Seasonally dry tropical, with a rainy season from June to November.

**Habitat:** Moderately well-drained, flat sedimentary soil, fine-textured and well packed. The scorpions were found on a little-used, unpaved access road called Eje Central. Surrounding vegetation was dense deciduous tropical forest, with cacti in higher, more exposed areas.

**Behavior and occurrence:** The species was seen on only two nights during the two-week study. Eight of the nine specimens were in the center of the unpaved road. Williams suggests it is an obligate burrowing species that rarely uses the surface, and that the male sightings may reflect synchronized courtship. It occurred sympatrically with *Centruroides elegans*, *V. s. occidentalis*, and *V. i. increpidus*. Land crabs were also present at the type locality.

**Comparative size:** It is the smallest member of the eusthenura group and possibly of the genus. The only possible rival is [[Vaejovis Minutis]], whose adults occasionally reach about 14 mm.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Vaejovis chamelaensis* is named after the biological station where it was found.
- **Smallest-scale record:** Mature males reach at most 14.5 mm, which places it among the smallest known *Vaejovis*.
- **Diagnostic traits:** 5-subrow primary denticles on both fingers, a distinct subaculear tooth, and a golden-yellow body with black carapace markings.
- **Type material:** Holotype (male), allotype, and 7 topoparatypes (males), all collected 10–11 July 1985 by S. C. Williams using [[Ultraviolet Detection]]. The holotype is deposited at the [[California Academy of Sciences]] as type no. 15744.
- **Restricted distribution:** Known only from the Chamela type locality, with no close known relatives.
- **Behavioral observation:** Burrowing lifestyle inferred from low surface activity; possible synchronized male courtship.
- **Research context:** 385 scorpions of four species were studied during the survey, with *Centruroides elegans* the dominant species.
