# Native Fish Population Estimates and Water Quality in Lower Colorado River Backwaters (2009–2010)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service report, prepared by the [[Arizona Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office]] in Parker, AZ, summarizes the stocking history, population estimates, and water quality monitoring for [[Razorback Sucker]] (RBS) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (BTC) in off-channel backwater habitats along the [[Colorado River]] from 2008 to 2010. The backwaters are managed under the [[Lower Colorado River Management Plan]] as a refuge from nonnative predators and a source of larger fish for release into the mainstem. Mark-recapture estimates were made at several sites, and the results show that some ponds can grow stocked fish to releasable size (≥400 mm) in under a year, while dissolved oxygen (DO) crashes caused fish kills at others. The authors recommend site-specific changes to stocking density, water quality management, and aeration.

## Background & Description

Off-channel protected habitats are an important tool for recovering threatened and endangered big-river fishes. The program follows three strategies: (1) using hatchery fish for reintroduction, (2) using natural or constructed habitats to grow fish to larger sizes before release, and (3) using habitats that become available through reservoir drawdown or drying to establish populations of native fish. The program also protects stocked fish from most [[Nonnative Fish Species]].

The report covers these sites:

- **[[High Levee Pond]]** (≈5.4 acres), [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (CNWR). Developed in 1993 as a native fish facility and used until 2005. [[Largemouth Bass]] were illegally introduced in 2003 and largely eliminated the RBS population by 2005. The pond was renovated with [[Rotenone]] in 2008 and restocked.
- **[[Emerald Canyon Golf Course]]** (ECGC), La Paz County, about 6 miles north of Parker, AZ. Five ponds, of which three are used as native fish habitat. Water is pumped from the Colorado River through a screen that excludes most nonnative fish and larvae.
- **[[Office Cove]]** (≈21 acres, average depth 7 ft), [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] (BWNWR). Barrier nets (1993) were only partly effective, so a berm built in 1995 reduced nonnative fish entry.
- **[[River's Edge Golf Course]]** (REGC), Needles, CA, near river miles 50–51. Two lined ponds (Inlet and Riverside) supplied by well water. The ponds were drained and dried in 2006 to remove nonnative fish.

Stocking history is summarized in Table 1 of the source. Sources included [[Bubbling Ponds State Fish Hatchery]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]], and the Achii Hanyo Native Fish Facility. Stocked sizes ranged from about 100 mm to 529 mm TL. The largest stocking events were 4,000 RBS (≈100 mm) at Office Cove in 2005 and 1,252 RBS (mean 354 mm) at High Levee Pond in October 2008.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Backwaters differ in how well they support fish. Their water sources, nonnative fish exclusion, and thermal and oxygen regimes control how many stockable fish they produce. Summer heat is the main constraint. Water temperatures near 30 °C and very low DO were recorded in several ponds. The report cites [[Bulkley and Pimentel 1983]] for the thermal limits of RBS, and [[Boyd 1990]] for DO guidelines in warmwater aquaculture (≥5 mg/L for maximum production; sustained exposure below 2 mg/L reduces growth and can cause death).

Predation by nonnative species is a persistent threat. Largemouth Bass caused the collapse of the High Levee RBS population, and nonnative fish entering through unscreened inlets were an early problem at ECGC.

## Key Findings

### Survey Methods
- Between November and December 2009, AZFWCO estimated fish populations in seven off-channel habitats using the [[Lincoln-Petersen Mark-Recapture Method]].
- Four trammel nets (150 ft long) were fished for 5 hours per habitat. Captured native fish were measured (TL, mm), weighed (g), and scanned for [[Passive Integrated Transponder Tags]]. Untagged fish were implanted with a PIT tag and released. Recapture occurred 24 hours later using the same protocol.
- When sample sizes were too small, hoop nets were used for presence/absence surveys.
- Water quality (temperature, DO, specific conductance, pH) was measured with a [[Hydrolab Quanta 3]] at fixed stations. Monitoring was monthly to bi-monthly, moving to quarterly in 2010, and returned to monthly afterward to help prevent fish kills.

### Site Results

**High Levee Pond (CNWR)**
- 159 RBS marked on 15 December 2009. The next day, 71 fish were captured, 29 of them recaptures. The population estimate was **383 RBS**.
- Mean TL of 202 measured fish was **483 mm**.
- The population retained about **30%** of the 1,252 RBS stocked in October 2008.
- Fish grew from a mean of 354 mm to about 483 mm over 13 months, or roughly **10 mm per month**.
- A significant share of the population was at or above 400 mm and ready for release.
- Six BTC were captured during marking, but none were recaptured, so no BTC estimate could be made.
- Mean water temperature was 22.9 °C (October 2008–October 2009). Temperature rose 18% to 28 °C in August 2009, when DO dropped to nearly 0 mg/L, creating anoxic conditions.
- Mean specific conductance was 1.36 mS/cm and mean pH was 8.0.

**Emerald Canyon Golf Course, Pond 1**
- 18 RBS marked on 25 January 2010. Twelve were sampled the next day, including one recapture. The estimate was **123 RBS** (reported 95% CI 12–244, OCR-ambiguous).
- Mean TL was **377 mm** (range 325–450 mm).
- Only about 2% of the fish were from the 2008 stocking, so most stocked fish had left the pond.
- Ponds 11 and 1 held fish at or above 400 mm.
- A fish kill in Pond 11 in August (year garbled in the source, most likely 2009) was caused by low DO. Mean DO was **0.187 mg/L**, about 95% below the yearly average.
- Specific conductance and pH were relatively stable.

**Office Cove (BWNWR)**
- 70 RBS marked on 9 November 2009. Thirty-one were sampled the next day. The estimate was **226 RBS** (95% CI roughly 123–329, OCR-ambiguous).
- Measured fish ranged from 313 to 500 mm TL.
- Fish stocked in 2005 at about 100 mm were recaptured at about 432 mm, a growth of **332 mm in 4 years** (≈7 mm per month).
- Fish above 400 mm were ready for release.
- Mean temperature was about 24 °C, and mean DO about 5 mg/L; the two were inversely related. Specific conductance stayed stable.

**River's Edge Golf Course (REGC)**
- A May 2009 fish kill in Riverside Pond made a mark-recapture estimate impossible, so a presence/absence survey was used.
- Ten fish were captured: four RBS and six BTC.
- Five of the six BTC were young-of-year, suggesting limited reproduction in the pond.
- The one adult RBS was about 481 mm and had grown about 291 mm since February 2008 (≈12 mm per month).
- The one adult BTC measured 360 mm and had grown at about 6.8 mm per month since its December 2007 stocking.
- Temperatures averaged near 28–29 °C with highs close to 30 °C. Mean DO was **5.65 mg/L** with a low of **3.52 mg/L**. Specific conductance dropped suddenly in August, possibly due to low ionic content.

### Conclusions and Recommendations
- Backwaters differ widely in their ability to produce stockable fish, so management should be site-specific.
- Several backwaters can produce releasable (>400 mm) native fish in under one year at a growth rate of about 10 mm per month.
- Summer DO falls below tolerance levels in some ponds. Remedies include adjusting stocking regimes and adding solar-powered aerators.
- Planned actions:
  1. Test stocking densities in backwater habitats along the Lower Colorado River.
  2. Monitor water quality monthly and bi-weekly during warm months.
  3. Remove and stock larger fish (≥400 mm) annually from each backwater.
  4. Track survival of released larger fish in the mainstem.
- Program success will be judged by the number and size of fish harvested and by survival in the backwaters and after release.
