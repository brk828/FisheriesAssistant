# Three New Species of *Echinogammarus* Related to *E. veneris* from Italy and Switzerland

#ichthyology #marine-biology #amphipoda

## Executive Summary

This Beaufortia miscellaneous publication (Zoological Museum of the University of Amsterdam, No. 228, Vol. 17, 1970) by S. Pinkster and J. H. Stock describes three new freshwater amphipod species of the [[Echinogammarus Pungens Group]]: *E. fluminensis* n.sp., *E. ruffoi* n.sp. and *E. tibaldii* n.sp. The authors examined 41 new samples from Italy and Switzerland plus 13 preserved samples held in the British Museum (Natural History) and the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle. The species are morphologically distinguished from the closely related [[Echinogammarus Veneris]] and [[Echinogammarus Pungens]], and the paper provides a dichotomous key, detailed morphological descriptions, locality records with water chemistry data, and corrections to earlier Italian records.

## Background & Description

The paper follows a revision of the *E. pungens* group by [[Stock 1968]], which redescribed *E. veneris* from type material and recorded it from several Italian localities. Stock noted that Italian samples were too few to rule out further splitting of veneris-like forms. The present study was enabled by the authors' own collecting, particularly in the great lakes of northern Italy and the [[Po River System]], and by samples from A. Nocentini and E. Tibaldi.

Key morphological features used to separate the species include:

- **Third peduncle segment of antenna 1**: 2 to 2½ times as long as wide in some species, nearly or entirely 3 times in others.
- **Setation of antenna 2 peduncle segments 4 and 5**: density and length of setae.
- **Gnathopod 2 palmar angle spines**: three in *E. fluminensis* and *E. veneris* (per the text), two in *E. ruffoi*, four in *E. tibaldii*.
- **Posterior margin of legs 5–7 and uropod 3 armature**: setule density, spine/seta lengths and number of plumose setae.
- **Male calceoli on antenna 2**: usually absent in *E. tibaldii*, present in *E. veneris*.

**Key identification (summary):** The dichotomous key separates *E. foxi* (Chivasso, affluent of Po), *E. stammeri* (Monfalcone, near Trieste), *E. pungens* ("Mont Cassini en Italie"), *E. ruffoi* (Plain of the Po), *E. veneris* (entire Italy, streams and lakes), *E. tibaldii* (central Italy) and *E. fluminensis* (northern Italy).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Co-occurrence:** In one locality, *E. fluminensis*, *E. ruffoi* and *E. veneris* s.str. occurred together. No illegitimate pairings or morphological intermediates were found, which the authors interpret as evidence that the forms behave as good species rather than ecophenotypic variants ("Standortsmodifikationen").

**Habitat and distribution:**
- *E. fluminensis* is the only gammarid in the littoral zone of the great lakes south of the Alps (Como, Lugano, Garda, Maggiore). It is also the most common species in the Po river system.
- *E. ruffoi* occurs in small brooks and the Plain of the Po.
- *E. tibaldii* is recorded from the Fiume Garigliano and an older record from "Riete" (interpreted as Rieti).

**Water chemistry:** Several localities list chloride (Cl) and calcium (Ca) concentrations, for example:
- Lago di Garda, Cassone spring discharge: Cl 2 mg/l, Ca 29.6 mg/l.
- Fiume Tartaro, Pellegrina: Cl 13 mg/l, Ca 73.8 mg/l.
- Torrente Agogna, SW of Novara: Cl 25 mg/l, Ca 15.6 mg/l.
- Brook between Leno and Manerbio: Cl 8 mg/l, Ca 99.8 mg/l.

The species occur across a wide range of these values, and the Fiume Tione site is described as slightly polluted.

## Key Findings

- **Three new species described:**
  - ***E. fluminensis*** (holotype from Lago di Garda, Cassone shore): large males are more frequent in rivers than lakes; maximal total length about 12 mm; second antenna peduncle segments 4 and 5 have long ventral setae; third uropod armature mostly plumose.
  - ***E. ruffoi*** (holotype from a small brook 7 km E of Leno, Brescia): maximal length about 15 mm; only two palmar angle spines on gnathopod 2; reduced number of plumose setae on uropod 3; "very flat" urosome contour. Dedicated to Dr. S. Ruffo of Verona.
  - ***E. tibaldii*** (holotype from Fiume Garigliano, Campania, collected 6 Mar. 1969): maximal length about 12 mm; very hairy first and second antennae; calceoli usually absent. Dedicated to Dr. E. Tibaldi of Milan.
- **Sample base:** 41 new samples from Italy and Switzerland, plus 13 preserved samples from London and Paris.
- ***E. fluminensis* records:** about 30 localities are listed across Lago di Garda, Fiume Tartaro, Fiume Oglio, Fiume Adda, Lago Maggiore, Lago di Como, Lago di Lugano and the Po system, with the Grotta del Mago cave (Rancate, Ticino, Switzerland) being a notable subterranean record.
- **Corrections to earlier records:** The locality "Riete" was removed from the Italian list (a misidentification for E. tibaldii). The "Grotta del Mago" was reclassified as Swiss, not Italian, and its specimen was identified as *E. fluminensis*, not *E. veneris*.
- **New *E. veneris* Italian records:** Roncoferraro (Mantova), Po near Fiume Mincio confluence, and Lago di Bolsena (Roma, 5 m depth, 13 specimens).
- **Key diagnostic contrasts with *E. veneris*:** longer antenna 1 peduncle segments 2 and 3; more groups of setae on antenna 1 segments; more setae in the proximal group of mandible palp segment 2; fewer setae on antenna 2.
- **Nomenclatural note:** The text cites *E. pungens* as H. Milne Edwards, 1840 in the key and 1830 in the remarks, an inconsistency left unresolved in the source.
