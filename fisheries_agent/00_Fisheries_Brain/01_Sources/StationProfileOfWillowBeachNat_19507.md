## Executive Summary

The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] is a mitigation hatchery established in 1962 under Public Land Order No. 1941 (August 12, 1959) to compensate for the loss of natural riverine habitat caused by the construction of [[Hoover Dam]]. Located on the Colorado River at the Nevada/Arizona border within the [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]], the station's mission is to rear and distribute [[Rainbow Trout]] to support a coldwater sport fishery in the Lower Colorado River. The facility operates under a Memorandum of Understanding (dated April 24, 1959) between the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[National Park Service]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. In addition to its primary trout stocking program, the hatchery also plays a role in the conservation of the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), distributing this native species to Colorado as part of endangered species recovery efforts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Origin**: The hatchery exploits an artificial coldwater habitat created by [[Hoover Dam]] releases, which altered the natural warmwater regime of the Colorado River, making the tailwater suitable for coldwater salmonids like [[Rainbow Trout]] rather than native warmwater cyprinids.
- **Mitigation Context**: Functions as compensatory mitigation for the loss of natural riverine fish habitat and native fish populations displaced by dam construction and reservoir formation (Lake Mead/Lake Mohave).
- **Distribution Range**: Reared fish are distributed across state lines into [[Arizona]], [[California]], [[Nevada]], and [[Colorado]], indicating a multi-state fishery management program centered on the [[Colorado River]] and [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Endangered Species Role**: The inclusion of [[Colorado Squawfish]] in the distribution program indicates the hatchery's dual role — supporting recreational trout fisheries while also contributing to conservation stocking of a federally endangered native cyprinid in Colorado waters.
- **Congressional/Administrative Ecology**: The station's operation spans two states' congressional districts (Arizona and Nevada), reflecting the transboundary nature of the Colorado River fishery it serves.
