# Effects of Operating Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant on Twin Lakes, Colorado: 1982 Report of Findings

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation report presents the 1982 limnological monitoring results for [[Twin Lakes]], a montane lake system in central Colorado, and compares them with pre-operation data. The study is part of the long-term [[Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant]] assessment (project DR-331). The report concludes that the lakes are oligotrophic, that the year's very high inflow dominated the ecology, and that powerplant operation, which began in September 1981, measurably altered thermal structure, dissolved oxygen recharge, water clarity, and conductivity, mainly in the [[Lower Lake]]. Fish kills in the powerplant tailrace in May and June 1982 were attributed to likely [[Cadmium]] input from the forebay.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** Twin Lakes lie on [[Lake Creek]] at the eastern foot of the Sawatch Range at about 2802 m elevation. The lakes were probably formed by morainic damming.
- **Morphometry:** The upper lake has a maximum surface area of 263.4 ha and depth of about 28 m. The lower lake has 736.5 ha and about 27 m depth, and is described as the largest natural mountain lake in Colorado.
- **Study history:** Ecological studies began in 1971. The powerplant was tested in July 1981 and began sporadic operation in September 1981. Unit 2 (a second 100-MW unit) and a new Twin Lakes Dam were planned for 1984-85. A 15-year final monograph was under consideration.
- **Biota:** Introduced [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*), [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*), and [[Opossum Shrimp]] (*Mysis relicta*, introduced 1957 as lake trout forage) are key species. Other fauna include chironomids, oligochaetes and [[Fingernail Clams]] (*Pisidium* spp.).
- **Methods:** Hydrolab System 8000 multiparameter probes, Van Dorn sampling, ¹⁴C primary productivity, chlorophyll extraction (Parsons and Strickland), plankton hauls with No. 20 net, benthos from Ekman dredge samples, and standard USGS water analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Inflow and hydrology.** Total 1982 inflow was about 31.10 × 10⁷ m³ of outflow against inflow totals of 30.57 × 10⁷ m³ (as tabulated), roughly double the 1972-81 average. Over 38 percent of inflow came via the Mt. Elbert Conduit from Halfmoon and Turquoise reservoirs, entering the lower lake directly. Lake Creek runoff was above normal due to heavy snowpack.

**Thermal regime.** The upper lake was ice-free from May 6 to November 24 (202 days). The lower lake was ice-free from May 8 to December 9 (215 days). Stratification was shorter than previously recorded: the lower lake stratified about May 30 to October 6 (130 days), and the upper lake May 20 to October 10 (about 143 days). A large fraction of lake volume stayed at or below 10 °C, which the authors judged adequate for [[Lake Trout]] habitat.

**Dissolved oxygen.** Bottom DO never fell below 20 percent saturation during 1982. The authors attribute this to turbulence and aeration from powerplant operation, which contrasts with earlier winters when hypolimnetic DO sometimes fell below this level.

**Water chemistry.** Waters are very soft, dominated by calcium bicarbonate and/or sulfate, and poorly buffered (conductivity below 90 µS/cm). Average conductivities in 1982 were 75 (upper lake), 61 (lower lake) and 44 µS/cm (forebay), 14 to 25 percent lower than 1981. Forebay water has low TDS and buffering capacity, which increases [[Heavy Metal Toxicity]] risk.

**Nutrients and trophic status.** Phosphorus is limiting. Mean total phosphorus averaged 1.2 µg/L (upper lake) and 2.3 µg/L (lower lake). Nitrogen concentrations were generally below 250 µg/L. Per [[Likens Trophic Classification]], both lakes were classed as oligotrophic (ultra-oligotrophic to oligotrophic by some parameters).

**Light and clarity.** Upper-lake clarity was reduced by Lake Creek runoff, which acts as a silt-settling basin. Transmissivity from January to May averaged 51% (upper) and 54% (lower) in 1982, versus 71% and 68% in 1981. The authors attribute about a 17 percent decline to powerplant operation.

**Plankton and benthos.** Phytoplankton was dominated by diatoms ([[Asterionella Formosa]], [[Synedra]] spp.). Phytoplankton density in 1982 was the lowest of 1979-82 (1185/L lower lake, 304/L upper lake). Zooplankton fell by more than 50 percent from 1981, with copepods and rotifers dominant and cladocerans nearly absent. Benthic densities were chironomids 762/m² and Pisidium 651/m² in the lower lake; oligochaetes dominated the upper lake (1585/m²).

**Lake Creek macroinvertebrates (appendix).** Surveys in 1981 found much lower macroinvertebrate abundance above Twin Lakes (mean 10 per Surber sample) than below (mean 469). Dissolved copper above the lakes often reached the 0.01 mg/L criterion, likely limiting algae, bacteria and fungi and thus food for invertebrates.

## Key Findings

1. **Flushing dominance:** Above-average runoff produced very high inflow and turbidity, and the authors state that flushing influenced 1982 ecology more than in any earlier year.
2. **Powerplant effects on the lower lake:** Operation altered thermal structure, delayed stratification, weakened stratification, and increased winter DO, and the authors regard the powerplant as the main cause of the lower lake's changes.
3. **Reduced clarity:** Transmissivity fell an average of 17 percent after powerplant operation began.
4. **Plankton decline:** Phytoplankton and zooplankton densities were lower in 1982 than in 1981, consistent with flushing.
5. **Cadmium concern:** Cadmium at 1.85 µg/L (upper lake) and 0.50 µg/L (lower lake) on June 2, 1982, exceeded the 0.4 µg/L recommended upper limit. Two fish kills (45 dead fish on May 15 and 3 rainbow trout on June 17) in the tailrace were suspected to be cadmium-caused.
6. **Trophic status:** Both lakes remained oligotrophic.
7. **Lake trout habitat:** Temperatures at or below 10 °C across much of the water volume indicate continued suitability for lake trout.
8. **Recommendations:** Field studies continue through September 1985 to assess the full effect of Unit 2 and the new dam, with the authors emphasizing that 1982 must be considered when interpreting chemical and biological data.
