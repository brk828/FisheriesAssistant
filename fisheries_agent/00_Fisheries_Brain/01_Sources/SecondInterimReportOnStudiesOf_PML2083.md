# Razorback Sucker Larval Growth and Survival Under Experimental Pond Fertilization

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This interim report, the second of its series, documents an experiment on how pond fertilization affects the growth and survival of hatchery-reared [[Razorback Sucker]] larvae. Work was done at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH) in 1985 by Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Department of Zoology, Tempe, AZ) and Diana Papoulias, under the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Office of Endangered Species, Albuquerque, NM. The study compared three fertilization regimes (high, medium and low/none) in replicated ponds and tracked chlorophyll a, plankton and larval length and weight over an eight-week period. Fertilized ponds with developing algal populations produced more chlorophyll and faster larval growth than the unfertilized low treatment. Herbicide treatment with [[Igran Herbicide]] was used to control aquatic macrophytes late in the experiment.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is a large catostomid fish native to the Colorado River basin and is listed as endangered. Propagation of the species at hatcheries is part of its recovery effort. The report states that it is the second interim report on the studies, covering growth and survival of larvae in ponds at DNFH. Earlier results are not included in the text.

Larvae were produced by artificial fertilization of wild-collected eggs, which were incubated and hatched in early February. Fish were stocked into the ponds at about the fourth to fifth day after hatching (the OCR is partly illegible here). The study period ran from ponds being fertilized and filled in early February to draining and fish recovery in mid-April. The schedule (Table 1) lists eight weeks of activities, including fish spawning, hatching, swim-up, larval stocking, weekly chlorophyll/plankton/fish sampling, and herbicide treatment.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Pond design and treatments**
- Ponds were fertilized at high, medium and low (none) rates. Fertilizer rates were given as kg/ha values for the high and medium treatments; the low treatment received no fertilizer.
- Half of the total fertilizer for each treatment was applied at the start of the experiment, and the rest was added later, in a second fertilization period.
- Some ponds were treated with [[Igran Herbicide]] to suppress aquatic macrophytes, and the treatment was later followed by fertilization in some ponds.

**Sampling methods**
- Ponds were sampled weekly.
- Plankton was collected by raptor-style (dip-net) sampling from a small boat at each pond, and a light was used at night to attract larvae.
- Chlorophyll a was extracted with acetone and measured with a spectrophotometer as an index of relative [[Chlorophyll A]] concentration. Turbidity and phaeophytin measurements were not made because of technical failures and equipment limits.
- Fish were preserved in 5% buffered formalin with sugar added to prevent pigment degradation. Lengths were measured with an ocular micrometer on a dissecting microscope, and weights were measured on preserved specimens after drying and weighing on an analytical balance.
- Regression analyses of length and weight were performed on log-transformed data (Appendices C and D).

## Key Findings

1. **Chlorophyll response:** Chlorophyll peaked in high-treatment ponds during week 1, then declined gradually until week 7, when a slight increase was observed. Medium and low treatments showed the same general pattern. Only in week 1 was chlorophyll significantly greater in the high treatment than in the medium and low treatments. The report attributes the later decline in chlorophyll largely to increased macrophyte growth (data not provided).

2. **Growth divergence:** Length and weight differences between high and low treatments appeared by week 4. Length and weight differences between medium and low treatments began in week 6. Weight was significantly different between high and low treatments from week 4 onward, and between medium and low treatments at weeks 6 and 8. No significant difference was found between medium and high treatments in the displayed analyses.

3. **Growth rate:** Both high and medium treatments showed higher growth rates than the low treatment throughout the experiment, with the faster growth in fertilized ponds.

4. **Herbicide effect:** Ponds treated with [[Igran Herbicide]] were used to suppress aquatic macrophytes. Chlorophyll declines began before the herbicide treatment, so the report concludes that they were not caused by it.

5. **Regression models:** Log-log length-weight regressions were fitted for each treatment. Several fits are legible with high R² values (about .96 to .98), while others are lower (about .72 to .87). Regression equations are given for each treatment and for weight, total length and standard length (Appendices C and D).

6. **Interpretation:** The authors conclude that fertilization increased primary productivity, which is shown by the chlorophyll data, and that this increase supported higher larval growth. The conclusions are preliminary and are based on interim data.
