# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies: Native Fish and Tailwater Research Correspondence (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This compilation of 1991 documents from the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program covers four types of material: an information-request memo, a meeting-agreement letter, an Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) trip report on native fishes in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) and mainstream Colorado River, the June 1991 AGFD annual summary report, and letters on [[Humpback Chub]] propagation at the [[Page Springs Hatchery]]. The core theme is how operations of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] affect the tailwater food web, [[Rainbow Trout]] reproduction, and the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] in the LCR. The material documents research in progress, not final results.

## Background & Description

**Program context.** GCES Phase I research was funded by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] under a Cooperative Agreement with AGFD. Work was tied to Conservation Measure objectives under a [[Section 7 Consultation]] on dam operations. Other participants included [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[BIO/WEST]], the [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]], and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS). AGFD research was organized into three areas: (1) ecosystem processes and lower trophic levels, (2) trout, and (3) native fishes.

**Species of focus.**
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*): an endangered native cyprinid. Adults were collected in the LCR for egg collection and hatchery propagation.
- **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*): the dominant sport fish of the tailwater. Its diet depends heavily on the amphipod.
- **[[Gammarus lacustris]]**: an amphipod that is an important trout food item in the tailwater.
- Other fishes recorded at the LCR or its mouth: [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] (*Pantosteus discobolus*), [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), and one [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*, 650 mm TL).

**Study sites.** The main sites are the Glen Canyon tailwater from the dam to Lee's Ferry, the mainstream confluence zone near the LCR mouth, and the lower 22 km perennial reach of the LCR, which includes Salt Trail Canyon, Blue Springs, and the Triple Drop area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Flow regime.** Daily fluctuating and steady releases from the dam create stranding, desiccation, and displacement hazards. Above about 5,000 cfs, the document notes, there are essentially no permanent habitats or algal food resources for [[Gammarus lacustris]], and natural cobble substrates had not recolonized with algae since research flows began.

**Water chemistry in the LCR (March–April 1991).** The LCR is strongly influenced by the saline, mineral-rich [[Blue Springs]] base flow:
- Conductivity fell from 3630 µS/cm (12.5 km, March 29) to 2180 µS/cm (1.8 km, March 30), then rose to 2640 µS/cm by the last reading of the trip. Prior base-flow conductivity in the lower LCR averaged near 5000 µS/cm.
- Alkalinity was 484 mg/l as CaCO₃ at 12.5 km and 256 mg/l at 1.8 km. Field titrations may be less reliable because of high turbidity.
- Dissolved oxygen was 8.5–9.1 mg/l and pH 7.7–8.0. Water temperatures were 12.1–13.8 °C (March 30) and 12.4–15.9 °C (March 31) at the 1.8 km camp, and 18.3 °C at 12.5 km on the first morning.

**Hydrology.** The USGS Cameron gauge read a constant 228 cfs, probably because the gauge had silted in. Flow peaked at 474 cfs on March 29. Estimated lower-LCR discharge was 450–700 cfs over March 28–April 1, based on added Blue Springs base flow.

**Humpback chub movement.** Chub appeared to move back to the LCR mouth when water temperatures dropped on March 30. A sharp drop in catch rates at the 1.8 km camp followed, and nearly all large chub caught after that date were at the mouth.

## Key Findings

### 1. Trip report: LCR humpback chub collection (March 26–April 14, 1991)
- Approximately **50 humpback chub** were collected, **14 of which were recaptures**. **10 adults** (5 males, 255–358 mm TL; 5 females, 328–437 mm TL) were flown by helicopter to [[Page Springs Hatchery]] for propagation.
- All chub were taken at or below the 1.8 km camp. Two hoop nets and a trammel set below Triple Drop on March 28 caught no fish.
- Total lengths ranged from 69 to 446 mm and weights from under 10 g to 1037 g.
- Eggs were extrudable from only **one female** at the 1.8 km camp, and only a small proportion of males were ripe. No larval fish were seen in cursory examination, and no larvae were taken in larval seines. Drift samples were not yet analyzed.
- A recaptured female (374 mm TL) lost weight in captivity, from 521 g at capture to 486 g on April 15.
- Radio-tagged chub were detected near the LCR mouth. Radiotelemetry within the LCR proper was negative.

### 2. Annual summary report (June 28, 1991): key work items
- **Work Item 1.1 (nutrient and primary production budgets):** Eleven input–output sampling efforts had been completed. Analysis of particulate organic matter nutrients was delayed because the USGS lab could not reach the required detection limits. Primary productivity measurements were very limited.
- **Work Item 1.2 (Gammarus life history and mortality):** Mini-Ponar dredge samples were used to map amphipod distribution. A 24-hour trout diet sampling effort was combined with simultaneous drift sampling. The report notes that Ponar sampling is not equally effective across habitats and is not suitable for quantitative density estimates.
- **Work Item 1.3 (flow fluctuation and colonization):** Sandstone colonization substrates showed more algal biomass on accumulating sand than expected, suggesting that sand substrates may be a relatively stable primary-production source.
- **Work Item 1.4 (desiccation and algal nutritive quality):** Sluiceway experiments tested how desiccation affects algal biomass and chlorophyll. Nutritive quality analysis was contracted out.
- **Work Item 2.1 (trout spawning habitat):** Spawning bars at River Miles 4.0, 6.1, 8.2, and 14.0 were surveyed at 5,000 cfs. Redd counts were mapped during the spawning season. Background data noted that 27% of adult rainbow trout collected at Lee's Ferry in 1984–1986 were naturally produced.
- **Work Item 2.2 (trout stranding and mortality):** Ten major stranding sites were identified in the tailwater. Surveys tracked stranded trout, dead-fish condition, and water quality. Trout survival was difficult to measure because of low recapture rates.
- **Work Item 2.3 (trout age and growth):** Stocked trout were marked with external fluorescent pigment since 1989. Pigment retention was low, and an alternative marker was being explored.
- **Work Item 2.4 (trout energetics and movement):** Radio-tagged adult trout were tracked at different flow regimes, with habitat measured at each location.
- **Native fishes work items (3.1–3.6):** These covered mainstream and LCR larval fish collections, a propagation study of humpback chub, the role of tributary confluences, native fish habitat use in the Colorado River, and aging and thermal-mark studies of humpback chub.

### 3. Meeting agreements (September 3, 1991)
- FWS would focus microhabitat studies in two 500 m sections of the LCR, at Salt Trail Camp and Powell Canyon Camp.
- FWS would provide a written sampling protocol, and staffing needs would be assessed from it.
- Hoop nets would be checked every 12 hours, as stated in the FWS permits. ASU would assess whether a longer interval was justified.
- A procedure would be created to evaluate the impact of fish sampling on endangered and native fishes. This would be developed into a document for [[National Park Service]] review.

### 4. Propagation correspondence (1991)
- A request was made to bring Grand Canyon humpback chub into the propagation program, with the objective of producing offspring for later stocking.
- The [[Page Springs Hatchery]] (Arizona State Game Department) was approved for holding and propagating the species. Concerns were raised about coordinating with the Fish and Wildlife Service and about genetics.
- The correspondence also concerned the need for a USFWS collecting permit covering LCR and Grand Canyon fishes. Some collecting was tied to tribal and Navajo Nation approvals.

### 5. Document quality note
The source is a scanned compilation with many fragments, missing pages, and OCR errors. Several reports are marked preliminary, and some results (such as Gammarus density, larval fish analysis, and nutritive quality) were still pending when the documents were written.
