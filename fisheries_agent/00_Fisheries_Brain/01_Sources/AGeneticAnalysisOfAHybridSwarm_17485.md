# Genetic Analysis of a Hybrid Swarm Between Two Pupfishes (*Cyprinodon pecosensis* and *C. variegatus*): Geographical Pattern

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1987 Master of Science thesis from [[Oklahoma State University]] (Zoology, 45 pages, by Patrick J. Connor) uses an [[Allozyme Analysis]] survey to document hybridization between the endemic [[Pecos Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*) and the introduced [[Sheepshead Minnow]] (*C. variegatus*) in the Texas portion of the Pecos River. Four diagnostic [[Allozyme Loci]] were scored in 1,140 specimens from 18 localities. The study shows that a hybrid swarm, probably less than five years old, has replaced the native pupfish across more than 400 km of its historic range, and that natural selection appears to favor *C. variegatus*-like individuals.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** The Pecos pupfish is known only from the saline mid-reaches of the Pecos River and nearby springs and sinkholes in southeastern New Mexico and West Texas. Before 1984 it was the only pupfish recorded in the Pecos River proper.
- **Introduced species:** The sheepshead minnow is a widespread coastal species. Its introduction into the Texas Pecos River is described as producing genetic change that appears unprecedented in the hybridization literature.
- **Other pupfishes:** *C. bovinus* and *C. elegans* occur in isolated spring systems of the Pecos basin and had not been reported from the river.
- **Taxonomic context:** Most of the roughly 30 *Cyprinodon* species are allopatric. Divergent species share a diploid number of 48 with no known karyotypic differentiation, and the author notes that *C. pecosensis* and *C. variegatus* are generally regarded as belonging to the same species complex.
- **Prior introductions:** Two earlier accidental introductions of *C. variegatus* in the Pecos basin, at a reservoir–*C. elegans* contact zone and in Leon Creek with *C. bovinus*, also produced hybridization. A large eradication effort was carried out at the Leon Creek site.
- **Likely vector:** Stocking of sportfish, specifically [[Red Drum]] and [[Striped Bass]] from the 1960s onward, may have carried *C. variegatus* in with the stocked fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sampling:** Collections were made in March and August 1985 from 20 localities in the Pecos River drainage of Texas. Total sample was 4,150 specimens, with vouchers preserved and the rest frozen for electrophoresis. Reference samples came from [[Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge]] (*C. pecosensis*) and Galveston Bay (*C. variegatus*).
- **Laboratory methods:** Horizontal starch-gel electrophoresis was used on four presumptive loci: Adh-A, Est-1, Gpi-A and Pdp-A. Analyses used the BIOSYS-1 program. The character-index scored each specimen by allele, with –1 for *C. pecosensis* and +1 for *C. variegatus* alleles.
- **Spatial pattern:** Allele frequencies changed clinally downstream of Red Bluff Reservoir. The *variegatus*-allele frequency averaged 0.17 near locality 3 (about 1 km above the mouth of Salt Creek), peaked at 0.84 near Pecos, Reeves Co., and declined to about 0.40 roughly 175 km downstream. Abrupt changes coincided with [[Irrigation Diversion Dams]], which are 8–15 m high.
- **Habitat:** The author describes the Pecos River as relatively uniform across the study area, with no obvious environmental changes associated with the clines.
- **Conservation status:** *C. pecosensis* is classed as endangered. The only known "pure" populations are in New Mexico, at Salt Creek and at a gravel pit in Pecos Co., Texas.

## Key Findings

- **Extent of replacement:** Of 927 specimens from the Pecos River, only 31 (3.3%) had genotypes expected of "pure" *C. pecosensis*. The author estimates that about 430 river km of the Texas Pecos River has been replaced by a hybrid population.
- **Timing:** Hybridization probably began between 1980 and 1984. Pupfish collected in 1980 showed no *C. variegatus* influence, but collections in August 1984 at Pecos and Iraan did.
- **Origin and spread:** Clinal patterns point to an original introduction near Pecos, Reeves Co., with dispersal upstream and downstream. Irrigation-diversion dams appear to retard gene flow, especially upstream. The "gene-flow" explanation is favored over environmental heterogeneity.
- **Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium:** Of 72 single-locus tests, only three departed significantly (*p* < 0.05): Gpi-A at locality 14 and Pdp-A at localities 6 and 7. The author treats the populations as panmictic.
- **Character-index:** Scores at most localities were unimodal and broader than in the reference samples. Mode shifts matched the allele clines.
- **Linkage disequilibrium:** D′ values were predominantly positive (sign test χ² = 19.0, *P* < 0.005). The Est-1 × Gpi-A pair was markedly higher, with mean D′ of about 0.70, compared with an average of about 0.11 for the other pairs. Positive values persisted after removing Est-1 × Gpi-A (χ² = 11.8, *P* < 0.005).
- **Interpretation:** The author argues that natural selection maintains the Est-1 × Gpi-A disequilibrium, because the two loci's allele frequencies differ strongly, which tight linkage would not produce. The other pairs are assumed to assort independently.
- **Selection and sexual selection:** The author concludes that intense selection favors *C. variegatus*-like individuals over both *C. pecosensis* and intermediates. Possible mechanisms include [[Runaway Sexual Selection]] in this lek-breeding species, and females' apparent preference for heterospecific mates.
- **Genetic revolution:** Following [[Mayr]]'s (1954) framework, the author describes the change as a "genetic revolution" driven by hybridization.
- **Conservation recommendation:** The author calls for public education and measures to prevent further fish transport, given the endangered status of *C. pecosensis*.
