# First Record of the Mountain Mullet (*Agonostomus monticola*) in Louisiana

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Mugilidae

## Executive Summary
This 1956 paper by Royal D. Suttkus documents the **first record of the mountain mullet, [[Agonostomus monticola]], in Louisiana**, based on a single juvenile specimen (33 mm SL) collected from the Mississippi River at Point à la Hache, Plaquemines Parish, on 22 October 1950. Prior to this, the species' known U.S. range was limited to Florida (Carr and Goin, 1955), specifically Atlantic drainage streams and a west-coast population in Pinellas County. The author compares 21 total specimens from Mexico, Louisiana, Florida, and Puerto Rico to characterize meristic and morphometric variation, and corrects prior fin-ray counts reported by [[Carr and Goin 1955]].

## Background & Description
- **Mouth**: Terminal, *not* sucker-like (distinguishing feature from [[Mugil]] species).
- **Lips**: Thick, notably thicker than in *Mugil* spp.
- **Lower jaw**: Rounded, lacking the symphyseal knob present in *Mugil*; described as becoming subinferior with age per Meek (1914), though this trend was not clearly observed between the smallest (33 mm) and largest (106 mm) specimens examined.
- **Scalation**: Scales extend roughly one-third to halfway out onto the anal and second dorsal fins.
- **Fin ray counts** (all 21 specimens examined):
  - Dorsal fins: IV (rudimentary + principal rays), 9 soft rays in second dorsal fin
  - Anal fin: II (rudimentary + principal rays), 10 soft rays
  - Note: The rudimentary ray anterior to the soft rays in the second dorsal and anal fins was likely misidentified as a true spine in Carr and Goin (1955); this paper's counts (9 dorsal soft rays, 10 anal soft rays) contradict their published data.
- **Coloration** (fresh specimens, TU 251, Florida):
  - Bright yellow on the caudal fin above and below a central dark blotch
  - Yellow bases on the dorsal and anal fins
  - Consistent with descriptions by [[Evermann and Marsh 1900]]
  - The small Louisiana specimen (33 mm) was noted as a uniform dull color, initially misidentified as a species of *Mugil*
- **Size range examined in study**: 33–106 mm standard length (this paper's dataset); broader comparative material ranged up to ~106 mm SL among the 21 total specimens with detailed proportional measurements.
- **Fin condition (Louisiana specimen)**: Pectoral and pelvic fins were noted as deformed, rendering fin measurements atypical for that specimen.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat associations**:
  - Florida specimens (TU 251) were collected in fast water between two small waterfalls formed by limestone outcroppings, indicating a preference for lotic, rocky freshwater habitat — consistent with the common name "mountain mullet."
  - The Louisiana specimen was collected in **shallow marginal water of the Mississippi River**, a markedly different lowland, low-gradient habitat compared to the rocky stream habitats typical of the species elsewhere.
  - Proximity to marine influence: the Pinellas County, Florida site was only a few miles from Clearwater Bay, which connects to the open Gulf of Mexico, suggesting some tolerance for movement between fresh and brackish/marine systems — relevant to [[Diadromous Fish Migration]].
- **Geographic range**: Common throughout Mexico, Central America, and the West Indies; only recently documented in the continental United States at time of publication, restricted to Florida's Atlantic drainage and west coast, now extended to Louisiana via the Mississippi River drainage.
- **Taxonomic comparison**: Closely compared with [[Mugil]] (true mullets) due to superficial similarity, but distinguished by terminal (non-sucker-like) mouth, thicker lips, and jaw morphology.

## Key Findings
1. **First documented occurrence of *Agonostomus monticola* in Louisiana**, based on a single 33 mm SL specimen from the Mississippi River (Plaquemines Parish, 1950), extending the species' known U.S. range westward from Florida.
2. **Correction of meristic data** from Carr and Goin (1955): all 21 examined specimens consistently showed 9 soft rays in the second dorsal fin and 10 soft rays in the anal fin, rather than counts implying a true spine, likely due to misidentification of a rudimentary ray as a spine.
3. **Morphological confirmation** that *Agonostomus* is reliably distinguished from *Mugil* by mouth and jaw structure (terminal mouth, thick lips, rounded jaw without symphyseal knob) rather than relying solely on coloration or general body shape.
4. **Habitat plasticity** suggested by contrasting collection environments: fast rocky stream habitat in Florida vs. slow marginal river habitat in Louisiana, indicating the species may occupy a broader range of freshwater habitat types than previously assumed.
5. Specimens studied were sourced from a wide geographic range (Mexico: San Luis Potosí, Veracruz; Louisiana; Florida; Puerto Rico), supporting the description of population-level variation in proportional measurements and scale/fin ray counts, though these appear consistent across the species' range.
