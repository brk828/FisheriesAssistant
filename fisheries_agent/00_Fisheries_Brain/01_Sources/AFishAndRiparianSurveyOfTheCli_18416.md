# Fish and Riparian Survey of the Clifton Ranger District

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This draft final report, prepared by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] Nongame Branch for the [[US Forest Service]] (dated December 23, 1988), documents a fish and riparian survey of streams on the Clifton Ranger District of the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]] in east-central Arizona. Fieldwork (about 147 person-days, January, February and June 1988) covered 24 tributaries and mainstem rivers on 14 grazing allotments in the Blue River, Eagle Creek and San Francisco River drainages. Fish were sampled by [[Electrofishing]] and dip-netting, and riparian condition was described reach by reach with [[Riparian Area Scorecard]] ratings, photo points and tree-size data. The report pairs new surveys with historical collections from the [[University Of Michigan Museum Of Zoology]], [[Silvey And Thompson 1978]] and other studies.

## Background & Description

The Clifton District streams were described as among the least surveyed in Arizona, yet they hold important populations of threatened and endangered fishes (Table 1). The survey aimed to give managers information on fish distributions, abundances and riparian condition to support management, planning and project evaluation. Table 1 lists state and federal status for fishes found or potentially occurring on the district, including [[Gila Trout]] (endangered), [[Razorback Sucker]] (endangered), [[Apache Trout]] (threatened), [[Colorado Roundtail Chub]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] (threatened).

Methods included:
- Reach-based stream description, with reaches numbered downstream to upstream and perenniality judged by hydrophytic trees (alder, willow) and summer flow.
- Fish sampling with a Coffelt 12-V backpack electrofisher and two netters; CPUE expressed as catch per 100 seconds of actual shocking time.
- Population estimates from depletion samples using formulae of [[Everhart And Youngs 1981]].
- Voucher specimens preserved in 10% formalin and deposited at Arizona State University.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Riparian condition.** Reach descriptions cover substrate, gradient, terrace development, tree overstory, understory, shrub midstory and grazing damage. Upland species such as ponderosa pine and juniper invaded many terraces where flow was ephemeral. Common perennial-reach dominants included [[Alder]] (Alnus oblongifolia), [[Narrowleaf Cottonwood]], [[Sycamore]] (Platanus wrightii) and [[Boxelder]].

**Grazing impact.** The report concludes that riparian areas were heavily and negatively affected by cattle. Shrub and herbaceous layers were absent on many terraces, and concentration areas on Little Blue and Chitty Creeks were badly trampled. Reaches too rugged for cattle, such as Hannah Springs Creek and parts of Dark Canyon, retained intact vegetation and, in some cases, fish.

**Flow and barriers.** Fish distribution was mainly limited by permanence of water and barriers to upstream movement such as falls and drops. In tributaries, fish were usually found in lower reaches near the mouth or near springs.

**Distinctive habitat.** [[Arizona Cypress]] was found only in Dark Canyon and along Whitewater Creek, where it was common and some trees were very large.

**Water temperature** was recorded at many sites, ranging from about 6 °C (Turkey Creek, Sheep Wash area sites) to 23 °C (Horse Canyon, Whitewater Creek).

## Key Findings

**Fish assemblages**
- Of the three drainages, only Eagle Creek lacked tributaries with a solely native fish population; the San Francisco and Blue drainages together had 8 streams with no exotic species.
- [[Harden Cienega Creek]] was the most notable native-dominated stream, with 5 to 6 species. Its chub may be a state-threatened subspecies needing taxonomic study.
- [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were the most widespread species.
- No federally threatened or endangered species were taken in the tributaries.

**Key site results**
- Little Blue Creek (Alma Mesa, 19 June 1988): 120 speckled dace, mean CPUE 17.0 ± 5.0.
- Turkey Creek (6 Feb 1988): 469 longfin dace; population estimate 51 per 6 m of riffle.
- Blue River (Sandrock, 12 June 1988): 84 fish of 5 species, with 40 Gila sucker (48%) and 23 speckled dace (27%); the only non-native was rainbow trout (4, 5%).
- Hannah Springs Creek (14 June 1988): longfin and speckled dace in reproductive condition, the latter coloration noted as active.
- Sardine Creek (27 June 1988): longfin and speckled dace below the falls; no fish above the falls.
- Eagle Creek (Big Dry) recaptures of razorback sucker from prior experimental stockings.

**Threatened and endangered species**
- No federally listed species were taken in tributary streams.
- [[Spikedace]] was found at many Eagle Creek locations.
- [[Loach Minnow]] occurred in the Blue and San Francisco Rivers; a 1950 Eagle Creek collection suggests local extirpation.
- Recaptures of the re-introduced [[Razorback Sucker]] in Eagle Creek and the Blue River were described as encouraging for recovery.
- The trout in [[Chitty Creek]] may be [[Gila Trout]]; the report calls for definitive identification, as the trout were tentatively identified as Gila x rainbow hybrids and the taxonomy remained unresolved.

**Management conclusions**
- Riparian areas on the district were heavily degraded by livestock.
- Fish distribution tracks water permanence, barriers and grazing refuges.
- A century of agriculture and ranching has likely altered fish faunas, but historical data are too sparse to measure the change.
- The report provides photo points, compass readings and scorecard data for future monitoring (Appendices A and B).
