## Executive Summary

This document is a **Biological Opinion (BO)** issued under Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act, addressing the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Federal Aid support to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) for stocking catchable-size (8–10 inch) [[Rainbow Trout]] into three impoundments — Nelson Reservoir, Blue Ridge Reservoir, and Knoll Lake — within the Little Colorado River (LCR) basin. The consultation evaluates potential impacts of this non-native sport fish stocking program on the federally listed [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*) and its designated critical habitat. The Service concluded a finding of **"no jeopardy"** to the spinedace and **"no adverse modification"** of its critical habitat, contingent upon implementation of specific reasonable and prudent measures, monitoring terms, and an incidental take framework based on tagged trout recovery and predation evidence rather than direct quantification.

## Background & Description

- **Little Colorado Spinedace** (*Lepidomeda vittata*): A small-bodied native cyprinid endemic to the LCR basin; specific morphometric data not detailed in this document, but the species is characterized elsewhere by spines associated with its dorsal fin (genus *Lepidomeda* = "spinedace").
- **Rainbow Trout** (stocked fish): Hatchery-reared, catchable-size individuals measuring **8–10 inches**, marked with **coded wire tags** to enable tracking and identification of escapees from the stocked reservoirs.
- **Apache Trout** (*Oncorhynchus apache*): Referenced as a native alternative salmonid; no morphological description provided, but noted as a candidate for substitution in stocking programs to reduce ecological risk to native species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Little Colorado Spinedace**: Occupies stream habitats within the LCR watershed, including reaches connected to the affluent (inflow) and effluent (outflow) of the three studied reservoir systems. Its critical habitat is legally designated and subject to protection under the ESA.
- **Rainbow Trout (stocked)**: Introduced non-native predator occupying reservoir habitats; risk arises from potential migration/escape into connecting stream segments occupied by spinedace, where trout may act as a predator on the smaller native cyprinid.
- **Reservoir–Stream Connectivity**: The ecological risk pathway is explicitly tied to hydrological connection between each reservoir and its upstream/downstream stream segments ("reservoir system"), which serve as potential corridors for trout escape and subsequent spinedace predation.
- **Interspecific Interaction**: The central ecological concern is **predator-prey dynamics** between stocked rainbow trout (predator) and the native spinedace (prey), evaluated through stomach content analysis and tagged-fish recapture in spinedace-occupied habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Biological Opinion Outcome**: No jeopardy determination for the spinedace; no adverse modification determination for its designated critical habitat.
- **Incidental Take Trigger Thresholds** (per individual reservoir system):
  - More than **1 spinedace** found in the stomach contents of tagged trout, **OR**
  - More than **10 tagged trout** collected from affluent/effluent stream segments outside the reservoir.
  - Either threshold requires **reinitiation of consultation** for that specific reservoir system.
- **Reasonable and Prudent Measures**:
  1. Stocking timed to occur only when reservoir water levels are below spillway level to minimize trout escape risk.
  2. Only **hatchery-reared, coded-wire-tagged** catchable rainbow trout may be stocked (enables traceability).
  3. Stocking restricted to the summer fishing season (~Memorial Day to Labor Day).
  4. Mandatory **upstream/downstream stream surveys** at each reservoir to detect tagged trout movement into spinedace habitat; stomach content analysis required if tagged trout are found in occupied habitat.
  5. Annual reporting of monitoring results to the [[Arizona Ecological Services Office]] (AESO).
- **Conservation Recommendations** (discretionary, non-binding):
  1. AGFD to submit a list of actions to sustain/enhance recreational fishing while supporting spinedace recovery.
  2. AGFD to submit a list of actions necessary for spinedace recovery, aligned with the forthcoming official recovery plan.
  3. Evaluate feasibility of substituting native **[[Apache Trout]]** for non-native rainbow trout in suitable LCR watershed habitats to balance recreational and conservation goals.
- **Monitoring Framework**: Take assessment relies on indirect biological evidence (predation confirmation via stomach analysis, tagged trout recapture rates) rather than direct population-level quantification, reflecting the practical challenges of monitoring rare, small-bodied stream fish interactions with stocked reservoir predators.
