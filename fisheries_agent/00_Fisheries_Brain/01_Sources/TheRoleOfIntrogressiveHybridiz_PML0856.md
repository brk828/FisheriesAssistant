# Introgressive Hybridization in the Gila robusta Complex (Cyprinidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study uses mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) variation to test how far [[Introgressive Hybridization]] has shaped the [[Gila Robusta Complex]], a group of endemic cyprinid fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Lower-basin populations of *G. robusta*, *G. elegans* and *G. cypha* carried largely distinct mtDNA, with limited introgression of *G. elegans* into *G. cypha*. In the upper basin, by contrast, nearly all sampled fish carried *G. cypha* mtDNA, and no individual carried *G. robusta* mtDNA, even in morphologically "pure" *G. robusta*. The authors conclude that introgression is far more extensive than morphology suggested, predates human influence, and has decoupled mtDNA variation from morphological variation.

## Background & Description

The *Gila robusta* species group is an evolutionarily complex assemblage of seven taxa: *G. cypha*, *G. elegans*, *G. intermedia*, *G. nigra*, *G. robusta jordani*, *G. robusta robusta* and *G. seminuda*. Three allopatric lower-basin taxa (*G. intermedia*, *G. nigra* and *G. r. robusta*) could not be separated by allozyme or mtDNA characters, so they are treated collectively as "*G. robusta*" in this study. *G. robusta jordani* (endemic to the Pluvial White River) and *G. seminuda* (Virgin and Moapa rivers) are considered hybrid-origin taxa.

*G. cypha* and *G. elegans* are large (up to 500 mm), long-lived (>50 years) fishes that are morphologically distinct from the other taxa. Both are listed as endangered by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. *G. elegans* was once common in larger rivers but is now virtually extirpated from the upper basin, persisting mainly in [[Lake Mohave]]. *G. cypha* was historically abundant in canyon-bound reaches, though several populations have been lost.

Earlier work had shown that morphology and allozymes could identify fish in the lower basin, but that upper-basin morphology and molecular data both indicated hybridization. Because the species' rarity raised concern that human modification had increased hybridization, this study re-examined tissue samples from specimens previously analyzed morphometrically by [[McElroy and Douglas]] (1995).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling design.** Collection localities were divided into "allopatric" lower-basin sites, where the three species rarely co-occurred historically (e.g., [[Aravaipa Creek]], [[Lake Mohave]], and the [[Little Colorado River]] confluence), and "sympatric" upper-basin sites, where they co-occurred regularly. Upper-basin sites included [[Grand Canyon]]-adjacent reaches, [[Cataract Canyon]], [[Desolation Canyon]], [[Westwater Canyon]], the [[Yampa River]], [[Black Rocks]], the [[Gunnison River]] and [[Little Snake River]], among others. Source material is summarized here rather than listed in full.

**Genetic markers.** Species identity was assigned from mtDNA using restriction enzyme analysis of the cytochrome *b* gene (*cytb*, with enzymes AccI, HinfI and ScrFI) and single-stranded conformational polymorphism (SSCP) of *cytb* and [[ND2]] fragments. Sequence divergence between species pairs at *cytb* was roughly 49–63 substitutions, so *cytb* was chosen as the diagnostic locus. Results from individuals examined by multiple methods were concordant.

**Historical isolation.** The [[Pluvial White River]] population has been isolated from the mainstem for approximately 14,000 years, and the upper [[Little Colorado River]] for approximately 250,000 years. These intervals were considered too recent for mtDNA divergence yet old enough to exclude major modern-human effects.

**Hatchery context.** The stock at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]] is monomorphic for a single *G. elegans* haplotype, so several wild haplotypes are absent from captive broodstock.

## Key Findings

**Lower basin (allopatric) populations**
- All 40 *G. robusta* individuals (38 from six additional localities) carried *G. robusta* mtDNA.
- All 76 *G. elegans* individuals from Lake Mohave carried distinctive *G. elegans* haplotypes.
- Of 37 *G. cypha* from the Little Colorado River, 28 carried the common *G. cypha* haplotype, while three carried a *G. elegans* haplotype from Lake Mohave, indicating limited introgression.

**Upper basin (sympatric) populations**
- MtDNA haplotypes were determined for 384 upper-basin fishes: 50 carried *G. elegans* mtDNA and 334 carried *G. cypha* mtDNA.
- No individual carried *G. robusta* mtDNA, despite large samples of morphologically "pure" *G. robusta* (e.g., Debeque N=20, Rifle N=20, Gunnison River N=15, Pluvial White River N=14, Chevelon Creek N=8, San Juan River N=10, San Rafael River N=9).
- The 50 *G. elegans*-type fish included all 41 *G. seminuda* from the Moapa and Virgin rivers, plus a small number of individuals from Cataract Canyon, Westwater Canyon and Desolation Canyon.

**Hybrid taxa**
- *G. seminuda* carries *G. robusta* and *G. elegans* allozymes with *G. elegans* mtDNA, consistent with hybrid origin.
- *G. robusta jordani* shows a mosaic of *G. robusta* and *G. cypha* allozymes with *G. cypha* mtDNA. All eight additional specimens examined carried *G. cypha* mtDNA. The authors argue it persists as a separate hybrid taxon and should be recognized as *G. jordani*.

**Extent and origin of introgression**
- Introgression is "far too extensive" to be explained by recent human activity, because replacing *G. robusta* haplotypes would take much longer than humans have been in the region.
- Two explanations are considered: in situ introgression or post-glacial colonization by individuals of mixed ancestry, with the latter resembling patterns in *Mus musculus* and *Luxilus chrysocephalus*.
- Complete absence of *G. robusta* mtDNA in the upper basin may reflect founder effects, directional introgression, selection, or a combination.

**Genetic diversity and structure**
- *G. elegans*: 11 composite haplotypes from eight localities. Haplotype diversity ranged from 0 (Moapa River) to 0.83 (Westwater Canyon); nucleotide diversity reached a maximum of 0.0050 (Virgin River). Two divergent lineages exist, one widespread and one restricted to the Virgin River and Lake Mohave.
- *G. cypha*: 22 composite haplotypes from 15 localities (254 fish). Maximum nucleotide diversity was 0.002. Only the Pluvial White River and Chevelon Creek populations showed no variability.
- Among-population differentiation in *G. cypha* was significant (overall F_ST = 0.29, p<0.001). Among drainages (F_CT = 0.18, p=0.014) and among populations within drainages (F_SC = 0.14, p<0.001) were both significant.
- Grand Canyon and Chevelon Creek samples form a distinct group, differing from the rest by about 0.06% sequence divergence. Their pattern is attributed to a haplotype (AA) being absent from Grand Canyon and to Little Colorado River variants being transferred downstream.
- In the upper basin only, differentiation was low (F_ST = 0.12, p<0.001), driven mainly by widespread common haplotypes AA and AB.

**Morphology versus mtDNA**
- Morphological variation between *G. cypha* and *G. robusta* was largely not significant among morphotypes (F_CT = 0.05, p=0.15).
- MtDNA shows that introgression is much more extensive than morphology suggests. Virtually all specimens carried *G. cypha* mtDNA, distributed essentially at random across morphotypes.
- The authors conclude that morphology and mtDNA are "decoupled": mtDNA moves freely among *G. cypha* and *G. robusta*, while morphology is shaped by local environment.

**Conservation recommendations**
- Hybridization and local adaptation are both proposed as important evolutionary forces, with introgression potentially enhancing adaptation in shifting desert environments.
- Because several wild *G. elegans* haplotypes are missing from hatchery stock, the authors recommend adding diversity from wild *G. elegans* (including haplotypes found at Virgin River, Cataract Canyon and Lake Mohave) to existing broodstock.
- Distinguishing among the alternative explanations for the upper-basin pattern requires experiments on the fitness of different hybrid classes in different environments.
