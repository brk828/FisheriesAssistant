## Executive Summary

This 1985 letter from William G. Kepner (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Ecological Services, Phoenix) to Dr. W. L. Minckley of the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] compiles a chronological summary of aquatic inventories and native fish museum voucher records collected across seven major Arizona drainages between 1978 and 1984. The correspondence documents field survey efforts undertaken largely under BLM Technical Note publications and Bureau of Reclamation/USFWS cooperative studies, with voucher specimens deposited primarily at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The letter explicitly notes that, as of 1985, the **Agua Fria River is the only major western Arizona drainage lacking a detailed aquatic inventory** (a discrepancy with the drainage's own listed 1978/1983 records, likely referring to incomplete coverage). The document serves as a historical baseline record for native fish distribution in the Gila River basin prior to substantial declines from habitat alteration and nonnative species introductions.

## Background & Description

The letter is a survey/records compilation and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions of individual species. However, it identifies a consistent native fish assemblage recurring across multiple Sonoran Desert drainages, characterized by two dominant functional groups:

- **Small-bodied cyprinids**: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) and [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) — tolerant minnows adapted to intermittent, high-temperature desert streams.
- **Large-bodied catostomids (suckers)**: [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarkii*) and [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*) — benthic-feeding suckers occurring together in nearly every drainage surveyed.
- **Chub species** (Gila spp.): [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) recorded only in the Agua Fria drainage; [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) recorded in the Bill Williams, Hassayampa, and Verde drainages.
- **Cold/upland-adapted catostomids**: [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]] (*Catostomus discobolus*) and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*) recorded uniquely in the Paria River system, reflecting its higher-elevation, cooler-water Colorado Plateau character.
- **Euryhaline/marine-derived taxa**: [[Pacific Tenpounder]] (*Elops affinis*) and [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*) recorded in the Lower Gila River near its confluence with the Colorado River — indicating connectivity to estuarine/marine influence historically extending up the Colorado River system.
- **Spikedace** (*Meda fulgida*), a slender, small pelagic cyprinid, was recorded only in the Upper Verde River survey (1984), a system now recognized as one of its last strongholds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The surveys collectively describe native fish assemblages of Sonoran Desert and Colorado Plateau lotic systems, spanning a range of drainage types:

- **Agua Fria Drainage** (1978, 1983): Assemblage of longfin dace, speckled dace, desert sucker, Sonora sucker, and [[Gila Chub]], indicating perennial pool/riffle habitat supporting a full native guild.
- **Upper Bill Williams Drainage** (Big Sandy River, Trout Creek, Burro Creek, Santa Maria River, Alamo Lake; 1978–79): Longfin dace, speckled dace, [[Roundtail Chub]], desert sucker, Sonora sucker — documented in Kepner (1979), BLM Technical Note 352.
- **Hassayampa and Bill Williams Drainages** (Hassayampa River, Kirkland Creek, Date Creek, Bill Williams River; 1980): Same core assemblage as above, documented in Kepner (1980), BLM Technical Note 354.
- **Lower Gila River** (Salt/Gila confluence to Colorado/Gila confluence; 1981): Notably dominated by euryhaline species — [[Pacific Tenpounder]] and [[Striped Mullet]] — reflecting proximity to the Colorado River and historic tidal/estuarine influence from the Gulf of California prior to major flow alterations.
- **Middle Gila River** (Gila/Dripping Spring Wash confluence to Ashurst-Hayden Dam; November 1982): Longfin dace, desert sucker, Sonora sucker — documented in Kepner, Jakle, and Baucom (1983), a Bureau of Reclamation Arizona Projects Office study.
- **Paria River** (BLM Primitive Area; May 1982): A distinct, cooler Colorado Plateau assemblage of speckled dace, [[Bluehead Mountain Sucker]], and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] — vouchers cataloged (Nos. 9817–9835), documented in Kepner (1982) draft inventory.
- **Upper Verde River** (Sullivan Lake to Sycamore Creek confluence; September 1984): The most diverse assemblage recorded — longfin dace, [[Spikedace]], speckled dace, roundtail chub, desert sucker, and Sonora sucker — documented in Barrett, Kepner, Burton, and Jakle (1985), an Ecological Services/USFWS Phoenix Field Office study.

## Key Findings

- A **standardized core native assemblage** (longfin dace, speckled dace, desert sucker, Sonora sucker) recurs across nearly all Sonoran Desert tributaries of the Gila River basin surveyed between 1978–1984, suggesting broad historical distribution of these species prior to documented 20th-century declines.
- The **[[Agua Fria River]]** is flagged as lacking comprehensive detailed aquatic inventory data relative to other western Arizona drainages, despite having some voucher records — highlighting a data gap acknowledged by USFWS as of 1985.
- The **[[Lower Gila River]]** survey (1981) is ecologically distinct, recording only euryhaline/marine-associated taxa (Pacific tenpounder, striped mullet) rather than the desert-adapted cyprinid/catostomid guild found upstream, indicating strong influence from Colorado River connectivity near the confluence.
- The **[[Paria River]]** assemblage is unique among the surveys for containing Colorado Plateau-associated catostomids (bluehead mountain sucker, flannelmouth sucker) absent from all other listed drainages, reflecting its distinct hydrogeomorphology and cooler thermal regime.
- The **[[Upper Verde River]]** (1984) survey is the only one to document [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), now a federally listed species, providing an important historical distributional record predating its later range contraction.
- All voucher specimens were deposited at accredited institutions ([[Arizona State University]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), and most surveys were formalized in BLM Technical Notes or agency reports, establishing a traceable museum and literature record for future longitudinal comparison of native fish decline in Arizona's Gila River basin.
- The letter itself functions as a historical index connecting unpublished museum voucher data with formal agency technical reports, useful for reconstructing pre-1985 baseline distributions of Arizona's native fish fauna, several of which (Gila chub, roundtail chub, spikedace) are now protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
