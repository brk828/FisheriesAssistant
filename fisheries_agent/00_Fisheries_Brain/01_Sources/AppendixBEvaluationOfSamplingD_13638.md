# Evaluation of Sampling Design for Humpback Chub in the Colorado River, Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-cypha

## Executive Summary

This appendix to the [[Humpback Chub]] life history report evaluates the sampling design, gear performance, marking methods and handling-related injury used to study humpback chub in the [[Colorado River]] through [[Grand Canyon]], October 1990 to November 1993. The work was conducted by [[Bio West]] under a [[Bureau of Reclamation]] contract to assess effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The authors (Valdez, Masslich and Wasowicz) conclude that the stratified design was effective, that most gears captured the target age classes, that [[PIT Tags]] are the most reliable marking method, and that [[Radiotelemetry]] and surgical implantation carried the greatest risk of long-term harm.

## Background & Description

The study addressed humpback chub, a long-lived native cyprinid of the Colorado River. The report states that its life history was described in a companion Final Report, and that the methods are detailed in a separate Data Collection Plan (Valdez and Trinca 1995). Its purpose was to judge how effectively the design and gears sampled the fish community, how much effort was distributed, and how much injury handling caused. Sampling covered native fishes (humpback chub, [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]]) and non-native species such as [[Rainbow Trout]], common carp and brown trout.

Twenty-three gear types were used (15 passive, 8 active). The largest sampling categories were:

- **Gill and trammel nets:** 10,800 sets totaling 22,532 hours.
- **Minnow traps:** 4,562 sets totaling 85,111 hours.
- **Electrofishing:** 2,886 runs totaling 784 hours.
- **Seines:** 524 standard hauls, plus 8 sweeps with floated nets.
- **Hoop nets and angling:** small, supplementary efforts.

Total humpback chub processed by Bio West was 6,294.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spatial stratification.** The river was split into four regions (0, I, II and III), defined by biological production, water quality, fish distribution and tributary inflows, and further divided into 11 [[Geomorphic Reaches]] (Schmidt and Graf 1990). Region 0 (RM 1–56) received much less effort, being sampled in only 11 of 36 months. Netting and electrofishing effort was concentrated in Regions I and II, while trap and seine effort was concentrated in Region I. River conditions limited some gears: Region II's swift shoreline currents precluded seining, and steep gradients restricted net-set locations.

**Temporal and diel stratification.** Sampling was approximately proportional by season, except for seines, which were over-used in spring and fall. Diel sampling was weighted toward dawn and dusk, when humpback chub were most susceptible to capture. Four time blocks (night, dawn, day, dusk) were defined relative to sunrise and sunset, and adjusted seasonally. Gill and trammel nets were set for a maximum of 2 hours to limit the saturation effect on catch.

**Gear efficiency.** Efficiency was affected by season, water temperature, level, turbidity, fish behavior, morphology and personnel experience. Subadult minnow trap and seine catches were significantly higher in high turbidity. Electrofishing was less effective in clear or daylight conditions. The Grand Canyon electrode configuration (spherical anode and cathode near the surface) was less effective than a cable cathode system used elsewhere in the basin.

## Key Findings

**Sampling effort and distribution**
- Region 0 was under-sampled; each Region 0 stratum was sampled at least twice except RM 35.9–43.7, which was sampled once.
- Strata in Region I were visited 33 times (every trip) at RM 56.0–61.5 and 61.5–65.5 (LCR inflow).
- Net sampling covered 54%, 95%, 58% and 77% of 1-mile sections by region; electrofishing covered 67%, 95%, 75% and 89%.

**Gear performance**
- Trammel nets were about twice as effective as gill nets for adult humpback chub. The most effective gears for adults were 1.0- and 1.5-inch trammel nets (50 and 75 ft).
- Electrofishing captured 2,177 humpback chub (1,272 YOY, 767 juveniles, 138 adults). It was the only gear capturing large numbers of all age classes.
- Minnow traps caught 928 subadults at 1.1 fish/100 hr, with 98% in Region I. Escapement was observed, so traps were checked every 12 hours from 1993.
- Seines captured 1,385 humpback chub, 98% in Region I.
- Length-frequency analysis was bimodal, with peaks at 60 mm and 380 mm TL, and a gap between 150 and 300 mm.

**Catch composition**
- Nets (7,167 fish): rainbow trout 38%, humpback chub 24%, flannelmouth sucker 23%.
- Hoop nets (936 fish): bluehead sucker 39%, flannelmouth sucker 30%, rainbow trout 22%.
- Minnow traps (1,349 fish): humpback chub 69%.
- Seines (3,682 fish): humpback chub 40%.
- Electrofishing (14,971 fish): rainbow trout 53%, humpback chub 15%, common carp 13%.

**Marking**
- About 8,500 humpback chub were PIT-tagged; PIT tag loss was under 1% (4 of 8,500). Condition factor did not differ significantly between recaptured and initial fish.
- Of 78 radio-tagged fish, 23 (29%) were recaptured.
- 1,249 YOY and juvenile humpback chub were fin-punched; 12 were recaptured, and fin regeneration was documented in two.
- Flannelmouth suckers: 1,071 PIT-tagged, 176 (16%) recaptured. Bluehead suckers: 394 tagged, 13 (3%) recaptured.

**Injury and mortality**
- Of 6,294 humpback chub processed, 17 died incidentally and 2 were sacrificed.
- Nets: 2 direct humpback chub mortalities; 148 of 2,754 rainbow trout (5%) were known dead.
- Electrofishing: 93 of 11,543 fish (0.8%) showed external effects. Humpback chub had 6 such cases; 4 humpback chub died. 39 of 7,977 rainbow trout died.
- Minnow traps: 7 humpback chub died, cause undetermined.
- [[Radiotelemetry]]: known mortality 1 of 75 (1.3%). Incisions or antenna exits were in fair or poor condition in 12 of 32 recaptures (38%). Weight decreased in 26 of 30 recaptures (87%).
- [[Surgical Procedures]] were refined over the study. The [[Sheathed Needle Antenna Guide]] (SNAG) and absorbable Maxon sutures reduced infection; antenna exits with mosquito forceps were the main source of infection.
- [[Stomach Pumping]]: one adult (406 mm TL) died in September 1992 from intestinal blockage. Of 15 pumped fish recaptured, mean weight change was −39 g, attributed to spawning timing. Asian tapeworms were expelled from 6 (3.6%) chubs.

**Recommendations**
- Continue stratified random sampling by geomorphic reach and longitudinal stratum, and integrate with GIS.
- Use four diel time blocks and sample winter and spring runoff, which are usually undersampled.
- Use PIT tags for native fishes (minimum about 150–175 mm TL), with thorough operator training.
- Avoid fin punching in large-scale studies; limit it to small, short-term work.
- Use lateral incisions, no incision at the antenna exit, the SNAG device and absorbable sutures in any future radiotelemetry, with caution given its time and personnel demands.
- Restrict stomach pumping to experienced personnel, since excessive pressure is harmful.
