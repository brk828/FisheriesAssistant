# Metabolism of an Arid Region Canal Ecosystem

#aquatic-ecology #freshwater-ecology #ecosystem-metabolism #canal-ecology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This study by Marsh and Fisher (1987, *Journal of Arid Environments*) estimates whole-system and component oxygen metabolism in the [[Arizona Canal]], a 12-km, concrete-lined irrigation canal near [[Phoenix Metropolitan Area]], Arizona. The canal was measured on four dates in 1983 using diel dissolved-oxygen curves, with component rates from algae, [[Aquatic Macrophytes]], sediments and water. Mean gross primary production (Pg) was 6.70 g O₂/m²/day and ecosystem respiration (R) was 5.82 g O₂/m²/day, giving a Pg/R of 1.16, so the canal was autotrophic. Epiconcretic algae dominated production, and the study also tested whether longitudinal changes in a physically uniform channel could be attributed to biotic factors. Only downstream nitrate decline could be clearly linked to biotic uptake. The document does not study fish; it mentions the fish community only in passing.

## Background & Description

Canals are major flowing-water features in the Southwestern U.S. deserts. The text reports more than 11,000 km of canals, about 2,000 km of them in the Phoenix area, compared with about 6,000 km of natural rivers and streams in Arizona. Canals are built to move water for agricultural, industrial, municipal and domestic use, and they are morphometrically uniform to maximise conservation and delivery efficiency. The authors argue that this uniformity makes canals a useful model for testing causes of longitudinal pattern in lotic systems, since the physical template is nearly constant.

The study reach is the Arizona Canal, immediately downstream of [[Granite Reef Diversion Dam]] on the [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]]. Key morphometry includes:

- Bottom width averaging 16.5 m (range 13.2–19.2 m) and a mean sidewall angle of 45°.
- At modal summer discharge (about 34 m³/s), mean depth of 1.7 m, surface width of 19.2 m and velocity of 1.15 m/s.
- Total submerged surface area of 25.35 ha, of which 22.1% is sidewall and 77.9% bottom.

Water quality varied with the mix of influent from the two rivers. Salt River water is higher in conductance and ions than Verde River water. During the study year, canal water was slightly basic (pH c. 7.5), conductance ranged from 250 to 835 µS/cm, and nitrate-N ranged from 0 to 125 µg/L. Water temperatures ranged from about 10°C to 30°C.

Sediments covered only 14% of the bottom area. Macrophytes included [[Potamogeton Pectinatus]], [[Potamogeton Crispus]] and [[Zannichellia Palustris]]. Concrete surfaces were colonised by [[Cladophora Glomerata]] and epiconcretic diatoms. The invertebrate fauna was sparse and dominated by collector-gatherers and filterers, including the introduced [[Asiatic Clam]] ([[Corbicula Fluminea]]), which reached several thousand individuals per m² in some areas. The canal has a diverse and abundant fish community, several species of which are angling quality, but there is no sport fishery. The authors did not assess benthos or fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Metabolism was estimated with the diel dissolved-oxygen method, using modified Winkler titrations at stations A, C and E (0, 5 and 12 km downflow of the dam). Component metabolism was measured separately:

- **Algae:** in situ light and dark chambers on colonised ceramic tiles.
- **Macrophytes:** light and dark bottle incubations of leaves.
- **Sediments:** dark in situ cores during canal draw-down.
- **Water column:** light and dark bottles, which showed no measurable activity.

A concurrent 12-day check in November 1983, using about 3,000 coverage observations along 75 transects, tested whether summed component rates matched whole-system rates. The authors' framework used Pg and R values combined with area weighting to scale component rates to the whole reach.

Nutrient and physical context is also important. Canal flow is nearly uniform, lacks tributary inputs and has no riparian vegetation. Maintenance dredging occurs one to three times a year, and the canal is drained for about four weeks each November–December. Regional precipitation averages 23.4 cm/year, and summer air temperatures can exceed 43°C.

Comparison with other Sonoran Desert systems places the canal's metabolic rates within the intermediate-high productivity category. Ecosystem rates were only slightly lower than those in [[Sycamore Creek]] and Pinto Creek, but the canal differs from them physically, with more macrophytes and no flood disturbance.

## Key Findings

- **Whole-system metabolism:** Pg averaged 6.70 g O₂/m²/day and R averaged 5.82 g O₂/m²/day over four dates. Pg/R averaged 1.16 (range 0.93–1.69), so the canal was autotrophic and exported or accumulated organic matter.
- **Seasonality:** Pg was highest in June and fell to a minimum in November. R was highest in July and was low in November. The authors attribute these patterns mainly to differences in solar radiation.
- **Component contributions:** Epiconcretic algae contributed more than 80% of Pg and more than 50% of R (55% in the abstract). Macrophytes contributed about 20% of Pg and 15% of R. Sediment respiration accounted for about 30% of R.
- **Algal metabolism:** Algal Pg/R was consistently high, with a mean of 2.84 (SD 0.59; N = 18), confirming strong autotrophy. Algal rates did not vary consistently with depth, location or colonisation period.
- **Macrophyte metabolism:** Macrophyte Pg/R was 2.58. Standing crop averaged 154.2 g dry mass/m² (SD 105.3; N = 20). Tip leaves of *P. pectinatus* had higher specific metabolism than mid-section leaves.
- **Sediment respiration:** R averaged 1.70 g O₂/m²/day (SD 1.08; N = 12) and was entirely heterotrophic. This is about half the deep-sediment respiration reported for Sycamore Creek.
- **Summation mismatch:** Summed component Pg was higher and summed R lower than whole-system values. Summed Pg/R (1.40–2.50) exceeded the November whole-system value (0.88–1.02). The authors attribute the gap mainly to underestimated sediment respiration or unaccounted components, such as roots and Corbicula, rather than overestimated algal or macrophyte production.
- **Nitrate decline:** NO₃-N declined downstream on 23 of 24 dates. The average reduction at station E was 43% of station A concentrations. This was the only parameter with a consistent longitudinal pattern independent of physical change.
- **Phosphorus and ammonia:** Soluble reactive phosphorus fell significantly downstream only on 26–27 July (by 38%). Ammonia-N showed no longitudinal pattern.
- **Biotic versus physical control:** The distribution of macrophytes followed physical features, especially bends. Algal community shifts reflected both biotic and physical factors, and the authors could not separate their relative effects. Even small morphological changes appear able to produce biotic responses.
- **Future work:** The authors suggest that newer, more uniform canals, such as those of the [[Central Arizona Project]] under construction, could provide a better test of longitudinal-change hypotheses than the older Arizona Canal.
