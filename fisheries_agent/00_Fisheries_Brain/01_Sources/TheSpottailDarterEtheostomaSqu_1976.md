## Executive Summary
This 1976 note re-examines the historical and contemporary status of the [[Spottail Darter]] (*Etheostoma squamiceps*) in Indiana, a species first documented in the state by [[Jordan 1890]] at two Posey County localities. Despite intensive collecting efforts by numerous subsequent authors over more than 80 years, no new Indiana records were published until this study. A 1974 field survey of 18 sites in Posey County relocated the species at only two localities, each yielding a single specimen, confirming its continued but extremely rare presence. The authors attribute this rarity to stream habitat degradation—siltation, agricultural and oil pollution, and loss of slab-stone substrate—and recommend the species be added to Indiana's list of rare and endangered species due to its precarious, geographically restricted population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat substrate dependency**: The [[Spottail Darter]] relies on **slab stones** for both cover and nesting sites (per [[Page 1974]]), a critical microhabitat requirement.
- **Stream type**: Small tributary and headwater creeks with rocky/slab substrate, as opposed to silt/sand-bottomed streams.
- **Habitat degradation factors**:
  - Severe **siltation** burying slab-stone substrate
  - **Agricultural pollution** runoff
  - Sporadic **oil pollution**
  - **Stream desiccation**, possibly linked to lowered water tables during droughts, especially in upstream headwater areas
- **Range**: Southern Illinois, western Kentucky, west-central Tennessee, the Tennessee River drainage of Alabama and Mississippi, and southwestern Indiana (Posey County) — representing the species' range periphery/disjunct northern extent in Indiana.
- **Population structure**: Small, isolated, and seemingly restricted to marginal habitat in Indiana, with limited capacity for recolonization or population replenishment due to poor stream conditions preventing a sustaining "reservoir" population.

## Key Findings
1. **Historical records**: [[Jordan 1890]] first recorded *E. squamiceps* in Indiana at two Posey County sites: Gresham's Creek (1.5 mi E New Harmony) and Black River (New Harmony).
2. **Citation errors propagated**: [[Eigenmann and Beeson 1894]] erroneously substituted "Big Creek" for "Black River" when citing Jordan's records; this error was subsequently repeated by [[Blatchley 1938]].
3. **Museum specimen records**: [[Page 1974]] mapped two additional Indiana localities based on preserved specimens:
   - Rush Creek tributary, 3 km S New Harmony (INHS, 12 specimens, collected 29 March 1963)
   - Poseyville, Posey County (FMNH 43952, 5 specimens, collected 28 April 1942)
4. **No new field observations** were published between 1890 and 1974 despite multiple statewide fish surveys (Hay 1894; Meek 1908; Gerking 1945, 1955; Nelson and Gerking 1968), even though Gerking (1945) noted the region was "intensively collected."
5. **1974 resurvey results**: Of 18 sites visited in Posey County (including all historically documented localities), spottail darters were found at only **two** sites:
   - Tributary, Rush Creek, 3 km S New Harmony
   - Tributary, Black River, 3 km W Stewartsville
   - Only **one specimen** was collected at each site, indicating extreme rarity.
6. **Causes of rarity**: Attributed primarily to (a) degraded stream conditions (siltation, agricultural/oil pollution) and (b) scarcity of slab stones needed for cover and nesting substrate.
7. **Conservation recommendation**: Authors conclude the species is unlikely to persist long-term in Indiana given habitat trends and recommend formal listing as a state rare and endangered species.
