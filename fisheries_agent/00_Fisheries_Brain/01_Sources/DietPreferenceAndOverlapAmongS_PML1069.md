# Diet Preference and Overlap Among Small Native and Non-Native Fishes in Backwaters of the Colorado River in Grand Canyon

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study examines the diets of early life stages of native and non-native fishes captured in [[Backwater Habitat]]s of the [[Colorado River]] in [[Grand Canyon]]. Backwaters matter for native fish early life stages because [[Glen Canyon Dam]] alters water temperature in the river. The authors measured [[Prey Preference]] and [[Diet Overlap]] among larval (<30 mm) and juvenile/small (30–120 mm) fishes. Diets were broadly similar across species, but they differed in breadth and in how much they overlapped, with non-native species potentially influencing how native fishes use backwaters.

## Background & Description

- **Study system:** Backwaters of the Colorado River in Grand Canyon, a river regulated by Glen Canyon Dam.
- **Focal species:** Native fishes including [[Humpback Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]] and [[Speckled Dace]]; non-native fishes including [[Plains Killifish]] and [[Fathead Minnow]].
- **Size classes:** Larval (<30 mm) and juvenile/small (30–120 mm) individuals.
- **Rationale:** Backwaters are particularly important nursery habitat for early life stages of native fishes because of dam-altered water temperatures.
- **Sample sizes:** The abstract does not report the number of fish or prey samples analyzed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Prey availability:** The most available prey were [[Copepods]], oligochaetes, [[Chironomid Larvae]], nematodes and ostracods.
- **Commonly consumed prey:** [[Chironomid Larvae]] and pupae were eaten most often by all species, but were not ranked highly in preference.
- **Preferred prey:** Most fishes preferred [[Simuliid Larvae]] and pupae, bivalves, ceratopogonid pupae, tipulid larvae, gastropods, amphipods and chironomids over other prey.
- **Interspecific influence:** The authors suggest that [[Non-Native Fishes]] may affect both the diet and the use of backwaters by native species.

## Key Findings

- **Dietary breadth:** Humpback chub consumed the widest variety of prey. Plains killifish and fathead minnow had the most restricted diets.
- **Greatest diet overlap:** Fathead minnow–humpback chub, fathead minnow–plains killifish, humpback chub–plains killifish, and fathead minnow–bluehead sucker.
- **Least diet overlap:** Flannelmouth sucker–bluehead sucker, speckled dace–bluehead sucker, humpback chub–flannelmouth sucker, flannelmouth sucker–speckled dace, and flannelmouth sucker–plains killifish.
- **Life-stage pattern:** The diet of larval native fishes overlapped more with that of other native fishes. The diet of juvenile native fishes overlapped more with that of non-native fishes.
- **Management implication:** Non-native fish presence may alter how native fishes feed and use backwater habitats, which is relevant to native fish recovery in regulated reaches.
