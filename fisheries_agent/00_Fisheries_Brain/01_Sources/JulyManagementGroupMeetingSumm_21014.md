# Colorado River Recovery Program Management Group Meeting Summary, July 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) Program Director John Hamill transmits the summary of the July 13-14, 1989 meeting of the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] Management Group, held at the FWS Regional Office in Lakewood, Colorado. The meeting approved a revised FY-90 Work Plan, reviewed the status of the FY-89 work plan, and addressed water rights acquisition, [[Section 7 Consultation]] and [[Biological Opinion]] matters aimed at recovering endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin. Central themes were securing instream flows in the [[Yampa River]] and the [[15-Mile Reach]], and using [[Windy Gap Funds]] for [[Water Rights Acquisition]].

## Background & Description

The Recovery Program coordinates federal, state, water-user and conservation partners to protect endangered fishes, including the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Bonytail]]. The document is primarily a management and budget record rather than a research study. It includes:

- Meeting minutes and attendee list (about 19 attendees from FWS, the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]] (CWCB), Utah, Colorado, Wyoming, [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC), and environmental groups).
- The FY-90 Work Plan tables, dated July 6 and July 21, 1989.
- Attachments on the Ruedi Reservoir public meeting, Yampa River Phase II flow recommendations, the Little Snake River water rights proposal, the Cross Mountain Ranch evaluation, and the Price-San Rafael salinity project.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Yampa River:** Designated the first priority for water rights acquisition because of its biological importance to endangered fishes. Yampa Phase I was completed May 1, 1989.
- **Little Snake River:** Contributes roughly 350,000 to 400,000 acre-feet (AF) per year to the Yampa and Green rivers. Under Article IX of the 1948 Upper Colorado River Basin Compact, Colorado and Wyoming share this undeveloped supply equally. Senior conditional rights held by Pittsburgh and Midway Coal Mining Company (P&M), totaling 150,000 AF in Colorado plus a 22,865 AF Wyoming permit, could consume at least 100,000 AF, so any junior instream right is insecure.
- **15-Mile Reach and Ruedi Reservoir:** The reach is the focus of flow protection. The proposed conservation releases of 10,000 AF from Ruedi would raise Fryingpan River flows to about 150 cfs (from about 110 cfs).
- **Fryingpan River trout habitat:** Projected effects were modest. Wadable area fell about 3.5 percent (62,221 to 59,926 square feet per 1,000 feet of river). Juvenile rainbow trout habitat could drop up to 4.5 percent and juvenile brown trout habitat about 3 percent, which Reclamation judged insignificant.
- **Nonnative species:** The Colorado Wildlife Council did not vote on Utah's proposal to introduce [[Rainbow Smelt]] into [[Lake Powell]] and asked for review with FWS and the National Park Service.
- **Yampa oil spill:** [[Amoco]] is funding studies of residual impacts. No immediate impact had been detected.

## Key Findings

### 1. FY-90 Work Plan
- The Technical Group's FY-90 Work Plan was revised by the Management Group. The Table 3.1 version totaled **$2,825.5K**, and the July 21 revised version totaled **$2,757.4K**.
- The FWS share in the revised plan was about $623.7K, Bureau of Reclamation about $1,556.5K, Colorado about $486.8K, Utah about $66.4K, and Wyoming $24.0K.
- Category subtotals in the July 21 table: Habitat Management $924.4K, Stocking $660.4K, Res. Monitoring/Data Management $503.5K, Other $129.5K, Nonnative Fish/Sportfish $127.3K, Program Management $362.3K, Habitat Development $50.0K.
- Major changes included: Yampa Basin Reservoir feasibility study cost shared (Colorado $50.0K listed, plus $56.2K credit requested by CWCB); Yampa flow recommendations increased by $23.3K for October-June provisional flows; deletion of the Grand Valley imprinting study and razorback pond work; addition of $37.4K for Kenney Reservoir sportfishery; addition of $66.0K for the Flaming Gorge tailwater trout study; and a $5.0K FWS contribution to the Razorback Management Plan.

### 2. Section 6 Allocation
- The Service's $200.0K of Section 6 funds for FY-90 (if appropriated) was allocated to projects with totals of **$97.8K for Utah** and **$101.5K for Colorado**. The largest items were Standardized Monitoring ($35.6K Utah, $18.2K Colorado) and Fish Interactions ($72.3K).

### 3. Funding Constraints
- Utah lacked FY-90 funds beyond roughly $10,000 in matching money. A $73.0K legislative request was expected to be decided in January 1990, with funds available July 1990.
- Congress: a request to add $324.0K to the Service's Recovery Program budget had cleared the House Appropriations Committee.

### 4. Ruedi Reservoir Releases
- A public meeting on July 7, 1989, in Glenwood Springs drew about 25 people, mostly from Pitkin County. Concerns focused on dam operation priorities (water sales and endangered fishes versus boating and trout fishing) rather than flow impacts.
- Proposed release: 10,000 AF over 62 days starting August 15. Reservoir levels were projected at about 95,000 AF near Labor Day, above the 85,000 AF minimum the [[Forest Service]] considers good for recreation.
- A lease among Grand Valley water users, the Department of the Interior, and CWCB was under review. Issues included the legal basis (a possible declaratory judgment), the 40-year lease maximum, and Green Mountain Reservoir decree limits. CWCB action is expected September 21-22, 1989. As a fallback, a preliminary notice was issued for an instream flow right of 168 cfs in the 15-Mile Reach.

### 5. Little Snake River Water Rights Evaluation
- The Nature Conservancy's revised proposal, with a cap of **$37,500**, covers review of existing documentation ($7,500), additional operation studies ($15,000), and a market report on P&M rights ($7,500; a $7,500 increase was added for a fuller appraisal). The base budget was $30,000.
- The work was approved with conditions: staged work so appraisal proceeds only if hydrology shows instream flow benefits, consideration of alternative uses, and technical oversight by a panel including Kuhn, Wigington, Jencsok, G. Smith and Alan Mauzy (WY).

### 6. Cross Mountain Ranch
- The Lily Park property on the lower Yampa River comprises 27,000 acres and about **4,500 AF** of senior agricultural water rights (April-September use). Estimated purchase cost was about **$300,000**, with an independent appraisal costing about $15,000.
- The Management Group unanimously recommended that TNC obtain an appraisal and attempt to purchase the water rights.

### 7. Section 7 and Biological Opinions
- **Muddy Creek:** The draft opinion said water rights acquisition only in the impacted 15-Mile Reach could relieve Muddy Creek's obligation. The Service agreed to consider progress in other basins when finalizing the opinion and MOU, while stressing protection in both the Colorado and Green (including Yampa) basins.
- **Sandstone:** A draft was due end of July 1989. It relies on Wyoming's commitment not to use 20,000 AF of firm yield for six years. After six years, the Service would evaluate Recovery Program progress and possibly reinitiate consultation on a $10/AF charge.
- **Price-San Rafael Salinity Project:** Under Section 7 consultation. The Soil Conservation Service would approach Utah about legally protecting accretions from the Uintah Basin Salinity Project as an offset.
- **Flaming Gorge:** The draft consolidated biological report was on schedule; the draft Hydrology report was about three weeks late. The draft biological opinion remains due March 15, 1990.

### 8. Yampa Phase II Flow Recommendations
- Phase II steps A through C are due October 1, 1989; step D by November 15, 1989; and step E (detailed scopes of work) by February 1, 1990. Interim flow recommendations were targeted for fall 1989.

### 9. Upcoming Dates
- Implementation Committee meeting: August 30, 1989.
- Next Management Group meeting: September 13, 1989.
- CWCB Board action on Ruedi lease: September 21-22, 1989.
