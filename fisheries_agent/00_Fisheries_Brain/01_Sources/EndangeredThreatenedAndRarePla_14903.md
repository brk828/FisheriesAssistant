## Executive Summary

This 1986 paper by Warren et al., published in the *Transactions of the Kentucky Academy of Science*, presents a revised and updated statewide list of rare, threatened, and endangered plants and animals in Kentucky, produced jointly by the Endangered Species Committee of the Kentucky Academy of Science (KAS) and the Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission (KNPC). The list updates a 1981 predecessor (Branson et al.) and includes 337 plant taxa and 242 animal taxa assigned conservation statuses (Endangered, Threatened, Special Concern) at the state level, cross-referenced with federal status categories (Endangered, Threatened, Category 1, Category 2) under the U.S. Endangered Species Act. Of direct ichthyological relevance, the paper identifies approximately **31% of Kentucky's native fish fauna** as falling into an Endangered, Threatened, or Special Concern category — one of the highest proportional impact rates among all taxonomic groups surveyed (second only to pelecypods/freshwater mussels at 36%). The fish list itself enumerates roughly 68 species/subspecies spanning lampreys, sturgeons, gars, minnows, suckers, topminnows, darters, madtoms, sunfishes, and cavefishes.

## Background & Description

The list was compiled using The Nature Conservancy's standardized Natural Heritage Program methodology, incorporating criteria such as the number, recency, and accuracy of occurrence records; historic versus present geographic distribution; habitat requirements; relative threat of habitat destruction; and ecological fragility. Data were current as of June 30, 1985, and the list underwent peer review before approval by the KAS at its 71st Annual Meeting (November 9, 1985, Morehead, Kentucky).

Fish nomenclature followed [[Bailey Et Al 1970]], [[Carney 1985]], [[Robins Et Al 1980]], and [[Stauffer Et Al 1982]]. Three non-regulatory state-level status categories were applied:

- **Endangered (E)** — in danger of extirpation/extinction throughout all or a significant part of its Kentucky range.
- **Threatened (T)** — likely to become endangered in the foreseeable future.
- **Special Concern (S)** — warrants monitoring due to limited range, vulnerability to habitat change, biological sensitivity, or insufficient data.

These were cross-tabulated against federal designations: Endangered (E), Threatened (T), Category 1 (C1, sufficient data to support listing), and Category 2 (C2, possible concern but inconclusive data).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish taxa listed inhabit a broad range of Kentucky freshwater habitats, including:
- **Large river systems** (e.g., Ohio, Mississippi, Cumberland, Tennessee drainages) — home to species like [[Lake Sturgeon]], [[Pallid Sturgeon]], [[Blue Sucker]], and [[Alligator Gar]].
- **Cave and karst/groundwater systems** — represented by troglobitic and troglophilic species such as [[Northern Cavefish]] and [[Southern Cavefish]], reflecting Kentucky's globally significant karst landscape (e.g., Mammoth Cave region).
- **Small headwater streams and springs** — habitat for numerous darter (*Etheostoma*, *Percina*) and madtom (*Noturus*) species with narrow endemic ranges, such as [[Blackside Dace]] and [[Ashy Darter]].
- **Sluggish lowland waters, swamps, and oxbows** — supporting species like [[Swamp Darter]], [[Cypress Minnow]], [[Lake Chubsucker]], and [[Central Mudminnow]].

Threats implicitly underlying these designations include habitat destruction, stream impoundment, siltation, and range restriction — consistent with the broader Endangered Species Act rationale cited in the paper regarding aesthetic, ecological, and scientific value of imperiled species and the ecosystems that support them. Several fish species overlap federal listing status (e.g., Category 1/2 review taxa), signaling early recognition of the vulnerability of Kentucky's endemic and disjunct fish populations, particularly those tied to the Cumberland Plateau and karst regions.

## Key Findings

- **Overall impact**: ~31% of Kentucky's native fish species were designated Endangered, Threatened, or Special Concern under the 1986 KAS-KNPC list — comparable to reptiles/amphibians (23%) and mammals (33%), and second only to pelecypods (freshwater mussels, 36%).
- **Cavefish species**: [[Northern Cavefish]] (*Amblyopsis spelaea*, S/C2) and [[Southern Cavefish]] (*Typhlichthys subterraneus*, status listed) highlight Kentucky's unique subterranean ichthyofauna tied to karst aquifers.
- **Sturgeons and large-river specialists**: [[Lake Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*, E/C2), [[Pallid Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus albus*, E/C2), and [[Blue Sucker]] (*Cycleptus elongatus*, C2) reflect declines in large-river migratory fish linked to impoundment and habitat alteration.
- **Darter diversity under threat**: Numerous *Etheostoma* and *Percina* species were listed, including [[Ashy Darter]] (T), [[Spotted Darter]] (T), [[Blackside Dace]] (*Phoxinus cumberlandensis*, E/C1) — the latter notable as a federally co-listed Cumberland Plateau endemic.
- **Undescribed taxa included**: The list incorporated then-undescribed species such as *Notropis* sp. ("Palezone Shiner," E/C2) and *Notropis* sp. ("Sawfin Shiner," E), demonstrating that new taxonomic discoveries were actively being folded into conservation planning.
- **Lampreys represented across the status spectrum**: Species such as [[Northern Brook Lamprey]] (T), [[Southern Brook Lamprey]] (E), [[Mountain Brook Lamprey]] (T), and [[American Brook Lamprey]] (T) indicate widespread regional sensitivity of lamprey populations.
- **Madtoms (*Noturus*) heavily represented**: Including [[Slender Madtom]] (E), [[Least Madtom]] (S), [[Brown Madtom]] (S), and [[Northern Madtom]] (S), underscoring the vulnerability of small benthic catfishes to habitat degradation.
- **Methodological rigor**: Status assignments derived from a structured Natural Heritage Program framework and were subject to peer review, distinguishing the list from a purely anecdotal survey and lending it lasting value as a baseline for Kentucky ichthyofaunal conservation status through the mid-1980s.
- **Non-regulatory nature**: The list carried no direct legal authority but served as a scientific and management tool to guide research priorities, environmental review, and future formal listing decisions by the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]].
