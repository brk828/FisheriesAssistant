# A Revision of the Squirrelfish Genus Myripristis Cuvier (Pisces: Holocentridae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #holocentridae

## Executive Summary

This Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County Science Bulletin (No. 19, June 30, 1974, by David W. Greenfield) revises the squirrelfish genus [[Myripristis]]. Of 45 nominal species, 15 are recognized as valid, and three new species are proposed from island habitats of Oceania. The total recognized count is stated as 18 species, with [[Adioryx]] the only holocentrid genus with more species. The genus is split into two subgenera, the nominate *Myripristis* and the new *Archaeomyripristis*, based on osteological characters. Identifications rest on 1,721 examined specimens, and the work provides a dichotomous key, synonymies, and meristic tables.

## Background & Description

The family [[Holocentridae]] is placed in suborder Berycoidei of Beryciformes. [[Nelson 1955]] split it into subfamilies Holocentrinae and Myripristinae based on swim bladder and auditory bulla morphology. [[Dunkle and Olsen 1959]] described the diagnostic differences, and [[Frizzell and Lamber 1961]] used fossil otoliths to argue for familial elevation. Greenfield disagrees with that elevation, citing shared characters between the subfamilies, particularly the intermediate frontal bones of *Archaeomyripristis*.

Diagnostic characters used in the key include:
- Perforated lateral-line scale counts, the primary meristic character.
- Gill raker counts on the upper and lower limbs.
- Frontal bone shape (concave or convex from dorsal view).
- Scalation on the inner face of the pectoral axil.
- Relative length of the third and fourth anal spines.
- Opercular membrane pigmentation.
- Interorbital width as a proportion of head length.

Species accounts cover synonymy, material examined, description, colour in alcohol and in life, and remarks. The 15 valid species treated are: *amaenus*, *chryseres*, *clarionensis*, *gildi*, *hexagonus*, *jacobus*, *adustus*, *kuntee*, *leiognathos*, *murdjan*, *parvidens*, *pralinius*, *trachyacron*, *violaceus*, and *vittatus*. The three new species are *tiki*, *woodsi*, and *randalli*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Natural history data for the genus are scarce. Reported observations include:

- **Diet:** [[Randall 1967]] reported that *M. jacobus* in the Caribbean feeds mainly on plankton. Major prey by volume were shrimps and shrimp larvae (30.3%), stomatopod larvae (17.4%), crabs and crab larvae (14.9%), mysids (11.2%), polychaetes (7%), fish larvae (5.4%) and amphipods (3.2%). [[Hiatt and Strasburg 1960]] found shrimps, crustaceans, polychaetes and small fishes in stomachs of the genus on Marshall Islands reefs.
- **Behaviour:** Squirrelfishes shelter under ledges and in caverns by day. [[Hobson 1965]] in the Gulf of California found *M. leiognathos* feeding mainly at night on small crustaceans, consistent with the large eyes.
- **Depth and zonation:** [[Gosline 1965]] recorded *M. kuntee* across three Hawaiian zones, *M. amaenus* in the surge and sub-surge zones, and *M. murdjan* only in the sub-surge zone. Several species (*chryseres*, *trachyacron*, *vittatus*) appear to be deep-water forms.
- **Sound production:** [[Salmon 1965]] documented four sound types in *M. berndti* (= *murdjan*) from Hawaii, produced by bilateral muscles acting on the air bladder. The author suggests sound plays a role in warning or species recognition.
- **Melanosis:** Melanistic specimens, interpreted as melanosis, were found in five species, including 24 of 103 *M. pralinius* from the Comoro Archipelago. The author notes the genus is used as a food fish.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic outcome:** 45 nominal species reduced to 15 valid species plus 3 new ones (18 total recognized).
- **New species:**
  - *M. tiki*: Easter Island, Rapa and Pitcairn; 35–40 lateral-line scales (mode 37). It differs from *M. amaenus* mainly in lateral-line count, and 55 of 69 specimens (79.7%) can be separated on this basis. Sympatry with *amaenus* on Pitcairn suggests character displacement.
  - *M. woodsi*: central Pacific; scales absent from pectoral axil; 14 or more upper gill rakers. Named for Loren P. Woods.
  - *M. randalli*: Austral and Pitcairn Islands; 28–31 lateral-line scales; 41–45 total gill rakers. Named for John E. Randall.
- **Subgenera:** *Archaeomyripristis* is erected for *M. trachyacron* (type species) and *M. vittatus*, based on convex frontals with an orbital shelf and an enlarged third anal spine. The author considers it the most primitive subgenus. *M. jacobus* stays in nominate *Myripristis* (type species *M. jacobus*).
- **Family status:** The author rejects elevating the subfamilies Myripristinae and Holocentrinae to family rank.
- **Key diagnostics:**
  - *M. murdjan* has a narrow interorbital (4.00–5.54), one symphysial tooth set, and 27–30 lateral-line scales.
  - *M. parvidens* has a wide interorbital and 27–31 scales. The two are often confused.
  - *M. hexagonus* has a double set of symphysial tooth patches and scales across the entire pectoral axil.
  - *M. jacobus* is Atlantic-only and has scales on the soft dorsal and anal membranes.
  - *M. kuntee* and *M. pralinius* differ mainly in the black opercular bar.
- **Nomenclature decisions:**
  - *M. murdjan* is retained for the narrow-interorbital species, because the Forsskål holotype is too damaged to serve as a name bearer.
  - *M. berndti*, *M. multiradiatus*, *M. phaeopus*, *M. mooreanus*, *M. sanguineus* and *M. macrolepis* are treated as junior synonyms.
  - *M. macrolepis* and *M. melanostictus* are synonyms of *M. hexagonus*.
- **Geographic patterns:** *M. amaenus* occurs in the central and western Pacific but not the Eastern Pacific or Indian Ocean. *M. violaceus*, *M. parvidens*, *M. pralinius*, *M. murdjan* and *M. hexagonus* range widely across the Indo-Pacific.
- **Data scope:** 1,721 specimens examined across 18 species. The largest samples are *M. murdjan* (234), *M. violaceus* (221) and *M. parvidens* (201). Meristic summary tables (Tables 1–17) give lateral-line, fin-ray and gill-raker frequencies by species and locality.
- **Statistics:** For *M. pralinius*, the Indian versus Pacific difference in lateral-line scales gave F = 7.006 (1, 180 df), above the 1% critical value. For *M. kuntee*, F = 14.476 (1, 166 df). The author judges both differences subpopulational rather than subspecific.
- **Colour and mimicry:** The black opercular bar in *M. kuntee* may act as disruptive coloration, a species-recognition signal, or both.
- **Research gaps:** Life-history data are rare. The author notes that deep-water species are under-sampled and that melanosis within the genus warrants further study.
