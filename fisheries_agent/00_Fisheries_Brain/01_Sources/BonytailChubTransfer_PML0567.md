# Bonytail Chub Transfer Memorandum (1992)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bonytail-chub

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum from the Arizona Game and Fish Department's Native Fish Crew, dated May 27, 1992, documents the salvage, holding, relocation, tagging, and release of [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*, not named in the source; the document refers only to "bonytail chub") from a pond at the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]]. Of 22 fish salvaged in April 1992, two were kept in a preserve lobby aquarium as a breeding pair, and the remaining 20 were moved in stages to [[Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery]] and then to the [[Arizona State University Research Park]]. The document reports a successful release of 18 fish with no signs of distress.

## Background & Description

- **Origin:** On April 20–24, 1992, 22 bonytail chub were salvaged from [[Palm Lake]] at the Hassayampa River Preserve near Wickenburg, Arizona.
- **Author and recipients:** Bruce Denova, Native Fish Crew Leader, addressed the memo "To: All Interested Parties." Duane L. Shroufe was listed as Director and Thomas W. Spalding as Deputy Director.
- **Enclosures:** A detailed memo on the transfer, a copy of the stocking slip, and a page of length and weight data for tagged fish.
- **Scope of transfer:** The document covers capture, interim holding, tagging, transport, and release. It does not address spawning or population monitoring beyond the pond observation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Holding and transfer chronology**

1. **Salvage (April 20–24, 1992):** 22 fish removed from Palm Lake.
2. **Preserve display:** Two fish kept in the large lobby aquarium "for educational purposes." The memo describes them as a breeding pair and states that approximately 100 bonytail are now in the aquarium. This figure appears inconsistent with the numbers given elsewhere and should be checked against the original record.
3. **Hatchery holding:** 20 fish moved to Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery. Manager Gene Okamoto reported that two died and the hatchery could no longer hold the remaining 18.
4. **Research Park:** The 18 fish were transferred to Arizona State University's Research Park.

**Transport conditions**

- The transport tank was described as "not large" but not crowded, with constant air flow from a compressed tank and mechanical agitators.
- Water temperature was 22 °C at loading from the hatchery circular, matching the transport tank.
- On arrival, the transport tank had warmed to 25 °C. The fish were reported to be "doing fine."

**Thermal acclimation at release site**

- The pond shoreline measured 32 °C, so water was drained from the tank and replaced with pond water to equalize temperatures.
- Fish were acclimated to the new 28 °C water before release near the dirt boat ramp.
- The released fish "showed no signs of discomfort" and swam normally to deep water.
- Later observation of the pond "yielded no signs of distressed fish."

**Tagging**

- On July 24, 1992, Bruce Denova and Dave Weedman travelled to Flagstaff and borrowed a PIT (passive integrated transponder) tag reader and 18 tags from Rob Carlson's [[Colorado River Research]] crew.
- Sixteen bonytail were tagged when loaded into the transport tank. Two fish already carried tags.
- The data table lists PIT tag numbers, length (mm), weight (g), and comments for the fish. Comments include "Previously tagged" (two entries) and "Male (ripe)" (two entries). The scan is partly degraded and some values cannot be read reliably, so individual measurements should not be cited without checking the original.

## Key Findings

- **Salvage and holding:** 22 bonytail chub were removed from Palm Lake in April 1992. Two were kept as a breeding pair in a preserve aquarium, and 20 were held at Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery.
- **Mortality and capacity:** Two of the 20 hatchery fish died, leaving 18. The hatchery could not hold these fish, so they were moved to the Research Park.
- **Transport success:** Fish were transported in an uncrowded tank with aeration and agitation. Temperature rose from 22 °C to 25 °C in transit, and all fish were reported to be in good condition on arrival.
- **Release protocol:** Because the pond shoreline was 32 °C, the transport water was replaced with pond water and the fish were acclimated to 28 °C before release. The fish showed no distress and swam to deep water.
- **Tagging:** Sixteen fish were tagged with PIT tags, and two arrived already tagged. Tag numbers and biometric data were recorded in a table, including two ripe males.
- **Post-release status:** Pond observation after release revealed no signs of stressed fish. No mortality after release is reported.
- **Data caveat:** The approximately 100 fish cited for the preserve aquarium do not match the 2-fish count implied elsewhere. Treat that figure as unverified.
