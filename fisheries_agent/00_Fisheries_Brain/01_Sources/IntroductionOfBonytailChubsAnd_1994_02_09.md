# Introduction of Bonytail Chubs and Razorback Suckers into Lake Havasu

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #conservation

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 internal memorandum from the Parker Fish and Wildlife Resource Office (FRO) detailing a fish translocation and reintroduction event. A total of 35 adult [[Bonytail Chub]] and two adult [[Razorback Sucker]] were transported from the Hassayampa River Preserve to [[Lake Havasu]], releasing them at Office Cove on the [[Bill Williams River]] National Wildlife Refuge. This action was part of a multi-agency cooperative conservation effort (The Nature Conservancy, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State University, and the USFWS) utilizing "Palm Lake" as a growout facility. The released bonytail were F2 generation fish produced at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], highlighting the reliance on captive propagation for the recovery of these critically endangered Colorado River Basin native species.

## Background & Description
Data collected prior to release provides biometric benchmarks for grow-out facility-reared native big-river fishes:

### Bonytail Chub (Gila elegans)
*   **Sample Size:** 35 adults
*   **Total Length (TL):** 303–421 mm
*   **Weight:** 280–570 g
*   **Genetic Lineage:** F2 generation (captive-bred descendants of wild broodstock)
*   **Health Status:** Active; no observable fungal infection or excessive physical damage.

### Razorback Sucker (Xyrauchen texanus)
*   **Sample Size:** 2 adults (1 male, 1 female)
*   **Total Length (TL):** 484 mm and 567 mm
*   **Weight:** 1.35 kg and 2.3 kg
*   **Reproductive Status:** Both individuals were noted as "ripe" (gonadally mature and ready to spawn), suggesting release timing aligned with the species' spawning season.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
*   **Habitat Type:** [[Lake Havasu]] represents a reservoir/lacustrine environment on the lower Colorado River, distinct from the historic riverine habitats of these species but utilized as a refuge due to reduced predation pressure compared to mainstem river habitats.
*   **Release Site:** Office Cove, a specific inlet associated with the [[Bill Williams River]] confluence, likely selected for structural complexity, reduced flow, or reduced density of non-native predatory fishes (e.g., sunfish, bass) common in the open reservoir.
*   **Behavioral Notes:** Upon release, both species schooled together and moved uniformly away from the release point into the lake, indicating strong initial group cohesion and immediate acclimation to a large water body.
*   **Trophic/Life-history Context:** Both species are pelagic-broadcast spawners historically adapted to the swift, turbid waters of the [[Colorado River]] mainstem; their introduction to a reservoir setting is a management strategy to bypass the loss of natural riverine spawning/rearing habitat (see [[Lake Havasu Native Fish Program]]).

## Key Findings
*   **Conservation Program:** The release is a component of the **Lake Havasu Native Fish Program**, an interagency initiative combining hatchery propagation with strategic reservoir stocking to bolster wild populations of endangered Colorado River fishes.
*   **Tracking Methodology:** All 37 fish were **PIT-tagged** (Passive Integrated Transponder) by Arizona Game and Fish Department prior to transport, enabling long-term individual tracking of growth, survival, and movement post-release.
*   **Growout Facility Model:** "Palm Lake" at the Hassayampa River Preserve functioned as an intermediate growout facility, suggesting a staged conservation strategy: hatchery (Dexter NFH) → growout pond (Palm Lake) → wild reintroduction (Lake Havasu).
*   **Transport Welfare:** Fish were held in a live car for less than 30 minutes prior to release, minimizing handling stress; post-release health assessment showed no signs of fungal disease or damage, indicating a successful, low-mortality-risk transport protocol.
*   **Species Status Context:** Both the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are federally listed as endangered species native to the [[Colorado River]] Basin, and this event exemplifies active recovery efforts under the Endangered Species Act framework.
