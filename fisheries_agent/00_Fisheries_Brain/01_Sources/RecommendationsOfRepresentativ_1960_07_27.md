## Executive Summary
This document is a historical administrative and research-coordination record rather than a primary biological dataset. It summarizes recommendations made on July 27, 1960, in Boise, Idaho, by directors and technicians of fish and game departments from eleven western U.S. states, alongside representatives of the [[U.S. Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] and commercial trout industry stakeholders. The meeting was convened in response to an emerging outbreak of **[[Hepatoma]]** (liver tumors) among hatchery-reared **[[Trout]]**, a condition later linked in the broader scientific literature to aflatoxin contamination in fish feed. The group established a coordinated plan for standardized data collection, inter-agency reporting, and a formal research agenda to identify the causative agent(s) and assess risks to fish populations and public health.

## Key Findings
- **Outbreak scope:** Hepatoma was observed among trout across multiple hatcheries in eleven western states, prompting a multi-agency emergency response.
- **Standardized data collection initiated:** A committee (Drs. Jackson, Van Cleve, Calhoun, Rucker, and Tanner) was tasked with designing a standardized reporting form to document:
  - Food/diet histories
  - Water chemistry
  - Water temperatures
  - Incidence of diseased livers
  - (Recommended) histories of unaffected lots fed similar diets, for comparative control data.
- **Centralized reporting:** The [[U.S. Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] (Washington, D.C.) was designated to collect all standardized forms, tabulate data, and issue a **weekly progress bulletin**, with each state agency submitting weekly activity reports.
- **Cross-institutional review planned:** Assembled data was slated for further discussion at the **American Fisheries Society meeting**, Denver, Colorado, September 12–14, 1960.
- **Research program objectives established:**
  1. Identify the causative agent(s) of [[Hepatoma]] in trout.
  2. Determine whether the condition poses **hazards to human health**.
  3. Determine **transmissibility** to other fish species and animals.
- **Preservation of evidence:** Samples of implicated fish foods fed to diseased lots were ordered into **cold storage** for future research use — an early recognition of diet (feed contamination) as a likely etiological factor.
- **Governance:** Recommendations were formally adopted by the Directors and Commissioners of the eleven participating western states on July 27, 1960.
- **Historical significance:** This meeting represents an early, coordinated multi-state/federal response to what is now recognized in fisheries science as one of the first major documented outbreaks of **diet-induced hepatocellular carcinoma** in hatchery fish, foreshadowing later identification of aflatoxin-contaminated feed as a causative agent in similar [[Fish Hepatoma]] cases.
