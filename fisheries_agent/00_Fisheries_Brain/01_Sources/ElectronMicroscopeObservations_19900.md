# Ultrastructure of the Thyroid Gland in the Banded Dogfish, Triakis scyllia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #chondrichthyes

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Yoshiharu Honma]] and [[Akira Chiba]] (Report of the [[Sado Marine Biological Station]], Niigata University, No. 23, 1993) describes the fine structure of the thyroid gland in the [[Banded Dogfish]] (*Triakis scyllia*), a cartilaginous fish sampled off [[Sado Island]] in the [[Sea of Japan]]. Electron microscopy of three female specimens showed a typical vertebrate thyroid architecture, including secretory and reabsorptive granules and evidence of [[Holocrine Secretion]]. The most notable finding was heavy [[Lymphocytic Infiltration]] of the interfollicular connective tissue. The authors compared this with [[Struma Lymphomatosa]] and [[Hashimoto's Thyroiditis]] but concluded that the resemblance is superficial, because the gland shows no severe thyroiditis.

## Background & Description

- **Prior work:** A preliminary report (Honma, Iwata and Chiba, 1987) compared the macroscopic and histological thyroid anatomy of 10 Japanese elasmobranch species. That study first noted abundant lymphocytes in the interfollicular spaces of the banded dogfish.
- **Literature gap:** Only two earlier electron microscope studies of the chondrichthyan thyroid existed. These covered a chimaeroid ([[Holocephali]], Nakai and Gorbman, 1969) and a marbled dogfish (Alluchon-Gérald, 1979). The present work was intended to provide comparisons with other vertebrates.
- **Gland anatomy:** The thyroid is a fan-shaped, pale disk on the ventral side of the basihyal bone, next to the anteroventral ventral aorta. It is buried in loose connective tissue between the coracohyal and coracomandibular muscles. Glands are several millimetres long and are visible to the naked eye even in immature animals.

### Materials and Methods

- **Sample:** Three female banded dogfish. One (caught 12 December 1982) was held at the Ageshima Aquarium, Aikawa. Two were caught on 13 August and 14 October 1984 in a set net off the Sado Marine Biological Station.
- **Light microscopy:** Glands were fixed in [[Bouin's Solution]], embedded in paraplast, and sectioned at 6–10 µm. Stains were hematoxylin-eosin, Heidenhain's azan trichrome, and PAS with light green-orange G counterstain.
- **Electron microscopy:** Tissue was fixed in 2% glutaraldehyde in 0.1 M cacodylate buffer (pH 7.4) for 2 days, then postfixed in 1% OsO₄ for 2 hours. It was embedded in Epon 812. Semithin sections (1 µm) were stained with toluidine blue. Ultrathin sections were double stained with uranyl acetate and lead nitrate and viewed on a Hitachi H-500 at [[Transmission Electron Microscopy]] magnifications.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat and setting:** Specimens came from coastal waters on the northwestern coast of Sado Island. The lymphoid invasion may be linked to captivity. The authors noted it was uncertain whether confinement in a narrow aquarium, or other external stressors, caused the lymphoid change.
- **Captive comparison:** Prince Masahito et al. (1979) reported thyroid tumour-bearing elasmobranchs kept for long periods at the Ueno Aquarium, Tokyo. The authors cited this as context for thyroidal hyperplasia in cartilaginous fishes.
- **Phylogenetic context:** The authors emphasised the value of chondrichthyan thyroid data for [[Vertebrate Endocrinology]], because cartilaginous fishes are phylogenetically important.

## Key Findings

1. **Cell structure:** Follicles are lined by light columnar epithelial cells with basal nuclei. The cells contain abundant rough endoplasmic reticulum, which is often dilated, plus [[Golgi Apparatus]] and few mitochondria.
2. **Granules:** Large dense bodies of 0.5–1.0 µm are present in the upper half of the cell. They are considered reabsorptive, in line with prior work on lamprey, eel, labrid, rockfish and ratfish. Smaller granules, thought to contain [[Thyroglobulin]] and to be secreted, were few and lay near the Golgi field.
3. **Crystalloids:** None were detected. This contrasts with earlier reports in yellowtail, labrid, rockfish and ratfish.
4. **Holocrine secretion:** Degenerated epithelial cells were found both attached to and detached in the lumen. This supports a holocrine secretory process, as previously noted in an anadromous lamprey.
5. **Endothelium:** Capillary endothelial cells showed basal infolding, interdigitation, and pinocytotic vesicles. Zonula occludens was well developed.
6. **Lymphoid infiltration:** Interfollicular connective tissue contained lymphocytes, lymphoblasts, macrophages, fibroblasts and degenerated lymphoid cells. Macrophages held large dense bodies with heterogeneous inclusions.
7. **Comparison with Hashimoto's disease:** The authors judged the earlier diagnostic resemblance to [[Hashimoto's Thyroiditis]] to be superficial. There was no severe thyroiditis, no denaturation of follicular epithelium, and no fibrous hyperplasia of the kind used to diagnose the human disease. They could not assess the animal's autoimmune status.
8. **Overall conclusion:** Fine structure of chondrichthyan thyroids does not differ strikingly from that of higher vertebrates. Minor differences from ratfish include a low degree of basal infolding and the absence of large low-density bodies and crystalloids.
9. **Open questions:** Further material from different seasons, ages and sexes is needed, along with rearing experiments in aquaria, to determine whether lymphoid invasion reflects stress or a true pathology.
