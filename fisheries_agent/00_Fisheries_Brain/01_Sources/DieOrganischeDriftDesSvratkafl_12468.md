# Organic Drift of the Svratka (Schwarza) River Between Two Reservoirs (Vir, Kninicky)

#zooplankton #stream-drift #limnology #freshwater-invertebrates #reservoir-effects

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Frantisek Kubicek]] (Zoological Institute, J. E. Purkyně University, Brno, 1967) describes the organic drift of invertebrates and other drifting organisms in the torrential (fast-flowing) reach of the [[Svratka River]], a Moravian river that runs between the [[Vir Reservoir]] and the [[Kninicky Reservoir]]. Samples were taken at six sites over 55 km during 1965. The drift was dominated by planktonic organisms released from the reservoir, mainly crustaceans and rotifers. Plankton abundance fell with distance downstream, while benthic and rheobenthic drift stayed low. The reservoir's outflow, its autumn circulation, and its downstream balancing basin were identified as the main controls on drift composition.

## Background & Description

Organic drift, meaning all organisms carried by flowing water, had received growing attention in the previous decade, with work by [[Muller]] on field methods and on the role of lakes and slow zones in stream productivity. The author notes that systematic drift studies had not previously been carried out in Czechoslovakia.

The study used 30 water samples collected between May and November 1965 at six sites on a 55 km reach. Sites were 0.2 km to 55 km below the Vir dam. Channel width was about 10 to 25 m, mean depth about 40 cm, and current velocity 35 to 50 cm/s. Sampling took place between 9:00 and 15:00 each day. Drift was collected with an adapted 5 cm diameter, 3 m long pipe that discharged into a plankton net. Between 50 and 100 litres were filtered per sample, depending on flow and seston density. Samples were fixed in 4% formaldehyde in the field. Biomass was estimated from mean organism weights.

Sampling frequency was six visits per site (11 May, 11 June, 5 July, 16 September, 11 October, 2 November), except that site 5 was sampled only three times and site 6 five times. No water flowed at site 1 on 11 June. The total filtered volume was 3000 litres.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Composition of the drift.** A total of 63 taxa were recorded. Crustaceans and rotifers made up most of the taxa (48 taxa, including 20 euplanktonic crustaceans). Rheobenthic organisms (8 taxa) and indifferent taxa (7 taxa) accounted for the remaining 15. The author estimated rheobenthic organisms at 12.7% of drifting individuals. The remaining 87.3% came from the reservoir outflow or from species that normally live in slow river sections, such as bays, weirs, and balancing basins.

**Individual abundance.** Of 790 individuals counted, 643 (81.4%) were zooplankton and 147 (18.6%) were other organisms. Rotifers made up 36.3%, cladocerans 22.5%, copepods 22.0%, and Diptera 12.5%. Benthic organisms ranged from 2 to 25 individuals per 10 L, whereas zooplankton ranged from 1 to 106 individuals per 10 L. Chironomid larvae were the most frequent non-plankton group, reaching a maximum of 24 individuals per 10 L.

**Longitudinal change.** Taxon richness was highest at the first site (below the dam) and the last site (55 km downstream). The author attributed the high count at site 6 to inputs from tributaries, mainly rotifers of the genera *Euchlanis*, *Mytilina*, and *Brachionus*. The lowest richness was at site 5, 40 km below the dam, at 22.2% of total taxa. Compared with the fluvial zone of the same river, taxon numbers in the torrential reach declined more slowly, dropping only after 40 km rather than after 19 km.

**Seasonality.** Taxon richness peaked in autumn. Rotifers from the Vir reservoir and from the river's potamoplankton drove this peak, particularly in the lower reach.

**Reservoir influence.** Most of the year, the reservoir released water from the nutrient-poor [[Hypolimnion]], where zooplankton numbers stayed low, from single individuals to tens per litre. During autumn circulation, plankton mixed into deeper water used for turbines (maximum depth about 60 m), and drift abundance rose, with a delay of more than one month after the late-summer zooplankton peak in the reservoir. Peak zooplankton drift (106 individuals per 10 L) was 50 to 100 times lower than the reservoir's zooplankton production, based on an 8-year mean.

**Other factors discussed.** The author lists several controls:
- **Balancing basin** (about 1.5 km below the dam): it concentrated reservoir zooplankton and allowed reproduction, but it also produced "front waves" of released water that could distort sampling.
- **Tributaries** from the Bohemian-Moravian Highlands carried little zooplankton. In one tributary, the author found that drifting zooplankton was mainly removed mechanically over about 3 km in fast riffles.
- **Riverbed vegetation** between sites 1 and 2 (mosses, filamentous algae, and higher plants) may trap drifting organisms. Net-spinning caddisfly larvae (*Hydropsyche* and *Polycentropus*) were thought to reduce zooplankton drift. An inverse relationship was observed between zooplankton and caddisfly larvae counts at several sites on 16 September and 11 October.
- **Pollution** was judged to have an unfavourable effect, but it was not measured directly.
- **Fish selection** was not studied.

## Key Findings

- **Drift is mostly planktonic.** Zooplankton, mainly rotifers and crustaceans, made up 81.4% of the 790 drifting individuals, and the drift as a whole was characterised as potamoplankton.
- **Reservoir outflow dominates.** Euplanktonic forms (such as *Daphnia hyalina*, *Diaphanosoma*, and *Eudiaptomus gracilis*) were carried downstream from the Vir reservoir, especially in autumn. Some remained largely intact at least as far as the Kninicky reservoir, more than 55 km away.
- **Euplankton declines quickly downstream.** Normal discharge caused a sharp fall in zooplankton by about the midpoint of the reach (after 28 km). *Daphnia hyalina* declined noticeably within 3 km.
- **Rheobenthic organisms are rare.** They made up only 12.7% of drift, mainly chironomid larvae, with mayflies, stoneflies, and blackflies occurring only occasionally.
- **Damage was low.** Rotifers and copepods showed no visible damage in transport. The highest damage rate was about 25% in *Bosmina longirostris* over 28 km. Only 1% of *Daphnia hyalina* individuals were deformed.
- **Abundance was lower than in comparable systems.** Maximum zooplankton drift (106 individuals per 10 L) was far below values reported from the Tisza canal (454 to 863 per 10 L) and from the Dunajec reservoir (about 100 per litre). The author suggests that unusual hydrobiological conditions in 1965 may have contributed.
- **Biomass estimates are theoretical.** Biomass values in the study were calculated by scaling drift counts to discharge (from the Hydrometeorological Service in Brno). The calculation assumed homogeneous dispersion across the river cross-section. The author considered it close to reality because the torrential zone covers most of the profile at sites 2 and 6.
- **Results agree with prior work.** Conclusions match those of [[Szczepanski]] (1958), and the effect of the Vir reservoir resembles that reported by [[Krzanowski]] (1965) for the Dunajec.
- **Gaps remain.** The reservoir's 1965 overflow event was not sampled. Vegetation trapping, fish selection, and pollution effects were not quantified.
