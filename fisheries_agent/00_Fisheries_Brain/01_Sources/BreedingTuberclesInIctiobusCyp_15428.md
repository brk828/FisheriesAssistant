# Breeding Tubercles in Ictiobus cyprinellus (Pisces: Catostomidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This short research note reports, for the first time, [[Breeding Tubercles]] on male [[Ictiobus Cyprinellus]] (the [[Bigmouth Buffalo]]). Four tuberculate males (335 mm SL) were collected on 1 May 1978 from the Dry Creek Area of [[Rend Lake]], Franklin County, Illinois. The tubercles were distributed over the head, predorsal area, midlateral body, [[Caudal Peduncle]] and fin rays. Histological examination showed keratinized caps over hypertrophied, hyperplastic epidermal cells, a structure resembling that of [[Moxostoma Erythrurum]]. The authors suggest the tubercles function as contact organs or to stimulate females during spawning. The note also challenges earlier reports that this species does not develop tubercles.

## Background & Description

Breeding tubercles occur on males of nearly all [[Catostomidae]] genera, the exceptions being *Lagochila* and *Myxocyprinus*. Before this study, [[Ictiobus Bubalus]] (the smallmouth buffalo) was the only buffalo fish known to bear tubercles. Earlier authors reported that *I. niger* and *I. cyprinellus* do not develop them (Forbes and Richardson, 1920; Scott and Crossman, 1973; Pflieger, 1975).

**Materials and methods:**
- Specimens were fixed in 10% formalin and stored in 70% ethanol.
- Skin from the head, caudal peduncle scales, and anal and caudal fin rays were processed for paraffin histology. Some scales and fin rays were decalcified in formic acid-sodium citrate.
- Sections were cut at 10 µm and stained with Mallory-Heidenhain triple stain or hematoxylin-eosin.
- Measurements were taken with an ocular micrometer at 430X.

The fish were collected by personnel from the [[Southern Illinois University]] Cooperative Fisheries Laboratory.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution on the body.** Tubercles were white, discrete, knob-like structures. Their pattern was similar across all four specimens, although development varied between individuals. The tubercles were best developed on the caudal peduncle scales, which is the same pattern described for other tuberculate suckers.

**Spawning behaviour.** Published accounts describe a great deal of splashing during spawning (Churchill and Over, 1938; Johnson, 1963; Brown, 1971). Brown (1971) reported that one female is accompanied by several males. The male-female breeding interaction of this species has not been adequately described. The authors note that the tubercles are seasonal and short-lived, and their location is consistent with a role in maintaining contact during mating.

**Comparative context.** The distribution of tubercles in *I. cyprinellus* resembles that of [[Carpiodes Velifer]] and several [[Moxostoma]] species. However, tubercle size and the location of the largest tubercles differ. In *Moxostoma*, the largest tubercles are usually on the anal fin. The pattern in *I. cyprinellus* also differs from that of *I. bubalus*, in which minute tubercles are confined to the head.

## Key Findings

**1. Presence and distribution of tubercles**
- Tubercles were found on all four males examined. They occurred on the head (except the branchiostegals), predorsal area, midlateral body, caudal peduncle, and all fins.
- **Caudal peduncle scales** carried the most tubercles, with 10–20 per scale (usually about 15). Each scale had a row of 6–12 tiny tubercles (about 0.1 mm diameter) along its free margin and a few larger ones (up to 0.8 mm, usually 0.6 mm) near its centre.
- A band of tubercles 0.1–0.4 mm (usually 0.2 mm) in diameter ran forward from the caudal peduncle over the lateral body scales.
- **Head tubercles** were tiny (0.1–0.4 mm). In three fish the upper opercle tubercles were slightly larger.
- **Fins:** Most fin-ray tubercles were about 0.1 mm across.
  - Pectoral fin tubercles were confined to the upper surface.
  - Pelvic fin tubercles were best developed on the upper side, with a few distal ones on the lower side.
  - Dorsal fin tubercles were mostly on the first 2–5 rays.
  - Fin-ray tubercles occurred in 1–4 rows.

**2. Histological structure**
- Tubercles from all body regions had similar structure: a mass of hyperplastic and hypertrophied epidermal cells capped by keratinized cells.
- **Head tubercles** had thinner keratin caps (about 57 µm high, 312 µm wide). **Fin-ray and scale tubercles** had caps up to about 114 µm high and 426 µm wide.
- Total height from basement membrane to cap top was:
  - Head: 327 µm
  - Caudal fin ray: 256 µm
  - Caudal peduncle scale: 341 µm
- Hypertrophied cells below the cap averaged 20 µm in diameter, compared with 13 µm for normal epidermal cells at the same level.
- No vascularization and no dermal papillae were found within the tubercles.

**3. Comparison with other suckers**
- The snout tubercles of [[Erimyzon Sucetta]] are solid keratin cones with vascularized epithelial cores, a type similar to some cyprinid tubercles.
- The tubercles of *Moxostoma erythrurum* have dermal papillae extending into the epidermis. Otherwise they resemble those of *I. cyprinellus*.

**4. Proposed function**
- The authors suggest that the tubercles act as contact organs that help maintain contact between the sexes during mating, or that they stimulate females during spawning. Their location and brief seasonal presence support this interpretation.

**5. Recommendations and implications**
- Other *Ictiobus* species, and *I. cyprinellus* from other regions, should be examined for tubercles.
- Tuberculation may be more widespread in the genus than currently thought.
- Tubercle pattern and structure may help clarify relationships within *Ictiobus* and among closely related genera such as *Carpiodes*.
