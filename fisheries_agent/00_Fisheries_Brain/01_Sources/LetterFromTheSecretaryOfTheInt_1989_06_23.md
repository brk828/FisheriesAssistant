## Executive Summary

This document is a 1989 administrative letter from the U.S. Secretary of the Interior to Colorado Governor Roy Romer, concerning federal funding commitments to the **[[Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin]]** (referred to as the "Recovery Program"). The letter is not a primary scientific study but a policy/funding communication addressing budgetary shortfalls (~$200,000–$324,600) affecting Fiscal Year 1990 support for endangered fish conservation efforts tied to the **[[Endangered Species Act]]**. No direct morphological, physiological, or population data on the fish species themselves are presented; the content is administrative in nature, reflecting the political and financial dimensions of endangered fish recovery in a major western U.S. river basin.

## Key Findings

- The **Bush Administration's FY1990 budget** did not allocate specific funds for Endangered Species Act **Section 6 grants** to states, potentially reducing support to the Recovery Program by **$200,000**.
- Of the **$624,600** total commitment needed for FY1990, only **$300,000** in ESA recovery funding was contained in the federal budget request; the remaining **$124,600** was not guaranteed and depended on final FY1990 appropriations.
- The only potential funding source for the shortfall was identified as the **Fish and Wildlife Service's Region 6 operating budget**, which was described as "limited."
- The Secretary indicated no objection to Congress restoring **FY1989 funding levels** for the Recovery Program, provided such action remained consistent with the **[[Bipartisan Budget Agreement]]** and related budget resolutions.
- The letter reflects broader **institutional and political dynamics** affecting endangered species conservation funding, illustrating how recovery programs for imperiled freshwater fish are contingent on annual federal appropriations processes rather than purely biological or ecological need.
