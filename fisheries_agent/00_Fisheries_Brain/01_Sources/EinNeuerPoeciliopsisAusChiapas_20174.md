# *Poeciliopsis hnilickai* n. sp., a New Livebearer from Chiapas, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This paper formally describes *Poeciliopsis hnilickai* n. sp., a livebearing freshwater fish of the family [[Poeciliidae]], from a small mountain stream about 1 km west of Ixtapa in [[Chiapas]], [[Mexico]]. The species is distinguished by a slender body, a rounded head, a dark blue midside streak in adults, and a hooked gonopodium tip in males. Its known range is restricted to the type locality, which is treated as evidence of endemism and as the southernmost record of the genus in Mexico. The description is based on one holotype and eight paratypes deposited in the [[Senckenberg Museum]] fish collection (SMF), and it is accompanied by a comparison with related [[Poeciliopsis]] species.

## Background & Description

**Genus context.** Following the revision by [[Rosen And Bailey 1963 Revision]], *Poeciliopsis* is divided into two subgenera. [[Allophallus]] contains two taxa, expanded by *P. paucimaculata*. [[Poeciliopsis Subgenus]] contains thirteen taxa, plus *P. turneri* and [[Poeciliopsis Catemaco|*P. catemaco*]], both described in 1975. The authors add *P. hnilickai* to the subgenus *Poeciliopsis*.

**Type material.**
- **Holotype:** SMF 15526, collected 26 February 1980 by D. Vogel and E. Hnilicka.
- **Paratypes:** five females (SMF 15527–15531) and three juveniles (SMF 15532–15534), collected together with the holotype.
- The species is named in honour of Erich Hnilicka of Puebla, whose private research, supported by the Departamento de Pesca in Mexico City, contributed substantially to the discovery of several poeciliid species.

**Diagnosis and morphology.**
- Rounded head and elongated body, with all fins rounded at the tips.
- Meristic counts: D 8, C 30, A 9–10, V 6, P 13, transverse scale series 6–7, lateral scale series 28–29, 30 vertebrae.
- Adult males have an anal fin modified into a long [[Gonopodium]] ending in a hook. The fourth anal ray (ray 4p) bears broad teeth.
- The [[Gonapophysis]] on vertebrae 3–15 of the suspensorium vary in shape. Interhaemal spines 8 and 9 lie close together.

**Coloration.**
- The ground colour is grey, and the belly shows a silvery-green sheen in reflected light.
- A broad dark blue midline stripe runs from the head to the caudal peduncle base in adults.
- The first two dorsal rays are pigmented black. Other fins are colourless.
- Adult females show small black spots around the genital opening, and this feature is weaker in adult males.
- Newborns lack melanophore patterns. Juveniles develop black dots along the midline that increase in number with growth.
- The sexes show no other colour differences.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The type locality is a slow-flowing mountain stream running through a partly dried riverbed, surrounded by variable scrubland and a hilly mountain range.
- The flat riverbed is about 10 m wide, with gravel, sand banks, and large eroded rocks.
- At the type locality, the stream is 3–5 m wide and is interrupted by scour holes of 1.20–1.60 m depth.
- Shallow areas have a gravel-sand substrate with larger stones. Deeper areas are covered by a black mud layer several centimetres thick.
- Aquatic plants and overhanging vegetation are absent.
- The water is colourless and crystal clear at the collection site.
- The only other organisms collected were toad larvae (*Kröten-Larven*). No other bycatch was recorded.

**Distribution.** The species is known only from the type locality, which suggests endemism. The authors describe this as the southernmost known locality for *Poeciliopsis* within Mexico.

## Key Findings

- **New species:** *P. hnilickai* is described from a single stream near Ixtapa, Chiapas, based on one holotype and eight paratypes.
- **Diagnostic traits:** The species combines a rounded head, elongated body, dark blue midline stripe, black-pigmented first two dorsal rays, and a hooked gonopodium with broad-toothed ray 4p. This combination is claimed to separate it from all previously known *Poeciliopsis*.
- **Closest relatives:** The authors compare the new species with several congeners:
  - [[Poeciliopsis Gracilis]] and [[Poeciliopsis Catemaco]]: the gonopodial hook and broad teeth are less pronounced in these species. The new species also differs in colour, body form, and suspensorium shape.
  - [[Poeciliopsis Prolifica]]: colour is superficially similar, but the midline stripe is narrower and does not reach the body midpoint, and the first dorsal rays lack melanophores.
  - [[Poeciliopsis Viriosa]] and [[Poeciliopsis Latidens]]: similarities are noted in body form and dorsal pigmentation.
  - [[Poeciliopsis Infans]]: the shape and size of gonapophyses on vertebrae 13–15 are similar.
- **Juvenile development:** Juveniles of the new species resemble *P. gracilis* in colour, but melanophore formation along the midline begins only at about 14 days and increases with growth.
- **Taxonomic approach:** The authors decline to assign the genus to species groups, following Rosen and Bailey (1963). They note that attempts at species-group classification within the Poeciliidae, including in [[Gambusia]], have produced conflicting results among authors.
- **Conservation status:** The paper does not assess conservation status. Its restricted, single-locality range is the main ecological point of concern.
