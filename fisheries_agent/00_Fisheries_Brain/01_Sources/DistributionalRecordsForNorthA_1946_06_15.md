# Distributional Records for North American Fishes, with Nomenclatorial Notes on the Genus Psenes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Nomeidae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #Gadidae

## Executive Summary

This 1946 paper by Robert R. Miller (U.S. National Museum) documents newly discovered distributional records for North American fishes—both freshwater and marine—based on a backlog of unidentified specimens accumulated at the [[U.S. National Museum]], many collected decades earlier (some by Marshall McDonald in 1885, others by the research vessel *Albatross*). The paper extends known ranges for six freshwater species across Virginia, Arizona, California, and Oregon drainages, and provides new or extended marine records for eight species, including significant taxonomic clarification of the oceanic genus **[[Psenes]]** (family [[Nomeidae]]). A major contribution is the untangling of long-standing confusion in Goode and Bean's (1895) identification and locality data for *Psenes regulus*, *P. pellucidus*, and *P. maculatus*, including evidence that the long-used name *Psenes maculatus* likely does not belong to the genus *Psenes* at all, based on the presence of palatine and vomerine teeth in Lütken's type description—characters that exclude it from *Psenes* under current generic definitions.

## Background & Description

### Freshwater Species
- **[[Thoburnia rhothoeca]]** (Catostomidae): An aberrant sucker distinguished at the population level; previously confined taxonomically to the James River system.
- **[[Agosia chrysogaster]]** (Cyprinidae): Diagnosed by teeth formula 4-4; scales with radii on all fields; maxillary barbel present; elongate anal fin with 7 rays; small, subterminal, nearly horizontal mouth.
- **[[Hesperoleucus mitrulus]]** (Cyprinidae): Small dorsal (8 rays) and anal (7 rays) fins; radii present on all scale fields — traits shared with some headwater populations of *symmetricus*, suggesting possible intergradation.
- **[[Rhinichthys osculus]]** (Cyprinidae): Subgenus *Apocope* (formerly a separate genus, now placed in synonymy with *Rhinichthys*) exhibits a variably developed frenum (weak in type specimens), whereas the subgenus *Rhinichthys* always shows a strongly developed frenum.
- **[[Chrosomus oreas]]** (Cyprinidae): New River (Kanawha system) population slightly finer-scaled than Roanoke River (type locality) populations.
- **[[Ameiurus natalis]]** (Ameiuridae): Diagnosed by 25 anal rays, broadly rounded caudal fin, and whitish mental barbels.

### Marine Species
- **[[Psenes regulus]]** (Nomeidae): Dorsal fin XI, I, 15; anal fin III, 15; ~50–55 lateral line scales; large round-to-oblong dark body spots. Distinct fin-ray counts and coloration set it apart from other *Psenes* species.
- **[[Psenes pellucidus]]**: Very fine scales (~120–140); transparent, pellucid body; elevated fin-ray counts relative to congeners. Comparative fin-ray tables (dorsal and anal) distinguish three informal species groups within *Psenes*:
  1. *pellucidus*-group — high ray counts, very fine scales, transparent body
  2. *cyanophrys*-group — intermediate ray counts, large scales (~50–65)
  3. *maculatus*-group — few rays, large scales
- **[[Psenes maculatus]]** (sensu Lütken): Reported by Lütken to possess palatine teeth and at least one vomerine tooth — dentition inconsistent with the modern generic diagnosis of *Psenes*, which lacks both. Body form notably slender, atypical for *Psenes*.
- **[[Psenes cyanophrys]]**: Distinctive coloration — longitudinal, lighter streaks between scale rows following the gently arched lateral line dorsally, running horizontally below it. Confirmed specimen 117 mm standard length.
- **[[Urophycis chesteri]]** (Gadidae): Much-prolonged third dorsal filament (depressed length ~2.2× in standard length); elongated pelvic fins (right fin ~1.6× in standard length); ~90 lateral line scales; 14–15 gill rakers; dorsal fin rays 9+89; anal 50–51.
- **[[Urophycis regius]]**: Short chin barbel (≤ pupil length); head depth ≈ width (not notably depressed); moderate scale count (<100); first dorsal usually 8–9 rays.
- **[[Urophycis floridanus]]**: Chin barbel always longer than pupil; 110–130 oblique scale series above lateral line; dorsal 12–13+54–59; anal rays 40–49; lateral line marked by a black streak interrupted by pale spots; first dorsal dusky/black but not white-margined (distinguishing it from *U. regius*).
- **[[Scophthalmus aquosus]]** (Scophthalmidae, the windowpane flatfish): Previously known as *Lophopsetta maculata*; family-level ranking followed per Hubbs and Hubbs (1945).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Freshwater systems**: Records span diverse North American drainages — the Kanawha and Potomac River systems (Virginia), the Gila/Colorado River Basin (Arizona), the Sacramento/Pit River system (California), the Santa Clara River Basin (southern California), and Pacific Northwest lakes (Oregon). Several records document species at range margins or in drainages connected historically via stream capture (e.g., [[Hesperoleucus mitrulus]] linking Goose Lake and the Pit River via historic tributary connections).
- **Introduction dynamics**: [[Ameiurus natalis]] represents a confirmed non-native introduction to Oregon, joining *A. nebulosus* and *A. melas* as introduced ameiurids in the Pacific Northwest. Similarly, the sudden appearance of [[Rhinichthys osculus]] in the well-surveyed Santa Clara River Basin raises the possibility of bait-bucket introduction by anglers, despite lacking a clear explanatory mechanism.
- **Marine habitat**: The *Psenes* and *Urophycis* records derive largely from deep continental shelf and open ocean *Albatross* trawl stations along the U.S. Atlantic coast and Gulf of Mexico, at depths ranging from a few meters (shrimp trawls) to 528 fathoms. Notably, *Psenes regulus*, despite being trawled from great depths (353–528 fathoms), is characterized as **not a deep-water fish**, having been caught near the surface by an open (non-closing) beam trawl that fished the entire water column.
- **Range extensions**: Multiple marine species show extensions southward (e.g., [[Urophycis regius]] to Florida, [[Urophycis floridanus]] to Texas, [[Scophthalmus aquosus]] to Georgia) or northward ([[Psenes cyanophrys]] to Massachusetts), reflecting the broad, often underdocumented ranges typical of oceanic and continental shelf fishes.

## Key Findings

1. **[[Thoburnia rhothoeca]]** range is definitively extended to the Kanawha and Potomac River drainages in Virginia, based on 1885 McDonald collections, resolving prior doubt from Hubbs (1930) and Hubbs and Raney (1944).
2. **[[Agosia chrysogaster]]**, previously confined to the Gila River Basin, is recorded for the first time from the Big Sandy River (Colorado River Basin), Mohave County, Arizona — a notable biogeographic outlier given the strong faunal distinction between the Gila and broader Colorado basins.
3. **[[Hesperoleucus mitrulus]]** is confirmed as part of the upper Pit River (Sacramento System) fauna, extending its known range beyond the previously reported Goose Lake tributaries.
4. **[[Rhinichthys osculus]]** is newly recorded from the Santa Clara River Basin, filling a previously unexplained gap in its Pacific coast distribution; possible bait-introduction origin flagged but unresolved.
