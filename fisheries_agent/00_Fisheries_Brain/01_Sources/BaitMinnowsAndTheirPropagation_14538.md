# Bait minnows and their propagation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This 1946 Illinois Natural History Survey bulletin addresses the commercial and practical need for live bait minnows used in angling for [[Bass]], [[Crappie]], [[Pickerel]], [[Walleye]], and [[Catfish]]. It warns against the unsustainable practice of wild seining and the ecological dangers of releasing non-native species like [[Common Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] into natural waters. The core recommendation is pond-based propagation of five hardy, pond-spawning species native to Illinois, detailing their spawning biology, growth rates, and pond management requirements for sustainable bait production.

## Background & Description
Five species are highlighted as suitable for artificial pond propagation due to size, hardiness, and spawning behavior:

- **[[Bluntnose Minnow]]** (*Hyborhynchus notatus*): Small-bodied cyprinid; deposits adhesive eggs in layers on submerged flat surfaces.
- **[[Fathead Minnow]]** (*Pimephales promelas promelas*): Adults average **3½ inches**; adhesive egg-layer; short-lived post-spawning.
- **[[Golden Shiner]]** (*Notemigonus crysoleucas auratus*): Larger-bodied, brightly colored cyprinid; adults reach **8–10 inches**; among the largest of the recommended bait species.
- **[[Silvery Minnow]]** (*Hybognathus nuchalis nuchalis*): Adults reach up to **6 inches**; less hardy than other listed species; suited to mud-bottom habitats.
- **[[Chub Sucker]]** (*Erimyzon sucetta kennerlii*): Not a true minnow (family [[Catostomidae]]); largest of the group, reaching **10 inches**; produces non-adhesive eggs.

Preferred angling sizes vary seasonally: **~2–2.5 inches** for early spring fishing, and **3–6 inches** for later-season bass/pickerel fishing. Species meeting this demand include the [[Common Shiner]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Bluntnose Minnow]], and [[Creek Chub]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native range**: More than 40 true minnow species are native to Illinois, though fewer than a dozen are practical for pond culture and bait use.
- **Distribution**: The five recommended species are nearly statewide, with exceptions — the [[Fathead Minnow]] is absent from southeastern Illinois, and the [[Chub Sucker]] is more abundant east of the state's north-south centerline.
- **Spawning substrate preferences**:
  - [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[Bluntnose Minnow]]: adhesive eggs on submerged flat objects (tile, rock, wood slabs); males guard nests aggressively.
  - [[Golden Shiner]]: eggs deposited among algae and aquatic plants; hatch in ~10 days.
  - [[Silvery Minnow]]: spawns over silty pond bottoms in shallow water.
  - [[Chub Sucker]]: prefers stream-spawning conditions but adapts to ponds; eggs laid over moss, filamentous algae, and submerged sod; hatch in ~7 days at 75°F.
- **Water temperature triggers**: Spawning generally initiated between **60–80°F**, spanning April/May through August depending on species.
- **Predation dynamics**: Medium-to-large golden shiners are noted to prey on fry of their own and other species — an important consideration for pond stocking density.
- **Invasive species risk**: The bulletin explicitly cautions against using [[Common Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] as bait, since accidental introduction into lakes has caused population establishment, rapid reproduction, and competition with game and pan fish.

## Key Findings
- **Pond suitability**: Small ponds (¼ to 1 acre) are recommended over large ponds due to lower spillway construction costs and easier management. Long, narrow ponds are preferable to round ponds for efficient seining.
- **Depth requirements**: Shallow marginal areas (<2 ft) promote aquatic vegetation cover for minnow refuge; ~30% of pond area should be 6–8 ft deep for overwintering (8 ft required in northern Illinois absent spring-fed water).
- **Production yields** (experimental):
  - [[Fathead Minnow]] / [[Bluntnose Minnow]]: 100,000–200,000 individuals per acre.
  - [[Silvery Minnow]]: up to 50,000 individuals per acre.
- **Growth timeline**: Under proper management, minnows reach minimum bait size by the end of the first year post-stocking; early-spawned [[Chub Sucker]] and [[Golden Shiner]] individuals may reach usable size by late summer of the same year.
- **Fertilization strategy**: Pond fertilization is recommended only in areas with poor soil fertility; unnecessary in regions with naturally productive soils.
- **Post-harvest handling**: Concrete or metal holding tanks (preferred over wood for cleaning efficiency) with shading, cool clean water circulation, and carborundum-block aeration are essential to prevent mortality and maintain marketability.
- **Economic viability**: High initial investment in ponds and equipment naturally limits market competition, sustaining favorable sale prices in high-demand areas and providing consistent income through the fishing season.
- **Species-specific mortality note**: [[Fathead Minnow]] adults die shortly after spawning, necessitating prompt removal and sale of spent breeders to maintain pond health.
