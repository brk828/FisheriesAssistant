# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants: Animal Candidate Review for Listing as Endangered or Threatened Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-act

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register notice (Vol. 59, No. 219, November 15, 1994, Proposed Rules, pp. 58982–59028) from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Service), Department of the Interior, presents an updated compilation of vertebrate and invertebrate animal taxa native to the United States under review for possible addition to the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The notice supersedes all previous animal notices of review. It reports 52 taxa proposed for listing, 86 Category 1 candidates, 1,919 Category 2 candidates (representing about 2,001 taxa), and 90 Category 3 taxa. It explicitly states that candidate status confers no substantive or procedural protection under the Act, while encouraging consideration in environmental planning and requesting further status information.

## Background & Description

- **Legal basis:** The Act (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.) requires the Secretary of the Interior (or Commerce) to determine endangered or threatened status based on the best available scientific and commercial data. Comprehensive notices are issued under 50 CFR 424.15.
- **Purpose of the notice:** Names species considered candidates for listing and indicates whether sufficient information exists to propose listing. It also solicits additional information.
- **Changes from prior notices:** (1) addition of new candidate taxa; (2) changes in category for some candidates; (3) additions and deletions in State historic distributions; (4) changes in status trend for some taxa.
- **Continuing procedures:** PE/PT categories for proposed species; alphabetical organization by scientific name under major group headings; omission of non-candidates; and assignment of a lead [[Fish and Wildlife Service Region]] for each taxon.
- **Prior notices cited:** November 21, 1991 (56 FR 58804); January 6, 1989 (54 FR 554–579); September 18, 1985 (50 FR 37958–37967); December 30, 1982 (47 FR 58454–58460); May 22, 1984 (49 FR 21664–21675, initial invertebrate review).
- **Table structure:** Columns cover category, status trend (I = Improving, S = Stable, D = Declining, U = Unknown, N = Not applicable), lead Region, scientific name, family, common name, and historic range by postal abbreviation. Insect entries marked with an asterisk have no sightings since 1963 in that State.
- **Compiled by:** Ecological Services staff in Regional Offices and Field Stations; compiled and edited by Dr. George Drewry, Division of Endangered Species, Washington Office.
- **Signatory:** Dated August 31, 1994, signed by Mollie H. Beattie, Director.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Category definitions**
- **PE:** Taxa already proposed to be listed as endangered.
- **PT:** Taxa already proposed to be listed as threatened.
- **Category 1:** Sufficient information on biological vulnerability and threats to support listing proposals; proposed rules precluded by other listing activity. Taxa in "warranted but precluded" status are automatically assigned here.
- **Category 2:** Listing possibly appropriate, but persuasive data are lacking. An asterisk (*) indicates possible extinction. Many are expected to be found not warranted for listing.
- **Category 3:** No longer candidates. Subdivided into:
  - **3A:** Persuasive evidence of extinction (habitats lost; most extinctions predate the 1973 Act).
  - **3B:** Not distinct taxa under current taxonomy, including populations not meeting the Act's definition of "species."
  - **3C:** Found more abundant or widespread than thought, or not subject to identifiable threat.

**Taxa represented in the table (by major group)**
- Mammals, including [[Kangaroo Rat]], [[Pocket Gopher]], [[Mastiff Bat]], [[Pygmy Rabbit]], [[Jaguar]], and [[Sea Otter]]-relatives such as the [[Dugong]]
- Birds, including [[Northern Goshawk]], [[Western Snowy Plover]], [[Mountain Plover]], and [[Trumpeter Swan]]
- Reptiles, including [[Desert Tortoise]], [[Gopher Tortoise]], [[Gila Monster]], and [[Bog Turtle]]
- Amphibians, including [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]], [[California Red-Legged Frog]], and [[Hellbender]]
- Fishes, including [[Lake Sturgeon]], [[Green Sturgeon]], [[Atlantic Salmon]], [[Bull Trout]], [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]]-related taxa, and [[Paddlefish]]
- Invertebrates: snails (e.g., [[Pleurocera]], [[Elimia]], [[Partulina]] tree snails), clams and mussels (e.g., [[Pearlymussel]] species), millipedes, insects (beetles, butterflies, dragonflies, mayflies, caddisflies), arachnids, crustaceans, earthworms, flatworms, hydroids, and sponges

**Aquatic-relevant taxa noted in the table (examples, as listed)**
- Fishes: *Acipenser fulvescens* (lake sturgeon, historic range includes AL, AR, GA, IA, IL and others); *Acipenser medirostris* (green sturgeon, CA, OR, WA, AK, Canada); *Acipenser transmontanus* (white sturgeon, Kootenai River population, ID); *Salmo salar* (Atlantic salmon, Dennys, Machias, East Machias, Narraguagus, Sheepscot, Ducktrap populations, ME); *Salvelinus confluentus* (bull trout, CA, ID, MT, NV, OR, WA); *Thymallus arcticus montanus* (Montana Arctic grayling, MT, Category 1); *Crystallaria asprella* (crystal darter); *Cycleptus elongatus* (blue sucker).
- Invertebrates: numerous freshwater snails and mussels in Alabama, Tennessee, Florida, and Georgia, plus cave amphipods, isopods, and crayfishes.

**Habitat and threat notes**
- The notice states that candidate status is not a finding of threat. Status trend is only one part of a taxon's status and may reverse owing to natural and human causes.
- The Service emphasizes that many Category 3C taxa may not be specially sensitive to extinction, correcting common misinterpretations.
- Status trends and threats are documented per taxon, but the notice does not provide detailed habitat descriptions for most entries.

## Key Findings

1. **Summary totals (as given):**
   - Proposed for listing: 52 (PE 44; PT 8)
   - Category 1: 86
   - Category 2: 1,919 (representing about 2,001 taxa)
   - Category 3: 90 (3A 32; 3B 14; 3C 44)
   - Cumulative Category 3 taxa in this and previous notices: 424 (3A 156; 3B 61; 3C 207)

2. **Legal status of candidates:** Neither substantive nor procedural provisions of the Act apply to candidate taxa. Species proposed for listing are covered by the conference procedure of Section 7(a)(4) of the Act. Federal agencies are encouraged to consider candidates in environmental planning, including under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]].

3. **Monitoring:** The Service intends to monitor all listing candidates "to the fullest extent possible," with emphasis on taxa facing imminent threat, referencing listing priority guidelines published September 21, 1983 (48 FR 43098).

4. **Request for information** from the public covers:
   - Data suggesting a different category
   - Nominations of omitted taxa
   - Recommendations of critical habitat, or statements that critical habitat is not prudent
   - Documentation of threats and their degree
   - Taxonomic or nomenclatural changes
   - Appropriate common names
   - Identification of errors, such as in historical distributions

5. **Publication schedule:** Comments are requested until an update is published, anticipated in 1996. Substantive changes will be published on a two-year cycle.

6. **Comment routing:** Taxon-specific comments go to the Regional Director with lead responsibility for that taxon. General comments go to the Chief, Division of Endangered Species, Mail Stop 452 ARLSQ, Washington, D.C. 20240. Contact: Jamie Rappaport Clark, Chief, Division of Endangered Species (703-358-2171).

7. **Regional structure:** Seven Regions are defined, with Region 1 covering the Pacific and western states, Region 3 the Upper Midwest, Region 4 the Southeast, Region 5 the Northeast and Mid-Atlantic, Region 6 the Mountain and Plains states, Region 7 Alaska, and Region 2 the Southwest.

8. **Taxonomic caveat:** Many invertebrates lack standardized common names, so the table uses group names (e.g., "snail," "mussel," "beetle") in parentheses for recognition.

9. **Authority:** Published under the Endangered Species Act (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.).
