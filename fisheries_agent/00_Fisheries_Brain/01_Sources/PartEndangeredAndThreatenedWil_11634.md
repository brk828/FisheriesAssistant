# Title 50—Wildlife and Fisheries, Part 17—Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#endangered-species #conservation-law #regulatory-document #biodiversity #federal-register

## Executive Summary

This document is a comprehensive compilation of Title 50, Code of Federal Regulations, Part 17, which lists all species of wildlife and plants determined by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and National Marine Fisheries Service to be Endangered or Threatened under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The list is current as of the date shown on the cover and includes mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, clams, snails, insects, arachnids, crustaceans, and plants. Each entry provides scientific and common names, historic range, vertebrate population status, listing date, critical habitat designation, and special rules. The document also includes species removed from the list due to recovery, extinction, or original data error.

## Background & Description

The regulatory framework establishes standardized listing procedures under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Species are designated as "E" (Endangered), "T" (Threatened), "E(S/A)" or "T(S/A)" (similarity of appearance), "XE" (essential experimental population), or "XN" (nonessential experimental population). Scientific nomenclature follows the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature and International Code of Botanical Nomenclature. Common names are provided for reference but cannot be relied upon for specimen identification due to regional variation.

The list encompasses taxa from diverse families including [[Salmonidae]] (salmon and trout), [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows and chubs), [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), [[Unionidae]] (freshwater mussels), and numerous plant families. Geographic coverage spans the entire United States, including Hawaii, Puerto Rico, Virgin Islands, Guam, and other Pacific territories, as well as international species protected under U.S. jurisdiction.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Listed species face threats from five primary factors: (1) habitat destruction, modification, or curtailment of range; (2) overutilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes; (3) disease or predation; (4) inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms; and (5) other natural or manmade factors affecting continued existence.

Aquatic ecosystems are heavily represented, with numerous fish species listed including [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail chub), [[Chasmistes cujus]] (cui-ui), [[Acipenser brevirostrum]] (shortnose sturgeon), and [[Oncorhynchus tshawytscha]] (chinook salmon). Freshwater mussels show particularly high endangerment rates, with species like [[Epioblasma torulosa rangiana]] (northern riffleshell) and [[Pleurobema clava]] (clubshell) listed. Marine species include sea turtles ([[Chelonia mydas]], [[Eretmochelys imbricata]], [[Caretta caretta]]), marine mammals ([[Trichechus manatus]], [[Eumetopias jubatus]]), and anadromous fish.

Critical habitat designations are specified for select species under 17.95 and 17.96, providing geographic areas essential for conservation. Special rules under 17.40-17.46 establish management provisions for specific taxa.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Coverage**: The list includes 1,000+ species across vertebrates (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes) and invertebrates (mollusks, insects, arachnids, crustaceans), plus extensive plant listings. Fish species represent a substantial portion, reflecting aquatic ecosystem vulnerability.

**Geographic Patterns**: Highest concentrations occur in [[Hawaii]] (endemic birds, plants, invertebrates), [[California]] (plants, fish, invertebrates), [[Florida]] (plants, reptiles, freshwater species), and southeastern states (freshwater mussels, fish). Many species have highly restricted ranges, often single watersheds or islands.

**Aquatic Species Emphasis**: Freshwater ecosystems show critical endangerment with 100+ fish species listed including [[Cyprinidae]] (chubs, minnows, dace), [[Catostomidae]] (suckers), [[Salmonidae]] (trout, salmon), [[Percidae]] (darters), and [[Cyprinodontidae]] (pupfish). Freshwater mussels ([[Unionidae]]) comprise 50+ listings. Marine species include all sea turtle species, multiple whale species, and coastal fish.

**Delisting Records**: Species removed from the list include [[Cyprinodon nevadensis calidae]] (Tecopa pupfish, extinct), [[Coregonus alpenae]] (longjaw cisco, extinct), [[Stizostedion vitreum glaucum]] (blue pike, extinct), and [[Melospiza melodia graminea]] (Santa Barbara song sparrow, recovered). Delisting reasons: extinction, recovery, or original data error.

**Regulatory Structure**: Federal Register citations span 1967-1993, documenting listing chronology. Critical habitat designations (17.95-17.96) and special rules (17.40-17.46) provide species-specific management frameworks. Experimental population designations allow reintroduction programs for species like [[Canis lupus]] (gray wolf) and [[Mustela nigripes]] (black-footed ferret).

**Conservation Status**: The document reflects biodiversity crisis across U.S. ecosystems, with particular concern for freshwater habitats, island endemics, and species with restricted ranges. Listing under the Act triggers federal protection, recovery planning, and consultation requirements for federal actions.
