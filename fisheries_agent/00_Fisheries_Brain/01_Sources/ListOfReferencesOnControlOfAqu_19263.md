# List of References on Control of Aquatic Plants, Including Algae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquatic-vegetation

## Executive Summary

This document is a bibliographic compilation of literature on the control of aquatic plants and algae, prepared by Margaret Greenwald of the Research Division of the [[Chipman Chemical Company]], Bound Brook, New Jersey, in January 1956. It replaces an earlier 1954 list and is explicitly described as neither complete nor selected, with emphasis on recent works. It contains roughly 900 entries, spanning the 1890s to 1956, covering herbicides, algicides, mechanical removal, biological control, and the effects of control agents on fish and wildlife. The list also includes handbooks and general texts used for plant identification and for understanding water-quality conditions.

## Background & Description

- **Format:** A chronological-alphabetical list of author, year, title, and publication details, with occasional annotations (e.g., "Not plant control," "Useful for identification").
- **Scope:** Mainly North American work (USDA, Bureau of Reclamation, state fish and game agencies, Fish and Wildlife Service), with substantial material from Canada, Britain, Europe, India, Australia, and Southeast Asia.
- **Purpose:** The compiler notes that the list is unfinished and invites contributions; a supplement was proposed for the following year.
- **Document type:** Reference bibliography (no original data, sample sizes or statistics).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The entries address a wide range of nuisance and target organisms:

- **Vascular aquatic plants:** [[Water Hyacinth]] (*Eichhornia crassipes*), [[Alligatorweed]], [[Cattails]] (*Typha* spp.), water chestnut (*Trapa natans*), [[Water Milfoil]] (*Myriophyllum*), pondweeds (*Potamogeton*), American lotus (*Nelumbo lutea*), giant cutgrass, *Juncus* rushes, *Phragmites*, and eelgrass (*Zostera marina*).
- **Free-floating and submersed plants:** duckweeds (*Lemna*, *Spirodela*), *Salvinia*, *Pistia*, and *Najas* (naiad).
- **Algae:** filamentous algae, blue-green algae (*Anabaena*, *Microcystis*-type blooms), diatoms and phytoplankton; plankton control at municipal water supplies is a recurring theme.
- **Fish and wildlife interactions:** Numerous entries examine toxicity of herbicides and algicides to fish (e.g., [[Copper Sulfate]] and [[Sodium Arsenite]]), effects on [[Largemouth Bass]], bluegill, carp and trout, the role of aquatic plants in fish habitat and oxygen supply, and the use of [[Grass Carp]]-type biological control (e.g., German carp as "nature's weed killer").
- **Water quality:** Dissolved oxygen, taste and odor from algae, and industrial pollutant toxicity to fish are cross-referenced because they bear on control decisions.

Major chemical categories represented include phenoxy herbicides such as [[2,4-D]], aromatic solvents, [[Dalapon]], copper sulfate, sodium arsenite, chlorine and bromine compounds, and proprietary algicides.

## Key Findings

The compilation yields the following main points:

1. **Scale of the literature:** Roughly 900 references are listed, reflecting a large body of work on aquatic weed and algae control dating back to about 1890 (the earliest-listed authors use copper sulfate for algae in water supplies, with major activity around 1904–1936).
2. **Chemical control history:** Copper sulfate was the earliest and most widely used algicide in reservoirs and water supplies. Sodium arsenite was a standard submersed-weed treatment for fish ponds (Surber, Bureau of Fisheries, 1929–1955). Phenoxy herbicides (2,4-D) became prominent from the mid-1940s onward, particularly against water hyacinth and emergent plants.
3. **Herbicide research on target plants:** Major series of tests were reported for [[Water Hyacinth]] control (Hitchcock, Zimmerman, Earle, 1949–1950; Penfound and Earle, 1948), cattail control in the western U.S. and Canada (Bruns, Timmons, Hodgson, Bowser, Dow Chemical), and submersed weeds in irrigation canals using aromatic solvents (Bruns, Lee, Shaw, Timmons).
4. **Environmental and fish concerns:** A substantial subset examines toxicity to fish and other biota, including Doudoroff and Katz's critical reviews (1950, 1953), Surber's toxicity studies, and the note that copper and arsenic accumulate in fish (Wiebe et al., 1931).
5. **Alternative methods:** Mechanical cutting, underwater mowers, pond-scrubbing machines, drawdown, fertilization to shade out plants (Swingle, Smith), and grass carp/carp grazing are also covered.
6. **Notable gaps acknowledged by compiler:** Early literature is incomplete, biological and ecological studies were largely excluded, and the list has not been annotated or subdivided.
7. **Items of Regional Interest:** Many state and provincial reports (Alabama, Ohio, New Jersey, New York, Tennessee, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Wisconsin) document local control programs, indicating decades of applied fisheries and wildlife management work on aquatic vegetation.
