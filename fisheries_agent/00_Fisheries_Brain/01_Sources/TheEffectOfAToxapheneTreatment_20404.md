# The Effect of a Toxaphene Treatment for Fish Eradication on the Bottom Fauna of a Wisconsin Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Chironomidae #benthic-ecology #toxicology

## Executive Summary

This study examined the impact of toxaphene treatment for fish eradication on the benthic invertebrate community of [[Hartlaub Lake]], Wisconsin. The 37-acre lake was treated with 0.1 ppm toxaphene on July 10, 1962, to remove undesirable fish populations. Sampling from the profundal zone (23 feet depth) revealed that toxaphene eliminated [[Chaoborus]] (phantom midge) larvae and fish while leaving bottom-dwelling [[Tendipes]] larvae and copepods initially unaffected. Four months post-treatment, the [[Tendipes]] population exploded from approximately 10 larvae per square foot to over 600 larvae per square foot, likely due to release from predation pressure by both Chaoborus larvae and fish.

## Background & Description

The [[Wisconsin Conservation Department]] routinely treated lakes with toxaphene to remove undesirable fish populations. Personnel had observed large numbers of dead [[Tendipes]] larvae on lake surfaces following treatments, prompting investigation of recovery dynamics and faunal changes.

[[Hartlaub Lake]] characteristics:
- Area: 37 acres
- Maximum depth: >40 feet
- Thermocline depth at treatment: 18 feet below surface
- Hypolimnion: anoxic (no oxygen present)
- Bottom substrate: muck at sampling site (23 feet depth)

Sampling methodology employed [[Ekman Dredge]] collections (eight samples per date) sieved through 60-mesh sieve. Insect larvae and pupae >5 mm were counted directly; smaller organisms (<5 mm) were subsampled. Initial screening of multiple Wisconsin lakes in early May 1962 identified only [[Hartlaub Lake]] and [[Merrill Lake]] with [[Tendipes]] populations exceeding 10 larvae per square foot.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The lake's thermal stratification and oxygen profile created distinct habitat zones. The epilimnion (upper 18 feet) contained oxygen and supported fish populations, while the anoxic hypolimnion excluded fish but supported benthic invertebrates adapted to low-oxygen conditions.

[[Chaoborus]] larvae exhibit diel vertical migration, dwelling in profundal sediments during daylight but migrating to the limnetic zone (epilimnion) during darkness to feed. This behavior pattern proved critical to their differential mortality following toxaphene application.

The trophic structure included:
- Fish predators (consuming larger [[Tendipes]] instars)
- [[Chaoborus]] larvae (predating early instar [[Tendipes]])
- [[Tendipes]] larvae (benthic detritivores/filter feeders)
- Copepods (benthic microcrustaceans)

## Key Findings

**Immediate Effects (July 13, 3 days post-treatment):**
- [[Chaoborus]] population eliminated (76 larvae/ft² reduced to 0)
- [[Tendipes]] population unchanged (10 larvae/ft²)
- Copepod population unchanged (8,740/ft²)
- Dead and dying [[Chaoborus]] larvae observed on lake surface with dead fish

**Four-Month Response (November 8, 1962):**
- [[Tendipes]] population explosion to 602 larvae/ft² (60-fold increase)
- Population dominated by [[Tendipes attenuatus]] (probable identification)
- Physidae snails (2-10 mm) appeared in profundal zone (43/ft²), previously absent
- Population remained elevated through winter (March 15, 1963: 502 larvae/ft²)

**Population Dynamics by Size Class (November 8):**
- Larvae <5 mm: 10/ft²
- Larvae 6-8 mm: 43/ft²
- Larvae 9-13 mm: 502/ft²
- Larvae 14-20 mm: 43/ft²
- Larvae 21-30 mm: 4/ft²

**Mechanism of Differential Mortality:**
The toxaphene remained concentrated in the epilimnion due to thermal stratification. Bottom-dwelling organisms ([[Tendipes]] larvae, copepods) in the profundal zone at 23 feet depth remained below the thermocline and avoided exposure. [[Chaoborus]] larvae were killed during nocturnal vertical migration into the toxaphene-laden epilimnion. Fish were confined to the epilimnion by hypolimnetic anoxia and thus received lethal toxaphene exposure.

**Trophic Release:**
Elimination of both [[Chaoborus]] larvae (predators of early instars) and fish (predators of late instars) released [[Tendipes]] populations from top-down control, enabling the observed population explosion.

**Other Taxa:**
- [[Procladius]] spp. (8-12 mm larvae): 2/ft² pre-treatment, 1/ft² post-treatment
- [[Cryptochironomus]] spp. (9-14 mm larvae): 1.5/ft² detected August 17
- [[Culicoides]] sp. and [[Palpomyia]] spp.: appeared in low numbers (1-20/ft²) post-treatment
- Hydracarina (mites): 0.5/ft² detected February 7
- Cladocera: 100/ft² detected November 8
- Ostracoda: 180/ft² detected November 8

The study was ongoing at time of presentation, with plans to continue monitoring until a stable benthic community re-established following lake detoxification and fish restocking.
