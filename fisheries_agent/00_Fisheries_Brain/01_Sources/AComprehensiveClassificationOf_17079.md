## Executive Summary

Robert L. Martin (1960) proposes a revised, more comprehensive classification scheme for **vertebral centrum shape-variants**, addressing gaps in the traditional shape-based classification system used in comparative anatomy. The paper is explicitly framed as a response to and refinement of **[[Hobart M. Smith]]'s 1949 intervertebral-type classification**, which Martin argues is useful for understanding *formation* of centrum shapes but is not practical for anatomical identification or teaching purposes. Martin's revision retains much of the historical terminology (amphicoelous, opisthocoelous, procoelous, platyan, amphicyrtean) while adding new subdivisions — including polyamphicoelous, polyopisthocoelous, polyprocoelous, and polyamphicyrtean forms — to ensure the scheme can account for **all possible centrum shapes**, including asymmetrical, multiple-articulation, and hypothetical forms not yet observed in nature.

## Background & Description

- **Historical context:** Traditional vertebral shape classification is described as "artificial and inadequate." [[Hobart M. Smith]] (1949) had proposed an alternative scheme based on **intervertebral types** rather than shape-variants, published in the same journal (*Turtox News* 27(6):130–133).
- **Problem identified:** Neither the traditional shape-based system nor Smith's intervertebral-type system alone is sufficient for straightforward anatomical identification or teaching. Intervertebral-type classification illuminates *mechanisms of formation* of shapes but is not designed for direct anatomical identification.
- **Martin's approach:** Retains the framework and terminology reviewed by Smith, modifying and expanding it to cover previously omitted centrum shapes, while remaining anchored in shape (rather than developmental/intervertebral) criteria.

## Key Findings

### Revised Classification Scheme of Vertebral Centrum Shapes

- **[[Amphicoelous]]**
  - a. Perforate amphicoelous
  - b. Imperforate amphicoelous
  - c. **Polyamphicoelous** (new term) — multiple concavity forms of the amphicoelous type

- **[[Opisthocoelous]]**
  - a. **True opisthocoelous** — "true" used in the sense of conforming to the historical/standard usage
  - b. **False opisthocoelous (Pseudopisthocoelous)** — shape achieved without fusion of intervertebral body to centrum (e.g., seen in large ungulates), as opposed to true fusion-based formation; included for pedagogical value due to noted affinities with the amphiplatyan type
  - **Polyopisthocoelous** (new term) — multiple concavity forms of the opisthocoelous type

- **[[Procoelous]]**
  - a. True procoelous
  - b. **Polyprocoelous** — multiple concavity forms of the procoelous type; includes:
    - **"Saddle" forms** — synonymous with the traditional term **heterocoelous**; proposed as retained subdivision of polyprocoelous on grounds of common usage despite lacking independent anatomical justification
    - **"Cylindrical" forms** — proposed as a separate subdivision of polyprocoelous, distinct from saddle/heterocoelous forms

- **[[Platyan]]**
  - a. Amphiplatyan (biplane)
  - b. Hemiplatyan
  - Covers centra with either end flat, regardless of opposite end's shape

- **[[Amphicyrtean]]**
  - a. **True amphicyrtean** — replaces the older term "acoelous," which Martin argues was ambiguous (could be misread to include amphiplatyan forms despite intending biconvex-only forms); "amphispheroid" was considered but rejected due to potential confusion with intervertebral-type terminology (suffix "-spheroid")
  - b. **Polyamphicyrtean** (new term)

### Methodological Notes
- Following **[[Ernest E. Williams]] (1950)**, partly convex surfaces are treated as fully convex for classification purposes.
- The scheme explicitly accommodates **asymmetrical and multiple-articulation centrum types**, including **four hypothetical forms** illustrated (stippled) in Figure I, anticipating future anatomical discoveries that may not yet be documented.
- Martin argues the revised scheme is designed to **minimize classification difficulty** for novel or unusual forms rather than adding complexity.
