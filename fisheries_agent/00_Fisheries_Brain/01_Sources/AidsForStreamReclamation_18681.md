# Stream Reclamation Toxicant Delivery, Fish Catching Box, and Anti-germ 77 Feeding Trial

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1963 *Progressive Fish-Culturist* item bundles three fishery-management contributions. The first describes a constant-flow [[Drip Station]] device, adapted from [[Mariotte's Bottle]], for applying toxicant to flowing streams, supported by two [[Nomograph]] calculators. The second describes a portable box for catching [[Largemouth Bass]] when drainable ponds are lowered. The third reports a feeding trial showing that the disinfectant additive [[Anti-germ 77]] in a [[Terramycin]] poultry mix caused no significant difference in three salmonid species: [[Brook Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]], and [[Brown Trout]].

## Background & Description

**Stream reclamation.** The article notes that stream treatment for fish removal is receiving more attention from fishery managers. Although mechanics differ from lake treatment, the goal is the same: maintain a predetermined toxicant concentration for a set period. Standard practice is to measure flow in cubic feet per second and introduce toxicant from a drip station at a rate calculated to produce the target concentration, relying on water movement for mixing. Drip stations are typically drums with a drain valve. Delivery is measured in cubic centimeters per minute with a graduated cylinder and stopwatch. Because flow drops as the liquid head falls, operators must constantly reset the valve.

**Fish catching box.** The second item describes a catching device for drainable ponds that lack a catch basin. It is used below the outlet once water has dropped far enough that 4- to 7-inch largemouth bass begin to leave the pond.

**Anti-germ 77 study.** Terramycin (oxytetracycline hydrochloride) is used against ulcer disease and furunculosis in trout. Pfizer's Terramycin Poultry Formula is cheaper than pure drug, which makes it attractive for hatcheries. Its disinfectant component, Anti-germ 77, is [[Benzethonium Chloride]]. The mix contains 5.5 percent Terramycin, 5.5 percent benzethonium chloride, and 89 percent inert ingredients. The [[Eastern Fish Disease Laboratory]] at Kearneysville, West Virginia, requested an assessment of the additive's effect on fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Hydraulic basis of toxicant delivery:** Uniform toxicant distribution in streams depends on natural water movement, so accurate calculation of flow and delivery rate is critical.
- **Temperature effects on flow:** Changes in liquid temperature alter density and viscosity, which the authors believe explains a small shift in delivery rate during the test.
- **Pond drawdown:** Lowering water levels concentrates fish at outlets, which the catching box exploits to capture bass.
- **Hatchery conditions:** The feeding trial held water temperature at an average of 54°F, with uniform water level and flow across troughs.

## Key Findings

**1. Constant-flow drip station (Price and Haus, S. B. Penick and Company, Research Division)**

- The Mariotte's bottle principle provides flow under constant head, equal to the height of the bottom of the air inlet tube above the liquid outlet.
- In a 55-gallon drum test (Figure 2 data), the drain valve was set once and not changed. Delivery stabilized within a few minutes at 150 cubic centimeters per minute.
- Measurements over 12.5 hours showed approximately 30 gallons drained from the drum. Flow changed from 150 to 158 cubic centimeters per minute over the period, which the authors attribute to temperature-related changes in density and viscosity.
- With the air inlet tube omitted, the initial delivery rate was much faster and progressively declined as the head fell. This confirms the need for the air inlet tube.
- The air inlet seal must be airtight. The authors recommend Teflon tape instead of pipe paste for threaded fittings.

**2. Nomographs for calculating toxicant requirements**

- **Nomograph 1 (Figure 3):** Relates stream flow (A, cubic feet per second) and target concentration (B, ppm) to drip rate (X, cc/min). A straight edge between A and B gives X.
- **Nomograph 2 (Figure 4):** Relates drip rate (X, cc/min) and run time (C, hours) to total toxicant required (Y, gallons), using Y = X × 0.0158 × C.
- Any two scales determine the third, so other combinations are possible. Copies of the nomographs (8.5 by 11 inches) were available from the authors.

**3. Fish catching box (B. I. Severson, Iowa State Conservation Commission)**

- The box measures 6 by 4 by 3 feet and is built of 1- by 12-inch pine. Tapered ends let surplus water flow past it. A half-tube chute with hardware cloth and galvanized tin guides water and bass into the box. The bottom is covered with 1/2-inch hardware cloth so water can drain out.
- The chute is hinged to the 20-inch discharge tube and lowered only during catching. The box sits far enough from the discharge tube that most water exits through the chute sides. Steel posts anchor it.
- **Performance:** Three men removed 24,500 bass in 3 days. Previously, five men took 1 week to do the same work.

**4. Effect of Anti-germ 77 on salmonid fingerlings (John W. Kincheloe, Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife)**

- **Species:** Brook trout (*Salvelinus fontinalis*), rainbow trout (*Salmo gairdneri*), and brown trout (*Salmo trutta*), each tested in two replicates.
- **Design:** 225-gram lots of fingerlings were distributed among 12 troughs. Troughs were cleaned daily and mortalities recorded. Fish were weighed every 2 weeks, and feeding rates were adjusted accordingly. Fish were fed three times daily.
- **Diet:** The control and carrier diet was 50 percent beef spleen, 30 percent beef liver, and 20 percent commercial dry component (fish meal, cottonseed meal, distillers' solubles, wheat middlings, and salt). The drug was mixed into the meal, which was then blended with the meat.
- **Dose:** Terramycin was supplied at 3.5 grams per 100 pounds of fish per day. Anti-germ 77 was therefore delivered at the same rate.
- **Result:** Per the source's Table 1, Pfizer's Terramycin Poultry Formula with Anti-germ 77 caused no significant difference in the fish. The source text is cut off at this point, so full statistics are not available here.
