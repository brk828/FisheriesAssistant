# The Instars of Simulium ornatum var. nitidifrons Edwards (Diptera, Simuliidae)

#entomology #diptera #simuliidae #larval-development #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study confirms that [[Simulium ornatum]] var. nitidifrons, a blackfly species from a chalk stream near [[Romsey]], [[Hampshire]], undergoes six larval instars before pupation. Larvae were reared from field-collected eggs in laboratory apparatus. Instars were distinguished by measuring frons-clypeus width and counting cephalic fan filaments, with additional morphological characters (egg-burster, imaginal buds) aiding identification of specific stages. The geometric size progression expected under [[Dyar's Rule]] was not observed in later instars. This work tests [[Crosskey]]'s (1960) hypothesis that six instars are typical across [[Simuliidae]], following [[Terterjan]]'s (1957) demonstration of six instars in [[Wilhelmia equina]].

## Background & Description

The [[Simuliidae]] (blackflies) are a [[Diptera]]n family for which systematic larval studies have been limited, with considerable disagreement about instar numbers based on field observations. [[Terterjan]] (1957) demonstrated six larval instars in [[Wilhelmia equina]] through head capsule measurements, and [[Crosskey]] (1960) suggested this pattern likely applies family-wide. Understanding larval instars is essential for insect life history studies.

[[Simulium ornatum]] var. nitidifrons inhabits a small [[chalk stream]] near [[Romsey]], [[Hampshire]], England. Larvae were reared from eggs in specialized apparatus (Hall and Harrod, 1963).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study species inhabits [[chalk stream]] environments in southern England. Larvae were collected from the field and reared under controlled laboratory conditions using a specialized rearing apparatus designed for [[Simuliidae]]. The chalk stream habitat near [[Romsey]] provided the source population for egg collection.

## Key Findings

**Instar Determination:**
Six larval instars were identified and separated using frons-clypeus width measurements:
- First instar: 0.04 mm
- Second instar: 0.06-0.10 mm
- Third instar: 0.14-0.18 mm
- Fourth instar: 0.22-0.32 mm
- Fifth instar: mean 0.38 mm (range 0.36-0.54 mm group)
- Sixth instar: mean 0.50 mm (range 0.36-0.54 mm group)

The fifth and sixth instars were distinguished within the overlapping size range using [[Harding's method]] (1949) for analyzing polymodal frequency distributions, which revealed two distinct size peaks.

**Cephalic Fan Filament Counts:**
Filament numbers in cephalic fans increased progressively:
- First instar: 15-18 filaments per fan
- Second instar: 19-22 filaments
- Third instar: 23-28 filaments
- Fourth instar: 28-35 filaments
- Fifth instar: 37-42 filaments
- Sixth instar: 44-49 filaments

**Morphological Characters:**
- First instar: Distinguished by presence of egg-burster on frons-clypeus
- Fourth instar: Six small round imaginal buds visible on each side of thorax (leg, wing, haltere, and pupal respiratory organ primordia)
- Fifth instar: Imaginal buds enlarged; pupal respiratory organ bud shows four pairs of closely coiled respiratory filaments
- Sixth instar: Imaginal buds very large; respiratory filaments clearly visible, darkening as pupation approaches; legs, wings, and halteres begin developing immediately before pupation

**Developmental Pattern:**
The approximate geometric size increase of frons-clypeus observed in the first three instars was not maintained in succeeding instars, indicating that larval development does not follow [[Dyar's Rule]] (1890), which predicts consistent geometric progression in sclerotized structure dimensions across instars.

**Taxonomic Implications:**
Results support [[Crosskey]]'s generalization that six larval instars are characteristic of [[Simuliidae]], extending [[Terterjan]]'s findings from [[Wilhelmia equina]] to [[Simulium ornatum]].
