# Training Local Citizens in Wetland Management: Cuatro Cienegas Reserve, Coahuila, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This USFWS–Mexico Cooperative Program grant proposal requests **$48,031.00 (US)** for one year to train local citizens and municipal public servants of [[Cuatro Cienegas]] in management of a newly created biological reserve of roughly 150,000 hectares. The applicants are the Municipality of Cuatro Cienegas de Carranza, [[Arizona State University]] (AZSU), and the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC). The project implements part of an Agreement of Mutual Assistance (AMA) between the municipality and DFC. Activities include site visits to conservation units in the western USA, a US team visit and workshop in Cuatro Cienegas, and conference participation. The document also includes a narrative on the basin's biodiversity, a budget, supporting documents, a project review form, and tax-status and agreement materials.

## Background & Description

The Cuatro Cienegas basin is an intermontane desert valley of roughly 30 by 40 km in Coahuila, Mexico. It contains thermal springs, creeks, marshes, and saline lakes fed largely by springs rising near encircling limestone mountains. The narrative, paraphrased from [[Minckley 1992]], reports at least 80 discrete springs surveyed, some with flows above 4.0 L/s, and discharges above 1.0 m³/s for individual springs.

The basin's biota is highly concentrated and partly endemic. Reported figures include:

- **15 fishes (12 endemic)**, four endemic reptiles, six crustaceans, at least 10 molluscs, more than 20 endemic plants, and numerous insects and scorpions.
- 64 amphibians and reptiles below 1000 m elevation, which the narrative states is more than 37% of the 170 species of the Chihuahuan Desert herpetofauna.
- More than 50 taxa known only from the basin, across plants, invertebrates, fishes, and reptiles.

Comparison sites cited in the text are [[Ash Meadows]] (five endemic fishes, one extinct) and [[Dalhousie Springs]] in Australia (six fishes, four endemic). The authors describe such clustering of biodiversity as exceptional. The basin was first surveyed for biology in the 1930s, and its full biotic diversity was recognized about 20 years later.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal describes several pressures and drivers:

- **Habitat change:** Mexican scientists reported "alarm at negative changes" in the basin, and US scientists documented degradation of some habitats.
- **Regional development:** The authors state that [[North American Free Trade Agreement]] (NAFTA) could bring unsustainable industrialization, pollution, and resource destruction to the borderlands unless environmental safeguards are enforced.
- **Desertification and climate:** The authors cite desertification as increasing and projected global warming as likely to intensify water limitation for now-mesic, aquatic environments.
- **Biogeographic significance:** The Chihuahuan and Sonoran deserts are described as supporting the largest indigenous aquatic biota persisting under arid conditions. Cuatro Cienegas is framed as a primary site for research and conservation with global relevance.

Conservation history includes a 1983 set-aside of some basin sections, 1980s–1990s government recognition of basin biodiversity, and inclusion of basin organisms on endangered and imperiled species lists (e.g., [[NOM-059-ECOL-1994]]).

## Key Findings

**Project objectives (one year):**
1. Promote contacts and direct observation at established preserves, refuges, and conservation facilities.
2. Build experience and expertise for municipal servants and local citizens in decision-making.
3. Focus on conservation of the basin's aquatic and semi-aquatic biota as part of a wider borderlands program.
4. Provide information and education capacity, including computer connectivity, literature, and materials.

**Work plan:**
- **Month 1:** Municipality selects participants (one municipal representative, one educator, two laypersons); AZSU/DFC plan visits.
- **Months 4 and 7:** Eight- to 12-day site visits to US reserves and refuges, including [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], Death Valley National Park, Ash Meadows, Big Bend National Park, and TNC preserves. Visits also include a [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] meeting as observers.
- **Month 9:** US team visit to Cuatro Cienegas (about 12 days), preserve tour, and a three-day workshop on problems, strategies, monitoring, and risk assessment.
- **Month 12:** Synthesis and final report, to be presented at the DFC annual meeting in La Paz, BCS, Mexico, in November 1996.
- Two Mexican participants attend each of the [[Society for Conservation Biology]] meeting and a DFC meeting in 1995.

**Monitoring:** Monthly telephone consultations, participant evaluations, and interim reports at month six.

**Agreement of Mutual Assistance (AMA), signed April 1994:** DFC (represented by E. Philip Pister) commits advice and technical assistance. The municipality (represented by Susana Moncada Diaz de Leon) commits to a registration system for research in the valley, dissemination of results, and maintenance of documentation. Local investigators must register their activities.

**Reviewer assessment:** A project review form by David L. Pearson (Smithsonian Institution) strongly recommends funding and rates most criteria 9 or 10 out of 10. The reviewer states that without this project the reserve "will most likely be a reserve on paper only."

**Supporting materials:** Tax-exempt status confirmation for Arizona State University under 26 U.S.C. § 115, Arizona constitutional and statutory provisions, a DFC web-presence notice, and a 1994 shipping record for documents to Mexico.
