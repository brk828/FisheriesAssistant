# Spawning Habits of the Largescale Sucker (Catostomus macrocheilus) at Stave Lake, British Columbia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This study by McCart and Aspinwall (1970) documents the first published description of spawning behavior in the [[Largescale Sucker]] (*Catostomus macrocheilus*), observed at [[Stave Lake]], British Columbia, during spring 1967. Researchers used direct daytime observation, 16mm color motion pictures, and infrared viewing apparatus for nocturnal spawning events. The species exhibits highly flexible spawning habits, utilizing inlet streams, outlet streams, and lakeshore gravel beds depending on local hydrological and habitat conditions. The [[Largescale Sucker]] is characterized as a **mass spawner**, with groups of males attending single females during a brief, violent "quivering" spawning act. Behavior closely parallels that of the closely related [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*), with which it is known to hybridize in overlapping ranges.

## Background & Description
The study notes distinct **sexual dimorphism** in spawning adults:
- **Size:** Males are noticeably smaller than females.
- **Coloration:** Males possess a distinct dark stripe running from the caudal peduncle to the snout; this stripe is typically poorly developed or absent in females (contrary to a prior hypothesis by Nelson (1968) that banding develops in females after repeated spawning, which this study could not confirm).
- **Fin Structure:**
  - Males possess an enlarged anal fin relative to females.
  - Males exhibit **nuptial tubercles** on the pelvic, pectoral, anal, and caudal fins.
  - These fins are smooth ("naked") in females, lacking tubercles.
- **Function:** The extended male anal fin appears to serve a hydrodynamic function, raising the caudal fin off the substrate to help maintain position against current flow.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Flexibility:** Spawning occurs across a wide variety of physical situations, including the upper/lower reaches of inlet streams (e.g., [[Devil's Creek]]), outlet streams, and lake gravel shorelines (e.g., [[Devil's Lake]]).
- **Substrate:** Preferred spawning substrate is fine, pea-sized gravel with an admixture of sand, though spawning was also observed in interstitial spaces between large boulders.
- **Hydrology:** At Murdo Creek, average water depth at spawning sites was 21 cm (range 15–30 cm), with average water velocities of 33 cm/sec (range 27–40 cm/sec). Notably, spawning in the upper Devil's Creek occurred in nearly standing water, highlighting habitat plasticity.
- **Thermal Triggers:** Spawning onset is likely temperature-regulated; no spawning was observed below 9°C (daytime temperature). Initial spawning was recorded at 10-12°C.
- **Temporal Patterns:** The spawning season is protracted, lasting at least 3-4 weeks (observed May 12 – June 8, 1967).
- **Diel Periodicity (Predation Avoidance):** A key ecological finding was the divergence in daily timing based on stream cover:
  - In exposed streams with low water levels and no vegetative cover (e.g., [[Devil's Creek]], [[Cold Creek]]), suckers entered strictly at dusk, spawned nocturnally (confirmed via infrared), and vacated the stream by morning—suggesting an anti-predator adaptation to exposure.
  - In larger streams with better natural cover (e.g., [[Sayres Creek]], [[Murdo Creek]]), fish resided continuously and spawned openly during daylight hours, regardless of cloud cover.
- **Egg Predation:** Male suckers were frequently observed in a "head-down, tail-up" feeding posture within spawning beds immediately following spawning acts, strongly suggesting cannibalism of freshly laid eggs (stomach contents were not examined to confirm).

## Key Findings
1. **Mass Spawning Behavior:** The species is confirmed as a mass spawner; of 9 complete spawning acts recorded, females averaged 2.8 attendant males (range 2–5) at the start of the act, increasing to an average of 5.3 males (range 3–8) by the act's completion.
2. **Spawning Act Duration:** The complete spawning act—characterized by violent quivering and water splashing—was brief, averaging 2.9 seconds (range 0.8–5.0 sec).
3. **Pre-Spawning Ritual:** Females initiate spawning by leaving concealment and swimming along the stream bottom (up to 50-60 ft), attracting mobile males who join and leave the escort group fluidly, while resident males show limited movement from established resting territories.
4. **Egg Deposition:** Eggs are adhesive and demersal, deposited both on the gravel surface and buried beneath it via the physical disturbance (quivering) of the spawning fish, which creates shallow depressions (one measured at 17 cm diameter, 4 cm depth).
5. **Comparative Taxonomy:** The observed spawning mechanics are highly similar to the [[White Sucker]] (*C. commersoni*), reinforcing prior findings (Nelson, 1968) that similarity in reproductive behavior facilitates natural hybridization between the two species in zones of geographic overlap in British Columbia.
