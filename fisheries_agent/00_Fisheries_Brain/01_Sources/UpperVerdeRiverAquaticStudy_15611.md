# Upper Verde River Aquatic Study

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1985 interagency baseline survey (USFWS, AGFD, USBR) characterizes the fish community and physicochemical conditions of the upper [[Verde River]], Arizona, in advance of a proposed subsurface water diversion by the City of Prescott and the Yavapai-Prescott Indian Reservation under the Central Arizona Project (CAP). Six stations spanning ~32 river miles were sampled for water quality, stream discharge, riparian vegetation, and fish assemblages. The study documented 13 fish taxa across five families, dominated numerically by native cyprinids and catostomids, and confirmed the presence of two state-listed species of concern: [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and the [[Colorado River Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta robusta*). No federally listed species were collected, though spikedace was under consideration for federal threatened status at the time. Water chemistry was stable, hard, alkaline, and of high quality, consistent with a limestone/dolomite-influenced desert stream supporting a warmwater fishery designation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **System type:** Perennial warmwater desert stream, hard/alkaline water chemistry (hardness >300 mg/L as CaCO₃; pH 7.6–9.4; alkalinity ~187 ± 16 mg/L as CaCO₃), influenced by limestone/dolomite geology. Dissolved oxygen high and stable (8.5 ± 0.8 mg/L). Turbidity low overall (mean 7.1 NTU) but elevated at mid-reach stations following thunderstorm inputs from tributaries.
- **Riparian vegetation:** Pinyon-Juniper series dominates uplands; riparian corridor is Mixed Broadleaf, with seep willow (*Baccharis salicifolia*) and desert willow (*Chilopsis linearis*) most common, plus scattered mature Fremont cottonwood (*Populus fremontii*) and Goodding willow (*Salix gooddingii*). Species richness of riparian trees increases downstream, with net-leaf hackberry and Arizona walnut appearing at Hell Canyon (Station 4), and Arizona sycamore and mesquite appearing at Station 6 near Sycamore Creek.
- **Aquatic macrophytes/algae:** Watercress (*Rorippa nasturtium-aquaticum*), *Scirpus* sp., and *Veronica* sp. dominant emergent macrophytes; algae limited to encrusting/short filamentous greens (*Cladophora glomerata*, *Hydrodictyon* sp.).
- **Habitat use by fishes:** Riffles supported the highest taxa richness and fish density (X̄ = 1.82 taxa/m²; up to 5.53 fish/m² in riffle habitat at some stations), followed by pools (X̄ = 0.73 fish/m²) and runs (X̄ = 0.37 fish/m²). A one-way ANOVA on fish abundance across riffle/run/pool treatments showed no statistically significant difference (p = 0.16), though sample size was limited and backwaters (important nursery/nesting habitat) were excluded from formal analysis due to insufficient replication.
- **Spatial distribution:** Species composition and taxon richness were largely consistent from upstream to downstream stations, with one notable exception — [[Spikedace]] was collected **only at the proposed diversion site** (Station 1), raising concern given the planned subsurface water withdrawal at that exact location.
- **Predator/wildlife interactions:** [[Osprey]] (*Pandion haliaetus carolinensis*, state Group 3 species) was observed capturing a sucker; sign of [[River Otter]] (*Lutra canadensis*, state Group 2 species — "in jeopardy") was also documented, indicating the river's role as a prey base for both resident and seasonal wildlife.

## Key Findings

- **13 fish taxa** representing **5 families** were collected from 5 localities (Morgan Ranch to Perkins Bridge) on the upper Verde River; no new drainage records and no federally listed species were found.
- **Native fishes comprised 92% of total catch**; introduced species (carp, red shiner, ictalurids, mosquitofish, smallmouth bass, green sunfish) made up the remaining 8% — comparable to native fish dominance patterns reported for [[Aravaipa Creek]], [[Bonita Creek]], [[Eagle Creek]], and the [[San Francisco River]] in Arizona.
- Two **state-listed (Group 3) species** were documented: **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) and **[[Colorado River Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta robusta*) — both classified as species "whose continued existence in Arizona may be in jeopardy in the near future" (AGFD 1982). Spikedace was additionally under review for federal threatened status, triggering potential Section 7 ESA consultation requirements for the diversion project.
- Spikedace was found **exclusively at the proposed diversion site**, a critical finding given plans for a subsurface water diversion at that location — highlighting potential conflict between water development and habitat for a sensitive species.
- Water quality across all 6 stations met or exceeded Arizona surface water standards for warmwater fishery and full-body-contact recreation designations, with **pH exceedances at Stations 1 and 3** (by 0.1 and 0.4 standard units respectively) being the only deviations noted.
- Stream discharge increased predictably downstream except at **Station 4 (Hell Point)**, which showed an anomalous decrease (Δ = –1.4 cfs), possibly due to subsurface flow loss through alluvium, unmeasured diversions, or sampling error.
- Flow at the diversion site is fairly uniform year-round (base-flow dominated), based on 10-year median monthly discharge data from the nearby USGS Paulden Gage.
- The upper Verde fishery was estimated to provide **$274,540/year** in angler economic value (headwaters to Camp Verde), compared to **$514,115/year** for the nearby East Verde River, underscoring the resource's comparatively low recreational visibility despite ecological significance.
- Riffle habitats supported disproportionately higher fish density and taxon richness relative to runs and pools, reinforcing the ecological importance of heterogeneous mesohabitat structure in sustaining the diverse native [[Desert Fish Assemblage]] of the Verde River system.
