## Executive Summary

This document is **not a biological or ichthyological research paper**; it is a committee report to the [[American Fisheries Society]] evaluating whether to establish a new quarterly scientific journal, separate from or replacing the Society's long-running publication, the [[Transactions Of The American Fisheries Society]]. The report traces the publication history of the Society (originally the *Proceedings of the American Fish Culturists' Association*), reviews prior experiments with quarterly publication (1914–1920), and evaluates three alternative publishing models. It reflects the Society's institutional concerns regarding editorial capacity, cost, membership growth, and the dissemination of fishery science rather than any specific taxonomic, morphological, or ecological findings.

## Key Findings

- **Publication history**: The Society's proceedings began publication in 1872; the title *Transactions of the American Fisheries Society* was adopted in the 1885 volume. Annual volumes were published continuously except 1914–1920, when [[Volume 44 (1914)]] onward briefly became quarterly issues without a title change.
- **Reversion to annual format**: Publication returned to a single annual volume with [[Volume 50]], primarily due to lower cost, better second-class mail eligibility, and reduced risk of members losing individual quarterly issues (leading to incomplete sets).
- **1947 crisis**: At the time of this report, [[Volume 74]], [[Volume 75]], and [[Volume 76]] were all delayed in printing/editing, prompting reconsideration of the publication model.
- **Three publication plans evaluated**:
  - **Plan A** — Continue single annual volume (status quo).
  - **Plan B** — Discontinue the *Transactions*; replace with a new quarterly journal incorporating Society business, symposia, and papers.
  - **Plan C** — Retain the *Transactions* as an annual volume for Society business/symposia, and create a *separate* new quarterly journal dedicated to formal scientific papers.
- **Rejected option**: Simply splitting the existing annual *Transactions* into four quarterly installments was unanimously rejected by the committee as costly and providing no real benefit in publication speed.
- **Trade-off analysis**:
  - Plan B/C would enable faster publication, decouple manuscript submission from the annual meeting schedule, and add modern journal features (notes section, book reviews, current literature, news items).
  - Both alternatives would increase editorial workload and printing/mailing costs relative to Plan A.
  - Plan B risks ending the ~80-year-old *Transactions* title/series; Plan C preserves it by segregating business/symposia content from scientific papers.
- **Committee recommendation** (4-to-1 majority): Adopt **Plan C** — retain the annual *Transactions* for Society business and symposia, and establish a new quarterly scientific journal for formal papers. Dissenting member ([[John Gottschalk]]) preferred Plan B but accepted Plan C.
- **Contingencies**: The committee urged that no immediate change occur until the Editorial Board cleared the backlog of delayed volumes (74–76), citing an anticipated delay to ~1950.
- **Floor discussion outcomes**: Members (Higgins, Langlois, Gottschalk, Van Oosten) debated timing and scope; the Society formally **accepted the committee's report** and voted to **extend the special committee's tenure** to develop a detailed follow-up proposal for the next annual meeting.
- **Rationale for a new journal**: Cited need to centralize the "widely scattered" fishery biology literature, increase both active and library membership, and modernize format/scope of fishery science publishing — foreshadowing the eventual creation of a distinct primary research journal by the Society (historically resulting in what became the modern [[Transactions Of The American Fisheries Society]] as a peer-reviewed quarterly).
