# Warmouth, Lepomis gulosus, a Freshwater Fish New to Canada

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This note documents the first confirmed occurrence of the [[Warmouth]] ([[Lepomis Gulosus]]), a warm-adapted [[Centrarchidae|centrarchid]] sunfish, in Canada. Specimens were collected from [[Lake Erie]] at [[Rondeau Provincial Park]] from 1966 to 1968 and from [[Lake Pond]] in [[Point Pelee National Park]] from 1983. The Point Pelee captures included 46 further specimens, and a breeding population appears to have become established there. The authors treat the species as a recent natural migrant, part of a continuing northward spread of freshwater fishes since the retreat of the [[Wisconsin Glacier]].

## Background & Description

Warmouth is a centrarchid with a large mouth. The document describes it as the latest freshwater fish added to the Canadian fauna through natural northward movement. The authors note that pioneer populations of such species often go undetected, or are mistaken for known species, for several years before they are documented. Since 1968, at least six warm-adapted species have been reported from southern Ontario.

The species' known range lies south of a line from central Wisconsin to Maryland. It extends southward to the Gulf coast (Florida to Texas) and westward from the Atlantic coast to New Mexico. The nearest populations are in Michigan tributaries of Lake Erie, and the species also occurs in Wisconsin (about 45°N, 89°W), as cited from [[Atlas Of North American Freshwater Fishes]] (Lee et al. 1980).

Identification notes from the document:

- **Versus [[Rock Bass]] (*Ambloplites rupestris*):** Warmouth have three anal spines, while Rock Bass have six.
- **Versus [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*):** Warmouth have many teeth on the tongue, round clear spots on the second dorsal fin (hard to see in preserved specimens), and dark bands radiating from the eye. Green Sunfish usually have an obvious black spot at the base of the last second-dorsal rays, and Warmouth lack this. Instead, Warmouth show a line of black pigment across the base of all dorsal rays.
- Breeding males of Warmouth show a bright orange spot at the base of the last dorsal fin rays.
- The authors warn that existing keys to Canadian centrarchids, written without reference to Warmouth, will probably identify Warmouth as Green Sunfish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All known Warmouth captures came from habitats typical of sunfishes. At Rondeau Provincial Park in 1968, the species was taken alongside other centrarchids, including Pumpkinseed (*L. gibbosus*), Bluegill (*L. macrochirus*), White Crappie (*Pomoxis annularis*), Black Crappie (*P. nigromaculatus*), and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). Other warm-water species in those catches were Bowfin (*Amia calva*), Brown Bullhead (*Ictalurus nebulosus*), and Yellow Perch (*Perca flavescens*).

The authors also note that the nearby [[Orange-Spotted Sunfish]] ([[Lepomis Humilis]]) has recently been recorded as new to Canada, at Cedar Creek (Lake Erie) and the Canard River (Detroit River). They suggest that Warmouth may also occur north of Lake Erie.

## Key Findings

- **First Canadian record:** Warmouth is documented in Canada for the first time, based on specimens from Lake Erie and nearby waters.
- **Earliest specimen:** One specimen was captured at Rondeau Provincial Park (42°17'N, 82°31'W) by Roger E. Roy on 5 June 1966 (Rondeau Park Museum RPM F103-66). Two more were taken there in 1967 and three in 1968.
- **Point Pelee records:** One specimen (ROM 42752) was captured by G. Mouland at Lake Pond, Point Pelee National Park (41°58'N, 82°31'W), on 31 March 1983. Between 3 June and 18 October 1983, a further 46 specimens were captured from the same site: 28 adults and 18 young-of-the-year. All but two were released alive. Two specimens (155 mm and 90 mm TL) were retained as reference material (ROM 43022).
- **Breeding population:** A breeding population appears to have been established at Point Pelee, even though no further specimens have been reported from the original Rondeau location.
- **Dispersal inference:** The authors consider it more likely that the pioneers moved around the lake shore than across it. Because of this, they would have expected the species to appear first at Point Pelee rather than Rondeau, which is an unresolved point in the record.
- **Morphology:** Meristic and morphometric characters of the ROM specimens (34267 from Rondeau; 42752 and 43022 from Point Pelee) all fall within published ranges for the species.
- **Survey effort:** Queries sent in late 1983 to all fisheries agencies along the Ontario shores of Lake Erie and the Detroit and St. Clair rivers produced no other records. Agencies along the Canadian Lake Erie shore and tributaries were asked to report the species to track its rate of establishment.
- **Prior notice:** The only earlier notation of the species in Canada was a brief note in a new-information section of the third printing (1979) of [[Freshwater Fishes Of Canada]] (Scott and Crossman 1973).
- **Acknowledged verification:** The 1966 specimen was tentatively identified by Bruce Thacker, and specimen identifications were verified by S. J. Nepszy and D. E. McAllister.
