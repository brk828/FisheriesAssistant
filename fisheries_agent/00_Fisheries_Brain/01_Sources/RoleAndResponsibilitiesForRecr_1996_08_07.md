# Role and Responsibilities for Recreational Fisheries - Partners' Perspective

#ichthyology #marine-biology #recreational-fisheries #fisheries-policy #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This 1996 report, produced by the [[Sport Fishing And Boating Partnership Council]] for the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Service), articulates the Service's comprehensive role in supporting recreational fisheries across the United States. Rather than presenting new biological data on fish species, the document defines seven core institutional responsibilities—ranging from habitat mitigation to law enforcement—grounded in existing Federal mandates. It emphasizes that healthy aquatic ecosystems underpin sustainable recreational fisheries and that no single agency can achieve conservation goals alone; success requires partnerships among Federal, state, and tribal governments, industry, and private stakeholders. The report was developed in response to [[Executive Order 12962]], which directs Federal agencies to collaboratively improve aquatic resources for recreational fishing opportunities via a national Recreational Fishery Resources Conservation Plan.

## Background & Description

The document traces its authority to the **[[Fish And Wildlife Act Of 1956]]** (16 USC 742a), which established the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife (later reorganized into the Service) and enshrined the public's right to fish for pleasure, enjoyment, and betterment. It also stems from **[[Executive Order 12962]]** (June 7, 1995), which mandated Federal agencies to cooperate with states and tribes to enhance the quantity, function, sustainable productivity, and distribution of U.S. aquatic resources.

The Service's stated **goal** for recreational fisheries is "to provide people with productive and sustainable recreational fisheries," underpinned by three principles:
- Healthy aquatic systems are essential for sustainable, productive recreational fisheries.
- All stakeholders—including private citizens—share responsibility for conserving and enhancing recreational fishery resources.
- The Service will provide leadership in implementing Executive Order 12962.

No specific fish species biology, morphology, or population data are presented; the focus is administrative and legislative, addressing how the Service should structure its recreational fisheries program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although not a biological study, the report situates recreational fisheries management within broader aquatic ecosystem health concerns:

- **Habitat alteration and mitigation**: Federally licensed, funded, or permitted water development projects (dams, hydropower, navigation projects) can permanently alter aquatic systems, sometimes preventing natural fish production and necessitating perpetual hatchery-based augmentation (Objective 1.A.3).
- **Anadromous, migratory, and interjurisdictional fishes**: Special emphasis is placed on restoring depleted stocks of these fish groups, which cross state and tribal jurisdictional boundaries and require coordinated multi-state management (Responsibility 2).
- **Nonindigenous nuisance species**: The Service is tasked with preventing introductions and controlling the spread of non-native species that impair recreational fisheries (Responsibility 2.C).
- **Water quality and quantity**: Restoring and protecting water availability for fishery resources and aquatic ecosystem integrity is identified as a core responsibility (Responsibility 4.C), acknowledging the interlinkage between water resource management and fish population sustainability.
- **Federal land and water holdings**: The Federal government controls roughly one-third of U.S. land, including more than half of five western states, much of which contains significant fish habitat; this creates a stewardship obligation for the Service, particularly on **[[National Wildlife Refuge]]** lands.
- **Demographic and environmental pressures**: The report notes population shifts toward coastal, southern, and western regions increasing competition for land and water use, alongside a growing "detachment of youth from the natural environment," both of which have downstream implications for aquatic conservation and angler recruitment.

## Key Findings

The Service's role is organized into **seven primary responsibilities**:

1. **Protect Resources or Mitigate Impacts**
   - Priority sequence: avoid/prevent → minimize → mitigate → compensate impacts from federally licensed/funded/permitted activities.
   - Includes perpetual augmentation of native/non-native fish populations via cultured (hatchery) fish where systems are permanently altered (Objective 1.A.3).
   - Authorities: [[Fish And Wildlife Coordination Act]], Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956, Watershed Protection and Flood Prevention Act, Rivers and Harbors Act of 1899, Federal Power Act, Clean Water Act, Mitchell Act, Water Resources Development Act of 1976, Pacific Northwest Electric Power Planning and Conservation Act, NEPA.

2. **Fishery Management Support**
   - Recover species listed under the **[[Endangered Species Act]]** to self-sustaining, fishable levels; assist states with species at risk of listing.
   - Restore depleted anadromous, migratory, or interjurisdictional fish stocks.
   - Prevent/control nonindigenous nuisance species.
   - Authorities: Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956, Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act, Endangered Species Act, National Aquaculture Act, Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration Act, Nonindigenous Aquatic Nuisance Prevention and Control Act. Specific watershed responsibilities cited: Klamath/Trinity River, Chehalis River, Russian River.

3. **Administer the Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration Program**
   - Funded by excise taxes/import duties on tackle and equipment, gasoline taxes, and interest.
   - **Key statistic**: By 1991, approximately 50 million anglers (age 6+) participated in fishing; anglers 16+ spent **$24.0 billion** on fishing-related recreation that year.
   - **State apportionment for FY1991**: $196,510,000 distributed to states via the Sport Fish Restoration Program.
   - Legislative basis: 1950 [[Dingell-Johnson Act]] (Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration Act) and 1984 [[Wallop-Breaux Amendment]].

4. **Land and Water Management**
   - Provide technical assistance and cultured fish on tribal/Federal lands when requested.
   - Manage recreational fishing on **National Wildlife Refuge System** properties (each refuge with fishery potential to have a management plan; Objective 4.B.1).
   - Restore/protect water quality and quantity for aquatic ecosystem integrity.
   - Authorities: Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act, [[Sikes Act]], National Wildlife Refuge System Administration Act, Recreational Use of Conservation Areas Act.

5. **Technical Support and Research**
   - Maintain core professional staff in management, fish health, and fish culture.
   - Transfer National Biological Service fisheries research/technology center resources to the Service to reduce redundancy (Objective 5.A.4).
   - Continue in-stream flow technology support for evaluating **[[FERC]]**-licensed hydropower projects.
   - Authorities: Cooperative Research and Training Units Act, [[Anadromous Fish Conservation Act]] (includes striped bass restoration research in Atlantic coastal waters), Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956, National Aquaculture Act of 1980.

6. **Education and Outreach**
   - Develop outreach programs emphasizing social value of recreational fishing, targeting urban populations.
   - Identify and address impediments to recruitment of new anglers.
   - Expand urban and disabled fishing access.
   - Promote aquatic stewardship education.
   - Authority: Fish and Wildlife Act of 1956 (public information distribution mandate).

7. **Enforce Federal Laws and Regulations**
   - Provide fisheries law enforcement to combat illegal activity.
   - Assist states in controlling interstate transfer of fish and aquatic species.
   - Authorities: **[[Lacey Act]]** (prohibits illegal interstate trade of fish/wildlife/plants), Fish and Wildlife Improvement Act of 1978 (law enforcement training and cooperative agreements).

### Strategic Implementation Framework
The report recommends a **3-step process** for translating these responsibilities into action under the 5-year **Recreational Fishery Resources Conservation Plan** (unveiled during National Fishing Week 1996):
- **Develop the Strategy**: Incorporate Section IV responsibilities; involve local stakeholders on regional issues.
- **Implementation**: Regional Directors and field offices execute strategy; Service Directorate periodically reviews status.
- **Reporting**: Annual regional reports compiled into a comprehensive agency report, delivered to the Partnership Council by March 31 annually beginning 1997; measurement criteria set by each region for consistency.
