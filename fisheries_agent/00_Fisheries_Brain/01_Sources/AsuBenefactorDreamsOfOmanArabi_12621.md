# Virginia M. Ullman, Arabian Oryx Conservation and the Proposed Phoenix Biopark

## Executive Summary

The article profiles [[Virginia M. Ullman]], a Phoenix philanthropist and conservationist who donated $500,000 to [[Arizona State University]] to establish the Ullman Professorship of Ecology in the Zoology Department. The piece traces her long involvement with the [[Arabian Oryx]] recovery effort ([[Project White Oryx]]), her role on the [[Phoenix Zoo]] board, and her proposal for a [[Phoenix Biopark]], an ecological preserve for research and education. The biopark remained a concept at publication: no site had been identified and no funds raised.

## Background & Description

- **Benefactor:** Virginia M. Ullman, Phoenix resident living at the foot of Camelback Mountain. Her civic roles include starting the Canal Bank Beautification Project in Scottsdale and serving on the boards of the Phoenix Zoo, [[Desert Botanical Garden]], Phoenix Art Museum and Phoenix Museum of Science and Technology.
- **Focal species:** The [[Arabian Oryx]], a large antelope with two long, rapier-like horns, which the article suggests may have inspired the unicorn legend. The oryx can go 11 months without water.
- **Interviewees:** [[W.L. Minckley]] (ASU zoology professor and longtime friend), [[Robert Breunig]] (Desert Botanical Garden director), and [[Michael H. Robinson]] (director of the National Zoo, Washington, D.C.).
- **Field context:** Minckley introduced Ullman to the [[Desert Fishes Council]], which she joined, and took her on field trips including the San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge in southwestern Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Oman and the oryx.** Oman (population about 2 million, roughly the size of New Mexico) had few roads, hospitals or schools until Sultan Qaboos bin Said took power in 1970. Water in the Bedouin camp had to be hauled 250 miles. Ullman visited in 1987 and again about a year before the article. Relatively few Americans had visited Oman in recent years.

**Oryx population history (as reported):**
- Hunters decimated the population about 30 years before the article.
- A 1962 expedition led by [[Ian Grimwood]], Kenya's chief game warden, captured two males and one female for a captive breeding program.
- Prince Bernhard of the Netherlands, then WWF president, chose the [[Phoenix Zoo]] (opened November 1962) to raise the animals. The London Zoo, Kuwait and Saudi Arabia later sent six more oryxes.
- By 1972 the species appeared extinct in the wild.
- By 1979 the first Phoenix-raised oryxes were released into part of their natural habitat in Oman.
- The world count was about 1,000 animals, many descended from Phoenix stock. Only about 100 animals were in Oman's herd at the time of Ullman's second visit.

**Biopark concept.** Robinson describes a biopark as a combination of natural history museum, anthropological museum, zoo, aquarium and botanical garden. Ullman envisions a collaboration among the Phoenix Zoo, Desert Botanical Garden and ASU. Breunig argues that zoos and botanical gardens have developed as separate entities, so the connections between their domains are often missed.

## Key Findings

- **Endowment:** Ullman's $500,000 gift established the Ullman Professorship of Ecology at ASU. The article states the university must now fill the chair with a "world-class ecologist."
- **Oryx recovery:** Captive breeding at the Phoenix Zoo, begun after a 1962 expedition, supplied animals for reintroduction to Oman starting in 1979, after the species was thought extinct in the wild by 1972.
- **Conservation status:** The world population had reached roughly 1,000, but only about 100 remained in Oman as of the second visit.
- **Biopark proposal:** A Phoenix biopark would link the Phoenix Zoo, Desert Botanical Garden and ASU. As of publication it had no site and no funding. Minckley called it "a prime possibility"; Breunig called the concept sound.
- **Leadership model:** Ullman's $50,000 personal gift to the Desert Botanical Garden capital campaign, with a challenge to the board to raise $100,000, is cited as an example of her motivational approach. The garden's board ultimately raised much more.
- **Philosophy:** Ullman states that "We're the custodians of the Earth," and that the environment must be "cared for and respected."
