## Executive Summary

This paper, presented by Bernard L. Berger of the Fish Control Laboratory in La Crosse, Wisconsin, summarizes laboratory and field research on **antimycin (trade name Fintrol)** as a selective piscicide for freshwater fisheries management. Derived from the actinomycete bacterium *[[Streptomyces]]*, antimycin was identified by Dunshee, Leben, Keitt, and Strong (1949) and later shown by Derse and Strong (1963) to be lethal to goldfish at concentrations as low as 1 p.p.b. while exhibiting low mammalian toxicity. The compound acts by selectively inhibiting electron transport in the oxidation-phosphorylation pathway, effectively halting cellular respiration in fish, likely via gill absorption. Extensive laboratory screening against 81 fish species and field trials across 17 ponds in six U.S. states demonstrated that antimycin is highly effective against undesirable species such as [[Common Carp]], while showing reduced efficacy against bullheads, gar, and bowfin. The chemical degrades rapidly in the environment and can be neutralized with potassium permanganate, making it an attractive tool for targeted fish population control.

## Background & Description

- **Mechanism of Toxicity:** Antimycin is a highly selective inhibitor of electron transport in the oxidation-phosphorylation (oxidative phosphorylation) system (Strong, 1956), effectively retarding cellular respiration.
- **Route of Uptake:** The toxicant is presumed to be absorbed through the **gills** of fish, consistent with its mode of action targeting respiratory function.
- **Species Sensitivity Variation:** Susceptibility to antimycin varies significantly across taxa, suggesting differences in gill permeability, metabolic rate, or respiratory enzyme systems among species (e.g., high sensitivity in [[Gizzard Shad]] vs. high resistance in [[Bullhead Catfish]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Range Tested:** Field trials spanned 17 ponds across six U.S. states (Wisconsin, Georgia, New Hampshire, New York, Arkansas, Nebraska), covering diverse freshwater conditions:
  - Total hardness: 10–220 p.p.b.
  - Temperature: 50–90° F
  - pH: 6.8–8.9
  - Turbidity: clear to moderate
  - Vegetation: light to moderately weed-infested waters
- **Water Chemistry Sensitivity:**
  - Antimycin degrades rapidly at **pH above 8.5**.
  - Slightly more effective in **soft water** than hard water.
  - Efficacy increases with **rising water temperature** across all species tested.
  - Complete degradation occurs in 2–7 days in moderately hard waters; up to 2 weeks in cool, soft waters.
- **Non-Target Ecological Impact:**
  - Minimal effect on **phytoplankton**, **zooplankton**, **aquatic insects**, and **amphibians** at effective fish-killing doses.
  - *[[Daphnia]]*: killed at 10 p.p.b. (24 hr, 54°F)
  - Damselfly larvae: killed at 100 p.p.b. (48 hr, 54°F)
  - Bullfrog tadpoles: killed at 40 p.p.b. (24 hr, 54°F)
  - Tiger salamanders: required 600 p.p.b. (24 hr, 54°F) — high resistance
- **Detoxification:** Antimycin can be neutralized in situ using **potassium permanganate (KMnO₄)**; 10 p.p.b. of toxicant deactivated by 1.0 p.p.m. KMnO₄, allowing controlled ecosystem recovery.

## Key Findings

### Origin & Chemistry
- Antimycin is an antibiotic isolated from *[[Streptomyces]]* bacteria (Dunshee, Leben, Keitt & Strong, 1949, University of Wisconsin).
- Extremely toxic to goldfish (1 p.p.b.) but low toxicity to mammals (Derse & Strong, 1963).
- Rapid environmental degradation supports use as a manageable fishery tool.

### Species Susceptibility (Laboratory Trials, 81 species tested)
**High sensitivity (lethal at 1 p.p.b.):**
- [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Lake Trout]], [[White Sucker]], [[Iowa Darter]], [[Yellow Perch]], [[Walleye]], [[Northern Pike]], [[Stoneroller]], [[Goldfish]], [[Common Carp]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Bigmouth Buffalo]], [[Brook Stickleback]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Pumpkinseed]], [[Bluegill]], [[Longear Sunfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[White Crappie]], [[Freshwater Drum]]

**Moderate resistance (up to 20 p.p.b.):**
- [[Shortnose Gar]], [[Bowfin]], [[Channel Catfish]]

**High resistance (up to 120 p.p.b.):**
- [[Black Bullhead]], [[Yellow Bullhead]]

### Egg Toxicity
- Rainbow trout, northern pike, white sucker eggs: killed at 10 p.p.b. (54°F, 2-hr exposure)
- Goldfish eggs: killed at 7.5 p.p.b. (63°F, 2-hr exposure)
- Carp eggs: killed at 5.0 p.p.b. (63°F, 2-hr exposure)

### Field Trial Results
- **Delafield, Wisconsin:** Acetone formulation, 10 p.p.b. killed 16 species (medium hard water); gar, bowfin, bullheads largely unaffected.
- **Georgia (soft water pond):** 1.2 p.p.b. acetone formulation killed 12 species.
- **New Hampshire (soft, cool water):** Sand formulation, 1.2 p.p.b. killed 17 species; only black bullheads survived.
- **Northern New York (medium hard water):** Sand formulation, 10 p.p.b. effective on trouts, cyprinids, sunfishes, and freshwater drum; bowfin and brown bullheads survived.
- **Stuttgart, Arkansas:** 7.5–10 p.p.b. produced 100% mortality across 12+ species; only catfish survived.
- **Valentine, Nebraska:** 10 p.p.b. eliminated large carp, fathead minnow, and green sunfish populations; some adult northern pike, largemouth bass, black crappie, and bullheads survived in one pond (possible incomplete toxicant distribution due to wind); complete kill in second pond.

### Practical & Regulatory Significance
- Effective dose range for target species (including problematic [[Common Carp]] populations): **5–10 p.p.b.**
- Sand-based formulation offers superior field logistics, particularly for temperature-stratified or weed-infested waters.
- **Ayerst Laboratories** (New York) filed a registration application with the USDA to market the compound commercially as **Fintrol**.

### Significance for Fisheries Management
- Antimycin represents a major advance over prior piscicides due to its:
  - Selectivity for problem species like carp
  - Irreversible toxic effect on most target fish
  - Lack of fish-repellent behavior (unlike other toxicants)
  - Effectiveness across a range of thermal and vegetative conditions
  - Rapid natural degradation and chemical neutralization option (KMnO₄)
