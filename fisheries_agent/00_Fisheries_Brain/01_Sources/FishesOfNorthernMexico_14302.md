# Faunal Relationships and Systematics of the Northeastern Mexican River Systems

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a scanned, partly garbled extract from a freshwater ichthyological survey of the river systems of northeastern Mexico. It covers the [[Rio Salado]], [[San Juan River System]], [[San Fernando System]], [[Rio Soto la Marina]], [[Rio Tamesi]] and [[Rio Panuco]], and it relates their fish faunas to the [[Rio Grande]] and to the Middle American fauna of the south. The authors argue that most of the fauna is North American in affinity, with a southern (Middle American) component that diminishes northward. They attribute several anomalous distributions to [[Faunal Capture]] and [[Stream Piracy]] at headwater divides, and they carry out a taxonomic revision of the [[Notropis Lutrensis]] group, in which they recognize [[Notropis Rutilus]] as a distinct species. OCR errors are common, so several counts and some species names could not be recovered with confidence.

## Background & Description

The study area lies on the eastern slope of the Sierra Madre Oriental and the northern lowlands of Tamaulipas and Nuevo León. The systems described are:

- **Rio Salado:** Rises near the centre of the [[Sonoran Upland]] and flows east across Nuevo León, then enters the Rio Grande in Tamaulipas.
- **San Juan System:** Its tributaries rise in the Sonoran Upland and enter the Rio Grande about one hundred miles above its mouth. The authors describe the fauna as "Holarctic" with a "neotropical" admixture.
- **San Fernando System:** Drains the eastern Sierra Madre and enters the [[Rio Grande]] between the Rio Grande and the Soto la Marina. The authors name it for the San Fernando region. Its fish collections were made with Meek and the authors near Linares.
- **Rio Soto la Marina:** A system of moderate size whose headwaters drain the southern face of the Sierra San Carlos. It discharges into the Gulf of Mexico just south of the Laguna Madre.
- **Rio Tamesi:** Its lower course borders the Rio Panuco and shares a substantial fauna with it.

The authors describe the eastern Mexican fauna as "essentially North American" in composition. Of the 20 known species of the Papaloapan system, the authors say that 16 (reported as 79%) are definitely North American. Other drainages follow a similar pattern, with the proportion of North American species declining southward.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution and biogeographic patterns**
- **Rio Salado:** Of 20 known forms, 15 also occur in the San Juan system, 5 in the San Fernando, 6 in the Soto la Marina, and 5 each in the Tamesi and Panuco. The authors note that one restricted species constitutes 5% of the known fauna of the basin.
- **San Juan:** Several widespread U.S. species reach their southern limit here, including *Ictalurus*-group catfishes and [[Dionda Episcopa]]. Other species extend southward to the San Fernando, Soto la Marina, Panuco, and Papaloapan systems.
- **Dispersal mechanisms:** The authors propose that *Dionda episcopa* reached the San Juan headwaters through [[Faunal Capture]] as headwater streams cut back into the Sierra Madre. A Sonoran-type cyprinid, *Tiaroga*, is recorded near Saltillo, which the authors also interpret as a capture event.
- **Gambusia and Platypoecilus:** Three species of *Gambusia* in the Soto la Marina are thought to have entered by headwater stream piracy. *Platypoecilus* may have arrived by the same route. Other fishes are known to be marine-derived and enter through lagoons or river mouths.

**Habitat and water quality**
- The authors quote an earlier description of the Rio Salado and related rivers as "brackish and unwholesome," owing to Lower Rio Grande salinity.
- Certain species, such as *Xiphophorus* and *Dionda* forms, occur only in the clear waters of limestone spring origin. The authors therefore treat water purity as a major control on local fish distribution.
- Several species are estuarine-tolerant and can live in brackish conditions. This supports the authors' explanation for their dispersal through lowland or lagoon routes.

## Key Findings

1. **Predominantly North American fauna.** Each system is dominated by North American species, and the proportion of northern forms is highest in the Salado and San Juan systems. The San Fernando is reported at 7 of 11 species (64%) North American.
2. **Southern limits.** Several widespread U.S. species reach their southern limit in the San Juan system. Some Middle American forms (for example, *Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus* and *Heterandria*-group poeciliids) extend northward into Texas and New Mexico.
3. **Faunal capture and stream piracy.** The authors invoke headwater capture to explain several disjunct distributions, including *Dionda episcopa* and *Tiaroga* in the San Juan, and *Gambusia* species in the Soto la Marina.
4. **Relations to the Panuco fauna.** The Soto la Marina fauna is linked to the Panuco through several distinctive types, including *Ictalurus* and *Gambusia* taxa. Seven Soto la Marina forms have close relatives in the Tamesi-Panuco fauna. Stream systems south of the Panuco share only 0 to 4 Soto la Marina species.
5. **Notropis lutrensis group revision.** Examination of [[Anal Ray Variation]] across populations led the authors to treat *Notropis rutilus* as a distinct species from *N. lutrensis*. Anal ray counts vary from north to south but show no intergradation in other characters. *Notropis proserpina* is described as intermediate in several respects but is regarded as distinct. The authors base the separation on type material and on counts from Texas and Mexican localities.
6. **Type specimens.** The document describes examined types of [[Notropis Proserpina]] and related forms, with meristic characters including scale counts, body depth, anal ray counts, and [[Nuptial Tubercles]] on breeding males. Full data are not recoverable from the scan.
7. **Limitations.** The authors note that the lower San Juan and San Fernando systems are incompletely sampled, and that additional southern elements may be found when their lower waters are explored.
