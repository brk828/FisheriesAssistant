# Inheritance of Melanophore Patterns and Sex Determination in the Montezuma Swordtail, *Xiphophorus montezumae cortezi*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 genetic study by Kallman, published in the [[New York Zoological Society]] journal *Zoologica*, examines how four melanophore (pigment) patterns are inherited in the Montezuma swordtail, *Xiphophorus montezumae cortezi*, a freshwater fish endemic to parts of the [[Rio Panuco]] system in Mexico. Three patterns are macromelanophore-based (At, Cam, Sc) and one is micromelanophore-based (Cb). The author concludes that the patterns are controlled by four unlinked loci, none associated with sex, and that no abnormal sex ratios were obtained in the breeding data. The study also documents incomplete penetrance of the spotted-caudal gene Sc, which can produce melanomas in a laboratory stock, and describes a newly recognised pattern, Cam (carbomaculatus).

## Background & Description

*Xiphophorus* is a genus of livebearing poeciliids that serves as a model for evolutionary and genetic research because many traits, including pigment patterns, sex determination, behaviour and gonopodial morphology, can be analysed genetically across related forms. Of the 17 described species or subspecies, nine are polymorphic for one or more macromelanophore patterns. The best-studied system is [[Xiphophorus Maculatus]], where macromelanophore patterns are sex-linked and governed by a complex locus.

*X. montezumae* is endemic to the Rio Panuco-Rio Tamesi drainage and comprises two subspecies. *X. m. montezumae* occurs in headwaters of the Rio Tamesi and northern and western tributaries of the Rio Panuco, while *X. m. cortezi* is restricted to headwaters of the Rio Moctezuma and Rio Tempoal (Rio Calaboza), draining into the Rio Panuco from the south. Most specimens were collected along the Pan American Highway between Tamazunchale and a point about 44 km north of that town.

The four patterns studied are:

- **Cb (caudal blotch):** a large oval area of micromelanophores in the proximal caudal fin.
- **Sc (spotted caudal):** irregular streaks or spots of macromelanophores in the caudal fin; in the laboratory it can give rise to melanomas.
- **At (atromaculatus):** numerous black macromelanophore spots on the flank and dorsal fin, concentrated below the dorsal fin.
- **Cam (carbomaculatus):** relatively few but large flank spots, rarely on the dorsal fin; newly described in this paper.

Laboratory stock "strain 38" was derived from fish collected in the Rio Axtla in 1939 and had been maintained for 21 generations at the time of writing.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish inhabit small streams and arroyos in the Rio Panuco drainage. Collections were examined from [[Arroyo Matlapa]], [[Arroyo Palitla]], the [[Rio Axtla]], the [[Rio Moctezuma]] at Tamazunchale, and the Rio Calaboza system in Veracruz. Preserved collections from the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ) spanning roughly four decades formed the basis of the natural-population analysis. Pattern frequencies varied among localities, and Cb was absent from two collections and absent from all four Rio Calaboza fish examined.

The author notes that melanomas were never seen in wild-caught or hybrid preserved material, suggesting that fish genotypes permitting tumour development may be eliminated by predators before preservation. The Sc frequency in the Rio Axtla population was estimated at about 59 percent, implying a potentially deleterious gene maintained at high frequency in nature, with modifiers presumed to keep its expression in check.

## Key Findings

- **Patterns and loci:** Four melanophore patterns are controlled by four loci that are unlinked and not associated with sex. Cam and At segregate independently, as do Sc and Cb.
- **Dominance:** At, Cam and Cb are dominant autosomal traits. Sc shows incomplete penetrance.
- **Cam recognition:** Cam fish have few large spots (1 to 16 on the flank in the experiments) and at most one or two large dorsal spots, whereas At fish have from one to several dozen small spots. The At pattern increases in spot number with age; the Cam pattern does not.
- **Cam inheritance:** Cam is inherited as a dominant trait not associated with sex; male and female frequencies did not differ significantly. Crosses of At Cam x wild-type gave a 2 At : 1 Cam : 1 wild-type ratio (41 At, 17 Cam, 23 wild-type).
- **Sc penetrance:** Penetrance was about 88 percent in the inbred stock as reported in the abstract, but the summary states about 22 percent of Sc fish in the stock do not express the gene. In hybrids with wild fish, penetrance fell to about 30 percent (22 to 30 percent across pedigrees).
- **Sc frequency:** Assuming 30 percent penetrance, the Sc frequency in the Rio Axtla population was estimated at up to about 59 percent, with roughly 83 percent of fish carrying Sc.
- **Melanomas:** Sc melanomas occur in strain 38 without prior hybridisation. Large tumours appeared in fish as young as seven months, and affected fish showed high mortality. No melanomas were found in wild or hybrid preserved material.
- **Sex determination:** No sex association was found for any pattern. Only two of the pedigrees (1800 and 2471) deviated significantly from a 1:1 sex ratio. The author argues that significant sex-ratio deviations cannot automatically be attributed to genetic sex reversal and that Zander's (1965) claimed XX males are not supported by the data.
- **Natural populations:** All major populations sampled were polymorphic for the four patterns. Cam made up 28 percent of fish from [[Arroyo Matlapa]] but was absent from one Rio Axtla arroyo. At frequencies ranged from 11 to 40 percent. Sc frequencies ranged from 6 to 43 percent.
- **Comparative context:** In *X. maculatus* and *X. variatus*, macromelanophore patterns are controlled by the same gene or supergene, contrasting with the three unlinked macromelanophore loci in *X. m. cortezi*.
- **Evolutionary hypothesis:** The author suggests that the macromelanophore factors of *cortezi* and *maculatus* may share a common ancestral complex locus, later separated by chromosomal rearrangement.
