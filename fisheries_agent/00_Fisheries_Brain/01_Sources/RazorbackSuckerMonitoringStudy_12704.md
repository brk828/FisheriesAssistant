# 1988 Razorback Sucker Monitoring Study Progress Report V

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species #fish-stocking

## Executive Summary

This progress report documents field efforts for the 1988 [[Razorback Sucker]] monitoring study under Contract FG-7494. The study focused on three main activities: monitoring dispersal and predation following barrier removal at [[Goose Flats Backwater]] isolated coves, removal and stocking of juvenile razorbacks from [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]], and electro-shocking surveys of the main channel [[Colorado River]] to recover stocked fish. A total of 816 razorbacks were marked across three coves prior to barrier removal on 21 November 1988. Post-barrier monitoring revealed minimal initial movement, strong association with aquatic vegetation ([[Myriophyllum]] sp.), and evidence of predation by [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Channel Catfish]]. Stocking efforts placed 702 razorbacks (≥200 mm TL) into the Colorado River at River Mile 87, with one recapture documented 3.5 miles downstream.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]], is an endangered native fish species of the [[Colorado River]] system. This monitoring study employed mark-recapture techniques to assess juvenile survival, movement patterns, and predation rates following release from protected rearing environments.

Three isolated coves at [[Goose Flats Backwater]] were seined prior to barrier removal, yielding 816 marked razorbacks: Cove 1 (N=132, ventral caudal fin clip), Cove 2 (N=394, dorsal fin punch), and Cove 3 (N=290, caudal fin punch). All fish received a right pelvic fin clip identifying them as 1988 stock. Fish ranged from 126-275 mm total length (TL).

At [[Niland Native Fish Ponds]], Pond #7 (0.5 acre) was seined on 7 December 1988, producing 2,277 razorbacks (mean 237 mm TL, range 147-315 mm TL, 3.7 fish/lb). Only fish ≥200 mm TL (N=702, mean 255 mm TL, range 203-320 mm TL, 2.4 fish/lb) were selected for stocking, with smaller individuals returned for further growout.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Goose Flats Backwater]] is located at River Mile 119 on the Arizona-California border. The backwater system includes multiple coves connected by channels to the main [[Colorado River]]. Habitat characteristics included extensive beds of aquatic vegetation ([[Myriophyllum]] sp.), bare rip-rap, inundated riparian vegetation, sand bars, and a small marina lagoon.

Water temperature declined from initial sampling to 55°F by the 4-week post-barrier survey, coinciding with winter onset and deterioration of [[Myriophyllum]] beds. Nearly all razorbacks captured (both inside and outside coves) were found along margins of or within weed beds, indicating strong preference for vegetative cover. Cove 1 lacked aquatic vegetation and yielded no recaptures.

Main channel [[Colorado River]] shoreline habitats surveyed (River Miles 88-62) included seven distinct types: natural bank vegetation with inundated riparian vegetation with/without weed beds, bare rip-rap, eddy pools, bare sand shoreline, dead cattail shoreline, pool areas downstream of mid-channel sandbars with weed beds, and thick overhanging [[Phragmites]] grass.

Predator assemblages included [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*). [[Flathead Catfish]] dominated in dense inundated riparian habitats.

## Key Findings

**Dispersal and Movement Patterns:**

- One day post-barrier removal: 118 razorbacks captured, with minimal movement as only 19 (16%) were found outside coves. The majority remained in rearing coves (46 in Cove 2, 17 in Cove 3). Maximum movement was approximately 1,000 m up backwater channels by a Cove 2 individual. No Cove 1 fish were recovered.

- One week post-barrier removal: 21 razorbacks captured, with only 5 (24%) outside coves. Catch within coves greatly reduced compared to previous week. Maximum movement increased to 1,800 m up backwater channels (away from main channel) by Cove 2 individuals. The razorback nearest the main channel was 1,000 m away.

- Four weeks post-barrier removal: Only 1 razorback captured in Cove 2 (167 mm TL, Cove 3 mark). Deterioration of [[Myriophyllum]] beds due to winter conditions (55°F water temperature) likely contributed to low capture success.

- Main channel shoreline electro-shocking the day after barrier removal found no razorbacks from the backwater-river confluence to 3.5 miles downstream. One razorback (183 mm TL, Cove 3 mark) was found approximately 100 m from the backwater-river confluence, suggesting movement toward main channel.

**Electro-shocking Efficiency:**

Capture rates in coves declined dramatically over time: 6.7 razorbacks/min (1 day), 2.4 razorbacks/min (1 week), 0.11 razorbacks/min (4 weeks). Channel section rates were consistently lower: 0.17 razorbacks/min (1 day), 0.05 razorbacks/min (1 week), 0 razorbacks/min (4 weeks).

**Predation at Goose Flats Backwater:**

- One day post-barrier: 9 [[Largemouth Bass]] and 2 [[Channel Catfish]] collected. One bass (11% occurrence) contained a razorback (146 mm TL). Both catfish stomachs empty.

- One week post-barrier: 5 [[Largemouth Bass]] and 1 [[Channel Catfish]] collected. Two bass (40% occurrence) contained razorbacks (150 and 160 mm TL). Catfish stomach empty.

- Four weeks post-barrier: 1 [[Channel Catfish]] collected with empty stomach.

- Overall predation incidence: 21% of [[Largemouth Bass]] stomachs (N=14, mean 410 mm TL, range 392-576 mm TL) contained razorbacks (mean 152 mm TL, range 146-160 mm TL). Zero percent of [[Channel Catfish]] stomachs (N=4) contained razorbacks.

**Stocking and Recovery:**

- 702 razorbacks (≥200 mm TL) stocked at River Mile 87 on 7 December 1988 in an unchannelized section of [[Colorado River]].

- Main channel electro-shocking survey (13-15 December 1988, River Miles 88-62, 265.8 minutes shock time) recovered one stocked razorback at River Mile 83.5, representing 3.5 miles downstream movement. Fish (217 mm TL) was found along dead cattail shoreline, 3 m from California bank at 2.5 m depth, near a small shoreline point and associated eddy pool.

**Main Channel Predators:**

33 predators examined from main channel survey: 29 [[Flathead Catfish]] (mean 770 mm TL, range 450-1,120 mm TL), 1 [[Channel Catfish]] (600 mm TL), 3 [[Largemouth Bass]] (mean 450 mm TL, range 388-543 mm TL), and 1 [[Striped Bass]]. Zero razorbacks found in any predator stomachs (0% occurrence). Two [[Flathead Catfish]] (912 and 1,000 mm TL) each contained one [[Tilapia]] (385 and 360 mm TL respectively), demonstrating predation on prey exceeding maximum stocked razorback size (320 mm TL).

**Additional Surveys:**

C-10 Backwater electro-shocked on 21 December 1988 (8 river miles downstream of [[Goose Flats Backwater]]) yielded no razorbacks.
