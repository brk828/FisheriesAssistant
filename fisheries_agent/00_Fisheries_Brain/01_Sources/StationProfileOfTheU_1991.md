# Station Profile of the Uvalde National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #aquaculture #fisheries-management #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #Moronidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region Two) administrative and operational profile of the **[[Uvalde National Fish Hatchery]]**, a federal warmwater fish production facility located in Uvalde, Texas. Established in 1935 under the **[[White Act]]** (46 Stat. 371), the station's mission is to produce and distribute warmwater game fishes to federal and state waters, including Corps of Engineers (COE) and Bureau of Reclamation (BOR) waters, as well as tribal and military lands. The report summarizes physical infrastructure, funding/personnel trends (FY1990–1994), and fish distribution data (FY1989–1991), during which the station distributed over 1.2 million fish of four species to five states in FY1991. The profile emphasizes the hatchery's role in supporting the Service's broader stocking and habitat-support mission rather than presenting original biological or ecological research.

## Background & Description

This is a station/programmatic profile rather than a species-specific morphological study; no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data are presented for the species reared. The four species produced and distributed by the hatchery are:

- **[[Channel Catfish]]** (*Ictalurus punctatus*) — family [[Ictaluridae]]
- **[[Florida Largemouth Bass]]** (*Micropterus salmoides floridanus*) — family [[Centrarchidae]]
- **[[Striped Bass]]** (*Morone saxatilis*) — family [[Moronidae]]
- **[[Goldfish]]** (*Carassius auratus*) — family [[Cyprinidae]], likely reared as forage/prey fish rather than a stocked game species

No size-at-release, age class, or growth performance data are included in the source text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Facility type:** Warmwater fish hatchery operating 47 ponds (mostly one-acre) on a 100-acre site, with four ponds being subdivided into smaller production units — indicating active management of rearing habitat heterogeneity for different species/life stages.
- **Production role:** The hatchery functions as a supply node within a broader **[[Warmwater Game Fish]]** stocking network, distributing fish to federal reservoirs/impoundments (via FWS and other federal agencies such as COE/BOR), state waters, and lands managed for tribal (Indian) and military purposes.
- **Geographic distribution reach (FY1991):** Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, Oklahoma, and Texas — indicating the hatchery supports fisheries management across a multi-state, semi-arid Southwestern U.S. region, likely supplementing reservoir and impoundment fisheries where natural recruitment is limited.
- **Human interaction/education:** The station also serves an educational/outreach niche, with ~4,380 visitors annually (primarily school children in May), reflecting a public engagement role alongside production.
- **Institutional linkage:** Operates under the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Role and Responsibilities Statement, linking hatchery production to federal land and water management goals, distinguishing it from purely commercial aquaculture.

## Key Findings

- **Establishment & Authority:** Founded in 1935 under the **[[White Act]]** (46 Stat. 371); administered under USFWS Region Two, led by Project Leader Lloyd B. Strobeck.
- **Infrastructure:** 47 ponds (mostly 1 acre each) occupy slightly over half of the station's 100-acre footprint; five service buildings, three small buildings, and four residences comprise the remaining infrastructure. Four ponds were being subdivided into smaller units at time of reporting, suggesting an operational shift toward more flexible or intensive pond management.
- **Funding trends (1990–1994):** Operations budget rose from $216.0K (1990 actual) to an estimated $305.0K (1994); maintenance budget rose modestly from $34.5K to an estimated $40.0K; FTE staffing increased from 5.25 to 6.3 and remained stable through 1994 estimates. Cost recoverables ranged $8.5K–$15.6K with no associated FTE.
- **Personnel changes:** Two employees transferred to other USFWS field stations in the reporting year and were replaced by new hires from outside the Service.
- **Distribution volume trend (FY1989–FY1991):**
  - FY89: 1,830,651 total fish distributed (211,892 FWS other-federal waters; 513,648 other federal waters; 1,105,111 state waters; 0 private waters)
  - FY90: 1,478,536 total (14,210 FWS; 1,012,125 other federal; 452,201 state; 0 private)
  - FY91: 1,250,585 total (78,518 FWS; 1,090,600 other federal; 81,467 state; 0 private)
  - Notable trend: a marked and consistent decline in total fish distributed year-over-year from FY89 to FY91, alongside a major relative shift toward "other federal waters" (e.g., COE/BOR reservoirs) and away from state waters distribution, particularly steep between FY89 and FY90/91.
- **FY1991 species/state distribution:** Four species (Channel Catfish, Florida Largemouth Bass, Striped Bass, Goldfish) were distributed to five states (Arizona, New Mexico, Nevada, Oklahoma, Texas), with over 1.2 million fish shipped, indicating the hatchery's continued role as a significant regional supplier despite the declining multi-year total.
- **Data scope note:** Distribution figures explicitly exclude eggs and fry, meaning true total production output is likely higher than the reported adult/fingerling distribution numbers.
- **Public engagement:** Approximately 4,380 visitors toured the facility in the reporting year, concentrated among school groups in May, highlighting an active outreach/education component of the station's mission.
