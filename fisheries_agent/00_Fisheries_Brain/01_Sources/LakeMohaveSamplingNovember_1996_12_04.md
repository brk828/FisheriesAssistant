# Lake Mohave Sampling, November 1996

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This report documents a 4-day fisheries monitoring survey conducted by Arizona State University (27–30 November 1996) on [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] spanning Arizona and Nevada. The primary objective was to monitor the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) population, with secondary efforts targeting stocked [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) in a backwater habitat. Sampling employed trammel nets across main-lake sites and hoop/trammel nets in a designated backwater nursery area. Seven fish species were documented overall, with a notable emphasis on recapture data for tagged razorback suckers, indicating survival and persistence of previously stocked or marked individuals.

## Background & Description
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): Adults captured ranged in **total length (TL) 550–640 mm**, consistent with mature adult size class for this species.
- **Bonytail Chub** (*Gila elegans*): A size threshold of **TL > 250 mm** was used as the minimum size for PIT tagging eligibility, distinguishing "taggable" from "sub-taggable" juveniles.
- No detailed morphometric data were reported for the other five incidentally captured species: [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Bluegill]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use**: Razorback suckers were sampled across a range of depths (1–10 m) in main-lake habitats including shoreline zones, embayments, and coves (e.g., [[Carp Cove]], [[Cottonwood East]], [[N. Mesquite Cove]], [[Tequila Cove]]), reflecting broad habitat occupancy by adults in the reservoir.
- **Backwater nursery habitat**: [[Yuma Cove]] backwater served as a distinct, likely predator-refuge or stocking/rearing habitat for juvenile razorback sucker and bonytail chub, sampled separately using hoop nets and trammel nets rather than main-lake gear.
- **Predation pressure**: One sub-taggable bonytail chub was documented as **eaten by crayfish** while in captivity/handling, highlighting crayfish as a predation or scavenging threat to juvenile native fishes in backwater settings — a relevant ecological interaction given ongoing concerns about invasive crayfish impacts on native Colorado River fish recruitment.
- **Community composition**: The lake supports a mixed assemblage of native (razorback sucker) and non-native/sport fish species (carp, channel catfish, yellow bullhead, striped bass, largemouth bass, bluegill), typical of a Lower Colorado River reservoir with a history of both native fish conservation stocking and sport fish introduction.

## Key Findings
- **Species richness**: Seven fish species were collected in total during main-lake sampling: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Common Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Bluegill]].
- **Razorback sucker abundance**: 22 adult razorback suckers were captured (TL 550–640 mm) using six 91-m trammel nets fished across a total effort of **30.06 units** (1 unit = 100 m of netting fished for 24 hours).
- **Recapture/tagging rate**: 5 of 22 captured razorback suckers (**23%**) bore pre-existing tags, indicating a substantial proportion of the adult population consists of previously marked individuals.
- **Stocking origin**: None of the recaptured tagged razorback suckers originated from recent stocking events, suggesting these are older, established individuals rather than recently released fish — relevant to assessing long-term survivorship of the wild/established population versus recruitment from ongoing augmentation efforts.
- **Yuma Cove backwater results**: Targeted sampling yielded 1 razorback sucker and 6 bonytail chub.
  - The razorback sucker and 1 bonytail chub met the size threshold (TL > 250 mm) for PIT tagging and were subsequently released into the main lake.
  - 1 sub-taggable bonytail chub was lost to **crayfish predation**.
  - The remaining 4 sub-taggable bonytail chub were returned untagged to the Yuma Cove backwater, presumably to continue growth before future tagging/release.
- **Monitoring implications**: This survey provides ongoing population monitoring data critical for the [[Razorback Sucker Recovery Program]] and highlights [[Bonytail Chub]] as a co-occurring, critically imperiled species benefiting from similar backwater rearing and tagging protocols in the [[Lower Colorado River]] basin.
