# Fishery Resurvey of Lower Clear Creek, Navajo County, Arizona (August 2005)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This report documents a second fish survey of lower [[Clear Creek]], a tributary of the [[Little Colorado River]] in Navajo County, Arizona, conducted August 17-19, 2005. It was prepared by Robert W. Clarkson (Bureau of Reclamation) and Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University). The survey supported a biological assessment and environmental impact statement for a proposed [[C-Aquifer]] well-field development, and was funded by Southern California Edison.

The resurvey confirmed that the fish fauna is numerically dominated by nonnative species and that nonnative crayfish are exceptionally abundant. It also found reproducing, multi-age populations of native [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Little Colorado Sucker]] above [[Clear Creek Reservoir]], which the authors suggest is the largest remaining roundtail chub population center in the Little Colorado River basin. The federally threatened [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] was again not detected. The authors call for a nonnative fish and crayfish control program.

## Background & Description

**Purpose.** The survey was needed because a prior 2004 study (Clarkson and Marsh 2005) detected the petitioned roundtail chub in lower Clear Creek but not the spinedace, and used relatively low sampling effort. The 2004 sampling was limited by logistical problems, so a resurvey was requested by the project's private and federal partners.

**Study area.** Three Clear Creek reaches and one [[Chevelon Creek]] site were sampled:

- **Reach 1 (Moon Canyon pool):** A roughly 0.5 km perennial pool in an otherwise ephemeral reach, formed above an ephemeral tributary known locally as [[Moon Canyon]]. The pool is deep (>3 m) and turbid, bounded by canyon walls about 50 m high, and maintained by surface runoff impounded by a debris fan. Substrates are sand, gravel, and boulders.
- **Reach 2:** A series of pools and riffles near the origin of perennial flow, about 3 km downstream of Reach 1. Water is clear at base flow with specific conductance above 1,200 µS/cm. Perennial flow results from channel incision into the Coconino sandstone of the C-Aquifer. Canyon walls are about 75 m high and substrates are mostly bedrock and sand.
- **Reach 3:** A broad, shallow (<10 cm) marshy channel flowing into an unlined aqueduct (up to 50 cm deep) fed by seepage below [[Clear Creek (McHood) Reservoir]]. Substrates are silts and clays with organic matter. Vegetation included an undetermined buttercup (Ranunculus sp.), tule (Scirpus sp.), duckweed (Lemna sp.), and dense tamarisk (Tamarix sp.) along the lower banks.
- **Chevelon Creek:** A single long pool, about 10 m wide, near its confluence with the Little Colorado River, with little discernible flow, firm silt substrate, and tamarisk-dominated banks. This site was sampled opportunistically and only cursorily.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Sampling methods.** Reaches 1 and 2 were repeat surveys of the 2004 sites. Reach 3 was chosen for accessibility to flowing water. The gear used included:

- [[Seine Netting]] (two seine types)
- [[Gill Netting]] (experimental multifilament nets)
- Trammel nets
- [[Minnow Trapping]] (baited with commercial fish feed)
- [[Electroshocking]] (Smith-Root Model 12-A backpack unit) with dip nets
- Stand-alone dip nets

Effort was recorded where meaningful. Young-of-year captured in large numbers in seine hauls were not counted; instead, relative abundance was categorized as rare (<10), common (10-100), or abundant (>100). Voucher specimens and photographs documented records. Fin clips and whole specimens of roundtail chub and [[Plains Killifish]] were preserved in 95% ethanol, and additional specimens were fixed in formalin for deposition in the [[Arizona State University Collection of Fishes]].

**Listing status.** Roundtail chub was petitioned for listing, and Little Colorado spinedace is federally threatened. Chevelon Creek is designated critical habitat for spinedace, according to the report.

**Habitat condition.** Nonnative vegetation (tamarisk) dominated several banks, and the Reach 3 habitat was judged to be in poor condition, which made the presence of young chub there unexpected.

## Key Findings

**Species captured.** Nine fish species were recorded across the sites (the report names both native and nonnative taxa). The main species were:

- [[Green Sunfish]] (nonnative), which numerically dominated lower Clear Creek.
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (native), with multiple age classes, including young-of-year, in Reaches 1 and 2.
- [[Little Colorado Sucker]] (native), with multiple age classes in Reaches 1 and 2.
- [[Fathead Minnow]] (nonnative), absent from Reach 1 in 2005 but present in 2004.
- [[Plains Killifish]] (nonnative), detected for the first time in the basin.
- [[Common Carp]] (nonnative), observed as adults at Chevelon Creek.
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (nonnative), the only fish captured at Chevelon Creek.
- [[Rock Bass]], which was not found in lower Clear Creek in 2005 after being recorded previously only just above Clear Creek Reservoir.

**Roundtail chub.** Reproduction and recruitment occur in the Moon Canyon pool and downstream perennial reaches despite nonnative dominance. The authors estimate that the Moon Canyon pool population "undoubtedly numbers in the hundreds." They describe it as possibly the largest remaining roundtail chub population center in the Little Colorado River basin. The species was historically widespread (Voeltz 2002), but before this survey it had been found only in scattered, low-number collections in Chevelon Canyon, East Clear Creek, and upper Clear Creek.

**Young-of-year chub below the reservoir.** A single young-of-year roundtail chub captured below Clear Creek Reservoir (Reach 3) was an unexpected and enigmatic finding. Arizona Game and Fish Department surveys of the reservoir in 1999 and 2003 did not detect the species. The authors consider reservoir reproduction with spillway transport during floods to be possible but uncertain, and they judged in-stream reproduction below the reservoir unlikely given the habitat condition.

**Little Colorado spinedace.** The species was again not detected in lower Clear Creek. The authors caution that this does not prove absence, because the species is known for extreme population fluctuations (Minckley and Carufel 1962; Miller 1963).

**Missing native species.** Speckled dace, bluehead sucker, and Little Colorado spinedace were not found, although they likely occupied lower Clear Creek historically. The authors see this as evidence of continuing deterioration of the native fish community.

**Crayfish.** Nonnative [[Northern Crayfish]] (Orconectes virilis) were extremely abundant in the Reach 1 pool, often exceeding fish numbers in seine hauls. They had begun consuming roundtail chub and sucker in entanglement nets, and they were also abundant in Reach 2 but absent from Reach 3 and Chevelon Creek.

**Management recommendation.** The authors "strongly recommend" starting a nonnative fish and crayfish control program in the basin before the Moon Canyon chub population is lost. They note that control options, especially for crayfish, are few.

**Broader conclusions.** The authors argue that native fish populations may disappear from a stream for years or decades and then reappear, so the apparent absence of spinedace does not settle the question. They also note that significant native fish discoveries continue more than 50 years after Miller (1961) and more than 30 years after the [[Endangered Species Act]]. They conclude that basic distribution data for native nongame fishes remain sparse, and that the continued expansion of nonnative fishes makes further investigation urgent.
