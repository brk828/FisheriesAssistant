# Hazard Assessment of Selenium to Endangered Razorback Suckers (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 2002 paper in *The Science of the Total Environment* assesses the hazard posed by [[Selenium]] to the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] in the upper [[Colorado River Basin]]. The assessment draws on two reproduction studies (1995–1996 and 1996–1997) conducted at three sites near Grand Junction, Colorado. Using the [[Lemly Hazard Assessment Protocol]] and a [[Margin of Uncertainty]] (BEC/EEC quotient) approach, the authors conclude that selenium contamination adversely affects the reproductive success of razorback suckers. The Horsethief site received a moderate hazard rating, while Adobe Creek and North Pond received high hazard ratings.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered fish indigenous to the upper Colorado River basin. It was selected because it is endangered and native, not because it is the most sensitive species.
- **Co-listed endangered fishes:** [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]] also inhabit the upper basin and are covered by the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]], a 15-year effort started in 1987.
- **Population status:** The middle Green River population was estimated at about 10⁵ individuals in 1988 and 300–600 in 1992. Only 10 fish were recorded in the upper Colorado River between 1989 and 1996.
- **Historical context:** Selenium contamination in the Colorado River basin predates mainstream dam construction and has been hypothesized to have contributed to the decline of native fishes.
- **Restoration context:** The [[Floodplain Habitat Restoration Program]] reconnects floodplain habitats to the main channel to benefit razorback sucker larvae and adults. Site selection aimed to avoid selenium contamination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study sites** (all near Grand Junction, CO, USA):
- **Horsethief site** (Horsethief Canyon State Wildlife Area): hatchery ponds used for endangered fish propagation, assumed to have little or no selenium; about 19 km west of Grand Junction city limits.
- **Adobe Creek site:** a dyked tertiary channel, assumed to have moderate selenium; about 5 km west.
- **North Pond site** (Walter Walker State Wildlife Area): assumed to have elevated selenium; about 0.5 km southwest.

Adult fish were held in specific enclosures during exposure, so their exposure was controlled rather than reflecting free movement in the river.

**Other contaminants:** Several inorganic elements were measured (including aluminum, arsenic, boron, cadmium, copper, iron, lead, manganese, nickel, and zinc). Only selenium reached concentrations reported to cause adverse effects. The authors cite parallel cases (Belews Lake, Hyco Reservoir, Kesterson-type irrigation drainage systems, the Blackfoot River, and the San Joaquin Valley) where selenium was the sole element of concern. Skorupa (1998) reviewed twelve selenium poisoning cases in nature.

**Other threats to the upper basin fishes** (USFWS, 1987) include stream alteration, water withdrawals, dewatering, loss of spawning and nursery habitat, flow regime changes, nonnative species competition, parasitism, and altered food base.

## Key Findings

**Reproduction studies and selenium uptake**
- Adults readily accumulated selenium in tissues, including eggs.
- Food organisms containing 4.6 µg/g selenium caused increased larval mortality. An on-site toxicity test using zooplankton from Horsethief East Wetland at that level caused nearly complete mortality in larvae.
- Adults were exposed for 9 months, then hand-spawned in late spring when water temperatures matched natural spawning.

**Hazard ratings (Lemly four-component protocol)**

| Site | 1995–1996 rating | 1996–1997 rating |
|---|---|---|
| Horsethief | Moderate (total 14) | Moderate (total 14) |
| Adobe Creek | High (total 20) | High (total 17) |
| North Pond | High | High |

Sediment at Adobe Creek was rated minimal in 1995–1996 and low in 1996–1997, yet water, invertebrate, and fish egg selenium levels were rated high.

**Protocol limitations identified by the authors**
- The protocol uses benthic invertebrates rather than water-column zooplankton as the dietary component.
- Water, fish egg values came from hatchery ponds, while sediment and invertebrate values came from Horsethief East Wetland, which may have underestimated the true hazard.
- Sediment rich in organic matter may hold a large selenium reservoir.
- The dietary toxic threshold of 3 µg/g suggests the high-hazard cutoff (>5 µg/g) may be too permissive.

**Margin of uncertainty approach**
- Comparing biological effects concentrations (BEC) with environmental concentrations (EEC) gave a high hazard for razorback sucker, consistent with the protocol.
- Margins of uncertainty below one suggest adverse effects are likely.

**Conclusions**
- Both assessment approaches indicate selenium in the upper Colorado River basin adversely affects razorback sucker reproductive success.
- The authors state the assessment is conservative, because the larval toxicity test was more severe than the protocol predicted for Horsethief.
- Recovery planning should include selenium screening of potential restoration sites in the upper basin, alongside control of other factors in the decline of endangered fishes.
