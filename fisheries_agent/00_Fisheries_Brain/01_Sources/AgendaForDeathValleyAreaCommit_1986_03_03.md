# Status of Death Valley Area Fishes (1986 Desert Fishes Council Agenda)

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative agenda from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] for its 1986 [[Death Valley Area Committee]] meeting, held at Death Valley National Monument. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological data, it catalogs a suite of highly endemic, range-restricted desert fish taxa of conservation concern in the Death Valley hydrographic region, and outlines policy, legal, and management issues affecting their survival — including habitat rehabilitation, state and federal endangered species funding, water diversion threats, and pending legislation (e.g., the [[Gramm-Rudman-Hollings Act]] and California endangered species law). The species list functions as a conservation status roster for some of the most narrowly distributed vertebrates in North America, many restricted to single springs or spring systems.

## Background & Description
The source text contains no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical descriptions of the listed taxa. It is a policy/status agenda rather than a taxonomic or morphological study. Morphological differentiation among these species and subspecies (e.g., distinguishing [[Cyprinodon nevadensis]] subspecies such as the [[Ash Meadows Pupfish]], [[Warm Springs Pupfish]], [[Saratoga Springs Pupfish]], and [[Amargosa Pupfish]]) is implied by their subspecific taxonomic status but not detailed here. Readers seeking morphological data should consult primary taxonomic literature (e.g., Miller 1948, La Bounty & Deacon) for these forms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
All listed taxa are endemic to isolated, thermally and chemically extreme aquatic habitats within the Death Valley hydrographic system, an endorheic (closed) desert basin with no ocean outlet. Habitats include:

- **Spring systems**: [[Devils Hole Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon diabolis*) — the most range-restricted vertebrate known, confined to a single geothermal pool at Devils Hole.
- **Ash Meadows spring complex**: [[Ash Meadows Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus nevadensis*), [[Ash Meadows Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis mionectes*), and [[Warm Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis pectoralis*).
- **Amargosa River drainage**: [[Amargosa River Dace]] populations (*Rhinichthys osculus* subsp.), [[Amargosa Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis amargosae*), [[Saratoga Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis nevadensis*).
- **Owens Valley system**: [[Owens Chub]] (*Gila bicolor snyderi*), [[Owens Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus* subsp.), [[Owens Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon radiosus*), and [[Owens Sucker]] (*Catostomus fumeiventris*).
- **Mojave River system**: [[Mohave Chub]] (*Gila bicolor mohavensis*), threatened by proposed water diversions addressed in the agenda.
- **Death Valley salt pans and playas**: [[Cottonball Marsh Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon milleri*) and [[Salt Creek Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon salinus*), inhabiting hypersaline remnant waters.
- **Colorado Desert/Salton Sink**: [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), extending the geographic scope beyond Death Valley proper.

These species collectively occupy some of the most extreme lentic and lotic freshwater niches in North America — small springs, marshes, and saline creeks — subject to high temperature, salinity, and habitat fragmentation, with populations vulnerable to groundwater pumping, water diversion, and habitat degradation.

## Key Findings
- The [[Desert Fishes Council]] Death Valley Area Committee, chaired by [[E.P. Pister]], convened to review conservation status of 16 fish taxa endemic to the Death Valley hydrographic region.
- Agenda items link fish conservation directly to policy and funding mechanisms: implementation of the [[Fish Slough A.C.E.C. Management Plan]] (Inyo/Mono counties, California), the [[Ash Meadows Rehabilitation Plan]], and status of state nongame/endangered species funding.
- Federal budget legislation ([[Gramm-Rudman-Hollings Act]]) was flagged as a potential threat to habitat and species recovery funding.
- Proposed water diversions from the [[Mojave River]] were formally protested due to threats to [[Mohave Chub]] habitat.
- The committee considered potential affiliation with the [[California Desert Protection League]], indicating coordination with broader desert conservation advocacy.
- The 16 taxa reviewed represent a mix of full species and subspecies, several confined to single spring systems (e.g., *Cyprinodon diabolis* at Devils Hole), illustrating extreme micro-endemism driven by Pleistocene pluvial lake desiccation and subsequent isolation in the Death Valley system.
- The document underscores the interdependence of legislative, regulatory, and funding processes with the survival of narrowly endemic desert fishes, rather than providing new empirical biological data.
