# Ictalurus chihuahua, a New Catfish from New Mexico, Texas and Northeastern Mexico, with Remarks on the Status of Ictalurus lupus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This near-final draft manuscript (dated October 7, 1998) by Julian Humphries and Robert Rush Miller describes [[Ictalurus Chihuahua]], a new catfish of the [[Ictaluridae]] from the Chihuahuan Desert region. The species had been confused with [[Headwater Catfish]] (*I. lupus*) and [[Channel Catfish]] (*I. punctatus*), with which it overlaps geographically. The authors also resolve a nomenclatural problem: the names *lupus* and *vulpes* were published in the same year, and as first revisers they designate *lupus* as the valid name for the headwater catfish. The paper combines a morphological diagnosis, morphometric and meristic comparisons, a distribution review, an archaeological record, and a conservation assessment highlighting hybridization with introduced channel catfish as the main threat.

## Background & Description

- **Genus context:** *Ictalurus* (Rafinesque, 1820) includes eight described living species, several undescribed Mexican forms, and four extinct species. All living species and one fossil species are part of the Mexican ichthyofauna. Its diversity is attributed largely to allopatric drainage-level speciation.
- **Diagnosis:** A member of the *punctatus* species group (per [[Lundberg Phylogeny of Ictalurid Catfishes]], 1992). It is separable by 22–26 anal rays (usually 23 or 24), 38–42 vertebrae (usually 39–41), a shallowly forked caudal fin, short pectoral and dorsal spines, a robust body with a deep caudal peduncle and broad head, and a distinctive coronoid process, shorter humeral process, expanded vomer, and smooth-anterior pectoral spine with weak posterior serration.
- **Size and meristics:** Reaches at least 280 mm SL (female skeleton, UMMZ 203009). Gill rakers 13–17; pectoral rays I,9 (occasionally 8); dorsal rays I,6; pelvic rays I,7.
- **Coloration (preserved specimens):** Brownish laterals with small to moderate spots (1.5–4.0 mm diameter), concentrated above the midline; venter pale and unspotted; fins pale; barbels dark dorsally and pale ventrally. Life colors were not observed.
- **Holotype:** Adult male, 177 mm SL, UMMZ 214590, Rio San Pedro, tributary to Rio Conchos, near Satevó, Chihuahua (elevation 1350 m), collected 23 May 1978 by R. R. Miller, E. Marsh and M. L. Smith. Paratypes are held across New Mexico, Texas and Mexico institutions.
- **Etymology:** Named for the Chihuahuan Desert region, to which the species is endemic.
- **Methods:** Anal-ray counts from radiographs, skeletons and cleared-and-stained specimens (counts average about one ray higher than published accounts); vertebrae counted as post-Weberian only (add five for total). Measurements were straight-line, log-transformed, and approximated the truss system of Strauss and Bookstein (1982). Multivariate shape analysis failed to separate taxa because diagnostic characters (especially spine lengths) varied widely within species, so regressions and ratios were used instead.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** Upper and middle Rio Conchos basin (Chihuahua and Durango); Rio Grande from El Paso to just above Laredo, Texas (formerly); Pecos River to at least 7.2 km north of Roswell, New Mexico (formerly); and one record from the Rio San Fernando basin, Tamaulipas. Probably once occurred throughout the Rio Grande in New Mexico.
- **Recent surveys:** Two ten-day trips in March and April 1977 found a limited population in the Texas–Chihuahua Rio Grande. A resurvey by Bestgen and Platania (1988, unpublished) captured no specimens.
- **Gila River records:** Nine collections (1949–1989) from the Gila River in western New Mexico were identified as *I. chihuahua*. Catfish are not native to the Pacific Slope north of the Rios Yaqui and Sonora, so these populations likely derive from stocking; hatchery records from the early 20th century (e.g., 700 catfish from Dexter Hatchery in 1935–36) document uncertain origins. Putative pure stock persists in the East Fork of the Gila and Beaver Creek.
- **Habitat:** Similar to its close relative the headwater catfish, with which it is commonly sympatric except in the Rio Conchos basin. Water is often clear or muddy; currents range from strong to none; aquatic vegetation includes algae and *Potamogeton*; depths commonly reach 2 m or more.
- **Archaeology:** Rocky Arroyo, a site about 11 km SW of Roswell, New Mexico (Upper Pecos valley), dated to about 1100 AD and rich in fish remains. Five catfish species were present, four *Ictalurus* plus *Pylodictis*; a lower jaw, pectoral spine and cleithrum were identified as *I. chihuahua*.
- **Threats:** Hybridization with introduced [[Channel Catfish]] (*I. punctatus*), which is the most widely stocked catfish in North America, and with *I. lupus*. Introduced *I. punctatus* has also replaced [[Pricei Catfish]] (*I. pricei*) in the Rio Yaqui through hybridization and competition.
- **Conservation status:** Certainly endangered in the Rio Grande (Texas and New Mexico); special concern in the Rio Conchos basin (Williams et al., 1989). Likely extirpated from the Pecos River.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Ictalurus chihuahua* is distinguished from congeners by its anal ray count, vertebral count, forked caudal fin, short spines, broad mouth, deep caudal peduncle and unique osteology.
- **Morphometric differences:** Dorsal and pectoral spines of *I. chihuahua* are shorter and grow more slowly than in *I. lupus*. Mouth width and caudal peduncle depth allow identification when compared with spine lengths.
- **Meristic comparison (Table 2):** Anal rays were 22–26 (mean 23.6) in *chihuahua*, 23–27 (mean 24.9) in *lupus*, 20–24 (mean 21.7) in *mexicanus* and 26–32 (mean 29.7) in *punctatus*. Vertebrae were 38–43 (mean 40.0), 39–46 (mean 40.8), 38–40 (mean 39.1) and 42–46 (mean 43.9), respectively.
- **Hybrid problem:** Outlying counts in reservoir-influenced populations complicate identification; allozyme markers are recommended to confirm pure stock.
- **Nomenclature:** *Lupus* (Girard, 1858) and *vulpes* (Girard, 1859) were both published, probably on the last day of 1858 according to the authors' reasoning. As first revisers under Article 24 of the ICZN Code, the authors designate *lupus* as the valid name, relegating *vulpes* to the synonymy of *I. punctatus*.
- **Conservation recommendations:** An electrophoretic isozyme survey of Gila River populations, compared with Rio Conchos specimens and *I. punctatus* and *I. lupus*, is needed to confirm purity. The Gila East Fork and Beaver Creek populations are candidate sources for reintroduction if Rio Grande populations are extirpated.
