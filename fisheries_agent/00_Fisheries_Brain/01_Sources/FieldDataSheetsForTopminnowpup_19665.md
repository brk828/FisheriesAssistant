# Topminnow and Pupfish Monitoring 1994–1995 (Arizona)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a transmittal letter and a set of field data sheets from a statewide survey of [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] in Arizona, conducted in June and July 1994 under the Arizona [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) native fish program. [[David Weedman]], Native Fish Biologist, sent the material on 12 August 1994 to Sally Stefferud of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Ecological Services office in Phoenix. The package included the field sheets and a summary table of results for all monitored sites. The letter highlights two topminnow records: a return to [[Kayler Spring]] after an absence since 1992, and a first record at [[Mescal Warm Spring]], where earlier [[Bureau of Land Management]] surveys had found none.

## Background & Description

- **Correspondence:** A one-page letter from AGFD (Phoenix) to USFWS, dated August 12, 1994, copied to Kirk L. Young. It asks the recipient to call with questions.
- **Summary table:** "Results of FY94 Topminnow Monitoring" lists about twenty sites with sample date, site number, and outcome. Outcome categories are *recommended for stocking*, *not recommended for stocking* (including "in sections surveyed"), *failed* (species not found), and *extant* (species present).
- **Field data sheets:** About 40 sheets titled "Topminnow and Pupfish Monitoring 1994–1995." Each records:
  - Site name, number, date, quadrangle, township/range, section, and GPS-style coordinates and elevation
  - Habitat type (mostly springs, tanks, and streams) and subhabitat (pool, riffle, run, backwater, dirt or cement tank)
  - Riparian vegetation (cottonwood, willow, sycamore, walnut, mesquite, seep willow) and canopy density
  - Water quality where measured: dissolved oxygen, pH, conductivity, and water temperature
  - Substrate percentages (silt, sand, gravel, pebble, cobble, boulder, bedrock, detritus)
  - Effort units with length, width, depth, aquatic vegetation, and cover type
  - Gear and effort: [[Seine Sampling]], [[Dipnet Sampling]], [[Visual Survey]], [[Minnow Trap]], and [[Cast Net]] sampling
  - Captured species, counts, total length (mm), weight (g), preservation status, and notes
  - Photo log, land-management impacts, and management recommendations

Several sheets carry the same dates and the same site names. Site names and some numbers are partly illegible in the scan, so exact counts per site are not reliably recoverable.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The monitored waters are small, often isolated, desert aquatic habitats in southern and central Arizona. Many are spring-fed pools, artesian wells, or stock tanks with limited flow. Recurring observations include:

- **Associated fauna:** Aquatic insects, beetle larvae, belostomatid bugs, tadpoles, snails, and in several places a fish identified by the code AGCH (possibly [[Longfin Dace]]). The sheet for [[West Fork of Pinto Creek]] also notes code PACL.
- **Drought:** Some sheets record low or dry conditions, with a note that the drainage was "very dry" and water was confined to pools.
- **Livestock:** Several sheets report heavy [[Cattle Grazing]] at or near sites, including trampled banks, cattle at tanks, and cattle access through a tunnel. Others note cattle grazing on stream bottoms.
- **Water chemistry:** Sheets record pH (one value of 7.9), conductivity, dissolved oxygen, and water temperature where meters were used. Some meter readings are unclear in the scan.
- **Human features:** Stock tanks, cement tanks, pipelines, and fenced springs were noted at several sites, along with recreation and road access.

## Key Findings

1. **Kayler Spring:** Topminnow were found again for the first time since 1992. The field notes report topminnow in the Kayler Spring drainage, with two specimens collected.
2. **Mescal Warm Spring:** Topminnow were found here. Earlier BLM surveys had failed to detect them, so this is a new record for the site.
3. **Stocking decisions:** The summary table recommends stocking only at the **West Fork of Pinto Creek** (surveyed 5 July 1994, with AGCH and PACL present). Other sites are classed as not recommended or failed.
4. **Failed sites:** Topminnow were not found at several sites, including Artesian Well #4 (28 July 1994) and the Santa Cruz River at Lochiel (18 July 1994).
5. **Extant populations:** Sonoita Creek below Fresno Canyon (22 July 1994) was recorded as extant.
6. **Conditions:** Drought, cattle impacts, and altered spring and tank habitats are recurring concerns. Several sheets include management recommendations on grazing, habitat protection, and possible stocking.
7. **Data limits:** The scans contain many handwritten or garbled entries. Exact counts, measurements, and some site names should be verified against the original sheets before use in analysis.
