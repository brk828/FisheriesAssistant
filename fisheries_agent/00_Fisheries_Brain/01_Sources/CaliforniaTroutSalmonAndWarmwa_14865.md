# California Trout, Salmon, and Warmwater Fish Production and Costs, 1964-65

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #aquaculture #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

During the 1964-65 fiscal year, California Department of Fish and Game hatcheries planted a record-breaking **60,129,973 fish** totaling **1,879,769 pounds**, though total weight was 207,290 pounds less than the previous year. The December 1964 flood destroyed [[Cedar Creek Hatchery]] on the [[Eel River]], causing significant losses of steelhead trout yearlings at [[Nimbus Hatchery]] and increased mortality at [[Trinity River Hatchery]] due to silt-laden waters. Catchable-sized trout (7,040,390 fish weighing 1,506,522 pounds, averaging 4.7 fish per pound) cost $0.192 each or $0.904 per pound. Total operating costs for state-financed hatcheries reached $1,966,403, approximately $66,000 more than the previous year due to rising labor and operational expenses. Early-year losses from poor-quality commercial fish food were corrected through enhanced quality control testing.

**Trout and Kokanee:**

- [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*): 564,783 fish, 19,560 pounds
- [[Eastern Brook Trout]] (*Salvelinus fontinalis*): 1,613,923 fish, 23,987 pounds
- [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*): 70,344 fish, 17,586 pounds
- [[Golden Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus aguabonita*): 605,480 fish, 1,017 pounds
- [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki*): 584,025 fish, 484 pounds
- [[Kokanee Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus nerka*): 4,286,102 fish, 1,722 pounds

**Anadromous Fish:**
- [[King Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*): 29,129,011 fish, 42,692 pounds
- [[Silver Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus kisutch*): 1,310,281 fish, 87,585 pounds
- [[Steelhead Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*): 2,121,102 fish, 30,516 pounds

**Warmwater Fish:**
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*): 753,672 fish, 1,583 pounds
- [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*): 35,148 fish, 985 pounds
- [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis auritus*): 85,716 fish, 275 pounds
- Redeve Bass: 30 fish, 8 pounds

### Hatchery Infrastructure

The Department operated multiple facilities across five regions:
- **Region 1:** [[Crystal Lake]], [[Darrah Springs]], [[Iron Gate]], [[Mt. Shasta]], [[Trinity River Hatchery]]
- **Region 2:** [[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]], [[Nimbus Hatchery]], mobile crews
- **Region 3:** [[Cedar Creek Hatchery]] (destroyed), mobile crews
- **Region 4:** [[Kern River]], [[Moccasin Creek]], [[Moorehouse Springs]], [[San Joaquin]]
- **Region 5:** [[Fillmore]], [[Fish Springs]], [[Hot Creek]], [[Mojave River]], [[Mt. Whitney]], [[Black Rock]], [[Bishop Creek]], [[Chino Fisheries Base]]

Top three producers by weight: [[Darrah Springs]] (407,433 pounds), [[San Joaquin]] (174,185 pounds), [[Mojave River]] (164,250 pounds).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### December 1964 Flood Impacts

The catastrophic December 1964 flood had severe consequences:
- Complete destruction of [[Cedar Creek Hatchery]] on the [[Eel River]], including all fish on hand
- Loss of most steelhead trout yearlings at [[Nimbus Hatchery]]
- Increased mortality at [[Trinity River Hatchery]] from silt-laden flood waters
- Steelhead production dropped from 2,772,032 fish (101,635 pounds) in 1963-64 to 2,121,102 fish (30,516 pounds) in 1964-65

### Water Quality and Food Quality Issues

Early fiscal year losses among fingerlings held for catchable-sized trout production resulted from inferior commercial fish food due to "over-enthusiastic competition by fish food vendors." The Department implemented a more rigorous quality control testing program, eliminating further losses from this cause.

### Hatchery Operations

**[[Nimbus Hatchery]]** exceeded its king salmon egg capacity, necessitating transfer of eggs to [[Moccasin]] and [[San Joaquin]] hatcheries, which produced nearly 3,000,000 king salmon fingerlings from these eggs.

**[[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]]** completed its first full operational year, producing 10,047 pounds of salmon and steelhead. This combination steelhead hatchery and salmon spawning channel has capacity for 15,000,000 king salmon eggs and 100,000 yearling steelhead trout.

**[[Trinity River Hatchery]]** reached full operation, with production dependent on adult salmon runs entering the facility. Silver salmon production increased dramatically from zero in 1963-64 to 624,600 fish (31,607 pounds) in 1964-65.

### Federally and Municipally Financed Operations

Three facilities operated by the Department with federal or municipal reimbursement:
- [[Trinity River Hatchery]]: Bureau of Reclamation reimbursement
- [[Nimbus Hatchery]]: Bureau of Reclamation reimbursement  
- [[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]]: East Bay Municipal Utility District reimbursement

These hatcheries were built by water project developers to compensate for blocked salmon and steelhead runs.

## Key Findings

### Production Statistics

**Total Production:** 60,129,973 fish, 1,879,769 pounds (record number, but 207,290 pounds less than 1963-64)

**Trout Production (including Kokanee):** 26,695,013 fish, 1,716,125 pounds
- Exceeded 1965 calendar year production goal of 1,652,390 pounds
- Catchable-sized trout: 7,040,390 fish, 1,506,522 pounds (4.7 fish/pound average)
- Fingerlings: 18,898,538 fish, 72,950 pounds
- Subcatchables: 718,204 fish, 72,436 pounds
- Broodstock: 37,881 fish, 57,217 pounds

**Salmon Production:** 29,129,012 fish, 42,692 pounds
- King salmon increased from 11,871,312 fish (30,186 pounds) in 1963-64 to 29,129,011 fish (42,692 pounds)
- Silver salmon rose from 685,409 fish (65,463 pounds) to 1,310,281 fish (87,585 pounds)

**Steelhead Production:** 2,121,102 fish, 30,516 pounds
- Substantial decrease from 2,772,032 fish (101,635 pounds) in 1963-64
- Reduction attributed to December 1964 flood impacts

**Warmwater Fish Production:** 674,566 fish, 2,851 pounds
- Nearly doubled from 450,898 fish (1,580 pounds) in 1963-64
- Channel catfish: 753,672 fish, 1,583 pounds (up from 357,274 fish, 1,192 pounds)
- Largemouth bass: 35,148 fish, 985 pounds (none produced in 1963-64)

### Cost Analysis

**State-Financed Hatcheries Total Cost:** $1,966,403 (approximately $66,000 increase from 1963-64)

**Trout Costs:**
- Catchable-sized: $0.904/pound, $0.192/fish (up $0.119/pound from 1963-64, but $0.061 less than 1962-63)
- Fingerlings: $3.76/pound, $0.016/fish (smaller size reduced per-fish cost from $0.018)
- Subcatchables: $1.18/pound, $0.119/fish
- Broodstock: $0.90/pound, $1.36/fish
- **Overall trout cost:** $1.04/pound

**Anadromous Fish Costs:**
- Total program: $136,173 (approximately $30,000 more than 1963-64)
- King salmon: $3.87/pound fingerlings, $0.96/pound yearlings
- Silver salmon: $1.77/pound total
- Steelhead: $8.68/pound (increased from $2.30/pound due to flood losses at [[Cedar Creek Hatchery]])
- Total steelhead program cost: $27,974 (slightly less than 1963-64's $29,937)

**Warmwater Fish Costs:**
- $17.97/pound
- Total operating cost: $51,227 (decreased from $52,437 in 1963-64)
- Rescue operations responsible for major portion of warmwater fish costs

**Federally/Municipally Financed Hatcheries (operated by state):**
- [[Trinity River Hatchery]]: $128,506 total, $2.20/pound average
- [[Mokelumne River Fish Installation]]: $30,262 total, $3.01/pound average
- [[Nimbus Hatchery]]: $119,028 total, $4.25/pound average

**Three-Year Average Cost (all trout sizes):** $1.01/pound (1964-65), up from $0.99 (1963-64)

**Cost Components (1964-65):**
- Labor: $786,555 (46%)
- Food: $389,960 (23%)
- Operating expense: $295,640 (18%)
- Equipment: $70,413 (4%)
- Production overhead: $141,690 (8%)
- Administration overhead: $94,545 (5%)
- Information and training: included in operating expense

### Operational Efficiency

Average cost per pound of all fish planted: $1.10 (increased from $0.964 in 1963-64), reflecting higher total costs coupled with fewer pounds produced due to flood losses and food quality issues early in the year.
