# NOM-059-ECOL-1994: Protected Wild Flora and Fauna of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-regulation

## Executive Summary

This document is the official Mexican standard NOM-059-ECOL-1994, published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación on 16 May 1994 and issued by the Secretaría de Desarrollo Social through the [[Instituto Nacional de Ecología]]. It determines which wild terrestrial and aquatic species and subspecies of flora and fauna are in danger of extinction (P), threatened (A), rare (R), or subject to special protection (Pr), with particular attention to endemics of the Mexican Republic and federal waters. It also sets the conditions for possession, use, collection and habitat conservation of these taxa. The standard is a regulatory list rather than a research study. It has no field data of its own, and its main content is the species listings, the definitions and the management rules.

## Background & Description

The standard was drafted after the procedure set by the Ley Federal sobre Metrología y Normalización. A draft, initially coded NOM-PA-CRN-001/93, was published on 2 August 1993 for public comment. It was then reviewed by the Comité Consultivo Nacional de Normalización para la Protección Ambiental, renamed NOM-059-ECOL-1994, and finalized. The text lists more than 35 participating bodies, including federal secretariats (Desarrollo Social, Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos, Energía, Minas e Industria Paraestatal, Pesca), the [[Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México]] and other public universities, research centres, and industry and NGO partners such as [[PEMEX]] and [[Comisión Federal de Electricidad]].

The standard defines key terms:
- **Endemic species**: distribution restricted to the Mexican Republic and federal waters.
- **Habitat**: the specific physical environment and biotic community occupied by an organism or species.
- **Endangered** (P): populations or range drastically reduced, with viability at risk.
- **Threatened** (A): likely to become endangered if threatening factors continue. Equivalent to "vulnerable."
- **Rare** (R): biologically viable but naturally very scarce, often restricted to a small range or specialized habitats.
- **Special protection** (Pr): subject to harvest limits or closed seasons because of small or restricted populations, or to aid recovery of associated species.
- **Population study**: an assessment of size, density, sex and age ratios, birth, death and growth rates, and sustainable harvest.

Section 4 (Símbolos) defines a symbol for endemic species, which is applied in the listings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The listings cover the following taxonomic groups:

- **Flora**: vascular plants, fungi and some algae/cyanobacteria. The list includes many [[Cactaceae]] (cacti, the largest group), [[Orchidaceae]] (orchids), [[Palmae]] (palms), [[Agavaceae]], [[Zamiaceae]] (cycads), [[Pinaceae]] (pines and firs) and [[Podostemaceae]] (river weeds).
- **Fauna**: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes and invertebrates. Marine mammals (for example [[Vaquita]], *Phocoena sinus*, and whales) and sea turtles (for example *Eretmochelys imbricata* and *Lepidochelys kempi*) are included.
- **Aquatic fauna** is listed in several groups. Fishes include about 80 taxa, mostly from the [[Cyprinodontidae]] (killifishes), [[Goodeidae]], [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]]. Several are endemic to Mexican inland waters. Freshwater and marine invertebrates such as corals, crabs, shrimps, snails and clams are also covered.

Section 6 sets the regulatory specifications:
- Specimens of endangered, threatened, rare or protected species may be taken for scientific collection, but they may not be sold.
- Specimens under permanent closure may be taken to make breeding stock, mother plants, seeds or propagules for registered reproduction units. Their offspring need authorization from the Secretaría de Desarrollo Social, with the Secretarías de Pesca and de Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos.
- Commercial use of rare or protected species under temporary or no closure should come mainly from authorized reproduction units where captive breeding is possible.
- Harvest in the wild is allowed only at rates set by the competent authority, based on population studies.
- Habitat use must ensure conservation under current law.
- Authorizations for taking wild specimens or commercializing them are issued by the Secretaría de Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos for terrestrial taxa and the Secretaría de Pesca for aquatic taxa.

Enforcement is assigned to the Procuraduría Federal de Protección al Ambiente and the Secretarías de Pesca and de Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos. Violations are penalized under the [[Ley General del Equilibrio Ecológico y la Protección al Ambiente]], the Ley de Pesca, the Ley Forestal, the Ley Federal de Caza and the Penal Code.

Section 10 notes that the standard partly matches the U.S. [[Endangered Species Act]] (1973) and the Canadian Species at Risk framework (1992).

## Key Findings

- **Scope**: NOM-059-ECOL-1994 is a regulatory list of species, subspecies and endemics in danger of extinction, threatened, rare or subject to special protection, and it sets rules for their protection.
- **Categories**: four categories are used: P (endangered), A (threatened), R (rare) and Pr (special protection).
- **Taxonomic coverage**: the list includes flora (plants and fungi) and fauna (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes and invertebrates). Cacti, orchids, palms and pines form the largest plant groups.
- **Aquatic species**: the standard covers many freshwater and marine taxa, including fishes such as [[Totoaba]] (*Totoaba macdonaldi*), *Gila* and *Cyprinella* species, and the [[Vaquita]], as well as sea turtles and invertebrates.
- **Harvest rules**: wild harvest requires population studies and authorized quotas. Specimens taken from the wild may not be sold, and commercial trade should rely on captive reproduction.
- **Responsible agencies**: the [[Secretaría de Desarrollo Social]] coordinates the standard. The Secretarías de Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos and de Pesca authorize terrestrial and aquatic use, respectively. The Procuraduría Federal de Protección al Ambiente enforces it.
- **Legal basis**: the standard operates under the Ley General del Equilibrio Ecológico y la Protección al Ambiente and related laws. Sanctions follow those laws.
- **Bibliography**: Section 9 lists about 111 references, mostly on herpetology, mammalogy, ichthyology, malacology, cactology and sea turtles.
- **Effective date**: the standard entered into force on the day after publication in the Diario Oficial, 16 May 1994. It was signed in Mexico City on 28 April 1994.
