# Oklahoma Fishes: Annotated List, Distributional Notes and Keys

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is the 1954 monograph *Oklahoma Fishes, with Distributional Notes and Keys* by [[George A. Moore]] of the Oklahoma Agricultural and Mechanical College. It presents an [[Annotated List]] of the state's freshwater fishes, dichotomous [[Dichotomous Keys]] to families and species, a bibliography and a glossary. The present list includes 23 families, 62 genera and 159 species. This is an increase of 27 species over the 132 species in the earlier manuscript list by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] (1941). The author notes the list may not yet be complete, particularly for southeastern Oklahoma. The front matter also contains a page of errata, which has been applied silently to the text.

## Background & Description

- **Historical collections:** The first Oklahoma collection was made in 1852 by a Pacific Railway Survey party under Captain Randolph B. Marcy. It was described by Baird and Girard (1853) from Otter Creek, Comanche County. The 1853 Mollhausen and Shumard collections near Fort Smith gave the type localities of several species, including [[Notropis Illecebrosus]] and [[Hadropterus Shumardi]].
- **Later surveys:** Jordan and Gilbert collected in the Poteau River system in the 1880s (the year is partly illegible in the scan). Seth E. Meek collected in the early 1890s (Meek 1891, 1895, 1896). Ortenburger and Hubbs (1927) and Hubbs and Ortenburger (1929a, 1929b) produced the first extensive evaluation of the state fauna under the [[Oklahoma Biological Survey]].
- **Nomenclature:** The author follows the American Fisheries Society common-name list. *Notropis* specific names are kept in the masculine form pending an International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature ruling on gender. *Pimephales* includes *Hyborhynchus* and *Ceratichthys*. *Hybopsis* is treated as feminine and absorbs *Nocomis*, *Platygobio*, *Erimystax* and *Extrarius*.
- **Keys:** The keys are artificial dichotomous keys. The author warns that aberrant individuals, hybrids, new State records and undescribed forms may not fit, and that preserved colors fade.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Drainages:** Species are grouped by the [[Arkansas River]], the [[Red River]] and their tributaries, including the [[Poteau River]], [[Mountain Fork River]], [[Illinois River]], [[Kiamichi River]], [[Little River]] and [[Blue River]]. Reservoirs and lakes mentioned include [[Lake Texoma]], Grand Lake, Wister Reservoir and Canton Reservoir.
- **Habitat associations:** Many species are tied to habitat type. Examples include the Ozark clear-stream fauna (e.g., [[Ozark Minnow]], [[Golden Redhorse]]), swamp and backwater species of McCurtain County (e.g., [[Pirateperch]], [[Pugnose Minnow]]), and sand- and gravel-bottom darters.
- **Impoundment effects:** The author reports that [[White Bass]] have increased since large impoundments were built on the turbid rivers. He also notes that [[Black Bullhead]] is often extirpated from lakes several years after impoundment.
- **Introductions and exotics:** [[Carp]] and [[Goldfish]] are exotic. [[Rainbow Trout]] do not occur naturally, and hatchery plantings have been made. [[Walleye]] were introduced to Canton Reservoir in 1950 and spread by bait dealers. [[Bluegill]] and [[Redear Sunfish]] were widely planted.
- **Possible introduction:** [[Astyanax Fasciatus]] was reported as a possible introduction from a single specimen taken from an insular pond in Lake Texoma in summer 1952 by Professor Carl D. Riggs.

## Key Findings

1. **Species count:** 159 species in 62 genera and 23 families, compared with 132 species in 73 genera and 22 families in the earlier list. The number of genera fell because of merging.
2. **Incompleteness:** Recent collections from southeastern Oklahoma yielded new State records, so the author states the list is not yet complete.
3. **Lampreys and sturgeons:** Two lamprey species (*Ichthyomyzon castaneus*, *Ichthyomyzon gagei*) and the [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] (*Scaphirhynchus platorynchus*) are recorded. Sight records of the sturgeon below Texoma Dam are considered reliable.
4. **Paddlefish:** [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*) is definitely known from the Arkansas River and its tributaries, and from the Kiamichi River and the Red River below Lake Texoma.
5. **Gars:** [[Alligator Gar]], [[Shortnose Gar]], [[Spotted Gar]] and [[Longnose Gar]] are recorded. The longnose gar is widely distributed.
6. **Trout:** No trout occur naturally in the state. The author reports plantings at several sites, a 1949 Spavinaw Lake specimen, and that a Westville hatchery was discontinued.
7. **Pickerels:** [[Grass Pickerel]] is abundant in southeastern counties. [[Chain Pickerel]] is a doubtful record from the Red River near Arthur City, Texas (Meek, 1896).
8. **Rare or restricted species:** The [[Highfin Carpsucker]] is apparently rare. The [[Blue Sucker]] is recorded from Texoma and Grand lakes. The [[Red River Pupfish]] is restricted to the Red River drainage west of the Wichita Mountains.
9. **Hubbs' Ozark records:** The [[Ozark Blindfish]] (*Troglichthys rosae*) appears only in the supplementary list, as a species known from southeast Missouri and Arkansas.
10. **Nomenclatural decisions:** The gender decision for *Notropis* was deferred, and *Hybopsis* is corrected to feminine following Bailey (1951) and Hubbs (1951).
11. **Errata highlights:** Corrections include *Esox* spelling, *Minytrema*, *Red River*, *Comanche*, *Schilbeodes mollis* name correction, a new entry for *Ammocrypta clara* (Western Sand Darter) from the Red River near the mouth of Blue River, Bryan County, and removal of item 107a in the list.
12. **Supplementary list:** Species known from regions near but not yet definitely recorded from Oklahoma are included, for example [[Mooneye]], [[Plains Carpsucker]], [[Common Studfish]] and [[Bluestripe Darter]]. These are candidates for future discovery.
13. **Acknowledgments:** Collections were contributed by university zoology classes, Oklahoma Game and Fish Department staff (notably G. E. Hall), Dr. A. P. Blair and the Corps of Engineers, Tulsa District.
