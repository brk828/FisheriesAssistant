#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by E. E. Prather at the Auburn University Agricultural Experiment Station, compares production, survival, and growth of albino ("golden") and normal-colored [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) reared together in pond culture. Two one-acre ponds were each stocked with 1,000 albino and 1,000 normal channel catfish fingerlings on December 1–4, 1959, and drained approximately one year later (November 14–15, 1960). Fish were fed a pelleted diet ([[Auburn No. 2 Fish Feed]]) six days per week at increasing rates through the growing season. [[Largemouth Bass]] fry were stocked at low density to control contamination by wild fish. The principal finding was a markedly lower survival rate among albino catfish compared to normal catfish, while growth rate and angling vulnerability were statistically indistinguishable between the two color morphs.

## Background & Description
- **Albino ("golden") channel catfish**: A recessive color morph of *Ictalurus punctatus*, first described among hatchery fry as "golden catfish" by Nelson (1957). Albinism was segregating in the broodstock population even though parent fish displayed normal pigmentation — some breeding pairs produced up to 25% albino fry, indicating a recessive heritable trait.
- **Normal channel catfish**: Standard wild-type pigmentation, used as the experimental control/comparison group.
- **Size classes**: Fingerlings stocked at 3–6 inches total length for both morphs.
- **Average weight at stocking**: ~0.02 lb per fish for both albino and normal fish in both ponds (functionally identical starting size).
- **Average weight at harvest**:
  - Pond 1: Albino 0.96 lb; Normal 0.92 lb
  - Pond 2: Albino 1.02 lb (recovered pounds 833.9 / 825 fish); Normal 1.08 lb (recovered pounds 1,007.9 / 935 fish)
- **Gain in average weight per fish**:
  - Pond 1: Albino 0.94 lb; Normal 0.90 lb
  - Pond 2: Albino 1.00 lb; Normal 1.06 lb
- No consistent or statistically meaningful difference in individual growth rate was detected between the two morphs across ponds (differences of +0.04 lb in one pond, −0.06 lb in the other favor no morph consistently).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Two one-acre freshwater experimental ponds, unfertilized, supplementally fed.
- **Co-stocked species**: [[Largemouth Bass]] fry added at 100 per pond (May 19, 1960) specifically to control/predate upon incidental reproduction or contamination by unwanted wild fish species — not intended as a predator-prey growth variable, though bass predation is discussed as a possible driver of differential albino mortality.
- **Feeding regime**: Pelleted [[Auburn No. 2 Fish Feed]] applied 6 days/week at graduated rates:
  - Feb 1 – Mar 31: 2 lb/acre/day
  - Apr 1 – Apr 30: 5 lb/acre/day
  - May 2 – Jul 9: 10 lb/acre/day
  - Jul 11 – Jul 30: 15 lb/acre/day
  - Aug 1 – Oct 3: 25 lb/acre/day
  - Oct – Nov 10: 30 lb/acre/day
- **Predation vulnerability**: The author hypothesizes that albino channel catfish, lacking cryptic pigmentation, may be more visually conspicuous and thus more susceptible to predation (potentially by the co-stocked largemouth bass), offering a plausible ecological explanation for their reduced survival relative to normal-colored conspecifics.
- **Angling behavior/vulnerability**: Albino and normal catfish were caught on baited hooks with similar frequency, suggesting no difference in feeding behavior, hook susceptibility, or angler-detectable behavioral differences between morphs — relevant to [[Sport Fishery Management]] considerations.

## Key Findings
1. **Survival**: Albino channel catfish exhibited substantially and consistently lower survival than normal channel catfish when co-stocked in the same ponds:
   - Pond 1: Albino 79.8% vs. Normal ~99.9%
   - Pond 2: Albino 82.5% vs. Normal 93.5%
2. **Growth rate**: No consistent difference in growth rate between albino and normal channel catfish; weight gain differences were minor and inconsistent in direction between the two ponds (abstract reports no significant difference over a 36-day comparison window).
3. **Production data (Pond 2 example)**:
   - Albino: 1,000 stocked → 825 recovered (82.5% survival); 25.0 lb stocked → 833.9 lb recovered; 808.9 lb gained.
   - Normal: 1,000 stocked → 935 recovered (93.5% survival); 21.8 lb stocked → 1,007.9 lb recovered; 986.1 lb gained.
4. **Angling success**: Albino and normal catfish were caught by hook-and-line at similar rates, indicating comparable [[Behavioral Ecology|behavioral]] responses to bait and no morph-specific fishing bias.
5. **Practical implication**: While albino channel catfish are equally desirable from a growth and catchability standpoint, their significantly higher mortality (likely predation-related) represents a distinct disadvantage for commercial and recreational [[Aquaculture]] production compared to normally pigmented channel catfish.
6. **Genetic note**: Albinism appears to be a recessive trait in channel catfish broodstock, as phenotypically normal parent fish produced substantial proportions (up to 25%) of albino offspring in some pairings.
