# Pennsylvanian and Early Permian Depositional History, Southeastern Arizona

#marine-biology #foraminifera #fusulinacean

## Executive Summary

This paper by [[Charles A. Ross]] (AAPG Bulletin, 1973) reconstructs the depositional history of Pennsylvanian and Lower Permian (Wolfcampian) strata in southeastern Arizona. The study subdivides the [[Black Prince Limestone]], [[Horquilla Limestone]], [[Earp Formation]], [[Colina Limestone]] and [[Supai Formation]] into 15 regional unconformities and 14 marker-defined units called formats. Ages are assigned mainly from [[Fusulinacean]] (Foraminifera) assemblages. The work is a stratigraphic and paleogeographic synthesis rather than an ecological study. Its main conclusions are that four tectonic-sedimentary settings controlled facies, that sea level and faulting drove repeated transgressions and regressions, and that the region shifted from carbonate-dominated shelves to clastic-dominated shelves by the Early Permian.

## Background & Description

**Scope and nomenclature.** Pennsylvanian and Lower Permian strata cover more than 30,000 sq mi of southeastern Arizona. Earlier work used a small number of thick, broadly defined formations, which made detailed regional correlation difficult. [[Gilluly et al. 1954]] grouped these units into the Naco Group. [[Stoyanow]] and [[Winters]] had earlier worked on the Supai and Naco successions to the north.

**Field and sampling basis.** The study rests on 38 measured or remeasured stratigraphic sections (Fig. 2 index). Eight of these had been reported previously. Sampling included about 800 fusulinacean-bearing limestone samples and about 100 nonfusulinacean limestone samples. Seven published sections from southwestern New Mexico (sections 39 to 45) were added to outline the [[Pedregosa Basin]] more completely. The work was funded by National Science Foundation Grant GB-2934.

**Stratigraphic units.**
- **Black Prince Limestone (Format A):** red clastics with light-gray limestone, mostly Morrowan in age, reflecting the earliest Pennsylvanian marine transgression.
- **Horquilla Limestone (Formats B to L, upper part):** about 1,000 ft in the type section, subdivided into five informal parts. Its base is marked by a red silty succession of reworked lateritic soil.
- **Earp Formation:** a mixed carbonate and clastic unit of Virgilian to Wolfcampian age that intertongues with the Colina and Supai.
- **Supai Formation:** red beds of Pennsylvanian to Lower Permian age, with the Fort Apache Limestone Member as a regional marker.
- **Colina Limestone:** dark-gray to black, fetid, laminated limestone of roughly 600 ft in the type section, with mollusk-dominated faunas.

**Tectonic setting.** Post-Paleozoic erosion, igneous activity, thrusting and block faulting have obscured the pre-Mesozoic structure. Sections along the Mogollon Rim lie near their original depositional positions on the Colorado Plateau. Sections farther south are probably displaced by Cenozoic thrusts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper describes marine depositional environments rather than living communities. Documented settings include:

- **Black Prince carbonates:** shallow-subtidal, tidal and supratidal deposits with algal biscuits (*Osagia*), oolites, echinoderm and brachiopod fragments, algal nodules and brecciated algal-mat surfaces. Chaetetes biostromes occur locally, and rugose corals are noted.
- **Horquilla carbonates:** micritic limestones rich in fusulinaceans, corals, bryozoans, brachiopods and crinoid fragments.
- **Pedregosa Basin margin:** reef and carbonate-bank facies that supported abundant marine life. Water circulation and nutrients are described as sufficient for this, even though the basin was not very deep.
- **Shelf and clastic settings:** fluvial channels, point bars, dunes, interdistributary bays, lagoons, and local gypsiferous evaporite pans. The Mogollon Rim area was often emergent and received deltaic clastics.
- **Mollusks and gastropods:** the Fort Apache Limestone and Colina Limestone contain abundant mollusk and gastropod faunas.

**Tectonic-sedimentary zones.** Four main zones are recognized:
1. **[[Papago Inner Shelf]]** (southwest): shallow marine, thinner sequence.
2. **[[San Pedro Outer Shelf]]** (central): slightly deeper water limestones.
3. **[[Mogollon Inner Shelf]]** (north and northeast): frequently emergent, dominated by clastic deltaic sediments.
4. **[[Pedregosa Basin]]** (southeast): carbonate-rimmed, with thick clastic fill in its center during Late Pennsylvanian and Early Permian time.

Faulted blocks separated the San Pedro shelf from the Pedregosa Basin. Each block had its own history of uplift, subsidence and sedimentation.

## Key Findings

1. **Unconformities as datum planes.** Fifteen regional unconformities were traced across the region and used to subdivide the strata into 14 formats. Only two regional hiatuses are long: one within the Derryan Series and one at the base of the Missourian Series. Correlation of several unconformities required fusulinacean ages.

2. **Ages from fusulinaceans.** Depositional units range from Morrowan to early late Wolfcampian. Key zonal assemblages include:
   - Morrowan: *Millerella marblensis* in the upper Black Prince.
   - Early Derryan: *Profusulinella walnutensis* in section 25.
   - Desmoinesian: *Beedeina* and *Wedekindellina* assemblages in formats D to G.
   - Missourian and Virgilian: *Triticites* assemblages in formats H to L.
   - Wolfcampian: *Schwagerina* and *Pseudoschwagerina* assemblages in formats M and N.

3. **Sediment thickness.** Pennsylvanian and Early Permian sediment thickness between unconformity 1 and the base of the Colina Limestone was:
   - Papago Inner Shelf: 1,300 to 2,000 ft
   - Mogollon Inner Shelf: 1,700 to 2,500 ft
   - San Pedro Outer Shelf: 2,500 to 3,000 ft
   - Pedregosa Basin: 3,000 to 6,000 ft

   The author suggests that uniform thickness within each zone reflects reverse movements on fault blocks and isostatic balance at the start of the Colina transgression.

4. **Carbonate percentages.** Carbonate content increased southward across the Mogollon shelf from about 3% to nearly 40%. It was 40 to 75% on the Papago and San Pedro shelves and 60 to 75% at the Pedregosa margin. The basin interior was about 30% carbonate, which the author attributes to deep-water clastic deposition below the zone of high carbonate production.

5. **Transgressive and regressive phases.** The initial Pennsylvanian transgression deposited the Black Prince across the basin axis. Later transgressions covered the full San Pedro and Papago shelves. Middle Pennsylvanian transgressions became less extensive as clastic input grew. By the end of the Middle Pennsylvanian, marine deposition was mostly limited to the San Pedro shelf and Pedregosa Basin.

6. **Shift to clastic-dominated shelves.** The appearance of Supai continental redbeds and Earp clastics during Virgilian time marks progressive shallowing. By the beginning of the Permian, inner shelves and the outer shelf received mostly clastic sediment, and the Pedregosa margin remained rimmed by carbonate reefs and shoals.

7. **Early Permian basin fill.** Thick accumulations rapidly filled the Pedregosa Basin during the early Permian. Format M reached more than 1,200 ft in section 29, compared with a regional average of 200 to 300 ft. The basin fill later became a restricted, cut-off silty-shelf facies after unconformity 15.

8. **Structural control.** Faulting and fault-block movement were persistent. The Papago shelf was likely emergent during much of the Horquilla interval. Structural adjustments also modified the boundaries between the Pedregosa Basin and the adjacent shelves. The author notes that Cenozoic thrusting may have displaced southern sections, so their original positions are uncertain.

9. **Remaining uncertainties.** Ages for the strata above unconformity 15 remain uncertain. These strata could be latest Wolfcampian or early Leonardian. The author notes that a cephalopod identification (*Perrinites* sp. or *Properrinites*) would resolve this, depending on which genus is correct.
