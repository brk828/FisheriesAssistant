# FY-89 Reporting and Accounting Requirements for Colorado River Recovery Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-management

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] establishes reporting and accounting requirements for all projects funded under the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] in fiscal year 1989. The [[Colorado River Implementation Committee]] mandated that each funded project submit an annual report summarizing work accomplished, preliminary data analysis, and recommendations for the coming year. Reports were due December 15, 1989, and would directly influence FY-91 funding allocations. The document includes a standardized report format and lists 44 funded projects across multiple agencies focusing on endangered fish recovery, flow management, habitat studies, and monitoring in the [[Colorado River Basin]].

## Background & Description

The memorandum, dated October 10, 1989, was issued by John Hamill, Program Director for the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], Region 6. The reporting requirement was approved as part of the FY 1989 Work Plan and reiterated at the August 30, 1989, Implementation Committee meeting. Lead implementing agencies included the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BR), Colorado (CO), [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), Utah (UT), and Wyoming (WY).

The 44 funded projects covered diverse topics including: [[Razorback Sucker]] management and propagation, [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (squawfish) studies, [[Bonytail]] introduction, flow recommendations for multiple river reaches, sediment and trophic dynamics at [[Flaming Gorge]], backwater habitat mapping, fish passage, genetic management, standardized monitoring protocols, database management, creel surveys, information and education programs, and interactions between game fish and endangered species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] operated within a complex multi-state, multi-agency framework addressing recovery of endangered fish species in the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Key geographic areas included the [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[San Juan River]], [[Flaming Gorge]] tailwater, and the 15-mile reach of the [[Colorado River]]. Projects addressed critical ecological factors including flow regimes, water temperature, sediment dynamics, trophic relationships, overwinter survival of young-of-year fish, backwater habitat availability, and interactions between native endangered species and introduced sport fisheries. The program integrated hydrological modeling ([[IFIM]]/[[PHABSIM]]), habitat assessment, propagation and stocking efforts, and long-term monitoring to support species recovery.

## Key Findings

The memorandum established the following requirements and administrative framework:

**Reporting Requirements:**
- Annual reports required for all 44 FY-89 funded projects
- Reports must summarize work accomplished relative to objectives
- Preliminary data analysis required
- Recommendations for the coming year mandatory
- Accounting of funds expended in FY-89 required
- Submission deadline: December 15, 1989
- [[Technical Group]] would evaluate reports and provide recommendations to Implementation Committee at winter meeting

**Report Format (Attachment 2):**
- Recovery Program project number
- Project title and study schedule (initial and final years)
- Study goals and objectives
- FY-89 tasks and deliverables
- Accomplishments and shortcomings with results summary
- Budget summary comparing funds provided versus expended
- Explanation of budget discrepancies
- Recommendations
- Optional appendices for comprehensive reports and supporting documentation

**Funded Project Categories:**
- Program management (BR, CO, FWS, UT projects 2, 3, 4, 1)
- Flow studies and recommendations (projects 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, FWS 6, 8, 9, BR Flow Options)
- [[Flaming Gorge]] studies: backwater mapping, trophic dynamics, sediment dynamics, operational studies (BR 14, 15, 16; FWS 6, 13)
- Endangered species propagation and augmentation (FWS 19, 21, 22; UT 20)
- Management plans for [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[San Juan River]] (BR 38, 39)
- Monitoring and data management (FWS 32, 33)
- Fish interactions and survival studies (CO 31; FWS 28)
- Creel surveys and information/education programs (CO 27; UT 27; CO 26; UT 24)
- Taxonomy and information synthesis (FWS 36; FWS Compendium)

**Administrative Emphasis:**
- Report preparation described as "more than an academic exercise"
- Information would have "major influence" on FY-91 fund allocation
- Management Group representatives responsible for officially notifying principal investigators
- Program Director would compile, copy, and distribute reports by December 22, 1989
