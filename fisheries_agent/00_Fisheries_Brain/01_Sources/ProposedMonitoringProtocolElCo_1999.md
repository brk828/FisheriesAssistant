## Executive Summary
This document outlines a proposed monitoring protocol developed for the **El Coronado Ranch (ECR) Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP)** in West Turkey Creek (WTC), Arizona, part of the [[Rio Yaqui Basin]]. Although not a primary taxonomic or morphological study, the plan provides critical ecological and management data for three imperiled native fish species: the [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], and [[Yaqui Catfish]]. The protocol was designed following the 1999 reintroduction of Longfin Dace and Yaqui Chub into WTC, establishing standardized sampling intensity, methodology, scheduling, and emergency response procedures (e.g., for nonindigenous species invasion or drought) to ensure population persistence and HCP compliance under the [[Endangered Species Act]] framework.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type**: WTC comprises a mix of natural permanent stream pools and **canal-fed artificial ponds** (Pond H, Guesthouse ponds, Big Tank), reflecting a heterogeneous mix of lotic and lentic desert habitats used by the plan species.
- **Distribution**: Monitoring sites span the [[US Forest Service]] and [[El Coronado Ranch]] boundaries, covering the full elevational/longitudinal HCP area from the eastern (USFS/ECR boundary) to western boundary (Big Tank) of the ranch.
- **Reproductive Ecology**: Autumn monitoring is prioritized to assess **adult populations and seasonal reproductive success**, while spring sampling (when feasible) evaluates **over-wintering survival** and pre-spawning adult population size, indicating a spring-summer reproductive period for these species.
- **Threats**:
  - **Nonindigenous fish species** invasion, requiring gill-net detection and removal protocols.
  - **Asian tapeworm (*Bothriocephalus acheilognathi*)**, not yet documented in WTC but present in nearby San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge, posing a parasitic disease risk to native [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] fishes.
  - **Drought** and **environmental contaminants**, which may necessitate emergency relocation of plan species to a "safe haven."
- **Community Context**: The presence/absence of nonindigenous fishes is treated as a key ecosystem health indicator, with active removal and habitat renovation planned as a management response.

## Key Findings
- **Reintroduction Event**: [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Yaqui Chub]] were reintroduced to West Turkey Creek in **1999**, triggering the onset of this formal monitoring program.
- **Monitoring Design**: 3–5 permanent 100-m monitoring sites will be established on WTC proper (spanning USFS and ECR lands), plus three designated ponds (Pond H, Guesthouse ponds, Big Tank), supplemented by opportunistic surveys elsewhere.
- **Sampling Methods**:
  - **Backpack electrofishing** (single-pass) for stream sites, with voltage, pulse width, frequency, and duration recorded.
  - **Minnow traps and seines** for pond sampling (adults and juveniles enumerated separately).
  - **Hoop nets with PIT tag identification** specifically for monitoring [[Yaqui Catfish]].
- **Monitoring Schedule**: Quarterly monitoring for the first two years post-reintroduction, transitioning to biannual/annual monitoring thereafter; a comprehensive linear habitat/fish distribution survey of the entire HCP area is required **every five years**; a 10-year partner review is mandated to evaluate whether monitoring goals are being met.
- **Health Surveillance**: Annual parasite screening (minimum 10 individuals/species, scaling to ~50 as populations grow) is recommended due to regional Asian tapeworm risk.
- **Data Governance**: Annual reporting to all HCP partners, with **Arizona Ecological Services (USFWS)** designated as the central data repository.
- **Regulatory Context**: The monitoring plan operates under the 1998 [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] and Implementing Agreement for El Coronado Ranch, with a Forest Service Section 7 consultation requiring renewal before its **2004** expiration.
- **Institutional Partners**: Permittee (private landowners Joe & Valer Austin), [[US Forest Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] jointly review and approve the monitoring plan.
