# Spatial Habitat Selection of Roundtail Chub (*Gila robusta*) in Two Central Arizona Streams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1995 study in *The Southwestern Naturalist* (vol. 40, no. 3) by Barrett and Maughan (National Biological Survey and University of Arizona) developed [[Habitat Suitability Index]] (HSI) curves for depth, velocity, substrate, and cover used by [[Roundtail Chub]] in [[Fossil Creek]] and [[Wet Beaver Creek]], two tributaries of the [[Verde River]]. Roundtail chub is a declining species in Arizona and was considered threatened, and habitat-use data were lacking. The study also produced selection indices for juvenile and adult [[Roundtail Chub]] and for a small introduced population of [[Razorback Sucker]] in Fossil Creek. Adults used deep, slow water in both streams, while juveniles in Fossil Creek selected shallower, lower-velocity water. The authors concluded that HSI curves did not transfer cleanly between the two streams because of large differences in habitat, fish community, and temperature.

## Background & Description

- **Species status:** Roundtail chub were historically abundant in most warmwater streams of the [[Colorado River Basin]] (Minckley, 1973). By the study period they occurred in only a few Arizona drainages and were listed as threatened by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (1988).
- **Information gap:** Little was known about roundtail chub habitat use, and such data were needed for management.
- **Study species:** Adult roundtail chub (TL > 150 mm) in Wet Beaver Creek; adult and juvenile roundtail chub in Fossil Creek; and razorback sucker (220–290 mm TL) in Fossil Creek. About 16 razorback suckers were estimated to be present at the time of the study.
- **Methods framework:** Curves followed [[Habitat Suitability Index]] approaches used in the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM) and [[Habitat Evaluation Procedures]] (HEP), as recommended by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

**Key definitions used in the study:**
- *Utilization:* proportion of sampled fish occupying a given habitat condition.
- *Availability:* relative proportion of surface area with that condition.
- *Selection:* utilization/availability ratio, scaled to the largest ratio calculated (index range 0.0–1.0).

**Methods summary:**
- Single 150-m study reach per creek, each a riffle, run, and pool complex.
- Habitat factors: depth, velocity, substrate, and cover. Shadows were not counted as instream cover because they changed with time of day.
- Observations were made by [[Snorkel Survey]] from June through August 1989 and 1990.
- Curves were smoothed by the Stages method, then a three-point running mean was applied.
- Selection curves were not normally distributed and were compared only visually.
- Sample sizes gave 95% confidence for the [[Roundtail Chub]] and [[Smallmouth Bass]] curves and the razorback sucker velocity curve, and 80% confidence for the razorback sucker depth curve.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fossil Creek** originates from a large spring less than 1 km upstream and has a constant temperature of 19°C and constant discharge of 1.2 cubic meters per second (cms). Bedrock substrate was limited. The stream also contained [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) and [[Gila Mountain Chub]]. The absence of an annual temperature cycle allowed roundtail chub to occupy the same habitats year-round.

**Wet Beaver Creek** had a constant discharge of 0.2 cms and temperatures ranging from 19°C in summer to about 7°C in winter. Bedrock substrate was extensive. The stream contained a reproducing population of [[Smallmouth Bass]] and an occasional stocked [[Rainbow Trout]]. Roundtail chub could not be located there during winter.

**Cross-stream comparison:** The creeks differed in available habitat, temperature, structure, and fish community. The authors could not attribute differences in habitat selection to any single factor. Smallmouth bass presence was one possibility for lower habitat suitability in Wet Beaver Creek.

## Key Findings

### Roundtail Chub in Wet Beaver Creek (adults, n = 85 snorkel observations; 739 availability points)
- Chub consistently used the deepest (>1.8 m) and lowest-velocity (<0.086 mps) areas.
- The most-used area had a bedrock bottom beside a large cliff. A deep crevice at the cliff base served as escape cover.
- Fish aggregated near large boulders and moved into their shadows when disturbed.
- **Selection:** Adults avoided velocities >0.14 mps. Selection peaked at a depth of 2.1 m and dropped sharply at greater and lesser depths. Adults selected bedrock, large boulders, and instream cover.

### Roundtail Chub in Fossil Creek (adults, n = 176 snorkel observations; 543 availability points)
- Adults generally used deep (>1.8 m) and slow (<0.10 mps) water but occasionally entered shallow (<0.9 m) and swift (>0.46 mps) areas.
- **Selection:** Velocity selection peaked at 0.23 mps (range 0–0.96 mps). Depth selection was bimodal, with lower selection at 0.9 and 1.2 m and higher selection at 2.1–3.7 m.
- Adults selected small-boulder and gravel substrates and large instream objects.
- Adults selected a wider range of velocity and depth than juveniles.

### Juvenile Roundtail Chub in Fossil Creek (n = 350 snorkel observations; 543 availability points)
- **Velocity:** Selection peaked near 0.15 mps and velocities >0.61 mps were avoided.
- **Depth:** Selection was high at 0.9–1.5 m and low at depths >2.1 m.
- **Substrate:** Juveniles used a variety of substrates, particularly sand, with little use of bedrock and large boulders.
- **Cover:** All cover types were selected.
- Juveniles used riffles and shallower, lower-velocity water than adults. The text on juvenile versus adult differences is cut off in the source.

### Razorback Sucker in Fossil Creek (n = 31 snorkel observations; 543 availability points)
- Velocity selection was for flows <0.3 mps, with the highest selection at 0.15 mps.
- Depth selection was 1.5–2.7 m, with a preference for 2.0 m.
- Most fish occurred over silt. Small and large boulders and bedrock were also used. Sand and gravel were not used.
- Fish positioned themselves near large instream objects and overhead cover, and also used other cover types.

### Synthesis and Management Implications
- The Fossil Creek roundtail chub population was large and included multiple life stages, which suggests habitat requirements were being met.
- The Wet Beaver Creek population was small and contained only adults. The absence of juveniles and the presence of smallmouth bass suggest that adult-selected habitats there may not be "typical" and may fail to meet species needs.
- Because the two creeks differed substantially, HSI curves developed in one stream could not be directly applied to the other.
- Information on habitat use is needed for future roundtail chub management in Arizona.
