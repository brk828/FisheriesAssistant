# A Note on Eels (Anguilla spp.) from the Zambesi River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Anguillidae #freshwater-ecology #African-ichthyology

## Executive Summary

This note documents eel specimens collected by P. I. R. Maclaren from the [[Zambesi River]] system in [[Northern Rhodesia]] between 1954 and 1956. Ten specimens were examined and identified using morphometric characters and coloration patterns following [[Ege]]'s (1939) taxonomic revision. The primary species identified was [[Anguilla nebulosa labiata]] (mottled long-finned eel), with questionable records of [[Anguilla marmorata]] and [[Anguilla mossambica]]. These collections significantly extend knowledge of eel distribution in African freshwaters and provide evidence for catadromous behavior in A. nebulosa labiata.

## Background & Description

The identification of [[Anguilla]] species follows Ege's (1939) classic revision using multiple characters including coloration, dentition, and body proportions. The key diagnostic character employed was the "distance between verticals through anus and origin of dorsal fin in percentage of total length" (designated as "D" value), combined with color patterns.

Four species of eels are recorded from African fresh waters:
- [[Anguilla nebulosa labiata]] Peters: mottled long-finned eel
- [[Anguilla marmorata]] Quoy and Gaimard: mottled eel
- [[Anguilla mossambica]] Peters: plain long-finned eel
- [[Anguilla bicolor bicolor]] McClelland: plain short-finned eel

Specimens examined ranged from 95.5 to 158.0 cm in length and 6.20 to 10.12 kg in weight. All specimens were preserved in formalin. The D values for confirmed A. nebulosa labiata ranged from 12.1 to 13.8.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Lunsemfwa River]] specimens were collected above Power Station Falls, captured by a grid at the intake of a hydro-electric station, suggesting downstream migration. Young eels reaching this location would have had to surmount cascades totaling 250 feet in height.

The discovery of two elvers of A. nebulosa labiata in [[Mzinga Creek]], [[Dar-es-Salaam]], [[Tanganyika]] (200 yards above high water mark) in January-February 1956 provides strong evidence for catadromous behavior in this species. This contradicts earlier speculation that the species might not be catadromous based on the apparent impossibility of ascending the falls and rapids.

Eels are widely distributed throughout the [[Zambesi River]] system, though no definite records exist above [[Victoria Falls]]. The distribution map prepared by Maclaren shows known eel occurrences throughout the river system, with probable barriers marked.

## Key Findings

**Species Distribution in Zambesi System:**

From [[Lunsemfwa River]]:
- Seven whole specimens plus one head of [[Anguilla nebulosa labiata]]
- One specimen tentatively identified as [[Anguilla marmorata]] (D=18.4), though this identification is questioned as Ege (1939) recorded only a single mainland African specimen of this species, and recent correspondence suggests that specimen was misidentified
- One specimen (158.0 cm, unmottled) not identifiable due to insufficient data

From Lower [[Zambesi River]] ([[Chipepo]], 28°50'E, 16°45'S):
- One specimen of [[Anguilla nebulosa labiata]] (95.5 cm, mottled, female, D=12.2)
- One possible specimen of [[Anguilla mossambica]] (97.0 cm, unmottled, D=14.1), though confusion in correspondence suggests this may be the same specimen as the confirmed A. nebulosa labiata

**Morphometric Data for A. nebulosa labiata:**
- Length range: 95.5-153.6 cm
- Weight range: 6.20-10.12 kg
- D values: 12.1-13.8
- All sexed specimens were female
- All exhibited mottled coloration

**Biogeographic Significance:**
- These represent the only records of [[Anguilla nebulosa labiata]] from the Zambesi River system in [[Northern Rhodesia]]
- Previous records of A. nebulosa labiata from the Zambesi watershed were limited to [[Tette]] in [[Portuguese East Africa]] and the [[Licuare River]], [[Boror]]
- No records of [[Anguilla mossambica]] exist from the Zambesi system
- [[Anguilla bicolor bicolor]] has been recorded from only one locality in the region (Licuare River, Boror, Portuguese East Africa)

**Evidence for Catadromy:**
- First elvers of A. nebulosa labiata discovered in [[Mzinga Creek]], 200 yards above high water mark
- Suggests adults descend to sea to breed
- Implies elvers entering the Zambesi River can negotiate substantial obstacles (250-foot cascades) during upstream migration

These collections substantially extend knowledge of [[Anguilla]] distribution in African freshwaters and contribute to understanding eel biology in Africa, about which little was previously known.
