# White Mountain Research Station 40th Anniversary Symposium Solicitation (1990)

#aquatic-ecology #limnology #water-history

## Executive Summary

This is a 1990 letter from [[Clarence A. Hall Jr.]], Director of the [[White Mountain Research Station]] (a [[University Of California]] facility), to past station users. It announces the station's fortieth-anniversary symposium, "The History of Water: Eastern Sierra, Owens Valley-White-Inyo Mountains," to be held September 19-22, 1991. The letter solicits nominations for four White Mountain Medals for Distinguished Contributions to the Science of the White-Inyo Region, invites a $1,000 award for the best original student paper, and asks recipients to return an attendance or presentation form by 1 December 1990. An attached page lists proposed symposium topics and possible academic disciplines. The document does not report any data on fish, invertebrates, or other organisms.

## Background & Description

- **Sender:** Clarence A. Hall Jr., Director, White Mountain Research Station, 405 Hilgard Avenue, Los Angeles (letterhead lists the UC campus system: Berkeley, Davis, Irvine, Los Angeles, Riverside, San Diego, San Francisco, Santa Barbara, Santa Cruz).
- **Recipients:** White Mountain Research Station users from 1950 to 1990.
- **Occasion:** Forty years of scientific research at the station.
- **Symposium:** "The History of Water: Eastern Sierra, Owens Valley-White-Inyo Mountains," [[September 19-22, 1991]].
- **Banquet:** Medals to be presented by [[David J. Gardner]], UC President, at the banquet on Saturday, September 21, 1991. (The cc list prints the name as "David P. Gardner"; the body text uses "David J.")
- **Medal selection:** Four medals, two to physical scientists and two to biological/health scientists, chosen by a special selection committee.
- **Nomination deadline:** 1 December 1990. Multiple nominees are allowed, each with a separate letter.
- **Student award:** $1,000 for the best original student paper presented at the symposium.
- **Response:** Attendance or paper-presentation interest via the enclosed self-mailer by 1 December 1990.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The symposium's framing centers on water in the Eastern Sierra and White-Inyo region, a semi-arid landscape where the [[Owens Valley]] and surrounding ranges have been shaped by water availability and management. The second page lists proposed topics:

- **Deep-time and past environments:** Ancient Seas and Waterways; Paleogeography/Paleoecology; Paleoenvironments; Paleoclimate and paleoclimatic indicators (geologic, biologic, archaeologic) and projections.
- **Human and present-day water issues:** Water usage; Prehistoric/Archaeologic records; Present-day, future, and impact studies.

Possible disciplines listed include anthropology/archaeology, atmospheric science, biology, ecology, economics, environmental science, evolutionary biology and paleobiology, geology, geomorphology, sociology, and toxicology/groundwater geology/engineering.

Institutional partners named in the list (partly garbled by OCR) include the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (OCR text reads "Department of Fish..."), the Inyo County agencies, the [[Los Angeles Department of Water and Power]], the [[UC Natural Reserve System]], and the [[UC Water Resources Institute]].

## Key Findings

1. **Symposium scheduled:** The fortieth-anniversary "History of Water" symposium is set for September 19-22, 1991, in the White-Inyo region.
2. **Medals:** Four [[White Mountain Medal]]s will honor distinguished contributions to White-Inyo science: two to physical scientists and two to biological/health scientists.
3. **Nomination process:** Nominations are open until 1 December 1990, submitted as individual letters per nominee, and judged by a special selection committee.
4. **Student recognition:** A $1,000 prize goes to the best original student paper.
5. **Scope of research:** Topics span paleoclimate, paleoecology, prehistoric archaeology, water use, and present-day and future water impacts, reflecting a multidisciplinary approach.
6. **Action required:** Recipients must return the enclosed self-mailer by 1 December 1990 to register attendance or a paper proposal.

No quantitative biological data, species records, or sample sizes are reported in this document.
