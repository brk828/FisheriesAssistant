# Genetics, Morphology and Systematics of Klamath Basin Suckers (Morphology Section)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #freshwater-ecology #systematics

## Executive Summary
This document is a research proposal submitted by Douglas F. Markle (Oregon State University) to the Klamath Tribe, outlining a morphological framework for resolving unresolved taxonomic and systematic questions surrounding the four recognized catostomid ([[Catostomidae]]) species of the Klamath Basin: [[Klamath Smallscale Sucker]] (*Catostomus rimiculus*), [[Klamath Largescale Sucker]] (*Catostomus snyderi*), [[Shortnose Sucker]] (*Chasmistes brevirostris*), and [[Lost River Sucker]] (*Deltistes luxatus*). Two of these species are federally listed as endangered. The paper does not present new empirical results but instead lays out a comprehensive plan to (1) identify diagnostic morphological characters at the species and population level, and (2) construct a defensible phylogenetic framework — building on and critiquing [[Smith 1992 Catostomidae Phylogeny]] — to properly interpret those characters, including cases of suspected hybridization (e.g., [[Clear Lake Suckers]]) and character shifts driven by ecology rather than ancestry (homoplasy).

## Background & Description
The proposal specifies a detailed, standardized suite of external morphometric and meristic characters to be collected under the "KLAMGEN" sampling protocol, following [[Hubbs and Lagler 1964]] methodology.

**External Morphometrics** (22 measurements; Table 1), including:
- Standard length (SL), fork length (FL)
- Head-region measures: length to anterior/posterior edge of eye (LAE, LPE), head length (HL), interorbital width (WINTERORB)
- Body shape: width and depth at pectoral-fin base (WP1, DP1), depth at dorsal-fin base (DDO), depth of caudal peduncle (DCAUDPED)
- Fin-position measures: origin/insertion of pelvic, dorsal, and anal fins (LOP2, LOD, LID, LOA, LIA)
- Inter-fin distances: dorsal-to-anal origin (LD_A), dorsal origin/insertion to caudal base (LDO_C, LDI_C), posterior eye edge to dorsal origin (LPE_LOD), pelvic-to-anal origin (LP_OA)
- Fin lengths: pectoral (LP1), pelvic (LP2)

**Meristic Characters** (Table 2), including:
- Caudal-fin ray counts (procurrent and primary, upper/lower)
- Dorsal- and anal-fin ray counts (procurrent and primary)
- Pectoral- and pelvic-fin ray counts (P1, P2)
- Gill raker counts on first arch (GR)
- Vertebral counts: precaudal, caudal, and total vertebrae (with/without ribs), vertebrae anterior to dorsal- and anal-fin origins (VDO, VAO)
- Presence/absence of doubled neural/haemal spines on preural centra 2 and 3 (diagnostic skeletal anomalies)
- Scale counts: lateral line (LL), rows to dorsal/anal origin (LLD, LLA), circumpeduncular scale count (SCP)

These characters were selected because vertebral and other less-visible meristic traits (rather than easily observed external features) are noted as more reliable species- and population-level diagnostics ([[Markle and Simon]], unpublished), and because meristic series link directly to larval developmental stages ([[Powles and Markle 1984]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The Klamath Basin suckers occupy a mix of **lentic** (lake/reservoir) and **lotic** (river/stream) freshwater habitats within the Upper Klamath Basin, including Upper Klamath Lake, Clear Lake, Copco Reservoir, Gerber Reservoir, Tule Lake, the Williamson River, Butte Creek, and the Rogue River. A central ecological theme is that morphological characters can be **environmentally plastic** rather than strictly phylogenetically informative:
- [[Dunham et al. 1979]] demonstrated biotic- and abiotic-mediated character shifts between sucker populations, meaning a "diagnostic" trait in one habitat or sympatric context may not hold in another (e.g., a species may show a diagnostic character only in sympatry with a close relative, not in allopatry).
- The [[Warner Sucker]] (*Catostomus warnerensis*) is highlighted as a useful comparative model because it exhibits both lentic and lotic life-history strategies, relevant to understanding variation in the Klamath Largescale Sucker.
- Sympatric speciation is proposed as a distinctive process in the Klamath Basin — Smith (1992) notes that most catostomid speciation results from allopatric drainage isolation, with rare exceptions (sympatric sister taxa) occurring in "large water" habitats like the Mississippi system and the Upper Klamath Basin (Lost River Sucker and Shortnose Sucker).
- Ambiguous populations, such as those in [[Clear Lake]], illustrate the ecological/taxonomic problem directly: they could represent Shortnose Suckers retaining ancestral traits, Klamath Largescale Suckers shifted by lake habitat and lack of competitors, or hybrid (introgressed) populations.

## Key Findings
- **No new morphological data are presented**; this is a proposal establishing methodology, target taxa, and outgroup selection criteria for a future comprehensive study.
- Prior claimed morphological discrepancies between Lost River Sucker and Shortnose Sucker were shown by Markle and Simon (unpublished) to be partly artifacts of **ontogenetic change** rather than true taxonomic distinction.
- Evidence for historical hybridization ([[Miller and Smith 1981]]) was based on small, temporally disjunct samples and is questioned as insufficiently robust.
- [[Smith 1992 Catostomidae Phylogeny]] is the only published phylogeny including all four Klamath species, but it has substantial data gaps (39 of 157 characters unknown for *Chasmistes brevirostris* and *Deltistes luxatus*; 46 of 157 for *Catostomus snyderi*) and weak congruence statistics (consistency index = 0.35; rescaled consistency index = 0.28), indicating many characters conflict with the proposed tree — likely due to ecological homoplasy rather than shared ancestry.
- A rigorous **outgroup taxon list** (12 taxa) was defined using three criteria — sister-taxon relationships, geographic adjacency, and taxa ignored by Smith (1992) — spanning taxa such as [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), [[Largescale Sucker]] (*Catostomus macrocheilus*), [[Owens Sucker]], [[Tahoe Sucker]], [[Bridgelip Sucker]], [[Modoc Sucker]], [[Sacramento Sucker]], [[Warner Sucker]], the undescribed **Wall Canyon Creek sucker**, and [[Tsiltcoos Sucker]].
- Target sampling design: 30 specimens per population across multiple populations per species (e.g., Shortnose Sucker N=150 across 5 populations; Lost River Sucker N=90 across 3 populations; Klamath Largescale Sucker N=60; Klamath Smallscale Sucker N=90), with outgroup taxa targeted at 15–30 specimens each.
- Total planned external morphometric sample size for target taxa: **390 specimens**, collected under the standardized KLAMGEN protocol to enable both morphometric and meristic (including osteological/vertebral) analyses.
- The study explicitly frames **osteological data** as central to linking Klamath Basin findings to the broader catostomine phylogeny established by Smith (1992).
