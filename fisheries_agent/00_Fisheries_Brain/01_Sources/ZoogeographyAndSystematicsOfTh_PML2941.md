# Zoogeography and Systematics of the Lanternfishes of the Genus Nannobrachium (Myctophidae: Lampanyctini)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #myctophidae

## Executive Summary

This monograph (Smithsonian Contributions to Zoology, No. 607, 2000) by [[Bernard J. Zahuranec]] revises the short-pectoral-finned lanternfishes formerly placed in [[Lampanyctus]] and recognizes them as the genus [[Nannobrachium]]. Based on more than 9000 specimens, the author recognizes 17 species in five phenetic species groups, of which four species and one species group are described as new. The distributions of the species are discrete and largely correlate with known open-ocean mesopelagic zoogeographic regions. The genus is supported by a combination of morphological, otolith and larval characters, though the monophyly of the species groups is not yet confirmed.

## Background & Description

- **Family context:** Lanternfishes (Myctophidae) are among the most abundant open-ocean midwater fishes. Within the family, [[Diaphus]] has the most species, while [[Lampanyctus]] (sensu lato) is among the most widespread and second in species number (Backus et al., 1977).
- **Taxonomic history:** [[Lampanyctus]] was erected by Bonaparte (1840) with *Gasteropelecus crocodilus* Risso (1810) as type species. It became a "catch-all" taxon, and several groups were removed by Bolin (1959), Hubbs and Wisner (1964) and Paxton (1972), who treated the short-finned group as genus [[Nannobrachium]] (Günther, 1887; type species *N. nigrum*).
- **Diagnostic features of the genus:** Short (never beyond pelvic-fin base), rudimentary or absent pectoral fins; vertically elongate, squarish otolith; weak musculature producing a soft, flaccid body; "pinched" body profile; swim bladder atrophied in adults.
- **Methods:** Meristics followed [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1958); photophore nomenclature followed Wisner (1976) and Nafpaktitis et al. (1977). Twenty point-to-point measurements were analyzed for allometry. Species groups were tested by stepwise discriminant analysis, and [[Discrimination Value]] (D.V.) keys were built for paired species identification.
- **Acknowledgments and reviews:** Dedicated to the late [[Robert H. Gibbs Jr.]], the author's advisor, and reviewed by several specialists including John Paxton and Robert K. Johnson.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Vertical distribution:** Species are generally mesopelagic. For example, *N. atrum* occurs at about 700–800 m by day and as shallow as 100 m at night in the North Atlantic (Nafpaktitis et al., 1977). *N. cuprarium* occurs at 600–1200 m by day near Bermuda and at 700–900 m near the Canary Islands. *N. ritteri* is captured as shallow as about 20 m at night, and *N. fernae* only in tows at 200 m or deeper.
- **Zoogeographic framework:** Distributions were mapped onto regions following Backus et al. (1977) for the Atlantic, Sverdrup et al. (1942) and Wyrtki (1971) for the Indian Ocean, and McGowan (1976) and Wisner (1976) for the Pacific. Faunal breaks often correlate with major physical features such as the Gulf Stream edge, the Subtropical Convergence and the Equatorial Frontal Water.
- **Water mass associations:** *N. achirus* is a subantarctic circumglobal species. *N. wisneri* is a temperate-semisubtropical species, nearly circumglobal in Subtropical Convergence waters. *N. isaacsi* is associated with the Mauritanian Upwelling Region. *N. phyllisae* is restricted to the Peru-Chile Current area. *N. idostigma* has affinities with the Peruvian Upwelling Region.
- **Larvae:** Larvae are robust with large heads. Pectoral fins are large in many species, and in *N. hawaiiensis* they are very large and fan-shaped.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic results**
- 17 species of short-pectoral-finned Nannobrachium are recognized across five species groups: [[Nigrum Group]], [[Regale Group]], [[Cuprarium Group]], [[Achirus Group]] and [[Isaacsi Group]].
- Four species are new: *N. gibbsi*, *N. indicum*, *N. bristori*, *N. hawaiiensis*, *N. wisneri*, *N. crypticum*, and *N. phyllisae*. The number of new species is seven in total, with new combinations for *N. nigrum* (type species), *N. atrum*, *N. cuprarium*, *N. lineatum*, *N. achirus*, *N. regale*, *N. ritteri*, *N. idostigma*, *N. fernae* and *N. isaacsi*. The text describes this group as "17 species" and "four species are newly described" for the Achirus group alone.
- Species groups are phenetic groups, not formal taxa. Each group shares at least one unique character, and the species-group distinctions are supported by discriminant analysis, though group monophyly awaits further study.

**Diagnostic characters**
- Nigrum group: extremely reduced, flexible pectoral rays; VLO nearly touching the lateral line.
- Regale group: VLO low on the body (more than three photophore diameters below the lateral line); relatively robust pectoral fins.
- Cuprarium group: black pigment "hood" on posterior tips of supracaudal and infracaudal glands.
- Achirus group: pectoral fins that stop growing, thicken and become overgrown with skin; loss complete in adults.
- Isaacsi group: VO2 photophore raised above VO1 (unique in the genus).

**Distribution patterns**
- Distributions show little overlap between species and between species groups. The exceptions are *N. ritteri* in the Regale group and *N. lineatum* in the Cuprarium group.
- *N. atrum* is the most widespread species, in the North and South Atlantic, South Indian, and western South Pacific.
- *N. nigrum* is tropical Pacific through the East Indian archipelago. *N. indicum* is endemic to the Indian Ocean.
- *N. cuprarium* is restricted to the Atlantic and is subtropical. *N. lineatum* is the largest species, exceeding 200 mm in the Atlantic.
- *N. regale* is found across the North Pacific. *N. fernae* has the most restricted distribution of any species of the genus.

**Material and conclusions**
- Over 9000 specimens were examined. Several species are based on small series, for example *N. isaacsi* (99 specimens) and *N. fernae* (62 specimens).
- The author notes that the species have overlapping morphometrics and that morphometrics alone cannot separate species or groups, so photophore arrangement and meristics remain essential.
- Specimens of undescribed species may be expected, particularly from the southern hemisphere and the Indo-Pacific.
