# Station Profile: White Sulphur Springs National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
This profile documents the operational status of the **White Sulphur Springs National Fish Hatchery**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in White Sulphur Springs, West Virginia (Region 5). Established in 1902 under the authority of the U.S. Fish Commission (31 Stat. 681), the station is undergoing a significant strategic transition. As of 1993, the hatchery is shifting away from its historical role as a **National Broodstock Program** facility toward new responsibilities focused on culturing threatened and endangered indigenous fish species and propagating [[Spiny Mussels]] (Unionidae). This shift was accelerated by a reinfection event of **Bacterial Kidney Disease (BKD)**, which forced a reevaluation of the station's core mission and production capabilities.

## Background & Description
*Note: The source document is an administrative station profile rather than a biological/morphological study. No direct morphometric data (length, weight, meristic counts) for [[Rainbow Trout]] or [[Brook Trout]] is provided. The facility's infrastructure, however, is detailed as follows:*

- **Broodstock Building** and **Covered Broodstock Raceways**: Used for holding mature breeding stock.
- **California Raceways**: Standard flow-through raceway design for rearing.
- **Hatchery Building Raceways (Concrete)**: Primary rearing infrastructure.
- **Egg Incubation Room**: Facility for early developmental stages.
- **Earthen Fish Pond**: Used likely for extensive rearing or display purposes.
- **Spring House (Upper and Lower)**: Water source management, critical given the "White Sulphur Springs" location, suggesting a natural spring-fed water supply.
- **Disinfection Building**: Critical for pathogen control (relevant to the noted BKD outbreak).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The hatchery interfaces with the surrounding **Appalachian freshwater ecosystem** in West Virginia, Pennsylvania, and Virginia through fish stocking distribution.

- **Target Habitat**: Coldwater freshwater streams and rivers suitable for [[Salmonidae]] species.
- **Primary Species Stocked**: [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brook Trout]], distributed into Federal, State, and Private waters across a multi-state region.
- **Emerging Ecological Role**: A pivot toward conservation aquaculture, specifically the propagation of **spiny mussels** (a group of freshwater bivalves highly sensitive to water quality, often serving as indicator species) and unspecified **threatened and endangered indigenous fish species**. This indicates a niche shift from a "put-and-take" recreational fishery supplier to a **conservation hatchery** supporting native biodiversity recovery efforts.
- **Pathogen Pressure**: The presence of **BKD (Bacterial Kidney Disease)**, caused by the bacterium *Renibacterium salmoninarum*, represents a significant ecological/health constraint on the resident salmonid population, impacting the facility's viability for continued broodstock production.

## Key Findings

- **Establishment**: The hatchery was established in 1902 under the U.S. Fish Commission, one of the older facilities in the National Fish Hatchery System.
- **Mission Transition (1993)**: A pivotal shift occurred in 1993, moving the station away from the National Broodstock Program toward culturing threatened/endangered indigenous fish and [[Spiny Mussels]].
- **BKD Outbreak**: A reinfection of Bacterial Kidney Disease necessitated an examination of the current program, directly influencing the decision to change the station's mission.
- **Distribution Data (Species)**: Fish distributed to state, federal, and private waters were exclusively **Rainbow Trout** and **Brook Trout** during FY 1993.
- **Distribution Trends (Numbers)**:
  - FY 1991: 220,820 total fish distributed (73,380 Federal/USFWS; 68,174 Other Federal Waters; 77,266 State Waters)
  - FY 1992: 196,607 total fish distributed (129,793 Other Federal Waters; 5,000 to Virginia state waters)
  - FY 1993: 98,864 total fish distributed (7,600 Federal/USFWS; 91,264 Other Federal Waters) — representing a **significant decline (~50%)** in output compared to FY 1992, likely correlating with the BKD outbreak and mission transition.
- **Budget Growth**: Operational budget increased steadily from $209.2K (1991 Actual) to a projected $300.0K (1995 Est.), while Maintenance costs fluctuated, peaking at $40.2K in 1995 Est.
- **Staffing**: Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staff increased slightly from 5.0 (1991-1992) to a stable 6.0 (1993-1995).
- **Public Engagement**: The station served a high volume of visitors (65,000 in FY 1994) and conducted 25 staff presentations, indicating a strong public outreach/education component alongside its production duties.
