# Lake Havasu Followup Survey Trip Report (August 1972)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation trip report, dated August 28, 1972, documents a followup survey of [[Lake Havasu]] conducted August 16–18, 1972, as part of an [[Environmental Impact Study]] of the lake's aquatic resources. Five of the originally investigated coves were resurveyed by [[Scuba Diving Survey|scuba diver observation]]. The survey found disease in fingerling and yearling [[Bass]] and an external parasite on [[Channel Catfish]] in one cove, fair fish populations with no apparent disease in two Colorado River coves, and very low fish abundance with poor visibility in Bill Williams River coves. The authors planned one more survey in September or October 1972 and a new proposal for the following year.

## Background & Description

- **Origin:** Memorandum from James Romero (Fishery Biologist) and Wayne O. Deason (aquatic specialist) to the Regional Director, Region 3, [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Boulder City, Nevada.
- **Participants:** A representative of the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] and Mr. Kenney of the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] joined the survey. The [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] was contacted but could not participate.
- **Survey objectives:**
  1. Gather additional information to help define long-range objectives of the study proposal.
  2. Compare the frequency of fingerling bass with the number of fry observed in the earlier spring survey.
  3. Record the species of fish observed.
  4. Record the extent of [[Aquatic Vegetation]].
- **Methods:** Data were collected by scuba diver observations. Observation data sheets and maps of the investigated areas were enclosed.
- **Cove sample:** Of the original cove sites, five were selected for this survey. The document refers to a total of 16 original coves, though the figure is partly garbled in the scan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Colorado River arm.** Coves C and D showed fair populations of fish and no apparent disease. Aquatic vegetation was abundant in both and provided excellent cover.

**Bill Williams River arm.** Coves B and C showed very few fish. Visibility was very poor, so fish counts were impossible in Cove B. Visibility in Cove G3 was reported as 1.0 foot, and twenty adult bass were observed there.

**Disease and parasites.** The survey recorded:
- Fingerling bass with fungus on gills and fins.
- A yearling bass with a lesion beneath its pectoral fin and a completely decayed dorsal fin.
- Channel catfish with an external parasite behind the pectoral fin (parasite unidentified).

**Monitoring history.** Follow-up surveys were conducted on 11 of the original cove sites. Five were included in this survey and six in a previous survey on July 27 and 28. The earlier investigation of the Colorado River arm and Bill Williams River arm is also referenced.

## Key Findings

1. **Survey scope:** Five of the original coves were resurveyed by scuba observation, August 16–18, 1972.
2. **Disease:** Fungus was found on fingerling bass, and a yearling bass had a decayed dorsal fin. A parasite was found on channel catfish. The document does not identify the causes.
3. **Colorado River coves (C and D):** Fair fish populations, no apparent disease, abundant vegetation and excellent cover.
4. **Bill Williams River coves (B and C):** Very few fish and very poor visibility, which made counts impossible in Cove B.
5. **Cove G3:** Visibility of 1.0 foot, with twenty adult bass observed.
6. **Next steps:** One more survey is planned for September or October 1972. After the surveys are complete, the data will be analyzed and a new proposal prepared for the next year.

**Enclosures:** Observation data sheets, maps of the surveyed cove areas, and a USGS 15-minute quadrangle map (Whipple Mountains, California-Arizona).
