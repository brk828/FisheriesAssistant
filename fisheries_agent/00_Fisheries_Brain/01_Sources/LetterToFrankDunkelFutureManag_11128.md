# Ash Meadows Management Correspondence, Desert Fishes Council (1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is two letters from the [[Desert Fishes Council]] to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) in 1986 about the management of [[Ash Meadows National Wildlife Refuge]] in Nye County, Nevada. The Council, which helped secure federal ownership of Ash Meadows, asks for a management plan that protects the ecosystem and its endangered fishes, invertebrates, and plants. Its main concerns are the planned opening of the refuge to hunting before such a plan exists, and the lack of response to earlier correspondence. The letters propose a symposium discussion and offer the Council's expertise. No species data, sample sizes, or measurements are reported.

## Background & Description

- **Letter 1 (October 22, 1986):** Addressed to Frank Dunkel, Director of the USFWS. It says the Council's formation nearly 20 years earlier was tied to protecting habitats and species in Ash Meadows. It notes earlier correspondence with no response and schedules a special discussion for November 21 at the Eighteenth Annual Symposium (November 20–22, St. George, Utah). It asks that key refuge staff from Las Vegas and the Portland Regional Office take part.
- **Letter 2 (June 27, 1986):** Addressed to the Associate Director for Wildlife Resources, USFWS, about opening the refuge to hunting in 1986. It describes the Council as having more than 350 members from federal, state, and university science, conservation groups, and the public.
- **Signatory:** [[Edwin P. Pister]], Executive Secretary. The name in Letter 1 is not legible in the scan.
- **Legal history:** The Council played an active role in the [[Devils Hole Decision]], which gave legal protection to the aquifer supplying [[Devils Hole]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Ash Meadows is a spring-fed desert system whose habitats and species the Council wants protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- The area supports endangered fishes, invertebrates, and plants. The letters do not name species or give population data.
- The aquifer that feeds Devils Hole is the legal basis for protecting the water supply that sustains the ecosystem.
- Future management is expected to combine consumptive uses (such as hunting) with species preservation, which the Council says creates a chance for long-range planning.

## Key Findings

- **Management plan requested:** The Council urges the USFWS to fund and implement a management plan that assures protection of the Ash Meadows ecosystem and its biota "at the earliest possible date."
- **Hunting concerns:** The Council has serious reservations about opening the refuge to hunting before a protective plan exists. If hunting proceeds, it asks that the Service manage it to guarantee protection.
- **Unanswered correspondence:** The Council says it sent earlier letters on this issue and got no reply, which led to the scheduled symposium discussion.
- **Offer of expertise:** The Council offers its technical experience to help the USFWS meet its legal obligations at Ash Meadows.
- **Requested assurance:** The June letter asks the Service to confirm in writing that a management plan will be funded and implemented.
- **Key stakeholders:** Refuge managers from Las Vegas and the Portland Regional Office, with the Portland Regional Director copied.
