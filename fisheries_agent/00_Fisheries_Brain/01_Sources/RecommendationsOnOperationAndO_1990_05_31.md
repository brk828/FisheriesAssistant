## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative/organizational memorandum from the **[[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]]** (FWS Region 6) rather than a primary scientific research paper. It outlines a 1990 proposal to reorganize the Program's Technical Group and Management Group structure in response to concerns raised by biological researchers (via the **[[Biology Researchers Advisory Group]]**, or BioRAG) about inadequate input into work planning and evaluation. While the paper contains minimal direct morphological, physiological, or ecological data on fish species, it provides valuable context on the **institutional framework governing endangered fish recovery efforts** in the Upper Colorado River Basin, including the standing committees (Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information & Education) tasked with overseeing biological research, propagation, genetics, instream flow, and sediment-related studies affecting the basin's endangered big-river fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The document implicitly concerns the **big-river endangered fish community of the Colorado River Basin**, historically including the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]], though these species are not explicitly named in this text.
- **Instream flow needs** are identified as a critical ecological consideration, with the proposed **Water Acquisition Committee** (renamed from "Water Rights") responsible for translating biologically-based flow recommendations into water management actions — indicating that flow regime is treated as a primary limiting factor for these species' habitat.
- **Sediment dynamics** are noted as an active area of technical concern (via a now-disbanding Sediment Subcommittee), suggesting sediment transport/geomorphology was considered ecologically relevant to spawning or habitat conditions for the endangered fishes.
- **Native/non-native fish interactions** are flagged as a subject warranting a dedicated ad hoc subcommittee, indicating recognized ecological competition or predation concerns between native endangered fishes and introduced sportfish species.
- **Propagation and stocking** are explicitly framed as recovery *tools*, to be biologically driven and evaluated by the Biology Committee — reflecting a niche-restoration strategy (augmentation) rather than pure in-situ conservation.
- **Genetics** work (via a Propagation and Genetics Subcommittee) suggests concern for maintaining genetic diversity/integrity in hatchery-supplementation programs, relevant to the population viability of the [[Bonytail]] and other imperiled taxa.

## Key Findings

- The Recovery Program's Technical Group was recommended for **abolition**, with its responsibilities redistributed to three new standing technical committees: **Biology**, **Water Acquisition**, and **Information & Education (I&E)** — each reporting to the Management Group.
- The **Biology Committee** was designated as the central technical body responsible for:
  - Reviewing/evaluating biological studies and reports
  - Coordinating research across the basin
  - Preparing annual biology-related program guidance
  - Overseeing ad hoc subcommittees (Gila taxonomy, propagation/genetics, standardized monitoring, sediment)
  - Evaluating instream flow proposals for the endangered fishes
  - Determining how propagation/stocking tools should be applied to recover wild populations
- Existing **ad hoc biological subcommittees** at the time (1990) included: **Gila Taxonomy**, **Propagation and Genetics**, **Standardized Monitoring**, and **Sediment** — reflecting the key active research fronts for endangered Colorado River fish recovery.
- Additional ad hoc groups were proposed for **native/non-native fish research**, **instream flow quantification**, and **sport fish management**, highlighting recognized knowledge gaps in interspecific interactions and flow-ecology relationships.
- The **Water Acquisition Committee** (retitled from "Water Rights") was tasked with implementing Service-derived, biologically-based instream flow recommendations — establishing a formal link between biological science and water management/legal water rights processes.
- Concerns were raised (BioRAG, Dr. Richard Valdez of Bio/West) that field/research biologists lacked adequate influence over program work planning, prompting recommendations to design committee processes that minimize researchers' administrative burden while still capturing their technical input via "mid-level" biologist representatives.
- The paper underscores that recovery decision-making should proceed through a **biology-first, policy-second sequence** — technical/biological evaluation at the committee level should precede policy interjection at the Management Group level.
