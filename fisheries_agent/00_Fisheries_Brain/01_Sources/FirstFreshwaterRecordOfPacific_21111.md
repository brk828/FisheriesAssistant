# First Freshwater Record of Pacific Lamprey from Baja California, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Petromyzontidae #lampreys #range-extension

## Executive Summary

This note documents the first freshwater record of [[Pacific Lamprey]] (*Lampetra tridentata*) from [[Baja California]], Mexico. An ammocoete (larval lamprey) was collected on 19 February 1995 in the lower [[Rio Santo Domingo]], approximately 600 m above its mouth at Bahia San Ramon, 6 km west of Vicente Guerrero. This represents the southernmost freshwater record for the species in western North America, extending the known range approximately 500 km south from the previous southernmost record at the [[Santa Ana River]] in southern California.

## Background & Description

[[Pacific Lamprey]] (*Lampetra tridentata*) is an anadromous species distributed from Hokkaido, Japan, through the Bering Sea and Aleutian Islands to Punta Canoas, Baja California, Mexico. The species commonly spawns in rivers of western North America from Alaska to southern California. In southern California, the species maintains runs in several unaltered creeks from the [[Carmel River]] south to the [[Santa Ana River]].

The ammocoete stage lacks a sucker and lives buried in mud, feeding on micro-organisms for approximately 5 years. After metamorphosis, the young adult (about 135 mm total length) migrates downstream to the sea. Habitat requirements and distribution are similar to those of anadromous [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) in southern California.

The collected specimen (126.5 mm TL, 3.0 g) was identified by diagnostic characteristics including: hood surrounding toothless mouth, nasal pit, confluent gill openings, and body brown on sides and back with light silver below. Morphometric measurements included: tail length 0.289 TL; body depth 0.058 TL; branchial length 0.111 TL; height of first dorsal 0.006 TL; height of second dorsal 0.014 TL; length of first dorsal 0.153 TL; and 67 trunk myomeres.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimen was captured with a minnow seine at a branch in the stream measuring 5 m wide and 40 cm deep, with bottom substrate of sand and gravel, and salinity of 0.3 ppt. The collection site was flowing into a lagoon near the mouth of [[Rio Santo Domingo]].

Prior to this record, [[Pacific Lamprey]] had not been reported from freshwater in [[Baja California]]. The only previous record from Baja California was a marine occurrence documented by Hubbs (1967): a juvenile male (170 mm TL) captured 55 km southwest of Punta Canoas (28°58'N, 115°25'W) during a pelagic research trawl along with [[Pacific Hake]] (*Merluccius productus*). Hubbs speculated that the juvenile lamprey "hitch-hiked a ride on a hake" from streams tributary to [[Monterey Bay]], central California.

## Key Findings

- **First freshwater record**: This represents the first documented freshwater occurrence of [[Pacific Lamprey]] in [[Baja California]], Mexico
- **Range extension**: The collection extends the southernmost known freshwater record for the species from the [[Santa Ana River]] in southern California to [[Rio Santo Domingo]] in Baja California, Mexico (30°43'N, 116°02'W)
- **Specimen details**: Single ammocoete collected 19 February 1995, measuring 126.5 mm TL and 3.0 g
- **Habitat characteristics**: Collection site featured sand and gravel substrate, 5 m width, 40 cm depth, and 0.3 ppt salinity
- **Voucher specimen**: Specimen (UABC-0111) deposited in Fish Collection, Facultad de Ciencias, Universidad Autonoma de Baja California, Ensenada
- **Biogeographic significance**: This record suggests that [[Pacific Lamprey]] may utilize streams in northern [[Baja California]] for spawning, similar to populations in southern California
- **Ecological parallel**: The species' habitat requirements and distribution pattern mirror those of anadromous [[Rainbow Trout]] in the region
