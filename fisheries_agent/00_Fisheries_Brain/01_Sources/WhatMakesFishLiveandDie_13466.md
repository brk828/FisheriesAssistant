## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Oxygen solubility and temperature.** In fresh waters, temperature is the most important factor controlling [[Oxygen Solubility]]. Temperature also changes the lethal DO concentration. Burdick reported that smallmouth bass died within 5 to 9 hours at 0.7 to 1.17 ppm DO across 52°F to 72°F.

**Interference with oxygen uptake.** The text identifies several factors that impair oxygen uptake or raise oxygen demand:
- High and low [[pH]] levels impair the ability of fishes to absorb oxygen from water.
- High [[Carbon Dioxide]] concentrations interfere with the use of dissolved oxygen.
- Under pollution, fish generally require more oxygen.
- At low DO, fish succumb to toxic materials that they would tolerate at high DO.
- Activity increases oxygen requirements, and acclimation can change resistance to low DO.

**Blood chemistry.** [[Fry and Black]] found that the common sucker, whose blood is sensitive to CO2, could not extract oxygen from water with CO2 tensions that did not hinder bullheads, whose blood has very low CO2 sensitivity.

**Species differences.** The ability to extract oxygen and tolerate low DO varies by species. Dogfish, carp and gar survive much lower DO than trout and several other species.

## Key Findings

**Lethal and turnover thresholds (Burdick and related reports):**
- Smallmouth bass died in 5 to 9 hours at 0.7 to 1.17 ppm DO (52°F to 72°F).
- At 55°F and 1 to 2 ppm DO, turnover times were: brook trout 1½ hours, brown trout 2½ hours, rainbow trout 3 hours.
- At 69°F to 71°F, these trout turned over in roughly the same time at 2.3 to 3.4 ppm DO.

**Minimum tolerances reported:**
- Some brook trout endured 1.2 ppm DO for a short period, but asphyxiation occurred at 2.5 ppm.
- Small black bass lived for a time at 0.4 ppm DO.
- Ultimate minimal DO tolerance of brook trout at 49°F was 0.9 ppm.
- Asphyxial level of trout was 1.1 ppm at 6.5°C and 3.4 ppm at 25°C.
- Carp and buffalo fish lived at 2.2 ppm DO.
- A variety of fishes appeared only above 4 ppm DO, and the greatest variety occurred at 9 ppm.
- Fish died overnight in water below 2 ppm DO.

**Respiratory compensation:** Goldfish, perch, catfish and other freshwater species held at constant flow, composition and temperature (20°C to 25°C) compensated in both ventilation volume and rate once DO fell slightly below 5 ppm.

**Oxygen supersaturation:** Some fish tolerate sudden large DO changes and live in supersaturated water. Rising DO slowed their respiratory movements.

**Conclusions and plan:**
- Tentative DO criteria should be set now and revised as data improve.
- Criteria must account for temperature, CO2, pH, pollution, species and acclimation.
- Final species-specific criteria await further research.
