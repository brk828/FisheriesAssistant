# Five New Species of Gyrodactylus (Trematoda: Monogenea) from the Southeastern U.S.

#ichthyology #marine-biology #parasitology #monogenea #gyrodactylidae #freshwater-parasites

## Executive Summary

This 1967 taxonomic paper by Wellborn and Rogers describes **five new species** of the monogenean skin/fin ectoparasite genus *[[Gyrodactylus]]* collected from freshwater fish hosts across the southeastern United States (Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina). The parasites were recovered during surveys of fish parasites at National Fish Hatcheries and via the Southeastern Cooperative Fish Parasite and Disease Project. Each species is distinguished primarily by sclerotized hard-part morphology of the haptor (anchors, dorsal bar, ventral bar/shield, marginal hooks) and reproductive structures (cirrus with stylets), following the descriptive methodology of Rogers and Wellborn (1965) and the collection technique of Putz and Hoffman (1963). One species, *[[Gyrodactylus megacanthus]]*, is notable for possessing a previously undescribed **sclerotized peduncular bar**, unique among known North American *Gyrodactylus* species at the time.

## Background & Description

All five species share the general *Gyrodactylus* body plan: a **fusiform body** with a **smooth, thin cuticle**, lacking eyespots (typical of the genus), and a posterior attachment organ (**haptor**) bearing **16 marginal hooklets**, paired **anchors**, a **dorsal bar**, and a **ventral bar with shield**. Reproductive morphology includes a **cirrus** armed with a large spine plus variable numbers of accessory stylets.

### Key Diagnostic Notes by Species

- **G. etheostomae**: Cirrus pouch with one large spine + five stylets; pharynx bilobed, 36 µm; vesicular glands 15–16 µm. Dorsal bar bears a *simple* (not C-shaped) notch, distinguishing it from *[[Gyrodactylus macrochiri]]* and *[[Gyrodactylus percinae]]*.
- **G. hoffmani**: Cirrus with one large spine + 4–6 stylets; ventral shield with 11–14 longitudinal striations; distal shield edge slightly notched. Named in honor of Dr. Glenn L. Hoffman.
- **G. megacanthus**: Possesses a unique **inverted V-shaped sclerotized peduncular bar** (width 32 µm, height 16 µm) located on the ventral peduncle anterior to the haptor — a structure absent in all other known North American congeners at time of publication. Aberrant (distorted) dorsal bar observed in a single specimen, attributed to mounting artifact. Genus name derived from Greek *mega* (large) + *acanthus* (thorn).
- **G. baeacanthus**: Unique among congeners for a cirrus bearing **two rows of stylets** (outer row 6–7 large stylets, inner row 5–6 small stylets, visible only under phase microscopy); anchors small and stout with greatly expanded base. Name from Greek *baeo* (little) + *acanthus* (thorn).
- **G. viriosus**: Largest species described in this set (up to 730 µm body length); marginal hook base expanded into a distinctive crescent-like plate; ventral bar lateral projections angled ~70° from horizontal (versus ~45° in other species). Name from Latin *viriosus* (robust).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Gyrodactylus* spp. are **viviparous ectoparasitic monogeneans** that attach to the **fins and body surface** of freshwater fish hosts via the haptor's hooks and anchors — all five species in this study were recovered specifically from these locations. Host-parasite associations documented:

- *[[Gyrodactylus etheostomae]]* ← *[[Etheostoma radiosum]]* (orangebelly darter) — Warm Fork of Spring River, Arkansas
- *[[Gyrodactylus hoffmani]]* ← *[[Pimephales promelas]]* (fathead minnow) — experimental farm ponds, Alabama
- *[[Gyrodactylus megacanthus]]* ← *[[Fundulus olivaceus]]* (blackspotted topminnow, type host, Mississippi) and *[[Fundulus notti]]* (starhead topminnow, Alabama) — demonstrates capacity to parasitize multiple congeneric hosts within *Fundulus*
- *[[Gyrodactylus baeacanthus]]* ← *[[Notropis venustus]]* (blacktail shiner) — Lake Seminole, Georgia, plus multiple creek localities in Alabama and Louisiana
- *[[Gyrodactylus viriosus]]* ← *[[Hypentelium etowanum]]* (Alabama hog sucker, type host, Georgia) and *[[Hypentelium nigricans]]* (northern hog sucker, North Carolina) — another case of cross-species host use within a single sucker genus

This pattern illustrates a recurring theme in the *[[Gyrodactylidae]]*: species-level host specificity is often constrained to closely related host genera (e.g., congeneric *Fundulus* or *Hypentelium* species) rather than strict single-host specificity. Hosts were sampled from lotic (rivers, creeks) and lentic (lakes, ponds, drainage ditches) freshwater habitats, including both wild streams and National Fish Hatchery systems, indicating these parasites are widespread across natural and managed freshwater environments in the region.

## Key Findings

- **Five new *Gyrodactylus* species** are formally described and diagnosed based on sclerotized haptoral and reproductive hard-part morphology, all recovered from freshwater fish in the southeastern U.S.
- ***Gyrodactylus megacanthus*** is distinguished from **all known North American *Gyrodactylus*** by possessing a unique **sclerotized peduncular bar**, a novel structural feature for the genus at the time of publication.
- ***Gyrodactylus baeacanthus*** possesses a **cirrus with two distinct rows of stylets**, an unusual reproductive armature feature described as diagnostic and requiring phase-contrast microscopy to fully resolve the inner row.
- Several species show close morphological resemblance to previously described congeners, allowing comparative taxonomy:
  - *G. etheostomae* compared to *[[Gyrodactylus eucaliae]]*, *[[Gyrodactylus macrochiri]]*, *[[Gyrodactylus heterodactylus]]*, *[[Gyrodactylus percinae]]*.
  - *G. hoffmani* compared to *[[Gyrodactylus bullatarudis]]*, *[[Gyrodactylus couesius]]*, *[[Gyrodactylus gambusiae]]*.
  - *G. viriosus* most closely resembles *[[Gyrodactylus percinae]]* but is larger with differently shaped dorsal/ventral bars.
- Host-switching between closely related congeneric fish species (*Fundulus olivaceus*/*Fundulus notti*; *Hypentelium etowanum*/*Hypentelium nigricans*) was documented for two of the five parasite species, suggesting genus-level rather than strict species-level host specificity in some *Gyrodactylus* taxa.
- All type and paratype specimens were deposited in the **USNM Helminthological Collection**, with additional paratypes retained in the authors' collections, establishing a permanent taxonomic reference framework for future comparative work.
- This study builds directly on prior methodology and taxonomic framework established by **Rogers and Wellborn (1965)** and the collection technique of **Putz and Hoffman (1963)**, reflecting a systematic, cumulative effort to catalog *Gyrodactylus* diversity on North American freshwater fishes.
