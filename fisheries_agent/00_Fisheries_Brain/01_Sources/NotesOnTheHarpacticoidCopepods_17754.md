# Harpacticoid Copepods Attheyella pilosa and A. carolinensis: Crayfish Associates in the Eastern United States

#freshwater-biology #carcinology #harpacticoida

## Executive Summary

This 1968 paper in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington* examines two harpacticoid copepods of the genus [[Attheyella]], [[Attheyella Pilosa]] and [[Attheyella Carolinensis]], which live on [[Crayfish]] hosts in the eastern United States. The authors redescribe both species, provide a diagnostic key separating them, map their distributions across southeastern drainages, and present a life-history study of *A. pilosa* at [[Doe Run]], Kentucky, based on 22 samples. The paper also corrects an earlier misidentification: specimens recorded as *A. carolinensis* from Doe Run are actually *A. pilosa*.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** Both species were first described by [[Chappuis]]. *A. pilosa* was based on material from [[Mammoth Cave]], Kentucky, and Donnaldson Cave, Indiana (1929a). *A. carolinensis* was described from Chapel Hill, North Carolina (1932). Later reports were limited: *A. carolinensis* was described in detail by Coker (1934), reported from Mountain Lake, Virginia (Carter, 1944), and erroneously reported from Doe Run (Prins, 1964). The only published record of *A. pilosa* after its original description was a single female from Luchil Cave, Yucatan (C. B. Wilson, 1936), which the authors consider unconfirmed.

**Diagnostic morphology of *A. pilosa*.**
- Female body length usually 0.58–0.70 mm (extremes 0.53–0.76 mm); male usually 0.52–0.64 mm (extremes 0.46–0.68 mm).
- Urosomite spine rows are complete dorsally, and the caudal ramus is narrow, with a comb of fine setae on the medial margin.
- Female leg 5 has a basal expansion bearing 5 setae, and the distal segment is about 1.7 times as long as wide.
- Leg 3 endopod is 2-segmented in females and 3-segmented in males.

**Key differences from *A. carolinensis*.** The authors found items 2 and 5 most useful in practice:
- The spine rows on urosomites are complete in *A. pilosa* but limited to the ventral and lateral parts in *A. carolinensis*.
- The female leg 5 distal segment in *A. carolinensis* is longer and narrower (about 2.6 times as long as wide), with its longest seta more than twice the medial seta.
- Other differences include caudal ramus width, setal combs, and the number of setae on the basal expansion of female leg 5.

**Regional context.** Of the 9 North American *Attheyella* species, five occur east of the Mississippi River. *A. americana* and *A. illinoisensis* have short caudal rami, and *A. obatogamensis* has a spiniform process on the caudal ramus, so *A. pilosa* and *A. carolinensis* are the two most similar.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hosts and infestation.** Both copepods were collected from crayfishes, which are the "associates" in the title. At Doe Run, *A. pilosa* formed "dense reddish assemblages" on the pleopods, coxal bases of the pereiopods, sternal hairs, and other undersurfaces of [[Cambarus Tenebrosus]] and [[Orconectes Rusticus Rusticus]]. Other *A. carolinensis* records came from [[Cambarus Asperimanus]] and an undescribed *Cambarus* of the *C. bartonii* group.

**Distribution.** Records are too sparse for firm generalizations, but the authors note:
- *A. pilosa* predominates west of the Appalachians.
- *A. carolinensis* is concentrated in Atlantic-draining streams, including the upper [[Savannah River]] and [[Saluda River]], and in streams feeding the [[Tennessee River]] and [[Coosa River]].
- Downstream in the Coosa drainage (and possibly the Tennessee), *A. carolinensis* is replaced by *A. pilosa*.
- Only one collection contained both species: a stream in the Saluda drainage near Cleveland, Greenville County, South Carolina.
- A *A. carolinensis* record from Mill Creek, Cumberland River drainage, Kentucky (from *Cambarus distans*), breaks the otherwise clean east-west separation.

The authors suggest that drainage boundaries do not fully limit movement, possibly through crayfish overland migration, underground connections, or stream piracy.

**Life history at Doe Run.** The study used 22 samples collected between November 1962 and September 1964 from three stations on [[Doe Run]], Meade County, Kentucky: Station I (stream source), Station IA (about ⅓ mile downstream), and Station II (2 miles downstream). Between 50 and 160 specimens were randomly selected per sample. Key findings include:
- **Host seasonality:** Infestation of *Orconectes* was highest in winter, when the crayfish is torpid and rarely molts, and lowest in midsummer, when molting is most frequent. This suggests the copepods do not transfer back to the host after molting, unlike [[Branchiobdellid Worms]] and [[Entocytherid Ostracods]].
- **Life stages:** Only adult males and females were found despite searching, implying that immature stages are free-living on the stream bottom. The authors cite [[Nitocrella Divaricata]], in which copepodids remain on the host, for comparison.
- **Breeding:** Females with egg sacs were found in February, March, April, June, and September, so breeding probably occurs year-round.
- **Sex ratio:** Males ranged from 25% (5 May 1964) to 65% (22 Nov 1964, Station IA), with 49.8% males across all samples. The authors consider the fluctuations fortuitous.
- **Population structure:** Size-frequency histograms showed 2 or more poorly defined peaks, consistent with a [[Multivoltine Species]] producing perhaps 5–10 generations per year with generation intervals of 1–2 months. Mixed generations from multiple local populations make host assemblages heterogeneous.
- **Size and temperature:** Coefficients of variability ranged from 2.4 to 8.4 (usually 3–6). No relationship between size and water temperature was found. Annual temperature ranges were small in this spring-fed stream: Station I 2.6 °C, Station IA 4 °C, and Station II about 8 °C.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomic correction:** Specimens reported as *A. carolinensis* from Doe Run (Prins, 1964) were reexamined and identified as *A. pilosa*.
- **Redescription:** *A. pilosa* and *A. carolinensis* are redescribed and compared, with the authors recommending comparison of the urosomite spine rows and the distal leg 5 setal lengths as the most reliable diagnostic characters.
- **Unverified record:** The Yucatan record of *A. pilosa* (Luchil Cave) is not in Smithsonian collections and cannot be accepted without confirmation.
- **Distribution pattern:** *A. pilosa* dominates west of the Appalachians; *A. carolinensis* dominates Atlantic-draining streams; the two replace each other downstream in the Coosa drainage. Only one sympatric collection was documented.
- **Host relationships:** Both species are crayfish associates in spring-fed streams. Infestation at Doe Run tracks host molting, with the highest prevalence in winter.
- **Life history:** The population is multivoltine, breeds year-round, has a near-equal sex ratio (49.8% males), and shows no size–temperature relationship within a narrow thermal range.
- **Research gaps:** The authors state that the distribution data are "far too inadequate" for detailed generalizations, and that the free-living copepodid stages and the degree of host dependence remain unknown.
