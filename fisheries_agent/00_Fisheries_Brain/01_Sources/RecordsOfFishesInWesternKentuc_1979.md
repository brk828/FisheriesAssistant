# Records of Fishes in Western Kentucky With Additions to the Known Fauna

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #biogeography #petromyzontidae #esocidae #cyprinidae #ictaluridae #centrarchidae #percidae #kentucky-fauna

## Executive Summary
This 1979 faunal survey by Burr and Mayden documents significant additions and distributional records to the ichthyofauna of western Kentucky, specifically the region west of the [[Green River]]. The study formally adds **four species** new to the state checklist: *Ichthyomyzon gagei*, *Noturus hildebrandi*, *Lepomis marginatus*, and *Etheostoma parvipinne*. Additionally, the paper provides critical range extensions and habitat data for eleven other poorly known species, including *Lampetra aepyptera*, *Esox niger*, and members of the *[[Etheostoma]]* genus. The research raises the total known species count for the state of Kentucky to **211**, driven largely by targeted collecting in previously under-surveyed tributaries of the [[Tennessee River]] and [[Obion River]] drainages. A key taxonomic focus is the morphological differentiation between the sympatric/allopatric species pairs *E. swaini*/*E. asprigene* and *E. neopterum*/*E. squamiceps*.

## Background & Description
The paper relies heavily on meristic (countable) characters to distinguish closely related species, often overlapping in general morphology but separable via specific counts or breeding coloration.

*   **[[Ichthyomyzon Gagei]] (Southern Brook Lamprey):**
    *   Distinguished from parasitic *[[Ichthyomyzon Castaneus]]* by smaller size, narrower disc, and poorly developed posterior teeth.
    *   Specimen data: 54 myomeres, shallowly notched dorsal fin.
*   **[[Lampetra Aepyptera]] (Least Brook Lamprey):**
    *   Myomere counts: Ammocoetes 53–58; Adults 54–57.
*   **[[Esox Niger]] (Chain Pickerel) vs. *E. americanus*:**
    *   *E. niger* possesses 14–15 branchiostegal rays and a longer snout.
    *   *E. americanus* typically has 11–12 branchiostegal rays and a shorter snout.
*   **[[Lepomis Marginatus]] (Dollar Sunfish) vs. *L. megalotis*:**
    *   *L. marginatus*: 12–12 pectoral rays, 9 infraorbital pores, 4 rows of cheek scales.
    *   *L. megalotis*: 13–13 pectoral rays, 5–6 rows of cheek scales, 12–13 infraorbital pores.
*   **[[Etheostoma Swaini]] vs. [[Etheostoma Asprigene]] (Table 1 Data):**
    *   *E. swaini* exhibits brighter breeding coloration (alternating red-orange/blue vertical bands).
    *   Meristics show overlap but differ in central tendencies for lateral line scales (mean 41.3 vs 35.3) and dorsal fin rays (mean 11.5 vs 13.1).
*   **[[Etheostoma Neopterum]] vs. [[Etheostoma Squamiceps]] (Table 2 Data):**
    *   Primary diagnostic character is sexually dimorphic: Breeding male *E. neopterum* develop expanded "knobs" on the second dorsal fin with membranes absent distally (rays free).
    *   *E. squamiceps* lacks knobs; interradial membranes extend nearly to the fin edge.
    *   Infraorbital canal is complete in the majority of *E. neopterum* (45/46) but incomplete in the majority of *E. squamiceps* (42/49).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The survey details specific microhabitat preferences essential for locating these species, emphasizing structural complexity (debris) and substrate type.

*   **Lithophilic/Riffle Specialists:** *[[Noturus Phaeus]]* and *[[Noturus Hildebrandi]]* were consistently found in gravel/sand riffles beneath accumulated debris (log jams, leaves), often nocturnal and syntopic. *N. hildebrandi* was found at depths of 16–40 cm.
*   **Lentic/Swamp Specialists:** *[[Lepomis Punctatus]]* (Spotted Sunfish) and *[[Lepomis Marginatus]]* inhabit vegetated oxbow lakes and cypress swamps with mud/sand substrates overlain by organic sediment (0.5–1 m depth). *L. marginatus* was collected alongside *[[Lepomis Symmetricus]]* and *[[Elassoma Zonatum]]* in a heavily vegetated cypress swamp (Murpheys Pond).
*   **Spring/Headwater Specialists:** *[[Etheostoma Parvipinne]]* (Goldstripe Darter) was restricted to small (1–2 m wide) spring-fed creeks with overhanging banks and sand/gravel substrate, notably lacking aquatic vegetation.
*   **Vegetated Slow-water:** *[[Etheostoma Proeliare]]* (Cypress Darter) favors leaf-laden, sluggish water bodies and oxbow lake margins.
*   **Reproductive Notes:** *Lampetra aepyptera* was observed building nests in sand/gravel in Beechy Creek (March), while *Etheostoma zonale* and *E. swaini* females were documented as "ripe with eggs" in March and July respectively, indicating localized spawning windows.

## Key Findings
*   **New State Records:** Formal confirmation of *Ichthyomyzon gagei*, *Noturus hildebrandi* (subspecies *N. h. lautus*), *Lepomis marginatus*, and *Etheostoma parvipinne* in Kentucky waters.
*   **Range Extensions:**
    *   *I. gagei* found in the [[Clarks River]] represents the northernmost record for the Tennessee River drainage, aligning latitudinally with a Missouri (Gasconade River) record reported by Pflieger (1975).
    *   *[[Percina Copelandi]]* (Channel Darter) and *[[Percina Evides]]* (Gilt Darter) records from a 1942 pre-impoundment TVA survey extend their known range ~200 km west, though the collection site is now inundated by [[Kentucky Lake]].
    *   *[[Clinostomus Funduloides]]* (Rosyside Dace) confirmed in the Cumberland drainage (Donaldson Creek), likely representing the subspecies *C. f. estor*.
*   **Taxonomic Clarification:** *Etheostoma neopterum* (Lollypop Darter), only recently described from Tennessee/Alabama, is confirmed as "abundant" in Kentucky tributaries of the Tennessee River, previously misidentified as *E. squamiceps*. The two species appear allopatric in Kentucky.
*   **Updated Faunal List:** The total confirmed fish species count for Kentucky is now **211** (down from a technical 213 due to the removal of *Notropis heterolepis* and *N. coccogenis* for lack of extant specimens).
*   **Conservation Status:** The authors propose a list of species for **threatened status** in Kentucky due to limited habitat/specimens, including *I. gagei*, *E. niger*, *N. phaeus*, *N. hildebrandi*, *L. punctatus*, *L. marginatus*, *E. parvipinne*, and *E. swaini*. Conversely, *E. asprigene* and *E. proeliare* are suggested for removal from threatened lists due to being more common than previously believed.
*   **Future Search Targets:** Several Mississippi River species known from the Missouri side (e.g., *[[Scaphirhynchus Albus]]*, *Hybognathus argyritis*) are flagged as likely candidates for future discovery on the Kentucky side of the river.
