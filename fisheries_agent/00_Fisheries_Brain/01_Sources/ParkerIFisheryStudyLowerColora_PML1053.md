# Parker I Fishery Study, Lower Colorado River (October 1985 – October 1986)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #Cyprinidae #Ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Bureau of Reclamation report, prepared by Steven Hiebert and Stephen Grabowski of the Division of Research and Laboratory Services (Denver), documents a year-long fishery and limnological survey of the [[Parker I Subdivision]] of the lower [[Colorado River]]. The study sampled seven bankline habitat types with [[Electrofishing]] and beach seines, plus larval fish tows, water chemistry, and aquatic macrophyte surveys. Its purpose was to supply baseline data for evaluating proposed river modifications in the [[Parker II Subdivision]].

Eighteen fish species and one turtle species were collected. Backwater riparian habitat supported the highest fish catch per unit effort (CPUE), while main-river cobble yielded the lowest. Nearly all fish collected were introduced species.

## Background & Description

- **Study area:** The Parker Division is a 71.3 km (44.3 mi) reach. Parker I extends about 22.4 km (14 mi) from Headgate Rock Dam (R.M. 177.7) to just below Agnes Wilson Bridge (R.M. 163.0). Its channel was highly modified by [[Bank Stabilization]] and riverfront development, which created backwaters. Parker II has not been extensively modified.
- **Sampling schedule:** Seven sampling trips were made: October 20–22 and December 16–18, 1985; February 24–26, April 21–23, June 15–17, August 18–20, and October 20–22, 1986.
- **Electrofishing:** A direct-current, pulsed-current unit mounted on a 5.5 m aluminum boat was run at night along premeasured 300 m bankline sections, with two passes per site. Fish were identified, weighed, measured, and released; selected game fish were tagged. Data were analyzed in SPSS.
- **CPUE standard:** Defined as the number or weight of fish per 100 m of specific bank type.
- **Other methods:** Fine-mesh beach seines sampled shallow, low-flow water. Paired larval-fish nets (0.505 mm mesh) with flowmeters sampled ichthyoplankton. Macrophytes were sampled with a 1 m² quadrat. Temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, and specific conductance were measured with a multiparameter probe at 8–9 sites. Water chemistry samples were analyzed in December 1985 and June 1986.
- **Sites:** Fifteen sample sites characterized the major habitat types, which were mapped from 1:24,000 Bureau of Reclamation river maps.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat composition (Table 2):** Vegetated riprap made up 29.0% of Parker I bankline and backwater riparian bank 28.0% combined with backwater riparian; natural riparian riverbank 16.4%; nonvegetated riprap 9.6%; Deer Island riparian 9.2%; cattails about 1.4–15%; cobble 1.4%; muck 5.5%.

**Limnology:**
- Backwaters were warmer and had higher specific conductance than the main river. The "natural" backwater (site 2) reached 33.4 °C in August.
- Dissolved oxygen stayed above 7.6 mg/L at all sites.
- Main river specific conductance averaged 994 µS/cm, versus 1,449 µS/cm in the "natural" backwater.
- Main-river pH averaged 8.20; backwater pH averaged 8.10.
- Light extinction coefficients ranged from 0.48 in the main river to 3.5 in the "natural" backwater.
- Secchi depth averaged about 2.2 m in the main river and was lowest in the "natural" and medium backwaters.

**Water chemistry:** Sodium in the "natural" backwater reached 115.0 mg/L in December 1985, and magnesium reached 39.2 mg/L in the same backwater (30.5 mg/L in June 1986). Phosphorus and orthophosphate were very low or undetected, and nitrite-nitrogen was always below detection limits. The main river and most backwaters were phosphorus-limited. Manganese in the "natural" backwater (0.46 mg/L in December 1985) exceeded the EPA criterion of 0.050 mg/L, while other trace metals were generally below EPA freshwater life limits.

**Aquatic macrophytes:** Found primarily in backwaters. Watermilfoil (*Myriophyllum* sp.) peaked in summer and declined in autumn.

**Deer Island Lake:** A breach in the upstream dike initially made the lake behave like a side channel of the main river. After the breach was repaired midway through the study, the lake developed limnology similar to other backwaters.

**Biota:** Benthic macroinvertebrates and plankton were collected but not analyzed. Crayfish were not collected but were observed. Muskrats and beavers were seen on each survey. Razorback sucker fry (329,800 in March, 100,000 in May, and 50,000 in June 1986) were stocked at the "natural" backwater, and their fate is unknown.

## Key Findings

**Fish community**
- Eighteen fish species and one turtle species were collected. Carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) was the most numerous species and dominated nongame biomass (about 59% of nongame fish count). Bluegill (*Lepomis macrochirus*) was the most commonly collected and widespread game fish.
- Backwater riparian habitat had the highest CPUE for nearly all species and the closest game-to-nongame weight ratio (1:4.4). Main-river cobble had the lowest CPUE.
- Largemouth bass, bluegill, carp, redear sunfish, crappie, threadfin shad, and channel catfish were highest in backwaters.
- Flathead catfish were associated mainly with riprap (81% of 58 collected).
- Striped bass (two individuals, 30 and 38 g) and rainbow trout (29 individuals, 31% from cobble below Headgate Rock Dam) were sparse. Rainbow trout were stocked for the Colorado River Indian Tribes.

**Species of concern and notable records**
- Razorback sucker was not collected, despite stocking.
- Threadfin shad larvae were abundant in April backwaters (up to 6.27 per m³).
- Redbelly tilapia was collected mainly from backwater riparian habitat, with 96% of 104 individuals.

**Comparison with Parker II**
- Parker I had a higher percentage of backwater and vegetated riprap; Parker II had more cattails (32% vs. 15%) and cutbanks (38% vs. 0%).
- Limnological differences were small. Specific conductance averaged 1,005 µS/cm in Parker II versus 994 µS/cm in Parker I. Total dissolved solids averaged 638 mg/L in Parker II and 549 mg/L in Parker I.
- Largemouth bass CPUE was considerably higher in Parker II main-river riparian habitat.

**Tagging:** Recovery rates were low. The highest recapture percentage was for smallmouth bass from riprapped bankline. Thirty-one percent of recovered largemouth bass and 30% of recovered smallmouth bass were recaptured.

**Conclusions and planning use:** The report provides quantitative baseline data for evaluating river modifications in Parker II. It notes that backwaters act as winter thermal refugia for fish and that backwater habitats are the most productive for fish in this reach.
