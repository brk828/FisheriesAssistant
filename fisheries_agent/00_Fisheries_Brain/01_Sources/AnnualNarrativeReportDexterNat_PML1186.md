# Dexter National Fish Hatchery Annual Narrative Report, Calendar Year 1985

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This annual narrative report from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, New Mexico, Pecos River Valley) covers calendar year 1985. The station works entirely on holding, spawning, rearing and distributing imperiled southwestern fishes. Its main output in 1985 was 3,151,708 [[Razorback Sucker]] weighing about 2,121 pounds, distributed to the Gila, Salt and Verde river systems in Arizona and to Lake Mohave. The report also covers [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] production, fish health, preserved-specimen supply, staffing, facilities, cooperative research and outreach. The OCR source contains many garbled numerals, so some values below are flagged where the reading is uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Setting:** The hatchery is in the Pecos River Valley of southeastern New Mexico. Construction began in 1931 and fish rearing started in 1932.
- **Original mission:** The station was built to produce warmwater game fish. New laws and Service policies later shifted it to endangered southwestern species.
- **Staffing:** Seven permanent (PFT) employees and four seasonal or temporary employees worked at the station in 1985. Positions included Hatchery Manager (Buddy Jensen), Assistant Manager, Fishery Biologist, Fishery Management Biologist, two Maintenance Workers, an Office Assistant and a YCC Group Leader. James Brooks joined on November 24.
- **Species held in 1985:** Table 1 lists about 25 fish taxa held at the station, mostly from the families Cyprinidae, Cyprinodontidae, Poeciliidae and Catostomidae. They include the Colorado squawfish, bonytail, humpback chub, Pahranagat roundtail chub, Chihuahua chub, Yaqui chub, woundfin, beautiful shiner, three pupfish species, several gambusias and topminnows, and the razorback sucker.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution areas:** Stocking covered portions of the [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] and their tributaries in eastern and central Arizona, plus [[Lake Mohave]] on the mainstream Colorado River near Kingman, Arizona.
- **Threat context:** Most species are listed as endangered or threatened. The report describes recovery efforts as the core purpose of the hatchery.
- **Habitat and survival:** Recaptures in 1985 documented razorback sucker survival at four Arizona localities, including the [[Verde River]] at Beasley Flat, the [[Gila River]] at Allenville, [[Carrizo Creek]] and [[Cedar Creek]] on the [[Ft. Apache Indian Reservation]], and [[Bonito Creek]].
- **Disease:** Lernaea, a parasitic copepod, was eliminated for the first time since Colorado River fishes arrived at the hatchery in 1979–1980. Beautiful shiners still carry Asian tapeworm, and Comanche Springs pupfish populations remain subdued by [[Ichthyophonus]].
- **Water supply:** Water for the hatchery comes from wells. Well #3 was replaced in 1985.

## Key Findings

### Razorback sucker (RBS)

- **Domestic broodstock:** 134 three- and four-year-old domestic females were spawned using chorionic gonadotropin ([[Chorionic Gonadotropin]]). The reported total was about 817,144 eggs (the mean egg take figure is garbled in the OCR). Egg viability averaged 62.5%. Swim-up fry numbered about 2,185,372, or 52.4% of expected (OCR-uncertain).
- **Incubation comparison:** Viability was similar in [[Heath Tray]]s (62.3%) and jars (63.2%). Swim-up fry were 54% of expected in Heath trays but only 46.7% in jars, so the report concludes that Heath trays are superior.
- **Wild broodstock:** Four wild females averaging 7.86 pounds produced 505,607 eggs. Egg viability averaged 86.4%, and swim-up fry numbered about 361,268 (82.7% of expected).
- **Rearing and stocking:** About 276,200 fry went into four production ponds. Of these, 264,896 were harvested as 1.5-inch subfingerlings (95.9% return). Other fry went to research ponds, to the [[Page Springs Fish Hatchery]] (120,000) and to the Salt River or its tributaries in central Arizona (2,537,072). A 200,578-fry subset went to Diana Welsman's larval feeding ecology study.
- **Pond performance:** One pond group yielded 140,663 three-inch fish from 150,051 subfingerlings (93.7% return). Another yielded 50,905 five-inch fingerlings from 52,930 three-inch fingerlings (96.2% return). Feed conversion was 2.13.
- **Total distribution:** 3,151,708 RBS weighing about 2,121 pounds were distributed in 1985 (Table 2).
- **Genetic diversity:** About 2,000 larval RBS were collected from Lake Mohave to broaden the broodstock. Of 1,539 survivors, 625 were kept as future broodfish, 100 went to Dr. Don Buth at UCLA for electrophoretic analysis, and 814 were stocked in Eagle Creek, a Gila River tributary. The station also maintains 336 three- and four-year-old broodfish.

### Colorado squawfish (CSF)

- **Spawning:** Eighteen domestic females averaging 3.63 pounds were spawned using carp pituitary. The total egg take was about 1,490,106 (OCR-uncertain). Egg viability averaged 54.7%, and swim-up fry numbered about 460,383 (56.5% of expected).
- **Rearing:** About 299,600 fry went into five production ponds. Of these, 137,690 (46%) were harvested as subfingerlings. Another 107,314 subfingerlings were moved into three ponds, and 86,145 three-inch fingerlings were harvested (80.3% return). Feed conversion was 4.53.
- **Experimental population status:** A final rule published July 24, 1985 designated "experimental population" status for relocated Colorado squawfish and woundfin. Under this rule, 196 four-year-old CSF were released into the [[Verde River]] near Perkinsville on August 26. About 30,000 two-inch fingerlings were stocked in [[Cherry Creek]] on August 28.
- **Broodstock:** 302 four-year-old future brood and 198 eleven-year-old CSF broodfish were maintained.

### Bonytail chub (BTC)

- No bonytail were spawned artificially in 1985. Fish spawned naturally in the ponds when broodfish were moved in early fall. 12,618 four-inch fingerlings were collected and stocked in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona.
- The station maintains 494 four-year-old BTC broodfish and 1,424 excess BTC scheduled for "experimental" stocking in 1986 or 1987.

### Woundfin and other species

- **Woundfin:** Spawning was carried out in a pond with a plugged outflow and drilled holes that allowed water to pass through. 128 woundfin were placed in the spawning pond. Spawning occurred, and larval woundfin were moved to the holding house. The report says no spawning technique was fully reliable.
- **Pahranagat roundtail chub:** 45 fish from the State of Nevada arrived on May 22. Many were dead on arrival (the exact count is unclear in the OCR). Fourteen survivors grew to about six inches.
- **Comanche Springs pupfish:** These are reported to be doing well, and the station is working to conserve the species.
- **Other stocking:** About 2,000 Gila topminnows were stocked in east-central Arizona (Table 4).

### Fish health, preserved specimens and outreach

- **Health:** All other fish were healthy, and no problems occurred with fish scheduled for distribution in 1985. The health program was assisted by Ron Major.
- **Preserved specimens:** Requests came from the Arizona Game and Fish Department, New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, Arizona State University, the University of Utah and the University of Southern Colorado.
- **Publications:** Three papers by Hamman (on razorback sucker, bonytail and Colorado squawfish) were in press with the *Progressive Fish Culturist*. Jensen presented a paper at an AFS meeting in Missouri, and Jensen served as president of the Arizona-New Mexico Chapter of the American Fisheries Society in 1985.
- **Cooperation:** Dr. W. L. Minckley worked at Dexter for 14 months under an IPA agreement with Arizona State University. Staff supported the Arizona Game and Fish Department with a razorback sucker post-stocking dispersal study and monitoring on Bonito and Eagle creeks. Staff also served on the [[Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team]].
- **Visitors:** About 2,000 people toured the station in 1985. Dexter also filmed footage for educational programs.

### Facilities, energy and operations

- **Energy audit:** EMC Engineers of Denver completed an energy audit. Most systems were found to be in good condition, but some energy retrofit projects will require funding.
- **Standby power:** A 60 kW standby generator was installed in early spring. It starts automatically when regular power fails, but it cannot detect problems beyond the switching box.
- **Improvements:** A paving project improved the station entrance and half of the parking lot. The Well #3 pump and motor were replaced, and the station's wells are now on an eight-year replacement schedule.
- **Youth programs:** The YCC crew carried out landscaping, brush clearing, painting and other maintenance tasks.
