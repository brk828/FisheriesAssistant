# Speocirolana thermydronis, A New Species of Cirolanid Isopod from Central Coahuila, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cirolanidae

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *Speocirolana thermydronis* n. sp., a cirolanid isopod crustacean known from a single female holotype (USNM catalog 113054) collected on 12 April 1964 at [[Pozos de la Becerra]], a warm spring in the [[Cuatro Cienegas Basin]], Coahuila, Mexico. The species is distinguished from its congeners [[Speocirolana Pelaezi]] and [[Speocirolana Bolivari]] by head, antennal, mandibular, maxillipedal, pleopodal and uropodal characters. The authors argue that the genus may be of pre-Tertiary origin, since the species lives high in the Sierra Madre Oriental, and that the basin harbors an ancient, highly endemic fauna persisting in spring habitats. The authors also report that the type locality was severely modified by canal construction in 1964, which drained marshes and reduced water and marsh area from perhaps 10 km² to less than 0.2 km².

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic placement:** Assigned to *Speocirolana* Bolívar (1950), originally a subgenus of *Cirolana* Leach and raised to generic rank by Bowman (1964). The defining generic character is that pereopods 1–3 are prehensile and pereopods 4–7 are ambulatory. Among North American troglobitic cirolanids, this feature is otherwise known only in the poorly known [[Conilera Stygia]] from near Monterrey, Nuevo León.
- **Type material:** One female, 15 mm long (head tip to telson end), collected by Mary L. Allesio of the University of Colorado and referred for study by Clarence J. McCoy.
- **Etymology:** From Greek *thermydron*, "warm spring," alluding to its thermal-water habitat.
- **Diagnostic characters:** Clypeus with rounded posterior ends and no deep anterior notch; first antennal flagellum of 14 articles (peduncle/flagellum ratio about 0.9); second antenna with 35 flagellar articles reaching the seventh pereonite when deflected; lacinia of maxilliped paddle-shaped with 12 plumose setae; all pleopodal endopods lacking terminal setae; uropodal endopod inner surfaces spineless; telson broad, shield-like, ending in an acute point.
- **Comparative morphology:** All three species are eyeless and unpigmented. Body length to greatest width is 3.5 in *S. thermydronis* versus about 2.7 in the other two. The second antenna has 35 flagellar articles, versus 48–52 in *S. bolivari* and 30 in *S. pelaezi*. The first antenna has 14 flagellar articles, versus about 20 in *S. pelaezi* and 22–28 in *S. bolivari*.
- **Note on generic characters:** The authors contend that Bowman (1964) erred in implying that setose first and second pleopodal endopods characterize *Speocirolana*, since all endopods of the new species lack distal setae. The character requires further study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Type locality (before 1964):** Pozos de la Becerra was an elongate, irregular laguna, up to more than 10 m deep at spring inflows, about 25 to more than 150 m wide, and perhaps 2 km long. Water was extremely clear. Source temperatures ranged from 29.4 to 32.2 °C across seven measurements. Discharge at the outlet was estimated at about 1.34 m³/s. Bottoms were gravel and rubble at inflows and deep calcareous silt elsewhere, with dense [[Waterlily]] (*Nymphaea*) beds.
- **Anthropogenic disruption:** A bathing facility (1961) caused minor modification. In 1964 a canal drained the water level by 46 cm in about two days. By April 1965 the outlet had downcut and lowered the surface more than a meter. Swimmers muddied the water, silting gravel bottoms and drying or uprooting most *Nymphaea* beds.
- **Biogeographic context:** Troglobitic cirolanids of the Western Hemisphere (except [[Antrolana Lira]] from Virginia) occur in an arc around the Gulf of Mexico. The congeners and *Conilera stygia* lie in a compact triangle in northeastern Mexico, within the area inundated by the mid-Cretaceous Mexican Geosyncline. Cirolanid isopods in freshwater caves are thought to derive from marine forms relicted by marine regressions.
- **Endemism:** The basin holds endemic vertebrates and an unusual, undescribed molluscan fauna showing few relationships to other living Mexican forms, suggesting great habitat age.
- **Fish and dispersal:** The authors note that epigean fishes are unlikely to clarify the isopod's origin. However, the endemic platyfish *Xiphophorus gordoni* shares characters with *X. variatus xiphidium* of the Río Soto la Marina system, and [[Gambusia marshi]] in the Río Salado (the basin's drainage) is related to Río Soto la Marina–Río Pánuco forms. These patterns suggest overland dispersal via stream captures.
- **Other cavernicolous fauna:** A blind, depigmented catfish, *Prietella phreatophila*, occurs at Muzquiz, north of Cuatro Cienegas.

## Key Findings

- *Speocirolana thermydronis* is described from a single female specimen, the sole known individual despite repeated searches since 1958 and three expeditions after 1964.
- The species represents a new record for the genus in a high-elevation thermal spring of the Sierra Madre Oriental, which the authors interpret as evidence for a possible pre-Tertiary origin of *Speocirolana*.
- The type locality was physically altered in 1964–1965, with the surface area of water and marsh reduced from perhaps 10 km² to under 0.2 km², indicating high risk of extinction for the population.
- Generic characters previously attributed to *Speocirolana* (pleopodal endopod setation) are questioned and require further study.
- The authors call for additional exploration of the Cuatro Cienegas basin and the Edwards Plateau of Texas, which show high cavernicolous diversity, and defer speculation about the species' intrageneric relationships until more material is collected.
