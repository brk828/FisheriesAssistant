## Executive Summary

This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum from the Parker Fish and Wildlife Resource Office (FRO), dated 15 January 1994, rather than a primary scientific study. It summarizes a meeting of [[Lower Colorado River]] National Wildlife Refuge (NWR) managers regarding a proposal from the [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]] to install artificial habitat structures (plastic structures) and brush piles into refuge waters. The refuge managers—representing [[Bill Williams River NWR]], [[Havasu NWR]], [[Cibola NWR]], and [[Imperial NWR]]—collectively decided **not** to permit such structures, citing a management mandate focused on native species conservation rather than enhancement of sportsfishing habitat. The memo also flags a broader institutional shift within the USFWS toward native species management and away from sportfish introduction, and recommends the Service formalize a standardized policy on artificial habitat structures.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type discussed:** Lower Colorado River refuge waters, including reservoir habitat associated with [[Lake Havasu]].
- **Proposed habitat modification:** Introduction of artificial structures (plastic habitat units) and brush piles intended to enhance fish habitat complexity, primarily to benefit recreational sportsfish populations.
- **Management position:** Refuge managers determined that artificial structure placement was incompatible with the refuges' core mission of managing for **native species** rather than non-native sportfish enhancement.
- **Alternative compatible action:** Refuges instead contribute to the Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program via **native fish growout facilities**, viewed as more aligned with refuge conservation goals.
- **Broader ecological context:** The memo references an ongoing agency-wide reevaluation of **sportsfish introduction policy** in the [[Lower Colorado River]] system, reflecting a shift in USFWS management philosophy toward prioritizing native aquatic species over introduced game fish.

## Key Findings

- On 11 January 1994, lower Colorado River refuge managers (Bill Williams River NWR, Havasu NWR, Cibola NWR; Imperial NWR absent) met to discuss placement of artificial habitat structures and brush piles proposed under the [[Lake Havasu Fisheries Improvement Program]].
- **Consensus decision:** Artificial structures and brush piles would **not** be permitted in lower Colorado River refuge waters, as this was judged inconsistent with the refuges' native species management mandate.
- The geographic scope of refuge waters affected by this restriction is small, and the decision is expected to have minimal impact on the overall Fisheries Improvement Program.
- Refuges will continue to support the program indirectly through **native fish growout facilities**, a role considered more compatible with refuge conservation objectives.
- The memo notes a parallel, ongoing Service-wide discussion about restricting continued **sportsfish introductions**, indicating an institutional trend toward native species prioritization.
- The author recommends that the Service develop a **standardized policy** regarding artificial habitat structures and their potential ecological impacts on native species, to ensure consistency across refuges and programs.
