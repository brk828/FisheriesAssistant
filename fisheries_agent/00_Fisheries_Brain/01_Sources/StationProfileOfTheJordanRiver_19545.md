## Executive Summary

This document is a **station profile** for the [[Jordan River National Fish Hatchery]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1963 under the authority of the March 1934 Coordination Act (48 Statute 401). The hatchery is located in Elmira, Michigan, and operates within the 1st Congressional District. Its core mission is production-oriented rather than observational field research: the facility is a central component of a high-priority **international program to restore self-sustaining, naturally spawning populations of [[Lake Trout]] (*Salvelinus namaycush*)** throughout the [[Great Lakes]]. This restoration effort is explicitly linked to a parallel, larger binational program targeting the **elimination or suppression of parasitic [[Sea Lamprey]] (*Petromyzon marinus*)** populations, a key limiting factor in native salmonid recovery. In FY1993, the hatchery produced and transferred approximately 974,900 two-inch lake trout fingerlings to the Hiawatha Forest and Pendills Creek National Fish Hatcheries in Michigan, and hosted roughly 2,276 visitors.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native habitat**: Lake trout are cold-water salmonids native to deep, oligotrophic lakes of the [[Great Lakes]] basin and other northern freshwater systems in North America.
- **Restoration context**: The document frames the species' ecological role as a keystone native predator whose populations were historically decimated, necessitating artificial propagation to rebuild self-sustaining wild spawning stocks.
- **Limiting factor — Sea Lamprey**: The parasitic [[Sea Lamprey]] is identified as a major ecological threat suppressing lake trout recovery through predation/parasitism, requiring coordinated control efforts alongside hatchery stocking.
- **Facility-based rearing environment**: The hatchery itself functions as an artificial ecological niche substitute, using:
  - Two spring impoundments with degasser towers (water quality/gas management)
  - 48 outside raceways (fingerling rearing)
  - An effluent settling basin (wastewater/environmental management)
  - A metal rearing building and hatchery/office building for early life-stage propagation

## Key Findings

- **Institutional mandate**: The Jordan River National Fish Hatchery (established 1963) exists specifically to support Great Lakes-wide [[Lake Trout]] restoration, operating under a binational (international) fisheries management framework.
- **Production output (FY1993)**: 974,900 two-inch fingerling lake trout were successfully reared and transferred to two sister hatcheries (Hiawatha Forest NFH and Pendills Creek NFH), indicating substantial inter-facility stocking logistics within the Michigan hatchery network.
- **Integrated pest management linkage**: Lake trout restoration is explicitly coupled with [[Sea Lamprey]] control programs, reflecting an ecosystem-level management approach rather than stocking in isolation.
- **Infrastructure scale**: The facility maintains substantial physical infrastructure (48 raceways, dual spring impoundments, degasser towers, multiple storage buildings, five staff residences), indicating a mid-to-large scale federal hatchery operation.
