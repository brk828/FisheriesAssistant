# Temperature Preference and Avoidance by Adult Razorback Suckers

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1983 study by Bulkley and Pimentel investigated the thermal preference and avoidance behavior of adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered endemic species of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Using an electronic shuttlebox apparatus, researchers determined the **final thermal preferendum** to lie between **22.9 and 24.8 °C**, with upper avoidance temperatures ranging from **27.4–31.6 °C** and lower avoidance temperatures ranging from **8.0–14.7 °C**. The findings provide critical design criteria for water-control structures (dams, reservoirs) on the upper Colorado River to preserve suitable thermal habitat for this species, recommending summer water temperatures be maintained between 22 and 25 °C.

## Background & Description

- **Species**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — a rare, endemic catostomid fish of the Colorado River basin, formally recognized as endangered.
- **Context of decline**: Population declines linked to impoundments, water withdrawals, and effluent returns altering natural river thermal regimes, particularly downstream of large dams (e.g., [[Flaming Gorge Dam]]).
- **Study population**: Experimental fish were offspring of a single female spawned in 1974 at the Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery (Arizona), reared in outdoor concrete raceways on commercial trout feed. Seven hundred fish (15–30 cm) were transferred to the Utah Water Research Laboratory (Logan, Utah) in June 1980; fish were sexually mature, in their seventh year of life at testing.
- **Acclimation protocol**: Fish held in 833-L circular tanks (14 h light:10 h dark photoperiod) and acclimated to four temperatures: **8, 14, 20, or 26 °C** (temperature changed 1 °C/day). Acclimation held for ≥30 days (cooling) or ≥1 week (warming) prior to testing.
- **Water quality**: Dechlorinated, flow-through water; dissolved oxygen 90–100% saturation; ammonia <0.2 mg/L; pH ~8.0.
- **Disease control**: Terramycin used to treat *Flexibacter columnaris* infections; affected fish excluded from testing for 21 days post-treatment.

### Methodology: Electronic Shuttlebox

- Apparatus based on design by Neill et al. (1972), modified by McCauley et al. (1977) to use heated/chilled inflow water rather than submerged heaters.
- 45-L plexiglass two-compartment chamber connected via narrow passage; fish movement between compartments triggered photocell-controlled flows of warm/cold water (0.7–0.8 L/min), altering local temperature at a max rate of 0.2 °C/min (range 4–41 °C).
- Fish tested individually for 48 hours: first 24 h used to estimate **avoidance temperatures** (extreme temps experienced); second 24 h used to estimate **final preferendum** (modal occupied temperature).
- Statistical comparisons: ANOVA and Fisher's LSD test for avoidance/preference differences among acclimation groups; Chi-square analysis for temporal patterns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historical case study — Flaming Gorge Dam**: After dam construction (1963) on the [[Green River]] (Utah), mean summer water temperature below the dam dropped from ~18 °C to 6.8 °C, with annual post-impoundment temperatures ranging 1.7–10 °C — well below the lower avoidance threshold (8.0–14.7 °C) established in this study.
- Razorback suckers disappeared from a 105 km stretch of the Green River below the dam down to the confluence with the warmer [[Yampa River]], where 1964 mean summer temperatures (16.9 °C; range 15.0–18.9 °C) remained within tolerable ranges and fish persisted.
- This supports the hypothesis that **thermal alteration from dam tailwater releases** contributed to the disappearance of razorback suckers and three other native endangered fishes from affected river reaches.
- **Comparative species** (same research group, Bulkley et al. 1981) — final preferenda for other Colorado River endemics:
  - [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): 25.4 °C
  - [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*): 24.0 °C
  - [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*): 24.2 °C
  - These values closely align with the razorback sucker's 24.8 °C geometric-mean estimate, suggesting shared thermal ecology among co-occurring endangered Colorado River fishes.
- **Taxonomic comparison**: [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*) final preferendum reported at 24.1 °C (Reynolds & Casterlin 1978), falling within the razorback sucker's 94% confidence limits — supporting Mathur et al. (1981)'s findings of taxonomic/geographic similarity in thermal preferences among freshwater fishes.

## Key Findings

### Final Thermal Preferendum
- Estimated at **22.9–24.8 °C** (two calculation methods):
  - Mean modal temperature from 20 °C and 26 °C acclimation groups: **22.9 °C** (equality-of-acclimation-and-preference criterion).
  - Geometric mean across all four acclimation groups: **24.8 °C**.
- No significant diel (day/night) variation in temperature selection was detected.
- Fish acclimated to 14 °C preferred a significantly **higher** final temperature than those acclimated to 20 °C or 26 °C — attributed to a likely **acclimation overshoot** effect in cold-acclimated fish (consistent with patterns reported by Beitinger & Magnuson 1976; Reynolds 1978).

### Avoidance Temperatures

| Acclimation Temp (°C) | N  | Mean Upper Avoidance (°C) | Mean Lower Avoidance (°C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8  | 4  | 27.4 a | 8.0 a |
| 14 | 10 | 31.6 b | 12.2 ab |
| 20 | 20 | 27.4 a | 12.4 ab |
| 26 | 20 | 28.2 a | 14.7 b |

*(Values sharing a letter within a column are not significantly different, LSD test, P<0.05)*

- **Upper avoidance temperature**: ranged 27.4–31.6 °C, averaged **28.6 °C** overall. Fish acclimated to 14 °C showed significantly higher upper avoidance than other groups (P<0.05).
- **Lower avoidance temperature**: ranged 8.0–14.7 °C, averaged **11.8 °C**. Positively correlated with acclimation temperature (P<0.01, r=0.39, df=52) — i.e., fish acclimated to colder water tolerated (avoided at) colder extremes.
- Upper avoidance temperature was **not** significantly correlated with acclimation temperature.
- Study's avoidance estimates were more liberal (wider range) than traditional "turnaround temperature" methods (Neill & Magnuson 1974), intentionally providing conservative/inclusive design criteria.

### Behavioral & Physiological Observations

- **Occupation temperature variability** declined as acclimation temperature approached the final preferendum:
  - 8 °C acclimation: coefficient of variation = 16.7%
  - 26 °C acclimation: coefficient of variation = 7.6%
  - Suggests fish acclimated closer to their preferendum settle into preferred temperatures more quickly/precisely.
- **Cold acclimation mortality risk**: Fish acclimated to 8 °C and 14 °C exhibited **Low Thermal Responsiveness (LTR)** — sluggish shuttling behavior:
  - At 8 °C acclimation: of 19 fish tested, 10 died (raised chamber temp too rapidly), 5 showed no movement/preference, only 4 successfully regulated temperature.
  - At 14 °C acclimation: of 21 fish, 6 died or escaped, 5 showed no preference, 10 successfully operated the shuttlebox.
  - Mean maximum lethal temperature reached by fish that died: 35 °C (SD=3.7, range 30–41 °C).
  - **No mortality** occurred in 20 °C or 26 °C acclimation groups (20/20 fish successfully operated device in each).
  - This pattern parallels findings in [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) by Beitinger & Magnuson (1976).

### Comparison to Coutant's (1975) Generality
- Coutant (1975) found upper avoidance temperatures typically ~2 °C above final preferendum across fish species generally; confirmed for white sucker (2.6 °C difference; Reynolds & Casterlin 1978).
- Razorback sucker upper avoidance averaged **3.7 °C above** final preferendum (range 2.4–5.3 °C) — a larger gap attributed to methodological differences (avoidance estimated from Day 1 rather than Day 2 of testing, capturing wider extremes before fish converged on preferendum).

## Conclusions / Management Implications

- Recommended summer water temperature range for new water-control structures in the upper Colorado River basin: **22–25 °C**, to preserve viable habitat for adult [[Razorback Sucker]].
- Temperatures above ~29 °C and below ~12 °C during summer are likely to be avoided by adult razorback suckers when alternative temperatures are available.
- Laboratory-derived thermal preference data are considered reliably extrapolatable to wild populations, based on precedent from other shuttlebox studies (Neill & Magnuson 1974; Stauffer et al. 1975; Magnuson et al. 1979).
- Findings support broader conservation concerns regarding hypolimnetic dam releases and their disruption of native thermal habitat for endangered Colorado River Basin fishes, including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]].
