# Evaluation of Instream Flow Methodologies for Fish in Nebraska

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This Nebraska Game and Parks Commission technical report (Nebraska Technical Series No. 10, 1982, by Phil Hilgert, funded by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service) tests five [[Instream Flow Methods]] on twelve Nebraska streams to determine how much water must remain in channels to protect fisheries. The methods are the [[Tennant Method]], a [[Modified Tennant Method]], the [[Single Cross-Section Method]] (IFG1A), the [[Incremental Method]] using [[WSP Hydraulic Model]] with HABTAT, and the Incremental Method using [[IFG4 Hydraulic Model]] with HABTAT. The authors conclude that no single method suits every stream: the Modified Tennant method is the most useful for reconnaissance, while the Incremental method is the most informative for resolving conflicts, though it requires greater time and funding.

## Background & Description

The report addresses rising competition for water in Nebraska, where instream values (fish, wildlife, recreation, navigation, aesthetics) are often unrecognized relative to withdrawal uses. Its stated aim is to evaluate methods for quantifying minimum flows for fish and wildlife, and to recommend monthly flow regimes.

Study streams were selected from a ranked list of 12 reaches representing a range of stream-form classes, and were chosen using the representative-reach concept of [[Bovee and Milhous]] (1978). Field data were collected at high, medium and low flows, and each study site was described using multiple transects. The focal target species were [[Rainbow Trout]] (Nine Mile Creek, Long Pine Creek, Snake River), [[Brown Trout]], and [[Channel Catfish]] (sand-bed and mud-bed rivers). Habitat suitability curves for channel catfish were judged insufficient and modified for Nebraska sand-bed streams (Appendix A).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Nine Mile Creek** (Scotts Bluff County): a small coldwater tributary to the [[North Platte River]] and a spawning and nursery stream for McConaughy rainbow trout, which migrate from [[Lake McConaughy]]. Irrigation canal "silt runs" and return flows add turbidity; heavy grazing, channelization and feedlot runoff also degrade the fishery. Nine Mile Creek produces 42% of the rainbow trout caught by North Platte Valley stream anglers.
- **Snake River** (Cherry County): below [[Merritt Reservoir]], a reach with brown trout fishing and a state-record brown trout of 20.1 pounds (9.1 kg). Water temperatures ranged from 55°F (12.8°C) to about 75°F (23.9°C) in summer.
- **Republican River**: flows are controlled by the Superior-Courtland Diversion and the Harlan County Dam. Summer low flows are a serious detriment to the fishery.
- **Cedar River**: a sandhills stream fed by groundwater, with rare channel catfish and a fishery in [[Lake Ericson]] partially renovated in 1967.
- **Long Pine Creek**: a stocked and naturally reproducing brown and rainbow trout stream.

The report notes the Sand Hills region covers roughly two-fifths of Nebraska and produces little overland runoff, so its streams depend on groundwater and show very uniform discharge.

## Key Findings

- **Tennant method limits:** It is quick, cheap and requires little expertise, but it produces coarse recommendations, often flows not normally available, and cannot distinguish between fish communities or handle regulated streams.
- **Modified Tennant method:** Uses median monthly flow as the base. It generally yields higher October–March and lower April–September recommendations than Tennant. It is recommended as the most useful interim tool for Nebraska reconnaissance.
- **Single Cross-Section method:** Derived a minimum flow satisfying a biological criterion (25% of wetted width with depth above 0.4 to 0.6 ft). It is not well suited to Nebraska because species-specific criteria are lacking and sand-bed channels are unstable.
- **Incremental method:** Predicts weighted usable area (WUA) for each life stage across flows. The IFG4 model requires three measurements and works only on stable beds; the WSP model needs one set of measurements and works on unstable channels. It is the most useful for resolving conflicts but needs stream-specific fish distribution and abundance data.
- **Nine Mile Creek:** IFG4/HABTAT output showed high flows harmful to all rainbow trout life stages; WSP/HABTAT peaked at lower flows and gave lower recommendations. Minimum flows satisfying depth criteria (25% of width >0.5 ft spawning, >0.6 ft passage) were tabulated for the upper, middle and lower sites.
- **Snake River below Merritt Dam:** Tennant and Modified Tennant recommendations required three to four times present average flows, which is unlikely given irrigation demand. Brown trout fry WUA was about five times greater than rainbow fry WUA, and the present flow regime favors fall-spawning brown trout over spring-spawning rainbow trout.
- **Merritt Reservoir operations (Appendix B):** Proposed changes included a minimum 70 cfs release during spring filling (instead of 15 cfs), which would raise brown trout WUA by about 300%; evenly distributed mid-summer releases; and slower fall filling. Releasing the August 1978 high flows (134 cfs) at a constant 50 cfs was projected to increase brown trout WUA by 210%.
- **Long Pine Creek:** Upper site WUA was low because of sand substrate; substituting gravel raised it roughly threefold. The Single Cross-Section recommendation at the middle and lower sites was based on rainbow trout criteria from Nine Mile Creek.
- **Fish diversity:** The 1973 statewide inventory recorded 26 species in the Elkhorn River, including the bluntnose minnow, believed extinct in the state. Its tables list 47 species across study streams in total; the complete checklists are summarized here, not reproduced.
- **Overall recommendation:** Regardless of method, flow recommendations should be given as a monthly regime, including periodic high flushing flows that maintain channel form. Stream-specific data on fish populations are needed before the Incremental method can be fully used.
