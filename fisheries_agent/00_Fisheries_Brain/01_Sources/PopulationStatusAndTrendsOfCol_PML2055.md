# Population Status and Trends of Colorado Pikeminnow in the Upper Colorado River, 1991-2005

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service final report (Osmundson and White, 2009) synthesizes three multi-year mark-recapture efforts (1991-1994, 1998-2000, 2003-2005) to assess the status of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) in the mainstem [[Upper Colorado River]], the second-largest extant population after the [[Green River]] sub-basin. Abundance and survival were estimated with the [[Huggins Estimator]] in the [[Robust Design Multi-State Model]] (Program MARK). The combined-reach population of fish >450 mm TL rose to 889 in 2005, from roughly 200 in 1991, and a significant positive trend was detected. Survival of fish >500 mm TL appeared to decline across periods, but not significantly. The report recommends continued mark-recapture monitoring, no further hatchery stocking upstream, and several research and management actions.

## Background & Description

The Colorado pikeminnow was once widespread across warmwater reaches of the Colorado River Basin, from Wyoming to the Gulf of California. It is now restricted to upper basin reaches upstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]] and is federally listed as endangered (USFWS 2000). The study area covered 287 km of mainstem river, split into:

- **Lower reach:** rk 0-181, from the [[Green River]] confluence in Canyonlands National Park, Utah, upstream to Cottonwood Wash.
- **Upper reach:** rk 201-303, from Westwater Wash to the [[Price-Stubb Diversion Dam]] at Palisade, Colorado, including the lowermost 3.5 km of the [[Gunnison River]] below the [[Redlands Diversion Dam]].
- **Westwater Canyon** (20 km) was excluded because of logistic difficulty and low expected fish occurrence.

Fish were captured mainly by [[Backwater Trammel Netting]] supplemented with [[Electrofishing]], tagged with [[PIT Tags]], measured for total length, and released. Methods were refined in 2003-2005 to increase passes per year (four to five) and to equalize passes between reaches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Spawning and habitat use:** Subadults and adults congregate in low-velocity backwaters during spring runoff, which shapes the sampling window (mid-April to mid-June).
- **Between-reach movement:** Most movement was upstream (lower to upper reach). Movement from the lower reach was 0-30% per interval, depending on year, while upper-to-lower movement was rare.
- **Inter-system exchange:** Fish moved between the Colorado and [[Green River Sub-basin]] systems at low frequency, and the two groups appear demographically largely independent.
- **Carrying capacity:** An earlier hypothesis that the population was near carrying capacity (about 700 adults) was tested against body condition and abundance data.
- **Hydrology and temperature:** Growth was linked to main-channel [[Annual Thermal Units]] (ATU), with a mean of 92.9 ATU over 20 years, and the report discusses flow-sediment-biota relations.
- **Regulation context:** The [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] funded the work, and [[Recovery Goals]] (USFWS 2002) set downlisting criteria based on adult abundance and self-sustainability.

## Key Findings

1. **Abundance increased.** Combined-reach point estimates for fish >450 mm TL in 2005 were 412 in the lower reach (2.3 fish/km) and 477 in the upper reach (4.5 fish/km), for a river-wide total of 889 (95% CI 746-1,075). The trend slope for fish >450 mm was +12.26 fish/year (SE 4.12; Wald chi-square 8.8, P = 0.003). For fish >500 mm, slope was +10.29/year (P = 0.002).
2. **Estimator difference.** Huggins estimates were about 14% higher on average than CAPTURE estimates (Models Mh and Mt), because length was included as a covariate of capture probability.
3. **Capture probability.** Probability varied among years and was generally lowest in the most recent three-year period. Lower-reach fish had higher capture probabilities than upper-reach fish. Fish near 750 mm TL were 3-4 times more catchable than 250 mm fish.
4. **Survival.** Upper-reach fish had significantly higher survival than lower-reach fish. Survival peaked at about 450-650 mm TL (about 80% in the lower reach and 89% in the upper reach). Very large fish (>850 mm) had lower survival. Survival for fish >500 mm TL was 88.2% (1991-1994), 85.9% (1998-2000), and 80.4% (2003-2005); differences were not significant.
5. **Precision and power.** Mean CVs were 24%, 14%, and 15% across the three efforts. With a CV of about 15%, a decline of roughly 30% between two years would be required for significance at α = 0.05. With the 2005 CV of 9.4%, a decline of about 20% would be needed.
6. **Recruitment exceeded adult mortality.** Recruitment (fish 400-449 mm) exceeded adult mortality in six of nine years, with a net gain of 332 fish >450 mm. Gaps in sampling precluded a full-period total, and precision for this size class was low.
7. **Electrofishing catch rates did not track abundance.** ISMP catch rates (1986-2005) showed no overall trend. When the low 2003-2005 rates were removed, an upward trend appeared. The authors attribute the mismatch to variable capture probability and changed protocols and gear (WP vs. GPP units).
8. **Year-class strength.** Of 14 years (1985-2000), the authors identified seven very weak and two very strong year-classes (1986 and 1998). A strong 1996 fall YOY cohort did not produce a strong age-5 cohort, which questions whether YOY abundance predicts later recruitment.
9. **Body condition.** Relative condition (Kn) of lower-reach fish declined between 1991-1994 and 1998-2000, then recovered by 2003-2005. Kn of 500-599 mm fish showed no relationship with upper-reach abundance, so the carrying-capacity hypothesis was not supported.
10. **Inter-system movement.** Of 2,117 PIT-tagged fish with multiple captures, 33 inter-system movements (1.42%) were documented from 1990 to 2005. Movements were roughly balanced between systems, and the authors describe a possible [[Metapopulation]] structure.
11. **Stocking.** Hatchery-reared fish stocked in 2003-2004 dispersed downstream. A mark-recapture estimate of 190-192 stocked fish (about 3.7% of the 5,084 stocked) was found in 2005. None was found in the upstream stocking reaches, so the authors recommend no further upstream stocking.
12. **Recommendations.** Continue mark-recapture monitoring (three sampling years followed by two non-sampling years, aiming for four passes per year); build boat launches between Government Highline and Price-Stubb dams; evaluate carrying capacity; assess the Grand Valley and Gunnison assessment options; monitor larval production; and develop methods to assign year-of-origin for 300-400 mm fish.
