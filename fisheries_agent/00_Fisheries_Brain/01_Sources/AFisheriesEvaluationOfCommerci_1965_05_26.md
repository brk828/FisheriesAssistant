## Executive Summary

This 1965 Job Completion Report, filed under the Texas Federal Aid in Fisheries Restoration Act (Project F-3-R-12, Job B-21), evaluated the impact of commercial [[Aquatic Moss Harvesting]] on fish populations in [[Caddo Lake]], Texas. The stated objective was to determine fish availability in lake areas cleared of submerged aquatic vegetation via commercial harvest. During the reporting period (February 1964–January 1965), no commercial harvesting activity occurred, so no new fieldwork was conducted. Data from a prior 1963 survey period (nineteen netting collections comparing cut versus uncut vegetation areas) showed little variation in fish populations between the two habitat types. The report recommends terminating this job until large-scale commercial moss harvesting resumes and warrants renewed study.

## Background & Description

- **Project Framework:** Conducted under Texas Federal Aid Project F-3-R-12, "Fisheries Investigations and Surveys of the Waters of Region 3-B," Job No. B-21 (Segment 2).
- **Location:** [[Caddo Lake]], spanning Harrison and Marion Counties, Texas — a large natural lake known for extensive [[Submerged Aquatic Vegetation]] cover.
- **Reporting Period:** February 1, 1964 – January 31, 1965.
- **Project Leader:** Joe E. Toole, with oversight from the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department (Executive Director J. Weldon Watson; Assistant Director for Wildlife Eugene A. Walker; D-J Coordinator Marion Toole; Regional Supervisor Charles E. Gray).
- **Study Design (from prior 1963 segment):** Nineteen netting collections were performed in paired cut (harvested) and uncut (unharvested) areas of the lake to compare fish population metrics associated with the presence or removal of [[Aquatic Vegetation]] via commercial moss harvesting operations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Type:** Caddo Lake is characterized by dense stands of submerged and floating aquatic moss/vegetation, which provide structural habitat, cover, and potential foraging grounds for fish communities.
- **Disturbance Factor:** Commercial moss harvesting mechanically removes submerged vegetation, altering habitat structure in localized "cut" zones compared to adjacent "uncut" reference zones.
- **Ecological Question:** The study aimed to assess whether vegetation removal significantly affects fish distribution, abundance, or availability — a key consideration for balancing commercial aquatic plant harvesting with fisheries conservation in vegetated lake systems.
- **Observed Pattern:** Comparative netting data indicated minimal ecological difference in fish populations between vegetated (uncut) and cleared (cut) areas, suggesting either rapid fish recolonization, limited harvesting scale/impact, or a lack of strong dependence on the specific moss habitat structure at the intensity tested.

## Key Findings

- **No active commercial harvesting** occurred during the 1964–1965 reporting segment, precluding new data collection.
- **Prior 1963 data (19 netting collections):** Comparison of cut vs. uncut vegetation areas showed **little variation in fish populations**, indicating no strong short-term negative impact of localized moss harvesting on fish availability at the scale studied.
- **Management Recommendation:** The job was recommended for **termination** unless and until large-scale commercial moss harvesting resumes on Caddo Lake, at which point renewed fisheries study would be warranted.
- **Broader Implication:** This finding suggests that small-to-moderate scale [[Vegetation Removal]] for commercial purposes may not necessitate intensive fisheries mitigation, though the report explicitly notes this conclusion applies only to the scale of harvesting activity observed and not to potential large-scale operations.
