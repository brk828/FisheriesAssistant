# The Fossil Freshwater Fishes of Mexico

## Executive Summary
This 1978 compilation by Michael L. Smith catalogs fossil freshwater fish assemblages recovered from stratified sedimentary deposits across central and northern Mexico, spanning from the Miocene/Early Pliocene through the Late Pleistocene (and in some cases Recent) periods. The record is geographically anchored in the Lake Chapala basin ([[Jalisco]]), with additional sites in [[Puebla]] (Valsequillo Formation), [[Sonora]] (Rancho La Brisca), [[Chihuahua]] (Yépomera), and the Basin of Mexico ([[Lago de Texcoco]]). The fossil fauna is dominated by families still endemic or characteristic to Mexican freshwaters today: [[Goodeidae]], [[Cyprinidae]], [[Ictaluridae]], [[Atherinidae]] (silversides, genus *Chirostoma*), [[Catostomidae]], and [[Centrarchidae]]. Notably, a species of [[Salmonidae]] (*Salmo*, related to *Salmo clarki*/cutthroat trout lineage) is documented from high-elevation Jalisco deposits, indicating a cooler paleoclimate and past connectivity or independent colonization events for trout in the region. The data underscore the long evolutionary residency and diversification of the endemic Chapala Basin ichthyofauna, particularly the goodeids and silversides (*Chirostoma*).

## Background & Description
Because this source is a stratigraphic/taxonomic locality list rather than a morphological description, direct anatomical data are limited. However, key systematic and morphological notes can be extracted:

- **[[Tapatia occidentalis]]**: A goodeid taxon described from the Santa Rosa locality (Jalisco), representing one of the oldest fossil goodeids known, indicating this livebearer family was already differentiated by the Early Pliocene/Miocene.
- **[[Chapalichthys encaustus]]**: An extinct or fossil goodeid species recorded across multiple Chapala-basin sites (Chapala Formation, Jocotepec Quarry), taxonomically distinct from congeners in the modern Lake Chapala.
- **[[Ictalurus dugesi]]**: A fossil catfish (Ictaluridae) recurring across nearly all Jalisco Pleistocene sites, suggesting a persistent, morphologically stable lineage through the Plio-Pleistocene into the Late Pleistocene.
- **Synonymies noted in the record** reflect taxonomic revision over time:
  - *Algansea rubescens* = *A. tincella*
  - *Xystrosus popoche* = *Algansea popoche*
  - *Falcularius chapala* = *Yuriria alta*
  - *Otalia promelas* = *Chirostoma promelas*
  
  These synonymies indicate that several genera erected in early 20th-century Mexican ichthyology (*Xystrosus*, *Falcularius*, *Otalia*) have since been subsumed into modern genera ([[Algansea]], [[Yuriria]], [[Chirostoma]]), reflecting ongoing taxonomic consolidation of the endemic Mexican freshwater fish fauna.
- **[[Micropterus relictus]]**: An extinct/relict centrarchid (bass) recorded from Chapala Formation deposits, notable because native centrarchids are rare in Mexican freshwater fossil records, implying either a relict native lineage or early basin colonization distinct from later human-introduced *Micropterus* species.
- **[[Salmo clarki]]** (cutthroat trout) and an undescribed *Salmo* n. sp. (informally "*australis*") from Laguna San Marcos and Santa Rosa, respectively — morphologically allied to extant cutthroat trout lineages, indicating cold-adapted salmonids penetrated as far south as Jalisco during Pleistocene glacial/pluvial intervals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Paleolacustrine systems**: The majority of fossil assemblages derive from paleo-lake deposits in the Jalisco highlands (Lake Chapala, Lake Zacoalco, Jocotepec Quarry, Atotonilco El Bajo, Laguna San Marcos), indicating a mosaic of interconnected or successive lake basins persisting from the Pliocene through the Recent.
- **Trophic/habitat diversity represented by family composition**:
  - [[Catostomidae]] (*Moxostoma* spp.) — benthic sucker-feeders indicating soft-bottom lacustrine or fluvial habitat.
  - [[Cyprinidae]] (*Algansea*, *Yuriria*, *Evarra*, *Notropis*) — generalist minnows occupying open water and littoral zones.
  - [[Ictaluridae]] (*Ictalurus dugesi*) — benthic/nocturnal catfish niche.
  - [[Goodeidae]] (*Tapatia*, *Chapalichthys*, *Xenotoca*) — livebearing endemics typically associated with shallow, vegetated littoral habitats, a lineage highly diversified and endemic to the Mexican Plateau.
  - [[Atherinidae]] (*Chirostoma* spp., extremely diverse in the Late Pleistocene Lake Chapala/Zacoalco assemblage, with at least 9 named species) — pelagic zooplanktivorous silversides, indicating a productive, open-water lacustrine environment supporting significant niche partitioning/speciation within a single lake system.
  - [[Centrarchidae]] (*Micropterus relictus*) — piscivorous/predatory niche.
  - [[Salmonidae]] (*Salmo* spp.) — cold-water, oxygen-rich stream/lake habitat, implying cooler regional paleoclimate during deposition (Pliocene Santa Rosa and Late Pleistocene Laguna San Marcos).
- **Basin of Mexico site** (Lago de Texcoco, D.F.) yielded only *Chirostoma humboldtianum*, consistent with the historically atherinid-dominated fish fauna of the high-elevation Basin of Mexico lakes.
- **Northern arid-zone sites** (Rancho La Brisca, Sonora; Yépomera, Chihuahua) yielded only unidentified cyprinid/fish remains, suggesting poorer preservation or lower diversity/lower sampling in these more marginal, drier Pleistocene fluvial/lacustrine settings compared to the species-rich Jalisco basins.

## Key Findings
- The [[Lake Chapala]] basin (Jalisco) hosts the richest and longest continuous fossil freshwater fish record in Mexico, spanning from the Early Pliocene/Miocene (Santa Rosa) through the Plio-Pleistocene (Chapala Formation, Jocotepec Quarry) to the Late Pleistocene/Recent (Lake Chapala, Lake Zacoalco), documenting in-situ evolution and persistence of endemic lineages such as [[Goodeidae]] and [[Chirostoma]].
- The genus *Chirostoma* (Atherinidae) shows a remarkable Late Pleistocene radiation in the Chapala/Zacoalco system, with at least nine co-occurring named species (*C. arge, C. chapalae, C. consocium, C. diazi, C. labarcae, C. lucius, C. ocotlanae, C. sphyraena,* plus *C. promelas*), supporting the hypothesis of rapid intralacustrine speciation analogous to other endemic silverside/cichlid radiations.
- Presence of fossil [[Salmonidae]] (*Salmo* n. sp. and *S. clarki*) in Jalisco (Santa Rosa, Laguna San Marcos) provides biogeographic evidence that trout lineages extended much farther south into the Mexican Plateau during cooler Pliocene/Pleistocene intervals than their present-day relictual distribution suggests.
- *Ictalurus dugesi* is the most temporally persistent taxon in the record, appearing across nearly every Jalisco locality from the Plio-Pleistocene Chapala Formation through the Late Pleistocene Laguna San Marcos deposits, indicating long-term lineage stability of Ictaluridae in this basin.
- The record of *Micropterus relictus* (Centrarchidae) in the Chapala Formation is significant given the general rarity of native centrarchids in Mexican fossil freshwater deposits, suggesting a previously unrecognized native centrarchid lineage predating anthropogenic bass introductions.
- Numerous taxonomic synonymies (*Xystrosus* = *Algansea*, *Falcularius* = *Yuriria*, *Otalia* = *Chirostoma*) reflect substantial revision of early Mexican ichthyological nomenclature, underscoring the importance of systematic reassessment when interpreting older fossil fish literature.
- Sites outside the Jalisco highlands (Puebla's Valsequillo Formation, Sonora's Rancho La Brisca, Chihuahua's Yépomera, and the Basin of Mexico's Lago de Texcoco) show comparatively lower documented diversity, either reflecting genuine lower paleodiversity, taphonomic limitations, or incomplete study at the time of this compilation.
