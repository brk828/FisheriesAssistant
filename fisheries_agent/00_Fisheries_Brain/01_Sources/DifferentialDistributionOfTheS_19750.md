# Differential Distribution of the Striped Mullet, Mugil cephalus Linnaeus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Mugilidae #migration #life-history

## Executive Summary

This study documents the distribution patterns of [[Striped Mullet]] (Mugil cephalus) in the [[Colorado River]] and [[Gulf of California]] during 1967-1968, revealing evidence of upstream migration and questioning traditional understanding of salinity requirements for spawning. Collections from marine and freshwater sites showed distinct size-class distributions, with larger fish found progressively farther upstream. A critical finding was the collection of postlarval mullet 120 miles upstream in freshwater, suggesting successful spawning without marine salinity requirements.

## Background & Description

The [[Striped Mullet]] (family [[Mugilidae]]) inhabits coastal waters and estuaries in tropical and subtropical zones between latitudes 42°N and 42°S. Current literature suggests the species is catadromous, though conflicting reports on life history are common. Collections were made at approximately 2-month intervals during 1967-1968 from:

- [[Hunters Hole]], a slough near Gadsden, Arizona (110 miles upstream from Gulf)
- Below [[Imperial Dam]], Colorado River (150 miles upstream from Gulf)
- [[El Golfo de Santa Clara]], Sonora, Mexico (near Colorado River delta)

Collections were conducted during a study examining effects of seasonal factors, reproduction, and migration associated with salinity variations on endocrine organ histology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Five distinct size groups (standard length) were identified:

1. **7-13 cm**: Gulf collections during July, August, September (approaching age 1)
2. **17-20 cm**: Gulf collections (age 1-2)
3. **21-28 cm**: Gulf and [[Hunters Hole]] collections (age 2)
4. **29-37 cm**: Gulf, Hunters Hole, and [[Imperial Dam]] collections (age 3-4)
5. **39-46 cm**: Gulf and Imperial Dam collections (age 4-5)

Mean standard length for 86 fish from [[Colorado River]] was 33.1 ± 1.4 cm, while 756 [[El Golfo]] fish averaged 22.4 ± 1.7 cm. Larger size classes were difficult to collect in Gulf waters. Gear selectivity did not appear to explain the absence of smaller size classes in river samples.

## Key Findings

**Size Distribution and Migration Pattern**: The correlation of progressively larger size classes with distance from the Gulf indicates an extremely slow, alimental upstream migration of [[Striped Mullet]]. These findings support a marine origin for striped mullet of the [[Colorado River]].

**Postlarval Collection in Freshwater**: On March 19, 1966, a collection of 31 postlarval striped mullet (A.S.U. No. 2293) was made below [[Morales Dam]], Colorado River, 120 miles upstream. Mean standard length was 32.4 ± 1.1 mm (range 28-40 mm). The size indicates they were progeny from spawning occurring in December and January.

**Spawning Location Implications**: Since postlarvae were collected at such a great distance from salt water, it appears they arose from spawning of [[Striped Mullet]] in fresh water. This is the first time larval mullet have been reported this far upstream at this stage of life history. Larval mullet have been reported to enter Australian estuaries when large enough to swim against tidal streams, 2-3 months after hatching.

**Salinity Requirements Questioned**: These findings put in question any salinity requirement for successful [[Striped Mullet]] spawning, challenging the traditional understanding of the species as obligately catadromous.
