# Checklist of the Fishes of Caddo and Bossier Parishes, Louisiana

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Actinopterygii

## Executive Summary

This 1979 checklist, published as Number 3 of the [[Bulletin Of The Museum Of Life Sciences]] by [[Louisiana State University In Shreveport]], documents the fish fauna of Caddo and Bossier Parishes in northwestern [[Louisiana]]. The authors, [[Laurence M. Hardy]] and [[William H. LeGrande]], list 93 species in 21 families. The list is based on museum voucher specimens and published literature. Eleven species are new records for the two parishes. The fauna represents 62.0% of the freshwater fish species known from Louisiana. The authors describe the parish ichthyofauna as still poorly known and call for more collecting.

## Background & Description

The checklist follows the family classification of [[Nelson 1976]] and the common and scientific names of the [[American Fisheries Society]] (Bailey et al., 1970). Each entry gives the scientific name, the authority, the common name, and a source code. Source codes are either a museum acronym or a literature reference. Museum codes include:

- **LSUS**: [[Museum Of Life Sciences]], LSU in Shreveport
- **NLU**: Northeast Louisiana University, Museum of Zoology
- **BPNP**: Bossier Parish Nature Park
- **CC**: Centenary College of Louisiana
- **SWEPCO**: Southwestern Electric Power Co., Shreveport

Literature-only records are cited to [[Lambou 1962]] (Lake Bistineau fishes) or [[Douglas 1974]] (*Freshwater Fishes of Louisiana*). Douglas (1974) is the main statewide reference. Specimen identifications were made by Hardy, LeGrande, Larry Raymond, and [[Neil H. Douglas]].

The bulletin is a public document, printed at a cost of 47 cents per copy. Its back matter includes author instructions and a list of previous titles, including a plant checklist of Caddo Parish and an amphibian and reptile checklist of Caddo and Bossier Parishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Drainage systems.** Most of the study area drains to the [[Red River]] and its tributaries. A small, poorly collected portion drains to the [[Sabine River]] system. Collection localities are plotted on a drainage map. Those localities are concentrated where specimens are held in the museum collections.

**Biogeographic comparison.** The authors compared the Caddo-Bossier fauna with four other Louisiana parishes using Douglas (1974) data. They used a faunal resemblance factor (FRF), calculated as 2C/(N₁ + N₂), where C is the number of shared species and N₁ and N₂ are the species totals of each area. An FRF of 1 means identical faunas, and 0 means no shared species. The [[Faunal Resemblance Factor]] values in Table 1 range from 0.66 to 0.85.

- [[Ouachita Parish]] is the most similar area, with 96 species (64.0% of the state fauna) and an FRF of 0.85.
- [[Calcasieu Parish]], with only 66 species, shows a higher FRF (0.82) than [[East Baton Rouge Parish]] (FRF 0.76).
- [[Orleans Parish]] has the lowest FRF (0.66), which the authors attribute to its extensive marshland environment.

**Ichthyofauna status.** The authors state that the fauna is "still poorly known." They note that many species need further study to document their distribution and ecological requirements, and that literature-only records need verification.

## Key Findings

**Species richness and taxonomic composition**

- The checklist includes 93 species in 21 families.
- The fauna is 62.0% of the freshwater fish fauna of Louisiana.
- Superclass Agnatha includes one lamprey, [[Chestnut Lamprey]] (*Ichthyomyzon castaneus*). Superclass Gnathostomata contains the remaining 92 species.
- The largest family is Cyprinidae (carps, shiners, and minnows), with 25 species. The genus *Notropis* has 16 species, the largest genus.
- Centrarchidae (black basses and sunfishes) has 14 species. [[Lepomis]] has 9 of them.
- Percidae (perches and darters) has 10 species, including seven of *Etheostoma*.
- Catostomidae (suckers) and Ictaluridae (catfishes) each have 8 species.
- Percoidei (Perciformes) has 28 species, Cypriniformes has 33, and Siluriformes has 8.

**New records**

Eleven species had not previously been reported from Caddo or Bossier Parish:

- [[Paddlefish]] (*Polyodon spathula*)
- Goldeye (*Hiodon alosoides*)
- Skipjack herring (*Alosa chrysochloris*)
- [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idellus*)
- *Notropis boops*, *N. chrysocephalus*, *N. fumeus*, and *N. volucellus*
- Creek chub (*Semotilus atromaculatus*)
- Yellow bass (*Morone mississippiensis*)
- Striped mullet (*Mugil cephalus*), a marine-associated species included in the freshwater checklist

**Nomenclature and records requiring verification**

- The authors recognize *Fundulus blairae* as distinct from *F. notti*. The latter is no longer considered to have a statewide distribution (Wiley and Hall, 1975).
- Five species are listed by Douglas (1974) as statewide but have no parish specimens in LSUS, NLU, or BPNP collections as of July 31, 1978. They are [[Alligator Gar]] (*Atractosteus spatula*), black buffalo (*Ictiobus niger*), flathead catfish (*Pylodictis olivaris*), American eel (*Anguilla rostrata*), and *Fundulus notti*. Three of them were collected after August 1, 1978.
- A skeleton of *A. spatula* was found on a Red River sandbar east of Hosston (Bill Davis, personal communication). This is a documented record.
- Several species have only literature records, including Lambou (1962) records from Lake Bistineau without a specific locality or voucher.

**Conclusions and recommendations**

- The authors conclude that the Caddo-Bossier ichthyofauna is poorly known.
- They call for additional collecting to document distributions, ecological requirements, and verification of literature-only species.
- The authors acknowledge museum curators and several students and colleagues who helped with collecting and identification.
