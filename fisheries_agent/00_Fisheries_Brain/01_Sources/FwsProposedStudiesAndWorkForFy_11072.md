# FWS Proposed Studies and Work for FY95: Analysis of 1991–1994 GCES Phase II Studies

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #humpback-chub #glen-canyon-environmental-studies

## Executive Summary

This internal FWS planning document assesses the status of data analysis from the 1991–1994 Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) Phase II research program in the Grand Canyon, with a focus on the [[Humpback Chub]] and other native fishes of the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR). The report highlights a critical gap between data *collection* (deemed highly successful, with over 75% of all fish captures occurring in summer 1993) and data *analysis* (severely underfunded and understaffed). The final Phase II report will cover only a fraction of collected data — primarily LCR habitat/fish data from summer 1993 base flow conditions — leaving numerous datasets (spawning habitat, seine samples, water quality, confluence data, high-flow sampling) unanalyzed. The document proposes six new/continuing field study priorities for interim GCES work, including humpback chub spawning habitat description, yearling and post-larval life-history ecology, LCR confluence habitat use, cryopreservation of germplasm, and a fish disease monitoring framework — the latter motivated by emerging observations of parasitic infestations (*Lernaea* and Asian tapeworm) in LCR humpback chub populations.

## Background & Description

The [[GCES Phase II Studies]] (1991–1994) were designed primarily to *collect* extensive habitat and fish-use data in the Grand Canyon reach of the Colorado River, particularly in the [[Little Colorado River]] and its confluence with the mainstem, as well as other tributary confluences ([[Shinumo Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], [[Havasu Creek]], [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Tapeats Creek]], [[Deer Creek]]).

Target species for habitat-use analysis included:
- [[Humpback Chub]] (all life stages: YOY, subadult, adult)
- [[Speckled Dace]]
- [[Bluehead Sucker]]
- [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]

Fish sampled ranged from 50 mm TL to >300 mm TL, collected via USFWS study grids under base flow conditions in summer 1993. The final report was structured to cover:
- Transect habitat data (habitat availability) for LCR under base flow, summer 1993
- FWS sample grid habitat/fish data describing native fish habitat ecology in the LCR
- Transect habitat data at confluences of LCR, Shinumo, Kanab, and Havasu
- Discriminant function analysis (modeling) of tributary confluence habitat

A substantial list of collected datasets — including 1991–92 transect data, all seine sample data (1991–94), high-flow sample grid data, special sampling studies (1991), observational habitat-use surveys (1993–94), humpback chub (HBC) spawning habitat studies (1992–1994), confluence datasets (1991–1992), water quality data (1991–94), and ASU hoopnet habitat data (1992) — were explicitly excluded from the final report due to resource constraints.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The LCR serves as a model system for assessing whether other Grand Canyon tributaries could support humpback chub populations, underscoring the importance of the LCR confluence habitat-use data. However, a critical data gap exists at the LCR confluence itself, since FWS was not permitted to conduct fish sampling there directly using their own protocols, limiting comparability to other tributary confluence datasets.

Distinct life stages of humpback chub occupy different habitats and ecological niches within the LCR:
- **YOY** (young-of-year)
- **Subadults** (>1 year old)
- **Adults**
- **Post-larval juveniles** (20–50 mm TL) — largely unstudied life stage, exhibiting rapid growth and marked behavioral/ecological shifts near 50 mm TL
- **Yearlings** (~1 year old) — an ecological gap identified in the 1993 age-group study, being addressed via 1994 LCR field trips

Emerging fish health concerns were documented during the study period:
- Most fish captured in **Kanab Creek** (June 1993) carried one or more **Lernaea** (parasitic copepod) infections
- **Lernaea** were commonly observed on YOY humpback chub in the LCR (August 1993)
- **Lernaea** and **Asian tapeworm** infections became even more prevalent among LCR humpback chub by spring 1994

These findings suggest an unaddressed epizootic burden affecting both native fishes and introduced trout populations in the Grand Canyon, highlighting fish disease monitoring as an urgent, previously neglected ecological concern.

## Key Findings

- **Data collection vs. analysis imbalance**: Over 75% of all fish captures during GCES Phase II (1991–1994) occurred in summer 1993 alone; however, budget and staffing were insufficient to analyze the majority of collected data, risking loss of ecological insight relevant to [[Humpback Chub]] recovery and management.
- **Staffing proposal**: A minimum of one GS-11 temporary position (PhD-level statistician) was proposed to assist with data analysis, though this was acknowledged as insufficient to complete all needed analyses.
- **Six proposed interim field studies**:
  1. **Spawning habitat use** by humpback chub in the LCR — previously unsuccessful due to poor contractor coordination, permitting delays, and funding/review bottlenecks; proposed solution involves a dispersed sampling approach (per ASU-NNHP methodology) followed by targeted USFWS grid sampling.
  2. **Yearling humpback chub habitat use** in the LCR — fills a gap in the 1993 three-age-group study (YOY, subadult, adult); being addressed via 1994 field trips.
  3. **Early life-history ecology of post-larval humpback chub** (20–50 mm TL) — requires development of specialized sampling gear and continuous water-quality instrumentation to characterize rapid ontogenetic shifts in habitat, diet, and behavior.
  4. **Habitat use at the LCR confluence** — proposed partnership with [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZFGD) or [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) to apply FWS sampling protocols, since FWS was previously barred from independent sampling at this site; necessary for using LCR as a comparative model for other tributary confluences.
  5. **Cryopreservation of humpback chub germplasm** — building on 1994 USFWS success with sperm/egg cryopreservation and fertilization in [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]; goals include assessing genetic diversity, banking against population bottlenecks, and enabling genetically appropriate hatchery stock production.
  6. **Fish health monitoring framework** — proposed in response to observed *Lernaea* and Asian tapeworm outbreaks in LCR humpback chub and Kanab Creek fishes; recommended as a permanent component of long-term Grand Canyon fish monitoring.
- **Explicit analytical omissions**: The final GCES Phase II report will not include analysis of 1991–92 transect data, seine sample data (1991–94), high-flow sample grid data, 1991 special sampling studies, 1993–94 observational habitat surveys, 1992–1994 HBC spawning habitat studies, 1991–1992 confluence datasets, 1991–94 water quality data, or 1992 ASU hoopnet habitat data — nor any transect/habitat/fish-use analyses for upstream Grand Canyon tributary areas (Bright Angel, Shinumo, Tapeats, Deer, Kanab, Havasu).
