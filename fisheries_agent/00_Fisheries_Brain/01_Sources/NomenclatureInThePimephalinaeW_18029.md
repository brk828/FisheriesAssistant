# Nomenclature in the Pimephalinae: The Bullhead Minnow *Pimephales vigilax perspicuus*

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic note by [[Frank B Cross]] (University of Kansas) addresses the correct name for the bullhead minnow, long known as [[Ceratichthys]] *perspicuus*. Recent publications had used several names because of two changes: [[Hubbs 1951]] reduced the form to subspecific rank, and [[Bailey 1951]] placed all of the [[Pimephalinae]] genera under *Pimephales*. Cross concludes that *Pimephales vigilax perspicuus* is the correct name. He accepts the single-genus arrangement but argues that the subspecies distinction is valid. His evidence comes from nuptial tubercle counts, intestinal length variation, and the geographic distribution of specimens, including fish from a lake-stream system where the two forms had mixed.

## Background & Description

- **Subfamily characters:** Pimephalinae differ from other American cyprinids in two traits. First, the second (rudimentary) dorsal fin ray is enlarged, blunt, and separated from the third ray by a membrane. Second, breeding males have a limited number of [[Nuptial Tubercles]] arranged in one to three transverse rows on the snout.
- **Genera historically recognized:** Three genera were used in recent literature: *Ceratichthys*, *Pimephales*, and *Hyborhynchus*. *Pimephales* and *Hyborhynchus* had been separated from *Ceratichthys* mainly on relative [[Intestinal Length]].
- **Hyborhynchus:** Its separation from *Pimephales* rested on weak characters: a more ventral mouth, minor body contour differences, a more distinct caudal spot, and a temporary barbel-like jaw expansion in breeding males. Its complete lateral line, which contrasts with the incomplete line of *P. promelas*, is not a generic-level character. *Pimephales promelas confertus* also has a complete lateral line and intergrades with the typical subspecies.
- **Taxonomic history:** [[Hubbs and Black 1947]] revised *Ceratichthys*, recognized *perspicuus* and *vigilax* as full species, and assigned breeding males with 5-4 tubercle arrangements to *perspicuus*. [[Hubbs 1951]] reduced the two to subspecies in a passing remark that gave no further evidence. Cross notes that this remark was the only substantiation for the synonymy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Intestinal length variation.** Cross measured alimentary canal length relative to standard length in *Pimephales notatus* (now treated as *Hyborhynchus notatus*) and compared it with other species:

- *P. notatus* from Kansas streams (Fall River, Sugar Creek, Greenwood, Douglas and other counties) ranged from about 1.4 to 2.3 times standard length, averaging slightly under twice standard length.
- Specimens from Crooked Creek (Michigan) and Cayuga Inlet (New York) ranged from 1.2 to 2.6 times standard length.
- *P. promelas* has a long, convoluted intestine that can exceed 3.5 times standard length.
- *Ceratichthys* has a single forward loop, as in *Notropis*.

Cross concluded that *P. notatus* is intermediate between *P. promelas* and *Ceratichthys*. Because the genera are otherwise compact and similar, he judged the intestinal variation insufficient for generic division.

**Stillwater Creek introduction.** Stillwater Creek (Payne County, Oklahoma) originally held only *perspicuus*. In 1938, *vigilax* from an artificial lake was introduced into the lake with sport fish stock. The lake's outflow through spillways and gates allows fish to enter the creek, so the two forms overlap there. Dr. George A. Moore confirmed the presence of the *vigilax* stock.

**Geographic spread of tubercle patterns.** Specimens from the *vigilax* range in the Arkansas and Red River drainages, supplied by collaborators in Texas and Oklahoma, showed the following patterns:

- Beaver Creek (Oklahoma): one 5-0 specimen among other Arkansas River tributaries.
- A flood-control impoundment on the North Canadian River: one 5-3 specimen, about 100 miles west of Stillwater Creek.
- Texas (Palo Duro Creek, Grosebeck Creek, Beaver Creek below Beaver Creek Dam, Nueces River, Lake Texoma): patterns included 5-0, 6 primary with one tubercle between the nostrils, 4-0, 5-1, 5-2, and tubercle-bearing specimens from Lake Texoma.

## Key Findings

1. **Correct name:** *Pimephales vigilax perspicuus* (Girard) is the appropriate name for the bullhead minnow. The name had not been applied earlier.
2. **Genus:** Cross accepts Bailey's single genus *Pimephales* for the subfamily. The evidence does not support separate genera.
3. **Subspecies status:** The tubercle patterns are consistent with subspecific rather than full specific differences, which means Hubbs' 1951 reduction stands with a valid basis.
4. **Tubercle counts from Stillwater Creek (1946, 1948–1949):** A single 7-1 specimen was taken in 1946. In 1948 and 1949, 32 spawning males gave these counts: 5-0 (8), 6-0 (4), 7-0 (1), 6-1 (2), 7-1 (1), 8-1 (1), 5-2 (5), 6-2 (4), 5-3 (2), 6-3 (1), 5-4 (2), and 7-5 (1). The 7-5 specimen also had 4 chin tubercles.
5. **Tubercle variation:** Supernumerary tubercles cluster near positions normally held by single tubercles. When all four second-row tubercles are present, two lie between the nares and one lies on each side between the nares and eye. The lateral pair is the first to be lost.
6. **Diagnostic reliability:** Degree of jaw exposure in breeding males, used by Hubbs and Black, is less reliable than pearl-organ counts. The Stillwater Creek series varied widely in jaw exposure, and some fish with *perspicuus* jaw patterns had *vigilax* tubercle patterns.
7. **Intestinal length:** Variation in intestinal length within *P. notatus* is continuous, so it does not justify separate genera.
