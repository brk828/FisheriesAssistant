# Desert Fishes Council Annual Symposium Program 1982

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #desert-fishes #symposium

## Executive Summary

This document is the program for the Desert Fishes Council annual symposium held November 18-20, 1982, at the Granada Royale Hometel in Tempe, Arizona, hosted by [[Arizona State University]] Department of Zoology. The symposium focused on research, management, and conservation of desert fishes and aquatic ecosystems in the southwestern United States and northern Mexico, with special sessions on the [[Cuatrociénegas]] basin ecosystem and the [[Razorback Sucker]]. The program included presentations on fish ecology, reproductive biology, thermal tolerance, predation dynamics, habitat modeling, and conservation status of various threatened and endangered desert fish species.

## Background & Description

The Desert Fishes Council symposium brought together researchers, managers, and conservation professionals dedicated to preserving desert fish species. The program was organized into multiple sessions covering different taxonomic groups and conservation topics. Session III featured a comprehensive symposium on the [[Cuatrociénegas]] basin of Coahuila, Mexico, coordinated by Paul C. Marsh, covering crustaceans, fishes, flora, herpetology, gastropods, and environmental impacts. Session V focused on research and management papers addressing various desert fish species and their habitats across the southwestern United States.

Key taxa and species mentioned include: [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), Mexican pupfishes (*Cyprinodon eximius* complex), *Cyprinodon diabolis*, [[Relict Dace]] (*Relictus solitarius*), [[Woundfin]], [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*), and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Geographic focus areas included Sonoran Desert streams in Arizona, the Virgin River (Utah, Arizona, Nevada), Nevada systems, and the Cuatrociénegas basin in Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The symposium addressed desert aquatic ecosystems characterized by spatial intermittency, thermal extremes, and increasing anthropogenic impacts. Presentations covered multiple ecological scales from individual species thermal tolerances to community-level predation dynamics and energy flow in intermittent streams. The [[Cuatrociénegas]] basin received special attention as a unique desert aquatic ecosystem with endemic species across multiple taxonomic groups (fishes, crustaceans, gastropods, flora, herpetofauna).

Conservation contexts included refugium management (Hoover Dam Refugium for *Cyprinodon diabolis*), the establishment of the San Bernardino Ranch as the first U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service National Fish Refuge, and ciénegas as endangered southwestern habitats. Stream flow relationships to fish recruitment (Virgin River woundfin study, 1977-82) and physical habitat usability modeling for spring-fed desert streams represented applied conservation approaches.

## Key Findings

**Research Presentations Scheduled:**

- Reproductive biology of [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) in Sonoran Desert streams (W.G. Kepner and W.L. Minckley)
- Evolutionary ecology and life history studies of three Mexican pupfishes (*Cyprinodon eximius* complex), including thermal tolerance investigations (D.L. Soltz and C.R. Feldmeth)
- Predation dynamics in desert aquatic communities (Gary Meffe)
- Energy movement through predation in spatially intermittent streams (M. Busdosh and R. Freeman)
- Ecology of [[Relict Dace]] (*Relictus solitarius*) in Nevada (Steven Vigg)
- *Cyprinodon diabolis* population changes at Hoover Dam Refugium examining facultative versus genetic adaptation (Astrid Kodric-Brown)
- [[Woundfin]] recruitment relationships to stream flow in Virgin River, 1977-82 (James E. Deacon)
- Physical habitat usability model application to spring-fed desert stream fish communities (T.B. Hardy, C.G. Prewitt, K.A. Voos)
- Status assessment of [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki henshawi*) (Don Sada)
- Area cladograms for western North American fish faunas (D.A. Hendrickson and W.L. Minckley)
- Ciénegas as endangered southwestern habitats (W.L. Minckley and D.A. Hendrickson)
- Records of native southwestern fish transplants in Arizona (W.L. Minckley and J.E. Brooks)
- Thermal ecology of native and introduced Virgin River fishes across Utah, Arizona, and Nevada (P.B. Schumann, E.L. Stuenkel, J.E. Deacon)

**[[Cuatrociénegas]] Symposium Components:**
Comprehensive ecosystem assessment covering crustaceans (G.R. Cole), fishes (W.L. Minckley), flora (Donald J. Pinkava), herpetology (C.J. McCoy), gastropods (Robert Hershler), environmental impacts (Salvador Contreras-Balderas), with synthesis by James E. Johnson.

**Conservation Actions:**
Establishment of San Bernardino Ranch as first U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service National Fish Refuge (Gerald L. Burton). Business meeting agenda included constitution and bylaws review, resolutions, and leadership transition with Salvador Contreras-Balderas as Chairman-elect.
