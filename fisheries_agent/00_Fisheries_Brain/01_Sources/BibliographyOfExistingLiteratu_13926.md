# Bibliography of Existing Literature on Selectivity of Inland Water Fishing Gear Published by European Authors

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fishing-gear-selectivity

## Executive Summary

This document is EIFAC Occasional Paper No. 18 (FAO, Rome, 1987), a annotated bibliography compiled by E. Dahm of the Bundesforschungsanstalt für Fischerei, Hamburg. It collects roughly 50 publications on the [[Selectivity]] of inland water fishing gear authored by European scientists, many previously unknown to the wider scientific community because of language barriers. Each entry is abstracted in a standard format covering gear, fishing conditions, species, bycatch, selective effects and water body. The compilation was recommended by the Fourteenth Session of [[EIFAC]] (May–June 1986) and was prepared by a gear selectivity group formed at the Twelfth Session in Budapest. The author states the collection is far from complete and is intended to stimulate additional contributions and reveal knowledge gaps.

## Background & Description

The bibliography grew from the formation of a gear selectivity group under [[EIFAC]] Subcommission I, following interest in gear efficiency raised during the Eleventh Session in Stavanger and the [[COPLAKE]] (Cooperative Research Programme on Lake Fisheries Management) group. Members from twelve European nations joined. The group noted that selectivity affects all stock assessment methods, yet it is rarely considered because data are scarce.

Ten national contributors each reviewed the literature of their own country: J. Libosvarsky and J. Vostradovsky (Czechoslovakia), J. Nielsen (Denmark), K. Salojärvi (Finland), E. Dahm and H. Loeffler (Federal Republic of Germany), W.L.T. van Densen (Netherlands), T. Qvenild (Norway), L. Swierzowska (Poland) and O. Enderlein (Sweden).

A standard abstract format was agreed at the Thirteenth Session (Aarhus), covering:
- **Title** (translated into an official EIFAC language)
- **Fishing gear**: material, mesh size, yarn size, hanging coefficient, colour, stiffness, floats and weights
- **Fishing conditions**: weather, time and season, place, effort, mode of operation
- **Species fished**: length, girth, weight, food, behaviour
- **Bycatch**: species and percentage of total catch
- **Selective effects**: modal length, selection range, trends
- **Type of water**: area, depth, temperature, oxygen, visibility, exploitation
- **General remarks**

Author and subject indices are included. Entries are numbered 1 to 52 (author index), with the subject index referring to contribution numbers rather than pages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The abstracts span a wide range of water bodies:
- **Lowland and eutrophic lakes** such as [[Lake Keitele]], [[Lake Mamry]], [[Lake Warniak]] and [[Lake Tjeukemeer]].
- **Oligotrophic and subalpine lakes** such as [[Lake Constance]], [[Lake Starnberger See]], [[Lake Schluchsee]] and [[Lake Tyrifjorden]].
- **Coastal and brackish waters**, including [[Lake Gardno]] (connected to the Baltic) and the sea area off Helsinki and near Tvärminne.
- **Reservoirs**, including the Orava, Lipno, Svihov (Zelivka), Klicava and Slapy reservoirs.
- **Arctic and boreal lakes** in Newfoundland, Labrador, Northwest Territories (Lac la Martre) and Greenland.
- **Tropical waters**, namely Cuban lagoons and reservoirs.

Water chemistry and habitat details recorded include oxygen depletion limiting vendace to the upper 15 m in Lake Maroz, high conductivity in Lake Gardno (up to 1400 µS), submerged vegetation loss linked to pike collapse in Lake Warniak, and water transparency affecting colour-selectivity of gillnets in Swedish and Cuban waters.

Target and bycatch species frequently mentioned include [[Perch]], [[Roach]], [[Pikeperch]], [[Pike]], [[Bream]], [[Whitefish]], [[Vendace]], [[Eel]], [[Brown Trout]] and [[Arctic Char]].

## Key Findings

The document is a bibliography rather than a primary study, so its "findings" are the principal themes and notable results across the abstracts:

- **Scope and volume**: About 50 European-authored selectivity studies (1944–1984), covering gillnets, trammel nets, seines, fyke and trap nets, wing nets, eel pair nets, electrofishing, longlines, hook-and-line and crayfish traps. Publications appear in Polish, Finnish, Swedish, German, Czech, Danish, Norwegian and Dutch.
- **Gillnet mesh-to-length relationships**: Many studies derive linear relationships between modal length and mesh size or girth-to-mesh-perimeter ratios. Examples include a roach relation of l = 2.74 + 5.298 m (Ciepielewski/Korycki series) and girth/mesh-perimeter modal ratios of about 1.1–1.3 for most species (e.g. 1.33 for whitefish in Lake Tyrifjorden, 1.21 for brown trout in Heimdalsvatn).
- **Species-specific selectivity**: Pikeperch show higher girth/mesh ratios (2.2 for pikeperch vs 1.3 for bream in Lake Skanderborg) and are caught in larger mesh sizes than perch or roach.
- **Colour effects**: Molin's series (1958–1961) on Swedish lakes showed catch distributions among white, green, blue, brown, black and red nets varying with water colour and bottom colour, with dark nets more effective in clearer water in some cases.
- **Legal minimum mesh size**: Several studies (Kolbing 1974–1977, Havinga and Deelder 1949, Numann and Quoss 1971) challenge the rule of dividing minimum fish length by 8 and show the influence of yarn thickness (0.12 vs 0.15 mm yielded 66% more whitefish) and of enlarged mesh on catch weight (mean weight rose from 285 g to 400 g in Starnberger See).
- **Seasonal and depth effects**: Selectivity varies with month, depth and fishing effort (e.g. Leopold and Nowak's fyke net catches showed strong seasonal species shifts).
- **Management relevance**: Several contributions aim at regulating stocks, evaluating new legal minimum mesh sizes, or assessing gear effects on bycatch and exploitation, such as the Qvenild crayfish study (a shift from 17.5 mm to 21 mm mesh was calculated to double survival of undersized crayfish).
- **Notable quantitative results**: Eel selectivity factors of 23.5 and 25.3 in an eel trap net (Swierzowski and Dembinski, 1978); a 50% selection length of 61.2 cm for eel in a 14.1 mm codend; and pikeperch gillnet modal lengths of 38.7–54.5 cm across mesh sizes 40–65 mm (Nagiec and Ostrowski, 1973).
- **Stated gaps**: The compiler notes the collection is incomplete and that a fuller version would expose knowledge gaps and could support joint international exercises similar to the EIFAC Intercalibration Working Group.
