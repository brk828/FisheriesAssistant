# Station Profile: Kooskia National Fish Hatchery (FY1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary
[[Kooskia National Fish Hatchery]] is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility in north-central Idaho, authorized in 1961 and constructed beginning in 1966, dedicated to producing [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] (Clear Creek stock) for release into the Middle Fork of the [[Clearwater River]] to support commercial and sport fisheries. Since 1978, Kooskia has operated as part of a management complex with [[Dworshak National Fish Hatchery]], sharing adult holding, spawning, and early rearing infrastructure. The station's production is fundamentally constrained by degrading Clear Creek water quality in summer, disease pressures (BKD and "Ich"), and highly variable annual adult returns, which directly dictate egg collection and smolt output. FY1993 saw a substantial rebound in adult returns (1,180 fish, the 3rd highest in a 20-year history) following a critically low 1992 return of only 312 fish.

## Background & Description
- **Target smolt size:** Strictly controlled growth regime produces smolts at **20 fish per pound** at release.
- **Adult return size:** Returning adults are documented as **7- to 20-pound** spring chinook, returning 2-3 years post-release.
- **Egg-to-smolt ratio:** Approximately **1.1 million green eggs** are required to produce **800,000 smolts** (~40,000 lbs) at full production capacity.
- **Rearing timeline:** Eyed eggs received in September are incubated in 39-40°F chilled well water; hatching occurs mid-November, with ponding in mid-February.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Primary habitat:** [[Clear Creek (Idaho)]], a tributary feeding into the Middle Fork of the [[Clearwater River]], part of the larger Columbia River anadromous salmon system in the [[Pacific Ocean]] basin.
- **Life history:** Anadromous — juveniles reared and released as yearling smolts, migrate to the ocean, and return as adults 2-3 years later to spawn at the Clear Creek trap.
- **Water quality dependency:** Creek water temperatures rise in summer months, degrading rearing conditions; the hatchery depends on a **biofilter and recirculating chilled well-water system** to buffer fish from thermal stress, and reverts to cold Clear Creek water for fingerling rearing in winter, which slows growth rates.
- **Disease ecology:** Persistent pressure from **bacterial kidney disease (BKD)** and **"Ich" (Ichthyophthirius)** are managed via medicated (erythromycin-laced) feed (OMP diet) and standard therapeutic treatments — both diseases are recurring constraints on the rearing environment.
- **Stock specificity:** Only **Clear Creek-origin spring chinook stock** is used; no outside stock is introduced, preserving local genetic lineage but making production entirely dependent on wild/hatchery-origin return strength.
- **Secondary program:** From December 1992–May 1993, Kooskia temporarily reared [[Rainbow Trout]] (200,000 subcatchables for Dworshak Reservoir) because Dworshak NFH's facilities were compromised by an **IHN virus** outbreak, illustrating cross-station disease risk management within the complex.

## Key Findings
- **Authorization & History:** Authorized August 31, 1961 (75 Stat. 255); construction began 1966; operated under Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife before transfer to USFWS.
- **Complex Formation:** April 1978 merger with [[Dworshak National Fish Hatchery]] under a single Project Leader improved efficiency: Dworshak handles adult holding/spawning; eyed eggs are returned to Kooskia for rearing/release.
- **Return Variability:**
  - 1992: only **312** adult spring chinook returned — a successive low.
  - 1993: **1,180** adults returned — 3rd highest in 20-year station history, prompting full-capacity production plans for 1994.
- **Production Cycle Example:** 300,000 chinook fingerlings were reared/incubated at Dworshak and returned to Kooskia in March 1993 for release as yearling smolts in spring 1994.
- **Staffing & Budget (FY1993):** 3.5 FTE staff years (3 full-time permanent + 1 part-time biological aid); $225,000 operations/maintenance funding (subactivity 1311); YCC program supplied 6 enrollees + 1 crew leader for an 8-week summer period.
- **Budget Trend (1992–1996):** Operations funding ranged from $194K (1993 actual) to a projected $250K (1996), with FTEs stable at 3.0–3.5.
- **Physical Infrastructure:** Includes a Clear Creek diversion dam/intake, six deep wells, six 17'x80' recirculating rearing ponds, twelve 8'x80' raceways, a 4,162 sq ft hatchery building with 27 egg incubators and 42 six-foot circular tanks, a biological filter/recirculation system, mill pond sludge treatment, an electrical fish barrier, and adult holding facilities.
- **Fish Distribution Data (Spring Chinook Salmon, Idaho releases):**

| Fiscal Year | Number Released | Weight (lbs) |
|---|---|---|
| FY 1991 | 701,454 | 33,429 |
| FY 1992 | 800,075 | 42,685 |
| FY 1993 | 343,437 | 17,650 |

- **Trend Note:** The sharp decline in FY1993 distribution numbers (343,437 vs. 800,075 in FY1992) directly reflects the compounding effects of the 1992 low adult return on egg collection and subsequent smolt production, despite the encouraging 1993 return rebound (whose benefits would not appear in distribution figures until later fiscal years).
