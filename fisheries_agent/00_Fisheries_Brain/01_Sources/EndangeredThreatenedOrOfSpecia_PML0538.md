# Endangered, Threatened, or of Special Concern Fishes of North America: 1979

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This special section of the [[American Fisheries Society]] (AFS) journal *Fisheries* (Vol. 4, No. 2, March-April 1979) presents the 1979 AFS Endangered Species Committee list of native North American fishes considered endangered (E), threatened (T), or of special concern (SC). The list was compiled by James E. Deacon, Gail C. Kobetich, James D. Williams, and Salvador Contreras, with other committee members. It was built over three years, reviewed by state and provincial fish and game agencies, and extended to northern Mexico.

The final list contains **251 taxa**, up from 192 in the U.S. and Canada before review, and up to 203 for U.S. and Canadian taxa before Mexican taxa were added. The authors conclude that habitat destruction or modification is the dominant threat, affecting 98% of listed taxa, and that habitat protection and restoration, not restrictive harvest regulation, is the most effective path to recovery.

## Background & Description

The list is a revision of an earlier AFS state-by-state compilation (Miller 1972). It was prompted by the [[Endangered Species Act of 1973]], which spurred new research and public awareness of native fishes. The committee defined its categories following that Act:

- **Endangered:** in danger of extinction throughout all or a significant portion of its range.
- **Threatened:** likely to become endangered within the foreseeable future.
- **Special Concern (new category):** species that could become threatened or endangered through relatively minor habitat disturbance, or that need more information to determine status.

Status was assessed for each taxon throughout its North American range, regardless of political boundaries, and the committee dropped the earlier "peripheral" category. Each entry gives the common and scientific name, status, threat codes, and historical distribution by state, province, or Mexican state. Threat codes are: (1) habitat destruction, modification, or curtailment; (2) overutilization; (3) disease; (4) other natural or man-made factors such as hybridization, introduced species, predation, or competition; and (5) restricted range. Common names follow Bailey et al. (1970), and the list separates described from undescribed taxa, the latter recognized by specialists but not yet formally named.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The list covers a wide range of freshwater habitats, with many taxa from desert springs and streams in the Southwest and Mexican plateau. Examples include:

- **Desert springs and spring-fed systems:** many [[Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon*) and [[Tui Chub]] (*Gila bicolor*) populations, several of which are isolated in single springs.
- **Cave systems:** blind catfishes, cavefishes, and Texas blindcats.
- **Great Lakes:** ciscoes (*Coregonus*), which declined through habitat change, overfishing, and competition with introduced species.
- **Southeastern rivers:** darters (*Etheostoma*, *Percina*) and madtoms (*Noturus*), many with very restricted ranges.
- **Colorado River system:** [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and other big-river fishes affected by impoundment and flow alteration.

Habitat loss is the central theme. The authors argue that human use of environmental resources reduces availability for other species, and they note that restoration of habitat would benefit most listed fishes. Regulatory measures were judged to have largely prevented overexploitation from driving extinctions, with the exception of several ciscoes and catfishes.

## Key Findings

**Status changes and list totals**

- Review by state and provincial agencies produced recommendations to add **117 taxa**. Of these, 46 were recommended for endangered status, 36 for threatened, 26 for special concern, and 9 had differing opinions among reviewers but were still recommended for listing.
- **41 taxa** were recommended for deletion: 10 endangered, 28 threatened, and 3 special concern. Another 14 taxa were recommended for deletion by some reviewers and retention by others.
- Status was recommended to change for 16 taxa: 11 to a less critical category and 5 to a more critical one.
- One state accounted for 32.5% of recommended deletions, and one state accounted for about 30% of recommended additions. The authors note that many additions reflected status within state boundaries only.
- States and provinces responded to the review from all jurisdictions except New Hampshire.

**Threat analysis across 251 taxa**

| Threat | Taxa affected | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Habitat modification | 246 | 98% |
| Other natural or man-made factors | 93 | 37% |
| Restricted range | 41 | 16% |
| Overexploitation | 8 | 3% |
| Disease | 5 | 2% |

- Overexploitation is involved in the decline of six ciscoes and two catfishes. Otherwise, the authors judge it to be virtually absent as a cause.
- The authors find no evidence that scientific or educational collecting contributed to the decline of any North American taxon. They argue that the strict permit systems for collecting restrict the information needed for habitat and species management.

**Extinctions and rediscoveries**

- Three taxa were removed by extinction: the [[Tecopa Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis calidae*), the Whiteline topminnow (*Fundulus albolineatus*), and the Banff Longnose dace (*Rhinichthys cataractae smithi*).
- The [[Big Spring Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis pratensis*), considered extinct since 1959, was rediscovered in Condor Canyon, Lincoln County, Nevada, by Cal Allen of the Nevada Department of Fish and Game.
- A population of Sunapee trout (*Salvelinus alpinus aureolus*) was found established in Idaho, far outside its native range.

**Notable listed taxa**

- Endangered: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*, threats 1, 3, 4), Woundfin (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), Blue pike (*Stizostedion vitreum glaucum*, Lakes Erie and Ontario), Shortjaw cisco, and the snail darter (*Percina tanasi*).
- Threatened: Lake sturgeon (*Acipenser fulvescens*), Pallid sturgeon (*Scaphirhynchus albus*), and Paddlefish (*Polyodon spathula*, Special Concern).
- Special concern: Paddlefish, and many southeastern darters and minnows.

**Undescribed taxa**

- The list includes separately **numerous undescribed taxa** (e.g., undescribed cutthroat trout, tui chubs, pupfishes, and darters). They are listed separately solely to flag them for protection until they are formally described.

**Recommendations and conclusions**

- The committee recommends a **biennial revision** of the list and asks for documentation of status changes.
- The authors state that the status of many freshwater fishes is changing rapidly, and that more than 90% of endangered and threatened fishes could be removed from their categories through nationwide habitat restoration and protection programs.
- The authors conclude that effective species management follows the same lesson as fisheries management: defining and protecting optimal habitat is far more effective than imposing restrictive regulations.
