# Eretmobrycon bayano: A New Genus and Species of Characid Fish from the Bayano River Basin, Panama

#ichthyology #marine-biology #characidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper by [[William L. Fink]] describes *Eretmobrycon bayano*, a new species in a new monotypic genus of characid fish (Cypriniformes) from the upper tributaries of the [[Bayano River]] in Panama. The species was collected by [[J. D. McPhail]] and R. Dressler, and additional specimens were supplied by [[Donald L. Kramer]] of the [[Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute]]. The genus is defined by a pelvic fin ray count of i,8, a hypertrophied lower caudal fin lobe in adult males, and a deeper caudal peduncle in males. The author concludes that the species is most closely related to [[Bryconamericus]] and [[Astyanax]], but places it in a new genus because existing genera are polyphyletic or poorly defined and the unique specializations cannot be assigned to any known lineage.

## Background & Description

The paper extends the work of [[Meek and Hildebrand]] (1916) on the fishes of Panama. Their survey covered the lower Bayano River, but the upper tributaries were not sampled because of difficult access. New roads opened these areas, revealing a distinct fauna. McPhail's ecological study of the region provided the specimens. Methods follow [[Fink and Weitzman]] (1974).

**Type material and specimens**
- **Holotype:** USNM 213842, male, 51.7 mm standard length (SL), collected 10 March 1973 from a pool in a small stream about 19 km along the road from El Llano to Carti.
- **Paratypes:** USNM 213843 (22 specimens, 32.1–55.9 mm), plus small series at ANSP, CAS, BMNH, and ZMA (two specimens each).
- **Additional material:** USNM 214006 (7 specimens), USNM 214007 (40 specimens), and USNM 214664 (36 specimens), all from the same Bayano basin drainage and collected in 1974–1975.

**Morphology**
- Body elongate and laterally compressed. Greatest depth is 27.7–34.7% of SL.
- Pelvic fin has i,8 rays in all specimens. This ray count is shared only with the distantly related genus *Crenuchus*.
- Caudal fin is forked, with 10/9 principal rays. In adult males and large females, the lower lobe is much enlarged and dorsoventrally expanded, giving a paddle-like shape. The genus name refers to this feature.
- Dorsal fin has 2 unbranched and 9 branched rays (most common count).
- Anal fin has v unbranched and 28 branched rays (most common count).
- Pectoral fin has 1 unbranched and 10 branched rays (most common count).
- Lateral line is complete, with 37–38 perforated scales. Scales above the lateral line number 6–7, and scales below number 5.
- Precaudal vertebrae number 17–18, and total vertebrae number 37–38.
- Premaxilla has an irregular outer row of 4–6 (usually 5) tricuspid teeth and four inner row teeth with 5–6 cusps. The dentary has four large quinquecuspid teeth anteriorly.
- The third infraorbital is well ossified and contacts the preopercle ventrally.
- Breeding males bear thin spinules on the anal, pelvic, pectoral, and dorsal fin rays. Non-breeding males may show small nubbins instead.

**Coloration**
- In alcohol, the ground color is cream brown, with reticulate chromatophore patterns on the back and sides. There is a dark caudal blotch and no well-defined humeral spot.
- In life, the body is light olive brown above and silvery white below, with a silver or greenish gold mid-lateral band along the posterior two-thirds of the body. Males have yellow at the base of the upper caudal lobe and in the middle of the lower lobe.

**Morphometric ranges (percent of SL)**
Ranges are reported separately for males and females, with holotype values also given. Examples include head length (24.2–27.8), eye diameter (9.1–11.1), and caudal peduncle depth (11.6–15.8). The full table appears in the source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat**
- The watershed consists of heavily forested steep hills.
- The type pool was rocky-bottomed, about 15 m long, 8 m wide, and less than 50 cm deep.
- Kramer's collecting site was a small stream of moderate gradient, with pools alternating with riffles and small falls. The mean width was about 3 m, and the depth rarely exceeded 1 m.
- The stream bed is sandy with some stones and ledges, except where red clay eroded from the road covers the bottom. Forest debris provides moderate cover. No aquatic macrophytes are present.
- Stream temperature was 24.5 °C in mid-afternoon in mid-January (early dry season). Flow was estimated at about 0.05 m³/s.

**Associated fauna**
Other fishes collected with the species include *Brachyraphis*, *Brycon*, *Bryconamericus*, *Chaetostoma*, *Hypopomus*, *Poecilia*, *Pygidium*, and *Rivulus*.

**Diet**
- Most stomachs were empty when examined at midday by McPhail.
- Intestines of many specimens contained seeds and terrestrial insects. Two individuals contained a few fish scales (not of *Eretmobrycon*).
- In five specimens, Kramer found aquatic insect larvae, terrestrial insects, and algae to be important stomach contents.

**Sexual dimorphism and reproduction**
- Mature non-breeding males are identified by the enlarged lower caudal lobe rays. Some large females show a weaker version of this trait.
- Caudal peduncle depth is greater in males than in females. In the limited data, male peduncle depth appears to increase during sexual ripening. Female peduncle depth was similar across breeding and non-breeding populations.
- The smallest maturing male measured 43 mm SL, and the smallest maturing female measured 40 mm SL (Kramer, in litt.).
- Breeding is seasonal, occurring in January and February. The breeding population is mainly two-year-old fish, with a few three-year-old fish breeding a second time. Many sampled fish were immature one-year-olds.

## Key Findings

1. **New genus and species:** *Eretmobrycon bayano* is described as a new monotypic genus and species. It is known from the Bayano River basin, which gives the specific epithet.

2. **Diagnostic characters:** The genus is defined by a pelvic fin count of i,8, a hypertrophied lower caudal lobe in adult males and large females, and a deeper caudal peduncle in males. The pelvic ray count is shared only with *Crenuchus*, a distantly related characoid. Both traits are considered autapomorphic, so they are not informative about relationships.

3. **Relationships:** The species most probably relates to *Bryconamericus* and *Astyanax*. It shares dentition and infraorbital traits with *Bryconamericus*. Its anal fin spinules resemble those of *Bryconamericus* in distribution, but they point toward the fin base, as in *Astyanax*. The author considers the spinule resemblance probably convergent and not evidence of close relationship.

4. **Taxonomic problem:** The author argues that *Bryconamericus* is polyphyletic and consists of several difficult-to-define lineages. For this reason, placing *E. bayano* there is not justified. The paper suggests it may be a specialized relict of a *Bryconamericus*-like form that entered Panama earlier, or it may descend from a common ancestor of a *Bryconamericus* lineage.

5. **Diagnostic reliability:** Several generic characters separating *Astyanax* and *Bryconamericus* (premaxillary tooth counts, outer tooth row regularity, third infraorbital width, and fin hooks versus spinules) are variable. Fin spinules and hooks appear only during the breeding season, so their presence depends on timing.

6. **Ecology:** The species inhabits shallow, forested montane streams. It eats seeds, insects, and algae, and is likely an opportunistic feeder. Breeding occurs in January–February.

7. **Future work:** Function of the hypertrophied caudal lobe is probably linked to breeding behavior. Confirming this requires aquarium or field observations of spawning. A broader revision of *Bryconamericus* and related genera is needed to clarify the placement of this species.
