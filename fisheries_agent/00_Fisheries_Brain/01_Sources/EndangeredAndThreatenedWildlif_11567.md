# Removal of Gambusia amistadensis From Endangered Species List

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #poeciliidae #extinction #endangered-species-act

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register document announces the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's final rule removing the [[Amistad Gambusia]] (*Gambusia amistadensis*) from the List of Endangered and Threatened Wildlife, effective January 4, 1988. The delisting is based on determination that the species is extinct. The fish was known only from [[Goodenough Spring]] in Val Verde County, Texas, and was eliminated by inundation from [[Amistad Reservoir]] constructed in 1968. All captive populations subsequently died or were eliminated through hybridization with and predation by [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*). This regulatory action removes Federal protection under the Endangered Species Act and eliminates consultation requirements for Federal agencies.

## Background & Description

*Gambusia amistadensis* was described by Dr. Alex Peden in 1973 based on specimens collected in 1968 from [[Goodenough Spring]], a tributary of the [[Rio Grande]] in [[Val Verde County]], Texas. The species was not recognized as distinct until after reservoir construction had begun. Peden conducted extensive collecting in spring areas immediately upstream and downstream from the [[Amistad Reservoir]] but found no additional *G. amistadensis*, leading him to believe the species was restricted to the Goodenough Spring area.

The species was listed as endangered on April 30, 1980 (45 FR 28721), at which time it occurred only in captivity at the [[University of Texas]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Gambusia amistadensis* inhabited spring-fed freshwater habitat in the [[Rio Grande]] drainage system. The species' extremely limited natural range—known only from a single spring—made it exceptionally vulnerable to habitat destruction.

In July 1968, backwaters of the [[Amistad Reservoir]], constructed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, began permanent flooding of the Goodenough Spring area. Subsequent visits found the spring under more than 21.3 meters (70 feet) of silt-laden water. Peden (1973) believed the species was probably extirpated at that location.

In 1979, all Texas springs listed by Brune (1981) within 50 kilometers (31 miles) of Goodenough Spring with outflow exceeding 10 liters per second (0.353 cubic feet per second) were surveyed, but no *G. amistadensis* were found (Hubbs and Jensen 1984).

Captive populations were eliminated through hybridization with and predation by the [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), a closely related and more widespread species.

## Key Findings

**Extinction Determination**: After careful review of all available data, the Service determined that *Gambusia amistadensis* is extinct and no longer requires protection under the Endangered Species Act of 1973, as amended.

**Habitat Destruction**: The primary factor in extinction was the destruction and modification of habitat. The Amistad gambusia was known to occur only in Goodenough Spring, which was permanently flooded by [[Amistad Reservoir]] beginning in July 1968.

**Survey Results**: Extensive surveys of potential habitat found no wild populations. All Texas springs within 50 kilometers of the original location with significant outflow were examined in 1979 without finding the species.

**Captive Population Failure**: All captive populations maintained at the [[University of Texas]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] died or were eliminated through hybridization with and predation by *Gambusia affinis*.

**Public Comment**: Five comments were received on the proposed delisting rule. Four supported delisting (Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists, and two university faculty members). One opposed delisting, arguing the species should remain listed for automatic protection if rediscovered; the Service responded that maintaining extinct species on the list creates unnecessary burden on Federal agencies and that emergency relisting provisions exist if needed.

**Regulatory Effect**: Delisting removes Federal protection, eliminates section 7 consultation requirements for Federal agencies, and removes Federal restrictions on taking. No critical habitat had been designated for this species.

**Legal Basis**: Delisting authority derives from 50 CFR 424.11, which permits delisting when a species becomes extinct, recovers, or was listed in error. The Service determined sufficient evidence exists to recognize *G. amistadensis* as extinct.
