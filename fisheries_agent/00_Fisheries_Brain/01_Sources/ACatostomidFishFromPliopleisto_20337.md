# A Catostomid Fish from Plio-Pleistocene Lake Idaho

#ichthyology #paleontology #catostomidae #fossil-fish

## Executive Summary

This paper describes a partial skeleton of the head region of *[[Chasmistes spatulifer]]*, a catostomid fish from [[Plio-Pleistocene]] [[Lake Idaho]] deposits near [[Fossil Butte]] in [[Owyhee County]], Idaho. The specimen (ISUM 32034), collected June 17, 1971, greatly expands knowledge of this species previously known only from maxillary bones. The articulated head and pectoral girdle regions provide detailed osteological information about this extinct sucker species dated to 3.0-3.5 million years ago.

## Background & Description

*[[Chasmistes spatulifer]]* was originally described by [[Miller and Smith]] (1967) based on a nearly complete right maxillary (UMMP V55556) and a maxillary fragment from [[Lake Idaho]] deposits. The type specimens measured 25.6 mm and 25.0 mm and were estimated to come from an individual approximately 425 mm standard length. The species was assigned a date of 3.0 to 3.5 million years based on whole-rock analysis of basalt associated with fossil-bearing strata.

The new specimen was collected from fine sandy-silt deposits of lacustrine origin (T4S, R2W, Sec. 2, NE). The material was weathered and very fragile. Osteological terminology follows [[Weisel]] (1960).

### Osteological Description

**Oromandibular Region**: The complete left maxillary measures 37.2 mm from anterodorsal process to postvental corner and 38.2 mm from anteromedial process to postdorsal corner. Based on proportional measurements, this specimen represents an individual approximately 620 mm standard length. Both maxillaries agree with the type specimen description. Paired dentaries, angulars, and articulars were intact when collected. The right dentary measures 43.4 mm greatest length and 26.1 mm depth; left dentary measures 43.1 mm and 24.8 mm respectively.

**Mandibular Morphology**: The somewhat elongated mandible and maxillary, along with very gradual curvature of the mandibles, suggest an animal with a longer snout and lower jaw than modern [[Snake River]] suckers, confirming affinity with modern *[[Chasmistes]]* species. Both premaxillaries were present. The kinethmoid (rostral bone) was found intact. Right and left quadrates were recovered.

**Opercular Series**: The relatively complete right operculum was preserved. Branchiostegals were less well preserved with one missing and others fractured ventrally.

**Pectoral Girdle**: Both cleithra were found. Postcleithra present but incomplete, as are right supracleithrum and posttemporal. Individual bones of the right girdle and fin are present but not discernible without destruction.

**Neurocranium**: Readily identifiable bones include the lacrymal and two unusually large suborbitals. Incomplete bones present include prefrontal, frontal, parasphenoid, autosphenotic, and supratemporal. Remnants of sclerotic bones present.

**Hypobranchial Region**: Right hyomandibular and symplectic well preserved. Fragments of left hyomandibular collected. Part of right pharyngeal bone with teeth and parts of hyoid and branchial series recovered.

**Additional Elements**: Fragments recovered include left pectoral girdle, left opercular series, basioccipital, [[Weberian apparatus]], vertebrae, ribs, fin rays, pre-ethmoid, and pterygiophores. Some skin with scales was in position adjacent to right opercle.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The specimen derives from [[Lake Idaho]], a large [[Plio-Pleistocene]] lake system in what is now southwestern Idaho. The fine sandy-silt deposits of lacustrine origin indicate deposition in a lake environment. The age of 3.0-3.5 million years places this fauna in the [[Pliocene]]-[[Pleistocene]] transition.

The morphology of *[[Chasmistes spatulifer]]* suggests ecological similarities to modern *[[Chasmistes]]* species, which are specialized lake-dwelling suckers. The elongated mandible and maxillary with gradual curvature indicate feeding adaptations distinct from riverine sucker species currently found in the [[Snake River]].

Most fish species described from [[Lake Idaho]] deposits have been based on isolated bones, making this articulated specimen particularly valuable for understanding the paleofauna of this ancient lake system.

## Key Findings

1. **Specimen Size**: The new specimen represents an individual approximately 620 mm standard length, substantially larger than the type specimens (estimated 425 mm).

2. **Morphological Confirmation**: The complete left maxillary and nearly complete right maxillary agree with the original description by [[Miller and Smith]] (1967), validating the species diagnosis.

3. **Distinctive Morphology**: The elongated mandible and maxillary with gradual curvature confirm affinity with modern *[[Chasmistes]]* rather than riverine sucker species, indicating specialized lake-dwelling adaptations.

4. **Osteological Detail**: The articulated head and pectoral girdle provide unprecedented detail on cranial and girdle osteology for this extinct species, including:
   - Complete measurements of dentaries (43.4 mm and 43.1 mm)
   - Unusually large suborbitals
   - Well-preserved hyomandibular and symplectic
   - Intact kinethmoid

5. **Preservation**: Despite weathering and fragility, the specimen preserves articulated elements including opercular series, pectoral girdle components, pharyngeal bones with teeth, and skin with scales.

6. **Taxonomic Significance**: This specimen greatly expands knowledge of *[[Chasmistes spatulifer]]*, transforming understanding from a single bone to a comprehensive view of cranial and girdle morphology.

7. **Depositional Context**: Recovery from fine sandy-silt lacustrine deposits near [[Fossil Butte]] provides additional stratigraphic context for [[Lake Idaho]] paleoenvironments.
