## Executive Summary

This entry is a scholarly book review by Carl L. Hubbs of *Fishes of South Dakota* (1962), a comprehensive faunal monograph by [[Reeve M. Bailey]] and [[Marvin O. Allum]] published through the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology. The work is presented as a milestone in regional systematic ichthyology, documenting the fish fauna of South Dakota through historical review, station-based collection records (137 sites), taxonomic keys, annotated species accounts, and zoogeographic synthesis. The review highlights the monograph's rigor in resolving taxonomic ambiguities, particularly within [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows), and its treatment of postglacial fish distribution shaped by historical hydrographic connections such as [[Warren River]], the [[Belle Fourche River]], and drainage piracy events.

## Background & Description

Although this is a faunal/taxonomic review rather than a morphology-focused paper, several diagnostic morphological analyses are singled out as noteworthy:

- **[[Rhinichthys cataractae]]** (Longnose Dace): Notable regional variation in **gas bladder size**, used as a key variational character across the state.
- **[[Notropis atherinoides]]**: Treated as conspecific with *N. percobromus*, the latter regarded as a phenotypic variant rather than a distinct species.
- **[[Notropis stramineus]]**, **[[Catostomus commersonnii]]**, and **[[Boleosoma nigrum]]**: Subject to detailed variational (morphometric/meristic) analyses across South Dakota populations.
- **[[Hybognathus nuchalis]]** vs. **[[Hybognathus placitus]]**: Reconfirmed as fully distinct sympatric species based on valid diagnostic characters, most notably a well-illustrated and "trenchant" difference in **skull structure** — resolving prior taxonomic uncertainty.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The monograph documents species across **137 collecting stations** in South Dakota, each annotated with brief ecological notes and cross-referenced species records.
- Several species are identified as **glacial relicts** — northern fishes persisting in isolated, ecologically appropriate waters within South Dakota and adjacent states, notably:
  - **[[Semotilus margarita]]** (Pearl Dace)
  - **[[Chrosomus neogaeus]]** (Finescale Dace)
- Three species are highlighted as characteristic of the **northern Great Plains** ecoregion, with dedicated range maps:
  - **[[Hybopsis gelida]]**
  - **[[Hybopsis meeki]]**
  - **[[Notropis topeka]]**
- **Postglacial hydrographic interchange** is treated as a major driver of the current fish distribution pattern in the state, including:
  - The **[[Warren River]]** as a historical pathway connecting South Dakota waters to Arctic drainage.
  - A proposed ancient connection between the **upper Mississippi** and **middle Missouri** river systems.
  - **Stream piracy**: capture of a **Little Missouri River** headwater by the **[[Belle Fourche River]]**, altering faunal distributions between basins.

## Key Findings

- The monograph is considered a **systematic milestone**, exemplifying rigorous state-level faunal review despite the reviewer's preference for river-system-based (rather than political-boundary-based) treatments for freshwater fauna.
- Provides critical, alternative **taxonomic keys** by family and genus, alongside South Dakota-specific annotated synonymies.
- Notable taxonomic conclusions include:
  - Reaffirmation of **[[Hybognathus nuchalis]]** and **[[Hybognathus placitus]]** as distinct species, resolving earlier synonymy.
  - Treatment of *Notropis percobromus* as a phenotypic variant within **[[Notropis atherinoides]]**.
  - Discussion (in "Comments on the Genera *Semotilus* and *Nocomis*") of senior author Bailey's broader taxonomic "lumping" philosophy, including a trend toward **downgrading subspecies status**, which the reviewer regards as "particularly disturbing."
- Documents **interspecific hybrids** recorded within South Dakota waters.
- Concludes with a **zoogeographic synthesis** explaining the origin and composition of the entire South Dakota fish fauna, integrating glacial history, drainage connectivity, and regional endemism.
- The work includes an extensive **historical bibliography** of South Dakota ichthyological literature, positioning it as a foundational reference for the region.
