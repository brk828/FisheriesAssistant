## Executive Summary

This report documents a single-day fish survey conducted on **4/12/96** along **West Turkey Creek**, a small stream system in the Chiricahua Mountains (Gila River drainage, Arizona), surveyed jointly by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) and USDA Forest Service (FS). Using electro-shocking techniques, surveyors sampled eight discrete pools spanning from a "Dispersed Campsite" pool downstream through the "Lower Waterfall Base" pool. Three species were documented: **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (recorded as *Salmo gairdneri*, now *Oncorhynchus mykiss*), the imperiled **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*), and the **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*). Notably, evidence of active spawning was recorded, including trout with milt and roe, a stripped female trout, and observed fry potentially belonging to the native cyprinid species — suggesting concurrent reproductive activity among introduced and native fishes in the same reach.

## Background & Description

### Salmo gairdneri (Rainbow Trout / [[Oncorhynchus mykiss]])
- **Sample size:** 13 males, 1 female (across all pools), plus additional unspecified individuals noted in pool-by-pool comments.
- **Male total length:** Average 151 mm (reported as "15.1 cm"); range **101–280 mm**.
- **Female total length:** Single measured female = 263 mm (also a separate 263 mm female recorded at Pool 4, Upper Sycamore Waterfall — possibly the same size class but distinct capture).
- **Reproductive condition:** Multiple males expressing milt (Pools 8 and 7); one female at Pool 4 carrying roe and later artificially stripped by surveyors.
- **Size range across individual captures:** 101 mm, 125 mm, 127 mm, 263 mm, 280 mm.

### Gila purpurea ([[Yaqui Chub]])
- **Sample size:** 2 individuals total (1 visual observation, 1 physical capture).
- **Total length:** 80.0 mm (captured individual); sex undetermined.
- **Additional observation:** A possible female (sex uncertain, denoted "female(?)") observed visually at Pool 5 (SE Tributary) carrying eggs.
- **Fry observation:** 4–5 fry observed at Pool 4, tentatively attributed to either *Gila purpurea* or *Agosia chrysogaster* (species not distinguishable at fry stage in field conditions).

### Agosia chrysogaster ([[Longfin Dace]])
- **Sample size:** 5 individuals total (1 visual, 4 captures).
- **Average total length:** 66 mm.
- **Distribution:** Individuals recorded at Pool 7 (Rock Outcrop — one visual observation described as "dace-like form") and multiple unsexed individuals at Pool 1 (Lower Waterfall Base).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure:** The surveyed reach of West Turkey Creek comprises a discontinuous series of at least 8 named pools separated by waterfalls, a dam, and downed woody debris, indicating a high-gradient, structurally complex montane stream typical of southeastern Arizona sky-island drainages.
- **Species segregation by pool:**
  - **Pool 8 (Dispersed Campsite):** Dominated by *Salmo gairdneri*, including a large individual (280 mm) and multiple milt-bearing males — suggests this pool functioned as an active trout spawning/holding area, associated with human dispersed-camping activity at the site.
  - **Pool 7 (Rock Outcrop):** Mixed assemblage — *Gila purpurea*, *Agosia chrysogaster*, and additional milt-bearing *Salmo gairdneri* — indicating potential niche overlap or reach-scale coexistence between introduced trout and native cyprinids.
  - **Pool 6 (Downed debris):** No fish captured or observed — possibly unsuitable habitat (e.g., heavy woody debris, limited pool volume) or sampling limitation.
  - **Pool 5 (SE Tributary):** Native *Gila purpurea* (visual, gravid female) alongside multiple male *Salmo gairdneri* in varying milt condition — a tributary confluence zone potentially important for native fish refugia.
  - **Pool 4 (Upper Sycamore Waterfall):** Site of confirmed trout reproduction (roe-bearing female, artificially stripped) and observation of native/cyprinid fry — suggesting this pool serves as a nursery area, with waterfall barriers likely limiting upstream trout dispersal and potentially protecting upstream native fish populations.
  - **Pools 3 (Upper Dam) and 2 (Lower Waterfall):** No captures or observations — likely represent barrier features (dam, waterfall) restricting fish movement and habitat use.
  - **Pool 1 (Lower Waterfall Base):** Exclusively *Agosia chrysogaster* — suggests this downstream pool, isolated by the waterfall barrier above, supports a native-fish-only assemblage, potentially free of trout predation/competition.
- **Barrier effects:** The presence of waterfalls (Pools 2 and 4) and a dam (Pool 3) appears to structure fish distribution longitudinally, with introduced trout concentrated in more accessible downstream/mid-reach pools and native cyprinids persisting in isolated pools above and below these barriers.

## Key Findings

- Three fish species documented in West Turkey Creek: introduced **[[Rainbow Trout]]** (*Salmo gairdneri*), and two native Sonoran Desert cyprinids, the **[[Yaqui Chub]]** (*Gila purpurea*) and **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*).
- Active trout spawning behavior documented at multiple pools (milt-expressing males at Pools 8, 7, and 5; a roe-bearing, stripped female at Pool 4), indicating this stream segment supports self-sustaining or actively spawning rainbow trout populations.
- Native *Gila purpurea* was found in low abundance (only 2 individuals total), with a possible gravid female observed, raising conservation concerns given the species' imperiled status in Arizona's Gila River drainage.
- Fry observed at Pool 4 could not be definitively assigned to either *Gila purpurea* or *Agosia chrysogaster*, highlighting field identification challenges for juvenile native cyprinids.
- Several pools (Pools 6, 3, and 2) yielded no fish captures or observations, suggesting habitat barriers (waterfalls, dam, debris) segment the stream into isolated fish assemblages.
- The spatial pattern suggests potential co-occurrence and possible competitive/predatory interaction between non-native trout and native cyprinids in mid-reach pools (Pools 7 and 5), an important consideration for future **[[Native Fish Conservation]]** and trout management planning in this drainage.
- Survey methodology (electro-shocking parameters — voltage, pulse rate) was not recorded, limiting reproducibility and comparison with future survey efforts.
