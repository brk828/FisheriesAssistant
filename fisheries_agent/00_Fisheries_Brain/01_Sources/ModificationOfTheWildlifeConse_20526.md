# Modification of Wildlife Conservation Biology Major Curriculum at Arizona State University

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #curriculum #wildlife-management

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from April 7, 1997 documents a comprehensive curriculum revision for the Wildlife Conservation Biology major at [[Arizona State University]]'s Department of Zoology. The proposal renames the major to "Conservation Biology," restructures core courses to eliminate the terrestrial/aquatic division, and modernizes the curriculum to reflect broader conservation science principles. The changes coincide with the department's transition from Zoology to Biology. Key modifications include renaming ZOL 411 to "Advanced Conservation Biology I" and ZOL 413 (renumbered to 412) to "Advanced Conservation Biology II," eliminating restrictive terrestrial and aquatic options in favor of flexible elective requirements, and updating course prerequisites.

## Background & Description

The Department of Zoology at Arizona State University underwent significant restructuring in 1996-97, converting to a Department of Biology. This transition prompted a two-year review of the Wildlife Conservation Biology undergraduate curriculum. The existing program featured separate "Terrestrial" and "Aquatic" options, with courses like ZOL 411 "Biology and Management of Terrestrial Wildlife" and ZOL 413 "Biology and Management of Aquatic Resources." The curriculum required 45 semester hours in major courses and 13 hours in related fields, plus mathematics proficiency. Related field requirements included chemistry (CHM 113, 115, 231, 235 or CHM 331, 332, 335, 336) and mathematics (MAT 210 or calculus).

The terrestrial option historically required courses such as [[Mammalogy]] (ZOL 471 or 472) and [[Ornithology]], while the aquatic option included [[Limnology]] (ZOL 473) and [[Ichthyology]] (BIO 426). The program maintained connections to federal and state employment requirements for wildlife and fisheries biology positions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The curriculum revision reflects evolving understanding of conservation science as an integrated discipline rather than separate terrestrial and aquatic domains. The memorandum acknowledges that traditional divisions between "wildlife biology" and "fisheries biology" are anachronistic, limiting students' preparation for modern conservation challenges. The new structure emphasizes [[North American Ecosystems]] in the first advanced course and global biodiversity patterns, [[Global Environmental Change]], and sustainable use of natural resources in the second course.

The proposal recognizes the need to maintain course identification for resource management applications while broadening the conceptual framework. Faculty noted that terms like "management" and "conservation" in course descriptions would remain acceptable to prospective employers while allowing more comprehensive coverage of conservation biology principles.

## Key Findings

**Major Curriculum Changes:**

1. **Name Change**: Wildlife Conservation Biology major renamed to Conservation Biology to eliminate unnecessary restriction and better reflect curriculum scope

2. **Course Restructuring**: 
   - ZOL 411 retitled "Advanced Conservation Biology I" (3 credits, Spring semester) covering principles of conservation science, biology of threatened species, and management principles emphasizing [[North American Ecosystems]]
   - ZOL 413 renumbered to ZOL 412 and retitled "Advanced Conservation Biology II" (3 credits, Fall semester) covering global biodiversity patterns, processes and conservation, [[Global Environmental Change]], and sustainable use of natural resources with international approaches
   - Prerequisites changed from BIO 217 and 320 plus specific taxon courses (ZOL 471, 472, or 473) to BIO 217 and 320 only

3. **Option Elimination**: Terrestrial and Aquatic options removed, replaced with 10 semester hours of elective courses chosen by students in consultation with advisors, allowing specialization within conservation biology while maintaining flexibility

4. **Related Fields**: ENG 301 (Writing for the Professions) eliminated as related field requirement since it no longer satisfies CLAS Humanities Distribution Requirement

5. **Prefix Changes**: All ZOL course prefixes to change to BIO prefixes following departmental reorganization

**Justifications Provided:**

- Allows clearer heuristic orientation to conservation biology field
- Eliminates overlapping and anachronistic terrestrial/aquatic division
- Enables participation by wider cross-section of faculty
- Broadens student options and specialization opportunities
- Ensures curriculum remains truly departmental (possibly interdepartmental) offering
- Maintains careful advising to ensure students meet Federal and State employment requirements

**Implementation**: Effective Fall 1997, with new advising brochure planned to outline available options and aid student decision-making regarding course selection
