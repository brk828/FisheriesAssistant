# Development of Fishes of the Chesapeake Bay Region: Atlas of Egg, Larval, and Juvenile Stages (Part I)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This atlas excerpt, edited by [[Earl E. Deubler Jr.]] from work by Alice J. Mansueti and Jeol D. Hardy Jr., documents the egg, yolk-sac larval, larval, prejuvenile and juvenile stages of freshwater fishes occurring in the Chesapeake Bay region. The text covers the families [[Cyprinidae]] (minnows and carps) and [[Ictaluridae]] (freshwater catfishes), together with an introductory list of fish groups (sturgeons, gars, bowfin, tarpon, herrings, pikes, minnows, suckers, sea catfishes). For each species it gives meristic counts, distribution in Chesapeake tributaries, habitat, spawning behavior, egg descriptions, timed developmental series, pigmentation and size-at-stage data, drawn from a large body of cited literature.

## Background & Description

The document is a regional developmental atlas. Each species account follows a standard sequence: diagnostic characters (adult fin ray and scale counts, vertebrae, gill rakers), distribution and ecology, spawning (location, season, time, temperature, fecundity), egg description, embryonic development, and larval, prejuvenile and juvenile stages, closing with literature cited and additional references.

Species treated in the excerpt:

- [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*): native to eastern Asia and Europe, widely introduced; adult ca. 457 mm maximum length; recorded in Chesapeake tributaries in Maryland.
- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*): native to Asia, introduced in North America; adult maximum ca. 1,219 mm (recorded as 1,219 mm in the text), maximum weight 90 pounds; tidal tributaries as far north as Havre de Grace, Maryland.
- [[Golden Shiner]] (*Notemigonus crysoleucas*): range from Nova Scotia and Manitoba south to Florida; maximum length ca. 305 mm; tidal tributaries in Maryland and Virginia.
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*): maximum length ca. 465 mm (adult 190 mm TL specimen figured); Virginia tidal tributaries.
- [[Brown Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus nebulosus*): maximum length ca. 508 mm; Virginia and Maryland tidal tributaries; maximum salinity 7.6 o/oo.
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*): maximum length ca. 1,168 mm; Pamunkey River, Virginia, and tidal freshwater of the Susquehanna River, Maryland; maximum salinity 15.1 o/oo.
- [[Tadpole Madtom]] (*Noturus gyrinus*): maximum length ca. 114 mm; distribution and developmental data are presented in the adult and juvenile descriptions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Goldfish and carp favor shallow, vegetated, warm water; carp occasionally enter rocky shoals and swift water during spawning runs. Golden shiner and bullheads occupy weedy lakes, ponds, quiet streams and sloughs; bullheads tolerate low oxygen. Channel catfish use large river channels and lakes, and some populations migrate into small streams to spawn.
- **Salinity tolerance:** Goldfish are unable to withstand prolonged salinity above 15 o/oo (maximum recorded 10 o/oo in the text). Carp were recorded at 17.6 o/oo in Chesapeake Bay. Golden shiner reached 14.4 o/oo; channel catfish 15.1 o/oo; brown bullhead 7.6 o/oo.
- **Temperature:** Goldfish spawning at about 21–23 °C; carp optimum 18–22 °C, ceasing spawning below ca. 14 °C in New York; golden shiner spawns at 20–27 °C; channel catfish 21–29 °C, optimum ca. 27 °C.
- **Spawning seasons:** Goldfish late March to mid-August in Maryland; carp June in Maryland, April to August in North America; golden shiner late March to August; brown bullhead late spring to August; channel catfish early April through July.
- **Nest and substrate:** Goldfish eggs adhere to aquatic plants and fixed objects; carp eggs in clusters of 500–70,000 on vegetation, twigs and stones; golden shiner eggs on filamentous algae and in bass nests; brown bullhead builds shallow nests in sand, gravel or under logs; channel catfish use cavities, tunnels, tin cans and similar shelters.
- **Parental care:** Brown bullhead and yellow bullhead males guard eggs and larvae; channel catfish larvae are apparently guarded by the male.

## Key Findings

**Goldfish (Carassius auratus)**
- Fecundity 2,000–400,000 eggs; egg diameter 1.0–1.7 mm (unfertilized ca. 1.6 mm).
- Incubation 4–6 days at 25 °C range given as 46–54 hours at 29 °C; 8–10 days at 18 °C; 5 days at 24 °C.
- Hatching length 4.5–5.0 mm TL; gas bladder apparent within 20 hours of hatching; age at maturity usually 2–4 years.

**Common Carp (Cyprinus carpio)**
- Fecundity 93,000 to 2,059,750 eggs; primary spawning 50,000–620,000 eggs.
- Eggs ca. 2.0 mm, deposited in clusters of 500–700 per area of about 6 feet diameter.
- Incubation 8–14 days depending on temperature; 5 days at 20 °C; 5 days at 15 °C.
- Age at maturity: females 2–5 years, males 1–4 years; minimum size females ca. 300 mm, males ca. 178 mm.

**Golden Shiner (Notemigonus crysoleucas)**
- Fecundity up to 200,000 eggs; fertilized egg diameter 1.0 mm, adhesive, lacking oil globules.
- Incubation about 4 days at ca. 27 °C; larvae undescribed; prejuvenile 18–24 mm TL.
- Age at maturity 1–3 years, mostly in the second year, at 50–70 mm.

**Yellow Bullhead (Ameiurus natalis)**
- Fecundity 1,650–7,000 eggs; 800–700 eggs per nest; incubation 5–10 days.
- Juveniles described at 17–21 mm TL; age at maturity during the third year.

**Brown Bullhead (Ictalurus nebulosus)**
- Fecundity ca. 2,000–13,800; 50–10,000 eggs per nest; fertilized egg diameter ca. 3.0 mm.
- Development at 21 °C: blastodisc evident at 1 h 12 min; embryonic shield at 1 day; 14 somites at 2 days.
- Incubation 5 days at 25 °C; 8 days at 20–21 °C. Hatching length 4.0–4.5 mm; age at maturity 1 year.

**Channel Catfish (Ictalurus punctatus)**
- Fecundity 1,600–70,000; eggs in gelatinous masses of 2,500–20,000; egg diameter 3.5 mm (8.2 mm with capsule removed).
- Incubation 7–10 days at 23–25 °C; 6–9 days at 27–29 °C. Duration of yolk-sac stage 6 days; minimum hatching size ca. 6.4 mm.
- Age at maturity 2nd to 5th year; minimum size ca. 178 mm.

**Tadpole Madtom (Noturus gyrinus)**
- Vertebrae 32–37; prejuvenile and juvenile stages described; adipose fin notch inconspicuous.

**Document Scope**
- The excerpt covers fish groups beyond the detailed species accounts (sturgeons, gars, bowfin, tarpon, herrings, pikes, suckers, sea catfishes), but developmental series are given only for the Cyprinidae and Ictaluridae accounts.
- Descriptive data are from a compilation of cited sources (e.g. Battle 1940; Dmitrieva 1957; Smith 1909; Schwartz 1964), with many additional references listed.
