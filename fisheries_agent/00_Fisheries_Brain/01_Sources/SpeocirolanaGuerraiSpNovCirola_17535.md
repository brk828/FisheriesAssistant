# Speocirolana guerrai sp. nov.: A New Blind Troglobitic Cirolanid Isopod from Cueva de la Chorrera, Nuevo León, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cirolanidae

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *Speocirolana guerrai* sp. nov., a blind, depigmented troglobitic isopod of the family [[Cirolanidae]] from [[Cueva de la Chorrera]] in the Sierra Madre Oriental near Linares, Nuevo León, Mexico. The description rests on a type series of 48 specimens collected in 1971 and 1972. The species is distinguished from its congeners by a combination of pereiopod spination, uropod and telson characters, and maxilliped palp spines. The authors also discuss the probable origin of the genus, arguing for a pre-Tertiary (likely Cretaceous-era) coastal origin, and review the relationship of the species to [[Speocirolana]] populations across northeastern Mexico. The species is named in honor of Adán F. Guerra, who financed the fieldwork.

## Background & Description

The Sierra Madre Oriental is dominated by limestone and contains many caves, some with permanent water. Cueva de la Chorrera, located at kilometer 27.3 of the Linares–Galeana highway, yielded the isopod during collections by students and staff of the [[Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León]] (UANL), sponsored by the Aquarium Municipal Felipe de J. Benavides.

**Type material:**
- **Holotype:** UANL 53, female, 24.6 mm, collected 19 March 1971 by Héctor Medina Pedraza.
- **Paratypes:** 48 specimens in total across several lots (UANL 54, 56, 57, and 64), with duplicates deposited at the British Museum (BM), UNAM, [[Tulane University]], and the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM 140471).
- Total length observed: 9.0–25.9 mm.

**Morphology (key characters):**
- Body length to maximum width ratio 2.5–3.2; head about twice as wide as long.
- No epimera on pereionite I; small rounded epimera on II and III; strong posterolateral angles on IV–VII.
- Pleon of five visible segments.
- Antennula with 3 peduncular articles and 18 (9–21) flagellar articles; antenna with 6 peduncular articles and 44 (23–51) flagellar articles. Flagellar counts scale with body length (Fig. 5).
- Mandibles with four teeth on the incisor; lacinia mobilis with 16 (10–25) spines; molar with 29 (21–37) small teeth.
- Maxilliped lacinia with 13 (9–14) plumose setae and 3 (1–3) retrorse coupling hooks; palp spines 7 (2–7) on segment 2 and 4 (1–4) on segment 3.
- Pereiopods I–III are prehensile (subchelate); meropods bear 1, 2, and 2–3 internal apical spines respectively, plus a medial/subapical spine on each.
- Pereiopods IV–VII are ambulatory; pereiopod VII propodite has a subdistal and a medial spine.
- Male pleopod II bears an arched copulatory organ; the male genital organ is foliar and subtriangular.
- Uropods are sexually dimorphic: the male exopodite is rounded and pilose, while the female exopodite is foliar. Both sexes have 6–7 stout spines on the endopodite inner margin and one on the exopodite.
- Telson rounded, length-to-width ratio 1.06–1.13.
- A feature not previously noted in the genus is 7–14 submarginal discoidal thickenings with small filaments on pleopods III–V.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The cave is small, mostly about 3 m in diameter and branched. Its lower gallery ends in a water body that dried during the third collection, after which animals were found in an interior cavity connected by a tube. Recorded air temperatures were 26.5 °C inside and 22 °C in the aquatic habitat (exterior 34 °C and 30 °C on two dates). Water is still, clear, and shallow except behind the deeper pool. The bottom is muddy with some gravel, the walls are rocky, and a beach forms only near the entrance.

Feeding was observed directly: isopods fed on bat guano deposits, the principal cave inhabitants being vampire bats. Specimens survived 40 days in captivity on this diet alone. Associated organisms included unidentified fungi and protozoans and an epizoic ostracod, *Sphaeromicola cirolanae* (Entocytheridae), identified by Horton H. Hobbs Jr.

Distribution is known only from the type locality. The authors note that the surrounding karst is fractured and cavernous, so the species may occur more widely in the region.

**Related species and biogeography:**
- [[Speocirolana thermydronis]] (Cole & Minckley, 1966): Cuatro Ciénegas, Coahuila; lives in springs and lacks setae on pleopod endopodites I and II.
- [[Speocirolana bolivari]] (Rioja, 1953): Grutas de Quintero, Tamaulipas.
- [[Speocirolana pelaezi]] (Bolívar, 1950): Gruta Los Sabinos, Valles, San Luis Potosí; often sympatric with *S. bolivari*.
- "[[Conilera stygia]]" Packard (1900): Monterrey wells, not recollected; the authors consider it probably a *Speocirolana* and a nomen dubium.

## Key Findings

- *Speocirolana guerrai* is described as new from 48 specimens, distinguished by pereiopod meropod spination, maxilliped palp spines, uropod spination, and sexually dimorphic uropods.
- Its closest morphological resemblances are to *S. bolivari* (pereiopod propodite spination, antennal size, coupling hook number) and *S. pelaezi* (antennal segment proportions, head shape, rounded telson). It is the only congener with a subapical internal spine on each of the first three meropods combined with the uropod and palp traits described.
- Flagellar article counts in the antennula and antenna correlate with body length; the authors caution that these counts may be less reliable as diagnostic characters.
- The species feeds on bat guano and survived 40 days in captivity on that diet.
- Origin of the genus is likely pre-Tertiary. Of three hypotheses, the authors favor a coastal origin (Jurassic–Cretaceous coastline) or a Cretaceous coastal origin linked to the uplift of the Sierra Madre Oriental, and reject overland migration from the sea via a terrestrial intermediary.
- The authors reaffirm that the genus has a marine origin, which is consistent with the broader Cirolanidae pattern of freshwater and cave colonizations around the Gulf of Mexico, Cuba, and the Mediterranean.
- The species is named in honor of Adán F. Guerra, who financed the collections.
