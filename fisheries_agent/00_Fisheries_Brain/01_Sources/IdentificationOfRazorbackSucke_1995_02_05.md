## Executive Summary
This document is a correspondence report (dated 2 February 1995) from the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] at Colorado State University, detailing the taxonomic identification of seven fish larvae specimens collected from the [[Colorado River]] near River Mile 58 in Mohave County, Arizona. The specimens were collected on 21 February 1995. Of the seven larvae examined, six were confidently identified as [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), while the largest specimen (18 mm TL) was tentatively identified as razorback sucker but could not be morphologically distinguished with certainty from [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*). The report highlights the diagnostic challenges of larval fish identification within the family [[Catostomidae]] and notes the potential value of the specimens as a reference series for larval developmental staging.

## Background & Description
The collection consisted of seven larval specimens at two distinct developmental stages, classified according to standard larval fish ontogeny terminology:

- **Flexion Mesolarva** (n=1): One specimen, measuring **12 mm TL**, tentatively classified as flexion mesolarva. Identification stage was uncertain (denoted with "?") due to tail damage and absence of yolk.
  - *Definition:* Larvae in which the end of the notochord is flexing upward, with some but not all principal caudal fin rays clearly present.

- **Postflexion Mesolarvae** (n=6): Six specimens measuring **13, 13, 14, 15, 16, and 18 mm TL**.
  - *Definition:* Larvae in which the notochord end is fully flexed and all principal caudal fin rays are present, though dorsal and/or anal fin rays remain incomplete.

**Diagnostic Notes:**
- The six smaller postflexion specimens (13–16 mm TL) were identified with certainty as *Xyrauchen texanus* based on distinguishing morphological characters.
- The largest specimen (18 mm TL) presented ambiguous morphological characters, preventing definitive discrimination between [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] at this size/stage. This reflects a known challenge in larval catostomid taxonomy, where morphological characters separating congeneric/confamilial species can converge or remain undifferentiated at certain larval sizes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Specimens were collected from the mainstem [[Colorado River]] at River Mile 58, within Mohave County, Arizona — a reach relevant to the historic range of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Species Co-occurrence:** The identification uncertainty for the largest specimen hinges on whether [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] is known to spawn or occur (or occurs within a reasonable upstream distance) in this reach of the Colorado River. The author notes that if flannelmouth sucker is not possible or likely in this reach, the identification of the 18-mm specimen as razorback sucker would be considered certain, underscoring the importance of local species distribution data in resolving larval identification ambiguities.
- **Life History Context:** The collection date (late February) and larval sizes (12–18 mm TL) are consistent with early spring spawning and larval drift/development periods documented for [[Razorback Sucker]] populations in the Colorado River Basin.

## Key Findings
- 6 of 7 larvae (13–16 mm TL, postflexion mesolarvae) were confidently identified as **razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**.
- 1 specimen (18 mm TL) was likely razorback sucker but could not be conclusively distinguished from **flannelmouth sucker (*Catostomus latipinnis*)** using morphological characters alone.
- 1 specimen (12 mm TL) was tentatively staged as a flexion mesolarva, though its condition (damaged tail, no yolk) limited definitive staging.
- Species-level identification confidence for the largest specimen is contingent upon regional distributional data — specifically the known presence/absence of [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] in or near the collection reach.
- The specimen series spans a useful size range (12–18 mm TL) and was proposed as a potential **reference series** for the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]]'s catalogued collection, pending disposition decisions by the requesting party.
- This case exemplifies broader difficulties in **larval catostomid identification**, particularly when sympatric or parapatric congeners/confamilials exhibit overlapping morphological traits during early developmental stages.
