# Minutes of the February 21, 1989 Recovery Implementation Committee Meeting

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-colorado-fishes

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum transmits the minutes of the February 21, 1989 meeting of the [[Recovery Implementation Committee]], which oversees the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for four endangered or rare fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The meeting covered the FY-89 work plan and budget, the FY-90 budget adjustment, [[Windy Gap Funds]], a cooperative agreement with the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]], [[Section 7 Consultation]] issues (including [[Sediment Impacts]] and "sufficient progress"), instream flow work on the [[Yampa River]] and [[15-Mile Reach]], water rights acquisition, and the supporting resolutions of environmental groups. Attachments include budget tables, a schedule, a depletion-charge table, draft letters, cooperative agreement versions and a draft resolution honoring [[Clifford Barrett]].

## Background & Description

The Recovery Implementation Program is a 15-year effort (term ending January 22, 2003) to recover the endangered fishes while water development proceeds in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It was formalized by a Cooperative Agreement executed January 21–22, 1988 by the Secretary of the Interior, the Western Area Power Administration Administrator and the Governors of Colorado, Utah and Wyoming. The Program is overseen by the Implementation Committee, supported by a [[Management Group]] and a [[Technical Group]].

The minutes were circulated by John Hamill, Program Director, FWS Region 6, dated April 26, 1989. Attendance is listed in Attachment 1 (roughly two dozen named participants from FWS, [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the three states, water users and environmental groups; the document marks Recovery Implementation Committee members with an asterisk).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Listed status:** The [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] are federally listed as endangered. The [[Razorback Sucker]] was a candidate for listing and, per Attachment 1 comments, was not protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and had no recovery plan.
- **Key habitats:** Flow needs were central to the discussion for the [[Yampa River]], the [[15-Mile Reach]] (Grand Valley), the [[Little Snake River]], the [[San Juan River]] and the [[Grand Canyon]] humpback chub habitat, plus releases from [[Flaming Gorge]] and [[Ruedi Reservoir]].
- **Squawfish range:** The draft recovery plan notes the White River was impounded by Taylor Draw Dam (1984) above Rangely, Colorado. Recent studies indicate the San Juan River supports one of only three remaining natural Colorado squawfish populations.
- **Threats discussed:** Depletions from water projects, sediment reduction from upstream dams, fragmentation of habitat, and nonnative fish management (exotic fish issue paper, creel surveys).
- **Water rights:** Instream flows require legal protection. Attachment 14 states that permanent protection is best achieved through original appropriation or fee purchase rather than lease.

## Key Findings

**1. Budget and work plan**
- The FY-89 work plan approved in August 1988 was $2,303.2K. The revised plan (January 12, 1989) totals $2,324.7K.
- The final FY-89 requests exceeded available funds by about $268,000 because of higher project requests and a shortfall in FWS cash contributions.
- Deferred in FY-89: habitat development options ($10.0K), bonytail retrieval ($2.0K), exotic fish issue paper ($10.0K), establishment of recovery goals ($30.0K) and razorback management plan ($10.0K). Surplus funds, if any, will go first to the razorback plan and then to recovery goals.
- Major increases: FWS program management ($70.0K to $157.0K); Yampa River flow recommendations ($72.5K to $140.5K); Little Snake sediment dynamics ($23.0K to $44.6K).
- Colorado River flow recommendations were reduced ($176.5K to $133.0K) to focus on the 15-Mile Reach.
- Drought-related changes at Flaming Gorge delayed research; the draft biological opinion is now due 3/15/90.

**2. FY-90 budget adjustment**
- Under Section 5.2 of the Program document, the FY-90 budget rises 4.1% based on the 1988 Consumer Price Index, effective October 1, 1989.
- The depletion charge rises from $10.00 to $10.41 per acre-foot.
- Contributions for FY-90 (1988 → 1989): Reclamation $1,500,000 → $1,561,500; Service $600,000 → $624,000; Colorado $104,000 → $108,300; Utah $73,000 → $76,000; Wyoming $23,000 → $24,000.
- The FY-90 work plan totals $2,825.5K (Attachment 4 table, dated July 6, 1989), with Reclamation at $1,561.2K and FWS at $623.9K.

**3. Federal funding request**
- Governor Roy Romer's draft letter to Secretary Manuel Lujan requests $630,000 for the FWS in FY-90: $330,000 base funding plus $300,000 under Section 6 of the ESA.
- Participants planned a Washington, D.C. briefing in early March 1989.

**4. Windy Gap funds**
- Since 1981, nearly 60 biological opinions have produced Windy Gap contributions. A total of $762,000 has been spent; $553,000 is currently available; about $2.98 million is outstanding, though much may never be received because projects may not be built.
- The FWS will use the funds only for endangered fish recovery, honoring conditions in each opinion. Discretionary funds will be allocated after consulting the Implementation Committee, but the FWS keeps final authority. These funds will not count toward the FWS's $600,000/year contribution.

**5. Cooperative agreement with the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation**
- Three draft versions were reviewed, differing in which funds transfer to the Foundation (existing Section 7 funds, new Section 7 funds, or Congressional water rights appropriations).
- The Foundation's reimbursement for expenses is capped at 2% of funds received annually.
- The total Program cost is estimated at $58.5 million over 15 years, with water project proponents contributing about $9–10 million.

**6. Section 7 consultations and sediment impacts**
- About two dozen Section 7 consultations involving water depletions are listed in Attachment 11, including Flaming Gorge operations, Sandstone Reservoir, Muddy Creek and several coal mines.
- A Management Group meeting (February 9, 1989) concluded that sediment reductions from water projects are a depletion impact, though no conclusive record showed how sediment was handled when the Program was developed.
- The Implementation Committee adopted clarifying language (Attachment 13) and directed the Management Group to continue work on defining "sufficient progress" and a process to ensure depletion impacts are addressed.
- Sandstone Reservoir offsetting measures will be negotiated with Wyoming.

**7. San Juan River and New Mexico**
- Reclamation will fund New Mexico's field studies through FY-90, at a cost not to exceed $20,000.
- The San Juan River management plan was deferred from March 1, 1989 to September 30, 1989.

**8. Humpback chub studies in Grand Canyon**
- The Committee was split on whether to credit Program funds for Grand Canyon humpback chub conservation against Reclamation's $1.5 million/year contribution. The Service and Reclamation will report back.

**9. Instream flows and water rights**
- **Yampa River:** The biological report is due 5/1/89. The Service will develop provisional flow estimates by the end of FY-89, provided this does not interfere with the Flaming Gorge consultation. The Colorado River Water Conservation District will evaluate alternative reservoir sites in lieu of the [[Juniper-Cross Mountain Water Rights]].
- **15-Mile Reach:** The final flow report for July–September is due 5/1/89. Ruedi releases will be delivered to the Orchard Mesa power plant, with a Reclamation contract pending.
- **Lease vs. purchase:** The Service prefers purchase because leases give no permanent legal protection; locals prefer a 50-year lease with renewal option.
- **Little Snake River:** Wyoming's discussions with The Nature Conservancy and appropriators remain conceptual.
- **Colorado Water Conservation Board:** Bill McDonald's concern that piecemeal appropriation of instream rights could preclude full compact development was raised, but no resolution was reached.

**10. Environmental group support**
- Supporting resolutions with conditions came from the National Audubon Society (Dr. Carse Pustmueller nominated as committee representative), Environmental Defense Fund and Colorado Wildlife Federation.
- Wyoming Wildlife Federation was considering a resolution; Utah Wildlife Federation and National Wildlife Federation declined.
- Trout Unlimited will not sign until the Ruedi trout fishery impacts are resolved.
- The Nature Conservancy will not formally participate but will assist with real estate transactions.
- All conditioned resolutions withhold support for no-jeopardy biological opinions until water rights are secured, and condition hatchery funding on demonstrated need and genetic safeguards.

**11. Colorado squawfish recovery plan conflict**
- Tom Pitts argued that the draft recovery goals conflict with Program goals on essential reaches and on fragmentation threats. The Service agreed not to finalize the plan until the Management Group weighs in.

**12. Other items**
- The Implementation Committee unanimously adopted a resolution honoring Clifford Barrett, retiring Regional Director of Reclamation's Upper Colorado Region.
- Reclamation will offer a rafting trip (maximum 25 people) from Moab, Utah through Cataract Canyon to Lake Powell in July or August 1989.
- A 5-year information and education plan is in preparation.
- Dr. Holt Williamson was hired to direct the endangered fish propagation and genetics plan.
- The next Implementation Committee meeting is set for August 30, 1989, in Lakewood, Colorado.
