# Razorback Sucker Studies on Lake Mead, Nevada: 1998-1999 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This annual report (January 2000) covers the third year (July 1998 to June 1999) of a cooperative study of the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) population in [[Lake Mead]], Nevada-Arizona. The study was funded by the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]] (SNWA) and the [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]], with contractor [[BIO/WEST, Inc.]] conducting fieldwork, the [[Nevada Department of Wildlife]] (NDOW) providing equipment and support, and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (BOR) adding funding tied to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) biological opinion on Lower Colorado River operations.

Emphasis shifted from trammel netting and sonic telemetry toward larval sampling, juvenile sampling, and aging techniques. Adult trammel netting was reduced to avoid stressing spawning fish. Spawning activity was documented on video at [[Echo Bay]] in March 1999, but aerial surveys found no aggregations. Lakewide larval sampling located only two new larvae, indicating no new spawning concentrations had been identified.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is a large, endemic catostomid of the Colorado River Basin that has declined sharply from historic levels and is listed as endangered. Its decline is linked to dam construction, reservoir habitat replacing riverine conditions, and predation by nonnative fishes (particularly catfish, sunfish, and bass) on larvae and juveniles. Lake Mead historically supported the largest known population, but adult captures from the 1990s raised questions about population size, recruitment, and age structure.

Study objectives were to:
1. Determine the population size of razorback sucker in Lake Mead.
2. Determine habitat use and life history characteristics of the Lake Mead population.
3. Determine use and habitat of known spawning locations.
4. Search for new populations via larval light-trapping (added by the BOR).
5. Enhance juvenile sampling at both established study sites (added by the BOR).

Two spawning areas are recognized: [[Echo Bay]] (Overton Arm) and [[Blackbird Point]] (Las Vegas Bay).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water levels:** Lake level rose to about 1,216 ft amsl in October 1998 and receded to about 1,206 ft amsl by June 1999, against a full pool of 1,229 ft. Higher water inundated tamarisk and other shoreline vegetation.

**Las Vegas Wash and Bay:** The confluence of [[Las Vegas Wash]] and [[Las Vegas Bay]] shifts with lake level. The flowing portion of the bay is short (about 200 to 300 yards). After mid-1998, a mudflat formed at the lake interface, blocking boat access up the wash.

**Predation pressure:** In fyke nets, all 946 fish captured were nonnative. Young green sunfish (67%) and largemouth bass (27%) dominated, and are believed to be among the primary predators of young razorback sucker.

**Growth environment:** Las Vegas Bay fish showed higher growth than Echo Bay fish, attributed to greater food production in Las Vegas Bay.

**Population estimates from earlier years:** Echo Bay fewer than 100 fish; Las Vegas Bay 200 to 400 fish. No new estimate was made in 1998-1999 due to reduced netting.

## Key Findings

**Adult sampling and captures**
- 92 trammel net nights were used: 50 in Las Vegas Bay/Boulder Basin and 42 in Echo Bay/Overton Arm.
- 5 razorback sucker were captured in Las Vegas Bay and 20 in Echo Bay during the year.
- Of the fish captured, 14 were recaptures, a recapture rate similar to the previous study year.
- Across the first 3 years, 95 individual razorback sucker were captured (excluding larvae).
- Catch rate was about 0.10 per net night in Las Vegas Bay and 0.48 per net night in Echo Bay.

**Spawning**
- Video from Echo Bay in March 1999 recorded a group of six or seven fish over 3 days, including egg deposition, fertilization, and predation on eggs by catfish.
- Aerial spawning surveys with the BOR helicopter in March 1999 located no razorback sucker.

**Larval sampling**
- Larvae were collected at Las Vegas Bay (slightly lower than 1998, catch rate 0.10 per minute) and at Echo Bay (much reduced, catch rate 0.08 per minute, continuing a downward trend).
- Highest single-night catch was 21 larvae near the Las Vegas Wash inflow (CPM 1.4, March 30, 1999).
- Lakewide sampling covered 155 of 220 identified sites. Only two new larvae were found: at Swallow Bay (March 13, 1999) and at north Rogers Bay (March 11, 1999). No additional larvae were found nearby.

**Juvenile sampling**
- No juvenile razorback sucker were collected, despite 44 fyke net nights at Echo Bay and 16 at Las Vegas Bay, plus gill netting.

**Telemetry**
- Sonic-tagged fish were usually within about 200 yards of shore, in water less than 60 feet deep, and often stationary near the bottom.
- Echo Bay and Blackbird Point fish did not overlap in distribution in any study year.
- Echo Bay and Blackbird Point are the only known spawning areas in Lake Mead.
- Tag losses and expirations were noted. One fish (tag 365) appears to have died after trammel netting stress.

**Growth**
- Mean annual growth of recaptured fish at Echo Bay was 7.0 mm, down from 10.7 mm in the previous year but still higher than wild populations in Lake Mohave and the Green River.
- A fish captured as a larva in 1997 grew about 16.8 mm per year.

**Aging**
- Pectoral fin ray sections produced ages consistent with otolith ages in preliminary tests on captive fish. Fin ray removal caused no apparent harm to the fish.

**Recommendations for Year Four**
1. Continue focused trammel and fyke netting at Echo Bay and Las Vegas Bay to locate juvenile razorback sucker.
2. Continue sonic tracking of tagged fish.
3. Monitor the two spawning areas for larval production.
4. Continue searching new areas of the lake for larvae, with trammel netting and sonic tagging if new concentrations are found.
5. Age all trammel-netted fish using pectoral fin rays, pending USFWS approval.
6. Sample adults and larvae in the flowing portion of Las Vegas Bay and Las Vegas Wash, including a boat for wash access, monthly trammel netting, weekly light trapping, and drift netting from February 15 to May 15, 2000.
7. Use weekly video surveillance at Echo Bay and Las Vegas Bay.
