# Information Letter No. 10 — Lower Colorado River Drainage System Endangered Fishes Recovery Team

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #FreshwaterConservation

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative communication from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife) dated July 25, 1973, addressed to members of the [[Lower Colorado River Drainage System Endangered Fishes Recovery Team]]. It announces an upcoming recovery team meeting at the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] and provides a status update on an interstate brood stock acquisition operation for the [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), a species central to early Colorado River native fish conservation efforts. The letter also references parallel recovery planning for the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). While not a primary scientific data paper, it documents contemporaneous field survey, propagation, and interagency coordination activities critical to the historical recovery record of these two endangered fishes.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological data (e.g., measurements, meristics, coloration) are provided in this letter. The document is purely administrative/operational in nature. Species referenced:
- **Colorado Squawfish** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — largest North American cyprinid, historically top piscivorous predator of the Colorado River Basin; subject of active brood stock collection (~20 individuals) for captive propagation.
- **Razorback Sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — distinguished by a pronounced dorsal keel behind the head; mentioned only in context of recovery planning, no descriptive data given.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat referenced**: [[Lake Mohave]] and the [[Grand Canyon]] segment of the Colorado River are identified as priority survey areas for native fish populations.
- **Geographic scope**: Operations span multiple jurisdictions — Colorado (source population, Grand Junction), Nevada (transit/stopover at Boulder City), and California (executing agency, California Department of Fish and Game aircraft transport).
- **Species interactions/context**: The Colorado Squawfish and Razorback Sucker are both endemic to the Colorado River Basin and were, by 1973, already recognized as imperiled, prompting coordinated interstate recovery action — an early example of multi-agency endangered species management prior to the full implementation of the U.S. Endangered Species Act (1973).
- **Hatchery role**: The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] serves as a regional hub for propagation and culture efforts for the [[Colorado Squawfish]].

## Key Findings
- A Recovery Team meeting was scheduled for **August 28, 1973**, at Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery, with an agenda covering:
  1. Survey of [[Lake Mohave]] and the [[Grand Canyon]]
  2. Culture and propagation of the [[Colorado Squawfish]]
  3. Publicity for the squawfish conservation operation
  4. Recovery plans for the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- An active **brood stock acquisition operation** was underway: the California Department of Fish and Game planned to fly to Grand Junction, Colorado, on **July 27, 1973**, to collect approximately **20 Colorado Squawfish** for transport to Boulder City, Nevada.
- Interagency coordination included formal notification to the **Nevada Department of Fish and Game** (regarding the Boulder City stopover) and a pending approval request to the **Colorado Division of Wildlife**, highlighting the multi-state regulatory cooperation required for endangered species translocation.
- The letter reflects an early, foundational stage of organized recovery planning for two now well-documented endangered Colorado River endemics, predating many later formalized recovery plans.
