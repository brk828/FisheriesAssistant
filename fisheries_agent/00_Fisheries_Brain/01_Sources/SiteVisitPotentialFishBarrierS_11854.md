# Site Visit Report: Potential Fish Barrier Sites on Tule Creek

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Poeciliidae #Cyprinidae #fish-barriers

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents a May 3, 1990 site visit by biologists from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and [[Bureau of Reclamation]] to evaluate four potential fish barrier sites on [[Tule Creek]], [[Yavapai County]], Arizona. The group reached consensus that Site A (T.7N., R.1E., NE1/4 Sec. 10) was the most suitable location from a biological perspective. The barrier construction is required to comply with conservation recommendation number 6 in the Plan 6 [[Central Arizona Water Control Study]] Biological Opinion dated March 8, 1983. The site supports populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Longfin Dace]], both native species requiring protection from non-native fish invasion.

## Background & Description

Four potential sites for fish barrier construction on [[Tule Creek]] had been previously identified in the December 29, 1989 "Tule Creek Fish Barrier Progress Report." The purpose of the barrier is to prevent upstream movement of non-native fishes and protect native fish populations in the upper reaches of the creek. Site A features an existing 4+ foot drop that likely already functions as a partial fish barrier at approximate elevation 1718 feet.

[[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*, family [[Poeciliidae]]) and [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) inhabit watered areas of the creek during at least some years. Both species were observed below Site D during the May 1990 site visit.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish barrier project addresses the need to protect native fish populations from invasion by non-native species in [[Tule Creek]]. Sites C and D were located upstream from some watered areas that support native fish populations, making them less desirable from a conservation standpoint. Site A's downstream position provides protection for a larger area of native fish habitat.

The creek experiences variable flow conditions, with some areas supporting fish populations only during certain years. The barrier site selection must account for both biological effectiveness and hydrological considerations, including flood risk from the upstream dam structure.

## Key Findings

**Site Selection Consensus**: Site A (T.7N., R.1E., NE1/4 Sec. 10) was identified as the best of four potential barrier sites through group consensus of participating biologists.

**Biological Advantages of Site A**:
- Furthest downstream location among the four sites
- Protects maximum area of native fish habitat
- Sites C and D are upstream from watered areas supporting [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Longfin Dace]]
- Existing 4+ foot drop already functions as a partial barrier
- Should require minimal modification to ensure complete prevention of upstream fish movement

**Flood Risk Assessment**: Site A is located below the maximum flood inundation level at approximate elevation 1718 feet. Inundation would only occur during floods slightly above the predicted 1 in 500 year level (predicted elevation 1711.3 feet + 1 foot). The assessment notes uncertainty about whether the predicted flood elevation accounts for the "fuse plug" feature of the dam.

**Risk Evaluation**: The biologists concluded that risks from potential inundation of the barrier at Site A are smaller than other project risks and should not eliminate Site A from consideration for barrier construction.

**Species Observations**: Both [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Longfin Dace]] were present below Site D during the May 3, 1990 site visit, confirming active native fish populations in the area.
