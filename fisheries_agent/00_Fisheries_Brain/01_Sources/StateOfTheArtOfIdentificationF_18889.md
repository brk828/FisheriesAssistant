# State of the Art of Identification for Cyprinid Fish Larvae from Eastern North America

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This state-of-the-art review compiles the known morphological characters of cyprinid larvae in eastern North America. Larval morphology is documented for 62 species in 22 genera, out of 179 species in 25 genera recorded in the study area. The authors group species by four diagnostic characters: relative [[Preanal Length]], [[Eye Shape]], [[Preanal Myomere Number]], and [[Ventral Pigmentation]]. The paper is explicitly not a taxonomic key, because larvae of roughly two-thirds of the species remain unknown. It also flags literature errors, identifies taxa needing description, and discusses practical identification aids such as spawning season, syntopy, and age.

## Background & Description

Identifying North American cyprinids is difficult at every life stage because of the large number of species and their morphological similarity. The problem is greater for larvae, which show even less differentiation, have sparse comparative literature, and contain misidentifications in older publications. The scope is limited to species east of the Continental Divide. [[Robins et al. 1980]] list 221 cyprinid species in 43 genera for the United States and Canada. Of these, 179 species in 25 genera occur in the eastern study area, which represents 78% of species and 58% of genera.

Species are discussed in groups defined by character states, and a species appears only under the first group that describes it. Lengths are reported as total length (TL). Preanal myomeres are counted as all segments anterior to a vertical line through the anus. Myomere counts from [[Fish 1932]] were excluded because they conflicted with later studies and adult vertebra counts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Several ecological points bear on identification:

- **Distribution and syntopy:** Many species have non-overlapping ranges, and species with overlapping ranges may not occur together. Both conditions can narrow the candidate list.
- **Spawning season:** This is a key clue to which larvae should be present at a given time and place.
- **Age and size:** Where spawning seasons are consecutive, larval age, estimated from size, developmental stage, or otolith rings, helps separate taxa.
- **Habitat:** [[Couesius Plumbeus]] is usually lacustrine and enters stream mouths only to spawn. In Louisiana, [[Hybognathus nuchalis]] larvae are restricted to marginal floodplain areas where [[Ictiobus]] and [[Notemigonus crysoleucas]] larvae are also abundant. [[Notropis blennius]] is riverine, while [[Notropis longirostris]] prefers upland creeks.
- **Conservation:** Several species, including Gila nigrescens, Hybopsis cahni, Notropis calliatenia, and Phoxinus cumberlandensis, were listed as endangered or threatened in 1979. Larval descriptions are needed for their reproductive and early-life requirements.
- **Non-native species:** All species in the high-preanal-length group are Eurasian introductions.

## Key Findings

**1. Four diagnostic character groups.** Nearly all North American cyprinid larvae have a relative preanal length of 45–65% (usually 55–62%). Five Eurasian introductions exceed this range, at 65–74%. Allometry lowers this value with growth, which eases overlap at 65%. The other three characters are eye shape (flattened or rounded), preanal myomere number (ranging from about 18 to 33), and midventral or other pigment pattern.

**2. Species groups.** Table 1 assigns species to groups:
- *High preanal length:* 5 species, including [[Ctenopharyngodon idella]], [[Carassius auratus]], [[Cyprinus carpio]], [[Tinca tinca]], and [[Rhodeus sericeus]].
- *Flattened eye:* about 15 species, including [[Hybopsis aestivalis]], [[Hybopsis storeriana]], [[Notropis emiliae]], and [[Pimephales notatus]].
- *High preanal myomere number (>25):* about 13 species, including [[Semotilus corporalis]], [[Semotilus atromaculatus]], [[Campostoma anomalum]], and [[Couesius plumbeus]].
- *Midventral stripe:* about 14 species, including [[Notemigonus crysoleucas]], [[Notropis atherinoides]], and [[Pimephales promelas]].
- *Scattered breast melanophores:* about 7 species.
- *Outlined gut:* about 12 species, including [[Exoglossum maxillingua]] and [[Rhinichthys atratulus]].
- *Unassigned:* 7 species.

**3. Key diagnostic examples.**
- *Ctenopharyngodon idella* has 30–33 preanal myomeres, the highest count in the group.
- *Carassius auratus* has 17–20 whole preanal myomeres, while *Cyprinus carpio* has 21–24. The dorsal branch of the pigment "Y" in *C. auratus* extends beyond the auditory vesicle, while in *C. carpio* it ends at the vesicle's anterior edge.
- *Rhinichthys atratulus* usually has 25 preanal myomeres and a terminal mouth, while *R. cataractae* usually has 26 and a subterminal to inferior mouth.
- *Notropis chalybaeus* has a single dorsal stripe of melanophores, unlike the two rows in most cyprinids, and 19–20 preanal myomeres.
- *Notemigonus crysoleucas* is among the smallest cyprinid larvae (hatch 3.0 mm) and has a superior mouth by 6 mm.

**4. Literature errors identified.** The authors list several misidentifications in [[Fish 1932]]:
- The 7.5-mm "Cyprinus carpio" was actually *Moxostoma* (*M. aureolum*).
- Only the 13.3-mm "C. carpio" description is likely *C. carpio*; the rest probably relate to *C. auratus*.
- Stages described as *Erinemus storerianus* are a darter (Percidae), except the 21-mm juvenile.
- The trident-pattern larva attributed to *Notropis cornutus chrysocephalus* is probably a percid, perhaps *Etheostoma* sp.
- The 5.0-mm "Notropis hudsonius" may be a percid.

Other errors include Hogue et al.'s (1976) low myomere counts (34+), which conflict with vertebra counts of 38–40, and the misidentification of the 6.5-, 7.6-, and 8.7-mm specimens reproduced by Taber (1969) as *Pimephales vigilax*.

**5. Taxa needing description.** Larvae are unknown for about two-thirds of the species. Three genera (*Dionda*, *Ericymba*, *Hemitremia*) lack any larval descriptions. Common, widespread species such as *Hybognathus placitus*, *Notropis buchanani*, *Notropis heterolepis*, *Notropis umbratilis*, *Phoxinus erythrogaster*, and *Semotilus margarita* also need descriptions for community management.

**6. Character reliability.** Myomere counts are the most useful meristic character, and total myomere number can be estimated from adults as total vertebrae (including four Weberian vertebrae) minus one. Pigment patterns are useful, but intensity varies greatly, with wild-caught larvae often pale and laboratory-reared larvae dark. Melanophore counts are seldom diagnostic. Ventral pattern is consistent within species but can change ontogenetically. Size at hatching, yolk absorption, and pelvic-bud formation is used throughout the diagnoses, but size data for many other stages are missing.

**7. Scope and purpose.** The authors present current diagnoses to aid identification and future descriptive work. They explicitly state that the diagnoses may change as the remaining species are described, and that a taxonomic key would be premature.
