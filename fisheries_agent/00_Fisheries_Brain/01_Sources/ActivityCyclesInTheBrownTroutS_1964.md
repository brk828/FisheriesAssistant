# Activity Cycles in the Brown Trout (Salmo trutta L.) — 2. Fish Artificially Fed

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #freshwater-ecology #fish-behavior

## Executive Summary

This 1964 study by D. R. Swift (Freshwater Biological Association) investigates the diurnal and annual locomotory activity cycles of four [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) confined in individual netting cages on the bed of [[Windermere]]. Unlike a prior companion study (Swift, 1962) examining underfed, naturally-feeding trout, this experiment tested **artificially fed** fish under two regimes: irregular once-daily feeding (Fish 5, 6) and automated feeding every 2 hours, day and night (Fish 3, 4). Despite differing feeding regimes, all four fish exhibited strikingly similar diurnal and annual activity patterns — characterized by a sharp activity increase at dawn, sustained activity through daylight, and a rapid decline at dusk. The consistency of these patterns across all three feeding conditions (underfed, once-daily, and continuous 2-hr feeding) strongly suggests that **light, not food availability**, is the primary exogenous stimulus governing locomotory activity in brown trout. This supports the interpretation that trout feed because they are active, rather than becoming active because they are feeding — inverting the previously assumed causal relationship in fish behavioral ecology.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Salmo trutta* L. (Brown Trout), Family [[Salmonidae]]
- **Source population:** Hatchery-reared trout (selected because they more readily accept prepared liver feed vs. wild-caught fish)
- **Size range at start:** 20–26 cm total length
- **Growth outcome:** Average length increase of **30.7%** over the ~9.3-month experimental period
- **Condition:** Fish were reported in "excellent condition" at experiment's end, contrasting with the poorer condition of underfed fish in the previous (1962) study
- **Sex determination:** Only discovered post-experiment; Fish 6 was found to be the sole female in the group, later linked to divergent autumn activity patterns (possible sexual maturation effect)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Benthic zone of a temperate lake ([[Windermere]]), at depths of 1.8–2.4 m (6–8 ft)
- **Confinement apparatus:** Netting cages, 2.4 m (8 ft) long × 0.6 m (2 ft) cross-section, elevated 0.6 m (2 ft) above the lake bed on wooden legs
- **Feeding ecology (experimental):**
  - Fish 5 & 6: fed irregular times daily (except Sundays) with minced beef liver set in gelatine, delivered via surface tube
  - Fish 3 & 4: fed automatically every 2 hours (day and night) starting May 11, via automatic feeding device
- **Environmental monitoring:** Daily water temperature (thermistor); total solar radiation via moll-thermopile (10-day averages)
- **Comparative context:** Contrasted against [[Brown Trout Natural Feeding Activity]] (Swift, 1962), where fish were underfed and dependent on invertebrates passing through cage netting
- **Broader taxonomic comparison:** Similar dawn-activity diurnal rhythms documented across diverse fish taxa and feeding ecologies, including:
  - [[Roach]] (*Rutilus rutilus*), [[Bream]] (*Abramis brama*), [[Perch]] (*Perca fluviatilis*) — Alabaster & Robertson, 1961
  - [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*) — Hirata, 1957; Hirata & Kobayashi, 1956
  - [[Baikal Grayling]] (*Thymallus arcticus baicalensis*) — Oliphan, 1951
  - Bleak, Prussian carp, tench, ruff, burbot — Kuchin, 1900 (via Oliphan)
  - [[Herring]] (*Clupea harengus*) — Musinic, 1931; Sushkina, 1939

## Key Findings

- **Diurnal activity pattern:** All four fish showed a sharp, synchronized rise in locomotory activity at dawn, sustained activity throughout daylight hours, and a rapid decline at dusk — regardless of feeding schedule.
- **No feeding-cue effect:** The 2-hourly automated feeding regime (Fish 3, 4) produced **no detectable disruption or modulation** of the diurnal activity pattern relative to once-daily fed fish (Fish 5, 6), indicating food presentation does not drive short-term activity bursts.
- **Seasonal/annual pattern:** Activity was lowest in winter, rising through May to a peak in June, dipping in July, and rising again in August — consistent across three of four fish.
- **Sex-linked deviation:** Fish 6 (the only female, identified post hoc) deviated from the August activity peak seen in the other three fish; this autumn activity anomaly is hypothesized to relate to **sexual maturation**.
- **Onset of rhythmicity:** During January–March, fish activity appeared largely random across the 24-hr cycle; the dawn-triggered diurnal rhythm first emerged in March (Fish 3) and was subsequently exhibited by all fish for the remainder of the experiment.
- **No correlation with abiotic variables:** No clear relationship was found between seasonal activity variation and either water temperature or solar radiation — the mid-summer (July) activity dip in particular remains unexplained.
- **Cross-regime consistency:** Comparing this study's well-fed fish to the previous study's underfed fish (Swift, 1962) revealed **no major differences** in diurnal or annual activity patterns across three distinct feeding regimes (underfed/natural, once-daily artificial, continuous 2-hr artificial).
- **Central conclusion:** Light — via photoperiod/dawn onset — rather than food stimulus (sight/smell), is proposed as the primary driver of locomotory activity onset in brown trout. The authors reframe the feeding-activity relationship: **"The trout feeds because it is active, and is not active because it is feeding."**
- **Cross-species generality:** The dawn-activity-onset rhythm appears broadly conserved across multiple freshwater and marine fish taxa despite differing ecologies, supporting light as a universal exogenous zeitgeber for fish locomotory activity.
