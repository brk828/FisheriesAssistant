# Diagnostic Assistance - Willow Beach NFH

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #fish-health #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This memorandum documents a fish health diagnostic visit conducted May 10–12, 1995, by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service Fish Health Center (Pinetop, AZ) to Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery (NFH). The visit addressed the condition of a wild-captured adult [[Bonytail Chub]] transferred from [[Lake Mohave]] by the Parker Fishery Resource Office (FRO), as well as parasite infestations in [[Razorback Sucker]] and Bonytail Chub fingerlings held at the facility. The adult Bonytail Chub exhibited significant handling-related injuries (caudal peduncle, dorsal body, and head abrasions) attributed to poor field handling and photography practices during capture. Despite salt bath treatment (1.0% saline) initiated on-site, a follow-up update (May 19, 1995) confirmed the fish died. Concurrently, Razorback Sucker fingerlings in trough culture showed moderate-to-severe *Costia* protozoan infestations, successfully treated with a 1:4000 formalin flow bath. The report underscores critical gaps in inter-office protocol communication regarding handling of listed [[Threatened and Endangered Fish Species]] and recommends stricter capture/handling procedures for future operations.

## Background & Description

- **Bonytail Chub (adult, wild-captured)**
  - Visible **abrasions on the caudal peduncle, dorsal body, and head region** — consistent with physical trauma from capture, handling, and restraint (e.g., holding fish for photographs).
  - Overall body condition otherwise described as "stable" upon arrival at the hatchery, prior to eventual mortality.

- **Razorback Sucker fingerlings**
  - Infested with the ciliated protozoan parasite ***Costia*** (likely *Ichthyobodo* sp.) at moderate-to-severe levels in four trough replicates.
  - Fingerlings held in aquaria (separate from trough system) were **parasite-free**, suggesting a trough-specific vector or water source issue.

- **Bonytail Chub fingerlings**
  - Examined sample was **negative for parasites** — no *Costia* or other infestations noted.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Source population**: Adult Bonytail Chub were captured directly from **[[Lake Mohave]]**, a Colorado River reservoir representing one of the last remaining wild-source populations for this critically endangered cyprinid.
- **Conservation context**: Both the [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are federally listed under the Endangered Species Act as part of the **Colorado River Basin native fish recovery program**. Willow Beach NFH functions as a **captive refuge/grow-out facility** for these species, receiving wild broodstock via the Parker FRO for propagation and genetic conservation purposes.
- **Husbandry environment**: Fish were maintained in a controlled hatchery setting using saline bath treatments and monitored trough/aquaria systems, reflecting standard captive management protocols for sensitive, wild-origin individuals of threatened/endangered species.
- **Pathogen risk pathway**: The presence of *Costia* in trough-reared Razorback Sucker fingerlings—but not in aquaria-reared or Bonytail Chub fingerlings—highlights differential parasite exposure risk tied to specific culture systems within the same facility, an important consideration for [[Fish Pathogen Containment]] protocols in T&E species hatcheries.

## Key Findings

1. **Handling-Induced Trauma**: The adult Bonytail Chub's caudal, dorsal, and head abrasions were attributed to excessive handling during field capture, likely exacerbated by staff handling fish for photographs — a practice explicitly criticized as contributing to stress and eventual mortality.
2. **Treatment Protocol Administered**: A salt bath treatment was applied, escalating to a constant **1.0% saline solution**; recommendations for future captures included:
   - Minimizing handling time
   - Maintaining fish in 0.5–1.0% saline solution
   - Using **MS-222 anesthetic at 25–35 ppm** (transport only)
   - Keeping transport containers covered
3. **Mortality Outcome**: Despite treatment, the adult Bonytail Chub died (reported by Conrad Vjetiend, per May 19, 1995 update), prompting the author to note the original memo's recommendations should have been communicated more explicitly.
4. **Parasite Outbreak & Treatment Efficacy**: Razorback Sucker fingerlings in troughs had moderate-to-severe ***Costia*** infestations; a **1:4000 formalin flow treatment (1–2 hour duration)** eliminated detectable adult parasites within one hour post-treatment. Weekly prophylactic formalin treatments were recommended to prevent recurrence.
5. **Institutional/Protocol Failure**: A referenced February 17, 1994 Regional Director memorandum ("Guidelines for Biological Investigations of Threatened & Endangered Fishes") assigns the Fish Health Center responsibility for **pathogen containment, disease examination, and treatment approval** for T&E species. A communication lapse meant the Parker FRO did not follow established Bonytail Chub handling protocols, contributing to the fish's injuries and death.
6. **Recommendation for Future Captures**: The author strongly recommends that Fish Health Center personnel or an experienced fish culturist be directly involved on-site during any future Bonytail Chub capture operations, rather than relying on protocol transmission alone.
