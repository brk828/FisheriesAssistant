# A New Species of Aquatic Isopod Crustacean (Genus Asellus) from the Puebla Plateau, Central Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Asellidae #isopod #freshwater-invertebrates

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *Asellus puebla*, a new species of freshwater isopod crustacean collected from the [[Rio Cosala]] in [[Puebla]], [[Mexico]]. The discovery extends the known southern distribution limit of the genus [[Asellus]] from approximately 30° latitude to just below 20° latitude. The species was collected at 2,300 meters elevation in a clear, cool stream with aquatic vegetation and detritus. Detailed morphological descriptions and illustrations of the holotype, allotype, and paratypes are provided, with specimens deposited at the [[U.S. National Museum]], [[Nacional Coleccion de Mexico]], and [[National Museum of Canada]].

## Background & Description

*Asellus puebla* is a small freshwater isopod species. The largest male measured 10.3 mm long, and the largest ovigerous female (allotype) measured 6.5 mm long. The holotype male was 6.9 mm. Body proportions range from 2.6 to 2.8 times width. The telson length is approximately equal to width, and the eye is small, longer than broad, and black in color, reflecting violet in sunlight.

Key morphological features include:
- **Antenna 1**: 10-11 flagellar segments
- **Antenna 2**: Flagellum with approximately 72 segments, reaching to 7th pereonite when reflected
- **Mandible**: Right incisor with 4 teeth; spine row with 5-6 dentate spines distally and 11-12 plumose spines proximally
- **Maxilla 1**: Outer plate apex with 11 spines (all denticulate except 2 outer and 1 innermost) and 2 smooth subapical facial setae
- **Pereopod 1**: Male propodus palmar margin with 2-3 proximal robust spines, one large acute mid-process, and one short blunt distal process; female with 2 sharp spines at proximal corner and only the short blunt mid-palmar process
- **Pereopods 2-7**: Dactyls bearing 3, rarely 4, spines
- **Pleopod 1**: Distal segment with 16-20 smooth, relatively short setae on outer surface and apex
- **Pleopod 2** (male): Endopod narrow, almost straight with gentle lateral curve; prominent lateral apophysis; median process curving laterally

Color in life is gray and mottled with lighter underparts.

The species name "puebla" refers to the intermontane [[Puebla Plateau]] from which it was collected.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collection site was the [[Rio Cosala]] at the edge of [[San Martin Texmelucan]], [[Puebla]], [[Mexico]], visited on 18 December 1966 by Robert R. Miller and W. L. Minckley. The stream drains from the eastern slopes of the volcano [[Iztaccihuatl]] and enters the [[Rio Atoyac]] downstream, which flows through the [[Puebla Plateau]] between [[Iztaccihuatl]] and [[Popocatepetl]] on the west and [[Volcan La Malinche]] on the east, then bends south and west to flow into the [[Rio Balsas]] and ultimately to the [[Pacific Ocean]].

**Habitat characteristics at 2,300 meters elevation:**
- Clear, cool water flowing approximately 0.33 cubic meters per second
- Gently sloping, grassy banks
- Narrow rim of emergent aquatic vegetation including *Nasturtium*, *Ludwigia*, and sedges
- Bottoms of sand and mud in pools, gravel on riffles
- Substantial deposits of detritus (twigs, leaves) in all areas
- Local disturbance from laundry washing and children wading, but otherwise unmodified

**Associated fauna:**
- Isopods most abundant in aquatic plants, also present in debris on and lateral to swifter water
- Abundant insects: [[Hemiptera]], [[Ephemeroptera]], [[Trichoptera]]
- Talitrid amphipod: genus [[Hyalella]]

The presence of *A. puebla* in the westward-draining [[Rio Balsas]] system reflects dispersal from the east, as only one other epigean form (*A. tomalensis* Harford) is described from the Pacific drainage of North America.

## Key Findings

1. **Taxonomic Description**: *Asellus puebla* represents a new species of freshwater isopod crustacean with distinctive morphological features, particularly in the male pleopods and pereopod structures.

2. **Geographic Range Extension**: This discovery extends the known southern distribution of genus [[Asellus]] from approximately 30° N latitude (previously reported by Birstein, 1964) to just below 20° N latitude, representing a significant southward range extension of approximately 10 degrees latitude.

3. **Systematic Relationships**: The new species is tentatively related to the Eastern American group of species (subgenus *Conasellus* Stammer), though subgeneric classifications have been criticized. Four described epigean *Asellus* from Eastern North America show resemblance: *A. brevicauda*, *A. dentadactylus*, *A. kenki*, and *A. oculata*.

4. **Morphological Distinctions**: *A. puebla* differs from similar species in having:
   - Distal segment of first pleopod armed with relatively short, smooth setae (rather than long, plumose setae)
   - Distinctive male second pleopod structure
   - Specific dactylus characteristics on first pereopod

5. **Body Proportions**: The species appears elongate and relatively narrow in life, reminiscent of some troglobitic species. Body length:width ratios (2.6-2.8) and telson proportions indicate an epigean (surface-dwelling) form, though the eye is only slightly larger than that of *A. kenki*, a spring-dwelling species.

6. **Type Specimens**: Holotype (6.9 mm male), allotype (6.5 mm ovigerous female), and paratypes deposited at [[U.S. National Museum]], [[Nacional Coleccion de Mexico]], and [[National Museum of Canada]].

7. **Biogeographic Significance**: The occurrence in the Pacific-draining [[Rio Balsas]] system indicates eastward dispersal, contrasting with the predominantly eastern North American distribution of related species.
