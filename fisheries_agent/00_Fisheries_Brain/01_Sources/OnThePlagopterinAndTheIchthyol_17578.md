# Plagopterinae and the Ichthyology of Utah (Cope, 1874)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1874 *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society* extract, in its fish-related portion, is a taxonomic paper by [[Edward Drinker Cope]]. It describes a new subfamily, the [[Plagopterinae]] (Cyprinidae) of the [[Colorado River Basin]], with three genera: [[Plagopterus]], [[Lepidomeda]] and [[Meda]]. It also reports and describes freshwater fishes collected by the [[United States Geological and Geographical Survey West of the 100th Meridian]] in Utah, Arizona and Colorado. The extract also includes a short note on crustaceans of temporary plains pools in Kansas and Colorado. The opening section, on meteors, auroras and the solar atmosphere, is not aquatic and is summarized only briefly below.

## Background & Description

- **Meteor and aurora section (non-aquatic):** The first pages discuss luminosity of meteoric clouds, atmospheric latent heat, and the great comet of 1843 (printed "1943" in the OCR). The comet passed within sixty thousand miles of the sun's surface at about 350 miles per second at perihelion on Feb. 27. The author proposes latent heat of expansion as an explanation for unexplained brightening.
- **Authorship and collections:** The fish study is by Edward D. Cope, A.M., read before the [[American Philosophical Society]] on March 20, 1874. The collections were made under [[Lieutenant George M. Wheeler]], with [[Dr. Henry C. Yarrow]] (zoology) and A. W. Henshaw (assistant) providing much of the material.
- **Subfamily diagnosis (Plagopterinae):** The group is defined by two strong osseous dorsal fin rays, the posterior received into a groove of the anterior to form a compound defensive spine. Ventral fin rays (excluding the first two) are similarly modified into osseous, dagger-shaped spines. The author notes that the only other ossified ventral rays occur in the extinct Sauroctonidae of the Cretaceous and, among recent fishes, the internal ventral spine of *Amphacanthus*. Dentition and intestine indicate carnivorous habits.
- **Genus Plagopterus (gen. nov.):** Scaleless, with a terminal maxillary barbel, a well-developed lateral line, and an inner pharyngeal dental series of 2-4 (rather than 4-4).
- **Genus Lepidomeda (gen. nov.):** Scaled, with a lateral line, pharyngeal teeth 4-4 in the inner row, and no barbels.
- **Genus Meda:** Scaleless like *Plagopterus*, but without barbels and with pharyngeal teeth reduced to 4-4.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Endemism:** Cope states that the Plagopterinae are the only type of fishes not known from waters outside the Colorado basin.
- **Utah Lake fauna:** The fishes of the Utah basin are described as occurring in "a body of pure fresh water," whereas many lakes in the basin were alkaline or salt.
- **Beaver River and Sevier Lake:** [[Myloleucus parvanus]] and [[Clinostomus phlegethontis]] were collected in [[Beaver River]], which flows into [[Sevier Lake]], "a very alkaline body of water" in which the survey found no fishes.
- **Twin Lake, South Park, Colorado:** Situated at an elevation of 9,500 feet above sea level.
- **Harmony, Southern Utah:** [[Ceratichthys biguttatus]] was found here, an unexpected discovery extending the species' known range. The Smoky Hill River had previously been its westernmost known locality.
- **Temporary plains pools (Kansas and Colorado):** Rain creates temporary pools in surface depressions that last a few days or weeks and are dried up by the end of September. Some still support branchiopod crustaceans, worms and insect larvae. Adults come to the surface for air or live on the surface film. Cope notes the difficulty of explaining how strictly aquatic crustaceans propagate and disperse under these conditions.

## Key Findings

**New subfamily and genera**
- Plagopterinae (Cyprinidae) is proposed for three Colorado basin genera: *Plagopterus*, *Lepidomeda*, and *Meda*.
- *Plagopterus argentissimus* sp. nov.: small, silver fish. Total length given as M. 0.071. Numerous specimens from the San Luis Valley, Western Colorado. Radial formula D. II. 7; C. 19; A. I 10-9; V. 2, V 3; P. 16.
- *Lepidomeda vittata* sp. nov.: 26 scale series above the lateral line. Specimens from the Colorado Chiquito River, Arizona, collected by Dr. Newberry Jr. Described as the largest species of the group.
- *Lepidomeda jarrovii* sp. nov.: more elongate and weakly scaled than *L. vittata*. Specimens from the Colorado Chiquito River, Arizona. Named for Dr. Yarrow.
- *Meda fulgida* (Girard): cited from Girard's account; Cope questions the accuracy of Girard's figure. *Meda* is known from the Rio San Pedro, a Gila tributary in Southern Arizona.

**Utah Lake fauna (twelve species recorded)**
- Salmonidae: *Salmo virginalis* (Girard).
- Coregonidae: *Coregonus williamsonii* (Girard).
- Cyprinidae: *Siboma atraria* (Girard); *Alburnellus* sp. nov. (scales i, anal radii I. 8-7, teeth 2.4-4.2); *Alburnellus rhinichthyoides* (Cope), abundant at Provo; *Clinostomus hydrophlox* (Cope), abundant; *Clinostomus tenia* sp. nov., from Provo; *Hybopsis timpanogensis* sp. nov.; *Rhinichthys henshawii* sp. nov., from Provo (No. 48a).
- Catostomidae: *Minomus platyrhynchus* sp. nov., with total length M. 0.168 and several specimens near Provo; *Minomus jarrovii* sp. nov., two specimens (204a) from Provo; *Catostomus* ? *generosus* (Girard), with specimens of 2.5 lb from Provo.
- The largest Cyprinidae specimens from Utah Lake weighed one and two pounds.

**Other localities**
- *Ceratichthys biguttatus* (Kirtland): abundant at Harmony, Southern Utah.
- *Ceratichthys ventricosus* sp. nov.: from Arizona; deeper body than *C. henshawii*, with more scales below the lateral line than above it, contrary to the usual Cyprinidae pattern.
- *Myloleucus parvanus* sp. nov.: numerous specimens from Beaver River, SW Utah.
- *Clinostomus phlegethontis* sp. nov.: a single immature specimen from Beaver River, with no lateral line; scales 11 longitudinal and 37 transverse series.
- *Catostomus alticolus* sp. nov.: numerous specimens from Twin Lake, Colorado (9,500 ft), collected by Dr. J. T. Rothrock.
- *Catostomus bisinuatus* (Cope): from the Zuni River, Arizona, and an unspecified Arizona locality (No. 504).
- *Haplochilus floripinnis* sp. nov.: numerous specimens from the Platte River near Denver, Colorado (No. 65).
- *Uranidea wheeleri* sp. nov.: described as the only physoclistous (spiny-rayed) fish yet found in the Great Basin of Utah; from Beaver River, SW Utah. Dedicated to Lieutenant Wheeler.

**Temporary pool fauna**
- A large entomostracan, *Limnadella kite* (named by Girard after Thomas Kite of Cincinnati), occurs in temporary rain pools in Pennsylvania, New Jersey and the plains of Kansas and Colorado. It is not known from flowing water.
- Cope notes a similar species reported by M. Tissandier in flood pools of the Seine valley.
