## Executive Summary
This document is a formal letter from the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team (DFRT)** to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regarding conservation strategies for the **[[Chihuahua Chub]]** (*Gila nigrescens*). Following the species' rediscovery in the United States in 1975, the DFRT reviewed the existing Recovery Plan and issued three primary recommendations: (1) supplemental stocking of hatchery-reared chub to expand the species' limited range within the **[[Mimbres River]]** drainage, (2) acquisition of critical riparian property to protect and expand habitat, and (3) establishment of formal inter-agency agreements to prevent habitat degradation from poor land management and unregulated instream/riparian modifications.

## Background & Description
*The source document is a policy/recovery letter and does not provide specific morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data for the Chihuahua Chub. Taxonomic identity is noted below:*

- **Scientific Name:** *Gila nigrescens*
- **Common Name:** [[Chihuahua Chub]]
- **Genus:** *Gila* (a genus of North American freshwater minnows, part of the family [[Cyprinidae]])
- **Taxonomic Note:** Related to other southwestern desert *Gila* species monitored by the DFRT, such as the [[Gila Chub]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Restricted to freshwater mainstream and tributary habitats within the **[[Mimbres River]]** drainage of New Mexico, USA (with historic/native range extending into Chihuahua, Mexico).
- **Range Restriction:** As of 1986, the U.S. population was documented to inhabit only approximately **8 kilometers** of the Mimbres River mainstream.
- **Population Status:** A residual population estimated at only **~400 individuals**, indicating a critically small and vulnerable population size.
- **Limiting Factors:** Range expansion has been slow, potentially due to the small residual population size acting as a bottleneck to natural recolonization and dispersal.
- **Threats:** Poor land management practices and unregulated instream/riparian habitat modifications (e.g., channel alteration, riparian clearing) are cited as primary contributors to the species' historical decline.
- **Associated Habitats for Potential Recovery:**
  - **McKnight Creek:** A tributary site below an established fish barrier (protecting from invasive/competing species).
  - **Mimbres River Mainstream:** A parcel acquired by the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (NMDGF), located ~2 km downstream from the Mimbres Post Office.
  - **[[Gallinas Creek]]:** A Mimbres River tributary identified as a potential (but uninventoried) stocking site.

## Key Findings
- The **[[Chihuahua Chub]]** was rediscovered in the United States in **1975**, prior to which it had no officially designated Recovery Team for USFWS biological consultation.
- The DFRT formally recommended **supplemental stocking** of hatchery-reared individuals from the **[[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]** to bolster the small wild population (~400 individuals) and aid range expansion.
- Two primary stocking sites were identified as biologically suitable: **McKnight Creek** (below an existing barrier) and a **NMDGF-owned parcel** on the Mimbres River mainstream.
- A third site, **Gallinas Creek**, was proposed but required a biological inventory prior to any introduction effort.
- The DFRT stipulated that any stocked populations must receive **equivalent legal/conservation protection** to the existing wild population.
- Land acquisition in the **Mimbres River Valley** was recommended as a strategic priority, to be coordinated with **[[The Nature Conservancy]]** and **NMDGF**.
- The letter emphasizes the need for a **multi-agency stream management strategy** involving the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, state/federal agencies, and local stakeholders to prevent further habitat degradation via uncoordinated instream or riparian modifications.
