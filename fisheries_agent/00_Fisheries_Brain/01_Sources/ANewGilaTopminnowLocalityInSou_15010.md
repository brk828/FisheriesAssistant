# A New Gila Topminnow Locality in Southern Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Poeciliidae #endangered-species #conservation

## Executive Summary

This research note documents the discovery of a new population of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) in [[Redrock Canyon]] on the [[Coronado National Forest]] in southern Arizona. First observed in winter 1978 and confirmed in May 1979, this population represents only the eighth natural locality for this once-common native fish species. The discovery is particularly significant as it represents only the second population occurring on federal lands. The authors provide detailed habitat characterization and management recommendations to protect this population from threats including livestock grazing, mining operations, off-road vehicles, and nonnative fish species, particularly [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis affinis*).

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) is a diminutive livebearer in the family [[Poeciliidae]], order Atheriniformes. Once one of the most common low desert native fish species in Arizona, the species experienced dramatic range reduction and population decline, resulting in its designation as an endangered species by the U.S. Department of the Interior in 1976.

Prior to this discovery, only seven natural populations persisted: six in the [[Santa Cruz River]] drainage and one along the [[Gila River]] near Bylas. Of these, four occurred on private land, with one each on Indian reservation, state, and Bureau of Land Management lands. Two localities—[[Bylas Springs]] and [[Cocio Wash]]—were discovered within the decade preceding this report. An eighth population in the [[San Pedro River Valley]] was eliminated by water mining within months of discovery.

Attempts to reestablish this species in its native range have met with limited success, related to factors including geographic location of introduction sites, permanence of surface waters, flooding variability, and presence of nonnative fishes, principally [[Mosquitofish]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Redrock Canyon]] population inhabits an intermittent desert stream system at elevations between 1,348 and 1,395 meters. Two distinct subpopulations were documented:

**Redrock Ranch Subpopulation (1,348 m elevation):**
- Side-channel pool: 10 m × 2 m, 0.5 m deep
- Water temperature: 22°C (air temperature 36°C)
- pH: 7.5
- Thickly overgrown with aquatic vegetation
- Located approximately 0.2 km upstream from a 2-meter bedrock waterfall

**Gate Spring Subpopulation (1,395 m elevation):**
- 150-meter reach of mainstream adjacent to and below Gate Spring
- Channel characterized by bedrock and numerous inflowing seeps
- Stream width: 1-2 meters
- Riffles: seldom exceeding 15 cm depth
- Pools: 30 cm deep
- Discharge: 0.44 m³ per minute
- Water temperature range: 14°C (7:00 p.m.) to 24°C (1:00 p.m.)
- Extensive algal mats present

**Water Chemistry:**

Mainstream (Gate Spring area):
- CO₂: 40 mg/L
- Specific conductance: 1,200 mmhos
- Ca²⁺ hardness (as CaCO₃): 350 mg/L
- Mg²⁺ hardness (as CaCO₃): 170 mg/L
- Total hardness (as CaCO₃): 520 mg/L
- Total alkalinity (as CaCO₃): 200 mg/L
- pH: 8.5

Elevated side-channel spring:
- CO₂: 60 mg/L
- Specific conductance: 2,300 mmhos
- Ca²⁺ hardness (as CaCO₃): 1,050 mg/L
- Mg²⁺ hardness (as CaCO₃): 150 mg/L
- Total hardness (as CaCO₃): 1,200 mg/L
- Total alkalinity (as CaCO₃): 170 mg/L
- pH: 7.4

**Associated Fish Fauna:**
- Adult [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) observed below falls near Redrock Ranch
- Young-of-year dace noted 6 km downstream in late May
- Small (<150 mm) [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) abundant in headwater tank 7 km upstream
- Single [[Mosquitofish]] captured in headwater tank (March 1979)

**Habitat Utilization:**
Topminnows occupied areas of algal mats almost exclusively, particularly using them as escape cover upon human approach. The extensive algal mats also provided shade under summer conditions.

## Key Findings

**Population Discovery Timeline:**
- Winter 1978: First observation of topminnows in Redrock Canyon (water temperature 5°C, few fish in mainstream pool)
- Reports indicate presence as early as late 1960s
- March 1979: Initial survey by Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State University, and USDA Forest Service found no topminnows
- May 8, 1979: Confirmation of side-channel pool population downstream from Redrock Ranch
- May 18-19, 1979: Collection of specimens from Gate Spring area; population estimated at less than 100 fish (reduced by harsh winter conditions)
- Early July 1979: Topminnows (mostly young-of-year) abundant in mainstream
- August 23, 1979: Population persisted despite recent severe flooding (estimated flow 500 m³ per minute); abundance at Gate Spring higher than May but lower than expected due to flood impacts

**Population Status:**
- Five voucher specimens preserved under federal permit PRT-8-238-C
- Total population at Gate Spring in late May estimated at less than 100 individuals
- Population demonstrated resilience to flooding events
- Young-of-year production documented, indicating successful reproduction

**Management Recommendations:**

1. **Livestock Grazing Protection:** Fence 150-200 meters of stream channel to protect both Gate Spring and Redrock Ranch subpopulations from domestic livestock impacts

2. **Mining Protection:** Protect stream reach above and within topminnow habitat from negative impacts of future mining operations, including water mining (groundwater pumping for irrigation or domestic supply). Exploratory bore holes were observed in the canyon.

3. **Nonnative Species Control:** Resurvey headwater stock tank and other impoundments to determine abundance and distribution of competitive [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis affinis*); remove this species from the drainage

4. **Vehicle Access Restriction:** Prohibit vehicular travel on primitive road up stream channel to Gate Spring in reaches containing topminnows

5. **Population Expansion:** During summer when topminnows become abundant, transplant gravid females on experimental basis to adjacent springs maintaining suitable surface water (e.g., [[Cottonwood Springs]]); monitor to ensure perpetuation and range expansion

6. **Continued Surveys:** Re-examine springs in and adjacent to Redrock Canyon during summer when populations normally become abundant due to the species' prolific reproductive capacity

**Conservation Significance:**
This locality represents only the second [[Gila Topminnow]] population occurring on federal lands, presenting an excellent opportunity for both maintenance and introduction activities. The endangered status of this species necessitates continued efforts to maintain natural populations and reintroduce it into suitable habitats within its natural range. Existing management documents (Rickel 1979) delineate specific conservation actions for the [[Coronado National Forest]].
