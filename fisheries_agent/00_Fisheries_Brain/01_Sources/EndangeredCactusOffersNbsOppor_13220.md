# Pima Pineapple Cactus Conservation and Research Opportunity

#botany #desert-ecology #Cactaceae #endangered-species #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary

The [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] (*Coryphantha scheeri* var. *robustispina*) was listed as endangered by the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] in October 1993. This rare [[Sonoran Desert]] species occurs in a restricted range from south [[Tucson]] to south of [[Nogales]], [[Mexico]]. The [[National Biological Survey]] (NBS), in consortium with multiple agencies including USFWS, [[Arizona Department of Agriculture]], and the [[University of Arizona]], is conducting ecological research to support conservation and management of this species. Major threats include dam construction and retirement community expansion in areas with highest known concentrations.

## Background & Description

The [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] is one of the rarest cacti species in the [[Sonoran Desert]]. It grows up to 7-8 inches tall and 4 inches across, making it easily overlooked in its habitat. The species is most commonly found in communities dominated by [[Mesquite]] and [[Haplopappus]], sometimes with grasses. Most observed individuals grow in open areas rather than hidden beneath larger plants, though whether this represents preference or circumstance remains unknown.

The species has the highest degree of protection at both federal and state levels under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Its distribution spans south-central [[Arizona]] and adjacent [[Sonora]], [[Mexico]], roughly from south [[Tucson]] to somewhere south of [[Nogales]]. Populations range from scattered groups to widely dispersed individuals. Total distribution and population numbers have not been definitively established.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] inhabits the upper [[Sonoran Desert]] in plant communities with large species mixtures. The dominant vegetation includes [[Mesquite]] and [[Haplopappus]], with variable grass presence. Current land management in occupied habitat typically focuses on wildlife habitat, grazing range, or human development rather than cactus-specific conservation needs.

A major conservation challenge is that no single agency controls a large percentage of the populations, necessitating multi-agency coordination. Some of the highest known concentrations occur in areas threatened by dam construction and rapidly expanding retirement communities, creating urgent conservation concerns.

## Key Findings

**Conservation Status & Protection:**
- Listed as endangered in October 1993 by [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]]
- Protected under federal [[Endangered Species Act]] and state regulations
- Multi-agency consortium formed including USFWS, NBS, [[Arizona Department of Agriculture]] (Native Plant Program), UA, and researchers

**Research Objectives:**
- Gathering basic ecological information on germination rates, habitat characteristics, growth rates, and reproduction
- Future studies planned on fire ecology, reproduction biology (including pollination), growth and development, and relationships to topography and plant community development

**Management Implications:**
- Current land management often ignores cactus habitat requirements
- Preservation may require establishing a dedicated [[Pima Pineapple Cactus]] preserve
- NBS designed to provide biological information to land managing agencies for proper species management
- Research conducted throughout species range to assist in developing optimal management/recovery plan

**Distribution & Threats:**
- Restricted range in south-central [[Arizona]] and adjacent [[Sonora]], [[Mexico]]
- Highest concentrations threatened by infrastructure development and urban expansion
- Total distribution and population size not definitively established
