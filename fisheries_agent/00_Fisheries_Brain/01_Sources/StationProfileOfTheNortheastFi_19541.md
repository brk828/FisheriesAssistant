# Station Profile: Northeast Fishery Center (Lamar National Fish Hatchery & Fish Technology Center)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Acipenseridae #aquaculture #fish-hatchery

## Executive Summary

This station profile documents FY1993 operations of the [[Northeast Fishery Center]] (Lamar National Fish Hatchery and Fish Technology Center) in Lamar, Pennsylvania, a USFWS Region 5 facility established in 1931. The report captures a pivotal transitional year in which the station shifted from decades of traditional put-and-take [[Rainbow Trout]] stocking toward advanced fisheries technology research, most notably the first successful captive spawning of Hudson River [[Atlantic Sturgeon]] at the facility. Key activities included sturgeon capture and induced spawning using [[LHRHa]] hormone injection, fry/fingerling diet trials, construction and start-up of a 7,000-gallon closed saltwater recirculation system, [[Atlantic Salmon]] egg transport methodology studies, and efficacy trials of [[Potassium Permanganate]] (KMnO4) against parasitic and bacterial pathogens in [[Striped Bass]]. The station also managed nationwide coordination of an Investigative New Animal Drug (INAD) permit for KMnO4 use across Service facilities.

## Background & Description

- **Location:** One mile south of I-80 off State Route 64, Tylersville Road, Lamar, Porter Township, Clinton County, Pennsylvania.
- **Established:** Site purchased in 1931; first fish distribution in FY1935 (24,000 [[Brook Trout]] and 14,000 Rainbow Trout). The Fish Technology Center was created on the hatchery grounds in 1966.
- **Authority:** White Act (Omnibus Authorization for Fish Cultural Stations and Other Establishments), 46 Stat. 371, approved 5-21-30.
- **Project Leader:** Michael A. Hendrix (Region 5).
- **Physical Facilities:** Office/hatchery building complex, intensive culture building, coldwater experimental building, technology laboratory, 20 concrete raceways, 5 hypalon-lined ponds, 10 earthen ponds (unusable at time of report), feed storage building, maintenance shop, and garages.

### Station Mission
- Develop cost-effective water use/reuse and effluent control methods compatible with [[Threatened Endangered and Candidate Species]] propagation.
- Develop culture techniques ensuring genetic/functional compatibility of propagated T,E,C species with wild populations.
- Support INAD compliance for aquaculture drugs and chemicals.
- Support interjurisdictional river management plans and hatchery product evaluation methodology.
- Protect Service facilities from non-indigenous aquatic species and study impacts of aquatic nuisance species on T&E species/habitats.
- Disseminate findings via scientific publications, workshops, and technical assistance to federal, state, tribal, and private aquaculturists.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Watershed:** NEFC is located within, and draws water supply from, the [[Fishing Creek Watershed]] (Pennsylvania); staff participated in the Fishing Creek Watershed Group (formed by the Northcentral Pennsylvania Conservancy) to address watershed degradation.
- **Source Populations:** Wild [[Hudson River Atlantic Sturgeon]] were captured by cooperating commercial fishermen at two sites — a private dock on Catskill Creek (Catskill, NY) and Norrie Point State Park (Staatsburg, NY) — for broodstock collection and captive spawning trials. Captive stock also included sturgeon from the [[Delaware River]].
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Monthly effluent monitoring conducted under the National Pollution Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) permit, testing for BOD, suspended solids, phosphorus, and ammonium.
- **Habitat Restoration:** Phase 1 planning initiated for a constructed wetland (converting an abandoned earthen basin into a forested wetland) for effluent treatment and public outreach; seed from eight hydrophyte species collected for FY94 planting.
- **Species Recovery Context:** Recovery of Chesapeake Bay [[Striped Bass]] populations and declining federal priority for trout put-and-take stocking drove the station's strategic shift away from traditional stocking toward T,E,C species research (notably Atlantic sturgeon).
- **Disease Ecology:** *[[Icthyobodo sp.]]* and *[[Flexibacter columnaris]]* outbreaks documented in Phase II striped bass populations, prompting KMnO4 efficacy trials.
- **Fungal Pathogen Impact:** *[[Saprolegnia sp.]]* infection significantly reduced hatch success in incubating Atlantic sturgeon eggs.

## Key Findings

### Atlantic Sturgeon Spawning Program (1993)
- First-ever successful captive spawning of a Hudson River Atlantic Sturgeon achieved at NEFC.
- A 275 lb (>8 ft) female captured June 6, 1993 had already spent her eggs; released after ovarian tissue biopsy.
- A 140 lb female captured in early trial failed to fertilize on-site using White Sturgeon protocols; eggs proved largely unsuitable.
- A ripe 134 lb female captured late June 1993 was transported to NEFC and successfully induced to spawn using [[LHRHa]] hormone injection.
- Eggs (420,000 total) fertilized using milt from 5 males, including a portion using cryopreserved milt (with NFRDL Wellsboro).
- High *Saprolegnia sp.* fungal infection during incubation; final hatch ≈ 14,000 fry (~3.3% hatch rate from 420,000 eggs).
- Cryopreserved milt-fertilized eggs showed early embryonic development but did not hatch due to fungal infection.
- Technical assistance provided by Joel Van Eenennaam (UC Davis white sturgeon expert) and Wellsboro NFRDL staff.

### Atlantic Sturgeon Fry/Fingerling Diet Trials
- ~5,500 yolk-sac fry sent to NFRDL Wellsboro; remainder used in feeding trials at NEFC.
- Fry fed live brine shrimp significantly outperformed those on dry diets alone in survival/growth.
- Brine shrimp-raised fry converted successfully and immediately to dry diets afterward.
- 60-day fingerling trial (4 dry diets, ~600 surviving juveniles):
  - **Biokyowa** diet (3% body weight/day): up to 87% weight gain over 2 weeks.
  - **Bozeman Technology Center sturgeon starter diet**: up to 78% weight gain over same period.

### Captive Atlantic Sturgeon Husbandry
- 8 immature + 5 mature Atlantic sturgeon (Hudson & Delaware River origin) maintained in ambient-temperature freshwater circular tanks, fed Zeigler GR-7 trout pellets.
- Immature fish showed weight gains up to 2x January baseline by September; only one mature fish gained weight over the same period.

### Saltwater Recirculation System
- 7,000-gallon closed system commissioned in intensive culture building (6.25 m diameter rearing tank, clarifier, CO2 strippers, heat exchanger, UV disinfection, fluidized bed biofilters, pure oxygen generation, titanium heater).
- Nitrifying bacteria establishment initiated via ammonium chloride dosing (April 27); ammonia below detection ~4 weeks later.
- Stocked with 2 juvenile Atlantic sturgeon and 3 mature Striped Bass; later added mature male/female Atlantic sturgeon (Nov. 8) to promote captive egg/sperm production.
- Ammonia remained low, nitrate rose steadily — confirming functional biofiltration.

### Atlantic Salmon Egg Transport Study
- Investigated causes of declining egg eye-up rates (<60% in some cases).
- Compared insulated jug transport (with/without ice) vs. egg trays; also tested unfertilized egg transport and iodophor disinfection.
- Preliminary results: eggs transported with ice and unfertilized eggs showed relatively higher eye-up rates.
- Broodstock sources: Kensington State Hatchery (CT, domestic) and Cronin NFH (MA, sea-run); incubated at White River NFH (VT).

### Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4) Efficacy Trials — Striped Bass
- Trial 1: 13,500 Phase II Striped Bass in six 6-ft tanks (3 treatment, 3 control).
- 2 ppm KMnO4 flush treatments applied on 30 of 40 trial days due to persistent mortality above threshold (5/day).
- Mortality: Control tanks 91.2% vs. Treated tanks 35.2% — treatment significantly reduced mortality, though disease persisted in all tanks.
- Trial 2 (ongoing at time of report): 9 tanks comparing NaCl (1%) vs. KMnO4 (2 ppm) vs. combined treatment vs. control.
- Supports nationwide **[[INAD]]** (Investigative New Animal Drug) permit development for KMnO4, coordinated by NEFC across 25 Service facilities plus 7 additional non-federal cooperators (state agencies and private aquaculture).

### Hatchery Production Summary (FY93)
- Total distribution: 210,077 fish / 56,716 lbs across Maryland, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania.
- **Rainbow Trout:** 143,451 fish (54,111 lbs) distributed; 70,543 (11") and 7,732 (15") catchable trout; 78% released in federally managed waters. Feed conversion ratio declined from 1.27 (FY92) to 1.55 (FY93), attributed to compressed feeding schedule from labor shortages. Total trout weight declined by 20,885 lbs to 38,708 lbs due to reallocation/curtailment of production (Sikes Act stocking cancelled; overall trout stocking program phased out by year end).
- **Striped Bass:** FY93 Nanticoke stock harvest (Nov. 1992) = 61,101 Phase II fish, a 170% increase over FY92 (27,746 — 1,142 lbs/surface acre). Subsequent crop (reared FY93, harvested Nov. 1993) = 69,331 fish at 72.5% survival, 31,514 — 2,434 lbs/surface acre (record weight yield).
- This marked the historic end of scheduled put-and-take fish production for recreational stocking at Lamar NFH (production history dating to FY1938), transitioning the station toward technology/research support functions.
