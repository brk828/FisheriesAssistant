# River Basin Investigations: Visitor Use and Fishing Quality Survey of Five Arizona Lakes (1965–1966)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fisheries-management #recreational-fisheries #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report (Project FW-16-R-5) documents a **visitor-use and fisheries survey** conducted on five Arizona reservoirs — [[Bartlett Lake]], [[Pena Blanca Lake]], [[Parker Canyon Lake]], [[Black Canyon Lake]], and [[Lyman Lake]] — between June 1, 1965, and September 15, 1965 (with the broader project running through July 1966). The study is not a species-morphology paper but a **management-oriented survey** quantifying recreational use (fishing, camping, picnicking, boating/skiing, sightseeing) to inform the economic valuation of surface water acreage and to guide siting of future impoundments under the [[Central Arizona Project]] and the [[Land and Water Conservation Fund Act of 1965]]. Two survey methodologies were tested — a **roadside check interview** ([[Webb 1962 Roadside Check Method]]) and a **spot count method** — with the spot count method abandoned mid-survey due to inadequate data quality.

## Background & Description

No individual fish specimens were morphologically described in this report. Instead, the source characterizes **fishery types** by management regime and habitat class:

- **[[Bartlett Lake]]** — Typical **warm-water fishery**, desert environment, ~55 miles from Phoenix; fish-per-hour catch rates ranged **0.35–0.53** over 1963–1965 (statewide creel census, [[Barnes 1963]], [[Barnes 1964]], [[Barnes 1965]]).
- **[[Parker Canyon Lake]]** — Managed as a **trout fingerling stocking fishery**; located in southeastern Arizona with no comparable nearby water body.
- **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]** — **Combination trout and warm-water fishery**, near Nogales; managed for both salmonid and warm-water species simultaneously.
- **[[Black Canyon Lake]]** — **Trout stocking program**; newly completed (June 1964), minimal facility development.
- **[[Lyman Lake]]** — **Fingerling trout stocking system**; water is notably turbid, which is cited as a limiting factor on angling use.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Bartlett Lake]]**: Desert warm-water reservoir habitat; relative isolation (rough access roads) versus nearby [[Saguaro Lake]] (paved roads, high development) creates a niche differentiation — Bartlett functions primarily as a low-disturbance "fisherman's lake," while Saguaro absorbs boating/skiing pressure.
- **[[Parker Canyon Lake]]**: Isolated southeastern Arizona trout habitat; serves as the sole substantial water body in its region, elevating its role in regional recreation and fish habitat provisioning; fishing success is high year-round except summer months.
- **[[Pena Blanca Lake]]**: Dual-habitat (trout + warm-water) fishery benefiting from proximity to Tucson/Nogales metro areas and well-developed access infrastructure, yielding balanced recreational use across activity types.
- **[[Black Canyon Lake]]**: Trout habitat south of Heber; low development acts as a "buffer," concentrating use toward fishing rather than camping/picnicking, unlike more developed neighboring lakes.
- **[[Lyman Lake]]**: Turbid-water trout habitat near Springerville/St. Johns; despite good highway access, State Parks facilities, and an adjacent bison herd attraction, poor water clarity and lack of forested scenery — combined with competing scenic trout lakes nearby — suppress fishing/camping/picnicking use.

## Key Findings

- **Survey design**: Roadside check interviews (per [[Webb 1962]]) proved the only reliable method; the spot-check method was **abandoned in August 1965** due to inferior data quality, and its data were excluded from analysis.
- **Survey scope**: 32 weekend/holiday days and 75 weekdays were sampled to represent daily visitor-use patterns across the five lakes.
- **Use composition drivers**: Access road quality, proximity to metropolitan population centers, facility development, water clarity, scenic quality, and presence of competing nearby lakes were identified as the principal determinants of **recreational use composition** (fishing vs. camping vs. picnicking vs. boating/skiing vs. sightseeing).
- **Bartlett Lake** exhibited high fishing-use percentage attributable to both relative solitude (poor roads deterring boaters/skiers) and consistently favorable fish-per-hour catch rates (0.35–0.53).
- **Parker Canyon Lake** showed high non-fishing recreational use, attributed to the scarcity of alternative water bodies in its region rather than to any deficiency in its fishery.
- **Pena Blanca Lake** demonstrated the most balanced use composition among the five lakes, linked to dual trout/warm-water management and strong urban accessibility.
- **Black Canyon Lake**'s minimal infrastructure inadvertently concentrated visitor use toward fishing, a pattern predicted to persist absent future development/publicity.
- **Lyman Lake** had comparatively depressed fishing and general recreational use, primarily due to water turbidity and lack of scenic forest habitat relative to regional competitors.
- **Management application**: Visitation data were intended to feed **predictive models for expected use on proposed future reservoirs**, particularly relevant given anticipated water-storage expansion under the [[Central Arizona Project]] and new federal funding streams via the [[Land and Water Conservation Fund Act of 1965]].
- **Data limitation**: [[Lynx Lake]] was surveyed briefly but excluded from analysis due to insufficient data.
