# Letter to Secretary Babbitt Requesting a State of the Nation's Rivers Report

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific study but a 1993 advocacy letter from members of American Rivers' Scientific and Technical Advisory Committee (STAC) — a group of prominent stream ecologists and aquatic scientists — addressed to then-Secretary of the Interior Bruce Babbitt. The letter requests the creation of a comprehensive national "[[State of the Nation's Rivers Report]]" to assess the ecological health of United States river and stream systems. The signatories cite the National Academy of Sciences report, "[[Restoration of Aquatic Ecosystems]]," as evidence that [[Aquatic Species Decline]] is occurring at a rate exceeding that of terrestrial species, while management agencies lack sufficient data to make informed river management decisions. No specific fish species, morphological data, or site-specific ecological findings are presented; the letter is programmatic and policy-oriented in nature.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no specific taxa or niches are detailed, the letter frames rivers and streams as foundational ecological infrastructure, describing them as:
- The "lifeline of virtually every major ecosystem on the continent."
- Systems that supply water for drinking, agriculture, industry, and recreation.
- Conduits that receive and transport both natural and anthropogenic outputs from their watersheds.
- Critical support systems nourishing downstream [[Wetlands]] and [[Estuaries]].

The letter implicitly identifies river ecosystems as complex, interconnected habitats whose degradation has cascading effects on [[Riparian Zones]], [[Watershed Health]], and downstream aquatic biological communities, though it does not specify particular fish assemblages, guilds, or trophic relationships.

## Key Findings
- **Core request:** The signatories ask Secretary Babbitt to initiate a national "[[State of the Nation's Rivers Report]]," arguing it could be built from existing data with little new original research required.
- **Cited crisis:** Reference to the National Academy of Sciences report "Restoration of Aquatic Ecosystems" indicating that [[Aquatic Biodiversity Decline]] is occurring faster than terrestrial biodiversity decline.
- **Management data gap:** Federal and state agencies are described as lacking the necessary baseline information to make sound [[River Management]] decisions.
- **Proposed report components:**
  1. Evaluation of current status and historic trends in flow regime (quantity/pattern), water quality, biological condition, watershed/channel conditions, riparian zone health, and socioeconomic values.
  2. Identification of major current and potential threats to river systems.
  3. Identification of river systems remaining largely in natural/undisturbed condition.
  4. Identification of the most degraded river systems and those with greatest restoration potential.
- **Institutional context:** The report is proposed as an appropriate first initiative for the newly forming [[National Biological Survey]], suggesting alignment with broader 1990s federal biological monitoring efforts.
- **Signatories:** Notable co-signing aquatic scientists include Dr. Judy Meyer (University of Georgia), Dr. James Karr (University of Washington) — known for the [[Index of Biotic Integrity]] — Dr. Jack Stanford (University of Montana), Dr. Arthur Benke (University of Alabama), and Dr. Walter Minckley (Arizona State University), among others, reflecting a broad, interdisciplinary consensus among leading stream and riverine ecologists of the era.
- **Intended impact:** The letter frames the proposed report as both a management tool for river systems nationwide and a public education instrument regarding the ecological importance of [[Freshwater Ecosystems]].
