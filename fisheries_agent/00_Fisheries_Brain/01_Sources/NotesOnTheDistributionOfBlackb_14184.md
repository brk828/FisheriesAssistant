## Executive Summary
This 1968 notes-section paper documents the first recorded occurrence of **four sympatric species of black bass (*Micropterus*)** in a single locality — the Upper San Marcos River, Hays County, Texas. Prior work had established two species (*M. salmoides* and *M. treculi*) as long-term residents, with a third (*M. punctulatus*) documented as sympatric via later collections. The key new finding is the capture of a putative **smallmouth bass (*Micropterus dolomieui*)** at Station 1 on 24 March 1967, linked to a documented Texas Parks and Wildlife stocking event in October 1966. The specimen's scale counts fell distinctly outside the ranges of the other three co-occurring species, supporting species-level identification despite the fish's poor physical condition. The paper underscores the San Marcos River's unusual capacity to support multiple, normally allopatric or minimally-sympatric, congeneric top predators due to its unique thermal and hydrological structure.

**Specimen-specific observations (THNC 7123):**
- Standard length: **102 mm S.L.**
- Overall condition: poor
- Coloration consistent with *[[Micropterus dolomieui]]* (smallmouth bass)
- Scale counts (lateral line, caudal peduncle, above/below lateral line) fall outside ranges for *[[Micropterus punctulatus]]*, *[[Micropterus treculi]]*, and *[[Micropterus salmoides]]*, supporting distinct species identity
- Proportionally **large head**, indicative of stunted growth
- **Left pelvic fin missing** — matches the fin-clipping mark used to identify 1,500 hatchery-released smallmouth bass
- Heavy **nematode parasite load** in the intestine
- **6 large cysts** present bilaterally at the base of the caudal fin

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **System type:** The San Marcos River originates from a large spring within San Marcos city limits, producing water that is clear and thermally stable ([[Brown 1953]]).
- **Downstream transition:** ~5 km southeast of the spring source, the [[Blanco River]] joins the San Marcos, after which the river becomes appreciably murkier with greater daily/seasonal temperature variation.
- **Endemism:** The upper river segment supports a disproportionately large number of endemic fishes ([[Hubbs 1957]]), insects, amphibians, and other undescribed endemic organisms.
- **Novel assemblages:** The river has also received successful introductions of north temperate and subtropical fish species, creating unusual co-occurring species combinations.
- **Trophic role of *Micropterus*:** Blackbasses are described as significant piscivores and function as **"top predators"** in spring-fed streams, a niche filled by serranids (temperate basses) in larger lotic systems. Both groups are primarily **sight feeders** favoring clear water, and both are prized by sportfishermen — distinguishing them ecologically and economically from other piscivores (snakes, gar, bowfin, pickerel).
- **Study sites:**
  - **Station 1 ("Thompson's Island"):** ~200 m segment from the I-35 highway bridge to the San Marcos State Fish Hatchery outlet ditch; ~3 km southeast of the river's spring source; classic clear, spring-fed ecology.
  - **Station 2:** ~30 m on each side of the next downstream road crossing, ~3.5 km east of Station 1 and ~1 km east of the Blanco River confluence; southwest edge remains clear and thermally constant despite proximity to the murkier confluence zone.
- **Sympatry documented:**
  - *M. salmoides* and *M. treculi*: long-established co-residents of the upper San Marcos ([[Jurgens 1951]]; [[Hubbs 1954]]).
  - *M. punctulatus*: previously recorded at Martindale (6 km southeast of Station 2); subsequently collected at Station 2 itself (including 24 March 1967), confirming three-species sympatry across most/all of the San Marcos River.
  - *M. dolomieui*: introduced via stocking, representing a fourth co-occurring species at Station 1.

## Key Findings
- **First documented record** of four sympatric *Micropterus* species in a single locality (Upper San Marcos River) — notable because no more than 3 of the 6 known southern U.S. *Micropterus* species had previously been recorded together in any one area, likely due to overlapping habitat requirements.
- The smallmouth bass record is directly traceable to an active **Texas Parks and Wildlife Department stocking program**: 1,500 left-pelvic-clipped smallmouth bass were released in the Upper San Marcos on **6 October 1966** by biologist Richard White.
- The recovered specimen's **poor condition, small size (102 mm SL), stunted morphology, parasite load, and cyst presence** suggest **low survival/fitness** of stocked smallmouth bass in this river system, despite confirmed persistence (additional smallmouth bass collected through 1968).
- Meristic (scale count) data proved a reliable diagnostic tool for distinguishing *M. dolomieui* from the three resident *Micropterus* species even when coloration alone might be suggestive but insufficient.
- The findings reinforce the San Marcos River's ecological uniqueness as a system capable of supporting atypical predator diversity, driven by its stable spring-fed thermal regime, confluence dynamics with the Blanco River, and history of both natural endemism and successful non-native fish introductions.
