# Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants: 50 CFR 17.11 and 17.12 (1992 Compilation)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1992 compilation of the U.S. federal regulations at 50 CFR §17.11 (Endangered and Threatened Wildlife) and §17.12 (Endangered and Threatened Plants), prepared by the Division of Endangered Species of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. It is a regulatory reference rather than a research study. It defines how species are listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], explains the status codes and columns used in the official lists, and gives the rules for listing, reclassifying, and [[Delisting]] species. Fish appear only in limited form, mainly in the table of species removed from the lists. The document does not report field data, population counts, or ecological measurements.

## Background & Description

The document sets out the framework for the official lists:

- **Scope (§17.11(a)):** The wildlife list names all species determined by the Services to be [[Endangered Status|Endangered]] or [[Threatened Status|Threatened]], plus species treated as such under [[Similarity of Appearance]] provisions.
- **Naming rules (§17.11(b)):** Listings are defined by scientific name and vertebrate population boundaries. Common names are included but are not reliable for identifying specimens. The Services use the most recently accepted scientific name and follow the [[International Code of Zoological Nomenclature]]. The term "Entire" means all populations across the species' present range are listed.
- **Status codes (§17.11(c)):** "E" denotes Endangered, "T" denotes Threatened, and "E [or T] (S/A)" denotes similarity-of-appearance species.
- **Non-regulatory columns (§17.11(d)):** Spelling, historic range, footnotes, synonyms and current names may be amended without public notice. Changes to a species' definition or status require the procedure of [[Part 424]].
- **Historic range (§17.11(e)):** This column shows the known general distribution from the scientific literature. Current distribution may be much smaller, and [[Prohibitions Under the Act]] apply to all individuals wherever found.
- **Footnotes and listing dates (§17.11(f)):** Footnotes cite the [[Federal Register]] documents that list or reclassify species, with effective dates.
- **Special rules and critical habitat (§17.11(f)(2)):** These columns cross-reference other sections of Parts 17, 222, 225, 227 and 402. They also identify [[Experimental Populations]] as essential (XE) or nonessential (XN). "NA" means no special rules or critical habitat apply, but other general rules still do.
- **Taxonomic inclusion (§17.11(g)):** Listing a taxon includes all lower units. The document gives [[Gibbons]] (genus *Hylobates*, Endangered throughout its range) and the [[Bald Eagle]] (Threatened only in listed U.S. states, not its full population) as examples.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is silent on most ecological detail, but it lists the factors used to decide whether a species should be listed or delisted (§17.11(c) and (d)). These [[Listing Factors]] are:

1. Present or threatened destruction, modification, or curtailment of habitat or range.
2. Overutilization for commercial, recreational, scientific, or educational purposes.
3. Disease or predation.
4. Inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms.
5. Other natural or manmade factors affecting continued existence.

For delisting, the document recognizes three grounds: [[Extinction]] (after a sufficient period without detection), [[Recovery]] (the goal is to return species to a point where Act protection is no longer required), and original data error (earlier classification data or interpretation were wrong).

Fish-related habitat is described only in the removed-species table. That table covers the freshwater [[Tecopa Pupfish]] and the [[Longjaw Cisco]], which were in the Great Lakes region. The text does not provide habitat details for either.

## Key Findings

- **Structure of the lists:** Part 17 has two main sections: §17.11 for wildlife and §17.12 for plants. Both use the same column structure, status codes and footnote numbering, because plants and animals are often listed in the same Federal Register document.
- **Footnote scale:** Footnote numbers run in one sequence across both sections, reaching about 476 by 1992. Each footnote gives the Federal Register citation, date, and listing basis.
- **Current status:** The 1992 edition incorporated minor corrections to the October 1, 1991 compilation and all final rules published since then. The document states that no entry was significantly affected.
- **Removed-species table:** The document lists species fully removed from §17.11 and §17.12 for informational purposes only. Each entry gives the reason for removal: extinction, recovery, or original data error. Fish entries include:
  - **Tecopa Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon nevadensis calidae*), U.S.A. (California), removed under a listing decision published in 1983.
  - **Longjaw Cisco** (*Coregonus alpenae*), U.S.A. and Canada (Lakes Michigan, Huron, Erie), removed under a listing decision published in 1979.
  The rest of the table covers non-fish species, including mollusks, plants, birds and reptiles. The full table is not reproduced here.
- **Regulatory principles:** Listing decisions must rest on "the best scientific and commercial data available." Delisting requires data showing the species is neither endangered nor threatened.
- **Limits of the lists:** The lists are not a complete guide to regulated activity. Other rules in Parts 17, 217–227 and 402, and in other Federal agency or state regulations, may still apply.
