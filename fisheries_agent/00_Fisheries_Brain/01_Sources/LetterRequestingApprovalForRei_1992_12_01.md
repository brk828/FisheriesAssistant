## Executive Summary

This is a 1992 letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) to the Arizona State Land Department requesting written approval to reintroduce two federally endangered fish species — the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — into AD Wash, a perennial desert stream reach on State Land near Lake Pleasant, Arizona. The document identifies AD Wash as high-quality habitat lacking exotic predators and proposes stocking approximately 1,000 Gila topminnow and 500 desert pupfish immediately below Layton Tank. The letter frames this action as a contribution toward future downlisting of both species under the Endangered Species Act.

## Background & Description

- **Requesting Agency:** Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD), Nongame and Endangered Wildlife Program.
- **Recipient Agency:** Arizona State Land Department.
- **Author:** Terry B. Johnson, Chief, Nongame and Endangered Wildlife Program, AGFD.
- **Species proposed for reintroduction:**
  - [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — family [[Poeciliidae]], a small live-bearing fish endemic to the Gila River basin, federally listed as endangered.
  - [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — family [[Cyprinodontidae]], a small euryhaline/eurythermal fish native to the Sonoran Desert region, federally listed as endangered.
- **Site identification:** AGFD staff identified a perennial reach of desert stream on State Land, located northwest of Lake Pleasant (Township 8 North, Range 2 West, Section 36, southwest quarter).
- **Site evaluation date:** August 20, 1992.
- **Proposed release location:** AD Wash, immediately below Layton Tank.
- **Proposed stocking numbers:**
  - ~1,000 Gila topminnow
  - ~500 desert pupfish
- **Approval process:** AGFD sought written authorization from the State Land Department prior to stocking, following a verbal indication of support from Deputy Commissioner Glen E. Collins to Kirk Young (AGFD Native Fish Project Manager).
- **Distribution/coordination list:** Kirk L. Young (Native Fish Project Manager), Jim Warnecke (Fisheries Program Manager, Region VI), Sally Stefferud (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Phoenix), Tim Hughes (Bureau of Land Management, Phoenix) — indicating multi-agency coordination typical of endangered species reintroduction efforts under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** [[Desert Stream Habitat]] characterized by a combination of pool/riffle morphology, which supports diverse microhabitats suitable for small-bodied native fishes.
- **Water permanence:** The reach was identified as having **permanent (perennial) water**, a critical and often limiting resource for native fish persistence in arid Southwestern ecosystems.
- **Water quality:** Described as "good," supporting viability for sensitive endangered species.
- **Vegetation:** "Abundant aquatic vegetation" noted, likely providing cover, spawning substrate, and food resources (e.g., invertebrate prey) for both topminnow and pupfish.
- **Biotic conditions:** Notably, the site was reported to lack **exotic predators** — a key factor given that predation and competition from introduced species (e.g., mosquitofish, sunfish, crayfish) is a primary driver of decline for both [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] throughout their range.
- **Regional context:** AD Wash lies within the broader Sonoran Desert/Gila River drainage ecosystem near Lake Pleasant, Arizona — a region historically impacted by water diversion, habitat fragmentation, and introduction of non-native fishes, all of which have contributed to the endangered status of both species.

## Key Findings

- AD Wash was assessed by AGFD biologists (August 20, 1992) as an ecologically suitable reintroduction site for two federally endangered desert fishes based on four key habitat attributes: (1) pool/riffle structure, (2) permanent water, (3) good water quality, and (4) absence of exotic predators.
- AGFD proposed a translocation effort of **1,000 Gila topminnow** and **500 desert pupfish** into AD Wash below Layton Tank, contingent upon written interagency approval.
- The action was explicitly positioned as supporting **recovery and eventual downlisting** of both species under federal endangered species status.
- The effort exemplifies a standard interagency native fish conservation protocol in Arizona, involving coordination among state wildlife management ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), state land management (Arizona State Land Department), and federal agencies ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]]).
- This letter serves as a historical administrative record of on-the-ground endangered species reintroduction planning, illustrating the practical, permission-based workflow required for native fish conservation actions on state-managed lands in Arizona during the early 1990s.
