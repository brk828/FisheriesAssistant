## Executive Summary

This document is a society proceedings report from *Science* (1958) summarizing the second annual meeting of the [[Southeastern Society Of Ichthyologists And Herpetologists]] (formerly the Southeastern Herpetological Society), held Oct. 24–25 at the University of Alabama, Tuscaloosa. The meeting was attended by 35 representatives from 14 institutions and included research presentations, institutional reports, examination of vertebrate collections, and field trips. While the bulk of the reported research concerns snake endocrinology and taxonomy, one presentation directly addresses fish biogeography: [[Edwin B. Joseph]]'s analysis of the fish fauna of [[Alligator Harbor]] on the northern Gulf coast of Florida. The business session also addressed the organization's formal request to become a Southeastern Division of the [[American Society Of Ichthyologists And Herpetologists]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fish fauna of Alligator Harbor, Florida (northern Gulf coast)**: [[Edwin B. Joseph]] analyzed 108 fish species and identified four distinct faunal components based on biogeographic/ecological affinity:
  1. A **tropical-water** component (largest group)
  2. A **temperate-water** component (second largest)
  3. A component **endemic to the Gulf of Mexico**
  4. A component of species **restricted to the Gulf and southeastern U.S. coast**

  This four-part faunal structure illustrates the transitional, mixed biogeographic character of estuarine/coastal habitats at the boundary between tropical and temperate marine zones in the Gulf of Mexico.

- **Reptile ecology (contextual)**: The ecological separation between [[Rainbow Snake]] and [[Mud Snake]] was noted as more significant than their morphological differences, suggesting niche partitioning drives taxonomic distinction in this group.

## Key Findings

- **Fish biogeography**: The [[Alligator Harbor]] fish fauna (108 species) is composed of four ecologically distinct groups—tropical, temperate, Gulf-endemic, and Gulf/southeastern-U.S.-restricted—reflecting the region's status as a biogeographic transition zone in the [[Gulf Of Mexico]].
- **Endocrinology (non-fish)**: [[Clarence H. Shockley]] demonstrated that in male water snakes, mammalian gonadotropins FSH and LH lack the response specificity seen in birds/mammals; each fraction could partially replicate the other's effects.
- **Taxonomic revision (non-fish)**: 
  - [[Wilfred T. Neill]] proposed moving the rainbow snake to genus *Farancia*.
  - [[Walter Auffenberg]] proposed unifying *Coluber* and *Masticophis* into a single genus, *Coluber*.
- **Organizational development**: The Society moved to formalize its status as a Southeastern Division of the [[American Society Of Ichthyologists And Herpetologists]], covering an 11-jurisdiction region (Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia, Florida, Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana, Tennessee, Kentucky, and the District of Columbia).
- **Leadership**: Officers elected for 1953 included [[Ralph W. Yerger]] (President, Florida State University), [[Donald C. Scott]] (Vice President, University of Georgia), and [[Wilfred T. Neill]] (Secretary-Treasurer, Reptile Institute, Silver Springs, Fla.).
- **Future meeting**: The third annual meeting was scheduled for fall 1953 at the University of Georgia, Athens, with a planned symposium on "Factors Affecting Distribution of Poikilothermic Vertebrates in the Southeastern States."
