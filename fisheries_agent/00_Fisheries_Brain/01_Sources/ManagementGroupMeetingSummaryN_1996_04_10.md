## Executive Summary

This document is a **management/administrative meeting summary** from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program]] (FWS Region 6), dated November 7, 1989. While not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, it contains critical **conservation status, hydrology, and regulatory data** pertaining to the endangered fish fauna of the Upper Colorado River Basin — principally the [[Razorback Sucker]], along with the broader "Colorado River fishes" complex (including [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail]]). Key discussion topics include the impending federal listing determination for the razorback sucker, interim flow recommendations for the [[Yampa River]], Section 7 consultation status on multiple water development biological opinions (Muddy Creek, Sandstone), and water rights acquisition strategies (Cross Mountain Ranch, Hinman Park, Maybell/Roberts Ditch) intended to protect instream flows for native fish recovery.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/Flow Regime:** The [[Yampa River]] is characterized in the Phase II flow recommendations report as having a natural hydrograph with **high peak spring flows** followed by **low, stable flows** for the remainder of the year — a regime the FWS recommended be *maintained* (not altered by new water development) to preserve conditions suitable for native fish recovery and spawning cues.
- **Instream Flow Protection:** Water rights acquisition efforts (Cross Mountain Ranch, Hinman Park donation, Maybell/Roberts Ditch senior water rights) are explicitly ecological measures aimed at securing **instream flows** for endangered fish habitat in the Yampa and Colorado River systems.
- **Ruedi Reservoir Releases:** Coordinated reservoir releases through the **15-mile reach** of the Colorado River (an area of documented critical habitat for endangered fishes) were finalized among FWS, Bureau of Reclamation (BR), and Colorado Water Conservation Board (CWCB) to supplement flows in this reach.
- **Biological Opinions & Habitat Impact Mitigation:**
  - The **Muddy Creek Biological Opinion** was revised to evaluate "sufficient progress" across **all five recovery elements** (not solely instream flow protection), broadening the ecological criteria used to assess whether water development impacts are being adequately offset.
  - The **Sandstone Biological Opinion** (due for finalization by March 31, 1990) remained contingent on unresolved issues: reservoir operation pending water marketing, and additional conservation measures to offset project impacts on fish habitat.
- **Emergency Response Niche Gap:** The Management Group identified a lack of rapid-response capability for field-level ecological emergencies (e.g., oil spills, fish kills) affecting these riverine habitats, though this was designated an agency (not Recovery Program) responsibility.

## Key Findings

- **Razorback Sucker Listing:** A listing package for the [[Razorback Sucker]] had been prepared for internal FWS review, with a legal determination deadline of **March 1990** — marking a pivotal regulatory milestone for this catostomid species.
- **Yampa River Flow Recommendations:** The FWS's November 9, 1989 interim report recommended **preserving the existing natural flow regime** of the Yampa River rather than issuing blanket jeopardy opinions on water development, with a public comment period through **December 15, 1989**.
- **Section 7 Consultation Status:**
  - **Muddy Creek Opinion** revised and near-finalization; accepted by FWS, State, and River District, using a five-element recovery progress standard.
  - **Sandstone Opinion** targeted for finalization by **March 31, 1990**, pending resolution of reservoir operations and conservation offset measures.
- **Water Rights Acquisition Progress:** Multiple concurrent efforts — Cross Mountain Ranch (appraisal in progress via The Nature Conservancy), Hinman Park donation (under TNC evaluation), and Maybell/Roberts Ditch senior rights (pending owner response) — represent the operational backbone of habitat flow protection strategy for the recovery program.
- **Species Impacted by Proposed Non-Native Introduction:** A planned meeting with Utah Division of Wildlife Resources concerned the potential introduction of **rainbow smelt** into Lake Powell, a non-native species introduction with possible ecological risk implications for the endangered native fish community of the Colorado River system.
- **Programmatic/Funding Context:** FY-90 budget reductions of 5–10% (Gramm-Rudman cuts) threatened program capacity, while a new policy on **multiyear project funding continuity** was being drafted, tying continued funding to demonstrated recovery progress — indirectly affecting sustained monitoring/research capacity for these fish populations.
- **Institutional Review:** The Technical Group structure supporting fish recovery science was flagged for restructuring evaluation (Lee Mills review), reflecting concerns about the scientific/technical orientation of program decision-making bodies. A **5-year "road map"** planning document for recovery actions was slated for draft review by February 1, 1990.
