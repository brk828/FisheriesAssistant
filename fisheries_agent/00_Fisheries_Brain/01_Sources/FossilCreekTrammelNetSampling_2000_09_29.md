## Executive Summary
This record documents a two-day trammel and gill net survey (September 25–26, 2000) conducted by Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) and USFWS Region 6 Fisheries personnel near the confluence of the [[Verde River]] and [[Fossil Creek]], Arizona. The stated primary objective was to capture native [[Gila Sp.]] (chub) individuals for genetic analysis at Arizona State University; **no Gila sp. were captured** during this effort. Instead, the catch was overwhelmingly dominated by non-native [[Centrarchidae|centrarchids]] and [[Ictaluridae|ictalurids]], with only a small number of native [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarkii*) recorded. Incidental bycatch included three [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriense longifemorale*), a state-sensitive subspecies.

## Background & Description
No morphometric, length, or weight data were recorded in this field summary; the report is a catch-tally/effort log rather than a biometric dataset. Species were identified only via standard AGFD four-letter alphanumeric codes:

| Code | Species | Common Name |
|------|---------|--------------|
| LECY | *Lepomis cyanellus* | [[Green Sunfish]] |
| AMNA | *Ameiurus natalis* | [[Yellow Bullhead]] |
| MIDO | *Micropterus dolomieu* | [[Smallmouth Bass]] |
| MISA | *Micropterus salmoides* | [[Largemouth Bass]] |
| PACL | *Pantosteus clarkii* | [[Desert Sucker]] |

No [[Gila Sp.]] individuals were morphologically assessed since none were captured.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Sampling occurred in the lentic-to-lotic transition zone at the [[Verde River]]–[[Fossil Creek]] confluence, a site of conservation interest for native [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]] due to its proximity to Fossil Creek's spring-fed, travertine-rich habitat.
- **Gear types**: A mix of 50 ft and 60 ft trammel nets (¾-inch mesh) and 150 ft "green meanie" gill nets were deployed across seven discrete stations, suggesting an effort to sample both nearshore/backwater and mid-channel habitats.
- **Community composition**: The catch was dominated by introduced piscivorous and habitat-generalist species (*Lepomis cyanellus*, *Ameiurus natalis*, *Micropterus dolomieu*, *Micropterus salmoides*), indicative of a non-native-dominated fish assemblage typical of lower Verde River reaches impacted by reservoir influence and non-native introductions.
- **Native species scarcity**: Only *Pantosteus clarkii* (desert sucker) was recorded among native fishes (3 individuals, Site #4), and the target native cyprinid, [[Gila Sp.]], was entirely absent from the catch — a notable finding given this was the stated primary sampling objective.
- **Non-fish bycatch**: Three [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] individuals (*Kinosternon sonoriense longifemorale*) were captured at Sites #3 and #5, indicating co-occurrence of this sensitive turtle subspecies with the fish assemblage in trammel-netted habitat.

## Key Findings
- **Zero target-species capture**: Despite being the explicit purpose of the survey, no *Gila sp.* were captured across all seven sampling stations over two days, highlighting either very low native chub abundance/density or detectability issues near this confluence at the time of sampling.
- **Non-native dominance**: Aggregate catch totals across all stations were approximately:
  - LECY (green sunfish): ~88 individuals
  - AMNA (yellow bullhead): ~40 individuals
  - MIDO (smallmouth bass): ~14 individuals
  - MISA (largemouth bass): ~6 individuals
  - PACL (desert sucker): 3 individuals
  This pattern (>97% non-native catch) underscores the degree of non-native fish establishment in this reach of the [[Verde River]] system.
- **Spatial pattern**: Non-native abundance (particularly LECY and AMNA) was consistently high across all seven stations regardless of gear type (trammel vs. gill net), while native desert sucker occurrence was limited to a single station (#4).
- **Conservation implication**: The complete absence of *Gila sp.* combined with heavy non-native presence supports concerns regarding non-native species impacts on native [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]] recovery efforts in the [[Verde River]] drainage, relevant to ongoing management and genetic sampling programs for native chub conservation.
- **Incidental sensitive-species data**: Documentation of *Kinosternon sonoriense longifemorale* at two sites adds incidental occurrence data for this AGFD Species of Greatest Conservation Need within the survey area.
