# Razorback Sucker Monitoring in the Verde River, Perkinsville, Arizona (1993–1994)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Native Fish Program]] (author: [[Kirk L Young]], dated January 1–7, 1995) reports monitoring of [[Razorback Sucker]] in the [[Verde River]] near [[Perkinsville Arizona]] during 1993 and 1994. Approximately 1,200 razorbacks, all over 300 mm total length (TL), were stocked in March 1993. The stocked fish have formed a semi-resident population that has persisted in a large, connected [[Backwater Habitat]] near the stocking site. Monitoring relied on [[Mark-Recapture Monitoring]] with [[PIT Tagging]] and on recording the incidence of [[Lernaea]] (anchor worm) infection and scarring on captured fish.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]], a large catostomid (sucker) native to the Colorado River system.
- **Stocking:** About 1,200 individuals released in March 1993 at sizes greater than 300 mm TL.
- **Monitoring focus:** Capture totals, tagging, recaptures, and the proportion of fish carrying [[Lernaea]] parasites or scars from prior infection.
- **Tagging method:** Individuals were tagged with PIT tags, which allowed individual identification and length and weight tracking across capture events.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The monitored population uses a large, connected backwater adjacent to the stocking site on the Verde River. The memo does not report water chemistry, flow, or other habitat measurements.
- **Parasitism:** Lernaea infection was recorded at every sampling date. Active infections and healed scars both increased over the 1994 season, suggesting sustained exposure to the parasite. The memo does not identify causes or host-parasite dynamics beyond these observations.
- **Scope:** The document is a monitoring memo. It does not describe other aquatic taxa, plankton, or invertebrates in the study area.

## Key Findings

**1. Population establishment**
- A semi-resident population of razorback suckers was established following the March 1993 stocking.
- The population has persisted in a large connected backwater near the stocking site.

- Infection incidence (scars or active Lernaea) rose from 2.3% in the first sample to 94–100% in the August, September, and October 1994 samples.
- Scars were more common than active infections in every sample after the first, indicating that many fish had been infected previously.
- Recaptures occurred in the 1994 samples. The October 1994 sample is very small (n = 2).

**3. Individual recapture records (Table 2)**
- Table 2 lists PIT tag numbers with length (mm) and weight (g) at each capture and recapture (for example, 1 FOE641 55A, 7F7D181264, and 1F3CO34F53 in the early section).
- Recaptured individuals show length and weight changes between dates. Several records show gains, and a few show losses.
- The full table contains a long list of individual tag records (roughly 150), which are not reproduced here.
- Across the listed single-capture records, total lengths range from about 324 mm to 464 mm and weights from about 300 g to 950 g.

**4. Conclusions and status**
- The stocked fish have survived and remained in the study area for more than a year.
- Lernaea infection and scarring were widespread by late summer 1994, a notable finding for the population's health.
- The memo provides monitoring data and does not state management recommendations or a formal conclusion.
