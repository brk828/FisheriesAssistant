# Lake Mohave Experimental Gill Netting Survey

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #catostomidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document records experimental gill netting and electrofishing surveys conducted on [[Lake Mohave]] in May 1986 targeting [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The survey captured 68 razorback suckers via gill netting (26 net nights effort) and 18 via electrofishing (202 minutes effort), documenting length and weight measurements. A small incidental catch of channel catfish was also recorded. The data represent standardized sampling of this endangered catostomid species in a Colorado River reservoir system.

## Background & Description

[[Razorback Sucker]] (abbreviated "RzSu" in data tables) is a large-bodied catostomid fish native to the [[Colorado River]] basin. The species is characterized by a distinctive dorsal keel or "razorback" ridge. This survey targeted populations in [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir formed by [[Davis Dam]] on the Colorado River between Arizona and California, downstream of [[Hoover Dam]].

The document includes field data sheets with location maps showing sampling areas including [[Cottonwood Cove]], [[Eagle Cove]], [[Power Line Wash]], and other named locations along the reservoir. Survey methods included experimental gill netting and boat electrofishing, standard techniques for sampling large-bodied fish in reservoir habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Mohave]] represents critical habitat for [[Razorback Sucker]], an endangered species that has experienced dramatic population declines throughout its native range in the Colorado River system. The reservoir environment provides spawning and rearing habitat, though recruitment success has been limited in many Colorado River impoundments.

The survey captured primarily adult-sized fish, with total lengths ranging from 499-698 mm in the gill net sample and 500-636 mm in the electrofishing sample. No young-of-year or juvenile fish are evident in the dataset, suggesting potential recruitment limitations typical of razorback sucker populations in regulated river systems.

Incidental catch included [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), a non-native species in the Colorado River system that may compete with or prey upon native fishes.

## Key Findings

**Gill Netting Results (May 7-8, 1986):**
- Effort: 26 net nights
- Total [[Razorback Sucker]] captured: 68 individuals
- Length range: 499-698 mm total length
- Weight range: 1,340-4,000 grams
- Largest specimen: 698 mm TL, 3,500 g
- Incidental catch: 2 [[Channel Catfish]] (385-425 mm, 580-650 g)

**Electrofishing Results (May 6, 1986):**
- Effort: 202 minutes
- Total [[Razorback Sucker]] captured: 18 individuals (50 additional observed visually but not collected)
- Mean length: 569.7 mm (SD = 46.3)
- Mean weight: 2,160 g (SD = 562.1)
- Length range: 500-636 mm total length
- Weight range: 1,300-3,150 grams

**Population Characteristics:**
- All captured fish were adult-sized (>499 mm)
- No evidence of recent recruitment (no juveniles captured)
- Size distribution suggests an aging population without successful reproduction
- Weight-length relationships indicate generally good body condition

The absence of juvenile razorback suckers in this comprehensive sampling effort highlights conservation concerns regarding recruitment failure in [[Lake Mohave]], a pattern documented across much of the species' remaining range in the Colorado River basin.
