# Serological Analysis of Three Populations of Golden Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmo-aguabonita

## Executive Summary

This study compares three populations of [[Golden Trout]] (*[[Salmo Aguabonita]]*) using [[Erythrocyte Antigens]] and [[Agglutination Test|agglutination]] scores. Two populations, from [[Golden Trout Creek]] and the [[South Fork Of The Kern River]], are isolated streams within the species' ancestral home range. The third, [[Cottonwood Lakes]], was founded by 12 trout moved from the South Fork of the Kern River about 90 years earlier. Golden trout from Golden Trout Creek differed significantly from both the Cottonwood Lakes and South Fork of the Kern River fish. No significant difference was detected between the Cottonwood Lakes and South Fork of the Kern River populations. The authors conclude that the antigenic method is feasible for population discrimination and that the pattern agrees with historical records.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Golden Trout]] (*[[Salmo Aguabonita]]*), a trout of the eastern Sierra Nevada in California.
- **Comparison species:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*) served as the reference strain. Other species examined in earlier work by [[Calaprice And Cushing 1964]] included brown trout (*Salmo trutta*), [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarkii henshawi*), and eastern brook trout (*Salvelinus fontinalis*).
- **Study populations:**
  - [[Golden Trout Creek]] (Tulare County), sampled at Big Whitney Meadows by fly fishing. Part of the ancestral home range.
  - [[South Fork Of The Kern River]] (Tulare County), sampled about 2 miles east of Tunnel Ranger Station by fly fishing. Part of the ancestral home range.
  - [[Cottonwood Lakes]] (Inyo County), sampled by trapping at Cottonwood Lakes 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. Originally barren; founded by 12 fish.
- **Transplant history:** According to [[Evermann 1906]], 12 golden trout from the South Fork of the Kern River were introduced into Cottonwood Creek in 1876. In 1891, fish from the creek were moved to the Cottonwood Lakes. No further transplants among the three populations are recorded. A later attempt to divert water from Golden Trout Creek into the South Fork of the Kern River, dug in 1883 by ranchers, was abandoned after the tunnel and ditch collapsed.
- **Methods overview:**
  - Field work at the [[California Department Of Fish And Game]] spawning station at Cottonwood Lakes (June 1963) and at the [[Convict Creek Experiment Station]], Mono County (August 1963). Live fish were held at [[Hot Creek Fish Hatchery]].
  - Blood was collected by heart puncture into modified [[Alsever's Solution]]. Samples were stored for several days in snowbanks.
  - Tests used capillary tubes or slide plates. Scores were recorded as – (no agglutination), 1 to 4 (increasing agglutination), and tr (trace). The capillary tube method became the standard.
  - Immune sera were made by injecting rabbits with erythrocytes (1 cc daily for 7 days), bleeding after 14 days. Sera were then absorbed with washed erythrocytes from individual fish, agitated up to 30 minutes, and centrifuged.
  - Key reagents: anti-golden serum 27 and anti-rainbow serum 26, both used in unabsorbed and [[Absorbed Immune Serum|absorbed]] forms.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Stream versus lake setting:** The South Fork of the Kern River and Golden Trout Creek are streams. The Cottonwood Lakes were barren before stocking. The authors note that differences in selection pressure between stream and lake environments could have driven divergence.
- **Isolation and drift:** The Cottonwood Lakes population descends from a small founder group. The authors list three likely sources of divergence from the parent stock: a non-representative founder sample, [[Genetic Drift]], and differential selection between stream and lake habitats.
- **Hybridization concerns:** Some "rainbow-appearing" trout occurred in the Cottonwood Lakes and in lakes stocked with its progeny. Earlier suspicion of rainbow-golden hybridization is not supported by the present results. The authors suggest alternative explanations: strain history, lake development, and hatchery selection during egg-taking and rearing.
- **Remote, lightly disturbed habitat:** Many Sierra Nevada golden trout lakes are remote and little affected by people. Maintenance stocking of young fish is noted as an opportunity to study selection.
- **Broader context:** Many other freshwater game fish in California were introduced from elsewhere, sometimes from very few fish. The authors expect genetic change under such conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **Intrapopulation variation exists in all three populations.** Unabsorbed sera showed considerable antigenic diversity. Absorption with selected erythrocytes made the distinctions clearer. Fish could be tentatively assigned to four classes using anti-golden serum absorbed with rainbow erythrocytes, and anti-rainbow serum absorbed with golden erythrocytes.
2. **Cottonwood Lakes:** Anti-golden serum 27 and anti-rainbow serum 26 detected antigenic variation among individual fish (Table 1). The variation was more obvious with absorbed sera. Classification into antigenic categories is possible but was not yet systematically investigated.
3. **Golden Trout Creek:** Fish showed a varied pattern of agglutination with the same unabsorbed sera. Absorption refined the distinctions (Table 2). Absorbed reagents were useful in separating antigenic types.
4. **South Fork of the Kern River:** All South Fork fish tested gave negative reactions with absorbed anti-golden serum. Reaction patterns with unabsorbed anti-rainbow serum were somewhat similar to those of Golden Trout Creek.
5. **Interpopulation comparison:** Under standardized conditions (same bleeding day, same sera), absorbed anti-rainbow serum 26 separated two antigenic classes in each population (Table 3).
   - Cottonwood Lakes and South Fork of the Kern River did not differ significantly. Fisher's exact probability was 0.3983 at dilution 1/4 and 0.3376 at 1/8 (both n.s.).
   - Golden Trout Creek differed significantly from both. Reported probabilities for the Golden Trout Creek comparisons were 0.0438 and 0.0072 at 1/4 dilution, and 0.0003 and 0.0031 at 1/8 dilution.
   - Conclusions were the same at both dilutions.
6. **Rainbow-like antigens:** The frequency of "rainbow-like" antigens was higher in the Cottonwood Lakes and South Fork of the Kern River populations than in Golden Trout Creek. Table 4 implies positive/negative totals of 24 (South Fork of the Kern River), 21 (Cottonwood Lake 3), and 36 (Golden Trout Creek) at dilution 1/4.
7. **Genetic interpretation:** The close antigenic resemblance of Cottonwood and South Fork fish suggests little genetic mixing from the 1883 diversion. The authors call this similarity "an interesting evolutionary problem," given the 90-year separation and small founder group. The Golden Trout Creek differences agree with earlier spotting and morphological distinctions reported by [[Evermann 1906]].
8. **Methodological conclusion:** The authors judge that erythrocyte antigen tests are feasible for population studies of golden trout. They recommend extending the work with additional reagents or larger samples, and combining with other serum constituents (cf. [[Grunder 1966]]). Quantification and [[Multivariate Analysis]] are suggested to improve discrimination.
9. **Management implication:** Small-sample genetic and ecological studies of transplanted populations are proposed as a basis for sound management and as data for evolutionary research. The authors cite [[Utter And Ridgway 1966]], who used similar techniques to study inbreeding in Lahontan cutthroat trout.
