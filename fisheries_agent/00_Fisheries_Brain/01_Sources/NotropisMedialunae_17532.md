# Notropis medialunae n. sp.: A New Endemic Cyprinid from La Media Luna, San Luis Potosí, Mexico, with Remarks on the Subgenus Aztecula and the Genus Dionda

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, Contribution No. 15 from the Laboratorio de Vertebrados, Facultad de Ciencias Biológicas, [[Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León]] (Monterrey, Mexico), by S. Contreras-B and Jorge Verduzco-M, describes [[Notropis Medialunae]] as a new species of [[Cyprinidae]] known only from a distributary channel of the [[La Media Luna Springs]], 7 km south of Rioverde, San Luis Potosí. The fish is assigned to the [[Subgenus Aztecula]] of [[Notropis]]. The authors distinguish it mainly by low lateral-line scale counts and few predorsal scales, and they report a double S-shaped intestine that they consider a possible subgeneric character. They also discuss a similar gut form in [[Dionda]] and suggest that the subgenus and related lineages may represent independent derivations within Notropis.

## Background & Description

**Collection and material.** The La Media Luna springs were explored in July 1963 by the authors and a group from the Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León. The material later revealed additional novelties with Nearctic affinities, especially Lerma-Valley of México and Upper Pánuco basin assemblages. Twenty-five specimens in two series were examined.

- **Holotype:** UNAM (Instituto de Biología), male, 43.7 mm standard length, collected at a creek 7 km SSE of Rioverde, San Luis Potosí, July 1968, by S. Contreras et al. (Table I lists 43.3 mm; Figure 1 gives 43.7 mm).
- **Paratypes:** UANL No. 1032 (13 specimens; 30.8–47.5 mm SL) and UANL No. 1320 (11 specimens; 32.0–48.2 mm SL), same locality as holotype, 15 July 1968. Table I lists the paratypes as 19 individuals.

**Diagnosis.** Intestine forming a double S-shape; lower lateral-line scale count (38–44) than related species; 19–23 predorsal scales; pharyngeal teeth 4-4 (20 specimens counted).

**Description.**
- Body robust and slightly compressed; caudal peduncle slender; dorsal profile more convex than ventral profile.
- Head conical, with width subequal to height.
- Mouth terminal, inferior lip slightly protruding; lower jaw cut on side view and forming an angle with the front of the head.
- Lateral line decurved in anterior half, continuous with the lateral band in the posterior half.
- Dorsal fin origin behind the pelvic insertion; anal fin origin behind the dorsal fin base. Fins are short.
- Breeding tubercles small, better developed on top of the head and anterodorsally on the body, less so on the lower side of the caudal peduncle.

**Fin and scale counts (holotype and series).** Pelvic rays 8 (15 specimens) and 9 (25 specimens) were recorded. Lateral-line scales were 38–44 across the series (Table II). Predorsal scales were 19–21, with a range of 18–23 in Table II.

**Coloration.**
- *Alive:* all fins have basal two-thirds yellowish; dorsal fin has a blackish distal third with a whitish border; other fins are milky on the distal third, except the caudal fin, which is usually dark. Body creamy with a longitudinal black band ending in a darker basicaudal spot nearly fused with the band. Dorsum of head and body green.
- *Preserved:* scale pockets finely marked, except on venter. The lateral band begins at the snout, is narrower than the pupil, passes the insertion of the opercle, and continues straight to the basicaudal spots. Several deep, irregular lines run backwards dorsal to the band, branching finely. The opercle is silvery. The venter appears silvery or dark due to the dark peritoneum and translucent body walls.

**Etymology.** The name *medialunae* refers to La Media Luna, the type locality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat at type locality.**
- Water clear and colourless.
- Current moderate to strong.
- Banks steep, nearly vertical; sides and bottom hard with little sediment, except in shallow side bays where the bottom is silt.
- The channel may be artificial, roughly 4 ft wide and 3 ft deep.
- Aquatic vegetation absent or scarce.
- Water temperature 27–29 °C.
- Adults collected in the channel; young collected in the shallows.

**Associated species** (as listed by the authors): *Astyanax fasciatus* (Cuvier); a *Dionda* sp. under study by the authors; *Cualac tesselatus* Miller; *Ataeniobius toweri* (Meek); *[genus illegible in scan] bartoni* (Bean); and *C. labridens* Pellegrin.

**Distribution.** Known only from this distributary channel of La Media Luna. The authors note that the location is isolated, and the fish is endemic to the channel.

**Biogeographic affinities.** The authors describe the assemblage as Nearctic, with links to the Lerma-Valley of Mexico and Upper Pánuco basins. The fish belongs to the Río Pánuco basin, related to the Río Pánuco fauna.

The authors regard the *N. sallaei* specimens from the Río San Juan as *N. mexicanus*, a species not recognized by their source. They suggest that *N. sallaei* may combine characters of *N. lermae* and *N. mexicanus*, and that it may be a misidentification of extreme specimens of both species.

**Gut morphology and related genera.**
- The double S-shaped intestine occurs in *N. medialunae*, in the specimens of *N. sallaei* (= *N. mexicanus*?), and in a recent description of [[Notropis Mekistocholas]] from the Cape Fear River, North Carolina (Snelson, 1971).
- In [[Dionda]], an undescribed form from the Río Pánuco (Tamesí) with a very close double S-shaped intestine was discovered by Carl L. Hubbs (Darnell, 1962). [[Dionda Rasconis]] has a more coiled double S. [[Dionda Diaboli]] and [[Dionda Episcopa]] show further coiling.
- Developmental observations: all *Dionda* species examined begin with a simpler coiling of the gut that becomes more complex with growth.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Notropis medialunae* n. sp., known from the holotype (UNAM, 43.7 mm SL) and paratypes (UANL Nos. 1032 and 1320) collected at La Media Luna, San Luis Potosí.
- **Endemism:** the species is known only from one distributary channel of La Media Luna, a restricted habitat with clear, fast water, hard substrate, little vegetation, and temperatures of 27–29 °C.
- **Diagnostic characters:** the lowest lateral-line count in the subgenus (38–44 scales), fewest predorsal scales (19–23), double S-shaped intestine, pharyngeal teeth 4-4, and a lateral band that is unique in the group as described.
- **Taxonomic confusion:** the authors consider the nominal species *N. sallaei* to be a possible misidentification combining characters of *N. lermae* and *N. mexicanus*, and they recommend caution in its identification.
- **Subgeneric character proposed:** the double S-shaped intestine may be a useful subgeneric criterion, although the authors doubt it is sufficient alone. The character may also be important in studies of evolution and herbivory, possibly showing character displacement between sympatric *Dionda* species.
- **Phylogenetic view:** the authors suggest that the subgenus Aztecula may be monophyletic and constitute an archetypal lineage, but they do not postulate direct relationships between *N. medialunae*, *N. mekistocholas*, and *Dionda*. They consider these three independent derivations from the genus Notropis.
- **Developmental work:** the authors plan to describe developmental stages of the intestine in a separate publication.
