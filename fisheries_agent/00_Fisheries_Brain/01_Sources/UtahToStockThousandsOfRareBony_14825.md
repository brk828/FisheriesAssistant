# Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program Newsletter, Fall/Winter 1998

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This Fall/Winter 1998 newsletter from the [[Upper Colorado River Endangered Fish Recovery Program]] reports on recovery work for four endangered fishes of the upper [[Colorado River]] basin: [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. It covers a planned stocking of 20,000 bonytails by the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], a coordinated reservoir release that boosted spring flows for spawning, the opening of the [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]], and a profile of program director [[Henry Maddux]]. The program's stated goal is to re-establish self-sustaining populations while providing for future water development.

## Background & Description

- **Program structure:** A multi-agency effort. Participants include the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Western Area Power Administration]], the states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, and several conservation, water-user, and energy groups, among them the [[Environmental Defense Fund]] and [[National Audubon Society]].
- **Bonytail status:** The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] listed the bonytail as endangered in 1980. In the upper Colorado River basin it is nearly extinct. It was once "among the most numerous species in the Colorado River."
- **Suspected causes of decline:** Habitat changes from dams that altered the natural river environment, and conflicts with non-native fish species.
- **Stocking program:** At least 2,000 bonytails were stocked annually for the last three years, mostly 4 to 5 inches long, with a few about 12 inches. Fish were raised at the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico, then grown at the [[Wahweap Hatchery]] in Utah.
- **Tagging:** All stocked fish received electronic tags, which are read with a hand-held scanner when recaptured. Radio tags were implanted in 10 adult fish for weekly tracking.
- **Director background:** [[Henry Maddux]] earned a B.S. in biology from the University of Arizona in 1982 and an M.S. in fish biology from South Dakota State University in 1985. He worked for the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service before becoming program director in October 1997.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow management:** Spring peak flows clean spawning beds and keep sediment from smothering endangered fish eggs. Coordinated releases were used to mimic the river's natural peak.
- **Habitat restoration:** The program prioritizes restoring wetland habitat, providing adequate stream flows, and restoring bottomlands.
- **Fish passage:** The [[Redlands Fish Ladder]] allows endangered fish to reach upstream habitat in the [[Gunnison River]].
- **Non-native species:** Managing conflicts with non-native fish is identified as an ongoing program need.
- **Broader benefits:** Maddux states that maintaining river flows also supports other wildlife and the broader health of upper basin states.

## Key Findings

- **Bonytail stocking:** The Utah Division of Wildlife Resources planned to stock a total of 20,000 endangered bonytails in the Colorado and Green rivers by the next spring.
- **Tracking results:** One radio-tagged bonytail was found to have moved downstream 80 miles. Two adult bonytails displayed spawning behavior in June, moving in tandem through one "hot" summer night.
- **Testing a hypothesis:** Researchers had assumed that recovery efforts for the Colorado squawfish would also benefit the bonytail. Restocking allows this assumption to be tested. Chart noted that bonytail habitat preferences and spawning behavior still need study.
- **Coordinated reservoir releases:** Flows in the "15-mile reach" of the Grand Valley were boosted by 2,470 cubic feet per second (cfs) during the spring peak. On May 31 the peak reached 16,000 cfs.
  - Green Mountain Reservoir: 1,150 cfs
  - Ruedi Reservoir: 620 cfs
  - Wolford Mountain Reservoir: 300 cfs
  - Dillon Reservoir: 200 cfs
  - Williams Fork Reservoir: 200 cfs
  - Releases returned to average or below by June 3.
- **Coordinated Reservoir Operations team:** Formed in 1995 with representatives from the Colorado River Water Conservation District, Denver Water Department, the cities of Aurora and Colorado Springs, and several federal and state agencies. Public notification was handled by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]]'s Eastern Colorado Area Office.
- **Hatchery opening:** Razorback suckers and other endangered fish are raised at the [[Ouray National Fish Hatchery]] near Vernal, Utah. The hatchery's dedication and open house took place on September 19.
- **Program progress:** The director's message reports that squawfish populations are growing, bonytails have been reintroduced in both the Colorado and Green rivers, and razorback suckers are being stocked to bolster low numbers. Funding is shifting from research toward on-the-ground work such as restoration, fish raising, non-native fish control, fish ladder construction, and population monitoring.
