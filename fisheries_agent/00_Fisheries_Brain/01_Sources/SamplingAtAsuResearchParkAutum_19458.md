# Sampling at ASU Research Park, Autumn 1992

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #centrarchidae #catostomidae #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

Personnel from [[Arizona State University]] conducted fish sampling at [[ASU Research Park]] in Tempe, Arizona on 07-08 October 1992 to assess fish community status. Multiple sampling methods including trammel nets, experimental gill nets, and seining captured 1,315 individuals representing 11 taxa. The survey documented presence of endangered native species including [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), both of which were PIT tagged and released. [[Largemouth Bass]] dominated entanglement net catches (47%), while juvenile [[Green Sunfish]] predominated seine collections (97.4%). The report also documents stocking history complications and an incidental capture of non-native [[Pacu]] (*Colossoma* sp.).

## Background & Description

The sampling effort employed multiple gear types deployed across multiple lakes within the research park facility. Entanglement nets included 5 trammel net sets and 3 experimental gill net sets deployed overnight for 12 hours. Seine sampling utilized two net sizes: 1.8-m-long and 4.6-m-long nets, both 1.2-m-deep with 0.32-cm and 0.62-cm mesh sizes respectively, conducted along shorelines during daylight hours.

The [[ASU Research Park]] lakes have received multiple fish stockings over time, including [[Grass Carp]] (*Ctenopharyngodon idella*) stocked by the Park in June 1990, and 17 adult [[Bonytail]] stocked by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] on 24 July 1992. The July 1992 bonytail were salvaged from Palm Lake at the [[Arizona Nature Conservancy Hassayampa Preserve]] in April 1992, originally derived from stockings of 879 juveniles in November 1986 and 4,600 young in November 1987 from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Dexter National Hatchery and Research Center]], New Mexico.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish community at [[ASU Research Park]] represents a mixture of native endangered species, sport fish, and non-native species. The presence of endangered [[Colorado River]] native fishes ([[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and [[Colorado Squawfish]]) reflects ongoing conservation and recovery efforts for these imperiled taxa. The facility serves as a refuge and research site for monitoring survival and behavior of stocked endangered species.

Species composition varied seasonally, with spring 1992 sampling showing predominance of [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]], while autumn 1992 sampling revealed dominance of juvenile [[Green Sunfish]], reflecting different phenologies among species. The capture of non-native [[Pacu]] indicates ongoing problems with aquarium releases into the system.

[[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) proved difficult to capture despite angler reports of the species being taken by hook and line during summer. This reflects the species' behavior and the limitations of routine sampling techniques, combined with relatively low sampling effort.

## Key Findings

**Entanglement Net Results (n = 68 individuals, 8 taxa):**
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: 32 individuals (47% of catch)
- [[Bluegill]]: 11 individuals (16%)
- [[Threadfin Shad]]: 10 individuals (15%)
- [[Grass Carp]], [[Bonytail]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Blue Tilapia]]: 1-5 individuals each (2-7% each)

**Endangered Species Captures:**
- One female [[Bonytail]] captured from Lake B: 41.2 cm TL, 0.420 kg, PIT tagged (7F7DOE2216) and released
- Four male [[Razorback Sucker]] captured from Lakes A and B: 47.3, 49.2, and 52.0 cm TL, all exceeded 1.00 kg, PIT tagged (7F7D-0E3E47, -0D6712, -OE297A) and released
- No [[Colorado Squawfish]] captured despite angler reports

**Seine Results (n = 1,247 individuals, 3 taxa):**
- [[Green Sunfish]]: 1,214 individuals (97.4%), mostly young-of-year <3.5 cm TL
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: 28 individuals (2.2%)
- [[Redbelly Tilapia]]: 5 individuals (0.2%)

**Non-Native Species Issue:**
- Adult [[Pacu]] (*Colossoma* sp.: Characidae) caught 04 November 1992: 42.5 cm TL, 1.485 kg
- Angler reported catching four such fish total, with observations of numerous others up to 3 kg
- Represents aquarium releases requiring management attention

**Stocking History Complications:**
- None of the 17 PIT-tagged bonytail stocked in July 1992 were recaptured during autumn sampling
- Documentation gaps exist regarding complete stocking history
- Request made for comprehensive stocking records, measurements, and PIT tag numbers

**Future Sampling Plans:**
- Larval fish sampling planned for winter-spring
- Netting-seining scheduled for May 1993
