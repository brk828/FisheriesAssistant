# Black River and Little Colorado River Tributaries Fisheries Survey (July 21-25, 1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

A five-day fisheries survey of tributaries of the [[Black River]] and [[Little Colorado River]] in Apache, Greenlee, and Coconino Counties, Arizona, was conducted July 21-25, 1997, by a six-person team from [[Arizona State University]]. Sampling covered 14 Black River drainage localities and 5 Little Colorado River drainage localities. Sixteen fish species were collected, including [[Apache Trout]], several non-native salmonids, and native cyprinids and catostomids. Notable observations included a continuing population of [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] at Dines Tank, a downstream range extension for [[Loach Minnow]], and a brown trout found above fish barriers that had been treated in 1996 and restocked with Apache trout.

## Background & Description

The survey was a field-based fisheries inventory, not a research study with stated hypotheses. The document identifies the participants and sampling localities, describes methods and results, and provides site-level field data in an appendix.

**Participants:** Brian Bagley, Rachel Bradford, Pat Chase, Terry Inman, Paul Marsh, and Patti Sowka (Arizona State University).

**Sampling localities:**
- *Black River drainage (14 sites):* [[Boneyard Creek]] above Sierra Blanca Lake; several reaches of the [[East Fork Black River]] (Open Draw, Diamond Rock, and F.S. rd. 24); two North Fork East Fork sites (Crosby Crossing and Three Forks); and eight [[West Fork Black River]] sites around F.S. rd. 116 (above, between, and below fish barriers), the campground, F.S. rd. 68, and F.S. rd. 25.
- *Little Colorado River drainage (5 sites):* [[Dines Tank]] (Leonard Canyon), East Clear Creek at Barbershop Canyon, Knoll Lake (Creek below), West Fork Little Colorado River at F.S. rd. 113, and Willow Springs Lake.

**Methods:** Habitats were sampled with a gas-powered [[Smith-Root DC Backpack Electrofisher]], dip nets, gill nets, trammel nets, and seines. Catch was reported as number of fish caught and [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (fish caught per minute of shocking).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Fish community:** The 16 species collected comprised five salmonids (Apache trout, an Apache-rainbow hybrid, rainbow trout, brown trout, and brook trout), a range of native and other cyprinids and catostomids, and two centrarchids (largemouth bass and green sunfish). The document does not classify species as native or introduced.
- **Fish barriers:** The West Fork Black River site near F.S. rd. 116 had barriers that divided the stream into upper, middle, and lower reaches. The area was poisoned in 1996 and then stocked with Apache trout. A brown trout found above the barriers indicates that the treatment did not fully exclude this species, or that it reached the area afterward. The document does not say which.
- **Invertebrates and herpetofauna:** Crayfish were recorded as present at several East Fork sites but absent at Boneyard Creek. Three narrow-headed garter snakes were seen at the Open Draw confluence and two at the Diamond Rock reach. One native clam shell was also encountered. [[Narrow-Headed Garter Snake]] observations are noted as incidental.
- **Equipment limitation:** At the Open Draw site, the shocker battery was weak during sampling (425 seconds of shocking), which may have affected catch efficiency there.

## Key Findings

**Overall results**
- 16 fish species were collected across the survey.
- Site totals from the appendix (all reported with shocking time):

| Site | Total fish | Shocking time (s) | Species recorded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boneyard Creek (above Sierra Blanca Lake), July 24 | 84 | 488 | Brook trout 39, speckled dace 40, Sonora sucker 3, brown trout 1, desert sucker 1 |
| East Fork Black River at Open Draw (0.5 mi downstream), July 23 | 40 | 425 | Speckled dace 27, Sonora sucker 7, desert sucker 4, loach minnow 2 |
| East Fork Black River, Diamond Rock to 1.2 km upstream, July 23 | 71 | 1730 | Speckled dace 59, desert sucker 11, brown trout 1 |
| East Fork Black River, Diamond Rock to F.S. rd. 24 | 84 | 1657 | Speckled dace 31, brown trout 19, desert sucker 24, Sonora sucker 7, Apache trout 3 |

- Boneyard Creek had the highest catch rate recorded in the appendix, at 10.3 fish per minute of shocking, consistent with the table.

**Special notes**
- **Little Colorado Spinedace:** Dines Tank still held "a fair number" of spinedace.
- **Loach Minnow:** Two individuals were collected at the Open Draw confluence on the East Fork Black River, 0.5 miles downstream of the previously known downstream limit.
- **Brown trout above barriers:** One brown trout was collected above the West Fork fish barriers near F.S. rd. 116, an area poisoned in 1996 and subsequently stocked with [[Apache Trout]].
