# Batch Consultation Letter - Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest Section 7 Projects

#endangered-species #consultation #forest-management #habitat-conservation #prescribed-fire

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal consultation letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Arizona Ecological Services Field Office to the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]] regarding 27 proposed forest management projects submitted for Section 7 consultation under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The consultation, dated February 19, 1997, addresses projects including prescribed burns, timber sales, trail reconstruction, and vegetation treatments. Of the 27 projects reviewed, the Service concurred with "may affect, not likely to adversely affect" determinations for 22 projects. Two projects ([[Milligan Burn]] and [[Tutt Creek]]) were withdrawn by the Forest Service, and three projects required additional consultation. The consultation process involved collaboration with USFWS personnel from the Western Washington Office in Olympia, Washington, and included meetings on February 11-12, 1997, in Springerville, Arizona.

## Background & Description

The consultation covers a diverse array of forest management activities across the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]], including:

- **Prescribed burns**: Multiple projects designed to reduce fuel loading from slash accumulations and natural fuel buildup, typically implemented as cool-season, low-intensity fires after August 31 to create mosaic burn patterns
- **Timber sales and thinning**: Commercial and precommercial thinning operations targeting trees of various diameter classes, with prescriptions designed to improve forest health, reduce dwarf-mistletoe infection, and accelerate development of old-growth characteristics
- **Trail reconstruction**: Relocation of trails out of riparian areas to reduce erosion and sedimentation
- **Vegetation treatments**: Opening creation, fuel break development, and restoration activities
- **Infrastructure projects**: Road reconstruction, parking lot construction, land acquisition, and easement grants

Projects were evaluated based on biological assessments and evaluations (BA&Es) submitted by the Forest Service, supplemented by field meetings and additional documentation. The consultation addressed effects to multiple listed and proposed species, including [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (MSO), [[Bald Eagle]], [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Mexican Grey Wolf]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], and [[Jaguar]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The project areas encompass diverse habitat types across elevation gradients:

- **Ponderosa pine forests**: Dominant vegetation type, often with screwleaf muhly understory, classified as "other forest and woodland type" in the [[MSO Recovery Plan]]
- **Mixed conifer**: Douglas fir, white fir, spruce associations along major drainages and north-facing slopes, providing critical nesting and roosting habitat for [[Mexican Spotted Owl]]
- **Pine-oak woodlands**: Gambel oak with ponderosa pine, designated as "restricted habitat" under MSO Recovery Plan guidelines
- **Pinyon-juniper**: Lower elevation woodlands targeted for opening creation and fuel reduction
- **Riparian corridors**: Critical habitat for [[Loach Minnow]], [[Apache Trout]], and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] along streams including [[Campbell Blue Creek]], [[Blue River]], [[San Francisco River]], [[Black River]], [[Willow Creek]], and [[Clear Creek]]

The consultation extensively addresses [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] habitat management, with detailed discussion of Protected Activity Centers (PACs), restricted habitat, protected habitat, and critical habitat designations. PACs are designed to encompass approximately 600 acres providing 75% of the area used by owls for nesting, roosting, foraging, and dispersal. The [[MSO Recovery Plan]] establishes threshold conditions for habitat management, including specific targets for tree density by diameter class, basal area (150 square feet per acre), and retention of large trees (≥24 inches dbh).

Aquatic habitats support threatened and endangered fish species with specific habitat requirements. [[Loach Minnow]] populations in [[Campbell Blue Creek]] and [[Blue River]] are vulnerable to sedimentation from upslope activities. [[Apache Trout]] restoration efforts focus on headwater streams with suitable spawning habitat. The consultation addresses cumulative watershed effects, noting that substantial changes to stream channels and hydrologic regimes have occurred over 150 years due to multiple land uses.

## Key Findings

**Consultation Outcomes:**
- **22 projects**: Concurrence with "may affect, not likely to adversely affect" determinations for listed species
- **2 projects withdrawn**: [[Milligan Burn]] and [[Tutt Creek]] removed from consultation package by Forest Service
- **3 projects**: Consultation not concluded, requiring additional information, site visits, or formal consultation initiation

**Mexican Spotted Owl Management:**
- No treatments permitted within designated PACs during breeding season (March 1 - August 31)
- Projects must maintain trees ≥24 inches dbh in restricted and protected habitats
- Prescribed burns designed with buffers (typically 660 feet from streams, 0.25-1.0 miles from PACs) and conducted outside breeding season with wind direction away from PACs
- Snags ≥18 inches dbh and ≥30 feet tall, and down logs ≥12 inches diameter at midpoint protected through wet-lining or scratch-lining
- Several projects designate specific acreages (e.g., 181 acres in [[Little Timber Sale]], 973 acres in [[Blue Ridge Morgan]]) for management toward threshold conditions
- Critical habitat treatments generally limited to small percentages of total designated habitat (e.g., 1,000 acres of 81,873-acre unit in [[Iris Spring]] project)

**Bald Eagle Conservation:**
- Winter surveys required prior to project implementation during eagle use period (October 1 - April 15)
- Activity restrictions within 0.25 miles of winter roosts or foraging areas, or 0.5 miles line-of-sight
- No treatments in suitable roosting habitat along major drainages
- Projects timed to avoid disturbance during peak winter use (late December - January)
- Large trees (perch and roost sites) retained in project designs

**Peregrine Falcon Protection:**
- All disturbing activities >0.5 miles from known eyries or conducted outside breeding season (March 1 - July 31)
- Surveys required for potential nesting habitat within project areas prior to breeding season activities
- Prescribed burning scheduled to direct smoke away from eyries

**Aquatic Species Conservation:**
- Buffer strips (75-660 feet) along perennial and intermittent streams
- Livestock grazing deferred for one full growing season following prescribed burns
- Low-intensity burns and mosaic patterns to minimize sediment delivery
- Road closures and rehabilitation to reduce sediment sources
- Riparian restoration including willow planting, headcut stabilization, and livestock exclosures
- Short-term sedimentation increases acknowledged but long-term habitat improvements anticipated

**Forest Health and Fire Management:**
- Fuel loading reductions from 15.6 tons/acre to 7.8 tons/acre (typical target)
- Basal area reductions from 120-180 square feet/acre to 50-100 square feet/acre in thinning projects
- Variable density thinning prescriptions to create diverse vegetation structural stages (VSS)
- Open road density reductions (e.g., 4.4 to 1.9 miles/section in [[Little Timber Sale]])
- Dwarf-mistletoe management through removal of infected trees while retaining large Douglas fir with witches brooms not directly overtopping regeneration

**Programmatic Recommendations:**
- Service and Forest Service committed to developing programmatic approaches and "sideboards" for effects determinations
- Advisory group proposed (2-3 Forest Service staff, 1-2 USFWS staff) to expedite future consultations
- Need for landscape-level analysis in designating habitat for threshold management
- Burn plans required for review prior to prescribed fire in critical habitat (e.g., [[Badger Knoll]] project deferred pending burn plan completion)

**Conservation Measures Standardized Across Projects:**
- Breeding season restrictions for MSO, peregrine falcon, and southwestern willow flycatcher
- Protection of large woody debris and snags during prescribed burns
- Survey protocols following approved methodologies
- Mitigation for discovered roosts/nests during project implementation
- Post-project monitoring and reporting requirements
