## Executive Summary

This document set is a collection of 1996–1997 interagency correspondence regarding the proposed reintroduction of [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) into its historical native range within Arizona, particularly the [[Gila River]] drainage and the [[Tonto National Forest]]. The letters — from the Zane Grey Chapter of [[Trout Unlimited]], the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Arizona and New Mexico Ecological Services offices), and the [[Tonto National Forest]] (U.S. Forest Service) — establish the administrative and collaborative framework for a proposed experimental population reintroduction targeted for 1997. The correspondence does not contain morphological, physiological, or direct ecological survey data on Gila Trout; rather, it documents the conservation management and multi-agency coordination process central to the species' recovery, modeled explicitly on the successful management trajectory of the related [[Apache Trout]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Native cold-water streams within the [[Gila River]] drainage system in Arizona and New Mexico — a large drainage area described as well-suited for reintroduction efforts.
- **Historical range**: Arizona portion of the Gila River drainage, including waters within the [[Tonto National Forest]], identified as a candidate reintroduction site.
- **Land management context**: Reintroduction habitat falls under multi-jurisdictional oversight, involving the [[U.S. Forest Service]] as the land management agency responsible for administering potential stocking waters, alongside the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].
- **Regulatory status**: The species is protected under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 (as amended), placing formal recovery and reintroduction actions under a federally guided [[Gila Trout Recovery Plan]] and associated recovery team.
- **Comparative case**: The [[Apache Trout]], Arizona's other native trout species, serves as a precedent and management model — having previously been "down-listed" under a similar recovery and reintroduction strategy, which the Gila Trout initiative aims to emulate.

## Key Findings

- **Reintroduction initiative**: The Zane Grey Chapter of [[Trout Unlimited]] formally requested (November 20, 1996) that the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Arizona Ecological Services Field Office collaborate on reintroducing Gila Trout to Arizona waters, with a stated goal of establishing an experimental population by 1997.
- **Jurisdictional structure**: The New Mexico Ecological Services Field Office of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] holds lead responsibility for Gila Trout recovery nationally; any Arizona reintroduction must follow the priorities set by the federal [[Gila Trout Recovery Plan]] and receive guidance from that office (per Debra Bills, AZ FWS staff).
- **Recovery team engagement**: A Gila Trout recovery team meeting was scheduled for mid-January 1997, at which the Arizona reintroduction request was to be formally added to the agenda for discussion.
- **Prior proposal**: A May 30, 1996 letter from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to the FWS Regional Office had already requested formation of a workgroup to explore Gila Trout reintroduction into Arizona — indicating this initiative predates the Trout Unlimited request and reflects sustained multi-agency interest.
- **Forest Service position**: The [[Tonto National Forest]] (U.S. Forest Service) expressed conceptual support for the reintroduction project, acknowledging its role in providing habitat for species listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], while noting unresolved land-management issues that would need to be addressed prior to stocking Forest Service-administered lands.
- **Multi-agency partners identified**: Key organizations coordinating on this recovery effort include [[Trout Unlimited]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Arizona and New Mexico offices), [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], and the [[U.S. Forest Service]] (Tonto National Forest).
- **Strategic precedent**: Recovery planners explicitly referenced the [[Apache Trout]] downlisting success as a template for eventual Gila Trout status improvement following successful reintroduction.
