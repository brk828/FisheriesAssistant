# Macroinvertebrate Inventory of the Concrete-Lined Coachella Canal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #benthic-macroinvertebrates

## Executive Summary

This technical report, a special study prepared for the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] under the Water Conservation Opportunities project of the [[Imperial Irrigation District]], inventories benthic macroinvertebrates in the concrete-lined reach of the [[Coachella Canal]]. The lining replaced the uppermost earthen section in 1980. Sampling at 10 sites in November 1982 and May 1983 was compared with earlier data from the earthen reach. The concrete reach supported a similar taxonomic assemblage but about four times the total abundance and roughly one-fifth to one-twentieth the biomass of the earthen canal. Most of the biomass difference reflected the loss of large [[Corbicula Fluminea]]. The report also finds that fish populations appear largely intact, with a possible concern about morphological anomalies in [[Threadfin Shad]].

## Background & Description

- **Context:** Artificial canals are central to agriculture and urban growth in the arid southwestern United States. They extend aquatic habitats into desert lands and support sport, forage and non-game fishes. Canal macroinvertebrate faunas are typically sparse in both abundance and diversity.
- **Trophic role:** [[Channel Catfish]] in canals feed on macroinvertebrates, and growth there is faster than in most other habitats. The report suggests other canal fishes likely depend on invertebrates too, though data are lacking.
- **Rationale:** Earthen canals are being lined with concrete for water conservation and transport efficiency. Few data existed on fish or invertebrate resources in lined canals.
- **Objectives:** (1) quantify species composition, relative abundance, biomass and community structure of macroinvertebrates in a concrete-lined canal; (2) compare these with unlined (earthen) canals; (3) hypothesize how invertebrate differences might affect other biotic components.
- **Study setting:** The canal is 138 km long, running from the [[All American Canal]] to its outlet at the [[Salton Sea]]. The upper portion was relined in 1980. Sampling occurred 2 to 2.5 years after inundation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Methods**
- Ten sites were selected: 4 mid-channel sites, 4 immediately upstream of structures (Parshall flume, check drops, inverted box siphon), and 2 immediately downstream (check and siphon).
- Water measurements were taken at 0.5 m depth using a [[Hydrolab Water Quality Probe]] and a [[Teledyne Gurley Flow Meter]].
- Benthos was sampled in duplicate: mid-channel with a [[Ponar Dredge]] (232 cm²), and canal sidewalls with a modified [[Surber-Type Net]] (225 cm², 0.50 mm mesh) at 0.5 to 1.0 m above the bottom.
- Samples were sieved in the field, preserved in 10% formalin, and sorted, identified, enumerated and weighed in the laboratory.

**Physicochemical conditions**
- Conditions were similar across sites, with few spatial or seasonal trends.
- November means (±1 SD, N=10): dissolved oxygen 11.0 ± 0.9 mg/L; conductivity 1489 ± 20 µmhos/cm; pH 8.3 ± 0.1; temperature 15.0 ± 0.3 °C.
- May means: dissolved oxygen 9.1 ± 0.6 mg/L; conductivity 1283 ± 6 µmhos/cm; pH 8.2 ± 0.2; temperature 23.4 ± 1.1 °C.
- Water velocity ranged from 0.02 to 0.30 m/s. It was higher mid-channel (0.21 ± 0.09, N=9) than at sidewalls (0.09 ± 0.09, N=7).

**Substrates and vegetation**
- Main-channel substrates were mostly coarse, sometimes fine, sand. The concrete was barren below the siphon.
- Some sediments near structures were dark and smelled of hydrogen sulfide, suggesting anaerobic conditions.
- Sidewalls carried an encrusted diatom-based periphyton layer of 1 to 2 m thickness. Dense [[Cladophora Glomerata]] grew along sidewalls at structures and on the bottom at station 1.
- Macrophytes were scarce; [[Potamogeton Crispus]] was present but rare.

## Key Findings

**Species composition**
- At least 16 invertebrate taxa were collected in the concrete reach, including Nematoda, Ostracoda and Turbellaria (not enumerated).
- Autumn (November) yielded 16 taxa and summer (May) 14. [[Baetis Sp]] and [[Nectopsyche Sp]] were absent in summer, probably reflecting emergence.
- The earthen canal had 19 taxa. Three rare taxa found there in 1980 (two damselflies and a chrysops fly) were not recorded in the concrete reach. A dragonfly (Libellulidae) and a microcaddisfly (Ochrotrichia sp.) were found only in the lined canal.
- Fewer taxa were found in dredge samples (4 to 8) than in sidewall samples (6 to 9).

**Relative abundance**
- [[Chironomidae]] was the dominant taxon, making up about 49% of individuals. [[Corbicula Fluminea]] accounted for 34%, [[Oligochaeta]] 13% and [[Hydracarina]] 4%. All other taxa were at most about 0.5%.
- [[Smicridea Utica]] was rare in the concrete reach but locally abundant on structures in the earthen canal. Hydracarina showed the opposite pattern.
- Total abundance was about 4 times greater in the concrete reach. November 1982 means were 12,951/m² (sidewall) and 17,032/m² (bottom), compared with roughly 159/m² and 4,050/m² in the earthen canal in November 1980.
- Most of the increase was associated with sidewalls. The authors state that predictions of enhanced secondary production in lined canals appear to have been fulfilled.
- Autumn abundance was about 3 times summer abundance, most strongly for Chironomidae and Corbicula, probably reflecting warm-season reproduction and spring hatching.
- Standing crops immediately downstream of structures were nearly 3 times those in main-channel and upstream reaches. Juvenile Corbicula (under 2 mm) drove much of this, especially below siphons in autumn.

**Biomass**
- Corbicula made up about 50% of total invertebrate biomass in the concrete reach, at roughly 200 mg DW/m² of about 400 mg DW/m² total. Most individuals were under 2 mm in shell width.
- Chironomidae and Oligochaeta each contributed about 25% of standing stock. Other taxa made up at most about 1% each.
- In the earthen canal, mostly large (12 mm) Corbicula made up about 98% of biomass, which totalled roughly 2,000 to 7,500 mg DW/m².
- Total biomass in the concrete reach was about one-fifth to one-twentieth of the earthen reach.
- Smicridea, the most important food item of channel catfish, declined roughly 30-fold in comparable November bottom samples and about 10-fold overall. The predicted increase did not occur.
- Chironomidae and Oligochaeta biomass rose several-fold and about twofold, respectively, relative to the earthen canal.

**Fishes**
- Visual surveys and test netting in November 1981 found [[Threadfin Shad]] and channel catfish abundant. Carp, red shiner, mosquitofish and largemouth bass were rare.
- Scuba observations in 1982 and 1983 confirmed these results and added tilapia (Tilapia sp.) and sailfin molly.
- All these species occurred in the earthen canal. Bluegill was abundant there but not seen in the concrete reach. Seven other species recorded in 1980 were rare or in inaccessible habitats.
- Channel catfish appeared to shift their diet toward abundant Chironomidae larvae and away from Smicridea.
- The authors found no evidence that lining has harmed the fishery, but noted a high incidence of spinal deformity in threadfin shad (Marsh and Rinne 1983). They recommend further fish sampling.

**Interpretation and conclusions**
- Sidewalls provide a stable attachment surface for periphyton, which traps suspended fine particles. Chironomidae and Oligochaeta appear to use this matrix for refuge and food.
- Shifting sand substrates in the main channel are considered inhospitable to most invertebrates, which explains low abundance there.
- The scarcity of large Corbicula is considered problematic. Harsh bottom conditions likely explain low numbers mid-channel, but not the low densities near structures. Recruitment may simply not yet have occurred.
- Smicridea's decline is attributed to high suspended particulate loads from higher flows and primary production, which may clog its filtering nets.
- Community stability may not yet have been reached, and natural temporal variability in canal communities has never been studied.
- The report calls for future sampling of Corbicula, the [[All American Canal]] source population, and fishes.
