## Executive Summary

This document contains the draft minutes of the **Virgin River Recovery Team (VRRT)** meeting held on April 25, 1997, in St. George, Utah. The meeting focused on multi-agency coordination for the conservation and recovery of native fish species in the Virgin River Basin, most notably the federally protected **[[Woundfin]]** and the **[[Virgin Spinedace]]**, alongside co-occurring native species including **[[Roundtail Chub]]**, **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]**, and **[[Desert Sucker]]**, as well as the non-native **[[Red Shiner]]**. Key agenda items included updates on the Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program, the Virgin Spinedace Conservation Agreement/Strategy, ongoing field sampling results from multiple agencies (BLM, Bio/West, Utah DWR, USU), a Dexter Hatchery woundfin stocking/tagging project, and management concerns regarding land-use impacts (river re-routing, livestock grazing) on native fish habitat.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body proportions, coloration) were reported in this meeting; discussion was limited to field identification, tagging, and relative abundance. Notable field-based observations include:

- **[[Woundfin]]**: Wire-tagged (5,000 individuals) as part of a Dexter Hatchery propagation/reintroduction project; tagged fish were recovered in the wild (e.g., 1 tagged individual at Bunkerville Diversion), confirming post-release survival and detectability via tagging.
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]** and **[[Desert Sucker]]**: Field crews (Bio/West) noted that sampled individuals "appeared to be big and healthy," suggesting good condition/growth in sampled reaches.
- **[[Red Shiner]]**: Described as "very healthy" and moderately abundant in one survey reach, contrasted with low abundance reported below the Woundfin/spinedace Distribution area (WFD) by Utah DWR.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Virgin River system supports a native desert fish assemblage adapted to a naturally variable, low-flow desert stream environment. Key ecological findings and concerns include:

- **Flow dependency**: The Santa Clara/Gunlock pipeline project was flagged as a major threat — a stream reach naturally carrying ~50 cfs could be reduced to ~3 cfs, drastically altering habitat availability for **[[Virgin Spinedace]]**.
- **Habitat-population relationships**: USU (Thom Hardy) is investigating the relationship between habitat availability and fish population estimates, aiming to determine whether current monitoring adequately captures population and habitat-use trends, including seasonal shifts in habitat use.
- **Species co-occurrence**: Sampling reaches show co-occurrence of **[[Woundfin]]**, **[[Roundtail Chub]]**, **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]**, **[[Desert Sucker]]**, and **[[Red Shiner]]**, with relative abundances varying by location and year (e.g., a chemically treated reach yielded 2 woundfin, 20 spinedace, and 20 chub).
- **Anthropogenic stressors**:
  - **Grazing**: BLM's Resource Management Plan revision (shifting from year-round to winter-only grazing) raised questions about impacts on streambank stability and erosion. The VRRT consensus was that halting grazing during spring/summer/fall would likely benefit fish habitat; increased turbidity was thought unlikely to affect **[[Woundfin]]** but could impact **[[Virgin Spinedace]]**.
  - **Channel modification**: An incident (Bunkerville Incident) involving a private landowner re-routing the river prompted discussion of regulatory response (EPA fine and required river restoration) and the VRRT's advisory role via USFWS.
  - **Water diversion**: Proposed pipeline projects threaten to substantially reduce stream discharge in spinedace habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Multi-agency field sampling (Spring 1997)**:
  - **Bio/West**: 10–12 woundfin captured below Mesquite bridge (~50% tagged); woundfin also observed at Riverside. A 3-mile reach of the "BDW" yielded 25–30 chubs and abundant suckers, but **no woundfin** were observed there.
  - **Utah DWR**: Woundfin numbers were low but higher than fall 1996 sampling, suggesting good 1995 reproduction; shiner numbers were low below WFD; elevated flows (~300 cfs) confounded population estimates, prompting plans for more intensive sampling before the next chemical treatment.
  - **Arizona**: Sampling not yet conducted at time of meeting.
  - **USU**: Focused on methodology — refining GIS-integrated databases and developing an improved monitoring strategy for habitat/population assessment.

- **Woundfin Reintroduction Program (Dexter Hatchery)**:
  - 5,000 wire-tagged woundfin produced; half released at Mesquite and Riverside bridge locations, remainder held for later release.
  - Request made for an additional 4,000–6,000 fish to study post-release movement and habitat use, with potential expansion to movement studies on the **Muddy River**.
  - Unresolved policy questions raised regarding reintroduction between Quail Creek Diversion and Pah Tempe: whether such stocking counts toward recovery goals, appropriateness of the brood stock's genetic source, whether stocked fish should be designated "experimental," and whether critical habitat redesignation is needed.
  - Action items assigned: UDWR to evaluate stocking options post-fall treatment; Cynthia Martinez to investigate brood stock source population; all members to submit priority action items by May 9.

- **Policy and Coordination**:
  - The **Virgin River Basin Integrated Resource Management and Recovery Program** remains stalled — draft implementation plan undistributed as of the meeting; signatories to meet May 29, 1997.
  - The **Virgin Spinedace Conservation Team (VSCT)** needs reconvening to address emerging threats (e.g., pipeline projects) given the Program's delay.
  - Disagreement noted between Tom Burke (who downplayed genetic considerations in woundfin stocking) and Anna Toline (who advocated a pragmatic approach prioritizing long-term survival of both stocked and wild populations).
