## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative/correspondence letter (dated 08 August 1991) from Arizona State University's (ASU) Center for Environmental Studies to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation regarding Cooperative Agreement Number 1-FC-40-10490, titled "Ecology and Conservation Biology of Humpback Chub, *Gila cypha*, in the Little Colorado River, Arizona." While not a primary scientific data report, the letter contains important taxonomic, life-history, and research-methodology information regarding [[Humpback Chub]] conservation research conducted under the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program. The letter proposes eliminating redundant sampling of young-of-year juveniles by ASU, deferring this task entirely to the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD), to streamline data collection across cooperating agencies.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: *Gila cypha* ([[Humpback Chub]]), a member of the family Cyprinidae.
- **Life-stage size benchmark**: Young-of-year (age 0) chub are reported to reach a total length of approximately **75–100 mm** by the end of their first year of life.
- No further morphological descriptors (e.g., fin counts, hump development, coloration) are provided in this administrative correspondence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: The [[Little Colorado River]], a major tributary of the [[Colorado River]] in Arizona, serves as critical spawning and rearing habitat for *Gila cypha*.
- **Research context**: Studies were conducted under the auspices of [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES), a multi-agency research initiative examining the ecological effects of Glen Canyon Dam operations on the Colorado River ecosystem.
- **Life-stage partitioning of research effort**:
  - **Larval and young-of-year (age 0) chub** (up to ~75–100 mm TL, through end of first year): Sampling responsibility assigned to [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD).
  - **Post-larval and pre-reproductive juvenile chub** (>1 year of age): Originally proposed for study by ASU, per the Technical Proposal and Cooperative Agreement (Objective 3).
  - This letter proposes removing ASU's overlapping young-of-year juvenile sampling component to avoid redundant field effort, thereby ensuring complete life-stage coverage across cooperating institutions without duplication.
- **Cooperating institutions**: 
  - [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) — Project co-directors: W.L. Minckley and [[Michael E. Douglas]].
  - [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AZGFD)
  - [[Navajo Nation]] (NNFW)
  - [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS)
  - [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] (funding/contracting agency)

## Key Findings
- **Life-history sampling division**: The letter formalizes a division of labor among cooperating agencies to sample discrete *Gila cypha* life stages — larval and young-of-year chub (AZGFD) vs. juvenile/pre-reproductive chub (ASU) — ensuring comprehensive life-stage data without duplicative field effort.
- **Definition clarification**: For *Gila cypha*, "young-of-year" is explicitly defined as encompassing both larval and post-larval juvenile stages up to one year of age (~75–100 mm TL), distinguishing this cohort from older pre-reproductive juveniles.
- **Proposed protocol change**: ASU recommends deleting its proposed young-of-year juvenile chub collections from its Technical Proposal and Cooperative Agreement (originally specified on pages 11–14, 16–17, and under Objective 3) to eliminate redundancy with AZGFD's larval/YOY sampling efforts.
- **Administrative impact**: The proposed change is expected to require **no modification to manpower or budget**, and is anticipated to **streamline** ASU's overall research effort, thereby enhancing data collection efficiency under other research protocols.
- **Approval chain**: The modification required concurrence from the Contracting Officer's Technical Representative ([[D.L. Wegner]], GCES) and formal written modification of the Cooperative Agreement, coordinated through ASU's Office of Sponsored Programs Administration.
- **Broader significance**: This correspondence reflects adaptive, cooperative management of endangered species research logistics for *Gila cypha*, a federally listed endangered cyprinid endemic to the Colorado River basin, within the multi-agency [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] framework examining Glen Canyon Dam's ecological effects.
