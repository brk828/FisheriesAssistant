# Blood Formation in the African Lungfish under Normal and Prolonged Estivation Conditions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #protopteridae

## Executive Summary

This 1931 study by H. E. Jordan and C. C. Speidel (University of Virginia, Laboratory of Histology and Embryology) describes blood cell formation (hemocytopoiesis) in the [[African Lungfish]], *Protopterus ethiopicus*, under normal conditions and after prolonged dry [[Estivation]] followed by recovery in water. The blood cells are very large and resemble those of urodeles (salamanders), and leucocytes are unusually numerous and varied. The authors identify the spleen, the spiral-valve region of the intestine, and the kidney and gonad capsules as the main sites of blood cell production, and they argue that the lungfish spleen marks an intermediate stage in [[Spleen Phylogeny]]. Estivation largely halts erythrocyte production while granulocytes proliferate and degenerate, which the authors link to fat metabolism.

## Background & Description

- **Study rationale:** Lungfishes had received no recent detailed work on blood or blood-forming organs. The authors noted Parker (1892) considered the African lungfish probably richer in leucocytes than any other vertebrate.
- **Estivation habit:** Lungfishes can remain torpid for months in moist or dry earth after their water evaporates, a state the authors expected to strongly affect hemocytopoiesis.
- **Material:** Specimens were supplied by Prof. H. W. Smith (New York University). The series comprised young fish (about 14 cm, roughly two years old), adult fish (about 60 cm, six or more years old), fry (2.5 cm, about two months old), animals held in dry estivation for periods up to 427 days, and 20 formalin-fixed fish of 15–35 cm (Nos. 51–70). Blood smears came from animals nos. 1, 8, 13, 21, 33 and 34.
- **Methods:** Smears were stained by the Wright technique. Tissues were fixed in Zenker-formol (Helly) and stained with eosin-azure (Giemsa) or after Delafield hematoxylin (Maximow method).
- **Study regions:** General circulation; [[Spleen]] (lymphocytopoiesis, erythrocytopoiesis, granulocytopoiesis); the spiral-valve region of the [[Intestinal Granulocytopoietic Organ]]; and kidney and gonad capsules.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Estivation as a physiological state:** Animals were kept in dry estivation for periods from about one year (animal no. 8) to 427 days (no. 21). Animal no. 29 was held at 90 °F for three weeks and 103 °F for four days. Infection was common in several estivating animals and was treated with potassium permanganate in no. 13.
- **Recovery conditions:** Animal no. 1 was in moist earth for two months, then in water for five days. Animal no. 21 was returned to water for ten days after 427 days of estivation and received thyroxin (0.5 mg), which raised metabolic rate by 100 per cent.
- **Body weight changes:** Animal no. 21 weighed 182 g originally, 133 g after dry estivation, and 152 g after water absorption.
- **Comparative context:** The authors compare the lungfish with [[Salamanders]] (Triturus, Necturus, Axolotl), the hagfish *Myxine*, the lamprey, and turtle and frog species, drawing on their earlier work.

## Key Findings

**Blood cell types**
- Erythrocytes, thrombocytes, monocytes and macrophages, eosinophils, special eosinophils, meta-eosinophils, basophils, and lymphocytes of several sizes were all present. Leucocytes were very numerous relative to erythrocytes.
- Erythrocyte development follows a pro-erythroblast, erythroblast, erythrocyte sequence, closely resembling the salamander.

**Hemocytopoietic organs**
- **Spleen:** Chief site of erythrocytopoiesis and lymphocytopoiesis. The spleen is embedded in the stomach wall and is a compact organ.
- **Intestinal granulocytopoietic organ:** Source of coarse-granule eosinophils, special eosinophils and basophils.
- **Kidney and gonad capsules:** Additional granulocytopoiesis and sites of granulocyte degeneration and pigment formation.
- **Liver, heart, pancreas, lungs, vertebrae, and muscle:** Judged minor or absent as hemocytopoietic sites.

**Amitosis**
- In animal no. 1, a count of 10,000 erythrocytes showed fourteen definite cases of amitosis and thirteen probable early cases. Mitosis of erythrocytes was not seen in this animal.
- Amitosis also occurred in thromboblasts, granulocytes, monocytes and lymphoid hemoblasts.
- The authors interpret amitosis as a rapid emergency response to sudden change, and mitosis as a response to gradual change.

**Effects of estivation**
- Erythrocytopoiesis practically ceased. Monocytes were absent from the blood of estivating animals and reappeared after return to water.
- Granulocytes degenerated in large numbers, and the spleen was packed with eosinophils. Pigment from granulocyte débris was eliminated through the kidneys, intestine and liver.
- In animal no. 29 and no. 21, 235 and 45 out of 1,000 erythrocytes respectively showed intracellular nuclear degeneration.

**Granulocytes and fat metabolism**
- The authors propose that the granules of eosinophils and meta-eosinophils act as an intracellular mechanism for fat transport. Granulocytes may also supply partly formed material for metabolism, similar to yolk granules in fry.
- The varied meta-eosinophil granules are considered a feature of the lungfish linked to estivation.

**Thrombocytes**
- Spherical and spindle-shaped thrombocytes were both present. In the lungfish the small lymphocyte is the usual mother cell for thrombocytes, unlike the large hemoblast in salamanders.

**Phylogenetic conclusion**
- The authors place the lungfish spleen between the disperse intra-enteral spleen of cyclostomes (hagfish, lamprey) and the compact extra-enteral spleen of other vertebrates.

**Limitations**
- Estivating specimens were few, and the authors state that some conclusions are necessarily uncertain. Infection was present in several animals.
