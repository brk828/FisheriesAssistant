# Lake Mohave Bonytail Chub Roundup, May 14–18, 2007

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

The fisheries group from the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Boulder City office), assisted by Mike Burrell, carried out the annual Lake Mohave [[Bonytail Chub]] roundup from May 14 to 18, 2007. Both shorelines of the reservoir were sampled from Chalk Cliffs to about RM 29 using [[Trammel Netting]] and [[Electrofishing]], with day and night effort. The roundup captured nine fish species in nets and several more in electrofishing, including two [[Razorback Sucker]] and a set of recaptured, previously tagged fish. The note also summarizes a fish-history table tracing release and recapture records for individuals first contacted during this event.

## Background & Description

The document is a field report on the annual roundup, which is aimed at monitoring the bonytail population in [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]]. Bonytail and razorback suckers are native, imperiled fishes of the river system, so the roundup focuses on locating, identifying and tagging them. Fish are marked with passive integrated transponder (PIT) tags, and the report lists tag numbers, lengths and locations to track individuals over time (see [[PIT Tagging]]).

The sampling effort included:

- **Survey area:** both shorelines of the lake, from Chalk Cliffs to about RM 29.
- **Gear:** 300-foot, 12-foot-deep, 1.5-inch mesh trammel nets, and electrofishing with day and night effort.
- **Trammel netting:** 28 nets set over the sampling period (the source text reads "14 nights," which is probably an OCR error for 4 nights).
- **Electrofishing:** 5,552 seconds of effort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Lake Mohave supports a mixed fish community. Non-native and sport species were common in the catch, alongside the native targets. The report explicitly names:

- [[Common Carp]]: the most abundant electrofishing catch, with 180 individuals.
- [[Bluegill]]: 18 individuals captured by electrofishing.
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: one individual captured by electrofishing.
- Razorback suckers: two individuals captured by electrofishing near RM 29.

The trammel-net catch included nine species in total. The source text is partly garbled for the remaining species names, so they are not reproduced here. Several fish captured in the native-species sampling were recaptures of animals released in earlier years, which points to repeated sampling of the same population over time.

## Key Findings

- **Trammel netting:** 28 nets were set, and nine species were recorded. Four fish had 400 kHz PIT tags, and two had other tags. The source text is partly unclear about whether all fish were scanned for existing tags.
- **Electrofishing:** 5,552 seconds of effort yielded 180 carp, 18 bluegill, two razorback suckers, one largemouth bass, and other species that are not legible in the source.
- **Razorback suckers:** two fish were captured near RM 29, measuring 525 mm and 475 mm total length. Both were injected with 134.2 kHz PIT tags. The text notes that neither fish had been contacted before and that both were released.
- **Fish history:** the table lists about eight fish with release and capture records. Release dates range from 1995 to 2005, and lengths at capture range from about 295 mm to 665 mm. Most entries are marked "First Contact" at the 2007 capture, indicating that these fish had not been recaptured since their release. Some entries have no tag or unknown release data.
- **Release and recapture:** the table distinguishes data collected at release from data collected during a sampling event (capture), which supports population tracking across years.
