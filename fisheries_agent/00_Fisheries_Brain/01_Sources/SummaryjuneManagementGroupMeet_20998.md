# Colorado River Recovery Program Management Group Meeting Summary (June 23, 1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This administrative document is a memorandum from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] (FWS) transmitting the summary of the June 23, 1988 meeting of the [[Management Group]] of the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]], held at the FWS Regional Office in Lakewood, Colorado. The memo header is dated "AUG 5 1998," which appears to be a typographical error for 1988, given the meeting context. The meeting covered program funding, legal strategy for water rights and instream flow protection, technical reports on the [[Yampa River]] and [[Fifteen-Mile Reach]], the FY 89 work plan, [[Section 7 Consultation]] language, and a proposed strategy for the [[San Juan River]]. The attached San Juan River discussion draft assesses recovery potential for [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] and sets out a two-part strategy (recovery studies and depletion charges).

- **Document type:** Memorandum with attached meeting summary, agenda (Attachment 1), attendee list (Attachment 2), a Yampa River instream flow milestone schedule, and a San Juan River issues and strategy discussion draft.
- **Author:** John Hamill, Program Director, Colorado River Recovery Implementation Program, FWS Region 6, Denver, Colorado.

- **Key program bodies:** [[Management Group]], [[Technical Group]], [[Implementation Committee]], [[Instream Flow Ad Hoc Group]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Colorado squawfish:** The San Juan is described as one of only three remaining natural populations. In 1987, 6 adult fish and 20 young-of-year fish were captured (figures as stated in the source). In 1988 to date, three adult Colorado squawfish had been captured in Utah and New Mexico.
- **Razorback sucker:** One ripe individual was captured in the San Juan River in 1988. Field results indicate adults are present in low numbers and reproduction occurs at least periodically.
- **San Juan River habitat:** About 200 miles of unobstructed riverine habitat is available. Exotic/predatory fish abundance is similar to Upper Basin rivers, and water quality appears suitable. Physical habitat appears suitable for spawning, recruitment, and adults, but 1987 was an abnormally high water year, and the river may go dry in the future.
- **Causes of low numbers:** No ready explanation exists. The river has been degraded by past dewatering and poisoning, which may have depressed the population.
- **Flow and hydrology:** Operations at [[Navajo Dam]] and [[Navajo Reservoir]] may need refinement to meet the flow needs of the rare fishes. There is uncertainty about how much operational flexibility and uncommitted water remains.
- **Water depletion pressures:** Animas-La Plata is a "big" project proposing to deplete 198,000 acre-feet (AF), or 13 percent of the average annual flow. Several small depletion projects are expected to enter Section 7 consultation soon.
- **Instream flow and Ruedi releases:** Protection of flows in the 15-Mile Reach and Yampa River depends on delivery of releases from [[Ruedi Reservoir]] and acquisition of water rights.

## Key Findings

**Program administration and funding**
- The Service will not transfer $1M of water rights funds to the [[Fish And Wildlife Foundation]]; it will manage money internally while developing a Cooperative Agreement with the Foundation for funds derived from the $10 per acre-foot depletion charge.
- Acquisition of Colorado River water rights ranked 28th nationally for [[Land And Water Conservation Fund]] priorities. Laurie Mathews will investigate the process and timetable.
- The Bureau of Reclamation requested two Solicitor opinions: (a) use of [[Colorado River Storage Project]] (CRSP) funds for endangered fish studies and (b) priority of use for operating CRSP facilities.
- The Bureau's $1.5M annual contribution to the Recovery Program was objected to by Bill MacDonald for funding lower basin fish recovery.
- The Uintah Mountain Club is contemplating a lawsuit alleging inadequate [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA) compliance for the Recovery Program.

**Taxonomy and outreach**
- The Service was moving forward with a 3-4 year study to clarify the taxonomy of the genus Gila ([[Gila Taxonomy]]). Hamill agreed to send the scope of work to the Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit at Colorado State University.
- A Recovery Program mailing list review was requested by July 15, 1988. The group agreed on the need for a Recovery Program newsletter, with an outline to be prepared by the next meeting.

**San Juan River strategy**
- The proposed strategy was adopted, limiting Recovery Program funds on the San Juan River to $50K in FY 89, to finish technical studies and prepare a recovery/management plan by March 1989.
- Recovery Program funds for future San Juan activities will be evaluated case by case by the Implementation Committee.
- The discussion draft states the San Juan was excluded from the Recovery Program due to apparently low recovery potential and New Mexico's reluctance to participate. New Mexico has no instream flow legislation or program, and no funds are identified for a San Juan recovery effort.
- The 1985 Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team recommended (September 13, 1985 letter) that the San Juan be treated as an integral part of the upper Colorado River basin for recovery.
- Planned studies: 1988 field studies on abundance, distribution, habitat, and limiting factors (New Mexico and Utah lead; final report due January 31, 1989); hydrology and environmental baseline modeling, including Navajo Reservoir flexibility (Bureau lead; final report due January 31, 1989); and a draft recovery/management plan by March 1, 1989 (FWS/Bureau).
- Seek Implementation Committee approval of the San Juan strategy at its August 30, 1988 meeting.
- **Section 7 component:** Implement a depletion charge for small projects, using a San Juan-specific depletion formula. Evaluate large projects on a case-by-case basis.

**Yampa River and 15-Mile Reach instream flow reports**
- The Technical Group recommended the Yampa report be redrafted with the flow recommendation section removed. Concerns included the link between data and recommendations, whether room remains for depletions, and how flow recommendations would be quantified. A revised schedule was accepted by the Management Group.
- The Yampa milestones: Part 1 (biology) comments due 6/30/88, revised report 8/1/88 and 8/10/88; Part 2 (flow recommendations) draft plan 7/19/88, final work plan to Implementation Committee 8/31/88, draft report 11/1/88, draft to work groups 11/19/88, final report 1/89. Dates are subject to change based on the final work plan.
- The 15-Mile Reach report's main problem was the velocity suitability index curve, which Tom Pitts believed showed high suitability for high water velocities. The Service will rerun the habitat analysis with revised curves and report at the next Technical Group meeting. The Management Group asked that the report be finalized by the August 30, 1988 Implementation Committee meeting, if possible.

**CWCB work plan and Ruedi/15-Mile Reach water rights**
- The final CWCB work plan for the rest of FY 88 was approved, with Task 1 supporting 15-Mile Reach/Ruedi instream flow water rights protection, and an interim report due October 1988.
- Preliminary schedule: August 30, 1988, Implementation Committee approval of 15-Mile Reach instream flow recommendations; November-December 1988, preliminary notice of intent to file for an instream flow right at a CWCB meeting.
- Legal issues include leasing Ruedi water to the State for instream flow, whether Ruedi authorizing legislation covers instream flow use, and enforcement of 15-Mile Reach rights. The State Attorney General will report to the CWCB on July 14, 1988, with a follow-up meeting on August 10, 1988.
- Zallen requested that the list of legal concerns include (a) conflicts between federal and state standards for determining flow quantity (recovery versus preserving the environment to a reasonable degree), and (b) the need for a legal mechanism to transfer federal property rights to a state. She also requested deletion of item 7(d).
- The State of Colorado will re-initiate talks with Craig-area local governments on Yampa flow protection and acquisition of the Juniper-Cross Mountain water right. Private party Al Guenther volunteered to evaluate upper Yampa recreational values and develop a marketing plan.

**FY 89 work plan**
- The Technical Group will rank FY 89 technical proposals at its July 19, 1988 meeting. Proposals were due to Bennett by June 30, 1988. Hamill will prepare a draft FY 89 work plan for the next Management Group meeting.
- Utah has no specific FY 89 funds for the Recovery Program but submitted a proposal for credit for its Flaming Gorge tailwater trout fishery work and matching Section 6 funds. The proposal was referred to the Technical Group.

**Instream flow criteria and Section 7 language**
- The Instream Flow Ad Hoc Group drafted criteria for biologically defensible instream flow recommendations. The Management Group asked that the criteria be finalized with an explanatory narrative, and that the group not pursue legal and policy information needs at this time.
- Proposed Section 7 language for Colorado River biological opinions drew environmental group concerns. Colorado called the language "barely" acceptable and asked that requirements not be made more stringent. Pitts proposed extending the "sufficient progress" timetable to October 1990. Utah preferred case-by-case determination of "sufficient progress" instead of standard language. The Service will review written and oral comments and revise the language.

**Propagation and next steps**
- The Service committed to implement the Endangered Fish Propagation Plan as revised by Colorado, which accelerated the schedule by about 3 months, with staff in place beginning August 1, 1988.
- Next meeting: August 18, 1988, 10:00 am to 3:00 pm, and August 19, 1988, in the FWS 3rd floor conference room, 134 Union Blvd., Lakewood, Colorado. (The source text is garbled on these dates; the stated days are reported as written.)
