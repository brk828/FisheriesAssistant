# Durchschneidung des Reissnerschen Fadens im Rückenmark von Salmo irideus

## Executive Summary
This 1962 study by W. Fahrmann, conducted at the [[Hydrobiologische Anstalt Der Max-Planck-Gesellschaft]] (Fuldastation, Schlitz/Hessen), investigates the fate of the **Reissner's Fiber (RF)** — a glycoprotein secretory thread running through the central canal of the spinal cord — following surgical transection of the spinal cord in juvenile [[Salmo irideus]] (rainbow trout). Using 17- and 19-day-old trout, the spinal cord was severed via a lateral stab incision at the level of the dorsal fin, with animals fixed for histological examination at intervals ranging from 5 hours to 14 days post-operation. The study tracks the temporal degeneration of the RF distal to the lesion and the formation of a secondary "Massa" (aggregated fiber material) in both the proximal stump and the newly formed ampulla. The findings corroborate earlier work by Olsson (1957).

## Background & Description
- **Reissner's Fiber (RF):** A continuous, thread-like structure running through the central canal (Zentralkanal) of the spinal cord, normally terminating as a **Massa** in the **Ampulla caudalis**.
- **Central Canal (Zentralkanal):** At the site of transection, becomes progressively blocked by post-operative tissue changes in both the proximal and distal stumps.
- **Ampulla formation:** A new ampulla-like dilation of the central canal develops just proximal to the lesion site beginning ~2 days post-operation.
- **Massa (secondary aggregate):** A newly formed mass of fiber material appears near or within the loosely organized tissue of the proximal stump, often preceding formation within the new ampulla itself; fine ramifications of this material extend into the surrounding loose cellular tissue.
- **Experimental subjects:** Juvenile trout aged 17–19 days at time of surgery; lesion induced by lateral stab incision at dorsal fin level.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
This study is a laboratory-based experimental neurohistology investigation rather than a field ecological survey; it does not address habitat, trophic ecology, or population dynamics of [[Salmo irideus]]. The relevant "niche" here is developmental/physiological: the juvenile trout spinal cord serves as a model system for studying regenerative and degenerative responses of ependymal secretory structures (the Reissner's fiber system) to mechanical injury — a process with implications for understanding central nervous system repair mechanisms in vertebrates, including [[Teleost Fish]] more broadly.

## Key Findings
- **5–12 hours post-op:** No visible change in the RF; it runs straight to the operation site and terminates in the proximal stump. Distally, RF shows normal course and terminates as Massa in the Ampulla caudalis.
- **~2 days post-op:** RF in the distal spinal cord gradually thins; proximal RF remains unchanged. The central canal just proximal to the lesion begins to dilate ampulla-like.
- **3–4 days post-op:** A new Massa begins to form, often located near/within the loose proximal stump tissue rather than directly in the new ampulla — interpreted as the initial site of fiber material accumulation. Fine branching of this material into surrounding tissue is observed.
- **4–7 days post-op:** Complete loss of RF in the distal spinal cord occurs (with individual variation).
- **The Massa caudalis persists somewhat longer** than the distal RF itself.
- **13–14 days post-op:** Only remnants of the Massa caudalis remain detectable.
- **Conclusion:** These results confirm and are consistent with the earlier findings of [[Olsson 1957]] (Z. Zellforsch. 46, 12).
