# Bonytail and Razorback Sucker Stocking in Davis Cove

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This email to the [[Lower Colorado River Native Fish Work Group]] (LCRNFWG) listserv reports that about 1,500 [[Bonytail]] were stocked into [[Davis Cove]] the previous week. The stocking, made with help from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[National Park Service]] (NPS), and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, completes the salvage, renovation, and stocking phase for the site. The author also plans brush shelters, spawning monitoring, and a management plan with NPS. The group is asked to help identify at least two additional sites for future stocking.

## Background & Description

- **Source type:** Partly legible OCR-scanned listserv message, dated Tuesday, November 22, 2005, posted on behalf of Gordon Mueller.
- **Hatchery source:** Fish were raised by [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].
- **Size at stocking:** Fish ranged from 4 to 12 inches.
- **Pond population after stocking:** About 150 mature [[Razorback Sucker]] and 1,500 Bonytail.
- **Acknowledgments:** The author thanks partners for their support the previous year and looks forward to more work in the coming year.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat management:** Brush shelters are planned for Davis Cove before the next meeting, presumably to provide cover for stocked fish. Volunteers are asked to help with installation.
- **Monitoring:** The author is watching for potential spawning activity, which suggests that the stocked population may be establishing reproduction.
- **Site selection:** More than a dozen other candidate sites have been identified. Sites differ in suitability, and the group is prioritizing them for presentation at the upcoming meeting.
- **Recovery context:** Stocking is part of native fish recovery in the Lower Colorado River system, a goal that depends on partnerships among agencies and local volunteers.

## Key Findings

1. **Stocking completed:** About 1,500 Bonytail (4 to 12 inches) were released into Davis Cove, completing the site's salvage, renovation, and stocking phase.
2. **Current population:** The pond holds about 150 mature Razorback Suckers and 1,500 Bonytail.
3. **Next steps:** Install brush shelters before the upcoming meeting, monitor potential spawning, and develop a site management plan with NPS.
4. **Site prioritization:** More than a dozen alternative sites have been identified. Two or more are sought for preparation and stocking.
5. **Call for help:** The author requests listserv suggestions for new sites and volunteers to build brush shelters, with contact through Jeanette or the author.
