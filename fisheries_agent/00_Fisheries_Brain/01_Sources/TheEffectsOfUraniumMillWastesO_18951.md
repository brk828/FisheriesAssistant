# Effects of Uranium Mill Wastes on Stream Biota

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae #catostomidae #cyprinidae #radiation-ecology

## Executive Summary

Bulletin 462 of the Utah Agricultural Experiment Station (Utah State University, Logan, December 1966) reports a 1960–1963 field study of how [[Uranium Milling]] wastes affect stream biota in the Animas, San Miguel, and Dolores Rivers of southwestern Colorado. The work focused on [[Radium-226]], a long-lived (half-life 1620 years) alpha emitter with the lowest maximum permissible concentration in water of any isotope in the uranium-radium series, and on its movement among water, sediments, algae, aquatic insects, and fish. Radium levels and biotic damage were consistently highest immediately below the mills at Durango and Uravan. The authors conclude that mill wastes, especially the acidic, flocculent discharges at Uravan, nearly eliminated aquatic life below the mill, and that controlling waste discharge and stabilising tailings piles is essential.

## Background & Description

The study was motivated by the uranium boom, when ore-processing mills were built along rivers in the Colorado River watershed and discharged liquid and solid wastes before effective treatment existed. Mill wastes contain sulfates, nitrates, chlorides, manganese, iron, lead, arsenic, fluoride, and organics, and nearly all the radium from the ore remains in the tailings. Radium is chemically similar to calcium, replaces it in skeletal tissue, and is considered the isotope most hazardous to human health.

Key background values:
- Uranium ores average about 0.25 percent U3O8.
- About 800 picograms of radium occur per gram of an average ore.
- Natural river water contains roughly 0.1 pg/l of radium.
- Eisenbud (1963) estimated 10 curies of radium-226 per day were discharged to western streams by uranium refineries.

The research aimed to (1) measure radium-226 in streams, (2) follow its deposition, (3) determine levels in aquatic plants and animals, and (4) examine the roles of water and sediments as carriers and reservoirs. Laboratory companion studies examined radium effects on [[Benthic Algae]] (Angelovic, 1964) and on [[Goldfish]] (Linn, 1962). Fieldwork ran from June 1960 to July 1963 across 22 sampling trips, with two later surveys in 1963 that were not analysed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study rivers**
- **[[Animas River]]**: Flows from the San Juan Mountains past Durango, Colorado, to Farmington, New Mexico. A uranium mill operated at Durango from 1948 to 1963. The river supplies drinking water for Aztec and Farmington, roughly 30,000 people, and irrigates 26,000 acres.
- **[[San Miguel River]]**: A mountain stream that receives discharges from a concentrator at Naturita and a mill at Uravan, operated by Union Carbide Nuclear Company.
- **[[Dolores River]]**: Flows from high meadows through Paradox Valley, a region of saline soils and salt wells, and receives the San Miguel before reaching the Colorado River near Moab, Utah. A concentrator at Slick Rock closed in January 1962.

**Sampling methods**: Water was filtered and analysed for radium by barium sulfate co-precipitation. Sediments were hand-collected and analysed by radon emanation. Algae and insects were collected with a Surber sampler, and fish by electrofishing, hand netting, and rotenone. Sampling was limited by variable waste discharge, high spring flows that scoured the bed, and difficulty of access.

**Hydrology**: Animas flows are snowmelt-driven, peaking in early June. Spring flows often exceed 4,000 cfs, compared with a usual flow of a few hundred cfs.

## Key Findings

**Animas River (Durango mill)**
- Dissolved radium-226 averaged 0.06 pg/l at the background station (A-1), against 0.53 (A-2), 0.35 (A-3), and 0.30 (A-4) pg/l below the mill.
- Downstream stations averaged five to eight times background.
- Sediment background was about 1.1 pg/g dry weight. Early in the study, concentrations below Durango reached 27 pg/g, but after trip VII no value exceeded 5.6 pg/g. By 1963 levels were about two to three times background.
- Algae and insects contained similar radium levels. Insects at the background station held 3.1 pg/g ash, against 11.2, 12, and 8.1 pg/g at A-2, A-3, and A-4.
- Bottom fauna were virtually eliminated below the mill in 1958, but by 1960–1963 clean-water insects such as caddisflies, mayflies, and stoneflies were again present.
- Fish homogenates below the mill averaged 10 to 15 times background. Radium content decreased with distance downstream.
- The tailings pile at Durango contains about 900 pg of radium per gram and is a major potential reservoir if it erodes into the river.

**San Miguel and Dolores Rivers (Uravan mill and Naturita)**
- Background dissolved radium averaged 0.04 pg/l (M-0) and 0.06 pg/l (D-1), with a range of 0.03–0.10 pg/l.
- Below Uravan, dissolved radium averaged about 50 times background and reached several hundred times background at times. It showed no decline during the three-year study.
- Water pH at M-2 ranged from 4.7 to 8.2, against 7.2–8.8 at the upstream station. Low pH was associated with high radium. Average radium rose as pH fell, and the lowest pH groups held the highest radium.
- The pH reached 4.3 at one point, and dead fish, algae, and insects were recorded below the mill.
- Sediments below Uravan reached up to about 50 pg/g, roughly 20 times background on average. Above-background radium at Naturita persisted even when that facility was inactive, which the authors attribute to leaching from the tailings pile.
- Algae were rare or absent below Uravan. Insects were nearly absent from the lower San Miguel and lower Dolores, with one composite sample available for radium analysis across three years.

**Fish populations**
- Of 14 species recorded in 1950–1956, only eight remained in the 1960–1963 collections. Six were absent from the study collections. Four others declined.
- Channel catfish declined from 840 per mile in 1953 to 180 per mile in 1956 and 142 per mile in 1963. The estimated 1963 population was about 17 percent of the 1953 level.
- Dolores catfish grew slowly and were in poor condition. More than half their food was terrestrial.
- Angler success fell from several fish per hour before 1956 to less than one fish per hour in 1963.
- Sculpin and dace collected at the background station showed radium levels differing by species. Dace, which graze algae and ingest silt, had higher concentrations.

**Conclusions and recommendations**
- The Animas River below the Durango mill showed recovery after waste treatment began in 1959, and aquatic life was re-established.
- Mill wastes, particularly the acidic and flocculent discharges at Uravan, were the dominant stressor in the San Miguel–Dolores system. Dilution by the San Miguel partly offset the natural salinity of the Dolores.
- The authors argue that waste discharges should be eliminated as far as possible and that stream habitat should be preserved regardless of present use.
- Permanent stabilisation of abandoned tailings piles is urgent, because tailings hold most of the ore's radium and are subject to leaching and erosion.
