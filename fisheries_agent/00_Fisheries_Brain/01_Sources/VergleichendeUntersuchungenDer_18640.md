# Vergleichende Untersuchungen der Chromosomenzahlen bei Süßwasser-Teleosteern (Comparative Study of Chromosome Numbers in Freshwater Teleosts)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #teleostei #cytogenetics

## Executive Summary

This 1965 paper by [[Alfred Post]] (Zoologisches Staatsinstitut und Zoologisches Museum Hamburg) reports haploid chromosome counts for freshwater teleost fishes, using a hypotonic-solution squash method for testis tissue adapted from [[Hsu and Pomerat Method]] and [[Karbe Method]]. The author examined 103 species from 19 families and 6 orders in his own material, and compiled a table of 210 teleost species with known numbers from the literature. The dominant haploid number is n = 24 (about 52% of all records; 73% fall between n = 22 and n = 26). The author argues that n = 24 is probably an ancestral trait of teleosts, that lower numbers arose mainly by chromosome fusion within narrow groups, and that a polyploid origin explains the higher numbers in salmonids.

## Background & Description

- **Historical context:** The earliest teleost count was by Bohm (1891) on *Salmo fario* (n = 12), later revised upward by Prokofieva (1934) and Post-era workers to 2n = 84 in salmonids. [[Lebistes Reticulatus]] (guppy, n = 23) gave the first reliable teleost count (Winge, 1922).
- **Why fish cytology lagged:** Small, numerous and sticky chromosomes, and a seasonal breeding window that limits material.
- **Method:** Testis fragments were placed in a hypotonic Ringer solution (0.65 g NaCl, 0.04 g CaCl, 0.04 g KCl, 0.02 g NaHCO3 per litre) for 30 minutes, fixed and stained in 50% orcein-acetic acid, squashed, then made permanent. Anaesthesia used MS 222. At least two males were examined per species, and about 200 plates were counted per species.
- **Key terminology:** Meiotic stages (leptotene, pachytene, diakinesis, metaphase), bouquet stage, and tetrad types (rod, cross, ring).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Most species are aquarium fishes, chosen for ease of breeding and availability (e.g. [[Guppy]], [[Siamese Fighting Fish]], [[Zebrafish]], [[Neon Tetra]]-type characins).
- Annual-cycle killifish (*Nothobranchius*, *Pterolebias*, *Cynolebias*, *Cynopoecilus*) live in periodically drying waters and produce diapausing embryos, which makes their chromosome numbers ecologically interesting.
- The study notes that the reduced-number species occupy ecological niches similar to 24-chromosome relatives, so karyotype reduction does not correspond to a niche shift.
- Material is almost entirely freshwater; the author warns that conclusions cannot be extended to marine teleosts without further data.

## Key Findings

- **Sample:** 103 species (Ostariophysi, Percomorphi, Microcyprini, Gasterosteiformes, Synentognathi, Mugiliformes) were examined. Table 1 compiles 210 teleost species with reliable counts, and the Summary reports 106 species from 19 families and 6 orders.
- **Dominant number:** n = 24 occurs in about 52% of records. Within Ostariophysi, nearly all characins and cyprinids show n = 24 (exceptions: n = 26 in *Abramis brama*, 2n = 52 in *Scardinius erythrophthalmus*).
- **Deviant numbers:**
  - *Nothobranchius rachowi* n = 8 (lowest recorded), with the largest nuclei (about 11.5 µm diameter).
  - *Pterolebias longipinnis* n = 10.
  - Other *Nothobranchius* species and *Aphyosemion* species: n = 19 and n = 20.
  - *Gasterosteidae* (*Gasterosteus aculeatus*, *Pungitius pungitius*): n = 21 throughout the family.
  - *Lebistes reticulatus* and *Mollienesia velifera*: n = 23.
- **Salmonids:** Counts range from 2n = 36 to 2n = 102. The author views the 2n ≈ 96 Coregonus (Karbe 1963: n = 48, 2n = 96) as a likely autopolyploid series from n = 24.
- **Fusion hypothesis:** Enlarged chromosomes and ring tetrads in *Nothobranchius*, *Aphyosemion* and *Mollienesia* are read as products of Robertsonian-type fusion of non-homologues. A hybrid F1 of *Mollienesia velifera* × *M. sphenops* was reported as fertile, supporting fusion as the cause of the 23 count.
- **Nuclear size:** Nuclear diameter rises as chromosome number falls: 6.5–7 µm at n = 23–24, 8–8.5 µm at n = 19–20, and 11.5 µm at n = 8.
- **Phylogeny:** The author suggests n = 24 is ancestral to teleosts because it is shared across distant families and continents. Cyclostomes (*Eptatretus burgeri* n = 24; *E. okinoseanus* n = 23) and Amphibia (FN = 24) may show the same ancestral count, but the evidence is limited. Elasmobranchs show no such pattern.
- **Conclusions (Summary):** 79 of 106 species had n = 24. Only three species exceeded 24, and the 21 others ranged from n = 8 to n = 23. Chromosome reduction is restricted to narrow groups and is mainly due to fusion. A direct link between chromosome-number change and new systematic groups is considered unlikely.
