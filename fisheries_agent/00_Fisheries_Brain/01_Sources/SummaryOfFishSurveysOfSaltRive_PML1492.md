# Central Arizona Project Fish Monitoring: Summary of Fish Surveys, 1995–1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-community-monitoring

## Executive Summary

This technical report by Paul C. Marsh ([[Arizona State University]], Center for Environmental Studies) summarizes fish surveys conducted from 1995 to 1997 in canals operated by the [[Salt River Project]] (Arizona Canal and South Canal), the [[Central Arizona Project]], and the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] [[Florence-Casa Grande Canal]]. Surveys were carried out during routine canal dry-up periods using seines, trammel nets, bag seines, and dip nets. The report documents site-level catches, field methods, and data-handling procedures. It also records deviations from the established monitoring protocol. Catches were dominated by a few species, especially the species coded CYLU and PACL, and were strongly concentrated at certain sites. The report was submitted to the Bureau of Reclamation Phoenix Area Office on 10 June 1997, under a cooperative agreement with Arizona State University.

## Background & Description

The survey was a cooperative effort under the [[Long-Term Monitoring Plan]] framework for fish populations in the [[Gila River Basin]]. The protocol was finalized in 1996, after the 1995 Salt River Project collections, so those surveys did not fully conform to it. Fish were identified using species codes (e.g., CYLU, PACL, CAIN, GIRO, MISA, CTID, GAAF), which follow the established protocol. The source does not expand these codes into scientific or common names, and it does not provide family-level taxonomy.

Field crews included Paul C. Marsh, Robert W. Clarkson (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation), Kirk L. Young (Arizona Game and Fish Department), and several Arizona State University staff. Legal right-of-entry was obtained for all sampled properties. Collection was authorized by a permit from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

Sampling used the following gear:
- [[Straight Seine]] (various sizes, e.g., 10 × 4 ft, 15 × 4 ft, 30 × 5 ft)
- [[Trammel Net]] (e.g., 125 × 8 ft, 75 × 6 ft, 25 × 6 ft)
- [[Bag Seine]] (30 × 6 ft)
- [[Dip Net]] (1 × 1 ft)
- Observation only at some sites, where fish were seen but not captured

Sites included the [[Electric Fish Barrier]] reaches, [[Granite Reef Dam]] downstream reaches, the [[Indian Bend Wash Siphon]], the Evergreen Drain, Pima Road, the Pima Lateral, and the China Wash fish barrier.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Surveys were timed to routine canal dry-up periods, which concentrate fish in remaining wetted sections and make complete sampling of reaches possible. Monitored reaches include sections below the electric fish barrier, which is presumably intended to limit fish movement. The report does not provide water chemistry, temperature, flow, or habitat measurements, so environmental conditions beyond the sampling setting are not documented.

The data suggest that catches were heavily dominated by a small number of species, which may reflect either the species present or the sampling conditions. The source does not interpret these patterns ecologically.

## Key Findings

**Data and deviations**
- No publications arose from this research.
- Voucher specimens were not submitted to the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation as planned. Vouchers from other specimens were photographed for a permanent record.
- The protocol and field manuals (Clarkson 1996; USBR 1996) were not available during the 1995 Salt River Project collections, so those data only partly followed protocol.
- Data-entry software was not available until 1 April 1997, which missed the prescribed submission date of 15 December 1996. Data were entered on two PCs and copied to disc.

**1995 surveys**
- *Salt River Project Arizona Canal (25 November 1995):* Below the electric fish barrier, 5 hauls yielded 1,024 fish, of which about 1,000 (~98%) were CYLU. Sites 2.5 miles below Granite Reef Dam produced 50 CYLU. The site 0.3 miles downstream of U.S. Highway 87 yielded 224 fish, with CYLU at 200 (89%). At the Indian Bend Wash Siphon, trammel-net catches (66 fish) were led by PACL (35, 53%) and CAIN (24, 36%). Dip-net catches totaled 20 fish.
- *Salt River Project South Canal (21 October 1995):* Below the electric fish barrier, 10 seine hauls yielded 354 fish (110 juveniles, 244 adults). At the Central Arizona Project (Granite Reef Aqueduct) turnout, 488 fish were captured, with CYLU at 480 (98%). Two miles below the barrier, 98 fish were captured, led by PACL (69, 70%) and CAIN (27, 28%). Trammel-net catches and observation-only sites are reported in the source but are incompletely recoverable from the scanned tables.

**1996–1997 surveys**
- *South Canal (26 October 1996):* Below the electric fish barrier, 378 fish were captured, with CYLU at 296 (78%). At the River Road siphon, 84 fish were captured, mostly MISA (65, 77%). At the terminus (3-way junction), 131 fish were captured, including CYLU (82, 63%) and PACL (43, 33%).
- *Roosevelt Water Conservation District turnout (26 October 1996):* 769 fish were recorded, with CYLU at 699 (~91%) and GAAF at 46 (~6%).
- *Florence-Casa Grande Canal (October 1996):* At Ashurst-Hayden Dam, 105 fish were recorded, with CYLU at 28 (26%). At the China Wash fish barrier, 307 fish were captured, with CYLU at 247 (80%). At the Pima Lateral turnout, 7 fish were captured, including CYLU (4, 57%) and DOPE (3, 43%). At the Pima Feeder confluence, 11 fish were captured, including GAAF (6, 55%). At the concrete weir above the Pima Lateral, 6 fish were captured, including LECY (5, 83%). Two Pima Lateral sites yielded no fish.
- *Arizona Canal (18 January 1997):* Below the electric fish barrier (dip net), 64 fish were captured, with CAIN at 36 (56%) and PACL at 23 (36%). Below U.S. Highway 87, 6 fish were captured. At the Evergreen Drain, a 30 × 6 ft bag seine yielded 288 fish, with PACL at 240 (83%) and CAIN at 43 (15%). At the Indian Bend Wash Siphon, a trammel net yielded 33 fish, led by CAIN (16, 48%) and CTID (9, 27%). Observation-only records were also made at some sites.

**Overall**
- The most abundant species across surveys were CYLU and PACL, with CAIN prominent at several Arizona Canal and Indian Bend sites. Site-level dominance was often high, with a single taxon making up more than half the catch at many locations.
- Field data were tabulated by station, reach, sampling gear, number, and age class, and summarized in Tables 1–5 of the report. Field notes and data sheets are attached as a separate volume.
