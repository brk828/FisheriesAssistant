# Memorandum on Developing a Long-term Monitoring Program for Bonytail and Razorback Sucker Stocking

## Executive Summary
This document is an internal memorandum from the National Biological Service (NBS) Denver Field Station, addressed to the Core Members of the [[Native Fish Work Group]] (NFWG). It does not present primary morphological or ecological research data; rather, it outlines an administrative proposal to develop a **long-term, structured monitoring program** for two federally significant Colorado River fish species: the [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The memo proposes expanding existing larval collection efforts to include year-round adult monitoring and stocking assessment, coordinated across multiple agencies including [[Arizona State University]] (ASU).

## Background & Description
*No morphological, meristic, or physical characteristic data for [[Bonytail]] or [[Razorback Sucker]] were provided in this source document.* The text is strictly programmatic/administrative, focusing on monitoring logistics rather than species biology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While specific ecological data (diet, habitat preferences, water chemistry) are not detailed, the memo implies the following ecological context:
- **Habitat System:** The [[Colorado River Basin]], specifically areas near **Laughlin** (likely the lower Colorado River/Lake Mohave region based on the proposed meeting location).
- **Life Stage Focus:** The current monitoring protocol focuses heavily on **larvae collection and rearing activities**, suggesting these species are managed via a stocking/augmentation program (likely due to wild population decline).
- **Spawning Period:** The group identifies a need to monitor adult fish (via scanning, likely for PIT tags) throughout the "entire spawning period," indicating a seasonal reproductive cycle that requires expanded temporal survey coverage.
- **Conservation Status Implication:** Both species are subject to active "stocking projects," a management technique typically reserved for endangered or threatened species requiring population augmentation (both are federally listed as endangered).

## Key Findings
- **Program Proposal:** The author (NBS Denver) volunteers to lead and coordinate a new, centralized long-term monitoring program to assess the success of bonytail and razorback sucker stocking.
- **Methodological Gap:** Current monitoring is limited to larval collection; there is an identified need to scan and collect **adult fish** to track stocked individuals more broadly.
- **Species Expansion:** [[Bonytail]] monitoring needs specific expansion, as current efforts appear more heavily focused on the [[Razorback Sucker]].
- **Resource Reallocation:** The [[Razorback Sucker Telemetry Study]] is concluding, allowing its resources (funding/personnel) to be redirected toward this new, broader monitoring initiative.
- **Program Design:** The proposal explicitly states the new program should **augment, not replace** existing monitoring efforts, with a goal of filling data gaps and centralizing data analysis.
- **Field Effort:** A specific target of **10–15 field surveys per year** is proposed, requiring significant multi-agency participation (NFWG agencies).
- **Funding Status:** NBS funding is secured through Fiscal Year 1996 (FY-96) and possibly FY-97, but is described as "limited and vulnerable to redirection," necessitating political support from other agencies for future funding.
- **Key Personnel/Logistics:** "Ty" is tasked with developing a field sampling schedule; the plan is slated for detailed discussion at an upcoming meeting in Laughlin.
