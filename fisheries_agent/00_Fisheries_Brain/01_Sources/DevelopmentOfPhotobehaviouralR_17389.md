# Development of Photobehavioural Responses in Young Rainbow Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

Carey and [[Noakes]] (1981) tracked the photobehaviour of young [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) from hatching through completed emergence, using a laboratory [[Artificial Turf Substrate]] incubation system. Free-embryos moved downward into the substrate at all tested light energies, and the rate of descent did not follow the order of light energy levels. A nocturnal activity rhythm was established early in the downward phase. A sudden photoresponse reversal from photonegative to photopositive occurred on day 21, coinciding with synchronous emergence and depletion of 85% of the yolk reserve, when fish were about 25 mm long with paired and median fins fully developed.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** Young rainbow trout, a salmonid. The authors note earlier work on salmonids (e.g., coho salmon, Oncorhynchus kisutch) showing a progressive weakening of initial photonegativity during incubation.
- **Developmental intervals:** The study covers the free-embryo (eleutheroembryonic) interval through alevin emergence from the redd, following terminology of [[Balon Developmental Terminology]].
- **Knowledge gap:** Intragravel light responses were rarely studied because of limited visibility in gravel substrates, so the authors used a transparent-observation artificial turf system.
- **Fish source:** About 1000 spring-spawned embryos from the Normandale Fish Culture Station, transferred to the University of Guelph on 22 April 1980, held in darkness at 9 ± 1 °C.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Experimental system**
- Ten flow-through tanks (six plexiglass, four plywood with fibreglass-coated sides), with a 20 cm deep stack of perforated artificial turf layers as substrate.
- Water temperature 9.5 ± 0.3 °C; dissolved oxygen never below 97% air saturation; flow about 31 min⁻¹.
- Lighting: 15 W fluorescent lights; five experimental light energy levels (100%, 75%, 50%, 25%, 0.8% of maximum), with light measured by underwater pyranometer and attenuated through turf layers (Table I).
- Photoperiod 10 L : 14 D, lights on at 08.00 h, no twilight.

**Morphological monitoring**
- Twelve cylindrical incubators run alongside, sampled periodically; a [[Morphological Index]] (MI) was built from features such as yolk sac volume, fin heights, eye diameter, retinal thickness and total length.

## Key Findings

1. **Initial descent:** Free-embryos moved rapidly into the substrate within 24 h of hatching, averaging 5–6 cm below the surface. Descent slowed from day 2 onward, and fish reached stable mean positions near the tank bottoms on days 16–21.
2. **Light-level effects:** Fish at 75% and 0.8% light energy descended significantly more slowly and less far (P < 0.05) than other groups, but descent rates did not reflect a simple ordering of light levels. The authors propose a possible gravitational or random component.
3. **Synchronous emergence:** Alevins began emerging on day 21 in all groups. Emergence took eight to ten days and reached 97% completion by day 37. Emergence rates did not differ significantly across light levels (P > 0.05).
4. **Nocturnal rhythm:** Vertical position changed only during the dark phase. Emerged fish moved near the water surface after lights off, indicating a nocturnal activity pattern established during incubation.
5. **Photoresponse reversal:** The phototactic index rose from about 10% on day 2 to 84% on day 35. An aggregate shift to photopositive behaviour occurred on day 21, at an index of 50%. The log-linear regression gave log y = 0.03x + 0.89 (r² = 0.91).
6. **Morphological correlates:** The photoresponse change coincided with about 85% yolk volume depletion and with the dorsal, anal and pectoral fins roughly doubling. Yolk sac volume fell to about 10% of original by day 24. Fish were about 25 mm long at the change.
7. **Interpretation:** The authors argue the abrupt shift supports [[Balon Saltational Development Theory]], in which ontogeny proceeds through steps and thresholds rather than gradually. Phototactic reversal may be a precondition for emergence.
8. **Contrast with marine fishes:** Newly-hatched marine larvae lack retinomotor responses and are typically photopositive, whereas salmonids are photonegative at hatching. This marks a major behavioural divergence between marine and freshwater early life histories.
9. **Open questions:** Causal factors for the photoresponse change remain unknown. Metabolic and nutritional explanations were judged insufficient, and the authors suggest links to changes in the ratio of paired visual pigments, which have not been documented ontogenetically in salmonids.
