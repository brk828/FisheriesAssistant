## Executive Summary

This document records the minutes of an interagency meeting held on 12 April 2000 at Arizona State University to develop long-term fisheries management strategies for [[Fossil Creek]], Arizona, in anticipation of the decommissioning of the Arizona Public Service (APS) Childs/Irving Hydroelectric Facility. Fossil Creek is a spring-fed tributary of the [[Verde River]] with unique travertine water chemistry, supporting a native fish assemblage including the [[Headwater Chub]] and [[Roundtail Chub]]. Participating agencies included the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Arizona State University]], [[U.S. Forest Service]], and [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The meeting established a long-term vision of native fish conservation and sanctuary creation upstream of a constructed fish exclusion barrier, contingent on the return of full spring flows (ca. 43 cfs) after facility decommissioning (proposed no later than 31 December 2004).

## Background & Description

While this document is primarily a management planning record rather than a morphological study, it notes taxonomic distinctions relevant to species identification and hybridization concerns:

- **[[Headwater Chub]]** (*Gila nigra*) — distinguished taxonomically from the **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*); occurs in the reach above the Fossil Springs diversion dam.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*) — confirmed present in the [[Verde River]]; its co-occurrence and possible hybridization/introgression with *G. nigra* within Fossil Creek itself is undetermined and flagged for genetic/tissue sampling.
- Native suckers present include **[[Sonora Sucker]]** and **[[Desert Sucker]]**, both extant in the current stream channel despite reduced flows.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) were experimentally stocked above the Fossil Springs diversion dam and persisted for only a few years, suggesting marginal habitat suitability under current diverted-flow conditions.
- Razorback sucker were also documented in **[[Stehr Lake]]** in 1999, a small regulating reservoir associated with the hydroelectric project.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure**: Fossil Creek comprises ~14 miles of aquatic habitat from Fossil Springs to the confluence with the [[Verde River]]. Under current hydroelectric operations, nearly all spring discharge (~43 cfs) is diverted via flumes/pipelines to the Irving and Childs plants, leaving only ca. 0.2 cfs baseflow in the upper reach and 2+ cfs below Irving.
- **Natural barriers**: Two natural waterfall barriers exist in the upper 3 miles of the creek (one near Irving, one ~1 mile above Irving), preventing upstream fish movement and effectively excluding nonnative fishes above them. The **[[Fossil Springs Diversion Dam]]** also acts as a barrier, likely reinforced by an underlying bedrock ledge.
- **Nonnative species impacts**: Nonnative fishes — [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Fathead Minnow]], and [[Flathead Catfish]] — occur throughout much of the creek but are notably absent above the natural barriers and above the diversion dam. Nonnative [[Crayfish]] and [[Asian Clam]] are also present and considered undesirable, with no effective removal method currently available.
- **Macroinvertebrate community**: The travertine chemistry of Fossil Springs water likely supports a unique and potentially rare macroinvertebrate assemblage (including springsnails), warranting documentation prior to any piscicide treatment.
- **Stocktank connectivity**: Nonnative fish populations in stocktanks within the watershed pose a risk of colonizing Fossil Creek via flood-driven washout, threatening long-term native fish conservation goals.
- **Wilderness context**: Portions of the creek, including candidate barrier construction sites, lie within the [[Mazatzal Wilderness]], requiring special management consideration.

## Key Findings

- **Decommissioning driver**: APS proposed surrender of its FERC license by 31 December 2004, which would restore full natural flow (~43 cfs) to Fossil Creek and enable ecosystem recovery — but would also make future in-stream construction and piscicide renovation far more difficult once flows are restored.
- **Management vision**: Consensus to manage Fossil Creek for native fish conservation, extending protection as close to the Verde River confluence as possible via construction of a fish exclusion barrier in the lower 5 miles, followed by chemical removal ([[Antimycin]]) of nonnative fishes upstream and restocking with salvaged natives.
- **Phased renovation strategy**:
  1. Stage 1: 3-mile reach between the diversion dam and Irving (existing natural barriers), targeted for renovation beginning May 2002, requiring a minimum of two chemical treatments spaced weeks apart.
  2. Stage 2: 7–10 mile reach from Irving downstream to a yet-to-be-built exclusion barrier.
- **Barrier siting**: The [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] identified and surveyed two candidate barrier sites, with a third under investigation, all located within Mazatzal Wilderness.
- **Diversion dam disposition**: Agreed to leave the Fossil Springs Diversion Dam in place at least until after renovation is completed, due to concerns about sediment release and channel downcutting if removed.
- **Genetic/taxonomic uncertainty**: The distribution and potential co-occurrence of *Gila nigra* and *G. robusta* within Fossil Creek is unresolved; tissue sampling (June 2000) and analysis by W. L. Minckley were assigned to clarify this.
- **Stehr Lake fate**: Following decommissioning, Stehr Lake will no longer receive flume-transported water and is expected to dry up, eliminating its small nonnative sport fishery (~200 angler-days/year) and associated wetlands — considered an acceptable tradeoff given anticipated native fishery gains in Fossil Creek.
- **Non-fish concerns**: Piscicide use raises concerns for aquatic macroinvertebrates and amphibians; renovation timing will be adjusted to minimize impacts on amphibian eggs/larvae, and macroinvertebrate surveys will be conducted beforehand.
- **Long-term opportunities**: Potential future establishment of sport angling for [[Headwater Chub]], fish-watching/environmental education programs, and compatibility with broader Verde River native fish recovery efforts (e.g., razorback sucker) pending biological review.
- **Interagency assignments**: Specific tasks were delegated across agencies for NEPA compliance, barrier design/funding, renovation protocols, macroinvertebrate studies, genetic sampling, stocktank management, public involvement, and Endangered Species Act consultation streamlining.
