# Second Quarterly Progress Report on Spikedace and Loach Minnow Study

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #southwestern-us-fishes

## Executive Summary

This progress report (covering 1 May–31 July 1987) documents field and laboratory activities conducted by [[Arizona State University]] under contract to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] to study the distribution, microhabitat associations, and diet of [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), two imperiled cyprinids native to the Gila River basin. Standardized sampling protocols developed at [[Aravaipa Creek]] were successfully applied at three additional study systems — the [[Verde River]], the [[Upper Gila River]] (New Mexico), and the [[Blue River]] (Arizona) — yielding paired fish/microhabitat, aquatic drift, and benthic macroinvertebrate datasets. Laboratory work focused on invertebrate taxonomy for diet analysis and development of larval fish identification criteria. Data entry and preliminary preparation for discriminant function/clustering analyses of fish–microhabitat relationships were initiated.

## Background & Description

- **Target taxa:**
  - [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) — small-bodied cyprinid of concern, native to the Gila River basin.
  - [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — small benthic cyprinid, co-occurring with spikedace in riffle habitats.
  - [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) — sampled as an associated/comparative species, including for dietary and drift analysis.
- **Study objective:** Quantify relationships between target fish species distributions and microhabitat variables using paired fish/microhabitat seine sampling, supplemented by aquatic drift and benthic macroinvertebrate sampling (prey/food-web context) and dietary analysis of preserved specimens.
- **Methodological development:** Sampling protocols for fish, microhabitat characterization, benthic macroinvertebrates (kick sampling), and aquatic drift were field-tested and finalized during March–April 1987 visits to [[Aravaipa Creek]], establishing the methodological template applied at all subsequent sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Aravaipa Creek, Arizona** — Site of protocol development; referenced from the First Progress Report as the source of an established fish/microhabitat dataset.
- **Verde River, Arizona** — Study sites established at:
  - U.S. Forest Service Road (FSR) 638, near [[Perkinsville]]
  - Above the confluence with [[Sycamore Creek]]
  - Sampling at the Sycamore Creek site was **ineffective using seine methods** due to excessive water volume/depth and habitat size; site flagged for future **electrofishing**.
- **Upper Gila River, New Mexico** — Three study reaches established near the community of [[Gila, New Mexico]]; supported ontogenetic (developmental) series collections of spikedace, loach minnow, and red shiner.
- **Blue River, Arizona** — Three locales sampled in the [[Juan Miller Crossing]] area; loach minnow was the focal species for diel drift sampling here.
- **Sampling design across sites** integrated:
  - Paired fish/microhabitat seine collections
  - Diel (noon and midnight) paired fish/aquatic drift samples
  - Triplicate benthic macroinvertebrate kick samples
  - At Perkinsville, a fine-scale temporal series: paired red shiner/duplicate aquatic drift samples every 2–4 hours over a full 24-hour cycle.
  - At Blue River, paired loach minnow/duplicate aquatic drift samples every 4 hours over 24 hours.

## Key Findings

- **Protocol finalization:** Field methods for fish, microhabitat, benthic macroinvertebrate, and drift sampling were successfully standardized at Aravaipa Creek and transferred to three additional river systems without major modification (except where site conditions precluded seining).
- **Sampling effort by site:**
  - Verde River (FSR 638 & Perkinsville): 50 paired fish/microhabitat samples each, plus duplicate diel drift and triplicate kick samples; 24-hr fine-interval red shiner/drift series at Perkinsville.
  - Upper Gila River, NM (3 locales near Gila): 80 paired fish/microhabitat samples per locale; ontogenetic series of spikedace, loach minnow, red shiner collected; diel drift and triplicate benthic samples taken.
  - Blue River, AZ (3 locales, Juan Miller crossing): 80 paired fish/microhabitat samples per locale; 24-hr loach minnow/drift series at 4-hr intervals; triplicate benthic macroinvertebrate samples.
- **Site limitation identified:** Standard seine-based fish/microhabitat sampling was not viable at the Sycamore Creek confluence site on the Verde River due to hydrological conditions (depth, volume) and habitat scale — indicating a need for **electrofishing** as an alternative technique in larger/deeper habitats.
- **Specimen preservation:** Ontogenetic (life-stage) series of spikedace, loach minnow, red shiner, and associated fishes were preserved and returned to ASU specifically for **dietary analysis**.
- **Laboratory progress:**
  - Development of taxonomic familiarity with aquatic macroinvertebrates across three sample types (benthic kick, drift, fish stomach contents).
  - Establishment of criteria for **differentiating larval fish species** encountered across samples — a key methodological step for accurate ontogenetic and community-level analysis.
- **Data management:** Fish/microhabitat data from Aravaipa Creek and Verde River were formatted, coded, and entered into the ASU mainframe computer in preparation for **discriminant function and/or clustering analyses** aimed at quantifying species–microhabitat relationships. Additional site data entry was deferred pending validation of analytical approach.
