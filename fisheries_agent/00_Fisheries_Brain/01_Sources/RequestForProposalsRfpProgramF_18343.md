# Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program: Request for Proposals Package (1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-species-act #conservation-planning

## Executive Summary

This 1997 Request for Proposals (RFP) package from the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (LCR MSCP) Steering Committee solicits two separate consultant contracts to support development of a multi-species conservation plan for the Lower Colorado River. RFP-1 seeks [[Program Facilitation]] services; RFP-2 seeks [[Conservation Plan Development]] services. Both proposals were due March 19, 1997, and the Program is scheduled for completion by May 15, 2002, with a 50-year implementation period. The Steering Committee aims to produce a plan that satisfies [[Endangered Species Act]] section 7 and section 10 requirements, [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA), and [[California Endangered Species Act]] obligations, within a budget not exceeding $4.5 million.

## Background & Description

The Steering Committee is a partnership of federal, state, tribal, and public and private stakeholders. Members include water, hydropower, and wildlife agencies in Arizona, California, and Nevada, Lower Colorado River tribal governments, environmental organizations, and the [[U.S. Department of the Interior]]. The [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]] (NFWF), an ex officio member, administers the consultant contracts on the Steering Committee's behalf.

Program history:
- 1993: Discussions between State parties and the Department of the Interior began.
- November 1994: Partnership Memorandum of Understanding executed, establishing the Steering Committee.
- December 1994: Feasibility Assessment completed, recommending a proactive multi-species planning effort.
- August 1995: Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) executed, initiating Program development, with a three-year funding commitment.
- July 1996: Memorandum of Clarification (MOC) signed by the seven original MOA signatories.
- June 1996: Federal-State cost-sharing agreement executed for an approximately three-year, $4.5 million development period.

The biological scope covers 102 species on the "Included Species" list, drawn from federal and state listed and sensitive species within the mainstem and 100-year floodplain. The planning area runs from below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] to the Mexican border and includes full-pool reservoir elevations. Land ownership is mixed, spanning federal, tribal, state, local, and private lands.

Organizational structure: the Steering Committee sets policy and provides funding and oversight; a Work Group serves as staff; ad hoc subcommittees (biology, legal, legislative, grantsmanship, cost allocation, finance) report upward. A Point of Contact (POC) manages technical liaison, and the NFWF handles contract administration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Lower Colorado River ecosystem includes aquatic, wetland, riparian, and upland components. The Steering Committee notes that the traditional terrestrial reserve-and-corridor model of conservation planning must be modified to address both aquatic and terrestrial systems, potentially through an [[Aquatic GAP Analysis]] or a habitat-based ecosystem model using biotic and abiotic parameters.

Species of conservation concern named in the Included Species list (Attachment 2) include:
- Fishes: [[Razorback Sucker]] (federally endangered), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (federally endangered), [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], and Virgin River Chub, Moapa speckled dace, and Mohave tui chub listed as species for possible exclusion or further review.
- Other taxa: [[Desert Tortoise]], [[Southwestern River Otter]], [[Yuma Clapper Rail]], western willow flycatcher, and several bird, bat, reptile, amphibian, and invertebrate species.

The attachment contains three tables: 52 species with potential direct or indirect effects (Table 1), 22 species that may be excluded later (Table 2), and 28 species to add pending further information (Table 3). Table 1 is summarized here rather than reproduced in full; of the 102 species, fishes and aquatic invertebrates form a substantial portion of the most sensitive taxa.

Interim conservation measures (ICMs) target [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] for 1995-96 implementation, with additional ICMs to be identified annually.

## Key Findings

**Program structure and scope**
- Two separate RFPs, each requiring a separate submittal: RFP-1 (facilitation) and RFP-2 (plan development).
- Proposals due by 12:30 p.m. Eastern Time, March 19, 1997; 20 copies required.
- Pre-submittal workshop: February 25, 1997, Las Vegas, Nevada.
- Interviews for short-listed respondents: April 14-15, 1997, for both RFPs.
- Written clarification requests due by noon Pacific Time, February 24, 1997.
- All work, including public review and permits, must be complete by May 15, 2002.

**Deliverables (RFP-2)**
- A comprehensive Lower Colorado River MSCP conservation plan for 50-year implementation.
- A non-federal [[Habitat Conservation Plan]] and section 10(a)(1)(B) incidental take permit application(s).
- A revised [[Biological Assessment]] of Reclamation's Lower Colorado River operations, under section 7.
- A regional GIS (ARC/INFO-based, Oracle-compatible), with five digital copies and monthly updates.
- Environmental documentation: an EIS/EIR is assumed (rather than an EA and Initial Study), in addition to the NEPA and CEQA requirements.
- Long-term monitoring program, funding strategies, and implementation agreements.

**Meeting load (for cost estimating)**
- 45 one-day meetings assumed per year, including 23 Steering Committee or Work Group meetings (18 in Las Vegas, five in Phoenix), 10 stakeholder briefings, and 12 subcommittee meetings.
- Baseline: 40 meetings held in 1995.
- Minutes must be distributed within 10 business days.

**Evaluation criteria**
- RFP-1 weights are not ranked; criteria include technical process approach, management, professional and institutional qualifications, scope understanding, and cost.
- RFP-2 criteria include technical approach (GIS, ecosystem modeling, economics, "Law of the River" knowledge), integration of ESA, NEPA, and CEQA compliance, outside coordination (peer review, public outreach, funding), and familiarity with lower Colorado River fauna and flora.

**Budget and cost constraints**
- Total Program development funding is capped at $4.5 million, covering both RFPs, ICMs, and Program management.
- Phasing of tasks is likely because of annual appropriations; proposals must reflect this.
- CEQA- and California ESA-specific costs must be budgeted separately from NEPA and federal ESA costs.

**Notable program constraints**
- No new data collection by the consultant unless approved by the Steering Committee.
- Confidentiality of special-status species and cultural resource data must be addressed.
- Key personnel and subconsultants cannot be replaced without written Steering Committee approval.
