# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Critical Habitat Recommendations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #recovery-team #critical-habitat

## Executive Summary

This document is a formal letter dated April 14, 1993, from the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] to the Regional Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, providing recommendations on the proposed rule to establish critical habitat for four endangered [[Colorado River]] fish species: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*). The Recovery Team met in [[Grand Junction]], Colorado, on April 12-13, 1993, to compile consensus recommendations focused solely on biological ramifications of critical habitat designation. The Team strongly supports critical habitat designation as essential for species recovery and provides specific recommendations for additions and deletions to proposed critical habitat areas, representing a net increase of less than 200 miles.

## Background & Description

The four endangered [[Colorado River]] fish species addressed in this document are all members of the family [[Cyprinidae]]:

**[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*): Historically captured in multiple reaches of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. In the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]], individuals are captured in flooded backwaters. The species is rare in the [[Upper Basin]], and sampling difficulty in flooded bottomlands may mask the importance of these habitats.

**[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*): Recently captured in the [[Yampa River]], [[Little Snake River]], [[Colorado River]] (Blackrocks/Westwater reach), [[Little Colorado River]], and [[Grand Canyon]] reaches. The species occupies habitats including tributary streams and potential spawning sites.

**[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): Historic and recent captures documented throughout the basin. Recent use by adults confirmed by radio telemetry studies. The species utilizes tributaries for spawning and staging during migration periods.

**[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): Documented in multiple river reaches and reservoirs. The species utilizes both riverine and reservoir habitats, with reservoirs playing a role in maintaining genetic stocks of adults in altered habitats.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Recovery Team emphasizes that recovery cannot be achieved without secure habitats. Critical habitat recommendations extend beyond "essential habitats" identified in approved recovery plans, based on information that became available since plan completion.

**Reservoir Habitats**: Extensions of critical habitat into mainstem reservoirs ([[Lake Powell]], [[Lake Mead]], [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]]) acknowledge their importance in maintaining genetic stocks of adult endangered fishes in altered habitats. Suitability of these areas for sustaining adult fish and spawning (e.g., [[Lake Mohave]]) depends on pool elevation and may shift in location as pool elevation changes over time. Although these reservoirs do not provide for the full life cycle of the species, they may play a key role in conservation and recovery.

**Floodplain Habitats**: The 100-year floodplain is recommended for inclusion along designated reaches, particularly for [[Bonytail Chub]], as individuals are captured in flooded backwaters in the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]].

**Tributary Systems**: Multiple tributary streams are identified as important for spawning, staging, and population expansion, including [[Bright Angel Creek]], [[Shinumo Creek]], [[Kanab Creek]], [[Havasu Creek]], [[Little Snake River]], [[Animas River]], [[Mancos River]], and others.

**Habitat Degradation**: Some reaches are noted as severely channelized and riprapped (e.g., Davis Dam to I-40 bridge at Needles, CA), subjected to intense human use with attendant impacts, and regarded as of limited value in recovery.

## Key Findings

**General Recommendations:**

1. The [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] strongly supports designation of critical habitat for all four endangered [[Colorado River]] fish species, consistent with law and essential for ultimate recovery.

2. The Team requests that the biological support document be reviewed by the Recovery Team prior to formal publication to strengthen the document by providing additional information and independent validation.

3. Net increase in critical habitat attributable to recommendations is expected to be less than 200 miles, with many additional miles situated in or near national parks where certain constraints are already imposed.

4. Longer reaches identified by individual Team members as potential critical habitat were eliminated from recommendations based on assessment that they did not meet current standards for designation, though many should be re-evaluated in light of future information.

**Species-Specific Recommendations:**

**[[Bonytail Chub]]:**
- Include 100-year floodplain along all designated reaches
- [[Yampa River]]: extend upstream from east boundary of [[Dinosaur National Monument]] to head of [[Cross Mountain Canyon]] (ca. RM 58) - site of historic captures
- [[Green River]]: extend downstream from lower end of [[Dinosaur National Monument]] to Jensen bridge - site of historic captures with diverse habitats
- [[Colorado River]] (Blackrocks/Westwater reach): extend downstream to Potash (ca. RM 47) - historic captures and diverse habitats
- DELETE: Davis Dam to I-40 bridge at Needles, CA (ca. 30 miles) - severely channelized, riprapped, and subject to intense human use

**[[Humpback Chub]]:**
- [[Yampa River]]: extend upstream to head of [[Cross Mountain Canyon]] (ca. RM 58); extend into [[Little Snake River]] from confluence upstream to bridge at Moffat County Road 10 (about 7 miles) - recent captures
- [[Colorado River]] (Blackrocks/Westwater reach): extend downstream to Moab bridge - recent and historic captures
- [[Little Colorado River]]: extend upstream to and including [[Blue Springs]] complex (ca. RM 22) - currently occupied, protects critical water source
- [[Grand Canyon]]: extend (a) upstream to mouth of [[Paria River]], (b) downstream to [[Diamond Creek]] (ca. RM 226), (c) lower 1.0 miles of [[Bright Angel Creek]], (d) lower 0.5 miles of [[Shinumo Creek]], (e) lower 1.0 miles of [[Kanab Creek]], (f) lower 20 miles of [[Havasu Creek]] (below Mooney Falls), (g) 100-year floodplain extending into tributary streams - recent and historic captures, potential spawning sites

**[[Colorado Squawfish]]:**
- [[Yampa River]]: extend upstream to confluence with [[Elkhead Creek]] (ca. RM 96); extend into [[Elkhead Creek]] upstream to bridge at Moffat County Road 29 (ca. 10 miles) - historic and recent captures, important area conditioning during spawning/migration periods
- [[Green River]]: extend upstream to confluence with [[Yampa River]] (ca. RM 385); extend into [[Lodore Canyon]] to confluence with [[Pot Creek]] (ca. RM 481) - historic habitat, recent adult use confirmed by radio telemetry
- [[Colorado River]]: extend to include lower 2 miles of [[Gunnison River]] - historic and recent captures, early season warming for conditioning and staging
- [[San Juan River]]: extend upstream from [[Animas River]] to Colorado state line; extend into [[Mancos River]] to ca. RM 1.5 - historic captures, potential for expanded population occupation

**[[Razorback Sucker]]:**
- Suggested consideration: extend critical habitat below [[Redlands Diversion Dam]] - provides habitat for expanding population
- [[Colorado River]] (in Utah): extend into [[Lake Powell]] to about 5 miles upstream
- [[San Juan River]]: extend upstream to confluence with [[Animas River]]; extend into [[Animas River]] to state line; extend downstream into [[Lake Powell]] - upper reach has historic captures and diverse habitats, lower reach incorporates potential spawning area
- [[Colorado River]] (Grand Canyon): extend to include (a) confluence with [[Little Colorado River]], (b) [[Blue Springs]] complex (ca. RM 22), (c) lower 20 miles of [[Bright Angel Creek]], (d) lower 0.5 miles of [[Shinumo Creek]], (e) lower 1.0 miles of [[Kanab Creek]], (f) lower 20 miles of [[Havasu Creek]] (below Mooney Falls), (g) 100-year floodplain extending into tributary streams - additional reaches provide habitat for expanded populations
- [[Gila River]]: extend critical habitat approximately 30 miles upstream into New Mexico; extend upstream in [[Bonita Creek]] to Reservation boundary; extend into [[Willow Creek]]; extend into [[Cibeque Creek]] - potential for reintroduction in [[Salt River]] and [[Verde River]] systems
