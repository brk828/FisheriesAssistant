# Accuracy of the Scale Method in Determining Ages of Largemouth Bass and Bluegill from Alabama Waters

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Centrarchidae #age-determination #scale-analysis

## Executive Summary

This study evaluated the accuracy of the scale method for age determination in [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*) and [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) from Alabama waters using fish of known ages from experimental ponds. Photographic prints of scales were examined by a technician without prior knowledge of fish ages. For largemouth bass (n=272 scales), 80.1% were aged correctly initially, with an additional 3.7% showing correct annuli upon re-examination, leaving 16.2% without annuli corresponding to known ages. For bluegill (n=264 scales), 76.1% were aged correctly initially, with an additional 4.9% correct upon re-examination, leaving 18.9% without corresponding annuli. The majority of aging errors resulted from lack of distinct annulus formation rather than misinterpretation, with nearly all errors representing underaging (too few annuli counted).

## Background & Description

Age determinations using the [[Scale Method]] have been used extensively for determining growth rates of fishes in natural waters, following methods described by [[Van Oosten]] (1928). However, considerable doubt has been expressed concerning the accuracy of age determinations, especially for fish from southern waters.

Fish were obtained from four fertilized ponds stocked at 1,500 bluegill and 100 largemouth bass per acre, and one unfertilized pond stocked at 400 bluegill and 30 largemouth bass per acre. Thirty bass were taken from feeding experiments where they received different amounts and kinds of food from June to October, with scales, weights, and lengths recorded at monthly intervals. Complete records were maintained on hatching dates, stocking dates, sampling dates, and fish measurements.

**Scale Collection and Preparation Methods:**
- Scales removed from area between dorsal and pectoral fins, immediately above or below lateral line
- Dozen or more scales removed with forceps from each fish and placed in envelopes
- Two to three dried scales selected using binocular microscope for mounting
- Only typical, clear, non-regenerated specimens selected
- Scales cleaned by rubbing between moistened layers of cheese cloth
- Mounted on glass slides in water-glass and glycerin solution (60 g sodium silicate dissolved in 100 ml boiling water, filtered, then 10 ml glycerin mixed with 40 ml water-glass solution)
- Negative prints made by projecting scale image directly onto photographic printing paper using microprojection machine
- Smaller scales enlarged to cover major portion of 5×7 inch print
- Larger scales: each half enlarged to cover 5×7 inch print

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study utilized experimental ponds at [[Auburn University]] representing different management regimes:
- **Fertilized ponds:** Stocked at higher densities (1,500 bluegill and 100 largemouth bass per acre)
- **Unfertilized pond:** Stocked at lower densities (400 bluegill and 30 largemouth bass per acre)
- **Feeding experiments:** 30 bass received controlled feeding regimes with different amounts and kinds of food from June to October

The experimental design allowed for known-age fish with documented life histories, providing ground truth for validating the scale aging method. The author had gained considerable experience studying scales from known-age fish from these experimental ponds before conducting the formal examinations.

## Key Findings

**Largemouth Bass Results (n=272 scales):**
- Age distribution: Age-group 0 = 18 fish, Age-group I = 238 fish, Age-group II = 21 fish
- **Initial examination (ages unknown):** 218 scales (80.1%) aged correctly
  - Of these, 38 appeared doubtful but were aged correctly
- **Re-examination (ages known):** Additional 10 scales (3.7%) found to have correct number of annuli, though many were indistinct
- **Incorrect aging:** 44 scales (16.2%) did not possess annuli corresponding to known ages
  - 43 scales were underaged (showed too few annuli)
  - Only 1 scale was overaged
- Monthly variation in accuracy documented (Table 2, specific percentages not provided in text)

**Bluegill Results (n=264 scales):**
- Age distribution: Age-group I = 1 fish, Age-group II = 201 fish, Age-group III = 62 fish
- **Initial examination (ages unknown):** 201 scales (76.1%) aged correctly
  - Of these, 86 appeared doubtful but were aged correctly
- **Re-examination (ages known):** Additional 13 scales (4.9%) found to have correct number of annuli, though many were not distinct
- **Incorrect aging:** 50 scales (18.9%) did not possess annuli corresponding to known ages
  - All incorrectly aged scales were underaged (too few annuli)
  - No scales were overaged

**Primary Conclusions:**
- Scales of both species often contained indefinite annuli
- Majority of aging errors caused by lack of formation of distinct annuli rather than misinterpretation
- Underaging (missing annuli) was the predominant error type
- Even experienced technicians with knowledge of known ages could not identify correct annuli in 16-19% of scales
- The scale method shows reasonable but imperfect accuracy for age determination in Alabama waters
- Concerns about accuracy of scale aging in southern waters appear warranted based on these results
