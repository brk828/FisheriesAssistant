# Sexual Differentiation of Bluegills by the Urogenital Opening

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lepomis

## Executive Summary

This short methods note, reprinted from *The Progressive Fish-Culturist* (Vol. 30, No. 1, January 1968), describes an external method for sexing [[Bluegill]] ([[Lepomis Macrochirus]]) by examining the [[Urogenital Opening]]. The author, [[Thomas S. McComish]] of the [[Missouri Cooperative Fishery Unit]] at the [[University of Missouri]] in [[Columbia Missouri]], reports that males and females differ reliably in opening shape and surrounding tissue. The method was validated against dissection on 200 fish and worked for other researchers in winter and spring. It is limited to fish that have reached [[Sexual Maturity]].

## Background & Description

The note is aimed at [[Fishery Management]], research, and [[Fish Hatchery Production]] settings where sexing bluegills by external features is needed.

**Female urogenital opening**
- Pink and fleshy, resembling a small, swollen, doughnut-like ring, probably from slight eversion of the urogenital tract.
- Swelling varies with season and is greatest during the breeding season.
- Outside diameter of the fleshy area is considerably larger than in a male of comparable size.

**Male urogenital opening**
- Usually only a very small fleshy area, and often none.
- Never ring-like in appearance.
- Usually terminates in a small funnel-shaped pore, though this depends on the robustness of the male.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Validation sites:** Pond populations of bluegill near Columbia, Missouri, plus a lake and a large impoundment.
- **Seasonal reliability:** Sexing worked in September and October, in winter (lake fish), and in early May (large impoundment). Differentiation appears permanent across seasons once fish are sexually mature.
- **Maturity threshold:** In central Missouri, sexual maturity and the associated differentiation usually occur at about 75 to 100 mm in length.

## Key Findings

1. **Reliable external sexing:** The urogenital opening offers a dependable external method for sexing bluegills, with a clear contrast between the ring-like, enlarged female opening and the small, non-ring male opening with a funnel-shaped pore.
2. **Validation accuracy:** In September and October, 200 bluegills measuring 90 to 144 mm were sexed without error, confirmed by dissection.
3. **Transferability:** After a demonstration, another researcher sexed bluegills from a lake in winter with complete success. The author also sexed fish from a large impoundment in early May with ease.
4. **Season effects:** Female swelling peaks during the breeding season, so the female signal is strongest then. Once fish are mature, differentiation appears permanent regardless of season.
5. **Key limitation:** The method only works on sexually mature fish. In central Missouri, this is typically reached at 75 to 100 mm, so smaller juveniles cannot be sexed this way.
6. **Practical value:** The technique offers a low-cost, non-lethal alternative to dissection for sex determination in managed and cultured bluegill populations, at least for fish past maturity.
