# Aravaipa Creek Red Shiner Sampling and Monitoring Correspondence (1998)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is an Arizona Game & Fish Department memorandum dated June 19, 1998, with an attached letter and field data sheets. It concerns fish sampling in [[Aravaipa Creek]] after a concern about declining native fish presence and the invasion of [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). The memo was sent by [[Heidi B. Blasius]] of the [[Region V Fisheries Program]] to [[Dr. W. L. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]], who had raised the concern after [[ASU]] sampling in mid-April 1998. Region V sampled the creek on June 9–10 and proposed an intensive follow-up survey for July 6–9, 1998. The stated goals were to document red shiner reproduction, track its movement and establishment, and remove as many exotic fish as possible. The June 9 sampling found no red shiners at the site sampled, while larval and juvenile fishes were common in all areas sampled.

## Background & Description

The memo explains the origin of the project. [[Dr. W. L. Minckley]] wrote to the agency about the lack of native fish and the red shiner invasion observed during the April ASU survey. The [[Region V Fisheries Program]] responded by sampling four localities on June 9 and 10. It then asked for expert opinion, suggestions, and field help for an intensive survey.

The planned July survey had three objectives:
1. Document red shiner reproduction.
2. Delineate red shiner movement and establishment in Aravaipa Creek.
3. Remove as many exotic fishes as possible.

The memo also states that the data and suggestions would be used to build a long-term [[Fish Monitoring]] schedule for the creek.

The attached letter describes the June 9–10 field work in detail. On June 9, a reach about 200 m downstream of the [[Turkey Creek]] confluence was broken into 25 m segments and sampled by [[Seining]] and [[Electrofishing]]. On June 10, sampling ran from the [[Deer Canyon]] confluence to about 900 m above [[Parsons Canyon]].

Pages 3–10 are scanned field data sheets and maps. Most entries are illegible after OCR, so they are not summarized in detail.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Aravaipa Creek is a stream system where native fish communities are a conservation concern and where nonnative fishes such as the red shiner are a threat. The memo links the red shiner's spread to the decline of native fish. Its proposed actions (removing exotics, tracking reproduction and movement) reflect standard [[Nonnative Species]] management.

The letter notes that the sections above and below Turkey Creek held "hundreds of larval fishes," showing active spawning or recruitment in the reaches sampled. Larval and juvenile fishes were recorded at all sampled areas.

## Key Findings

- **No red shiners at the June 9 site:** The reach about 200 m downstream of the Turkey Creek confluence, sampled in 25 m segments by seining and electrofishing, contained no red shiners.
- **Abundant early life stages:** Hundreds of larval fishes were found above and below Turkey Creek. Larval and juvenile fishes were observed in every area sampled.
- **Species count table:** The letter includes a catch table for four localities (LOC 1–4) with eight species columns, coded AGCH, MEFU, RHOS, GIRO, TICO, PACL, CAIN, and AMNA. The codes are not expanded in the document. Recorded totals by locality were:
  - Locality 1: 161
  - Locality 2: 49
  - Locality 3: 300
  - Locality 4: 123
  
  Locality 3 had the highest total, and Locality 2 the lowest. Some OCR digits in the table may be imprecise.
- **Planned intensive survey:** July 6–9, 1998, with three goals: document reproduction, delineate movement and establishment, and remove exotics.
- **Monitoring program:** The agency plans to use the results to set up a continuing [[Fish Monitoring]] schedule for Aravaipa Creek.
- **Expert collaboration requested:** [[Dr. W. L. Minckley]] was asked for opinions, suggestions, and field assistance. Contact information for the author was given in the memo.
