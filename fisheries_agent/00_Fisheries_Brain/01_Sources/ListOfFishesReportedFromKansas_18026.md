# List of Fishes Reported from Kansas

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #species-checklist #Great-Plains

## Executive Summary

This document presents a comprehensive taxonomic checklist of fish species documented in Kansas waters. The list encompasses 122 species across 22 families, ranging from primitive lampreys and sturgeons to advanced percids and centrarchids. Many species are further subdivided into subspecies, reflecting geographic variation across Kansas river systems including the [[Missouri River]], [[Arkansas River]], and [[Neosho River]]. The fauna represents typical Great Plains freshwater assemblages with notable diversity in minnows (Cyprinidae), suckers (Catostomidae), catfishes (Ictaluridae), sunfishes (Centrarchidae), and darters (Percidae).

## Background & Description

The checklist follows standard taxonomic nomenclature with scientific names and authorities, organized by family. Major taxonomic groups include:

**Primitive Fishes**: [[Petromyzonidae]] (lampreys), [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons including [[Lake Sturgeon]] *Acipenser fulvescens*, [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]] *Scaphirhynchus platorhynchus*, [[Pallid Sturgeon]] *Scaphirhynchus album*), [[Polyodontidae]] ([[Paddlefish]] *Polyodon spathula*), [[Lepisosteidae]] (gars), and [[Amiidae]] ([[Bowfin]] *Amia calva*).

**Cyprinidae** (minnows and carps): The most diverse family with 48 species including multiple *Notropis* species (shiners), *Hybopsis* species (chubs), *Pimephales* species, and introduced [[Carp]] *Cyprinus carpio* and [[Goldfish]] *Carassius auratus*.

**Catostomidae** (suckers): 14 species including *Catostomus*, *Carpiodes*, *Ictiobus* (buffalos), and *Moxostoma* (redhorses).

**Ictaluridae** (catfishes): 11 species including [[Channel Catfish]] *Ictalurus punctatus*, [[Blue Catfish]] *Ictalurus furcatus*, [[Flathead Catfish]] *Pilodictis olivaris*, bullheads (*Ameiurus*), and madtoms (*Schilbeodes*, *Noturus*).

**Centrarchidae** (sunfishes and basses): 11 species including [[Largemouth Bass]] *Micropterus salmoides*, [[Smallmouth Bass]] *Micropterus dolomieui*, [[Spotted Bass]] *Micropterus punctulatus*, [[Bluegill]] *Lepomis macrochirus*, [[Green Sunfish]] *Lepomis cyanellus*, and crappies (*Pomoxis*).

**Percidae** (perches and darters): 19 species with extensive darter diversity (*Etheostoma*, *Hadropterus*, *Percina*, *Ammocrypta*), plus [[Yellow Perch]] *Perca flavescens*, [[Walleye]] *Stizostedion vitreum*, and [[Sauger]] *Stizostedion canadense*.

Subspecific designations frequently distinguish geographic variants (e.g., "northern" vs. "southern," "plains" vs. "central," "Missouri River" vs. "Arkansas River" forms), reflecting biogeographic patterns across Kansas watersheds.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Kansas fish fauna reflects the state's position at the confluence of multiple biogeographic regions and major river systems. Geographic subspecies designations indicate:

**Missouri River drainage** influences in northern Kansas (e.g., [[Missouri River Speckled Chub]] *Hybopsis aestivalis* subsp., [[Plains Flathead Chub]] *Hybopsis gracilis communis*).

**Arkansas River drainage** influences in southern Kansas (e.g., [[Arkansas River Speckled Chub]] *Hybopsis aestivalis tetranemus*, [[Arkansas River Shiner]] *Notropis girardi*, [[Arkansas River Darter]] *Etheostoma cragini*, [[Arkansas Striped Shiner]] *Notropis zonata pilsbryi*).

**Neosho River** endemism (e.g., [[Neosho River Madtom]] *Schilbeodes* species, [[Neosho Smallmouth Bass]] *Micropterus dolomieui velox*, [[Neosho Mountain Minnow]] *Pimephales tenellus tenellus*).

Plains-adapted species are prominent, with numerous "plains" subspecies (e.g., [[Plains Stoneroller]] *Campostoma anomalum plumbeum*, [[Plains Minnow]] *Hybognathus placita*, [[Plains Killifish]] *Fundulus kansae*, [[Plains Topminnow]] *Fundulus sciadicus*).

The presence of [[American Eel]] *Anguilla rostrata* and [[Skipjack]] *Alosa chrysochloris* indicates historical connectivity to the Mississippi River system. The [[Burbot]] *Lota lota* represents a glacial relict.

## Key Findings

1. **Species Richness**: Kansas supports 122 documented fish species across 22 families, representing substantial freshwater biodiversity for a Great Plains state.

2. **Cyprinid Dominance**: The [[Cyprinidae]] is the most diverse family with 48 species (39% of total fauna), typical of North American freshwater systems.

3. **Subspecific Diversity**: Extensive subspecific variation documented, with many species divided into 2-3 geographic races reflecting drainage-specific evolution.

4. **Major Drainage Systems**: Three primary biogeographic influences evident: [[Missouri River]] (northern), [[Arkansas River]] (southern/southwestern), and [[Neosho River]] (southeastern).

5. **Endemic Forms**: Several taxa appear endemic or near-endemic to Kansas waters, including [[Neosho River Madtom]], [[Topeka Shiner]] *Notropis topeka*, and various drainage-specific subspecies.

6. **Conservation Concerns**: Multiple species listed are now recognized as imperiled, including [[Pallid Sturgeon]], [[Sicklefin Chub]] *Hybopsis meeki*, [[Arkansas River Shiner]], and [[Arkansas River Darter]].

7. **Introduced Species**: Two non-native cyprinids documented: [[Carp]] and [[Goldfish]].

8. **Darter Diversity**: Exceptional darter (Percidae) diversity with 19 species, reflecting habitat heterogeneity in Kansas streams.

9. **Game Fish Assemblage**: Complete suite of North American centrarchid game fishes present, including three *Micropterus* bass species, multiple *Lepomis* sunfishes, and both crappie species.

10. **Taxonomic Coverage**: Fauna spans from primitive jawless fishes ([[Chestnut Lamprey]] *Ichthyomyzon castaneus*) through ancient lineages (sturgeons, paddlefish, gars, bowfin) to advanced teleosts, representing ~400 million years of fish evolution.
