## Executive Summary
This 1970 paper by G. E. Gates (Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington) redescribes **[[Pheretima Mazarredoi]]**, a large, strikingly blue-green earthworm species from **[[Marinduque Island]]**, Philippines, known previously only from an inadequate 1894 Italian description of the holotype (Rosa, 1894). New material collected by a Peace Corps Volunteer (Halford Jones) allowed confirmation and expansion of morphological and internal anatomical characters. The paper also describes a related but distinct **[[Pheretima Sp. (Cave Form)]]** found in a nearby cave, differing in coloration, spermathecal structure, and intestinal origin, raising the possibility of cave-driven speciation. The work contributes to systematics within the genus **[[Pheretima Kinberg 1866]]** and subgenus **[[Archipheretima]]**, discussing which characters are primitive versus derived within the group.

## Background & Description

### *Pheretima mazarredoi*
- **Size:** 250–325 mm long × 15 mm wide (aclitellate adults); juveniles 60 × 5 mm.
- **Segment counts:** 114, 116, 120 (×2), 122, 124, 131.
- **Prostomium:** Epilobous, tongue closed.
- **Color:** Very dark, almost midnight blue with greenish tinge; pigment located in connective tissue between epidermis and circular muscle layer.
- **Setae:** Small, numerous, deeply retracted.
- **First dorsal pore:** At intersegmental furrow 12/13.
- **Spermathecal pores:** Octothecal, at 5/6–8/9.
- **Male pores:** Minute, superficial, equatorial, widely separated, in segment XVIII.
- **Genital markings:** Paired whitish transverse depressions near male porophores (at 17/18–18/19, or 18/19–20/21).
- **Internal anatomy:**
  - Septa complete from 5/6 posteriorly.
  - Gizzard in segment VIII (~6 mm long, 7 mm thick).
  - Esophagus widened in X–XIII, valvular in XIV–XVI.
  - Intestine deeply sacculated anteriorly, **acaecal and atyphlosolate** (no caeca or typhlosole).
  - Holandric reproductive system; no testis sacs; polyplicate male funnels.
  - Two pairs of seminal vesicles (segments XI, XII), spongelike texture.
  - Compact prostates confined to segment XVIII.
  - Spermathecae with small heart-shaped to spheroidal diverticula.
  - No genital marking (GM) glands observed.
  - Hearts: lateral in VI–IX, lateroesophageal in XXIII.
  - Subneural vascular trunk not detected.

### *Pheretima sp.* (cave form)
- **Size:** 235–385 mm × 20 mm.
- **Segment counts:** 120, 126.
- **Body shape:** Shortly, transversely elliptical in cross-section.
- **Color:** Uniform slate-gray (dorsum and ventrum).
- **Spermathecal pores:** Small transverse slits, 6–7 mm apart.
- **Male pores:** Small transverse slits in XVIII, each within a translucent elliptical epidermal patch.
- **Genital markings:** Single preclitellar marking obliterating 9/10; paired postclitellar markings near male pore level.
- **Internal anatomy:**
  - Esophagus with lamelliform ridges (X–XIII); valve near 14/15.
  - Intestinal origin at segment XV (vs. XVII in *P. mazarredoi*).
  - Seminal chambers in two vertical rows on spermathecal duct.
  - Diagonal ridges on intestinal inner wall spanning multiple segments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Terrestrial soil-dwelling species; *P. mazarredoi* found on the ground surface near caves; *Pheretima sp.* found within a cave environment on **[[Marinduque Island]]**.
- **Diet/Ingesta (cave form):** Mostly organic matter, including a fragment of wood (~18 mm) and fragments suggesting incidental ingestion of small arachnids (tick-like).
- **Parasites/Associated organisms:** Brown bodies in the coelom contained nematode eggs, pseudonavicellid spores, possible gregarine-like bodies, and other unidentified microorganisms.
- **Cultural ecology:** Local Filipino residents reportedly mistake these large, blue-colored worms for snakes; coelomic fluid (as in the related **[[Pheretima Ophioides]]**) is described by native collectors as corrosive, particularly hazardous to eyes.
- **Potential speciation driver:** Isolation of the cave population from surface populations is hypothesized as a mechanism that could support distinct speciation of the cave-dwelling *Pheretima sp.*

## Key Findings
- Confirms and expands the original 1894 description of **[[Pheretima Mazarredoi]]**, previously known only from a single holotype.
- Documents **absence of testis sacs**, a condition rarely recorded outside of male-sterile morphs or juveniles in amphimictic *Pheretima* species — of uncertain systematic significance, since specimens were immature.
- Establishes several characters (absence of intestinal caeca/typhlosole, complete septa 8/9–9/10, paired lateral hearts in VIII/IX) as likely **primitive traits** within *Pheretima*.
- Argues that presence of hearts in segment XIII, once considered primitive, is more likely an **advanced (derived) character**.
- Critiques the subgenus **[[Archipheretima]]** (Michaelsen, 1928, 1934) as inadequately diagnosable; notes that its defining trait (absence of intestinal caeca) is shared broadly across other *Pheretima* subgenera.
- Proposes close relationship between *P. mazarredoi* and **[[Pheretima Iris]]**, **[[Pheretima Margaritacea]]** (Samar Island), and **[[Pheretima Ophioides]]** (Luzon), all sharing blue/blue-green coloration.
- Highlights microanatomical study of parietal (body wall) musculature by W. J. Harman, confirming typical *Pheretima*-type circular and longitudinal muscle fiber organization despite some quantitative differences.
- Suggests the newly described cave-dwelling *Pheretima sp.* may represent a distinct taxon from *P. mazarredoi* based on differences in coloration, spermathecal pore spacing, and intestinal origin (segment XV vs. XVII), potentially resulting from geographic/cave isolation.
- Notes that alcohol preservative fluid became discolored (bright yellow for *P. mazarredoi*, dark greenish for cave specimens), likely due to leaching of body pigment onto tissues (epidermis, brown bodies, prostatic/spermathecal ducts) post-preservation — a taphonomic artifact relevant to interpreting coloration in preserved oligochaete specimens.
