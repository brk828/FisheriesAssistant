# Review of Freshwater Fishes of Canada (Scott & Crossman, 1973)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #taxonomy #Ictaluridae #Cyprinidae #Acipenseridae #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This note synthesizes a 1975 peer review by Reeve M. Bailey (University of Michigan) of the landmark reference work *[[Freshwater Fishes of Canada]]* by W. B. Scott and E. J. Crossman (Bulletin 184, Fisheries Research Board of Canada, 1973). The book is described as the most significant North American freshwater fish compendium since Jordan and Evermann, covering **181 species in 24 families**. Although titled "of Canada," its scope extends into the northern United States, capturing majorities of species found in states such as New Hampshire (94%), Michigan (83%), Montana (80%), Wisconsin (78%), Wyoming (72%), and Ohio (65%). The work is part of a broader Fisheries Research Board of Canada series alongside *[[Pacific Fishes of Canada]]* (Hart), *[[Fishes of the Atlantic Coast of Canada]]* (Leim & Scott), and *[[Freshwater Fishes of Northwestern Canada and Alaska]]* (McPhail & Lindsey), with an Arctic Ocean fishes volume still pending at time of review.

## Background & Description
The review highlights the book's structural approach to morphological documentation rather than presenting novel primary data:

- **Format of species accounts**: description, color, systematic notes, distribution, biology, relation to man, nomenclature, and family-level suggested reading; most accounts include a drawing and distribution map.
- **Body proportions** are expressed as percentages of **total length** (rather than the more customary standard length), a notable methodological departure.
- **Illustrations**: 15 species are illustrated in color; most others receive line drawings.
- **Identified morphological errors** in the original text (flagged by the reviewer):
  - *[[Pseudobranch]]* mislabeled as a "hemibranch" (p. 21).
  - *[[White Sturgeon]]* (Acipenseridae) erroneously cited as reaching 20 feet in length (p. 78, 96) — no freshwater fish attains this size.
  - *[[Black Bullhead]]* (Ictaluridae): contradictory size data — described as "usually only 5–7 inches" (true only for stunted populations) yet cited elsewhere as reaching 12 lb 2 oz (likely a transcription error for 2 lb 12 oz); adipose fin length and anal fin base measurements given as 12.2–20.4% of total length.
  - *[[Brown Bullhead]]* (p. 599): principal caudal ray counts given as 23–22, inconsistent with the characteristic **17 rays (15 branched)** typical of larger ictalurids; upper and lower gill raker counts were transposed.
  - Family-level generalization errors: Ictaluridae erroneously described with palatine teeth (never present), incorrect genus/species counts (stated as 5 genera/25 species vs. actual ~6 genera/~44 species), and incorrect fossil record dating (Miocene cited vs. actual Eocene origin).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While the reviewed work is primarily taxonomic, it incorporates ecological and biogeographic dimensions:

- **Geographic scope**: Comprehensive coverage of Canadian drainage systems plus adjacent U.S. states, with distribution maps showing shaded ranges for most areas and spot records specifically for Alaska.
- **World distribution maps** accompany each family but were criticized for inaccuracies outside Canada:
  - Lampreys (*[[Petromyzontidae]]*) shown missing from their known southern range into Mexico.
  - Suckers (*[[Catostomidae]]*) and ictalurid catfishes omitted from their range extending into Guatemala.
  - Gars (*[[Lepisosteidae]]*) omitted from ranges extending to Costa Rica and Cuba.
- **Native vs. introduced range conflation**: The review specifically criticizes the failure to distinguish natural from introduced distributions — e.g., *[[Centrarchidae]]* (sunfishes) world map shows only three isolated non-native populations in western North America while omitting extensive introductions across the western U.S., Middle America, and other continents.
- **Omitted taxa**: *[[Campostoma Anomalum]]* and *[[Notropis Photogenis]]* (both recently discovered in southern Ontario) relegated to footnotes; *[[Salvelinus Anaktuvukensis]]* (a newly described Alaskan char) and an introduced madtom, *[[Noturus Insignis]]*, were discovered too late for inclusion; the *[[Paddlefish]]* (*Polyodon spathula*) was excluded despite historically authentic Great Lakes records, as it is no longer extant in Canadian waters.

## Key Findings
- **Scope**: 181 species across 24 families, described as functionally broader than "Canada" implies due to extensive overlap with U.S. state faunas.
- **Taxonomic conservatism**: The authors retain traditional generic distinctions (*[[Platygobio]]*, *[[Chrosomus]]*, *[[Clinostomus]]*, *[[Richardsonius]]* within Cyprinidae; monotypic *[[Entosphenus]]* for the Pacific lamprey) rather than adopting more consolidated modern classifications, which the reviewer argues obscures true genetic relationships.
- **Unresolved systematic problems**: The book avoids addressing two significant species-complex controversies in Canadian ichthyology — the *[[Notropis Cornutus]]* complex and the *[[Etheostoma Nigrum]]* complex — each argued by the reviewer to comprise two distinct, well-marked species rather than single taxa.
- **Nomenclature**: Largely follows the 1970 American Fisheries Society names list, with noted exceptions in generic-level conservatism.
- **Overall assessment**: Despite noted taxonomic and factual errors (mostly at the family and species-account level), the work is deemed a "monumental" and near-indispensable reference, with the reviewer recommending corrections be made prior to a planned French-language edition.
- **Structural apparatus**: Includes prefatory geographic/drainage listings, anatomical sketches, a family key, a glossary, a 52-page reference section, and a 45-page double-column index.
