# Maricopa Audubon Society Letter Regarding Endangered Species Legislation

#wildlife-policy #conservation-advocacy #endangered-species-act

## Executive Summary

This correspondence from the [[Maricopa Audubon Society]] to Representative John Shadegg, dated October 31, 1995, addresses concerns regarding proposed federal legislation that would weaken protections for imperiled wildlife. The letter specifically critiques H.R. 2364 (the John Shadegg Endangered Species Recovery Act) and the Young/Pombo bill, arguing that both represent anti-environmental agendas contrary to constituent interests. The organization poses 18 detailed questions challenging various provisions of the proposed legislation and requests reconsideration of support for these bills.

## Background & Description

The Maricopa Audubon Society, dedicated to the protection of natural wetlands in an arid environment, had previously written to Representative Shadegg on September 27, 1995, requesting withdrawal of co-sponsorship from the Young/Pombo bill. After nearly one month without response, the organization wrote this follow-up letter addressing both the original bill and newly introduced H.R. 2364.

The letter identifies the organization's unwavering position that the Young/Pombo bill "dramatically weakens protection for imperiled species...insures that protection of necessary habitat will be nearly impossible...[and]...guarantees that species extinction will become increasingly more likely."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The correspondence emphasizes several critical ecological concerns:

- **[[Habitat Protection]]**: The letter challenges provisions that would disassociate habitat destruction from direct injury as a means of harming wildlife, noting that no wildlife can survive without habitat
- **[[San Pedro River]]**: Identified as "the last living river in the Southwest" and belonging to all Americans including future generations; currently threatened by water use rights
- **[[Arctic National Wildlife Refuge]]**: Described as "the last unimpeded mammalian [migration route] left on Earth"
- **State vs. Federal Protection**: Questions Arizona's capacity for wildlife protection, noting that "Arizona State lands in particular are among the areas most abused in the State"
- **Cumulative Habitat Loss**: Identifies piecemeal destruction of habitat as "the most common reason for the imperiling of wildlife"

## Key Findings

The letter raises 18 specific concerns about H.R. 2364:

1. Requests concrete examples of regulatory violations of private property rights
2. Questions the basis for assigning primary conservation responsibility to states with poor wildlife protection records
3. Challenges provisions separating habitat destruction from wildlife harm
4. Objects to allowing lessees of public lands to control public access based on "possessory interest"
5. Questions inclusion of "right to use or receive water" in definition of property, citing [[San Pedro River]] destruction
6. Concerns that "property based on custom, usage" could allow ranching lessees to claim ownership of public lands
7. Notes complete omission of "threatened" species from the bill, eliminating preventative conservation
8. Identifies lack of limitations on sensitive species information to protect from collectors or harm
9. Questions absence of limitations for small federal projects despite cumulative impacts
10. Challenges restriction of habitat protection updates to one window per 10-year period
11. Objects to limiting imperiled wildlife protection to [[National Parks]] or [[National Wildlife Refuges]] alone
12. Notes penalties would be reduced to "cost of doing business" levels
13. Questions limiting protection of imperiled predators, noting most predation problems relate to poor husbandry
14. Challenges protection of interstate Compacts from oversight even when resulting in wildlife loss
15. Objects to provisions affecting [[Arctic National Wildlife Refuge]] for "a few years of oil production"
16. Questions attempts to financially weaken environmental groups protecting public lands
17. Challenges limitations on production of "jeopardy" opinions
18. Objects to elimination of [[Critical Habitat]] designation, which allows evaluation and monitoring of habitat use and loss

The letter concludes by requesting reconsideration of support for anti-environmental legislation and response to correspondence, noting the legislator's environmental voting record is "so contrary to the urban constituency he represents."
