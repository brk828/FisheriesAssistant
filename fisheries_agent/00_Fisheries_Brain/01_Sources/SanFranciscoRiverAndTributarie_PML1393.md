# San Francisco River and Tributaries Fisheries Survey, Greenlee County, Arizona (1996)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A series of three field surveys in summer 1996 documented the fish assemblage of the [[San Francisco River]], the [[Blue River]], and their northern tributaries in [[Greenlee County]], Arizona. The work was carried out by [[Arizona State University]] researchers with staff from the [[United States Forest Service]]. The first survey (June 20–23) recorded eleven fish species, but most were scarce, and abundance rose steadily downstream toward [[Martinez Ranch]]. The second survey (July 1–3) used pool sampling on the Blue and San Francisco rivers, found five species, and recorded no [[Razorback Sucker]], although the target of the effort was that reintroduced fish. The third survey (August 22–24) covered northern tributaries and found no fish at any site visited.

## Background & Description

The surveys were conducted by a small team that included [[Brian Bagley]], [[Gary Schiffmiller]], [[Bob Csargo]], [[Terry Meyers]], and [[Patti Sowka]]. Methods varied by survey:

- **June 20–23, 1996 (main survey):** Sampling used a gas-powered [[Smith-Root Backpack Electrofisher]], dip nets, and [[Trammel Net]]s across eight localities on the San Francisco River and its tributaries.
- **July 1–3, 1996 (pool sampling):** Most pools deeper than 1.5 m were sampled with a 20 m × 2 m trammel net with 4 cm inner mesh. The aim was to target deep pool habitats that had not been well sampled in 1995 and to look for reintroduced razorback suckers. Sites were numbered upstream to downstream, starting at the Juan Miller road crossing on the Blue River. In total, 24 pools were seined on the Blue River and 13 on the San Francisco River.
- **August 22–24, 1996 (northern tributaries):** Habitats were examined by visual observation. A backpack electroshocker was on hand but was not used because no fish were seen.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The San Francisco River near the New Mexico border was mostly shallow water over a shifting sand bottom. Several tributaries (Citizen, Coalson, Copperas, and Indian canyons) were intermittent. Water was often present only in short reaches or pools, and some reaches had waterfalls that blocked fish movement. The field notes record a 15 ft waterfall on Indian Creek and an 8 ft waterfall on Coalson Canyon, as well as a 13 ft wall on Copperas Canyon.

Incidental observations included a leopard frog and a narrow-headed garter snake (Pool #3, Blue River), abundant leopard frog tadpoles (Pool #11), and a possible juvenile [[Smallmouth Bass]] sighted but not captured on the San Francisco River. Two ducks were caught in a trammel net at Pool #18, which ended that effort.

## Key Findings

### June 20–23, 1996: San Francisco River and Tributaries

- **Species richness:** Eleven species were collected: carp (*Cyprinus carpio*), [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*), [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*), [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*), [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*), [[Desert Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*), [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*), [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*), and [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*).
- **Abundance:** All eleven species were present, but none was plentiful. Catch totals rose downstream.
- **Tributary sites:** Coalson Canyon, Copperas Canyon, and Indian Creek were fishless on visual survey. At Citizen Canyon, speckled dace were the only fish taken: 49 (21 preserved) at the site 1/4 mile upstream of the river, after 65 seconds of shocking, and 31 (20 preserved) at the site 1.5 miles upstream, after 32 seconds.
- **San Francisco River sites (catch totals as reported):**
  - *At Citizen Canyon confluence* (842 s shocking plus dip netting): total 90, including longfin dace 46 and mosquitofish 23.
  - *At Coalson Canyon* (988 s): total 84 in Table 1, including longfin dace 48 and channel catfish 13.
  - *100 m downstream of Indian Cave Spring Canyon* (trammel net set for 5 h): channel catfish 4 and Sonora sucker 2, with carp also present.
  - *2.5 miles downstream of Indian Creek* (1907 s): total 152, including longfin dace 54, red shiner 27, fathead minnow 11, flathead catfish 10, and mosquitofish 20.
  - *Near Martinez Ranch* (1452 s, upstream 1/4 mile to the diversion dam base): total 215, including Sonora sucker 66, longfin dace 44, red shiner 34, desert sucker 24, and speckled dace 24.
- **Flathead catfish diet:** One flathead catfish at the Citizen Canyon confluence had a 150 mm sucker in its stomach. Another at Coalson Canyon had consumed a longfin dace.

### July 1–3, 1996: Blue and San Francisco Rivers Pool Sampling

- **Sites:** 37 pools were sampled, 24 on the Blue River and 13 on the San Francisco River.
- **Species:** Five species were collected: [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*), carp, Sonora sucker, desert sucker, and channel catfish.
- **Razorback sucker:** None were found. The survey noted that a few individuals might still exist.
- **Rainbow trout:** Two were caught at Pool #6 on the Blue River, about 2 miles downstream of the Juan Miller road. The team was surprised to find trout this far downstream, since they are common upstream.
- **Channel catfish in the Blue River:** Two were caught at Pool #17, about 2 miles above the confluence. Channel catfish are plentiful in the San Francisco River but were not expected this far up the Blue.
- **Notable pools:** Pool #27 on the San Francisco River (30 m × 15 m × 1 m) held 8 Sonora suckers. Pool #29 (20 m × 20 m × 1.5 m) held 20 Sonora suckers and 4 channel catfish. Pool #9 on the Blue River held 3 Sonora suckers.

### August 22–24, 1996: Northern Tributaries

- **Coalson Canyon:** Water was present at Coalson Spring and intermittently for 250 m upstream. No fish were observed.
- **Copperas Canyon:** Continuous water covered 50 m near a cement dam and cattle drinker. Water was intermittent for the next 1/4 mile. No fish were observed.
- **Upper Oak Spring:** The only water was at a pipe-fed cattle trough, with mud puddles from overflow. No fish were present.
- **Indian Creek:** Not reached because of mechanical problems with the ATVs. The team had visited the lower confluence with the San Francisco River in June 1996, where water was present but no fish were found.

### Conclusions

- The San Francisco River supports a diverse but sparse assemblage of native and non-native fish, with greater abundance downstream.
- Intermittent tributaries and waterfalls limit fish distribution to the main river and its lower reaches.
- The absence of razorback sucker in deep pool sampling means that the reintroduced population could not be confirmed in 1996.
