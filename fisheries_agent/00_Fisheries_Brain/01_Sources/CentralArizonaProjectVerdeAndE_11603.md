# Central Arizona Project Verde and East Verde River Diversions: Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act Draft Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This draft [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]] report, prepared by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] in July 1989 and cooperating with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], assesses how proposed [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water exchanges and direct diversions by [[Bureau of Reclamation]] on the [[Verde River]] and [[East Verde River]] would affect fish and wildlife. Eight CAP allottees hold a combined 17,343 acre-feet per year (A-F/yr) for agricultural, municipal and industrial use. The report addresses only effects of water removal on aquatic and riparian habitat, not pipelines, pumping plants or water rights.

The Service finds that the diversions would cause significant adverse impacts, chiefly through reduced low flows that remove habitat for native fishes, including the threatened [[Spikedace]]. It recommends that no withdrawals be made from Verde River Reaches 1–7, that East Verde minimum flows and diversion caps be enforced, that long-term monitoring be established, and that an Environmental Impact Statement be prepared before diversions are authorized.

## Background & Description

**Study area.** The Verde and East Verde Rivers flow south from the Mogollon Rim region of central Arizona. Perennial flow in the Verde begins below Chino Valley and runs about 125 miles undammed to Horseshoe Dam. The East Verde has about 40 perennial miles and receives intermittent trans-basin water imports. The Verde and East Verde together support 165 miles of high-quality aquatic and riparian resources, including extensive mature cottonwood stands. The study area contains a State Park, a National Monument and a Wild and Scenic River segment. [[Dead Horse Ranch State Park]] holds 325 acres, with an additional 300 acres of riparian greenbelt.

**Project.** The CAP was authorized in 1968 under the Colorado River Basin Project Act (Public Law 90-537). In March 1983, the Secretary of the Interior allocated CAP water to 120 users. Eight of them plan to exchange allocations and divert from the rivers. The total is 17,343 A-F/yr, equivalent to 23.9 cfs at constant rate, over a 100-year project life. Only three allottees (City of Prescott with Yavapai-Prescott Indian Community, Town of Payson, and the Tonto Apache community in the East Verde context) submitted detailed plans. The rest were modeled by Reclamation as conceptual plans.

**Evaluation method.** Impacts were assessed with the [[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology]] (IFIM; Bovee 1982), using the combined Water Surface Profile and IFG-4 hydraulic models and the HABTAT habitat program. Ten study reaches were defined: seven on the Verde and three on the East Verde. An interagency team (Reclamation, AGFD, Coconino and Tonto National Forests, and the Service) conducted the study. Nine fish species were chosen as evaluation species (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Native fish community.** The upper Verde is rare in Arizona for retaining a strong native fish community. Six native species remain there: [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Spikedace]]. The report notes these represent only 66 percent of the river's original native species. Introduced species include [[Red Shiner]], mosquitofish, yellow bullhead, [[Carp]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], green sunfish, flathead catfish and threadfin shad. Native dominance declines below Perkinsville and non-native fish dominate by Tapco. Spikedace are essentially absent below Sycamore Creek. [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Colorado Squawfish]] have been reintroduced, the squawfish as an experimental nonessential population.

**Riparian habitat.** Riparian vegetation ranges from seep willow and velvet ash in the headwaters to [[Fremont Cottonwood]] and Arizona sycamore downstream. The report cites an estimated 95 percent loss of Arizona's historic riparian habitat. Present riparian cover is 101,793 acres, or 0.15 percent of the state's acreage.

**Listed and sensitive species.** Three federally listed species occur: the endangered [[Bald Eagle]] and peregrine falcon, and the threatened spikedace. Spikedace critical habitat has been proposed. Nine other state-listed species are present, including roundtail chub and razorback sucker. The southwestern river otter is not confirmed in the upper Verde; a non-native Louisiana otter subspecies was introduced in 1983–85 and now ranges from Perkinsville to Horseshoe Dam.

**Human pressures.** Cattle grazing, abandoned mines, off-road vehicle use and residential growth affect the basin. Prescott's population was 20,055 in 1980, projected at 23,720 by 1988. The Verde Valley population was 13,735 in 1988, projected at 38,195 by 2030. The East Verde's Payson population was 7,745 in 1988. The East Verde also receives water imported from East Clear Creek under a Phelps-Dodge and Salt River Project exchange, which makes flows fluctuate widely.

## Key Findings

**1. Proposed diversions and flow loss**
- Headwater flow reductions: about one-half of normal flow in the Verde headwaters (Reaches 1–2) and about two-thirds in the East Verde.
- Diversions would remove about 4.4 percent of the average annual flow of the Verde at the Tangle Creek gage.
- Reduction of median monthly flow ranges from 0.8 to 42.1 percent, depending on the diversion point.
- Flood flows (2-, 5-, 10- and 100-year peaks) are essentially unaffected, at 0.01–0.46 percent removed.

**2. Reach-level impacts**
- **Reaches 1–3 (upper Verde):** The Prescott/Yavapai-Prescott diversion would remove 42–63 percent of median monthly flow. Roundtail chub habitat is essentially eliminated below about 10 cfs. Withdrawals reduce habitat for suckers and spikedace while sharply increasing red shiner habitat.
- **Reaches 4–5 (Verde Valley):** Existing irrigation diversions already dewater the river. Proposed withdrawals would intensify this. If maximum rates occurred together, mean monthly median flow would fall by about half and the historic low at Clarkdale by about 70 percent.
- **Reaches 6–7:** Little change is expected, but habitat for adult fish in Reach 6 declines steadily as flow drops.
- **Reaches 8–10 (East Verde):** Diversions could cause extended total dewatering when trans-basin imports stop. The report warns this could devastate the put-and-take trout fishery.

**3. Riparian vegetation**
- Brock (1987) concluded that "Only complete water depletion of the stream system would result in major impacts" to riparian vegetation. Later clarification noted that lateral displacement of regeneration and reduced seedling survival in years 1–5 are likely.
- Long-term narrowing of the riparian zone and widening of the channel are expected, especially in Reaches 4–5.
- Black hawks depend on riparian-associated prey; fish, amphibians and garter snakes can make up more than 78 percent of their diet.

**4. Mitigation categories**
- Reaches 1–5 and 6–7 are Resource Category 1 (no loss of existing habitat value). Reaches 8–10 are Category 1 under AGFD guidelines.
- The Service considers mitigation in-kind infeasible for the Verde River project area.
- Carothers and Johnson (1972) found 56.8 percent of Verde Valley cottonwood-nesting birds depend exclusively on riparian habitat.

**5. IFIM and preference curves**
- Nine evaluation species: Rainbow Trout, Roundtail Chub, Desert Sucker, Sonora Sucker, Carp, Channel Catfish, Spikedace, Smallmouth Bass and Red Shiner.
- The larval survey comprised 185 collections yielding 2,646 larval fish of 8 species, including 5 target species.
- The Service judged adults to be the limiting life stage. Spawning habitat results were not considered limiting after field observation.

**6. Recommendations**
1. Prepare an Environmental Impact Statement addressing Reclamation's selected alternative.
2. Make no water withdrawals from Verde River Reaches 1–7.
3. Maintain the Table 9 minimum flows in East Verde reaches; when natural flow falls below these values during non-transbasin periods, provide the natural flow.
4. Cap diversion rates on the East Verde at 1 cfs each for the Tonto Apache Reservation and E&R Water Company, and 4 cfs for the Town of Payson.
5. Establish long-term riparian and aquatic monitoring on the East Verde, beginning before diversions or construction start.
6. Re-analyze any changes to proposed diversions, or any added diversions or impoundments.

**7. Conclusions**
- The Service does not believe the Verde diversions should proceed without adequate study of CAP alternatives.
- If plans change, the Service needs an opportunity to supplement the report.
- Simultaneous withdrawals by all allottees would leave insufficient flow in some reaches. The state adjudication process and the Arizona Department of Water Resources must resolve this before withdrawals are permitted.

**8. Other points**
- The Verde Wild and Scenic River proposal would cover about 39.5 miles, with 17.5 miles classed as "Wild."
- Payson's diversion would be 1.7 cfs (1,231 A-F) until 2010, then 3.6 cfs (2,606 A-F). The 1989 table states Payson's maximum planned use is 2,606 A-F.
- Recreation is significant: 52,000 angler days were recorded on the East Verde in 1986. Floating between Perkinsville and Cottonwood is among Arizona's best.
