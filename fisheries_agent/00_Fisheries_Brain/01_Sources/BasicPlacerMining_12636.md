# Wildlife Views Magazine - Gold Prospecting and Environmental Notes

#non-research #outdoor-recreation #environmental-technology

## Executive Summary

This document is a magazine article from "Wildlife Views" focusing primarily on recreational gold prospecting and placer mining techniques in Arizona, with additional notes on desert agriculture technology and air pollution legislation. The main article provides historical context about Arizona's mining heritage and practical guidance for amateur prospectors, including equipment, methods, and locations. A secondary section covers environmental innovations including desalination-powered desert agriculture and legislative efforts to reduce automotive air pollution in the 1970s.

## Background & Description

The article opens with a humorous "alphabet quiz" listing various government agencies and organizations using acronyms. The main feature article, authored by Bob Schimmel, discusses Arizona's mining history beginning with the California gold rush era. Historical placer deposits are described, including [[Rich Hill]] near Yarnell where $40,000 in gold nuggets were reportedly recovered from a single acre. Early mining towns mentioned include Stanton, Weaver Creek, Lynx Creek, and Big Bug.

The article transitions to practical prospecting guidance, describing equipment (16-18 inch gold pans, shovels, picks, magnifying glass) and various extraction methods including panning, use of the batea (cone-shaped device), dry concentration through winnowing, and amalgamation with mercury. Detailed instructions are provided for panning technique, bedrock crevice sampling, and concentrate processing.

The "Outdoor Notes" section describes research at the [[University of Arizona Environmental Research Laboratory]] led by Carl N. Hodges, developing integrated systems using waste heat from generators to desalt seawater for greenhouse agriculture. A pilot facility operated at [[Puerto Penasco]] on the [[Gulf of California]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The article addresses land ownership and prospecting regulations, noting that permission is required on private property but not on "unreserved, vacant, public land of the United States." County assessor plat maps are recommended for determining land ownership, though claimed lands may not be surveyed until patent.

The environmental technology section describes efforts to convert desert coastlines into productive agricultural areas through controlled-environment plastic greenhouses using desalinated seawater. This represents early work in sustainable desert agriculture systems.

The air pollution discussion addresses automotive emissions exceeding 100 million tons annually. Legislative efforts included Congressman Farbstein's defeated amendment to the [[Clean Air Act]] that would have prohibited internal combustion engine manufacture after January 1, 1977, and California Senate Bill 26-5 to ban gasoline and diesel engine sales beginning January 1, 1975.

## Key Findings

- Historical placer deposits in Arizona still contain recoverable gold, though easily accessible deposits have been largely exhausted
- Modern recreational prospectors can recover "several dollars a day" through careful panning and crevice sampling in formerly productive areas
- Thunderstorms wash new gold into streams, making return visits to historical sites potentially productive
- California scuba divers continue to recover corn kernel-sized nuggets from deep pools near main roads
- Gold panning technique involves filling pan three-quarters full, submerging in water, removing large gravel by hand, breaking up clay, and using circular motion to wash away lighter materials
- Concentrates accumulate heavier materials (gold, black sand, high specific gravity substances) requiring further processing
- Dry concentration methods using winnowing are less efficient than wet methods and limited by moisture content in most "dry placers"
- Stream placers historically most productive, with greatest gold concentrations near or in bedrock crevices
- Amalgamation with mercury forms alloy for gold recovery but requires complicated home processing
- [[University of Arizona]] pilot facility at [[Puerto Penasco]] successfully demonstrated integrated power-water-food production system for desert coasts
- Automotive air pollution legislation in 1970s included proposals to ban internal combustion engines by mid-1970s, though these measures were defeated
