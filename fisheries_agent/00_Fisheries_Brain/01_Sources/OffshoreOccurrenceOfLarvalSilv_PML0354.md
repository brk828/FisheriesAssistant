# Offshore Occurrence of Larval Silver Mullet (Mugil curema) in the Western Gulf of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #mugil

## Executive Summary

This short research note reports the first known series of offshore-caught larval [[Silver Mullet]] (*Mugil curema*) from the western [[Gulf of Mexico]]. Fourteen specimens, 7.2 to 9.8 mm standard length, were dip-netted at the surface over deep water about 55 miles from land. The authors argue that these larvae, together with the small sizes of inshore Gulf specimens (23 to 24 mm), indicate that silver mullet spawn offshore in the Gulf as they do in the Atlantic. The paper also documents developmental changes in the fins, nostrils and preorbital bone at these early sizes.

## Background & Description

The note is set against a long debate over where American mullets (genus *Mugil*) spawn. Earlier work by Broadhead, Arnold and Thompson, and Anderson indicated that the [[Striped Mullet]] (*Mugil cephalus*) spawns offshore. Anderson had also shown that silver mullet spawn offshore in the Atlantic, but he had no records of this from the Gulf of Mexico. Hollister had earlier collected larval silver mullet of 6 to 12 mm over deep water, presumably off Bermuda.

Several regional studies of Gulf coast fishes reported that the smallest silver mullet they collected inshore were 23 to 24 mm standard length. These were Gunter (Texas, 1945), Kilby (middle Gulf coast of Florida, 1955), and Joseph and Yerger (northern Gulf coast of Florida, 1956). The authors treat this size gap as indirect evidence of offshore spawning, since larvae this small are absent from inshore catches.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collection took place at [[Station 1074]] aboard the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service M/V *Oregon*, on May 25, 1954, during a cruise in the [[Western Gulf of Mexico]].

The larvae were taken as a tiny school, about 4 inches in diameter, swimming at the surface near the drifting vessel. The surface conditions were warm and the larvae were concentrated near the surface in open oceanic water rather than in inshore nursery habitat.

## Key Findings

- **Offshore larval series:** Fourteen larval silver mullet, 7.2 to 9.8 mm standard length, were collected by [[Dip-Netting]] at Station 1074. Voucher specimens are held at the [[University of Florida]] Collections (UF 3913). The authors describe this as the first known series of offshore-caught larval silver mullet from the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Evidence for offshore spawning:** The smallest inshore Gulf specimens were 23 to 24 mm standard length. The offshore larvae are much smaller, which supports the authors' conclusion that silver mullet spawn offshore in the Gulf as well as the Atlantic.
- **Smallest Gulf records:** The authors state that these are the smallest silver mullet reported from the Gulf of Mexico.
- **Developmental stages:**
  - The 7.2 mm specimen has all fins and rays fully developed and a shallowly concave caudal fin, though the lobes remain well rounded.
  - Its single nostril slit has begun to pinch in midway along its length.
  - In the 9.8 mm specimen, the double nostril of the juvenile is fully formed.
  - The preorbital bone is just beginning to appear in the 9.8 mm specimen, without serrations.
- **Comparison with prior specimens:** The fins of these larvae are more like juvenile silver mullet than those of the 7.0 mm larva described by [[Anderson]]. In general characters they more closely resemble Anderson's 14.5 mm specimen. This comparison helps define the sizes at which key ontogenetic changes occur.
- **Conclusion:** Offshore spawning by silver mullet is supported for both the Gulf of Mexico and the Atlantic. The specimens also clarify the early life history of *Mugil curema*.
