# Systematics of the North American Subterranean Amphipod Genus *Apocrangonyx* (Gammaridae), with Remarks on Ecology and Zoogeography

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 systematic revision by [[John R. Holsinger]] (East Tennessee State University) treats the North American subterranean amphipod genus *Apocrangonyx*, long the least understood of the five subterranean amphipod genera in North America. Based on all available material and literature, the author recognizes six species: two previously described ([[Apocrangonyx Lucifugus]] and [[Apocrangonyx Subtilis]]) and four newly described (*A. nortoni*, *A. araeus*, *A. ephemerus*, and *A. parvus*). The genus is known exclusively from groundwater habitats (seeps, caves, and wells) from eastern Missouri to the Coastal Plain of southeastern Virginia. The paper is a taxonomic study, not a fish study; no fish species are treated as subjects of the document, though the author cites a cavefish as evidence of subfluvial dispersal.

## Background & Description

- **Genus history:** The first species, *A. lucifugus*, was described by O. P. Hay (1882) from a well in Knox Co., Illinois, under *Crangonyx*. [[Stebbing]] (1899) erected *Apocrangonyx* for it. [[Hubricht]] (1943) described *A. subtilis* from southwestern Illinois.
- **Diagnosis:** Eyeless and unpigmented, adults 2.50–7.00 mm long. Key diagnostic traits include loss or extreme reduction of the outer ramus of uropod 3, reduction or absence of the distal peduncular process of uropod 1 in males, and fused or nearly fused uronites.
- **Affinities:** The genus is closely allied with [[Stygonectes]] and [[Stygobromus]]. Overlap among the three genera is extensive, and the author notes that separating *Apocrangonyx* in practice is often difficult. The genus is retained provisionally pending revision of *Stygobromus*.
- **Species key:** Species are separated mainly by gnathopod 2 propod form, male palmar processes, telson spine number, uropod spination, and female body size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** All material came from groundwater seeps, springs, caves, and drip pools. Most localities lie in unconsolidated or loosely consolidated Coastal Plain sands and gravels, or in Mississippian and Ordovician limestone caves of the Appalachian and Interior regions.
- **Biogeography:** The range of *A. subtilis* (about 192 miles) crosses the Mississippi River. The author proposes subfluvial dispersal through continuous cavernous limestone beneath the river, supported by the distribution of the cavefish [[Typhlichthys Subterraneus]] and the isopod *Asellus stygius*. The Virginia species *A. araeus* lies near the brackish Chesapeake Bay and was probably derived from a brackish-water ancestor during the middle to late Cenozoic.
- **Phylogenetic interpretation:** *A. ephemerus* and *A. parvus* are judged to share a common ancestor, separated today by north–south mountain ranges. The author cannot rule out derivation of these two species from [[Stygonectes]].
- **Associated fauna:** At the type locality of *A. ephemerus*, an undescribed [[Stygobromus]] species co-occurs, along with the surface-stream amphipod *Gammarus minus*.

## Key Findings

- **Six species recognized:** Two previously known (*A. lucifugus*, *A. subtilis*) and four new (*A. nortoni*, *A. araeus*, *A. ephemerus*, *A. parvus*). An undescribed species from Blount Co., Alabama (three females) was noted but not named, pending a male.
- **Status of *A. lucifugus*:** Not rediscovered since 1882. Hay did not designate a type or deposit a type series. Hubricht searched near Knox Co. three times without success. The author suspects it may be conspecific with *A. subtilis* but does not synonymize it.
- ***A. subtilis* distribution:** About 192 miles, from Adams Co. to Union Co., Illinois, plus one cave in St. Genevieve Co., Missouri. Five Illinois localities (two caves, three seeps) and one Missouri cave are recorded.
- ***A. nortoni*:** Known only from Ingram Cave, Clay Co., Tennessee (holotype male, 3.85 mm; allotype female; 12 paratypes). It is the most morphologically isolated species and may be an aberrant *Stygobromus*.
- ***A. araeus*:** Known from Virginia Coastal Plain seeps (type series 123 specimens: 102 males and 21 females). Range is about 50 miles. In a February collection of 241 specimens from Norfolk Co., 24 ovigerous females (3.45–5.50 mm; mean 4.03 mm) had clutches of 6–18 eggs (mean 9.33, SD 3.06, CV 32%).
- ***A. ephemerus*:** Known from two caves about 2.5 miles apart in Sinking Creek Valley, Giles Co., Virginia (Tawneys and Canoe Caves). Specimens are rare; found on only one of three recent visits to Tawneys Cave.
- ***A. parvus*:** The smallest North American freshwater amphipod described to date (largest male 2.50 mm, largest female 2.70 mm). Known from Crawford Cave No. 2 (Randolph Co.) and Cassell-Windy Cave, West Virginia.
- **Reproduction:** Ovigerous females were found mainly in spring (April and February–March collections). The *A. subtilis* Jersey Co. seep sample had three ovigerous females, each carrying two large embryos.
- **Zoogeographic conclusions:** Dispersal in *A. subtilis* is inferred to be strong, reflecting vagility of populations on either side of the Mississippi River. The author suggests a polyphyletic origin for *Apocrangonyx* as a whole.
- **Taxonomic status:** The genus is retained as separate because a full revision of *Stygobromus* and restudy of *Stygonectes* remain pending.
