# Topminnow and Pupfish Statewide Safe Harbor Agreement: Annual Report, Fiscal Year 2011

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This annual report from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] covers fiscal year (FY) 2011 (July 1, 2010 to June 30, 2011) under the statewide [[Safe Harbor Agreement]] (SHA) for [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]]. The agreement was signed by the Department and the [[US Fish and Wildlife Service]] on March 12, 2008, and is reported under Section 10(a)(1)(A) of the [[Endangered Species Act]].

By the end of FY 2011, 8 cooperators were enrolled and 14 populations were being managed under the SHA. Three new sites were enrolled and stocked in FY 2011. Staff also evaluated several additional sites for future enrollment. Monitoring results were mixed: pupfish populations generally did well and reproduced at several sites, while topminnow populations were rare at some sites and one site failed.

## Background & Description

The SHA is a voluntary conservation agreement. Private landowners, municipalities, agencies and non-profit organizations enroll ponds and tanks in which listed fish are stocked and managed. The two focal species are small freshwater fishes that depend on desert springs, ponds and tanks.

- **Gila topminnow** is a small live-bearing fish. Stocks were drawn from several lineages, including Redrock Canyon and Sharp Spring.
- **Desert pupfish** is a small cyprinodontid. Stocks came from several sources, including captive populations, salvaged pond populations and [[Rio Sonoyta Pupfish]] lineages.
- **Longfin dace** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) was stocked at one site as a companion species, in the hope it might persist there.

Management activities in FY 2011 included evaluating, stocking and monitoring enrolled sites, and evaluating candidate sites. Funding came from the Arizona Heritage Fund, State Wildlife Grants, the Arizona Game and Fish Fund and the CAP Funds Transfer Program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Enrolled sites are small, isolated, human-made or spring-fed waters in the Sonoran Desert. Several are managed by county parks, the state wildlife area or private landowners. Habitat conditions vary: the report notes that the Spur Cross site is relatively shallow and may be unsuitable for both species, and that the Willow Springs site was dry at monitoring.

Two genetic and provenance issues shape the program:

- **Hybridization.** Stocks at the Hernbrode and Onofryton ponds were long suspected to be hybrids between [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] and desert pupfish. Recent [[Genetic Analysis]] identified them as pure Rio Sonoyta pupfish, not hybrids, and not from Quitobaquito Springs. The report states these are the only stocks derived from Rio Sonoyta pupfish in the US.
- **Source populations.** Many stocks were moved from one site to another, or salvaged from ponds being drained or diminishing. This makes provenance tracking important.

## Key Findings

### Enrollment and Site Evaluation

- Cumulative enrollment at the end of FY 2011: 8 cooperators, 14 managed populations.
- Three new sites were enrolled and stocked in FY 2011.
- Sites evaluated in FY 2011 included Terry Morgan Ranch, Appleton-Whittell Research Ranch, [[Rio Salado Audubon Center]], [[Kei Sundt Pond]], John Scheuring Pond, Dale Keiser Pond, Usery Mountain Regional Park, the SEMPRA Mesquite Power Plant Wildlife Oasis, Black Canyon Heritage Park and San Rafael Cattle Company.

### Site-by-Site Monitoring Results

- **[[Timbucktwo Pond]]** (private, southern Arizona): first stocked with Redrock Canyon-lineage Gila topminnow on September 3, 2008. No monitoring in FY 2011.
- **[[Pemberton Pond]]** ([[McDowell Mountain Regional Park]]): Gila topminnow stocked in 2009 and desert pupfish in October 2009. In June 2011, topminnow were rare (n=2), while pupfish did well (n=302).
- **[[Spur Cross Solar Oasis]]**: stocked in 2009 with both species. In June 2011, Gila topminnow were low to moderate (n=100) with multiple size classes, indicating reproduction. Desert pupfish were abundant (940 captured) with multiple size classes.
- **[[Hernbrode Pond]]** (private, Tucson): stocked April 10, 2009 with desert pupfish of Rio Sonoyta origin. Monitoring in September 2010 and April 2011 found dozens of active pupfish, many males in breeding colors, and young fish, indicating successful recruitment.
- **[[Onofryton Pond]]** (private, Phoenix): stocked September 29, 2009 with Rio Sonoyta-origin pupfish from Hernbrode Pond. One-year monitoring in September 2010 found several size classes; the population was estimated at over 1,000 fish, with males in full color defending territories.
- **[[Cienega High School Pond]]** (Vail School District): stocked November 9, 2009 with Gila topminnow from Davidson Pond, plus longfin dace. Monitoring on April 7, 2010 found topminnow very rare (n<5). No monitoring in FY 2011.
- **[[Robbins Butte Wildlife Area]]** (Arizona Game and Fish Commission): four SHA tanks. Gila topminnow were stocked in Swimming Pool and Stop Sign tanks, and desert pupfish in Twin and Cottonwood tanks. In June 2011, all topminnow populations were increasing and were used as a source to establish other populations. Twin Tanks had 1,333 pupfish and Cottonwood Tank 186, with multiple size classes at both.
- **[[Willow Springs]]** ([[McDowell Mountain Regional Park]], White Tanks): enrolled after assurances of a perennial water source. On October 30, 2009 the site was dry and all fish were gone. It is considered a failed site. Park managers plan a dual-purpose pond, so the certificate of inclusion will likely be amended.
- **[[Nursery Tank]]** ([[McDowell Mountain Regional Park]]): stocked June 17, 2010 with desert pupfish from Boyce Thompson Arboretum. In June 2011, pupfish were extremely abundant (6,699 captured) with multiple size classes.
- **[[Appleton-Whittell Research Ranch]]** (National Audubon Society): stocked June 4, 2011 with 229 desert pupfish, 115 from each of the Twin and Cottonwood tanks. No further action in FY 2011; a one-year monitoring effort is planned for May 2012.
- **[[Rio Salado Audubon Center]]**: stocked August 31, 2010 with 496 desert pupfish and 544 Gila topminnow. Nine juvenile pupfish died during stocking. Additional salvage and stocking followed in late September 2010. A further 1,500 topminnow from the ASU Animal Care Facility were stocked October 15, 2010 (2 mortalities), sourced from Redrock Canyon stocks.
- **[[Kei Sundt Pond]]**: enrolled in 2010 and stocked November 9, 2010 with 194 desert pupfish from the captive population at The Nature Conservancy's Lower San Pedro River Preserve. Four fish died during stocking. A six-month monitoring effort on May 11, 2011 sampled 38 pupfish of multiple size classes. Minnow traps proved ineffective, likely due to low density, so dip-netting was used. No mortalities occurred during sampling.

### Sourcing and Salvage

- Pupfish were salvaged from [[Libby Elementary School Pond]] in Avondale. In August and September 2010, 2,297 desert pupfish were moved to Cottonwood Tank, and 829 were salvaged from the diminishing Libby pond on September 28, 2010. Fish were redistributed to the Rio Salado Audubon Center, Desert Botanical Garden and Phoenix Zoo ponds, and 47 juveniles died in transit.
- Gila topminnow were supplied through [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and the Bubbling Ponds Fish Hatchery.

### Overall Status

Pupfish populations were stable or increasing at most monitored sites, with reproduction documented at several. Topminnow populations were mixed: they were increasing at Robbins Butte and reproducing at Spur Cross, but rare at Pemberton and Cienega High School, and extirpated at Willow Springs.
