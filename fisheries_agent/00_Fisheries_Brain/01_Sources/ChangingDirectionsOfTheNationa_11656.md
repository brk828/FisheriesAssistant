# Fish Hatchery Policy and Captive Propagation of Listed Fishes: U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Planning Documents

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #hatchery-management

## Executive Summary

This compiled set of U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) documents, dated roughly 1991–1994, addresses how the [[National Fish Hatchery System]] (NFHS) can better conserve wild stocks, maintain genetic integrity, and propagate threatened and endangered fishes. Central proposals include the "Hatcheries in Transition" (HIT) program, requesting $4 million in Year 1 new funding; a draft report on captive propagation of listed fishes; the Fisheries and Aquatic Ecosystems Action Plan; a Hatchery Subactivity Analysis; a Fish Technology Center review; the "Helping Aquaculture Grow" plan; drug and chemical use updates; and FY 1994–1995 budget and policy items. The documents consistently argue that hatcheries have been expanded ad hoc, lack a national framework, and are under-resourced.

## Background & Description

The compilation contains the following main items:

- **Hatcheries in Transition (HIT):** a proposed five-part program on genetics management, product evaluation, wild stock management, endangered species management, and drug/chemical reduction.
- **Role of Service Fish Hatcheries in Captive Propagation of Threatened and Endangered Fishes (Draft, March 2, 1994):** the first comprehensive review of listed-fish propagation at Service facilities.
- **Fishery Resources and Aquatic Ecosystems Action Plan:** a ten-component national strategy prefaced by Director [[Mollie Beattie]].
- **Hatchery Subactivity Analysis Report (November 1991):** a six-area review (production, maintenance, technology centers, fish health, outreach, administration) with recommendations and Directorate responses.
- **Report on Fish Technology Centers (March 1993):** fifteen findings, with an advisory group proposal.
- **Helping Aquaculture Grow:** an aquaculture plan with three objectives and roughly 30 tasks over a three-year timetable.
- **Program change justifications (FY 1995)** for anadromous and inland/Great Lakes hatchery operations and maintenance.
- **Drug and Chemical Use updates (1993–1994)** on INAD applications and [[Food and Drug Administration]] (FDA) actions.
- **National Policy Issuance #94-03:** FY 1994 Director's priority objectives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The documents describe a serious decline in North American fishes. At least 27 species and 13 subspecies have gone extinct in roughly the past 100 years, mostly from habitat loss or alteration. The draft report states that 100 finfish in the coterminous U.S. are listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]], with a further 183 proposed or candidate species. The American Fisheries Society considers about 250 additional species imperiled. Fish make up 29% of listed animal species in the continental U.S. (46% if mussels and clams are included). The Action Plan cites the Atlantic Coast [[Striped Bass]] decline, Great Lakes economic losses estimated at $1.4 billion annually, and falling Pacific salmon harvest values. Invasive species such as [[Zebra Mussel]] and ruffe are identified as major threats. The documents also highlight disease transmission between hatchery and wild fish, genetic impacts such as hybridization and domestication, and water supply limits for aquaculture.

## Key Findings

**Hatcheries in Transition**
- Proposed Year 1 new funding of $4 million: Product Evaluation $2,000,000; Wild Stock Management $1,000,000; Genetics Management/Conservation Biology $500,000; Endangered Species Management $500,000; Drug and Chemical Use $0 (funded separately).
- Six anticipated benefits, including a genetic inventory of brood stocks and an automated strain registry.
- Proposed as a 5-year cross-program effort led by the Fisheries Program.
- The backlog is stated as $140 million for maintenance and construction, plus a $5 million operations need.

**Captive Propagation of Listed Fishes (Draft)**
- 33 Service facilities handle endangered, threatened and candidate fishes: 28 National Fish Hatcheries, two Fish Technology Centers, two Fishery Resource Offices and one National Wildlife Refuge.
- Three hatcheries are entirely devoted to listed species: [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], Lahontan NFH and Bears Bluff NFH. Alchesay/Williams Creek NFH and Orangeburg NFH are substantially involved (about 30% and 12% of budgets).
- 29 of 100 listed fishes, plus nine candidate or proposed species (38 total), are propagated or held in refugia.
- 78 of 100 listed finfish have recovery plans. Captive propagation is an approved strategy for 21 of the 29 listed fishes at Service facilities; only 3 of 29 lack it as a preferred tool.
- An additional 24 fish recovery plans identify captive propagation, but these species are not held by the Service.
- Successes cited: Cui-ui (Nevada), Pecos gambusia (Texas), Greenback cutthroat trout (Colorado), Apache trout and Yaqui chub (Arizona), Lahontan cutthroat trout (Nevada), and Gila trout reintroduction.
- The draft concludes that the System has operated without a national framework, specific funding or a cohesive policy, and recommends a comprehensive management approach, priority designation in budget documents, and cohesive policy. It stresses that propagation must not substitute for wild conservation.

**Hatchery Subactivity Analysis (1991)**
- The Hatchery subactivity comprised 80% of Fisheries appropriations; Fisheries was 18% of the Service's FY 1990 non-research budget ($220,625,000 total).
- Within the Hatchery subactivity, Operations accounted for 69%, Maintenance 20%, Technology Centers 6% and Health Centers 5%.
- Maintenance funding rose from $2.4 million (1985) to $8.5 million (1991). Maintenance needs totaled $116 million as of March 1991, including $51 million in major rehabilitation.
- Recommended a separate account for major rehabilitation.
- Identified the lack of a comprehensive hatchery product evaluation program as a "glaring and universal shortcoming."
- Fish health: the Service operated 9 Fish Health Centers serving 78 broodstock and production hatcheries.
- Outreach survey: all 75 responding hatcheries would expand outreach with resources; estimated upgrade need of $52,000,500.

**Fish Technology Centers (1993)**
- Five Centers were operating in the Hatchery review (Abernathy, San Marcos, Dexter, Lamar, Bozeman), with Warm Springs proposed. The 1993 report lists six operating centers.
- Three Centers maintained 23 of 91 listed threatened fishes at the time (the report also states the Service was perpetuating 30 of 91).
- Proposed a National Fish Technology Center Advisory Group of nine Regional voting members. A February 1993 ARD meeting agreed to pursue it, with targets of 20%, 50% and 75% of Center work redirected to national priorities within 1, 3 and 5 years.

**Action Plan (Fishery Resources and Aquatic Ecosystems)**
- Ten action components: ecosystem and watershed conservation; habitat; water quality and quantity; program evaluation; technology development; National Wildlife Refuge fishery management; state Federal Aid grants; aquaculture; public education; law enforcement.
- Stated vision includes 30-pound naturally reproduced lake trout in the Great Lakes and no aquatic species threatened with extinction.

**Helping Aquaculture Grow**
- Three objectives: protect quality, health and productivity of aquatic resources; reduce conflicts between public and private aquaculture; provide leadership and technical support.
- Three-year funding timetable: Year 1 total $11,280,000; Year 2 total $16,870,000; Year 3 total $18,670,000 (cumulative additions of $5.59 million in Year 2 and $1.8 million in Year 3).

**FY 1995 Program Changes**
- Anadromous Hatchery Operations: Protecting Wild Stocks +$375,000 (5 FTE); Genetics Management +$150,000 (2 FTE).
- Inland/Great Lakes Hatchery Operations: Protecting Wild Stocks +$350,000 (4 FTE); Genetics Management +$375,000 (5 FTE), including $200,000 for lake trout strain isolation at Rydell NWR.
- Hatchery Maintenance: +$646,000 for fisheries maintenance and engineering support, reduced from the customary 15% to 7.5%.

**Drug and Chemical Use (1993–1994 updates)**
- 11 INAD applications submitted to FDA; only 2 approved (common carp pituitary hormone and copper sulfate/Cutrine).
- Eight primary essential compounds were identified from an original list of more than 50 drugs and chemicals.
- Federal FDA–Service memorandum of understanding on endangered and threatened fishes was under review.
- A cease-and-desist order was signed for high-regulatory-priority aquaculture drugs, with disposal due by December 31, 1994.
- A State-funded INAD research agreement calls for $20,000 per state annually for five years (about $1,000,000 per year, 35 states participating so far).
- A key constraint: NPDES permits are required for discharge, and FDA changed its policy in November 1993 on this requirement; the policy was revised again as of March 1994.

**Policy Issuance #94-03 (FY 1994)**
- The Director's priority objectives for Fisheries include finalizing a comprehensive fishery resources plan, developing a Servicewide genetics policy by September, and developing National propagation guidelines for listed fishes (draft due March 1, final June 30).
