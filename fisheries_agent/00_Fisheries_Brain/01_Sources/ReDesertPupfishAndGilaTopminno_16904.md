# Desert Pupfish and Gila Topminnow Refugia, Permits and Monitoring Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) records from 2000 covering three linked items: a letter arranging the establishment of [[Refugia Populations]] of the [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]] and [[Gila Topminnow]] at a Scottsdale Community College pond; a [[Federal Endangered Species Permit]] (TE821577-0) and accompanying [[Section 10 Permit]] authorizations under the [[Endangered Species Act]]; and a [[Job 09 Monitoring And Reintroduction]] workplan for [[Desert Pupfish]] and Gila topminnow surveys, stocking and extirpation reporting. The materials combine species-specific collection limits, handling and transport guidance, and detailed reporting obligations.

## Background & Description

The 3 October 2000 letter from Rob Bettaso, Native Fish Program Manager, to Dr. W.L. Minckley of [[Arizona State University]] describes the planned refugia. The [[Scottsdale Community College]] pond was examined by Department biologists and judged suitable for maintaining long-term, genetically viable populations of both species. Federal permits would be held by the Department, with the college as a sub-permittee. The Quitobaquito pupfish population at ASU was established before the species was listed and is described as grandfathered under the Endangered Species Act. The Gila topminnow at ASU is covered by an ASU Research Permit, from which the state may take fish for reintroduction and recovery.

The permit is issued to AGFD Director Duane L. Shroufe under the [[Threatened And Endangered Species]] permit type. It is valid from 03/02/2000 to 03/31/2002, with an annual report due 12/31. It is issued under 16 USC 1539(a) and 50 CFR 17 and 50 CFR 13 provisions, and it is tied to the [[Section 6 Conservation Agreement]] between the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] and AGFD. Activities are identified in separate annual work plans held by the USFWS Albuquerque Regional Office.

The permit lists about 110 named AGFD primary field agents. Non-permanent staff, interns and volunteers must work under their direct supervision.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The documents focus on rare, native desert fishes whose survival depends on managed refugia and reintroduction. The Gila topminnow permit covers fish from four lineages (Sharp, Bylas, Monkey and Cienega) held at ASU Animal Care. Monitoring targets are sites in Arizona, with Sonora, Mexico possibly included.

Site-level survey and reintroduction criteria emphasize habitat condition. Recorded variables include gradient calculated from 7.5 minute topographic maps, flood vulnerability, stream size, elevation, land ownership, human activities, the condition of aquatic and riparian communities, predators, and potential threats. Surveys are scheduled for April through November, when detection is most likely.

The permit also covers other listed species, including [[Kanab Ambersnail]], [[Willow Flycatcher]], [[Bald Eagle]], [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Jaguar]], [[Mexican Long-Nosed Bat]] and [[Hualapai Mexican Vole]]. These authorizations concern vouchers, contaminant sampling, or capture for research.

## Key Findings

**1. Refugia and stocking plans (letter, 3 October 2000)**
- AGFD planned to collect about 50 Quitobaquito pupfish for the Scottsdale Community College pond.
- It planned a mixed-genetic-background Gila topminnow population from 15–20 gravid females from each of four lineages (Sharp, Bylas, Monkey and Cienega), held at ASU Animal Care.
- Fish were to be transported in large, aerated coolers with battery-powered air pumps. Water temperature was to be tempered to within 1 degree C of the source to reduce thermal stress at stocking.

**3. Exempt and other listed taxa**
- Species under Special Rules need only a valid State permit: Little Colorado spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*), Sonoran chub (*Gila ditaenia*), spikedace (*Meda fulgida*), loach minnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*), and Apache (Arizona) trout (*Oncorhynchus apache*).
- Other authorizations include: one voucher specimen of the New Mexican Ridge-nosed Rattlesnake; up to 20 voucher specimens of Kanab Ambersnail from newly discovered populations; up to 60 Willow Flycatcher nests per year for ectoparasite studies; one jaguar capture for radio-collaring; and up to 10 voucher specimens each of Mexican Long-Nosed Bat and Hualapai Mexican Vole per population.

**4. Emergency and general conditions**
- Non-permit taking by AGFD staff is allowed for sick, injured or orphaned specimens, disposal and salvage of dead specimens, removal of non-immediate human-safety threats, and self-defense. Any such taking must be reported in writing to the USFWS Regional Director within 5 days.
- Live wildlife may not be released or disposed of without prior written USFWS approval.
- Shipments must carry the permit, a copy of the letter and a cover letter naming recipients and numbers.
- The permit does not allow trespass. Research on Tribal lands requires separate Tribal permits.
- Permit renewal requests must be submitted 30 days before expiration (50 CFR §13.22).

**5. Job 09 monitoring and reintroduction workplan**
- Species: Sonoran topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and desert pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*), including Quitobaquito pupfish.
- Cost code FCN5569, PCA 06109. Funding: New USFWS match (75%) of $36,000; New State match (25%) of $12,000. No other project funds were listed.
- Approach: monitor all extant natural and reintroduced sites annually; prepare provisional extirpation reports for sites meeting criteria; restock or report on failed sites; evaluate potential transplant sites; stock suitable sites under the Arizona Game and Fish Commission directives of August 1996.
- Current year targets: survey at least once all natural sites (n=14) and all extant reintroduced sites (n=18). Use fine-mesh dip nets or seines, and deposit voucher specimens in the ASU Collection of Fishes.
- Completion report: covers the five-year project period and compares success or failure of reintroductions, following earlier reports (Simons 1987; Bagley et al. 1990; Brown and Abarca 1992).

**6. Reporting and timing requirements**
- Field notes and data forms are due to USFWS within 30 days of each site visit.
- New sites must be surveyed for suitability and reported to USFWS at least three months before stocking.
- Stocking reports are due within 30 days of stocking and must include date, genetic origin, source location, number stocked, personnel, mortalities, behavior, and location (legal description, GPS and map).
- Stocked sites are monitored at 1 month, 6 months and 11–13 months after stocking, then moved into the annual program.
- Extirpation reports cover sites qualifying between July 1999 and June 2000.
- Heritage Data Management System locality updates are due in January, April, July and October. Annual performance reports are due 12/31. Failure to report can lead to permit revocation or denial.
