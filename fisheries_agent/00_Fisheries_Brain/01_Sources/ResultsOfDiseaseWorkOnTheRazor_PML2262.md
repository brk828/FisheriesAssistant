# Razorback Sucker Disease Survey Memorandum, Willow Beach NFH

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Government memorandum reports a fish health survey of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) collected from a netting operation, probably in [[Lake Mohave]] on the [[Colorado River]]. Five fish were selected from 21 individuals, based on pathological signs, and examined at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. The survey found no major bacterial, fungal or viral pathogens. The main problems were eye damage, external parasites, skin and fin bleeding ulcers, and spores of unidentified origin. The author recommends that fish with bad eyes not be transported and that hatchery staff monitor the population closely. The memo is signed by [[Bruce Rosenlund]], Hatchery Manager. Parts of the OCR text are garbled, so some details are approximate.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Razorback Sucker]], a member of the [[Sucker Family]] (Catostomidae).
- **Sample collection:** The netting operation collected 21 individuals. Five were chosen for examination based on pathological signs. Of these, one was moribund in the holding tank, one had a tumorlike growth on the ventral surface, two had bad eyes, and one was a healthy fish taken for comparison.
- **Specimen data:** The table lists five fish, with fork lengths of roughly 20 to 24 cm and weights of roughly 119 to 127 g (values are OCR-uncertain). Sexes are recorded as male or female.
- **Examination procedure:** The five fish were transported live to the hatchery. Examinations included external and internal parasitology, [[Bacteriological Sampling]] of the kidney and liver, and [[Cell Culture Virus Testing]] of tissue homogenates (kidney, liver, spleen, heart, brain).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The fish came from a wild population managed by the Lake Mohave area, and the memo links the problems to field conditions.
- The author states that bleeding ulcers in the skin and fins are "a fact shared by all" fish in the wild population. This suggests that field conditions, not hatchery conditions, are the likely cause.
- The [[Myxosporean Spores]] are thought to be the greatest threat to recovery. The memo suggests they may have come from another wild source or from a related fish species, and their origin is still unknown.
- Some fish had heavy parasite loads, and a fish with a tumorlike growth was identified.

## Key Findings

**Parasitology**
- External parasites were found on the fish. A monogenean-type flatworm (described in the OCR as a trematode of the suborder Polyopisthocotylea) was found on the gills. The author says identification is still in progress.
- A [[Lernaea]] (anchor worm) had penetrated tissue in some fish and caused inflammation with bacteria present.
- Internal parasites included trematodes, cestodes and nematodes. Their number and type were not clearly recorded.

**Bacteriology and Virology**
- No signs of chronic bacterial, fungal or viral disease were found.
- Bacteria were found in the eyes of some fish, but the author concluded that the bacteria were not the cause of eye degeneration.
- Cell cultures were inoculated and incubated at 15°C and 22°C. No cytopathic effects were observed in the fish cell lines.

**Gross Pathology**
- **Gills:** In fine shape, with no pathogens.
- **Eyes:** Two of the five fish were exophthalmic (bulging eyes). The cornea of one was completely opaque, and the other tissues of the eye were badly atrophied.
- **Skin and fins:** Multiple bleeding ulcers were found on the skin and fins.
- **Body cavity:** Internal organs were normal, but the fish had large amounts of fat.
- **Tumorlike growth:** One fish had a tumorlike growth on the ventral surface.

**Management Recommendations**
- Fish with bad eyes should not be transported to other hatcheries or facilities.
- Hatchery staff should monitor the fish closely for eye conditions.
- The effluent from razorback sucker tanks should not be discharged into tanks holding other fish species.
- Further classification of the parasites and spores is needed before any conclusions are drawn.
