# Results of disease work on the razorback sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1973 U.S. Government memorandum reports a disease examination of five [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) collected by gill netting during a [[Rare and Endangered Fish Survey]] of [[Lake Mohave]]. The fish were selected on the basis of pathological signs. Two had an exophthalmic (popeyed) condition, one had a tumor-like growth on the ventral surface, one was moribund, and one was healthy as a comparison. The two major problems were [[Lernaea]] infestation and a [[Myxosoma]] sp. infection associated with eye degeneration. The author judged *Myxosoma* the greatest threat to the fish and to the selection of animals for future recovery work. A companion memo notes that blind and normal fish showed similar behavior and condition, and recommends restricting transport of fish with bad eyes.

## Background & Description

- **Survey and collection:** Five fish were chosen from 21 individuals in the survey and collected by gill netting on 18-19 October 1973.
- **Examination:** Fish were transported by distribution truck to the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] laboratory. Each received a complete external and internal parasitological examination. Bacteriological samples were taken from kidney and liver, and virus homogenates (kidney, liver, spleen) were sent to the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] laboratory in Springville, Utah.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Lake Mohave is a warm-water environment. The author notes that Lernaea infestation is shared by all fish there, so it is not unique to the razorbacks.
- The [[Myxosporidea]] spores are described as highly host- and tissue-specific. The author suspects long-term association with the fish, but notes they could have come from another sucker species, and could be transferred to other suckers.
- The second memo describes the fish as very lethargic. Sight of humans, hands in the water, and gentle handling did not visibly disturb them.
- Blind and normal fish were fat and feeding equally well, with no clear behavioral difference.

## Key Findings

**Pathology by tissue (five fish):**

- **Skin:** All specimens showed damage from *Lernaea*, though few parasites were attached. Other external parasites were one Ich-type parasite ("Ich tomite") and one monogenean trematode (suborder Polyopisthocotylea). The tumor-like growth was still under histopathological study, and some workers have reported tumor-like growths around embedded *Lernaea*.
- **Gills:** Normal, with no signs of parasites or other problems.
- **Musculature:** No pathogens, with good tone and color, except where *Lernaea* had penetrated and caused inflammation. Small amounts of bacteria were found in inflamed areas.
- **Eyes:** Two of five fish were exophthalmic. Their corneas were opaque with abnormal fluid between cornea and iris, the lens was completely dissolved, and the eye was badly atrophied. Bacteria, mostly *Aeromonas*, were found in the fluid. Tissue from the choroid-sclera region contained large numbers of *Myxosoma* sp. spores, while the cornea and iris were clear. The author considers the bacteria secondary and *Myxosoma* the primary cause of eye degeneration.
- **Body cavity:** Mesenteries, organs, intestine, and bladder were negative for pathogens and appeared healthy, except for *Lernaea* embedded in the pharynx. All specimens had large fat stores.
- **Virology:** Homogenates inoculated on rainbow trout (RTG-2) and fathead minnow (FHM) cell lines and incubated at 15 °C and 22 °C for two weeks showed no cytopathic effects.

**Summary and conclusions:**

- Dominant problems were *Lernaea* and *Myxosoma*. *Lernaea* causes bleeding ulcers in skin and muscle. *Myxosoma* is considered the greatest threat to fish health and to selecting animals for recovery work.
- The moribund fish probably died from netting injury, since no pathogens were found.
- No viral agents were detected.

**Recommendations (Companion Memo):**

- Fish with bad eyes should not be transported.
- Fish held in California should be watched closely for the eye condition.
- Until further classification is complete, razorbacks should not be mixed with other suckers.
