# Arizona State University Faculty Mentoring Award Nomination Packet (1996)

#faculty-mentoring #graduate-education #academic-awards

> **Scope note:** This document is an administrative and academic nomination packet. It contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton, water-chemistry or habitat data. The note therefore summarizes the award process and the nominee dossiers.

## Executive Summary

This packet, assembled by the [[Graduate College]] at [[Arizona State University]] (ASU) in mid-1996, supports the selection of the 1996 Faculty Mentoring Award (also styled Outstanding Graduate Mentor Award). A memo from [[Deborah N. Losse]], Associate Dean, dated July 17, 1996, scheduled a selection committee meeting for September 11, 1996 at 1:30 p.m. in Dixie Gammage room 116D. The packet contains the finalist dossiers for two principal nominees, [[Constantine A. Balanis]] (Electrical Engineering) and [[Robert Strom]] (Psychology in Education, Lifespan Developmental Psychology). Both dossiers include mentoring statements, letters of reference, and records of supervised doctoral students.

## Background & Description

**Award history.** The packet lists past winners and finalists, which document the award's development:

- **Outstanding Graduate Mentors (1987–1995):** Eleven named faculty, including [[Ronald Greeley]], [[Peter Buseck]], [[W.L. Minckley]], [[Geoffrey Clark]], [[Stuart Lindsay]], [[Manuel Barrera]], [[Helen Reed]] and [[Constantine Balanis]], with Nancy Kerr, Marvin Fisher and David Foster also listed.
- **1992 finalists (5):** Allan Bieber, Geoffrey Clark (awardee), David Ferry, Daniel M. Landers and Howard Sullivan.
- **1993–94 nominees (12):** Stuart Lindsay (awardee) and finalists including David Ferry and Sharon E. Robinson.
- **1994 nominees:** Manuel Barrera and Helen Reed were co-awardees; John Holloway was a finalist.
- **1995 nominees (5):** John R. Holloway (awardee); Constantine A. Balanis was a finalist.

**Nominee 1: Constantine A. Balanis.** Regents' Professor and Director of the [[Telecommunications Research Center]] (TRC). The dossier includes:

- A mentoring philosophy essay, *Mentoring: A Challenging and Stimulating Partnership*, framing the mentor as teacher, advisor, scholar, manager, role model and partner.
- A list of **10 completed dissertations** (1976–1995) at West Virginia University and ASU, and **3 dissertations expected in 1996**.
- A statement on former PhD students, noting that most of his journal and conference papers were co-authored with students who were lead authors.
- Reported scholarly output: **over 90 refereed journal papers**, **over 130 international conference papers**, and two textbooks used at **over 100 universities**, one translated into Chinese.
- Honors: [[Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers]] (IEEE) Fellow (1986) and Regents' Professor (1991).
- A patent, shared with an MS student, for reducing signal distortion and crosstalk in closely packed computer interconnects.

**Nominee 2: Robert Strom.** Professor, Division of Psychology in Education. The dossier includes:

- A statement, *Mentoring in Higher Education* (June 1996), organized around seven goals: optimism, continued learning, active learning, collaboration across disciplines and countries, interdependence in research, respect for foreign students, and publication involvement. The statement also argues that mentors teach largely by example.
- Some 20 or more supporting letters from former doctoral students, colleagues and external faculty. Among the letter writers are [[Lydia P. Buki]] (Outstanding Research Award, ASU Division of Psychology in Education), [[Susan O. Mitchell]], [[Troy Beckert]], [[Pat Collinsworth Huish]] and [[Dianne L. Griswold]].
- A colleague's list of former doctoral students who now hold academic posts, including deans and department chairs at several universities.

**Support letters for Balanis.** Letters came from former student [[Panayiotis A. Tirkas]], retired colleague [[Thomas E. Tice]] (two letters), a Navy engineer and former student, and Associate Professor [[Andreas Spanias]]. Several letters describe him as one of the top three instructors in departmental evaluations and cite his Electromagnetics program and the [[Electromagnetic Anechoic Chamber]] (EMAC), which one letter describes as one of the top two such university facilities. The packet also notes an invited panel role in a national teleconference on the advisor's role.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document has no ecological, aquatic or environmental-science content. The only institutional context is the ASU academic setting and the field of [[Electromagnetics]] (Balanis) and lifespan developmental psychology (Strom). No species, habitat or water-quality data are reported.

## Key Findings

1. **Mentoring is defined as a partnership.** Balanis describes it as a shared responsibility between advisor and student, with students expected to show trust, respect, initiative and commitment. Strom frames mentoring as teaching by example, grounded in optimism and collaboration.

2. **Publication and co-authorship are central to both dossiers.** Balanis reports that most of his journal and conference papers list graduate students as lead authors. Strom describes publication involvement as a core mentoring goal, and letters credit him with ensuring that students complete a conference paper and a refereed journal article.

3. **Student outcomes are the main evidence.** Balanis's former students include faculty, a Navy principal engineer, a Hong Kong company president and an IEEE Transactions associate editor. Strom's former students hold dean, chair and director positions in several institutions. Letters also cite an IEEE outstanding journal paper award and a Radio Technical Commission for Aeronautics award for Balanis's students.

4. **Funding and national reputation are cited as supporting evidence.** Balanis's research is reported as funded at "the millions of dollars" by government agencies and aerospace companies, and he holds two national fellowships' worth of student support (NSF and ONR). Strom's international reputation is supported by translated assessment instruments and Fulbright and cross-cultural awards.

5. **Teaching quality is a shared criterion.** Strom's cooperative learning approach is supported by a cited meta-analysis of more than 500 studies (Johnson & Johnson, 1989). His mentoring statement also cites a University of California at Berkeley calculus initiative, in which study workshops reportedly lowered a 60% minority failure rate. Balanis's teaching is supported by course evaluations and student testimony.

6. **Committee process.** The committee was to consider the finalists at its September 11, 1996 meeting. The memo invited written comments in advance, and the letters of reference and mentor statements were enclosed for each finalist.

7. **No final decision appears in the packet.** The document records nominations and supporting evidence only. The selected recipient is not identified here.
