# Colorado River Endangered Fishes Recovery Implementation Program Correspondence

#ichthyology #conservation-policy #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This correspondence documents a 1988 exchange between the [[Desert Fishes Council]] (DFC) and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) regarding the [[Colorado River Endangered Fishes Recovery Implementation Program]]. The exchange addresses concerns about how DFC's December 9, 1986 review comments were received and characterized by FWS staff, particularly regarding four endangered fish species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. The correspondence clarifies official Service positions versus personal staff comments and reaffirms the value of external scientific review in endangered species recovery planning.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Endangered Fishes Recovery Implementation Program]] was developed through the Upper Colorado River Basin Coordinating Committee, established in 1984 by FWS to resolve conflicts between endangered species protection and water resource development. Four fish species are the focus: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]] (federally listed as endangered), plus [[Razorback Sucker]] (candidate for listing at time of correspondence).

Since 1978, FWS had issued over 100 biological opinions on water project impacts to these endangered fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]], concluding that cumulative and site-specific impacts of water projects and associated depletions were jeopardizing the continued existence of these species. The Recovery Program represents a comprehensive 15-year initiative designed to recover endangered fishes while remaining consistent with Interstate Compacts and State water rights systems.

Participants in the Coordinating Committee included the states of [[Colorado]], [[Wyoming]], and [[Utah]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], FWS, environmental groups, and water development interests. On October 29, 1987, the Coordinating Committee endorsed the Recovery Program and recommended execution of a cooperative agreement by the Secretary of the Interior and state governors.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The endangered fishes inhabit the [[Colorado River]] system, with the [[Yampa River]] identified as the top priority for water rights acquisition in the upper basin. The Recovery Program addresses impacts to hydropower production, river recreation, and sportfishing. An Environmental Assessment was completed with a finding of no significant impact (FONSI).

Water development interests had strongly objected to FWS positions on minimum flows as violations of State water law and Interstate Compacts, with threats of lawsuits and amendments to the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The Recovery Program attempts to balance endangered species protection with water resource development needs across the three-state region.

FWS and [[The Nature Conservancy]], in cooperation with the Coordinating Committee, were evaluating opportunities for protecting flows on the [[Yampa River]] as of November 1987. The program called for Congressional appropriation of $10.0 million for acquisition of water rights to meet instream flow needs, with the Senate appropriation committee identifying $2.5 million for water rights acquisition in FY 88 budget.

## Key Findings

**Program Structure and Funding:**
- Annual implementation cost estimated at $2.3 million, with proposed funding from FWS ($0.6M), states of Colorado, Wyoming, and Utah ($0.2M), and Bureau of Reclamation ($1.5M)
- Additional $10.0 million Congressional appropriation requested for water rights acquisition
- 15-year comprehensive program timeline

**Stakeholder Positions:**
- States of Colorado, Wyoming, and Utah formally expressed support
- Environmental groups supported the program assuming full implementation and better definition of water rights acquisition
- [[Colorado Water Congress]] and State of Colorado actively lobbying Congress for program funding
- [[Western Area Power Administration]] expressed concerns about hydropower production impacts from Federal Reservoirs (e.g., [[Flaming Gorge]]) but agreed to participate

**Interagency Communication Issues:**
- DFC provided formal review comments in January 1987 that were characterized as "very negative," "emotional," and "not based on complete fact" in internal FWS memorandum
- FWS staff comments were discounted in the review process based on these characterizations
- Regional Director Galen L. Buterbaugh clarified that staff comments did not represent official Service position
- Several changes in the Recovery Program document were made to reflect DFC comments
- DFC placed on permanent mailing list for Recovery Program materials

**DFC Concerns:**
- Questioned validity of discounting substantive scientific review based on perceived emotionalism
- Requested specification of errors or misinterpretations if comments were factually incorrect
- Sought assurance of continued inclusion in review processes and timely receipt of program materials
- Expressed commitment to maximal participation in endangered species recovery processes
