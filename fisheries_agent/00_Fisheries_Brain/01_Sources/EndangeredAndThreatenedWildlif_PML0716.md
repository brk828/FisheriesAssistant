# Withdrawal of the Expired Proposal to List the Razorback Sucker Under the Endangered Species Act

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register document is a regulatory notice, not a research report. Its main part is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) notice withdrawing the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) listing proposal. The proposal was issued on April 24, 1978, to list the species as Threatened without Critical Habitat, and it lapsed because no final rule was adopted within the two-year limit set by the [[Endangered Species Act]] (as amended November 10, 1978). The notice also records that the related [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), proposed in the same action, was finalized for Endangered status on April 23, 1980.

The document also includes a separate [[National Highway Traffic Safety Administration]] (NHTSA) comment-procedure notice on a proposed rule under the Uniform Tire Quality Grading (UTQG) Standards, which is unrelated to fish. Its comment period is limited to 30 days.

## Background & Description

**Species profile (from the notice):**
- **Razorback sucker** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): a native freshwater fish known to occur in the rivers of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. It was originally proposed for listing as Threatened without Critical Habitat on April 24, 1978 (43 FR 17375-77).
- **Bonytail chub** (*Gila elegans*): proposed for listing as Endangered without Critical Habitat in the same 1978 proposal and finalized on April 23, 1980 (45 FR 27710-13, as read from OCR).

The notice does not give morphology, life history, population counts or distribution beyond the six states listed below.

**Legal framework:**
- Section 4(b)(5) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] (16 U.S.C. 1533) requires a final rule within two years of a proposal's publication. If it is not adopted, the Secretary must withdraw the proposal and publish notice of withdrawal in the [[Federal Register]] within 30 days after the period ends.
- A withdrawn species can be re-proposed only if sufficient new information is available.
- A one-year suspension on withdrawals for proposals made before the 1978 amendments ran until November 10, 1979.
- The notice is issued under the ESA of 1973, as amended (16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.; 87 Stat. 884; 92 Stat. 3751).

**Document details:**
- FWS notice: primary author Dr. James D. Williams, Office of Endangered Species, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Washington, D.C.; dated May 1980 (the OCR reads "May 18," with the year partly garbled); signed by the Acting Director, Fish and Wildlife Service.
- NHTSA notice: principal authors Dr. F. Cecil Brenner (Office of Automotive Ratings) and Richard L. Hipolit (Office of Chief Counsel); issued May 19, 1980 under 15 U.S.C. 1392, 1401, 1407, 1421 and 1423, and delegations at 49 CFR 1.50 and 801.81.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document places the razorback sucker within the **Colorado River basin**, specifically the rivers of Arizona, California, Colorado, Nevada, Utah and Wyoming. The notice does not describe habitat requirements, threats, population status, or the water chemistry or invertebrate communities of these rivers. The regulatory context is the reason for the withdrawal, not ecological evidence. The notice makes no population or conservation-status claims beyond the listing decisions.

## Key Findings

1. **Withdrawal of the razorback sucker proposal.** The April 24, 1978 proposal to list the razorback sucker as Threatened without Critical Habitat (43 FR 17375-77) was withdrawn under the ESA's two-year time limit because no final rule was adopted in time.
2. **Bonytail chub listed.** The bonytail chub, proposed in the same 1978 action as Endangered without Critical Habitat, was finalized on April 23, 1980.
3. **Re-proposal conditions.** The razorback sucker may be proposed again only if new information warrants it.
4. **Public comment procedure (NHTSA notice).** Written comments are invited on the UTQG proposal within a 30-day period. Confidential submissions require three complete copies and seven redacted copies, and claims of confidentiality must cite 5 U.S.C. 552(b)(4) and show likely substantial competitive damage. Comments received after the deadline will be treated as suggestions for future rulemaking.
5. **Scope limitation.** The notice contains no new scientific data on the razorback sucker, its habitat or its population. It serves an administrative and legal function only.
