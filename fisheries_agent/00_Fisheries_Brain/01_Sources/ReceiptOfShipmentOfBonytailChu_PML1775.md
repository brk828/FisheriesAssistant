# Razorback Sucker and Bonytail Chub: 1995 Hatchery Shipment and Stocking Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Parker Fish and Wildlife Office documents the receipt and release of a 1995 shipment of endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center. Fish were received on 21 November 1995 and distributed to several sites along the Lower Colorado River system. Length measurements were taken on 200 fish of each species, and tissue samples were frozen for genetic analysis. The accompanying stocking data sheets record individual fish lengths by site.

## Background & Description

- **Razorback Sucker (RZBK):** An estimated 17,887 individuals were included in the shipment.
- **Bonytail Chub (BTC):** The shipment count is partly illegible in the scan (reported as roughly "?0,000"), so the exact number cannot be confirmed.
- **Sample lengths (n = 200 per species):**
  - Bonytail Chub: mean total length 79.7 mm (range 53–105 mm)
  - Razorback Sucker: mean total length 91.9 mm (range 68–168 mm)
- The fish were transported by personnel from Dexter, with refuge staff assisting at several release sites.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both species are native to the [[Colorado River Basin]] and are managed as endangered fishes. Stocking targeted several habitat types:

- **Refuge ponds and holding facilities:** [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]] (farmer's pond facility), [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (high levee pond), and [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] (office pond). Fish that arrived late were held overnight at a fish farm.
- **Lake Havasu and adjacent coves:** Releases were made at Pittsburgh Point, Helicopter Cove, Bulkhead Cove, and the north and south twin coves of [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]], the last reached by pontoon boat.

Stocking locations sit within the reservoir and river systems of the Lower Colorado River, which serve as the recovery habitat for these species.

## Key Findings

- **Shipment and transport:** About 17,887 razorback suckers and a partly illegible count of bonytail chubs were delivered to the Parker Fish and Wildlife Office on 21 November 1995.
- **Release at refuge ponds:** About 4,000 razorback suckers were released at Imperial NWR, and about 4,000 razorback suckers plus a partly illegible number of bonytail chubs were released at Cibola NWR. The Bill Williams River NWR release count is also illegible.
- **Lake Havasu releases:**
  - Pittsburgh Point: 300 RZBK and 200 BTC
  - Helicopter Cove: 200 RZBK
  - Bulkhead Cove: 1,083 RZBK
  - South twin cove: about 3,545 RZBK
  - North twin cove: about 3,259 RZBK
- **Size data:** Mean total lengths were 79.7 mm for bonytail chubs and 91.9 mm for razorback suckers, based on 200 fish of each species.
- **Stocking data sheets:** Four pages of individual total length records, grouped by species and site, accompany the memo. Most entries are illegible in the scan, so they are not reproduced here.
- **Genetic sampling:** Tissue samples from both species were frozen for [[Genetic Analysis]], supporting population management and recovery planning.
