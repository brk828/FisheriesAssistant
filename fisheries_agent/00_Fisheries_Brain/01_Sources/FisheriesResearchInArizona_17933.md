# Fisheries Research in Arizona: Crappie Population Limits and Woods Canyon Lake Fertilization (Project F-14-R-1, 1966–1967)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department job completion report (Federal Aid Project F-14-R-1, March 1, 1966 to February 28, 1967, prepared by Andrew J. Kemmerer) covers two work segments. Segment 1 investigates factors limiting [[Black Crappie]] population growth in [[Roosevelt Lake]], where crappie proved scarce and difficult to sample. Segment 2 tests fertilization of [[Woods Canyon Lake]], a 50-acre infertile lake, using polyethylene tube enclosures. Both segments were marked as incomplete, with a continuation recommended. The most effective fertilizer combination was nitrogen plus phosphorus; hydrated lime generally suppressed production.

## Background & Description

The report is a federally funded job completion report under the [[Federal Aid in Wildlife Restoration Act]], approved by Director Wendell G. Swank and Federal Aid Coordinator Thomas W. Taylor. Project leader Steve Gallizioli and Federal Aid Coordinator Roger J. Gruenewald are also credited.

**Segment 1 – Crappie (Work Plan 1, Job 1):** The overall objective was to determine factors influencing crappie populations in Roosevelt Lake. Segment objectives covered spawning periods and behavior, food habits, whether [[Threadfin Shad]] prey on crappie eggs and fry, length frequencies and condition factors, scale-based age and growth, and preferred areas and depths.

**Segment 2 – Woods Canyon Lake Fertilization (Work Plan 2, Job 1):** The overall objective was to determine the cost per pound of increased harvest from artificially fertilizing an infertile 50-acre lake. Objectives covered the fertilizer type and quantity for optimum productivity, growth and condition of fingerling trout, and limnological features.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Roosevelt Lake** is a large reservoir with a low crappie population. Threadfin shad were present, and the lake supported buffalo and shiner populations. Water-level records came from the Salt River Project. Temperature profiles showed no strong summer stratification, with winter turnover beginning in December. Secchi readings varied between stations, suggesting turbidity differences that could affect fish distribution. The 1966 water level was lower in December and peaked in March at the highest level reported since 1941.

**Woods Canyon Lake** (Coconino County, constructed 1956 by the Arizona Game and Fish Department, renovated in September 1965) is a put-and-take trout fishery. Photic depth was 5 to 5.5 m during the fertilization series. Thermocline behavior limited the mixing of additives into the hypolimnion. Golden shiners survived the 1965 renovation and were a potential limit on trout growth.

## Key Findings

### Crappie Study (Segment 1)

- **Collection methods:** Crappie were most successfully collected by [[Hook and Line Angling]] (most crappie overall, mainly off Fraiser's Boat Dock), [[Electrofishing]] with a 220 V A.C. generator, and [[Trammel Nets]] (about half of attempts unsuccessful). Fyke nets caught at most three crappie per net. Gill nets, and partial cove renovations with rotenone, caught no crappie.
- **Sample:** The diet of 100 crappie was analyzed. Gonad and length-frequency work used 79 crappie collected in November.
- **Condition:** Mean Kₙ = 3.72 (n = 203, 1966). Highest condition in fall; drop in spring, suggesting a link between weight loss and spawning. Highest values in the 180–280 mm size class.
- **Comparison:** Roosevelt crappie condition was significantly higher than values from other U.S. waters (e.g., Clear Lake, California: 3.44; Norris Reservoir, Tennessee: 3.02). The report notes the sample was weighted toward high-condition months.
- **Conversion issue:** The 4.05 value reported by [[Beers and McConnell]] (1966) was about 9% higher, which the author attributes mainly to a different total-to-standard length conversion factor (0.769 vs. 0.785). Adjusting for this seven percent difference brought the figures into agreement.
- **Age and growth:** Scales from below the lateral line gave a better length–scale relationship (r = 0.978) than scales from above (r = 0.798). Age-class mean lengths were 21.8 cm (age I, n = 77), 27.5 cm (II, n = 22), 34.8 cm (III, n = 5), and 37.8 cm (IV, n = 1). False annuli were identified and small crappie under 10 cm observed in December raised doubts about data completeness.
- **Food habits:** Threadfin shad were the dominant food item (78% average frequency of occurrence). Chironomidae (midge larvae and pupae) were second (22%). Twigs appeared in April stomachs, possibly linked to nest building. Insufficient data existed to confirm a shift from fish to insect diet during breeding.
- **Reproduction:** Spawning in 1966 ended by May. Gonads fell sharply after April. Gonad enlargement began in late summer, most males were ripe by February, and no ripe females were found before the segment ended, suggesting males spawn before females.
- **Species status:** All crappie collected were black crappie. No white crappie were found, though they were considered abundant from 1958 to 1960.

### Woods Canyon Lake Fertilization (Segment 2)

- **Design:** Two series of experiments were run in isolated water columns (polyethylene tubes 2 ft in diameter and 30 ft long). Series 1 ran July 7–18 (12 days); Series 2 ran August 24–September 13 (21 days). Each series had four replicated treatments and two controls, randomly assigned to ten tubes.
- **Treatments:** Single super phosphate, ammonium nitrate, and hydrated lime were used alone and in combination. Target concentrations were 2.0 ppm N, 0.5 ppm P, and 75.0 ppm CaCO₃ in Series 1, reduced to 37.5 ppm CaCO₃ in Series 2.
- **Best treatment:** Nitrogen plus phosphorus gave the greatest productivity. In Series 2 it reached 1.75 mg O₂/L gross photosynthesis, 41.88 mg/m³ chlorophyll, and 57.40 mg zooplankton dry weight per 20 ft haul. Nitrogen or phosphorus alone had little effect.
- **Hydrated lime:** Treatments containing calcium carbonate (hydrated lime) generally suppressed production. A slight improvement occurred only in N+P+CaCO₃ tubes when lime was halved. The author suggests lime precipitated as calcium carbonate instead of forming bicarbonate, which phytoplankton can use.
- **Recommendation:** Another material is needed to raise bicarbonate alkalinity in Woods Canyon Lake.
- **Tube validity:** Replicate tubes agreed closely. Oxygen extinction in a control tube matched adjacent open water. The thermocline acted as a barrier to additives.
- **Trout growth:** Marked fingerling [[Rainbow Trout]] (about 10,000 planted May 11, 1966; double fin clip) grew from a mean of 3.42 in (May) to 7.34 in (September 30), about 3.92 inches in five months, or 0.78 inches per month. Condition (Kₙ) rose from 1.92 (June 22) to 2.14 (September 30).
- **Unmarked catchables:** Kₙ was 1.86 (n = 21, June 22) and 1.73 (n = 34, September 30). These values likely reflect hatchery condition.
- **Mortality:** Gill nets killed many catchable-size trout, so only four collection attempts were made. In a holding trial of about 700 marked fish, only seven died.
- **Stocking:** The appendix lists about 24 stocking events in 1966, mostly catchable rainbow trout (8.5–12 in), with 10,000 fingerlings in May.
