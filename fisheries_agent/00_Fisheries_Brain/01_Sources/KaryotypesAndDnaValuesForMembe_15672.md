# Karyotypes and DNA Values for Members of the Suborder Esocoidei (Osteichthyes: Salmoniformes)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #esocidae

## Executive Summary

This 1971 cytogenetic study by [[R. J. Beamish]], [[M. J. Merrilees]] and [[E. J. Crossman]] (University of Toronto and Royal Ontario Museum) examines chromosome counts and nuclear DNA content across the suborder [[Esocoidei]], a group of primary freshwater fishes comprising pikes, mudminnows and the Alaska blackfish. Karyotypes were prepared from gill, gonad, fin, liver and spleen tissue, and relative DNA content was measured by [[Feulgen Microdensitometry]] of erythrocyte nuclei. The authors conclude that combining the genera [[Umbra]], [[Novumbra]] and [[Dallia]] into a single family, Umbridae, "may be ill advised."

## Background & Description

The suborder contains the genus [[Esox]] (pikes and pickerels), [[Umbra]] and [[Novumbra]] (mudminnows), and [[Dallia]] (Alaska blackfish). Depending on the authority, these four genera represent 2 to 4 families and 10 to 13 living species. The placement of Dallia pectoralis and Novumbra hubbsi was the main point of disagreement; both were previously treated as sole members of the families Dalliidae and Novumbridae, but were then usually placed in Umbridae.

The family Esocidae was treated as six species:
- [[Northern Pike]] (*Esox lucius*), circumpolar in the Northern Hemisphere.
- [[Amur Pike]] (*Esox reicherti*), native to the Amur River and introduced in Pennsylvania, USA.
- [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy*).
- [[Chain Pickerel]] (*Esox niger*), [[Redfin Pickerel]] (*Esox americanus americanus*) and [[Grass Pickerel]] (*Esox americanus vermiculatus*), native to eastern and central North America.

The family Umbridae was listed as five species: [[European Mudminnow]] (*Umbra krameri*), [[Eastern Mudminnow]] (*Umbra pygmaea*), [[Central Mudminnow]] (*Umbra limi*), [[Olympic Mudminnow]] (*Novumbra hubbsi*) and [[Alaska Blackfish]] (*Dallia pectoralis*).

Prior karyotype reports were few: Foley (1926) found 22 metacentrics in *Umbra limi*; McGregor (1970) reported 50 acrocentrics in *Esox masquinongy*; and European authors reported 18 to 50 chromosomes in *Esox lucius*, with 2n = 18 judged probably erroneous by Nygren et al. (1968).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Specimens were collected from multiple habitats, including two localities in Ontario, Lake Windermere (England) for *E. lucius*, and single localities in North Carolina, Washington State and Alaska for other taxa. Eggs of *E. reicherti* came from the Amur River and were reared at a Pennsylvania Fish Commission hatchery. The authors tested whether karyotypic variation existed between individuals from different habitats and found none.

Because *Umbra krameri* is now rare, it was not sampled. The study therefore covers all living representatives of the suborder except this species.

## Key Findings

**Diploid chromosome numbers (2n):**
- *Esox* spp.: 50 acrocentric chromosomes; counts below 50 were attributed to chromosome loss during squashing, and 51 or 52 to chromatid separation.
- *Umbra limi* and *U. pygmaea*: 22 large metacentric and submetacentric chromosomes.
- *Novumbra hubbsi*: 48 metacentric, submetacentric and acrocentric chromosomes (counts of 47 also obtained in both sexes).
- *Dallia pectoralis*: 78 acrocentric, metacentric and submetacentric chromosomes (counts of 77 in both sexes).

**Polymorphism:** No chromosomal polymorphism was observed among tissues, sexes, geographic localities or habitats.

**Relative DNA content (Feulgen, human epithelial cells = 1.000):**

| Species | Relative DNA | Category |
|---|---|---|
| *Esox lucius* | 0.388 ± 0.006 | ~30-39% group |
| *Novumbra hubbsi* | 0.297 ± 0.004 | ~30-39% group |
| *Dallia pectoralis* | 0.361 ± 0.006 | ~30-39% group |
| *Umbra limi* and *U. pygmaea* | ~68-72% | Umbra group |

Values for other *Esox* taxa were estimated at 2.25 to 2.56 × 10⁻⁹ mg DNA/cell, and for *Umbra* at 4.82 to 5.03 × 10⁻⁹ mg DNA/cell. Goldfish (*Carassius auratus*) gave 0.490 ± 0.044, consistent with Ohno and Atkin (1966). Each species was based on 40 cells; goldfish on 60.

**Polyploidy:** Apparent polyploid mitoses in testes (reported by Nygren et al., 1968) were attributed to chromosome confluence during squashing. Red blood cell DNA measurements showed no sign of polyploid nuclei.

**Interpretation:**
- The authors suggest 2n = 27 to 50 for *Esox lucius* across its range, and possibly for the whole family Esocidae, though the reported 27 count is ambiguous in the source text.
- Both karyotypes and DNA values argue against placing *Umbra*, *Novumbra* and *Dallia* together in Umbridae.
- *Umbra* karyotypes could derive from an esocoid ancestor by Robertsonian fusions. *Novumbra* may have arisen by pericentric inversions or centromere shifts. No simple chromosomal link could be proposed for *Dallia*, which is tied to the esocids only by its DNA value.
