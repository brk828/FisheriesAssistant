# Biological Opinion for Repair of Blue River Low-Water Crossing on Forest Road 475

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #threatened-species #ESA-consultation #habitat-assessment

## Executive Summary

This biological opinion, issued April 21, 1995, addresses emergency and follow-up repair of flood damage to a low-water ford crossing of the [[Blue River]] on Forest Road 475 in the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]], [[Greenlee County]], [[Arizona]]. The U.S. Forest Service (lead agency) and U.S. Army Corps of Engineers consulted under Section 7 of the Endangered Species Act regarding impacts to the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and its designated critical habitat. The opinion concludes nonjeopardy and no destruction or adverse modification of critical habitat, with specific terms and conditions to minimize incidental take during repair activities.

## Background & Description

The [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was listed as threatened on October 28, 1986, with critical habitat designated March 8, 1994. This small, slender, elongate cyprinid fish exhibits markedly upwardly-directed eyes and historically ranged throughout the Verde, Salt, San Pedro, San Francisco, and Gila River basins. Habitat destruction, competition, and predation by non-native fish have reduced the species' range by approximately 85 percent. The species now persists in limited portions of the upper Gila, San Francisco, Blue, Tularosa, and White Rivers, and in Aravaipa, Eagle, Campbell Blue, and Dry Blue Creeks in Arizona and New Mexico.

[[Loach Minnow]] is a bottom-dwelling inhabitant of shallow, swift water over gravel, cobble, and rubble substrates. The species uses interstitial spaces between and in the lee of larger substrate for resting and spawning. It is rare or absent from habitats where fine sediments fill these spaces. Some studies indicate filamentous algae may be an important habitat component. Life span is approximately 2 years. The species feeds exclusively on aquatic insects. Spawning occurs primarily March through May, with recent reports confirming autumn spawning under certain circumstances. Eggs are attached to the underside of rocks forming the roof of small substrate cavities on the downstream side, with limited data indicating males may guard nests during incubation.

In the [[Blue River]], [[Loach Minnow]] is found in suitable habitat throughout areas of perennial flow. The proposed project is located within designated critical habitat. Recent biochemical genetic work reveals substantial differences in genetic makeup between remnant populations, which occupy isolated fragments of the Gila basin. Based on this work, Tibbets (1992) recommended managing genetically distinctive units as separate units to preserve existing genetic variation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Blue River]] is a degraded system. Human uses of the river and its watershed have resulted in destabilization of the stream channel characterized by wide shallow water channel profile, high sediment levels, eroding banks, braided channels, and depauperate riparian vegetation. Contributing human uses include livestock grazing, roads, cropping, aquaculture, and off-road vehicle use.

Status of [[Loach Minnow]] within the [[Blue River]] system is poorly known. Past surveys have been spotty and no trends can be discerned. Ongoing studies by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and Paul Marsh of [[Arizona State University]] for the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] are expected to provide a more complete picture of the species' status in the Blue River.

The status of [[Loach Minnow]] is declining rangewide. Although currently listed as threatened, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has found it warrants uplisting to endangered status. A reclassification proposal is pending but precluded due to work on other higher priority listing actions. The need for reclassification is based upon increases in serious threats to a large portion of its habitat rather than documented declines in the species itself.

Only two other formal consultations have been conducted on effects of Federal actions on [[Loach Minnow]] in the [[Blue River]] basin. In May 1986, a biological opinion on the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] Land and Resources Management Plan concluded that implementation should provide net benefits to the species. In May 1993, a biological opinion concluded that the Campbell and Isabelle Timber Sales on [[Campbell Blue Creek]] would not jeopardize the species and would not adversely modify its proposed critical habitat.

The affected area is remote but subject to various human uses. Most watershed land is under U.S. Forest Service jurisdiction, with activities affecting [[Loach Minnow]] subject to Section 7 consultation. Recreation is light with generally minor river impact. Off-road and all-terrain vehicle use in the stream bottom causes some adverse impacts. Primary cumulative effects derive from private lands in the valley bottom on the upper [[Blue River]]: livestock grazing, cropping, and residential development remove water and add to system instability. An aquaculture operation feeds predatory nonnative fish species into the river and diverts water. Forest Road 281, located along the river for approximately 25 miles with substantial portions in the floodplain and several low-water ford crossings, is considered a major adverse impact on the [[Blue River]] aquatic and riparian ecosystems.

## Key Findings

**Project Description:** Emergency repair work was conducted February 7, 1995, consisting of smoothing road approaches to the crossing. Expected placement of boulders and rock into the stream crossing was not done due to high water levels. Sand and gravel materials extracted from the roadway were placed along the western edge well away from channel flow. Additional repair work would entail 1 to 2 days of work in late April or early May using a front-end loader and small bulldozer. The low-water crossing would be raised to evenly distribute water surface by placement of fill consisting primarily of boulders (2-4 feet diameter) and cobble (3-10 inches diameter). Fill would tie into existing riprap on streambanks.

**Direct and Indirect Effects:** The proposed action is expected to have localized adverse effects on [[Loach Minnow]] but not cause long-term population changes in the [[Blue River]]. [[Loach Minnow]] are susceptible to mortality when heavy equipment is used in the stream channel or at low-water crossings. Because eggs are fixed to rocks in shallow riffle areas, they are susceptible to crushing if equipment or vehicle use occurs during spring or fall spawning seasons. The species may be adversely affected by increased sediment deposition on the streambottom. Because of their benthic habit, [[Loach Minnow]] and their eggs are particularly vulnerable to substrate sedimentation.

Channel alteration may affect long-term configuration and stability of the stream channel and availability of various habitat types. Upstream and downstream changes in the stream channel normally result from any change in bed elevation and shape. Such changes would add to [[Blue River]] channel instability and may adversely affect quantity and quality of [[Loach Minnow]] habitat. However, the existing low-water crossing site is already highly modified and subject to repeated changes in bed elevation due to frequent repair. The proposed repair work is not substantially different from past repair work at this site and the area of direct modification is small. Although this alteration contributes cumulatively to overall stream channel and habitat degradation, it is not expected by itself to cause stream channel changes resulting in unacceptably high adverse impacts.

During repair work, potential exists for introduction of toxic substances such as petroleum products into the stream. This potential is expected to be minimal given provisions for refueling and other staging removed from the river or other flowing water.

**Biological Opinion Conclusion:** After reviewing current status of [[Loach Minnow]], the environmental baseline for the action area, direct and indirect effects of the proposed repair, and cumulative effects, the Service's biological opinion concludes that implementation of the FR 475 repair as proposed is not likely to jeopardize the continued existence of [[Loach Minnow]] or to destroy or adversely modify designated critical habitat.

**Incidental Take Statement:** The Service anticipates the proposed repair will result in incidental take of [[Loach Minnow]] through direct mortality and through indirect mortality due to habitat loss or alteration. [[Loach Minnow]] or eggs present in the work area may be crushed or stranded during heavy equipment operation or poisoned by accidental introduction of toxic substances. Indirect take would also occur through destruction or alteration of habitat resulting from substrate disturbance and channel modification and from sedimentation and erosion as byproducts of long-term channel morphology changes.

Anticipated level of incidental take cannot be directly quantified due to low level of data on the [[Loach Minnow]] population in the area and inability to predict long-term project effects. Because of their small size and benthic habitat and due to river current, it is unlikely that [[Loach Minnow]] or eggs killed as a result of the proposed project will be observed. Therefore, anticipated levels of take are indexed to the total fish community and habitat.

**Take Limits:** Anticipated take will be considered exceeded if at any time during project activities: (1) more than 20 dead fish of any species are found in the project area or within 500 yards downstream, (2) any spill of toxic materials occurs in the [[Blue River]] or its floodplain during project implementation, or (3) loss of all [[Loach Minnow]] for 25 feet on either side of the ford crossing centerline occurs.

**Reasonable and Prudent Measures:** Four objectives are specified: (1) conduct all proposed actions to minimize direct mortality of [[Loach Minnow]], (2) conduct all proposed actions to minimize loss and alteration of [[Loach Minnow]] habitat, (3) monitor the fish community and habitat to document levels of incidental take, and (4) maintain complete and accurate records of actions which may result in take of [[Loach Minnow]] and their habitat.

**Terms and Conditions:** Mandatory requirements include: minimizing activities within the wetted channel; ensuring no pollutants enter surface waters; limiting channel alteration and heavy equipment use to within 25 linear feet perpendicular to the crossing centerline and existing roadbed; minimizing damage to or loss of riparian vegetation; monitoring for dead or dying fish in and for 500 yards downstream of the project area; immediate notification to the Service upon detection of more than 20 dead or dying fish of any species; and submission of a written report within 60 days after project completion documenting the project as implemented, including photographs before and after, and discussion of compliance with terms and conditions.
