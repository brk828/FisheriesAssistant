## Executive Summary

This document compiles internal U.S. Forest Service (Coconino National Forest) meeting notes (19 August 1994) and expert correspondence (Dr. W.L. Minckley, Arizona State University) regarding the relicensing of the Childs-Irving Hydroelectric Project on **[[Fossil Creek]]**, Arizona. The core scientific issue is evaluating four instream flow alternatives and their effects on native fish habitat, with particular concern that the standard **[[Instream Flow Incremental Methodology (IFIM)]]** study conducted by contractor CH2M Hill failed to account for **[[Travertine Deposition]]** dynamics and resulting channel morphology changes. The assembled fisheries biologists (AGFD, USFWS, USFS) reached consensus that natural baseflows (Alternative IV: 43 cfs/43 cfs) would maximize habitat heterogeneity, invertebrate production, and benefit the four native "Candidate 2" fish species inhabiting the creek: **[[Roundtail Chub]]**, **[[Sonora Sucker]]**, **[[Desert Sucker]]**, and **[[Longfin Dace]]**. Dr. Minckley's independent review strongly endorsed decommissioning/delicensing the hydroelectric project entirely, citing the rarity of free-flowing, travertine-forming spring systems in Arizona and the conservation value for imperiled native fishes, including potential future reintroduction of **[[Razorback Sucker]]** and **[[Spikedace]]**.

## Background & Description

Species-specific trophic morphology noted by the CH2M Hill contractor and corroborated by Jerry Stefferud (Tonto NF):

- **[[Sonora Sucker]]** (*Catostomus insignis*): Possesses **soft, fleshy mouths** adapted for sucking up fine sand and detritus from substrate; feeds predominantly on sand with occasional dabbling on boulders.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Catostomus clarkii*): Possesses a **cartilaginous ridge** on the mouth suited for scraping attached algae and periphyton off boulders and smooth bedrock surfaces; nocturnal riffle forager.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*): No specialized scraping/sucking morphology described; exhibits an **opportunistic drift-feeding** strategy, positioning in quiet water adjacent to current to dart out and capture entrained prey — analogous to a facultative predator/insectivore body plan suited for holding position in pools while sallying into faster water.

Habitat substrate context: Fossil Creek's channel is dominated by **large boulder and bedrock habitat units** (upper reach: 35% pool, 40% boulder run, 25% bedrock riffle; lower reach: 30% pool, 60% boulder run, 10% bedrock run), a structural template that constrains how incremental flow changes translate into usable fish habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Interspecific feeding relationships (commensal/facilitative dynamics):**
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** was observed to follow foraging suckers and feed on food items (invertebrates, detritus) disturbed by sucker feeding activity — a form of **positive interspecific facilitation** analogous to opportunistic drift-feeding behavior recorded for the species in other streams.
- **[[Sonora Sucker]]** and **[[Desert Sucker]]** partition food resources spatially and by substrate type despite habitat overlap, reducing direct competition (sand/detritus vs. attached algae/periphyton).

**Flow-dependent habitat limitations (current low-flow baseline, 0.2–2 cfs conditions):**
- Riffle/shallow macroinvertebrate-producing areas are limited in areal extent and flow heterogeneity, constraining food availability for chub.
- Pool current velocities are very low (<0.5 ft/sec), limiting drift-food delivery into pools.

**Predicted effects of increased/natural flows (Alternative IV, 43 cfs):**
- Expansion of riffle area and food-producing microhabitats would substantially enhance roundtail chub feeding opportunity via greater insect entrainment.
- Elevated flows would enhance desert sucker nocturnal riffle-scraping foraging by expanding riffle habitat.
- Sonora sucker feeding likely enhanced by higher flows as well, though it is characterized as less strictly riffle-dependent than desert sucker, which is described as "riffle-oriented," preferring fast, turbulent water over rough substrate.

**Travertine-mediated habitat architecture (Upper Reach):**
- **[[Travertine Deposition]]** is a keystone geomorphic process in Fossil Creek, driven by degassing of CO2-rich spring water and precipitation of calcium carbonate.
- At natural baseflow (Alternative IV), travertine dam formation is maximized, fragmenting large boulder/bedrock habitat units into smaller, more heterogeneous mainstem pools — increasing habitat complexity, cover, organic/inorganic sediment retention, and overwinter rearing habitat.
- At reduced flow (10 cfs, Alternative II), only laminar travertine coating (not dam formation) is expected, due to reduced velocity/turbulence; travertine setbacks from flood scour may increase due to lower structural resilience.
- Potential risks of travertine dams: creation of upstream passage barriers and cementation of spawning gravels.

**Connectivity and invasion risk:**
- Fossil Creek's connectivity to the **[[Verde River]]** is a management concern: increased flows could facilitate upstream invasion by non-native fishes from the Verde River (potentially mitigated via a barrier), while Minckley argues the creek's high gradient and flash-flood regime will naturally resist long-term non-native establishment.
- Concern also raised regarding increased angler access/temptation for illegal non-native gamefish introductions under higher-flow scenarios.

**Lower Reach dynamics:**
- Little to no travertine deposition expected regardless of alternative; habitat quality driven mainly by surface area, depth, and velocity changes.
- Large, low-complexity boulder-run habitat units in the lower reach may specifically benefit **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (an endangered species with potential for enhancement/reintroduction).

## Key Findings

- **IFIM Methodology Rejected as Inadequate**: The multidisciplinary team and independent reviewer Dr. Minckley concurred that standard IFIM (weighted usable area) modeling has little applicability to Fossil Creek because it does not account for dynamic channel morphology changes driven by travertine deposition; IFIM results showed a counterintuitive inflection at 10 cfs with *decreasing* weighted usable area toward natural baseflow (43 cfs), which the team distrusted as an artifact of the model's assumptions.
- **All four extant native fishes are Candidate 2 species** (roundtail chub, Sonora sucker, desert sucker, longfin dace), and management consensus favored natural baseflow restoration as the option best supporting these populations, in the absence of identified limiting factors elsewhere in the system.
- **Four Flow Alternatives Evaluated**: (1) No Action, 0.2/2 cfs; (2) Proposed Action, 10/5 cfs; (3) blended alternative, 43/5 cfs; (4) Full natural restoration, 43/43 cfs — with Alternative IV judged most ecologically beneficial by both the interagency team and Dr. Minckley.
- **Travertine deposition is flow-dependent and non-linear**: reduced flows (10 cfs = ~23% of natural baseflow) may either lengthen or shorten the zone of travertine influence depending on whether reduced turbulence keeps calcium carbonate in suspension longer, versus simply reducing the total carbonate available for precipitation — an unresolved uncertainty acknowledged by the team.
- **Interspecific feeding facilitation observed**: roundtail chub benefits from foraging activity of co-occurring sucker species, a subtle community-ecology dynamic relevant to predicting how habitat changes cascade across the native fish assemblage.
- **Broader conservation significance**: Dr. Minckley emphasized Fossil Creek as one of very few remaining free-flowing, indigenous-fish-supporting, spring-fed travertine ecosystems in Arizona, with potential to support reintroduction of **[[Spikedace]]** (Threatened) and **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (Endangered) in addition to its four resident Candidate 2 species, and recommended full delicensing of the Childs-Irving Hydroelectric Project as the scientifically and ethically preferable outcome.
