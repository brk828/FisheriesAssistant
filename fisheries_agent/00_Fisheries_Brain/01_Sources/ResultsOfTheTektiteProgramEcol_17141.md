# Observations on Cleaner Shrimps of the Genus Periclimenes

#marine-biology #crustacean-ecology #Palaemonidae #cleaning-symbiosis #coral-reef-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study documents the distribution, abundance, and cleaning behavior of [[Periclimenes]] shrimps in [[Lameshur Bay]], [[Virgin Islands]], conducted during the [[Tektite I]] saturation diving mission (February 15–April 15, 1969). The most abundant cleaner shrimp was [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] (Pederson cleaning shrimp), followed by [[Periclimenes yucatanicus]] (spotted cleaner). Both species were almost invariably associated with the anemone [[Bartholomea annulata]]. Five distinct physiognomic reef zones were surveyed: back reef, back reef canyon, reef front, unvegetated sand strip, and algal plain. The anemone [[Bartholomea annulata]] was equally abundant across habitats, but shrimp density per anemone varied significantly by zone. [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] reached highest densities on the unvegetated sand strip (3.30 shrimp per anemone), interpreted as a response to high fish traffic and cleaning opportunities rather than physical habitat preference. Population monitoring of 23 marked anemones over 25 days revealed significant temporal variation in shrimp numbers, contradicting the hypothesis that these shrimps are strictly territorial. Repopulation experiments showed slow recolonization rates (~1 shrimp per 3.5 days) after removal. The study provides insights into cleaning symbiosis ecology and speculates on the evolutionary pathway from simple commensalism to obligate cleaning behavior.

## Background & Description

### Study Context and Methods

The research was conducted from the [[Tektite Habitat]], a manned underwater laboratory enabling saturation diving techniques that eliminated depth and time constraints of conventional SCUBA. The study area encompassed five distinct physiognomic zones within swimming distance of the habitat, all surveyed completely at least once during the 60-day mission.

### Survey Zones

**Back Reef (Plot #1):** Located in relatively shallow water (13 m depth) between the Tektite Habitat and beach bedrock outcrop. Extended 65 m across reef in a 3.3 m corridor. Characterized by scattered coral boulders, rubble, profuse alcyonarians, exposed carbonate sand, and diverse hard corals corresponding to Goreau's (1959) "cervicornis" zone.

**Canyon (Plot #2):** North-south re-entrant containing the Tektite Habitat. Steep-sided near habitat (rising 1-2 m above floor), becoming diffuse at south end. Moderate herbivorous fish grazing kept corridor clean of algae.

**Reef Front (Plot #3):** Deep seaward reef face typical of deep Caribbean reefs but lacking intermediate buttress zone and shallow [[Acropora palmata]] stands (attributed to low-energy conditions in [[Lameshur Bay]]). Dominated by [[Montastrea annularis]] but no single hard coral species clearly dominant.

**Unvegetated Sand Strip (Plot #4):** Averaging 10 m wide, separating reef from vegetated plain. This persistent feature results from grazing activity of herbivorous reef fishes; strip width corresponds to maximum distance these fish venture from reef shelter. Using aerial photographs, Kumpf and Randall (1961) delineated this sand strip along more than half of St. John's 58-mile coastline. Sharpness of seaward border most distinct where reef borders sea grasses ([[Thalassia]], [[Syringodium]]) and where reef is well-developed.

**Algal Plain (Plot #5):** Extensive sand flats occupying major portion of [[Lameshur Bay]] seaward of reef wall. Primary vegetation: [[Thalassia]], [[Udotea]], [[Syringodium]], with patches of [[Penicillus]] and [[Halimeda]].

### Species Descriptions

**[[Periclimenes pedersoni]]:** Small shrimp with maximum carapace length ~6 cm. Distribution: Bahamas, Virgin Islands, Antigua, Florida. Inhabits areas protected from violent surge along coral-sand boundary, particularly near prominent underwater points or passes between coral heads. Everywhere except west Florida associates with [[Bartholomea annulata]], hanging directly on annulated tentacles or occupying same hole. Bright coloration makes it conspicuous against drab anemone background. Best termed ectocommensal (no evidence of benefit to anemone).

**[[Periclimenes yucatanicus]]:** Smaller species (<3 cm long). Widely distributed from Colombia to southern Florida. In Bahamas, Puerto Rico, and Virgin Islands found associated with [[Bartholomea annulata]] and [[Condylactis gigantea]], either nestling in anemone or occupying same crevice. Also observed with two other anemones and the flat-belled medusa [[Cassiopeia]] sp. (1-3 individuals in tentacles). Has limited ability to assume background coloration matching host coelenterate (normally clear/transparent on [[Bartholomea annulata]], darker green on green anemones, darker on [[Cassiopeia]]).

**[[Periclimenes rathbunae]]:** Drab colored species observed on several anemone species but never cleaning or attempting to attract fish. Moved with impunity among tentacles; when disturbed, burrowed deeper until almost completely hidden. At depth appeared transparent brown peppered with black spots, well camouflaged in anemone. Positive identification awaits further study (possibly two undescribed species involved, per F.A. Chace Jr.).

**[[Periclimenes americanus]]:** One of most common species in tropical American waters. Two individuals observed picking through sand at base of [[Bartholomea annulata]] beneath protective umbrella of extended tentacles but not touching anemone. May be more than casual visitor (F.A. Chace Jr. also observed this association during Smithsonian-Bredin Caribbean expeditions).

**[[Stenopus hispidus]]:** Red banded coral shrimp, largest of known cleaner shrimps. Frequented reef proper. Despite many specimens present, infrequently observed cleaning fishes; importance as cleaners considerably less than [[Periclimenes]].

**[[Stenopus scutellatus]]:** Smaller species observed most often in coral boulders on algal plains. Unlike Bahamas occurrence, no extensive eel grass beds in near vicinity. Infrequently observed cleaning fishes.

**Unidentified [[Thor]] species:** Found regularly in small numbers on certain anemones, including [[Bartholomea annulata]] and several unidentified anemones (one with extremely powerful nematocysts). Lived among tentacles or clinging to anemone sides.

### Host Anemone: [[Bartholomea annulata]]

Associated with complex community of organisms (mostly crustaceans), including at least four [[Periclimenes]] species. Interrelations between organisms in this microcosm poorly known, but many probably associate for protection against predators and (for cleaner shrimp) for platform easily recognized by soliciting fishes. Usually attached to hard substrate buried in mud or fine sand from just below low tide mark to at least 23 m depth. Numerically dominant solitary anemone in [[Lameshur Bay]], most often attached to base of coral heads or buried objects (dead coral, empty conch shells).

Other associates noted by Chace (1958) and observed in present study: red mysid [[Heteromysis actinae]]; purple snapping shrimp [[Alpheus armatus]]; hermit crabs; arrow crab [[Stenorynchus seticornis]]; two fishes ([[Paraclinus]] sp. and an apogonid).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Anemone Distribution Patterns

Back reef and reef front contained greatest species diversity and abundance of anemones due to solid substrate availability. [[Bartholomea annulata]] was most common single species, about equally abundant in all areas except algal flat. In canyon, back reef, and reef front, [[Bartholomea annulata]] constituted 43-65% of all anemones. On sand strip and algal flat (lacking suitable hard substrate for other anemones), [[Bartholomea annulata]] became even more dominant: 87% and 74% of all anemones, respectively.

Abundance per 100 m²:
- **Back reef:** 12 [[Bartholomea annulata]], 16 other anemones
- **Canyon:** 10 [[Bartholomea annulata]], 13 other anemones  
- **Reef front:** 13 [[Bartholomea annulata]], 17 other anemones
- **Sand strip:** 13 [[Bartholomea annulata]], 2 other anemones
- **Algal flat:** 8 [[Bartholomea annulata]], 3 other anemones

### Shrimp Distribution and Habitat Preferences

**[[Periclimenes pedersoni]]:** Most abundant cleaner shrimp per unit area on sand strip and reef front. Large populations maintained at base of reef despite decrease in available anemones compared to reef proper. Shrimp per [[Bartholomea annulata]]:
- Back reef: 1.68
- Canyon: 1.15
- Reef front: 1.12
- **Sand strip: 3.30** (highest)
- Algal flat: 2.47

Statistical tests showed significant difference (P = 0.05) in average number per [[Bartholomea annulata]] between survey areas.

**[[Periclimenes yucatanicus]]:** Most abundant on algal plain. Occurred most often in habitats characterized by isolated coral boulders on sand substrate, not as often in sand strip, seldom on main reef. Greatest abundance on algal flat, especially northern part adjacent to sand strip portion where [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] also concentrated. Shrimp per [[Bartholomea annulata]]:
- Back reef: 0.11
- Canyon: 0.08
- Reef front: 0.00
- Sand strip: 0.20
- **Algal flat: 0.72** (highest)

### Host Preference and Competition

Both species showed high degree of preference for [[Bartholomea annulata]] but neither exclusive to this anemone. Frequency of occurrence on [[Bartholomea annulata]] varied significantly between tracts (chi-square, P = 0.05), dependent on availability of other anemones and competition for space.

**[[Periclimenes pedersoni]] on [[Bartholomea annulata]]:**
- Reef front: 59% occurrence (45% of available individuals occupied)
- Sand strip: 95% occurrence (83% of available individuals occupied)

Where other anemones scarce and shrimp abundant (sand strip), competition for space greatest, resulting in higher occupancy rate of [[Bartholomea annulata]].

### Fish Traffic and Cleaning Station Success

[[Periclimenes pedersoni]] most abundant in sand strip where fish traffic greatest. Greatest cleaning activity and diversity of host fish observed in northern sand strip portion where large localized populations occurred on certain anemones. Large shrimp colonies invariably coincided with popularity of anemone as cleaning station for fish.

Reef stations attract smaller, more territorial fish species; frequented less by large fishes unable/unwilling to work into crevices between coral boulders where anemone stations located. Some reef-dwelling [[Bartholomea annulata]] located in sand-filled cracks atop living [[Montastrea annularis]] (good vantage points) but contained few cleaner shrimp.

**Midwater fishes** ([[Ocyurus chrysurus]] yellowtail snapper, [[Caranx ruber]] bar jack) rarely observed soliciting cleaning from [[Periclimenes]] (single instance each). These fishes serviced by cleaner fishes ([[Bodianus rufus]] Spanish hogfish, [[Thalassoma bifasciatum]] bluehead). Sessile shrimp tend to clean fishes in open areas of good accessibility; cleaner fishes more successful soliciting clients in water column above main reef areas.

Both cleaner shrimp species concentrated near reef tip jutting into bay, intercepting large expanse of algal flat. Moving fishes encountering reef barrier and fishes dependent on reef for protection find natural corridor for movement in space above sand strip. Shrimp seek anemones where fish traffic greatest—in sand strip.

**Interpretation:** Abundance of [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] varies from low on reef face to high on sand strip, probably representing response to availability of fish hosts (food) rather than habitat change or ecotone. Local concentrations of fishes may be dependent on cleaners for well-being, but no conclusive evidence that abundance of [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] in sand strip is more than opportunistic aggregation in response to abundant fish hosts.

**Visual cues:** Dark-colored [[Bartholomea annulata]] contrasts with light sandy substrate, presenting good focal point for potential fish clients. Visual cue heightened by lashing, light-colored shrimp antennae against dark anemone background, which contrasts with large interspaces of cleanly grazed sand.

### Sand Strip Formation and Ecological Significance

Unvegetated sand strip results from grazing activity of herbivorous reef fishes. Strip width probably coincides with maximum distance these fish venture from reef shelter. Bare sand zone also seen as concentric bands around patch reefs. Sharpness of seaward border most distinct where reef bordered by sea grasses ([[Thalassia]], [[Syringodium]]). Bare zone most distinct where adjoining reef best developed—where reef massive and well-developed (affording cover for more grazing fish), outer border of sand strip distinct. Where reef fragmentary or composed of scattered coral boulders and rubble, sand strip becomes diffuse and difficult to recognize.

**Importance:** Sand strip contained largest population of Pederson cleaner shrimp among five surveyed areas. Fish activity probably primary factor determining abundance of [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] in sand strip. Sand strip seen as natural corridor for fish movement along reef.

## Key Findings

### Population Dynamics and Territoriality

**Monitoring Study Design:** Populations of [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] on 23 individual [[Bartholomea annulata]] in unvegetated sand strip monitored seven times over 25-day period (March 2-26). Each anemone marked with numbered styrofoam cube tied to sandbag with nylon string. Anemones distributed in sand strip from reef front adjacent to Habitat south to within 40 m of south canyon.

**Methodology:** Shrimp fearless and easily enticed to browse along diver's hand. Routine during each survey: count and measure with calipers total body length of each living shrimp on anemone without harming or removing shrimp.

**Key Result—Population Variation:** Analysis of variance showed **significant variation (P = 0.05)** in numbers of [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] on individual anemones between surveys and in total population between surveys. This contradicts theory that [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] is territorial, remaining for extended periods within meter or less of same spot (Limbaugh et al., 1961).

**Pattern of Variation:** Although statistically significant, variation generally limited to moderate changes in shrimp number per anemone. Anemones with large numbers (7-11 shrimp) consistently contained abundant populations. Anemones with few shrimp (0-3) remained sparsely populated throughout surveys. [[Periclimenes yucatanicus]] and [[Periclimenes rathbunae]] found infrequently but recurrently on same anemones.

**Interpretation:** Relatively low abundance of [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] in all but sand strip area and significant variation in population size with time suggests that movements in population that do occur are confined primarily to unvegetated sand strip.

### Repopulation Experiments

**Experimental Design:** All shrimp removed from four anemones in sand strip on March 30; anemones visited daily thereafter to determine repopulation rate.

**Results:** Repopulation slow at all removal sites despite abundance of shrimp on nearby anemones. Anemones resettled at rate of **~1 shrimp per 3.5 days**.

**Example (Site 1):** 10 shrimp removed from large [[Bartholomea annulata]] centrally located in area of scattered coral boulders. Within 6 m radius remained 36 [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] and 2 [[Periclimenes yucatanicus]] occupying 11 anemones. After nearly two weeks, only 3 [[Periclimenes pedersoni]] had settled on depopulated anemone.

**Fish Response:** Fishes observed near first anemone on first day but none attempted to solicit cleaning. No soliciting fishes observed to loiter at any removal sites after second day.

### Factors Contributing to Population Variation

**Sampling Error:** Difficult to see smaller transparent shrimp when located on sand around anemone base. Counting error may account for part of observed variation.

**Agonistic Behavior:** In large aggregations, larger individuals chased smaller shrimp from anemone. Displaced small shrimp took up residence on nearby vantage point (sponge, coral) or on sand near anemone. Small shrimp on sand away from contrasting brown anemone especially difficult to see. At removal site 4 (26 shrimp), population so large it scattered about adjoining coral boulder with ~half individuals occupying nearby branching sponge.

**Inadvertent Translocation:** Shrimp seen to "ride" fish clients when disturbed during cleaning; this inadvertent dislocation via fleeing fish may account for some movement to/from anemones.

**Recruitment:** Ovigerous females
