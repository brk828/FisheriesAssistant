# Arizona Hydrobiidae (Prosobranchia: Rissoacea)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #hydrobiidae

## Executive Summary

This Smithsonian Contributions to Zoology monograph (No. 459, 1988) by [[Robert Hershler]] and [[J. Jerry Landye]] provides the first systematic revision of the freshwater snail family Hydrobiidae in Arizona. The authors recognize 14 species in two genera, [[Pyrgulopsis]] (12 species, 11 of them new to science in this work, plus one new state record) and [[Tryonia]] (2 species, both new). The taxa are microsnails from isolated springs, many of them restricted to a single spring. The study combines shell and anatomical morphometrics with [[Stepwise Discriminant Analysis]] to test species distinctions. No fish are treated; the work is a malacological (non-fish) taxonomic document, so the ichthyology and marine tags reflect the template only.

## Background & Description

- **Group:** Prosobranch snails of the family Hydrobiidae, superfamily Rissooidea, a diverse component of the inland aquatic fauna of the [[American Southwest]].
- **Conservation status:** 12 of 21 southwestern hydrobiid species then under federal consideration for listing as threatened or endangered (USDI 1984) were undescribed, underlining the urgency of systematic work.
- **Prior knowledge:** No modern comprehensive treatment existed. Before 1969 the only live-collected Arizona record was a single spring in the Huachuca Mountains (Pilsbry and Ferriss 1909, *Paludestrina* sp.). The junior author surveyed Arizona springs for about 18 years.
- **Material base:** Specimens came from 38 localities in Arizona plus one in Sonora, Mexico (Ojo Caliente, 44 km S of Nogales). Material comprised 48 lots, mostly over 100 specimens each, housed in the [[United States National Museum]] (USNM) and compared with Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia (ANSP) material.
- **Methods:** Shells and animals were measured at x50 under dissecting microscopes; SEM was used for shells, opercula, radulae and penial morphology. Shell characters included number of whorls, shell height and width, aperture dimensions and whorl ratios. Anatomical characters included penis, penial filament, [[Bursa Copulatrix]], seminal receptacle and ctenidium measures.
- **Sample design:** Shell series of n = 7 to 11 and anatomical series of n = 5 per population, drawn from [[Topotype]] and other populations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Localities include spring pools, stream outflows and [[Cienega]] wetlands, from small seeps to [[Montezuma Well]], a limnocrene about 112 m long and 12 m (as given in the text) across.
- **Microhabitat partitioning:** *Pyrgulopsis* typically occurs on rock or aquatic macrophytes in moderate current, usually at headsprings and upper outflows. *Tryonia* is restricted to soft sediment in still water.
- **Biogeography:** Many taxa are narrowly distributed, several are single-spring endemics, and the genera are allopatric. Regional sites include the Virgin River drainage (Mohave County, AZ and Washington County, UT), the Verde Valley and the Gila River between Bylas and Pima.
- **Threats:** The authors attribute the decline of these snails to deterioration or disappearance of natural aquatic habitats, the basis for federal listing consideration.
- **Related ecological literature cited:** [[Cole and Barry 1973]] on Montezuma Well, [[Hendrickson and Minckley 1985]] on cienegas, and [[Williams et al. 1985]] on endangered aquatic ecosystems and vanishing fishes of North American deserts.

## Key Findings

- **Species count:** 14 species in two genera. Thirteen are new or, in the case of *P. deserta*, a new state record (originally described from Utah). Twelve *Pyrgulopsis* species are new to science in the full sense; *P. deserta* is previously described.
- **New taxa:** *P. glandulosus*, *P. bacchus*, *P. conicus*, *P. morrisoni*, *P. montezumensis*, *P. solus*, *P. simplex*, *P. confluentis*, *P. sancarlosensis*, *P. cochisi*, *P. thompsoni*, *Tryonia gilae* and *T. quitobaquitae*.
- **Diagnostic character:** Penial morphology (filament shape, number of [[Glandular Ridges]], accessory crests, lobe form) is the most useful character for separating species. Male anatomy gave a better separation than shell data.
- **Classification accuracy (grouped topotypes):** correct classification was 89% overall for male shell data, 93% for female shell data, and 100% for analyses using anatomical data (as given in the text). Ungrouped specimens classified much less reliably, with ranges from 0 to 70% depending on the dataset.
- **Discriminant analysis:** Shell functions explained 68.8% (male) and 78.7% (female) of total variance; anatomical functions explained 71.2% and 67.6%. Closest groups *P. simplex* and *P. conicus* were not significantly separated for female shell data.
- **Key morphological notes:** *P. glandulosus* has a large ovate shell (2.0 to 2.8 mm) with a massive penis and multiple glandular ridges. *P. solus* has the smallest penis (about 12% of body length). *P. sancarlosensis* lacks the penial lobe. *Tryonia* has an enlarged glandular penial lobe and two lobes on the inner curvature, and females are ovoviviparous.
- **Systematic conclusion:** The authors support division of Arizona *Pyrgulopsis* into 12 species, with each differentiated by discriminant equations.
- **Addendum (after publication):** Based on [[Taylor 1987]], *Tryonia gilae* Hershler is a junior primary homonym, *P. morrisoni* and *P. cochisi* are junior synonyms of named taxa of *Fontelicella* and *Apachecoccus* (as given in the text). Revised names are therefore required.
- **Generic status:** *Hyalopyrgus* is treated as a synonym of *Tryonia*. A detailed generic description is provided by [[Hershler and Thompson 1987]].
