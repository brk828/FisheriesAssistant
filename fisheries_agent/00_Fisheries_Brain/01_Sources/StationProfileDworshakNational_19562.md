# Dworshak National Fish Hatchery — Station Profile (FY 1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #FisheriesManagement #Aquaculture

## Executive Summary

[[Dworshak National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH), operated by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in Region One, is located at the confluence of the North Fork Clearwater River and the main stem [[Clearwater River]] near Ahsahka, Idaho — one mile downstream of [[Dworshak Dam]]. Constructed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and completed in 1969, the hatchery was built as mitigation for the loss of the native "B" strain [[Steelhead Trout]] run in the North Fork Clearwater following dam construction. It is described as the world's largest producer of summer steelhead. The facility also functions as a donor egg station for the [[Salmon River]] drainage, supplies [[Rainbow Trout]] to [[Dworshak Reservoir]], and, since a 1982 expansion under the Lower Snake River Compensation Plan (LSRCP), rears [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] for restoration in the Clearwater River drainage. The station forms an administrative complex with the [[Kooskia National Fish Hatchery]] and co-locates the Dworshak Fish Health Center and the Idaho Fishery Resource Office.

## Background & Description

The source text is a station/production profile rather than a biological morphology study, so direct morphometric data on the species is not provided. Notable biologically relevant details include:

- **"B" strain steelhead**: A genetically and life-history distinct run of anadromous [[Steelhead Trout]] native to the North Fork Clearwater River, historically threatened with extinction by the construction of Dworshak Dam. This strain is the founding broodstock rationale for the hatchery's existence.
- **Life stages managed**: Eggs (incubated in vertical stack trays), fry/fingerlings (nursery tanks), smolts (steelhead and spring chinook), sub-catchable and yearling rainbow trout, and returning adults (used for broodstock/spawning).
- **Production size metrics** (FY1993): Steelhead trout released as 2,388,993 fish totaling 323,308 lbs (~0.135 lb/fish average); Rainbow trout released as 195,760 fish totaling 9,732 lbs (~0.05 lb/fish, sub-catchable size); Spring chinook salmon released as 467,222 fish totaling 27,384 lbs (~0.059 lb/fish).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Anadromous life history**: [[Steelhead Trout]] and [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] are anadromous, migrating from the Clearwater River drainage to the Pacific Ocean via the Snake and Columbia Rivers, and returning as adults to the hatchery/river system to spawn — linking this freshwater hatchery directly to North Pacific ocean-basin ecology.
- **Resident population support**: [[Rainbow Trout]] are non-anadromous and stocked directly into [[Dworshak Reservoir]] to support a resident sport fishery, filling a lentic reservoir niche created by the impoundment of the North Fork Clearwater River behind Dworshak Dam.
- **Mitigation ecology**: The hatchery exists specifically to compensate for anadromous fish habitat lost to the construction of [[Dworshak Dam]], which blocked natural upstream spawning and rearing habitat in the North Fork Clearwater River.
- **Water source ecology**: The hatchery draws from multiple thermal/water-quality regimes — Dworshak Reservoir releases (via dam selector gates), a North Fork pump station (75,000 gpm capacity), and a "clean" gravity-flow pipeline (6,400 gpm, added 1992) used to protect early life stages from Infectious Hematopoietic Necrosis (IHN) virus.
- **Predation pressure management**: Overhead netting (~130,000 sq ft) was engineered to exclude avian predators from rearing ponds, indicating bird predation as a significant ecological pressure on hatchery-reared juveniles.
- **Water quality challenges historically included**: supersaturated nitrogen gas exposure (mitigated via "Koch" ring column degassers) and soft water mineral deficiency (corrected with sodium chloride/potassium chloride supplementation), both of which affected fish health and survival in the water-reuse system.

## Key Findings

- **Mitigation targets**: Adult return goals to the Clearwater River are 20,000 steelhead and 9,000 spring chinook. Steelhead goals are consistently met; spring chinook returns remain well below mitigation targets.
- **Production capacity**: DNFH capacity is 2.3 million steelhead smolts, 1.4 million yearling chinook smolts, and 300,000 sub-catchable rainbow trout, with an annual production capacity exceeding 550,000 lbs of fish.
- **Operational reforms (mid-1970s)**: In response to failure to meet production/mitigation goals, the hatchery removed a computerized pneumatic feed system, eliminated UV water treatment, redesigned water flow toward maximized single-pass use, adopted cooler water temperature selection from Dworshak Reservoir, added mineral supplementation to soft water, and removed supersaturated nitrogen gas — collectively improving fish culture outcomes.
- **Technological innovations developed on-site**: 
  - "Koch" ring column degassers for aeration/nitrogen gas protection.
  - The "Dworshak hatching jar," enabling fry to swim directly into nursery tanks post-incubation.
  - Demand feeders, which performed well enough in trials to be adopted hatchery-wide for steelhead production ponds.
  - An overhead net-enclosure system protecting ~130,000 sq ft of rearing area from bird predation.
- **Fish distribution data (FY1991–FY1993)**:

| Fiscal Year | Spring Chinook Salmon (No. / lbs) | Steelhead Trout (No. / lbs) | Rainbow Trout (No. / lbs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FY1991 | 2,450,618 / 78,151 | 2,656,076 / 431,286 | 156,814 / 16,095 |
| FY1992 | 1,564,199 / 93,752 | 2,309,052 / 429,623 | 246,212 / 9,089 |
| FY1993 | 467,222 / 27,384 | 2,388,993 / 323,308 | 195,760 / 9,732 |

  Notably, spring chinook salmon numbers released dropped sharply from FY1991 (~2.45M) to FY1993 (~467K), a decline of roughly 81%, consistent with the stated shortfall against mitigation goals.
- **Facilities inventory**: 84 recirculating Burrows ponds (17'×75') for steelhead, 128 inside nursery tanks (3'×16'), 58 vertical stack egg incubators (16-tray), nine adult holding ponds (17'×75', five modified into twelve 8'×75' raceways for rainbow trout), and thirty 8'×80' concrete raceways for spring chinook salmon.
- **Funding structure**: Steelhead and rainbow trout production is funded by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers (via Walla Walla District); spring chinook salmon production is funded through the Lower Snake River Compensation Plan (LSRCP). FY1993 operations budget was $1,719K with 21.2 FTE staff.
- **Workforce/training legacy**: Since 1977, youth employment programs (YACC, YCC) have engaged over 180 teenagers; over 30 professional fishery biologists began their careers at DNFH through the Service's employee career development program, making it a significant training pipeline within the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Public engagement**: Over 30,000 visitors annually, supported by senior citizen volunteers, guided/self-guided tours, and annual "Open House" events tied to National Fishing Week.
