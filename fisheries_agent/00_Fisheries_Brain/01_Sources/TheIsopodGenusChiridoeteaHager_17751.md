# Revision of the Isopod Genus Chiridotea Harger with a New Brackish-Water Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #idoteidae

*Note: this document concerns crustacean (isopod) taxonomy rather than fish. The tag above reflects the family under study.*

## Executive Summary

Tomas E. Bowman (U.S. National Museum, 1955) revised the valviferous isopod genus [[Chiridotea]] Harger, 1878, using specimens collected by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Shad Investigations (1937–1941). The paper gives an updated generic diagnosis, a key to the three recognized species, and describes a new species, *Chiridotea almyra*, from low-salinity waters. It also corrects several published mouthpart and anatomical accounts and reviews the nomenclature of the genus.

## Background & Description

The genus *Chiridotea* comprises small isopods known from the east coast of the United States and Canada, from Florida to Nova Scotia, on sandy bottoms. Its closest relative, [[Saduria]] Adams, differs in having a molar on the mandible, a five-segmented maxillipedal palp, and a much shorter uropod inner ramus; *Saduria* species are large and restricted to arctic and subarctic waters. The type species, by original designation, is *C. coeca* (Say).

Bowman notes that the name *Mesidotea* Richardson, 1905, commonly applied to this group, cannot be used because older available names exist (*Idotaea* Lockington, 1876, and *Saduria* Adams, 1852).

**Species recognized:**
- **[[Chiridotea Coeca]]** (Say, 1818): head lobes quadrate; antenna 2 only slightly longer than antenna 1; flagellum 5-segmented; length up to 13 mm excluding antennae.
- **[[Chiridotea Tuftsi]]** (Stimpson, 1883): antenna 2 more than twice antenna 1; flagellum 11–12 segmented; length 5–6 mm excluding antennae.
- **[[Chiridotea Almyra]]** n. sp.: head evenly rounded anterior to clefts; antenna 2 about twice antenna 1; flagellum 7–9 segmented; length 4.5–6.5 mm excluding antennae.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat of *C. coeca*:** sand bottoms, usually intertidal, occasionally to 17 fathoms. Range from Florida to Halifax, Nova Scotia; National Museum specimens extend south to Beaufort, North Carolina.
- **Habitat of *C. tuftsi*:** fine, uniform sand from low tide to 30 fathoms, generally deeper than *C. coeca* (Tait, 1927). Range from Long Island Sound to the Gulf of St. Lawrence. The type was dredged in 10 fathoms off Grand Manan Island, New Brunswick.
- **Habitat of *C. almyra*:** brackish water. Type locality at Willtown Bluff, Edisto River, S.C. (April 1, 1940). Also recorded from Kings Ferry, Ogeechee River, Ga., and two sites in the Hudson River, N.Y. (near Barrytown and Haverstraw). Salinity was not measured, but the sites lie well upstream of river mouths and are presumed to be of very low salinity.
- **Associated fauna:** Samples from Willtown Bluff and Kings Ferry contained euryhaline brackish-water taxa, including the copepod *Eurytemora hirundoides*, the amphipod *Corophium lacustre*, the isopod *Cyathura carinata*, and the polychaete *Scolelepides viridis*, along with freshwater copepods (*Osphranticum labronectum*, *Diaptomus*, *Cyclops*, *Macrocyclops*). Haverstraw samples included *Leptocheirus plumulosus*, *Corophium lacustre* and *Cyathura carinata*.
- **Feeding and behaviour:** The strictly marine *C. coeca* and *C. tuftsi* are scavengers that burrow just beneath the sand surface (Tait, 1927). Bowman presumes *C. almyra* lives similarly.

## Key Findings

- **Revised generic definition:** Mandible lacks a molar; inner lobe of maxilla 1 bears one long plumose seta and a minute seta; maxilliped palp three-segmented; pereiopods 1–5 of females bear oostegites; inner uropod ramus about half or a little more than half the outer ramus.
- **Maxilliped palp:** Bowman examined palps of *C. coeca* and *C. tuftsi* and found only three segments, contradicting Collinge (1918), who figured a four-segmented palp in *C. tuftsi*. Bowman attributes this to an anomalous specimen.
- **New species:** *Chiridotea almyra* is separated from congeners by its rounded, unlobed head, its pleotelson shape (sides nearly parallel for over half their length), and its propodus and dactyl setation. The holotype is an adult male of 5.8 mm (USNM no. 96960); the allotype is a female of 4.6 mm (USNM no. 96961); 44 paratypes are catalogued as USNM no. 96962.
- **Low-salinity restriction:** *C. almyra* appears restricted to low-salinity water and may enter fresh water, whereas the other two species are marine.
- **Eyes not observed:** Bowman could not find dorsal eyes reported for *C. coeca* and *C. tuftsi*, attributing this to preservative action, since Tait (1927) observed eyes in living animals.
- **Pleopod function:** Pleopods 1–2 and the exopod of pleopod 3 are natatory; the endopod of pleopod 3 and pleopods 4–5 are respiratory. This division occurs throughout *Chiridotea* and *Saduria*.
- **Nomenclature:** *Mesidotea* Richardson, 1905, is invalid because of older names; the genus should be called *Chiridotea* Harger, 1878.
