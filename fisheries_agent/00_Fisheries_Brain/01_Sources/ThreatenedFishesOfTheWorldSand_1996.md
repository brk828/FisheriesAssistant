# Threatened Fishes of the World: *Sandelia bainsii* Castelnau, 1861 (Anabantidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Anabantidae

## Executive Summary

*Sandelia bainsii*, the **Eastern Cape Rocky**, is a threatened anabantid fish endemic to a narrow geographic range in the Eastern Cape province of South Africa. Listed as **Vulnerable** in the South African Red Data Book, the species faces declining populations across its already restricted range due to habitat degradation, water abstraction, invasive alien species, and angling pressure. It is one of only two species in the genus [[Sandelia]], both endemic to South Africa, and is notable among the family [[Anabantidae]] for its reduced air-breathing organ relative to other members of the family.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy**: Genus *Sandelia*, family [[Anabantidae]] (climbing gouramies/labyrinth fishes); endemic to South Africa with only two described species: *S. bainsii* and [[Sandelia capensis]].
- **Diagnostic features**:
  - Smooth (non-spinous) edge to lower half of gill cover (shared with *S. capensis*).
  - Greatly reduced air-breathing organ compared to other Anabantidae.
  - Fin formula: Dorsal XV–XVI + 9–10; Anal VII–VIII + 9–10.
  - Lateral line (LL): 30–32 scales; 22 scales around the caudal peduncle.
  - Gill cover bears **2 spines** separated by a deep, membrane-covered notch.
  - Caudal fin truncate and tessellated.
  - Large mouth with sharp, conical teeth; large, forward-set eyes.
  - Coloration: olive to grey-brown with scattered flecks and spots; single grey bar behind the eye.
  - **Sexual dimorphism/breeding coloration**: Breeding males darken and develop visible **ctenii** on cheek scales (contact organs).
  - Maximum recorded size: **260 mm SL**.
  - Illustrated by Penny Meakin (adult male).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Rocky streams; the species wedges itself under rocks or other submerged objects, facing upstream to ambush prey — a **sedentary, sit-and-wait predator** strategy.
- **Diet**: Insects, crabs, and small fish.
- **Distribution**: Restricted to the Eastern Cape region of South Africa (32°34'–33°33'S; 26°06'–27°35'E), occurring in tributaries of the:
  - [[Buffalo River]]
  - [[Keiskamma River]] (including the [[Tyume River]] tributary)
  - [[Great Fish River]]
  - [[Kowie River]] (including the [[Bloukrans River]])
  - A small confirmed population also exists in the [[Nahoon River]].
- **Population status**:
  - Dwindling numbers across most known populations.
  - Kowie River system survey: only **6 specimens** found after 4 days of survey effort.
  - Former prime habitat in the Great Fish River now yields only introduced sharptooth catfish, [[Clarias gariepinus]] — indicating local extirpation of *S. bainsii*.
  - Tyume River (Keiskamma tributary) population is small and vulnerable to local extinction.
- **Threats**:
  - Poor water quality.
  - Invasive alien fish and plant species (notably [[Azolla filiculoides]], a floating water fern that alters aquatic habitat structure).
  - Habitat loss from water abstraction and damming.
  - Small population sizes and limited range amplify vulnerability to all above threats.
  - High vulnerability to **angling pressure**.

## Key Findings

### Reproduction & Life History
- Sexual maturity reached in the **second year of life**.
  - Males (♂): mature at **80–90 mm SL**.
  - Females (♀): mature at **60–69 mm SL**.
- **Breeding season**: Summer, October to February, coinciding with the rainy season; spawns **multiple times per season**.
- **Parental care**: Males guard the eggs (as in *S. capensis*) and prepare a dedicated spawning site.
- **Egg characteristics**:
  - Adhesive eggs, **1.3 mm diameter**.
  - Small oil globule, **0.6 mm diameter**.
- **Embryonic development**: At 22–23°C, embryos hatch in **less than 2 days**, emerging at **3.4 mm TL**.
- **Sexual growth dimorphism**: Males grow faster, live longer, and reach larger sizes than females.

### Conservation Action & Recommendations
- **Existing protected area**: [[Blaauwkrantz Nature Reserve]] (Bloukrans River, Kowie River system), managed by the Algoa Regional Services Council — the only reserve specifically established for this species.
  - However, the reserve receives nutrient-enriched water from upstream urban/rural sources, promoting invasive *Azolla filiculoides* growth, which is degrading the aquatic habitat.
- **Captive propagation potential**: *S. bainsii* appears to breed well in ponds, suggesting captive propagation is feasible if needed.
- **Recommended actions**:
  - Prioritize natural habitat restoration and conservation in remaining wild populations.
  - Utilize farm dams along river systems as breeding and refuge habitats.
  - Establish sanctuary areas high in river catchments to avoid downstream threats (poor water quality, water abstraction, alien predatory fish).
  - Further research needed on:
    - Specific spawning habitat requirements.
    - Species tolerance to increasing salinity and eutrophication.
