# Subpermit 89-9: Authorization for Virgin River Chub Research

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Gila #ConservationPermitting #EndangeredSpecies

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1993 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) regulatory authorization (Subpermit 89-9, under master permit PRT-704930) granted to Dr. W.L. Minckley and collaborators (Dr. Paul C. Marsh, Dr. Michael E. Douglas) of Arizona State University. It does not present novel primary field data but instead codifies the legal and scientific-collection parameters for research on the [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta seminuda*), a threatened cyprinid endemic to the Virgin River drainage in Utah/Arizona/Nevada. The permit authorizes limited seine-net capture of young-of-year chub for genetic (protein electrophoresis) and mitochondrial DNA (restriction endonuclease) analyses aimed at resolving taxonomic/genetic diversity questions within the *Gila robusta* complex. The permit also imposes strict incidental-take constraints for the co-occurring endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), reflecting the sensitive multi-species conservation context of the Virgin River ecosystem. The broader master permit (PRT-704930) additionally lists take authorizations for numerous other endangered fish taxa in the Colorado River Basin, providing useful comparative regulatory/ecological context.

## Background & Description

Direct morphological data on *Gila robusta seminuda* are not presented in this permit; however, the document specifies methodological parameters relevant to morphological and genetic characterization:

- **Life stage targeted**: Young-of-the-year (YOY) individuals only, up to 20 total specimens.
- **Sampling gear**: Fine mesh seines (a technique appropriate for capturing small-bodied juvenile cyprinids without excessive injury).
- **Analytical methods specified**:
  - Protein electrophoretic analysis (used to assess allelic/enzymatic variation, a proxy for population-level genetic and morphological divergence within the *Gila robusta* complex, including [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Humpback Chub]]).
  - Biochemical endonuclease (restriction fragment) analysis of mitochondrial DNA — used to resolve subspecific taxonomic status and evolutionary relationships among *Gila* taxa in the Colorado River Basin.
- These techniques imply that *G. r. seminuda* was, at the time, taxonomically contentious within the broader *Gila robusta* species complex, warranting combined morphological and molecular verification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Virgin River channel in Utah — a warm-water, turbid, desert river system tributary to the Colorado River, characterized by fluctuating flows typical of arid Southwestern USA streams.
- **Spatial sampling design**: Collections were mandated to be distributed across **four or more sampling sites**, each separated by **≥1 mile**, indicating an intent to capture spatial genetic/population structure across the river's longitudinal gradient rather than a single localized subpopulation.
- **Sympatric species**: The Virgin River supports the endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*), which is highly sensitive to incidental capture; the permit imposes a **zero-take authorization** for woundfin pending recovery plan review — underscoring the co-management complexity of these two imperiled cyprinids sharing habitat.
- **Population sensitivity**: Incidental lethal take of Virgin River Chub associated with these studies was expected not to exceed **1% of the population**, reflecting the species' threatened/small-population status.
- **Regional context**: The parent permit (PRT-704930) covers a broad suite of Colorado River Basin endangered fishes — [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (as "Colorado squawfish"), [[June Sucker]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and the geographically isolated [[Kendall Warm Springs Dace]] — situating *G. r. seminuda* within a broader desert-fish conservation framework tied to Endangered Species Act Section 7 consultations.

## Key Findings

- **Collection limits**: A maximum of 20 YOY Virgin River Chub authorized total; no more than 5 individuals per sampling site; excess captures must be released immediately at the point of capture.
- **Spatial design**: Minimum of 4 sampling sites, each ≥1 mile apart, to ensure representative sampling across the river reach.
- **Woundfin protection**: All incidentally captured woundfin must be released unharmed immediately; any incidental captures must be logged by location and age class and reported to USFWS within 72 hours — no take of woundfin authorized at time of permit issuance, pending recovery plan review.
- **Genetic/taxonomic objective**: The core scientific purpose was resolving genetic diversity and taxonomic status of *Gila robusta seminuda* via combined allozyme electrophoresis and mtDNA restriction analysis — a common approach of the era for disentangling cryptic species/subspecies within morphologically conservative desert fish genera like *Gila*.
- **Regulatory framework**: Research was conducted under the authority of the U.S. Endangered Species Act (16 USC 1531 et seq.), specifically sections 1539(a) [permits for scientific purposes] and 1533(d) [protective regulations], with reporting obligations to both the USFWS Field Supervisor (Salt Lake City) and the Office of Management Authority (Arlington, VA).
- **Incidental take governance across master permit**: Comparable but more liberal incidental-take allowances were granted for other Colorado Basin fishes under PRT-704930 — e.g., up to 8 [[Razorback Sucker]] deaths/injuries permitted across Regions 2 and 6 combined, while **zero incidental take** was authorized for [[Pallid Sturgeon]] and [[Neosho Madtom]], indicating a species-by-species risk-tolerance calibration based on population status.
- **Chain of custody/reporting**: A full activity report was required by December 31, 1993, and the subpermit itself plus a copy of PRT-704930 had to be physically carried during all fieldwork — reflecting stringent ESA Section 7(a)(2) compliance to prevent duplicated research effort or unauthorized take across overlapping projects.
