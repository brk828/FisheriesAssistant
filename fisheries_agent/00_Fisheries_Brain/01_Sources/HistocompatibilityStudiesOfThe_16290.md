# Histocompatibility Studies of the Northern Platyfish, Xiphophorus gordoni (Poeciliidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #immunogenetics #population-genetics

## Executive Summary

This 1967 doctoral dissertation by Jeanette H. Lysne at [[Arizona State University]] presents histocompatibility studies of [[Xiphophorus gordoni]] (northern platyfish) from three isolated lagunas in the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] of Coahuila, México. Using dorsal fin transplantation techniques, the research examined genetic variability within and between populations from [[Quintero]], [[Los Fresnos]], and [[Santa Tecla]] lagunas. Results demonstrated high graft acceptance rates within populations (median survival time ~34 days), suggesting significant inbreeding in these isolated habitats. Inter-population transplants showed faster rejection, indicating genetic differentiation between lagunas. The [[Quintero]] population exhibited reduced histocompatibility gene diversity compared to [[Los Fresnos]] and [[Santa Tecla]], likely due to founder effects or population bottlenecks. This work contributes to understanding population structure, genetic isolation, and immune response evolution in small, isolated fish populations.

## Background & Description

[[Xiphophorus gordoni]] (Miller and Minckley, 1963) is an ovoviviparous poeciliid fish endemic to three lagunas in the southeastern lobe of the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], central Coahuila, México, approximately 250 kilometers south-southeast of the Big Bend of the Rio Grande. The basin represents a uniquely isolated desert region with abundant aquatic habitats including springs, marshes, and lagunas, with some freshwater habitats estimated to originate in the middle or early Tertiary based on endemic mollusk divergence.

The three study populations occupy distinct habitats:

**[[Santa Tecla]] Laguna** (~27 km SSE of Cuatro Ciénegas): Main pool approximately 30 × 70 meters, surrounded by emergent vegetation (*Typha*, *Scirpus*, *Eleocharis*). Springs rise from the laguna bottom and shallow channels. Platyfish most abundant in spring inflows and vegetated coves along outflow canals. This locality yielded the type series of *X. gordoni*.

**[[Los Fresnos]] Laguna** (~1 km northwest of Santa Tecla): Main pool ~25 meters diameter, heavily vegetated, with complex canal systems. Water levels markedly lowered in recent years due to human activities. Connected intermittently to Santa Tecla via canal systems.

**[[Quintero]] Laguna** (~2.5 km northwest of Santa Tecla): Smallest laguna, 10 × 20 meters, fed by subsurface springs with one outflow stream. Minimal human disturbance but livestock trampling has reduced marginal vegetation. Most isolated of the three populations.

Like other xiphophorin fishes, *X. gordoni* exhibits strict habitat preferences: intimate association with aquatic vegetation, shallow water, and slow currents. This habitat specificity limits dispersal rates and promotes population structuring. The species' attributes of easy maintenance and year-round reproduction make it suitable for laboratory immunogenetic studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]] represents a desert bolsón with exceptional aquatic biodiversity and endemism. The basin's isolation in the Rocky Mountain-Guadalupe Mountain-Sierra Madre Oriental axis has created conditions favoring speciation and genetic differentiation. Water temperature in experimental conditions was maintained between 23-27°C, reflecting natural thermal regimes.

Platyfish populations in this system exhibit non-uniform distribution consisting of subpopulations with considerable internal genetic stability, similar to patterns observed in guppies (*Lebistes reticulatus*) in Trinidad streams. Populations are distributed sparsely in drainage channels but concentrate in especially favorable locations. Genetic interchange between population centers within a spring system is likely minimal; between springs, interchange must be rare.

Periodic desiccation events can destroy or severely reduce populations in temporary pool extensions. Subsequent repopulation by few survivors or chance invaders (potentially a single fertilized female) can establish micropopulations with reduced genetic variability compared to parental stocks. The small [[Quintero]] population likely originated as an offshoot of larger [[Santa Tecla]] or [[Los Fresnos]] populations, experiencing genetic bottlenecks through catastrophic reductions. [[Quintero]]'s smaller size, less stable water levels, reduced fish viability under aquarium conditions, and greater isolation from the other two lagunas support this hypothesis.

Human impacts vary by location. [[Los Fresnos]] has experienced water-level reduction from irrigation demands. [[Santa Tecla]] connects to basin outlet canals. [[Quintero]] shows minimal direct human disturbance but livestock effects on riparian vegetation.

## Key Findings

**Methodological Development:**
- Refined fin transplantation technique based on Held (1951) and Kallman and Gordon (1957, 1958): dorsal fin transplanted into hypaxial musculature of caudal peduncle
- Anesthesia: 1:80,000 aqueous Quinaldine dilution for 5 minutes
- Post-operative recovery: 48 hours in 0.6% sodium chloride solution in darkness
- Observation schedule: 3-5 day intervals until rejection or regeneration
- Rejection criterion: complete clearing or loss of transplanted fin
- Median Survival Time (MST): time when 50% of transplants rejected

**Autotransplantation Validation:**
- 87 *[[Poeciliopsis occidentalis]]* autotransplants: only 5% rejected by day 88 (high mortality terminated experiment)
- 20 *X. gordoni* autotransplants: 20% rejected by day 50; 10 of 13 remaining showed regeneration by day 168
- Results validated surgical technique reliability

**Intra-Population Experiments:**
All three populations showed identical MST of **34 days**, indicating similar genetic variability in histocompatibility genes within each laguna:
- [[Quintero]]: 22 transplants, rejection range 11-65 days, 40% host mortality
- [[Los Fresnos]]: 44 transplants, rejection range 11-65 days, 3 fins showed regeneration by day 65, 20% mortality
- [[Santa Tecla]]: 48 transplants, rejection range 11-89 days, 1 fin survived without regeneration, 50% mortality

High acceptance rates (compared to typical wild vertebrates) suggest significant inbreeding within each isolated population.

**Inter-Population Experiments:**
Demonstrated genetic differentiation between populations:

*[[Santa Tecla]] ↔ [[Los Fresnos]]:*
- ST donor → LF host: MST = 19 days (range 11-75 days), 18% mortality
- LF donor → ST host: MST = 19 days (range 11-75 days), 11% mortality
- Reciprocal experiments statistically indistinguishable, indicating similar histocompatibility gene differences in both directions

*[[Quintero]] ↔ [[Los Fresnos]]:*
- Q donor → LF host: MST = 27 days (range 8-58 days), 33% mortality
- LF donor → Q host: MST = 11 days (range 11-32 days), 33% mortality
- Highly significant difference between reciprocals: [[Quintero]] hosts rejected [[Los Fresnos]] fins much faster than reverse

*[[Quintero]] ↔ [[Santa Tecla]]:*
- Q donor → ST host: MST = 25 days (range 8-58 days), 20% mortality
- ST donor → Q host: MST = 14 days (range 8-37 days), 33% mortality
- Significant reciprocal difference: [[Quintero]] hosts rejected [[Santa Tecla]] fins rapidly

**Critical Finding:** [[Quintero]] population as host rejected fins from both [[Los Fresnos]] (MST = 11 days) and [[Santa Tecla]] (MST = 14 days) much faster than when [[Quintero]] served as donor (MST = 27 and 25 days respectively). This strongly indicates [[Quintero]] **lacks many histocompatibility genes present** in the other two populations, consistent with founder effect or bottleneck hypothesis.

**Syngenesiotransplantation (Within Sibships):**
- [[Quintero]]: 13 transplants among 4 sibships, MST = 36 days, 1 regeneration by day 85
- [[Los Fresnos]]: 10 transplants among 4 sibships, MST = 36 days, 1 regeneration by day 95
- [[Santa Tecla]]: 4 transplants in 1 sibship, rejection between days 35-50

MST values (~36 days) similar to intra-population experiments (34 days), suggesting comparable histocompatibility variation within sibships and within laguna populations.

**Heterotransplantation (*X. gordoni* × *X. couchianus*):**
- *X. couchianus* donor → *X. gordoni* host: MST = 15 days, rapid rejection starting day 7
- *X. gordoni* donor → *X. couchianus* host: no rejection until day 46, 50% mortality by day 25

Results suggest *X. gordoni* lacks many histocompatibility genes present in *[[Xiphophorus couchianus]]*, consistent with hypothesis that *X. gordoni* represents an offshoot of the *X. couchianus* lineage (Miller and Minckley, 1963). However, limited sample size requires cautious interpretation.

**Statistical Analysis:**
- Regression analysis with weighted data points (20-80% rejection double-weighted)
- F-tests confirmed variance equality between most inter- and intra-population experiments
- Student's t-tests demonstrated five of six inter-population experiments significantly different from intra-population baseline (p < 0.05)
- Exception: [[Quintero]] donor → [[Los Fresnos]] host showed quantitatively similar incompatibilities to intra-population variation

**Population Genetic Implications:**
1. All three populations show evidence of substantial inbreeding (high graft acceptance within populations)
2. Genetic differentiation exists between all three populations despite potential canal connections
3. [[Quintero]] shows reduced genetic diversity, likely due to:
   - Origin from small founder population
   - Periodic severe population bottlenecks with recovery from few individuals
   - Greater geographic isolation from other populations
   - Smaller, less stable habitat
4. [[Los Fresnos]] and [[Santa Tecla]] populations more similar to each other than either is to [[Quintero]]
5. Limited dispersal and gene flow between lagunas maintains population differentiation

**Comparative Context:**
Previous work by Kallman (1964a) on wild *[[Xiphophorus couchianus]]* (Monterrey) and *X. gordoni* (Cuatro Ciénegas) offspring showed 0-69% and 0-15% graft acceptance respectively in intra-population transplants—much higher than typical wild vertebrates. The current study's detailed quantitative analysis extends these findings by demonstrating measurable genetic differentiation between closely-spaced populations and identifying specific patterns of reduced diversity in the smallest, most isolated population.

**Technical Notes:**
- Mortality rates varied (11-50%) but generally did not substantially influence MST calculations as deaths occurred late in observation periods
- Rotational (rather than reciprocal) transplantation design avoided potential confounding effects
- Age and sex diversity in experimental populations accepted as necessary trade-off for larger sample sizes
- Preliminary investigations indicated host/donor sex did not influence results
- Computer program developed using G.E. BASIC language for regression analysis, F-tests, and t-tests

This research demonstrates that fin transplantation provides a quantitative method for assessing genetic relationships and population structure in small, isolated fish populations, with implications for understanding speciation processes, conservation genetics, and immune system evolution in teleost fishes.
