# Environmental Assessment: Quail Creek Hydroplant #2 — Virgin River Fish Community

## Executive Summary
This 1993 environmental assessment evaluates the construction of Quail Creek Hydroplant #2, an enhancement to the existing Quail Creek Reservoir hydroelectric project on the Virgin River in Washington County, Utah. While primarily an engineering and regulatory document, it contains a baseline fisheries assessment of the Virgin River, home to six native fish species — two of which, the [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin River Chub]], are federally listed as endangered. The assessment concludes that because the project will not alter existing water quality, quantity, or temperature regimes (water is routed through an existing 176 acre-ft regulating pond before re-entering the river at essentially the same location and rate), no short- or long-term adverse effects on the native fish community are anticipated. The document also notes ongoing efforts by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service to eradicate the invasive [[Red Shiner]], which has displaced woundfin populations in lower reaches of the river.

## Background & Description
The source document does not provide detailed morphological descriptions (body plan, fin counts, coloration) of the fish species discussed. It is a regulatory/engineering environmental assessment rather than a taxonomic or morphological study. Species are referenced only by common name in the context of habitat association and conservation status:

- **Native species**: [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Virgin River Spinedace]], [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Chub]]
- **Exotic/introduced species**: Largemouth Bass, Bluegill, Green Sunfish, Channel Catfish, Black Bullhead, [[Red Shiner]]

No morphometric, meristic, or physiological data are reported.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native fish assemblage**: The six native species historically coexisted in the Virgin River and its associated tributaries, having evolved tolerance for the region's naturally variable habitat conditions (fluctuating flow, temperature, turbidity, and intermittency typical of a semi-arid desert river system).
- **Habitat partitioning**:
  - [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and [[Desert Sucker]] are generally confined to the **mainstem** of the Virgin River and the **mouths of major tributaries**. Woundfin has also been documented in La Verkin Creek (Cross, 1975).
  - [[Speckled Dace]] and [[Virgin River Spinedace]] occur more broadly, throughout the mainstem **and** in permanent tributaries (Cross, 1975; Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, 1988).
- **Exotic species pressure**: Largemouth Bass, Bluegill, Green Sunfish, Channel Catfish, and Black Bullhead prey upon and compete with native fish for food, but with the exception of the Red Shiner, none have established persistent breeding populations in the Virgin River itself — occasional individuals collected are presumed to represent migrants from upstream reservoirs within the basin.
- **Red Shiner invasion**: The [[Red Shiner]] is the singular exotic species that has successfully established itself in the Virgin River, directly competing with native fish for food and living space. It has **nearly replaced the [[Woundfin]]** in portions of the lower river reaches — a case study in competitive displacement by an invasive cyprinid.
- **Conservation management**: The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, in cooperation with state and local agencies (including the Washington County Water Conservancy District), is actively working to eradicate Red Shiner from the Virgin River and its tributaries above the Virgin River Gorge narrows and to prevent its re-entry into upstream reaches — an early basin-scale invasive-species control effort.
- **Project water regime**: Water for the hydroplant is sourced from the Virgin River (via the Quail Creek Reservoir inlet pipeline/tailwater from Hydroplant #1) and/or from Quail Creek Reservoir outflow, discharging into an existing 176 acre-ft regulating pond before returning to the river roughly a quarter-mile downstream of the existing outlet, at the same approximate volume and schedule as current operations — designed explicitly to preserve downstream flow requirements for endangered/sensitive species.

## Key Findings
- Six native, non-game fish species inhabit the Virgin River: [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Virgin River Spinedace]], [[Woundfin]], and [[Virgin River Chub]].
- Two of these — [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin River Chub]] — are federally listed endangered species known to occur in the Virgin River adjacent to the project area.
- The invasive [[Red Shiner]] is the only exotic fish species that has established a self-sustaining population in the Virgin River and poses a direct competitive threat to native fish, having nearly displaced Woundfin in lower river reaches.
- Active interagency eradication efforts targeting Red Shiner are underway above the Virgin River Gorge narrows to protect native and endangered fish populations.
- The assessment concludes the hydroplant project will produce **no expected change** in water quality, quantity, or temperature in the Virgin River, and therefore **no anticipated short- or long-term impact** on the resident fish community, including endangered species.
- No new fisheries studies were conducted or proposed in connection with this project; conclusions rely on prior literature (Cross, 1975; Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, 1988) and the operational premise that existing flow/discharge patterns are preserved.
