# Species Responsibilities of Desert Fishes Recovery Team

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum dated December 7, 1989, delineating the roster of imperiled freshwater fish species under the jurisdiction of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT). Rather than presenting morphological or ecological data directly, the source is a taxonomic/regulatory inventory clarifying which species remain under DFRT stewardship and which have been reassigned to other recovery teams — notably the [[Gila Trout Chihuahua Chub Recovery Team]] and the [[Woundfin Recovery Team]]. The memo reflects an active period of jurisdictional reorganization among U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service recovery teams responsible for endangered and threatened desert fishes in the American Southwest and northern Mexico.

## Background & Description
No morphological descriptions are provided in the source text. The document exclusively addresses species-level nomenclature and administrative/jurisdictional assignment. For reference, the listed taxa span four families:
- **Cyprinidae** (minnows/chubs): [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Roundtail Chub]], [[Mexican Stoneroller]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Virgin River Chub]], [[Virgin River Spinedace]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]]
- **Ictaluridae** (catfishes): [[Yaqui Catfish]]
- **Cyprinodontidae** (pupfishes): [[Desert Pupfish]]
- **Poeciliidae** (livebearers): [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]]
- **Catostomidae** (suckers, inferred): [[Razorback Sucker]]

Subspecific designations are noted for two taxa: *Gila robusta grahami* ([[Gila Roundtail Chub]]) and the Arizona/adjacent Mexico population of *Campostoma ornatum* ([[Mexican Stoneroller]]), indicating recovery efforts are geographically and taxonomically fine-scaled.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
All listed species occupy desert aquatic habitats characteristic of the arid American Southwest and northern Mexico, including the [[Gila River Basin]], [[Yaqui River Basin]], [[Virgin River]], and [[Colorado River Basin]] — isolated, often fragmented spring- and stream-fed systems typical of endorheic desert drainages. These habitats are characterized by:
- Low, variable flow regimes
- High endemism and isolation among drainage basins
- Vulnerability to water diversion, groundwater pumping, and introduced non-native species

The document implies a management ecology framework wherein species are grouped by drainage basin and recovery team jurisdiction rather than by shared ecological guild, though most taxa (chubs, minnows, topminnows, pupfish) are small-bodied, benthic to midwater fishes adapted to fluctuating desert stream conditions.

## Key Findings
- **Current DFRT Responsibility (as of 12/7/1989):** [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Roundtail Chub]], and the Arizona/Mexico population of [[Mexican Stoneroller]].
- **Reassigned Species:**
  - [[Chihuahua Chub]] — transferred to the Gila Trout/Chihuahua Chub Recovery Team (per memo, Regional Director to Propst, 4-17-89).
  - [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]] — removed from DFRT with no Region 1 team currently assigned (per letter, Regional Director to Minckley, 5-17-89).
  - [[Virgin River Chub]] and [[Virgin River Spinedace]] — removed from DFRT and reassigned to the [[Woundfin Recovery Team]] (same 5-17-89 letter).
  - [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] — tentatively assigned to the [[Apache Trout Recovery Team]], though the source flags this assignment with explicit uncertainty.
- **Administrative Implication:** The memo documents an evolving, multi-agency reorganization of desert fish conservation responsibility, underscoring the complexity of managing endangered species recovery across overlapping river basins and jurisdictional boundaries in the late 1980s.
- **Data Gap:** No population status, threat assessments, or biological data are included in this source; it functions purely as a jurisdictional/administrative record.
