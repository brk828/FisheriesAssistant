# Humpback Chub Population Estimate in Black Rocks, Colorado River (FY 2000–FY 2001 Annual Project Reports)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

These two annual reports from the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]] (Project 22-A-3) cover the population estimate of [[Humpback Chub]] in Black Rocks, Colorado. The work was carried out under the [[Interagency Standardized Monitoring Program]] (ISMP), which since 1996 has monitored population trends of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] and humpback chub in the [[Colorado River]] Basin. The FY 2000 report covers the field season and data collection, and the FY 2001 report presents the final population estimates and summarizes the study. Both reports were prepared by Frank K. Pfeifer (Project Leader) and Charles W. McAda (Fishery Biologist), U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Grand Junction, Colorado.

Catch rates declined markedly in 2000 compared with 1998 and 1999. The Black Rocks population estimate fell from 949 (1998) to 921 (1999) to 539 (2000). Both reports state that all data were submitted to the [[Upper Colorado River Basin Database]], and each year's budget was fully expended ($22,000 in FY 2000; $24,000 in FY 2001).

## Background & Description

- **Program origin:** The ISMP was developed in 1996. The original design had three components:
  1. Spring [[Electrofishing]] for subadult and adult Colorado Pikeminnow in the Green, Colorado, White, and Yampa rivers, covering about 20–30% (FY00 text) or 20–40% (FY01 text) of occupied habitat in each river. The OCR of the two reports differs on this range.
  2. Autumn backwater [[Seining]] for young-of-year Colorado Pikeminnow in the Colorado and Green rivers.
  3. Spring sampling for adult humpback chub with [[Trammel Net]]s in Black Rocks and Westwater Canyon.
- **Monitoring approach:** Trends were tracked with [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPUE). A 1996–1998 summary report was produced, and reports have been issued regularly since.
- **Expansion:** In 1998 the ISMP was expanded to add [[Mark-Recapture]] population estimates for the major Colorado Pikeminnow and humpback chub populations. Mark-recapture estimates were meant to supplement the CPUE data. Humpback chub in the Green River subbasin was monitored separately.
- **Study schedule:** 1998–2001.
- **Program link:** The work falls under the General Recovery Program Support Action Plan, item V.A.1 ("Conduct Standardized Monitoring Program").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** Black Rocks on the Colorado River. Sampling concentrated on eddy lines and rock faces where humpback chub were readily captured. Sites were limited to areas away from high-velocity main-channel flows and violent turbulence along portions of the shoreline.
- **Timing:** FY 2000 sampling used four-day rotations in late summer and/or late fall. FY 2001 notes that sampling occurred in late summer to early fall, after water temperatures began to cool for the year.
- **Co-captured species:** Roundtail Chub were captured and identified alongside humpback chub. Other fish were counted and released at the sample site in FY 2000. In FY 2001, a small number of other species were also handled. Specific numbers are not legible in the scan.
- **Water temperature and handling:** In FY 2001, fish were placed in a 5% salt bath for 0.5–1 min before release. About 10% of the fish handled were held overnight in live cages to check for initial mortality. No overnight mortality was observed, and all fish appeared healthy on release.

## Key Findings

### Sampling Design and Methods

- **FY 2000:** Three to four sampling trips were planned. Black Rocks was sampled in four-day periods (rotations 9801–0003) with 6–8 trammel nets set at one time. Nets were checked at 1–1.5 hr intervals, left in place for a morning or evening period, and moved if they caught few fish. Each four-day rotation covered the full reach, but nets were concentrated in a 0.25–0.5 mi section during the morning or evening period.
- **FY 2001:** Sampling used 1-inch inner-mesh trammel nets, supplemented by angling in all years and by electrofishing and trap nets in 1998. Nets were checked at 2 hr intervals, with a mean of 1.5 hr and a maximum of 2 hr. Trips took place in 1998, 1999, and 2000, with four trips in 1999 and 2000.
- **Handling:** All chubs were identified as humpback or roundtail, measured (total length, mm), weighed (g), and checked for a PIT tag. Untagged fish were tagged. Sex was not determined in most cases.

### Catch and Recapture Results

- **FY 2000 catch:** Fewer chubs were collected than in earlier years. Rotation-level recapture rates ranged from about 9% to 35% (the OCR of rotation values is partly garbled). Overall within-year recapture rates were 30–40%, including multiple recaptures within a season.
- **Total handled:** The FY 2001 report gives 184 humpback chubs handled in one year (partly garbled), 293 in 1999 (four trips), and a lower total for 2000 (OCR illegible).
- **Previously tagged fish:** 15 humpback chubs that had originally been tagged in Westwater Canyon were recaptured at Black Rocks.

### Population Structure and Growth

- **Length-frequency distributions** were bimodal in all years, with modes at about 230–250 mm and 310–330 mm (OCR-approximate).
- **Growth:** Recaptured fish grew an average of 7 mm per year. Growth differences between size groups were not significant, based on the length-frequency partitions.
- **Sex ratio:** Sex was determined on fish in the last 1999 trip, when experienced staff were available. The sex ratio did not differ significantly from expectation.

### Population Estimates

- **Model dependence:** Estimates varied with the model chosen. In 1998, estimates ranged from 348 (Jackknife) to 1,495. The "best estimator" was selected for the final analysis.
- **Best-estimator results (approximate; confidence intervals illegible in scan):**
  - 1998: 949
  - 1999: 921
  - 2000: 539

### Conclusions and Recommendations

- **Status:** The project is nearing completion, and the final report is in its final stages. The FY 2001 report states that catch rates declined markedly in 2000.
- **Recommendation:** Review the conclusions and recommendations of all researchers involved in humpback chub mark-recapture estimates. Use these to devise a sampling regime and schedule for a long-term monitoring program. Recommendations should reflect all humpback chub sampling across the different populations and subbasins.
