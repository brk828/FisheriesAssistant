# Limnological Reconnaissance of Seminoe Reservoir, Wyoming

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae #Salmonidae #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1976 Bureau of Reclamation report (GR-2-77, by J.F. LaBounty, J.J. Sartoris and R.A. Roline) presents a baseline limnological survey of [[Seminoe Reservoir]], a mainstream [[North Platte River]] impoundment in south-central Wyoming. Field work took place August 31 to September 3, 1976, at five sampling stations. The study was commissioned to support the [[Seminoe Dam Modification Studies]] and was framed as a template for baseline surveys elsewhere in the Bureau's area of operation. The reservoir is characterized as a dimictic, mesotrophic, riverine, high-plains reservoir of moderate primary and secondary productivity.

## Background & Description

- **Dam and reservoir:** [[Seminoe Dam]] was built in 1939 by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and is the uppermost of six major storage facilities on the North Platte River.
- **Capacity:** 1 246 800 000 m³ (1 011 000 acre-ft) at water surface elevation 1938 m (6357 ft). Average annual volume is 555 000 000 to 617 000 000 m³ (450 000 to 500 000 acre-ft), with a surface area of about 4050 ha (10 000 acres).
- **Fishery:** The reservoir is one of Wyoming's major fisheries, accounting for 1.4 percent of total annual state fishing. Game fishes, in order of abundance, are [[Walleye]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brook Trout]].
- **Nongame fishes:** Nine species are listed, including [[White Sucker]], [[Longnose Sucker]], [[Carp]], [[Creek Chub]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Northern Plains Minnow]], [[Bigmouth Shiner]], [[Longnose Dace]] and [[Iowa Darter]].
- **Methods overview:** Physical-chemical profiles used a [[Hydrolab Surveyor Multiparameter Probe]]; benthos used a [[Petersen Dredge]]; zooplankton used a [[Clarke-Bumpus Sampler]]; light penetration used a [[Secchi Disk]] and [[Limnophotometer]]; chlorophyll was analyzed by the [[Parsons and Strickland Method]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stratification and oxygen.** The reservoir is only weakly stratified, with a thermocline apparent roughly 15 to 25 m deep at all stations. Turnover is believed to occur twice yearly (April or May, and October or November), and wind is thought to strongly influence conditions. Surface dissolved oxygen was at or near saturation, but hypolimnetic values at stations 3 and 4 were 3.6 and 1.9 mg/litre. The authors state that values at station 4 are too low for many cold-water game species such as rainbow trout.

**Water chemistry.** The reservoir is nonalkaline, with pH above 7.0 (7.6 to 8.6 recorded). [[Calcium]] is the dominant cation and bicarbonate and sulfate the dominant anions. Average conductivity was about 420 µS/cm. Station 3 in the [[Medicine Bow Arm]] had the highest ion concentrations, indicating the [[Medicine Bow River]] as the main contributor.

**Productivity.** The blue-green alga [[Aphanizomenon]] was abundant, giving the water an appearance of "grass clippings." Chlorophyll *a* and light extinction values classify the reservoir as mesotrophic. The Medicine Bow and North Platte arms were the most productive, indicating nutrient loading from the two rivers.

**Benthos and zooplankton.** Benthic fauna consisted of [[Chironomidae]] (nonbiting midges) and [[Oligochaeta]] (aquatic earthworms), with the sparsest communities in the riverine arms and the richest near the dam. Zooplankton were dominated by [[Cladocera]] and [[Copepoda]], with copepods prevailing below 10 m.

**Heavy metals.** Copper, lead, manganese, iron and zinc were detected, mostly near the bottom and at stations nearest the dam. The authors judged them probably biologically unimportant under the measured pH, redox and oxygen conditions, although samples were unfiltered and dissolved fractions remain unknown.

## Key Findings

1. **Classification:** Seminoe Reservoir is a dimictic, mesotrophic, riverine, high-plains reservoir of moderate primary and secondary productivity. Under the Northcote and Larkin scheme, benthic abundance averaged 406 ± 352/m², an intermediate value.
2. **Light and chlorophyll:** Secchi depths ranged from 1.4 m (Medicine Bow arm) to 5.0 m (near dam). Light extinction coefficients ranged from 0.55 m⁻¹ (near dam) to 1.60 m⁻¹ (Medicine Bow arm). Surface chlorophyll *a* reached 35.99 mg/m³ at the confluence bay, and values dropped sharply at the thermocline.
3. **Nutrients:** Ortho-phosphate, ammonia-nitrogen and nitrate-nitrogen were at or below detection limits (0.025 and 0.40 mg/litre) in almost every sample. The authors suggest nutrients were tied up in biomass, and that winter or spring sampling might show more nutrients.
4. **Zooplankton:** Concentrations were highest in the North Platte arm (station 2, 44.25 organisms/litre at 0–10 m) and lowest near the dam (station 1, 10.50 at 0–10 m). Cladocerans were 46.9% of the 0–10 m community, and copepods were 80.4% of the community below 20 m.
5. **Benthos:** Abundance ranged from 129/m² (riverine arms) to 961/m² (near dam). Benthic data are considered of limited value because the sample was a single date and cyclical patterns exist.
6. **Oxygen stress:** Hypolimnetic dissolved oxygen fell to 1.9 mg/litre, so the authors recommended caution for cold-water game species.
7. **Fish implications:** The authors expected larger fish populations in the two arms, but fish data were unavailable to test this.
8. **Recommendations:**
   - Investigate the cause and extent of trace element accumulation.
   - Study fish distribution relative to nutrient and food supply in the arms.
   - Identify internal (autochthonous) and external (allochthonous) nutrient sources.
   - Assess the impacts of reservoir enlargement on stratification and nutrient enrichment.
