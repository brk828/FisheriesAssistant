# Fishery Inventory of the Coachella Canal, Southeastern California

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Ictaluridae #Centrarchidae #canal-systems #desert-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This report documents a comprehensive fishery inventory of the uppermost 78 km (48.7 miles) of the [[Coachella Canal]] in southeastern [[California]], conducted in November 1980 prior to replacement of the earthen channel with concrete lining. Using rotenone sampling in three study sections, investigators identified 16 introduced fish species and quantified standing crops ranging from 49 to 102 g/m² in open canal reaches, with exceptional concentrations (594 g/m²) in an inverted siphon. [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) dominated numerically (21.6-79.5% of total catch depending on section), followed by [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*). The canal supported an estimated minimum of 78,000 kg (85 tons) of fish, with "game fishes" comprising 84.3% of total biomass. All fishes exhibited good body condition with substantial visceral fat deposits. The study provides baseline data for evaluating impacts of canal lining on fishery resources and demonstrates that unlined desert canals constitute valuable aquatic habitat in otherwise fishery-poor regions.

## Background & Description

The [[Coachella Canal]] was completed in 1948 to convey water from the [[Colorado River]] via the [[All American Canal]] through the [[Colorado Desert]] region of southeastern California. The canal has a capacity of 70.8 m³/second (2,500 cfs) at the turnout, decreasing to 36.8 m³/second (1,300 cfs) at 137.8 km downstream. The study area encompassed the uppermost 78 km along the [[Imperial East Mesa]] adjacent to the [[Sand Hills]] (Alagodones Dunes) and the western bajada of the [[Chocolate Mountains]]. The region drains into the [[Salton Sea]], a saline closed-basin water body.

Three study sections were selected for intensive sampling:

**Section A** (8 km below turnout): 403 m long × 20 m wide (8,026 m² surface area), including a pool below Check Drop 5 and a straight reach. Sandy substrate with concrete apron below check structure. Maximum depth 2-5 m at capacity. Sparse emergent vegetation ([[Phragmites australis]]), no submergent vegetation.

**Section B** (68 km below turnout): 650 m long × 20.5 m wide (13,325 m² surface area), straight reach with no control structures. Maximum depth 2 m. Sandy bottom with small amounts of [[Sago Pondweed]] (*Potamogeton pectinatus*) and [[Water Milfoil]] (*Myriophyllum* sp.).

**Section C** (same location as Section B): Double-barreled concrete box siphon (Siphon No. 2), each barrel 165 m long × 3 m wide (1,023 m² total sample area including approach zones). Concrete bottom littered with debris.

All 16 fish species documented are introduced to the region. Previous electrofishing surveys by St. Amant et al. (1974) recorded 11 species from the canal, with 30 species known from waters directly supplying the system.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Coachella Canal]] represents an artificial aquatic corridor through the [[Colorado Desert]], extending fish distributions far beyond natural limits in this extremely arid region. The canal operates continuously for year-round water delivery, creating permanent flowing habitat in an environment where natural surface water is scarce or absent. Water originates from the [[Colorado River]] system, which has been extensively modified and supports a diverse assemblage of introduced fishes.

The canal's physical characteristics—high velocity, substantial depth, concrete control structures, and seasonal aquatic plant growth—create sampling challenges but also provide diverse microhabitats. Check drops create pool-like areas with reduced current, siphons offer dark, swift-water refugia, and vegetated reaches provide cover and foraging habitat. Water depths at capacity ranged from 2-5 m, with velocities varying by location and proximity to control structures.

The study occurred in November 1980, immediately before conversion to concrete lining as part of the [[Colorado River Salinity Control]] project. This timing provided a unique opportunity to document the fishery resource in an earthen canal system before habitat modification. The region's climate is characterized by extreme aridity, high temperatures, and minimal precipitation, making artificial water conveyance systems critical for both human use and aquatic ecosystem persistence.

Comparison with other low-desert aquatic systems reveals the canal's exceptional productivity. Standing crops of 49-102 g/m² in open canal sections approach or exceed maxima recorded in natural desert streams: [[Aravaipa Creek]] (120.6 g/m²), [[Bonita Creek]] (~150 g/m²), and [[Eagle Creek]] (104.3 g/m²) in Arizona. The canal's continuous flow, stable water supply, and nutrient inputs from the [[Colorado River]] likely contribute to high productivity despite the artificial nature of the habitat.

## Key Findings

**Species Composition and Relative Abundance:**

Sixteen fish species were collected, all introduced. [[Channel Catfish]] clearly dominated the fauna:
- Section A: 21.6% of total catch, 0.120 fish/m²
- Section B: 67.8% of total catch, 0.209 fish/m²
- Section C (siphon): 79.5% of total catch, 0.904 fish/m²

[[Threadfin Shad]] (*Dorosoma petenense*) showed extreme localization, comprising 65.5% of catch in Section A (0.363 fish/m²) below Check Drop 5, but rare elsewhere. This concentration pattern is typical for the species, which rarely thrives in flowing waters and typically aggregates below control structures.

[[Bluegill]] exhibited remarkable uniformity across sections (0.13-0.32 fish/m²), followed closely by [[Largemouth Bass]] in relative abundance. [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) concentrated in the dark, swift siphon (4.5% of catch, 0.049 fish/m²). [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus natalis*) were rare and only taken in Section A.

[[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) occurred throughout at relatively low densities (0.003-0.056 fish/m², 1.0-5.1% of total). [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*), a small forage species, showed consistent absolute abundance (0.016-0.031 fish/m²) despite likely passage through 2.54-cm blocking nets.

Other species occurred in trace amounts: [[Striped Bass]] (*Morone saxatilis*), [[Zill's Tilapia]] (*Tilapia zilli*), [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*; one individual), [[Redear Sunfish]] (*Lepomis microlophus*), [[Warmouth]] (*Chaenobryttus gulosus*), and [[Black Crappie]] (*Pomoxis nigromaculatus*). [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) and [[Sailfin Molly]] (*Poecilia latipinna*) were present along shorelines but not quantified.

**Standing Crops and Biomass:**

Total fish biomass was consistently high but variable:
- Section B: 49 g/m² (437 lbs/acre)—considered most representative of uniform canal habitat
- Section A: 102 g/m² (910 lbs/acre)—elevated by concentrations of [[Threadfin Shad]] (16.5 g/m²) and [[Carp]] (59.5 g/m²) below check structure
- Section C (siphon): 594 g/m² (5,300 lbs/acre)—exceptional concentration of large catfishes in specialized habitat

Using Section B as representative of uniform canal reaches, the 156 surface hectares (385.5 acres) of the study reach supported an estimated minimum of 78,000 kg (85 tons) of fish. This estimate is conservative because: (1) concentrations near 15 check drops, turnouts, and 6 siphons were not fully accounted for; (2) 2.54-cm blocking nets allowed small fish passage; (3) some fish loss occurred after rotenone application; and (4) no second-day pickup was conducted in the siphon.

"Game fishes" (catfishes and sunfishes including [[Largemouth Bass]]) comprised 66,000 kg (72 tons, 84.3%) of total fish biomass.

Biomass composition in Section A: [[Carp]] 58.3%, [[Channel Catfish]] 23.3%, [[Threadfin Shad]] 16.1%, [[Largemouth Bass]] 1.1%, other species <1% each.

**Size and Age Structure:**

Size-frequency distributions indicated successful reproduction for most species, with strong year-class separation evident for [[Red Shiner]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Bluegill]]. Small individuals of river-adapted catfishes were particularly abundant, indicating the canal provides suitable spawning and nursery habitat.

[[Channel Catfish]] showed bimodal size distribution in Section A (modes at 26-28 cm and 32-34 cm total length) versus unimodal distribution in Sections B and C (30-34 cm), possibly reflecting stocking by California Department of Fish and Game.

Most [[Carp]] and [[Threadfin Shad]] were large adults. Absence of young [[Carp]] and weak year-class structure in [[Threadfin Shad]] suggest immigration from the water-supply system and/or sporadic reproductive success rather than consistent in-canal recruitment.

All fishes were in good body condition with substantial visceral fat deposits. No emaciated or visibly unhealthy individuals were observed. Length-weight correlations were moderate (r = 0.71 for [[Channel Catfish]], r = 0.65 for [[Flathead Catfish]], n = 110 and 94 respectively), precluding calculation of condition factors, but qualitative assessment indicated excellent physiological condition.

**Comparison with Other Systems:**

Standing crops in the [[Coachella Canal]] (49-102 g/m² in open reaches) approach or exceed maxima documented in other low-desert streams and far exceed most temperate-region streams. Maximum biomass in low-desert streams: [[Aravaipa Creek]] 120.6 g/m², [[Bonita Creek]] ~150 g/m², [[Eagle Creek]] 104.3 g/m², [[Tecopa Bore]] 95.8 g/m². Most temperate streams support <30 g/m².

Previous electrofishing estimates by St. Amant et al. (1974) of 3,000 "game fish" per mile (0.09 fish/m²) contrast with rotenone data showing 0.015-1.002 fish/m² depending on habitat type. Their estimate of 0.05 [[Largemouth Bass]]/m² closely matches rotenone data (0.012 ± 0.005 fish/m²), suggesting electrofishing effectively sampled this species but underestimated others, particularly catfishes.

Comparison with [[Yuma Valley]] canals (Arizona) after 1974 pesticide spill: similar species composition but different community structure. Yuma canals supported 10.84 fish/m² dominated by small forage species ([[Red Shiner]] 3.5/m², [[Mosquitofish]] 3.8/m², [[Sailfin Molly]] 1.0/m²) with total biomass of 24.47 g/m². Game fish comprised only 0.31 fish/m² and 32.8% of biomass, versus 84.3% in [[Coachella Canal]].

**Management Implications:**

The [[Coachella Canal]] constitutes a valuable fishery resource in a desert region with minimal natural aquatic habitat. River-adapted [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Flathead Catfish]] dominate and reproduce successfully, with young depending on invertebrate foods. [[Red Shiner]], an important forage species, feeds on drifting allochthonous and autochthonous materials.

Concrete lining may impact different species groups differentially. Catfishes require holes and crevices for spawning, which will be less available in lined canals. However, if adequate food resources persist (invertebrate drift, plankton), the fish assemblage should maintain relatively high populations. [[Largemouth Bass]] and other sunfishes, which are not adapted to swift flow and were already relatively scarce, are anticipated to decline further in concrete-lined conditions.

The study demonstrates that unlined canals operating for continuous water delivery provide important aquatic habitat and support substantial fishery resources. The 78 km study reach supported a minimum of 78 metric tons of fish, predominantly desirable sport and food species, in good physiological condition with evidence of successful reproduction.
