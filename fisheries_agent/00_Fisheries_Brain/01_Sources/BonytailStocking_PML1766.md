# Bonytail Stocking Memorandum, October 1994

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Department of the Interior, Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum, dated 31 October 1994 and issued from the Parker office, reports the third scheduled [[Bonytail]] stocking of the year. An estimated 56,500 fish were transported from [[Willow Beach]] to a hatchery, and a further group was released into [[Twin Coves]] (north and south) the same day. The memo was addressed to the project leader, the [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]] manager, and the recovery coordinator. Its main content is a field account of the release and a blank trip-record form.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Bonytail]], a native Colorado River fish that is the focus of a federal recovery effort. The memo does not give a scientific name, so the frontmatter value is taken from general knowledge.
- **Program context:** The stocking is part of a recovery effort run by the Parker Fish and Wildlife Office. The memo is addressed to the recovery coordinator and interested persons.
- **Fish condition:** The fish were small, averaging about 2 to 3 inches (the OCR text is partly garbled). A few were 4 to 6 inches.
- **Tagging:** Fish were to be nose-tagged, and the memo notes a later step in November. The exact wording is partly garbled in the scan.
- **Agencies and personnel:** Named staff included the Project Coordinator (author), Mitch Thorson, Al Doelker, and Tom Pradetto of the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM). Stuart Leon (Project Leader, AZFRO) and Jim Good (Manager, Havasu NWR) were addressees.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo describes a stocking operation in the lower Colorado River reservoir system near [[Willow Beach]]. Key operational features:

- **Transport:** A [[Dexter Truck]] carried fish from Willow Beach to a hatchery. A separate Willow Beach truck took fish from the offloading point.
- **Release sites:** Fish were released at [[Twin Coves]] (north and south), reached from the Willow Beach boat ramp by pontoon boats.
- **Pontoon transfer:** Remaining fish were moved to the release site in two containers on the Service pontoon and one container on the BLM pontoon.
- **Timing:** The team was underway to Twin Coves by about 0930 (OCR partly unreadable), and the fish were in the water by about 1030.
- **Mortality:** Very few mortalities were observed. The memo gives a count of 14 (OCR "(.14)").
- **Density:** The memo estimates the number released into each cove. The figure is garbled in the scan and is not reliably recoverable.

## Key Findings

1. **Stocking event:** The third scheduled stocking for the year took place on the memo date.
2. **Numbers:** About 56,500 bonytail were off-loaded at Willow Beach for hatchery transport. The memo gives a separate estimated number per cove (OCR-garbled, not reliably recoverable).
3. **Survival at release:** Very few mortalities occurred during transfer and release. The memo gives 14 as the count.
4. **Fish size:** Most fish averaged about 2 to 3 inches, with a few at 4 to 6 inches.
5. **Logistics:** Transfer from truck to pontoon to release cove took roughly 4 hours, from the Willow Beach boat ramp to fish in the water at Twin Coves.
6. **Follow-up:** Fish are to be nose-tagged, with a later step planned for November.
7. **Trip record (page 2):** The attached form lists fields for truck costs, per diem, total cost, departure and return times, management area, species number, number of applications and farm ponds stocked, and acreage stocked. Most entries are blank or illegible in the scan, so no cost or acreage figures can be reported.
