## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative report from the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (NMDGF) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, detailing field encounters with federally threatened and endangered fish species during the period December 1991–November 1992. The report catalogs capture, handling, and release data for six species: [[Gila Trout]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], and [[Pecos Gambusia]]. Activities documented include population surveys, conservation stocking (fingerling releases), genetic/reproductive sampling (gamete collection), disease monitoring, and telemetry studies (radio transmitter implantation). The data reflects active recovery and monitoring efforts across the [[Gila River Basin]], [[San Juan River]], and [[Pecos River]] drainages.

## Background & Description
The source document is a regulatory field report and does not provide detailed morphological descriptions. However, contextual data on life stages and biological sampling can be inferred:

- **[[Gila Trout]]** (*Oncorhynchus gilae*): Adults present in sufficient numbers for gamete extraction (8 female, 29 male from 37 adults sampled at McKnight Creek); fingerling stage used in stocking efforts (~1,100 released at Mogollon Creek from Mescalero National Fish Hatchery).
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): Only adult specimens (1 female, 2 male over three events) were captured, consistent with the species' status as North America's largest native cyprinid; individuals were large enough to surgically implant radio transmitters.
- **[[Chihuahua Chub]]** (*Gila nigrescens*): Small sample sizes (17 and 1 specimens) suggest a restricted, low-density population at the time of survey.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*): Both are small-bodied benthic cyprinids typically associated with swift, gravelly riffle habitats; captured in moderate to high numbers (up to 240 specimens for spikedace), indicating detectable, sample-able populations in the Gila River system.
- **[[Pecos Gambusia]]** (*Gambusia nobilis*): A small live-bearing poeciliid; only a few individuals were captured, with 2 retained as voucher specimens.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Gila Trout]]**: Endemic to cold, high-elevation headwater streams of the Gila River drainage (McKnight Creek, Mogollon Creek, Trail Canyon, Corral Canyon, Woodrow Canyon) in Grant and Catron Counties, New Mexico. Requires clean, oxygenated water typical of montane trout streams.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (Colorado Pikeminnow): A large-river pelagic predator native to the Colorado River Basin; documented here in the [[San Juan River]], a major tributary, indicating ongoing reintroduction/monitoring via telemetry.
- **[[Chihuahua Chub]]**: Inhabits pools and slow-water habitats of the Mimbres River drainage, an isolated basin in southwestern New Mexico with no outlet to the sea (endorheic system).
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** and **[[Spikedace]]**: Co-occur in swift riffle-run habitats with cobble/gravel substrates across the Gila River mainstem and its major forks (West Fork, Middle Fork) and tributaries (San Francisco River, Tularosa River) — classic indicator species for healthy, unregulated warmwater stream reaches.
- **[[Pecos Gambusia]]**: Restricted to spring-fed aquatic habitats with stable temperature and chemistry, as evidenced by its collection at Blue Spring, Eddy Co. — part of the disjunct spring systems of the [[Pecos River]] basin, an area characterized by relict, isolated gambusiid populations.

## Key Findings
- **Active Recovery Program for Gila Trout**: Extensive population monitoring across five distinct Gila drainage streams; over 1,000 individuals handled. Notably, 5 specimens were retained specifically for **disease analysis** in partnership with Alchesay National Fish Hatchery, and gametes from 37 adults were used to produce ~1,200 fertilized eggs sent to Mescalero National Fish Hatchery for captive propagation.
- **Successful Stocking Event**: Approximately 1,100 hatchery-reared Gila Trout fingerlings were released into Mogollon Creek, demonstrating an active augmentation strategy to bolster wild populations.
- **Telemetry Study on Colorado Squawfish**: Three individuals (across three separate dates) were fitted with radio transmitters in the San Juan River, indicating a longitudinal movement/habitat-use study for this endangered large-river cyprinid.
- **Confirmed Persistence of Chihuahua Chub**: Despite low capture numbers, the species was confirmed present in the Mimbres River at both a private/spring-fed site (Moreno Springs) and NMDGF property, suggesting continued but limited distribution.
- **Robust Detection of Loach Minnow and Spikedace**: Both small-bodied cyprinids were detected in high numbers across multiple Gila River Basin sites (San Francisco River, Tularosa River, Gila River mainstem, Middle and West Forks), suggesting these critical habitat reaches continued to support viable populations as of late 1992.
- **Minimal Impact Sampling Methodology**: Across nearly all survey events, the vast majority of captured specimens were released alive, reflecting a conservation-oriented, minimally invasive sampling protocol; only a small number of individuals per species were retained as vouchers or for disease/genetic analysis.
- **Multi-Basin Scope**: The report spans three major New Mexico drainages — the [[Gila River Basin]], [[San Juan River]] (Colorado River Basin), and [[Pecos River]] Basin — illustrating the geographic breadth of federally coordinated endangered species monitoring in the state during this period.
