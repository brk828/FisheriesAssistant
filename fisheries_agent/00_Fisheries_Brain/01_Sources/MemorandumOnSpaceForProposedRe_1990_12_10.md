## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary scientific paper but an internal administrative memorandum from the Arizona State University (ASU) Department of Zoology, authored by Chair J. Collins, addressed to the Associate Dean of CLAS. It concerns laboratory and storage space requirements tied to two funded/proposed research programs on North American freshwater cyprinid fishes: (1) a study on the [[Humpback Chub]] (PIs: [[Michael Douglas]] and [[Paul Marsh]]), and (2) a four-part taxonomic research program on the genus [[Gila]] (PIs: [[W.L. Minckley]], [[Thomas Dowling]], [[Michael Douglas]], and [[Paul Marsh]]), funded through the Bureau of Reclamation. While no direct morphological or ecological data on the fish themselves are presented, the memo provides valuable metadata on the *research methodology* being applied to Gila taxonomy — specifically morphological analysis, protein electrophoresis, and DNA-based approaches — and documents the institutional/logistical context (funding, staffing, lab space) surrounding a major systematics research effort on this genus.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data on the [[Humpback Chub]] or other [[Gila]] species are reported in this text. However, the memo references a dedicated research schedule explicitly targeting morphology:

- **"Gila Taxonomy – Schedule I: Morphology"** (PI: [[Michael Douglas]]) — a 4-year, $267,343 project intended to characterize morphological variation within the genus *Gila* as part of a broader taxonomic revision effort.
- This morphological study was to be complemented by two molecular approaches (see Key Findings), reflecting an integrative taxonomic strategy combining traditional morphometrics with protein and DNA-based methods — a common approach for resolving cryptic or contested species boundaries in [[Cyprinidae]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document does not describe habitat use, diet, or ecological interactions directly. Contextually, however:

- The [[Humpback Chub]] and other *Gila* species inhabit the [[Colorado River Basin]], a system historically associated with high endemism and taxonomic uncertainty among native cyprinids.
- The scale and urgency of the funded taxonomic work (four parallel schedules: morphology, protein electrophoresis, DNA, and field sampling) implies that species boundaries within *Gila* were poorly resolved at the time, likely with direct implications for conservation status and management of imperiled Colorado River fishes such as the [[Humpback Chub]].
- Field sampling activities ("Gila Taxonomy – Sampling," PIs: [[Paul Marsh]] and [[W.L. Minckley]]) required significant new field equipment (600 ft² of storage), indicating substantial fieldwork across the species' native range was planned.

## Key Findings

- **Humpback Chub Proposal**: 5-year budget, $1,398,765 total, PIs Douglas and Marsh; reported as likely funded by ASU at the time of writing.
- **Gila Genus Taxonomy Program** (submitted to the Bureau of Reclamation, split into four linked proposals after revision):
  - *Schedule I – Morphology* (Douglas): $267,343 over 4 years.
  - *Schedule II – Protein Electrophoresis* (Dowling): $275,574 over 4 years.
  - *Schedule III – DNA* (Dowling): $316,793 over 4 years.
  - *Sampling* (Marsh & Minckley): $45,830 over 4 years, supporting Schedules I–III.
- This multi-schedule design reflects a deliberate integration of **morphological, biochemical (allozyme/protein electrophoresis), and molecular (DNA) methods** to resolve taxonomic uncertainty within the genus [[Gila]] — an approach notable for its early use of combined molecular and morphological systematics in freshwater fish taxonomy (circa 1990–1991).
- Funding probabilities varied by schedule: high probability for Schedule I, good probability for Schedule II or III individually, and low probability that all four (Schedules I–III plus Sampling) would be simultaneously funded — indicating resource constraints on a comprehensive taxonomic resolution effort.
- Logistical outcomes (lab space, field equipment storage, personnel housing for postdocs and technicians) were explicitly tied to *which combination* of proposals was funded, underscoring how administrative capacity can directly gate the scope of taxonomic and systematic fish research.
- No biological, morphometric, or genetic results are presented in this document; it is purely a planning/funding-status record relevant to the research history and institutional support behind [[Gila]] systematics studies.
