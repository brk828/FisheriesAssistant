# A Revision of the Fishes of the Subfamily Alfarinae in the Family Poeciliidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1952 taxonomic revision by Donn Eric Rosen, published in *Zoologica* (New York Zoological Society), re-examines the poeciliid subfamily [[Alfarinae]]. It compares the Central American genera [[Alfaro]] and [[Furcipenis]], which were long kept separate, and concludes that they should be merged. Rosen argues that the features used to separate them are minor, variable, or shared across populations. He therefore proposes suppressing [[Furcipenis]] as a synonym of [[Alfaro]] and recognizing two species: [[Alfaro Cultratus]] and [[Alfaro Huberi]].

## Background & Description

**Historical placement.** The affinities of [[Alfaro]] were debated for decades:

- [[Regan]] (1913) linked it with [[Tomeurus]], because both have a ventral keel of scales on the caudal peduncle.
- [[Hubbs]] (1924) grouped the two genera in the subfamily Tomeurinae.
- In 1926 Hubbs reversed this, judging the ventral keels non-homologous, and moved the alfarins to the Poeciliinae. [[Tomeurus]] was left as the single species *Tomeurus gracilis*.
- [[Fowler]] (1923) described the species *huberi* as *Priapichthys huberi* because of its resemblance to *P. annectens*.
- Hubbs (1931) later found poeciliin features in it, and [[Howell Rivero & Hubbs]] (1936) placed both genera in the separate subfamily Alfarinae.

**Diagnostic features of Alfarinae** (after Howell Rivero & Hubbs, 1936):

- A well-developed or incipient keel of scales on the ventral margin of the caudal peduncle.
- Short [[Gonopodium]], with a well-developed [[Gonopodial Hood]] on the anterior margin of ray 3.
- Gonopodial rays without serrae; gonapophyses without uncinatoid processes.
- Simple gonopodial suspensorium.

**Additional alfarin traits** identified by Rosen:

- A strongly compressed body, with the dorsal fin origin closer to the caudal base than to the skull. This gives a streamlined, [[Gambusia]]-like appearance.
- Confluent interorbital and postorbital head pores, forming a deep frontal furrow.
- A series of lateral wings on the gonopodial ray 3 spines, which may partly support the enlarged gonopodial hood.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** The genus [[Alfaro]] occurs in Central America (distribution map, Text-fig. 10). [[Alfaro Huberi]] has been collected in Honduras, including the inland Department of Cortez and the coastal Department of Atlántida, about 10 km west of Ceiba. [[Alfaro Cultratus]] is known from Costa Rica.

**Habitat.** The document describes the habitat of both species as clear, shaded, rocky streams. [[Alfaro Huberi]] also occurs in lakes, notably [[Lake Yojoa]] in Cortez, Honduras.

**Population variation.** Two geographic populations of *Alfaro huberi* were collected by [[Myron Gordon]] in Honduras in 1951. Their urosomal keel differs by locality:

- Inland Cortez specimens show a slender keel.
- Coastal Atlántida specimens show a fully expressed keel, especially in adult males, with the median scale row reduced to a few remnants or absent.

**Coloration.** *A. huberi* has two melanophore layers (scale and dermal). The dermal layer forms a reticular pattern, sometimes with lateral streaks and a black belly blotch. *A. cultratus* lacks reticulations, having only a fine dusting and a thin lateral streak.

## Key Findings

**1. Generic synonymy is recommended.** The two genera agree in most morphology, especially in genitalic elements. Their differences are small and constant, but they are not enough to support two monotypic genera. Rosen proposes that *Furcipenis* be suppressed and its species placed in *Alfaro*, which has priority.

**2. The urosomal keel is not a reliable criterion.** The keel was the "most trenchant" argument for separating the genera. However, it is strongly expressed in some geographic populations of *Furcipenis*, so it cannot be used to separate the alfarin genera.

**3. Gonopodial differences are minor.** Three differences are noted:

- Serrae on ray 4a are present in *Furcipenis* but absent in *Alfaro*.
- Proximal spines of ray 3 are angular in *Furcipenis* but not in *Alfaro*.
- The gonopodial hood is larger and extends farther in *Furcipenis*.

**4. Meristic and axial differences are variable.** The table below summarizes the comparison.

| Character | *Furcipenis* | *Alfaro* |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral scales | 30 or 31 | 31 to 33 |
| Dorsal fin rays | 9 or 10 | 7 |
| Caudal vertebrae bearing ribs | 4 | 6 |
| Coloration | Heavily reticulated; black belly blotch | No reticulations or dark markings |

Rosen notes that these counts have never been used as a basis for generic separation in poeciliids, because of their variability. Gordon & Benzer (1945) report similar variation within [[Xiphophorus]].

**5. Comparison with related genera.** Rosen cites the synonymy of [[Xiphophorus]] and [[Platypoecilus]] as a precedent. In that group, each of the seven *Xiphophorus* species shares several features with the others. Rosen also notes that [[Gambusia]] contains at least five species groups, each with a distinct geographic range.

**6. Taxonomic conclusions.**

- The two species differ "only superficially, or at most statistically."
- Merging the genera highlights their close phylogenetic relationship and reduces the risk of exaggerating small differences.
- Final synonymy:
  - *Alfaro* Meek, 1912 (senior name, with *Petalosoma* Regan and *Petalurichthys* Regan as junior synonyms).
  - *[[Alfaro Cultratus]]* (Regan, 1908), with *A. acutiventralis* Meek as a synonym.
  - *[[Alfaro Huberi]]* (Fowler, 1923), formerly *Furcipenis huberi* Hubbs, 1931.

**7. Identification key.** The paper provides a dichotomous key for the alfarin species. *A. huberi* has dark body reticulations, a keel that is well developed or incipient, angulate ray-3 spines, and a hood reaching or passing the fin tip. *A. cultratus* lacks reticulations, has a sharply keeled caudal peduncle, vertical ray-3 spines, and a hood that does not reach the fin tip.

**8. Related genera.** The key also separates the alfarins from the related genera [[Mollienesia]], [[Poecilia]], [[Micropoecilia]], [[Parapoecilia]], [[Allopoecilia]], [[Limia]], and [[Lebistes]], which have serrated ray 4a.
