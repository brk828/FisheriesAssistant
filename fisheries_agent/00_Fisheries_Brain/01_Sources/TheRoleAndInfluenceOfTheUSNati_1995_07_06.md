## Executive Summary

This source document is **not a fish science, ichthyology, or aquatic ecology paper**. It is a policy and administrative analysis of the U.S. National Science Foundation (NSF) grant-making process, authored by James McCullough (NSF Senior Executive), presented at a 1993 symposium on research grants management in Canberra, Australia. The paper describes how NSF program officers review, evaluate, and award research grants across all scientific disciplines (mathematics, physics, geosciences, biosciences, engineering, social sciences, education), compares NSF's practices to those of funding agencies in Australia, the UK, Canada, and the U.S. National Institutes of Health (NIH), and evaluates the "Small Grant for Exploratory Research" (SGER) mechanism.

## Key Findings

Although irrelevant to aquatic/fish science, for completeness the paper's substantive (non-biological) findings are:

- NSF operates as an independent U.S. federal agency governed by a 24-member presidentially appointed National Science Board, distinct from mission-driven agencies like NIH, NASA, or EPA.
- NSF program officers exercise significant independent discretion in funding decisions, integrating external peer review with personal expertise and Foundation policy — a stronger independent role than analogous officers in Canada, Australia, UK, or NIH.
- Roughly 30,000 proposals are reviewed annually across ~170 NSF programs, with about one-third funded; ~160,000 external reviews are processed yearly from over 50,000 volunteer reviewers.
- Review styles vary by field: ad hoc mail review (math, physics, engineering, computer science), panel review (education), or a hybrid (biosciences, social sciences, geosciences) — notably, **biosciences programs are cited as a field employing panel-based review**, one of the only tangential references to a life-science-adjacent field in the text.
- The "Small Grant for Exploratory Research" (SGER) mechanism (est. ~1990) allows individual program officers to fund high-risk, exploratory proposals (<$50,000) without external peer review, using up to 5% of their budgets; in FY1992, 330 SGER proposals were submitted (mostly in engineering and biosciences) with a 62% funding rate.
- Proposals are funded, on average, at ~70% of the PI's requested budget amount.
- Institutional oversight mechanisms include division director signoff, separation of "scientific" from "business" grant approval, triennial external Committees of Visitors review, and Inspector General audits.
