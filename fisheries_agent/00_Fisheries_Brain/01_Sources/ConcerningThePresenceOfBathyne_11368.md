# Bathynella natans stammeri Jakobi (Crustacea: Syncarida)

#Bathynellidae #Syncarida #groundwater-fauna #stygobiont-crustaceans

## Executive Summary

This 1965 taxonomic and morphological study by Serban and Gledhill compares two geographically distant (~2000 km) populations of the subterranean syncarid crustacean **[[Bathynella Natans Stammeri]]** — one from **Great Douk Cave** in Yorkshire, England, and one from **Pestera Lazului** in Gorj, Oltenia, Rumania. Despite the large geographic separation, the two populations show remarkable morphological uniformity, particularly in mandibular structure, the male 8th pereiopod, and uropod/furca chaetotaxy. The paper provides the first detailed description of the basal region of the male 8th pereiopod, a structure of high taxonomic value that had previously been oversimplified in the literature. The authors also raise broader systematic questions about the validity of subspecies-level classification within **[[Bathynella Natans]]**, suggesting that *B. n. stammeri* and *B. n. natans*, found co-occurring in the same Rumanian cave, may be reproductively isolated and thus warrant recognition as distinct species rather than subspecies.

## Background & Description

**General body size:**
- Great Douk Cave specimens (England): 0.8–1.3 mm
- Pestera Lazului specimens (Rumania): 0.7–1.0 mm (smaller than English population)

**Head appendages** (antennule, antenna, mandible, maxillule, maxilla examined):
- The **mandible**, particularly the masticatory surface (incisor and molar processes), is of greatest taxonomic interest; Jakobi (1954) first used mandibular structure to distinguish four German races of *B. natans*: *stammeri*, *nolli*, *haineae*, and *freiburgensis*.
- Mandibular morphology in both English and Rumanian specimens matches Jakobi's original description of *B. n. stammeri*.

**Male 8th pereiopod (genital appendage) — key diagnostic structure:**
- Composed of a well-developed **sympodite**, an **exopodite** of similar length, and a small **endopodite**.
- The sympodite is markedly bulkier at its base, housing a previously undescribed complex of four structures:
  - **Anterior plate (a):** Rectangular, frontally placed, with a distinctive rhombic prolongation at the lower external angle (previously misidentified by Jakobi as a "spine"); bears three spines near its distal margin; partially covered by a membranous structure.
  - **Internal lobe (b):** Cone-shaped, rounded apex, <0.02 mm long, attached to the proximal region by a narrow neck.
  - **Large lobe (c):** The largest structure in the complex; forms the outer boundary of the whole assembly and overlaps part of the sympodite's base.
  - **Small lobe (d):** Positioned between the large lobe and internal lobe; appears as two finger-like projections joined at their proximal ends.
- Developmental observations (from an incompletely developed male) indicate:
  1. Internal lobes develop **before** anterior plates.
  2. Anterior plates originate internally and undergo an **outward rotary migration** during development to reach their final position.
  3. The anterior plate is **not** a true segment/basipodite of the pereiopod but a structure superimposed over the sympodite's frontal hollow.

**Female 8th pereiopod and other appendages:**
- **Epipodite** well developed, roughly equal in length to the combined basis + exopodite.
- **Exopodite** (bearing two terminal setae) much larger than the endopodite — differing from the "standard" equal-sized condition described for *B. natans* Vejd. by other specialists.
- Group of setae present on inner distal angle of the coxa (as also noted by Jakobi 1954).
- Genital pereiopods smaller than the pleopods.
- Female 8th pereiopod morphology closely matches Jakobi's (1954) figures for certain German populations (e.g., Binghöhle).

**Uropods and furca:**
- **Uropodal basipodite:** Consistently bears **4 spines** in all examined specimens (contrasting with Jakobi's claim of high variability in spine number).
- **Furca:** One spine is always notably longer than the others, but never reaches the length typically described/illustrated for nominal *B. natans* Vejd.; chaetotaxy pattern matches Jakobi's (1954) figures for the Möhrendorf population.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Strictly subterranean/groundwater environments — cave pools and interstitial sediment (gravel, sand, detritus) at the bottom of cave water bodies.
- **Collection method (England):** Gravel from a cave pool bottom sieved through two mesh sizes; fine sediment washed and examined under microscope.
- **Collection method (Rumania):** Karaman–Chappuis method (a standard technique for sampling phreatic/interstitial groundwater fauna).
- **Co-occurrence:** In Pestera Lazului, Rumania, *B. n. stammeri* (23 specimens: 9♂, 14♀) and *B. n. natans* (27 specimens) were found **together in the same cave**, within the recognized zoogeographic range of *B. natans natans* (sensu Kulhavy) — a finding of major biogeographic significance, suggesting sympatric occurrence of two forms previously thought to be geographically or subspecifically separate.
- **Biogeographic distribution:** *B. natans stammeri* is now documented across a wide geographic range — England, Germany, Italy, Czechoslovakia, and Rumania — indicating broad distribution and morphological stability of this taxon across highly disjunct groundwater systems, a pattern relevant to the study of [[Stygobiont Biogeography]] and subterranean crustacean dispersal.

## Key Findings

1. **First detailed description** of the basal complex of the male 8th pereiopod sympodite (anterior plate, internal lobe, large lobe, small lobe) in *Bathynella natans stammeri*, revealing a far more complex structure than previously reported in the specialist literature.
2. **Morphological uniformity** between English and Rumanian populations of *B. n. stammeri* across ~2000 km, despite minor size differences (English specimens larger: 0.8–1.3 mm vs. Rumanian 0.7–1.0 mm).
3. **Reinterpretation of the "rhombic prolongation"**: previously described by Jakobi (1954) as a spine, but reidentified here as an actual structural prolongation of the anterior plate.
4. **Developmental sequence proposed**: internal lobes form first; anterior plates develop basally and rotate outward during maturation; anterior plate is not a true pereiopod segment (not a basipodite).
5. **Constancy of uropodal (4 spines) and furcal chaetotaxy** across both populations challenges Jakobi's (1954) assertion of high variability in these features within *B. natans*, suggesting possible mixing of forms in some German populations he studied.
6. **Taxonomic implications**: The co-occurrence of *B. n. stammeri* and *B. n. natans* in the same Rumanian cave (Pestera Lazului) suggests **reproductive isolation**, arguing against treating *stammeri* as a mere subspecies or lower taxonomic category (contra Delamare Deboutteville 1960 and Sterba 1963) and toward recognition as a **distinct species**.
7. **Broader systematic controversy raised**: structural differences in the male 8th pereiopod between *B. natans* Vejd. (sensu Jakobi) and populations previously assigned to **[[Bathynella Chappuisi]]** Delachaux (from Transylvania, Rumania) support treating these as distinct species — a problem the authors flag for further dedicated study.
8. Findings corroborate and extend earlier identifications of *B. n. stammeri* in Italy (Delamare Deboutteville 1960) and Czechoslovakia (Kulhavy 1961; Sterba 1963), reinforcing the taxon's wide European distribution.
