# Cui-ui Recovery Team Meeting Minutes (August 2, 1989)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

These minutes record the initial meeting of the reconvened [[Cui-ui]] Recovery Team, held August 2, 1989, in Reno, Nevada. Attendees were T. Strekal, W. Minckley, G. Scoppettone, G. Vinyard, and P. Wagner, with [[Robert Smith]] (Assistant Regional Director, Fish and Wildlife Enhancement) present at the start. The team was reconvened under the 1988 Amendments to the [[Endangered Species Act]], which emphasize recovery. The Regional Director asked for site-specific recovery actions, measurable de-listing or down-listing criteria, time and cost estimates, and public review of the plan.

The main unresolved issue is possible [[Hybridization]] between [[Cui-ui]] and [[Tahoe Sucker]]. The team concluded that the hybrid question must be answered before the recovery plan can be finalized. It planned an [[Electrophoretic Analysis]] study, a [[Request for Proposal]], and possible culling and brood-stock measures. The team also agreed that the [[Self-Sustaining Population]] concept needs modeling and that [[De-Listing]] is probably unrealistic.

## Background & Description

**Regulatory framework**
- The team is among the first to work under the 1988 Amendments to the [[Endangered Species Act]], which focus on recovery.
- The [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] was drafting recovery plan guidelines to match the amendments.
- Required plan content, to the maximum degree practicable:
  1. Site-specific management actions needed for recovery.
  2. Measurable criteria for [[De-Listing]] or [[Down-Listing]], which may be habitat-based or population-based rather than numeric.
  3. Time and cost estimates for the ultimate goal and for intermediate steps.
  4. Public involvement, through review and written comment before the Regional Director signs.
- Team proceedings are protected under the [[Federal Advisory Committee Act]]. The team may invite outside participants.
- [[Chester Buchanan]] is the Service liaison between the team and the Regional Director.
- Only biological factors must be considered. Political considerations are optional, and the Regional Director will weigh them at implementation.
- An [[Advisory Group]] of non-technical stakeholders was formed. It is expected to focus on implementation, and it is insulated from the team, with its comments passed back through filtering.
- The team may also prepare an implementation plan covering expected [[Section 7 Consultation]] and [[Section 10A Permit]] actions.

**Schedule and purpose**
- First draft of the plan due January 31, 1990. Final draft due June 30, 1991.
- The team is charged with rewriting the [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]] to address all relevant threats.
- The Regional Director wants an intermediate product showing the team's direction and approach.

**System overview**
- Study system: the [[Truckee River]]/[[Pyramid Lake]] system and [[Stampede Reservoir]] (presented by Thomas Strekal).
- Infrastructure: the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and its fishway.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Population and life history**
- Adult cui-ui are scarce, so estimating adult population size and age structure will be very difficult. Adult behavior may also reduce catchability, since adults may move less than young fish.
- Population size and mortality can be estimated for young fish (3-4 years old). Most recent trapping catches were 200-400 mm in length. Mortality estimates will be rough and will take several years.
- Many cui-ui are non-annual spawners. Histological examination of gonads may help, and some evidence links spawning frequency to environmental factors.

**Fishway effects**
- Scoppettone's team will assess mortality at the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and its effect on run age composition. The fishway may prevent older, larger fish from ascending.
- The fishway may delay spawning run timing, which may increase the chance of hybridization.

**Hybridization**
- Cui-ui may have hybridized with [[Tahoe Sucker]] during the 1980 and/or 1981 spawning runs.
- About 20-25 percent of these young adults show a few physical characteristics unlike pure cui-ui.
- Minckley's meristic counts indicated the suspect fish were typical of *Chasmistes*. Bob Miller later agreed there is some confusion.
- Human activity probably stimulated the hybrid outbreak. Earlier hybrids may have existed but were likely too few or had too low survival to affect genetic integrity.
- Any confirmed hybrids must be prevented from reproducing.

**Habitat and water**
- Review of the 1983 plan's step-down items found that spawning habitat and incubation water chemistry were completed.
- Impoundment has eliminated most threats from native and exotic animals.
- Illegal dredge and fill in the river is addressed through the 404 permit process.
- The effects of total dissolved solids (TDS) and of nitrogen and phosphorus on the lake and river are partly or largely unknown.

## Key Findings

**1. Hybrid study design (electrophoretic analysis)**
- Stage one: identify genetic markers that separate pure cui-ui from cui-ui x Tahoe sucker hybrids. Survey 15 pure cui-ui and 15 pure Tahoe suckers.
- Stage two, if stage one succeeds: use markers to separate pure cui-ui from hybrids. About 85 "hybrids" and 15 pure fish are required.
- Cost quotes: a Texas group would survey 10 to 30 loci in stage one for $600 to $4200, depending on loci count. Stage two would cost about $5000.
- Minckley will find a lab. Scoppettone will collect fish. The study should be completed by the end of the year.
- The team should formally request Service funding from the Regional Director, emphasizing urgency, the study outline, and funding needs.

**2. Hybrid management options**
- Culling may use trapping at the fishway mouth or near-shore netting. Movement across the delta must be prevented.
- Service Fishery Resources Program should consider an electric barrier across the delta this spring and a trapping facility at the fishway's terminal end.
- Culling should start as early as possible, while hybrids are small relative to pure fish.
- Physical characteristics should be studied using carcasses from the electrophoretic analysis.
- Brood-stock options: release in another lake, hold at the tribal hatchery, or tag pure individuals in Pyramid Lake. Holding non-reproducing stock in Walker Lake is cheaper than a hatchery but raises political and biological issues. The hatchery option is considered less complex.
- A geneticist should advise on how many adults to hold as fall-back brood stock.

**3. Request for Proposal (RFP) timeline**
- Outline the RFP in anticipation of hybrid confirmation. A geneticist should help, with the RFP ready by December.
- Send a draft to Fishery Resources Program and Nevada Department of Wildlife for comment.
- Develop options for evaluating hybridization. Longevity may complicate hybrid identification.
- The team will probably not have a complete answer on hybrids before 1991.

**4. Plan development**
- The plan cannot be finalized until the hybrid question is answered, because hybrid presence will shape the plan.
- Some members proposed an action plan focused on hybrids first, followed by the remaining threats.

**5. Definition of recovery**
- Recovery should be defined from a pure biological standpoint, allowing continued evolution under the species' natural trajectory (extinction, maintenance, or expansion), with the ultimate goal of no human intervention.
- Related items include water availability, changing values, and tangible benefits such as fisheries. Recovery must protect the lake's water supply, or others may claim it after recovery.
- A [[Self-Sustaining Population]] may fall between a few thousand and several hundred thousand fish, based on genetic characteristics. These figures should be adjusted upward for human use and environmental conditions.
- The spawning population should be no smaller than today, roughly 200,000, but not so high that it becomes self-limiting.

**6. De-listing and down-listing**
- De-listing is probably unreasonable. The Regional Director should be notified. De-listing cannot occur without natural habitat, and the species should need no human support.
- Down-listing should rest on criteria covering historical and projected river uses and human intervention. Intervention should be minimal in high-water years, and significant in normal and low-water years to maintain reproductive capacity.
- Down-listing criteria may use a stochastic population dynamics model that includes vital statistics, inflow, and population genetics.
- Population dynamics data are needed to set the number of year classes, year-class size, and spawning frequency for down-listing.

**7. Knowledge status (1983 step-down plan review)**
- Life history: much known.
- Population dynamics: little known.
- Environmental stimuli: more information required.
- Spawning habitat: completed.
- Water chemistry for incubation: completed.
- Lake habitat for adults: little available. Juvenile data being collected.
- Effect of TDS: basically unknown.
- Effect of nitrogen and phosphorus on the lake: being studied by UC Davis.
- Effect of nitrogen and phosphorus on the river: needs work.
- Operating the hatchery to produce 10 million larvae: impact on recovery is unknown.
- Self-sustaining population size: not identified.

**8. Summary**
- Biologists have a good grasp of the species' biology.
- The team knows how to sustain the population but cannot quantify a self-sustaining population. Modeling may provide at least a "ballpark" estimate.
- Genetic characteristics of the cui-ui population must be assessed. This information is needed to define recovery.
