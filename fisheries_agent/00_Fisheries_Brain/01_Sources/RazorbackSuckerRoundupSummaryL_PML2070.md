# Razorback Sucker Roundup Summary: Lake Mohave, AZ-NV, 2007

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This summary reports the results of three [[Razorback Sucker]] roundups conducted on [[Lake Mohave]] (Arizona-Nevada) in March, May and November 2007. The document was prepared by Carol Pacey, Brian Kesner and Paul Marsh for the [[Colorado River Aquatic Biologists Meeting]] (January 9-10, 2008, Laughlin, NV). It combines mark-recapture estimates for wild adults and [[Repatriation|repatriated]] fish, [[PIT Tagging|PIT-tagged]] capture records by rearing origin, site fidelity and movement rates among lake zones, and an analysis of size-selective mortality in repatriated [[Bonytail Chub]]. Most tabular content is partly garbled in the scanned source, so only values that can be read reliably are reported here.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] is a large catostomid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Populations in the lower basin are supported by a combination of wild adults and fish reared in off-site facilities and released back into the system. The source document is a set of presentation slides, not a full research report. It therefore lacks methods, sample-size explanations and discussion.

The roundup program used [[PIT Tagging]] (passive integrated transponder tags) to identify individual fish at capture. This allowed analysts to separate:

- **Wild adults**, with no record of hatchery or rearing origin.
- **Repatriated fish**, grouped by rearing location, including [[Boulder City Golf Course Ponds]], [[Boulder City Wetlands Park]], and lakeside backwater sites such as [[Nine Mile Cove]] and [[North Chem Cove]].
- **Fish captured without PIT tags**, which were recorded separately.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The analyses describe movement among three habitat units in and around Lake Mohave:

- **River** (riverine reach)
- **Arizona Bay**
- **Basin**

Site fidelity and transition rates among these units were estimated with [[Mark-Recapture Analysis]]. The results indicate that movement between zones is uncommon in most directions, with the exception of the flow from [[Basin]] to [[Arizona Bay]].

Size structure is also relevant. The [[Bonytail Chub]] analysis compares the total length (TL) distribution of all released fish with the TL distribution of fish that were later recaptured. This comparison is used to test for [[Size-Selective Mortality]] after release.

## Key Findings

**Wild adult population (Lake Mohave, 2006-2007)**
- The wild adult population estimate and its 95% confidence interval are shown in the source figure. The numerical values are not reliably legible in the scanned text.

**Repatriated population**
- The repatriate population estimate has a lower 95% confidence limit of **2,752** and an upper limit of **124,458**. The reported estimated survival value for repatriates is illegible in the source.

**2007 roundup captures**
- Capture tables by month (March, May, November) and by PIT-tag status exist in the source. The cell values are too garbled to report reliably. The source notes that same-trip captures were included in the analysis.
- Paired release-capture data were used for repatriate tables by rearing location.

**Site fidelity and movement (wild fish)**
Estimates from the mark-recapture model, with 95% confidence intervals:

| Parameter | Estimate | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| River to Arizona Bay | 17.9% | 12.5-25.0% |
| Arizona Bay to River | 2.7% | 1.6-4.4% |
| Arizona Bay to Basin | 19.0% | 15.2-23.6% |
| Basin to Arizona Bay | 40.0% | 32.0-48.6% |
| Basin to River | 3.2% | 1.9-5.2% |
| River to Basin | 4.4% | 2.4-7.9% |

- Survival rate estimates appear on the same slide (approximately 73.6%, 69.5% and 77.8%). Their labels are not legible in the source, so they cannot be assigned to specific categories.
- A survival estimate "without zones" of **10.7%** is listed. The accompanying confidence interval (69.4-72.0%) does not match that value, so the figure is likely a scan or transcription error and should be verified against the original analysis.
- The site fidelity slide also lists totals of 957 and 10,466 with a value of 13. Their meanings are not stated in the source.

**Size-selective mortality (repatriated Bonytail Chub)**
- The release size distribution includes **56,465** fish. A subset of **35** fish with a capture record is also shown.
- The two distributions are plotted against total length from roughly 125 to 500 mm.
- The source reports an apparent size-selective pattern in mortality. It does not provide a formal statistic for this pattern.

**Limitations**
