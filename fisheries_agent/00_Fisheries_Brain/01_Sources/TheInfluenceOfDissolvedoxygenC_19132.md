# Dissolved Oxygen and Un-Ionized Ammonia Toxicity to Rainbow Trout

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmo-gairdneri

## Executive Summary

This 1955 study by [[Kathleen M. Downing]] and J. C. Merkens tested how [[Dissolved Oxygen]] concentration modifies the lethal effect of [[Un-Ionized Ammonia]] on juvenile [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri* Richardson). Fingerling trout were exposed in controlled laboratory streams at 19.8 °C and two pH levels (7.0 and 8.0). Survival time rose as dissolved oxygen increased from about 1.5 to 8.5 ppm and fell as un-ionized ammonia rose. The protective effect of oxygen was strongest at the lowest ammonia concentration. A published erratum states that the ammonia concentrations used in the paper were miscalculated and should read 0.60, 0.93 and 1.29 ppm as N, not 0.86, 1.38 and 1.96 ppm.

## Background & Description

The authors built on earlier work on oxygen and toxicity. [[Downing 1954]] showed that oxygen reduced the toxicity of [[Potassium Cyanide]] to trout. [[Wuhrmann And Woker 1948]] showed that the poisonous action of ammonium salts depends mainly on the concentration of un-ionized ammonia. [[Wuhrmann 1952]] found that oxygen also modified ammonia toxicity to minnows over 1 to 10 ppm un-ionized ammonia. The present study asked whether the same pattern holds for rainbow trout at two pH values.

The test subjects were fingerling rainbow trout with a mean length of 7.3 cm (SD 2.5 cm), obtained at the same time from a commercial farm. About twenty-four fish were used per test. Fish were acclimatized at 19.8 °C for two days, were not fed during acclimatization or testing, and were tested in a random order.

Water was prepared from tap water brought to the required temperature. Carbonates and bicarbonates were converted to carbon dioxide with [[Hydrochloric Acid]], and the carbon dioxide was then removed by aeration until less than 1.0 ppm free CO₂ remained. [[Sodium Hydroxide]] and [[Ammonium Chloride]] were added at a constant rate to set pH and the poison concentration. Oxygen was controlled with the apparatus described by Downing (1954).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addresses the water-quality question of how ammonia pollution becomes more or less dangerous to fish depending on oxygen supply. Because the concentration of [[Un-Ionized Ammonia]] depends on pH, the authors also considered [[Ph Value]] effects. The experiment could not fully separate pH from the concentration of ammonium salt. At pH 7.0 the ammonium chloride needed to produce each un-ionized ammonia level was about ten times that needed at pH 8.0.

Conditions were tightly controlled. Temperature was held at 19.8 °C (SD ±2.3). Ammonium chloride concentration varied by ±5.9% of the mean. pH was maintained at 7.0 ± 0.05 and 8.0 ± 0.06. Dissolved oxygen varied by ±0.3 to ±0.48 ppm in three tests and by less than ±0.25 ppm in the rest.

These results are laboratory data for acute exposure, measured as survival time. They do not describe natural streams, field populations or long-term exposure.

## Key Findings

- **Oxygen protects trout:** Survival periods increased as dissolved oxygen was raised from about 1.5 to 8.5 ppm across all un-ionized ammonia concentrations.
- **Ammonia shortens survival:** At each oxygen level, survival fell as un-ionized ammonia rose. The paper reports the range 0.86–1.96 ppm N, which the erratum corrects to 0.60–1.29 ppm N.
- **Oxygen matters most at low ammonia:** The increase in survival per unit of added oxygen was greatest at the lowest ammonia concentration.
- **Curve shape:** Response curves resembled those reported for minnows by [[Wuhrmann 1952]] but differed from the potassium cyanide results of [[Downing 1954]].
- **pH interaction:** At the lowest ammonia level, fish survived longer at pH 7.0 than at pH 8.0. At higher ammonia levels the reverse usually occurred.
- **Statistical support:** Using log-transformed survival times, [[Analysis Of Variance]] showed that the dissolved oxygen effect was highly significant (mean square 42.93, marked ***). The effect of un-ionized ammonia was significant (5.03, **). The pH and ammonium salt term was not significant (1.073). The oxygen × ammonia interaction was significant at P = 0.05 (1.37, *). The residual had 510 degrees of freedom and the total 539. Log transformation was chosen because log survival times were closer to normal, as judged by the [[Bliss Method]].
- **Test design:** Thirty tests were run at five dissolved oxygen levels between 1 and 9 ppm and three un-ionized ammonia levels (equivalent to 1.96, 1.38 and 0.86 ppm N as originally calculated). Two tests, at pH 7.0 and 8.0, were run at each combination.
- **Endpoint:** Death was scored as loss of equilibrium followed by no swimming movement for 15 seconds, measured from immersion.
- **Erratum:** The corrected un-ionized ammonia values are 0.60, 0.93 and 1.29 ppm as N. Readers should use these values in any comparison with other studies.
