# Making Muskies: Muskellunge Propagation at Bemus Point Hatchery

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Esocidae #fish-culture #hatchery-management

## Executive Summary

This document describes the intensive hatchery operations for [[Muskellunge]] (Esox masquinongy) conducted by the New York State Conservation Department at [[Bemus Point]] on [[Chautauqua Lake]]. The operation involves collecting eggs from wild fish through stripping, incubating approximately 165,000 eggs per female in battery jars with constant agitation, and rearing fingerlings in outdoor ponds with carefully managed live food supplies. The process requires approximately four months of intensive labor, from ice-out in April through fingerling harvest in August, and demonstrates the specialized requirements of musky propagation including precise food size matching and extensive minnow collection operations.

## Background & Description

The [[Muskellunge]] is characterized as an apex predator with voracious feeding habits, consuming all smaller fish. Young muskies reach 3 inches in length at 3 months of age and will consume minnows 2 inches long at that stage. Growth rates are sexually dimorphic: females grow faster than males, with both sexes reaching approximately 14 years of age at 34 inches (male) and 40 inches (female). A 40-pound specimen represents the consumption of several tons of other fish over its lifetime.

Newly hatched fry are extremely selective feeders, accepting only live and moving food of particular sizes. Growth is rapid initially (4 inches in 100 days) then slows considerably. The species' predatory nature is so extreme that maintaining muskies in small ponds results in eventual single-fish dominance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Chautauqua Lake]] population serves as the broodstock source for New York State's musky propagation program. Wild fish are captured in pound nets during late April and early May spawning season, with operations beginning shortly after ice-out (April 11 in the documented season). Fish are netted, stripped of gametes, tagged for long-term monitoring (some individuals carrying tags for at least 16 years), and released unharmed.

The hatchery operation at [[Bemus Point]] represents a specialized facility requiring extensive supporting infrastructure. Minnow collection operations extend up to 60 miles from the hatchery across a dozen counties, carefully targeting waters where removal will not impact game fish food supplies. The planned postwar expansion includes a facility designed to grow its own live food, eliminating dependence on wild minnow collection.

Rearing pond preparation involves precise environmental management: chloride of lime sterilization, controlled filling, aquatic plant ([[Elodea]]) establishment, and fertilization with cottonseed meal (100-150 lbs total) to support zooplankton populations.

## Key Findings

**Egg Collection and Incubation:**
- Normal output per large female: approximately 3 quarts or 165,000 eggs
- Stripping operations: 60 fish may be processed at a single net location in one hour
- 15-16 nets deployed, some separated by 15 miles
- Operations conducted 7 days per week for nearly 3 weeks
- First eggs taken April 19; last eggs taken May 7
- Incubation period: 10-20 days depending on water temperature
- Eggs require constant agitation with feathers day and night to prevent sticking
- First hatch: May 4; last hatch: May 17

**Rearing Operations:**
- Single 0.5-acre pond production: 17,699 fingerlings weighing 1,308 lbs total
- Size distribution at harvest: 11,699 at 5 inches; 6,000 at 6 inches
- Rearing period: approximately 100 days (May 13 - August 20-30)
- Food progression: [[Daphnia]] for newly hatched fry → [[Carp]] fry (45 quarts eggs taken May 22, hatched May 26, stocked May 27) → progressively larger minnows
- Carp fry only accepted before scale development
- Approximately 1 million carefully sorted minnows consumed per season
- Muskies first appear at surface May 22 after egg sac absorption
- Artesian well water mixed with lake water beginning May 6

**Facility Operations:**
- Battery jar system for indoor incubation with 24-hour monitoring
- Indoor hatchery troughs stocked with [[Elodea]] and [[Daphnia]] (May 11), then fry (May 15)
- Later-stage fry transferred from troughs to outdoor pond (June 20)
- Multi-generational operation: Art Redband Sr. continuing work of his father
- Field crew: Mark Bue, Tommy Thompson, Art Redband Jr., Don Pasco (fish researcher)
- Supporting infrastructure: 2,500 yards of deep nets and leaders with tons of supporting poles

**Management Implications:**
- Fingerlings distributed throughout New York's musky waters
- World's largest musky hatchery planned for construction north of [[Bemus Point]] under postwar program
- Long-term tagging program provides population data spanning at least 16 years
- Scale samples collected for age and growth analysis
