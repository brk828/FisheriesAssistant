## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology and water quality**
- Water enters and exits the basin through underground springs.
- Water is very clear, with visibility of about 300 feet or more.
- The color of the [[Pozas Azules]] comes from carbonate particles mixed with [[Phytoplankton]]. The deepest area is 45 feet.
- The Rio Mesquites is tapped about 100 miles away for the steel industry. Its water is unusable for crops or drinking because of high mineral content, mostly gypsum.

**Geology**
- [[Gypsum Dunes]] were built over eons by evaporation (leaving gypsum behind) and wind. Captions state they are now only about 20 percent of their former extent because gypsum is mined for the drywall industry.
- An ancient [[Travertine]] waterway is made from fossilized [[Stromatolites]].

**Food web and trophic structure**
- The captions say the entire wetland ecology is based on snails. Snails and their byproducts serve as a food source for many endemic fish.
- Fish dive head-first into silt to gather a mouthful of [[Detritus]], a byproduct of the snails, and eject material they cannot use.
- Cichlids clear silt to expose snails, which they crush with specialized teeth.

**Biodiversity and endemism**
- The captions state there are more than 50 recognized endemic plant and animal species in the basin.
- Aquatic life is abundant. Hundreds of fish gather around swimmers to bite at their skin.
- Other organisms pictured include a [[Possum Shrimp]] carrying eggs and waterlilies that can bloom below the surface.

## Key Findings

1. **Rarity of habitat:** The basin is one of only five desert springfield systems on Earth, and it is described as the smallest yet most diverse of the five.
2. **Endemic fish community:** The fish include [[Endemic Cichlids]], [[Tetras]], [[Coahuilan Pupfish]], and minnows. The captions note that endemic cichlids look very similar but have very different feeding habits, and that some scientists consider them distinct species.
3. **Snail-based ecology:** Snails support the wetland food web directly (as cichlid prey) and indirectly (through detritus that fish feed on).
4. **Endemic symbol species:** The [[Aquatic Coahuilan Box Turtle]] lives in water, unlike most box turtles, and is the symbol of the region. Local people use it in artwork.
5. **Aggressive pupfish behavior:** Coahuilan pupfish are described as very fast and aggressive, with one captured interaction showing a male biting a rival's anal fin.
6. **Threats:** Gypsum mining has reduced the dunes to about 20 percent of their former size. Water extraction for industry (the Rio Mesquites) makes river water unusable for crops or drinking because of mineral content.
7. **Conservation-relevant local knowledge:** The captions include a local naturalist, Pepe Lugo, examining the fossil travertine waterway, indicating local expertise in the basin's natural history.
