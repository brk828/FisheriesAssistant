# Vertebrate Checklist for Arizona and New Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #vertebrate-checklist

## Executive Summary

This document is a Forest Service checklist of the vertebrate fauna of [[Arizona]] and [[New Mexico]], issued as the Wildlife Habitat Technical Bulletin No. 5 of the U.S.D.A. Forest Service Southwest Region. It was compiled by R. Patton, Principal Wildlife Biologist, at the Mountain Forest and Range Experiment Station in Tempe, Arizona, and David R. Rocky. The checklist was prepared to store wildlife data systematically for the 1979 Wildlife Assessment required by the Resource Planning Act of 1974 and for use in the [[RUN WILD]] information storage and retrieval system. It covers five vertebrate groups: amphibians, birds, fishes, mammals and reptiles. Species are listed with coded status and occurrence flags, and each entry carries a RUN WILD number where one has been assigned. The text is primarily a reference list rather than a research study.

## Background & Description

The checklist was prepared primarily for use by the eleven National Forests in Arizona and New Mexico, though the author hopes it will be useful to other biologists. Scientific and common names follow named authorities for each group, including [[Stebbins Field Guide to Western Reptiles and Amphibians]], [[Koster Guide to the Fishes of New Mexico]], [[Minckley Fishes of Arizona]], [[Findley Mammals of New Mexico]], and [[Phillips Birds of Arizona]]. The author notes that taxonomists differ on names, so regional or state publications were used where needed. A species was included if it occurs in either state; when its presence was in doubt it was left out.

Coding conventions used in the list:

- **A**: occurs in Arizona; **N**: occurs in New Mexico
- **\***: threatened or endangered species or subspecies
- **1**: introduced species; **2**: peripheral, accidental, rare or uncommon occurrence; **3**: feral
- A leading "+" marks continuation of a one-word name (for example, bullfrog)

The checklist is meant to be updated periodically, and subspecies were planned for a second edition around 1980.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document lists aquatic and riparian-associated species but does not describe habitats, water chemistry, plankton, or invertebrate communities. Its only environmental references are to status (threatened or endangered), range and distribution, and to regional fish surveys such as the survey of the Pecos, Canadian and Arkansas drainages in New Mexico. Fish entries are the most directly relevant to aquatic ecology, and include native and introduced species such as [[Rio Grande Killifish]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Colorado River Squawfish]], and various [[Pupfish]] and [[Trout]] species.

## Key Findings

- **Amphibians (Group 1):** 27 species, including [[Bullfrog]], [[Woodhouse's Toad]], and several spadefoot toads. Several are coded as threatened or endangered, and some as introduced or peripheral.
- **Birds (Group 2):** about 300 species, the largest group, spanning waterfowl, raptors, shorebirds, and passerines. Many are coded as occurring in both states, and several are coded as peripheral or accidental.
- **Fishes (Group 3):** about 105 species, the most aquatic-focused group, including basses, catfishes, chubs, dace, minnows, pupfishes, shiners, suckers, salmonids, and sunfishes. Many are coded as introduced (1), threatened or endangered (\*), or both. Notable species include the threatened [[Humpback Chub]], [[Gila Trout]], and [[Woundfin]], and introduced species such as [[Common Carp]] and [[Goldfish]].
- **Mammals (Group 4):** about 150 species, including bats, rodents, carnivores, and ungulates. Entries include [[Pronghorn]], [[Black Bear]], [[Mountain Lion]], and several bat and pocket mouse species.
- **Reptiles (Group 5):** about 120 species, comprising lizards, snakes, turtles, and whiptails. Many snakes are venomous rattlesnakes or coral snakes. Turtles include [[Desert Tortoise]] and [[Painted Turtle]].
- The checklist is intended to be a living document. The author requested additional records and species information from users.
