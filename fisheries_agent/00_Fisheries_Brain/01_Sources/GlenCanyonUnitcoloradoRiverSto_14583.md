# Glen Canyon Unit Colorado River Storage Project Reservoir Fisheries Investigation Creel Census and Plankton Studies February 1965 to January 1966

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #salmonidae #ictaluridae #reservoir-ecology #limnology

## Executive Summary

This report documents the second year (February 1, 1965 - January 31, 1966) of cooperative fisheries investigation at [[Lake Powell]] ([[Glen Canyon Reservoir]]) conducted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and [[Utah Fish and Game Department]]. The study combined systematic creel census operations at [[Wahweap Marina]] and [[Hall's Crossing Marina]] with monthly plankton sampling from 12 stations throughout the reservoir. Creel census documented 5,804 angler trips at Wahweap producing 17,529 fish in 30,531 hours, with estimated total pressure of 17,516 trips yielding 54,213 fish. The creel composition was dominated by [[Largemouth Bass]] (60%), [[Rainbow Trout]] (30%), and [[Channel Catfish]] (6%). Plankton studies identified 14 zooplankton genera and 16 phytoplankton genera, with highest densities reaching 109.7 organisms per liter at the Rincon station in June. Night fishing showed notably higher success rates (1.35 fish per hour) compared to daytime boat fishing (0.57 fish per hour).

## Background & Description

[[Lake Powell]] is formed by [[Glen Canyon Dam]] located at Page, Arizona, authorized under Section 8 of the [[Colorado River Storage Project]] (Public Law 485). At the study period's close, the reservoir stood at approximately 3,540 feet elevation; when filled it will reach 3,700 feet and extend approximately 185 miles upstream. This investigation represents the second study segment of ongoing fisheries management research.

The creel census methodology evolved during the study period. Initially (February-September 1965), census was conducted two days per week at Wahweap Marina (one weekday, one weekend day). This was modified in the final four months to six days per month at Wahweap (three weekdays, three weekend days) plus two days per month at Hall's Crossing Marina. Additional specialized censuses targeted night anglers (summer and fall) and shore anglers (winter months). The dock-based census approach proved highly effective because Wahweap Marina provided the only access to the lower reservoir, and Hall's Crossing offered the only gasoline station for 40 miles in the upper reservoir.

Plankton sampling employed a closing net of #20 mesh to collect 100-foot vertical water columns (five inches diameter) from 12 stations distributed throughout the reservoir. Samples were preserved in 5% formalin and analyzed quantitatively using [[Sedgwick-Rafter Counting Chamber]] methodology following P.S. Welch's limnological methods. Zooplankton organisms were counted in triplicate subsamples under binocular microscope at 3.5x to 20x magnification. Periodic stratified samples examined depth zones: below 100 feet, 100-50 feet, 50-25 feet, and 25 feet to surface at three major control points ([[Wahweap Channel]], [[San Juan River]], [[Hall's Crossing]]).

The fish community consisted primarily of three species: [[Micropterus salmoides]] (largemouth bass), [[Oncorhynchus mykiss]] (rainbow trout), and [[Ictalurus punctatus]] (channel catfish), with minor contributions from [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Carp]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Lake Powell]] represents a newly filling reservoir system in the Colorado River basin, creating a transitional aquatic ecosystem. The reservoir's plankton community showed typical stratification patterns with distinct vertical zonation. The upper 100 feet contained the most productive plankton zone, with densities ranging from 109.7 organisms per liter (June, Rincon station) to 0.1 organisms per liter (September, Warm Creek; January, Rincon).

Dominant zooplankton genera included [[Daphnia]] (153 occurrences), [[Cyclops]] (155 occurrences), [[Diaptomus]] (101 occurrences), and the rotifer [[Keratella]] (79 occurrences). Copepod nauplii larvae were nearly ubiquitous (157 occurrences). Phytoplankton assemblages were dominated by diatoms [[Fragilaria]] (84 occurrences) and [[Golenkinia]] (63 occurrences), the desmid [[Staurastrum]] (84 occurrences), and the green alga [[Pediastrum]] (46 occurrences).

Notable ecological events included a massive bloom of the blue-green alga [[Anacystis]] in the [[Kane Creek]]-[[Last Chance Creek]] area in October, so dense it could be scooped from the surface. The largest plankton volume (11 ml) was collected at [[Tapestry Wall]] station in July, containing large quantities of the desmid [[Gonatozygon]]. Freshwater [[Hydra]] were collected twice: 120 per sample at Warm Creek in June and 30 per sample at Rock Creek in November.

Fishing pressure distribution reflected both accessibility and productivity. The [[Wahweap]] area received 44.7% of angler trips due to proximity to the launch area. However, "hot spots" like [[Last Chance Creek]] (13.6% of effort, 17.5% of catch, 0.71 fish/hour) and [[San Juan River]] (8.7% of effort, 10.8% of catch, 0.70 fish/hour) attracted disproportionate pressure due to higher catch rates.

## Key Findings

**Creel Census - Wahweap Marina (Daytime):**
- 5,804 angler trips interviewed on 91 census days produced 17,529 fish in 30,531 hours
- Estimated total pressure: 17,516 trips producing 54,213 fish in 91,811 hours
- Creel composition: 60% largemouth bass (32,600 fish), 30% rainbow trout (16,376 fish), 6% channel catfish (3,044 fish), 4% other species (2,193 fish, mostly green sunfish)
- Average success rate: 0.57 fish per hour
- Average trip: 5.26 hours producing 3.02 fish
- Monthly success ranged from 0.47 fish/hour (June) to 0.74 fish/hour (November)

**Creel Census - Hall's Crossing Marina:**
- 134 trips interviewed on 8 census days produced 179 fish in 300 hours
- Estimated total pressure (October 1965-January 1966): 1,445 trips producing 3,253 fish in 5,025 hours
- Creel composition: 96% largemouth bass (3,130 fish), 1% rainbow trout (31 fish), 3% channel catfish (92 fish)
- Average success rate: 0.60 fish per hour
- Average trip: 2.24 hours producing 1.34 fish

**Night Fishing Census - Wahweap Area:**
- 79 angler trips checked (July-November 1965) produced 305 fish in 214 hours
- Estimated pressure: 564 trips producing 2,605 fish in 1,790 hours
- Creel composition: 84% rainbow trout (2,162 fish), 11% largemouth bass (286 fish), 4.6% channel catfish (118 fish)
- Average success rate: 1.35 fish per hour (highest of all fishing methods)

**Shore Fishing Census - Wahweap Area:**
- 53 trips checked produced 93 fish in 150 hours
- Estimated pressure (November 1965-January 1966): 617 trips producing 1,081 fish in 1,746 hours
- Success rate: 0.62 fish per hour
- Creel composed almost entirely of rainbow trout

**Angler Origin:**
- Wahweap Marina: 66.9% Arizona residents (21.7% from Page alone), 15.1% Utah residents, 18% other states
- Hall's Crossing Marina: 41.8% Utah residents, 25.5% New Mexico residents, 21% Colorado residents

**Spatial Distribution of Fishing Effort (Wahweap Marina):**
From 5,715 trips with location data:
- Wahweap area: 44.7% (2,554 trips)
- Navajo area: 15.2% (871 trips)
- Last Chance Creek: 11.2% (639 trips)
- Warm Creek: 8.9% (507 trips)
- San Juan River: 6.7% (382 trips)
- Rock Creek: 5.9% (341 trips)
- Kane Creek: 4.1% (233 trips)
- Above San Juan: 3.3% (188 trips)

**Plankton Community Structure:**
- 160 samples collected from 12 stations throughout study period
- 14 zooplankton genera and 16 phytoplankton genera identified
- Highest zooplankton density: 44,525 organisms per sample (109.7/liter) at Rincon station, June
- Lowest density: 0.1 organisms/liter at Warm Creek (September) and Rincon (January)
- Maximum plankton volume: 11 ml at Tapestry Wall, July (dominated by [[Gonatozygon]])
- Most prevalent zooplankton: [[Daphnia]], [[Cyclops]], [[Diaptomus]], [[Keratella]]
- Most prevalent phytoplankton: [[Fragilaria]], [[Golenkinia]], [[Staurastrum]], [[Anacystis]]

**Car Counter Correlation:**
- Entrance road counter: 9.46 clicks per angler trip (average of monthly means)
- Ramp counter (September 1965-January 1966): 9.98 clicks per angler trip
- Notable variation from previous study period (5.56 clicks/trip), indicating need for continued calibration

**Warm Creek Access Point (June-July 1965):**
- 42 anglers checked produced 324 fish in 513 hours
- Estimated pressure: 640 trips producing 4,957 fish in 7,803 hours
- Success rate: 0.63 fish per hour
- August-January car counter data: 684 estimated angler trips

**Recommendations:**
1. Continue creel census at Wahweap Marina at present rate
2. Initiate more thorough census of night fishermen, shore fishermen, and Hall's Crossing-Bullfrog area pressure if manpower available
3. Continue plankton studies at established stations
4. Establish additional stations in shallow bays during spring and summer to evaluate food availability for bass fry and fingerlings
