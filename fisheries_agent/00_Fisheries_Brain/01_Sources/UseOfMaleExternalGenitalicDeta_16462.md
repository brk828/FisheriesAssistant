# Use of Male External Genitalic Details as Taxonomic Characters in Palaemonetes

#marine-biology #decapoda #palaemonidae

## Executive Summary

This study evaluates the external genital details of the [[Second Pleopod]] of male [[Palaemonetes]] shrimp as taxonomic characters. Species in the genus have traditionally been identified by spinose body ornamentation, which is considered susceptible to ecological modification and produces overlap and variation. The author argues that the setal ornamentation of the [[Appendix Masculina]] tip, together with the gross morphology of the second pleopod, is stable and species-specific, and can improve identification of adult males within the family [[Palaemonidae]]. Five epigean species from the [[Gulf Coastal Plain]] were examined in detail (1,582 specimens), and three type-material species from outside the region were also assessed at the [[Smithsonian Institution]]. The method separates most species, but not [[Palaemonetes Vulgaris]] from [[Palaemonetes Intermedius]].

## Background & Description

- **Genus and family:** [[Palaemonetes]] belongs to the family [[Palaemonidae]] (subfamily Palaemoninae). [[Holthuis]] (1949, 1952) listed six epigean species occurring in the United States. Holthuis' 1952 revision is described as the primary source for taxonomic work in this group.
- **Species studied (Gulf Coastal Plain):**
  - *P. (P.) kadiakensis* Rathbun, 1902
  - *P. (P.) paludosus* (Gibbes, 1850)
  - *P. (P.) vulgaris* (Say, 1818)
  - *P. (P.) pugio* Holthuis, 1949
  - *P. (P.) intermedius* Holthuis, 1949
- **Type material examined at SI:** *P. (P.) schmitti* Holthuis; *P. (P.) suttkusi* Smalley; *P. (Alaocaris) antrorum* Benedict (a subterranean species). *P. (P.) eigenmanni* Hay and *P. (P.) hiltoni* Schmitt were also examined, but their pleopods were disarticulated, shriveled or damaged, so they yielded little usable data.
- **Anatomy of the second pleopod:** In males, the second pleopod carries a non-podomere [[Appendix Interna]] (called the retinaculum by [[Meehean]], 1936) on the inner margin, and an accessory process termed the appendix masculina arises between the endopodite proper and the appendix interna. The first pleopod is not a copulatory organ, but the male endopodite is about three times as long as the female's.
- **Rationale:** The author notes that spinose ornamentation is probably ecologically modified, whereas structures used in amplexus in most Crustacea are usually stable and little affected by ecological factors.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat of collected specimens:** Specimens were collected from large and small ponds, roadside sloughs, running streams, inlet bayous of the [[Mississippi Sound]] and the Gulf of Mexico itself.
- **Freshwater species:** Most abundant in floating, submerged and emergent vegetation, including duckweed (*Spirodela* sp. and *Lemna* sp.), water hyacinth (*Eichhornia* sp.), pondweed (*Potamogeton* spp.) and bladderwort (*Utricularia* sp.).
- **Brackish and marine species:** Mostly obtained from eelgrass (*Vallisneria* spp.) growths.
- **Larval development:** Similarity in larval development was reported for *P. pugio*, *P. intermedius* and *P. vulgaris* (Broad, 1957; Broad and Hubschman, 1962). Freshwater forms (*P. kadiakensis* per Broad and Hubschman, 1963; *P. paludosus* per Dobkin, 1963) differ from salt-water forms in showing a tendency toward condensation of stages, larger eggs and larger larvae.
- **Subterranean habitat:** *P. antrorum* is a subterranean species.
- **Limits of the method:** The author notes that the male genital characters do not resolve identification of juveniles and females. Their value lies in greater confidence for adult male identifications.

## Key Findings

**Sampling and methods**
- Total specimens studied: 1,582 (845 *P. kadiakensis*; 169 *P. paludosus*; 430 *P. pugio*; 71 *P. vulgaris*; 67 *P. intermedius*).
- Collections by state: Alabama, 5 collections from 2 counties; Louisiana, 21 collections from 5 parishes; Texas, 2 collections from 2 counties; Mississippi, 42 collections from 15 counties.
- Most *P. paludosus* were collected in St. Charles and St. James parishes, Louisiana.
- Second left pleopods were removed, mounted in [[Monks Mounting Medium]] (5cc corn syrup, 5cc fruit pectin, 3cc water with thymol), and examined by compound microscopy. Some pleopods were rearranged and re-examined to validate orientation.
- Camera lucida drawings were made at 35x (whole pleopod) and 450x (appendix masculina tip). In situ drawings of second right pleopods were made at a magnification given as 38 to validate interpretations of disarticulated appendages.
- The study was supported in part by NSF Grant No. GB-4719 to J. F. Fitzpatrick, Jr.

**Setal ornamentation of the appendix masculina tip (apical / subapical setae)**
- *P. kadiakensis*: 3 apical, 1 subapical.
- *P. paludosus*: 4 apical, 1 subapical.
- *P. pugio*: 5 apical, 1 or 2 subapical.
- *P. vulgaris* and *P. intermedius*: 4 apical, 2 subapical (identical, so this character cannot separate them).
- Type material: *P. schmitti* has 8 long apical setae and 1 short subapical seta; *P. suttkusi* has 5 very short apical setae arranged in a circle and 1 short subapical seta; *P. antrorum* has 2 long apical and 3 long subapical setae.

**Gross morphology of the second pleopod**
- *Extent of appendix interna along the appendix masculina:*
  - *P. kadiakensis*: slightly more than proximal one-half.
  - *P. paludosus*: less than proximal one-half.
  - *P. vulgaris*: proximal three-fourths; in some specimens the two appendices are subequal.
  - *P. pugio* and *P. intermedius*: proximal two-thirds.
- *Extent of appendix masculina along the endopodite proper:*
  - *P. vulgaris* and *P. pugio*: distal one-third.
  - *P. intermedius*: distal one-fourth.
  - *P. kadiakensis*: distal one-eighth.
  - *P. paludosus*: distal one-tenth; in some specimens the two appendices are subequal.
- *Tip of appendix interna:* large, flat or paddle-shaped in *P. paludosus*; small and round in all other species.
- *Shape of appendix masculina:* gently curved laterad in *P. kadiakensis* and *P. pugio*; straight in *P. paludosus*, *P. vulgaris* and *P. intermedius*. *P. paludosus* is straight and stiffened.
- Type material: *P. schmitti* has an appendix interna that turns upward and outward; *P. suttkusi* has a very short, straight appendix interna; *P. antrorum* has a relatively straight appendix interna and a very stout appendix masculina.

**Discrimination summary**
- *P. kadiakensis*, *P. paludosus* and *P. pugio* can be separated from one another by setal counts, and all can be separated from *P. vulgaris* and *P. intermedius*.
- Relative appendix interna length separates *P. kadiakensis*, *P. paludosus* and *P. vulgaris*. It does not separate *P. pugio* from *P. intermedius*.
- Relative appendix masculina length is distinctive in *P. paludosus*, *P. kadiakensis* and *P. intermedius*, but similar in *P. vulgaris* and *P. pugio*.
- *P. vulgaris* and *P. intermedius* can be distinguished only through the proportional lengths of the appendix interna and appendix masculina.
- Type specimens of *P. schmitti*, *P. suttkusi* and *P. antrorum* (at USNM) can be separated from each other and from other species using these criteria.

**Taxonomic conclusions**
- *P. pugio* is related to *P. vulgaris* and *P. intermedius* but is conspicuously different and specifically distinct.
- *P. vulgaris* and *P. intermedius* are very similar, and the author suggests they could be conspecific. Because of distinct morphological differences and no evidence of interbreeding, the author considers them best treated as specifically distinct for now. Further populational and ecological data are needed.
- *P. kadiakensis* and *P. paludosus* are clearly morphologically distinct from each other and from the other species, and represent separate species.
- Epigean species from Mexico and the Canal Zone also appear specifically unique by these characteristics. Subterranean species such as *P. antrorum* may be identifiable similarly.

**Overall conclusion**
- The second pleopod of the male can be used in taxonomic evaluation of epigean *Palaemonetes*. It is expected to ease identification difficulties and to support evaluation of evolutionary and interspecific relationships with more stable characters.
