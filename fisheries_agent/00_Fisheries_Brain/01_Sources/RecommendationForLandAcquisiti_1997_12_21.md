## Executive Summary

This document is a formal recommendation letter from the Desert Fishes Recovery Team (Arizona State University) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, dated 21 December 1997. Rather than presenting primary morphological or ecological research data, the letter addresses land acquisition strategy as a conservation tool for the recovery of two federally listed cyprinid minnows: the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) and [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*). The Team identifies two specific private inholdings within the Gila National Forest on the lower East Fork Gila River (Grant County, New Mexico) as high-priority parcels for acquisition, citing the need to prevent habitat degradation from future development and to preserve the ecological integrity of the drainage.

## Background & Description

No morphological data (e.g., meristics, body proportions, coloration) for *Tiaroga cobitis*, *Meda fulgida*, or *Salmo gilae* are provided in this source document. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Type:** Lower East Fork of the [[Gila River]], a drainage within the Gila National Forest, Grant County, New Mexico.
- **Legal/Land Status:** The critical habitat parcels are located in Township 13S, Range 13W, and include:
  - NE 1/4, NW 1/4, Sec. 13
  - N 1/2, NE 1/4, Sec. 11
  - N 1/2, SE 1/4, Sec. 4
  - W 1/2, SW 1/4, Sec. 3
- **Co-occurring Species:** The habitat supports the [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] alongside [[Gila Trout]] (*Salmo gilae*), as well as unspecified additional federally and state-listed and candidate species, and other native (non-listed) animals and plants.
- **Threats:** The primary identified ecological threat is anticipated future development on the two private inholdings, which could compromise the hydrological and habitat integrity of the lower East Fork drainage — a factor considered essential to sustaining viable minnow populations.
- **Conservation Mechanism:** The recommended intervention is transfer of management control (via Federal or State agency, or private conservation organization acquisition) rather than direct habitat manipulation, positioning land tenure as a proxy for habitat protection.

## Key Findings

- The [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] formally reviewed draft Recovery Plans for *Tiaroga cobitis* and *Meda fulgida*, both of which cite selective land acquisition as a key recovery strategy but lack specific parcel identification.
- Two private inholdings on the lower East Fork Gila River were identified by the Team as having high strategic value for the recovery of both minnow species.
- Acquisition of management control (not necessarily fee-title ownership) over these parcels is recommended as a preventative measure against habitat-degrading development.
- The ecological integrity of the lower East Fork Gila River drainage is framed as important not only to [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] but also to [[Gila Trout]] and a broader, unspecified suite of protected and native taxa.
- The letter indicates this is part of an ongoing, iterative process — the Recovery Team states it will submit further land acquisition recommendations as additional key habitat areas are identified.
- Signed by W. L. Minckley, Team Leader and Professor of Zoology, indicating institutional backing from Arizona State University's Department of Zoology.
