## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 administrative memorandum from the Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fishes of the Upper Colorado River Basin, organizing a "Razorback Sucker Genetics Meeting" in Salt Lake City, Utah. The meeting was convened to bring together fishery geneticists, agency biologists, and program managers to review existing genetic data on the endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (and other endangered Colorado River fishes) and to develop management recommendations for maintaining genetic integrity of wild stocks. While the document contains no primary morphological or ecological data itself, it outlines the scientific and management questions that were the focus of contemporary [[Conservation Genetics]] efforts for this species, including stock identification, hybridization risk, and captive propagation protocols.

## Key Findings

- **Purpose of meeting:** To have fishery geneticists interpret completed genetic analyses and provide recommendations for maintaining the genetic integrity of wild endangered fish stocks in the Upper Colorado River Basin.
- **Key agenda items identified for scientific discussion:**
  1. **Review of Genetics Studies** – whether all analyses were included in existing reports and plans for publication.
  2. **Genetic Identification and Characterization** – development of non-lethal sampling protocols (muscle and fin tissue) and determination of best methodologies.
  3. **Interpretation of Genetic Information** – whether existing data identify definitive genetic markers capable of distinguishing stocks, and whether further analyses were warranted.
  4. **Presumptive Stocks of Endangered Fish** – development of a **Genetics Management Plan** integrating available ecological and genetic information on distinct stocks in the upper basin.
  5. **Hybridization** – general risks/concerns of hybridization in wild and captive populations, with a specific case flagged involving razorback suckers at **Etter Pond**.
  6. **Stocking Protocols** – including:
     - "Nearest neighbor" protocol for source stock selection in reintroduction.
     - Captive-reared broodstock development (breeding strategies, genetic risk mitigation, experimental stocking design).
     - Use of **wild larvae** collected from natural reproduction in off-channel open-water habitats, including strategies for larval collection, stocking protocols, release protocols, genetic risk mitigation, and experimental designs for rearing/release (including post-release genetic monitoring of surviving fish).
- **Institutional context:** The meeting was organized under the multi-agency **Recovery Implementation Program**, involving USFWS, Bureau of Reclamation, Western Area Power Administration, state wildlife agencies (Colorado, Utah, Wyoming), and NGO/water-user stakeholders — reflecting the applied conservation-genetics management context rather than a controlled experimental study.
- **Named experts convened:** Included leading conservation geneticists and ichthyologists of the era (e.g., Fred Allendorf, Tom Dowling, W.L. Minckley, Dave Philipp, Tony Echelle), underscoring the scientific weight placed on resolving razorback sucker population genetics for recovery planning.
