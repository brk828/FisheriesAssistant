# Marble Bluff Fishway Modification Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cui-ui

## Executive Summary

This 1995 letter from [[Paul Wagner]] of [[Pyramid Lake Fisheries]] to [[Carlos Mendoza]], Nevada State Director of the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]], urges prompt field testing of alternative fishway designs at [[Marble Bluff Dam]]. The existing [[Pool And Weir Fishway]] is judged unsuitable for [[Cui-Ui]], a bottom-oriented fish. The author proposes converting the terminal ladder at the lake interface to a [[Vertical Slot Fishway]] using dimensions from the [[Seton Creek Fishway]], to be tested in the 1996 season. The letter ties this test to a [[U.S. Army Corps Of Engineers]] feasibility study starting in October 1995 and to a [[Nature Conservancy]] engineered channel project.

## Background & Description

- **Facility:** The Marble Bluff fishway is a concrete pool-and-weir ladder. The author notes the facility operated relatively successfully in 1995 but is "nowhere near where it should be."
- **Target species:** The cui-ui is described as a bottom-oriented fish, for which the author argues the present ladder design is poorly suited.
- **Design rationale:** Citing [[Clay 1995]] (*Design of Fishways and Other Fish Facilities*), the letter lists three advantages of vertical slot designs, originally framed for adult salmon:
  1. Ascent is possible at any depth the fish selects, which varies with time of day, light, and turbidity.
  2. The passage path is not tortuous. Staggered orifices or baffles have been linked to fish difficulty or distress, possibly because fish orient to their distance from the walls, and slot passage occurs near the walls.
  3. Resting pools are satisfactory, though the physiological requirements are not clearly understood. The author concedes that whether this design meets resting needs better than the existing design "is pure conjecture."
- **Proposed dimensions:** Adopted from the Seton Creek Fishway in Western Canada: fishway width 8 ft, baffle spacing 10 ft, slot width 1 ft 5 in (as printed). Modification would involve sawing existing concrete baffle walls and pouring a wing wall to form the slot.
- **Alternative tested:** [[Denil Ladder]] systems are also named as generally better for bottom dwellers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Problem zone:** Both alternatives would be built at the "Lake interface," described as the most problematic section of the fishway.
- **Population status:** The author judges the cui-ui population "large enough at this time" that a field test would not put it at risk even if the change made conditions worse.
- **Water conditions:** Adequate water is expected to be available in the coming season.
- **Hydraulic gap:** The letter notes that the water height in the fishway without the weirs has not been calculated. This analysis, assigned to [[Brent Mefford]], may change the slot width but is not expected to materially affect cost.
- **Parallel channel project:** [[The Nature Conservancy]] plans an [[Engineered Channel]] with more natural features, as an alternative to the concrete box fishway. Its design is complete and bids were to be solicited in October 1995.

## Key Findings

1. **Current fishway is inadequate:** The author believes the pool-and-weir ladder is not suitable for a bottom-oriented fish, and that better designs exist in the literature.
2. **Recommended test:** Convert the terminal ladder to a vertical slot design during the coming season, and evaluate it alongside a conventional alternative. The author states "the fish will let us know what alternative they prefer."
3. **Cost estimate:** The modification would cost about $3,000 in materials, with labor supplied by Pyramid Lake Fisheries in about 10 days. The author states that "cost is not an issue."
4. **Itemized figures as printed:** Unit rates include $15 per foot for sawing baffle walls, $62 per cubic yard of concrete, $500 per ton of rebar, $30 per sheet of form ply, and $30 per box of snap ties. The sawing line is listed at $186, which does not clearly reconcile with the stated rate for 14 baffles of 12 ft each. The document should be checked before budgeting.
5. **Quantities:** 14 baffles, 12 ft of wall sawn per baffle, 0.2 cubic yard of concrete per baffle, 0.5 ton of rebar, and 5 sheets of form ply.
6. **Coordination:** The author argues that a field test this year would give the [[U.S. Army Corps Of Engineers]] the information needed for its [[Feasibility Study]], rather than leaving the Corps to hire a consultant for the new fishway design.
7. **Multi-agency request:** The letter asks the Service to coordinate with the Corps, the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], The Nature Conservancy, and Pyramid Lake Fisheries, and to commit to a firm 1995 decision and test.
