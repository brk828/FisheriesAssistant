# Estimation of Catch and Related Statistics of the Stream Rainbow Trout Fishery of the Finger Lakes Region

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #fishery-management #creel-census

## Executive Summary

A creel census was conducted during the opening week of stream fishing for [[Rainbow Trout]] in the [[Finger Lakes Region]] of New York during 1955, 1956, and 1957. The study examined four tributary streams: [[Grout Brook]] (Skaneateles Lake inlet), [[Catherine Creek]] (Seneca Lake inlet), [[Cold Brook]] (Keuka Lake inlet), and [[Naples Creek]] (Canandaigua Lake inlet). The census estimated total catch, fishing pressure, fishing success rates, and catch distribution among anglers. Of 19,646 anglers contacted over three years, only 1,652 (8.4%) caught fish. The study evaluated the effectiveness of the five-fish daily creel limit and found it did not function to restrict catch, as only 1-2% of fishermen reached the limit.

## Background & Description

[[Rainbow Trout]] in the [[Finger Lakes Region]] are taken by angling mainly during the spawning season in tributary streams. This study was part of a joint program conducted by the New York State Conservation Department and [[Cornell University]] to gather statistics on total catch, fishing pressure, fishing success, and catch distribution for biology and management purposes.

The methodology employed a partial creel census combined with fish tagging data. Since a complete census was not possible, estimates were derived using the ratio of tagged to untagged fish in anglers' catches. State game protectors patrolled streams during the opening week and recorded: (1) unsuccessful fishermen contacted, (2) fish caught by individual successful fishermen, and (3) tagged fish checked during census. Tags reported by mail (tag-letter returns) provided a measure of catch by fishermen not contacted during the census.

The census covered approximately 5 miles of [[Grout Brook]], 11 miles of [[Catherine Creek]], 4.5 miles of [[Cold Brook]], and 13.5 miles of [[Naples Creek]], representing most of the trout fishing mileage on these streams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study focused on [[Rainbow Trout]] spawning runs in tributary streams of the [[Finger Lakes Region]]. These fish migrate from the lakes into tributaries during the spawning season, creating concentrated fishing opportunities. The timing of the fishery coincides with the April 1 opening of stream fishing season, with the opening week representing the peak fishing period.

Stream characteristics and accessibility varied among the four tributaries studied. [[Catherine Creek]] experienced substantially higher fishing pressure than the other streams, likely due to accessibility and reputation. The distribution of fishing effort and success across different streams reflected both fish abundance and angler competition dynamics.

The daily creel limit during the study period (April 1 to May 15) was five fish per day for these and certain other specified streams in the [[Finger Lakes Region]].

## Key Findings

**Catch Estimates:**
- Opening-week catches ranged from 140 trout in 1957 at [[Cold Brook]] to 980 in 1955 at [[Catherine Creek]]
- [[Catherine Creek]] consistently produced the highest catches (580-980 fish), substantially above other streams
- [[Grout Brook]], [[Cold Brook]], and [[Naples Creek]] generally yielded 100-300 fish per opening week
- More fish were caught on opening day than any succeeding day
- In all cases, more than half of the opening week's catch was taken in the first two days of fishing

**Fishing Pressure:**
- Opening-day fishing pressure at [[Catherine Creek]] ranged from 365 to 635 anglers per mile of stream
- Other streams experienced considerably less pressure, seldom exceeding 100 fishermen per mile
- [[Catherine Creek]] opening-day contacts ranged from 1,815 to 3,122 anglers
- Fishing pressure decreased sharply after opening day

**Fishing Success:**
- Of 19,646 anglers contacted over three years, only 1,652 (8.4%) caught fish
- Highest average fishing success occurred at [[Grout Brook]] (16-37%)
- Lowest fishing success occurred at [[Catherine Creek]] (5-6%), likely reflecting tremendous fishing pressure
- Despite large opening-day harvests, fishing success generally maintained the same level throughout the opening week
- Possible explanations for maintained success rates: reduced fishing pressure after opening day, greater individual fishing efficiency, higher caliber of continuing fishermen, and augmentation with fresh-run fish

**Catch Distribution:**
- Most successful fishermen caught only one or two fish
- Distribution was similar across different streams and years
- Only 1% of all fishermen in 1955, 2% in 1956, and 0% in 1957 caught the five-fish creel limit
- Only 4% of successful fishermen in 1955 and 1956, and 1% in 1957 caught more than three fish

**Creel Limit Evaluation:**
- The five-fish daily creel limit did not function in restricting the catch
- No significant reductions in catch would be obtained by setting the creel limit at three fish
- The creel limit would not become an effective means of restricting catch until reduced to two fish
- No evidence existed that further limiting exploitation of spawning runs would be desirable

**Methodology Notes:**
- Tag and recapture data were unsuitable for estimating spawning population size due to recruitment of fresh-run, untagged fish
- The method's disadvantage of relying on voluntary tag-letter returns was offset by sufficient accuracy to fix the magnitude of statistics
- Estimates are considered minimum values, assuming similar percentages of unreported tags across streams
