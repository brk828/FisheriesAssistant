# Fish Sampling at ASU Karsten Golf Course, 9–10 May 1990

## Executive Summary
This internal memorandum from Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies reports on a fish health and growth assessment conducted at the ASU Karsten Golf Course (ASUGC) water features. The study targeted three federally significant native fish species — [[Bonytail]], [[Colorado Squawfish]] (Colorado Pikeminnow), and [[Razorback Sucker]] — that had been reared at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico) and stocked into the golf course lakes in September–October 1988 as part of a conservation/refugium effort. Approximately two years post-stocking, all three species were recaptured in good condition with growth rates matching or exceeding hatchery benchmarks. The lakes also host a community of non-native species, including recently introduced [[Grass Carp]], raising questions about competitive and predatory interactions with the native fishes.

## Background & Description

### Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)
- Mean Total Length (TL): 29.0 ± 5.5 cm (SD); range 21.6–38.5 cm (n = 10)
- Mean weight: 196 ± 117 g (range 68–480 g)
- Growth: TL increased ~6.3 cm since October 1989
- Sex ratio: 13 male (10 ripe), 3 female (1 expressing eggs), 4 undetermined
- Health: Good overall condition; *Lernaea cyprinacea* (parasitic copepod) infestation on 2 individuals only

### Colorado Squawfish / Pikeminnow (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- Mean TL: 35.7 ± 0.9 cm (range 34.6–36.7 cm, n = 5)
- Mean weight: 329 ± 37 g (range 292–370 g)
- Growth: TL increased ~5.8 cm since October 1989
- Sex: Undetermined externally (all apparently immature); to be confirmed via lab necropsy of mortalities

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- Mean TL: 32.5 ± 1.4 cm (range 29.8–35.3 cm, n = 27)
- Mean weight: 333 ± 53 g (range 216–470 g)
- Sex ratio: 16 male (1 ripe), 10 female, 1 undetermined
- Health: Generally excellent; one individual with caudal spinal kyphosis (curvature); one emaciated individual missing one eye

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Site Description
ASUGC comprises two artificial lake systems — **South Lake** and **North Lake** — functioning as a novel refugium habitat for three imperiled [[Colorado River Basin]] native fishes outside their historic range.

### Species Distribution Within Site
| Species | South Lake | North Lake |
|---|---|---|
| [[Bonytail]] | 0 | 20 (captured only here, despite original stocking in South Lake) |
| [[Colorado Squawfish]] | 0 | 5 (originally stocked here) |
| [[Razorback Sucker]] | 27 | 0 |

- Notably, bonytail were originally stocked into South Lake, yet were captured exclusively in North Lake at this sampling — despite numerous individuals reportedly observed in South Lake, suggesting inter-lake movement/connectivity or sampling bias.

### Co-occurring Non-Native Species
Incidental catch (removed from system):
- [[Threadfin Shad]]
- [[Common Carp]]
- [[Grass Carp]] (White Amur) — intentionally stocked by ASUGC staff, autumn 1989
- [[Yellow Bullhead]]
- [[Green Sunfish]]

Other invaders (source: Salt River Project canal system), previously collected but not encountered this round:
- [[Desert Sucker]]
- [[Sonora Sucker]]
- [[Yellow Bass]]

Abundant but not captured by nets used (targeted for future seining):
- [[Red Shiner]]
- [[Mosquitofish]]

### Predation & Competition Considerations
- [[Centrarchidae]] (bass/sunfishes) and [[Ictaluridae]] (catfishes/bullheads) — known predators of native larval fish — remain uncommon at ASUGC, a favorable condition for native recruitment.
- [[Red Shiner]] and [[Mosquitofish]] are potential larval predators, though no direct evidence yet links them to impacts on bonytail, squawfish, or razorback sucker larvae.
- Ecological role of newly introduced [[Grass Carp]] on native species remains unknown and flagged for future monitoring.

## Key Findings

1. **Successful establishment**: All three native species — bonytail, Colorado squawfish, and razorback sucker — were successfully recaptured via trammel and experimental gill nets, confirming ~2-year post-stocking survival.
2. **Superior growth performance**: Growth rates at ASUGC equaled or exceeded those recorded in other habitats, including hatchery settings, for all three species.
3. **Reproductive readiness**: Size and sexual condition (including ripe males/females and one female expressing eggs) indicate all three species are approaching reproductive maturity and could spawn as early as the following year; bonytail may have already attempted spawning.
4. **Low predator pressure**: The relative absence of centrarchids and ictalurids at ASUGC — both known larval predators — creates favorable conditions for potential native fish recruitment.
5. **Research opportunity**: The site presents a unique opportunity to study recruitment dynamics of endangered native fishes within a non-native fish community, particularly interactions with mosquitofish, red shiner, and the newly introduced grass carp.
6. **Health status**: Overall health was good to excellent across all native species; parasite loads (*Lernaea cyprinacea*) were minimal, limited to two bonytail individuals. Minor pathologies (kyphosis, eye loss, emaciation) were noted in isolated razorback sucker individuals.
7. **Future sampling plan**: Comprehensive entanglement/entrapment sampling planned for autumn 1990; larval fish sampling to begin November 1990 through late spring/early summer 1991, targeting small-bodied species (red shiner, mosquitofish) and native recruitment monitoring.
