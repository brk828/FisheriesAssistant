# Federal Register Notices, March 25, 1998: Critical Habitat Revocation and Alaska Community Development Quota Approvals

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #pleuronectidae

## Executive Summary

This Federal Register issue (Vol. 63, No. 57, pages 14378–14379) is a compilation of regulatory notices rather than a research paper. Its main item is a final rule from the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] that removes designated [[Critical Habitat]] for the [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Spikedace]] after federal courts set aside those designations for failure to analyze effects under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]]. The issue also includes a [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] notice approving 1998–2000 [[Community Development Quota Program]] plans for [[Pacific Halibut]], [[Sablefish]] and crab in the Bering Sea and Aleutian Islands, and an [[Environmental Protection Agency]] rule setting pesticide residue tolerances for grain sorghum.

## Background & Description

**Critical habitat revocation (fish and bird).** The loach minnow (*Rhinichthys cobitis*) and spikedace (*Meda fulgida*) are listed as fishes, and the Mexican spotted owl (*Strix occidentalis lucida*) is listed as a bird. The Service's final rule amends 50 CFR 17.11(h) to change the critical habitat entry for each species to "NA," and removes the associated maps and text from 50 CFR 17.95(b) and 17.95(e). The rule was issued under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended.

**Designation history.** Critical habitat for the loach minnow and spikedace was designated on March 8, 1994 (59 FR 10898 and 59 FR 10906). On October 13, 1994, the U.S. District Court for the District of New Mexico set aside these designations, ruling that the Service must analyze the effects of critical habitat designation under NEPA. The Tenth Circuit affirmed in 1996. Critical habitat for the Mexican spotted owl was designated on June 6, 1995 (60 FR 29914), and a separate New Mexico court also set that designation aside (CIV No. 95-1285-M). Related rulings in Arizona likewise held that NEPA analysis had not been completed before designation.

**Community Development Quota (CDQ) program.** The CDQ program for Pacific halibut and fixed gear sablefish was developed by the [[North Pacific Fishery Management Council]] and implemented under subpart C of 50 CFR part 679. The crab CDQ program was added as part of Amendment 5 to the [[Fishery Management Plan]] for the Commercial King and Tanner Crab Fisheries in the Bering Sea and Aleutian Islands Area, approved September 12, 1997. Implementing regulations for the crab CDQ reserves were published February 19, 1998 (63 FR 8356).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The notice concerns federally designated critical habitat for two stream fishes and one owl. The document does not describe the habitat's physical or biological characteristics.
- **Legal framework:** The removal depends on court decisions requiring NEPA analysis before designation. The Service states that it will no longer apply critical habitat for these three species in [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the Endangered Species Act.
- **Fisheries framework:** The CDQ program allocates reserves of halibut, sablefish and crab in the [[Bering Sea]] to eligible western Alaska communities, with reference to the [[Magnuson-Stevens Act]] goals.
- **Pesticide tolerances:** The EPA rule covers grain sorghum commodities (grain, forage and stover) under 40 CFR 180.472. The text is OCR-degraded and the chemical name is not clearly legible, so it is not identified here.

## Key Findings

1. **Critical habitat revoked.** The [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] removed critical habitat for the Mexican spotted owl, loach minnow and spikedace. The rule is effective March 25, 1998.
2. **Basis for revocation.** Because the District Court of New Mexico set aside the designations, the Service concluded that critical habitat for these species was no longer in effect. It therefore removed it without a notice-and-comment period.
3. **Good cause waiver.** The Service waived the 30-day delay in effective date under 5 U.S.C. 553(d), finding good cause because the habitat designations had already been set aside.
4. **Regulatory changes.** The rule changes 50 CFR 17.11(h) for three species and removes critical habitat maps and text from 50 CFR 17.95(b) and 17.95(e).
5. **No new information collection.** The revocation contains no information collection requirements under the Paperwork Reduction Act and imposes no unfunded mandates.
6. **CDQ plans approved.** The [[National Marine Fisheries Service]] approved the State of Alaska's recommended 1998–2000 Community Development Plans for halibut, fixed gear sablefish and crab. The approval took effect March 23, 1998.
7. **Application volume.** Eligible western Alaska communities submitted six proposed CDPs. The State held a public hearing on September 9, 1997, in Anchorage.
8. **Contact.** Questions on the CDQ approval are directed to Sally Bibb (907-586-7228). Questions on the critical habitat rule are directed to Sally Spangle of the Service's Division of Ecological Services.
9. **Pesticide tolerances.** The EPA set residue tolerances for grain sorghum commodities. The document reports no quantitative residue values in the readable text.
