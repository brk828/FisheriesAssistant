## Executive Summary

This notice reports completion of a Service-wide 5-year review of species listed as endangered or threatened before 1976 and in 1979–1980 (462 species total: 423 animals, 39 plants), concluding that **no status changes were warranted** for any reviewed species. A companion **proposed rule** simultaneously opens a new 5-year review cycle for species listed in **1976, 1977, 1981, and 1982** (213 animal and 13 plant species), soliciting public and scientific comment through November 4, 1987. The review mechanism is procedural, governed by [[Endangered Species Act]] §4 and 50 CFR 424.21, and does not itself alter any species' legal status — it is a data-gathering and reassessment exercise. The document lists numerous freshwater aquatic invertebrates (notably [[Freshwater Pearly Mussels]], family Unionidae) alongside terrestrial and reptilian taxa, all administered under the same regulatory framework as listed fish species elsewhere in 50 CFR 17.11.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context:** The freshwater mussels (Unionidae) listed — e.g., Alabama lamp pearly mussel, Appalachian monkeyface, Cumberland bean, Curtis' pearly mussel, dromedary pearly mussel, green-blossom pearly mussel, orange-footed pearly mussel, white catspaw, yellow-blossom pearly mussel — are restricted to specific river drainages in the southeastern United States (Alabama, Tennessee, Virginia, Kentucky, Indiana, Ohio, Missouri, Arkansas, Pennsylvania, West Virginia). These species depend on host fish for their glochidia (larval) life stage, linking their conservation status directly to the health of native [[Freshwater Fish Assemblages]] in these same watersheds.
- **Regulatory/geographic niche:** Species under review span multiple biogeographic realms — Palearctic, Afrotropical, Indomalayan, Neotropical, and Nearctic — reflecting the ESA's global reach for species imported/traded in the U.S. or under international agreements (e.g., CITES-referenced taxa such as Komodo dragon, gharial, various Asian primates).
- **Threat factors enumerated (50 CFR 424.11(c)):** (a) habitat destruction/modification; (b) overutilization (commercial, recreational, scientific, educational); (c) disease or predation; (d) inadequacy of existing regulatory mechanisms; (e) other natural or manmade factors — this general threat framework applies equally to any fish species reviewed under the broader Act, even though none appear explicitly in this excerpt.

## Key Findings

- **462 species reviewed (1987 first review):** 423 animals + 39 plants, listed pre-1976 and in 1979–1980; **no data supported any status change** — all retained current classifications.
- **New review cycle proposed:** 213 animal species + 13 plant species listed in 1976, 1977, 1981, and 1982 placed under formal 5-year review, per [[Endangered Species Act]] §4(c)(2) and 50 CFR 424.21 requirements.
- **No fish taxa are identifiable in the extracted OCR text** — the visible species tables cover Mammalia, Reptilia, Amphibia, Gastropoda (one snail, *Achatinella* sp., Oahu), Bivalvia (numerous Unionidae "pearly mussels," "pigtoe," "fatmucket," etc.), Crustacea, Insecta (five butterfly species), and vascular plants.
- **Freshwater mussels dominate the aquatic invertebrate listings**, with heavy concentration in the Tennessee, Cumberland, and Alabama River systems — watersheds also historically rich in endemic freshwater fish diversity, underscoring an indirect ecological link between mussel conservation status and fish host-species conservation in these systems.
- **Procedural/administrative outcome only:** This notice neither lists nor delists any species; it solicits scientific/commercial data from the public, State agencies, and researchers to inform potential future rulemaking.
