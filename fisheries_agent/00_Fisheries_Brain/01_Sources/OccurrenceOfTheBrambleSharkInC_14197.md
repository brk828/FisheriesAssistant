# Occurrence of the Bramble Shark (*Echinorhinus brucus*) in California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #echinorhinus-brucus

## Executive Summary

This 1945 reprint from *[[California Fish And Game]]* confirms the first record of the [[Bramble Shark]] (*[[Echinorhinus Brucus]]*) for the eastern Pacific. A 6-foot-5-inch specimen was caught in a gill-net on August 21, 1944, off [[Point Vicente]] in Los Angeles County, and was later deposited in the [[United States National Museum]]. The authors, Carl L. Hubbs of the [[Scripps Institution Of Oceanography]] and Frances N. Clark of the [[Bureau Of Marine Fisheries]], provide a morphological description, measurements, and a synonymy argument. They conclude that several nominal species are synonyms of *E. brucus*.

## Background & Description

- **Prior suspicion:** A 62-inch, 100-pound shark photographed at [[Moss Landing]] after a July 1939 capture off [[Santa Barbara]] was suspected to be this species. It was not preserved and was not published because the identification was uncertain.
- **Capture and specimen:** On August 21, 1944, Capt. John DiMeglio of the boat *Arcturus* caught a specimen "more than six feet long" in a gill-net set in 50 to 55 fathoms off Portuguese Bend (approx. Lat. 33° 43.0′ N, Long. 118° 22.5′ W). It was presented to the [[California State Fisheries Laboratory]], then donated to the United States National Museum as catalogue number 130667. The authors state this is apparently the only preserved specimen of the genus in any North American museum.
- **Diagnostic characters:** The species lacks an anal fin and has two small dorsal fins, the anterior originating about opposite the insertion of the long-based pelvic fin. Teeth are similar in each jaw, with a very oblique main cusp and a single subhorizontal secondary cusp on each side. The skin bears isolated tubercular scales with a stellate base and a small summit spine, which gives the common name. The nostrils are divided by a sharply pointed flap.
- **Coloration:** After two months in cold storage the specimen was almost uniform dark brown, with no clear dark spots.
- **Nomenclature:** The authors treat *Echinorhinus cookei* (Hawaii), *E. obesus* (South Africa), and *E. (Rubusqualus) mccoyi* (Australia) as synonyms of *E. brucus*. They follow [[Garman]] and later authors in using *E. brucus* (Bonnaterre, 1788) over *E. spinosus* (Gmelin, 1789) on priority.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Previous North American and New World records:** A seven-foot specimen washed ashore at Provincetown, Massachusetts, in December 1878 (Goode and Bean, 1879). A specimen of about 2.5 m (nearly 10 feet) was reported from Mar del Plata, Buenos Aires Province, Argentina (Berg, 1898).
- **Global distribution:** The species is somewhat common only on the Atlantic coast of Europe. It has also been recorded from the Mediterranean Sea, West and South Africa, Australia, Tasmania, New Zealand, and Japan. The authors consider it likely to have a wide range in temperate and subtropical waters.
- **Depth:** The record comes from moderate depths along the California coast, the zone the authors argue is under-sampled. Barnhart and Hubbs (1944) had previously recommended more thorough exploration of this fauna.
- **Maturity:** The male was judged not fully mature, because its claspers are simple, grooved on one side, and do not reach the posterior angle of the pelvic fin.

## Key Findings

- **First eastern Pacific record:** The bramble shark is confirmed for California by a specimen taken on August 21, 1944, off Point Vicente, at 50 to 55 fathoms.
- **Specimen size:** Total length was 196 cm (about 6 ft 5 in) before preservation.
- **Body proportions (per thousandth of total length, 196 cm):**
  - Greatest body depth: 141
  - Least caudal peduncle depth: 62
  - Snout tip to first gill slit: 192; to last gill slit: 261
  - To pectoral insertion: 260; to pelvic insertion: 583; to first dorsal origin: 590
  - Between dorsal fin origins: 103
  - Between bases of second dorsal and caudal fins: 37
- **Fin measurements (per thousandth of total length):**
  - Front margin of first dorsal: 97; second dorsal: 88; upper caudal lobe: 218; lower caudal lobe: 117; pectoral: 115; pelvic: 89
  - Base of first dorsal: 55; second dorsal: 51; pectoral: 72; pelvic: 104
- **Head and gill measurements (per thousandth of total length):** Preocular snout length 81; preoral snout length 72; least interorbital width 91; orbit length 18; mouth width 111; first gill slit height 44; last gill slit height 65.
- **Teeth:** Counts were reported as "124-1325" in the upper jaw and "12+-11-23" in the lower jaw. The OCR is garbled here, so these counts are unverified. The authors state they fall within the 22 to 26 range commonly given for each jaw.
- **Taxonomic synonymy:** *E. cookei*, *E. obesus*, and *E. mccoyi* are treated as synonyms of *E. brucus*. Bigelow compared photographs and a sketch of a European specimen and found no difference between Pacific and Atlantic forms.
- **Citation discrepancies in source:** The text cites Garman as 1913, while the Literature Cited lists 1918. The text cites Pietschmann as 1930, while the Literature Cited lists 1980. These are reported as they appear in the document.
- **Conservation note:** The authors predict that *Echinorhinus* "will prove to be much less rare in California than the available data would seem to indicate."
