# The Spiny-Rayed Cyprinid Fishes (Plagopterini) of the Colorado River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 monograph by [[Robert Rush Miller]] and [[Carl L. Hubbs]] (Miscellaneous Publications No. 115, [[Museum of Zoology University of Michigan]]) revises the cyprinid tribe Plagopterini, comprising three genera: [[Lepidomeda]], [[Meda Fulgida|Meda]], and [[Plagopterus Argentissimus|Plagopterus]]. The authors recognize seven forms, including three new species and one new subspecies. The group is endemic to the lower and middle [[Colorado River System]] and is judged threatened. Two of the most restricted forms were already reported exterminated after 1959 collections. The paper also reconstructs a probable Tertiary origin and documents the decline of spring and stream habitats.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** Revision of a "spiny-rayed" group of minnows distinguished by modified dorsal, pectoral and pelvic fin rays. Measurements and ray counts followed [[Hubbs and Lagler]] criteria. Radiographs were used to show spine structure and vertebral counts (the hypural complex counted as one vertebra, the [[Weberian Apparatus]] as four).
- **Genera and their traits:**
  - *Lepidomeda* is the least modified genus. It retains well-developed scales (deeply embedded, lacking basal radii), has a rounded head and belly, and has 41 to 48 vertebrae. It is the only polytypic genus of the three.
  - *Meda* and *Plagopterus* are scaleless, except for possible vestigial platelets. They have flattened heads, usually about 40 vertebrae, and ventral pelvic demitrichs that form strong pungent spines. Mature males have dilated, denticulate pectoral rays.
  - *Plagopterus* is the most highly modified genus, with the heaviest spines, a horizontal mouth, and papillae on the lower jaw region.
- **Systematics:** The authors regard the Plagopterini as a tribe within the Leuciscinae. *Lepidomeda* appears most generalized, and *Plagopterus* most specialized. The spinous modifications parallel those of Old World spiny-rayed cyprinids such as *Cyprinus* and *Barbus*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Endemism:** The group is one of the main elements of the highly endemic Colorado River fauna. Its members occupy remnants of Pleistocene [[Pluvial Lakes]] drainages, including the White, Carpenter, and Pahranagat systems in southeastern Nevada.
- **Habitat:** *Lepidomeda albivallis* lives in cool springs (65 to 71°F) and their outflows. *L. altivelis* inhabits outflows and a lake, with water up to 88°F at sources. *Plagopterus argentissimus* is found in swift, silty or gravelly currents.
- **Threats:** Stream flow deterioration due to drought, erosion, water storage and stream diversion. Introduced species include [[Gambusia Affinis]], bullfrog, [[Largemouth Bass]], and [[Bluegill]], along with salmonids planted in the same waters. The authors note that *Plagopterus* was formerly present in the lower Gila River from Tempe to Yuma until the late 1800s.
- **Associated fauna:** Species found with the spinedace include *Pantosteus intermedius*, *Rhinichthys osculus*, *Crenichthys baileyi*, *Gila robusta jordani*, and carp (*Cyprinus carpio*).
- **Fossil evidence:** Three extinct Pliocene species from the Bidahochi Formation of northern Arizona appear referable to living Colorado River genera (two to *Gila*, one to *Ptychocheilus*).

## Key Findings

- **Seven forms** of Plagopterini are recognized. Three new species were described: *L. altivelis*, *L. albivallis*, and the subspecies *L. m. pratensis*.
- **Extirpation:** Two of the most range-restricted forms, *L. m. pratensis* (Big Spring) and *L. altivelis* (Ash Spring), were found exterminated during 1959 collections. The authors consider all seven forms threatened.
- ***Lepidomeda albivallis*** is the largest species of the genus and the most lightly colored. The holotype (UMMZ 178781) is 69.5 mm long. The type series includes 428 paratopotypes, and 579 further specimens were examined from nearby springs. Dental formula is typically 2, 5-4, 2, with 79 to 92 lateral-line scales and 42 to 44 vertebrae.
- ***Lepidomeda vittata*** was synonymized with *L. jarrovii*. The authors found no evidence that more than one species occurs in the Little Colorado River system. Apparent differences in the types were interpreted as phenotypic variation.
- **Origin:** The group probably arose from a *Gila*-like cyprinid, with *Lepidomeda* as the ancestral type. Radiation into *Meda* and *Plagopterus* is thought to have occurred within the same river system, likely in the Tertiary, possibly as early as the Pliocene.
- **Population structure:** Isolated spring populations differ strongly from one another and from river populations. The authors argue that these differences are not environmentally induced because samples from each locality agree in diagnostic characters.
- **Taxonomic synthesis:** Analytical keys separate the genera and species using fin spine development, scale counts, vertebral counts, tooth formula, mouth obliquity, and pigmentation.
