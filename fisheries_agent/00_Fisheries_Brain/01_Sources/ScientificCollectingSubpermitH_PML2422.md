# Scientific Collecting Subpermit 91-07: Humpback and Bonytail Chub Research Authorization

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1991 letter from the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Regional Director authorizes Dr. Wayne Starnes of the [[Gila Taxonomy Program]] and designated staff to conduct field research on endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) in the Colorado River system. Work is covered under Subpermit 91-07, a subpermittee arrangement tied to two parent research permits (scanned as PRT-704930 and PRT-6768-1, partly illegible). Activities run through December 15, 1991. The authorization is provisional and bound by strict handling, tagging, mortality-reporting, and disposition conditions.

## Background & Description

The subpermit authorizes capture, measurement, weighing, tissue sampling, release, and photography of both species. Field methods include seines, trammel nets, hoop nets, and electrofishing. Fish may receive passive integrated transponder (PIT) tags, which are described in a separate protocol document. The letter is addressed to Dr. Starnes, and copies were distributed to regional and field offices of the Fish and Wildlife Service, including Salt Lake City, Golden, Albuquerque, Portland, and Denver offices, and the Law Enforcement division.

Key authorized activities:

- **Capture and handling:** Seines, trammel nets, hoop nets, and electrofishing, with handling governed by the attached "Non-Lethal Sampling Protocol."
- **Tagging:** PIT tag insertion. Tags are not to be used on fish under 150 mm in length.
- **Tissue sampling:** Non-lethal sampling for both species, with separate limits for humpback chubs.
- **Transport:** Wild bonytail chubs may be held and transported under a separate attached method document for the upper Colorado River basin.
- **Permanent holding:** A permanent holding facility at a national fish hatchery is authorized, with capacity partly illegible in the scan. Fish bearing radio implants are excluded.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter's conservation emphasis is clear. Both chub species are federally listed endangered fish, and the letter specifies that endangered fish taken during activities remain the property of the Service. Several conditions protect young fish and sensitive populations:

- Every effort must prevent mortality of young-of-the-year fish. Sampling sites are to be cleared of filamentous algae before seining to reduce handling time and stress.
- Only fish that cannot be identified accurately in the field may be sacrificed. All other fish are to be released at their point of capture unless otherwise authorized.
- Coordination with the [[National Park Service]] is required for humpback chub work within park boundaries, including Grand Canyon-area sampling.
- Access to private land requires landowner permission, and state agency permits are required in Colorado, Utah, Arizona, and Wyoming.

Specimens that are dead or collected incidentally are to be preserved and provided to the Larval Fish Laboratory at Colorado State University in Fort Collins, Colorado, for identification and maintenance. Disposition of any materials the Service no longer wishes to hold is determined by the Assistant Regional Director, Law Enforcement, in Denver.

## Key Findings

The letter sets out the following requirements and authorizations:

1. **Scope:** Subpermit 91-07 covers research on humpback and bonytail chubs in the Yampa, Green, Colorado, and Virgin Rivers, valid through December 15, 1991.
2. **Tissue sampling limits:** Up to 50 adult humpback chubs may be sampled from the Colorado River in Arizona. Tissue samples may also be taken from humpback chubs captured and killed by other agencies, in coordination with the National Park Service and the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
3. **Mortality protocol:** Any unexpected death of an authorized species must be reported within 24 hours to the Fish and Wildlife Service Field Supervisor in Salt Lake City.
4. **Reporting:** A complete report of all activities is due by December 31, 1991, and young-of-the-year counts, site data, and specimen distribution are to be reported annually.
5. **Compliance:** The subpermittee must hold valid state and federal permits, coordinate with the Service, and carry the authorization letter and the enclosed parent permit copies while in the field.
6. **Dispositions:** Dead or incidentally collected specimens go to the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]], and endangered fish held by the Service remain its property.

The document is an authorization and conditions letter, not a research study, so it reports no population data, sample results, or statistics beyond the limits above.
