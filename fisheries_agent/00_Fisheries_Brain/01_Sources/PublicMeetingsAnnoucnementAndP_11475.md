# Upper Gila Water Supply Study Public Meeting Document (1986)

#water-resources #environmental-planning #native-fish #endangered-species #gila-river

## Executive Summary

This document announces public meetings (April 14-17, 1986) regarding the Bureau of Reclamation's Upper Gila Water Supply Study and selection of a proposed action for the Draft Environmental Impact Statement. The study evaluates six alternative plans (including a "No Federal Action" option) to develop up to 18,000 acre-feet of water annually for southwest New Mexico under the [[Colorado River Basin Project Act]]. The alternatives include [[Conner Dam]] (two sizes), [[Hooker Dam]] with offstream storage, direct pumping with offstream storage (two sizes), and a no-action pipeline alternative. All alternatives must operate without causing economic injury to downstream water rights holders through exchange for [[Central Arizona Project]] (CAP) water. Critical environmental concerns include impacts to native fish species ([[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]), both proposed for threatened status, and destruction of Resource Category 1 riparian habitats along the [[Gila River]].

## Background & Description

The [[Hooker Dam]] was originally authorized under the Colorado River Basin Project Act as part of the [[Central Arizona Project]] to develop 18,000 acre-feet annually for southwest New Mexico. In 1977, the President recommended elimination of this feature primarily for environmental reasons. In 1982, the Bureau of Reclamation initiated the Upper Gila Water Supply Study to evaluate Hooker Dam and alternatives.

**Study Area Context:**
- Central Grant County population: 28,000 (1986), projected 45,000 by 2045
- Current water use: ~5,200 acre-feet from overdrafted groundwater
- Projected 2045 water use: 9,000 acre-feet (8,300 acre-feet municipal, 700 acre-feet rural domestic)
- Per capita use projected to increase from 105 to 145 gallons per capita per day by 2030

**Alternative Plans Evaluated:**

1. **Conner Dam - 1**: 290-foot concrete arch dam, 109,000 acre-feet conservation storage (including 24,000 acre-feet sediment), 15-mile reservoir, develops full 18,000 acre-feet
2. **Conner Dam - 2**: 288-foot dam, 105,000 acre-feet storage, develops 9,000 acre-feet for projected use through 2045
3. **Direct Pumping - 1**: [[Mangas Creek Dam]] (300 feet high, 100,000 acre-feet storage), two 750 cfs pumping plants, develops 18,000 acre-feet
4. **Direct Pumping - 2**: Mangas Creek Dam (290 feet high, 80,000 acre-feet storage), two 350 cfs pumping plants, develops 9,000 acre-feet
5. **Hooker Dam with Offstream Storage**: 120-foot dam at Hooker site (32,000 acre-feet storage including 7,000 acre-feet sediment), Mangas Creek Dam, 420 cfs pumping capacity
6. **No Federal Action**: 50-mile pipeline to Deming area constructed by 2005, acquiring 8,300 acre-feet of water rights from overdrafted Mimbres Basin groundwater

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Aquatic Habitat Classification:**
- [[Gila River]] riparian and active floodplain habitats: Resource Category 1 (goal: no loss of habitat values due to uniqueness, rarity, and value to fish and wildlife)
- [[Mangas Creek]] riparian habitats: Resource Category 2 (goal: replacement in-kind if lost)

**Native Fish Species of Concern:**
On June 18, 1985, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] proposed two fish species for Federal threatened listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]]:
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)

Both species occur within the proposed project area, with portions of the Gila River proposed as critical habitat. Final listing determination expected late 1986.

**Habitat Impacts by Alternative:**

*Conner Dam - 1* (most environmentally damaging):
- Inundates 1,077 acres Resource Category 1 riparian/strand habitats
- Inundates 1,193 acres upland habitat
- Inundates 15 miles river habitat
- Affects 29 miles proposed spikedace critical habitat
- Affects 20 miles proposed loach minnow critical habitat
- Inundates portions of [[Gila River Bird Habitat Management Unit]], Research Natural Area, and BLM Area of Critical Environmental Concern
- Downstream flow alterations adversely modify native fish habitat to [[San Francisco River]] confluence
- Inhibits riparian regeneration to Lower Box

*Conner Dam - 2*: Impacts closely resemble Conner - 1; environmental impacts not substantially less despite smaller size

*Hooker Dam with Offstream Storage*:
- Inundates 375 acres Resource Category 1 riparian/strand habitats
- Inundates 1,663 acres upland habitat
- Inundates 5 miles river habitat
- Affects 29 miles proposed spikedace critical habitat
- Affects 20 miles proposed loach minnow critical habitat
- Flow alterations affect Cliff-Gila Valley through Redrock
- May affect [[Nature Conservancy]] managed lands
- Extreme flood events (290,000 cfs, estimated) would cause encroachment into [[Gila Wilderness Area]] for less than 5 hours (highest recorded flow since 1928: 32,400 cfs in 1978)

*Mangas Creek Dam* (component of Hooker and Direct Pumping alternatives):
- Least biological impacts of any dam
- Inundates 82 acres Resource Category 2 riparian/wetland habitats
- Inundates 1,246 acres upland habitat
- Inundates 6 miles river habitat (no special status fish habitat)
- Offstream location avoids direct Gila River impacts

*Direct Pumping - 1 and - 2*:
- Impacts include trapping of sensitive fish species
- Decreases water volumes during peak flood events (minimal relative to event volumes)
- Direct Pumping - 2 has fewer impacts than - 1 (lesser flow diminishment)
- **Least environmental impacts of action alternatives**
- Expected that impacts could be mitigated

**U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Position (February 10, 1986 informal consultation):**
- Available information suggests Hooker and Conner Dam alternatives would severely impact both proposed threatened species
- No reasonable or prudent alternatives appear to allow mainstream dam construction if species are listed
- Direct pumping with offstream storage may avoid impacts, but more information needed
- If species listed and jeopardy cannot be avoided, only solution is recovery and delisting
- Recovery could take 20+ years with costs exceeding $10 million
- No southwestern native fish species has yet been successfully recovered and delisted

**Preliminary Mitigation Measures:**
- Acquisition of floodplain and river habitat
- Revegetation with riparian plant species
- Fencing of acquired/revegetated lands and 5 miles riverine habitat to exclude cattle
- Reclamation of [[Turkey Creek]] for native fish
- For Conner Dams: multi-level intake structure for temperature/flow regulation; limiting capture to maintain minimum 28 cfs flows; fencing Cliff-Gila and Redrock Valleys mitigation lands
- For Hooker and Conner: refinement of instream flow studies for minimum flow requirements
- For Direct Pumping and Hooker: intake structures that do not attract or trap native fish

**Water Quality Concerns:**
- Both Mangas Creek and Conner Reservoirs expected to be eutrophic
- High aquatic plant production in warm months
- Potential problems: unpleasant tastes/odors, "swimmer's itch," trihalomethane formation during disinfection
- Metals of concern: cadmium, mercury, manganese, copper (require close monitoring and potential additional treatment)
- Mangas Creek Reservoir: greater potential for problems due to operation and location
- Unresolved concern: potential impacts from [[Phelps-Dodge Tyrone Mine]] tailings pond spills (have occurred in past)

**Cultural Resources:**
- Class I and II surveys completed; Class III intensive inventory planned for 1986
- General study area: 6-8 sites per square mile density
- Recorded sites: 29 (Mangas Creek), 16 (Hooker Reservoir), 33 (Conner Reservoir)
- Mitigation/avoidance plan to be developed in consultation with New Mexico State Historic Preservation Officer

## Key Findings

**Economic Analysis:**
- All Federal action alternatives have benefit/cost ratios less than 1.0
- Conner Dam alternatives and Direct Pumping - 2: B/C ratio = 0.6
- Direct Pumping - 1: B/C ratio = 0.4
- "No Federal Action" alternative costs less than all Federal alternatives
- However, "No Federal Action" does not provide renewable water supply (continues reliance on overdrafted groundwater)
- Using 1968 authorization interest rate (3.25% vs. current 8.625%), Conner alternatives have B/C ratios approximately equal to 1.0

**Capital Costs (1986 dollars, discounted):**
- No Federal Action: $93 million
- Conner Dam - 1: $126 million
- Conner Dam - 2: $124 million
- Direct Pumping - 1: $222 million
- Direct Pumping - 2: $150 million
- Hooker with Offstream Storage: $222 million

**Annual Costs:**
- No Federal Action: $11.6 million
- Conner - 1: $14.8 million
- Conner - 2: $14.5 million
- Direct Pumping - 1: $22.0 million
- Direct Pumping - 2: $18.0 million
- Hooker: $22.0 million

**Benefits:**
- Flood control (Conner alternatives only): $252,000 annually (60% reduction of $420,000 projected annual damages)
- Recreation (all Federal alternatives): $242,000-$345,000 annually depending on alternative
- Additional flood control storage at Conner not economically justified

**New Mexico CAP Repayment Obligations (80% financed over 50 years at 3.242%):**
- Conner - 1: $92 million
- Conner - 2: $90 million
- Direct Pumping - 1: $161 million
- Direct Pumping - 2: $109 million
- Hooker: $161 million
- CAP Operation, Maintenance, and Replacement: $55 per acre-foot

**Social and Economic Impacts:**
- Construction jobs for local residents: Conner (114), Hooker (184), Direct Pumping - 1 (166), Direct Pumping - 2 (109)
- Income increase in southwest New Mexico: Conner ($30 million), Hooker ($48 million), Direct Pumping - 1 ($43 million), Direct Pumping - 2 ($28 million)
- Mangas Creek Reservoir area residents would require relocation under [[Uniform Federal Relocation and Assistance Act]]

**Environmental Ranking (least to most destructive):**
1. No Federal Action (least impact)
2. Direct Pumping - 2
3. Direct Pumping - 1
4. Hooker with Offstream Storage
5. Conner Dam - 2
6. Conner Dam - 1 (most environmentally damaging)

**Critical Uncertainties:**
- Exchange agreement with downstream water rights holders not yet developed
- Section 7 Endangered Species Act consultation cannot begin until species formally listed (expected late 1986)
- Formal consultation results may show Conner and possibly Hooker cannot be constructed
- Alternative hydrologic analysis requested by New Mexico Natural Resources Department (based on anticipated downstream needs vs. full rights) not available for draft EIS
- Groundwater recharge/storage concept requires extensive additional study
- Mangas Creek diversion facility design requires additional studies for native fish effects and engineering

**Recreation Impacts:**
- Existing recreation displacement ranking (most to least): Conner - 1, Conner - 2, Hooker, Direct Pumping - 1, Direct Pumping - 2, No Federal Action
- Conner and Mangas Reservoirs offer similar flatwater recreational opportunities
- Hooker Reservoir offers extremely limited flatwater opportunities
- Federal agencies could develop/manage recreation at Conner; sponsor needed for Mangas Creek
- Without sponsor, Reclamation provides only minimum health and safety facilities

**Key Constraints:**
- All alternatives must operate without economic injury or cost to downstream water rights holders
- Exchange for CAP water required with downstream Gila River water rights holders
- Federal District Court of Arizona approval needed for changes in Gila Decree accounting
- Project water cannot be developed until exchange provisions finalized
- If spikedace and loach minnow listed as threatened, mainstream dams likely cannot proceed without species recovery and delisting (20+ year timeline, $10+ million cost, no guarantee of success)

**Public Input Process:**
- Public meetings scheduled April 14-17, 1986 in Albuquerque, Silver City, Safford, and Coolidge
- Comments requested by April 25, 1986
- Results summary and "proposed action" identification to be published in Newsletter Issue VII (early July 1986)
- Draft EIS scheduled for September 1986
