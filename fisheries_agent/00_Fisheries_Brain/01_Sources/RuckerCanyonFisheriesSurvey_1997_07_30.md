# Rucker Canyon Fisheries Survey

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Leuciscidae #Cyprinidae #desert-fishes

## Executive Summary
This brief field survey report documents a seine-based fish sampling effort conducted on July 30, 1997, in [[Rucker Canyon]], Cochise County, Arizona, by researchers from Arizona State University (Brian Bagley, Patti Sowka). Two sites along the canyon stream were sampled: one near the Red Rock Canyon road turnoff (150 m seine haul) and one at a second road crossing downstream of [[Rucker Canyon Lake]] (30 m seine haul, cut short by an approaching heavy rainstorm). The survey recorded low-diversity assemblages dominated by native [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (longfin dace) and [[Campostoma ornatum]] (Mexican stoneroller), with a single non-native [[Pimephales promelas]] (fathead minnow) individual captured at the upstream site. Notably, the report documents that [[Rucker Canyon Lake]] had been reduced to a braided, heavily silted channel following a wildfire several years prior, indicating significant post-fire habitat degradation.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide morphometric, meristic, or detailed morphological descriptions of the sampled specimens. Only presence/absence and abundance counts by species were recorded. General taxonomic notes relevant to the species collected:

- **[[Agosia chrysogaster]]** (longfin dace) — a small-bodied native minnow (Leuciscidae) characteristic of desert stream systems in the American Southwest; tolerant of intermittent, warm, and turbid flows.
- **[[Campostoma ornatum]]** (Mexican stoneroller) — a grazing minnow species with a specialized cartilaginous jaw ridge adapted for scraping algae/periphyton from substrate; native to Sonoran/Chihuahuan desert drainages.
- **[[Pimephales promelas]]** (fathead minnow) — a small, hardy, non-native cyprinid widely introduced across North America, often as baitfish; tolerant of poor water quality and habitat disturbance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** Intermittent/perennial desert canyon stream within the [[Rucker Canyon]] drainage, Cochise County, Arizona — a Madrean sky-island riparian system.
- **Site 1 (upstream, at Red Rock Canyon road turnoff):** Sampled over a 150 m seine haul; supported three species, suggesting relatively intact instream habitat conditions at time of sampling.
- **Site 2 (downstream, at 2nd road crossing below Rucker Canyon Lake):** Sampled over a shorter 30 m haul due to an incoming storm; supported two of the three species (native minnows only), with no fathead minnow detected.
- **Disturbance context:** [[Rucker Canyon Lake]], once a standing water body, had degraded into "nothing more than a braided channel" due to heavy siltation resulting from a wildfire several years before the survey — a clear example of post-fire sedimentation impacting aquatic habitat structure and likely reducing lentic habitat availability for fish and other aquatic organisms.
- **Species co-occurrence:** [[Agosia chrysogaster]] and [[Campostoma ornatum]] co-occurred at both sites, suggesting shared habitat preference for flowing, seined-accessible stream reaches. The presence of non-native [[Pimephales promelas]] at only the upstream site may reflect stocking/bait-bucket introduction pathways or connectivity to human access points (road crossing).

## Key Findings
- Total of three fish species documented in the July 1997 survey: [[Agosia chrysogaster]] (29 individuals combined across sites), [[Campostoma ornatum]] (16 individuals combined across sites), and [[Pimephales promelas]] (1 individual, upstream site only).
- Site 1 (150 m seine): Agosia chrysogaster (22), Campostoma ornatum (13), Pimephales promelas (1).
- Site 2 (30 m seine): Agosia chrysogaster (7), Campostoma ornatum (3).
- Native desert minnows ([[Agosia chrysogaster]], [[Campostoma ornatum]]) dominated the catch numerically at both locations, indicating a largely native-dominated assemblage at the time of survey.
- [[Rucker Canyon Lake]] was found to be functionally non-existent as a lake — reduced to a braided, siltation-choked channel — attributed to post-wildfire erosion and sediment deposition, representing a significant habitat alteration event with likely long-term consequences for aquatic community structure.
- Survey duration/effort at the downstream site was truncated due to onset of heavy rains, introducing a potential sampling bias (lower effort, fewer individuals captured) relative to the upstream site.
- The report highlights the vulnerability of small desert stream and reservoir systems in the [[Rucker Canyon]] watershed to wildfire-driven sedimentation impacts, an important consideration for management of native fish habitat in Madrean sky-island streams of southeastern Arizona.
