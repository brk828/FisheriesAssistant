# The Parasitic Copepods Achtheres percarum and Salmincola gordoni in Yorkshire

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lernaeopodidae #parasitology #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This report documents new distribution records for two parasitic copepods of the family [[Lernaeopodidae]] in Yorkshire, England. [[Achtheres percarum]] Nordmann is recorded from three Yorkshire localities (Doe Park Reservoir, Bretton Park Lake, and River Ouse at Lower Dunsforth) representing significant range extensions from its 1954 first British record in the Thames drainage. [[Salmincola gordoni]] Gurney, previously known only from salmonid hosts, is reported from the River Swale and remarkably from [[Perch]] (Perca fluviatilis) in Bretton Park Lake, representing an unusual host record. The study documents considerable morphological variation in A. percarum, particularly in maxillary arm length, and discusses taxonomic separation of these morphologically similar species.

## Background & Description

[[Achtheres percarum]] was first collected in Britain in 1954 from the River Colne and canal systems in the Thames drainage area (Harding & Gervers, 1956), with subsequent reports from Rostherne Mere, Cheshire. The species exhibits considerable morphological variability in body parts not involved in attachment and feeding. Adult females range from 2.5-5 mm in length depending on population. The species shows marked variation in maxillary arm length, with some individuals having very short arms scarcely extending beyond the cephalothorax, while others have arms more than twice as long extending far beyond the cephalothorax limits. The maxillary arms jointly give rise to a single median bulla inserted into the host tissue. The cephalothorax is more or less rectangular when viewed dorsally, and the hindbody shows distinct signs of segmentation.

[[Salmincola gordoni]] was originally described from specimens collected in the River Rye, Yorkshire, parasitizing [[Brown Trout]] (Salmo trutta) and [[Grayling]] (Thymallus vulgaris). The species has since been reported from Scotland and the Isle of Man (on Sea Trout), suggesting euryhaline capabilities similar to its close relative S. salmonea. The species is distinguished by a distinctly triangular cephalothorax when viewed dorsally and absence of clear hindbody segmentation. Like many lernaeopodid parasites, it is of marine ancestry and presumably became established in freshwater during post-glacial times.

Both species belong to [[Lernaeopodidae]] and have been confused historically due to morphological similarities. However, they can be separated by unambiguous differences in cephalic appendages, cephalothorax shape, and bulla structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Yorkshire material of [[Achtheres percarum]] came from three distinct drainage systems: Doe Park Reservoir (overflow to Harden Beck joining River Aire at Bingley), Bretton Park Lake (overflow to River Dearne joining the Don at Denaby), and the River Ouse at Lower Dunsforth. All specimens were females collected from [[Perch]] (Perca fluviatilis). The buccal cavity is the most usual attachment site; specimens from the Ouse were located there and caused considerable inflammation in host tissues.

At Bretton Park, 33 parasites were obtained from four fishes, with one apparently uninfected Perch in the sample. The specimens from this locality were particularly squat and fat compared to more slender individuals from other populations. Some eggs from Ouse specimens contained copepodid larvae beginning to emerge; all naupliar stages are passed through within the egg.

[[Salmincola gordoni]] is typically a parasite of salmonid fishes ([[Brown Trout]], [[Grayling]], [[Salmon]]). Its occurrence on [[Perch]] at Bretton Park represents an unusual host record. The single specimen from Bretton Park was an egg-bearing female found among A. percarum parasites. The species appears markedly euryhaline, with populations established both in freshwater far from the sea (River Rye) and on Sea Trout in marine environments (Port Erin Bay, Isle of Man).

The Yorkshire Ouse system now hosts four lernaeopodid copepod species: A. percarum, S. gordoni, [[Salmincola thymalli]] (parasitizing Grayling in River Rye), and [[Tracheliastes polycolpus]] (parasitizing [[Dace]] and [[Chub]]). Both A. percarum and T. polycolpus are probably comparatively recent introductions to British fauna.

## Key Findings

**Distribution Records for Achtheres percarum:**
- Doe Park Reservoir near Denholme (SE 077340): 3 specimens from one Perch, June 1958
- Bretton Park Lake (SE 285125): 33 specimens from four Perch, August 1961
- River Ouse at Lower Dunsforth (SE 450645): 3 specimens from two Perch, July 1966
- All collected material consisted entirely of females

**Morphological Variation in A. percarum:**
- Considerable inter- and intra-population variation in non-functional body parts
- Bretton Park specimens particularly squat and fat
- Maxillary arm length highly variable: shortest arms scarcely extended beyond cephalothorax, longest arms more than twice as long in animals of approximately the same size
- Size variation: most Bretton Park specimens ~2.5 mm length (smaller than literature reports of 3-5 mm); two Ouse specimens ~3 mm
- Variation suspected to be functionally significant and related to parasite establishment position

**Salmincola gordoni Records:**
- River Rye (type locality): specimen collected 1960 on [[Brown Trout]]
- River Swale at Richmond: single specimen from under operculum of Brown Trout
- Bretton Park Lake: one specimen from [[Perch]] (unusual host record)
- Co-occurrence with A. percarum at Bretton Park suggests wider distribution than currently realized

**Taxonomic Separation:**
- Cephalothorax shape: A. percarum rectangular dorsally, S. gordoni distinctly triangular
- Hindbody segmentation: distinct in A. percarum, absent in S. gordoni
- Bulla structure differs between species
- Unambiguous differences in cephalic appendages

**Host Specificity:**
- A. percarum: typical host [[Perch]] (Perca fluviatilis)
- S. gordoni: typical hosts salmonids ([[Brown Trout]], [[Grayling]], [[Salmon]]); unusual record from [[Perch]] at Bretton Park
- S. gordoni specimen from Perch was egg-bearing female, indicating successful maturation on atypical host

**Ecological Implications:**
- Both species may be more widely distributed than current records indicate
- A. percarum and T. polycolpus likely recent introductions to British fauna
- S. gordoni demonstrates euryhaline capabilities, occurring in both freshwater and marine environments
- Potential for S. salmonea to be added to Yorkshire Ouse fauna if searched for on [[Salmon]] and Sea Trout
