# The Larva of a New Subterranean Water Beetle, Haideoporus texanus (Coleoptera: Dytiscidae: Hydroporinae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #dytiscidae #coleoptera #subterranean-ecology

## Executive Summary

This study describes the larva of *[[Haideoporus texanus]]*, the first hypogeous (subterranean) dytiscid beetle discovered in North America. Three larvae were collected from a flowing artesian well at the Aquatic Station (formerly [[San Marcos Federal Fish Hatchery]]) in [[San Marcos, Texas]]. The well draws water from the [[Edwards Aquifer]] at a depth of 59.5 m from a 1.5 m cavern. The larva exhibits typical troglobitic adaptations including complete absence of ocelli and transparent body coloration. Morphological comparisons with the Japanese subterranean species *[[Morimotoa phreatica]]* reveal close similarities with minor differences in antennal structure, cercal segmentation, and maxillary setation.

## Background & Description

*[[Haideoporus texanus]]* belongs to the family [[Dytiscidae]], subfamily [[Hydroporinae]]. The species was originally described by Young and Longley (1976) from 29 adult specimens; an additional 8 adults have since been collected, bringing the total to 37 known adults.

**Larval Morphology:**
- Length (including cerci): 5.5 mm
- Greatest width: 1.8 mm
- Body transparent with light yellow-brown tint
- Ocelli completely absent from ocular area (troglobitic adaptation)
- Head almost quadrate behind nasale
- Nasale broad, reflexed, elongate with club-shaped setae on anteroventral margin
- Antenna cylindrical, 4-segmented; 3rd segment with 3 acicular setae
- Gular sutures barely visible on ventral head surface
- Mandible long, falciform
- Maxillary palpus slender, elongate, 3-segmented
- Labial palpus slender, 2-segmented
- Legs elongate, 5-segmented with 2 elongate tarsal claws
- Abdomen 8-segmented, setose
- Cerci unsegmented with specific setal arrangement: 2 lateral setae at basal third, 1 lateral seta at apical third, 4 apical setae

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collection site is an artesian well drilled in 1895 at the [[Aquatic Station]], [[Southwest Texas State University]]. The well penetrates to a 1.5 m cavern at 59.5 m depth within the [[Edwards Aquifer]], a large groundwater reservoir in Edwards and associated limestones along the [[Balcones Escarpment]] in Texas. The water source is shared with [[San Marcos Springs]], a large first-order spring located approximately 670 m from the well.

This locality is the type-locality for numerous subterranean organisms including:
- [[Texas Blind Salamander]] (*[[Typhlomolge rathbuni]]*, synonym *[[Eurycea rathbuni]]*)
- Several blind, depigmented invertebrates (crustaceans, planarian, snail)

**Collection Methods:**
A 12-inch pipe carries water from the well to a distribution chamber where nets were placed. Nets were removed and contents preserved approximately every two days. Regular netting began December 1973 and continued through the study period.

**Environmental Conditions:**
The subterranean habitat provides uniform environmental conditions year-round, characteristic of troglobitic ecosystems.

## Key Findings

**Larval Collections:**
- First larva: 18 September 1974 (collected by Joe Kolb)
- Second larva: 17 August 1975 (collected by Glenn Longley)
- Third larva: 5 October 1975 (collected by Glenn Longley)

**Adult Collections:**
- Adults collected in all months except August and November
- One teneral (newly emerged) adult collected 9 January 1974
- Total of 37 adults known

**Breeding Season:**
Evidence indicates *H. texanus* breeds during the last 4-6 months of the year (August to January). Additional collecting needed to determine if breeding occurs throughout the year or is restricted to this period.

**Comparative Morphology:**
The larva of *H. texanus* is very similar to *[[Morimotoa phreatica]]* Ueno, a blind hydroporine from Japan. Key differences:

1. **Antenna:** *M. phreatica* terminates in small apical segment accompanied by seta of similar size; verification needed for *H. texanus* using compound microscopy
2. **Cerci:** *M. phreatica* has apparent apical segment with 3 long basal setae and 1 subapical seta; *H. texanus* cerci unsegmented with different setal arrangement
3. **Maxillary palpus:** *M. phreatica* bears 3 subapical setae on last segment; *H. texanus* has only 1
4. **Stipes:** *M. phreatica* bears 3 ventrobasal setae; *H. texanus* has 2 ventral setae (1 basal, 1 apical)

**Pupation Mystery:**
Since all known dytiscid larvae pupate in damp places away from water, the authors hypothesize that *H. texanus* larvae pupate either:
- On margins of partially-filled cavern streams
- In air spaces trapped in irregularities in limestone cavern sides and ceilings when filled with water

**Specimen Deposition:**
The three larvae deposited in the [[National Museum of Natural History]], [[Smithsonian Institution]], Washington, D.C.
