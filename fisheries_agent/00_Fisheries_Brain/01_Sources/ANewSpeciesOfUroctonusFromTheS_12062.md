# Uroctonus franckei: A New Scorpion from the Sierra Nevada of California

#arachnology #scorpiones #vaejovidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes a new scorpion, [[Uroctonus Franckei]] Williams, 1986, from the eastern slope of the [[Sierra Nevada]] in California. The species is known only from elevations above 2133 m, on slopes dominated by [[Pinus Jeffreyi]] (yellow pine). Its closest relative is [[Uroctonus Mordax]] Thorell. The document is a terrestrial arachnology study, not an aquatic one, so it contains no data on fish, invertebrates in water bodies, plankton, or water chemistry.

## Background & Description

- **Author and affiliation:** [[Stanley C. Williams]], San Francisco State University.
- **Context:** Field collecting along the eastern Sierra Nevada in 1980 found an abundant and diverse scorpion community above 2000 m. A large, dark, undescribed species stood out because it occurred only above 2133 m.
- **Etymology:** Named after [[Oscar F. Francke]] of Texas Tech University, in recognition of his contributions to scorpion biology and systematics.
- **Holotype and allotype:** Male and female. Collected 17 September 1980, 17.7 km (11 miles) W of Big Pine, Inyo County, at 2376 m (7796 ft). Deposited at the [[California Academy of Sciences]] (Entomology Type No. 15749).

**Diagnostic characters (as reported):**
- Total length up to 57 mm; body dark reddish-brown, often appearing blackish.
- Carapace width to diad diameter ratio 6.2–6.8.
- Chela length to palm width ratio 3.3–3.4 (described as 2.0 or greater in the comparison with *U. mordax*).
- Pectine teeth 13–14 in males and 9–12 in females.
- Supernumerary denticles 7 on the fixed finger and 8 on the movable finger.
- Primary row denticles in 6 subrows on the fixed finger and 7 on the movable finger.
- Telotarsal soles with a single ventral row of spiniform setae (11–12 per sole).

**Differences from *U. mordax* (as reported):**
- More slender metasomal segments, with metasomal length to width ratio above 8.0.
- A more swollen chela palm.
- Reduced macrosetae on metasomal keels, with a dorsolateral macrosetal formula of 1-1-1-2 on segments I–IV (versus 1-2-2-2).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Elevation and vegetation:** The species occurs only above 2133 m, on slopes dominated by yellow pine.
- **Abundance:** The species "was never found to be abundant."
- **Detection method:** It was collected only by [[Ultraviolet Detection]], a nocturnal fluorescence-based search method.
- **Sympatry:** It occurs alongside [[Paruroctonus Boreus]] (Girard).
- **Geographic variation:** Smaller individuals are lighter in color than larger ones. Specimens from near Whitney Portal are less reddish than those further north.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Uroctonus franckei* is distinguished from its nearest relative, *U. mordax*, by metasomal slenderness, chela palm shape, and macrosetal arrangement.
- **Size and sexual dimorphism:**
  - Holotype total length is 58 mm and allotype is 52 mm (the diagnosis gives up to 57 mm; the discrepancy is in the source).
  - Paratype total length is 39–57 mm in males and 38–54 mm in females.
  - Pectine tooth counts are 13–14 (mode 14) in males and 9–12 (mode 11) in females.
- **Type series:**
  - Holotype and allotype from Inyo County, 2376 m.
  - Topoparatypes listed as "3 males, 2 females," but the itemized records give 6 males from Oh Ridge Camp, June Lake (Mono County, 2133 m, 20 June 1980) and 2 females from 4.8 km E of Whitney Portal (Inyo County, 30 September 1980). The source's count is internally inconsistent.
- **Rarity:** The species is restricted to high elevations, is uncommon, and is found only with UV detection.
- **Nomenclature:** The species is named for Oscar F. Francke.
