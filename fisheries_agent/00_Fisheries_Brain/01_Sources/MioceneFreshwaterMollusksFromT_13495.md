## Executive Summary
This paper by Dwight W. Taylor (1985) describes five fossil mollusk specimens representing three species collected from the Miocene-aged [[Clarkia Fossil Site]] in Idaho. The assemblage includes a unionid clam (*Anodonta oregonensis*), a pleurocerid gastropod (*Juga silicula*), and an indeterminate viviparid gastropod (*Bellamya* sp.). Though sparse, this mollusk fauna provides corroborating paleoclimatic and paleoecological evidence — consistent with associated floral data — indicating that the Miocene climate of northern Idaho was more humid (especially in summer) and had milder winters than the present-day climate. The specimens are taphonomically significant as they demonstrate transport into a lacustrine (lake) depositional environment from surrounding stream habitats, rather than in-situ deposition.

## Background & Description
Preservation quality is poor to moderate across all specimens, as is typical for shells preserved alongside fossil leaves — the original calcareous shell material has dissolved, leaving compressions of the external periostracum.

- **[[Anodonta oregonensis]]** (clam, family [[Unionidae]]): One probably half-grown specimen. Preserves the shell outline, notably the arcuate (curved) ventral margin diagnostic of the species.
- **[[Bellamya]] sp.** (gastropod, family [[Viviparidae]]): Two compressed shells lacking surface sculpture detail, plus one operculum measuring 15 mm long × 12 mm wide, matching the outline of extant congeners. Insufficient detail to distinguish from the related Miocene species *Bellamya turneri* (Hannibal).
- **[[Juga silicula]]** (gastropod, family [[Pleuroceridae]]): One compression specimen with an external sculpture mold — indicating unusually slow shell dissolution relative to sediment compression. Preserves ~6 whorls of the spire with:
  - **Axial sculpture**: opisthocyrt (backward-curving) ribs, rounded in cross-section, nearly straight over the anterior two-thirds of the whorl, curving sharply toward (but terminating before) the posterior suture.
  - **Spiral sculpture**: ~6 spiral cords crossing the ribs, absent in the interspaces; the strongest cord lies adjacent to the anterior suture.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **[[Juga silicula]]**: A characteristic lotic (stream/river) species, occasionally found in small lakes only where wave action maintains year-round oxygenation. It could not have survived in the quiet, soft-substrate, and reportedly toxic/anaerobic conditions of [[Clarkia Lake]] — its presence in the fossil deposit is interpreted as the result of transport (washing in) from nearby streams rather than in-situ habitation.
- **[[Anodonta oregonensis]]**: Inhabits lakes and slow-moving streams, ranging from northernmost California to southernmost Alaska. Nearest modern occurrence to the fossil site is Latah Creek near Spokane, WA (~70 miles NW).
- **[[Bellamya]]**: A genus typically found in lake environments; today restricted to Old World tropical-to-temperate regions and notably absent from severe-winter climates.
- Both *Anodonta* and *Bellamya*, despite being lake-dwellers, show evidence of being washed into the depositional site rather than living there directly, based on geochemical evidence of toxic/anaerobic lake-bottom conditions (per Smiley and Rember 1979). Taylor proposes a taphonomic mechanism: decomposition gases from the soft bodies of these thin-shelled, large-bodied taxa likely caused the shells to float until transported to the burial site.

## Key Findings
- The Clarkia mollusk assemblage (*Juga*, *Anodonta*, *Bellamya*) represents a combination of genera that **do not co-occur** anywhere in modern Idaho or even the broader western United States today, indicating a distinct Miocene paleoenvironment.
- **Paleoclimatic humidity signal**: *Juga silicula* today survives only on the humid western slopes of the Cascade Range, implying the Miocene climate at Clarkia was significantly more humid than present, particularly during summer months.
- **Paleoclimatic temperature signal**: *Bellamya* does not tolerate severe winters in its modern range, supporting independent botanical evidence for a warmer climate with milder winters during the Miocene at this site.
- **Taphonomic conclusion**: None of the mollusks likely lived directly within [[Clarkia Lake]] itself; all specimens appear to have been transported into the lake basin — *Juga* from streams, and *Anodonta*/*Bellamya* via post-mortem flotation from surrounding lake habitats.
- **Biogeographic displacement**: The nearest living populations of these taxa are considerably distant from the fossil site (*Anodonta*: ~70 miles NW; *Juga*: ~200 miles west), underscoring the magnitude of Miocene-to-present climatic and range shifts in the Pacific Northwest.
- This mollusk evidence is presented as convergent with, and corroborating, contemporaneous paleobotanical findings from the same [[Clarkia Fossil Site]] regarding Miocene environmental conditions in the Pacific Northwest.
