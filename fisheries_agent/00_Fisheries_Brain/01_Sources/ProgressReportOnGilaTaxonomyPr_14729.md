# Gila Taxonomy Project Progress Report (December 1992)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #taxonomy #conservation-genetics

## Executive Summary

This progress report documents the status of the [[Gila Taxonomy Project]] as of December 11, 1992, submitted to the Biology Committee of the [[Colorado River Implementation Recovery Committee]]. The project, managed by Dr. Wayne C. Starnes of the [[Smithsonian Institution]], aims to comprehensively sample and analyze all [[Colorado River]] drainage and Mexican [[Gila]] populations using genetic (protein electrophoresis and DNA analyses) and morphological methods. By April 1993, approximately 80-85 present or former Gila populations will have been sampled, representing all targeted populations. Fresh material has been obtained from 44 populations, with museum specimens supplementing the study. The project involves collaboration with [[Arizona State University]], [[University of Michigan]], [[Brigham Young University]], and [[Genetics Analyses Incorporated]].

## Background & Description

The [[Gila Taxonomy Project]] represents a comprehensive taxonomic investigation of Gila species across their range in the [[Colorado River]] drainage and northern Mexico. The study employs three complementary analytical approaches:

**Genetic Analyses:**
- Protein electrophoresis examining approximately 55 presumptive loci (conducted by [[Genetics Analyses Incorporated]], Smithville, Texas)
- DNA analyses including RAPD techniques and cytochrome-B mitochondrial DNA sequencing (conducted at [[Brigham Young University]])
- DNA extraction from both fresh and preserved museum material using cloning techniques combined with polymerase chain reaction

**Morphological Studies:**
- Computer-based analyses of captured images (conducted at [[Arizona State University]] by Dr. Michael Douglas)
- Non-lethal sampling methods employed where possible
- Integration with genetic data for comprehensive population characterization

**Sampling Coverage:**
The project targets all extant and historical Gila populations across multiple river systems including the [[Colorado River]] mainstem, [[Green River]], [[Yampa River]], [[Gila River]], [[Salt River]], [[Verde River]], [[San Pedro River]], [[Virgin River]], and related Mexican drainages ([[Rio Yaqui]], [[Rio Fuerte]], [[Rio Mayo]], [[Rio Sinaloa]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study addresses populations across diverse aquatic habitats throughout the [[Colorado River Basin]], from high-elevation tributaries in Colorado and Wyoming to desert streams in Arizona and New Mexico. Several populations face conservation challenges:

**Population Status Concerns:**
- [[Agua Fria]] system populations may be on the verge of extirpation
- Four populations on the [[White Mountain Apache Reservation]] in [[Salt River]] tributaries are not accessible due to permit denial
- [[White River]] (Colorado) and upper [[Yampa River]] populations are underrepresented by fresh material
- Many historical populations are represented only by museum specimens from the 1900s-1960s

**Museum Collections Sampled:**
- [[Arizona State University]]
- [[University of Michigan]]
- [[Smithsonian Institution]]
- USFWS Fort Collins collection

The project documents both extant populations and those potentially extirpated, providing critical baseline data for conservation and recovery efforts.

## Key Findings

**Sampling Progress (as of December 1992):**
- Total specimens sampled for genetic tissue: 1,185
  - Fresh/captive specimens: 867
  - Preserved museum specimens: 318
- Seventy-five collection attempts made for populations believed extant
- Fresh material successfully obtained from 44 populations
- Anticipated 200 additional fresh specimens to be collected (total ~1,400 available for DNA analyses)

**Genetic Analysis Status:**

*DNA Studies:*
- DNA extracted from 457 samples (fresh and preserved material)
- Amplification achieved on 214 fresh samples via RAPD techniques (currently under analysis)
- Cytochrome-B mitochondrial DNA amplified for 50 samples (museum and fresh combined; data currently being examined)
- Anticipated that approximately 500 of available specimens will be used in DNA investigations
- DNA extractions largely completed; amplification well underway

*Protein Electrophoresis:*
- 557 of 867 fresh specimens analyzed for ~55 presumptive loci
- Anticipated total of ~1,050 specimens to be analyzed
- Work approximately 53% complete
- Not applicable to museum specimens (requires fresh tissue)

**Morphological Analysis Status:**
- Images captured on 423 specimens (primarily from non-lethal sampling)
- Approximately 900+ preserved specimens remain to be imaged
- Anticipated total of ~2,000 specimens for morphological analysis
- Post-doctoral fellow recently added to assist Dr. Michael Douglas at [[Arizona State University]]
- Image capture and analysis described as relatively rapid once initiated

**Geographic Coverage:**
- Major river systems comprehensively sampled including [[Colorado River]] mainstem (Arizona, Colorado, Utah), [[Green River]] system, [[Gila River]] system, and tributaries
- Mexican populations in five northern river systems to be sampled in spring 1993 with assistance from Mexican biologists and Dr. Dean Hendrickson ([[University of Texas]])
- Specific population data provided for 100+ sampling locations with detailed status of genetic and morphological work

**Collection Methodology:**
- Both lethal and non-lethal sampling employed
- Museum specimens utilized for populations no longer accessible or extirpated
- Collection codes assigned for cross-referencing genetic and morphological specimens
- Sample sizes range from 1-120 specimens per population depending on availability and population status
