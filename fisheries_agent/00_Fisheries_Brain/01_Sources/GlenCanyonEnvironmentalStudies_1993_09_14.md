# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies – Proposed Report Development

## Executive Summary
This document is **not a primary fish-science or morphological data source**; it is an internal Bureau of Reclamation memorandum (dated September 14, 1993) from Dave Wegner to Duncan Patten outlining a **report-development strategy** for the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program. The memo proposes a tiered structure of scientific reports (individual, integrated, monograph, and popularized public versions) intended to consolidate research findings — including aquatic ecosystem studies — from the Glen Canyon Dam / Grand Canyon Colorado River system into a peer-reviewed, publicly accessible format to support [[Long-Term Monitoring and Adaptive Management]] of the dam-regulated river. No specific fish species, morphological descriptions, or quantitative ecological data are presented in the source text.

## Background & Description
No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic data on fish or other organisms are provided in this document. The text is administrative in nature and does not describe any species' physical characteristics.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no direct ecological data are given, the document identifies the **institutional framework** through which aquatic ecosystem research in the [[Colorado River]] below [[Glen Canyon Dam]] was to be organized and reported:

- The GCES program's Phase II and interim flow studies are structured around **five major integrated report categories**: 
  1. Physical system
  2. **Aquatic system** (the category most relevant to fish and aquatic ecology)
  3. Terrestrial system
  4. Economics
  5. Cultural resources
  — plus a sixth synthesis chapter on ecosystem response.
- The "Aquatic system" volume was intended to integrate individual researcher reports (e.g., contributions attributed to authors such as Schmidt, Graf, Nelson, Andrews, Webb) addressing sediment transport, flow regime, and downstream habitat conditions in the [[Grand Canyon]] reach of the Colorado River.
- A recurring theme is the relationship between **dam operations (flow regimes)** and downstream ecological/habitat conditions, framed as "[[Implications to Dam Management]]" — indicating that aquatic/fish habitat responses to controlled discharge from Glen Canyon Dam were a central management concern.
- Reference to a planned **sediment meeting** (October 1993, involving Schmidt, Lewis, Dawdy, Hughes, and Graf) suggests linked concurrent research on sediment dynamics, which is ecologically significant for aquatic habitat (e.g., substrate conditions for native fish spawning) in the regulated river below the dam.

## Key Findings
- The memo establishes a **four-tier reporting hierarchy** (A: Individual Reports; B: Integrated Monograph Reports; C: Integrated Volume Reports I–VI; D: Popularized Public Monograph) for consolidating GCES scientific findings, including aquatic ecosystem research, into peer-reviewed and publicly accessible formats.
- Target completion timeline: Integration outlines by **January 31, 1994**; draft integration chapters by **September 30, 1994**; final integration chapters by **December 31, 1994**; completed monograph volume by **March 1, 1995**; final integrated reports by **July 31, 1995**.
- Explicit programmatic goal: prevent GCES research (including aquatic/fish studies) from becoming inaccessible "grey literature," and instead route findings through peer review to build scientific credibility for [[Adaptive Management]] of Glen Canyon Dam operations.
- The **Aquatic system** is formally designated as one of five core scientific domains of the Grand Canyon ecosystem monitoring effort, though this source contains no substantive fish or aquatic species data itself — it is a meta-document about how such data would eventually be reported.
- All consolidated products were intended to be archived within the **GCES Scientific Information Management** system, linked electronically to Bureau of Reclamation and Museum of Northern Arizona libraries and national scientific databases.
