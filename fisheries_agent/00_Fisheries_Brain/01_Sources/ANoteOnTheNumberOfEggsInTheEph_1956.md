## Executive Summary
This 1956 note by W. J. P. Smyly (Freshwater Biological Association, Ambleside) addresses a taxonomic and biological misconception regarding resting-egg counts in the ephippium of the cladoceran *Acantholebris curvirostris*. Contrary to the widely held belief (per Scourfield & Harding 1941) that *[[Eurycercus Lamellatus]]* was the only cladoceran species with more than two resting eggs per ephippium, Smyly presents empirical count data from 109 ephippia collected from English Lake District tarns, demonstrating that *A. curvirostris* commonly bears 1–6 resting eggs, and exceptionally up to 8. This finding corroborates and extends earlier, less-documented observations by Schödler (1846) and Berg (1929).

## Background & Description
- **Structure in question:** The **ephippium** — a protective covering for resting eggs, formed from the portion of the shell-valves overlying the brood-pouch.
- **Taxonomic distribution of ephippia:** Present in four cladoceran families:
  - **[[Daphniidae]]** — ephippium always contains 2 resting eggs (fixed number, per Schödler 1846).
  - **[[Macrothricidae]]** — most highly developed ephippium; egg number is variable (2–6, per Schödler 1846 and Berg 1929), and in *A. curvirostris* specifically, 1–8 eggs (Smyly, this study).
  - **[[Bosminidae]]** — typically 1 resting egg.
  - **[[Chydoridae]]** — typically 1 resting egg, with exceptions:
    - *[[Camptocercus Rectirostus]]* (Schödler) — occasionally 2 (Weismann 1877).
    - *[[Eurycercus Lamellatus]]* — 2 to 10, exceptionally 13 (Scourfield 1901, 1902).
- **Key morphological note (Schödler 1846):** In *Acanthocercus* (= *Acantholebris*) and *Eurycercus lamellatus*, egg number in the ephippium appears to increase with the age of the female — unlike in Daphniidae, where the number remains fixed at two regardless of age.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Freshwater tarns of the English Lake District; specimens were obtained from plankton/benthic collections across various tarns.
- **Taxon group:** Order [[Cladocera]] (water fleas), a group of small freshwater/planktonic crustaceans that reproduce via cyclical parthenogenesis, producing resting eggs (via the ephippium) as a resistant overwintering/dispersal stage under adverse conditions.
- **Reproductive ecology:** The ephippium functions as a diapausing egg case, protecting embryos through unfavorable environmental periods; variability in egg number per ephippium may reflect female age, resource allocation, or species-specific reproductive strategy.

## Key Findings
- **Empirical dataset:** Frequency distribution of resting eggs across 109 ephippia of *A. curvirostris*:

| No. of eggs per ephippium | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No. of ephippia | 4 | 26 | 27 | 28 | 15 |

  (Note: totals sum to 100 of the 109 reported; text indicates counts range up to 6, exceptionally 8, suggesting additional low-frequency categories beyond the transcribed table rows.)

- **Central conclusion:** Ephippia with 3 or 4 resting eggs are **just as common** as those with 2 eggs — directly refuting the notion that *Eurycercus lamellatus* is uniquely capable of bearing more than two resting eggs.
- **Corroboration of prior literature:**
  - Fryer (1953) described a single ephippium of *A. curvirostris* with 3 eggs, but sample size was insufficient to establish frequency.
  - Neither Berg (1929) nor Schödler (1846) quantified how often ephippia with >2 eggs occurred, despite noting the phenomenon.
- **Historical clarification:** Smyly reproduces Schödler's (1846) original German passage verbatim, confirming that Schödler had already observed 2, 3, and 4 eggs in "Acanthocercus" ephippia, and 2–10 in *Eurycercus*, contrasting with the fixed count of 2 in Daphniidae.
- **Significance:** This note corrects a persistent error in cladoceran taxonomic literature (originating from [[Scourfield And Harding 1941]]) regarding the uniqueness of *Eurycercus lamellatus*'s multi-egg ephippium, reinforcing that variable, higher egg counts also characterize *Acantholebris curvirostris* within [[Macrothricidae]].
