# Effects of Strip Mining on Small-Stream Fishes in East-Central Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Percidae #StripMining #StreamEcology

## Executive Summary
This 17-month field study (May 1967–September 1969) examined the impacts of coal [[Strip Mining]] on fish communities in two small tributary streams of the [[North Fork Kentucky River]] drainage: [[Leatherwood Creek]] (mined) and [[Bear Branch]] (initially unmined control, later mined). Unlike many prior studies focused on acid-mine drainage causing dramatic fish kills, this study found that **siltation and turbidity**—rather than acidity—were the dominant disruptive forces on the fish fauna. pH remained within a range (5.6–7.8, rarely dropping to 4.0) that did not cause direct toxic fish kills, but heavy siltation from spoil-bank erosion smothered benthic habitat, reduced macroinvertebrate food organisms by ~90%, and caused a longitudinal "reverse succession" of fish species retreating downstream or being extirpated. The creek chub (*[[Semotilus atromaculatus]]*) emerged as uniquely resistant to siltation due to its surface/terrestrial-feeding habits.

## Background & Description
This paper is not primarily morphological/taxonomic but does note feeding-habit-linked morphology/ecology relevant to resistance to siltation:

- **[[Semotilus atromaculatus]]** (Creek Chub): Gut content analysis of 243 individuals from Leatherwood Creek showed:
  - 30–70% of guts examined were empty
  - Non-empty guts contained primarily **terrestrial dipterans, coleopterans, adult trichopterans**, and a few mayfly larvae
  - Classified functionally as a **surface-feeding fish**, distinguishing it from bottom-feeding species like *Campostoma*, *Etheostoma*, and *Percina*, which rely on benthic food sources vulnerable to siltation.

- Bottom-feeding taxa (*[[Campostoma Anomalum]]*, *[[Etheostoma]]* spp., *[[Percina]]* spp.) were disproportionately affected because their food supply (benthic invertebrates) was eliminated by silt smothering.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
**Habitat type:** Order I–II headwater streams, Lee Formation, Appalachian Plateau, Breathitt County, KY.

- **[[Leatherwood Creek]]**: Tributary of Quicksand Creek; 3.5 miles long; drains ~4 sq. mi.; mining commenced Aug 15, 1967, ceased Dec 17, 1968. Fish fauna at its mouth was already altered by earlier stripping in Quicksand Creek headwaters (pre-1967).
- **[[Bear Branch]]**: Tributary of Buckhorn Creek; 2 miles long; drains ~2.5 sq. mi.; served as an initial unpolluted control; mining began mid-August 1969 (with silt-trapping dams/roads constructed as early as May 1969).
- Both streams are segments of the **North Fork Kentucky River** system.

**Water chemistry parameters monitored:** specific conductivity, suspended sediment, turbidity (Jackson units), Fe²⁺, total Fe, SO₄, Al, Mg, Mn, Ca, Zn, total alkalinity, bicarbonates, pH.

- **pH range:** 5.6–7.8 (occasional dip to 4.0); no observed fish kills attributable directly to acidity — contrasts with prior studies (e.g., [[Beaver Creek]], [[Cane Creek]]) where acid-mine water eliminated fish entirely.
- **Sulfate levels:** Baseline regional level <100 ppm even during heavy rain; contaminated waters elsewhere reported up to 832 ppm (Biesecker & George 1966). Leatherwood mean ≈18.6 ppm vs. Bear Branch mean ≈10.0 ppm (pre-mining).
- **Turbidity:** Rose from ~30 Jackson units to nearly 400 in Leatherwood Creek post-mining; silt loads exceeded 3,000 ppm during peak turbidity events.
- **Substrate impact:** Stream bottoms covered 2–6 inches deep with clay; gravel/rocks cemented to bottom; salamanders (*[[Eurycea Bislineata]]*, *[[Desmognathus]]*) found entombed beneath rocks.
- **Benthic community impact:** Mayflies (Ephemeroptera) and crayfish (decapod crustaceans) reduced by ~90%.

**Community structure — longitudinal succession:**
- Fish species richness/abundance increased from headwaters to mouth (consistent with [[Kuehne 1962]] findings in nearby Buckhorn Creek).
- **Bear Branch (pre-mining control):** 9 genera, 17 species; ~15.4 lb fish/acre (Oct 1968) in lower reaches.
- **Leatherwood Creek (mined):** 7 genera, 12 species; ~5.8 lb fish/acre (Oct 1968) in lower reaches — reduced species richness attributed to earlier upstream (Quicksand Creek) mining effects predating the study.
- **Bear Branch post-mining onset (Sept 1969):** Biomass dropped to 8.6 lb/acre.
- Comparative severity: less dramatic than [[Goose Creek]] (Turner 1958: 61.3 lb/acre unpolluted vs. 5.38 lb/acre polluted) or total elimination cases in [[Beaver Creek]] (Sheridan 1966) and [[Cane Creek]] (Smith 1964), likely due to lower sulfur content in the local overburden limiting acid drainage severity.

**Species documented across six sampling stations included:**
*[[Semotilus Atromaculatus]]*, *[[Campostoma Anomalum]]*, *[[Erimyzon Buccata]]*, *[[Notropis Ardens]]*, *[[Notropis Chrysocephalus]]*, *[[Notropis Photogenis]]*, *[[Notropis Volucellus]]*, *[[Pimephales Notatus]]*, *[[Hybopsis Micropogon]]*, *[[Hypentelium Nigricans]]*, *[[Etheostoma Nigrum]]*, *[[Etheostoma Variatum]]*, and the rare/endangered *[[Etheostoma Sagitta]]*.

## Key Findings
1. **Siltation, not acidity, was the primary driver of fish community decline** — pH stayed largely above lethal thresholds (>4.0), diverging from earlier acid-mine drainage studies that emphasized direct chemical toxicity.
2. **Turbidity increased ~13-fold** (from ~30 to ~400 Jackson units) in the actively mined Leatherwood Creek, with peak silt loads over 3,000 ppm during high-water events.
3. **Benthic food base collapsed:** Ephemeroptera and decapod crustaceans declined by ~90% within one year of mining onset, removing food resources for bottom-feeding fish.
4. **Reverse longitudinal succession:** Fish decline began upstream and progressed downstream; species were either forced to emigrate downstream or were extirpated locally — the opposite pattern of natural species accumulation toward stream mouths.
5. **Reproductive failure observed:** Gravid females and ripe males were collected, but no young fish were found in Leatherwood Creek during later visits — suggesting either curtailed spawning behavior or smothering of eggs/fry by silt.
6. **[[Semotilus Atromaculatus]] showed marked resistance** to siltation/turbidity, attributed to its surface- and terrestrial-insect-feeding strategy, consistent with findings by [[Smith 1964]] that this species is typically the last (though eventually) eliminated under acid-mine pollution.
7. **Conservation concern:** The rare species *[[Etheostoma Sagitta]]* was present in the study streams; continued siltation pressure raises risk of extirpation for such range-restricted taxa.
8. **Regional context:** Comparable Kentucky strip-mining studies ([[Goose Creek]], [[Beaver Creek]], [[Cane Creek]]) showed more severe or total fish elimination, but those systems were larger and more acid-affected; the present study's streams were buffered by lower sulfur content in overburden, isolating siltation as a distinguishable, independently damaging mechanism.
9. Study was part of a planned **5-year monitoring project** (this report covers only the first 17 months), intended to track long-term recovery potential — authors expressed pessimism given intensifying regional mining activity.
