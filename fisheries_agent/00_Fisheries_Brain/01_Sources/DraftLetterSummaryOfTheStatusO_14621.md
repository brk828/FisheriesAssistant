# Cui-ui Recovery Team Letter to U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (December 1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This letter from the [[Cui-Ui Recovery Team]] Leader Thomas A. Strekal to [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Regional Director Marvin Plenert (dated December 7, 1990) summarizes the status of the Draft [[Cui-Ui Recovery Plan]] and the Team's key recommendations. The Team concludes that recovery (delisting) of [[Cui-Ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) is possible if inflow from the [[Truckee River]] to [[Pyramid Lake]] can be increased. Because reduced [[Newlands Project]] demand alone is unlikely to deliver enough water, the Team urges immediate initiation of a water-right purchase program under the [[Truckee-Carson-Pyramid Lake Water Settlement Act]], coordinated with the [[Lahontan Valley Wetlands]] purchase program.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Cui-Ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), a listed large catostomid (sucker) endemic to Pyramid Lake and the lower Truckee River system.
- **Governing legislation:** The [[Truckee-Carson-Pyramid Lake Water Settlement Act]] (P.L. 101-618) mandates a Cui-ui Recovery Plan and also identifies a [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]] Recovery Plan.
- **Team status:** The [[Cui-Ui Recovery Team]] met at the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Reno Office on November 29-30, 1990. The Draft Recovery Plan, originally due January 31, 1991, was delayed by the federal budget process and a more complex [[Stochastic Hydrology]] study using the [[Cui-Ui Population Index Model]]; completion was expected by early spring 1991.
- **Collaborators:** Dr. John Emlen ([[National Fishery Research Center]], Seattle) for modeling; Dr. Peter Brussard ([[University of Nevada, Reno]]) for genetics under contract.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow regime:** Recovery depends on lower [[Truckee River]] flow. Newlands Project diversions at [[Derby Dam]] are expected to fall, which should increase flows, but the timing and amount of benefit are unspecified and depend on the [[Operating Criteria and Procedures]] (OCAP).
- **California depletions:** Increases specified in the Interstate Allocation section of the Act will reduce potential benefits to listed fish.
- **[[Truckee River Operating Agreement]]:** Expected to improve the timing of lower river flows but may slightly reduce total volume.
- **Water quality:** Truckee River water quality standards must be met, which requires reducing nutrient loads. The planned [[Dodge Flats Rapid Infiltration]] land application project for [[Reno-Sparks Wastewater]] effluent was postponed pending transfer of water resource management authority from the cities to [[Washoe County]], which could delay water quality solutions.
- **Spawning habitat:** Cui-ui egg survival is favored by well-sorted, oxygenated gravels and cool water temperatures (below 63°F). A [[Army Corps Of Engineers]] reconnaissance study, authorized under P.L. 101-618 and the Water Resource Act of 1990, will address rehabilitation of the lower Truckee River and passage facilities for cui-ui and Lahontan cutthroat trout around the delta.
- **Fish passage:** [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] and [[Pyramid Lake Fishway]] require adequate operation and maintenance funding to allow upstream spawning migration, and their efficacy should be reassessed as lake and river conditions change.

## Key Findings

1. **Recovery is feasible, but not through reduced diversions alone.** Computer modeling indicates delisting is possible if Truckee River inflow to Pyramid Lake increases. Reclassification to "threatened" may be achievable through relative increases in lower river flow, but full recovery likely requires a firm additional water source.
2. **Water acquisition is urgent.** The Team estimates that tens of thousands of acre-feet may need to be acquired over the next several years. A water-right purchase program should begin immediately.
3. **Coordinated purchases to avoid conflict.** Wetlands water rights should be bought from lands receiving Carson River water (e.g., Carson Division), and Pyramid Lake rights from lands receiving Truckee River water (e.g., Truckee Division). The environmental document should analyze both programs concurrently.
4. **Genetic integrity confirmed.** No evidence of hybridization between cui-ui and [[Tahoe Sucker]] was found.
5. **Bottleneck study proposed.** A proposed study of heterozygosity in three groups (the 1950 and 1969 year classes and the [[Big Canyon Spawning Group]]) would test for a recent reproductive bottleneck. It could be completed by summer 1991 at an estimated cost of $3,000 and would influence fish passage and hatchery management.
6. **Mitochondrial DNA option.** Reproductive fluids can be sampled from spawners non-lethally. An amendment to a [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] contract (Salt Lake City) on Colorado River fishes could add cui-ui analysis.
7. **Hatchery role.** The [[Cui-Ui Hatchery]] would maintain genetic stock against catastrophes (toxic spills, extended drought), improve stock quality, support research, and guide selective breeding.
8. **Continued research needs.** Further work on population dynamics is needed to refine life-stage mortality factors, improve the Truckee-Carson Hydrologic Model, and refine the Cui-ui Population Index Model.
9. **Ancillary benefits for Lahontan cutthroat trout.** Many cui-ui recommendations could also benefit the Pyramid Lake Lahontan cutthroat trout population, though no Lahontan Cutthroat Trout Recovery Team currently exists.
10. **Summary conditions for recovery.** Recovery is possible if natural recruitment continues, genetic variability is maintained, lower Truckee River water quality standards are met, and sufficient water is secured through diversion reductions and purchases with delivery. In the absence of proven remedies, the Team recommends immediately developing a water-right purchase program alongside the wetlands program.
