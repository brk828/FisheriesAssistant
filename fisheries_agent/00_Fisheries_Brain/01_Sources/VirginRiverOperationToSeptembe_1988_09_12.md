## Executive Summary

This field note documents a rotenone eradication/survey operation conducted on a 6.4 km reach of the [[Virgin River]] (Washington Fields Diversion to Washington Fields Bridge) in September 1988. The primary objective appears to have been the removal/sampling of native and introduced fish fauna, with particular focus on the endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*). The operation revealed distinct spatial zonation of fish assemblages correlated with channel morphology (riffle vs. pool vs. braided sand channel), with native cyprinids and catostomids dominating upstream reaches while introduced [[Red Shiner]] and centrarchids/ictalurids increased in relative abundance downstream. A striking absence of aquatic invertebrates was noted, attributed to recent flash flooding.

## Background & Description

Specific morphological observations were recorded for several native taxa during the pickup phase:

- **[[Woundfin]]**: Large adults (second- and third-summer) were noted as tuberculate, indicating breeding condition. At least three distinct size/age classes were identified, including rare very large adults potentially in their fourth summer.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]**: Two morphotypes noted — a "swift-water form" (elongated body) in the uppermost reach near the diversion, and a "stubby-bodied form" observed in middle and lower reaches (km 2.0–5.5).
- **[[Flannelmouth Sucker]]**: A 20 cm adult was described in detail — lateral band suffused with pink, dorsum and sides flecked in sunlight (compared to [[Colorado Pikeminnow|Squawfish]]), white belly, sandy gray-to-brown dorsum. Noted as more streamlined than conspecifics in the Green or Yampa rivers.
- **[[Red Shiner]]**: Large adults observed in breeding condition — tuberculate, brightly colored, and expressing milt/eggs ("stripping") with slight pressure. Subadults were not in breeding condition.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitat use and spatial distribution varied strongly by reach and microhabitat:

- **Upper Reach (0–1.5 km below diversion)**: Dominated by adult [[Woundfin]] concentrations, likely a breeding aggregation. Substrate was fine "sugar sand" with fecal organic content (H₂S odor detected), interspersed with rocky riffles at ~0.5 km intervals.
- **Middle Reach (km 2.0–4.0)**: Pools associated with rocky substrate favored larger [[Desert Sucker]] individuals. Adult [[Roundtail Chub]] were restricted to pools and undercut banks with overhanging vegetation, while juveniles were more broadly distributed in pool habitat. [[Woundfin]] here were predominantly young-of-the-year.
- **Canyon-bound Reach (km 4.5–5.5)**: Bedrock and boulder-associated pools (2–4 m wide, 10–20 m long, up to 1 m deep) supported dense aggregations of adult [[Roundtail Chub]] (10–40 individuals/pool) and [[Desert Sucker]] (5–15/pool), with [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] rare (1–3/pool).
- **Lower Braided Reach (km 3.5–6.4, upper portion described)**: Broad, shallow, sandy, braided channel with minimal cover/pool development, dominated numerically by young-of-the-year [[Red Shiner]] and [[Woundfin]].
- **Riparian corridor**: Cottonwood, willow, and tamarisk dominated the first kilometer downstream of the diversion; tamarisk became increasingly dominant further downstream, with occasional willow and isolated cottonwood stands.
- **Invertebrate community**: Depauperate overall — helgrammites (*Corydalus*) common; naucorid bugs (*Ambrysus*), Gomphidae dragonfly naiads, agrionid damselfly naiads, and baetid mayflies were rare; one crayfish observed. This scarcity was attributed to recent flash flooding events.
- **Scavengers**: Ravens began feeding heavily on dead/dying fish by ~3:00 pm, notably later in the day than typically expected; no other depredation (by other taxa) was observed.
- **No amphibians or reptiles** were directly observed, though probable softshell turtle tracks were noted ~2 km below the diversion.

## Key Findings

- **Relative abundance shifts downstream**: Native species proportions shifted markedly along the 6.4 km reach — [[Woundfin]] dominance in the uppermost reach gave way to increasing dominance by introduced [[Red Shiner]] (up to 30–60% of sampled fish) in braided, unshaded downstream sections.
- **Species composition near diversion**: [[Red Shiner]] rare (1–2%), [[Roundtail Chub]] <1%, [[Speckled Dace]] infrequent (1–2%, no large adults), suckers (mostly [[Desert Sucker]]) <1–2%, [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] rare with no adults observed.
- **Mid-reach shifts (km 2–4)**: [[Red Shiner]] rose sharply to 20–30% of fish (large breeding adults present); juvenile [[Roundtail Chub]] ~4–5%; [[Woundfin]] (mostly YOY) ~40%; [[Speckled Dace]] 5–10%.
- **Bottom counts in canyon reach (km 4.5–5.5)**: Sample of ~300 fish showed 30–50% [[Red Shiner]], 20–30% YOY [[Woundfin]], 5–10% [[Mountain Sucker]], 3–10% [[Speckled Dace]], 1–5% YOY [[Roundtail Chub]], plus minor introduced species presence.
- **Braided lower channel counts**: [[Woundfin]] 15–30%, [[Red Shiner]] (mostly YOY) 30–60%, [[Speckled Dace]] ~5%, juvenile [[Desert Sucker]] 5–10%, [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] YOY trace.
- **Introduced species presence**: Consistently minor across all reaches — [[Channel Catfish]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]] (possible single individual), and [[Yellow Bullhead]]/[[Black Bullhead]], mostly as young-of-the-year.
- **Single [[Virgin Spinedace]]** individual noted and frozen for further study — indicating rarity or marginal habitat presence in this reach.
- **Water withdrawal impacts**: Approximately 25 cfs withdrawn via the south-side irrigation intake, with an additional unauthorized 1–2 cfs intake on the north side, substantially reducing downstream flow (<3–4 cfs at Washington Fields Bridge) and likely concentrating fish populations in remaining wetted channel and pools.
- **Beaver activity**: Deep, water-filled bank dens excavated by beaver were noted at a minimum of three locations along the reach, representing rare refugial habitat amid otherwise reduced flow conditions.
- **Operational note**: Rotenone application began ~9:30 am at the diversion; fish distress was evident by 10:00 am; the toxicant front did not reach Washington Fields Bridge (6.4 km downstream) until approximately dark (~7:00 pm), indicating a slow diffusion/travel rate consistent with very low discharge.
