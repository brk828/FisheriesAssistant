# Saco River fishery management

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #AtlanticSalmon #FisheryManagement #RiverEcology

## Executive Summary

This 1956 report by the [[Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Game]] and the [[Atlantic Salmon Commission]] presents a comprehensive drainage-wide inventory of the [[Saco River]] system, conducted 1954–55 by Regional Fishery Biologist [[Stuart Deroche]]. The study evaluates the feasibility of restoring [[Atlantic Salmon]] and other anadromous fish migrations through a river system heavily modified by hydroelectric development. The analysis concludes that habitat conditions — including 21 dams (only 2 with fishways), severe pollution near [[Biddeford]]-[[Saco, Maine|Saco]], critically high summer water temperatures, and heavy competition from warmwater fish — make large-scale salmon restoration **impractical**. The report recommends managing the lower river (below [[Bonney Eagle Dam]]) as warmwater game fish habitat and the upper river as limited coldwater trout fishery, with the [[Ossipee River]] noted for good bass fishing.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose**: Part of a state-wide study of obstructions to fish migration intended to inform restoration and management decisions for Maine's rivers.
- **Methodology**: Field survey (1954–55) measuring physical/biological characteristics governing fish production potential:
  1. Availability/extent of spawning and nursery habitat
  2. Number/height of migration obstructions
  3. Industrial and domestic pollution loads
  4. Economic feasibility of fishway construction and pollution abatement
- **River overview**: The Saco River is used primarily for **hydroelectric power generation**, with secondary uses including domestic/industrial waste disposal and recreation.
- **Drainage scale**: 67 miles of main river from [[Swan Falls Dam|Swan Falls]] (Fryeburg) to Biddeford; total drainage and tributaries measured at 150.25 miles.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Obstructions
- **21 dams** total obstruct fish passage in the drainage: 11 on the main river ([[Biddeford]] to [[Fryeburg]]), 10 on tributaries.
- Only **2 dams have fishways**: [[Upper York Dam]] (Biddeford) and [[Skelton Dam]] (Union Falls) — both require modification for efficient fish passage.

- **Tributary obstructions** (Little Ossipee River, Ossipee River, Swan Pond Creek, Cooks Brook, Hancock Brook): Multiple sawmill and storage dams; most tributary spawning areas deemed poor or inaccessible without fishway construction; fishway construction **not recommended** at Kezar Falls, Swan Pond Creek, or Cooks Brook dams due to limited value.

### Pollution
Data sourced from the "Gold Book" (New England-New York Inter-Agency Committee).

- **Domestic pollution**: 15 municipal sewage sources, contributing population ~22,100 persons; no significant industrial waste interception.
- **Industrial pollution**: 9 plants (machinery, food processing, textiles, paper, leather) with organic waste population-equivalent of ~8,300 persons; textile machinery manufacturer contributes primarily inorganic waste.
- **Worst impact zone**: Last 5 miles of river (Biddeford-Saco) rated **Condition IV** (suitable only for waste transport, power, navigation — not recreation or fish habitat).
- Tannery waste at Bar Mills creates localized problems during low flow/high temperature periods, acting as an additional migration obstruction near fishway entrances.
- High spring/fall flows during peak salmon migration periods may dilute pollutants enough to allow passage, but juvenile outmigrants would also need to traverse this polluted zone.

### Water Temperature
- Main river summer temperatures ranged **73°F–84°F**; from Hiram Falls downstream, lowest recorded temperature was **78°F** — critically high for salmonid survival.
- **Skelton Flowage** (largest impoundment, Union Falls): surface-to-72ft profile showed **82°F–77°F** with no cold-water refuge; oxygen deficiency below 50 feet.
- Ossipee and Little Ossipee Rivers showed comparatively better temperatures: **64°F–76°F**.

### Competition from Other Fish Species
- [[Chain Pickerel]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], and [[White Perch]] thrive from [[Bonney Eagle Dam]] to Biddeford, acting as serious predators/competitors against coldwater juveniles.
- [[Brown Trout]] already established above Bonney Eagle Dam, competing directly with [[Atlantic Salmon]] and [[Rainbow Trout]] for limited spawning/nursery habitat.
- Pickerel and bass also present in Ossipee/Little Ossipee Rivers, though competitive pressure is lessened by more favorable habitat there.

## Key Findings

### Spawning & Nursery Habitat (1954–55 Inventory)
- Main river (Swan Falls to Biddeford, 67 mi): only **4.71 miles total** of spawning/nursery habitat (3.28 mi good, 0.45 mi fair, 0.98 mi poor) — remaining 62 miles of little value for anadromous fish production.
- Of the 4.71 miles of usable habitat, **3.8 miles lie above [[Bonney Eagle Dam]]**, requiring migrating fish to pass 6–7 obstructions.
- **Ossipee River** and **Little Ossipee River** are the only tributaries offering good spawning/nursery habitat, but require passage of 6–7 main-river barriers plus 2 additional tributary obstructions.
- Other tributaries (Shepards River, Little Saco River, Cooks Brook, Swan Pond Creek) offer minimal value — gravel too fine and nursery areas too limited for salmon.
- **Total drainage spawning/nursery area**: 31,125 sq. yards across 150 miles — compared to **846,000 sq. yards** in the 60-mile [[Machias River]], illustrating the Saco's comparatively poor potential.

### Management Conclusions
1. **Atlantic salmon restoration deemed impractical** given the combination of:
   - High fishway construction costs relative to limited habitat gains
   - Unresolved pollution abatement costs
   - Critically high summer temperatures throughout the main river
   - Established competition from warmwater fish and brown trout
2. **Recommended management zones**:
   - **Below [[Bonney Eagle Dam]]**: Manage as warmwater game fish habitat (pickerel, bass, perch).
   - **Above Bonney Eagle Dam**: Continue limited coldwater trout fishery.
   - **[[Ossipee River]]**: Maintain/promote as good summer bass fishery.
