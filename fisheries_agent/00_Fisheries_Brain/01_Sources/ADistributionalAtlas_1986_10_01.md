# A Distributional Atlas of Kentucky Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #ichthyofauna #kentucky-fishes

## Executive Summary
This entry summarizes a 1986 announcement for *[[A Distributional Atlas of Kentucky Fishes]]*, authored by [[Brooks M. Burr]] and [[Melvin L. Warren Jr.]] and published by the [[Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission]] as part of its Scientific and Technical Series (No. 4). The atlas is a comprehensive statewide ichthyofaunal reference documenting **242 fish species** historically or currently present in [[Kentucky]]. It combines historical (pre-1920) and contemporary distribution data, taxonomic notes, habitat classification, and conservation status assessments, making it a foundational biogeographic resource for freshwater fish research in the region. The work totals xvi + 398 pages and includes a color frontispiece, extensive bibliography, and cluster analyses of drainage-based faunal affinities.

## Background & Description
The source text is a publication announcement rather than a primary morphological study; it does not itself provide anatomical or morphometric data for individual species. However, it indicates that each of the 242 species accounts within the atlas includes:
- Common and scientific nomenclature
- Discussion of **intraspecific variation** and **evolutionary relationships**
- Documentation of recognized **subspecies** occurring within Kentucky
- Etymological interpretation of scientific names

No specific morphological measurements, meristic counts, or diagnostic characters are presented in this announcement text itself.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The atlas frames Kentucky's ichthyofauna within a habitat-classification and biogeographic framework rather than detailing single-species niches. Key ecological/habitat elements referenced include:
- A **classification and characterization of major ichthyofaunal habitats** within the state (e.g., differing stream orders, drainage types, physiographic provinces)
- A synthesis of Kentucky's **physiography and hydrography**, linking landscape features to fish distribution patterns
- Species-level **habitat descriptions** included in each account
- Discussion of **historical events and environmental conditions** that shaped distributional patterns (e.g., glacial history, drainage connectivity, stream capture events typical of Appalachian/Interior Plateau ichthyogeography)
- **Cluster analysis** used to quantify ichthyofaunal similarity among [[Kentucky Drainages]] and [[Physiographic Provinces]], effectively defining biogeographic "niches" at the drainage-basin scale rather than the individual species scale

This positions the atlas as a study of **regional freshwater fish biogeography** as much as a species-by-species taxonomic reference.

## Key Findings
- The atlas documents **242 fish species** known from Kentucky, past and present, each with an accompanying distribution map.
- Distribution maps are **temporally split**, contrasting distributions **prior to 1920** with **present-day (as of the mid-1980s)** distributions — enabling analysis of range contraction, expansion, or extirpation over roughly six decades.
- Each species map includes an **inset of native North American range**, situating Kentucky populations within continental-scale distribution context.
- **Conservation status categories** are assigned to species where appropriate, indicating early formal use of state-level imperilment assessment for fishes in [[Kentucky]].
- **Brief family-level accounts** precede individual species accounts, suggesting a taxonomically organized structure (Order > Family > Species).
- A concluding synthesis chapter:
  - Summarizes overall **fish faunal composition** of the state
  - Interprets distributional patterns via **historical biogeography** and **environmental determinism**
  - Applies **cluster analysis** to quantify similarity among Kentucky's river drainages and physiographic provinces, a method characteristic of community ichthyofaunal biogeography studies of the era
- The work is positioned as a major reference text, evidenced by its length (**398 pages**), comprehensive **bibliography**, and detailed table of contents.
- Published under the **Kentucky Nature Preserves Commission Scientific and Technical Series No. 4** (1986), priced at $10.00 at release — indicative of a state-agency-sponsored conservation and research document rather than a commercial academic monograph.
