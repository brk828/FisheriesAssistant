# Concurrence Letter on Erosion Control Measures at Campbell Blue Creek, U.S. Highway 191

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology #ESA-consultation

## Executive Summary

This document is a **Section 7 Endangered Species Act concurrence letter** from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) responding to the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests' request for concurrence on a "not likely to adversely affect" determination for erosion control construction on **Campbell Blue Creek** at the U.S. Highway 191 crossing (Greenlee County, Arizona). The primary aquatic species of concern is the threatened **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*), whose designated critical habitat overlaps with the project area. The letter is not a primary research paper but synthesizes findings from a Biological Assessment/Evaluation (BA/E) and Environmental Assessment (EA) to determine sediment and habitat-modification risk to the species. FWS concurred that impacts to loach minnow would be **insignificant and discountable**, alongside similar findings for the American peregrine falcon and Mexican spotted owl.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type:** Riffle-dependent stream reaches; the action area is a stream crossing subject to construction-related channel and sediment disturbance.
- **Location:** Campbell Blue Creek, a stream within the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests (Alpine Ranger District), Greenlee County, Arizona — part of the broader [[Gila River Basin]] system where loach minnow critical habitat is designated.
- **Critical habitat:** The letter explicitly references "critical habitat" for the loach minnow near the project site, indicating this stream segment falls under federally designated protections.
- **Hydrological sensitivity:** The species' habitat is characterized by sensitivity to **sediment loading** and **channel/flow alteration**, both cited as primary mechanisms of potential effect from the proposed construction.
- **Co-occurring listed species:** The action area also supports habitat considerations for the [[American Peregrine Falcon]] and [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], indicating a riparian/canyon ecosystem context, though these are terrestrial/avian species not directly tied to the aquatic niche.

## Key Findings

- **Determination:** The proposed erosion control project is "**not likely to adversely affect**" the threatened [[Loach Minnow]], consistent with findings for the peregrine falcon and Mexican spotted owl.
- **Project scope:** Construction extends approximately 75–95 feet along Campbell Blue Creek, involving excavation of ~330 tons of material, replacement with sediment control fabric and rock, and capping/reseeding with native vegetation.
- **Sediment impact assessment:**
  - Downstream sediment movement into loach minnow critical habitat was judged **insignificant** — defined as "so low it cannot be meaningfully measured, detected, or evaluated."
  - Sediment risk would be further reduced if construction is timed during periods of **no surface flow** in Campbell Blue Creek.
- **Channel modification risk:**
  - Potential exists for **long-term or cumulative effects** via radiating channel changes following bank modification.
  - However, the presence of an **artificially confined and controlled channel** at the road crossing was determined to prevent downstream channel changes.
  - Upstream channel changes affecting loach minnow via altered flows/sediment loads were deemed **discountable** — "extremely unlikely to occur."
- **Regulatory framework:** The consultation satisfies Section 7 ESA compliance for two federal actions concurrently — the Forest Service's construction authorization (lead agency) and the Army Corps of Engineers' Section 404 Clean Water Act permit.
- **Broader "no effect" findings:** The letter also concurs with "no effect" determinations for the [[Bald Eagle]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Mexican Gray Wolf]], [[Black-Footed Ferret]], and proposed endangered [[Jaguar]], though none of these pertain directly to aquatic/fish ecology.
