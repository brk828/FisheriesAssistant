## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 internal memorandum from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) to W.L. Minckley, summarizing a telephone consultation with U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) personnel (George Divine, Jan Harp) regarding renewal of a **Federal Endangered/Threatened Species Permit**. The memo does not present original morphological, physiological, or ecological data; rather, it clarifies **regulatory and administrative requirements** for researchers working with federally listed [[Threatened Fish Species]] and [[Endangered Fish Species]] in Arizona, New Mexico, and Mexico. It is nonetheless valuable as a primary-source record of the legal/conservation status and jurisdictional handling of several imperiled southwestern U.S. fish taxa during the early-to-mid 1990s, a period of active recovery planning for the [[Gila River Basin]] and [[Yaqui River Basin]] ichthyofauna.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source document. ## Ecological & Environmental Context

While explicit habitat descriptions are absent, the document implicitly situates these species within threatened freshwater stream ecosystems of the arid American Southwest and adjacent Mexico:

- **Geographic/Jurisdictional Scope**: Species distributions span Arizona, New Mexico, and Mexico (Sonora, Chihuahua), consistent with headwater and mainstem stream habitats of the [[Gila River]], [[Little Colorado River]], and [[Yaqui River]] systems.
- **Conservation Context**: All eight named species are classified as **federally threatened** under a **Special Rule** (Section 4(d) of the Endangered Species Act), which permits state-level (rather than federal-level) regulatory jurisdiction for most research/management activities — a status reflecting their occurrence in fragmented, human-impacted desert stream habitats typical of [[Southwestern Desert Fishes]].
- **Refugium/Captive Populations**: The memo notes that captive **refugium stocks** derived from wild-caught individuals remain a recognized ex-situ conservation tool, implying these species are subject to active captive propagation and reintroduction efforts due to wild population declines — a hallmark ecological/management feature of endangered [[Cyprinid Fishes]] and [[Ictalurid Catfishes]] in the region.

## Key Findings

- **Permit Authority Clarified**: Only the FWS permit application packet accompanying ARD Young's letter (21 Sept. 1994) is valid; prior guidance documents (Hansen, Rogers, Fjetland memos) are to be disregarded.
- **Institutional vs. Individual Permits**: Multiple co-investigators (Douglas, Dowling, Hedrick, Marsh, Minckley) and their supervised students/staff may operate under a single **"institutional" permit**, provided one signatory (e.g., Collins) holds fiduciary responsibility for all collective research activities. The alternative is individual permits per researcher.
- **Federal Permit Requirement — General Rule**: A federal permit is required for **any** activity involving federally listed threatened/endangered fish — not only "take" — including maintenance of tissue samples for genetic research.
- **Special Rule Exemption — Eight Species**: The following federally threatened species require only **state-level compliance** (AZ, NM, Mexico) and are **exempt from federal permitting**:
  - [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*)
  - [[Beautiful Shiner]] (*Cyprinella formosa*)
  - [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*)
  - [[Little Colorado Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
  - [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
  - [[Sonora Chub]] (*Gila ditaenia*)
  - [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*)
  - [[Yaqui Catfish]] (*Ictalurus pricei*)
  
  A federal permit **is** still required for other threatened species not covered under this special rule.
- **Grandfathering of Existing Stocks**: Refugium and captive research populations originally collected under a now-expired federal permit remain legally covered by that original permit's authority — applying to live specimens, offspring, and preserved/frozen material. A new federal permit is only needed to add **new** material collected from wild populations.
- **Application Procedure**: Completion of **Form 3-200** is required, with the front documenting the institutional nature of the application and the back requiring individualized statements (items 1–5) from each co-permittee, including full listing of supervised students and staff by name.
- **Regulatory Contacts**: Key FWS Regional Office (Albuquerque, NM) contacts identified for permit inquiries: George Divine and Jan Harp (505/766-2914).
