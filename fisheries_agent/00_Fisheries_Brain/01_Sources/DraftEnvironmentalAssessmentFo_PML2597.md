# Redfield Canyon Fish Barrier: Finding of No Significant Impact and Environmental Assessment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Phoenix Area Office) prepared a Draft and Final Environmental Assessment (EA) and a Finding of No Significant Impact (FONSI) for a proposed concrete [[Fish Barrier]] on Redfield Canyon Creek, Graham County, Arizona. The barrier would block upstream invasion of nonnative fishes from the [[San Pedro River]] into a reach that holds native fishes, including federally listed species repatriated since 2007. Reclamation determined the project is not a major Federal action significantly affecting the human environment. The project is required under a 2008 [[Biological Opinion]] for [[Central Arizona Project]] water deliveries. Seven public comment letters were received; they generally supported the project and recommended measures to reduce construction disturbance.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** Four-section reinforced concrete barrier plugging gaps between three 15–20 ft boulders and the canyon walls, each section with a 4-ft drop onto a sloped apron. Sections are 2, 4, 10 and 21 ft wide, with buried scour walls about 10 ft deep in the alluvium.
- **Design flood:** Built to withstand a 100-year flood. Estimated peak flows at the site range from 853 cfs (2-year) to 7,106 cfs (100-year).
- **Land acquisition:** A 5.97–6-acre parcel of [[Arizona State Land Department]] trust land, acquired by condemnation (preferred) or relinquishment.
- **Construction:** About 6 weeks, by a crew of 4–5 workers. Materials are airlifted by [[Helicopter Transport]] or moved by an optional temporary cable tram. Staging covers about 0.25 acre on the south rim.
- **Livestock ramp:** Built on the right abutment to keep grazing access open for leased pastures.
- **Monitoring:** A 5-year fish community monitoring program, with post-flood inspections (5-year flood or greater).
- **Setting:** Redfield Canyon Creek is a tributary of the San Pedro River, roughly 24 miles long, with a 62 sq mi watershed. The proposed site lies about 8.2 linear miles (9.5 stream miles) upstream of the confluence.

Background: native Gila River Basin fishes have declined sharply. Of 21 native fish species, 11 are federally listed, two are candidates, and one is recently extinct. Nonnative predators and habitat fragmentation are the main drivers, and barriers are considered the only feasible long-term tool to segregate imperiled fishes from invaders.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Fish assemblage.** The native fish assemblage includes [[Speckled Dace]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]] and [[Gila Chub]]. [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Desert Pupfish]] were repatriated between 2007 and 2010; reproduction and recruitment were reported for some of these. Only one nonnative species, [[Green Sunfish]], shows a small surviving population, and it is under active mechanical removal. Other nonnatives recorded are fathead minnow (1998) and black bullhead (1998).

**Habitat.** The site sits in an intermittent reach, well downstream of the perennial reach, so fishes reach the barrier area only seasonally. Vegetation is Sonoran Desertscrub and Semidesert Grassland on the uplands, with a narrow Sonoran Riparian Deciduous Forest and Woodland corridor along the stream. Redfield Canyon lies within the [[Muleshoe Cooperative Management Area]], and parts of it are designated wilderness.

**Water and soils.** The stream is intermittent, with a mean flow of 3.9 cfs in perennial reaches. ADEQ's 2006/2008 assessment reported no water quality exceedances, though it was inconclusive due to limited sampling. The site lies in the Basin and Range province, with tuff escarpments and coarse fluvial deposits.

**Wildlife and listed species.** Riparian habitat supports much of Arizona's wildlife. The project would affect the following:

- Riparian vegetation: about 1.13 acres (permanent <0.01 acre; temporary about 1.12 acres), mitigated at a 10:1 ratio at the [[3 Links Farm Mitigation Bank]].
- Federally listed species assessed in the EA: [[Lesser Long-Nosed Bat]], [[Mexican Spotted Owl]] and [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] would have no effect. Gila chub, spikedace, loach minnow, Gila topminnow and desert pupfish were assessed for potential direct mortality during construction and long-term benefit from excluding nonnatives.
- Special status species include the [[Western Yellow-Billed Cuckoo]], [[Sonoran Desert Tortoise]], [[Lowland Leopard Frog]], [[Arizona Toad]] and [[Canyon Spotted Whiptail]], among others.

**Cultural resources.** Two Class III surveys (May 14, 2009; November 4, 2010) found no cultural resources. The [[Arizona State Historic Preservation Office]] concurred with a no-effect finding for the 2009 survey. Nine tribes were included in the combined NEPA/NHPA consultation. The tribes did not comment on trust assets.

**Air, hazardous materials and justice.** The area is in attainment for all NAAQS. Class I Galiuro Wilderness is about 2.3 miles away. Construction emissions are minor and short-term. Hazardous material management follows federal and state rules. No minority or low-income communities are present.

## Key Findings

- **Decision:** Reclamation found no significant effects and issued a FONSI. The Area Manager approves funding for construction and land acquisition.
- **Purpose:** To satisfy a key conservation measure of the 2008 BO, protecting Gila chub and Chiricahua leopard frog populations and enabling [[Repatriation]] of spikedace and loach minnow.
- **Alternatives:** Only the proposed action and no-action alternative were analyzed in detail. The proposed site was chosen as the most cost-effective and least damaging, since other sites were wider.
- **Fish benefits:** The barrier is expected to have substantial, positive benefits by preventing upstream nonnative invasion. It may slightly reduce gene flow, but only during flood flows, and effects are judged minimal.
- **Drift:** Losses from larval drift and flood transport over the barrier are expected to be negligible.
- **Geomorphic effects:** Gradient reduction of about 0.2 percent; sediment aggradation of about 840 ft upstream; scour and deposition changes about 200 ft downstream.
- **Water rights:** No impoundment, so no surface water right application is needed. No measurable effect on downstream water or groundwater.
- **Comments:** Seven letters and emails were received during the June 27, 2011 comment period. Reclamation's responses are in Appendix C of the final EA. A fishery scientist with over 30 years of experience supported the project and foresaw no negative consequences.
- **Regulatory compliance:** CWA Section 404 and 401 permits were issued. Section 402 coverage is needed before construction. The project is consistent with the ESA, NEPA, NHPA and the MBTA (fall/winter construction avoids most breeding).
- **Key mitigation:** Tree removal limited to those needed for access; contractor use areas revegetated; halt construction if listed non-fish species are found; ramp built for livestock and wildlife.
