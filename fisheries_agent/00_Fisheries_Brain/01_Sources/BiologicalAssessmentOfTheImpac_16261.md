# Biological Assessment of Hoover Powerplant Modification Impacts on Bonytail Chub and Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #endangered-species #dam-impacts

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation memorandum (October 16, 1981) presents a biological assessment of the proposed [[Hoover Dam]] Powerplant Modification Project's impacts on the endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in accordance with Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The project would increase peak releases from 40,000 ft³/s to 62,000 ft³/s, adding 500 MW generating capacity. The Bureau concludes the project may impact these species and enters formal consultation with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The assessment evaluates changes to water level fluctuations, flow velocities, substrate stability, primary productivity, temperature regimes, and fishery resources in [[Black Canyon]] and [[Lake Mohave]]. The Bureau concludes that no bonytails are present in the cold-water (54°F) Black Canyon section, and that the Lake Mohave population shows no evidence of successful reproduction since 1950.

## Background & Description

### Study Area

The assessment covers two distinct aquatic systems below [[Hoover Dam]]:

- **[[Black Canyon]]**: Cold (54°F), deep, swift river section extending approximately 18 miles downstream from Hoover Dam
- **[[Lake Mohave]] Proper**: Wide basin with typical lake characteristics, extending from approximately mile 18 to [[Davis Dam]]

### Target Species

**[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*):
- Status: Endangered; considered extinct in [[Lake Mead]] and endangered in Lake Mohave
- Historical range greatly declined; only represented by Lake Mohave and Upper [[Colorado River]] ([[Green River]], Utah) populations
- Last documented collection of young: June 15, 1950, at [[Cottonwood Landing]], Nevada
- Optimum habitat (from Upper Colorado River collections): shifting sand substrate, water depths 3-4 feet, moderately swift current
- Feeding behavior: Primarily surface feeder on terrestrial insects and plant debris
- Recent collections: Ripe adults taken throughout Lake Mohave; spawning observed by Jonez and Sumner (1954) over gravel shelf 10 miles below [[Eldorado Canyon]] (36 miles below Hoover Dam)

**[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*):
- Status: Endangered in Lake Mohave
- Spawns in shallow water
- Subject to impacts from lake level fluctuations

### Proposed Project

The [[Hoover Powerplant Modification]] would:
- Increase peak releases from existing 40,000 ft³/s to 62,000 ft³/s
- Add 500 MW generating capacity through three alternatives
- Require 5-year construction period with cofferdam installation
- Alter operational patterns: higher mid-week releases, lower weekend releases (0-2,000 ft³/s)

### Existing Conditions

**Hydrology:**
- Current peak releases: 40,000 ft³/s
- Current minimum releases: 0-2,000 ft³/s
- Daily fluctuations at dam (elevation 647): 10 feet
- Daily fluctuations at [[Willow Beach]]: 0.5 feet

**Water Velocities (existing, Lake Mohave elevation 647):**
- Peak release mean velocity: 1.9 mph
- Peak release maximum velocity: 3.8 mph
- Minimum release mean velocity: 0.1 mph

**Temperature:**
- Releases from hypolimnion of [[Lake Mead]]: constant 54°F year-round
- [[Lake Mead]] thermally stratified during warmer months
- Current operational goal: maintain downstream temperatures within 54-57°F (3°F fluctuation)

**Substrate and Primary Productivity:**
- Rocky substrate in upper 3 miles maintained by periodic high releases (>30,000 ft³/s) that scour sand and gravel
- Armored substrate (rocks >3-5 inches) remains stable and productive
- Dominant periphyton: *Cladophora glomerata* (filamentous algae), diatoms (*Diatoma vulgare*, *Melosira varians*)
- Periphyton most productive in shallow areas (<15 feet) with highest velocities
- Daily and seasonal water level fluctuations expose periphyton to air, reducing overall abundance

**Invertebrate Community:**
- First 8 miles: Very low diversity, dominated by amphipod (*Hyalella*) and chironomid larvae
- Periphyton vital for sustaining herbivorous *Hyalella*
- Beyond 8 miles: Oligochaetes predominate
- Decline of *Hyalella* downstream linked to decreased periphyton productivity

**Fish Community:**
Study of [[Rainbow Trout]] stomach contents at Willow Beach (1971-1976): 60% consumed algae, 57% consumed invertebrates, indicating importance of periphyton-based food web.

### Historical Fish Community Changes

**[[Lake Mead]]:**
- Pre-dam native species: [[Colorado River Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], Bonytail Chub, [[Humpback Sucker]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Carp]]
- 1935-42 introductions: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Bluegill]], [[Green Sunfish]]
- 1954: [[Threadfin Shad]] introduced as forage
- Mid-1960s: Largemouth bass fishery declined
- 1969-present: Cold-water species stocked (rainbow, cutthroat, hybrid bowcutt trout, silver salmon)
- 1969-72: [[Striped Bass]] stocked; reproducing population established

**[[Lake Mohave]]:**
- Post-Hoover Dam: Changed from warm, silt-laden river to cold, clear, swift stream
- 1935-51: 3,714,054 rainbow trout stocked
- 1951: Lake Mohave filled; trout fishery modified, warm-water fishery developed in lower reservoir
- 1955: Threadfin shad introduced
- Recent (spring 1981): Small striped bass reported; source unknown (accidental/intentional introduction or turbine passage)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Aquatic Ecosystem Structure

The [[Black Canyon]] ecosystem exhibits a periphyton-based food web dependent on stable rocky substrate and cold, clear water. Primary productivity is concentrated in the upper 3 miles where:

1. High water velocities maintain armored substrate (rocks >3-5 inches)
2. Stable substrate supports periphyton attachment and growth
3. Periphyton supports herbivorous invertebrates (*Hyalella*, chironomids)
4. Invertebrates provide food for fish (rainbow trout, other species)

Substrate stability is maintained by periodic high releases (>30,000 ft³/s) that flush accumulated sand and gravel from tributary washes, preventing burial of productive rocky areas.

### Thermal Regime and Stratification

**[[Lake Mead]]:**
- Thermal stratification during warm months: warm upper layer over cold deep layer
- Deep water (hypolimnion): constant 54°F year-round
- Upper layer responds to atmospheric temperature changes
- Winter: Fairly uniform temperature top to bottom
- Hoover Dam withdrawals from hypolimnion maintain cold downstream temperatures

**Downstream Effects:**
- Cold releases (54°F) limit species diversity below dam
- Only cold-water adapted species thrive in Black Canyon
- Temperature barrier prevents warm-water species from upper Lake Mohave from colonizing Black Canyon

### Interface Dynamics

The thermal interface between cold Black Canyon water and warm Lake Mohave proper moves upstream when:
- Lake Mohave elevations are high
- Hoover Dam releases are low

Interface location calculated using Paulson and Baker (1980) formula. Interface typically carries debris and can affect water quality.

### Endangered Species Status and Reproduction

**Bonytail Chub Population Concerns:**
- Frequency of observations greatly declined since Davis Dam closure (1951)
- No evidence of successful hatching or fry development in Lake Mohave
- Speculation: Either extremely limited recruitment or no successful spawning; population may consist only of old fish (20+ years)
- Ripe adults successfully spawned in hatchery facilities, indicating physiological capability
- Lack of natural recruitment attributed to: unsuitable spawning habitat, predation by introduced species, or other unknown factors

**Black Canyon Assessment:**
- Last bonytails collected 20 years ago (1961) at Willow Beach
- Extensive scuba surveys by Bureau and [[University of Nevada Las Vegas]] found no bonytails
- Eddy and pool habitat (preferred by bonytails) extremely limited, especially above Willow Beach (mile 12)
- Canyon forms deep channel (30-90 feet) with uniform currents, few back eddies
- Temperature experiments at [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Toney, 1974): 54°F discharge too cold for egg development
- Conclusion: Black Canyon does not support bonytail reproduction

**Lake Mohave Proper:**
- Contains bonytail chubs; ripe individuals collected throughout
- Only recorded spawning: Jonez and Sumner (1954) observed aggregate of 500 individuals over gravel shelf 10 miles below Eldorado Canyon
- Recent collections of ripe bonytails in same area (W. Minckley, [[Arizona State University]])
- Substrate does not appear suitable; no moderately swift current along shore
- Temperatures suitable for spawning
- Inability to sustain population may be due to: lack of spawning habitat, predation by introduced game/exotic fish

### Largemouth Bass Reproduction Study

Five-year study of [[Lake Mead]] largemouth bass population (extrapolated to Lake Mohave):
- 50-58% of nests successful (eggs hatch)
- Nests 0-1 foot below surface: not successful
- Nests 1-2 feet below surface: 25% successful
- Nests below 2 feet: 57% successful

Data used to assess long-term impacts on Lake Mohave fishery from proposed project.

## Key Findings

### Predicted Hydrological Changes

**Water Level Fluctuations (Table 1):**

At Lake Mohave elevation 647:
- Peak releases increase from 40,000 to 62,000 ft³/s
- Daily fluctuation at dam: 10 feet (existing) → 16 feet (Alternatives 1 and 3)
- Daily fluctuation at Willow Beach: 0.5 feet (existing) → 2 feet (Alternatives 1 and 3)

At Lake Mohave elevation 630:
- Daily fluctuation at dam: 24 feet (existing) → 31 feet (Alternatives 1 and 3)
- Daily fluctuation at Willow Beach: 3 feet (existing) → 7 feet (Alternatives 1 and 3)

**Water Velocities (Table 2):**

Peak releases at elevation 647 (Alternatives 1 and 3):
- Mean velocity: 1.9 mph (existing) → 2.8 mph (+0.9 mph increase)
- Maximum velocity: 3.8 mph (existing) → 4.5 mph (+0.7 mph increase)

Peak releases at elevation 630 (Alternatives 1 and 3):
- Mean velocity: 2.5 mph (existing) → 3.6 mph (+1.1 mph increase)
- Maximum velocity: 4.4 mph (existing) → 5.1 mph (+0.7 mph increase)

Minimum release velocities remain unchanged.

**Lake Mohave Operational Pattern (Figure 7):**
- Weekly fluctuations: Rise ~3 feet Monday-Friday, decrease ~2 feet over weekend (first half of year)
- Opposite pattern second half of year: 2-foot rise, 3-foot drop
- Expected to increase bank erosion slightly
- Expected to impact spawning activities of certain fish species

### Construction Period Impacts (5 years)

**Cofferdam Effects:**
- Increase average cross-section velocity from 1.9 mph to 9.2 mph through 500-800 foot section immediately downstream from Arizona transformer deck
- Cause shifting of loose sand, gravel, cobbles, small boulders
- Potential short-term turbidity increase (not expected to approach magnitude of natural flash flood turbidity)
- Periphyton disturbance; food production reduced by unknown percentage
- Effects limited to small section immediately below dam
- Cofferdam materials to be hauled to existing dump site, not released into river

### Substrate Stability and Primary Productivity Impacts

**Three Possible Long-Term Outcomes (in order of probability):**

**a) Enhanced Productivity (most probable):**
- Increased velocities (up to 5.1 mph maximum) would scour substrate until particle size reaches 5 inches or greater (vs. current 3-5 inches minimum)
- Smaller gravel and rock debris washed downstream
- Exposure of larger area of rocky substrate would enhance algae attachment and growth
- Substrate conditions favorable for periphyton could spread to areas currently unproductive due to sand accumulation
- Armoring reestablishment expected to take one high release season (~6 months)
- Fluctuations would continue breaking filamentous algae loose
- Seasonal and daily water level fluctuations would continue exposing periphyton as currently experienced

**b) No Noticeable Change:**
- Degradation and enhancement would balance, maintaining present productivity
- Increased velocities would reduce periphyton in maximum velocity areas but enhance growth in sandy areas by scouring to rocky substrate
- Communities may reestablish in areas maintaining present conditions
- Periphyton exposure from fluctuations unchanged

**c) Reduced Productivity (least probable):**
- Scouring and water abrasion may reduce total biomass in first 3 miles
- Substrate instability would persist indefinitely, aggravated by influx of materials from rain-fed washes
- Periphyton community unlikely to be completely eliminated but could be reduced
- Periphyton exposure from fluctuations unchanged

**Assessment:** Even with maximum disruption, highly unlikely that periphyton community would be completely eliminated. Research by Paulson and Miller et al. (1980) and similar studies (Matter et al., 1980) support these predictions.

### Temperature Impacts

**Operational Commitment:**
- Bureau intends to continue releasing water such that downstream temperatures do not fluctuate more than 3°F (54-57°F range)
- 3°F fluctuation determined by most stringent operational conditions: Lake Mead elevation 1120 (lowest predicted for next 60 years), 62,000 ft³/s release, using lower gates on all but one tower
- Maximum temperature: 57°F; minimum: 54°F

**Worst-Case Scenario:**
- If modified penstock (drawing from both upper and lower gates) used alone: 54-61°F range (7°F fluctuation)
- Not expected to occur
- Even under extreme conditions, temperature fluctuations would have little effect on fishery

**Effects on Black Canyon and Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery:**
- Daily fluctuation of 3°F and maximum 57°F: no effect on Black Canyon fishery or hatchery
- Hatchery located 12 miles downstream

**Lake Mead Productivity:**
- Increased releases would further increase temperature instability above Hoover Dam
- No appreciable change in total weekly or monthly releases; temperature and current patterns in [[Boulder Basin]] should not change significantly
- Incremental increase may slightly accelerate vertical mixing above dam, influencing nutrient distribution and phytoplankton productivity in summer
- Slight increase in phytoplankton and zooplankton productivity expected, but not perceptible without limnological monitoring equipment (no nuisance algae blooms)
- Lowering Lake Mead to elevation 1120 should not alter impacts on plankton productivity beyond above discussion

### Interface and Fish Hatchery Impacts

**Interface Movement:**
- Moves farthest upstream when Lake Mohave elevations high and Hoover Dam releases low
- Under Alternative 1: Mid-week releases greater, weekend releases less than current
- Lowest mean daily release: 0-2,000 ft³/s on summer weekends
- At Lake Mohave elevation 647 (worst case), interface located 11.9-12.6 miles below Hoover Dam
- [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] located 12 miles below dam

**Potential Hatchery Impacts:**
- Interface may occasionally interfere with hatchery operation
- Interface typically carries debris, could interfere with intake and cause nuisance problems at [[Willow Beach Resort]]
- Mitigation planned if monitoring shows interference: relocate intake pipe or install water cooling device

### Fishery Resource Impacts

**Lake Mead:**
- No impacts expected to fishery
- Striped bass spawn near Arizona spillway; remote possibility of eggs/fry entrainment into upper intake gate
- Upper intake gate ~80 feet below lowest spawning area; depth, distance, and low intake velocities make entrainment unlikely
- One of many striped bass spawning areas; even if impingement occurred, would not decrease population

**Lake Mohave - Striped Bass:**
- Three specimens collected in 1981; unknown if isolated case or self-sustaining population
- Possibility of additional striped bass entering through Hoover Powerplant considered very remote
- More likely introduced by accidental/intentional release by fishermen
- Proposed project would not affect striped bass population or contribute to introduction
