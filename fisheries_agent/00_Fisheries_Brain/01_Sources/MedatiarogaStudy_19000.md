# Spikedace and Loach Minnow Study - Research Proposal

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #endangered-species #Arizona

## Executive Summary

This administrative document represents a research proposal submission from [[Arizona State University]] to the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] for a study focused on [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*). The proposal was submitted on July 30, 1986, with principal investigators Dr. W. L. Minckley (Department of Zoology) and Dr. Paul C. Marsh (Center for Environmental Studies). The project was structured as a cost-reimbursable contract rather than a fixed-fee arrangement, with a project period from September 1, 1986 to September 30, 1987.

## Background & Description

The [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] are both members of the family [[Cyprinidae]] and are native freshwater fish species found in Arizona streams. This proposal represents an institutional effort to secure funding for research on these two species, both of which are of conservation concern in the southwestern United States.

The proposal was coordinated through Arizona State University's Office of Research Development and Administration, with Grant and Contract Administrator Gerald Simpson handling the administrative aspects. The submission required two copies and was due at 3:00 PM on July 31, 1986.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study was proposed during a period when both [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]] populations were experiencing significant conservation challenges in Arizona's freshwater systems. The involvement of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] as the funding sponsor indicates state-level concern for these species' status and management needs.

The research was to be conducted through Arizona State University's Department of Zoology and Center for Environmental Studies, leveraging institutional expertise in southwestern ichthyology and environmental research.
