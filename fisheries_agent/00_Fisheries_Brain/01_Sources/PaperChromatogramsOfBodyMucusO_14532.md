# Paper Chromatograms of Body Mucus of Some Suckers (Family Catostomidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 methods study by [[Gene R. Huntsman]], published in the Iowa Academy of Science, tested whether [[Paper Chromatography]] of body mucus could separate catostomid fishes (suckers) taxonomically. Mucus was sampled non-lethally from five genera: *Carpiodes*, *Catostomus*, *Ictiobus*, *Moxostoma*, and *Hypentelium*. Descending chromatography in a [[Butanol Acetic Acid Water Solvent]] system, viewed under short-wave [[Ultraviolet Light]], gave the most reproducible patterns. These patterns separated most genera and split the subfamilies, but the four [[Carpiodes]] species could not be distinguished. The author concluded that the original goal remained unfulfilled and that further work was needed.

## Background & Description

Morphology alone is often insufficient to separate the four carpsuckers (*Carpiodes*), especially smaller specimens. Earlier work showed that chromatograms of muscle extracts could reflect taxonomic relationships in fishes ([[Buzzati-Traverso and Rechnitzer]], 1953, and later workers). [[Barry and O'Rourke]] (1959) separated closely related *Sebastes* and *Gadus* species using body mucus. Mucus was considered more practical than muscle because sampling does not kill the fish.

Mucus is mainly a simple protein with a similar amino acid profile across species, plus variable glycoproteins. Its sugar components include galactose, glucose, fucose, ribose, glucosamine, galactosamine, and sialic acid. It also contains nucleic acids and serum protein antigens, which the author suggests could provide a basis for species specificity.

The taxonomic framework followed [[Hubbs]] (1930) and the [[American Fisheries Society]] (1960) common names. The taxa were grouped as follows:
- Subfamily Ictiobinae: *Ictiobus* sp. and four *Carpiodes* species (*carpio*, *velifer*, *forbesi*, *cyprinus*).
- Subfamily Catostominae, tribe Moxostomatini: three *Moxostoma* species (*anisurum*, *erythrurum*, *macrolepidotum*).
- Subfamily Catostominae, tribe Catostomini: *Catostomus commersoni* and *Hypentelium nigricans*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fish were captured from the Des Moines River in Boone County (electrofishing) and the South Fork of the Iowa River in Hardin County (seining). Mucus was scraped with a dull knife or spatula. Samples dried on paper and kept for several days without refrigeration, and dried and fresh mucus gave identical chromatograms. Fish were kept from crowding to avoid mixing of mucus.

Fish sampled were roughly 5 to 13 inches long, and the study covered August through November. No seasonal effect was seen, and fish from both rivers gave the same patterns. Sex was not determined, and the author found no effect of age, sex, or collection area on the patterns.

Methods included:
- Horizontal circular chromatography (3 to 4 hours per run), which was abandoned because the maximum run length of about 12 cm gave no effective separations.
- Two-dimensional chromatography (24 to 48+ hours), discontinued after early trials showed little promise.
- Descending chromatography on 1 x 18-inch Whatman No. 1 or No. 3 paper, the main method used.

Solvent systems tested:
- Phenol:water (8:2 v/v): least satisfactory, with blurred patterns, though it separated some genera.
- Butanol:acetic acid:water (100:22:50 v/v): clearest and most reproducible.
- Phenol-ammonia: not fully evaluated.

Visualization methods:
- [[Ninhydrin]] stain, which detects proteins, peptides, amino acids, and primary amines. Results varied with temperature and humidity, and spots faded quickly.
- Ammoniacal silver nitrate, which indicated sugar-like substances.
- Ultraviolet light, the most effective method. Patterns persisted for at least 4 months, and short-wave light (2537 Å) was most useful.

## Key Findings

- **Horizontal runs failed to separate carpsuckers.** Both phenol:water and butanol:acetic acid:water systems failed to separate the four *Carpiodes* species, and ninhydrin patterns were the same across species within a run.
- **Ninhydrin variability limited comparisons.** Ninhydrin spots intergraded and varied between runs. In butanol:acetic acid:water runs, *C. carpio* gave 3 to 7 spots (31 chromatograms), *C. velifer* 2 to 8 spots (17 chromatograms), and *C. cyprinus* 3 to 8 spots (14 chromatograms). Phenol:water gave 5 to 8 spots in 5 *C. carpio* chromatograms.
- **Ultraviolet method was the most reproducible.** A 24-hour descending run in butanol:acetic acid:water, examined under ultraviolet light, was the best approach. In total, 74 chromatograms were developed: 16 *C. carpio*, 12 *C. cyprinus*, 9 *C. velifer*, 3 unidentified *Carpiodes*, 11 *M. macrolepidotum*, 4 *M. anisurum*, 7 *H. nigricans*, 8 *C. commersoni*, and 4 *Ictiobus* sp.
- **Subfamily-level differences.** *Catostomus*, *Moxostoma*, and *Hypentelium* (Catostominae) showed a set of prominent fluorescent bands. *Carpiodes* (Ictiobinae) showed absorption bands and lacked this fluorescence.
- **Distinctive *Hypentelium* pattern.** *H. nigricans* lacked the prominent dark absorption band near the origin that was present in the other suckers. It did have three fluorescent bands matching the upper bands of *Moxostoma* and *Catostomus*.
- **Carpiodes absorption band linked to protein.** In *Carpiodes*, the absorption band at Rf 0.7 always had a ninhydrin-staining counterpart, suggesting a proteinaceous nature.
- **Sugar-like substance identified.** Ammoniacal silver nitrate staining correlated with the dark absorption band near the origin in *Moxostoma* and *Carpiodes* (2 and 6 chromatograms, respectively).
- **Phylogenetic trends with caveats.** Patterns partly fit Hubbs' subfamily arrangement but not his tribal arrangement. *Moxostoma* (tribe Moxostomatini) resembled *Catostomus* (tribe Catostomini) more than *Hypentelium* did. The author judged that phylogenetic conclusions would be premature.
- **Unresolved goal.** The four *Carpiodes* species could not be separated by mucus chromatography. Suggested next steps included better temperature control, alternative ninhydrin stains, untested solvent systems, hybridization studies, and [[Electrophoresis]].
