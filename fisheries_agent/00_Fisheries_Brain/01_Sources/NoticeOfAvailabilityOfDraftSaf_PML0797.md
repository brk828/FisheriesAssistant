# Safe Harbor Agreement for Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish in the Aravaipa Creek Watershed

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a February 2005 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) correspondence and Federal Register notice announcing the availability of a draft [[Safe Harbor Agreement]] (SHA) and an application for an [[Enhancement of Survival Permit]] submitted by the Arizona Chapter of [[The Nature Conservancy]] (TNC). The agreement would support recovery of two federally listed fishes, the [[Gila Topminnow]] and the [[Desert Pupfish]], on TNC-owned lands within the [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed in Graham and Pinal counties, Arizona. The permit would run for 20 years under Section 10(a)(1)(A) of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The Service invited public comment on the application, the draft agreement, and an accompanying [[National Environmental Policy Act]] screening form, with comments due by March 28, 2005.

## Background & Description

**Species covered**

- **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), a federally listed endangered fish.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*), a federally listed endangered fish.

Both species are native to the [[Gila River Basin]]. Historically they occurred in extensive populations, but the text states that neither species is currently known to be present in the Aravaipa Creek watershed.

**Parties and instruments**

- **Applicant:** The Arizona Chapter of [[The Nature Conservancy]], a non-governmental conservation organization.
- **Developers of the draft agreement:** TNC, in coordination with the Arizona Game and Fish Department, the Bureau of Land Management, and the Service.
- **Agency:** [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, Phoenix.
- **Permit reference:** TE-069809-0 (as read from scanned text).
- **Duration:** 20 years.

**Scope of the agreement**

The agreement would reestablish Gila topminnow and desert pupfish at three sites on the south rim of Aravaipa Creek and maintain existing habitats. The notice describes the agreement as a "Low Effect" agreement, which qualifies it for a [[Categorical Exclusion]] under NEPA.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat goal:** Establishing new populations and maintaining current habitats in the Aravaipa Creek watershed, which covers approximately 14,000 km² according to the text.
- **Baseline principle:** Under the SHA, the landowner undertakes recovery actions on private land, such as establishing new populations, while the Service provides assurance that future property-use restrictions will not be imposed beyond those required to maintain the current species baseline.
- **Regulatory protection:** The agreement and permit are intended to shield the applicant from further Endangered Species Act regulation for its ongoing private land management, in areas where covered species populations are established and in habitat that may be occupied as a result of implementing the agreement.
- **Take authorization:** Section 9 of the Act prohibits the take of listed species. Under Section 10, the Service may issue permits authorizing incidental take that is not the purpose of otherwise lawful activities. The governing regulations are 50 CFR 17.22 (endangered species) and 50 CFR 17.32 (threatened species).

## Key Findings

1. **Conservation benefit:** The SHA is expected to provide a net conservation benefit for the Gila topminnow and desert pupfish.
2. **Reintroduction:** The agreement would reestablish both species in three locations on the south rim of Aravaipa Creek, where they are currently absent.
3. **Landowner incentive:** The agreement encourages private landowners to undertake recovery through certainty that future land-use restrictions will be limited to maintaining the existing baseline.
4. **Regulatory status:** The agreement qualifies as a Low Effect action with no significant individual or cumulative impact on the human environment, and the permit may be categorically excluded from NEPA review.
5. **Public process:**
   - Comments on the application, draft SHA, and NEPA screening form were due by March 28, 2005, to the Field Supervisor in Phoenix.
   - Documents were available for review at the Service's Phoenix office or online.
   - The notice was published in the *Federal Register* (Vol. 70, No. 36, February 24, 2005).
6. **Information collection:** A separate U.S. Geological Survey notice, appended to the same Federal Register issue, requested public comment on an information collection submitted to OMB under the Paperwork Reduction Act, with specific comments requested on the necessity and utility of the collection.
