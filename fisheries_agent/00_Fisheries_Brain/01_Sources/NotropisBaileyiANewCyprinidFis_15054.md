# Notropis Baileyi: A New Cyprinid Fish from the Pascagoula and Mobile Bay Drainages of Mississippi and Alabama

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes [[Notropis Baileyi]], a new small cyprinid fish from the Pascagoula and Mobile Bay drainages of Mississippi and Alabama. The authors, Royal D. Suttkus ([[Tulane University]]) and Edward C. Raney ([[Cornell University]]), distinguish the species from its closest relatives, [[Notropis Lutipinnis]] and [[Notropis Chrosomus]], mainly by anal ray count (7 versus 8), along with differences in pattern, proportions, lateral line pores, scale counts, nuptial tubercles and sexual dimorphism. The species is named for [[Reeve M. Bailey]], curator of fishes at the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]]. The paper also clarifies the identities of two previously described species, and it notes that the genus *Notropis* is the largest genus of North American Cyprinidae.

## Background & Description

- **Genus context:** *Notropis* is the largest genus of North American Cyprinidae. Recent intensive field work in the southeastern United States had revealed several new forms.
- **Type material:** 222 specimens (21–63 mm standard length) from seven localities in the Alabama River System. An additional 338 specimens (19–55 mm) came from 19 localities in the Tombigbee and Pascagoula drainages.
- **Holotype:** [[CU 28224]], an adult female, 59.6 mm standard length. It was collected on June 12, 1949, in a tributary of Sawacklahatchee Creek, Macon County, Alabama, by Suttkus, Charles F. Cole and Robert H. Gibbs.
- **Paratypes:** 66 specimens (29–63 mm) from the holotype locality (CU 16020), plus numerous additional lots in the [[Tulane University Collection]], [[Cornell University Collection]], [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] and [[United States National Museum]].
- **Diagnosis:** A *Notropis* with 2,4–4,2 pharyngeal teeth and 7 anal rays. Dorsal 8 rays, pectoral 14 (occasionally 15), pelvic 8. Predorsal scale rows 15 (rarely 16), lateral line 36 (sometimes 37), circumferential scales before dorsal 23–30 (usually 24–28), and caudal peduncle scales 12 (sometimes 13). Maximum size is about 60 mm standard length.
- **Morphology:** The body is fusiform, moderately deep and compressed. The mouth is terminal to inclined, with the upper jaw much longer than the eye. A dark middorsal streak encircles the dorsal fin base, a dark lateral band runs along the body, and a prominent dark basicaudal spot is present.
- **Coloration:** Typically a black-banded fish with a light stripe above the band. Breeding males and females show bright shades of red through yellow, and field notes describe metallic and orange-red colors in some populations.
- **Sexual dimorphism:** In males, the pelvic fins are longer and nearly always reach the anal fin origin. Males also have broader pectoral fins and more developed nuptial tubercles on the head and body.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** The species occurs in the Upper Coastal Plain and adjacent Piedmont. Its range extends from the Leaf and Chickasawhay rivers of the Pascagoula drainage in Mississippi eastward through the Mobile Bay drainage of Alabama, where it has been taken in the Tombigbee, Black Warrior and Alabama rivers. In the Alabama basin it reaches eastward to the Tallapoosa River.
- **Piedmont record:** A single specimen from 5 miles west of Attalla, in the Coosa River system, suggests scattered populations may exist throughout the upper Piedmont.
- **Habitat:** All material came from small wooded streams, usually 5–10 feet wide (rarely over 15 feet), with moderate flow. The bottom was almost always sand and gravel, the water was clear to white where noted, and aquatic vegetation was usually absent.
- **Infraspecific variation:** Adults from the Black Warrior system lack the dark ovals found below the lateral band in other populations. They also have more circumferential scales (26–30, usually 26–28, versus 23–27, usually 24–26 elsewhere) and larger, coarser nuptial tubercles.
- **Conservation note:** The authors describe the habits and ecology of these small fishes as worthwhile subjects for regional ichthyologists, implying that they were poorly known at the time.

## Key Findings

1. **New species:** *Notropis baileyi* is a valid new species of Cyprinidae. It is most closely related to *N. lutipinnis* (Savannah, Altamaha and Chattahoochee rivers) and *N. chrosomus* (Alabama River system).
2. **Diagnostic character:** The anal ray count is the clearest separator. *N. baileyi* averages 7.06 (n = 323), whereas *N. lutipinnis* averages 8.03 (n = 174) and *N. chrosomus* averages 8.09 (n = 91). Pattern, proportions, lateral line pore counts, tubercle arrangement and scale counts also separate the three species.
3. **Lateral line pores:** The supratemporal canal is usually incomplete with two pores per side in *N. baileyi* and *N. chrosomus*, but usually complete with three pores in *N. lutipinnis*. The infraorbital and preoperculomandibular pore counts also differ among the species.
4. **Nomenclatural clarifications:** On re-examination, the types of *N. norrisi* Fowler are actually *Hybopsis harperi* (Fowler), and *N. williami* Fowler is a synonym of *N. petersoni* Fowler. The types of *N. williami* have 2,4–4,2 teeth, not the 1,4–4,1 given in the original description.
5. **Separation from similar species:** *N. baileyi* differs from *N. petersoni*, *N. xaenocephalus*, *N. roseus* and an undescribed Mobile Bay species (all with 7 anal rays and 2,4–4,2 teeth) in its continuous middorsal stripe encircling the dorsal base, its larger mouth with the upper jaw longer than the eye, the uniform dark lateral band behind the eye, and the even density of the lateral band. The authors also conclude that it is not the "wide mouth" variety of *N. xaenocephalus* described by Jordan (1877).
6. **Pattern:** Dark ovals below the lateral band (absent in Black Warrior adults), a quadrate basicaudal blotch, a heart-shaped mark on the back of the head, and a diamond-shaped bar across the head are key pattern features.
7. **Breeding features:** Nuptial tubercles are well developed in both sexes, and both sexes show bright spawning colors. The authors note that males were observed with a wide whitish or lead-colored lateral line in the field.
8. **Distribution:** Restricted to the Upper Coastal Plain and nearby Piedmont of the Pascagoula and Mobile Bay drainages, from the Leaf and Chickasawhay rivers to the Tallapoosa River, with one possible outlying Piedmont record.
