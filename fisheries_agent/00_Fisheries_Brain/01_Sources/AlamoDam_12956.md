# Alamo Dam Project Completion Report (Project FW-16-R-6, Work Plan 2, Job 3)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae #ictaluridae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department completion report (August 1, 1966 to March 31, 1967) covers the construction phase of the [[Alamo Dam]] and [[Alamo Reservoir]] on the [[Bill Williams River]] drainage. The 283-foot earthfill embankment was still under construction. Outlet works, a spillway, administration facilities and an access road were completed or nearly complete. The [[Army Corps of Engineers]], the [[Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife]] and the Department agreed on a joint General Plan for the 17,300-acre [[Ocotillo Game Management Area]]. The Department also drafted a [[Fisheries Management Plan]] recommending [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Plains Red Shiner]] as the stocking combination. The report describes its objective as determining the effects of the dam on fish and wildlife and recommending measures to maximize benefits.

## Background & Description

The project objective was to assess how Alamo Dam would affect fish and wildlife resources and to recommend measures that would maximize benefits in the affected area. Work during this segment consisted mainly of correspondence, meetings with agencies, field trips, and inspections of construction to confirm that suggested fish and wildlife provisions were being incorporated. The report is prepared by Richard C. Endress, Assistant Leader, and approved by Wendell G. Swank, Director, and Thomas W. Taylor, Federal Aid Coordinator.

**Dam and structures**
- Earthfill embankment: crest length 975 feet, crest width 30 feet, maximum height 283 feet above the streambed.
- Completed features: concrete-lined outlet tunnel through the left abutment, concrete intake structure, control shaft with gate chamber, detached broad-crested spillway cut through a rock saddle, administration area facilities, and a paved access road.

**Recreation and administration facilities**
- Per [[Design Memorandum No. 3]] (1964), facilities include two three-bedroom houses, an administration and service building, a trailer parking area with 24 concrete slabs, parking for 100 vehicles, an amphitheater, restrooms, 25 campsites, and a picnic area with twelve ramadas. All have water, sewage and power.
- Planned Alamo fishing camp (30 acres): parking for 200 vehicles and trailers, a service and administration building, a floating dock, a boat launch ramp, and a 4' x 20' fish cleaning table with a unit that pulverizes fish waste (subject to Corps approval).
- Recreation is planned on 80 acres total: a 50-acre water area in the recreational pool and 30 acres at the fishing camp.

**Land and jurisdiction**
- The Department is acquiring management jurisdiction over land and water within the Alamo Dam Project boundaries. Recreation administration may be assigned to a third party under sublease.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Reservoir clearing**
- Brush and other boating hazards were removed from approximately 700 acres of the reservoir area, nearly complete at the end of the segment.
- Tree and brush areas are retained in rectangular block units in the upper end of the recreational pool for fisheries management (Essbach, 1966).

**Pre-impoundment fish fauna**
- A [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] survey of the upper and lower Bill Williams drainages (including [[Trout Creek]], [[Burro Creek]], [[Big Sandy River]], [[Conger Creek]] and [[Santa Maria River]]) recorded Gila sucker, coarsescale sucker, bonytail, plains red shiner, longfin dace and dusky dace.
- Carp and a sunfish (probably bluegill or green sunfish) were observed but not collected. Bullheads were reported in the drainage.
- Undesirable species (sunfish, carp, bullheads, suckers) were judged a potential problem if they became resident in the reservoir. A [[Rotenone]] eradication program was planned for known concentration areas, with helicopter dispersal of toxicant suggested for remote reaches.

**Water chemistry (June 1966 sampling)**
- Sampled sites: Big Sandy River and Santa Maria River (upstream of their confluence with the Bill Williams River) and the Bill Williams River about one mile upstream of the dam site.
- Methyl orange alkalinity: 400 ppm (Bill Williams and Big Sandy rivers), 500 ppm (Santa Maria River).
- Average magnesium 168 ppm, calcium 120 ppm, chloride 275 ppm. These were attributed to mineral concentration during low flows and were expected to dilute after filling.

**Invertebrates and habitat**
- Invertebrate production appeared adequate given limited stones, gravel and debris; the stream bed is mostly sand.
- Stoneflies (Plecoptera) were the predominant aquatic insect. Dragonfly larvae (Odonata), water beetles (Coleoptera) and chironomid fly larvae also occurred.
- Insect populations were expected to increase in the reservoir due to exposed rock, rubble, trees and brush.

**Aquatic vegetation**
- Chemical vegetation control may be needed after the reservoir stabilizes. Vegetation density was considered unpredictable, mainly because of turbidity.

**Wildlife management**
- Priority is [[Ocotillo Game Management Area]] development for small game and waterfowl, with habitat improvement for all game species. Observation of river flow patterns after initial filling is needed before a program is finalized.

## Key Findings

- **Dam status:** The 283-foot earthfill embankment was under construction. Construction began in 1963, with completion scheduled for July 1968, when certain areas and public access facilities will transfer to the Department.
- **Joint General Plan:** The Corps, the Bureau of Sport Fisheries and Wildlife and the Department agreed on a General Plan (drafted in segment five) making the 17,300-acre Ocotillo Game Management Area available for fish and wildlife management. Signing by all three agencies was expected in segment seven, with a lease agreement still in coordination.
- **Recommended stocking program:** The June 1966 Fisheries Management Plan recommended largemouth bass, channel catfish and plains red shiner as forage. Bluegill were excluded because of the shallow impoundment and the risk of overpopulation and stunting.
- **Stocking sequence:** Plains red shiner would be introduced shortly after impoundment begins to establish reproduction before bass and catfish stocking. Bass and catfish would be stocked at a size and density to ensure first-season spawning. Introduction of the spring 1968 run-off was hoped for. Shad or crappie may be added later if deemed desirable.
- **Pre-impoundment fauna:** Nine fish taxa were recorded in the drainage (see Ecological & Environmental Context), with undesirable species targeted for rotenone removal.
- **Management staffing:** A full-time area maintenance man was planned for early 1968 to coordinate fish, wildlife and recreation programs across the project lands and waters.
- **Reservoir size discrepancy:** The Fisheries Management Plan cites an initial design pool of 500 acres, while the clearing operation covered about 700 acres. The report does not reconcile these figures.
- **Information gaps:** The wildlife plan and the fish and wildlife management plan were still in formulation. The report says a period of observation after initial filling is needed before management programs can be finalized.
