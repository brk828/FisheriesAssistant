# Nationwide Permits Under 33 CFR Part 330 (Federal Register, November 13, 1988)

#marine-biology #environmental-regulation #nationwide-permits

## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory text, not a research study. It reproduces Part 330 of Title 33 of the Code of Federal Regulations, published in the [[Federal Register]] on November 13, 1988. It governs the [[Army Corps Of Engineers]] Nationwide Permit (NWP) program, which pre-authorizes certain activities affecting [[Wetlands]] and other waters of the United States across the country. The rules set out the list of NWPs, notification and review procedures, regional and activity-specific conditions, certification requirements under the [[Clean Water Act]] and [[Coastal Zone Management Act]], and the five-year expiration cycle. No species, fish, or ecological field data are reported.

## Background & Description

- **Purpose:** To describe the Department of the Army's nationwide permit program and list all current NWPs issued by publication in the rule.
- **Nature of permits:** A nationwide permit is a form of general permit valid nationwide. It is distinct from a [[Regional Permits]] type, which division or district engineers issue on a regional basis under 33 CFR Part 325.
- **Design goal:** To allow certain activities to proceed "with little, if any, delay or paperwork." Failure to meet a permit condition does not automatically bar the activity; it means the activity must instead be authorized through an [[Individual Permits]] or regional permit.
- **Legal authority:** NWPs may satisfy requirements of Section 10 of the Rivers and Harbors Act of 1899, Section 404 of the Clean Water Act, and/or Section 103 of the Marine Protection, Research and Sanctuaries Act. The applicable authority is noted at the end of each permit.
- **Key definitions (§ 330.2):**
  - [[Headwaters]]: the point on a non-tidal stream above which the mean annual flow is less than five cubic feet per second. Where a stream is dry for long periods, the district engineer may use the point where a flow of five cubic feet per second is equaled or exceeded 50 percent of the time.
  - [[Discretionary Authority]]: the power delegated to division engineers to override NWPs, add regional conditions, or require individual permit applications.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document addresses environmental protection mainly through conditions and safeguards rather than ecological data. Relevant provisions include:

- **Aquatic life movement:** The activity must not significantly disrupt the movement of indigenous aquatic species, and must not restrict the passage of normal or expected high flows.
- **Spawning and shellfish areas:** Discharges in spawning areas during spawning seasons are restricted. Discharges are not permitted where they would affect concentrated [[Shellfish Production Areas]] or public water supply lakes.
- **Wetlands:** Discharges into wetlands are to be avoided. Heavy equipment working in wetlands must be placed on mats.
- **Endangered species:** The activity must not jeopardize threatened or endangered species or adversely modify their critical habitat, under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Where it may do so, the district engineer must initiate consultation with the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] or the National Marine Fisheries Service.
- **Water quality and materials:** Dredged or fill material must be free of toxic pollutants in toxic amounts, as defined under Section 307 of the Clean Water Act.
- **Wild and scenic rivers:** Activities cannot occur in rivers designated, or under study, for wild and scenic status.
- **Cultural resources:** Permittees must notify the district engineer if historic properties may be affected, and must halt work upon discovering eligible historic sites. See [[Historic Properties]].
- **Tribal rights:** Activities must not impair reserved tribal rights.
- **Outer continental shelf:** Oil, gas, and mineral structures are covered only in areas leased by the [[Minerals Management Service]] and must avoid designated shipping safety fairways.

## Key Findings

**1. Structure of the NWP program**
- The rule lists NWPs numbered in sequence, with authorized activities including navigation aids, canal structures in residential developments, fish and wildlife harvesting devices (pound nets, crab traps, eel pots, lobster traps, duck blinds, clam and oyster digging), staff gages and water quality devices, survey and exploratory boreholes, and outfall and intake structures covered by NPDES permits.
- Other NWPs cover repair and replacement of existing structures, non-commercial single-boat mooring buoys, temporary recreational buoys, utility line backfill, and bank stabilization.

**2. Bank stabilization conditions (NWP 13)**
- Activity must be under 500 feet in length, necessary for erosion prevention, and limited to an average of one cubic yard per running foot placed along the bank.
- No material may be placed in wetlands or in a way that impairs surface water flow. Only clean material free of waste, organics, and debris may be used.
- The activity must be a single and complete project.

**3. Exclusions**
- Maintenance dredging and beach restoration are not authorized under the repair NWP.
- Drilling of exploratory oil and gas bore holes is not authorized, although plugging of such holes is.

**4. Notification and review**
- Several NWPs require prior notification to the district engineer, with no work to proceed until notified, or until 20 days pass without objection (unless the work is covered by a waiver or an individual permit is required).
- The district engineer reviews notifications and may forward concerns from resource agencies to the division engineer. The division engineer decides whether an [[Individual Permits]] is required.
- Notifications must be in writing and include the permittee's name, address, and phone number; the location of the work; a description of the work, its purpose, and the approximate area of waters affected; and any other required information.

**5. Discretionary authority and conditions**
- Division engineers may add regional conditions, add activity-specific conditions, suspend NWPs in specific cases, or require individual permits.
- Only the [[Chief Of Engineers]] may modify, suspend, or revoke NWPs on a statewide or nationwide basis.
- Regional conditions must be developed under standard permit procedures and publicized.

**6. Certification requirements**
- **[[Water Quality Certification]] under Section 401:** Required for NWPs that may result in any discharge. If a state denies certification for a given NWP, authorization is denied without prejudice within that state. A waiver applies if a state fails to act within a reasonable period.
- **Certification categories:**
  - No certification needed: NWPs including 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 19, since they involve no discharges.
  - Certification sometimes required: NWPs such as 3, 6, 7, 13, and 20 through 23, depending on whether a given activity involves a discharge.
  - Certification required: NWPs including 12, 14 through 18, and 24 through 26, which involve discharges.
- **[[Coastal Zone Management]] consistency:** Where a state has not concurred that an NWP is consistent with its approved coastal zone management plan, authorization is denied without prejudice until the applicant obtains concurrence. Consistency is presumed if the state does not act within six months.

**7. Verification and expiration**
- Permittees may request written verification from the district engineer. Verification is valid for no more than two years, unless a shorter period is deemed appropriate.
- The Chief of Engineers reviews NWPs continually and must modify, reissue, or revoke them at least every five years. An NWP not reissued within five years of publication expires automatically.
- Activities commenced or under contract under an NWP remain authorized if completed within twelve months after the permit expires or is revoked, unless discretionary authority has been exercised.

**8. Limits on permit validity**
- NWPs do not grant property rights, exclusive privileges, or authority to interfere with existing navigation or other legal uses.
- NWPs do not replace the need for other federal, state, or local authorizations.
