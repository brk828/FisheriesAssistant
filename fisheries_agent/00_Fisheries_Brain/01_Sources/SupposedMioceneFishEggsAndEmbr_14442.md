# Supposed Miocene Fish Eggs and Embryo from Calico Mountains Nodules Identified as Ostracodes

#marine-biology #paleontology #ostracoda

## Executive Summary

This 1961 bulletin note by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Robert R. Miller]] re-examines silicified microfossils from [[Calico Mountains Nodules]] that [[W. Dwight Pierce]] had reported as Miocene fish eggs and an embryo. After review by several specialists, the authors conclude that the specimens are [[Ostracoda]] (seed shrimp) crustaceans, not vertebrate eggs. The note is a correction to the earlier fish-egg interpretation.

## Background & Description

- **Prior claim:** Pierce (1959a-b) described small fossils from the nodules as fish eggs at developmental stages from undifferentiated solid bodies to embryos. He stated that the absence of an amnion led him to consider them fish. He also reported two fossil fishes from the same nodules, which he interpreted as possibly [[Cyprinodontidae]].
- **Age of nodules:** Middle or late [[Miocene Epoch]], per [[Allison R. Palmer]] (1957). The nodules also contain well-preserved, largely silicified arthropods, mainly insects.
- **Review chain:** The senior author raised the question with the junior author, who was studying fossil fishes of western North America. Specimens were then sent to:
  - Dr. R. Jack Schultz, who studies early development of cyprinodontid fishes and found no sign of fish origin.
  - Dr. Claude W. Hibbard, [[University of Michigan Museum of Paleontology]], who confirmed the ostracode identification.
  - Dr. Robert V. Kesling, a specialist in Paleozoic ostracodes, who confirmed ostracode affinity and noted resemblance to [[Candona]].
  - Dr. Erhard M. Winkler, [[University of Notre Dame]], a specialist in Cenozoic ostracodes, who gave the detailed identifications summarized below.
- **Sample:** Pierce's original list comprised numbered specimen lots (about 9 "eggs" in total were newly supplied from six additional lots). Pierce's Specimens 8 and 9 were not provided.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The specimens are fossils preserved in acid-treated silicified nodules. The original shells, including muscle scars and hinge details, were largely dissolved during acid preparation, which limited generic identification.
- The nodules are from the [[Mojave Desert]] region of southern California. The text gives no information on the ancient aquatic habitat, water chemistry, or community composition.
- Miller (1945) previously described four fossil cyprinodontids from eastern California, including *Fundulus davidae* from the Mohave Desert near Black Mountain, providing regional context for the fish fossils.

## Key Findings

- **Reclassification:** The supposed fish eggs and embryo are ostracode crustaceans. The authors state that, "so far as at all recognizable," the specimens represent ostracodes and not fish eggs.
- **No blastomeres:** No structures that could be interpreted or counted as blastomeres were observed.
- **Specimen-level identifications by Winkler (1961, letter dated February 16):**
  - Specimen 193 ("3 eggs"): *Eucypris* or *Cyprinotus*. Specimen 193c shows clear bivalve characteristics in dorsal view.
  - Specimen 114 ("3 eggs"): outline of *Cyprinotus* or *Candona*, with a clearly visible hinge line.
  - Specimen 274 ("10 eggs"): *Cyprinotus* or *Cytheridea*, with clearly visible bivalve character.
  - Specimen 225 ("egg"): outline of *Cyprinotus*, possibly *Candona*.
  - Specimen 113 ("embryo"): part of the posterior shell margin of *Eucypris* (?). The authors note that the shell fragments bear a striking resemblance to a vertebrate embryo but are clearly shell.
  - Specimen 10 ("egg?"): broken residue of *Eucypris*.
  - Specimen 34 ("2 eggs"): dorsal hinge of *Eucypris*.
  - Specimen 197 ("egg"): outline of *Cyprinotus* or *Candona*.
  - Specimen 177 ("broken egg"): no clear outline visible.
  - Specimen 229 ("fish egg"): irregular, potato-shaped mass of plasma.
- **Additional lots:** The six additional specimen lots contributed nothing to the identifications.
- **Genus-level limits:** Generic identification was especially difficult because the shell's diagnostic features were dissolved during acid treatment. Winkler found a few specimens too damaged for identification.
- **Fish fossils:** The two fish fossils from the same nodules, figured by Pierce as possibly cyprinodontid, are "obviously referable to the Cyprinodontidae." The authors planned to identify them further, but stated these fossils have no bearing on the fish-egg question.
- **Figures:** Plate 33 refigures specimens 193a-c (photomicrographs by Carol J. Bumgardner) and specimen 113f.
- **Conclusion:** The "fish eggs" and "embryo" from the Calico Mountains nodules are ostracode remains, and the claim of Miocene fish eggs is not supported.
