# Lake Mohave Sucker Sampling and Trammel Net Catch Correspondence, November 1991

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a partly legible OCR scan of a letter from the [[Arizona State University]] Center for Environmental Studies (Tempe, Arizona), dated December 1991. It reports fish sampling on [[Lake Mohave]] during 23–27 November 1991, conducted in cooperation with the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]]. The sampling captured suckers, which were measured for weight, sex, and condition. Reproductive condition was assessed, and the letter notes that no ova could be expressed from any female examined. The letter also includes [[Trammel Net]] catch statistics for the same period and a proposal for further sampling. Many words and table values are lost to scan damage, so the species name, individual measurements, and most catch figures cannot be recovered with confidence.

## Background & Description

- **Organism group:** Suckers (family [[Catostomidae]]). The OCR fragment "bk suckers" suggests a [[Razorback Sucker]]-type fish, but no scientific name is legible, so the species is not confirmed.
- **Sampling period:** 23–27 November 1991 on Lake Mohave.
- **Data collected per fish:** Weight, sex, and condition. Fish were measured and sexed where they were captured.
- **Earlier record:** One fish was noted from March 1991. The OCR text suggests it was recaptured or handled again, but this is unclear.
- **Reproductive observations:** Some individuals were described as tuberculate (having breeding tubercles). Females were checked for ripe eggs, and none were expressed.
- **Planned follow-up:** The letter mentions a sampling period from 27 January to a later date in 1992 (exact dates garbled). It also refers to a planned movement of fish from the lower reach of the river toward the Yuma area, and it asks for comments or suggestions on the plan.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** Lake Mohave is a reservoir on the lower [[Colorado River]] in Arizona. It is managed under federal reclamation programs, which explains the involvement of the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation.
- **Sampling method:** Trammel nets were used to sample the fish community. The catch was expressed as catch per unit effort (CPE, fish per unit effort), using the total number of net units deployed.
- **Reproductive state:** Breeding tubercles and the absence of expressible eggs suggest the sampled fish were either in pre-spawning or post-spawning condition during late November, which is outside the typical spawning period. This is an interpretation, not a stated finding.
- **Data gaps:** The source does not give water quality, habitat, or invertebrate data.

## Key Findings

- Suckers were captured and sampled on Lake Mohave, 23–27 November 1991, with weight, sex, and condition recorded.
- Some captured fish showed breeding tubercles, but no ova could be expressed from any female examined.
- A trammel net catch table for Lake Mohave covers the same sampling period. It reports total numbers captured, percent of catch by species, and CPE in fish per unit effort. The OCR does not preserve the species rows or values, and the total effort is reported as 45.07 units.
- The letter proposes further sampling in 1992 and invites comments on the plan. It mentions the cooperation between the university and the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]].
