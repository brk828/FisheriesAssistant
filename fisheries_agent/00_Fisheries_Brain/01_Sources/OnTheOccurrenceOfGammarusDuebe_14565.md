# On the Occurrence of Gammarus duebeni and Asellus meridianus in Western France

## Executive Summary
This 1958 field study by H.B.N. Hynes documents the discovery of freshwater populations of [[Gammarus Duebeni]] in small streams of Brittany, France (between Audierne and Pointe du Raz), extending known freshwater distribution of the species southward in Europe. The study also confirms that [[Asellus Meridianus]] is the sole *Asellus* species present in the surveyed area, supporting hypotheses about differential colonization history of *Asellus* species in Western Europe. The paper situates these findings within the broader framework of **interspecific competitive exclusion** between *G. duebeni* and its freshwater congener [[Gammarus Pulex]], a pattern previously established in Britain (Hynes 1954, 1955a, 1955b).

## Background & Description
This is primarily a distributional/ecological paper rather than a morphological study; no detailed morphometric data are given. Key taxonomic notes:
- **Gammarus duebeni** (Lilljeborg) — an amphipod crustacean; reproductive females observed **carrying eggs**, confirming active breeding in the freshwater sites sampled.
- **Asellus meridianus** (Racovitza) — an isopod crustacean, taxonomically distinguished from its congener *Asellus aquaticus* per [[Racovitza 1919]] and [[Chappuis 1950]].
- No *Gammarus pulex* individuals were found co-occurring with *G. duebeni* in any collection, consistent with the mutual exclusivity pattern.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Freshwater habitat records (Brittany, 1958):** *G. duebeni* found in small streams at altitudes up to **40 m** and up to **2 km inland** from the coast.
- **Co-occurring stream fauna:** *G. duebeni* was found alongside typical lotic invertebrates including [[Polycelis]], [[Baetis]], [[Ecdyonurus]], [[Plectrocnemia]], [[Helmis]], [[Latelmis]], [[Rheotanytarsus]], and [[Ancylastrum]] — indicative of a well-oxygenated, clean freshwater stream community.
- **Brackish habitat records:** *G. duebeni* was found only at the **mouths of two small streams** south of Audierne; it was notably **absent from rock-pools at high spring-tide level**, a habitat where it is typically abundant in Britain and Scandinavia.
- **Biogeographic significance:** The Audierne–Pointe du Raz area represents the **southernmost known limit of *G. duebeni* in Europe**.
- **Competitive exclusion hypothesis:** *G. duebeni* and [[Gammarus Pulex]] are proposed to be **mutually exclusive** in freshwater across Britain and (by extension) France, likely due to *G. pulex*'s superior reproductive rate outcompeting *G. duebeni* (per [[Hynes 1955a]]). The Brittany sites, like the [[Lizard Peninsula]] in Cornwall and a site near [[Stranraer]] in Wigtownshire, Scotland, are interpreted as **refugial "pockets"** not yet invaded by *G. pulex*.
- **Temperature as a limiting factor:** The absence of *G. duebeni* from warm rock-pools in southern Brittany (despite their suitability further north) supports the hypothesis that **temperature limits the southern boundary of its brackish-water distribution**, restricting it to cooler stream-mouth habitats.
- **Norwegian analog:** A freshwater population of *G. duebeni* was also recently found in Norway (per J. Økland, Oslo University) in an area lacking *G. pulex*, reinforcing the pattern of relict freshwater populations persisting only where the competitor is absent.
- **Asellus distribution:** Only *A. meridianus* was found near Audierne — no *A. aquaticus* was recorded. This is consistent with the hypothesis (Chappuis 1950; Racovitza 1919) that *A. aquaticus* is a more recent invader of France, spreading north to south and potentially displacing *A. meridianus*. The persistence of *A. meridianus* alone on islands such as the [[Isle of Man]] and [[Bardsey Island]] similarly suggests it colonized Western Europe earlier.

## Key Findings
1. **First confirmed freshwater breeding population** of *Gammarus duebeni* documented in Brittany, France, with egg-bearing females found up to 2 km inland and 40 m altitude.
2. **No co-occurrence with *Gammarus pulex*** was observed, supporting the vicariance/competitive exclusion model established in British populations.
3. The Brittany locality is proposed as an **uninvaded refugium**, analogous to the Lizard Peninsula (Cornwall) and a Scottish site near Stranraer.
4. **Temperature is implicated** as a key factor restricting *G. duebeni* from warm brackish rock-pools in southern Brittany, confining it to cooler stream-mouth habitats — marking the species' southern European limit.
5. A parallel **relict freshwater population in Norway** (absent *G. pulex*) further supports the general model that *G. duebeni* persists in freshwater only where competitive exclusion pressure from *G. pulex* is absent.
6. Only **Asellus meridianus**, not *A. aquaticus*, was recorded in the surveyed Breton streams, consistent with a hypothesized earlier colonization of Western Europe by *A. meridianus* prior to the more recent northward-to-southward spread of *A. aquaticus*.
7. The author calls for **systematic French surveys** in Brittany and Normandy to test whether *G. pulex* and *G. duebeni* show strict vicariant (mutually exclusive) distributions, and to study brackish-water thermal limits along linear stream-mouth gradients.
