# Food Habits of Four Centrarchids in Blanca Lake

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This graduate thesis, submitted to the Graduate College in the Department of Biological Sciences with a major in fisheries management, compares the diets of four [[Centrarchidae]] fishes in [[Blanca Lake]]: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Bluegill]]. The study examines the relative volume and occurrence of food groups in each species' stomachs, seasonal and monthly shifts in diet, and the degree of food overlap and possible competition. ## Key Findings

- **Bluegill:** Primarily fed on zooplankton. Its diet overlapped with that of other species in aquatic insects and terrestrial insects.
- **Green Sunfish:** Fish made up the largest share of its consumed volume. This share was high except during periods when aquatic insects dominated the diet.
- **Largemouth Bass:** Fed largely on zooplankton, except in one season when insects became dominant.
- **Black Crappie and Bluegill:** Consumed large amounts of zooplankton. The author suggests they may compete with young [[Largemouth Bass]] for this food.
- **Competition:** Overlap between the diets of the sunfishes was greatest in insects and zooplankton. The author judges the competition not severe enough to explain the low bass catch. The author does conclude that competition could occur for shared prey.
