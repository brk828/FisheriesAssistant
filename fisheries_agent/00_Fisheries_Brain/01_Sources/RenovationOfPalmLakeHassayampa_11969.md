# Palm Lake Renovation Plan for Exotic Fish Removal and Native Species Introduction

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #cyprinidae #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from Ken Wiley to David Mladenoff, dated July 1, 1987, presents a first draft plan for renovating [[Palm Lake]] at the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] in Arizona. The project aims to remove exotic fish species and introduce endangered native species, specifically [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail chub). The proposal involves collaboration between [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[Arizona Department of Game and Fish]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and [[Arizona State University]]. Two primary concerns are addressed: potential transfer of exotic fish to the adjacent river during pond drainage, and preservation of the pond's diverse riparian habitat character during renovation.

## Background & Description

[[Palm Lake]] is located on the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]] in Arizona. The adjacent [[Hassayampa River]] has approximately 3.5 miles of perennial waters downstream from the preserve, followed by about 30 miles of dry riverbed before joining the [[Gila River]] at Gillespie Dam. The perennial section is very shallow and experiences several annual flood events.

Current exotic fish species present in Palm Lake include [[Gambusia affinis]] (mosquitofish), [[Lepomis cyanellus]] (green sunfish), and possibly [[Micropterus]] spp. (bass). Both mosquitofish and green sunfish are already present in the preserve stretch of the river. Bass are found sporadically throughout the Hassayampa drainage, though their current presence in the preserve river section is unknown.

The pond's existing habitat features extensive [[Typha]] (cattail) stands, willows, cottonwoods, and a "jungle-like character" with high habitat diversity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Hassayampa River]]'s perennial section provides extremely marginal habitat for bass species, which are not expected to reproduce successfully in these conditions. The shallow depth and frequent flood events naturally limit exotic fish populations. Expert consultation with native fish specialists indicates that mosquitofish and green sunfish are the species most likely to survive if transferred to the river during reclamation.

The proposed renovation will result in a slightly larger and deeper pond with enhanced shoreline complexity including bays, peninsulas, and indentations. Submerged aquatic vegetation will be less obvious, and [[Typha]] removed during renovation is expected to reestablish quickly. The project references work by Minckley and Galat, establishing connections with [[Arizona State University]].

## Key Findings

**Primary Concerns and Resolutions:**

1. **Exotic Fish Transfer Risk**: Consultation with native fish experts concluded that inevitable transfer of some exotic fish from the pond to the river during drainage is "not of much consequence, given the reality of the existing situation." Mosquitofish and green sunfish already inhabit the preserve river stretch, and bass populations face marginal habitat conditions with poor reproduction prospects and natural removal during high water events.

2. **Habitat Preservation**: The memorandum emphasizes that [[The Nature Conservancy]] will maintain on-site oversight of all renovation aspects to prevent the pond from becoming "a sterile bowl, with no esthetic appeal and no habitat diversity, good for nothing but a fish farm." [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] understands and supports these habitat diversity concerns.

**Project Benefits:**
- Strengthens relationships with [[Arizona Department of Game and Fish]] and local [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] personnel
- Establishes collaboration with [[Arizona State University]]
- Provides appropriate use of Palm Lake waters for conservation purposes
- Will house a substantial portion of the gene pool for [[Gila elegans]] (bonytail chub), an endangered species

**Resource Requirements:**
- No [[The Nature Conservancy]] funding required
- Staff time needed for renovation phase oversight
- Minimal long-term staff time for occasional water quality monitoring and correspondence
- No artificial feeding required for proposed native species
- Substantial financial output from [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]

**Recommendation**: The author recommends proceeding with the project contingent on [[The Nature Conservancy]] maintaining "a major role in each step of the operation." Input requested on cooperative agreement structure and liability concerns given the significant genetic conservation responsibility for [[Gila elegans]].
