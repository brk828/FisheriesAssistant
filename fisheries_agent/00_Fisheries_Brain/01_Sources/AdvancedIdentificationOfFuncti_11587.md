# Advanced Identification of Functions and Values of the Verde River, Arizona - Final Study Plan

#ichthyology #riparian-ecology #wetland-assessment #aquatic-ecosystem #environmental-planning

## Executive Summary

This study plan outlines an Advanced Identification (ADID) process for the [[Verde River]], Arizona, conducted by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Phoenix Field Office in August 1991. The ADID aims to protect the Verde River aquatic ecosystem by evaluating approximately 125 river miles from Sullivan Lake to Horseshoe Dam. The Verde River is identified as one of the nation's most significant free-flowing rivers in the Department of the Interior's National Rivers Inventory. The study will assess functions and values including groundwater discharge/recharge, sediment stabilization, floodflow alteration, nutrient removal/transformation, production export, wildlife diversity/abundance, aquatic diversity/abundance, recreation, and uniqueness/heritage. The ADID process serves as an advanced planning tool under Section 404 of the Clean Water Act to identify areas suitable or unsuitable for future dredge and fill activities. Only five percent of Arizona's original riparian habitat remains, making this assessment critical for conservation planning.

## Background & Description

The Verde River is one of Arizona's major perennial streams, providing valuable fish and wildlife habitat and recreational opportunities. The ADID is implemented pursuant to Section 404(b)(1) of the Clean Water Act and EPA regulations (40 CFR 230) to provide environmental criteria for permit issuance involving discharge of dredged or fill material into waters of the U.S. The fundamental precept is that such discharges should not occur unless they will not have unacceptable adverse effects on the aquatic ecosystem, recognizing that degradation may represent irreversible loss of valuable resources.

The study area encompasses approximately 125 river miles from Sullivan Lake to Horseshoe Dam, with lateral boundaries defined by the upland edge of riparian habitat as shown on [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] National Wetland Inventory (NWI) maps. Assessment extends one mile up major tributaries including [[Granite Creek]], [[Hell Canyon]], [[Upper Verde River]], [[Sycamore Creek]], [[Oak Creek]], [[Dry Beaver Creek]], [[Wet Beaver Creek]], [[West Clear Creek]], [[Fossil Creek]], and [[East Verde River]].

The riparian ecosystem has been significantly degraded nationwide, with only five percent of original riparian habitat estimated to remain in Arizona (Warner 1979). Documented threats to the Verde River riparian ecosystem include sand and gravel mining, residential and commercial development, flood control, bank stabilization, and agricultural diversions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Riverine systems with associated riparian communities provide diverse functions and values. Functions are physical, chemical, and biological processes or attributes without regard to human importance. Values are processes or attributes beneficial to society in non-monetary terms. Functions and values assessed include:

**Hydrological Functions:**
- Groundwater recharge occurs during high flow events when surface water moves to groundwater, maintaining constant base flow during low flow periods and sustaining aquifers
- Groundwater discharge occurs when local water tables exceed regional water tables, with discharge from alluvium to stream channels during low flow periods
- Floodflow alteration through water storage and velocity reduction, lessening downstream flooding

**Sediment and Water Quality:**
- Sediment stabilization through retention and deposition within the aquatic ecosystem, preventing erosion and reducing turbidity
- Sediment/toxicant retention through reduced water velocity, uptake by organisms, and biogeochemical transformations
- Nutrient removal and transformation, particularly nitrogen (through nitrification/denitrification) and phosphorus (through plant uptake and chemical reactions)

**Biological Functions:**
- Production export of organic plant matter supporting aquatic invertebrates, which serve as food sources for fish and wildlife
- Wildlife diversity and abundance, with the riparian ecosystem providing niches for over 60 percent of vertebrates in the surrounding area
- Aquatic diversity and abundance, with the Verde River maintaining a strong native fish community in headwaters

**Social Values:**
- Recreation including boating, fishing, hunting, camping, and hiking
- Uniqueness/heritage including Wild and Scenic designations, geologic formations, cultural resources, and threatened/endangered species habitat

The Verde River supports three federally listed species and 10 state-listed species. The cottonwood-willow riparian gallery forest is considered a globally endangered community. Economically important wildlife species are common, with habitats constituting important resources for game and non-game wildlife, especially raptors, songbirds, small mammals, reptiles, and amphibians.

## Key Findings

**Study Objectives:**
1. Assess performance and maintenance of functions and values of the Verde River aquatic ecosystem
2. Qualitatively rank performance and maintenance of functions and values for decision-making regarding fill activities
3. Assess impacts to the Verde River ecosystem with reference to activities regulated under Guidelines for Specification of Disposal Sites for Dredged or Fill Material

**Methodology:**
- Functions and values assessed using modified Wetland Evaluation Technique (WET) (Adamus et al. 1987) combined with narrative evaluations based on existing data
- Study sites designated by stream reach using EPA River Reach File, version 3
- Assessment based primarily on existing data supplemented by floating and flying the river
- Additional vegetation/wildlife habitat surveys conducted in areas lacking previous surveys, particularly between Clarkdale and West Clear Creek
- Comprehensive approach examining historic and present land use patterns within watersheds

**Data Sources:**
- USGS water resource reports and topographical maps
- Bureau of Reclamation and Arizona Department of Water Resources data
- Soil Conservation Service soil maps
- National Wetland Inventory maps with ground truthing
- FEMA and Yavapai County Flood Control maps
- Arizona Game and Fish Department Heritage Database
- Forest Service studies
- Aerial photography (1989 photos at 1:24,000 scale; Camp Verde at 1:10,000 scale; entire study area at 1:40,000 scale)

**Assessment Criteria:**
Rating system developed with: 1 = function occurring; 2 = function lacking; 3 = insufficient data. Criteria evaluate groundwater discharge/recharge, sediment stabilization (based on 50-100% streambank vegetation coverage), floodflow alteration, nutrient removal/transformation, production export, wildlife habitat (including special status species), fish habitat, recreation (public access and activity diversity), and uniqueness/heritage (special designations and rare features).

**Vegetation Sampling:**
- Line-intercept technique with 100-meter transects
- Shannon-Weiner Index for species diversity
- Szaro's (1989) classification system for Arizona and New Mexico plant communities
- Sampling until standard error ≤0.20
- Species area curves to verify adequate sample size

**Land Use Impact Assessment:**
Examination of cumulative impacts from activities in both aquatic and terrestrial ecosystems, including agriculture, urbanization, forestry practices, sand and gravel mining, residential/commercial development, flood control, bank stabilization, and agricultural diversions. Historic conditions estimated from aerial photography, ADEQ reports, Forest Service reports, and other sources.

**Schedule:**
- Finalized work plan to EPA: August 1991
- First public workshop: September 1991
- Field data collection: July 1991 - April 1992
- Draft ADID to EPA: August 31, 1992
- Final ADID to EPA: October 31, 1992

**Products:**
1. Soil maps (characteristics, slope, alluvial deposits)
2. Vegetation maps
3. Floodplain maps (100-year and 10/50-year where available)
4. Special status species distribution maps, incidental wildlife observations, and native fish (excluding threatened/endangered species)
5. Land ownership maps
6. Map of seeps and springs
7. Narrative of functions and values by stream reach
8. Matrix showing probability of functional performance and value maintenance by stream reach

**Regulatory Context:**
Results are informational and advisory, not regulatory. Individuals may still apply for Section 404 permits in areas identified as generally unsuitable, and permits may be denied in areas identified as suitable depending on specific circumstances. The ADID enables more effective advance planning, increases public awareness of aquatic ecosystem importance, and provides the regulated community with indication of permit issuance likelihood.
