# Reproductive Isolation in the Snails *Pomatiopsis lapidaria* and *P. cincinnatiensis*

#malacology #freshwater-biology #gastropoda #pomatiopsidae

## Executive Summary

This 1962 paper by [[Henry van der Schalie]] and [[Lowell L. Getz]], published in *The American Midland Naturalist*, tests whether two North American snails, [[Pomatiopsis Lapidaria]] and [[Pomatiopsis Cincinnatiensis]], are reproductively isolated where their ranges overlap. The species are usually ecologically separated: *P. lapidaria* lives in seepage areas and *P. cincinnatiensis* lives on river banks. The authors report a site on the [[River Raisin]] in Michigan where both occur together. Several hundred specimens collected over about three years showed no evidence of interbreeding. The paper also reviews hybridization work on the Asian genus [[Oncomelania]], the intermediate hosts of [[Schistosoma Japonicum]], and concludes that *P. cincinnatiensis* probably belongs to a different taxon from *P. lapidaria* and the Oncomelania species.

## Background & Description

- **Study context:** The work was supported by a contract with the U.S. Army Medical Research and Development Command (DA-49-007-MD-604), sponsored by the Commission on Parasitic Diseases, Armed Forces Epidemiological Board. The authors were at the University of Michigan and the University of Connecticut.
- **Oriental genus:** Before World War II, [[Bartsch 1936]] grouped the Oriental snail hosts of the [[Human Blood Fluke]] under three genera (*Katayama*, *Schistosomophora*, and *Oncomelania*). [[Abbott 1948]] merged them into the single genus *Oncomelania*. Four species are recognized in it: *O. quadrasi*, *O. nosophora*, *O. formosana*, and *O. hupensis*. Their status is still disputed.
- **North American taxa:** *P. lapidaria* (Say) and *P. cincinnatiensis* (Lea) are amphibious operculate snails. Their ranges overlap over a broad area (van der Schalie and Dundee, 1955; Dundee, 1957).
- **Chromosomes:** [[Burch 1960]] reported differing karyotypes. *P. lapidaria* spermatogonia have 33 chromosomes (16 pairs plus a heterochromatic, presumably sex, chromosome). *P. cincinnatiensis* has 32 chromosomes (15 pairs plus a heteromorphic pair). *O. nosophora* and *O. quadrasi* share the *P. lapidaria* count.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat partitioning:** Long-term observation indicates that *P. cincinnatiensis* occurs only on river banks, while *P. lapidaria* normally lives in seepage areas near streams and lakes. *P. lapidaria* sometimes appears on river banks, probably after rain washes individuals into rivers, but these records are too few to show whether the species is reproductively isolated.
- **Sympatric study site:** On the River Raisin, about one mile north of Clinton, Washtenaw County, Michigan, a small seepage zone roughly 30 m wide runs down to the river bank. It holds a large, thriving *P. lapidaria* colony. The adjacent bank holds extensive *P. cincinnatiensis* colonies, so the two species meet at the edge of their habitats.
- **Quadrat sample:** A 25 × 30 cm quadrat on the lower bank, in the normal *P. cincinnatiensis* habitat, contained 146 *P. lapidaria* and 89 *P. cincinnatiensis*.
- **Grid distribution (Table I):** A 45-square grid (5 × 5 cm cells, nine rows by five columns) mapped both species across the overlap zone. The table records counts of each species per cell and row totals, showing intermixing at the habitat boundary.

## Key Findings

1. **No interbreeding in the field:** Intensive collections over about three years, including re-examination of several hundred specimens from the seepage area and adjacent bank, showed no evidence of hybridization. The authors conclude that the two species do not interbreed under natural sympatric conditions.
2. **Chromosomal difference:** The 33 versus 32 chromosome counts suggest that *P. cincinnatiensis* would not produce fertile hybrids with *P. lapidaria* or the Oncomelania species.
3. **Laboratory support:** Culture methods for *P. cincinnatiensis* were not yet fully developed. Laboratory crosses to date support reproductive isolation (van der Schalie et al., in press).
4. **Taxonomic conclusion:** Field data, laboratory results, and other differences indicate that *P. cincinnatiensis* belongs to a different taxon from *P. lapidaria* and the Oncomelania species. The authors planned a fuller account of species relationships.
5. **Oncomelania hybridization:**
   - [[Pesigan]] and co-workers (unpublished) crossed *O. quadrasi*, *O. nosophora*, and *O. formosana* in every combination. [[Wagner and Chi Wong 1959]] confirmed these crosses and also crossed *O. hupensis* with the other three species.
   - All these Oncomelania hybrids produced fertile offspring.
6. **Cross-continental hybrids:** Males of *P. lapidaria* crossed with females of *O. quadrasi* and *O. formosana* (van der Schalie et al., in press). The offspring of at least the *O. quadrasi* cross were fertile.
7. **Failed crosses:** Attempted crosses of *P. cincinnatiensis* with all four Oncomelania species and with *P. lapidaria* were unsuccessful.
8. **Allopatry and species definition:** All nominal Oncomelania species are allopatric, so their taxonomic status depends on how a species is defined for geographically separated populations. The same question applies to relationships between Oncomelania and the widely separated *Pomatiopsis* groups.
