## Executive Summary
This report documents a cooperative interstate stocking effort by the California and Arizona Fish & Game Departments in 1962 to introduce [[Striped Bass]] into the [[Colorado River]] between [[Parker Dam]] and [[Davis Dam]]. Fish were salvaged from water diversion structures operated by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]] near Tracy, California, transported to a holding facility, and subsequently air-dropped into the river and [[Lake Havasu]] system. The report is primarily an operational account of the translocation logistics rather than a biological study, though it provides quantitative stocking data and notes plans for post-introduction monitoring via electrofishing.

## Background & Description
- Two size classes of [[Striped Bass]] were stocked:
  - **Juvenile/fingerling class**: approximately 2–3 inches in length (majority of stocked fish, ~38,000 individuals)
  - **Sub-adult class**: approximately 8–10 inches in length (minority of stocked fish, 225 individuals)
- No further morphometric, meristic, or physiological data are reported in the source text.
- Fish were noted to have survived an ~150-foot aerial drop into water without apparent immediate mortality ("everything appeared in order"), suggesting some tolerance to impact stress at these size classes, though no systematic post-drop condition assessment is described.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Source population**: Fish were harvested incidentally from Bureau of Reclamation fish-separation structures on irrigation canals approximately 10–15 miles west of [[Tracy, California]], indicating an existing established population of [[Striped Bass]] in the California Central Valley/Delta water conveyance system.
- **Recipient habitat**: The Colorado River reach between [[Parker Dam]] and [[Davis Dam]], including areas near [[Havasu Landing]] and extending toward [[Needles, California]], and the reservoir habitat of [[Lake Havasu]].
- **Introduction pathway**: This represents a deliberate interbasin translocation/stocking event, moving a non-native piscivorous species from the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta system into the lower [[Colorado River]] basin — a management action with potential implications for native and existing sport fish assemblages in the receiving system.
- **Habitat use following release**: Not documented; the report explicitly states no post-release sightings or catches were confirmed at time of writing.

## Key Findings
- Approximately **38,000** Striped Bass of 2–3 inch size class and **225** Striped Bass of 8–10 inch size class were air-dropped into the Colorado River system in 1962.
- Stocking effort was a **joint interstate operation** between the California and Arizona Fish & Game Departments, involving approximately seven personnel.
- Fish were sourced via passive salvage from irrigation infrastructure (Bureau of Reclamation canal fish-separation structures) rather than targeted collection or hatchery production.
- Logistics chain: capture near Tracy, CA → holding/observation at [[Elk Grove, California]] (2–3 days) → air transport → aerial release into the Colorado River near Havasu Landing and downstream of Davis Dam toward Needles.
- Aerial drop height was approximately **150 feet**; initial observation by boat crew indicated fish appeared unaffected by the drop.
- As of the report's writing, **no confirmed sightings or recaptures** of stocked fish had occurred.
- Future monitoring was planned using **electric shocker (electrofishing) surveys** on Lake Havasu to assess establishment success.
- The report lacks data on survival rates, growth, reproduction, or ecological impact — representing only the initial stocking phase of what appears to be a longer-term introduction and monitoring program.
