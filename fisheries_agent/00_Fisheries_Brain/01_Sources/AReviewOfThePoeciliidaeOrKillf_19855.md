# A Review of the Poeciliidae or Killifishes of Japan

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Poeciliidae #killifishes #taxonomic-review

## Executive Summary

This 1906 taxonomic review by [[Jordan]] and [[Snyder]] documents two species of killifishes (family [[Poeciliidae]]) from Japan: [[Fundulichthys virescens]] and [[Oryzias latipes]]. Both are small fishes inhabiting rice swamps, pools, and ditches in southern Japan, likely feeding on mosquitoes. The authors establish the new genus [[Oryzias]] to accommodate O. latipes, distinguishing it from related genera by its short jaws, absence of vomerine teeth, and very long anal fin (17-20 rays). Specimens were collected by the authors in 1900 and deposited in the [[United States National Museum]] and [[Stanford University]] museum.

## Background & Description

The family [[Poeciliidae]] (also called [[Cyprinodontidae]]) comprises small freshwater fishes characterized by oblong to moderately elongate bodies, compressed posteriorly and depressed anteriorly. Bodies are covered with large cycloid scales; lateral line is wanting or represented by few imperfect pores. Heads are scaly above. Mouths are terminal and small with projecting lower jaws; premaxillaries form the entire upper jaw margin and are extremely protractile. Teeth are incisor-like or villiform, sometimes present on vomer but usually restricted to jaws. Third upper pharyngeal is enlarged, fourth wanting or united to third. Gill membranes are somewhat connected but free from isthmus; gill rakers very short and thick. Branchiostegals number 4-6; pseudobranchiae absent. Dorsal fin is single, inserted posteriorly, composed of soft rays only. Caudal fin not forked; ventral fins abdominal, rarely wanting. Stomach is siphonal without pyloric appendages. Air bladder simple, often wanting. Many species are ovoviviparous. Distribution includes southern Europe, Asia, Africa, and America, with some occurring in marine bays and arms. Japanese species are known as "Medaka."

**[[Fundulichthys virescens]]** (Omedaka, Akitabira): Known only from Schlegel's 1846 description based on Bürger's drawing. B.4, D.9, V.7, P.14, C.18. Head length equals depth at pectorals, nearly one-fifth total length. Jaws with many tooth series, median teeth largest. Eyes moderate. Vent nearer caudal tip than head tip. Small dorsal fin at mid-back; small anal fin. Caudal slightly notched. Color in life: olive green dorsally, paler laterally, whitish ventrally; dorsal, anal, and especially caudal orange-yellow. Length slightly over 2 inches. Reported as very common in brooks and rivers flowing into [[Nagasaki Bay]]. Not collected by authors; no descriptions since Schlegel.

**[[Oryzias latipes]]** (Medaka): Head 3.4 in length to caudal base; depth 3.4; caudal peduncle depth 7.3; eye 2.4 in head; snout 4; interorbital space 2.4; D.6; A.17; lateral scales 31. Very large eyes; flat interorbital space; short snout; slightly projecting lower jaw. Gill openings not restricted above, extending to upper pectoral edge. Branchiostegals 5. First arch with 13 short, blunt gill rakers. Jaws with 2 rows of simple pointed teeth, posterior row minute; vomer smooth; pharyngeals with minute teeth in 12-13 transverse rows above, 6-7 below. Intestinal canal approximately equal to body length. Peritoneum dense black internally, bright silvery externally. Head top and sides, throat, and chin naked; lateral line absent. Short dorsal fin originates above anal mid-point; height equal to or greater than snout-to-posterior-eye distance. Anal base very long, posterior rays highest (equal to dorsal height), extending to caudal base when depressed. Pectorals inserted partly below body median line, length 4.4 times in body. Ventrals pointed, short, just reaching vent when depressed. Caudal truncate, 4.5 in length.

Males: light brownish in alcohol with silvery peritoneum visible through abdominal walls; narrow median dusky stripe from occiput to dorsal; body sides sparsely stippled with black, scale edges dusky; median dusky line from pectoral tip to caudal base; fin membranes dusky, darkening toward edges; ventrals black; narrow light area on caudal base.

Females: more slender body posterior to anal opening; lower anal; usually longer ventrals often reaching beyond anal base; ventrals immaculate or slightly dusky. No anal ray modification distinguishes either sex.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Both species inhabit rice swamps, pools, and ditches in southern [[Japan]], environments associated with mosquito breeding. [[Oryzias latipes]] ranges north to [[Tokyo]]. The species likely play ecological roles as mosquito predators in these agricultural and stagnant water habitats. [[Oryzias latipes]] was collected from stagnant pools at [[Wakanoura]] and rice field ditches at [[Kawatana]] on [[Omura Bay]] north of [[Nagasaki]], co-occurring with the minnow [[Rhodeus oryzae]]. The species is described as "very common in all mosquito-breeding waters in southern Japan."

The genus [[Oryzias]] is distinguished from [[Aplocheilus]] (=[[Panchax]]) of India by short jaws and absence of vomerine teeth, and from [[Zygonectes]] by the very long anal fin. The African genus [[Aplocheilichthys]] is noted as approaching [[Oryzias]] but remaining sufficiently distinct.

## Key Findings

1. **Two species documented**: [[Fundulichthys virescens]] and [[Oryzias latipes]] represent the complete killifish fauna of Japan known at time of publication.

2. **New genus established**: [[Oryzias]] Jordan and Snyder (new genus) erected to accommodate O. latipes, with type species [[Oryzias latipes]]. Generic diagnosis emphasizes elliptical compressed body, large scales, small mouth with 2 rows of simple pointed teeth, no vomerine teeth, unrestricted gill opening, short intestinal canal (approximately equal to body length), black peritoneum, short dorsal inserted above anal mid-point, very long anal (17-20 rays), and truncate caudal. Sexes similar except in coloration; anal not modified in males.

3. **Specimens collected 1900**: Authors collected specimens during 1900 fieldwork; material deposited in [[United States National Museum]] and [[Stanford University]] museum.

4. **[[Fundulichthys virescens]]** status uncertain: Species known only from Schlegel's 1846 description based on Bürger's drawing; not collected by authors; no subsequent descriptions available. Reported from brooks of southern Japan, particularly [[Nagasaki Bay]] area.

5. **[[Oryzias latipes]]** widely distributed: Collected from multiple localities including [[Wakanoura]], [[Kawatana]], and [[Tokyo]] area. Very common in mosquito-breeding waters throughout southern Japan.

6. **Sexual dimorphism in [[Oryzias latipes]]**: Females more slender posteriorly, with lower anal fins and longer ventrals; males with black ventrals and more robust posterior body. No intromittent organ modification observed.

7. **Ecological role**: Both species inhabit rice swamps, pools, and ditches; likely feed on mosquitoes based on habitat associations.

8. **Taxonomic relationships**: [[Fundulichthys]] differs from American [[Zygonectes]] in having small dorsal inserted wholly anterior to equally small anal; caudal fin lunate. [[Oryzias]] distinguished from [[Aplocheilus]] by short jaws and lack of vomerine teeth; from [[Zygonectes]] by very long anal fin; approaches but remains distinct from African [[Aplocheilichthys]].
