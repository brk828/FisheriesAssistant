# Biological Opinion on the Mimbres Resource Area Resource Management Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #tyrannidae #falconidae #cactaceae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) draft biological opinion, issued under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]], evaluates whether the [[Mimbres Resource Management Plan]] (RMP) of the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Mimbres Resource Area (MRA) is likely to jeopardize five listed species: [[Northern Aplomado Falcon]], [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[Sneed Pincushion Cactus]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Spikedace]]. The Service concludes that the RMP is **not likely to jeopardize** the aplomado falcon, Sneed pincushion cactus, loach minnow, and spikedace, but **is likely to jeopardize** the southwestern willow flycatcher. Reasonable and prudent alternatives, reasonable and prudent measures, and incidental take terms are issued for the flycatcher and the fishes, with the most consequential concern being [[Livestock Grazing]] in occupied or potential habitat without adequate monitoring.

## Background & Description

The consultation was initiated after a series of amended [[Biological Assessment]]s (BAs). The BLM requested concurrence on 23 species (12 endangered, 6 threatened, 2 proposed, 3 candidates). The Service concurred with 8 species on July 9, 1996, and later concurred with 7 of the 12 remaining species on September 10, 1996. Concurrence was withheld for the five species addressed here due to insufficient information, lack of protective measures, or disagreement with determinations. A draft opinion was sent to BLM on March 19, 1997, and a site visit to the [[Gila Middle Box]] ACEC was made on April 15, 1997, which led to revisions.

The MRA covers about 3 million surface acres and 4.1 million acres of federal subsurface minerals across Doña Ana, Luna, Hidalgo, and Grant counties. The RMP, governed by [[Federal Land Policy and Management Act]] (FLPMA) and [[National Environmental Policy Act]] (NEPA), guides 14 resource programs for up to 20 years. Key RMP decisions include:

- **Land ownership:** 156,460 acres of public land identified for disposal; 93,110 acres of State trust land and 56,210 acres of private land identified for potential acquisition.
- **ACECs:** 21 Areas of Critical Environmental Concern totaling about 183,000 acres, 15 of them (about 181,000 acres) designated for biological, special status species, or riparian concerns.
- **Vehicle use:** 16,190 acres open; 2,731,630 acres limited to existing roads and trails; 532,530 acres limited to designated roads and trails; 133,470 acres closed.
- **Minerals:** about 267,000 acres closed to fluid mineral leasing; about 64,000 acres closed to locatable entry; all ACECs and Research Natural Areas closed to saleable minerals.
- **Grazing:** 347 allotments, of which 196 (56%) are Category M (satisfactory), 38 (11%) Category C, and 113 (33%) Category I (unsatisfactory but improvable).
- **Wildlife:** 489 wildlife species (excluding invertebrates) recorded in the resource area.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Sneed Pincushion Cactus
This endangered cactus grows in limestone cracks in mountainous desert grassland, with a clumped, densely spined habit and brownish-pink flowers. Known from 12 sites in Texas and New Mexico, but the Service considers its range restricted to the [[Franklin Mountains]] and Bishop's Cap after reclassifying Carlsbad and Guadalupe records as hybrids. Three BLM populations (under 640 acres combined) lie within the [[Organ/Franklin Mountain ACEC]]. The main threats are illegal collection and urban growth in El Paso, with locatable mineral entry the key concern on BLM land (19 active mining claims, 294 abandoned).

### Spikedace and Loach Minnow
Both threatened fishes inhabit flowing water over sand, gravel, and cobble. Spikedace live about 2 years and spawn March through May; loach minnow are bottom dwellers that use substrate interstices and have a range reduced by about 85 percent. Both are isolated in fragments of the Gila basin. Critical habitat designations (March 8, 1994) were set aside following a Tenth Circuit Court decision. Key threats are [[Nonnative Fish Species]] (predation and competition), habitat degradation, and watershed alteration. The upper Gila River community includes at least 13 nonnative species, which predominate downstream of the Middle Box.

### Southwestern Willow Flycatcher
This endangered neotropical migrant breeds in dense riparian thickets, typically willow, with a canopy cover above 85 percent. Breeding has been documented in New Mexico, Arizona, and California, with about 420 known territories range-wide and an estimated maximum of 500. Brown-headed cowbird parasitism is a major threat, with rates of 50 to 80 percent recorded on the Kern River before trapping reduced them to under 10 percent.

### Northern Aplomado Falcon
Listed as endangered in 1986, this falcon historically bred in desert grasslands of southern New Mexico and Texas. The last known natural nest in the United States was near Deming in 1952. Recent sightings in New Mexico and west Texas since 1991 likely derive from a breeding population of 60 to 70 individuals in northern Chihuahua, Mexico. Threats include brush encroachment linked to heavy grazing, pesticide residues, and collection.

## Key Findings

**Jeopardy determinations**
- **Jeopardy (southwestern willow flycatcher):** At least two breeding pairs nest in the Gila Lower Box ACEC; the species has reached the jeopardy threshold range-wide; grazing on adjacent allotments may be feeding local cowbird populations; and the RMP allows grazing in occupied riparian habitat without adequate protection.
- **No jeopardy:** Sneed pincushion cactus, loach minnow, spikedace, and northern aplomado falcon, with conditions.

**Sneed pincushion cactus**
- Withdrawal of locatable mineral entry from the Organ/Franklin Mountain ACEC is recommended, and the RMP should be amended to include field protection measures.

**Spikedace and loach minnow**
- Livestock grazing in the Gila Middle Box ACEC is expected to cause incidental take through sedimentation, channel destabilization, and loss of habitat complexity. Adverse effects are likely because BLM has not monitored fish or habitat.
- The Middle Box area covers about 2.5 miles (840 acres), with two allotments totaling 17,025 acres of public land, 10,833 acres of private land, and 2,080 acres of State land.
- Reasonable and prudent measures call for managing allotments to minimize sedimentation, moving Category I and C allotments toward M, and surveying fish populations twice annually for 5 years in the Lower and Middle Box ACECs.

**Southwestern willow flycatcher**
- The April 1997 site visit confirmed no current breeding habitat in the Gila Middle Box ACEC, but the Gila Lower Box ACEC (about 900 acres of riparian habitat, at least two nesting pairs) is occupied.
- Reasonable and prudent alternatives include removing or defining the RMP's livestock-grazing language for occupied habitat; initiating cowbird trapping if parasitism exceeds 10 percent; prohibiting grazing in occupied or unsurveyed potential habitat from April 15 to August 15; and developing an interim management plan.
- Take is defined as cowbird parasitism of one or both pairs in a year, and take is exceeded after two consecutive parasitized years or abandonment of both pairs.

**Northern aplomado falcon**
- The Service found that BLM lacks data to determine whether current livestock management is hindering recovery, and that 47 allotments covering 950,843 acres of public land (32% of MRA surface area) contain potential habitat, all open to grazing.
- Trends were stable or improving on about 60% of relevant allotments and unknown on 40%.
- Measures require a habitat inventory with GIS and remote sensing, at least two breeding seasons of surveys, a comparison with occupied Chihuahuan habitat, and a five-year evaluation.

**Procedural notes**
- Plan-level consultation does not preclude site-specific consultations; the RMP itself is the subject here.
- Reinitiation is required if incidental take limits are exceeded, new information emerges, actions change, or new species are listed.
