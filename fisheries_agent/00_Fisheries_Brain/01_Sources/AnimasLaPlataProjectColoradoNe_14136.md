## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary morphological or ecological research paper, but an administrative memorandum and meeting summary from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) concerning the **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), an endangered cyprinid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The core subject is the reinitiation of Endangered Species Act **[[Section 7 Consultation]]** between the FWS and the **[[Bureau of Reclamation]]** regarding the **[[Animas-La Plata Project]]** in Colorado/New Mexico. The memo and attached meeting notes (dated January 31, 1990) establish that the **[[San Juan River]]** is biologically essential to the recovery of the Colorado squawfish in the Colorado River Basin, and that new survey data justify reopening consultation on a project previously evaluated in a December 28, 1979 biological opinion.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat association**: The [[San Juan River]] is identified as biologically important and "an integral and essential link" for the recovery of the Colorado squawfish within the broader [[Colorado River Basin]] ecosystem.
- **Range context**: The species' recovery is tied to connectivity between the **Upper Basin** river systems and the San Juan River, implying a life-history dependence on large-river, main-stem habitats — consistent with known Colorado squawfish ecology as a large-bodied, migratory, pelagic-spawning cyprinid requiring long, unfragmented river reaches.
- **Threats/pressures**: The [[Animas-La Plata Project]] (a Bureau of Reclamation water development project) is identified as having potential effects on the squawfish and its habitat that were not adequately considered in the original 1979 biological opinion, implying concerns over flow alteration, habitat fragmentation, or water depletion in the San Juan/Animas/La Plata river systems.
- **Recovery planning**: The Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team was to be consulted to confirm and formally incorporate the San Juan River's essential status into the draft **[[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan]]**.

## Key Findings
- The FWS Region 6 (Denver) is reinitiating Section 7 consultation with the Bureau of Reclamation on the Animas-La Plata Project due to new information on effects to the endangered Colorado squawfish.
- A joint Region 6 / Region 2 meeting (Albuquerque, NM; January 31, 1990) concluded:
  - The **San Juan River** is essential to Colorado squawfish recovery and should be formally included in the recovery plan.
  - Newly completed **San Juan River fishery surveys** (along with Upper Basin data) provide sufficient new information to warrant reinitiating consultation.
  - This new information indicates potential project effects on the squawfish not addressed in the original **December 28, 1979 biological opinion**.
  - FWS had requested Bureau reconsultation in **1985** and **1986**, but agreed to delay until San Juan River surveys were completed; those surveys are now complete.
  - Region 6 had been urging reinitiation since **June 1989**, but the Bureau delayed despite having construction funding and planning a **groundbreaking ceremony in May 1990**.
  - The FWS is prepared to unilaterally request reinitiation and begin drafting a new **biological opinion** if the Bureau does not act promptly.
- The Service prioritized completing consultation **before** any irreversible or irretrievable commitment of resources to construction, reflecting a procedural safeguard under the [[Endangered Species Act]].
- Attendees represented FWS Region 6, Region 2, and the Albuquerque Field Office, reflecting interagency coordination on the [[Colorado Squawfish Recovery Team]] effort.
