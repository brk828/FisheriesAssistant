# Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee Minutes, February 21, 1989

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

These are draft minutes of the February 21, 1989 meeting of the [[Implementation Committee]] that oversees the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the Upper Colorado River Basin. The Program is a 15-year, consensus-based effort to recover four fish species while allowing water development to continue under [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7. The meeting covered the FY-89 work plan and budget shortfall, FY-90 funding requests, the use of [[Windy Gap Funds]], a draft cooperative agreement with the [[National Fish and Wildlife Foundation]], [[Section 7 Consultation]] cases, sediment and "sufficient progress" policy, instream flow work on the [[Yampa River]] and [[15-Mile Reach]], and water rights acquisition. Most items were resolved or delegated for further work. Instream flow protection and the basin's approach to sediment impacts remained contentious.

## Background & Description

The Program was created under a Cooperative Agreement signed January 21–22, 1988 by the Secretary of the Interior, the Western Area Power Administration Administrator, and the governors of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming. The Recovery Program document is dated September 29, 1987. Its purpose is to let federal water development in the Upper Basin proceed without jeopardizing the continued existence of the listed fishes.

The target species are:

- [[Colorado Squawfish]] (federally endangered)
- [[Humpback Chub]] (federally endangered)
- [[Bonytail Chub]] (federally endangered)
- [[Razorback Sucker]] (candidate for listing; the minutes note it has no recovery plan)

Program funding is estimated at $58.5 million over 15 years. Water project proponents are expected to supply about $9–10 million through a one-time [[Depletion Charge]] of $10 per acre-foot of average annual depletion. The Program is governed by the Implementation Committee, with a Management Group and a Technical Group supporting it. Participants include the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the states, water users, and environmental groups.

Environmental organizations that submitted supporting resolutions included the National Audubon Society, the Environmental Defense Fund, and the Colorado Wildlife Federation. All three conditioned support on the same points: no-jeopardy opinions should not be issued until water rights or reservoir operations adequate for recovery are secured, and hatchery funding should depend on proven need and genetic safeguards. Trout Unlimited conditioned its support on resolution of the [[Ruedi Reservoir]] trout fishery issue.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The minutes focus on managing river flows, sediment, and habitat for the endangered fishes rather than on fish biology. Relevant points include:

- **Instream flows:** Flow recommendations for the [[Yampa River]], [[15-Mile Reach]], and the Colorado River below the Gunnison confluence. Concerns were raised about how much proof is needed to support instream flow recommendations.
- **Sediment:** Whether reduced sediment from water projects counts as a depletion impact. This question affects habitat and offsetting obligations.
- **Dams and fragmentation:** [[Taylor Draw Dam]] impounded the White River in 1984. The recovery team says the lower 240 km of the White River may still be important for recovery, and it recommends either restoring passage or presenting data showing the loss will not impair recovery.
- **San Juan River:** The minutes cite the San Juan as supporting one of only three remaining natural [[Colorado Squawfish]] populations. The San Juan between Shiprock and Lake Powell is to be treated as a high-priority recovery area.
- **Flaming Gorge operations:** Dam operations affect the [[Flaming Gorge Operations]] biological opinion, which controls timing for several research and flow studies.
- **Drought:** Drought in 1988 changed Flaming Gorge operations and delayed research studies.

## Key Findings

### Budget and work plan

- The FY-89 work plan approved in August 1988 totaled **$2,303.2K** across 39 projects. The final request exceeded available funds by about **$268,000**, mainly because of higher project requests and a shortfall in Service cash contributions.
- The Management and Technical Groups recommended a revised FY-89 total of **$2,324.7K**. Five projects were deferred: habitat development options ($10.0K), bonytail retrieval ($2.0K), exotic fish management ($10.0K), establishing recovery goals ($30.0K), and the razorback management plan ($10.0K).
- The Committee decided that any surplus FY-89 funds would go first to the razorback management plan and second to recovery goals. Otherwise both move to FY-90 priority.
- Key budget increases: FWS program management rose from $70.0K to $157.0K. Yampa River flow recommendations rose from $72.5K to $140.5K. The Little Snake sediment study rose from $23.0K to $44.6K. The Colorado River flow recommendations were cut from $176.5K to $133.0K. Gila taxonomy was reduced from $90K to $53K.
- A [[Fish Passage]] issue paper was funded at $12.0K.
- The Committee accepted a FY-90 budget increase of **4.1%** based on 1988 Consumer Price Index data. The adjustment takes effect October 1, 1989.

- Congress had appropriated **$1 million** for water rights acquisition.

### Windy Gap Funds

- Nearly 60 biological opinions from 1981 to January 1988 generated the Windy Gap fund. Of this, **$762,000** has been spent on recovery activities, and **$553,000** remains available.
- About **$2,980,000** is outstanding. Payment is due when depletion begins, and much of it may never be collected.
- The Service keeps final authority over the use of discretionary funds and does not want them used to cover shortfalls. They are separate from the Service's $600,000/year contribution, and Two Forks/Williams Fork funds are not Windy Gap funds.

### Cooperative agreement with the National Fish and Wildlife Foundation

- The Foundation would be reimbursed for expenses capped at **2%** of annual funds received.
- Three versions were drafted, differing in which funds transfer to the Foundation: (1) only new contributed funds, (2) new and existing contributed funds, and (3) all funds including congressional appropriations.

### Section 7 consultations

- The February 21, 1989 table lists **17** consultations (8 opinions issued and 9 pending). Pending projects include [[Sandstone Reservoir]] (30,600 AF), [[Muddy Creek]] (10,000 AF), and Price-San Rafael (21,550 AF).
- The Service will try to negotiate offsetting measures for the Sandstone Reservoir Project with Wyoming before the Management Group steps in.

### Sediment and "sufficient progress"

- The Management Group met February 9, 1989 and concluded that sediment impacts are a depletion impact. Most members agreed that this applies in spirit even though the Program document does not explicitly address it.
- The Committee adopted clarifying language treating sediment effects as depletion impacts. It also established that the Service decides case by case whether "sufficient progress" has been or is being made. The Management Group was directed to develop a process for ensuring depletion impacts are addressed.
- Open questions include whether progress in one basin region can offset impacts elsewhere, and what criteria define sufficiency.

### San Juan River and Grand Canyon

- Reclamation will fund New Mexico's San Juan studies through FY-90 at no more than **$20,000**. A draft management plan is due by the end of FY-89, and the final plan is deferred to September 30, 1989.
- The Committee was split on whether to credit Recovery Program funds spent on [[Humpback Chub]] conservation in the Grand Canyon against Reclamation's **$1.5 million/year** contribution. The Service and Reclamation will report back.

### Instream flows and water rights

- **Yampa River:** The biological report is due May 1, 1989. Provisional flow estimates will be developed by the end of FY-89 if they do not interfere with the Flaming Gorge schedule. Formal flow recommendations are deferred to FY-90.
- **15-Mile Reach:** The flow report for July–September is due May 1, 1989. A Reclamation appraisal of instream flow options is estimated at $62,000.
- **Little Snake River:** Wyoming, The Nature Conservancy, and appropriators are discussing large conditional water rights. An RFP is planned at no more than $50,000.
- **Ruedi/Green Mountain releases:** Releases will be delivered to the Orchard Mesa power plant at the head of the 15-mile reach. A Reclamation contract with water users is expected within months.
- **Juniper-Cross Mountain rights:** The Colorado River Water Conservation District will look for alternative small reservoir sites in the Yampa basin that could replace these projects.
- **Lease vs. purchase:** The Service prefers permanent legal protection through appropriation or fee purchase. It is skeptical of leases, which may be hard to fund and would not offer permanent protection. Local interests prefer a 50-year lease with renewal option.
- **Colorado Water Conservation Board:** Chairman [[Bill McDonald]] is reluctant to grant instream flow rights piecemeal before all instream needs are known. The issue was not resolved.

### Other decisions

- The Committee adopted a resolution honoring Clifford I. Barrett, Regional Director of the Upper Colorado Region, Reclamation, who is leaving federal service.
- Tom Pitts argued that the draft [[Colorado Squawfish]] recovery goals conflict with Program goals on essential reaches and fragmentation. The Service agreed not to finalize the plan until the Management Group reviews the issue.
- The Uintah Mountain Club and National Wildlife Federation lawsuits were not discussed for lack of time.
- Reclamation will arrange a July or August 1989 rafting trip for up to 25 participants, from Moab through Cataract Canyon to Lake Powell.
- The next Implementation Committee meeting is **August 30, 1989**.
