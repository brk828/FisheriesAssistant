# Review of Draft Final Report on Spikedace and Red Shiner Interactions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a July 19, 1989 review letter from [[David L. Propst]], Endangered Species Biologist with the [[New Mexico Department of Game and Fish]], to Dr. Dean A. Hendrickson of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (Nongame Branch). It evaluates a draft final report by Marsh et al. on interactions between the threatened [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) and the introduced [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*). The study was contracted between [[Arizona State University]], the Arizona Game and Fish Department, and the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish.

The reviewer concludes that the report does not demonstrate that red shiner has displaced spikedace. The main objections are:

- No consideration of [[Habitat Availability]], so shifts in [[Microhabitat Use]] may reflect availability rather than competition.
- Use of the term "[[Habitat Preference]]" without a comparison of available and used habitat.
- Interactions recorded during red shiner spawning in August were used as evidence despite the authors' own caveats.
- Conclusions appear to have been formed before the data were analysed.
- Weak integration with the [[Interim Report]] (Douglas et al. 1988), so the final report does not "stand alone."

The letter also lists editorial problems, including unclear pronoun antecedents and insufficient tables and figures, and recommends a site map and microhabitat tables.

## Background & Description

**Document type:** Formal correspondence (letter with page-by-page attached comments), copied to John P. Hubbard.

**Subject study:** The draft final report, "Interactions between threatened spikedace (*Meda fulgida*) and introduced red shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*)," was submitted by Marsh et al. under a multi-agency contract.

**Species of concern:**
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*): described as threatened. The letter refers to its declining populations and range.
- [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*): described as introduced. It now occurs in several areas where spikedace and [[Loach Minnow]] formerly occurred.
- Loach minnow: a native species discussed alongside spikedace. The document does not give its scientific name.

**Study sites named in the document:**
- [[Sycamore Creek]], whose habitat is probably different from other sites.
- [[Redrock Valley]], where spikedace occurrence is irregular and habitat differs from other sites.
- [[Cliff-Gila Valley]], used as a comparison with Redrock Valley.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The review centres on three ecological variables:

- **Microhabitat:** [[Current Velocity]] was the main variable analysed. The reviewer argues that [[Water Depth]] and [[Substrate]] should also be considered, and that these variables should be examined in combination.
- **Habitat availability versus use:** Preference can only be inferred when used habitat is compared with available habitat. The reviewer says this comparison was missing, and that differences in use among streams cannot be interpreted without it.
- **Reproductive timing:** Red shiner spawn in August. [[Spawning Behavior]] and [[Agonistic Behavior]] of red shiner may produce interactions with spikedace that are not evidence of competition.

The reviewer also notes that field and laboratory results appear to contradict each other. The report's artificial-stream velocities were not shown to match the velocities used in nature, which further limits comparison.

## Key Findings

**1. Causation is not demonstrated.** The reviewer states that the report does not show that red shiner caused the decline of spikedace and loach minnow. The only firm fact is that red shiner now occurs where these natives formerly lived. The reviewer states that this "does not mean that red shiner 'displaced' either native." (Page 2.)

**2. Apparent interactions are undefined.** The report refers to "apparent interactions" but also states that research "has yet to demonstrate the specific process" of decline. The reviewer asks how both claims can be true. (Page 2.)

**3. Habitat availability was ignored.** The reviewer considers this the report's major flaw, particularly when streams are compared. Shifts in spikedace habitat use may reflect availability. Measures of habitat differences among streams are needed. (Pages 1–4.)

**4. "Preference" was not demonstrated.** Only use was documented. Preference requires comparison with what is available, and this comparison was not made. (Pages 1–2, 4.)

**5. August observations are unreliable.** Red shiner spawning in August produces agonistic behaviour, so interactions observed in that month should be "largely, if not completely disregarded." The authors used these interactions as support anyway. (Pages 1–2, 10, 13.)

**6. Inconsistencies in the report.** The reviewer lists four problems: displacement was noted only in August, during red shiner spawning; availability was not accounted for; artificial-stream velocities may not match natural conditions; and "preferred" habitat was claimed without evidence. (Page 13.)

**7. Redrock Valley needs special treatment.** Irregular spikedace occurrence and different habitat must be considered in analysis. (Page 4.)

**8. Summary section is inadequate.** The reviewer asks for mean or median velocities, with ranges, for each species in each stream, and for depth and substrate. (Page 5.)

**9. Interpretation should use combined variables.** The reviewer recommends examining velocity, depth, and substrate together, not velocity alone. (Page 19.)

**10. Editorial and structural requirements.** The reviewer requests:
- Clearer writing, with unambiguous pronoun antecedents.
- A map of study sites.
- Tables of microhabitat data.
- A final report that does not depend on the interim report for its key data.

**11. Overall verdict.** The reviewer agrees that interactions between spikedace and red shiner may occur. However, the authors "formed their conclusion first and then tried to make the data fit." The reviewer agrees with the report's own statement that it "remains to be demonstrated whether or not declines of spikedace populations are a result of red shiner presence." (Page 2.)
