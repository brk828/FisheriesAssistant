# Report on PRT-676811 Activity (Dec 1991 – Nov 1992)

## Executive Summary

This annual report, submitted by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] as a subpermittee under [[USFWS]] Blanket Permit PRT-676811, documents monitoring, research, propagation, and salvage activities conducted on numerous federally listed threatened and endangered fish species across Arizona during 1991–1992. Species-specific activities ranged from population monitoring and voucher specimen collection to captive propagation, radio-telemetry studies, and interagency genetic research. Key taxa include large-river cyprinids of the Colorado River Basin (e.g., [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]]), imperiled catostomids ([[Razorback Sucker]]), small-bodied desert cyprinids ([[Spikedace]], [[Loachminnow]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]]), livebearers ([[Gila Topminnow]]), and two salmonid trout species native to Arizona mountain streams ([[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]]). While several species saw active management (stocking, salvage, spawning), others (e.g., [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Quitobaquito Pupfish]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Woundfin]], [[Gila Trout]]) had no recorded take or possession during the reporting period.

## Background & Description

The report catalogs permit-authorized activities involving federally protected fish species in Arizona, conducted primarily by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) Research Branch, in cooperation with the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[Northern Arizona University]] (NAU), [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (DNFH), [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]] (WCNFH), and state hatcheries including [[Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery]] (BPSH), [[Sterling Springs State Hatchery]] (SSSH), [[Page Springs State Hatchery]] (PSSH), and [[Tonto Creek State Hatchery]] (TCSH).

Activities documented include:
- Population monitoring via electrofishing and trammel netting
- Larval and juvenile collection for ex-situ research
- Radio-telemetry implantation and tracking
- Captive propagation, spawning, and stocking
- Fish salvage from isolated or drying habitats
- Voucher specimen preservation for taxonomic/genetic studies
- Disease management (tapeworm control, ich infection response)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Activities spanned multiple river basins and isolated aquatic habitats critical to native Arizona fish conservation:

- **Verde River** – stocking site for [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]; voucher collection site for [[Spikedace]]
- **Lake Mohave** (Arizona Bay, Yuma Cove, Three Mile Flat) – key wild source population for [[Razorback Sucker]] larvae, juveniles, and adults
- **Little Colorado River (LCR)** – primary spawning and monitoring site for [[Humpback Chub]]
- **Colorado River, Grand Canyon** – secondary monitoring site for [[Humpback Chub]]
- **Aravaipa Creek** (Graham/Pinal Cos.) – stronghold monitoring site for [[Spikedace]] and [[Loachminnow]]
- **Palm Lake, Hassayampa River Preserve** – isolated population and salvage site for [[Bonytail Chub]] and monitoring site for [[Colorado Squawfish]]
- **Sycamore Creek** (Santa Cruz Co.) – collection site for [[Sonoran Chub]]
- **Rudd Creek, Nutrioso Creek, East Clear Creek, Chevelon Creek** (Apache/Navajo/Coconino Cos.) – native range monitoring and research sites for [[Little Colorado Spinedace]]
- **Sonoita Creek, Cienega Creek, Monkey Spring, Redrock Canyon, Tule Creek, and associated springs** – multiple isolated spring/stream refugia for [[Gila Topminnow]]
- **Campbell Blue Creek and Blue River** (Greenlee Co.) – genetic research sites for [[Loachminnow]]

These systems represent a mix of mainstem large-river habitat (Colorado, Verde), isolated spring/pond refugia (critical for topminnow and pupfish persistence), and montane headwater streams (trout habitat), reflecting the fragmented and imperiled status of Arizona's native fish fauna. Disease pressures noted include tapeworm infestation in squawfish broodstock and an ich outbreak causing mortality in captive [[Humpback Chub]] broodstock.

## Key Findings

### Colorado Squawfish (*Ptychocheilus lucius*)
- 1 fish captured, measured, and released at Palm Lake monitoring (Oct 27)
- 15 radio-tagged fish from BPSH stocked into upper Verde River; 3 recaptured and released
- BPSH moved 301 adults for tapeworm control; 6 mortalities

### Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- 318 larvae taken from Lake Mohave (Feb 4–Mar 26) to ASU for research
- 58 juveniles taken from Lake Mohave (Apr 8–Jun 22) to ASU for research
- 8 adults trammel netted, measured, released at Three Mile Flat (Mar 15–21) semiannual monitoring
- 16 adults (506–662 mm TL, 1.6–3.5 kg) sampled during June electrofishing on Lake Mohave
- 894 broodstock and 100,000 fry received at BPSH from DNFH; 138 mortalities
- 261 fish stocked into Verde River near Perkinsville (12–14 radio-tagged)

### Gila Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*)
- 563 total voucher specimens preserved across 13 sites, including Fresno Canyon/Sonoita Creek (88), Fresno Canyon (132), Cienega Creek (69), Sonoita Creek (42), Redrock Canyon (44), Walnut Spring (56), and others

### Little Colorado Spinedace (*Lepidomeda vittata*)
- 50 captured/released, 15 preserved as vouchers, Rudd Creek (White Mountain Hereford Ranch)
- Additional research removals: 53 (Nutrioso Creek, NAU), 71 (Rudd Creek, NAU), 27 (East Clear Creek), and fish from Chevelon Creek (ASU)
- 45 transplanted to Flagstaff Arboretum; remainder sacrificed or used in predation experiments

### Sonoran Chub (*Gila ditaenia*)
- 200 specimens collected from Sycamore Creek (May 22) for AGFD research

### Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*)
- Fish salvage at Palm Lake (Hassayampa River Preserve, Apr 21): 20 adults to BPSH, 2 to Visitor Center aquarium, 9 natural mortalities preserved at ASU
- 18 surviving BPSH bonytails relocated to ASU Research Park (Jul 24)
- 32 bonytails trammel netted and released at Palm Lake during routine monitoring (Oct 27)

### Humpback Chub (*Gila cypha*)
- 95 chubs handled during spawning operations in Little Colorado River (LCR); released unharmed
- 10 adults removed to BPSH died from ich infection
- 1,724 chubs >29 mm TL captured in LCR; 68 preserved (incl. 7 net mortalities) for otolith/diet studies; 2 additional dead fish preserved
- 122 individuals >29 mm TL collected from Colorado River, Grand Canyon; 5 preserved for otolith/diet analysis
- Take of individuals <29 mm TL unrestricted by permit; no formal count maintained

### Spikedace (*Meda fulgida*)
- 2,881 individuals captured/released during Aravaipa Creek monitoring
- 5 individuals collected from Verde River as vouchers

### Loachminnow (*Tiaroga cobitis*)
- 863 individuals captured/released during Aravaipa Creek monitoring
- 15 from Campbell Blue Creek and 12 from Blue River sent to ASU for genetic analysis

### Apache Trout (*Oncorhynchus apache*)
- 33,883 eggs (Lot AZT2WCF1P) received at SSSH from WCNFH; 7,240 surviving fingerlings transferred to PSSH; growth rate 0.315 in/month at SSSH
- TCSH received Lot 2WCF1P eggs from WCNFH; hatch rate ~15% (14,000 fry); fry-stage mortality 62.4%
- 5,264 surviving fish (16 lbs total, 332/lb), averaging 2.0 in TL by early November; growth rate 0.325 in/month

### No Take/Possession Recorded (1991–92 reporting period)
- Yaqui Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis sonoriensis*)
- Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius macularius*)
- Quitobaquito Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius eremus*)
- Beautiful Shiner (*Cyprinella formosa*)
- Yaqui Chub (*Gila purpurea*)
- Yaqui Catfish (*Ictalurus pricei*)
- Woundfin (*Plagopterus argentissimus*)
- Gila Trout (*Oncorhynchus gilae*)
