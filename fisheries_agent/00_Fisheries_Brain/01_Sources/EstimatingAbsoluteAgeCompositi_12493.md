# Estimating Absolute Age Composition of California Salmon Landings

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This Fish Bulletin (No. 120, California Department of Fish and Game, 1963) by [[Joseph H. Kutkuhn]] develops and evaluates statistical methods for estimating the absolute age structure of [[King Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) landings off California, using scale-annuli age data collected in 1952–1957. The central tool is the [[Two-Phase Sampling]] (double sampling for stratification) estimator, in which a length sample is measured first and a random subsample is aged and stratified by length. The author compares it with [[Simple Random Sampling]], the [[Age-Length Key]] approach, and a two-way [[Port-Month Cluster]] estimator. The paper's main conclusion is that, for salmon with only a few age groups, the simpler direct method is generally preferable unless age determination costs at least five times length measurement, and that about 200 aged scales per port-month, with an average cluster size near 250, suffice for precise (CV ≈ 10%) total estimates.

## Background & Description

- **Study species:** [[King Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*), the primary focus, and [[Silver Salmon]] (*O. kisutch*), which share the same coastal fishing grounds. Silver salmon are named in the distribution section; the age analysis concerns king salmon.
- **Origin of data:** The parent study evolved from a 1949 large-scale salmon marking program run by the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] with the [[Pacific Marine Fisheries Commission]]. Length and age were sampled incidentally alongside marked-fish recovery. Supplementary data came from the [[Ocean Salmon Study]] (Dingell-Johnson Project F-12-R) on the sport catch.
- **Fishery:** Both sport and commercial fleets take salmon by trolling in coastal waters, covering roughly 12,000 square nautical miles. Trips rarely exceed 12 hours. Major landing ports include Avila, Morro Bay, Monterey, Moss Landing, Santa Cruz, Princeton, San Francisco, Point Reyes, Bodega Bay, Fort Bragg, Eureka, and Crescent City.
- **Regulations shaping the sample:** Commercial king salmon had to be at least 26 inches (about 66 cm) overall length; sport fish at least 22 inches. Prior to 1955, sport anglers could keep a limited number of smaller fish. These size limits make the landings unrepresentative of the full population.
- **Age determination:** Mid-lateral scales were mounted dry and read independently by at least two readers, with a third resolving disagreements. Among 15,000–18,000 scales, disagreements were under 2%. Ages were counted as effective annuli, with overlooked "ocean-type" false winter marks excluded so that ages correspond to brood year.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The paper treats the ocean catch as a mixture of subpopulations that cannot be defined or separated in the fishery, which limits its value for stock dynamics. The author notes that:

- Only **3- and 4-year-old** fish consistently make up the bulk of California landings. Using year-class strength to forecast a short-term yield is therefore questionable.
- Fishing effort can be measured only as troller-days with a marketable yield, which is a weak index of abundance.
- Fishing effort is strongly shaped by weather, markets, and price disputes. Fishing is largely confined to areas near home ports, so port-based sampling captures most landings.
- Gear selectivity and differing availability and vulnerability of subpopulations may bias the sampled parent population in ways that cannot be measured.
- Full prediction would require offshore research-vessel sampling of pre-recruitment and recruited phases.

The author frames the work as one tool within a "modern" fishery-management framework for predicting sustainable yield, not as a complete population model.

## Key Findings

1. **Two-phase estimator outperforms simple random sampling only modestly.** Its variance is usually lower, but the gain is often small. In the July 1955 Crescent City–Eureka sport sample (n = 2,875 measured; m = 130 aged), the 3-year-old proportion was 0.62815 (variance 0.001140), compared with [[Simple Random Sampling]] estimates in the same data.
2. **Cost ratio decides the method.** The two-phase design is worthwhile only when age determination costs at least about five times length measurement. Age was roughly ten times as costly as length, so the stratified design was judged justified only under those conditions. For salmon, the direct method was recommended as simpler and cheaper otherwise.
3. **Sample size guidance.** For ±15% precision on the proportion of 3-year-olds in the July 1955 sport sample, the calculation gave m ≈ 43. Tighter ±10% and ±5% targets required about 30 and 123 fish respectively for that proportion. Overall, the author recommends at least **200 aged scales per port-month** for reasonably precise estimates. Not more than ±20% error was required in most cases.
4. **Stratum width.** Five-centimeter minimum strata were recommended, with an average of about eight effective strata in commercial landings. Reducing from 2.5 cm to 10 cm strata gave little loss in precision for the principal age groups.
5. **1952 commercial landings (Table 4).** Estimated total landed was **474,620** fish. Age proportions were: age 2 = 0.0007; age 3 = 0.6000 (284,770 fish); age 4 = 0.3681 (174,710 fish); age 5 = 0.0312 (14,810 fish). The 95% intervals are reported in the table.
6. **1955 commercial landings (Table 6).** Estimated total was **747,252** fish. Age proportions were: age 2 = 0.0076 (5,679); age 3 = 0.5265 (393,428); age 4 = 0.4353 (325,279); age 5 = 0.0306 (22,866). The two-way estimator was corrected for cluster effects, and all four estimators in Table 5 agreed closely.
7. **Two-way cluster estimator.** Accounting for both [[Port Area]] and month effects, the author found that an average cluster size of about 250 age determinations gives total estimates with a CV near 10%. The actual 1955 mean cluster size was 164. That was adequate for 3- and 4-year-olds but inadequate for 2- and 5-year-olds, which would have needed about 3,290 and 260 fish respectively.
8. **Recommended design.** The author recommends the simpler direct age-sampling approach for most future programs, with the two-phase method reserved for cases where age processing is much costlier than length measurement. Reallocating effort from low-variability ports to larger samples in the remaining ports could improve precision without extra total effort.
9. **Historical context.** The bulletin is one of a long series of California Department of Fish and Game Fish Bulletins (Nos. 1–120) on fish catch statistics, sardines, tunas, rockfishes, and salmon life histories.
