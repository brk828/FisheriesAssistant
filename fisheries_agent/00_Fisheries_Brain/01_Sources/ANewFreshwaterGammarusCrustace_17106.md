# *Gammarus pecos*: A New Freshwater Amphipod from Western Texas

#crustacea #amphipoda #gammarus

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *Gammarus pecos*, a new species of freshwater amphipod crustacean from a spring and springbrook in the [[Pecos River]] drainage near [[Fort Stockton]], Pecos County, Texas. The species was collected from 1964 to 1968, largely through the efforts of [[W. L. Minckley]] of [[Arizona State University]]. It is conspicuously banded, reddish or greenish in colour, and resembles both [[Gammarus Bousfieldi]] and the [[Gammarus Fasciatus]]–[[Gammarus Tigrinus]] complex. Its subgeneric placement is uncertain, so the authors deferred a full phylogenetic assessment to later work.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** [[Willbank Spring]] is a subcircular [[Limnocrene]], about 30 m in diameter and 6 m deep, at roughly 880 m above sea level, 13.6 km north and 5.6 km west of the centre of Fort Stockton. It is the source of [[Leon Creek]], a tributary of the Pecos River, about 265 km upstream from the confluence of the Pecos and Rio Grande. Amphipods are abundant under stones at the spring outlet and have also been collected several times from *Chara* and grasses where Leon Creek is bridged by Highway 18 (863 m a.s.l.; 16.3 km north of Fort Stockton).

**Diagnosis.** The species has an elongate-reniform eye, an elongate antennal flagellum on antenna 1, and a richly setose antenna 2 that lacks calceoli in mature males. Other diagnostic traits include a broad mandibular palp segment 3, several long anteroventral setae on coxal plates 1 and 2, dorsolateral spine pairs on urosome segments 1 and 2, and dense setal and spine clusters on peraeopods 3 to 5.

**Size and sexual dimorphism.**
- Males: 12–14.9 mm. The holotype is a male of 14.3 mm.
- Females: 9–11 mm. Females are smaller and more compact, with shorter, weaker and less setose antenna 2, weakly developed gnathopod propodi, shorter peraeopods, shorter uropod 3, and a less elongate eye. Ovigerous females bear brood plates on pereonite segments 2–5.

**Selected morphological counts (males, as reported):**
- Antenna 1 flagellum: up to 40 segments; accessory flagellum 4–5 segmented.
- Antenna 2 flagellum: 12–15 richly setose segments.
- Maxilla 1, inner plate: 15–17 plumose marginal setae.
- Gnathopod 1 propodus: one stout truncate spine-tooth near the middle, with 7–10 smaller spines near the posterior angle.
- Gnathopod 2 propodus: one truncate spine-tooth and 6–7 shorter spines; 7–9 groups of marginal setae posteriorly.
- Pleopods: inner rami average 19, 18 and 17 segments (pleopods 1–3); outer rami average 22, 21 and 19 segments.
- Uropod 3: inner ramus 80–87% of the length of the outer ramus.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Temperature.** Water at Willbank Spring is usually about 20 °C. Leon Creek at the highway crossing is seasonal, reaching 28 °C on 6 August 1967 and 4–5 °C on 26 December 1966.

**Water chemistry.** A full chemical analysis was not carried out. The spring water is described as highly mineralised, with a filtrable residue of 6.65 g/litre.

**Habitat associations.** Specimens occur under stones at the spring outlet and among *Chara* and grasses in the creek. The authors describe the habitat as spring-fed and high in dissolved solids.

**Relevant comparison species.** [[Gammarus Bousfieldi]] is known only from one stream in Kentucky. [[Gammarus Fasciatus]] occurs mainly in large rivers and [[Gammarus Tigrinus]] mainly in estuaries along the American Atlantic coast. The authors note that other undescribed gammarids related to *G. pecos* occur in the Pecos River system.

## Key Findings

1. **A new species is described.** *Gammarus pecos* is named from the Pecos River drainage. The holotype is a male of 14.3 mm (U.S. National Museum, U.S.N.M. 123821). A female paratype of 11 mm (U.S.N.M. 123822) is also deposited there. Further paratype series went to the [[U.S. National Museum]] and the [[National Museum of Canada]].

2. **Relationships are mixed.** The species shares many features with *G. bousfieldi*, including the shape of the interantennal lobe, two distal spines on peduncular segment 1 of antenna 1, the mandibular palp, the maxilliped, the concave palmar margin of gnathopod 2, and the free lobes of the bases of peraeopods 4 and 5. It also shares similarities with the *G. fasciatus*–*G. tigrinus* complex, including setose antenna 2, setose coxal plates, and setose peraeopods.

3. **Distinguishing characters from *G. bousfieldi*.** *G. pecos* has strongly setose coxal plates, no antennal calceoli in males, a dorsally unhumped urosome, and a spinose lower margin of epimeron 3.

4. **Distinctness from typical Rivulogammarus.** The interantennal lobe is sharply rounded, the gnathopod propodus is narrower with a deeply concave palmar margin, and the free lobe of the bases of peraeopods 4 and 5 bears spines or setae. The authors consider the absence of calceoli not diagnostic at subgeneric level, because some mature *Rivulogammarus* males in other basins also lack them during reproduction.

5. **Subgeneric status is unresolved.** The authors judged that the affinities cannot yet be clearly assigned to either subgenus. They deferred a full taxonomic and phylogenetic assessment of the *G. pecos*–*G. bousfieldi* complex to a later study. They reinforced an earlier suggestion that this complex may link the subgenera *Gammarus* and *Rivulogammarus*.
