# Cichlasoma loisellei — New Nandopsis-Group Cichlid from Central America

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cichlidae #Nandopsis #CentralAmerica #FreshwaterFish #Cichlasoma

## Executive Summary

This paper formally describes **[[Cichlasoma loisellei]]**, a large-mouthed "guapote" cichlid previously misidentified as a southern population of **[[Cichlasoma friedrichsthalii]]**. The species was named in honor of cichlid specialist Dr. Paul V. Loiselle. Bussing distinguishes *C. loisellei* from true *C. friedrichsthalii* primarily by adult coloration — a **broad continuous black lateral stripe** in *C. loisellei* versus **distinct vertical black bars** (six to twelve) in *C. friedrichsthalii*. The two species also show a geographic split: *C. loisellei* occupies southern Central America (from the Río Negro, Honduras, to western Panama, plus Pacific-slope Nicaragua and Costa Rica), while true *C. friedrichsthalii* is restricted to Belize, Guatemala (Petén), and southern Mexico. The description resolves decades of taxonomic confusion among the "guapote" cichlids of the **[[Nandopsis]]** section, which also includes *C. dovii*, *C. managuense*, and *C. motaguense*.

## Background & Description

### Taxonomic History
- First recorded in literature (misidentified) as *Cichlasoma friedrichsthalii* by Meek (1914) from Costa Rica.
- Confusion arose from the strong meristic/proportional similarity among the four large-mouthed "guapote" species and their variable color phases (sex, age, disposition, locality).
- Regan's keys (1905; 1906-08) to *C. friedrichsthalii*, *motaguense*, *managuense*, and *dovii* were based on too few specimens to be reliable.
- Moya (1979) validated all four species but did not recognize *C. loisellei* as distinct.
- Regan (1905) erected subgenus *Parapetenia* for large-jawed *Cichlasoma* with enlarged canines; Kullander (1983) showed **[[Nandopsis]]** Gill 1862 has priority over *Parapetenia* (same type species).

### Holotype & Type Locality
- **Holotype:** LACM 44405-1, adult male, 123.0 mm SL.
- **Locality:** Río San Miguel, tributary of Río Matina, road to Puerto Limón (elev. 12 m), Limón Province, Costa Rica.
- Collected 30 Sep. 1977 by W. Bussing, Klaus Gocke, and ichthyology students (orig. field no. UCR 1126-8).
- Deposited across ANSP, LACM, UF/FSU, UMMZ, UCR, and USNM.

### Diagnosis
A large species of *Cichlasoma* in the canine-toothed "guapote" group of the **[[Nandopsis]]** section, distinguished by:
- Long maxillary reaching the anterior margin of the eye.
- Well-developed canines.
- Dorsal fin: **XVI–XVIII spines, 9–12 rays** (modally XVII,10).
- Anal fin: **VII–IX spines, 7–10 rays** (modally IX,9).
- Usually 8–9 anal spines (vs. 6–7 in *C. dovii*, *managuense*, *motaguense*).
- Lacks the preopercular lobe seen in *C. managuense*.
- Lower total gill-raker count (10–13) vs. *C. managuense* (14–15).
- Fewer longitudinal scales (27–31) vs. *C. motaguense*/*C. dovii* (31–35).

### Morphological Description
- **Body:** Moderately deep, deepest in juveniles; head profile straight in young, becoming concave over eye with age.
- **Mouth:** Turned upward; lower jaw slightly projecting; maxillary nearly reaching vertical from front of eye; lower lip fold without frenum.
- **Teeth:** Conical, recurved, brown-tipped, round in cross-section; enlarged outer row with anterior canines in upper jaw; lower jaw canine-like teeth in positions 2–3.
- **Pharyngeal dentition:** Toothed area 1.5× broader than long; six tooth rows; posterior row with 22 teeth; median rows massive and compressed with curved major cusp.
- **Gill rakers:** Short, expanded; total on first arch 10 (n=6), 11 (n=25), 12 (n=43), 13 (n=6); holotype 2+9/2+10.
- **Lateral line:** Upper line pored scales mode 18–19; lower line pored scales mode 10–11; holotype 19/20 (upper), 12/11 (lower).
- **Scales:** Longitudinal series 26–31 (mode 29–30); transverse rows dorsal-to-lateral-line 3–4; anal-to-lateral-line 8–11 (mode 9–10).
- **Fins:**
  - Dorsal spines XV–XVIII (mode XVII–XVIII); soft rays 9–12.
  - Anal spines 7–9; soft rays 7–10.
  - Pectoral rays 13–14 (mode 14).
  - Pelvic rays I,5.
  - Caudal fin rounded, 16 (rarely 17) principal rays.
  - Dorsal/anal fin tips filamentous in large males.

### Coloration
- **Ground color:** Golden-brown to gray dorsally, silvery below; sides/belly turn golden-yellow during breeding.
- **Pattern:** Prominent discontinuous black quadrangle band from eye to caudal base (the diagnostic broad lateral stripe); broken band from eye to lower opercle; hidden pectoral-axil blotch.
- **Juveniles:** Up to eight faint vertical bars, sometimes persisting diffusely in subadults (<80 mm SL) or on upper flanks of adults.
- **Fins:** Pectorals transparent; pelvics dusky with darker leading edges.

### Sexual Dichromatism & Dimorphism
- Dichromatism first evident ~100 mm SL.
- Males: dark spotting spreads across dorsal/caudal fins and soft anal rays; dark streaks between anal spines; square-scale spotted pattern and vermiculate head pattern develop with size; longer pelvic-fin filaments.
- Females: lose spotting or retain few diffuse dorsal spots; lateral stripe bordered by iridescent yellow, brightest in ripe females, paling in parental fish of both sexes.
- **Urogenital papilla:** Males conical with bifid ragged tip; females stouter with crenulate-bordered ventral aperture and internal papillae.
- **Size:** Largest male 191 mm SL (Sixaola drainage); largest female 130 mm SL (Tortuguero); ripe females with distended ovipositors recorded at 80 mm SL (Río Cocolis, Sixaola).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Sluggish streams, backwaters, and swamps — contrasts with syntopic congeners.
- **Sympatry:** Co-occurs with **[[Cichlasoma dovii]]** and **[[Cichlasoma managuense]]** but partitions habitat:
  - *C. dovii* — prefers moderately swift waters.
  - *C. managuense* — basically lacustrine, dominant guapote in Lake Nicaragua and other lakes (including via introductions).
  - *C. loisellei* — sluggish-water specialist.
- **Diet:** Less piscivorous than congeners; favors aquatic and terrestrial insects, only occasionally taking fish.
- **Distribution:**
  - Atlantic versant: Río Negro (eastern Honduras) south to Laguna de Chiriquí drainages (western Panama).
  - Pacific slope: Río Tamarindo drainage (Nicaragua); Costa Rica (San Isidro de El General, Monteverde — likely inadvertent introductions with *C. dovii*); probable introductions to Río Coto Colorado (Costa Rica) and Río Chiriquí Viejo headwaters (Panama).
- **Biogeography:** Zone of contact between *C. loisellei* and *C. friedrichsthalii* coincides with a major ichthyofaunal discontinuity in the Belize/Guatemala/Honduras border region — historically the **Chapayal seaway**, persisting into the Pliocene as the **Amatique Basin**.

## Key Findings

1. **New species erected:** *Cichlasoma loisellei* is formally separated from *C. friedrichsthalii* based on consistent color-pattern differences (broad lateral stripe vs. vertical bars) despite overlapping meristic ranges.
2. **Meristic overlap ≠ species synonymy:** Meristic characters of *C. loisellei* (esp. Sixaola drainage population) overlap with Petén *C. friedrichsthalii*, but coloration is treated as the primary species-diagnostic trait — following the precedent set by **[[Trewavas 1983]]** in tilapiine cichlids (*Oreochromis mossambicus* vs. *O. mortimeri*/*O. spilurus*).
3. **Type locality clarification for *C. friedrichsthalii*:** Historical confusion over Heckel's (1840) original "Central-Amerika" locality is resolved — evidence (via Steindachner 1864, Günther 1868, Hubbs 1935) points to **Lake Petén, Guatemala** as the true type locality, not the Río San Juan (Nicaragua) as wrongly restricted by Jordan & Evermann (1898).
4. **Heckel's original description** (six wide blackish vertical bars, four split above the lateral line) definitively rules out application of the name *friedrichsthalii* to the southern, stripe-patterned form (*C. loisellei*).
5. **Uncertain taxonomic zone:** Guapotes from Lago de Yojoa, Río Ulúa, and the Río Chamelecón/Choluteca drainages show possible intergradation between *C. friedrichsthalii* and *C. motaguense*, requiring further study.
6. **Human-mediated introductions:** *C. managuense* presence in the Río Motagua drainage (Guatemala) is attributed to human introduction (via R.R. Miller, pers. comm.), as is likely the case for scattered Honduran records given the species' aquacultural importance.
7. **Comparative meristics (Table 2):** Dorsal spine counts, lower lateral-line scale counts, longitudinal scale counts, and scales below lateral line were compared across *C. friedrichsthalii* (Petén) and four *C. loisellei* populations (Honduras, Nicaragua, Matina C.R., Sixaola C.R.), showing geographic meristic variation within *C. loisellei* without corresponding color-pattern divergence.
8. **Etymology:** Named for **[[Paul V. Loiselle]]**, who informally dubbed the fish "guapote amarillo" (yellow guapote) prior to its formal description.
