# New Species of Winter Stoneflies, Genus Allocapnia (Plecoptera, Capniidae)

#entomology #plecoptera #capniidae

## Executive Summary

This 1964 taxonomic paper by [[Herbert H. Ross]] and [[William E. Ricker]] describes six new species of winter stoneflies in the genus [[Allocapnia]] (family [[Capniidae]]) from the temperate deciduous forest of eastern North America. The species are diagnosed mainly by the male genitalia, specifically the processes of the seventh and eighth tergites and the upper and lower supra-anal processes. The authors frame the genus as a potential indicator of faunal movements and dispersal linked to [[Pleistocene Glaciation]] events.

## Background & Description

[[Allocapnia]] is a genus of small, dark stoneflies. Aquatic nymphs mature in late autumn and early winter, and adults emerge, mate, and lay eggs from late November to late March, sometimes into early April in the north. The genus is known only from the [[Temperate Deciduous Forest]] and its northern ecotone with the [[Boreal Coniferous Forest]].

The new species are morphologically very similar to one another and to previously described species. Their diagnostic differences lie in the shapes of a few sclerites and processes at the terminal end of the body, linked to genitalic structures. Related taxonomic work by [[Frison]] (1935, 1942), [[Hanson]] (1942) and [[Ricker]] (1952) is cited throughout. Unless otherwise stated, types are deposited in the [[Illinois Natural History Survey]], with paratype duplicates in the [[Canadian National Museum]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat and phenology:** Winter-active adults and aquatic nymphs in small, often forested streams, with activity concentrated in the cold months.
- **Biogeography:** Restricted ranges for several species suggest that the genus records faunal movements during the Pleistocene. The authors argue this is particularly relevant to the [[Appalachian Region]] and the [[Ouachita Region]].
- **Biogeographic hypothesis:** The sister pair [[Allocapnia frisoni]] and [[Allocapnia peltoides]] is interpreted as the product of a common ancestor that dispersed between the Appalachian and Ouachita regions. Isolation then split it into eastern and western populations, which evolved into the two species.
- **Research support:** The work was funded by a [[National Science Foundation]] research grant. About sixty collaborators collected winter stoneflies, and the study was still ongoing at publication.

## Key Findings

**Six new species were described** (with states of occurrence):

1. **[[Allocapnia pechumani]]** (New York). Male length 5 mm, female 6 mm. Wings reach only to the fourth tergite in males. The seventh tergite process is high and conical, with a cleft apex. The eighth tergite process has a cleft apex forming a pair of wide lobes. Closest to [[Allocapnia forbesi]]; differs from [[Allocapnia maria]] in the wide lobes of the eighth tergite process. Type series: holotype male, allotype female, and 11 male and female paratypes from Starkville, Herkimer County, New York, plus a large set of additional New York paratypes.

2. **[[Allocapnia tennessa]]** (Tennessee). Male length given as similar to the preceding species; female 7 mm. Males are wingless. Female wings vary from short to long. Closest relative is [[Allocapnia vivipara]], differing in the shorter basal segment of the upper supra-anal process and in less extensive fusion of the female seventh and eighth sterna. Type series: holotype male, allotype female, and 4 male and female paratypes, mainly from Tennessee.

3. **[[Allocapnia frisoni]]** (New York, Pennsylvania, West Virginia). Male wings reach the sixth segment; female wings extend beyond the abdomen. The eighth tergite has a flat, shield-shaped dorsal area with small setose projections below. Most closely related to [[Allocapnia granulata]]. Type series: holotype male, allotype female, and 7 male and female paratypes, with a broad distribution in New York, Ohio, Pennsylvania and West Virginia.

4. **[[Allocapnia peltoides]]** (Arkansas, Oklahoma). Morphologically similar to A. frisoni, distinguished by a larger dorsal shield on the eighth tergite and a nearly uniform-width upper supra-anal process. Female indistinguishable from A. frisoni. Type series: holotype male, allotype female, and 1 male paratype (Oklahoma), plus Arkansas and Oklahoma paratypes.

5. **[[Allocapnia mohri]]** (Oklahoma). Male length 4.5 mm. The eighth tergite process is moderately low with a wide, arcuate posterior aspect. The upper supra-anal process is short, wide and almost foliaceous. Closest to [[Allocapnia recta]], from which it differs in the wide, arcuate apex of the eighth tergite process. Type series: holotype male, allotype female, and 70 male and female paratypes, all from Leflore County, Oklahoma.

6. **[[Allocapnia ohioensis]]** (Indiana, Kentucky, Ohio). Male length 5 mm; wings reach the fifth segment. The eighth tergite process is tridentate, with a lower middle tooth. The lower supra-anal process tapers to a somewhat triangular tip. Very close to [[Allocapnia indianae]], which has a narrow, tongue-like lower supra-anal apex and a mesal tooth as high as the lateral ones. Type series: holotype male, allotype female, and 10 male and 4 female paratypes, with many additional paratypes from Indiana, Kentucky and Ohio.

**Other notes:**
- The species are distinguished mainly by male genitalic structures, illustrated in Figure 1 (A–J) of the original paper.
- Some paratypes of A. ohioensis were segregated from the A. indianae paratype series by Ricker.
