# Proposal to Re-establish Desert Suckers and Longfin Dace into Arnett Creek and Telegraph Canyon

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #conservation #species-reintroduction

## Executive Summary

This 1996 proposal by the Arizona Game and Fish Department outlines a plan to re-establish native fish populations of [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*) and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*) into [[Arnett Creek]] and [[Telegraph Canyon]] in the [[Gila River]] basin. The project involves removing exotic species ([[Green Sunfish]] and [[Mosquitofish]]), constructing a fish barrier to prevent upstream movement of exotics from [[Queen Creek]], and stocking native fish collected from the Gila River between [[Coolidge Dam]] and [[Florence Diversion Dam]]. The initiative represents a collaborative effort between Region V Arizona Game and Fish Department and the [[Tonto National Forest]], with implementation scheduled for 1996-1997 and monitoring planned for 10 years.

## Background & Description

[[Arnett Creek]] is a small 1st/2nd order stream tributary to [[Queen Creek]], located west-southwest of Superior, Arizona in Unit 37B. Historically, the creek was heavily impacted by water use associated with mining and other human activities. Improved management over the previous decade resulted in perennial stream flow in several miles of stream. The creek supports a fairly diverse riparian area but does not provide recreational fishing opportunities.

Historic collection records document the presence of [[Longfin Dace]], [[Desert Sucker]], and [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*) in Arnett Creek at its confluence with Queen Creek. Just decades prior, western streams contained abundant native fishes that had survived millennia of geologic and climatic changes. Historic habitat was dominated by climax riparian plant communities modified by beaver activity.

The target species for reintroduction are:
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*, [[Cyprinidae]]): Current status described as "numerous and wide spread" throughout the Gila River watershed
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Catostomus clarki*, [[Catostomidae]]): Current status described as "common and wide spread"

Neither species is federally listed or under consideration for listing. Physical conditions in intermediate-elevation streams are highly variable, carrying remarkable loads of eroded material from mountains. Chemically, most streams remain within limits for healthy fish populations, though habitat and perennial stretches have experienced the greatest decline and loss.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The creek and watershed were currently (1996) dominated by exotic species: [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*) and [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), two aggressive fish known to prey on native fish species. Early diversions for mineral production, cattle operations, and irrigated agriculture, followed by giant mainstream dams, resulted in native fish extermination throughout the region.

The [[Tonto National Forest]] conducted an aquatic habitat inventory of the creek and found adequate values to support the target native fishes. The project required blocking upstream movement of exotic fish into Arnett Creek from Queen Creek during high flow periods through barrier construction.

Historic management activities focused on introduction of nonnative fishes, contributing to the decline of native species. The current restoration opportunity arose from improved water management resulting in restored perennial flow.

## Key Findings

**Project Timeline and Implementation:**
- Opportunity identified in 1992 by Arizona Game and Fish Department and Tonto National Forest
- Habitat and fish surveys: Completed
- Riparian pasture fencing: Completed
- Environmental Assessment (EA) for barrier construction and exotic fish removal: Completed with final decision given
- Barrier construction: July 1 to October 31, 1996
- Exotic fish removal (renovation): September 1 to November 30, 1996
- Re-treatment: December 1, 1996 to July 31, 1997
- Native fish capture and stocking: April 1 to October 31, 1997
- Monitoring surveys: May 1, 1997 to April 30, 2007 (10 years)

**Methodology:**
- **Capture location**: Gila River between [[Coolidge Dam]] and [[Florence Diversion Dam]]
- **Capture techniques**: Four-person crew from Region V using seines and dip nets; on-site examination for diseases and parasites by Fish Branch pathologist; quarantine and treatment at [[Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery]] if pathogens detected
- **Transport**: Regional fish tanks with standard techniques (oxygenation, temperature monitoring, aeration)
- **Release**: Temperature acclimation in [[Arnett Creek]]; [[Telegraph Canyon]] release requiring three-person hike with backpack transport
- **Monitoring**: Biannual surveys during initial three years using seine and dip nets; specimen capture for identification, age/size class assignment, relative abundance and catch-per-unit effort determination, perennial water length measurement, water quality data collection; depletion estimates if necessary

**Data Collection:**
- Fish identification and age/size classification
- Relative abundance and catch-per-unit effort
- Perennial water length measurements
- Water quality and flow data
- Annual surveys for 10 years with periodic restocking as required

**Budget:**
Annual costs of $500 to $1,000 incorporated into Regional fish program Federal Aid base for time, travel, and operating expenses.

**Coordination:**
Joint project between Region V Arizona Game and Fish Department and Globe District, Tonto National Forest. Two Heritage grant applications prepared for inventory phase and barrier construction. Exhaustive public involvement including agencies, sportsmen groups, conservationists, public land users, and organizations. All conflicts discussed and resolved.

**Management Decision:**
EA concluded with decision to build barrier at preferred site and stock [[Longfin Dace]] and [[Desert Sucker]]. Region V fisheries program recommended project continuation as outlined. Proposal submitted for internal and external review (Step 8 of Department's 12-step species re-establishment process), with review requested from Dr. William Minckley at Arizona State University by October 1, 1996.
