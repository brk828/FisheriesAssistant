# Description of a New Finless Ophichthid Eel from the Gulf of California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ophichthidae

## Executive Summary

This Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science paper (1953) by J. Bohlke of [[Stanford University]] describes a new, completely finless and scaleless ophichthid eel, *Caecula (Sphagebranchus) gymnocelus*. The holotype and one paratype were dredged during the 1952 [[Sefton-Stanford Expedition]] in the [[Gulf of California]]. The species is placed in *Caecula* following [[Myers And Wade 1941]] and [[Gosline 1951]]. The document also contains a second, unrelated paper, a truncated study on [[X-Radiation Effects]] on [[Intestinal Phosphatase]] in mice. That paper is not aquatic and is summarized separately below.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The new species lacks all vestiges of fins and would normally fall in the genus *Sphagebranchus* Bloch. The author treats *Sphagebranchus* as a synonym of *Caecula* Vahl, which includes eels with "vertical fins absent or feebly developed." The completely finless species are retained in the subgenus *Sphagebranchus* within *Caecula*.

**Type material.**
- **Holotype:** [[Smithsonian Institution|S.U.]] No. 17589, 217.5 mm total length. Dredged at Station 38-D-2 on May 8, 1952. Collectors: D. Cohen, J. Lindbergh, and J. Bohlke. Haul depth 58-62 fathoms, 10-minute haul.
- **Paratype:** S.U. No. 17590, one large gravid female, 240 mm, from the same haul.

**Morphology (holotype values, paratype in parentheses).**
- Body very elongate and round in cross-section. It is nowhere compressed, and the tail ends in a rounded point.
- Depth at anus is 2.9 (2.7) in head length, or 42.6 (44.4) in total length.
- Head is very short, 6.9 (7.6) in head plus trunk length.
- Eyes are small and lateral, 12.6 (12.9) in head length, and only slightly protruding.
- Snout is long and bluntly rounded, 4.9 (5.0) in head length. It is greatly produced beyond the lower jaw.
- Interorbital width is 9.9 (10.6) in head length.
- Teeth are small and backward-pointing. Each intermaxillary has 2-3 large teeth. Each maxillary has about 17-24 teeth. The mandible has about 22 teeth. The vomer has 3-4 teeth at its anterior end.
- Gill openings are small and boomerang-shaped in ventral view. The isthmus is very narrow.
- Lateral line system is well developed, with 130-132 pores on the head and body.

**Coloration in alcohol.** The body is uniform light tan, lightest ventrally. It has a light sprinkling of chromatophores along the sides. The head and branchial region have irregular small dark blotches. Two distinctive black transverse stripes cross the lower surface of the head.

**Etymology.** The name *gymnocelus* combines the Greek *gymnus* (naked) and *celum* (arrow shaft), referring to the body shape and absence of fins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The holotype was taken from a large bay on the east side of Isla Angel de la Guarda. The substrate was largely very fine oozy sand with a small amount of crushed shell, at 58-62 fathoms. This is consistent with a burrowing, benthic lifestyle, although the paper does not discuss behavior directly.

**Biogeography and relatives.**
- The new species is very closely related to [[Caecula Equatorialis]], described from the [[Galapagos Islands]] by Myers and Wade (1941). The author notes it may eventually prove to be a northern subspecies.
- Its only congener in the Gulf of California is [[Caecula Selachops]], from which it differs in having a rounded snout, an anterior nostril nearer the lower jaw tip, and a posterior nostril inside the lip.

**Mouse study context.** The second paper concerns laboratory mice (male CH mice from Carworth Farms, New City, N.J.). Mice were acclimated for at least ten days at 70 °F with food and water ad libitum. They were aged 1-3 months, a period when intestinal phosphatase is relatively constant. The excerpt ends during the methods section, so no results are available.

## Key Findings

- **New species described:** *Caecula (Sphagebranchus) gymnocelus* is a completely finless, scaleless eel. It is distinguished from *C. equatorialis* by ten characters. The most notable are head proportions (head 6.9-7.6 in head plus trunk, versus 5.8 in *C. equatorialis*), eye size (12.6-12.9 in head, versus 16), and interorbital width (9.9-10.6 in head, versus 13.3).
- **Diagnostic characters:** The posterior nostril is round (versus oval in *C. equatorialis*). The transverse and longitudinal parts of the gill opening are approximately equal in length. The anterior mandibular series has five pores (versus four). The body color has distinctive dark head markings absent in *C. equatorialis*.
- **Gulf of California placement:** The species is endemic to the region represented by the type locality and is the only finless member of *Caecula* from the Gulf found in this study. This is inferred from the limited sample of two specimens.
- **Sample size:** The description rests on only two specimens (one holotype, one paratype). The author does not provide measurements of variation.
- **Nomenclature:** The author follows [[Myers And Wade 1941]] in treating *Sphagebranchus* as a synonym of *Caecula*, and follows [[Gosline 1951]] in keeping finless species in the subgenus.
- **Radiation study (mouse):** The paper outlines a study of how a measured dose of x-rays alters alkaline phosphatase activity in the intestinal tract of mice. It combines microscopic (histochemical, Gomori methods) and chemical (homogenate) approaches. The document is incomplete, and no results or dose values are provided. It was supported by a U.S. Army Surgeon General contract (W 49-007-MD-466).
