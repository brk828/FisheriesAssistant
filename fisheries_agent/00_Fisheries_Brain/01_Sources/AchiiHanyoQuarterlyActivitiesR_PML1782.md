# Achii Hanyo Quarterly Activities Report, February–April 2000

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) quarterly report from the [[Arizona Fisheries Resource Office]] in Parker, Arizona, covers the [[Achii Hanyo]] captive-propagation facility for endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] from February to April 2000. The facility aims to produce large numbers of [[Big River Fishes]] listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Estimated stock on site was 22,262 fish. A [[Ichthyophthirius Multifiliis]] (Ich) outbreak in February was controlled with [[Copper Sulfate]] treatments and was absent by 6 April. Fish grew significantly over the quarter, from 101–125 mm and 9–16 g in February to 130–144 mm and 18–24 g in April.

## Background & Description

The Achii Hanyo Project was started as a pilot in 1997, when six earthen ponds were renovated. Fish were first reared at the facility in 1999. The [[Bonytail Chub]] is one of four "big river fishes" (with [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Colorado Pikeminnow]]) listed under the Endangered Species Act. Biological opinions require captive propagation of these fish, and existing hatcheries are at capacity.

Bonytail chubs were delivered from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] on 13 October 1999. The count was estimated from Dexter's rate of 57 fish per pound. Four ponds totaling 3.2 acres were in use during this period.

Production targets are:
- Lake Mohave: 25,000 bonytail chubs, 10–12 inches, needed annually for five years.
- Lake Havasu: 30,000 bonytail chubs (total) needed.
- Full operation: 100,000 ten-inch bonytail chubs annually for the Lower Colorado River.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water quality.** Temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen (DO), conductivity, and redox were measured twice daily with a [[Hydrolab]] instrument (Appendix B). Reported ranges were:
- Temperature: 11.50 °C in February to 23.84 °C in April.
- Dissolved oxygen: 16.70 mg/L in February to 7.8 mg/L in April, as reported in the source.

Pond averages were tabulated for Ponds 1, 2, 3A, 3B, and the CRIT Canal, but the scanned columns are partly garbled, so only the summary ranges are given here.

**Feeding.** Feed rates depend on average water temperature (see [[Fish Feeding Rates]]):
- Above 70 °F: 3% of body mass per day.
- 60–70 °F: 2% of body mass per day.
- Below 60 °F: 1% of body mass per day.

Feed was set at 3% in October, when water was very warm, dropped to 1% in winter, and was at 3% of body weight at the time of reporting.

**Aquatic vegetation.** [[Copper Sulfate]] was used in all ponds to control filamentous algae and other aquatic plants, and it nearly eliminated aquatic vegetation. [[Sonar Herbicide]] was used once in Pond 1. Salt cedar was removed manually from dry ponds and the drainage canal, which is an ongoing task. Chemical use was recorded on INAD forms.

**Bird predation.** Predation was minimal because all ponds were covered with bird netting. [[Crow Tribe Fish and Game]] (CRIT) helped remove waterfowl and other birds.

## Key Findings

**1. Sampling and growth.** Fifty fish per pond were sampled monthly with hoop nets or a 100′ × 10′ × 1/4″ seine. Each fish was measured and weighed, then returned to its pond. Averages rose significantly from February to April:
- Length: 101–144 mm.
- Weight: 9–22 g.

Appendix A and Figures 1–3 summarize the data, including length and weight histograms and temperature and DO graphs for Pond 1.

**2. Ich outbreak and control.**
- 9 February: Ich was detected in Ponds 3A and 3B, with near-100% frequency in Pond 3B.
- 10 February: Three fish from Pond 3B were sent to the [[Pinetop Fish Health Center]], which confirmed Ich.
- 17 February: All ponds were infected, with Pond 2 the worst. [[Manuel Ulibarri]] recommended chemical treatment.
- 3 March: Only traces of the parasite remained, in Pond 2. Gill blunting was noted, attributed to aggressive chemical treatment, and treatments were reduced.
- 6 April: No signs of disease.

**3. Chemical treatment protocol.**
- 9 February: Copper sulfate at 2 lbs per surface acre across all ponds.
- After Ich detection: 3 lbs per surface acre every two days.
- After 17 February: 5 lbs per surface acre every two days, with extra treatment in Pond 2 for two weeks.
- After 2 March: 4 lbs per surface acre once a week.
- March onward: [[Citric Acid]] added at 1/2 lb per 1 lb of copper sulfate to extend copper's effectiveness.
- After 6 April: Treatment frequency reduced to once every two weeks.

Appendix C lists daily or near-daily applications by pond.
