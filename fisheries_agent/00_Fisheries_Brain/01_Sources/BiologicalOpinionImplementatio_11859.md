# Biological Opinion on Livestock Grazing under the Bar V Bar and Campaign Allotment Management Plan: Gila Topminnow

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) on August 7, 1991, under [[Section 7 Consultation]] of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It addresses proposed livestock grazing and associated structural improvements under the Bar V Bar and Campaign [[Allotment Management Plan]] on the Tonto Basin Ranger District of the [[Tonto National Forest]], Gila County, Arizona. The listed species of concern is the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*).

The FWS concluded that the action is **not likely to jeopardize the continued existence** of the Gila topminnow. The opinion anticipates mixed effects: continued grazing will keep adverse pressure on habitat, while a rotational system with rest periods is expected to improve watershed and riparian conditions over time. The opinion also authorizes a level of [[Incidental Take Statement|incidental take]] that cannot be quantified numerically, and sets monitoring, reporting and reinitiation requirements.

## Background & Description

**Species.** The Gila topminnow is a small livebearing fish listed as endangered on March 11, 1967. No critical habitat has been designated. It occurs in the Gila, Sonora and de la Concepcion river drainages of Arizona, New Mexico and Sonora, Mexico. It was once among the most common fishes of the Gila River and its tributaries, but it has been extirpated from most of its range through water diversion, channelization, impoundment, vegetation clearing and predation or competition by nonnative [[Mosquitofish]].

**Reintroduction.** Campaign Creek and Upper Horrell Springs lie within the historic range and were stocked in 1983 as part of the [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Program]]. The stock came from [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]] and is of mixed Monkey Spring, Cocio Wash and Bylas Springs lineage. Stocking was covered by a 1982 Memorandum of Understanding among the FWS, USFS and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD), with a May 20, 1983 amendment adding the two sites.

**Study sites.**
- *Upper Horrell Springs*: several hillside seeps and two moderate springs, one of which is modified for domestic supply to the Reevis Mountain School. These springs are the primary water source for the perennial reach of [[Campaign Creek]].
- *Campaign Creek*: a small, rocky stream with a perennial section of about 1 to 2 miles, tributary to the Salt River at [[Lake Roosevelt]]. Riparian vegetation includes cottonwood, willow, walnut, cattails, grasses, sedges and rushes. Vegetation is much denser on ungrazed private land than on USFS land. The stream has a high proportion of pools.

**Management area.** The [[Bar V Bar and Campaign Allotments]] total 34,158 acres and comprise the entire Campaign Creek watershed. Elevation ranges from about 2,400 to 5,700 feet. Vegetation types include Sonoran desert scrub, semi-desert grassland, interior chaparral, Madrean evergreen woodland and Fremont cottonwood riparian.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Current grazing.** The allotments are grazed by 575 head of cattle on a continuous yearlong basis. Under the proposed AMP the same number would graze under a controlled [[Rotational Grazing]] system with shorter grazing periods alternating with rest.
- *Lower elevation*: 11 pastures, grazing of 10 to 65 days, with each pasture entered no more than once a year and use rotated for growing-season rest.
- *Higher elevation* (Two Bar, Jackson, Reevis, Granite): a rest-rotation system with four months of use per pasture on a four-year cycle, followed by a full year of rest.
- *Special use*: 6 pastures (Gans Hole, HN, Tule, Dry, Heifer, Horse) used as needed for bulls, heifers, yearlings or horses.

**Structural projects.** The grazing system requires spring developments, pipelines, stock troughs, fencing, corrals, road work, water storage tanks, horizontal wells, trick tanks and cattleguards. Table 1 of the opinion lists these by pasture; it covers the numbered regular pastures and the special-use pastures, and is summarized here rather than reproduced.

**Environmental baseline.** The Campaign Creek population was one of the longest-surviving and largest reintroduced populations. In 1991, however, no topminnow were seen despite several collection efforts. Possible causes include late-winter 1991 flooding, below-average winter temperatures in 1990–91, natural fluctuation or unidentified habitat change. The population had done well for seven years.

**Longfin dace.** [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), a native Gila basin fish that coexists historically with the topminnow, had not been reported from Campaign Creek before 1989. Its recent, extensive presence may indicate habitat change. A report of stocking the creek with crayfish (a predatory nonnative) and an unidentified fish was also noted. The FWS states that a stockpond is an unlikely source for longfin dace, and that no other fish have been found in the creek.

**Effects of grazing.** Grazing degrades watersheds, streambanks, channel substrate and morphology; it increases flood frequency and severity, reduces aquatic habitat complexity and riparian vegetation, and may reduce perennial flow (Platts 1981; Chaney et al. 1990; Schulz and Leininger 1990; Pearsons and Li 1991). The topminnow tolerates a wide range of conditions and can persist in degraded habitat in the short term, but long-term survival through floods and droughts depends on a diverse, complex environment. Improved [[Riparian Habitat]] condition under the proposed system is therefore expected to help the species.

**Water diversion.** Spring and groundwater diversion into storage and watering facilities could reduce surface flow in Campaign Creek. The FWS judged the volume used by livestock facilities to be small and not expected to cause a measurable flow effect.

**Cumulative effects.** Anticipated non-federal actions include continued diversion of part of Upper Horrell Springs for the Reevis Mountain School, continued residence and food-crop production there, and increased recreational use. Future federal actions fall under their own consultation requirements and are not counted as cumulative.

## Key Findings

1. **Jeopardy determination:** Implementing the proposed grazing management system, including structural improvements, is **not likely to jeopardize** the Gila topminnow.

2. **Scope:** Covered are grazing under the Bar V Bar and Campaign AMP and the structural improvements described in the February 28, 1991 Biological Evaluation. Excluded are two proposed prescribed burns, and related projects in the draft EA (lake fish habitat work, parking areas, cultural interpretation, goose forage enhancement). Each of these needs its own evaluation and, where it may affect listed species, separate consultation.

3. **Effects of the action:**
   - Continued grazing in the Campaign Creek watershed and Upper Horrell Springs riparian zone causes ongoing adverse habitat impacts.
   - Shorter grazing periods with rest are probably beneficial, and could enhance the species' status if watershed and riparian conditions improve.
   - Fences, cattleguards, corrals, road work and trick tanks are not expected to harm the species and are expected to bring long-term benefit.
   - Ten springs in the Campaign Creek watershed and two horizontal wells are not expected to have substantial effects.

4. **Population status:** The Campaign Creek population is in serious condition for unknown reasons. Under the joint FWS–AGFD monitoring policy, a population is declared extirpated only after no topminnow are found in at least two thorough samplings over at least two years. Low-density populations are hard to detect, and past instances show species absent one year and abundant the next. Supplemental stocking may be considered.

5. **Incidental take:** Take is expected through habitat destruction and loss. It cannot be quantified directly as individuals lost, because reliable population estimates are unobtainable, and the species is short-lived with high fecundity. Take is expected to include reduced reproduction and increased losses in floods, droughts and other catastrophes. The proposed system should reduce past levels of take.

6. **Reinitiation triggers:** Consultation must be reinitiated if take exceeds what was anticipated, or if the livestock management provisions are changed or violated. Operations must stop during reinitiation if the additional taking would cause irreversible, adverse impacts, per 50 CFR 402.14(i).

7. **Reasonable and prudent measures and terms:**
   - Minimize take of the Gila topminnow and its habitat.
   - Keep complete, accurate records.
   - Implement entry and withdrawal dates, head counts, structural developments and management exactly as in the BE and draft AMP.
   - Report any changes or violations in writing to the FWS at least 30 days before a change, or within 30 days after a violation.

8. **Conservation recommendations:**
   - Annual monitoring of the Campaign Creek population by the USFS with AGFD. AGFD currently monitors every other year, and the USFS should fund or conduct the off years, sharing all data with the FWS.
   - Annual riparian vegetation monitoring after an initial three-year recovery period.
   - If monitoring shows a need for a "riparian management pasture," FWS involvement in its planning and design, with formal consultation if it may affect the species.

9. **Conclusion:** Formal consultation is concluded on the actions described in the BE. The FWS requests notification of conservation recommendations' implementation.
