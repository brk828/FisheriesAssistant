# Request to Amend Endangered Fish Permit PRT-676811

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinodontidae #Leuciscidae #conservation-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary
This document is administrative correspondence, not a primary research paper, but it holds conservation-relevant taxonomic and regulatory data. Dated 8 July 1986, it is a letter from a researcher at the [[Arizona State University]] Department of Zoology to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Regional Director Mike Spear, Albuquerque office), requesting an amendment to an existing federal scientific collecting/propagation permit (PRT-676811). The amendment seeks to add four imperiled Southwestern U.S. freshwater fish species — the [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Spikedace]], [[Sonora Chub]], and [[Loach Minnow]] — to the permit's authorized species list, and to add [[Michael Douglas]] (newly appointed Curator of Vertebrates) as a co-permittee. The letter reflects active, ongoing captive propagation and field research programs for these species in cooperation with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

## Background & Description
The source text contains no morphometric, meristic, or anatomical descriptions of the species involved. It is a permitting document rather than a taxonomic or morphological study. For reference, the species named are broadly characterized elsewhere in the ichthyological literature as follows (not detailed in this source):
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — small-bodied cyprinodontid adapted to extreme desert aquatic conditions.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) — small, slender-bodied cyprinid of Gila River Basin streams.
- **[[Sonora Chub]]** (*Gila ditaenia*) — cyprinid restricted to the Rio Sonora and Rio Magdalena drainages (including Arizona's Sycamore Creek).
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — small benthic cyprinid with a loach-like body form adapted to swift, riffle habitats.

No morphological data are provided in this document itself.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- All four species are native to **Southwestern U.S. desert freshwater systems**, primarily associated with the **[[Gila River Basin]]** and broader **Sonoran Desert** aquatic habitats.
- The letter identifies these taxa as species of active conservation concern, noting:
  - *Cyprinodon macularius* (Desert Pupfish): described as "newly-listed," implying recent federal endangered/threatened status.
  - *Meda fulgida* (Spikedace) and *Gila ditaenia* (Sonora Chub): also "newly-listed" at time of writing.
  - *Tiaroga cobitis* (Loach Minnow): described as "soon-to-be-listed," indicating imminent federal listing.
- The document confirms **ex-situ propagation** ("maintaining desert pupfish stocks for a number of years") as an active conservation strategy undertaken jointly by academic ([[Arizona State University]]), state ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]), and federal ([[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]) partners — an early example of coordinated captive-assurance colonies for endangered desert fishes.
- The request to formally curate specimens into the **Arizona State University Museum** points to voucher-specimen-based research and long-term biodiversity archiving as part of the recovery framework.

## Key Findings
- This 1986 letter documents an early institutional and regulatory step in the recovery planning for four imperiled Southwestern fish taxa: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Spikedace]], [[Sonora Chub]], and [[Loach Minnow]].
- It provides historical evidence of the **timeline of federal listing status** for these species circa mid-1986 (some newly listed, one pending imminent listing under the Endangered Species Act).
- It demonstrates an established **multi-agency cooperative model** (university–state–federal) for endangered desert fish propagation and research predating many later formalized recovery programs.
- The addition of a dedicated **Curator of Vertebrates** ([[Michael Douglas]]) at Arizona State University signals institutional investment in long-term curation, research continuity, and museum-based voucher collections supporting endangered species science.
- No quantitative ecological, morphological, or population data are presented; the scientific value of this document lies in its role as a **regulatory/historical record** of desert fish conservation efforts under USFWS Permit PRT-676811.
