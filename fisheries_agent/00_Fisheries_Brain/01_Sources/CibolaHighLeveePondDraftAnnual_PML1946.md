# Cibola High Levee Pond: Bonytail and Razorback Sucker Recruitment, FY-2002 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

After roughly fifty years, [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] were again producing young in the lower [[Colorado River]] basin. Biologists first detected reproduction at the isolated [[Cibola High Levee Pond]] (CHLP) in 1998, when both species had successfully spawned. CHLP had been stocked as a grow-out facility between 1993 and 1996, and stocking and removal were suspended after the 1998 discovery so the community could develop naturally. The annual report covers FY-2002 sampling (electrofishing, netting, light trapping, limnology, spawning observations) and population estimates, and outlines plans for 2002-2003. The authors note that a non-flowing, nearly nonnative-free pond supporting recruitment suggests historical oxbow communities may have functioned similarly, a feature largely overlooked in native fish research.

## Background & Description

- **Origin and stocking:** CHLP was renovated in 1993 and stocked by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service with about 58,300 juvenile bonytail and 14,000 razorback suckers (the source gives both 58,300 and 58,400 in different places; 58,300 is used here). Fish were small at stocking (razorbacks averaged 93 mm; bonytail 66 mm).
- **Fish removal and tagging:** Fish reaching about 30 cm were PIT-tagged and relocated to Lake Havasu or the Colorado River (more than 225 bonytail and 760 razorback suckers between 1993 and 1995).
- **Discovery:** During fall 1998 removal, young fish under 15 cm of both species were collected, including a 25 mm bonytail larva, confirming reproduction.
- **Site:** A two-hectare pond roughly 350 m long and 100 m wide, volume about 25,000 m³, maximum depth slightly over 3 m. It was isolated when the river was straightened by dredging, and lies on the California-Arizona state boundary about 5 miles south of Palo Verde, California.
- **Habitat:** Riprap covers about 40% of the shoreline and provides fish cover. Submerged vegetation includes spiny naiad (*Najas marina*) and sago pondweed (*Potamogeton pectinatus*). Cattails dominate the northwest shore; a large windrow of tumbleweeds lies in the deeper portion.
- **Water level:** The pond's level fluctuates with the river, generally about half a meter lower than the river.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water quality:** Temperatures ranged from 10.7 °C to 24.9 °C, with a rapid warming trend to about 23 °C in two weeks in spring. Dissolved oxygen stayed above 7 mg/L. Conductivity averaged 1,182 µS/cm (range 1,167-1,199) and pH averaged 7.9 (7.6-8.0).
- **Invertebrates:** Overnight light-trap samples contained large numbers of aquatic insects and crustaceans. Scuds were the most abundant taxon by count (about 6,050 in overnight traps; dragonfly and damselfly nymphs were also predators of larval fish). Fish predation by odonate nymphs (Horn et al. 1994) is a plausible source of larval loss.
- **Predators and nonnative fish:** The pond has few or no nonnative fish; a single large channel catfish (about 10 kg) was the only nonnative species captured. [[Crayfish]] (Carpenter 2000) are effective predators of native fish and frequently caused gear losses.
- **Spawning habitat:** Razorback suckers spawned over gravel substrate, while bonytail spawned over riprap rubble, where eggs were deposited deep among rocks.

## Key Findings

**Sampling effort and methods**
- Over 4,000 net-hours of sampling captured nearly 1,100 bonytail and over 1,900 razorback suckers (figures as reported; the source's exact totals are partly garbled in the scan).
- **[[Electrofishing]]** was the most effective method (about 100 fish per hour), but selective toward larger fish. The November 2001 run captured 160 bonytail, 84 razorback suckers, and one channel catfish.
- **[[Trammel Net]]** sets captured 1,280 fish from 47 net-set pairs; rates exceeded 40 fish per 100 m² of net fished. The razorback-to-bonytail ratio was about 1:10 (Table 4 and text).
- **Fyke and minnow traps** had poor capture rates (fyke: 0.08 fish/hour; minnow traps: 0.001 fish/hour, 35 fish in 2,600 hours).
- **[[Light Trap]]** sampling yielded only three bonytail larvae in the first season; a later test produced five larvae from three traps in a short set, questioning whether larval predation occurred inside traps.

**Population estimates (2001-2002)**
- Bonytail larger than 20 cm: pooled estimate 1,653 (range 1,145-2,972).
- Bonytail under 20 cm: 6,189 (range 4,914-8,663).
- Razorback sucker over 20 cm: 987 (range 844-1,190).
- Overall estimate: 7,842 bonytail (95% CL 4,959-11,635) and 987 razorback suckers (95% CL 844-1,190).
- The 1999 mark-recapture survey estimated about 3,000 razorback suckers. Standing crop was reported at about 635.8 kg of fish per hectare larger than 10 cm. Bonytail made up roughly 98.5% of the population by number.
- Recapture rates ranged from 15% for small bonytail to 6% for razorback suckers.

**Spawning**
- Bonytail spawned in early April (ripe fish in the first three weeks of April), and the season appeared much shorter than that of razorback suckers.
- Razorback suckers were ripe from November to May; 45 sexually ripe fish (18 females, 27 males) were collected, with ripe males ranging 37 to 51 cm and females 39 to 52 cm.
- A school of about 100 small bonytail (10-20 cm) was observed feeding and spawning over riprap, and underwater video recorded the activity.

**Recruitment**
- Bonytail produced young in each of the last three years (1999-2002), including year-class 1 fish schooling in the pond.
- Razorback sucker young survived in 1999 but apparently not in 2000 or 2001, with no evidence of survival in 2002. Reasons are not yet clear.
- Predation on sucker eggs by bonytail was hypothesized.

**Work planned for FY-2002/2003**
- Map bottom type, cover, and location in summer or early fall; use tagging for a telemetry study of spawning behavior and recruitment limits.
- Track ten razorback suckers by radio telemetry over 8 weeks in 2003, with a similar study in FY-2004.
- Test predatory behavior of 16 nonnative fish species, with razorback sucker larvae and fry (under 45 mm) as potential prey, in cooperation with [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]].
