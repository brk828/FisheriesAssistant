## Executive Summary

This 1969 note documents the confirmed collection and establishment of the introduced freshwater shrimp **[[Palaemonetes paludosus]]** in the **[[Rio Hardy]]** and **[[Colorado River]]**, Baja California, Mexico — south of the U.S.-Mexico border. Originally introduced into the lower Colorado River in California by the California Department of Fish and Game in 1958, the species had since expanded its range southward into Mexican waters, where it was found thriving in habitats also occupied by marine shrimps and fishes, indicating notable euryhaline tolerance. The record confirms a self-sustaining, well-established population well beyond the original introduction sites.

## Background & Description

- No detailed morphometric data provided in this note (species identification only).
- Life stages collected included **adult males**, **egg-carrying (ovigerous) females**, and **juveniles**, indicating an actively reproducing population with a full size/age structure present at collection sites.
- Species identity was confirmed by taxonomic specialists: Allyn G. Smith (California Academy of Sciences) and Tom Cukr (Scripps Institution of Oceanography), following tentative field identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Confluence zone of freshwater ([[Rio Hardy]]) and the combined/mixed flow of the [[Rio Hardy]] and [[Colorado River]], representing a freshwater-to-brackish gradient near the [[Gulf of California]].
- **Salinity tolerance**: A water sample at Vuelta La Corvina Camp recorded a **specific gravity of 1.020 at 23°C**, indicating notably brackish conditions — demonstrating that *P. paludosus*, typically considered a freshwater species, tolerates elevated salinity.
- **Co-occurring fauna**: Found living alongside **marine shrimps and marine fishes**, suggesting overlap between freshwater-adapted and euryhaline/marine species in this estuarine-like transition zone.
- **Substrate/cover association**: Consistently collected in association with **sago pondweed** (*[[Potamogeton pectinatus]]*), which provided essential structural cover; shrimp were reliably captured via dip net wherever this vegetation was present.
- **Reproductive ecology**: Multiple successive spawning events were observed over several months in freshwater aquaria at the Chino Fish and Wildlife Base, indicating an extended breeding season and high reproductive output under captive freshwater conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **Confirmed range expansion**: *P. paludosus*, introduced to the lower Colorado River (California) in 1958 (approx. 225 individuals near [[Lake Havasu]]; 1,000 individuals in three ponds at [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]]), has expanded its range south across the international border into the **Rio Hardy and Colorado River in Baja California, Mexico** — a range extension of at least 42–80 miles south of Mexicali.
2. **Established, self-sustaining population**: Presence of adult males, ovigerous females, and juveniles across multiple collection sites and dates (March, May, June 1968) confirms active local reproduction rather than a transient or non-breeding occurrence.
3. **Salinity tolerance broader than expected**: Collection in water with a specific gravity of 1.020 alongside marine shrimp and fish species suggests *P. paludosus* possesses greater euryhalinity than typically documented for the species.
4. **Habitat dependency on vegetation**: Successful, high-density collection was consistently tied to the presence of submerged aquatic vegetation (*Potamogeton pectinatus*), highlighting this plant as a key structural habitat requirement/refuge for the species.
5. **Ecological significance**: This record represents an early documented case of a deliberately introduced freshwater invertebrate becoming established as an **invasive/naturalized species** across an international border and into novel brackish-water habitat, relevant to broader discussions of [[Species Introduction]] and [[Invasive Species Management]] in the [[Colorado River]] basin.
