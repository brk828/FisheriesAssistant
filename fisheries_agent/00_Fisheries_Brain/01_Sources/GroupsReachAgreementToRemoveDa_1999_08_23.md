## Executive Summary
This 1999 news article from the *Daily Journal of Commerce* reports on a landmark agreement to remove [[Condit Dam]] from the [[White Salmon River]] in Washington State. Negotiated among the [[Yakama Nation]], environmental groups, state/federal agencies, and dam operator [[PacifiCorp]], the agreement resolves a long-standing barrier to anadromous fish passage. Rather than invest $30 million in fish-passage infrastructure required for hydroelectric license renewal, PacifiCorp opted for a $17.1 million dam removal and mitigation package. While not a scientific study, the article contains quantitative projections for salmonid population restoration and is relevant to the ecology of migratory [[Salmonidae]] species in the Columbia River basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: The [[White Salmon River]] is a tributary of the [[Columbia River]], historically blocked by [[Condit Dam]] (built 1913, ~125 ft high), which impounds a 1.5-mile-long reservoir known as **Northwest Lake**.
- **Barrier effect**: The dam has prevented anadromous fish migration to upstream spawning habitat for over 85 years, forcing reliance on a hatchery program located three miles below the dam, which was characterized as "fairly unsuccessful."
- **Restoration pathway**: Dam removal is expected to reopen access to upstream spawning and rearing habitat, including tributaries requiring separate restoration efforts, funded partly through a $1 million fishery improvement fund administered by the [[Yakama Nation]].
- **Broader connectivity**: The river's restoration is linked to potential designation as a [[National Wild and Scenic River]], connecting headwaters to the [[Columbia River]] confluence, reinforcing habitat corridor value for migratory fish populations.
- **Cultural-ecological link**: A traditional Indian fishing site at the confluence of the White Salmon and Columbia rivers will also receive improvements ($500,000), highlighting the intersection of Indigenous fisheries and river ecology.

## Key Findings
- [[Condit Dam]] removal, announced August 1999, will occur within seven years of the agreement, following continued dam operation to offset removal costs via power generation.
- Cost comparison: $30 million for fish-passage upgrades (license renewal requirement) vs. $17.1 million for the full removal/mitigation package ($13.6M removal, $2M permitting/mitigation, $1M fishery improvements, $0.5M cultural fishing site improvements).
- Projected post-restoration adult salmonid run sizes (per Lee Van Tussenbrook, WA Dept. of Fish and Wildlife):
  - ~700 [[Steelhead Trout]] adults
  - ~4,000 [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] adults
  - ~1,100 [[Fall Chinook Salmon]] adults
  - ~2,000 [[Coho Salmon]] adults
- Full natural fishery restoration is estimated to take approximately 10 years, including tributary rehabilitation.
- Removal methodology (per a prior engineering study): breach the dam, drain the reservoir (Northwest Lake), dismantle the structure, then assess and manage sediment before habitat restoration begins.
- The agreement represents a rare cooperative model between a private utility ([[PacifiCorp]]), Indigenous nations, and conservation groups (e.g., [[American Rivers]]), described as a "milestone" after seven years of negotiation.
- Potential future designation of the [[White Salmon River]] as a [[National Wild and Scenic River]] would extend protected status from headwaters to the Columbia, elevating its conservation significance within the Pacific Northwest's anadromous fish habitat network.
