# ASU Research Park Fish Sampling Memorandum (May 1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum from the Arizona State University (ASU) Center for Environmental Studies, Tempe, reports a fish survey of lakes at the [[ASU Research Park]] on 15–16 May 1990. Trammel and experimental [[Gill Net]] sets were fished overnight in Lakes A, B, C and E. The survey captured 614 fish (the table total reads 624; the scan is ambiguous) representing 13 taxa. Catches were dominated by [[Threadfin Shad]] and included endangered natives: [[Razorback Sucker]] (17 fish) and [[Bonytail]] (5 fish, Lake E only). The central issue is whether [[Razorback Sucker]] are reproducing naturally. Two apparent size groups were found, and the author argues that the smaller group is probably of natural origin, though this is not yet confirmed. The memo closes with a plan for seining, autumn netting, earlier larval sampling and a possible [[Recruitment Dynamics]] study.

## Background & Description

The survey aimed to assess the status of fish populations in the lakes at the ASU Research Park. Nets were deployed overnight, and seining was not conducted during this visit. The memo was circulated to interested parties.

Key species and attributes noted in the text:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): A federally listed native catostomid. The park has had only one authorised stocking, in 1986. The memo also discusses scale and vertebra ageing.
- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*): A native cyprinid listed as endangered. Five were captured, all from Lake E. The memo reports a mean of 33.3 cm TL for eight April-captured individuals, with a maximum of 34.6 cm.
- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): Not captured during the survey. Private parties reported catching fish they described as Colorado squawfish over the previous two years.
- **Introduced and non-native species:** [[Threadfin Shad]], [[Goldfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Channel Catfish]], yellow bullhead, [[Green Sunfish]], Mozambique tilapia and redbelly tilapia. All had been previously recorded at ASU Research Park.

Sampling gear:

- **[[Trammel Net]]:** Six sets, fished overnight in one lake (not specified in the scan).
- **[[Gill Net]]:** Experimental sets, fished overnight in Lakes A and C.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The park's fish community is dominated by introduced species. [[Threadfin Shad]] made up the large majority of the catch (about 80% of total numbers per the text), and were taken only in gill nets. [[Bluegill]] (43 fish, about 7%) and [[Largemouth Bass]] (about 3%) were the next most numerous species.

Lake-specific patterns:

- **Lake A:** [[Razorback Sucker]] was the most abundant native (7 individuals).
- **Lake E:** [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Threadfin Shad]] were dominant.
- **Lake C:** [[Bluegill]] and [[Largemouth Bass]] were predominant.
- **Lake E only:** All five [[Bonytail]] were captured here.

Angler interaction is a conservation concern. During netting, a young angler said he had repeatedly caught a [[Bonytail]] on hook-and-line and released it. He was unaware of the fish's native status and endangered listing. Interpretive signs had been posted but evidently were not reaching anglers. The memo concludes that public awareness needs to be expanded.

## Key Findings

**1. Catch composition**
- Total catch: 614 fish (table total 624) from 13 taxa.
- Table 1 lists catches by lake and gear. Its two gill-net effort rows give the sampling effort in m (values not fully legible in the scan).

**2. [[Razorback Sucker]] (n = 17)**
- Captured in all lakes.
- Two apparent size groups were present:
  - **Small group** (n = 4 mentioned as netted dead): mean 17.3 ± 3.8 cm TL in the text; the printed range (31.5–37.0 cm) conflicts with this mean and the scan is partly garbled.
  - **Larger group:** Larger individuals with no overlap in TL or weight with the small group. Weights are partly illegible; one value appears as 1,333 ± 362 g.
- The largest "small" fish (37.0 cm) has not been caught since September 1987, the memo notes.
- Only one authorised stocking occurred at ASU Research Park, in 1986.

**3. Interpretation of the two size groups**
- **Hypothesis A (larger group):** Consistent with the 1986 stocked cohort, which the author notes has been followed at the hatchery and in the field.
- **Hypothesis B (small group):** Probably produced by natural reproduction in the park lakes, possibly in winter or autumn 1986 or 1987, since no stocking was made after 1986. Two distinct bands were seen on the scales of some specimens, which may be annuli, though the author cautions that this is not confirmed. If the fish are natural, at least two cohorts are present.
- The author argues it is unlikely that the small group was missed in earlier sampling, because they were captured in 1986 and 1987 and the apparent size difference was maintained across years.

**4. Limitations in detecting natural reproduction**
- Sampling began in mid-February, but spawning may have occurred earlier in winter.
- Larval sampling had not been conducted in time to catch larvae before they moved beyond the protolarval stage.
- Smallest [[Razorback Sucker]] (under about 10 cm) may avoid nets because they live in deeper water (over about 1 m) or in macrophyte beds.

**5. [[Bonytail]] (n = 5)**
- Captured only in Lake E.
- Angler interview indicated the species is occasionally caught by hook-and-line, which raises concern about public awareness of native fish.

**6. [[Colorado Squawfish]]**
- None caught during this survey. Private reports of catches over the past two years have not been verified. The author suggests the species may simply move little in the park and is therefore not captured by passive gear.

**7. Planned follow-up**
- Seining is planned for summer 1990.
- Additional [[Gill Net]] sampling is scheduled for autumn.
- Larval sampling will begin earlier, perhaps in October.
- Sampling strategies for small juvenile [[Razorback Sucker]] (under about 10 cm) in unsampled habitats (water deeper than about 1 m and macrophyte beds) are under consideration.
- If natural reproduction is confirmed by collecting larvae or small juveniles, a [[Recruitment Dynamics]] study will be developed.
