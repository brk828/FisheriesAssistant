# Arizona Game & Fish Department University Collaboration Meeting Proposal

#wildlife-management #interagency-collaboration #administrative

## Executive Summary

This 1987 correspondence from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] proposes establishing annual collaborative meetings between the Department and Arizona universities ([[University of Arizona]], [[Northern Arizona University]], [[Arizona State University]]) to promote cooperative wildlife research efforts, avoid duplication, and achieve mutual resource management objectives more efficiently. The letter, addressed to Dr. Wendell Minckley at [[Arizona State University]], outlines a structured meeting format following a successful initial session with the [[University of Arizona]] in early 1986.

## Background & Description

The Wildlife Management Division of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], under Director Temple A. Reynolds and Chief Bruce Taubert, initiated this collaborative framework to strengthen relationships between state wildlife management agencies and university natural resource departments. The proposal emerged from a successful pilot meeting held in early 1986 with the [[University of Arizona]] Department of Renewable Natural Resources, where presentations were exchanged and Department personnel found the session highly valuable.

The meeting format specifies rotating annual venues among institutions: [[University of Arizona]] (1986, completed), [[Northern Arizona University]] (1987, planning stage), and [[Arizona State University]] (1988, to be planned). Coordinators from both the Department and universities would arrange sessions with alternating host locations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collaboration addresses wildlife resources broadly across Arizona's diverse ecosystems. The proposal emphasizes field studies rather than literature reviews, indicating focus on empirical research relevant to state wildlife management priorities. The framework explicitly mentions potential expansion to include multiple university departments (Range, Forestry, Zoology), Department regions and divisions (Special Services, Information & Education), other state and federal agencies, and community colleges, suggesting comprehensive ecosystem and wildlife management scope.

## Key Findings

**Meeting Structure Requirements:**
- In-progress study presentations: 10-15 minutes maximum
- Final report presentations: 20-30 minutes maximum
- Future study proposals: 5 minutes maximum
- All presentations must use slide talks and/or visual aids
- Field studies only; literature reviews excluded

**Coordination Elements:**
- Meeting agenda prepared by Department and University coordinators
- Program chairman/committee establishes presentation schedules and priorities
- Selection process may exclude proposals due to time constraints or inappropriate content
- Exchange periods include panel discussions, workshops, and question-and-answer sessions

**Strategic Objectives:**
- Promote cooperative effort between agencies and universities
- Avoid duplication of research efforts
- Achieve mutual objectives more efficiently and economically
- Use existing Department plans, goals, and objectives as subjects for current and future studies
- Establish friendly acquaintances and better cooperative relationships
- Social period planned following each scheduled meeting

**Implementation Status (May 1987):**
- Initial concept well-received by contacted addressees
- Primary challenge identified: fitting meetings into crowded schedules
- Solution proposed: advanced planning and scheduling
