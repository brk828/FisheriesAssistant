## Executive Summary
This document is an internal correspondence memo from the Arizona State University Department of Zoology (authored by Patti Sowka, addressed to W. L. Minckley) reporting on a conservation stocking event for the endangered **[[Gila topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) at the Phoenix Zoo. The memo details a pre-stocking survey of the African Veldt exhibit pond/moat (which yielded no fish or crayfish), followed by the transport and release of topminnows sourced from Heron Spring stock (originally derived from **[[Sharp Spring]]**) under a Federal Permit administered by the Arizona Game and Fish Department. This record documents an active reintroduction/refugial population management effort for a federally listed species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Recipient habitat:** African Veldt exhibit at the **[[Phoenix Zoo]]**, comprising a pond and connected moat, functioning as an artificial refuge/exhibit habitat for the species.
- **Source population lineage:** Individuals were collected from **[[Heron Spring]]**, a site itself stocked from fish originally collected at **[[Sharp Spring]]** — indicating a chain of translocations typical of endangered species refuge management.
- **Pre-stocking survey:** Seining of the pond and moat prior to stocking found **no fish or crayfish** present, confirming the habitat was unoccupied and suitable (or at least unoccupied by competitors/predators) for introduction.
- **Connectivity risk:** The memo notes the topminnows were released directly into the veldt pond but could potentially disperse into the adjoining moat and subsequently into the **main lake** of the exhibit complex, representing a potential unintended range expansion within the zoo's water system.
- **Regulatory context:** Collection and transport were conducted under a **Federal Permit** issued through the **Arizona Game and Fish Department**, reflecting the species' protected status under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.

## Key Findings
- **Survey result:** No fish or crayfish detected in the Phoenix Zoo veldt pond/moat prior to stocking (23 October 1996).
- **Stocking event:** Gila topminnows transported in five ice chests from the ASU Animal Care Center to the Phoenix Zoo (30 October 1996) and released into the veldt pond.
- **Source-to-sink chain:** Sharp Spring → Heron Spring → Phoenix Zoo veldt pond, illustrating a multi-step conservation translocation pathway.
- **Dispersal concern:** Potential for fish to move from the stocking pond into the moat and main lake was explicitly flagged as a monitoring consideration.
- **Institutional collaboration:** Effort involved coordination between **[[Arizona State University]]** (Patti Sowka, Ruby Sheffer), the **[[Phoenix Zoo]]** (Jeff Williamson), and the **Arizona Game and Fish Department**, exemplifying a multi-agency approach to endangered species refugial management.
