# Geochemical Characterization of Late Pleistocene and Holocene Tephra Layers from the Basin of Mexico

#tephrochronology #paleolimnology #quaternary-geology

## Executive Summary

This paper geochemically characterizes tephra (volcanic ash and pumice) layers preserved in lake sediments of the [[Chalco Sub-Basin]] and [[Texcoco Sub-Basin]], in the southern [[Basin of Mexico]]. The authors use glass-shard electron microprobe analysis, petrography and [[Radiocarbon Dating]] to build a stratigraphic framework for the Late Pleistocene and Holocene, with tephra ages ranging from >34,000 to ca. 2600 ¹⁴C yr B.P. The study is explicitly aimed at palaeoenvironmental research. It is a geology and tephrochronology study, not a fish or aquatic-biota study. The document contains no fish, invertebrate, plankton or water-chemistry data.

## Background & Description

- **Subject:** Tephra layers (airfall volcanic deposits of glass shards, pumice, minerals and lithic fragments) in lake sediments. These serve as stratigraphic markers and isochrones, which are time-equivalent layers that link sediment sequences.
- **Study area:** The Basin of Mexico is a hydrologically closed basin in the central-eastern [[Transmexican Volcanic Belt]]. A Quaternary lake system developed there, with the [[Texcoco Sub-Basin]] the largest in the central part and the [[Chalco Sub-Basin]] and [[Xochimilco Sub-Basin]] to the south, separated by the Santa Catarina Range. Lake sediments reach up to 350 m thick.
- **Volcanic sources:** Stratovolcanoes of the [[Sierra Nevada]] ([[Popocatepetl]] and [[Iztaccihuatl]]) and [[Nevado de Toluca]] are the main sources discussed. The [[Chichinautzin Range]] is a monogenetic volcanic field whose most recent activity is the [[Xitle Volcano]]. Popocatepetl resumed activity in December 1994. Nearly 25% of Mexico's population lives in this volcanically active region.
- **Previous work:** Earlier tephra identifications in the Chalco area were largely based on subjective criteria, and no geochemical data had been published. Key earlier tephra names included the "gran ceniza basáltica" (GCB), "pómez gruesa con fragmentos de andesita" (PCA) and "pómez marcadora superior" (PMS).
- **Methods:**
  - Tephra was sampled from cores B, D and E (Lake Chalco) and core T (Lake Texcoco).
  - Radiocarbon ages were AMS dates on pollen or wood collected just below the tephra layers. Ages for undated layers were inferred by linear interpolation.
  - Petrography used thin sections with at least 200 point counts per sample.
  - Major-element glass chemistry used electron probe microanalysis (Cambridge Instruments Microscan V; 20 kV accelerating voltage, 15 nA beam current, beam diameter about 8 µm), with ZAF correction.
  - Only glass analyses with totals between 95% and 100% were accepted. Data are presented unnormalized.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Palaeoenvironmental value:** Lake basins such as Chalco and Texcoco hold long palaeoenvironmental records of climatic and environmental change. Reliable tephrochronology is needed to date these records, since dating is problematic in the region.
- **Depositional contrast:** Between ca. 34,000 and 14,500 ¹⁴C yr B.P., the depositional rate was about 0.63 mm/yr in Chalco versus about 0.28 mm/yr in Texcoco. The authors attribute this to greater subsidence in Chalco, which allowed a thicker stratigraphic column to build up.
- **Lacustrine and organic units:** Tephra layers are interbedded with lacustrine silts, diatomite and organic (black and olive-gray) silts. Pollen is used for dating.
- **Hazard relevance:** Popocatepetl tephra has been found in the Texcoco basin about 60 km from the eruption site. Xitle is cited as a potential threat to the area.
- **Human disturbance:** Farming disturbs soil to at least 40 cm, and water extraction produces cracks that can move younger material into older sediment. This is a possible contamination concern for one radiocarbon date.

## Key Findings

**Tephra stratigraphy and chemistry**
- Tephra glass ranges from basaltic andesite to rhyolite and is calc-alkaline.
- SiO₂ is bimodal. Most tephras contain 53 to 65 wt% SiO₂ (andesites and basaltic andesites), with a smaller more-acidic group at 67 to 76 wt% (dacites to rhyolites).
- The study identifies 11 tephra layers, listed from oldest to youngest below. The source of the tephras is inferred, not confirmed.

| Tephra | Approximate age (¹⁴C yr B.P.) | Key notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tlahuac | >34,000 | Basalt-andesite; Chalco at 18.58 m, SE Texcoco ~10 m, Tlapacoya. Previously called GCB. |
| Tx4 | Not directly dated | Texcoco core T; correlated with Tlahuac (sample b). Lower sample a is distinct. |
| Miahuacan | ca. 16,000 | Andesitic; cannot be correlated with other layers. |
| Tlapacoya 1 | 15,020 ± 450 to 14,430 ± 190 | Gray andesitic to rhyolitic ash; present in all Chalco sections. |
| Tlapacoya 2 | ca. 14,400 | Yellowish lapilli plus black ash (PCA); present in all Chalco and Texcoco sections. |
| Chimalpa | 14,015 ± 130 | Basaltic andesite to andesite; overlies San Martin. |
| San Martin | 13,990 ± 100 | Dacitic to rhyolitic; overlies Tlapacoya 2. |
| Tephra V | ca. 14,000 | Andesitic to dacitic; resembles Tlapacoya 1 and San Martin. Less reliable identification. |
| Tephra II | 12,520 ± 135 | Rhyolitic; correlates with Upper Toluca Pumice. Up to 15 cm thick. |
| Tephra I | 9395 ± 255 | Basaltic andesite to andesite; most similar to Chimalpa. |
| Huitzilzingo | 2645 ± 55 | Rhyolitic, creamy white ash; date may be unreliable. |

**Main conclusions and interpretations**
- **Tlahuac tephra as a regional marker:** This is a widespread horizon linking the Chalco and Texcoco sub-basins. Geochemistry shows it is basaltic andesite, not basaltic as previously reported, so the term GCB should no longer be used.
- **Tlapacoya 1 and 2 and the repeating sequence:** Tlapacoya 1 is overlain by the less-silicic Tlapacoya 2. The silicic San Martin tephra is overlain by the less-silicic Chimalpa tephra. The authors suggest this may reflect two events during one eruption, with decreasing SiO₂.
- **Likely source:** Popocatepetl is the probable source of the Tlahuac, Tlapacoya 1 and Tlapacoya 2 tephras. The authors note thinning toward the northwest, from Chalco to Texcoco, as supporting evidence.
- **Upper Toluca Pumice correlation:** Tephra II is geochemically similar to the Upper Toluca Pumice (FeO comparison), and it is the first electron-microprobe analysis of UTP glass. Its radiocarbon age of 12,520 ± 135 is about 1000 years older than the widely cited mean of 11,630 ± 70 for the UTP. Other UTP dates range from 11,110 ± 50 to 12,900 ± 400, so UTP dating remains inconsistent.
- **Unresolved correlations:** The Huitzilzingo tephra is rhyolitic and younger than the andesitic PMS, so the two cannot be correlated. Miahuacan cannot be correlated with any other layer.
- **Limitations:** The authors state that the first geochemical glass-shard analyses for the Basin of Mexico were produced, but that a reliable tephrochronology still requires more geochemical data and better radiocarbon dates. Many dates have large errors because of low organic content in the sediments.
- **Recommended approach:** Use stratigraphic, chronologic and geochemical data together. Geochemistry alone should not be used without supporting evidence.
