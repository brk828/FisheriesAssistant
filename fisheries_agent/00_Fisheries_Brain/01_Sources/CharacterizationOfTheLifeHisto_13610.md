# Humpback Chub Life History and Ecology in the Grand Canyon: Trip #7 Report (1991)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This preliminary trip report from [[BIO/WEST Inc.]], prepared for the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and dated August 5, 1991, documents Trip #7 of a [[Humpback Chub]] study in the [[Grand Canyon]]. The field trip ran from July 9 to July 28, 1991. Crews handled 133 humpback chub, most of them in Reach 1 between RM 57.0 and RM 64.8 in the main channel near the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) confluence. The team also recorded several non-native and sport fish species, four new radio-transmitter implants, and evidence that some chub were still using the LCR, possibly for spawning. Equipment problems included lost telemetry data, a lost hoop net, and a damaged outboard motor. The report closes with recommendations for telemetry station maintenance, expanded sampling, and aerial photography. All data are described by the authors as preliminary.

## Background & Description

The report is a trip summary rather than a full research paper. It covers logistics, personnel, data collected, problems, observations, and recommendations. Most results appear in tables, which are not reproduced here.

- **Study species:** [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a native Colorado River cyprinid. Its adults showed breeding tubercles and coloration, and the study tracks its movements and life stages.
- **Field methods:** [[Electrofishing]], [[Trammel Net]] and gill net sets, [[Minnow Trap]] sets, hoop nets, seines, angling, and [[Radiotelemetry]] with external antennas. Fish were tagged with [[PIT Tagging]] tags and Carlin and Floy tags to identify recaptures.
- **Personnel:** Two field teams. Project leaders were Richard A. Valdez (Principal Investigator), William J. Masslich, and William Leibfried. David R. VanHaverbeke was Senior Biologist. Team members included Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) staff, Grand Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) and NPS-linked volunteers, and OARS volunteers. AGFD personnel mostly ran separate, concurrent research.
- **Schedule:** Team 1 and Team 2 followed river itineraries with reach-based activities: travel and reconnaissance (T&R), fish sampling (FS), telemetry (TE), and habitat quantification (HQ).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat use:** Chub were found in the main channel below the LCR confluence, in the LCR and its lower intermittent channel, and in the mixing zone where LCR water meets main-channel water. Water in the mixing zone was about 20 °C. Juveniles and young-of-year (YOY) were found in low-velocity main-channel habitat. Minnow traps caught them effectively there.
- **Spawning signals:** Five radio-tagged chub were located in the LCR, and a gravid female was captured in the mixing zone. Together these suggest that spawning may still be going on in July.
- **Movement:** Netting catch rates between RM 57.3 and RM 65.5 were higher than in May, which suggests that fish were returning from the LCR to the main channel after spawning. Catch rates between RM 60.0 and RM 61.8 remained below winter levels.
- **Co-occurring species:** Carp were numerous near the confluence and may prey on the eggs and larvae of native fish. Aggregations of flannelmouth suckers, including ripe males, were found in the confluence mixing zone and between RM 60.0 and RM 61.2. Striped bass, channel catfish, and walleye were also collected in Reaches II and III.
- **Non-native fish:** Striped bass were found far upstream, at RM 156.4, and the team recorded them at every site down to Diamond Creek.

## Key Findings

**Humpback chub captures and distribution**

- A total of **133 humpback chub** were handled during Trip #7.
- **122 chub** were captured in Reach 1 (RM 57.0 to RM 64.8). This group included **67 adults, 49 juveniles, and 6 YOY**. Twenty-seven were recaptures bearing Carlin, Floy, or PIT tags.
- Almost all large juveniles came from the main channel at or below the LCR confluence, using electrofishing or minnow traps.
- **11 chub** were collected in Reach II. Ten were new fish and one was a recapture from earlier [[BIO/WEST Inc.]] work.
- Two new chub collection sites were found:
  - **RM 118.5:** one running-ripe male with extreme tubercles, taken by electrofishing. It was very robust and expressing gametes.
  - **RM 127.0:** four chub, found in the Middle Granite Gorge.
- Six new chub were collected near [[Shinumo Creek]] at RM 108.4.

**Recaptured radio-tagged chub**

- This fish was radio-tagged in October 1990 and released at RM 64.7. Its last telemetry contact was in December 1990, at the same location.
- By November 1991 it had moved several tenths of a mile up and downstream of its release point. It was later recaptured at RM 127.0, about **50 miles downstream**.
- The transmitter was assumed expired, so the external antenna was removed to let the exit wound heal. The fish was in good condition with complete healing of its midline sutures, though it had lost about 46 grams. Photographs and video were taken.

**Radiotelemetry results**

- Four chub were newly implanted with radio transmitters during the trip.
- Six chub tagged on earlier trips were recontacted. Three were in the lower 200 m of the LCR at one or more contacts.
- Two of the newly implanted fish moved into the lower 200 m of the LCR shortly after surgery and stayed there for the rest of the trip.
- Five radio-tagged chub in total were found in the LCR.

**Non-native and sport fish**

- **12 [[Striped Bass]]**, **19 [[Channel Catfish]]**, and **one [[Walleye]]** (*Stizostedion vitreum*) were collected from Reaches II and III. Six were taken by electrofishing, six by netting, and one by angling.
- Stomachs were taken from all bass, the walleye, and most catfish. All samples were preserved for food habit analysis in the laboratory.
- Striped bass were found **58 miles farther upstream in July than in May 1991**, and the most upstream capture was at RM 156.4.
- One walleye was collected at RM 179.8.

**Condition and reproduction**

- Nearly all chub appeared robust, vigorous, and in good condition. The exception was one recaptured PIT-tagged fish that was thin and slightly emaciated.
- Adult chub were still in various stages of reproductive condition, but the intensity of sexual characters (tubercles and coloration) had declined.
- The capture of 6 YOY (under 50 mm) suggests that smaller size classes are dispersing into the main channel.

**Problems encountered**

- About **one month of data** was lost from the LCR remote telemetry station. The data logger and receiver were found non-operational, and the scrambled data and software faults point to a likely static discharge (lightning). Lightning arresters or other protection are planned.
- One hoop net was lost on July 18 in the lower intermittent channel of the LCR, probably during high flows. The net was not recovered. Future sets there will use heavier line.
- One outboard motor lower unit was damaged at Granite Falls during 5000 cfs test flows. It was replaced, and no sampling effort was lost.

**Recommendations**

1. Keep the LCR remote telemetry station running until Trip #8 (1991), since many chub still use the LCR and information on movement timing is essential to the study. Maintenance will be coordinated with NPS and GCES. Future stations will get lightning arresters or similar protection.
2. Expand sampling below Lava/Chuar in September, because juvenile catch rates in the main channel below the confluence to RM 65.5 were high. Trip #8 should focus mainly on radiotelemetry and habitat evaluation.
3. Request 1:2400 black-and-white aerial photo coverage of the LCR up to about 2 km above the confluence. The photos would be used to build photographic river guides with mylar overlays, so that fish locations can be mapped for movement data and [[Habitat Quantification]] work.
