# Fishes of the Rio Tamesi and Related Coastal Lagoons in East-Central Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes #euryhaline-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1962 faunal and zoogeographic paper, by [[Reeve M. Darnell]] of Marquette University, reports fishes from the [[Rio Tamesi]] tributary system and the [[Laguna de Chairel]] near Tampico, Mexico. Based on 11,043 specimens from 66 collections (1950–53), plus literature records, the author recognizes 60 species: 23 freshwater and 37 euryhaline. Twelve species are endemic to the [[Panuco-Tamesi System]], all strictly freshwater. The paper argues that eastern Mexico, including the [[Tampico Embayment]], is a transition zone between northern (temperate) and southern (tropical) faunas, and that freshwater taxa of southern origin dominate small upstream waters while northern taxa occupy larger downstream waters.

## Background & Description

- **Author and purpose:** Darnell began ecological studies of headwater aquatic communities in 1950. Taxonomic problems and the long-delayed Hubbs and Gordon manuscript on northeastern Mexican fishes motivated publication of this background paper.
- **Study scope:** Originally intended for freshwater fishes only, the scope was expanded to include brackish and marine species from coastal lagoons, because many headwater species do not respect strict freshwater limits.
- **Collecting methods:** Minnow seines (14-inch mesh), a 150-foot bag seine, fyke nets, hook, spear, minnow traps, gill nets, and window screening. Diurnal and nocturnal collections were made, and behavioral observations were carried out in clear upper waters of the [[Rio Sabinas]].
- **Specimen identification:** Preliminary identifications by Darnell; checks by Robert R. Miller and Myron Gordon; final species determinations by [[Carl L. Hubbs]].
- **Deposition:** 1950–51 material went to the Chicago Natural History Museum (CNHM); 1952–53 material went to Tulane University (TU).
- **Historical periods:** A descriptive period (1901–1908; Jordan and Snyder, Meek, Jordan and Dickerson) and a synthetic period beginning with Hubbs (1926). The fauna was largely neglected for about half a century.
- **Taxonomic policy:** No subspecies are recognized, following Bailey et al. (1954) and Bailey (1956), who questioned subspecies validity.
- **Geography:** The Tamesi system lies just south of the Tropic of Cancer on an alluvial plain sloping eastward from the [[Sierra Madre Oriental]] to the Gulf of Mexico, roughly 100–150 km wide, with the plain at about 100 m elevation at the mountain foot.
- **Precipitation:** Strongly seasonal; front ranges receive an estimated 2,000–2,500 mm annually, with the wettest months June and September.
- **Drainage:** Major tributaries (north to south) are the [[Rio Guayalejo]], [[Rio Sabinas]], [[Rio Frio]], [[Rio Boquilla]], and [[Rio Mante]], joining to form the Rio Tamesi, which meets the Rio Panuco near Tampico. Many headwater streams are spring-fed "nacimientos" with dry-season isolation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Headwater habitats:** Spring-fed arroyos (e.g., [[Arroyo Encino]], [[Arroyo La Flor]], Arroyo Sarco) with pools, riffles, and backwaters; some reduced to disconnected ponds in the dry season, acting as barriers to dispersal.
- **Lowland habitats:** Sluggish, turbid streams with silt or shale bottoms (lower Rio Sabinas, Rio Frio), and marshes and shallow lagoons around Tampico.
- **Anthropogenic impacts:** Sewage and organic pollution at Encino and Ciudad Mante; the Rio Mante dammed with a reservoir and irrigation diversions.
- **Dispersal barriers:** Lower Sabinas and Frio sections, and salinity barriers along the coast. Fluctuations in coastal lagoon freshness (tied to Gulf water level and barrier beach processes) likely modulated coastwise dispersal.
- **Notable observations:** Dry-season water chemistry at one spring showed 3.2 ppm dissolved oxygen and 28.0 ppm CO2, under which only [[Xiphophorus variatus]] persisted.

## Key Findings

- **Species richness:** 60 species total (23 freshwater, 37 euryhaline). Distribution counts by area: Rio Sabinas 16, Rio Frio 13, Rio Mante 10, Rio Guayalejo 20, Tampico coastal lagoons and nearby areas 45.
- **Endemism:** 12 species are endemic to the Panuco-Tamesi system, all strictly freshwater.
- **Faunal affinity:** Among freshwater species, southern families slightly outnumber northern families (13:10). Among euryhaline species, southern families greatly predominate (35:2), or 25:12 when northern representatives of tropical families are counted.
- **Habitat pattern:** Southern-derived freshwater fishes (poeciliids, *Astyanax fasciatus*, cichlids) inhabit small upstream waters. Northern-derived fishes (gars, suckers, shiners, catfishes) occupy larger, deeper, downstream waters.
- **Freshwater affinities:** Seven of 15 strictly freshwater species are northern and eight are southern. Strictly freshwater northern forms are primary-division families; southern forms are secondary-division.
- **Salinity-based classification:** Freshwater species were grouped into strictly freshwater and facultative brackish invaders. Euryhaline species were grouped into anadromous (4), catadromous (2), frequent freshwater invaders (5), sporadic freshwater invaders (9), and marine facultative low-salinity invaders (17).
- **Key species notes:**
  - [[Astyanax fasciatus]]: 2,025 specimens, present in nearly every freshwater collection; vegetation made up 62% of stomach contents, and spawning was inferred to begin no earlier than mid-June.
  - [[Gambusia vittata]]: 1,139 specimens; algae-rich diet (about 50% filamentous algae) unusual for the genus; females fed mainly on algae near parturition.
  - [[Gambusia regani]]: 171 specimens; diet over 95% animal matter, dominated by chironomid larvae.
  - [[Mollienesia sphenops]]: 2,949 specimens; the most abundant species in the Tamesi along with *A. fasciatus* and *Dionda rasconis*.
  - [[Cichlasoma cyanoguttatum]]: 778 specimens; bursa structure serves as storage; gut length ratio about 2.5.
  - [[Cichlasoma steindachneri]]: 365 specimens; predominantly carnivorous (gut ratio about 1.2); nest-building observed in April 1951.
  - [[Gobiomorus dormitor]]: catadromous status provisional; nocturnal terrestrial foraging documented.
- **Gambusia partitioning:** The three gambusias ([[Gambusia panuco]], [[Gambusia regani]], [[Gambusia affinis]]) have mutually exclusive distributions, suggesting competitive elimination among ecologically equivalent species.
- **Hybridization concern:** Possible introgression between *Xiphophorus variatus* and *X. montezumae* in the Rio Sabinas, based on tail spotting; the author recommended genetic analysis.
- **Zoogeographic framework:** The Panuco-Tamesi system is a zone of greatest overlap between northern and southern faunas. Freshwater northern elements appear to have arrived when northern barriers were effective, with southern secondary-division forms dispersing coastwise through brackish water.
- **Regional transition:** The main faunal break for euryhaline and littoral marine fishes lies near Tampico, shifting from croakers and weakfishes (Sciaenidae) to robalos and mojarras.
- **Conservation note:** A young fer-de-lance (*Bothrops atrox*) was captured near Station 4D, marking an approximate northern distribution limit for this species.
- **Taxonomic revisions noted:** *Cichlasoma laurae* synonymized with *C. cyanoguttatum*; *Ictalurus australis* and *I. mexicanus* confirmed as distinct species; *Fundulus grandis* shifted to euryhaline category.
- **Unrecorded species:** Mountain mullet (*Agonostomus monticola*) and *Joturus pichardi* were only observed, not captured. Several marine species listed for Tampico (e.g., Lutjanus cyanopterus, Pogonias cromis) rest on early literature records or require verification.
- **Acknowledgment of gaps:** Larger streams and Tampico lagoons were inadequately sampled; many listed species await confirmation.
