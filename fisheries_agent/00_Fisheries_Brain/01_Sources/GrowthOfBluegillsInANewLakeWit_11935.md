# Growth of Bluegills in a New Lake, with Particular Reference to False Annuli

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[George Sprugel Jr.]] of [[Iowa State College]] examines the age and growth of [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) in [[McFarland Pond]], a newly built 7-acre impoundment in Story County, Iowa. The population descended from 23 two-year-old fish stocked in May 1947. Scales from the 1947–1949 year classes were used to age fish using the [[Scale Method]], with particular attention to separating [[False Annuli]] (supernumerary scale marks) from true [[Annulus]] formation. The author concludes that false marks are common in some year classes, that they are linked to sexual activity after the first year, and that the population became severely stunted by 1949. The paper also compares a linear and a third-degree [[Body-Scale Relationship]] and offers practical criteria for recognising false marks.

## Background & Description

The [[Scale Method]] was widely used for fish age determination, but the author argues that misreading scale marks can produce erroneous growth conclusions. The study was part of Project No. 42 of the [[Iowa Cooperative Fisheries Research Unit]], and draws on a PhD dissertation completed in December 1950.

**Study site.** McFarland Pond was constructed in fall 1946. It has a surface area of 7 acres and a maximum depth of 23 feet. Thermal stratification and subsurface stagnation were pronounced, so dissolved oxygen was severely depleted at depths of 2 m or more in late winter and summer. Fish were confined to shallower water during those periods (see [[Thermal Stratification]]).

**Stocking.** The population originated from 23 two-year-old bluegills, 72 to 120 mm total length, stocked on May 23, 1947.

**Sampling.** Hoop nets, a minnow seine, basket traps, experimental gill nets and angling were used. Scales were taken from 1,235 bluegills between May 1949 and July 1950. Scales were removed near the posterior tip of the pectoral fin, mounted dry between microscope slides, and read four times on a microprojector at 42.0 diameters. Fewer than 5 percent were rejected as unreadable.

**Length conversions.** Based on 985 specimens, and applied without regard to sex or length, the factors were: standard to fork length 1.217; standard to total length 1.300; fork to total length 1.068.

**Condition.** The [[Coefficient of Condition]] (K) was calculated from weight and standard length. Table 1 ([[Length-Weight Relationship]] data, based on 981 fish) showed a progressive increase in K beyond about 40 mm standard length. Condition was higher in May to July for fish older than 1 year, probably due to sexual development. No consistent sex difference in K was found.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Annulus timing.** Annuli formed from mid-February to mid-June, with most fish completing them in the last half of May or early June. Annulus formation was completed by the first week in June in 1949, and all bluegills had completed annulus production by mid-June. Earlier annulus formation in 1949 was roughly correlated with earlier rises in water temperature, though the relationship was not clear-cut. The author notes that Burress reported new annuli in Missouri ponds as early as February 22, and Beckman reported progressively later formation from south to north in Michigan.

**Growing season.** Growth began about when new annuli formed. Most growth occurred in late May and early to mid-June, and most of the season's growth was complete by early July. Young-of-the-year fish grew rapidly to late June 1949, then slowly until perhaps late September or early October. Yearlings showed little or no growth after late July. Activity was restricted to shallow water from mid-June because of stagnation and oxygen depletion.

**Food and growth.** The author suggests food scarcity, possibly from a change in the type of food eaten by fry, may have affected the 1947 year class. Delayed spawning is another explanation: the brood stock showed no sign of readiness to spawn when released on May 23, 1947, which may have shortened the 1947 growing season.

**Stunting.** By 1949 the population was severely stunted. The average second-year increment of 15 mm was described as lower than any reported for that year of life for bluegills in other studies. Fish in age-groups I and II showed little growth from July 1949 to July 1950.

## Key Findings

**Growth and age structure**

- The 1945 year class, which spent its first two years in a different water body, grew faster than the later pond-spawned year classes in corresponding years of life.
- First-year growth improved from the 1947 to the 1949 year class, but growth increments fell sharply in the 1947 and 1948 year classes as age increased.
- Males grew faster than females after the first year, though the 1945 year class showed female growth nearly double that of males in its fourth year.
- The faster-growing 1947 year-class fish appear to have disappeared before their third year, leaving slower-growing individuals. The author notes this may also be partly due to sampling error or to false marks being read as true annuli.

**Body-scale relationship**

- Scale size relative to body length (L/Sc) decreased as standard length increased, based on 1,019 fish.
- A linear regression of standard length on anterior scale radius, L = 8.41 + 0.887 S (S in mm at ×42.0), was nearly as accurate as a third-degree polynomial: L = 11.42 + 0.79469 S + 0.00048 S² + 0.000000106 S³.
- Sums of squares of deviations were 33.81 for the linear line and 4.24 for the curvilinear line, so the curve fitted better mathematically. The author judged the linear formula satisfactory for practical growth back-calculation, though slightly less accurate for larger fish.
- No consistent sex difference was found in the body-scale relationship.

**False annuli**

- Most first-year accessory marks (false annuli formed before the first true annulus) occurred in some year classes and not others. The percentages were 89.4 percent (1949 class, 228 fish), 93.8 percent (1948 class, 584 fish) and 10.6 percent (1947 class, 374 fish). The 1945 class had 80.0 percent (4 of 5 fish). Abstract phrasing rounds these to "about 90 percent" and "10 percent."
- The author argues that false marks in the first year were produced by some factor affecting the whole young-of-the-year population, not by the conditions of individual fish.
- Around 10 percent of II-group bluegills taken after June 1, 1949, and III-group fish taken in 1950 had a false mark after the first or second annulus. These marks formed in early or mid-June after a rapid increment of 20 mm or more in about three weeks.
- Only 1 percent of 549 bluegills from the 1948 year class had extra marks after the first year.
- Every II-group individual with a new false check in 1949 was male. Four I-group fish with such marks were female. In most cases sexual activity was probably involved.
- Physical and chemical water conditions during false-mark formation did not account for the marks.
- If the first checks on 1947-class fish caught in 1949 had been read as true annuli, those fish would have been aged 3 years. No age-group III fish could have been in the pond at that time, which confirmed the marks were false.

**Validity and criteria**

- Several lines of evidence support annual true annuli: similar lengths within age groups; calculated first-year lengths matching fish collected in 1949 and 1950; and increased calculated growth beyond the last annulus during the summer.
- No simple, dependable criteria were found for recognising false annuli, so no definitive rules are offered. General observations include:
  - (a) A mark preceded by widely spaced circuli and followed by closely spaced circuli is usually false.
  - (b) Adjacent scales from the same fish often show a false mark as indistinct or absent, whereas true annuli are usually clear on all scales.
  - (c) Particularly dark bands should be treated with suspicion.
  - (d) Limited anastomosis of circuli with the mark and limited extension across the postero-lateral field often indicate, but do not confirm, a false check.
  - (e) A mark whose position differs greatly from marks on other fish of the same size group within a year class may be false.

**Summary of conclusions**

The author concludes that scale-based ageing of McFarland Pond bluegills is generally valid, but that false annuli, sometimes linked to sexual activity and rapid growth, can distort age and growth estimates. He recommends caution, cross-checking adjacent scales, and awareness of year-class-specific marking patterns in fishery studies.
