# Pecos Pupfish (Cyprinodon pecosensis)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #freshwater-fish #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) proposed on January 30, 1998, to list the **Pecos Pupfish** (*Cyprinodon pecosensis*) as an **endangered species** under the Endangered Species Act (ESA), without accompanying critical habitat designation. The species, endemic to the [[Pecos River]] drainage of New Mexico and Texas, has lost more than two-thirds of its historical range, primarily due to **hybridization and competitive replacement by the introduced [[Sheepshead Minnow]]** (*Cyprinodon variegatus*), compounded by long-term habitat degradation from water diversion, groundwater depletion, channelization, and watershed disturbance. Genetically pure populations are now largely restricted to isolated sinkholes, springs, and off-channel habitats disconnected from the main river channel.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomy**: Described by Echelle and Echelle (1978); family [[Cyprinodontidae]] (pupfishes). Taxonomic status was uncertain for 30+ years due to confusion with *Cyprinodon bovinus*, originally described from the Pecos River by Baird and Girard (1853). The type specimens were lost/degraded; a surviving population of *C. bovinus* at Leon Springs, Texas, was later confirmed genetically distinct (Echelle and Miller 1974).
- **Morphology**: 
  - Small, deep-bodied fish, standard length 2.8–4.6 cm (1.1–1.8 in.)
  - Gray-to-brown coloration
  - Males: black dorsal and anal fins (near margin), **no yellow pigmentation** on dorsal, anal, or caudal fins (key diagnostic distinguishing it from hybrids/*C. variegatus*)
  - Females: lateral bars typically broken into ventrolateral blotches
  - Abdomen largely naked (scaleless) except a few scales anterior to pelvic fins and a patch behind the gill membrane isthmus
  - 20–21 gill rakers
  - Usually 3–4 preorbital pores per side of head

- **Historical Range**: 
  - Pecos River mainstream from [[Bitter Lake National Wildlife Refuge]] and [[Bottomless Lakes State Park]] (near Roswell, Chaves County, NM) downstream ~650 km (404 mi) to the mouth of Independence Creek near Sheffield, Pecos County, TX
  - Associated gypsum sinkholes, saline springs, and tributary lakes, including the Salt Creek Wilderness Area and Salt Creek, Reeves County, TX

- **Current Known Distribution (Genetically Pure Populations)**:
  - Upper Salt Creek, Culberson and Reeves counties, TX
  - Possibly water-filled gravel pits (Phipps Gravel Company), Pecos County, TX (purity uncertain, unsurveyed since 1989)
  - Pecos River from north of Malaga upstream to Bitter Lake NWR, NM
  - Salt Creek Wilderness Area (North Tract), Bitter Lake NWR (sinkholes, springs, Salt Creek)
  - Bottomless Lakes State Park, NM

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat types utilized**:
- Main river channel (Pecos River)
- Low-velocity floodplain habitats — serve as refugia from high-flow events and provide elevated productivity/stable food sources for this omnivorous species
- Gypsum sinkholes and saline springs — critical refugia, historically interconnected via higher groundwater tables
- Backwaters and marshes

**Habitat degradation drivers**:
- Impoundment converting flowing water to slack water
- Altered flow regimes (perennial → intermittent/no-flow), disrupted natural flooding
- Altered sediment/bed load dynamics
- Loss of marshes and backwaters
- Temperature regime shifts
- Channel transformation: vegetated, diverse channels → deeply incised, unstable arroyos with uniform substrate
- **Salt cedar (invasive riparian vegetation)** encroachment exacerbating channel armoring and incision
- Groundwater depletion from agricultural/drinking water withdrawal, lowering water tables and disconnecting previously interlinked sinkholes

**Species interactions**:
- Competitive displacement and hybridization by nonnative [[Sheepshead Minnow]] (*Cyprinodon variegatus*), introduced ~1980–1984 near Pecos, Texas
- Minimal premating isolation mechanisms between *C. pecosensis* and *C. variegatus* → ready hybridization (Cokendolpher 1980)
- Predation pressure from introduced game fish — sinkholes with game fish show reduced pupfish densities (Echelle and Echelle 1978)
- Vulnerability increases as habitat loss reduces available refugia

**Water quality threats**:
- Algal bloom-induced fish kills documented in Pecos River, TX, in 1985 and 1986 (Rhodes and Hubbs 1992)
- Oil pipeline spills into Salt Creek, TX — ongoing water quality threat
- Piscicide application by anglers to eliminate unwanted fish prior to sport fish stocking, incidentally harming pupfish populations

## Key Findings

### Population & Range Decline
- **>2/3 of historical range lost** (Echelle and Connor 1989)
- By late 1980s, hybrid expansion had reduced range by ~60% (Wilde and Echelle 1992)
- By 1984, hybridization confirmed at four sites below Red Bluff Reservoir, TX; pupfish fully replaced by hybrids near Pecos, TX
- Hybridization signal detectable 50 km upstream to 250 km downstream of introduction point (Echelle 1985; Echelle and Connor 1989)
- 1986 surveys found sheepshead minnow genetic markers in Red Bluff Reservoir, NM pupfish (Wilde and Echelle 1992)
- 1995: hybrids detected near Loving Bridge, Eddy County, NM — upstream of the pure population at [[Malaga Bend]]
- Spread has occurred despite presence of **six irrigation diversion dams**, via both natural dispersal and "bait bucket" introductions

### Refugial Population Significance
- Sinkhole populations, isolated from river hybridization pressure, represent the best-protected genetic reservoirs of pure *C. pecosensis*
- Loss of sinkhole populations would seriously jeopardize overall species survival
- Sinkhole/spring populations remain vulnerable to:
  - Flood-mediated connectivity allowing exotic species incursion
  - Angler-mediated introduction of sheepshead minnows or game fish

### Regulatory Status (Pre-Listing)
- **New Mexico**: State-listed as Threatened (New Mexico Wildlife Conservation Act); limited habitat protection via Habitat Protection Act; no instream flow water-rights mechanism for fish/wildlife protection
- **Texas**: State-listed as Threatened (March 1, 1987) under Texas Parks and Wildlife Code §68.015; prohibits take/possession/transport but provides **no habitat protection**
- Both states permit live bait use in Pecos River habitat, facilitating continued sheepshead minnow introductions
- Federal Candidate status history: Category 2 (1982, 1985) → Category 1 (1989, 1991) → Candidate (1996, 1997) → Proposed Endangered (1998)

### Listing Determination
- Recommended status: **Endangered** (not Threatened) — reflecting severity of population decline, range contraction, and imminent hybridization/habitat threats
- **No critical habitat proposed** — USFWS determined designation would not provide additional conservation benefit beyond ESA listing protections, citing:
  - Federal land managers (Bitter Lake NWR, BLM Bottomless Lakes) already aware of occupied habitat
  - Given small range size, any adverse habitat modification would likely also constitute jeopardy to the species, making separate critical habitat "adverse modification" standard redundant with "jeopardy" standard

### Prohibited/Permitted Activities Post-Listing (Section 9 Analysis)
**Likely NOT a violation**:
- Livestock grazing not degrading occupied habitat
- Groundwater pumping unconnected to occupied riverine/sinkhole habitat
- Oil/gas exploration unconnected hydrologically to occupied habitat

**Likely a violation**:
- Habitat-degrading livestock grazing
- Stocking piscivorous fish or sheepshead minnows in/near occupied or connected habitat
- Groundwater pumping significantly reducing water quantity/quality in occupied areas
- Channelization or dewatering activities
- Activities causing significant water quality degradation

### Federal Nexus Activities Requiring Section 7 Consultation
- U.S. Bureau of Reclamation / Army Corps of Engineers dam operations (regulated under the [[Pecos River Compact]])
- EPA NPDES discharge permits and pesticide registration
- BLM grazing permits, oil/gas leases
- COE Clean Water Act §404 permitting (dredge/fill, pipeline construction)
- USDA APHIS programs (e.g., rangeland grasshopper control), NRCS and Farm Service Agency programs
- HUD Community Development Block Grant program
