# Arizona Region VII Fishery Investigations: Roosevelt and San Carlos Lakes, 1965

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This Arizona Game and Fish Department job completion report covers the Periodic Survey of Waters in Region VII under [[Federal Aid Project F-7-R-8]] for January 1 to December 31, 1965. Work focused on [[Roosevelt Lake]] and [[San Carlos Lake]], with cove sampling in June and July, electrofishing in December, and stream renovation of Grant and Ash Creeks. Largemouth bass reproduction was excellent on Roosevelt Lake but weaker on San Carlos Lake. Electrofishing was delayed on Roosevelt Lake by equipment failure and was hampered on San Carlos Lake by flood run-off and debris. The report recommends continued electrofishing and summer cove sampling, earlier electrofishing timing, new sampling methods for [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Black Crappie]], a commercial-use study of [[Threadfin Shad]] and [[Carp]], and periodic monitoring of the newly stocked [[Arizona Native Trout]].

## Background & Description

**Project and authorship.** The report is a Statewide Fisheries Investigations completion report from the Fish Management Division of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. It is a Federal Aid survey-and-investigations project. The project leader was James R. Bruce and the coordinator was Roger J. Gruenewald. Field work was prepared by Don Wingfield, Regional Supervisor, and approved by Pavia A. Roe, Jr., Federal Aid Coordinator. Several other staff are listed as contributors, including Alban R. Essbach, Robert H. White, Levi Packard, Wes Martin, Don Smith, T. O. Barnes, Ted Knipe and Louis H. Carufel.

**Objective.** The stated aim of the regional work is to determine current physical, chemical and biological conditions in the waters of the region, so that current management techniques can be evaluated.

**Structure.** The full report covers seven regions (Work Plans 1 to 7), plus watershed fishery surveys, redevelopment surveys and fish-stocking evaluations. This note covers only the Region VII material, which is the substance of the document. The table of contents also lists a Lake Mohave trout stocking evaluation under Region III, which is not included in the text provided.

**Methods.**
- **Cove sampling:** Shallow coves in representative areas of each lake were treated with synergized [[Rotenone]]. Treated areas averaged about 50 feet by 50 feet, at roughly two feet deep.
- **Electrofishing:** On Roosevelt Lake, a large shocker was used. On San Carlos Lake, only a small shocker was used. All work was done at night, when bass normally feed in the margins.
- **Stream renovation:** Grant and Ash Creeks were treated with rotenone from drip stations spaced through most of each stream. Inspections checked that a complete kill had been achieved.
- **Temperature monitoring:** Water temperatures were logged by recording thermometers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Roosevelt Lake.** Cove sampling on June 21, 1965 (Table I) recovered a mixed fish community. Young-of-year largemouth bass were the most numerous species in every cove sampled. Survival was attributed to flooded marginal brush and debris, which provided effective feeding and hiding cover for young bass. Conditions were also judged favorable for channel catfish and bluegill. Green sunfish were present at considerably higher densities than black crappie or bluegill. The report warns that green sunfish could become a serious problem if bass cannot control them.

**San Carlos Lake.** Cove sampling on July 1, 1965 found an even greater population of green sunfish than Roosevelt Lake. Survival of young-of-year bass was about one-tenth of the Roosevelt sample, although the San Carlos bass averaged about one inch larger. Lake levels were low, so few extensive shallow coves were available and the shoreline drop-off was steep. The report suggests this may have introduced sampling bias. Channel catfish did not appear in the samples as expected. The lack of shallow sampling water was again considered the likely cause.

**Hydrology.** Heavy run-off in late 2025 December increased water levels on San Carlos Lake. The reservoir's storage at the end of December 1965 was lower than the 39,787 acre-feet recorded at the end of December 1964. The 1965 figure is garbled in the scan and is not reported here.

**Grant and Ash Creeks.** Both streams were renovated with rotenone in June 1965. The aim was to establish a pure strain of [[Arizona Native Trout]] (cited as *S. gila*). An earlier attempt in Grant Creek in 1951 failed because the fish were hybrids with rainbow trout. Ash Creek had not been renovated before 1965. Grant Creek survey work was not done in 1965 because of the renovation effort.

**Eagle Creek.** Water temperatures were recorded daily for six months. Results are given in Table IV of Work Plan 8, Job R-5, which is outside the text provided.

## Key Findings

- **Roosevelt cove sampling (June 21, 1965):** Largemouth bass reproduction was described as excellent. Young-of-year bass were the most numerous species in all cove areas.
- **Roosevelt electrofishing (December 1965):** Work ran about six weeks behind schedule because of mechanical problems with the large shocker. The catch was 87 fewer largemouth bass than in 1964.
- **San Carlos cove sampling (July 1, 1965):** Young-of-year bass survival was about one-tenth of the Roosevelt sample. Green sunfish were more abundant than on Roosevelt. Channel catfish were absent from samples.
- **San Carlos electrofishing (late December 1965):** Only 204 largemouth bass were taken, a large drop from 1964. The 1964 figure is garbled in the scan. Muddy water, rapidly rising water levels and drift wood and debris hampered the crew.
- **Grant and Ash Creeks:** Renovated in June 1965, with Arizona native trout introduced into the streams in September 1965.
- **Recommendations:**
  1. Continue electrofishing on Roosevelt and San Carlos, starting about two months earlier if water levels and turbidity allow.
  2. Continue summer cove sampling on both lakes to monitor reproductive success.
  3. Investigate additional methods for monitoring channel catfish and black crappie populations on both lakes.
  4. Start a research program at San Carlos Lake to assess commercial uses of threadfin shad and carp.
  5. Sample Grant and Ash Creeks periodically to track population and growth of the recently introduced native trout.
