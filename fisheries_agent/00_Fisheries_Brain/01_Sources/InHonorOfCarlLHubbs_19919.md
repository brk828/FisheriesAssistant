# In Honor of Carl L. Hubbs

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1976 tribute, reprinted from the [[Bulletin of the Southern California Academy of Sciences]] (Vol. 75, No. 2, pp. 57-59), dedicates the issue to [[Carl L. Hubbs]], described as the foremost figure in North American ichthyology in the middle half of the twentieth century. The piece reviews his research range, professional service, honors, family, and mentorship of colleagues and students. It is a biographical tribute rather than a research study, so it reports no new field data, sample sizes, or species measurements.

## Background & Description

- **Scope of work:** Hubbs' bibliography includes about 700 titles, beginning with his first publication in 1915. His interests span taxonomy, distribution, ecology, variation, life history and evolution of fishes, fishery biology, marine mammals, paleohydrography, oceanography, human ecology, and paleoclimatology.
- **Mentorship:** He followed [[David Starr Jordan]], who dominated the field in the late 1800s and early 1900s.
- **Early life and education:** Born 18 October 1894 in Williams, Arizona. He received A.B. and A.M. degrees from Stanford University (1916, 1917) and a Ph.D. in zoology from the University of Michigan (1927).
- **Career positions:** Curatorial and professorial roles at the University of Michigan (1920-1944), then at the [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]] (1944-1969), followed by emeritus status from 1969.
- **Professional service:** Active in the [[American Society of Ichthyologists and Herpetologists]] (ASIH), which he helped shape from near its founding in 1913. He served as ASIH secretary (1928-31) and president (1934-35, 1946-48), and as editor of [[Copeia]] (1930-1937). He was also review editor of the American Naturalist (1941-1947), vice-president of the American Society of Naturalists (1964), and president of the Society of Systematic Zoology (1951).
- **Conservation:** Involved with the Nature Conservancy, the Torrey Pines Association, and the Desert Fishes Council.
- **Honors:** Included election to the National Academy of Sciences (1952), a Guggenheim fellowship (1952-53), the Joseph Leidy Award and Medal (1964), membership in the Linnean Society of London (1965), the San Diego Zoological Society Gold Medal (1970), the Shinkishi Hatai medal of Japan (1971), and the American Fisheries Society Award of Excellence (1973). He received the 1975 Headliner Award from the San Diego Press Club.
- **Eponyms:** According to Kenneth Norris, five fish genera, 22 fish species, and numerous other organisms and a Nevada lake have been named in his honor.
- **Family:** He is married to Laura, who recorded field notes and joined research projects. Their children include Frances, Clark, and Earl. Clark Hubbs and Robert Rush Miller (Frances' husband) are both ichthyologists, and Miller collaborated with him on freshwater fish studies.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The tribute highlights several research areas that frame Hubbs' ecological and biogeographic work:

- **Zoogeography:** Studies of the Eastern Pacific, including the relationship of temperature to coastal fish distributions, and an edited volume on zoogeography (1958).
- **Hydrographic history:** Fish distributions and drainage history of the [[Great Basin]].
- **Marine mammal populations:** Status of the [[Northern Elephant Seal]], [[Guadalupe Fur Seal]], and [[Pacific Gray Whale]].
- **Kelp ecosystems:** Co-editing (1968, with Wheeler J. North) a bulletin on utilization of [[Kelp Bed Resources]] in southern California.
- **Desert fishes:** Long-term efforts to protect the desert pupfishes, the genus-level group within [[Cyprinodontidae]].

## Key Findings

- **Speciation and variation:** Hubbs contributed extensively to work on speciation, hybridization, and geographic variation in fishes.
- **Cyprinodontid research:** His work on taxonomy, distribution, and reproduction of cyprinodontids included discovery of the [[Formosa Molly]] (*Poecilia formosa*), an all-female species maintained by males of another species.
- **Standard references:** The treatise on fishes of the Great Lakes region, co-authored with [[Karl F. Lagler]], has been an important synthesis since its first edition in 1941 and a standard reference for counts and measurements of fish specimens.
- **Reviews:** He wrote more than 140 book reviews, noted for critical analysis and original concepts.
- **Mentorship:** His encouragement of colleagues and students, and his attention to current literature, are described as central to many careers in the field.
- **Issue contents:** The issue contains 18 papers spanning systematic, ecological, behavioral, and physiological studies of marine and freshwater fishes, written by authors from graduate students to senior scientists.
- **Outlook:** At the time of writing, Hubbs was preparing for a family trip to Alaska and other parts of the United States, with planned new species descriptions and an exhaustive checklist of California fishes still in progress.
