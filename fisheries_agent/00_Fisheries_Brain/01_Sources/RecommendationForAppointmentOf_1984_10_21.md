## Executive Summary
This document is a 1984 letter from W. L. Minckley, Professor of Zoology and Leader of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] (DFRT), to Michael Spear, Regional Director of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service. The letter formally recommends the appointment of Ms. Lourdes Juarez Romero, a biologist affiliated with the Instituto de Ecologia, Gobierno del Estado de Sonora, to the DFRT. The correspondence underscores the binational conservation challenges facing endangered desert fish species along the U.S.-Mexico border, particularly those inhabiting the [[Rio Yaqui]] basin, and cites precedent for Mexican National representation on cross-border recovery teams.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: The fishes of concern inhabit the [[Rio Yaqui]] drainage, a desert riverine ecosystem spanning the U.S.-Mexico borderlands in Sonora, Mexico, and the American Southwest.
- **Conservation status**: Referred to as "listed fishes," indicating formal endangered or threatened status under U.S. Endangered Species Act frameworks.
- **Transboundary ecology**: The letter emphasizes that the region's aquatic ecosystems and associated biological problems (e.g., population status, habitat degradation, species conservation vs. development pressures) cross the International Boundary, necessitating coordinated binational management.
- **Brood stock initiative**: Efforts were underway to obtain brood stocks of Rio Yaqui fishes for ex-situ conservation at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], suggesting active captive propagation programs for endangered desert fish populations.
- **Comparable system**: The [[Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team]] is cited as a parallel binational recovery effort, indicating similar ecological and conservation dynamics along the Rio Grande drainage.

## Key Findings
- **Personnel recommendation**: Ms. Lourdes Juarez Romero of the Instituto de Ecologia (Sonora, Mexico) is recommended for appointment to the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] based on her expertise in species conservation and prior substantive contributions as a guest at a DFRT meeting.
- **Precedent for Mexican representation**: Dr. Salvador Contreras Balderas of the Universidad Autonoma de Nuevo Leon already serves on the [[Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team]], establishing precedent for Mexican National involvement in U.S. endangered species recovery efforts.
- **Active conservation involvement**: Juarez Romero was already engaged in assessing the status of Mexican populations of listed fish species and securing brood stock for hatchery propagation, directly supporting recovery objectives for [[Rio Yaqui Fishes]].
- **Binational conservation imperative**: The letter explicitly recognizes that endangered species and habitat issues in the U.S.-Mexico borderlands are inherently transboundary, warranting formal international collaboration on recovery teams.
- **Institutional endorsement**: The appointment proposal had already been discussed and concurred upon by U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service staff (Jim Johnson and Jerry Burton) and DFRT members prior to formal written recommendation.
