# Fish and Fish Egg Distribution Report of the National Fish Hatchery System - Fiscal Year 1992

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management #hatchery-operations #conservation

## Executive Summary

This report documents the distribution of fish and fish eggs by the [[National Fish Hatchery System]] during Fiscal Year 1992 (October 1, 1991 through September 30, 1992). The system distributed 188,769,500 fish weighing 5,983,740 pounds and produced 132,329,133 eggs across coldwater, coolwater, and warmwater species categories. The report represents the 27th annual distribution report and reflects the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]'s century-long commitment to artificial propagation as a tool for restoring depleted fish stocks. Major programs included [[Striped Bass]] restoration, [[Atlantic Salmon]] restoration, [[Pacific Salmon]] and [[Steelhead Trout]] restoration, and [[Great Lakes]] [[Lake Trout]] restoration. The system operates facilities dating back over 100 years, including [[Craig Brook NFH]] in Maine and [[Leadville NFH]] in Colorado.

## Background & Description

The federal responsibility for fishery stewardship dates to 1871 when Congress established the position of U.S. Commissioner of Fish and Fisheries in response to declining domestic food fish supplies. Declines in fish populations over two centuries resulted from overfishing, degraded habitat, water pollution, dam construction, and other natural and human-caused phenomena. Artificial propagation was recognized early as essential for restoring depleted stocks.

The report documents that over 34.8 million U.S. anglers 16 years and older fish an average of 14.5 days annually, contributing $25 billion to the economy and supporting over 700,000 full-time jobs. Experts predicted a 40% increase in recreational fishing by 2030. The [[National Recreational Fisheries Policy]] and the Service's [[Fisheries - USA]] policy, implemented through the "Action for Angling" plan signed November 25, 1991, provided direction for field programs. This aligned with the Department of Interior's "Enjoy Outdoors America" initiative.

Species categories included:
- **Coldwater species**: Fish spawning below 55°F, mainly trout and salmon
- **Coolwater species**: Fish spawning between 40-60°F, including [[Northern Pike]], [[Muskellunge]], [[Walleye]], [[Sauger]], and [[Yellow Perch]]
- **Warmwater species**: Fish spawning above 60°F, including bass, sturgeon, sunfish, and catfish

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report emphasizes that hatchery production alone cannot sustain healthy fish populations. Success requires integration with harvest restrictions, pollution reduction in breeding grounds, and expanded biological knowledge. The system operated across seven regions covering all U.S. states, with facilities ranging from National Fish Hatcheries to Fish Technology Centers, Fish Health Centers, and fishway facilities.

Regional distribution patterns reflected habitat and species requirements:
- **Region 1** (Pacific Northwest): Dominated by [[Pacific Salmon]] and [[Steelhead]] production (68.6 million fish distributed)
- **Region 4** (Southeast): Focus on warmwater species and [[Striped Bass]] restoration (25.1 million fish)
- **Region 6** (Mountain West): Emphasis on coldwater trout species and [[Lake Trout]] (53.3 million fish)

Environmental challenges included:
- [[Sea Lamprey]] predation in the [[Great Lakes]] (controlled to 10% of pretreatment levels)
- Hydroelectric dam impacts on anadromous fish migration
- Water pollution in breeding grounds
- Habitat degradation from changing land use practices

## Key Findings

**Overall Distribution Statistics:**
- Total fish distributed: 188,769,500 individuals weighing 5,983,740 pounds
- Total eggs produced: 132,329,133
- Coldwater species: 98,156,088 fish (5,701,651 lbs), 77,955,125 eggs
- Coolwater species: 62,787,289 fish (15,224 lbs), 48,352,508 eggs
- Warmwater species: 27,826,123 fish (266,865 lbs), 6,021,500 eggs

**Striped Bass Restoration (10-year summary):**
- Over 51 million [[Striped Bass]] distributed during past decade along Atlantic and Gulf Coasts
- 1989 Maryland young-of-the-year index showed significant population increase in [[Chesapeake Bay]]
- Some states reopened limited harvest fisheries since 1990
- Historical context: Mid-1970s decline cost Atlantic Coast 7,000 jobs and $200 million (1974-1980)
- [[Chesapeake Bay]] historically produced 90% of Atlantic Ocean striped bass

**Atlantic Salmon Restoration (10-year summary):**
- Over 32 million [[Atlantic Salmon]] distributed (nearly 2 million pounds) in past decade
- Majority stocked in [[Penobscot River]], [[Connecticut River]], [[Merrimack River]], [[Union River]], and [[St. Croix River]]
- Historical population: 28 major New England rivers with 300,000 adult returns annually
- Current returns: 3,000-7,000 adults annually, with 1,500-2,500 from natural reproduction
- Improved cultural techniques reduced smolt development time from 2+ years to 1 year

**Pacific Salmon and Steelhead Restoration (10-year summary):**
- Over 729 million [[Pacific Salmon]] and [[Steelhead]] released (23 million pounds) in past decade
- Network of 17 National Fish Hatcheries in Northwest
- 1986 commercial harvest: 7.1 million fish valued at $230 million (Washington, Oregon, California)
- 1986 sport fishery: 940,000 salmon worth $107.5 million
- [[American Fisheries Society]] endangered species committee identified 159 stocks at high/moderate extinction risk, with 54 additional stocks at high potential for near-future extinction

**Great Lakes Lake Trout Restoration (10-year summary):**
- Nearly 65 million [[Lake Trout]] distributed (over 3 million pounds) in past decade
- Stocking initiated by Service in 1955
- [[Sea Lamprey]] populations held to 10% of pretreatment levels
- Lake-specific progress varied: [[Lake Ontario]] producing eggs/fry but no adults; [[Lake Superior]] with healthy adult population; [[Lake Huron]] showing recovery signs in 1991; [[Lake Erie]] with large 6-year-old adults raising natural reproduction hopes; [[Lake Michigan]] producing fry but no unmarked fingerlings found

**Top Species by Distribution (FY 1992):**
- [[Fall Chinook Salmon]]: 40,266,564 fish (411,206 lbs), 10,256,221 eggs
- [[Walleye]]: 34,135,466 fish (7,337 lbs), 40,533,226 eggs
- [[Northern Pike]]: 19,385,393 fish (5,630 lbs), 1,119,600 eggs
- [[Spring Chinook Salmon]]: 12,291,226 fish (593,673 lbs), 4,428,511 eggs
- [[Rainbow Trout]]: 13,621,957 fish (2,474,885 lbs), 32,551,635 eggs

**Top Producing Facilities (FY 1992):**
- [[Coleman NFH]] (California): 18,344,774 fish
- [[Spring Creek NFH]] (Washington): 15,140,710 fish, 8,796,637 eggs
- [[Garrison Dam NFH]] (North Dakota): 21,928,348 fish, 654,280 eggs
- [[Ennis NFH]] (Montana): 63,937 fish, 27,544,705 eggs (highest egg production)

**Program Distribution:**
- Inland Restoration/Mitigation: 86,196,816 fish (2,530,663 lbs), 60,962,996 eggs
- Pacific Salmon and Steelhead Program: 67,619,098 fish (2,115,813 lbs), 14,868,652 eggs
- Indian Tribal Program: 9,331,453 fish (433,914 lbs), 1,913,113 eggs
- Striped Bass Restoration Program: 8,019,694 fish (50,186 lbs)
- Atlantic Salmon Restoration Program: 4,512,738 fish (274,270 lbs), 7,242,959 eggs
- Great Lakes Lake Trout Program: 6,343,456 fish (352,516 lbs)
