# Fourth Endangered Fish Uses Gunnison River "Ladder"

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This news release from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (July 23, 1997) reports the fourth successful passage of an endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] through the [[Redlands Fish Ladder]] on the Gunnison River near Grand Junction, Colorado. The event marks the third squawfish to use the 336-foot-long fish passage structure in a two-week span, suggesting a possible correlation with spawning migration behavior. The report also highlights broader ecological restoration outcomes, noting nearly 15,000 other native fish have used the ladder since its completion, effectively reconnecting fragmented habitat in the upper [[Colorado River Basin]] blocked by the [[Redlands Diversion Dam]].

## Background & Description
- **Species observed (July 23, 1997 individual):** Colorado squawfish
  - **Length:** 23 inches
  - **Weight:** 3 pounds
  - **Identification method:** Electronic PIT (passive integrated transponder) tag, read via barcode-like scanner passed under the fish's belly
  - **Tag ID:** No. 1741386206
- **Other native species passing through ladder (non-morphological detail given):**
  - [[Flannelmouth Sucker]]
  - [[Bluehead Sucker]]
  - [[Roundtail Chub]]

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context:** Gunnison River, a tributary within the upper [[Colorado River Basin]], historically fragmented by the 12-foot-high concrete [[Redlands Diversion Dam]], which blocked fish migration for nearly a century.
- **Fish Ladder structure:**
  - Length: 330 feet (also referenced as 336 feet)
  - Diverted flow through ladder: 25 cubic feet per second (cfs)
  - Design: Series of ascending steps from bottom entry to a "holding area" at top, where biologists sort and count fish before release upstream.
  - Completed: 1996 (per "completed last year" relative to July 1997 report)
- **Migration behavior:** The tagged squawfish had migrated over 100 miles upstream from a location near Moab, Utah (originally tagged/captured in 1995 by Utah fishery biologists in the [[Colorado River]] near Moab).
- **Environmental trigger:** Biologists attribute the recent increase in squawfish passage to receding river flows and rising water temperatures, potentially linked to spawning migration timing.
- **Broader basin context:** The lower [[Colorado River Basin]] (downstream of Lake Powell) has experienced significant native fish decline due to damming and diversion-caused habitat fragmentation. The Redlands Fish Ladder is presented as a proactive measure to prevent similar decline in the upper basin.

## Key Findings
- **Fourth endangered fish** (specifically Colorado squawfish) to successfully use the Redlands Fish Ladder since its completion in 1996.
- **Three squawfish** passed through the ladder within a 14-day period, an unusually high frequency interpreted as a potentially encouraging sign of ladder efficacy and possibly linked to spawning activity.
- **Electronic tagging infrastructure** (PIT tags) enabled researchers to trace individual fish movement history, confirming a migration of 100+ miles from the Moab, Utah area to the Gunnison River.
- **Nearly 15,000 other native fish** (flannelmouth sucker, bluehead sucker, roundtail chub) have used the ladder, indicating substantial restoration of connectivity for non-endangered native species as well.
- **Structural significance:** The fish ladder allows a managed diversion (25 cfs) around the Redlands Diversion Dam, re-establishing a more naturally functioning river corridor despite the continued presence of the dam.
- **Conservation implication:** FWS biologist Bob Burdick frames the project as a preventive strategy to protect upper basin native fish populations from the kind of severe declines observed in the dam-fragmented lower Colorado River Basin.
