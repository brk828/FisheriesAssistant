# El Coronado Ranch Habitat Conservation Plan: Yaqui Chub and Yaqui Longfin Dace Conservation

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #cyprinidae #conservation-biology #habitat-conservation-plan

## Executive Summary

This document is a draft 1995 Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) and Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP) between the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, U.S. Forest Service, Arizona Game and Fish Commission, and the private landowners of the [[El Coronado Ranch]] in Cochise County, Arizona. The primary conservation targets are the [[Yaqui Chub]] (*Gila purpurea*), a federally endangered freshwater cyprinid, and the [[Yaqui Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), a USFWS "Candidate 2" species. The plan is notable as a rare example of a private landowner-initiated HCP intended to provide refugia habitat for endangered fish under Section 10 of the [[Endangered Species Act]], while allowing continued ranching operations. Key threats identified include introduction of non-indigenous predatory fish, the catastrophic 1994 [[Rattlesnake Fire]] and resulting ash/sediment runoff, water system maintenance activities, and overgrazing-driven habitat degradation. The plan outlines mitigation measures, quantitative take estimates, and funding/monitoring commitments aimed at recovery consistent with the USFWS "Recovery Plan for the Fishes of the Rio Yaqui" (approved March 3, 1995).

## Background & Description

The source document is a legal/regulatory conservation plan and does **not** provide detailed morphological descriptions (e.g., meristics, coloration, body plan) for either species. Data extracted from the text is limited to taxonomic and distributional identifiers:

- **[[Yaqui Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*, "Yaqui form"): A distinct form of longfin dace; designated Candidate 2 status by USFWS in 1994.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historic/Current Range**: The [[Yaqui Chub]] is restricted to the upper Rio Yaqui basin in southern Arizona and northern Sonora, Mexico. It formerly occupied the endorheic [[Sulphur Springs Valley]] of Arizona.
- **Reintroduction History**: In 1986, USFWS restocked the chub into at least one pond on the El Coronado Ranch (then state-owned land) in [[West Turkey Creek]]. The population subsequently expanded upstream and downstream via interconnected ponds and creek habitat.
- **Yaqui Longfin Dace Distribution**: West Turkey Creek is believed to harbor the last known population of this form in the Sulphur Springs Valley (W.L. Minckley, pers. comm.).
- **Habitat Type**: Habitat consists of a mix of natural stream reaches (West Turkey Creek) and artificial impoundments/ponds connected via a ranch water delivery system. Watersheds are described as "tight," with bedrock near the surface producing rapid runoff and minimal groundwater storage — a hydrologically fragile system.
- **Surrounding Terrestrial Communities**: Desert grassland and desertscrub at lower elevations, transitioning to Madrean evergreen woodland/chaparral (oak-dominated) and upper-elevation pine-fir forest — a classic "sky island" gradient in the [[Chiricahua Mountains]].
- **Co-occurring Threats (Non-native Species)**: Green sunfish, bass, bluegill, trout, and catfish, plus bullfrogs, were documented increasing in the system and negatively impacting native chub populations prior to the 1994 fire.
- **Disturbance Event**: The 1994 [[Rattlesnake Fire]] caused massive ash runoff into the lower watershed, eliminating fish and most aquatic organisms from the stream and most ponds; a subsequent survey found chub and dace survived in only a few refuge ponds.
- **Species of Secondary Concern**: Other sensitive plant and animal species occurring in Cochise County (listed in an unincluded appendix) may be indirectly affected by management actions targeting the two focal fish species.

## Key Findings

- **Resilience of Yaqui Chub**: Cited as remarkably resilient (DeMarais & Minckley 1991, 1993) — populations can grow from a few tens of individuals to thousands within months, and the species can persist through severe conditions and rebound when conditions improve.
- **Resilience of Longfin Dace**: Also resilient post-catastrophe; reproduces essentially year-round. Post-fire observations confirmed spawning activity in a Turkey Creek watershed pond (Minckley, original data).
- **Quantitative Take Estimates**: The plan anticipates:
  - Tens of individuals lost to physical injury, canal diversions, and habitat loss during routine water system manipulation.
  - Hundreds lost when canals/ponds are dried for maintenance or repair.
  - Potentially thousands lost during sustained drought or from predation/competition by non-indigenous species.
- **Primary Incidental Take Mechanisms**:
  1. Water system maintenance/repair (canals, ponds, tanks) altering water quantity/quality, nutrients, or oxygen.
  2. Stranding from water shutoffs or diversions.
  3. Introduction (accidental or purposeful) of exotic fish species.
  4. Indirect impacts: erosion/siltation from overgrazing or poor forest management, improperly managed burns, upstream water development, and recreational use impacts on water quality.
- **Alternatives Analysis**: Four alternatives evaluated — (1) No action (rejected: legal liability risk, restricts land use, jeopardizes species); (2) Removal of species from private land (rejected: conflicts with Recovery Plan and AGFD goals); (3) Petition for delisting/downlisting (rejected: premature given restricted distribution); (4) **Incidental Take Permit via approved HCP (Section 10 ESA)** — selected as preferred alternative, offering maximum management flexibility.
- **Mitigation Commitments** by the Applicant (El Coronado Ranch):
  1. Prohibit/prevent introduction of non-compatible non-native fish.
  2. Routine maintenance of water delivery infrastructure (tanks, ponds) to prevent excessive leakage/habitat loss.
  3. Grant agency access for monitoring, sampling, and fish relocation as needed.
  4. Maintain adequate water levels/oxygen in impoundments (barring drought or uncontrollable events).
  5. Develop riparian grazing controls in consultation with USFWS, AGFD, and USFS.
- **Regulatory Framework**: Plan designed to be consistent with the USFWS "[[Recovery Plan for the Fishes of the Rio Yaqui]]" (approved March 3, 1995) and characterized as a likely "low-effect" HCP per USFWS Draft Habitat Conservation Planning Handbook (Sept. 15, 1995).
- **Funding Structure**: Combination of in-kind landowner contributions (infrastructure maintenance), landowner cash contributions to monitoring/research, and USFWS funding aligned with Recovery Plan targets; USFS funding role left unspecified in the draft.
- **Unforeseen Circumstances Clause**: Applicant commits to annual review meetings with FWS; explicitly, "unforeseen circumstances" will not obligate the Applicant to provide habitat or compensation beyond what is specified in the agreement.
