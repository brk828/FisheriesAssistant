# Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin: FY-1989 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) annual report, published March 1990 by Region 6 (Denver), summarizes status and accounting for the FY-1989 projects of the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for endangered fishes in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. It covers 39 Work Plan projects plus two projects begun in FY-1989 outside the Work Plan. The program targets four endangered species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]] and [[Humpback Chub]]. Project work ranged from flow recommendations and hydrology modeling to hatchery propagation, stocking, public outreach and database management. The total FY-1989 budget was $2,326.5 thousand allocated and $2,288.6 thousand expended, according to the table total.

## Background & Description

The report follows a directive from the [[Colorado River Recovery Implementation Committee]]. Starting in FY-1989, every funded project must submit an annual report that covers accomplishments against objectives, a preliminary data analysis and next-year recommendations. The [[Technical Group]] reviews these reports and passes recommendations to the Implementation Committee. The Committee's August 30, 1989 meeting added an accounting of funds spent.

The report is organized into four sections:
- 1.0 Introduction
- 2.0 Summary Status Reports
- 3.0 Accounting Summary
- 4.0 Appendix: Individual Project Reports (available on request)

Project contacts are drawn from the [[Utah Division of Wildlife Resources]], the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]], the [[Wyoming Attorney General]] and FWS offices in Denver, Grand Junction, Vernal and Salt Lake City.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area is the upper basin's regulated river system. Several findings depend on flow and reservoir operations:

- **Flaming Gorge Dam** releases affect channel form, backwater nursery habitat and water temperature. Work on these effects supports the [[Flaming Gorge Biological Opinion]].
- **15-Mile Reach** flows are the target of a supplemental water supply. The 40-year lease with [[Ruedi Reservoir]] provides 10,000 acre-feet per year to the reach. It was finalized in February 1990.
- **Habitat modeling** with [[PHABSIM]] proved problematic on large, turbid rivers. The report says it will need "many years of field validation" before it can guide Green River flow recommendations.
- **Predation and competition** from nonnative fishes are studied through stomach sampling and radio telemetry. The main predators named are northern pike, channel catfish and smallmouth bass.
- **Hydrology and temperature** monitoring used the [[HYDROSS Model]] and a Stream Network Temperature Model.

## Key Findings

**Status of projects (Section 2.0)**
- Most projects were on track or complete. Several were delayed by staffing, review backlogs or low flows.
- The Yampa and Colorado flow recommendations were completed. Interim Yampa flow recommendations were sent in November 1989. Colorado River recommendations for July–September in the 15-Mile Reach are described as "biologically defensible."
- Working hypotheses from the Colorado River work: (1) adult humpback chub are limited by availability of their preferred habitat; (2) squawfish reproductive success relates to peak discharge; (3) age-0 squawfish habitat use resembles that of several nonnative fishes but differs from some native fishes.
- Razorback sucker propagation involved capture of wild fish, artificial spawning and larval rearing. The draft Razorback Sucker Management Plan was completed; finalization is set for October 1990.

**Stocking and monitoring**
- About 32,000 Colorado squawfish were stocked in [[Kenney Reservoir]] in late April 1989. Of these, 246 were recovered by seining, gillnetting and electrofishing. Two hundred thirteen were collected up to 28.5 river miles below Taylor Draw Dam, and none within 10 miles of the White River upstream.
- Colorado Division of Wildlife recommended stocking at a minimum of 4 inches in late May 1990 and implanting ultrasonic transmitters in six adults.
- The Bonytail Introduction failed. Few stocked fish survived, and handling and treatment techniques did not improve survival. The report says adult hatchery bonytails are not feasible for habitat studies and recommends further work on introduction protocols.
- The Bonytail Retrieval Program was cancelled because of procedural concerns and cost overruns.
- A PIT tag study with [[Colorado Squawfish]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] could not evaluate effects on growth and survival because of limited target fish, unsuitable holding ponds and low survival in test ponds.
- Overwinter laboratory work on hatchery-reared age-0 squawfish related survival to length, condition factor and lipid content. Results were used to build length-survival curves for wild populations.
- The standardized monitoring program for adult and young-of-year squawfish was completed. Its data analysis was not finished.

**Predation studies (Project 31)**
- No Colorado squawfish were found in stomach samples from northern pike, channel catfish and smallmouth bass in the Yampa River. Identification of all ingested fish was incomplete.
- Drift-net sampling in Yampa Canyon collected no drifting larvae of any species, so the predation study yielded nothing.

**Data management (Project 33)**
- The database held 158 data files from four organizations at the time of reporting. Many researchers could not meet the March 1 submission deadline.

**Public outreach (Projects 24–27)**
- The "Swimming Upstream" brochure was completed and the first Recovery Program Newsletter was published.
- Of 14 planned permanent signs, five were produced and installed.
- The creel survey was implemented in Utah only. No incidental take was observed. Most anglers were local, and they were already aware of the program.

**Recommendations for FY-1990 (examples)**
- Finish the Compendium of Existing Information by April 30, 1990.
- Complete a more thorough dietary overlap analysis for Flaming Gorge trophic dynamics, if feasible.
- Install river gauges in the 15-Mile Reach to measure inflow and downstream gain, and measure discharge at the Orchard Mesa Pumping/Powerplant radial gates.
- Establish recovery goals, deferred to FY-1990 by reprogramming.
- Finalize the San Juan River Management Plan, with a draft fishery plan requested by July 1, 1990.

**Accounting (Section 3.0)**
- Projects more than 10% over or under allocation were explained. Overruns were often covered by cooperating agencies at no cost to the program. Examples include the [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]] ($84,743 spent on Game/Endangered Fish Interactions against $75,000 allocated) and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (an extra $30,000 for the Larval Fish Lab).
- The Gila taxonomy study cost $62,000 against $53,000 allocated, because the Smithsonian underestimated overhead by $9,000.
- Utah's Creel Survey spent $20,000 of $25,000 allocated. The Federal share for Colorado's creel project was reverted when that state funded a Little Snake River survey instead.
- The Razorback Sucker Management Plan had no allocation but $10,000 was spent. The Bureau covered the cost.
- Table 1 totals: $2,326.5 thousand allocated and $2,288.6 thousand expended. The text gives the expended total as $2,288.4 thousand, a discrepancy with the table. The text also lists the San Juan allocation as $53,000, while the table shows $59.0 thousand.
