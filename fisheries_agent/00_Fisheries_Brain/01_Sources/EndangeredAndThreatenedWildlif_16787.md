## Executive Summary
This document is a **U.S. Federal Register proposed rule** (dated June 18, 1985) issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) to list the [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) as an endangered/threatened species under the [[Endangered Species Act]] and to designate its **critical habitat**. The rule specifies protective regulations restricting take, possession, sale, and transport of the species, alongside detailed geographic descriptions of proposed critical habitat segments across the [[Gila River Basin]] in Arizona and New Mexico. The document is primarily regulatory/legal in nature but contains embedded ecological data regarding the species' required habitat conditions.

## Key Findings
- The [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*) was proposed for federal protection under the [[Endangered Species Act]] due to habitat loss and degradation within its native range in the [[Gila River]] drainage of Arizona and New Mexico.
- A special regulatory rule (§17.44) permitted limited take only for **scientific, educational, propagation, or conservation purposes**, or incidental catch during state-permitted recreational fishing (with mandatory immediate release).
- Any violation of applicable **state** fish and wildlife laws regarding this species would simultaneously constitute a federal **Endangered Species Act violation**, establishing a dual-enforcement mechanism.
- **Critical habitat** was defined not merely by geographic boundaries but by explicit **constituent elements**: permanent flow, water quality, riffle turbulence, minimum depth over gravel/cobble substrate, filamentous algae presence, and periodic flooding disturbance regimes.
- The designation reflects the species' status as an **indicator of aquatic ecosystem health** in southwestern desert riverine systems, where flow permanence and habitat connectivity are increasingly threatened by water diversion, impoundment, and land use change.
- This rule represents an early instance of the FWS pairing species listing with **detailed physical habitat criteria**, a precursor to modern critical habitat designations that specify not just location but functional ecological requirements — relevant to later conservation efforts for co-occurring species like [[Spikedace]] and other Gila River Basin endemics.
