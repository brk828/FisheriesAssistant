# Invertebrate Recolonization of Defaunated Hyporheic Sediments in a Sonoran Desert Stream

#macroinvertebrates #meiofauna #hyporheic-ecology

## Executive Summary

This experimental study, published in *Freshwater Biology* (1991), tests how invertebrates recolonize small patches of defaunated [[Hyporheic Zone]] sediment in [[Sycamore Creek]], a [[Sonoran Desert]] stream. Fine-mesh traps filled with sediment that had been cleared of fauna by [[Elutriation]] were buried 30 cm deep and recovered after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 27 days. Traps were open to upstream, downstream or below-ground colonization, and fully closed traps served as controls. Upstream-facing traps collected the most organic matter, taxa and individuals, indicating that recolonization was largely passive and driven by downstream transport of [[Detritus Transport|fine particulate detritus]]. Two predictions were not supported: taxa with planktonic surface relatives did not favour upstream entry, and blind phreatic forms did not favour entry from below. The authors also argue that pooling closely related taxa can mask species-specific recolonization behaviour.

## Background & Description

**Study system.** The hyporheos (interstitial fauna beneath and alongside streambeds, following [[Williams and Hynes]] 1974) has been studied mostly descriptively in North America. The authors compare this with [[Marine Meiofauna]] research, where experimental recolonization studies are well established. Their design follows [[Williams and Hynes]] (1976), who used traps open to upstream migration, downstream drift, aerial oviposition, vertical migration and all four sources.

**Hypotheses.** The authors tested two predictions:
1. Blind, unpigmented [[Phreatic Fauna]] would colonize mainly from below.
2. Hyporheic microcrustaceans with epigean planktonic relatives (e.g., [[Cyclopoid Copepods]]) would enter mainly from upstream.

**Study site.** The experiment was conducted in a gravel run (phi mode = −1) about 100 m long and 2–6 m wide. The stream is spatially intermittent and drains a 505 km² mountainous catchment with altitudes from 425 to over 2000 m. During July–August 1990:
- Mean surface current velocity was 7 cm s⁻¹ and mean depth 16 cm.
- Summer surface temperatures average 20–30 °C; winter 10–20 °C.
- At 30 cm depth, water temperature was 23–26 °C and dissolved oxygen 0.21–5.34 g m⁻³.
- Modal particle size was 1–4.75 mm.
- Riparian shrubs ([[Seepwillow]] and [[Burro Bush]]) are too short to shade the channel, so benthic algae stand crops are high.

**Experimental design.**
- *Traps:* Fine-mesh (63 µm) bags with 5-mm entrance rings of 150 mm PVC; mean volume 4560 cm³ (SE = 227 cm³).
- *Treatments:* Upstream, downstream, below-facing and closed controls (the closed controls estimate minimum colonization and test elutriation efficiency).
- *Replication:* Four replicates per treatment at each of five recovery dates (1, 3, 7, 14, 27 days). The run was divided into four blocks to account for longitudinal gradients, and n = 80 traps in total.
- *Spacing:* Traps were buried more than 1 m apart. A test using PVC wells confirmed that trap placement and recovery did not alter nearby hyporheos.
- *Defaunation:* Sediment (0.5–4.75 mm) was elutriated 8–12 times. Preliminary trials showed 93% of species and 96% of individuals were removed after four elutriations.
- *Statistics:* Randomized complete-block ANOVAs, Kruskal–Wallis tests, Scheffé post hoc tests, Mann–Whitney paired tests, Pearson correlations, TWINSPAN classification and hybrid multidimensional scaling (HMDS).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Physical setting.** Abiotic variables (depth to bedrock, vertical hydraulic gradient, grain size) differed mainly between Block 1 (furthest upstream) and the other blocks. Depth to bedrock increased downstream, the vertical gradient shifted from upwelling to downwelling, and finer particles became more abundant downstream. Subsurface interstitial velocities at similar sites were 0.035–1.25 mm s⁻¹ (Valett, Fisher & Stanley 1990).

**Disturbance.** The authors note that [[Flash Floods]] rework desert-stream sediments and reduce the pool of potential recolonists. Drying is also a major disturbance: within two days of rehydration, copepods, ceratopogonid larvae, nematodes, water mites and oligochaetes appeared in previously dry wells. This suggests that recolonization of large areas can be very rapid, although it is unclear whether taxa are passively carried by the advancing saturated front or move in later.

**Comparison with marine meiofauna.** Marine meiofauna mainly recruit through the water column, but the stream hyporheos appears to differ. The surface and hyporheic faunas overlapped very little, which suggests that exchange through the water column may be minimal except during high discharge.

**Organic matter.** Organic matter, measured as [[Ash-Free Dry Mass]] (AFDM), was strongly linked to invertebrate abundance. The authors suggest that accumulation of detritus, rather than physical habitat, is the main driver of hyporheic density in these traps.

## Key Findings

**1. Detritus accumulates in upstream-facing traps.**
- Closed traps held less than 30 mg AFDM per 5000 cm³ throughout the study.
- Upstream-facing traps reached over 270 mg AFDM per 5000 cm³ by day 27, with accumulation rates declining over time.
- Downstream- and below-facing traps gained detritus more gradually, always less than upstream traps but more than closed traps.
- The authors interpret this as evidence of downstream hyporheic transport of fine particulate detritus, although flocculation and hydrological artefacts from the trap itself cannot be excluded.

**2. Upstream traps capture the most taxa and individuals.**
- Closed traps always had the fewest taxa and individuals, mostly oligochaetes, nematodes and ceratopogonid larvae.
- A few individuals of most taxa colonized from all three directions within one day.
- Total individual numbers rose over about two weeks before levelling off. By day 27, the three open treatments had similar totals.
- Live nematodes and ceratopogonid larvae could pass through the 63 µm mesh, so the closed control was an imperfect test for some taxa.

**3. Seventeen taxa were collected from the 80 traps.**
- Ceratopogonid larvae ([[Probezzia Sp.]] and [[Bezzia Sp.]]), nematodes and ostracods dominated.
- The most frequent taxa were *Probezzia* sp. (77.50% occurrence), small nematodes (75.00%), large nematodes (72.50%), Ostracoda (67.50%) and Oligochaeta (67.50%).
- Obligate hyporheic peracarids (amphipods, isopods), bathynellaceans and Protziid water mites were rare, possibly because traps were buried only 30 cm deep.
- Ten taxa were common (more than 10% occurrence) and analysed individually.

**4. Taxon-specific patterns.**
- Upstream traps held significantly more *Probezzia* sp., large nematodes, microturbellarians, non-Protziid water mites and harpacticoids.
- Densities of *Bezzia* sp. did not differ among treatments.
- Cyclopoid copepods did not favour upstream traps, which contradicted the hyporheic-drift prediction.
- Blind, unpigmented taxa (microturbellarians, harpacticoids, isopods, amphipods, bathynellaceans) did not preferentially colonize from below, although many were too rare for firm conclusions.

**5. Closely related taxa behave differently.**
- *Bezzia* sp. and *Probezzia* sp. (Ceratopogonidae) showed major differences in recolonization direction and rate.
- The authors argue that pooling taxa can hide species-specific migration behaviour. They pooled several morphologically similar groups because of limited taxonomic expertise and to gain statistical power.

**6. Organic matter correlates with fauna.**
- AFDM correlated with total taxa (r = 0.76), total individuals (r = 0.72), and every common taxon (r from 0.34 to 0.60; d.f. = 78).
- Four of the correlated common taxa were predators, so the relationship may be indirect, operating through prey feeding on detritus.
- Invertebrate density was more closely related to organic matter than to any measured physical variable. Neither depth to bedrock nor vertical gradient strongly affected density, and grain size was uncorrelated with individual taxa.

**7. High variability.**
- Even traps with equal sediment grain size showed large variance in individual counts, so the authors call for larger sample sizes.
- They conclude that the hyporheic zone is a spatially heterogeneous mosaic, paralleling patch dynamics in surface stream communities.

**8. Recommendations.** The authors advocate experimental manipulations of the hyporheos. They suggest future work on [[Recolonization Dynamics]] after [[Flash Floods]] to test resistance and resilience to disturbance.
