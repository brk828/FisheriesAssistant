# Endandered and threatened wildlife and plants; extension of comment period and notice of public hearing on proposed threatened status for the virgin spinedace (Lepidomeda mollispinis mossispsinis)

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Federal Register notice concerning the proposed listing of the **Virgin spinedace** (*Lepidomeda mollispinis mollispinis*) as a **threatened species** under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973. The notice announces a public hearing (July 13, 1994, St. George, Utah) and a 30-day extension of the public comment period (extended to August 17, 1994) due to insufficient public awareness of the original proposal. The species is a small member of the minnow family ([[Cyprinidae]]) endemic to the [[Virgin River]] drainage, a tributary of the [[Colorado River]] system in the southwestern United States.

## Background & Description
- Classified as a **small fish** within the minnow family [[Cyprinidae]].
- Species-level taxonomic identity: *Lepidomeda mollispinis*, subspecies *mollispinis* — genus [[Lepidomeda]], commonly known as "spinedace," a group characterized by spine-like features associated with fin rays (typical of the genus).
- No detailed morphometric, meristic, or coloration data are provided in this regulatory excerpt; the source is administrative/legal rather than a biological field study.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution**: Endemic to the **Virgin River drainage**, a tributary of the [[Colorado River]], spanning:
  - Southwestern [[Utah]]
  - Northwestern [[Arizona]]
  - Southeastern [[Nevada]]
- **Habitat association**: Found in main-stem and tributary reaches of the Virgin River system; potentially co-occurring in close proximity with other imperiled native fishes, including the **woundfin** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) and possibly a related sucker species.
- **Habitat loss**: Approximately **40% of historical habitat** has been lost or degraded due to:
  - Habitat fragmentation (likely from dams/diversions)
  - Introduction of [[Nonnative Fishes]]
  - Dewatering from agriculture, mining, and urbanization
- These anthropogenic stressors are described as **ongoing threats** to the species' persistence.

## Key Findings
- The FWS published a **proposed rule** (50 FR 25875) in March 1994 to list the Virgin spinedace as **threatened** under the Endangered Species Act.
- A **public hearing** was mandated under Section 4(b)(5)(E) of the Act (required within 45 days of proposal publication) and was scheduled for **July 13, 1994**, in St. George, Utah, following a request from local stakeholders, including [[Ronald W. Thompson]], District Manager of the St. George City Water Department.
- The public **comment period was extended by 30 days**, from the original closing date of July 18, 1994, to **August 17, 1994**, due to a survey revealing that many affected individuals were unaware of the proposed rule.
- The notice explicitly requests public and scientific input on:
  1. Biological and population data on the species and its habitat.
  2. Whether specific areas should or should not be designated as [[Critical Habitat]].
  3. Distribution and population size estimates.
  4. Current or planned land/water use activities that could impact the species or its habitat.
- No **critical habitat** was designated concurrently with the proposed listing at the time of this notice; a separate rulemaking process was anticipated pending review of public comments.
- This case illustrates the regulatory and procedural process underlying imperiled freshwater fish conservation in the [[Colorado River Basin]], distinct from marine ichthyological contexts, and highlights the role of endemic minnows as indicator species for arid-region river ecosystem health.
