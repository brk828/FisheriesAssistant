# Meristic and Muscle Protein Characteristics of Galaxiid Fish Populations

#ichthyology #marine-biology #galaxiidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 study by P. M. Greenham, published in the *Australian Journal of Zoology*, compares six populations of [[Galaxiid Fishes]] collected from streams in and around the [[Australian Capital Territory]]. Populations were compared on two axes: meristic counts (vertebrae and fin rays) and the characteristics of water-soluble skeletal muscle protein, assessed by [[Cellulose Acetate Electrophoresis]] and the [[Ouchterlony Immunodiffusion]] method. Meristic counts differed significantly between groups, whereas muscle protein profiles were judged to be essentially identical. The author attributes meristic differences to genetic isolation or environmental influences, and concludes that the biochemical methods could not resolve any real protein differences.

## Background & Description

Biochemical markers such as electrophoretic and chromatographic patterns of muscle or serum proteins have been shown to be species-specific in many vertebrate and invertebrate groups. The author cites earlier work including [[Dannevig 1955]], which separated two populations of the marine fish *Gadus morrhua* (Atlantic cod) by refined chromatography, and [[Hewitt et al. 1963]], who found electrophoretic differences in muscle proteins between geographically isolated populations of *Mollienesia sphenops*.

The present study asks whether phenotypic variation among local galaxiid populations is also visible at the level of water-soluble muscle protein.

**Methods summary**

- **Collection:** Six groups of at least 20 fish were collected with hand-nets. Groups 1 to 4 came from different streams; groups 5 and 6 came from the headwater and lower reaches of one stream. Groups 5 and 6 were the closest geographically, about 3 miles apart. Groups 3 and 4 were roughly 50 water-miles from the others.
- **Morphology:** Ten fish per group were cleared and stained in alizarin red S. Vertebrae and fin rays (caudal, dorsal, anal, left pectoral, left pelvic) were counted.
- **Electrophoresis:** Four fish per group were analysed on cellulose acetate strips at pH 6.5 for 2 hours at 200 V, then stained with Coomassie blue.
- **Immunology:** Pooled muscle protein from six fish per group was injected into New Zealand White rabbits. Antisera were tested against all six group antigens on agar gel slides.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The collection sites were small, headwater and creek systems. Several features suggested that populations were isolated: small waterfalls, swampy areas with little surface water, and miniature rapids were observed in every stream sampled. Groups 3, 4, and 6 were in creeks that carried little water at the time but showed signs of past vigorous flow. Groups 1, 2, and 5 drained well-vegetated catchments with no sign of sustained flooding.

The author suggests that transient flooding could mix populations downstream, and that rainfall may temporarily reduce barrier effects. Environmental influences on meristic development are also considered, drawing on [[Tanning 1952]] (temperature and salinity effects on vertebral and fin ray counts in [[Sea Trout]]) and [[Heuts 1949]] (salinity and temperature effects on fin rays in [[Threespine Stickleback]]). Because galaxiids often develop in very small, weather-sensitive pools, environmental modification of meristic traits was regarded as plausible.

Fish lengths ranged from 43 to 60 mm, with group means of 46 to 54 mm. Evidence of sexual maturity had been observed below 45 mm, so age differences may have affected the electrophoretic results.

## Key Findings

**Meristic data (Table 1)**

- Caudal and left pelvic fin ray counts showed no significant differences between groups (pelvic rays were 6 to 7 in most groups).
- Significant differences (5% level) were found for dorsal fin rays, anal fin rays, pectoral fin rays, and vertebrae.
- Group 5 had significantly fewer dorsal and anal rays than the other groups.
- Groups 1 and 2 had significantly more pectoral rays than group 4.
- Vertebral numbers differed significantly between groups 5 and 6, groups 1 and 2, and groups 3 and 4.
- Vertebral number was not correlated with dorsal, anal, or pectoral ray counts.

**Electrophoresis**

- All groups showed four consistent colour bands (A, B, C, D), with B and C of approximately equal intensity and stronger than A and D.
- Migration distances differed significantly between groups for each band (variance ratios 4.62 to 5.10, excluding band A at 1.6, which was also significant at the 5% level).
- The author judged these differences to reflect experimental variability, including buffer pH changes between runs, batch variation in cellulose acetate, and fish age differences, rather than real protein differences. Average migration differences between paired strips were nearly 2 mm, more than two-thirds of the least significant difference.

**Ouchterlony test**

- Slides 1 to 12 showed a consistently high degree of similarity between muscle proteins from all groups.
- Heterologous reactions generally produced one precipitation band; homologous reactions produced two bands.
- One slide cracked and its results were disregarded. One apparent absence of reaction was attributed to differences in rabbit antibody response or agar density.

**Conclusions**

- The six groups differ meristically, but there is no evidence that the geographically closest populations (groups 5 and 6) are more closely related than more distant ones.
- Meristic differences are attributed to genetic divergence from geographic isolation, or to environmental modification during development.
- Muscle protein differences are too small to be resolved by the methods used.

**Taxonomic note**

The fish most closely resembled *Galaxias bong-bong*, described by [[Macleay 1881]] from a single specimen with 11 dorsal, 11 anal, 14 pectoral, and 7 pelvic rays. The author noted that dorsal ray counts fell within the observed range, but anal counts were lower than in most groups, and pectoral counts were lower than in groups 1 and 2. Comparison at the population level was considered unreliable because the type specimen was not cleared and stained.
