## Executive Summary

This document is a 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) consultation letter addressed to a Department of Defense contractor (ManTech Field Engineering) regarding potential impacts of base realignment activities at Fort Huachuca Military Reservation, Cochise County, Arizona. While the letter is primarily regulatory rather than a scientific study, it enumerates federally listed, proposed, and candidate species — including seven fish taxa — that occur or could benefit from recovery efforts within the Fort Huachuca area and the adjacent [[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]]. The letter emphasizes that groundwater pumping and surface water diversion associated with military and municipal water use threaten to de-water the [[San Pedro River]] within approximately twenty years, jeopardizing native fish populations and downstream senior water rights. No morphological, physiological, or population-abundance data are provided; the document is a species-status and habitat-risk advisory rather than a primary research paper.

## Background & Description

No morphological data for any fish species are presented in this source. The letter is administrative correspondence listing taxonomic and regulatory status only. Species mentioned include:

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Family Catostomidae; federally Endangered.
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — Family Cyprinodontidae; federally Endangered.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) — Family Cyprinidae; federally Threatened.
- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — Family Cyprinidae; federally Threatened.
- **[[Longfin Dace]]** (*Agosia chrysogaster*) — Family Cyprinidae; Candidate Category 2.
- **[[Desert Sucker]]** (*Catostomus clarki*, syn. *Pantosteus clarki*) — Family Catostomidae; Candidate Category 2.
- **[[Gila Chub]]** (*Gila intermedia*) — Family Cyprinidae; Candidate Category 2.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- The species list is tied to the **[[Huachuca Mountains]]** and the **[[San Pedro River]]** drainage, an area described as one of the most biologically diverse mountain ranges in southern Arizona, shaped by rugged topography, varied substrates, bi-seasonal rainfall, perennial surface water, and biogeographic influence from the subtropical **[[Sierra Madre Occidental]]**.
- The **[[San Pedro Riparian National Conservation Area]]** is identified as potential recovery habitat for the listed and candidate fish species, none of which currently occur there but which could benefit from habitat restoration and water management.
- Native desert fish fauna (desert pupfish, razorback sucker, spikedace, loach minnow, longfin dace, desert sucker, Gila chub) are all dependent on perennial or intermittent warm-water streams typical of the Gila River Basin — habitats highly sensitive to reduced base flow.
- The letter explicitly links fish conservation to **groundwater and surface water management**: diversion of surface water in Garden Canyon and groundwater pumping by Fort Huachuca and the city of Sierra Vista are intercepting water that would otherwise sustain base flows in the San Pedro River.
- Continued water use at current rates is projected to **de-water the San Pedro River within approximately 20 years**, with earlier de-watering possible if usage increases — a direct threat to aquatic habitat for all listed/candidate fish taxa.
- Downstream, senior water rights held by the **[[Gila River Indian Tribe]]** are also implicated, linking fish habitat conservation to broader water-rights and tribal resource issues.
- Other co-occurring listed/candidate taxa in the same watershed context include the **[[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]]**, **[[Huachuca Water Umbel]]**, **[[Huachuca Springsnail]]** (*Pyrgulopsis thompsoni*), and **[[Sonoran Tiger Salamander]]**, indicating a broader aquatic/riparian ecosystem of concern beyond fish alone.

## Key Findings

- Seven fish species are flagged in connection with Fort Huachuca and the San Pedro RNCA: two Endangered (razorback sucker, desert pupfish), two Threatened (spikedace, loach minnow), and three Candidate Category 2 (longfin dace, desert sucker, Gila chub).
- These fish species are marked with an asterisk in the original list, denoting species **not currently known to occur** in the area but with **potential habitat for recovery** in the San Pedro River — an important distinction from actively occurring populations.
- The primary anthropogenic threat identified is **hydrological depletion** from groundwater pumping and surface diversion tied to military base operations and the City of Sierra Vista, projected to de-water the San Pedro River in about two decades absent management change.
- The letter underscores that **Candidate Category 1** species (e.g., Chiricahua leopard frog) have sufficient data to support listing, while **Category 2** species (including the three candidate fish) lack sufficient data — a regulatory distinction relevant to conservation prioritization but not a biological/morphological one.
- No population counts, survey data, or site-specific occurrence records for the fish species are included; the note functions as a compliance checklist rather than an ecological survey, and readers seeking demographic or habitat-quality metrics should consult referenced USFWS biological opinions or the August 22, 1994 predecessor letter.
- Broader implication: management of Fort Huachuca water use is explicitly connected to the survival prospects of desert fish species in the [[San Pedro River]] and to protection of senior water rights for the [[Gila River Indian Tribe]], framing fish conservation within a regional water-resource conflict.
