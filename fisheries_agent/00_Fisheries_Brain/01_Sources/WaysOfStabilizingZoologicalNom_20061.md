# Ways of Stabilizing Zoological Nomenclature

#ichthyology #marine-biology #taxonomy #nomenclature #systematics

## Executive Summary

This document is a reprint of a presentation by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] at the XIV International Congress of Zoology in Copenhagen, 1953, addressing the critical need for stability in zoological nomenclature. Hubbs discusses the controversies arising from the Paris decisions five years prior, reports on actions taken at the Copenhagen Colloquium on Zoological Nomenclature to resolve these issues, and proposes ways to achieve more uniform and stable scientific naming practices worldwide. The presentation emphasizes the importance of the [[International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature]] and the [[International Rules of Zoological Nomenclature]] in maintaining nomenclatural stability while acknowledging the need for practical flexibility.

## Background & Description

The presentation was delivered at the General Meeting of the XIV International Congress of Zoology on August 7, 1953, and represents Contributions from the [[Scripps Institution of Oceanography]], New Series, No. 839. The document addresses fundamental challenges in zoological nomenclature that had emerged following the Paris Congress decisions five years earlier, which had led to considerable dissention and controversy, including suggestions that the International Rules be ignored or abandoned.

Hubbs identifies several prerequisites for nomenclatural stability: free interchange of scientific results through publications, societies, and congresses; development of systematics as a sound scientific field; recognition of the scientific nature of systematics by biologists generally; and mutual respect between systematists and other biologists. He notes the rapid development of systematic societies, including the [[Society of Systematic Zoology]] with its new journal, as demonstration of both need and hope for improvement.

The presentation emphasizes that stability depends largely on the actions and attitudes of the [[International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature]] and the Rules it has drawn up. Hubbs particularly acknowledges the work of the Commission's Secretary, Mr. [[Francis Hemming]], in organizing the Colloquium on Zoological Nomenclature that preceded the Congress.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While this document does not address specific ecological or environmental contexts, it has profound implications for all biological research, including aquatic ecology and ichthyology. Hubbs references surveys of successive check-lists of [[North American Fishes]] as examples showing that the main cause for name changes lies in generic reclassification rather than strictly nomenclatural issues. The stability of scientific names directly affects the ability of researchers to communicate findings about species distributions, ecological relationships, conservation status, and evolutionary patterns across temporal and geographic scales.

The document emphasizes that international cooperation and the free flow of scientific information are essential for nomenclatural uniformity. Any "curtain that is pulled down creates isolation, and with this, the prospect for variations in nomenclature," highlighting the importance of maintaining international scientific connections for effective biological research and conservation efforts.

## Key Findings

**Main Principles for Nomenclatural Stability:**
- Recognize and follow the principle of maintaining the status quo until there is good reason for change
- Tolerate understandingly and accept promptly changes based on sound need
- Recognize that the language of systematics is a living and growing entity requiring directed change along reasonable and orderly lines

**Copenhagen Colloquium Achievements:**
- A representative group of systematists from many countries considered pressing nomenclature problems in fine spirit and sympathetic understanding
- Retracted some Paris decisions that proved most objectionable
- Reached essential agreement, preparing the way for a new grammar of systematic zoology
- Decisions to be issued free to Colloquium members and at reasonable price to others in brief and clear language

**Criticisms of Paris Enactments:**
- Several enactments would have led to endless difficulties and uncertainties if confirmed
- Some subjects presented in ways requiring long and expanding series of exceptions
- Complications would render Rules unusable to average zoologists
- Discussions and proposals were confusing enough to discourage some systematic zoologists from their field

**Publication and Dissemination Issues:**
- Widespread opposition to withholding publication rights on International Rules and associated material
- Restriction of republication may help prevent incomplete or misleading statements but limits essential dissemination
- Rules should be considered the "bible of nomenclatural procedure" and should not be copyrighted
- Presentation in lengthy semi-legal terminology difficult to grasp, not conducive to wide support
- Need for brevity, simplicity, and clarity in statements

**Priority Principle Recommendations:**
- Absolutely strict and invariable adherence to law of priority has been unwise
- Priority should be set aside only by official action (Plenary Power or new principle) on compelling grounds
- Should only be used to avoid changes in names generally current among zoologists
- General abandonment of priority would lead to instability and injustice
- When two or more names commonly used, older names should be adopted by concerted action

**New Principle for Action:**
- Publishing facts with recommendation for conservatory action
- Recording action in [[Bulletin of Zoological Nomenclature]]
- Approval of recommendation if no protest received within stated period
- Initiative handed to zoologists with rules to control unscrupulous systematists

**Generic Reclassification Impact:**
- Surveys show overwhelmingly main cause for name changes lies in generic reclassification
- Activity traditionally and rightly beyond scope of International Commission
- Every generic name change requires alteration of every species name in genus
- More lumping creates greater number of name changes
- No way to rule against name changes due to revised classification
- Appeal to good sense of systematists to be conservative in making changes

**Hubbs' Final Proposal (from Discussion):**
- No further attempt to secure agreement on detailed proposal for one unresolved question
- Insert paragraph recommending Régles include provision limiting application of Law of Priority
- Preserve well-known names in use for considerable period from being sunk as junior synonyms of much older unused names
- Include safeguards against abuse by unscrupulous persons
- Entrust Commission with preparing definite provision for forthcoming edition of Régles
- Provision should be as brief and concise as circumstances permit
