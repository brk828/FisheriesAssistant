# Technical Proposal Evaluation for Genetic and Morphological Variation Studies in Gila

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #genetics #morphometrics

## Executive Summary

This document is a Technical Proposal Evaluation Committee (TPEC) review of proposals submitted by [[Arizona State University]] for Solicitation No. 0-SP-40-09700, investigating genetic and morphological variation in the cyprinid fish genus [[Gila]] throughout the [[Colorado River]] basin. The evaluation covers three research schedules: Schedule I (Morphological Investigations), Schedule II (Protein Electrophoresis), and Schedule III (DNA Investigations). The TPEC determined that ASU's proposals are in the competitive range but identified significant concerns regarding costs, equipment dedication, personnel allocation, and sampling adequacy. The evaluation was conducted by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Upper Colorado Regional Office.

## Background & Description

The solicitation sought proposals for comprehensive investigations of [[Gila]] species variation across the [[Colorado River]] basin, including both upper and lower basin populations and Mexican populations. The research was designed to address three complementary approaches:

**Morphological Studies (Schedule I)**: Video camera imaging techniques for non-lethal morphometric analysis, utilizing existing material at ASU and photo repositories. The approach proposed sampling from 30 localities and included methods to address ontogenetic and environmental variation, as well as preservation-related shrinkage effects.

**Protein Electrophoresis (Schedule II)**: Allozyme analysis using large sample sizes with few markers per specimen to enable non-lethal sampling. Preliminary data showed fixed differences particularly in lower [[Colorado River]] basin populations. The approach included cladistic analysis of resulting data.

**DNA Investigations (Schedule III)**: Restriction enzyme analysis of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) to examine genetic variation and hybridization patterns. The technique had demonstrated preliminary success with [[Gila]] species, with considerable baseline data and tissue material already available.

Principal investigators included Dr. W.L. Minckley, Dr. Dowling (genetics/electrophoresis), Dr. Douglas (morphometrics), and Dr. Marsh (field collections). Co-investigator Dr. Wayne Starnes from the [[Smithsonian Institution]] was copied on correspondence.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The research addresses critical conservation questions regarding [[Gila]] species in the [[Colorado River]] basin, particularly:

- **Hybridization and Introgression**: The studies aimed to determine direction of hybridization using mtDNA and to identify potentially hybridizing populations. A key research need was determining whether forms that existed pre-impoundment are still extant or if introgressed forms occurred historically.

- **Geographic Variation**: Sampling strategy encompassed both upper and lower basin populations, recognizing different patterns of genetic differentiation. Preliminary electrophoretic work detected fixed differences in lower basin but not upper basin populations.

- **Habitat Accessibility**: Lower basin collection sites present logistical challenges requiring knowledge of land ownership and access permissions, expertise held by co-PIs Minckley and Marsh.

- **Mexican Populations**: The RFP emphasized the need for broad geographic sampling including Mexican populations to fully characterize species variation.

- **Non-lethal Sampling**: The research design emphasized compatibility with non-lethal sampling techniques, important for conservation of potentially rare or threatened populations.

## Key Findings

**Schedule I (Morphological Investigations) - Evaluation Results**:

*Strengths*:
- Well-designed methods addressing ontogenetic and environmental variation
- Proven non-lethal video imaging technique
- Considerable material already available at ASU and identified photo repositories
- Excellent morphometric and statistical analysis methods proposed by Co-PI Douglas
- Prior successful application to [[Colorado River]] [[Gila]] species

*Weaknesses*:
- No proposed method for gathering transverse measurements or squamation data during non-lethal sampling
- Sampling from 30 localities may be insufficient to describe basin-wide variation
- No strategy addressed for video imaging badly bent museum specimens
- Prior success rate of method not discussed
- PI commitments to other projects could impact this work

*Cost Concerns*:
- Existing ASU equipment committed to other projects, requiring purchase of new equipment at considerable expense
- Excessive funding for PIs whose involvement with morphological work would be modest (beyond Douglas)
- Having PI (Marsh), postdoc, technician, and administrative assistant for data gathering seems excessive
- Proposed additional postdoc and technician for Mexican work deemed excessive
- Overall costs not realistic; sampling meets only minimum criteria

**Schedule II (Protein Electrophoresis) - Evaluation Results**:

*Strengths*:
- Good preliminary data on [[Gila]] spp., particularly from lower [[Colorado River]] basin
- Fixed differences already identified for further study
- Many pertinent samples already in possession
- Large sample size/few markers approach compatible with non-lethal sampling
- Cladistic approach to data analysis
- Good understanding of need for broad study including Mexico
- PI Dowling has extensive experience with electrophoretic techniques and cyprinid fishes
- Strong publication record

*Weaknesses*:
- Total populations proposed to sample possibly inadequate
- Technical help not identified, difficult to assess expertise
- Number of loci meets only minimum RFP criteria; initial screening of larger number desirable, especially for upper basin where no fixed differences yet detected
- Impression of "too much management without indication of who will actually do the work"
- Concern about predisposed ideas due to prior closeness to problem
- Concern about timeliness given diverse group and past contract reporting issues
- PI commitments to other large contracts may affect timeliness

*Cost Concerns*:
- Failure to commit existing equipment greatly increases costs
- Excessive involvement and funding for PIs beyond Dowling relative to this study area
- Funding of full- and half-time technician plus administrative assistant plus PIs seems excessive
- Sampling meets only minimum criteria; additional work would require more funds
- Additional costs for Mexican work including postdoc and technician seem excessive

**Schedule III (DNA Investigations) - Evaluation Results**:

*Strengths*:
- Restriction enzyme technique on mtDNA proven in fish studies
- Good preliminary results with [[Gila]] in particular
- Very good baseline dataset already in place
- Access to considerable tissue material
- Demonstrated success with techniques
- Strong dedication to problem
- mtDNA will provide information on direction of hybridization
- Thorough understanding of problem and need for broad study including Mexican populations
- Good sampling strategy
- PI Dowling has excellent experience with similar cyprinid studies using proposed techniques
- Strong publication record
- Extensive field collection experience with [[Gila]]

*Weaknesses*:
- No capability proposed to examine DNA of preserved historic material for comparison with present-day populations
- Does not address research need to determine if pre-impoundment forms are extant or if introgressed forms occurred formerly
- Amount of tissue required not as compatible with non-lethal sampling compared to other techniques (e.g., [[PCR]])
- Concern that proposed sample sizes from some populations may be too low
- PIs may not be grounded in techniques facilitating use of museum material (e.g., [[PCR]])
- Research associates/technicians not identified
- Concern about ASU's willingness to supply reports in timely manner
- Concern about possible preconceived taxonomic interpretations due to prior closeness to problem
- PIs not offering existing equipment, requiring purchase of additional equipment at considerable expense

*Cost Concerns*:
- Beyond salaries, some costs not well trackable to specified work
- Amount of involvement of persons other than main PI (Dowling) and research associate seems excessive and unjustifiable
- Full- and half-time technician plus administrative assistant and research assistant seem excessive
- Treating Mexican studies as full-fledged additional pursuit with additional postdoc and technician very excessive and not how RFP should be construed
- Considering no technique to amplify DNA from preserved material proposed, overall cost very high
- Sampling meets minimum criteria; additional work would require upward "budget adjustments"
- Failure to dedicate existing equipment severely increases project costs
- Time/salary commitment of "higher level" personnel beyond PI Dowling more than required

**General Recommendations**:

The TPEC requested that ASU discuss:
- Ability to perform DNA analysis of museum material
- Use of mtDNA to analyze potential hybridization problems (if not already in original proposal)
- Reevaluation of costs relative to committee comments

The committee determined proposals were in competitive range and scheduled telephone negotiations to address these concerns, after which ASU would submit a best and final offer responding to TPEC comments.
