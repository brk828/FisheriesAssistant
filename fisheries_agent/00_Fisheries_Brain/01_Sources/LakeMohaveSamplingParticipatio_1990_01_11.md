## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 administrative letter from the Nevada Department of Wildlife (Region III) to Dr. Paul C. Marsh of Arizona State University, confirming Nevada's participation in a scheduled interagency sampling effort for [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] on Lake Mojave (March 18–25, 1990). While primarily logistical in nature (crew and equipment commitments), the letter contains a notable field observation relayed by an experienced diver regarding apparent disease symptoms in wild [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Common Carp]] populations below Willow Beach. This qualifies the document as an informal but relevant source of ecological/health monitoring data for endangered Colorado River fishes.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological, meristic, or biometric data are provided in this source. The letter does reference an observable external condition:
- **Lesions/Sores**: Multiple individuals of both [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Common Carp]] were observed with skin sores/lesions.
- **Suspected Etiology**: Described as "consistent with a possible fungal infection," though this is a lay diver's field assessment, not a laboratory diagnosis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: [[Lake Mojave]], a reservoir on the [[Colorado River]] impounded by Davis Dam, spanning the Nevada–Arizona border.
- **Location of Observation**: Downstream of [[Willow Beach]], specifically in the vicinity of River Mile Marker 49 and further downstream.
- **Sympatric Species**: The affected area supports co-occurring populations of native [[Razorback Sucker]] and non-native [[Common Carp]], indicating potential for cross-species pathogen transmission or shared environmental stressors (e.g., water quality, temperature stress) in this reach of the reservoir.
- **Endangered Species Context**: Both [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] are the target taxa of the referenced multi-agency monitoring program, reflecting their status as imperiled native Colorado River Basin fishes subject to coordinated recovery/sampling efforts (a precursor to what became the multi-agency [[Native Fish Monitoring Program]] on Lake Mojave).

## Key Findings
- **Interagency Collaboration**: Nevada Department of Wildlife formally committed to supplying at least one two-person field crew daily throughout the March 1990 [[Lake Mojave]] sampling effort for [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]], coordinated by [[Arizona State University]] (Center for Environmental Studies).
- **Equipment/Logistics Constraints**: The agency reported an inability to supply functional trammel nets at the time, though a boat for crew transport was available — a practical constraint relevant to reconstructing survey methodology and effort for this historical sampling event.
- **Disease/Health Observation**: An experienced diver (Hugh Winesett) documented numerous [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Common Carp]] with sores suggestive of fungal infection in the reach below [[Willow Beach]] (~River Mile 49 and downstream). This represents an early, informal record of potential disease pressure on wild razorback sucker populations in Lake Mojave.
- **Data Provenance Note**: The diver maintained "extensive dive notes," suggesting a potentially valuable but likely unpublished/gray-literature source of longitudinal observational data on fish health and distribution in this system.
- **Historical Context**: This letter (dated January 1990) provides a dated data point in the long-term monitoring history of endangered Colorado River fishes in Lake Mojave, useful for tracing the origins of cooperative sampling programs among Nevada Department of Wildlife, Arizona State University, and likely other basin stakeholders (e.g., USFWS, Arizona Game and Fish).
