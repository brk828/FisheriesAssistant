# Lawsuit Against the Gila River Flood-Control Dike, Arizona (1995)

#freshwater-ecology #river-ecosystem #riparian-vegetation

## Executive Summary

In late May 1995, four environmental advocacy groups sued the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] in the U.S. District Court in Phoenix to halt a $73 million, 56-mile flood-control dike along the [[Gila River]] in southwestern Arizona. The plaintiffs argue that the dike would destroy natural vegetation regrowth on the river's floodplain and that the Corps ignored the environmental harm. They propose buying out roughly 125 farmers in the [[Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation District]] as an alternative. The Corps' environmental review, completed the previous month, had concluded the project would not harm the river. Construction was expected to begin later in summer 1995, after winter flows subsided. The Corps did not return media calls.

## Background & Description

- **Project:** A diked, earthen flood-control channel roughly the width of a football field, stretching 56 miles, estimated at $73 million.
- **Purpose:** Protect about 125 farmers in the Wellton-Mohawk Irrigation District from flooding.
- **Trigger:** Record statewide floods in 1993 (see [[1993 Arizona Floods]]) damaged riverside farms. Farmers complained that federal action was too slow, so they repaired a dike themselves without government approval.
- **Lead agency:** The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, which conducted an [[Environmental Impact Review]].
- **Plaintiffs:** [[Southwest Center for Biological Diversity]], [[Defenders of Wildlife]], the [[Greater Gila Biodiversity Project]], and [[Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Gila is normally a dry river outside winter storm flows. Since the 1993 flood, trees and other vegetation have regrown across the natural [[Floodplain Restoration]] zone. Plaintiffs argue this [[Riparian Vegetation]] shows the river ecosystem can recover on its own if left alone. According to biologist [[Peter Galvin]], the project "would just wipe out all of the regrowth." The plaintiffs argue that a buyout would let vegetation and wildlife along the river continue to flourish.

## Key Findings

- **Legal challenge:** Four groups sued to stop the dike, alleging that the Corps ignored environmental harm from a $73 million project.
- **Alternative proposed:** Plaintiffs recommend a government buyout of the roughly 125 farmers instead of building the dike.
- **Regrowth at stake:** Floodplain vegetation has rebounded since the 1993 flood, and plaintiffs argue the dike would destroy it.
- **Federal review disputed:** The Corps' review concluded the project would not harm the Gila, a finding the plaintiffs contest.
- **Subsidy claim:** The lawsuit states that the federal government has subsidized each farm in the irrigation district by an average of nearly $4.8 million since 1952 for dams, diversions, and a [[Desalinization Plant]] that does not work.
- **Local opposition to buyout:** Farmer [[Layne Peterson]] of Wellton opposed both a new dike and a government buyout, asking about the schools and businesses in town.
- **Timeline:** Construction could begin later in summer 1995, once winter flows have receded.
