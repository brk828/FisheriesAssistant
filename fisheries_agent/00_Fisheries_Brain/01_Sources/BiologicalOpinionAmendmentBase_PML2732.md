# Biological Opinion Amendment: Baseline and Horse Springs Livestock Grazing

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, dated January 15, 1998) amends the July 20, 1995 [[Biological Opinion]] on the allotment management plan for the Baseline and Horse Springs livestock grazing allotments, managed by the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]] in Greenlee County, Arizona. The 1995 opinion addressed [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], and [[Bald Eagle]].

The [[Forest Service]] revised its grazing plan after the 1995 opinion, adopting a seasonal deferred rotation system that resembles the backup plan considered in 1995. Because the change was similar to that backup plan, the Service decided that full reinitiation of consultation was not needed. Instead, it issued this amendment. The Service concluded that the revised action is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of any of the four listed species. It also found that no critical habitat for these species is present in the action area.

## Background & Description

**Consultation history**
- The Forest Service initiated consultation on January 23, 1995. The Service issued a nonjeopardy opinion on July 20, 1995.
- The permittee found the 1995 intensive management system (Holistic Resource Management) difficult to implement, so the Forest Service adopted a different action. A FONSI was signed November 24, 1997.
- On December 11, 1997, the Forest Service withdrew the FONSI/Decision Notice. A January 7, 1998 meeting agreed that the final action resembled the 1995 backup proposal, so an amendment was appropriate. Full reinitiation was not required, provided that Incidental Take Statement reports were furnished promptly.

**Proposed action changes (Table 1, summarized)**
- The 1995 "biologically controlled" plan used one allotment, 188–300 head, 3,019–4,818 AUMs, yearlong use, and 33 pastures.
- The 1995 backup plan used two allotments, 188–300 head, 3,019 AUMs, deferred rotation, yearlong use, and 7 pastures.
- The current proposed plan uses one allotment, 190–405 head (cow/calf and yearling), 3,019 AUMs, and seasonal deferred rotation from September 1 to May 30 (dormant season), with 7 pastures.
- Growing-season rest is increased relative to 1995. Livestock grazing on the streams of Bear and Bee Canyons is restricted to September 1–May 30.
- New water developments include about 47,000 feet of pipeline from an existing well to 13 drinkers, 3–8,000-gallon fiberglass storage tanks, and a water storage system. Erosion control and seeding of vertisol areas continue. Prescribed fire and fuelwood harvest are excluded.

**Species status updates**
- Critical habitat for spikedace and loach minnow was set aside by the U.S. District Court for New Mexico (Catron County Board of Commissioners v. U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Order of October 13, 1994). The former designations did not include habitat in the action area.
- Eagle Creek was not part of the former critical habitat for either fish. The spikedace and loach minnow populations there were unknown when critical habitat was proposed. The recovery plans recommend adding Eagle Creek to critical habitat in future decisions.
- The bald eagle was reclassified to threatened status on July 12, 1995.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Eagle Creek:** About three miles of [[Eagle Creek]] lie within these allotments and are heavily influenced by upstream watershed and channel conditions. Eagle Creek is a potential reintroduction area for loach minnow.
- **Riparian zones:** Livestock grazing in lower [[Bear Canyon]] and [[Bee Canyon]] is a major factor limiting regeneration of woody riparian species. Removing livestock during the summer growing season should ease these effects. Some impacts remain from spring grazing and from physical disturbance of streambanks.
- **Vegetation and soils:** Per the Forest Service's Final Environmental Analysis (FEA), the proposed system is predicted to increase upland vegetation in good to excellent condition by 35%, riparian areas in satisfactory condition by 45%, and satisfactory soil condition by 24%. Table 2 reports baseline condition classes for upland vegetation (about 51% good to fair, 49% poor to very poor) and riparian vegetation (90% unsatisfactory), along with soil condition (about 41% satisfactory, 37% impaired, 22% unsatisfactory). The table's column assignments are ambiguous in the scanned source.
- **Vertisol soils:** About 20–30% of the allotment is vertisol soil, which is targeted for seeding.
- **Nonnative fish risk:** The new water system was designed to limit [[Nonnative Fish]] establishment. Three of four storage tanks are enclosed, and all are artificial. The 250-gallon drinkers are located at least 1/4 mile from four-wheel-drive roads with minimal traffic, and they will not be placed in natural drainages where flooding could allow fish to escape.
- **Water source:** The new development does not draw water from Eagle Creek. It uses an existing non-alluvial well.

## Key Findings

**Effects on listed species**
- **Razorback sucker, spikedace, and loach minnow:** The revised action is expected to produce greater improvement to Eagle Creek than the 1995 action because it removes livestock from the stream channels. However, channel stabilization and increased surface flows may be limited by upstream impacts.
- **Bald eagle:** Effects act through perching sites and food base availability. The seasonal rest is expected to reduce adverse effects to the riparian zone and buffering capacity of Bear and Bee Canyons.
- **Incidental take:** The Service anticipates incidental take of spikedace, loach minnow, and razorback sucker. Direct take could occur through fish loss during cattle entry into the creek when exclusion fences are breached. Indirect take would occur through short- and long-term habitat degradation caused by watershed and streambank impacts. No take of bald eagles is expected.

**Conclusion**
- Under the 1997 seasonal deferred rotation system, the Service concluded that livestock grazing is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the spikedace, loach minnow, razorback sucker, or bald eagle.
- No finding was required on former critical habitat, because the court set those designations aside. The removal of critical habitat from consideration does not change the findings of the 1995 opinion.

**Terms and conditions**
- The Service acknowledged receipt of the Eagle Creek photopoint and channel cross-section data required by the 1995 Incidental Take Statement.
- The reinitiation notice of the 1995 opinion applies to this amendment. Conservation recommendations and the Reasonable and Prudent Measures are unchanged.
- A new reference was added: the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 1995 bald eagle reclassification rule (*Federal Register* 60 FR 35999–36010).
