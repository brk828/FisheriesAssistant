# Echinogammarus paxjuliensis sp. n. from a Well in Southern Portugal

#crustacea #amphipoda #gammaridae

## Executive Summary

This 1979 taxonomic paper by [[Amilcar Mateus]], [[Emilia Mateus]] and [[Odette Afonso]] describes a new epigean amphipod, *Echinogammarus paxjuliensis* sp. n. ([[Gammaridae]]), from the Monte da Calcada area near Beja in the [[Alentejo]] region of southern [[Portugal]]. The animals were found unexpectedly in a well during a collecting trip aimed at troglobiotic (cave-adapted) amphipods. The authors separate the species from the closely related *[[Echinogammarus Simoni]]* and *[[Echinogammarus Villaticus]]* on several morphological characters and place it among the known Portuguese *Echinogammarus* fauna. The paper also documents the vertical distribution of the animals within the well, their seasonal development, and the apparent absence of the genus from other Alentejo sites that were surveyed.

## Background & Description

**Context of the study.** The authors had been studying Portuguese Amphipoda, both marine and freshwater, for several years. They had already recorded new localities for known epigean species and described *[[Echinogammarus Villaticus]]* (Mateus, 1974). They also had published work on hypogean (subterranean) amphipods ([[Hypogean Fauna]]). Earlier surveys by Pinkster & Stock (1972) and Pinkster (1973) had covered much of the Iberian Peninsula, with many unproductive Portuguese stations. Pinkster (1973) added only *[[Echinogammarus Meridionalis]]* to the Portuguese fauna. The Monte da Calcada material was collected in the Évora–Beja–Serpa region, which had not been adequately explored before. Study funding came from the Centro de Zoo-sistemática of the Instituto Nacional de Investigação Científica.

**Collecting methods.**
- **August 1978:** Gauze bags were placed on the bottom of many wells. Most yielded isopods, which were passed to Afonso for study. Only one well (Monte da Calcada) yielded amphipods, along with two troglobiotic isopods. The bags were retrieved the following day.
- **April 1979:** A custom sampler was built from a weighted tin box, suspended on strings marked at one-metre intervals. Its lid was hinged and weighted with lead so it could be opened at chosen depths.
- Streams around the well and a second well about 500 m downstream were also sampled.

**Site description.** The well is circular and brick-lined, with filamentous green algae (probably *Spirogyra*) attached to the walls. Its total depth is 2.75 m and the water level is held constant by an outflow pipe 1 m from the rim. The water remained at 1.75 m from the bottom in both sampling periods. The water is used for drinking.

**Species description.** The new species is described in detail from the holotype and paratypes. The description covers:
- Head, eyes and body proportions
- Antennae I and II, including flagellum and article counts
- Mouthparts: mandibles (including the accessory lamina and the palp), maxillae I and II, lower lip and maxillipeds
- Gnathopods I and II
- Pereiopods IV–VII
- Pleopods, uropods I–III and telson

The main sexual dimorphism is in body size and in the setation of antennae II, which is denser in males.

**Holotype and type material.** The holotype is a male (specimen no. 427a) measuring 11.1 mm, collected at Monte da Calcada (Beja) on 7 April 1979. The type series is held in the collections of the Instituto de Zoologia Dr. Augusto Nobre. Body size figures given in the text:
- Males: mean 11.3 mm (largest observed 13.2 mm)
- Females: mean 9.06 mm (largest observed 10.5 mm)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Vertical distribution in the well.** The April 1979 sampling showed a clear depth pattern:
- Abundance was highest at about 1 m below the surface.
- Surface samples yielded few individuals.
- Samples taken close to the bottom (1.75 m) yielded more individuals than the surface samples, but fewer than the mid-depth zone.

The authors state that the animals were not troglobiotic and showed no sign of the morphological or ecological adaptations to cave life that might have been expected from a well habitat.

**Surface and stream habitat.** In April 1979 the surrounding area was less dry than in August 1978, and streams were flowing. Amphipods were found in the stream next to the first well, but in smaller numbers. A stream flowing past a second well about 500 m away, at a lower elevation and with a more torrential flow, had more abundant amphipods. The animals lived among gravel under flowing water, among grass on the stream margins. Sampling in the second well produced nothing.

**Seasonal patterns.** Specimens collected in April 1979 were larger than those from August 1978 and included ovigerous females and juveniles. The authors conclude that breeding probably takes place in winter and extends into spring.

**Regional distribution.** Nothing was found in the Beja district outside Monte da Calcada, including the montes along the Santa Clara de Louredo–Penedo Gordo road, Aljustrel, the Roxo dam area, or Ferreira do Alentejo. Pinkster & Stock (1972) also reported no amphipods from the Alentejo. The authors regard the Monte da Calcada records as especially significant for this reason.

**Biogeographical context.** The Portuguese *Echinogammarus* species are distributed as follows:
- *[[Echinogammarus Lusitanus]]* occurs only in the north, within about 40 km of the coast, north of the Douro.
- *E. villaticus* occurs in a restricted area near Coimbra.
- *E. meridionalis* occurs around Leiria, where *[[Echinogammarus Simoni]]* also begins to appear.
- *E. simoni* extends south through the country.

The Monte da Calcada locality is about 100 km from the coast, well beyond the range that the authors give for the genus. Other gammarids recorded during the survey were *Gammarus chevreuxi* at Óbidos and *G. pulex* at Francelos, south of Porto.

## Key Findings

- **A new species is described.** *Echinogammarus paxjuliensis* sp. n. is an epigean freshwater amphipod from a well and adjacent streams at Monte da Calcada, Beja. The authors consider it new to science.
- **Closest relative.** The new species most resembles *E. simoni*, followed by *E. villaticus*. It differs from both in body size, eye shape, antennae II, mandibular palp setation, gnathopods, telson and uropod III. Differences in the mouthparts and in the basal articles of pereiopods other than VII are also noted, though considered less important.
- **Restricted distribution.** The species was found only at one well, its adjacent streams and a nearby stream. No other sites in the surrounding Alentejo yielded the genus, and the authors consider the locality isolated from the other known *Echinogammarus* records.
- **Depth preference.** Within the well, the animals were most abundant at about 1 m depth.
- **Non-troglobiotic.** The authors found no evidence that the population is cave-adapted, despite the well habitat.
- **Breeding season.** Reproduction probably occurs in winter and extends into spring, based on ovigerous females and juveniles collected in April 1979.
- **Taxonomic significance.** The authors argue that the discovery matters both zoogeographically (the species lies far from the coast) and taxonomically (it is a distinct species). They also conclude that the Portuguese freshwater epigean amphipod fauna is relatively poor, though some areas still need careful study.
