## Executive Summary

This USFWS-funded final report by Brussard, Berg, and Bartley presents an allozyme electrophoresis study designed to detect genetic evidence of hybridization between the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*), endemic to Pyramid Lake, Nevada, and the sympatric [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*). The primary motivation was to determine whether cui-ui individuals with **aberrant mouth morphology** (Group B) represented natural hybrids with Tahoe sucker, as opposed to typical cui-ui (Group A). A secondary objective was to identify diagnostic electrophoretic markers capable of distinguishing pure cui-ui, pure Tahoe sucker, and hybrid fry in hatchery contexts. Using starch-gel electrophoresis across 41 isozyme loci, the study found remarkably low genetic divergence between the morphologically distinct cui-ui groups (A and B), with virtually all loci fixed for identical alleles, casting doubt on hybridization as the source of the aberrant mouth morphology. In contrast, Tahoe sucker showed several diagnostic allele frequency differences from both cui-ui groups.

## Background & Description

- **Focal trait under investigation:** Aberrant (abnormal) mouth morphology in a subset of cui-ui individuals from [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, hypothesized to result from introgressive hybridization with [[Tahoe Sucker]].
- **Sample groupings** (based on mouth morphology, not genetics a priori):
  - **Group A:** "Pure" cui-ui (typical mouth morphology).
  - **Group B:** Presumptive cui-ui/Tahoe sucker hybrids (aberrant mouth morphology).
- Tissue types sampled for enzyme assays in adults: **liver (L), muscle (M), eye (E)**.
- Fry samples were too small for tissue-specific dissection; head and gut were removed and the remaining carcass (predominantly **muscle** tissue) was homogenized whole.
- No direct external morphometric data (e.g., meristics, mouth measurements) are tabulated in the surviving text; classification into Groups A/B was performed by USFWS prior to genetic analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Cui-ui** (*Chasmistes cujus*): Endemic, endangered catostomid restricted to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada — a terminal desert lake within the Lahontan Basin (Great Basin hydrographic system, no ocean outlet).
- **Tahoe Sucker** (*Catostomus tahoensis*): A congeneric catostomid found in [[Lake Tahoe]] (sampled at Sand Point, NV) and connected Great Basin waters, sympatric or parapatric with cui-ui populations, raising the potential for natural hybridization at shared spawning/tributary sites.
- Both species belong to family **Catostomidae** (suckers), a group noted for **polyploid evolutionary history**, duplicated gene loci, and complex patterns of gene silencing — relevant to the interpretation of multiple isozyme loci observed in this study.
- Hatchery-reared fry (cui-ui, Tahoe sucker, and their putative hybrids) were also examined, indicating an applied conservation/aquaculture management context aimed at species identification for stocking and recovery programs.

## Key Findings

- **41 variable isozyme loci** were screened across ~30 enzyme systems (e.g., [[Aat]], [[Ah]], [[Ck]], [[Est]], [[Gpi]], [[Idh]], [[Mdh]], [[Pgm]], [[Sod]], [[Tpi]], and others) using multiple buffer systems (CAM, R, TC, TG) following standard starch-gel electrophoresis protocols (Aebersold et al. 1987; Shaw & Prasad 1970).
- **Average heterozygosity (H):**
  - Tahoe Sucker: **H = 0.061**
  - Cui-ui Group A: **H = 0.002**
  - Cui-ui Group B: **H = 0.003**
  - This indicates cui-ui populations (both A and B) have extremely low genetic diversity relative to Tahoe sucker, consistent with a small, isolated, potentially bottlenecked endemic population.
- **Cui-ui Groups A and B were nearly genetically identical** across almost all 41 loci — both groups were fixed (frequency = 1.000) for the same alleles at the vast majority of loci (e.g., mAat-1, mAat-2, Aat-1/2/3, Ah-1/2, Fba-1, Est-3/4/5, Ck-3, Gpdh-1, Gpi-2, Gr, Idh-1/3, Mdh-4, Mdhp, Mpi, PepD, PepB, Pgm-1/2/3/4, Pk-1, Pgk-1/2, Pgdh, Sod-2, Tat, Tpi-1, Gda, B-Ga, α-Glu-1/2).
- Minor allele frequency differences between Group A and Group B were detected at only a few loci:
  - **Gpi-4**: A = 0.962/0.038; B = 0.917/0.056/0.027 (three alleles in B vs. two in A)
  - **Idh-2**: A = 0.038/0.962 pattern shift relative to B
  - **Mdh-3**: A = 1.000; B = 0.972/0.028
  - These differences are minor and do not provide strong support for Group B being hybrids rather than natural variants within cui-ui.
- **Tahoe Sucker showed clear allelic distinctiveness** from both cui-ui groups at numerous loci, including polymorphisms not seen in cui-ui at all (e.g., Aat-2, Aat-3, Gpdh-1, Gpi-2, Gr, Idh-1, Mdh-4, PepB, Pgm-1, Pgm-2, Pgm-3, Tat), confirming that diagnostic markers **do exist to distinguish Tahoe sucker from cui-ui**, supporting the feasibility of the secondary objective (identifying hybrids/fry).
- The overall pattern — near-fixation and lack of variation within cui-ui, contrasted with polymorphism in Tahoe sucker — suggests that the **aberrant mouth morphology in Group B cui-ui is not strongly associated with introgressive hybridization** detectable via these nuclear/mitochondrial allozyme markers, though the authors note the analysis remains limited by low resolution of purely fixed cui-ui loci.
- The discussion references the evolutionary complexity of the **Catostomidae**, noting apparent **duplicated gene loci**, delayed **tissue-specific silencing** of duplicate genes during development, and mutation-driven divergence at structural loci — framed as an area warranting further evolutionary study (citing [[Ferris & Whitt 1977]] on loss of duplicate gene expression after polyploidization).
- Methodologically, the study established a **reference allele-frequency table** for 41 loci across Tahoe sucker and both cui-ui morphotypes, providing a baseline dataset for future hybrid identification efforts in hatchery and wild populations of the [[Pyramid Lake]] fish community.
