## Executive Summary

This document is a formal comment letter from the Arizona Game and Fish Department (AGFD) addressed to Dr. Duncan Patten (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) regarding the third draft of the **Long-Term Monitoring Plan for Grand Canyon**, developed under the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies (GCES) program. Rather than presenting novel primary research, the letter critiques monitoring design for the regulated Colorado River ecosystem downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]], with particular emphasis on inadequate sampling frequency and life-stage coverage for native, including federally endangered, fish species. The letter also raises concerns regarding sediment dynamics, water chemistry protocols, riparian vegetation monitoring, and the treatment of invertebrates and other vertebrates relative to fish-monitoring priorities — highlighting an institutional disparity in monitoring rigor between fishes directly affected by dam operations and other taxa only indirectly affected.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body measurements) are presented in this document, as it is a policy/monitoring-design review rather than a field or laboratory study. However, the letter implicitly distinguishes taxa by **life stage** categories relevant to population structure monitoring:

- **Pre-adult life stages** (larval and juvenile) of native fishes — identified as a monitoring priority since 1984.
- **Adult life stages** — noted as historically over-emphasized relative to pre-adult stages in some sampling regimes.
- Reference to **[[Rainbow Trout]]** and **[[Brown Trout]]** reproduction is tied to substrate characteristics: sediment armoring in the [[Lee's Ferry]] reach is described as restricting availability of suitable spawning sediments for natural trout reproduction, linking sediment grain-size dynamics to reproductive habitat suitability.
- **Stranding** of trout in backwaters/receding flows is mentioned as a mortality factor mitigated by the "low fluctuating flow" alternative.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter frames the aquatic ecosystem of Grand Canyon as shaped by the combined structural and operational effects of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]:

- **Backwaters**: Identified as critical native fish habitat; their maintenance/rejuvenation is tied to experimental habitat maintenance flows rather than the standard low fluctuating flow regime.
- **Sediment dynamics**: Coarsening/armoring of substrate in the Lee's Ferry reach simultaneously benefits [[Periphyton]] growth (via increased hard-substrate availability) while degrading spawning substrate for trout — illustrating a trade-off between primary production habitat and fish reproductive habitat.
- **Tailrace zone**: Proposed as the site for coupling periphyton standing crop measurements with organic matter budgets (mainstem organic matter flux, analogous to sediment budgets).
- **Lake Powell forebay**: Site of physical/chemical/phytoplankton monitoring feeding into selective withdrawal structure modeling; data here extend beyond a single-purpose model and have interdependence with mainstem thermal regimes.
- **Water chemistry gradient**: A sampling frequency disparity is noted between the dam forebay (monthly) and the mainstem downstream (seasonal) for dissolved oxygen, particulate/dissolved organic matter, nitrogen, and phosphorus — raising concerns about comparability of organic matter budgets along the river continuum.
- **Riparian-aquatic interface**: The Old High-Water Zone (OHWZ) vegetation boundary (~100,000 cfs) is discussed in relation to habitat maintenance flows, an important interface for both riparian and epibenthic invertebrate communities.
- **Endangered and special-status species**: The letter singles out federally listed native fishes and the [[Kanab Ambersnail]] as species requiring dedicated, clearly assigned monitoring responsibility (unclear whether the National Park Service would monitor the ambersnail).

## Key Findings

- **Sampling frequency inadequacy**: AGFD reiterates (from comments on the second draft) that native fish monitoring, especially for **Endangered Species Act (ESA)-listed species**, remains insufficient in frequency. They recommend a minimum of **twice-annual sampling**: one during the spring larval fish presence period, and one in autumn following summer floods.
- **Life-stage sampling disparity**: Pre-adult (larval/juvenile) life stages of native fish were prioritized in mainstem monitoring 1987–1989, but the current draft plan does not adequately balance effort across life stages relative to sampling logistics/costs.
- **Cost-efficiency argument**: Since the dominant cost of fish sampling in Grand Canyon is crew mobilization to remote sites, AGFD argues all life stages should be sampled whenever present, maximizing per-trip data yield.
- **Cross-taxon monitoring inequity**: Terrestrial vertebrates and other taxa "only indirectly affected by dam operations" are proposed for more frequent monitoring than native fishes directly impacted by dam operations — a prioritization AGFD considers backward.
- **Sediment-fish linkage**: Armoring of substrate in the Lee's Ferry reach is flagged as a mechanism reducing trout spawning habitat availability, recommended for explicit inclusion in sediment dynamics monitoring objectives.
- **Backwater habitat value**: Backwater maintenance is not currently credited as a benefit of the selected low fluctuating flow alternative, despite ecological importance to native fish; reduction in trout stranding should be added as a documented benefit of this flow regime.
- **Water chemistry protocol gaps**: Discrepant sampling frequencies between the Glen Canyon Dam forebay (monthly) and downstream mainstem (seasonal) undermine the ability to construct a coherent organic matter budget for the highly productive reach below the dam; AGFD recommends equalized frequency across forebay, dam inputs/outputs, and Lee's Ferry.
- **Phytoplankton/pigment methodology critique**: Full taxonomic phytoplankton studies at Lake Powell are described as costly/time-consuming; AGFD recommends higher-resolution pigment analysis (e.g., chlorophyll *a* fraction) as a more practical surrogate, noting "total chlorophyll" is a non-standard metric.
- **Data redundancy concern**: Statistical variability of physical/chemical forebay parameters was already characterized by USGS during GCES Phase II and need not be re-assessed via new monthly sampling.
- **Modeling data gaps**: Adequate modeling of water temperature changes under a selective withdrawal structure remains impossible due to persistent absence of complete meteorological stations in Glen and Grand Canyons, a gap recognized since GCES Phase I (Ron Ferrari's work).
- **Invertebrate taxonomy note**: Riparian invertebrate taxa in Grand Canyon have reportedly already been substantially identified by [[Larry Stevens]] and colleagues, suggesting redundancy risk in new invertebrate survey efforts.
- **Unresolved governance question**: The letter questions which body (long-term monitoring program, adaptive management work group, or higher authority) will define "acceptable levels of change" for resources — a foundational unresolved issue for interpreting monitoring data.
