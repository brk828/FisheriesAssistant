# A Revision of the Viviparous Perches

#ichthyology #marine-biology #embiotocidae #taxonomy #viviparity

## Executive Summary

This 1918 systematic revision by Carl L. Hubbs reorganizes the family [[Embiotocidae]] (viviparous perches or surf-fishes) of [[California]], establishing three subfamilies based on anal fin structure and dentition. The [[Embiotocidae]] represent a highly evolved example of viviparity in fishes, forming a compact, well-differentiated group within the Percoid fishes. The revision includes detailed analytical keys to all genera and species, with particular attention to sexual dimorphism in anal fin morphology and the biogeographic distribution spanning from [[Japan]] to [[Puget Sound]].

## Background & Description

The [[Embiotocidae]] are viviparous perches endemic primarily to the [[California]] coast, with two genera occurring in [[Japan]]. The family exhibits remarkable reproductive specialization, with viviparity developed to a high degree of perfection. Most genera are now regarded as monotypic, expressing their isolated phylogenetic positions relative to one another.

The family is divided into three subfamilies:

**[[Embiotocinae]]** (primitive group): Contains three-fifths of the genera, including forms with unusual range or habitat. Includes the two Japanese genera ([[Ditrema temminckii]], [[Neoditrema ransonnetii]]), species ranging north to [[Puget Sound]], one species ([[Zalembius rosaceus]]) descending to moderate depths on the continental shelf, and the only freshwater representative ([[Hysterocarpus traski]]). Other species inhabit bays, beaches, and reefs of the Californian faunal region.

**[[Amphistichinae]]** (new subfamily): Comprises several surf fishes of [[California]].

**[[Micrometrinae]]** (new subfamily): A specialized offshoot from [[Embiotocinae]], including only two herbivorous species ([[Amphigonopterus aurora]], [[Micrometrus minimus]]) living almost exclusively along rocky reefs and rock-pools of [[California]].

### Morphological Characteristics

**Dentition**: Teeth vary by subfamily - bluntly conic and uniserial in [[Embiotocinae]], bluntly conic in two series in [[Amphistichinae]] (except lower jaw of [[Hyperprosopon argenteus]]), and tricuspid uniserial in [[Micrometrinae]].

**Anal Fin Sexual Dimorphism**: 
- [[Embiotocinae]]: Males and females have same number of fin-rays; males possess enlarged fleshy front portion modified into hard excrescence followed by large oval gland-like structure
- [[Amphistichinae]]: Same ray count in both sexes; males have rays crowded anteriorly with one ray (about twelfth) modified into large triangular plate
- [[Micrometrinae]]: Males contain more rays than females (unique among bony fishes except South American [[Cynolebias]]); males possess large oval depression with well-defined rim near front of anal fin

**Vertebral Counts**: Vary by species, e.g., [[Hysterocarpus traski]] 14+20=34, [[Rhacochilus toxotes]] 14+22=36.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Distribution**:
- Most species inhabit coastal marine environments: bays, beaches, reefs, surf zones
- [[Hysterocarpus traski]]: Only freshwater representative
- [[Zalembius rosaceus]]: Descends to moderate depths on continental shelf
- [[Amphigonopterus aurora]]: Inhabits rock-pools of central [[California]] coast
- [[Micrometrinae]] species: Live almost exclusively along rocky reefs

**Geographic Range**: 
- Primary distribution: [[California]] coast (Californian faunal region)
- Northern extent: [[Puget Sound]] fauna
- Disjunct populations: Two genera in [[Japan]] ([[Ditrema]], [[Neoditrema]])

**Feeding Ecology**: The two [[Micrometrinae]] species are herbivorous with longer intestines than other groups.

## Key Findings

1. **Subfamily Classification**: Hubbs establishes three subfamilies within [[Embiotocidae]] based on anal fin structure and dentition: [[Embiotocinae]] (primitive, most diverse), [[Amphistichinae]] (new subfamily), and [[Micrometrinae]] (new subfamily, specialized herbivores).

2. **Sexual Dimorphism**: Extensive anal fin modifications in males vary by subfamily, representing different evolutionary solutions for reproductive function. [[Micrometrinae]] males uniquely possess more anal rays than females, a condition known in only one other fish genus ([[Cynolebias]]).

3. **Phylogenetic Relationships**: [[Embiotocinae]] appears most primitive, containing greatest generic diversity and species with unusual ranges. [[Micrometrinae]] represents specialized offshoot from [[Embiotocinae]].

4. **Biogeographic Patterns**: Family shows interesting disjunct distribution with two Japanese genera ([[Ditrema temminckii]], [[Neoditrema ransonnetii]]) separated from primarily Californian fauna. Northern range extends to [[Puget Sound]].

5. **Habitat Specialization**: Single freshwater species ([[Hysterocarpus traski]]) regarded as closely related to marine [[Embiotocinae]], not warranting separate subfamily status as previously proposed.

6. **Taxonomic Revisions**: 
   - New genus: [[Amphigonopterus]] (genotype: Abeona aurora)
   - New subgenus: [[Tocichthys]] (subgenotype: [[Hyperprosopon agassizii]])
   - [[Micrometrus]] Gibbons used in place of Abeona Girard
   - Multiple Japanese species ([[Ditrema laeve]], [[Ditrema smitti]], [[Ditrema temmincki jordani]]) considered synonyms of [[Ditrema temminckii]]

7. **Meristic Variation**: Detailed fin-ray counts provided for multiple species, with [[Micrometrus minimus]] showing extraordinarily wide variation (dorsal IX or X, rarely VIII, 12-15 rays; anal 13-18 in females, 20-23 in males from ~90 specimens).

8. **Morphological Specializations**: [[Micrometrinae]] species show scale size reduction in area of male oval depression, herbivorous diet with elongated intestine, and tricuspid teeth unique within family.
