## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary scientific paper** but a regulatory compliance letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), Arizona Ecological Services Field Office, to the U.S. Forest Service, dated October 22, 1993. It addresses non-compliance with a May 7, 1993, [[Biological Opinion]] and associated [[Incidental Take Statement]] governing riparian buffer zones along watercourses within the **Campbell and Isabelle Timber Sales** in the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, Arizona. While no specific fish species is named in the excerpt, the consultation mechanism (ESA Section 7) and the emphasis on protecting watercourse integrity strongly indicate the presence of a federally listed aquatic species — consistent with historic FWS consultations in this region involving species such as [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], or [[Apache Trout]]. The letter underscores that required 132-foot streamside buffers were not being implemented as mandated, constituting a violation of the take statement's terms and conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although species-specific ecological data are absent, the letter provides substantial detail on **habitat protection requirements** relevant to aquatic ecosystem management:

- **Riparian buffer requirement**: A mandated 132-foot buffer on both sides of any watercourse depicted on USGS topographic maps, within which timber harvest, vehicle use, post-harvest activity, and fuel treatment are prohibited except at designated crossings.
- **Function of buffers**: Intended to protect [[Watercourse Integrity]], streambank stability, sediment control, and presumably water quality/thermal regime important to aquatic species survival — consistent with protections typically afforded to sensitive freshwater fish in the [[Little Colorado River]] and [[White Mountains, Arizona]] drainage systems.
- **Non-compliant management units identified**:
  - **CU 18**: Reduced 50–75 ft buffer with light harvest (non-compliant).
  - **CU 19**: No buffer on central drainage; "protected drainage clause" + 50-ft filter strip used instead; harvesting allowed in strip, no skidders.
  - **CU 31**: No buffer — Forest Service claimed no visible drainage.
  - **CU 27**: No buffer — claimed no visible drainage; 50-ft filter strip with harvesting and skid trail crossings allowed.
  - **CU 26**: 50-ft buffer in most of stand; elsewhere only a 50-ft filter strip with harvesting permitted.
  - **CUs 2, 8, 10, 28, 29**: 50-ft filter strip on slopes <15%; 100-ft filter strip on slopes >15%; harvesting allowed within strips (no equipment entry); steep pitches into drainages to be avoided.
- **FWS position**: All listed units contain drainages depicted on USGS topographic maps and therefore fall under the mandatory 132-ft buffer requirement — the reduced filter-strip approach was deemed insufficient to protect aquatic habitat and out of compliance with the [[Endangered Species Act]] Section 7 incidental take terms.

## Key Findings

- The Forest Service's proposed buffer widths (0–100 ft, depending on unit) fell substantially short of the legally required **132-foot buffer** stipulated in the May 7, 1993 biological opinion.
- FWS classified the Forest Service's alternative "protected drainage clause" and reduced filter-strip approach as **non-compliant** with the incidental take statement.
- Compliance with terms and conditions of an incidental take statement is **non-discretionary**; failure to comply constitutes a **violation of Section 9 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]**, carrying potential civil and criminal penalties.
- FWS offered a formal remedy pathway: if the Forest Service possessed new or additional supporting information, it could **reinitiate formal Section 7 consultation** to seek revised terms and conditions via an amended biological opinion.
- The Campbell Timber Sale was reported as largely compliant, including the relocation of an existing road out of a drainage bottom in Stand 19, representing a positive conservation outcome for [[Riparian Zone Management]].
- This correspondence illustrates the broader regulatory framework by which the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] enforces aquatic habitat protections for listed species affected by upland forest management activities (logging, road building, vehicle access) in Southwestern U.S. National Forests.
