# A Preliminary List of Stoneflies (Plecoptera) from the Vicinity of Cultus Lake, British Columbia

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Plecoptera #Aquatic-Entomology #Freshwater-Ecology

> **Note:** This source concerns [[Stoneflies|Plecoptera]] (aquatic insects), not fish. The note has been structured to faithfully reflect the invertebrate and freshwater habitat focus of the original paper, per instructions to synthesize according to actual taxonomic content.

## Executive Summary
William E. Ricker's survey documents the stonefly (Plecoptera) fauna of the [[Cultus Lake]] region in southwestern British Columbia, near the Washington State border. Prior to this study, the entire British Columbia and Washington combined checklist stood at only 60 species (B.C.: 42; combined duplications possible). Ricker's intensive regional survey — the first sustained, locality-focused Plecoptera collection effort in the region — recorded **52 described species**, of which **25 were new records for British Columbia**, plus **8 undescribed species** and roughly **20 unplaced/indeterminate taxa**. The work establishes [[Cultus Lake]] and its associated river/creek systems as a significant biodiversity hotspot for [[Plecoptera]] in the Pacific Northwest coastal rain-forest ecoregion, and catalogs species distribution across a gradient of stream types, elevations, and thermal regimes.

## Background & Description
While the paper is primarily a taxonomic and distributional checklist rather than a morphological treatise, it notes several taxon-specific morphological/phenotypic observations:

- **[[Alloperla Fidelis]]**: The majority of collected specimens of both sexes were **brachypterous** (having reduced/short wings), an adaptation often associated with high-elevation, wind-exposed montane creek habitats.
- Several species (marked with a star, *) had specimens directly **compared against type specimens** by their original describers (e.g., [[Nemoura Dimicki]], [[Nemoura Lobata]], [[Nemoura Oregonensis]], [[Nemoura Producta]], [[Leuctra Glabra]], [[Leuctra Infuscata]], [[Perlomyia Solitaria]], [[Capnia Gracilaria]], [[Hydroperla Parallela]], [[Isoperla Pinta]], and multiple *Alloperla* species), lending taxonomic reliability to the identifications.
- Synonymies were clarified: *[[Nemoura Completa]]* (= *N. glabra* Claassen) and *[[Nemoura Producta]]* (= *N. tuberculifera* Frison).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Ricker classified the region's aquatic habitats into eight discrete stream/creek categories based on size, elevation, water temperature, and substrate stability — a framework central to interpreting species distributions:

1. **Large base-level rivers** near tidal influence (Fraser, Sumas Rivers)
2. **Large cold rivers, low elevation**, boulder-bed, flood-scoured (Chilliwack/Vedder River, Nooksack River above falls)
3. **Medium cold rivers, low elevation**, stable substrate (Lower Liumchin Creek, Frost Creek)
4. **Medium warm rivers, low elevation** (Sweltzer Creek/Cultus Lake outlet; summer temps regularly >21°C vs. ~16°C elsewhere)
5. **Small cold creeks, low elevation** (Hatchery, Reservoir, [[Ascaphus Creek]], Dumvill, Smith Falls, Watt Creeks; Spring Brook)
6. **Medium cold rivers, moderate elevation (~1,000 m)** (Little Liumchin Creek, Canyon Creek, Tamihi Creek)
7. **Small cold creeks, moderate elevation** (Calypso, Cornuta Creeks)
8. **Small cold creeks, high elevation (>1,500 m)** (Saxifrage Creek, Boundary Creek)

Key ecological patterns observed:
- Several species show strong **thermal habitat partitioning** — e.g., *[[Kathroperla Perdita]]* was ubiquitous across all habitat types, while others like *[[Nemoura Cataractae]]* and *[[Capnia Oenone]]* were restricted to single cold, low-order creeks.
- **Sweltzer Creek**, the unusually warm outlet stream of Cultus Lake, hosted dense populations of several species (*[[Pteronarcys Californica]]*, *[[Capnia Gracilaria]]*, *[[Isoperla Pinta]]*, *[[Alloperla Elevata]]*, *[[Alloperla Pacifica]]*) — notable given its atypically high summer temperatures relative to other regional streams.
- **Elevation gradients** strongly structured species assemblages: e.g., *[[Alloperla Fidelis]]* and *[[Alloperla Occidens]]* were confined to high-elevation creeks, while *[[Eucapnopsis Brevicauda]]* and *[[Acroneuria Californica]]* favored low-elevation medium-to-large streams.
- The **Fraser River**, a large base-level system, supported several B.C.-exclusive records (*[[Taenionema Pacifica]]*, *[[Capnia Columbiana]]*, *[[Isogenus]]* cf. *frontalis*), and was flagged by the author as a priority for future collecting alongside moderate-to-high elevation streams.

## Key Findings
- **52 described stonefly species** identified from the Cultus Lake region, nearly matching the entire prior combined B.C./Washington checklist (60 species) in a single localized study area.
- **25 species recorded for British Columbia for the first time**, substantially expanding known provincial Plecoptera diversity.
- **8 undescribed species** and **~20 indeterminate taxa** discovered, indicating the fauna remains incompletely characterized even after intensive sampling.
- Prior published knowledge of B.C./Washington Plecoptera was sparse and fragmentary (Banks' scattered pre-1925 records; Needham & Claassen's 1925 Monograph listing only 27 B.C. + 30 Washington species), underscoring that **no locality had previously been thoroughly sampled** for this insect order.
- Species were organized taxonomically across 9 families: [[Pteronarcidae]], [[Peltoperlidae]], [[Taenioptergidae]], [[Nemouridae]], [[Leuctridae]], [[Capniidae]], [[Perlidae]], [[Perlodidae]], and [[Chloroperlidae]].
- Habitat-based collecting strategy (8 stream-type categories across elevation/thermal gradients) proved effective for capturing regional diversity and is proposed as a template for future faunal surveys.
- The Chilliwack (Vedder) River and Sweltzer Creek were identified as the most heavily sampled and most species-rich sites, while the Fraser River and moderate-to-high elevation streams were flagged as **underexplored and likely to yield further new records**.
- Determinations were cross-validated by leading Plecoptera taxonomists of the era (Dr. P.W. Claassen, Cornell University; Dr. T.H. Frison, Illinois Natural History Survey), lending strong taxonomic credibility to the list.
