# Trash Catcher Fish Stream Dams

#ichthyology #marine-biology #habitat-restoration #stream-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document provides construction specifications and guidelines for "Trash Catcher" dams, simple and inexpensive structures designed to improve fish habitat in streams. These low-profile dams (maximum 20 inches high) use steel posts, hogwire fencing, rocks, and accumulated stream debris to create pools and slow water flow in otherwise unproductive shallow stream sections. The design emphasizes proper installation techniques to prevent bank erosion and maximize habitat value.

## Background & Description

The [[Trash Catcher Dam]] is a habitat improvement structure consisting of:
- Steel fence posts (6-inch posts cut in half) spaced approximately 2 feet apart
- 32-inch heavy duty hogwire (9 gauge top and bottom wires, 11 gauge bar and stay wires, 6-inch spacing; American Fence Products Design 832 Specification #11)
- No. 12 stay wire or fence post clamps for attachment
- Boulders of various sizes for stabilization
- Natural stream debris (leaves, twigs, aquatic plants) for water impoundment

The structure functions as a hogwire fence constructed across the stream channel, with the fence supported by steel posts and weighted by rocks on the upstream apron. The dam must remain relatively low (not exceeding 20 inches in the deepest part of the stream bed) with a level top across the channel to provide maximum ponding above and below the structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Improvement Applications:**
- Targets flat rapids and shallow stream stretches that are typically unproductive fish habitat
- Creates pools by slowing water flow
- Optimizes habitat when positioned to deepen holes around stumps, large boulders, and steep banks
- Functions best when pools receive shade during major portions of the day
- [[Willow]] cuttings recommended for planting where existing shade is limited

**Site Selection Criteria:**
- Shallow stream stretches with flat rapids
- Areas where series of dams can create multiple productive pools
- Locations that allow natural shade or potential for vegetation establishment

## Key Findings

**Construction Specifications:**
1. Posts driven into stream bed and banks with tops even with planned top wire line
2. Hogwire placed on upstream side of posts with small mesh upright against posts and wider mesh extending upstream
3. Hogwire fastened with fence post clamps and stay wire
4. Boulders placed on upstream-extending hogwire for stability
5. In areas with considerable freezing, rocks should be piled almost to hogwire top height to reduce maintenance

**Critical Design Features:**
- Dam ends must extend into banks on both sides to prevent water cutting around structure
- Ends protected by raising hogwire slightly and providing adequate rock riprap
- Stream banks excavated if necessary to allow hogwire to ascend gradually rather than abruptly
- Top of hogwire must be level across stream channel
- Higher elevation at each end prevents bank erosion

**Functional Characteristics:**
- When functioning properly, water over-pour is approximately equal across main channel
- Over-pour diminishes to nothing at banks on each side
- Flash floods typically required to collect sufficient debris for water impoundment
- Debris can be scattered above dam to initiate functioning

**Required Tools:**
Wire cutters, hip boots, crow bars, picks, shovels, sledge hammer, and modified fence post driver (to handle short posts)
