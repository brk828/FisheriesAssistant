## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper reporting morphological or ecological data on a single fish species; rather, it is an administrative memo and project description for the **Second Annual October Fish Count (OFC)**, a volunteer-based, long-term native fish monitoring program organized by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] and the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish. Authored by Dean A. Hendrickson (Native Fish Biologist), the memo outlines the program's rationale, structure, and a call for volunteer participation. The initiative is explicitly modeled after the [[Christmas Bird Count]] run by the Audubon Society, and aims to generate consistent, long-term, multi-site data on native fish populations and stream habitat conditions in the arid Southwest, addressing a critical gap in baseline ecological data needed for conservation, litigation, and habitat management decisions. A related fundraising initiative, the "[[Verde Paddlethon]]," is also described, in which a volunteer canoeist plans to traverse the ~180-mile boatable stretch of the [[Verde River]] to raise pledge-based donations for native fish and riparian conservation projects.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: Freshwater lotic systems (streams and rivers) of the arid Southwestern United States, specifically referenced sites include [[Aravaipa Creek]] (site of an annual training workshop) and the [[Verde River]] (site of the fundraising paddle event).
- **Conservation status**: The source explicitly characterizes regional native fish and their riparian habitats as being in a "present endangered and threatened condition," underscoring the vulnerability of Southwestern freshwater fish communities to habitat degradation.
- **Threats and management context**: Native fish populations face increasing pressure from land/water development projects, prompting greater need for fish and riparian data in **project evaluation, impact assessment, and habitat management** decisions.
- **Data gap noted**: Existing fish community data for the region are described as largely "point-in-time," often limited to simple presence/absence records, lacking the resolution needed for trend analysis or legal/scientific rigor.
- **Riparian linkage**: The program and associated paddlethon fundraiser explicitly link native fish conservation to broader [[Riparian Habitat Conservation]] efforts, recognizing the interdependence of aquatic species and streamside vegetation/habitat structure.

## Key Findings

- The **October Fish Count** began in 1988 as a response to bureaucratic obstacles preventing agency-funded, long-term native fish monitoring programs.
- The inaugural year (1988) involved **over 200 volunteer participants sampling 60 sites**, demonstrating strong public interest and feasibility of a citizen-science approach.
- The program's stated long-term goal is to build a **statistically rigorous, multi-year dataset** capable of detecting population trends in native fish communities, robust enough to support legal proceedings ("litigations") and correlate fish population changes with environmental variables.
- Methodological philosophy: while a single standardized sampling method across all sites is ideal, the program prioritizes **intra-site consistency over time** rather than strict inter-site standardization, given practical constraints on volunteer equipment and expertise.
- The program operates as a **matchmaking network**, connecting volunteers who have equipment/expertise (e.g., backpack electrofishers, seines, permits) with motivated but under-equipped volunteers, coordinated by state agency biologists as intermediaries.
- A **training workshop** for sampling and data recording procedures was scheduled for October 7–8, 1989, at [[Aravaipa Creek]].
- Participant recruitment used a structured **questionnaire** capturing: interest level, type of contribution (identification expertise, sampling expertise, equipment, manpower, transportation), professional status (temporary vs. long-term vs. administrator), and preferred monitoring site.
- The parallel **Verde Paddlethon** initiative (organized by canoeist Jim Slingluff) sought pledge-based donations tied to river mileage paddled, with proceeds directed to the Nongame Branch of AZGFD for native fish, endangered species, and habitat conservation projects.
- The overarching conservation rationale emphasizes that empirical, long-term monitoring data are necessary to move natural resource management away from intuition-based decision-making toward defensible, evidence-based practices, particularly in anticipation of future environmental litigation.
