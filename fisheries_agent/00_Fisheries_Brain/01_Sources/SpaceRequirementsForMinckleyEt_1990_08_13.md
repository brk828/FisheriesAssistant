## Executive Summary

This document is **not a primary biological research paper**, but rather internal Arizona State University administrative correspondence (memoranda, budget forms, and facilities requests) concerning logistical and space requirements for a proposed research project titled **"Gila Taxonomy"**, submitted by W.L. Minckley and colleagues (T.E. Dowling, and a co-investigator listed as "Dougla...") to the **U.S. Bureau of Reclamation**. The proposed research (1990–1994, ~3.5 years, total budget $993,693) aimed to resolve the taxonomy and phylogeny of the freshwater fish genus **[[Gila]]** (family [[Cyprinidae]]) using an integrative multi-disciplinary approach combining **morphology**, **protein electrophoresis (allozymes)**, and **mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)** analysis. While the source contains almost no direct ecological or morphological data on *Gila* itself, it documents the intended research design, personnel structure, and institutional resource needs (laboratory space, storage, staffing) required to execute the taxonomic study.

## Key Findings

- **Research scope**: The proposed project sought to resolve **taxonomy and phylogeny** of the genus *Gila* using three integrated approaches: (1) morphological analysis, (2) protein electrophoresis (allozyme) studies led by co-investigator T.E. Dowling (genetics), and (3) mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) sequencing — reflecting an early (1990) multi-marker approach to fish systematics that presaged later molecular phylogenetics norms in [[Ichthyology]].
- **Funding and scale**: Requested budget totaled **$993,693** ($691,110 direct costs; $302,583 indirect costs) from the **U.S. Bureau of Reclamation**, submitted for a **3.5-year period** (proposed start ~October 1990, projected end 1994).
- **Personnel structure**: Project organized around a coordinator (Minckley) overseeing co-investigators for genetics (Dowling) and additional taxonomic/morphological work, supported by **two postdoctoral associates, an Administrative Assistant, and several technicians** — indicating a substantial team-based approach to resolving *Gila* systematics.
- **Infrastructure constraints**: The Department of Zoology at ASU faced significant space limitations, requiring **600 ft² of new field equipment storage**, **300 ft² of new office space**, and **600 ft² of new wet/dry laboratory space** to support the project's technicians, postdocs, and new analytical equipment (incubator, ultracold freezer, ultracentrifuge, high-speed centrifuge, gel driers).
- **Institutional strategy**: Approval and funding of the *Gila* taxonomy project was explicitly linked by the department chair to broader facility planning, including construction of storage sheds, a chain-link/block wall partition of the Life Sciences compound, and reallocation of renovated lab space (e.g., LS 281) originally intended for an incoming **Ullman Professor** in ecology — illustrating how large extramural awards such as this one shaped internal academic space allocation at ASU circa 1990.
- **Broader taxonomic relevance**: The project reflects historical efforts by W.L. Minckley and collaborators to clarify the systematics of *Gila*, a genus notable for extensive **hybridization, morphological plasticity, and endangered status** among several of its constituent species (e.g., [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Gila Topminnow]]) — an active area of concern for freshwater fish conservation in the arid Southwestern United States.
