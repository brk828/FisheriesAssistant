## Executive Summary

This document is a 1991 consultation letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) to the U.S. Forest Service regarding a proposed recreational fishing reservoir on Pigeon Creek within the [[Blue River Basin]] (Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests, Arizona). The letter does not present primary morphological or field-survey fish data, but is critical for documenting the **conservation status and community composition of native fishes** in one of the last intact native fish assemblages in the [[Gila River Basin]]. The FWS raises concern that the reservoir project could disrupt the basin's largely unaltered hydrograph, threatening a six-species native fish community and several other threatened, endangered, or candidate species. The letter requests basin-wide cumulative impact planning rather than piecemeal, single-site review.

## Background & Description

No direct morphological data (e.g., meristics, body measurements) are provided in this regulatory letter. The document instead identifies taxa of concern by common and scientific name:

- **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — small benthic cyprinid; federally threatened status at time of writing, with proposed critical habitat in the Blue River.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — large-bodied catostomid characterized (in broader literature) by a pronounced dorsal keel/hump behind the head; proposed for endangered listing.
- **[[Roundtail Chub]]** (*Gila robusta*) — medium-sized cyprinid, at the time designated a Category 2 candidate species (species for which listing may be warranted but data were insufficient).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat integrity**: The Blue River watershed is described as being in "relative pristine condition" with a "relatively unaltered hydrograph," which is cited as the key factor enabling persistence of one of the last intact native fish communities in the [[Gila River Basin]].
- **Community comparison**: The Blue River supports **six native fish species** — a richness matched only by the upper [[Verde River]] and exceeded only by the [[Upper Gila River]] (New Mexico) and [[Aravaipa Creek]].
- **Hydrological dependency**: Native fish persistence is explicitly linked to maintenance of natural flow regimes; the letter warns that "any significant perturbation of the hydrograph or aquatic system" could cause losses within this native fish community.
- **Co-occurring sensitive taxa**: The proposed project area may also support other water-dependent and riparian-obligate species of concern, indicating a broader wetland/riparian ecological context tied to fish habitat quality:
  - [[Chiricahua Leopard Frog]] (*Rana chiricahuensis*)
  - [[Lowland Leopard Frog]] (*Rana yavapaiensis*)
  - [[Narrow-Headed Garter Snake]] (*Thamnophis eques*) — a species ecologically dependent on native fish and amphibian prey bases.
  - [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]] (*Empidonax traillii extimus*) — riparian vegetation-dependent.
  - [[Bald Eagle]] and [[Peregrine Falcon]] — piscivorous/avian predators potentially affected by changes in fish community structure.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory status summary of fish species of concern**:
  | Species | Scientific Name | Status (1991) |
  |---|---|---|
  | [[Loach Minnow]] | *Tiaroga cobitis* | Threatened; proposed critical habitat in Blue River |
  | [[Razorback Sucker]] | *Xyrauchen texanus* | Proposed Endangered |

- **Basin significance**: The Blue River's native fish assemblage (6 species) ranks among the richest remaining native fish communities in the [[Gila River Basin]], comparable only to the [[Verde River]] and surpassed only by the [[Upper Gila River]] and [[Aravaipa Creek]].

- **Primary management concern**: FWS calls for a **basin-wide, cumulative-impact planning approach** for reservoir development in the Blue River basin over a 50–100 year horizon, rather than isolated project-by-project review, due to concerns over individual, cumulative, and synergistic hydrological effects on native fish and riparian communities.

- **Data gaps requiring action**: The letter formally requests surveys of native fish and wetland/riparian communities in the project area, and requests supporting documents (Delta Environmental Consultants feasibility study, Pigeon Creek fish survey report, and the Greenlee County–Forest Service Memorandum of Understanding) to enable adequate impact analysis.

- **Regulatory hooks**: Future review of this and similar projects will be governed by the [[Endangered Species Act]], [[Fish and Wildlife Coordination Act]], and Section 404 of the [[Clean Water Act]], underscoring the legal weight of native fish conservation status in reservoir permitting decisions.

- **Unresolved infrastructure-linked risks**: Potential upgrades to Highway 666 and Forest Road 475, and speculative future highway construction along the San Francisco and Blue Rivers, are flagged as indirect but potentially significant threats to aquatic and riparian habitat supporting these fish communities.
