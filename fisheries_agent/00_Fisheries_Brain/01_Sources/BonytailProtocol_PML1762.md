# Bonytail Collection Protocol Memorandum

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1993 internal memorandum, dated 18 June 1993, sets out a draft protocol for collecting [[Bonytail]] from [[Lake Mohave]] for transfer to a hatchery. The author, a park-level fisheries staff member, addresses the timing of collection, fish health screening, hatchery transport, and gear selection. The memo asks recipients to take an active role in planning and to coordinate with the individuals involved. The OCR scan is partly garbled, so some passages are paraphrased with uncertainty.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Memorandum (OCR scan, partly illegible).
- **Date:** 18 June 1993.
- **Subject:** "Bonytail protocol."
- **Recipient:** An associate manager or staff member (name only partly legible in the scan).
- **Author:** Chuck Hinckley, identified as a park staff member (surname and title partly garbled).
- **Target species:** Bonytail, collected from Lake Mohave.

The memo is a planning document. It does not report research data, sample sizes, or measured results.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Setting:** Lake Mohave, a reservoir on the Colorado River system, serves as the source population for the collection.
- **Fish health:** The plan treats fish health as a central concern. Fish health staff are to be involved in deciding how captured fish are screened and treated. The memo suggests that fish found to need treatment should be handled immediately.
- **Holding and transport:** The plan considers holding fish in the lake or in the hatchery before transfer. Captured fish would be moved to the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]].
- **Habitat-related matters:** The memo does not address hatchery production protocols and assumes that concern is secondary.

## Key Findings

1. **Timing and coordination:** The author asks that all parties be consulted well in advance to decide when collection should occur, and whether all parts of the plan should proceed.
2. **Fish health screening:** Fish health personnel should determine the appropriate screening and treatment of captured fish. Fish that need treatment should be treated immediately. The memo asks whether fish should be treated during capture, or after their personnel have been notified.
3. **Hatchery transport:** The hatchery should transport fish either on site during collection or on a schedule that has the hatchery receive them about 24 hours before the planned collection. Whether to hold fish in the lake or in the hatchery is left to the fish health staff and hatchery staff.
4. **Gear selection:**
   - [[Trammel Net]] sets are proposed for capturing bonytail. The memo states that nets should be set for no more than about 6 hours.
   - [[Electrofishing]] is described as the standard method.
   - Gill nets, seines, and larger traps with long leads, and night work, are judged less effective for bonytail. The memo notes that a larger-gear attempt would need separate justification.
5. **Stakeholder involvement:** The author stresses that active participation by all individuals involved in planning and implementation is the most important part of the protocol.
6. **Open items:** The author has not addressed hatchery protocol and assumes the issue is handled elsewhere. Later revisions are to be made as appropriate.
