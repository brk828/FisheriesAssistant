# Nitrogen Limitation in a Sonoran Desert Stream

#ichthyology #marine-biology #periphyton

*Note: This is a limnology and benthic ecology study on periphyton. No fish taxa are addressed, so the fish-specific tags are not applicable; the taxon tag reflects the algal/periphyton focus.*

## Executive Summary

Grimm and Fisher (1986, *Journal of the North American Benthological Society*) tested whether [[Nitrogen Limitation]] constrains periphyton growth in [[Sycamore Creek]], a spatially intermittent Sonoran Desert stream in central Arizona. Four in situ [[Nutrient Enrichment Bioassay]] experiments were run in summer and autumn 1983 using [[Nutrient-Diffusing Substrates]] and drip-enriched [[Artificial Stream Channels]]. Nitrogen enrichment consistently raised chlorophyll *a* accrual, primary production and nutrient uptake, while phosphorus additions had no significant effect. The authors argue that nitrogen limitation is regionally common across southwestern streams, and that it governs the rate, not the ultimate level, of periphyton recovery after [[Flood Disturbance]].

## Background & Description

- **Context:** Unidirectional flow was long thought to prevent nutrient limitation of stream periphyton. Enrichment studies since the 1970s have increasingly shown nitrogen, phosphorus, or both to be limiting, and the [[Redfield Ratio]] (N:P = 16) is used as a benchmark for potential limitation.
- **Study site:** Sycamore Creek, a mid-sized, spatially intermittent desert stream with a mean low-flow N:P of 2.8, indicating potential nitrogen limitation.
- **Hydrology during study:** Discharge was stable at 0.05–0.06 m³/s, with three small flash floods (~1 m³/s, 8, 9 and 13 August) and one large flood (5–8 m³/s, 6 October) that occurred between experiments.
- **Conditions:** Daily maximum water temperatures were 22–27 °C and minima 18–20 °C. Monthly mean solar radiation declined from ~30,000 kJ m⁻² d⁻¹ in midsummer to ~20,000 kJ m⁻² d⁻¹ in autumn. No sites were shaded during the day.
- **Regional survey:** A prior survey of 92 arid and semi-arid southwestern streams (Fisher and Grimm 1983) gave a mean low-flow N:P of 11.4 (SE = 7.1).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Nutrient dynamics:** Desert stream water is often sourced from in-channel springs fed by interstitial reservoirs. These sources are typically nitrate-rich with N:P > 16, yet nitrogen limitation still occurs downstream. The authors suggest phosphorus concentrations are physicochemically buffered (adsorption/desorption equilibria, calcium phosphate solubility control) and are high in volcanic watersheds typical of the region.
- **Regional pattern:** Thut and Haydu (1971) found over half of Washington's surface waters potentially N-limited (N:P < 10), mostly in volcanic regions. Potentially P-limited streams drained glacial or granitic watersheds.
- **Disturbance ecology:** Desert streams are frequently "reset" by flooding, reducing algal standing crops to near zero, and postflood recovery can be lengthy. The authors argue nitrogen supply strongly shapes recolonization kinetics.
- **Community composition:** Diatoms, the chlorophytes *Oedogonium* sp., *Stigeoclonium* sp. and *Cladophora glomerata*, and the cyanobacteria *Calothrix* sp. and *Schizothrix* sp. dominated treatment and stream substrata alike. No clear taxonomic shifts among treatments were detected quantitatively.
- **Grazers:** Snails (*Physa virgata*) were stocked at three densities in experiment 4; grazer effects were inconclusive and are not discussed further.

**Principal results**

- **Experiment 1 (clay pots, N only):** No significant difference at day 7. Significant N-driven chlorophyll *a* differences appeared at day 14 (t = 5.15, 2 df, p < 0.05) and persisted at day 20 (t = 5.47, 10 df, p < 0.001).
- **Experiment 2 (clay pots, factorial):** N enrichment raised chlorophyll *a* strongly (F = 84.08, 3,20 df, p < 0.0001) and net primary production (F = 12.66, 3,20 df, p = 0.0001). Community respiration did not differ among treatments. Phosphorus alone had no effect, and N+P did not differ from N alone.
- **Experiment 3 (channels, factorial):** After 12 d, chlorophyll *a* reached 97.7 and 116.4 mg/m² in +N and +N+P channels, versus 35.2 and 39.4 mg/m² in control and +P channels. Chlorophyll:AFDM ratios were highest in +N+P (4.16 mg/g), intermediate in +N (2.94 mg/g) and +P (2.24 mg/g), and lowest in control (1.36 mg/g). Nitrate-N uptake was ~28–39 mg m⁻² h⁻¹ in N-enriched channels versus ~2.3–2.6 in control and +P channels. SRP uptake did not differ among treatments, which the authors interpret as luxury P uptake by N-limited periphyton. Inferential statistics were not applied because channels were treated as pseudoreplicates (Hurlbert 1984).
- **Experiment 4 (channels, N only):** On day 10 chlorophyll *a* was 20.7 vs. 55.6 mg/m² (control vs. enriched). On day 21 it was 54.5 vs. 113.5 mg/m², and enriched channels had significantly higher gross and net production and respiration. Ammonium-N was taken up preferentially. By day 35, most treatment effects were no longer significant.
- **Threshold concentration:** Nitrogen stimulated periphyton growth at all ambient nitrate-N concentrations tested up to 0.055 mg/L, which the authors adopt as a conservative limiting concentration.
- **Regional extrapolation:** Of 92 southwestern sites, 75 (82%) had nitrate-N < 0.055 mg/L and are presumed N-limited at low flow. Also, 87% had N:P < 16 and 80% had N:P < 10.

**Conclusions and hypotheses**

- Nitrogen, not phosphorus, was the limiting nutrient in Sycamore Creek. No additive effect of P was observed.
- Nitrogen limitation probably controls the **rate** of primary production and chlorophyll *a* accrual, but not the **maximum** steady-state standing crop, which may be set by light, sloughing or grazing.
- Predictions to test: N-limited periphyton should show lower maximal production rates but similar maximum standing crops; enriched communities should retain standing crop after enrichment ends; and recovery after floods should depend on nitrogen supply.
- The model is proposed as applicable to any lotic system, although in heavily shaded streams light limitation would dominate recolonization after disturbance.
