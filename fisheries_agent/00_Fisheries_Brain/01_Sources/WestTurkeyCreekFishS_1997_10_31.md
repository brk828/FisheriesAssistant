## Executive Summary

This report documents a single-day fish survey conducted on **West Turkey Creek** (Chiricahua Mountains, Arizona) on October 31, 1997, using electro-shocking techniques. The survey targeted a series of pools along the creek, from the "Campsite" pool downstream/upstream through "Tributary," "Sycamore Waterfall," "Upper Sycamore Dam," "Lower Sycamore Waterfall," and "Lower Waterfall Base." Two primary species were documented: the native, imperiled **[[Gila Purpurea]]** (Yaqui Chub) and the non-native **[[Pimephales Promelas]]** (Fathead Minnow), with a possible incidental observation of **[[Agosia Chrysogaster]]** (Longfin Dace) fry. Data quality is limited by OCR degradation of the original tabular pool-by-pool records, but aggregate totals and several pool-specific measurements were recoverable.

## Background & Description

- **Gila purpurea** (Yaqui Chub)
  - Total individuals recorded: 19 (16 captured + 3 visual observations)
  - Size range: 33.0 mm – 120.0 mm (total length, presumed)
  - Pool 8 ("Campsite") specimen: male, 68.0 mm TL, captured at water temperature of 10°C
  - A female specimen from the "Sycamore Waterfall" pool was found **with roe** and was manually stripped; this event was associated with the observation of **4–5 fry**, tentatively identified as either *Gila purpurea* or *Agosia chrysogaster*
  - A separate individual from a pool near a tributary confluence was recorded at approximately **30 g** body mass

- **Pimephales promelas** (Fathead Minnow)
  - Total count: 178 individuals across the surveyed reach
  - Subsample of 7 individuals sized/weighed: ranged from **64.0 mm / 2.0 g** to **90.0 mm / 6.5 g**
  - Large capture events noted at individual pools: 36 individuals captured at one pool near "Campsite," and 81 individuals captured at another pool (likely near the "Tributary" pool)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat structure**: The survey reach comprises a discontinuous series of pools separated by waterfalls and a dam feature ("Upper Sycamore Dam"), indicating a steep, structurally complex mountain stream typical of southeastern Arizona sky island drainages.
- **Water temperature**: Recorded at 10°C in the "Campsite" pool, consistent with cool, high-elevation autumn stream conditions.
- **Native vs. non-native co-occurrence**: *Gila purpurea* is a range-restricted, federally recognized species of conservation concern native to the Rio Yaqui basin; its co-occurrence with the non-native *Pimephales promelas* (178 individuals vs. 19 *Gila purpurea*) suggests potential competitive or predatory pressure from an introduced cyprinid in a system supporting a vulnerable endemic fish.
- **Reproductive activity**: Evidence of active spawning/reproduction in *Gila purpurea* was documented via a roe-bearing female and observed fry, indicating the surveyed reach functions as viable spawning and nursery habitat.
- **Longitudinal distribution pattern**: Fish abundance appears concentrated in mid-reach pools (e.g., "Campsite," "Tributary," "Sycamore Waterfall"), while the lowermost pool ("Lower Waterfall Base") yielded **no captures or observations**, possibly indicating a barrier effect from the waterfall/dam features restricting fish movement or reflecting unsuitable habitat at that pool.

## Key Findings

- **Species dominance**: *Pimephales promelas* was numerically dominant (178 individuals) relative to the native *Gila purpurea* (19 individuals) across the surveyed pools, raising concern regarding non-native fish pressure on this Rio Yaqui basin endemic.
- **Size range of Gila purpurea**: 33.0–120.0 mm TL, spanning juvenile to adult size classes, with direct evidence of reproduction (roe, fry observations).
- **Barrier effects**: The presence of waterfalls and a dam (Upper Sycamore Dam, Lower Sycamore Waterfall, Lower Waterfall Base) likely fragments the population distribution, with the terminal downstream pool showing zero fish detections.
- **Data limitations**: Electro-shocking parameters (voltage, pulse rate) were not recorded, and portions of the pool-by-pool data table were unrecoverable due to OCR/scan degradation, limiting full reconstruction of per-pool sex ratios and exact capture counts.
- **Conservation relevance**: Given *Gila purpurea*'s limited range and imperiled status, this survey provides important baseline presence/absence and size-class data for population monitoring in [[West Turkey Creek]] within the broader [[Rio Yaqui Basin]] conservation context.
