# Arizona Endangered Species: Lead Responsibilities for Fish Species

## Executive Summary
This January 20, 1989 document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2) administrative memo assigning **lead office and staff responsibilities** for Arizona's imperiled freshwater fish taxa. Rather than a morphological or ecological research paper, it functions as a **regulatory status roster**, cataloging 21 fish species/subspecies native to Arizona's desert river systems (primarily the [[Colorado River Basin]] and [[Gila River Basin]]), their legal protection status under the Endangered Species Act, and the designated federal office (Phoenix, San Bernardino NWR, Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office, Albuquerque, Fort Worth) and staff contact responsible for each. The list is dominated by small-bodied cyprinids (chubs, minnows, shiners, spinedace), livebearers (topminnows), pupfish, and catostomids (suckers), reflecting the endemic desert fish fauna of the American Southwest, many of which are affected by habitat fragmentation, dewatering, and non-native species introductions.

## Background & Description
The source document does not provide direct morphological descriptions, measurements, or anatomical data for any species. However, taxonomic groupings imply broad morphotypes characteristic of desert fish guilds:

- **Chubs (*Gila* spp.)** — including [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Gila Chub]], and [[Gila Roundtail Chub]] — are cyprinids historically noted for humped nuchal morphology (in *G. cypha*) and generalized fusiform body plans adapted to variable desert river flows.
- **Minnows and spinedace** ([[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], [[Virgin Spinedace]]) are small benthic to midwater cyprinids, some bearing distinctive dorsal spines (spinedace).
- **Pupfish** ([[Desert Pupfish]], [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]]) are small, robust-bodied *Cyprinodon* species adapted to extreme thermal and saline spring habitats.
- **Topminnows** ([[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]]) are small poeciliid livebearers with dorsally-positioned mouths for surface feeding.
- **Squawfish/Pikeminnow** ([[Colorado Squawfish]]) represents the largest-bodied cyprinid in the list, a historically piscivorous apex minnow.
- **Suckers** ([[Razorback Sucker]]) are catostomids noted elsewhere in the literature for a pronounced dorsal keel behind the head — an adaptation to riverine flow.
- **Woundfin** (*Plagopterus argentissimus*) is a small, scaleless cyprinid adapted to swift, silty desert streams.
- **Catfish** ([[Yaqui Catfish]]) is the sole ictalurid listed.

No specific length, weight, coloration, or meristic data are given in this source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While explicit habitat descriptions are absent, the taxa listed collectively occupy the aquatic niches of the arid Southwest:

- **River mainstems and large tributaries**: [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] are all large-river Colorado River Basin endemics associated with historically swift, turbid, warm-water riverine habitats.
- **Smaller perennial streams and headwaters**: [[Gila Chub]], [[Gila Roundtail Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Little Colorado Spinedace]], and [[Virgin Spinedace]] inhabit mid-elevation Gila River Basin and Virgin River tributary systems.
- **Isolated springs and cienegas**: [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], and [[Yaqui Topminnow]] occupy fragmented desert spring and marsh habitats, often in Sonora-adjacent border drainages (reflected by the [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]] lead office assignment).
- **Cold, montane headwaters**: [[Apache Trout]] (*Salmo apache*) is unique among the list as a salmonid, native to high-elevation streams of the White Mountains, Arizona (Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office lead).
- **Mexican border drainages**: [[Sonora Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], and [[Mexican Stoneroller]] indicate shared binational ichthyofauna with Sonora, Mexico, tied to the Yaqui River system.

These niches are broadly threatened by water diversion, groundwater pumping, non-native fish introductions (e.g., centrarchids, ictalurids, poeciliids), and habitat desiccation — recurring themes in [[Southwestern Desert Fish Conservation]].

## Key Findings
- **21 fish taxa** are enumerated with federal Endangered Species Act status as of 1989, spanning **Endangered (E)**, **Threatened (T)**, **Proposed Endangered (PE)**, **Category 2 candidate**, and **Category 3A** designations.
- Several species carry **Critical Habitat (CH)** or **Proposed Critical Habitat (PCH)** designations, including [[Sonora Chub]], [[Virgin River Roundtail Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Woundfin]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], and [[Little Colorado Spinedace]].
- Administrative lead responsibility was concentrated primarily at the **Phoenix (PHX)** office, with secondary leads at **San Bernardino NWR (SBNWR)**, **Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office (PINFAO)**, **Albuquerque (ALB)**, and **Fort Worth (FW)**.
- Regional (Region 2) staff biologists named as contacts include **Stefferud** (lead for the majority of Gila Basin taxa), **Robertson**, **Hanson**, **Baucom**, and **Burton** — reflecting a distributed but Stefferud-centralized case management structure.
- The [[Monkey Springs Pupfish]] is noted with an ambiguous/unusual status code ("3A" and "EX" annotation), possibly indicating a taxon considered possibly extinct or under review for delisting due to extinction — consistent with the historical loss of this Arizona endemic.
- The list illustrates the disproportionate endangerment of desert cyprinids and cyprinodontids in Arizona's Colorado and Gila River systems, foreshadowing decades of subsequent recovery planning under the ESA for taxa such as [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Gila Topminnow]].
- This document serves as a valuable historical snapshot of **species status and agency organization** in Southwestern fish conservation circa 1989, useful for tracing the administrative lineage of later recovery plans.
