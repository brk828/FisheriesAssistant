# Fisheries across America project description

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-biology #freshwater-fishes #colorado-river-basin #data-archiving

## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary morphological or ecological study, but a **conservation infrastructure project proposal** submitted under the Fisheries Across America program. The project's central goal is establishing and maintaining a centralized **archive of scientific literature, unpublished data sets, and information** pertaining to native fishes of the [[Southwestern United States]], with particular emphasis on the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]]. The proposal is spearheaded by [[Arizona State University]], through a partnership between the Department of Zoology and the Special Collections Department at Hayden Library, coordinated by C.O. Minckley (Project Leader, Parker Fisheries Resource Office). The rationale is that rapid research growth on southwestern fish fauna over the preceding decade has produced a large, decentralized body of literature and data — much of it at risk of being lost or overlooked — disproportionately affecting the management and study of federally [[Listed Species]] (i.e., "big river fishes" of the Colorado River system).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While no direct ecological data (habitat parameters, diet, reproduction) is presented, the proposal implicitly defines an ecological/conservation scope:

- **Geographic focus**: [[Lower Colorado River Basin]] native fish fauna.
- **Target taxa**: Native southwestern fishes broadly, with priority given to species with **federal listing status** (endangered/threatened "big river fishes" such as those historically associated with the Colorado River mainstem and tributaries, e.g. [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Bonytail]], and other [[Catostomidae]]/[[Cyprinidae]] taxa typical of this system, though not explicitly named in text).
- **Conservation framing**: The fauna is described as a "unique part of our heritage which is fast disappearing," situating the archive within a broader biodiversity-loss and endangered-species-management context.
- **Long-term scope**: Intended eventual inclusion of information on all native fish species within the lower Colorado River basin, not just currently listed taxa.

## Key Findings

- **Problem identified**: A decade of increased research on southwestern fishes has generated a large volume of published and unpublished data, articles, and data sets that are **not centrally archived**, leading to accessibility gaps for researchers and resource managers.
- **Proposed solution**: Development of a formal **Archive of Southwestern Fishes** at [[Arizona State University]], jointly managed by the Department of Zoology and the Special Collections Department at Hayden Library.
- **Institutional structure**:
  - Project Coordinator: C.O. Minckley (Parker Fisheries Resource Office, Parker, AZ)
  - Host institution: Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ
  - Duration: FY93–FY94
- **Budget/contributions**:
  - Requested external funding: **$2,000.00**
  - In-kind contribution from Department of Zoology (labor): **$1,000.00**
  - In-kind contribution from Special Collections/Hayden Library (labor, equipment, supplies): **$3,000.00**
- **Anticipated benefit**: Improved accessibility of historical and ongoing research data will directly support [[Species Conservation Management]] and research activities for federally listed and other native fishes within the [[Colorado River Basin]], reducing redundancy and information loss in future scientific and regulatory efforts.
- **Significance for the field**: Highlights a recurring challenge in [[Ichthyology]] and [[Conservation Biology]] — the fragmentation of ecological and taxonomic data across unpublished sources — and proposes a scalable institutional model for data stewardship applicable to other imperiled freshwater fish faunas.
