# Lake Havasu Fishery Study: Baseline Fish Community at the Havasu Intake (Central Arizona Project)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #Moronidae #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This 1989 cooperative report by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] documents baseline fishery conditions at the Havasu Intake and Pumping Plant, a feature of the [[Central Arizona Project]], on [[Lake Havasu]]. Fish were sampled quarterly from March 1984 to December 1985 (seven periods) by gill net and electrofishing. The intake area holds all life stages of most Lake Havasu fish species and is fertilized by nutrients from the [[Bill Williams River]]. The report recommends a meeting of the concerned agencies after the [[Granite Reef Aqueduct]] fishery report is finished, to evaluate [[Entrainment]] and set operating criteria.

## Background & Description
- [[Parker Dam]] created Lake Havasu in 1938. The reservoir is about 40 miles (65 km) long and covers 25,000 acres (10,100 ha).
- The pumping plant can lift 2.2 million acre-feet per year about 800 vertical feet into the Granite Reef Aqueduct, which carries the water 189 miles to Phoenix. It has six pump units of 500 cfs each. Trash racks have 6 x 16 inch openings, and the predicted approach velocity is 1.1 ft/s.
- Early planning did not expect the intake to harm the fishery, but concern grew over entrainment and transport of aquatic organisms.
- The intake sits in a cement-lined channel and embayment behind an earth-and-rock dike, inside the Bill Williams Unit of [[Havasu National Wildlife Refuge]].
- Sampling stations were on the outer and inner sides of the dike.
- Gear: experimental gill nets (1000 ft² effort units) set overnight for two nights, plus [[Electrofishing]] at night (Coffelt unit, or the AGFD 15-minute unit).
- Fish were measured for length, weight and K-factor. Scales were taken from largemouth and striped bass.
- Sample periods: spring 84, summer 84, fall 84, winter 85, spring 85, summer 85 and fall 85 (the last on December 4-6, 1985).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- The study ran during high, sustained Colorado River flows. Mean monthly Hoover Dam release was 11,460 cfs for 1977-1982 versus 25,930 cfs for 1983-1985, and flood spillway releases occurred in 1983.
- Only four periods were sampled before the plant began sporadic operation in March 1985, so the baseline is difficult to establish.
- A concurrent limnology study ([[Roline And Lieberman Limnology Study]]) found similar conditions at the inner and outer stations. Surface temperature differed by 3.1 °F at most and dissolved oxygen by 0.9 mg/L at most. Secchi readings were 89% and 50% lower at the outer station on two occasions.
- The embayment is an important zooplankton (cladoceran) production area. Lake Havasu was more productive than Apache, Roosevelt, Canyon and Saguaro Lakes.
- Refuge birds that feed on young-of-year fish include the western grebe, American white pelican, double-crested cormorant and black-crowned night-heron.

## Key Findings
- **Composition:** Green sunfish (34.3%), threadfin shad (21.6%) and bluegill (21.2%) made up 77.1% of the catch. The rest were carp (9.0%), striped bass (5.9%), channel catfish (2.9%), largemouth bass (2.7%), black crappie (0.9%), flathead catfish (0.4%) and redear sunfish (0.2%).
- **Year-to-year change (1984 vs 1985):** Largemouth bass, striped bass, threadfin shad and flathead catfish increased in relative abundance; the other six species decreased.
- **CPUE:** Lakewide AGFD electrofishing CPUE (82.7 fish/15 min) was comparable to this study. Values at Havasu were much higher than those reported for [[Lake Mohave]] (e.g., green sunfish 58.04 vs 1.85; threadfin shad 28.00 vs 2.57).
- **Striped bass:** The highest catch was in fall 85 by gill net. Of 27 stomachs examined, all but one contained food, and 25 held [[Threadfin Shad]]. Mean stomach volume occupied was 67%. Fork length to total length: y = 5.99 + 0.91x, r² = 0.94.
- **Threadfin shad and striped bass:** The highest threadfin shad gill-net CPUE and the largest striped bass CPUE both occurred in fall 85.
- **Outer vs inner dike:**
  - The same species occurred on both sides (p > 0.10).
  - Significantly more fish were caught on the inner side (paired t-test, t = 3.191, p between 0.01 and 0.02).
  - Biomass was greater on the outer side (t = 2.637, significant), but the Wilcoxon test was not significant. Five of seven periods had more biomass outside.
  - The inner embayment appears to favor smaller individuals.
- **Habitat use:** The area supports adult and juvenile habitat for 10 species and young-of-year habitat for at least 8.
- **Razorback sucker:** None were found at the intake, though AGFD collected an adult at Black Rock Cove in 1984. Species at the intake closely matched those later collected in the Granite Reef Aqueduct, except that flathead catfish were absent from the aqueduct.
- **Entrainment risk:**
  - Bluegill, green sunfish and threadfin shad peaked in October 1984 and late September 1985.
  - Cyclic pump operation may entrain more small fish than continuous pumping.
  - One hypothesis is minimal impact because of low intake velocities. The other is that many eggs, larvae and young-of-year fish would be lost, with greater significance in the protected refuge.
- **Recommendations:**
  - Review the Granite Reef Aqueduct fishery report (a four-year Reclamation study with two years of egg and larval work) and convene AGFD, the Central Arizona Water Conservation District, Reclamation and the Service.
  - Set operating criteria, decide on further monitoring and sport-fishing concerns, and consider the fate of Bill Williams River nutrients. Include nutrient data in any future monitoring.
