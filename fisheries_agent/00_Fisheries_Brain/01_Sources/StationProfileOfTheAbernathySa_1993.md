## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the operations, research, and production outputs of the **[[Abernathy Salmon Culture Technology Center]]**, a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1959 under the Mitchell Act to support restoration of **[[Columbia River]]** salmon and steelhead runs. The Center's dual mandate encompasses production-scale **[[Fall Chinook Salmon]]** propagation and applied research into hatchery technology, fish nutrition, fish health, and rearing density optimization. During FY1993, the Center produced over 1.1 million fall chinook eggs and released nearly 980,000 smolts, while conducting parallel research on feed formulation (feather meal substitution), diagnostic assays for **[[Bacterial Kidney Disease]]** (BKD), fungal water-quality monitoring, immunomodulator feeding trials, and rearing density experiments with both **[[Chinook Salmon]]** and newly introduced **[[White Sturgeon]]** larvae.

## Background & Description

While the source is primarily an operational/administrative station profile rather than a morphological study, several fish-health and physical production metrics are reported:

- **Fall Chinook Smolts (1993 release cohort):** 979,184 individuals, total biomass 26,119 lbs (~26.6 g average weight per fish).
- **Egg/Fry Production (1992-brood):** 1,158,209 eggs spawned; 8.2% mortality during incubation, yielding 1,063,401 fry at start of rearing season.
- **Fingerling/Smolt Mortality:** 8.9% total mortality during fingerling rearing phase.
- **Health Status:** No overt bacterial kidney disease detected in routine examinations; a prerelease sample of 200 fish showed 2 individuals (1%) with low-level BKD titers, indicating subclinical infection prevalence.
- **Growth Response to Diet:** Inclusion of feather meal up to 20% of the **[[Abernathy Diet]]** produced no significant deviation in juvenile chinook growth rates compared to controls.
- Larval **[[White Sturgeon]]** were newly introduced into experimental circular tanks (12°C well water) for growth/survival trials — no morphometric data yet reported for this cohort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species reared:** Primarily **[[Fall Chinook Salmon]]** (anadromous, Columbia River origin stock), with concurrent research programs involving **[[Spring Chinook Salmon]]**, **[[Coho Salmon]]**, **[[White Sturgeon]]**, and (via collaborative aquaculture research) **[[Nile Tilapia]]** (*Oreochromis niloticus*).
- **Habitat/Infrastructure:** Fish are cultured in a hybrid freshwater system combining diverted creek water (via intake screens, sand/silt settling basin), two deep wells, 12 raceways, and a water recycle system — mimicking natural anadromous life-stage progression from egg incubation through smolt release into the Columbia River system for outmigration to the Pacific.
- **Water Quality Interactions:** Fungal zoospore assays revealed *Saprolegnia* present at low densities in creek water and absent as aseptate fungi in well water; however, fungal densities increased substantially within the closed water recycle system as the rearing season progressed, highlighting a density-dependent pathogen risk in intensive recirculating aquaculture.
- **Density-Dependent Ecology:** Multiple experiments examined the relationship between hatchery rearing density and post-release survival/production:
  - Raceways with 20,000 **[[Spring Chinook Salmon]]** juveniles yielded equivalent total adult return numbers as raceways stocked with 60,000 smolts — indicating a density threshold beyond which marginal returns diminish.
  - New trials (6,700–20,000 fish/raceway) are testing whether even lower densities improve per-capita adult contribution, combined with prophylactic **[[Erythromycin]]** feeding.
  - At **[[Spring Creek Hatchery]]**, "Tule" fall chinook reared at very high density (364,000 fish per 20'×80' raceway) showed reduced post-release survival rates but higher *total* adult contribution due to sheer numbers released — illustrating a survival-vs-yield tradeoff central to hatchery ecological management.
  - Reduced-density fish exhibited faster individual growth rates than fish reared in large populations, consistent with intraspecific competition for food/space in raceway environments.

## Key Findings

- **Production Metrics (FY1993):** 979,184 fall chinook smolts released (26,119 lbs) into Washington state waters under Mitchell Act authority; historical distribution data show annual releases in the range of ~1.4–1.8 million fish in preceding years (1990–1992), indicating 1993 release volume was comparatively lower.
- **Feed Research:** Feather meal is a viable partial substitute in the [[Abernathy Diet]] without compromising juvenile chinook growth, though both feather meal and fish meal showed cross-reactivity in **[[ELISA]]**-based BKD screening — raising concern about false-positive diagnostic results from intestinal content contamination.
- **Pathogen Monitoring:** A novel fungal zoospore assay (modified from Celio and Padget procedure) was developed for water supply monitoring, revealing that recirculating water systems harbor elevated fungal loads relative to source creek/well water.
- **Immunomodulator Trial:** Oral administration of **[[Tetraselmis]]** and **[[Levucell]]** immunomodulators over a two-month trial was palatable to fall chinook fingerlings with no adverse growth effects, supporting their potential as prophylactic health interventions.
- **Density Optimization:** Findings across multiple hatchery systems consistently show a nonlinear relationship between stocking density, individual growth, survival post-release, and total adult return — informing ongoing efforts to optimize "Total Adult Yield" (a key hatchery management metric) rather than simply maximizing per-fish survival.
- **Diversification of Research Scope:** Initiation of **[[White Sturgeon]]** larval rearing trials (photoperiod, light level, density, flow effects) signals programmatic expansion beyond salmonids into other anadromous/riverine species of conservation interest.
- **Publication Output:** Center research contributed to peer-reviewed literature (e.g., Banks 1992, *The Progressive Fish-Culturist*) and international aquaculture collaboration reporting (Gannam & Phillips 1992, CRSP Administrative Report on *Oreochromis niloticus* thermal growth response).
- **Public Engagement:** The Center served an educational/outreach role, hosting ~2,060 visitors in FY1993, including ~1,000 students exposed to hatchery operations and salmon spawning demonstrations.
