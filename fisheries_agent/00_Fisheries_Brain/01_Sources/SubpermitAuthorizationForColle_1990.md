## Executive Summary
This document is a 1990 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) subpermit authorization letter granting Dr. Anthony A. Echelle (Oklahoma State University) permission to collect two federally protected pupfish species — the [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon bovinus*) and the [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon elegans*) — from their native Texas spring habitats. The permit authorizes limited, non-lethal and lethal collection for genetic/evolutionary research, including mitochondrial DNA analysis to be conducted at Arizona State University in collaboration with Drs. W.L. Minckley and Thomas E. Dowling. While not a primary scientific paper, the letter contains valuable regulatory and ecological metadata regarding species distribution, permitted collection methods, and conservation oversight structure.

## Background & Description
- **Permit Holder:** Dr. Anthony A. Echelle, Professor, Zoology Department, Oklahoma State University.
- **Authorizing Body:** U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Region 2 (Albuquerque, NM), acting under the Regional Director's blanket Endangered Species Permit **PRT-676811**.
- **Subpermit Validity:** Through December 1990; annual renewal report due December 1, 1990.
- **Species Covered:**
  - [[Leon Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon bovinus*) — collected from **Leon Creek, Pecos County, Texas**.
  - [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon elegans*) — collected from the **Toyah Creek drainage, Reeves and Jeff Davis Counties, Texas**.
- **Collection Methods:** Minnow trap and small seine; capped at **100 adults per species**.
- **Collaborating Institutions:**
  - Oklahoma State University (Zoology Department) — primary specimen repository.
  - Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ — recipient of 10 live specimens of each species for mitochondrial DNA studies (Drs. W.L. Minckley and Thomas E. Dowling).
- **Downstream Handling Protocol:**
  - 10 individuals of each species transported alive to Arizona State University, held ~2 weeks in aquaria, then sacrificed for biochemical analysis.
  - Remaining specimens (~80 per species) flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen in the field for biochemical/evolutionary analyses of the genus *[[Cyprinodon]]*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both species are restricted **desert spring endemics** in the Chihuahuan Desert region of West Texas, representative of the broader [[Pupfish]] radiation adapted to isolated, thermally stable spring systems.
- **Leon Creek** (Pecos County) and the **Toyah Creek drainage** (Reeves/Jeff Davis Counties) represent critically limited, spring-fed aquatic habitats historically vulnerable to groundwater depletion, agricultural water withdrawal, and habitat fragmentation — key drivers behind the endangered status of both species.
- These habitats are part of a broader network of isolated desert springs (e.g., Comanche Springs, San Solomon Springs) supporting relict, geographically restricted [[Cyprinodontidae]] populations, echoing conservation dynamics seen in other desert fish taxa (e.g., [[Gila Chub]], [[Devils Hole Pupfish]]).
- The permit explicitly ties collection activities to captive-holding infrastructure at the **Dexter National Fish Hatchery** (New Mexico), a key federal refugium facility historically used for endangered desert fish propagation and genetic banking.
- Regulatory oversight involved coordination across multiple agencies: FWS Region 2, Oklahoma Department of Wildlife Conservation, Texas Parks & Wildlife Department, and the FWS Office of Management Authority — reflecting the multi-jurisdictional conservation status of these species under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.

## Key Findings
- **Population sampling cap:** Collection limited to 100 adult individuals per species, reflecting conservation sensitivity and small effective population sizes of both pupfish species.
- **Genetic research objective:** Specimens were destined for **mitochondrial DNA analysis** to clarify **evolutionary relationships within the genus *Cyprinodon***, indicating active taxonomic/phylogenetic research interest in desert pupfish lineages circa 1990.
- **Chain-of-custody requirements:** Strict federal protocols governed live transport, temporary captivity, sacrifice, and disposal of specimens — underscoring the legal sensitivity of handling Endangered Species Act-listed taxa.
- **Multi-institutional collaboration:** The permit formalized a research partnership between Oklahoma State University (Echelle) and Arizona State University (Minckley, Dowling), representative of coordinated academic efforts to study endangered desert fish genetics.
- **Habitat specificity:** Confirms precise historical collection localities for both species — Leon Creek for *C. bovinus* and the Toyah Creek drainage for *C. elegans* — valuable baseline geographic/distributional data for long-term monitoring of these endangered desert spring fish.
- **No release authorization:** All live wildlife handling required prior FWS written approval before any release or disposal, highlighting strict biosecurity and conservation control measures typical of Endangered Species Permit frameworks.
