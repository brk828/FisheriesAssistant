# Analysis of Past Collections from the Green River for Presence of Young Razorback Sucker or Humpback Chub

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #ColoradoRiverBasin

## Executive Summary

This FY93 report (Colorado River Recovery Program, Project 18-6) describes an ongoing archival specimen-analysis study led by the [[Larval Fish Laboratory]] (Colorado State University) aimed at determining whether young [[Razorback Sucker]] or [[Humpback Chub]] were present in historic ichthyoplankton and juvenile fish collections from the [[Green River]] system (Utah), primarily 1986–1992. Rather than new field sampling, the study re-examines ~500 sucker and ~300 chub specimens already housed in existing collections, applying newly developed diagnostic morphological criteria to resolve taxonomic identities previously listed only as "tentative," "possible," or generic-level (*Gila* sp.). The work supports the broader [[5-Year Flaming Gorge Flow Investigations Program]] and reduces the need for additional invasive field sampling of endangered fishes, addressing a stated Recovery Program concern about sampling impacts on already-imperiled populations. As of the report date, the study was incomplete (delayed start due to contracting issues), with meristic data compiled but full morphometric/diagnostic analyses (soft-tissue clearing, skeletal staining) still pending.

## Background & Description

The study does not present new morphometric findings for identified specimens (analysis incomplete) but outlines the diagnostic methodology to be applied:

- **Sucker specimens (Catostomidae):**
  - Diagnostic separation of young [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[White Sucker]] larvae/early juveniles relies on:
    - Size and shape of **interneural bones**
    - Size and shape of the **frontoparietal fontanelle**
  - Methods require **soft-tissue clearing and skeletal staining** techniques.
  - Diagnostic framework based on Snyder & Muth (1990), *Descriptions and identification of razorback, flannelmouth, white, Utah, bluehead, and mountain sucker larvae and early juveniles* (CDOW Technical Publication No. 38).

- **Chub specimens (Cyprinidae, genus *Gila*):**
  - Separation of young [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail]], and [[Roundtail Chub]] relies on **morphometric analyses** (body proportions/shape).
  - Diagnostic framework based on Muth (1990) Ph.D. dissertation, *Ontogeny and taxonomy of humpback chub, bonytail, and roundtail chub larvae and early juveniles* (Colorado State University).
  - Prior to these criteria, field/lab identifications of young *Gila* were often left unresolved at the genus level due to lack of reliable distinguishing characters.

- **Preliminary data:** Meristic data (primarily **fin-ray counts**) have been compiled for all pulled specimens; analysis of these counts is ongoing as a first screening step before selecting candidates for detailed morphometric/skeletal examination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat/Location:** Specimens were collected predominantly from the **middle and lower reaches of the Green River, Utah**, during **summer–fall periods across 1986–1992**.
- **Life stages targeted:** Late larvae and early juveniles — critical early life-history stages linked to recruitment success.
- **Relevance to flow management:** The study is tied to Recovery Program RIPRAP actions concerning instream flow provision and habitat management on the Green River above and below the [[Duchesne River]], including operations of [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] to provide biologically appropriate summer, fall, winter, and spring flows.
- **Habitat restoration linkage:** Connected to monitoring efforts at [[Old Charlie Wash]], a restoration/habitat development site relevant to recruitment of these species.
- **Conservation context:** Both target species are federally endangered fishes of the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]; understanding their reproductive and recruitment success under varying flow/temperature regimes is central to recovery planning. The project also explicitly seeks to identify additional [[Razorback Sucker]] spawning areas in the lower Green River.

## Key Findings

- **Study status (as of report date):** Incomplete — work began late (July 1993) due to contract finalization delays; completion projected for **March 1994**.
- **Specimens processed:** ~500 sucker specimens and ~300 chub specimens pulled from existing LFL collections for analysis.
- **Data compiled:** Meristic (fin-ray count) data collected on all pulled specimens; statistical analysis ongoing.
- **Pending work:** 
  - Selection of candidate razorback sucker/humpback chub specimens based on meristic screening.
  - Skeletal staining and soft-tissue clearing for sucker specimens (interneural bone/fontanelle analysis).
  - Morphometric analysis for chub specimens.
  - If positive identifications are made, capture/environmental data will be requested from original collectors and cross-referenced with flow/temperature records.
- **Budget:** Of $5,000 allocated for FY93, only $2,000 was expended, with the $3,000 shortfall attributed directly to project incompletion.
- **Rationale/Significance:** The approach demonstrates a **sampling-reduction strategy** for endangered fish research — leveraging existing archival collections rather than new field collection efforts — directly addressing Recovery Program concerns about the adverse effects of sampling on endangered fish populations.
- **No recommendations** were issued in this interim report; final synthesis was intended for inclusion in the 5-Year Flaming Gorge Flow Investigations Program final report.
