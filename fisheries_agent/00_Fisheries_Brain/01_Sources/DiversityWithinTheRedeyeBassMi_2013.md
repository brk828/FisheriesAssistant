# Diversity within the Redeye Bass, Micropterus coosae (Perciformes: Centrarchidae) species group, with descriptions of four new species

#ichthyology #Centrarchidae #black_bass #taxonomy

## Species Profile & Ecological Context

**Taxonomic & Life-History Summary:** This study revises the [[Redeye Bass]] (*Micropterus coosae*) species complex, originally described by Hubbs and Bailey (1940) from the Mobile River basin, Chattahoochee, and Savannah rivers. Examination of morphological variation and mitochondrial DNA (ND2 gene) across 182 specimens revealed four new species: *M. cahabae* (Cahaba Bass), *M. tallapoosae* (Tallapoosa Bass), *M. warriorensis* (Warrior Bass), and *M. chattahoochae* (Chattahoochee Bass). *M. coosae* sensu stricto is now restricted to the Coosa River system. All species are small-bodied basses attaining 360–406 mm SL and 0.7–1.8 kg. Age, maturity, fecundity, and growth parameters not reported. Meristic characters include: anterior dorsal-fin spines 9, posterior dorsal-fin spines 1, anal-fin spines 3, pectoral-fin rays 14–17 (mode 15), posterior dorsal-fin rays 11–14 (mode 12), anal-fin rays 10–11 (mode 10), caudal-fin rays 17; pyloric caeca unbranched. Species differ in lateral-line scale counts (61–74, modes vary by species), scales around caudal peduncle (26–32, modes vary), scale width as percentage of standard length (2.2–3.2%, means 2.5–2.9%), postfrontal head width (10.5–12.2% SL), and tongue tooth patch size (0–6.0 mm, often absent in *M. warriorensis*).

**Habitat Associations:** All members of the *M. coosae* species group are endemic to upland streams in the Piedmont region of Alabama and Georgia. Each species is restricted to a single drainage system: *M. coosae* (Coosa River), *M. cahabae* (Cahaba River), *M. tallapoosae* (Tallapoosa River), *M. warriorensis* (Black Warrior River), and *M. chattahoochae* (Chattahoochee River). Species occupy rocky pools and upland stream habitats above the fall line. Physiographic restriction to upland habitats has limited gene flow among populations separated by larger lowland rivers. *M. warriorensis* is scarce or absent from tributaries in central and northern portions of the Black Warrior drainage, likely due to flat, shallow streambeds lacking deep pools that serve as refugia during low water or drought.

## Behavioral & Ecological Findings

**Movement Patterns & Migration:** Not reported.

**Trophic & Community Interactions:** Morphologically intermediate hybrids between *M. coosae* species group members and the sympatric [[Alabama Spotted Bass]] (*M. henshalli*) were observed at several localities. Hybrids are recognizable by white caudal fin margins shorter and smaller than typical for the *M. coosae* species group, with intermediate scale counts, eye crescent development, and caudal-fin shape. Hybrids were particularly common in the Chattahoochee River, which now contains *M. henshalli*, *M. punctulatus*, *M. cataractae*, *M. chattahoochae*, and *M. salmoides* via introductions and natural ranges; hybrids of every combination have been identified.

**Environmental Drivers:** Species distributions reflect physiographic barriers and drainage divides. Each species is allopatric to others, isolated in upland stream reaches by larger mainstem habitats or drainage boundaries. No intermediate forms suggestive of contact or intergrade zones were observed between any of the *M. coosae* species. Historical headwater drainage connections or past widespread distributions may explain close relationships among species from the Cahaba, Coosa, and Black Warrior rivers, and between *M. tallapoosae* and the *M. coosae*/*M. cahabae* clade.

## Conservation Status & Population Dynamics

**Population Trajectory:** *M. chattahoochae* has rapidly declined due to habitat degradation. Recent surveys suggest significant distributional decline for *M. warriorensis*. *M. coosae* and *M. tallapoosae* are relatively common in upland streams within their ranges. Population trends for *M. cahabae* not explicitly reported but species is uncommon.

**Threat & Risk Assessment:** *M. warriorensis*, *M. cahabae*, and *M. chattahoochae* are uncommon and may warrant protection. Primary threats include: (1) habitat degradation, (2) introduction of non-native bass species, and (3) hybridization with introduced species. The distinctive population in the Savannah River (*M. cf. coosae*) is in danger of extirpation via hybridization with *M. henshalli*, which was introduced to enhance sport fisheries. Hybridization is a conservation concern when uncommon species are involved due to potential for extirpation of native species via genetic swamping. Stocking of nonindigenous bass species should be discontinued to prevent further decline. *M. chattahoochae* may be affected by introductions of multiple bass species into the Chattahoochee River system.

## Management Guidance & Policy Takeaways

**Actionable Recommendations:** (1) Conduct comprehensive surveys for *M. warriorensis* throughout its range to assess current distribution and abundance. (2) Implement conservation measures to ensure persistence of the Warrior Bass. (3) Discontinue stocking of nonindigenous bass species in all drainages containing endemic *M. coosae* species group members to prevent further decline via hybridization. (4) Protect upland stream habitats in the Piedmont region from degradation. (5) Recognize *M. cahabae*, *M. tallapoosae*, *M. warriorensis*, and *M. chattahoochae* as distinct species requiring separate management and conservation strategies. (6) Consider formal protection status for *M. warriorensis*, *M. cahabae*, and *M. chattahoochae* given their uncommon status and restricted ranges.
