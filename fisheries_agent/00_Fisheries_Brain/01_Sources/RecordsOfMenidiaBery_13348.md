# Records of *Menidia beryllina* from Several Central Texas Impoundments

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Atherinopsidae

## Executive Summary

This short faunal note documents the unexpected inland range extension of the **tidewater silverside** ([[Menidia beryllina]]), a species typically associated with coastal and brackish waters of Texas, into freshwater reservoirs of the Colorado River system in Central Texas. The authors report substantial populations in **Lake Inks, Lake Buchanan, and Marble Falls Lake** based on 1963 survey work, building on an earlier isolated record from **Lake Brownwood** (1959) and a single specimen from the Colorado River near La Grange, Texas (Tilton, 1961). The paper hypothesizes an anthropogenic or hydrological introduction pathway from coastal populations into these inland impoundments, either directly or via upstream reservoir stocking/connectivity.

## Background & Description

- No new morphological description is provided in this note; species identity was confirmed via expert consultation (Dr. Clark Hubbs) rather than original taxonomic redescription.
- The paper relies on established species diagnosis from [[Hubbs 1957]] (*Distributional Patterns of Texas Freshwater Fishes*) for confirming identification of *M. beryllina* specimens.
- Referenced as a small-bodied [[Atherinopsidae|silverside]] species, consistent with the genus *Menidia*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native/typical habitat**: Coastal and estuarine brackish waters of the Texas Gulf Coast region ([[Gulf of Mexico]] drainage).
- **Documented range extension**:
  - ~150 miles upstream in the **Rio Grande River** (Robinson, 1959)
  - **Colorado River** near La Grange, Texas (single specimen, Tilton 1961)
  - **Lake Brownwood**, Brown County, Texas (1959; Campbell, Dingell-Johnson Report F-5-R-7)
  - **Lakes Inks, Buchanan, and Marble Falls** — Colorado River impoundments, Central Texas (1963 survey; substantial populations)
- **Competitive interaction**: At Lake Brownwood, *M. beryllina* was reported as **more abundant** than the previously dominant native cyprinid, [[Notropis lutrensis]] (red shiner), suggesting possible competitive displacement or niche overlap in reservoir habitats.
- **Habitat plasticity**: The species demonstrates notable euryhaline tolerance, successfully establishing populations in freshwater impoundments despite typical association with brackish/coastal systems — a trait relevant to its invasive potential.
- **Introduction pathway hypothesis**: Authors propose two possible mechanisms:
  1. Direct introduction from coastal areas into the three lakes (Inks, Buchanan, Marble Falls)
  2. Indirect introduction via upstream reservoirs (e.g., Lake Brownwood) with subsequent downstream/connected dispersal

## Key Findings

- First documented substantial, self-sustaining populations of *Menidia beryllina* in three Central Texas Colorado River impoundments (Lakes Inks, Buchanan, Marble Falls), based on 1963 survey data.
- Establishes a **chronological record** of inland range expansion: Rio Grande (1959) → Colorado River single specimen (1961) → Lake Brownwood (1959 report) → Lakes Inks/Buchanan/Marble Falls (1963).
- Absence of this species in earlier ichthyofaunal collections of these lakes supports an **introduction hypothesis** rather than a previously undetected native population.
- Ecological displacement noted: *M. beryllina* surpassed [[Notropis lutrensis]] in abundance at Lake Brownwood, indicating potential impacts on native cyprinid communities in invaded reservoirs.
- This note serves as an early example of documenting [[species range expansion]] and potential [[freshwater fish introductions]] of a coastal/brackish species into inland Texas reservoir systems, with implications for regional fish community composition.
