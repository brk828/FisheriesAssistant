# California Trout, Salmon, and Warmwater Fish Production and Costs, 1966-67

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #fisheries-management #hatchery-operations

## Executive Summary

This administrative report documents record-breaking fish production from [[California Department of Fish and Game]] hatcheries during fiscal year 1966-67. Total production reached 52,167,408 fish weighing 2,380,115 pounds, representing the highest weight produced in thirteen years despite two hatcheries being offline. The average size of all fish planted increased from 27.7 per pound in 1965-66 to 21.5 per pound in 1966-67. Total operational costs for Department-financed hatcheries reached $2,256,489, with an average cost of $1.05 per pound. The greatest production increase occurred in [[Steelhead Trout]] from mitigation hatcheries (168,461 pounds vs. 57,051 pounds in 1965-66), while [[King Salmon]] production decreased by approximately 25,000 pounds.

## Background & Description

The report represents the fourteenth annual hatchery cost and production summary from California's Department of Fish and Game [[Inland Fisheries Branch]]. Production occurred across multiple regional facilities including [[Darrah Springs Hatchery]] (highest producer at 457,847 pounds), [[San Joaquin Hatchery]] (320,401 pounds), [[Moccasin Creek Hatchery]] (208,682 pounds), and others. The system included both state-financed and "mitigation" hatcheries operated by the Department but financed by water development agencies ([[Iron Gate Hatchery]], [[Trinity River Hatchery]], [[Nimbus Hatchery]], [[Mokelumne River Hatchery]]).

Fish production was categorized into three major groups: resident fish (22,831,253 fish weighing 2,071,011 pounds), anadromous fish (28,560,220 fish weighing 296,294 pounds), and warmwater fish (775,935 fish weighing 12,810 pounds). Resident fish production included catchable-sized trout (7,343,288 fish weighing 1,742,180 pounds averaging 4.2 per pound), subcatchable-sized trout, and fingerlings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The 1966-67 production year was affected by significant environmental challenges. [[Kern River Hatchery]] was lost to flood damage in December 1966, and [[Fillmore Hatchery]] was closed for modernization to increase capacity for 1967-68. Despite these setbacks, record poundage was achieved through strategic management decisions including holding fish longer to increase average size.

Anadromous fish production fluctuated based on preceding adult runs, with [[Trinity River Hatchery]] producing excellent yearling to two-year-old [[Steelhead Trout]]. Adult salmon runs at both Trinity River and Nimbus hatcheries in 1965-66 were smaller, resulting in reduced [[King Salmon]] production in 1966-67 (25,456,381 fish weighing 34,087 pounds vs. 26,784,287 fish weighing 59,045 pounds in 1965-66).

Greater use of subcatchable-sized trout in lakes and reservoirs led to increased production of this size class (179,241 pounds in 1966-67 vs. 147,313 pounds in 1965-66). Space availability at [[Darrah Springs Hatchery]] late in the season enabled holding advanced fingerling [[Eagle Lake Trout]] to larger sizes before planting.

## Key Findings

**Production Records:**
- Total production: 52,167,408 fish weighing 2,380,115 pounds (103,220 pounds more than 1965-66)
- Record weight achieved despite 10,250,412 fewer fish produced than previous year
- Average fish size increased from 27.7 per pound (1965-66) to 21.5 per pound (1966-67)

**Resident Fish Production:**
- Total: 22,831,253 fish weighing 2,071,011 pounds
- Catchable-sized trout: 7,343,288 fish (1,742,180 pounds) averaging 4.2 per pound
- Subcatchable production increased to 179,241 pounds from 147,313 pounds
- Fingerling production: 101,431 pounds (13,672,020 fish)

**Anadromous Fish Production:**
- Total: 28,560,220 fish weighing 296,294 pounds (100,000+ pounds increase over 1965-66)
- [[Steelhead Trout]]: 168,461 pounds (greatest weight increase), including 101,870 pounds of yearlings
- [[King Salmon]]: 25,456,381 fish (34,087 pounds), down ~25,000 pounds from previous year
- [[Silver Salmon]]: 1,747,288 fish (93,746 pounds) with average size 18.6 per pound vs. 39.6 per pound in 1965-66

**Warmwater Fish Production:**
- Total: 775,935 fish weighing 12,810 pounds (increased from 3,676 pounds in 1965-66)
- [[Channel Catfish]]: 2,452 pounds (up from 1,519 pounds)
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: 7,691 pounds (up from 1,466 pounds)
- Average size: 60.57 per pound (97% fingerling-sized)
- Production increase partly due to record-keeping changes including procurement from reservoirs

**Cost Analysis:**
- Total operational cost: $2,256,489 (increase of $96,689 over 1965-66)
- Average cost per pound: $1.05 (vs. $1.00 in 1965-66, $1.10 in 1964-65)
- Feed costs decreased to $419,678 from $471,861 (due to dry feed use and competitive pricing)
- Subcatchable/larger trout: $0.854 per pound (down from $0.874)
- Catchable trout: $0.203 each (size 4.20 per pound)
- Fingerling trout: $3.553 per pound, $0.026 each (increased from $2.437 per pound)
- Warmwater fish: $6.62 per pound, $0.109 each (down from $20.53 per pound due to record-keeping changes)

**Facility Performance:**
- [[Darrah Springs Hatchery]]: 457,847 pounds (record production)
- [[San Joaquin Hatchery]]: 320,401 pounds
- [[Moccasin Creek Hatchery]]: 208,682 pounds
- [[Fish Springs Hatchery]]: 188,623 pounds
- [[Trinity River Hatchery]]: 170,111 pounds

**Management Factors:**
- Commission policy of 10% "trophy" fish per load increased average catchable size
- 400,000 fewer catchables produced (5.2% reduction) but only 28,635 pounds less (1.6% reduction)
- Importance of operating hatcheries at full capacity demonstrated by cost-per-pound variations
- Reimbursable "mitigation" hatcheries operated for federal government and public utility districts
