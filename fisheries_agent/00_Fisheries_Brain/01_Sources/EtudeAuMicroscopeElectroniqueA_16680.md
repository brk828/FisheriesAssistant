# Scanning Electron Microscopy of Proasellus Copulatory Pleopods

#carcinology #isopoda #asellidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 note by [[Henry and Magniez]] applies [[Scanning Electron Microscopy]] to the second [[Copulatory Pleopod]] (pleopod II, male) of two epigean [[Asellidae]] isopods, [[Proasellus Meridianus]] and [[Proasellus Coxalis]]. The authors use the method to resolve the shape of the copulatory organ in three dimensions, which light microscopy often fails to show clearly. Their main result is that [[Proasellus Coxalis Banyulensis]], described by [[Racovitza]] in 1919 as a separate species, has a copulatory organ identical to that of the type form. They therefore reduce it to a geographical variety of the polytypic species P. coxalis. The note also confirms that the structure of the copulatory organ is a key diagnostic feature of the genus [[Proasellus]].

## Background & Description

The [[Asellota]] are isopods whose copulatory pleopods are central to genus- and species-level taxonomy in [[Asellidae]]. [[Racovitza]] was the first to recognise the high taxonomic value of pleopod II in these isopods. Light microscopy usually requires mounting the appendages in liquid between slide and coverslip, which can distort their true form. The authors therefore examined them under high magnification with a binocular loupe and then with the Stereoscan electron microscope.

**Methods:**
- Material was fixed for a long period in 70% alcohol, dehydrated in absolute alcohol and toluene, air-dried, mounted on stubs, and gold-coated under vacuum.
- Pleopods were either dissected free or left attached to the pleotelson, with the distal end oriented upward.
- Scale bars on the plates read 10 µ, 20 µ or 40 µ, depending on the figure.

No numerical sample sizes are given. The authors describe examining "numerous specimens" of P. coxalis from all sources and report that specimens were identical in structure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Proasellus meridianus** is a stable, well-characterised epigean species. Its identification is unambiguous, so it serves as the reference example of the genus. Studied material came from the Ouche River at Longvic-les-Dijon (Côte-d'Or), along with localities in Haute-Garonne, Ariège, Charente, Nièvre, Saône-et-Loire, and the Saône at Saint-Usage.
- **Proasellus coxalis** is the lead species of a group of Peri-Mediterranean forms whose systematic value was still unclear. Type material came from fresh waters of the Middle East, collected between 1890 and 1911 by Barois, Festa and Gadeau de Kerrville in Syria and Palestine. These specimens are part of the [[Dollfus Collection]] at the [[Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle]], Paris.
- **Proasellus coxalis banyulensis** was recorded from a freshwater cave at Marseille-Veyre (Bouches-du-Rhône), and from along the Mediterranean littoral of France (material collected by Berner).
- The authors note that the genus, including both epigean and groundwater-dwelling species, comprises "more than 40 species" across the Peri-Mediterranean and Atlantic region.
- The study also extended SEM examination to other asellotes: [[Asellus]], [[Conasellus]], [[Lirceus]] and [[Stenasellus]]. Each showed a structure distinct from the others.

## Key Findings

**1. Structure of the copulatory organ in Proasellus meridianus**
- The distal endopodite of pleopod II bears a single tergal apophysis.
- The organ extends into a **goulot** (a tubular process). This is laterally split, not closed, and opens at the distal end into the funnel-shaped orifice of the internal vesicle.
- The tergal apophysis and goulot form an angle "approaching 180°."
- The authors identify this split tubular goulot, with a single tergal apophysis, as the most typical diagnostic feature of the genus *Proasellus*.

**2. Proasellus coxalis coxalis (type form)**
- The sternal invagination slit of the internal vesicle is persistent, and the goulot remains split along its full length.
- The tergal apophysis is shorter and more massive than in P. meridianus.
- The angle between goulot and apophysis is much smaller than in P. meridianus.

**3. Proasellus coxalis banyulensis**
- The sternal invagination slit is "rigoureusement identique" to that of the type form and extends along the full length of the goulot.
- The tergal apophysis and the goulot–apophysis angle closely match the type form.
- The distal end of the goulot ends in a whistle-like form, with a very small terminal orifice placed distal-internally. This differs from the wide, flared orifice of P. meridianus.
- Racovitza's claim that the goulot is a closed tube is refuted. The authors show it is split, like that of the type form.

**4. Taxonomic conclusion**
- Because the copulatory organs of P. c. banyulensis and P. c. coxalis are "rigoureusement identiques," the authors question the validity of the French form as a species.
- They treat *banyulensis* as a **geographical variety** of the polytypic species P. coxalis. This agrees with earlier caryological studies by [[Montalenti and Rocchi]] (1964), who had reached a similar view.

**5. Generic status of Proasellus**
- The authors argue that the Peri-Mediterranean and Atlantic group of more than 40 species should be treated as a genuinely independent genus, citing their earlier work (Henry and Magniez, 1968).
- The diagnostic features of the distal endopodite are: (a) the internal vesicle orifice always borne on a laterally split, hollow tubular process (goulot), and (b) a single tergal apophysis relative to the goulot.
- Details such as the relative size of the apophysis and goulot, their precise shape, and the angle between them vary widely within the genus but are highly constant within each species. This makes them reliable species-level characters, as Racovitza had shown.

**6. Methodological advance**
- SEM allowed three-dimensional observation of the copulatory pleopods at very high magnification, avoiding the distortion caused by liquid mounting under the light microscope.
- The authors conclude that SEM is a valuable systematic tool for Asellidae.
