## Executive Summary

This document profiles two distinct U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) field stations that share the name "Warm Springs" but operate in entirely different regions and ecological contexts:

1. **Warm Springs National Fish Hatchery (Oregon, Region 1)** — Located on the [[Warm Springs Indian Reservation]] in Wasco County, Oregon, this station was established in 1978 under Federal Statute 184 (May 31, 1966) to enhance [[Anadromous Fish]] runs, primarily [[Spring Chinook Salmon]], and to supply [[Rainbow Trout]] to reservation waters. It distributes several hundred thousand fish annually to federal waters.

2. **Warm Springs Regional Fisheries Center (Georgia, Southeast Region)** — One of the oldest continuously operated warmwater hatcheries in the USFWS system (established 1899), this complex has evolved from a bass/catfish/sunfish production facility into a modern hub combining a **National Fish Hatchery**, a **Fish Health Center** (est. 1989), and (in development) a **Fisheries Technology Center**. Its current mission centers on restoration of [[Gulf Coast Striped Bass]] in the Apalachicola River system and Chesapeake Bay, plus experimental rearing of [[Gulf Sturgeon]] beginning in 1990.

Together, these profiles illustrate the dual role of USFWS hatchery infrastructure: supporting Native American treaty fishery obligations in the Pacific Northwest, and driving imperiled anadromous/estuarine species restoration and fish health diagnostics in the Southeast.

## Background & Description

The source is an administrative station profile rather than a biological study, so morphological data on the fish themselves is not provided. Species-level identity is limited to common names:

- **Spring Chinook Salmon** (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) — anadromous salmonid, run-timing designation "spring," reared for reservation enhancement.
- **Rainbow Trout** (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) — reared for stocking reservation waters.
- **Gulf Coast Striped Bass** (*Morone saxatilis*) — anadromous moronid, the primary current production species at the Georgia complex.
- **Gulf Sturgeon** (*Acipenser oxyrinchus desotoi*) — large anadromous chondrostean under experimental rearing since 1990.
- Historical/legacy production species at the Georgia hatchery: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Catfish]] (Ictaluridae), and various [[Sunfish]] (Centrarchidae), reflecting the facility's original 1899 warmwater mission.

No morphometric, meristic, or life-stage size data are reported in this text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Oregon Station (Warm Springs NFH):**
- Situated within the Columbia River basin, supporting reservation-based anadromous fisheries tied to tribal treaty rights.
- Facilities include a [[Fish Ladder]] and [[Fish Barrier Dam]], indicating active management of upstream salmon passage and broodstock capture for the hatchery's spring Chinook program.
- Water source and hydrological details are not specified beyond pond infrastructure (30 rearing ponds, adult holding ponds).

**Georgia Station (Warm Springs Regional Fisheries Center):**
- Water supply is gravity-fed from **Cold Springs**, delivering 1,200 gallons/minute total flow; the hatchery holds rights to two-thirds (800 gpm), with the remaining third allocated to the City of Warm Springs for domestic use — a direct water-sharing relationship between hatchery operations and a municipal supply.
- 56.2 total acres, including 18.23 surface acres across 40 ponds; a portion of the land carries a reversionary deed clause tied to continued use as a fish hatchery.
- Supports restoration ecology for anadromous/estuarine species across multiple river systems and coastal states: **Apalachicola River** (Florida), **Chesapeake Bay** (Maryland, Virginia), and additional distribution to North Carolina, South Carolina, and Georgia — indicating a broad Southeastern coastal service area for [[Striped Bass Restoration]].
- The co-located **Fish Health Center** provides regional diagnostic and certification services (including triploid grass carp certification for private aquaculture), linking the station to broader Southeastern private and commercial aquaculture biosecurity.

## Key Findings

- **Oregon hatchery mission**: Enhance anadromous fish runs (Spring Chinook Salmon) and supply Rainbow Trout on the Warm Springs Indian Reservation, established via Federal Statute in 1966, operational since 1978.
- **Distribution trends (Oregon)**: Fish distributed to federal waters declined from 883,000 (FY91) → 729,000 (FY92) → 529,000 (FY93), with all fish counted excluding eggs and fry.
- **Staffing/funding trends (Oregon)**: FTE decreased from 5.11 (1990) to an estimated 5.50 (1994 est.) after dipping to 4.58 in 1992; operations budget rose steadily from $383K (1990) to a projected $467K (1993–94 est.); maintenance spending fluctuated ($39K–$52K range).
- **Georgia complex age and legacy**: Warm Springs Hatchery (est. 1899) is among the oldest warmwater hatcheries still operated by USFWS, originally built for largemouth bass, catfish, and sunfish culture.
- **Institutional consolidation**: The 1990 creation of the Warm Springs Regional Fisheries Center unified a National Fish Hatchery, Fish Health Center, and planned Fisheries Technology/Environmental Education units into a single complex — reflecting a regional model for integrated fish health, culture, and technology support.
- **Fish Health Center origins**: Established in 1989 by consolidating diagnostic functions from two closed labs (Heber Springs, AR; Pisgah Forest, NC), centralizing fish disease diagnostics for the Southeast Region.
- **Species restoration focus shift**: The Georgia hatchery's modern production has pivoted from historic warmwater sportfish to **Gulf Coast Striped Bass** restoration (Apalachicola River, Chesapeake Bay) and exploratory **Gulf Sturgeon** culture (from 1990).
- **Distribution data (Georgia, Striped Bass)**: Totals distributed rose from 66,538 (1990) to 59,227 (1992), with contributions spanning FWS, FWS-Research, Army Corps of Engineers (COE), state waters, and international waters — indicating multi-agency and multi-jurisdictional partnership in striped bass restoration.
- **Funding/staffing trends (Georgia)**: Operations budget increased from $438.4K (1991) to an estimated $595.3K (1994), while FTEs grew from 10.0 (1991) to 12.8 (1992), reflecting expansion of the Center's consolidated mission.
- **Water rights arrangement**: A notable non-biological finding is the formal water-sharing agreement between the hatchery and the City of Warm Springs, GA, splitting Cold Springs flow (2:1 ratio) between hatchery operations and municipal domestic supply.
