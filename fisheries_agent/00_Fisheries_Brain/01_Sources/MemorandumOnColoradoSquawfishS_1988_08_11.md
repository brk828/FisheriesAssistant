## Executive Summary
This 1988 memorandum from Paul C. Marsh (Arizona State University, Center for Environmental Studies) documents a stocking event of juvenile [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) into the southernmost lake at the ASU Research Park (ASURP) on 10 August 1988. Approximately 20,000 hatchery-reared young fish, produced by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service hatchery in [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery|Dexter, New Mexico]], were stocked by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]. The note reports low transport/post-stocking mortality and good fish condition, and contrasts this release with a 1986 stocking cohort that yielded very poor recapture rates, prompting the present effort to investigate causes of low detectability or survival in this endangered cyprinid.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), family [[Cyprinidae]]
- **Life stage**: Juvenile ("young fish"), hatchery-reared
- **Total Length (TL)**: Mean 40 ± 4 mm (range 32–50 mm)
- **Weight**: Mean 0.42 ± 0.16 g (range 0.14–0.80 g)
- **Sample basis**: Measured subsample of 100 specimens from the ~20,000 stocked
- **Condition at release**: Fish were active and in apparent good physical condition

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat context**: Stocked into a constructed lake system at [[ASU Research Park]], a non-native, artificial lentic habitat used as a refugial/experimental site for endangered Colorado River Basin fishes.
- **Native range context**: *Ptychocheilus lucius* is native to the [[Colorado River Basin]], historically a large-river piscivorous cyprinid; its presence in a small impounded lake represents an ex-situ conservation/refuge stocking strategy rather than natural habitat use.
- **Co-occurring stocked species**: [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] were stocked at ASURP around the same time period, allowing for comparative survival/recapture assessment among three federally listed Colorado River fishes.
- **Sampling regime**: Intensive sampling using "a variety of collection gears" was conducted on the 1986 cohort; routine follow-up sampling was scheduled for September 1988 to assess this new cohort.

## Key Findings
- **Stocking event**: ~20,000 juvenile Colorado squawfish released at 1700 hrs on 10 August 1988 into the southernmost ASURP lake.
- **Source**: Fish produced spring 1988 at the USFWS [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (Dexter, NM); stocking logistics handled by [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].
- **Short-term mortality**: Transport and immediate post-stocking mortality was minimal (~"scarcely exceeded a dozen fish" out of 20,000); no dead or distressed fish observed at 2–3 hr and 12–13 hr post-release checks.
- **Historical comparison**: A prior cohort of 1,048 juvenile Colorado squawfish stocked in October 1986 yielded extremely poor recapture success — only a handful of individuals recovered on two sampling occasions despite intensive effort with multiple gear types.
- **Contrast with other species**: Recaptures of co-stocked [[Bonytail]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] from the same 1986 period were comparatively high ("impressive"), raising the question of whether Colorado squawfish are inherently harder to sample/detect or are experiencing disproportionately higher post-stocking mortality.
- **Research objective**: The 1988 stocking was explicitly framed as an investigative effort to determine the reason(s) behind the low recapture rate of Colorado squawfish observed since 1986 — an early example of stocking used as an applied research tool for endangered species reintroduction monitoring.
- **Conservation relevance**: This record documents early augmentation/reintroduction efforts for the federally endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] as part of Colorado River Basin native fish recovery programs, involving coordination among ASU, AZGFD, and USFWS (Albuquerque office).
