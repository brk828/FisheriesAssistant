#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1974 technical report, prepared for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, investigates external secondary sex characteristics in the adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a threatened/endangered catostomid endemic to the Lower Colorado River Basin. The goal was to establish non-lethal sexing criteria for use in population management, given the species' conservation status. Using preserved museum specimens (30.5–48.1 cm standard length) from the Arizona State University Collection of Fishes, the author examined four criteria of [[Sexual Dimorphism]]: breeding coloration, nuptial [[Tuberculation]] patterns, relative fin proportions, and [[Urogenital Papillae]] morphology. Findings indicate that males display more extensive tuberculation and proportionally longer pelvic/anal fins, while females possess significantly larger urogenital papillae. These traits, particularly fin proportions and papillae size, offer viable non-sacrificial sexing criteria outside of the breeding season.

## Background & Description

### Breeding Coloration (Seasonal: Late Feb–Early June)
- **Non-breeding (both sexes):** Dark brown to olivaceous dorsum/upper sides; red-brown to pink laterally; pale yellow ventrally; yellow anal fin; brownish-yellow lower caudal lobe.
- **Breeding Males:** Undergo dramatic color shift — blackish dorsally and along upper flanks (to ~1 inch below lateral line), with brilliant orange lateral and ventral surfaces.
- **Breeding Females:** Retain standard (non-breeding) coloration year-round.

### Nuptial Tuberculation
- Tubercles are conical, range 0.5–3.0 mm in diameter, and are associated with fin rays (not interradial membranes) and caudal peduncle scales.
- **Males (n=3 tuberculate):** Widespread distribution — dorsal surfaces of pelvic fins, anal fin, entire caudal peduncle (except dorsum), and lower caudal fin lobe. Often occurs in double rows on branched rays.
- **Females (n=8 tuberculate):** Restricted distribution — limited to the caudal peduncle below the lateral line and the lower caudal fin lobe only.
- Where tubercles occur on homologous structures in both sexes, males consistently show higher counts (see [[Key Findings]] for data).
- Some spent females showed resorbed or worn tubercles, visible as scars, which were counted alongside intact tubercles.

### Fin Proportions
- Measured via friction dividers from fin origin to longest ray tip, normalized against standard length.
- **Pectoral fins:** No sexual dimorphism detected.
- **Pelvic fins:** Males average 1.8% longer (relative to SL) than females.
- **Anal fins:** Males average 2.4% longer (relative to SL) than females.

### Urogenital Papillae
- Measured for length (base to urogenital opening, ventral axis) and width (across base).
- **Females:** Papillae averaged 39.9% larger than males' (in 7 of 8 paired comparisons).
- **Exception:** One case showed male papilla 11.1% larger than the paired female.
- **Qualitative shape differences:** Male papillae are more pointed at the apex and oriented more parallel to the body's long axis; female papillae are broader/blunter at the apex.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

*Xyrauchen texanus* inhabits large river channels within the [[Lower Colorado River Basin]], including historical collection sites at Lake Mohave, Lake Mead, the Yampa River (Utah), and the Colorado River near Imperial Dam. As a large-bodied [[Catostomidae|catostomid]], the species is closely tied to riverine and reservoir habitats undergoing significant anthropogenic alteration (damming, flow regulation), which contributed to its designation as threatened/endangered. Breeding behavior is seasonally restricted (late February–early June), during which secondary sexual characteristics (coloration, tuberculation) become expressed, presumably functioning in mate recognition and spawning substrate contact.

## Key Findings

1. **Coloration is a reliable breeding-season sexing tool**: Males turn blackish-dorsal/orange-ventral; females remain cryptically colored year-round.
2. **Tubercle distribution differs by sex on homologous structures**: Males exhibit tubercles on pelvic fins, anal fin, and the *entire* caudal peduncle circumference; females only on the ventral caudal peduncle and lower caudal lobe.
3. **Quantitative tubercle counts favor males** (representative data from Table 2):
   - Males: Caudal peduncle counts 148–196; Caudal fin counts 206–260.
   - Females: Caudal peduncle counts 0–40; Caudal fin counts 13–132.
4. **Fin length dimorphism is a year-round (non-seasonal) sexing criterion**: Males show consistently longer pelvic (+1.8%) and anal (+2.4%) fins relative to standard length; pectoral fins show no difference.
5. **Urogenital papillae size is inversely dimorphic compared to fins**: Females possess notably larger papillae (avg. +39.9%) than males, with one anomalous exception.
6. **Comparative taxonomy**: Contrary to Branson (1966), who likened *Xyrauchen*'s tubercle pattern to *[[Catostomus]] macrocheilus*, this study found closer affinity to *[[Moxostoma]] carinatum*, since *Xyrauchen* produces tubercles on both the pelvic fins/caudal peduncle *and* double rows on branched anal/caudal rays — a combination not seen in *Catostomus*.
7. **Research gaps identified**: Current data pertain only to adults; larval, postlarval, and juvenile dimorphism remain uncharacterized. Larger sample sizes (especially of tuberculate specimens) are needed to strengthen reliability of these sexing criteria.
