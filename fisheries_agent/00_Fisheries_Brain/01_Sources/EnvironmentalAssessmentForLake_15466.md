# Lake Mohave Native Fish Rearing Facility Environmental Assessment

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Catostomidae #endangered-species #habitat-restoration

## Executive Summary

This 1990 environmental assessment proposes establishing a native fish rearing facility at [[Yuma Cove]], [[Lake Mohave]], to prevent extirpation of the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The species, once common throughout the [[Colorado River]] system from Wyoming to Mexico, is now reduced to remnant populations with the largest remaining population of approximately 50,000 individuals in [[Lake Mohave]]. Despite successful spawning, recruitment failure occurs when larvae disappear at 10-12 mm total length, attributed primarily to predation by non-native fishes. The existing population consists entirely of old fish (25-45 years) with no juvenile recruitment, making extirpation within ten years likely without intervention. The preferred alternative involves modifying a natural backwater at [[Yuma Cove]] to rear juveniles to 25-30 cm TL before release.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) is a large catostomid fish endemic to the [[Colorado River]] system of western North America. Current populations are restricted to:
- [[Green River]], Utah
- Upper [[Colorado River]], Colorado  
- [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona and Nevada (largest population: ~50,000 individuals)
- Scattered individuals elsewhere

The species is a candidate for federal listing as endangered due to dwindling numbers and continuing threats. Monitoring over several years has revealed that all populations consist of old fish ranging from 25 to 45 years of age. While spawning, incubation, and hatching are successful, larval razorback suckers disappear at 10-12 mm total length. Recruitment to juvenile or adult life stages is non-existent, and remaining populations are destined to be lost to natural aging. Predation by non-native fishes is identified as the single most important factor responsible for recruitment failure.

The species has proven amenable to hatchery propagation, but on-site natural reproduction and grow-out represents a fiscally responsible alternative to remote hatchery facilities.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Yuma Cove]] is located on the Arizona side of [[Lake Mohave]] immediately north of [[Arizona Bay]]. Wave action-induced erosion has created a natural sand and gravel berm blocking the cove mouth, forming a backwater pond. The berm elevation is approximately 645 feet, two feet below maximum [[Lake Mohave]] operational elevation of 647 feet.

The site was selected because:
1. [[Razorback Sucker]]s currently spawn naturally along the lake side of this berm
2. A successful 1984 trial demonstrated spawning within the pond, with young suckers exhibiting rapid growth rates
3. The 1984 trial ended when rising reservoir elevations and storm-induced wave action breached the natural berm, allowing invasion by other fish and subsequent predation

The backwater environment provides natural conditions for spawning and rearing while offering protection from predators when properly isolated. The borrow area for construction materials is currently unvegetated to sparsely vegetated, with vegetation consisting chiefly of exotic [[Tamarisk]].

## Key Findings

**Preferred Alternative - Yuma Cove Backwater Rearing Facility:**

*Construction Phase:*
- Modify existing gravel berm to elevation of 652 feet (5 feet above maximum lake elevation of 647 feet)
- Place approximately 1,575 cubic yards of gravel and sand materials on berm
- Materials sourced from approximately one-acre borrow area within cove, below high lake level
- Construction using rubber-tired front-end loader, potentially transported by barge
- If barge unavailable, loader will traverse previously closed/rehabilitated road and desert wash (two passes only)
- Tracks to be raked by drag screen and hand raking

*Fish Management Phase:*
- Late August/September 1990: Apply rotenone (piscicide) to remove competing and predatory exotic fish
- Late fall 1990: Second piscicide application (antimycin)
- Both piscicides are EPA-registered for this use
- December 1990: Collect and place approximately 20 female and 60 male [[Razorback Sucker]]s via beach seine
- Anticipated spawning: March 1991
- Rearing period: 12-18 months until juveniles reach 25-30 cm TL
- Release: Breach berm by hand tools to release juveniles into [[Lake Mohave]]

*Project Goal:*
Produce juveniles of sufficient size to avoid heavy predation pressure and enable recruitment into adult population.

**Alternative Approaches Considered:**

1. **No Action Alternative:** Would likely result in extirpation of [[Razorback Sucker]]s from [[Lake Mohave]] within ten years, eliminating the largest known population

2. **Hardened Structure Alternative:** Use of rip-rap, sandbags, concrete, or wooden walls rejected due to:
   - Major disturbances to berm used for current spawning
   - Substantially higher cost with insufficient funding
   - Would create on-site hatchery rather than natural rearing conditions

3. **Alternative Cove Sites:** [[Yuma Cove]] is the only location with existing backwater and known spawning history; other potential sites noted for future expansion

4. **Off-site Hatchery:** Could be successful but rejected due to:
   - Cost an order of magnitude above preferred alternative
   - Loss of advantage of near-natural conditions

**Environmental Impacts and Mitigation:**

*Disturbance Impacts:*
- Front-end loader passage: Two passes across rehabilitated road and desert wash
- Mitigation: Rubber-tired vehicle only, drag screen to remove tracks, hand raking where necessary
- Responsible party: Resource management specialist

*Water Quality:*
- Temporary and localized turbidity from berm material placement
- All materials placed approximately 4+ feet above waterline
- Siltation expected to be temporary and localized

*Vegetation and Habitat:*
- Approximately 1,575 cubic yards removed from one-acre borrow area
- Unvegetated areas prioritized for borrow
- Existing willow stakes protected; no construction within 30 feet
- Natural shoreline processes expected to reshape borrow area and replace materials over time

**Regulatory Coordination:**

Project sponsored by Native Fish Work Group comprising:
- [[National Park Service]] (lead for Environmental Assessment and U.S. Army Corps of Engineers coordination)
- [[Bureau of Reclamation]] (berm modification design and implementation)
- [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (lead for Endangered Species Act clearances)
- [[Arizona Fish and Game Department]] (piscicide application)
- [[Arizona State University]] (biological justification and work statement development by Drs. Marsh and Minckley)

No adverse impacts identified on endangered species, wetlands, or cultural resources. Archaeological clearances completed. The project is undertaken to enhance populations of a species in serious jeopardy of extirpation along the [[Colorado River]] system, with high expectation of success and relatively low cost.
