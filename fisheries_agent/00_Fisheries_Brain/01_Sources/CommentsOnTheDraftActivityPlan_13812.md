## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 expert review letter (from Dr. W.L. Minckley, Professor of Zoology, Arizona State University) commenting on the draft Activity Plan/Environmental Assessment for the [[Gila Box Riparian National Conservation Area]] (NCA), managed by the U.S. Bureau of Land Management (BLM). While primarily a policy critique, the letter contains significant scientific commentary on the conservation status of the [[Gila River Basin]] native fish fauna, highlighting it as a system of unparalleled conservation concern in North America. The letter specifically critiques poorly documented claims regarding [[Razorback Sucker]] stocking efforts in [[Bonita Creek]], and underscores the ecological importance of remaining unmodified river reaches for recovery of endangered fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** The [[Gila River]] basin represents a transition zone between Sonoran and Chihuahuan desert (or semi-desert grassland), bordered to the north by extensions of the Rocky Mountains and isolated to the south from Mexican biotic influences by the Gila River valley itself.
- **Remaining unmodified habitat:** Only **four undammed and relatively unmodified river reaches** remain in the entire Gila River Basin system available to support recovery actions for native fishes:
  1. [[Verde River]] (central Arizona)
  2. [[Salt River]] (eastern Arizona)
  3. [[San Pedro River]]
  4. [[Gila River]] (from the vicinity of Safford, AZ, through the NCA and into New Mexico)
- **Corridor function:** The NCA and adjacent riparian corridors (including [[Bonita Creek]] and [[Eagle Creek]]) also serve as critical migratory corridors for avifauna, indicating the broader ecological significance of maintaining connected, functional riparian ecosystems for multiple taxa, not fish alone.
- **Threats:** Upstream mining operations (Clifton-Morenci area, Sanchez Mine) pose potential impacts to water quality and habitat integrity within the Eagle Creek drainage and the broader NCA ecosystem.

## Key Findings
- **Basin-wide conservation crisis:** The Gila River basin is identified as "the only system of its size in North America to now have all its native fishes either extinct, extirpated, listed as endangered or threatened, considered as candidates for listing, or recommended for candidacy." This represents a near-total collapse of a native freshwater fish fauna at the basin scale.
- **Disproportionate management emphasis:** Despite this crisis, the draft Activity Plan allegedly gave inadequate emphasis to fish conservation, with only "Alternative I" of the proposed management alternatives containing substantive provisions for BLM participation in fish recovery efforts.
- **Unsubstantiated stocking claims:** The draft document made unreferenced claims regarding "hundreds of thousands of small razorbacks" being released into privately owned reaches of [[Bonita Creek]], with follow-up assertions that "only a few large individuals remain" and "no evidence of a reproducing, self-sustaining population." The reviewer criticizes the complete absence of citations, source data, stocking dates, locations, or definitions of terms such as "survivors" and "limiting factor" (the latter applied to "large pools" as a supposed constraint on razorback recruitment).
- **Lack of scientific documentation:** The overall Activity Plan/EA is criticized for its near-total absence of citations to the substantial pre-existing scientific literature on the NCA's ecosystem (accumulated over approximately 30 years of prior research), undermining the credibility and verifiability of biological and management claims, including those related to fish population status and habitat limiting factors.
- **Policy alternative preference:** The reviewer explicitly favors "Alternative I" among the proposed management options as most consistent with the ecosystem integrity and biodiversity goals implied by the enabling legislation (P.L. 101-628) for the NCA, citing its greater alignment with native fish and ecosystem recovery priorities compared to alternatives emphasizing grazing, recreation, and access development.
- **Call for accountability:** The letter calls for BLM to provide verifiable citations and data sources for any biological claims used to justify future management or monitoring decisions affecting NCA fish populations and other biota.
