# Working Group Update: Tucson Native Fish Projects

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fish-conservation

## Executive Summary

This two-page email update from [[Don Mitchell]], Fish Program Manager for Region V of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], reports on the Tucson native fish program and its [[Working Group]]. It covers a rescheduled group meeting, an experimental study on chub predation of tadpoles, and several projects to restore or augment [[Gila Topminnow]] and [[Pupfish]] populations at sites including [[Sabino Canyon]] and [[Turkey Creek]]. The dates in the header are partly illegible after OCR, and the 2007 timing is inferred from the filing date.

## Background & Description

The update is a correspondence item rather than a research report. It contains:

- **Meeting logistics:** The Working Group meeting was rescheduled. The original announcement was cancelled, and the new time was given in the email as a change from a morning start to an evening start (exact times are garbled in the scan).
- **Forwarded research summary:** A message from a University of Arizona Assistant Research Scientist at the School of Natural Resources (name illegible in scan) with contact details for the Tucson office.
- **Program status:** A summary of several fish program projects, some in planning, some under construction, and some with fieldwork underway.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Chub predation on tadpoles.** The forwarded message describes a mesocosm experiment on [[Tadpole Survivorship]] in which chubs reduced tadpole survival. Treatments included chub alone, [[Pupfish]] alone, and both species together. The author notes that tadpole survival would likely be higher if chubs were absent, and that chub density is a factor to consider in less complex, field-based settings. The source does not give sample sizes or statistical results.

**Habitat and population projects:**

- A landowner completed a small spring-fed pond on Turkey Creek. The pond is near a planned project to re-establish topminnows, and the author notes that rock, pond, and other work is ongoing.
- Papers were in progress for the re-establishment of topminnows at Sabino Canyon. The author is also working on repairs at the Sabino location, and the project is described as moving forward.
- Plans were under way to augment a pupfish population through a captive stock, and to address a population of a related lineage at the same location.
- Surveys on Canyon Creek and Turkey Creek were being planned or conducted, with the aim of expanding the range of native fish.

**Agreements and permits.** The program had an agreement (the abbreviation "SHA" appears in the scan, most likely a [[Safe Harbor Agreement]]) in final editing. The author expected it to be ready for signing soon. Another item, a project with a federal or state partner, was described as "not holding our breath," meaning the timeline was uncertain.

## Key Findings

- The Working Group meeting was rescheduled and the earlier date was cancelled.
- A forwarded experiment found that chubs reduced [[Tadpole Survivorship]] in a [[Mesocosm Experiment]] that compared chub alone, pupfish alone, and both together. The author suggests tadpole survival would be higher without chubs. No sample sizes or statistics are reported.
- A landowner on Turkey Creek completed a small spring-fed pond, which may support future topminnow work.
- Re-establishment of [[Gila Topminnow]] populations at Sabino Canyon is in planning, with repairs and paperwork underway.
- Augmentation of pupfish populations and related surveys on Canyon Creek and Turkey Creek are planned or in progress.
- The Safe Harbor Agreement is in final edit and expected to be ready soon.
- The author expects the topminnow project to be completed by the end of the year, with some work continuing into the following year. The next Working Group meeting is in February.
