# Zoologie Agricole- Etude del la morphologie cranienne des grands Campagnols (Arvicola Lacepide) par l'anayse factorielle des correspondances

#zoology #mammalogy #rodentia #arvicolinae #cranial-morphometrics #factorial-correspondence-analysis

> **Note:** This source concerns rodent (water vole) cranial systematics, not ichthyology. Tags and structure have been adapted to faithfully reflect the taxon actually studied — the genus *Arvicola* (Lacépède), commonly known as "grands campagnols" or water voles.

## Executive Summary

This 1972 note by Spitz and Morel applies **factorial correspondence analysis (analyse factorielle des correspondances)** — a multivariate statistical technique developed by Benzécri — to a collection of **144 skulls** of the rodent genus *Arvicola* held at the INRA Laboratoire des Vertébrés (Jouy-en-Josas). Each skull was subjected to **10 cranial measurements**. The analysis resolved the sample into **two principal groups**, which correspond to the two previously known **chromosomal species** of the genus: *[[Arvicola Sapidus]]* (2N = 40) and the *[[Arvicola Terrestris]]* complex (2N = 36, sensu lato). The first two factorial axes accounted for **~70% of total inertia** (Factor 1: 43.5%; Factor 2: 25%), enabling a two-dimensional graphical representation of both specimens and cranial characters simultaneously — a hallmark advantage of the correspondence-analysis method over classical factor analysis.

## Background & Description

- **Measurement protocol:** Ten linear cranial mensurations were taken per skull (illustrated in Fig. 1 of the original), forming a 144 × 10 data matrix.
- **Factor 1 (43.5% of inertia):** Primarily separates the two chromosomal species; a large hiatus intersecting Factor 1 near abscissa ≈ −0.0036 cleanly divides *[[Arvicola Sapidus]]* (left of hiatus) from the *[[Arvicola Terrestris]]* group (right of hiatus).
- **Factor 2 (25% of inertia):** Interpreted as an **age/maturation gradient** ("facteur de maturation" per Benzécri and Bellier). Specimens independently classified as "young" based on cranial crest and suture development cluster in the upper region of the factorial plane, supporting this interpretation.
- **Point-cloud structure:** Samples form bands oriented parallel to the bisector of the Factor 1–Factor 2 angle, rather than discrete clusters, suggesting **morphological continuity** rather than sharp discontinuities within the terrestris complex.
- **Sub-structuring within the terrestris group (right of hiatus), among "older" individuals (below Axis 1):**
  - A subgroup comprising **La Fajolle, Peisey-Nancroix (Savoie), and Doubs** populations.
  - A distinct subgroup comprising **Mende (Lozère)** and probably **Le Sappey (Isère)**.
  - These two subgroups **overlap completely** above Axis 1 (i.e., among younger individuals), indicating that age-related cranial change obscures the geographic/taxonomic signal.
- **Geographically distinct outlying groups within terrestris sensu lato:**
  - Five specimens from **Nord and Pas-de-Calais**, positioned at the edge of the hiatus (closest to *sapidus*).
  - The **La Giettaz (Haute-Savoie)** sample, positioned further right.
  - A broad composite group spanning from **La Fajolle (Aude)** to **Le Sappey (Isère)**.
- **Isolated/extreme individuals:**
  - A *[[Arvicola Exitus]]* specimen from the Swiss plateau plotted furthest right of all samples.
  - A specimen from **Zerlest, Germany** plotted similarly far right.
  - An *A. italicus* from **Ticino** and an unidentified "Campagnol d'eau" from **Suippes (Marne)** aligned with the Nord–Pas-de-Calais axis.
  - A **hybrid between *italicus* and *exitus*** (Swiss plateau) coincided with the Peisey-Nancroix–La Fajolle group.
  - Notably, two "Campagnol d'eau" individuals captured **at the same locality** in Suippes were classified respectively into the *italicus*-affiliated group and the *sapidus* group — direct morphometric evidence that **the two chromosomal species can cohabit in sympatric aquatic form**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- *Arvicola* species are **semi-aquatic riparian rodents** ("campagnols d'eau" / water voles) inhabiting riverbanks, streams, and wetland margins across France, Switzerland, and Germany.
- The study demonstrates **sympatric cohabitation** of the two chromosomal species (*sapidus* and the *italicus/exitus/terrestris* complex) at at least one locality (Suippes, Marne), both captured under the same "aquatic form" habitat descriptor — indicating shared ecological niche use despite deep genetic/morphological divergence.
- Broader geographic sampling spans lowland (Nord, Pas-de-Calais, Somme, Marne, Indre) and montane/alpine (Savoie, Haute-Savoie, Isère, Swiss plateau) habitats, suggesting the *terrestris* complex occupies a wide altitudinal and climatic range, consistent with a history of **glacial-refugium population divergence and secondary contact**.

## Key Findings

1. **Two robust chromosomal/morphological groups** were confirmed within French *Arvicola*: *[[Arvicola Sapidus]]* (2N = 40) and the *[[Arvicola Terrestris]]* complex (2N = 36), separated by a clear hiatus on the Factor 1 axis.
2. *A. sapidus* forms an **extremely homogeneous** morphological cluster, in contrast to the internal heterogeneity of the *terrestris* group.
3. Factor 2 likely reflects an **ontogenetic/age-related maturation gradient**, which partially confounds geographic/taxonomic signal among younger specimens.
4. Within the *terrestris* complex, at least **three geographically structured sub-regions** are discernible (Nord–Pas-de-Calais; La Giettaz; and a broad Fajolle-to-Sappey group), with further age-dependent subdivision into two subgroups (Fajolle/Peisey-Nancroix/Doubs vs. Mende/Sappey) among older individuals.
5. Evidence of a **natural hybrid** between *[[Arvicola Italicus]]* and *[[Arvicola Exitus]]* (Swiss plateau), morphologically intermediate and clustering with the Peisey-Nancroix–La Fajolle group.
6. Direct evidence of **sympatry between the two chromosomal species** in aquatic habitat at a single locality (Suippes), demonstrating that ecological cohabitation does not imply morphological convergence.
7. Given the **chromosomal stability** of the *terrestris* group (unlike the more karyotypically variable genus *[[Pitymys]]*), the authors favor the hypothesis that it represents a **single species** with populations differentiated by past glacial isolation and secondary reunion, rather than an actively speciating complex — though the alternative (ongoing speciation) cannot be excluded.
8. The work is presented as a **preliminary methodological proof-of-concept** for applying factorial correspondence analysis to systematic problems in rodent cranial morphometrics, advocating for expanded sampling in future studies.
