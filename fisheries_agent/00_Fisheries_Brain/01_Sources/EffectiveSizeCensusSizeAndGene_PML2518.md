# Effective Size, Census Size, and Genetic Monitoring of the Endangered Razorback Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study estimates the genetic effective population size of the federally endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) in [[Lake Mohave]], on the [[Colorado River]], and compares it with the adult census size. The population has had no natural recruitment for decades because [[Non-Native Predatory Fishes]] prey on larvae. A [[Repatriation Program]] therefore collects wild larvae after natural spawning, rears them in protective custody, and releases them at a larger, less vulnerable size. Using temporal shifts in [[Mitochondrial DNA]] haplotype frequencies across seven larval cohorts (1997–2003), the authors estimated the annual female effective size (N_eb) and the generational female effective size (N_eg). They compared these with census estimates from [[Mark-Recapture]] studies. Generational N_eg was 706, and the N_eg/N_c ratio was 0.29–0.38. The authors conclude that larval sampling captures adequate genetic diversity and that repatriated fish are contributing genetically to each year's cohorts.

## Background & Description

*Xyrauchen texanus* is a long-lived, [[Iteroparity|iteroparous]] catostomid with high fecundity. Mature females can produce more than 80,000 eggs per spawn. This reproductive capacity supports large numbers of offspring for repatriation. The genetic concern is that one or a few breeding pairs could produce most repatriates each year. That would pass on only a small part of the spawning stock's genetic diversity and increase [[Inbreeding]]. Inbred lines show reduced viability relative to outbred lines (Dowling et al. 1996a).

In 2004 the spawning stock comprised approximately 420 wild adults plus 1,500 repatriates. The wild adult stock had declined by at least an order of magnitude over the preceding 13 years. This decline makes it increasingly important to verify whether repatriated individuals reproduce.

The study relies on two standard concepts:

- **Variance effective size (N_e):** Genetic drift changes allele frequencies at a rate inversely proportional to the number of breeders. The [[Temporal Method]] (Nei and Tajima 1981) estimates N_e from the standardized variance of these temporal shifts.
- **N_e/N_c ratio:** Comparing N_e with census size (N_c) indicates the proportion of successful breeders each generation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study system.** Larval razorback suckers were sampled at four localities in Lake Mohave. Sampling was repeated up to five times per year across the January–April spawning season, for seven years (1997–2003). The Lake Mohave stock is bounded by dams upstream and downstream, so the authors treated it as closed to migration. Haplotype frequencies were pooled across sites and sampling dates within each year. Pooling was justified because there was no evidence of temporally stable genetic divergence among localities (F = 0.003, P = 0.084).

**Genetic methods.** A 311-bp fragment of mitochondrial cytochrome b (see [[Cytochrome B]]) was assayed with [[Single-Strand Conformation Polymorphism]] (SSCP) analysis and direct sequencing. Of 2,432 larvae assayed, 28 unique haplotypes were identified.

**Census estimation.** Adult female census size was estimated with an adjusted Petersen method (Ricker 1975) using wild and repatriated mark-recapture data. The adult sex ratio did not differ significantly from 1:1 (9,954 females to 10,997 males; P = 0.96), so the number of reproductively capable females was taken as 0.5 × total adults.

**Life history for demographic correction.** Average age at female maturity was about 4 years. Generation time was 9 years. Survival was estimated at 80% from catch data. The von Bertalanffy growth parameters were L∞ = 597 mm, k = 0.84, and t₀ = 0.91. A [[Jorde and Ryman Correction]] for [[Overlapping Generations]] was applied to adjust N_eb to N_eg.

**Benchmarks and comparisons.** The authors compare results with the 50/500 rule (Franklin 1980) and with N_e/N ratios across taxa:

- Rio Grande silvery minnow: about 0.001
- Northern pike: 0.03–0.14
- Banded sculpin: 0.09
- Brown trout: 0.12–0.59
- Steelhead: greater than 0.5
- Several long-lived marine species (plaice, vermilion snapper, New Zealand snapper, cod, red drum, red snapper): three to four orders of magnitude below 0.5

Razorback suckers share with these marine fishes a high-fecundity, high-early-mortality (type III survivorship) life history. Such life histories can produce extreme variance in reproductive success through the [[Hedgecock Effect]] (see [[Match-Mismatch Recruitment]]).

## Key Findings

**1. Effective and census sizes**

- Arithmetic mean annual female effective size (N_eb) across six temporally adjacent cohort pairs: **160**.
- Generational female effective size (N_eg): **706** (95% CI 227–3,193).
- Correction factor C = 40.37; generation time G = 9.03.
- N_eg/N_c ratio: **0.29** and **0.38** for arithmetic and harmonic mean census size, respectively. The paper reports these as 0.29 and 0.38 for arithmetic and harmonic means, respectively.
- Mean N_eb/N_c values were 0.078 and 0.080.

**2. Breeding contribution**

- Using the [[Nunney and Elam Model]] for minimal demography, the authors estimate that roughly 3–16% of potentially breeding females (about 8% on average) contribute offspring each year. Results sections give a narrower 4–8% range for the same estimate.
- The Discussion summary of the proportion of females contributing per generation is illegible in the scanned text and is not reported here.
- If p (proportion of females successfully breeding each year) is below 3%, N_eg/N_c falls below 0.25. Above 63%, it exceeds 0.75.
- Variance in female reproductive success, not survival, is the main driver of the N_eg/N_c ratio. The ratio changed little across plausible survival values (for example, S = 0.70 gave N_eg/N_c of 0.24 and 0.18).

**3. Sampling adequacy and correlations**

- N_eb was significantly and positively correlated with the number of distinct larval groups sampled per year (k), with r = 0.94 and P = 0.005. This suggests that collecting larvae across the entire spawning season matters for capturing genetic variation.
- N_eb was not correlated with N_c (r = 0.07, P = 0.47). Other correlations approached but did not reach significance in permutation tests.
- The authors conclude that current larval sampling encompasses enough genetic diversity to preclude extremely low N_e/N_c values.

**4. Management implications**

- Repatriated fish are contributing genetically to annual cohorts, and they make up a growing share of the spawning stock, from about 2002 onward.
- Annual N_eb estimates should be useful for detecting declines in the number of breeding females.
- Simultaneous genetic and demographic monitoring gives insights that neither approach yields alone.

**5. Methodological caveats**

- The semelparity assumption behind the N_eb estimate does not hold for this iteroparous species, so the estimate may be biased unless age-class overlap in reproductive contribution is minimal (Hedrick 2005b).
- Only six cohort pairs were available, which limits statistical power.
- No published model yet links N_eb and N_eg in iteroparous species with overlapping generations.
