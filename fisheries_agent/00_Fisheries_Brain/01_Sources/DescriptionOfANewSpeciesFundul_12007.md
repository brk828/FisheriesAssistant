# Fundulus Julisia Sp. Nov. and the Redescription of Fundulus Albolineatus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes a new topminnow, [[Fundulus Julisia]] (Barrens topminnow), from springs on the [[Barrens Plateau]] of middle Tennessee. It also redescribes the poorly known [[Fundulus Albolineatus]] (whiteline topminnow) from Huntsville, Alabama, which is probably extinct. The authors diagnose the subgenus [[Subgenus Xenisma]], which contains five species. The new species is known from a single spring-run population, estimated at no more than 200 to 500 individuals, and is considered one of the most critically endangered fishes in eastern North America.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic history.** Specimens of the Barrens topminnow were collected during pre-impoundment surveys by the [[Tennessee Valley Authority]] in the late 1930s. Three collections from 1937 were recognized as a distinct species by C. L. Hubbs in 1938, but they were catalogued at the University of Michigan as *F. albolineatus*. The species was not collected again until the mid-1960s. Until recently, it was considered conspecific with *F. albolineatus*, but the authors concluded that they are two species.

**Subgenus Xenisma.** The authors diagnose [[Subgenus Xenisma]] as a valid, monophyletic group containing [[Fundulus Catenatus]], [[Fundulus Stellifer]], [[Fundulus Rathbuni]], *F. albolineatus* and *F. julisia*. Diagnostic characters include:
- Absence of a broad lateral band, a subocular "teardrop" blotch and vertical bars in all stages of both sexes.
- Nuptial males with [[Nuptial Tubercles]] on the dorsal and anal fins, cheeks and opercles, and mid-lateral scales (3–5 per scale).
- Vertebrae 33–38; caudal rays 14–19; branchiostegal rays 6 (rarely 5 in *rathbuni*); total gill rakers 4–7.
- Dorsal fin origin posterior to anal fin origin.
- Lateral line scales 34–52; dorsal fin rays 9–15; anal fin rays 10–17.

**Fundulus julisia (Barrens topminnow).** The holotype is an adult male (USNM 225997, 53.8 mm SL) collected on 1 April 1973 from a spring tributary to West Fork Hickory Creek, Summitville, [[Coffee County Tennessee]]. The authors describe 10 adult males and 10 adult females. Nuptial males have pale blue-green to yellow-green sides, scattered orange to dark red spots, and an iridescent white to pale gold mid-dorsal stripe. Breeding males of *julisia* lack the interrupted whitish streaks seen in *albolineatus*, and they lack tubercles on the paired fins and caudal peduncle. Compared with *albolineatus*, *julisia* has a lower modal lateral line count (38 or 39 vs. 40) and a higher modal vertebral count (35 vs. 34).

**Fundulus albolineatus (whiteline topminnow).** The species was described by Gilbert (1891) from the [[Big Spring]] and [[Spring Creek]] system in Huntsville, [[Madison County Alabama]]. The lectotype is USNM 125055, a 70 mm SL male collected 27–28 May 1889 by P. H. Kirsch. The authors know of only 25 extant specimens, all faded to an almost uniform brown. Males reach 78 mm SL and females 75 mm SL. The authors note that Gilbert's report that the dorsal and anal fin origins were at the same level appears to be an error, since the dorsal origin is slightly posterior to the anal origin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat of F. julisia.** The species occurs in springs, spring runs and small streams that are strongly influenced by groundwater. [[Watercress]] (*Nasturtium officinale*) forms lush growths at the type locality and at the Little Duck River. The spring run is about 2–3 m wide and 10–30 cm deep, with moderate current. Chert gravel dominates the swifter areas, while silt and mud occur in the calmer, vegetated pools. Associated species include *Hemitremia flammea*, *Chologaster agassizi* and *Etheostoma luteovinctum*. A low dam built by a landowner to create a trout pool has, unintentionally, provided favorable habitat. Trout disappeared downstream, but the pool holds several schools of topminnows. The spring run flows about 400 m into West Fork Hickory Creek, which receives effluent from a trailer court.

**Habitat of F. albolineatus.** The only known habitat was Big Spring and its outflow, Spring Creek, in Huntsville. Gilbert (1891) described the stream as about 18 feet wide, fed by a single spring, and full of fishes. The spring has since been heavily altered. It has been pumped dry several times, its banks and run lined with cement, and the outflow impounded and stocked with carp and goldfish. Armstrong and Williams (1971) found no *albolineatus* in two later collections from Big Spring. Native fishes they reported there include *Semotilus atromaculatus*, *Rhinichthys atratulus*, *Campostoma anomalum*, *Catostomus commersoni* and *Etheostoma squamiceps*.

**Regional endemism.** Spring habitats near the lower bend of the Tennessee River and the upper Mobile Basin contain at least five other endemic species. These include *Cottus pygmaeus*, *Etheostoma nuchale*, *Etheostoma ditrema*, *Elassoma* sp. and *Etheostoma tuscumbia*. Species of the subgenus Xenisma are almost entirely restricted to fresh waters above the [[Fall Line]] in the southeastern and south-central United States. The exceptions are *F. stellifer* (coastal plain populations in the Alabama River drainage) and *F. catenatus* (tributaries of the Mississippi River in southwest Mississippi).

**Biology.** Spawning appears to occur in early spring, based on the presence of tuberculate males in collections. [[Howell and Black]] reared the species in aquaria at room temperature, feeding it frozen brine shrimp and Tetramin. Spawning occurred at 20–22 °C. Adult males were very aggressive and killed conspecifics, and on one occasion quickly killed a *Fundulus olivaceus* of comparable size. Fecundity is unknown, but a single pair produced about 25 eggs per day, which adhered to the spawning substrate.

## Key Findings

- **A new species is described.** *Fundulus julisia* differs from *F. albolineatus* in lacking the whitish streaks on the posterior body and in having different modal lateral line and vertebral counts (38–39 vs. 40; 35 vs. 34).
- **Subgenus Xenisma is diagnosed.** The five included species share a suite of pigment, tubercle, meristic and sensory canal characters, supporting its recognition as a monophyletic subgenus.
- **Karyotype.** Somatic counts from gill and scale tissue gave 2n = 48 in both sexes, with no sexual dimorphism. Meiotic counts from testes gave n = 24. Chromosomes are acrocentric except for one pair of small submetacentrics. The authors defer detailed karyotype comparisons to specialists.
- **Conservation status.** *F. julisia* is restricted to the spring run at the type locality, with an estimated population of 200–500 individuals. It has not been collected at the Little Duck River since 1964 and has disappeared from Doak Springs and Lake Ovaca, where only 1938 collections are known. A 1975 survey of about 40 small streams in Coffee County found no additional populations.
- **Conservation action.** On 13 August 1980, the Tennessee Wildlife Resources Agency, [[The Nature Conservancy]] and landowner Joseph R. Banks signed a [[Cooperative Conservation Agreement]] to maintain the population and monitor its habitat. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service supported further searches for populations.
- **New population.** After the manuscript was prepared, a second population was found in October 1981 in Meadow Creek, West Fork Hickory Creek system, about 2.6 air miles north-northeast of the type locality.
- **Fundulus albolineatus is probably extinct.** Repeated collecting in Big Spring and Moss Spring has failed to find it, and the authors consider extinction more likely than an extant population near the lower bend of the Tennessee River.
- **Ecology and captive behavior.** The species is groundwater-dependent and occurs in watercress-vegetated pools. Captive males are highly aggressive, and spawning was initiated by photoperiod manipulation at 20–22 °C.
- **Etymology.** The epithet *julisia* derives from the Cherokee *amjulisi* (watercress) and *atsat* (fish). The common name "Barrens topminnow" was suggested by J. S. Ramsey and refers to the Barrens Plateau of the Highland Rim.
