# Central Arizona Project and Salt River Project Interconnection: Finding of No Significant Impact

#ichthyology #aquatic-ecology #non-native-fish

## Executive Summary

This note covers two linked documents. The first is an Arizona State Clearinghouse review request sent to state agencies, which asks whether a proposed project is consistent with agency goals, duplicates other programs, and complies with applicable laws. The second, and the main substance, is a [[Finding of No Significant Impact]] (FONSI) issued by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] for the [[Central Arizona Project]] / [[Salt River Project]] (CAP/SRP) Interconnection Facility. The Interconnect would move water from the CAP aqueduct into SRP's canal system. The main environmental concern is that non-native fish from the Colorado River could enter the Salt and Verde River systems. Reclamation concluded that the preferred option, with its mitigation commitments, would not significantly affect the quality of the human environment, so no [[Environmental Impact Statement]] would be prepared.

## Background & Description

- **Project purpose:** Provide a means of conveying water from the CAP aqueduct to the SRP water distribution system. Diversions would occur at an existing SRP turnout structure near [[Granite Reef Diversion Dam]], feeding the Arizona and South Canals.
- **Lead agency and document:** Reclamation (lead federal agency) required SRP, as project proponent, to prepare an [[Environmental Assessment]] (EA) under the [[National Environmental Policy Act]]. Reclamation used the EA to decide whether an EIS was needed or a FONSI could be issued.
- **Alternatives considered in detail:**
  - *Alternative 1 (Proposed Action):* Two options were analyzed.
    - *Option A (preferred and selected):* Direct delivery to the Arizona and South Canals, with a river crossing downstream of the diversion dam.
    - *Option B:* Same as Option A, except the river crossing would be upstream of the diversion dam.
    - Both options include a discharge outlet to the Salt River downstream of Granite Reef for emergency evacuation of the CAP aqueduct or future groundwater recharge deliveries. Construction would use either a pressure pipe with regulating valves, or a combined pressure pipe and open channel with regulating valves and radial gates.
  - *Alternative 2 (No Action):* The Interconnect would not be built, and no CAP water would enter SRP canals.
- **Review process:** A draft EA was sent to 48 individuals, groups, and agencies. Ten comment letters were received. Consulted parties included the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], the [[Arizona State Historic Preservation Office]], the Tonto National Forest, and the Maricopa County Flood Control District.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The key ecological issue is the **introduction of undesirable non-native fish** from the Colorado River into the Salt and Verde River systems through the Interconnect. Two mitigation measures address this risk:

1. **Downstream delivery requirement:** CAP water deliveries to SRP canals must occur downstream of Granite Reef Diversion Dam.
2. **Electrical fish barriers:** Barriers in the Arizona and South Canals, between the Interconnect outlet and the diversion dam, are paired with water velocities and drops that prevent upstream movement of non-native fish.

Reclamation committed to a **monitoring program** (described on page 49 of the final EA), carried out with SRP, the Fish and Wildlife Service, and the Arizona Game and Fish Department. The program will check whether the electrical barriers stop upstream movement of non-native fish in canal reaches between the barriers and the spillway gates of Granite Reef Diversion Dam. If the barriers prove ineffective, further investigation will be required.

Other environmental considerations:
- **Threatened and endangered species:** Reclamation committed to avoiding or reducing impacts, with particular attention to [[Bald Eagle]] populations.
- **Cultural resources:** Mitigation actions for features 1, 2, and 3 of Site AZ 9:26:2 (ASU) are specified on pages 50 and 51 of the EA and will be fully implemented by SRP.
- **Floodplains and wetlands:** Floodplain impacts were evaluated under [[Executive Order 11999]]. No wetlands will be affected. SRP must obtain a Section 404 permit from the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]] before construction.
- **Public health and safety:** Impacts are mitigated through the safety measures described in the EA.
- **Cumulative effects:** Impacts on wildlife, habitat, listed species, human safety, vegetation, and water quality were judged mitigated below significance.

## Key Findings

- **Finding:** Option A, with the committed mitigation, will not significantly affect the quality of the human environment, so no EIS is required.
- **Basis for the finding (six factors):**
  1. All significant environmental concerns raised by the public and agencies were explored and analyzed, and mitigation commitments were developed in response.
  2. Electrical fish barriers, combined with water velocities and drops, prevent upstream movement of non-native fish. The original proposal was changed to add these measures.
  3. Public health and safety impacts are mitigated.
  4. No adverse effects on cultural resources are expected, provided the specified actions are carried out.
  5. Floodplain impacts were evaluated, no wetlands will be affected, and a 404 permit is required.
  6. Cumulative effects are mitigated to a minimum, insignificant level.
- **Decision:** Reclamation has concurred with the FONSI. The document is dated in the OCR text; the exact date is partly illegible.
- **Mitigation enforcement:** Commitments will be incorporated into contract specifications or interagency agreements, and implemented through project administration.
- **Clearinghouse review:** The state review form asks whether the project is consistent with agency goals, duplicates other programs, adequately addresses effects on target populations, complies with applicable laws, adversely affects existing programs, or conflicts with agency rules. Responses were due within 17 working days of the request date. Reviewer responses are mostly illegible in the OCR text.
