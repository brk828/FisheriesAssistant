# State Fishery Agencies Versus the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service: A 1994 Policy Letter

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This is a 1994 letter from [[Steven M. Huffaker]], writing on behalf of the [[Western Association Of Fish And Wildlife Agencies]] (WAFWA) Fish Chiefs, to [[Jerry Conley]], President of the [[International Association Of Fish And Wildlife Agencies]]. It is a formal complaint that the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] (the Service) is undermining state fishery management. The letter claims federal policy is being set without input from state agencies, that state stocking and non-native species programs are being restricted, and that proposed uses of sportfish funds and reviews of refuge fishing and hatchery systems are troubling. It ends with a warning that the state fisheries chiefs may seek a "divorce" from current Service leadership unless the relationship is repaired. The letter contains no new biological data; its content is political and administrative.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Correspondence (letter), not research. It is dated August 9, 1994.
- **Author:** Steven M. Huffaker, on behalf of WAFWA Fish Chiefs.
- **Recipient:** Jerry Conley, President, International Association of Fish and Wildlife Agencies, Board of Directors (also addressed in care of the [[American Sportfishing Association]]).
- **Copies to:** Norville Prosser (ASA, Washington DC), Noreen Clough (USFWS, Washington DC), and Harv Forsgren (USFS, Washington DC).
- **Context:** The letter refers to discussions with other western "fish chiefs" and to the program at a bassmaster tournament, which the text gives as the "1998 Bassmaster Classic." That year is later than the letter's 1994 date. It is reported here as written, and the discrepancy may reflect a scanning or drafting error.
- **Author's state:** The writer's state is Idaho, which the letter says has "excellent relations" with local Service personnel.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter does not report field data, species populations, or habitat measurements. Its environmental content is limited to policy concerns:

- **Stocking and non-indigenous species:** Federal agencies are reported to require federal approval for state fish stocking, including stocking on federal land. Introduction or enhancement of [[Non-Indigenous Species]] in altered habitats that still hold remnant populations of listed species is cited as a source of conflict.
- **Biodiversity and ecosystem management:** The Service's [[Biodiversity]] and [[Ecosystem Management]] initiative is described as a source of tension. The authors argue that rare-species conflicts with stocking exist mainly "in the minds of people" with limited understanding of the fish and habitats involved.
- **Hatcheries and genetics:** A review of the [[National Hatchery System]] is described as following non-indigenous species control legislation and "genetic micromanagement attitudes."
- **Refuge use:** A broad environmental review of whether fishing and hunting are "appropriate" on [[National Wildlife Refuges]] is described as "disconcerting."
- **Water and habitat:** The authors state that the public most committed to clean air, clean water, and healthy habitat for fish and wildlife may be alienated by the current course.

## Key Findings

1. **Perceived federal overreach.** The states believe the Service views state fish management as at odds with its biodiversity agenda. The letter cites federal approval requirements for state stocking and species enhancement as evidence.

2. **Proposed diversion of sportfish funds.** The writers object to the Service's desire to shift part of the [[Dingell-Johnson And Wallop-Breaux Sportfish Restoration Fund]] from its historic use to help fund the [[National Biological Survey]] and new biodiversity programs.

3. **Refuge and hatchery reviews.** The review of fishing and hunting on refuges and the review of the National Hatchery System are described as unwelcome.

4. **Top-down policy without consultation.** Initiatives are said to be announced by federal "Partners" before state agencies can comment. The letter describes goals as "dictated," problems as "dismissed," and evaluation as limited to a small group with "the ear of the current administration."

5. **Mixed signals.** The Service says it "strongly supports sport fisheries" while, according to the letter, restricting state stocking and enhancement programs.

6. **Claimed expertise and authority.** The states describe themselves as holding the best knowledge of state fisheries and say they are "the final authority" over fish and aquatic life not listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]].

7. **Section 6 as a partnership tool.** The letter argues that [[Section 6 Of The Endangered Species Act]] could make states full and equal partners in managing listed species, not just a funding mechanism. It calls on the Service to use this capability.

8. **Field versus leadership.** The authors report good relations with local Service staff in Idaho and most places. They place the main problem with Service leadership "inside the beltway" and suggest that field staff may be equally confused.

9. **Recommended remedies.** The letter asks the Service to:
   - Call a "time out" and regroup.
   - Formally include state fishery managers in decision-making.
   - Rebuild partnerships in the field, not only in Washington DC.
   - Recognize that a [[White House Ecosystem Management Task Force]] does not resolve conflicts among managers, anglers, and federal staff.

10. **Escalation threat.** If the Service does not respond, the writers say the rift will deepen and the states may seek a "divorce" from current leadership and "find a new partner."

11. **Overall tone.** The authors describe the letter as "harsh, but constructive," and say state fisheries chiefs are "ready, willing, and able" to help improve the situation.
