# Colorado River Recovery Management Group Meeting Summary and FY-91 Work Plan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #gila-chubs-squawfish

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of program records from the [[Recovery Implementation Program]] for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It contains the final summary of the July 11, 1990 [[Management Group]] meeting, the draft [[FY-91 Work Plan]] (dated June 28, 1990), several attachments on funding, reorganization, and propagation, and Section 7 consultation records. The main subjects are the FY-91 budget of about $2.56 million, the allocation of [[Section 7 Funds]], the handling of a FY-90 [[Gila Taxonomy Study]] budget shortfall, proposed reorganization of the program's committee structure, and a request to waive the [[Fish and Wildlife Service Overhead Charge]] on Bureau transfer funds.

## Background & Description

The [[Recovery Implementation Program]] coordinates recovery of four endangered Colorado River fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. Roundtail chub are discussed as part of the taxonomic question in the [[Gila Taxonomy Study]]. The program was adopted by a cooperative agreement signed in January 1988 by the states of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, the [[Western Area Power Administration]], and the Department of the Interior. Water development and environmental interests also participate.

The program is governed by the [[Recovery Implementation Committee]], chaired at the time by [[Galen Buterbaugh]]. Its working bodies were the [[Management Group]] (led by Program Director [[John Hamill]]) and the [[Technical Group]], which ranked about 85 projects submitted for FY-91 funding. Major participants named in the records include [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Colorado Division of Wildlife]], [[Colorado Water Conservation Board]], and the [[Upper Basin Water Users]] (represented by [[Tom Pitts]]).

The work plan was drafted by the Technical and Management groups and is scheduled for approval by the Implementation Committee on August 22, 1990. The stated goal is to have all activities contracted and funded by the end of the first quarter of FY-91.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The documents focus on flow management and habitat for the rare fishes. Key elements include:

- **Flaming Gorge operations:** Studies through FY-90 produced seasonal flow recommendations for spring, summer/fall, and winter. The summer/fall recommendations were best supported by long-term data. The consensus was that all seasonal recommendations need further refinement, particularly for spring and winter, and that effects on the nonnative fish community must be addressed.
- **Instream flows:** Water acquisition for the [[Yampa River]] and the [[15-Mile Reach]] of the Colorado River is a high priority. The 15-mile reach is considered a somewhat artificial designation by some participants.
- **Yampa reservoir siting:** A reservoir compatible with endangered fish needs, providing 50,000 to 150,000 acre-feet of storage, was studied as a potential route to acquiring [[Juniper-Cross Mountain Water Rights]].
- **Aspinall consultation:** The Service argued that flow regulation from [[Aspinall Unit]] affects about 170 miles of occupied habitat and is central to recovery decisions. The Colorado squawfish population in the mainstem Colorado River was described as apparently declining.
- **Nonnative species:** Studies address competition between warmwater game fish and native fishes, predation, and competition between Colorado squawfish and red shiner. The [[Kenney Reservoir]] stocking evaluation addresses Colorado squawfish as a sportfish.
- **Propagation:** Hatchery and refuge work is conducted at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and the [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]] propagation facility. Razorback sucker genetics work is underway.
- **Rare fishes propagation strategy:** Attachment 10 classifies populations in historic habitats by status codes (S = natural population sustaining, R = relict, U = unknown, A/I = adequate or inadequate recruitment, O = occasional capture, X = not present) and assigns propagation priorities. Mainstream Green River and Yampa populations are an example of the classification.

## Key Findings

**Budget and funding**

- The recommended FY-91 Recovery Program budget is $2.5M, described as a 4.8 percent increase based on the 1989 Consumer Price Index over the FY-90 budget of $2.39M.
- Stated funding sources are: Fish and Wildlife Service $654,000 (the Management Group discussion and the draft letter use $624,000, with a $30,000 shortfall explained by Congressional appropriation limits); Bureau of Reclamation $1,636,500; Colorado $113,500; Utah $79,700; Wyoming $25,200; total $2,557,900.
- Work plan totals by element: Program Management $451.5K; Habitat Management (instream flows) $1,057.1K; Artificial Propagation (genetics management) $347.0K; Nonnative Fish/Sportfish $86.4K; Research, Monitoring, and Data Management $615.9K.
- The work plan's Table 3.1 lists the projects. The Management Group discussed inflation adjustments and added water users as a funding source.
- The FY-91 discretionary funds are shown as $(278.2)K, with $712.0K projected for FY-92 and $1,235.3K for FY-93 in the table. These figures are reported as given in the draft table.

**Section 7 Funds and Redlands**

- A stipulation directs $300K of Section 7 funds toward fish passage work at Redlands. The Management Group asked whether this could be modified. The Service agreed to report at the next Implementation Committee meeting.
- The Management Group agreed to fund the Yampa River flow quantification ($58,800) and 15-mile reach water availability studies with Section 7 funds. It also agreed to add the Aspinall consultation work plan and the Colorado squawfish genetics survey to the FY-91 work plan.
- Hamill's memo noted that Section 7 funds are not earning interest and lose value by at least 5 percent per year. He recommended using $50,000 to $100,000 for water acquisition projects in FY-91.

**Gila Taxonomy and Larval Fish Lab**

- The Bureau may be unable to obligate about $100K of FY-90 Gila contract money before the fiscal year ends. The Group recommended asking [[Roland Robinson]] to obligate the funds in FY-90 or restore them to the FY-91 budget. The Bureau later agreed to roll the money into the FY-92 budget.
- The Bureau and Service exceeded the Larval Fish Lab contract by about $26K, of which $12K came from Grand Junction CRFP work. The Service agreed to cover its share.

**Overhead waiver**

- The Service charges a 17.65 percent overhead on Bureau transfer funds. Overhead amounts were $50,601 in 1989 and $118,256 in 1990.
- The draft letter to Secretary Lujan asks for a complete waiver. The letter estimates overhead could exceed $1 million over the 15-year program. It was approved by non-Federal members and will be signed by the governors' representatives.

**Yampa and 15-mile reach studies**

- The Yampa River Feasibility Study ($106,200 total) is jointly funded by the Colorado Water Conservation Board, the Colorado River Water Conservation District, and the Recovery Program. The Colorado Water Conservation Board can receive up to $60,000 in credit over three years.
- The 15-mile reach flow recommendations study ($75,400) will evaluate habitat availability and develop year-round recommendations. The Service agreed to evaluate expanding the work to the 18-mile reach.
- The 15-mile reach water availability study ($47,000) will be contracted by the Colorado Water Conservation Board.

**Major project costs (FY-91)**

- Flaming Gorge cooperative research: $308,800 (combined study ending 1995; outyear cost $1,120,500).
- Colorado squawfish backwater habitat mapping: $157,000.
- Gila taxonomy and field study: $252,000.
- Standardized monitoring: $113,200 (outyear cost $100,000 per year).
- Implementation of the propagation and genetics plan: $111,800.
- Razorback genetics and propagation: $126,200.

**Recovery goals and priorities**

- Quantitative recovery goals were not funded in FY-91 because more baseline information is needed. Projects that collect basic data are funded instead. The Technical Group ranked the goals proposal low because the work was considered paid for in FY-90.
- Hamill noted that several projects once considered high priority, including the White River flow recommendations, recovery goals, and Aspinall prerequisites, were dropped from the FY-91 plan despite FY-90 funding.

**Reorganization**

- The reorganization memo proposes abolishing the Technical Group and creating a Management Committee plus three technical committees (Biology, Water Acquisition, and Information and Education).
- Decisions in the meeting: consultants will not chair committees; committee staff are encouraged not to chair meetings; credit for committee participation is limited to designated technical committee members; the Sediment subcommittee falls under the Biology Committee.
- The Biological Researchers Advisory Group's future role remained unresolved.

**Propagation and river trip**

- The Service reported that razorback sucker genetics work was underway and refuge populations and broodstocks were being developed. Holt Williamson will present a propagation update at the next meeting.
- The river trip (August 23–25, 1990, Westwater Canyon to Black Rocks) had 36 registered participants for 35 spots. Angela Kantola was assigned to the Utah research boat if no one cancels.

**Section 7 consultations**

- The attached Section 7 consultation table lists water depletion consultations after January 1988 across Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming. Projects include coal mines, Bureau and Bureau of Land Management actions, and Forest Service plans. Depletion charges were assessed at $10 per acre-foot, and several projects were exempt. Pending consultations include the Animas-La Plata, Flaming Gorge operations, Sandstone Reservoir, and Price-San Rafael projects.
