# Colorado River Endangered Fishes Recovery: Ruedi Reservoir Releases Lease Proposal

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1989 Colorado Water Conservation Board (CWCB) memorandum and attached draft contract propose a legally enforceable mechanism for delivering 10,000 acre-feet of stored water from the regulatory capacity of [[Ruedi Reservoir]] to the 15 Mile Reach of the [[Colorado River]] below Palisade. The releases are a conservation measure required by the [[Biological Opinion]] of June 15, 1987, which found that water sales from Ruedi and Green Mountain reservoirs could jeopardize the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. The proposed solution is a three-party lease among the [[United States]], the Board, and the [[Grand Valley Water Users Association]] and [[Orchard Mesa Irrigation District]], with enforcement through Colorado water court.

## Background & Description

The memorandum (Agenda Item 8a, July 10-11, 1989 Board meeting, from Director Bill McDonald) describes the regulatory framework for the release program:

- **Scope of releases:** 5,000 acre-feet withheld from sale annually, plus another 5,000 acre-feet available on average four years in five through modified reservoir operations.
- **Study area:** The 15 Mile Reach runs from the Grand Valley Irrigation Company diversion dam downstream to the confluence with the Gunnison River.
- **Legal basis:** Section 37-92-102(3), Colorado Revised Statutes, allows the Board to acquire water by lease for minimum streamflows to preserve the natural environment, and makes contracts with the Board enforceable in court as water matters.
- **Governing documents:** The [[Fryingpan-Arkansas Project]] [[Operating Principles]] (House Document 130), Article 6(b), and the 1959 supplemental decree (Garfield County District Court, Civil Action No. 4613) both permit sale or lease of Ruedi storage to Colorado users for any purpose recognized by U.S. law.
- **Capital cost:** The regulatory capacity of Ruedi Reservoir is allocated $9,222,000 (including interest during construction), reimbursable within 50 years from August 1969.

The draft contract (dated 7/3/89) is a standard Bureau of Reclamation 40-year lease, terminable earlier under defined conditions.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document centers on three endangered Colorado River fishes named in the Biological Opinion: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Humpback Chub]]. Releases are intended for "occupied endangered fish habitat" in the 15 Mile Reach, and the contract specifies that water be used "for the benefit of the Colorado squawfish." Releases are limited to July through October, and their amount and timing are set by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] Contracting Officer after consultation with the Board and the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].

The text contains no population data, sampling results, habitat measurements, or flow-response studies for these species. Ecological benefit is asserted in general terms only.

Water policy context includes the [[Green Mountain Reservoir]] alternative source, [[Senate Document 80]] operating rights, and the [[Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the Upper Colorado River Basin]] (September 29, 1987).

## Key Findings

- **Legal enforceability resolved:** The Board can contract with the United States under section 37-92-102(3) and make the agreement enforceable in Water Division No. 5 court (article 16), addressing the Service's concern about a legally enforceable mechanism.
- **Lease rather than instream flow right:** No instream flow appropriation is involved; the Board leases Ruedi storage, and the Ruedi water right must include instream flow fishery purposes. The Board had previously read the 1958 decree's "piscatorial purposes" as limited to the reservoir impoundment, but the 1959 supplemental decree language is used as the basis for the proposal.
- **Three-party structure:** Water leased to the Board for instream use would first be available for diversion through the [[Grand Valley Powerplant]] for the District and Association, which is non-consumptive. Return flows have no legal standing as instream flow rights.
- **No charge but higher unit costs:** The Board pays nothing for the water (article 4), but the capital costs allocable to regulatory capacity are not reduced, so per-unit costs to other water service contractors rise.
- **Delivery terms:** Releases occur at the Ruedi outlet works (article 5); the Board must call for administration through the 15 Mile Reach (article 7); and the United States controls amount and timing (article 8).
- **Shortage provisions:** The 5,000 acre-feet withheld from sale is protected from reductions unless demand reaches 40,000 acre-feet; the second 5,000 acre-feet absorbs shortages first (article 13).
- **Term:** The contract runs from January 1, 1990, to the earliest of December 31, 2029, or a set of listing, delisting, or alternative-measure events (article 2).
- **Environmental compliance:** The District, Association, and Board need no federal permits, and implementation satisfies the first conservation measure for Round II water sales (article 14).
- **Commission role:** The [[Fryingpan-Arkansas Commission]] is asked to adopt a resolution finding the lease consistent with the Operating Principles, under its power to collect data and make findings of fact (Operating Principles paragraph 19).
- **Recommendations:** The Board is asked to (1) recommend an interim Grand Valley Powerplant delivery agreement, (2) request lease negotiations with the United States, (3) authorize requests for agency recommendations under section 37-92-102(3), and (4) authorize the Director to propose the Commission resolution. A formal Board determination is targeted for the September 1989 meeting.
