# Comments on Virgin River Environmental Assessment for Red Shiner Removal

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #Cyprinidae #invasive-species #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This internal memorandum provides critical review of a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Environmental Assessment (EA) proposing rotenone treatment of the [[Virgin River]] to eradicate introduced [[Red Shiner]] (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) and facilitate recovery of the endangered [[Woundfin]] (*Plagopterus argentissimus*). While supporting the project's urgency, the reviewer identifies major deficiencies: inadequate salvage provisions for non-target native fishes, poor documentation, insufficient survey protocols that could ensure project failure, and exclusion of key scientists from the review process. The memo emphasizes that this single intervention will not solve all woundfin recovery needs and advocates for expanded salvage operations and more rigorous treatment protocols.

## Background & Description

The FWS EA proposes renovation (chemical reclamation) of a selected reach of the [[Virgin River]] to remove the invasive [[Red Shiner]], which threatens the endangered [[Woundfin]], [[Virgin River Chub]] (*Gila robusta*), and [[Virgin Spinedace]] (*Lepidomeda mollispinis*). The treatment method involves [[Rotenone]] application followed by detoxification procedures. The EA proposes survey sampling to determine areas of red shiner occurrence prior to directing treatment efforts.

The salvage plan targets only three native species (woundfin, chub, and spinedace), with other native Virgin River fishes excluded from collection and relocation efforts. The rationale provided centers on limited manpower and holding/transport facility constraints. The EA references a 1982 California Department of Fish and Game study implicating red shiner in declines of [[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]], though the reviewer questions this citation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Virgin River]] system contains multiple native cyprinid species, some potentially representing unique, undescribed taxa. The reviewer warns that non-target natives dismissed as "widespread and abundant" may become tomorrow's threatened and endangered (TRE) species if current attitudes persist. The ecosystem includes the main river channel, intermittent tributaries with standing water, and ponds that discharge to the river or tributaries during high flows.

Habitat quality within the selected reclamation area is presented as superior for woundfin compared to downstream reaches, though the reviewer questions whether this can be documented. The EA claims Virgin River invertebrates are "hardy with respect to rotenone" and "accustomed to catastrophic poisoning," typically reestablishing within a few months—assertions the reviewer dismisses as inadequately documented.

The document notes that in Arizona, rotenone can only be applied by state-licensed applicators, a regulatory constraint affecting project implementation.

## Key Findings

**Project Support with Reservations**: The reviewer completely supports red shiner removal despite poor EA quality and disagreement with the preferred alternative, citing "overwhelming" and "immediate" need for woundfin recovery action. However, strong concern exists that stakeholders may view this single project as solving all woundfin problems when "further recovery activities obviously are needed."

**Critical Protocol Deficiency**: The proposal to determine red shiner occurrence through survey sampling and use results to direct treatment is characterized as "ludicrous." The reviewer states: "Red shiner eradication cannot be assured unless all waters of the Virgin and confluent systems (including intermittent tributaries with standing water, ponds that discharge during high flow to the river or tributaries, etc) are treated as though they in fact contain the shiner, whether or not sampling demonstrates their presence." This concern is identified as critical to project success.

**Inadequate Salvage Provisions**: The EA fails to provide for salvage of native fishes other than the three target species. The reviewer's personnel and equipment "can readily be diverted during the survey/salvage period to assist in collection, transport, and release to safety of non-target native fishes," and suggests making this formal offer. Involvement of university museum personnel is noted as a potential mechanism to secure broader salvage opportunities.

**Distribution and Review Process Failures**: The EA distribution failed to include the Desert Fishes Council (DFC) and knowledgeable non-agency scientists. A Butterbaugh letter of 14 May 1988 regarding such distributions provides "a strong position from which to make a statement of the service's blatant disregard for its professed commitments."

**Inadequate Documentation**: The EA lacks proper documentation throughout, including unverified habitat quality claims, unsupported statements about invertebrate resilience to rotenone, and questionable literature citations. The document gives percentages on treatment efficiency (page 11) without source attribution.

**Detoxification Concerns**: Detoxification procedures require "the same careful planning as treatment" but receive only brief discussion "inadequate to insure that downstream resources are not destroyed or severely impacted." Arizona and Nevada are expected to raise strong objections.

**Post-Treatment Verification**: The EA does not specify who will conduct "extensive sampling" to verify treatment success. The reviewer notes that "past performance does not suggest the service, state of Utah, or woundfin recovery team has the perspicacity to do the job right, especially considering their apparent concern with cost."

**Site Selection Concerns**: The reviewer expresses concern that site selection was based on "bureaucratic and logistic convenience rather than comparative physical/biological attributes of various areas," which is "ass backwards." A comprehensive habitat value assessment throughout the Virgin River would be prudent for selecting optimal renovation areas.

**Reintroduction Risk**: Brief discussion addresses bait bucket transfer of shiners back into reclaimed areas, but inadequate consideration is given to curtailing such activities. Public education presents a "two-edged sword" as opponents might use knowledge that a few shiners could undermine the entire effort.

**Alternative Analysis Deficiencies**: Dismissal of alternative approaches (particularly item 4, page 12) coupled with a barrier dam at the gorge is characterized as a "cop-out," with cost considerations apparently overriding biological optimization. The document contains internal contradictions regarding cultural resources (page 20 versus page 16).

**Biological Misunderstandings**: The statement that cohort loss would be recovered within a year or so "demonstrates lack of smarts," as recovery time depends on life span and other factors. At best, a lost cohort would not be replaced until one life-span later.
