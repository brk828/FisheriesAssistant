# Letter from David L. Propst to Robert Rush Miller on Chihuahua Chub Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #Cyprinodontidae #Chihuahua-Chub #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1990 personal letter from David L. Propst, an Endangered Species Biologist with the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, to Dr. Robert Rush Miller, a preeminent ichthyologist at the University of Michigan Museum of Zoology. The letter summarizes a field survey conducted in Chihuahua, Mexico, aimed at assessing the status of the [[Chihuahua Chub]] and other native fish species. The survey revisited historical sampling localities used by Miller in 1978. While specimen retention was not authorized by Mexican environmental authorities (SEDUE), the correspondence raises several taxonomic questions regarding species identification within the genera *Gila*, *Cyprinella*, and *Cyprinodon*, and notes a likely decline in native fish status since the previous survey.

## Background & Description

Specific morphological details are limited in this correspondence, but the following field observations are noted:

* **[[Cyprinella]] specimens (Ojo Galeana, Santa Maria drainage):** Ripe male specimens were observed to exhibit an "intense tangerine orange" coloration, suggesting active breeding condition at the time of collection.
* **[[Gila]] sp.:** No specific morphological descriptions provided, though the author possesses photographic documentation from each drainage/basin surveyed, intended to support future taxonomic and biochemical analysis.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

* **Primary Habitat:** The survey focused on the Rio Santa Maria drainage and the Bustillos Basin in Chihuahua, Mexico, specifically spring-fed habitats locally known as "ojos" (e.g., Ojo Galeana).
* **Biogeography:** The [[Chihuahua Chub]] and associated native fauna occupy isolated desert stream and spring systems. The letter suggests a comparative ecological link between the *Gila* populations of the Chihuahuan basins and the Mimbres drainage in New Mexico, implying a shared evolutionary or biogeographic history relevant to endangered species management.
* **Population Status:** The author explicitly notes that "the status of Chihuahua chub and other native species has probably declined" compared to observations made during the 1978 survey by R.R. Miller.

## Key Findings

* **Survey Scope:** Propst successfully sampled the majority of localities surveyed by Miller in 1978, plus additional sites, providing a valuable comparative dataset for tracking population trends of the [[Chihuahua Chub]].
* **Taxonomic Uncertainty (Cyprinella):** Propst questions whether all *Cyprinella* specimens collected should be classified as *C. ormosus*. He references [[Salvador Contreras]]'s treatment of the Rio Santa Maria form as a distinct subspecies, *Notropis lutrensis santamaria* (citing A.D. Espinoza's 1988 M.S. thesis), creating a discrepancy with the standard classification.
* **Potential Range Extension/Introduction:** The letter notes that neither Smith and Miller (1986) nor Mayden (1989) attributed any *Cyprinella* species to the Bustillos Basin. Propst raises the hypothesis that specimens found there might represent a **recent introduction** rather than a native, previously undocumented population.
* **Taxonomic Uncertainty (Cyprinodon):** Identification of *Cyprinodon* specimens remains tentative; Propst planned to consult with [[Tony Echelle]] for species-level confirmation.
* **Future Research Proposal:** A primary goal outlined for future permitted work is to conduct combined biochemical and traditional taxonomic analyses on *Gila* species across the Chihuahua and Mimbres drainages, seeking to clarify relationships within this genus.
* **Regulatory Constraints:** The survey was constrained by an inability to secure authorization from Mexican authorities (SEDUE) for the retention of physical specimens, limiting the analysis to field identification and photographic documentation.
