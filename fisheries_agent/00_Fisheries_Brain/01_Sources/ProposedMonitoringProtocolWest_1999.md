# Proposed Monitoring Protocol: West Turkey Creek Habitat Conservation Plan Area, Arizona

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #ictaluridae #conservation-biology

## Executive Summary
This document outlines a long-term monitoring protocol for native fishes reintroduced into **West Turkey Creek (WTC)**, Arizona, part of the **El Coronado Ranch (ECR)** Habitat Conservation Plan (HCP) area within the Río Yaqui drainage. The plan establishes standardized sampling sites, methods, and schedules to track population trends of native fishes—most notably the **[[Yaqui Catfish]]**—and to detect and rapidly respond to reinvasion by non-native species. The protocol emphasizes minimally invasive sampling (backpack electrofishing, minnow traps, seines, hoop nets, PIT tagging), disease surveillance for **Asian tapeworm**, and coordinated reporting among federal, state, and private land partners (USFWS, USFS, ASU, ECR) under an Endangered Species Act Section 7 consultation framework.

## Background & Description
The source text is a monitoring/management protocol rather than a morphological study, so detailed morphometric data are not provided. However, relevant biological handling parameters include:
- Fish are classified into two life-stage categories during sampling: **juveniles** and **adults**.
- **[[Yaqui Catfish]]** individuals captured via hoop nets are identified using **PIT (Passive Integrated Transponder) tags**, and are individually **measured** and **weighed** before release, indicating individual-level growth and identity tracking is a program priority.
- No length-frequency, weight, or diagnostic morphological data are given for other native species in this excerpt.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type:** WTC is characterized by discrete permanent pools within an otherwise intermittent desert stream channel, plus three artificial **canal-fed ponds** (Pond H, Guesthouse ponds, Big Tank) distributed along the ECR/USFS boundary gradient, serving as refugia and expansion habitat for reintroduced natives.
- **Substrate & vegetation:** Site-level habitat quality (habitat type, substrate, vegetation) is visually surveyed at each sampling event, indicating these are recognized as key determinants of habitat suitability for [[Native Fish Assemblage]] persistence.
- **Biotic threats:** Presence of **non-native fishes** is treated as a critical ecological threat, triggering immediate **native-fish removal** and system **renovation** protocols. The **Asian tapeworm** (a nonindigenous parasite documented near WTC at San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge) represents an emerging parasitic threat to the native fish community, prompting annual fish health screening.
- **Reproductive ecology:** Autumn monitoring targets adult populations and **seasonal reproductive success**; optional spring monitoring assesses **over-wintering survival** and pre-spawning adult population size, implying a spring–summer reproductive period for the native fish community, consistent with many [[Sonoran Desert Fishes]].
- **Landscape context:** The monitoring area spans a management mosaic of USFS and private ranch (ECR) lands within the broader **Río Yaqui basin**, linking WTC populations to regional recovery efforts for threatened/endangered Yaqui fishes (per USFWS 1995 Recovery Plan).

## Key Findings
- **Site design:** 3–5 permanent 100 m monitoring sites will be established on WTC across USFS/ECR lands, targeting known permanent pools, plus 3 designated ponds (Pond H, Guesthouse ponds, Big Tank) representing upper, mid, and lower ranch positions.
- **Site documentation:** Locations are fixed using USGS 7.5' topographic maps, GPS coordinates, written landmark descriptions, and permanent photographic reference points archived with each report.
- **Sampling methods:**
  - Stream sites: **backpack electrofishing** (single pass to minimize mortality; voltage, pulse width, frequency, and duration recorded).
  - Ponds: **minnow traps** and **seines** (effort metrics recorded: trap number, set duration, seine mesh size, haul number).
  - **[[Yaqui Catfish]]**: sampled via **hoop nets**, identified by PIT tag, measured, weighed, released alive.
  - All fish are enumerated by juvenile/adult class and returned alive; mortalities are preserved and archived at Arizona State University (ASU).
- **Non-native species contingency:** If non-natives reappear, **gill nets** will be deployed to confirm presence in ponds; detection triggers immediate native-fish removal and habitat renovation, with multi-partner cooperation for emergency response.
- **Disease surveillance:** Annual parasite screening by a USFWS fish health expert is recommended (minimum n=10 individuals per species initially, up to ~50 as populations grow), due to nearby documented **Asian tapeworm** and **bullfrog** eradication efforts at San Bernardino NWR.
- **Monitoring schedule:**
  - **Quarterly** monitoring for the first 2 years post-reintroduction to track population expansion.
  - **Biannual/annual** monitoring thereafter, satisfying HCP requirements.
  - **Autumn** sampling prioritized (adult populations, reproductive success); optional **spring** sampling (overwinter survival, pre-reproduction adult census).
  - Full basin-wide WTC survey every **5 years** per USFWS Implementation Agreement (1998).
  - **10-year** partner review of monitoring program efficacy following incidental take permit issuance.
- **Reporting structure:** Annual data summaries to all partners; separate annual incidental take reports to USFWS. **San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge** designated as lead depository for all WTC HCP documentation, given its lead role for Yaqui fish species conservation.
- **Regulatory context:** Monitoring operates under an active **Section 7 consultation** (valid until 2004), with recommendation for renewal prior to expiration to avoid lapses in management authority.
- **Institutional partners:** [[Arizona State University]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[U.S. Forest Service]] (Coronado National Forest), and **El Coronado Ranch** (private landowner) form the cooperative management framework.
