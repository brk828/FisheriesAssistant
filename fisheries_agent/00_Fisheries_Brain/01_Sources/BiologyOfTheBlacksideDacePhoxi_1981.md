# Biology of the Blackside Dace Phoxinus cumberlandensis

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This study (Starnes & Starnes, 1981) presents a comprehensive biological account of the [[Blackside Dace]] (*Phoxinus cumberlandensis*), a recently described cyprinid endemic to the upper Cumberland River drainage of Kentucky and Tennessee. Field investigations conducted in 1977, 1978, and 1981 examined habitat preferences, diet, reproduction, growth, and population dynamics. The species is a detritivore/periphyton grazer inhabiting small, cool, upland streams with near-equal riffle-to-pool ratios, favoring pool areas with extensive cover. Spawning occurs communally over stoneroller minnow (*Campostoma anomalum*) nests between April and July, with average fecundity of 1540 ova/female. The species exhibits a maximum lifespan of ~3 years, rapid first-year growth, and female-biased survivorship. The Blackside Dace is identified as threatened primarily due to habitat degradation from coal surface mining in its restricted range.

## Background & Description
- **Taxonomy**: *Phoxinus cumberlandensis* (Cyprinidae), described by [[Starnes And Starnes 1978]], closely related to *Phoxinus oreas* and sympatric/parapatric with *Phoxinus erythrogaster* ([[Southern Redbelly Dace]]).
- **Distinguishing biology**: Among the most brightly colored cyprinids, especially in nuptial condition — males display a brilliant golden dorsum, intense black lateral stripe, bright yellow fins, and scarlet coloration on the lower head, nape, and belly. Females also show notable (if less intense) red coloration on belly and nape, a finding that amends the original species description.
- **Distribution**: Known only from isolated upland streams in the upper [[Cumberland River Drainage]] of Kentucky and Tennessee, mostly above Cumberland Falls. Occurs sporadically in the higher-gradient [[Cumberland Mountains]] region, and more broadly across the lower-gradient [[Cumberland Plateau]].
- **Study sites**:
  - **Young's Creek** (primary site), Whitley Co., KY — small upland stream, altitude 265–340 m, deciduous/hemlock canopy, cobble/bedrock riffles, rubble/boulder/sand pools, stream temps rarely exceeding 23°C.
  - **Little Clear Creek**, Bell Co., KY — similar habitat but higher gradient, more limited habitat extent; site of direct spawning observations (1981).
- **Methods**: Population estimates via sodium cyanide sampling; scale-based aging (80X magnification) supplemented by length-frequency analysis; digestive tract dissection and microscopy (up to 540X) for diet analysis; nitric acid digestion to separate organic/inorganic gut content fractions; ovary dissection and ova counts/measurements for fecundity and spawning season determination.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat requirements**: Occurs most frequently where riffle-area to pool-area ratio approaches or falls below 60:40. Strongly associated with undercut banks, rock slabs, and brush cover in pools and gentle runs.
- **Habitat partitioning with [[Blacknose Dace]]** (*Rhinichthys atratulus*): High-gradient streams with elevated riffle-to-pool ratios in the Cumberland Mt. province are dominated by *R. atratulus*, largely allopatric to *P. cumberlandensis*, restricting the Blackside Dace to lower/middle stream reaches with reduced gradient.
- **Habitat partitioning with [[Southern Redbelly Dace]]** (*P. erythrogaster*): Distributions are largely complementary on the Cumberland Plateau with few syntopic collections, suggesting competitive displacement/exclusion. *P. cumberlandensis* dominates cooler, clearer, bedrock-rubble montane streams with hemlock/rhododendron riparian cover; *P. erythrogaster* favors warmer, more turbid, siltier streams with sparser canopy, often reflecting anthropogenic alteration. Neither species tolerates substantial warming, turbidity, or siltation well.
- **Anthropogenic threats**: Coal surface mining is the dominant driver of habitat degradation in the drainage, causing stream alteration, siltation, and turbidity that likely limits both feeding and reproductive habitat. Mining has recently been permitted in previously protected areas of the [[Daniel Boone National Forest]], including the headwaters of Eagle Creek — home to one of the largest known populations.
- **Diet and trophic ecology**: Primarily a detritivore consuming sand, periphyton, and organic detritus; diet composition shifts seasonally (heavy invertebrate reliance in winter) but not with fish size or age. Minimal dietary overlap/competition observed with most syntopic species except *Pimephales notatus* and *Campostoma anomalum*.
- **Reproductive dependency**: Spawns communally over gravel nests constructed and maintained by the [[Stoneroller Minnow]] (*Campostoma anomalum*), which appears to provide silt-free spawning substrate and incidental protection from egg predators (e.g., aggressive exclusion of *Semotilus* creek chubs from nests by nest-guarding male stonerollers).
- **Predation pressure**: Potential predators include [[Largemouth Bass]] (*Micropterus salmoides*), [[Creek Chub]] (*Semotilus atromaculatus*), and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*); the more vivid coloration of nuptial males may increase their vulnerability, contributing to male-biased mortality.

## Key Findings

### Habitat & Distribution
- Preferred riffle:pool ratio ≈ 60:40 or lower.
- Sporadic occurrence in Cumberland Mts.; more widespread historically on the Cumberland Plateau.
- Range often restricted to very limited stream reaches (e.g., ~75 m of habitat in Little Clear Creek) even within seemingly suitable streams.

### Feeding Behavior & Diet
- Distinctive head-down (≥20°) grazing posture on submerged surfaces, more pronounced than in related cyprinids (*P. erythrogaster*, *P. eos*, *P. oreas*, *Notropis* spp.).
- Forages in schools of ~5–20 individuals; grazes periphyton on rocks and plucks at sand substrate.
- Digestive tract content (by volume, annual average):
  - Sand: 36.7% (range 3.3% in December to 54.1% in August)
  - Unidentified fine organics: 32.1% (up to 42.1% in April; absent in December)
  - Algae/diatoms: 11.9% (filamentous algae ~8.4%: *Oedogonium*, *Spirogyra*; diatoms: *Cymbella*, *Navicula*, *Lyngbya*)
  - Invertebrates: 4.6% annual average, but dominant (up to 100%) in December samples, when 6/7 guts were ≥75% empty (major seasonal food reduction).
  - Root hairs: 7.4%
- Gut length ≈ 3.6 × SL; considered an obligate detritivore, with nutritional strategy possibly reliant on microbial/bacterial decomposers or diatom protoplasm rather than direct digestion of detritus/algae.
- Comparative diets: *Semotilus atromaculatus* (98.7% invertebrates, 81.2% terrestrial), *Etheostoma kennicotti* & *E. sagitta* (near-exclusive aquatic invertebrates), *Catostomus commersoni* (47.6% invertebrates plus organics/inorganics), *Pimephales notatus* (diet closely parallels *P. cumberlandensis*; insects 12.3% vs. 4.5%).

### Reproduction
- Sexual maturity reached in first autumn of life; females mature at ~35.3 mm SL average.
- Spawning season: April–July, based on mature ova presence.
- Fecundity: mean 1540 ova/female; Age I females: 724–1467 ova; Age II–III females: 1311–2872 ova.
- Fecundity–length relationship: Y = 5.19(X)^1.52 (r² = 0.72), where Y = ova count, X = SL (mm).
- Mature ova diameter: 0.82–1.36 mm; immature ova 0.57–0.82 mm; primordial ova <0.57 mm.
- Direct spawning observation (Little Clear Creek, 17 May 1981, water temp 17.5°C): Communal spawning over a *Campostoma anomalum* gravel nest; groups of ~10–12 nuptial males hovered over the nest, with trios pinning individual females to substrate during brief (<3 sec) spawning acts. Host stoneroller male tolerated dace but excluded larger predatory fish (e.g., nuptial male creek chubs) from the nest.
- Siltation appears to substantially limit availability of suitable spawning substrate; no spawning observed in silted areas even when females were present.
- No documented hybridization between *P. cumberlandensis* and co-spawning species, despite this being common in other *Phoxinus*-nest associate systems.

### Growth & Age Structure
- Maximum lifespan ~3 years.
- Growth is rapid in year 1, then slows in years 2–3, more markedly in females.
- Length-scale radius regression: Y = 0.62(X) + 0.36 (r² = 0.88); back-calculated mean SL: 35.3 mm (annulus I), 53.2 mm (annulus II), 66.1 mm (annulus III).
- At annulus III, males average 67.5 mm SL vs. females 64.7 mm SL.
- Length–weight relationships:
  - Males: Y = 1.88(X)^3.52 (r² = 0.98)
  - Females: Y = 1.86(X)^2.9 (r² = 0.94) [slowed growth post-maturity linked to egg production]

### Population Dynamics
- Sex ratio (September quantitative samples): 21 males : 29 females; overall collections ≈ 2 males : 3 females (n=73).
- Female-biased survivorship increases with age; greater proportion of females reach Age III.
- Population density estimates (Young's Creek, sodium cyanide sampling): 56.8–73.1 individuals per 100 m² in preferred habitat — among the highest known densities for the species, comparable only to Eagle Creek and Little Clear Creek populations.
- Associated species densities (per 100 m², Young's Creek): *Semotilus atromaculatus* 77.3–140.0; *Catostomus commersoni* 64.0–81.3; *Etheostoma kennicotti* 2.6–21.6; *E. sagitta* 0–2.6; *Micropterus salmoides* 0–1.5.
