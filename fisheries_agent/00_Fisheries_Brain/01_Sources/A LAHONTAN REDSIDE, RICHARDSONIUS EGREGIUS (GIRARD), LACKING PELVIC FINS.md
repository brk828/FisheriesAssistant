# A Lahontan Redside, *Richardsonius egregius* (Girard), Lacking Pelvic Fins

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae

## Executive Summary
This brief scientific note documents a rare morphological anomaly observed in a single specimen of [[Richardsonius egregius]] (Lahontan Redside), a cyprinid fish native to the [[Lahontan Drainage System]]. The specimen, collected from Independence Lake, California in 1964, exhibited a complete absence of pelvic fins. Radiographic (X-ray) analysis confirmed that this was a true skeletal anomaly rather than mere fin loss, as the underlying pelvic girdle bones (basipterygia) were also entirely absent. The specimen was otherwise phenotypically normal and has been preserved for future study.

## Background & Description
- **Standard Length (SL):** 72.9 mm
- **Weight:** 7.6 g
- **Anomaly:** Complete bilateral absence of pelvic fins.
- **Skeletal Analysis:** X-ray imaging revealed the **basipterygia** (pelvic girdle bones) were entirely absent, indicating the anomaly is developmental/skeletal in origin rather than a result of physical injury or predation.
- **Integument:** Scales fully covered the body in the region where pelvic fins would normally be located.
- **Scarring:** No scars, lesions, or aberrations were noted in the pelvic region, ruling out post-hatch traumatic loss (e.g., from a predator attack).
- **General Condition:** The fish appeared normal in all other respects, suggesting the anomaly did not significantly impair general growth or survival to this size.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Native Range:** [[Richardsonius egregius]] is endemic to the Lahontan drainage system in the Great Basin region of the western United States.
- **Specific Habitat:** The specimen was collected from **Independence Lake**, situated at the headwaters of a tributary feeding into the **Little Truckee River**.
- **Hydrological Context:** This places the specimen's habitat firmly within a cold, high-elevation lacustrine (lake) ecosystem, connected to the broader [[Little Truckee River]] and Truckee River watershed.
- **Ecological Significance:** As a species reliant on precise locomotion for feeding and predator avoidance, the loss of pelvic fins—typically used for stabilization, braking, and fine maneuvering—represents a notable deviation from the functional norm for a cyprinid in this niche.

## Key Findings
1. **First Documented Occurrence:** This note documents an unusual skeletal teratology (developmental abnormality) in *R. egregius*, specifically the total agenesis (failure to develop) of the pelvic fin and its supporting girdle.
2. **Confirmed Skeletal Origin:** The use of X-ray radiography was critical in distinguishing this condition as a congenital absence of the basipterygia, rather than a fin clipped by injury or trauma, which the intact, scarless scale covering also supports.
3. **Viability Despite Anomaly:** The specimen survived to a subadult/adult size (72.9 mm SL) despite the missing pelvic fins, suggesting this specific structure may not be strictly essential for short-term survival in this particular lacustrine environment, though its impact on long-term fitness or predator evasion is unknown.
4. **Archival Value:** The specimen, along with X-rays and photographic documentation, was deposited at the **California Academy of Sciences** (CAS 23718), preserving it as a reference point for future studies on skeletal development and anomalies in [[Cyprinidae]].
