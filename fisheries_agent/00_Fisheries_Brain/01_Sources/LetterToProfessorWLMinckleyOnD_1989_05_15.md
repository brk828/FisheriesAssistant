## Executive Summary

This 1989 letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Region 2, Albuquerque) to Professor W. L. Minckley, chair of the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]], provides a formal status update on recovery planning for a suite of imperiled southwestern desert fish species. Rather than presenting new morphological or ecological data, the document is a conservation-management record detailing the progress, delays, and bureaucratic responsibilities associated with Endangered Species Act recovery plans for the [[Sonoran Topminnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], [[Sonoran Chub]], Yaqui basin fishes, and [[Desert Pupfish]]. It also clarifies a jurisdictional handoff of "Big River" and Virgin River fish recovery responsibilities to Region 6, and reaffirms the Service's reliance on recovery teams such as Minckley's for scientific guidance.

## Background & Description

No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are presented in this source document. The letter is administrative in nature and pertains to species status, listing classifications, and recovery plan management rather than physical descriptions of the taxa involved.

Species discussed (by common reference) belong to distinct families with well-documented desert-adapted morphologies elsewhere in the literature:
- **[[Sonoran Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — a small live-bearing poeciliid, split taxonomically into two subspecies: *P. o. occidentalis* and *P. o. sonoriensis*.
- **[[Spikedace]]** (*Meda fulgida*) and **[[Loach Minnow]]** (*Tiaroga cobitis*) — small benthic cyprinids adapted to swift, rocky desert stream riffles.
- **Big River fishes** — [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — large-bodied, morphologically specialized Colorado River basin natives (e.g., the humpback chub's pronounced nuchal hump, an adaptation to turbulent mainstem flows).
- **Virgin River fishes** — [[Woundfin]] and [[Virgin River Chub]] — small-bodied cyprinids adapted to the Virgin River's unique hydrology.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **[[Sonoran Topminnow]]**: Occupies desert springs, cienegas, and slow-moving stream margins in the Gila River basin (Arizona) and Sonora, Mexico. Subject to a proposed reclassification splitting management by subspecies and geography.
- **[[Spikedace]] and [[Loach Minnow]]**: Inhabit riffle habitats in perennial desert streams of the Gila River basin (Arizona/New Mexico); both recovery plans were drafted by Dr. Paul Marsh (Arizona State University) in 1988.
- **[[Little Colorado River Spinedace]]**: Endemic to the Little Colorado River drainage, Arizona.
- **[[Sonoran Chub]]**: Found in the Rio de la Concepción drainage, Sonora, Mexico, and adjacent Arizona streams.
- **Yaqui basin fishes** (Yaqui catfish, Yaqui chub, beautiful shiner): Native to the Yaqui River drainage spanning southeastern Arizona and Sonora, Mexico; managed in coordination with [[San Bernardino National Wildlife Refuge]].
- **[[Desert Pupfish]]**: Occupies isolated desert springs, marshes, and man-made refugia in the Sonoran Desert (Salton Sea basin, lower Colorado River, and Mexico).
- **Big River fishes** (Colorado squawfish, bonytail chub, humpback chub, razorback sucker): Large-river specialists of the mainstem Colorado River system; recovery jurisdiction lies with [[FWS Region 6]] (Denver), with continued support for lower basin recovery actions.
- **Virgin River fishes** (woundfin, Virgin River chub): Endemic to the Virgin River, a tributary of the Colorado River spanning Utah, Arizona, and Nevada; also under Region 6 lead responsibility.

## Key Findings

- **Sonoran Topminnow**: Recovery plan signed March 15, 1984, developed with substantial input from Minckley and Desert Fishes Recovery Team members. A draft proposed rule to **downlist *Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis* from endangered to threatened** had been completed as of 1989; *P. o. sonoriensis* was to **retain endangered status**.
- **Spikedace and Loach Minnow**: Draft recovery plans completed in 1988 by Dr. Paul Marsh; target finalization date was **December 31, 1989**.
- **Little Colorado River Spinedace**: Recovery plan not yet initiated as of letter date; target draft completion date **September 30, 1989**.
- **Sonoran Chub**: Draft plan reviewed and revised; finalization expected **before September 30, 1989**.
- **Yaqui Fishes** (catfish, chub, beautiful shiner): Draft plan completed but finalization delayed following the death of San Bernardino Refuge Manager Ben Robertson; plan under internal review, expected completion **within the 1989 calendar year**.
- **Desert Pupfish**: Recovery plan **not yet initiated** due to lack of budgeted funding; development to begin once funds become available.
- **Jurisdictional realignment**: Responsibility for **Big River fishes** (Colorado squawfish, bonytail chub, humpback chub, razorback sucker) and **Virgin River fishes** (woundfin, Virgin River chub) recovery was formally reassigned to **[[FWS Region 6]]** to avoid duplication of effort, with continued Region 2 support for lower basin recovery actions.
- **Institutional role**: The letter reaffirms that the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]]'s core function, once a recovery plan is finalized, shifts to providing ongoing recommendations to keep plans current, while implementation responsibility rests with designated Service field stations (e.g., [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[Pinetop Fisheries Assistance Office]]).
- **Administrative acknowledgment**: The Service acknowledged a lapse in responding to prior correspondence from the Desert Fishes Recovery Team and committed to improved communication going forward.
