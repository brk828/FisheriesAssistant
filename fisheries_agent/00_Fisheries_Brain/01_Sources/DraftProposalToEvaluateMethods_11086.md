# Long-Term Monitoring of Native Fishes in Grand Canyon: FY95 Interim Work Plan and Budget Request

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a set of working materials from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) Arizona Fishery Resources Office, part of the [[Grand Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program. It contains three linked items: a draft outline for evaluating sampling gears and methods for a [[Long-Term Monitoring Plan]] for native fishes; notes from a November 1993 principal investigators meeting; and a 30 August 1994 memorandum requesting FY95 funding to finish Phase II analyses, fill data gaps, and prepare for long-term monitoring starting in FY96. The principal species of concern is the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), a federally listed native fish of the Colorado River. The FY95 request totals $289,616.

## Background & Description

The materials address native fishes of the Colorado River in Grand Canyon, with [[Humpback Chub]] as the main focus. Humpback chub are described in the notes as affected by [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The stated purpose of monitoring was "to characterize trends in population demographics of humpback chub in Grand Canyon as affected by Glen Canyon Dam." Three groups were identified for monitoring: young-of-the-year (YOY); a "super cohort" of age 0, I and II fish (up to about 200 mm total length); and adults age II+.

Historical fishery data cited include work from 1977 ([[Carothers and Minckley]]), 1980 ([[Kaeding and Zimmerman]]), and 1984 ([[Maddux et al.]]) to the present. Earlier work by [[Suttkus]] and others was still to be gathered, and metadata for all studies still needed to be developed. Phase II data (1991 to present) were to be reported in the Final Report, with earlier data handled in supplemental reports by [[Persons and Hoffnagle]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Key habitats discussed are the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR), its confluence with the Colorado River, backwaters, and other tributary mouths. The draft notes that sampling juveniles and YOY is standard where backwaters exist, while adults and smaller juveniles need more work. Sampling tributary mouths during spawning season was suggested as the most efficient way to collect adults.

Proposed gears include [[Electrofishing]], [[Trammel Nets]], [[Hoop Nets]], seines and minnow traps. Benthos, sediment, plankton sampling and backwater habitat mapping were to continue. The notes mention that [[Lernaea]] (a parasitic copepod) and Asiatic tapeworm infections were common among fish in Kanab Creek and the LCR in 1993 and 1994, and that fish health should become part of long-term monitoring.

The notes describe a sampling calendar by season:
- Winter (Jan–Mar): LCR seine sampling.
- Spring (Apr–Jun): LCR confluence sampling with seines, electrofishing and minnow traps.
- Summer and fall: sampling from the LCR confluence to Red Creek, and mainstem trips at roughly river miles (RM) 30–35, 61.3–76.5 and 117–128 every two to three years.

A 1995 trip schedule listed about 19 mainstem and backwater sample sites, from Reach 4 and warm springs near RM 30 to the Lava Falls area and RM 190–204, including tributary mouths such as [[Kanab Creek]], [[Havasu Creek]] and [[Diamond Creek]].

## Key Findings

**Monitoring framework and methods**
- Evaluate gear types and methods so monitoring reduces bias by using many gear types; catch efficiency in backwaters was to be tested by depletion sampling.
- Joint field trips, four in total, were to be coordinated, with a trip leader (Hoffnagle or Tuegle) suggested and possible subcontracting through Bio/West.
- A Long-Term Monitoring Plan was to be written, reviewed and approved, with specified authors and responsibilities. Data forms and analysis programs were to be compatible with the GCES Integrated Database.

**Phase II status and FY95 priorities**
- Data volume is so large that a comprehensive analysis for the Final Report is not feasible. The objective of identifying [[Humpback Chub Spawning Habitat]] was not fully achieved.
- FY95 priorities: complete data analysis, produce peer-reviewed technical reports, fill data gaps with field work, and complete database archiving.
- Proposed technical paper topics include habitat use and partitioning among native fishes of the LCR, ontogenetic habitat shifts, early life history habitat use, home range estimates, habitat models for assessing other tributaries, GIS mapping of macrohabitat, and methods for measuring stream habitat.

**Spawning and early life history**
- Habitat studies to identify spawning habitat were proposed for spring 1995.
- Little is known about post-larval humpback chub (20–50 mm TL); a proposal for early life history ecology was planned for FY96.
- Habitat data from all tributary confluences were comparable except for the LCR confluence, which limited assessment of the LCR as a model for other tributaries. FWS had been barred from sampling there, so the team planned to work with the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD) to collect one dataset in spring 1995.

**Genetics and disease**
- A pilot study of [[Cryopreservation]] of humpback chub sperm in the LCR was planned for spring 1995, to bank genetic diversity against bottlenecks and support hatchery stocks if needed.
- A fish disease monitoring framework was to be developed for FY96 implementation.
