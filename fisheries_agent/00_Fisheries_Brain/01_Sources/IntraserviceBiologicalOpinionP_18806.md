# Patagonia-Sonoita Creek Railroad Abutment Removal: Biological Opinion for Gila Topminnow

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This intra-Service biological opinion (Cons. #2-22-94-F-210, dated February 23, 1994) evaluates a [[Partners for Wildlife Challenge Cost Share Agreement]] between [[The Nature Conservancy]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]. The project would detonate and remove the southernmost of four railroad abutments in the active channel of [[Sonoita Creek]] within the [[Patagonia-Sonoita Creek Preserve]], recontour the adjacent embankment and floodplain, and restore natural hydrology. The Service concluded that the action is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]]. Incidental take is anticipated but cannot be quantified, so the opinion sets a take threshold, reasonable and prudent measures, and monitoring terms.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), a small (1–2 inch) livebearing fish of the family Poeciliidae, listed as endangered on March 11, 1967. No critical habitat has been designated.
- **Historical range:** Gila, Sonora and de la Concepcion river drainages in Arizona, New Mexico and Sonora, Mexico. Once among the most common fishes of the Gila River system.
- **Decline:** Habitat destruction (water diversion, downcutting, backwater draining, channelization, impoundment) and competition with or predation by non-native [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) caused extirpation across most of its range. Currently known from 9 naturally occurring populations in the United States and about 20 reintroduced populations.
- **Sonoita Creek status:** The fish formerly occurred throughout Sonoita Creek, but recent [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] monitoring recorded only small numbers.
- **Associated natives:** [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*), [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*) and [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus clarki*). The speckled dace population is the only remaining one in the Santa Cruz River watershed.
- **Preserve:** 317 acres along about 1.75 miles of Sonoita Creek. Dominant plant communities are cottonwood/willow gallery forest, mesquite bosque and cienega, all dependent on floodplain fluvial dynamics.
- **Railroad legacy:** A railroad built in 1882 (Fairbank to Nogales) followed the creek through the preserve. Two sets of large concrete abutments and an elevated roadbed berm remain. The abutments confine the creek, prevent overflow channel development and stream migration, and limit riparian regeneration (Stromberg 1993).
- **Project purpose:** Benefit Gila topminnow, restore natural hydrology, and improve [[Neotropical Migratory Birds]] habitat. The agreement term is 10 years (signed September 2, 1993).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The current channel upstream and downstream of the abutment is straight, with low habitat diversity and mainly moderate-to-high velocity runs. Gila topminnow prefer low-to-moderate velocity habitats such as backwaters, glides and pools. Removing the abutment is expected to let the creek meander, increasing channel length and habitat complexity (depth, velocity, cover). The pool currently formed behind the abutment will be lost.

Key project elements:
- The lower of four abutments in the active channel is detonated. Debris is removed off the preserve.
- Floodplain about 75 feet downstream is recontoured. A terrace about 2 feet above the active channel is lowered about 1 foot, affecting a maximum width of 20 feet. The low flow channel bottom stays above active channel elevation.
- No work occurs in the wetted channel. Heavy equipment is accessed from Highway 82 to the east.
- Embankment recontouring is limited to de minimis discharge. Greater discharge would require consultation with the [[U.S. Army Corps of Engineers]].

Cumulative non-Federal activities in the watershed include preserve management, County road maintenance, and flood control by the town of Patagonia and private residences upstream. The opinion notes these may adversely affect the topminnow and its habitat.

## Key Findings

**Effects and biological opinion**
- Restoring natural hydrologic processes is expected to improve Gila topminnow status at the site over the long term, though exact hydrologic predictions are unavailable.
- Adverse effects are limited to: fish loss from abutment debris falling into the creek on detonation; disturbance during material removal; and temporary turbidity and sediment deposition from cut-bank stabilization. Floodplain recontouring downstream is not expected to affect the active channel.
- The Service found the action **not likely to jeopardize** the species.
- The effect on the southwestern willow flycatcher, a proposed species, was judged beneficial.

**Incidental take**
- Direct mortality is expected during detonation, material removal and embankment recontouring. It cannot be quantified because reliable population estimates are unobtainable (sampling difficulty and rapid population changes in a short-lived, high-fecundity species).
- **Take threshold:** take is assumed exceeded if more than **10 fish of any species** are found dead downstream within **5 days** of detonation. Reinitiation is then required immediately, and operations stop if additional take would be irreversible.

**Reasonable and prudent measures and terms**
1. Minimize direct mortality (detonate to limit debris spread into the active channel).
2. Minimize habitat loss (no heavy equipment in the wetted channel; hand or minimal mechanical work on the low flow channel; access from Highway 82, not from the west).
3. Keep complete records of take-causing actions, filed with the Service's Private Lands Biologist.
4. Monitor the species and habitat, including visual observation by a qualified biologist during construction.

**Monitoring and recommendations**
- Six transects established in 1991 by The Nature Conservancy track floodplain topography, channel morphology and riparian recruitment. Base vegetation data were collected in 1991 and 1992. Monitoring follows significant floods, with long-term photomonitoring points.
- [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] monitors fish impacts under Section 6 funding.
- Conservation recommendation: survey Sonoita Creek within the preserve after every major hydrologic event to evaluate changes in potential topminnow habitat.

**Reinitiation triggers (50 CFR 402.16):** excess take; new information on unconsidered effects; modified action with unconsidered effects; or a newly listed species or critical habitat that may be affected.
