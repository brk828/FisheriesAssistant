# Systematic Status of Agassiz's 1859 Nicaraguan Cichlid Genera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic and nomenclatural study re-examines four cichlid genera and species that [[Louis Agassiz]] described in 1859 from fishes collected in [[Lake Nicaragua]] by [[Julius Froebel]]. Most later authors had ignored these names. The authors searched for surviving type material in the [[Museum Of Comparative Zoology]] (MCZ) and redescribed [[Parachromis Gulosus]] from two newly located syntypes, now designated a lectotype and paralectotype. They conclude that *P. gulosus* is a senior synonym of [[Parachromis Managuensis]], which is well known in the literature, and recommend formal retention of *managuensis* pending action by the [[International Code Of Zoological Nomenclature]] (ICZN). All four genus-level names are available under the ICZN, although the type material for two taxa could not be found.

## Background & Description

Agassiz presented the results at the 6 October 1858 meeting of the Boston Society of Natural History. The report was published in January 1859 as meeting minutes. It names four new species, each in its own new genus:

- **Parachromis gulosus**, which resembles the sparid *Dentex* except for its interrupted lateral line.
- **Hypsophrys unimaculatus**, which resembles *Chrysophrys* and has a single spot on the side.
- **Baiodon fasciatus**, which resembles *Boops*.
- **Amphilophus froebelii**, which has a large triangular lobe above the upper jaw and below the lower jaw instead of ordinary fleshy lips.

Agassiz's descriptions depend on comparisons with east Atlantic and Mediterranean sparids: *Boops boops* (for *Baiodon*), *Dentex dentex* (for *Parachromis*), and *Sparus auratus* (for *Hypsophrys*). The report also notes that all four taxa share a backward-extended second dorsal and anal fin, which places them in the Cichlidae (then termed "Chromids").

The MCZ holds only a small Froebel collection: three lots of cichlids (four specimens), plus single specimens of *Eleotris* sp. (MCZ 13434) and *Pimelodus* sp. (MCZ 7437), which are irrelevant to the names. Agassiz does not state how many specimens he examined.

The paper's full description of *P. gulosus* (lectotype and paralectotype) includes meristic counts, measurements, colour notes, and an etymology. It explains that the name is masculine, from the Greek *para* ("beside") and *chromis*, with the Latin adjective *gulosus* ("large-throated, gluttonous").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Agassiz's names were overlooked for more than sixty years. [[Jordan]] (1919) first listed them, and later nomenclators (Jordan et al. 1930; Schulze et al. 1926-1955; Neave 1939-1940; Eschmeyer & Bailey 1990) gave them differing status. Early workers on Lake Nicaragua, including [[Gunther]] (1864-1868), Gill & Bransford (1877), Meek (1907), and Regan (1905), did not cite Agassiz's paper.

[[Thorson]] (1976) reviewed the lake fish fauna. Several Nicaraguan cichlids are recorded there, including members of the *Amphilophus* complex (the "Midas"/"Red Devil"/"Arrow" cichlids), *Parachromis dovii*, *P. managuensis*, and *Hypsophrys nicaraguensis*. Barlow & Munsey (1976) distinguished *A. labiatus* from *A. citrinellus* in Lake Nicaragua by lip size and snout-to-pectoral-fin distance. The *Amphilophus* species group shows considerable colour-pattern variation, which the authors use to argue that the Froebel descriptions could match more than one species.

## Key Findings

**1. Parachromis gulosus and P. managuensis are the same species.**
- The lectotype is MCZ 16086, a young female, 110.1 mm SL, from Lake Nicaragua (collected 1852 by J. Froebel).
- The paralectotype is MCZ 144443, a female, 106.8 mm SL, from the same lot.
- The preopercle shape (narrow exposed vertical limb with a notch at the joint) matches Günther's figure of *P. managuensis* (1868: pl. 77, fig. 3). Günther gave the holotype a length of 7.5 inches (190.5 mm TL); Regan (1905) gave 187 mm.
- The authors synonymize *P. managuensis* (Lago de Managua) with *P. gulosus* (Lago de Nicaragua).
- Because *gulosus* is the senior name, the authors recommend keeping *managuensis* in use. This requires ICZN approval under Articles 23b and 79c.

**2. Parachromis is an available genus but a junior homonym of a later name.**
- Parachromis Agassiz, 1859 was reinstated by Kullander (1996) for a group of piscivorous Central American cichlids.
- Parachromis Regan, 1922 (type species *Hemichromis sacer*) is a junior homonym and was replaced by *Tristramella* by Trewavas (1942).

**3. Amphilophus froebelii has no surviving type, but the genus is available.**
- No specimen with enlarged lips from the Froebel collection could be found.
- The name is treated as a senior synonym of *Heros labiatus* Günther, 1864. Barlow & Munsey (1976) argued for keeping *labiatus*; the authors suggest maintaining existing usage until ICZN action is taken.
- *Amphilophus* is available, with *A. froebelii* as its type species by monotypy. Synonyms include *Astatheros* Pellegrin (1904), *Curraichthys* Fernández-Yépez (1969), and *Erythrichthus* Meek (1907).

**4. Baiodon fasciatus is a nomen inquirendum.**
- No type material has been located, and the description is minimal ("like *Boops*").
- The name is available, because it contains a diagnostic statement, but it cannot be assigned to any known species with certainty.
- Jordan et al. (1930) synonymized it with *Vieja zonata* (Meek, 1905), a Mexican species. The authors reject this as unexplained.

**5. Hypsophrys unimaculatus is a senior synonym of H. nicaraguensis, but the name is recommended for suppression.**
- Agassiz's description fits *Heros nicaraguensis* Günther, 1864 (Lake Nicaragua).
- No type material has been found.
- *Copora* Fernández-Yépez, 1969 (type species *Heros nicaraguensis*) is a junior synonym of *Hypsophrys*.
- *Hypsophrys* Wood-Mason, 1891 (a porter crab genus) is a junior homonym of *Hypsophrys* Agassiz, 1859.
- The authors suggest keeping *nicaraguensis* to preserve familiar nomenclature, and they leave the decision to a future revision.

**6. The missing specimens may hold key type material.**
- MCZ 15412 (catalogued as *Heros nicaraguensis*) is a Froebel specimen of *A. citrinellus*, but the authors do not consider it a type.
- MCZ 15619 (labelled *Mesops nicaraguensis*, Nicaragua, 1852) was loaned in 1978 to a university student, who left the program. The specimen has not been found, and it "may very well represent type material."

**7. All four names are available under ICZN Article 12a.**
- Each name is accompanied by a definition or indication, which satisfies Article 12a for names published before 1931.
- Each new genus has a type species that is the only species the author cited with it (Article 68d).
- Whether later workers find the descriptions adequate for recognition is considered irrelevant to availability.

**8. Conclusion.**
- Agassiz's taxa, though poorly documented, are available and should be applied in Central American cichlid systematics.
- Several nomenclatural decisions (suppressing *gulosus*, *froebelii*, and *unimaculatus*) require ICZN approval before they can take effect.
