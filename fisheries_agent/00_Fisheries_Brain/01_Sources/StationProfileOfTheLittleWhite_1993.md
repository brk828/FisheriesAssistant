## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the operations of the **[[Little White Salmon National Fish Hatchery]]** and its satellite facility, the **[[Willard National Fish Hatchery]]**, jointly administered as a complex in Skamania County, Washington under U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 1. Established in 1896 (Little White Salmon) and 1951 (Willard), the complex's mission is to fulfill federal mitigation responsibilities and restore depleted **[[Pacific Salmon]]** stocks of national significance. During Fiscal Year 1993, the complex produced and distributed millions of eggs and juveniles of **[[Coho Salmon]]**, **[[Spring Chinook Salmon]]**, and **[[Fall Chinook Salmon]]** (Up River Bright stock), supporting state, federal, and tribal fishery interests across Washington and Oregon. The facility recorded 34,211 visitors in FY 1993, reflecting its role in public outreach alongside conservation aquaculture.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat Context:** The complex operates on the **[[Little White Salmon River]]**, a tributary within the **[[Columbia River Basin]]**, historically significant for anadromous salmonid spawning runs.
- **Species Roles:**
  - **[[Coho Salmon]]** — Reared for direct release as smolts (3,063,615 released on-station in FY 1993) and limited transfer to educational institutions (e.g., Eastern Washington University).
  - **[[Spring Chinook Salmon]]** — Substantial egg-take (3,718,222 eggs) with distribution to multiple facilities including **[[Spring Creek National Fish Hatchery]]**; used for both smolt and sub-yearling release strategies.
  - **[[Fall Chinook Salmon]] (Up River Bright stock)** — Largest egg-take of the three species (5,640,292 eggs), reflecting emphasis on mitigating impacts to this economically and culturally important stock; substantial transfers to Oregon Department of Fish & Wildlife and Washington Department of Fisheries.
- **Infrastructure supporting ecological function:** Water intake systems with pipelines, spawning/holding ponds, and a combined total of over 100 raceways (54 at Little White Salmon, 50 at Willard) simulate rearing conditions for juvenile salmonids prior to release into natural waters.
- **Interagency Ecological Role:** The complex functions as a node in a broader interstate/interagency salmon restoration network, transferring eggs and fingerlings to state (Washington, Oregon) and federal (Spring Creek NFH, National Fisheries Research Center) partners, as well as academic institutions.

## Key Findings

- **Egg Production (FY 1993):**
  - Coho Salmon: 3,034,841 eggs
  - Spring Chinook Salmon: 3,718,222 eggs
  - Fall Chinook Salmon (Up River Bright): 5,640,292 eggs

- **Egg Transfers & Losses:**
  - 1,500,632 eyed fall chinook eggs transferred to Oregon Dept. of Fish & Wildlife.
  - 500 eyed spring chinook eggs transferred to **[[Spring Creek National Fish Hatchery]]**.
  - 1,478,163 spring chinook eggs destroyed due to surplus.

- **Fingerling Transfers:**
  - 3,313 spring chinook and several thousand fall chinook salmon transferred to the **[[National Fisheries Research Center]]** (Willard, WA).
  - 508,032 spring chinook and 1,704,605 fall chinook transferred to Washington Dept. of Fisheries.
  - 513,969 spring chinook transferred to Spring Creek NFH (Big White ponds).
  - 180 coho salmon transferred to Eastern Washington University.

- **On-Station Smolt Releases (FY 1993):**
  - Spring Chinook: 809,079
  - Coho: 3,063,615
  - Fall Chinook: 1,866,901
  - Sub-yearling Spring Chinook release: 503,458

- **Total Fish Distribution Trends (FY 1990–1993, excluding eggs/unfed fry):**
  | Fiscal Year | Total Distributed |
  |---|---|
  | FY 1990 | 7,846,876 |
  | FY 1991 | 7,619,187 |
  | FY 1992 | 10,388,099 |
  | FY 1993 | 8,978,418 |

- **Funding & Staffing Trends (1990–1994 est.):**
  - FWS Operations budget grew from $49.6K (1990) to est. $40K (1994), with fluctuation.
  - NMFS Cost Recoverables rose significantly from $580.0K (1990) to $763.0K (1993–94 est.), indicating increased reliance on **[[National Marine Fisheries Service]]** mitigation funding.
  - Full-Time Equivalent (FTE) staffing increased from 11.95 (1990) to an estimated 14.00 (1994).

- **Public Engagement:** 34,211 visitors recorded in FY 1993, underscoring the hatchery's dual role in conservation and public education.

- **Infrastructure:** Combined complex includes a hatchery building, cold storage, spawning/holding pond facilities, water intake pipelines, and over 100 raceways across both sites, supporting large-scale multi-species salmonid production.
