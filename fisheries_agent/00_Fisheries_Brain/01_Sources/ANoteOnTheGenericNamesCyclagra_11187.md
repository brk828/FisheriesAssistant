## Executive Summary

James A. Peters (1970) resolves a nomenclatural conflict between two genus names applied to semi-aquatic South American snakes: **Cyclagras Cope, 1885** and **Lejosophis Jan, 1863**. The dispute centers on which species — *Xenodon gigas* Duméril or *Coluber bicinctus* Hermann — should be considered the type-species of *Lejosophis*, and by extension, whether *Lejosophis* is a senior synonym that should displace the widely used name *Cyclagras*. Peters critiques the interpretive reasoning of [[Emmett Reid Dunn]] (1944) and [[Alphonse Richard Hoge]] (1958), who had argued that George Albert Boulenger's 1894 catalogue action inadvertently fixed *gigas* as the type-species of *Lejosophis*, making it synonymous with *Cyclagras*. Peters instead proposes that Jan's own later actions in the *Iconographie Générale des Ophidiens* (1863–1881) constitute a valid type-species fixation for *Lejosophis* via *Coluber bicinctus* Hermann, thereby preserving *Cyclagras* as a distinct, stable name for *Xenodon gigas*. The paper concludes with a formal taxonomic disposition recognizing two genera: **Hydrodynastes Fitzinger, 1843** (containing *H. bicinctus*) and **Cyclagras Cope, 1885** (containing *C. gigas*).

## Key Findings

- **Central dispute:** Whether *Lejosophis* Jan, 1863 and *Cyclagras* Cope, 1885 are objective synonyms (sharing the same type-species, *Xenodon gigas*) — as argued by [[Emmett Reid Dunn]] (1944) and supported by [[Alphonse Richard Hoge]] (1958) — or whether they are distinct genera with different type-species.
- **Boulenger's role:** [[George Albert Boulenger]] (1894), in his *Catalogue of the Snakes in the British Museum*, used "Leiosophis, part." in synonymies under *both* Cyclagras (referring to *gigas*) and *Urotheca* (referring to *bicinctus*) — meaning his citation cannot logically be read as a singular type-species restriction for *Lejosophis*, contradicting Dunn's interpretation.
- **Jan's later actions:** In his *Iconographie Générale des Ophidiens* (1863–1881), [[Giorgio Jan]] transferred *gigas* to genus *Xenodon*, implicitly leaving *Coluber bicinctus* Hermann as the sole remaining species in *Lejosophis* — constituting, per Peters, a valid subsequent monotypic fixation of the type-species as *bicinctus*, not *gigas*.
- **Cope's original intent (1885):** [[Edward Drinker Cope]] considered *Lejosophis* a misspelling of the preoccupied name *Liophis* (Wagler, 1830) and coined *Cyclagras* as a replacement name, discussing both *gigas* and *bicinctus* — meaning *Cyclagras* nominally inherited both species without a type designation at that time.
- **Resolution proposed by Peters:** Recognizes **two valid genera**:
  - **Hydrodynastes Fitzinger, 1843** — type-species *Coluber bicinctus* Hermann (= *Elaps Schrankii* Wagler); synonyms include *Lejosophis* Jan, 1863 and *Dugandia* Dunn, 1944.
  - **Cyclagras Cope, 1885** — type-species *Xenodon gigas* Duméril, fixed by subsequent monotypy via Boulenger (1894).
- **Practical outcome:** This resolution avoids destabilizing the long-established name *Cyclagras* and avoids requiring a petition to the [[International Commission on Zoological Nomenclature]], which would otherwise be needed if *Lejosophis* were upheld as senior synonym.
- **Adopted in:** Peters states this interpretation would be followed in the forthcoming *Catalogue of Neotropical Squamata* (Peters & Orejas-Miranda), cementing practical taxonomic stability over strict priority-based reinterpretation.
