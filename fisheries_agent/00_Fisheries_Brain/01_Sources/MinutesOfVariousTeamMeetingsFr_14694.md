# Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team Meeting Minutes (1977-1987)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #endangered-species #recovery-team

## Executive Summary

This document comprises meeting minutes and correspondence from the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] spanning 1977 to 1987. The Team, composed of representatives from state and federal agencies (including [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Bureau of Reclamation]], [[National Park Service]], and state wildlife divisions from Colorado, Utah, California, Arizona, Nevada, and New Mexico), was tasked with developing and implementing recovery plans for four endangered Colorado River fishes: [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). The minutes document the evolution of recovery strategies, including critical habitat designation, hatchery propagation programs, reintroduction efforts, research coordination, and responses to water development projects threatening these species.

## Background & Description

The [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] was established under the [[Endangered Species Act]] to coordinate recovery efforts for native large-river fishes of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. The Team held regular meetings (typically 2-3 times annually) at various locations including Salt Lake City, Las Vegas, Albuquerque, and St. George.

**Species Addressed:**

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*): The largest cyprinid native to North America, historically distributed throughout the Colorado River system. By the 1970s-80s, populations were severely reduced, with remaining populations in the [[Yampa River]], [[Green River]], upper [[Colorado River]], and [[White River]].

- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*): A distinctive cyprinid with a pronounced dorsal hump, occurring in localized populations in canyon reaches. Known populations included [[Black Rocks]], [[Westwater Canyon]], [[Cataract Canyon]], [[Desolation Canyon]], [[Gray Canyon]], [[Dinosaur National Monument]], and the [[Little Colorado River]].

- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*): By the 1980s, considered functionally extinct in riverine habitats of the Upper Basin, with only remnant populations in [[Lake Mohave]] and occasional individuals in [[Gray Canyon]].

- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*): A large catostomid with distinctive dorsal ridge, showing declining populations throughout the basin with no evidence of successful recruitment by the mid-1980s.

**Team Composition:** The Team included designated members from each state and federal agency, plus consultants including university researchers (Dr. C.O. Minckley, Dr. Robert Miller, Dr. Carl Hubbs, Dr. Ross Bulkley) and contractors (Bio/West, [[Ecological Consultants Inc.]]).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Requirements:** The endangered fishes evolved in a dynamic large-river system characterized by high spring flows, warm summer temperatures, and turbid conditions. Critical habitat features included:
- Canyon reaches with backwaters and eddies
- Spawning areas with cobble substrates
- Nursery habitats in low-velocity areas
- Migration corridors connecting spawning and rearing areas

**Threats Identified:**
1. **Habitat Modification:** Construction of dams ([[Flaming Gorge Dam]], [[Glen Canyon Dam]], [[Navajo Reservoir]]) altered flow regimes, temperatures, and sediment transport
2. **Water Depletion:** Proposed projects including [[White River Dam]], [[Juniper-Cross Mountain Project]], [[Dominguez Project]], [[Moon Lake Project]], [[Colorado-Big Thompson-Windy Gap]], [[Taylor Draw Dam]]
3. **Non-native Species:** Competition and predation from introduced species including northern pike, channel catfish, and striped bass
4. **Fragmentation:** Dams blocked migration routes and isolated populations

**Research Programs:** Multiple studies were coordinated through the Team:
- [[Colorado River Fishery Project]] (CRFP): Intensive life history, habitat use, and radio telemetry studies
- State monitoring programs (Colorado, Utah)
- [[Bio/West]] studies on [[Yampa River]], [[Green River]], and [[Flaming Gorge]] effects
- [[Utah State University]] studies on thermal preferences, swimming performance, and striped bass food habits
- Taxonomic and hybridization studies

## Key Findings

**Recovery Plan Development (1977-1987):**

1. **Colorado Squawfish Recovery Plan:** First drafted in 1978, revised multiple times through 1987. Final revision (1987) included:
   - Primary goal: Restore and maintain self-sustaining populations
   - Delisting criteria: Establishment of viable populations in historic range
   - Key strategies: Habitat protection, flow maintenance, hatchery propagation, reintroduction, research on life history and habitat requirements

2. **Humpback Chub Recovery Plan:** Developed as separate plan (not addendum to squawfish plan as initially proposed). 1987 revision established delisting criteria: "Six self-sustaining populations, two of which would be in the Lower Basin, and two refugia maintained with habitats legally protected."

3. **Bonytail Chub Recovery Plan:** Contracted to Bio/West (Paul Holden) in 1980-81. By 1987, drastically revised to prevent extinction:
   - Collect all wild individuals for captive propagation
   - Immediate reintroduction of ~100 adult F1 generation fish to [[Green River]] ([[Island Park]], [[Dinosaur National Monument]]) with radio telemetry
   - Release F1 offspring into [[Lake Mohave]] for grow-out
   - Establish research population in [[Gila River]] between Coulee and Asherst Dams

**Critical Habitat Designations:**

For [[Humpback Chub]] (November 1977):
1. [[Colorado River]] from Mack Wash to Cisco boat takeout below [[Westwater Canyon]]
2. [[Yampa River]] in [[Dinosaur National Monument]] and [[Green River]] below Yampa confluence
3. [[Green River]] ([[Desolation Canyon]] and [[Gray Canyon]]) from Sand Wash to diversion structure above Green River, Utah
4. Lower seven miles of [[Little Colorado River]] to Colorado River confluence
5. [[Colorado River]] from [[Little Colorado River]] to Shinumo Creek

**Hatchery Programs:**

- [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] (Arizona): Primary propagation facility for Colorado squawfish and humpback chub. Resolution passed (November 1977) restricting facility to these two species only; razorback suckers transferred to [[Hotchkiss National Fish Hatchery]] (Colorado) and [[Chino State Fish Hatchery]] (California)
- [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico): Developed as endangered fish facility; 100 1974-year-class squawfish transferred from Willow Beach (January 1979)
- Production numbers (1977): 2,100 squawfish at Hotchkiss (average 380 mm); squawfish at Chino averaged 300 mm

**Reintroduction Efforts:**

- **Lower Basin sites selected (July 1977):**
  - [[San Juan River]] below [[Navajo Reservoir]]
  - [[Salt River]] at Highway 60 bridge (1,200 razorback suckers and 1,500 Colorado squawfish planted)
  - [[Copper Wash]], [[Imperial Valley]], California (squawfish stocked)
  - [[Senator Wash Reservoir]] (deferred due to landownership problems)

- **Experimental populations:** By 1987, Team recommended against granting "non-essential experimental" status for additional Lower Basin squawfish populations, as limited protection might preclude populations from contributing to delisting criteria

**Research Findings:**

- **Movement and Migration:** Radio telemetry (1980) documented extensive movements: one squawfish traveled from [[Green River]] up [[Yampa River]], back to Green, then into [[White River]], covering several hundred miles. Another fish tagged by CRFP moved 100 miles up Yampa River.

- **Distribution discoveries:**
  - Young-of-year squawfish collected in [[Dinosaur National Monument]] nine miles upstream from Green River confluence (1980)
  - Adult squawfish captured 10 miles upstream from Rangely, Colorado in White River (1980)
  - 40+ adult squawfish captured in [[Gypsum Canyon]] below [[Cataract Canyon]] in [[Lake Powell]] arm; fish were extremely fat, apparently feeding on threadfin shad (1987)
  - Humpback chub populations located in [[Black Rocks]], [[Westwater]], [[Cataract]], and [[DeBeque Canyons]] (1980)
  - Squawfish captured upstream from Whitewater, Colorado on [[Gunnison River]] (1980)

- **Population Status:** By 1987, only ~1,000 adult razorback suckers estimated in Upper Basin with no successful recruitment. Team recommended immediate listing as "endangered" (upgraded from earlier "threatened" recommendation).

- **Hybridization:** Taxonomic confusion among *Gila* species complicated management. No "pure" bonytail individuals collected in Upper Basin by 1983, though specimens with ~80% *G. elegans* characteristics were found.

**Water Development Consultations:**

- **[[Section 7 Consultation]]:** By 1980, 20 biological opinions scheduled for Bureau of Reclamation projects on Yampa, White, Green, Colorado, and Gunnison Rivers
- **[[Taylor Draw Dam]]/[[Kenney Reservoir]]:** Team recommended (1987):
  - Defer stocking for squawfish sport fishery
  - Continue research on importance of upper [[White River]] reaches
  - Avoid stocking offspring of Dexter broodstock to preserve local genetics
  - If stocking proceeds, mark all fish and monitor effects on wild populations
  - Team recommended reopening Section 7 consultation due to new information and indefinite postponement of [[White River Dam]]

- **[[Redlands Diversion]]:** Team concurred with Biological Subcommittee (1987) to defer construction of fish passage, complete feasibility study, and retain funds for research

- **[[Tusher Wash]] (Thane Hydroelectric Plant):** Low-head hydropower project could take entire flow during low periods, presenting barrier to fish passage; exemption from environmental statement denied

**Conservation Planning:**

- **Upper Basin Conservation Plan:** Developed by FWS Region 6 (presented December 1982) to coordinate research, management, and Section 7 consultations. Proposed [[Upper Colorado River Basin Endangered Fishes Council]] with representatives from USBR, USFWS, state agencies, USFS, BLM, and NPS.

- **Team Position (May 1982):** Recovery Team voted to:
  - Support separation between Conservation Plan and Recovery Plans
  - Not recognize Conservation Plan as part of Recovery Plans but willing to review
  - Urge inclusion of Lower Basin in Conservation Plan or development of separate Lower Basin plan
  - Delete all Conservation Plan references from Recovery Plans

- **Lower Basin Approach:** Region 2 pursued interagency Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) rather than formal Conservation Plan. Lower Basin Subcommittee established to coordinate activities for 17 species proposed for listing, concentrating on four large river fishes.

**Coordination and Communication:**

- **Research Coordination:** Annual [[Colorado River Fishes Planning Meeting]] initiated (December 1982) to review research and coordinate efforts
- **Information Dissemination:** Informal newsletter established (1982) with Linda Ulmer (California Fish & Game) coordinating three-times-yearly updates from state and federal agencies
- **Tagging Protocol:** Problems identified with tagging scheme; agencies reminded to adhere to agreed-upon protocols to avoid misidentification

**Team Function and Budget:**

- Role redefined after plan completion (1979-1980): Team to develop plans, monitor implementation, revise plans, and designate critical habitat—not to implement plans directly
- Budget constraints limited meetings: FY 1981 budget of $8,000 for three species ($4,000 for bonytail plan contract, $2,000 per species for meetings at ~$1,600-1,800 each)
- By 1983, ~$3,000 remained for team activities
- Discussions of disbanding team after plan completion, but coordination and communication roles deemed valuable

**Scientific Collecting Permits:**

Application by Dr. C.O. Minckley (July 1977) approved with stipulations: gill nets not allowed as collection gear, and fish not to be placed in [[Dexter Fish Hatchery]] (New Mexico).

**Key Publications Available (by 1983):**

- "Fishes of the Upper Colorado River System: Present and Future" (Miller, Tyus and Carlson 1982)
- "Colorado Squawfish Investigations - White River, Utah" (Radant, Cranney, Ruesink and Rose 1983)
- "Colorado River Fishes Monitoring Project; First Annual Report" (Miller, Tyus and Kaeding 1983)
- "Windy Gap Fishes Study: First Annual Report" (Miller, Kaeding and Tyus 1983)
- "Movement, Migration and Habitat Preference of Radiotelemetered Colorado Squawfish" (Miller, Tyus and McAda 1983)
