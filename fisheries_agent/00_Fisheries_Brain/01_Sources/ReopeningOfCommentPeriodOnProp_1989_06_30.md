## Executive Summary
This document is a 1989 regulatory letter from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Ecological Services, Albuquerque, NM) reopening the public comment period on a proposal to reclassify the [[Gila Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus gilae*) from **Endangered** to **Threatened** status under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973, as amended. The letter is addressed to Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University) soliciting scientific comment and data. No critical habitat designation was proposed alongside this reclassification. The core scientific justification cited is the successful recovery-driven replication of the species' five original wild populations, each deemed biologically viable and secure.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Taxonomic context**: [[Gila Trout]] is a salmonid native to the [[Gila River]] drainage of the American Southwest (Arizona/New Mexico), historically restricted to cool, high-elevation headwater streams.
- **Population structure**: Recovery efforts referenced in this letter resulted in the **replication of five original populations**, implying a conservation strategy of translocating/establishing new populations from source (relict) populations to reduce extinction risk from stochastic events (fire, drought, flood).
- **Habitat**: Freshwater, coldwater mountain stream habitat is implied but not detailed; no [[Critical Habitat]] was proposed as part of this action, suggesting the Service considered existing protections/management sufficient at the time.
- **Conservation status trajectory**: Movement from Endangered → Threatened reflects an improving population trend, situating this species within the broader recovery narrative of Southwestern native trout alongside relatives like the [[Apache Trout]] and [[Rio Grande Cutthroat Trout]].

## Key Findings
- **Regulatory action**: Federal Register notice reopened the public comment period on reclassifying *Oncorhynchus gilae* from Endangered to Threatened.
- **Comment deadline**: July 26, 1989.
- **No critical habitat proposed** as part of this reclassification action.
- **Biological justification**: All five original wild populations of Gila trout have been successfully replicated through recovery efforts, and each replicate/original population is characterized as "biologically viable and secure."
- **Agency conclusion**: The Fish and Wildlife Service determined that reclassification to Threatened status is scientifically warranted based on this population security.
- **Contacts**: Gerald Burton (Endangered Species Biologist, USFWS Albuquerque) served as technical point of contact; Michael Z. Donahoo signed as Acting Field Supervisor.
