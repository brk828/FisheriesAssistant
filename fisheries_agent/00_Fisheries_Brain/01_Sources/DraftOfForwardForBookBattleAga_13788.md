# Forward Draft for "Battle Against Extinction: Native Fishes of the Western United States"

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This document is a July 1990 letter from a Department of Zoology faculty member to Stewart L. Udall, former Secretary of the Interior, requesting a forward for an edited volume on endangered western fishes. The letter includes a draft forward outlining key conservation controversies involving aquatic species in the American West, including the [[Devils Hole]] pupfish case, the [[Snail Darter]] and Tellico Dam controversy, and impacts of major water development projects on native fish populations. The book, co-edited with Dr. James E. Deacon of University of Nevada Las Vegas, was scheduled for publication by University of Arizona Press in late 1990 or early 1991, comprising approximately 700 pages with 20 chapters by leading aquatic scientists.

## Background & Description

The draft forward contextualizes endangered fish conservation within broader western water policy debates. Key historical cases mentioned include:

- **[[Flaming Gorge Dam]]** on the [[Green River]]: Fish poisoning operations in the early 1960s that attracted attention to aquatic conservation issues
- **[[Devils Hole]] pupfish case**: U.S. Supreme Court decision favoring habitat protection and pupfish over development in [[Ash Meadows]], Nevada, establishing precedent for Federal Reserved Water Rights
- **[[Snail Darter]]** controversy: Tellico Dam decision in Tennessee that led to exemption tactics under the [[Endangered Species Act]]
- **[[Glen Canyon Dam]]**: Peaking power operations causing tide-like fluctuations through [[Grand Canyon National Park]], eroding beaches and jeopardizing native fishes

The forward emphasizes that individual species serve as "messengers of change" even though ecosystem-level conservation should be the ultimate goal. Water development in the arid West is characterized as evolving from local, muscle-powered efforts to major federal engineering projects (dams, diversions, irrigation works, power plants) that enabled post-World War II growth but created unsustainable demands.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The draft describes native western fishes as part of a "spectacular natural system caught up in this maelstrom of change." Specific ecological impacts noted include:

- **Physical alterations**: Reservoir construction both upstream and downstream of native fish habitats
- **Biological impacts**: Introduction of non-native species and habitat degradation
- **Hydrological disruption**: Tide-like fluctuations from hydroelectric peaking power operations
- **Habitat loss**: Beach erosion, water level fluctuations, and ecosystem destabilization

The document criticizes water use for purposes beyond original development intent, including recreational lakes, ornamental fountains in urban sprawl around [[Phoenix]] and [[Las Vegas]], and excessive demands from projects like the [[Central Arizona Project]] affecting [[Tucson]].

The forward emphasizes that large tracts of public lands in the American West provide opportunity for conservation if restraint is applied, contrasting with newly colonized areas where exploitation typically continues until resources are exhausted.

## Key Findings

**Conservation Philosophy**: The draft argues that endangered species legislation and individual species protection, while not ideal compared to ecosystem-level approaches, remain essential tools. Cases involving aquatic species have been pivotal in:
- Solidifying resolve of aquatic scientists to fight for the [[Endangered Species Act]]
- Establishing legal precedents for Federal Reserved Water Rights
- Demonstrating interrelation of ground and surface water in western water law

**Policy Concerns**: The forward warns that exemption tactics first used for the [[Snail Darter]] have been repeated for other species including the [[Concho Water Snake]] in Texas, [[Mt. Graham Red Squirrel]] in Arizona, and potentially the [[Spotted Owl]] in the Pacific Northwest.

**Water Development Critique**: Major federal water projects are characterized as having "changed the region forever," with current uses driven by "avarice and greed" rather than regional needs. Specific problematic uses include:
- Recreational lake filling in water-poor deserts
- Hydroelectric power for ornamental fountains 50+ meters high
- Urban sprawl exceeding sustainable water supplies

**Educational Mission**: The book is positioned as providing vital information for conservation, dealing with "philosophy and ethics of conservation" and documenting "both failures and successes of the past as guides to the future." It presents native fishes in ecosystem context with positive recommendations for maintaining biodiversity.

**Stewardship Vision**: The forward calls for educating future generations to act as stewards of earth, developing "equity between humans and their environment" and "balance between resource use and human existence," with the American West serving as an example for global resource management.
