# 1982 Report on the Survey of the Mammals of the George Whittell Wildlife Preserve, Graham and Pinal Counties, Arizona

#mammalogy #wildlife-survey #biodiversity #population-ecology #desert-ecosystems

## Executive Summary

This is the fourth annual progress report (1979-1982) on a comprehensive mammal survey of the [[George Whittell Wildlife Preserve]] in the [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed, Arizona. The survey documented 60 mammal species as actual, probable, or possible inhabitants, with 45 species confirmed for the Preserve itself. Seven species previously unknown from the watershed were discovered: [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]], [[Cactus Mouse]], [[Yellow-nosed Cotton Rat]], [[Western Harvest Mouse]], [[Western Pipistrelle]], [[Red Bat]], and [[Long-tongued Bat]]. The study employed both direct methods (live-trapping grids, bat netting, reconnaissance) and indirect methods (literature review, museum records, local knowledge). Intensive population monitoring of nocturnal rodents across six vegetation associations revealed significant temporal and spatial variation in density and biomass, with highest values in Lower Desertscrub, Semi-desert Scrub with Juniper, and Riparian Forest associations. The area represents a biogeographic transition zone where Sonoran, Great Plains, Chihuahuan, and Madrean faunal elements converge.

## Background & Description

The [[Aravaipa Creek]] watershed and the [[George Whittell Wildlife Preserve]] within it had been poorly studied prior to this survey, with most existing records limited to larger, conspicuous species. The survey was funded entirely by the [[Defenders of Wildlife Trust]] in cooperation with the [[Bureau of Land Management]] and the [[Arizona Natural Heritage Program]]. Field work totaled 187 days from fall 1979 through October 1982 (26 days in 1979, 52 days in 1980, 52 days in 1981, 57 days in 1982).

The study area encompasses multiple biotic communities: [[Deciduous Riparian Forest Biome]], [[Sonoran Desertscrub Biome]], [[Desert Grassland]]/[[Semi-desert Scrubland Biome]], and [[Chaparral Biome]]. Elevations range from approximately 2,160 ft to over 6,000 ft. The watershed is bounded by the [[Galiuro Mountains]], [[Santa Teresa Mountains]], and [[Pinaleno Mountains]] (Graham Mountains).

The mammal fauna reflects the area's position at a biogeographic crossroads. The Preserve lies on the southeastern edge of [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]] range, northern edge of [[Coati]] and [[Yellow-nosed Cotton Rat]] ranges, eastern edge of [[Rock Pocket Mouse]] range, and near western limits of [[Spotted Ground Squirrel]] and [[Northern Grasshopper Mouse]]. This positioning creates a diverse assemblage combining Sonoran, Great Plains, Chihuahuan, and Madrean elements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat Associations**: Six major vegetation associations were intensively studied using permanent census grids:

1. **Riparian Forest** (2,600-3,110 ft): Floodplain communities along [[Aravaipa Creek]] dominated by cottonwood, sycamore, and willow
2. **Lower Desertscrub** (2,920 ft): Easterly-facing hillslopes with typical Sonoran Desert vegetation
3. **Yucca Shrub-grassland** (4,280 ft): Gently sloping ridgetops with yucca and grasses
4. **Semi-desert Scrub with Juniper** (4,030-4,090 ft): Sloping ridgetops with juniper and desert scrub elements
5. **Chaparral with Pinyon and Juniper** (4,350 ft): Level basin bottoms with mixed chaparral and woodland

**Environmental Influences**: Population dynamics of nocturnal rodents showed strong correlation with precipitation patterns. Populations peaked in late summer/fall 1979 following unusually heavy rains in fall/winter 1978-1979, then declined through winter 1979-1980 (decreases of 19.5% to 61.5%). Populations reached lows in spring 1982 before rebounding dramatically by fall 1982, possibly reflecting cumulative effects of two years of average to good rainfall.

**Community Structure**: High rodent density and biomass in Lower Desertscrub, Semi-desert Scrub with Juniper, and Riparian Forest correlated with high plant diversity and structural complexity. Total nocturnal rodent density ranged from 0.0 to 62.2 individuals/hectare across associations and seasons. Biomass estimates ranged from 0.0 to 3.8 kg/hectare.

**Historical Context**: The area has experienced multiple waves of human occupation spanning over 9,000 years, from [[Desert Cochise Culture]] (7500-200 B.C.) through [[Salado]] puebloan peoples (A.D. 1100-1450), [[Sobaipuri]] agricultural communities (Spanish contact period), [[Apache]] occupation (1700s-1870s), to modern Mexican and Anglo settlement. Three species were extirpated historically: [[Beaver]] (Castor canadensis), [[Muskrat]] (Ondatra zibethicus), and [[Gray Wolf]] (Canis lupus, Mexican subspecies).

## Key Findings

**Species Diversity and Documentation**:
- 60 mammal species documented for the Aravaipa Creek watershed (actual, probable, or possible occurrence)
- 45 species confirmed for the George Whittell Wildlife Preserve specifically
- 7 species newly documented for the watershed: [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]] (Perognathus baileyi), [[Cactus Mouse]] (Peromyscus eremicus), [[Yellow-nosed Cotton Rat]] (Sigmodon ochrognathus), [[Western Harvest Mouse]] (Reithrodontomys megalotis), [[Western Pipistrelle]] (Pipistrellus hesperus), [[Red Bat]] (Lasiurus borealis), [[Long-tongued Bat]] (Choeronycteris mexicana)
- Survey filled distribution gaps for 5 species and extended known ranges for 3 others

**Population Density Estimates** (nocturnal rodents, individuals/hectare):

*Lower Desertscrub*:
- [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]]: 0-50.8 (highest 40.8 in fall 1979)
- [[Cactus Mouse]]: 0-14.7
- Total density: 3.2-62.2 individuals/ha

*Riparian Forest East*:
- [[Brush Mouse]] (Peromyscus boylii): 7.3-18.2
- [[Arizona Cotton Rat]] (Sigmodon arizonae): 1.2-33.9
- Total density: 16.6-46.8 individuals/ha

*Semi-desert Scrub with Juniper*:
- [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]]: 5.8-20.3
- Total density: 8.5-31.7 individuals/ha

**Biomass Estimates** (kg/hectare):
- Lower Desertscrub: 0.21-1.88 kg/ha
- Riparian Forest East: 0.75-3.8 kg/ha (highest values)
- Semi-desert Scrub with Juniper: 0.49-1.48 kg/ha
- Chaparral: 0.0-0.53 kg/ha (lowest values)

**Temporal Patterns**:
- Population peak in fall 1979 followed heavy winter 1978-1979 precipitation
- Decline of 19.5-61.5% over winter 1979-1980
- Continued decline through spring 1982
- Dramatic increase by fall 1982 (possibly cumulative effect of 2 years good rainfall)
- Two distinct seasonal patterns emerged after 1980: (a) summer increases followed by winter decreases (expected temperate pattern), and (b) summer decreases with subsequent leveling (cause unclear)

**Notable Species Accounts**:

*Bats* (10 species documented):
- [[Long-tongued Bat]]: 3 individuals in adobe barn (September 1981), 5 individuals (September 1982); summer resident feeding on nectar and pollen
- [[Cave Myotis]] (Myotis velifer): Netted at both canyon ends; colonial roosting
- [[Western Pipistrelle]]: Most easily observed bat, active before sunset; year-round resident
- [[Townsend's Big-eared Bat]] (Plecotus townsendii): Maternity colony documented in Oak Grove Canyon (3,550 ft) in 1958; easily disturbed

*Carnivores*:
- [[Coati]] (Nasua nasua): Breeding population confirmed; troops of 15-25 individuals observed; represents northern limit of documented breeding; diurnal, travels in troops
- [[Mountain Lion]] (Felis concolor): Common at higher elevations, rare at lower; tracks and scat found; mainly nocturnal
- [[Ringtail]] (Bassariscus astutus): Common but rarely observed; strictly nocturnal; rock and cliff specialist

*Ungulates*:
- [[Desert Bighorn Sheep]] (Ovis canadensis mexicana): Reintroduced 1973 (22 animals released); population increased to 34-80 by 1980; dispersed widely including to [[San Carlos Apache Reservation]], [[Santa Teresa Mountains]], [[Galiuro Mountains]]
- [[Mule Deer]] (Odocoileus hemionus): Often observed/common; observed ratio to white-tailed deer approximately 2:1
- [[White-tailed Deer]] (Odocoileus virginianus): Often observed/common; more use of herbs and grasses than mule deer

*Rodents*:
- [[White-throated Woodrat]] (Neotoma albigula): Usually observed/common at lower elevations; conspicuous stick dens; mean weight 171.9 g
- [[Arizona Cotton Rat]]: Density 1.2-33.9/ha in Riparian Forest; rapid population response to favorable conditions; may breed year-round starting at 6 weeks
- [[Cliff Chipmunk]] (Eutamias dorsalis): Usually observed/common around cliffs and rocky canyons; diurnal; distinctive tail-flicking behavior

**Biogeographic Significance**:
- Area represents edge of breeding distribution for at least 11 small mammal species
- Positioned at both east-west and north-south biogeographic dividing lines
- Fauna includes Sonoran forms (western desertscrub), Great Plains and Chihuahuan forms (southeastern grasslands), and Madrean forms (southern evergreen woodlands)

**Conservation Status**:
- [[Desert Bighorn Sheep]] and [[Gray Wolf]] (Mexican subspecies) listed as threatened/endangered
- [[Gray Wolf]] probably extirpated; last confirmed records from 1950s in [[Pinaleno Mountains]]
- [[Beaver]] and [[Muskrat]] historically present but now extirpated
- [[Grizzly Bear]] (Ursus arctos) extirpated by 1912

**Methodological Notes**:
- Census grids: 32 stations per grid (two parallel lines, 16 stations each, 15 m spacing)
- Two Sherman live traps per station
- Trapping conducted 3 nights per session, preferably during last/first quarter moon
- Population estimates calculated using [[Peterson Index]] (Lincoln Index) modified by [[Schnabel Method]]
- Area sampled estimated by extending grid boundaries by home range radius or perpendicular movement distance
- Assessment lines at 45° or 90° to census lines provided movement data

**Habitat-Density Relationships**:
- Highest densities and biomass correlated with high plant diversity and structural complexity
- [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]] found across five associations (2,600-4,300 ft); common characteristic: rocky soil with vegetation openings
- [[Cactus Mouse]] and [[Bailey's Pocket Mouse]] most abundant rodents at lower elevations in rocky habitats
- [[Brush Mouse]] generally at higher elevations, brushier and more mesic than [[Cactus Mouse]]
