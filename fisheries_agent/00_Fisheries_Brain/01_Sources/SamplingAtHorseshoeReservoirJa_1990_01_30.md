## Executive Summary

This memorandum reports results of a January 1990 fish survey at [[Horseshoe Reservoir]] on the [[Verde River]], Arizona, conducted under severely drawn-down conditions (~1% of reservoir capacity). Sampling combined trammel/gill netting and boat electroshocking and yielded 705 netted fish (7 species) and 1,001 electroshocked fish (11 species). The critical finding was the recovery of two [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) specimens, plus a third individual observed but not captured, confirming that reintroduced razorback suckers stocked upstream in the Verde River can survive, disperse, and reach the first downstream impoundment. This is presented as evidence supporting the viability of the species' reintroduction program in the [[Colorado River Basin]] and as justification for continued targeted sampling effort at this site.

## Background & Description

Only the two captured [[Razorback Sucker]] specimens received individual morphological documentation:

- **Specimen 1 (gravid female):** 44 cm total length (TL), 957 g body mass
- **Specimen 2 (immature male):** 28 cm TL, 220 g body mass

Both fish were examined under ultraviolet light for fluorescent pigment marking (a common hatchery-stocking marker technique); **no fluorescent pigment was detected** in either specimen, suggesting these fish were not marked via that method at stocking, or that pigment had degraded/was undetectable by the time of recapture. Otoliths were extracted from both specimens for future aging analysis, intended to back-calculate approximate stocking dates and time-at-large. Both specimens were retained and deposited at Arizona State University (ASU) for further study.

No detailed morphological data were recorded for other species collected; the report is limited to enumerative catch data (see [[Key Findings]] table below).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat conditions at time of sampling:** Reservoir at ~1% capacity, restricting deep-water habitat to a single 2-hectare pool near the dam extending into Deadman Wash, with maximum depth ~1 m. River inflow zone was extremely shallow (<0.25 m), highly turbid, with silt-dominated substrate — indicative of a stressed, drought-affected lentic system transitioning to riverine conditions upstream.
- **Community composition:** The reservoir fish assemblage was dominated numerically by non-native [[Common Carp]] (up to 83% of net catch, 43-63% of electroshock catch) and [[Threadfin Shad]] (10-20%), reflecting a heavily altered, non-native-dominated reservoir fish community typical of impounded southwestern U.S. rivers.
- **Native species representation:** Native fishes were a minor component of total catch, represented only by [[Razorback Sucker]] (trace abundance) and [[Desert Sucker]] (trace abundance, netting only), underscoring the rarity of native catostomids relative to introduced centrarchids, ictalurids, and cyprinids in this system.
- **Ecological significance of razorback sucker recovery:** The recovery of stocked razorback suckers at Horseshoe Reservoir — the first impoundment downstream of Verde River stocking sites — demonstrates realized connectivity and downstream dispersal capacity of reintroduced individuals, an important consideration for [[Endangered Fish Reintroduction Programs]] in the Colorado River Basin, where razorback sucker populations have collapsed largely due to habitat alteration and predation/competition from non-native fishes documented in this same survey (carp, sunfishes, catfishes).
- **Low-water opportunity:** The drought-driven concentration of fish into a small residual pool inadvertently created favorable, low-effort sampling conditions for detecting rare native species that would otherwise be difficult to capture in a full reservoir.

- **Total netting catch:** 705 fish, 7 species (12-14 January)
- **Total electroshocking catch:** 1,001 fish, 11 species (26-27 January); overall CPE = 6.0 fish/hour
- **Razorback sucker recovery:** 2 confirmed captures (1 gravid female, 1 immature male) + 1 reliable sight observation not recovered = minimum 3 individuals detected
- **UV fluorescent marking check:** Negative for both captured razorback suckers
- **Age/stocking assessment:** Otoliths extracted from both razorback suckers for future age determination to back-calculate stocking timelines
- **Management conclusion:** Reintroduced razorback suckers can survive and migrate downstream to reach Horseshoe Reservoir from upstream Verde River stocking sites; given low sampling effort required and favorable low-water conditions, the report recommends continued/expanded sampling to locate additional reintroduced individuals and strengthen the reintroduction program assessment dataset.
- **Specimen disposition:** Both razorback sucker specimens retained and deposited at Arizona State University for future research use.
