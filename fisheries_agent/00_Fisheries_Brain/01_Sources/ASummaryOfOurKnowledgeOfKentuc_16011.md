# A Summary of Our Knowledge of Kentucky Crustaceans

#crustacea #freshwater-biology #cladocera #copepoda #ostracoda #isopoda #amphipoda #decapoda

## Executive Summary

This 1959 review by [[Gerald A. Cole]] consolidates published records of crustaceans from [[Kentucky]], reports taxa not previously listed for the state, and flags unresolved taxonomic problems. More than 20 species had been described from Kentucky material, yet knowledge of the class within state boundaries was considered incomplete. The author's compiled list of apparently valid species and subspecies includes 116 names, of which 28 are reported for the first time. The paper is a guide to the literature rather than a complete bibliography.

## Background & Description

The paper covers the major crustacean groups recorded in Kentucky: Branchiopoda (fairy shrimps and conchostracans), Cladocera, Copepoda (calanoids, cyclopoids, harpacticoids and parasitic forms), Ostracoda, and Malacostraca (isopods, amphipods and decapods). Three lists were deliberately omitted: [[Reelfoot Lake]] crustaceans (Hoff, 1943, 1944a), which were collected mainly in Tennessee, and Ward's (1940) Cincinnati-area pond entomostracans from Ohio.

Taxonomy is a central theme. Many older Kentucky records use names that have since been revised, synonymized, or shown to be unrecognizable, and several rely on uncertain type data.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Habitats represented in the records include:

- **Temporary vernal pools:** Fairy shrimps are present from roughly December or January into July. Several copepods (for example, [[Osphranticum labronectum]] and *Cyclops latipes*) are associated with a typical temporary-pond fauna.
- **Lakes and reservoirs:** [[Tom Wallace Lake]] (Jefferson County) and [[Dale Hollow Lake]] supplied plankton and net-plankton material; [[Cumberland Lake]] contributed a southern cladoceran.
- **Caves and subterranean waters:** [[Mammoth Cave]] (including Echo River and Wandering Willie's Spring), Horse Cave, and other sites yielded blind or cave-adapted crustaceans.
- **Springs, streams and rivulets:** Spring-fed streams near Lexington and eastern Kentucky support asellid and amphipod communities.
- **Ohio River and farm ponds:** Louisville-area river and pond samples contributed calanoids, cyclopoids and cladocerans.
- **Human-associated habitats:** Several records come from aquaria, a campground wood pile, and a cemetery duck pond, indicating accidental introductions or unusual microhabitats.

Biogeographic notes include the observation that the cladoceran *Bosminopsis deitersi* is probably the only typically southern cladoceran known from the state, and that most terrestrial isopods recorded were probably introduced from Europe.

## Key Findings

**Branchiopoda**
- Three fairy shrimp species are recorded: [[Eubranchipus Vernalis]], [[Eubranchipus Neglectus]] (Garman, 1920, from the "Bluegrass" region, with no record since Dexter's 1956 Ohio specimens), and *Streptocephalus seali*. Taxonomic status of *E. neglectus* is problematic; the author urges a concentrated search of temporary ponds around the type locality.
- Only one conchostracan, *Cyzicus mexicanus*, is definitely known from Kentucky. Other undetermined conchostracans suggest there may be more than one species.

**Cladocera**
- Eighteen cladoceran records were published before this paper. Cole (1957b) listed 16 planktonic and microbenthic species from Tom Wallace Lake.
- New state records include *Bosmina coregoni*, *Bosminopsis deitersi*, *Moina affinis* and *Leydigia acanthocercoides*.
- Historical records by Herrick (1883) are questionable. The Kentucky "Decatur" localities may actually refer to Decatur, Alabama, and collections may have been from Paducah.
- Records of [[Daphnia Pulex]] and *D. longispina* require reexamination after Brooks' (1957) monograph, which showed *D. longispina* does not occur in North America.

**Copepoda**
- *Calanoida:* Records include *Diaptomus pallidus*, *D. sanguineus*, *D. stagnalis*, and *Osphranticum labronectum*. Earlier reports of *D. castor* from America are probably in error.
- *Cyclopoida:* Cole (1953) listed 10 cyclopoids from the Louisville region, and later Jefferson County records added *Macrocyclops ater* and *Cyclops latipes*. Many historical Mammoth Cave names have been reassigned to other genera.
- *Harpacticoida:* Four species have been reported, two of them doubtful. Subterranean forms include *Attheyella pilosa* and *Bryocamptus morrisoni elegans*. At least six or seven additional harpacticoid species are estimated to await recording.
- *Parasitic forms:* [[Cauloxenus Stygius]] was reported from cave fish, but its host in Mammoth Cave is doubtful because *Amblyopsis* is scarce there. Lernaeids on bluegills and an *Argulus* from an aquarium are noted.

**Ostracoda**
- Only six species had been recorded before this paper. Five further species are added: *Cyprinotus* sp., *Darwinula stevensoni*, *Cypria turneri*, a *Candona* sp., and *Chlamydotheca unispinosa*.
- The author estimates that the ten known species may represent no more than a third of the state fauna. *Entocythere* in crayfish branchial chambers is expected to occur.

**Isopoda**
- *Asellota:* Cole (1957a) recorded five epigean asellids near Louisville, four new to the state. [[Lirceus Fontinalis]] (Rafinesque, 1820) is the most abundant species; its name was prioritized over Garman's *Mancasellus macrourus* by Hubricht and Mackin (1949).
- The subterranean [[Asellus Stygius]] (originally *Caecidotea stygia*, Packard, 1871) is fairly common in some caves and wells.
- *Oniscoidea:* Eight terrestrial species are recorded for the state, compared with 10 in Indiana. Most are introduced European species, including *Trachelipus rathkei* and *Porcellio scaber*. *Ligidium longicaudatum* is a new state record collected near Brandenburg Station.

**Amphipoda**
- Hubricht (1943) described four new species with Kentucky type localities: *Stygobromus vitreus*, *S. exilis*, *Crangonyx anomalus* and *Synurella dentata*.
- Cole (1957a) recorded *Crangonyx obliquus* and *Hyalella azteca* for the first time in the state.
- Bousfield (1958) split *C. shoemakeri* into two species, so the authentic Kentucky record of *C. shoemakeri* may be lost. Specimens from a Meade County cold stream represent an undescribed *Gammarus*.

**Decapoda**
- *Palaemonetes kadiakensis* is probably the only species of the genus in Kentucky, despite earlier labels of *P. paludosus*.
- *Macrobrachium ohione* is a large river shrimp originally described from the Ohio River.
- [[Palaemonias Gantert]], a blind, translucent atyid, is known only from Mammoth Cave.
- Rhoades (1944) listed 27 crayfish species and subspecies, adding six new species and four new subspecies. Subspecies such as *Cambarus bartoni*, represented by five forms, suggest strong isolation between drainage systems. The author notes that a "lumper" approach would reduce this diversity.

**Overall**
- The state checklist includes 116 apparently valid names, 28 of them new records. Major gaps remain in cave isopods, copepods, ostracods and crayfish taxonomy, and several historical names need reexamination.
