# Pupfish Population Supplement (Peoples Canyon)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #Poeciliidae #desert-pupfish #gila-topminnow #freshwater-conservation

## Executive Summary
This set of U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Ecological Services, Phoenix, AZ) memoranda documents a series of management actions and field observations concerning the reintroduction and population supplementation of two federally significant desert fish species: the [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) and the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) in the [[Peoples Canyon]] drainage and associated sites (Grapevine Spring, BLM Kingman Resource Area) in Arizona, under FWS Loan Agreement LA87-14 with the [[Bureau of Land Management]]. Fish stocks originated from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], sourced from the [[Santa Clara Slough]] (Mexico) lineage for pupfish and Sharp/Sheehy springs lineage for topminnow. Field monitoring in June 1988 found topminnow persisting with active recruitment but noted a complete absence of pupfish from an earlier (1982-1983) introduction attempt, prompting a subsequent large-scale supplemental transfer of ~1,000 individuals in September 1988.

## Background & Description
- **Desert Pupfish (*Cyprinodon macularius*)**: No direct morphometric data provided in source; field notes reference presence/absence and general size-class variation among transferred hatchery stock.
- **Gila Topminnow (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*)**: Observed with multiple size classes present in wild population at Peoples Canyon, indicating a naturally reproducing, age-structured population. Most females observed were **gravid** (visibly carrying developing embryos), consistent with live-bearing (poeciliid) reproductive strategy.
- No quantitative morphometric measurements (length, weight, meristics) were recorded in these memoranda; observations were qualitative field assessments during snorkel/mask surveys.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Isolated desert canyon spring-fed pools and riparian stream reaches; specific microhabitats include the "bottom pools" near canyon trail entrance and a spring-fed pool above the "grotto" in [[Peoples Canyon]] (T12, R10W, SW¼ Sec 14, elevation 2,440 ft).
- **Associated habitat vegetation**: [[Yerba Mansa]] noted at Grapevine Spring release site, indicating wetland/riparian plant association.
- **Co-occurring fauna**:
  - [[Leopard Frog]] — abundant during June 1988 survey.
  - [[Canyon Tree Frog]] — notably **absent** during the same survey, despite being previously common in the lower canyon reaches; suggests a possible amphibian community shift or decline.
  - Aquatic invertebrates — described as "abundant and diverse," indicating a healthy prey base and generally intact aquatic food web.
- **Riparian condition**: Judged "excellent" during the site visit, suggesting the physical habitat itself was not a limiting factor for fish persistence.
- **Population source/origin lineage**:
  - Pupfish stock: [[Santa Clara Slough]], Mexico (via Dexter National Fish Hatchery propagation).
  - Topminnow stock: Sharp and Sheehy springs source population (via Dexter National Fish Hatchery propagation).
- **Management context**: Translocation/reintroduction effort under the U.S. Endangered Species Act framework, involving interagency coordination between [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], and [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]].

## Key Findings
- **1988-09-20 (Peoples Canyon transfer)**: ~1,000 desert pupfish transferred to Peoples Canyon on September 9, 1988, to supplement stocks originally introduced in 1982 and 1983; only 7 mortalities occurred during air transport from Dexter National Fish Hatchery — indicating high transfer survival rate.
- **Gila topminnow status (Sept. 1988 visit)**: Found abundant at three locations in the lower canyon, with multiple size classes and gravid females observed — indicating a self-sustaining, reproducing population.
- **1988-08-15 (Multi-site transfer)**: Separate transfer event distributed ~75 pupfish to BLM Kingman Resource Area Office and 250 pupfish + 250 topminnow to [[Grapevine Spring]]; all fish arrived in good condition.
- **1988-07-20 (Field monitoring report)**: Critical finding — **desert pupfish were completely absent** from Peoples Canyon despite prior 1982-1983 introductions, even after targeted snorkel search of the spring-fed pool above the grotto. Author hypothesizes this failure may be due to introduction of a **less-than-threshold founding population size**, insufficient to establish a viable population (an early acknowledgment of minimum viable population concerns in reintroduction biology).
- **Topminnow trends**: Topminnow were observed to be "less numerous" and "less distributed throughout the canyon" compared to the observer's prior recollections, despite continued recruitment — suggesting a possible population decline or contraction in distribution even for the more successfully established species.
- **Amphibian community shift**: Absence of Canyon tree frogs (previously common) alongside abundant leopard frogs may warrant further ecological investigation, though no causal link to fish reintroductions is established in the source.
- **Adaptive management response**: The documented absence of pupfish in July 1988 directly informed and justified the large supplemental transfer of ~1,000 individuals completed two months later (September 1988), demonstrating an iterative, monitoring-driven reintroduction strategy.
