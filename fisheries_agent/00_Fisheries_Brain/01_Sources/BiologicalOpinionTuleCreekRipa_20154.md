# Biological Opinion: Tule Creek Riparian Exclosure and Gila Topminnow

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a [[Biological Opinion]] issued by the [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS) on February 21, 1991, under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. It responds to a November 21, 1990 request from the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) Phoenix District for formal consultation on a fenced riparian exclosure at [[Tule Creek]], Yavapai County, Arizona. The listed species of concern is the [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), an endangered livebearing fish.

The FWS concluded that construction of the exclosure is **not likely to jeopardize** the continued existence of the Gila topminnow. The project fences about 70 acres, including roughly 1/2 mile of stream, to keep out livestock and feral burros, and closes an eroding ford crossing. The FWS expects a net improvement in the species' status at the site. It also set an incidental take threshold of more than 10 dead fish and required monitoring and reporting.

## Background & Description

**Species.** The Gila topminnow was listed as endangered on March 11, 1967. No critical habitat has been designated. It is a small livebearing fish found in the Gila, Sonora, and de la Concepcion River drainages of Arizona, New Mexico, and Sonora, Mexico. It was once among the most common fishes of the Gila River and its tributaries. Its decline is attributed to habitat destruction (water diversion, channelization, impoundment, vegetation clearing) and to predation and competition from the nonnative [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*), which has led to extirpation across most of its range.

**Population history at Tule Creek.**
- The site lies within the species' historic range. Fish were stocked in 1968 as part of a recovery effort.
- A severe flood in 1978 eliminated the population and incised the channel, removing part of the [[Cienega Habitat]] deposits.
- Fish were restocked in 1981. Both stockings used stock from [[Monkey Springs]], routed through [[Boyce-Thompson Arboretum]].
- Since 1981 the species has been abundant, though the linear extent of its distribution varies yearly. The Tule Creek population is considered among the most successful reintroduced populations.
- The best habitat is an approximately 1/2 mile perennial reach in T.8N., R.1E., Sections 28 and 29.

**Project setting.** Tule Creek is an intermittent tributary of the [[Agua Fria River]], entering just upstream of the upper end of [[Lake Pleasant]]. The 1/2 mile target reach flows mainly through cienega-type organic deposits. Vegetation includes willow, seepwillow, cottonwood, salt cedar, bulrush, cattail, arrowweed, and bermuda grass. The stream lies in the Boulder Creek grazing allotment and is grazed by cattle and feral burros. BLM rates the riparian vegetation as unsatisfactory, and its 1982 [[Black Canyon Habitat Management Plan]] called for fencing to exclude livestock.

**Proposed action.**
- Four-strand barbed wire fence with a smooth bottom wire, enclosing about 70 acres and 1/2 mile of channel, built on ridgelines where practicable, with most posts drilled.
- Hanging pipe watergaps at the two stream crossings.
- A gate at the lower end to remove livestock or burros that enter, and a walk-through at the vehicle turnaround for recreational access.
- Materials moved by helicopter and truck, with some vehicle traffic on existing fords during construction.
- A user-maintained road crosses the stream by ford at several points, and several crossings are eroding. The road will be signed closed and made impassable with a backhoe.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Expected effects.** The FWS expects an overall improvement in the species' status at the site.
- *Short-term:* minor effects from vehicles crossing the stream during construction.
- *Long-term:* stabilization of [[Riparian Exclosure]] communities, denser riparian vegetation, greater bank stability, less sedimentation, and moderated water temperatures.
- Increased stability and water retention may lengthen the cienega habitat.

**Legal framework.**
- [[Section 9 Take Prohibition]] bars taking listed fish, including significant habitat modification that impairs breeding, feeding, or sheltering.
- Under [[Incidental Take Statement]] provisions, take incidental to, and not intended as part of, the agency action is not prohibited if the terms of the statement are followed.

**Take anticipated.** Take is expected as direct loss of individual fish from vehicle and human traffic in the channel during construction. Because reliable population estimates are not obtainable (sampling difficulties and rapid population change in a short-lived, highly fecund species), take cannot be quantified directly. Instead, the FWS set a threshold: more than **10 dead fish of any species** observed in the project area indicates greater-than-anticipated take.

## Key Findings

1. **Opinion:** The proposed exclosure is **not likely to jeopardize** the Gila topminnow.
2. **Trigger for reinitiation:** If more than 10 dead fish are observed, BLM must reinitiate [[Section 7 Consultation]] immediately to avoid violating Section 9. Operations must stop during reinitiation if the additional take would cause an irreversible, adverse impact (50 CFR 402.14(i)). BLM must explain the causes of the take.
3. **Reasonable and Prudent Measures:**
   - Minimize direct mortality of Gila topminnow.
   - Keep complete and accurate records of actions that may cause take or affect habitat.
4. **Terms and Conditions (mandatory for exemption):**
   - Minimize disturbance of the stream channel and cienega.
   - Regularly inspect and repair the exclosure fence.
   - Submit a written [[Post-Construction Report]] within two months of completion, with maps and before-and-after photographs.
5. **Conservation Recommendations (discretionary):**
   - Place the gate to limit access by unauthorized persons and vehicles.
   - Prevent pollutants from entering the water during implementation.
   - Monitor effects on riparian and aquatic communities, channel morphology, and the species, using long-term photo points, cross-channel transects, and yearly fish sampling.
6. **Reinitiation conditions (50 CFR 402.16):** Consultation must be reopened if take limits are reached, if new information reveals unconsidered effects, if the action is modified in an unconsidered way, or if a new species is listed or critical habitat is designated.
7. **Sources:** The opinion draws on the BLM biological evaluation of November 21, 1990, FWS files, and about eight cited works. These include [[Minckley]] (1973) on Arizona fishes, the [[Gila and Yaqui Topminnow Recovery Plan]] (FWS 1984), [[Meffe]] et al. (1983) on decline factors, and [[Collins]] et al. (1981) on flood impacts at Tule Creek.
