# Description of *Bogidiella helenae* sp. n. (Crustacea, Amphipoda) from the Portuguese Psammon, with Notes on the Genus

#ichthyology #marine-biology #bogidiellidae

*Note: this document concerns an amphipod crustacean, not a fish. The taxon tag above is adjusted to the actual subject.*

## Executive Summary

Mateus and Maciel (1967, Instituto de Zoologia "Dr. Augusto Nobre", Porto) describe a new interstitial amphipod, [[Bogidiella Helenae]] sp. n., of the family [[Bogidiellidae]]. The 42 type specimens (22 males, 20 females) were collected from interstitial sand at Cabedelo, on the left bank near the mouth of the [[Douro Estuary]], using the Chappuis method. The species is named in honour of [[Maria Helena Galhano]], the only Portuguese researcher known to the authors to have studied this interstitial fauna. The paper also reviews the 12 (possibly 13 or 14) known species of [[Bogidiella]], discusses their distribution, and proposes a hypothetical evolutionary scheme for the genus.

## Background & Description

- **Genus context:** *Bogidiella* is the sole genus of Bogidiellidae. The type species, [[Bogidiella Albertimagni]] Hertzog, 1933, was found in groundwater near Strasbourg (Bas-Rhin, France).
- **Holotype:** specimen No. 179 in the Coleção de Amphipoda portugueses, 2.5 mm, collected 2 July 1965 at Cabedelo by M. H. Galhano. Paratypes are held at the same institute.
- **Morphology (summary):** Depigmented, eyeless, body slightly elongate. Mean length 1.9 mm (males) and 2.2 mm (females), range 1.5–2.5 mm (males) and 2.0–2.5 mm (females). Telson subrectangular with two dorsal spines near the posterior angles. Antenna I has a biarticulate accessory flagellum; the mandible has a 6-toothed cutting edge; gnathopods I and II, pereiopods III–VII, and pleopods with a vestigial inner ramus are described in detail. Sexes are distinguished by oostegites, and female uropod III is longer and more spined than in males. No ovigerous females were found.
- **Comparison:** The new species resembles [[Bogidiella Albertimagni]] (two telson spines, biarticulate accessory flagellum, two lobe setae on maxilla I) and [[Bogidiella Chappuisi]] (two lobe setae on maxilla I, no elliptical organs). It differs from both in gnathopod form, pleopod and uropod setation, and in the presence of a vestigial inner pleopod ramus, a feature the authors note is disputed for *B. albertimagni*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Interstitial (psammon) sediments, collected in marine beach sand near a river mouth. Many *Bogidiella* species are stygobiotic or phreatic (groundwater), and some are cave-dwelling, such as [[Bogidiella Bredini]], [[Bogidiella Tabascensis]] and [[Bogidiella Lindbergi]].
- **Salinity:** [[Bogidiella Chappuisi]] occurs in littoral interstitial water with salinity of 2.3–12.6 g/l (Racou, southern France). [[Bogidiella Brasiliensis]] is also from saline interstitial groundwater.
- **Global distribution:** Recorded from Europe, Asia (Afghanistan, Yugoslavia-Macedonia, Israel's Dead Sea area), the Americas (Mexico, Brazil, Amazonas, Caribbean), Africa's neighbouring regions, and the Indian Ocean (Réunion). The authors call the distribution very wide and suggest the genus is ancient, with a probable dispersal centre in the central Atlantic.
- **Research gaps:** Portuguese interstitial fauna is poorly studied, and large regions of the genus range remain unexplored.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described:** *Bogidiella helenae* sp. n., from 42 specimens at Cabedelo (Douro estuary). A single further specimen was found later at Francelos.
2. **Diagnostic traits:** Two telson spines, biarticulate accessory flagellum on antenna I, vestigial inner pleopod ramus, and absence of elliptical organs.
3. **Genus status:** 12 species are recognised, with possibly 13 (if *B. denticulata* is valid) or 14 (if *B. glacialis* is a full species, as Ruffo 1963 suggests).
4. **Taxonomic disputes:** Several names are contested in synonymy. *B. skopljensis* was treated variously as a species and a subspecies. *B. dalmatina* was placed in synonymy with *B. chappuisi* by Ruffo. *B. denticulata* is considered valid by some authors and placed in *B. albertimagni* by others (Dancău & Șerban 1965).
5. **Evolutionary hypothesis:** The authors propose an origin from a marine, ubiquitous ancestor in the central Atlantic, perhaps before the Palaeogene. Colonisation of the Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean, a Tethys-linked spread to Eurasia, and an Indian Ocean route via the Mozambique Channel are suggested. The species are considered to have originated largely independently, so no phylogeny is inferred. Reduction of telson spines and maxilla I setae is noted within two lineages, one with two pleopod rami and one with a single ramus.
6. **Stated limitations:** Phylogenetic relations cannot be resolved with current data, since there are no palaeontological records for the group and morphology of living forms is the only evidence. The authors also note that lip preparations could not be obtained for examination.
