# Common Plankton of Twin Lakes, Colorado

#ichthyology #marine-biology #plankton

## Executive Summary

This Bureau of Reclamation technical report (REC-ERC-82-21, February 1983) by Davine M. Lieberman provides a taxonomic species list of the common plankton collected from [[Twin Lakes]] and the associated [[Mt. Elbert Forebay]] since 1974. The work supports a broader study of the effects of the [[Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant]] on lake ecology. The study identified 11 zooplankton and 14 phytoplankton species from the limnetic zone. Rotifers and copepods dominate the zooplankton, and cladocerans are rare, likely because of predation by the introduced opossum shrimp [[Mysis Relicta]]. Phytoplankton is dominated by [[Dinobryon Cylindricum]] and the diatoms [[Asterionella Formosa]] and [[Synedra]] species.

## Background & Description

Twin Lakes are two connected dimictic lakes on Lake Creek, a tributary of the Arkansas River on the eastern slope of the Continental Divide. The lakes formed through glacial action on alluvial deposits. The lower lake is described as the largest natural mountain lake in Colorado. A dam built in 1901 on the Lake Creek outlet permits water-level fluctuations of 7.8 m in the lower lake and about 5.8 m in the upper lake. The surface elevation is 2802 m above sea level; maximum depths are about 27 m (lower) and 28 m (upper). The lakes operate as a reservoir.

The Mt. Elbert Pumped-Storage Powerplant, on the northwest shore of the lower lake, has operated sporadically since mid-1981. A new dam at the shallow eastern end of the lower lake is planned for use in 1984 to increase mixing between the two lakes.

Plankton was sampled at regular intervals from one station in each lake, with Mt. Elbert Forebay sampling beginning in fall 1981. Sampling was monthly in winter and biweekly in summer from May 1974 onward. Vertical hauls used a No. 20 closing net (0.076 mm mesh) at depths of 0–5, 5–10, 10–15, and 15–20 m or the bottom. Samples were preserved in 2% formalin and identified with standard references, including [[Pennak]] (1978) and [[Dodson]] (1981). Photomicrography was used to document key anatomical features.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historical comparison:** Juday (1906) recorded a more diverse fauna around 1900: 6 rotifer, 5 copepod, and 10 cladoceran species. In recent years only three cladoceran species have been collected, mostly from the forebay.
- **Predation:** The nocturnal [[Mysis Relicta]], introduced to Twin Lakes in 1958, is very abundant but was not captured in daytime plankton samples. Its predation is cited as the likely cause of low cladoceran numbers.
- **Forebay origin of Daphnia:** Forebay Daphnia may be autochthonous or imported from [[Turquoise Reservoir]] through the Mt. Elbert conduit.
- **Spatial gradient:** The lower lake supports higher zooplankton and phytoplankton densities than the upper lake.
- **Fish context:** Related Twin Lakes studies cover [[Lake Trout]], catostomids, and the fishery, which depend on this plankton base. No fish data were collected in this report.

## Key Findings

**Species inventory**
- **Zooplankton (11 species):** 4 Rotifera, 3 Copepoda, 3 Cladocera, and 1 Mysidacea. Species identified to species level include *Daphnia pulex*, *D. rosea*, *Bosmina longirostris*, *Diaptomus judayi*, *D. connexus*, *Cyclops bicuspidatus thomasi*, *Kellicottia longispina*, *Keratella cochlearis*, and *Mysis relicta*. *Polyarthra* sp. and *Brachionus* sp. were not identified to species.
- **Phytoplankton (14 species in Table 2):** 3 Chrysophyta, 3 Chlorophyta, 6 Bacillariophyta, and 2 Cyanophyta. The report's Division labels are inconsistent with standard taxonomy (for example, Chrysophyta are labelled "blue-green algae"). The full Norton (1976) appendix lists 50 species.

**Zooplankton results**
- Rotifers and copepods made up 99% of the zooplankton throughout the year.
- Cladocerans made up less than 1% of the population.
- [[Keratella Cochlearis]] is the dominant rotifer, followed by [[Kellicottia Longispina]] and [[Polyarthra]]. Its peak was in July.
- [[Cyclops Bicuspidatus Thomasi]] is the dominant copepod and occurs year-round. Diaptomus is more abundant from February to June.
- Zooplankton density in July 1981 averaged 116 individuals/L in the lower lake and 53 individuals/L in the upper lake.
- Rotifers comprised 83% (lower lake) and 78% (upper lake) of the July zooplankton population.
- Low lower-lake density in September was 16.5 individuals/L, and low upper-lake density in June was 5.2 individuals/L.

**Phytoplankton results**
- The 1981 lower-lake maximum was 18,042 organisms/L in early March. The yearly low was 184 organisms/L in early September.
- The 1981 upper-lake maximum was 4,870 organisms/L in early September. The low was 704 organisms/L in late September.
- Dinobryon dominated the upper lake in 1981. Synedra dominated the lower lake from January to March and again from May to September. Asterionella peaked in April.
- Blue-green algae were nearly absent in 1981, while Mallomonas and Dictyosphaerium were abundant in 1980. The authors note these fluctuations are probably linked to nutrient loading during spring runoff.

**Conclusions**
- Plankton composition and density vary strongly between lakes and between years.
- Predation by Mysis relicta is the most likely driver of low cladoceran abundance.
- The report provides an updated species list to replace the outdated Reed and Olive (1958) inventory.
