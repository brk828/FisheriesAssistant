## Executive Summary
This document is an internal U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service memorandum (dated 4 April 1994) from the Parker Fisheries Resource Office, addressed to regional stakeholders in [[Arizona]] native fish conservation (Hassayampa River Preserve, Arizona Game and Fish Department, Arizona State University). The memo proposes an electrofishing operation at **Palm Lake** to remove additional individuals of two federally listed endangered fishes — the [[Bonytail Chub]] and the [[Razorback Sucker]] — prior to the onset of summer conditions. The stated goal is to redistribute captured fish to two recipient facilities: **Lake Havasu** and the **Arizona State University Research Park**, as part of an ongoing captive/refuge population management and translocation program for endangered Lower Colorado River Basin fishes.

## Background & Description
No morphological descriptions are provided in this source; the document is administrative/operational rather than a scientific data report. However, standard field protocol details relevant to individual-level data collection are specified:
- All captured fish were to be **PIT-tagged** (Passive Integrated Transponder) for individual identification and long-term tracking.
- Fish were to be **weighed** and **measured** prior to release/transfer.
- Distinction is made between generational cohorts of Bonytail Chub, referred to as **F1's** (first filial generation, presumably hatchery-reared or first-generation offspring of wild broodstock).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Palm Lake** functions as a source/holding population site for endangered [[Colorado River]] Basin fishes, likely used as a refuge or grow-out population separate from the wild Colorado River mainstem.
- **Lake Havasu** is designated as a recipient site for razorback suckers and a subset of bonytail chub F1 individuals, suggesting its use as an established or developing refugial population water body for endangered cyprinids and catostomids.
- The **Arizona State University Research Park** is designated as a research/holding facility for the remaining bonytail chub F1 fish, indicating a research-oriented component to the translocation (likely growth, survivorship, or genetic studies).
- Mention of "squawfish" (a historical common name for the [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], *Ptychocheilus lucius*) as a candidate species for inclusion in the Research Park transfer indicates coordinated multi-species endangered fish management across these sites.

## Key Findings
- This memo documents an active, multi-agency **removal and translocation effort** for endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] populations from Palm Lake, scheduled for the **last week of April 1994** (proposed date: 27 April).
- The operation reflects standard **endangered species refuge management practices** in the Lower Colorado River Basin, including electrofishing capture methods, PIT-tagging for individual tracking, and biometric data collection (weight, length).
- Coordination among federal (USFWS), state (Arizona Game and Fish Department), academic (Arizona State University Center for Environmental Studies), and non-profit ([[Hassayampa River Preserve]]) entities highlights the collaborative, interagency nature of endangered Southwestern fish conservation programs during this period.
- The mention of possible inclusion of [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] ("squawfish") suggests concurrent, integrated management of multiple endangered Colorado River Basin fish species at the same facilities.
- This document serves primarily as a **logistical/administrative record** rather than a primary data source, but is valuable for reconstructing the historical timeline and methodology of endangered fish translocation and refuge population establishment in Arizona during the mid-1990s.
