# Analysis of the Cui-ui Recovery Plan as Justification for 1994 Stampede Reservoir Water Releases

## Executive Summary
This document is a critical policy/scientific review, commissioned by the Town of Truckee, California, evaluating the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's (Service) justification for a large-scale 1994 drawdown of [[Stampede Reservoir]] to benefit spawning runs of the endangered [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) in [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada. The reviewers — led by Ray Butler, Chairman of the Nevada County Fish and Wildlife Commission — contest six core points made by the Service, which were based on Judge Solomon's 1982 decision, the 1990 Negotiated Settlement Agreement, and the Second Revision of the Cui-ui Recovery Plan (1992). The review concludes that the Plan does not provide sufficient scientific evidence that a 1994 spawning event is critical to species recovery or survival, particularly given a population increase from ~150,000 individuals in the early 1980s to ~1,100,000 by September 1993.

## Background & Description
The source text does not describe external morphology of the Cui-ui in detail (this is a policy/management document, not a taxonomic description), but the following **life-history and physiological parameters** are extracted:

- **Maximum longevity**: Individuals can live to at least 40 years; fish at this age are described as being "at the end of their productivity" with limited or no reproductive potential (per Plan, p. 7 fecundity discussion).
- **Age at maturity**: 7–12 years.
- **Larval survival rate**: Estimated at ~0.26 (26%) — identified as the key bottleneck limiting recruitment.
- **Juvenile survival rate**: ~75%.
- **Adult mortality**: Considered subject to "normal" background mortality, discounting stochastic events (e.g., toxic spills, drought).
- **Genetic (allozyme) divergence** between older and younger age-classes: approximately 0.5%, characterized in the Plan as biologically non-significant. The oldest fish sampled in the 1990 allozyme study belonged to the 1950 cohort.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Endemism**: Cui-ui is restricted entirely to [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada — a terminal (endorheic) lake fed by the [[Truckee River]].
- **Spawning migration**: Adults migrate from Pyramid Lake into the lower [[Truckee River]] to spawn; this migration/spawning event historically has required specific water releases (temperature control, attraction flows, migration flows) from upstream reservoirs, including Stampede Reservoir, managed since at least 1976 by the Federal Watermaster.
- **Spawning habitat capacity**: Under highly favorable lake levels that alleviate downstream spawning impediments, identified spawning habitat in the lower Truckee could accommodate a maximum of approximately 200,000 female spawners (Plan, p. C-6), while a separate delisting criterion (p. iii) requires "at least 300,000 adult spawners" during a spawning run — creating an internal inconsistency the reviewers highlight regarding sex ratios and run timing (early/late/peak).
- **Lake hydrology**: Pyramid Lake's evaporative water loss is approximately 440,000 acre-feet (AF) per year, equivalent to roughly 1.2 meters (4 feet) of vertical lake-level drop annually. Average annual inflow to the lake is cited as ~400,000 AF (USFWS News Release), implying a long-term net deficit absent managed releases.
- **Water release history**: Federal Watermaster records (since 1976) show the largest historical fish-water release occurred in 1987 (84,470 AF). The most biologically successful spawning run in at least two decades reportedly occurred in 1986, attributed to a synergistic effect of an extremely high-precipitation year in both the upper Truckee Basin and the Truckee Meadows — not water release volume alone.
- **1994 drought context**: Projected 1994 releases of 155,000–160,000 AF were planned under drought conditions with minimal Truckee Basin precipitation, raising doubts about whether release volume alone (absent favorable precipitation) could replicate prior successful spawning events. Under the Service's "worst case scenario," Stampede Reservoir would be drawn down to residual storage of only 20,000–25,000 AF.
- **Watershed-scale ecology**: The review notes unaddressed upstream (California) ecological issues, including impacts to riparian systems, at least 43 species of native wildlife with special conservation status, and benthic/littoral organisms sharing the same ecological system as Cui-ui — none of which were incorporated into the Recovery Plan's scope.
- **Critical habitat**: No critical habitat has been formally designated under the Endangered Species Act. The reviewers identify the biologically obvious critical habitat as all of Pyramid Lake and the Truckee River corridor from the lake downstream to around Wadsworth, Nevada, consistent with historical spawning, migration, rearing, and foraging records (citing [[Snyder 1917]]).

## Key Findings
1. **Population trend contradicts urgency claims**: Cui-ui population estimates rose from ~150,000 (early 1980s) to ~1,100,000 (September 1993), which the reviewers argue undermines the Service's characterization of a genetic/demographic emergency requiring immediate 1994 water releases.
2. **Genetic bottleneck argument questioned**: The cited allozyme divergence (~0.5%) between age classes is biologically negligible; oldest fish in the study (1950 cohort) would already have had multiple spawning opportunities (1976, 1978–1987), and dendrochronological evidence indicates the Cui-ui population already survived more severe historical drought/bottleneck conditions prior to 20th-century water diversions.
3. **Recruitment bottleneck misidentified**: The reviewers argue that larval survival (~26%) — not adult spawning access — is the primary limiting factor for recruitment, and that water quality, substrate restoration, and streamside canopy/vegetation stabilization (Plan pp. 21–40) are higher-priority, unaddressed actions.
4. **Internal Plan inconsistencies**: Conflicting spawner-capacity figures (200,000 female spawner habitat capacity vs. 300,000 adult spawner delisting criterion) are unresolved and undefined regarding sex composition and run timing.
5. **Undefined success criteria**: The Plan lacks clear, quantitative definitions of "stable or increasing populations" over 15 years, creating risk of future management inflexibility and conflict.
6. **Missing population dynamics data**: Despite an age-structure study initiated in 1988, no preliminary data were presented to justify the necessity of a 1994 spawning event.
7. **Unaddressed upstream and watershed-scale issues**: The Recovery Plan omits California/upstream Truckee Basin impacts, growing water demand from Reno/Sparks development, and lacks an ecosystem-level management framework.
8. **No designated critical habitat**: Absence of formal critical habitat designation is identified as a likely impediment to long-term recovery and potential delisting of the [[Cui-ui]].
9. **Risk of catastrophic contamination**: The reviewers warn that draining Stampede Reservoir to 20,000–25,000 AF residual storage would eliminate dilution capacity needed to mitigate a toxic spill event in the Truckee River system, drawing a direct parallel to a contemporaneous railroad-caused toxic spill on the upper Sacramento River.

*Source: Analysis prepared April 8, 1994, by Ray Butler, Chairman, Nevada County Fish and Wildlife Commission, at the request of the Mayor and Town Council of [[Truckee, California]], in response to U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Reno Field Office presentations and the Second Revision of the Cui-ui Recovery Plan (1992).*
