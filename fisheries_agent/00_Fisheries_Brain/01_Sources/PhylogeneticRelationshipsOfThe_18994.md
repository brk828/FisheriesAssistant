# Phylogenetic Relationships of the Creek Chubs and Spine-Fins: An Enigmatic Group of North American Cyprinid Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[Andrew M. Simons]] and [[Richard L. Mayden]] (Department of Biological Sciences, University of Alabama) uses the complete sequence of the mitochondrial 12S ribosomal RNA gene to resolve the phylogeny of major North American [[Cyprinidae]] clades. The analysis recovers four monophyletic groups and refutes earlier morphology-based hypotheses. The authors elevate [[Snyderichthys]] to generic rank and find that the [[Plagopterin Clade]] (spine-fins) is sister to the [[Creek Chub Clade]].

## Background & Description

- **Family scale:** North American Cyprinidae (chubs, shiners, minnows) is the largest freshwater fish family on the continent, with over 300 species in about 50 genera (Burr and Mayden, 1992). The document states that cyprinids constitute nearly 30% of the North American ichthyofauna.
- **Previous work:** Only two explicitly phylogenetic studies covered much of the fauna: [[Mayden 1989]] (open posterior myodome taxa) and [[Coburn and Cavender 1992]] (nearly all genera). Both used morphology and reached different conclusions.
- **Creek chubs:** Large insectivorous species with a complex taxonomic history. *Semotilus* includes four species (*S. atromaculatus*, *S. thoreauianus*, *S. lumbee*, *S. corporalis*). Males build large gravel nests during spawning. The genus *Margariscus* (monotypic, pearl dace *M. margarita*) and *Couesius* were previously assigned to other genera, and *Hemitremia* (single species, *H. flammea*) is a spring-restricted, barbel-less chub of Alabama, Georgia and Tennessee.
- **Plagopterins (spine-fins):** Six species in three genera (*Plagopterus*, *Lepidomeda*, *Meda*), endemic to the lower Colorado Basin of Utah, Nevada and Arizona. Their populations have declined sharply because of stream alteration and exotic fishes. They have spine-like dorsal and pelvic fin rays and bright silvery coloration.
- **Leatherside chub:** *Snyderichthys copei*, endemic to the Bonneville and Snake river systems (Utah, Idaho, Wyoming), has been placed in six different genera over time.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Cyprinids are a major component of most freshwater ecosystems and a focus of behavioral, ecological, biogeographical and evolutionary research.
- Plagopterin populations have decreased "dramatically" because of altered stream systems and introduced exotic fishes.
- *Hemitremia flammea* is restricted to spring habitats in Alabama, Georgia and Tennessee.
- The document notes that, without explicit phylogenetic hypotheses, progress on evolutionary and ecological studies of these species is limited (Mayden, 1992).

## Key Findings

- **Four consistently resolved clades** (all analyses):
  1. **Western Clade:** *Acrocheilus*, *Gila*
  2. **Creek Chub Clade:** *Couesius*, *Hemitremia*, *Semotilus*, *Margariscus*
  3. **Open Posterior Myodome Clade:** *Campostoma*, *Phenacobius*, *Cyprinella*, *Notropis*, *Platygobio*, *Rhinichthys*
  4. **Plagopterin Clade:** *Snyderichthys*, *Lepidomeda*, *Meda*
- **Taxonomic action:** *Snyderichthys* is elevated from synonymy with *Gila*, because *S. copei* is not related to *Gila*.
- **Placement:** Weighted analyses (2:1, 3:1) gave a single topology in which the Plagopterin Clade is sister to the Creek Chub Clade. This group is sister to the Western Clade, and the whole assemblage is sister to the Open Posterior Myodome Clade. Unweighted analyses gave four or five equally parsimonious trees, varying in plagopterin placement.
- **Morphology conflicts:** Results refute the earlier morphology-based hypotheses. The Creek Chub Clade does not match the paraphyletic grade of [[Coburn and Cavender 1992]], and the Open Posterior Myodome Clade of [[Mayden 1989]] is dismembered in that study.
- **Open posterior myodome:** The authors argue this opening is a synapomorphy of the Open Posterior Myodome Clade, so the closed condition in *Campostoma* and other chubs is secondarily derived.
- **Bootstrap support:**
  - Open Posterior Myodome Clade: 92%
  - Western Clade: 99%
  - Creek Chub Clade plus Plagopterin Clade: 53%
  - Creek Chub Clade: 53%
  - Plagopterin Clade: 58%
  - Lower values were attributed to the relatively few characters supporting these nodes.
- **Data quality:**
  - Sequence data were significantly more structured than random (g1 = −0.807; g1 = −0.626 after excluding congeneric taxa; PTP = 0.001).
  - Transition bias was higher than in Reeder's (1995) data, with a mean ratio of 3.5 versus 2.2 for pairs under 4.0% divergence.
  - Transversion-only parsimony gave poor resolution, showing that transitions contribute substantially to resolving the Creek Chub Clade.
- **Sampling:** The analysis included 19 taxa and 970 aligned characters. Sequences were deposited in GenBank under accessions AF023183–AF023201. Outgroup: *Notemigonus crysoleucas* in the figure labels; the appendix names *not_chry05* as the outgroup.
- **Unresolved points:** Relationships within the plagopterins and the exact plagopterin position (sister to the Western Clade, the Creek Chub Clade, or nested within it as sister to *Margariscus*) remain uncertain.
