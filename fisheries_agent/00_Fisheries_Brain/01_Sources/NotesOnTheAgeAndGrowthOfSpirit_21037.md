# Notes on the Age and Growth of Spirit Lake Yellow Pikeperch (*Stizostedion vitreum vitreum*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #percidae

## Executive Summary

This 1951 study by Earl T. Rose estimates the [[Age and Growth]] of [[Yellow Pikeperch]] (also known as [[Walleye]]) in [[Spirit Lake]], [[Iowa]]. It uses [[Scale Analysis]] of adult fish collected during spring [[Gill-Netting]] and tests the validity of the [[Annulus]] as a true year mark using fin-clipped fish of known age. The study reports above-average growth, a grand average [[Coefficient of Condition]] (K) of 1.638, and a [[Length-Weight Relationship]] expressed as Log W = -5.01127 + 3.09036 Log L. The author attributes the growth to good environmental conditions, including high water that connected the lake with adjoining sloughs and increased forage.

## Background & Description

The paper opens by stating the premise of most life-history work: annual rings form on scales, and scale diameter grows in proportion to fish length. This allows growth to be back-calculated for each year of life.

**Sampling**
- In spring 1947, 556 adult walleyes were captured in [[State Hatchery]]-operated gill nets, tagged, and released as part of a population appraisal (see [[Rose 1949 Population Study]]).
- Scales, weights, and measurements were taken from 252 of these fish.
- Scale samples from 88 additional walleyes, collected in 1946, 1948, and 1950, provided data on small and large adult size groups.
- Most fish were taken during the spawning runs of late April and early May.

**Methods**
- Scales were taken mainly from below the [[Lateral Line]] in the anterior body, without selecting "key" scales.
- Scales were mounted between glass slides and read by [[Microprojection]].
- Annulus positions were marked on tag-board strips and converted to growth using [[Nomograph]]s (Carlander and Smith, 1944).
- Because key scales were not collected in 1947, the [[Body-Scale Relationship]] could not be established. Growth was therefore calculated from zero on the nomograph and computed by [[Direct Proportion Method]]. The author notes that several hundred samples per size group would have allowed the body-scale relationship to be determined.
- Standard length is measured from the snout tip to the base of the hypural plate. Total length runs from the snout tip to the tips of the compressed caudal lobes. The table footnote gives Total Length = 1.185 × Standard Length.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Growth pattern**
- As is typical for walleye, the first-year increment far exceeds all later increments. Growth levels off in the older age classes.
- Scales collected in early July of 1946 and 1947 all had annuli at the extreme anterior margin. The author therefore assumes the maximum length for each year was reached by capture, and that each age class in Table I has one fewer annulus than inferred, plus a full year's increment.

**Comparison with other waters (Table II)**
- Spirit Lake walleyes grew somewhat faster than those from other Iowa waters and considerably faster than those from northern Minnesota.
- Compared sites include [[Lake of the Woods]] (Minnesota), other Iowa lakes, [[Clear Lake]] (Iowa), and an earlier Spirit Lake sample from 1941 to 1945 (Carlander, 1948).
- The author treats the earlier sample as a check on possible changes after environmental improvement from 1944 through 1947.

**Environmental drivers**
- High water during 1944–1947 connected Spirit Lake with adjoining sloughs, which likely increased forage and may explain the faster growth.
- Growth rates are not static. The author cautions that any reported growth reflects only the year-classes studied and should not be considered "normal."

**Comparative condition**
- The mean K for all adult size groups was high, indicating good growing conditions.
- Clear Lake, which is similar in many physical and biological features, had an average K of 1.507 for fish collected from 1941 through 1947 (Cleary, 1949).

**Hatchery and stocking context**
- Fingerlings from the nursery ponds were fin-clipped before stocking to evaluate the [[Stocking Program]].
- These fish were about 5 months old at stocking and usually 4 to 7 inches in total length.
- A different paired fin was removed from each year's cohort so that age at recapture could be identified.

## Key Findings

**1. Growth of adult walleyes (Table I)**
- Calculations covered eight age groups of 320 Spirit Lake walleyes (the Summary section cites 312 adults, a discrepancy in the source).
- Average calculated standard lengths at each annulus (mm): 155, 239, 309, 376, 427, 476, 512, 533, 557, and 597 for annuli 1 through 10.
- Average total lengths at each annulus (inches): 7.2, 11.1, 14.4, 17.5, 19.9, 22.2, 23.7, 24.9, 26.0, and 27.8.
- Average annual increments (inches): 7.2, 3.9, 3.3, 3.1, 2.4, 2.3, 1.5, 1.2, 1.1, and 1.8.
- Actual lengths at capture matched the maximum calculated lengths.

**2. Length-weight relationship (Table III, Figure 1)**
- The least-squares equation is Log W = -5.01127 + 3.09036 Log L (W in grams, L in standard length in millimeters).
- Table III lists 14 length groups from 259 to 572 mm, with 340 fish in total. Observed and calculated weights agree closely across the range.

**3. Coefficient of condition**
- Grand average K = 1.638, calculated from spent females and males from hatchery spawn-taking operations.
- The author states this equals the highest K reported for the species in United States and Canadian waters (Carlander, 1950). The summary calls it "considerably above most previous reports from other waters."

**4. Validation of the annulus as a true year mark**
- Fin-clipped fingerlings recaptured in April 1947 and 1948 (Table V) showed scale annuli matching their known ages.
- Growth of these known-age fish compared favorably with that of unmarked fish in Table I.
- Table V lists about 15 recaptured fish stocked in 1941 and 1944, with an average calculated standard length of 171, 149, 225, 302, 366, and 398 mm at successive annuli.

**5. Conclusions**
- Spirit Lake approached optimal walleye growth conditions for its latitude and for the year-classes studied.
- The author cautions that conclusions apply only to the period studied.
