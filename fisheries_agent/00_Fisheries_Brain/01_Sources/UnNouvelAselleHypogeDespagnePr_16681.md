# Un Nouvel Aselle Hypogé d'Espagne: *Proasellus cantabricus* n. sp. (Crustacé Isopode)

#isopoda #asellidae #subterranean-biology

## Executive Summary

Henry and Magniez (Laboratoire de Biologie animale et générale, Faculté des Sciences, Dijon; *Annales de Spéléologie*, tome 23, fasc. 2, 1968) describe a new eyeless, unpigmented [[Asellidae|asellid]] isopod, [[Proasellus Cantabricus]] n. sp., from seven caves in the province of Santander, Spain. The species belongs to the [[Coxalis Group]] of the genus [[Proasellus]]. The authors place it among the hypogean forms of the Atlantic side of the Iberian Peninsula and south-western France, and argue that it is closely related to the epigean [[Proasellus Ibericus]] (Braga, 1946). A total of 32 specimens was collected; the paper combines a collection record, a morphological redescription with emphasis on male pleopods, and a biogeographic argument for a shared epigean ancestor.

## Background & Description

The material derives from speleological exploration by the Spéléo-Club de Dijon (S.C.D.) in Urgonian karst massifs of the Cantabrian Chain between 1959 and 1967. Several other cave species, both terrestrial and aquatic, were collected during these campaigns, including [[Stenasellus Virei Buchneri]] and the cave-dwelling isopod *Cantabroniscus primitivus*.

**General morphology**
- Colourless body, eyes absent, typical genus form; body about four times longer than wide.
- Pleotelson as long as wide, not exceeding one-fifth of body length.
- Antennules shorter than the antennal peduncle; flagellum of 7 articles, with 4 olfactory lamellae in males and 3 in females (largest specimens).
- Antennae nearly as long as the body, with flagella of more than 70 articles.
- Mouthparts typical of the genus. The maxilliped of ovigerous females bears an oostegite with about 10 plumose setae, a feature shared with the *cavaticus* group but absent in the hypogean *coxalis*-group species [[Proasellus Spelaeus]] and [[Proasellus Albigensis]].
- Pereiopod I has a sternal row of 6–7 dactylar spines in both sexes; the dactylopodites of pereiopods II–VII carry at most 8 sternal spines. Male pereiopod IV is neither differentiated nor shortened.

**Male pleopods (diagnostic features)**
- *Pleopod I:* trapezoidal protopodite with a single hook-shaped retinaculum on each side. The elongate, oval exopodite (twice as long as wide) carries 34–36 smooth setae along the outer margin.
- *Pleopod II:* the endopodite (copulatory organ) has a short, slightly curved neck typical of the *coxalis* group. Its dorsal apophysis is strong, directed outward, and terminates in a swollen, denticulate (capitate) tip. This apophysis is unique to the species according to the authors.

**Female pleopods:** pleopod II triangular, with about 9 long plumose setae on the distal half of the outer margin. Pleopods III–V are progressively reduced in setation and become fleshy.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Setting.** All seven caves lie in karst massifs flanking the valleys of the Río Asón and two tributaries, the Río Gandara and Río Bustablado. Maximum distances between sites do not exceed 15 km. Sites range from about 168 m to 950 m altitude (for example, Cueva Fría at 950 m, Cueva Cubera at 168 m, Cueva del Molino at 210 m, Cueva Canuela at 267 m).

**Habitat conditions.**
- Water temperatures recorded: 9 °C (Canuela), 10.2 °C (Molino), 10.8 °C (Cubera), about 10 °C (Coventosa).
- Canuela: pools and potholes in upper galleries, with the lower network flooded.
- Molino: a large cave with water-level traces in its middle level and a stream in its lower level.
- Gandara: a small active cave beside the Gandara resurgence, feeding a short tributary.
- Cubera: a major resurgence rising in the Asón valley.
- Coventosa: lower-level underground stream that drains through the Cubera system.
- Fría: sandy-bottomed underground stream.

**Population density.** Density was low at every site. The authors note that captures made by sight rather than fine-mesh nets missed individuals between 1.2 and 3.1 mm.

**Associated fauna and biology.** Guts of all specimens were filled with clayey silt containing mica flakes. One specimen from the Molino was heavily parasitised by Acanthocephalans that filled its body cavity. The authors describe the morphological variation in setation of the pleopods as minor and endemic, typical of troglobiotic asellids.

**Biogeography.** The authors argue that these troglobiont forms, each confined to a separate karst massif or drainage basin, probably derive from a common epigean ancestor. [[Proasellus Ibericus]] was originally described from northern Portugal. It has since been recorded at the Sare cave in the Basque Country and at the Goueil di Her cave in Haute-Garonne, about 700 km from its original locality, where it showed clear troglophilic tendencies.

## Key Findings

**Collection record (32 specimens across 7 sites)**
- [[Cueva Canuela]]: 1 ovigerous female (3.1 mm) with 4 fully segmented embryos (0.9 mm); collected 08-08-1959.
- [[Cueva del Molino]]: 14 asellids over three visits (19-08-1961, 20-08-1961, 02-08-1962), plus young [[Stenasellus Virei Buchneri]].
- [[Cueva del Gandara]]: 1 male, 01-08-1962.
- [[Cueva La Cubera]]: 11 specimens, 06-08-1962, including 4 ovigerous females (4.3, 4.7, 4.9 and 6.5 mm) carrying 10, 20, 18 and 19 embryos or young. Released young measured 1.2 mm.
- [[Cueva Cullalvera]]: 2 females collected by Vandel on 07-07-1965, with a rich aquatic fauna.
- [[Cueva Coventosa]]: 2 specimens, April 1967 (B. Humbel).
- [[Cueva Fría]]: 1 male, 19-07-1967 (S. Derain).

**Size and sex structure**
- Size range: 3.1–6.5 mm.
- Males (n = 7): 3.8–6 mm, mean 5 mm.
- Ovigerous females (n = 5): 3.1–6.5 mm.
- Adult non-ovigerous females (n = 15): 3.9–6.5 mm, mean 5.1 mm; oocytes 12–21 per ovary where visible.
- Immature females (n = 5): 4.2–5 mm, mean 4.5 mm, without oostegites.
- Males were found at four stations only (Molino, Gandara, Cubera, Fría).

**Taxonomic conclusions**
- External characters are highly homogeneous across all seven sites, so the authors treat the populations as one species.
- The male endopodite apophysis separates *P. cantabricus* from *[[Proasellus Lusitanicus]]*. In *P. lusitanicus* the apophysis is mamillar and smooth; in *P. cantabricus* it is capitate and denticulate. The two species occupy limestone massifs about 500 km apart, separated by crystalline terrain.
- *P. ibericus* should be raised to full species rank, *P. (Coxasellus) ibericus*, rather than treated as a subspecies of *P. coxalis*.
- The authors call for separate description of each closely related troglobiont species rather than premature synonymy, and present this description as a preliminary contribution to a synthetic treatment of the group.

**Related publications cited:** Racovitza (1922) on *P. spelaeus*; Braga (1945, 1946) on Portuguese asellids; Frade (1938) on *P. lusitanicus*; Magniez (1965, 1966) on *P. albigensis* and *P. ibericus* in France; Delamare-Deboutteville (1960) on the value of subterranean asellid species; Vandel (1964) on biospeleology.
