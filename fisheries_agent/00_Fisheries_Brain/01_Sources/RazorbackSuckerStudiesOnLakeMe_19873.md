# Razorback Sucker Studies on Lake Mead, Nevada: 1997-1998 Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This report covers the second year (July 1997-June 1998) of a cooperative [[Razorback Sucker]] study on [[Lake Mead]], Nevada, prepared by BIO/WEST, Inc. for the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]]. Field work used [[Trammel Netting]] as the main adult sampling gear, [[Larval Sampling]] with dip nets and 12-volt crappie lights, and [[Sonic Telemetry]] to track adult movements. Most effort was concentrated at [[Las Vegas Bay]] and [[Echo Bay]], with extension to [[Overton Arm]], [[Gregg Basin]] and other lake areas, partly funded by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]].

Key results:
- 137 trammel net nights yielded 45 razorback suckers.
- Four juveniles (318-384 mm TL) were caught in Echo Bay, confirming recruitment.
- Spawning was most likely at Blackbird Point and the back of Echo Bay, from late February to early May.
- Growth averaged about 22 mm/year at Las Vegas Bay and 11 mm/year at Echo Bay.
- Population estimates were roughly 170-200 fish at Las Vegas Bay and fewer than 100 at Echo Bay.
- Larval numbers fell sharply at Echo Bay but rose at Las Vegas Bay.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endangered catostomid that uses the Lake Mead shoreline for spawning and feeding. The study was started in 1996 under a cooperative agreement among the [[Southern Nevada Water Authority]], the [[Colorado River Commission of Nevada]], and the [[Nevada Division of Wildlife]]. BIO/WEST, Inc. designed the study and conducted most of the field work. Other cooperators included the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], the [[National Park Service]] and the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]].

The first-year report (Holden et al. 1997) found two small populations, totaling a few hundred fish, at Las Vegas Bay and Echo Bay that did not intermix. Growth was higher than in any other known location, which suggested recruitment. The second-year objectives added:
1. Searching for new population concentrations through larval light-trapping outside the two study areas.
2. Enhancing juvenile sampling at both established sites.

Following a July 1998 cooperative agreement, the study period was extended into 2000. This was linked to Provision #10 of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Biological and Conference Opinion on Lower Colorado River operations (USFWS 1997).

**Study areas and conditions.** Work focused on the Las Vegas Bay (Blackbird Point, Las Vegas Wash) and Echo Bay/Overton Arm areas. Gregg Basin, about 3 miles west of Pearce Ferry, was sampled for the first time because historic records suggested a spawning population. Aerial searches covered all major basins. Lake elevation rose from about 1,190 ft at the start of the study to 1,211 ft by June 1998, inundating tamarisk and other shoreline vegetation.

**Methods.** The second year used the same core techniques as the first year: trammel nets for adults, sonic tags for habitat use, dip nets and crappie lights for larvae, and minnow traps, seines, hoop and fyke nets, and limited electrofishing for juveniles. Sonic tags were a 60-day model (4 g in air) that was smaller than the longer-lived tags used in year one. A new aging approach was added, using otoliths, opercular bones, pectoral fin rays and scales from fish found dead. Population estimates used the [[CAPTURE Software]] program and the Schnabel estimator.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Spawning and reproductive condition.** Ripe fish were found from late October 1997 to late May 1998. The first male with breeding tubercles was taken at Echo Bay on October 23, 1997. The first milting male was caught at Blackbird Point on January 5, 1998. Females expressing eggs were taken on March 9 (Blackbird Point) and March 11 (Echo Bay), 1998. Spent fish appeared in May. Spawning at Echo Bay ran from early March to mid-late April in 1998. At Las Vegas Bay it ran from mid-March to early May.

**Habitat use.** Sonic telemetry showed a seasonal shift. Fish concentrated at spawning areas during the spawning season and moved out during the post-spawning period (May-October). Echo Bay fish moved to Rogers Bay and points on the northwest shore of Overton Arm. Las Vegas Bay fish moved along the northern shore and in and out of the flowing portion of Las Vegas Wash. Tracked fish were usually within about 200 yards of shore, in water less than 60 feet deep, and mostly stationary near the bottom. The sonic-tagged populations of the two areas did not overlap.

**Lake-level effects.** The 1998 rise in lake level was linked to a reduction in larvae at Echo Bay. The lake rose an average of about 15 feet between seasons, nearly doubling the size of the Echo Bay cove where larvae had been concentrated in 1997. Wind direction also strongly affected larval catches, as reported for [[Lake Mohave]].

**Growth.** Mean annual growth for recaptured fish was about 22 mm at Las Vegas Bay (range 4.85-45.03 mm) and about 11 mm at Echo Bay (range 0.00-21.73 mm). These rates matched year one. Faster growth at Las Vegas Bay was attributed to higher zooplankton food levels, which may come from nutrient inflow from Las Vegas Wash. Lake Mead growth rates are much higher than those reported for [[Lake Mohave]] (a few mm/year) and the Green River (2-3 mm/year).

**Condition.** Captured fish had healthy fins and almost no lesions, tumors, blindness or parasites, similar to "young-looking" suckers previously caught by NDOW.

## Key Findings

**Adult captures and effort**
- 137 trammel net nights (Las Vegas Bay 62, Echo Bay/Overton Arm 75), compared with 134 nights in year one.
- 45 razorback suckers caught: 19 at Blackbird Point, 2 at the Las Vegas Wash inflow, 20 at Echo Bay and 4 at Rogers Bay. Year one produced 71 (61 in the first report's table, plus later captures).
- Catch rates were 0.34/net night at Las Vegas Bay and 0.32 at Echo Bay in year two. Echo Bay fell from 0.85 in year one.
- Four sonic-tagged fish and the four Rogers Bay fish were caught in areas where tagged adults were located.

**Juveniles and recruitment**
- Four presumptive juveniles (318, 381, and two in the 318-384 mm range) were caught in Echo Bay in trammel nets between November 1997 and February 1998, one each month.
- Aging of a 381-mm juvenile gave 4-5 years. A 588-mm adult gave 7-10 years, based on otoliths and fin rays.
- The authors concluded that recruitment is occurring and the population is relatively young, though with limited recruitment.

**Recaptures and growth**
- 20 of 45 captures were recaptures. Las Vegas Bay had 10 of 21; Echo Bay had 10 of 17 (including six previously PIT-tagged fish).
- Growth: about 22 mm/year (Las Vegas Bay), about 11 mm/year (Echo Bay).

**Spawning**
- Spawning occurred at Blackbird Point and the back of Echo Bay, as in year one.
- Spawning period: late February to early May.
- An aerial survey (March 19, 1998) found no spawning razorback suckers across all major basins. Only common carp were seen, and the authors concluded that aerial surveys may be unreliable on Lake Mead.

**Larvae**
- Las Vegas Bay: 159 larvae in 1998 versus 25 in 1997. Blackbird Point had 82 (CPM 0.08) and Pitch Fork Cove 75 (CPM 0.13).
- Echo Bay: about 197 larvae in 1998 (CPM 0.19) versus over 5,000 in 1997 (CPM 3.95).
- No larvae were found in Gregg Basin (18 stations, March 14-16, 1998).
- Scattered larvae found elsewhere were thought to have drifted from Blackbird Point or Echo Bay.

**Population estimates** (Schnabel and CAPTURE models)
- Las Vegas Bay: about 400 fish in year one, revised to 170-200 with all data, and 90-100 using only BIO/WEST data.
- Echo Bay: 50-60 fish in year one, revised to about 70-90 with all data. The Executive Summary states fewer than 100.
- Changes were attributed to changes in marked versus unmarked fish captured, and to improved precision rather than actual population change.

**Sonic telemetry**
- Two Blackbird Point tags were found dead or stationary; tag 374 (a 588-mm male) was found floating dead on May 13, 1998.
- Around 50% of implanted tags became inactive, as with previous studies on the [[San Juan River]] (Ryden 1997) and [[Upper Colorado River]] populations.
- Because of tag losses and fish mortality, sonic implants will no longer be placed in fish from Echo Bay or Las Vegas Wash. Future implants will use noninvasive external attachment.

**Plans for year three (July 1998-June 1999)**
1. Monthly trammel netting at Echo Bay and Las Vegas Bay to estimate population size.
2. Continued sonic tracking for habitat use and life-history data.
3. Monitoring of the two spawning areas for larval production.
4. Larval light-trapping across new lake areas, with trammel netting and sonic implants if new concentrations are found.
5. Juvenile sampling at Echo Bay and Blackbird Point/Las Vegas Bay.
