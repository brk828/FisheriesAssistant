# Letter on Experimental Reintroduction of Bonytail Chub in Arizona (April 26, 1988)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This April 26, 1988 letter from Temple A. Reynolds, Director of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], to Michael J. Spear, Regional Director of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], states the department's agreement with a course of action to reintroduce [[Bonytail Chub]] in Arizona under [[Experimental Nonessential Rules]]. The letter follows an earlier discussion summarized in a letter from Russell Earnest dated April 1. The department concludes that no other approach is available to begin recovery of the species in Arizona.

## Background & Description

- **Document type:** Official correspondence (letter), with a carbon copy to Terry B. Johnson.
- **Sender:** Temple A. Reynolds, Director, Arizona Game and Fish Department (signed "TAR: TBJ: rp").
- **Recipient:** Michael J. Spear, Regional Director, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Subject:** Reintroduction of the bonytail chub (*Gila elegans*) under experimental nonessential rules.
- **Commission:** The letterhead lists the Arizona Game and Fish Commission, chaired by Jerry D. Adams of Bullhead City, with commissioners Frances W. Werner (Tucson), Thomas L. Woods Jr. (Phoenix), Phillip W. Ashcroft (Eagar), and Gordon K. Whiting (Kingman). Deputy Director Duane L. Shroufe is also listed.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The letter gives no data on bonytail population status, habitat conditions, water chemistry, or sample sizes. Its context is regulatory and administrative. It refers to a recovery effort for the species in Arizona that depends on experimental nonessential status under a federal framework. It also refers to a [[Section 6 Agreement]] between state and federal agencies, which the department expects will support recovery actions.

## Key Findings

- The Arizona Game and Fish Department concurs with the proposed course of action to reintroduce bonytail chub under experimental nonessential rules.
- The department considers this the only feasible way to start recovery of bonytail chub in Arizona, based on the arguments presented in earlier discussions.
- The Russell Earnest letter of April 1, 1988 is cited as an accurate summary of the earlier discussion on the arguments for and against reintroduction.
- The department expects U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service support for recovery actions under the existing Section 6 Agreement.
- Recovery activities should be integrated into current department projects to pursue bonytail recovery effectively and efficiently.
- The department commits to working with the Service so that necessary steps are taken in a timely fashion.
