# Report of the Twenty-Second Session of the European Inland Fisheries Advisory Commission (EIFAC)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #inland-fisheries

## Executive Summary

This FAO Fisheries Report (No. 681, 2002) records the proceedings of the Twenty-second Session of the [[European Inland Fisheries Advisory Commission]] (EIFAC), held in Windermere, United Kingdom, 12–19 June 2002, alongside a Symposium on [[Inland Fisheries Management and the Aquatic Environment]]. The session reviewed EIFAC's work since 2000 across four sub-commissions: fishery biology and management, aquaculture, protection of the aquatic resource, and social and economic issues. It adopted decisions on working parties (several established, merged, suspended or cancelled), elected officers, and set the Twenty-third Session for Poland (26 May to 2 June 2004).

## Background & Description

EIFAC is an intergovernmental body operating under the [[Food and Agriculture Organization]] (FAO). The session was attended by representatives of 20 Member countries, one non-Member nation, and observers from two international non-governmental organizations, with 34 participants recorded in the list of participants. The Session was chaired by [[Rudolf Müller]] (Switzerland). Opening addresses were given by [[Elliott Morley]] (UK Parliamentary Under-Secretary, DEFRA), [[Sir John Harman]] (Chairman, Environment Agency), and [[Heiner Naeve]] (EIFAC Secretary), with a closing address by Mr Müller.

Background context from the addresses:
- EIFAC was founded in 1957 and had grown from 16 to 33 members.
- The Environment Agency of England and Wales, Europe's largest environmental regulator, stated a statutory duty to maintain, improve and develop fisheries.
- Fishing in England and Wales was cited as contributing £2,400 million in annual expenditure, with more than £1,000 spent per angler per year.
- Naeve noted that this was his last session as Secretary.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The session addressed several threats and management problems affecting inland fish stocks:

- **[[European Eel]] decline:** Recruitment fell in the 1980s, stayed low in the 1990s and was lower again in 2001. Landings have declined over a longer period. Provisional advice recommends escapement of at least 30 percent of unexploited spawner production, managed on a river catchment basis.
- **Habitat modification:** Dams, drainage, arterial drainage, riparian vegetation removal and overgrazing were identified as key degradation problems for salmonid rivers.
- **Non-native species and stocking:** Introductions can increase niche overlap and competition, co-introduce parasites, and spread disease. Risks are higher for exotic species than for transplants, so a precautionary approach is advised.
- **Cormorant predation:** Bird predation was a recurring conflict between fisheries and ornithologists, addressed by the [[REDCAFE]] project.
- **[[Sturgeon]] conservation:** Sturgeons are listed as endangered and protected under [[CITES]]; the Lower Danube stock collapse was linked to inadequate conservation.
- **Endocrine disruptors:** The related working party was discontinued after GESAMP withdrew, as no new substantive information had emerged.

## Key Findings

**Eels**
- The Joint EIFAC/ICES Ad hoc Working Party on Eels (Copenhagen, August 2001; 19 people from 10 countries) recommended an international commission for eel management, a recovery plan with reduced fishing mortality, and a provisional escapement limit of at least 30 percent of the unexploited state.
- EIFAC expressed concern at the slow progress toward a European eel management plan and asked the EC DG-FISH to note the Nantes working group meeting (2–6 September 2002).

**Symposium outcomes**
- The Symposium (114 participants from 27 countries; 29 papers and 29 posters) concluded that fisheries management benefits ecosystems and stakeholders, but stocking and introductions can have negative impacts.
- Traditional management is not always implemented successfully, so participatory and risk-based approaches are needed.
- Biomanipulation with small brown trout at low stocking densities was not effective in central European reservoirs. Including experienced anglers and avoiding catch-and-release were suggested.
- Oxygenation can expand salmonid habitat in summer-depleted lakes, but continuing nutrient input perpetuates eutrophication.
- Cutaway peat-bog lakes showed excellent water quality, with plant colonization of nearly 95 percent within three years, and were estimated to support tench and carp fisheries at a stocking rate of 200 kg/ha.

**Water Framework Directive (WFD)**
- The WFD (published December 2000) requires fish fauna to be assessed as a biological quality element. A Liaison Group (I.G. Cowx and A.J.P. Raat) was established, and its aims were merged into the Fish Monitoring and Mapping working parties.
- Work is linked to CEN standardization of fish sampling and the EU FAME project.

**Working party decisions**
- *Established:* Management of Sturgeon (joint EIFAC/GFCM, convened by M. Chebanov); Handling of Fishes in Fisheries and Aquaculture (convened by A.J.P. Raat), to produce a code of practice on handling and welfare.
- *Cancelled or suspended:* Fish and Crustacean Nutrition; Evaluation of Endocrine Disrupting Substances; Communication and Education.
- *Pending:* Aquatic Environmental Hazard Assessment, to be explored with R. Blust.

**Aquaculture (Sub-Commission II)**
- Organic fish farming: a questionnaire and a scoping workshop (Nürnberg, 18 February 2002) identified a need for uniform criteria, standards and certification.
- Market perspectives: an EIFAC/EC working party (Brussels, May 2001; 26 invited experts) produced a report published as EIFAC Occasional Paper No. 35.
- An International Conference on Aquaculture Economics and Marketing is set for Szarvas, Hungary, 29 September to 2 October 2002.

**Recreational and socio-economic fisheries (Sub-Commission IV)**
- The Nordic recreational fisheries survey was completed.
- The Third World Recreational Fisheries Congress (Darwin, May 2002) proposed a World Recreational Fisheries Forum and an international Code of Practice.
- The Fourth Congress will be held in Norway in 2005.

**Future programme and decisions**
- Symposium for the Twenty-third Session: "Aquaculture Development – Partnership between Science and Producer Associations," convened by L. Váradi, chaired by K. Goryczko, Wierzba, Poland, 26 May to 2 June 2004. Abstracts were due by 31 August 2003.
- An ad hoc group, convened by R. Müller, will evaluate EIFAC's role and functioning, working primarily by correspondence.
- Elected officers: R. Müller (Switzerland), Chairperson; K. Pintér (Hungary), First Vice-Chairperson; P. Hickley (UK), Second Vice-Chairperson.
- The Executive Committee meets 20–22 May 2003 in Rome.

**Recommendations to Members (Symposium, adopted by the Commission)**
- Improve communication and education on inland water protection.
- Include all stakeholders in decision-making, moving toward participatory management.
- Fully evaluate catchments before restoration, set realistic goals, and disseminate post-project monitoring results.
- Adopt risk-based approaches to management, with legislation scaled to risk.
- Establish mechanisms for managing shared international water bodies.
- Develop in vivo conservation mechanisms for endangered fish, with sturgeons as a priority.
