# Development of a Preliminary Index of Biotic Integrity (IBI) Based on Fish Assemblages to Assess Ecosystem Condition in the Lakes of Central Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Atherinidae #Goodeidae #index-of-biotic-integrity

## Executive Summary

This study proposes a preliminary fish-based [[Index of Biotic Integrity]] (IBI) for the lakes of central Mexico, a region whose lakes are of high cultural, economic and biological value but are degrading at an accelerating rate. The index was built from historical fish data for 19 lakes and tested on four well-studied case-study lakes: [[Xochimilco]], [[Cuitzeo]], [[Lake Chapala]] and [[Lake Patzcuaro]]. It comprises 10 metrics scored against lake-specific expected values. Scores (out of 100) were 15 for Xochimilco, 15 for Cuitzeo, 30 for Chapala and 90 for Patzcuaro, matching the a priori ranking of degradation. The authors stress that this was not an independent validation, because the same data were used for development and application.

## Background & Description

Central Mexican lakes supported pre-Columbian civilizations such as the [[Aztecs]] and [[Purépecha]] (Tarascans), and became the political and economic heart of the nation after the Spanish conquest. They are also home to many endemic species, especially within the [[Chirostoma]] "species flock" and the [[Goodeidae]] family.

Degradation has been driven by:
- Hydrologic modification and water extraction, with several lakes reduced in size or eliminated since 1900.
- Hyper-eutrophication and turbidity from watershed erosion and municipal, industrial and agricultural pollution.
- Overfishing and the introduction of exotic species, which have fundamentally altered fish communities in nearly all lakes.

Existing restoration efforts were hampered by the lack of an integrated, holistic indicator of lake ecosystem health. Fish communities were chosen as the indicator group because they are well studied, ecologically important, valued by people, and integrate effects across broad spatial and temporal scales. The IBI was originally developed for streams in the central United States and has since been adapted to Mexican streams ([[Lyons et al. 1995]]), but lake IBIs remain scarce.

**Approach:** Historical fish communities were reconstructed from literature and museum collections, including the Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan, and the Colección Nacional de Peces Dulceacuícolas Mexicanos. Species were classified by origin (native or exotic), relative abundance (high, moderate, low), feeding guild (carnivore, herbivore, omnivore) and tolerance (sensitive, moderate, tolerant). Data from before 1900 were used for lakes near large cities, and data from as late as the 1950s for more distant lakes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Study lakes:** The 19 lakes ranged in surface area from 300 to 125,000 ha. Chapala (eutrophic, shallow, about 7 m maximum depth) contrasts with Zirahuén (oligotrophic, deep, maximum depth over 43 m).

**Fauna:** 49 native species and two widely introduced exotics, *Carassius auratus* and *Cyprinus carpio*, were recorded. At least 10 further exotics had been introduced since 1900. Native species per lake ranged from 3 to 26.

**Lake area relationships:** Native species richness correlated strongly with lake area (Pearson's r = 0.91, P < 0.0001). This relationship weakened to r = 0.56 (P = 0.0207) when the two largest lakes, Chapala and Cuitzeo, were excluded. Richness of common species, sensitive species and Chirostoma species also correlated with area.

**Dominant families:** Goodeidae (17 species, 19 lakes) and Atherinidae (17 Chirostoma species, 18 lakes) were the most widespread. Cyprinidae (10 native species) and Poeciliidae (3 native species) were also common, while Petromyzontidae, Catostomidae and Ictaluridae were restricted.

**Fisheries:** Every lake supported a fishery. Target species nearly always included a large-bodied species (over 150 mm SL), though small Chirostoma were also important in some lakes. Disease was uncommon, and parasite burdens were low in historical samples.

## Key Findings

**Metrics of the preliminary IBI (10 total):**
1. Number of total native species
2. Number of common native species
3. Number of native Goodeidae species
4. Number of native Chirostoma species
5. Number of native sensitive species
6. Percent of biomass as tolerant species
7. Percent of biomass as exotic species
8. Percent of biomass as native carnivores
9. Maximum standard length of native species
10. Percent of exotic invertebrate parasite species in or on native fishes

Metrics 1 to 5 and 9 use lake-specific expected values (Table 3). Metrics 6 to 8 and 10 use fixed criteria. Each metric scores 0, 5 or 10 points, with total scores out of 100.

**Case-study results:**

| Lake | Degradation (relative) | IBI score (/100) |
|---|---|---|
| [[Xochimilco]] | Extreme | 15 |
| [[Cuitzeo]] | Extreme | 15 |
| [[Lake Chapala]] | Severe | 30 |
| [[Lake Patzcuaro]] | Moderate | 90 |

**Xochimilco:** Historically 6 native species, 4 sensitive carnivores. Drainage and pollution led to the extinction of *Evarra bustamantei* and *E. eigenmanni*. *Cyprinus carpio* came to dominate, and the fishery collapsed because of contamination. Restoration began in the 1980s, but water quality remains far below pre-1900 levels.

**Cuitzeo:** Historically 15 native species, including 6 sensitive species. By the early 1990s only 4 native species persisted, and the lake was hypereutrophic, with night dissolved oxygen sometimes below 1 mg/l. Exotic carp and tilapia (*Oreochromis aureus*) made up 79% of fishery biomass by 1989.

**Chapala:** Historically 26 native species, including 11 sensitive species. A 1990 drought caused a 21% decline in surface area and a 69% decline in volume. Four high-value species (*Ictalurus dugesi*, *Chirostoma lucius*, *C. promelas*, *C. sphyraena*) were overexploited. The largest *I. dugesi* once exceeded 1000 mm SL, but now rarely exceeds 500 mm.

**Patzcuaro:** Historically 10 native species, including 2 sensitive species. Watershed erosion has reduced subbasin depths by over 2 m, and Secchi depth fell from 3–4 m to 0.2–0.5 m. The fishery of *Chirostoma estor* (pescado blanco) is threatened, possibly by predation from *[[Micropterus salmoides]]*. Because Patzcuaro retains a relatively intact fauna, it scored highest.

**Key conclusions:**
- The IBI accurately ranked the relative degradation of the four case-study lakes, but this is preliminary and requires independent testing on other lakes.
- Fishery survey data are more practical than scientific surveys across most central Mexican lakes, though some scientific sampling is still needed for small species.
- The IBI is best suited to tracking long-term trends in ecosystem health, not short-term changes.
- The index currently applies only to lakes with sufficient pre-impact historical data. Future development should use least-impacted reference lakes, particularly among the smaller lakes.
- Fishless or species-poor high-altitude lakes will need invertebrate, zooplankton and phytoplankton metrics, as in [[Harig & Bain 1998]].
- Exotic parasites were a sensitive indicator, and the metric uses exotic parasite species per fish as a share of all parasite species.
