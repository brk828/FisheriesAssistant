# Colombian Freshwater Crab Notes

#carcinology #freshwater-crabs #trichodactylidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 paper by [[Waldo L. Schmitt]] of the [[Smithsonian Institution]] reviews a collection of freshwater crabs from Colombia. The material was sent to the [[United States National Museum]] in 1940–41 by [[Brother Niceforo Maria]], director of the museum of the Instituto de La Salle in Bogotá. The notes establish several new locality records, describe one new species, and comment on taxonomic problems with species and subspecies described by [[Gerhard Pretzmann]]. The central argument is that carapace armature varies with sex, age and size, so the male [[Gonopods]] are the only reliable diagnostic character for species identification.

## Background & Description

The notes are a belated and unorthodox account of specimens collected from Puerto Asís, Putumayo, near the Ecuadorian border, to Cúcuta and vicinity in Norte de Santander, near the Venezuelan border. The collection contained four new species and one new subspecies. Pretzmann published all of these except the one Schmitt describes here, [[Hypolobocera niceforoi]].

The paper covers two families:

- **Trichodactylidae**: [[Valdivia niceforoi]] (with subspecies *cucutensis*), [[Valdivia serrata]], [[Valdivia piriformis]], [[Valdivia devillei]] (questionable identification), and [[Holthuisia pictus]], an Argentine species used to illustrate variation.
- **Pseudothelphusidae**: [[Hypolobocera bouvieri]], [[Hypolobocera macropa]], [[Hypolobocera guenteri]], [[Hypolobocera niceforoi]] (new species), and [[Pseudothelphusa agrestis]].

Gonopod figures are at about three times natural size. Carapace figures are close to natural size.

Key taxonomic history:

- **Valdivia niceforoi**: Pretzmann first named it *Trichodactylus (Valdivia) niceforei* in 1968. Schmitt notes the correct spelling is *niceforoi*. Pretzmann placed it in the new subgenus *Rotundovaldivia*. The type was a 32.1 mm specimen from Pamplona, of uncertain sex. Schmitt's detailed description is based on a 34 mm specimen from Cúcuta (USNM 112117).
- **Subgenus Valdivia**: Pretzmann validated the genus *Valdivia* White, 1847, and created two subgenera, *Valdivia* and *Rotundovaldivia*.
- **Hypolobocera guenteri**: Pretzmann first described it as *Strengeria (Neostrengeria) guenteri* (1965). He later found that *Strengeria* was a junior synonym of *Hypolobocera*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document is taxonomic and gives little ecological detail. Localities are named as rivers, towns and provinces, including Río Caquetá, Río Magdalena (Puerto Salgar), Río Caquetá (Intendencia de Caquetá), and the Sardinata area northwest of Cúcuta. It does not report altitude, water chemistry, habitat type or population density.

Two biological patterns are described:

- Carapace armature, especially the lateral spines, varies with **growth and size**. In male *V. piriformis*, spines become reduced and nearly obsolete in the largest males. Females keep sharp spines even when large.
- Some collected males were recently moulted (soft), which affects measurements and armature.

The author suggests the same pattern holds for the American crayfish genus *Procambarus*, which Horton H. Hobbs Jr. studied.

## Key Findings

**Collection and new taxa**
- The Niceforo material included four new species and a new subspecies. Pretzmann published all but one.
- Schmitt describes *Hypolobocera (Phyllothelphusa) niceforoi* n. sp. from the holotype male (USNM 125133), which measures 22.3 mm in carapace length by 38 mm wide. The specimen is from San Gil, south of Bucaramanga.

**Valdivia niceforoi**
- The description is based on a 34 mm female-or-male specimen from Cúcuta (USNM 112117). The collection includes three males and two females.
- The chelae are markedly unequal in the type. The right is more than twice the height of the left.
- The anterior gonopods are unique among trichodactylids examined by Schmitt. Their distal two-thirds form a hollow sheath through which the second gonopods project up to 2 mm.
- The frontal armature varies. Females have one to three frontal spines, while males typically have two.

**Valdivia serrata**
- Three specimens from Puerto Asís are identified as *V. serrata*: a male (48 × 55.6 mm) and two females (42.7 × 49.2 mm and 38 × 45.5 mm).

**Valdivia piriformis**
- Pretzmann's holotype is a 48 mm male from Cúcuta (Niceforo collection). Schmitt identifies it as Niceforo specimen No. 23 from Sardinata. The male is just over 49 mm long by 51 mm wide.
- The Niceforo collection contains other specimens: a male of 44.2 × 45.9 mm from Cúcuta (No. 20), a female of 54.5 × 59.2 mm from Puerto Salgar, Río Magdalena (No. 22), and females of 36, 37 and 40 mm from near the Venezuelan border.
- Schmitt concludes that spine reduction in males is progressive with size. Females show no such reduction.

**Valdivia devillei**
- A lone female, identified by Schmitt as *T. (V.) peruvianus* (51.8 × 56 mm), carries a *devillei* label added by Pretzmann. Its final identity is left open.

**Hypolobocera species**
- *H. bouvieri*: five specimens (one male, four females) from San Gil, Sasaima, Río Caquetá, and Chinácota (42 km south of Cúcuta). Pretzmann agreed with these identifications.
- *H. macropa*: a single male (31 × 52 mm) from Bogotá.
- *H. guenteri*: the holotype male is 25 mm, from Bogotá. Two further specimens (26.5 × 44.3 mm and 28.4 × 49.9 mm) came from Pennsilvania, Caldas.
- *H. niceforoi* is placed in subgenus *Phyllothelphusa*, but Schmitt says it may belong in *Neostrengeria*. The spined funnel margin of the gonopod and the carpal spine separate it from *H. guenteri*.

**Holthuisia pictus**
- Three males from Acequia and Ovata, Tucumán, Argentina, show a size-related reduction in lateral spines. The largest male (44.2 × 52.6 mm) retains some sharp spines. Gonopods are alike in all three.

**Conclusions and taxonomic argument**
- Variation in carapace form, spination and convexity occurs with sex, age and size in these crabs.
- Male gonopods are the most stable diagnostic character. Schmitt argues that gonopod figures are essential for any species description.
- Schmitt commends illustrated works by [[Alfred E. Smalley]] (1964a, b, c), Rodríguez (1966a, b) and Bott (1956). He also publishes a new gonopod figure for *Pseudothelphusa agrestis*, the type of subgenus *Achlidon*.
