## Executive Summary

This document is a **Scientific Collecting Permit renewal application** submitted by Dr. W. L. Minckley (Arizona State University, Department of Biology) to the Arizona Game and Fish Department in December 1998. Rather than presenting primary experimental data, the document provides administrative and programmatic justification for statewide collecting privileges tied to ongoing research, teaching, and conservation programs involving federally and state-listed fishes. It identifies key native fish taxa of conservation concern in Arizona and the geographic scope of the applicant's research, including work with the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team** and the **Native Fish Work Group**.

## Background & Description

Dr. Minckley describes three major justifications for a general, statewide scientific collecting permit:

1. **Teaching & Curatorial Responsibilities**: Minckley oversees the [[Conservation Biology]] curriculum at Arizona State University, including maintenance of terrestrial and aquatic wildlife collections used for teaching and research. This includes salvage of road-killed vertebrates and specimens (skins, skulls, wings) of game and non-game species. A course titled "Field Methods in Conservation Biology" trains students in small-mammal trapping, mist-netting, and fish sampling techniques statewide.

2. **Fish Collection Curation**: Minckley is responsible for the comprehensive **Arizona State University Fish Collection**, curated by Dr. [[Michael Douglas]], and for a statewide database tracking both native and non-native fish distributions across Arizona's natural and artificial aquatic habitats.

3. **Listed Species Research & Recovery**: As team leader for the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team**, Minckley conducts active research and monitoring on federally and state-listed fish species across multiple Arizona drainages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The application specifies several key study locations and their associated conservation frameworks:

- **[[Aravaipa Creek]]** (Graham and Pinal counties) — long-term native fish monitoring site.
- **West Turkey Creek** (Cochise County) — studied in association with the **El Coronado Ranch Habitat Conservation Plan**.
- **[[Gila River Basin]]** and **[[Yaqui River Basin]]** — range-wide research on native topminnows.
- **[[Gila River Basin]]** and **Lower Colorado River Basin** — range-wide research on native pupfishes.
- **Gila and Colorado Rivers** — "big-river" fish research conducted in association with the **Native Fish Work Group**, focused on large-bodied endemic fishes.

These sites represent critical desert aquatic habitats supporting endemic, isolated fish populations vulnerable to habitat fragmentation, non-native species introduction, and water scarcity typical of the arid Southwest.

## Key Findings

- **Target Taxa of Conservation Concern** identified in the permit application include:
  - **[[Gila Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) — Gila River Basin.
  - **[[Yaqui Topminnow]]** (*Poeciliopsis sonoriensis*) — Yaqui River Basin.
  - **[[Quitobaquito Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon eremus*) — Gila Basin.
  - **[[Desert Pupfish]]** (*Cyprinodon macularius*) — Gila and Lower Colorado Basins.
  - **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) — Gila and Colorado Rivers ("big-river" fish fauna).
  - **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — Gila and Colorado Rivers ("big-river" fish fauna).

- **Institutional Infrastructure**: The application confirms the existence and active maintenance of a statewide native/non-native fish distribution database curated through Arizona State University, supporting long-term biogeographic and conservation research in Arizona.

- **Collection Methods Authorized** under the permit include electroshocking, angling, gill netting, seining/hand capture, drugs (for immobilization), and live trapping — standard techniques for small-bodied desert stream fishes.

- **Disposition Categories** permitted include salvage of dead specimens, collection by killing, and live capture/release, indicating a mixed-methods approach balancing lethal voucher collection with live population monitoring for sensitive listed species.

- **Programmatic Scope**: The permit supports a multi-institutional conservation network spanning the **Desert Fishes Recovery Team**, the **Native Fish Work Group**, and private-land habitat conservation planning (El Coronado Ranch HCP), reflecting the collaborative, multi-agency structure typical of endangered desert fish recovery efforts in Arizona during the late 1990s.
