# Notice of intent to prepare EA on Rare Fish Recovery program (51 Fed. Reg. 27256)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #ColoradoRiverBasin

> **Note on source material:** This document is not a primary scientific study but a 1986 comment letter from the Colorado River Water Conservation District to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Galen Buterbaugh, Regional Director) regarding a proposed Environmental Assessment (EA) for the "Rare Fish Recovery Program" (51 Fed. Reg. 27256) in the Upper Colorado River Basin. It contains policy commentary rather than morphological, physiological, or population data. The note below extracts the taxonomic and ecological-management content present in the text.

## Executive Summary
The letter addresses a proposed federal recovery program targeting four fish species native to the Upper [[Colorado River Basin]]: the [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]]. Of these, only the first three were federally listed as endangered at the time of writing (1986); the Razorback Sucker was a "candidate" species being newly considered for inclusion. The District questions the scientific basis and urgency for adding a fourth species to the recovery plan, raises concerns about a 15-year species restoration timeline, and requests reconsideration of mandated [[Instream Flow Requirements]] in favor of alternative habitat management strategies. The letter also emphasizes that hydropower production and water resource development activities (including obligations under interstate compacts) must be weighed against conservation measures.

## Background & Description
No morphological, meristic, or anatomical data are provided in this source. The document is administrative/regulatory in nature and does not describe body form, coloration, fin structure, or size classes for any of the four species referenced.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
Limited ecological content is present, framed entirely around water management rather than autecology:

- **Habitat dependency**: All four species are implicitly tied to riverine flow regimes in the Upper Colorado River system; the recovery program's central regulatory mechanism concerns [[Instream Flow Requirements]] as a tool for habitat protection.
- **Proposed alternative habitats**: The letter suggests substituting or supplementing natural instream flows with engineered habitat features, including:
  - Man-made backwaters
  - Artificial spawning habitat
  - Artificial rearing areas
  - Jetties and other channel/habitat management structures
- **Population status context**: The [[Bonytail Chub]] is noted as having sparse and dated occurrence data, raising questions about whether current population information supports its continued endangered listing or the resources allocated to it within the recovery plan.
- **Competing water uses**: The niche discussion is bounded by competing consumptive water uses — hydropower generation, water storage, and beneficial consumptive use under state and interstate legal frameworks (e.g., interstate compacts) — positioned as factors that constrain habitat/flow-based conservation measures.

## Key Findings
- **Species roster under review**: The Rare Fish Recovery Program proposal (as of the 1986 Federal Register notice, 51 Fed. Reg. 27256) considered expanding recovery efforts from three federally endangered species ([[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]]) to include a fourth, the [[Razorback Sucker]], then only a listing candidate.
- **Data adequacy concern**: The commenters flag that occurrence/distribution data for the [[Bonytail Chub]] are sparse and dated, suggesting a need for updated field surveys before finalizing recovery obligations, and raise the possibility of delisting the Bonytail Chub to simplify the overall recovery proposal.
- **Timeline feasibility**: The plan's stated goal of restoring the three endangered species within 15 years is characterized as ambitious, with concern that adding a fourth species with potentially different recovery requirements could complicate achieving that timeline.
- **Flow policy alternative recommended**: The District recommends that the EA evaluate alternatives to legally mandated [[Instream Flow Requirements]] (e.g., constructed backwaters, artificial spawning/rearing habitat, jetties) rather than defaulting to instream flow water rights, arguing current science had not conclusively established instream flows as the only viable protective measure.
- **Water resource development impacts**: Any recovery alternatives should be assessed against impacts on hydropower production and other consumptive water development, including obligations under interstate compacts governing the Colorado River Basin.
- **Procedural stance**: The commenting body agreed with prior submitters (including the Colorado Water Conservation Board) that a full EA/EIS may be premature at this early planning stage, but submitted these comments preemptively in case the agency proceeded.
