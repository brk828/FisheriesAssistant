## Executive Summary
This document is a 1988 interagency memorandum from the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation (Lower Colorado Regional Office) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), transmitting a Biological Assessment for the [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*) in connection with the proposed Central Arizona Water Exchange Project on the upper [[Verde River]] in central Arizona. The memo does not contain detailed morphological or ecological data on the species itself; rather, it documents the regulatory history and the determination that the proposed water diversion project "may affect" the spikedace, triggering formal consultation under Section 7 of the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The spikedace was Federally listed as threatened in 1986, following its identification as a Category 1 candidate species in 1983.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat association**: The [[Spikedace]] occurs in the upper [[Verde River]] system in central Arizona, an area subject to stream flow diversion under the proposed project.
- **Co-occurring listed species**: The project area also supports the [[Bald Eagle]] (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*) and [[Peregrine Falcon]] (*Falco peregrinus anatum*), both Federally listed at the time, indicating a riparian corridor of broader conservation significance.
- **Threat pathway**: The Central Arizona Water Exchange Project would divert stream flow within the spikedace's range, implicating alteration of flow regime, habitat availability (e.g., riffle/run habitat dependent on maintained discharge), and connectivity as primary mechanisms of potential impact — consistent with known sensitivity of small-bodied [[Cyprinidae]] stream fishes to flow reduction and habitat fragmentation.
- **Regulatory classification history**: Listed as a Category 1 candidate species (1983) prior to formal Federal listing as Threatened (1986), reflecting escalating recognition of population vulnerability.
