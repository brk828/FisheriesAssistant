## Executive Summary

Personnel from Arizona State University surveyed multiple impoundments at [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] on 12–13 January 1988 to assess reintroduced stocks of the endangered [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*). Sampling included fish collection (gill nets, trammel nets, seining) for growth/dietary analysis, water sampling for plankton, and Ekman-grab sediment sampling for benthic invertebrates. Bonytail persisted only in Road Camp Tank, with populations in Kings and Big Bertha tanks apparently extirpated. Razorback suckers were recovered from Mormon Lake, Rock Tank, and Tequila Tank, with all recovered Mormon Lake fish attributable to a 1987 fry stocking; larger fish from an earlier 1986 stocking were not captured, likely due to gear-avoidance behavior rather than true absence.

## Background & Description

This report documents a targeted fisheries assessment of captive/refuge populations of two federally significant Colorado River basin fishes:

- **[[Bonytail]]** (*Gila elegans*, family [[Cyprinidae]]) — a critically endangered cyprinid native to the Colorado River basin, maintained in refuge tanks as part of a conservation stocking program.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*, family [[Catostomidae]]) — an endangered sucker species also native to the Colorado River basin, subject to repeated fry/fingerling stocking efforts at the refuge.

Sampling gear included:
- Experimental gill nets (30-minute sets) — Road Camp Tank (bonytail)
- Overnight trammel net sets and daytime seining with trammel nets — Mormon Lake
- Unspecified capture methods — Rock Tank and Tequila Tank

Ancillary sampling: water samples for plankton analysis; Ekman-grab benthic invertebrate samples — indicating a broader ecosystem monitoring effort beyond target fish species, though invertebrate/plankton results are not detailed in this report.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Site network**: The refuge contains multiple discrete water bodies (Road Camp Tank, Kings Tank, Big Bertha Tank, Mormon Lake, Little Mormon Lake, Rock Tank, Tequila Tank) functioning as isolated grow-out/refugial habitats for endangered fish stocks.
- **Population isolation risk**: Bonytail have been extirpated from Kings and Big Bertha tanks, leaving Road Camp Tank as the sole remaining bonytail population on the refuge — a significant genetic/demographic bottleneck risk.
- **Growth and habitat size effects**: Razorback suckers in smaller tanks (Rock, Tequila) were substantially smaller (~25% shorter, ~30% lighter) than conspecifics in the larger Mormon Lake, suggesting habitat size (or associated productivity/density factors) influences growth rates.
- **Gear selectivity bias**: Larger razorback suckers are noted to evade trammel nets and seines via a "burrowing" head-down escape behavior at the net's leading edge, a behavior corroborated by parallel observations at [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]]. This suggests standard netting methods systematically undersample larger, older cohorts, biasing population assessments toward smaller/younger fish.
- **Recommended gear improvement**: A heavy-leaded haul seine (12–15 ft deep × 150–200 ft long, 1.5" mesh) or partial lake drainage is proposed to improve capture of larger razorback suckers and better realize the grow-out potential of these habitats.

## Key Findings

### Bonytail — Road Camp Tank
- **n = 28** (gill net sample)
- Average TL: **190 mm** (range 163–247 mm)
- Average weight: **51 g** (range 34–118 g)
- Condition: excellent; no external parasites observed
- ~1/3 of fish showed rosy-red coloration on/near paired fin bases (sex undetermined)
- No sex products expressible from any fish
- 10 fish sacrificed: 4 immature males, 6 females (containing developing but immature ova)
- **Management implication**: Potential spring spawning event predicted; recommend larvae collection and translocation to Mormon or Little Mormon Lake to establish a second bonytail stock and reduce extirpation risk.

### Razorback Sucker — Mormon Lake
- **n = 100** (overnight trammel net + daytime seining)
- Average TL: **197 mm** (range 170–243 mm)
- Average weight: **85 g** (range 40–114 g)
- Condition: good; no external parasites
- No tubercle formation observed; no expressible sex products
- All recovered fish attributed to the **31 March 1987** stocking of 40,000 fry (~10 mm TL)
- **No fish recovered** from the earlier **3 November 1986** stocking (3,000 fish, ~127 mm TL) — consistent with independent sampling by Simons et al. (17 December 1987)
- Interpretation: Absence of larger cohort in samples likely reflects **gear avoidance by larger fish**, not true extirpation; size convergence between faster-growing Nov. 1986 fish and the March 1987 cohort may also make cohorts indistinguishable

### Razorback Sucker — Rock Tank
- **n = 65**
- Average TL: **152 mm** (range 123–219 mm)
- Average weight: **36 g** (range 22–78 g)

### Razorback Sucker — Tequila Tank
- **n = 29**
- Average TL: **164 mm** (range 141–206 mm)
- Average weight: **45 g** (range 26–89 g)

### Comparative Growth Note
Razorback suckers in Rock and Tequila tanks were ~25% shorter and ~30% lighter than those in Mormon Lake, potentially due to differences in habitat size or productivity.
