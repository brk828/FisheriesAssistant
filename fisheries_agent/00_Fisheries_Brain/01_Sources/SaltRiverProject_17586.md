# Salt River Project: Irrigation, Storage, and Power History (1960 Report)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-hydrology

## Executive Summary

This 1960 organizational report, attributed to the [[Salt River Project]] (SRP), traces the history, infrastructure, and statistics of a federal reclamation project in the [[Salt River Valley]] of central Arizona. It covers pre-Columbian canal irrigation by the [[Hohokam]], the 1902 federal reclamation effort under the [[Reclamation Act of 1902]], dam construction, water-rights adjudication, the 1917 transfer of operations to the local Association, and later power-plant expansion. The document contains no fish, invertebrate, or other aquatic species data. Its ecological content is limited to water supply, watershed runoff, and vegetation effects.

## Background & Description

- **Origin:** Irrigation in the valley predates European settlement. The Hohokam diverted Salt River water through about 125 miles of hand-dug canals around 200 B.C. Local settlers formed informal ditch companies from 1869, which led to disputes over priority and equitable distribution.
- **Federal project:** The Salt River Valley Water Users' Association was incorporated on February 9, 1903. A basic agreement was executed June 25, 1904, and the Association was the first written agreement of record with the United States.
- **Governance:** The Salt River Project Agricultural Improvement and Power District was formed in 1937 with the same boundaries as the Association. In 1949 the District took over the Project, and the Association continued to operate the irrigation system as its agent.
- **Operations transfer:** A contract signed September 6, 1917 turned over operation and maintenance to the Association. Possession began November 1, 1917.
- **Scope:** The report is a corporate and historical memorandum. It contains no sampling data, species lists, or aquatic-biology methods.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Water supply:** The valley lacked storage in its early period, so flood waters were largely lost. Diversion dams were temporary rock and brush structures that washed out in heavy rain.
- **Storage dams:** Six major storage structures were built on the Salt and Verde Rivers:
  - [[Roosevelt Dam]]: 1,381,580 acre-feet, 280 ft high
  - [[Horse Mesa Dam]]: 245,138 acre-feet
  - [[Mormon Flat Dam]]: 57,852 acre-feet
  - [[Stewart Mountain Dam]]: 69,765 acre-feet
  - [[Horseshoe Dam]] (Verde River): 142,830 acre-feet, 60 ft high
  - [[Bartlett Dam]] (Verde River): 179,548 acre-feet, 283 ft high
- **Watershed:** The drainage area supplying irrigation water to the reservoirs is about 13,000 square miles. Average annual rainfall is 19.07 inches at Phoenix, 18.65 inches on the Salt River, and 12 inches on the Verde River.
- **Runoff decline:** Rainfall stayed average, but runoff into the reservoirs dropped year by year. The report attributes this to uneconomic vegetation consuming water, including mesquite, willows, salt cedars, cedars, manzanitas, oak brush, and jackpine thickets. Grazing had also been reduced, and the loss of grass cover increased erosion. Estimated decreased runoff averages 30% or more. The estimated loss from 1920 to 1954 was 16 million acre-feet.
- **Watershed treatment:** A 1955 program studied and began vegetation eradication in selected watershed areas to increase runoff.

## Key Findings

**Water rights**
- The Kent Decree, entered March 1, 1910 (effective April 1, 1910), established normal-flow rights from 1869 to 1909. These cover 151,083 of the Project's 240,000 acres.
- Stored and developed water rights cover all 240,000 acres for shareholders who hold capital stock and signed a water-right application.
- The [[Pump Water Right]] program, begun in 1948, was priced at $14 per acre-foot, up to two acre-feet per acre. As of December 31, 1959, 156,990 acres held 233,765 acre-feet of pump water rights.

**Costs**
- The original Roosevelt Dam estimate was about $3,000,000 (or $2,700,000 in the 1904 agreement, $15 per acre on 180,000 acres).
- Project cost to June 30, 1912 was $9,508,831.12.
- The [[Central Board of Review]] set the net cost at $10,109,345.96 as of June 1, 1915. By January 1, 1917 this had risen to $10,279,190.98, then reduced by credits to $10,166,021.97.
- The final announced cost was $60 per acre.

**Infrastructure and power**
- Roosevelt Dam was completed in 1911, dedicated March 18, 1911, and was then the world's largest storage reservoir and dam. It remains the world's highest masonry dam.
- Additional reservoirs were built between 1922 and 1930 with bond funds. Bartlett Dam was completed in 1939, and a standby steam plant of 37,500 HP was built at Crosscut in 1941.
- Total Project generating capacity was 152,900 HP in 1941 and 428,162 KW after the Agua Fria units were completed in 1958. A third Agua Fria unit, scheduled for April 1961, adds 165,000 KW.
- The [[Kyrene Plant]] units came online in 1952 (30,000 KW) and 1954 (60,000 KW).
- Power customers grew from 45 in 1921 to 8,000 in 1941 and 79,975 in 1960.

**Water service**
- The [[City of Phoenix]] received about 56% of its water supply from the Project, serving about 375,000 customers, through a contract that delivers water rights from subdivided lots that have stopped irrigating.
- Project statistics (December 31, 1959) include 238,582 member acres, 107,571 assessed water accounts, 2,076,713 acre-feet of storage capacity (as listed), 1,271 miles of canals and laterals, and 450,000 people in the Salt River Valley.
- Total plant in service was $143,997,215. Bonds outstanding were $62,716,000.

**Modernization**
- The canal system is being rehabilitated, with concrete replacing wooden structures. The program began in 1950 and was scheduled to continue through 1967, reducing water losses and operating costs.
- The zanjero (ditch-rider) system was replaced around 1950 with radiophone-equipped dispatch service.
