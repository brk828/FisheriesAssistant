## Executive Summary
This 1955 note by Clark Hubbs re-examines the sole published Texas record of the bigeye shiner, *Notropis boops* Gilbert, which had been reported by Knapp (1953) from a juvenile specimen (32 mm) collected from the Neches River near Fastrill, Cherokee-Anderson County line, Texas (catalogued as Texas A&M No. M-3-d-505, purportedly from the "Red River" per the figure caption but labeled Neches R. on the specimen tag). Upon direct examination of the loaned specimen, Hubbs determined that it is **not** *N. boops*, contradicting the only existing Texas range record for the species. Comparative morphological analysis instead suggests the specimen is a natural hybrid between *[[Notropis venustus]]* and *[[Notropis fumeus]]*, two related cyprinid shiners. The paper thus revokes the Texas record of *N. boops* pending further confirmation and proposes a novel intersubgeneric hybrid identification.

## Background & Description
**Original Specimen Data (Knapp 1953, Fig. 85 caption):**
- Identified as juvenile *N. boops*, 1.3 in (32 mm) long
- Catalogue No. M-3-d-505, Texas A&M College

**Re-examination findings — differences from true *N. boops*:**
- Peritoneum dusky, not intense black
- Eye smaller (contained ~3× in head length, vs. 2.7× in *N. boops*)
- Middorsal stripe conspicuous
- No double marks on lateral line scales
- Dark caudal spot located mostly on fin, not on peduncle
- Body deeper and more slabsided
- Specimen in poor/battered condition (pharyngeal teeth, opercles, scales absent; fins broken), limiting further diagnostic comparison

**Comparison to *Notropis venustus*:**
Similarities:
- Melanophores on interradial membranes of dorsal fin
- Black caudal spot present
- Terminal mouth

Differences:
- Caudal spot less distinct, connected to lateral band (rather than separate)
- Lateral band extends forward through the eye
- Colors above/below lateral band fainter
- Dorsal fin somewhat more triangular in shape

**Meristic data used for hybrid diagnosis:**

| Character | Specimen | *N. venustus* (avg) | *N. fumeus* (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Anal rays | 8 | 7 | 10 |
| Lateral line scales | ~37 | 34 | 42 |

These intermediate counts, combined with intermediate coloration and body shape (more slabsided than *N. venustus*, pale flanks and triangular dorsal reminiscent of *N. fumeus*), support a hybrid hypothesis rather than a pure species identification.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Freshwater stream system; specimen collected at "St. 8 NRS," Neches River, ~5 miles east of Fastrill on Highway 294, Cherokee-Anderson County line, Texas.
- **Distributional context:** The Neches River drainage in northeast Texas hosts multiple *Notropis* species with terminal mouths, including *[[Notropis percobromus]]*, *[[Notropis fumeus]]*, *[[Notropis umbratilis]]*, and *[[Notropis lutrensis]]*, all considered as candidate parental taxa before elimination.
- Two additional species, *[[Notropis oxyrhynchus]]* and *[[Notropis brazosensis]]* (Hubbs and Bonham), were considered geographically plausible but not confirmed from this locality.
- If the original Red River locality (from the figure caption) were correct instead of the Neches River label, only *[[Notropis atherinoides]]* and *[[Notropis cornutus]]* could be added as candidate parental species — highlighting a locality discrepancy between the original publication and the specimen's actual collection tag.
- The case illustrates the taxonomic risk of unconfirmed range extensions based on morphologically ambiguous or hybrid individuals within diverse shiner (*Notropis*) assemblages of East Texas streams.

## Key Findings
- **No confirmed Texas record of *Notropis boops*:** The only previously published Texas occurrence of the bigeye shiner is invalidated; the voucher specimen used for the original description and figure is not this species.
- **Specimen identity:** The fish is most likely a natural hybrid between *[[Notropis venustus]]* and *[[Notropis fumeus]]*, based on intermediate anal ray counts, lateral line scale counts, coloration, and body morphology.
- **Expert corroboration:** Drs. George A. Moore and Royal D. Suttkus examined the specimen and confirmed it was not *N. boops*; Dr. Moore independently proposed the *venustus* × *fumeus* hybrid hypothesis.
- **Elimination of alternative hybrid parent:** Comparative specimen examination ruled out *[[Notropis percobromus]]* as a substitute parental species for *N. fumeus* in the proposed hybrid combination.
- **Taxonomic/systematic significance:** This represents a potential case of natural hybridization between the cyprinid subgenera *Lythrurus* and *Ericymba/Erogala* (as referenced via Hubbs' broader hybridization framework), a combination not previously reported in Hubbs (1955, *Syst. Zool.* 4:1–20), though intergeneric/intersubgeneric hybridization is otherwise known among minnows (Cyprinidae).
- **Recommendation:** The presence of true *N. boops* in northeast Texas remains unconfirmed and requires additional verified specimens for validation.
