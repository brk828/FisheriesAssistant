# Fishing Success for Channel Catfish and White Catfish in Ponds with Daily Feeding

#ichthyology #marine-biology #channel-catfish

## Executive Summary

This 1969 proceedings paper by [[E. E. Prather]] of the [[Auburn University]] Agricultural Experiment Station reports public sport-fishing results from experimental ponds stocked with [[Channel Catfish]] and [[White Catfish]], often alongside [[Largemouth Bass]], and fed daily. Stocking densities ranged from 1,500 to 5,000 catfish per acre. Public harvest ranged from about 1,292 to 2,938 pounds per acre, depending on the experiment. Channel catfish were consistently easier to catch than white catfish. Larger initial fingerlings (7 to 11 inches) produced harvestable fish 2 to 3 months earlier than 4-inch fingerlings. [[Aeration]] by [[Agitators]] or an [[Air Blower]] reduced [[Fish Kill]] risk from low [[Dissolved Oxygen]] and allowed heavier stocking and feeding.

## Background & Description

- **Research program:** The work builds on earlier results from [[Prather 1959]] (channel catfish as sport fish), [[Prather 1964]] (white catfish as sport fish), and [[Prather 1968]].
- **Baseline comparison:** Ponds stocked with 2,000 fingerlings per acre and fed daily 6 days a week produced about 1,300 pounds of catfish per acre in the following year. At 3,000 fingerlings per acre, the catch rose to about 1,700 pounds per acre with similar fishing effort.
- **Stocking and feeding:** Fish were fed [[Auburn No. 2 Pellets]], usually daily except Sundays, at rates of 2 to 3 percent of body weight in the abstract's account. Ponds were fertilized with [[Triple Superphosphate]]. Fish were treated with 50 ppm [[Formalin]] and 5 ppm [[Acriflavine]] before stocking.
- **Public fishing rules:** Permits cost $1 each, with a limit of 5 catfish and/or bass plus 10 tilapia per permit. Anglers could take an additional 5 catfish at $0.30 each.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Oxygen dynamics:** Fish kills occurred when low oxygen built up in the deeper water, roughly 10 to 18 feet deep, in earlier experiments. Algal die-offs were a recurring trigger.
- **Aeration equipment:** A commercial air blower (36 cfm) ran continuously from March 28 to November 20, 1968. It was released through a 1.5-inch outlet 18 inches above the pond bottom, near the drain pipe in the deepest water. Electricity use was 41.56 kWh per day, costing $0.91 daily.
- **Agitators:** Two ½ H.P. agitators raised water from 3 feet depth and sprayed it on the surface. One ran near the pond center from June 16 to November 8, 1968. A second was installed in 1969 in the deepest water (6 feet) near the dam.
- **Reproduction and predation:** Channel and white catfish spawned at 2 years old under high-density daily feeding. Few young survived where largemouth bass, bluegill and green sunfish were present. Largemouth bass seldom spawned alongside catfish under heavy feeding.
- **Species interactions:** White catfish tolerated low oxygen better than channel catfish. Of the fish present before one kill, 23.4 percent of white catfish survived, compared with 0.5 percent of channel catfish.

## Key Findings

### Harvest and Fishing Success

- **Experiment 1 (2.5 acres, 2,000 channel + 1,000 white catfish per acre):** 852.4 fishermen per acre caught 2,440 pounds per acre: 1,790.9 lb channel catfish, 544.8 lb white catfish, 14.5 lb largemouth bass, 85.1 lb tilapia, and 4.7 lb bluegill and green sunfish. Each fisherman averaged 2.86 lb, or 0.46 lb per hour.
  - Channel catfish: 66.2 percent caught, 7.2 percent recovered on draining, 73.4 percent total recovery; average catch 18 lb.
  - White catfish: 60.1 percent caught, 19.6 percent recovered on draining, 79.7 percent total; average size 0.91 lb.
- **Experiment 2 (2,000 channel + 1,000 white catfish per acre):** 715.9 fishermen per acre caught 1,954.1 lb, including 1,448.4 lb channel catfish, 442.3 lb white catfish, 59.4 lb bass, and 4.0 lb miscellaneous. Average catch was 2.7 lb per angler, or 0.47 lb per hour. Channel catfish were 85.5 percent caught; white catfish 42.0 percent.
- **Experiment 3 (1,500 channel + 1,500 white catfish per acre):** 550.2 fishermen per acre caught 1,657.8 lb, including 1,124.8 lb channel and 458.2 lb white catfish. Channel catfish were 70.8 percent caught versus 39.0 percent of white catfish.
- **Experiment 4 (3,000 larger channel catfish per acre):** 1,096 fishermen per acre caught 2,718.3 lb, including 2,654.5 lb channel catfish. Average catfish weight was 1.04 lb. Fishermen caught 84.9 percent of stocked catfish, and 1.6 percent were recovered on draining (86.5 percent total).
- **Experiment 5 (3,333 channel + 1,667 white catfish per acre):** 1,088 fishermen per acre caught 3,052.5 lb, including 2,009.6 lb channel and 928.3 lb white catfish, 105.3 lb bass, and 9.1 lb grass carp. Channel catfish were 67 percent caught versus 53 percent of white catfish. Total recovery was 81.2 percent for channel and 69.9 percent for white catfish, or 77.4 percent of the 5,000 stocked.
- **Abstract highlights:**
  - Ponds stocked with 2,000 fingerlings per acre yielded up to 1,292 lb per acre over 12 months after first opening.
  - Ponds with 7 to 11 inch channel catfish stocked at 3,000 per acre reached an average of 0.7 lb by August. Across August 1 to October 25 and March 15 to September 7, 1,096 fishermen caught 2,655 lb per acre.
  - One pond stocked with 5,000 fingerlings per acre and fed daily yielded 2,938 lb per acre over 12 months.

### Fish Kills and Survival

- **Experiment 3 fish kills:** Low-oxygen kills occurred on August 27, 1965 and June 17, 1966. The June 1966 kill recovered 205 channel catfish (333.9 lb) and 480 white catfish (332.9 lb) per acre. Feeding stopped and the pond closed after this kill. Final accounting at draining (November 21, 1966): channel catfish 1.2 percent (1965 trace), caught 1965 at 46.8 percent, fish kill 1966 at 13.7 percent, caught 1966 at 24.5 percent; white catfish 32.0 percent lost in the 1966 kill and 80.8 percent total accounted for.
- **Experiment 2 losses:** No losses from parasites, disease or low oxygen. Only 2.8 percent of stocked channel catfish and 30.2 percent of white catfish remained at draining (November 20, 1968), giving 88.1 and 72.2 percent accounted for by fishing and draining.

### Growth, Timing and Stocking Size

- Four-inch fingerlings stocked by February and fed at 2 to 3 percent body weight daily reached 0.7 lb by October.
- Larger initial stock (7 to 11 inches) reached 0.7 lb average by August and allowed fishing 2 to 3 months earlier than 5-inch fingerlings in the conclusions' comparison. The text describes the earlier comparison as 4-inch fingerlings.
- Fishing success was poor when ponds reopened in March and improved as water warmed, but never matched the first month's catch.

### Aeration Results

- The air blower's effect on production could not be determined because it ran only in the second year. It did maintain better water quality in deep areas, and fish were observed in distress only once, after an algal die-off.
- Agitators in the 2.5-acre pond (5,000 catfish per acre, feeding at 10,786 lb per acre) produced distress only on July 4, 1968, after a sudden algal die-off. The authors call for more testing on whether agitators can prevent low oxygen after heavy algal kills.

### Conclusions (as stated)

1. Fishing success was considerably better for channel than white catfish at both 2:1 and 1:1 ratios, though white catfish still provided good fishing.
2. Harvest by fishermen increased as catfish stocking density increased.
3. Ponds stocked with 3,000 to 5,000 fingerlings per acre with daily feeding gave good fishing for only 8 to 12 months, with 550 to 1,096 fishermen per acre.
4. Larger initial catfish gave fishing 2 to 3 months earlier and more pounds per acre.
5. At high densities with daily feeding, catfish spawned at 2 years old, but insufficient young escaped predation where bass, bluegill and green sunfish were present.
6. Largemouth bass seldom spawned with catfish under heavy feeding.
7. Agitator aeration appeared to raise oxygen levels, allowing heavier stocking and feeding.

### Data Notes

- The abstract states that a pond stocked with 5,000 fingerlings per acre yielded 2,938 lb per acre, but the body text does not give a table for this figure.
- The printed Table V grand total for pounds (8,052.5) conflicts with the text total of 3,052.5 lb; the text figure is used here.
- Pond sizes for two experiments are printed as 22 acres in the source, and the abstract gives size classes as 7-11 inches, while the body refers to 7-inch fish. Values are reported as printed.
