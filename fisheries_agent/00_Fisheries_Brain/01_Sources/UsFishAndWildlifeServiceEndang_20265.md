# Arizona Fishery Resources Office 1993 Endangered Species Permit Annual Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae #salmonidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] Arizona Fishery Resources Office (AzFRO) reports 1993 activities under Endangered Species Permit/Subpermit PRT-676811. It covers stocking, collecting, monitoring and mortality records for listed fishes across four satellite offices (Parker, Flagstaff, Pinetop and San Carlos). The report also appends a 1993 [[Little Colorado River]] research trip report, a loach minnow survey of Fort Apache Indian Reservation streams, and an annual report on [[Sonoran Topminnow]] at Bylas Springs. The main concerns are the incidental mortality of young-of-year [[Humpback Chub]] and the lack of loach minnow found in the surveyed streams.

## Background & Description

The document is an administrative annual report rather than a single research study. It covers listed species under the Endangered Species Act, including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Little Colorado River Spinedace]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Apache Trout]], [[Loach Minnow]] and [[Sonoran Topminnow]]. Activities were carried out under the permit, and records were grouped by satellite office. Stocking hatchery fish, collecting and tagging wild fish, and monitoring populations all required reporting of mortalities.

Hatchery-reared fish came mainly from [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and Technology Center, with additional work at [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]]. Stocking and collecting took place at several sites:
- [[Bill Williams River National Wildlife Refuge]] (Office Cove)
- [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]] (High Levee Pond)
- [[Lake Mohave]] (collections, backwater AZ-1, and Pittsburgh Point Cove)
- [[Colorado River Indian Tribe]] lands (12 Mile Lake and the main canal)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Lower Colorado River and Lake Mohave.** Razorback suckers were collected at Willow Beach (river miles 42-60), and Lake Mohave was sampled for several species. Canal drawdown on Colorado River Indian Tribe lands caused some mortalities that were not caused by collecting.

**Little Colorado River and Powell and Salt study areas (August 1993).** The trip report notes that water was unusually clear and at base flow. Turbidity was below 3 NTU at Powell and ranged from 3.1 to 9.7 NTU at Salt. Young-of-year (YOY) fishes dominated the catches. Adult chubs were reported to prey on YOY fishes. As fish grew, they moved from shallow edge habitat in June into deep, offshore habitat by August.

**Fort Apache Indian Reservation.** Stream surveys covered Big Bonito, Tonto and Deep Creeks and Elk Canyon Creek. The surveys examined riffle habitat, which loach minnow require, and collected brood-stock milt for [[Apache Trout]] breeding.

**Bylas Springs.** Sonoran topminnow were monitored monthly from November 1992 through August 1993 at three springs, with the highest relative abundance recorded at the East Spring Head site.

## Key Findings

**Stocking and collection totals (AzFRO Parker, razorback sucker and bonytail chub):**
- Bonytail chub: 20,900 stocked at Office Cove (21 October), 19,000 at High Levee Pond (21 October) and 100 at Pittsburgh Point (21 October). Fifteen were collected from Lake Mohave on 10-13 May. Ten of these died, one was released, and four were held at the hatchery.
- Razorback sucker: 843 stocked at Office Cove and 50 at 12 Mile Lake (both 17 February). A total of 750 were collected from Lake Mohave in March, of which 111 were recaptures. Four died during this effort, three of them attributed to collecting. On 18 October, 4,000 were stocked at Pittsburgh Point Cove and 18,000 at High Levee Pond. Six mortalities were reported from that stocking.
- Razorback sucker: 1,289 fish (16.5 inches) were stocked into the Salt River below the Fort Apache Indian Reservation.

**Mortalities:** Incidental mortalities are reported separately. Most came from collecting or handling, or from canal drawdown.
- Razorback sucker: one mortality from collecting at river mile 42.3 on 23 July, and five canal-related deaths in January-February.
- Humpback chub: 9,893 individuals handled, with 33 incidental mortalities. All were YOY fish under 74 mm total length. The size-class breakdown in the report is partly garbled but reads as 4, 16 and 13 fish.

**Humpback chub during the August trip:** More than 7,000 YOY fish were captured at the Salt study area. Of 18 incidental mortalities during the trip, 17 were found dead in nets during normal sets, and one fish flipped into grass and died. The report notes that this single event was unrelated to mishandling. Thin, emaciated YOY chubs were also observed and may be related to diet changes as fish shifted habitat. The cumulative YOY chub mortality (33) now exceeds the incidental mortality limit set for the year.

**Other AzFRO records:**
- Flannelmouth sucker: 171 handled, with no mortalities.
- Little Colorado River spinedace: one fish collected from lower Chevelon Creek and frozen for genetic analysis at Arizona State University. Five fish from the Flagstaff Arboretum pond were returned alive.
- Apache trout: surveys of Deep Creek and Elk Canyon Creek aimed to collect milt from up to 20 males per stream. Five fish per stream were sacrificed for kidney and spleen disease analysis.
- Loach minnow: no individuals were found in Big Bonito or Tonto creeks during July through November surveys.
- Colorado squawfish: no activity was recorded.

**Loach minnow surveys (Appendix C):** Five sites were surveyed on Tonto Creek and two on Big Bonito Creek. The report states that results are preliminary. Tonto Creek yielded 1,569 fishes in 30 hauls, with [[Speckled Dace]] comprising 79.3% of the catch. Big Bonito Creek was much less productive, with 38 specimens of five species and none found at location 2. The absence of loach minnow is attributed to the scarcity and embeddedness of [[Riffle Habitat]]. The surveys also recorded [[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brown Trout]]. Brown trout were introduced after Hurricane Lake was built in 1959. No Apache trout were found.

**Sonoran topminnow (Appendix D):** Monthly monitoring at Bylas Springs found no mortalities. Relative abundance at the East Spring Head site ranged from 32% to 66% across the sampling period. Percentages at the Below Barrier site were much lower, and the Road Crossing site was often absent.

**Management implications:** The report highlights the need to (1) manage incidental mortality limits for YOY humpback chub, (2) continue survey work to delineate loach minnow distribution, and (3) monitor how non-native trout affect native fish communities.
