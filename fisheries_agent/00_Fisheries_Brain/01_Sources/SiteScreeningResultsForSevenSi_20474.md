# Site Screening Results for Seven Sites Along the Colorado and Gunnison Rivers, Colorado

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This November 1994 report from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Grand Junction Projects Office presents Level I contaminant screening results for seven of 30 inventory sites in the [[Flooded Bottomland Habitat Restoration Program]] along the [[Colorado River]] and [[Gunnison River]]. Another group of 10 sites was planned for screening by mid-December 1994. The screenings focus on [[Selenium]] and salinity (measured as [[Electrical Conductivity]]) in surface water, seeps, and ground water. Biological tissue data are limited to a few fish and wildlife records.

The most significant finding is a fish-tissue problem at [[Walter Walker State Wildlife Area]]. Of 39 [[Colorado Squawfish]] sampled in 1994, 15 exceeded the 8 ppm selenium guideline for reproductive failure in fish, and 12 of those came from this area. Several sites also showed high salinity, and one site (Clifton) holds uranium mill tailings. A Level II survey is recommended for Clifton.

## Background & Description

The report is a Level I screening package. Each site is documented with a Supplemental Contaminant Checklist covering desk audit items (expert consultation, geology, hydrology, mining and energy history, permits, land use, water quality, and contaminant data) and an on-site reconnaissance. Each site also has a schematic figure. The work was prepared by [[Kathy Holley]] (Environmental Specialist) and [[Ken Weston]] (Project Geologist).

Seven sites were screened:

- [[Adobe Creek]] (River Mile 159.0–160.0), on the north bank of the Colorado River. Former agricultural land, converted to a golf course before 1991.
- [[Walter Walker State Wildlife Area]] (RM 163.3–164.7), a former gravel-mining area donated to the Colorado Division of Wildlife as a waterfowl sanctuary.
- 29-5/8 Pond Complex (RM 174.0–175.2), a former gravel-pit area being reclaimed. Surveyed October 18, 1994.
- Griffith's (RM 175.0–176.5), a riparian and wetland area with no commercial or agricultural use. The survey is incomplete.
- [[Clifton Sanitation]] #1 and Clifton Pond (RM 178.0), the site of an old city dump covered with uranium mill tailings.
- Pike's (RM 179.1–179.5), former river channel now used for livestock and irrigated pasture.
- [[Johnson Boy's Slough]] (Gunnison River, RM 53.6–54.2), an ox-bow near Delta, Colorado, with riparian and marsh habitat.

Standard site-specific geology is described across the sites: river-deposited alluvium over a cobble and gravel aquifer, which sits on [[Mancos Shale]] bedrock. These permeable deposits transmit ground water from leaking canals, laterals, and irrigation percolation to the rivers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sites are shaped by irrigation return flows, floodwater, gravel-pit legacies, and agricultural drains. Key context from the checklists:

- **Salinity and selenium sources:** Irrigation return flows and drains carry dissolved minerals and selenium. Many drains discharge directly into the rivers upstream and downstream of the sites.
- **Evaporative concentration:** At Walter Walker, ponded rainwater left salt behind as it evaporated. Conductivity rose to 32,000 umhos/cm on October 6, 1994.
- **Hydrological change:** The 1983 flood breached the dike at Walter Walker, destroying three ponds and a nearly 100-acre lake. The 1975–1993 aerial and orthophoto comparisons at 29-5/8 Pond and Griffith's show substantial river channel shifts.
- **Nearby industry:** The [[Gilsonite Plant]] lies west of Fruita. The [[Cameo Coal-Fired Power Plant]] is roughly 8 to 12 miles from several sites. The [[Persigo Waste Water Treatment Plant]], built in 1984, lies across Interstate 70 from Walter Walker.
- **Agricultural nutrients:** [[U.S. Geological Survey]] staff reported that Adobe Creek carries high nitrate loads, and that golf course fertilizers would add to them.

Regional fish concerns are tied to the endangered fish recovery context. The Clifton real estate survey was prepared for the "Recovery Program for Endangered Fishes." [[Razorback Sucker]] tissue values from the Green River were cited as comparable to the squawfish results.

## Key Findings

**Adobe Creek**
- Three water samples collected October 6, 1994 (two from the secondary channel, one from the tertiary channel) each contained 4 ug/L selenium. A ground-water seep also measured 4 ug/L.
- [[U.S. Geological Survey]] data (1991–1993) ranged up to 92 ug/L selenium, with 9 ug/L in August and 76 and 80 ug/L in winter.
- Conductivity of 4600–4900 umhos/cm in a ground-water inflow reach was about four times higher than surface-water readings.
- Golf course pond discharge was about 100 gpm on September 1 and 67 gpm on September 28–29. The site has no biota data, and tissue sampling was not done.

**Walter Walker State Wildlife Area**
- North Pond surface water contained 60 ug/L selenium. A seepage area contained less than 1 ug/L.
- Of 39 squawfish sampled in 1994, 15 exceeded 8 ppm selenium in tissue. Twelve of those were from this area.
- Squawfish selenium ranged from 3.2 to 30.7 ug/g dry weight across all 39 fish. The highest value (30.7) came from RM 163.5, and the lowest (3.2) from RM 182.6. Area-specific values ranged from 4.4 to 30.7 ug/g.
- USGS reported elevated selenium in three washes draining to the area in 1991–1992: Hunter Wash (30–35 ug/L winter, 7 ug/L summer), Pritchard Wash (17–23 ug/L winter, 12 ug/L summer), and Persigo Wash (reported as "86-66" ug/L winter, 15 ug/L summer).
- North Pond conductivity was 18,840 umhos/cm on October 6, 1994. Ground-water wells nearby averaged 10,641 and 9,322 umhos/cm.

**29-5/8 Pond Complex**
- No water samples were taken because of time limits. Earlier data from Cooper reported "no heavy metals or selenium occurred in significant concentrations."
- Ponds are remnants of gravel pits. Water sources are irrigation returns, possible ground-water inflow, and floodwater.

**Griffith's**
- The screening is incomplete, and most of the area had no standing water. No abiota or biota data exist.

**Clifton Sanitation #1 and Clifton Pond**
- Surface water adjacent to the pond contained 1 ug/L selenium.
- About 10,000 cubic yards of uranium mill tailings cover the old Clifton dump. Uranium analyses were pending.
- USGS ditch data (1991–1992): one ditch at D & 32 1/2 Roads had 86–100 ug/L selenium in winter, 18 ug/L in summer, and conductivity of 5700 umhos/cm. The other ditch was at detection limits, with one sample at 3 ug/L, and conductivity of 4580–4700 umhos/cm.
- No further field work will proceed until an ICP scan of one water sample is returned. Rust Geotech was unwilling to remediate because of liability concerns.
- The Clifton Pond restoration is being pursued by the Partners in Wildlife Program.
- The real estate contaminant survey (prepared October 10, 1994) recommends a Level II survey, not Level III. It notes that a Level II survey is only recommended if the Flooded Bottomlands Habitat Restoration Work Group still wishes to pursue the site. Approving official: [[Steve McCall]], Environmental Officer.

**Pike's**
- No sampling has been done here, and no standing water was present apart from scattered rainwater puddles.
- Drains GV-30 and GV-40 flow through an abandoned meander channel that parallels the site. Drain conductivity averaged 3998 and 1273 umhos/cm, respectively.
- The area appears to have been irrigated and used for livestock.

**Johnson Boy's Slough (Gunnison River)**
- Previously reported selenium values from the Gunnison River near Escalante State Wildlife Area, per [[Rick Krueger]] (FWS):
  - Water (1988): 4.0–10.0 ug/L
  - Invertebrates: 4.8 ug/L (7 samples)
  - Plants: 1.7–3.4 ug/L
  - Large predators: 1.9–7.9 ug/L (3 samples)
  - Bottom feeders: 2.5–10.3 ug/L (4 samples)
  - Small omnivores: 4.8–8.1 ug/L (4 samples)
  - Mallards: 9.2–32.6 ug/L (4 samples)
  - Mergansers: 37.2–52.4 ug/L (3 samples)
  - Killdeer: 33.0–54.2 ug/L (3 samples)
- On-site, the slough is marshy with two large ponds. Irrigation return flows supply the water. No on-site contaminant sampling was done.

**Overall**
- Level I screening identified selenium and salinity concerns across the sites. Walter Walker has the most serious documented fish-tissue issue.
- The Clifton site requires further investigation for uranium contamination.
