# Arizona Game and Fish Department Annual Reports under USFWS Blanket Permit PRT-676811 (1992–1993)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a cover letter and two annual activity reports (December 1991–November 1992 and December 1992–November 1993) filed by the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD) as a subpermittee of the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS) Region 2 [[Blanket Permit PRT-676811]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The permit authorizes take and possession of federally listed endangered or threatened species. The reports cover monitoring, captures, stocking, hatchery work, voucher collections, wildlife rehabilitation, and nest surveys. Most entries concern fish, with additional records for raptors, pelicans, a turtle, a mammal and an antelope. Many species had no take or possession in a given year. The letter describes the annotations as preliminary and abbreviated, since detailed data were to be provided in separate special and performance reports.

## Background & Description

The permit is held by the Arizona Game and Fish Department under the USFWS Region 2 Regional Director's blanket permit system. The cover letter is dated December 13, 1993, and was signed by Director Duane L. Shroufe. The Endangered Species Coordinator, Terry B. Johnson, was the department contact.

Reports are organized by taxon, with each entry listing the year's take or possession status and, where applicable, counts, locations and dispositions. Fish records include:

- [[Colorado Pikeminnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Woundfin]], [[Yaqui Catfish]]
- [[Apache Trout]], [[Gila Trout]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Yaqui Chub]], and several pupfish and minnow taxa with no take recorded

Non-fish records include [[Desert Tortoise]], [[Bald Eagle]], [[Peregrine Falcon]], [[Golden Eagle]], [[Brown Pelican]], [[Mexican Spotted Owl]], [[Thick-Billed Parrot]], [[Yuma Clapper Rail]], [[Sonoran Pronghorn]] and [[Mount Graham Red Squirrel]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fish records centre on Arizona's desert river systems and reservoirs. Key localities include:

- **[[Verde River]]**: stocking and radio-telemetry work for Colorado Pikeminnow and Razorback Sucker, near Perkinsville and in the upper reaches
- **[[Aravaipa Creek]]**: the main monitoring site for Spikedace and Loach Minnow
- **[[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Mead]]**: Razorback Sucker capture, collection and tagging at Willow Beach
- **[[Palm Lake]] at the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]]**: Bonytail Chub salvage and monitoring
- **[[Little Colorado River]] and [[Grand Canyon]]**: Humpback Chub spawning and sampling
- **Multiple springs and washes in southern Arizona**: Gila Topminnow collection, including Monkey Spring, Sonoita Creek, Redrock Canyon and Fresno Canyon

Hatcheries and rearing facilities were central to recovery work. These included the [[Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery]], [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], [[Williams Creek National Fish Hatchery]], [[Sterling Springs State Hatchery]], [[Page Springs State Hatchery]] and [[Tonto Creek State Hatchery]].

Raptor and bird records reflect nest monitoring, banding, and the recovery of carcasses and eggs for USFWS analysis, along with rehabilitation at the Department's Arizona Men's Wildlife Center.

## Key Findings

### Fish

- **Colorado Pikeminnow (1992 and 1993):** No take or possession in 1993. In 1992, one fish was measured and released at Palm Lake. Fifteen radio-implanted fish were stocked in the upper Verde River and three were recaptured. In 1993, radio-telemetry monitoring continued in the Verde River.
- **Razorback Sucker (1993):** 1,120 fish were stocked into the upper Verde River (630 on March 17 and 490 on March 31). Sixty-six fish were handled at Lake Mohave in June. Of these, 6 were captured by electrofishing and 60 by trammel net. Twelve were blind in one eye and one was blind in both eyes. Mean length was 597.9 mm (range 528–680 mm). At Willow Beach, 118 fish were captured by gill and trammel nets and 9 by electrofishing. All were released alive.
- **Razorback Sucker (1992):** 318 larvae were taken from Lake Mohave for ASU research, and 58 juveniles were taken from April to June. Sixteen adults (506–662 mm total length, 1.6–3.5 kg) were collected in June. The Dexter hatchery supplied 894 brood stock and 100,000 fry, and the report recorded 138 mortalities. 261 fish were stocked into the Verde River.
- **Gila Topminnow:** In 1993, about 50 fish were stocked into AD Wash. Captures and preserved vouchers were recorded at more than 20 sites, with the largest captures at Redrock Canyon (350 released) and Monkey Spring (300). In 1992, 563 specimens were preserved as vouchers across 13 localities.
- **Spikedace:** In 1993, 10,685 fish were captured and released in Aravaipa Creek. In 1992, 2,881 were captured and released there, and 5 were preserved as vouchers from the Verde River.
- **Loach Minnow:** In 1993, 4,009 fish were captured and released in Aravaipa Creek. In 1992, 863 were captured and released there, with 15 fish sent to ASU for genetic analysis from Campbell Blue Creek.
- **Woundfin:** Seven fish were captured and released in the Virgin River in 1993. There was no take in 1992.
- **Apache Trout (1992):** 33,883 eggs were received at Sterling Springs. 7,240 surviving fingerlings were moved to Page Springs. Growth was 0.315 inches per month. At Tonto Creek, hatch-out was about 15% (about 14,000), and fry mortality was 62.4%. Survivors averaged 2.0 inches total length by early November, growing at 0.325 inches per month.
- **Bonytail Chub (1992):** Two adults were salvaged from Palm Lake in April, and nine natural mortalities were preserved. In October, 32 fish were released after monitoring.
- **Humpback Chub (1992):** Spawning assistance was given in the Little Colorado River. Ten adults held at the Bubbling Ponds State Hatchery died of ich infection. Sixty-eight fish, including mortalities, were preserved for otolith and diet studies. 122 fish over 29 mm were collected from the Colorado River in Grand Canyon, and 5 were preserved.
- **Sonora Chub (1992):** 200 specimens were collected from Sycamore Creek on May 22 and retained for research.
- **Yaqui Catfish, Yaqui Chub, Gila Trout and Desert Pupfish:** No take or possession was recorded in either year.

### Non-Fish Species

- **[[Desert Tortoise]] (1993):** Blood samples were taken from 10 tortoises at City Creek (Utah), 1 at Paradise Canyon (Utah) and 13 at Littlefield (Arizona). There were no mortalities, and all animals were released on site. One long-term captive tortoise illegally imported from California was held by the Department pending final disposition.
- **[[Bald Eagle]] (1992):** Two dead adults were recovered. One was a nestling blown from its nest by a storm, and the other was an adult that had been shot. Eagle nests were entered to band nestlings and to collect eggs and prey remains. 14 nestlings were banded.
- **Bald Eagle (1993):** Two bald eagles were found dead. AGFD monitored nests and banded 22 nestlings with USFWS bands and blue visual identification bands.
- **[[Peregrine Falcon]] (1991–1992):** Two birds captured in Phoenix were fitted with radio transmitters. One returned to the Phoenix area by November 1992.
- **[[Golden Eagle]] (1992):** Seven birds were handled, all released or transferred. No take was recorded in 1993.
- **[[Brown Pelican]]:** Several birds were rehabilitated and transferred to SeaWorld in San Diego in 1992 and 1993.
- **[[Mexican Spotted Owl]] (1993):** Owls were surveyed at 11 sites. Prey remains were collected from 7 nests and one egg was collected.
- **[[Thick-Billed Parrot]]:** Captive birds were released to the wild in the Chiricahua Mountains. 18 birds were released in 1991, and 5 of these were recaptured. Further releases occurred in 1993.
- **[[Sonoran Pronghorn]] (1993):** No take occurred in the United States. Animals were captured, radio-collared and sampled in northern Sonora, Mexico, under Mexican permits, with no mortalities recorded.
- **[[Mount Graham Red Squirrel]] and [[Hualapai Mexican Vole]]:** AGFD conducted midden surveys and habitat surveys. Three vole mortalities occurred in the Hualapai Mountains.
