# Guidelines for the Screening and Evaluation of Potential Conservation Areas (Lower Colorado River MSCP)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

These draft guidelines, issued by the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] Lower Colorado Region on August 10, 2006, define how Reclamation will screen and evaluate lands and backwaters for creating habitat under the [[Lower Colorado River Multi-Species Conservation Program]] (LCR MSCP). The program is a 50-year Federal and non-Federal partnership to balance water use on the lower Colorado River with conservation of at least 26 covered species, implemented through a [[Habitat Conservation Plan]]. The HCP calls for creating and managing 8,132 acres of riparian (cottonwood-willow, honey mesquite, marsh) and 360 acres of backwater habitat. Two separate six-step (riparian) and five-step (backwater) processes are described. The guidelines remain in effect until at least one riparian and one backwater site have been evaluated and proposed for securement, after which Reclamation will revise them.

## Background & Description

The LCR MSCP covers a broad set of species, most of them state- and/or federally listed. Reclamation is the implementing agency, supported by a Steering Committee of 54 entities. Habitat is created on conservation areas secured by Reclamation through long-term agreements (through April 30, 2055) or in perpetuity, and may be transferred to state or federal resource agencies such as the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[Bureau of Land Management]].

Habitat creation targets (Table 1 of the source) are:

| Habitat | Total acres | Prioritized in California |
|---|---|---|
| Cottonwood-willow | 5,940 | 1,566 |
| Honey mesquite | 7,320 | 1,048 |
| Marsh | 512 | 240 |
| Backwater | 360 | 194 |
| **Total** | **8,132** | **3,048** |

Note that the table's habitat rows sum to 14,132 acres, which differs from the stated 8,132 total; the source does not reconcile this.

The HCP identified 30 conservation opportunity areas (COAs) covering about 37,500 acres. Preference for site selection goes to Federal, state, and tribal lands, then willing private sellers, with emphasis on large tracts.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Covered fishes:** The backwater criteria focus on four endemic Colorado River fishes: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*), both federally endangered, plus [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), not listed but rare in the LCR, and [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) as listed in the source's species list.
- **Riparian covered species** named in the criteria include the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], [[MacNeill's Sootywing]], western red and western yellow bats, Yuma hispid cotton rat, California black rail, and western least bittern.
- **Key stressors:** Selenium (above 2 ppb in water is a concern), salinity, low dissolved oxygen combined with high summer temperatures, and non-native fish predators. Mosquito production near urban areas is a recognized nuisance risk.
- **Backwater classes:** The source distinguishes connected backwaters (seasonal or year-long surface connection to the LCR) from isolated backwaters. More than 400 backwaters exist along the LCR.

## Key Findings

**Riparian habitat process (Chapter 2)**

1. **Six steps:** identify sites (landowner request, Reclamation identification, stakeholder request, or Request for Proposals); reconnaissance site visits by an interdisciplinary team; initial screening; site assessment (suitability plus cost); opportunity rating (high, moderate, low); and selection for securement through the Annual Work Plan.
2. **Minimum screening requirements** include clear title to land and water, availability through 2055 or in perpetuity, no restrictive easements, and adequate size (10, 25, or 50 acres for cottonwood-willow depending on requirements; 10 contiguous acres for honey mesquite; 5 for marsh). Sites with hazardous materials or unpracticable creation conditions are rejected, though they may be reconsidered.
3. **Assessment criteria** are water availability, soil condition (texture and salinity, from NRCS soil surveys), site location (proximity to the LCR and occupied habitat, connectivity), habitat development potential (patch size and mosaic value), and long-term management (human activity, buffers, mosquito risk, adjacent land uses).
4. **Patch size targets** from the HCP for cottonwood-willow: 1,702 acres in patches of at least 50 acres, 2,348 acres in patches of at least 25 acres, and 1,890 acres in patches of at least 10 acres.
5. **Cost assessment** covers land and water, site access, infrastructure, site preparation, habitat restoration, operation and maintenance, and regulatory compliance (NEPA, CEQA, NHPA Section 106, Clean Water Act Section 404). Conceptual plans are roughly a 20-30% level of design.
6. **Final selection** is by the LCR MSCP Program Manager, who ranks sites and may weigh budgets and stakeholder interests.

**Backwater habitat process (Chapter 3 and Appendix B)**

1. **Five steps:** inventory and initial screening; summer site visits; rating; site assessment with seasonal monitoring (fall, winter, spring) and conceptual plan; and selection.
2. **Initial screening** uses a minimum size of 1 acre, connection to the LCR, year-round permanence, landowner interest, accessibility, and current use. Isolated backwaters are preferred for razorback sucker and bonytail because connected sites are vulnerable to non-native predators. Flannelmouth sucker habitat must be connected to the river.
3. **Biological suitability score** sums six criteria: water quality (dissolved oxygen, temperature, salinity, pH, selenium, chlorophyll a and cyanobacteria), cover (vegetation 10–60% of area, rip-rap, turbidity of 10–100 NTU), depth, spawning gravel (at least 5% of perimeter), larval forage base (more than 50 zooplankters per liter), and bio-indicators (fish presence).
4. **Rating thresholds:** Excellent 19–30; High 14–18.9; Moderate 9–13.9; Low 5–8.9.
5. **Size ratings:** High for 5–40 acres; Low for 1–4 acres or greater than 40 acres. The 5-acre lower bound is based on the Cibola High Levee Pond, which is about 5 acres.
6. **Flannelmouth sucker** is not rated on Step 3 criteria due to lack of early-life data. Sites are evaluated case by case. Spawning is thought to occur in April–May, so inundation is recommended for March–August. Spawning congregations were located within 40 km downstream of Davis Dam.
7. **Data gaps** are acknowledged throughout. The guidelines will be revised through adaptive management, and the rating system has not yet been field-validated.

**Document-level note:** The guidelines are a draft. The source contains references to [[Imperial National Wildlife Refuge]], Butler Lake, McAlister Lake, and Cibola High Levee Pond as reference sites for backwater design.
