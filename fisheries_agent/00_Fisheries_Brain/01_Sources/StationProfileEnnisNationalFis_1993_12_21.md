## Executive Summary

This station profile documents the 1993 operations of the **Ennis National Fish Hatchery (NFH)**, located in Ennis, Montana, and established in 1931 under the authority of the White Act (46 Stat. 371). Ennis NFH functions as the largest facility within the National Broodstock Program, specializing exclusively in [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) egg production. In FY 1993, the hatchery produced **29,515,558 eggs** from **seven distinct broodstock strains**, representing a year-over-year increase from 27.5 million (FY1992) and 24.2 million (FY1991) eggs. Beyond egg production, the facility distributed 39,532 live fish to federal, state, and private waters. The hatchery's primary ecological role is as a genetic and egg-supply hub supporting stocking programs across at least 28 U.S. states, rather than a direct wild population management site.

## Background & Description

The source document does not provide morphometric, meristic, or anatomical data on individual fish. However, key **broodstock characteristics** are noted:

- **Species:** [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus mykiss*) — sole species maintained at this station.
- **Strain Diversity:** Seven genetically distinct rainbow trout broodstock strains are maintained, indicating deliberate genetic diversification for the National Broodstock Program.
- **Life Stage Focus:** Station is optimized for adult broodstock holding and egg production, rather than grow-out to catchable size; culled broodstock (post-spawning adults) are redistributed to various waters rather than retained.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Ennis NFH occupies a specialized ecological and management niche as a **broodstock and egg-supply station** rather than a stocking hatchery for direct wild population enhancement:

- **Physical Infrastructure:** 36 raceways (8' x 80') and 3 larger raceways (10' x 100') provide the controlled aquatic environment for broodstock holding, egg incubation, and early rearing.
- **Distribution Network:** Eggs were shipped to hatcheries across 28 states (AZ, AR, CA, CO, CT, GA, ID, IL, KY, MA, MI, MN, MO, MT, NE, NV, NM, NY, NC, ND, SC, SD, TN, UT, VA, WV, WI, WY), while live fish were distributed more narrowly to Colorado, Idaho, and Montana.
- **Water Management Partners:** Culled broodstock are transferred into a wide range of jurisdictional waters, including [[Fish and Wildlife Service]] lands, [[Bureau of Reclamation]] and [[Bureau of Indian Affairs]] waters, [[U.S. Forest Service]] lands, State waters, and Military Reservation waters — illustrating the multi-agency cooperative role of the [[National Broodstock Program]].
- **Visitor/Public Use:** The station also serves an educational/public engagement niche, hosting approximately 3,000 visitors in FY 1993.

## Key Findings

- **Egg Production Trend:** Egg distribution increased consistently year-over-year: 24,163,421 (FY91) → 27,468,433 (FY92) → 29,515,558 (FY93), reflecting expanding broodstock productivity or program demand.
- **Fish Distribution Trend:** Total fish distributed fluctuated notably: 31,881 (FY91) → 55,226 (FY92) → 39,532 (FY93), with FY92 showing a distribution spike (46,753 to state waters) not seen in other years.
- **Federal vs. State Allocation:** In FY93, the largest distribution category was "Other Federal Waters" (17,408 fish), followed by "State Waters" (12,124), with smaller allocations to FWS waters (8,555) and Private waters (1,445).
- **Budget Growth:** Station operations funding grew from $325.0K (FY91) to $378.0K (FY93 actual), with projected increases to $360.0K by FY95 (though below FY93 actual, suggesting budget normalization).
- **Staffing:** Full-time equivalent (FTE) staffing peaked at 7.0 in FY92, settling to 6.8 in FY93 and projected at 6.6 for FY94–95.
- **No Cost-Recoverable Revenue:** The station reported $0 in cost-recoverable funding across all years (1991–1995), indicating it operates purely as a federally funded conservation/production facility rather than a revenue-generating operation.
- **Single-Species Specialization:** Unlike multi-species hatcheries, Ennis NFH's entire operational output (fish and eggs) is dedicated to [[Rainbow Trout]], reinforcing its specialized role within the [[National Broodstock Program]].
