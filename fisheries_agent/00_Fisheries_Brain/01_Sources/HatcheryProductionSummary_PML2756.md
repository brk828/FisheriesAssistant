# Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery Production Summary

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This hatchery production summary reports the fish released by the [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] in one period. The hatchery distributed three species: [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail]]. The releases went to sites along the [[Colorado River]], [[Lake Mohave]] and [[Lake Havasu]], and to tribal lands and state facilities. The document is a production table, so it gives totals and distribution sites but no analysis, methods or study design. The source is partly garbled by OCR, so some per-site figures could not be attributed with confidence and are omitted.

## Background & Description

The document is a single-page summary of hatchery output, with the following totals as reported:

- **Rainbow trout:** 146,799 fish, totalling 40,511 kg (89,428 lbs).
- **Razorback sucker:** 50,884 fish, totalling 3,062 kg (6,761 lbs).
- **Bonytail:** 14,075 fish, totalling 1,392 kg (3,072 lbs).

The rainbow trout total is the largest by both number and biomass. The razorback sucker and bonytail are native Colorado River fishes, and the document lists stocking for both. The document uses common names only, so the scientific names in the frontmatter are the standard identifications for these fishes, not terms taken from the source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The summary lists release locations in two groups:

- **Mainstem and reservoir sites:** Colorado River below [[Hoover Dam]], [[Davis Dam]] tailwaters, [[Lake Mohave]], and [[Lake Havasu]].
- **Tribal lands:** Colorado River Indian Tribes, Yuma Indian Reservation, Kaibab Band of Paiutes, Fort Mojave Indian Reservation, [[Hualapai Indian Reservation]], Navajo Indian Reservation, and nine New Mexico pueblos and tribal nations (including Picuris, San Juan, Tesuque, San Ildefonso, Acoma, Isleta, Sandia, Mescalero and Nambe).
- **State and federal facilities:** State of Arizona Bubbling Ponds, State of Nevada, [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]], and Beal Lake.

Stocking is spread across the lower Colorado River basin and across several tribal and state partners. Because the summary covers only hatchery output, it says nothing about survival, habitat quality or population status after release.

## Key Findings

- The [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] distributed 146,799 rainbow trout, 50,884 razorback suckers and 14,075 bonytail, a total of 211,758 fish.
- Rainbow trout accounted for the largest biomass, at 40,511 kg (89,428 lbs).
- Razorback suckers were distributed to Colorado River and Lake Mohave sites. Bonytail were distributed to Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu.
- Recipients include federal, state and at least ten tribal or pueblo partners, showing a multi-agency distribution network.
- The document contains no statistics on survival, growth or population response, and no methods, so its conclusions are limited to hatchery output and distribution.
