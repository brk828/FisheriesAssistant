# Use of Antimycin for Selective Thinning of Sunfish Populations in Ponds

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae #fisheries-management #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary
This 1969 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service investigation (Burress & Luhning) evaluated the piscicide **[[Antimycin]]** (Fintrol-5 formulation) as a selective tool for thinning overcrowded **[[Sunfish]]** populations in six soft-water, spring-fed ponds in west-central Georgia. Conducted between December 1966 and September 1967, the study tested concentrations of 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, and 1.0 parts per billion (p.p.b.) across water temperatures of 46–75°F. The treatments demonstrated that low doses of antimycin (0.4–0.6 p.p.b.) selectively remove small sunfishes (bluegill, redear sunfish) while sparing **[[Largemouth Bass]]**, particularly at lower temperatures, offering a more flexible and temperature-independent alternative to traditional **[[Rotenone]]**-based selective thinning. Higher concentrations (0.8–1.0 p.p.b.) proved excessively toxic to bass as well. Surviving fish retained normal reproductive capacity after multiple sublethal exposures.

## Background & Description
This is a population-management/toxicology study rather than a morphological one; however, several size- and condition-based observations were recorded:

- **Size-selectivity**: Smaller individuals of each species (particularly sunfish <4.0 inches) were consistently more susceptible to antimycin than larger conspecifics.
- **Species-level resistance**: [[Redear Sunfish]] were more resistant to antimycin than [[Bluegill]]; fingerling [[Largemouth Bass]] were more resistant than adult sunfishes.
- **Sublethal stress symptoms** in bluegill and bass included:
  - Blotchy skin discoloration
  - Shreds of mucus trailing from the body ("ragged" appearance)
  - Darkening of body color
  - Grayish, opaque eyes
- **Sex-differential vulnerability**: Of 39 adult bass found dead across five ponds during the pre-spawn period (March 7–28), 30 were gravid females, suggesting females are disproportionately vulnerable to antimycin just before spawning.
- Unaffected/resistant species observed: **[[Channel Catfish]]** and **[[Brown Bullhead]]** were essentially unharmed at all tested concentrations; **[[Mosquitofish]]** showed only minor mortality.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat type**: Six privately owned, soft-water, spring-fed ponds (0.69–9.80 acres; 3–6 ft average depth; overflow 3.5–106.0 g.p.m.) near Warm Springs, Georgia. Water chemistry was characteristically soft and slightly acidic (total hardness 7.0–15.0 p.p.m.; total alkalinity 7.5–14.0 p.p.m.; pH 6.55–7.32).
- **Community composition**: Each pond hosted unbalanced multispecies assemblages typical of unmanaged farm ponds — overcrowded [[Bluegill]] and/or [[Redear Sunfish]] populations alongside [[Largemouth Bass]], with some ponds also containing [[White Crappie]], [[Redbreast Sunfish]], [[Orangespotted Sunfish]], [[Golden Shiner]], [[Brown Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]], and [[Mosquitofish]].
- **Vegetation**: [[Henry Pond]] contained a dense stand of cattail (*Typha latifolia*); other ponds had only sparse rooted vegetation.
- **Trophic interaction note**: Large bass were observed actively feeding on moribund, antimycin-stressed bluegill during treatment — an opportunistic predation response documented during dosing.
- **Thermal stratification**: Antimycin efficacy varied with depth; fish held below the 5-foot level (below the thermocline) in live boxes experienced slower and sometimes reduced mortality, indicating the toxicant should be dosed relative to pond volume *above* the thermocline rather than total volume to avoid unintended overdosing.
- **Management context**: This selective-thinning approach is positioned as an alternative to established shallow-water [[Rotenone]] applications (per Swingle et al. 1953; Hooper & Crance 1960; Bennett 1960; Thomaston 1962), overcoming rotenone's limitations tied to weather, water temperature, and fish behavior/distribution.

## Key Findings
1. **Dose-response by concentration**:
   - **0.4 p.p.b.**: At 75°F, removed 2.9 lb/acre of largemouth bass <6 in and 69 lb/acre of all sunfish sizes ([[Biggers Pond]]); at cooler temps (~48°F), removed up to 8.4–13.8 lb/acre of small bluegill with negligible bass mortality ([[Todd Pond]], [[Henry Pond]]).
   - **0.6 p.p.b.**: Reduced small bluegill numbers by about half with minimal bass impact in cold water; in warmer water killed 80.6 lb/acre bluegill and only 4.4 lb/acre bass ([[Slaughter Pond]]), and 64.5 lb/acre sunfish vs. 2.3 lb/acre bass ([[Reynolds Pond]]).
   - **0.8 p.p.b.**: Excessively toxic for soft-water ponds (pH 6.75–6.98); virtually eliminated sunfish and reduced small bass by 78.7–94.7%, larger bass by 12.2–31.6%.
   - **1.0 p.p.b.**: Too severe at 65°F, leaving only 6.7% of sunfish and 26.1% of bass population ([[Hill Pond]]).
2. **Temperature effect**: All species, especially largemouth bass, showed greater resistance to antimycin at low water temperatures; treatment response was slower in cold water.
3. **Reproductive recovery**: Largemouth bass, bluegill, and redear sunfish that survived single or multiple exposures (up to 1.0 p.p.b.) reproduced normally, both in treated ponds ([[Hill Pond]], [[Slaughter Pond]], [[Reynolds Pond]]) and in hatchery broodponds at the National Fish Hatchery, Marion, Alabama.
4. **Non-target species largely unaffected**: Channel catfish and brown bullhead showed no mortality across all tested concentrations; mosquitofish showed minimal mortality.
5. **Cost-effectiveness**: Based on a $48.00/8.25-lb can of Fintrol-5, treatment costs were $0.64/acre-foot (0.4 p.p.b.), $0.97 (0.6 p.p.b.), $1.28 (0.8 p.p.b.), and $1.60 (1.0 p.p.b.) — substantially cheaper and logistically simpler than rotenone treatments.
6. **Application methodology**: No difference in efficacy was found among the three delivery methods (beaker dribble, hand seed spreader, powered spreader); open water tolerated boat passes ~30 ft apart, while shallow/weedy zones required ≤10 ft spacing. Diurnal pH fluctuations were minor, permitting treatment at any time of day.
7. **Confounding factors**: Poor results in early [[Henry Pond]] treatments were attributed to dilution/turbidity from rain-driven overflow and possible toxicant loss into soft muddy bottom after partial dewatering — indicating that lowering a pond before treatment does not guarantee improved efficacy and may even reduce it.
8. **Stratification risk**: Dosing based on total pond volume rather than volume above the thermocline risks unintended overdosing (e.g., a calculated 0.4 p.p.b. in [[Biggers Pond]] could effectively become 0.5 p.p.b. above a 5-ft thermocline — a 25% unintended increase).
9. **Management implication**: Antimycin extends the effective treatment season for selective sunfish thinning well beyond the narrow weather/temperature windows required for rotenone, allowing fishery managers greater flexibility, potentially paired with supplemental bass fingerling stocking following treatment.
