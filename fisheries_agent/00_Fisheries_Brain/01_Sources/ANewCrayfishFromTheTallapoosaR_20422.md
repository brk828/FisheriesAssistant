# Cambarus (Depressicambarus) englishi: A New Crayfish from the Tallapoosa River, Georgia

#carcinology #freshwater-biology #astacidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper describes *Cambarus (Depressicambarus) englishi*, a new freshwater crayfish (Decapoda: Astacidae) known from only two localities in the upper [[Tallapoosa River]] in [[Haralson County]], Georgia. The species lives syntopically (in the same place) with its close relative [[Cambarus (Depressicambarus) halli]], which is very similar in coloration and general appearance. The authors separate the two mainly by habitat (riffles versus shoreline litter and roots) and by morphology of the first pleopod, areola, and rostral margins. The paper is a formal species description with diagnosis, holotype and allotype measurements, type deposition at the [[Smithsonian Institution]] (National Museum of Natural History), range, variation, relationships, and life history notes.

## Background & Description

- **Authors:** Horton H. Hobbs, Jr. (Smithsonian Institution, Washington, D.C.) and Edward T. Hall, Jr. (Georgia Water Quality Control Board, Atlanta, Georgia).
- **Publication:** *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington*, Vol. 85, No. 12, pp. 151-162, 30 August 1972.
- **Genus and subgenus:** *Cambarus* (subgenus *Depressicambarus*). Close relatives named in the text include [[Cambarus (Depressicambarus) jordani]] (Coosa River system) and [[Cambarus (Depressicambarus) obstipus]] (Black Warrior drainage).
- **Diagnostic features:**
  - Areola 4.2 to 4.9 times longer than wide, comprising 33.2 to 38.0 percent of carapace length (41.3 to 46.7 percent of postorbital length), with four to six punctations across its narrowest part.
  - Rostrum usually with marginal spines or tubercles; long acumen.
  - Chela with two rows of tubercles on the mesial palm surface, the mesialmost usually bearing seven.
  - First pleopod of Form I males with a long, narrow, recurved central projection (about 110 degrees) lacking a subterminal notch, and a mesial process with a distinct gap between the bulbiform portion and the shaft.
  - Annulus ventralis of the female is distinctly asymmetrical with a deep median trough.
- **Type series:** Holotype (Form I male), allotype (female), and morphotype (Form II male), plus paratypes.
- **Etymology:** Named in honor of Teddy A. English, Jr., a friend and frequent companion on collecting trips in Georgia.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Type locality:** Tallapoosa River, riffle area immediately downstream from the City of Tallapoosa water intake, 1 mile north of Tallapoosa, a few hundred yards east of the bridge on State Route 100.
- **Physical setting:** River about 40 feet wide, and only 1 or 2 feet deep in the riffle. The bed consists of moderately large stones partly embedded in sand, supporting a luxuriant growth of *[[Podostemum ceratophyllum]]*.
- **Water conditions:** At low water the water is almost clear. After rains it becomes reddish orange from heavy clay and silt load and can exceed 4 feet in depth.
- **Co-occurring crayfishes:** Four crayfishes frequent the segment. *C. (D.) englishi* dominates the riffle. *[[Cambarus (Depressicambarus) halli]]* occupies litter and root mats of plants along the banks. Less abundant are *[[Procambarus (Pennides) spiculifer]]* and *[[Cambarus (Depressicambarus) latimanus]]*.
- **Habitat partitioning:** Riffle specialization in *C. (D.) englishi* versus bank and litter specialization in *C. (D.) halli*, despite strong morphological resemblance.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described:** *Cambarus (Depressicambarus) englishi* is established as new, based on a holotypic male (Form I), allotypic female, and morphotypic male (Form II).
2. **Restricted range:** The species is known from only two localities, both in the upper Tallapoosa River, Haralson County, Georgia.
3. **Holotype size:** Carapace length 29.1 mm, width 14.5 mm, height 11.0 mm (Table 1). Abdomen length 28.5 mm, slightly shorter than the carapace. Areola length 35.4 percent of carapace length.
4. **Allotype and morphotype size (Table 1):**
   - Allotype: carapace length 36.5 mm, width 18.5 mm, height 13.0 mm.
   - Morphotype: carapace length 34.3 mm, width 18.0 mm, height 13.1 mm.
5. **Size range:** Largest specimen is a female with carapace length 39.2 mm. The largest and smallest Form I males measure 36.4 mm and 29.1 mm.
6. **Variation:**
   - Rostral marginal tubercles range from well-developed spines (all juveniles and some large individuals) to none in some adults.
   - Areola ratio ranges 4.2 to 4.9 times, and 33.2 to 38.0 percent of carapace length (average 35.4 percent).
   - Mesial palm tubercle row ranges from five to eight, with seven usual.
   - Dorsal merus spines: two in most specimens, three in one.
7. **Type deposition:** Holotype (No. 131700), allotype (No. 131701), and morphotype (No. 132519) are deposited at the National Museum of Natural History, Smithsonian Institution, together with paratypes (reported as 83 males, 62 females, 9 juvenile males, and 10 juvenile females).
8. **Specimens examined outside the type series:** One juvenile male and two juvenile females, collected 2.5 miles west of Tallapoosa by Max W. Walker and E. T. Hall, Jr. on 3 September 1969.
9. **Collection dates for type series:** 3 September 1969, 13 October 1969, and 23 September 1971, collected by T. A. English, Jr., R. F. Holbrook, E. T. Hall, Jr., and H. H. Hobbs, Jr.
10. **Relationships:** Closest relative is the syntopic *C. (D.) halli*, which was initially thought to be conspecific. *C. (D.) englishi* differs from *halli* in the gently curved, entire central projection of the first pleopod, a narrower, longer, less densely punctate areola, and thickened, slightly concave rostral margins. It differs from *obstipus* in a longer, more strongly recurved central projection and a broader, shorter areola. It differs from *jordani* in a longer, slenderer, less strongly recurved central projection and more spiniform carapace and cheliped tubercles.
11. **Life history:** First form males were collected in September and October. Males collected on 23 September 1971 were encrusted, indicating they had not molted for several months, which suggests a breeding season extending through the summer. In contrast, adult males from September and October 1969 had recently molted to Form I. Only one Form II male (the morphotype) has been obtained.
