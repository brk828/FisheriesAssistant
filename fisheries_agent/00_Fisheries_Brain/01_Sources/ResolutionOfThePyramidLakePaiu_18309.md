# Cui-ui (Chasmistes cujus) — Pyramid Lake Population Status Resolution

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 tribal resolution and policy statement issued by the [[Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe]] opposing the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's proposal to downlist the [[Cui-ui]] (*Chasmistes cujus*) from **Endangered** to **Threatened** status. The Cui-ui is an endemic, single-population fish species found only in [[Pyramid Lake]], Nevada, and holds deep cultural significance to the Tribe (whose name, *Kuyuidokado*, translates to "cui-ui eaters"). While the paper contains no morphological data, it provides substantial population ecology, recruitment, and hydrological data underpinning the species' conservation classification debate, referencing the 1992 [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]] developed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

## Background & Description
The source text does not provide morphological descriptions of the Cui-ui. ## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Endemic to a single terminal lake ecosystem — [[Pyramid Lake]] — a closed-basin desert lake fed by the [[Truckee River]].
- **Spawning behavior**: Migrates from the lake to spawn in the lower [[Truckee River]] and over the river delta; spawning access is highly dependent on lake level and delta configuration.
- **Population range**: Restricted entirely to Pyramid Lake — described as having "no room for error" due to lack of distinct sub-populations or refuge ecosystems, unlike wider-ranging threatened species.
- **Predation pressure**: Significant predation on juveniles by pelicans at the confined spawning access points, resulting in estimated losses of "several hundred thousand cui-ui a year."
- **Lake productivity link**: Lake productivity (affecting juvenile recruitment) was documented to decline substantially following a 6-year drought, independent of adult population size.
- **Hydrological dependency**: Species survival is tightly coupled to Truckee River inflow volume, lake level, and delta morphology, all of which govern spawning ground accessibility.

## Key Findings
- **Population trend context**: Cui-ui numbers increased significantly in the early 1980s relative to historic lows since 1967, but the causes of that increase remain scientifically uncertain.
- **Three hypothesized (unconfirmed) causes for the 1980s population increase**:
  1. An unusually wet hydrologic cycle raised Pyramid Lake's level by 30 feet from 1980–1986, improving spawning access.
  2. A transient, favorable delta configuration may have temporarily enabled reproductive success.
  3. An ecological "void" from historically low adult numbers may have been filled by strong recruiting year-classes, a one-time effect unlikely to recur.
- **Recruitment collapse**: No known spawning occurred from 1988–1992; spawning events in 1986 and 1987 produced minimal recruitment to the adult population.
- **Lake level decline**: Pyramid Lake fell 24 feet from its 1986 peak by the time of this 1994 statement, threatening the persistence of spawning access achieved during high-flow years.
- **1992 Recovery Plan downlisting criteria** (none satisfied at time of writing):
  1. Species must have ≥0.85 probability of persisting for 200 years.
  2. Securing an additional 45,000 acre-feet/year of Truckee River inflow to Pyramid Lake (minimum incremental rate of 5,000 acre-feet/year), or equivalent benefit.
  3. Stable or increasing estimated adult and juvenile year-class numbers over the previous 15 years.
- **Tribal position**: The Tribe argued that classification status should be based on the species' vulnerability to extinction under the [[Endangered Species Act]] framework rather than short-term population upticks potentially driven by anomalous ("freak") hydrologic events.
- **Policy recommendation**: The Tribe called for the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and Cui-ui Recovery Team to prioritize securing additional water rights/inflow (the plan's core long-term solution) rather than revising classification status, warning that downlisting could create a "false sense of security" undermining Recovery Plan implementation.
- **Review mechanism**: The Recovery Plan specifies a scheduled review by the Recovery Team every three years; the Tribe insisted downlisting should only proceed once empirical, science-based causes for the population increase are documented and shown likely to recur.
