# Collection and Transportation of Razorback Suckers (Xyrauchen texanus)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This field report documents a 1981 collection operation for [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) broodstock from [[Lake Mohave]], conducted jointly by state/federal fishery services and academic personnel (Dr. [[W.L. Minckley]], Arizona State University). Fish were captured via gill nets, electroshocking, and trammel nets from shallow spawning coves, treated prophylactically with antibiotics, and successfully transported live to [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico for conservation propagation purposes. The operation is notable for its low mortality/stress outcome despite the invasive capture techniques used. A secondary, heavily degraded OCR table (Table 2) summarizes trammel net catch composition at Lake Mohave across multiple sampling dates from 1975–1980, indicating the presence of [[Razorback Sucker]] alongside [[Common Carp]], [[Green Sunfish]], and [[Bluegill]].

## Background & Description
- **Sexual size dimorphism observed in broodstock:**
  - Males: ~5 lbs (2.3 kg) average body weight
  - Females: ~8 lbs (3.6 kg) average body weight
- **Total broodstock collected:** 134 individuals (76 females, 58 males)
- **Total biomass transported:** ~900 lbs (~408 kg)
- **Condition notes:** Some individuals exhibited hemorrhaging attributable to electro-shocking capture method, and minor fungal infections related to handling stress — otherwise fish were in "excellent condition."
- **Behavioral state during transport:** Docile and inactive, consistent with cold-water torpor/reduced metabolic stress response.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Spawning habitat:** Shallow coves in 2–3 ft of water at [[Lake Mohave]]; spawning substrate described as smooth gravel ranging from ~0.25" to ~1.5" in diameter, observed "whipped clean" by spawning activity — indicative of typical [[Razorback Sucker]] lithophilic (gravel-broadcast) spawning behavior.
- **Thermal tolerance:** Fish were collected/held at ambient lake water temperatures of 49–52°F, transported successfully, and tempered from 51°F to 64°F upon arrival at the hatchery without adverse effects — suggesting considerable eurythermal tolerance for acclimation shifts in this species.
- **Water chemistry during transport:**
  - Dissolved oxygen maintained at 10–13 ppm using aerators and supplemental bottled O₂ (8 liters), no chemical additives required
  - Fish transported in unmodified/pure lake water
- **Co-occurring taxa (from Table 2, Lake Mohave trammel net surveys 1975–1980):** [[Common Carp]], [[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], and unspecified additional species — suggesting a mixed native-introduced fish community sharing littoral habitat with [[Razorback Sucker]] in this reservoir system.

## Key Findings
- Multi-technique capture approach (gill nets, electroshocking, trammel nets) proved effective for targeting reproductively active adult [[Razorback Sucker]] in spawning-condition aggregations.
- Prophylactic antibiotic treatment protocol: fish were treated with **furacin** at base camp post-capture, then injected with **terramycin at 10 mg/lb** prior to transport as a bacterial infection countermeasure.
- Transport method: individuals were bagged in **wet burlap sacks** to reduce mechanical/handling trauma during the ~900-mile truck transport from Lake Mohave to Dexter, New Mexico.
- Transport survivorship was high, with the report explicitly noting fish "arrived at Dexter NFH in better shape than we had hoped," underscoring the practical viability of long-distance live transport protocols for this endangered [[Catostomidae]] species.
- This operation reflects broader conservation aquaculture efforts for the [[Razorback Sucker]], a species already in decline in the wild by 1981 due to habitat alteration in the [[Colorado River Basin]] and competition/predation from introduced species (e.g., [[Common Carp]], centrarchids).
- The accompanying (partially illegible) Table 2 data suggest long-term monitoring of species composition via standardized trammel-net CPUE (catch per 100 m of net) at Lake Mohave spanning 1975–1980, useful for tracking relative [[Razorback Sucker]] abundance trends against introduced competitor/predator species.

> **Note:** Table 2 in the original source is significantly corrupted by OCR errors (garbled numeric values and column headers). Only qualitative taxon identifications (Carp, Green Sunfish, Bluegill, and implied Razorback Sucker as target species) could be reliably extracted; precise catch percentages and net-fishing-effort statistics per date are not confidently recoverable from this scan.
