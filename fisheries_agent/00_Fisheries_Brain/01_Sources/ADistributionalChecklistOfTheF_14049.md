# A Distributional Checklist of the Fishes of Kentucky

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fish-checklist

## Executive Summary

This 1980 checklist compiles records of lampreys and fishes from Kentucky waters, drawn from museum specimens, personal collecting, state agency surveys and accepted literature. It documents **229 species** that occur or formerly occurred in the state, of which **10 or 11** were introduced by people. The author built an individual spot distribution map for every species, and discarded unsubstantiated records. The annotated list adds new distributional data, including several species not previously reported from Kentucky, and closes with a **problematical list of five hypothetical species**. The checklist is presented as an interim step toward a fuller [[Fishes of Kentucky]] reference.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** 229 species in 69 genera and 27 families. Kentucky's fauna is described as more diverse than that of any other inland area of comparable size in North America except Tennessee and Alabama.
- **Historical studies:** [[Constantine Samuel Rafinesque]] (1820) began the work with Ohio River valley fishes. Later reports came from Woolman (1892), Garman (1894), Evermann (1918) and Clay (1975). Woolman's work documents distributions before much human alteration. Garman's and Evermann's reports are mostly compilations. Clay (1975) omitted nearly 30 species now known from the state and contained outdated distributions.
- **Data sources:** Specimens from 22 institutional collections, including the [[Southern Illinois University Carbondale]], [[Illinois Natural History Survey]], [[Kentucky Fish and Wildlife Resources Agency]] and [[Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History]]. About 1,150 sampling stations, mostly sampled since 1950, were plotted to reveal gaps.
- **Organization:** Families follow [[Greenwood et al. 1966]] in part, and names follow [[Bailey et al. 1970]]. Kentucky is divided into **11 subunits** matching river systems or faunal blocks. Abundance terms ("generally distributed," "occasional," "sporadic") follow Smith (1965).
- **Nomenclatural notes:** *Fundulus notti* probably becomes *F. dispar*; *Menidia audens* probably becomes *M. beryllina*; the population known as *Percina uranidea* is now *P. ouachitae*. The author kept *Notropis chrysocephalus* despite hybridization with *N. cornutus*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat associations described in the list:**

- **Large rivers and main channels** of the Ohio and Mississippi rivers support many species, such as the [[Paddlefish]], [[Shovelnose Sturgeon]], [[Longnose Gar]], [[Gizzard Shad]], [[Freshwater Drum]], and [[Flathead Catfish]].
- **Small, clear, upland streams** of the eastern two-thirds of the state host species such as the [[Least Brook Lamprey]], [[Southern Redbelly Dace]] and many darters.
- **Coastal Plain swamps, oxbows and sloughs** support species such as the [[Bowfin]], [[Banded Pygmy Sunfish]], [[Mosquitofish]] and several topminnows.
- **Caves and springs** hold cavefishes, including the [[Northern Cavefish]], [[Southern Cavefish]] and [[Spring Cavefish]].

**Anthropogenic pressures:**

- **Impoundment** of the Cumberland, Tennessee and Green rivers changed fish assemblages. Several species declined or disappeared after impoundment.
- **Strip mine pollution** is cited as the threat to [[Blackside Dace]], a species endemic to the upper Cumberland River.
- **Introductions** include [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brown Trout]], [[Grass Carp]], [[Striped Bass]] and [[Northern Pike]], many stocked into reservoirs.
- **Power plant impingement:** Hoyt (1979) recorded 20 individuals of [[Alligator Gar]] during a two-year study at the Shawnee Steam Plant, Paducah.

## Key Findings

**1. Species inventory**
- Total: 229 species (lampreys and fishes), 69 genera, 27 families.
- Introduced: 10 or 11 species.
- Twelve species were not recently encountered and their status is discussed: *Alosa alabamae*, *Clinostomus elongatus*, *Hemitremia flammea*, *Hybopsis x-punctata*, *Notropis amnis*, *Lagochila lacera*, *Lota lota*, *Ammocrypta asprella*, *A. clara*, *A. vivax*, *Etheostoma microperca* and *Percina burtoni*.

**2. Extirpated or likely extirpated species**
- *Lagochila lacera* (harelip sucker): listed as extinct; formerly known from five localities.
- *Ammocrypta asprella* (crystal darter): not collected in Kentucky since 1929; considered extirpated.
- *Ammocrypta clara* (western sand darter): not collected since 1925; probably extirpated.
- *Hemitremia flammea* (flame chub): no reports since Jordan's work; probably extirpated.
- *Percina burtoni* (blotchside logperch): probably extirpated.

**3. Rare and restricted species**
- *Phoxinus cumberlandensis* (blackside dace): endemic to the upper Cumberland River, uncommon, and considered endangered.
- *Moxostoma atripinne* (blackfin sucker): endemic to the Barren River system, known from nine localities.
- *Noturus hildebrandi*, *N. phaeus* and *Etheostoma swaini*: each known only from western Kentucky tributaries such as Terrapin Creek and Obion River.
- *Erimyzon sucetta* (lake chubsucker): one of the rarest, known from five localities.
- *Notropis amnis* (pallid shiner): very rare and probably nearing extinction in Kentucky.

**4. Notable new or unusual records**
- *Osmerus mordax* (rainbow smelt): captured from the Mississippi River, Carlisle County, and now known along the Mississippi from the Missouri River mouth to Louisiana. All collections were in December and January of 1978–1979.
- Species with few records include *Lepisosteus spatula*, *Hybopsis gelida*, *Hybopsis meeki* and *Notropis hudsonius*, each from a single locality near the Ohio–Mississippi confluence.
- Several species are recorded from only one or a few records, and the author states that these were checked against specimens.

**5. Sampling gaps**
- The Licking River is poorly sampled and most records there are of game fishes.
- The upper Cumberland and Big Sandy rivers also need further collecting.

**6. Problematical list (5 species)**
- *Campostoma oligolepis* (largescale stoneroller): probably occurs but is not yet confirmed, so Kentucky stonerollers are kept as *C. anomalum*.
- *Notropis coccogenis* (warpaint shiner): based on a 1892 Big Sandy record that is probably an error, plus a Cumberland Gap specimen.
- *Notropis heterolepis* (blacknose shiner): the record is probably based on *N. boops*; deleted from the state list until verified.
- *Notropis hubbsi* (bluehead shiner): known from southern Illinois; should be sought in western Kentucky lowland lakes.
- *Rhinichthys cataractae* (longnose dace): possible in the upper Cumberland and Big Sandy drainages.

**7. Taxonomic issues**
- The lamprey *Ichthyomyzon bdellium* vs. *I. castaneus* identity is unresolved. Specimens from the middle and upper Ohio are assigned to *bdellium* on geographic grounds.
- The [[Silver Redhorse]] and other taxa are retained under older names pending revision.
