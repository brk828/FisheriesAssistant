# Los Peces del Valle de México: Fish Fauna of the Valley of Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fish #cyprinidae #goodeidae #atherinidae

## Executive Summary

This monograph, published by the Secretaría de Marina (Dirección General de Pesca e Industrias Conexas) and the Comisión para el Fomento de la Piscicultura Rural (1957) and authored by J. Álvarez del Villar and Leopoldo Navarro G., provides the first comprehensive treatment of all fish species of the [[Valley of Mexico]] closed basin. It combines a historical review of ichthyological study, a dichotomous identification key, detailed morphometric and meristic descriptions with variation tables, nomenclatural and type-specimen revisions, and a biogeographic hypothesis on the origin of the fauna. The authors recognize 15 species in five families, of which three families are native, one is introduced and one is naturalized. The work also documents the decline of the lakes and of the native fauna caused by drainage, groundwater extraction and urban growth.

## Background & Description

The Valley of Mexico is a closed, roughly elliptical basin of about 7,868 km² (including the 379 km² secondary Apam basin) on the Mesa Central, about 125 km north to south and 90 km east to west. It was once occupied by a large lake whose extent fluctuated with climate; fossil beaches, diatomite deposits more than 50 m above the present floor and caliche layers attest to this. Lakes still covered most of the area in the late nineteenth century, but the drainage tunnel completed in 1900 and the pumping of wells for Mexico City markedly reduced lacustrine areas.

The monograph's stated aims are to present a complete monograph of the regional fauna, resolve outstanding systematic problems, and serve as a field guide for non-taxonomic work. Acknowledgements are made to [[Lawrence P. Woods]] of the Chicago Natural History Museum, who supplied type and specimen data, and to the collections of the [[Instituto de Biología UNAM]].

The historical review traces ichthyological knowledge from pre-Columbian Aztec names recorded by [[Francisco Hernández]] (1651) through European describers such as [[Cuvier and Valenciennes]] (1835), American workers including [[David Starr Jordan]], [[Carl H. Eigenmann]] and [[Samuel E. Meek]], and Mexican naturalists such as [[Miguel Bustamante]] (1837), [[Esteban Chazari]] (1884) and [[Alfonso Herrera]]. Important later Mexican contributions include work by [[Francisco De Buen]] at the Estación Limnológica de Pátzcuaro and by J. Álvarez (1950 key).

The text includes a sampling map of 26 collecting localities (ponds, canals, lakes such as Texcoco, Xochimilco and Zumpango, and reservoirs such as Guadalupe and La Concepción) and a dichotomous key to all species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The authors describe a native fauna in decline: some species are very scarce and others considered threatened with extinction, owing to lake desiccation, groundwater drawdown and natural ageing of the basin. Introduced species, notably [[Common Carp]] and [[Goldfish]], were widely spread through rural food use and aquaculture, while [[Rainbow Trout]] and [[Brook Trout]] were introduced in the late nineteenth century and restricted to streams on the slopes of the Sierra Nevada. [[Largemouth Bass]] and [[Bluegill]] are North American centrarchids recently introduced and naturalized.

Biogeographically, the Cyprinidae are considered of Nearctic invasion origin, with the genus [[Notropis]] indicating a north-to-south expansion. The genera Chirostoma and the family Goodeidae are regarded as endemic elements of the Lerma-Santiago basin and the Valley, possibly derived from marine ancestors (Atherinidae) and cyprinodontids (Goodeidae) isolated since the early Cenozoic. The authors propose that the Valley of Mexico acted as a centre of dispersal for Chirostoma and Goodeidae, and suggest that an ancient lake (Lago de Anáhuac) connected to the Llanos del Salado and later drained toward the Pacific via the Lerma basin. Relationships with the Río Balsas (Balsadichthys whitei, Thyrina) and the Río Pánuco are discussed as only partly direct.

## Key Findings

- **Species inventory:** 15 species in five families are recognized for the Valley of Mexico: Atherinidae (3 species), Cyprinidae (7), Goodeidae (1), Centrarchidae (2) and Salmonidae (2).
- **Native cyprinids:** [[Algansea tincella]], [[Notropis aztecus]], and three endemic [[Evarra]] species (E. eigenmanni, E. tlahuacensis, E. bustamantei) occur in the basin; Evarra is restricted to the Valley.
- **Nomenclatural decisions:** The genus Aztecula is synonymized under Notropis, because its diagnostic characters (scale counts, body robustness, pharyngeal teeth) overlap with Notropis.
- **Evarra tlahuacensis:** Known only from the single holotype (Meek, 1901 collection; Chicago museum examined; 14 anal rays confirmed), probably rare or extinct, as no specimens were recaptured despite extensive sampling.
- **Evarra bustamantei:** Described by Navarro (1955) from 20 specimens from San Gregorio Atlapulco, holotype a female of 46.8 mm standard length; separated from E. eigenmanni by rounded fin tips, subterminal mouth, and scale counts (76–90 vs 74–88).
- **Goodeidae:** Only [[Girardinichthys viviparus]] is validated for the Valley. The authors conclude that Neoophorus diazi, Skiffia lermae and Zoogoneticus miniatus were erroneously attributed to Lake Chalco because Meek's itinerary shows the locality was probably a different "Chalco" near La Palma, Michoacán.
- **Bustamante's Cyprinus viviparus (1837):** Shown to be the same species as Bleeker's Girardinichthys innominatus (1860); the valid name adopted is Girardinichthys viviparus.
- **Atherinidae:** Three species occur now: [[Chirostoma humboldtianum]], [[Chirostoma jordani]] and [[Chirostoma regani]]. [[Chirostoma estor]] is considered not part of the Valley fauna; Meek's specimens labelled from Lake Xochimilco were probably bought in the Mexico City market and came from Pátzcuaro.
- **Atherina fontinalis Chazari (1884):** Recognized as a Chirostoma but its identity is uncertain; provisionally placed in the synonymy of C. regani and C. humboldtianum.
- **Introduced species:** Cyprinus carpio and Carassius auratus are widely consumed and cultivated; the authors give no variation data for introduced taxa.
- **Overall fauna character:** Cyprinidae dominate, indicating a predominantly Nearctic (neártica) fauna, with Goodeidae and Chirostoma as endemic characteristic elements.
