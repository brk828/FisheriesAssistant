# Fish Handling Protocol for Humpback Chub Population Monitoring

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a field fish-handling protocol, associated with the Arizona [[Navajo Natural Heritage Program]] (NNHP), for capturing, processing, and preserving fish during a [[Humpback Chub]] population study. It sets out the capture methods ([[Hoop Net]], [[Trammel Net]], and [[Angling]]), the measurement and release procedures for target and incidental native fish, the removal of [[Non-Native Fish]], and the handling of mortalities. The text is partly garbled by OCR, so some numbers and phrases below are uncertain and are flagged where relevant.

## Background & Description

**Objective.** The protocol aims to optimize data acquisition for the humpback chub population study. It is also meant to cover incidental capture of other native and non-native fish, and to address the effects of sampling on the target population. The document describes itself as a protocol that was reviewed and accepted.

**Species focus.** The target species is the humpback chub (*Gila cypha*, family [[Cyprinidae]]). Other native fishes are captured incidentally. [[Predatory Non-Native Fish]] such as trout, channel catfish, and striped bass are also sampled, mainly to examine their digestive tract contents.

**Personnel.** Gear is to be deployed by personnel trained and experienced in the use of such devices.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The protocol addresses the risk that capture and handling could harm native fish. It contains several safeguards:

- Fish are kept moist during handling to avoid damage to the integrity of the skin and other tissues.
- Fish are released near the point of capture, and fish showing damage or stress are to be treated according to the mortality procedures.
- Non-native predators are removed from the capture gear, and predatory non-native fish are not returned to the water alive.
- Gear checking intervals are set to limit time-in-gear and stress.

The document references an interest in habitat and in "thermal" impacts on the population, though the OCR text is too corrupted to confirm the details of those impacts.

## Key Findings

**1. Hoop nets (Section 1)**
- Set by trained personnel and deployed at locations chosen for the study.
- Checked at regular intervals. The stated interval is garbled in the scan, but appears to run from about 2 to 7 hours depending on location.
- Humpback chub and other native fish are measured for total length (TL) and weighed. Fish in the 100 to 147 mm TL range are noted in the text, though OCR makes the exact size bands uncertain.
- Released near the capture point after processing.
- Non-native fish are removed from the nets and processed separately as described in Section 1.8. Non-native predators are not released alive.
- Any unusual or non-target fish are recorded and enumerated as practicable.

**2. Trammel nets (Section 2)**
- Deployed by trained personnel, with a stated target of humpback chub.
- Trammel nets are to be cleared at intervals of **not more than 6 hours**. Extended soak times are considered an extreme concern.
- Captured native fish are processed as in Section 1.6 and non-native fish as in Section 1.8.

**3. Angling (Section 3)**
- Purpose: to collect predatory non-native fish (e.g., trout, channel catfish, striped bass) for digestive tract examination, and to capture humpback chub from habitats where other methods are impractical.
- Humpback chub are caught on single hook artificial lures, which reduce the risk of injury from swallowed baited hooks. Captured fish are carefully removed and processed as in Section 1.6.
- Baited hooks, large enough that adult humpback chub cannot take them, are used for predatory non-native fish.

**4. Mortalities (Section 4)**
- **Humpback chub:** Specimens of 150 mm TL are preserved in ethanol. Other specimens are skeletonized and dried. Each specimen is labeled. Digestive tracts and gonads from the sampled fish are preserved in formalin or ethanol.
- **Other native fish:** Mortalities are recorded, then preserved or destroyed as appropriate under the conditions in the field.
- **Non-native fish:** Mortalities are recorded, then preserved or destroyed as appropriate.

**5. Overall procedural logic**
- Native fish are measured, weighed, and released.
- Non-native predators are retained and handled by lethal or preservation methods.
- Each method is subject to checking intervals and to the requirement that all handling minimizes injury.
