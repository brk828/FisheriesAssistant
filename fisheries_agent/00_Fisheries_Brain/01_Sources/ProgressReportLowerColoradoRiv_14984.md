## Executive Summary

This progress report documents ongoing fisheries investigations on the Lower Colorado River, focused on determining optimal water circulation regimes for backwater "side lakes" to maximize [[Fisheries Production]] and angler harvest. Four primary study lakes—[[Martinez Lake]], [[Mittry Lake]], and two unnamed impoundments near [[Imperial Dam]] (No Name #1 and No Name #2)—were monitored for limnological conditions, reproductive success, and population composition between 1960-1961, with historical comparison data from 1951-1952. Key findings indicate that water chemistry has remained largely stable since 1951, but [[Largemouth Bass]] growth rates and condition factors have declined significantly, correlating with reduced average catch size and catch-per-hour despite increased fishing pressure. The report recommends continued multi-year monitoring and revised bag limits to address these emerging productivity concerns.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat & Water Circulation:**
Investigations centered on the relationship between water circulation, siltation, and water quality in backwater lakes connected to the Lower [[Colorado River]] system. Lakes studied varied in circulation type, which in turn affected total dissolved solids and nutrient regimes.

**Water Chemistry Summary:**
| Lake | Total Solids (ppm) | Chlorides (ppm) | Sulfates (ppm) | Total Alkalinity (ppm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Mittry Lake]] | ~2,500 | ~600 | 550–106 | ~350 |
| [[Martinez Lake]] | ~750 | 120 | ~218 | 125 |
| No Name #1 & #2 | ~700 | - | - | 132 |

- **Temperature:** 85-90°F in summer; 50-55°F in winter (minimum).
- **Dissolved Oxygen:** Adequate across all lakes, ranging up to 6.0 ppm.
- **pH:** Fairly stable, around 8.0-8.5.
- Sluicing operations at [[Imperial Dam]] (1947/48–1958, intermittently through 1960) to remove silt occurred continuously through spawning seasons but showed **no apparent detrimental or beneficial effect** on fisheries.
- Plant encroachment along shorelines was noted as an impediment to angler access rather than a direct ecological threat to fish habitat use.

**Lake-Specific Spawning Success:**
- **Mittry Lake:** Poor bass spawn; no juvenile channel catfish or black crappie recovered; shad, bluegill, redear abundant.
- **Martinez Lake:** Strong bass and channel catfish spawn; minimal crappie fry detected; shad/bluegill/redear abundant.
- **Imperial Dam Area (No Name #1/#2):** Good catfish and bass spawn; crappie spawn insufficient to confirm success; shad/bluegill/redear abundant.

## Key Findings

- **Declining Bass Growth & Condition:** Largemouth bass in the [[Lower Colorado River]] system show a documented decline in growth rate and condition factor compared to 1951-52 baselines, with an average growth deficit of 1.2 inches by age 4.

- **Creel Census Trends (Imperial National Wildlife Refuge):**
  | Metric | Feb-June 1951 | April-June 1961 |
  |---|---|---|
  | Total Anglers | 344 | 240 |
  | Total Man Hours | 1,375 | 1,176.5 |
  | No. Fish Caught | 526 | 347 |
  | Fish/Hour | 0.381 | 0.295 |
  | Bass/Hour | 0.304 | 0.135 |
  | Crappie/Hour | 0.11 | 0.042 |
  | Channel Catfish/Hour | 0.032 | 0.066 |
  | Bluegill/Hour | 0.036 | 0.086 |

- **Average bass length** declined from 14.0 inches (1951-52) to 12.4 inches (1961), a 1.6-inch reduction, attributed partly to increased novice angling pressure but primarily linked to the documented slower growth rate.

- **Fishing Pressure Increase:** Anglers per surface acre of open water in the refuge rose from 1.8 (1954-55) to 6.7 (1960), yet usage remains "only a fraction" of the system's potential capacity.

- **Comparative Lake Quality:** Among Imperial Refuge lakes, [[Martinez Lake]] was assessed as having the most favorable limnological/biological conditions, followed by No Name #1 and No Name #2.
