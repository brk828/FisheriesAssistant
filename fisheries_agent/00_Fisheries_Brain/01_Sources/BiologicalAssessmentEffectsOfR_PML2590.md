# Biological Assessment: Rotenone Re-Treatment and Native Fish Management in Bonita Creek, Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #cobitidae #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This [[Biological Assessment]] was submitted by the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] and the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (FWS). It describes a proposed second chemical renovation of a reach of [[Bonita Creek]] using the piscicide [[Rotenone]], followed by salvage, holding, repatriation, translocation, and augmentation of native fishes. The first renovation (2007–2008) was intended to remove nonnative fishes so that federally listed species could be restored. Since then, [[Mosquitofish]] and [[Green Sunfish]] have re-established populations in the treated reach. The authors conclude that the project, if successful, would significantly improve the conservation status of five federally listed fishes, but that expected mortality "may affect, and is likely to adversely affect" each of them.

## Background & Description

**Project setting.** The treated reach lies between the [[Infiltration Gallery]] dike of the City of Safford and a constructed [[Fish Barrier]]. The barrier was built to prevent upstream invasions of nonnative aquatic organisms, but it also incidentally blocks upstream movement of native fishes.

**Species of concern.** The document addresses five federally listed fishes:
- [[Gila Chub]] (*Gila intermedia*), endangered
- [[Loach Minnow]] (*Tiaroga cobitis*), translocated
- [[Spikedace]] (*Meda fulgida*), translocated
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), translocated
- [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*), translocated

Two nonnative species, [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) and [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*), are the offending species that re-established after the first treatment. The document also mentions the [[Bald Eagle]] and the [[Southwestern Willow Flycatcher]], which are not fish.

**Prior consultation.** The original 2007 BA (submitted February 2, 2007) concluded:
- No effect to southwestern willow flycatcher.
- May affect, not likely to adversely affect, bald eagle.
- May affect, likely to adversely affect, Gila chub, desert pupfish, loach minnow, spikedace, and Gila topminnow.

The FWS [[Biological Opinion]] (AESO/SE 22410-2007-F-0233, dated June 28, 2007) found the project not likely to jeopardize the five fishes. Neither the BA nor the BO considered impacts of a second renovation on newly translocated fishes.

**Terminology used in the document:**
- [[Translocation]]: moving an organism within its native range.
- [[Repatriation]]: reintroducing an organism to a place where it was formerly present.
- [[Augmentation]]: adding individuals to an existing wild population.

**Management intent.** Upstream of the barrier, Bonita Creek is to be managed as a native-only stream for the foreseeable future. Options include mechanical and chemical removal of invaders, closure to fishing, and translocation, repatriation, and augmentation of natives.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Environmental baseline changes since 2007:**
- The piscicide successfully removed several nonnative fishes from lower Bonita Creek, but mosquitofish and green sunfish re-established.
- All four translocated species (loach minnow, spikedace, desert pupfish, Gila topminnow) were stocked into the renovated reach in 2009. Spikedace was also stocked upstream of the infiltration gallery.
- All species except desert pupfish were detected in subsequent monitoring.
- Native fishes, including Gila chub, that were present before the renovation were repatriated, but only a subset of original population sizes was stocked. A pre- and post-renovation population comparison is not yet possible.

**Stocking and monitoring results (2008–2009):**
- [[Gila Topminnow]]: 975 individuals (printed as "n=9751") from [[Bylas Springs]] stock were translocated in 2009. The population reached a possibly very large size (perhaps hundreds of thousands) in the lower reach but declined by late summer 2009 due to partial stream desiccation from drought, pumping of a City of Safford well, and expansion of mosquitofish.
- [[Loach Minnow]]: 678 individuals from [[Aravaipa Creek]] stock were translocated in 2009. Monitoring recaptured a number that is illegible in the scanned text (OCR reads "zo").
- [[Spikedace]]: 448 Aravaipa Creek stock were stocked into the renovated reach in 2009, and 165 were stocked upstream of the infiltration gallery. Monitoring (lower reach only) has detected 21 spikedace (OCR "zt"), one of which died in the net.
- [[Desert Pupfish]]: 147 individuals from [[El Doctor Marsh]] stock were stocked in 2009. Monitoring has not detected their continued presence.
- [[Gila Chub]]: 230 were repatriated to lower Bonita Creek in 2008, and 179 were recaptured in 2009, some possibly from 2009 reproduction.

**Holding and propagation.** Fish will be held in facilities such as the [[Arizona-Sonora Desert Museum]], the University of Arizona, and [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]]. Holding will last at least through the summer after the initial re-treatment (scheduled for June 2010) and possibly up to a year or more. If any species is held through winter 2010–2011, propagation may use volunteer spawning or injection of the synthetic hormone [[Ovaprim]]. Progeny would be grown to autumn of their first year before stocking.

**Downstream and upstream context.** Repatriation and translocation may occur anywhere downstream of the San Carlos Apache Reservation and upstream of the fish barrier. If fewer than about 200 individuals of a species are salvaged, they may be moved above the infiltration gallery to reduce holding mortality. Helicopter transport would be used for these moves.

**Recreational fishing.** Closing all or part of Bonita Creek to recreational fishing is a statutory prerogative of the [[Arizona Game And Fish Commission]]. The document states that such a closure would have no negative effect on listed fishes.

## Key Findings

1. **Proposed action.** A second rotenone renovation of the lower Bonita Creek reach is proposed, to remove nonnative mosquitofish and green sunfish and protect the native assemblage. Salvage methods are identical to the 2007 BA, but holding periods will be longer (up to a year or more instead of one to two weeks).

2. **Gila topminnow handling.** Gila topminnow will not be salvaged alive, to avoid mixing with similar-looking mosquitofish. Poeciliids captured during salvage will be euthanized or returned to the stream to die from the piscicide. Gila topminnow may be salvaged in future unplanned renovations if mosquitofish is not the target.

3. **Scope of re-treatment triggers.** Re-application may be needed if a renovation fails to kill all fishes, if nonnatives are transferred upstream of the barrier, if fish pass a failed barrier, or after other unforeseen incidents. Treatment of Bonita Creek upstream of the infiltration gallery may also become necessary.

4. **Mortality expectations.** All federally listed fishes repatriated or translocated, except Gila topminnow, will likely experience some unquantified mortality from salvage, transport, holding, and piscicide exposure. All Gila topminnow will be killed by the piscicide. Mortality will reduce the supply of fish for future repatriation.

5. **Source populations buffered.** The Gila chub population upstream of the infiltration gallery (and spikedace there, if established) is unaffected by the immediate action and can serve as a source. Captive stocks at Bubbling Ponds Hatchery, the Lower San Pedro River Preserve ponds, and Arizona State University are available. Wild sources are Aravaipa Creek, Bylas Springs, and El Doctor Marsh. These sources were already planned for augmentation, so the project should have relatively little impact on them.

6. **Conservation benefit.** If successful, the project would remove the known limiting factor (nonnative fishes) for all five listed species. Protection by the fish barrier and possible fishing closure is expected to enhance their conservation status and aid recovery.

7. **Cumulative effects.** These are not substantially different from those described in the 2007 BA.

8. **Conclusions.** The project is expected to significantly improve the conservation status of Gila chub, loach minnow, spikedace, desert pupfish, and Gila topminnow. Expected mortality from salvage, transport, holding, stocking, and renovation may affect, and is likely to adversely affect, each of these species.
