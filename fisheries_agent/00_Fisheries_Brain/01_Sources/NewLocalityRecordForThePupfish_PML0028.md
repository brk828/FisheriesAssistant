# New Locality Record for the Pupfish Cyprinodon Nevadensis

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae #hexagrammidae

## Background & Description

**Reference block (non-fish content).** The page opens with three cited works on bivalves and introduced mollusks: a study on the biology and propagation of young hard clams (*Mercenaria mercenaria*), a survey of introduced mollusks of western North America, and a report on the Maryland hardshell clam fishery. These are citations only and contain no data for this note.

**Badwater pupfish record.** The authors, from the Department of Zoology at [[California State College at Los Angeles]], describe a single [[Amargosa Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon nevadensis amargosae*) captured by hand on July 24, 1970, at 2300 hours. The specimen had a standard length of 43.5 mm.

**Platy addition to the state fauna.** The authors, from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] Inland Fisheries Branch, report about 200 adult and juvenile [[Variated Platy]] (*Xiphophorus variatus*), a native of Mexico, collected on December 24, 1969, in the [[Avenue 82 Drain]] ditch. [[Carl L. Hubbs]] confirmed the identification.

**Painted greenling study.** The note reports observations from a study of demersal fish populations on small subtidal reefs off Cannery Row, conducted from mid-July 1968 through mid-February 1969. It focuses on sexual dimorphism in the [[Painted Greenling]] (*Oxylebius pictus*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Badwater, Death Valley.** Water conditions at the collection point were extreme:
- Water temperature: 30 (unit as given in source, presumably °C)
- Dissolved oxygen: 0.4 ppm
- Salinity: 27.75‰ (as given in source)

The authors recorded paired readings on the western shoreline, opposite the capture site: temperature 22.5 C, dissolved oxygen 4.5 ppm, and salinity 43.70%. They suggest these values may indicate an east-to-west gradient across the basin. The authors had monitored Badwater monthly since November 1969 and saw no pupfish before July 24. They propose that the fish was carried in by flooding of the [[Amargosa River]] in 1969 or by later flash floods, and note that a pupfish could survive the harsh conditions of the season. Other [[Cyprinodon]] species occur in the Death Valley drainage, but this was the first recorded fish capture from Badwater.

**Avenue 82 Drain, Salton Sea area.** This irrigation-type drainage ditch lies between State Highway 86 and the Salton Sea, about 4 miles east of Oasis. Fishes collected with the platys and identified by Hubbs were:
- [[Redshiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*)
- [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*)
- [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)
- [[Mexican Molly]] (*Poecilia mexicana*) and [[Sailfin Molly]] (*P. latipinna*)

According to Jack Wood, owner of an adjacent tropical fish farm, the platys escaped from one of his ponds into the ditch around 1956.

**Monterey Bay reefs.** The greenling study concerns fish communities on small subtidal reefs, and the authors compare the painted greenling with the [[Kelp Greenling]] (*Hexagrammos decagrammus*), another hexagrammid common in the area. The painted greenling shows a less clear male-female distinction than the kelp greenling.

## Key Findings

**1. Badwater pupfish record.**
- One [[Amargosa Pupfish]], standard length 43.5 mm, captured by hand in water 4.0 cm deep near the eastern shoreline.
- First recorded fish from Badwater; no fishes seen in monthly sampling from November 1969 until July 24, 1970, and no additional fish found afterward.
- Probable introduction by floodwater, which is relevant to dispersal in [[Death Valley]] drainages.

**2. First verified establishment of the variated platy in California.**
- About 200 adult and juvenile [[Variated Platy]] collected December 24, 1969, with more collected January 7, 1970.
- The authors call these the first verified records of *X. variatus* established in California. Earlier sightings by [[St. Amant and Hoover]] (1969) in Orange County flood-control canals, and a July 10, 1970 collection, had not been confirmed as established populations.
- An earlier, unverified report from the Salton Sea area in the 1950s could not be confirmed because the specimens were lost before identification.
- Escape from a fish-farm pond is the likely source. This record adds to the [[Invasive Species]] documentation for California.

**3. Sexual dimorphism in the painted greenling.**
- 28 specimens, 108 to 164 mm total length, were collected from late November to mid-January: 16 males and 12 females.
- Males had well-developed, paired white testes. All females carried bright orange eggs.
- Females were brighter overall, with more distinct irregular vertical brown bars. Their fins were yellow and light orange, with burnt orange and brown bars. Males showed less distinct coloration, so dimorphism is weaker than in the [[Kelp Greenling]].
- The note's description of egg-mass defense is cut off in the source, so the behavioral findings cannot be reported.
