# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Letter on Recovery Plan Authority for Endangered Colorado River Fishes

#ichthyology #endangered-species #recovery-planning #Colorado-River-fishes

## Executive Summary

This 1989 correspondence from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Regional Director clarifies the statutory authority for establishing recovery goals for three endangered Colorado River fish species: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]]. The letter asserts that under Section 4(f) of the Endangered Species Act, the Secretary of the Interior (through delegated authority to the Service) retains ultimate responsibility for developing recovery plans and establishing recovery goals, not the Recovery Implementation Committee. The document includes excerpts from the 1988 amendments to the Endangered Species Act addressing recovery plan requirements.

## Background & Description

The letter addresses a jurisdictional question raised at the April 4, 1989 Management Group meeting regarding which entity has authority to establish recovery goals for endangered [[Colorado River]] fishes. Three species are specifically identified: [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], and [[Bonytail Chub]], all federally listed as endangered.

The Recovery Implementation Program for Endangered Fish Species in the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]] was established through a Cooperative Agreement involving multiple parties. A Recovery Implementation Committee was created to coordinate implementation activities, while a [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]] provides scientific review and advice.

Section 4(f) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] requires the Secretary to develop and implement recovery plans for listed species. The 1988 amendments added Section 4(f)(1)(B), mandating that recovery plans incorporate "objective, measurable criteria which, when met, would result in a determination...that the species be removed from the list."

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The three endangered fish species inhabit the [[Upper Colorado River Basin]]. The Recovery Program generates information on population trends and parameters constituting "self-sustaining, viable populations" of these species through monitoring and recovery activities.

The recovery planning process occurs within a complex institutional framework involving federal statutory responsibilities, cooperative agreements among multiple agencies and states, and scientific peer review. The basinwide perspective is emphasized for understanding these species' conservation needs.

The letter notes that upper basin recovery activities may conflict with "construction or other development projects or other forms of economic activity," highlighting the tension between species conservation and water resource development in the [[Colorado River Basin]].

## Key Findings

**Statutory Authority Structure:**
- Section 4(f) of the [[Endangered Species Act]] places responsibility for developing recovery plans, including recovery goals, on the Secretary of the Interior
- This authority has been delegated to the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]
- The Service retains final approval authority over plan contents, even when recovery teams or contractors prepare plans
- These statutory responsibilities cannot be redelegated to the [[Recovery Implementation Committee]]

**Role of Recovery Implementation Committee:**
- The Cooperative Agreement establishing the Recovery Program did not and cannot delegate or abrogate statutory responsibilities
- The Implementation Committee's role is to provide coordinated implementation of recovery plans in the upper basin
- The Committee can use monitoring data to develop more specific recovery goals and recommend them to the Service
- Recommendations with strong technical basis will receive serious consideration

**Recovery Goal Development Process:**
- Recovery activities generate information on population trends and viable population parameters
- The Implementation Committee will use this information to develop specific recovery goals for the upper basin
- The Service may request review by the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Team]]
- The Service retains final approval authority for recovery goals
- Scientific information from upper basin recovery efforts will enable refinement and quantification of goals

**Recovery Team Function:**
- Provides basinwide perspective, scientific credibility, and political autonomy
- Delivers unbiased, high-quality peer review of biological bases for upper basin activities
- Continues to advise the Service on biological matters
- Review and input improves overall credibility of the Recovery Program

**1988 ESA Amendments Requirements:**
- Recovery plans must incorporate objective, measurable criteria for delisting
- Recovery goals must be as specific as possible within bounds of good science
- Plans must include site-specific management actions, measurable criteria, and cost/time estimates
- Public notice and comment required before final approval of new or revised recovery plans
- Monitoring system required for recovered species for at least five years post-delisting
