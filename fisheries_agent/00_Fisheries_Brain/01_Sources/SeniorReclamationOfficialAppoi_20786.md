# Glen Canyon Environmental Studies: Coordinator Appointment and Study Status (December 1988)

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #fisheries-management

## Executive Summary

This document is an administrative package from the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], Upper Colorado Region, dated December 13, 1988. It consists of a cover letter to constituents of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) and a news release. The release announces that Dr. [[Wayne N. Marchant]] was designated coordinator of the GCES program, working with Regional Director [[Clifford I. Barrett]] to manage the study. The study examines how [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations affect the [[Grand Canyon]] and the [[Colorado River]], including effects on trout and endangered fish populations. The document contains no species-level data, sample sizes, or statistics.

## Background & Description

- **Study origin:** The GCES began in 1982 under Departmental direction to study the effects of Glen Canyon Dam operations on the Grand Canyon.
- **Scope:** All aspects of dam operations are under review, including water delivery and power production.
- **Study areas:** Impacts on power revenues, fisheries (both [[Trout]] and [[Endangered Fish Species]]), beach erosion and sediment transport within Grand Canyon, and the recreation industry.
- **Study age:** The release describes the study as six years old at the time of the announcement.
- **Leadership change:** [[James W. Ziglar]], Assistant Secretary of the Interior, announced the appointment. Marchant was formerly Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary of the Interior and was recently named Chief of the Research and Laboratory Services Division in Denver.
- **Personnel:** Marchant is a career government employee and a member of the [[Senior Executive Service]]. He will continue to reside in Denver, and GCES supervision is one of his duties.
- **Technical staff:** The current technical staff working on the studies will be retained.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Data limitations:** Much of the information gathered to date was collected during periods of abnormally high water levels on the Colorado River.
- **Additional data needs:** The release states that more data are needed under more nearly normal conditions to evaluate:
  - Operational impacts on endangered and [[Sport Fisheries]]
  - [[Beach Erosion and Sediment Transport]] conditions
  - Further economic analysis related to power generation
- **Stakeholders:** Concluding the studies will require extensive coordination with the seven basin states, power customers, and recreation and environmental interests.
- **Regulated flow:** The study's focus on dam operations indicates that flow regime, rather than natural hydrology, is the main environmental driver under review. The document does not quantify flows or habitat conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **Coordinator appointed:** Dr. Wayne N. Marchant was designated overall coordinator of the GCES program and will assist the Regional Director in managing it.
2. **Work largely complete, with gaps:** The majority of the six-year study is finished, but key data were collected under abnormally high river flows and must be supplemented under near-normal conditions.
3. **Fisheries assessment pending:** Operational impacts on endangered and sport fisheries have not yet been fully evaluated.
4. **Economic analysis needed:** Further economic analysis of power generation effects is required.
5. **Coordination plan:** Concluding the study requires coordination with seven basin states, power customers, and recreation and environmental interests.
6. **Next milestone:** A meeting is planned for late January (1989) to finalize the draft study plan that recipients had recently received.
7. **Continuity:** The technical staff will be retained during the transition.
