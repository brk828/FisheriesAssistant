## Key Findings

Since no aquatic/fish science exists in this text, below are the paper's **actual (non-ichthyological) key findings**, provided for completeness and transparency:

- Establishes the general unbiased estimator for a population total using sampling probabilities: $\hat{V} = \sum (v_i/P_i)$, where $v_i$ is the value of interest and $P_i$ is the inclusion probability — the foundational equation of the [[Horvitz-Thompson Estimator]] framework.
- Demonstrates that classical forestry sampling designs (simple random sampling, stratified random sampling, [[Bitterlich Point Sampling]] / variable radius plot sampling, probability-proportional-to-size (PPS) sampling, and Grosenbaugh's [[Three-P Sampling]]) are all special cases of a single general probability-based estimation framework.
- Discusses joint inclusion probabilities ($P_{ij}$) and their role in determining sampling variance, independent of the population total estimate itself.
- Presents a worked forest-management case study (100 compartments, two divisions, pine bark beetle outbreak compartments) showing how sampling probabilities can be deliberately restructured to meet multiple management objectives (beetle-outbreak monitoring, balanced workload across divisions, volume estimation) rather than purely minimizing statistical variance.
- Concludes that skillful, purposeful manipulation of sampling probabilities — rather than variance optimization alone — is the key to aligning inventory design with real-world management information objectives.
