# Annotated Check List of the Vertebrates of Lyon County, Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #vertebrates

## Executive Summary

This annotated checklist, compiled by the [[Kansas State Teachers College]] Biology Department in Emporia, is the first complete inventory of the vertebrate fauna of Lyon County, Kansas. It covers fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds, and mammals, using contemporary taxonomy and relative-abundance terms. The authors describe it as a non-exhaustive base for future study that also invites contributions from amateur naturalists. The county's fish fauna is the most intensively studied group, and the county is split between two major drainages: the [[Marais Des Cygnes River]] to the north and the [[Neosho River]] system to the south.

## Background & Description

The list updates earlier county-level work on amphibians and reptiles (Breukelman and Downs, 1936; Breukelman and Clarke, 1951) and birds (Downs and Breukelman, 1941). Taxonomic arrangement follows Schmidt (1953) for herpetofauna, Tordoff (1956) for birds, and Hall (1955) for mammals. Common names follow *Copeia* (1956). Where a subspecies' range closely approaches the county, the local subspecies is named.

Relative abundance is defined as follows, applying only within each species' habitat and seasonal presence:

- **Abundant:** expected in large numbers on almost any visit.
- **Common:** expected regularly in small numbers.
- **Occasional:** found irregularly; often not seen on a given visit.
- **Scarce:** only a few individuals recorded; the county is assumed to be within the natural range.
- **Rare:** one or two specimens; possibly strays or introductions.

Records come from published accounts, preserved specimens in the college museum, catalog records, unpublished master's theses, and staff field observations. Fish collections were made by [[John Breukelman]] from 1930 onward, with specimens held in the college museum.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Hydrology.** The northern part of the county drains into the Marais des Cygnes River, which flows east to the Missouri River. The southern part drains into the Neosho River, which flows southeast to the Arkansas and then the Mississippi River. The authors note that northern and southern fish assemblages differ accordingly. The [[Cottonwood River]], a Neosho tributary, hosts several species found nowhere else in the county.

**Habitat diversity.** The county includes riffles and smaller streams, permanent rivers, farm ponds, and reservoirs such as [[Lyon County State Lake]] and [[Emporia Country Club Lake]]. Its upland landscape includes rocky prairie hillsides, cultivated fields, and wooded river bottoms. Urban Emporia adds further habitat, with several species recorded there.

**Introductions.** The [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*) has no natural record in the county; it has been established in Lake Wooster on the campus since its introduction in 1954. The Ring-necked Pheasant was introduced several times but is not established. The Starling is described as having increased to a nuisance level over the last decade.

**Conservation-relevant notes.** The Carolina Paroquet is listed as extinct in the county, with a single specimen from 1890. The Greater Prairie Chicken has increased greatly in the last decade. The Western Massasauga is common in the southwest and occasional elsewhere, and the Northern Copperhead is occasional, mostly in the northeast.

## Key Findings

**Fishes (69 species, 12 families).** The total includes introduced forms with breeding populations.
- The [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*) is abundant and was taken in more collections and localities than any other county fish.
- The [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) is common to abundant and ranks second in number of records. Most specimens are intermediate between *P. promelas* and *P. confertus*.
- Several species are restricted to particular waters: the [[Blue Sucker]] and [[River Redhorse]] occur only in the Cottonwood River, and the Fantail Darter is known only from Jacob's Creek.
- The [[Topeka Shiner]] is scarce and occurs only in Neosho tributaries.
- The American Eel is scarce, with only a few specimens from the Cottonwood River.

**Amphibians (14 species, 6 families).** These are three salamanders, two toads, and nine frogs. The [[Western Chorus Frog]] and the [[Northern Cricket Frog]] are abundant. The [[Northern Wood Frog]] is recorded from a single specimen, the only Kansas record. The [[Crayfish Frog]] is scarce, with one individual collected northeast of Emporia.

**Reptiles (40 species, 10 families).** These include 9 turtles, 9 lizards, and 22 snakes. Two venomous snakes are present: the [[Northern Copperhead]] and the [[Western Massasauga]]. Among the turtles, the [[Missouri Slider]] is found only in Emporia Country Club Lake. The [[Eastern Collared Lizard]], [[Northern Earless Lizard]], and [[Texas Horned Lizard]] are common in the southwest. The [[Red-sided Garter Snake]] is abundant in all habitat types.

**Birds (248 species; 76 known to nest; 49 families).** The count comes from the summary, which draws on Downs and Breukelman (1941), with doubtful species omitted. Notable points:
- Waterfowl and shorebirds are numerous at Lyon County State Lake during migration, including large White Pelican flocks and abundant Pectoral and Baird Sandpipers.
- The [[Great Blue Heron]] is a common summer resident, with at least 8 colonies containing about 100 active nests.
- The [[Greater Prairie Chicken]] is abundant in local pasture areas.
- The [[Bob-white]] is abundant throughout the county.

**Mammals (37 species, 21 families; plus 12 hypothetical).** Intensive mammal collecting has not been done in the county. The 12 hypothetical species, listed in brackets, are expected to occur but have not been recorded. The bats are the main gap. Notable records include:
- The [[Coyote]] is abundant.
- The [[Eastern Cottontail]] is abundant.
- The [[Deer Mouse]] is abundant.
- The [[Eastern Wood Rat]] is occasional, with one known colony near the Chase County line.
- The [[Porcupine]] is rare, with a single specimen from northeast of Emporia.
- The [[Ring-tailed Cat]] is rare, with one specimen from 1956.
- The [[White-tailed Deer]] is occasional, and farmers along the Cottonwood and Neosho rivers report seeing it frequently.

**Overall.** The checklist provides a baseline and a call for further study. It states that the list is incomplete, particularly for mammals (bats) and for rare or stray species. It also encourages reports of unusual specimens to the Biology Department in Emporia.
