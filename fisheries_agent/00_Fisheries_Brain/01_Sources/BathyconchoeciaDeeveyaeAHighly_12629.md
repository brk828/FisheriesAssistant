# Bathyconchoecia deeveyae, A Highly Ornamented New Species of Ostracoda (Halocyprididae) from the Peru-Chile Trench System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ostracoda #halocyprididae #deep-sea-fauna

## Executive Summary

This paper describes *Bathyconchoecia deeveyae*, a new species of deep-sea benthic ostracod collected from the [[Peru-Chile Trench System]] at 520 m depth during Cruise 11 of the R/V Anton Bruun in October 1965. The species is distinguished by its highly ornamented carapace bearing 7 long hollow spines, making it unique among members of the genus [[Bathyconchoecia]]. The single juvenile specimen (holotype USNM 123335) was collected by Dr. R. J. Menzies using a benthic trawl. The species is named in honor of Dr. Georgiana B. Deevey, who established the genus in 1968.

## Background & Description

The specimen belongs to Suborder [[Halocypridina]], Family [[Halocyprididae]], genus *Bathyconchoecia* (type-species: *B. paulula* Deevey, 1968). The appendages differ from the genus diagnosis in having only 6 claws on the furca instead of the usual 8, likely a juvenile character.

**Morphological Description:**
- **Carapace**: Shell surface with minute pits especially apparent under phase illumination; deep incisure with rounded inner margin; rostrum with pointed tip bearing long hollow spine; long hollow spine on dorsal margin of posterior right valve; each valve with short spine near dorsal margin anterior to valve middle and short spine near valve middle below center; asymmetrical glands present symmetrically near posterodorsal corner of each valve
- **Size**: Length without spines 1.12 mm; length including spines 1.61 mm; height without spine 0.89 mm
- **First antenna**: Short shaft with indistinct segments; brushlike structure on 3rd segment with approximately 148 filaments in about 8 rows (18 filaments each); long stout spinous bristle distal to brush; end segment with 1 long and 2 shorter bristles
- **Second antenna**: Endopodite with 1st segment bearing 2 distodorsal bristles; end segment with 5 setae and filaments; exopodite with distal margins of 3rd and 4th segments bearing comb of fine teeth; bristles of segments 2-8 with sparse natatory hairs; 9th segment with 3 bristles (1 long, 1 medium, 1 short)
- **Mandible**: Coxa pars incisiva with 5-6 teeth on fingerlike posterior process; single small tooth anterior and proximal to large anterior tooth; proximal tooth list with 4-5 saw-like teeth; distal tooth list with 8-9 teeth; basale distal edge with 6 large triangular teeth followed by 2 recurved processes; endopodite with complex bristle arrangement
- **Maxilla**: Precoxale endite with 6 bristles; coxale endite with 12 bristles (6 on each lobe); endopodite with end segment bearing 2 short stout curved claws and 4 short slender bristles between claws
- **Fifth limb**: Exopodite 1st segment with extremely long bristle reaching past bristles on end segment; endopodite with 2 claw-like bristles; epipodial appendage with 12 bristles in 3 groups of 4
- **Sixth limb**: Exopodite 1st joint with 3 short bristles and 1 extremely long distodorsal bristle reaching just past bristles of 4th joint
- **Seventh limb**: End segment with 2 long subequal bristles
- **Furca**: Each lamella with 6 claws; claw 1 longer than claw 2; claw 2 only slightly longer than claws 3-6; long spinous bristle present between lamellae following last claw; lamellae surface with short spines
- **Pigmentation**: Black pigmentation present in preserved specimen posterior to 1st segment of each 1st antenna and anterior to heart

## Key Findings

1. **New Species Description**: *Bathyconchoecia deeveyae* represents a new species distinguished by extreme spinosity—7 long spines on the carapace, unique among all known members of genus *Bathyconchoecia*
2. **Taxonomic Placement**: Despite having only 6 furcal claws (versus typical 8), the species is placed in *Bathyconchoecia*; the reduced claw number is interpreted as a juvenile character
3. **Specimen Status**: Single juvenile specimen (holotype USNM 123335) collected; carapace preserved in alcohol, appendages on slide in glycerine
4. **Morphological Uniqueness**: The spinosity of the carapace separates this species from all other members of the genus
5. **Habitat Characterization**: Deep-sea benthic habitat at 520 m depth in conditions of extreme hypoxia (0.15 ml/l dissolved oxygen)
6. **Geographic Range**: Known only from type locality in [[Peru-Chile Trench System]]
7. **Nomenclature**: Species named for Dr. Georgiana B. Deevey, who established the genus *Bathyconchoecia* in 1968

The description provides detailed morphological data on all appendages, establishing baseline knowledge for this highly ornamented deep-sea ostracod species. The presence of elaborate spines in a deep-sea benthic environment raises questions about their functional significance in low-oxygen, high-pressure habitats.
