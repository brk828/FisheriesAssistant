# Pimephales vigilax (Pisces, Cyprinidae) Established in the Missouri River Basin

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1984 note in the *Transactions of the Kansas Academy of Science* by Frank B. Cross and Stephen G. Hasloufer reports that the [[Bullhead Minnow]] (*[[Pimephales Vigilax]]*) has become established in the [[Kansas River]] basin, part of the [[Missouri River Basin]], where it was previously considered absent. Earlier published records from the Missouri Basin were reassessed and largely excluded, leaving only [[Bait-Bucket Introduction]] events as explanations for scattered captures. Since 1976, the species has been recorded in increasing numbers, and the authors conclude that the Kansas River population is self-sustaining and derives from eastern stocks.

## Background & Description

- **Native range:** Widespread in the [[Mississippi River System]] and in Gulf coastal drainages from the Alabama River to the Rio Grande. It is not indigenous to the Missouri River basin.
- **Taxonomic history:** Early reports from eastern Nebraska (Evermann and Cox, 1896, as *Cliola vigilax*) and South Dakota (Churchill and Over, 1933, as *Ceratichthys vigilax*) were initially accepted by Hubbs and Black (1947) for *Ceratichthys perspicuus*. Hubbs (1951) later synonymized *C. perspicuus* with *C. vigilax* and deleted the northwestern Missouri drainage records. Bailey and Allum (1962) confirmed this and referred the Nebraska and South Dakota records to [[Pimephales Notatus]].
- **Subspecies:** The eastern subspecies, [[Pimephales Vigilax Perspicuus]], is distinguished from the southwestern [[Pimephales Vigilax Vigilax]] by breeding male tubercle patterns. Eastern males have pearl organs in two rows across the snout (5-4), whereas southwestern males have a single row, usually of five tubercles.
- **Congeners in the Kansas River:** [[Pimephales Notatus]] (bluntnose minnow) and [[Pimephales Promelas]] (fathead minnow), both native to the basin.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Historical Missouri Basin status:** Reports from Iowa, Nebraska, and South Dakota were attributed to bait-bucket introductions rather than natural range. A record from "Jones Pond," Coffee County, Kansas, was also considered an introduction. The authors have evidence of persistence only in the lower Kansas River basin.
- **Habitat preferences observed:** The highest density was recorded at a sand-dredged site with sluggish flow over fine sand and silt (particle size <0.4 mm). Channelized sections, such as [[Soldier Creek]] near Topeka, also supported the species.
- **Range expansion:** Captures extended the known range westward to the [[Smoky Hill River]] in Geary County and eastward to the confluence of the Kansas and Missouri rivers.
- **Competitive context:** In 1979, the bullhead minnow made up 20% of *Pimephales* captured between DeSoto and Kansas City. By 1981, it accounted for 96% of *Pimephales* caught, with trends consistent across all sampling sites.

## Key Findings

1. **Historical records excluded:** Earlier Missouri Basin reports of the species were reassessed as misidentifications of related taxa and largely removed from its range (Hubbs, 1951; Bailey and Allum, 1962).
2. **Bait-bucket origin:** Known Missouri Basin occurrences outside the Kansas River were attributed to bait-bucket introductions.
3. **Early absence:** No bullhead minnows were found in extensive Kansas River system surveys before 1976.
4. **Establishment by 1976:** In 1976, 27 specimens (23–69 mm standard length) were collected at [[Lawrence Kansas]] in the Kansas River, and 156 specimens (20–43 mm) at Soldier Creek. The presence of juveniles and adults indicates natural reproduction at both sites.
5. **Abundance increase:** Across 14 seined sites between DeSoto and Kansas City, Kansas, the species occurred at all sites. Average density rose from under 1 fish per 100 m² in 1979 to 16.5 per 100 m² in 1981.
6. **Peak density:** The maximum density was 128 per 100 m² in 1981, at a sand-dredged site.
7. **Community rank:** In 1981, the species ranked fourth in abundance among 18 cyprinid species collected at the 14 sites.
8. **Origin:** Tubercle characters of breeding males indicate the Kansas River population derives from eastern stocks (*P. v. perspicuus*).
9. **Conclusion:** The bullhead minnow is now established in the lower [[Kansas River Basin]], and its range expansion is attributed to natural spread from this founding population rather than to ongoing bait-bucket introductions alone.
