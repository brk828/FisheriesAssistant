# Preliminary Report on the Second Collecting Trip to Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This preliminary field report documents the second fish-collecting trip to Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge (27–28 February 1987), led by collectors Jim Starr and Mike Perkinson. Sampling targeted two imperiled Southwestern desert fish species — the [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) and the [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — across four artificial water bodies: Road Camp Tank, Mormon Lake, King's Tank, and Big Bertha Tank. Gill nets and seines (100-ft bag seine and 30-ft seine) were the primary capture methods. Morphometric data (total length, fork length, weight) were recorded for each captured individual, and results were compared against prior sampling events (November 1986, January 1987) to track population size structure and growth trends over time.

## Background & Description
Data collected include total length (TL, mm), fork length (FL, mm), and weight (Wt, g) for all captured specimens, summarized below by site.

### [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*)
- **Road Camp Tank (27 Feb 87)**: n = 38 (36 individual records tabulated); Mean TL = 142.9 mm, Mean FL = 126.9 mm, Mean Wt = 28.0 g. Size range approx. TL 112–185 mm.
  - Notable coloration: a rosy-red hue observed at the base of the anal fin and each paired fin, consistent with active breeding/feeding condition.
- **King's Tank (28 Feb 87)**: n = 37; Mean TL = 116.4 mm, Mean FL = 103.6 mm, Mean Wt = 14.5 g. Size range approx. TL 97–165 mm — smaller-bodied cohort than Road Camp Tank.
- **Big Bertha Tank (28 Feb 87)**: Only **one individual** captured after seven hauls with a 30-ft seine, suggesting very low population density at this site.

### [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*)
- **Mormon Lake (27 Feb 87)**: n = 12–14 (gill net, two sequential sets at 1845 and 2030 hrs); Mean TL = 172.1 mm, Mean FL = 155.1 mm, Mean Wt = 57.9 g.
  - One individual noted with a **kyphotic tail** deformity (weight 40 g).
  - Size range approx. TL 103–227 mm.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- Both target species inhabit isolated, artificial desert stock tanks/reservoirs within the [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], representing refugial populations of native Southwestern freshwater fishes.
- **[[Gila elegans]]** was captured across three separate tank habitats (Road Camp Tank, King's Tank, Big Bertha Tank), indicating a broad but patchy distribution with highly variable local abundance (e.g., near absence at Big Bertha Tank vs. moderate catches at Road Camp and King's Tank).
- **[[Xyrauchen texanus]]** was restricted to Mormon Lake in this survey, sampled via gill net during evening/night hours (1845–2200 hrs), consistent with known crepuscular/nocturnal activity patterns in this species.
- Fish at Road Camp Tank were observed **actively feeding** near sunset (~1730 hrs), suggesting crepuscular feeding behavior for *Gila elegans* in this habitat.
- Environmental conditions noted at Mormon Lake included sub-freezing air temperatures and a water level approximately two feet lower than the previous month's survey, indicating potential habitat stress from drawdown during winter sampling.
- The presence of a kyphotic (deformed) individual in the *Xyrauchen texanus* sample may indicate developmental abnormalities possibly linked to genetic bottlenecking or environmental stressors in this isolated population.

## Key Findings
- **Population structure shift**: Mean size of *Gila elegans* increased across nearly all sites from January to February 1987 (e.g., Road Camp Tank Mean TL rose from 109.2 mm to 142.9 mm), possibly reflecting seasonal growth, recruitment of larger individuals, or gear selectivity differences.
- **Site-specific scarcity**: Big Bertha Tank yielded only a single *Gila elegans* despite extensive seining effort (seven hauls), highlighting this site as a low-density or possibly declining population relative to Road Camp Tank and King's Tank.
- **Breeding condition indicator**: Rosy-red fin coloration in *Gila elegans* at Road Camp Tank may serve as a visual indicator of reproductive readiness or seasonal physiological state.
- **Morphological anomaly**: Documentation of a kyphotic-tailed *Xyrauchen texanus* individual is a notable morphological observation warranting further investigation into population health.
- **Temporal comparison value**: The cumulative dataset (Nov 1986–Feb 1987) establishes an important baseline for monitoring long-term population trends of both [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]] in refuge waters, both of which are federally recognized as endangered/threatened species within the [[Colorado River Basin]] fish fauna.
- **Methodology**: Gill nets were used for larger/deeper water bodies (Mormon Lake, Road Camp Tank), while seines (100-ft bag seine, 30-ft seine) were used for shallower tanks (King's Tank, Big Bertha Tank), reflecting adaptive sampling strategy based on habitat characteristics.
