# Taxonomic Status, Ecology, and Distribution of the Chihuahua Chub, Gila nigrescens

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cyprinidae #taxonomy #morphometrics #reproductive-biology #endangered-species

## Executive Summary

This final report (February 1980) documents comprehensive taxonomic, morphometric, and reproductive studies of the [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*), an endangered cyprinid inhabiting the [[Mimbres River]] in New Mexico and several drainages in Chihuahua, Mexico. The study examined 202+ specimens from museum collections, employing multivariate morphometric analyses ([[Principal Components Analysis]]), meristic counts, and reproductive assessments. Key findings establish *G. nigrescens* as taxonomically distinct from its closest relatives (*G. pandora* and *G. robusta*), identify a potentially distinct population in the [[Laguna de Bustillos]] basin (referred to as *Gila cf. nigrescens*), and determine that sexual maturity occurs at approximately 80 mm standard length, hypothesized to correspond to one year of age. The [[Mimbres River]] population shows greatest phenetic similarity to [[Rio Casas Grandes]] stocks.

## Background & Description

The [[Chihuahua Chub]] (*Gila nigrescens*) is a cyprinid fish endemic to the Chihuahuan Desert region. The species was originally described from multiple type localities including the Rio Mimbres (as *Gila pulchella*, USNM 233), Boca Grande (*Tigoma nigrescens*, USNM 220), and Rio Janos (*Tigoma nigrescens*, USNM 219). The nomenclatural history involves taxonomic confusion with related species *G. pandora* ([[Rio Grande Chub]]) and *G. robusta* ([[Roundtail Chub]]).

Morphologically, *G. nigrescens* is characterized by: 9 dorsal rays (modally), 8 anal rays, 9-9 pelvic rays, 65-79 lateral-line scales, 53-64 total head pores (preoperculo-mandibular, infraorbital, and supraorbital combined), 39-44 vertebrae, pharyngeal tooth formula typically 2,5-4,2, and scales lacking radii on the anterior field. Standard length of examined specimens ranged from 40 mm to over 160 mm.

Sexual dimorphism is pronounced in American cyprinids including this species. Males exhibit significantly greater values than females for: caudal-peduncle least depth, caudal-peduncle width, dorsal-fin height, anal-fin height, pectoral-fin length, and pelvic-fin length. Females show greater predorsal length, preanal length, head length, upper jaw length, and body width. Males can be distinguished from females without dissection down to approximately 45 mm SL by comparing pectoral fin length to anal fin height; longer pectoral fins indicate males.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Chihuahua Chub]] inhabits five major drainage systems: the [[Mimbres River]] (Grant County, New Mexico), [[Rio Casas Grandes]] (including Rio Piedras Verdes and Rio San Miguel tributaries), [[Rio Santa Maria]], [[Rio del Carmen]], and the endorheic [[Laguna de Bustillos]] basin (all in Chihuahua, Mexico). These drainages represent the Chihuahuan Desert aquatic ecosystem, characterized by variable flow regimes and increasing anthropogenic pressures.

Field observations from the May-June 1979 expedition (reported separately) indicated that adult habitat requirements may be limiting factors for population persistence. The species faces ongoing threats from surface-water extraction for agriculture and general habitat loss throughout its Mexican range. The [[Mimbres River]] population has been extirpated or severely reduced, necessitating captive propagation at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico.

The study examined specimens from multiple institutional collections: [[Arizona State University]] (ASU), [[California Academy of Sciences]] (CAS), [[Eastern New Mexico University]] (ENMU), [[Field Museum of Natural History]] (FMNH), [[Texas Natural History Collection]] (TNHC), Universidad Autónoma de Nuevo León (UANL), [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]] (UMMZ), [[University of New Mexico]] (UNM), and [[United States National Museum]] (USNM). Sample sizes per drainage ranged from 4 specimens (Mimbres River) to 350+ (Rio Casas Grandes vicinity).

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Distinctiveness:**

*Gila nigrescens* is clearly distinct at the species level from its closest relatives *G. pandora* (Pecos River and upper Rio Grande) and *G. robusta* (Colorado River basin and Gulf of California drainages). Diagnostic characters separating these three species include:

- *G. nigrescens*: 9 dorsal rays, 9-9 pelvic rays, 8 anal rays, 65-79 lateral-line scales, 53-64 total head pores, 39-44 vertebrae
- *G. pandora*: 8 dorsal rays, 8-8 or 9-9 pelvic rays, 8 anal rays, 51-67 lateral-line scales, 43-58 total head pores, 40-43 vertebrae
- *G. robusta*: 9 dorsal rays, 9-9 pelvic rays, 9 anal rays, 79-96 lateral-line scales, 73-83 total head pores, 43-49 vertebrae

[[Principal Components Analysis]] incorporating morphometric, meristic, and head-pore characters achieved complete separation of *G. nigrescens* from *G. pandora* on the first two principal components. The PCA also revealed complete separation of Pecos River and Rio Grande races of *G. pandora*, supporting earlier findings by Miller and Hubbs (1962) based on pelvic ray counts (8-8 vs. 9-9) and pectoral ray counts (15-18 vs. 12-15).

**Geographic Variation within *G. nigrescens*:**

Analysis of variance (ANOVA) revealed significant morphometric differences among populations, particularly for the [[Laguna de Bustillos]] basin specimens. Bustillos fish differed significantly in:

- Males: snout length, upper jaw length, body depth, caudal-peduncle least depth, caudal-peduncle length, dorsal-fin height
- Females: postorbital head length, snout length, upper jaw length, body depth, caudal-peduncle least depth, caudal-peduncle length, dorsal-fin height

Principal components analysis of log-transformed morphometric data (96.5% variance explained by Component I for males, 97.2% for females) revealed distinctive allometric growth patterns for Bustillos populations. As Bustillos males grow, they develop wider caudal peduncles, longer pectoral and pelvic fins, and relatively smaller eyes and jaw lengths compared to other populations. This growth trajectory is unique and not observed in other *G. nigrescens* populations, which show wide variance but no consistent directional trend.

Meristic analysis showed Bustillos fish consistently have lower counts for lateral-line scales (59-70, mean 64.6 vs. 63-80, mean 69.7 for other populations) and head pores. A bivariate plot of lateral-line scales versus total head pores completely separated Bustillos specimens from other *G. nigrescens*. Multivariate PCA incorporating morphometric, meristic, and head-pore characters (Component I: 73.8% variance; Component II: 12.2% variance) achieved complete separation of Bustillos fish from other *G. nigrescens* populations on axes I vs. II.

Based on distinctive allometric growth characteristics and covariant trends toward fewer lateral-line scales and head pores, the Bustillos population is probably distinct at some taxonomic level and is referred to as *Gila cf. nigrescens* pending further study.

**Mimbres River Population Relationships:**

The limited sample size from the [[Mimbres River]] (n=4, only 1 female) hampers definitive conclusions, but available data indicate:

- Male Mimbres specimens most closely resemble [[Rio Casas Grandes]] populations in morphometric space (Component I vs. III plot), distinguished from Santa Maria, Carmen, and Bustillos populations by larger eye size and narrower body width and depth
- Mimbres fish tend toward lower meristic values, approaching *G. cf. nigrescens* in certain features
- One syntype of *Gila pulchella* (USNM 233) actually overlaps the Bustillos cluster on Component II
- The Mimbres River historically may have joined the Casas Grandes drainage in [[Lago de Guzman]]

**Recommendation:** If the Mimbres River stock becomes extinct, restocking should utilize chubs from the Rio Casas Grandes in the vicinity of Casas Grandes or [[Colonia Juarez]] as the most phenetically similar populations.

**Reproductive Biology:**

Examination of 202 individuals (40 mm to >100 mm SL) from all five drainages, classified by gonadal maturity, revealed:

- Sexual maturity is attained at approximately 80 mm standard length for both males and females
- Only 1 of 165 specimens <80 mm SL was mature (a male from Mimbres River, UNM 3933)
- Only 6 individuals >80 mm SL were immature (1 male, 5 females)
- Chi-square contingency analysis showed highly significant deviation from expected (P<0.00001 for both sexes)

Females were classified as mature if they possessed secondary oocytes or mature ova; males were classified as mature if testes were enlarged and complete. Males can be distinguished from females without dissection down to approximately 45 mm SL by relative fin lengths.

Preliminary scale analysis suggests 80-mm fish have one annulus, larger fish have more, and fish <70 mm have none. **Hypothesis:** The Chihuahua chub reaches sexual maturity in one year. However, this hypothesis requires rigorous verification through controlled growth studies, as the mark could represent a spawning check rather than a true annulus. If maturity actually requires two years, the extended lag time could have grave consequences for population maintenance, especially if breeding stocks are impacted.

**Critical Research Need:** Controlled growth studies should be undertaken immediately at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] to verify the size-age-maturity relationship. This information is essential for conservation planning and population management.

**Conservation Status and Recommendations:**

1. *G. nigrescens* (sensu stricto) inhabits the Mimbres River, rios Casas Grandes, Santa Maria, and del Carmen basins
2. *G. cf. nigrescens* inhabits the endorheic Laguna de Bustillos basin and is probably distinct at some taxonomic level
3. Maintenance of Mimbres River and other stocks should continue at Dexter National Fish Hatchery
4. No expectation exists for status improvement in Mexico due to ongoing pressure for surface-water use and agriculture, resulting in continued habitat loss
5. Habitat requirements of adults (identified as limiting factors) should be re-established in the Mimbres River prior to reintroduction
6. A mountainous tributary of the Rio Casas Grandes might someday serve as a refuge, but this remains uncertain
7. Additional specimens from Bustillos basin should be obtained (planned for May 1980) to clarify taxonomic status

**Methodological Notes:**

The study employed rigorous statistical methods including arcsine transformation of morphometric ratios to correct for non-normal (platykurtic) distributions, analysis of variance with 5% significance threshold, product-moment correlation, and principal components analysis extracted from correlation matrices with standardized variables (zero means, unit standard deviations). Meristic variables included dorsal rays, anal rays, pelvic rays (left and right), lateral-line scales, body-circumference scales, scales above and below lateral line, scales around caudal peduncle, total vertebrae, and pharyngeal teeth. Head pore counts followed Illick (1956) methodology for preoperculo-mandibular, infraorbital, and supraorbital pores.
