# The Generic Relationships and Nomenclature of the California Sardine

#ichthyology #marine-biology #clupeidae #taxonomy #nomenclature

## Executive Summary

This 1929 taxonomic revision by [[Carl L Hubbs]] establishes the new genus *Sardinops* for the [[California Sardine]] and related Pacific species, distinguishing them from the European sardines (genus *Sardina*). The work resolves long-standing confusion in sardine nomenclature by demonstrating fundamental morphological differences between Californian and European sardines, particularly in scale arrangement and gillraker structure. Hubbs designates *Maletta cerulea* Girard, 1854 as the type species of *Sardinops* and clarifies that Poey's *Sardinia pseudo-hispanica* should be synonymized with *Sardinella*, not applied to either Californian or European sardines.

## Background & Description

The California sardine had been previously referred to the European genus *Sardina* by Regan (1916). Jordan (1918) subsequently synonymized *Sardina* Antipa, 1906 with *Sardinia* Poey, 1858, based on examination of a specimen thought to be Poey's type. However, confusion persisted regarding the proper generic placement and nomenclature of sardines from different ocean basins.

Thompson (1926) identified numerous morphological characters distinguishing Californian from European sardines, including: (1) presence of a row of dark blotches behind the head in American species; (2) regular scale arrangement with each alternate row not nearly overlapped by the one in front; (3) weaker, less keeled ventral scutes with less expanded bases; (4) gillrakers on lower limb becoming gradually shortened toward the angle; (5) more expanded interopercle widely exposed behind preopercle; and (6) strongly oblique opercular ridges and preopercular edge.

Hubbs verified all of Thompson's observations and added critical new characters. The gillrakers of the upper limb fold down over those of the lower limb near the angle in Californian species but not in European species—a character Regan had used elsewhere for primary generic separation in [[Clupeidae]].

Dr. [[Henry B Bigelow]] discovered a fundamental difference in gillraker microstructure: European sardines (*pilchardus* and *sardina*) possess simple, slightly-bent, sharply pointed spines on gillrakers (about one-third as long as gillraker width, spaced about three per width). In contrast, Californian, Chilean, Japanese, and Australian forms have complex processes composed of a flask-shaped base and a distinct, fimbriate, grooved, leaf-like terminal element (nearly half or more than half as long as gillraker width, more crowded at about five per width). This provides a much finer straining apparatus in *Sardinops*, possibly correlated with seas where diatoms are relatively more abundant and crustaceans scarcer than in European waters.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The fundamental differences in gillraker structure between *Sardinops* and *Sardina* suggest ecological adaptations to different planktonic communities. The complex, fimbriate gillraker processes and finer straining apparatus of *Sardinops* species may reflect adaptation to waters with higher diatom abundance and lower crustacean density compared to European waters inhabited by *Sardina pilchardus*.

The geographic distribution of *Sardinops* species spans temperate waters of the [[Pacific Ocean]] (California, Chile, Japan, Australia) and extends to [[South Africa]], suggesting adaptation to similar oceanographic conditions across these regions. The absence of either *Sardinops* or *Sardina* types from the western [[Atlantic Ocean]] is notable.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Revision:**
- Established new genus *Sardinops* Hubbs, 1929, with type species *Maletta cerulea* Girard, 1854
- Generic diagnosis includes: gillrakers of upper limb folded over lower limb; gillrakers progressively shortened toward angle; scale-rows regularly spaced with subequal exposed areas; weak keels on ventral scutes; strongly oblique opercular ridges; row of dark spots typically present on upper sides behind head
- Retained *Sardina* Antipa, 1906 for European species (*Sardina pilchardus*)
- Synonymized *Sardinia* Poey, 1858 with *Sardinella* Cuvier & Valenciennes, 1847

**Morphological Distinctions:**
- Scale arrangement fundamentally different: *Sardinops* has regular rows with apparent number equal to true number; *Sardina* has alternate rows nearly overlapped (apparent number about half the true number)
- Gillraker microstructure: *Sardinops* possesses complex flask-shaped processes with fimbriate terminal elements (nearly half gillraker width, ~5 per width); *Sardina* has simple pointed spines (one-third gillraker width, ~3 per width)
- Gillraker folding pattern differs between genera
- Ventral scutes weaker and less keeled in *Sardinops*
- Interopercle more expanded and widely exposed in *Sardinops*
- Opercular ridges and preopercular edge strongly oblique in *Sardinops* versus nearly vertical in *Sardina*

**Species Recognition:**
Five species assigned to *Sardinops*:
1. *Sardinops cerulea* (Girard), 1854 - Californian
2. *Sardinops sagax* (Jenyns), 1842 - Chilean
3. *Sardinops melanosticta* (Temminck & Schlegel), 1846 - Japanese
4. *Sardinops neopilchardus* (Steindachner), 1879 - Australian
5. *Sardinops ocellata* (Pappé), 1853 - South African

**Nomenclatural Clarification:**
- Specimen labeled as Poey's type of *Sardinia pseudo-hispanica* in [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]] is not the actual type (smaller size, 51 vertebrae versus 46 in Poey's description, different dorsal ray count)
- Poey's *Sardinia pseudo-hispanica* likely represents *Sardinella anchovia* (46 vertebrae matching Poey's count)
- No evidence for occurrence of either *Sardinops* or *Sardina* type sardines in western Atlantic

**Taxonomic Uncertainty:**
- Distinctness of species within *Sardinops* questionable; Thompson unable to differentiate *S. cerulea* from *S. sagax*
- Critical comparison of material from all localities needed to resolve species boundaries
- Question of immediate common origin between *Sardinops* and *Sardina* remains open
