# Dexter National Fish Hatchery Fiscal Year 1981 Annual Narrative Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This annual narrative report covers fiscal year 1981 (FY81) at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] in Dexter, New Mexico. The station's program consisted entirely of holding, spawning, rearing, and distributing captive-propagated threatened, endangered, and other listed species of southwestern fishes. About 130,000 hatchery-reared fish were stocked in Arizona and California, and 21 taxa (19 species plus two hybrid crosses) were held during the year. Notable events included successful spawning of [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], and [[Woundfin]]; a fin-clip marking experiment; staff turnover; and the completion of 20 new ponds by a Youth Conservation Corps (YACC) crew.

## Background & Description

The station was established with a group of six original species in 1974. By FY81 it held a broad set of southwestern natives, most listed under the Endangered Species Act (E), Threatened (T), Proposed (P), Candidate (C), or Undetermined status of special concern (USC). The report indicates the source population of each stock in parentheses.

Staffing changed during the year. Hatchery Manager Jensen attended the Fish Hatchery Managers Course at the Fisheries Academy in Leetown, West Virginia. Theop Inslee served as acting manager, then remained as Chief Fish Culturist. The YACC staff ended at the close of the fiscal year.

Species held (Table 1):

- Native fishes: [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Yaqui Sucker]], [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Chihuahua Chub]] (two populations), [[Yaqui Chub]], [[Woundfin]], [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Bluntnose Shiner]]
- Pupfishes: [[Comanche Springs Pupfish]], [[Leon Springs Pupfish]]
- Livebearers: [[Pecos Gambusia]], [[Big Bend Gambusia]], [[Amistad Gambusia]], [[San Marcos Gambusia]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Yaqui Topminnow]]
- Darter: [[Fountain Darter]]
- Hybrids: [[Gila Hybrid]] I (G. robusta x G. elegans) and Gila Hybrid II (G. cypha x G. elegans)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Most of the work targets recovery of imperiled populations within their historic ranges. Key stocking and transfer activities included:

- **Razorback Sucker:** 7,000 fish planted in the Gila, Salt, and Verde rivers in Arizona, with a stated goal of 100,000 per year over ten years (about 1,000,000 total).
- **Bonytail Chub:** 43,000 juveniles planted in [[Lake Mohave]].
- **Yaqui Chub:** 32,000 fish moved to waters on the San Bernardino Ranch in southeastern Arizona, intended to establish self-sustaining populations within the native range.
- **Colorado Squawfish:** about 20,000 fingerlings reached the small fingerling stage and were transferred to California for rearing, with additional fry and eggs sent to Region 5.

Environmental observations were limited but relevant. Temperature was identified as the likely main cause of losses in [[Big Bend Gambusia]]. Recent flooding in the San Marcos area may have eliminated the only natural population of [[San Marcos Gambusia]]. Gila topminnow populations showed typical seasonal fluctuations, with high numbers in summer and fall and a winter crash.

Other interagency work included assisting the Arizona Game and Fish Department with sampling on the Gila and Salt rivers, searching for [[Bluntnose Shiner]] in the Pecos River with New Mexico Game and Fish, and supporting the [[Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team]].

## Key Findings

### Razorback Sucker (E) — Lake Mohave source
- 134 adults were transported from [[Lake Mohave]] on January 15–16 and arrived ripe. Spawning began January 17.
- Early spawning attempts were discouraging, but continued work produced about 40,000–45,000 fry.
- Fry were fed on size "0" feed progressing to #4; fingerlings were fed at 3% body weight.
- Fish were marked with oxytetracycline before planting.
- A fin-clip experiment removed either the right pelvic or left pelvic fin and cauterized the base with liquid nitrogen. Freeze cauterization was tested to prevent fin regrowth. Preliminary results looked good, but final results await the following spring.
- 75 adults remain. The report recommends obtaining at least 100 additional fish from Lake Mohave.
- Preserved specimens were sent to the Vernal, Utah office of the Colorado River Fishery Project.

### Yaqui Sucker (USC) — Rio Sirupa, Chihuahua, Mexico
- Only two individuals are held. They have never spawned, and their sex has not been determined.
- The report recommends securing additional broodstock from Mexico to start culture work. The alternative is to preserve the two fish and obtain new broodstock later.

### Colorado Squawfish (E) — Willow Beach source
- 271 fish arrived from [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] in March 1980, averaging 14.74 inches and 305 g. By September 1980 they averaged 16.45 inches and 567 g.
- Fish ripened in early June 1981. About 20,000 fingerlings were produced.
- Transfers included 10,000 fry and 5,000 eggs to Joe Valentine in Region 5, and fish sent to California in November 1981.
- On September 24, the station received 100 domestic broodstock, 13 wild broodstock, and 1,000 young-of-the-year fish from Willow Beach.
- The adult broodstock will support a commitment of 100,000 fingerlings in FY82.

### Bonytail Chub (E) — Lake Mohave and Clark County, Nevada
- About 50,000 fry arrived from Willow Beach on May 19 and were placed in Pond 28, then split into four C-series ponds.
- At the October harvest, 43,000 fish weighing 733 pounds were removed, averaging 4.35 inches. They were marked with terramycin before planting in Lake Mohave.
- Ten broodfish were transferred from Willow Beach.

### Chihuahua Chub (PE) — Mimbres River, New Mexico, and Rio Piedras Verde, Mexico
- The Mimbres River stock grew from 10 fish in 1979 to a present high of 102. Most exceed five inches, and the largest are about 12 inches long.
- Successful spawning in the following year could increase the population four- to five-fold.
- The Rio Piedras Verde stock has also increased, though no count is available. Ten adults were originally brought in 1978, and about 30 were seen together at one time.

### Yaqui Chub (C) — Leslie Creek, Cochise County, Arizona
- 225 fish were brought from the Rio Yaqui in 1976. They did extremely well in ponds, and 32,000 were stocked in October 1980.

### Woundfin (E) — Virgin River, Arizona and Nevada
- 240 fish were received in 1979 and placed in Pond D. They did not reproduce. The pond was drained, and 25 fish were moved to the stream habitat, where they reproduced, but most fry washed out through the screens.
- In April 1981, 395 more fish arrived from the Virgin River. Some were placed in the stream, some in Pond D (which had a gravel substrate), some in a fiberglass tank, and some in the tank house for induced-spawning experiments.
- Natural spawning occurred only in Pond D. On May 16, all fish were moved to Pond 5C, including 185 fry from Pond D, which confirmed successful reproduction.

### Beautiful Shiner (C) — Arroyo Moctezuma, Chihuahua, Mexico
- Sixteen fish were brought in June 1978. The population now numbers in the thousands during summer and is held with Chihuahua Chub from Mexico.

### Bluntnose Shiner (C) — Pecos River, Chaves County, New Mexico
- Ten fish were brought in November 1980. In August 1981 the pond was drained, six adults were removed (one died), and no reproduction was observed.
- The five survivors were moved to Pond 4C. More fish are expected from the Pecos River, but the FY82 workload is already full.

### Comanche Springs Pupfish (E) — Giffen Spring, Reeves County, Texas
- About 35 fish were brought in 1974. The population has reached the hundreds of thousands during summer and fall.

### Leon Springs Pupfish (E) — Leon Creek, Pecos County, Texas
- Introduced in 1974. Numbers remain much lower than Comanche Springs Pupfish, mainly due to limited space and competition. The species was moved to Pond 7C.

### Pecos Gambusia (E) — Leon Creek, Pecos County, Texas
- One of the original six species. It has done extremely well and has been very abundant.
- It was removed from the station's inventory at the end of the fiscal year on the recommendation of the Rio Grande Fishes Recovery Team, which also recommended downlisting from endangered to threatened.

### Big Bend Gambusia (E) — Big Bend National Park, Texas
- The station lost stock for three consecutive years. New stock from Big Bend National Park was obtained for the fourth winter.
- Temperature is believed to be the main cause of losses. Fish are now held in a flow-through pond and in a tank inside the holding house.

### Amistad Gambusia (E) — Goodenough Spring, Val Verde County, Texas
- Extinct in the wild. The population has done extremely well and is very abundant.

### San Marcos Gambusia (E) — San Marcos River, Hays County, Texas
- Brought in September 1980. Overwintered well and increased greatly.
- Because flooding may have eliminated the only natural population, the station plans to overwinter this species indoors as a precaution.

### Gila Topminnow (E) — Monkey Springs, Santa Cruz County, Arizona
- 200 fish were brought in 1976. The population shows typical seasonal fluctuations.
- Fish are held in a flow-through pond and in the holding house to supply the FY82 distribution commitment.

### Yaqui Topminnow (E) — Pipe Spring, Cochise County, Arizona
- 200 fish were brought from the San Bernardino Ranch in October 1980. The population is substantial, and the species will be overwintered in the holding house.

### Fountain Darter (E) — San Marcos River, Hays County, Texas
- One of the first six species. Darters reproduced in Pond A, but none were recorded in Pond 7B, and no darters were found when that pond was drained in the fall.
- The San Marcos Recovery Team recommended removing this species from the station, provided any fish remained.

### Gila Hybrids I and II
- Hybrid crosses were made at Willow Beach and shipped as fry in May and June 1980.
- Each hybrid will be spawned in 1982 to test fertility or sterility, including six crosses in total. The results may help clarify the complex Gila problem in the upper Colorado River basin.

### Distribution and Other Activities
- The station shipped about 600 Gila topminnows to Arizona State University and about 100 individuals of several other species to Oklahoma State University and the University of Maryland. Live and preserved specimens also went to USFWS Region 6, Joe Valentine (USFWS, Salt Lake City), and other institutions.
- 511 Socorro isopods (E) were received from New Mexico Game and Fish for holding. They reproduced but died steadily, and all were dead by late October.
- Theop Inslee began bioassays on all species held. Results will be reported later.
- The YACC completed 20 new ponds in early June. Work on a new visitors center was about 65% complete when the program ended in September.
- Cyclical maintenance included a new well, looped water delivery lines for several ponds, and storm windows on all residences.
- Visitor records were incomplete for the first half of the year. Estimated visitation for calendar year 1981 was 500–600.
