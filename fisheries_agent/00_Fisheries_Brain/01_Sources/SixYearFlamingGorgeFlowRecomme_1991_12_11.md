## Executive Summary
This FY92 annual report (Recovery Program Project #18-3) outlines a study led by researchers from the [[Utah Division Of Wildlife Resources]] and [[Utah State University]] to address non-native fish management within the [[Colorado River Fishes Recovery Program]]. The study's primary focus is assessing the ecological threat posed by the invasive [[Northern Pike]] to the endangered [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (referred to in the source by its historical name, "Colorado squawfish") in the [[Green River]], Utah. The research aims to characterize non-native fish distribution drivers, quantify predation pressure via radio telemetry and diet analysis, and develop feasibility strategies for selective non-native fish control.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The study focuses on the interaction between an apex invasive predator and a native endangered species within a specific river reach:

- **Invasive Predator:** [[Northern Pike]] — A non-salmonid, non-native piscivore introduced into the [[Green River]] system.
- **Target Prey/Species of Concern:** Young-of-year (YOY) [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (Colorado squawfish), an endangered [[Cyprinidae|cyprinid]] endemic to the Colorado River Basin.
- **Habitat Zone:** The specific study reach is the Green River between the confluences of the [[Yampa River]] and the [[Duchesne River]].
- **Trophic Interaction:** The central ecological question is whether [[Northern Pike]] predation represents a significant limiting factor on YOY Colorado Pikeminnow survival and recruitment, framed within a "life history and zoogeographical perspective" of non-native fish distribution.
- **Management Context:** This work sits within the broader [[Flaming Gorge Dam]] flow recommendation framework, implying that flow regulation may be a variable influencing non-native species establishment and native fish vulnerability.

- **Objective 1:** Identify and assess factors limiting the distribution and abundance of all non-salmonid, non-native fishes using a life-history and zoogeographic lens.
- **Objective 2:** Evaluate the potential predatory impact of [[Northern Pike]] on YOY [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] in the [[Green River]].

- **Methodology (Task 2) — Pike Impact Assessment:**

  - (b) Assess prey selection and growth rates of tagged Northern Pike.
  - (c) Estimate fish consumption rates required to support observed pike growth parameters (bioenergetics modeling).
