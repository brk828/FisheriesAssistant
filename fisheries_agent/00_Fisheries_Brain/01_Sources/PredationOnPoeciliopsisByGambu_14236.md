# Gambusia Predation on Young Poeciliopsis: Proposal for a Recovery Test

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a letter dated March 25, 1991, from [[Clark Hubbs]], Department of Zoology, University of Texas at Austin, to [[George Devine]] of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Endangered Species Office. It proposes a possible conservation strategy for [[Poeciliopsis]] topminnows in Arizona springs. The idea is to replace local stocks of [[Gambusia Affinis]] (Western Mosquitofish) with the [[Big Bend Gambusia]] population, because Big Bend fish rarely eat young fish. The letter also includes a survivorship table (partly garbled by OCR) showing how young fish fared when exposed to adult Gambusia from several populations. Hubbs recommends controlled predation experiments before any field trial.

## Background & Description

- **Author and recipients:** Clark Hubbs wrote to George Devine, with copies to [[W.L. Minckley]] and [[Dean Hendrickson]].
- **Origin of the idea:** Hubbs states that he and Hendrickson developed the proposal in an informal discussion.
- **Target genus:** [[Poeciliopsis]] topminnows, whose decline has been linked to predation by Gambusia affinis on their young.
- **Focal population:** The Big Bend population of [[Gambusia Affinis]], described as a poor predator of young fish.
- **Other Gambusia taxa in the table:** [[Gambusia Geiseri]] (listed as a predator) and [[Gambusia Speciosa]].

The letter notes that a controlled experiment would require a Poeciliopsis collecting or handling permit, which the author's laboratory did not then hold.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Predation as a driver of decline.** Hubbs states that predation by G. affinis on young Poeciliopsis "has been documented to play a major role" in the decline of the genus. This is the mechanism the proposed management strategy targets.

**Relative predation rates.** The letter compares Big Bend G. affinis with other populations:
- Young are eaten at about half the rate of most populations.
- The rate is about two-thirds that of other populations.
- The rate is an order of magnitude lower than that of one unnamed population.

These comparisons are the basis for the proposed substitution. The letter does not give the underlying sample sizes or statistical tests.

**Geographic range of the comparison.** The survivorship table covers populations ordered "southwest to northeast," including Big Bend, Balmorhea, Diamond-Y, Clear Creek, Junction, Bat Cave, Cow Creek, Fessenden, San Marcos, Woman Hollering, Big Brown and Village Creek. Most of these are Texas sites, which places the test populations across the Texas range of the genus.

**Proposed management context.** The idea is to introduce Big Bend G. affinis into "selected Arizona springs" to replace existing G. affinis stocks. This is a translocation strategy aimed at reducing predation pressure on native Poeciliopsis.

## Key Findings

1. **Core proposal:** Big Bend G. affinis could be used to replace resident G. affinis in selected Arizona springs as a means of supporting Poeciliopsis recovery.
2. **Predation comparison:** Big Bend Gambusia eat young fish at roughly half the rate of most populations, two-thirds the rate of others, and one-tenth the rate of one population (as stated in the letter, "an order of magnitude lower").
3. **Required validation:** Hubbs recommends checking predation rates in controlled laboratory experiments before any field test. This would require a Poeciliopsis permit.
4. **Survivorship data (table):** The table reports percentage survival of young exposed to adult Gambusia from many populations, separated by sex of the adult. Values range widely. For example, Big Bend shows female 67% and male 85% in the first survivorship block, and the highest values reach 100% in several populations. Because the OCR scrambled column alignment, the mapping of values to specific species and sexes cannot be confirmed from this text.
5. **Sample-size coding:** The table legend (bold = more than 100 young; roman = 50 to 100 young; italic = fewer than 50 young) relates to sample size, but the typeface was lost in scanning, so the coding cannot be applied.
6. **Status of the work:** The letter is a proposal and a request for consideration. It reports no completed field trial or experimental results beyond the table.

**Data caveats:** The table is an OCR reconstruction with misaligned columns and missing formatting. The letter gives no species authority for Poeciliopsis, no sample sizes in the text, and no statistical analysis. Values should be verified against the original scan before use.
