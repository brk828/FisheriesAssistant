# The Scoliolegnia asterophora Aggregate (Formerly Saprolegnia asterophora de Bary, Oomycetes)

#oomycetes #saprolegniaceae #taxonomy

## Executive Summary

M. W. Dick (1969, *Botanical Journal of the Linnean Society*, vol. 62, pp. 255–266) re-examined isolates previously referred to [[Saprolegnia Asterophora]] de Bary, a water mould of acidic, organic-rich wetlands. The study resolves the group into an aggregate of three segregate species. Two are described as new, [[Scoliolegnia Subeccentrica]] and [[Scoliolegnia Blelhamensis]], and [[Saprolegnia Asterophora]] is redefined and transferred as [[Scoliolegnia Asterophora]]. All three are placed in a new genus, [[Scoliolegnia]], within the Saprolegniaceae. The author argues that existing genera cannot accommodate the group without distorting their definitions, so a new genus is erected instead.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Scoliolegnia]] is named for the bent, crooked vegetative mycelium (Greek *skolios*), which is also aberrant relative to [[Saprolegnia]]. Its diagnostic features are:

- Delicate, flaccid, flexuous vegetative hyphae that look "gritty" under low-power microscopy.
- Elongate [[Zoosporangia]] whose zoospores swim away from the discharge opening.
- Secondary zoosporangia formed by internal proliferation, basipetally or laterally.
- Thin-walled, unpitted, more or less papillate [[Oogonia]] containing one or more (usually few) [[Oospores]].
- Aplerotic oospores with a thin wall and variable mature internal structure.

The author notes that these characters are not individually sufficient to define the genus. The type species is *Scoliolegnia asterophora*.

**Species profiles**

- ***Scoliolegnia asterophora*** (comb. nov.): Zoosporangia are rare and short, and secondary zoosporangia form by internal proliferation. Oogonia are abundant, regularly papillate, and measure (25)42–56(66) µm. Antheridia are present on more than half the oogonia and arise from a cluster of hyphae below the oogonium. Each oogonium holds one to six oospores, mostly one or two. Oospores measure (17)26–33(44) µm and are subcentric with small oil droplets. The type locality is Germany (de Bary, 1860).
- ***Scoliolegnia subeccentrica*** (sp. nov.): Zoosporangia are abundant, long, cylindrical or clavate, and occasionally contorted. Secondary zoosporangia arise basipetally, by internal proliferation, or by lateral renewal. Oogonia measure (30)39–57(76) µm, and antheridia are usually absent. Oogonia almost always contain a single oospore, which measures (19)28–37(49) µm and is subeccentric. The type locality is Domewood Lake.
- ***Scoliolegnia blelhamensis*** (sp. nov.): Zoosporangia form only in young cultures and are very short, clavate or cylindrical. Secondary zoosporangia form by internal proliferation. Oogonia are extremely variable, from smooth to densely papillate, and measure (26)43–63(92) µm. Antheridia are absent. Each oogonium holds 1–11 oospores, mostly 2–3. Oospores measure (17)26–31(39) µm and are subeccentric. The type locality is Blelham Tarn.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

All three species occur in similar habitats: sphagnum bogs and other wet areas with very low pH and high organic content. The author links the delicate, flexuous hyphae and the very low pH of these habitats to a possible kinship with [[Leptolegnia]], in contrast to the stout, straight hyphae of [[Saprolegnia]] and [[Isoachlya]].

Collection sites named in the paper include:

- **[[Domewood Lake]]:** The north shore at Hedgecourt Farm (O.S. grid ref. TQ 353404) was the most consistent source of the species. It was revisited in May 1967, when three pure cultures were prepared that matched a 1957 isolate. An isolate from the Pickling Yard, Mortimer (O.S. grid ref. SU 640648), collected in March 1968, also matched the Domewood Lake isolates.
- **[[Blelham Tarn]]:** Isolates came from sites 42, 32, 41 and 15, collected between 1962 and 1964 (collection data in Dick, 1966). The isolate from site 42 matched the early accounts of *S. asterophora*. The remaining isolates formed a second variant, which became *S. blelhamensis*.

The author treats the Domewood Lake and Blelham Tarn variants as recognizable morphological entities that persist in nature. Temperature and culture medium affect sporulation and antheridium production. Notably, *S. subeccentrica* antheridium frequency ranged from less than 5% in isolate 292a (glass-distilled water) to substantially more than 50% in isolate 292c (1:1 glass-distilled water and water squeezed from Sphagnum).

## Key Findings

1. **New genus.** [[Scoliolegnia]] Dick, gen. nov. is erected for the three species. The author argues that [[Coker Genus Classification]] schemes, which rely on zoospore behaviour, renewal method, or oospore number, cannot place the group in [[Saprolegnia]] or [[Isoachlya]] without destroying those genera's integrity.

2. **Aggregate concept.** The three forms are segregate species within an aggregate, following the [[Heywood Species Aggregate]] concept. Their vegetative phase is probably diploid, and they are almost exclusively self-fertilized or form oospores parthenogenetically. The author therefore rejects infraspecific rank and retains de Bary's epithet for the aggregate.

3. **Key to species.** The paper separates the species on sexual and asexual characters:
   - Zoosporangia abundant, with production continuing after oogenesis, and oogonia mostly with one oospore: *S. subeccentrica*.
   - Zoosporangia rare or limited to a short period before oogenesis, with oogonia often having two or more oospores: proceed to the next couplet.
     - Antheridia present, oogonia regularly papillate, oospores subcentric: *S. asterophora*.
     - Antheridia absent, oogonia irregularly papillate, oospores subeccentric: *S. blelhamensis*.

4. **Distinguishing traits.**
   - *S. subeccentrica* produces the largest oospores (19–37 µm, versus 17–33 µm in *S. asterophora* and 17–31 µm in *S. blelhamensis*). Its zoosporangial papillae are thick, cup-shaped or flaring.
   - *S. blelhamensis* has the most oospores per oogonium (more than half of its oogonia hold three or more) and extremely variable oogonia.
   - *S. asterophora* is the only species with a subcentric oospore arrangement, and its antheridial branches arise as a convoluted knot. Its zoosporangia are extremely rare.
   - Zoosporangial timing also differs. *S. blelhamensis* forms many zoosporangia in five-day-old cultures, especially near 12°C. *S. subeccentrica* produces abundant zoosporangia at 20°C and continues into 21-day-old cultures.

5. **Culture CBS 180.44.** This isolate (Appendix 2) could not produce zoosporangia despite sub-culturing under varied conditions, and it gave only about 20 oogonia. The author considers it to have "lost character." Its antheridial branch origin and oospore size resemble *S. subeccentrica*, but it cannot be assigned to any of the three species, and it is not designated as a type for a fourth species until a more vigorous culture is obtained.

6. **Nomenclature and type material.** Type and paratype cultures are held in the [[Aquatic Phycomycete Culture Collection]] at Reading University. These include the provisional lectotype of *S. asterophora* (291a), the holotype and paratypes of *S. subeccentrica* (292a–c), and the holotype and paratypes of *S. blelhamensis* (293a–c).

7. **Generic debate.** The author reviews earlier classifications. [[Coker Genus Classification]] (1923) placed *S. asterophora* in a subgenus, *Pseudosaprolegnia*, based on oospore number and papillate oogonia, but the author does not retain that name. [[Cladolegnia]] (Johannes, 1955), defined by one to three oospores per oogonium, is also rejected because it groups unrelated species. The author suggests it might survive as a subgenus of *Isoachlya*.
