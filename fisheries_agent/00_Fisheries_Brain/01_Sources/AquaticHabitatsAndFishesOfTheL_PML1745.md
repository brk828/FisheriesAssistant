# Aquatic Habitats and Fishes of the Palo Verde Division, Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This appendix reports a 1974–75 survey of fishes, benthos, higher aquatic plants, pigments, and physical-chemical conditions in the [[Palo Verde Division]] of the [[Lower Colorado River]]. The division is a channelized, nearly all-flowing reach of about 45 km. Sixteen fish kinds are known from it, 12 of them were taken or observed in this survey, and the mainstream fauna is dominated by [[Red Shiner]] in large numbers. Benthic invertebrate biomass is low over most of the reach because the sandy, unstable bottom supports little, with substantial populations confined to the most downstream reach and to backwaters. The findings supersede an earlier interim report dated 2 May 1975, unless noted otherwise.

## Background & Description

The division extends from the [[Palo Verde Irrigation Diversion Dam]] (PVID) for almost 45 km to Taylor Ferry. Aquatic habitat is almost entirely flowing water, with an estimated total of less than 2.5% backwaters. Surviving lateral habitats are mostly artificial and connect to the channel through culverts that penetrate dikes or levees. A large share of the low banks is armored with rip-rap, levee fill, or other stony material (the percentage is garbled in the scan). The channel runs through a broad valley of finely divided alluvial soils.

Physical setting:
- **Substrate:** More than 60% sand in the mainstream (OCR-read), except near PVID, where scouring leaves gravels and small boulders.
- **Depth:** Water depth at maximum discharge rarely exceeds 3.5 m.
- **Flow:** Currents are relatively uniform because the channel is contained.
- **Water level:** Daily fluctuations of about 1.0 to 1.5 m (OCR-read) are much smaller than seasonal changes.
- **Dynamics:** Sand bars and other channel features change almost daily.

Sampling was done mainly in winter and spring 1974–75.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Fishes were collected by [[Seine Sampling]], [[Hoop Nets]], and [[Gill Netting]], and angler catches were reviewed for January–March 1978. Benthos was sampled with [[Ekman Dredge Sampling]]. Plant cover was assessed along the banks and in backwaters. Pigments and water chemistry were measured at fixed sites, including [[Inflowing Drains]] and [[Backwaters]].

Key habitat contrasts:
- **Mainstream channel:** Dominated by sand and shifting bars, with low benthic biomass.
- **Backwaters:** Hold more fish density, benthic life, and pigment concentrations. Emergent beds of sedge (mostly *Scirpus californicus*) and cattail are common.
- **Inflowing drains:** Carry wastewater with higher conductance and phosphate, and are warmer than the river.

Riparian vegetation was sparse. Mesquite, arrowweed (*Baccharis* spp.), and other woody plants and forbs grew on modified banks, while cottonwood and willow were rare and restricted to unmodified habitats. Submergent plants (sago pondweed, coontail, and sparse *Chara* spp.) occurred only among emergent beds. The most extensive vegetation was in the lowermost reach. Drift of organic material was negligible during the study.

## Key Findings

### Fishes
- **Species count:** 16 fish kinds are known from the division. Twelve were caught or observed in this survey, and three had not previously been recorded from the division.
- **Species list:** The source table lists introduced and native kinds, including [[Threadfin Shad]], carp, [[Red Shiner]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Flathead Catfish]], brown bullhead, [[Mosquitofish]], sailfin molly, [[Striped Bass]], [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], green sunfish, [[Bluegill]], [[Redear Sunfish]], black crappie, tilapia, and striped mullet. Striped mullet and striped bass were not taken in this survey; specimens were obtained from other collectors, and striped bass were observed jumping against PVID in 1974.
- **Seining, mainstream:** Almost 15,000 individuals, about 4.7 kg/ha (OCR-read). Five species were taken: red shiner, bluegill, mosquitofish, and equal numbers of redear sunfish and largemouth bass.
- **Seining, backwaters:** About 5,500 individuals per ha (OCR-read). Red shiner and mosquitofish were nearly equal in number, followed by bluegill, channel catfish, redear sunfish, and largemouth bass.
- **Segment densities:** About 32,000, 20,500, and 7,800 fish/ha in three river-mile segments (OCR-read). Red shiner made up most of the catch in each segment. Its numbers were lower in the channel than in backwaters.
- **Larger fishes:** Uncommon in the channel, more abundant in backwaters and in intermediate inflowing drains.
- **Hoop nets:** Set only in the channel habitat, 17 days plus 35 nights, with no catches.
- **Gill nets:** No fish caught in daytime. At night in the mainstream, largemouth bass, channel catfish, bluegill, and threadfin shad were taken, with an average of about 2.8 fish weighing 2.3 kg per set (OCR-read).
- **Inflowing drains:** About 0.75 fish per day and about the same per night as the channel. Weights were 0.65 kg per day and 3.27 kg per night (OCR-read).
- **Backwater gill nets:** Threadfin shad, channel catfish, flathead catfish, and largemouth bass were prominent. No gill nets were set in daylight.
- **Angler catches (Jan–Mar 1978):** Smallmouth bass, channel catfish, largemouth and smallmouth bass, green sunfish, and bluegill were recorded.

### Benthos
- **Mainstream:** Twenty-one Ekman dredge samples yielded 9 taxa. Numbers per m² ranged from none to a few hundred, averaging about 120.
- **Biomass by segment:** In 2 of the 4 segments, average weight was below 0.8 kg/ha. Excluding *Corbicula* clams and large dragonfly larvae, samples in one segment (about 1.8 kg/ha) matched the others, with less than 0.2 kg/ha of chironomid midges and oligochaete worms.
- **Most downstream reach:** The only place with substantial numbers and weights, about 4.5 kg/ha (OCR-read).
- **Backwaters:** Eleven samples had a fauna similar in composition to the channel, with 110 to 1,450 individuals per m² (OCR-read) and 0.09 to about 179.5 kg/ha. Means were 474/m² (9 samples) and 716/m² with 67.2 kg/ha (2 samples dominated by *Corbicula*). Higher counts were found on sand-silt bottoms mixed with organic debris.
- **Diet note:** Smallmouth bass stomachs from January–March 1975 contained adult mayflies, but no mayfly larvae were found in bottom samples.

### Higher Aquatic Plants and Algae
- Sparse riparian cover, with plants often typical of adjacent terraces rather than true riparian habitat.
- Sedges, cattail, and a few sparse stands of giant reed occupied limited bank sections.
- Backwaters held extensive beds of sedge and cattail.

### Pigments
- Chlorophyll A increased gradually from upstream to downstream across the 23–24 hour sampling, but the average did not change by a level of magnitude. Other pigments fluctuated without a clear pattern.
- A grab sample near PVID on 9 March was higher than the 1 March sample there, and matched downstream data. The authors judged that the gradient reflected a basin-wide upstream change rather than a local influence.
- Samples from inflowing drains were richer in pigments than river water.
- Backwaters held substantially more pigment than the mainstream, with many chlorophyll A values in level III and a few in level IV.
- Little diel cycling was seen. Surface and bottom samples (to about 2–3 m) differed, suggesting active movement of pigment-carrying organisms in the water column.

### Physical-Chemical Conditions
- **Temperature:** Mainstream about 11.6 to 23 °C (OCR-read) between March and May 1975. Backwaters were similar, and drains were consistently 2 to 4 °C warmer. Daytime temperatures (14:00–18:00) were 3.5 to 4.? °C higher than at night (OCR-garbled).
- **Dissolved oxygen:** Relatively high and tracking temperature. At RM 590.0, the mean was 9.9 mg/L (range 8.2–12.3 mg/L), about 1 mg/L below 100% saturation (10.8 mg/L at 13.8 °C). Drain waters had lower oxygen than the mainstream.
- **Conductance:** Mainstream averaged slightly above 1,000 µmhos/cm (range 800–1,350). Wastewater entering the reach measured 1,500–1,900 µmhos/cm, and its influence was detected for several kilometers downstream.
- **pH:** 7.2–9.2 in the river, mostly 8.0–8.3. Drains were more stable, at 7.8–8.0.
- **Turbidity:** About 14 JTU in river samples, similar in drains, and scarcely detected in backwaters.
- **Phosphate (PO4-P):** River 0.05–0.07 mg/L, rising to 0.12–0.16 mg/L below drain inflows. Drains ranged 0.15–1.00 mg/L in 5 samples. Two backwater samples showed depletion (none detected to 0.01 mg/L), perhaps from dense phytoplankton. No nitrate-nitrogen data were obtained.
