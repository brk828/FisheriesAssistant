# Fish Monitoring of Palm Lake, Hassayampa River Preserve (1990)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 23 May 1990 memorandum from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] reports a fish monitoring survey of [[Palm Lake]] at the [[Hassayampa River Preserve]]. Written by [[Dean Hendrickson]], Nongame Fish Biologist, to interested parties, it documents captures of two endangered-status native species, [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]], along with [[Desert Pupfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]]. The fish appeared healthy and several razorback suckers were in ripe, spawning-ready condition. The memo also notes that a position under [[Francisco Abarca]] will be involved in follow-up work, with relocation to riverine habitat on the Salt and/or Verde rivers mentioned as a possibility.

## Background & Description

The memo is a short field report, not a full research paper. It covers:

- **Target species:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*), both native Arizona fishes of conservation concern. The memo describes the razorback sucker stocking and monitoring context in this lake.
- **Other species captured:** [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ameiurus natalis*), and a topminnow, which was seined but not identified to species in the scanned text.
- **Sampling methods:**
  - [[Trammel Net]] sets: two nets, 50 ft and 75 ft long, placed across the deepest section of the lake and checked at intervals through the day.
  - [[Seining]] and [[Backpack Electrofishing]] (described as "shocking"), used in shallow and vegetated margins.
- **Data tables:** Table 1 gives trammel net catches by set time, species and totals. Table 2 gives length and weight ranges and averages for bonytail chub and razorback sucker. Scan quality is poor, so only legible values are reported in this note.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Palm Lake and nearby springs contain deep open water and heavily vegetated shallows. Trammel nets were set in the deepest area, while seining and electrofishing covered the vegetated margins.
- **Fish condition:** Fish caught appeared generally healthy and free of external parasites.
- **Reproduction:** Several razorback suckers were in running-ripe condition, suggesting active spawning readiness during the survey.
- **Diet:** Stomach contents of some captured fish included crayfish, indicating that crayfish are a food resource in the lake.
- **Relocation context:** The memo mentions that the Nongame Fish Biology position may move fish to riverine habitat on the [[Salt River]] and/or [[Verde River]], which places the lake population within a wider Arizona conservation plan.

## Key Findings

- **Trammel net captures:** Nine [[Razorback Sucker]] and a number of [[Bonytail Chub]] were caught in the trammel nets. Table 1 gives set-by-set totals; the bonytail total is legible as 29 in the scan. One razorback sucker escaped from the net.
- **Size data (Table 2):** Legible values for bonytail chub include a total length range of 290–395 mm (average 318.9 mm), and for razorback sucker a total length range of 395–511 mm (average 447.0 mm) with weights of roughly 354–1530 g (average 1202.7 g). Some weight values are partly illegible, so treat these as approximate.
- **Stocking-related sample:** One group of fish, averaging 101.5 mm TL, is mentioned in the memo. The scanned text does not make clear which group this is.
- **Seining and electrofishing:** Five adult female [[Desert Pupfish]] were captured in the pool sampled by seining and backpack electrofishing. A single topminnow was also seined.
- **Yellow Bullhead:** Some [[Yellow Bullhead]] were captured. The scan does not give a reliable count.
- **Health and reproduction:** Captured fish appeared healthy and free of external parasites. Razorback suckers in running-ripe condition were noted.
- **Diet:** Stomach contents of some fish were mostly crayfish, and at least one female was described as heavy with eggs.
- **Follow-up:** The memo indicates continued monitoring under the Nongame Fish Biology position, with possible relocation of fish to the Salt and/or Verde rivers.
