# Annual Report of the Hydrobiological, Ichthyological, Fisheries, Fish Disease and Waterfowl Research Units, Brno (1967)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This annual report, issued in Brno in April 1968 by the Hydrobiological and Ichthyological Section of the [[Natural History Club]] in Brno, summarises the 1967 research of several university and institute units in Czechoslovakia. The work covers stream and reservoir ecology, brown trout population dynamics, fish reproduction and rearing, pond culture, fish parasitology and diseases, waterfowl ecology and lamprey biology. Most fieldwork was in the [[Loutka Creek]], the [[Beskydy Mountains]] streams, and [[Dyje River]] floodplain sites. Fieldwork in Ghana and Yugoslavia is also reported. The report lists staff activities and an extensive set of 1967 publications.

## Background & Description

The report is organised by unit, each with its staff, ongoing projects and publications:

- **Chair of Zoology and Anthropology, Hydrobiological Division, Brno**: zoobenthos, drift, and trout stream studies (e.g., Prof. S. Hrabě on Oligochaeta and Chironomidae taxonomy).
- **Institute of Vertebrate Zoology, Czechoslovak Academy of Sciences, Department of Ichthyology, Brno**: trout population studies, electrofishing methods, and experimental manipulation of fish density in streams.
- **Institute of Vertebrate Zoology, Department of Experimental Ecology, Studenec-Třebíč**: embryonic development of [[Chub]] and early rearing of waterfowl.
- **Chair of Fisheries and Hydrobiology, University of Agriculture, Brno**: growth, fecundity and technology of carp, whitefish and wels catfish.
- **Biological Station, Lednice na Moravě**: carp fry intensification in ponds, plankton, and waterfowl counts.
- **Moravian Museum, Department of Zoology, Brno**: goose and mallard ecology on the Dyje floodplain and lamprey life cycles.
- **Chair of Parasitology and Invasive Diseases, Veterinary Faculty, Brno**: fish parasites, trematodes, and fish disease.

The report is written in a mix of English, German, Czech and Slovak, and includes many OCR-degraded passages. Staff names are given with academic titles and no contact details.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Stream benthos and drift (Loutka Creek).** Quantitative zoobenthos sampling was carried out 1964–1966 with 47 samplings at three localities. Average biomass was 13.3 g/m² and average abundance 1,086 individuals/m². Caddis flies dominated biomass and mayflies dominated abundance. The authors attributed differences among localities to anthropogenic interference with the stream.

**Drift.** Drifting organisms were classed as planktonic, rheobenthic, or indifferent. Abundance ranged from 10 to 1,220 individuals per m³, and the number of taxa decreased downstream. Macrobenthic drift supplying trout food varied between 24% and 33%.

**Reservoir effects.** Two river valley reservoirs were studied for their effects on trout stream biology. Work on the [[Vir Valley Reservoir]] examined the Svratka River below the dam. A separate study examined Tilapia during the final filling phase of the [[Volta River Reservoir]] in Ghana.

**Trout streams in the Beskydy Mountains.** Two streams were manipulated: two sections were kept fish-free, one had its stock increased many times, and two were left in natural condition. Abiotic factors, zoobenthos, drift, bottom vegetation and fish diet were monitored.

**Ghana and Yugoslavia.** In Ghana, gill-net selectivity was tested and a 3-inch mesh was judged most satisfactory for Tilapia. Vertical fish distribution was studied in a stream entering Lake Bosuntwi, reported as a first in West Africa. Lungs of [[Lungfish]] and [[Bichir]] were examined. In Yugoslavia, lamprey larvae near Sarajevo could not be identified with certainty as *Lampetra mariae*.

## Key Findings

**Brown trout population (Loutka Creek).**
- Lifespan is short, about 4 to 5 years.
- 30% of males mature at age 1+, and more than 75% of individuals are mature by age 2+.
- Females slightly predominate in sex ratio (53.6%).
- Average absolute fecundity was 281 eggs per female; relative fecundity was 2,300 eggs per kg of female body weight.
- Average production of age groups II and III in the lower Loutka Creek was 21.2 kg/ha (three-year average). Three-year-old production was about one quarter that of two-year-olds.
- Angling harvest of the 1963 year class was estimated at 19% of biomass.

**Trout feeding.** Stomachs were fullest in April. From May to October, airborne organisms, caddis larvae (*Hydropsyche*-type, "Trichoptera") and mayfly larvae (*Baetis*) were the main prey. Feeding occurred in several rations over 24 hours and increased with daily maximum temperature.

**Chub development.** Incubation at 18 °C lasted 75.4 to 107.3 hours. It varied mainly with the female parent, with little influence from the male. Early mortality was attributed mainly to intrinsic factors.

**Carp and pond culture.**
- Carp fry reared at 49,000 individuals per hectare yielded 13 q/ha in seven ponds of 0.5 to 1 ha, using N+P fertilisers and granulated feed.
- Carp growth, fecundity and fat content were studied. Scaled carp grew faster in their third year.
- Ammoniacal water at two concentrations gave results similar to ammonium sulphate in plankton and fish tests.

**Other fish studies.**
- Wels catfish (*Silurus glanis*): growth, fecundity, and maturity indices were studied.
- European whitefish (*Coregonus lavaretus*): technological losses during processing and sperm quality were assessed.
- Lampreys: a *Lampetra fluviatilis* f. *praecox* larva metamorphosed in August 1967, faster than other central European lampreys (1 to 1.5 months for sucker and teeth development). Spawning was inferred for spring 1968.

**Waterfowl.** Monthly censuses (September–April) were made in 146 localities, with less systematic counts in 190 more. Hand-rearing of wild duck was refined and graylag goose and mallard ecology was studied on the Dyje floodplain.

**Parasitology.** Seasonal dynamics and pathogenicity of monogenean trematodes and sanguinicolosis were investigated in carp fry. Parasite faunas of cyprinid and predatory fishes were surveyed in the Kníničky reservoir. Snail intermediate hosts of trematodes were surveyed in the flooded Dyje area, and control methods were tested.

**Research methods.** Electrofishing was evaluated for population estimates, with [[Successive Removal Electrofishing]] proposed as a reliable method for small streams. Methods for IBP fish-production studies were also standardised.
