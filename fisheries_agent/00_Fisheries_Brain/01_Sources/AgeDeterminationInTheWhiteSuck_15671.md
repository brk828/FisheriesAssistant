# Age Determination in the White Sucker

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study by [[R. J. Beamish]] and [[H. H. Harvey]] (University of Toronto) tests whether [[Scale Annuli]] reliably age [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*). The authors compare scale ages with ages from thin sections of pectoral fin rays, a method that does not require killing the fish. Work was done in [[George Lake]], Ontario. Scales agreed with fin-ray ages up to about 5 years, but became progressively less reliable beyond that, and after 8 years scale ages were always lower than fin-ray ages, sometimes by as much as 5 years. The authors advise verifying scale ages with an independent technique for older suckers.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** [[White Sucker]], a catostomid fish. The related [[Longnose Sucker]] (*Catostomus catostomus*) is also discussed.
- **Aging basis:** Fish are generally aged from annual growth marks on bony structures such as scales, spines, bones, or otoliths.
- **Prior literature on scale reliability was contradictory:**
  - [[Stewart 1926]] had difficulty identifying annuli beyond the 7th year.
  - [[Spoor 1939]] considered scale annuli recognizable and formed once a year, between October and May.
  - [[Harris 1962]] aged longnose suckers to 19 years using scales.
  - [[Ovchynnyk 1965]] found high variability in annuli and doubted scale reliability.
  - [[Geen et al. 1966]] reported that scale-based estimates were low when checked against marked recaptures.
  - [[Coble 1967]] found that an annulus was not formed every year.
- **Fin-ray method:** Introduced by [[Scidmore 1953]]; the approach was chosen because it does not require killing the fish, unlike bone-based techniques.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study site:** [[George Lake]], an oligotrophic lake at about 46°2′N and 81°24′W in the Cloche Mountains of Ontario.
  - Maximum depth: 39.7 m (130 ft)
  - Mean depth: 18.4 m (60.4 ft)
  - Surface area: 182 ha (0.702 sq mi)
  - The lake supports a modest population of relatively large suckers.
- **Population context:** The study addressed factors influencing age and size at maturity in white suckers. Data collected included length, weight, sex, and maturity.
- **Sampling design:**
  - 250 suckers were sampled in September and October 1967. Each was tagged with numbered spaghetti tubing and had four pectoral rays clipped.
  - 25 of these tagged fish were recaptured in September 1968, one year later.
  - A separate set of 157 fish was aged by scales and fin rays for comparison.
- **Scale sampling:** Scales were taken above the lateral line, just posterior to the dorsal fin base, and read from acetate impressions.

## Key Findings

1. **Fin-ray method validated by recapture:** Of the 25 recaptured fish, 22 showed clearly one additional annulus after a year. Three fish were ambiguous: two large fish could not be read easily, and one had an uncertain annulus count.
2. **Recapture age range:** Recaptured fish were aged 2 to 12 years when tagged.
3. **Method details:** Fin rays were sectioned at about 0.5 mm thickness using a jeweler's saw, embedded in epoxy and mounted in Permount. The first annulus is hard to identify in old fish.
4. **Scale reliability depends on age:**
   - Scale and fin-ray ages agree for fish up to about 5 years.
   - Beyond 5 years, scale annuli are progressively fewer.
   - Among fish over 8 years, scale age was never equal to fin-ray age.
   - Scale age was sometimes up to 5 years lower than fin-ray age.
   - Example: fish aged 10 by fin rays were read as 5+, 5+, 6, 7+, 8+, and 9 years by scales.
5. **Error is not proportional:** Scale error was not consistent across the 0.5 to 14 year range, and was not proportional to age.
6. **Seasonal effect:** Early-spring fish showed an incomplete annulus on scales, which made scale ages about a fractional year lower than fin-ray ages until new growth appeared by fall.
7. **Comparison with prior studies:** Earlier scale-based studies such as [[Raney and Webster 1942]] and [[Eddy and Carlander 1942]] mostly sampled fish under about 7 years, where scales are reliable. This may explain why workers disagreed about scale reliability.
8. **Conclusion:** Scales are reliable in this population up to about 5 years. For older white suckers, scales have little value for age determination, so scale ages should be verified with [[Fin-Ray Sectioning]] or bone annuli.
