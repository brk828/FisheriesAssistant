# Eagle Creek Fisheries Survey, Greenlee County, Arizona (June 1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

A fisheries survey of [[Eagle Creek]] in [[Greenlee County]], Arizona, was conducted June 23–25, 1997, by [[Arizona State University]] researchers Brian Bagley and Paul Marsh. Seven sites were sampled from [[Honeymoon Campground]] to 1.2 km (0.75 mi) below the Eagle Creek pump station. Fifteen fish species were collected. The survey documented a sharp contrast in fish communities above and below the [[PD Diversion Dam]], with native fishes dominant above the structure and non-native fishes dominant below it. The federally threatened [[Loach Minnow]] was present only in very low numbers, apparently displaced by habitat degradation at its last known location. [[Spikedace]] was not found and has not been collected since 1989.

## Background & Description

The survey used a [[Smith-Root]] DC [[Backpack Electrofishing]] unit, dip nets, and seines. Catch was standardized as [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] (CPE), expressed as fish caught per minute of shocking time.

Species collected (15 total):
- **Native (per document, 7 above the dam):** [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Gila Chub]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Speckled Dace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Desert Sucker]]
- **Non-native (per document):** [[Rainbow Trout]], [[Carp]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Fathead Minnow]], [[Channel Catfish]], [[Yellow Bullhead]], [[Mosquitofish]], [[Smallmouth Bass]]

The document states that 11 of these species were encountered on previous trips. [[Crayfish]] were present at all sampling locations.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Effect of the PD Diversion Dam.** Seven native and five non-native species were collected above the dam. Below it, seven non-native species and only one native species (Sonora Sucker) were taken.

- Native CPE above the dam ranged from 4.69 to 75.72 fish/min (the table values run to 15.72; the discrepancy is in the source). Below the dam, native CPE was 0.29–0.30 fish/min.
- Non-native CPE was 0.05–1.06 fish/min above and 1.86–5.69 fish/min below.
- The native-to-non-native CPE ratio was reported as roughly 5.7–31.4:1 above the dam (OCR-garbled in source) and 0.05–0.16:1 below.

**Loach Minnow habitat.** The area around the first road crossing below Honeymoon Campground is the only known Eagle Creek location for the federally threatened loach minnow. The species was rediscovered there in 1994, when most fish came from the riffle created by the road crossing. In 1997 that riffle had lost the habitat the species prefers, and none were found there. Only two specimens were collected, from a riffle 100 m downstream, after considerable effort. Changes since earlier collections included:
- uniform flattening and widening of the channel, with reduced depth
- greater substrate imbrication and embeddedness
- a larger proportion of fine sediment

The authors noted similar substrate changes at all other downstream sites.

**Spikedace.** A focused search of the P-Bar Ranch area covered 2.4 km (1.5 mi) of creek with numerous sampling locations, and no spikedace were found. The authors believe the species still inhabits parts of Eagle Creek.

**Smallmouth Bass.** Juveniles were more common than in previous years, which the authors interpret as indicating a favorable spring in 1997.

## Key Findings

1. **Fifteen species** were collected across seven sites. Eleven had been recorded on previous trips.
2. **Strong upstream–downstream divide.** Native fishes were far more abundant above the [[PD Diversion Dam]]. Below it, the community was dominated by non-native species.
3. **Loach Minnow decline at its only known site.** Habitat modification at the road crossing displaced the species. Only two individuals were found, which points to the crossing's maintenance as a significant factor for the local population. Future sampling is planned to define the population's geographic extent.
4. **Spikedace not detected** since 1989, despite targeted effort at P-Bar Ranch. The species is presumed to persist in parts of the creek.
5. **Smallmouth Bass recruitment** improved in 1997, with more juveniles than in prior years.
6. **Crayfish** were present at every sampling location.
