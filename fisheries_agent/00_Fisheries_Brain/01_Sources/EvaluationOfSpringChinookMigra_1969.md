# Evaluation of Spring Chinook Migrants at Rapid River Hatchery, 1969

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This 1969 study conducted at [[Rapid River Hatchery]] near Riggins, Idaho evaluated the survival, migration, and management of [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] (*Oncorhynchus tshawytscha*) using liquid nitrogen branding and fin-clipping techniques. Approximately 75,000 of 900,000 hatchery-released smolts were marked to assess downstream survival to [[Ice Harbor Dam]], yielding an estimated 28% survival rate. Upstream, 3,907 adult spring Chinook and 215 native summer Chinook returned to the Rapid River trap. The study also evaluated a transplant program moving surplus jacks and adults into the [[Little Salmon River]] to create a put-and-take fishery, finding only 22% of transplants returned to the trap and roughly 12% were harvested by anglers. Substantial mortality was documented among Snake River adults transported to the hatchery for spawning (68% unspawned mortality) compared to native Rapid River returnees (22%). The report also details hatchery spawning statistics, egg survival rates, and comparative efficiency of nitrogen branding versus fin-clipping as identification methods.

## Background & Description

- **Smolt size (downstream migrants):** Marked smolts ranged 94–146 mm fork length, averaging **118 mm** at release; by the time of capture at Ice Harbor, average fork length increased to **129 mm**.
- **Adult returnees (spring Chinook, Rapid River hatchery stock):**
  - 3-year-old jacks: average **18 inches** (length range 11–22 in)
  - 4-year-olds: average **29 inches** (length range 22–32 in)
  - 5-year-olds: average **35 inches** (length range 32–41 in)
- **Age-class composition of the 3,907 spring Chinook run:**
  - 28% three-year-old jacks
  - 67% four-year-olds
  - 5% five-year-olds
- **Transported Snake River adults (672 total):** 92% were 4-year-olds; 8% were 5-year-olds.
- **Summer (native) Chinook (215 fish):** 36% three-year-olds, 35% four-year-olds, 29% five-year-olds.
- **Marking methods compared:**
  - Liquid nitrogen branding (letter "R"), applied at -196°C, produced distinctive black brands visible after 3–4 days.
  - Left ventral (LV) fin clip.
  - Combination brand + clip.
  - Branding application rate: 781 fish/hour vs. 625 fish/hour (fin clip) vs. 347 fish/hour (combination).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Anadromous migratory corridor:** [[Chinook Salmon]] smolts emigrated from Rapid River Hatchery between March 26–April 9, 1969, with peak emigration April 6–8, transiting downstream through the [[Snake River]] and [[Columbia River]] system to [[Ice Harbor Dam]], where peak arrival was recorded April 29.
- **Spawning habitat:** Adults returned to the Rapid River trap between June 6 and August 31, 1969, segregated temporally into a "spring (hatchery)" run and a "summer (native)" run, the latter released upstream to preserve wild spawning stock.
- **Transplant fishery habitat:** Surplus jacks and adults were relocated to eight sites spanning the [[Little Salmon River]] and [[Main Salmon River]], ranging from near-trap sites (Spring Bar, downriver) to distant upriver sites (Zim's Resort, 28 miles upriver; Hazard Creek, 17 miles), simulating a put-and-take recreational fishery independent of natal spawning grounds.
- **Trap-return behavior:** Transplanted fish exhibited variable homing/downstream drift behavior back to the trap, with residency in the Little Salmon River ranging 5–54 days (average 15 days) before recapture at the trap.
- **Interaction with hydroelectric infrastructure:** Downstream survival evaluation relied on fish counting facilities at Ice Harbor Dam, highlighting the ecological pressure of Snake/Columbia River dam passage on juvenile Chinook survival.

## Key Findings

### Downstream Migrant Survival
- ~900,000 smolts (including 75,000 marked) emigrated from the hatchery; estimated **28% survival** to Ice Harbor Dam (~252,000 fish of the total release).
- Clipped-only (LV) fish were undercounted by observers at Ice Harbor (~50% miss rate); clipped and branded fish showed similar survival (211 vs. 237 recaptures).
- Marking rates: nitrogen branding was most efficient at 781 fish/hour; combination marking was slowest (347/hour) and costliest in labor.

### Adult Returns & Enumeration
- 2,817 adults + 1,090 jacks = **3,907 total spring Chinook** counted at the trap (June 6–July 22).
- 215 native summer Chinook (77 jacks, 128 adults) were counted and released upstream (July 23–August 31).
- Only 138 surplus adults were used in the transplant program.

### Spawning Mortality
- Of 2,817 spring Chinook adults: 1,659 females, 1,158 males.
- **19% of females (310)** and **3% of males (32)** died prior to/during spawning.
- 2,337 fish were successfully spawned (87%); 155 spawned-out males released alive.

### Transported (Snake River) Adults
- 672 adults transported from Hells Canyon dams to Rapid River Hatchery.
- **68% unspawned mortality** among transported fish (143 of 209 females; 315 of 463 males died before spawning) — markedly higher than the 22% mortality of native Rapid River returnees.

### Egg-Take and Survival
- 1,415 females spawned (1,349 Rapid River + 66 Snake River origin).
- Total egg collection: **5,171,697 eggs**, averaging 3,655 eggs/female.
- 93% survival to eyed-egg stage (4,816,401 eyed eggs); 497,610 eggs shipped to [[Dworshak Hatchery]], leaving 4,318,791 at Rapid River.
- 100% survival from eyed-egg to fry stage.

### Transplant Program Performance
- 1,200 of 1,228 available surplus jacks/adults tagged with Floy T-bar tags and transplanted to 8 sites on Little Salmon and Main Salmon Rivers.
- Only **2 of 1,200** fish died during transport (negligible transportation mortality).
- **267 fish (22%) returned to the trap** at least once; some returned multiple times (up to 5 times for one individual).
- June-planted fish showed higher trap-return rates (25%) than July-planted fish (7%).
- Average residency before trap return: 15 days; Main Salmon River (Spring Bar) plants returned fastest.
- Hazard Creek site received 61% of all transplants (maximum distance, 15 miles upriver).

### Angler Harvest of Transplants
- Minimum of 87 (7%) transplants confirmed caught by anglers via tag returns; expanded estimate suggests **~141 fish (12%)** were harvested overall.
- Average time-at-large before angler capture: 9 days (range 1–47 days).
- Peak catch period was June, with 78 of 87 confirmed catches occurring then, and 54 of these near the Hazard Creek plant site.

### Program Cost
- Total transplant program cost: **$564** (labor + transportation), reflecting modest expenditure relative to fishery benefit generated.

### Marking Technique Evaluation
- Liquid nitrogen branding using a 110-liter insulated Norco container transferred via intermediary 1-liter and 350 ml foam containers proved efficient, safe, and cost-effective (~$9.50 for 50 liters, albeit with wastage).
- Nitrogen brands avoided the ice/slime buildup issues seen in thermal or alcohol-dry ice cold-branding methods (cf. [[Groves and Novotny 1965]]; [[Mighell 1968]]).
