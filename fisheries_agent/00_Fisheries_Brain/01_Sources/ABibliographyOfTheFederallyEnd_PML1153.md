# Bibliography of the Federally Endangered Bonytail Chub (*Gila elegans*) in the Colorado River of the American Southwest

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a annotated-style reference bibliography, prepared June 1995 by Terry C. Inman, [[Paul C. Marsh]] and [[Wendell L. Minckley]] of Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies and Department of Zoology. It was commissioned by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker, Arizona) and the ASU Hayden Library Special Collections. Its purpose is to compile the scientific, management, regulatory and correspondence literature on the federally endangered [[Bonytail Chub]] (*Gila elegans*) of the [[Colorado River Basin]].

The bibliography runs to 12 alphabetical volumes (Abbott 1861 through Wydoski et al. 1991) and is followed by supplementary lists, drawn from C.O. Minckley's humpback chub bibliography, that are organized by volume number. It contains no original data; its value lies in the breadth of sources it indexes, from 19th-century taxonomy to 1990s recovery-program memoranda.

## Background & Description

The bonytail chub was described by Baird and Girard in 1853 and is one of several large-bodied, endemic minnows of the Colorado River system. The bibliography repeatedly places it alongside the [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*), [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) and [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) as the "big river fishes" listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973.

Taxonomic work is a recurring theme. Sources cover the systematics of the *Gila* complex (Holden and Stalnaker 1970; Miller 1945; Rinne 1976; Rosenfeld 1991; Starnes 1989–1990), hybridization among chubs (Dowling and DeMarais 1993; DeMarais et al. 1992), and the identification of larvae and early juveniles (Muth 1988; Muth et al. 1985; Douglas et al. 1989). Starnes's Colorado River Basin *Gila* taxonomy project and Marsh's 1995 review in *Environmental Biology of Fishes* ("Threatened fishes of the world: *Gila elegans*") are among the most directly relevant items.

The document also records that the bibliography's earliest-dated entries include Abbott (1861) and the Baird and Girard series (1853–1854), and that 1970s–1980s federal rulemaking (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 1978 proposed endangered status; 1980 determination as endangered) formalized the species' protected status.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The bibliography's sources frame the bonytail chub's decline in terms of several pressures:

- **Reservoir and dam impacts.** Impoundments and regulated flows are central, including [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations (Spear 1986; Reynolds 1988; Spiller 1991, 1993), [[Flaming Gorge Reservoir]] and Green River studies (Holden and Crist 1981; Tyus and Karp 1989, 1991), and tailwater ecology (Walburg et al. 1981; Valdez 1992).
- **Lake Mohave and Lake Havasu populations.** Surveys and reproductive studies are numerous (Bozek et al. 1984; Marsh and Minckley 1989, 1992; Brooks 1987; Baucom 1991, 1993). Hutcheson (1993) documents the loss of Lake Mohave bonytail.
- **Non-native species and stocking.** Records of non-native fish introductions, including [[Rainbow Smelt]] in Lake Powell (Gustaveson 1989), stocking protocols (Hamill 1994), and Lake Mohave stocking histories (Brooks 1986), are indexed.
- **Contaminants.** Toxicity studies of carbaryl and malathion (Beyers et al. 1994), toxaphene (Hemphill 1954), and contaminant effects in the upper basin (Hamilton and Buhl 1992; Holden 1991, on Green River poisoning) appear.
- **Habitat requirements and flow needs.** Habitat tolerance and preference work (Bulkley et al. 1981), instream flow studies (Stanford 1994), and Yampa and Green River habitat use (Tyus and Karp 1989, 1991) are indexed.
- **Genetic and hatchery concerns.** Allozyme variation and broodstock origin (Minckley et al. 1989; Echelle 1991) are linked to hatchery culture at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]], where mortality events, including a March 23, 1992 fish loss (Jensen 1992; Frazier 1992), are documented.

Sampling and rediscovery records cover sites including [[Lake Mohave]], [[Lake Havasu]], Palm Lake, the ASU Research Park, Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge, [[Grand Canyon]], Cataract Canyon, the Green and Yampa rivers, and the Salton Sea/Lake Cahuilla.

## Key Findings

The document is a bibliography rather than a study, so its "findings" are its scope and the main research and management threads it indexes:

1. **Scale of the literature.** Twelve main volumes plus supplementary lists index a broad body of work from 1853 onward, including journal articles, agency reports, memoranda, correspondence, newspaper items and theses.

2. **Core research threads.**
   - Systematics and hybridization of the *Gila* complex.
   - Reproduction, including temperature effects on embryo survival (Marsh 1982, 1985; Marsh and Pisano 1982).
   - Hatchery culture and induced spawning (Hamman 1981, 1982, 1985; Jensen 1983, 1993).
   - Population monitoring in Lake Mohave and the upper basin.
   - Habitat use, flow needs and contaminants.

3. **Recovery program records.** Entries document the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Colorado River recovery effort, including the Recovery Implementation Program (1989), the bonytail revised recovery plan (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service 1990, 35 pages), the five-year roadmap (1990), and Section 7 consultation materials (Hoover Dam powerplant modification; Glen Canyon Dam biological opinion).

4. **Management controversies.** Correspondence and press items address the proposed experimental nonessential reintroduction (Earnest 1988), the authority of the Fish and Wildlife Service over recovery goals (Buterbaugh 1989), critical habitat designation (Connolly 1994; Pearson et al. 1994), and disputes over West Slope fish plans (Saile 1994).

5. **Gaps the compiler flags.** Several entries are marked "not available," and the humpback chub material from Minckley's bibliography was deliberately not reduplicated, so readers should consult that companion work for the humpback chub literature.

6. **Key reference points for future work.** Marsh and Minckley (1992) on Lake Mohave status; Minckley et al. (1989) on hatchery broodstock origin; Valdez and Clemmer (1982) on life history and recovery prospects for humpback and bonytail chubs; and Marsh (1995) as a recent synthesis.
