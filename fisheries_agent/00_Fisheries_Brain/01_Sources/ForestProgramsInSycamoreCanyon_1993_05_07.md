## Executive Summary

This document is an interagency memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) to the Coronado National Forest Supervisor, dated May 7, 1993. It concerns implementation of the September 1992 recovery plan for the **Sonora chub** (*Gila ditaenia*), a federally protected cyprinid fish endemic to a small area of southern Arizona. The memo does not present primary morphological, physiological, or population data; rather, it documents coordination efforts among the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]], [[Coronado National Forest]], [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] to develop a habitat and population monitoring protocol for the species in [[Sycamore Canyon]], and to review land-management plans (e.g., the Bear Valley Allotment Management Plan) that may affect [[Sonora Chub]] habitat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat type**: [[Sycamore Canyon]], a perennial desert stream drainage on the Nogales Ranger District of the [[Coronado National Forest]], Arizona.
- **Watershed context**: The canyon lies within the **Bear Valley Allotment**, a grazing management unit whose Allotment Management Plan was scheduled for update in fiscal year 1994, with implications for aquatic/riparian habitat quality.
- **Land management interactions**: Livestock grazing operations and other Forest Service programs/projects within the Sycamore Canyon watershed are identified as potential influences on habitat condition for the [[Sonora Chub]].
- **Regulatory status implied**: The species is subject to a federal recovery plan, indicating listing under the Endangered Species Act and status as a range-restricted, vulnerable desert fish (part of the broader [[Desert Fishes]] fauna of the U.S. Southwest and northern Mexico borderlands).

## Key Findings

- The FWS issued a formal **Recovery Plan for the Sonora chub (*Gila ditaenia*)** in **September 1992**.
- A collaborative effort is underway among the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]], [[Coronado National Forest]], [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]], and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] to implement the recovery plan.
- The immediate priority action identified is the **development of a monitoring protocol** for both the Sonora chub population and its physical habitat in [[Sycamore Canyon]].
- The FWS requested from the Forest Service: (1) documentation of Coronado National Forest management programs referencing Sycamore Canyon, and (2) a list of ongoing and proposed projects within the Sycamore Canyon watershed — indicating concern that forest management activities could affect chub habitat.
- The **Bear Valley Allotment Management Plan** (covering grazing within Sycamore Canyon) was slated for revision in **FY1994**, and the Service sought direct involvement in that review process to safeguard [[Sonora Chub]] habitat.

Newman (Coronado NF, Nogales Ranger District), and academic advisors Paul Marsh and W.L. Minckley of [[Arizona State University]], both noted contributors to Southwestern [[Desert Fishes]] conservation science.
