# Distribution of Striped Bass Fry in Relation to Major Water Diversions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #striped-bass

## Executive Summary

This 1951 survey by the [[California Department Of Fish And Game]], run jointly with the [[Fish And Wildlife Service]] and the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]], was the first quantitative estimate of young-of-year [[Striped Bass]] abundance in the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. Using oblique tow-net hauls at high slack tide in mid-July and late July, the authors estimated about 35,000,000 inch-long fry in mid-July and 20,000,000 in late July. Water diversions, notably the [[Tracy Pumping Plant]] and the [[Contra Costa Steam Plant]], draw from the principal nursery area. The authors conclude that, without protection, all diversions together might destroy as many as 25 percent of the fry during the month they are about one inch long.

## Background & Description

The striped bass is a recreational resource that the paper credits with about 2,000,000 days of angling and 1,500,000 high-quality food fish each year for 200,000 sportsmen (Calhoun, 1951). Spawning normally peaks in May and is centred in the delta and its tributary rivers, with little spawning believed to occur below Antioch.

Earlier surveys (Hatton, 1940; Hatton and Clark, 1942; Calhoun and Woodhull, 1948; Calhoun, Woodhull and Johnson, 1950; Erkkila et al., 1950) were largely qualitative. The 1951 study divided the delta and Suisun Bay into numbered divisions and assigned division-level abundances:

- **Upper delta:** Divisions 1 to 26, collected by the Fish and Wildlife Service.
- **Lower delta, Suisun Bay and related waters:** Divisions 27 to 66, collected by the California Department of Fish and Game.

Other fisheries are also at risk, including [[King Salmon]], shad and catfish, plus forage fishes. The authors state that these were excluded from the analysis only for scope, not because they are unimportant.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Tidal hydrodynamics.** Tidal currents keep the delta and Suisun Bay in nearly constant motion. In one observation, a float released near Antioch at high tide travelled about 20 miles down the San Joaquin River to Martinez on a single ebb. Sampling was therefore restricted to the lower of the two daily high slack tides, when the system briefly behaved like a lake. The results describe the high-tide distribution only, and a swing of up to about 20 miles should be kept in mind when interpreting them.

**Water masses.** Salinity studies indicated that water masses keep their identity and return to similar positions at the same tidal height on succeeding cycles. This allowed several days of sampling at comparable tides to be combined.

**Shallow-water trapping.** High concentrations occurred in [[Grizzly Bay]] and [[Honker Bay]], which the authors consider effective traps for fry. Similar concentrations were recorded there in 1949 and 1950. The extensive flats of northern [[San Pablo Bay]] may trap fry moving down through Carquinez Strait. No bass fry have ever been taken in deep water in San Pablo Bay. Shallow areas seem to be where small bass concentrate until they can control their own location.

**Timing and San Pablo Bay.** Fry reached San Pablo Bay in early August in 1949 and 1950. In 1951 they were late: on July 30 only two fry were taken at ten stations. The authors argue that in late years such as 1951, more fry remain upriver and within reach of the diversions for longer.

**Sampling gear.** The tow net was mounted on skis to slide over soft bottoms and was towed obliquely from bottom to surface. Routine delta tows lasted ten minutes. Towing speed was about 24 feet per second as printed, far faster than the roughly one foot per second swimming speed of inch-long bass. The net mouth was about five feet across. Water turbidity (Secchi disc typically a foot or less) was thought to prevent fish from seeing the net.

## Key Findings

### Abundance and Distribution

- **Total abundance:** About 35,149,000 fry in mid-July and 20,334,000 in late July, in an area of about 41,677 million cubic feet of water (the table's total volume).
- **Regional breakdown (mid-July / late July):**

| Region | Area | Mid-July fry | Share | Late-July fry | Share | Decline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (upper delta, Divisions 1-19, 22-25) | 8,133 million cu ft | 3,837,000 | 10.9% | 3,143,000 | 15.5% | 18.1% |
| 2 (lower delta, Divisions 20-21, 26a-41) | 18,376 million cu ft | 22,225,000 | 63.2% | 15,885,000 | 78.1% | 28.5% |
| 3 (Suisun Bay, Divisions 42-66) | 15,168 million cu ft | 9,087,000 | 25.9% | 1,306,000 | 6.4% | 85.6% |

- **Concentrations:** Mid-July averages were about 1 fry per 2,000 cubic feet in Region 1, about 1 per 1,000 cubic feet in Region 2, and 0.6 per 1,000 cubic feet in Region 3. By late July, Region 3 had fallen to 0.09 per 1,000 cubic feet.
- **Interpretation:** The authors attribute the Suisun Bay decline mostly to permanent downstream transfer into San Pablo Bay. Increased escape from the net due to clearer water and larger size is also possible.
- **Uniformity:** Fry were distributed fairly uniformly over broad areas, and catch-frequency curves for replicate tows were roughly normal with narrow ranges.

### Sample Calculation

In Division 39 in mid-July, 48 fry were caught in 34,833 cubic feet of water (1.378 fry per 1,000 cubic feet). With an estimated 437,000,000 cubic feet in the division, the expected total was about 603,000 fry, assuming the sample was representative.

### Size

- Mid-July: 2,265 fish measured, mean length 1.10 inches (SD 0.25).
- Late July: 936 fish measured, mean length 1.46 inches (SD 0.30).
- The authors describe size as remarkably uniform across the whole area.

### Diversion Impacts

- **[[Tracy Pumping Plant]]:** Part of the [[Central Valley Project]]. It will eventually pump 4,600 cubic feet per second from Old River, lifting water 200 feet into the [[Delta-Mendota Canal]] for a 120-mile gravity flow to the San Joaquin Valley. Limited diversion in summer 1951 sucked in many small fish. The temporary intake and screens are being used for studies to reduce losses. The planned [[Feather River Project]] diversion, also near Tracy, is in the planning stage. Its proximity to Tracy may draw fish across the delta out of proportion to the added water withdrawn.
- **[[Contra Costa Steam Plant]]:** Began operation in 1951 and pumps about 1,000 cubic feet per second from the San Joaquin River near Antioch. Most fish entering it are being saved, according to the authors.
- **[[Pittsburg Steam Plant]]:** Under construction and redesigned, based on Contra Costa experience, to virtually eliminate the fish problem.
- **Hypothetical losses:** At a combined intake of about 3,000 cubic feet per second with no protection and average concentrations of one fry per 1,000 cubic feet, losses could reach about three fish per second, or about 8,000,000 for July alone.
- **Temperature tolerance:** Experiments at Contra Costa suggest most very small bass passing the screens will survive and return to the river.

### Conclusions and Recommendations

- Without protective measures, all diversions together might destroy up to 25 percent of fish of the year during the month they are about one inch long.
- Such losses "cannot be condoned," and the authors endorse ongoing development of rescue methods for small fish at the diversions.
- Fish less than an inch long cannot be screened and must be accepted as natural mortality. Given their numbers, diversion losses should be small relative to natural mortality.
- Yearling and older bass have much greater swimming ability and are less vulnerable.
- Pacific Gas and Electric Company is credited with accepting responsibility for fish protection at its plants. The authors expect the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation to handle the Tracy site similarly, though it was too early to judge.

### Limitations Noted by the Authors

- Only the 1951 data were comparable. Earlier years used surface tows restricted to the top five feet and were not tide-controlled.
- Projections assume a brief spawning period. Late spawning, for example after storms chill the delta, would lead to underestimates.
- Delta lakes such as [[Sherman Lake]], Franks Tract and Big Break were excluded. Sherman Lake was estimated at about 500,000 fry (about 2 percent of Region 2), and Franks Tract at about 350,000 (about 9 percent).
