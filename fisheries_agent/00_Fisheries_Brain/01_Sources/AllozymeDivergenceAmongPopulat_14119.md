# Allozyme Divergence Among Populations of the Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a research proposal submitted by [[Donald G. Buth]] of the [[University Of California Los Angeles]] Department of Biology to the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]] (Upper Colorado River Region). It proposes to use [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]] to assess genetic structure, introgressive hybridization, and regional differentiation in [[Razorback Sucker]] populations across their range. The work is intended to determine whether the [[Dexter Fish Hatchery]] stock, derived from [[Lake Mohave]] breeding stock, is suitable for wide release. The study is designed as a companion to a separate [[Mitochondrial DNA]] proposal by Drs. Thomas E. Dowling and W. L. Minckley of Arizona State University. The total requested cost is $45,329 over two years.

## Background & Description

The [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) has declined throughout the [[Colorado River]] basin. Most extant populations are small, numbering in the hundreds or a few thousand individuals, with [[Lake Mohave]] as the notable exception, possibly holding tens of thousands. No population is recruiting, and most consist of aging adults, although local reproduction has been noted in some cases.

Recovery planning includes artificial propagation at the [[Dexter Fish Hatchery]] in New Mexico, using breeding stock from Lake Mohave. Earlier concern about the genetic makeup of the hatchery stock led to an allozyme comparison of parental and hatchery stocks (Buth et al., 1987). That study identified several polymorphic enzyme systems in the razorback sucker and diagnostic enzymes for distinguishing it from the [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (*Catostomus latipinnis*), which allow hybridization to be measured.

The species belongs to [[Catostomidae]], whose members are [[Allotetraploidy|allotetraploid]]. This gives them more functional genes than diploid fishes. It makes the resulting multi-locus gene products harder to interpret than the autotetraploid patterns seen in salmonids.

Key methods and concepts referenced:
- [[Allozyme Electrophoresis]]: starch gel separation of gene products with histochemical staining. Advantages cited include low resolution cost, no environmental influence on structural gene products, biparental inheritance, codominance for heterozygote identification, and variation at low taxonomic levels. The most recent detailed description is Murphy et al. (1990).
- [[Wright's F-Statistics]]: used to partition variation within and among populations.
- [[Mitochondrial DNA]]: maternally inherited marker, used to complement allozymes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The proposal identifies four sampling locations within the species' range:
- Mainstream [[Colorado River]]
- [[Green River]]
- [[Lake Mohave]]
- [[Lake Powell]]

Tissue sampling is designed to be largely non-destructive. A small muscle biopsy is taken in the field from each specimen. Frozen tissue is said to retain enzyme activity for several years. Tissue availability is a limiting factor. Only muscle-predominating enzyme systems can be assessed from biopsies, whereas intact specimens (for example, from the Dexter Fish Hatchery) provide access to liver, heart, and brain tissue.

Potential hybridization partners in sympatry with razorback suckers include:
- *Catostomus latipinnis* (Flannelmouth Sucker)
- *Catostomus commersoni* (White Sucker; introduced)
- *Catostomus discobolus*
- *Catostomus platyrhynchus*

Hybridization with other *Catostomus* species is documented in the literature (Hubbs and Miller; Wick et al., 1982).

## Key Findings

**Prior results (Buth et al., 1987)**
- Introgressed genes made up only 1% of the Dexter Hatchery stock genome.
- Native Lake Mohave stock had 1.5–2.8% introgressed genes.
- Eleven polymorphic enzyme loci were examined in razorback suckers, using liver, heart, brain, and skeletal muscle.
- Fifty-two razorback suckers from Dexter Hatchery stock had already been examined electrophoretically.
- The authors state that the hatchery stock should certainly be used in the recovery effort. Wide release, however, depends on assessing the genetic composition of other extant populations.

**Objectives**
1. Measure population differentiation among razorback suckers in the Colorado River mainstream, Green River, Lake Mohave, and Lake Powell.
2. Measure intrapopulation genetic diversity, including variation originating through hybridization.
3. Examine allozyme variation in Dexter Hatchery stock for characters beyond those identified by Buth et al. (1987).

**Sampling plan**
- Target of 30 individuals from each of the 4 locations. This is intended to resolve 95% of enzymatic alleles with 95% confidence.
- The PI expects that 30 specimens will not likely be obtained from Lake Powell or the mainstream Colorado River (W. L. Minckley, pers. comm.). Comparisons will be based on whatever samples are obtained.
- An additional 30 Dexter Hatchery specimens are proposed for intra-gel comparisons, testing year-class differences, and investigating new muscle-expressed enzyme systems.
- 10–20 specimens (where possible) of each congeneric species are proposed for introgression comparisons.
- If limited to muscle biopsies, products of only 8 of the 11 polymorphic loci can be examined.

**Anticipated analyses**
- *Differentiation*: [[Wright's F-Statistics]] to infer inbreeding, gene flow, and degree of differentiation. Phylogenetic analysis of allozyme data to compare evolutionary relationships with geographic distribution.
- *Diversity*: Heterozygosity and allozyme autapomorphy, interpreted with caution because differences may arise from bottlenecks or secondary contact. The authors note that failing to reject the null hypothesis of no difference may be the easiest result to interpret.
- *Hybridization*: Codominant allozyme data to identify hybrids and distinguish F1 individuals from backcrosses. [[Mitochondrial DNA]] data, owing to maternal inheritance, to determine the direction of hybridization.
