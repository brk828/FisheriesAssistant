# Keys to the Freshwater and Anadromous Fishes of California

#ichthyology #taxonomic-key #freshwater-fish #anadromous-fish #California-fauna

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive taxonomic key, published in 1960 by J.B. Kimsey (U.S. Bureau of Commercial Fisheries) and Leonard O. Fisk (California Department of Fish and Game), provides dichotomous identification keys for all freshwater and anadromous fish species known from California at that time. The document addresses 101 full species (65 native, 26 introduced as of 1950), with additions bringing the total to include 11 additional species and 7 subspecies by 1959. The keys are designed for field identification by both scientific workers and laymen, minimizing the need for microscopic examination or complex statistical analysis. The work includes keys to 24 fish families, detailed species-level keys, anatomical diagrams, measurement methods, and a comprehensive glossary of ichthyological terms.

## Background & Description

The authors note that while numerous keys to [[California]] fishes had been published previously, they were restricted either to specific drainages (e.g., [[Miller 1952]], [[Murphy 1941]], [[Rutter 1908]]) or to particular taxonomic groups (e.g., [[Beland 1953]], [[Curtis 1949]]). This represented the first comprehensive key covering all freshwater and anadromous species statewide.

The taxonomic coverage includes:
- **[[Petromyzontidae]]** (lamprey family): 3 species including [[Pacific Lamprey]] (*Entosphenus tridentatus*), [[River Lamprey]] (*Lampetra ayresii*), [[Brook Lamprey]] (*Lampetra planeri*)
- **[[Acipenseridae]]** (sturgeon family): [[White Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser transmontanus*), [[Green Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser medirostris*)
- **[[Salmonidae]]** (salmon and trout family): Multiple species including five Pacific salmon species (*Oncorhynchus* spp.), [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdnerii*), [[Cutthroat Trout]] (*Salmo clarki*), [[Golden Trout]] (*Salmo aguabonita*)
- **[[Cyprinidae]]** (minnow family): Extensive coverage with numerous native genera including *Lavinia*, *Pogonichthys*, *Ptychocheilus*, *Gila*, *Siphateles*, *Hesperoleucus*, *Rhinichthys*
- **[[Catostomidae]]** (sucker family): Multiple *Catostomus* species and subspecies, *Chasmistes*, *Deltistes*, *Pantosteus*
- **[[Centrarchidae]]** (sunfish family): Native [[Sacramento Perch]] (*Archoplites interruptus*) plus introduced basses and sunfishes
- Additional families including [[Ictaluridae]], [[Cottidae]], [[Poeciliidae]], [[Cyprinodontidae]], [[Percidae]], [[Embiotocidae]], [[Gobiidae]], and several euryhaline families

The keys utilize standard morphological characters including fin ray counts, scale counts, gill raker counts, pharyngeal teeth formulas (e.g., 2,5-5,2 notation), pyloric caeca counts, and anatomical features. Measurement methods follow [[Hubbs and Lagler 1958]] conventions, with occasional departures. Common and scientific names follow [[Shapovalov, Dill, and Cordone 1959]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document reveals significant anthropogenic changes to California's fish fauna. The rate of introductions accelerated dramatically through the 20th century:
- Approximately 18 successful introductions occurred prior to 1900
- Nine additional introductions in the first 50 years of the 20th century (1900-1950)
- Seven more introductions in just the decade 1950-1960

The authors note that California's fish fauna was "losing its unique character at an accelerated rate," contrasting with [[Grinnell 1922]]'s postulation that all North American bird species might eventually occur in California. The authors considered this scenario unlikely for fishes but acknowledged the ongoing transformation.

Geographic distribution patterns are emphasized for subspecies identification, particularly within [[Cyprinidae]] and [[Catostomidae]]. Many native minnows had been "widely transported as bait" and occurred well outside their original ranges. An extreme example cited: a [[Sacramento Hitch]] (*Lavinia exilicauda exilicauda*) collected in 1954 from Ramer Lake, [[Imperial County]], approximately 320 road miles south of its regular range.

The keys distinguish between strictly freshwater species and euryhaline species (those tolerating fresh, brackish, and salt water), including [[Striped Bass]] (*Roccus saxatilis*), [[Starry Flounder]] (*Platichthys stellatus*), various smelts ([[Osmeridae]]), [[Pacific Herring]] (*Clupea pallasii*), and several [[Cottidae]] and [[Gobiidae]] species.

Drainage-specific subspecies are documented for:
- [[Klamath River]] drainage
- [[Sacramento-San Joaquin River]] drainage
- [[Lahontan]] drainage
- [[Colorado River]] drainage
- [[Russian River]]
- Coastal drainages (Eel, Mad, Navarro, Gualala rivers)
- [[Goose Lake]]
- [[Death Valley]] system

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Structure and Methodology:**
- The keys employ dichotomous format with alternative characters (1a/1b, 2a/2b, etc.)
- Technical language minimized; glossary of 60+ terms provided
- Internal characters limited primarily to pharyngeal teeth and pyloric caeca counts
- Hand lens sufficient for most identifications; microscopic examination unnecessary
- Anatomical diagrams illustrate measurement methods, fin structures, gill arches, pharyngeal bones, and specialized features

**Scale Count Notation:**
Standard format: "10 to 12—54 to 60—6 or 7" represents:
- First number: scale rows above lateral line (from dorsal fin origin to lateral line)
- Second number: scales in lateral series
- Third number: scale rows below lateral line (from anal fin origin upward)

**Fin Ray Notation:**
- Roman numerals (I, II, III) indicate spines
- Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) indicate soft rays
- Combined notation (e.g., VIII, 12) indicates 8 spines and 12 soft rays

**Pharyngeal Teeth Formulas:**
- Format "2,5-5,2" indicates left bone has 2 outer row teeth and 5 inner row teeth; right bone has 5 inner and 2 outer
- Format "5-4" indicates single row on each bone (5 teeth left, 4 teeth right)
- Alveoli (empty tooth sockets) must be counted

**Family-Level Diagnostic Characters:**
- Presence/absence of adipose fin separates [[Salmonidae]], [[Osmeridae]], [[Coregonidae]], and [[Ictaluridae]] from other families
- Sucking disc mouth distinguishes [[Petromyzontidae]] (7 gill openings) and [[Catostomidae]] (4 gill openings under operculum)
- Pelvic fin position (abdominal vs. thoracic) provides major division
- Body scalation patterns (presence, absence, or modification of scales)
- Specialized structures: gular plate ([[Elopidae]]), united pelvic fins forming sucker ([[Gobiidae]]), belly scutes ([[Clupeidae]])

**Species-Level Distinctions:**
Within [[Salmonidae]]:
- Anal fin ray counts separate salmon (13-19 rays) from trout (9-12 rays)
- Gill raker counts distinguish species (19-40 in salmon, ≤20 in trout)
- Scale counts in lateral series: [[Pink Salmon]] has 170-232 (very small), others <160
- Parr mark patterns differ among juvenile salmonids
- Vomer dentition separates trout groups

Within [[Cyprinidae]]:
- Presence/absence of barbels
- Pharyngeal teeth arrangement (one vs. two rows)
- Frenum presence/absence on premaxillaries
- Scale counts and distribution patterns
- Dorsal fin position relative to pelvic fin insertion

Within [[Catostomidae]]:
- Mouth position (terminal vs. inferior)
- Lip structure (thin vs. thick with papillae)
- Jaw modifications (horny cutting edges in *Pantosteus*)
- Fontanelle presence/absence
- Geographic distribution critical for subspecies

**Introduced Species Documentation:**
The keys explicitly identify introduced species including:
- [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*)
- [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*)
- [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*)
- Multiple [[Centrarchidae]]: [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Smallmouth Bass]], [[Spotted Bass]], various sunfishes and crappies
- [[Ictaluridae]]: [[Channel Catfish]], [[White Catfish]], [[Brown Bullhead]], [[Black Bullhead]]
- [[Percidae]]: [[Yellow Perch]], [[Walleye]]
- [[Poeciliidae]]: [[Mosquitofish]] (*Gambusia affinis*)

**Limitations and Future Revisions:**
The authors acknowledge that "these keys will need to be revised at intervals" and that "in the interim between revisions, it will be possible to collect fish that cannot be keyed out with them," recognizing the ongoing nature of species introductions and taxonomic revisions.
