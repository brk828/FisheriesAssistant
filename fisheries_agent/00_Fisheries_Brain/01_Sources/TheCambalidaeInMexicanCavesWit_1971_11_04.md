## Executive Summary
This 1971 taxonomic paper by Nell B. Causey (Louisiana State University), published in the *Proceedings of the Biological Society of Washington*, revises the family [[Cambalidae]] as known from Mexican caves. All Mexican Cambalidae are obligate troglobites, assignable to two genera: [[Cambala]] (represented by a single species, *Cambala speobia*, shared with Texas) and the endemic Mexican genus [[Mexicambala]]. The paper describes three new species of *Mexicambala* — *[[Mexicambala fishi]]*, *[[Mexicambala blandus]]*, and *[[Mexicambala inopis]]* — and redescribes the previously known *[[Mexicambala russelli]]*. Collectively these four *Mexicambala* species form the "russelli superspecies complex," distributed along a clinal north–south gradient through the Sierra de Guatemala, Sierra del Abra, and Sierra Madre Oriental of Tamaulipas, San Luis Potosí, and (disjunctly) northern Oaxaca. The work also briefly treats the epigean family [[Leioderidae]] (e.g., *Ergonus setosus*), providing new Mexican locality records.

## Background & Description
**General troglobitic adaptations (Cambalidae):**
- Eyeless (ocelli absent, except occasional vestiges in immature *Cambala speobia*).
- Depigmented body (flesh-colored to grayish in preservative), retaining red stink-pore pigment briefly in life.
- Elongated antennae and legs relative to epigean relatives (more pronounced in *Mexicambala* than *Cambala*, suggesting earlier/longer cave adaptation).
- Body length 18–36 mm depending on species; segment counts range approximately 34–47.

**Genus-level distinguishing features:**
- ***[[Cambala]]*** (*C. speobia*): Collum sides low and flared; dorsal crests without notches; peritremes no higher than dorsal crests; paraprocts slightly visible beyond epiproct dorsally; ventral crests as 18–20 narrow ridges; males have rounded ventral lobes on article 4 of legpairs 6–7.
- ***[[Mexicambala]]***: Collum sides high, notched (vertex margin articulates); dorsal crests divided by 1–2 notches into 2–3 teeth (rounded or angular); peritremes taller than dorsal crests (subcylindrical or conical); epiproct covers paraprocts in dorsal view; antennae reach segment 5 with enlarged sensory areas on articles 5–6; anterior gonopod coxite apex narrowly rounded, undivided.

**Species-specific gonopod and surface-sculpture diagnostics:**
- *M. fishi*: Largest species (max approximately 34–36 mm); 3 conspicuous angular dorsal-crest teeth; subcylindrical, conspicuous peritremes; epiproct apex rounded, asetose; paraprocts with approximately 9 setae restricted to mesial margin; telopodite of anterior gonopods **as long as** coxite (unique among genus).
- *M. russelli*: Max approximately 26 mm; 2 notches, mostly angular teeth; subcylindrical peritremes; epiproct rounded, asetose; paraprocts most densely setose in genus; telopodite distinctly shorter than coxite.
- *M. blandus*: 1 notch, rounded teeth; peritremes conical, salient, prominent; epiproct bears a distinctive setose apical **peg** (largest at Rancho del Cielo, diminishing southward toward Bee Cave).
- *M. inopis*: 1 notch, rounded/subangular teeth; peritremes subcylindrical, low; epiproct rounded, lacking apical setae in typical form; some intergradation with *M. blandus* noted geographically.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat:** Strictly cavernicolous (troglobitic); collected from cave floor substrates, including decaying organic matter.
- **Geographic distribution:**
  - *Cambala speobia*: Numerous caves of the Edwards Plateau, central/southwest Texas, associated with the Balcones Fault zone; single Mexican record from Cueva de los Lagos, Coahuila (subsequently flooded by the Amistad Reservoir).
  - *Mexicambala inopis*: Sierra de Guatemala, southern Tamaulipas (3 caves, approximately 8 mile range) — northernmost species.
  - *Mexicambala blandus*: Sierra del Abra, southern Tamaulipas (6 caves, approximately 21 mile range), between *inopis* and *russelli* ranges.
  - *Mexicambala russelli*: Sierra Madre Oriental, eastern San Luis Potosí (8 caves near Aquismón/Xilitla, approximately 24 mile range).
  - *Mexicambala fishi*: Single known locality, Cueva Arriba de Río Iglesia, northern Oaxaca (approximately 265 miles south of nearest *M. russelli* site) — southernmost and most morphologically derived species.
- **Co-occurring fauna:** The polydesmoid millipede genus *[[Speodesmus]]* typically co-occurs with *C. speobia* in Edwards Plateau caves, though notably absent at the Mexican *speobia* site.
- **Biogeographic hypothesis:** A clinal north-to-south trend is proposed wherein peritreme elongation and dorsal-crest notching/enlargement intensify with more southerly distribution, suggesting progressive/ongoing troglomorphic adaptation correlated with southward range expansion of *Mexicambala*.
- **Evolutionary inference:** Shorter legs/antennae in *C. speobia* versus *Mexicambala* spp. suggest a more recent or shallower transition to obligate cave life for *Cambala*, with ancestors possibly persisting epigeally until the early Pleistocene (per Bull & Mitchell, in press).

## Key Findings
- Establishes **three new species**: *Mexicambala fishi*, *Mexicambala blandus*, and *Mexicambala inopis*, expanding the known troglobitic Cambalidae fauna of Mexico to five total species across two genera.
- Confirms *Mexicambala* as a valid genus distinct from *Cambala*, contra Shear (1969), based on well-defined somatic (collum, antennae, crest morphology) and sexual (gonopod) characters.
- Defines the **"russelli superspecies"** — *M. inopis*, *M. blandus*, and *M. russelli* — as a group of parapatric, morphologically similar species differing mainly in degree of surface sculpture, epiproct form, and paraproct setation, forming a geographic cline from Tamaulipas to San Luis Potosí.
- *M. fishi*, geographically disjunct (approximately 265 mi south in Oaxaca) and morphologically most derived (largest body, unique gonopod proportions), is excluded from the russelli superspecies, indicating a distinct, more isolated evolutionary lineage.
- Documents that *Cambala speobia* is the only cambaloid millipede known to be troglobitic in the Edwards Plateau, with its single Mexican population now extinct due to reservoir flooding — an early documented case of cave-fauna habitat loss from anthropogenic water management.
- Provides updated Mexican locality records for the epigean family [[Leioderidae]] (*Ergonus setosus*), including a potential undescribed genus from Tuxpan, Veracruz, indicating unresolved diversity outside the Cambalidae.
- Notes taxonomic disagreement with Shear (1969) regarding generic boundaries, reinforcing the diagnostic weight of gonopod and crest morphology in Cambalidae systematics.
