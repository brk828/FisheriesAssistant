# Federal Acquisition Regulation Excerpts: Unsolicited Proposals and Source Selection

#federal-acquisition #contracting-regulation #unsolicited-proposals

## Executive Summary

This document is not ichthyological or ecological research. It is a short cover note from a colleague, followed by excerpts from the [[Federal Acquisition Regulation]] (FAR) (FAC 84-60). The cover note recommends the material to colleagues who may submit unsolicited proposals to a government agency. The excerpts cover Subpart 15.5 (Unsolicited Proposals) and Subpart 15.6 (Source Selection). They set out the required content of unsolicited proposals, agency handling procedures, evaluation criteria, restrictions on the use of proprietary data, and the basics of source selection in competitive negotiated acquisitions.

## Background & Description

The cover note is a brief internal message. The sender passes along the attached contracting-office material, which "points out just what format you need for an unsolicited proposal" and explains how the agency handles proposals once received. The recipient is asked to share it with a colleague named Minck and others who might benefit.

The FAR excerpts are regulatory text, not project descriptions. Key definitions in Subpart 15.5 include:

- **Unsolicited proposal:** a proposal submitted on the offeror's own initiative to obtain a government contract, not in response to a formal or informal request (other than a publicized general statement of agency needs).
- **Contribution:** a concept, suggestion, or idea presented to the government with no indication that the submitter intends to devote further effort to it on the government's behalf.
- **Advertising material:** material meant to acquaint the government with a prospective contractor's capabilities or to gauge government interest in buying products.
- **Technical correspondence:** written requests for information on government research interests, research descriptions, preproposal explanations, and other technical inquiries.
- **Coordinating office:** the agency point of contact for receiving, evaluating, and disposing of unsolicited proposals.

Subpart 15.6 defines **clarification** (minor correction of irregularities, not revision of the proposal), **deficiency** (any part of a proposal that fails to meet government requirements), **discussion** (exchanges that may let an offeror revise its proposal), and **source selection authority** (the official choosing the source, often someone other than the contracting officer).

## Key Findings

**Required content of an unsolicited proposal (FAR 15.505):**

- *Basic information:* offeror's name, address, and organization type (profit, nonprofit, educational, small business); contact names and telephone numbers for technical and business personnel; identification of proprietary data limited to evaluation; names of other federal, state, or local agencies or parties receiving the proposal or funding the effort; submission date; and the signature of an authorized person who can bind the offeror.
- *Technical information:* a concise title and an abstract of approximately 200 words; a complete discussion of objectives, approach, extent of effort, anticipated results, and how the work supports the agency's mission; names and biographical information for key personnel, including alternates; and the type of support needed from the agency (facilities, equipment, materials, or personnel).
- *Supporting information:* proposed price or total estimated cost in enough detail for evaluation; a validity period (six months is suggested as a minimum); the type of contract preferred; proposed duration of effort; a description of the organization and its relevant experience and facilities; and any required statements on conflicts of interest, security clearances, and environmental impacts.

**Agency handling (FAR 15.506):**

- Agencies must set up procedures for receiving, evaluating, and disposing of proposals, including controls on reproduction and disposition of proprietary material.
- A designated contact point must determine, before a full evaluation, whether the proposal has sufficient technical and cost information, has been approved by an authorized official, and complies with the marking requirements.
- If it meets these requirements, the contact point must promptly acknowledge and process it. If not, the offeror must be given a chance to submit the missing data.
- Agencies are not required to evaluate proposals unrelated to their missions. In such cases the contact point must promptly reply, explain how the agency interprets the proposal, and explain why it is not being evaluated.

**Evaluation criteria (FAR 15.507):** Evaluators consider, along with other relevant factors:
1. Unique and innovative methods, approaches, or concepts.
2. Overall scientific, technical, or socioeconomic merit.
3. Potential contribution to the agency's specific mission.
4. The offeror's capabilities, related experience, facilities, techniques, or unique combinations of these.
5. The qualifications, experience, and availability of the principal investigator, team leader, or other critical personnel.

Evaluators must report conclusions and recommendations to the coordinating office.

**Award conditions (FAR 15.504 and 15.508):**

- Agencies must encourage preliminary contacts with agency technical personnel before offerors spend substantial effort on a detailed proposal.
- An unsolicited proposal may be accepted without competition only if it is unique and innovative, is not available from other sources, and does not closely resemble a pending competitive requirement.
- Negotiations may begin only after a favorable comprehensive evaluation, a documented justification that precludes competition, available funds, and required certifications.
- A proposal accepted without competition becomes the basis for negotiation.

**Data protection (FAR 15.509):**

- An offeror who wants to restrict use of data must mark the title page with a "Use and Disclosure of Data" legend. Restricted data may be used only for evaluation, unless the resulting contract states otherwise.
- Government personnel may not use data, concepts, or ideas from an unsolicited proposal in a solicitation or negotiations with other firms without the offeror's notice and agreement.
- Disclosing trade secrets or confidential commercial information without authorization may result in criminal penalties under 18 U.S.C. 1905.
- Release of a restricted proposal to outside evaluators requires the offeror's written permission and written non-disclosure agreements, except where evaluators are at educational or nonprofit institutions.

**Source selection (FAR Subpart 15.6):** Its procedures aim to maximize competition, minimize complexity, ensure impartial evaluation, and select the source whose proposal has the highest degree of realism and is expected to best meet government requirements. Agency heads or designees are responsible for source selection. Contracting officers conduct negotiations and select the source unless another official is designated.

**Overall message:** The material is a procedural guide. Unsolicited proposals are valued as a way to obtain innovative approaches from outside the government. They must be original, self-initiated, sufficiently detailed, and unavailable through competitive acquisition to qualify for sole-source consideration.
