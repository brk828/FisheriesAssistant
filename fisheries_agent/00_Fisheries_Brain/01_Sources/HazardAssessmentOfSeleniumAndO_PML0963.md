# Selenium and Trace Element Hazards to Wild Larval Razorback Sucker in the Green River, Utah

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This study assesses the hazard posed by [[Selenium]] and other [[Trace Elements]] to wild larval [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), an endangered catostomid, in the [[Green River]] of northeastern Utah. The Green River supports the largest remaining riverine population of this species. Larvae were collected by light trap at five sites in 1994 and analyzed for selenium by [[Neutron Activation Analysis]], with a composite sample analyzed by [[ICP-MS]] for 61 elements. Whole-body selenium in every site's larvae equaled or exceeded the proposed toxic threshold of 4 µg/g dry weight. The authors conclude that selenium contamination, particularly from [[Stewart Lake Drain]] and its surroundings, may limit survival of razorback sucker from the larval stage to the juvenile stage.

## Background & Description

The razorback sucker is an endangered fish of the Colorado River basin. Its population in the Green River is the focus of recovery efforts, but juvenile recruitment has been very low. Earlier investigations by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, the U.S. Geological Survey, and the Bureau of Reclamation, conducted under the [[National Irrigation Water Quality Program]] (NIWQP), documented elevated selenium in water, sediment, and biota of the Stewart Lake Drain/Ashley Creek area, Sportsman's Drain, and [[Ouray National Wildlife Refuge]].

Larval fish were collected between May and June 1994 from five sites along the middle Green River:

- [[Cliff Creek]] (Escalante reach)
- [[Stewart Lake Drain]] (Jensen reach)
- [[Sportsman's Drain]] (Jensen reach)
- [[Greasewood Corral]] (Ouray reach)
- [[Old Charlie Wash]] inlet (Ouray reach)

Larvae were preserved in ethanol. Selenium was measured in 10 larvae per site, either individually or as composites of 2 to 10 larvae. Analytical quality control used the NIST 1577 (bovine liver) reference material and the NIST 1643d (water) reference material. The detection limit for selenium by neutron activation was 15 ng/g dry weight.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water and sediment selenium.** The Green River normally has low selenium, typically below 1 to 3 µg/L. Water concentrations were far higher in contaminated tributaries and drains:

- Stewart Lake Drain outflow: 2–12 µg/L (1988–1989 data)
- Ashley Creek: well above the national water quality criterion
- Sportsman's Lake: 31 µg/L in water (1986); inflow 26–130 µg/L (1988–1989)
- Ouray NWR ponds and groundwater: high concentrations, including the Sheppard Bottom area

**Pathways.** Irrigation drainage and tailwaters enter the system through Stewart Lake Drain, Ashley Creek, and the Lower Union Canal. Sportsman's Lake receives Ashley Creek water via the canal, and Old Charlie Wash receives river water via its inlet canal. Cliff Creek does not receive irrigation drainage but is exposed to loading from upstream [[Brush Creek]].

**Biota.** Aquatic invertebrates from Stewart Lake Drain (up to 27 µg/g) and Sportsman's Lake (up to 41 µg/g) were far above the 3 µg/g dietary toxic threshold proposed by [[Lemly 1993]]. Fish from these drains also showed elevated selenium.

**Adult source.** Muscle plugs from wild adults at Stewart Lake Drain (n = 10, all above 8 µg/g) and Ashley Creek showed high selenium. Of 26 adults from the Escalante Bar–Razorback Bar area, 10 exceeded 8 µg/g. None of 9 adults from Old Charlie Wash did. Because adults used these drains, maternal transfer to eggs and larvae is a plausible route.

**Juvenile recruitment.** Larvae (under 35 mm) were abundant in some years but juveniles (over 43 mm) were rare. Old Charlie Wash yielded 28 young-of-year juveniles in 1995 and 45 in 1996, but no selenium data exist for those juveniles.

## Key Findings

**Larval abundance and distribution**
- Larvae collected in the Green River from 1992 to 1996 totalled 1,735 by year count (20 in 1992, 292 in 1993, 1,217 in 1994, 32 in 1995, 174 in 1996). The 1994 collection was used for the selenium analyses. (The introduction gives a different figure, 2,175, which may be a scan error.)
- By reach, 40% came from Escalante, 41% from Jensen, and 20% from Ouray.

**Selenium in larvae**
- Whole-body selenium ranged from 2.24 µg/g at Cliff Creek to 7.42 µg/g at Stewart Lake Drain (dry weight).
- Stewart Lake Drain individual values reached 7.04 and 7.42 µg/g.
- The ICP-MS composite of 279 larvae from Stewart Lake Drain (mean total length 11.5 mm) gave 8.0 µg/g. The three neutron-activation samples from the same site averaged 5.8 µg/g.
- All five sites equaled or exceeded the 4 µg/g toxic threshold.

**Trends with size and time**
- Cliff Creek: selenium was positively correlated with total length (R = 0.65, P = 0.16), suggesting accumulation.
- Stewart Lake Drain: positive correlation (R = 0.996, P = 0.05), suggesting accumulation.
- Greasewood Corral: positive correlation (R = 0.77, P = 0.13), slower accumulation.
- Sportsman's Drain: negative relationship, interpreted as depuration of selenium from larvae, possibly by maternal transfer to gonads.
- Old Charlie Wash (one collection): no pattern.

**Other elements (ICP-MS)**
- Cadmium, copper, lead, selenium, vanadium, and zinc appeared elevated in the composite.
- Ethanol storage did not appear to alter inorganic concentrations in larvae. Only calcium, sodium, and zinc were elevated in the ethanol used to store larvae. Selenium recovery from spiked ethanol was poor (55%), but selenium in the storage ethanol was negligible.

**Hazard assessment**
- Selenium concentrations in larvae were higher than control fish in laboratory and field studies (0.4–2.0 µg/g).
- Acute and 90-day chronic tests with a nine-element mixture simulating Stewart Lake Drain water showed adverse effects (reduced growth, swimming performance, and survival) at concentrations within 2 to 8 times measured environmental levels.
- A 1992 on-site test found no appreciable mortality in fathead minnow or 40–60-day-old razorback sucker, but 30% mortality and impaired reproduction in *Ceriodaphnia*.
- Egg selenium at Razorback Bar in 1992 ranged from 3.7 to 10.6 µg/g. Eggs from captive adults at elevated-selenium sites in Colorado reached 38–46 µg/g, with larval deformities and reduced survival.

**Conclusions**
- Larval razorback sucker in the Green River accumulate selenium at levels equal to or above the 4 µg/g whole-body toxic threshold.
- Stewart Lake Drain/Ashley Creek is the most selenium-contaminated site in the Green River, and its inputs are probably responsible for elevated larval selenium.
- Selenium contamination may limit larval-to-juvenile survival, and it is a likely contributor to the low juvenile recruitment in the Green River.
- Because larvae were collected from a demographically open population in a free-flowing river, effects were not directly measured. The authors note that adverse effects are probable but unverified in the field.
