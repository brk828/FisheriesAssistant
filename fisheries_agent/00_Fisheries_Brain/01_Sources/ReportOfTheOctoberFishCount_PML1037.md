# 1988 October Fish Count: Volunteer Freshwater Fish Survey Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-fishes

## Executive Summary

This report, prepared by [[Diann Lyn Sheldon]] and [[Dean A. Hendrickson]] of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] ([[Nongame Branch]]), summarizes the first annual [[October Fish Count]] (OFC) held in October 1988. The OFC used volunteers to collect fish data across Arizona and parts of New Mexico. Volunteers sampled 60 sites, and the department calculated that the volunteer approach cost roughly $30.76 per volunteer-day, compared with $211.44 per day for a department employee. The authors describe the project as a successful, cost-effective start to a long-term fish community database.

## Background & Description

The OFC was a volunteer-based fish sampling effort coordinated by the Arizona Game and Fish Department. Organizers mailed about 225 letters and questionnaires to agencies, parks, universities, sportsmen's groups, and individuals. Completed questionnaires came from 73 people. Of these, 61 were positive responses, and the organizers selected 21 [[Team Leaders]] based on willingness to lead, fish identification expertise, and the sites they wanted to sample. Team Leaders recruited their own team members, scheduled sampling dates, and borrowed equipment when needed.

Organizers held a two-day training workshop on September 24–25, 1988, at a guest house on the [[Aravaipa Canyon]] property owned by [[The Nature Conservancy]]. About 30 participants attended, and the workshop included a field session on [[Aravaipa Creek]]. Participants used simple standardized [[Data Sheets]] and worked with the department's coded species list.

The main conflict reported by participants was the opening of hunting seasons, which drew some people away from sampling. The timing of the project, set in October, was chosen to suit university class schedules while avoiding finals.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The report frames the OFC as the beginning of a broad, repeated sampling program that could show changes in fish communities over time. The authors state that earlier data on these fish communities were mostly anecdotal or expert opinion, and that single-season snapshots become much more useful when repeated annually.

Sampling covered a range of habitats: rivers, creeks, springs, stock ponds, and pools. The authors emphasized threatened and endangered species. At least one taxon on the Arizona Game and Fish Department's threatened and endangered native fish list was sampled at 34 of the 60 sites. Methods included [[Seine Netting]], [[Backpack Electrofishing]], and [[Dip Netting]].

Sites recorded in the report include [[Gila River]], [[Verde River]], [[Tonto Creek]], [[Canyon Creek]], [[Blue River]], [[East Clear Creek]], [[Redfield Canyon]], and several springs. Each site entry lists the habitat, date, gear, average area sampled, collectors, and a table of species counts, length ranges, and average lengths and weights. Common species in the tables are shown using four-letter codes, and the appendix links each code to a common name. Taxa marked with asterisks are native fishes in the latest revision of the list. Full site tables are summarized here rather than reproduced.

## Key Findings

- **Participation:** About 225 mailings yielded 73 completed questionnaires, 61 positive responses, and 21 Team Leaders. Some respondents could not participate, and two asked to be removed from the mailing list.
- **Data coverage:** Data came from 60 sites. Because some sites were large, one mapped point could represent multiple collections. Threatened or endangered taxa were sampled at 34 sites.
- **Workshop:** The September 24–25 workshop at Aravaipa Canyon drew about 30 participants and was described by the authors as a success.
- **Recruitment costs:** Total recruitment costs were $1,106.75, which equals $18.14 per volunteer across 61 participants. (The report also gives a figure of $1,105.75 in one place, probably a scan error.)
- **Volunteer cost per day:** $25.76 per volunteer-day without equipment, or $30.76 with equipment rental. This includes a $7.62 data entry fee.
- **Employee cost per day:** About $211.44 per [[Arizona Game and Fish Seasonal Employees]] day in the field, including trip preparation ($30.48), one person-day in the field ($60.96), travel money ($20.00), truck rental for 250 miles ($95.00), and equipment ($5.00).
- **Cost comparison:** The report states that volunteers cost substantially less than paid staff. Using the unit costs, 60 site-days at the employee rate would total about $12,686, versus a volunteer total of roughly $1,900 to $2,000. The printed savings figure is garbled in the scan, but the difference is more than $10,000. The authors note that two employees usually travel together, so actual employee costs would be higher.
- **Long-term value:** The authors see the OFC as the start of a [[Long-Term Monitoring]] dataset on fish community changes. They recommend continuing the project to provide systematic evaluation of change across the region's habitats.
- **Next steps:** Organizers planned to mail new invitations for the following October and asked participants to recruit colleagues and submit overdue data sheets. The data are held in a computerized database at the department, and copies are available on request.
