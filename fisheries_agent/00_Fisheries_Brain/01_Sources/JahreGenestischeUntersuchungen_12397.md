# 40 Years of Genetic Studies on Fishes (Kosswig, 1965)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This review by [[Curt Kosswig]], published in the *Abhandlungen und Verhandlungen des Naturwissenschaftlichen Vereins in Hamburg* (N.F. Bd. X, 1965), summarizes four decades of genetic research on fishes, mainly live-bearing toothcarps (Poeciliidae, Xiphophorini) and cyprinodonts. It argues that differences between related species and races are usually polygenic, that domestication and aquarium hybridization have produced valuable genetic material, and that sex determination, polymorphism, reproductive isolation and cave adaptation all show complex multi-gene control. The paper is a selective overview, with a bibliography that the author states is not complete.

## Background & Description

- **Scope:** The paper opens with a general theory of polygenic inheritance (additive versus non-additive polymery) and the "transfer of gene function" principle attributed to [[Munier]] (1939).
- **Historical framework:** Early work began with Gerschler (1914) on crosses between live-bearing toothcarp genera. From about 1924 a network of research schools developed in the USA, Germany and Turkey. Named workers include [[Myron Gordon]], [[Hans Haussler]] and [[Winge]].
- **Model organisms:** The main study systems were [[Xiphophorus helleri]] (swordtail), [[Platypoecilus maculatus]] (southern platyfish), [[Lebistes reticulatus]] (guppy), [[Oryzias latipes]] (medaka), [[Carassius auratus]] (goldfish), [[Aphanius anatoliae]], [[Gasterosteus aculeatus]] and [[Astyanax Mexicanus]] (cave and surface forms).
- **Scale:** The author notes that eight Xiphophorini species, some with several subspecies, were studied across "many tens of thousands" of fish.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Domestication:** Goldfish (from the Prussian carp lineage) and the carp have long histories of selective breeding. The Shubunkin is a heterozygote between common goldfish and a less attractive form.
- **Anadromous stickleback:** The North Sea population of *Gasterosteus aculeatus* contains three morphs (trachurus, semiarmatus, leiurus). Freshwater populations in central and northern Europe, and in Iceland, are almost entirely leiurus. Gene T (trachurus) and t (leiurus) are associated with only small selective differences. Iznik Lake (Turkey) populations are semiarmatus-like, through a different genetic basis.
- **Cave fish:** The surface *Astyanax mexicanus* is already negatively phototactic and can spawn and rear young in darkness, which is considered pre-adaptation to cave life.
- **Aminoacid hypothesis:** Gordon's work suggests that colour-gene modifier systems in Xiphophorini may help control free amino acid levels, which are characteristic of each species.
- **Chromosome numbers:** Most Aphaniini and many other toothcarps have a haploid number of n = 24. Exceptions include *Mollienesia velifera* (n = 23), *Lebistes reticulatus* (n = 22), *Notobranchius* spp. (n = 19), and *Pterolebias* (n = 10).

## Key Findings

**1. Polygenic basis of differences**
- Interspecific and interracial differences are usually polygenic. Single Mendelian allele differences mostly concern intrapopulation variants such as melanism.
- Colour gene expression in hybrids is controlled by separate modifier systems for each gene (e.g. N, Sd, Sp, Sr, Sb, Dr).

**2. Introgressive hybridization**
- Combining a colour gene from one species with modifiers from another can produce novel phenotypes (e.g. red helleri albinos, Wagtail platy).
- Melanomas and erythrophoromas can arise in hybrids. The author notes that melanomas can regress where they border developing erythrophoromas.

**3. Sex determination**
- *Lebistes* was first shown to have Y-linked sex determination (Winge, 1922). Later work showed that most species have heterogametic males, but some populations (e.g. in *Platypoecilus*) have heterogametic females.
- *Xiphophorus helleri* shows polygenic sex determination with additive "masculinizing" genes. Tilapia hybrid crosses that yield all-male progeny are used in aquaculture.
- Dzwillo (1962) showed experimentally that hormone treatment of the embryonic gonad determines sex in *Lebistes*.
- Intersexes and true hermaphrodites (*Rivulus marmoratus*) occur as rare exceptions.

**4. Polymorphism**
- Transitory polymorphism is contrasted with balanced polymorphism. The stickleback example is transitory, and balanced polymorphism is suggested for *Platypoecilus*.
- In *Lebistes*, male colour morphs are inherited via Y-linked "supergenes". The author disputes that these have selective value.

**5. Systematic characters**
- Limia species, *Xiphophorus* species (gonopodium morphology) and *Mollienesia* differ polygenically.
- Sword length in *Xiphophorus* is polymeric. Courtship behaviour is inherited independently of sword length. The author proposes a possible three-gene system for courtship.

**6. Speciation mechanisms**
- Twin species (*Aphanius anatoliae* and *A. chantrei*) produce hybrids that are fertile in females but sterile in males because of complementary sterility genes.
- Repeated backcrossing gradually removes male sterility.
- In live-bearing toothcarps, species-specific sperm transfer gives reproductive isolation. Heterologous sperm are less able to reach the ovary and less viable in the oviduct.
- *Mollienesia formosa* is a gynogenetic all-female species that requires sperm from a related species to trigger development (merospermy).

**7. Cave fish**
- Crosses between surface *Astyanax* and cave *Anoptichthys* produce polygenic segregation for eye size and pigmentation. The author favours mutation pressure over selection as the cause of degenerate traits, citing Darwin's and Remane's principle that "what does no harm is retained".
