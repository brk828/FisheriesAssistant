# Descriptive Anatomy of Cairnsichthys rhombosomoides and Iriatherina werneri, and a Phylogenetic Analysis of Melanotaeniidae

#ichthyology #marine-biology #melanotaeniidae

## Executive Summary

This paper provides a detailed comparative osteological description of two monotypic [[Rainbowfish]] species, [[Cairnsichthys Rhombosomoides]] and [[Iriatherina Werneri]], and uses the new anatomical data in a 46-character cladistic analysis of 22 atheriniform taxa. The authors conclude that [[Melanotaeniidae]] is monophyletic, comprising eight genera: [[Bedotia]] and [[Rheocles]] from Madagascar and six genera from Australia and New Guinea. They propose a new subfamily, Iriatherininae, for Iriatherina, and retain the family as defined by Allen (1980). The analysis resolves most relationships within the family but leaves a trichotomy among Chilatherina, Glossolepis and Melanotaenia unresolved. The authors hypothesise that the family's disjunct distribution reflects recent evolution from cosmopolitan marine Atheriniformes.

## Background & Description

The family has a long and contested taxonomic history, traced from Richardson (1843) and Gill (1862) through Regan (1914), Weber (1908), Munro (1967) and Allen (1980). Previous placements varied widely: Melanotaeniidae was treated as a subfamily of Atherinidae by several authors, while Derijst (1989) established priority for the name Pseudomugilidae over Melanotaeniidae for Allen's definition of the family.

Cairnsichthys rhombosomoides (Nichols & Raven) is a slender, rhomboid-fusiform fish with a terminal mouth, a notched upper jaw and 36-38 vertebrae. Its largest documented specimen is 66 mm SL (Allen, 1980), while the largest examined osteologically was 64 mm SL. Iriatherina werneri (Meinken, 1974) is a small fish with a reduced gape, extravagant fin elongation in adult males, and 32-34 vertebrae. The largest specimen examined osteologically was 34 mm SL.

Anatomical methods followed Aarn & Ivantsoff (1996), using cleared-and-stained specimens. Fin elements were counted on up to 12 fish per collection.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Melanotaeniidae are brackish- and freshwater fishes. Iriatherina inhabits the upper water level of densely vegetated, slow-flowing waters and has a distribution spanning the Torres Strait. Bedotia and Rheocles occur on the eastern seaboard of Madagascar, where they likely occupy oligotrophic highland streams, a habitat similar to that of Cairnsichthys in eastern Australia. Rhadinocentrus is restricted to freshwater in subtropical eastern Australia, from highland streams to coastal lakes. Melanotaenia is a wide-ranging, environmentally tolerant and speciose genus.

The authors note that Madagascar was isolated from mainland Africa for at least 65 Ma (Archer, 1984), and that Australia and New Guinea were linked as recently as 6000 years BP (Powell et al., 1984). These geological events are used to frame the biogeographic argument for the family's distribution. Bamber & Henderson (1988) proposed that phenotypic plasticity enabled marine atherinids to colonise estuaries, a process the authors see as analogous to the origin of the melanotaeniid freshwater radiation.

Life-history and morphological traits are linked to habitat. Reduced vertebral number has been associated with maturity at small size and with deep-bodied lacustrine forms, and the authors suggest that small body size may be an adaptation to the specific habitat of Iriatherina.

## Key Findings

- **Osteology:** Both Cairnsichthys and Iriatherina are described as monotypic and each has autapomorphies confirming its monotypic status. Cairnsichthys has a truncated rostrum, a caudally-directed vomerine condyle arrangement, a nasal substantially within the orbit, and a pelvic fin attached ligamentously to the fourth pleural rib. Iriatherina has a compact premaxilla, an unpaired reduced maxilla external ramus, a plate-like first anal pterygiophore, and an accessory axialpectoral ligament.
- **Subfamily Iriatherininae:** A new subfamily is erected for Iriatherina, based on a reduced maxillary external ramus, compact premaxilla, plate-like first anal pterygiophore, accessory axialpectoral ligament and odontodes around the jaws.
- **Phylogenetic dataset:** Forty-six characters were scored across 22 taxa using outgroup comparison in PAUP 3.1.1, with 100 bootstrap replicates and majority-rule consensus.
- **Most-parsimonious trees:** The heuristic search returned 12 most-parsimonious trees of length 184. Ordering of (Dentatherina + Phallostethinae), (Atherinidae + Telmatherinidae), and the Chilatherina/Glossolepis/Melanotaenia trichotomy varied among them.
- **Consensus tree:** The bootstrap consensus left a six-taxon polytomy among the in-group families (Atherionidae, Phallostethidae, Atherinidae, Telmatherinidae, Pseudomugilidae and Melanotaeniidae).
- **Melanotaeniid hierarchy:** (Iriatherina, (((Bedotia, Rheocles), (Cairnsichthys, Rhadinocentrus)), (Chilatherina/Glossolepis/Melanotaenia))).
- **Unresolved trichotomy:** No consistent characters were found to resolve Chilatherina, Glossolepis and Melanotaenia. The authors suggest these genera may need redefinition on a basis other than osteology and meristics.
- **Pseudomugilidae and Telmatherinidae:** Both are retained as valid families. Dentatherina is placed in Phallostethidae following Dyer & Chernoff (1996).
- **Biogeographic hypothesis:** The disjunct distribution of Melanotaeniidae, including Madagascar's Bedotia and Rheocles, is considered to reflect recent evolution from cosmopolitan marine Atheriniformes. Support for grouping Bedotia and Rheocles with the Australasian melanotaeniids on biogeographic grounds is acknowledged as speculative.
- **Caveats:** The authors warn that parsimony may equate convergent character states, potentially producing false monophyletic groupings. They recommend comparative developmental and molecular genetic evidence for future work.
- **Material:** Anatomy was examined across 16 atherine taxa and 6 outgroups. Specimen counts per lot are listed in the source's material-examined section (not reproduced here).
