# Leech Control (*Platybdella soleae*) on Flatfishes in an Aquaculture Unit

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Soleidae #Hirudinea #parasitology #aquaculture

## Executive Summary
This 1992 study addresses a severe ectoparasitic leech infestation (*Platybdella soleae*, family Hirudinea) observed in wild-caught [[Flatfish|flatfishes]] intended for establishing a captive broodstock for aquaculture. Between March and November 1982–1983, flatfish captured at the mouth of the [[Tagus Estuary]] near Trafaria, Lisbon, exhibited heavy leech infestations — in some cases up to ~20 leeches/cm². Researchers trialed five chemical treatments (malachite green, Neguvon, formalin, copper chloride, and Furanace) across multiple trials to identify an effective, low-mortality protocol. The optimal regimen identified was **copper chloride (5 mg/l, 20-minute bath)** to eliminate the parasites, followed by a **Furanace (12 mg/l, 20-minute bath)** treatment to prevent secondary opportunistic infections at the wound sites left by parasite removal.

## Background & Description
- **Parasite**: *Platybdella soleae* (Grasse, 1959), a marine leech (Hirudinea) ectoparasitic on soleid flatfish.
  - Attaches to host via a **posterior sucker**, anchored specifically to a fish scale.
  - Manual removal ("hand cleaning with a nipper") of the leech pulls out the attached scale, creating an open wound — a portal for **opportunistic secondary infections** (myxobacteria, ciliates).
  - Infestation density observed: up to **~20 leeches/cm²** on some host individuals.
- **Host species**: Three [[Sole (Fish)|sole]] species were affected and used in the captive broodstock trials:
  - *Solea vulgaris* (common sole)
  - *Solea senegalensis* (Senegalese sole)
  - *Solea lascaris* (sand sole)
- Host skin response to treatment stress included excessive mucus production (notable with 4% formalin treatment), and secondary **myxobacterial skin infections** and **ciliate infestation** observed after ineffective initial treatments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat**: Wild flatfish were captured from the **mouth of the Tagus Estuary** (Trafaria, near Lisbon, Portugal), a brackish/estuarine environment, then transferred into a **closed seawater aquaculture system** for broodstock development.
- **Host-parasite dynamics**: The leech *Platybdella soleae* is a scale-attaching ectoparasite of estuarine [[Soleidae|soleid flatfishes]], with infestation intensity apparently linked to wild capture conditions in the estuary (heavy infestation noted broadly from March to November).
- **Aquaculture context**: This study is situated within efforts to establish sole broodstocks for [[Aquaculture]] production, highlighting the practical challenge of transitioning wild-caught estuarine broodstock into captive systems while managing parasite load and secondary disease risk.

## Key Findings
- **Five treatment regimens** were systematically tested and compared (see below), evaluated on: (1) effect on parasites, (2) need for hand-cleaning, (3) secondary infection outcome, and (4) overall mortality.

| Treatment | Effect on Parasites | Hand Cleaning Needed | Overall Mortality | Remarks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Malachite green (2 ppm, 20 min) + Neguvon (0.8 ppm, 3 min) | No effect (malachite); larger parasites killed (Neguvon) | Yes | 31.9% | Heavy myxobacterial + ciliate secondary infection |
| Neguvon (0.8 ppm, 3 min) + Furanace (12 mg/l, 20 min) | Larger parasites killed | Yes | None | No secondary infections |
| Formalin 4% (1:4000, 20 min) + Furanace (12 mg/l, 20 min) | Larger parasites killed | Yes | None | No secondary infections |
| Formalin 4% (1:4000, 45 min) + Furanace (12 mg/l, 20 min) | Larger parasites killed | Yes | None | No secondary infections |
| Formalin 4% (1:4000, alt. trial) | Larger parasites killed | Yes | 75% | Heavy skin infections |
| **Copper chloride (5 mg/l, 20 min) — 1st treatment** | Only smallest parasites remained | No | None | No secondary infections |
| **Copper chloride (5 mg/l, 20 min) — 2nd treatment** | Remaining leeches killed | No | None | — |
| **Copper chloride (5 mg/l, 20 min) + Furanace (12 mg/l, 20 min)** | Same effective results | No | None | No secondary infections |

- **Best overall protocol**: **Copper chloride at 5 mg/l** was the most effective agent for complete ectoparasite removal **without requiring hand cleaning**, thereby avoiding the scale-loss wounds that predispose fish to secondary infection.
- **Furanace (12 mg/l)** treatment following parasiticide baths is critical for **preventing opportunistic secondary infections**, especially when hand-removal of residual parasites is necessary.
- **4% Formalin** was effective against the leech but caused excessive stress and abnormal mucus production, and in one trial led to **75% mortality** — thus **not recommended** despite parasiticidal efficacy.
- **Malachite green (zinc-free)** showed **no effect** on the parasite and was associated with the highest recorded mortality (31.9%) and secondary infections.
- The study concludes that the optimal treatment sequence for aquaculture leech control is **copper chloride (5 mg/l) followed by Furanace (12 mg/l)**, balancing parasiticidal efficacy with fish welfare and disease prevention.
- Findings are relevant to broader [[Fish Disease Management]] and [[Ectoparasite Control]] practices in marine finfish aquaculture, particularly for species transitioning from wild capture to closed recirculating systems.
