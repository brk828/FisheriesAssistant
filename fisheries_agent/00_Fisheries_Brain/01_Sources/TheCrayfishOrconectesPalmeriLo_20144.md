# The Crayfish Orconectes palmeri longimanus in Kansas

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cambaridae

*Note: the source is a crustacean study, not a fish study. Its taxon tag is #cambaridae, and the ichthyology and marine-biology tags are retained only because the system format requires them.*

## Executive Summary

Metcalf and Distler report the first Kansas records of the crayfish [[Orconectes Palmeri]] subspecies *longimanus*, collected from two western tributaries of the [[Big Caney River]] (Verdigris River System) in Cowley County between October 1961 and August 1962. The authors document morphology, coloration, habitat, and reproductive timing, and they note that the Kansas populations may be somewhat isolated and may have diverged slightly from populations to the south in Oklahoma.

## Background & Description

- **Taxon:** [[Orconectes Palmeri]] subspecies *longimanus* (Faxon), previously recorded as *Cambarus longimanus*.
- **Prior records:** [[Creaser and Ortenburger 1933]] reported the subspecies from the Verdigris River in Rogers County, Oklahoma. [[Williams 1954]] mapped an additional Tulsa County, Oklahoma record that the authors could not verify in preserved collections. [[Penn 1947]] mapped several localities in northeastern and northern Oklahoma.
- **Range (per [[Penn 1957]]):** western tributaries of the Mississippi River from the Arkansas River to the Gulf, plus Gulf drainages from the Mississippi to the Guadalupe River, Texas.
- **Morphology:** Adults were 30–50 mm in cephalothorax length in the measured sample. Ratios (cephalothorax/areola, chela length/dactyl length, and others) were compared with Penn's data. The areola was never obliterated among 35 adults examined.
- **Coloration:** Brownish olive dorsum, light tan stripe on the branchiostegite with 5–25 dark brown spots, and red-orange and blue-green accents on the appendages. Spotting on the branchiostegite was not seen in several Oklahoma and Arkansas drainages.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat:** The Kansas collection sites drain the eastern flank of the [[Flint Hills]], which are cuesta scarps on Permian strata. Gradients were 31.0 ft/mile (Cedar Creek) and 27.4 ft/mile (Otter Creek), higher than streams to the west and east, and riffles are frequent. Bottoms are limestone and chert rubble and gravel over limestone bedrock. The water is clear except after heavy rain. The watershed is mostly uncultivated ranchland with tall prairie grasses and fringe forest. During drought, surface flow ceases and crayfish are confined to intermittent pools, as observed in Cedar Creek on August 27, 1962.

**Microhabitat use:** In August–November 1961, crayfish were found under large stones, in crevices, and under ledges. In January 1962 (water temperature 43.5 °F), they were taken by disturbing gravel and rubble, and they appeared torpid. In May 1962, adults were captured by seining and by hand in deep pools. In June 1962, young were common in shallow streamside pools, backwaters, and bank vegetation after a flood on June 2.

**Biogeographic inference:** The authors suggest that the areolar characters may reflect lotic conditions, citing [[Hobbs and Marchand 1943]], who associate wide areolae with restricted lotic environments. A specimen from the [[North Canadian River]] at about 101° 30' W, in a sandy, braided, low-gradient High Plains stream, had obliterated areolae. The authors are undecided whether these differences have taxonomic weight or reflect local environmental conditions.

## Key Findings

1. **New state record:** The collections represent the first records of *O. palmeri* in Kansas. Total specimens reported: Otter Creek 30 (KU 858); Cedar Creek 4 + 8 + 51 + 93 + 55 + 43 (KU 859–864) across seven collection dates from October 1961 to August 1962.
2. **Morphological divergence:** Kansas specimens show a proportionately longer areola and a lower cephalothorax/chela ratio (a longer chela) than Penn's samples. Female ratios fell below every value Penn reported. The authors describe the Kansas populations as possibly diverged from southern populations and possibly isolated.
3. **Reproduction:** No ovigerous females and no crayfish smaller than 6.5 mm were found. Young were first observed on May 21, 1962. Spawning likely occurred before May 5, 1962. Egg sizes of 0.9–1.4 mm (October) and 1.7–2.1 mm (November) suggest spawning early in the year. The smallest form I male was 27 mm, taken August 27, 1962.
4. **Growth lag:** Mean lengths were similar on July 8 and August 27, 1962. The authors propose three explanations: selective sampling, an extended reproductive period, or a growth lag from nutritional limits, reduced flow, or high density.
5. **Taxonomic uncertainty:** The authors find the subspecies boundaries of *O. palmeri* poorly defined, following [[Penn 1957]], and leave open whether the variation is taxonomic or ontogenetic.
