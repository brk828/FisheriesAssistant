# Redescription and Distribution of the Exotic Snail Thiara (Melanoides) tuberculata (Gastropoda: Thiaridae) in Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #thiaridae

## Executive Summary

This Spanish-language paper (with English summary) by [[Contreras-Arquieta]], [[Guajardo-Martínez]] and [[Contreras-Balderas]] redescribes the exotic freshwater prosobranch snail *Thiara (Melanoides) tuberculata* ([[Müller]], 1774) in Mexico and maps its national distribution. The species is thought to have been introduced around the 1960s, probably through the [[Aquarium Trade]]. The authors report 63 new localities out of 65 known, spread across 12 states, with [[Veracruz]] holding the most (42 localities). The study redescribes shell, operculum and radular characters, and documents habitat conditions and possible dispersal pathways.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomy and history.** The first Mexican record was [[Abbott]]'s 1973 report from the vicinity of Veracruz. A second record (as *Oncomelania* sp.) came from Parras, Coahuila, in 1980 ([[Jiménez-G]] et al.), and a third (as *Tarebia* sp.) from the same locality in 1985. [[Pointier]] and [[McCullough]] (1989) also reported it from Parras. Several synonyms were linked to the species, and the authors suggest that *Melanoides maculata* reported by [[Reddell]] (1981) from Cantil Blanco cave, Veracruz, probably refers to this species.

**Type locality and native range.** The type locality is the Coromandel Coast, India, described by Müller in 1774. Worldwide, the species occurs across many African countries, South and Southeast Asia, Pacific islands, and parts of the Caribbean and South America. It has also been introduced to the southern United States, including Texas, Florida, Arizona and California.

**Morphology (summary).**
- Shell: narrowly conical-elongate, imperforate, 11 to 13 whorls, brown with reddish-brown blotches; reticulate sculpture; oval aperture with a brown band on the columellar lip; deep sutures.
- Shell length: 22.7 to 45 mm (maximum in the Tehuacán, Puebla population, 45 mm, apex eroded by about 6 mm).
- Operculum: paucispiral, dark brown to black, with an eccentric nucleus of 2 to 2.5 whorls.
- Reproduction: parthenogenetic and ovoviviparous, with 62 to 88 juveniles per brood pouch.
- Gills: 157 to 240 uniserial triangular lamellae.
- Mantle collar: 12 digitiform papillae (occasionally 11).
- Radula: taenioglossate, 69 to 121 rows of 7 transverse teeth; formula R (4–6)-1-(4–6), L 1-1-(4–6), inner marginal 10–11 cusps, outer marginal 10–12 cusps.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Found in springs, streams, rivers, ponds and cenotes. In Veracruz it occurred in 95% of sampled rivers, at water temperatures of 17–23 °C and conductivity of 200–900 µS/cm. In Durango it lives in thermal waters at 33 °C and pH 7, with densities of 1,500 to 2,500 individuals per m².
- **Behaviour:** Buries in sand and mud by day and emerges at night and in the morning to climb substrates up to the air interface.
- **Pollution:** Apparently intolerant of contaminated water. In Guadalupe, Nuevo León, it was found only in a spring tributary of the Río La Silla, which receives domestic and industrial effluents upstream and downstream of the confluence.
- **Dispersal:** The exact mechanism is unknown. The authors consider the aquarium trade the most probable main source of introduction, since aquarists discard gravel-wash water into drains that reach rivers. The operculum may help survival during transport. Waterbirds carrying juveniles on feet or feathers are another possible vector. A single individual can found a new population.
- **Ecological significance:** The species matters for community structure and density, and as a potential vector of disease. In the study area it was recorded as an intermediate host of the trematode *Centrocestus formosanus* at the Manantial Las Estacas, Morelos.

## Key Findings

- **Distribution:** 63 new localities were recorded, and the total is 65 localities across 23 hydrological basins and 12 states (Chiapas 2, Chihuahua 2, Coahuila 4, Durango 2, Morelos 2, Nayarit 4, Nuevo León 2, Puebla 1, Quintana Roo 1, Tabasco 1, Tamaulipas 1, Veracruz 42). The species is now established and reproductive in Mexican waters and is considered part of the introduced fauna.
- **Morphometrics:** Morphometric data were compared between two populations (Nuevo León and Coahuila, Table 1). Shell height ranged from 13.11 to 35.55 mm and the aperture ratio from 1.49 to 1.85.
- **Morphs:** Following [[Pointier]] (1989), the Mexican specimens best match the "Goiser" morph, though some populations show the "Falaise" morph (without the columellar band). Some Veracruz and Nayarit populations have decollate shells that lack the embryonic whorl, probably due to water chemistry (low mineral salts).
- **Taxonomic conclusions:** Records of *Oncomelania* sp. and *Tarebia* sp. from Parras are synonyms of *T. tuberculata*. Morphological differences from *T. granifera* were also noted (higher, narrower shell, finer reticulate sculpture, no nodules).
- **Documented populations:** Notable records include the Presa Rodrigo Gómez, Nuevo León (334 specimens); Ojo de Agua "Parque La Mota", Ocampo, Coahuila (264 specimens); and the Balneario La Concha, Peñón Blanco, Durango (321 specimens).
- **Implications:** The authors suggest the species may occur in other, still unsurveyed states and recommend further study of its ecological effects and dispersal routes.
