# Title: Code of Federal Regulations, 50 CFR Part 17: Endangered and Threatened Wildlife and Plants

#ichthyology #marine-biology #fishes

## Executive Summary

This document is an excerpt of the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service regulations under 50 CFR Part 17, which implement the [[Endangered Species Act]] of 1973 for endangered and threatened wildlife and plants. It sets out definitions, prohibitions, permit procedures, special rules, and the official lists of protected species, including fishes, with a footnote key tracking when each listing was published. The text is a regulatory framework rather than a research study, so its "findings" are the legal rules and listing criteria it establishes.

## Background & Description

The regulations (Rev. 1/1/89) identify species determined by the Director to be endangered or threatened under section 4(a) of the Act, and carry over species designated as endangered under the Endangered Species Conservation Act of 1969. Key structure:

- **Subparts A–H** cover general provisions, lists, endangered and threatened wildlife and plants, similarity of appearance, and experimental populations.
- **Subpart J** covers [[Manatee Protection Areas]] (§§ 17.100–17.108).
- **Authority:** Pub. L. 93-205 (1973) and later amendments, codified at 16 U.S.C. 1531 et seq.

Key defined terms include:

- **"Take"**: an intentional or negligent act creating a likelihood of injury by significantly disrupting normal behavior such as breeding, feeding or sheltering. "Harm" may include significant habitat modification where it actually kills or injures wildlife.
- **"Population"**: a group of fish or wildlife in the same taxon below subspecies level, in common spatial arrangement, that interbreeds when mature.
- **"Captive-bred"** and **"Enhance the propagation or survival"**: defined for captive populations and conservation breeding.
- **"Incidental taking"**: taking that is incidental to, and not the purpose of, a lawful activity.
- **"Subsistence"** and **"Wasteful manner"**: defined in relation to Alaska Native harvest.

The listing tables (§ 17.11 for wildlife, § 17.12 for plants) give common name, scientific name, vertebrate population where endangered or threatened, status (E, T, E(S/A), T(S/A), XE, XN), when listed (Federal Register citation footnote), critical habitat and special rules. Plants are grouped by family.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The regulations apply to species threatened by habitat loss, overutilization, disease or predation, inadequate regulation, and other natural or manmade factors (50 CFR 424.11(c)). Delisting is allowed only for extinction, recovery, or original classification error (50 CFR 424.11(d)). The historic range column notes that present distributions may be greatly reduced from historic ranges.

Aquatic and freshwater taxa are prominent in the listings and include [[Gila Chub]], [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]], [[Shortnose Sturgeon]], and numerous darters, dace, madtoms and pupfishes, along with mussels, snails, crayfish and cave-dwelling invertebrates. Marine species include sea turtles, [[West Indian Manatee]] and [[Caribbean Monk Seal]].

## Key Findings

**Listing scale and structure (as stated in the document):**
- Section 17.11 lists the endangered and threatened wildlife, organized into mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fishes, snails, clams, crustaceans, insects and arachnids. The list is extensive, running across roughly 20 printed pages.
- Section 17.12 lists endangered and threatened plants grouped by family (for example Cactaceae, Asteraceae, Orchidaceae, Polygonaceae).
- The footnote key covers Federal Register listing citations from 1967 through 1988 (footnotes 1 to 342).

**Fishes (§ 17.44 special rules and listed fishes):**
- Special rules are provided for a number of fish species, including [[Lahontan Cutthroat Trout]], [[Paiute Cutthroat Trout]], [[Arizona Trout]], [[Bayou Darter]], [[Slender Chub]], [[Spotfin Chub]], [[Slackwater Darter]], [[Yellowfin Madtom]], [[Leopard Darter]], [[Little Kern Golden Trout]], [[Greenback Cutthroat Trout]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], [[Yaqui Catfish]], [[Big Spring Spinedace]], [[Niangua Darter]], [[Warner Sucker]], [[Desert Dace]], [[Railroad Valley Springfish]], [[Sonora Chub]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Pecos Bluntnose Shiner]], [[Waccamaw Silverside]], and [[Little Colorado Spinedace]].
- Many of these fish rules allow take under applicable State law, and some allow incidental catch during State-permitted recreational fishing with immediate return of the fish to habitat.
- Most special rules prohibit possession, sale, transport, import or export of fish taken in violation of the rules.

**Key protections and permits:**
- § 17.21 prohibits import, export, take, interstate sale and commercial transactions in endangered wildlife, with exceptions for self-defense, government agents and captive-bred animals.
- § 17.22 authorizes permits for scientific purposes, enhancement of propagation or survival, and incidental taking, requiring a conservation plan for incidental take.
- § 17.23 authorizes economic hardship permits, based on contracts binding before the listing notice.
- § 17.31 extends most endangered-wildlife prohibitions to threatened wildlife.
- § 17.4 exempts pre-Act wildlife held in captivity on December 28, 1973 (with four worked examples).
- § 17.5 exempts qualifying Alaska Natives taking wildlife for subsistence.
- § 17.7 exempts certain captive raptors held on November 10, 1978.

**Special rules for mammals and other taxa (§§ 17.40–17.48):**
- Eastern Gray, Red and Western Gray kangaroos (§ 17.40(a)), the [[Grizzly Bear]] (§ 17.40(b), with detailed Montana hunting-area provisions), primates (§ 17.40(c)), the [[Gray Wolf]] in Minnesota (§ 17.40(d), with five zones), [[African Elephant]] ivory trade (§ 17.40(e)), [[Leopard]] in southern Africa (§ 17.40(f)), and [[Utah Prairie Dog]] (§ 17.40(g), capped at 5,000 animals annually).
- [[American Alligator]] (§ 17.42(a)) and sea turtles (§ 17.42(b)).
- [[San Marcos Salamander]] (§ 17.43(a)).
- Madison Cave isopod (§ 17.46(a)).

**Similarity of appearance and experimental populations:**
- Subpart E allows a non-listed species that closely resembles a listed one to be treated as E or T, marked "(S/A)", based on enforcement difficulty, added threat, and enforcement benefit.
- Subpart H defines experimental populations (essential and nonessential, "XE" and "XN"), with separate special rules for vertebrates.

**Delisting record:** The document lists species removed from the lists (for example Tecopa pupfish, longjaw cisco, blue pike, Sampson's pearly mussel, Amistad gambusia), with reasons such as extinction, original data in error, or recovery (e.g. Palau dove).

**Manatee protection:** Subpart J establishes manatee protection areas, including emergency establishment procedures and enforcement facilitation (§§ 17.100–17.108).
