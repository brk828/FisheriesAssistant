# Etheostoma aquali, a New Percid Fish (Subgenus Nothonotus) from the Duck and Buffalo Rivers, Tennessee

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Percidae

## Executive Summary
Williams and Etnier (1978) describe the Coppercheek Darter, *Etheostoma aquali*, a new species of the subgenus [[Nothonotus]] known only from the [[Duck River]] and [[Buffalo River]], tributaries of the [[Tennessee River]] in central Tennessee. It is most similar to [[Etheostoma maculatum sanguifluum]] but differs in squamation and pigmentation, notably copper-colored wavy lines on the cheeks of both sexes and no dark suborbital bar. The species is state-protected as threatened. Dams and pollution threaten it. The paper also reports recent distribution records for three other Nothonotus species. The new species raises the subgenus to twelve species.

## Background & Description
- **Taxonomic context:** Research over about 15 years on [[Nothonotus]] darters produced five new species, summarized by [[Zorach]] (1972). *E. aquali* brings the subgenus to twelve species. The three allopatric subspecies of [[Etheostoma maculatum]] may eventually be recognized as species.
- **Earlier misidentification:** Duck River specimens were previously identified as [[Etheostoma microlepidum]] (Raney and Zorach, 1967) and later flagged as an undescribed species (Zorach, 1972).
- **Type material:** Holotype is an adult male, TU 105479, 55 mm SL, from Flat Creek, a Duck River tributary at Tennessee Highway 64, Bedford Co., 13 April 1967. The allotype is a female, TU 105480, 43 mm SL. Many paratypes come from both river systems, held in several museum collections (TU, USNM, UAIC, UT, INHS, FSM, UMMZ).
- **Methods:** Counts and measurements follow [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1958); diagonal scale counts follow Raney and Suttkus (1964); vertebral counts follow Bailey and Gosline (1955).
- **Size and form:** Robust, moderate to large Nothonotus. The largest specimen seen is a male of 67 mm SL. Adult males are typically about 60 mm SL and adult females about 50 mm SL (largest 56 mm).
- **Meristics:**
  - Lateral line is complete, with 57-62 scales in the description text (mean 62.2, holotype 60). The diagnosis gives 62 (57-67).
  - Dorsal fin has XII-XIV spines (mean 12.9) and 11-14 soft rays (mean 12.7).
  - Total dorsal rays 24-27 (mean 25.5).
  - Anal rays 8-10 (mean 9.2).
  - Pectoral rays 12-16 (mean 13.6).
  - Vertebrae 38-40 (mean 38.96).
  - Scale rows above the lateral line 5-8 (mean 6.9).
  - Caudal peduncle scale rows 21-26.
- **Cephalic pores (left side, 25 individuals):** supratemporal 3; lateral 5 (23) or 6 (2); supraorbital 3; infraorbital 8 (24) or 9 (1); preoperculomandibular 10 (24) or 11 (1). Gill membranes are separate, with branchiostegal rays 6-6 and a broad frenum.
- **Squamation:** Opercles are completely scaled. The cheeks have about 3-5 ctenoid (occasionally cycloid) scales behind the eye. These scales are also present in *E. microlepidum*, *E. moorei*, *E. rubrum* and the three *E. maculatum* subspecies, and absent in other Nothonotus and in *E. juliae*.
- **Male coloration:** Body grayish to dark olive brown, with about 20-35 bright red lateral spots, mostly on the posterior three-fourths of the body. The cheeks carry 2-3 copper-colored lines. The anal fin is bright red distally, and the pelvic fins are bright red. Colors show little seasonal variation (samples from February, April, July and October).
- **Female coloration:** Olivaceous with brownish to dark gray blotches. No red spots were observed. The cheeks have fainter copper reticulations, and the median fins are pale yellow with dark spots.
- **Etymology:** The epithet comes from the Cherokee "agaquali," meaning "cheek" (Alexander, 1971), referring to the diagnostic copper cheek markings.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Distribution:** In the Duck River it occurs only in the main channel and the lower portions of a few larger tributaries in Bedford, Maury, Hickman and Humphreys counties. In the Buffalo River it occurs only in the main channel in Lewis, Wayne and Perry counties.
- **Habitat:** Shoals in large streams and rivers, 0.3-1 m deep with moderate to swift current. Substrate is gravel and cobble mixed with large unconsolidated rocks, and the darter is most often found among the large rocks.
- **Sampling difficulty:** Large-stream habitats are hard to sample, which may explain scarce material. [[Etheostoma tippecanoe]] (Bedford Co.) and [[Etheostoma camurum]] (Maury Co.) were only recently taken in the Duck River after many years of collecting. *E. microlepidum* was recently taken for the first time in the [[Red River]] in Robertson Co.
- **Threats:**
  - [[Normandy Dam]] (upper Duck River, recently completed) is expected to reduce or eliminate nearby populations.
  - [[Columbia Dam]] (middle Duck River, under construction) will eliminate populations in that area. Its effects on the roughly 120 river miles below the dam site cannot be predicted precisely, but the authors expect reduced species diversity, with disproportionate effects on threatened fishes and on endangered and threatened mollusks.
  - Nothonotus are intolerant of reservoirs and show only limited tolerance of tailwater habitats.
  - Inadequately treated industrial and municipal sewage affects parts of the Duck River and, to a lesser extent, the Buffalo River.
- **Conservation status:** Protected as threatened by Tennessee, and under consideration for similar status by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service.

## Key Findings
- *E. aquali* is a new species of the *E. maculatum* species group within [[Nothonotus]], probably most closely related to *E. m. sanguifluum* of the [[Cumberland River]] system.
- **Diagnostic characters:**
  - It differs from *E. acuticeps* in having the opercles and anterodorsal cheeks scaled.
  - It lacks the black marginal bands on the anal, caudal and soft dorsal fins that occur in males of *E. m. vulneratum*, *E. microlepidum*, *E. moorei* and *E. rubrum*.
  - It differs from *E. m. maculatum* in having red or orange in the male anal fin.
  - It differs from *E. m. sanguifluum* in having wavy copper lines on the cheeks of both sexes and lacking a dark suborbital bar.
  - Male red spots do not extend forward onto the head, unlike all *E. maculatum* subspecies.
  - Females resemble *E. m. maculatum* in lacking red lateral spots.
- **Comparison with *E. m. sanguifluum*:** Mean lateral-line scales 62.18 versus 58.07; total dorsal rays mean 25.53 versus 24.97; anal rays mean 9.22 versus 8.18; scales above the lateral line mean 6.89 versus 7.53.
- **Morphometrics (Table 1):** For males (FSM 24499/UT 91.1282), mean SL was 61.6 mm (58.5-65.7), and for females 50.6 mm (45.1-55.2). Mean head length was 251.2 thousandths of SL in males and 253.2 in females.
- **Taxonomic corrections:**
  - The Duck River *E. microlepidum* record (TU 32989) was actually *E. aquali*.
  - Gilbert's (1891) Duck River *E. maculatum* record is probably *E. aquali*, but those specimens appear lost.
  - Two USNM 36670 specimens from Shoal Creek, Florence, Alabama (1884), previously called *E. microlepidum*, are neither that species, *E. m. vulneratum*, nor *E. aquali*. They lack scales behind the eye.
  - With a single Elk River specimen (TU 30271, Fayetteville, Lincoln Co.), they may represent an undescribed taxon, with 63-64 lateral scale rows and affinities to the *E. maculatum* group. The populations may be extinct, and the authors recommend more collecting in northern Alabama and southern Tennessee.
- **Footnote correction:** Part of the published color description of *E. m. vulneratum* (Zorach and Raney, 1967) was actually based on John Ramsey's notes for *E. chlorobranchium*.
- **Conservation outlook:** Impoundment and pollution of the [[Duck River]] imperil the species and its diverse associated fauna.
