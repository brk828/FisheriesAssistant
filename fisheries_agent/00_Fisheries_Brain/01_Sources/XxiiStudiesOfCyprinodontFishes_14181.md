# Variation in Lucania parva, Its Establishment in Western United States, and a New Species from Coahuila, México

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This 1965 monograph by [[Carl L. Hubbs]] and [[Robert Rush Miller]] revises the genus [[Lucania]] (Fundulini), recognizes three species, and describes [[Lucania interioris]] as a new endemic from the [[Cuatro Ciénegas Basin]], Coahuila, México. It analyzes geographic variation in [[Lucania parva]] across its native Atlantic and Gulf range, and documents its recent, unexplained appearance at five western United States sites. The authors attribute the Utah and southern California populations to inadvertent transfer with gamefish from federal hatcheries on the [[Pecos River]], New Mexico, and the San Francisco and Yaquina bay populations to introduction with eastern oysters or ship ballast water.

## Background & Description

- **Nomenclature:** The authors argue that the species should be credited to Baird alone (*Cyprinodon parvus* Baird, 1855), not "Baird and Girard," under Article 50 of the International Code of Zoological Nomenclature. *Limia venusta* Girard is a synonym of *L. parva*. *Limia poecilioides* is synonymized with *Poecilia latipinna*. *Lucania browni* is a synonym of *Cyprinodon macularius*.
- **Genus limits:** *Lucania* is restricted to three species: *L. parva*, *L. interioris*, and *L. goodei* (formerly *Chriopeops goodei*, treated as a subgenus). *Leptolucania ommata* is excluded as a separate phyletic line. *Cubanichthys cubensis* and *Chriopeops pengelleyi* may be related but are not congeneric.
- **Diagnostic counts:** Meristic traits used include dorsal and anal rays, caudal and pectoral rays, lateral scale rows, vertebrae, gill rakers, and cephalic sensory pores (preopercular, supraorbital, lachrymal, mandibular), following [[Gosline]] (1949) pore patterns.
- **New species:** *Lucania interioris* is a small fish (maximum standard length 31 mm) with a shorter pelvic fin, longer predorsal length, more posterior dorsal and anal fins, and reduced cephalic pores. Its pelvic-to-predorsal ratio (4.9–6.5) separates it from *L. parva* (2.5–4.8). Males have chalky-blue dorsal fins and spawn against fibrous substrata near the bottom, unlike *L. parva* and *L. goodei*, which spawn near the surface.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Native range:** *L. parva* ranges from the south side of Cape Cod to the lower Rio Pánuco near Tampico. It occurs mainly in protected salt and brackish coastal waters, but in Florida it extends into freshwater, some far from the coast. It is abundant in the highly mineralized lower Pecos River.
- **Interior endemism:** *L. interioris* is confined to the nearly enclosed Cuatro Ciénegas Basin, a desert area with springs, marshes, and canals. Associated fishes include *Gambusia marshi*, *Gambusia longispinis*, an undescribed *Cyprinodon*, *Astyanax fasciatus mexicanus*, and *Cichlasoma* cf. *cyanoguttatum*. Radiocarbon dates from Qo de la Becerra (2,070 ± 250 B.P.) and cave sites suggest long-term isolation and surface water in the basin.
- **Variation and environment:** Many differences, such as body depth and fin-ray counts, appear partly phenotypic. Florida shows the most intense local differentiation, with the Florida Keys form deep-bodied with 26 modal vertebrae, and interior freshwater forms (Juniper Springs Creek) slender with 29 modal vertebrae.
- **Introductions:** Gamefish source hatcheries, oyster beds, and ship ballast are proposed as pathways. The authors consider mosquitofish stocking and aquarium release unlikely.

## Key Findings

- **Variation in *L. parva*:** Dorsal rays range from 9 to 14 (modal 11), anal rays from 8 to 13 (modal 9 or 10; grand mean 9.49), caudal rays from 12 to 18, pectoral rays from 12 to 15 (modal 13 or 14), and lateral scale rows from 23 to 29 (modal 27).
- **Gill rakers:** Range from 4 to 12 (modal 6 to 9). Southern New England and Long Island populations have 9 to 12 rakers, while Gulf and Pecos stocks have fewer.
- **Cephalic pores:** Marked reduction and proliferation occur in northern populations, which show preopercular and supraorbital variance, and in Florida Keys and Pecos stocks. Pore formulas for 750 specimens were tallied.
- **Florida cline:** Vertebral mode rises from 26 (Florida Keys) to 29 (Juniper Springs Creek), with body depth ratios from 2.9–3.0 (Keys) to 3.7 (interior).
- **Introduction to Utah and California:** Timpie Springs (first collected April 11, 1959; 14 adults), Blue Lake (December 28, 1961; 10 specimens), and Irvine Lake (November 5, 1963, and June 18, 1964; 1 and 6 specimens) are linked to [[Pecos River]] gamefish stocking from Dexter and Roswell hatcheries.
- **Introduction to San Francisco and Yaquina bays:** Records begin in 1958 (Richmond Tidal Slough, May 18, 1958; Corte Madera Creek, 1958–1961, with 114 additional specimens on October 10, 1959). The authors propose transport on eastern oysters (*Crassostrea virginica*) or in ship ballast. Yaquina Bay records include 1958 and 1960 captures and 15 specimens collected March 28, 1963.
- **Origin evidence:** Gill-raker counts and preopercular/supraorbital pore variance suggest bay populations derive from northern Atlantic stock. Low scale counts and anal-ray means in interior western stocks suggest Pecos origin.
- **Interior endemic:** *L. interioris* is a full species, distinct from *L. parva*. Its Pecos-like reduced pores may reflect a Pleistocene relict origin or retained juvenile traits. The type series (holotype UMMZ 179850, 26.5 mm standard length, La Angostura Canal, April 8, 1961) and numerous paratypes are catalogued.
- **Conservation note:** The authors report that habitats of *L. interioris* change severely; in June 1964 the saline marsh pools were about 5 cm deep at 41.5 °C, and no *Lucania* remained.
- **Future research:** The authors call for experimental rearing and transplant studies to separate genetic from environmental effects, especially in Florida.
