# Larval Razorback Sucker Collections at Lake Mohave, January-February 1997

#ichthyology #marine-biology #razorback-sucker

## Executive Summary

This letter from [[Paul C. Marsh]] (Research Professor, Arizona State University Technical Reports) to Thomas A. Burke of the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], dated 24 March 1997, transmits documentation of larval [[Razorback Sucker]] collections made in [[Lake Mohave]] between 20 January and 9 February 1997. The work was performed under a Bureau of Reclamation order (order number partly illegible in the scan). Field effort began late in the period because larvae were not considered abundant enough to justify travel. Collections were concentrated at [[Tequila Cove]] and yielded 2,194 larvae over two nights. The letter requests the client's approval that the documentation satisfies the terms of the agreement.

## Background & Description

The document is a transmittal letter, not a research report. It addresses only the razorback sucker, referred to by its common name; no scientific name, morphology, or life-history data are given. The author's affiliation is Arizona State University, and the letter lists the sender's address and contact details for the university's fish program.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Study area:** [[Lake Mohave]], a reservoir on the Arizona-Nevada border.
- **Site selection:** Collections were made at Tequila Cove (NPS Cove Number 102) because few larvae were available at other locations.
- **Abundance threshold:** According to the letter, field operations were deferred until larvae were available in "sufficient abundance" to justify travel to the site.
- **Sampling method:** Light-based collection. Effort is expressed in light-hours, implying that lights were used to attract larvae at night.
- **Post-collection handling:** Larvae were processed at the National Park Service compound at [[Cottonwood Cove]], NV, then transferred to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility at [[Willow Beach]], AZ.

## Key Findings

- **Collection period:** 20 January to 9 February 1997 (the scan's date is partly garbled; 9 February is the most consistent reading).
- **Total effort at Tequila Cove:** 22 person-hours, equal to 56 light-hours.
- **Collection windows:**
  - Night 1 (7-9 February): approximately 2300 to 0130 hrs.
  - Night 2 (8-9 February): approximately 1900 to 2200 hrs.
- **Catch:** 932 larvae on the first night and 1,262 on the second, a two-night total of 2,194 larvae. The two nightly totals sum correctly to this figure.
- **Equipment lesson:** Running multiple lights from one battery shortened battery life and limited collection time. The team has since switched to using additional batteries to power multiple lights.
- **Deliverable status:** The author asks the client to confirm that the documentation meets the agreement's terms. No formal findings beyond the catch figures are reported.
