# California Desert Conservation Plan Multiple-Use Classes

#land-management #conservation-planning #desert-ecology

## Executive Summary

This document is a planning map from the [[Bureau of Land Management]] (BLM) [[Desert Planning Staff]] depicting the [[California Desert Conservation Plan]] multiple-use classification system. The map delineates various land management categories across the [[California Desert]], including areas in [[San Bernardino County]], with designations ranging from controlled use to intensive use, as well as special management areas such as [[Areas of Critical Environmental Concern]] (ACECs) and [[National Scenic Areas]].

## Background & Description

The [[California Desert Conservation Plan]] establishes a framework for managing public lands administered by the [[United States Department of the Interior]] through the [[Bureau of Land Management]]. The planning system employs a multiple-use classification approach to balance conservation objectives with various land uses across the desert landscape.

The map legend identifies four primary multiple-use classes:
- **Class C (Controlled)**: Most restrictive use category
- **Class L (Limited)**: Restricted use with specific limitations
- **Class M (Moderate)**: Moderate intensity use permitted
- **Class I (Intensive)**: Highest intensity use allowed

Additional land categories shown include public lands not within specific multiple-use classes, private lands, state lands, and other federally managed lands. Special designations include planning units, [[National Scenic Areas]], [[Independence Area]], [[Areas of Critical Environmental Concern]] (ACEC), and competitive event corridors.

Geographic features identified include [[McCain Valley]], [[Riverside County]], and various routes including [[Parker Route]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[California Desert]] represents a unique terrestrial ecosystem requiring specialized management approaches to balance conservation with human activities. The multiple-use classification system reflects varying levels of ecological sensitivity and resource values across the desert landscape. [[Areas of Critical Environmental Concern]] designate locations with particularly significant ecological, cultural, or scenic values requiring special management attention.

The planning framework addresses desert habitat protection while accommodating recreational use, resource extraction, and other land uses appropriate to each classification level.

## Key Findings

- The [[California Desert Conservation Plan]] employs a four-tier multiple-use classification system (Classes C, L, M, and I) to manage public lands with varying intensity levels
- Special management designations include [[National Scenic Areas]], [[Areas of Critical Environmental Concern]] (ACECs), and competitive event corridors
- The planning area encompasses portions of [[San Bernardino County]] and [[Riverside County]], including [[McCain Valley]] and [[Independence Area]]
- The map distinguishes between BLM-managed public lands, private lands, state lands, and other federal lands
- The classification system provides a spatial framework for implementing conservation objectives while permitting appropriate multiple uses across the [[California Desert]] landscape
