#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document records the minutes of the reconvened [[Cui-ui Recovery Team]] meeting (August 2, 1989, Reno, NV), formed under the 1988 Amendments to the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The meeting outlines the mandate to rewrite the [[Cui-ui Recovery Plan]] with site-specific recovery actions, measurable de-listing/down-listing criteria, and time/cost estimates. Central scientific issues discussed include: (1) methodological challenges in estimating adult vs. juvenile [[Cui-ui]] population size and mortality in [[Pyramid Lake]]; (2) mounting evidence of hybridization between cui-ui and [[Tahoe Sucker]] potentially driven by the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] fishway; (3) a proposed two-stage electrophoretic genetic study to distinguish pure cui-ui from hybrids; and (4) a structured review of knowledge gaps feeding into a population-dynamics-based definition of recovery. No de-listing was deemed biologically feasible absent full natural habitat restoration.

## Background & Description

- **Meristic analysis**: Meristic counts performed by W. Minckley on putative "hybrid" specimens (sent by G. Scoppettone) indicated these fish were meristically typical of the genus *[[Chasmistes]]*, casting doubt on earlier hybrid identification by Bob Miller, who had relied only on general (non-meristic) observations. Miller later agreed there was ambiguity after conducting his own meristic counts.
- **Putative hybrid phenotype**: Approximately 20–25% of young adult cui-ui sampled from the 1980 and/or 1981 spawning runs exhibited "a few physical characteristics dissimilar to pure cui-ui," suggestive of possible hybridization with [[Tahoe Sucker]] (*Catostomus tahoensis*).
- **Size class data**: Recent trapping operations captured predominantly fish measuring 200–400 mm in length, interpreted as likely reflecting behavioral (movement) patterns rather than true population size structure.
- **Age at capture**: Bulk of catchable young fish were approximately 3–4 years old.
- Planned **Stage 2** morphological/physical-characteristic analysis of confirmed hybrids was proposed using carcasses retained from the electrophoretic (genetic) sampling, to enable a spring culling operation based on visually/physically distinguishable traits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: Cui-ui inhabit [[Pyramid Lake]] (a terminal, endorheic desert lake) and undertake spawning migrations up the [[Truckee River]], passing through the [[Marble Bluff Fish Facility]] fishway.
- **Reproductive strategy**: Cui-ui are documented as largely **non-annual spawners**, with spawning frequency hypothesized to be linked to environmental/hydrological conditions. Histological gonad examination was proposed to clarify spawning periodicity.
- **Fishway effects**: The Marble Bluff fishway is suspected to (a) selectively exclude older, larger fish from ascending, altering age composition of the spawning run, and (b) delay spawning-run timing, which may have increased temporal/spatial overlap with Tahoe suckers and thus elevated hybridization risk — an anthropogenically induced phenomenon rather than a natural, longstanding one.
- **Population dynamics**: Adults are scarce and behaviorally less mobile than juveniles, making adult abundance/age-structure estimation difficult; juvenile population estimates and rough mortality rates are more tractable but will require multi-year study.
- **Current spawning population**: Recent estimates place the spawning population at roughly 200,000 individuals, considered a candidate lower bound for a functional (non-self-limiting) population under current conditions.
- **Habitat/water quality knowledge gaps**: Status review flagged unresolved items including effects of total dissolved solids (TDS), nitrogen/phosphorus loading (lake and river), physical/temperature river conditions, exotic species interactions, channelization, and flow regime requirements for larval and lake habitat — many marked as requiring further study (see [[Key Findings]]).

## Key Findings

- **Recovery planning mandate**: Under the 1988 ESA Amendments, the team must define (1) site-specific recovery actions, (2) measurable de-listing/down-listing criteria (potentially habitat-based rather than purely numeric), and (3) time/cost estimates. First draft plan due January 31, 1990; final draft due June 30, 1991.
- **De-listing infeasible**: The team concluded de-listing is likely an unreasonable goal absent restored natural habitat and elimination of the need for human intervention — this determination was to be communicated to the Regional Director.
- **Down-listing criteria**: Proposed to be based on historical/projected river use and a tiered intervention strategy — minimal human intervention in high-water years, significant intervention in normal/low-water years — informed by a stochastic population dynamics model incorporating vital statistics, inflow, and genetics.
- **Hybridization concern is central**: The presence/extent of cui-ui × Tahoe sucker hybridization was deemed likely to dictate the entire recovery plan structure; some team members advocated resolving the hybrid question via an interim action plan before finalizing broader recovery strategy.
- **Genetic study design**: A two-stage electrophoretic analysis was proposed — Stage 1 (15 pure cui-ui vs. 15 pure Tahoe suckers, 10–30 loci, ~$600–$4,200) to identify diagnostic genetic markers; Stage 2 (~85 suspected hybrids + 15 pure fish, ~$5,000) to apply markers for hybrid identification. Target completion: end of 1989, with formal funding request to be routed through the Regional Director.
- **Brood stock conservation**: Proposals to safeguard genetic integrity included maintaining a pure, non-reproducing brood stock (options: Walker Lake, tribal hatchery, or in-lake tagging), with tribal hatchery holding viewed as the least logistically/politically complex option. Consultation with a geneticist was recommended to determine minimum brood stock size for maintaining genetic diversity.
- **Proposed culling/containment measures**: If hybrids are confirmed, culling should begin promptly (while hybrids remain morphologically distinguishable from pure fish as juveniles), potentially via trapping at the fishway mouth or nearshore netting; an electric barrier across the river delta and a terminal trapping facility at the fishway were suggested as delta-crossing deterrents.
- **Knowledge status review** (against 1983 Recovery Plan step-down outline): Life history and river-quality-for-adults items are well understood ("much is known," "sufficient information"); population dynamics, environmental stimuli, water chemistry for incubation, and TDS/nutrient effects on the lake/river remain poorly known or require more work; a self-sustaining population size has not yet been identified, though it is estimated to fall somewhere between a few thousand and several hundred thousand individuals, adjustable for human use and environmental variables.
- **Definition of recovery**: Framed from a purely biological standpoint — creating conditions allowing the species' natural evolutionary trajectory (whether extinction, maintenance, or expansion) with the ultimate goal of no continued human intervention; must also address water availability and long-term water rights protection for the lake.
- **Overall assessment**: Biologists have a solid grasp of cui-ui biology and know how to sustain the population operationally, but currently lack the quantitative/modeling basis to define a specific self-sustaining population target; population genetics data were identified as a critical missing input for finalizing the recovery definition.
