# Bonytail Chub Mortality at Dexter National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This April 24, 1989 memorandum from the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] Manager to the Regional Director of [[Region 2]] (Albuquerque, NM) reports the death of a [[Bonytail Chub]] collected from [[Lake Mohave]] in March 1989. The cause of death is unknown. The fish had received prophylactic treatments for fungal and bacterial infections before it was held in a pond with six other chubs collected from Lake Mohave in 1988. When the pond was drained, the recently collected chub was missing, and the other six were in excellent condition.

- **Copies to:** Nine named recipients, including Jim Brooks, Kyle Miller, Scott Hess, Sam Spiller, Jim Hanson, George Divine, Dean Hendrickson, W. L. Minckley, and Paul Marsh, plus file copies.

- **Individual identification:** The six surviving chubs were marked with [[Pit Tag]]s implanted [[Intraperitoneal Implantation|intraperitoneally]] (the implantation date is partly garbled in the scan, probably "last fall").

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The memo gives little ecological data. It concerns a captive-held, wild-collected population of a native fish. Key health context:

- The March 1989 collection from Lake Mohave was given [[Prophylactic Treatment]] for [[Fungal Infection]] and [[Bacterial Infection]].
- The fish was then held in a pond with six chubs from the 1988 Lake Mohave collection, so the dead individual shared holding conditions with the marked group.
- No water chemistry, temperature, habitat, or population-size data are reported.

## Key Findings

1. **One bonytail chub died.** The fish collected from Lake Mohave in March 1989 died, and the cause is unknown.
2. **Treatment history.** The fish received prophylactic treatments for fungal and bacterial infections before its death.
3. **Pond drainage.** When the pond was drained (date partly garbled in the scan, probably 4/13), the recently collected chub was no longer present, so its fate is unconfirmed beyond the reported absence.
4. **Surviving group is healthy.** All six chubs collected from Lake Mohave in 1988 were in excellent condition and were individually identified by their pit tags.
5. **Purpose of the report.** The memo informs the Regional Director of the loss of the March specimen and the status of the remaining group. It does not propose further action.
