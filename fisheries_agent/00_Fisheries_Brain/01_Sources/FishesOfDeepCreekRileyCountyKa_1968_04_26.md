# Fishes of Deep Creek, Riley County, Kansas: Establishment of Spotted Bass (*Micropterus punctulatus*)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Centrarchidae

## Executive Summary
This short note documents an 11-year (almost-annual) faunal survey of Deep Creek, a tributary system in the Kansas River basin (Riley County, Kansas), with particular emphasis on confirming the successful establishment of an introduced population of [[Spotted Bass]] (*Micropterus punctulatus*). The stocking event occurred on 16 August 1957, when 12 adults and 247 young-of-year fish were transplanted from Otter Creek (Cowley County, Kansas) into Deep Creek — representing the first known introduction of the species into the Kansas River system. Following the initial stocking, the species was captured in 1957–1958, then went unrecorded for eight years (1959–1966), before being rediscovered in 1967 as a range of size classes (50–260 mm SL), confirming natural reproduction and true establishment rather than a transient stocked population. The paper also provides a comprehensive checklist of 29 fish species/hybrid combinations from the Deep Creek system and updated meristic data supporting subspecific intergradation in [[Moxostoma macrolepidotum]].

## Background & Description
- **Spotted Bass (*Micropterus punctulatus*)**: 1967 recaptured specimens ranged 50–260 mm standard length (SL), indicating multiple year classes and successful natural recruitment since the original 1957 introduction. Specimens noted to be in "excellent condition," preserved at Arizona State University.
- **Northern Redhorse (*Moxostoma macrolepidotum*)**: A series of 31 specimens (107–150 mm SL) collected at Pillsbury Crossing in 1967 was used to assess lip morphology as a taxonomic character.
  - Upper lip thickness relative to lower lip width: range 3.5–5.2 mm, mean 4.3 mm (n=31)
  - Compared to reference values:
    - Typical *M. m. macrolepidotum*: 4.8–5.6 mm, mean 5.2 mm (n=7)
    - *M. m. pisolabrum* (Trautman and Martin): 3.4–4.3 mm, mean 3.9 mm (n=7)
  - The intermediate mean (4.3 mm) supports intergradation between the two subspecies in the Kansas River basin, consistent with [[Minckley and Cross 1960]].
- **Longear Sunfish misidentification**: A prior record of *Lepomis megalotis* (Minckley, 1956) was determined by Metcalf (1966) to be a misidentification of [[Orange-Spotted Sunfish]] (*Lepomis humilis*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **System**: [[Deep Creek]], a prairie/Great Plains stream tributary to the Kansas River, within the Kansas River drainage basin, Riley County, Kansas.
- **Habitat structure**: Includes riffle-pool sequences; notable collecting localities include the confluence with the Kansas River and the pools immediately below **Pillsbury Crossing** (previously described by Minckley, 1956).
- **Spotted Bass establishment**: Occupies pool habitat below Pillsbury Crossing; successful colonization of a novel Great Plains stream system following introduction, representing a documented case of range expansion via anthropogenic stocking within the [[Kansas River Basin]].
- **Community context**: Deep Creek supports a diverse assemblage including cyprinids (e.g., [[Topeka Shiner]], [[Red Shiner]], [[Emerald Shiner]]), catostomids, ictalurids, and centrarchids, alongside percids ([[Logperch]], [[Orange-Throated Darter]], [[Johnny Darter]]).
- **Species interactions/hybridization**: Three natural hybrid cyprinid combinations documented in the system:
  - *Chrosomus erythrogaster* × *Semotilus atromaculatus*
  - *Chrosomus erythrogaster* × *Notropis cornutus*
  - *Chrosomus erythrogaster* × *Campostoma anomalum*
- **Predicted future colonizers**: [[Bluegill]] (*Lepomis macrochirus*) and [[Crappie]] (*Pomoxis* spp.), likely via escape from farm ponds; [[Flathead Catfish]] (*Pylodictis olivaris*) and [[Longnose Gar]] (*Lepisosteus osseus*), likely via upstream movement from the Kansas River during high-water events.
- **Largemouth Bass** (*Micropterus salmoides*) contrast: Infrequent in the mainstream of Deep Creek but common in farm ponds throughout the drainage — a notable ecological contrast to the stream-adapted, self-sustaining Spotted Bass population.

## Key Findings
- **First confirmed establishment** of *Micropterus punctulatus* in the Kansas River system, originating from a single stocking event (16 August 1957) of 259 total fish (12 adults + 247 young-of-year) from Otter Creek, Cowley County.
- **Eight-year detection gap** (1959–1966) followed by rediscovery in 1967 with a broad size range (50–260 mm SL) strongly supports natural reproduction and true population establishment, not merely survival of stocked individuals.
- Total of **29 fish species** (plus 3 interspecific hybrid combinations) documented from the Deep Creek system as of this survey, consolidating and updating records from Jennings (1942), Minckley (1956, 1959), Minckley and Cross (1959, 1960), Metcalf (1966), and Cross (1967).
- Morphometric data on *Moxostoma macrolepidotum* lip thickness support prior conclusions of subspecific intergradation (*M. m. macrolepidotum* × *M. m. pisolabrum*) within the Kansas River basin.
- Correction of a historical misidentification: earlier Deep Creek records of *Lepomis megalotis* (longear sunfish) are reassigned to *Lepomis humilis* (orange-spotted sunfish).
- New species records for the Deep Creek system in this paper include *Carpiodes carpio* (1958/1967 captures), *Noturus flavus* (1967, Pillsbury Crossing), and confirmed *Percina caprodes* records (1966–1967).
- The paper anticipates future range additions to the system (bluegill, crappie, flathead catfish, longnose gar) based on pond-stocking practices and connectivity with the Kansas River mainstem.
