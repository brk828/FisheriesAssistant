# Station Profile: Leavenworth National Fish Hatchery

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Salmonidae #fish-hatchery #ColumbiaRiver

## Executive Summary

The [[Leavenworth National Fish Hatchery]] is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility established in 1938 under the [[Grand Coulee Fish Maintenance Project]] and the [[Mitchell Act]] to mitigate the loss of anadromous fish habitat caused by the construction of the [[Grand Coulee Dam]]. Located on the [[Icicle River]], a tributary of the [[Wenatchee River]] and [[Columbia River]], the hatchery's mission is to artificially produce a downstream migration of salmonids equivalent to that historically produced by the 1,245 miles of stream habitat blocked above Grand Coulee Dam. The station annually produces approximately 1.75 million [[Spring Chinook Salmon]] and 100,000 [[Summer Steelhead Trout]] for release into the Columbia River drainage. Beyond production, the hatchery serves a significant public engagement role, hosting the Wenatchee River Salmon Festival and supporting an alternative high school program, while contributing broodstock to sustain both tribal and sport fisheries on the Icicle and Wenatchee Rivers.

## Background & Description

This station profile is administrative/operational in nature and does not provide detailed morphological data on the species reared. Species-level morphological characteristics for [[Spring Chinook Salmon]], [[Summer Steelhead Trout]], and [[Kokanee]] would need to be sourced from dedicated species accounts. Notable population/production metrics reported include:

- **Spring Chinook Salmon**: 510 breeding pairs spawned in August 1993, yielding more than 2.0 million eggs.
- **Summer Steelhead Trout**: 25 breeding pairs spawned in early May 1993, yielding 125,000 eggs.
- **Adult Chinook returns**: A record 12,223 adult spring chinook salmon returned to the Icicle River in 1993, providing broodstock as well as surplus fish for sport and tribal (subsistence) fisheries.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat**: The hatchery sits on 170 acres adjacent to the [[Icicle River]], within the Wenatchee River system, part of the larger [[Columbia River]] drainage. The facility draws on groundwater-rich local hydrology, utilizing more than 8,500 gallons per minute of pathogen-free well water — a key feature supporting salmonid egg incubation and rearing.
- **Additional land holdings**: The hatchery holds title to over 700 acres containing two lakes within the [[Alpine Lakes Wilderness Area]], immediately south of the hatchery site.
- **Wild population context**: The Wenatchee River system supports relatively healthy wild populations of spring and summer [[Chinook Salmon]], [[Sockeye Salmon]], and [[Steelhead Trout]], coexisting alongside the hatchery-supplemented population — indicating an ecologically significant mixed wild/hatchery salmonid system.
- **Anthropogenic context**: The hatchery exists specifically to compensate for anadromous fish habitat lost due to the [[Grand Coulee Dam]], which blocked upstream migration to 1,245 miles of natal streams and tributaries.
- **Human-ecosystem interface**: The hatchery functions as a major regional ecotourism and environmental education hub, drawing more than 58,000 visitors annually, including 10,000+ attendees at the four-day Wenatchee River Salmon Festival co-sponsored with the U.S. Forest Service.

## Key Findings

- **Founding purpose**: Established in 1938 as compensatory mitigation for fish habitat lost to the Grand Coulee Dam, under joint authority of the Grand Coulee Fish Maintenance Project (1937) and the Mitchell Act (1938).
- **Production output (FY1993 target)**: ~1.75 million Spring Chinook Salmon and ~100,000 Summer Steelhead Trout released annually into the Columbia River drainage.
- **Historical distribution trends** (documented total releases):
  - **FY1992**: 2.69M Spring Chinook + 0.119M Steelhead = 2.811M total
  - **FY1991**: 2.372M Spring Chinook + 0.114M Steelhead = 2.681M total
  - **FY1990**: 4.558M Spring Chinook + 0.106M Steelhead + 0.350M Kokanee = 5.014M total
  - These figures show a declining total output trend from FY1990 to FY1992, with Kokanee production dropped after FY1990.
- **Record return year**: 1993 marked a record return of 12,223 adult spring chinook salmon to the Icicle River, supporting both broodstock collection and fisheries.
- **Infrastructure**: Facility includes a 26,000 ft² hatchery building, two adult holding ponds (15'×150'), 38 Foster-Lucas ponds, 45 rectangular raceways (8'×80'), two water screen chambers, a pollution abatement pond, and five residences plus a mobile home pad.
- **Community/education programs**: Establishment of the Information and Education Department (headed by Corky Broaddus, formerly of the U.S. Forest Service) to address the Regional Outreach Strategy; partnership with Cascade School District to run the [[Cascade Discovery Program]], an alternative high school for at-risk and gifted/talented students on hatchery grounds.
- **Recreational infrastructure**: 7.5 km of Nordic ski trails and a 1-mile interpretive trail loop support hatchery-based outdoor recreation and education.
