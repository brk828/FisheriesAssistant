# Apache Trout Population Survey and Genetic Sampling (1988)

#ichthyology #salmonidae #conservation-biology #threatened-species

## Executive Summary

This 1988 field report documents population surveys and specimen collection of [[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*), a threatened species endemic to the [[White Mountains]] of Arizona. The survey was conducted across 45 stream sites on the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] and [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]] as part of the 1987 tentative recovery plan. Electrofishing surveys collected 538 [[Apache Trout]] specimens for genetic analysis (tissue samples sent to Genean Laboratories, Smithville, Texas) and morphometric examination (carcasses preserved in formalin). The survey revealed drastic population declines, widespread presence of introduced salmonids ([[Rainbow Trout]] *Oncorhynchus mykiss*, [[Cutthroat Trout]] *O. clarki*, [[German Brown Trout]] *Salmo trutta*, [[Brook Trout]] *Salvelinus fontinalis*), and a striking male-biased sex ratio (average 2.3:1) in [[Apache Trout]] populations. Mean total length was 152.5 mm and mean weight was 31.5 grams across all sampled streams.

## Background & Description

[[Apache Trout]] (*Oncorhynchus apache*) is a threatened species endemic to the [[White Mountains]] of Arizona, occurring primarily on the [[Fort Apache Indian Reservation]] with natural populations also on the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest]]. Since the 1940s, agencies have stocked multiple non-native salmonid species throughout the area originally inhabited by [[Apache Trout]]. The presumed reasons for decline include: (a) interspecific hybridization with both [[Cutthroat Trout]] and [[Rainbow Trout]]; (b) predation by and competition from introduced species; and (c) loss of habitat.

At least three morphometric characteristics distinguish [[Apache Trout]] from [[Rainbow Trout]]: counts of pyloric caeca, vertebrae, and scales above the lateral line (Rinne, 1985; Schmidt, pers. comm.). At least one diagnostic genetic locus exists between all three hybridizing species (R. Schmidt, pers. comm.). The 1987 tentative recovery plan's first step was to survey [[Apache Trout]] streams to determine abundance and collect specimens for taxonomic examination to determine genetic purity.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling was conducted via backpack electrofishing at stream locations corresponding primarily with road crossings, though some sites required hiking (Ord, Smith, and Paddy on the reservation; Fish, Snake, Conklin, and Double Cienega on the forest). Fish tissues were obtained, isolated, and placed on dry ice in the field—a severe time constraint that limited the number of streams accessible.

Habitat parameters differed between [[Apache Trout]]-dominated and [[Brown Trout]]-dominated streams. Where [[Apache Trout]] dominated (25 of 45 streams sampled), mean elevation was 2443 m (7940 ft), temperature was 14.97°C, and pH was 7.3. Where [[Brown Trout]] dominated (17 of 45 streams sampled), mean elevation was 2567 m (8343 ft), temperature was 13.53°C, and pH was 7.2. This implies [[Brown Trout]] prefer higher stream reaches than [[Apache Trout]], even though the latter are believed to be holding out in headwaters. Whether these differences are statistically significant is unknown.

Community composition varied dramatically across sites. [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthyes osculus*) were present in several streams. The survey documented 710 [[Brown Trout]], 136 [[Brook Trout]], 171 [[Speckled Dace]], and 1122 [[Apache Trout]] across all sites. In some streams, [[Brown Trout]] outnumbered [[Apache Trout]] 50:1 (Flash Creek).

## Key Findings

**Population Status and Distribution:**
- Total of 538 [[Apache Trout]] specimens collected from 28 streams for genetic analysis
- Only 7 of 28 streams yielded the requested 30 fish; most populations too small to sustain that level of sampling
- Drastic population declines documented on the reservation since last extensive surveys
- Mean total length: 152.5 mm; mean weight: 31.5 grams across all streams
- [[Paradise Creek]]: Low numbers of [[Apache Trout]] with unexpected upstream advancement of [[German Brown Trout]]; took two days to collect 24 specimens
- [[Crooked Creek]]: 22 [[Apache Trout]] in 200 yards with no [[Brown Trout]]; fish looked good; identified as good candidate for barrier installation
- [[Deep Creek]]: 80 [[Apache Trout]] over 450 yards with no [[Brown Trout]]; definite barrier/broodstock potential
- [[Ord Creek]]: Beautiful natives initially observed; heavy rains later widened stream and reduced visibility

**Introduced Species Impact:**
- [[Brown Trout]] dominated 17 of 45 streams; [[Apache Trout]] dominated 25 of 45 streams
- [[Flash Creek]]: [[Brown Trout]] outnumbered [[Apache Trout]] 50:1
- [[Little Diamond Creek]]: 111 [[Brown Trout]] to 26 [[Apache Trout]]
- [[Squaw Creek]]: All [[Brown Trout]], no [[Apache Trout]]
- [[Becker Creek]]: 30 [[Brown Trout]] to 16 [[Brook Trout]], no [[Apache Trout]]
- [[West Fork Black River]]: 42 [[Brown Trout]] taken in Hall Cienega portion, no [[Apache Trout]]

**Sex Ratio Anomaly:**
- Sex ratios strongly male-biased, in some cases as high as 7.7:1
- Average sex ratio across all data: 2.3:1 males to females
- [[Paddy Creek]]: 10 males, 2 females (5.0:1 ratio)
- [[Centerfire Creek]]: 9 males, 9 females (1.0:1 ratio)
- [[Conklin Creek]]: 21 males, 5 females (4.2:1 ratio)
- [[Snake Creek]]: 19 males, 5 females (3.8:1 ratio)
- [[Hanagan Creek]]: 23 males, 3 females (7.67:1 ratio)
- [[Fish Creek]]: 14 males, 9 females (1.56:1 ratio)
- [[Corduroy Creek]]: 16 males, 10 females (1.6:1 ratio)
- [[Double Cienega Creek]]: 9 males, 8 females (1.13:1 ratio)

**Management Implications:**
- Skewed sex ratio may increase importance of female choice in mating
- Genetically red-sided [[Rainbow Trout]] may have advantage as mating stimulus for females
- Male [[Rainbow Trout]] bred for rapid growth may be outcompeting [[Apache Trout]] males in intrasexual competition
- Since hybridization is a major cause of decline, sexual selection should be a major research focus
- Asymmetrical sex ratio may vary seasonally or at spawning grounds; requires more intensive sampling to determine

**Habitat Characteristics:**
- [[Apache Trout]]-dominated streams: mean elevation 2443 m, temperature 14.97°C, pH 7.3
- [[Brown Trout]]-dominated streams: mean elevation 2567 m, temperature 13.53°C, pH 7.2
- [[Brown Trout]] appear to prefer higher stream reaches despite belief that [[Apache Trout]] are restricted to headwaters

**Streams with Renovation/Barrier Potential:**
- [[Crooked Creek]]: Good candidate for barrier installation
- [[Deep Creek]]: Definite barrier/broodstock potential
- [[Little Bonito Creek]]: Good candidate for renovation/enhancement/isolation in Lofer Bench area
- [[Boggy Creek]] (reservation): Another good candidate for barrier
- [[Ord Creek]]: Definite barrier/broodstock potential; very naive fish allowing close approach

**Sampling Methodology Refinements:**
- Initial target of 30 fish per stream revised after [[Paradise Creek]] experience
- Streams surveyed 15-30 minutes prior to collection to estimate relative abundance
- Sampling regime developed as "every other fish" or "every third fish" from particular reaches
- Forest streams believed capable of sustaining 30-fish collections; reservation streams lacked same confidence
- Tissue samples isolated and placed on dry ice in field for genetic analysis
- Carcasses preserved in formalin for future morphometric analysis at Pinetop Fisheries Assistance office
