#ichthyology #marine-biology #Acipenseridae

## Executive Summary
This study, conducted by Max R. Schreiber (California Department of Fish and Game, 1960), presents a diet analysis of 30 juvenile [[White Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser transmontanus*) collected at the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation's Tracy Fish Collecting Facility on Old River, in the southern [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]]. Stomach and esophageal contents were examined to characterize early-life-stage food habits. Despite a small sample size limiting broad conclusions, the amphipod *[[Corophium spinicorne]]* emerged as the dominant prey item by both frequency of occurrence and volume, suggesting it is the primary food source for juvenile sturgeon in this region during late summer and fall.

## Background & Description
- **Species examined**: *Acipenser transmontanus* (White Sturgeon), all classified as age 0 (young-of-the-year).
- **Mean fork length**: 8.0 inches.
- **Age determination method**: Cross-sectioning of pectoral fin rays, following the technique of [[Pycha 1955]] ("A quick method of preparing permanent fin-ray and spine sections").
- **Preservation method**: Specimens preserved in 10% formalin; stomach contents subsequently identified, counted, and volume-measured via alcohol displacement after removal of excess water with blotting paper.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Capture location**: Old River, ~8 miles northwest of Tracy, San Joaquin County, California — within the southern portion of the [[Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta]].
- **Temporal context**: Samples collected during August, September, and October of 1956 and 1958, representing a late-summer/fall feeding period.
- **Trophic role**: Juvenile white sturgeon appear to function as benthic/epibenthic invertivores, feeding primarily on small crustaceans (amphipods) and secondarily on mysid shrimp and midge (Tendipedidae/Chironomidae) larvae and adults — indicating a diet centered on benthic invertebrate prey within estuarine delta habitats.
- **Prey species context**:
  - *[[Corophium spinicorne]]* — a benthic tube-dwelling amphipod, dominant prey item.
  - *[[Neomysis mercedis]]* — an estuarine mysid shrimp, minor dietary component.
  - Tendipedidae (Chironomidae) larvae and adults — midge life stages, minor dietary component.

## Key Findings
- **Sample composition**: Of 30 stomachs examined, 9 (30%) were empty; food contents were analyzed from the remaining 21.
- **Dominant prey**: *Corophium spinicorne* occurred in 90% of non-empty stomachs, with counts ranging from 1–45 individuals (mean 6) per stomach, totaling 108 individuals across the sample. Volume ranged from trace amounts to 0.1 ml.
- **Secondary prey items**:
  - Tendipedidae larvae: 19% frequency of occurrence, 1–6 individuals per stomach (mean 2), total of 9 individuals, volume trace to 0.05 ml.
  - *Neomysis mercedis*: 10% frequency of occurrence, only 1 individual found (total), trace volume.
  - Tendipedidae adults: 5% frequency of occurrence, 1 individual found, trace volume.
- **Stomach fullness**: Food volumes were universally small; the single largest stomach content volume observed was 0.1 ml, consisting of 45 *Corophium spinicorne* plus trace amounts of body/appendage fragments. All other stomachs contained 14 or fewer organisms.
- **Interpretive conclusion**: Given the very small sample size, the authors caution against broad generalization, but note that *Corophium spinicorne* appears to be the primary food source for juvenile white sturgeon in the southern Delta during late summer/fall, with *Neomysis mercedis* and tendipedid life stages playing comparatively minor dietary roles.
- **Funding/context**: Research conducted under Dingell-Johnson Project California F-9-R, "A Study of [[Sturgeon]] and [[Striped Bass]]," supported by Federal Aid to Fish Restoration funds.
