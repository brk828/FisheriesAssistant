## Executive Summary
This document is a **bibliography of research outputs** — trip reports, annual reports, technical reports, and conference papers — generated by BIO/WEST, Inc. between October 1990 and December 1993 under contract to the Bureau of Reclamation (Contract No. 0-CS-40-09110). The research program, titled "Characterization of the Life History and Ecology of the Humpback Chub in Grand Canyon," represents a multi-year field investigation into the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in the Colorado River within Grand Canyon, conducted in the context of environmental monitoring for Glen Canyon Dam operations. The document itself does not present raw data, but its titles and reference list reveal the scope of methodologies, comparator species, and ecological questions investigated over the study period, including habitat use, radiotelemetry, flow effects, turbidity, and predation.

## Background & Description
No direct morphological, meristic, or biometric data are reported in this bibliographic source. However, referenced companion studies indicate methodological approaches relevant to morphology and physiology, including:
- Development of a **non-lethal gastric (stomach) pumping technique** to recover gut contents from [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) and later adapted for the endangered [[Humpback Chub]], enabling diet analysis without sacrificing individuals (Valdez & Wasowicz 1992; Wasowicz & Valdez, in review).
- Use of **radiotelemetry** tagging to track individual Humpback Chub movement and habitat association in the Colorado River (Masslich et al. 1992).
- Studies on the physiological/behavioral tolerance of Roundtail Chub to **pulsed DC electroshock**, relevant to safe capture and handling protocols (Cowdell & Valdez 1993).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The referenced studies collectively characterize the ecological niche of the Humpback Chub within the regulated Colorado River ecosystem in [[Grand Canyon]], downstream of [[Glen Canyon Dam]]:
- **Habitat use**: Investigated directly (Valdez 1992, "Habitat Use of Humpback Chub in the Grand Canyon"), implicating association with backwaters, eddies, and specific mainstem/tributary confluence habitats typical of the species.
- **Flow regime effects**: Activity patterns of Humpback Chub were studied under **fluctuating flows** resulting from hydropower dam operations (Valdez & Masslich 1992).
- **Turbidity effects**: Movement and activity of Humpback Chub were assessed relative to water turbidity conditions in the river (Masslich & Valdez, in review) — indicating turbidity as a potentially important environmental driver of behavior.
- **Distribution**: Present-day mainstem distribution of Humpback Chub in Grand Canyon was mapped and reported (Valdez et al. 1992).
- **Predation pressure**: Documented predation by [[Osprey]] on Humpback Chub, indicating an avian predator link in the food web (Wasowicz & Yard 1993, *Great Basin Naturalist*).
- **Co-occurring species**: The [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) served as both a comparator species and subject of parallel methodological studies (electroshock effects, stomach content sampling) within the same river reach.
- **Spatial/GIS context**: A multi-temporal, multi-accuracy fisheries GIS database was developed for the Colorado River in Grand Canyon to support habitat and distribution analyses (Hougaard & Valdez, in review).

## Key Findings
- The research program produced a near-continuous monthly trip-report record (1990–1993) documenting field characterization of Humpback Chub life history and ecology in Grand Canyon.
- A **population model** for Humpback Chub was under development (Ryel & Valdez 1993, Phase I Feasibility Evaluation), representing an early step toward quantitative demographic assessment of the species.
- A validated **non-lethal stomach pumping technique** was established as a methodological advance, allowing repeated, non-destructive diet sampling of both Roundtail Chub and the endangered Humpback Chub.
- Documented **osprey predation** on Humpback Chub represents a rare direct observation of a natural predation event on this endangered species.
- Movement and activity of Humpback Chub were shown to be influenced by both **flow fluctuation** (dam operations) and **turbidity**, highlighting the species' sensitivity to hydrologic alteration from [[Glen Canyon Dam]] operations.
- The cumulative dataset and reports fed into a **Long-Term Monitoring Plan for Native Fishes of the Colorado River in Grand Canyon** (Valdez 1992), linking applied research directly to conservation and dam-operation management policy under the Glen Canyon Environmental Studies program.
- This body of work directly supports [[Endangered Species]] management for *Gila cypha*, one of the principal native fishes of concern in the regulated [[Colorado River]] ecosystem.
