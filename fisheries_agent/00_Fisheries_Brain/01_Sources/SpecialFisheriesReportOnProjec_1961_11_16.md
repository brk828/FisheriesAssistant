# Special Fisheries Report on Project Vela-Uniform: Lake Mead Test Explosions

#ichthyology #marine-biology #freshwater-ecology #Centrarchidae #Ictaluridae #Cyprinidae #Catostomidae #FisheriesManagement

## Executive Summary
This 1961 special fisheries report documents fish and wildlife mortality resulting from three underwater test explosions conducted in [[Lake Mead]] as part of the [[Project Vela-Uniform]] program, a U.S. Geological Survey initiative under the Department of Defense designed to simulate underground nuclear blast shock waves using ammonium nitrate charges. Across three sequential detonations (1-ton, 2-ton, and 3-ton charges) conducted on November 14–16, 1961, a cumulative total of 145.9 pounds of game fish were killed, alongside significant losses of non-game fish (carp, humpback sucker) and 421 [[Eared Grebe]] (a diving waterbird). Notably, mortality decreased with each successive, larger blast, likely due to fish avoidance behavior or population depletion in the test zone following the first shot. The report recommends reimbursement for game fish losses at $4.50/lb, totaling approximately $656.56, while explicitly excluding non-game fish and avian losses from compensation claims.

## Background & Description
The [[Project Vela-Uniform]] program was a Cold War-era U.S. Department of Defense initiative to develop and calibrate techniques for detecting underground nuclear detonations. As part of this effort, the U.S. Geological Survey conducted controlled underwater explosive tests using ammonium nitrate charges, chosen because their shock wave profiles closely simulate those produced by underground nuclear blasts. [[Lake Mead]], a large reservoir on the Colorado River formed by [[Hoover Dam]], was selected as the test site due to its sufficient depth (blasts were detonated at approximately 190 feet).

Three sequential test explosions were conducted:
- **November 14, 1961** – 1-ton ammonium nitrate charge, detonated ~7:00 a.m.
- **November 15, 1961** – 2-ton charge, detonated ~8:30 a.m., same location
- **November 16, 1961** – 3-ton charge, detonated ~9:30 a.m., same location

Recording trucks equipped with seismic/shock instrumentation were stationed at varying distances to capture blast wave data. Wildlife mortality cleanup and cataloguing were coordinated by the [[Nevada Fish and Game Department]], with assistance from the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[National Park Service]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]], and volunteer sportsmen.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The test site was located within the pelagic/benthic transition zone of a deep reservoir environment, hosting a mixed assemblage of native and introduced fish species typical of Colorado River impoundments:

- **Game fish** (economically/recreationally valued): [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Black Crappie]], [[Channel Catfish]]
- **Non-game fish**: [[Common Carp]] (introduced), [[Humpback Sucker]] (native, later listed as endangered)
- **Forage fish**: [[Threadfin Shad]] — noted as suffering heavy losses on the first blast and moderate losses on the second, though not quantified in the species table
- **Avifauna**: [[Eared Grebe]], a diving waterbird species, suffered substantial collateral mortality (421 individuals total), likely due to their diving behavior placing them within the lethal blast radius underwater

The underwater detonations produced barotraumatic shock waves capable of rupturing swim bladders and internal organs in fish, a well-documented mechanism of explosive-related fish kills. The progressive decrease in game fish mortality despite increasing charge size across the three tests suggests either localized depletion of vulnerable fish stocks near the detonation point after the first blast, or behavioral avoidance/dispersal of fish from the test zone following initial disturbance.

Wind conditions (15–20 mph, out of the northeast) complicated efforts to precisely delineate the blast's effective mortality radius across all three tests, and caused a one-day delay of the first test (originally scheduled November 13).

### Economic Impact & Reimbursement Recommendation
- **Game fish only** were deemed eligible for compensation; non-game fish (182 individuals total) and grebes (421) were explicitly excluded.
- Total game fish poundage killed: **145.9 lbs**
- Species contribution to game fish loss:
  - [[Largemouth Bass]]: 81.1 lbs (55.6%)
  - [[Black Crappie]]: 59.8 lbs (41.0%)
  - [[Channel Catfish]]: 5.0 lbs (3.4%)
- Proposed replacement cost: **$4.50 per pound**
- **Total recommended reimbursement: ~$656.56**

### Notable Ecological Observations
- Mortality **inversely correlated** with blast size across the three sequential tests — largest charge (3-ton) produced the *smallest* game fish kill, suggesting depletion or avoidance effects.
- [[Threadfin Shad]], though a major forage species, suffered substantial unquantified losses on the first blast and moderate losses on the second, but were excluded from formal reimbursement calculations due to non-game status.
- Diving waterbirds ([[Eared Grebe]]) were disproportionately affected relative to fish, with mortality peaking on the second (2-ton) blast (311 individuals) — a pattern distinct from the fish mortality trend, possibly reflecting differences in grebe foraging depth/behavior relative to the blast zone on that specific day.
