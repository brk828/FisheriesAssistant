# California Scientific Collector's Permit and Protected Species Regulations (1982)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This document is a 1982 California Department of Fish and Game Scientific Collector's Permit, issued to W.L. Minckler of Tempe, Arizona, together with supporting material: lists of state and federally protected species, the 1975 list of fully protected animals, a directory of Department facilities, and the statutory and regulatory text governing scientific collecting. The permit authorizes fish sampling in the [[Colorado River]] near Blythe, with strict limits on lethal take and a requirement for prior coordination with the Department's Blythe office. The package is administrative rather than research-based, so it contains no original field data.

## Background & Description

The permit was issued December 28, 1982 and expired December 31, 1983. It was issued under Sections 1002 and 1003 of the [[California Fish and Game Code]] and Section 650, Title 14, California Administrative Code (see [[Scientific Collecting Permit]]). Permitted methods were nets, seines, traps and electroshocking.

The protected-species lists are organized by taxonomic group and status codes:

- **SE**: State-listed endangered
- **SR**: State-listed rare
- **FE**: Federally listed endangered
- **FT**: Federally listed threatened

The state list (revised September 15, 1980) includes roughly 150 entries spanning gastropods, crustaceans, insects, fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals. Fishes make up 18 of these entries (see [[Endangered Rare And Threatened Species]]).

The 1975 [[Fully Protected Species]] list adds 37 taxa: 13 birds, 9 mammals, 5 reptiles and amphibians, and 10 fishes.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Permit terms for the Colorado River.** The permit allows unlimited capture of fish, reptiles, amphibians and invertebrates for enumeration, data collection and release unharmed back into the river. Lethal take (sacrifice) is capped:

| Taxon | Maximum sacrificed |
|---|---|
| [[Tilapia]] spp. | 500 |
| [[Catfish]] (Ictalurus spp.) | 150 |
| [[Sunfish]] (Lepomis spp.) | 500 |
| [[Striped Bass]] (Morone saxatilis) | 50 |
| Any other fish, reptile, amphibian or invertebrate not listed | 100 per species |

Taxa that are state- or federally listed as endangered, rare or threatened may not be taken lethally. Incidental captures of these species must be released alive, or, if found dead, turned over to the Department of Fish and Game in Blythe. Sampling must be coordinated with the Blythe office (contact: Jinda Ulmer, (714) 922-5613) before it begins.

**Colorado River listed fishes.** Several listed species occur in the Colorado River basin, including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Mohave Chub]]. Each is listed as endangered by both the state and the federal government, which makes non-lethal handling the only permissible approach to these species.

**Standard exceptions.** The permit excludes the [[California Condor]], all hawks, eagles and vultures, and parks, reserves or recreation areas. Bird and mammal collecting falls under a separate provision (Section 650(8), Title 14, CAC).

**Department infrastructure.** The package lists 21 fish hatcheries, 33 wildlife areas, five regional offices, several branch offices and research laboratories (see [[California Department Of Fish And Game]] and [[Fish Hatcheries]]). Hatchery types include state-operated, [[Department Of Water Resources]]-owned, and privately owned but Department-operated facilities.

## Key Findings

- **Permit scope:** Single-year (1982–1983), non-transferable permit for Colorado River fish sampling near Blythe, with release-alive requirements and defined sacrifice quotas.
- **Protected-species coverage:** The state list covers 18 fish, 10 invertebrate taxa, 8 amphibians, 8 reptiles, 19 birds and 23 mammals. The 1975 fully protected list adds 37 taxa.
- **Colorado River fishes:** Several listed species (including [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Sucker]], [[Bonytail Chub]] and [[Mohave Chub]]) are protected and may not be sacrificed.
- **Regulatory framework:**
  - Permit fee is $10 per year; the student permit is $5.
  - Permits run for one calendar year and are not issued to anyone under 16 (except qualifying students).
  - Regional notification is required before any collection.
  - Annual reports of specimens collected are required before renewal.
  - Mist nets may be used on protected birds only with specific authorization and must be attended at all times.
  - Shipping specimens requires prior written approval from the Department.
- **Collector categories:** The regulations recognize employees of state and federal agencies, faculty and professional staff, private collectors sponsored by two faculty or staff members, students, commercial-fishing class instructors, aquaria collectors (who must hold a commercial fishing license), and bona fide malacological society members.
- **Limitations:** The Department may limit the number and kind of specimens, the gear used, and the species taken. Applications listing more than six species may be summarized by ecological category (such as trophic level).
- **Enforcement:** Permits may be cancelled or suspended for violations. Department employees or sponsors may inspect any collection at any time.
