# Morphologic Variation in Western American Fishes of the Genus Gila

#ichthyology #cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology #biogeography #hybridization

## Executive Summary

This study examines high levels of morphological variation among cyprinid fishes of the genus [[Gila]] from the lower [[Colorado River]] basin, focusing on [[Gila intermedia]] and [[Gila robusta robusta]]. Populations exhibiting phenotypic intermediacy between these two taxa are common throughout the [[Gila River]] basin, though the species are not syntopic. The authors reject ecophenotypic and ecotypic explanations for observed variation, instead proposing that past hybridization occurred during periods of geologic activity along the developing margin of the [[Colorado Plateau]]. Certain populations of both species apparently avoided interspecific gene flow: monomorphic [[Gila intermedia]] persists in temporally stable, long-isolated habitats of southeastern Arizona, while [[Gila robusta robusta]] occupies mainstreams and likely represents an invader following Mio-Pliocene integration with the Colorado River mainstream.

## Background & Description

The genus [[Gila]] (subgenus Gila) in the [[Colorado River]] basin exhibits remarkable morphological diversity, ranging from streamlined [[Gila elegans]] of large rivers, through [[Gila robusta robusta]] of intermediate-sized streams, to thick-bodied [[Gila intermedia]] of creeks and marshes. Additional distinctive forms occupy the region, many intermediate between recognized species, creating graded morphological series. [[Gila cypha]] represents the most specialized morphology in the genus, falling outside these trends.

[[Gila intermedia]] is characterized by: thick body; large scales (lateral-line scales averaging 70±, versus 80+ in [[Gila robusta robusta]]); small, rounded fins with reduced ray counts (8 dorsal, 7-8 anal fin-rays versus 9 in G. robusta); fewer vertebrae; posterior insertion of anal and dorsal fins; and presence of basal radii on scales. These features are shared with spring-adapted species and members of subgenus Temeculina, though [[Gila intermedia]] lacks the plate-like dermosphenotic diagnostic of that subgenus.

The study examined five taxa from the lower Colorado River basin, with representative samples from throughout the [[Gila River]] basin. Canonical Discriminant Analysis (CDA) was performed on morphometric and meristic data from specimens >50 mm standard length. Geographic populations were designated across seven major drainage areas in Arizona, plus [[Bill Williams River]], [[Rio Yaqui]], and [[Little Colorado River]] basins.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study region encompasses two major physiographic provinces: the [[Colorado Plateau]] (elevated 1,500-2,000 m above sea level) drained by the [[Little Colorado River]], and the [[Basin and Range Province]] to the south and west (characterized by northwest-southeast trending mountains and parallel valleys). Between these lies a Transition Zone of highly variable relief. Most of the Basin and Range and Transition Zone are drained by the [[Gila River]] watershed.

Geologic history is critical to understanding observed patterns. During late Oligocene through Pleistocene, drainage in central and northern Arizona flowed southwest to northeast into closed basins. Development of the [[Colorado Plateau]] margin (Mogollon Rim) in Miocene isolated south-flowing tributaries from the upper Colorado, with drainage reversals occurring locally and piecemeal. Structural troughs (Perkinsville, Verde, Payson, Tonto, San Carlos basins) developed parallel to the Plateau margin, accumulating Miocene and Pliocene sediments.

Integration of upper and lower Colorado basin occurred in late Miocene-early Pliocene when headward erosion captured the upper segment. The mainstream [[Colorado River]] entered the Gulf of California less than 5.5 million years ago, with the lowermost [[Gila River]] also achieving external drainage at this time. South of the Transition Zone, basin and range formation during Miocene (27-3 million years ago) created northwest-trending basins and ranges through extensional tectonism.

[[Gila intermedia]] and [[Gila robusta robusta]] maintain mutually exclusive distributions where they co-occur in single basins, a pattern typical of multiple [[Gila]] species. The authors collected both forms within hundreds of meters of one another but never found them syntopic despite decades of sampling. This allotopy is the rule when two or more kinds of [[Gila]] inhabit a single river system.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Status and Morphological Patterns:**
- [[Gila intermedia]] warrants specific status based on: (1) wide phenotypic divergence from [[Gila robusta robusta]]; (2) slight differentiation among populations that does not trend toward G. robusta; (3) mosaic distribution of phenotypes inconsistent with subspecific status; (4) maintenance of independence in contiguous populations over time.
- [[Gila robusta robusta]] populations upstream from Grand Canyon are monomorphic and correspond with type descriptions. Specimens from larger streams in the lower Colorado River basin are similar.
- [[Gila intermedia]] from Santa Cruz, upper San Pedro, and San Simon drainages are remarkably homogeneous and interpreted as genetically pure stocks. These populations inhabit an area of geologic drainage continuity with long potential for isolation.
- Problematic populations along the southern margin of the [[Colorado Plateau]] (referred by previous authors to "G. r. grahami") form a continuum between [[Gila robusta robusta]] and [[Gila intermedia]], with little geographic congruence to present drainage relationships.

**Rejection of Alternative Hypotheses:**
- **Ecophenotypy rejected:** Intrademic temporal stability documented; absolute stream size is poor correlate of morphology; temperature does not explain meristic patterns (cold headwaters support fish with lower meristics than downstream populations); restriction of [[Gila intermedia]] and problematic stocks to [[Gila River]] basin refutes environmental determinism (similar habitats in upper Colorado and Mexican drainages lack these forms).
- **Ecotypy rejected:** Phenotypic uniformity of [[Gila intermedia]] across diverse habitats (farm ponds, springs, marshlands, erosive streams) argues against independent evolution; strong correlations among presumably polygenic characters would not be expected in ecotypes; similar character complexes unlikely to arise independently among allopatric populations.
- Low meristic features strongly intercorrelated (r values in Table 3) and associated with chubby overall morphology, inconsistent with independent character acquisition.

**Hybridization Hypothesis:**
- Past genetic exchange proposed as origin for intermediate stocks, with plausible sequence of hydrographic development leading to origin and maintenance of hybrid stocks while perpetuating both parental species.
- **First mechanism:** Drainage transfers during development and evolution of [[Colorado Plateau]] margin. Miocene faulting and volcanism created structural troughs and basins. Transfer of streams with escarpment development and later drainage adjustments may have introduced [[Gila robusta]] south into forming [[Gila River]] watershed and range of [[Gila intermedia]].
- Hybridization would have occurred when rising water levels in closed basins incorporated stream and spring habitats into lacustrine systems and as drainages were disrupted and reinstated. Mutually exclusive distributions would have been disrupted during these periods.
- Intermediate stocks could have originated independently at multiple places along Plateau margin or in one place and dispersed through endorheic systems. Development of isolated basins precluded later invasions, so populations in creeks draining Colorado Plateau margin remained intact.
- **Second mechanism:** Invasion and replacement following integration of upper and lower Colorado basin in late Miocene-early Pliocene. Vagile [[Gila robusta]] would have dispersed through larger streams following system integration less than 5.5 million years ago.
- Some [[Gila intermedia]] stocks resisted swamping and retained integrity (small streams, springs, marshlands), while others were assimilated into dominating [[Gila robusta]] phenotype (larger creeks, smaller rivers).

**Geographic and Temporal Patterns:**
- Monomorphic [[Gila intermedia]] populations in Santa Cruz and upper San Pedro basins indicate long-term isolation. Intimate Plio-Pleistocene connections documented by St. David Formation. Absence of large-river species ([[Gila elegans]], [[Ptychocheilus lucius]], [[Catostomus latipinnis]], [[Xyrauchen texanus]]) and Gila River endemics ([[Meda fulgida]], [[Tiaroga cobitis]]) in Santa Cruz system supports isolation hypothesis.
- Fossil [[Gila robusta]] in Bidahochi Formation places species on [[Colorado Plateau]] in late Miocene, yet species widely distributed in major river basins south to Rio Culiacan, Sinaloa, Mexico. Authors suspect [[Gila robusta]] arose in Mexico and attained Colorado River through tectonic transport prior to basin and range formation.
- Morphology of Fossil Springs [[Gila]] reflects event occurring prior to Plio-Pleistocene breaching and drainage of Mio-Pliocene lake. Fossil Springs isolated by >500 m of precipitous channel including numerous vertical falls. Identity with East Verde River and Tonto Creek stocks documents substantial age of origin.
- Absence of [[Gila intermedia]] and problematic stocks in upper Salt River system may relate to volcanic activity in White Mountains (late Miocene to late Pleistocene) and subsequent dispersal of [[Gila robusta]] through larger streams.

**Unrecognized Taxa Possibility:**
- Low lateral-line scale counts, basilar radii on scales, and other features of [[Gila intermedia]] and problematic populations also present in [[Gila crassicauda]], [[Gila purpurea]], [[Gila ditaenia]], and [[Gila orcutti]] (subgenus Temeculina), though Gila River forms lack plate-like dermosphenotic of that subgenus.
- Phenetic classification would align problematic populations more closely with [[Gila nigrescens]] and [[Gila pandora]] (Lago de Guzman and Rio Grande basins) than to [[Gila robusta]]. These species virtually indistinguishable osteologically from [[Gila intermedia]] and problematic forms.
- Character gradation from [[Gila pandora]] through [[Gila nigrescens]] to [[Gila robusta]] remarkably similar to data set for [[Gila intermedia]], problematic populations, and [[Gila robusta]].
- Alternative exists that these taxa comprise species derived independently from [[Gila robusta]] lineage, representing ancient inhabitants of early upper Gila River-Rio Grande drainage complex.

**Methodological Notes:**
- Canonical Discriminant Analysis demonstrates continuum with [[Gila robusta robusta]] at one extreme and [[Gila intermedia]] at other, but little geographic congruence with present drainage relationships.
- Each problematic population is unique; none includes entire range of variation; none exhibits markedly greater variability than either parental species (Table 2).
- Problematic stocks tend toward three morphological states: (1) near identity with [[Gila intermedia]] (upper Walker and Oak creeks, Big Chino Wash, uppermost Agua Fria watershed); (2) evident intermediacy suggesting F1 hybrids (Fossil Creek, Fossil Springs, upper West Clear Creek, East Verde River, Tonto Creek); (3) near identity with [[Gila robusta robusta]] (Burro Creek, Aravaipa Creek, some Salt River tributaries, lower Eagle Creek, uppermost Gila River in New Mexico).
