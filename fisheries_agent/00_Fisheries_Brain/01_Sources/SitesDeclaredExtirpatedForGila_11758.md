# Extirpation Determinations and Recovery Site Status for Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish in Arizona

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

A letter dated January 15, 1999, from the [[US Fish And Wildlife Service]] Arizona Ecological Services Field Office transmits the final decision on the extirpation of stocked populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) at nine Arizona sites. The decision follows the 1997 [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] Provisional Extirpation Report and Status Report and was made in coordination with that agency. Three site lists accompany the letter: sites declared extirpated and unsuitable for restocking, sites recommended for additional management or restocking, and sites surveyed as potential reintroduction locations. The letter also explains how [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] applies to each category.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** The Gila topminnow and desert pupfish are native Arizona fishes listed under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. Both were stocked at multiple sites, and the letter treats them as a recovery pair.
- **Extirpation criteria:** A population that meets [[Extirpation Criteria]] triggers a [[Provisional Extirpation Report]]. That report is reviewed by the US Fish and Wildlife Service, land management agencies, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] before a final decision is made.
- **Scope of the decision:** The letter covers sites reviewed in the 1997 Arizona Game and Fish Department reports. Sites confirmed to hold populations, or where extirpation is doubtful, are not listed. Information on those sites is available on request.
- **Land ownership:** Sites fall under the [[Tonto National Forest]], [[Prescott National Forest]], [[Coronado National Forest]], [[Coconino National Forest]], [[Phoenix Bureau Of Land Management]], [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]], [[The Nature Conservancy]], and private landowners.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The sites are mostly small springs, spring-fed tanks, and stream reaches in central and southern Arizona. Habitat condition is the main factor in each site's status:

- **Extirpated sites** no longer sustain either species and have been judged inadequate for restocking. Management should still consider their value for other listed or sensitive species and for watershed and riparian function.
- **Recovery sites** are thought to be extirpated but have habitat conditions sufficient for additional management or restocking. Section 7 compliance may apply if federal actions could affect them or prevent their use for recovery.
- **Reintroduction sites** were surveyed for suitability. Several already hold other fishes, which the letter lists for each site. Among them are [[Razorback Sucker]] at Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge tanks, [[Largemouth Bass]] at Fresno Tank #2 on private land, and a dace and [[Desert Sucker]] at West Fork Pinto Creek. Non-native predators such as largemouth bass are relevant to whether a site can support reintroduction.
- A footnote in Table 1 notes that one site was changed from the previous final extirpation report and recommended for restocking, often because of dryness at the site.

## Key Findings

- **Nine sites** were declared extirpated for Gila topminnow or desert pupfish. Table 1 lists them with site numbers, locations, and ownership, mainly on Tonto, Prescott, and Phoenix BLM lands. Declared-extirpated sites no longer require Section 7 consultation for these two species.
- **Recovery sites:** A second table lists roughly a dozen sites thought to be extirpated but considered valuable for recovery. These include Oxbow Spring, Bronco Canyon Spring, Fish Creek (Maricopa County, The Nature Conservancy), East Verde River (Coconino National Forest), Sycamore Creek, and Verde River reaches near Perkins and Chino Valley. Management action or restocking is recommended for these sites, and Section 7 applies if federal actions could affect them.
- **Reintroduction candidates:** A third table lists about a dozen surveyed sites recommended for reintroduction. Examples are Sevenmile Wash, Amen Creek, Buckhorn Spring #2, Carrizo Dam Tank, Rock Tank 321, Triangle/HQ Tank, Babocomari River/T-4 Spring, and Fresno Tank #2.
- **Process requests:** Recipients were asked to report any evidence of misclassification within 30 days. Misclassified landowners would be notified. Recipients were also asked to suggest additional reintroduction sites and to comment on the forthcoming 1998 Arizona Game and Fish Department Provisional Extirpation Report.
