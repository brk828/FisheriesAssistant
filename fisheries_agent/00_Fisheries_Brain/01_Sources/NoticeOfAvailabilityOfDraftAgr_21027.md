## Executive Summary

This document is a **1993 Federal Register Notice** issued by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) announcing the availability of a draft **Section 7 Consultation Agreement** and a **Recovery Implementation Program Recovery Action Plan (RIPRAP)** for four endangered fish species native to the **Upper Colorado River Basin**. Rather than presenting new biological or morphological data, the notice summarizes the regulatory and conservation framework — rooted in the **Endangered Species Act (ESA)** — established to recover these species while allowing continued water development in compliance with state and interstate law. The [[Recovery Implementation Program]] (est. 1986) is a multi-stakeholder cooperative effort involving federal agencies, three states, power administration bodies, and water/conservation interest groups.

## Background & Description

The source document is regulatory/administrative in nature and does not provide morphological descriptions. However, the four target taxa are noted for identification purposes:

- **[[Colorado Squawfish]]** (*Ptychocheilus lucius*) — a large-bodied cyprinid, historically the top native piscivore of the basin.
- **[[Humpback Chub]]** (*Gila cypha*) — distinguished by a pronounced dorsal hump, an adaptation associated with turbulent canyon-river habitats.
- **[[Bonytail Chub]]** (*Gila elegans*) — a streamlined cyprinid adapted to swift, historically abundant mainstem river currents.
- **[[Razorback Sucker]]** (*Xyrauchen texanus*) — a catostomid characterized by a sharp-edged dorsal keel behind the head.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** All four species are native to the **Upper Colorado River Basin**, defined administratively as the Colorado River drainage upstream of [[Lake Powell]], excluding the San Juan River drainage.
- **Historical Status:** Each species was formerly abundant throughout the basin but has undergone severe population decline.
- **Threats Identified:**
  - Direct loss of physical habitat
  - Altered water flow and temperature regimes (attributed to dam/reservoir operations and water development)
  - Blockage of migration routes (fragmentation of river connectivity)
  - Competitive/predatory interactions with introduced **nonnative fish species**
- **Regulatory Ecology:** The FWS has maintained a formal **jeopardy finding** for these species since 1978, articulated through more than 180 biological opinions issued under ESA Section 7 review of water development projects in the basin.

## Key Findings

- **Federal Listing:** All four species — [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Chub]], [[Bonytail Chub]], and [[Razorback Sucker]] — are federally listed as **endangered** under the ESA.
- **Recovery Program Origin:** Established in 1986 via cooperative agreement among the Governors of Colorado, Utah, and Wyoming, the Secretary of the Interior, and the Western Area Power Administration.
- **Program Goal:** Recover and delist the four endangered fishes by restoring/establishing **self-sustaining populations** and protecting sufficient habitat, while enabling continued water development consistent with the ESA, Interstate Compacts, and state law.
- **Draft Agreement (dated March 11, 1993):** Clarifies procedures for ESA Section 7 consultations regarding depletion impacts from **new water projects** and **historic projects** (existing projects requiring new federal action) in the Upper Basin.
- **RIPRAP (dated July 19, 1993):** Identifies specific recovery actions and timeframes deemed necessary for the most expeditious recovery of the endangered fish; serves as a measurable accomplishment benchmark supporting the Recovery Program's role as the ESA-required "reasonable and prudent alternative" to jeopardy.
- **NEPA Compliance:** A programmatic Environmental Assessment for the Recovery Program was completed in November 1987; additional site-specific NEPA compliance will be conducted for individual RIPRAP actions.
- **Critical Habitat:** The Recovery Program is intended to also serve as the reasonable and prudent alternative avoiding destruction/adverse modification of **critical habitat**, with amendments anticipated once critical habitat is formally designated.
- **Public Process:** A 30-day public comment period was opened on the draft Agreement and RIPRAP, with document review coordinated through the FWS Denver office (Assistant Regional Director, Ecological Services).
- **Institutional Participants:** U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Bureau of Reclamation, States of Utah, Wyoming, and Colorado, Western Area Power Administration, water development interests, and conservation groups; the [[Colorado River Energy Distributors Association]] participates as a nonvoting member.
