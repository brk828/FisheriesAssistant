## Executive Summary

This document is a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) memorandum from the Arizona Ecological Services State Office, amending a prior Section 7 Biological Opinion (dated September 13, 1991, with subsequent amendments in April 1992 and February 1993) concerning the collection of the endangered [[Humpback Chub]] (*Gila cypha*) in [[Grand Canyon National Park]]. The memo increases the authorized level of "incidental take" for the species based on unexpectedly high field abundance data observed during 1993 collection efforts in the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR). The revision reflects an adaptive management response to on-the-ground field data superseding earlier, more conservative take estimates.

## Background & Description

- **Species:** *Gila cypha* ([[Humpback Chub]]), family [[Cyprinidae]].
- **Size class distinctions are central to the take permit structure:**
  - **Young-of-year (YOY) chubs:** Defined operationally here as individuals in the 31–74 mm total length (TL) range; found to be "very abundant" in the 1993 LCR sampling season.
  - **Sub-adults/juveniles >30 mm TL:** Subject to prior incidental take caps (see Key Findings).
  - **No specimens larger than 75 mm TL** were incidentally taken during the reporting period, suggesting the observed abundance surge was concentrated in newly recruited, smaller size classes.
- Note: The source document does not provide detailed morphological/anatomical descriptions (e.g., nuchal hump development, fin ray counts) — data is limited to total length (TL) size-class thresholds relevant to permit compliance.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The [[Little Colorado River]], a major tributary confluence within [[Grand Canyon National Park]], serves as critical spawning and rearing habitat for *Gila cypha*. This tributary is a well-documented refugial and reproductive habitat for the species compared to the mainstem [[Colorado River]].
- **Life stage habitat use:** The abundance of young-of-year individuals in the LCR during the June–August (summer) sampling window is consistent with known spawning/recruitment timing for the species, where larval and juvenile chubs concentrate in warmer, turbid tributary waters before potentially dispersing.
- **Vulnerability to handling stress:** The memo notes that as YOY fish age and grow, they are expected to become "more hardy and less inclined to be harmed from net or handling stress" — an ecological/physiological observation relevant to survivorship during scientific collection activities.
- **Management context:** The species is protected under the Endangered Species Act, necessitating Section 7 consultation and strict incidental take limits for any handling, netting, or collection activity performed by permitted researchers (FWS Fishery Resources Office, Pinetop, AZ).

## Key Findings

- **Original incidental take limits** (for humpback chub >30 mm TL) established in prior biological opinions:
  - 1991: 20 individuals
  - 1992: 25 individuals
  - 1993 (pre-amendment): 10 individuals
- **Observed 1993 field abundance:** FWS "handled almost 10,000 humpback chubs" in the LCR between June 6 and August 15, 1993 — vastly exceeding earlier projections and indicating an unusually strong young-of-year cohort that year.
- **Take ceiling concern:** FWS was reportedly nearing its incidental take limit (after accounting for fish taken purposefully for study purposes vs. incidentally).
- **Size-based mitigation:** No incidentally-taken humpback chub exceeded 75 mm TL, indicating collection impacts were concentrated on the smallest, most abundant size class.
- **Permit amendment outcome:** An additional allowance of **up to 20 incidental takes** of humpback chub in the 31–74 mm TL range was authorized for the remainder of the permit period (through end of 1993), covering one confirmed and one potential additional FWS field trip to the LCR.
- **Continued compliance requirement:** The applicant (FWS) was directed to continue following the reasonable and prudent measures and terms/conditions of the original 1991 Biological Opinion to minimize incidental take.
- **Distribution list** indicates broad inter-agency coordination, including [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]], [[Navajo Fish and Wildlife]], the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program, and Arizona State University's Center for Environmental Studies — reflecting the multi-agency management framework surrounding [[Colorado River]] endangered fish conservation.
