# Aravaipa Creek Red Shiner Survey: November 1995 Summary Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This memorandum from the [[Nongame Native Fish Program]] of the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] reports the November 1995 [[Red Shiner]] survey of the lower reach of [[Aravaipa Creek]], from [[Brandenburg Wash]] to near the [[San Pedro River]] confluence. The survey used the same approach as an October 1993 survey, but concentrated on the lower creek. Crews made 964 seine hauls over about 50 person-hours at nine locations. **No red shiners were found.** The catch was dominated by [[Longfin Dace]] (92.3% of 2,205 fish in the summary table). Native species were present across the reach, and two nonnative species, [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]], were collected. The report also flags a large diversion pond near the end of surface flow as a possible reservoir for nonnative fishes and recommends gill netting to assess it.

## Background & Description

The survey was designed to locate and collect red shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*) by sampling all habitats within Aravaipa Creek. The 1993 protocol covered the creek from its confluence with [[Turkey Creek]] to the San Pedro River, along with 50 m up each flowing tributary and about 1 km of the San Pedro on either side of the confluence. The secondary goal was quantitative data on the whole creek fish community, obtained by counting all species in every fifth or tenth seine haul when resources allowed.

The 1995 survey covered the lower creek. Thirteen biologists took part on the first day and seven on the second. Crews included agency staff (Arizona Game and Fish Department, [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[Natural Resources Conservation Service]], Arizona Department of Environmental Quality), university biologists, [[The Nature Conservancy]] staff, and volunteers. Weather on both days was cool, calm, and warm. The creek was clear and at base flow.

**Methods (from the 1993 protocol and 1995 field notes):**
- Seines of 1/4 or 1/8 inch mesh, no longer than 20 feet, were used in all habitats (run, riffle, pool, backwater), with teams working upstream.
- Hauls were designed to cover about twice the seine length, and every fifth or tenth haul was counted by species.
- Nonnative fish were preserved in 10% [[Formalin Preservation]]. Native fish were released after inspection.
- Effort was reported as the estimated percentage of channel sampled, which varied by site from 40% to 100% because of boulders, depth, velocity, and time limits.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The lower creek is braided and shallow in places. Surface flow ends about six kilometers above the San Pedro confluence, and the crew suspected the creek runs subsurface from there to the river. Substrate and cover limited seining at several sites: boulders at Location 1, and overhanging vegetation and cutbanks at Location 7. Location 1 had one deep pool connected to the main channel that held most of the local green sunfish. Leopard frogs were fairly common (about 25 along the reach), and one specimen was measured at 80 mm snout-vent length.

The diversion pond about 100 m upstream of the end of surface flow is roughly 200 m² and more than 2 m deep, with dense vegetation around its edge. Crews seined about 10% of its margin and caught one longfin dace and six juvenile green sunfish. The authors note the pond could hold many nonnative fishes.

**Survey totals:**
- **Seine hauls:** 964 total, 124 empty (about 13%).
- **Red shiner:** not found at any location.
- **Nonnative species:** [[Green Sunfish]] and [[Yellow Bullhead]], most abundant in the uncounted hauls at Locations 6 and 7.
- **Native species:** [[Desert Sucker]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Longfin Dace]], [[Roundtail Chub]], [[Sonoran Sucker]], [[Speckled Dace]], and [[Spikedace]].

**Recommendations and notes:**
- The diversion pond (Location 9) should be sampled with gill nets to determine species composition and abundance before the nonnative populations are assessed further.
- Fish numbers were tallied only for every fifth haul, though some teams recorded all nonnative fish. The authors treat the counts as partial.
- The 1993 protocol specified a [[Red Shiner]] capture-location map for each crew leader, but this summary does not report any capture locations, since none were found.
