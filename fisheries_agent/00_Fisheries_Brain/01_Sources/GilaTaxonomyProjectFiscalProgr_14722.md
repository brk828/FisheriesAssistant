# Gila Taxonomy Project: Fiscal 1990 Progress Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This progress report, submitted by Project Manager Wayne C. Starnes (Division of Fishes, [[Smithsonian Institution]]) to the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (USFWS), covers the first full year of the [[Gila Taxonomy Project]] under a cooperative agreement. The project addresses unresolved taxonomy of the cyprinid genus *Gila* in the Colorado River drainage. Work in Fiscal 1990 included formation of a [[Review Panel]], completion of two Phase I reports (a literature and collections review, and a management and research plan), a [[Request For Proposals]] for research contracts, and the start of [[Non-Lethal Tissue Sampling]] experiments on hatchery fish. Contract awards were delayed, so about $110K of FY 1990 funds was rolled over to FY 1992.

## Background & Description

The project was launched under a cooperative agreement dated 28 April 1989, with the Project Manager starting work on 2 July 1989. Its tasks follow Scopes of Work received by the Colorado River Implementation Recovery program on 19 May 1989. The stated goal is to resolve taxonomic questions for Colorado River *Gila* that affect management and genetic decisions.

**Project structure**
- **Review Panel:** Scientists with expertise in fish taxonomy and genetics, plus biologists from federal and state agencies managing Colorado Basin aquatic resources. Members were Dr. Jim Bennett (Colorado Division of Wildlife), Dr. Brooks M. Burr (Southern Illinois University), Dr. Anthony A. Echelle (Oklahoma State University), Dr. Dean Hendrickson (Arizona Game & Fish, then University of Texas), Ms. Denise Knight (Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, later departed; Randy Radant acting), and Dr. David Philipp (Illinois Natural History Survey). Membership was finalized on 20 Nov. 1989.
- **Agency representatives** at the 6 Feb. 1990 review meeting: Holt Williamson, John Hamill, Robert Williams, Reed Harris, and Larry Shanks.

**Phase I Part 1: Existing Information Report.** Circulated in October 1989 to about 60 interested parties; the final report (75 copies) was delivered to USFWS, Denver on 23 Feb. 1990. Its 117 pages contain:
- Over 1000 journal articles and government reports canvassed, with about two hundred judged relevant and annotated.
- Research summaries from 19 university, agency, and consultant biologists.
- Collections resources at 26 institutions. The seven largest were visited by the Project Manager, and about 1600 lots of *Gila* were cross-listed by institution, catalog number, species, and drainage basin/subbasin.

**Phase I Part 2: Management Concerns and Research Plan.** Drafted around 20 Jan. 1990. It summarized taxonomic problems, management concerns, and priority research needs. The proposed approach combined three or four complementary morphological data sets with two or three genetic data sets ([[Protein Electrophoresis]], [[DNA Analysis]], and possibly chromosome studies), run simultaneously on cross-referenced specimens. Non-lethal methods were emphasized: fish were biopsied, image-recorded, and released alive. Comparisons with older museum material were intended to assess changes in population composition since habitat alteration.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document discusses the *Gila* species and the Colorado River habitat primarily in terms of management and taxonomy. It notes that habitat alteration by humans may have changed species composition in populations, and that questions of population change motivated comparisons with historical museum specimens. Future genetic and habitat management needs were identified as major outcomes of the work.

Field sampling was planned across multiple regions: the USFWS Colorado River Fishes Project (CRFP) offices at Grand Junction and Vernal, and the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources. Permitting covers Regions 1, 2, and 6. Ultracold freezers and portable liquid nitrogen refrigerators were placed at Vernal, Grand Junction, and Price, Utah, to store tissues and specimens for genetic work.

Hatchery fish used in the experiments were held at [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] (transfers to Ouray), [[Jones Hole National Fish Hatchery]], and the Ouray, Utah facility.

## Key Findings

**Organization and review**
- The Review Panel and agency representatives met at USFWS Denver on 6 Feb. 1990. Revisions strengthened the non-lethal sampling and genetics management sections. The revised draft went to about 60 parties on 19 Feb., and the final draft (75 copies) was submitted on 5 Mar. 1990.

**Cooperative agreement**
- Renewal was not completed by the 2 Mar. 1990 expiration. An extension ran to 13 Apr., and a renewed agreement (Modifications) was signed effective through 13 April 1991.

**Request for Proposals**
- Descriptions, specifications, and selection criteria were sent to the Bureau of Reclamation's Upper Colorado Contracts Office on 6 May 1990. The RFP was issued on 25 Jul. 1990 with proposals due 31 Aug.
- Separate schedules were offered for morphological investigations, protein electrophoresis, DNA studies, and, pending funding, chromosomal investigations (Schedule IV).
- Government Cost Estimates for the four schedules were submitted on 9 Aug. 1990.
- Eleven proposals from seven offerors were received.
- The Technical Proposal Evaluation Committee met 23–25 Sept. 1990 with panel members Burr, Echelle, Philipp, and Radant. Its 21-page TPEC report was submitted on 16 Oct. 1990.
- Best and Final Offers were requested within three weeks of notification. The Committee recommended withholding the Schedule IV offer until funding for the other three schedules was determined. Contract proceedings were expected to continue for much of calendar 1990.

**Fiscal 1991 planning**
- The FY 1991 Scope of Work, prepared 8 May 1990, proposed a budget of $252K, which was approved (per the Committee's draft work plan of 28 June 1990).
- A draft FY 1991 Work Plan (3 May 1990) drew constructive comments from USFWS CRFP offices (Grand Junction and Vernal), the Utah Division of Wildlife Resources, and New Mexico Game and Fish. The plan must be revised after research contractors are selected.

**Non-lethal tissue sampling experiments**
- The study, "Effects of Tissue Collection on Three Fish Species," was submitted 18 Apr. 1990 with Dr. Harold Tyus (USFWS) as Principal Investigator. It was to involve biopsy experiments on three or four hatchery species, including trout and one or two *Gila* species.
- After a site visit on 20 May, Holt Williamson joined the project as Technical Project Officer. The plan was submitted to the Bureau and funded on 24 May.
- **13 June:** 78 [[Bonytail Chub]], 98 [[Razorback Sucker]], and 100 juvenile [[Colorado Squawfish]] were transferred from Dexter NFH to Ouray.
- **6–18 July (Jones Hole NFH):** 120 trout received treatments ranging from anesthetized controls to fin punching, muscle biopsy, liver biopsy through surgery, surgery without biopsy, and combinations. No mortality occurred. On 26 Sept., wounds showed remarkable healing and continued growth and sexual development. Follow-up was planned through January 1991.
- **1 Oct.:** 45 experimental trout were terminated by the Fish Disease Lab to test for bacterial kidney disease at Jones Hole. Tests were negative.
- **7–8 August (Ouray):** Six treatments with 12 replicates were applied to bonytail chubs. No mortality occurred until about 15 Sept., when two tanks had *Ichthyophthirius* outbreaks with total mortality in each. Controls died along with treated fish, so experimental trauma was judged not to be the cause.
- **Survival and wound healing:** 26 bonytail survived in the third tank. Wound healing appeared slower at ambient laboratory temperatures, so heaters were added around 25 Sept. to test this. The 98 razorback suckers (treated 4–5 Sept.) escaped the outbreak, with a single mortality.
- The 100 young squawfish will be held until spring to reach sufficient size for the experiments.
- The team considers early results sufficient to prepare preliminary non-lethal sampling protocols, to be refined with further data.
- In September 1990, Genetics Analysis, Incorporated (Smithville, Texas) was contracted to screen a small number of *Gila* specimens for the maximum number of proteins resolvable from non-lethally obtained tissues.

**Budget**
- Surplus FY 1990 funds (approximately $110K) were rolled into the Fiscal 1992 budget, because contractors cannot be paid until work is done and FY 1991 was already allotted (per the 11 July Management Group meeting summary).

**Equipment**
- Ultracold freezers were stationed at Vernal and Grand Junction as interim tissue and specimen repositories.
- Three portable liquid nitrogen refrigerators were based at Vernal, Grand Junction, and Price, Utah.
- Biopsy instruments, blood-sampling syringes, vials, microcentrifuge tubes, holder/driers, and freezer racks were purchased.

**Permits**
- After meetings on 17 July and 21 Sept. 1990 with USFWS Enhancement staff (Max Schroeder and Larry Shanks), the agreed approach is a single permit to the Project Manager with subpermits from Regions 1, 2, and 6. Contractors will be subpermitted under this permit. The permit process awaits completion of contractual arrangements. The Project Manager will also seek state permits.
