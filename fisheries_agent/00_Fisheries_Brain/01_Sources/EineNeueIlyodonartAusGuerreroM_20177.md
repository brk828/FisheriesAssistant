# Ilyodon lennoni n. sp.: A New Goodeid Species from Guerrero, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #goodeidae

## Executive Summary

This taxonomic paper, written in German with an English summary, describes [[Ilyodon Lennoni]], a new viviparous livebearer of the family [[Goodeidae]] from the Arroyo Chacambero near Altamirano, Guerrero, Mexico. The species is compared morphologically and karyotypically with three congeners: [[Ilyodon Xantusi]], [[Ilyodon Whitei]], and [[Ilyodon Furcidens]]. The authors find that all four species share a diploid chromosome number of 48, but that the new species has a sex-chromosome pattern that is still unresolved. Based on tooth and trophotaeniae characters, they propose dividing the genus into two species groups.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The authors note that the family Goodeidae has been revised repeatedly since the foundational work of [[Hubbs And Turner 1939]], and that the family is currently divided into about 12 genera with roughly 42 species. The genus Ilyodon was considered taxonomically settled at the time, with three recognized taxa. The new species adds a fourth.

**Type material.** The following specimens were collected on 8 December 1980 by E. Hnilicka and are deposited in the Ichthyological Collection of the [[Museum Fur Tierkunde Dresden]] (MTD-F):

- Holotype: one male (MTD-F 1881)
- Paratypes: two females (MTD-F 1882–83), two males (MTD-F 1884–85), and five juveniles (MTD-F 1886–90)

The species is named in honor of John Winston Lennon of New York.

**Diagnosis and morphology.** The species has a rounded head and an elongate, robust body. Key characters include:

- Meristics: dorsal fin rays 15–16, caudal 36–38, anal 12–13, ventral 6, pectoral 14; lateral transverse scale rows 13–15; lateral line scales 37; vertebrae 38.
- Males have the anal fin modified into a gonopodium (andropodium).
- Adult males have widely scattered black comma-shaped marks along the body sides, and dark-brown body coloration on the upper half. Fins are yellow-orange, with black margins on the caudal fin.
- Adult females have a brown-yellow ground color and a central stripe of brown comma-like marks. Their fins are paler and less marked than those of males.
- Juveniles are grey-brown with a finely dashed central band.
- Tooth heads of the anterior elements are uniform in shape, with a deeper notch on the tooth head, and a keel (see [[Dental Morphology]]).
- Trophotaeniae (nutritive cords) number 10–12, nearly equal in length, with several short and robust cords.

**Measurements.** Table 1 gives measurements for the holotype and paratypes in millimetres (the first column is headed SL). For the holotype, the table lists 60.30 (SL), 51.50 (body depth), 9.30 (head length), 13.50 (caudal peduncle depth), 6.00 (eye–ventral distance), 17.60 (eye–dorsal distance), 23.60 (eye–dorsal distance, second measure), and 4.00 (eye diameter). The largest paratype is listed at 77.40 (SL), and the smallest at 12.00 to 12.80 (SL).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The Arroyo Chacambero is a slow-flowing stream draining into the [[Rio Balsas]] system. Its bed alternates between boulders, gravel, sand, and agate-bearing deposits. At the collecting sites the stream is about 4–5 m wide and divided into separate river compartments. At the end of the dry season, the stream forms isolated pools that heat strongly in the sun. These pools support dense filamentous algae, which the authors identify as the basic food of the Ilyodon species.

**Climate and surroundings.** The annual mean water temperature is 28 °C. The surrounding landscape is hilly scrubland with thorny shrubs and sparse trees, including reddish hardwood. Patches of agriculture include orchards of mango, lemon, and banana, and occasional maize fields.

**Associated fishes.** Companion species include [[Poeciliopsis Balsas]], *Astyanax* sp., *Cichlasoma* n. sp., and *Poecilia* n. sp.

**Water chemistry.** The water has a distinctly bitter taste, with pH 8.0, carbonate hardness of 10°, and total hardness of 15°. The authors state that the upper reaches of the Arroyo Chacambero remain poorly studied.

## Key Findings

**1. Morphological distinctiveness.** The new species differs clearly from all known Ilyodon species in tooth-head shape and trophotaeniae structure. Several of its nutritive cords are broad and short, which is unique within the genus. Its tooth notches are shallower than in *I. furcidens* and grade into tooth windows.

**2. Intermediate character states.** The outer main tooth elements of *I. lennoni* and *I. xantusi* are intermediate between the two extremes represented by *I. furcidens* and *I. whitei*. Trophotaeniae of *I. lennoni* are short, strong, and mostly equal in length, while those of *I. furcidens* are narrow and of unequal length. The new species is considered to correspond closely with *I. furcidens*.

**3. Proposed grouping within Ilyodon.** The authors propose dividing the genus into two species groups:

- Group 1: *I. furcidens* and *I. lennoni* n. sp.
- Group 2: *I. xantusi* and *I. whitei*

Whether these groups should be treated as subspecies is not resolved, because morphology alone cannot demonstrate this.

**4. Karyotypes.** All four species have a diploid complement of 48 chromosomes, and the karyotypes are very similar. Chromosomes are almost entirely acrocentric, decreasing continuously in size from about 4 to 2 µm, with about 6–7 pairs (or about 7 pairs in some species) showing short arms. Karyotypes were prepared from gill epithelium.

**5. Sex chromosomes.**

- *Ilyodon whitei* shows clear dimorphism of its sex chromosomes. A metacentric chromosome occurs in females and is interpreted as the female-determining chromosome, consistent with a WZ–ZZ mechanism.
- *Ilyodon lennoni* shows two submetacentric chromosomes in females. These were found in 27 metaphases (15 from the first female and 12 from the second). They were absent from all three males, which yielded 17 to 21 metaphases in two cases and only a few poor-quality metaphases in the third. The authors believe these are sex chromosomes, but the sex-determination mechanism remains unknown. They note that the observed pattern is not explained by normal egg-cell segregation, since the expected count would be a submetacentric chromosome in all six females. They recommend re-examining the sex ratio and performing karyotype analysis on a larger sample.
- *Ilyodon xantusi* and *I. furcidens* showed no detectable sex chromosomes.

**6. Relationship to earlier literature.** The paper relies on the revision of [[Hubbs And Turner 1939]] and on a broader family-level treatment by [[Turner 1946]]. It also cites [[Meyer 1981]] for a synopsis of the Goodeidae and [[Miller And Fitzsimons 1971]] for the taxonomy of related genera.
