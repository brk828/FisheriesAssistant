# Gila Topminnow and Desert Pupfish Extirpation Determinations and Recovery Sites, Arizona (1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This August 7, 1997 letter from the [[U.S. Fish And Wildlife Service]] (Arizona Ecological Services Field Office) transmits the final determination on extirpation of stocked populations of [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis*) and [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*) at sites in Arizona. The decision was made by the Service with the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] after reviewing the 1995 and 1996 Provisional Extirpation Reports. The letter also enclosed two tables: sites declared extirpated (no longer sustaining either species, judged unsuitable for restocking) and sites recommended for additional management or restocking because they retain significant recovery value.

## Background & Description

The letter covers two native Arizona fishes. The document itself gives no morphological, life-history or taxonomic detail beyond their names and their status as "indigenous fishes."

The determination process works as follows:

- A population that meets [[Extirpation Criteria]] receives a provisional extirpation report prepared by the Arizona Game and Fish Department.
- Each report is reviewed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, involved land management agencies, the Arizona Game and Fish Department, and the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]].
- The Service, in coordination with the state agency, then issues the final decision.

The letter states that the enclosed determinations cover **40 sites**. Counting the table entries, the two reports list 39 extirpated sites (21 from the 1995 report and 18 from the 1996 report). The discrepancy is noted here as it appears in the source.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Legal implications.** Sites declared extirpated no longer require [[Section 7 Consultation]] under the [[Endangered Species Act]] for these two species. Land managers should still evaluate these sites for other listed species, sensitive species, and watershed and riparian values. Sites where the fish persist, or where extirpation is doubtful, remain subject to Section 7 for any Federal action that may affect the species.

**Recovery sites.** Sites that are thought to be extirpated but retain significant conservation value are considered to have habitat conditions adequate for additional management or restocking. Section 7 compliance may apply if Federal actions could affect these sites or preclude their use for recovery.

**Land ownership.** The sites span a range of ownership, which affects management responsibilities:

- [[Tonto National Forest]] (most frequent, Tonto Basin, Globe, Mesa, Cave Creek, and Phoenix ranger districts)
- [[Coronado National Forest]] (Santa Catalina, Sierra Vista, and Nogales districts)
- [[Prescott National Forest]] (Bradshaw, Verde, and Prescott districts)
- [[Coconino National Forest]] (Sedona, Beaver Creek districts)
- [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (Phoenix, Tucson, Safford, and Mesa areas)
- [[Saguaro National Park]], [[State Land Department]], [[The Nature Conservancy]], [[National Audubon Society]], and private landowners

**Habitat types.** Named sites are mainly springs, artesian wells, tanks (stock tanks and troughs), creeks, and cienegas, with several in the Tonto and Coronado forests.

## Key Findings

**1. Extirpation determinations (Table 1).** Table 1 lists the extirpated sites by report:

- *1995 AGFD Report (21 sites):* Includes Artesian Well #3 and #4, Bear Canyon, Bench Well, Chalky Butte Well, Copper Canyon, El Pilar, Government Tank, Grapevine Spring, Little Outfit Tank, Pilot Tank, Shute Spring, Tres Alamos (BLM), White Tank (Coronado NF), and several unnamed springs and tanks.
- *1996 AGFD Report (18 sites):* Includes Chalk Tank, Chaney Place (private), Frog Spring, Jubilee Spring, McCann Spring Tank, Montezuma Tank, Peoples Canyon (BLM), Rock Spring #1, Sycamore Spring, Yellowstone Tank, and several unnamed springs and tanks.

Each entry includes site name, site number, township and range location, and ownership.

**2. Restocking and management recommendations (Table 2).** Table 2 lists sites that were judged to have significant recovery value and are recommended for additional management or restocking. It contains about 47 entries, drawn from both reports and an undated group. Hull Spring appears in both the 1995 and 1996 sections. Notable sites include:

- Several Tonto Basin and Cave Creek sites (for example, Campaign Creek, Red Creek, Zig Zag Spring, and Seven Springs)
- Nature Conservancy and BLM sites in the Santa Rita and Redfield Canyon area (O'Donnell Creek/Canelo Cienega, Redfield Canyon, Turkey Creek)
- Several Verde and Prescott sites (Cedar Spring, Oxbow Spring, Sheep Spring, Squaw Peak Spring)

**3. Management guidance.**

- Extirpated sites: assess other conservation values and monitor for other listed or sensitive species.
- Recovery sites: consider [[Reintroduction]] or [[Restocking]] and additional habitat management; expect Section 7 review if Federal actions are proposed.
- Sites where fish persist or extirpation is doubtful: no listing in this letter; available on request; Section 7 review applies.

**4. Procedural deadlines and requests.** Recipients may submit information showing a site was misclassified within **30 days**. The Service also asked for suggestions of other potential reintroduction sites. Comments on the forthcoming **1997 Provisional Extirpation Report** were requested, and the Service invited questions to Doug Duncan (520-670-4860) or Angie Brooks.

**5. Conservation context.** The letter ends by noting the Service's continued commitment to the "conservation and recovery of these indigenous fishes." It was signed by Field Supervisor Sam F. Spiller.
