# The Poeciliid Fishes of Rio Grande do Sul and the La Plata Basin

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #poeciliidae #taxonomy #south-america

## Executive Summary

This 1907 taxonomic revision by [[Carl H Eigenmann]] examines the [[Poeciliidae]] fauna of [[Rio Grande do Sul]] and the [[La Plata Basin]]. The study establishes several new genera based on detailed examination of teeth morphology and male reproductive organs (intromittent organs). Eigenmann describes one new species (*Ilyodon paraguayense*) and proposes three new genera (*Ilyodon*, *Phalloptychus*, and *Phalloceros*) to accommodate species previously misclassified. The work demonstrates that the Poeciliid fauna effectively distinguishes the Rio Grande do Sul-La Plata region as a distinct biogeographic province from the [[Amazonian Basin]] to the north.

## Background & Description

The study originated from examination of two specimens of a new Poeciliid found among [[Characins]] in the [[United States National Museum]] collections from [[Paraguay]]. This discovery prompted a comprehensive review of the entire Poeciliid fauna of the [[La Plata Basin]].

[[Von Ihering]] previously noted general similarity between freshwater faunas of [[Rio Grande do Sul]] and the [[La Plata Basin]]. All Poeciliid species recorded from Rio Grande do Sul also occur in the La Plata basin, supporting this biogeographic connection.

The taxonomic revision focuses on detailed morphological characters:
- **Dentition patterns**: bicuspid, tricuspid, spear-shaped, oar-shaped, or spoon-shaped teeth
- **Alimentary canal length**: ranging from equal to body length to 3 times body length
- **Male intromittent organ structure**: presence/absence of claspers, hooks, prepuce, and specific ray modifications

Key generic distinctions include:
- *[[Girardinus caudomaculatus]]* proven generically distinct from *[[Glaridichthys januarius]]*
- *januarius* shown to represent a new genus rather than *Glaridichthys*
- Multiple northern relatives placed in parentheses as extra-limital comparisons

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Geographic Distribution**: Species ranges extend from southeastern [[Brazil]] through [[Rio Grande do Sul]], [[Uruguay]] (Montevideo, Maldonado), [[Argentina]] (to Province Salta), and [[Paraguay]] (including [[Rio Apa]], [[Matto Grosso]], Villa Maria, Arroyo Trementina, Colonia Risso).

**Habitat**: All species occupy freshwater environments in rivers and streams of the La Plata drainage system.

**Biogeographic Significance**: The Poeciliids serve as effective biogeographic indicators, distinguishing the Rio Grande do Sul-La Plata province from the [[Amazonian Province]]. This represents one of the clearest faunal boundaries among South American freshwater fishes.

**Alimentary Canal Adaptations**: Intestinal length varies significantly among genera:
- *[[Ilyodon]]*: approximately 3 times body length
- *[[Fitzroya]]*: approximately equal to entire length
- *[[Phalloptychus]]* and *[[Phalloceros]]*: approximately twice body length

These variations suggest different feeding strategies and dietary specializations within the family.

## Key Findings

**New Taxa Described**:

1. **Genus *Ilyodon*** (new genus)
   - Type species: *Ilyodon paraguayense* (new species)
   - Distinguished by bicuspid teeth in single series and alimentary canal 3 times body length
   - D. 16-18; A. 14-15; scales approximately 45
   - Type specimen: Cat. No. 55642, U.S.N.M., 63 mm length, from [[Paraguay]]
   - Cotype: 64.4 mm length
   - Coloration: dorsal, caudal, and anal with conspicuous subterminal black bands with hyaline tips

2. **Genus *Phalloptychus*** (new genus)
   - Type species: *Girardinus januarius* Hensel
   - Intromittent organ with third, fourth, and fifth rays forming slender tube at tips
   - No claspers; organ ending in simple point
   - Fourth ray with very strong retrorse hooks
   - Intestine approximately twice length of fish

3. **Genus *Phalloceros*** (new genus)
   - Type species: *Girardinus caudomaculatus* Hensel
   - Distinguished by antler-like claspers at tip of second prolonged anal ray
   - Alimentary canal more than twice entire length
   - Fourth anal ray ending in antrorse point with two large antler-like structures
   - Geographic variation noted between [[Paraguay]] and [[Rio Grande do Sul]] specimens in tooth arrangement

**Taxonomic Key**: Comprehensive dichotomous key provided distinguishing genera based on:
- Tooth structure (bicuspid vs. tricuspid vs. conical)
- Number of tooth series
- Intromittent organ morphology
- Presence/absence of claspers and hooks
- Alimentary canal length
- Fin ray counts and scale counts

**Species Distributions Documented**:
- *[[Fitzroya lineata]]*: Rio Grande do Sul; Montevideo and Maldonado to Province Salta, Argentina
- *[[Phalloptychus januarius]]*: Southeastern Brazil to Maldonado and Paraguay
- *[[Cnesterodon decemmaculatus]]*: Rio Grande do Sul to Maldonado and Bolivian Chaco
- *[[Phalloceros caudomaculatus]]*: Rio Grande do Sul to Paraguay
- *[[Fundulus balzanii]]*: Villa Maria (Matto Grosso), Rio Paraguay
- *[[Fundulus paraguayensis]]*: Arroyo Trementina, Paraguay
- *[[Rivulus punctatus]]*: Colonia Risso, near Rio Apa
- *[[Cynolebias]]* species (5 species): La Plata region

**Morphological Observations**: Detailed illustrations provided of intromittent organ structures for multiple genera, showing specific modifications of third, fourth, and fifth anal rays. These structures prove diagnostic at generic level and demonstrate remarkable diversity in reproductive adaptations within [[Poeciliidae]].
