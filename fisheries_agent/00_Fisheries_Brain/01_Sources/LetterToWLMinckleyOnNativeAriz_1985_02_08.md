## Executive Summary

This document is a 1985 letter from William G. Kepner (U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, Ecological Services, Phoenix) to Dr. W. L. Minckley, intended to update Minckley's manuscript on native Arizona fish transplants through 1984. The letter provides tabulated translocation records for three imperiled native Arizona fish taxa: [[Agosia Chrysogaster]] (longfin/Hualapai dace), [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis Occidentalis]] (Gila topminnow), and [[Cyprinodon Macularius]] (desert pupfish). These records document cooperative recovery efforts between the [[Bureau Of Land Management]] (BLM) and the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] (AGFD), including source populations, recipient sites, introduction dates, numbers of individuals transplanted, and site elevations. The Hualapai Agosia records are highlighted as being of particular scientific interest, as they represent the only ichthyofaunal records for the Red Lake (=Hualapai) drainage.

## Background & Description

No morphological descriptions, meristic counts, or anatomical data are provided in this source. The document is strictly a transplant/introduction record log accompanying a management letter; it does not describe body form, coloration, or diagnostic features of any of the three species. Readers seeking morphological data for these taxa should consult primary species accounts for [[Agosia Chrysogaster]], [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis Occidentalis]], and [[Cyprinodon Macularius]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat types represented:** springs (e.g., Truxton Spring, Peoples Canyon Spring, Mesquite Spring, Grapevine Spring/Yerba Mansa), canyon pools and falls (Tres Alamos Falls, Peoples Canyon), a well (Howard Well), and artificial water storage structures (Dutchman Tank, Hunt's Tank), plus a natural creek (Cow Creek) and a river source population (Agua Fria River).
- **Elevation range of recipient sites:** approximately 1,360 ft (Grapevine Spring) to 3,420 ft (Howard Well), spanning desert lowland to mid-elevation canyon/upland habitats.
- **Source populations:**
  - *Agosia chrysogaster* — translocated from the Agua Fria River near Black Canyon City by a local rancher (Bill Robinson), subsequently further translocated to multiple secondary sites.
  - *Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis* (Gila topminnow) — sourced from Tule Creek for cooperative reintroductions.
  - *Cyprinodon macularius* (desert pupfish) — sourced from Boyce Thompson Arboretum for cooperative reintroductions.
- **Recipient drainages:** Hualapai (=Red Lake) drainage for Agosia; multiple sites in canyon/spring systems (Peoples Canyon, Tres Alamos Falls, Mesquite Spring, Howard Well, Grapevine Spring) for topminnow and pupfish, reflecting a broader Lower Colorado River Basin native fish recovery effort.
- **Co-management context:** All translocations of topminnow and pupfish were jointly conducted by [[Bureau Of Land Management]] and [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] as part of coordinated native species recovery programs, consistent with broader conservation strategies discussed in [[Minckley Native Arizona Fish Transplants]].
- **Data limitation noted:** Chemical/physical water quality data were available for all sites except the Hualapai drainage, indicating an ecological data gap for that particular introduction record.

## Key Findings

- A comprehensive transplant/introduction record set for three native Arizona fish taxa was compiled and forwarded to Minckley to update his manuscript on native Arizona fish transplants through 1984.
- **Agosia chrysogaster**: An original 1979 transplant of 200 individuals from the Agua Fria River to Truxton Spring (Hualapai drainage) was later followed by secondary transplants to three additional sites (Dutchman Tank, Hunt's Tank, Wright Creek), demonstrating a stepwise, rancher-initiated dispersal of the species within the watershed.
- The Truxton Spring/Hualapai (Red Lake) drainage record is noted as the **only ichthyofaunal record** for that drainage, underscoring its rarity and importance for regional biogeographic understanding.
- **Poeciliopsis o. occidentalis** (Gila topminnow) was introduced at four sites between 1981–1984 (Cow Creek, Peoples Canyon, Tres Alamos Falls, Grapevine Spring/Yerba Mansa), with stocking numbers ranging from 250 to 1,000 individuals, sourced from Tule Creek.
- **Cyprinodon macularius** (desert pupfish) was introduced at five sites between 1982–1983 (Tres Alamos Falls, Peoples Canyon, Peoples Canyon Spring, Mesquite Spring, Howard Well), with stocking numbers ranging from 75 to 200 individuals, sourced from Boyce Thompson Arboretum.
- Introductions were explicitly framed as **cooperative recovery efforts** between BLM and AGFD, reflecting coordinated interagency conservation action for two federally significant imperiled species, [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis Occidentalis]] and [[Cyprinodon Macularius]].
- The letter identifies a data gap: physical/chemical habitat data exist for nearly all sites except those in the Hualapai drainage, a limitation relevant to future habitat suitability assessments.
- This correspondence serves as a primary historical record supporting range expansion and reintroduction efforts for Arizona's native, imperiled desert fish fauna, complementing broader recovery literature such as [[Gila Topminnow Recovery Plan]] and [[Desert Pupfish Conservation]].
