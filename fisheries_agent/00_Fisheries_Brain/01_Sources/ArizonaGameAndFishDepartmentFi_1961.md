## Executive Summary

This report is a partial completion document from the [[Arizona Game And Fish Department]] describing a **recreation use study** of [[Pena Blanca Lake]], a reservoir in Arizona, conducted between May 1959 and September 1961. Rather than presenting morphological or population data on fish species, the study quantifies **recreational visitation patterns**, distinguishing angling from non-angling uses (sightseeing, picnicking, swimming, boating, hiking, camping) using pneumatic traffic counters and road-block interviews. The key finding is that **anglers accounted for less than 25% of total recreation visits** over the 28-month study period, with sightseeing and picnicking comprising the majority of non-angling use. This dataset was intended to inform state-level discussions, hearings, and litigation concerning recreational use of existing and planned reservoirs, and complements a separately published fish harvest estimate for the same water body (referenced but not detailed in this report).

## Background & Description

No morphological, anatomical, or taxonomic data on fish species are presented in this source. The report is methodologically and statistically focused on **human recreational behavior** rather than fish biology. Species-level information on the fish community of [[Pena Blanca Lake]] (e.g., target species for [[Sport Fishing]]) is not discussed; this is noted as a limitation of the available text, as the actual fish harvest data referenced (*"already been published"*) were not included in this partial report.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

While fish ecology is not directly addressed, the report provides indirect ecological and management context for [[Pena Blanca Lake]] as a **multi-use recreational reservoir**:

- The lake supports a mixed-use recreation area encompassing angling, boating, swimming, hiking, camping, and picnicking, indicating a **shallow-water, accessible impoundment** habitat typical of small Southwestern U.S. reservoirs.
- Seasonal visitation patterns (peaking in summer months, particularly June–August, and declining sharply October–February) suggest strong **seasonal thermal and climatic influence** on recreational (and likely angling) pressure, consistent with warmwater fishery dynamics in arid-region impoundments.
- The disproportionate non-angling use (sightseeing/picnicking dominating over fishing in most months) implies the lake functions ecologically and socially as a **general-purpose recreational habitat** rather than a fishery managed primarily for harvest, which has implications for [[Reservoir Management]] and allocation of conservation resources.
- Boat livery stubs and weekday/weekend comparisons imply some level of **year-round low-intensity angling access**, relevant to understanding fishing pressure as an anthropogenic ecological variable on the lake's fish populations.

## Key Findings

- **Angling was a minority activity**: across the full study period (May 1959–September 1961), anglers comprised roughly 27–32% of total party visits in most annual summaries, with non-angling parties (58–70%) consistently dominating.
- **Weighted annual averages** (Table I) show angling party percentages ranging from 31% to 42% depending on the year, with nonangler percentages correspondingly ranging from 58% to 69%.
- **Traffic counter expansions** (Table II) estimated total visitor numbers reaching into the tens of thousands annually (e.g., 52,210 total visitors in 1959 partial year; ~80,000+ in both 1960 and 1961), of which the majority were classified as non-anglers accompanying angling parties or visiting independently.
- **Activity breakdown** (Table III) confirms sightseeing and picnicking as the two largest non-angling activities, with sightseeing increasing from 22% (1959) to 47% (1960) and 41% (1961) of total recreational parties, while angling fluctuated between 31–51%.
- **Minor recreational activities** — swimming, boating, hiking, camping, and hunting — each represented small but measurable fractions of visitation (typically <10% combined in most years), with camping notably increasing from 1.7% (1959–60) to 5.8% (1961).
- **Statistical precision**: Monthly estimates of vehicle entries could deviate from true values by up to 20% (1-in-20 confidence), while 12-month aggregate estimates were accurate to within 5% at 95% confidence, reflecting sound survey design for [[Recreation Use Monitoring]] methodology.
- **Methodological caveat**: The definition of "serious angler" used in interviews likely **overestimated the true proportion of angling-motivated visits**, as any party containing one committed angler was classified as an angling party regardless of whether other members were sightseeing or picnicking.
- This report explicitly does **not** include fish harvest or catch data, which were published separately, limiting direct linkage to [[Fish Population Dynamics]] or [[Catch Per Unit Effort]] analyses for Pena Blanca Lake.
