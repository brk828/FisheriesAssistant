# A New Cyprinid Fish from Southern Arizona and Sonora, Mexico, with the Description of a New Subgenus of Gila and a Review of Related Species

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1945 taxonomic paper by Robert R. Miller, published in *Copeia*, revises the North American cyprinid genera once assigned to *Gila*, *Siboma*, *Tigoma*, *Cheonda*, and *Richardsonius*. It retains only [[Richardsonius]] and [[Gila]] at full generic rank and arranges the species of *Gila* into subgenera. Miller proposes one new subgenus, [[Klamathella]], and describes one new species, [[Gila Ditaenia]], from the [[Rio Magdalena]] and [[Rio Altar]] of Sonora, Mexico, and southern Arizona. Several earlier names are placed in synonymy.

## Background & Description

**Generic revision.** The paper examines scale structure and pharyngeal bone shape, which are treated as key diagnostic features. Miller reviews type specimens and concludes:
- [[Cheonda]] *cooperi* is probably an intergeneric hybrid between *Mylocheilus* and *Richardsonius* (following Schultz and Schaefer, 1936).
- *Tigoma humboldti* is a synonym of [[Richardsonius Balteatus]]. Its type was not collected in the Humboldt River, Nevada, which lies in the Lahontan basin inhabited by *Richardsonius egregius*.
- [[Richardsonius]] is restricted to *R. balteatus* and *R. egregius*, and is regarded as valid.
- *Laaimia conformis* is a large-scaled variant of [[Gila Crassicauda]].

**Subgenera of Gila.** Miller lists the species currently referred to *Gila*, grouped into subgenera. He stresses that these groupings are tentative and may change with new material.

| Subgenus | Key diagnostic traits | Representative species |
|---|---|---|
| [[Gila]] (nominate) | Small scales, usually >65 in lateral line; dorsal origin behind pelvics | [[Gila Robusta]], *G. elegans*, *G. nigrescens* |
| [[Siboma]] | Large scales, usually <65 in lateral line; dorsal origin over pelvics | *G. crassicauda*, *G. atraria*, *G. parva* |
| [[Temeculina]] | Moderate to small scales, 45–75 in lateral line, strong radii on all fields | [[Gila Orcuttii]], [[Gila Purpurea]] |
| [[Klamathella]] (new) | Small scales, shield-shaped at base, 60–70 in lateral line | *G. bicolor* |

Other taxonomic notes include *G. nigrescens* and *Tigoma* (noted as a synonym of *G. nigrescens* in one footnote, with the Rio Grande species possibly distinct) and *G. purpurea* possibly being a subspecies of *G. atraria*.

**Description of [[Gila Ditaenia]] (new species).** Miller describes this fine-scaled *Gila* with 63 to 75 scales in the lateral line, scales with prominent radii on all fields, an inferior mouth, 8 anal rays, and typically two prominent dark lateral bands on each side (hence *ditaenia*). The holotype is a breeding male, 64 mm standard length, collected by Ralph G. Miller on March 9, 1940, in the Rio Magdalena, 500 yards west of La Casita, Sonora (USNM No. 129954).

Key morphological and meristic data:
- **Paratypes:** 476 specimens in total (172 yearling to adult; 9 young; 40 yearling to adult; 14 young to adult; 241 young to adult), from the Rio Magdalena, Imuris, Sycamore (Bear) Canyon, and a spring/tributary in Santa Cruz and Cochise County areas, Arizona.
- **Pharyngeal arch:** Similar to that of *G. orcuttii*. The teeth are hooked, and the dental formula is almost always 2,5-4,2 (30 individuals examined). Two variants were noted: 2,5-4,3 and 1,5-4,2.
- **Scale radii:** 33 to 51 in total (usually 37 to 47) on 21 specimens from the type locality.
- **Gill rakers:** Short and few, numbering 6 to 10 (average 8.0), typical of the genus.
- **Fin rays:** Dorsal 8 (rarely 9); anal 8 (155 specimens counted); pectorals 15–17; pelvics invariably 8.
- **Lateral line counts:** 63–75 scales (usually 65–72); dorsal to lateral line 15–18 (usually 16); anal to lateral line 11–14 (usually 12); pelvic to lateral line 9–11.
- **Morphometrics:** Miller gives a table of 21 measurements for 10 males and 10 females, including standard length (male range 487–579 mm... as recorded in the table, with the source text partly garbled by OCR) and proportional body measurements.

**Coloration.** Breeding fish show bright "Chinese red" in the axils of the pectoral and pelvic fins and at the base of the anal fin, extending about two-thirds of the fin lengths. Other red patches occur at the shoulder and mouth corners. Smaller to medium adults have two prominent black lateral bands. The general body tone is olivaceous to purplish or nearly black above, with a white belly.

**Comparisons.** *G. ditaenia* differs from *G. orcuttii* and *G. purpurea* in its much finer scales (63–75 vs. 48–62 in lateral line) and in its bright breeding coloration. It differs from [[Gila Robusta Intermedia]] in its nearly horizontal (not oblique) mouth and stronger scale radii.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Type locality habitat.** The collector recorded the Rio Magdalena near La Casita as a stream bed 75 to 100 yards wide. On March 9, 1940, the clear stream was 4 to 5 feet wide and about 1 foot deep, with a fairly swift current over sand and gravel. The main vegetation was watercress in backwaters. Air temperature was 66.4°F and water temperature 62.4°F at 3:30 p.m. Most specimens came from a 3–4 foot deep pool formed by the roots of a fallen tree.

**Associated fauna.** Other species seined at this locality were the minnow [[Agosia Chrysogaster]], which was abundant, and a few individuals of the cyprinodont [[Poeciliopsis Occidentalis]].

**Distribution.** *G. ditaenia* is known only from the Rio Altar and Rio Magdalena, which unite to form the Rio de la Concepcion (also called Rio Asuncion). In flood, this river enters the [[Gulf of California]] about 160 miles south of the mouth of the Colorado River. Although Ralph G. Miller collected widely in coastal Sonora and Sinaloa, the species was not found elsewhere in that area. Miller concludes it is probably confined to this single drainage, and it appears to be the only *Gila* in that basin.

**Historical record.** Snyder (1915) provisionally identified the Bear Creek specimens as *Richardsonius gibbosus*, now regarded as a synonym of *Gila robusta intermedia*.

**Range notes for related taxa.** Miller lists the ranges of related *Gila* taxa, including *G. robusta* (Colorado River system and the White River in Nevada), *G. elegans* (California, Arizona, Nevada, and other western states), *G. crassicauda* (lower Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers, and Clear Lake), *G. bicolor* (Klamath River system, southern Oregon and northern California), and *G. orcuttii* (coastal southern California).

## Key Findings

- **Generic revision:** Only *Richardsonius* and *Gila* are retained at full generic rank. *Cheonda cooperi* is interpreted as an intergeneric hybrid.
- **New subgenus:** [[Klamathella]] is proposed for *Gila bicolor*, named after the Klamath River.
- **New species:** [[Gila Ditaenia]] is described from 476 paratypes plus the holotype, and is distinguished by fine scales (63–75 in lateral line), strong scale radii, an inferior mouth, and two black lateral bands.
- **Synonymies:** *Tigoma humboldti* is placed under *Richardsonius balteatus*. *Laaimia conformis* is placed under *Gila crassicauda*. *Snyder's* identification of *Richardsonius gibbosus* is reassigned to *G. robusta intermedia*.
- **Endemism:** *G. ditaenia* is restricted to the Rio Altar–Rio Magdalena system (Rio de la Concepcion), where no other *Gila* species occurs.
- **Status of classification:** Miller explicitly calls the species lists and subgeneric groupings tentative and subject to revision as more material becomes available.
