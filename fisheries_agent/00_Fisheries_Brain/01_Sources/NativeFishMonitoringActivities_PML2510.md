# Native Fish Monitoring Activities in the Colorado River within Grand Canyon during 2001

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This draft final report, prepared by [[SWCA Inc]] for the [[Grand Canyon Monitoring and Research Center]] (GCMRC), estimates the abundance of [[Humpback Chub]] (HBC, *Gila cypha*) in the mainstem [[Colorado River]] adjacent to the [[Little Colorado River]] (LCR) inflow during summer 2001. The largest HBC aggregation on the mainstem lies between RM 56.3 and RM 68.3. A two-pass [[Mark-Recapture]] design using [[Trammel Net]] and [[Hoop Net]] sampling yielded an estimate of 1,044 HBC greater than 200 mm TL (95% confidence interval 559 to 2,137; coefficient of variation 29%). Captures and recaptures were fewer than expected and precision was below target. The 2001 estimate was significantly lower than the 1991-1993 estimates (about 3,500), supporting a reported decline in the population.

## Background & Description

Humpback Chub are endangered cyprinids endemic to the Colorado River system. Research and management in Grand Canyon are administered by GCMRC. Stock assessment models using historic catch data indicated a population decline since the early 1990s, but no rigorous mainstem estimates had been made since 1996. The study was a cooperative effort among GCMRC, SWCA, the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] (FWS), and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] (AGFD).

The study had four objectives:
1. Assess the potential and effort required for two-pass mark-recapture mainstem abundance estimates.
2. Capture HBC greater than 100 mm TL to increase mark rates and support stock synthesis modeling.
3. Assess changes in mark rates and length frequencies over time to evaluate recruitment.
4. Assess predation by large non-native fish through stomach content analysis.

Objectives 1, 2, and 4 are addressed in this report; Objective 3 was deferred to a separate report with the FWS.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study area ran from RM 56.3 (Kwagunt Rapid, above the LCR) to RM 68.3 (Tanner Rapid, below the LCR), which delineate the LCR/mainstem aggregation. Sampling occurred in late July to early August and late August to early September 2001, when exchange between the LCR and mainstem was thought to be minimal. Monsoonal floods during the second event flushed smaller fish out of the LCR and increased the catch of HBC under 200 mm.

Other species captured included [[Flannelmouth Sucker]] (FMS), [[Brown Trout]] (BNT), [[Rainbow Trout]] (RBT), bluehead sucker, channel catfish, common carp, fathead minnow, red shiner, speckled dace, and black bullhead. Ten species were captured on Trip 1 and eleven on Trip 2.

Predation work focused on large brown and rainbow trout. Stomach contents were largely too digested for species-level identification, and only one HBC (121 mm, PIT-tagged the day before at the same site) was confirmed as prey.

## Key Findings

**Sampling effort and captures**
- Total effort: 264 person-days, 605 hoop net sets (13,524.5 sampling hours), and 1,102 trammel net sets (7,976.2 sampling hours); 859 samples on Trip 1 and 852 on Trip 2.
- Trammel net CPE was 9.8 (Trip 1) and 13.6 (Trip 2) per 100 hours per 100 ft, compared with a historic average of 41.2.
- 425 HBC were captured in total (121 on Trip 1; 304 on Trip 2 including 5 recaptured on Trip 2 and 2 escapes).

**Population estimate (Chapman-Petersen)**
- Marks: 104 HBC greater than 100 mm on Trip 1; 83 marked HBC greater than 200 mm were examined on Trip 2 with 8 recaptures.
- Estimate for HBC greater than 200 mm: N = 1,044 (95% CI 559-2,137; CV 29%). The target CV was 10-15%.
- Estimate for HBC greater than 250 mm: N = 1,190 (95% CI 561-2,747; CV 35%).
- The Chapman-Petersen estimate for HBC over 200 mm was considered conservative and preferred.
- Only 8% of the estimated population was marked on the first trip, against a target of 10-20%. The expected number of first-pass captures was 350.

**Bias and assumptions**
- No handling mortality, trap-happiness, or trap-shyness was detected. Trap-shyness could not be ruled out but was considered minimal.
- Length distributions differed between events, and all recaptures were 205 mm or larger. The estimate was therefore restricted to fish greater than 200 mm.
- Recaptured fish moved 0.1 to 3.1 miles (mean 0.75 mile), supporting adequate mixing across the reach.

**Flannelmouth sucker**
- Estimates were made for FMS greater than 200 mm (N = 2,052), 350 mm, and 430 mm (N = 454; CV 22%). These estimates are likely biased high, since closure was probably violated because FMS move throughout Grand Canyon.

**Trend**
- The 2001 estimate (1,044) was significantly lower than the 1991-1993 estimates (about 3,500, Valdez and Ryel 1995). The authors concluded this supports the FWS model of declining recruitment and abundance.

**Mark rates**
- Historical mark rate (fish greater than 250 mm): 83.4%; greater than 350 mm: 90%.
- The FWS LCR mark rate was 46%, while LCR-tagged fish made up only 8.6% of mainstem fish greater than 150 mm (18 of 210). The discrepancy was unresolved and deferred to a separate report.

**Predation**
- Of 11 BNT on Trip 1, 6 were sacrificed; 3 contained fish or fish bones.
- Of 27 BNT on Trip 2, 23 were examined: 17 were empty and 6 contained fish. One contained a HBC (121 mm).
- Two RBT (412 and 307 mm) appeared to contain fish.

**Recommendations and management implications**
- The authors recommend increasing initial effort to improve precision.
- Tagging fish under 150 mm should be discontinued, and fin clips should be used for estimates of 100-200 mm fish.
- Proportional mark-rate methods are likely too biased for routine monitoring, so further independent mainstem estimates are needed.

**Peer review (GCMRC external review, 31 May 2002)**
- The reviewer considered the report well written but criticized the lack of historical context, the use of a different estimator from earlier studies (Chapman-Petersen versus Chao Mh), the absence of explicit conclusions, and the lack of management applications.
- The reviewer suggested the report could be developed into a peer-reviewed publication.
