# RE: Desert pupfish inhabiting cheetah moat at Phoenix Zoo

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinodontidae #desert-pupfish #conservation

## Executive Summary
This document is a brief institutional correspondence (dated April 19, 1995) from Arizona State University to the Phoenix Zoo's General Curator, confirming a field sampling event conducted on April 5, 1995. Researchers Brian Bagley and Patti Sowka seined the moat areas surrounding both cheetah enclosures at the Phoenix Zoo. The survey confirmed the presence of a substantial population of [[Desert Pupfish]] (*Cyprinodon macularius*), a federally listed endangered species, thriving in this artificial, non-native aquatic habitat. No other fish species were captured, though [[Bullfrog]] tadpoles (*Lithobates catesbeianus*, likely an invasive/non-native species in this context) were noted as abundant in both reaches of the moat.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat Type:** Artificial zoo enclosure moat, functioning as a refuge/surrogate habitat for a desert fish species.
- **Species Composition:** Monotypic fish assemblage—exclusively [[Desert Pupfish]] were recovered from the seine samples in both cheetah enclosure moats.
- **Co-occurring Taxa:** [[Bullfrog]] tadpoles were present in high abundance alongside the pupfish in both moat reaches, suggesting a shared aquatic microhabitat despite bullfrogs being a known predator/competitor risk to native fish elsewhere in their range.
- **Conservation Context:** The use of zoo infrastructure (a cheetah moat) as an unintentional or intentional refugium for an endangered desert fish highlights an important conservation strategy for [[Cyprinodon macularius]], a species whose native habitat (desert springs and rivers of the Sonoran Desert) has been severely reduced by human activity.

## Key Findings
- **Confirmed Presence:** *Cyprinodon macularius* (Desert Pupfish) was verified as present and abundant in the Phoenix Zoo cheetah moat as of April 5, 1995.
- **Population Status:** Individuals were found in "substantial numbers" in both cheetah enclosure moats, indicating a self-sustaining or thriving population within this artificial system.
- **Species Purity:** Seining recovered only pupfish—no evidence of competing or hybridizing fish species was found in the sampled reaches.
- **Co-inhabitant Species:** Bullfrog tadpoles were also abundant, warranting further monitoring given bullfrogs' reputation as predators of small native fish and amphibians in the Southwestern U.S.
- **Institutional Collaboration:** The survey reflects an ongoing partnership between [[Arizona State University]] (Department of Zoology) and the [[Phoenix Zoo]] for monitoring and potentially conserving this [[Endangered Species Act|federally endangered]] fish, with copies routed to noted desert fish biologists W.L. Minckley and P. Marsh.
