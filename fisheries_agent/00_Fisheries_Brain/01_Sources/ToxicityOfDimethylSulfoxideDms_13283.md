# Toxicity of Dimethyl Sulfoxide (DMSO) to Fish

## Executive Summary

This 1967 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service study by Wayne A. Willford evaluated the acute toxicity of [[Dimethyl Sulfoxide]] (DMSO) to nine species of freshwater fish using static bioassays at 12°C over 24-, 48-, and 96-hour exposure periods. DMSO, a hygroscopic organic sulfoxide solvent with broad biomedical and agricultural applications, was investigated as a potential nontoxic solvent carrier for fish control chemicals and disease treatments. Results showed DMSO exhibits toxicity of very low order (~30 p.p.t.) across all species tested, with minimal interspecies variation and a strikingly narrow, nonselective toxicity threshold. Water hardness had negligible effect on toxicity, while increasing temperature substantially increased toxicity in [[Rainbow Trout]]. A preliminary co-toxicity trial found no significant interaction between DMSO and [[Antimycin]] toxicity in [[Bluegill]] at 96 hours, supporting DMSO's potential utility as an inert solvent vehicle in fisheries chemical research.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat context:** All species are freshwater fish native to North American lakes, rivers, and hatchery systems; tested in reconstituted deionized water under controlled laboratory water-quality regimes (soft, medium, hard — categorized by CaSO₄, MgSO₄, and KCl concentrations).
- **Trophic/taxonomic breadth:** The study spans multiple trophic guilds and families — coldwater salmonids ([[Rainbow Trout]], [[Brook Trout]], [[Lake Trout]]), benthic omnivores ([[Carp]], [[Black Bullhead]], [[Channel Catfish]]), and warmwater centrarchids/percids ([[Green Sunfish]], [[Bluegill]], [[Yellow Perch]]) — indicating an intentionally broad test of nonselective toxicant effects across taxa relevant to [[Fish Control]] management.
- **Environmental variables tested:** Water hardness (soft/medium/hard) and temperature (7°, 12°, 17°C) were manipulated to simulate variable field conditions encountered in fisheries management and pesticide/piscicide application.
- **Applied ecological relevance:** DMSO's near-universal miscibility with water and low intrinsic toxicity positioned it as a candidate solvent for delivering [[Piscicides]] (e.g., [[Antimycin]]) or therapeutic compounds to fish populations without inducing solvent-driven mortality, relevant to selective fish population control and fish disease treatment protocols.

## Key Findings

1. **Low-order toxicity across species:** DMSO's 96-hour LC50 values ranged narrowly from 32.3 p.p.t. (rainbow trout) to 43.0 p.p.t. (green sunfish) — indicating consistent, nonselective toxicity across all nine species.
2. **Steep dose-response curve:** Probit slope functions ranged from 1.06–1.20 (mean 1.11), indicating an abrupt transition from 0% to 100% mortality within a narrow concentration range — a hallmark of nonselective toxicants.
3. **Inconsistent species sensitivity ranking over time:** Susceptibility order shifted across observation periods (e.g., channel catfish most sensitive at 24–48h; rainbow trout most sensitive at 96h), reflecting the compound's broadly nonselective mode of action.
4. **Cross-study corroboration:** Independent findings from the Western Fish Nutrition Laboratory (coho salmon TLm: 72/55/46 p.p.t. at 24/48/96h) and Ball (1966) (goldfish 48h LC50 = 43 p.p.t. at 15°C) align closely with this study's results, reinforcing DMSO's consistent low toxicity profile.
5. **Water hardness has minimal effect:** LC50 confidence intervals overlapped across soft, medium, and hard water in nearly all cases (12°C), except hard water at 96 hours, where DMSO was slightly less toxic.
6. **Temperature significantly increases toxicity:** In rainbow trout, raising temperature from 7°C to 17°C increased toxicity by more than 10 p.p.t. across all observation periods — a critical consideration for field application timing.
7. **No significant interaction with antimycin toxicity:** Combining 1.0 p.p.t. DMSO with [[Antimycin]] produced a 96-hour LC50 of 0.084 p.p.b. versus 0.089 p.p.b. for antimycin/acetone alone — a difference attributed to normal biological variation, indicating DMSO does not potentiate antimycin toxicity to bluegill at this exposure duration.
8. **Comparison to other solvents:** Ball (1966) found DMSO did not significantly differ from acetone in affecting goldfish survival time when combined with DDT, suggesting DMSO may be a viable, potentially superior alternative solvent for pesticide toxicity studies.
9. **No enhanced dye uptake:** Rabinowitz and Myerson (1966) found no significant DMSO-mediated enhancement of radioactive dye uptake in aquarium fish, though osmotic stress in distilled water media may have influenced their higher toxicity estimates (LD50 ~19 p.p.t.) for ornamental species (neon tetras, platys, mollies, guppies).
10. **Practical implication:** Despite its potential as a membrane-penetrant carrier in mammalian pharmacology, DMSO showed no clear evidence of enhancing chemical absorption in fish under the tested conditions, though the authors note shorter-exposure bioassays might reveal absorption-enhancing effects not detected at 96 hours.
