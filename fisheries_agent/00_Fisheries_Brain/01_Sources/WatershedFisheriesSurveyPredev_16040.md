# Watershed Fisheries Survey: Predevelopment and Postdevelopment Evaluations (Arizona, 1965)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #salmonidae

## Executive Summary

This pair of Arizona Game and Fish Department completion reports (Project F-7-R-8, Work Plans for 1965, by Louis H. Carufel) evaluates two linked programs. Job R-4 is a predevelopment survey of nine proposed lake sites, gathered to estimate the fish production of planned impoundments and to rank candidate sites. Job R-5 is a postdevelopment evaluation of stream improvement structures on nine streams. Both were prepared for the [[Central Arizona Project]] fishing-lake expansion, which proposed 50 fishing lakes. Water chemistry, basin morphometry, temperature, flow, geology, land use and biological condition were examined. The main conclusions are that total alkalinity, nutrients, total soluble salts, shoal area and slope are the most useful production criteria, and that stream structures had limited measurable benefit and frequently failed under flood flows.

## Background & Description

The work sits within the [[Watershed Fisheries Survey]] program, administered through [[Federal Aid in Sport Fish Restoration]] project F-7-R-8. The 1965 completion reports were approved by Wendell G. Swank (Director) and David A. Roe, Jr. (Assistant Federal Aid Coordinator).

**Job R-4 (Predevelopment Surveys)** covered:
- Nine proposed lake sites: [[Bear Canyon Lake]], [[Black Canyon Lake]], [[Blue Ridge Reservoir]], [[Chevelon Canyon Lake]], [[Knoll Lake]], [[Lynx Lake]], [[Parker Canyon Lake]], [[Willow Springs Lake]] and [[Woods Canyon Lake]].
- Comparative data from nine established lakes, including [[Ashurst Lake]], [[Big Lake]], [[Luna Lake]], [[Lyman Lake]], [[Pena Blanca Lake]], [[Rainbow Lake]] and [[Show Low Lake]].
- Fourteen streams, including [[East Verde River]], [[Horton Creek]], [[Little Colorado River]], [[North Canyon Creek]] and [[Willow Creek]].

**Job R-5 (Postdevelopment Evaluations)** covered nine improved streams: [[Ash Creek]], [[Barbershop Creek]], [[Christopher Creek]], [[Eagle Creek]], [[Fish Creek]], [[Haigler Creek]], [[K. P. Creek]], [[South Fork of the Little Colorado River]] and [[Tonto Creek]].

**Standard methods** included the Hach D.R. colorimeter for water chemistry, Ryan recording thermometers (33-day) for temperature, electrofishing for population and carry-over, and soil analysis by the University of Arizona soils laboratory. Growth and condition factors were used as biological indicators.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Regional climate:** The northern region (Mogollon Rim) receives winter frontal snow plus summer convectional rain and its streams discharge mainly in spring. The southern region (Parker Canyon) receives mainly summer convectional storms and its streams discharge mainly in summer.
- **Geology:** Limestone and sandstone (Kaibab limestone, Coconino sandstone) on the Rim generally gave higher alkalinity than igneous or metamorphic watersheds. Lynx Lake was on schist (metamorphic); Parker Canyon had andesite and sedimentary deposits.
- **Base flow vs. flood flow:** Base flow (groundwater) was identified as the main source of basic minerals to lakes. Durum (1953) reported run-off made up 65 percent of stream discharge but only 24 percent of dissolved solids.
- **Land use:** Timber harvest and grazing were the predominant uses. Logging in the Fish Creek drainage caused siltation, with turbidity of 50 to 1,100 p.p.m. recorded.
- **Beaver:** Beaver dams on the South Fork of the Little Colorado (structures #45 to #50) raised surface temperatures to 64.0 to 68.0°F, against 50.0 to 60.0°F elsewhere on the stream.
- **Watershed condition:** Flood flows, poor ground cover and human activity have reduced fish habitat across the study streams.

## Key Findings

**Predevelopment (Job R-4)**

- **Water quality criteria:** Lakes with total alkalinity of 45 p.p.m. and higher, phosphate and nitrogen above 0.05 p.p.m., and total soluble salts of 100 p.p.m. and higher are expected to produce better. Of the nine proposed lakes, five had alkalinity of 50 to 100 p.p.m., and four had 10 to 40 p.p.m.
- **Morphometry:** Shoal area and slope predicted production better than shoreline development, which showed no correlation with production. Suggested shoal area for proposed lakes is 30 percent or greater at the 15- and 20-foot contours.
- **Mean depth:** Mean depths over 25 feet were linked to reduced shoal area and steep drainages.
- **Lake size:** Lakes of 135 to 550 surface acres in shallow basins suit the northern region. Lakes of 50 to 105 acres in moderately sloped basins suit southeastern Arizona.
- **Thermal stratification:** Thermoclines in August 1965 lay at 8 to 24 ft (Bear Canyon), 7 to 18 ft (Black Canyon), 10 to 21 ft (Blue Ridge) and 10 to 20 ft (Knoll Lake). Blue Ridge drawdown for over-the-Rim diversion was expected to restrict thermocline formation and food organisms.
- **Growing season:** Sites with a longer growing season (temperature of 32.0°F and above) were expected to produce more readily.
- **Toxicity:** Lynx Lake plankton was low (0.3 cc), probably due to copper at 0.85 p.p.m. Elements such as copper at 0.1 to 0.5 p.p.m. are toxic to micro-organisms (McKee and Wolf, 1963).
- **Biological data:** Insufficient to estimate production on proposed sites. Grayling in Bear Canyon Lake grew from 0.5 inch to 6.0 inches in 115 days, with estimated annual production of about 30 lb/acre (assuming no mortality). Parker Canyon production was estimated at 100 lb/acre (Glucksman, 1965), and Pena Blanca at 138 lb/acre (McConnell, 1963).
- **Stream recommendations:** Of 14 streams, seven were proposed for rainbow trout, three for native trout, one for brook trout, and three await more data. Over-the-Rim diversion may make the East Verde River more favorable for trout.
- **Recommendations:** Continue predevelopment surveys and use temperature monitoring to test stream feasibility.

**Postdevelopment (Job R-5)**

- **No measurable change:** Velocity, volume, temperature, biota and water quality showed no significant change from 1964 to 1965. A total of 160 water quality determinations found no significant changes.
- **Trout carry-over:** Marked rainbow trout carry-over was 1.12 percent (Haigler Creek) and 3.2 percent (Christopher Creek, one year). Marked brown trout carry-over was 8.8 percent in Christopher Creek.
- **Habitat use:** Small trout (2.0 to 5.0 inches) used natural low-water areas for escape cover, while large trout (6.0 inches and up) used deep natural and artificial pools.
- **Structure performance:**
  - Rock dams (Ash Creek) washed out and were judged unsuitable.
  - Trash catchers (Barbershop, Eagle, Haigler, Tonto) were damaged by floods, with silt filling pools.
  - Log dams deteriorated within about two years.
  - Log-plank dams created the best pools and flow.
  - Gabions (Haigler Creek) held up well in floods.
  - Log deflectors were suggested for future work.
- **Failure of improvements:** Log dams on K. P. Creek were judged non-functional, and no trout were observed there. Fish Creek structures were largely silted in.
- **Stream volume:** Log-plank dams, gabions and some trash catchers increased volume on five of nine streams.
- **Movement:** Trout moved downstream from improved sections, and no marked fish were recovered above the last upstream structure.
- **Angling:** Carufel (1964) found no change in catchable trout angling in improved areas, while Tarzwell and Shetter et al. (Michigan) reported a 120 percent increase in catch.

**Overall conclusions**

- Watershed cover and hydrological surveys should precede stream improvement.
- Stream improvement should not be done where logging is active until cover has recovered.
- Shoreline and stream bed cover are needed on several streams.
- Beaver effects are case-specific and were not yet evaluated on the South Fork.
