# Lake Mead Fisheries Program: 1980 Job Progress Report

#ichthyology #marine-biology #centrarchidae

## Executive Summary

This 1980 Nevada Department of Wildlife job progress report covers the statewide fisheries project for [[Lake Mead]], Clark County. It summarizes seven jobs: salmonid stocking, creel census, [[Largemouth Bass]] life history and tagging, food habits of fry and fingerling bass, [[Striped Bass]] investigations, shoreline vegetation and cover studies, and [[Channel Catfish]] life history. An 8,738-angler creel census over 413 survey days recorded 1.21 fish per angler and 0.25 fish per angler-hour, both lower than 1979. Largemouth bass catch rates rose, while striped bass, now the dominant catch species, declined in 1980 after several years of growth. The report also recommends curtailing salmonid stocking and raising the black crappie limit.

## Background & Description

Lake Mead has been managed as a warm-water fishery since its formation in 1935. Upstream impoundments, including [[Glen Canyon Dam]] (completed 1964, creating [[Lake Powell]]), altered water-level and temperature regimes and reduced game fish populations. Current research aims to support management of [[Largemouth Bass]], [[Striped Bass]], and stocked salmonids through the Lake Mead Fish Hatchery, using a plant barge for mid-lake stocking.

Seven jobs were active in 1980:
- **304-1:** Marked salmonid stocking (fin clips and dye marks)
- **304-2:** Sport fishery evaluation (creel survey)
- **304-3:** Largemouth bass life history (tagging, underwater surveys)
- **304-4:** Food habits of fry and fingerling bass (with [[University Of Nevada, Las Vegas]])
- **304-5:** Striped bass investigations
- **304-6:** Shoreline vegetation study
- **304-7:** Channel catfish life history (inactive in 1980)

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Water levels.** Lake level fluctuated 7.08 ft (2.16 m) in 1980, with a loss of 2.94 ft (0.90 m) during the largemouth bass spawning season (March to June). Maximum surface elevation was the highest recorded in many years. High levels flooded new vegetation and produced a "new lake" effect.

**Cover and vegetation.** Dominant submergent plants were [[Spiny Naiad]] (*Najas marina*), with pondweeds (*Potamogeton* spp.) and hard-stem bulrush (*Scirpus acutus*) in Swallow Bay. Tamarisk (*Tamarix pentandra*) and inundated [[Brittlebush]] provided the best cover for fry and fingerlings. Total cover was estimated for [[Burro Point]], [[Little Gyp Beds]], [[Quail Bay]], and a Lake Mohave cove. Cover for the three Lake Mead coves was predicted to peak near 1,204 ft (367 m) elevation.

**Zooplankton and food webs.** Bass fry relied almost entirely on crustacean zooplankton, especially copepods (*Diaptomus* spp.) and cladocerans. Fingerlings shifted from zooplankton to insects and fish as they grew. Zooplankton densities in Lake Mead were reported as having declined ten-fold since 1970 (unpublished data cited in the food habits report).

**Water quality.** Temperature and dissolved oxygen profiles for the Boulder Basin were obtained from the southern Nevada water system (figures only).

## Key Findings

### Creel survey and angler success
- 8,738 anglers were contacted during 413 creel days at six Nevada landings.
- Catch rate: 1.21 fish per angler (1979: 1.87); 0.25 fish per angler-hour (1979: 0.33).
- Decline attributed partly to increased survey effort in the Boulder Basin, which lessens the influence of high-catch Overton Boat Dock data.
- Nevada anglers made up 87.0% of users in 1980 (figures varied across years); Utah anglers returned in larger numbers because of the striped bass fishery.

### Species composition of catch (1980)
- [[Striped Bass]]: 36.4% of catch (1979: 40.1%), remaining the top species.
- [[Largemouth Bass]]: 32.1% (1979: 24.8%), showing some recovery.
- [[Black Crappie]]: 9.8% of catch.
- Channel catfish, rainbow trout, bluegill, and others made up the remainder.
- Angler effort: striped bass 62.3% of lake-wide effort; largemouth bass 6.3%; salmonids 0.6%.

### Salmonid stocking
- 28,216 salmonids were stocked from Lake Mead Hatchery: 10,491 rainbow trout and 17,725 brown trout.
- Rainbow trout plants fell 68.4% from 1979. All were released from shore at Hemenway Harbor in January and March.
- 73 rainbow trout were censused in creels, averaging 11.02 inches (27.99 cm).
- No Lahontan cutthroat trout were returned. One brown trout return (11.0 in, 27.94 cm) was found at Lake Mead Marina about a month after its March 1980 release.
- The authors cite poor survival, possible limnological change, heavy initial angler removal, emigration to the main Colorado River, and striped bass predation.

### Largemouth bass
- Catch rate index: 1.59 fish per angler; 0.21 fish per hour, an upturn from 1979.
- 13,544 bass were released from [[San Marcos National Fish Hatchery]] and [[Gavins Point National Fish Hatchery]].
- Tag returns: 34.9% overall (618 returns from 1,769 tagged wild bass), with 16.2% for wild bass from other sources and 5.4% for hatchery bass.
- Underwater surveys recorded 1,321,766 largemouth bass from March through October; spawning effort was rated fair and peaked in mid-April.
- Nest success was 64.7% in marked nests (11 of 17 successful).
- Fry and fingerling survival was rated good, attributed to the highest water levels since the initial filling of Lake Mead.
- Class 0 bass were 70% or more of relative abundance from June to October.
- A 1980 fingerling release of 9,434 fin-clipped bass from Gavins Point used liquid nitrogen cauterization to prevent fin regeneration. Two other shipments were lost to 100% mortality.
- The report recommends stocking largemouth bass at 5 inches rather than 3 inches where possible.

### Striped bass
- Stocking began in September 1969. From 1969 to 1972, about 60,300 fingerlings and 3,907 yearlings were introduced.
- 3,849 striped bass were censused in creels, with mean fork length of 18.67 in (47.42 cm), down from 21.47 in (54.53 cm) in 1979, reflecting dominance of naturally reproduced fish.
- Expanded harvest was estimated at 300,000 to 400,000 fish in 1980, down from 445,174 in 1979.
- Low summer catch was tied to smaller fish, poor weather, and poor condition (K factor) for much of the year.
- 285 striped bass were tagged; 28 returns (9.8%) were recorded, with some fish moving up to 35 miles (56.3 km).
- Natural reproduction was documented for the eighth consecutive year.

### Channel catfish and other notes
- Job 304-7 was inactive in 1980 due to problems with prepared samples.
- Black crappie remain high on the species list, mostly at Overton, where indiscriminate anglers using live bait dominate.

### Recommendations
- Curtail salmonid stocking unless a different species is obtained; keep the daily and possession limit at five.
- Raise the black crappie daily and possession limit to 25.
- Keep the striped bass limit at five fish with no size restriction, and continue growth, migration, and food habit studies.
- Continue creel surveys at the highest possible level and increase data from Temple Bar and the upper lake, with Arizona Game and Fish help.
- Discontinue the total cover study as its data are largely repetitive.
- Stock 125,000 federal-facility largemouth bass and an additional 2,000 wild-source bass.
- Continue monitoring tournaments and consider restricting them during extreme temperatures or rough water.
- Study genetic variation in naturally produced and stocked fish using electrofishing samples.
- Continue the fry and fingerling food habits work under contract.
