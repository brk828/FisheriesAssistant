# The Spiny-rayed Cyprinid Fishes (Plagopterini) of the Colorado River System

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1960 revision by [[Robert Rush Miller]] and [[Carl L. Hubbs]], published as Miscellaneous Publication No. 115 of the Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan, treats the endemic spiny-rayed cyprinid tribe Plagopterini of the [[Colorado River System]]. The tribe comprises three genera: [[Lepidomeda]] (polytypic), [[Meda]] (monotypic) and [[Plagopterus]] (monotypic). The authors describe three new species and one new subspecies, synonymize *L. jarrovii* with *L. vittata*, and report that two localized forms, *L. m. pratensis* and *L. altivelis*, had become extinct by 1959. Introduced fishes ([[Gambusia Affinis]], [[Common Carp]]) and habitat deterioration are identified as the main threats.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic status:** Cope (1874) erected Plagopterinae; Jordan, Evermann and Clark (1930) elevated the group to family Medidae; Hubbs (1955) reduced it to tribe Plagopterini within Leuciscinae. The authors retain the tribal rank.
- **Diagnostic traits:** Spinelike modification of anterior dorsal rays and of the ventral pelvic demitrich. Members are among the few North American cyprinids not known to hybridize with other genera.
- **Genus differences:**
  - *Lepidomeda* is the least modified genus, retains scales, and resembles other American leuciscines. It is considered the ancestral type.
  - *Meda* is scaleless (or nearly so), with dark speckling and a small body (under 3 inches total length).
  - *Plagopterus* is the most specialized, with a flattened head, a pendant barbel, a nearly horizontal mouth, and a nearly uniform silver body.
- **Species and forms:**
  - *L. mollispinis mollispinis* (holotype UMMZ 141673, 88 mm S.L.; 718 specimens examined)
  - *L. m. pratensis* (holotype UMMZ 124799, 55 mm S.L.; seven types)
  - *L. albivallis* (holotype UMMZ 173781, 69.5 mm S.L.; 1,007 specimens recorded)
  - *L. altivelis* (holotype UMMZ 125004, 56 mm S.L.)
  - *L. vittata* (synonymy of *L. jarrovii*; largest specimen 84 mm S.L.)
  - *Meda fulgida* (largest specimen 60 mm S.L.)
  - *Plagopterus argentissimus* (largest specimen 71 mm S.L.)
- **Analytical key:** The authors provide a differential key to the seven forms based on fin-ray counts, dental formula, scale counts, vertebral number, mouth obliquity, head shape and pigmentation.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Distribution:** The tribe is confined to the lower and middle Colorado River system, including remnants of Pluvial (Pleistocene) tributaries in southeastern Nevada.
  - *Meda* occurs in the Gila River system of Arizona and New Mexico, and its range is contracting.
  - *Plagopterus* is restricted to the Virgin River system (Utah, Arizona, Nevada). It formerly occurred in the lower Gila River, from Tempe to Yuma.
  - *Lepidomeda* is found in isolated springs of the White River system, a spring marsh on Meadow Valley Wash, the Virgin River system, and the upper Little Colorado River.
- **Habitat preferences:**
  - *L. m. mollispinis* inhabits moderate to swift current, chiefly in pools, over sand and gravel.
  - *L. albivallis* lives in cool springs (65–71°F) and their outflows.
  - *L. altivelis* was found in the swift, clear outflow of Ash Spring (up to 88°F at the head) and in alkaline Upper Pahranagat Lake. It shunned constantly warm springs (79–90°F).
  - *Meda fulgida* favors swift, deep pools and riffle mouths.
  - *Plagopterus argentissimus* frequents swift shallows, including "swift mud" water in the Virgin River.
- **Associated fauna:** Native associates include *Pantosteus*, *Rhinichthys osculus*, *Catostomus latipinnis*, *Crenichthys baileyi* and *Gila robusta*. Introduced species include *Cyprinus carpio*, *Gambusia affinis affinis*, *Micropterus salmoides*, *Lepomis macrochirus*, *Ictalurus melas*, and bullfrogs (*Rana catesbeiana*).
- **Threats:** Stream desiccation, drought, diversion and water storage, introduced predators and competitors, and habitat modification by bullfrogs.

## Key Findings

- **Monophyly and origin:** The tribe is considered to have arisen from a *Gila*-like cyprinid within the Colorado system, probably in the Pliocene. *Lepidomeda* is ancestral, and *Meda* and *Plagopterus* evolved from it.
- **Taxonomic changes:**
  - Three new species (*L. albivallis*, *L. altivelis*, *L. mollispinis*) and one new subspecies (*L. m. pratensis*) are described, with *L. m. mollispinis* as the nominate subspecies.
  - *L. jarrovii* is synonymized with *L. vittata*.
  - The Fremont River record of *L. vittata* is disregarded.
  - *Snyderichthys* is treated as a subgenus only, per Uyeno's osteological study.
- **Vertebral counts (Table 1 averages):** *L. m. mollispinis* 42.73; *L. albivallis* 43.13; *L. altivelis* 43.00; *L. vittata* 41.55; *Meda fulgida* 40.26; *Plagopterus argentissimus* 40.16.
- **Extinctions:** *L. m. pratensis* (Big Spring, near Panaca, Nevada) and *L. altivelis* (Ash Spring and Upper Pahranagat Lake, Nevada) were not found in 1959 field surveys. Their last known collections were in 1938 (and 1891 for Pahranagat Lake). The authors attribute the extirpation mainly to:
  - Water diversion and habitat loss at Big Spring.
  - Increased carp abundance and establishment of mosquitofish at Ash Spring.
- **Threatened status:** All seven forms are considered threatened because of restricted ranges and stream deterioration.
- **Hybridization:** The plagopterins are among the few American cyprinid groups not known to hybridize.
- **Speciation:** The authors note that the differentiation among isolated forms may reflect differential survival of already-differentiated Pluvial populations rather than roughly 11,000 years of isolation since the Pluvial period ended. Environmental induction of the observed differences is considered unlikely.
