## Executive Summary
This document is a 1994 U.S. Bureau of Reclamation memorandum announcing an interagency scoping meeting to discuss the feasibility of constructing a **[[Razorback Sucker]]** rearing facility at Rockhouse Farm, a parcel of land being acquired by Reclamation along the Salt Arm of **[[Roosevelt Lake]]**, Arizona. The proposed project centers on rearing ponds intended to support reintroduction of this federally endangered catostomid into formerly occupied habitat and to help maintain additional genetic stock diversity for the species. The letter itself contains no new morphological, physiological, or ecological data; rather, it documents the administrative and consultative process (agency notification, meeting logistics, and distribution list) preceding a conservation aquaculture project. It is included here as a record of conservation planning history relevant to **[[Razorback Sucker]]** recovery efforts in the **[[Gila River Basin]]**.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Species status:** The razorback sucker is identified explicitly as an **endangered native species**, underscoring its conservation priority under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.
- **Historic range context:** The project is framed around reintroduction into "formerly occupied habitat," implying that the species has experienced range contraction or local extirpation from areas of **[[Roosevelt Lake]]** and the broader **[[Salt River]]** system.
- **Habitat association:** The proposed rearing ponds would be sited on land adjacent to the Salt Arm of Roosevelt Lake (Rockhouse Farm), suggesting a reservoir-margin habitat suitable for pond-based aquaculture and eventual stocking into lentic/reservoir or connected riverine habitat.
- **Genetic conservation role:** The facility is intended not only for reintroduction but also to maintain **additional genetic stock**, indicating a broader ex-situ conservation strategy to preserve genetic diversity within remaining razorback sucker populations, complementing wild population management efforts led by agencies such as **[[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]** and the **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]**.

## Key Findings
- **Purpose of meeting:** A scoping meeting was scheduled for October 19, 1994, at the Tonto National Forest Roosevelt Work Center, to discuss construction of razorback sucker rearing ponds at Rockhouse Farm on the Salt Arm of Roosevelt Lake.
- **Agency involvement:** Initial outreach (August 31, 1994) to multiple agencies and researchers—including **[[W.L. Minckley]]** and **[[Paul Marsh]]** of Arizona State University, the **[[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]]** (Arizona Fisheries Resources Office and Endangered Species Program), the **[[Arizona Game and Fish Department]]**, and the **[[Tonto National Forest]]**—yielded positive responses regarding project feasibility.
- **Meeting agenda:** Planned discussion topics included a detailed project proposal, agency positions, scoping of environmental/biological/public concerns, and formation of a guiding committee for the project.
- **Site visit:** Contingent on permission, a site visit to Rockhouse Farm was planned to give participants firsthand exposure to site conditions relevant to facility siting.
- **Conservation objective:** The overarching goal is twofold — (1) reintroduction of razorback sucker into historically occupied habitat within the Roosevelt Lake/Salt River system, and (2) establishment of a secondary genetic reservoir population to support long-term species recovery.
- **Institutional stakeholders:** Distribution of the memo indicates a broad, multi-agency collaborative framework typical of Endangered Species Act Section 7 consultation and recovery planning processes for the **[[Razorback Sucker]]** in Arizona.
