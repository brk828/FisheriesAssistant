# Poecilia maylandi n. sp.: A New Poeciliid from the Arroyo Chacambero, Guerrero, Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #poeciliidae

## Executive Summary

This 1983 taxonomic paper by [[Manfred K. Meyer]] describes *Poecilia maylandi* n. sp., a freshwater poeciliid from the Arroyo Chacambero near Altamirano, Guerrero, Mexico, a tributary system of the [[Rio Balsas]]. The description is based on a holotype and paratypes (three males examined for gonopodial characters). The species is placed in the [[Poecilia sphenops Complex]] (sensu stricto), closest to [[Poecilia butleri]] and [[Poecilia sphenops]], with the best overall concordance with [[Poecilia mexicana]]. The author also reviews the classification of the genus [[Poecilia]] and argues for retaining the [[Rosen and Bailey Classification]] for the subgenus *Poecilia*.

## Background & Description

**Taxonomic context.** The genus [[Poecilia]] was subdivided into subgenera following [[Rosen and Bailey 1963]] revision of the [[Poeciliidae]]. The subgenus *Poecilia* contained 15 species and one subspecies under the author's scheme, and the new species was added to it. [[Rivas]] (1980) elevated [[Limia]] to generic rank, and [[Miller]] (1973, 1975) proposed the subgenus *Mollinesia*, which the author did not accept.

**Diagnosis.** A slender, long-bodied species with a large, pointed head. Breeding males have an enlarged dorsal fin extending toward the caudal region; both sexes are olive-brown with 5-6 horizontal rows of orange spots. Key diagnostic traits:
- Inner jaw teeth tricuspid, with a elongated median cusp.
- Gonopodium: a small membranous hook on ray 3 and a large retrorse spine on ray 5p.
- Meristics: D 8-9, C 32-36, A 9, V 6, P 14-15; lateral scale count 26; transverse scale series 8-9; 27 vertebrae.

**Type material.** Holotype: male (SMF 17855), Arroyo Chacambero near Altamirano, collected 9 October 1980 by [[E. Hnilicka]]. Paratypes: three females (SMF 17856-17858), two males (SMF 17859-17860), and three juveniles (SMF 17861-17863). The species is named in honour of [[Hans J. Mayland]].

**Morphometrics.** Standard length of the holotype and paratypes is given in Table I (for example, holotype SL 53.80 mm; head length 18.45 mm; eye diameter 4.0 mm). Body depth at the caudal peduncle is 4.50-5.50 times in standard length; the head is 2.70-3.00 times in the head length for the mouth width; the eye is 3.30-3.55 times in head length. The first gill arch bears 16-20 gill rakers.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The Arroyo Chacambero is a slow stream flowing into the Rio Balsas system, with alternating boulders, gravel and sand in an agate-bearing rock region. At the collecting site the stream is 4-5 m wide and split into several channels. In the dry season, sun-heated pools persist and are colonized by filamentous algae, which the author identifies as the staple food of the new species. Mean annual water temperature is about 28 °C. Water is described as having a flat taste, pH 8.0, carbonate hardness 10° and total hardness 15°. Associated fauna includes [[Poeciliopsis balsas]], *Astyanax* sp., an undescribed *Cichlasoma*, and [[Ilyodon lennoni]]. Surrounding land includes thorny scrub, scattered trees, and agricultural plantings (mango, citrus, banana, maize). The upper course of the stream has been little explored.

## Key Findings

- *Poecilia maylandi* is distinguished from all known *Poecilia* by a combination of head shape, dorsal ray count, gonopodial hook and spine, and body proportions.
- It is the only member of the *sphenops* complex with tricuspid teeth in the posterior jaw regions, where the median cusp is strongly elongated.
- The gonopodial spine on ray 5p is well developed; only *Poecilia mexicana* matches its size.
- Among the *sphenops* complex, the new species has the lowest mean gill raker count and the smallest gonopodial hook on ray 3.
- Compared with *Poecilia butleri* (its closest relative), it has tricuspid teeth, 9 anal rays, a larger spine, a smaller hook, and a more slender body.
- Compared with *Poecilia sphenops*, it is larger, has fewer gill rakers, and lacks dark spot rows on the female flank.
- Compared with *Poecilia catemaconis*, it differs in gill rakers, lateral scale count, spine development, male dorsal fin shape and body proportions.
- Overall concordance is greatest with *Poecilia mexicana*, although that species is highly variable, and the new species can still be separated from its mean values.
- The author concludes that current *Poecilia* taxonomy is not definitive, that the position of *Poecilia vivipara* and *Poecilia caucana* remains unexplained, and that further character analyses and crossing experiments are needed.
- The author proposes retaining the [[Rosen and Bailey Classification]] for the subgenus *Poecilia*, as it is currently the most advanced arrangement.
