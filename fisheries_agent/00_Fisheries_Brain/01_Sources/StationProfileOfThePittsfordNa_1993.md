# Station Profile: Pittsford National Fish Hatchery

## Executive Summary
This document is a 1993 annual station profile for the **Pittsford National Fish Hatchery** (est. 1906), operated under U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Region 5 in Pittsford, Vermont. The facility's mission centers on propagating **[[Landlocked Atlantic Salmon]]** (LAS) and **[[Lake Trout]]** (LAT) for the **[[Lake Champlain Restoration Program]]**, and supplying **[[Atlantic Salmon]]** fry to the **[[Connecticut River Program]]**. The report details production output, distribution logistics, infrastructure challenges (well water supply, gravity-fed spring system faults), staff training, community outreach, and comparative funding/personnel data across fiscal years 1989–1993.

## Background & Description
The source is an administrative/operational station profile rather than a morphological study; no anatomical, meristic, or taxonomic descriptive data are provided. Species are referenced only by common abbreviation and management category:
- **LAS** — Landlocked Atlantic Salmon
- **LAT** — Lake Trout
- **ATS** — Atlantic Salmon (anadromous form, distributed as fry)

No length, weight-at-age, coloration, or fin-ray data are given; only aggregate production weights are reported (see Key Findings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Target ecosystems:** [[Lake Champlain]] watershed (primary stocking destination for LAS and LAT) and tributaries of the [[Connecticut River]] (recipient of ATS fry).
- **Restoration context:** Stocking supports the [[Lake Champlain Restoration Program]], indicating historical depletion of native/naturalized salmonid populations requiring artificial propagation support.
- **Water source ecology:** The hatchery relies on two wells (rated 600 gpm and 300 gpm) and a gravity-fed spring system, both experiencing operational deficiencies (suspected faulty valves, air lock), directly constraining fish-rearing capacity and water quality management.
- **Non-target ecological engagement:** Staff assisted [[Lake Champlain Fishery Resources Office|LCFRO]] personnel in electrofishing the Pike River to survey **sea lamprey ammocoetes** (larval [[Sea Lamprey]]), reflecting the hatchery's role in broader watershed invasive-species/parasite monitoring tied to salmonid restoration.
- **Community/conservation interface:** Outreach included fishing derbies for elderly/handicapped/retired populations, school tours (13 groups), and construction of twelve bluebird nesting boxes for International Migratory Bird Day, indicating multi-species stewardship beyond core hatchery operations.

## Key Findings
- **1993 Production:**
  - 250,063 **[[Landlocked Atlantic Salmon]]** (14,569 lbs) distributed to the Lake Champlain watershed.
  - 48,922 **[[Lake Trout]]** (2,752 lbs) distributed to the Lake Champlain watershed.
  - 24,524 **[[Atlantic Salmon]]** fry distributed to Connecticut River tributaries.
- **Distribution by state (FY1993):** Vermont received 102,731 fish; New York received 147,332 fish.
- **Total fish distributed (excluding eggs/fry):**
  - FY93: 298,985
  - FY92: 257,194
  - FY91: 265,425
- **Visitation:** 6,403 total visitors in 1993, a continued decline from historical peaks exceeding 30,000 annually.
- **Infrastructure issues:**
  - Wells underperforming relative to rated capacity (Well #1: rated 600 gpm, actual ~200 gpm; Well #2: rated 300 gpm, actual ~200 gpm).
  - Pump upgrade (7.5 hp → 10 hp) on Well #1 caused pump/control burnout during testing.
  - Dye testing identified three faulty valves in the gravity-fed spring system; suspected air lock also implicated.
  - Downstream retaining wall at the dam and spalled concrete on the old intake structure were repaired under contract (Belden Company).
  - Domestic water line replacement designed by Engineering (EN) Division, contract awarded for spring construction.
- **Funding trends (Operations, $000):** 1989: 152.0 → 1990: 147.0 → 1991: 154.3 → 1992: 152.2 → 1993: 171.6 (rising trend).
- **Funding trends (Maintenance, $000):** Notable volatility — $0 in 1990, $8.2K in 1989, rising to $66.97K in 1993.
- **Staffing:** Consistent at 4 FTE across all reported years (1989–1993).
- **Physical facilities:** 36 production raceways.
- **Inventory discrepancy:** September inventory revealed a 96,000 LAS shortfall relative to management chart projections, indicating a significant production/record-keeping gap.
- **Staff training/development:** Completion of Introduction to Fisheries Program (Modules I & II), Introduction to Supervision, Defensive Driving Course, Small Purchase Course, and Visa credit card training — reflecting professional development investment alongside operational duties.
- **Interagency collaboration:** Engagement with [[Lake Champlain Progress Assessment Team|Lake Champlain PAT]], Regional Office (EN, SMO), and Warm Springs NFH (WRNFH) staff for both administrative and technical water-system troubleshooting.
