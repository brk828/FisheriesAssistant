# Drum for Good Eating

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sciaenidae

## Executive Summary

This Wisconsin popular-press article by Thomas L. Wirth, Project Leader for Winnebago Studies, argues that the [[Freshwater Drum]] (commonly called "sheepshead") is an underrated, nutritious food fish. It reports that the edible fillet of Lake Winnebago drum is high in protein and low in fat, and it offers practical guidance on handling, filleting, skinning, frying, beer-batter preparation, and smoking. The article concludes that anglers who learn to handle drum would make better use of an abundant native food resource.

## Background & Description

- **Common names:** Freshwater drum, commonly called "sheepshead."
- **Native range:** Several larger lakes and rivers in [[Wisconsin]].
- **Classification problem:** Drum have been placed in the "rough fish" category, which the author suggests is partly responsible for anglers' negative prejudice against them.
- **Angling character:** On the hook they are described as gamey, and they are readily taken on live bait and often on artificial lures during summer and early fall.
- **Morphology relevant to cooking:** Bone structure is similar to [[Walleye]], [[Yellow Perch]], and black bass species, which makes preparation for the table straightforward.
- **Diet:** Food habits parallel those of [[Bluegill]] and perch, which feed mostly on aquatic insects and other invertebrates.
- **Flavor:** Flesh is described as lacking strong or undesirable flavors.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- Drum are described as delicate and prone to dying quickly after capture. Fish should be placed on a stringer or in a fish bag immediately, and kept in a cooler or on ice to preserve freshness until cleaned.
- The article contains no population, habitat-quality, or water-chemistry data beyond the note that the species is native to the named waters and is abundant.
- The relative food value of several fish species from different waters is being determined by the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Fishery Technological Laboratory in Seattle, Washington. Lake Winnebago drum data are already available from this program.

## Key Findings

**Nutritional composition of Lake Winnebago drum edible fillet:**

| Component | Reported value |
|---|---|
| Protein | 16 to 18 percent |
| Oil (fat) | 1.5 to 6 percent |
| Ash | 1 percent |
| Moisture | 75 to 81 percent |

- Protein content is higher than several other fish species and about the same as [[Beef]] or [[Chicken]].
- Fat content is lower than beef and chicken, which supports the author's claim that drum suit a low-calorie, high-protein diet.

**Handling and filleting:**
- Use a thin, sharp knife 4 to 5 inches long. Slice behind the head along both sides of the dorsal fin to the ribs, cut through the lower section to the tail, then slice across the ribs to free each fillet.
- Turn the fish over and remove the second fillet last, since the remaining side is harder to fillet once the other is removed.
- Skinning is done skin-side down on a board, using a sawing motion with a thin blade.

**Preparation methods:**
- **Pan or French frying:** The simplest method. Fillets or sticks are rolled in flour, cornmeal, or crumbs, or dipped in batter. Fillets should not be fried too long, or they dry out and become hard.
- **Beer batter:** Recommended as an excellent coating for a light, crisp finish with a slight malt aroma. Charged water or white soda can substitute if beer is unwanted.
- **Smoking:** Drum may be smoked whole with only the viscera removed. They should not be heated too long because they are less oily than [[Carp]] or [[Channel Catfish]] and tend to dry out.

**Conclusions and recommendations:**
- Drum is a valuable, underused native food fish that deserves wider acceptance.
- Anglers are encouraged to try drum fishing in native waters and to prepare the catch at home.
