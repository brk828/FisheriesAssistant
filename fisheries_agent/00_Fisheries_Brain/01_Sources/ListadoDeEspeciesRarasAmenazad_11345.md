# Acuerdo CT-CERN-001-91: Ecological Criteria and National Listing of Rare, Threatened, Endangered and Specially Protected Species in Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-listing

## Executive Summary

This document is a regulatory decree (Acuerdo CT-CERN-001-91) published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación on 17 May 1991 and reproduced in the [[Gaceta Ecologica]] (Volumen III, Número 15, May 1991). Issued by the [[Secretaria De Desarrollo Urbano Y Ecologia]] (SEDUE) in coordination with the [[Secretaria De Agricultura Y Recursos Hidraulicos]] and the [[Secretaria De Pesca]], it sets out the ecological criteria for defining species that are rare, threatened, endangered, or subject to special protection, along with endemic species, across Mexico's terrestrial and aquatic flora and fauna.

The decree defines four conservation status categories, lists several hundred taxa by status, and specifies how endemic species are flagged. Its aquatic listings cover freshwater fish, crustaceans, molluscs, amphibians, marine mammals, sea turtles and corals. The listings can be revised through future coordinated studies (Article 11).

## Background & Description

**Legal basis.** The decree is grounded in the [[Ley General Del Equilibrio Ecologico Y La Proteccion Al Ambiente]] (LGEEPA) and the Ley Orgánica de la Administración Pública Federal. Its preamble ("Considerando") states that all wild terrestrial and aquatic fauna and flora in national territory are, by constitutional provision, under the protection of the Nation.

**Rationale.** The decree states that many species, several characteristic of representative Mexican ecosystems, face serious risk from drastic population decline caused by habitat disturbance and indiscriminate use and exploitation. It frames these species as essential to genetic diversity and ecological balance. It also cites the [[Plan Nacional De Desarrollo 1989-1994]], which calls for studies on conserving and recovering threatened and endangered species, and states that international guidelines were considered in drafting the criteria.

**Signatories.** The decree was signed in Mexico City on 13 May 1991 by Carlos Hank González (Agricultura y Recursos Hidráulicos), Patricio Chirinos Calero (SEDUE), and María de los Ángeles Moreno Uriegas (Pesca).

**Structure.** The decree has eleven articles and one transitory provision. Articles 1 to 8 define the categories, Articles 9 and 10 contain the species lists (flora and fauna), and Article 11 allows the lists to be modified. The transitory provision sets entry into force the day after publication in the Diario Oficial.

**Status definitions:**
- **Endemic (Art. 1):** species whose natural distribution is confined within national territory. Endemic taxa are marked with a symbol in the lists and are treated as equivalent to the endemic category (Art. 2).
- **Threatened (Art. 3):** species judged likely to move into the "endangered" category in the near future if the causal factors continue (Art. 4 lists them).
- **Endangered (Art. 5):** species whose populations are reduced to a critical numerical level and whose habitat has been so drastically reduced that they face immediate risk of disappearing (Art. 6 lists them).
- **Special protection (Art. 7):** species requiring specific regulation to limit their exploitation (Art. 8 lists them).
- **Rare:** the fourth category, which the decree notes is not explicitly defined in the LGEEPA but still requires protection measures.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The decree adopts a precautionary, population-based framework. Its central concerns are habitat loss, overexploitation, and the loss of endemic taxa, which are often restricted to particular ecosystems. The listing approach treats endemism as a separate protection trigger, so species that are simultaneously endemic and threatened, endangered or specially protected are not left unprotected under the law.

The aquatic coverage is broad. Freshwater listings include the Cuatro Ciénegas region (Coahuila) and central Mexican lakes such as Alchichica and Chapala, where many endemic fish, crustaceans and snails are listed. Marine and coastal listings include cetaceans, pinnipeds, sea turtles, and corals (Acropora, Plexaura and Antipathes). The fauna lists also include terrestrial vertebrates, birds, and insects, reflecting the decree's combined terrestrial and aquatic scope.

The flora lists span orchids, cacti, palms, cycads, conifers, ferns and lycophytes across all four categories, and several species are associated with specific regions and ecosystem types.

## Key Findings

**Overall structure of the listings**
- The decree classifies species into four statuses (rare, threatened, endangered, special protection) and applies a separate endemic flag.
- Article 9 lists flora, and Article 10 lists fauna, in each category. The lists are long and are summarized here, not reproduced in full.
- Article 11 allows the lists to be modified based on studies carried out in coordination by the competent agencies.

**Fish listed under each status**
- **Rare (Raras):** about 25 taxa are listed, including *Chirostoma bartoni*, *Chirostoma humboltiana*, *Poblana letholepis*, *Catostomus insignis*, *Cycleptus elongatus*, *Gila modesta*, *Gila robusta*, *Notropis imeldae*, *Goodea gracilis*, *Skiffia francesae*, *Ictalurus australis*, *Ictalurus mexicanus*, *Ictalurus pricei*, *Xiphophorus clemenciae* and *Salmo nelsoni*.
- **Threatened (Amenazadas):** a long list that includes *Poblana ferdebueni*, *Catostomus conchos*, *Xyrauchen texanus*, *Astyanax mexicanus* and *Gambusia puncticulata*, among others.
- **Endangered (En peligro de extinción):** includes *Poblana alchichica*, *Cichlasoma minckleyi*, *Gila elegans*, *Cyprinodon macularius*, *Ameca splendens*, *Characodon lateralis*, *Girardinichthys viviparus*, *Xenophorus captivus*, *Prietella phreatophila* and *Etheostoma australe*.
- **Special protection (Protección especial):** includes *Totoaba macdonaldi* (listed as *Totoaba (= Cynoscion) macdonaldi*) and several *Ambystoma* salamanders.

**Marine and coastal fauna**
- **Special protection:** the marine mammal listings include *Balaenoptera borealis*, *B. musculus*, *B. physalus*, *Megaptera novaeangliae*, *Eschrichtius robustus*, *Physeter macrocephalus*, *Orcinus orca*, *Enhydra lutris nereis*, and the vaquita, *Phocoena sinus*. Sea turtles listed for special protection include *Caretta caretta*, *Chelonia mydas*, *Eretmochelys imbricata*, *Lepidochelys kempii* and *Lepidochelys olivacea*.
- **Endangered:** coral and gorgonian taxa (*Acropora cervicornis*, *Acropora palmata*, *Plexaura dichotoma*) are listed.

**Freshwater invertebrates**
- Crustaceans (e.g., *Cambarellus* crayfish, *Macrobrachium* prawns, *Typhlatya* shrimp) and gastropods (e.g., *Mexipyrgus*, *Coahuilix*, *Mexithauma*, and *Nymphophilus minckleyi*, mostly from Cuatro Ciénegas) are listed across several categories.

**Other taxa**
- Birds, mammals, reptiles and amphibians are also listed; the flora lists cover multiple families of orchids, cacti, palms, cycads, conifers and ferns.

**Regulatory provisions**
- The decree is a policy and listing instrument. It creates the classification framework on which later protection rules depend, but it does not itself set harvest quotas or detail enforcement measures.
- The 1991 decree is intended to be updated through Article 11 as new studies become available.
