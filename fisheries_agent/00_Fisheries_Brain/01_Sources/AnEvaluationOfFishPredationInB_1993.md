# An Evaluation of Fish Predation in Backwater Habitats in the Green River (Flaming Gorge Studies)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cyprinidae #freshwater-ecology #ColoradoRiverRecoveryProgram

## Executive Summary

This FY93 annual project report documents an ongoing investigation (Colorado River Recovery Program Project #18(10)) into the ecological role of fish predation within backwater habitats of the [[Green River]], a critical nursery habitat for the endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] (*Ptychocheilus lucius*). Rather than presenting finalized morphological or ecological data on a single species, the report describes the study's rationale, progress, and methodology for evaluating whether **non-native fish predation** alters benthic and planktonic invertebrate community structure — a precursor step to demonstrating resource competition between introduced and native fishes. As of FY93, the project was in the sample-processing phase (39% of 1,000 backwater samples picked; 16% fully identified/quantified), with results to be reported in an MS thesis (Brigham Young University) expected in summer 1994. The project received and expended a modest budget of $4,000.

## Background & Description

No morphological data on fish specimens are presented in this report. The document is a project/administrative status report focused on **invertebrate community sampling design and progress** rather than direct fish anatomy, meristics, or taxonomy. Any morphological characterization of the target species — [[Colorado Squawfish]] or associated [[Non-native Fishes|non-native fish]] competitors/predators — is deferred to the forthcoming MS thesis and final study report.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat focus:** [[Backwater Habitats]] of the Green River, considered essential **nursery habitat** for endangered Colorado River fishes, including the Colorado squawfish.
- **Trophic framework:** The study investigates whether fish predation restructures **benthic and planktonic invertebrate communities**, which form the food base for larval and juvenile native fishes in backwaters.
- **Community interactions targeted:**
  - Invertebrate density and production dynamics of dominant taxa.
  - Relationship between **river discharge/flow regimes** and standing crop of prey/food organisms.
  - Potential **exploitive competition** (depletion of shared food resources) and **interference competition** (direct behavioral exclusion) between native and non-native fishes.
- **Conceptual model:** A stepwise inferential approach is used:
  1. Determine if fish predation measurably impacts backwater invertebrate community structure/function.
  2. If no impact is detected → resource sharing is presumed non-limiting.
  3. If a significant shift in food organism abundance/composition occurs → this implies **resource competition** exists between native and introduced fishes in the [[Colorado Squawfish Nursery Habitat|nursery habitat]].
- This places the study within the broader context of the [[Colorado River Recovery Program]]'s RIPRAP goal (III.A.1) to identify negative impacts (predation, competition) of **problem/non-native fish species** on endangered native fishes.

## Key Findings

- **Study design rationale:** Competition between native and non-native fishes is difficult to demonstrate directly in large river systems like the Green River; this project uses invertebrate community shifts as an indirect proxy for competitive effects mediated through predation.
- **Sample processing progress (FY93):**
  - 390 of 1,000 targeted backwater samples processed (39% picked).
  - Only 16% of samples fully counted and taxonomically identified.
  - Full sample completion projected for 1 May 1994.
- **Shortcoming noted:** Sample processing is proceeding slower than planned, primarily due to student proficiency in invertebrate identification; Brigham Young University (Dr. [[Dennis Shiozawa]]) supplemented project funding to offset added processing costs.
- **Deliverables in progress:** Findings are being compiled into a graduate **MS thesis**, anticipated for completion in **summer 1994**, which will contain the substantive ecological/statistical results (invertebrate density, production estimates, flow–food relationships).
- **Budget:** FY93 funds provided ($4,000) matched funds expended ($4,000); no budgetary discrepancy reported.
- **Programmatic significance:** This project directly supports Colorado River Recovery Program efforts to evaluate and document negative impacts of non-native fish species on endangered fishes such as the Colorado squawfish, Humpback Chub, and other [[Colorado River Endangered Fishes]].
