# Letter to John G. Rogers Regarding Lower Colorado River Endangered Species Conservation Program

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Catostomidae #Cyprinidae #EndangeredSpecies #ColoradoRiver

## Executive Summary
This document is not a primary scientific study but a **policy and advocacy letter** from the Environmental Defense Fund (EDF) to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS), critiquing the governance and conservation adequacy of the Lower Colorado River Endangered Species Conservation Program. The letter addresses a Habitat Conservation Program (HCP) being developed under a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) dated August 2, 1995, between Lower Basin water/power users and federal/state/tribal stakeholders. While it does not provide morphological or ecological data on fish species directly, it identifies the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] as the target "included species" of conservation concern, and critiques the process by which Interim Conservation Measures (ICMs) are being designed to prevent their extinction and preserve genetic diversity within the [[Lower Colorado River]] ecosystem.

## Background & Description
No morphological data on the [[Razorback Sucker]] or [[Bonytail Chub]] is provided in this source. The document is administrative/legal in nature and does not describe physical traits, size, coloration, or anatomical features of either species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat/Range**: The Program Area is defined as the mainstem of the [[Colorado River]] below Glen Canyon Dam to the Southerly International Boundary, including the 100-year floodplain and reservoir full-pool elevations across Arizona, California, and Nevada.
- **Ecosystem Context**: The letter references an "ecosystem-based approach" intended to guide HCP development, implying that habitat conditions such as river flow regimes, dam operations, and reservoir management directly affect the survival of these endangered fish.
- **Threats Noted**:
  - Water diversions and power production operations in the Lower Colorado River are identified as competing pressures against species conservation.
  - Dam and reservoir reoperation is noted as a potential major conservation benefit for fish and wildlife, but one resisted by water users due to perceived economic risk.
- **Species Status**: Both the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] are treated as critically endangered, with conservation objectives explicitly framed around (1) preventing extinction and (2) preserving genetic diversity — indicating these species face severe population bottlenecks in the wild.

## Key Findings
- **Included Species**: The MOA and Sufficient Progress Agreement identify the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]] as the primary "included species" for Interim Conservation Measures (ICMs) within the Lower Colorado River HCP process.
- **Conservation Objectives**: The Biological Working Group and Steering Committee agreed on two initial ICM objectives: (1) prevent extinction, and (2) preserve genetic diversity of these two species, to be implemented via funding projects through the Native Fish Work Group.
- **Disconnect Between ICMs and Program Milestones**: Critically, the Sufficient Progress Agreement explicitly states that "the implementation or success of ICMs and the level of funding dedicated in any one year to ICM implementation are not intended to be the basis for gauging sufficient progress" — meaning fish conservation outcomes are procedurally decoupled from the metrics used to judge program success.
- **Regulatory Structure**: The MOA sought to have the FWS treat the agreement itself as satisfying Section 7 "reasonable and prudent measures/alternatives" and conservation recommendations for a 3-year period, effectively providing a broad regulatory assurance to water users independent of measurable species recovery benchmarks.
- **Primary vs. Secondary Purpose**: The letter argues the MOA's stated primary purpose is to "accommodate current water diversions and power production and optimize opportunities for future water and power development," with species/habitat conservation goals positioned as secondary — a structural critique of how the [[Habitat Conservation Program]] was designed relative to the needs of the [[Razorback Sucker]] and [[Bonytail Chub]].
- **Funding Commitment**: Signatories agreed to provide $1.5 million annually over the 3-year period for program development and ICM implementation.
- **Dam Reoperation as Conservation Tool**: The letter notes that reoperation of dams and reservoirs may offer the greatest ecological benefit to fish and wildlife in the system, but this option is constrained by water users' interests being prioritized in the consensus-based HCP process.
- **Institutional Roles**: Multiple state and tribal fish and wildlife agencies were copied on this letter, including the Arizona Department of Fish and Game, California Department of Fish and Game, Nevada Division of Wildlife, and the Colorado River Indian Tribes — reflecting the multi-jurisdictional governance structure surrounding [[Lower Colorado River]] endangered species management.
