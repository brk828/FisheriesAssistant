# Establishment and early dispersal of a loach, Misgurnus anguillicaudatus (Cantor), in Michigan

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cobitidae #invasive-species #freshwater-ecology

## Executive Summary

This 1960 short paper documents the **first successful introduction of any species of the family [[Cobitidae]] (loaches) in the New World**: the Oriental weatherfish, or dojo (*[[Misgurnus anguillicaudatus]]*), in the Shiawassee River system near Holly, Oakland County, Michigan. The population was discovered incidentally during a 1958 rotenone/toxaphene pond treatment. Historical records trace the introduction to an escaped aquarium-trade shipment from Kobe, Japan, received in 1939. Follow-up electrofishing surveys in 1958–1959 confirmed a self-sustaining, reproducing population with a documented dispersal range of at least 10 miles through a chain of six lakes.

## Background & Description

- **Discovery Context:** In July 1958, Wayne Tody and Bruce Vollmar of the [[Michigan Department of Conservation]] treated a private pond (Hy Meadow Pond) on Hy Meadow Farm near Holly, Michigan, with **rotenone** and **toxaphene**, standard piscicide tools for fish population survey/management.
- **Identification:** Unfamiliar, small, eel-like fish specimens were submitted to the **Museum of Zoology, University of Michigan**, where Dr. Reeve M. Bailey confirmed their identity as *Misgurnus anguillicaudatus* (Cantor), commonly known as the **Oriental weatherfish** or **dojo**.
- **Origin Trace:** Museum records revealed that six specimens of the same species had been submitted for identification on **July 4, 1939**, from the **Sunset Water Gardens**, a Holly, Michigan aquarium supply business. That shipment originated from **Kobe, Japan**.
- **Escape Pathway:** Sunset Water Gardens used **Sunset Pond** as a rearing/retaining facility. Sunset Pond lies less than one mile from Hy Meadow Pond, and both drain into the same intermittent stream — strongly implicating escaped stock as the founder population.
- **Timeline of Establishment:** Introduction is estimated at approximately **19 years prior** to the 1958 discovery (i.e., ~1939). In 1951, Hy Meadow Pond was dredged and deepened, and an earthen dyke was built across the outlet channel to prevent overflow — meaning the dojo's entrance into the pond almost certainly predated 1951.
- **Taxonomic Note:** This is a member of family [[Cobitidae]], native to East Asia, notable for its eel-like body form and ability to tolerate low-oxygen, sluggish aquatic environments.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Watershed:** The [[Shiawassee River]] system, spanning Oakland and Genesee Counties, Michigan.
- **Survey Methodology:** A three-man stream-survey crew conducted **16 fish collections** using a **2,500-watt direct-current shocker** on four dates: September 15, 18, and 23, 1958, and August 18–20, 1959. Six stations were located upstream and ten downstream of Hy Meadow Pond.
- **Preferred Habitat:** Dojos were disproportionately found in **standing or sluggish water in marsh areas adjacent to flowing water**, rather than in the main river channel — consistent with the species' known ecological preference for stagnant, vegetated habitats.
- **Distribution Extent:** Dojos were collected at **5 of the 16 stations**, indicating a continuous distribution across at least **10 miles** of river passing through **six lakes** (some of which are impoundments in Holly and Fenton).
- **Barrier Ineffectiveness:** Dams associated with the lake impoundments did **not** prevent downstream dispersal of the species, suggesting either passage through/around structures or transport via other vectors (e.g., flooding, human activity).
- **Range Delimitation:** Absence of dojos at four localities above Station 5 and five stations below Station 11 was interpreted as marking the effective boundaries of the species' established range at the time of study.
- **Climatic Tolerance:** Collections spanning September 1958 and August 1959 confirmed the population survived seasonal weather extremes typical of southern Michigan, indicating strong physiological adaptability to the temperate climate — a trait relevant to the species' broader potential as an [[Invasive Species]].

## Key Findings

- **First North American Cobitidae record:** This is documented as the first successful establishment of any loach species (family Cobitidae) in the New World.
- **Confirmed Reproduction:** Size range of collected specimens (**1.2 to 7.1 inches total length**) demonstrated successful natural reproduction within the watershed, not merely survival of introduced adults.

- **Dispersal Vector Confirmed:** Escape from an aquarium/ornamental fish supply operation (Sunset Water Gardens) via a connected drainage system is the most probable establishment mechanism — a classic pathway for [[Aquarium Trade]]-mediated species introductions.
- **Ecological Significance:** The dojo's tolerance of marginal/marsh habitats and its resilience to seasonal extremes suggest a high potential for continued range expansion within interconnected freshwater systems, foreshadowing later concerns about *Misgurnus anguillicaudatus* as a broader North American [[Invasive Species]].
