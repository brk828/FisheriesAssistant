# Larval Fish Sampling with Light Traps and Predator Avoidance in Southwestern Native Fishes

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This document consists of two conference-style abstracts (English and Spanish) on larval fishes of the southwestern United States. The first reports a light-trap study of [[Razorback Sucker]] larvae in [[Lake Mohave]], Arizona. The second compares predator avoidance behavior in larvae of southwestern native fishes with that of larvae native to the [[Mississippi River System]]. Both studies address larval ecology of native fishes in the [[Colorado River Basin]] and related systems.

## Background & Description

**Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)** is described as a large, endangered catostomid of the [[Colorado River Basin]]. Larvae are [[Phototaxis|phototactic]] until they reach about 14 mm in length. Researchers have traditionally collected larvae with hand-held spotlights and aquarium dip nets. Earlier attempts using light traps had limited success.

**Predator avoidance study species** were grouped by origin:
- Southwestern natives: Colorado Pikeminnow (*Ptychocheilus lucius*), Bonytail (*Gila elegans*), and Razorback Sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*).
- Mississippi River system natives: White Sucker (*Catostomus commersoni*) and Red Shiner (*Cyprinella lutrensis*).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The southwestern fauna is described as depauperate, with few piscivorous predators. The Mississippi River system has a long evolutionary history with larger numbers of piscivorous species. This contrast framed the hypothesis tested in the predator avoidance study.

In the light-trap study, the trapped community was not limited to fish. Other aquatic organisms were attracted to all light sources, including:
- [[Odonata]] (both [[Anisoptera]] and [[Zygoptera]])
- [[Hemiptera]]
- [[Bufonidae]] (toads)
- [[Zooplankton]]

This indicates that light traps also sample a broader aquatic community, which is relevant for monitoring larval densities and for understanding prey and competitor assemblages.

## Key Findings

### Light-Trap Study (Lake Mohave)

- **Design:** PVC [[Light Traps]] were built and tested with four attractants:
  - Cyalume light sticks (green)
  - Dry dog food
  - White light at two intensities, 12 watt and 55 watt, powered by 12-volt gel-cell batteries
- **Effort:** 226 trap sets, totaling 1,327.6 trap hours.
- **Response of razorback sucker larvae:** Larvae were attracted only to white light. They did not respond to bait or to Cyalume light sticks.
- **Bycatch:** Odonates, hemipterans, bufonids, and zooplankton were attracted to all light sources.
- **Purpose:** The method was developed as a way to monitor larval densities of [[Razorback Sucker]].

### Predator Avoidance Study

- **Hypothesis:** Southwestern larvae would be more "predator naive" and show a less elaborate and less well-developed set of predator avoidance behaviors than Mississippi River system larvae.
- **Result:** The hypothesis was not supported. Southwestern larvae showed a trend toward equal or greater predator avoidance.
- **Strength of effect:**
  - Survival and distance kept from the predator: only mildly evident.
  - [[Schooling Behavior]]: strongly expressed, with clear differences between groups.
- **Conclusion:** For the species and parameters tested, naivety of larvae of native southwestern fishes was not supported.

### Relevance

Together, the studies provide a practical sampling method for endangered larval [[Razorback Sucker]] and evidence that southwestern larval fishes are not less capable of [[Predator Avoidance Behavior]] than related Mississippi-system species. This has implications for managing native fish recovery in [[Lake Mohave]] and the wider [[Colorado River Basin]].
