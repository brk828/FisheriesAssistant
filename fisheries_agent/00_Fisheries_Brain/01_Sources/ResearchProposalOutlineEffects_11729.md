# Oxygen Variation Effects on Devil's Hole Pupfish Egg Viability and Recruitment

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinodontidae

## Executive Summary

This [[National Park Service]] research proposal, submitted by [[James E. Deacon]] (principal investigator) and [[Eric S. Gustafson]] (graduate student) at the [[University of Nevada Las Vegas]], asks whether diel (daily) oxygen variation is needed for normal embryonic development and hatching in the endangered [[Cyprinodon Diabolis]]. The work would take place inside [[Devil's Hole]] because laboratory rearing has largely failed. The proposed start date is 03-01-95, with a duration of 1 to 2 years and a planned field period of 04/06-16/95. The requested funding is $2,493.00.

## Background & Description

*Cyprinodon diabolis* is restricted to [[Devil's Hole]], a thermal pool in [[Death Valley National Park]]. Its eggs and larvae have been successfully reared outside the monument only rarely.

- **Laboratory history:** For more than two decades, propagation attempts have been largely unsuccessful. Even when spawning occurred, egg viability stayed low and hatchlings were infrequent and short-lived. Oxygen was assumed to be near saturation but was not monitored.
- **Oxygen context:** Oxygen saturation in the pool is near the established lethal limit for other *Cyprinodon* species, except where [[Photosynthetic Algae]] grow.
- **Developmental precedent:** In cyprinodonts, embryonic development has been arrested by hypoxia (70% oxygen saturation at 31 °C) and restored by raising oxygen before serious damage occurred. Elevated temperature effects were also reduced by higher oxygen.
- **Principal investigator background:** Dr. Deacon is a Distinguished Professor of Biology. Mr. Gustafson has about 20 years of experience with native western fishes, including work on [[Razorback Sucker]] and bonytail at [[Lake Mohave]], and this study will partly fulfil his Master of Arts in Science requirement.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Thermal and chemical setting:** Water temperature stays near 32 °C. Oxygen saturation seldom exceeds 70% except over the inner part of the shallow rock shelf, where [[Photosynthetic Algae]] are abundant. Algal oxygen production is expected to vary with incident radiation, which would create [[Diel Oxygen Variation]].
- **Spawning behavior:** Fish are thought to spawn across the whole shelf, in both vegetated and unvegetated areas. They have also been observed spawning on the lower shelf. They do not normally spawn over open water.
- **Habitat constraint:** The proposal states that no other location can replace Devil's Hole as the only habitat where the species has reproduced and recruited successfully over long periods.
- **Management relevance:** The authors note that refugium populations and any future reintroduction depend on improved captive rearing techniques, so the research could support conservation and [[Captive Rearing]] efforts.

## Key Findings

### Hypotheses
1. Hatching success will be significantly greater over shelf areas with higher algal density than over areas with lower algal density.
2. Embryos arrest and resume development in response to diel oxygen variation in Devil's Hole. (The proposal labels two hypotheses as "(2)"; this is reproduced as written.)
3. Diel oxygen variation, and possibly temperature variation, is necessary for normal development.

### Experimental Design
- **Spawning mats:** Artificial [[Spawning Grass Mats]] (6 × 7 in) would be placed at 10–13 pre-identified points, with at least 9 on the upper shelf. Upper-shelf mats would be placed at inner, middle, and outer transects (3 mats per transect). One or two mats would go on the lower shelf and one or two would be suspended in open water.
- **Breeder boxes:** Eggs on mats would be counted (clear = viable, opaque = non-viable) and enclosed in 10 × 12 in fine-mesh [[Net Breeder Boxes]]. Opaque eggs would be left on the grass. Hatching would occur under the same natural conditions as deposition.
- **Monitoring:** Probes near each breeder box would measure [[Dissolved Oxygen]], pH, and temperature over 7–10 days. Readings would be taken every 15 minutes near expected oxygen maxima (midday) and minima (predawn), and at longer intervals in the postdusk to predawn period.
- **Equipment:** A Hydrolab would be used on the lower shelf and a hand-held water chemistry analyzer on the upper shelf.
- **Treatment sites:** About 13 treatment sites are planned, each with several embryos.

### Impact and Minimization
- No embryos, larvae, or adult fish would be sacrificed. Dead embryos and larvae would be preserved in the [[University of Nevada Las Vegas]] fish collection.
- Eggs may be laid in hypoxic areas, which the authors think is normal in Devil's Hole, so some embryonic death is expected from the design.
- Field work would be short, with personnel entering the pool in a limited way. Divers would work the lower shelf and a single person would monitor the upper shelf from a plank walkway.
- Researchers would camp at the Devil's Hole parking lot with propane and battery equipment, following NPS regulations.

### Personnel and Schedule
- **Roles:** Dr. Deacon would act as overall administrator and design advisor. Mr. Gustafson would lead field work and prepare reports. Mrs. Genie Dove, an undergraduate, is a field assistant. A second assistant for lower-shelf safety monitoring is to be recruited later.
- **Training:** Egg identification and mat transfer techniques would be refined with similar species at UNLV under Mrs. Francis Taylor. Pre-study access to Devil's Hole is requested to test equipment and build preliminary oxygen profiles from the lower to upper shelf.
- **Timing:** The initial study would run over spring break (04/06–16/95). Follow-up visits could continue to midsummer, with a possible second effort in 1996 depending on results and permits.

### Products and Deliverables
- Annual reports to the park superintendent before December 31, 1995, with lay summaries.
- A final report within 6 months of study completion, and publication of significant findings in the open literature.
- Photographs, displays, and possible poster presentations for park visitor centers.
