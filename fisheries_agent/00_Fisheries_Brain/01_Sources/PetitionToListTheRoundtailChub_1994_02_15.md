## Executive Summary

This document is an internal memorandum from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS), Arizona Ecological Services State Office, dated February 15, 1994, addressing administrative confusion surrounding a petition to list the [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) under the Endangered Species Act. The memo does not contain morphological, ecological, or population data on the species; rather, it documents a bureaucratic discrepancy between the Arizona Ecological Services Office (AESO) and the Washington Division of Endangered Species (DES) regarding whether a formal petition (No. 671, received March 25, 1993) actually existed. A Federal Register notice (58 FR 64828, December 9, 1993) had already published a "not warranted" finding with a "Category 2" status for the species, despite AESO — the lead office for the species — having no knowledge of the petition's existence or the basis for the finding. The memo highlights institutional concern that candidate species assessments may proceed without proper input from regional experts familiar with the species' biology and status.

## Key Findings

- A petition (No. 671) for listing the [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*) was reportedly received by USFWS on March 25, 1993, but its authenticity was disputed — the Washington [[Division of Endangered Species]] (DES) initially claimed "no petition exists," calling its inclusion in the December 16, 1993 review packet an "error."
- Despite this denial, USFWS published a petition finding in the Federal Register (58 FR 64828, December 9, 1993) concluding **"not warranted"** for listing, with the species assigned **candidate status = Category 2**.
- The [[Arizona Ecological Services Office]] (AESO), designated as the **lead office** for the species, was not consulted or informed about the petition, its review, or the basis for the finding — raising concerns about procedural integrity in the [[Endangered Species Act]] petition process.
- In **February 1992**, the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] had already recommended that the [[Roundtail Chub]] be listed, believing that sufficient supporting data existed, though not yet compiled into a coherent status report.
- AESO had been unsuccessfully seeking funding to conduct a formal **status survey** to consolidate existing data and fill information gaps on the species prior to this memo.
- The memo requests clarification from the Regional Director (Albuquerque, NM) on whether the petition genuinely exists, and if so, requests copies of the petition, associated findings, and supporting documentation; if not, it requests correction of erroneous records.
- This document underscores a broader administrative/regulatory gap in the candidate species assessment process rather than providing new biological or conservation science on *Gila robusta*, and should be cross-referenced with substantive status reviews such as those from the [[Desert Fishes Recovery Team]] for scientific content on the species.
