# Provisional Checklist of Canadian Freshwater Fishes

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #biogeography #taxonomy #Canada

## Executive Summary

This provisional checklist, compiled by the Department of Ichthyology and Herpetology at the [[Royal Ontario Museum]] in July 1967, documents 185 species of freshwater fishes across 25 families occurring in Canadian waters. The document was prepared as a working aid for a comprehensive text on Canadian freshwater fishes sponsored by the [[Fisheries Research Board of Canada]]. It includes updated nomenclature, provincial/territorial distribution data, and notes on introduced species. The checklist follows the taxonomic framework of Special Publication No. 2 of the [[American Fisheries Society]] and provides detailed geographic distribution by watershed basin (Atlantic, Hudson Bay, Arctic, Pacific, and Gulf of Mexico).

## Background & Description

The checklist represents a systematic inventory of Canada's freshwater fish fauna, organized taxonomically from Class [[Agnatha]] (lampreys) through Class [[Osteichthyes]] (bony fishes). Major taxonomic groups include:

- **[[Petromyzontidae]]** (lampreys): 9 species
- **[[Acipenseridae]]** (sturgeons): 5 species  
- **[[Salmonidae]]** (salmons, trouts, whitefishes, grayling): 38 species
- **[[Cyprinidae]]** (minnows): 48 species
- **[[Catostomidae]]** (suckers): 16 species
- **[[Percidae]]** (perches and darters): 16 species
- **[[Centrarchidae]]** (sunfishes): 10 species

The document notes significant nomenclatural changes since Scott's 1958 checklist, including genus-level revisions (e.g., *Leucichthys* becoming a subgenus of *[[Coregonus]]*) and species synonymizations. Provincial/territorial distributions are indicated by abbreviations, with asterisks marking introduced populations and question marks indicating uncertain establishment success.

Geographic coverage spans from [[Newfoundland]] and [[Labrador]] in the east to [[British Columbia]], [[Yukon Territory]], and [[Alaska]] in the west, encompassing all Canadian provinces and territories. The checklist deliberately avoids subspecific designations except where distinct common names exist, treating forms like the [[Coastal Cutthroat Trout]] and [[Yellowstone Cutthroat Trout]] as components of *[[Salmo clarki]]*.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The document organizes species by major watershed basins, revealing distinct biogeographic patterns:

- **Atlantic Watershed**: 146 species (highest diversity), including [[Lake Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser fulvescens*), [[American Shad]] (*Alosa sapidissima*), [[Muskellunge]] (*Esox masquinongy*)
- **Hudson Bay Watershed**: 92 species, dominated by cyprinids and percids
- **Arctic Watershed**: 57 species, including [[Arctic Cisco]] (*Coregonus autumnalis*), [[Inconnu]] (*Stenodus leucichthys*), [[Arctic Grayling]] (*Thymallus arcticus*)
- **Pacific Watershed**: 68 species, featuring five *[[Oncorhynchus]]* salmon species and Pacific lampreys
- **Gulf of Mexico Watershed**: 13 species (smallest fauna)

Provincial species richness varies dramatically: [[Ontario]] supports 133 species (highest), followed by [[Quebec]] (103 species), while [[Prince Edward Island]] has only 18 species. This pattern reflects watershed connectivity, habitat diversity, and glacial history.

The checklist documents numerous introduced species, particularly salmonids and centrarchids. Notable introductions include [[Rainbow Trout]] (*Salmo gairdneri*) widely stocked across provinces, [[Kokanee]] (landlocked *Oncorhynchus nerka*) establishing spawning runs in [[Georgian Bay]] by 1966, and [[Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) in multiple provinces. Some introductions remain uncertain, marked with asterisks and question marks (e.g., [[Pink Salmon]] in Newfoundland, [[Coho Salmon]] in Ontario from [[Lake Michigan]] stockings).

The document includes a supplementary list of marine species occasionally entering freshwater (e.g., [[Pacific Herring]], [[Shiner Perch]], [[Winter Flounder]]), acknowledging the transitional nature of estuarine habitats.

## Key Findings

**Taxonomic Revisions**: Major nomenclatural changes include:
- *[[Lampetra]]* species previously in *Entosphenus* now consolidated (distinction unclear)
- *Lampetra ayresi* elevated from synonymy with European *L. fluviatilis* (Vladykov and Follett 1958)
- *[[Perca flavescens]]* synonymized with *P. fluviatilis* (Svetovidov and Dorofeeva 1963)
- *[[Esox americanus]]* now encompasses both redfin and grass pickerels as subspecies (Crossman 1966)
- [[Aurora Trout]] (*Salvelinus timagamiensis*) synonymized with [[Brook Trout]] (*S. fontinalis*) (Sale 1967)
- [[Splake]] (hybrid *S. fontinalis* × *S. namaycush*) recognized as important artificial form

**New Species Descriptions**:
- *[[Coregonus canadensis]]* (Scott and Klawe 1967): [[Atlantic Whitefish]], restricted to Nova Scotia waters
- *[[Hybopsis x-punctata]]* (Hubbs and Crowe 1956): [[Gravel Chub]], replacing *Erimystax dissimilis*

**Range Extensions and First Records**:
- *[[Acipenser brevirostrum]]* ([[Shortnose Sturgeon]]): specimens from [[St. John River]], New Brunswick (Scott and Crossman 1959; Magnin 1963)
- *[[Minytrema melanops]]* ([[Spotted Sucker]]): first Canadian record from [[Lake St. Clair]], 1962 (Crossman and Ferguson 1963)
- *[[Gasterosteus wheatlandi]]* ([[Twospine Stickleback]]): reported from Quebec (McAllister 1960) and Newfoundland (Scott and Crossman 1964), previously confused with *G. aculeatus*

**Biogeographic Patterns**: Species richness by province/territory ranges from 18 (Prince Edward Island) to 133 (Ontario). Watershed analysis shows Atlantic basin with highest diversity (146 species), reflecting complex drainage history and habitat heterogeneity. Arctic basin (57 species) includes specialized cold-water forms like [[Broad Whitefish]] (*Coregonus nasus*) and [[Alaska Blackfish]] (*Dallia pectoralis*).

**Taxonomic Complexes Requiring Resolution**:
- *Coregonus clupeaformis* complex: possibly includes [[Humpback Whitefish]] (*C. pidschian*) and *C. nelsoni* (Lindsey 1963 validated *C. nelsoni* as separate species)
- *Coregonus autumnalis*/*C. laurettae* complex in Alaska (McPhail 1966)
- *[[Salvelinus alpinus]]* complex: McPhail (1961) placed Quebec red trout (*S. marstoni*), *S. oquassa*, and *S. aureolus* in synonymy
- *[[Myoxocephalus quadricornis]]* vs. *M. thompsoni*: taxonomic status unclear due to possible Arctic intermediates

**Introduced Species Success**: Document tracks 30+ introduced populations across provinces. Successful establishments include [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*) in 9 provinces, [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieui*) in 6 provinces, and [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*) in Ontario and British Columbia. Uncertain introductions include [[Chum Salmon]] in Ontario and [[Arctic Grayling]] experimental stockings (MacKay 1963).

**Methodological Notes**: Authors explicitly solicit criticisms and corrections, emphasizing the provisional nature of the checklist. They note the document will be combined with identification keys in future revisions. The decision to avoid subspecific designations reflects practical considerations for field identification, though this approach may obscure biologically significant variation in widespread species.
