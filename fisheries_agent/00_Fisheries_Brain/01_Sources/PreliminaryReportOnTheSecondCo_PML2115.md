# Preliminary Report on Fish Collecting Trips, Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge (1986–1987)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This preliminary report by [[Michael T. Perkinson]] documents two field collecting trips to the [[Buenos Aires National Wildlife Refuge]] in the winter of 1986–1987, carried out with collectors Jim Starr and Mike Perkinson. The surveys sampled [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) and [[Bonytail]] (*Gila elegans*) across several impoundments. Fish were measured for total and fork length and weighed, and the report compares the two sampling periods. Across the period, the razorbacks and bonytails sampled showed increases in mean length and weight, which the author interprets as growth.

## Background & Description

The report covers two trips, both led by the same collectors:

- **First trip:** late January 1987 (sampling dates 31 Jan 1987, with some earlier tank visits on 4 Nov 1986 and 3 Nov 1986 recorded in the summary).
- **Second trip:** late February 1987 (27–28 Feb 1987).

Collection used several gear types, including a 10 m bag seine, a 100 ft bag seine, a 30 ft seine, a [[Trammel Net]], and a [[Gill Net]]. Captures were recorded by site, date, and time, with individual total length (TL), fork length (FL), and weight, along with notes on feeding behavior and tank conditions.

The two target species belong to the families [[Catostomidae]] (razorback sucker) and [[Cyprinidae]] (bonytail).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Sampling took place in artificial and semi-natural water bodies within the refuge:

- **[[Mormon Lake]]**: sampled with seines and trammel nets on 31 Jan 1987.
- **[[Road Camp Tank]]**: sampled on 27 Feb 1987 and again on 1 Feb 1987.
- **[[King's Tank]]**: sampled on 28 Feb 1987 and 31 Jan 1987, including gill net sets.
- **[[Big Bertha Tank]]**: sampled on 28 Feb 1987 with a 30 ft seine.

Field notes describe behavior and conditions:

- Fish appeared actively feeding at the Road Camp Tank before sunset (approximately 1730 hrs).
- Razorbacks showed a rosy red coloration at the base of the anal and paired fins, as observed in the field.
- The air temperature dropped below freezing during the trip.
- The report mentions a change in water level of about two feet relative to the prior month, though this portion of the text is fragmentary.
- Notes on capture methods, fish locations within the tanks, and tank condition were also recorded but are not fully quantified.

## Key Findings

**Sample sizes and captures**

- Captures were recorded at multiple tanks over both trips, with sample counts (n) ranging from roughly 6 to over 90 fish per haul or set, depending on the gear.
- At the end of the second trip, the author notes that all bonytails captured across the sampling were recorded in a single summary (overall TL 100.9 mm, FL 94.7 mm, weight 9.5 g).

**Razorback sucker (*Xyrauchen texanus*)**

- Mean weight of individuals increased from 23.9 g to 64.7 g between sampling periods.
- Mean total length rose from 140 mm to 166.4 mm.
- Example site data: Mormon Lake (31 Jan 1987, combined seine and trammel net) gave n = 16, mean TL 156.4 mm, and mean weight 64.7 g.

**Bonytail (*Gila elegans*)**

- Mean total length increased from 76 mm to 100.9 mm.
- Mean weight increased from 6.2 g to 10.0 g.
- Across all sites, the summary reports a mean TL of 100.9 mm, mean FL of 94.7 mm, and mean weight of 9.5 g.

**Interpretation**

- The author concludes that the razorbacks and bonytails have grown in length and weight over the sampling period. This is the report's main conclusion. Because the study is preliminary, the growth interpretation rests on a limited number of trips and sites.
- Additional, less quantifiable information was gathered on capture methods, fish distribution within tanks, and tank conditions. The report presents these as qualitative observations.
