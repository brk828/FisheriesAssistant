# Bubbling Ponds Native Fish Conservation Facility Review and Action Items (August 2010)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #native-fish-conservation

## Executive Summary

This note summarizes the final action items from the August 27, 2010 review of the [[Bubbling Ponds Native Fish Conservation Facility]] (the Facility), operated under the [[Gila River Basin Native Fishes Conservation Program]] (the Program). The meeting, held at the [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]], produced 15 action items covering governance and documentation, broodstock genetics, restoration sourcing, facility expansion, operating funds, and the wastewater discharge problem. The main decisions were to reject draining the Facility directly to Oak Creek, to limit wild-fish augmentation and hatchery-reared (F1) additions to defined percentages, and to keep West Fork Gila River spikedace progeny out of the wild. The meeting also reviewed a standard fish disease treatment protocol for wild-caught native fish.

## Background & Description

The Facility is a holding and propagation center for imperiled native fishes of the Gila River Basin. The meeting agenda asked whether its primary purpose is holding imperiled lineages or production, and why it is called a research facility. The Facility is described as fully developed, so expansion was deferred until the existing [[Bubbling Ponds Hatchery]] is renovated.

The agenda covered:
- A facility tour explaining the purpose of each component (circular tanks, raceways, ponds) and its water source, water quality and supply limits.
- An office discussion of planning, disease management, broodstock status, production, restoration sites and budget.

Meeting details:
- Date: August 27, 2010 (Friday), 9:00 at the hatchery.
- Organizer: [[Robert Clarkson]] of the [[Bureau of Reclamation]], who sent the final agenda on August 25, 2010.
- Attendance and the sign-in sheet are referenced in the source but not included in the text.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Species discussed include the [[Loach Minnow]], the [[Spikedace]] (including a West Fork Gila River lineage), and the [[Eagle Creek Roundtail Chub]]. The Program works with lineages (stream-specific populations), which are the basis for prioritizing holding, propagation and reintroduction.

Key ecological and management points:
- **Wild-origin augmentation:** Broodstock of each loach minnow and spikedace lineage should receive at least 10–20% wild fish every other year. At target broodstock sizes of 500, that is 50–100 fish.
- **Source-population protection:** Progeny (F1s) should not be returned to their source populations unless that population is declining. The example given is the North Fork East Fork Black River.
- **Oak Creek discharge:** The Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD) proposed draining the Facility to Oak Creek. The group called this unacceptable and asked for an alternative.
- **Disease risk:** Wild-caught fish require a quarantine and treatment protocol before entering the Facility (see Key Findings).

## Key Findings

**1. Governance and documentation**
- [[David Ward]] will write a comprehensive guidance document on Facility procedures and operations.
- David Ward will also write brief broodstock management plans for each species held on station.
- Rob Clarkson will draft a document that ranks species and lineages for treatment. It will cover long-range plans for acquiring, holding, propagating and returning fish to the wild, and list potential receptor sites for each.

**2. Broodstock management**
- Augmentation: 10–20% wild fish at least every other year (50–100 fish at target size 500).
- F1 additions: no more than about 20% of broodstock at target size 500.
- West Fork Gila River spikedace: progeny from the three adult fish should not be stocked in the wild. They may be suitable for education or experiments.
- Hormone use: using [[Ovaprim]] to speed gonadal maturation of Eagle Creek roundtail chub was considered. The group agreed to wait another year to assess volunteer spawning.

**3. Restoration and sourcing**
- The Program should use more wild fish for stream restorations when possible, following appropriate quarantine.
- Several agenda questions asked which restoration sites are identified and whether schedules and plans exist. The handwritten answers are largely illegible in the scan.

**4. Facility capacity and expansion**
- Expansion will be pursued when the Bubbling Ponds Hatchery is renovated, because the existing Facility is fully developed.
- Draining to Oak Creek is rejected (see Ecological & Environmental Context).

**5. Funding and cost control**
- AZGFD will review overhead costs for the Facility, try to remove redundancies, and report back.
- The New Mexico Department of Game and Fish (NMDGF) will explore contributing funds to Facility operations.
- The Program will keep ensuring adequate Facility funding.
- Agenda item 16 asked what the Program can afford to contribute. The answer is not legible in the scan.

**6. Disease treatment prescription for wild-caught native fish**
The protocol gives two regimes. Both start with a [[Biofilter]] established using disposable fish, which takes 2–3 weeks, to prevent water-quality collapse.

*Standard regime (water temperature not below 20 °C / 68 °F), 30 days:*
- Day 1: Replace all water, raise salinity to 3 ppt (about 3 lb table salt per 100 gallons), add native fish, and keep water above 20 °C.
- Day 2: Dose with [[Dimilin]] (1 tablespoon per 60 gallons) for [[Lernaea]] (anchor worm).
- Day 14: Change water and re-dose Dimilin.
- Day 28: Change water and dose with [[Praziquantel]] at 1.5 mg/L for Asian tapeworm.
- Day 29: Change water and re-dose Praziquantel.
- Day 30: One-hour formalin bath at 26 ppm (1 ml per 10 gallons) in a separate container with extra aeration. The author recommends Proform C, which combines formalin and malachite green.

*Fast-track regime (water temperature not below 27 °C / 80 °F), 22 days:* Same steps, compressed: Dimilin on Days 2 and 10, Praziquantel on Days 20 and 21, and the formalin bath on Day 22.

The regimes are said to cover [[Ich]] (salt), lernea, Asian tapeworm, and external parasites not killed by salt, such as trichodina and Costia. Praziquantel must be frozen to stay effective and must be dissolved in 10–20 ml of alcohol first.
