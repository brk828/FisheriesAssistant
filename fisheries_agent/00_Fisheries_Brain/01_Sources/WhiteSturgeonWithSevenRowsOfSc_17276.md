# White Sturgeon with Seven Rows of Scutes

## Executive Summary
This 1955 note in *California Fish and Game* documents a rare morphological anomaly in [[White Sturgeon]] (*Acipenser transmontanus*): the presence of seven rows of bony scutes instead of the taxonomically diagnostic five. Observed during tagging studies in the Columbia River and confirmed in a single specimen from the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta, this anomaly occurred in approximately 0.3% of examined individuals, suggesting a low-frequency but recurring variant within the species.

## Background & Description
The family [[Acipenseridae]] (sturgeons) is primarily diagnosed by five longitudinal rows of bony plates, or scutes, covering the body: one dorsal row, two lateral rows, and two ventral rows. Between these primary rows, numerous small, irregularly placed stellate prickles typically occur, distinguished from true scutes by their smaller size and inconspicuous, non-uniform arrangement.

During a tagging study of [[White Sturgeon]] conducted in the Columbia River at Prescott, Oregon, beginning in the winter of 1950, the author (Alexander D. Bajkov) identified individuals exhibiting two additional rows of scutes, for a total of seven rows. These extra rows were located bilaterally between the normal dorsal and lateral rows (illustrated in Figure 1 of the original paper, a dorsal-view photograph).

Morphological characteristics of the anomalous rows:
- Size: In some specimens, the additional scutes matched the size of the lateral scutes; in others, they were somewhat smaller.
- Count: The additional rows contained between 20 and 30 scutes each in Columbia River specimens.
- Positioning: Scute counts in the extra rows were intermediate between the dorsal and lateral row counts, suggesting these are true supernumerary scute rows rather than enlarged prickle fields.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
The observations derive from two major river systems on the U.S. Pacific coast:
- **Columbia River** (at Prescott, Oregon, and above [[Bonneville Dam]]): Site of the majority of anomalous specimens, examined via an extensive tagging program (~7,000 individuals).
- **Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta**, California: Site of a single confirmed anomalous specimen, found during a 1954 tagging effort of 1,003 [[White Sturgeon]] by the California Department of Fish and Game.

Multiple independent long-term observers corroborated the phenomenon:
- **C. E. Graham**, a moorage owner and experienced sturgeon fisherman at Prescott, Oregon, reported having seen seven-scute-row specimens previously.
- **Dr. M. A. Kenney** (Rainier, Oregon) contributed additional specimens.
- **John Broughton**, a Columbia River commercial fisherman, independently caught and reported multiple such fish above Bonneville Dam.

This anecdotal corroboration from experienced fishermen, combined with the tagging survey data, indicates the anomaly is a recognized, if uncommon, natural variation rather than a singular aberration.

## Key Findings
- The diagnostic five-row scute pattern of [[Acipenseridae]] is not universally fixed; a seven-row variant exists in [[White Sturgeon]] populations.
- Extra scute rows occur symmetrically between the dorsal and lateral rows on each side of the body.
- Out of approximately 7,000 white sturgeon examined and tagged in the Columbia River, about 25 individuals (~0.3%) exhibited seven rows of scutes.
- A parallel, independent finding in the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta (1 of 1,003 tagged fish, 1954) suggests this trait is not geographically restricted to the Columbia River population.
- Scute counts in the extra rows (20–30) were intermediate between typical dorsal and lateral scute counts, implying a developmental relationship to normal scute patterning rather than an unrelated structure.
- The author notes it is unknown whether this seven-row anomaly occurs in other genera or species within [[Acipenseridae]], flagging this as an open question for future comparative research.
- This note serves as an early documented case of intraspecific meristic/morphological variation in a diagnostic taxonomic character for sturgeons, with relevance to fish [[Systematics]] and population-level [[Morphological Variation]] studies.
