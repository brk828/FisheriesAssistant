# Fourth Endangered Fish Uses Gunnison River Ladder

#ichthyology #marine-biology #colorado-squawfish

## Executive Summary

A U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service news release (July 23, 1997) reports that a fourth endangered [[Colorado Squawfish]] ascended the [[Redlands Fish Ladder]] on the [[Gunnison River]] near [[Grand Junction]], Colorado. The fish is the third endangered fish to use the ladder in two weeks and the fourth since the structure was completed in 1996. Biologists consider this an encouraging sign for the ladder's success. The ladder has also allowed nearly 15,000 other native fish to reach river reaches that had been blocked for almost a century.

## Background & Description

- **Structure:** The Redlands Fish Ladder is a 380-foot-long channel that bypasses the 12-foot-high concrete [[Redlands Diversion Dam]].
- **Operation:** About 25 cubic feet per second is diverted through the ladder. Fish enter at the bottom, swim up a series of steps to a holding area, and are sorted and counted by biologists before release upstream.
- **Focal species:** The [[Colorado Squawfish]] is an endangered native fish. Other native species passing through the ladder include [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], [[Bluehead Sucker]], and [[Roundtail Chub]].
- **Monitoring:** Researchers use implanted electronic tags, routinely applied in [[Endangered Fish Research]], read with a bar-code-style scanner.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Flow and temperature:** Squawfish movement into the ladder likely reflects receding river flows and rising water temperatures. Biologist Bob Burdick of the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] in Grand Junction links the movement to possible spawning.
- **Basin fragmentation:** Burdick notes that the lower [[Colorado River Basin]] (downstream of [[Lake Powell]]) has been fragmented by dams and diversions that block fish migrations. Many native fish there have declined in numbers. The goal is to prevent the same decline in the upper basin before populations become too low.
- **Restored connectivity:** Although the dam remains, the ladder has re-created a more naturally functioning river environment by reconnecting reaches that had been off-limits for nearly a century.

## Key Findings

- **Fourth endangered fish passage:** A Colorado squawfish moved through the ladder on Wednesday, July 23, 1997. It was the third endangered fish in roughly 14 days and the fourth since the ladder opened the previous year.
- **Fish identification:** The fish measured 23 inches and weighed 31 pounds. Its electronic tag (No. 1F41386206) showed it had been captured in 1995 in the [[Colorado River]] near [[Moab]], Utah, by Utah fishery biologists. It had migrated more than 100 miles downstream to the ladder.
- **Broader passage success:** Nearly 15,000 other native fish, including flannelmouth suckers, bluehead suckers, and roundtail chubs, have passed through the structure.
- **Interpretation:** Biologists see the recent passages as an encouraging sign for the ladder's potential success. Burdick suggests movement may be related to spawning.
