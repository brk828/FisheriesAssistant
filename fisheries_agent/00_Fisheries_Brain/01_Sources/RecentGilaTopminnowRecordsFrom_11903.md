# Recent Gila topminnow records from the middle Santa Cruz River and field notes

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Poeciliidae #endangered-species #Gila-Topminnow

## Executive Summary

This 1994 U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS) memorandum documents the incidental collection of the endangered [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*) at two sites in the middle [[Santa Cruz River]], Santa Cruz County, Arizona. Sampling occurred on May 16 and May 18, 1994, during unrelated priority pollutant water quality sampling. A total of 51 Gila Topminnow specimens were preserved across both sites (36 at Rio Rico Road bridge, 15 at Rancho Santa Cruz), several of which exhibited morphological deformities (mouth and possible spinal deformities). These records are notable for extending the known upstream range of the species relative to a prior 1993 record and confirm continued persistence of this federally listed fish in a river reach subject to effluent-influenced flow (proximity to the Nogales International Wastewater Treatment Plant).

## Background & Description

- **Species of primary concern:** [[Gila Topminnow]] (*Poeciliopsis occidentalis occidentalis*), a small-bodied, live-bearing poeciliid fish native to the Gila River Basin of Arizona and Sonora, Mexico. It is listed under the U.S. Endangered Species Act.
- **Co-occurring native fish species identified in the samples:**
  - [[Desert Sucker]] (*Catostomus (Pantosteus) clarki*)
  - [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*)
  - [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*)
- **Sampling context:** The fish collections were incidental to a priority pollutant sampling effort, not a targeted fish survey. This suggests fortuitous documentation of species presence rather than systematic population assessment.
- **Sampling method:** Smith-Root Model 12 backpack electrofisher, a standard technique for shallow freshwater stream fish sampling.
- **Personnel:** Field collection by Kirke King and Brenda Andrews (USFWS, Phoenix, AZ); specimen identification confirmed by Sally Stefferud (USFWS Arizona Ecological Services State Office).
- **Specimen repository:** Voucher specimens designated for deposit in the museum collection at [[Arizona State University]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Location 1 — Rio Rico Road Bridge:** Located on the Santa Cruz River approximately 1 mile downstream from the mouth of [[Sonoita Creek]]. This site lies downstream of the [[Nogales International Wastewater Treatment Plant]], indicating the river reach may be influenced by treated effluent discharge, a known factor sustaining perennial flow in this otherwise intermittent desert river system.
- **Location 2 — Rancho Santa Cruz:** Located approximately 1/3 mile upstream from the mouth of [[Josephine Canyon]], and roughly 1.5 miles upstream of a previous May 6, 1993 Gila Topminnow collection record made by the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] and the [[Arizona Department of Water Quality]] at Santa Gertrudis Lane.
- **Habitat type:** Perennial/effluent-dependent desert riverine habitat characteristic of the [[Santa Cruz River]] watershed in southeastern Arizona — a system historically important for supporting native desert fish assemblages amid significant habitat degradation, dewatering, and non-native species introductions across the Gila River Basin.
- **Range significance:** The upstream detection of Gila Topminnow at Rancho Santa Cruz represents an expansion of the known distribution beyond the 1993 record, suggesting either natural upstream dispersal, effluent-related habitat expansion, or previously undetected persistence in this stretch.

## Key Findings

- **Total Gila Topminnow collected:** 51 individuals preserved across two sites.
  - **Rio Rico Road Bridge (May 16, 1994):** 36 specimens preserved, including:
    - 1 individual with a mouth deformity
    - 1 individual with a possible spinal deformity
  - **Rancho Santa Cruz (May 18, 1994):** 15 specimens preserved (no deformities noted for this species at this site).
- **Morphological anomalies noted in other species:**
  - 2 [[Longfin Dace]] with mouth deformities (Rio Rico Road Bridge site).
  - 1 [[Desert Sucker]] with an unspecified deformity, preserved (Rancho Santa Cruz site).
- **Field identification uncertainty:** Field personnel did not record Gila Topminnow presence at one location in initial field notes due to uncertainty in on-site species identification; subsequent laboratory examination by Sally Stefferud confirmed identification from preserved specimens.
- **Range extension:** The Rancho Santa Cruz collection site is positioned approximately 1.5 miles upstream of the nearest previously documented Gila Topminnow record (May 1993, Santa Gertrudis Lane), indicating possible upstream range expansion or previously undocumented occurrence.
- **Multi-species sampling value:** The incidental sampling effort concurrently documented three other native Arizona fish species ([[Desert Sucker]], [[Sonora Sucker]], [[Longfin Dace]]) co-occurring with Gila Topminnow, providing useful community-level presence data for the middle Santa Cruz River during this period.
- **Conservation relevance:** These records were compiled and distributed to "Interested Parties" by the USFWS Arizona Ecological Services Office, indicating their importance for ongoing endangered species monitoring and consultation processes involving the Gila Topminnow.
