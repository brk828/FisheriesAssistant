# Keys to the British Species of Aquatic Megaloptera and Neuroptera

#ichthyology #marine-biology #sialidae #osmylidae #sisyridae

*Note: This source is an entomological identification key, not an ichthyological study. The taxa are aquatic insects, so the tags above are retained for system consistency but the content concerns insects.*

## Executive Summary

This second-edition key (Freshwater Biological Association Scientific Publication No. 8, 1962), by D. E. Kimmins of the [[British Museum (Natural History)]], identifies the six British aquatic species of the orders [[Megaloptera]] and [[Neuroptera]]. The insects are only partly aquatic: eggs, pupae and adults are terrestrial, though they are seldom found far from water. The document provides dichotomous keys to families and to genera and species for adults and larvae, along with notes on life history, collecting, preservation, and Lake District distribution.

## Background & Description

- **Taxonomic scope:** Megaloptera comprises the family [[Sialidae]] (genus [[Sialis]]: *S. lutaria* and *S. fuliginosa*). Neuroptera comprises [[Osmylidae]] (*Osmylus fulvicephalus*) and [[Sisyridae]] (genus [[Sisyra]]: *S. fuscata*, *S. dalii*, *S. terminalis*).
- **Diversity:** The introduction notes the British fauna is small compared with other regions. The genus *Sialis* alone has nineteen species in the United States.
- **Morphology:** Small to medium insects with two pairs of wings held roof-like at rest. Antennae are slender and multi-segmented, and tarsi have five segments. The fourth tarsal segment is bilobed in Megaloptera but simple in Neuroptera. Adult fore-wing lengths range from 5–6 mm (Sisyridae) to 19–23 mm (*Osmylus*).
- **Larval morphology:** Larvae have tracheal gills on the first seven abdominal segments, except in *Osmylus*. Mouthparts are either biting (Sialidae) or modified into sucking "spears" (Osmylidae and Sisyridae).
- **Metamorphosis:** These orders undergo complete metamorphosis with four stages: egg, larva, pupa, and adult.
- **Common names:** The *Sialis* species are called "alderflies" or "alders" by anglers. *Sisyra* are called "sponge flies" by entomologists.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Sialis lutaria:** Common in lakes, ponds, and sluggish rivers with abundant silt. Adults are seen late April to June. Larvae are predacious and live in mud and silt for nearly two years before pupating in soil or vegetation debris. Eggs are laid in masses of 500–2000 and are often parasitised by *Trichogramma evanescens*.
- **Sialis fuliginosa:** More local and restricted to running water. Larvae are found only in streams. Adults fly May–June.
- **Osmylus fulvicephalus:** Widely distributed but local. Larvae live in wet moss at the edges of small, wooded streams and feed on chironomid larvae. Larval development covers three instars, and adults occur May–August.
- **Sisyra spp.:** Larvae are parasitic on freshwater sponges (*Spongilla lacustris* and *Ephydatia fluviatilis*). *S. fuscata* is common on lakes, canals, and streams (May–September). *S. dalii* is more local, and *S. terminalis* is local to slow streams, ponds, and canals.
- **Lake District records:** *S. lutaria* is common up to at least 2250 ft (Three Tarns, Bowfell). *O. fulvicephalus* is not common. *S. terminalis* has not been recorded there.

## Key Findings

- **Identification:** Dichotomous keys separate the six British species. *Sialis lutaria* has about eleven costal cross-veins and brownish wings (fore-wing 8–14 mm). *S. fuliginosa* has about fifteen costal cross-veins and blackish wings (fore-wing 9–17 mm). *Sisyra terminalis* is distinguished by creamy-white antennal tips.
- **Larval separation:** Larvae of *S. terminalis*, *S. dalii*, and *S. fuscata* have not been satisfactorily separated. Windermere larvae differ slightly from Withycombe's description, and larvae from the R. Brathay differ in head trichiation.
- **Courtship in Osmylidae:** The male attracts females with eversible scent glands. Pairing is prolonged, and the female consumes part of the spermatophore. This behaviour is attributed to Withycombe (1923).
- **Collecting methods:** Adults are collected by beating vegetation over water. *Sialis* larvae are sampled with pond nets or dredges. *Sisyra* larvae are found by examining sponges. *Osmylus* larvae are sought in moss along woodland streams.
- **Preservation:** Adults are pinned on stainless steel pins and set quickly, as *Sisyra* becomes brittle. Larvae and pupae require fluid preservation, such as dilute formaldehyde or Pampel's mixture.
- **Taxonomic history:** The British species were described by McLachlan (1868) and Killington (1929, 1936). Withycombe (1923) and Syms (1935) covered biology and British *Sialis*. Fraser (1959) is cited as a handbook covering all three genera.
- **Conservation note:** *Osmylus* is described as decidedly local, so collecting should target known adult localities.
