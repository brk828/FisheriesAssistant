# Draft Candidate Conservation Guidance: Prelisting Conservation of Endangered Species Act Candidates

#ichthyology #marine-biology #conservation-policy

## Executive Summary

This U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Service) document is a draft review memorandum and attached guidance on [[Candidate Conservation]], the prelisting conservation of species that are candidates for listing under the [[Endangered Species Act]]. The memoranda, dated late 1993, request comments from Regional Ecological Services offices, with responses due within 30 days of signature. The guidance aims to reduce threats to candidate species before they are listed, so that listing becomes less necessary and resources can be focused on the species facing the greatest threats. It covers the purposes, authorities, example actions, priority-setting principles, and the structure of [[Conservation Agreements]], including a sample agreement format.

The source does not address any specific fish species, habitat, or water body. Its scope is policy and administration for listed and candidate species generally. The document refers to approximately 3,600 federal candidate species.

## Background & Description

**Transmittal memoranda.** A regional memo (Region 2, Albuquerque, New Mexico) asks all Ecological Services Field Offices to review the draft and submit comments by December 3, 1993. Contacts were given as Steve Chambers and Steve Spangle. A Washington, D.C. memo from the Director to all Regional Directors, the Assistant Directors for Refuges and Wildlife and for Fisheries, and the Office of the Solicitor, describes the draft as taking "an aggressive attitude toward conservation actions for candidate species," reflecting the intentions of Secretary [[Babbitt]] and recent budget emphasis on this activity. Comments were requested within 30 days of signature.

**Guidance structure.** The draft contains the following sections:
- Introduction
- Candidate Conservation Actions (purpose; responsibilities and authorities; definitions and examples; priorities and ranking; evaluation and tracking)
- Development of Conservation Agreements (description; agreements that make listing unnecessary; elements of an agreement)
- Sample Format

**Legal basis.** Opportunities for candidate conservation are identified in Sections 6, 7, and 10 of the Act. The 1988 Amendments encouraged grants to States under Section 6. Section 7(a)(1) directs the Secretary to use existing authorities to further the Act's purposes. Section 7(a)(2) applies directly only to listed or proposed species, but protection can extend to associated candidates. Section 10 [[Habitat Conservation Plans]] often include candidate species to avoid future project delays.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The guidance frames candidate conservation as part of [[Ecosystem Management]] across a broad land base. Its stated premise is that effective ecosystem management could reduce the need to list species.

Key ecological principles include:
- Early action is cheaper and more effective. Once species are listed, ecological situations are often critical, recovery options are limited, and research and management costs are high.
- Candidate actions aim to stabilize declining species, reduce threats, and maintain ecosystems that support candidates.
- Actions should not increase threats to candidates or force accelerated listing.
- Coordination must include States, which hold broad authority over native species within their borders, including fish and wildlife on federal lands (43 CFR Part 24).

Examples of threat-reduction measures listed in the draft include habitat protection (fencing, land acquisition, access control), habitat management and restoration (stream rehabilitation, erosion control, controlled burns, water management), species management (preventing overharvest, controlling disease, increasing law enforcement, reintroducing extirpated populations), education, legal and economic incentives such as [[State Conservation Easements]], coordination, and research such as taxonomic, genetic, and habitat characterization studies.

## Key Findings

- **Purpose:** Candidate conservation seeks to make listing unnecessary, stabilize declines while other problems are addressed, or remove threats so that listing priority can shift to species facing the greatest threats. Actions must effectively remove species from candidate status to obviate listing.
- **Priority:** Category 1 candidates receive priority over Category 2 candidates. Except for Category 2 status surveys, significant prelisting spending should go first to Category 1 species.
- **Ranking criteria:** (1) degree of threat or conflict reduction; (2) efficiency of funds and staff time, including multi-species benefits, ecosystem-wide benefits, complementarity with other efforts, and cost-sharing; (3) development of techniques applicable to other species; (4) assurance of monitoring; (5) no conflict with other obligations under the Act, and compliance with other laws.
- **Conservation Agreements:** A formal, voluntary written document between the Service and another federal agency, Tribe, State, local government, or private party. It specifies actions and responsibilities for each party. It is preferred over the term MOU or MOA because those are used for many other purposes.
- **Listing-avoidance agreements:** Beginning in FY94, a special source of 50:50 cost-sharing between the Service and non-Federal entities is available for agreements that would eliminate the need to list. Favorable conditions include: habitat controlled by one or a few land managers; a good understanding of habitat-species relationships; relatively intact habitat; actions that involve refraining from harmful activity rather than restoration; and readily available staff and funding.
- **Reconsideration:** The Service retains the ability to reconsider agreements. If expected outcomes are not achieved, proposed or emergency listing may follow.
- **Mandatory agreement elements:** Explicit milestones or recovery objectives; funding mechanisms; annual review and annual reporting; termination rights, with problems escalated to the Regional Office; coordination with ES in the Regional Office; signature by the Regional Director unless delegated; careful administrative records; and monitoring to support the 1988 Amendments' requirements.
- **Reporting:** Each signed agreement should be reported in the Endangered Species Technical Bulletin. Press releases and conservation awards, including awards for private landowners, may be appropriate.
- **Evaluation:** Implementing offices must keep records of monitoring, compliance, and effects, and report highlights to the Director for annual budget justifications to Congress.
- **Sample agreement:** The template has eight parts: species involved; involved parties; authority; status and distribution; problems facing the species (the Section 4(a) factors); conservation actions; duration; and signatures, plus attachments. The boilerplate sets a 3-year term with reviews at months 12, 24, and 36, and a 1-month notice requirement for changed circumstances. If threats cannot be resolved, the species will be initiated for listing under Section 4.
