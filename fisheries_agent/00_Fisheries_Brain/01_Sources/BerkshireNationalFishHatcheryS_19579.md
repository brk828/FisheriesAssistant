## Executive Summary

This document is a fiscal year station profile (Exhibit 1) for the [[Berkshire National Fish Hatchery]], a U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service facility located in Hartsville/Great Barrington, Massachusetts, within USFWS Region 5. Established in 1916 under Congressional Authorization (39 Statute 43), the hatchery's core mission is to support the [[Connecticut River Restoration Program]] by producing fertilized [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*) eggs sourced from sea-run, domestic, and reconditioned wild broodstock. The profile documents operational funding, staffing (FTE), and production statistics for the surrounding fiscal years (1989–1993), highlighting the facility's role in anadromous fish restoration rather than presenting original morphological or ecological research data.

## Background & Description

The source text does not provide direct morphological data on *Salmo salar*. However, key life-history/broodstock categories referenced include:

- **Sea-run broodstock**: Wild Atlantic Salmon that have completed an oceanic migration and returned to freshwater to spawn.
- **Domestic broodstock**: Hatchery-reared/maintained salmon lineage used for controlled breeding.
- **Reconditioned wild broodstock (kelts)**: Post-spawning wild Atlantic Salmon ("kelts") that are captured, held, and conditioned by the hatchery to recover sufficient physiological condition for a second spawning event. In 1991, **35 reconditioned kelts** were spawned.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Species**: [[Atlantic Salmon]] (*Salmo salar*), family [[Salmonidae]].
- **Life history strategy**: Anadromous — migrates between marine (North Atlantic) and freshwater (Connecticut River system) habitats, consistent with the broodstock categories described (sea-run vs. reconditioned kelts).
- **Habitat context**: The hatchery functions as an artificial spawning and rearing environment supporting restoration stocking efforts in the [[Connecticut River]] watershed, a historically significant Atlantic Salmon system in New England subject to restoration due to population declines from damming, habitat loss, and overfishing.
- **Restoration role**: The facility exists specifically to counteract the loss of self-sustaining wild runs by supplementing the river system with hatchery-produced fertilized eggs.

## Key Findings

- **Egg production (1991)**: 35 reconditioned Atlantic Salmon kelts produced **over 325,000 fertilized eggs** for the Connecticut River Restoration Program.
- **Public engagement**: Over **3,100 visitors** were accommodated in FY1991 through tours, presentations, and a National Fishing Week open-house event.
- **Infrastructure investment**: Over **$43,000** was spent on safety corrections and rehabilitation of government quarters at the facility in FY1991.
- **Staffing**: Consistent staffing level of **3 FTE** maintained across FY1989–1993.
- **Budget trends**:
  - Operations budget rose steadily from **$104,900 (1989)** to a projected **$118,000 (1993)**.
  - Maintenance budget fluctuated significantly, from **$5,030 (1990)** to **$11,539**, then jumping to **$50,000** projected for both 1992 and 1993 — suggesting planned capital improvements or deferred maintenance catch-up.
- **Data gaps**: The FY distribution table (species stocked, numbers distributed to federal/state/private waters) was left blank in the source, indicating incomplete reporting of actual fish distribution numbers (excluding eggs and fry, which were tracked separately under "Other Information").
- **Congressional oversight**: The hatchery falls under the jurisdiction of Massachusetts's 1st Congressional District (Rep. John Olver) and Senators Edward Kennedy and John Kerry, reflecting its political/administrative context within the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] Region 5 network.
