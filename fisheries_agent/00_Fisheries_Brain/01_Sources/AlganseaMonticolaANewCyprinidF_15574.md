# Algansea monticola: A New Cyprinid Fish from the Pacific Slope of Central Mexico

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

This 1968 taxonomic paper describes [[Algansea Monticola]], a new species of small cyprinid fish from the headwaters of the Río Juchipila and Río Colotlán in [[Zacatecas]] and [[Jalisco]], Mexico. The authors, Clyde D. Barbour and Salvador Contreras, collected 630 specimens in eight series during summer 1963. The species is distinguished from all other members of its genus chiefly by having seven rather than eight or more dorsal fin rays. The paper also documents meristic and morphometric variation, sexual dimorphism in pectoral fin length, habitat use, probable spawning timing, associated fauna, and its distribution within the [[Lerma-Santiago River System]] and contiguous waters.

## Background & Description

**Discovery and type material.** The specimens were first recognized as an undescribed cyprinid and later identified as members of *Algansea*, a genus widely distributed in the Lerma-Santiago system. The holotype (Tulane University no. 40869) is a male, 69.9 mm standard length, collected 30 June 1963 from the [[Río Juchipila]] near Jalpa at the Highway 70 bridge. The paratype series (Tulane University no. 30611) contains 62 specimens with the same data and is distributed among institutions including the [[British Museum of Natural History]], the [[Field Museum of Natural History]], the [[University of Michigan Museum of Zoology]], the [[United States National Museum]], and the [[Universidad de Nuevo León]].

**Diagnosis.** A small species, maximum length about 90 mm SL. It differs from all other known *Algansea* by having 7 rather than 8 or more dorsal rays.

**General morphology.** The body is robust with a relatively deep caudal peduncle. The snout is blunt and does not overhang the upper lip. The mouth is oblique and included. Maxillary barbels are present but rarely absent. The eyes are small, and the fins are moderate and fleshy. Scales are small and embedded in small specimens. There are 6 to 11 gill rakers on the first arch and pharyngeal teeth are 4-4.

Cephalic lateral line pores were counted in 40 specimens: supratemporal pores 4–9, supraorbital 7–10, infraorbital 14–20, and preoperculomandibular 8–14. Canals were occasionally disfigured, most often with the supratemporal canal divided at the midline.

**Morphometrics.** Table 1 gives measurements for the holotype and four paratypes as thousandths of standard length, following [[Hubbs and Lagler]] (1947). The holotype had a head length of 272, a pectoral length of 207, and a pelvic length of 143. The paratype mean standard length was 63.2 mm.

**Color.** Coloration is variable but always dull. The dorsal surface is dusky grey to grey-brown, with a dark, narrow middorsal stripe from nape to caudal fin. A diffuse rust-orange band may lie dorsal to the lateral line, and a small black caudal spot is always present, though it fades in alcohol. The ventral surface is dirty white.

**Etymology.** The name *monticola* is Latin for "a dweller in the mountains," referring to the rugged terrain of the species' range.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Habitat.** The species was ubiquitous across the sampled headwaters of the Río Juchipila and Río Colotlán. It occurred over mud, sand, gravel, and rock, in open water and under banks, in swift water, and in pools and impoundments. Water temperature ranged from 19 to 27 °C. It showed no association with a particular vegetation type.

**Reproduction.** Larger females carried large eggs, and males had well-developed testes and breeding tubercles. The authors infer spawning in late June and early July. The rust-orange coloration is probably linked to spawning, though no collections were made at other times of year. Turbid water prevented behavioral observations.

**Associated fauna.** Species collected alongside *A. monticola* included:
- [[Moxostoma Congestum]] (sucker)
- [[Hybopsis Alta]] (chub)
- [[Poeciliopsis Infans]] (poeciliid)
- *Goodea* sp. (goodeid)
- [[Chaenobryttus Gulosus]] and [[Micropterus Salmoides]] (centrarchids, both introduced; taken in the reservoir south of Tepechitlán)

No catfish were taken, although the authors believed they were probably present.

**Distribution.** The species is known from the headwaters of the Río Juchipila and Río Colotlán, with a sampling locality for each tributary and reservoir indicated in Fig. 3. A collection at Moyahua de Estrada, just below 1,200 m, yielded no *A. monticola* but included [[Cyprinus Carpio]], [[Ictalurus Pricei]], and *Cichlasoma* sp. The authors therefore suggest it is absent from the lower reaches of the Juchipila. It has not been found in the Río Verde to the east, and its limits in western tributaries of the Juchipila are unknown.

## Key Findings

1. **New species described.** *Algansea monticola* is separated from congeners by its seven dorsal rays. The description is based on 630 specimens from eight collections.
2. **Closest relatives.** It most resembles [[Algansea Barbata]] from the upper [[Río Lerma]] and an undescribed species from Nayarit (R. R. Miller, manuscript). All three have barbels, few gill rakers, and small size. The authors consider the resemblance probably the result of independent evolution from a common ancestor under similar conditions, not close relationship.
3. **Sexual dimorphism.** Although sexual differences had not been reported for the genus, breeding males had noticeably longer pectoral fins than females (Fig. 2). Many males also bore minute white breeding tubercles on the head, body, pectoral fins, and dorsal and anal fins. Coloration showed no sex difference.
4. **Probable spawning season.** Late June to early July, based on egg size, gonad development, and tubercle presence.
5. **Restricted range.** The species occurs in the upper Juchipila and Colotlán but was absent from a lower Juchipila site and the Río Grande de Santiago, where earlier surveys ([[Briggs and Miller]], 1960) did not record it.
6. **Ongoing taxonomic work.** The authors noted that the eleven nominal species of *Algansea* were under study by Barbour and [[Robert R. Miller]] at the University of Michigan.
7. **Funding and permits.** Fieldwork was supported by the [[National Institutes of Health]], the Sigma Xi–RESA, the University of Utah Research Fund, and the Banco de México, with a Mexican government collecting permit.
