# Habitat Suitability Index Models: Common Carp

#ichthyology #freshwater-ecology #Cyprinidae #habitat-modeling #invasive-species

## Executive Summary

This technical report presents Habitat Suitability Index (HSI) models for [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) in riverine and lacustrine habitats across the 48 contiguous United States. Developed by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service's Habitat Evaluation Procedures Group, the models synthesize literature on carp habitat requirements into mathematical frameworks that produce indices from 0 (unsuitable) to 1 (optimal). The models incorporate variables related to food availability, cover, water quality (temperature, dissolved oxygen, pH, turbidity, salinity), and reproduction requirements. Native to Asia, the common carp is now established on every continent except Antarctica and hybridizes naturally with [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*). The species thrives in enriched, shallow, warm, sluggish, well-vegetated waters with mud or silt substrates.

## Background & Description

### Taxonomy and Distribution

The [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*) is native to Asia but has been introduced globally, now occurring in all 48 contiguous U.S. states. The northern distribution limit appears to be the 18°C isotherm. The species hybridizes naturally with [[Goldfish]] (*Carassius auratus*).

### Life History

**Age and Growth**: Fast-growing males may mature at age I, though most mature at ages II-IV in temperate climates. Females generally mature between ages III-V. Maximum reported weights are 37.4 kg (South Africa) and 42.1 kg (North America).

**Feeding Ecology**: Adults are opportunistic feeders utilizing any available food source. Fry initially feed on [[Zooplankton]], switching to [[Phytoplankton]] when zooplankton density is low. As fish grow, they consume littoral fauna, bottom fauna (worms, aquatic insect larvae), and vegetable matter (seeds, algae, detritus).

**Reproduction**: Spawning generally occurs in spring (March-June in southern climates, May-June in northern climates), though ripe males have been observed throughout summer months in temperate zones. Adults congregate and deposit adhesive eggs on aquatic or submerged terrestrial vegetation. Spawning over dense vegetation increases reproductive success. Fluctuating reservoir water levels may be detrimental to populations.

### Physical Characteristics

The species exhibits extreme environmental tolerance, surviving in conditions lethal to most other fish species.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

### Habitat Preferences

**Riverine Habitats**: Carp prefer low to moderate gradient streams with pools, backwaters, and marshes. In the [[Missouri River]], they occurred in pools and chutes (<60 cm/sec) and main channel borders (60-120 cm/sec), but were most abundant in marshes and backwaters (<20 cm/sec). Deep pools with abundant cover (logs, brush) provide feeding and resting areas in swift rivers.

**Lacustrine Habitats**: The species thrives in reservoirs, lakes, bayous, estuaries, farm ponds, and sewage lagoons. Adults are usually found in association with abundant vegetation. Waters with diversity of both shallow and deep areas represent optimum habitat.

**Seasonal Movements**: Adults spend summer and early autumn in shallow areas of dense vegetation. As temperatures drop, fish move into deeper waters for winter.

### Water Quality Requirements

**Temperature**:
- **Adults**: High production correlates with midsummer temperatures and days >20°C. Large numbers collected at 18-27°C. Optimum growth at 20-28°C under laboratory conditions. Growth decreases at <13°C and ≥30°C. Upper lethal temperature ≥34.5°C. Feeding ceases at 5°C.
- **Embryos**: Spawning temperatures 18-23°C. Species generally will not spawn at average summer temperatures <18°C. Spawning activity decreases at >26°C and stops at 28°C. Temperatures <11°C increase embryo mortality.
- **Fry**: Low temperature threshold <7°C. Larvae survive and feed at 36°C but most die at 38°C. Preferred temperature 27°C, optimum growth at 30°C.
- **Juveniles**: Optimum growth at 28-30°C. Temperature preferences 27-33.5°C. Daily food consumption greatest at 23-27°C. Temperatures ≥38°C are lethal.

**Dissolved Oxygen**:
- **Adults**: Very tolerant of low DO levels. Can feed in oxygen-depleted hypolimnion (<2 mg/l). Can gulp surface air when DO <0.5 mg/l. Respiration elevated at 3-5 mg/l (13-23°C). Should remain at least 6-7 mg/l for good growth.
- **Embryos**: Tolerant of fluctuating oxygen levels. Some survive short exposures to 1.2 mg/l (25°C). Percentage hatching increases with increasing DO: 40% at 3 mg/l, 65% at 6 mg/l, 92% at 9 mg/l.
- **Fry**: Lower lethal levels <1.6 mg/l at 21-22°C.
- **Juveniles**: Lower lethal level <1.0 mg/l (<20°C). Growth rate decreases at approximately 2.1 mg/l at 20-23°C.

**pH**: Optimum growth of freshwater fish generally occurs at pH 6.8-7.5. Growth reduced at pH <6.0, probably due to reduced food supply. pH <5.0 reported as harmful to carp. Carp common in New Mexico reservoirs with pH 8.5-8.7. pH 10.5 is lethal.

**Turbidity**: Extremely tolerant of turbidity as long as food production is not limiting. Can tolerate turbidities far above those usually found in nature. Turbidity levels >200 JTU and Secchi disc visibilities <8 cm common at spawning sites. Feeding and spawning activities over silty bottoms increase turbidity.

**Salinity**: May occupy brackish or saline waters, but production is low. In Israel, grown in ponds at 0.1-5.0 ppt. Yields decrease at 2.0-3.0 ppt. Salinity of 7.2 ppt lethal after 36 days in laboratory. Rapid change from fresh to salt water can be lethal.

### Substrate and Cover

Prefer mud or silt substrates. Require aquatic or submerged terrestrial vegetation for spawning (preferred depths <0.5 m, but spawning observed up to 1.8 m deep). Dense vegetation provides cover for all life stages and habitat for food organisms. Structural features (logs, brush, submerged objects) provide cover in pools.

### Reservoir Characteristics

Reservoir storage ratio (SR) (ratio of mean reservoir water volume to annual discharge volume) affects habitat suitability. Standing crops peak at storage ratios <0.4 and at about 1.5, declining above 2.0.

## Key Findings

### HSI Model Structure

The report presents two comprehensive HSI models:

**Riverine Model Components**:
1. **Food (CF)**: Based on % vegetative cover (V₁) and % pools/backwaters/marsh areas (V₃)
2. **Cover (CC)**: Based on % vegetative cover (V₁), % pools/backwaters/marsh areas (V₃), and % cover in pools (V₂)
3. **Water Quality (CWQ)**: Based on turbidity (V₄), temperature for adults (V₅) and juveniles/fry (V₈), dissolved oxygen for adults/juveniles/fry (V₁₂) and embryos (V₁₃), pH (V₁₄), and optional salinity (V₁₅)
4. **Reproduction (CR)**: Based on % vegetative cover (V₁), % pools/backwaters/marsh areas (V₃), spawning temperature (V₆), maximum spawning depth (V₁₀), and embryo dissolved oxygen (V₁₃)

**Lacustrine Model Components**:
1. **Food-Cover (CF-C)**: Based on % vegetative cover (V₁) and % littoral area (V₇)
2. **Water Quality (CWQ)**: Same variables as riverine model
3. **Reproduction (CR)**: Based on % vegetative cover (V₁), % littoral area (V₇), maximum depth (V₁₆), spawning temperature (V₆), and embryo dissolved oxygen (V₁₃)
4. **Other (COT)**: Based on storage ratio (V₉)

### Model Calculations

**Riverine HSI**: HSI = (CF × CC × CWQ × CR)^(1/4)

If CWQ or CR <0.4, HSI equals the lowest of: CWQ, CR, or the above equation.

**Lacustrine HSI**: HSI = (CF-C × CWQ × CR × COT)^(1/4)

If CWQ or CR <0.4, HSI equals the lowest of: CWQ, CR, or the above equation.

### Simplified Models

**Model 1 (Riverine)**: Optimal habitat characterized by: warm waters (≥20°C during growing season), low gradient rivers (<1.5 m/km), shallow vegetated marshland for spawning, structural features for cover in pools, ≥50% of river area in pools/off-channel areas, and fertile conditions. HSI = (number of criteria present)/6.

**Model 2 (Lacustrine)**: Optimal habitat characterized by: fertile conditions, warm waters (≥20°C mid-June through August), aquatic or inundated vegetation for spawning, deeper waters for overwintering, and ≥25% littoral area. HSI = (number of criteria present)/5.

### Model Application and Interpretation

- Models produce indices between 0 (unsuitable) and 1 (optimal habitat)
- HSI does not necessarily represent actual population levels but rather habitat potential
- Proper interpretation is comparative: higher HSI indicates greater potential to support fish
- Models are complex hypotheses of species-habitat relationships, not proven cause-effect relationships
- Model verification conducted using sample data sets demonstrating positive relationships to spawning success and carrying capacity
- Models applicable wherever common carp occur in 48 contiguous states
- Northern applications will never achieve HSI of 1.0 due to temperature limitations
- Model provides rating for habitat's ability to support all life stages through all seasons
- HSI = 0.0 if any reproduction-related variable indicates species cannot reproduce in evaluated habitat

### Critical Habitat Variables

The models identify several variables as critical thresholds:
- If water quality component (CWQ) or reproduction component (CR) falls below 0.4, the overall HSI is constrained
- This reflects that poor water quality or inability to reproduce successfully limits overall habitat suitability regardless of food and cover availability
- Temperature and dissolved oxygen during spawning are particularly critical for reproduction success

### Management Implications

The models are designed for:
- Impact assessment of proposed developments or management actions
- Habitat management planning
- Comparative evaluation of different habitat areas
- Identifying limiting factors in carp habitat
- Setting clear study objectives for population management

The report emphasizes that models may require modification to meet specific study objectives and encourages users to provide feedback on model performance to improve utility and effectiveness.
