# Historical Freshwater Mollusk and Trout Records from the US-Mexico Border Region

#malacology #freshwater-mollusks #unionidae #ichthyology

## Executive Summary

This document is a mixed set of items centred on historical freshwater records from the US-Mexico border region. It contains a 1993 letter from [[Robert R. Miller]] questioning a trout record from northern Chihuahua, the contents page of a 1967 issue of [[The Veliger]], and the main text of a paper by [[Dwight W. Taylor]] reviewing freshwater mollusks collected during the [[Mexican Boundary Survey]] (1849–1855 and 1891–1894). The mollusk paper is the substantive scientific content. It relocates type localities precisely and corrects earlier identifications. The letter is the only fish-related material.

## Background & Description

**Trout record letter (1993).** Miller wrote to [[W. L. Minckley]] at Arizona State University about the natural range of a *Salmo* (now *Oncorhynchus*) species near *O. mykiss* in northern Chihuahua. He noted a trout report from a stream flowing toward Janos, part of the [[Rio Casas Grandes]], which rises in mountains above 1600 m. He doubted that the report could refer to [[Gila Nigrescens]], unlike earlier "trout" reports from the [[Gila River]], which he attributed to [[Gila Robusta]]. He also noted that Needham's party had collected trout in the uppermost western tributary of the Casas Grandes, and regretted that no one had collected in the upper [[Mimbres River]] in the 1850s.

**Contents page (The Veliger, Vol. 10, 1967).** The issue lists eight papers, mostly on marine gastropods (Muricidae, Cypraeidae, Nudibranchia, Tonnacea) and one on freshwater mollusks. Its single freshwater paper is Taylor's review.

**Boundary survey mollusk review (Taylor, 1967).** Taylor reviewed collections from two boundary commissions. The first (1849–1855) had never been treated as a group before. [[Dr. T. H. Webb]] collected many of the specimens, and [[George Thurber]] collected others as botanist. Taylor relied on [[John R. Bartlett]]'s 1854 narrative for precise relocation and cited collections at the [[Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia]], [[Museum of Comparative Zoology]], and [[United States National Museum]]. Localities are given in the [[International Map of the World]] quadrangle system (e.g., NH 14-5 C-5).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The localities span a range of freshwater habitats:

- **Llano River, Texas:** Type locality of two unionid mussels, collected October 18, 1850, near present-day Mason County.
- **Comanche Creek, Texas:** Type locality of a subspecies of *Anodonta imbecillis*, near the present site of Mason.
- **Pecos River, Texas:** Collection site of *Physa humerosa*, with Horse-head Crossing a plausible locality.
- **Colorado Desert (Cienega Grande), Baja California and California:** Type locality of *Biomphalaria gracilenta*. Webb was the likely collector.
- **Gila River near San Diego (Arizona):** Source of *Physa virgata*. The name refers to a Gila River site, not the California city.
- **Stream near Janos, Chihuahua (August 6, 1852):** Bartlett described a clear stream 30–40 feet wide, expanding into ponds, that is a branch of the Casas Grandes River. The party caught a catch sufficient for a meal (the taxon word is garbled in the scan) and collected abundant unionid mussels.
- **Ojo Caliente, Chihuahua:** A spring about 150 yards from a rocky hill, with a pool about 120 feet in circuit and 3–4 feet deep. Type locality of *Cochliopa chihuahua*, described by [[Henry A. Pilsbry]] (1928) from four shells on a herbarium sheet of *Myriophyllum heterophyllum*.

## Key Findings

- **Trout range (Miller, 1993):** Miller questioned a northern Chihuahua trout record from the Rio Casas Grandes headwaters and doubted it referred to *Gila nigrescens*. He sought Minckley's opinion. The letter gives no resolution.
- **Relocated type localities:** Taylor relocated at least seven molluscan type localities from Bartlett's 1854 account. Several are listed above.
- **Corrected identifications:** Taylor's review corrects earlier identifications and the erroneous localities in Dall's 1896 report on the second survey's collections.
- **Type material:** Johnson (1964) illustrated several types. Taylor examined a lectotype of *Physa virgata* at MCZ and a series of paratypes at USNM, but not the ANSP specimen identified by Baker (1964) as the holotype.
- **Unrecorded collections:** Bartlett's Janos entry is the only mention of mollusk collecting on that stretch. The specimens appear never to have been reported in the scientific literature.
- **Historical value:** The review supports historical biogeography and nomenclature by linking old collections to precise, modern-findable locations.
