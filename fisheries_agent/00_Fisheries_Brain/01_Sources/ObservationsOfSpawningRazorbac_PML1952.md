# Riverine Spawning of Razorback Sucker in the Lower Colorado River

#ichthyology #marine-biology #xyrauchen-texanus

## Executive Summary

This 1989 note in *The Southwestern Naturalist* (Vol. 34, No. 1) reports the first detailed observations of [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) spawning in riverine habitat of the [[Lower Colorado River]], downstream of [[Hoover Dam]]. Earlier spawning studies had focused on reservoir populations, and riverine spawning had been suspected but not confirmed by egg or larval collections. Divers using [[Scuba Surveys]] and [[Surber Sampling]] documented a spawning site of about 300 m² where spawning activity had formed substrate depressions, and recovered eggs and larvae from them. The author concludes that razorback suckers can spawn in flowing water below major impoundments, which may resemble the structural habitats the species used before river regulation.

## Background & Description

- **Species status:** Once common throughout the [[Colorado River]] drainage, the razorback sucker is now rare (Holden and Stalnaker 1975; Minckley 1983).
- **Recovery effort:** The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] and the [[Arizona Game and Fish Department]] are working to recover the species in the lower basin through hatchery stocking under a cooperative agreement.
- **Prior knowledge gap:** The species is generally considered riverine, yet lower-basin spawning studies focused on reservoir populations (Ulmer 1980; Minckley 1983; Marsh and Langhorst, in press). Riverine spawning was suspected (Minckley and Carothers 1979; Minckley 1983) but had not been substantiated by egg or larval collections.
- **Barriers to study:** Species scarcity, logistical difficulty of underwater work, and poor water visibility had limited information on riverine habitat use.
- **Initial observations:** In 1983 and 1984, razorback suckers were seen spawning in the Colorado River downstream of Hoover Dam. Later evidence of spawning was also found downstream of [[Davis Dam]], which impounds [[Lake Mohave]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Survey area:** On 28 January 1984, divers snorkeled a 6-km river section downstream of Hoover Dam that was thought to contain likely spawning areas, including coves, hot spring areas, and shoreline portions. Suckers were seen in several places, but spawning-related disturbance was found at only one location.
- **Spawning site:** Activity was confined to about 300 m² of scoured sands and gravels deposited in the channel from a dry wash about 4.8 km downstream of Hoover Dam. Substrate here differed from the larger armored material or fine sands found elsewhere.
- **Channel conditions:** Channel width downstream of Hoover Dam often exceeds 15 m.
- **Depth and flow:**
  - Depressions occurred at depths of 2.0 to 7.2 m.
  - Mean water velocities (Marsh McBirney flow meter) ranged from 0.00 to 0.37 m/s.
  - Nose velocities (10 cm above substrate) at five spawning sites averaged 0.15 m/s (range 0.12 to 0.18 m/s).
- **Substrate modification:** Spawning activity flushed finer material, leaving [[Spawning Depressions]] lined with larger gravel and cobble.

## Key Findings

**Survey effort and site characteristics**
- Scuba dives over the spawning area were conducted on 11 February, 25 February, and 11 March 1984, with 1 to 37 suckers observed per occasion.
- Eighteen depressions measuring 0.25 to 1 m² were identified within the spawning area.
- Spawning was observed repeatedly and was vigorous enough to produce obvious depressions.

**Egg and larval sampling**
- 23 Surber samples yielded 103 eggs and 34 larvae. Eggs and larvae were collected in January and February, but none in March.
- Samples taken directly from depressions (n = 19) contained 10.5 to 16.7 eggs/m² and 0.7 to 5.7 larvae/m².
- Samples from finer material outside depressions (n = 3) contained no eggs or larvae.
- One sample from large-cobble substrate immediately downstream of the site contained 52 eggs (273.7 eggs/m²) and 21 larvae (110.5 larvae/m²). The author attributes this to drift.

**Fecundity and reproductive strategy**
- Fecundity averages nearly 1,900 ova per cm standard length (OCR-uncertain value), and most females exceed 50 cm standard length, yielding about 100,000 ova per female (Minckley 1983).
- Given the maximum observed density of 274 eggs/m², females appear to release only a few eggs at a time.

**Spawning behavior**
- Behavior resembled that reported for reservoir populations, with some differences.
- Most fish seen were "small" males (about 40 cm total length) that held stationary positions at the downstream end of the site. Reservoir males are usually more mobile.
- Larger fish, presumably females, moved into the site from the adjacent river and attracted males to form spawning groups of three to eight fish, typically one female with several males.
- Spawning acts lasted only a few seconds. Groups sometimes sheltered downstream of large boulders and rolled together for several seconds.

**Conclusions**
- Razorback suckers can and do spawn in lotic environments downstream of major impoundments.
- Riverine spawning conditions may more closely resemble the structural habitats the species used before human alteration of the [[Colorado River]].
