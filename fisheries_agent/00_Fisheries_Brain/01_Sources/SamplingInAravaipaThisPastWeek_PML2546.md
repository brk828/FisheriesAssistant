# Email Report: Fish Resampling in Aravaipa Creek (November 1997)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae

## Executive Summary

An email dated 10 November 1997 from [[Peter Unmack]] to [[W.L. Minckley]] (copied to several colleagues, including [[Paul C. Marsh]], [[Brian Baltzly]], and [[Robert Clarkson]]) reports a weekend resampling of the lower end of [[Aravaipa Creek]]. Two reaches were sampled. The main findings were that [[Red Shiner]] are relatively common there, that only young-of-year (YOY) red shiners were collected (largest about 4 cm), and that non-native fishes were preserved, some without being counted, so their exact numbers are uncertain.

## Background & Description

- **Author and purpose:** Peter Unmack reports on a resampling trip to Aravaipa on Sunday, 9 November 1997, to resample parts of the lower creek.
- **Sampling reaches:**
  - Reach 1: from the TNC/BLM boundary downstream for about 250 m. Field crew listed as Unmack and Baltzly.
  - Reach 2: lowermost public access, roughly where the paved road ends, sample length about 250 m. Field crew listed as Unmack, Baltzly, J. Burton, and H. Burton.
- **Taxa recorded:** Eight genera were identified in Reach 1 and nine in total across both reaches, including [[Agosia]], [[Cyprinella]], [[Gambusia]], [[Pantosteus]], [[Catostomus]], [[Tiaroga]], [[Lepomis]], [[Meda]], and [[Ameiurus]].
- **Sample handling:** All non-native fish were preserved. Some were not counted before preservation, so their numbers are uncertain.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The email is a field note and does not describe habitat measurements, water chemistry, or methods beyond the reach lengths and general sampling. It places the work in the lower reach of Aravaipa Creek, near the [[The Nature Conservancy]] / [[Bureau of Land Management]] boundary. Taxa are described as native or non-native only in general terms. The email does not specify which genera are non-native, so the native/non-native status of each taxon cannot be confirmed from this text.

## Key Findings

- **Red shiner abundance:** Red shiners are "relatively common" in the lower reach.
- **Age structure:** Only young-of-year red shiners were collected, the largest about 4 cm.
- **Reach 1 (TNC/BLM boundary downstream, about 250 m) counts, as legible in the OCR:**
  - Agosia: 120
  - Cyprinella: about 20 (OCR uncertain)
  - Meda: about 18 (OCR uncertain)
  - Catostomus: about 10 (OCR uncertain)
  - Lepomis: about 8 (OCR uncertain)
  - Pantosteus: about 6 (OCR uncertain)
  - Ameiurus: about 3
  - Tiaroga: about 3
- **Reach 2 (lowermost public access, about 250 m) counts, as legible in the OCR:**
  - Agosia: 120
  - Cyprinella: 10
  - Gambusia: 5
  - Remaining taxa (Pantosteus, Catostomus, Tiaroga): low counts of 3, 1, and 1, though the OCR does not clearly assign these numbers to taxa.
- **Data quality caveat:** Some non-native fish were preserved before being counted, so their totals are uncertain.
