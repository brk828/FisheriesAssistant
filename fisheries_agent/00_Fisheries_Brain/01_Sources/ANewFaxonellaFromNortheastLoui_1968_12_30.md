# A New Faxonella from Northeast Louisiana (Decapoda, Astacidae)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #Cambaridae #Astacidae #Decapoda #crayfish-taxonomy

## Executive Summary

This 1968 paper by Jerry G. Walls describes **Faxonella creaseri**, a new species of crayfish (Decapoda, Astacidae) discovered in Caldwell Parish, northeast Louisiana. Prior to this description, the genus [[Faxonella]] (erected by Creaser, 1933; diagnosed by Fitzpatrick, 1963) contained only two recognized species: *F. clypeata* (Hay, 1899) and *F. beyeri* (Penn, 1950). The new species is morphologically intermediate between these two congeners and, like *F. beyeri*, is endemic to Louisiana. The description is based on 32 specimens collected from temporary roadside pools, with holotype, allotype, and morphotype deposited in the United States National Museum. The species is named in honor of Prof. Edwin P. Creaser, who rediscovered *F. clypeata* and first distinguished *Faxonella* as a genus separate from *Faxonius* (= *Orconectes*).

## Background & Description

**Diagnostic Features:**
- Lateral rostral spines and cervical spines **absent**
- Rostrum short and broad (≤1.5× as long as broad)
- Chela and carapace shape similar to *[[Faxonella Clypeata]]* and *[[Faxonella Beyeri]]*
- Areola: 25.9–33.1% of carapace length (average 30.0%); 2.3–5.3× as long as broad (average 3.8×) — greatest source of intraspecific variation
- Males possess copulatory hooks on **third pereiopods only** (acute in form I, blunt in form II)

**First Pleopod (Form I Male) — Key Taxonomic Character:**
- Terminates in two rami: a long, flattened central projection bent mesially, and a shorter mesial process (~half the length of the central projection), removed from and running parallel to the central projection
- In situ, terminal elements of the left pleopod overlap those of the right
- Distinctive among *Faxonella* species: mesial processes overlap **both each other and the central projections**

**Annulus Ventralis (Female):**
- Immovable, subovate, with U-shaped depression between cephalic tubercles
- Anterior margin bears two conspicuous tubercles (larger one strongly excavated caudally), not meeting at midline

**Sexual Dimorphism:**
- Allotypic female: chela proportionately shorter, broader, more depressed; fingers stouter with distinct tubercles, gaping narrowly
- Areola wider in females (3.9× as long as wide) with 4–6 punctations in narrowest part (vs. 2–3 in holotype male)
- Morphotype (Form II male): mesial process reduced to ~1/4 length of central projection; some individuals show further shrinkage

**Measurements (mm):** Holotype carapace length 14.5, Allotype 18.1, Morphotype 14.0. Overall carapace length range: males 13.5–17.6 (avg. 15.8); females 12.8–18.6 (avg. 15.0).

**Coloration:**
- Body translucent, pale brown with darker specks
- Paired grayish-brown dorsal stripes from behind eyes to telson tip, bordered laterally on abdomen by vivid reddish-brown stripes
- Carapace laterally grayish, nearly white
- Chela tinged light orange; some specimens show reddish-brown patch on cephalolateral carapace

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Exclusively temporary pools in roadside ditches and culverts; vegetation sparse where present (roadside grasses only)
- **Geographic range:** Restricted to Caldwell Parish, northeast Louisiana; found near the eastern edge of the trans-Mississippian range of *[[Faxonella Clypeata]]*
- **Sympatric/Associated species:**
  - *[[Procambarus Acutus]]* (Girard, 1852)
  - *[[Procambarus Tulanei]]* (Penn, 1953)
  - An undescribed subspecies of *[[Cambarus Hedgpethi]]* (Hobbs, 1948)
- **Biogeographic pattern:** *F. creaseri* and *[[Faxonella Beyeri]]* occupy mirrored, isolated distributions at similar latitudes flanking the range of *F. clypeata* (*F. beyeri* to the west, *F. creaseri* to the east), with no observed intergradation despite range proximity
- Considered the **only Faxonella species** present within its known range; both *F. creaseri* and *F. beyeri* are described as common within their respective ranges despite their isolated distributions

## Key Findings

- **Third species** described for the genus *[[Faxonella]]*, expanding it from two to three recognized species
- Morphologically **intermediate** between the two prior species: shares a long central projection of the first pleopod with *[[Faxonella Clypeata]]*, but shares a long, parallel mesial process with *[[Faxonella Beyeri]]*
- Uniquely distinguished by overlapping mesial processes that cross **both** each other and the central projections — a character not seen in either congener
- No evidence of intergradation with *F. clypeata* despite geographic proximity, though the authors note the possibility remains open
- Type series comprises 32 paratypes plus holotype (Form I male, USNM 129215), allotype (female, USNM 129216), and morphotype (Form II male, USNM 129214), with additional paratypes distributed to Tulane University, Museum of Comparative Zoology, Instituto de Biología (Mexico), Australian Museum (Sydney), and private collections
- Etymology honors Prof. Edwin P. Creaser for his historical contributions to establishing *[[Faxonella]]* as distinct from *Faxonius* (= *[[Orconectes]]*)
