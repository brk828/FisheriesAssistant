# Systematic Status, Variation and Distribution of Four Middle American Cichlid Fishes of the Amphilophus Species Group, Genus Cichlasoma

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cichlidae

## Executive Summary

This 1975 systematic revision by [[William A. Bussing]] and Michael Martin, published in [[Contributions in Science]] by the [[Natural History Museum of Los Angeles County]], clarifies the status of four Central American cichlids of the [[Longimanus Complex]] within the [[Amphilophus Group]]. The authors place *Cichlasoma popenoei* in the synonymy of *C. longimanus*, retain *C. margaritiferum* as a valid species known only from its holotype, and provide a key to all four species. They document geographic variation in meristic, morphometric and colour traits without naming subspecies, and propose that the four species evolved from a single widespread ancestor that was subdivided by Tertiary marine barriers. Competition is presented as a main driver of their present, largely non-overlapping distributions.

## Background & Description

The paper addresses a long-standing problem in Middle American cichlid taxonomy. [[Regan's Classification of Cichlasoma]] (1905; 1906–08) remains the only comprehensive treatment, and its keys often fail when applied to wide-ranging species. The authors follow [[Miller's Classification]] (1966) in assigning the longimanus complex to the Amphilophus group, since Amphilophus Agassiz has priority over Astatheros Pellegrin.

Diagnostic traits of the group include a produced snout, a straight upper snout profile, a maxilla not extending beyond the anterior eye margin, long pectoral fins, and five to nine cross bars on the body. The longimanus complex adds a large lateral blotch above the pectoral fin tip, a smaller caudal spot, spotted soft dorsal and anal fins, and often blue head spots.

Four species are compared:
- ***C. robertsoni*** (Atlantic lowlands, Veracruz to Honduras): truncate or rounded caudal fin, 8–9 body bars, anal spines modally 6.
- ***C. rostratum*** (Atlantic slope of Nicaragua and Costa Rica): emarginate caudal fin, seven bars, most distinctive by its pale-spotted caudal and soft fins and the largest gill raker count.
- ***C. longimanus*** (wide range, Atlantic and Pacific slopes): 7–8 bars, checkerboard soft dorsal fin, dorsal fin edged in red or orange in many populations.
- ***C. margaritiferum***: known only from the holotype (BMNH 1864-1-26-62), probably from the Atlantic slope of Guatemala or British Honduras.

Sexual dimorphism is assessed mainly through the shape of the [[Urogenital Papilla]], which is conical and pointed in males and broad and scalloped in females. Maturity is reached near 50–60 mm SL for coloration, and males often exceed 140 mm SL.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Distribution.** *C. robertsoni* occurs from the Rio Coatzacoalcos drainage (Veracruz) to Honduras, from sea level to 160 m in the Rio Ulua. *C. rostratum* is restricted to Atlantic drainages of Nicaragua and Costa Rica, from near sea level at Tortuguero to 135 m at Laguna de Masaya. *C. longimanus* spans the Atlantic slope from the Rio Aguan (Honduras) to the Rio Prinzapolka (Nicaragua), and the Pacific slope from Guatemala to the Rio Bebedero (Costa Rica), at 10–850 m. Its Pacific records are sparse, which the authors attribute mainly to limited collecting.

**Habitat and ecology.** *C. robertsoni* is a sluggish river and backwater species, common in leaf detritus and over silt-mud, and it inhabits rocky streams at higher elevation. Water temperatures ranged from 21 to 31 °C in Honduras. *C. rostratum* is associated with leaf and detritus bottoms and river mouths in Lago de Nicaragua, and it favours running water. *C. longimanus* occurs in main river channels and in warm, low-oxygen backwaters, and is most abundant over soft mud in Lago de Nicaragua. Where the two are sympatric they are ecologically separated.

**Diet.** Roughly 50 percent of the gut contents of [[Cichlasoma Rostratum]] were aquatic insect larvae, and most of the rest was detritus and silt.

**Biogeography.** The authors propose that the Chapayal Basin, the Amatique Embayment and the Nicaraguan Depression isolated segments of Middle America during the Middle and Upper Tertiary. The Pacific populations of *C. longimanus* may have reached the Atlantic slope by stream capture between the Rio Choluteca and Rio Patuca. [[Martin's Biogeographic Analysis]] (1972) is cited as a key prior source for this framework.

**Competition.** The near-mutual exclusion of the three species suggests strong competition. *C. robertsoni* and *C. longimanus* occur in adjacent Honduran basins without being recorded together.

## Key Findings

- **Taxonomy:** *Cichlasoma popenoei* Carr and Giovannoli (1950) is a junior synonym of *C. longimanus*. *C. margaritiferum* is retained as valid, though its status rests on one specimen.
- **Material examined:** 1,464 *C. longimanus*, 222 *C. rostratum* and 776 *C. robertsoni*, plus the holotype of *C. popenoei* and the holotype of *C. margaritiferum*.
- **Populations:** Specimens were grouped into six geographic units (A–F). Populations A and B are *robertsoni*, C–E are *longimanus* (Atlantic and Pacific), and F is an allopatric Great Lakes *longimanus* population.
- **Meristic means (dorsal spines / dorsal soft rays / anal spines / pectoral rays):**
  - *robertsoni*: 16.1 / 11.0 / 6.1 / 15.0
  - *rostratum*: 16.3 / 11.7 / 6.9 / 16.0
  - *longimanus*: 16.1 / 10.2 / 7.0 / 15.1
- **Gill rakers:** *rostratum* averages 19.3, versus 17.5 in *longimanus* and 17.8 in *robertsoni*.
- **Vertebrae:** *rostratum* 28–30 (mode 29), *robertsoni* 29–30 (mode 29), *longimanus* 27–29 (mode 28).
- **Key diagnostic characters:** The number of body bars (8–9 in *robertsoni*, 7 in *rostratum*) and the shape of the third bar (Y-shaped in *rostratum* and *longimanus*) separate the species. Fin spotting and the caudal fin margin also separate them.
- **Morphometrics:** Orbit diameter, caudal fin length, interorbital distance, dorsal base length, caudal peduncle depth and predorsal distance each differ consistently in at least some comparisons and distinguish the species.
- **Origin hypothesis:** The authors propose that the four species evolved from one ancestral form. *C. robertsoni* arose north of the Chapayal Basin, *C. rostratum* south of the Nicaraguan Depression, and *C. longimanus* between them. Gene exchange between Populations E and F through northern Costa Rica is considered probable. A former connection between F and C is suggested by meristic similarity.
- **Thorichthys link:** *C. margaritiferum* shares two traits unique to the Amphilophus group with the [[Thorichthys Group]] (five mandibular sensory pores and a subopercular blotch). The authors suggest Amphilophus gave rise to Thorichthys.
- **Conservation note:** If *C. margaritiferum* is not rediscovered, it may have become extinct over 110 years after its holotype was collected.
