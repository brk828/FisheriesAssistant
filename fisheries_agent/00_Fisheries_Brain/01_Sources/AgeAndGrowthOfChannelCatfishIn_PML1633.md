# Age and Growth of Channel Catfish in the Coachella Canal, California

#ichthyology #marine-biology #ictaluridae

## Executive Summary

This study examines the age, growth, length-weight relationship, and condition factor of [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*) in the [[Coachella Canal]], a large earthen canal in southeastern California. Fish ranged from 75 to 575 mm total length and from age 0 to 5 years. Average annual growth was 73 to 123 mm. The authors conclude that the canal supports a healthy, reproducing population of fast-growing catfish, and that the canal's habitat limitations should not prevent its management as a recreational fishery.

## Background & Description

[[Channel Catfish]] were introduced into the [[Lower Colorado River]] between 1912 and 1919 (the source gives "1g92-1g99", which is most likely a scan error for 1892–1899 and should be checked against the original). The species has since spread to nearly all suitable habitats in the region, including [[Arid-Region Canals]]. It supports an important [[Sport Fishery]] in the Southwest, but local life-history data were scarce. The authors place their work within a broader effort to study the aquatic fauna of arid-region canals.

**Methods (summary):**
- **Collection:** Fish were taken by [[Rotenone Sampling]] in the Coachella Canal in November 1980. Total length (TL, mm) and weight (nearest 0.1 g) were recorded, and specimens were preserved in 10% formalin.
- **Ageing:** Left pectoral spines were removed, dried, and sectioned using the [[Pectoral Spine Sectioning]] method (after Turner 1977). Sections were examined under ethanol at 10× magnification and annuli were measured with an ocular micrometer.
- **Back-calculation:** Lengths at each annulus were back-calculated with a [[Back-Calculation]] formula based on the spine-diameter to length regression.
- **Statistics:** Length-weight and length-spine diameter relationships were fitted as simple linear regressions. [[Condition Factor]] (K) was calculated as W × 10⁵ / TL³ (after Ricker 1975).

The work was published in the *Journal of the Arizona-Nevada Academy of Science* (vol. 17, 1982). Fish collection was supported by the [[U.S. Bureau of Reclamation]], and permits and assistance came from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** The Coachella Canal is an earthen, arid-region waterway. The authors note that its habitat constraints are "apparent" and do not preclude fishery management.
- **Invertebrate and food context:** The source cites prior studies of [[Benthic Invertebrates]] in the canal and of channel catfish food habits in the same system ([[Marsh 1981]]), which indicate a substantial invertebrate fauna. The original text is partly garbled here, so the exact findings should be verified.
- **Regional comparison:** Growth rates in the canal were similar to those of other southwestern populations but generally higher than those reported from outside the region. Comparative sites include the [[Colorado River]], [[Lake Havasu]], [[Salt River]] (Arizona), [[Eucha Lake]] and Little River (Oklahoma), Fort Gibson Reservoir, and the [[Mississippi River]].

## Key Findings

**Sample and size structure**
- The sample comprised **147 channel catfish** (the scanned text reads "r47", so confirm this figure).
- Total length ranged from **75–575 mm**, and age from **0–5 years**.
- The length-frequency distribution (Figure 1) was divided into age groups based on spine sections.

**Growth**
- Average annual growth increments were **73–123 mm**. The abstract reports this range, and the OCR of Table 1 is partly illegible.
- Growth was similar to other southwestern populations and generally greater than populations outside the region ([[Age and Growth]] comparison in Table 2).
- Table 2 lists comparative mean total lengths at consecutive annuli for [[Coachella Canal]] and eight other study areas. Many values are illegible in the scan and need checking against the original.

**Length-weight and condition**
- A simple linear length-weight regression was fitted, with a slope of approximately **2.96** and an intercept of approximately **−5.06** (Table 2, Present Study). See [[Length-Weight Relationship]].
- Mean [[Condition Factor]] (K) was similar to values previously reported for channel catfish elsewhere, and the length-weight relationship also resembled that of other populations.

**Management implications**
- The canal supports a **healthy, reproducing population** of rapidly growing catfish, including young-of-year fish.
- Habitat constraints in arid-region canals should not prevent these systems from being managed as recreational fisheries.
