## Executive Summary

This document is not a primary research paper on fish biology but an **institutional/regulatory communication** from Mexico's Instituto Nacional de Ecología (INE), dated 1994, inviting a technical-scientific advisory committee (**COCTECERC**) to convene. The purpose of this committee is to review and continually update Mexico's official list of at-risk flora and fauna species — including fish — established under **[[NOM-059-ECOL-1994]]**, and to serve as Mexico's Scientific Authority to **[[CITES]]** (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora). While the document does not present original ichthyological data (no species accounts, morphometrics, or population data are given), it is highly relevant to fish conservation because it establishes the **administrative and scientific governance structure** through which Mexican fish species are formally classified as rare, threatened, endangered, or under special protection, and through which CITES trade-listing proposals for Mexican fish are vetted.

## Background & Description

The letter, issued by the Dirección General de Aprovechamiento Ecológico de los Recursos Naturales (INE-SEDESOL), invites Dr. Francisco Abarca (Arizona Game and Fish Department) to a meeting on 8 July 1994 to review a formal proposal for creating the **Comité Consultivo Técnico Científico para la Actualización de los Listados de Especies de Flora y Fauna Silvestres Mexicanas en Riesgo y de la CITES (COCTECERC)**.

Key background points:
- On **16 May 1994**, Mexico published the **[[NOM-059-ECOL-1994]]** in the Diario Oficial de la Federación, replacing an earlier 1991 listing framework. This norm classifies terrestrial and aquatic wild species (flora and fauna) into four risk categories: *en peligro de extinción* (endangered), *amenazada* (threatened), *rara* (rare), and *sujeta a protección especial* (special protection).
- Mexico joined **[[CITES]]** in July 1991, adhering to the treaty established in Washington, D.C. on 3 March 1973, and submitted an initial species list based on the 1991 domestic listing.
- The document traces the history of species-risk listing efforts in Mexico back to 1983, under the then-Subsecretaría de Ecología, involving consultation with academic institutions (UNAM, IPN) and government agencies (SARH, SEPESCA).
- Drivers cited for updating listings include habitat degradation (e.g., the **Presa de Zimapán** dam, 1992; the **Bahía Magdalena thermoelectric plant**, 1992; and an 80,000-hectare land purchase by Mitsubishi in Bahía Magdalena for tourism development in 1988), documented population declines (citing Ehrlich & Ehrlich 1981; Wilson 1988; Dorst 1972; Navarro 1991), and illegal wildlife trafficking (González 1983 unpublished; Koopowitz & Kayo 1983).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Although no specific aquatic habitat or fish population data are presented, the document is directly relevant to **[[Aquatic Conservation Policy]]** in Mexico because:

- It explicitly recognizes **"flora y fauna silvestres terrestres y acuáticas"** (terrestrial and aquatic wildlife) as subject to risk classification, meaning fish and aquatic invertebrates fall under this regulatory umbrella alongside terrestrial taxa.
- Habitat modification is identified as a primary driver of species risk status change — the cited case studies (Zimapán Dam, Bahía Magdalena thermoelectric plant, and coastal tourism development) represent direct anthropogenic pressures on **freshwater (riverine/dam-impacted)** and **coastal marine/estuarine** ecosystems, both of which support Mexican ichthyofauna.
- The Committee structure explicitly assigns a dedicated **"peces" (fish) specialist subgroup**, situated within the broader Fauna Working Group alongside mammals, birds, herpetofauna (amphibians/reptiles), and invertebrates — indicating formal institutional recognition of fish as a taxon requiring dedicated expert review distinct from other vertebrate groups.
- The proposed committee is tasked with maintaining a **species-at-risk database** in coordination with **[[CONABIO]]** (Comisión Nacional para el Uso y Conservación de la Biodiversidad), which would include aquatic species records.

## Key Findings

- **Regulatory instrument**: [[NOM-059-ECOL-1994]] (published 16 May 1994) is the operative Mexican legal norm classifying at-risk terrestrial and aquatic species into four categories (endangered, threatened, rare, special protection), superseding the 1991 listing.
- **International linkage**: Mexico's CITES accession (July 1991) requires a parallel, continuously updated national species list for the Convention's Appendices I–III, with Appendix IV containing the treaty's foundational trade-permit principles.
- **Institutional structure proposed (COCTECERC)**:
  - **Coordinación General** (led by INE's Dirección General de Aprovechamiento Ecológico de los Recursos Naturales)
  - **Comisión Permanente** (11 members: 1 President, 1 Secretary, 9 vocales/members) representing government agencies (proposed: CONABIO, SEPESCA/INPESCA, SARH/INIF, CONACyT/SNI), academic institutions (UNAM, IPN, UAM, UAEM, UACH, ITESM), and civil associations (PRONATURA, DUMAC, Amigos de Sian Ka'an, BITMAR, CES, PROFAUNA, ECOSFERA, among others)
  - **Grupos de Trabajo** (2 groups: Flora and Fauna, ~5 members each)
  - **Subgrupos de Trabajo**, organized by taxonomic specialty — for fauna, this explicitly includes dedicated subgroups for **mammals, birds, herpetofauna (amphibians and reptiles), fish (peces), and invertebrates** (9-member subgroups: 1 President, 1 Secretary, 7 vocales)
- **Meeting cadence proposed**: Subgroups meet every 4 months; Working Groups meet quarterly; the Permanent Commission meets every 6 months, feeding into an annual publication cycle for updated risk listings.
- **Case studies cited as evidence of habitat pressure** relevant to aquatic systems: Zimapán Dam (1992), Bahía Magdalena thermoelectric plant (1992), and the 80,000-hectare Mitsubishi tourism land acquisition in Bahía Magdalena (1988) — all illustrating anthropogenic pressures on freshwater and coastal marine habitats that could affect **[[Ichthyofauna of Mexico]]**.
- **Funding and incentives**: The proposal recommends annual financial stipends/incentives, travel and lodging support, and formal recognition certificates for committee members, contingent on federal budget allocation.
- **Formal adoption mechanism**: The Committee's legal validity is to be established via an official Acuerdo published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación, mirroring the process used for NOM-059-ECOL-1994 itself.

**Note on scope**: This source contains no direct taxonomic, morphological, or population-level data on specific fish species. Its value for ichthyological research lies in documenting the **policy and governance architecture** underpinning Mexican fish conservation status assessments during the 1990s, and should be cross-referenced with the actual [[NOM-059-ECOL-1994]] species list for taxon-specific risk determinations.
