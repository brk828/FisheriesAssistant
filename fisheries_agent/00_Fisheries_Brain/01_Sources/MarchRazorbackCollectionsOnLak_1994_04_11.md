# March Razorback Collections on Lake Mohave

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Catostomidae

## Executive Summary
This set of internal memoranda from the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Parker Fisheries Resource Office) documents field operations conducted between 14 March and 9 April 1994 to collect, tag, and spawn adult [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*) on [[Lake Mohave]], a Colorado River impoundment straddling Arizona and Nevada. Efforts combined adult population sampling (electroshocking, trammel netting), larval dip-netting surveys, and artificial propagation to supply gametes to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]]. Across the reporting period, 257 adult razorback suckers were captured during two dedicated survey weeks, wild larval recruitment was consistently low, and 20 successful pairings yielded an estimated 105,000 eggs delivered to the hatchery, with early fungal infection issues in egg incubation successfully managed via chemical treatment.

## Background & Description
- **Species**: [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), family [[Catostomidae]] — a large-bodied catostomid distinguished by a pronounced dorsal keel ("razorback"), used here operationally for sex determination and PIT-tag identification rather than detailed morphometric description.
- **Sex ratio data (Week 1, 14–18 March)**: 105 adults — 49 males (47%), 54 females (51%), 6 undetermined (2%).
- **Sex ratio data (Week 2, 20–26 March)**: 152 adults — 81 males (53%), 69 females (45%), 2 undetermined (2%).
- **Recapture rates**: 18% (19/105) in Week 1 vs. 30% (46/152) in Week 2, suggesting increasing encounter overlap with a previously PIT-tagged population as sampling progressed.
- **Bycatch**: Non-target [[Largemouth Bass]] (4 individuals) captured incidentally during trammel netting at Cottonwood Landing on 8 April, indicating sympatric occurrence of introduced piscivores in razorback habitat.
- **Larval stage**: Wild-collected razorback sucker larvae were dip-netted in shallow backwaters (e.g., Owl Cove, Tequila Cove, Yuma Cove), with catch highly variable — from single digits to over 1,000 individuals in one exceptional nighttime effort at Tequila Cove (31 March).

## Ecological & Environmental Context
- **Habitat use**: Adult razorback suckers were concentrated in specific reservoir reaches (river miles 25–40) of [[Lake Mohave]], with collection effort focused on backwater coves (Owl Cove, Tequila Cove, Yuma Cove) that appear to serve as nursery/larval habitat.
- **Reproductive behavior**: Spawning aggregations were targeted directly via electroshocking and trammel netting for artificial propagation, indicating these coves and berm/cut-bank areas function as spawning or staging grounds during the March–April reproductive window.
- **Larval recruitment**: Extremely low and inconsistent wild larval catch (fewer than 100 in Week 1, fewer than 30 in Week 2) despite nightly sampling effort, highlighting a persistent recruitment bottleneck consistent with known conservation concerns for this species — likely driven by predation from non-native fishes such as [[Largemouth Bass]] co-occurring in the same habitat.
- **Interagency habitat management context**: The operation involved coordinated multi-agency effort (Bureau of Land Management, Bureau of Reclamation, [[Cibola National Wildlife Refuge]], [[Glen Canyon National Recreation Area]], [[Lake Mead National Recreation Area]], Northern Arizona University, and AZFRO/Parker FRO staff), reflecting the species' status as a focal taxon in Colorado River Basin native fish recovery programs.

## Key Findings
- **Total adults sampled**: 257 razorback suckers collected across two survey weeks in March 1994 (105 + 152), plus additional fish handled during targeted propagation trips (118 handled on 31 March; 9 + 52 netted on 7–8 April).
- **Propagation output**: 20 successful pairings produced an estimated 105,000 eggs delivered to [[Willow Beach National Fish Hatchery]] between 24 March and 9 April 1994.
- **Egg delivery timeline**:
  | Date | Pairings | Est. Eggs |
  |------|----------|-----------|
  | 24 Mar | 3 | 15,000 |
  | 25 Mar | 3 | 15,000 |
  | 26 Mar | 2 | 10,000 |
  | 31 Mar | 8 | 45,000 |
  | 9 Apr | 4 | 20,000 |
- **Hatchery outcomes**: Eggs delivered 24–26 March had largely hatched by 29 March; an initial fungal infection problem was resolved through manual egg-picking, followed by standard chemical treatment (referred to as "Argentine," likely a fungicidal agent) after the first week. Estimated fry production prior to the final 9 April delivery was ~5,000, though this figure may be an undercount due to the small size of larvae.
- **Wild larval recruitment concern**: Nightly dip-netting for wild larvae was largely unproductive (except for an anomalous catch of ~1,000 larvae at Tequila Cove on 31 March), underscoring the reliance on artificial propagation to supplement the wild [[Razorback Sucker]] population in Lake Mohave.
- **Recapture trends**: The rise in recapture rate from 18% to 30% between successive survey weeks may reflect a smaller effective adult population size or repeated sampling of the same spawning aggregation within the reservoir.
- **Non-native species presence**: Incidental capture of [[Largemouth Bass]] confirms continued co-occurrence of predatory sportfish within razorback sucker spawning/nursery habitat, a factor of ongoing concern for wild recruitment failure.
