# Salt River Fish and Aquatic Fauna Survey

#ichthyology #marine-biology #cyprinidae #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This field survey of the [[Salt River]] in Arizona was conducted in two excursions: one from [[Gleason Flats]] (Station 5) to [[Roosevelt Lake]], and one from the junction of the [[Black River]] and [[White River]] (Station 1) to the crossing of the Salt River at the [[Highway 60 Bridge]]. Sampling used gill nets and seining, supplemented by extra-station seining. The survey recorded eleven native and introduced fish species, several aquatic invertebrates and reptiles, and bird observations. It also documented the absence of several rare and endangered species from the sampled reaches, along with signs of predation pressure and a stressed population of [[Gila Sucker]]. The authors state that a critical segment, between the Highway 60 Bridge and Gleason Flats, could not be sampled because of lack of funds and time, so no hard conclusions should be drawn without that gap in mind.

## Background & Description

**Survey design and methods**
- Access was by floating, dragging, and carrying a rubber raft downriver.
- Station selection weighed habitat diversity, distance from prior stations, and travel time.
- Lower stations were much closer together than upper stations, and the lower reach received the most intense effort because it was considered the most likely place to find [[Colorado Squawfish]] or [[Humpback Sucker]].
- Gill nets were set in late afternoon, run just before dark, and checked again just after dawn.
- Seining was done at each station where possible and at many additional points, including rest and lunch stops and interesting habitat.

**Station locations (as listed in the document)**
- Station 1: Junction of Black and White Rivers (NE ¼ of Section 18, T-5N, R-20E)
- Gleason Flat, vicinity of the mouth of Medicine Creek (Station 5 area; SE ¼ of Section 31, T-5N, R-16E)
- Horseshoe Bend, vicinity of the mouth of Mud Springs Wash
- Junction of [[Cherry Creek]] with Salt River (Station 9 area)
- Junction of [[Coon Creek]] with Salt River (Station 11 area)
- 2 miles above Cherry Creek junction; 3 miles below junction of Lower Corral Canyon with Salt River
- 12 (approximately 2 miles above Arizona 288 bridge)

**Species accounts**
- [[Roundtail Chub]] (*Gila robusta*): taken at all stations; juveniles in all extra-station seinings. Adults were netted in pools at least 1.5 m deep. Juveniles were seined in eddies associated with the main current. Sizes ranged from 20 to 300 mm SL.
- [[Longfin Dace]] (*Agosia chrysogaster*): seined at stations 10, 11, and 13, and in Cherry and Coon Creeks. Taken only in backwaters at station 12. None were recorded at or above station 10 despite apparently suitable gravel-flat habitat 30 to 50 cm deep.
- [[Gila Sucker]] (*Pantosteus clarki*): taken at all stations. Very abundant from station to station 3, with schools of more than fifty individuals of 300 to 500 mm. Juveniles fed in algal mats along shores, beginning near station 3. Adults occurred only in mainstream current, not backwaters.
- [[Sonora Sucker]] (*Catostomus insignis*): taken at all stations except 1 and 4. Taken by gill net in pools of the mainstream and in backwaters; never taken in riffles or rapids. Young were common along shore, feeding in algal mats, but none were observed upstream of station 3.
- [[Channel Catfish]] (*Ictalurus punctatus*): taken at all stations except 4 and 8 (the text lists "deviation 8" in the OCR; interpreted as the station 4 fast-water pool and one other location). Sizes ranged from 20 to 300 mm SL; one individual of 400 to 500 mm was observed. A skull remnant from a backwater at station 8 measured 190 mm from back of skull to snout tip.
- [[Yellow Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus natalis*): taken at stations 2, 3, 8, 11, and 12. Sizes ranged from 30 to 146 mm SL; several individuals near 140 mm, none larger.
- [[Common Carp]] (*Cyprinus carpio*): taken or observed at stations 5, 6, 8, 10, and 11. Sizes ranged from 20 to 300 mm SL.
- [[Red Shiner]] (*Notropis lutrensis*): taken at stations 8, 9, and 10. Never more than two individuals per seine area or gill net set.
- [[Green Sunfish]] (*Lepomis cyanellus*): taken at stations 1 and 2; one individual observed near Quartzite Falls. Sizes around 140 mm SL.
- [[Smallmouth Bass]] (*Micropterus dolomieu*): taken at station 2; many individuals around 80 mm SL in the Black River and the Salt River down to station 2.
- [[Speckled Dace]] (*Rhinichthys osculus*): two individuals taken in Cherry Creek during this study.
- [[Brown Trout]] (*Salmo trutta*): one dead individual, 120 mm SL, found in Cherry Creek, apparently washed downstream.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Barriers and habitat**
- [[Quartzite Falls]] appears to be a barrier that prevents upstream movement of [[Red Shiner]]; the shiners in the section below it are thought to result from release, possibly as bait, in the heavily fished area just above [[Roosevelt Lake]].
- Juvenile roundtails and Gila suckers used current-associated eddies. Adults stayed in mainstream current.
- Turbulent canyons upstream of the Highway 60 Bridge were noted as possible but unverified habitat for [[Channel Catfish]] and [[Sonora Sucker]].

**Gila sucker mortality event**
- In the last two miles of the Black River, the team found forty dead [[Gila Sucker]]s and twenty dying, plus many sluggish individuals that could be approached and grabbed. All were adults of at least 200 mm SL. No young were found and no other fish appeared affected. The stressed zone ended at the confluence with the White River. No cause was identified.

**Predation hypothesis**
- Smallmouth bass were abundant in the Black River and the Salt River down to station 2. In these same areas no young suckers or roundtails were taken or observed, although adults of both were common. The authors suggest this lack implies heavy predation by bass on young fish.

**Smallmouth bass introduction**
- Smallmouth bass were planted in the Black River in 1963 or 1964 (Ercole 1967). Arizona Game and Fish personnel have authenticated a dozen or more catches from Roosevelt Lake in recent years. Smallmouth were also planted in lower Tonto Creek in summer of an unspecified year, although Ercole (1967) reported six adults in Roosevelt Lake almost a year before that plant.
- No references to smallmouth in the Salt River before 1967 were found.

**Invertebrates, parasites, and reptiles**
- [[Yellow Grub]] (*Clinostomum marginatum*) was noted at stations 1, 2, and 3. It was found on [[Gila Sucker]] and [[Sonora Sucker]] to a lesser extent, and on channel catfish. As many as forty grubs were found on a single sucker, under scales, at fin insertions, and in the mouth. The life cycle uses a snail (*Sublina*) as first host, fishes, frogs, and salamanders as second hosts, and birds as adult hosts; the great blue heron (*Ardea herodias*) is probably the common bird host.
- [[Sonoran Mud Turtle]] (*Kinosternon sonoriensis*) was found at stations 5, 9, and 10. Partially eaten roundtails were occasionally found in gill nets, apparently fed on by turtles.
- A native bivalve, [[Anodonta Californiensis]], was abundant at stations 1 and 2; none were observed below this point.

**Beaver evidence**
- Possible beaver presence was noted between stations 2 and 3. A 50 cm diameter sycamore was partially gnawed through, and a second 20 cm sycamore had been felled. Cuts were weathered, indicating they were not recent; no fresh sign was noted.

**Birds observed**
- Bald eagle (*Haliaeetus leucocephalus*), osprey (*Pandion haliaetus*), great blue heron, western grebe (*Aechmophorus occidentalis*), common merganser (*Mergus merganser*), blue-winged teal (*Anas discors*), mallard (*Anas platyrhynchos*), vermilion flycatcher (*Pyrocephalus rubinus*), killdeer (*Charadrius vociferus*), white-winged dove (*Zenaida asiatica*), black phoebe (*Sayornis nigricans*), cliff swallow (*Petrochelidon pyrrhonota*), cardinal (*Richmondena cardinalis*), canyon wren (*Catherpes mexicanus*), and green heron (*Butorides virescens*).
- Bald eagles: two adults at Horseshoe Bend, one adult at station 2, one adult between stations 3 and 4 (SW ¼ of Section 25, T-5N, R-18E). Two further possible sightings were unconfirmed.

**Species not taken by this survey but reported above Roosevelt Lake**
- [[Colorado Squawfish]], [[Humpback Sucker]], [[Spikedace]], [[Loach Minnow]], [[Flannelmouth Sucker]], and [[Black Bullhead]]. Flannelmouth suckers had been reported on occasion; black bullheads are not common in the river.

## Key Findings

1. **Survey coverage:** Two excursions covering the Black/White junction to the Highway 60 Bridge and Gleason Flats to Roosevelt Lake. The Highway 60 Bridge to Gleason Flats segment was not sampled due to lack of funds and poor conditions.
2. **Rare species status:** Four rare and endangered species (loach minnow, spikedace, Colorado squawfish, humpback sucker) were recorded in the unsampled-to-sampled reach between the Highway 60 Bridge and Gleason Flats, between 5 and 22 years before the survey. None were taken in this survey.
3. **Last recorded humpback sucker:** Reported in the lower Salt River (Minckley and Deacon 1968), but the authors could not rule out survival in unsampled deep holes.
4. **Colorado squawfish:** The last report came from this segment (Branson et al. 1966). Two individuals were taken below the mouth of Cibecue Creek in 1950.
5. **Speckled dace:** Taken in Cherry Creek during this study; recorded at the Highway 60 Bridge and below Cibecue Creek in 1950, 1966, and 1967.
6. **Gila topminnow:** Common in lower Salt River marshes in the 1800s. Only small marsh areas remained. Mosquitofish (*Gambusia affinis*), a major factor in topminnow decline, were absent above Roosevelt Lake.
7. **Predation concern:** Smallmouth bass may be suppressing recruitment of young suckers and roundtails.
8. **Red shiner management:** Quartzite Falls limits upstream spread; use as bait above this point would likely lead to establishment throughout the river.
9. **Gila sucker die-off:** Forty dead and twenty dying adults in the last two miles of the Black River. Cause unknown.
10. **Recommendation:** The authors recommend a thorough survey of the unsampled Highway 60 Bridge to Gleason Flats segment, which they say would complete the study of the Salt River.
