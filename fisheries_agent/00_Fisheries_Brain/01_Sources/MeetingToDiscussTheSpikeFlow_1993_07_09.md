# Meeting to Discuss the 1994 Spike Flow

#ichthyology #marine-biology #river-ecology #colorado-river-fishes

## Executive Summary
This document is an administrative memorandum from the [[Bureau Of Reclamation]]'s [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] (GCES) program, dated July 9, 1993. It announces a planning meeting to define the technical parameters of a proposed **1994 Spike Flow** experiment from [[Glen Canyon Dam]]. The memo itself contains no direct empirical fish data, morphological measurements, or ecological findings; rather, it is a procedural record identifying the experts convened to design an experimental flow regime intended to inform the [[1994 Annual Operating Plan]] for dam operations. The meeting is notable as a key organizational step in the broader [[Glen Canyon Dam Adaptive Management Program]], which sought to balance hydropower operations with the conservation of native Colorado River fish and the health of the downstream riverine ecosystem.

## Background & Description
No morphological, anatomical, or species-specific biological data are presented in this source document. The memo is purely organizational, listing invited participants and their disciplinary expertise rather than presenting biological specimens or measurements.

## Ecological & Environmental Context
While no direct ecological data are reported, the meeting's stated purpose situates it firmly within Colorado River ecosystem management. Key ecological/geomorphological domains represented among invited experts include:

- **[[Aquatic Ecology]]** — Rich Valdez ([[Bio/West]]), Dave Wegner ([[GCES]]/[[Bureau of Reclamation]])
- **[[Native Fish]] Conservation** — Wendell Minckley ([[Arizona State University]]), Bill Persons ([[Arizona Game and Fish Department]])
- **[[Geomorphology]]** — Jack Schmidt ([[Utah State University]])
- **[[Limnology]]** — Dick Marzolf ([[Utah State University]])
- **[[Flow Routing]] and Hydrology** — Jim Smith ([[USGS]])
- **[[Sedimentation]] Studies** — Bill Jackson ([[National Park Service]])
- **[[Terrestrial Ecology]]** — Larry Stevens ([[NPS]]), Duncan Patten ([[Arizona State University]])
- **Tribal and Water Scheduling Interests** — Don Bay ([[Hualapai Tribe]]), Randy Peterson ([[Bureau of Reclamation]])
- **[[Power Concerns]]** — David Sabo ([[Western Area Power Administration]])

The convening of specialists in native fish biology (Minckley, Persons) alongside geomorphologists and hydrologists underscores the interdisciplinary intent of the Spike Flow: an experimental high-flow release designed to mimic natural spring flooding, potentially benefiting native fish habitat (e.g., backwater and sandbar formation important for species such as [[Humpback Chub]] and [[Razorback Sucker]]) while managing sediment transport and hydropower constraints.

## Key Findings
- The memo establishes that the **1994 Spike Flow** experiment required determination of four core technical parameters: **timing**, **duration**, **magnitude**, and **shape** of the flow release from [[Glen Canyon Dam]].
- A separate parallel review process, led by **Duncan Patten**, was tasked with evaluating prior proposals related to the spike flow, distinct from the technical design meeting described here.
- The meeting (July 19, 1993, at [[Arizona State University]]) was intended to produce a formal **technical letter report** to the [[Bureau of Reclamation]] to guide official inclusion of the spike flow in the **1994 Annual Operating Plan**.
- The list of invited participants reflects the broad, multidisciplinary scope of the [[Glen Canyon Environmental Studies]] program, integrating native fish biology, aquatic and terrestrial ecology, sediment transport, hydrology, and power operations into experimental flow management decisions.
- No quantitative fish population, morphometric, or habitat-use data are contained in this document; it should be treated as a **procedural/administrative record** contextualizing the broader experimental adaptive management framework for the [[Colorado River]] ecosystem downstream of Glen Canyon Dam.
