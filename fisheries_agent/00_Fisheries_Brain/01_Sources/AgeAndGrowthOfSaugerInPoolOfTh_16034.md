#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Percidae

## Executive Summary

This study, conducted by the Iowa Cooperative Fishery Unit (1966–1968), examined the age, growth, and length-weight relationships of [[Sauger]] (*Stizostedion canadense*) collected from Pool 19 of the [[Mississippi River]]. The research was part of a broader investigation into fish ecology and food habits related to a planned harbor dredging project at Fort Madison. A total of 304 sauger scale samples were analyzed. The length-weight relationship indicated positive allometric growth (weight increasing faster than the cube of length), though this pattern was not consistent across all sampling seasons. Growth rates were comparable to other sections of the Mississippi River, slower than in newly formed reservoirs (where rapid growth is typical), but faster than populations in [[Lake of the Woods]], Minnesota. Year classes from 1963–1967 were represented with little variation in relative abundance, suggesting consistent annual reproductive success.

## Background & Description

- **Length-Weight Relationship:**
  `Log W = -5.552 + 3.184 Log L`
  Where W = weight in grams, L = total length in millimeters.

- **Allometric Growth:** The slope (3.184) was statistically significantly greater than 3.0 (t = 3.91, df = 301), indicating **positive allometric growth**—fish increase in relative plumpness (condition) as they grow longer, rather than growing isometrically.

- **Condition Factor (k):** Calculated as `k = W × 10^5 / L^3`. Average condition increased with length in most seasonal samples, though this trend reversed in specific periods (see Ecological Niche/Key Findings).

- **Age Structure:** Scale analysis (using the Smith 1954 plastic impression method) revealed **no fish older than 3 annuli** (age classes) in the sample population. No definitive evidence explained the absence of older individuals, though some ambiguous marks on scales were noted but not counted as true annuli.

- **Size Range Sampled:** Total lengths ranged from approximately 6.0 to 18.9 inches (per Table 2 size groupings).

## Ecological & Environmental Context

- **Habitat:** Collected via gill nets and electrofishing in Pool 19 of the Mississippi River, a large-river benthic/pelagic habitat subject to anthropogenic pressures (e.g., dredging for harbor development at Fort Madison).

- **Trophic Role:** The broader study context indicates sauger were assessed for their utilization of **bottom (benthic) organisms** as a food source, linking this species to the river's [[Benthic Fauna]] community structure.

- **Seasonal Condition Variability:** Condition factor (k) did not uniformly increase with length across all seasons:
  - **Increased with length:** July 1966, October–December 1966, May 1967.
  - **Did NOT increase with length:** August 1966, June 1967, July 1967.
  - Overall body condition was **better in 1966** than during the June–July 1967 sampling period.

- **Comparative Growth Environments:** Growth rates in Pool 19 were:
  - Similar to other Mississippi River pools (Pools 7-10, 11, 13-19).
  - **Slower** than in new impoundments/reservoirs like [[Lewis and Clark Lake]] (South Dakota) and [[Cherokee Lake]] (Tennessee), consistent with the general ecological pattern of rapid fish growth in newly flooded, nutrient-rich reservoir environments.
  - **Faster** than in [[Lake of the Woods]], Minnesota.

## Key Findings

1. **Growth Model:** The length-weight relationship for Pool 19 sauger follows `Log W = -5.552 + 3.184 Log L`, confirming positive allometric growth for the population.
2. **Age Limitation:** Despite sampling 304 individuals, no sauger with more than 3 annuli were identified, suggesting either a young population structure, high mortality of older individuals, or difficulty in scale interpretation for older fish (a potential source of analytical bias).
3. **Reservoir vs. River Growth:** Sauger in riverine environments (Mississippi River pools) grow slower than in new reservoirs but faster than in natural lake systems like Lake of the Woods, supporting the ecological principle that newly impounded waters offer temporarily enhanced growth conditions.
4. **Stable Recruitment:** The presence of 1963–1967 year classes in relatively even distribution suggests **consistent annual reproductive success** for sauger in Pool 19 during the study period, with no evidence of major recruitment failures or booms.
5. **Methodology Note:** Age determination followed [[Carlander]] (1961) methodology, with scales read multiple times to reach reader agreement, acknowledging potential bias in difficult-to-interpret scales.
