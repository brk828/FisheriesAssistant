# The Cupido Cichlid (*Biotodoma cupido*)

#ichthyology #freshwater-biology #Cichlidae #aquarium-fish

## Executive Summary

This article by Paul V. Loiselle discusses [[Biotodoma cupido]], a South American cichlid first imported to Germany in 1935 and reintroduced to United States aquarists. The species presents maintenance challenges requiring specific water conditions and has steadfastly refused to reproduce in captivity. The author advocates for the generic designation *Biotodoma* over *Geophagus* based on significant morphological differences, and reviews the species' coloration, behavior, husbandry requirements, and mysterious reproductive biology.

## Background & Description

[[Biotodoma cupido]] was scientifically described in 1840 by German ichthyologist Heckel as a *Geophagus* species. In 1904, Pellegrin reassigned it to genus *Biotodoma* based on differences in gill anatomy and skull structure, though this distinction has not been generally recognized in aquarium literature.

**Morphological Distinctions from *Geophagus*:**
- Lacks large pointed head, protractile upper jaw, and flattened throat/ventral profile characteristic of *Geophagus*
- Eye diameter approximately equal to snout length (considerably less in *Geophagus*)
- Dorsal and anal fins never display pointed extremities of adult *Geophagus*
- Metallic spangled coloration much reduced compared to *Geophagus*

**Coloration:**
- Base color: violet-grey with irregularly-shaped subdued coppery blotch on shoulder
- Each scale has minute green spangle, especially prominent on lower flanks
- Roughly eye-shaped black spot edged in shining white at far base of soft dorsal
- Broad black band through eye to base of gill cover
- Shining white patch behind eye bar
- Deep-blue wormlike markings on gill covers (more intense in males)
- Dorsal: metallic coppery with dusky margin in spiny portion, rosewood or subdued magenta in soft portion
- Notched tail with white-edged upper and lower borders
- Very large dusky violet ventrals with striking broad leading edge of shining white
- Large fan-shaped transparent pectorals

**Fright Coloration:**
When frightened, metallic coloration disappears along with coppery glow; fish becomes deep umber with three to seven light spots on dorsum; ocellated spot obscured.

**Sexual Dimorphism:**
- Males: larger size, minutely-pointed soft dorsal, more intense blue wormlike markings about gills, larger coppery shoulder spot, longer more vividly-edged ventral
- Females: generally less colorful, more rounded ventral profile
- Author preserved specimens displaying these differences and examined for presence of testes and ovaries to confirm validity

## Ecological & Environmental Context

[[Biotodoma cupido]] is definitively a fish of soft, warm, acid waters and does not show best coloration or health under other conditions.

**Water Parameters:**
- Author's specimens maintained in softened Los Angeles tap water: pH 7.2, hardness 40 ppm
- Fish remained healthy but did not spawn
- Suspected sodium ions from ion-exchange resin present in too great concentration to permit normal reproduction
- Likely requires conditions similar to [[Discus]] for reproduction
- Temperature range: 75-80°F adequate for general maintenance
- Markedly benefited from frequent partial water changes
- Reacts adversely to brilliant lighting; subdued illumination necessary for normal behavior

**Feeding Ecology:**
- Particular about food
- Eagerly accepted: [[Tubifex]] worms, live [[Artemia]], paste food
- Little enthusiasm for flake food (eaten only when quite hungry)
- Never observed ingesting boiled lettuce or other plant food
- Author suspects specialization on insect larvae and small crustaceans in wild
- Small, slightly bottom-placed mouth reinforces this suspicion

**Behavior:**
- Contrary to reputation as quarrelsome, author's specimens peaceful toward other fish to point of abject timidity
- Displayed only moderate aggressiveness toward one another
- Almost all attacks directed at eye-spot on upper flanks or white spot
- No attempts to establish territory
- Suspected to occur in small schools in nature
- Stable "peck order" established and maintained
- Initially timid but became accustomed to keeper after few days
- Introduction of net resulted in "virtual disappearing act"

## Key Findings

**Captive Maintenance:**
- Author maintained group of six specimens (three males, three females)
- Fish were 2.5 to 3.5 inches length when purchased
- Did not grow noticeably despite generous feeding, presumed sexually mature
- Despite favorable sex ratio and generous choice of mates, fish never engaged in reproductive behavior
- Chasing strictly random; sex hormones elicited negative response
- Survivors (two females, two males) transferred to colleague for further breeding attempts

**Reproductive Biology:**
According to observations by [[Eigenmann]] and Kennedy in the wild, [[Biotodoma cupido]] is a mouthbrooder that incubates ova within its gill chambers. If true, this represents a mode of reproduction quite without counterpart in the family [[Cichlidae]]. However, reproductive behavior remains unconfirmed in captivity and subject to considerable speculation.

**Taxonomic Recommendation:**
Author strongly prefers generic designation *Biotodoma* over *Geophagus* based on:
- Structural differences in head, jaw, and body profile
- Eye-to-snout proportions
- Fin morphology
- Coloration patterns
- Overall appearance ("looks like an [[Aequidens]] trying hard to become a [[Geophagus]]")

**Conservation Status in Aquarium Trade:**
Species represents "walking shadow" that passes briefly through aquarium trade. Success on reintroduction depends on meeting exacting water chemistry requirements and solving reproductive mystery. Tank-raised fish may prove less particular than wild-caught specimens.
