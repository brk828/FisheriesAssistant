# Reintroduction of Razorback Sucker into the Lower Colorado River (1987 Stocking Memorandum)

#ichthyology #marine-biology #catostomidae

## Executive Summary

This 1987 interagency memorandum from the [[California Department of Fish and Game]] (Region 5, Blythe office) reports the stocking of hatchery-reared [[Razorback Sucker]] fry into the [[Lower Colorado River]] and associated ponds and backwaters. The fry were supplied by the [[USFWS Dexter National Fish Hatchery]] and transported to the Blythe office, where they were divided by weight and distributed among multiple release sites. The memo is an operational record of the stocking effort. It does not report post-release survival, growth, or monitoring results.

## Background & Description

- **Species focus:** [[Razorback Sucker]] (*Xyrauchen texanus*), a large catostomid native to the [[Colorado River Basin]] and listed as a species of management concern, which is the reason for the reintroduction program.
- **Sender:** Dan Langhorst, Associate Fishery Biologist, Region 5, [[California Department of Fish and Game]], writing as the Razorback Sucker consultant to the Blythe office.
- **Date:** April 7, 1987 (the scan is partly illegible; the delivery date appears to be March 1987).
- **Subject:** Reintroduction of Razorback Sucker into the Lower Colorado River.
- **Life stage stocked:** One-week-old fry, measured at roughly 7.8 to 9.5 mm total length (OCR-garbled; treat as approximate).
- **Source:** Fry were delivered to the Blythe office by personnel from the [[USFWS Dexter National Fish Hatchery]].

## Ecological & Environmental Context

**Transport and handling.** Fry were divided by weight and counted into plastic-bag liners inside [[Styrofoam]] coolers. The coolers were filled with oxygen and sealed before transport to stocking sites. This is a standard approach for keeping larval fish alive during transit.

**Stocking locations.** The memo lists about a dozen release sites across the lower river and its managed water bodies, grouped by administrative division:

- **Blythe area:** Blythe Municipal Golf Course pond, Blythe Office Aquaria.
- **Palo Verde Division:** Colorado River Indian Reservation ponds, including the Hilland Native Fish Ponds (ponds of roughly 8, 10, and 12 acres).
- **Cibola Division:** Oxbow Pond.
- **Imperial Division:** Imperial National Wildlife Refuge backwaters, including the Draper Rock backwater (river mile 61.7), the backwater across from Draper, and the backwater upstream from Island Lake (river mile 102).
- **Parker Division:** Backwaters behind training structures at several sites.

**Habitat type.** Stocking targeted two habitat types: managed ponds and low-velocity river backwaters. Backwaters are slow-water habitats that give small fry refuge from high flows and predators.

## Key Findings

- **Total stocked:** The scan records a combined count of approximately 1,098,000 fry (reported as "1r098r8o4"; the final digits are illegible), with a total weight of approximately 5,409 grams. This gives roughly 200 fish per gram, though the printed rate in the scan is partly unreadable.
- **Distribution:** Fry were allocated in proportion to weight. Individual site numbers are partly illegible in the scan, but the legible values are about 100,000 at each of two sites, about 250,000 at each of two ponds, and about 500,000 at one Imperial Division site.
- **Logistics:** Oxygenated, sealed transport in coolers was used to move fry from the hatchery to stocking sites.
- **Program scope:** The stocking spanned multiple divisions and both pond and river backwater habitats, reflecting an attempt to establish refugia along the lower river.
- **Limitations:** The memo documents stocking only. It does not report survival estimates, recapture data, water quality measurements, or predation assessments.
