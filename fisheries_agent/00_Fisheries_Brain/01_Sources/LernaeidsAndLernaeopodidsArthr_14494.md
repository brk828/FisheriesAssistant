# Lernaeid and Lernaeopodid Copepod Parasites of Fishes in Eastern South Dakota

#ichthyology #marine-biology #lernaeidae #lernaeopodidae

## Executive Summary

This short parasitology report documents two parasitic copepods (Arthropoda: Eucopepoda) recovered from freshwater fishes in eastern South Dakota. The first is the [[Anchor Worm]], [[Lernaea Cyprinacea]], found embedded in the flesh of [[Fathead Minnow]] and [[White Sucker]] from the [[Big Sioux River]]. This was the first South Dakota record of the parasite. The second is [[Achtheres Ambloplitis]], attached to the gill arches and gill rakers of [[Black Bullhead]] from [[Wall Lake]] and [[Lake Hendricks]]. This appears to be the first report of this species from bullheads. The report also reviews western US records and synonymy questions affecting both genera.

## Background & Description

- **Anchor worm (Lernaea cyprinacea):** Reported under several names in the literature. The document notes that Uzmann and Rayner (1958) treated *L. elegans* Leigh-Sharpe, 1927 and *L. carassii* Tidd, 1933 as synonyms of *L. cyprinacea* Linnaeus, 1758. Haderlie (1953) had used *L. carassii* in California but suggested it might be synonymous with *L. cyprinacea*.
- **Achtheres ambloplitis:** Found on gill arches and gill rakers. It closely resembles [[Achtheres Pimelodi]], a species recorded from bullheads and catfish.
- **Taxonomic identifications:** Specimens were identified by T. E. Bowman of the U.S. National Museum, who suggested that further study might make *A. ambloplitis* a small form of *A. pimelodi*, which would make it a synonym.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The study covers freshwater habitats in eastern South Dakota. The sampled waters were:

- The [[Big Sioux River]], where *Lernaea cyprinacea* was found in the flesh of host fish.
- [[Wall Lake]] (Minnehaha County) and [[Lake Hendricks]] (Brookings County), where *Achtheres ambloplitis* was collected from bullheads.

The host fishes were:

- [[Fathead Minnow]] (*Pimephales promelas*) and [[White Sucker]] (*Catostomus commersoni*) for *Lernaea*.
- [[Black Bullhead]] (*Ictalurus melas*) for *Achtheres*.

The document also reports the parasite's range extending westward, with records from Colorado, California, Oregon, and Washington. These records show that the species had a broad distribution in western North America at the time.

## Key Findings

1. ***Lernaea cyprinacea* is newly recorded in South Dakota.** It was found in several fathead minnows and white suckers from the Big Sioux River. This extends the known range westward.
2. **Nomenclature is unsettled.** Several names (*L. carassii*, *L. elegans*) are considered synonyms of *L. cyprinacea* by some authors, though the author notes the synonymy was not universally accepted at the time.
3. ***Achtheres ambloplitis* is recorded from bullheads for the first time.** Specimens came from black bullheads at two South Dakota lakes.
4. **Possible synonymy with *A. pimelodi*.** The identifier suggested further study could reduce *A. ambloplitis* to a small form of *A. pimelodi*. The document does not resolve this question.
5. **No quantitative data are given.** The report gives no sample sizes, prevalence, or intensity values, so it documents occurrence only.
