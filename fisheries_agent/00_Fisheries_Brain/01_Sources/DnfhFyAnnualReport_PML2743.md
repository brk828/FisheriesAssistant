# Dexter NFH&TC Refugium Maintenance of Listed Fishes, 1999

#ichthyology #marine-biology #endangered-fishes

## Executive Summary

This source is a 1999 facility record (a funding or activity listing) for the [[Dexter National Fish Hatchery and Technology Center]]. It lists 16 line items covering 14 fish species that were "maintained as a refugium species" at the facility during 1999. Twelve items are [[Endangered Species]], three are [[Threatened Species]], and one is a [[Species of Special Concern]]. Each entry gives a per-species dollar amount. The document contains no methods, population data, or ecological measurements.

## Background & Description

Each record follows the same format: fiscal year, facility code, facility name, status category, species common name, and an amount. The entries describe the species only as listed fishes maintained in [[Captive Refugium Maintenance]]. Species are not described morphologically, and no scientific names, families, or native ranges are given. Some entries carry OCR errors, so the species names and amounts below were corrected where the intent was clear.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The source does not address habitat, water chemistry, distribution, or threats. The only ecological framing is conservation status:

- **Endangered (12 records):** [[Big Bend Gambusia]], [[Bonytail Chub]], [[Colorado Pikeminnow]] (three strains), [[Desert Pupfish]], [[Gila Topminnow]], [[Leon Springs Pupfish]], [[Pahranagat Roundtail Chub]], [[Razorback Sucker]], [[Virgin River Chub]], and [[Woundfin]].
- **Threatened (3 records):** [[Beautiful Shiner]], [[Chihuahua Chub]], and [[Warner Sucker]].
- **Species of Special Concern (1 record):** [[Yaqui Sucker]].

Most of the listed taxa are desert or Southwestern and Intermountain fishes, but the source does not say so, so that point is not drawn from the text.

## Key Findings

- **Scope:** 16 records covering 14 distinct species. Colorado Pikeminnow appears three times, once for each strain (Colorado River, Colorado/Green River, Yampa River).
- **Activity:** Every species was "maintained as a refugium species" at the facility in 1999. The document does not say whether any were propagated, stocked, or released.
- **Standard allocation:** Nine records list $14,175 each. These are Beautiful Shiner, Big Bend Gambusia, Desert Pupfish, Gila Topminnow, Leon Springs Pupfish, Pahranagat Roundtail Chub, Virgin River Chub, Warner Sucker, and Yaqui Sucker.
- **Higher amounts:**
  - Bonytail Chub: $129,792
  - Razorback Sucker: $106,167
  - Colorado Pikeminnow (Colorado/Green River strain): $56,166
  - Colorado Pikeminnow (Colorado River strain): $25,000
  - Colorado Pikeminnow (Yampa River strain): $25,000
  - Chihuahua Chub: $18,900
  - Woundfin: listed as "$1E,900" in the OCR text, most likely $18,900
- **Calculated total:** Summing the 16 listed amounts gives $507,500. This figure was derived for this note and does not appear in the source. The document does not state its currency or what each amount covers.
- **Largest single items:** The Bonytail Chub ($129,792) and Razorback Sucker ($106,167) records together account for about 46% of the calculated total.
- **Limits of the source:** It is an administrative listing. It gives no population counts, survival rates, genetic information, or outcomes, and it does not state which program or funding body issued the records.
