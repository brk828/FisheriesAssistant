# U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service Comments on Proposed Pigeon Creek Lake Project

#ichthyology #conservation-biology #endangered-species #native-fish #riparian-ecology

## Executive Summary

This August 12, 1991 letter from the [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] to the [[U.S. Forest Service]] provides formal comments on the proposed [[Pigeon Creek Lake]] project in [[Greenlee County]], [[Arizona]]. The Service raises significant concerns about the project's potential impacts to threatened and endangered species, native fish communities, riparian habitat, and cumulative watershed effects. The [[Blue River]] basin supports one of the few remaining native fish communities retaining the majority of original species, including the threatened [[Loach Minnow]] (Tiaroga cobitis) and proposed endangered [[Razorback Sucker]] (Xyrauchen texanus). The Service recommends preparation of a full Environmental Impact Statement and questions whether alternatives to damming [[Pigeon Creek]] have been adequately considered.

## Background & Description

The proposed project involves construction of a dam and reservoir on [[Pigeon Creek]], a tributary of the [[Blue River]] in the [[Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests]]. The project was added to the Forest Plan as a high priority capital investment in June 1990, with estimated costs ranging from $5 to $9 million. In September 1990, the Forest entered into a Memorandum of Understanding with [[Greenlee County]] to cooperate in planning and construction. A feasibility study dated October 30, 1991 considered seven alternatives, all representing different dam sites along a 6,000-foot length of [[Pigeon Creek]].

The stated project goal is to provide "some type of water based recreation facility in Greenlee County" to assist the local economy by increasing recreational opportunities. The [[U.S. Forest Service]] controls virtually the entire watershed of the [[Blue River]], providing unique opportunities for comprehensive watershed planning.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

The [[Pigeon Creek]] area provides habitat for three listed species, one proposed species, and nine candidate species. The nearby [[Blue Range Primitive Area]] is under consideration for reintroduction of the endangered [[Mexican Wolf]] (Canis lupus baileyi). The [[Blue River]] supports one of the few remaining native fish communities that still retain the majority of their original species.

The [[Blue River]] basin ranks high as a potential native fish management area due to: (1) the relatively large number of native fish species still remaining, (2) the relatively low number of species and individuals of nonnative fish, and (3) the relatively unaltered hydrograph of the basin. The draft Recovery Plan for the [[Loach Minnow]] recommends a strategy of setting aside certain drainage basins for native fish conservation with no stocking of nonnative fishes, while other areas would be managed for sport fishing and game fish stocking.

Riparian vegetation exists in the proposed inundation area, with concerns about both direct loss and downstream impacts. The [[U.S. Forest Service]] controls virtually the entire [[Blue River]] watershed, providing an unparalleled opportunity to demonstrate the value of watershed planning and avoid short-sighted, piecemeal development that has damaged other watersheds.

## Key Findings

**NEPA Process Concerns:**
- The project goal has never been clearly stated, shifting from assisting the [[Greenlee County]] economy through increased recreation to providing "some type of water based recreation facility"
- No alternatives to a dam on [[Pigeon Creek]] appear to have been considered; all seven alternatives in the feasibility study were simply alternative dam sites along the same 6,000-foot creek segment
- The project appears predetermined, with addition to the Forest Plan and Memorandum of Understanding executed before NEPA analysis completion
- Alternative forms of recreation or enhancement of existing recreational opportunities have not been mentioned in project documents

**Cumulative Effects:**
- Potential for cumulative impacts from future development of multiple impoundments in the [[Blue River]] basin requires basin-wide analysis
- Increased recreational traffic may necessitate highway improvements or construction of a new access road along the [[San Francisco River]] and [[Blue River]], with a proposal already existing for such a highway joining Highway 666 near [[Four Bar Mesa]]
- Traffic concerns on the narrow, tortuous Highway 666 with increased recreational vehicles and boats

**Threatened and Endangered Species:**
- Three listed species, one proposed species ([[Razorback Sucker]]), and nine candidate species inhabit the area
- The [[Blue River]] supports one of the few remaining native fish communities retaining majority of original species
- Construction would significantly impair the basin's suitability as a native fish management area, limiting future protection and recovery options for [[Loach Minnow]], [[Razorback Sucker]], and other native fishes
- Concern about sacrificing habitat for rare species to create habitat for common species and recreational opportunities that could be placed elsewhere

**Riparian and Wildlife Impacts:**
- Loss of existing riparian vegetation in the inundation area
- Impacts to downstream riparian areas from altered hydrology
- Direct effects of reservoir and habitat alteration plus long-term effects of increased human use
- Substantial impacts anticipated not only at project site but also to the [[Blue River]] near the mouth of [[Pigeon Creek]] and surrounding areas

**Recommendation:**
The [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service]] recommends preparation of an Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) based on: potential impacts to many wildlife and plant species including threatened and endangered species and wetlands; long-term effects that would increase over time; substantial cumulative and indirect impacts; and estimated project cost of $5 to $9 million, meeting NEPA criteria as a major Federal project with significant impacts to the human environment.
