# 1987 Arizona Environmental & Resource Conservation Directory

#environmental-management #conservation #resource-directory #Arizona

## Executive Summary

This comprehensive directory, published by the Commission on the Arizona Environment in 1987, catalogs businesses, organizations, and agencies concerned with environmental resources in Arizona. The document provides contact information and descriptions for over 200 entities across multiple sectors including business groups, citizen and nonprofit organizations, educational institutions, professional associations, county and local government agencies, state government offices, federal agencies, and political subdivisions. While not focused specifically on ichthyology or aquatic ecology, the directory includes numerous organizations involved in water resources management, fisheries conservation, riparian habitat protection, and aquatic ecosystem preservation throughout Arizona.

## Background & Description

The directory was compiled by the [[Commission on the Arizona Environment]] with assistance from Jackie Rich and Julie Hodgson, and design support from [[John Carollo Engineers]]. Financial support came from private sector companies including [[United Technologies]], [[Salt River Project]], and [[Western Technologies]]. The document is organized into eight major sections: Business Groups, Citizen and Nonprofit Organizations, Educational Institutions, Professional Associations, County & Local Government, State Government, Federal Government, and Political Subdivisions of Arizona (primarily Natural Resource Conservation Districts).

Organizations with direct relevance to aquatic ecosystems include [[Anglers United]], [[Arizona Flycasters' Club]], [[Trout Unlimited]], [[Ducks Unlimited]], [[Arizona Waterfowl Association]], [[Arizona Water Resources Committee]], [[Arizona Water Well Association]], [[Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit]], [[Arizona Riparian Council]], and the [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]]. Water quality and conservation organizations include the [[Arizona Water and Pollution Control Association]], [[Southern Arizona Water Resources Association]], and the [[Water Resources Research Center]] at the University of Arizona.

## Ecological & Environmental Context

Arizona's aquatic ecosystems face multiple challenges addressed by organizations in this directory. The state's arid and semi-arid climate makes water resources particularly critical. Key environmental concerns include:

**Water Resources**: Organizations focus on groundwater management, surface water allocation, [[Central Arizona Project]] water delivery, water quality monitoring, and conservation. The [[Arizona Department of Water Resources]] serves as the primary regulatory agency for surface water and groundwater laws.

**Riparian Systems**: The [[Arizona Riparian Council]] specifically addresses riparian ecology, management, and protection throughout Arizona and adjacent southwestern states. These systems support disproportionately high biodiversity in the desert landscape.

**Fisheries Management**: The [[Arizona Game & Fish Department]] manages wildlife resources including fish populations. Organizations like [[Trout Unlimited]] work on coldwater habitat enhancement and restoration, while [[Anglers United]] focuses on fish habitat development and preservation projects.

**Aquatic Habitat Threats**: Multiple organizations address threats including water pollution, habitat degradation from grazing and development, groundwater contamination (e.g., [[Cashion Citizens for Uncontaminated Water]] addressing pesticide contamination), and water allocation conflicts.

**Wetland and Waterfowl Habitat**: [[Ducks Unlimited]] and the [[Arizona Waterfowl Association]] work on developing, restoring, and maintaining waterfowl habitat, which provides critical ecosystem services.

## Key Findings

**Organizational Diversity**: The directory documents extensive organizational infrastructure for environmental and resource conservation in Arizona, with representation across government (federal, state, local), academic, nonprofit, and private sectors.

**Water-Focused Organizations**: A substantial proportion of listed organizations have water resources as a primary or significant focus, reflecting Arizona's water scarcity and the critical importance of aquatic resource management.

**Fisheries and Aquatic Conservation Groups**: Multiple organizations specifically address fish conservation and aquatic habitat:
- [[Anglers United]] raises funds for fish habitat development and preservation
- [[Arizona Flycasters' Club]] and [[Northern Arizona Flycasters]] promote flyfishing and conservation
- [[Trout Unlimited]] (Zane Grey and Old Pueblo chapters) focuses on coldwater fisheries and habitat restoration
- [[Arizona Cooperative Fish and Wildlife Research Unit]] conducts university-level research and training

**Riparian Ecosystem Focus**: The [[Arizona Riparian Council]] provides specialized attention to riparian systems, recognizing their ecological importance in arid landscapes.

**Water Quality Monitoring**: Multiple agencies monitor water quality including the [[Arizona Department of Environmental Quality]], [[Pima County Air Quality Control District]], county health departments, and the [[State Health Laboratory]].

**Educational and Research Institutions**: Universities provide critical research and education capacity through programs including the [[Water Resources Research Center]], [[Department of Soil & Water Science]], [[Environmental Engineering Program]], and [[School of Renewable Natural Resources]] at the University of Arizona, plus the [[Center for Environmental Studies]] at Arizona State University.

**Conservation Districts**: Arizona has 30 Natural Resource Conservation Districts and 5 Soil and Water Conservation Districts serving as legal subdivisions of state government, working cooperatively with landowners on soil and water conservation.

**Integrated Approach**: Many organizations address aquatic ecosystems within broader environmental contexts, recognizing connections between water quality, land use, air quality, wildlife habitat, and human activities.

**Volunteer and Citizen Engagement**: Numerous citizen organizations and volunteer groups contribute to environmental monitoring, habitat restoration, and conservation advocacy, demonstrating significant public engagement in aquatic resource protection.

**Regulatory Framework**: Multiple state and federal agencies provide regulatory oversight for water resources, including the [[U.S. Soil Conservation Service]], [[Bureau of Land Management]], [[U.S. Fish and Wildlife Services]], and state agencies managing water rights, quality standards, and wildlife resources.
